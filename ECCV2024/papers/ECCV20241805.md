## Problem

Standard vision-language models like MineCLIP capture video-text similarity only at a coarse entity level, failing to reflect the degree of task completion (e.g., how close the agent is to the target). This makes them poor intrinsic reward functions for RL, as agents cannot distinguish between similar states where the target is near versus far. Additionally, the MineDojo YouTube dataset suffers from significant video-text misalignment that hinders VLM training.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video snippet of 16 consecutive egocentric RGB frames and a natural language task prompt (e.g., 'hunt a cow in plains with a diamond sword'), producing a scalar similarity score that is converted to a reward via r_t = max(P_{G,t} - 1/N_t, 0), where N_t is the number of candidate prompts. The model handles unseen language prompts without fine-tuning thanks to CLIP's open-vocabulary capability, and the reward is designed to increase monotonically as the target entity grows larger in the frame (i.e., as the agent approaches the target).

## Method

CLIP4MC extends MineCLIP's dual-encoder architecture (spatial + temporal transformer for video, text encoder, similarity calculator) with a MaskCLIP-inspired modification that segments the target entity and estimates its bounding-box size in each frame without fine-tuning. The key training innovation is a swap-based contrastive objective: during training, a positive video sample is randomly replaced by a negative sample with probability P_swap = P_max · ReLU(1 − H_b·W_b / (τ·H·W)), where the target size H_b·W_b is inversely related to swap probability, so smaller (farther) targets are more likely to be demoted from positive to negative. This forces the model to assign lower similarity scores to states with small targets, making the reward signal increase with task completion. The model is trained on a 640K video-text dataset produced by a four-step cleaning pipeline (Whisper transcript correction, keyword filtering, Bellman k-segmentation for scene coherence, and dual-level correlation filtering at both global video and local object levels).

## Results

On the four hunt tasks (the most challenging), CLIP4MC achieves success rates of 39.8%, 45.9%, 30.6%, and 26.1% (hunt a cow, sheep, pig, chicken respectively), substantially outperforming MineCLIP [ours] (17.3%, 33.0%, 14.1%, 15.3%) and MineCLIP [official] (11.6%, 28.5%, 1.5%, 0.0%). On the easier harvest and combat tasks, all three methods perform comparably, with CLIP4MC achieving 84.5% on milk a cow and 85.8% on combat a spider. The Pearson correlation between target entity size and intrinsic reward is 0.81 for CLIP4MC versus 0.66 and 0.62 for the two baselines, confirming that CLIP4MC's reward signal more faithfully reflects task completion degree.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CLIP4MC against two baselines: MineCLIP [official] (the publicly released model from MineDojo) and MineCLIP [ours] (same architecture trained on the authors' cleaned dataset, also serving as an ablation without the swap operation). CLIP4MC decisively wins on all four hunt tasks, where the gap is largest: e.g., 39.8% vs. 17.3% vs. 11.6% on hunt a cow, and 26.1% vs. 15.3% vs. 0.0% on hunt a chicken. On harvest tasks (milk a cow, shear wool) and combat tasks, all three methods are within a few percentage points of each other, consistent with prior work showing MineCLIP already saturates on these easier tasks. MineCLIP [ours] consistently beats MineCLIP [official] across all tasks, confirming that the cleaned dataset alone improves reward quality. The ablation in Table 2 further shows that removing local correlation filtering (w/o LCF) degrades hunt a cow from 17.3% to 12.1%, and random sampling (RS) drops it to 4.0%, isolating the contribution of each dataset filtering step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MineCLIP (MineDojo, NeurIPS 2022), which first demonstrated that a VLM trained on narrated Minecraft YouTube videos can serve as an open-vocabulary intrinsic reward for RL agents. The key departure is incorporating task completion degree—proxied by target entity size in the frame—into the contrastive training objective via a size-dependent swap mechanism, building on MaskCLIP's (ECCV 2022) finding that CLIP features carry local spatial information. On the data side, the paper addresses the noise and misalignment in MineDojo's original 13.8M YouTube dataset through a four-step cleaning pipeline (transcript re-extraction with Whisper, keyword filtering, Bellman k-segmentation for scene coherence, and two-level correlation filtering), producing a reproducible 640K training set that MineDojo never publicly released.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical contribution of a clean, open-sourced 640K Minecraft video-text dataset is significant for the embodied AI community, as it removes a reproducibility barrier that has limited follow-up work on MineCLIP-style reward models. The finding that object-centric (local) correlation filtering improves RL performance while slightly degrading retrieval accuracy highlights a genuine tension between retrieval and reward objectives that future work should address. Limitations include evaluation on only eight programmatic tasks in a single game, a size-based proxy for task completion that may not generalize to tasks requiring distance or state changes beyond proximity, and the somewhat heuristic nature of the swap probability. Open questions include whether the RL-friendly reward formulation transfers to other open-ended environments or to creative (non-programmatic) MineDojo tasks.
