## Problem

Dynamic novel view synthesis from a single monocular video remains highly under-constrained: existing methods either require contemporaneous multi-viewpoint recordings or restrict camera changes to a few degrees. No prior approach can synthesize a temporally coherent video from an extreme novel viewpoint (e.g., 180° azimuth change) of a complex dynamic scene using only one input video, limiting applications in robotics, autonomous driving, and immersive media.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240635/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a monocular RGB video of T=14 frames at 384×256 resolution and a sequence of relative camera extrinsics ΔE ∈ SE(3)^T specifying the desired viewpoint transformation over time. It produces a temporally synchronized output video at the same resolution and frame count, depicting the same dynamic scene from the target viewpoint. Notably, no depth map or explicit 3D geometry is required as input, and camera intrinsics are assumed constant across the pose change.

## Method

GCD finetunes Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) as an end-to-end video-to-video translator by adding two key architectural modifications. First, camera pose conditioning is injected via a micro-conditioning pathway: the relative extrinsics ΔE are decomposed into per-frame rotation and translation, projected through a randomly initialized MLP, and added to feature vectors at multiple convolutional layers throughout the U-Net. Second, the standard image-to-video conditioning (which uses only the first frame) is replaced with full-video conditioning, where VAE-encoded frames of the entire input video are channel-concatenated with the denoised output at every timestamp, enabling the model to track scene dynamics over time. Training uses synthetic multi-view data from Kubric-4D and ParallelDomain-4D, where unproject-and-reproject routines generate paired videos from arbitrary camera trajectories. The model is initialized from the pretrained SVD image-to-video checkpoint and finetuned end-to-end, with a gradual (linearly interpolated) camera trajectory proving more effective than a direct jump to the target pose.

## Results

On Kubric-4D, GCD achieves 20.30 PSNR (all pixels) and 18.60 PSNR (occluded regions), substantially outperforming all baselines by 4.6–7.4 dB over the strongest competitor (ZeroNVS at 15.68 PSNR). On ParallelDomain-4D, GCD reaches 25.04 PSNR and 0.731 SSIM for RGB scene completion, and 43.4% mIoU for semantic completion, again leading all baselines. The model also demonstrates zero-shot generalization to real-world driving (Berkeley DeepDrive, TRI-DDAD), robotics (BridgeData V2), and object permanence (TCOW) videos without any real-world training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCD decisively outperforms all five baselines on both in-domain datasets. Against per-scene optimization methods, GCD gains +4.9 dB PSNR over HexPlane (20.30 vs 15.38), +5.4 dB over 4D-GS (20.30 vs 14.92), and +7.4 dB over DynIBaR (20.30 vs 12.86) on Kubric-4D; these baselines struggle to reconstruct a coherent 4D representation from a single viewpoint and produce severe artifacts. Against diffusion-based baselines, GCD beats Vanilla SVD by +6.5 dB PSNR (20.30 vs 13.85) because SVD cannot follow the prescribed camera trajectory or incorporate content from later frames, and beats ZeroNVS by +4.6 dB (20.30 vs 15.68) because ZeroNVS, applied frame-by-frame, lacks temporal coherence and does not respect scene dynamics. On ParallelDomain-4D, the margins widen further: GCD achieves 25.04 PSNR versus 12.88 for Vanilla SVD (+12.2 dB) and 18.88 for ZeroNVS (+6.2 dB). Even the privileged Reproject RGB-D baseline (which has access to ground-truth depth) scores only 12.51 PSNR on Kubric-4D, confirming that the input video alone contains limited geometric information and that GCD's generative prior is essential for filling in occluded regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) as a video-to-video translation backbone for dynamic novel view synthesis, adding camera extrinsics as a micro-conditioning signal and replacing the single-image conditioning with full-video conditioning. It departs from the dominant per-scene optimization paradigm (NeRF-based methods like HexPlane, 4D-GS, DynIBaR) by learning a single generalizable model, and extends prior monocular DVS work (which was limited to small viewpoint changes of a few degrees) to extreme transformations up to 180°. Conceptually, it follows the broader trend of leveraging large-scale diffusion priors for structured CV tasks (depth estimation, 3D reconstruction, amodal segmentation) but is the first to apply this to full 4D dynamic scene view synthesis with explicit camera control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a video diffusion model fine-tuned exclusively on synthetic multi-view data can generalize zero-shot to real-world dynamic scenes across driving, robotics, and indoor settings, including reconstructing fully occluded objects (object permanence) without any explicit 3D geometry modeling. This opens a path toward practical, in-the-wild novel view synthesis for embodied AI, AR/VR passthrough, and autonomous driving perception without requiring multi-camera rigs. Key limitations include the model's struggle with out-of-distribution content (e.g., moving humans), the reliance on synthetic training data, and the current resolution/frame-length constraints (384×256, 14 frames). Open questions include whether scaling to real multi-view video data or longer sequences would close the remaining gap, and whether the approach can handle truly unconstrained camera paths beyond the gradual interpolation protocol.
