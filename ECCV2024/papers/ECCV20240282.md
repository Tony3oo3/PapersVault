## Problem

Natural language-guided drone geolocalization lacks a large public multi-modal dataset with fine-grained spatial annotations, and existing cross-modal retrieval methods struggle to align text with the fine-grained visual details of drone-view scenes where similar-looking buildings coexist in neighboring regions. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing the GeoText-1652 benchmark (276K text-bbox pairs across 1,652 buildings) and a spatial-aware matching objective that explicitly encodes relative positions between regions.

## Input / Output

The model consumes drone-view images resized to 384×384 pixels (patch size 32) alongside natural language queries comprising image-level global descriptions (~70 words) and region-level short descriptions (~21.6 words) that include spatial phrases like 'upper left' or 'down right'. Outputs include a ranked list of retrieved images or text descriptions (for the two retrieval tasks), predicted bounding boxes in normalized [0,1] coordinates, and 9-class spatial relation labels (left/middle/right × top/middle/bottom) for pairs of regions of interest. Notably, data augmentation excludes random rotation and horizontal flipping to preserve the spatial information that the method relies on.

## Method

Building on the XVLM backbone (Swin Transformer image encoder + BERT text encoder + cross-modal transformer), the paper introduces 'blending spatial matching' that augments standard image-text contrastive (ITC) and matching (ITM) losses with two spatial objectives. The grounding loss predicts bounding boxes from region-level text via a 6-block cross-attention module followed by MLP, using L1 regression plus IoU loss. The spatial relation matching loss classifies the relative position between every pair of ROIs into 9 categories (3 horizontal × 3 vertical) by comparing center coordinates against half-width thresholds, trained with cross-entropy. The total loss is L_itc + L_itm + λ(L_grounding + L_spatial) with λ=0.1. The dataset itself is constructed through a two-phase human-computer annotation pipeline: a modality expansion phase where a visual LLM generates descriptions filtered by a referee model, and a spatial refinement phase where Grounding DINO localizes regions with iterative human-in-the-loop validation over 5 rounds.

## Results

On GeoText-1652, the proposed method achieves 13.6% Recall@1 and 31.2% Recall@10 for text-to-image retrieval (drone navigation via text), and 26.3% Recall@1 and 66.9% Recall@10 for image-to-text retrieval (drone-view geolocalization). These are the best results among all compared cross-modality methods, surpassing the strongest baseline XVLM_finetuned (16M) by +0.4% R@1 and +1.6% R@10 on text query, and by +1.3% R@1 and +1.8% R@10 on image query. The model uses 217M parameters with a Swin image encoder and BERT text encoder, pretrained on 16M images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four compared baselines (UNITER, METER-Swin, ALBEF, XVLM) in both text-to-image and image-to-text retrieval on GeoText-1652. Against the strongest fine-tuned baseline XVLM_finetuned (16M), the gains are modest but consistent: +0.4% R@1 and +1.6% R@10 on text query, +1.3% R@1 and +1.8% R@10 on image query. The advantage over ALBEF_finetuned (14M) is larger, with +1.1% R@1 and +2.7% R@10 on text query, and +3.1% R@1 and +4.5% R@10 on image query. UNITER_finetuned and METER-Swin_finetuned trail further behind, with the proposed method leading by +3.0% and +2.3% R@1 respectively on text query. Notably, all pre-trained (non-finetuned) models perform poorly (R@1 < 5%), underscoring the difficulty of aerial-view geolocalization and the value of the GeoText-1652 dataset for fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the University-1652 cross-view geolocalization benchmark by adding dense spatial-aware text annotations, and builds its method on the XVLM multi-grained vision-language pre-training framework (Zeng et al., ICML 2022). It departs from prior cross-view geolocalization work, which is purely image-to-image, by introducing natural language as the query modality and proposing a 'blending spatial matching' objective that couples grounding prediction with a 9-class relative-position classification. The annotation pipeline also diverges from manual labeling by leveraging a visual-LLM with a referee-model filtering loop, positioning the work at the intersection of LLM-assisted data synthesis and fine-grained spatial reasoning in vision-language models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GeoText-1652 is the first large-scale public benchmark specifically designed for natural language-guided drone geolocalization, filling a gap the authors identify as blocking progress in language-controlled UAV navigation. The spatial relation matching loss, while yielding only modest recall gains over the XVLM baseline (+0.4% R@1 text query), demonstrates that explicit 9-way orientation classification complements bounding-box regression for distinguishing visually similar objects. Open questions include whether the discrete 9-class spatial vocabulary is sufficient for arbitrary scenes, how the approach scales beyond 72 university campuses to open-world cities, and whether the LLM annotation pipeline generalizes to domains where spatial language is less structured (e.g., rural or natural terrain).
