## Problem

Existing 3D adversarial attacks primarily operate in white-box settings, introduce perturbations directly to xyz coordinates that produce visually unrealistic adversarial examples, and fail to transfer effectively to recently proposed 3D deep-learning models. Black-box 3D adversarial attacks in particular remain severely underdeveloped, with state-of-the-art methods achieving near-zero transfer success against modern architectures like PCT, PRC, and GDANet.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240748/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a partial point cloud of 200 points (z₀) as spatial prior and a ground-truth class label as input, then generates a complete 2048-point adversarial point cloud through diffusion-based shape completion. The output is constrained by an ℓ∞=0.16 norm per diffusion step and an ℓ0 budget limiting perturbations to only N=200 points per step, ensuring the generated shape remains visually coherent. The attack is untargeted: the adversarial example need only be misclassified by the target model, and 20 candidate views are generated per partial shape with only successful attacks retained.

## Method

The core mechanism repurposes a pre-trained 3D point-voxel diffusion model (PVD) for adversarial shape completion: starting from Gaussian noise, the reverse generative process is guided at selected timesteps by an I-FGSM-like adversarial gradient computed against an ensemble of substitute classifiers, without any retraining of the diffusion model. Three transferability strategies are layered on top: (1) model uncertainty via Monte Carlo estimation over M=5 randomly down-sampled point clouds (Bernoulli 0.5 sampling of non-partial points), (2) ensemble adversarial guidance that averages logits across PointNet, DGCNN, and PRC with adaptive weights, and (3) saliency-based critical point selection that restricts the adversarial gradient update to only the top-N points with highest summed xyz-channel saliency scores, preserving generation quality. The adversarial guidance is applied only during the early denoising steps (t ≤ 0.2T) when the shape is still disorganized, and a Clip function enforces the ℓ∞ constraint at each step.

## Results

On the full ShapeNet test set, 3DAdvDiff_ens achieves a 90.1% average attack success rate (ASR) across seven black-box 3D point cloud classifiers, compared to 36.6% for the white-box 3DAdvDiff and 6.2% for the best existing black-box baseline (PF-Attack). Against defenses, 3DAdvDiff_ens maintains 96.1% ASR under Adversarial Hybrid Training and 49.1% under SOR, substantially outperforming all baselines (which drop below 15% under most defenses). The generation quality of 3DAdvDiff_ens is competitive, with the lowest MSE (1.18) and Chamfer Distance (0.14×10⁻²) among all methods under the same ℓ∞=0.16 constraint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DAdvDiff_ens (90.1% avg ASR) overwhelmingly outperforms all six baselines on the full ShapeNet test set. The strongest existing black-box attack, PF-Attack, reaches only 6.2% average ASR — a gap of over 84 percentage points. White-box perturbation-based attacks (PGD 0.9%, KNN 1.1%, GeoA3 1.2%, SI-Adv 1.4%) and the generative black-box attack AdvPC (0.5%) are essentially ineffective in transfer, with ASRs below 2% on nearly all non-PointNet targets. The white-box 3DAdvDiff (36.6%) already surpasses all baselines by a wide margin, and adding ensemble adversarial guidance plus model uncertainty boosts it to 90.1% without increasing perturbation magnitude. Against defenses (Table 2), 3DAdvDiff_ens achieves 96.1% ASR under Hybrid Training and 49.1% under SOR, while all baselines fall below 15% under the same defenses, confirming the robustness advantage of the diffusion-based generation approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes pre-trained 3D diffusion models (specifically PVD) for adversarial generation, extending the 2D adversarial diffusion paradigm (AdvDiff, AdvDiffuser) to the 3D point cloud domain. It departs fundamentally from the perturbation-based lineage of 3D attacks (PGD, GeoA3, SI-Adv, PF-Attack) by generating adversarial point clouds from partial shapes rather than modifying existing ones, thereby avoiding the unrealistic coordinate shifts that plague prior methods. The transferability strategies draw on model uncertainty for black-box attacks (Li et al., ICLR 2022) and ensemble gradient methods, but adapt them to 3D via Monte Carlo estimation over randomly down-sampled point clouds—a technique enabled by the permutation invariance of point cloud classifiers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that existing 3D black-box attacks are essentially ineffective (1–6% average ASR) against modern architectures like PCT, PRC, and CurveNet, while the proposed diffusion-based approach achieves 85–90%, creating a new and much more demanding baseline for 3D adversarial robustness evaluation. The finding that long-tailed training distributions (ShapeNet) make underrepresented classes disproportionately vulnerable highlights a broader data-quality concern for 3D model deployment. Key limitations include the high per-example generation cost (~61 s), validation primarily on ShapeNet rather than the more standard ModelNet40, and residual vulnerability to reconstruction-based defenses like IF-Defense. Open questions include whether the generation-vs-perturbation paradigm extends to other 3D tasks (segmentation, detection) and whether faster samplers (DDIM) can close the efficiency gap without sacrificing ASR.
