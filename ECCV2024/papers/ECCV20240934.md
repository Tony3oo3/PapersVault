## Problem

Camouflaged object detection requires pixel-wise annotations that take approximately 60 minutes per image, making large-scale dataset construction prohibitively expensive. Existing weakly-supervised alternatives using scribbles still suffer from quality inconsistency and boundary ambiguity. This paper asks whether a single point click per object (~2 seconds) can provide sufficient supervision for accurate COD.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240934/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB image and a single point annotation per camouflaged object (clicking the most visually discriminative region) as training input, producing a pixel-wise binary segmentation mask at inference. The authors construct the P-COD dataset by relabeling 4,040 images from COD10K and CAMO with point annotations, where annotators simply click one point per object based on first impression without localizing boundaries. The model is also evaluated on standard SOD benchmarks (ECSSD, DUT-O, HKU-IS, DUTS-TE) to demonstrate cross-task transferability.

## Method

The framework uses a PVT backbone and three novel modules. (1) A hint area generator adaptively expands each point into a small circular region by warm-starting the encoder for a few epochs, estimating pseudo object area from the prediction map, and computing a radius scaled by a shrinkage factor α to avoid boundary overflow. (2) An attention regulator randomly shuffles and masks the labeled discriminative region during training, forcing the network to recover the full object from surrounding context rather than fixating on the most distinctive part. (3) A representation optimizer applies SimSiam-style unsupervised contrastive learning (L1 loss with stop-grad on one branch) over differently augmented image pairs (color jitter, Gaussian blur, scale, crop, flip, translate) to learn invariant features that disentangle foreground from background. The total loss combines the contrastive term with a partial cross-entropy computed only over the labeled hint regions, keeping the training signal minimal yet effective.

## Results

On the three COD benchmarks, the point-supervised method achieves MAE of 0.074/0.042/0.051 and Fβw of 0.727/0.650/0.748 on CAMO/COD10K/NC4K respectively. Compared to the strongest scribble-supervised baseline CRNet, it improves MAE by 17.6% on average and Fβw by 11.7% across all three datasets. The method also outperforms the unsupervised SAM by 12.9% MAE on CAMO and 34.6% MAE on NC4K, while using only a single point annotation per object (≈2 seconds per image vs. 60 minutes for pixel masks).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary weakly-supervised competitor CRNet (scribble-supervised), the proposed method wins on every metric across all three datasets, with the largest gap on COD10K Fβw (0.650 vs. 0.576, +12.8%) and CAMO MAE (0.074 vs. 0.092, −19.6%). Versus the unsupervised SAM, the method dominates on CAMO (MAE 0.074 vs. 0.132) and NC4K (MAE 0.051 vs. 0.078), though SAM edges ahead on COD10K Fβw (0.701 vs. 0.650). Among fully-supervised methods, the point-supervised approach beats PFNet on CAMO across all four metrics (e.g., MAE 0.074 vs. 0.085) and MGL-R on CAMO (MAE 0.074 vs. 0.088), but loses to the top fully-supervised models ZoomNet and UJSC on most metrics, particularly COD10K (ZoomNet MAE 0.029 vs. 0.042). The paper also reports that when the same framework is applied with scribble labels, it surpasses the fully-supervised ZoomNet on CAMO (MAE 0.065 vs. 0.066, Fβw 0.761 vs. 0.752), demonstrating transferability. On SOD benchmarks (Table 12), the method is competitive with the point-supervised PSOD, winning on DUT-O Fβmax (0.824 vs. 0.808) and DUTS-TE MAE (0.042 vs. 0.045) while trailing slightly on ECSSD MAE (0.045 vs. 0.036).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weakly-supervised camouflaged object detection line initiated by CRNet (scribble supervision) to a fundamentally simpler annotation paradigm: a single point click per object. The contrastive learning component directly adapts SimSiam's stop-grad positive-sample-only mechanism to the COD setting, while the attention regulator draws on the random-masking idea from HaS. The backbone (PVT) and the overall encoder-decoder structure follow standard dense-prediction designs, so the novelty is concentrated in the supervision strategy (hint area generator, attention regulator, representation optimizer) rather than in architectural innovation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30× reduction in annotation time (60 min → 2 s per image) makes large-scale COD dataset construction practically feasible, and the P-COD dataset is the first point-annotated COD benchmark. The finding that supervision *quality* (small, accurate hint regions) matters more than *quantity* (large pseudo-label regions from prediction maps or SAM) challenges the common assumption in weakly-supervised segmentation that more labeled area is always better. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-object scenes with many overlapping camouflaged objects, whether the single-point assumption holds for extremely small or highly fragmented targets, and whether the contrastive learning component transfers to other weakly-supervised dense-prediction tasks beyond COD and SOD.
