## Problem

Existing co-salient object detection methods rely on heuristically computed raw inter-image associations (e.g., pixel-wise or region-wise similarity) to guide image feature optimization, which is unreliable in complex scenarios with significant object variation or high foreground-background similarity. Moreover, optimizing image features under association guidance is an implicit and indirect approach to inter-image association modeling, increasing learning difficulty. This paper asks whether deploying deep networks directly on raw associations to produce learned deep association features can yield more explicit and robust inter-image association modeling.

## Input / Output

The method takes a group of N=6 related RGB images at 256×256 resolution and produces a per-image co-salient object segmentation mask. Internally, it constructs 6D hyperassociation tensors (N×H×W×N×H×W×L) capturing all pixel-pair-wise associations across the entire image group at three pyramid scales, which are then condensed to N×H×W×N×K×K×L (K=9) via correspondence-guided selection. The model also outputs pixel-level semantic correspondence estimates between images as a learned byproduct of the condensation process.

## Method

CONDA introduces a deep association learning strategy built on a VGG-16 + FPN backbone. The Progressive Association Generation (PAG) module computes 6D hyperassociations at three pyramid stages via l2-normalized inner products, then applies multi-scale aggregation networks (2D convolutions on both source and target dimensions with downsampling) to squeeze the 4D per-pixel association tensors into C-dimensional deep association features; higher-stage association features are upsampled and added to lower-stage VGG features to progressively improve hyperassociation quality. The Correspondence-induced Association Condensation (CAC) module replaces full-pixel associations with a K×K window centered on a learned semantic correspondence pixel (initially found by argmax similarity, refined by predicted spatial offsets), reducing MACs from 91.38G to 77.19G while eliminating noisy associations. An Object-aware Cycle Consistency (OCC) loss provides self-supervision for correspondence estimation by warping images forward and backward and computing SSIM only on ground-truth-masked co-salient regions, avoiding background interference.

## Results

CONDA achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three benchmark datasets (CoCA, CoSal2015, CoSOD3k) under four training settings. On the most challenging CoCA dataset with the DC+CS training set, CONDA reaches Sm=0.763, Eξ=0.839, Fβ=0.685, and M=0.089, surpassing the second-best method (GCoNet+) by 2.5% in Sm, 2.5% in Eξ, and 4.8% in Fβ. On CoSal2015 with DC+C9, CONDA achieves the best results with Sm=0.904, Eξ=0.940, Fβ=0.912, and M=0.042. The method consistently outperforms all baselines by meaningful margins across every dataset and training configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CONDA outperforms all eight compared baselines (GICD, GCoNet, GCoNet+, ICNet, DCFM, CADC, DMT, UGEM) on every metric across all three benchmark datasets and all four training settings. The most significant margins appear on the challenging CoCA dataset: with DC+CS training, CONDA surpasses GCoNet+ by 2.5% in Sm (0.763 vs 0.738), 2.5% in Eξ (0.839 vs 0.814), and 4.8% in Fβ (0.685 vs 0.637). On CoSal2015 with DC+C9, CONDA leads DMT (the strongest competitor) by 0.7% in Sm (0.904 vs 0.897) and 0.7% in Fβ (0.912 vs 0.905). On CoSOD3k with DC+CS, CONDA achieves the best Fβ of 0.853, exceeding UGEM (0.829) by 2.4%. The consistent superiority across all settings indicates that the deep association learning strategy generalizes well regardless of training data scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant CoSOD paradigm where raw inter-image associations (computed via pixel-wise, region-wise, or image-wise similarity) are used to optimize image features in an implicit manner. Instead, it repurposes the cost-volume / hyperassociation concept from stereo matching and few-shot segmentation, but applies deep aggregation networks directly on the associations to produce 'deep association features'—an explicit modeling strategy never explored in prior CoSOD work. The Progressive Association Generation module draws on the FPN philosophy of cross-scale feature enhancement but applies it to association features rather than backbone features, while the Correspondence-induced Association Condensation module introduces semantic correspondence estimation as a pretext task, a concept borrowed from the visual correspondence literature but novel to CoSOD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The deep association learning paradigm offers a more principled and explicit framework for inter-image modeling that could generalize to other group-based tasks beyond CoSOD. The introduction of pixel-level semantic correspondence as a pretext task opens a new research direction for understanding the fine-grained structure of co-salient objects. However, the method still relies on a VGG-16 backbone and heuristic initialization for correspondence estimation, and the correspondence quality remains limited by the unsupervised OCC loss. Open questions include whether the deep association learning strategy transfers to other group-based segmentation tasks (co-segmentation, video saliency) and whether more accurate correspondence estimation (e.g., with explicit supervision) would yield further gains.
