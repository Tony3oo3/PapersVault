## Problem

Self-supervised video object segmentation has been limited by reliance on auxiliary modalities (optical flow, depth) or slot attention with a fixed number of learnable queries, both of which restrict generalizability to in-the-wild multi-object scenes. This paper asks whether the object-level structural dependencies already present in DINO-pretrained Transformer attention maps can be extended to the spatio-temporal domain to produce coherent multi-object segmentations without any external signals or predefined object counts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241197/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes T=3 RGB frames sampled at temporal stride 4, resized to 192×384, and processes them through a frozen DINO ViT-S/8 backbone followed by a single learnable spatio-temporal Transformer block. At inference, it outputs temporally coherent multi-object segmentation masks via hierarchical clustering on the learned spatio-temporal attention maps, requiring only a single distance-threshold hyperparameter (set to 1.0) with no need to specify the number of objects. The architecture is flexible to arbitrary video lengths at test time, and no mask annotations are used during either training or inference.

## Method

The core contribution is a single spatio-temporal Transformer block (1.6M parameters) that ingests frame-wise DINO features concatenated along the temporal axis with learnable positional embeddings, producing spatio-temporal attention maps A_v ∈ R^{THW×THW} that encode structural dependencies across both space and time. Training is fully self-supervised: positive and negative patch pairs are sampled from the attention maps themselves (top-k highest/lowest attention scores per frame), and a dual margin loss enforces semantic consistency (cosine distance on fused features) and dynamic motion consistency (symmetric KL divergence on attention maps), weighted by inverse-entropy importance scores that prioritize informative regions. At inference, symmetric KL divergence between attention map rows serves as a pairwise distance metric for hierarchical clustering, which adaptively determines the number of object clusters and yields temporally coherent masks without any association step. The key insight is that attention maps preserve fine-grained structural correspondences more effectively than feature vectors, and extending them to the spatio-temporal domain captures object permanence across frames.

## Results

BA achieves state-of-the-art FG-ARI of 44.3 on YouTube-VIS-19 and 40.1 on DAVIS-17-Unsupervised, surpassing the previous best (TimeT) by 6.4 and 4.6 points respectively. On the synthetic MOVi-E benchmark, BA reaches 83.4 FG-ARI and 40.2 mIoU, outperforming the prior best SOLV by 2.6 FG-ARI points. The method also reports J&F of 43.9 on DAVIS-17, a 3.9-point gain over TimeT (40.0). Using DINOv2 ViT-S/14 as the frozen encoder (BA†) further improves DAVIS-17 FG-ARI to 41.6.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BA outperforms all seven baselines across every reported metric. Against the closest competitor TimeT [52], BA gains +6.4 FG-ARI on YTVIS-19 (44.3 vs 37.9) and +4.6 FG-ARI on DAVIS-17 (40.1 vs 35.5), while also improving mIoU by 9.7 points on YTVIS-19 (50.1 vs 40.4). Compared to the slot-attention-based methods SMTC [48] and SOLV [1], BA achieves substantially larger margins: +12.9 FG-ARI over SMTC and +15.2 FG-ARI over SOLV on YTVIS-19, and +6.8 / +7.9 FG-ARI over them respectively on DAVIS-17. On the synthetic MOVi-E benchmark, BA leads SOLV [1] by 2.6 FG-ARI (83.4 vs 80.8) and VideoSAUR [74] by 9.5 FG-ARI (83.4 vs 73.9). The optical-flow-based methods SAVi [29] and OCLR [65] trail by the largest margins (33.2 and 28.4 FG-ARI on YTVIS-19), confirming that relying on auxiliary motion signals is less effective than the proposed attention-based spatio-temporal correspondence for multi-object discovery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DINO's discovery of emerging objectness in self-supervised ViT attention maps, but departs from prior self-supervised VOS methods in two key ways. Whereas methods like SOLV, SMTC, and VideoSAUR rely on slot attention (requiring a predefined number of object slots) and methods like CIS, OCLR, and GWM depend on auxiliary modalities such as optical flow or depth, BA uses only a single spatio-temporal Transformer block on frozen DINO features plus parameter-free hierarchical clustering. Crucially, it shifts the clustering signal from DINO feature vectors (as in TimeT and SMTC) to spatio-temporal attention maps, arguing that attention encodes fine-grained structural correspondences more suitable for object grouping than high-level semantic features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single 1.6M-parameter block with no auxiliary inputs and no predefined object count achieves state-of-the-art multi-object segmentation, making the approach highly deployable in real-world settings where object counts are unknown and auxiliary sensors are unavailable. The finding that attention maps outperform feature vectors for clustering (by >10 points) offers a reusable insight for other object-discovery tasks. Open questions include scalability to long videos (quadratic memory cost of the spatio-temporal attention), the absence of multi-scale features limiting fine-grained boundaries, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the tested datasets to more diverse or longer-duration video content.
