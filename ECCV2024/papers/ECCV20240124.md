## Problem

Existing video MAE masking strategies rely on visual priors such as motion vectors or optical flow, whose effectiveness depends on input videos matching underlying statistical assumptions (e.g., foreground moves more than background). Natural language captions, which implicitly capture both objects and actions without modality-specific assumptions, have not been explored as a saliency signal for video MAE. Additionally, the potential synergy between the generative objective of MAE and the discriminative objective of video-text contrastive learning remains unexplored for video.

## Input / Output

The method takes 16-frame video clips at 224×224 resolution (split into 2×16×16 spatiotemporal patches) paired with text captions generated offline by BLIP-2 from 3 uniformly sampled keyframes per video. During pretraining, the model outputs reconstructed pixel patches (MSE target) and a mean-pooled video embedding for contrastive alignment with the text embedding. For downstream evaluation, the pretrained encoder produces action class predictions via finetuning or linear probe, and zero-shot retrieval rankings via cosine similarity in the CLIP-aligned space.

## Method

The core contribution is Text-Guided Masking (TGM), which computes per-patch visual features using a frozen ViT-B/32 and a text embedding from CLIP's [EOS] token, then masks the top-k patches by cosine similarity to the caption—requiring no explicit motion or segmentation cues. On top of this, the paper introduces a unified framework that optionally adds an InfoNCE video-text contrastive loss computed on the masked encoder's mean-pooled output, aligning visible-patch representations with the caption within the same mini-batch. The key insight is that TGM is competitive with motion-guided masking (MGM) without any visual saliency prior, and that the contrastive loss synergistically boosts linear probe performance by 4–12% across all masking algorithms (random, motion-guided, and text-guided). Training uses a ViT-Base asymmetric encoder-decoder from scratch on ~200K videos with a mask ratio of 0.6, and both BLIP and CLIP remain frozen throughout.

## Results

On Kinetics-400, TGM achieves 79.9% top-1 finetune accuracy (tied with MGM) and 33.8% linear probe accuracy (vs. 32.1% for MGM and 38.1% for tube masking). On Something-Something V2, TGM reaches 67.1% finetune and 26.2% linear probe, competitive with MGM (67.3% FT, 33.0% LP) and tube masking (66.6% FT, 25.7% LP). When combined with masked video-text contrastive learning, TGM improves to 67.5% FT and 33.4% LP on SSv2, and achieves up to 97.6% linear probe on UCF101 and 20.1% on Epic-Kitchens 55. The optimal mask ratio for TGM is 0.6, notably lower than the 0.75–0.9 used by other video MAE methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against tube (random) masking, TGM wins on SSv2 finetune (67.1% vs. 66.6%) and K400 finetune (79.9% vs. 78.4%), but trails on K400 linear probe (33.8% vs. 38.1%). Against motion-guided masking (MGM), TGM ties on K400 finetune (79.9% vs. 79.9%) and wins on K400 linear probe (33.8% vs. 32.1%), but loses on SSv2 finetune (67.1% vs. 67.3%) and SSv2 linear probe (26.2% vs. 33.0%). The key insight is that TGM achieves competitive performance with MGM without using any explicit motion cues, relying solely on CLIP-based text-video correspondence. When the masked video-text contrastive loss is added, TGM+V→T reaches 67.5% FT on SSv2 (beating MGM+V→T at 67.0%) and 37.2% FT on Epic-Kitchens 55 (beating MGM+V→T at 36.9%), while MGM retains an edge on SSv2 linear probe (37.1% vs. 33.4%). Overall, TGM is competitive across all six datasets and all evaluation protocols, with the contrastive loss providing the largest gains on linear probe (up to +22.7% on UCF101).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VideoMAE/ST-MAE line of masked video autoencoders by replacing visual saliency priors (random tube masking, motion vectors, optical flow) with a text-guided masking strategy that leverages CLIP's aligned vision-language space to identify salient regions. It also departs from prior contrastive video pretraining (CVRL, ρ-MoCo, LSTCL) by introducing a masked video-text contrastive loss that operates on the MAE encoder's visible-patch output, unifying the generative reconstruction objective with a discriminative alignment objective in a single backbone. The approach is positioned against both motion-guided masking (MGM, MGMAE) and prior vision-language pretraining (CLIP, FLIP, InternVideo), but uniquely combines text-guided masking with joint MAE-contrastive optimization without cross-attention layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that noisy, machine-generated captions (BLIP) can serve as a viable saliency signal for video masking without any explicit visual priors, and that MAE and video-text contrastive learning are synergistic rather than antagonistic (contradicting FLIP's image-domain finding). This opens a research direction for language-guided masked video modeling that could benefit from improving video captioning quality. Key limitations include dependence on strong frozen vision-language models (CLIP, BLIP) and the use of frame-wise image captions that miss temporal dynamics; the paper leaves open the theoretical question of why generative and discriminative objectives complement each other in this setting.
