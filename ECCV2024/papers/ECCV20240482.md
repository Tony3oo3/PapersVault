## Problem

Existing text-to-3D generation methods either depend on scarce human-annotated text-3D pairs (confining them to specific object classes), rely on per-sample diffusion-based optimization (requiring tens of minutes of inference), or use only global sentence-level CLIP embeddings (losing fine-grained textual details). This paper addresses how to achieve fast, fine-grained text-guided 3D textured mesh generation without human annotations and without expensive per-sample optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt as input, which is encoded by a pre-trained CLIP text encoder into a 512-dimensional sentence feature and 77×512 word-level features. During training, pseudo captions are generated by InstructBLIP from rendered 2D images of 3D shapes (1024×1024, 24–100 views per object), eliminating the need for human-annotated text-3D pairs. The output is a 3D textured mesh extracted via DMTet from summed geometry and texture triplane features at multiple resolutions.

## Method

TPA3D builds on GET3D's GAN-based triplane generator, adding a two-level text conditioning scheme: a sentence-level triplane generator (StyleGAN2-style modulated convolutions conditioned on CLIP sentence features) produces coarse geometry and texture triplanes, which are then refined by the proposed TriPlane Attention (TPA) block. Each TPA block performs three sequential attention operations: plane-wise self-attention for intra-plane consistency, cross-plane attention (treating the fused triplane as key/value and per-plane features as queries) for 3D spatial connectivity, and cross-word attention (using CLIP word features as key/value) to inject fine-grained textual details. The texture TPA additionally receives geometry triplane features (weighted at α=0.5) to maintain texture-geometry alignment. Training uses text-conditioned discriminators (RGB and mask) with a mismatching objective on negative text pairs and a CLIP similarity loss, all in an unsupervised adversarial setting requiring only rendered images of 3D shapes.

## Results

On ShapeNet, TPA3D achieves FID scores of 18.50 (Car), 38.11 (Chair), and 77.69 (Motorbike), approaching the unconditional upper bound of GET3D (11.50, 22.75, 49.98) while substantially outperforming TAPS3D (26.37, 44.70, 84.83). On the more diverse OmniObject3D dataset, TPA3D achieves FID of 68.80 (Vehicle) and 83.31 (Accessory), beating both GET3D (98.15, 145.66) and TAPS3D (152.34, 172.14). For text alignment, TPA3D achieves CLIP R-Precision@5 of 80.94 on Car and 65.26 on Vehicle, dramatically surpassing TAPS3D (12.55 and 9.47 respectively). Inference takes 0.09 s for rendering and 2.87 s for mesh extraction on a Tesla V100, compared to 90 min for DreamFusion and 40 min for Magic3D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TPA3D outperforms TAPS3D [52] on every metric across all five categories: FID is lower (e.g., 18.50 vs. 26.37 on Car; 68.80 vs. 152.34 on Vehicle) and CLIP R-Precision@5 is dramatically higher (e.g., 80.94 vs. 12.55 on Car; 64.44 vs. 6.67 on Accessory), confirming that word-level triplane refinement captures fine-grained text details that sentence-level-only guidance misses. Against the unconditional GET3D [15] upper bound, TPA3D is slightly worse on ShapeNet FID (18.50 vs. 11.50 on Car) but substantially better on the multi-class OmniObject3D dataset (68.80 vs. 98.15 on Vehicle), showing that text conditioning helps when class diversity is high. Compared to SDS-based methods DreamFusion [37] and Magic3D [29], TPA3D achieves far faster inference (0.09 s rendering vs. 90 min and 40 min) and, per qualitative comparisons in Figure 6, better color–part correspondence on complex prompts (e.g., correctly separating 'black office chair' from 'blue seat'). TITG3SG [30] is included only as a runtime reference (2.21 s for voxel output) and is not directly compared on quality metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

TPA3D builds directly on GET3D's triplane-based GAN architecture for high-fidelity 3D textured mesh generation, extending it from unconditional to text-conditioned generation. It departs from TAPS3D, which relies solely on CLIP sentence-level embeddings as text guidance, by introducing a TriPlane Attention (TPA) block that performs word-level refinement via cross-word attention on geometry and texture triplanes. The method also contrasts with SDS-based approaches (DreamFusion, Magic3D) that achieve fine-grained text alignment through per-sample optimization of NeRFs, instead achieving comparable alignment through a single forward pass of a GAN. Pseudo-caption generation via InstructBLIP follows the strategy of TAPS3D to eliminate the need for human-annotated text-3D pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TPA3D demonstrates that word-level text attention on triplane features can substantially improve text-shape alignment (CLIP R-Precision@5) while retaining GAN-level inference speed (~3 s for mesh), bridging the long-standing efficiency gap between diffusion/SDS-based and GAN-based text-to-3D methods. However, the approach remains limited to categories seen during training, depends on the quality of InstructBLIP pseudo-captions, and has not been evaluated on truly open-vocabulary or multi-class generalization settings. Open questions include whether the TPA mechanism transfers to other 3D representations (NeRF, Gaussian splatting), how pseudo-caption noise propagates into generation quality, and whether the method scales to the full ShapeNet or larger real-world datasets without per-category training.
