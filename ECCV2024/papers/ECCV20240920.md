## Problem

Existing 360° scanpath prediction methods operate on 2D equirectangular projections, which introduce severe distortion and a coordinate discontinuity at the ±180° longitude seam, leading to large prediction errors. Additionally, prior sequential models (RNNs, Markov chains) compress all historical fixations into a single hidden state, only indirectly modeling the temporal dependencies that are critical to human visual attention.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 360° equirectangular RGB image (2:1 aspect ratio) as input, projects it onto a unit sphere, and extracts features at 128×256 resolution. The output is an autoregressively generated sequence of 3D Cartesian fixation points (x, y, z ∈ [−1, 1]), with scanpath length varying by dataset (30 for Sitzmann, 25 for Salient360!, ~8 for AOI, 15 for JUFE). Ten scanpaths are sampled per image for evaluation, with each fixation drawn probabilistically from a 32,768-point latitude-longitude grid rather than taking the mode of the distribution.

## Method

Pathformer3D operates entirely in 3D spherical coordinates to avoid equirectangular distortion. A SphereNet backbone extracts multi-scale 3D features (C=448, H=128, W=256), which are average-pooled and flattened into 512 tokens, then contextualized by a 4-layer Transformer Encoder (D=128, 8 heads) with 3D positional encoding. A 4-layer Transformer Decoder generates fixation embeddings autoregressively: each step's query is initialized from the previous fixation (origin for the first), concatenated with all prior queries, and cross-attends to the encoder output, directly modeling dependencies between the current and all historical fixations via self-attention. A 3D Mixture Density Network (K=5 Gaussians) decodes each fixation embedding into mean vectors, full covariance matrices, and mixture weights, from which the fixation is sampled. Training minimizes negative log-likelihood over the ground-truth fixation positions, and the probabilistic sampling strategy (rather than argmax) preserves inter-subject variability in predicted scanpaths.

## Results

Pathformer3D achieves state-of-the-art scanpath prediction on all four 360° datasets (Sitzmann, Salient360!, AOI, JUFE), outperforming all baselines on the majority of six evaluation metrics. On Salient360!, it attains LEV 36.277, DTW 1515.883, and ScanMatch 0.484, approaching the Human baseline (LEV 35.090, DTW 1376.439, ScanMatch 0.513). On JUFE, it reaches LEV 22.595, DTW 1054.223, and ScanMatch 0.462, again closing the gap to the Human baseline (LEV 18.445, DTW 1030.970, ScanMatch 0.564). The model generates ~4 scanpaths of length 30 per second on a single RTX 3090, with a model size of 19.28 MB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pathformer3D consistently outperforms all three 360°-specific baselines (SaltiNet, ScanGAN, ScanDMM) on LEV, DTW, and ScanMatch across all four datasets. Against ScanDMM (the strongest baseline), Pathformer3D reduces DTW by ~11% on Salient360! (1515.9 vs 1706.7) and ~2.5% on JUFE (1054.2 vs 1081.5), while improving ScanMatch from 0.467 to 0.484 and 0.452 to 0.462 respectively. The only metrics where Pathformer3D trails ScanDMM are Sequence Score (SS) on AOI (0.225 vs 0.249) and JUFE (0.218 vs 0.221), and TDE on Sitzmann (19.887 vs 19.098). SaltiNet, the earliest 360° method, is substantially outperformed on every metric and dataset, with DTW gaps exceeding 200 units on Sitzmann and Salient360!. The gap between Pathformer3D and the Human baseline narrows considerably compared to prior methods, particularly on Salient360! where DTW is within ~10% of the human-to-human average.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of 360° scanpath prediction (SaltiNet, PathGAN, ScanGAN, ScanDMM) by shifting the prediction space from 2D equirectangular projection to 3D spherical coordinates, motivated by the distortion and longitude discontinuity inherent in equirectangular representations. Architecturally, it repurposes the Transformer encoder-decoder paradigm—previously applied to 2D scanpath prediction and object detection—for autoregressive fixation generation, with self-attention explicitly framed as modeling visual working memory. The 3D feature extraction relies on the existing SphereNet, and the probabilistic fixation output borrows the Mixture Density Network formulation from earlier probabilistic gaze models, but the combination of 3D spherical coordinates, Transformer-based temporal modeling, and 3D Gaussian sampling is novel in this domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By performing scanpath prediction natively in 3D spherical space, this work removes a systematic source of error (equirectangular distortion and the ±180° longitude seam) that has affected all prior 360° scanpath models, offering a more physically grounded formulation for VR/AR applications. The consistent but moderate improvements across four datasets and six metrics validate the coordinate-system argument, while the ablation confirming autoregressive over parallel decoding provides practical guidance for sequence generation in this setting. Open questions remain: whether the 3D advantage persists with stronger 3D backbones, how to incorporate semantic information to guide attention, and whether the autoregressive latency (~266 ms per scanpath) is acceptable for real-time foveated rendering pipelines.
