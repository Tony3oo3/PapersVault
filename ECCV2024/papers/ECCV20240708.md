## Problem

Open-World Instance Segmentation (OWIS) aims to segment arbitrary unknown objects by generalizing from a limited set of annotated classes, but state-of-the-art methods suffer from low precision because their pseudo annotations cover background areas. The paper addresses this by investigating how to focus the foundation model SAM on objects specifically, rather than segmenting both objects and stuff regions, through the use of learned object priors for prompt generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240708/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input and produces class-agnostic instance segmentation masks as output. The pipeline operates in two phases: an offline pseudo-annotation generation phase where DINO self-attention maps are computed per image to create 50 object-focused point prompts for SAM (ViT-H), and an online inference phase where only a trained class-agnostic Mask R-CNN (ResNet-50+FPN) is used. During training, up to 10 high-quality pseudo annotations per image are merged with the original annotations of known classes, though on average only 7.8 survive filtering across the COCO training set.

## Method

SOS consists of three blocks: an Object Localization Module that aggregates six self-attention maps from a DINO-pretrained ViT-S via elementwise max, rescales to a probability mass function, and iteratively samples 50 point coordinates with local pruning (N=20) to diversify across objects; a Pseudo Annotation Creator that prompts SAM with these points (allowing 3 segments per prompt), filters by confidence (τ=0.9) and NMS (IoU=0.95), suppresses pseudo annotations overlapping original annotations, and caps at 10 per image; and a standard class-agnostic Mask R-CNN trained on the merged annotations. The key insight is that DINO self-attention maps focus on discriminative object parts (e.g., faces), producing pseudo annotations that are substantially more precise than alternatives like saliency, superpixels, or contour density, yielding up to 81.6% precision improvement over prior OWIS methods.

## Results

On the primary cross-category COCO (VOC) → COCO (non-VOC) setting, SOS achieves AP=8.9, AR₁₀₀=39.3, and F₁=14.5, outperforming the second-best method GGN (F₁=8.4) by 6.1 in F₁, driven by an 81.6% precision improvement. On cross-dataset evaluations, SOS leads on COCO→LVIS (F₁=13.3), COCO→ADE20k (F₁=17.0), and COCO→UVO (F₁=28.0), with the largest margin over GGN being 3.7 on ADE20k. SOS also substantially outperforms vanilla SAM (F₁=6.7) on the cross-category setting, demonstrating that object-focused prompting is critical for instance segmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOS clearly outperforms all seven OWIS baselines (OLN, LDET, GGN, SWORD, UDOS, SOIS, OpenInst) across all three metrics on the cross-category COCO (VOC)→COCO (non-VOC) setting, with the largest gap over GGN (F₁ 14.5 vs. 8.4). The precision gain is the dominant factor: SOS's DINO-prompted SAM pseudo annotations avoid the background noise that plagues GGN and other methods. On cross-dataset COCO→LVIS and COCO→ADE20k, SOS again leads all methods in F₁, beating GGN by 2.8 and 3.7 respectively. On COCO→UVO, SOS (F₁=28.0) slightly trails LDET (F₁=28.5) in AP and F₁, which the authors attribute to UVO containing classes absent from ImageNet (the DINO pre-training set). Compared to the Mask R-CNN baseline, SOS improves F₁ by 12.7 on cross-category and by 1.5 on UVO, confirming that pseudo annotations are essential for open-world generalization. SOS also beats vanilla SAM (grid-prompted) by 7.8 in F₁ on cross-category, showing that object-focused prompting is necessary to avoid segmenting stuff regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOS follows the pseudo-annotation stream of OWIS methods (GGN, UDOS, LDET) that augment known-class annotations with segments for unknown objects, but departs fundamentally in how those pseudo annotations are generated. Rather than learning pixel affinities or grouping proposals, SOS leverages the foundation model SAM with object-focused point prompts derived from DINO self-attention maps, a combination not previously explored. The systematic comparison of hand-crafted (saliency, contour density, superpixels) and learned (CAM, U-Net, DINO) object priors for prompting SAM is a new empirical contribution that extends beyond OWIS to any task requiring class-agnostic, object-focused segmentation from a prompt-based foundation model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 81.6% precision gain over GGN demonstrates that the quality of pseudo annotations, not just their quantity, is the critical bottleneck in OWIS, and that foundation models can address this bottleneck when properly guided by object priors. The finding that DINO self-attention maps outperform purpose-built localization approaches (U-Net, CAM) for prompting SAM has broader implications for any application requiring object-focused segmentation from SAM. A key limitation is that DINO's ImageNet pre-training causes SOS to miss object classes absent from ImageNet (evident in the UVO results), suggesting that more diverse pre-training data or multi-prompt strategies could further improve coverage. Open questions include whether the object-prior prompting strategy transfers to video segmentation or to other foundation models beyond SAM.
