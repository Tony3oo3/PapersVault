## Problem

Whether pre-training on a geometric task (monocular depth estimation) can meaningfully benefit a downstream semantic task (semantic segmentation) remains unresolved, with prior evidence being mixed. This question is both practically important for reducing reliance on human-annotated pre-training data and scientifically relevant to understanding whether geometric priors can prime semantic understanding without human intervention.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240987/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (typically 256×256 crops or full-resolution frames from KITTI, Cityscapes, NYU-V2) and produces either a per-pixel depth map (during pre-training) or a per-pixel semantic label map (during fine-tuning). The depth pre-training stage additionally requires video sequences, stereo pairs, or LiDAR scans as supervision, while the segmentation fine-tuning stage requires only a small set of pixel-annotated images (as few as 16 in few-shot experiments). The same encoder-decoder architecture serves both tasks: the depth decoder's final layer is simply swapped for a fully connected segmentation head.

## Method

The paper pre-trains a network (Monodepth2 with ResNet18/50, or DPT/Depth Anything with ViT-L) on monocular depth estimation using various supervision signals (video reprojection error, stereo disparity, or LiDAR), then replaces the depth prediction head with a segmentation head and fine-tunes on a small labeled dataset. The key architectural insight is that depth maps are piecewise-smooth functions and segmentation maps are piecewise-constant functions over the same image domain, making the decoder naturally compatible between the two tasks. The authors formalize the comparison via an Information Bottleneck criterion, asking whether the depth-induced representation h minimizes H(y|h) + βI(h;x) relative to other pre-training representations. They demonstrate that depth pre-training outperforms ImageNet classification, optical flow, masked autoencoding, and contrastive learning (DINO, MoCo v2) across multiple datasets, architectures, and supervision forms, and that the learned features align with semantic boundaries as revealed by Grad-CAM visualizations.

## Results

On KITTI (few-shot, 16 training images, ResNet50, fine-tune all), monocular depth pre-training achieves 50.92 mIoU and 77.34 pixel accuracy, outperforming ImageNet classification pre-training (44.65 mIoU, 73.06 P.Acc) by 6.27 mIoU. On Cityscapes (full-scale, DeepLabV3), depth pre-training reaches 73.17 mIoU on validation vs. 70.41 for ImageNet. For out-of-domain transfer, Depth Anything (trained on 63.5M images) achieves 59.7 mIoU on ADE20k, 87.7 on PascalVOC, and 84.8 on CityScapes (fine-tuning), consistently surpassing DINO v2 and MAE baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Depth pre-training (50.92 mIoU) outperforms MAE reconstruction (47.18) by 3.74 mIoU and DINO contrastive (44.19) by 6.73 mIoU on KITTI, and substantially beats MOCoV2 (37.04) by 13.88 mIoU. The only method that edges out depth is supervised segmentation pre-training on MS-COCO (51.28 mIoU), a margin of just 0.36 mIoU, while depth achieves notably higher pixel accuracy (77.34 vs. 74.88). For large-scale out-of-domain transfer (Table 6), Depth Anything surpasses DINO v2 on all three datasets (ADE20k: 59.7 vs. 58.1; PascalVOC: 87.7 vs. 86.5; CityScapes: 84.8 vs. 82.7 mIoU) and MAE on ADE20k (59.7 vs. 53.6). Optical flow, despite optimizing the same photometric reprojection error as depth, is worse than random initialization (38.47 vs. 41.35 mIoU), confirming that the geometric structure of depth is critical rather than mere pixel-level reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of self-supervised depth estimation (building on Monodepth2, DPT, and Depth Anything) and semantic segmentation pre-training, testing a hypothesis that prior work has only partially addressed. It departs from the Taskonomy finding that depth is 'far' from segmentation by providing a systematic empirical protocol grounded in an Information Bottleneck formalization, and contrasts with the common practice of ImageNet classification pre-training by arguing that geometric supervision avoids the object-centric framing bias inherent in human-curated datasets. The optical flow comparison is a novel ablation not present in prior depth-to-segmentation work, isolating why the specific structure of the depth hypothesis space (rigid 3D scene) matters beyond merely minimizing photometric error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: monocular depth pre-training offers a viable, annotation-free alternative to ImageNet for segmentation, with the added benefit of in-domain data collection for specialized domains. The finding that optical flow—despite optimizing the same photometric reprojection loss—performs worse than random initialization challenges the assumption that any reconstruction-based pre-training yields transferable representations, pointing to the importance of the hypothesis space structure (rigid 3D geometry vs. arbitrary displacement fields). Open questions include whether the advantage persists for tasks with less spatial structure than segmentation, whether uncalibrated video can fully replace sensor-based depth supervision at scale, and the overfitting phenomenon observed with cropped-patch depth pre-training on Cityscapes.
