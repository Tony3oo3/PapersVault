## Problem

Existing image description evaluation metrics (BLEU, CIDEr, CLIPScore, etc.) were designed for single-sentence captioning and produce only a single overall matching score, making them inadequate for the growing diversity of description tasks—paragraphs, dense captioning, controllable captioning—that require fine-grained, interpretable assessment at multiple granularities. These metrics cannot localize errors within a description or adapt to varying text lengths and scopes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241667/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts an RGB image and a text description of arbitrary length (from a single caption to a multi-sentence paragraph) and produces a hierarchical parsing graph for each modality, from which fidelity, adequacy, and fluency scores are computed at every level of the hierarchy. The final output is a weighted total score, but intermediate node-level scores are also available, enabling error localization within the description. Human attention weights (saliency maps, reference frequency, or task-specific region masks) modulate the adequacy calculation depending on the evaluation scenario.

## Method

HiFi-Score models both the image and the text as Hierarchical Parsing Graphs (HPGs) organized by inclusion relationships, where the root represents the whole and child nodes represent progressively finer semantic regions or phrases, with attributes and semantic relationships as additional nodes and edges. The image HPG is constructed by combining SAM and OneFormer segmentations sorted by area, while the text HPG is parsed by GPT-3.5 into objects, attributes, and relationships. Text phrases are grounded to image regions via GLIP localization followed by SAM refinement, and local phrase-region matching scores are computed using a Visual Genome–fine-tuned BLIP-2 (weighted ITC + ITM). Fidelity and adequacy are then aggregated recursively from leaf nodes to the root along the graph tree, with adequacy weighted by human attention. Fluency is assessed separately by GPT-3.5 across grammatical correctness, clarity, and logical rationality.

## Results

On caption-level benchmarks, HiFi-Score (reference-free) achieves the best Kendall's τc on Flickr8k-Expert (58.4) and Composite (65.8), outperforming the strongest baselines InfoMetIC (54.2 / 59.2) and BLIPScore (52.5 / 61.6). On Pascal-50S, HiFi-Score scores 83.0 mean accuracy, slightly below InfoMetIC (86.5) and UMIC (85.1), while matching BLIPScore on THumB1.0 (0.45 total). At the paragraph level on ParaEval, HiFi-Score achieves the highest classification accuracy across all four negative types (95.4% plausible, 93.2% object, 87.7% attribute, 63.8% relation), surpassing BLIPScore by up to 16.6 points on object errors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiFi-Score clearly outperforms all reference-free baselines on Flickr8k-Expert (58.4 vs. 54.2 for InfoMetIC, 52.5 for BLIPScore) and Composite (65.8 vs. 61.6 for BLIPScore, 59.2 for InfoMetIC), demonstrating the benefit of hierarchical fine-grained matching over global cosine-similarity scoring. On Pascal-50S, HiFi-Score (83.0) trails InfoMetIC (86.5) and UMIC (85.1), which benefit from contrastive training specifically designed for pairwise caption selection; the paper attributes this to HiFi-Score's lack of targeted training for that task. On THumB1.0, HiFi-Score ties BLIPScore at 0.45 total, with the paper noting that shorter, less detailed model-generated captions in that dataset limit the advantage of fine-grained analysis. At the paragraph level (ParaEval), HiFi-Score dominates all baselines across every error type, with the largest margin over BLIPScore on object errors (93.2% vs. 76.6%) and relation errors (63.8% vs. 58.1%), confirming that structured graph matching is critical for detecting subtle semantic errors in long descriptions. Reference-based metrics (BLEU, CIDEr, SPICE, BERTScore) are consistently outperformed, underscoring the advantage of image-grounded evaluation over n-gram or embedding overlap with limited human references.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of image-based caption evaluation metrics (CLIPScore, UMIC, InfoMetIC) by replacing their flat, single-score matching with a hierarchical parsing graph (HPG) that organizes both image regions and text phrases into a tree structure. Conceptually, it builds on SPICE's scene-graph decomposition and FAIEr's fidelity/adequacy/fluency tripartite evaluation, but generalizes both: SPICE's flat scene graph becomes a multi-granularity hierarchy, and FAIEr's cross-modal scene graph matching is replaced by a recursive bottom-up scoring over the HPG. The pipeline repurposes several strong pre-trained components (SAM, OneFormer, GLIP, BLIP-2, GPT-3.5) as modular sub-routines rather than training a new end-to-end model, positioning itself as a structured orchestration of existing capabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As vision-language models generate increasingly diverse descriptions (paragraphs, dense captions, controllable captions), the field lacks a single evaluation framework that handles varying lengths, scopes, and granularity requirements while remaining interpretable. HiFi-Score's hierarchical structure provides per-node diagnostics that can localize errors in long texts, and the ParaEval dataset fills a notable gap in paragraph-level evaluation benchmarks. However, the approach's reliance on a chain of five+ pre-trained models introduces compounding error risk and OOD sensitivity, and the weakest dimension (relationship discrimination at ~64%) suggests that capturing inter-object semantics remains an open challenge for both the metric and the underlying VLMs it depends on.
