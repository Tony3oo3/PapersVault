## Problem

Existing 3D style transfer methods primarily transfer colors and textures from a style source but fail to faithfully replicate the geometry of the style scene onto the content scene. This paper reframes 3D stylization from a generative process driven by latent-space feature losses to an explicit distribution-matching problem between two Gaussian splatting representations, enabling high-fidelity geometric transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240553/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires two pre-trained 3D Gaussian splatting scenes (content and style) as input, with the style scene additionally regularized with anisotropic constraints to enforce near-spherical Gaussians and prevent needle-shaped artifacts. The content scene is first sampled to surface points to avoid interior Gaussians. The output is a stylized 3D Gaussian splatting scene (1M–4M Gaussians) that locally resembles the style geometry while globally preserving the content shape, optimized in approximately 8 minutes on a single A100 GPU (16 GB VRAM).

## Method

WaSt-3D partitions the content scene into ~400 clusters via K-Means on colored Gaussians, then for each content cluster solves a constrained rigid+scale alignment (translation, rotation, anisotropic scaling) to identify the best-fitting region of the style scene. The core optimization minimizes the Sinkhorn divergence (a debiased entropy-regularized Wasserstein-2 distance) between each content cluster and its assigned style cluster, operating jointly on Gaussian coordinates and luminance to align both geometry and shading. The entropy regularization parameter γ controls transport smoothness, and the Sinkhorn divergence is computed via the geomloss library. An anisotropic regularization loss applied during style-scene pretraining forces individual Gaussians toward spherical shapes, preventing artifacts when scenes are partitioned into subsets.

## Results

WaSt-3D achieves 84.40% CLIP high-level detail similarity, substantially outperforming the best baseline SNeRF (76.99%) by over 7 points. In the user study, WaSt-3D receives 75.5% human preference votes compared to 12.5% for ARF, 10.5% for SNeRF, and 1.5% for Style-RF. The method also runs fastest at 8 minutes per scene (vs. 11–30 min for baselines), at the cost of higher VRAM usage (16 GB vs. 6–9 GB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WaSt-3D decisively outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against ARF [54], it gains +9.6 points in CLIP similarity (84.40% vs. 74.79%) and +63 points in human preference (75.5% vs. 12.5%), while also being 3 minutes faster. Against Style-RF [29], the CLIP gap is +9.5 points and the human preference gap is a striking +74 points (75.5% vs. 1.5%), with WaSt-3D also running 10 minutes faster. Against SNeRF [33], the closest competitor, WaSt-3D still leads by +7.4 points in CLIP similarity and +65 points in human preference, while cutting runtime from 30 to 8 minutes. The only metric where WaSt-3D is worse is VRAM (16 GB vs. 6–9 GB), which the authors attribute to handling up to 8 million Gaussians in the style scene. Qualitatively, the paper highlights that only WaSt-3D faithfully preserves the 3D geometry of the style scene (e.g., grass texture, pebble shapes), whereas the baselines primarily transfer color and texture without altering geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in 3D style transfer (ARF, StyleRF, SNeRF), which operates by matching latent-space feature statistics (e.g., Gram matrices of VGG activations) between rendered views of the content scene and a 2D style image. Instead, WaSt-3D reframes stylization as an explicit optimal-transport problem: it directly matches the spatial distributions of 3D Gaussians between a content scene and a full 3D style scene using entropy-regularized Wasserstein-2 distance (Sinkhorn divergence). The method builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as the scene representation and on the Sinkhorn iteration framework from computational optimal transport, while introducing a K-Means-based scene partitioning strategy to make the transport problem tractable at scale. The assemblage-inspired decomposition—replacing each content cluster with a best-fitting style cluster—draws a conceptual parallel to classical art techniques but has no direct analogue in prior 3D stylization literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WaSt-3D is the first method to faithfully transfer 3D geometry (not just color and texture) from a style scene to a content scene, as evidenced by normal-map comparisons showing that prior methods introduce noise while WaSt-3D preserves the style's surface structure. A key limitation is the requirement for a fully reconstructed 3D style scene as input, which restricts practical applicability compared to methods that accept a single 2D style image or text prompt. Open questions include whether the approach can be extended to work with 2D style references (e.g., via depth estimation), how it scales to very large or unbounded scenes, and whether the fixed K-Means partitioning can be replaced by adaptive, content-aware segmentation. The training-free, optimization-based nature makes it easy to adopt but also means quality depends heavily on the quality of the pretrained Gaussian splattings.
