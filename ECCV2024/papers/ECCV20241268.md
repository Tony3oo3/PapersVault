## Problem

Existing world models for autonomous driving are predominantly built in gaming or simulated environments, lacking representation of real-world driving scenarios with their complex traffic constraints and long-tail conditions. This limits their utility for generating realistic driving data or predicting safe driving policies in actual road environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241268/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single reference driving frame (448×256), its corresponding 3-channel HDMap (lane boundary, divider, pedestrian crossing) projected onto the image plane, 8-corner 3D bounding boxes with category labels (zero-padded to a fixed max count), sequential ego-car actions (yaw angle and velocity), and a free-text prompt for scene style (weather, time of day). It produces a 16-frame future driving video at 12 Hz (~1.3 s) and 16 future driving action steps, with optional multi-view (6-camera) generation where temporal attention is restricted to neighboring views.

## Method

DriveDreamer builds on a frozen Stable Diffusion v1.4 backbone (Auto-DM) augmented with three condition pathways: spatially aligned HDMap features are concatenated directly with noisy latents, 3D box positions are encoded via CLIP category embeddings plus Fourier position embeddings and injected through GLIGEN-style gated self-attention, and text prompts enter via cross-attention; temporal attention layers (reshape → frame-wise self-attention → reshape) enforce inter-frame coherence. Training follows a two-stage pipeline: Stage 1 first trains single-frame image generation without temporal attention to learn structural traffic constraints, then extends to multi-frame video with temporal attention; Stage 2 introduces an ActionFormer that encodes initial HDMap/3D-box conditions into a latent hidden state, iteratively updates it via GRU conditioned on driving actions through cross-attention, and decodes future structural conditions at the feature level (avoiding pixel-level noise) to condition Auto-DM for joint video and action prediction. The joint video+action prediction is framed as a variational inference problem with a simplified ELBO (omitting posterior-prior matching), optimized with MSE for video and L1 for actions, while future driving actions are decoded from pooled multi-scale UNet features concatenated with historical action embeddings through MLP layers.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, DriveDreamer (front-view) achieves a FID of 14.71 and FVD of 110.23, outperforming the best image-generation baseline MagicDrive (FID 16.20) and the video-generation baseline DriveGAN (FID 27.83, FVD 390.82). The multi-view variant attains FID 15.22 and FVD 129.89. For driving action prediction, DriveDreamer achieves an average L2 trajectory error of 0.29 m and a collision rate of 0.15%, matching AD-MLP on L2 while reducing the collision rate by 21% relative to the AD-MLP baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DriveDreamer outperforms all three image-generation baselines (BEVGen, BEVControl, MagicDrive) on FID, with the largest margin over BEVGen (14.71 vs. 25.54) and BEVControl (14.71 vs. 24.58), and a 1.49-point improvement over MagicDrive (14.71 vs. 16.20). Against the only video-generation baseline DriveGAN, DriveDreamer achieves a substantially lower FVD (110.23 vs. 390.82) and FID (14.71 vs. 27.83), while also supporting structured traffic conditions that DriveGAN does not. For action prediction, DriveDreamer matches AD-MLP on L2 error (0.29 m) but reduces the collision rate from 0.19% to 0.15%, and clearly surpasses ST-P3 (2.11 m) and UniAD (1.65 m) on L2. DriveDreamer slightly trails VAD on collision rate (0.15% vs. 0.14%) but achieves a lower L2 error (0.29 m vs. 0.37 m), making it the best overall on the combined planning metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

DriveDreamer builds directly on Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) as its generative backbone and adapts GLIGEN's (Li et al., CVPR 2023) gated self-attention mechanism to inject 3D box position conditions into the diffusion process. It departs from prior driving world models (ISO-Dream, MILE, SEM2) that operated in simulation or BEV segmentation spaces by grounding the world model in real-world driving videos with explicit structured traffic conditions (HDMap, 3D boxes). The ActionFormer component extends the video prediction paradigm of DriveGAN (Kim et al., CVPR 2021) by predicting future structural conditions at the feature level via GRU-based recurrent updates rather than directly regressing pixel-level futures, and the two-stage curriculum (structure comprehension → temporal prediction) is a novel training strategy not seen in prior driving video generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first world model for autonomous driving trained entirely on real-world driving data, bridging a gap where prior work was confined to simulation or gaming environments. The demonstrated utility of generated videos for augmenting 3D detection and lane detection training suggests a practical data-engineering pipeline for long-tail scenario coverage. However, several open questions remain: predictions are limited to 16 frames (~1.3 s at 12 Hz), action evaluation is open-loop only, and the approach relies on high-quality HDMap and 3D box annotations that may not be available in all deployment settings. Scaling to longer horizons, closed-loop evaluation, and reducing dependence on dense structured annotations are natural next steps.
