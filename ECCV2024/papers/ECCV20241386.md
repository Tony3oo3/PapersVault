## Problem

Existing lightweight semantic segmentation models, constrained by limited feature representation capacity, struggle to accurately model foreground object boundaries and distinguish between categories, leading to boundary errors and misclassification. The paper addresses how to achieve state-of-the-art segmentation accuracy while keeping computational cost (FLOPs) and parameter count low enough for efficient deployment.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images resized to 1024×512 (ADE20K, COCO-Stuff) or 480×480 (Pascal Context) and outputs a per-pixel semantic segmentation mask with class-specific labels. The encoder is a stepwise-downsampling EfficientFormerV2 backbone producing four feature maps at H/4, H/8, H/16, and H/32 resolutions; the largest-scale feature (H/4) is dropped by the decoder to reduce computation, and the remaining three are average-pooled to H/64×W/64 before pyramid context extraction.

## Method

CGRSeg introduces a Rectangular Self-Calibration Module (RCM) that captures axial global context via horizontal and vertical pooling, adds the two resulting axis vectors to explicitly model a rectangular region of interest, and then applies a shape self-calibration function (decoupled large-kernel strip convolutions of size 1×11 and 11×1) to deform that rectangle toward foreground objects. RCM is used in two roles: pyramid context extraction (stacked RCMs over downsampled multi-scale features) and spatial feature reconstruction in the decoder (fusing encoder low-level features with decoder high-level features). A Dynamic Prototype Guided (DPG) head projects features into a class space, computes a dynamic prototype via matrix multiplication, compresses it to a C×1 class embedding vector, and uses broadcast Hadamard product to weight pixel features, enhancing inter-class discrimination at negligible extra FLOPs. The full pipeline achieves 43.6% mIoU on ADE20K with only 4.0 GFLOPs, outperforming SeaFormer and SegNeXt by 0.9% and 2.5% mIoU respectively while using ~38% fewer FLOPs.

## Results

On ADE20K, CGRSeg-T achieves 43.6% mIoU with only 4.0 GFLOPs and 138.4 Img/s throughput, outperforming SeaFormer-L (42.7%, 6.5 GFLOPs) by +0.9% mIoU with 38% fewer FLOPs, and SegNeXt-T (41.1%, 6.6 GFLOPs) by +2.5% mIoU. The base model CGRSeg-B reaches 45.5% mIoU at 7.6 GFLOPs, exceeding PEM-STDC2 (45.0%, 19.3 GFLOPs) by +0.5% mIoU at only 39% of the computation. The large model CGRSeg-L achieves 48.3% mIoU at 14.9 GFLOPs, surpassing Mask2Former (47.7%, 74.0 GFLOPs) by +0.6% mIoU with roughly 1/5 the FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGRSeg-T dominates the lightweight tier on ADE20K, beating SeaFormer-L by +0.9% mIoU (43.6 vs. 42.7) while using 2.5 GFLOPs fewer and 4.6M fewer parameters, and outperforming SegNeXt-T by +2.5% mIoU (43.6 vs. 41.1) at 39% lower FLOPs. In the mid-range tier, CGRSeg-B (45.5%, 7.6 GFLOPs) surpasses PEM-STDC2 (45.0%, 19.3 GFLOPs) by +0.5% mIoU at only 39% of the computation, and exceeds SegNeXt-S (44.3%, 15.9 GFLOPs) by +1.2% mIoU at half the FLOPs. In the large-model tier, CGRSeg-L (48.3%, 14.9 GFLOPs) outperforms Mask2Former (47.7%, 74.0 GFLOPs) by +0.6% mIoU at roughly 1/5 the FLOPs, and beats FeedFormer-B2 (48.0%, 42.7 GFLOPs) by +0.3% mIoU at 35% of the computation. On COCO-Stuff, CGRSeg-T leads SegNeXt-T by +3.5% mIoU (42.2 vs. 38.7), and CGRSeg-L edges out SegNeXt-B by +0.2% mIoU (46.0 vs. 45.8) with 57% fewer FLOPs. On Pascal Context, CGRSeg-L achieves the best mIoU of 58.5% at 14.9 GFLOPs, surpassing the next-best SegNeXt-S (56.1%, 15.9 GFLOPs) by +2.4% mIoU with fewer FLOPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

CGRSeg carves out a new position at the intersection of axial attention (Coordinate Attention, GatherExcite), the MetaNeXt block from ConvNeXt, and prototype-based classification (ProtoSeg), but does not directly extend any single prior architecture. Its core departure is the Rectangular Self-Calibration Module, which repurposes horizontal/vertical pooling to model a rectangular key region via broadcast addition and then calibrates that region's shape with learnable large-kernel strip convolutions—a mechanism absent from prior axial-attention designs. The Dynamic Prototype Guided head adapts the prototype-classification idea from ProtoSeg into a lightweight segmentation head, replacing the heavy OCRHead-style decoders used in prior efficient segmentation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a clear efficiency-accuracy Pareto improvement: 4.0 GFLOPs with 43.6% mIoU on ADE20K beats the prior best (SeaFormer-L at 6.5 GFLOPs, 42.7%) by both accuracy and compute, a gap that holds across COCO-Stuff and Pascal Context and across three model scales. The RCM is a drop-in module that outperforms CoordAtt and GatherExcite by ~2% mIoU at identical FLOPs, making it a potentially reusable building block for other efficient architectures. Open questions include whether the rectangular-attention concept generalizes beyond segmentation (e.g., detection, tracking), whether the strip-convolution calibration is truly necessary versus simpler alternatives, and whether the gains persist when the backbone is swapped to non-EfficientFormerV2 architectures at scale.
