## Problem

Vision-Language Large Models suffer from high computational costs (throughput, latency, memory) that impede real-world deployment, yet existing acceleration methods focus exclusively on the model perspective (pruning, quantization, distillation) and overlook data-side token redundancy. These model-centric approaches are architecture-specific, require re-training, and involve complex engineering, leaving a gap for a general, training-free acceleration that targets redundancy at the data source.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241236/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Turbo operates internally on n-token sequences (image patches and/or text tokens) within each attention block of a VLM, reducing them to (n−γ) tokens for understanding tasks or merging-then-restoring to n tokens for generation tasks. For Stable Diffusion experiments, inputs are 512×512 images with ImageNet-1k text prompts; for understanding, inputs are standard image-text pairs on benchmarks like COCO, Flickr30k, and NLVR2. The method is applied per-layer with a constant drop ratio γ per batch, making it compatible with any attention-based VLM regardless of modality or task.

## Method

Turbo defines an information degree E for each token by fusing two components: mutual redundancy R (maximum cosine similarity to other tokens, capturing inter-token duplication) and semantic value A (the token's attention weight from the cls token, capturing contribution to overall semantics). For understanding tasks, Turbo sorts tokens by E and merges the top-γ most redundant tokens into the rest, reducing sequence length from n to n−γ per layer. For generation tasks (e.g., Stable Diffusion), Turbo merges tokens before each UNet block and restores them via a weighted sum referencing the recorded information degree after the block, preserving pixel-level fidelity. The entire module is training-free, parameter-free, and orthogonal to model-perspective accelerations like GPTQ quantization or UPop pruning, enabling superposition for further speedup.

## Results

On multi-modal understanding tasks, Turbo achieves ~1.9–2.0× throughput improvement with minimal accuracy loss: 82.2 vs. 83.3 on NLVR2 (1.91×), 76.7 vs. 77.5 on VQAv2 (1.57×), and CIDEr 130.0 vs. 133.3 on COCO captioning with BLIP (1.98×). On BLIP2 captioning, Turbo reaches CIDEr 142.2 vs. 145.7 at 1.72× throughput. For text-to-image generation with Stable Diffusion, Turbo at γ=30 achieves FID 32.77 (vs. 32.12 baseline) at 1.56× throughput. On ImageNet-1k classification, Turbo matches or slightly exceeds ToMe's Top-1 accuracy at identical acceleration rates (e.g., 84.5/83.1 vs. 84.5/82.6 on ViT-B/16 at 1.62×).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UPop (model pruning), Turbo consistently wins on both accuracy and throughput: on NLVR2, Turbo achieves 82.2 test accuracy at 1.91× throughput versus UPop's 81.1 at 1.18×; on COCO captioning (BLIP), Turbo scores CIDEr 130.0 at 1.98× versus UPop's 128.9 at 1.65×; on Flickr30k retrieval (BLIP), Turbo retains I2T R@1 of 96.8 at 375.0 img/s while UPop drops to 92.5 at 322.2 img/s. Against GPTQ (4-bit quantization), Turbo is decisively superior: GPTQ on BLIP2 captioning yields only 1.02× throughput (essentially no speedup) while Turbo achieves 1.72×; on MiniGPTv2 VQA, GPTQ actually *slows down* inference to 0.87× whereas Turbo accelerates to 1.74×. Against ToMe (token merging for ViT), Turbo matches or slightly exceeds Top-1 accuracy at the same acceleration rate across all model sizes on ImageNet-1k (e.g., 84.5/83.1 vs. 84.5/82.6 on ViT-B/16 at 1.62×), confirming that the added semantic-value component provides a consistent but modest edge over pure redundancy-based merging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant model-perspective acceleration paradigm (pruning, quantization, distillation) by targeting data-side redundancy instead, framing token merging as an information-theoretic problem. Conceptually, it extends ToMe's mutual-redundancy-only token merging by introducing a semantic-value component derived from cls-token attention weights, and generalizes the setting from uni-modal image classification to multi-modal VLMs spanning both understanding (BLIP, BLIP2, MiniGPTv2) and generation (Stable Diffusion). The information-degree formulation (weighted difference or coupled division of redundancy and semantics) is the paper's core methodological contribution, positioned as orthogonal to and stackable with model-perspective techniques like GPTQ and UPop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: a training-free, plug-and-play module that delivers ~2× throughput for understanding and ~1.6× for generation with minimal quality loss, and can be layered on top of existing quantization/pruning for compounding gains. This lowers the barrier to efficient VLM deployment significantly, since no re-training or architecture-specific engineering is needed. Open questions include how the approach scales to much longer sequences (video, high-resolution generation), whether the semantic-value signal remains reliable in very deep or differently-architected models, and whether the merge-restore mechanism for generation can be made more aggressive without quality degradation. The paper's scope is limited to a handful of specific VLM architectures, leaving generalization to newer or differently-structured models untested.
