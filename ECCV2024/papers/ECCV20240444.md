## Problem

Large Vision-Language Models process hundreds of visual tokens per image, inflating computational cost and consuming limited token budgets. Existing token pruning approaches either require end-to-end training (making them incompatible with the standard practice of freezing the visual encoder) or operate only within the ViT, ignoring the textual instruction that determines which visual details actually matter for the response.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240444/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 336×336 RGB image (yielding 576 patch tokens via ViT-L/14) and a free-form text instruction, and produces a standard text response. A key design detail is that the textual pseudo CLS token used to guide LLM-stage pruning is computed on-the-fly via a frozen CLIP text encoder and is never fed into the LLM as an actual token; it exists solely to score visual token relevance during the attention computation. The method requires no retraining and can be applied in pure inference mode.

## Method

IVTP performs two-stage visual token pruning. In the first stage (ViT), a Group-wise Token Pruning (GTP) module aggregates attention weights across contiguous layer groups via attention rollout (recursive multiplication of (A+I) matrices), then prunes patches with the lowest CLS-row importance scores at each group boundary. In the second stage (first 12 LLM layers), a frozen CLIP text encoder computes per-token visual relevance scores for the instruction; tokens exceeding a threshold τ=0.2 are average-pooled into a textual pseudo CLS token, which is inserted between visual and text tokens during attention computation to re-score and prune instruction-irrelevant visual patches. The residual (attention-rollout) aggregation outperforms mean, max, or element-wise multiplication of layer attentions, and the CLIP-based relevance filtering outperforms directly pooling all LLM text tokens, which introduces noise due to the absence of contrastive image-text alignment in the LLM tokenizer.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B, IVTP reduces visual tokens from 576 to 64 (88.9% reduction) while achieving an average accuracy of 62.0 across 12 benchmarks, a drop of only 1.0% from the full-token baseline (63.0), with TFLOPs reduced from 15.4 to 8.2 (−46.8%). On LLaVA-1.5-13B, the average accuracy is 65.2 (−0.8% from baseline 66.0) with TFLOPs reduced from 29.4 to 15.6 (−46.9%). In pure inference mode (no retraining), IVTP retains 59.6% average accuracy at 64 tokens, surpassing the next-best method (ToMe at 57.5%) by over 2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IVTP outperforms all five compared baselines by a consistent margin of 2.0–2.5% in average accuracy on LLaVA-1.5-7B (62.0 vs. 59.5–60.0), and by 1.9–2.6% on LLaVA-1.5-13B (65.2 vs. 62.6–63.3). Against the strongest single-stage vision-only method ToMe [4] (60.0 / 63.3), IVTP gains +2.0 / +1.9 points, demonstrating that instruction-guided pruning in the LLM stage recovers information that purely visual similarity-based merging cannot. EViT [24] (59.9 / 62.6) and Qwen-VL [3] (59.9 / 63.0) trail IVTP by +2.1 / +2.6 and +2.1 / +2.2 respectively. Honeybee [5] (59.5 / 63.0) and LLaMA-ViD [22] (59.7 / 62.9) show the largest gaps at +2.5 / +2.2 and +2.3 / +2.3, indicating that their architecture-specific compression modules are less effective when re-implemented in the LLaVA-1.5 framework. The TFLOPs of IVTP (8.2) are marginally higher than single-stage methods (8.0–8.1) due to the two-stage pruning spanning both ViT and LLM, but the accuracy advantage of over 2% justifies this small overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends vision-only token pruning (DynamicViT, ToMe, EViT) to the LVLM setting, where the visual encoder is typically frozen and the LLM must follow diverse textual instructions. The core technical departure is the Group-wise Token Pruning (GTP) module, which repurposes attention rollout (Abnar & Zuidema, 2020) to aggregate attention weights across grouped ViT layers, making importance scoring robust without retraining the frozen encoder. The second-stage extension into the LLM introduces a CLIP-derived pseudo CLS token to inject instruction semantics into the pruning decision, departing from prior LVLM compression approaches (Honeybee, LLaMA-ViD, Qwen-VL) that rely on trainable projection modules tightly coupled to a specific architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, architecture-agnostic nature of IVTP makes it immediately deployable on existing LVLMs without retraining, and the 88.9% token reduction with ~1% accuracy drop and 46%+ compute savings is practically significant for latency-sensitive applications. However, the method is validated only on LLaVA-1.5 with a single image per input; generalization to multi-image, video, or higher-resolution settings remains open. The reliance on a frozen CLIP text encoder for instruction-visual relevance introduces a small but non-trivial overhead and couples the method to CLIP's tokenization, which may limit applicability to LVLMs using different visual encoders or text tokenizers.
