## Problem

Learned keypoint detectors produce internal scores that are not interpretable or comparable across methods, making it impossible to assess the quality of individual keypoints in a detector-agnostic way. No existing framework simultaneously refines keypoint positions and assigns interpretable, cross-method-comparable scores that capture both repeatability and localization accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242051/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a single RGB image and any keypoint detector (hand-crafted or learned) as input, extracting 2048 keypoints per image by default. It outputs sub-pixel refined keypoint positions (Gaussian means) plus two per-keypoint scores: robustness (an integer from 1 to 21, counting how many of the 21 warped-image detections fall within 3σ of the cluster) and deviation (6σ in pixels, encoding localization uncertainty). The method is fully detector-agnostic and uses identical hyperparameters regardless of the underlying detector.

## Method

The pipeline applies 21 affine warpings (isotropic and anisotropic scaling, shear) to the input image, runs the keypoint detector on each warped version, and reprojects all detections back into the original frame. A kernel density estimate identifies local maxima that initialize a Gaussian Mixture Model fit, which groups reprojected keypoints into clusters. The key technical contribution is a modified EM algorithm with two-stage outlier handling: an adaptive Gaussian decay weight (w1) during fitting down-weights distant points, followed by a hard 3σ truncation (w2) for final localization, plus a concentric-component merging rule to prevent weight dilution. Refined positions are the Gaussian means, while robustness and deviation are read directly from the component weight (count within 3σ) and variance (6σ), yielding interpretable scores without any learned component.

## Results

On the Image Matching Challenge (IMC) stereo benchmark, GMM-IKRS consistently improves all evaluated metrics across four deep detectors. SuperPoint sees the largest gain, with mAA@10° rising from 0.270 to 0.319 (+18%) on the validation set and inlier count increasing from 154 to 182. On HPatches v-set, the framework improves repeatability at 3px for all detectors (e.g., DISK from 0.464 to 0.499) and homography accuracy AUC, confirming gains beyond simple sub-pixel refinement. On IMC multiview, SuperPoint's 3D point count increases from 1332 to 1467 and mAA@10° from 0.568 to 0.601 on the validation set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMM-IKRS is applied as a post-processing refinement on top of six existing detectors, and it improves every metric for every method tested. SuperPoint benefits the most, gaining over 10% in stereo mAA@10° (0.270→0.319 val) and 7% in multiview mAA, likely because its original keypoints are less precisely localized. DISK, the strongest baseline on IMC, still improves in inlier count (438→469 val) and mAA (0.707→0.722 val), confirming the refinement adds value even to already-competitive detectors. MD-Net shows consistent gains across all IMC metrics (e.g., 3D points 1108→1133, mAA 0.727→0.740 val multiview). R2D2 sees the smallest absolute improvements (e.g., inliers 117→120 val stereo), suggesting its keypoints are already relatively well-localized. On HPatches, Harris tricks the standard repeatability metric due to dense detections but falls behind on the proposed RepMNN metric; DoG (SIFT), despite being already sub-pixel-accurate, still gains in repeatability, confirming the improvement comes from robust keypoint selection rather than mere sub-pixel refinement.

## Relation to Prior Work

GMM-IKRS repurposes classical statistical tools—kernel density estimation and a modified robust EM algorithm for Gaussian Mixture Models—as a detector-agnostic post-processing layer, rather than proposing a new learned architecture. Conceptually, it shares the image-warping augmentation idea with ASIFT and SuperPoint's homographic adaption, but departs from both by operating directly on discrete keypoints (not descriptors or heatmaps) and by applying a global clustering step that yields interpretable per-keypoint scores. It also generalizes the stability-scoring idea of NeSS-ST from a single hand-crafted detector (Shi-Tomasi) to arbitrary detectors, while eliminating the need for a local patch size. The paper thus carves new ground not by introducing a new detection paradigm but by showing that a simple, non-learned statistical refinement can uniformly improve and characterize the output of any existing detector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two interpretable scores (robustness and deviation) enable a cross-method comparison of individual keypoint quality that was previously impossible, offering practitioners a principled way to select or rank keypoints for specific downstream tasks (e.g., high robustness for visual localization, low deviation for robotic applications). The consistent improvements across all tested detectors and benchmarks (HPatches, IMC stereo and multiview) validate the approach, and the statistical analysis reveals qualitatively different detector behaviors (e.g., DoG excels at localization but not robustness, SuperPoint finds the most robust keypoints). Limitations include the offline-only setting (21 fixed augmentations), the isotropic covariance assumption that simplifies interpretation but may miss anisotropic uncertainty, and the fixed augmentation set that may not capture all real-world viewpoint changes. The authors suggest a promising future direction of using refined sub-pixel keypoints as ground truth in a teacher-student training loop for deep detectors.
