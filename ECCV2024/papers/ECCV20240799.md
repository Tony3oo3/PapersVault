## Problem

Text-to-image models and multimodal large language models consistently fail to correctly represent and reason over spatial relationships (e.g., left/right, above/below, in front of/behind) specified in text prompts. Existing controllable generation methods either require additional training, depend on bounding-box annotations, or cannot guarantee spatial correctness, leaving a gap between the deterministic accuracy of 3D rendering tools and the photorealism of diffusion-based T2I models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240799/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a natural-language text prompt (e.g., 'an airplane above a chair') and parses it into object names and a spatial relationship. REVISION renders a spatially accurate reference image in Blender using 101 3D asset classes (rescaled to 1 m cubes, positioned within a [-1 m, 1 m] coordinate frame with the camera at x = 5 m), then feeds this reference image plus the original prompt into a Stable Diffusion or ControlNet model to produce the final photorealistic output. For the RevQA benchmark, the input is a REVISION-rendered image paired with one of 16 templated yes/no questions (including negations, conjunctions, disjunctions, and adversarial object substitutions), and the output is a binary answer from an MLLM.

## Method

REVISION is a Blender-based rendering pipeline with four components: an Asset Library of 410 royalty-free 3D models across 101 classes, a Coordinate Generator that deterministically maps 11 spatial relationships to 3D object and camera positions, a Scene Synthesizer that assembles the camera, light source, 360° panorama background, textured floor, and two objects, and a Position Diversifier that randomizes orientations, background rotations, and camera jitter for variety. The key mechanism is a training-free reformulation of text-to-image generation as image-to-image synthesis: the REVISION-rendered reference image is noised and denoised via SDEdit within Stable Diffusion, or used as a canny-edge condition through ControlNet, so the diffusion model inherits guaranteed spatial layout while contributing photorealistic texture. This eliminates the need for bounding-box annotations, additional training, or LLM-based layout prediction. The paper also introduces RevQA, a 50k-pair benchmark with 16 question types (simple/opposite spatial, AND/OR/NOT, double negatives, random and adversarial object substitutions, and combined multi-clause variants) to stress-test MLLM spatial reasoning under adversarial perturbations.

## Results

On the VISOR benchmark, ControlNet+REVISION achieves the best spatial accuracy with 55.48% VISOR_cond, 97.54% VISOR_uncond, and 31.59% VISOR_4, outperforming the strongest existing method (Control-GPT) by 11.31%, 31.57%, and 11.11% respectively. On T2I-CompBench, REVISION-guided Stable Diffusion models achieve consistent spatial score improvements across all background types. On the RevQA benchmark, the best MLLM (LLaVA 1.5) scores only 0.720 average across 16 question types, with a 58.17% performance drop on opposite spatial questions and an 85.88% drop on adversarial combined AND questions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REVISION-guided methods dominate on spatial accuracy: ControlNet+REVISION achieves 55.48% VISOR_cond vs. Control-GPT's 44.17% (+11.31%) and DALL-E v2's 37.89% (+17.59%), while also surpassing DALL-E v2 on VISOR_uncond (97.54% vs. 59.27%) and VISOR_4 (31.59% vs. 7.49%). The one metric where REVISION trails is Object Accuracy: DALL-E v2 scores 63.93% OA versus ControlNet+REVISION's 56.88%, suggesting the rendering guidance trades some object realism for spatial correctness. Against training-based methods like GLIGEN and Composable Diffusion (not directly tabulated but discussed in related work), REVISION achieves competitive spatial scores without any additional training. On the RevQA benchmark, all five MLLMs (LLaVA 1.5, Fuyu-8B, InstructBLIP, LLaMA-Adapter 2.1, Qwen-VL-Chat) perform below 0.75 average, with LLaVA 1.5 leading at 0.720 and Fuyu-8B trailing at 0.446; all models drop below 50% on opposite spatial and double-negative questions, and suffer 40–86% accuracy losses under adversarial perturbations compared to their simpler counterparts.

## Relation to Prior Work

REVISION departs from the dominant line of training-based controllable generation (GLIGEN, Control-GPT, Composable Diffusion) and layout-guidance methods (Layout Guidance, LayoutGPT) by replacing learned spatial conditioning with a deterministic 3D rendering oracle built in Blender. The image-to-image guidance mechanism is directly inherited from SDEdit's noise-then-denoise procedure applied within Stable Diffusion, while the ControlNet variant adds canny-edge conditioning to preserve spatial structure. On the evaluation side, RevQA extends the logic-based VQA tradition (VQA-LOL) and the CLEVR lineage of synthetic-scene reasoning into a spatial-specific, adversarially-perturbed benchmark for MLLMs, filling a gap left by general-purpose suites like MMBench and MME that do not probe spatial relationships at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a lightweight, training-free rendering pipeline can close most of the spatial-accuracy gap in T2I models without any fine-tuning, offering a cost-efficient path for applications requiring reliable object placement (robotics, layout design). The RevQA results are more broadly concerning: even the strongest MLLM (LLaVA 1.5) drops from 94% to 39% on opposite-spatial questions and suffers an 86% collapse under adversarial conjunctions, suggesting that current spatial reasoning in MLLMs is brittle and largely memorized rather than compositional. Key limitations include the restriction to two objects per scene, 101 asset classes, and 11 relationships; the depth-relationship extension yields only modest gains (~5–7% on VISOR_cond), hinting that 2D rendering guidance may not transfer cleanly to occlusion-aware depth reasoning. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-object scenes, whether learned spatial priors can complement the rendering oracle, and whether the MLLM fragility revealed by RevQA can be addressed through targeted training data.
