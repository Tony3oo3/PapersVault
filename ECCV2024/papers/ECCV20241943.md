## Problem

Existing Data Availability Attacks (DAAs) excel against either Supervised Learning or Self-Supervised Learning but not both simultaneously. The most robust model-free DAA, CUDA, still permits ~70% clean test accuracy under SSL and suffers a severe trade-off between image quality and poisoning strength, making it impractical for real-world data sharing. Additionally, prior theoretical analysis of CUDA relied on unrealistic assumptions (Gaussian data, two-class, non-deep-learning) that limit design insights.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 64×64 for STL-10, 224×224 for ImageNet-100) with their class labels as input. For each class c, a κ×κ CUDA filter R_c is generated (one random entry set to 1, remaining entries drawn from U[0, p_u]), and a class-wise linear restoration model is fit on the filtered patches. The output is a poisoned image X_c * P_c where P_c = R_c * A_c is a (2κ+1)×(2κ+1) filter, producing visually high-quality images that are unlearnable by downstream models.

## Method

IRP builds on CUDA's class-specific convolutional filter R_c but adds an imperfect restoration step: after filtering class c images with R_c, a class-wise linear model α_c is fit via least squares to predict the center pixel of each κ×κ filtered patch from its surrounding values, yielding a restoration filter A_c. The final poison filter P_c = R_c * A_c is guaranteed (Theorem 2) to be imperfect, preserving the class-wise bias mechanism while maintaining high image quality. The theoretical analysis shows CUDA poisons by creating sub-optimal gradients and shifting cross-class information to incorrect spatial positions via peak shifts in R_{cπ} * R_i, while IRP retains this peak-shift mechanism but adds turbulent high-frequency patterns around the peaks, making the attack more complex and harder to remove. This design achieves state-of-the-art poisoning in both SL and SSL (e.g., 10.39% vs. 21.89% on CIFAR-10 SL, 43.24% vs. 66.58% on CIFAR-10 SSL) while producing images with significantly better perceptual quality than CUDA (LPIPS 0.142 vs. 0.272, SSIM 0.775 vs. 0.561).

## Results

On CIFAR-10, IRP achieves the lowest clean test accuracy across all training and defense scenarios: 10.39% (SL), 43.24% (SSL), 32.21% (AT), 10.85% (Cutout), 15.21% (CutMix), 16.03% (Mixup), and 29.42% (ISS), with a worst-case maximum of 43.24%. On ImageNet-100, IRP further dominates with 1.98% (SL), 9.30% (SSL), and 22.10% (AT), reducing clean accuracy to near-chance levels. Across four SSL algorithms (SimCLR, SimSiam, MoCoV3, BYOL) on ImageNet-100, IRP consistently suppresses accuracy to 7.82–11.6%, compared to clean accuracies of 70.68–75.54%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IRP outperforms all eight baselines on SSL, where the gap is most dramatic: 43.24% vs. 66.58% (CUDA), 73.08% (CP), 75.77% (AP), and 86–89% for EM, REM, LSP, OPS, and AR on CIFAR-10. On ImageNet-100, IRP achieves 9.30% SSL accuracy, beating CUDA (26.12%) and CP (18.76%) by wide margins. On SL, IRP (10.39% CIFAR-10, 1.98% ImageNet-100) matches or beats AR (10.95%) and AP (12.45%) on CIFAR-10 and dominates all baselines on ImageNet-100. IRP is the only method that maintains strong poisoning across all five defenses simultaneously; the closest competitor, CUDA, allows up to 48.58% under AT and 66.58% under SSL, whereas IRP caps at 43.24%. IRP concedes narrow losses to AP on Cutout (10.85% vs. 9.70%) and CutMix (15.21% vs. 8.38%), to OPS on AT (32.21% vs. 20.10%), and to CUDA on ISS (29.42% vs. 21.94%) on CIFAR-10, but reverses all of these on ImageNet-100 where IRP leads every defense.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CUDA (Sadasivan et al., 2023), which poisons images via class-specific random convolutional filters, by adding a learned 'imperfect restoration' step that partially inverts the filter to recover image quality while preserving the poisoning mechanism. The theoretical analysis departs from CUDA's original Gaussian-assumption analysis by working within a deep learning gradient framework, revealing that CUDA's effectiveness stems from peak-shift-induced class-wise bias rather than the sub-optimal gradient alone. In the broader DAA landscape, IRP positions itself as the first model-free method to simultaneously achieve strong poisoning under both supervised and self-supervised training, a gap that model-reliant methods (EM, AP, REM) and SSL-specific methods (CP) each fill only partially.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IRP's ability to maintain high image quality (LPIPS 0.142 vs CUDA's 0.272) while achieving substantially lower clean test accuracies across SL, SSL, and five defense mechanisms makes it the most practically deployable DAA for real-world data sharing scenarios where visual fidelity matters. A key limitation is that effectiveness drops sharply under partial poisoning (e.g., 80% poisoned data still yields ~86% accuracy on CIFAR-10), raising open questions about whether the class-wise bias mechanism can be made more robust to clean-data contamination. The theoretical characterization of peak-shift bias also opens the door to designing targeted defenses that specifically disrupt the spatial misalignment IRP exploits.
