## Problem

Training video generation models by fine-tuning entire T2I architectures requires massive in-house video datasets and diminishes the stylistic diversity of the original image model. The alternative of freezing the T2I model and training only temporal layers (as in AnimateDiff) is simpler but fails to converge reliably and struggles to produce diverse, temporally consistent videos. This paper asks: what additional inductive biases—architectural, loss-based, and sampling-based—are needed to make a fully frozen T2I model generate high-quality, diverse videos from a single public dataset?

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (up to 77 tokens) and produces 24-frame video clips at up to 512×512 resolution (4 seconds at 6 fps) in the Stable Diffusion VAE latent space. Optionally, skeleton or sketch conditions can be injected per-frame or at keyframes via ControlNet, and a reference image can guide generation via IP-Adapter, all without any additional training. The text prompt is further expanded by a learned frame-wise token generator that appends per-frame tokens to the original text embedding before cross-attention.

## Method

HARIVO freezes Stable Diffusion v1.5 entirely and trains only three lightweight components: a mapping network (temporal attention + 3D convolutions) that reshapes IID Gaussian noise into a video-correlated distribution before the U-Net, a frame-wise token generator that produces F×3×768 tokens from the text embedding to encode subtle inter-frame dynamics, and a projection head for contrastive learning. Training is supervised by four losses: standard denoising score matching, a temporal regularized self-attention loss that penalizes self-attention map differences between consecutive frames in the frozen decoder, a decoupled contrastive loss on the U-Net bottleneck (h-space) enforcing semantic consistency across all frames, and a regularization loss that keeps the mapping network's output close to the T2I model's native domain. At inference, a mitigating gradient sampling step adds a guidance term proportional to the gradient of inter-frame similarity, iteratively suppressing abrupt structural changes without affecting already-smooth sequences. The entire pipeline is single-stage, trained on WebVid-10M with only 0.45B trainable parameters.

## Results

On UCF101, HARIVO achieves an FVD of 787.87 and on MSR-VTT a CLIP Similarity of 0.2948, using only 0.45B trainable parameters in a single-stage training with a fully frozen StableDiffusion backbone. This represents a 2.5× FVD improvement over the closest comparable method AnimateDiff (1934.72) while matching or exceeding the CLIP Similarity of multi-stage methods like Video LDM (0.2929) and LAVIE (0.2949). In a user study with 50 participants, HARIVO is preferred over VideoLDM, PYoCo, and ModelScopeV2 on natural motion, consistency, and overall quality (65–76% preference rates).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AnimateDiff (the only other single-stage, frozen-T2I method), HARIVO reduces FVD from 1934.72 to 787.87 (a 59% reduction) and improves CLIP Similarity from 0.2785 to 0.2948, while also producing more structurally accurate personalized videos (e.g., no spurious branching arms with DreamBooth). Against multi-stage, non-frozen baselines like Make-A-Video (FVD 367.23, 9.7B params) and PYOCO (FVD 355.2, 1.08B params), HARIVO reports a higher FVD, but the authors attribute this partly to FVD instability and the tendency of frozen StableDiffusion to generate diverse styles (illustrations, gray photos) that inflate the metric on UCF101's human-action classes. On CLIP Similarity, HARIVO (0.2948) matches LAVIE (0.2949) and slightly exceeds Video LDM (0.2929), despite using 9–20× fewer trainable parameters. In the user study, HARIVO is preferred over VideoLDM (68–67%), PYoCo (74–76%), and ModelScopeV2 (66–72%) on all three criteria (natural motion, consistency, quality), suggesting that the FVD gap does not reflect perceived video quality. The paper positions HARIVO as the only method combining single-stage training, frozen T2I, and general (non-personalized) video generation, making it uniquely composable with off-the-shelf T2I tools like ControlNet and IP-Adapter without retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AnimateDiff paradigm of freezing a pretrained T2I model and training only temporal attention layers, but departs from it by arguing that IID Gaussian noise is fundamentally unsuitable for video generation due to inter-frame correlation. It introduces a mapping network to implicitly reshape the noise distribution, frame-wise tokens for subtle per-frame variation, and two novel losses (temporal regularized self-attention and decoupled contrastive loss on h-space) that encode video-specific inductive biases. The mitigating gradient sampling technique adapts ADM's classifier guidance framework to enforce temporal smoothness at inference, drawing on the observation that self-attention maps encode scene structure and h-space encodes semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in seamless compatibility with the entire ecosystem of off-the-shelf T2I personalization tools (ControlNet, DreamBooth, LoRA, IP-Adapter) without any retraining, which could accelerate the adoption of video generation in creative workflows. However, the method inherits StableDiffusion v1.5's known weaknesses (hands, limbs, style drift), and its FVD score (787.87) trails dedicated video models like PYoCo (355.2) and Make-A-Video (367.23), raising questions about whether the frozen-T2I approach can close the gap with fully-trained video models. Open questions include whether the mapping network and frame-wise tokens generalize to newer T2I backbones (SDXL, Flux) and whether the approach scales to longer video durations beyond 24 frames.
