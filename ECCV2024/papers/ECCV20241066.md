## Problem

Existing multimodal chain-of-thought frameworks, such as Multimodal-CoT, separate rationale generation from answer inference but produce rationales of insufficient quality, often yielding explanations that lack relevance to the posed question. The paper identifies that when rationales are fully accurate, model accuracy improves dramatically, yet the generated rationales fall well short of ground-truth quality, creating a bottleneck that limits the overall reasoning performance of small multimodal models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241066/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a text question paired with an RGB image and produces a two-stage output: first a free-form rationale (constrained to 512 tokens) that explains the reasoning, then a final answer (constrained to 64 tokens) conditioned on that rationale. During training, the model is sampled N_r times for rationale generation and N_a times for answer generation, with word-level majority voting applied at each rationale position and answer-level majority voting for the final prediction. The inference phase remains identical to standard Multimodal-CoT, requiring no additional sampling at test time.

## Method

MC-CoT (Multimodal Consistent Chain-of-Thought) is a self-consistency training strategy that exploits dropout-induced variability to generate multiple diverse rationales for the same input, then applies word-level majority voting across positions to construct a single refined rationale R*. A second voting stage generates multiple answers from R* and selects the final prediction via majority vote. The voting mechanism combines a simple mean of logits with a variance-weighted mean (weights inversely proportional to per-dimension standard deviation), balancing exploration and exploitation in a bias-variance trade-off. Theoretically, Jensen's inequality guarantees that aggregating logits reduces expected cross-entropy loss. Crucially, this voting operates during training rather than inference (unlike CoT-SC), so the deployed model incurs no additional computational cost at test time.

## Results

On ScienceQA, MC-CoT Base (223M parameters) achieves 90.64% average accuracy, matching the performance of 13B-parameter models like LLaVa (90.92%) and LaVIN-13B (90.50%). MC-CoT F-Large (783M) reaches 94.88% average accuracy, setting a new state-of-the-art that surpasses LLaVa+GPT-4 (92.53%) by 2.35%. On A-OKVQA, MC-CoT Base (223M) achieves 68.7% direct-answer accuracy, outperforming BLIP-2 (11B parameters) by 15.5 percentage points, and 71.0% multi-choice accuracy, exceeding BLIP-2's 70.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MC-CoT Base (223M) improves over its direct predecessor Multimodal-CoT Base by 5.73% average accuracy (90.64% vs. 84.91%), and MC-CoT Large (738M) improves over Multimodal-CoT Large by 1.69% (93.37% vs. 91.68%). The 223M MC-CoT Base matches or exceeds 13B-parameter models: it surpasses LaVIN-13B (90.50%) and is within 0.28% of LLaVa (90.92%), while using roughly 58× fewer parameters. MC-CoT F-Large (783M) achieves the best overall result at 94.88%, surpassing LLaVa+GPT-4 (92.53%) by 2.35% and GPT-4 w/ CoT (83.99%) by 10.89%. On A-OKVQA, MC-CoT Base (223M) dramatically outperforms BLIP-2 (11B) by 15.5% on direct-answer accuracy (68.7% vs. 53.2%) and by 0.8% on multi-choice (71.0% vs. 70.2%), while also exceeding Multimodal-CoT Base's multi-choice score by 20.4% (71.0% vs. 50.6%). The ablation confirms that rationale voting is the critical component: removing it drops accuracy from 90.64% to 84.70%, while removing answer voting causes a smaller drop to 90.19%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Multimodal-CoT [63], which established a two-stage framework separating rationale generation from answer inference for multimodal VQA. It adapts the self-consistency voting idea from CoT-SC [50] (originally an inference-time strategy for LLMs) into a training-time mechanism that exploits dropout-induced stochasticity to generate multiple rationales per sample. The key conceptual departure is that voting is applied at the word level during rationale generation (not just at the answer level) and operates during training rather than inference, producing a refined rationale that then conditions answer prediction. The paper also draws on the broader CoT literature (Tree-of-Thoughts, Graph-of-Thoughts, Skeleton-of-Thought) but positions its contribution specifically within the multimodal setting where rationale quality has been identified as the primary bottleneck.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is that a 223M-parameter model with MC-CoT training matches or exceeds 13B-parameter fine-tuned LLMs on ScienceQA (90.64% vs. 90.50–90.92%), and a 223M model outperforms an 11B BLIP-2 by 15.5% on A-OKVQA direct-answer accuracy. This suggests that rationale quality, not model scale, is the dominant factor in multimodal reasoning performance, opening a path for efficient deployment. Limitations include the absence of grammatical constraints on generated rationales (word-level voting can produce incoherent text), evaluation restricted to two benchmarks, and reliance on the T5/UnifiedQA architecture. Open questions include whether the training-time voting strategy transfers to autoregressive LLM-based multimodal models, whether it generalizes beyond multiple-choice VQA to open-ended generation, and whether the word-level voting can be replaced by more structured consistency criteria.
