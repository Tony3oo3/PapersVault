## Problem

Exemplar-guided image translation methods that capture local style details rely on establishing dense cross-domain correspondences between structural controls and exemplar images, incurring quadratic memory and computational costs while suffering from sparse matching failures that cause local distortion and semantic inconsistency. Meanwhile, diffusion models—despite their advantages in quality and stability—have not been fully exploited for this task, as existing approaches still depend on matching-based frameworks or require complex multi-conditioning (text prompts, ControlNet, CLIP embeddings) that is sensitive to hyperparameters.

## Input / Output

The method takes a structural control (segmentation mask, Canny edge map, or pose keypoints) and a separate exemplar RGB image as input, where the two are not spatially aligned and come from different domains. It produces a photo-realistic image that preserves the structure of the control while adopting the global style and fine-grained texture of the exemplar. All processing occurs in the Stable Diffusion VAE latent space, and training uses paired (control, target) data with independently sampled exemplar images from the target domain.

## Method

EBDM formulates exemplar-guided translation as a Brownian Bridge diffusion process where the structural control is the fixed starting point (x_T) and the target image is the fixed endpoint (x_0), so the denoising U-Net directly learns the translation without any explicit conditional injection mechanism. Style guidance is provided by three components: a Global Encoder (frozen DINOv2) that extracts a [CLS] token for global style via cross-attention, a siamese Exemplar Network that captures fine-grained texture at full resolution, and an Exemplar Attention Module that spatially concatenates exemplar features with denoising features and applies self-attention to selectively incorporate texture at each U-Net block. Training is two-stage: first the U-Net with Global Encoder is trained via reconstruction (using the target as its own exemplar), then the Exemplar Network and Attention Module are added and trained with predefined exemplar-target pairs while freezing other parameters. The loss is the BBDM ELBO, and inference uses a deterministic ODE sampler with the exemplar features computed once before the denoising loop.

## Results

On the three exemplar-guided translation tasks, EBDM achieves the best FID on DeepFashion pose-to-photo (10.62) and CelebA-HQ mask-to-photo (12.21), and the best SWD on CelebA-HQ edge-to-photo (12.10). On the mask-to-photo task, EBDM's FID of 12.21 substantially outperforms the best matching-based method (RABIT, 20.44), demonstrating the advantage of diffusion over cross-domain correspondence when structural cues are sparse. Against the Stable Diffusion + ControlNet + IP-Adapter baseline, EBDM achieves higher SSIM (0.901 vs. 0.894) and PSNR (36.40 vs. 35.94) on the edge-to-photo task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EBDM achieves the best FID on DeepFashion pose-to-photo (10.62) and CelebA-HQ mask-to-photo (12.21), outperforming the previous best diffusion-based method MIDMs (10.89 FID on DeepFashion) and all matching-based methods. On CelebA-HQ edge-to-photo, RABIT edges out EBDM on FID (11.67 vs. 11.84), but EBDM achieves a substantially lower SWD (12.10 vs. 14.22), indicating better distributional fidelity. The most striking advantage appears on mask-to-photo, where EBDM's FID of 12.21 dramatically surpasses the best matching-based baseline RABIT (20.44), confirming that diffusion-based translation excels when structural correspondence cues are scarce. On LPIPS (used here as a diversity metric, higher is better), matching-based methods like MCL-Net (0.286) and RABIT (0.284) slightly exceed EBDM (0.255) on DeepFashion, suggesting EBDM trades a small amount of diversity for higher perceptual quality. Against the Stable Diffusion + ControlNet + IP-Adapter pipeline, EBDM achieves higher SSIM (0.901 vs. 0.894) and PSNR (36.40 vs. 35.94), attributed to the single-conditioning Brownian bridge formulation being less sensitive to hyperparameter tuning than multi-conditioned noise-to-image diffusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on BBDM (Li et al., CVPR 2023), repurposing the Brownian Bridge diffusion process—originally designed for general image-to-image translation—as the backbone for exemplar-guided translation. It departs from the dominant matching-based paradigm (CoCosNet, UNITE, RABIT, DynaST) that establishes dense cross-domain correspondences between control and exemplar, arguing that the bridge's fixed start point (the structural control) eliminates the need for explicit spatial conditioning. It also positions against diffusion-based exemplar methods like MIDMs (Seo et al., AAAI 2023), which still embed a matching module before the diffusion stage, by conditioning the denoiser solely on exemplar style via DINOv2 global features and a siamese Exemplar Network with spatial attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is eliminating the quadratic-cost dense matching step that has been a bottleneck and source of artifacts (local distortion, semantic inconsistency) in exemplar-guided translation, particularly for mask-to-photo where correspondence cues are sparse. However, the improvements over the strongest matching-based baselines are modest on edge-to-photo (FID 11.84 vs. 11.67 for RABIT) and the evaluation is limited to two datasets (CelebA-HQ, DeepFashion) and two domains (faces, fashion). Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions, handles multiple or conflicting exemplars, and generalizes to domains with more complex structural controls (e.g., depth, layout). The reliance on a pre-trained DINOv2 encoder and Stable Diffusion VAE also raises questions about transferability to new domains without retraining.
