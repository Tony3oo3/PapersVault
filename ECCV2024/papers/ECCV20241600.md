## Problem

Fixed activation functions (ReLU, GELU, etc.) have limited expressiveness and impose a structural learning bias on networks, while existing trainable alternatives (PReLU, Swish, PELU) merely adjust a fixed base shape and Maxout/ACON scale their parameter count with network width. No prior trainable activation function achieves high expressiveness with a negligible, constant number of added parameters regardless of network size.

## Input / Output

DiTAC operates element-wise on scalar pre-activation values (the output of a linear layer) and produces a single transformed scalar per input, making it a direct drop-in replacement for any activation function in any architecture. During training, inputs are quantized to n discrete levels (typically 2^8) over a user-defined interval [a,b] to build a lookup table, and the straight-through estimator propagates CPAB derivatives through the quantization. Outside [a,b], the input passes through unchanged (GELU version) or through LReLU (Leaky-DiTAC version), so the method imposes no constraint on input magnitude.

## Method

DiTAC constructs its nonlinearity from a 1D CPAB diffeomorphism T^θ, which is the flow of a continuous piecewise-affine velocity field v^θ defined over a fixed partition of [a,b]; with a 10-cell partition this yields only 9 trainable parameters per activation instance. The main variant combines T^θ with GELU: DiTAC(x) = x̃·Φ(x) where x̃ = T^θ(x) for x ∈ [a,b] and x̃ = x otherwise, preserving GELU's zero-mean property while allowing the learned transformation to reshape the input non-monotonically within the interval. To keep training cost low, the interval is quantized to n points and a lookup table of T^θ values is rebuilt whenever θ changes, reducing per-batch evaluations from ~800K (ResNet-50 bottleneck) to ~1K; backpropagation uses a straight-through estimator that broadcasts CPAB derivatives from the nearest quantized point. A Gaussian smoothness prior on the velocity field (regularization term θ^T Σ_CPA^{-1} θ) prevents the network from learning degenerate or overly oscillatory transformations.

## Results

On ImageNet-50, DiTAC consistently outperforms all fixed and trainable AFs across MobileNet-V3 and ResNet configurations, achieving up to 84.19% top-1 accuracy (MN1.0) and 93.24% (RN-50). On ImageNet-1K, DiTAC improves ConvNeXt-T from 82.1% to 82.4% and Swin-T from 81.3% to 81.5% top-1 accuracy. On the 2D-GMM toy classification task, DiTAC reaches 89.06% top-1 accuracy versus 86.8% for the best trainable competitor (Swish) and 84.0% for fixed AFs. In image generation, DiTAC reduces DCGAN's FID from 133.5 to 34.6 on CelebA and BigGAN's FID from 10.3 to 10.0 on CIFAR-10, while adding only 9 trainable parameters per activation instance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiTAC outperforms all ten compared activation functions across every task and dataset evaluated. Against fixed AFs (ReLU, LReLU, GELU, ELU, Softplus, Mish), DiTAC consistently achieves the highest top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-50 (e.g., 84.19% vs. 83.51% for GELU on MN1.0) and the best R² on regression (96.26% vs. 89.78% for LReLU on 1D). Among trainable AFs, DiTAC surpasses Swish by 2.26 points on 2D-GMM (89.06% vs. 86.8%) and by up to 1.2 points on ImageNet-50 MobileNet configurations. PReLU and PDELU are the weakest trainable competitors, with PDELU failing to converge in several ResNet configurations. Meta-ACON is the closest competitor on ImageNet-50 (84.04% vs. 84.19% on MN1.0), but DiTAC still wins on all ResNet variants and all other tasks. In image generation, DiTAC dramatically outperforms the ReLU/LReLU baselines (DCGAN FID 34.6 vs. 133.5) and marginally improves over BigGAN's ReLU baseline (FID 10.0 vs. 10.3), while GELU degrades generation quality substantially (FID 516.5 on DCGAN).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes CPAB diffeomorphisms (Freifeld et al., ICCV 2015; TPAMI 2017), previously used exclusively for spatial or temporal signal transformations in transformer networks, as elementwise activation functions. It departs from the dominant TAF paradigm (PReLU, Swish, PDELU, Maxout, ACON) where learnable parameters either deform a fixed-shape base function or scale with the number of channels; instead, DiTAC's expressiveness comes from the diffeomorphism family itself, requiring only 9 parameters per instance regardless of network width. The main DiTAC variant composes the CPAB transformation with GELU (Hendrycks & Gimpel, 2016), positioning it as a drop-in upgrade for architectures already using GELU.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiTAC offers a practically attractive drop-in replacement: negligible parameter overhead (9 per layer), closed-form gradients, and a quantization-based lookup table that makes inference as fast as standard AFs. The consistent (if modest) gains across classification, segmentation, generation, and regression suggest the approach is broadly applicable rather than task-specific. An open question is whether the inherent monotonicity of CPAB transformations limits the class of learnable shapes, and whether more general (non-monotonic) diffeomorphism families could yield further gains. The paper also leaves open whether the quantization approximation interacts adversely with very deep or very wide networks at scale.
