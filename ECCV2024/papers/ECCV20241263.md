## Problem

Sparsely Annotated Object Detection (SAOD) suffers from two critical limitations in existing pseudo-labeling approaches: (1) they fail to fully exploit unlabeled objects mined from the student detector, and (2) the generated pseudo-labels contain substantial noise that degrades training. This leaves a significant performance gap between sparsely and fully annotated detection that prior methods have not adequately closed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241263/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (shorter edge resized to 800) with partial bounding-box annotations, processing them through three parallel branches: a raw image for the EMA teacher, a weakly augmented image (RandFlip, ColorJitter) for the weak student, and a strongly augmented image (adding Contrast, Equalize, Solarize, Sharpness, Posterize, RandTranslate, RandShear, RandEraser) for the strong student. Output consists of per-pixel classification scores, regression offsets, and center-ness maps from the FCOS head, which are post-processed via NMS into bounding boxes with class labels and confidence scores.

## Method

Co-Student comprises two parameter-shared student detectors (strong and weak augmentation branches) and an EMA-updated denoising teacher. Each branch generates pseudo-labels via a threshold-and-NMS pipeline, and the teacher's pseudo-labels are used to denoise the students' predictions through an IoU/class/score-matching algorithm (Algorithm 1) that revises, retains, or discards each student pseudo-label based on overlap and confidence with teacher predictions. The denoised pseudo-labels are then exchanged between the two students and merged with sparse ground-truth annotations (filtering out overlaps above an IoU threshold with matching class) to form the final supervision. Training uses a symmetric detection loss where each student is supervised by the other's denoised pseudo-labels, creating consistency regularization from the strong-weak augmentation gap while the teacher progressively suppresses noise accumulation.

## Results

On COCO-miss50p with FCOS-ResNet-50-FPN, Co-Student achieves 37.0 AP, surpassing the sparse-annotation FCOS baseline (33.5 AP) by 3.5 AP and Co-mining (35.7 AP) by 1.3 AP, reaching 95.1% of the fully-supervised FCOS performance (38.9 AP). With the deeper ResNeXt-32x8d-101-BiFPN backbone, Co-Student reaches 43.4 AP versus 39.2 AP for the sparse baseline and 40.8 AP for Co-mining. Across the four additional sparse settings (easy, hard, extreme, keep1), Co-Student consistently outperforms all baselines by 1.0–3.0 AP, with the largest gain of 3.0 AP in the extreme setting (26.1 vs. 23.1 AP for Co-mining).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Co-Student outperforms the FCOS sparse-annotation baseline by 3.5 AP on COCO-miss50p (ResNet-50) and 4.2 AP with ResNeXt-101, and by 1.0–3.0 AP across the easy, hard, extreme, and keep1 settings. Against Co-mining, the closest prior SAOD method, Co-Student gains 1.3 AP on COCO-miss50p (ResNet-50), 2.6 AP with ResNeXt-101, and 1.0–3.0 AP on the other four settings, with the largest margin (3.0 AP) in the extreme setting where only one annotation per image remains. Compared to the two-stage SparseDet, Co-Student leads by 1.1 AP on COCO-miss50p (despite using a lighter ResNet-50 backbone vs. ResNet-101) and by 1.8–2.7 AP on easy, hard, and extreme settings. On the keep1 setting, Co-Student (35.1 AP) surpasses DMiner (33.9 AP) by 1.2 AP. On cross-domain datasets, Co-Student also wins: 53.2 AP50 on BDD100k vs. 52.2 for Co-mining, and 66.0 F1 on TUPAC16 vs. 64.9 for Co-mining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Co-mining dual-branch pseudo-labeling framework for SAOD by incorporating two ideas borrowed from semi-supervised object detection: an EMA-based teacher for denoising pseudo-labels, and strong/weak data augmentations to increase the diversity of mined unlabeled objects. Unlike vanilla teacher-student SAOD methods (e.g., Calibrated Teacher) that discard student-generated pseudo-labels, or Siamese approaches (Co-mining) that exchange raw noisy pseudo-labels, Co-Student routes both students' pseudo-labels through a denoising teacher before exchange, addressing the mutual interference and noise accumulation that limit prior methods. The framework is instantiated with FCOS as the base detector, building on the one-stage SAOD line that includes SparseDet and Background Recalibration Loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By closing the SAOD-to-FAOD gap to 4.9% (95.1% relative performance) across five sparse settings and validating on two additional domains (BDD100k, TUPAC16), the paper demonstrates that the denoising-teacher-plus-strong/weak-student recipe is a practical path toward reducing annotation cost in real-world detection pipelines. The ablation revealing that strong augmentation alone degrades baseline performance by 5.1% AP—yet becomes beneficial only when paired with the denoising mechanism—highlights a non-trivial interaction that practitioners should account for. Open questions include whether the three-network overhead (teacher + two students) is justified at scale, whether the approach transfers to transformer-based detectors, and whether the 4.9% residual gap can be further reduced without additional labeled data.
