## Problem

CLIP's zero-shot classification ignores the distribution of target data, and existing methods that exploit unlabeled target images (e.g., InMaP) require offline, iterative access to the entire dataset. This paper tackles the more challenging and practically relevant online zero-shot transfer scenario, where each image arrives once in random order, must be classified immediately, and its representation cannot be stored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242073/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of RGB images arriving one at a time in random order, along with a fixed set of class names encoded as text prompts (using an ensemble of 7 CLIP prompts). Upon each image's arrival, the model must output a class label and a full probability distribution over all C classes before the next image arrives, with no access to previously seen representations. The only state carried forward is a small set of dual variables (one per class) and C vision-space proxy vectors, making the memory footprint independent of the number of images processed.

## Method

OnZeta combines two online learning components. First, Online Label Learning (OnLab) introduces Lagrange dual variables to enforce a minimum class-occupancy constraint (parameterized by α), yielding a closed-form re-weighted softmax prediction that balances assignments across classes; dual variables are updated by projected gradient ascent with step size O(1/√i), with convergence guaranteed at O(1/√n). Second, Online Proxy Learning updates C vision-space class proxies via online gradient descent on a cross-entropy loss using the OnLab pseudo-labels, with unit-norm projection after each step and the same O(1/√n) regret bound. To mitigate the high variance of the vision proxy during early iterations, the final prediction is a convex combination of the text-space (biased, low-variance) and vision-space (unbiased, high-variance) distributions, with a monotonically increasing weight λ_i = β√(i/n) that shifts reliance from text to vision as the proxy converges.

## Results

On ImageNet with ViT-L/14@336, OnZeta achieves 78.94% top-1 accuracy (best of 5 random-order runs), improving over the vanilla CLIP baseline (77.02%) by 1.92% and over TPT (not reported for this encoder) while remaining only 1.27% below the offline InMaP method (80.21%). Across 13 additional downstream tasks, OnZeta improves the baseline by 3.58% on average with ResNet-50 (58.64% vs. 55.06%) and by 3.16% with ViT-B/16 (68.95% vs. 65.79%). The method requires no stored image representations and processes each image exactly once in a streaming fashion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the vanilla CLIP zero-shot baseline, OnZeta consistently wins across all five vision encoders on ImageNet, with gains ranging from +2.10% (ResNet-50: 62.50% vs. 60.32%) to +1.92% (ViT-L/14@336: 78.94% vs. 77.02%), and by +3.58% / +3.16% on average over 13 downstream tasks with ResNet-50 / ViT-B/16. Against TPT (test-time prompt tuning), OnZeta outperforms by 1.76% on ResNet-50 (62.50% vs. 60.74%) and 2.01% on ViT-B/16 (70.99% vs. 68.98%) on ImageNet, and wins on 9 of 10 available downstream tasks, while being more efficient since it only requires a forward pass rather than 64 augmented back-propagations through the text encoder. Against InMaP (offline proxy learning with full dataset access), OnZeta trails by approximately 1% across all encoders (e.g., 78.94% vs. 80.21% on ViT-L/14@336), which is expected given that InMaP iterates over the entire unlabeled set 2,000 times while OnZeta visits each image exactly once without storage. The gap to InMaP narrows with more epochs (63.46% at 5 epochs vs. 63.74% for InMaP on ResNet-50), confirming the online convergence rate of O(1/√n).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP zero-shot classification paradigm by adapting the offline intra-modal proxy learning of InMaP (Qian et al., NeurIPS 2023) to a streaming, no-storage online setting. It departs from the prompt-learning line (CoOp, TPT) by operating in the vision space rather than the text space, consistent with InMaP's finding that optimal class proxies live in vision space. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of Lagrange-dual-based online label learning to capture global class distribution, combined with a bias-variance trade-off that blends text-space and vision-space predictions—neither of which appears in prior zero-shot transfer work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The online constraint (single pass, no representation storage, immediate prediction) is the practical bottleneck for deploying zero-shot CLIP on edge devices, robots, and streaming pipelines, and this paper shows it can be addressed with only ~1% loss relative to the offline oracle. The O(1/√n) convergence guarantees and the finding that even 5k iterations already beat the baseline make the method deployable in resource-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the convergence rate can be improved beyond O(1/√n), whether the method extends to multi-modal or video streaming, and how it behaves under adversarial or highly non-stationary class distributions where the dual-variable balancing assumption breaks down.
