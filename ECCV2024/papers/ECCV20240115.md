## Problem

Existing 3D semantic occupancy benchmarks derived from nuScenes are capped at 0.2m resolution with noisy, inaccurate annotations due to LiDAR sparsity, erroneous ego poses, and unaddressed data inconsistencies. Moreover, prior prediction methods can only generate occupancy at 0.4m or coarser, requiring post-upsampling to reach 0.2m, which defeats the purpose of high-resolution scene understanding. This paper addresses both the data quality gap and the computational bottleneck of direct high-resolution occupancy prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240115/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts multi-view surround camera images, LiDAR point clouds, or both as input, and produces a dense 3D semantic occupancy field at 0.1m (1024×1024×80) or 0.2m (512×512×40) resolution with 16 semantic classes. Crucially, the network does not directly regress the occupancy grid; instead it predicts a compact set of discrete VQ latent codes in a 32×32×3 space, which a frozen VQ-VAE decoder expands into the full-resolution occupancy. The nuCraft dataset itself is generated from nuScenes LiDAR and camera data through a multi-stage pipeline, yielding 8× higher resolution GT than prior benchmarks.

## Method

The paper contributes two coupled components. First, nuCraft is built via a pipeline that corrects ego poses with Kiss-ICP, aggregates LiDAR sweeps, reconstructs semantic scene meshes using a multi-level octree with SHINE-Mapping augmented by a mesh-vertex semantic prediction branch, and completes foreground objects with NKSR, producing clean 0.1m occupancy GT. Second, VQ-Occ decouples occupancy encoding from prediction: a 3D VQ-VAE (codebook of 2048 codes, 256-dim, latent 32×32×3) is pre-trained to compress and reconstruct occupancy GT, then a BEVDet-based encoder is trained to simulate the discrete VQ codes from sensor features using an L2 mimicking loss plus auxiliary depth supervision computed from the 0.1m GT. At inference, predicted features are snapped to the nearest codebook entry and decoded by the frozen VQ-VAE to yield full-resolution occupancy directly, eliminating the need for post-upsampling and reducing memory from ~500GB (naive 0.1m) to 77GB.

## Results

On OpenOccupancy, VQ-Occ achieves the best IoU and mIoU across all three input modalities: 21.5/13.6 (camera), 35.3/24.8 (LiDAR), and 36.8/25.5 (camera+LiDAR). The largest margin is in the multimodal setting, where VQ-Occ outperforms M-CONet by 7.3 IoU and 5.4 mIoU. On nuCraft at 0.2m, VQ-Occ reaches 37.5 IoU and 26.2 mIoU with C+L input, surpassing M-CNet by 7.6 IoU and 5.5 mIoU. At 0.1m resolution on nuCraft, VQ-Occ directly generates predictions (14.3 IoU, 9.6 mIoU) using only 77 GB memory, whereas C-CONet would require ~500 GB for native 0.1m prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VQ-Occ consistently outperforms all six baselines across every input modality on both OpenOccupancy and nuCraft. In the camera-only setting, VQ-Occ beats C-CONet (the strongest camera baseline) by 1.4 IoU and 0.8 mIoU on OpenOccupancy, and by 1.1 IoU and 0.7 mIoU on nuCraft. For LiDAR input, VQ-Occ surpasses PointOcc (the prior best LiDAR method) by 1.2 IoU and 0.9 mIoU on OpenOccupancy. The most dramatic gain appears in the multimodal (C+L) setting: VQ-Occ improves over M-CONet by 7.3 IoU and 5.4 mIoU on OpenOccupancy, and by 7.6 IoU and 5.5 mIoU on nuCraft, suggesting that the VQ latent-space formulation particularly benefits from richer multi-modal inputs. Older methods like MonoScene and TPVFormer (camera) and LMSCNet and JS3C-Net (LiDAR) trail significantly, with mIoU gaps of 6–18 points, confirming that the VQ-VAE encoding approach represents a substantial step forward over both early scene-completion and recent occupancy-prediction architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D semantic occupancy prediction line (Occ3D, OpenOccupancy) by addressing two bottlenecks: dataset resolution/quality and the quadratic memory cost of high-resolution prediction. For the dataset, it builds on SHINE-Mapping's octree-based mesh reconstruction and Kiss-ICP pose estimation to produce a 0.1m-resolution occupancy GT (nuCraft) that is 8× finer than OpenOccupancy. For the method, VQ-Occ adapts the VQ-VAE architecture from UltraLiDAR (which tokenizes LiDAR point clouds) to the occupancy domain, decoupling GT encoding from prediction so that the network only simulates discrete latent codes rather than regressing a full voxel field. This departs from prior occupancy methods (C-CONet, PointOcc) that predict at coarse resolution and post-upsample, which the authors argue defeats the purpose of high-resolution occupancy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

nuCraft provides a substantially cleaner and finer benchmark that could become the standard for evaluating high-resolution occupancy, and the VQ-VAE-based prediction framework offers a practical path to 0.1m occupancy without prohibitive memory costs. However, the 0.1m results are preliminary (batch size 1, 77 GB memory, single dataset), the VQ-VAE reconstruction quality is moderate (IoU ~68–73%), and cross-dataset generalization drops of ~4–5% mIoU suggest the learned codebook is dataset-specific. Open questions include whether the VQ latent space scales gracefully to even finer resolutions or larger scenes, whether the codebook can be shared across datasets, and whether the mesh-reconstruction pipeline generalizes beyond nuScenes to other sensor suites.
