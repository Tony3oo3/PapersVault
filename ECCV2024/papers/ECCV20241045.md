## Problem

Implicit video representations like NeRV achieve strong compression but suffer from prohibitively slow encoding, as each video requires gradient-based optimization to fit a dedicated neural network. Additionally, NeRV's decoding pipeline lacks the parallelism that modern GPU hardware offers, making it slower than even traditional codecs like H.264 for repeated video loading in research workflows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241045/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder takes a video clip (default 256×256, 8 frames) partitioned into 64×64 patches, and outputs a compact video-specific weight vector (~24.1k parameters) that serves as the compressed representation. Decoding takes a normalized frame index t ∈ [-1, 1], applies positional encoding, and feeds it through the NeRV convolutional stack to produce a full 256×256 RGB frame. After 4-bit weight quantization and Huffman encoding, the stored video is 65× smaller than the original while retaining 99% of reconstruction quality.

## Method

NeRV-Enc replaces per-video gradient optimization with a transformer hyper-network (6 encoder layers, hidden dim 720) that maps video patch tokens and learned initial weight tokens directly to video-specific weights in a single forward pass, achieving a 10⁴× encoding speedup. A key architectural choice is layer-adaptive weight token distribution, where different NeRV layers receive different numbers of modulation tokens (e.g., 1.1k/18.4k/4.6k/0 across 4 layers), outperforming both uniform (TransINR) and single-layer (GINR) schemes while using 50% fewer parameters. The final NeRV weights are computed as an element-wise product of the video-specific weights and shared video-agnostic parameters, keeping the per-video storage small. NeRV-Dec enables parallel multi-video decoding via group convolution (each group = one NeRV model), yielding near-linear speedup up to 16 concurrent videos and 11× faster loading than H.264 on CPU. The entire system is trained end-to-end with MSE reconstruction loss on Kinetics-400 using AdamW.

## Results

NeRV-Enc achieves 27.8 PSNR / 0.791 SSIM on K400 (8 frames, 1500 epochs), outperforming TransINR (22.3 PSNR) and GINR (23.9 PSNR) by +5.5 and +3.9 PSNR respectively, while training 7–15× faster. For encoding speed, NeRV-Enc is 10⁴× faster than the NeRV gradient-based baseline. For decoding, NeRV-Dec achieves 5175 videos/sec (6-bit quantization), which is 11× faster than H.264 (485 VPS on 8 CPUs) and 2.5× faster than loading pre-decoded videos from RAM (2031 VPS), while using 65× less storage (11.6 KB vs. 1.15 MB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against pixel-wise INR baselines, NeRV-Enc dominates on both quality and speed: at 8 frames with 150 epochs, it achieves +5.5 PSNR over TransINR (25.8 vs. 20.3 on K400) and +3.0 PSNR over GINR (25.8 vs. 22.8), while training 10.8× faster than TransINR and 11.2× faster than GINR. Against the NeRV gradient-based baseline [6], NeRV-Enc matches reconstruction quality (25.8 PSNR on K400) but encodes 10⁴× faster, enabling real-time encoding. Against traditional codecs, NeRV-Dec is 11× faster than H.264 (5175 vs. 485 VPS) and 2× faster even with 96 CPUs, though H.264 at CRF 40 achieves slightly higher PSNR (27.3 vs. 28.1 at 8-bit) and smaller file size (8.7 KB vs. 23.7 KB). Against AV1 (CRF 60), NeRV-Dec is ~16× faster in decoding (5175 vs. 313 VPS) but AV1 achieves higher PSNR (32.4 vs. 28.4). Against RAM loading of pre-decoded videos, NeRV-Dec is 2.5× faster (5175 vs. 2031 VPS) while being 65× smaller in storage (11.6 KB vs. 1.15 MB at 4-bit quantization).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NeRV line of work (image-wise implicit video representations using CNNs) by replacing its gradient-based per-video optimization with a transformer hyper-network (NeRV-Enc) that directly generates video-specific weights in a single forward pass. It adapts the hyper-network paradigm from pixel-wise INRs (TransINR, GINR) to the image-wise NeRV setting, introducing a layer-adaptive weight token distribution that departs from both TransINR's uniform allocation and GINR's single-layer restriction. On the decoding side, NeRV-Dec introduces group-convolution-based parallelism to decode multiple videos simultaneously, a practical extension not present in the original NeRV formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10^4× encoding speedup and 11× decoding speedup over H.264 make implicit video representations practically viable as a data-loading and compression tool in deep learning pipelines, where video I/O is a known bottleneck (46% slowdown in self-supervised learning). However, the paper explicitly acknowledges that NeRV-Dec's compression ratio and reconstruction quality still trail established codecs like H.264 and AV1, and that hybrid INR variants (HNeRV) require more complex design to fully exploit the hyper-network. Closing the quality gap with traditional codecs while maintaining the speed advantage remains the key open question for the field.
