## Problem

Existing X-ray-based KOA prognosis methods produce only a singular progression severity grade, overlooking the visual changes that clinicians need to understand and explain disease trajectory. Additionally, GAN-based generative approaches for medical imaging yield poor bone structures and inconsistent patient identity, limiting their clinical utility for longitudinal tracking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242252/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 224×224 baseline knee X-ray crop (extracted from bilateral OAI scans via joint localisation) as input and produces two outputs: a synthesized 12-month follow-up knee X-ray image and a predicted KOA progression severity grade on the 5-level Kellgren-Lawrence scale. The generated X-ray is conditioned on both the baseline scan and the target future KL grade, while the progression grade is predicted jointly from the baseline and generated images through a two-stream classifier.

## Method

IC-RDN comprises three modules: (1) an identity prior module using a pretrained ResNet-18 backbone trained with triplet contrastive loss to extract patient-specific bone structure embeddings; (2) a latent diffusion generative module (VQ-GAN encoder/decoder with a time-conditional UNet) where identity features are injected via cross-attention at each UNet layer, regularizing the denoising process to preserve patient identity while generating clinically plausible progression patterns; and (3) a two-stream CNN progression prediction module that processes both the baseline and generated 12-month X-rays through shared-architecture backbones (VGG/ResNet/DenseNet), concatenates the 128-dim features, and feeds a 256-dim MLP to predict the KL grade. The key architectural insight is that contrastive-learned identity priors, injected through cross-attention rather than simple conditioning, guide the diffusion to focus on clinically relevant structural changes while maintaining subject-specific bone geometry.

## Results

On the OAI dataset (7,923 joints), IC-RDN achieves an Inception Score of 1.98 and the highest KOA progression grading accuracy across all five CNN backbones, reaching 66.8% with VGG-16, 65.1% with VGG-19, 61.1% with ResNet-18, 61.7% with ResNet-50, and 64.4% with DenseNet-121. The ablation without the Identity Prior drops accuracy by 4–5.2 percentage points (e.g., 61.6% vs. 66.8% on VGG-16) and reduces IS from 1.98 to 1.72, confirming the contribution of the identity-guided diffusion. The method outperforms the baseline-image-only setting (66.3% VGG-16) by 0.5–1.9 points, demonstrating that the generated 12-month X-ray adds meaningful prognostic signal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IC-RDN outperforms all four generative baselines on progression grading accuracy across every backbone. Against Cycle GAN, IC-RDN gains 3.3–4.5 points (e.g., 66.8% vs. 62.5% on VGG-16) and a substantially higher IS (1.98 vs. 1.28), indicating both better image quality and more clinically useful structural fidelity. Compared to the I2I Diffusion Model, IC-RDN achieves a marginally lower IS (1.98 vs. 2.02) but a clear accuracy advantage of 3.5–4.4 points (e.g., 66.8% vs. 62.4% on VGG-16), attributed to the identity prior preserving patient-specific bone structure. Against CXR-IRGen and PITET GAN, IC-RDN leads by 4.4–5.7 and 2.9–4.2 points respectively, with the largest gap on ResNet-50 (61.7% vs. 59.1% and 60.7%). The ablation (IC-RDN without Identity Prior) confirms that the identity module contributes 4–5.2 points of accuracy and 0.26 IS, validating the contrastive-learning-based identity guidance as the key differentiator over a plain latent diffusion backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the latent diffusion model framework (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) and DDPM (Ho et al., NeurIPS 2020) to perform image-to-image translation in the knee X-ray domain, departing from the GAN-based approaches (e.g., CycleGAN) that prior medical image translation work has relied on. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of an identity prior module trained with triplet contrastive loss, which injects patient-specific bone structure information into the diffusion denoising process via cross-attention — a mechanism not present in standard I2I diffusion or prior KOA progression models. The downstream generation-guided progression prediction module, which concatenates features from both baseline and generated scans, extends the well-established KOA KL-grading task by incorporating a synthetic future image as an additional input modality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine clinical gap: existing KOA progression tools output only a numeric grade, whereas clinicians also need visual explanations of disease trajectory to support decision-making. The identity-consistency mechanism is a practical contribution for longitudinal medical imaging, where preserving patient-specific anatomy across time points is essential for trust and utility. However, the authors acknowledge that generated images alone yield suboptimal grading accuracy, raise concerns about 'computer hallucinations' in disease-specific changes, and limit evaluation to a single 12-month horizon on one dataset (OAI). Open questions include whether the identity-severity representations can be disentangled for finer-grained control, whether the approach generalizes to longer time horizons or other imaging modalities (MRI, CT), and whether the modest accuracy gains (~4–5% over I2I diffusion without identity prior) hold across diverse clinical populations.
