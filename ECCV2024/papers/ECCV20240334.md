## Problem

Existing deep-learning joint alignment methods (e.g., Neural Congealing, ASIC) rely on high-capacity models (7.9M–28.7M parameters), numerous regularization terms (4–8 auxiliary losses), and many hyperparameters, resulting in training times exceeding one hour and brittle, dataset-specific tuning. No lightweight, regularization-free approach has matched their alignment quality while operating at a fraction of the computational cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240334/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N RGB images of a single object class, extracts their DINO ViT features (384-channel spatial maps) and saliency-based binary masks, then reduces the features via PCA (to 25 components) and a small fully-convolutional autoencoder (to 3 channels) before alignment. It outputs per-image 8-DOF homography warping parameters, jointly aligned images and feature maps, and optionally a category atlas computed as the mean of warped features. Training is performed as test-time optimization, independently per category, with no explicit atlas maintained during the optimization loop.

## Method

SpaceJAM uses a compact recurrent Spatial Transformer Network (~16K total parameters: ~13K for a 2-layer localization CNN plus ~3K for the autoencoder) that predicts a cascade of 5 small homography transformations via inverse-compositional recurrences. The key novelty is an inverse-compositional pairwise loss that, for each image pair in a batch, warps one image forward and the other backward (using Lie-group inversion) and minimizes the L2 distance between the result and the target's features, thereby implicitly mapping all images to a shared space without regularization or explicit atlas maintenance. Transformations are parameterized in Lie algebras (se(2), aff(2), sl(3)) and mapped to their corresponding Lie groups via the matrix exponential, guaranteeing invertibility; a curriculum schedule progressively expands the transformation family from rigid SE(2) to full homographies. Reflections are handled by training on all flip configurations simultaneously while computing gradients only for the best one.

## Results

On SPair-71K, SpaceJAM (DINOv2, ViT-L) achieves a mean PCK@0.10 of 45.7 across all 18 object categories, outperforming ASIC and NeuCongealing (both 36.9) by nearly 9 points. On the challenging 'Cat' category, SpaceJAM scores 60.8 PCK@0.10 versus 54.8 for both ASIC and NeuCongealing. On CUB-200 subsets, SpaceJAM reaches 69.9 PCK@0.10, surpassing NeuCongealing (63.6) and GANgealing (56.8), though trailing ASIC (75.9) on the 3-category subset. SpaceJAM achieves these results with only 16K trainable parameters and ~6 minutes of training time on a single RTX 4090, compared to 7.9M–28.7M parameters and 67–77 minutes for the competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpaceJAM outperforms both ASIC [19] and NeuCongealing [48] on the SPair-71K overall mean PCK@0.10 (45.7 vs. 36.9) and on the 'Cat' category (60.8 vs. 54.8), while using 1/500th to 1/1800th the trainable parameters and training ~10× faster. On CUB-200 subsets, SpaceJAM (69.9) beats NeuCongealing (63.6) and GANgealing [51] (56.8) but falls short of ASIC (75.9) on the 3-category evaluation. Against the broader set of weakly-supervised train/test baselines (CNNGeo, A2Net, WeakAlign, NCNet, SFNet, PMD, PSCNet-SE), SpaceJAM's 45.7 overall PCK@0.10 is the highest among all methods in the table. The DINO+NN baseline [2] (31.3) and DINO+MLS [1] (31.1) are also clearly surpassed. Notably, SpaceJAM requires zero regularization hyperparameters and a single loss term, whereas ASIC uses 4 losses with 5 HPs and NeuCongealing uses 8 losses with 8 HPs, making SpaceJAM substantially easier to deploy and tune.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpaceJAM builds directly on the Spatial Transformer Network (STN) and its inverse-compositional variant (IC-STN), but departs sharply from recent JA methods like Neural Congealing and ASIC, which rely on high-capacity models (7.9M–28.7M parameters) and 4–8 auxiliary regularization losses to prevent trivial solutions. Conceptually, the method is closest to classical inverse-compositional least-squares congealing (IC-LSC), but reformulated in a differentiable, Lie-algebraic setting with DINO features. The key departure is the inverse-compositional pairwise loss, which implicitly defines a shared space without explicitly maintaining an atlas or penalizing transformations, thereby eliminating all regularization hyperparameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing JA to a 16K-parameter model with a single loss and zero tunable regularization hyperparameters, SpaceJAM makes the task dramatically more accessible and faster (≈6 min vs. ~1–1.5 h on a single GPU), which is practically important for test-time optimization pipelines and resource-constrained settings. Limitations include reliance on external mask quality, restriction to homographies (though the code supports other Lie groups), and a design not optimized for dense per-pixel correspondence. Open questions include whether the regularization-free inverse-compositional formulation extends to non-parametric or diffeomorphic warps, and whether the approach remains competitive when DINO features are replaced by cheaper or domain-specific encoders.
