## Problem

Existing multi-view pixel-aligned implicit models (Multi-view PIFu, DeepMultiCap, DoubleField, SeSDF) fuse features from V views in a point-wise, localized manner only at the very end of the pipeline via simple or weighted averaging. This means each view is processed independently and the multi-view task is effectively reduced to a single-view problem with a trivial final fusion step, leaving no global interaction between neighboring sample points and no opportunity for multi-view information to influence intermediate representations.

## Input / Output

The method takes V > 1 sparse RGB images (default 512×512, optionally 1024×1024) with known camera calibrations, typically 90° apart, plus an optional SMPL-X mesh. From each RGB image, front and back normal maps and a binary mask are predicted via PIFuHD; one view is designated primary and the rest secondary. The output is a 3D clothed human body mesh extracted via Marching Cubes from a 256×256×256 truncated SDF grid (values in [−1, 1]).

## Method

3DFG-PIFu is a two-stage pipeline that fuses multi-view information globally throughout the network. In Stage 1, 3D Feature Grids for Visual Hull (G_M) and Front Normals (G_N) are constructed from secondary view(s) by replicating mask/normal pixels along the camera axis, transforming them into the primary view's 3D camera space, and taking the 3D intersection (for G_M); these grids are concatenated with the primary view's RGB and normal maps and passed through a 2D CNN encoder and MLP to produce a structurally accurate base mesh, optionally conditioned on SDF-based SMPL-X features (G_X) and PaMIR voxel-aligned features. In Stage 2, an iterative refinement loop rotates the base mesh into each view's camera space, generates an SDF grid (G_S′) and a visibility grid (G_V) from the rotated mesh, concatenates them with that view's inputs, and trains an encoder+MLP with Depth Oriented Sampling to produce a partial refined mesh capturing fine-grained appearance details visible from that view. All partial refined meshes are then combined via visibility-based fusion—overwriting SDF values in the base mesh grid only at locations visible from their source view—yielding the final SDF grid G_F from which the final mesh is extracted.

## Results

On THuman2.0, 3DFG-PIFu (HR, w SM) achieves a Chamfer Distance of 3.555×10⁻⁵ and Point-to-Surface distance of 3.129×10⁻⁵, substantially outperforming the best baseline SeSDF (CD 6.302, P2S 9.181). On BUFF, it reaches CD 3.412×10⁻² and P2S 2.700×10⁻², and on MultiHuman, CD 5.391×10⁻⁵ and P2S 4.934×10⁻⁵. Notably, even the weakest configuration (no SMPL-X, no high-res images) already surpasses all baselines on every dataset, e.g. CD 5.796/P2S 5.811 on THuman2.0 vs. SeSDF's 6.302/9.181.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DFG-PIFu outperforms all six baselines across all three datasets and all three metrics (CD, P2S, Normal). Against the closest competitor SeSDF (which uses a groundtruth SMPL-X mesh), 3DFG-PIFu (HR, w SM) reduces CD by 43% on THuman2.0 (3.555 vs. 6.302) and by 11% on BUFF (3.412 vs. 3.848), while also improving P2S by 66% on THuman2.0 (3.129 vs. 9.181). DeepMultiCap, despite also using SMPL-X, performs poorly on BUFF (CD 12.45) and MultiHuman (CD 32.83), suggesting poor generalization beyond its training distribution. Multi-view PIFu and IntegratedPIFu, which do not use SMPL-X, are the weakest baselines, with THuman2.0 CD values above 10. Even 3DFG-PIFu's most constrained configuration (no SMPL-X, no high-res) beats SeSDF on THuman2.0 P2S (5.811 vs. 9.181) and BUFF CD (2.509 vs. 3.848), indicating the 3D Feature Grid architecture itself provides a substantial advantage over point-wise fusion strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pixel-aligned implicit model lineage (PIFu → Multi-view PIFu → DeepMultiCap → SeSDF) by fundamentally rethinking how multi-view information is fused. Existing methods process each view independently and combine point embeddings only at the pipeline's end via simple or weighted averaging, effectively reducing multi-view to single-view with a trivial fusion step. 3DFG-PIFu departs from this by introducing 3D Feature Grids that re-orient secondary-view information into the primary view's camera space, enabling global, end-to-end multi-view fusion. It also builds on PaMIR's voxel-aligned features and S-PIFu's SMPL-X integration, but replaces S-PIFu's 2D handcrafted features with SDF-based 3D feature grids to preserve volumetric information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a structural weakness in the multi-view pixel-aligned implicit model family that has persisted across several SOTA methods, and the 3D Feature Grid formulation is a clean, reusable architectural primitive that other implicit-surface methods could adopt. The iterative 2nd-stage mechanism allows incremental mesh refinement with additional views without retraining, which is practically valuable for capture setups where views are acquired sequentially. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to very sparse or uncalibrated settings, how it scales beyond V=4 views, and whether the 256³ grid resolution is sufficient for high-frequency clothing details. The method requires known camera calibrations, limiting its applicability to uncalibrated multi-view scenarios.
