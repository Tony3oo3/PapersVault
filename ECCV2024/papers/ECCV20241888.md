## Problem

Despite the success of synthetic data in domains like autonomous driving and embodied AI, its utility for egocentric hand-object interaction (HOI) detection remained unexplored due to the difficulty of generating photorealistic images of hands in physical contact with objects. This paper systematically investigates whether and how synthetic data can reduce dependence on expensive real-world annotations, answering concrete questions about the synthetic-real gap, the scale of synthetic data needed, and the role of in-domain alignment across unsupervised, semi-supervised, and fully-supervised domain adaptation settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241888/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single egocentric RGB frame and produces a structured prediction per hand-object pair: bounding boxes and pixel-wise segmentation masks for both the hand and the interacted object, a binary contact-state label (contact vs. no contact), a hand-side label (left/right), and a 2D offset vector encoding the spatial relation from hand to object. Evaluation uses a composite COCO Mask AP ('Overall') that jointly scores all of these predictions, alongside breakdown APs for hand (H), hand+side (H+S), hand+contact (H+C), and object (O) individually.

## Method

The core contribution is a Unity3D-based data generation pipeline that samples hand-object grasps from DexGraspNet, fits them to randomly generated human models (Unity SyntheticHumans), places the composite in HM3D 3D environments, and renders first-person views with a virtual camera at eye level; the simulator automatically outputs bounding boxes, segmentation masks, contact states, and hand-object relations. For domain adaptation, the authors adapt the VISOR HOS baseline (a PointRend instance segmentation network with hand-side, contact-state, and offset-vector heads) within the Adaptive Teacher (AT) unsupervised domain adaptation framework, extending AT with the additional HOI-specific prediction modules. They then benchmark five training configurations—Synthetic-Only, UDA, Real-Only, Synthetic+Real (pre-train then fine-tune), and SSDA/FSDA (AT with varying fractions of labeled real data merged with synthetic labels)—across three datasets (VISOR, EgoHOS, ENIGMA-51), including an in-domain vs. out-domain comparison on ENIGMA-51 where synthetic scenes reuse the actual 3D models of the target industrial laboratory.

## Results

Using only 10% of real labeled data, the proposed SSDA approach (Adaptive Teacher with synthetic data) achieves Overall Mask AP improvements of +5.67% on EPIC-KITCHENS VISOR (44.22% vs. 38.55% Real-Only), +8.24% on EgoHOS (36.68% vs. 28.44%), and +11.69% on ENIGMA-51 (57.08% vs. 45.39%). In the unsupervised setting (0% real labels), UDA narrows the synthetic-to-real gap by ~21–23% Overall AP across all three datasets, though a residual 30–40% gap persists versus fully supervised real-data baselines. With 25% real labels on VISOR, SSDA reaches 45.55% Overall AP, surpassing the 100%-real-data Real-Only baseline (45.33%) by +0.22%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the DA method comparison on VISOR with 25% real labels (Table 3), the Adaptive Teacher (AT) framework used by the paper achieves the best Overall AP at 45.55%, outperforming Unbiased Teacher (UT, 44.32%) by +1.23%, Mean Teacher + GRL (43.97%) by +1.58%, and Mean Teacher (MT, 43.69%) by +1.86%. However, AT does not dominate all sub-metrics: MT achieves the best Hand+Contact AP (60.94% vs. AT's 52.59%), and UT edges out AT on Hand AP (90.60% vs. 90.37%). AT leads on Object AP (22.15%) and Hand+Side AP (84.42%), confirming its strength in the holistic interaction prediction that drives the Overall score. The paper notes that the gap between AT and UT is narrow (+0.23% on H), suggesting room for further improvement in the benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VISOR HOS formulation [10] and its PointRend-based baseline architecture, extending it with domain adaptation modules (hand side, contact state, offset vector) to enable synthetic-to-real transfer. It adapts the Adaptive Teacher framework [27]—originally designed for cross-domain object detection—to the richer HOI detection task, and benchmarks it against other DA strategies (Mean Teacher, Unbiased Teacher, GRL). The data generation pipeline composes existing components (DexGraspNet for grasps, HM3D for environments, Unity3D for rendering) into a novel pipeline specifically targeting egocentric hand-object interaction, filling a gap where no prior simulator modeled hands, objects, and physical contact simultaneously for this task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HOI-Synth is the first benchmark explicitly enabling synthetic-to-real domain adaptation studies for egocentric HOI detection, providing a reproducible foundation for future work in this under-explored area. The practical finding that SSDA with only 10–25% real labels matches or exceeds fully supervised baselines is directly relevant to domains where annotation is expensive (industrial, healthcare). However, the persistent 30–40% synthetic-real gap and the limited gains in fully supervised settings (+1–4%) suggest that current photorealism and interaction modeling are still insufficient to close the gap, leaving open questions about whether better hand-object physics, greater environmental diversity, or generative approaches (e.g., diffusion-based) could further narrow it.
