## Problem

In egocentric videos, the wearer's self-motion causes static scene elements to shift in the head-locked reference frame, introducing variability that complicates spatial reasoning across modalities. Prior approaches either learn to offset this variability end-to-end or use self-motion as a self-supervised signal, neither of which fully stabilizes the scene representation. The paper asks whether explicitly transforming multisensory streams into a gravity-aligned, world-locked spherical coordinate system can reduce this variability at negligible computational cost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an egocentric video frame (3×Hv×Wv), a multichannel audio spectrogram (Ca×Ha×Wa) split into vertical patches aligned with head pose, a head-pose quaternion, and optional behavioral vectors (gaze, orientation, trajectory) as 3D unit vectors. Rather than warping inputs onto a panorama, each semantic token is paired with a 3D coordinate on a world-locked sphere, preserving the original image/spectrogram format. Outputs are flexible: pointwise scores on a sparse 5×10 grid of CLS tokens for active speaker localization, angular directions on the sphere for auditory source localization, or future behavioral directions at 300/500/700 ms for behavior anticipation, all decodable via sparse, dense 2D, or 1D horizontal strategies.

## Method

The core contribution is Implicit Spherical World-Locking (SWL), which assigns each multisensory embedding a 3D position on a world-locked sphere around the wearer's head using the current head-pose quaternion, without performing expensive explicit panoramic projection. The MuST encoder is a transformer whose self-attention incorporates two key modifications: (1) a quaternion-based spatial similarity matrix computed once per input from pairwise rotation quaternions between position vectors, added to the standard QK^T attention scores; and (2) modality-wise layer normalization and separate Q/K/V linear projections that harmonize heterogeneous video, audio, and behavioral tokens. Multiple CLS tokens, each parametrized by a learnable linear map from a sphere point to an embedding, serve as spatially localized query slots for flexible decoding. Training is supervised with binary cross-entropy, Adam at lr 1e-4, and 10 epochs, following DeiT-S-scale hyperparameters.

## Results

On the EasyCom active speaker localization benchmark, MuST achieves 89.88 mAP, outperforming the best prior method MAVASLC+E (86.32 mAP) by 3.6 percentage points and approaching the Oracle upper bound (91.03 mAP) within 1.2 points. On RLR-CHAT auditory spherical source localization, MuST reduces mean angular error to 15.23°/12.67° (prediction→ground-truth / ground-truth→prediction), a 3–4× improvement over the next-best baseline SAM (46.95°/44.90°). On the AEA behavior anticipation task, MuST_AVB reduces angular error by 20.5% (gaze), 12.8% (orientation), and 29.5% (trajectory) over the MultitaskGP baseline at the 300 ms horizon.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On EasyCom, MuST (89.88 mAP) decisively beats all nine baselines, with the largest gaps over BPNface (+14.7 pp), TalkNet (+20.8 pp), and the audio-processing-only DOA (+37.3 pp). The closest competitor, MAVASLC+E [33], trails by 3.6 pp, while the self-supervised AVLN [50] lags by 4.8 pp. On RLR-CHAT, MuST's 15.23°/12.67° MAE is roughly 3× better than SAM [78] (46.95°/44.90°) and 4× better than EchoNet [17] (66.99°/65.29°), confirming that explicit spherical world-locking with pose is far more effective than audio-only spatial reasoning. On AEA behavior anticipation, MuST_AVB outperforms MultitaskGP [19] on gaze (9.17° vs 11.42°) and trajectory (9.96° vs 13.75°) at 300 ms, but slightly underperforms on short-term head orientation (4.78° vs 4.70°), suggesting that orientation at very short horizons is already well-captured by a simple GP prior. Jointly training all three behaviors (MuST_AVB) consistently beats single-task variants, validating the cross-behavior coherence hypothesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant head-locked paradigm in egocentric audio-visual localization (Jiang et al. 2022, Murdock et al. 2024), where models must implicitly learn to compensate for self-motion variability, by explicitly leveraging IMU-derived head pose to re-anchor all modalities onto a world-locked sphere. It draws conceptual inspiration from spherical scene representation literature (spherical CNNs, panoramic transformers) but deliberately avoids expensive geometric projections, instead encoding spherical structure through quaternion-based position embeddings within a standard transformer. The multi-CLS token decoding strategy for spatially localized prediction and the modality-wise attention/normalization operations are novel architectural choices not present in prior egocentric or audio-visual localization frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The implicit SWL formulation offers a practical, low-overhead way to incorporate self-motion compensation into existing egocentric pipelines without requiring panoramic re-projection or distortion-aware convolutions, making it broadly applicable to any head-mounted device with IMU sensors. The new joint behavior anticipation benchmark on AEA (gaze + orientation + trajectory simultaneously) fills a gap in egocentric user understanding, though its small scale (143 recordings, 5 scenes) limits generalizability claims. Key open questions include whether the framework extends to translation (not just rotation), whether optical flow can substitute for IMU data in settings where pose sensors are unavailable, and whether the approach scales to longer temporal windows and larger datasets like Ego4D.
