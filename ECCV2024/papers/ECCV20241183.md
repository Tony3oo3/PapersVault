## Problem

Early smoke contains large regions of high transparency (30–50% of the area) where smoke features overlap heavily with background features in the embedding space, making standard Euclidean-distance-based losses (triplet, proxy-based) ineffective at separating the two classes. No prior method specifically addresses this large-area transparency challenge by constructing a discriminative feature space tailored to early smoke.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241183/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (512×512 for SmokeSeg, 480×480 for SMOKE5K, 256×256 for SYN70K) and produces pixel-level binary segmentation masks distinguishing smoke from background. DSA is a training-only auxiliary loss module that is plug-and-play with any segmentation backbone (CNN or ViT) and incurs zero additional computational cost at inference time.

## Method

DSA replaces pure Euclidean-distance optimization with scatter-matrix analysis to learn a discriminative embedding space. It operates from two perspectives: (1) IOS (pixel-proxy scatter) minimizes within-class scatter between pixel features and their matched proxies while maximizing between-class scatter via a non-parametric nearest-neighbor-proxy formulation, and (2) OOS (proxy-proxy scatter) prevents proxy collapse by maximizing scatter among proxies within the same class. Pixel-to-proxy assignment is solved via the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm with entropy regularization and an equipartition constraint to avoid trivial solutions. The final DSA loss is the reciprocal of the sum of two ratio-trace terms, Tr(Ξ_w′⁻¹Ξ_b) + Tr(Ξ_w′⁻¹Ξ_p), combined with standard cross-entropy loss, and is optimized end-to-end with the feature extractor during training only.

## Results

On the SmokeSeg early smoke segmentation dataset, DSA consistently improves all tested backbones by 0.91–2.16% in F1 (Total). The strongest result is FoSp (MiT-B3) + DSA reaching 73.40% F1, 59.91% mIoU, and 0.0075 mMse, surpassing the previous SOTA FoSp (72.05% F1) by 1.35%. On SMOKE5K, FoSp + DSA achieves 82.0% F1 and 0.001 mMse; on SYN70K, it reaches 83.30% mIoU and 0.0980 mMse (DS01). The largest single-model gain is SegFormer (MiT-B3) + DSA at 70.15% F1 (+2.16 over baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSA outperforms all 11 baselines on SmokeSeg. Among segmentation architectures, the largest gains come from SegFormer (MiT-B3: +2.16% F1) and Trans-BVM (+1.82% F1), while even the prior SOTA FoSp (MiT-B3) improves by +1.35% F1 to reach 73.40%. Against distance-based losses (Table 4), DSA dominates: Triplet Loss yields only +0.21% F1 on SegFormer and actually degrades FoSp (−0.16%), ProxyAnchor degrades Trans-BVM (−0.80%) and FoSp (−1.75%), and SoftTriple gives modest gains (+0.53% on SegFormer) — all far below DSA's +2.16%. DMLNet (triplet-loss segmentation) scores 67.95% F1 without a DSA variant, confirming that pair-based metric learning is insufficient for high-transparency early smoke. On SMOKE5K and SYN70K, DSA also improves every baseline (e.g., SegFormer MiT-B3: 78.6→79.5% F1 on SMOKE5K; 80.17→81.27% mIoU on SYN70K DS01), demonstrating generality beyond the dedicated ESS dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the scatter matrix formalism from classical Linear Discriminant Analysis (Fisher, 1936) and its deep-learning adaptation (DeepLDA, 2015) as a training-time loss for pixel-level segmentation, departing from the dominant distance-based metric learning paradigm (triplet loss, ProxyNCA, ProxyAnchor, SoftTriple). It builds on the multi-proxy framework of Chan et al. (2023) but replaces the Euclidean distance objective with within-class and between-class scatter matrices, and introduces a Sinkhorn-Knopp-based pixel-to-proxy matching with an equipartition constraint to avoid trivial assignments. The key conceptual departure is that scatter matrices capture inter-variable covariance structure in the feature space, which Euclidean distance (measuring only corresponding-variable relationships) cannot, making them better suited for the highly overlapping feature distributions caused by early smoke's large transparent regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DSA offers a practical, zero-inference-cost plug-in that consistently improves a wide range of segmentation backbones (CNN and ViT) on early smoke, addressing a gap that distance-based losses leave open when positive and negative features overlap substantially. The authors acknowledge that the construction of scatter matrices and the measurement of scatter remain open research questions, and the approach has only been validated on smoke segmentation, leaving unclear whether the scatter-based advantage generalizes to other tasks with similarly overlapping feature distributions (e.g., transparent medical imaging, thin structures). A limitation is that the method is binary-class specific in its current formulation, and the moderate gains (1–2% F1) suggest the ceiling may be constrained by the underlying feature extractor rather than the loss alone.
