## Problem

Pre-trained rPPG models suffer significant performance degradation when deployed in unseen domains due to environmental shifts in illumination, camera parameters, and head motion. Existing Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) methods are designed for classification tasks relying on entropy or pseudo-labels, making them ill-suited for regression tasks like rPPG, and single-instance tuning introduces instability that risks overfitting or catastrophic forgetting. This paper pioneers TTA in rPPG and proposes a bidirectional adaptation framework that addresses both the supervision gap and the stability challenge.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB face video processed into a 2D spatial-temporal feature map (STMap) of shape 256×25×3 (256 frames × 25 spatial patches × 3 channels), resized to 256×64×3 for the ResNet-18 backbone. Adaptation occurs strictly instance-by-instance during inference with batch size 1, requiring no target labels, no source data access, and no pre-training modifications. The model outputs both a BVP signal and a heart rate estimate, with evaluation using MAE, RMSE, and Pearson correlation on HR.

## Method

Bi-TTA combines two expert-knowledge-based self-supervised priors with a bidirectional adaptation strategy. The priors are a Temporal Consistency Loss (TCL) that penalizes HR prediction discrepancies between the original sample and a randomly time-shifted version, and a Spatial Consistency Loss (SCL) that enforces L1 similarity across spatially shifted latent feature maps at four residual-block depths in ResNet-18. The Prospective Adaptation (PA) module applies sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) to the combined prior loss, seeking flat minima in parameter space to filter domain-irrelevant noise from biased instances. The Retrospective Stabilization (RS) module tracks a trend gradient via exponential moving average and, upon detecting gradient oscillation (negative cosine with the trend), backtracks along the orthogonal projection to prevent performance degradation from overfitting or catastrophic forgetting, with an annealing schedule that gradually increases reliance on the trend gradient.

## Results

On the five rPPG datasets under the TTA protocol, Bi-TTA achieves the best or tied-best MAE on 4 of 5 datasets: VIPL-HR (7.09 bpm), PURE (5.02 bpm), UBFC-rPPG (3.53 bpm), and BUAA (1.49 bpm), while tying AdaODM on V4V (9.10 bpm). The method also attains the highest Pearson correlation on four datasets (r=0.63 on VIPL, 0.890 on PURE, 0.891 on UBFC, 0.99 on BUAA). Compared to the strongest domain-generalization baseline (NEST), Bi-TTA reduces MAE by 0.77 bpm on VIPL, 1.69 bpm on PURE, 1.14 bpm on UBFC, and 1.39 bpm on BUAA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Bi-TTA outperforms all 11 baselines on MAE for four of five datasets (VIPL-HR, PURE, UBFC-rPPG, BUAA), with the largest margins over DG methods (e.g., 1.69 bpm lower MAE than NEST on PURE, 1.39 bpm lower than NEST on BUAA). Among TTA baselines, Bi-TTA beats the strongest competitor ConPhys4 by 0.34 bpm MAE on VIPL, 1.07 bpm on PURE, 0.39 bpm on UBFC, and 0.26 bpm on BUAA. On V4V, Bi-TTA ties AdaODM on MAE (9.10 vs 9.10) but trails slightly on RMSE (11.02 vs 10.93) and Pearson r (0.54 vs 0.55), making it the only dataset where it does not achieve the best score across all three metrics. The ablation rows confirm that both the Prospective Adaptation and Retrospective Stabilization modules contribute: removing RS degrades MAE by 0.06–0.14 bpm, and removing PA degrades it by 0.05–0.13 bpm relative to the full Bi-TTA. Overall, TTA methods consistently surpass DG methods across all five datasets, validating the paper's core premise that instance-level adaptation at inference time is more effective than domain-generalization training for rPPG.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper pioneers the application of Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) to remote photoplethysmography (rPPG), a domain previously addressed only through domain generalization (e.g., NEST) or domain adaptation requiring target labels. The Prospective Adaptation module directly repurposes Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) from the classification literature, while the Retrospective Stabilization module introduces a novel gradient-oscillation detection mechanism with no direct prior in TTA. The self-supervised priors (temporal and spatial consistency losses) depart from standard TTA supervision signals (entropy minimization, pseudo-labels) that are ill-suited for regression, instead exploiting domain-specific physiological signal properties. The STMap construction and ResNet-18 backbone follow established rPPG pipelines, positioning the contribution squarely in the adaptation strategy rather than the feature extraction architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing the first TTA protocol and large-scale benchmark for rPPG, this work opens a privacy-preserving adaptation pathway that avoids both source data access and target label annotation—critical constraints in real-world physiological monitoring. The bidirectional (prospective + retrospective) design addresses a genuine gap: single-instance regression adaptation is unstable without mechanisms to both filter noise and prevent catastrophic forgetting. However, the evaluation is limited to ResNet-18 and five datasets, and the ablation shows the RS module's contribution is modest (e.g., MAE 7.15→7.09 on VIPL), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger architectures or more diverse deployment scenarios. Future work should explore real-time computational feasibility and extension to other physiological signals (respiration, HRV).
