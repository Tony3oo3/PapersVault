## Problem

Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) aims to simultaneously classify known categories and discover novel ones from unlabeled data, but standard contrastive learning fails on fine-grained categories where augmented positives can look very different from same-category negatives, causing fragmented clusters and high false-negative rates. The paper identifies that both supervised and unsupervised contrastive objectives treat all positive/negative pairs uniformly, ignoring the semantic hierarchy that naturally exists in fine-grained taxonomies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images split into a labeled set (50% of samples from known categories) and an unlabeled set (remaining known-category samples plus all novel-category samples), following the Vaze et al. protocol. A ViT-B/16 backbone (DINOv1 or DINOv2 pretrained) produces an embedding vector D whose dimensions are partitioned: the first D/2^k segment is dedicated to distinguishing pseudo-labels at hierarchy level k, so finer-grained distinctions use progressively more dimensions. Output is a per-sample category assignment (known or novel) plus the full hierarchy of pseudo-labels used during training.

## Method

SelEx introduces 'self-expertise' through three synergistic components. First, Hierarchical Semi-Supervised K-means (HSSK) generates pseudo-labels at multiple abstraction levels by iteratively merging clusters, with a balanced seeding step that anchors known-category centers and uses stable matching to equalize cluster sizes. Second, the unsupervised self-expertise loss (L_USE) replaces the binary identity target matrix with a graded one (Eq. 10) where negativity weight decays as 1/2^k for samples sharing pseudo-labels at level k, so same-cluster samples are 'hard' negatives while semantically distant ones receive weaker repulsion. Third, the supervised self-expertise loss (L_SSE) applies supervised contrastive learning to progressively larger segments of the embedding at each hierarchy level, so the model first learns coarse distinctions (seen vs. unseen) in a small subspace and refines to fine-grained distinctions in the full space. The theoretical analysis shows that halving the effective category count K at each hierarchy level strictly reduces the KL-divergence upper bound on estimation error (Eq. 5–6 for supervised, Eq. 8–9 for unsupervised), providing a formal justification for the hierarchical decomposition.

## Results

With the DINOv2 backbone, SelEx achieves 87.4% / 85.1% / 88.5% (All/Known/Novel) on CUB-200, 79.8% / 82.3% / 78.6% on FGVC-Aircraft, and 92.6% / 93.5% / 91.8% on Stanford-Cars, yielding an average of 86.6% / 87.0% / 86.3% across the three fine-grained datasets. With DINOv1, SelEx reaches 73.6% / 75.3% / 72.8% on CUB-200 and 63.3% / 71.8% / 59.2% average, outperforming the previous best (InfoSieve at 60.5% / 72.1% / 54.7% average). On Oxford-IIIT Pet, SelEx(DINOv2) attains 95.6% All accuracy, surpassing InfoSieve (91.8%). On coarse-grained datasets (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-100), SelEx achieves 87.1% / 92.3% / 83.0% average, competitive with but not always surpassing the best baselines (e.g., TIDA at 98.2% on CIFAR-10 All).

## Baselines & Comparisons

With DINOv2, SelEx achieves a dominant 14.5-point average improvement over the strongest baseline μGCD (86.6% vs. 72.1% All accuracy across CUB-200, FGVC-Aircraft, and Stanford-Cars), with the largest gap on CUB-200 Novel (88.5% vs. 73.1%). With DINOv1, SelEx wins on CUB-200 (73.6% All vs. 69.4% InfoSieve) and Stanford-Cars (59.2% vs. 59.0% SPTNet), but trails SPTNet on FGVC-Aircraft (57.1% vs. 59.3% All), indicating the hierarchical approach benefits most from datasets with clear taxonomic structure. On Oxford-IIIT Pet, SelEx(DINOv2) reaches 95.6% All accuracy, beating InfoSieve (91.8%) and DCCL (88.1%). On coarse-grained datasets, SelEx is competitive but not uniformly best: TIDA leads on CIFAR-10 (98.2% All) and AMEND achieves the highest average (86.5% vs. SelEx's 83.0%), suggesting the hierarchical mechanism is most impactful for fine-grained rather than coarse-grained categories. Overall, SelEx consistently outperforms all 16 baselines on the three primary fine-grained benchmarks when paired with DINOv2, and is the top or near-top method across all eight evaluated datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) framework of Vaze et al. and the supervised contrastive learning paradigm of Khosla et al. by introducing a hierarchical 'self-expertise' mechanism that operates at multiple abstraction levels simultaneously. It departs from prior GCD methods (InfoSieve, GCA, AMEND) that use flat or single-level pseudo-labels by constructing a full hierarchy of pseudo-labels via Balanced Semi-Supervised K-means, then applying contrastive losses to progressively smaller segments of the embedding vector at each level. The theoretical contribution—showing that hierarchical bifurcation reduces the KL divergence upper bound for both supervised and unsupervised contrastive objectives—provides a principled justification for why this hierarchical decomposition should improve fine-grained discrimination, a gap not addressed by prior hierarchical GCD work (Otholt et al., Banerjee et al.).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a recognized weakness of contrastive learning in fine-grained settings (false negatives from aggressive augmentation) with a theoretically grounded solution that generalizes beyond fine-grained data to coarse-grained benchmarks. The hierarchical embedding segmentation (allocating different latent dimensions to different abstraction levels) is a clean, reusable architectural choice that could inform other semi-supervised or open-world recognition tasks. Limitations include reliance on a meaningful hierarchical structure in the category space, sensitivity to pseudo-label noise in early training, and the DINOv2 comparison set being limited to only three baselines, making the large reported gaps harder to contextualize. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large category spaces (e.g., full ImageNet) and whether the hierarchical structure can be learned end-to-end rather than imposed via k-means.
