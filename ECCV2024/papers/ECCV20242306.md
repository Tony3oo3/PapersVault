## Problem

Existing adversarial style protection methods defend against all style transfer models indiscriminately, causing quality degradation for every model. In practice, artists may wish to authorize specific companies or models to use their artistic style while blocking unauthorized ones, a controlled-authorization requirement that prior work has not addressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242306/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean style image (from WikiArt) and a content image (from MS-COCO) as inputs. A WaveGAN-based generator produces an imperceptible perturbation δ (concentrated in high-frequency bands via DWT) that is added to the style image, yielding an adversarial style image. When this protected style image is paired with a content image and fed into a style transfer model, authorized models output high-quality stylized images while unauthorized models produce visibly degraded results. At test time, only the trained generator is needed to produce perturbations—no access to any style transfer model is required.

## Method

ASPS ensembles three known unauthorized style transfer models (AdaIN, CAST, StyleA2K) and one authorized model (e.g., SANet or AdaAttn), training a WaveGAN generator and fine-tuning the authorized model while keeping unauthorized models frozen. The total loss combines four terms: an L2 perturbation-norm loss for imperceptibility, a pixel-level reconstruction loss that is inverted (1/distance) for unauthorized models to maximize disruption but minimized for the authorized model to preserve quality, a VGG-based perceptual loss constraining feature-space distances, and a style loss that shifts mean/variance of unauthorized model features while aligning those of the authorized model. The authorized model's original training loss is additionally incorporated during fine-tuning to ensure its encoder adapts to the perturbed style images. This one-time training yields a standalone generator that, at inference, directly produces perturbations without requiring access to any downstream style transfer model.

## Results

When SANet is designated as the authorized model, its LPIPS drops from 0.486 to 0.235 (improvement of 0.251) and SSIM rises from 0.432 to 0.622 (improvement of 0.190), while unauthorized models (Adain, CAST, StyleA2K) show only marginal metric changes (e.g., Adain LPIPS: 0.514→0.473). When AdaAttn is authorized, its LPIPS improves from 0.458 to 0.242 (+0.216) and CD from 10.547 to 9.421 (+1.126), again with negligible shifts in unauthorized model outputs. The WaveGAN generator (0.794M params) outperforms U-Net (31.032M) and Flow (19.529M) generators in both authorized-model quality and unauthorized-model degradation, and fine-tuning the authorized model yields substantially better protection than training the generator alone (e.g., SANet LPIPS: 0.404 without fine-tuning vs. 0.236 with fine-tuning).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against other style-protection methods directly; instead, it contrasts its authorization-aware scheme against a no-authorization baseline where all five style transfer models (Adain, CAST, StyleA2K, SANet, AdaAttn) are treated as unauthorized. Under the no-authorization setting, all models suffer significant quality degradation (e.g., CAST SSIM drops to 0.180, SANet LPIPS rises to 0.486), confirming that the adversarial perturbations effectively disrupt style transfer universally. When SANet is designated as authorized, its LPIPS improves by 0.251 and SSIM by 0.190 relative to the no-authorization baseline, while the three known unauthorized models show only ±0.04 or less change in LPIPS, demonstrating selective protection. Similarly, designating AdaAttn as authorized yields a 0.216 LPIPS improvement and 1.126 CD improvement for AdaAttn with negligible impact on unauthorized models. The unknown unauthorized model (the one not used in ensemble training) also shows minimal metric shifts, indicating the method generalizes to unseen models. In the generator ablation, WaveGAN (0.794M params) achieves the best authorized-model LPIPS (0.236 for SANet) while simultaneously degrading unauthorized outputs more than U-Net (0.240) and Flow (0.257), despite being 39× and 25× smaller respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial style protection framework of Li et al. (WACV 2024), which defended against all style transfer models indiscriminately, by introducing a controlled authorization mechanism that distinguishes between authorized and unauthorized models. For perturbation generation, it repurposes the DWT-based UNet generator from WaveGAN, leveraging frequency-domain properties to produce imperceptible perturbations concentrated in high-frequency bands. The method departs from prior ensemble-attack approaches by jointly fine-tuning an authorized model alongside the generator, using a combined loss (reconstruction, perceptual, and style) that simultaneously degrades unauthorized outputs and preserves authorized ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The controlled-authorization framing addresses a practical gap in style protection: artists may license their style to specific companies while preventing broader misuse, and the framework avoids the risk of style leakage that comes from sharing raw style images. The authors note the framework generalizes beyond style transfer to deepfake defense and generative AI scenarios where selective access is needed. However, the approach requires white-box access to the authorized model's parameters and is validated on only five models with 200 test images, leaving open questions about scalability to many authorized parties, robustness to model updates, and performance against truly unseen model architectures.
