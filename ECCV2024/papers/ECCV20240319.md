## Problem

Existing diffusion model-based super-resolution methods apply a uniform number of denoising timesteps to all image regions, wasting computational resources on reconstructing invisible details in background areas that barely change with additional steps. This paper identifies that different regions (e.g., sky vs. building surfaces) require fundamentally different timesteps for satisfactory reconstruction, and that no prior DM-based SR method accounts for this region-wise variation in computational cost.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a low-resolution RGB image of arbitrary resolution and produces a corresponding high-resolution RGB image. Because the underlying Stable Diffusion 2.1-base model has a fixed 512×512 input resolution, the input is sliced into overlapping sub-regions of that size, each processed independently through the adaptive denoising pipeline. Sub-regions are then stitched back together in latent space using Gaussian weight maps to avoid pixel-space discontinuities at boundaries.

## Method

AdaDiffSR builds on a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion 2.1-base and introduces three components for adaptive, region-aware acceleration. First, a Multi-metrics Latent Entropy (MMLE) module computes per-timestep information gain by combining four full-reference IQA metrics (PSNR, LPIPS, AHIQ, NLPD) and two no-reference metrics (BRISQUE, MUSIQ), estimated by a lightweight CNN regressor trained with frozen diffusion weights. Second, a Dynamic Timesteps Sampling Strategy (DTSS) classifies each sub-region into stable, growing, or saturated categories based on the information gain trajectory, then applies different skip-step intervals (5, 10, 15, 20 DDPM steps) so that background regions finish early while foreground regions receive finer-grained denoising. Third, a Progressive Feature Injection (PFJ) module uses a small CNN to predict modulation coefficients from the current information gain, dynamically scaling the original image features injected into the denoising process to balance fidelity against the generative realism of the diffusion prior.

## Results

On DRealSR, AdaDiffSR achieves the best LPIPS (0.2627) and SSIM (0.8415) among all compared methods, with a 6.8% LPIPS improvement over Real-ESRGAN+ and 25.5% over GAN-based approaches. On DIV2K Valid, it attains the best MUSIQ (68.81) and LPIPS (0.2153). On DPED-iPhone (no-reference), AdaDiffSR achieves the best NIQE (3.09) and TOPIQ (0.4925). In terms of efficiency, AdaDiffSR requires 1.5× fewer inference time (4.8s vs 7.2s at 512×512) and 2.7× fewer FLOPs (751.4G vs 1004.2G) compared to StableSR while maintaining comparable reconstruction quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest DM-based competitor StableSR [51], AdaDiffSR wins on SSIM across all paired datasets (e.g., 0.8415 vs 0.8326 on DRealSR) and LPIPS on DRealSR (0.2627 vs 0.2853), while StableSR edges ahead on LPIPS for RealSR (0.2543 vs 0.2595) and AHIQ (0.3160 vs 0.3145). Compared to ResShift [67], AdaDiffSR substantially outperforms on LPIPS for DRealSR (0.2627 vs 0.5408) and DIV2K (0.2153 vs 0.4406), though ResShift achieves higher PSNR on DRealSR (27.87 vs 25.67) and MUSIQ (42.68 vs 38.66). Against GAN-based methods (Real-ESRGAN+ [55], BSRGAN [70]), AdaDiffSR achieves 6.8% lower LPIPS on DRealSR and comparable or better perceptual scores (MUSIQ, AHIQ) while trailing on PSNR/SSIM. On the no-reference DPED-iPhone benchmark, AdaDiffSR achieves the best NIQE (3.09) and TOPIQ (0.4925), outperforming FeMaSR [7] (NIQE 5.09, TOPIQ 0.5439) and all other methods. In terms of efficiency, AdaDiffSR runs in 4.8s at 512×512 versus 7.2s for StableSR and 19.7s for ResShift, with 2.7× fewer FLOPs than StableSR, making it the fastest DM-based SR method evaluated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of diffusion-model-based super-resolution methods (SR3, StableSR, ResShift) that fine-tune pre-trained latent diffusion models for real-world SR. It departs from prior DMs-based SR work by introducing a region-aware, timestep-adaptive inference strategy, drawing conceptual inspiration from adaptive inference literature (ClassSR, APE) that adjusts network depth per patch, but transposing that idea to the denoising-timestep dimension. The MMLE module's use of multiple IQA metrics to quantify latent information gain during denoising is a novel mechanism not present in prior DMs-based SR pipelines, and the PFJ module addresses the fidelity-realism trade-off that plagues generative SR methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in DMs-based SR: uniform timesteps waste compute on regions (e.g., sky, flat backgrounds) that require minimal denoising. The 1.5× speedup over StableSR with comparable perceptual quality is a meaningful efficiency gain for deployment, and the dynamic-timestep framework could generalize to other diffusion-based image-to-image tasks. However, the method remains substantially slower than CNN/GAN-based SR, and the static fixed-resolution slicing strategy partially undermines the region-aware motivation. Open questions include whether the denoising process itself can be further compressed (e.g., via distillation) and whether truly content-aware segmentation can replace the fixed patching without incurring the overhead of SAM-family models.
