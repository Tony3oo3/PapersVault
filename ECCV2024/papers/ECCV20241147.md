## Problem

Directly transferring dense RGB pre-training (patch-level similarity) to event camera data yields subpar performance because event images are spatially sparse, with many patches containing no meaningful information, which destabilizes training and introduces background noise. Existing event-specific self-supervised methods (e.g., ECDP, E-CLIP) rely on paired RGB images and enforce only image-level similarity, capturing overall structure but neglecting the fine-grained pixel-level features essential for dense prediction tasks like segmentation, depth estimation, and optical flow.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241147/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw asynchronous event streams converted into event images (480×640 resolution), which are tiled into N patches and augmented via 2D affine transformation, Gaussian blur, and color jitter to produce a distorted counterpart. During pre-training, the student network receives a randomly masked version of the augmented image while the teacher sees the full image. After pre-training, the backbone is transferred and fine-tuned to produce per-pixel outputs: semantic segmentation masks, depth maps, or dense optical flow fields on downstream event benchmarks.

## Method

The framework follows a DINOv2-style teacher-student architecture with EMA-updated teacher, but introduces a novel context-level similarity loss to combat event image sparsity. Specifically, K-means clustering is performed on the teacher's patch features to mine K semantic contexts (e.g., road, building, sky), and the resulting patch-to-context assignments are transferred to the student via the known affine correspondence between the two views. Features assigned to each context are aggregated via attention pooling into context embeddings, and a cross-entropy loss enforces similarity between student and teacher context embeddings. The total objective combines three terms: patch-level masked reconstruction (cross-entropy on individual patch embeddings), context-level similarity (cross-entropy on pooled context embeddings, weighted by λ₁=0.1), and image-level similarity (cross-entropy on global embeddings, weighted by λ₂=0.9). The pre-training dataset E-TartanAir is synthesized from TartanAir to provide diverse motion patterns and scenes beyond the limited square/vertical/horizontal motions of N-ImageNet.

## Results

On semantic segmentation, the proposed method with Swin-T/7 backbone achieves 62.525% mIoU and 74.301% mACC on DDD17, and 61.250% mIoU and 69.620% mACC on DSEC, surpassing the previous best (ESS) by over 8% mIoU on DSEC. For optical flow on MVSEC, the Swin-T/7 variant reduces endpoint error to 0.362/0.445/0.417 pixels (vs. 0.748/1.388/1.132 for DCEIFlow) with outlier ratios below 0.04%. On DSEC-Flow, the method achieves EPE 0.697 and AE 2.575, outperforming the previous top-ranked IDNet (0.719/2.723). For depth estimation on MVSEC, the ResNet50 variant attains RMS 6.805 and RMSlog 0.359, improving over HMNet (7.534/0.397) despite using only self-supervised pre-training without ground-truth depth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method consistently outperforms all baselines across three backbone families (ResNet50, ViT-S/16, Swin-T/7) on all three dense prediction tasks. Against the closest event-specific baseline ECDP [47], the proposed method gains +3.77% mIoU on DDD17 (ResNet50) and +8.73% mIoU on DSEC (Swin-T/7), demonstrating that context-level similarity and the E-TartanAir pre-training dataset are both critical. Against RGB pre-training methods (SimCLR, MoCo-v2, DenseCL, DINOv2), the advantage is even larger: DINOv2 trained on LVD-142M achieves only 53.846% mIoU on DDD17 with ViT-S/16, while the proposed method reaches 62.525% with Swin-T/7. On optical flow (MVSEC), the Swin-T/7 variant reduces EPE by ~50% compared to DCEIFlow [40] (0.362 vs. 0.748 on indoor_flying1) and cuts outlier ratios from 0.6% to 0.035%. On DSEC-Flow, the method surpasses the previous top-ranked IDNet [44] by 0.022 EPE and 0.148 AE. For depth estimation, all three backbone variants beat the supervised HMNet [23] by 0.6–0.7 in RMS, despite HMNet using ground-truth depth for pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DINOv2 teacher-student framework (EMA teacher, masked patch reconstruction via cross-entropy, projection heads) and adapts it to event camera data. It departs from prior event-specific pre-training methods (ECDP, E-CLIP) that rely on paired RGB images and CLIP guidance, which the authors argue focus on image-level structure at the expense of pixel-level detail needed for dense prediction. The key conceptual departure is introducing a context-level similarity loss that uses online K-means clustering to group sparse event patches into semantically coherent contexts, operating at an intermediate granularity between individual patches and the full image to mitigate the spatial sparsity inherent in event images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The context-level similarity loss addresses a fundamental limitation of applying dense RGB pre-training paradigms to event data: most event patches carry little or no information, making patch-level supervision noisy and unstable. The technique's generalizability is demonstrated by its plug-and-play improvement when added to ECDP, suggesting it could benefit other sparse-modality pre-training settings. The E-TartanAir dataset fills a practical gap by providing diverse motion patterns absent from N-ImageNet. However, the method still requires task-specific fine-tuning rather than zero-shot transfer, and the authors note the broader vision of extending this to an event-vision-language foundation model remains open.
