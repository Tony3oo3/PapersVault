## Problem

Spacecraft Terrain Relative Navigation requires recognizing individual terrain landmarks (e.g., specific craters) from in-situ imagery, but current metric learning approaches fail because their view-unaware attention mechanisms cannot handle the extreme multi-view appearance changes (full 360° rotation, translation, illumination shift) inherent to remote-sensing observation geometry. The inter-class similarity between individual crater instances is far more subtle than in terrestrial fine-grained benchmarks, and no existing loss or architecture explicitly constrains attention consistency across views.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB patch image of a terrain feature (crater, stadium, etc.) and applies SimCLR-style augmentation (random brightness, rotation, translation) to produce two views per sample. The encoder is a single-shot ResNeXt-101 with rotation-invariant coordinate convolutions and Coordinate Attention, producing a 512-d embedding via a GeM-pooling + linear + PReLU projection head. At inference, embeddings are matched against a sequentially growing Faiss database using cosine similarity with a 0.9 threshold; no multi-stage re-ranking or region proposals are used, keeping the pipeline lightweight for onboard spaceflight hardware.

## Method

The core contribution is Multi-view Attention Regularizations (MARs), a soft learning constraint that aligns the 'what' (channel) and 'where' (spatial) focus of attention between two augmented views of the same landmark. For each ResNeXt block, attention maps are pose-normalized via inverse augmentation transforms, channel-reduced through a 1×1 convolution, then projected into separate metric spaces by dedicated mini projection heads (one for channel, one each for height and width pooling). A cosine similarity loss is applied between the two views' channel embeddings and spatial embeddings, weighted by γ_Ch and γ_Sp, and summed across all blocks. This MARs loss is added to a standard metric learning loss (evaluated across nine losses including Proxy Anchor, NTXent, and SupCon), with hard sample mining via multi-similarity indices. The architecture additionally replaces standard convolutions with RIC-C layers for explicit rotational equivariance and swaps SE attention for Coordinate Attention to enable spatial awareness, combining learned and explicit equivariance with the attention alignment constraint.

## Results

On the Incremental Recall@1 task (the most challenging sequential recognition setting), MARs with Proxy Anchor achieves 78.06% on Earth Stadium, 71.10% on Mars Crater, and 94.78% on Moon Crater, representing best-in-class performance across all three datasets. The headline improvement is on Mars Crater where MARs boosts Proxy Anchor from 10.45% (RIC CA) to 71.10%, an ~85% relative gain. For Recall@1, MARs reaches 100% on Earth Stadium and Moon Crater (Proxy Anchor) and 99.14% on Mars Crater (Proxy Synthesis). On Moon Navigation, MARs with Proxy Anchor achieves 66.31% accuracy, the highest among all model/loss combinations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three architecture variants: conv2d SE (standard convolutions + SE channel attention, learned equivariance only), RIC CA (rotation-invariant convolutions + Coordinate Attention, learned + explicit equivariance), and MARs (RIC CA + multi-view attention regularization). MARs is best-in-class on Incremental Recall@1 for every dataset with Proxy Anchor, and substantially outperforms RIC CA on Mars Crater (71.10% vs. 10.45%, ~85% relative improvement) where explicit equivariance alone is insufficient. On Earth Stadium, conv2d SE is competitive with RIC CA for several losses (e.g., Circle, DR-MS) but MARs dominates with Proxy Anchor (78.06% vs. 72.86% vs. 4.48%). On Moon Crater, MARs improves 6 out of 9 losses over RIC CA for Incremental Recall@1, with the largest gain on SupCon (84.11% vs. 74.11%). However, MARs does not universally help: on Mars Crater with DR-MS, MARs (4.04%) underperforms RIC CA (48.48%), and on Moon Navigation, conv2d SE retains the top spot for 4/9 losses, suggesting that fully explicit equivariance can be counterproductive when training data is sufficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of metric learning for fine-grained recognition (FGVC, VPR, Re-ID) and spacecraft Terrain Relative Navigation (TRN), drawing on RIC-CNN for explicit rotational equivariance, Coordinate Attention for spatial awareness, and the SimCLR augmentation framework for multi-view batch construction. It departs from prior metric learning work by identifying the view-unaware attention mechanism as a specific failure mode when the same geological feature (e.g., a crater) is observed from unconstrained viewpoints, and proposes MARs as a soft regularization that aligns channel and spatial attention across views in auxiliary metric spaces. The paper also positions itself against the broader TRN literature (RETINA, NFT, MP-LVS) by arguing that template matching is too costly and sparse, and that lightweight single-shot learned descriptors are needed for in-situ operation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of metric learning for space terrain landmark description, and the introduction of Luna-1 (5,067 Moon crater samples plus 2,161 navigation frames from real NASA data) fills a critical gap—only two space landmark datasets existed at the time of writing. The finding that MARs can rescue losses that otherwise fail to converge (e.g., Subcenter ArcFace on Moon Crater) and that the regularization magnitude has surprisingly little effect on optimization opens questions about the role of attention consistency as a general inductive bias for multi-view recognition. Limitations include evaluation on a single architecture (ResNeXt-101), only three datasets, and the highly variable performance across losses, suggesting the interaction between attention alignment and embedding geometry is not yet fully understood.
