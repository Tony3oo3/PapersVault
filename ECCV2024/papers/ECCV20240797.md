## Problem

Federated Class Continual Learning (FCCL) must adapt to new classes across distributed clients without forgetting old ones, but privacy constraints prohibit storing or replaying historical data. Existing FCCL methods that generate replay data via federated GAN training or data-free knowledge distillation suffer from unstable generator training and produce low-quality, adversarial-like samples that provide limited guidance to the classifier.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240797/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-100, 64×64 TinyImageNet) and a compact learnable class embedding vector as input to a frozen pre-trained Latent Diffusion Model, producing both generated images for replay and class labels from a ResNet-18 classifier. The class embedding is a single vector in the LDM's text-embedding space (concatenated with a frozen prompt like 'a photo of') that encapsulates the visual identity of an entire class, requiring only this small parameter to be optimized and communicated across clients rather than the full generative model.

## Method

DDDR operates in two phases. In Federated Class Inversion, a frozen LDM (pre-trained on LAION-400M) is used to 'reverse-engineer' each class: a learnable embedding v is optimized by minimizing the LDM's denoising loss on that class's local images, with v concatenated to a frozen text prompt to form the conditioning; only v is communicated and aggregated via FedAvg across clients, drastically reducing computation versus training a full diffusion model. In Replay-Augmented Training, saved class embeddings from all prior tasks guide the LDM to generate images for both historical and current classes, which are then used to train a ResNet-18 classifier under four losses: cross-entropy on current-task real and generated data, a supervised contrastive loss that narrows the feature-space gap between generated and real samples within each class, cross-entropy on historical generated data, and knowledge distillation from the previous task's model. The contrastive loss is key to making the classifier generalize across the generated-data domain and the real-data domain, indirectly improving how well generated replay data represents real data.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (non-IID, T=5), DDDR achieves 48.45% average accuracy and 0.26 forgetting measure, outperforming the previous SOTA MFCL (41.19% / 0.34) by 7.3 points in accuracy and 0.08 in forgetting. On Tiny-ImageNet (non-IID, T=5), DDDR reaches 23.96% accuracy and 0.33 FM, surpassing MFCL (13.35% / 0.48) by over 10 points in accuracy. Across all settings (IID/non-IID, T=5/T=10, both datasets), DDDR consistently achieves the highest average accuracy and lowest forgetting measure among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDDR outperforms all three baselines across every experimental setting on both CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet. Against MFCL (the prior SOTA using data-free knowledge distillation with a refined generator loss), DDDR gains 7.3–12.3 points in average accuracy and reduces the forgetting measure by 0.05–0.25, with the largest margins on Tiny-ImageNet where MFCL's generated data quality degrades more severely. Against Target (another data-free distillation approach), the gap is even wider: DDDR leads by 14–17 points in accuracy on CIFAR-100 and 6–12 points on Tiny-ImageNet, reflecting Target's tendency to produce adversarial-like samples with poor distributional fidelity. Against FedEWC (regularization-based EWC in a federated setting), DDDR achieves roughly 2–3× the accuracy and halves the forgetting measure, confirming that regularization alone cannot compensate for the absence of data-level replay. The paper attributes these gains to the superior generation quality of the pre-trained LDM guided by Federated Class Inversion, combined with the contrastive learning loss that bridges the feature gap between generated and real data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the Federated Class Continual Learning (FCCL) line of work, departing from prior generative-replay methods that train GANs (FedCIL) or use data-free knowledge distillation (Target, MFCL) to synthesize historical data. Its core methodological departure is Federated Class Inversion: rather than training a new generator, it freezes a pre-trained Latent Diffusion Model (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) and searches for a compact class embedding in the model's text-conditioning input space, drawing on the textual-inversion paradigm (Gal et al., ICLR 2022) and image-editing techniques. A supervised contrastive learning objective is additionally introduced to close the feature-space gap between diffusion-generated and real data, a component absent from prior FCCL generative-replay methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a frozen, off-the-shelf diffusion model can serve as a high-quality replay source with only a small class-embedding vector to optimize and communicate, DDDR offers a resource-efficient template for generative replay in privacy-constrained federated settings. The ablation revealing that the contrastive loss is beneficial only when paired with generated current-task data (and actively harmful without it) highlights a non-trivial interaction that future replay-based methods should account for. Open questions include scalability beyond five clients and small datasets (CIFAR-100, TinyImageNet), dependence on the massive LAION-400M pre-training of the LDM, and whether the inversion approach generalizes to other diffusion architectures or non-image modalities.
