## Problem

Self-explainable models (SEMs) provide transparent predictions via prototypes but cannot natively generate counterfactual explanations without post-hoc methods that compromise transparency. Conversely, existing counterfactual explanation methods either require post-hoc training of a separate generator or demand expensive inference-time optimization, and typically rely on black-box classifiers. The paper bridges this gap by unifying a transparent prototype-based classifier with closed-form counterfactual generation in a single end-to-end trained model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241417/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (ranging from 28×28 MNIST to 1024×1024 FFHQ) and, for counterfactual generation, a user-specified target confidence value (expressed as a probability p_c ∈ [0.05, 0.95]). It outputs a class prediction with confidence, a reconstructed image, a counterfactual image that achieves the requested confidence, and visualizable class prototypes in input space. Unlike iterative CF methods, the counterfactual is produced analytically via a single linear shift in latent space followed by one decoder pass, requiring no per-sample optimization.

## Method

GdVAE is a conditional VAE with two coupled factorizations: a class-conditional prior p_θ(z|y) that defines the autoencoder (M1) and a Gaussian discriminant analysis classifier p_θ(y|z) that reuses the prior's means as class prototypes and covariances for Mahalanobis-distance-based scoring (M2), introducing no additional trainable parameters. At inference, an EM procedure marginalizes over the unknown class by iteratively sampling latent codes and applying the fixed GDA classifier. Counterfactuals are generated in closed form: local CFs shift the latent code along the discriminant gradient w (L2) or the Mahalanobis-adjusted direction Σ_z·w (Riemannian), while global CFs move toward the opposing class prototype. A consistency loss regularizes the latent space by penalizing the KL divergence between the encoder's output on a CF image and the analytically shifted latent distribution, ensuring the classifier reproduces the desired confidence when the CF is re-encoded.

## Results

On CelebA-Smiling, GdVAE's local methods (local-M and local-L2) achieve the best proximity (MSE 0.92 and 0.99 ×10⁻²) and best realism (FID 85.56 and 85.52), outperforming ECINN (FID 95.35, MSE 1.16) and EBPE (FID 191.67, MSE 1.54). On MNIST-Binary 0/1, local-M achieves the best consistency accuracy (44.6%) and second-best Pearson correlation (0.95), while the global method attains the highest correlation (0.97) and accuracy (54.2%) at the cost of realism (FID 125.45). For predictive performance, GdVAE's EM-based inference matches the black-box baseline on MNIST (99.0% vs. 99.3% ACC) and CelebA (96.7% vs. 96.7%), with a 4-point gap on CIFAR-10 (65.1% vs. 69.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CelebA-Smiling, GdVAE's local methods dominate in proximity (MSE 0.92–0.99 vs. ECINN's 1.16 and EBPE's 1.54) and realism (FID ~85.5 vs. ECINN's 95.35, C3LT's 101.46, and EBPE's 191.67), while EBPE leads in consistency MSE (1.22 vs. 3.51 for local-M). On MNIST-Binary 0/1, local-M matches EBPE on consistency accuracy (44.6%) and surpasses ECINN (33.0%) and C3LT (3.6%), with the global method achieving the highest correlation (0.97) and accuracy (54.2%) but at a realism cost (FID 125.45 vs. 89.91 for local-M). EBPE achieves the best consistency MSE on MNIST (0.50) but has the worst proximity (25.73), indicating large image modifications. ECINN provides the best proximity on MNIST (3.47) but trails GdVAE in consistency (ρ_p 0.93 vs. 0.95) and realism (FID 87.25 vs. 89.91). C3LT achieves the best FID on MNIST (57.09) but has very poor consistency accuracy (3.6%), suggesting its CFs do not reliably shift classifier confidence. Overall, GdVAE's local methods offer the best balance of all three CF criteria on CelebA, while the global method trades realism for superior consistency and prototype-level interpretability.

## Relation to Prior Work

GdVAE unifies two previously separate research threads: prototype-based self-explainable models (ProtoVAE, ProtoPNet, TesNET) and post-hoc counterfactual explanation methods (EBPE, C3LT, ECINN). It builds on the CVAE architecture [45] and Gaussian discriminant analysis [18], but departs from ProtoVAE by replacing learned similarity scores with a parameter-free GDA classifier whose linear discriminant yields a closed-form CF solution. Unlike ECINN, which requires post-hoc analysis to approximate the decision function, GdVAE's CF explainer is analytically derived from the classifier itself, eliminating the transparency gap. The EM-based inference for the unknown-class marginal draws on semi-supervised VAE techniques [28] but applies them to the GDA classification loop rather than standard GMM parameter estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a transparent, parameter-free classifier can generate counterfactuals of quality competitive with dedicated post-hoc methods, removing the need for separate CF training or inference-time optimization. This is practically significant for deployment settings where interpretability is a hard requirement and additional training pipelines are infeasible. However, the CF generation is currently limited to binary classification, the consistency-realism trade-off remains unresolved (global CFs are more consistent but blurrier), and the EM inference is approximate. Open questions include extending the closed-form CF solution to multi-class settings, whether the linear discriminant assumption holds for more complex decision boundaries, and how the approach scales to open-vocabulary or compositional explanation tasks.
