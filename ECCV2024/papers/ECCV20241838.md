## Problem

Existing watermarking strategies for diffusion models are either post-hoc (requiring a separate injection step that is vulnerable to circumvention and adds computational overhead) or confined to the latent decoder (applicable only to latent-space diffusion and easily defeated by retraining the decoder). No prior method embeds user-specific fingerprints directly into the generation process in a scalable, black-box API setting where the model provider serves many users without per-user fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241838/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the text-to-image model (Stable Diffusion V1.4, 512×512), the input is a text prompt plus a 48-bit user-specific binary watermark; for the unconditional model (256×256 ImageNet DDPM), the input is Gaussian noise plus the same watermark. The watermark is projected through a linear layer into a channel-expanded tensor (C̃×H×W) and concatenated with the latent at every denoising step. The output is a watermarked image from which a pre-trained decoder D recovers the watermark bits, enabling both binary detection (AUC) and multi-user identification (tracing accuracy among up to 10⁶ registered users).

## Method

WaDiff expands the input channels of a pre-trained diffusion model to accept a projected watermark vector concatenated with the noisy latent at each denoising step, integrating fingerprinting directly into the generation process rather than applying it post-hoc. Training uses two objectives: a message retrieval loss that ensures the single-step-reversed image x₀ᵗ carries a watermark recoverable by a pre-trained decoder D, and a consistency loss that aligns the watermarked model's noise prediction with the frozen original model's prediction. A threshold τ partitions the denoising schedule: for t > τ a null watermark is used (preserving cross-user consistency), while for t ≤ τ the actual user watermark is injected alongside the retrieval loss. Only the watermark projector and first input block are fine-tuned (θ_head), keeping the remaining architecture frozen to avoid quality degradation. A StegaStamp-style encoder-decoder pair is pre-trained with augmentation-aware training to provide the reference decoder D used during fine-tuning and at inference time.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion, WaDiff achieves a detection AUC of 0.999 and an average tracing accuracy of 96.13% across user pools of 10⁴–10⁶, with SSIM of 0.999 between watermarked images and only a +0.41 FID increase over the original model. On the 256×256 ImageNet diffusion model, WaDiff reaches AUC 1.000 and 99.28% average tracing accuracy with SSIM 0.997 and a mere +0.08 FID difference. Under combined data augmentations (resize + JPEG + color jitter), WaDiff retains 93.02% tracing accuracy among 10⁴ users on Stable Diffusion and 91.88% on ImageNet, while maintaining AUC ≥ 0.999 across all individual augmentations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the traditional DwtDct baseline, WaDiff substantially outperforms on both detection (AUC 0.999 vs. 0.917 on Stable Diffusion; 1.000 vs. 0.936 on ImageNet) and identification (96.13% vs. 74.63% average tracing on Stable Diffusion; 99.28% vs. 68.20% on ImageNet). Compared to the post-hoc StegaStamp, WaDiff trades a small tracing-accuracy gap (96.13% vs. 99.97% on Stable Diffusion; 99.28% vs. 99.98% on ImageNet) for a key practical advantage: the watermark is embedded during generation rather than as a separate post-processing step, making it harder to circumvent if the model is leaked. Against Tree-Ring (both Rand and Rings variants), WaDiff achieves a dramatic improvement in identification, reaching 96–99% tracing accuracy where Tree-Ring scores near 0% across all user-pool sizes, while also achieving far better image consistency (SSIM 0.999 vs. 0.457–0.652). In adaptive-attack experiments, WaDiff retains 77.50% tracing accuracy under a Diffusion-based purification attack (vs. 0.10% for StegaStamp) and 97.96% under a multi-message injection attack (vs. 0.02% for StegaStamp), confirming the robustness advantage of integrating the watermark into the generation process.

## Relation to Prior Work

WaDiff positions itself against three lines of prior work: post-hoc watermarking (DWT/DCT, StegaStamp), latent-decoder fingerprinting (Stable Signature and its extensions), and training-free frequency-domain fingerprinting (Tree-Ring). It departs from all three by treating the user watermark as a conditioned input to the diffusion model via channel expansion, integrating fingerprinting directly into the denoising process rather than as a post-processing step or a modification to a single decoder. The method builds on the DDPM/LDM framework (Ho et al., Rombach et al.) and borrows the pre-trained encoder-decoder pair from Stable Signature, but replaces the per-user decoder fine-tuning with a single fine-tuned model that accepts arbitrary watermark inputs at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically urgent need: attributing AI-generated images to specific users in black-box API settings without per-user model customization. The key open question is whether this approach generalizes beyond the two tested architectures (Stable Diffusion V1.4 and 256×256 ImageNet DDPM) to newer, larger, or video/3D diffusion models, and whether the fine-tuning requirement (even if limited to the first block) remains feasible as models grow. The paper acknowledges that performance is still slightly below post-hoc StegaStamp in some settings, and the robustness gap under aggressive adaptive attacks (Diff Attack drops accuracy to ~49% at 10^6 users) suggests the watermark is not yet tamper-proof. Broader implications include regulatory compliance for generative AI services and the feasibility of large-scale forensic auditing.
