## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves real-time rendering and fast training but stores 59 parameters per Gaussian across millions of Gaussians, making models an order of magnitude larger than NeRF-based approaches. This storage overhead limits deployment on edge devices, AR/VR headsets, and scenarios requiring communication or co-storage of multiple scene models. The paper asks how to compress 3DGS representations by 40–50× without sacrificing the rendering speed that distinguishes it from NeRF methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240852/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the same inputs as standard 3DGS: multi-view RGB images and an SfM-initialized point cloud. The output is a compressed model consisting of four small codebooks (for DC color, spherical harmonics, scale, and rotation), per-Gaussian integer code indices (further compressed via sorting and run-length encoding), and unquantized 3D position and opacity values. At inference, the codebook acts as a lookup table so each Gaussian's parameters are reconstructed from its index, reducing both storage and inference-time memory. The rendered output is a novel-view image produced at 2–3× the FPS of uncompressed 3DGS.

## Method

CompGS applies K-means vector quantization during training via a quantization-aware scheme: non-quantized parameters are maintained as the learnable state, but the forward pass substitutes centroid values for rendering and loss computation, while the backward pass propagates gradients to the non-quantized parameters through a straight-through estimator. To limit K-means overhead, centroids are updated every iteration but cluster assignments are refreshed only every 100 iterations. Parameters are grouped by semantic type (DC color, SH, scale, rotation) and quantized with independent codebooks, while position and opacity are left unquantized to avoid overlapping Gaussians and preserve per-Gaussian visibility. Indices are further compressed by sorting Gaussians by one index and storing run-length counts instead of individual integers. An L1 opacity regularizer (λ_reg = 10⁻⁷) is applied mid-training to drive many Gaussians to zero opacity, after which they are pruned, reducing Gaussian count by 3.5–5× and further cutting both storage and rendering time.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF360, CompGS 32K achieves PSNR 27.12, SSIM 0.806, and LPIPS 0.240 at 344 FPS with only 19 MB memory, compared to 3DGS's PSNR 27.42 at 149 FPS and 778 MB — a ~41× compression with a 0.3 dB PSNR drop and 2.3× speedup. On Tanks&Temples, CompGS 32K reaches PSNR 23.44 at 475 FPS and 13 MB versus 3DGS's PSNR 23.68 at 206 FPS and 433 MB (~33× compression, 2.3× speedup). The post-training bit-quantized variant (CompGS 32K BitQ) further reduces memory to 12 MB (Mip-NeRF360) and 8 MB (Tanks&Temples) with negligible additional quality loss. On the large-scale DL3DV-140 benchmark, CompGS 32K achieves ~30× compression (10 MB vs 291 MB) with a 0.64 dB PSNR drop.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the uncompressed 3DGS baseline, CompGS 32K sacrifices only 0.24–0.3 dB PSNR while achieving 33–41× memory reduction and 2.3× rendering speedup, making it the most favorable quality-to-compression tradeoff among all compared methods. Versus other 3DGS compression methods (LightGaussian, CGR, CGS), CompGS 32K matches or exceeds their PSNR (27.12 vs. 27.28/27.03/26.98 on Mip-NeRF360) while being 2–2.2× smaller in memory (19 MB vs. 42/29.1/28.8 MB) and 1.6–2.7× faster in rendering (344 FPS vs. 209/128/– FPS). Compared to NeRF-based methods, CompGS matches Mip-NeRF360's quality (PSNR 27.12 vs. 27.69) at 5,700× higher rendering speed (344 vs. 0.06 FPS) and 29× faster training (29.4 min vs. 48 h), while InstantNGP is smaller (13 MB) but lags by ~1.8 dB PSNR. On the large-scale DL3DV-140 dataset, CompGS 32K achieves 28.42 PSNR at 10 MB versus 3DGS's 29.06 PSNR at 291 MB, confirming the compression generalizes to scenes an order of magnitude larger than standard benchmarks. The bit-quantized variant (CompGS 32K BitQ) pushes compression to 65× on Mip-NeRF360 (12 MB) with only an additional 0.15 dB PSNR cost, approaching the memory footprint of NeRF models while retaining real-time rendering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by applying quantization-aware training (straight-through estimator) with K-means vector quantization to the Gaussian parameters during optimization, rather than compressing a pre-trained model. It departs from prior NeRF compression work (VQRF, VQAD) that targets grid-based or hash-based representations, instead operating on the explicit Gaussian parameter space unique to 3DGS. The approach is concurrent with LightGaussian, CGR, and CGS, which also combine VQ and pruning for 3DGS compression, but CompGS distinguishes itself by integrating quantization into the training loop and adding opacity regularization with run-length encoding of sorted indices for further compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 40–50× storage reduction and 2–3× faster rendering with only ~0.3 dB PSNR drop makes 3DGS viable on memory-constrained devices (AR/VR headsets, mobile), addressing the primary practical barrier to 3DGS adoption. A key limitation is that non-quantized position and opacity parameters still consume 68–81% of model memory, capping further compression gains; quantizing position without severe quality loss remains an open problem. The finding that a codebook trained on one scene generalizes well to others suggests potential for amortizing codebook learning across scene collections, though this is only briefly explored. The simplicity of the method (K-means + STE + L1 opacity penalty) makes it easy to adopt, but the K-means overhead during training (1.4–1.7× for 32K codebook) is a practical tradeoff that may matter for large-scale scene libraries.
