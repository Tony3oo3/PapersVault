## Problem

Discriminative models dominate camouflaged object detection but suffer from limited probabilistic understanding, poor generalization to unseen camouflage patterns, vulnerability to noise, and overfitting. The paper investigates whether generative diffusion models, which possess stronger scene comprehension and iterative refinement capabilities, can overcome these limitations and provide a more robust paradigm for detecting objects seamlessly blended into their surroundings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241410/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 384×384) as input, which is encoded by a pluggable backbone (PVTv2, ResNet-50, or ViT) into hierarchical features. The output is a camouflaged object mask (heatmap) at 288×288 spatial resolution, generated through 4 DDIM denoising steps from random Gaussian noise, along with a boundary map used for auxiliary supervision. The noise resolution is deliberately lower than the image resolution, and a scale factor b=0.1 modulates the signal-to-noise ratio during forward diffusion to ensure the denoising task remains sufficiently challenging.

## Method

FocusDiffuser reformulates COD as a conditional denoising problem: Gaussian noise is added to ground-truth masks during training, and the model learns to recover the clean mask from noise guided by image-derived conditions. Two specialized modules enhance detail perception: (1) the Boundary-Driven LookUp (BDLU) module computes a self-similarity correlation volume from boundary-gated features, pools it into a multi-scale pyramid, and performs local grid lookups (radius r=2) to extract fine-grained disparity features that highlight ambiguous object-background boundaries; (2) the Cyclic Positioning (CP) module iteratively constructs a bounding-box mask from the previous denoising step's prediction, multiplies it with the image and edge features to create a background-suppressed 4-channel input, and injects it via cascaded Texture Enhance Modules into the U-Net encoder. The model uses DDIM sampling with direct f₀ prediction (4 inference steps) and is trained with a combined MSE + BCE + IoU loss under a supervised paradigm.

## Results

FocusDiffuser-P (PVTv2 backbone) achieves state-of-the-art results across all three COD benchmarks: on CAMO, Sα=0.881, Eφ=0.939, Fβw=0.851, M=0.042; on COD10K, Sα=0.875, Eφ=0.939, Fβw=0.809, M=0.020; and on NC4K, Sα=0.891, Eφ=0.940, Fβw=0.854, M=0.029. On CAMO, it leads the nearest competitor (OPNet) by 2.3% in Sα, 2.4% in Eφ, and 3.4% in Fβw. On COD10K, it achieves the lowest mean absolute error (M=0.020) among all compared methods, and on NC4K it generalizes without additional training, surpassing all prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FocusDiffuser-P (PVTv2) outperforms all 22 compared baselines on every metric across all three datasets. On CAMO, it surpasses the strongest Transformer-based competitor OPNet (Sα=0.858, Eφ=0.915, Fβw=0.817) by 2.3%, 2.4%, and 3.4% respectively, and also beats HitNet (AAAI23) by 3.7% in Sα. On COD10K, it edges out HitNet (Sα=0.868, M=0.024) by 0.7% in Sα while achieving a substantially lower M (0.020 vs. 0.024), and leads OPNet by 1.8% in Sα and 4.2% in Fβw. On NC4K, it exceeds OPNet (Sα=0.883, Eφ=0.932) by 0.8% in Sα and 0.8% in Eφ without any additional training on that dataset. Among ResNet-50-based methods, FocusDiffuser-R trails the top Transformer methods but still surpasses most CNN baselines (e.g., ZoomNet, FDNet, SegMaR) on COD10K and NC4K. The ViT-based variant (FocusDiffuser-V) is competitive but consistently slightly below the PVTv2 variant, confirming the benefit of the hierarchical PVTv2 encoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant discriminative paradigm in camouflaged object detection (segmentation networks augmented with edge, depth, or category cues) by recasting COD as a conditional denoising problem. It builds directly on DDIM (Song et al., ICLR 2020) for the reverse sampling schedule and adapts RAFT's (Teed & Deng, ECCV 2020) correlation volume pyramid and lookup operations into the Boundary-Driven LookUp module for local disparity analysis. The Cyclic Positioning module further borrows the Texture Enhance Module from Fan et al. (TPAMI 2022) to inject multi-scale texture detail at each denoising step. Unlike the concurrent CamoDiffusion (AAAI 2024), which applies a standard conditional diffusion pipeline, FocusDiffuser introduces purpose-built modules (BDLU, CP) specifically targeting the fine-grained boundary perception that COD demands.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is one of the first credible demonstrations that a generative diffusion framework can match or exceed strong discriminative COD models, opening a new methodological axis for the field. The BDLU module's repurposing of dense-matching correlation lookups for boundary discrimination is a creative transfer that could inform other fine-grained segmentation tasks. However, the approach remains computationally heavier than single-pass discriminative networks, operates at a fixed 384×384 input with only 4 DDIM steps, and the generative framing's true advantage over a well-designed iterative decoder is not fully isolated. Open questions include whether the diffusion formulation scales to higher resolutions or multi-object scenes, and whether the iterative CP refinement can be replaced by a single-pass mechanism without losing the detail gains.
