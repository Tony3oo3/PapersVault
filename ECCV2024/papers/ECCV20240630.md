## Problem

Existing semantic image splitting methods for fluorescence microscopy, notably μSplit, degrade significantly under realistic imaging noise, inadvertently distributing noise across the predicted channel outputs. No prior method jointly addresses both the splitting and denoising challenges in a single end-to-end framework, forcing practitioners into fragile sequential pipelines that compound errors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240630/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single noisy microscopy image that is the pixel-wise sum of two unknown channel images (e.g., ER + clathrin-coated pits) and produces two noise-free, semantically unmixed channel images. Training is performed exclusively on noisy data; clean images are never seen during training and are used only for evaluation. Additionally, the variational nature of the model allows 50 posterior samples per input to yield pixel-wise uncertainty estimates, calibrated via learnable scaling factors against a held-out validation set.

## Method

denoiSplit replaces the autoencoder output of a Hierarchical VAE with a Variational Splitting Encoder-Decoder (VSE) that directly predicts two noise-free channel images. Two key modifications enable robust denoising: (1) the per-level KL-divergence loss is changed from a spatial average to a spatial sum, which upweights lower hierarchy levels where pixel-scale noise resides and prevents it from being encoded into the latent space; (2) the standard Gaussian decoder likelihood is replaced by two task-specific pixel noise models (Poisson and/or Gaussian), one per output channel, so the network is trained to predict clean intensities whose noise-model likelihood explains the observed noisy pixels. The overall objective is an ELBO combining the two noise-model log-likelihoods and the hierarchical KL term, trained fully unsupervised on noisy sums. Uncertainty is estimated by sampling 50 posterior predictions, computing pixel-wise standard deviations, and calibrating them with two learnable scalars via negative log-likelihood on a recalibration set.

## Results

Across four fluorescence microscopy splitting tasks (ER vs. CCPs, ER vs. MT, CCPs vs. MT, F-actin vs. ER) and eight synthetic noise levels, denoiSplit achieves the best PSNR and MS-SSIM in every setting. For example, on the CCPs vs. MT task at σ=1 Gaussian noise (no Poisson), denoiSplit reaches 40.1 dB PSNR / 0.986 MS-SSIM, compared to 30.5 dB for μSplit and 38.4 dB for the sequential HDN⊕μSplit baseline. Training time is dramatically reduced: denoiSplit requires only 1.5–1.6 hours on a single V100 GPU, versus 6.3–7.2 h for μSplit and 10–11 h for HDN⊕μSplit. On the real-noise Hagen et al. Actin–Mitochondria dataset, denoiSplit again outperforms both baselines in PSNR and range-invariant MS-SSIM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares denoiSplit against two baselines: (1) μSplit, the prior state-of-the-art splitting method, and (2) HDN⊕μSplit, a sequential pipeline that first denoises with Hierarchical DivNoising and then splits with μSplit. Against μSplit, denoiSplit wins on every task and every noise level, with the largest gaps at high noise (e.g., T2 ER vs. MT at σ=4, λ=0: 25.1 dB vs. 21.0 dB PSNR, a 4.1 dB improvement). Against HDN⊕μSplit, denoiSplit also wins consistently but by a smaller margin (typically 0.5–1.7 dB PSNR), while requiring roughly 7× less training time (1.5–1.6 h vs. 10–11 h). The Altered μSplit ablation (modified KL loss without noise models) already closes most of the gap to HDN⊕μSplit, confirming that the KL-loss reformulation is a major contributor, with the noise models providing the final incremental gain. On the real-noise Hagen et al. dataset, HDN training proved unstable (requiring reduced hierarchy depth), further favoring the end-to-end denoiSplit approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

denoiSplit directly extends μSplit [1], the state-of-the-art method for semantic image splitting in fluorescence microscopy, by modifying its hierarchical KL-divergence loss to prevent noise from seeping through lower hierarchy levels (Altered μSplit) and by replacing the standard Gaussian decoder likelihood with pixel-specific noise models borrowed from the unsupervised denoising literature (HDN/DivNoising [19,20]). The uncertainty calibration component adapts the posterior-sampling and learnable-scaling approach of [17] to the variational splitting setting. Conceptually, the paper's key move is the end-to-end integration of a denoising subtask into the splitting objective, rather than treating denoising as a separate preprocessing step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical gap: μSplit, the leading method for fluorescence image splitting, degrades significantly on noisy inputs, which are the norm in real microscopy due to sample sensitivity and hardware constraints. The end-to-end formulation is also substantially more efficient than the sequential denoise-then-split pipeline (~1.5 h vs. ~11 h training), and the calibrated per-pixel uncertainty estimates give microscopists a practical error gauge for downstream analysis. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond two-channel splitting, whether noise models can be learned jointly rather than requiring microscope-specific calibration, and how domain adaptation to different modalities or organisms would work.
