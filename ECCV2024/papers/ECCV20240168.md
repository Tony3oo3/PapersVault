## Problem

Vision Transformers produce coarse, low-resolution feature maps due to their patch-based tokenization, which severely limits their effectiveness on dense and local tasks like keypoint correspondence, detection, and segmentation. Existing densification strategies either require expensive architectural modifications, task-specific supervised training, or simply increasing input resolution—which quadruples FLOPs due to the quadratic cost of self-attention. The paper addresses the gap of a lightweight, task-agnostic, self-supervised method to densify ViT features without retraining or finetuning the backbone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240168/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LiFT takes two inputs simultaneously: coarse ViT feature descriptors at H/P × W/P resolution (P typically 16) and the original RGB image at the same spatial resolution used to generate those features. A single LiFT block outputs dense feature descriptors at 2H/P × 2W/P resolution, and the module is fully convolutional so it generalizes to arbitrary image sizes. The block can be applied recursively (e.g., 2×LiFT, 4×LiFT) to achieve pixel-dense feature maps without additional training.

## Method

LiFT is a U-Net-style block with skip connections that fuses two complementary information sources: semantically rich but spatially coarse ViT features and dense but shallow CNN features extracted from the original image via a small convolutional encoder. The CNN features are concatenated with the ViT features at multiple stages of an upsampling CNN, and a final transpose convolution produces the 2× upscaled output. Training uses a multi-scale self-supervised reconstruction loss (cosine distance) that compares ViT features at full resolution against LiFT-upscaled features derived from half- and quarter-resolution inputs, requiring no gradients through the frozen ViT backbone. The entire module has only 1.2M trainable parameters and is trained for 5 epochs on ImageNet in approximately 8 hours on a single GPU, adding just 22% FLOPs over the base ViT while delivering substantial gains across correspondence, segmentation, detection, and object discovery tasks.

## Results

On SPair-71k Keypoint Correspondence at 224×224, DINO+LiFT achieves PCK@0.1 of 28.68, outperforming base DINO (24.76), Leopart (23.33), SelfPatch (23.03), and all upsampling baselines (BL: 26.72, RC: 26.09, JBU: 24.87). On DAVIS Video Segmentation at 224×224, LiFT reaches J&F Mean of 44.3 versus 33.0 for DINO and 39.0 for DINO+JBU. For COCO Detection with ViTDet(MR)+LiFT, detection AP improves from 39.50 to 45.98 (+6.48) and segmentation AP from 37.81 to 40.78 (+2.97). LiFT achieves these gains with only a 22% increase in FLOPs (4.34G→5.30G at 224×224, stride 16) compared to a 270% increase from stride reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiFT consistently outperforms all baselines across every evaluated task. Against the base DINO backbone [Caron, ICCV 2021], LiFT improves PCK@0.1 by 3.92 points at 224×224 (28.68 vs 24.76) and J&F Mean by 11.3 points on DAVIS (44.3 vs 33.0). Against the backbone-finetuning methods Leopart [Ziegler, CVPR 2022] and SelfPatch [Yun, CVPR 2022], LiFT wins by 5.35 and 5.65 PCK@0.1 points respectively at 224×224, while requiring no backbone finetuning. Among upsampling baselines, LiFT beats JBU [Kopf, ACM TOG 2007] by 3.81 PCK@0.1 and 5.3 J&F Mean at 224×224, and beats Resize-Conv [Odena, Distill 2016] by 2.59 PCK@0.1. For COCO detection, ViTDet(MR)+LiFT [Li, ECCV 2022] improves detection AP by 6.48 over ViTDet(MR) alone. LiFT also generalizes across backbones: it improves MoCo [Chen, 2021] from 12.31 to 16.34 and supervised ViT [Dosovitskiy, ICLR 2021] from 16.9 to 18.69 PCK@0.1 at 448×448. Compared to the stride-reduction approach of Amir et al. [2021], LiFT at stride 16 (5.30G FLOPs) achieves PCK@0.1 of 28.68 versus 29.92 for DINO at stride 8 (16.07G FLOPs), delivering nearly equivalent performance at 3× less compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

LiFT positions itself as a task-agnostic, self-supervised post-processing module for 'Plain' ViT backbones, departing from prior dense-ViT approaches that either require task-specific supervised training (ViT-Adapter), full backbone finetuning with specialized losses (SelfPatch, Leopart), or quadratic FLOP increases from stride reduction (Amir et al.). Architecturally, it draws on U-Net-style skip connections and transpose convolutions familiar from CNN upsampling literature, but repurposes them to fuse frozen ViT descriptors with a small CNN's image-space features. The multi-scale self-supervised reconstruction objective is inspired by feature super-resolution work but applied in a backbone-agnostic, training-light setting that avoids gradient propagation through the transformer entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LiFT offers a practical, low-cost path to denser ViT features that practitioners can adopt without retraining or modifying their backbone, making it broadly applicable across self-supervised (DINO, MoCo) and supervised ViTs. The emergent properties—improved scale invariance and sharper object boundaries—suggest the multi-scale objective implicitly regularizes features in ways that benefit dense prediction beyond mere resolution. Open questions include whether LiFT generalizes to hierarchical ViTs (Swin, PVT), whether recursive application degrades gracefully at very high upsampling factors, and whether the gains persist when combined with other densification strategies at scale. The method's limitation to 'Plain' ViTs and its modest 5.7% parameter overhead, while small, are worth noting for deployment-sensitive settings.
