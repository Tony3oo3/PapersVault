## Problem

Language-conditioned object insertion into natural images remains poorly solved: existing instruction-based editing models (InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush) make global edits that destroy scene context, replace existing objects, and fail to spatially reason about where new objects should appear. The fundamental obstacle is the lack of high-quality paired training data showing realistic object insertions with correct lighting, occlusion, and spatial placement in in-the-wild scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242109/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a free-form text prompt specifying both the identity and spatial location of the object to insert (e.g., 'add a palm tree to the right of the zebra') and produces a full-resolution edited image with the object composited in while preserving all existing scene content. The image is encoded into a VAE latent space where diffusion operates, and the text prompt is cross-attended alongside the image conditioning. The model supports iterative use, where the output of one insertion step becomes the input to the next, enabling multi-object scene composition.

## Method

The key insight is that state-of-the-art inpainting models erase objects from natural images far more realistically than any model can insert them, so inverting the removal direction yields high-quality insertion training pairs. The data pipeline uses CogVLM to generate coarse and fine captions for objects in internet images, detects objects via grounding VLM with rejection sampling (three bounding boxes at temperature 0.2, accepting only if IoU > 80%), segments with SAM, and erases with LaMa inpainting, producing 65,000 (source image, text instruction, target image) triplets. A Stable Diffusion 1.5 backbone is fine-tuned in the VAE latent space with an expanded convolutional input channel for image conditioning, following the InstructPix2Pix architecture. For iterative scene composition, the model is combined with beam search (N=5, k=3) scored by CLIP text-image alignment to automatically select the best candidate at each insertion step.

## Results

On the EmuEdit object-insertion benchmark, EraseDraw achieves CLIPout 0.0930, CLIPdir 0.1383, and an 82.22% human win rate, outperforming all baselines despite being trained on only 65,000 images (an order of magnitude less than the 450,000+ used by InstructPix2Pix and MagicBrush). The CLIPdir score of 0.1383 is 42% higher than InstructPix2Pix (0.0976) and 49% higher than MagicBrush (0.0930), indicating substantially better alignment between the described edit and the actual image change. In human evaluation, EraseDraw wins 82.22% of pairwise comparisons versus 12.56% for InstructPix2Pix and 48.64% for MagicBrush.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EraseDraw decisively outperforms both baselines on every reported metric. Against InstructPix2Pix (trained on 450K auto-labeled images), EraseDraw improves CLIPout from 0.0746 to 0.0930 (+25%), CLIPdir from 0.0976 to 0.1383 (+42%), and human win rate from 12.56% to 82.22%. Against MagicBrush (fine-tuned on 5K human-annotated examples, 455K total), EraseDraw gains +4.8% on CLIPout, +49% on CLIPdir, and +33.6 points on win rate. The closest competitor, MagicBrush++, achieves a CLIPdir of 0.1304 but still trails EraseDraw by 6% on that metric and by 27 points in human preference. Notably, EraseDraw achieves these gains while using roughly 7× less training data than either baseline, attributing its advantage to the higher quality and spatial realism of its autonomously generated insertion pairs derived from real images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the InstructPix2Pix → MagicBrush lineage of instruction-based image editing, which relies on synthetic prompt-pair data or small human-annotated sets and struggles specifically with object insertion. The key conceptual departure is inverting a well-solved task: rather than generating insertion pairs directly, the authors use LaMa inpainting to *remove* objects from natural images, then treat the original image as the target and the erased image as the source, with VLM-generated captions as the insertion instruction. The diffusion model is initialized from Stable Diffusion 1.5 (as InstructPix2Pix is), but the training data is entirely autonomously generated from in-the-wild images via CogVLM detection, SAM segmentation, and LaMa erasure, sidestepping the sim-to-real gap of synthetic-rendering approaches like OBJect-3DIT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The autonomous data generation pipeline is the most transferable contribution: it demonstrates that high-quality insertion training data can be produced at scale without human annotation, and the 10× data-efficiency gap over InstructPix2Pix (65k vs 450k images) suggests the bottleneck for object insertion was data quality, not model capacity. The iterative composition with CLIP-guided beam search opens a practical path toward step-by-step scene building, though the approach is currently limited to 3–4 steps in the paper. A key limitation is that output fidelity is bounded by the SD 1.5 base model (e.g., anatomically inaccurate hands/faces), and the evaluation is confined to the EmuEdit benchmark; whether the pipeline generalizes to newer diffusion backbones or more diverse insertion scenarios remains open.
