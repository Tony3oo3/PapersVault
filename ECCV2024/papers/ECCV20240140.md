## Problem

Existing vector HD mapping methods perform per-frame detection without enforcing temporal consistency, producing noisy and broken global maps when reconstructions are merged over a drive-through. The single recurrent memory (GRU-style latent) used by prior streaming approaches like StreamMapNet is susceptible to memory loss in cluttered environments with occluding vehicles. Additionally, existing benchmarks lack temporally consistent ground truth and metrics that penalize frame-to-frame inconsistencies.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a stream of multi-camera perspective images (6 cameras at 480×800 for nuScenes, 7 at 608×608 for Argoverse2) along with camera intrinsics/extrinsics and inter-frame vehicle pose (4×4 affine transforms), maintaining memory buffers spanning the last 20 frames. It outputs per-frame vector road element geometries as 20-point polygonal curves in the BEV coordinate frame (covering 15 m lateral × 30 m longitudinal), class labels for three element types, and explicit tracking correspondences linking each reconstructed element to its identity in prior frames. The BEV latent memory is a 50×100×256 feature map, while the vector memory is a variable-length set of 512-dim latent vectors, one per active road element.

## Method

MapTracker reformulates online HD mapping as a tracking task by borrowing the query propagation paradigm from transformer-based multi-object tracking: vector latents for tracked road elements are propagated from the previous frame via a PropMLP that encodes the vehicle motion (quaternion + translation), while 100 new candidate latents are initialized from learnable embeddings. The key architectural novelty is a strided memory fusion mechanism that replaces the single recurrent latent with buffers of 20 past frames for both BEV raster and VEC vector representations; at each frame, four memories are selected at distance strides of 1 m / 5 m / 10 m / 15 m from the current ego position, aligned via affine warping, and fused through a lightweight residual convolution (BEV) or cross-attention (VEC). Training employs a tracking-aware matching loss with inductive label assignment that preserves ground-truth track identities across frames, a transformation loss that enforces geometry-preserving propagation in latent space, and Gaussian noise perturbation of the pose to improve robustness. The BEV module (2 iterations) and VEC module (6 iterations) are trained in three stages: BEV pre-training, vector decoder warm-up, and joint fine-tuning.

## Results

On nuScenes with the paper's consistent ground truth, MapTracker achieves 76.1 mAP and 69.1 C-mAP, outperforming the strongest baseline StreamMapNet (70.4 mAP, 56.4 C-mAP) by 5.7 and 12.7 points respectively. On Argoverse2, MapTracker reaches 76.9 mAP and 68.3 C-mAP versus StreamMapNet's 70.3 mAP and 57.5 C-mAP. On geographically non-overlapping splits, MapTracker maintains a 6.8 mAP advantage over StreamMapNet on nuScenes (40.3 vs 33.5) and a 6.9 mAP advantage on Argoverse2 (71.3 vs 64.4), confirming robust cross-scene generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MapTracker outperforms all six baselines on both datasets under the consistent ground truth. Against the strongest streaming baseline StreamMapNet, MapTracker gains +5.7 mAP (76.1 vs 70.4) and +12.7 C-mAP (69.1 vs 56.4) on nuScenes, and +6.6 mAP (76.9 vs 70.3) and +10.8 C-mAP (68.3 vs 57.5) on Argoverse2. Against the detection-based MapTRv2, the margins are even larger: +6.6 mAP and +18.6 C-mAP on nuScenes. The temporal denoising method SQD-MapNet trails MapTracker by 11.1 mAP on nuScenes (65.0 vs 76.1), and the geometry-based GeMap lags by 6.7 mAP. Notably, MapTracker is the only method that natively produces tracking correspondences, making it the sole system directly evaluable under the C-mAP metric without post-hoc track reconstruction; even after applying a simple matching algorithm to form tracks for baselines, the consistency gap remains substantial (12–19 points in C-mAP).

## Relation to Prior Work

MapTracker reframes online vector HD mapping from a per-frame detection problem (as in MapTR, MapTRv2) or a conditional-detection problem with a single recurrent latent (as in StreamMapNet) into an explicit tracking problem, borrowing the query-propagation paradigm from transformer-based multi-object tracking (TrackFormer, TransTrack, MOTR). Its memory mechanism extends StreamMapNet's single GRU-style latent into a buffer of multiple past latents in both BEV raster and vector representations, fused via distance-strided selection rather than full-history accumulation. The BEV module inherits the deformable cross-attention design from BEVFormer and StreamMapNet, while the VEC module's Multi-Point Attention and matching-based loss follow StreamMapNet and MapTR respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistency-aware mAP metric exposes a previously hidden failure mode: even the best prior methods (StreamMapNet) drop from ~70 mAP to ~56 C-mAP, revealing that per-frame accuracy does not guarantee temporally coherent maps—a critical gap for real-world deployment where a single drive-through must yield a usable global map. The tracking formulation and strided memory buffer offer a principled path toward closing this gap, though the approach still depends on accurate ego-vehicle pose and the strided selection is a fixed heuristic (1m/5m/10m/15m) that may not generalize to all driving speeds or environments. Open questions include scaling to much longer sequences, handling dynamic map changes (construction zones, new lanes), and whether the tracking formulation can be extended to 3D or multi-level road structures.
