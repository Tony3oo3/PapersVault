## Problem

Existing autonomous driving systems lack three human-like capabilities: holistic understanding of long-tail open-world scenarios, instant adaptation to new situations without retraining, and real-time error recovery through reflection. While large language models offer rich world knowledge, they are trained on general vision-language data and struggle in the driving domain where labeled text is limited in diversity and richness, and where fine-grained spatial reasoning over video is essential.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241207/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Dolphins consumes N video frames (typically 16, with the first N−1 serving as retrieved in-context examples), a text instruction, and optionally historical control signals (speed, accelerator, turn angle) whose count varies with video segment duration. Outputs are structured as Grounded Chain-of-Thought responses: a step-by-step articulation of scene description, spatial localization of relevant objects, and reasoning, concluding with the final answer. The four task types produce different output granularities—short action labels (e.g., 'the car stops'), justification sentences, numeric speed/turn-angle predictions for the next second, and extended conversational responses covering traffic rules, risks, and precautions.

## Method

Dolphins is built on OpenFlamingo (MPT-7B LLM + Video Swin Transformer vision encoder) and trained in two stages. First, a Grounded Chain-of-Thought (GCoT) instruction tuning stage uses ChatGPT-generated three-step reasoning responses (describe scene → localize object spatially → reason) to ground fine-grained multimodal reasoning in a general image VQA dataset. Second, a multi-modal in-context instruction tuning stage fine-tunes on 32k video-instruction-answer triplets from BDD-X spanning four driving tasks (behavior understanding, behavior reasoning, control-signal prediction, detailed conversation), where RICES-based retrieval selects up to k=3 in-context examples per sample using cosine similarity in both video (Video-Swin-Transformer) and text (text-embedding-ada-002) embedding spaces. Only the visual abstractor and LoRA parameters are updated during training, preserving the base model's in-context learning capacity. A Dolphins-Plus variant further self-improves by training on its own pseudo-labels generated on the Scenario Understanding Benchmark.

## Results

On the BDD-X test split, Dolphins achieves 223.6 CIDEr on action description and 134.2 CIDEr on justification, outperforming the previous SOTA LVLM (DriveGPT4) by 27% on justification. On the proposed Scenario Understanding Benchmark, Dolphins attains an average score of 45.26, surpassing the best video-based VLM baseline (Video-ChatGPT, 37.13) by 8.1 points. On DriveLM, Dolphins scores 38.19 average, exceeding Video-ChatGPT (29.75) by 8.4 points, with Dolphins-Plus (self-refined) reaching 39.19. Ablations confirm GCoT instruction tuning contributes +11.8% and in-context learning contributes +16.0% on the BDD-X action task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On BDD-X justification, Dolphins (134.2 CIDEr) substantially outperforms DriveGPT4 (102.7), RAG-Driver (83.1), and ADAPT (107.2), a 27% improvement over the prior SOTA LVLM. On BDD-X action, Dolphins (223.6 CIDEr) edges out RAG-Driver (222.1) and DriveGPT4 (214.0) but trails the non-LLM expert model ADAPT (249.9), which benefits from a simpler task scope. On the Scenario Understanding Benchmark (unseen instructions), Dolphins (45.26 avg) beats Video-ChatGPT (37.13), Video-LLaMA (30.06), Otter-Video (23.14), and OpenFlamingo (23.47) by 8.1, 15.2, 22.1, and 21.8 points respectively. On DriveLM, Dolphins (38.19) surpasses Video-ChatGPT (29.75), Video-LLaMA (28.91), and Otter-Video (25.56), with the self-refined Dolphins-Plus variant reaching 39.19. Notably, even without BDD-X fine-tuning, Dolphins-Image with in-context learning achieves 49.7 CIDEr on action (vs. 8.6 zero-shot), demonstrating rapid adaptation comparable to fully fine-tuned baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dolphins builds directly on OpenFlamingo (a reimplementation of Flamingo) as its base VLM and extends the line of LLM-based driving agents (DriveLM, DriveGPT4, GPT-Driver) by incorporating rich visual understanding alongside linguistic reasoning. It departs from prior driving VLMs (DriveGPT4, RAG-Driver, ADAPT) in two key ways: introducing a Grounded Chain of Thought (GCoT) pre-training stage on general image data to instill fine-grained spatial reasoning before domain transfer, and employing multimodal in-context instruction tuning with RICES-based retrieval to enable rapid adaptation to unseen instructions. The method also extends the broader LVLM instruction-tuning lineage (LLaVA, Otter, InstructBLIP) into the driving domain, where task diversity and text richness are inherently limited.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Dolphins demonstrates that a VLM fine-tuned on only 32k QA pairs across four driving tasks can generalize to diverse unseen perception, prediction, and planning instructions, and exhibits human-like capabilities (in-context adaptation, reflection-based error recovery) that prior feedforward ADS lack. However, the 1.34-second inference time on an A100 and associated power consumption pose significant barriers to real-time edge deployment, and the paper acknowledges that distilled or customized model variants will be necessary. Open questions include whether the GCoT + in-context tuning paradigm scales to richer driving datasets, how well the approach transfers to closed-loop planning, and whether the self-refinement (Dolphins-Plus) strategy can yield larger gains with more diverse pseudo-label sources.
