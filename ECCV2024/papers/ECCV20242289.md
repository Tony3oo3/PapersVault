## Problem

Positional encoding in transformers creates a conceptual decoupling between elemental and collective data representations, forcing separate learned representations for individual tokens and their sequential arrangement. For autoencoder tasks that learn condensed latent spaces of sequential data, this decoupling is particularly harmful: the decoder must disentangle positional from contextual information, and absolute positional encoding limits sequence-length extrapolation. The paper asks whether temporal information can be encoded implicitly in the latent space geometry itself, eliminating positional encoding entirely.

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes an arbitrary-length input sequence (2D curve points, image patches of size PS×PS×3, flattened 3D mesh vertex sequences, per-frame joint rotations, or 3D strand point sequences) and produces a fixed number of latent control points in R^d, where d is the bottleneck dimension (e.g., 8–256). The decoder uniformly samples the resulting latent spline over t∈[0,1] to generate the output token sequence, meaning the number of decoder tokens is determined by the sampling density rather than the input length. A distinctive output is the set of editable control points: translating them closer to or further from the spline endpoints changes the speed and detail of the reconstructed sequence without retraining.

## Method

Instead of appending a single [CLS] token to the input, the encoder concatenates n+1 learnable control tokens to the embedded input sequence and passes them through a T5-style transformer encoder with ALiBi attention bias. A linear projection maps the encoded control tokens to d-dimensional latent control points, which are interpreted as the control polygon of a cubic Bézier (a special case of B-Spline) curve in latent space. The decoder receives latent codes obtained by uniformly evaluating this spline at the output token positions, so temporal ordering is encoded in the trajectory's shape rather than in explicit positional offsets. Training uses a simple L2 reconstruction loss with the RAdam optimizer and cosine annealing; no positional encoding of any kind is applied to the latent codes. The key insight is that the spline trajectory can traverse latent space at variable speed, encoding temporal dynamics implicitly and freeing the decoder from having to separate positional and contextual signals.

## Results

Spline-based Transformers achieve consistent and often dramatic improvements over ALiBi across all tested modalities. On image reconstruction (CIFAR-10, 32D bottleneck), Spline reduces MSE from 0.266 to 0.107 (2.5× improvement); on human motion (HumanML3D, 64D), MSE drops from 0.059 to 0.006 (nearly 10× improvement); on hair strand reconstruction (32D), MSE improves from 1.5e-3 to 9.4e-4. Gains are largest at lower latent dimensions, where Spline's implicit temporal encoding most effectively replaces the explicit positional information.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against ALiBi [31] (the primary baseline, a state-of-the-art positional encoding that adds linear biases to attention) and ALiBi-Cat (a variant where sinusoidal positional encodings are concatenated to token embeddings rather than added, effectively doubling decoder width). Spline-based Transformers win on virtually every comparison across all five modalities (synthetic curves, images, facial animation, human motion, hair strands). The most dramatic wins appear in human motion reconstruction, where Spline at 64D achieves 0.006 MSE versus 0.059 for ALiBi (≈10×), and in low-dimensional latent spaces across all tasks. The only near-tie is the high-dimensional synthetic Bezier(64D) case (3.87e-3 vs 3.88e-3), where the large latent space already provides sufficient capacity. ALiBi-Cat consistently performs at or slightly below ALiBi, confirming that concatenation does not resolve the fundamental ambiguity between content and position that the spline trajectory eliminates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard transformer autoencoder paradigm for sequence reconstruction, which relies on positional encoding (absolute sinusoids, relative biases like ALiBi or RoPE) to inject temporal information into tokens. Instead, it replaces positional encoding entirely with a B-Spline trajectory in latent space, where learned control points define a continuous curve that is uniformly sampled to produce per-token latent codes. The approach draws on classical spline theory (Farin [11]) for the mathematical representation and incorporates ALiBi [31] as the attention bias within the transformer blocks, but the key conceptual departure is that temporal structure is encoded implicitly through the spline's shape rather than explicitly through position-dependent perturbations of token embeddings. This is inspired by animation workflows where splines naturally describe temporal sequences of poses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of positional encoding removes a well-known source of sequence-length extrapolation failure and overfitting, while the spline-based latent space provides a natural, interpretable control mechanism: users can directly manipulate control points to edit sequences (e.g., speeding up or slowing down motion) without retraining. The consistent 2×+ reconstruction improvements across five diverse modalities (2D curves, images, 3D facial animation, full-body motion, hair strands) suggest the benefit is not dataset-specific. Open questions include the observed sensitivity to learning rate (risk of control-point collapse), whether specialized scheduling can stabilize training, and whether the approach extends to generative or conditional settings beyond autoencoding.
