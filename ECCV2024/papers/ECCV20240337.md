## Problem

Denoising diffusion models have achieved remarkable success in 2D image generation but struggle to replicate this in 3D shape generation. Existing 3D diffusion approaches either operate on implicit functions (computationally expensive with cubic scaling in resolution) or on unstructured point clouds (limited to ~2048 points, requiring over 10,000 epochs to converge per category). The paper addresses the need for a 3D representation that is naturally compatible with 2D diffusion architectures while supporting high-resolution, fine-grained shape generation.

## Input / Output

The model takes N=8 multi-view depth maps (each 128×128) initialized as pure Gaussian noise and iteratively denoises them over T=1000 steps. The output is a set of 3D-consistent depth maps that, when back-projected, yield a dense point cloud of 20K+ points (roughly 10× more than point-cloud diffusion baselines), which can optionally be converted to a mesh via SAP. For depth completion, a single partial depth map conditions the generation of the remaining views; for GAN inversion, a 2D input image drives test-time optimization of a tri-plane radiance field regularized by the trained MVDD prior.

## Method

MVDD represents a 3D shape as 8 multi-view depth maps and applies a standard DDPM forward/reverse process on these 2D grids using a U-Net backbone. The key architectural contribution is an epipolar 'line segment' attention that conditions each view's denoising on R neighboring views: for each pixel, the current noisy depth is back-projected into 3D, and only k=10 points along the ray (rather than the full epipolar line) are projected onto neighboring views to form the K,V queries, making cross-view attention both efficient and geometrically targeted. A visibility mask (cross-attention thresholding) culls projected pixels whose depth disagrees with the neighboring view, and depth values are concatenated to the V features to anchor geometric alignment. A second mechanism, denoising depth fusion, is applied in the last 20 diffusion steps: after the U-Net predicts the denoised depth, reprojected depths from all visible neighboring views are averaged per pixel before adding the scheduled noise, explicitly eliminating 'double-layer' artifacts. Training uses the standard ε-prediction loss with cosine noise scheduling over 1000 steps, converging in ~3000 epochs on 8 A100 GPUs.

## Results

On unconditional 3D shape generation (ShapeNet), MVDD surpasses all baselines across airplane, car, and chair categories, particularly excelling in the 1-NNA (EMD) metric which captures both quality and diversity. For depth completion (GenRe benchmark), MVDD achieves EMD of 0.900 (airplane), 2.400 (chair), and 1.460 (car), outperforming the best baseline PVD by 12–38% across categories. A key practical advantage is that MVDD directly generates dense point clouds with 20K+ points, roughly 10× more than point-cloud diffusion baselines (2048 points), enabling fine-grained geometric detail. In the ablation (chair category), the full model achieves 1-NNA EMD of 54.51, improving from 90.00 without any cross-view attention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For 3D shape generation (Table 1), MVDD outperforms all six baselines—Vox-diff, DPM, 3D-LDM, IM-GAN, PVD, and LION—on the 1-NNA (EMD) metric across all three ShapeNet categories; for example, on chair, MVDD achieves 54.51 vs. LION's 57.31 and IM-GAN's 55.54. On depth completion (Table 2), MVDD beats PVD (the strongest baseline) by 12.6% on airplane (0.900 vs. 1.030), 18.3% on chair (2.400 vs. 2.939), and 31.9% on car (1.460 vs. 2.146), and margins over flow-based methods (SoftFlow, PointFlow, DPF-Net) are even larger. LION, the closest competitor in shape generation, is limited to 2048 points and its performance degrades significantly when up-sampled to higher densities (Fig. 5), whereas MVDD's 20K+ point output remains robust. Implicit methods (3D-LDM, IM-GAN) produce smoother shapes with less fine-grained detail compared to MVDD's dense point clouds. The paper also demonstrates that MVDD, when used as a 3D prior for GAN inversion, eliminates the 'wall' artifact that plagues unregularized PTI-based inversion, a capability none of the point-cloud or implicit baselines offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

MVDD builds directly on the DDPM diffusion framework but departs from prior 3D shape generation methods that operate on point clouds (DPM, PVD, LION), implicit functions (IM-GAN, 3D-LDM), or voxels (Vox-diff) by adopting multi-view depth maps as the generative representation. For enforcing cross-view consistency, it draws conceptual inspiration from multi-view stereo (MVSNet, surface normal diffusion) and multi-view diffusion models (MVDream's 3D self-attention, SyncDreamer's cost-volume correlation), but introduces a novel epipolar 'line segment' attention that exploits the depth values available during denoising to restrict attention to a small segment rather than the full epipolar line. The denoising depth fusion module similarly repurposes multi-view stereo reprojection logic inside the diffusion loop, a design not seen in prior generative 3D work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× increase in point count (20K+ vs. 2048) over prior diffusion-based point cloud generators is a practical win that enables fine-grained geometric detail previously unattainable with those methods, and the multi-view depth representation makes the model naturally composable as a 3D prior for downstream tasks like GAN inversion. However, the evaluation remains confined to ShapeNet categories with a fixed 8-view cube camera setup, and the 3000-epoch training cost on 8 A100s limits accessibility. Open questions include whether the approach scales to text-conditioned generation, arbitrary camera configurations, or more complex object categories beyond the standard ShapeNet benchmarks.
