## Problem

Existing IMLE-based few-shot image synthesis methods suffer from a latent space misalignment: the nearest-neighbor selection in the IMLE objective biases training toward latent codes close to data points, leaving large regions of the latent space untrained. At test time, latent codes drawn from the standard Gaussian prior fall into these untrained regions, producing low-quality samples. The paper provides a theoretical analysis of this phenomenon and proposes a principled fix.

## Input / Output

The model takes a small set of 256×256 RGB training images (as few as ~100) and, at inference, a random latent code sampled from a non-analytical prior distribution P (constructed via rejection sampling from N(0,I)) to produce a synthesized 256×256 image. The generator is a VQ-VAE decoder network, and the distance metric used for nearest-neighbor search operates on random-projection-compressed image vectors to keep computation tractable.

## Method

RS-IMLE modifies the IMLE training objective by replacing the standard Gaussian prior with a new distribution P designed so that the distribution of latent codes actually selected during training matches the test-time sampling distribution. The authors derive that P's PDF is proportional to c·φ(t)·f_{D_{i1}}(t), where φ(t) is a truncation factor, and show this can be sampled via rejection sampling: draw z ~ N(0,I), reject if d(x_i, T_θ(z)) < ε for any data point x_i, otherwise accept. This guarantees every training sample is at least ε-distance from all data points, forcing the model to learn from challenging, non-trivial samples rather than collapsing onto already-close neighbors. The hyperparameter ε is tuned via cross-validation, and the full procedure (Algorithm 1) integrates this rejection step into the standard IMLE training loop with k-nearest-neighbor search (DCI) for efficient filtering.

## Results

RS-IMLE achieves the best FID on all nine few-shot image datasets, with an average 45.9% reduction over the strongest baseline (AdaIMLE). Notable FID scores include 14.0 on Obama (vs. 25.0 for AdaIMLE), 3.5 on Panda (vs. 7.6), and 12.9 on FFHQ-100 (vs. 33.2). In terms of precision and recall, RS-IMLE attains near-perfect precision (0.93–1.00) while substantially outperforming all baselines on recall, e.g., 0.97 on Panda vs. 0.63 for AdaIMLE and 0.99 on FFHQ-100 vs. 0.77.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RS-IMLE outperforms all five baselines on FID across every dataset, with the largest margins against GAN-based methods. The closest competitor is AdaIMLE (the prior IMLE-based state-of-the-art), which RS-IMLE beats by 36–61% in FID (e.g., 12.9 vs. 33.2 on FFHQ-100, 3.5 vs. 7.6 on Panda). Among GAN baselines, FakeCLR is generally the strongest (e.g., 20.6 on Grumpy Cat, 8.8 on Panda), yet still trails RS-IMLE by 44–55% in FID. ReGAN and FreGAN perform poorly on several datasets (e.g., ReGAN reaches 236.1 FID on Shells, FreGAN 163.3 on Skulls), indicating persistent mode collapse. On precision-recall metrics, RS-IMLE achieves near-perfect precision (≥0.93) and dominates on recall (e.g., 0.97 on Panda vs. 0.63 for AdaIMLE; 0.99 on FFHQ-100 vs. 0.77), confirming superior mode coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Implicit Maximum Likelihood Estimation (IMLE) and its few-shot adaptation, Adaptive IMLE, which replaced the GAN adversarial objective with a nearest-neighbor-based criterion to avoid mode collapse. The key departure is identifying a latent-space misalignment: the min operation in the IMLE objective systematically selects latent codes whose distribution differs from the standard Gaussian prior used at test time. Rather than modifying the generator architecture or the distance metric (as GAN-based few-shot methods like FastGAN, FakeCLR, and ReGAN do), the authors introduce a rejection-sampling-based prior that is orthogonal to Adaptive IMLE's per-sample thresholding, making it a drop-in replacement for the training-time sampling distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical characterization of the misalignment (Equations 2–5) provides a principled explanation for why IMLE-based methods underperform at test time despite using the same nominal prior, and the fix is remarkably simple: reject samples within an ε-neighborhood of any data point before nearest-neighbor selection. The 45.9% average FID reduction across nine datasets is substantial and consistent, suggesting the misalignment was a significant hidden bottleneck. Open questions include whether the approach extends to conditional generation, higher resolutions, or other implicit generative frameworks, and whether the ε hyperparameter (tuned by cross-validation) can be made adaptive. The method's reliance on a specific distance metric and the DCI nearest-neighbor search also raises scalability questions for very large latent spaces.
