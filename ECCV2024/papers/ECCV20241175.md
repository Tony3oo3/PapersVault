## Problem

Part-level segmentation remains underexplored due to complex part boundaries and the scarcity of pixel-level annotations, while existing weakly-supervised methods focus on object-level segmentation with image-level labels. This paper introduces a new Weakly-supervised Part Segmentation (WPS) task that uses only bounding boxes or points as training supervision, and asks whether a pre-trained foundation model (SAM) can be adapted to perform autonomous, category-aware part segmentation without any manual prompt provision at inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241175/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image resized to 1024×1024 as input. During training, it additionally receives weak supervision in the form of part bounding boxes (or center points) paired with class labels; during inference, no prompts are provided. The output is a set of S pixel-level part masks (each 1024×1024) along with predicted part category labels, where S is a fixed number of queries (default 25) and background queries are discarded at inference based on the classification head.

## Method

WPS-SAM freezes SAM's image encoder (ViT) and mask decoder, replacing only the prompt encoder with a lightweight query-based Transformer 'student prompter' (6-layer encoder-decoder with cross-attention over downsampled image features). Training uses knowledge distillation: SAM's original prompt encoder acts as a teacher that converts weak labels (boxes/points) into high-dimensional prompt embeddings, and the student prompter is trained to reproduce these embeddings from image features alone via Hungarian-matched bipartite assignment with a combined cross-entropy (category) and smooth-L1 (prompt regression) loss. At inference, the student prompter autonomously generates part-specific prompt tokens from the image, which are fed into the frozen SAM mask decoder to produce pixel-level part masks—eliminating the need for any manual prompt or pseudo-label generation step.

## Results

On PartImageNet, WPS-SAM (ViT-B backbone, bounding-box supervision) achieves 68.93% mIoU and 79.53% mACC, surpassing the best fully-supervised method (MaskFormer with Swin-B at 65.24% mIoU) by 3.69% mIoU and the best WSSS method (FMA-WSSS at 56.74% mIoU) by 12.19% mIoU. On PASCAL-Part, WPS-SAM achieves 60.49% mIoU and 71.25% mACC, outperforming the strongest fully-supervised baseline (MaskFormer with Swin-B at 56.83% mIoU) by 3.66% mIoU. Notably, these gains are achieved using only sparse bounding-box annotations during training, with SAM's image encoder and mask decoder kept frozen.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WPS-SAM outperforms all seven baselines on both datasets. Against fully-supervised methods, it beats the strongest (MaskFormer with Swin-B, 65.24% mIoU) by 3.69% mIoU on PartImageNet and by 3.66% on PASCAL-Part, despite using only bounding-box labels rather than pixel-level masks. Among WSSS methods, the gap is even larger: WPS-SAM exceeds FMA-WSSS (56.74% mIoU, the best WSSS baseline) by 12.19% mIoU on PartImageNet and by 18.28% on PASCAL-Part. The box-supervised SIM [44] (49.51% mIoU) and category-supervised BECO [58] (42.37% mIoU) trail WPS-SAM by 19.42% and 26.56% mIoU respectively on PartImageNet. The paper also shows that a naive Det-SAM pipeline (Faster R-CNN + SAM) achieves only 63.7% mIoU, confirming that the end-to-end prompt learning design is critical for closing the gap to fully-supervised performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SAM's prompt-based segmentation architecture, replacing its interactive prompt encoder with a learned query-based transformer prompter trained via knowledge distillation from the original SAM prompt encoder. It departs from the WSSS literature, which typically uses image-level labels for object-level segmentation, by introducing a finer-grained part-level task with bounding-box or point annotations. It also contrasts with the pseudo-label paradigm common in WSSS (generate masks from CAMs or SAM, then train a separate segmentation model) by performing end-to-end prompt learning without intermediate mask generation. The Det-SAM ablation (detector + SAM pipeline) is positioned as a natural but suboptimal baseline that motivates the end-to-end design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that bbox-level weak supervision outperforms pixel-level full supervision by ~4% mIoU on PartImageNet is practically significant, as it dramatically reduces annotation cost for part segmentation while improving quality. However, the reliance on SAM's heavy ViT encoder limits deployment in resource-constrained settings, and the authors themselves suggest lightweight SAM variants (EfficientSAM, FastSAM) as a path forward. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to open-vocabulary or 3D part segmentation, and whether the performance gap over fully-supervised methods persists when the latter also leverage foundation-model backbones of comparable scale.
