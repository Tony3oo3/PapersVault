## Problem

Existing multi-modal semantic segmentation methods (e.g., CMNeXt) treat RGB as the primary modality and all others as auxiliary, yielding an asymmetric two-branch architecture that collapses when RGB is unreliable (e.g., nighttime, over-exposure). No prior method can perform robust segmentation with truly arbitrary subsets of modalities without depending on a specific sensor, leaving a gap in modality-agnostic, resilient multi-modal fusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241843/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework ingests a mini-batch of multi-modal inputs (RGB, depth, LiDAR, event on DELIVER; RGB, NIR, DoLP, AoLP on MCubeS), each converted to 3-channel images and processed through weight-shared backbone encoders (SegFormer-B0/B2). Crucially, during inference the model accepts any arbitrary subset of these modalities—only the corresponding modality-specific MLP layers are activated—enabling segmentation under partial sensor failure. The output is a pixel-wise semantic segmentation mask over K categories, produced by a shared segmentation head applied to both the MAM semantic feature and the ASM salient feature.

## Method

MAGIC comprises two plug-and-play modules that treat all modalities symmetrically. The Multi-modal Aggregation Module (MAM) applies parallel modality-specific MLPs and multi-scale parallel pooling (3×3, 7×7, 11×11) with residual connections to extract a unified semantic feature f_se from the multi-modal batch, supervised by standard cross-entropy. The Arbitrary-modal Selection Module (ASM), used only during training, ranks all modality features by cosine similarity to f_se, selects the top-1 (most robust) and last-1 (most fragile) features, and aggregates them through a second MAM to produce a salient prediction; a masked cross-entropy loss (L_S) softens supervision by retaining only pixels where MAM's argmax agrees with ground truth, and a consistency loss (L_C) implicitly aligns the remaining unselected features to f_se. At inference, only the backbone plus MAM are used, yielding a 42%-parameter model that outperforms CMNeXt by +1.33% mIoU in the standard multi-modal setting and by up to +19.41% mIoU in the modality-agnostic arbitrary-input setting.

## Results

Under the standard multi-modal setting (all four modalities present), MAGIC with SegFormer-B2 (24.73M params) achieves 67.66% mIoU on DELIVER and 53.01% mIoU on MCubeS, outperforming CMNeXt (58.73M params) by +1.33% and +1.47% mIoU respectively, while using only 42% of the parameters. In the novel modality-agnostic setting with arbitrary input combinations, MAGIC (Seg-B2) achieves a mean mIoU of 44.66% on DELIVER and 38.00% on MCubeS, surpassing CMNeXt (25.25% and 25.17%) by +19.41% and +14.97% mIoU. Even with the compact SegFormer-B0 backbone (3.72M params, only 6% of CMNeXt), MAGIC still outperforms CMNeXt by +15.24% and +9.39% mIoU on DELIVER and MCubeS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is CMNeXt (Zhang et al., CVPR 2023), which treats RGB as the primary modality and all others as auxiliary in an asymmetric two-branch architecture. MAGIC wins decisively across all evaluation settings: in the standard multi-modal setting, MAGIC (Seg-B2) beats CMNeXt by +1.33% mIoU on DELIVER (67.66 vs 66.30) and +1.47% on MCubeS (53.01 vs 51.54) while using only 42% of the parameters (24.73M vs 58.73M). The gap widens dramatically in the modality-agnostic setting: when RGB is absent, CMNeXt collapses (e.g., Depth-only: 0.81% mIoU) while MAGIC maintains strong performance (Depth-only: 57.59%), yielding a mean mIoU advantage of +19.41% on DELIVER and +14.97% on MCubeS. Even the lightweight MAGIC with SegFormer-B0 (3.72M params, 6% of CMNeXt) surpasses CMNeXt by +15.24% on DELIVER, confirming that the plug-and-play MAM and ASM modules are the primary source of the performance gain rather than backbone capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant RGB-centric paradigm in multi-modal semantic segmentation, where prior methods like CMNeXt and CMX treat RGB as the anchor modality and all others as auxiliary inputs fused into an asymmetric two-branch architecture. MAGIC instead treats all modalities symmetrically, introducing a Multi-modal Aggregation Module (MAM) that processes modalities as a batch with parallel MLPs and multi-scale pooling, and an Arbitrary-modal Selection Module (ASM) that uses cosine-similarity ranking against the aggregated semantic feature to dynamically select the most robust and most fragile modalities. The plug-and-play design builds on SegFormer-style vision transformer backbones but is explicitly backbone-agnostic, and the cross-modal consistency loss for aligning remaining features is a new training objective not present in prior fusion architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modality-agnostic setting addresses a critical practical gap: real-world sensor systems experience failures (nighttime RGB degradation, LiDAR jitter, motion blur), and a segmentation model that collapses without RGB is unreliable for deployment. The 60% parameter reduction while matching or exceeding CMNeXt's multi-modal performance makes the approach attractive for resource-constrained platforms. However, the evaluation is limited to two datasets and a single comparison baseline, and the authors acknowledge sub-optimal performance in certain scenarios due to inherent data characteristics. Open questions include scalability beyond four modalities, generalization to unseen sensor combinations, and whether the cosine-similarity ranking generalizes to fundamentally different feature spaces (e.g., 3D point clouds vs. 2D images).
