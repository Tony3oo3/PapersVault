## Problem

Existing Non-transferable Learning (NTL) methods for protecting pretrained DNN intellectual property rely on gradient-based objective optimization and are not robust when an attacker has full access to model parameters and can perform unrestricted fine-tuning. Moreover, prior evaluation metrics measure transferability only at a fixed fine-tuning data volume, failing to capture how much target data an attacker would need to recover model utility. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a structural modification (pruning) that is harder to reverse through fine-tuning, and a data-volume-aware evaluation metric.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242310/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained DNN (feature extractor plus linear classifier), 10% of the source-domain training set, 10% of the target-domain training set, and a desired sparsity level S (default maximum 0.99). It outputs a sparsified model in which individual weights are zeroed out (unstructured pruning) such that source-domain accuracy is largely preserved while the model's ability to transfer to the target domain is degraded. The pruned model is then evaluated by fully fine-tuning it on varying fractions of the target dataset and comparing against a from-scratch baseline at each data volume.

## Method

NTP formulates a constrained optimization that minimizes a composite loss—source-domain cross-entropy, a capped target-domain loss penalty, and a novel Fisher Space Regularization that collapses inter-class separation in the target feature space—subject to an L1 sparsity constraint on the weights. The optimization is solved via ADMM, alternating between a W-update (SGD on the NTP loss plus a quadratic coupling term), a Z-update (soft-thresholding for sparsity), and a U-update (dual variable), iterated until the desired sparsity is reached. The Fisher Space Regularizer (Eq. 2) explicitly minimizes the ratio of between-class mean distance to within-class variance in the target domain, blurring decision boundaries so that even a fully fine-tuned model cannot recover useful class structure. For evaluation, the paper introduces SLC-AUC, the signed area between the fine-tuned model's learning curve and the from-scratch model's curve across logarithmically spaced target-data fractions, providing a single scalar that captures transferability under all realistic fine-tuning budgets.

## Results

The headline metric is SLC-AUC (Area Under the Sample-wise Learning Curve), where negative values indicate the fine-tuned model performs worse than training from scratch. NTP achieves an average SLC-AUC of −0.54 across all source-target pairs on VGG-11, with individual values ranging from −1.56 (USPS→SVHN) to +0.51 (SYN→SVHN). In contrast, NTL ranges from 0.16 to 1.64 and CUTI from 0.68 to 2.12, all positive. Source-domain accuracy drops remain modest (≤4.1% for NTP), comparable to NTL and slightly higher than CUTI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NTP decisively outperforms both baselines on the SLC-AUC metric: it achieves negative values (indicating the fine-tuned model is worse than training from scratch) in 18 out of 20 source-target pairs, while NTL and CUTI remain positive in all 20 pairs. The gap is largest for the USPS source, where NTP reaches −1.56 (→SVHN) versus NTL's 0.83 and CUTI's 1.86. NTP also demonstrates superior robustness to fine-tuning hyperparameters: across learning rates 10⁻³ to 10⁻⁵ and both full fine-tuning and linear probing, NTP's target accuracy stays in the 0.13–0.32 range, whereas NTL and CUTI can spike to 0.96 under certain settings. On source-domain accuracy, NTP incurs a slightly larger drop than CUTI (e.g., 0.73% vs 0.59% on MNIST) but is comparable to NTL, representing a reasonable trade-off for the substantially stronger non-transferability guarantee.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Non-transferable Learning (NTL) framework introduced by Wang et al. [52] and the CUTI/Model Barrier approach [51], which formulate applicability authorization as a loss-optimization problem. The key departure is the threat model: prior NTL methods assume attackers have limited parameter access, whereas NTP targets the stronger scenario where the full model is exposed (e.g., on edge devices) and can be completely fine-tuned. To achieve robustness under full fine-tuning, the paper repurposes ADMM-based unstructured pruning [43, 61]—previously used for compression and adversarial robustness—as the mechanism for selectively destroying target-domain transferability, augmented with a Fisher space discriminative regularizer that collapses inter-class feature separation on the unauthorized domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SLC-AUC metric fills a genuine gap in NTL evaluation by measuring transferability across varying fine-tuning data volumes rather than at a single fixed point, making it more reflective of realistic attacker behavior. The negative average SLC-AUC (−0.54) demonstrates that pruning can make a pretrained model actively harmful for unauthorized transfer, a stronger guarantee than prior methods that only reduce (not eliminate) transfer benefit. However, the evaluation is concentrated on small digit-recognition datasets, with only CIFAR-10→STL-10 and ImageNette→ImageWoof as more complex cases, and the self-supervised results show NTP is less effective there. Open questions include scalability to transformer architectures, handling multiple simultaneous target domains, and the hyperparameter sensitivity the authors acknowledge for the Fisher regularization and sparsity level.
