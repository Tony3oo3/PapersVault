## Problem

Prior work suggested that Stochastic Neural Networks (SNNs) with Variational Bottlenecks or Bayesian Layers are inherently resistant to Gradient Inversion Attacks in Federated Learning, because their random sampling of features and parameters during forward propagation supposedly prevents a malicious server from emulating the client's training process. This paper overturns that assumption by demonstrating that SNN gradients implicitly encode the specific stochastic elements used during training, making SNNs equally vulnerable to GIAs as deterministic networks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240802/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The attack consumes the full gradient tensor ∇θ from a single local training step of an SNN (ResNet-18/34, ConvNet, or MLP with 256-dim VB or BL), the known model architecture and parameters, and the ground-truth label analytically extracted from the prediction-layer gradient signs. It jointly optimizes a dummy image x̂ (32×32 or 64×64 RGB), a feature correction multiplier m, and two noise vectors ε̂_vb and ε̂_b, producing reconstructed training images, the specific latent features z_vb sampled by the client's VB, and the specific parameters θ_b sampled by the client's BL. Unlike standard GIAs that only optimize the image, ISG must simultaneously recover the discarded stochastic components that the client never transmits.

## Method

ISG reformulates the stochastic forward pass as a deterministic one by replacing random sampling with jointly learnable noise terms: ε̂_vb constructs the dummy latent features via the reparameterization trick (ẑ_vb = μ̂_vb + σ̂_vb ⊙ ε̂_vb), and ε̂_b constructs the dummy BL parameters (θ̂_b = θ_μ + θ_σ ⊙ ε̂_b). A learnable feature correction multiplier m is inserted after the convolutional feature extractor to compensate for local minima caused by shared convolutional weights, with an L2 regularization term penalizing the discrepancy between raw and corrected features. KLD regularization constrains ε̂_vb and ε̂_b to remain close to standard Gaussian, while TV and L2 image regularization enforce natural-image priors. The full objective—MSE gradient matching plus all regularization terms—is optimized with Adam for 15,000 iterations, reducing the problem to a standard gradient-matching inversion once stochasticity is neutralized.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with VB-ResNet18, ISG achieves SSIM of 0.5831 and MSE of 0.0233 for reconstructed images, outperforming the best baseline (GGL, SSIM 0.1322) by approximately 4.4× in SSIM. On CelebA with BL-ConvNet, ISG achieves SSIM of 0.4736 and MSE of 0.0148, reducing MSE by roughly 14–16× compared to the strongest baselines. ISG also successfully reconstructs the stochastic elements: z_vb with MSE 0.0028 (SNR 26.81 dB) on CIFAR-10 and θ_b with MSE 0.2449 (SNR 6.10 dB) on CelebA, whereas all baselines produce noise-like outputs with SSIM below 0.13.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ISG decisively outperforms all six baselines (DLG, iDLG, IG, GI, GGL, GIFD) on both the CIFAR-10 (VB-ResNet18) and CelebA (BL-ConvNet) tasks. On CIFAR-10, ISG achieves SSIM 0.583 versus the best baseline GGL at 0.132 (≈4.4× improvement) and MSE 0.0233 versus GI's 0.1057 (≈4.5× lower). On CelebA, ISG reaches SSIM 0.474 versus GGL's 0.117 (≈4× improvement) and MSE 0.0148 versus DLG's 0.2102 (≈14× lower). All baselines produce visually indistinguishable noise (SSIM < 0.13), while ISG recovers recognizable facial and object structures. The generative-prior methods (GGL, GIFD) perform marginally better than the direct-optimization methods (DLG, iDLG, IG, GI) on PSNR but still fail to recover meaningful image content, confirming that the core issue is the inability to emulate stochastic forward propagation rather than the choice of image prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient inversion attack (GIA) literature—building directly on IG [9] for the optimization framework and iDLG [34] for label extraction—to the setting of Stochastic Neural Networks (SNNs) with Variational Bottlenecks and Bayesian Layers. It departs from the prior claim [26] that SNNs are inherently resistant to GIAs because their stochastic forward propagation cannot be replicated by a server; instead, the authors show that SNN gradients implicitly encode the sampled stochastic components, enabling an attacker to jointly recover them alongside the training data. The feature correction multiplier is a novel component introduced to address local minima caused by shared convolutional weights, a limitation observed in existing GIAs on CNNs but not previously formalized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work overturns a specific, published defense claim in the federated learning security literature, showing that perturbation-based defenses in forward propagation (SNNs) do not inherently protect against gradient inversion. The practical implication is that FL practitioners cannot rely on architectural stochasticity as a privacy guarantee and must turn to gradient-level defenses (clipping, noise, sparsification), which carry accuracy trade-offs. Open questions include whether the attack generalizes to multi-round training, differential privacy mechanisms, or non-image modalities, and whether the feature correction multiplier transfers to other architectures beyond the tested ResNet/ConvNet/MLP backbones.
