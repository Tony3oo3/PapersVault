## Problem

Deployed image classifiers frequently fail when test data violates training distribution assumptions (spurious correlations, class imbalance, covariate shifts), yet existing failure detection scores derived from the model itself (MSP, entropy, energy) become unreliable when the model is miscalibrated. No prior approach leverages the rich attribute-level semantic understanding of foundation models to systematically identify and explain these generalization gaps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242127/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a binary failure/success label determined by comparing a cross-entropy disagreement score against a threshold τ calibrated to match true validation accuracy. Beyond the binary label, DECIDER outputs a continuous disagreement score and, for failure cases, a set of attribute-level weights indicating which core visual attributes the original classifier underweighted in its decision. The PIM shares early-layer features with the original classifier (e.g., ResNet-18 block-1 output feeds into PIM's block-2), so no additional forward pass through the full backbone is needed at inference.

## Method

DECIDER trains a Prior Induced Model (PIM) that mirrors the original classifier's architecture but projects its final layer into CLIP's shared image-text embedding space. GPT-3 generates K visually descriptive core attributes per class offline, and the PIM is trained to maximize cosine similarity between its image embeddings and the CLIP text embeddings of those attributes, using cross-entropy loss with CutMix/AugMix augmentations and upweighting of disagreement cases. Failure is detected by computing the cross-entropy between the original classifier's and PIM's predicted probability distributions over classes; high disagreement signals likely failure. For explanations, an iterative attribute-ablation procedure adjusts per-attribute weights to minimize KL divergence between PIM and the original model's predictions, revealing which core attributes the biased classifier effectively ignored.

## Results

DECIDER achieves state-of-the-art failure detection across all evaluated benchmarks, measured by Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC), Failure Recall (FR), and Success Recall (SR). On the subpopulation shift benchmarks, DECIDER attains MCC of 0.4598 on Waterbirds (vs. 0.2827 for the best baseline Ent), 0.4928 on CelebA (vs. 0.3677 for Energy), and 0.544 on Cats vs Dogs (vs. 0.3738 for MSP). On input-level shifts, DECIDER reaches MCC of 0.5292 on clean CIFAR-100 and 0.4015 on CIFAR-100-C (severity 4), outperforming the best baseline (Ent) by 1.5 and 2.5 MCC points respectively. On large-scale covariate shifts (PACS, DomainNet, ImageNet-Sketch), DECIDER consistently surpasses the best baseline in both MCC and failure recall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DECIDER consistently outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and failure scenario. Against MSP [15], the largest MCC gap appears on Waterbirds (0.4598 vs. 0.2419, a 90% relative improvement) and Cats vs Dogs (0.544 vs. 0.3738). Against Predictive Entropy [21], which is typically the strongest single-model baseline, DECIDER gains 17.7 MCC points on Waterbirds (0.4598 vs. 0.2827) and 20.4 points on Cats vs Dogs (0.544 vs. 0.3402). Against Energy [25], DECIDER leads by 12.5 MCC points on Waterbirds and 20.1 points on Cats vs Dogs. Against the ensemble-based GDE [2,18], DECIDER achieves higher MCC on all three subpopulation shift datasets (Waterbirds, CelebA, Cats vs Dogs) as shown in Figure 6, demonstrating that disagreement between a debiased PIM and the original classifier is a more informative failure signal than disagreement among identically-trained ensemble members. Notably, DECIDER also maintains high success recall (>0.85) on Waterbirds and CelebA while simultaneously boosting failure recall, avoiding the conservative/overly-aggressive trade-off that plagues the baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

DECIDER sits at the intersection of failure detection scoring functions (MSP, entropy, energy, ensemble disagreement) and the emerging use of vision-language models to characterize model weaknesses. Prior VLM-based approaches either fit a post-hoc detector on VLM latent space requiring a calibration set [17] or measure raw latent-space agreement between a classifier and a VLM [4]. DECIDER departs from both by training a new 'Prior Induced Model' (PIM) that reuses the original classifier's early features but projects into CLIP's embedding space, guided by LLM-generated class-level attribute descriptions, and then uses cross-entropy disagreement between the original and PIM as the failure score. The attribute-ablation explanation mechanism, which adjusts per-attribute weights to match PIM's output to the original model's, is a novel interpretability layer not present in prior failure-detection work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, plug-in framework for detecting when a deployed vision classifier is likely to fail and for explaining *why* in human-readable attribute terms, which is valuable for safety-critical deployment. However, the approach is limited to distribution-shift failures (spurious correlations, class imbalance, covariate shifts) and explicitly excludes concept shifts; it also depends on the quality of GPT-3-generated attributes and has been validated primarily on ResNet backbones. Open questions include whether the PIM-disagreement signal remains informative when the original model is already well-calibrated, how the method scales to very large attribute sets or open-vocabulary settings, and whether the explanation mechanism generalizes beyond the tested architectures.
