## Problem

Diffusion-based counterfactual explanation methods like DiME require a full inner DDPM process at every reverse step to compute classifier gradients, yielding O(T²) complexity and making large-scale evaluation impractical. Additionally, no prior method provides an automated, quantitative pipeline for detecting and measuring the degree to which a classifier relies on a specific shortcut feature, as existing XAI approaches require manual inspection and cannot confirm causal reliance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242308/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an input image (128×128 for CelebA, 224×224 for medical datasets), a pre-trained DDPM, a guiding classifier (UNet for medical, DenseNet121 for CelebA), and a target attribute to add or remove. It outputs a counterfactual image that modifies only the target feature while preserving the rest of the image, and for shortcut detection, confidence difference metrics (MAD, MD) between original and counterfactually-flipped images. The guiding classifier for generation is distinct from the task classifier being evaluated for shortcut reliance (ResNet-18).

## Method

FastDiME replaces DiME's expensive inner DDPM sampling (O(T²)) with gradient estimation directly from the denoised image x̄_t^c, which is the MSE-optimal estimate of the clean image and asymptotically converges to the noise-free sample as noise decreases, reducing complexity to O(T) and yielding a ~20× speedup. A self-optimized masking scheme is integrated into the reverse diffusion process after a warm-up period τ_w: at each step, a spatial mask is automatically extracted by binarizing the difference between the denoised image and the original input, then applied to both the noisy and denoised images to confine changes to the shortcut region. Unlike ACE's post-hoc inpainting step, this masking is fully gradient-guided within the diffusion loop, preserving counterfactual validity while preventing unintended alterations to non-shortcut features. Two-step variants (FastDiME-2, FastDiME-2+) reuse a fixed mask from a completed run for further efficiency.

## Results

On CelebA, FastDiME-2+ achieves the best FID (3.24 for Smile, 3.60 for Age), lowest MNAC (2.91/2.44), and lowest BKL (0.098/0.179) among all diffusion-based methods, while remaining competitive with ACE on image realism (ACE l1: FID 1.27/1.45). On medical datasets, FastDiME-2+ achieves the best L1 distance (0.0894 CheXpert, 0.0536 NIH, 0.0621 ISIC) and lowest MAD (0.7581/0.2263/0.3905) across all three benchmarks. In terms of efficiency, FastDiME runs in 13.6 s per batch (1.6K MiB GPU) versus 218.8 s for DiME and 41.8 s for ACE (13.1K MiB), yielding a ~16× speedup over DiME and ~3× over ACE with 8× less GPU memory than ACE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiME [38], FastDiME variants consistently outperform on sparsity (MNAC 2.91 vs 3.72 for Smile), validity (BKL 0.083–0.098 vs 0.094), and flip ratio (FR 0.986–0.994 vs 0.972), while achieving a 16× reduction in batch time (13.6 s vs 218.8 s) at the same 1.6K MiB GPU memory. Against ACE [39], FastDiME-2+ matches or exceeds on BKL (0.098 vs 0.199 for Smile), MAD (0.82 vs 0.72), and FR (0.989 vs 0.976), but ACE achieves substantially lower FID (1.27 vs 3.24) due to its adversarial-attack formulation that only needs to cross the decision boundary. Against GMD [42], FastDiME is 2.3× faster (13.6 s vs 31.4 s) with 2.6× less GPU memory (1.6K vs 4.2K MiB). Against STEEX [37] and DiVE [63], FastDiME dominates on all shared metrics (FID 3.24 vs 10.2/29.4; MNAC 2.91 vs 4.11/–). On medical datasets (Table 2), FastDiME-2+ outperforms DiME on all three metrics (L1, MAD, FID) across CheXpert, NIH, and ISIC, e.g., reducing FID from 73.3 to 62.3 on CheXpert and from 121.5 to 87.0 on ISIC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends DiME (Jeanneret et al., ACCV 2022), which first adapted classifier guidance to diffusion models for counterfactual generation, by replacing DiME's expensive inner DDPM re-sampling step (O(T²)) with gradient estimation on the denoised image (O(T)), yielding a ~20× speedup. It also departs from ACE's (CVPR 2023) post-hoc inpainting strategy for spatially localizing changes by integrating a self-optimized mask directly into the guided diffusion loop, preserving counterfactual validity without an extra inference pass. For shortcut detection, the paper moves beyond XAI-based approaches (SmoothGrad, saliency maps) that require manual visual inspection, instead proposing an automated quantitative pipeline built on shortcut counterfactuals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20× inference speedup makes diffusion-based counterfactual generation practical for large-scale evaluations and iterative model debugging, which was previously a major bottleneck. The shortcut detection pipeline offers a concrete, automated alternative to XAI visual inspection for identifying spurious feature reliance in deployed medical classifiers. Key limitations include dependence on an unbiased shortcut classifier (the generative model can itself encode spurious correlations), restriction to spatially localized shortcuts, and the adversarially vulnerable classifier problem that causes generation failures. Open questions include extending the framework to non-local shortcuts, using shortcut counterfactuals for training-time mitigation rather than just detection, and whether the denoised-image gradient trick generalizes to other guided diffusion applications beyond counterfactuals.
