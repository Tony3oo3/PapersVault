## Problem

Human-centric video generation has advanced significantly, but jointly generating video and its corresponding depth map remains underexplored. Monocular depth estimation methods degrade on synthesized content, while multi-view approaches cannot precisely control human appearance and motion from single-view inputs. This paper tackles the challenge of simultaneously generating a high-fidelity video and spatially aligned depth sequence for human actions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240385/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a human foreground image, an arbitrary background image, and a pose sequence (OpenPose or DWPose keypoints) as input, and produces an L-frame video along with a corresponding depth map sequence. Depth maps are reframed as RGB images via colormap rendering (e.g., 'hot' colormap for HDNet foreground depth, grayscale for MiDaS whole-frame depth), converting the depth generation task into a stylized image synthesis problem compatible with pre-trained latent diffusion models. Evaluation uses 8-frame snippets with FID-FVD, FVD for video, L2 distance for depth, and FID for individual frames.

## Method

IDOL introduces a unified dual-modal U-Net that shares all parameters between video and depth denoising, with a learnable one-hot modality embedding added to the timestep conditioning to specify the denoising target. A cross-modal attention layer at each block performs spatial self-attention over concatenated video and depth features, enabling explicit mutual information flow during joint denoising. To enforce precise video-depth spatial alignment, the paper proposes a motion consistency loss that minimizes the MSE between cost-volume-derived motion fields of intermediate video and depth self-attention features, and a cross-attention map consistency loss that aligns the foreground-to-noise cross-attention maps across both modalities. The model is built on a 3D U-Net with temporal convolutions, uses ControlNet for pose conditioning, CLIP-encoded foreground latent for appearance, and introduces a human attribute outpainting (HAOP) pre-training stage with background dilation and random crop/resize to improve generalization.

## Results

On the TikTok dataset (HDNet depth), IDOL achieves FID-FVD 17.86, FVD 223.69, depth L2 0.0336, and FID 36.04, outperforming all baselines. On NTU120, IDOL reaches FID-FVD 20.23, FVD 314.82, depth L2 0.0317, and FID 50.70. The closest video-only competitor, DisCo, scores FID-FVD 20.75 (TikTok) and 26.21 (NTU120), while the adapted multi-modal baselines LDM3D and MM-Diffusion lag substantially (e.g., FVD 553.03 and 771.32 on TikTok respectively). IDOL also achieves the best depth accuracy, with L2 0.0336 (TikTok) and 0.0317 (NTU120), compared to 0.0975 for DisCo's post-hoc HDNet inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDOL outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against the strongest video-only baseline DisCo [68], IDOL improves FID-FVD from 20.75 to 17.86 and FVD from 257.90 to 223.69 on TikTok, while also achieving a 3× lower depth L2 (0.0336 vs. 0.0975) because DisCo's depth is obtained via post-hoc HDNet inference on generated frames. The adapted multi-modal baselines LDM3D [64] and MM-Diffusion [58] are substantially worse: on TikTok, IDOL's FVD of 223.69 beats LDM3D's 553.03 and MM-Diffusion's 771.32, and its FID of 36.04 beats 69.36 and 68.47 respectively. Among motion-transfer methods, FOMM [62], MRAA [63], TPS [79], and DreamPose [30] cannot generate depth at all and trail IDOL by 10–35 points in FID-FVD on TikTok. On NTU120, the gap widens further: IDOL's FVD of 314.82 is roughly 1.5–2× lower than all competitors, and its depth L2 of 0.0317 is the best among all methods that produce depth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DisCo's disentangled human dance generation framework (appearance via CLIP cross-attention, pose via ControlNet, background via latent addition) and extends it to jointly produce depth maps alongside video. It departs from prior multi-modal diffusion approaches (LDM3D for image-depth, MM-Diffusion for video-audio) by proposing a parameter-sharing unified dual-modal U-Net with cross-modal attention, rather than separate or coupled U-Net branches. The key conceptual departure is reframing depth synthesis as stylized RGB image generation (via colormap rendering), which allows direct reuse of pre-trained latent diffusion backbones, and introducing motion/cross-attention consistency losses to enforce video-depth spatial alignment in latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical gap: monocular depth estimators (HDNet, MiDaS) degrade significantly on synthesized human content, while multi-view 3D methods cannot control human appearance and motion, leaving no viable path to depth-aware human video for VR/AR and gaming applications. The finding that a single shared U-Net (half the parameters of separate networks) outperforms the separate-U-Net baseline on both modalities suggests that cross-modal structural information is genuinely beneficial rather than merely neutral. Open questions include whether the approach scales to non-human subjects or full 3D scene generation, whether the reliance on high-quality ground-truth depth maps for training can be relaxed via self-supervision, and whether the computational cost of dual-modal denoising can be reduced for real-time use.
