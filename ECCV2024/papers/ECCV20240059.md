## Problem

Existing depth completion methods face a fundamental trade-off: deep learning approaches achieve high in-domain accuracy but fail catastrophically under distribution shifts in scene type or sparsity patterns, while optimization-based methods generalize well but lack sufficient accuracy. No prior method achieves both state-of-the-art accuracy and strong cross-dataset/cross-density robustness simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240059/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, a sparse depth observation map, and a binary mask indicating valid observation locations, and produces a full-resolution dense depth map. Intermediate depth prediction operates at 1/4 resolution to balance accuracy and computational cost, with a final up-sampling and Spatial Propagation Network enhancement step to recover high-resolution details. The system is designed to handle a wide range of input sparsity (5 to 20,000 points on NYUv2; 8 to 64 Lidar lines on KITTI) with a single model, using random masking during training to build density invariance.

## Method

OGNI-DC replaces direct depth regression with iterative refinement of depth gradients coupled with a Differentiable Depth Integrator (DDI). A ConvGRU (architecture from RAFT) predicts gradient refinements at each of 5 iterations, conditioned on the current integrated depth map, while DDI solves a linear least-squares problem—balancing gradient consistency and sparse observation constraints—via the conjugate gradient method to produce an intermediate depth map. The integration result is fed back to the ConvGRU, creating a tightly coupled loop where the network is aware of the consequences of its gradient predictions. DDI is fully differentiable, enabling end-to-end training, and its explicit enforcement of observation constraints (as soft energy terms rather than hard constraints) is the key mechanism providing robustness to varying sparsity patterns without retraining. A CompletionFormer backbone extracts multi-scale features, and a DySPN module enhances the up-sampled depth map at full resolution.

## Results

On the NYUv2 benchmark, OGNI-DC (L1+L2) achieves the best RMSE of 0.087 m and REL of 0.011, improving over the previous best (BEV@DC, 0.089 m). On the KITTI benchmark, the L1-trained model achieves state-of-the-art MAE of 182.29 mm, iRMSE of 1.81, and iMAE of 0.79, while the L1+L2 model achieves RMSE of 708.38 mm. For zero-shot generalization, OGNI-DC reduces MAE by 34.2% on VOID150 (0.261 vs. 0.397 for VPP4DC) and RMSE by 25.0% on DDAD (6.876 vs. 9.164 for LRRU). On KITTI with reduced Lidar lines, OGNI-DC reduces MAE by 25.5% on 16-Lines input (451.9 vs. 606.9 mm for SpAgNet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OGNI-DC achieves the best in-domain performance on both standard benchmarks: on NYUv2 it beats BEV@DC (0.089 m) and CFormer/DySPN/RigNet (0.090 m) in RMSE, and on KITTI the L1 model sets new records for MAE (182.29 mm vs. CFormer's 183.88 mm), iRMSE (1.81 vs. 1.89), and iMAE (0.79 vs. 0.80). For zero-shot cross-dataset generalization, OGNI-DC outperforms all five baselines (NLSPN, SpAgNet, CFormer, LRRU, VPP4DC) on every VOID and DDAD metric, with the largest gains on the sparsest VOID150 setting (MAE 0.261 vs. 0.397 for VPP4DC). On sparsity robustness, OGNI-DC beats NLSPN, CFormer, and their masked variants across nearly all point counts on NYUv2 and all Lidar line counts on KITTI, though it is slightly worse than SpAgNet at the extreme 5-point case (RMSE 0.633 vs. 0.467 m). The L1+L2 variant's KITTI RMSE (708.38 mm) is marginally behind LRRU (696.51 mm) and BEV@DC (697.44 mm), but the L1 variant dominates on the other three KITTI metrics. Overall, OGNI-DC is the only method that simultaneously achieves SOTA in-domain accuracy and the strongest zero-shot and cross-sparsity generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

OGNI-DC bridges two previously separate lines of depth completion research: optimization-based methods (e.g., Zhang et al. 2018) that enforce sparse observation constraints and generalize well but lack accuracy, and deep-learning-based methods (SPN variants, CFormer) that achieve high in-domain accuracy but fail catastrophically under distribution shift. The core architectural idea—coupling a recurrent refinement network with a differentiable optimization layer—draws directly from DROID-SLAM and DPVO, which apply similar coupled iteration to multiview SLAM, but OGNI-DC is the first to adapt this pattern to a single-view task with a linear least-squares integrator rather than a non-linear bundle adjustment. The ConvGRU and convex up-sampling are adopted from RAFT, and the backbone is CompletionFormer, but the key departure is predicting depth gradients (a local property) rather than depth values (a global property), with the Differentiable Depth Integrator explicitly enforcing observation constraints at every iteration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the accuracy-robustness tradeoff in depth completion is not fundamental: by predicting an easier local quantity (depth gradients) and enforcing observation constraints through a differentiable optimization layer, a single model achieves both SOTA in-domain accuracy and large zero-shot generalization gains (35.5% MAE reduction on VOID, 25% RMSE reduction on DDAD). The DDI concept—a differentiable linear solver embedded as a network layer—could transfer to other tasks where local geometric properties are easier to predict than global ones. A noted limitation is that at extreme sparsity (5 points on NYUv2), gradient integration errors accumulate in large unobserved regions, though the authors argue this is unlikely in practice. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other sparse-to-dense reconstruction tasks and whether the conjugate-gradient solver can be replaced by learned approximations for further speedup.
