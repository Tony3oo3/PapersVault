## Problem

Existing methods for generating animatable 3D human avatars from images either rely on template-based canonicalization that fails for loose clothing and hair, require ground-truth canonical meshes for supervision, or are sensitive to pose estimation errors when warping posed reconstructions into canonical space. CanonicalFusion addresses this by fusing multiple posed reconstructions into a single canonical mesh via forward skinning-based differentiable rendering that simultaneously refines shape, color, and joint angles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240593/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an arbitrary number of RGB images (512×512) along with their corresponding SMPL-X parameters, which are used to generate dual-sided depth maps as network guidance. The output is a Flexicubes-reparametrized canonical mesh with per-vertex colors and full 55-dimensional skinning weights, enabling arbitrary reposing. Notably, the LBS weights are predicted in a compressed 3-dimensional latent space and decoded back to full dimensionality at inference time, and the framework imposes no restriction on the number of input views or pose variations.

## Method

A shared-encoder-dual-decoder ATUNet network jointly predicts dual-sided depth maps and compressed 3D LBS weight maps from each input image, where the LBS compression is achieved via a separately pretrained stacked autoencoder MLP that maps 55-dimensional skinning weights to a 3D latent code. Each posed mesh is canonicalized via inverse skinning, and holes in the canonical space (e.g., armpits, thighs) are filled by integrating signed distance fields from the canonicalized mesh and the SMPL-X template within a 2m³ volume. The resulting canonical mesh is reparametrized with Flexicubes for differentiability, then refined through forward skinning-based differentiable rendering (Neural Deferred Shading) that warps the canonical mesh to each input pose and minimizes normal, mask, Chamfer, and color losses while simultaneously updating joint angles to correct pose errors. A progressive update scheme gradually increases mesh resolution and loss weights over 2000 iterations, followed by a dedicated color optimization phase.

## Results

On the three standard monocular human reconstruction benchmarks (RenderPeople, 2K2K, THuman 3.0), CanonicalFusion (trained on TH2.0+RP) achieves the best point-to-surface distance (P2S) of 0.886 cm, Chamfer distance (CF) of 0.943 cm, and normal error (NR) of 0.987 on RP; 0.897/0.981/0.793 on 2K2K; and 1.072/1.165/1.278 on TH3.0, outperforming all baselines by a clear margin. In the canonical-space comparison against SCANimate (Table 2), CanonicalFusion reduces P2S from 1.362 cm to 0.244 cm (5-view, SET1) and CF from 1.316 cm to 0.260 cm, a roughly 5× improvement, demonstrating superior multi-view fusion in canonical space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CanonicalFusion outperforms all eight monocular reconstruction baselines across every metric on all three test datasets. The closest competitor, 2K2K* (also trained on TH2.0+RP), is beaten by 0.21 cm in P2S on RP (0.886 vs. 1.097) and by 0.34 cm on TH3.0 (1.072 vs. 1.416); its normal error is also lower (0.987 vs. 1.507 on RP). ICON* and PIFuHD, both trained on RP, trail by 0.4–0.5 cm in P2S on RP. ECON* (trained on TH2.0) performs worst overall, with P2S of 2.066 cm on RP, likely due to domain gap. In the canonical-space multi-view comparison (Table 2), CanonicalFusion reduces P2S by ~5.5× versus SCANimate (0.244 vs. 1.362 cm, 5-view SET1) and normal error by ~6× (0.482 vs. 2.921), confirming that forward-skinning-based fusion with pose refinement substantially outperforms SCANimate's implicit skinning-field approach, especially under pose errors.

## Relation to Prior Work

CanonicalFusion sits at the intersection of explicit depth-based human reconstruction (2K2K, ECON, PIFuHD) and drivable avatar generation (ARCH, ARCH++, SCANimate, SNARF). It departs from the inverse-skinning paradigm used by SCANimate and the implicit skinning fields of ARCH/SNARF by directly predicting compressed LBS weights (a 3D latent vector decoded via a pre-trained autoencoder) alongside dual-sided depth maps, enabling canonicalization without template fitting. The forward skinning-based differentiable rendering step, which simultaneously refines pose, shape, and color across multiple views, builds on Neural Deferred Shading and Flexicubes but applies them in a multi-image canonical fusion context not previously explored in this form.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, training-free canonicalization pipeline that works with an arbitrary number of input images and does not require ground-truth canonical meshes, making it applicable to in-the-wild photo sets. The compressed 3D LBS representation is an elegant solution to the high-dimensional, sparse nature of skinning weights that could be reused in other avatar pipelines. However, the authors acknowledge that non-rigid cloth and hair deformation remain unaddressed, and the method's reliance on SMPL-X parameters as input limits its applicability to scenarios where pose estimation is unreliable. Combining this explicit reconstruction with generative or diffusion-based techniques for diversification is identified as a natural next step.
