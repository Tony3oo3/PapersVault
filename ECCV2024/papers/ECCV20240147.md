## Problem

Existing benchmarks for evaluating large vision-language models suffer from two fundamental limitations: objective datasets (VQAv2, COCO Caption) lack fine-grained ability decomposition and produce false negatives due to exact-match scoring, while subjective benchmarks (OwlEval, LVLM-eHub) are expensive, biased, and not scalable. There is no systematic, objective, and comprehensive benchmark that can robustly assess the diverse perception and reasoning capabilities of VLMs across a balanced set of fine-grained skills.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240147/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation sample consists of an RGB image paired with a multiple-choice question containing 2–4 answer choices, presented in both English (MMBench) and Chinese (MMBench-CN). The VLM receives the image and question in a zero-shot open-ended generation setting and produces a free-form text prediction. The evaluation pipeline then maps this prediction to a choice label (via heuristic matching or GPT-4-based extraction) and scores it under CircularEval, where the question is re-presented N times with circularly shifted choices and the VLM must answer correctly in every pass. Final outputs are per-ability-dimension accuracy scores across 20 L-3 categories and an overall ranking.

## Method

MMBench organizes 3,217 multiple-choice questions into a three-level hierarchical taxonomy (Perception/Reasoning → 6 L-2 → 20 L-3 abilities) with roughly balanced question counts (≥125 per L-3). Two quality-control filters remove low-quality samples: majority voting across state-of-the-art LLMs (GPT-4, Gemini-Pro) flags questions answerable from text alone, and consensus failure across multiple VLMs flags potentially incorrect questions. To handle VLMs with weak instruction-following, a two-step choice extraction pipeline first attempts heuristic label matching and falls back to GPT-4 (gpt-4-0125) to align free-form predictions with candidate choices, achieving 91.5% alignment with human annotators. The key evaluation innovation is CircularEval: each question is presented N times (N = number of choices) with circularly shifted option orderings, and a VLM is scored correct only if it succeeds in all N passes, which amplifies performance gaps and reduces the impact of choice-position bias at a cost lower than N× full inference due to early termination on failure.

## Results

Under the CircularEval protocol on MMBench-test, the best open-source model InternLM-XComposer2 achieves 78.1% overall Top-1 accuracy, surpassing all proprietary VLMs including Qwen-VL-Max (75.4%) and GPT-4v (74.3%). Performance spans a wide range from 2.3% (OpenFlamingo v2, essentially random) to 78.1%, with the six L-2 ability dimensions (Coarse Perception, Fine-grained Perception single/cross-instance, Attribute Reasoning, Logic Reasoning, Relation Reasoning) revealing distinct model strengths. A text-only GPT-4 sanity check scores only 2.9%, confirming that questions cannot be answered without visual input. Switching from VanillaEval to CircularEval causes accuracy drops of 5–34% across models, with weaker models (e.g., OpenFlamingo v2: 36.7%→2.6%) suffering the largest penalties.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InternLM-XComposer2 (78.1%) outperforms all proprietary VLMs, beating Qwen-VL-Max (75.4%) by 2.7% and GPT-4v (74.3%) by 3.8% overall, with particularly large margins in Logic Reasoning (63.6% vs. 54.9% and 63.6%) and Fine-grained Perception single-instance (83.5% vs. 87.2% and 73.8%). Among proprietary models, Qwen-VL-Max leads on Fine-grained Perception single-instance (87.2%) and Relation Reasoning (70.5%), while GPT-4v excels in Attribute Reasoning (85.3%, tied with Qwen-VL-Max) and Logic Reasoning (63.6%). The LLaVA family shows clear scaling benefits: LLaVA-InternLM2-20B (72.3%) surpasses LLaVA-v1.5-13B (66.9%) by 5.4%, largely driven by the stronger InternLM2 backbone. Weaker models (MiniGPT4, IDEFICS, VisualGLM, InstructBLIP) cluster between 30–40%, while OpenFlamingo v2 (2.3%) and text-only GPT-4 (2.9%) perform at random level, confirming the benchmark's quality control. Proprietary models show a consistent advantage over the best open-source model specifically on structuralized image-text understanding and tasks requiring external knowledge (e.g., celebrity recognition), but do not dominate across all 20 fine-grained abilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

MMBench carves new ground at the intersection of two prior evaluation paradigms: objective benchmarks (VQAv2, GQA, COCO Caption) that suffer from false negatives and lack fine-grained ability decomposition, and subjective benchmarks (OwlEval, LVLM-eHub) that are expensive, biased, and non-scalable. It departs from both by introducing a hierarchical 20-ability taxonomy, a bilingual (EN/CN) question set, and two methodological innovations—LLM-based choice extraction (repurposing GPT-4 as a robust parser for free-form VLM outputs) and the CircularEval strategy (circularly shifting choices across multiple passes to eliminate choice-position bias). The LLM-as-judge component draws on the broader trend of using powerful LLMs for evaluation, but applies it specifically to the underexplored problem of aligning free-form VLM predictions with multiple-choice options.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMBench addresses a critical bottleneck in VLM research: the lack of a comprehensive, reproducible, and fine-grained evaluation standard. The CircularEval strategy is a practical methodological contribution that any future benchmark can adopt to reduce choice-bias inflation. Open questions remain: whether the 20 ability dimensions adequately capture emerging VLM capabilities (e.g., video, 3D, agentic reasoning), whether the LLM-based choice extraction introduces subtle systematic biases that differ across model families, and how quickly the benchmark will saturate as models improve. The finding that proprietary models' advantages are concentrated in structuralized understanding and external-knowledge tasks—rather than being uniform—provides a concrete roadmap for open-source model development.
