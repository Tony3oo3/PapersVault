## Problem

Most 3D human avatars reconstructed from casually captured monocular videos only support body motions, baking facial expressions and hand poses into the geometry. Existing whole-body avatars that do support facial expression animation (e.g., X-Avatar) require 3D observations such as 3D scans or RGBD images with accurate SMPL-X registrations and diverse facial expressions—assumptions that do not hold for short in-the-wild videos. The paper addresses both the limited pose/expression diversity in short videos and the absence of 3D supervision that causes artifacts in unobserved body parts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a short (~30-second) monocular RGB video of a single person in an in-the-wild setting as the sole training input, with no depth maps, multi-view images, or 3D scans. After training, the avatar is animated at test time by SMPL-X facial expression codes (ψ ∈ R^50) and 3D body/hand poses (θ ∈ R^{55×3}), producing photorealistic renderings at novel viewpoints and novel expressions/poses. The canonical mesh is upsampled to 167K vertices and 335K triangle faces via PyTorch3D subdivision, and all Gaussians are constrained to be isotropic (unit rotation, unit opacity) for better generalization.

## Method

ExAvatar introduces a hybrid representation that treats each 3D Gaussian as a vertex on a surface with pre-defined triangle-face connectivity inherited from SMPL-X mesh topology, rather than as an unstructured point cloud. A learnable triplane regresses per-vertex Gaussian assets (3D offset, scale, RGB) shared across all frames, while two additional MLPs predict pose-dependent offsets for geometry and appearance. Because the Gaussians share SMPL-X's exact topology, facial expression animation is achieved by directly adding SMPL-X/FLAME vertex offsets—no learned expression-to-implicit mapping is needed. A connectivity-based Laplacian regularizer (applied to positions, scales, and RGBs) enforces local similarity between neighboring Gaussians, dramatically reducing floating-point artifacts in novel poses compared to standard L2 regularization. A dedicated face loss using a differentiable mesh renderer with fixed FLAME UV textures enforces geometry-texture consistency in the face region, and identity-dependent joint and face offsets are optimized during registration to improve co-registration of hands and face.

## Results

On the NeuMan test set (foreground only), ExAvatar achieves 34.80 PSNR, 0.984 SSIM, and 0.009 LPIPS, outperforming the next-best method Vid2Avatar (30.70 PSNR) by over 4 dB. On X-Humans, ExAvatar surpasses X-Avatar (which uses additional RGBD depth maps) on all three subjects, e.g., 30.58 vs. 28.57 PSNR on subject 0028. When rendered backgrounds are included (Table 1), ExAvatar still leads with 27.47 PSNR vs. HUGS's 25.17 PSNR. The ablation confirms the Laplacian regularizer contributes the largest single gain, boosting PSNR from 28.21 to 34.80.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExAvatar outperforms all seven NeuMan baselines on every metric. The largest gap is against Vid2Avatar (30.70 PSNR) and GaussianAvatar (29.94 PSNR), with ExAvatar leading by 4.1 and 4.9 dB respectively; the smallest gap is against Vid2Avatar on SSIM (0.984 vs. 0.980). Against the volumetric baselines HumanNeRF (27.06 PSNR) and InstantAvatar (28.47 PSNR), the advantage is even more pronounced at 7.7 and 6.3 dB. On X-Humans, ExAvatar beats X-Avatar [41] on all three subjects despite not using the RGBD depth maps that X-Avatar relies on (e.g., 32.01 vs. 30.89 PSNR on subject 0087, 0.972 vs. 0.970 SSIM). In the background-included NeuMan evaluation (Table 1), ExAvatar leads HUGS by 2.3 dB and NeuMan by 3.3 dB, while Vid2Avatar degrades severely (15.41 PSNR) due to its scene decomposition approach. Notably, ExAvatar is the only method in all comparisons that supports simultaneous facial expression and hand pose animation, a capability none of the baselines provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

ExAvatar sits at the intersection of two lines of work: parametric mesh-based avatars (SMPL-X, FLAME) and 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering. Prior 3DGS-based avatars (GaussianAvatar, HUGS) treat Gaussians as unstructured point clouds, while volumetric avatars (NeuMan, Vid2Avatar, X-Avatar) lack explicit mesh connectivity. The key conceptual departure is imposing SMPL-X mesh topology on the 3D Gaussians, turning them into a connected surface mesh that can directly inherit SMPL-X's facial expression space and skinning weights, and enabling connectivity-based (Laplacian) regularizers that were previously inapplicable to point-cloud representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to produce a fully expressive whole-body avatar (face, hands, body) from a short monocular video without any 3D observations, removing the studio-capture requirement of X-Avatar. The dramatic ablation showing that a simple Laplacian regularizer (enabled by the mesh topology) yields a ~6.6 dB PSNR improvement over no regularization highlights that connectivity is a critical, previously underexploited signal for 3DGS-based human modeling. Open questions include handling dynamic clothing, hallucinating unobserved regions (mouth interior, palms), and extending to relightable rendering.
