## Problem

Existing real-time neural rendering methods on the web (e.g., MERF) are limited to small-scale scenes due to constrained computation, memory, and bandwidth on commodity devices. While block-based approaches like Block-NeRF and Mega-NeRF can handle large scenes, they require all block resources to be loaded simultaneously into a single shader, which is incompatible with web shader texture-unit limits. This paper addresses the gap of enabling real-time, high-fidelity rendering of large-scale scenes on the web under these resource-independent constraints.

## Input / Output

At rendering time, the system consumes per-block baked sparse voxel and triplane textures (compressed as PNG), multi-level occupancy grids for ray sampling, and small deferred MLP weights, all indexed by camera position and field of view to dynamically select which blocks and LOD levels to load. The output is a 1920×1080 RGB frame rendered at approximately 32 FPS on an RTX 3060, with each block rendered independently in its own shader and composited via depth-sorted alpha blending. Peak VRAM is kept around 1100 MB through the dynamic loading strategy, even though the full scene spans 1200×800 m² with 6515 training images.

## Method

The core contribution is a block-based multi-shader volume rendering strategy: each scene block is rendered independently in its own shader (respecting web texture-unit limits), then blocks are depth-sorted and combined via sequential alpha blending (Eq. 5), which is proven equivalent to standard volume rendering under the Lambertian assumption. To manage memory for large scenes, a hierarchical LOD scheme merges 2×2 blocks at each level, with LODs generated by downsampling virtual grid points and retraining a shared deferred MLP across merged blocks (taking ~4 hours vs. 12 hours for retraining from scratch). A global deferred MLP regularizes per-block specular colors to ensure smoothness across block boundaries, and a sparsity loss plus opacity loss encourage compact occupancy. During training, the same block-by-block rendering pipeline is used to avoid train-render inconsistency, and the final models are baked into sparse voxel + triplane textures for efficient web deployment.

## Results

On the four evaluation datasets (Matrix City, Campus, Rubble, Building), City-on-Web achieves the highest PSNR on Matrix City (25.87 dB) and Campus (24.73 dB), outperforming Mega-NeRF (25.43, 22.28) and NeRFacto (24.95, 23.47). On the real-time comparison (Table 2), the 16-block configuration reaches 25.13 PSNR and 0.167 LPIPS with 34 FPS on an RTX 3060, surpassing MERF (24.02 PSNR, 58 FPS) and 3D Gaussian Splatting at 100k iterations (24.94 PSNR, 58 FPS) while using substantially less VRAM (2040 MB vs. 1467 MB for GS). On the full Block All scene (Table 3), the method achieves 25.87 PSNR at 34 FPS with 912 MB VRAM, compared to Grid-NeRF at 0.55 FPS and Gaussian Splatting at 17 FPS with 1978 MB VRAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Mega-NeRF (the strongest large-scale NeRF baseline), City-on-Web wins on PSNR for Matrix City (+0.44 dB) and Campus (+2.45 dB) but loses on Rubble (−2.36 dB) and Building (−0.80 dB); it also achieves the best LPIPS and SSIM on Building (0.397 / 0.578) while trailing on the other three datasets. Against Instant-NGP, Ours dominates on PSNR across all four datasets (e.g., +2.32 dB on Matrix City, +2.82 dB on Campus) but reports higher LPIPS and lower SSIM, which the authors attribute to PSNR measuring pixel-wise color accuracy while LPIPS/SSIM are more sensitive to fine geometric detail. Against NeRFacto, Ours wins on PSNR for Matrix City and Campus but underperforms on Rubble and Building. In the real-time comparison (Table 2), Ours (16 blocks) outperforms MERF (4 blocks) by +1.11 dB PSNR and −0.087 LPIPS at the cost of lower FPS (34 vs. 58), and beats 3D Gaussian Splatting (100k iters) on PSNR (+0.19 dB) and LPIPS (−0.060) while using 30% less VRAM. On the full Block All scene (Table 3), Ours surpasses Grid-NeRF by +0.97 dB PSNR at 62× higher FPS (34 vs. 0.55) and Gaussian Splatting by +0.45 dB PSNR at 2× higher FPS (34 vs. 17) with half the VRAM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines MERF's memory-efficient hybrid representation (sparse voxel + triplane with deferred MLP) and Mega-NeRF's block-based scene partitioning, but departs from both in a critical way: it renders each block independently in its own shader rather than requiring all block resources to be loaded simultaneously. The key architectural contribution is a block-based volume rendering strategy with depth-sorted sequential alpha blending, proven equivalent to standard volume rendering under Lambertian assumptions, which solves the 3D consistency problem when a ray traverses multiple blocks rendered by different shaders. The LOD generation strategy (downsampling virtual grid points and retraining a shared deferred MLP across merged blocks) draws from traditional graphics techniques like progressive meshes and clipmaps, adapted to the neural rendering setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first system enabling real-time neural rendering of city-scale scenes in a web browser on consumer hardware (32 FPS on RTX 3060), which has practical implications for making large-scale 3D content accessible without specialized installations. The paper identifies an interesting broader implication: the independent-block rendering paradigm could reduce communication overhead in multi-GPU or distributed training by transferring only rendered color and opacity rather than per-sample attributes. Key limitations remain: visible seams on non-Lambertian surfaces (e.g., water), inability to run on mobile devices, and color discrepancies under variable lighting due to the deferred MLP's limited view-dependent modeling capacity.
