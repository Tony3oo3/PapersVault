## Problem

Existing anomaly synthesis strategies for industrial anomaly detection suffer from a fundamental trade-off: image-level methods (e.g., CutPaste, DRAEM) produce detailed textures but lack diversity, while feature-level methods (e.g., SimpleNet, UniAD) are efficient but synthesize anomalies in an undirected, uncontrollable manner. This limitation is particularly acute for weak defects—small-area or low-contrast anomalies that lie very close to the normal feature distribution—where random Gaussian noise fails to push synthetic anomalies across the classification boundary effectively.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images resized and center-cropped to 288×288 pixels. At inference, only the normal branch (frozen WideResNet50 backbone extracting level-2 and level-3 features, a trainable single-layer feature adaptor, and a single-hidden-layer MLP discriminator) processes the test image, producing a pixel-level anomaly score map via upsampling and Gaussian smoothing of the discriminator output, and an image-level anomaly score as the maximum value across all spatial locations. The training phase additionally consumes DTD texture images and Perlin noise masks for the LAS branch, but these are discarded at test time.

## Method

GLASS trains three branches jointly: a Normal branch, a feature-level Global Anomaly Synthesis (GAS) branch, and an image-level Local Anomaly Synthesis (LAS) branch, all sharing a frozen WideResNet50 extractor, a trainable feature adaptor, and a segmentation discriminator. The key innovation is GAS, which first adds Gaussian noise to normal features, then iteratively applies gradient ascent on the GAS-branch BCE loss to direct the perturbation away from the normal distribution, and finally applies truncated projection to clamp the displacement within a controllable annular region (r₁ < ‖Δ‖₂ < r₂ under the manifold hypothesis, or r₁′ < ‖Δ‖₂ < r₂′ from the hypersphere center under the hypersphere hypothesis). LAS complements GAS by overlaying augmented DTD textures onto Perlin-noise-shaped masks with a stochastic transparency coefficient, generating diverse strong local anomalies. The discriminator is trained end-to-end with BCE loss on normal and GAS features and Focal loss with Online Hard Example Mining on LAS features, yielding a tighter classification boundary that specifically improves weak-defect detection.

## Results

GLASS-j achieves state-of-the-art performance across four datasets, reaching 99.9% image-level AUROC and 99.3% pixel-level AUROC on MVTec AD, 98.8/98.8 on VisA, 99.6/99.4 on MPDD, and 100/98.9 on the self-built WFDD dataset, with an average of 99.6/99.1/95.7 (image AUROC / pixel AUROC / pixel PRO). On the weak defect test sets, GLASS achieves 97.6/96.3/88.9 average, substantially outperforming the next-best method (SimpleNet at 92.0/91.8/76.0). GLASS also delivers the highest inference throughput at 1327 img/s, exceeding SimpleNet (1306) and RD++ (623) while PatchCore runs at only 31 img/s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLASS-j outperforms all five baselines on every dataset and every metric. Against the closest competitor SimpleNet (also feature-level synthesis), GLASS-j gains +1.2% image AUROC, +0.9% pixel AUROC, and +3.9% pixel PRO on average, with the largest gap on MVTec AD pixel-level PRO (96.8 vs. 90.0). Compared to RD++ (image-level synthesis), GLASS-j leads by +3.0% image AUROC and +1.8% pixel PRO on average, while also running more than 2× faster (1327 vs. 623 img/s). PatchCore and BGAD trail by 3.7–3.8% in image AUROC on average; PatchCore is notably the slowest at 31 img/s. DSR is the weakest baseline, lagging GLASS-j by 9.0% image AUROC and 23.2% pixel PRO on average, particularly struggling on VisA (88.0 vs. 98.8 image AUROC). On the dedicated weak defect benchmarks (MAD-man, MAD-sys), GLASS's advantage over SimpleNet widens to +5.6% image AUROC and +12.9% pixel PRO, confirming that gradient-ascent-guided synthesis is most beneficial for near-in-distribution anomalies.

## Relation to Prior Work

GLASS unifies two previously separate lines of anomaly synthesis: feature-level synthesis (SimpleNet, UniAD) that adds undirected Gaussian noise to normal features, and image-level synthesis (DRAEM, CutPaste, NSA) that overlays DTD textures via Perlin masks. The key departure is the introduction of gradient ascent guided by the discriminator loss, combined with truncated projection under manifold or hypersphere distribution constraints, to make feature-level synthesis directional and controllable—addressing the core limitation that Gaussian noise alone cannot reliably produce near-in-distribution anomalies. The feature adaptor architecture is adapted from CFA, and the overall training paradigm (frozen backbone + trainable adaptor + discriminator) follows the SimpleNet lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for industrial inspection: GLASS achieves 99.9% image-level AUROC on MVTec AD at 1327 img/s throughput, and shows the largest gains (5.6% over SimpleNet) specifically on weak defects, which are the hardest and most economically costly to miss in production. The introduction of MAD-man (human-curated weak defects) and MAD-sys (synthetic weak defects at graded severity) provides the community with much-needed benchmarks for this underexplored regime. The authors acknowledge that logical anomalies (e.g., missing components, wrong assembly) remain unaddressed, and that the method still depends on an external DTD texture dataset for LAS—both are natural next steps.
