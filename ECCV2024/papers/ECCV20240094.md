## Problem

Existing text-to-motion diffusion models generate realistic human motions but treat characters in isolation, ignoring environmental context and object interactions. This limitation stems from the scarcity of datasets pairing 3D human motion with scene geometry and text annotations, making it difficult to learn scene-aware behaviors like navigating around obstacles or sitting on a chair while maintaining text-controllable style.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240094/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D scene (projected to a 2D bird's-eye floor map for navigation; represented as a 1024-point Basis Point Set within a 1 m sphere for interaction), a free-form text prompt, and start/goal pelvis poses (position + heading). It produces a two-part output: a scene-aware root trajectory (per-frame pelvis x,y,z,cosθ,sinθ) that is lifted to full-body motion via PriorMDM in-painting, followed by a full-body interaction sequence (268-dim per frame including joint positions, velocities, rotations, and foot contacts) conditioned on the target object's geometry.

## Method

TeSMo decomposes scene-aware motion generation into navigation and interaction stages, each built on a pre-trained scene-agnostic text-to-motion diffusion model (MDM) with an augmented ControlNet-style transformer branch fine-tuned on frozen base weights to inject scene awareness without degrading text controllability. For navigation, the scene-aware branch encodes a 2D floor map via ResNet-18 and queries per-frame pelvis positions to produce spatial features; goal-reaching is enforced through input masking of start/end poses plus test-time gradient guidance on an SDF-based collision objective. For interaction, the scene-aware branch uses Basis Point Sets to encode both object geometry and per-frame human-object proximity, and is fine-tuned on SAMP sitting motions re-targeted to diverse 3D-FRONT chair shapes. Training data is augmented by placing HumanML3D locomotion sequences into 3D-FRONT rooms (yielding ~95k Loco-3D-FRONT pairs) and extracting/annotating sub-sequences from SAMP with varied objects and text descriptions, enabling the model to generalize across object shapes, orientations, and initial poses.

## Results

On the Loco-3D-FRONT navigation test set, TeSMo achieves the best goal-reaching accuracy (16.9 cm position error, 0.119 rad orientation) and the lowest collision ratio (0.031) among all scene-aware methods, while maintaining competitive full-body motion quality (FID 20.47, R-precision 0.376, foot skating 0.056). On the SAMP sitting interaction test set, TeSMo reduces object penetration value by 4.4× (0.0043 vs. 0.0193) and penetration ratio by 43% (0.0611 vs. 0.1076) compared to DIMOS, and is preferred by users 71.9% of the time in a perceptual study.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GMD [18] and OmniControl [41] (scene-agnostic baselines), TeSMo achieves substantially better goal-reaching (16.9 cm vs. 37.4 cm and 122.6 cm position error) and the best foot skating (0.056 vs. 0.181 and 0.094), confirming that scene-awareness is added without sacrificing motion realism. Against TRACE [32], the closest scene-aware baseline, TeSMo wins on every metric: lower position error (16.9 vs. 20.5 cm), lower collision ratio (0.031 vs. 0.055), lower FID (20.47 vs. 22.67), and higher R-precision (0.376 vs. 0.144), demonstrating the benefit of the two-branch pre-train-then-fine-tune architecture over a single-branch design. For object interaction, TeSMo dominates DIMOS [52] across all metrics: 40% lower position error (14.5 vs. 20.2 cm), 10.7× lower height error (1.2 vs. 12.8 cm), 49% lower orientation error (0.241 vs. 0.473 rad), 4.4× lower penetration value, and a 71.9% vs. 29.1% user preference margin. The ablation (1-stage train row) further shows that pre-training on scene-agnostic data before fine-tuning the scene-aware branch is critical, as training from scratch degrades both goal-reaching and full-body motion quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

TeSMo builds on the text-to-motion diffusion model of Tevet et al. [36] as its scene-agnostic base, the PriorMDM in-painting framework [33] for lifting root trajectories to full-body motion, and the augmented ControlNet-style branch pattern from OmniControl [41] to inject scene information without degrading the pre-trained prior. For scene representation, it adopts the ResNet-18 floor-map encoding and test-time guidance from TRACE [32]. The key conceptual departure from prior scene-aware motion work (NSM, DIMOS, HUMANISE) is avoiding reliance on small paired datasets or RL by instead pre-training on large scene-agnostic data and fine-tuning a lightweight scene-aware branch on augmented data, analogous to how ControlNet extends text-to-image models to new conditioning signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to unify text-controllable navigation and object interaction in a single pipeline, addressing a gap where prior work handled either text-to-motion without scenes or scene-aware motion without text. The pre-train-then-fine-tune paradigm for scene awareness is a generalizable recipe likely to be adopted for other scene-conditioned generation tasks. However, the 2D floor map representation limits handling of intricate vertical interactions (e.g., stepping over small stools), the two-stage navigation-to-inpainting pipeline can disconnect pelvis from full-body poses, and interactions are currently limited to sitting/standing. Open questions include whether one-stage joint pelvis-and-pose generation can close this gap, and how the approach extends to dynamic objects and LLM-planned multi-step action sequences.
