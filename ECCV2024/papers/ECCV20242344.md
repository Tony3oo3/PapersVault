## Problem

Existing 3D hand pose estimation methods struggle with dynamic self-occlusion and hand-object occlusion because they rely on static graph structures to model kinematic correspondence between keypoints. These fixed topologies cannot adaptively shift reliance between local geometric detail and kinematic information depending on whether a specific keypoint is occluded or well-observed, limiting robustness in real-world interaction scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242344/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 2D depth image (resized to 128×128) and a randomly sampled 3D point cloud of 1,024 points as multi-modal input. The depth image is encoded by a ConvNeXt autoencoder into a 2D feature map, while the point cloud is processed by point set convolution into 3D super-point features; the 2D features are then projected into 3D space and concatenated with the 3D features to form fused super-point representations. The output is a set of 3D keypoint coordinates (16 for single-hand, 21 for hand-object datasets) regressed directly in 3D space to avoid 2D-to-3D non-linearity.

## Method

HandDAGT's core is an Adaptive Graph Transformer that first augments keypoint embeddings through a GCN to capture kinematic topology, then applies a novel attention mechanism using Hadamard product with sigmoid activation (rather than dot product with softmax) to produce two separate attention scores—one for local point features and one for graph-evolved keypoint embeddings. This dual-score design lets the model adaptively weight local geometric detail versus kinematic correspondence per keypoint, favoring local information when keypoints are well-observed and kinematic topology when they are occluded. The transformer is stacked three times for progressive refinement, with each stage regressing updated keypoint positions that guide the next patch grouping. A denoising training strategy injects random Gaussian noise (with a linear variance schedule borrowed from diffusion models) into initial patch positions during training only, forcing the network to learn robust corrections without any inference-time overhead.

## Results

HandDAGT achieves state-of-the-art mean keypoint errors of 5.66 mm on ICVL and 7.12 mm on NYU for single-hand pose estimation, outperforming the previous best (IPNet) by 0.10 mm and 0.05 mm respectively. On the hand-object DexYCB dataset, it attains the lowest average error of 8.03 mm across all four split protocols (S0–S3), surpassing IPNet's 8.36 mm. On HO3D, HandDAGT matches IPNet at 1.81 cm mean keypoint error, both substantially ahead of the next-best method HandVoxNet++ (2.46 cm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HandDAGT outperforms all 2D depth-image-based methods (e.g., PHG at 5.97/7.39 mm, SSRN at 6.01/7.37 mm) by margins of 0.3–1.7 mm on ICVL/NYU, attributed to its direct 3D-space processing that avoids 2D-to-3D non-linearity. Among point-cloud-only methods, it surpasses the strongest competitor HandR2N2 (5.70/7.27 mm) by 0.04/0.15 mm, and HandFoldingNet (5.95/8.58 mm) by 0.29/1.46 mm. The closest multi-modal competitor IPNet [35] is beaten on both single-hand datasets (5.66 vs 5.76 mm on ICVL; 7.12 vs 7.17 mm on NYU) and on DexYCB (8.03 vs 8.36 mm average across S0–S3). On HO3D, HandDAGT ties IPNet at 1.81 cm, while both substantially outperform RGB-based methods such as HandOccNet (2.49 cm) and ArtiBoost (2.53 cm). On DexYCB, the advantage over RGB-based baselines is dramatic: HandDAGT's 8.03 mm average is roughly 2–3× better than the best RGB method (METRO at 15.24 mm on S0 only, or Spurr et al. at 18.44 mm average).

## Relation to Prior Work

HandDAGT extends the multi-modal depth-image-plus-point-cloud pipeline established by IPNet, reusing its 2D-to-3D feature projection and overall input formulation, while replacing IPNet's CNN backbone with a transformer-based decoder. It draws on HandFoldingNet's joint-wise embedding concept for keypoint initialization and departs from static graph structures (as in HandGCNFormer) by introducing an adaptive attention mechanism that dynamically re-weights kinematic topology against local geometric detail per keypoint. The denoising training strategy borrows the linear noise-scheduling idea from diffusion models but applies it exclusively at training time, a departure from the standard diffusion paradigm of noise at both training and inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The adaptive attention mechanism offers a principled way to handle the fundamental tension in hand pose estimation between relying on local observations (which fail under occlusion) and relying on kinematic priors (which can propagate errors), and the ablations confirm both branches are necessary. However, the practical gains over the strong IPNet baseline are modest (e.g., 5.66 vs 5.76 mm on ICVL, 8.03 vs 8.36 mm on DexYCB), and the authors acknowledge that the method cannot handle interacting hands and incurs redundant computation from the 2D autoencoder. Open questions include whether the adaptive weighting generalizes to multi-hand or video settings, and whether a lighter 2D encoder could close the efficiency gap without sacrificing accuracy.
