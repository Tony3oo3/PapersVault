## Problem

Reconstructing a 3D CT volume from only two orthogonal X-ray projections is an extremely ill-posed inverse problem: the 2D projections lose depth information, causing severe ambiguity and overlapping-structure artifacts. Prior regression-based methods produce blurry outputs, GAN-based methods introduce severe artifacts, and existing diffusion-based CT reconstruction approaches still depend on CT scanners for 3D conditions and rely on 2D architectures that cannot enforce global 3D coherence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241156/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two 2D biplanar orthogonal X-ray images (a front view and a side view) as input and produces a 3D CT volume of shape 128×128×128 at isotropic 2×2×2 mm³ resolution. CT values are clipped to [−1024, 1500] and min-max normalized to [−1, 1]; paired X-rays for training are synthesized from CT via digitally reconstructed radiographs (DRR). The diffusion process operates directly in the 3D volume space rather than slice-by-slice, enabling global coherence across the entire volume.

## Method

DiffuX2CT formulates CT reconstruction as a 3D conditional denoising diffusion process (DDPM-style) where a 3D U-Net denoiser with shifted-window attention generates the CT volume iteratively. The key novelty is an implicit conditioning mechanism (ICM) that extracts 3D structural information from the two 2D X-rays: a tri-plane decoupling generator uses interleaved convolution to capture cross-view spatial correlations, view modulators to synthesize the absent third plane, and a learnable embedding with Gaussian perturbation to supply missing statistical information; an implicit neural decoder then maps the tri-plane features at assigned 3D coordinates to multi-resolution 3D structural representations via bilinear sampling and a lightweight MLP. These 3D conditions are concatenated with the U-Net encoder/decoder features at each resolution level, and training combines a standard L1 noise-prediction loss with a geometry projection loss that enforces consistency between the predicted CT's orthogonal projections and the input X-rays.

## Results

DiffuX2CT achieves the best or near-best results across all four datasets on both distortion and perceptual metrics. On the newly introduced LumbarV dataset, it reaches PSNR 34.32, SSIM3D 0.8544, FID 13.23, and LPIPS 0.1198. The most striking gains are in perceptual quality: FID drops from 75.28 (X-CTRSNet) and 35.04 (X2CT-GAN) to 13.23 on LumbarV, and from 143.73/16.65 to 4.52 on LIDC-IDRI. SSIM3D improvements are particularly large (e.g., 0.8544 vs. 0.7502 on LumbarV; 0.7423 vs. 0.6228 on LIDC-IDRI), reflecting superior 3D global coherence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffuX2CT is compared against two baselines: the regression-based X-CTRSNet and the GAN-based X2CT-GAN. DiffuX2CT outperforms both on PSNR, SSIM, SSIM3D, FID, and LPIPS across all four datasets, with the largest margins in perceptual metrics (FID and LPIPS). Against X-CTRSNet, the FID gap is especially dramatic (e.g., 13.23 vs. 75.28 on LumbarV; 4.52 vs. 143.73 on LIDC-IDRI), reflecting the regression model's tendency to produce blurry, averaged outputs. Against X2CT-GAN, DiffuX2CT wins on SSIM3D by a wide margin (e.g., 0.8544 vs. 0.6518 on LumbarV; 0.7423 vs. 0.6199 on LIDC-IDRI), indicating that the implicit conditioning mechanism recovers 3D global structure that the GAN's pixel-wise conditioning cannot. The one exception is PSNR3D on LIDC-IDRI, where X-CTRSNet (22.35) slightly edges out DiffuX2CT (21.15), though DiffuX2CT still leads on the slice-averaged PSNR (26.35 vs. 23.95). In the 3D segmentation evaluation on CTPelvic1K, DiffuX2CT achieves a mean DSC of 74.10, far surpassing X2CT-GAN (45.95) and X-CTRSNet (20.51), approaching the oracle real-CT score of 90.19.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffuX2CT sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) CT reconstruction from biplanar X-rays, previously addressed by regression networks (X-CTRSNet, XTransCT) and GANs (X2CT-GAN) that struggle with artifacts and blurry textures; (2) diffusion probabilistic models, whose forward/reverse Markov chain formulation is inherited from DDPM and SR3; and (3) tri-plane implicit representations from EG3D, which the paper repurposes not for 3D object generation but as a conditioning mechanism to extract 3D structural information from 2D X-ray inputs. The key departure from prior diffusion-based CT work (sparse-view, limited-angle) is that those methods still require CT-scanner-derived 3D conditions and operate with 2D denoising architectures, whereas DiffuX2CT uses a 3D U-Net with shifted-window attention and derives all 3D conditions solely from two orthogonal X-rays via the implicit conditioning mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a clinically acute gap: in many surgical settings (e.g., lumbar vertebra procedures with C-arm machines), CT is unavailable or impractical due to radiation and workflow constraints, yet surgeons need 3D structural information to locate implants and assess bone geometry. The new LumbarV dataset (268 real clinical CTs with implants) provides a much-needed benchmark for this specific use case. The main open limitation acknowledged by the authors is that soft-tissue structures (e.g., pulmonary nodules) may not be faithfully reconstructed from only two X-ray views, suggesting that multi-view X-ray inputs or additional modalities would be needed to close that gap. Whether the tri-plane conditioning mechanism generalizes beyond the biplanar setting to arbitrary sparse-view configurations remains an open question.
