## Problem

Point-drag-based image editing methods (e.g., DragDiffusion, SDE-Drag) suffer from two coupled limitations: sparse point instructions are inherently ambiguous and prone to misinterpretation, and the model must compensate by interpolating dozens of intermediate points and iterating over multiple sub-steps, inflating editing time to minutes per image. This paper addresses both issues by replacing point inputs with region pairs that provide denser spatial constraints and eliminate the need for iterative dragging.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240445/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB image, one or more handle-target region pairs (drawn as polygons or freeform brush strokes of arbitrary shape and size), and a text prompt, and outputs a single edited image. Each region pair corresponds to a dense set of mapped point pairs (median ~267 in DragBench-SR, ~201 in DragBench-DR), and the entire edit completes in one inversion-denoising cycle in approximately 1.5 seconds on a V100 GPU, compared to 43–178 seconds for point-drag baselines.

## Method

RegionDrag inverts the image latent to timestep t′=500 via DDPM inversion, blends the handle region with Gaussian noise (Eq. 5), then denoises while repeatedly copying the handle region's latent to the target region across timesteps t′ down to t″=200. A Region-to-Point Mapping algorithm (Algorithm 1) establishes dense correspondence between arbitrary-shaped handle and target regions through horizontal scaling followed by column-by-column vertical scaling, replacing the need for affine/perspective transforms. Identity is preserved via mutual self-attention control (MASACTRL), which swaps keys and values in the UNet's self-attention blocks from the original image's cached features, eliminating the need for per-image LoRA training or explicit masking. The entire pipeline is gradient-free and completes in a single editing iteration, achieving >100× speedup over DragDiffusion while improving both MD and LPIPS metrics.

## Results

RegionDrag achieves a mean editing time of 1.5 seconds on 512×512 images, which is ~20× faster than the second-fastest baseline (DiffEditor at 43.1 s) and ~100× faster than DragDiffusion (177.7 s). On the MD metric (lower is better), RegionDrag scores 6.4 on DragBench-SR and 6.6 on DragBench-DR, outperforming SDE-Drag (7.5 / 8.1) and matching or slightly beating DragDiffusion (7.0 / 6.7). On LPIPS (lower is better, indicating better identity preservation), RegionDrag achieves 9.9 and 9.2, the best scores across all methods on both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RegionDrag outperforms all three baselines on both MD and LPIPS metrics across both datasets. Against SDE-Drag [18], RegionDrag improves MD from 7.5→6.4 (DragBench-SR) and 8.1→6.6 (DragBench-DR), and LPIPS from 12.4→9.9 and 14.9→9.2, while being ~84× faster (1.5 s vs 126.1 s). Against DragDiffusion [24], RegionDrag achieves a lower MD on DragBench-SR (6.4 vs 7.0) and a marginally lower MD on DragBench-DR (6.6 vs 6.7), with substantially better LPIPS (9.9 vs 18.0; 9.2 vs 11.5) and ~118× faster inference. Against DiffEditor [17], RegionDrag is dramatically better on MD (6.4 vs 23.6; 6.6 vs 22.1) and LPIPS (9.9 vs 17.6; 9.2 vs 10.9), while being ~29× faster. The ablation study further confirms that reducing the number of input points (simulating sparser region coverage) degrades MD, validating that the dense region-to-point mapping is critical to RegionDrag's advantage over point-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

RegionDrag extends the point-drag-based diffusion editing lineage (DragDiffusion, SDE-Drag, DragonDiffusion, DiffEditor) by replacing sparse point inputs with dense handle-target region pairs and eliminating the iterative sub-step optimization that those methods require. It repurposes the gradient-free latent copy-and-paste mechanism from SDE-Drag but applies it to entire regions via a novel Region-to-Point Mapping algorithm, and incorporates mutual self-attention control (MAS-CTRL) as a training-free identity-preservation mechanism in place of per-image LoRA fine-tuning. The DDPM stochastic inversion framework from SDE-Drag is retained as the editing substrate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× speedup (1.5 s vs. ~177 s for DragDiffusion on 512×512 images) makes interactive drag-based editing practically viable for real-time creative workflows, a regime previously inaccessible to diffusion-based methods. By removing the need for per-image LoRA training and iterative sub-step dragging, RegionDrag simplifies the editing pipeline substantially. Open questions include how the method scales to higher resolutions, whether the column-by-column region-to-point mapping handles highly non-rigid deformations (e.g., rotations, large shape changes) gracefully, and whether the single-step copy-paste strategy generalizes to multi-object or scene-level rearrangements beyond the relatively simple edits in DragBench.
