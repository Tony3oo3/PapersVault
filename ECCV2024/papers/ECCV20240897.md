## Problem

Existing multi-modal semantic segmentation frameworks rely heavily on RGB imagery, which degrades or fails under adverse conditions such as night, fast motion, or extreme lighting. When one or more modalities are absent or corrupted, these methods lose robustness because they lack a principled way to re-weight and fuse the remaining inputs. This paper tackles the gap by learning a modality-agnostic representation that enables robust segmentation from any arbitrary combination of modalities in any visual condition.

## Input / Output

The method accepts up to four visual modalities per scene—RGB (3 channels), depth, LiDAR, and event stack images (each normalized to 3 channels)—at 1024×1024 on DELIVER or 512×512 on MCubeS, and produces a per-pixel semantic segmentation mask over K categories. A key design choice is that all modalities are processed through a single shared-weight SegFormer encoder rather than separate modality-specific encoders, and at inference the MFF module can gracefully handle any subset of the four modalities (modality-incomplete setting) without architectural changes. The LSCD distillation module is active only during training; inference uses solely the MFF forward pass.

## Method

Any2Seg combines two novel modules. The Language-guided Semantic Correlation Distillation (LSCD) module uses LanguageBind as a frozen teacher: LLaMA-Adapter first generates scene descriptions from visual inputs and category names, then inter-modal correlation is computed as cosine similarity between each modality's embedding and the scene embedding in LanguageBind's space, and this correlation structure is transferred to the student via KL divergence on matching similarity matrices; intra-modal semantic knowledge is additionally distilled by aligning Mask-Average-Pooled class-wise features to the text encoder's category embeddings. The Modality-agnostic Feature Fusion (MFF) module iteratively reweights multi-modal features by their cosine similarity to the previous iteration's modality-agnostic feature, performs element-wise max-similarity index selection across modalities to extract fine-grained features, and aggregates the reweighted and selected features via a residual connection to produce the final modality-agnostic representation fed to the segmentation head. Training optimizes a combined objective of cross-entropy supervision plus the two distillation losses, and the shared-encoder design means the model naturally generalizes to missing modalities at test time.

## Results

On the DELIVER dataset under the multi-modal segmentation (MSS) setting, Any2Seg achieves 68.25 mIoU with all four modalities (R+D+E+L), surpassing the prior SoTA CMNeXt by +1.95 mIoU. The largest gain is in the dual-modality R+D configuration, where Any2Seg reaches 67.12 mIoU versus CMNeXt's 63.58 (+3.54 mIoU). Under the challenging modality-incomplete segmentation (MISS) setting, Any2Seg achieves a mean mIoU of 45.04 across all 15 modality combinations, outperforming CMNeXt (25.25) by a remarkable +19.79 mIoU. On MCubeS, Any2Seg also leads with 51.91 mIoU using all four modalities (I-A-D-N), exceeding CMNeXt by +0.37 mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Any2Seg outperforms all six baselines in the majority of configurations. Against the strongest prior SoTA, CMNeXt [60], Any2Seg wins in 5 out of 6 modality groups on DELIVER (R+D: +3.54, R+E: +0.35, R+D+E: +2.31, R+D+L: +1.70, R+D+E+L: +1.95 mIoU), with a marginal loss of −0.12 mIoU on R+L. The gap is most dramatic under the MISS setting, where Any2Seg's mean mIoU of 45.04 exceeds CMNeXt's 25.25 by +19.79 mIoU, driven by large gains in depth+LiDAR (60.15 vs. 3.83) and depth-only (60.11 vs. 0.81) scenarios. Against CMX [58], Any2Seg leads by +4.45 mIoU on R+D and +10.63 mIoU on R+D+E+L. TokenFusion [46] is outperformed by +6.87 mIoU on R+D and +15.24 mIoU on R+D+E+L. HRFuser [56] and SegFormer [52] serve as weaker baselines, with Any2Seg exceeding them by 10+ mIoU in multi-modal settings. On MCubeS, Any2Seg surpasses both MCubeSNet [24] (by +7.85 mIoU with all four modalities) and CMNeXt (by +0.37 mIoU).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends multi-modal semantic segmentation (CMNeXt, CMX, TokenFusion) by replacing hand-crafted cross-modal fusion components with knowledge distillation from multi-modal vision-language models (LanguageBind), treating the MVLM as a frozen teacher that provides modality-agnostic semantic structure. It also departs from prior modality-incomplete segmentation approaches (FPT, ShaSpec) that rely on cross-modal compensation or missing-aware strategies, instead learning a unified modality-agnostic representation that naturally generalizes to any modality subset. The LSCD module's use of LLaMA-Adapter-generated scene descriptions as an intermediate linguistic bridge between visual modalities is a novel connection between generative VLMs and dense prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation—robust segmentation when sensors fail or modalities are absent—is critical for real-world autonomous driving and robotics deployment, and the +19.79 mIoU gain in the modality-incomplete setting demonstrates that modality-agnostic representations can substantially outperform architectures that over-rely on RGB. However, the authors acknowledge that current MVLMs are pretrained at image-level rather than pixel-level, limiting dense prediction performance, and that results remain sub-optimal with sparse modality inputs (e.g., single Event or LiDAR). Future work on pixel-wise MVLMs and improved modality-agnostic fusion could close these gaps and make the approach broadly applicable beyond the four modalities tested here.
