## Problem

Existing multi-modal test-time adaptation (MM-TTA) methods for 3D segmentation evaluate modal reliability in a frame-wise manner, but domain shift causes single-frame predictions to be noisy and temporally inconsistent. This instability leads to error propagation across modalities and degrades segmentation performance, particularly under challenging domain gaps. The paper identifies that geometric neighborhoods within consecutive frames are highly correlated yet previously unexploited for reliability estimation in MM-TTA.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240739/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes temporally consecutive frames of 2D RGB images (H×W×3) and 3D point clouds (N×4 with xyz + intensity), along with estimated poses from an off-the-shelf SLAM system (KISS-ICP). Frames are aggregated in a sliding window of size 3 and voxelized at 0.2 m resolution to form spatial-temporal correspondences. The output is per-point 3D semantic segmentation (K classes) produced as a cross-modal weighted combination of 2D and 3D predictions, where the weighting is determined by per-voxel ST entropy rather than a fixed softmax average.

## Method

Latte introduces Spatial-Temporal (ST) voxels constructed by merging consecutive point cloud frames within a sliding time window via pose transformation and voxelizing the result. For each ST voxel, the method computes ST entropy—the Shannon entropy of the average teacher (moving-average) predictions over all reference points in that voxel—as a measure of temporally local prediction consistency and certainty. Voxels whose ST entropy exceeds a configurable α-quantile are filtered out as unreliable. The remaining ST entropy values drive adaptive cross-modal attending: at both the voxel level (weighting the KL-divergence consistency loss) and the point level (weighting the cross-modal pseudo-label generation), the modality with lower ST entropy receives higher attention, suppressing contributions from the noisier modality. The overall training combines a cross-entropy loss against cross-modal pseudo-labels with the ST-entropy-weighted cross-modal consistency loss, updating only normalization-layer parameters in a one-pass online protocol.

## Results

On the cross-modal (xM) mIoU metric, Latte achieves an average of 47.3 across three MM-TTA benchmarks (U-to-S, A-to-S, S-to-S), surpassing the previous SOTA MMTTA (44.9) by over 5.3% relatively. The most dramatic gain is on the challenging Synthia-to-SemanticKITTI benchmark, where Latte reaches 41.6 xM mIoU versus MMTTA's 35.5 (14.6% relative improvement), while all other baselines fall below the source-only baseline (38.2). On the less challenging U-to-S and A-to-S benchmarks, Latte improves over MMTTA by 1.3% and 1.1% relatively, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Latte outperforms all five compared baselines on the cross-modal mIoU metric across all three benchmarks. Against the previous SOTA MMTTA [29], Latte gains +0.6 on U-to-S (46.0 vs 45.4), +0.6 on A-to-S (54.3 vs 53.7), and a substantial +6.1 on S-to-S (41.6 vs 35.5). Compared to xMUDA+PL [16], the improvements are +3.0, +3.4, and +5.3 respectively. Against single-modal TTA methods (TENT [39], ETA [22], SAR [23]), Latte leads by 4.9–8.7 points on xM mIoU, confirming that cross-modal learning provides non-trivial benefits over unimodal adaptation. Notably, on the most challenging S-to-S benchmark, every baseline except Latte performs worse than the source-only model (38.2 xM), whereas Latte exceeds it by 3.4 points, demonstrating that spatial-temporal voxel-based reliability estimation is critical under severe domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

Latte extends the MM-TTA line initiated by MMTTA [29], which first established cross-modal test-time adaptation for 3D segmentation using frame-wise prediction consistency. It departs from this frame-wise paradigm by introducing spatial-temporal voxel aggregation via a sliding window, drawing on temporal point-cloud processing ideas (e.g., 4D segmentation, scene flow) that had not previously been coupled with multi-modal online adaptation. The teacher-student moving-average framework and entropy-based reliability estimation follow the broader TTA literature (TENT, ETA, SAR), while the cross-modal KL-divergence loss and pseudo-label generation inherit from MM-UDA methods like xMUDA [16].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Latte provides a principled, computationally efficient mechanism (ST entropy within sliding-window voxels) for estimating per-voxel prediction reliability in online multi-modal adaptation, addressing the core instability that causes prior MM-TTA methods to degrade under large domain shifts. The 14.6% relative gain on the Synthia-to-SemanticKITTI benchmark—where all prior methods underperform the source-only baseline—demonstrates that temporal consistency is a critical, previously underexploited signal for online adaptation. The authors acknowledge that Latte cannot correct predictions that are consistently wrong across time, leaving open the question of how to detect and recover from systematic cross-temporal errors without ground-truth supervision.
