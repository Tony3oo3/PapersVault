## Problem

Fully fine-tuning pre-trained Vision Transformers on scarce AU-annotated datasets leads to severe overfitting, and existing Parameter-Efficient Transfer Learning (PETL) methods lack design tailored to AU-specific characteristics such as multi-scale muscle region sizes and inter-AU correlations. The paper asks whether a parameter-efficient adaptation mechanism can be designed that explicitly encodes these AU properties, enabling state-of-the-art detection without any additional relevant data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241343/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB facial image (or the apex frame extracted from a micro-expression video for CASME II) as input, which is patchified into 16×16 tokens for the ViT-B/16 backbone. Output is a vector of N binary AU activation probabilities (N=12 for BP4D, N=8 for DISFA and CASME II), obtained by projecting the [CLS] token through a linear layer and applying Sigmoid. An auxiliary head also predicts each AU independently from the final MoKE group outputs to enforce per-AU specialization.

## Method

AUFormer freezes a pre-trained ViT-B/16 and inserts N Mixture-of-Knowledge Expert (MoKE) modules—one per AU—into both the MHSA and MLP layers of every Transformer block. Each MoKE reshapes the 1D token sequence back to 2D, then applies a Multi-Receptive Field operator (three parallel 3×3 dilated convolutions at rates 1, 3, 5) to capture multi-scale features and a Context-Aware operator (local neighborhood attention with 3×3 receptive window) to model inter-muscle correlations. Within each block, the N MoKEs collaborate by averaging their personalized knowledge vectors, which are then added to the frozen ViT's residual stream, allowing the backbone to adapt with only ~2.3% learnable parameters. Training uses a novel MDWA-Loss that combines margin truncation (discarding suspected mislabeled negatives), difficulty-aware asymmetric weighting (γ inversely proportional to AU occurrence rate), and class-imbalance weighting, alongside a weighted dice loss and an auxiliary MDWA-Loss on MoKE outputs.

## Results

On within-domain evaluation, AUFormer achieves 66.2% average F1-score on BP4D and 66.4% on DISFA, outperforming the previous best (ME-GraphAU at 65.5% on BP4D, FG-Net at 65.4% on DISFA) by at least 0.7% and 1.0% respectively, while using only 2.3% and 0.7% of the learnable parameters of those methods. In cross-domain evaluation, AUFormer outperforms all baselines by at least 7.1% in the DISFA→BP4D direction and is second only to FG-Net in the BP4D→DISFA direction. On the micro-expression dataset CASME II, AUFormer achieves 70.4% average F1-score, surpassing the best prior method (ASP at 66.4%) by 4.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AUFormer outperforms all 13 within-domain baselines on both BP4D and DISFA. The closest competitors are ME-GraphAU (65.5% BP4D, 62.4% DISFA) and FG-Net (64.3% BP4D, 65.4% DISFA); AUFormer beats ME-GraphAU by 0.7% on BP4D and 4.0% on DISFA, and beats FG-Net by 1.9% on BP4D and 1.0% on DISFA. Notably, AUFormer achieves these gains without any additional auxiliary data (landmarks, large-scale facial data, or biomechanical priors), whereas JÄA-Net, KDSRL, FG-Net, and BG-AU all rely on such extra information. In cross-domain transfer, AUFormer dominates the DISFA→BP4D direction (beating FG-Net by 21.4% in average F1) but trails FG-Net in the BP4D→DISFA direction, which the authors attribute to FG-Net's use of StyleGAN2 features pre-trained on diverse FFHQ data. On CASME II (micro-expression), AUFormer (70.4%) surpasses ASP (66.4%) and ME-GraphAU (62.4%), the two strongest prior methods, by 4.0% and 8.0% respectively. In the data-efficiency study, AUFormer degrades more gracefully than ME-GraphAU and KS as training data shrinks, retaining strong performance even at 0.1% sampling rate (<100 samples).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to apply the Parameter-Efficient Transfer Learning (PETL) paradigm to facial AU detection, building on Convpass [18] as the base adaptation module but replacing it with a purpose-built Mixture-of-Knowledge Expert (MoKE) that integrates multi-receptive-field and context-aware operators tailored to AU characteristics. The proposed MDWA-Loss extends the weighted asymmetric loss (WA-Loss) [44] by adding margin truncation to discard potentially mislabeled samples and a difficulty-aware exponent γ to differentiate unactivated AUs by occurrence rate. Conceptually, the work bridges two previously separate threads: the PETL literature (Adapters, LoRA, VPT, Convpass) and the AU detection literature's emphasis on multi-scale features and inter-AU correlations, arguing that generic PETL modules are suboptimal for AU detection because they ignore these domain-specific requirements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that AU detection can be performed with only 2.3% of the learnable parameters of prior SOTA methods while simultaneously outperforming them, and that this efficiency extends to extreme data scarcity (0.1% of training samples). The evaluation on CASME II (micro-expression domain) is a novel benchmarking effort not attempted by prior AU detection methods, opening a bridge between macro- and micro-expression research. Open questions include whether the static averaging-based collaboration among MoKEs can be replaced by adaptive weighting, and whether the personalized per-AU features extracted by individual MoKEs can be leveraged for downstream tasks beyond binary AU classification. A limitation is that the within-domain gains over the strongest baselines (ME-GraphAU, FG-Net) are modest (0.7–1.0%), and the cross-domain BP4D→DISFA direction still trails FG-Net, which benefits from StyleGAN2-pretrained features.
