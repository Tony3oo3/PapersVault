## Problem

Existing vision-based joint perception and prediction (PnP) methods compress visual information into query representations during the perception stage, discarding the explicit visual interrelations between road agents (e.g., spatial proximity, occlusion, relative positioning) that are critical for accurate motion forecasting. This paper asks whether explicitly modeling these visual relations in the prediction stage can significantly improve trajectory prediction and provide explainable reasoning for agent behavior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241498/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ViRR consumes 2D image feature maps (from a ResNet-50 + FPN backbone) and 3D bounding boxes of tracked agents, projecting the boxes to 2D for RoIAlign-based pairwise relation extraction. Agents are paired using a 3D center-to-center distance threshold (10–20 m), and in multi-view settings, pairs visible in different cameras are still connected through the global 3D-distance-based adjacency. The module outputs per-agent visual relation features (d-dimensional embeddings) that are summed with the perception-stage queries before feeding the motion forecasting head, which produces multi-modal future trajectories.

## Method

ViRR constructs a visual scene graph where each node's feature is the average of RoIAlign-extracted pairwise relation features with all neighboring agents within the distance threshold, rather than assigning predefined edge types as in conventional scene graph generation. The adjacency matrix is weighted by the reciprocal of 3D inter-agent distances, enabling distance-aware message passing. Higher-order relation learning is achieved by computing P consecutive powers of the normalized adjacency matrix (P=4 optimal), where the p-th power captures paths of length p, modeling how interactions ripple through the scene. A combination layer concatenates the P order-specific GCN outputs and passes them through an MLP with learnable scalar weights (summing to 1) to distill meaningful multi-scale relational features. The entire module is fully differentiable and can be inserted between the perception and prediction stages of any existing vision-based PnP pipeline without architectural modification.

## Results

On the nuScenes benchmark, ViRR consistently improves motion forecasting across all tested PnP backbones. The most striking gains are in Miss Rate: UniAD (T+M+Mo) drops from 0.158 to 0.040 (74.55% reduction) and UniAD (T+Mo) from 0.161 to 0.051 (68.32% reduction). For ViP3D, minADE improves from 2.051 to 1.690 (17.60%) and minFDE from 2.862 to 2.075 (27.50%). In end-to-end joint optimization (Table 3), ViRR also improves tracking (AMOTA 0.360→0.369) alongside motion forecasting (MR 0.162→0.080, a 50.62% reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViRR is evaluated as a plug-in module on four PnP backbones: ViP3D, two UniAD variants, and a custom MOTR+CVAE pipeline. Against ViP3D, ViRR yields the largest absolute improvements (minADE −0.361, minFDE −0.787), confirming that a method relying solely on 3D agent queries benefits most from explicit visual relation semantics. Against UniAD (T+M+Mo), the MR reduction from 0.158 to 0.040 is the headline result, indicating that visual relation reasoning dramatically reduces catastrophic trajectory failures. In the ablation comparing relation learning strategies (Table 5), ViRR with P=4 higher-order degrees (minADE 0.628, minFDE 0.891, MR 0.040) outperforms standard GCN (0.683/0.987/0.073), MixHop (0.664/1.002/0.055), and Cross-Attention (0.655/0.929/0.049), with the advantage most pronounced on minFDE and MR. The MOTR+CVAE baseline shows the smallest gains (minADE +2.47%), suggesting that simpler prediction heads have less capacity to exploit the richer visual relation features. Overall, ViRR wins on every metric across every backbone, with the largest relative improvements on Miss Rate (up to 74.55%) and the smallest on minADE for the weakest baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViRR builds on the standard GCN formulation (Kipf & Welling) and the higher-order adjacency-matrix-power technique from the authors' prior pedestrian trajectory prediction work (Kim et al., CVPR 2024), repurposing both for a vision-based autonomous driving setting. It departs from conventional scene graph generation, which discretizes pairwise relations into pre-defined predicate labels, by instead aggregating continuous RoIAlign-extracted relation features into per-agent node representations. The paper positions itself against existing vision-based joint PnP pipelines (ViP3D, UniAD) that restrict visual feature usage to the perception stage, arguing that the prediction module is starved of explicit visual inter-agent semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, fully differentiable design makes ViRR immediately applicable to any vision-based PnP pipeline, and the consistent gains across four distinct baselines (ViP3D, two UniAD variants, MOTR+CVAE) strengthen the case that explicit visual relation reasoning is a missing ingredient in current motion forecasting. The t-SNE analysis and qualitative trajectory comparisons provide interpretable evidence that the learned features capture relational semantics absent from standard agent queries. However, evaluation is limited to a single dataset (nuScenes), and the paper acknowledges but does not fully explore the potential of visual relation reasoning to benefit other pipeline stages (e.g., tracking, planning), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to more complex or long-tail driving scenarios.
