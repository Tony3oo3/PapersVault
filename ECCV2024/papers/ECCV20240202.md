## Problem

In OCR-free document understanding models, the vision encoder processes the entire document image independently of the user query, producing visual features that may contain large amounts of information irrelevant to the specific question. This misalignment is particularly harmful for dense documents where many text patches are unrelated to the query, causing the model to dilute its attention across the full page rather than focusing on the relevant region.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240202/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a high-resolution document image (1536×768 pixels) and a free-form text prompt (question or instruction) as input, and produces a free-form text answer. Unusually, the prompt is consumed by both the vision encoder (via cross-attention in ViLMA layers at each patch-merging stage) and the language model, rather than being fed solely to the LM as in prior OCR-free methods. The vision encoder is SwinV2-Small and the language model is T5-Small or T5-Base, connected by a small MLP projection module.

## Method

VisFocus introduces Vision-Language Merging Attention (ViLMA) layers that replace Swin's standard patch-merging layers: at each downsampling stage, a multi-head cross-attention layer uses the visual feature map as queries and a frozen language encoder's prompt embedding as keys/values, so that the 2×2 patch aggregation is conditioned on the prompt. A novel pre-training task, Localized Masked Prompt Modeling (LMPM), feeds a randomly sampled snippet of the document's OCR text as the prompt and asks the model to predict masked tokens within that snippet, explicitly training the encoder to attend to text patches semantically related to the prompt. To prevent the language model from compensating for weak visual features, the prompt is randomly dropped from the LM input with probability 1−ρ during LMPM training (analogous to dropout), forcing the vision encoder to develop independent focusing capability. Training proceeds in three stages: Learn-to-Read (standard OCR prediction), LMPM (prompt-guided focusing), and downstream fine-tuning, with ViLMA layers randomly initialized only at the LMPM stage.

## Results

VisFocus-B (295M trainable / 408M total params) achieves 72.9 ANLS on DocVQA, 31.9 ANLS on InfoVQA, 57.1 RA on ChartQA, 70.0 EM on OCR-VQA, and 47.8 EM on AI2D, outperforming its baseline (Baseline-B) by +1.2, +5.1, +4.6, +3.1, and +2.2 points respectively. VisFocus-S (132M/171M) similarly improves over Baseline-S by +1.6 on DocVQA, +3.8 on InfoVQA, and +3.7 on ChartQA. In the base category, VisFocus-B surpasses Pix2Struct-B on DocVQA, ChartQA, OCR-VQA, and AI2D, though it trails on InfoVQA (31.9 vs. 38.2). The additional ViLMA parameters are an order of magnitude smaller than the total model size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the most directly comparable baseline (Baseline-B, same architecture without ViLMA/LMPM), VisFocus-B wins on all five benchmarks with the largest gains on InfoVQA (+5.1) and ChartQA (+4.6). Compared to Pix2Struct-B (282M), VisFocus-B (295M) outperforms on DocVQA (72.9 vs. 72.1), ChartQA (57.1 vs. 56.0), OCR-VQA (70.0 vs. 69.4), and AI2D (47.8 vs. 40.9), but trails on InfoVQA (31.9 vs. 38.2), which the authors attribute to Pix2Struct's more diverse pre-training data. Against ScreenAI-B (670M, more than 2× the parameters), VisFocus-B dominates on every reported benchmark, e.g., DocVQA 72.9 vs. 50.7 and OCR-VQA 70.0 vs. 54.8. In the small category, VisFocus-S beats Donut (176M) on DocVQA (68.6 vs. 67.5), InfoVQA (28.5 vs. 11.6), ChartQA (53.0 vs. 41.8), and OCR-VQA (67.3 vs. 66.0), and beats Dessurt (127M) on DocVQA (68.6 vs. 63.2). The only marginal loss is VisFocus-S vs. Baseline-S on AI2D (42.6 vs. 42.7, −0.1), which is within noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

VisFocus extends the OCR-free VDU architecture established by Donut and Dessert (vision encoder + LM cascade with Learn-to-Read pre-training) by injecting the user prompt directly into the SwinV2 vision encoder's patch-merging layers via new ViLMA cross-attention modules. This departs from prior OCR-free methods where the prompt is fed only to the language model, and from Pix2Struct's approach of rendering the prompt onto the input image, which the paper shows is less effective under LtR-only pre-training. The complementary LMPM pre-training task adapts T5's denoising objective to a localized span, creating a two-stage curriculum (general reading → prompt-focused reading) that has no direct precedent in the OCR-free VDU literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The density-dependent performance gap (increasing from +0.7 to +2.3 ANLS as document word count rises) provides concrete evidence that prompt-guided visual encoding addresses a real bottleneck in dense document understanding, where irrelevant text patches dilute attention. However, the method remains text-centric: it underperforms Pix2Struct on InfoVQA, suggesting that prompt-awareness must extend beyond text to infographics and figures for broader applicability. The lightweight nature of ViLMA (an order of magnitude fewer parameters than the base model) makes the approach practical, but the modest absolute gains (+1.2 to +5.1 points) mean it is an incremental refinement rather than a paradigm shift in OCR-free VDU.
