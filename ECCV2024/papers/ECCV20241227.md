## Problem

Existing self-supervised surrounding depth estimation methods either exploit multi-camera spatial cues from a single frame or multi-frame temporal cues from a single camera, but none jointly leverage both spatial and temporal information to produce consistent scale-aware depth across all surrounding views. This leaves a gap in achieving coherent metric depth that is simultaneously accurate per-camera and consistent across the full 360° surround.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241227/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two temporally adjacent frames (t and t−1) from C surrounding cameras with known intrinsics and extrinsics, downsampled to 384×640 (DDAD) or 352×640 (nuScenes). It produces per-camera metric depth maps at the current timestamp and a 6-DoF relative vehicle pose between the two frames; notably, the relative pose is not provided as input but is predicted by a dedicated branch, making the pipeline fully self-supervised. Depth is evaluated up to 200 m on DDAD and 80 m on nuScenes.

## Method

M² Depth constructs two sets of 3D cost volumes via plane-sweeping: spatial volumes by warping features from left/right adjacent cameras into the reference view, and temporal volumes by warping features from the previous frame, with depth samples guided by a mono-prior branch. A Spatial-Temporal Fusion (STF) module then combines these volumes using group-wise correlation weights to produce a unified spatial-temporal volume that is decoded into a depth probability distribution. The mono-prior branch employs a Multi-Grained Feature Fusion (MFF) module that fuses trainable ResNet-34 features with frozen MobileSAM features via attention-weighted Hadamard product, injecting fine-grained semantic priors to sharpen depth edges and reduce foreground-background ambiguity. Training is fully self-supervised, combining spatial and temporal photometric losses, edge-aware smoothness, a focal edge loss, and an SfM-based scale initialization loss, with the relative pose predicted end-to-end by a ResNet-34 pose encoder on the front camera pair.

## Results

On DDAD (depth up to 200 m), M² Depth achieves Abs.Rel. 0.183, Sq.Rel. 2.920, RMSE 11.963, and δ<1.25 0.756, outperforming the strongest single-frame baseline SurroundDepth (Abs.Rel. 0.208, Sq.Rel. 3.371) by 12.0% and 13.4% respectively. On nuScenes (depth up to 80 m), M² Depth records Abs.Rel. 0.259, RMSE 6.898, and δ<1.25 0.734, surpassing SurroundDepth (Abs.Rel. 0.280, RMSE 7.467) and VFDepth (Abs.Rel. 0.289, RMSE 7.551) while using only 2 frames versus the 5 frames required by R3D3. In terms of efficiency, M² Depth consumes 5,546 MB memory and 866 GFLOPs, roughly 3× less memory and 3× fewer FLOPs than R3D3 (8,371 MB, 2,622 GFLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DDAD, M² Depth (3\2 frames) decisively outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric: it beats SurroundDepth (the strongest single-frame method) by 12.0% on Abs.Rel. (0.183 vs 0.208) and 13.4% on Sq.Rel. (2.920 vs 3.371), and surpasses VFDepth and FSM* by even larger margins. R3D3*, when re-run with only 2 frames to match M² Depth's setting, degrades substantially (Abs.Rel. 0.311 vs 0.183), confirming that R3D3's original 6-frame advantage is critical. On nuScenes, M² Depth wins on Abs.Rel. (0.259 vs 0.280 for SurroundDepth), RMSE (6.898 vs 7.467), and δ<1.25 (0.734 vs 0.661), but narrowly trails SurroundDepth on Sq.Rel. (4.599 vs 4.401) and VFDepth on δ<1.25² (0.871 vs 0.876) and δ<1.25³ (0.928 vs 0.932). Overall, M² Depth achieves the best or near-best results across both benchmarks while using only 2 frames and consuming roughly one-third the FLOPs of R3D3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-supervised surrounding depth estimation line (FSM, SurroundDepth, VFDepth) by introducing a temporal dimension: prior methods use multi-camera images from a single time-step, while M² Depth constructs cost volumes in both spatial (adjacent cameras) and temporal (two consecutive frames) domains and fuses them via a novel STF module. It also departs from prior multi-frame depth work (MonoRec, ManyDepth) that operates on a single camera, by jointly exploiting calibrated multi-camera geometry for scale-aware metric depth. The use of frozen SAM features as a neural prior for depth estimation is a new application, with the MFF module adapting the group-wise correlation mechanism from stereo matching to volume fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

M² Depth demonstrates that two temporally adjacent frames from multiple cameras suffice to match or exceed methods requiring five-to-six frames (R3D3), offering a practical efficiency win for real-time autonomous driving where LiDAR replacement is the goal. The main limitation acknowledged is that memory scales linearly with the number of cameras since a separate cost volume is constructed per view; a unified volumetric representation for the full 360° surround remains an open problem. The modest but consistent gains from SAM integration (≈3% Abs.Rel. improvement) suggest that strong segmentation priors may transfer to geometric tasks more broadly, though the effect is not yet large enough to be transformative.
