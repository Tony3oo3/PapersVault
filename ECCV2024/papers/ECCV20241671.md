## Problem

Stochastic human motion prediction methods typically learn motion distributions through latent-space encoding-decoding, which discards spatial-temporal structure and requires complex multi-stage training pipelines. This design gap—despite the proven success of GCN-DCT architectures in deterministic HMP—leads to predictions that are inconsistent with the observed history and often physically unrealistic. The paper investigates why the GCN-DCT design has not been adopted in stochastic HMP and proposes a framework that bridges this gap.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D pose sequence of H observed frames (25 frames at 50 Hz for Human3.6M, 30 frames for AMASS) with 16 joints as input and produces k=2 diverse future pose sequences of F frames (100 or 120 frames respectively). The input history is fed directly into the generator at every denoising step rather than being encoded into a latent representation, and the output is structured as multiple complete pose trajectories that are jointly optimized via a relaxation technique to balance diversity and accuracy.

## Method

CoMusion is a single-stage, end-to-end diffusion model that predicts future motion directly (y₀) rather than noise, enabling the use of a GCN-DCT refinement architecture in DCT space. The generator consists of two stages: a Transformer-based reconstruction module F(y_t, t) that produces a smooth initial estimate ỹ₀ from the noisy target, and a GCN-based refinement module R(x, ỹ₀) that alternates between pose and DCT frequency spaces to capture spatial-temporal joint dependencies while conditioning on the full motion history. A modified cosine variance scheduler sets α̅₀ = 0.5 (instead of ≈1) to keep the prediction task non-trivial throughout denoising, preventing over-reliance on the clean history signal. Training uses a structure-aware ℓ₁ loss with kinematically weighted joints and a relaxation technique (k=2) that optimizes toward the best of multiple generated trajectories, all within a single 10-step DDPM training loop.

## Results

On Human3.6M, CoMusion achieves the best ADE (0.350), FDE (0.458), CMD (3.202), FID (0.102), and APDE (1.609) among all compared methods, representing a 35% improvement in CMD and 51% in FID over the previous best (BeLFusion). On AMASS, CoMusion attains state-of-the-art ADE (0.494), FDE (0.547), MMADE (0.469), MMFDE (0.466), and CMD (9.636), with a 43% CMD improvement over BeLFusion. Notably, CoMusion is a single-stage method using only 10 diffusion steps, whereas most competitive baselines (BeLFusion, DLow, GSPS, DivSamp) require multi-stage training pipelines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoMusion outperforms the strongest diffusion-based baseline BeLFusion [4] on ADE (0.350 vs 0.372), FDE (0.458 vs 0.474), CMD (3.202 vs 5.988, a 35% reduction), FID (0.102 vs 0.209, a 51% reduction), and APDE (1.609 vs 1.662), while BeLFusion retains a slight edge on MMADE (0.473 vs 0.494). Against the other single-stage DM method HumanMAC [11], CoMusion wins on ADE (0.350 vs 0.369), FDE (0.458 vs 0.480), and all fidelity metrics, though HumanMAC reports a lower APD (6.301 vs 7.632). Among VAE-based methods, CoMusion dominates on accuracy (ADE 0.350 vs best 0.370 for DivSamp) and fidelity (FID 0.102 vs best 1.255 for DLow), while multi-stage methods like GSPS and DivSamp achieve higher raw APD (14.757, 15.310) but at the cost of much worse APDE (6.749, 7.479) and CMD (10.758, 11.692), indicating their diversity is poorly calibrated to ground truth. On AMASS, CoMusion achieves the best ADE, FDE, MMADE, MMFDE, and CMD (9.636 vs BeLFusion's 16.995, a 43% improvement), though BeLFusion edges it on APDE (1.977 vs 2.328). Overall, CoMusion is the only method that simultaneously achieves top accuracy, top fidelity, and well-calibrated diversity in a single-stage training pipeline with only 10 diffusion steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoMusion bridges a notable design gap between deterministic and stochastic human motion prediction: while deterministic methods have long leveraged GCN-DCT architectures to model spatial-temporal joint relationships, prior stochastic (diffusion-based) methods have relied on latent-space encoding-decoding that discards this structure. The paper's key insight is diagnosing *why* this gap exists—stochastic models face a much larger input-to-groundtruth discrepancy in DCT space—and resolving it with a Transformer-based pre-processing step that produces a smooth initial reconstruction before GCN-DCT refinement. The framework also departs from standard DDPM by predicting motion directly (following MDM) rather than noise, and modifies the cosine variance scheduler with a simple offset change to maintain prediction difficulty throughout denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving single-stage, end-to-end training with only 10 diffusion steps while outperforming multi-stage baselines by 35% in CMD and 51% in FID, CoMusion offers a practical, efficient alternative for applications like autonomous driving and robotics where both speed and physical plausibility matter. The variance scheduler modification (setting α̅₀ = 0.5 instead of ≈1) is a simple, transferable insight for conditional diffusion in settings with strong history guidance. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer prediction horizons, multi-person scenes, or higher-dimensional motion representations (e.g., full-body SMPL parameters), and whether the 10-step efficiency advantage persists as sequence length or joint count increases.
