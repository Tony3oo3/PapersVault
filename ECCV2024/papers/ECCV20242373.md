## Problem

Dimension-contrastive SSL methods avoid negative sampling and architectural restrictions but converge slowly due to stepwise eigenvalue dynamics in the embedding covariance. Two known remedies—covariance eigenvalue regularization and using more views—are difficult to combine because eigenvalue-based regularization requires costly eigendecomposition. The paper asks whether an SSL objective can exploit both efficiency mechanisms simultaneously while remaining computationally cheap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242373/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a batch of N RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR/STL-10, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-100) and generates V augmented views per image (V ∈ {2, 4, 8}) using the same augmentation pipeline for all views. A shared-weight ResNet-18 encoder and an MLP projector (output dimension D=1024) map each view to an embedding; the SSL loss is computed on the projected embeddings, and the encoder is frozen for downstream linear-probe evaluation. The number of views is a first-order hyperparameter that trades VRAM and per-minibatch time against fewer required epochs.

## Method

FroSSL minimizes the log of the squared Frobenius norm of each view's normalized covariance matrix (variance term) plus a mean-squared-error between each view's projection and the mean projection across all views (invariance term). The key insight is that the Frobenius norm of the covariance equals the sum of squared eigenvalues—i.e., the 2-order (collision) Rényi entropy—so the objective directly shapes eigenvalue dynamics without any eigendecomposition, reducing complexity from O(min(D,N)³) to O(min(D,N)²). The logarithm makes the variance gradient self-regulating relative to the invariance term, preventing dead neurons. The invariance term is simplified via an algebraic identity so the loss scales linearly (not quadratically) in the number of views. The paper further shows that, up to embedding normalization, FroSSL is simultaneously dimension-contrastive and sample-contrastive, inheriting the empirical robustness advantages of the latter family.

## Results

On the standard linear probe evaluation, FroSSL with 8 views achieves the best average top-1 accuracy of 75.9% across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10, Tiny ImageNet, and ImageNet-100, outperforming Barlow Twins (74.7%), BYOL (74.4%), and MMCR 8 views (74.2%). On STL-10, FroSSL (8 views) reaches 90.9% top-1 accuracy, surpassing BYOL (88.7%) and Barlow Twins (85.0%). In terms of training efficiency, FroSSL with 8 views reaches 80% top-1 accuracy on STL-10 in just 55 epochs (1.2 hours wall-time), compared to 347 epochs for SimCLR, 360 for VICReg, and 370 for Barlow Twins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FroSSL (8 views) achieves the highest average accuracy (75.9%) across all five datasets, beating the strongest baselines: Barlow Twins (74.7%), BYOL (74.4%), and MMCR 8 views (74.2%). On STL-10, FroSSL (8 views) leads with 90.9%, outperforming BYOL (88.7%) by 2.2 points and Barlow Twins (85.0%) by 5.9 points. On CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, FroSSL (2 views) is competitive but slightly trails MoCo v2 (92.9 vs. 92.8) and Barlow Twins (70.9 vs. 70.6) respectively. On Tiny ImageNet, FroSSL (4/8 views) ties Barlow Twins at 45.3%, while on ImageNet-100, BYOL and MMCR 8 views (both 80.3%) narrowly edge out FroSSL 8 views (79.8%). In the low-data regime (10% ImageNet-1K, 100 epochs), FroSSL (8 views) achieves 38.2% top-1 accuracy, substantially outperforming SimCLR (31.1%), Barlow Twins (23.6%), and BYOL (12.7%). FroSSL also demonstrates greater robustness to augmentation strength on Tiny ImageNet, with only a 4.8% drop from weak to strong augmentations compared to 19.6% for VICReg and 17.0% for MMCR.

## Relation to Prior Work

FroSSL directly modifies the I-VNE objective (Kim et al., CVPR 2023) by replacing the von Neumann entropy variance term with a 2-order Petz-Rényi relative entropy computed via the Frobenius norm, thereby eliminating the need for eigendecomposition. The paper positions itself within the dimension-contrastive family (VICReg, Barlow Twins, CorInfoMax, W-MSE, I-VNE, MMCR) and introduces a unifying D_inv/D_var framework that recovers all these methods as special cases. A key conceptual departure is the demonstration that FroSSL is simultaneously sample-contrastive and dimension-contrastive up to embedding normalization, bridging two previously distinct families. The efficiency motivation draws on Simon et al.'s observation of stepwise eigenvalue dynamics and on multi-view contrastive coding (Tian et al., ECCV 2020), combining both levers into a single objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~6× reduction in epochs to reach 80% accuracy on STL-10 (55 vs. 347 for SimCLR) addresses a major practical pain point in SSL, where 1000+ epoch training runs are standard. The eigenvalue trajectory analysis provides a mechanistic explanation for why FroSSL converges faster, which could guide future objective design. However, all experiments use ResNet-18 on datasets up to ImageNet-100, leaving open whether the speedup persists at scale (deeper backbones, full ImageNet-1k) and whether the multi-view VRAM overhead (5.3 GB at 8 views) is acceptable in practice. The paper also does not explore interaction with multi-crop or patch-based view strategies, which are the dominant paradigms for large-scale SSL.
