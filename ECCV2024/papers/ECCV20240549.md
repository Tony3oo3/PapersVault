## Problem

Subsampling layers (strided convolutions, pooling) in deep networks discard a large fraction of spatial activations—e.g., 75% for a factor-of-2 subsampling—under the assumption that the retained activations suffice. This paper hypothesizes that the discarded activations still carry useful information and asks whether they can be selectively recovered and aggregated at test-time to improve predictions without retraining the backbone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240549/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 256px and center-cropped to 224×224 for classification; standard resolution for segmentation) and produces a class label or per-pixel segmentation mask. Unlike standard inference, it performs B_ours forward passes of the same pre-trained backbone, each with a different tuple of selection indices in the subsampling layers, then aggregates the resulting feature maps. The test-time budget B_ours (typically 4–30) controls the number of forward passes and thus the compute-accuracy trade-off.

## Method

The approach formulates the set of all possible activation maps (one per combination of selection indices across L subsampling layers) as a search space and uses a greedy top-down priority-queue search to identify the B_ours most promising states within a compute budget. A learned multi-head attention module aggregates the selected feature maps by computing pairwise attention weights and learning an offset from the mean, while a learning-free alternative weights features by prediction entropy. A spatial alignment step (Align_s) resizes each activation to input resolution and shifts it by a computed offset Δ to correct for relative displacement before aggregation. The search criterion is either the learned attention score (higher attention → more useful) or the negative entropy of the classifier output, both of which prioritize high-confidence activations. The aggregation module is trained on a small subset of the training set with the backbone frozen, and because attention is a set operator, the same trained module generalizes to any test-time budget without retraining.

## Results

On ImageNet (B_total=150), the proposed method consistently improves over all TTA baselines across four architectures: e.g., ResNet50 with AugTTA goes from 76.47% to 76.89% top-1 accuracy, and ClassTTA goes from 73.56% to 76.65%. On Flowers102, similar gains are observed (e.g., ResNet50 with ClassTTA: 90.06% → 91.02%). For semantic segmentation, the method improves mIoU on Cityscapes (MiT+SegFormer: 76.54 → 77.05) and ADE20K (MiT+SegFormer: 37.41 → 37.67) with B_ours=10. Overall, the method achieves an average 0.87% top-1 accuracy improvement over TTA baselines on ImageNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two families of learned TTA baselines: GPS [38] and the ClassTTA/AugTTA variants from Shanmugam et al. [52]. The proposed method outperforms all baselines in every configuration tested. The largest gains are over ClassTTA, which degrades significantly with increased budget (e.g., ResNet50 drops from 75.40% at B=30 to 73.56% at B=150 without the proposed method), whereas the proposed method recovers and surpasses the B=30 baseline (76.65% at B=150), yielding an average 2.01% improvement. Over GPS, which is budget-agnostic by design, the method adds a consistent 0.32% average gain. Over AugTTA, the improvement is smaller (0.19% average) since AugTTA already scales reasonably with budget. The method also complements standard TTA (horizontal flip) in segmentation, adding 0.30 mIoU on top of flip-based TTA for MiT+SegFormer on Cityscapes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes a long-standing architectural component—subsampling layers present since LeNet—as a source of test-time diversity, rather than introducing new data-space transformations as in TTA methods (GPS, ClassTTA, AugTTA). It is conceptually adjacent to anti-aliased CNNs, which attempted to preserve information lost during downsampling at training time, but here the discarded activations are recovered and selectively aggregated at inference. The aggregation module draws on set-transformer-style attention (permutation-invariant over variable-size feature sets), and the search procedure adapts the greedy policy search idea from GPS to a feature-space state space defined by selection indices across subsampling layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple architectural byproduct—activations thrown away by strided convolutions and pooling—carries recoverable signal, offering a complementary axis of test-time diversity orthogonal to input-space augmentation. However, the practical impact is tempered by the need for multiple forward passes (budget B_ours up to 30), making it unsuitable for real-time or resource-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the gains persist under domain shift or distributional mismatch, whether the search can be made differentiable or amortized to reduce inference cost, and whether the approach transfers to tasks without explicit subsampling (e.g., pure attention-based ViTs without patch-merging).
