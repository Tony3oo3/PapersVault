## Problem

Existing image-text alignment models provide only binary classification scores for text-image pairs, offering no insight into what specifically is misaligned. This lack of explanatory feedback limits the ability to diagnose VLM limitations, guide training of better generative models, or correct erroneous annotations in datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241525/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image paired with a free-form text caption and produces a single structured output: a binary alignment label (yes/no), and for misaligned pairs, a textual feedback sentence, the specific misaligned text segment, and one or more bounding boxes (normalized to 0–1000 coordinates) marking the visually misaligned region. All three feedback components are generated in a single inference pass by the same fine-tuned VLM, rather than requiring separate grounding or NLI modules.

## Method

The core contribution is ConGen-Feedback, an automated data-generation pipeline that constructs a 3M-instance training set (TV-Feedback) by: (1) collecting aligned image-text pairs from COCO, Flickr30K, PickaPic, ImageReward, ADE20K, and OpenImages; (2) using spaCy POS tagging to uniformly sample misalignment candidates across four categories (object, attribute, action, spatial relation); (3) prompting PaLM 2 with few-shot instructions to generate a contradiction caption, a textual explanation, a misalignment cue, and a bounding-box label; (4) filtering via NLI entailment validation; and (5) placing bounding boxes with GroundingDINO. A PaLI-series VLM is then fine-tuned in a multitask setup where a single model simultaneously predicts the alignment label and generates all three feedback components. The authors also release SeeTRUE-Feedback, a 2,008-instance human-annotated benchmark where three AMT annotators per instance verify both textual and visual feedback, retaining only unanimously agreed cases (66% of originals).

## Results

On the SeeTRUE-Feedback benchmark (out-of-distribution human-annotated test set), the fine-tuned PaLI model (PaLi ft. Multitask) achieves the highest Feedback NLI of 0.75, Textual Misalignment NLI of 0.78, and Visual Misalignment F1@0.75 of 0.65, while PaLi-X ft. Multitask attains the best Binary Alignment Accuracy of 0.79. The paper reports a ~10% increase in alignment accuracy, ~20% increase in entailment score for textual feedback, and a 213% increase in F1 for visual feedback over the strongest baselines. On the in-distribution validation set, fine-tuned PaLI models reach Feedback NLI of 0.87–0.88 and Textual Misalignment NLI of 0.90–0.92, substantially above all zero-shot baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All five zero-shot baselines are outperformed by the fine-tuned PaLI models on every metric. On Feedback NLI, the best baseline is mPLUG-Owl (0.63), while PaLi ft. Multitask reaches 0.75 (+19%); InstructBLIP (0.41), MiniGPT-v2 (0.46), LLaVa-1.5 (0.57), and PaLi-3 zero-shot (0.18) trail further behind. On Textual Misalignment NLI, InstructBLIP and MiniGPT-v2 tie at 0.56, but PaLi ft. Multitask achieves 0.78 (+39%), and LLaVa-1.5 is notably weak at 0.17. For Visual Misalignment F1@0.75, InstructBLIP leads the baselines at 0.48, while PaLi ft. Multitask reaches 0.65 (+35%); the remaining baselines cluster between 0.43 and 0.47. On Binary Alignment Accuracy, InstructBLIP (0.74) and LLaVa-1.5 (0.72) are the strongest baselines, with PaLi-X ft. Multitask achieving 0.79 and PaLi ft. Multitask 0.77, a modest but consistent gain. Notably, the larger PaLi-X model wins on binary classification but slightly underperforms the smaller PaLi model on feedback generation tasks, suggesting a trade-off in multitask fine-tuning efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image-text alignment evaluation line (CLIP, CLIPScore, ImageReward, SeeTRUE) by adding an explanation layer: rather than outputting only a binary alignment score, the model generates textual feedback, identifies misaligned text segments, and draws bounding boxes on the image. It departs from prior explainable-evaluation approaches (TIFA, VQ², DSG, VPEval) that rely on interrogative QA pipelines or sub-task decomposition, instead training a single VLM (PaLI family) end-to-end to produce all feedback in one inference. The training data is synthesized at scale via a ConGen-Feedback pipeline that combines PaLM 2 for contradiction generation with GroundingDINO for visual grounding, drawing on six source datasets (COCO, Flickr30K, PickaPic, ImageReward, ADE20K, OpenImages).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The publicly released TV-Feedback training set (3M instances) and SeeTRUE-Feedback human-annotated benchmark (2,008 instances) give the community a concrete resource for training and evaluating alignment-explanation models, a capability that was previously absent. The feedback signal is directly actionable for improving T2I generation, correcting noisy dataset annotations, and refining captioning models. Key open questions remain: the model struggles with missing-object misalignments (incorrectly drawing boxes), multi-misalignment cases (detecting only one issue per pass), and producing overly loose bounding boxes—limitations the authors flag but do not resolve. Whether the ConGen-Feedback data-generation recipe generalizes beyond the PaLI architecture family is also untested.
