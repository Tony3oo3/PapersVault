## Problem

Current large language and vision models (LLVMs) rely primarily on the large capacity and emergent capabilities of their LLM backbones for visual perception, overlooking the detailed real-world scene understanding—object existence, positions, relations, and text recognition—already available from decades of specialized computer vision models. This paper asks whether explicitly incorporating auxiliary visual information from external CV models (panoptic segmentation, open-world detection, scene graph generation, OCR) can substantially improve zero-shot VL performance without enlarging model size or curating additional instruction tuning datasets.

## Input / Output

MoAI takes an RGB image and a text question as input. The image is simultaneously processed by a CLIP-L/14 vision encoder (for visual features) and four external CV models (Mask2Former, OWLv2, panoptic SGG, PaddleOCR, totaling ~332M parameters), whose outputs are verbalized into natural-language descriptions and converted to auxiliary tokens via the MLM's word embeddings. The model produces a free-form text answer generated with deterministic beam search (n=3) under 4-bit NF4 quantization, achieving ~20 tokens/s inference speed comparable to baselines of similar MLM size.

## Method

MoAI introduces two modules that integrate auxiliary CV information into an InternLM2-7B backbone. The MoAI-Compressor (a 4-layer Perceiver Resampler) compresses the variable-length concatenation of verbalized tokens from all four CV models into a fixed set of 64 learnable tokens, preserving spatial locality from the segmentation map. The MoAI-Mixer, inserted at four specific MLM layers (7, 15, 23, 31), employs six LoRA-parameterized cross- and self-attention expert modules—three for visual features (querying auxiliary, language, and self) and three for language features (querying auxiliary, visual, and self)—to explicitly blend three intelligence types. A two-step training procedure first trains the experts with random expert sampling, then learns per-layer gating networks (single linear layer + softmax) that produce token-level confidence weights to dynamically combine the three expert outputs. This design ensures each expert specializes in a distinct modality pair while the gating mechanism adaptively determines how much auxiliary, visual, and language information each token should attend to.

## Results

MoAI-7B achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot performance across nine VL benchmarks, scoring 70.2 on Q-Bench, 83.5 on SQA-IMG, 67.8 on TextVQA, 87.1 on POPE, 1714/561 on MME (perception/cognition), 79.3 on MM-Bench, 76.5 on MM-Bench-CN, and 43.7 on MM-Vet. These results surpass all open-source LLVMs of comparable or larger size (e.g., LLaVA1.5-13B, ShareGPT4V-7B, Intern-XC-7B, LLaVA-XTuner-20B) and also outperform closed-source models such as GPT-4V and Gemini-Pro on most benchmarks. Notably, MoAI achieves this without enlarging model size or curating additional visual instruction tuning datasets, relying instead on auxiliary information from four external CV models (Mask2Former, OWLv2, Panoptic SGG, PaddleOCR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoAI-7B outperforms all open-source LLVMs of equal or larger size on nearly every benchmark. Against LLaVA1.5-7B, MoAI gains +11.5 on Q-Bench, +16.7 on SQA-IMG, +9.6 on TextVQA, +203 on MME-P, +267 on MME-C, +15.0 on MM-Bench, and +13.2 on MM-Vet. Against the larger LLaVA1.5-13B, MoAI still leads by +8.1 on Q-Bench, +11.9 on SQA-IMG, +183 on MME-P, +266 on MME-C, and +11.6 on MM-Vet. MoAI also surpasses the 20B LLaVA-XTuner by +4.2 on MM-Bench and +6.3 on MM-Vet, and beats the 17B CogVLM on all metrics except MM-Vet (43.7 vs. 54.5), where CogVLM retains a narrow edge. Compared to closed-source models (GPT-4V, Gemini-Pro, Qwen-VL-Plus) shown in Fig. 1 and Fig. 7, MoAI-7B achieves competitive or superior scores on most VL benchmarks despite being a 7B open-source model. The consistent advantage across scene-understanding dimensions (existence, position, spatial relation, OCR) in MME, SEED, MM-Bench, and MM-Vet (Fig. 2) confirms that the external CV model auxiliary information is the primary driver of MoAI's gains over baselines that rely solely on LLM backbone capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoAI departs from the dominant LLVM paradigm of scaling up model capacity or curating larger visual instruction tuning datasets, instead explicitly incorporating outputs from specialized CV models (panoptic segmentation, open-world detection, scene graph generation, OCR) that have been 'overshadowed' by large multimodal backbones. Architecturally, it repurposes the Mixture of Experts concept not for capacity scaling but for blending three distinct intelligence streams (visual, auxiliary, language) through six explicitly-assigned cross- and self-attention expert modules with learned gating. The MoAI-Compressor borrows the Perceiver Resampler structure from Flamingo [3] to condense variable-length verbalized CV outputs into fixed-size tokens, while LoRA-based parameterization keeps the expert modules computationally lightweight.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key insight—that a 7B model augmented with ~332M parameters of external CV models can outperform 13B, 34B, and closed-source LLVMs on zero-shot VL benchmarks—challenges the assumption that scaling is the primary lever for improving real-world scene understanding. This offers a practical, compute-efficient path for practitioners who cannot afford to train or deploy very large models. However, the approach remains limited to perception-level tasks (existence, position, relations, OCR) and does not address low-level vision, commonsense reasoning, charts, or math; the reliance on multiple external models also introduces potential error propagation and integration complexity that the authors acknowledge but do not fully resolve.
