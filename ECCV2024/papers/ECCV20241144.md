## Problem

Existing radiance field quantization methods (PTQ, LSQ+) compress all scenes to a single fixed bitwidth, ignoring that complex scenes with rich geometry and texture are far more sensitive to quantization than simple ones. No prior work provides a content-aware mechanism that automatically aligns model complexity with scene intricacy, leaving bitwidth selection to manual trial-and-error.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241144/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images of a scene (Synthetic-NeRF, RTMV, Mip-NeRF360) along with per-ray 3D position and 2D view-direction parameters, and produces a fully quantized Instant-NGP model (hash table + MLPs) with per-layer, per-scene integer bitwidths constrained to [2, 32]. The output supports integer-only inference for novel-view rendering at 800×800 resolution, with a user-defined accuracy metric (MSE threshold) controlling the trade-off between reconstruction fidelity and computational cost.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-part framework: (1) Learned Bitwidth Quantization (LBQ) introduces a floating-point 'soft bitwidth' b (with integer B = ⌊b⌋) alongside trainable range scale r_v and upper bound v_max, making the quantization bitwidth differentiable via the Straight-Through Estimator so that gradients from reconstruction error can directly update bitwidth; (2) Adversarial Content-Aware Quantization (A-CAQ) formulates a multi-task objective that alternately minimizes the NeRF volume-rendering MSE loss and a bitwidth penalty loss L^bit = √|L^NeRF − L^metric| + Σ εᵢBᵢ, creating an adversarial dynamic where reducing bitwidth increases reconstruction error, which in turn opens more search space for further bitwidth reduction. Three distinct quantization schemes (symmetric signed for weights, asymmetric unsigned for ReLU/exp activations, trainable-offset for positional encoding and hash table) are applied per component, and the framework learns both scene-wise and layer-wise bitwidths simultaneously, enabling mixed-precision models that achieve ~90% BitOps reduction with <0.5 dB PSNR loss in high-fidelity mode.

## Results

On Synthetic-NeRF, A-CAQ achieves a Feature Quantization Rate (FQR) of 7.76 bits under the Minimal Degradation Learning (MDL) setting while maintaining 32.00 dB PSNR, compared to 9.60 bits for both PTQ and LSQ+ baselines. Under the more aggressive Metric-Guided Learning (MGL, 10⁻³) setting, A-CAQ reduces FQR to 4.74 bits with 25.96 dB PSNR, substantially outperforming PTQ (17.75 dB) and LSQ+ (20.26 dB) at the same 5.60-bit budget. Across Mip-NeRF360 and RTMV, the same pattern holds: A-CAQ achieves lower FQR (6.58 and 5.67 vs. 6.60 and 5.60) with higher PSNR in the MGL scenario. In terms of computational complexity, A-CAQ reduces BitOps by ~90.78% and storage by ~77.68% relative to full-precision NGP in MDL, and by ~94.45% and ~89.43% in MGL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares A-CAQ against three baselines: full-precision Instant-NGP, post-training quantization (PTQ, from ICARUS [27]), and quantization-aware training with fixed 8-bit (LSQ+ [3]). In the MDL (high-fidelity) setting, A-CAQ achieves a lower FQR (7.76 vs. 9.60) while matching or exceeding PSNR (32.00 vs. 31.98 for PTQ, 32.00 vs. 32.11 for LSQ+ on Synthetic-NeRF), meaning it uses ~19% fewer bits at negligible quality cost. In the MGL (resource-constrained) settings, the advantage is dramatic: at MGL(10⁻³) on Synthetic-NeRF, A-CAQ reaches 25.96 dB PSNR at 4.74 bits, whereas PTQ drops to 17.75 dB and LSQ+ to 20.26 dB at 5.60 bits — a 5.7 dB and 5.7 dB PSNR gap respectively. On RTMV (V8) at MGL(10⁻³), the gap is even starker: 26.16 dB (A-CAQ) vs. 10.54 dB (PTQ) and 14.36 dB (LSQ+). A-CAQ consistently wins on both FQR (lower) and PSNR (higher) across all three datasets and all three accuracy settings, with the largest margins in the aggressive compression regime where fixed-bitwidth methods suffer severe degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the quantization-aware training (QAT) lineage from LSQ/LSQ+ to radiance field models, but departs from the fixed-bitwidth assumption by introducing a differentiable 'soft bitwidth' parameter that makes the integer bitwidth itself a learnable quantity. It builds directly on Instant-NGP's hash-encoding + MLP architecture as the baseline, and positions itself against prior radiance-field quantization work (ICARUS for PTQ, LSQ+ for QAT) that compresses all scenes to a single uniform bitwidth. The adversarial formulation—where bitwidth minimization and reconstruction quality are in tension—is conceptually related to mixed-precision quantization in CNNs (e.g., CADyQ for super-resolution) but is the first to apply it in a per-scene, content-adaptive manner for implicit 3D representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deploying radiance fields on resource-constrained edge devices, where a ~90% reduction in BitOps and ~78% reduction in storage (MDL scenario) while preserving PSNR is a meaningful efficiency gain. However, the scope is narrow: the method is validated only on Instant-NGP across three datasets, and the 'content-aware' signal is essentially the training MSE, which is a coarse proxy for scene complexity. The authors themselves note that a truly content-aware framework would require targeted architecture search guided by reliable 3D complexity indicators, leaving the broader vision of adaptive radiance fields largely unexplored. Whether the differentiable bitwidth formulation generalizes to other radiance field architectures (3DGS, TensoRF, etc.) remains an open question.
