## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods for vision Transformers exhibit significant performance disparities across different datasets and tasks, which the authors attribute to insufficient robustness during training. With limited fine-tuning data (often <1,000 samples), the optimization direction in early training is unstable, and current reparameterization methods like LoRA lack adequate regularization to guide the model toward robust solutions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240182/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on 224×224 RGB images fed into a ViT-B/16 backbone (85.8M parameters, 12 blocks, 768-dim hidden layers) pretrained on ImageNet-21K. The LoRA modules are applied specifically to the Query and Value linear projections in each attention block, with rank-8 down/up matrices (768×8 and 8×768). At inference, all trained branches are averaged and merged into the original weight matrices, so the output is a standard class label with zero additional FLOPs or parameters compared to the base model.

## Method

DMLoRA expands standard LoRA into n parallel up/down branches (MixLoRA) that are randomly routed per training step, providing stronger regularization and a more robust initial optimization direction. As training progresses, branches are gradually pruned by random dropout (halving every k epochs until a target count is reached), balancing early-stage regularization against late-stage accuracy. A 2-Stage Learning Scalar strategy first trains all branch parameters with a fixed appropriate scale (1 or 10), then freezes the branches and optimizes per-layer scale factors for 10 additional epochs to redistribute each layer's LoRA contribution. At inference, the multi-branch structure is collapsed by averaging branch parameters and absorbing them into the original weights, incurring no additional computational overhead. The approach outperforms prior PEFT baselines by ~0.9% average on VTAB-1K and matches the best on FGVC while using only 0.29M trainable parameters.

## Results

On the VTAB-1K benchmark (19 tasks, 1,000 training samples each), DMLoRA-LS achieves an average accuracy of 77.0% with only 0.29M trainable parameters and zero additional inference overhead, outperforming the best prior PEFT method (RepAdapter, 76.1%) by 0.9% and full fine-tuning (68.9%) by 8.1%. On the FGVC fine-grained classification suite (5 datasets), DMLoRA-LS achieves 90.7% average accuracy, tying SSF (90.7%) and surpassing full fine-tuning (88.5%) by 2.2%. The method also leads on Natural (+0.89%) and Structured (+0.85%) photo subsets of VTAB-1K over all prior PEFT approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMLoRA-LS (77.0% avg) outperforms all 10 PEFT baselines on VTAB-1K, with the closest competitor being RepAdapter (76.1%, +0.9% gap) and SSF (75.7%, +1.3% gap). On the FGVC suite, DMLoRA-LS ties SSF at 90.7% average but leads on individual datasets: CUB-200-2011 (89.8 vs. 89.5), NABirds (86.6 vs. 85.7), and Stanford Dogs (91.8 vs. 89.6), while SSF leads on Stanford Cars (89.2 vs. 85.7). Compared to the reparameterization baselines LoRA (74.5%) and FacT (75.6%), DMLoRA-LS gains 2.5% and 1.4% respectively, demonstrating the benefit of multi-branch dropout over single-branch low-rank updates. Prompt-based methods (VPT-Deep 72.0%, E²VPT 73.9%) and adapter-based methods (Adapter 73.9%, AdaptFormer 74.7%) all trail DMLoRA-LS by 2.3–5.0%, and unlike these methods, DMLoRA-LS introduces zero additional inference FLOPs or parameters (ΔP=0, ΔF=0). The method also surpasses full fine-tuning (68.9%) by 8.1% on VTAB-1K while using only 0.34% of the trainable parameters (0.29M vs. 85.8M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LoRA (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) by expanding its single up/down matrix pair into multiple randomly routed branches, drawing architectural inspiration from AdaMix (Wang et al., 2023), which applies a similar mixture-of-branches idea to naive adapters. The key departure from AdaMix is twofold: DMLoRA uses LoRA as the branch unit rather than a simple bottleneck adapter, and it introduces a gradual branch-dropout schedule that reduces the number of active branches over training, whereas AdaMix keeps a fixed branch count throughout. The 2-Stage Learning Scalar strategy is a novel training procedure specific to this work, decoupling the optimization of the LoRA scale factor from the branch parameters to avoid interference between the two.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical pain point in vision PEFT: the inconsistent performance of existing methods across diverse downstream tasks, which the authors attribute to insufficient regularization during fine-tuning. The reparameterization design ensures zero inference overhead, making the approach directly deployable without architectural changes. However, the gains are modest (≈0.9% on VTAB-1K, tied with SSF on FGVC), and the method is validated only on ViT-B/16 for classification; whether the multi-branch dropout strategy transfers to larger backbones, non-classification tasks (detection, segmentation), or different pre-training regimes remains open. The paper also acknowledges residual performance disparities across tasks, suggesting that robustness alone may not fully resolve the vision PEFT gap.
