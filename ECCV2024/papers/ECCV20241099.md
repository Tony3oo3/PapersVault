## Problem

GCN-based skeleton action recognition methods are constrained by fixed joint connectivity, limiting their ability to capture relations between physically distant joints (e.g., both hands during clapping). While transformer-based methods with full self-attention can model all joint pairs, they incur prohibitive O((VT)²) computational cost and memory usage. The paper addresses how to efficiently capture joint-element-level skeletal-temporal relations without full global attention or lossy tokenization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241099/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D skeleton sequence of shape (T, V, M, 3) where T=64 frames, V=48 joints (24 per individual × 2 individuals for NTU RGB+D) or V=20 for NW-UCLA, and M is the number of individuals. Joints from different individuals are flattened into a single V dimension. The output is a class probability vector over N_c action classes. For ensemble evaluation, separate networks are trained on joint, bone, joint-motion, and bone-motion modalities, with outputs combined via weighted averaging.

## Method

SkateFormer introduces Skate-MSA, which partitions the joint-frame feature map into four skeletal-temporal relation types: (1) neighboring joints + local motion, (2) distant joints + local motion, (3) neighboring joints + global motion, and (4) distant joints + global motion. Each partition undergoes independent multi-head self-attention at the joint-element level (no tokenization), reducing attention complexity by ~48× compared to naive full attention. The SkateFormer Block also incorporates a learnable-adjacency G-Conv and a 1D T-Conv in parallel with Skate-MSA to inject skeletal and temporal inductive biases. A novel Skate-Embedding forms positional encodings via the outer product of learnable skeletal features and fixed sinusoidal temporal index features, enabling robustness to variable-length input sequences. Training uses trimmed-uniform random frame sampling and inter-instance bone-length exchange augmentation to improve generalization.

## Results

On NTU RGB+D, SkateFormer achieves 92.6% top-1 accuracy (E1, X-Sub60) and 97.0% (E1, X-View60), outperforming the best GCN (HD-GCN: 90.6%/95.7%) and best Transformer (Hyperformer: 90.7%/95.1%) baselines. On NTU RGB+D 120, it reaches 87.7% (E1, X-Sub120) and 89.3% (E1, X-Set120), and on NW-UCLA it attains 98.3%. For interaction recognition, SkateFormer scores 97.1% (NTU-Inter X-Sub60) and 92.3% (NTU-Inter 120 X-Sub120), surpassing the previous best (SkeleTR: 94.9%/87.8%). The model uses only 2.03M parameters and 3.62G FLOPs, comparable to GCN-based methods and substantially lower than other Transformer approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SkateFormer outperforms all 19 compared baselines on nearly every benchmark, with the sole exception of NTU RGB+D 120 X-Sub120 under the 4-ensemble setting (89.8% vs. Koopman's 90.0%). Against the strongest GCN baseline (HD-GCN), SkateFormer gains +2.0% on X-Sub60 E1 (92.6% vs. 90.6%) and +1.3% on X-View60 E1 (97.0% vs. 95.7%). Against the strongest Transformer baseline (Hyperformer), the margins are +1.9% on X-Sub60 E1 and +1.9% on X-View60 E1. On the interaction-recognition subset (NTU-Inter), SkateFormer achieves 97.1% (X-Sub60) and 99.3% (X-View60), surpassing SkeleTR (94.9%/97.7%) by +2.2%/+1.6% and ISTA-Net by a wide margin on NTU-Inter 120 (92.3% vs. 90.6%). In terms of efficiency, SkateFormer (2.03M params, 3.62G FLOPs, 11.46 ms) matches GCN-level complexity while other Transformers like Hyperformer (2.71M, 9.64G, 18.07 ms) and ISTA-Net (6.22M, 68.18G, 21.71 ms) are substantially heavier.

## Relation to Prior Work

SkateFormer sits at the intersection of two lines of work in skeleton-based action recognition: GCN-based methods that propagate information along fixed graph edges (limited receptive field for distant joints) and transformer-based methods that attend over all joint-frame pairs (prohibitively expensive). It departs from prior transformer approaches (DSTA-Net, STST, FG-STFormer, Hyperformer) that either decouple skeletal and temporal attention or tokenize joint groups, by introducing a partition-specific multi-head self-attention (Skate-MSA) that operates at the joint-element level across four skeletal-temporal relation types. The design is conceptually inspired by multi-axis attention in vision (MaxViT) but restructured around the physically meaningful axes of skeletal proximity and temporal locality specific to human motion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving state-of-the-art accuracy with FLOPs (3.62G) comparable to GCN baselines while other transformer methods require 2–4× more compute, making skeleton-based recognition more deployable in real-time or resource-constrained settings. The Skate-Embedding (outer product of learnable skeletal and fixed sinusoidal temporal features) and trimmed-uniform random sampling are lightweight techniques likely to be adopted independently. Open questions include whether the fixed 4-type partition is optimal or should be learned, how the method scales to longer sequences or more joints (e.g., multi-person scenes beyond two), and whether the partition strategy transfers to other sequential graph-structured data beyond human skeletons.
