## Problem

Existing brain decoding methods produce a single modality (text or image), losing either spatial localization or fine-grained visual detail, and they require training a separate model per subject due to inter-subject variability in fMRI patterns. This limits practical brain-machine interfaces, especially for locked-in patients who need both concept recognition and spatial grounding in a unified, data-efficient framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240175/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes variable-length fMRI brain signals (arbitrary length L_s per subject, from the NSD dataset) and a free-form text instruction prompt as input. The brain encoder produces features aligned to CLIP ViT-L/14 intermediate representations (16×16×1024, reshaped to 256×D), which are then consumed by a frozen MLLM adapter. Depending on the prompt, the system outputs text captions, bounding-box coordinates for grounding, retrieved image indices, or reconstructed images via a diffusion model.

## Method

UMBRAE comprises a subject-specific tokenizer (projecting variable-length fMRI into fixed-length brain tokens plus learnable subject tokens) and a universal Perceiver-based encoder that uses cross-attention to map all subjects' brain tokens into a shared latent space. Cross-subject training employs a batch sampling strategy where θ=50% of each batch comes from a single subject and the remainder is drawn uniformly from other subjects, enabling one model to serve all subjects without extra compute. Alignment is achieved by minimizing MSE between brain encoder outputs and frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 intermediate image features (rather than text embeddings), preserving both semantic and spatial cues. At inference, the aligned brain features are fed into a frozen Shikra MLLM (Vicuna-7B/13B) via its adapter, and a simple prompt template routes the system to captioning, grounding, retrieval, or visual decoding tasks.

## Results

On Brain Captioning (S1), UMBRAE achieves BLEU-1 of 59.44, CIDEr of 61.06, SPICE of 12.79, and CLIP-S of 67.78, outperforming all baselines by significant margins. On Brain Retrieval, UMBRAE reaches 94.2% forward, 91.3% backward, and 93.8% exemplar accuracy, surpassing MindEye (93.6%/90.1%/93.2%) while using only 146M parameters vs. MindEye's 1,004M. On Brain Grounding (S1), UMBRAE achieves 18.93% acc@0.5 and 21.28 IoU, matching the best constructed baseline (Shikra-w/BrainDiffuser at 17.49%/19.34%) while running at least 10× faster (0.92s vs. 16.4s per sample).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UMBRAE outperforms all brain-decoding baselines on Brain Captioning: it beats BrainCap [12] by +3.5 BLEU-1 and +19.8 CIDEr, OneLLM [14] by +12.4 BLEU-1 and +38.1 CIDEr, and SDRecon [40] by +23.2 BLEU-1, with the gap attributable to aligning with image features rather than text embeddings. On Brain Retrieval, UMBRAE (94.2%/91.3%/93.8%) surpasses MindEye [37] (93.6%/90.1%/93.2%) and BrainDiffuser [31] (21.1%/30.3%) by large margins while using 85% fewer parameters than MindEye. On Brain Grounding, UMBRAE (18.93% acc@0.5) matches or slightly exceeds the best constructed baseline Shikra-w/BrainDiffuser [31] (17.49%) and Shikra-w/DREAM [49] (16.21%), while being ~18× faster (0.92s vs. 16.4s). On Visual Decoding, UMBRAE is competitive but not uniformly best: it leads on AlexNet(2) (95.5%) and SSIM (0.341) but trails MindEye [37] on Inception (91.7% vs. 93.8%) and CLIP (93.5% vs. 94.1%), and DREAM [49] on SwAV distance (0.393 vs. 0.418). The only method UMBRAE consistently loses to is the Shikra-w/img [7] upper bound, which uses ground-truth images rather than brain signals, confirming the inherent information loss in brain decoding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Perceiver architecture for its universal perceive encoder and on MLLM frameworks (Shikra, LLaVA) for the brain prompting interface, but departs from the dominant paradigm in brain decoding in two key ways. Prior methods (BrainDiffuser, MindEye, DREAM, OneLLM) train subject-specific models and decode into a single modality (image or text); UMBRAE instead learns a cross-subject universal encoder aligned to intermediate CLIP image features, enabling a single model to serve multiple subjects and to produce multimodal outputs (captions, bounding boxes, retrieved images) through a frozen MLLM. The alignment strategy—simple MSE reconstruction to image features rather than contrastive learning or text-space alignment—represents a deliberate departure from the contrastive pretraining practice common in multimodal brain alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-subject training strategy and weakly-supervised adaptation (comparable performance with 30% of a new subject's data) address a critical practical bottleneck: fMRI data collection is expensive and subject-specific, so a single model generalizing across subjects with minimal per-subject fine-tuning has direct implications for scalable brain-computer interfaces. The BrainHub benchmark, extending NSD with grounding and retrieval annotations, fills a gap in evaluation infrastructure for brain understanding beyond pixel-level reconstruction. However, the work is limited to four subjects on a single dataset (NSD), grounding accuracy remains far below the natural-image upper bound (~19% vs. ~52% acc@0.5), and the method's reliance on a frozen 7B/13B LLM raises questions about deployment in latency-sensitive BCI settings despite the 10× speed advantage over decode-then-ground pipelines.
