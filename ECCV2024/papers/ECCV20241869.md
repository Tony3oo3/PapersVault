## Problem

NeRF-based 3D surface reconstruction depends on accurate camera poses, but real-world SfM pipelines frequently produce outlier poses with large angular deviations that cannot be corrected by local optimization. Existing joint pose-scene methods (BARF, SCNeRF, L2G-NeRF, etc.) assume moderate initial errors and fail under significant pose noise, while the scene graph structure produced by SfM has never been leveraged to guide pose correction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241869/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of unorganized RGB images (18–108 per scene) with known camera intrinsics and no distortion, targeting inward-facing object-scanning scenarios. Initial camera poses and a scene graph are obtained via COLMAP with SuperPoint/SuperGlue, where 1/9 to 1/3 of poses may be significantly incorrect. The output is a 3D surface mesh extracted via marching cubes from the NeuS density field, along with refined camera poses each assigned an inlier-outlier confidence score; novel view synthesis is also available from the trained radiance field.

## Method

SG-NeRF jointly optimizes a NeuS-based radiance field and an SfM-initialized scene graph through alternating field-pose and confidence-update steps. A key mechanism is an adaptive inlier-outlier confidence score: initially computed from keypoint match counts per node, then iteratively refined by fusing PSNR-based rendering fidelity, which drives confidence-weighted sampling so that well-posed images dominate training. A novel two-view IoU loss is computed by projecting rays from matched keypoints, fitting a mixture of Gaussians along each ray using the top-8 density points, and maximizing the intersection-over-union between the two MoGs to simultaneously refine poses and surface geometry. A coarse-to-fine strategy applies a Gaussian filter with decreasing standard deviation to input images, initially suppressing high-frequency details to let the optimizer focus on pose correction before recovering fine surface structure.

## Results

On the proposed 8-scene dataset with significant outlier poses (1/9 to 1/3 of images), SG-NeRF achieves a mean Chamfer distance of 0.46 and F-score of 0.85, outperforming the NeuS backbone by 61% in Chamfer distance and 15% in F-score. On the DTU benchmark with injected pose noise (1/7 to 1/4 of images perturbed), SG-NeRF achieves a mean Chamfer distance of 1.13, surpassing the next-best method Neuralangelo (1.38) by 18%. The method also avoids the complete failure cases observed in several baselines (e.g., Neuralangelo on Bell, L2G-NeRF on Pavilion, Joint-TensoRF on scene 40).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SG-NeRF outperforms all eight baselines on both Chamfer distance and F-score across both datasets. The closest competitor on the proposed dataset is BARF* (Chamfer 1.05, F-score 0.75), which still trails SG-NeRF by 56% in Chamfer and 13% in F-score; the paper attributes BARF*'s relative strength to its simpler local optimization being less susceptible to catastrophic failure, but it cannot correct large angular deviations. On DTU, Neuralangelo is the nearest rival (Chamfer 1.38 vs. 1.13), benefiting from its high-fidelity surface representation when pose errors are moderate, but it degrades more sharply under the injected noise. The pose-optimization baselines (SCNeRF*, GARF*, L2G-NeRF*, Joint-TensoRF*) all suffer from their reliance on local optimization, which propagates errors from outlier poses to inlier poses, resulting in Chamfer distances 2–4× worse than SG-NeRF. PoRF, the only other joint pose-surface method evaluated, achieves Chamfer 1.42 and F-score 0.67, still substantially behind SG-NeRF. Notably, SG-NeRF is the only method that produces reasonable reconstructions across all 8 scenes without any complete failures, whereas Neuralangelo, L2G-NeRF, and Joint-TensoRF each fail on at least one scene.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on NeuS as its implicit surface backbone and on COLMAP (with SuperPoint/SuperGlue) for initial scene graph construction, but departs from the line of joint NeRF-pose optimization work (BARF, SCNeRF, L2G-NeRF, Joint-TensoRF) by reframing pose correction as a scene-graph-level problem with adaptive inlier-outlier confidence scoring rather than purely local bundle adjustment. The key conceptual departure is repurposing the SfM scene graph—previously treated as a static input—into a dynamically optimized structure whose node confidences are updated via rendering fidelity (PSNR) during training, effectively down-weighting outlier images. The proposed IoU loss, computed via mixture-of-Gaussians over matched keypoint rays, introduces a geometric consistency signal that prior pose-optimization methods lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuinely practical failure mode: SfM systems routinely produce a fraction of severely wrong poses that cascade into broken reconstructions, and existing joint-optimization methods cannot recover from such large errors. The confidence-based sampling mechanism offers a simple yet effective way to make NeRF training robust to this, and the new 8-scene dataset with 11–33% outlier poses provides a much harder benchmark than the moderate-noise settings in prior work. The authors acknowledge that outlier poses are only moderately improved (not fully rectified), pointing to visual (re)localization as a promising future direction for closing that gap. Broader impact is tempered by the method's dependence on a fixed graph topology and its 11–18 hour training cost per scene.
