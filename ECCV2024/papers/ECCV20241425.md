## Problem

Learning-based 3D shape matching methods achieve state-of-the-art accuracy but provide no guarantee of geometric consistency (neighbouring triangles mapping to neighbouring triangles), while existing axiomatic ILP formulations that enforce this property do not scale to practical mesh resolutions and require user-provided initialisations. The paper closes this gap by proposing an efficient, initialisation-free combinatorial solver that can handle the geometrically consistent shape matching ILP at practical sizes and certify global optimality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241425/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of 3D triangle meshes (M, N) and per-vertex features extracted by a pretrained network (URSSM's feature extractor), operating on the product space P of triangle-triangle, triangle-edge, and triangle-vertex correspondences to accommodate differing triangulations and local stretching. The output is a binary indicator vector x over P that defines a discrete diffeomorphism: every triangle in each mesh is matched exactly once, and geometric consistency constraints ensure adjacency is preserved. Main experiments use meshes downsampled to 450 faces; a coarse-to-fine extension handles up to 4000 triangles per shape.

## Method

DiscoMatch solves the geometrically consistent shape matching ILP via Lagrangian dual decomposition, extending FastDOG with two key solver improvements: (1) interleaving L-BFGS quasi-Newton updates with the first-order min-marginal averaging (MMA) scheme to exploit second-order curvature information, yielding 9–11× faster dual convergence, and (2) a BDD splitting technique that decomposes large constraint subproblems into smaller chunks solvable in parallel on GPUs. The cost function replaces hand-designed elastic energy with mixed-area-weighted distances between pretrained feature embeddings, injecting richer geometric information and reducing solver runtimes. The approach is fully initialisation-free (the dual is concave, so any local optimum is global) and provides primal-dual optimality gaps, certifying global optimality in 90% of tested instances. For higher resolutions, a coarse-to-fine pruning strategy restricts fine-level matchings to one- and two-ring neighbourhoods of coarser-level correspondences.

## Results

On FAUST (450 faces), DiscoMatch with the learned energy achieves a mean geodesic error of 0.041 in 134 s with only 2 infeasible solutions, compared to 0.041/327 s/4 for FastDOG and 0.042/376 s/9 for Sm-Comb. The method certifies global optimality in 90% of instances and is 9–11× faster in dual convergence than FastDOG. Geometric consistency constraints reduce mean geodesic errors by up to 50% relative to URSSM while consistently improving conformal distortion (smoothness) across all four datasets. At 4000-triangle resolution via coarse-to-fine, DiscoMatch achieves geodesic errors of 0.0193 (FAUST) and 0.0161 (DT4D-Intra), marginally outperforming URSSM (0.0197 and 0.0165) but at ~20 min runtime vs. seconds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against URSSM (the state-of-the-art learning-based method), DiscoMatch achieves lower mean geodesic errors (sometimes by >50%) and consistently lower conformal distortion errors across FAUST, SMAL, DT4D-Intra, and DT4D-Inter, while guaranteeing geometric consistency; URSSM is orders of magnitude faster (seconds vs. minutes) but produces locally non-smooth matchings. Against FastDOG, DiscoMatch's quasi-Newton + BDD-splitting solver converges 9–11× faster in the dual, finds globally optimal solutions in 90% of instances (vs. far fewer for FastDOG), and produces only 2 infeasible solutions vs. 4 for FastDOG and 9 for Sm-Comb on FAUST. Against Sm-Comb, DiscoMatch is roughly 2–3× faster and finds fewer infeasible solutions. Against LP-Relax, DiscoMatch scales to much larger problem sizes where LP-Relax fails. KrnlM (kernel matching) struggles on all datasets due to its permutation-matrix assumption being invalid for shapes with different discretisations, and is excluded from further comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: the ILP formulation for geometrically consistent shape matching (Windheuser et al. [68]) and the GPU-parallel dual decomposition solver FastDOG (Abbas & Swoboda [1]). It departs from prior combinatorial solvers by adding L-BFGS quasi-Newton updates and BDD constraint splitting to the min-marginal averaging scheme, and from prior learning-based matchers (e.g., URSSM [11]) by enforcing geometric consistency as hard constraints rather than a soft regulariser. The learned feature embeddings from [11] replace the hand-crafted elastic energy costs of [68], injecting richer descriptive information into the ILP objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that geometric consistency constraints, long considered a computational burden, can actually *improve* matching accuracy (up to 50% reduction in geodesic error) while certifying global optimality in 90% of instances — a strong argument for incorporating structural guarantees into shape matching pipelines. The main open limitation is the quadratic space complexity of the product-space formalism, which caps direct solving at ~1000 triangles per shape on a 48 GB GPU; the coarse-to-fine workaround (20 min for 4k triangles) remains orders of magnitude slower than learning-based methods (seconds). Future work could explore sparser product-space representations or learned pruning to close this gap, and the solver improvements (quasi-Newton + BDD splitting) are stated to be generally applicable beyond shape matching.
