## Problem

Existing novel class discovery methods for point cloud segmentation (e.g., NOPS) impose an equal class-size constraint on novel classes to avoid degenerate clustering, but the inherent size imbalance of 3D objects and point density in real scenes violates this assumption. Additionally, point-wise clustering ignores the rich spatial context of objects, yielding less expressive representations for semantic segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240340/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud scene (voxelized at 0.05 m) where known-class points carry ground-truth labels and novel-class points are unlabeled. Two augmented views are generated per scene via scale (0.95–1.05) and rotation (±π/20 per axis). The network outputs per-point class probabilities over both known and novel categories, and the self-labeling module produces soft pseudo-labels (an M×|C^u| transport plan) for unlabeled points and their DBSCAN-derived regions, which are then exchanged between the two views for training.

## Method

The framework uses a MinkowskiUNet-34C backbone to extract point features, then clusters unlabeled points into regions via DBSCAN and computes region features by average pooling, forming a dual-level representation. A shared prototype-based classifier (cosine similarity + softmax) predicts class probabilities at both point and region levels, enforcing intra-region consistency. Pseudo-labels for novel classes are generated by solving a semi-relaxed entropic Optimal Transport problem that relaxes the equal-size constraint and adds a KL regularization toward the uniform prior with weight γ. The key novelty is an adaptive annealing schedule for γ: it is multiplied by λ whenever the KL divergence between the pseudo-label class distribution and the uniform distribution stays below a threshold ρ for T consecutive iterations, progressively relaxing the constraint as the model learns meaningful clusters. Training alternates between pseudo-label generation (gradient-free) and full-model updates with cross-entropy losses on known classes, point-level novel classes, and region-level novel classes.

## Results

On SemanticPOSS, the proposed method achieves an average novel-class IoU of 30.2% across four splits, outperforming NOPS (22.5%) by 7.7% and EUMS (~15.0%) by a large margin. On SemanticKITTI, the method reaches 27.5% average novel-class IoU, surpassing NOPS (23.4%) by 4.1%. Under a more severe imbalance setting (two-way split of SemanticPOSS), the method still leads NOPS by 7.6% (split 0) and 5.1% (split 1) on novel classes. The fully supervised upper bounds for novel classes range from 26.9% to 72.7% depending on the split, and the method significantly narrows the gap to these bounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: NOPS (Riz et al., CVPR 2023), the most directly related point-cloud NCD method using equal-size OT constraints, and EUMS (Zhao et al., CVPR 2022), a 2D semantic segmentation NCD method adapted for comparison. On SemanticPOSS, the proposed method outperforms NOPS by 7.7% average novel-class IoU (30.2% vs. 22.5%) and EUMS by roughly 15% across all four splits. On SemanticKITTI, the improvement over NOPS is 4.1% (27.5% vs. 23.4%) on novel classes. Under a more severe two-way imbalance split of SemanticPOSS, the gap over NOPS widens to 7.6% (split 0) and 5.1% (split 1), with the method using a simpler pipeline (no multi-head or overclustering tricks that NOPS employs). The method also improves known-class IoU substantially (e.g., 53.8% vs. 44.2% on split 2), indicating that better novel-class clustering does not come at the expense of known-class performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends NOPS (Riz et al., CVPR 2023), the only prior work on novel class discovery for 3D point cloud segmentation, by replacing its rigid equal-class-size OT constraint with a semi-relaxed OT formulation featuring an adaptive, data-dependent regularization on the class distribution. The balanced OT pseudo-labeling framework is inherited from Asano et al. (ICLR 2020) and Zhang et al. (TMLR 2023), while the novel-class-count estimation follows Vaze et al. (CVPR 2022). The key conceptual departure is the dual-level (point + region) representation that enforces spatial consistency, combined with the insight that a fixed regularization strength is suboptimal for imbalanced novel classes and must be annealed based on the model's learning progress.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical gap: real-world 3D scenes have inherently imbalanced class distributions, and the uniform-size assumption in prior NCD methods causes degenerate clustering of large or small novel classes. The finding that even the ground-truth class distribution as a hard constraint underperforms the adaptive soft KL regularization is a useful counterintuitive result for the pseudo-labeling community. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the setup assumes all unlabeled points belong to novel classes, whereas realistic open-world scenarios would mix unlabeled known and novel classes — a more challenging setting left for future work.
