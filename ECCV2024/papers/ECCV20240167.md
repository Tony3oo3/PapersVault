## Problem

Existing query-based online VIS methods rely heavily on positional cues for instance association, causing identity switches during shot changes, trajectory intersections, and occlusions. The paper identifies that appearance information—a key axis in traditional trackers—is underutilized in the query matching process, and that current benchmarks do not adequately stress-test appearance awareness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240167/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequential video frames processed one at a time (online, frame-by-frame) through a Mask2Former detector with a ResNet-50 backbone, initialized from COCO pre-trained weights. At each frame it produces per-instance segmentation masks with class labels, and across frames it assigns persistent tracklet IDs via a lightweight matching pipeline that requires no NMS or handcrafted tracklet thresholds. A memory bank of window size 5 stores prior object and appearance embeddings to provide limited temporal context beyond the immediately preceding frame.

## Method

VISAGE augments the standard Mask2Former query-based detector with a lightweight appearance branch that applies average (mask) pooling over backbone feature maps guided by predicted masks, producing appearance queries complementary to the existing object queries. Both query types are projected into separate embedding spaces and trained with individual contrastive losses to sharpen intra-instance consistency and inter-instance separability. At inference, matching between consecutive frames uses a weighted cosine similarity—(1−α)·cos_sim(object) + α·cos_sim(appearance) with α=0.75—solved via the Hungarian algorithm, replacing the multi-stage heuristic trackers (NMS, threshold-based tracklet pruning) used by prior methods. A simple memory bank aggregates temporally weighted, confidence-scaled embeddings from the last W=5 frames to compensate for the lack of long-range temporal information inherent in frame-level online processing. The authors further construct a synthetic pseudo dataset (track and swap types) to rigorously validate appearance-awareness, where VISAGE outperforms baselines by a large margin on swap-type videos.

## Results

VISAGE achieves state-of-the-art results as an online method across all three benchmarks. On YouTube-VIS 2021, it reaches 51.6 AP (73.8 AP50, 56.1 AP75), surpassing the previous best online method CTVIS (50.1 AP) by 1.5 points. On YouTube-VIS 2019, it ties CTVIS at 55.1 AP while improving AP75 from 59.1 to 60.6. On OVIS, it achieves 36.2 AP (60.3 AP50), outperforming CTVIS (35.5 AP) and TCOVIS (35.3 AP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VISAGE is compared against 9 baselines spanning both online and offline VIS methods. On YouTube-VIS 2021, VISAGE (51.6 AP) clearly outperforms the strongest prior online method CTVIS (50.1 AP) by 1.5 AP and TCOVIS (49.5 AP) by 2.1 AP, while also surpassing offline methods like DVIS-offline (47.4 AP) and GenVIS-offline (46.3 AP). On YouTube-VIS 2019, VISAGE ties CTVIS at 55.1 AP but improves AP75 from 59.1 to 60.6, indicating better localization quality. On OVIS, VISAGE (36.2 AP) edges out CTVIS (35.5 AP) and TCOVIS (35.3 AP), with a more notable gap in AP75 (35.3 vs. 34.9 and 36.6 respectively). The pseudo-dataset experiments further demonstrate VISAGE's advantage: on swap-type videos where location cues are unreliable, VISAGE with appearance guidance achieves 66.1 AP versus 53.1 AP for CTVIS and 41.8 AP for GenVIS, a gap of 13+ points that highlights the critical role of appearance information in disambiguating object identities.

## Relation to Prior Work

VISAGE builds directly on the Mask2Former query-based detector as its backbone and operates within the online query-matching paradigm established by MinVIS, IDOL, and CTVIS. It departs from these methods by introducing a parallel appearance-embedding branch (extracted via mask pooling from backbone features) that complements the standard object embeddings during Hungarian matching, and by applying contrastive loss independently to both embedding types. The tracker is deliberately simplified relative to CTVIS/IDOL, removing NMS and handcrafted tracklet thresholds in favor of a lightweight memory bank with temporally weighted embeddings. The paper also introduces a synthetic pseudo dataset (track and swap types) to stress-test appearance awareness, a dimension absent from standard VIS benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete failure mode in query-based online VIS—over-reliance on positional cues causing ID switches during occlusions, shot changes, and trajectory crossings—by showing that appearance embeddings, when properly weighted, substantially improve association robustness. The pseudo dataset is a practical contribution for evaluating appearance sensitivity in a controlled setting. However, the method remains a tracking-by-detection pipeline that inherits frame-level detector errors, and the authors note that query-propagation methods share the same spatial-bias limitation, leaving an open question about integrating appearance guidance into that paradigm. The heavy dependence on the α hyperparameter (catastrophic failure at α=1.0) also suggests the balance between appearance and location cues is dataset-sensitive and not yet fully resolved.
