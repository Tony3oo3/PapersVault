## Problem

Existing scale arbitrary super-resolution methods are trained and evaluated exclusively on simulated datasets using bicubic downsampling, causing poor generalization to real-world degradations that are spatially variant and content-dependent. Furthermore, all prior real-world SR datasets contain only integer scale factors (×2, ×3, ×4), leaving no benchmark for training or evaluating models at non-integer scales. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a real-world dataset with arbitrary scales and a method that explicitly models the image-level and pixel-level deformations introduced by real camera optics.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single low-resolution RGB image and an arbitrary scale factor (including non-integer values such as ×1.7, ×2.3, ×2.7, ×3.3, ×3.7) and produces a corresponding high-resolution RGB image. The LR image is encoded by a CNN (EDSR or RDN without upsampling) into a 2D latent feature map, and HR pixels are generated by querying 5-layer MLP decoders at continuous spatial coordinates using bilinear interpolation of nearby latent codes. The RealArbiSR dataset provides LR-HR pairs captured with a Canon 5D3 and 24–105mm zoom lens, with focal lengths calibrated via a checkerboard to achieve precise non-integer magnifications, and pairs are pixel-aligned via an image registration algorithm.

## Method

DDIR employs a dual-branch architecture built on local implicit neural representation. The SR branch encodes the LR image and decodes HR RGB at query coordinates via an MLP, while the deformation branch predicts a pixel-level deformation field defined as the RGB residual between the ground truth and the bicubic-upscaled LR input, capturing spatially variant degradation effects. To handle image-level photometric variations (exposure, white balance shifts across zoom levels), an appearance embedding—computed as the spatial average pooling of the SR encoder's 2D feature map—is concatenated with latent codes in the deformation branch, giving the model global context beyond the local receptive field. The predicted deformation field is then concatenated with SR-branch latent codes before the final MLP decoding, so the SR output is informed by both the global appearance and the local degradation residual. Training uses L1 losses for both branches (deformation supervision on the residual target, SR supervision on the ground truth), with 48×48 patches and 2304 randomly sampled query pixels per patch.

## Results

On the RealArbiSR dataset, DDIR with the RDN backbone achieves the best PSNR at all six training scale factors, reaching 37.63 dB (×1.5), 35.02 dB (×2.0), 33.20 dB (×2.5), 31.91 dB (×3.0), 30.94 dB (×3.5), and 30.21 dB (×4.0). On the RealSR dataset, RDN-DDIR scores 34.35 dB (×2.0), 31.15 dB (×3.0), and 29.48 dB (×4.0). The headline gain is 0.32 dB over the previous SOTA (CiaoSR) at ×2.0 with the RDN backbone on RealArbiSR. DDIR also leads at all five unseen non-integer scale factors (×1.7, ×2.3, ×2.7, ×3.3, ×3.7) in Table 3, e.g., 35.07 dB at ×1.7 with RDN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDIR outperforms all four competing methods (Bicubic, LIIF, LTE, CiaoSR) at every scale factor on both RealArbiSR and RealSR, under both EDSR and RDN backbones. The largest margin is 0.32 dB over CiaoSR at ×2.0 with the RDN backbone on RealArbiSR (35.02 vs. 34.70 dB), and gains of 0.15–0.25 dB are typical at other scales. Against the fixed-scale EDSR-baseline, DDIR improves by roughly 0.6 dB at ×2.0 and 0.58 dB at ×4.0 on RealArbiSR, confirming the benefit of the continuous implicit formulation. In the cross-dataset experiment (trained on RealArbiSR, tested on RealSR), DDIR again leads all baselines, e.g., 32.59 dB vs. 32.42 dB (CiaoSR) at ×2.0 with RDN. On the unseen non-integer scale factors (×1.7, ×2.3, ×2.7, ×3.3, ×3.7), DDIR achieves the best PSNR at every point, with the largest gap of 0.53 dB over CiaoSR at ×1.7 (RDN backbone). The ablation in Table 6 confirms both the appearance embedding (+0.27 dB at ×2.0) and the deformation field (+0.18 dB at ×2.0) contribute independently, with their combination yielding the full 0.53 dB gain over the no-deformation baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the local implicit image function (LIIF) framework for scale arbitrary SR by introducing a dual-level deformable representation that explicitly models the gap between synthetic (bicubic) and real-world degradations. It departs from prior scale arbitrary SR methods (LIIF, LTE, CiaoSR) that assume bicubic downsampling, and from real-world SR methods (LP-KPN, CDC) that are restricted to fixed integer scale factors, by unifying both axes: real-world degradation kernels and arbitrary (including non-integer) magnification. The key conceptual move is reframing real-world degradation as a two-level deformation—image-level photometric shift handled by a global appearance embedding, and pixel-level spatially variant kernel effect handled by a residual deformation field—rather than attempting to model the degradation kernel directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RealArbiSR dataset is the first real-world SR benchmark with non-integer scale factors, filling a practical gap for continuous optical zoom applications that prior integer-only benchmarks (RealSR, DRealSR) could not support. The finding that a simple spatial average pooling vector (appearance embedding) yields substantially larger gains in real-world than synthetic settings provides a clean diagnostic for image-level photometric deformation, though the overall PSNR improvements over CiaoSR are modest (~0.2–0.3 dB). Limitations include the small dataset (250 scenes, single camera), evaluation limited to PSNR without perceptual metrics, and the deformation field being supervised by a training-time-only target (GT minus bicubic-upscaled LR), which raises questions about how well the learned residual generalizes to unseen degradation distributions.
