## Problem

State-of-the-art noisy-label learning methods rely on sample selection curricula that are either fixed, hand-tuned, or learned from pre-defined basis functions, none of which account for the actual label noise rate present in the training set. This mismatch causes overfitting when too many noisy samples are treated as clean, or underfitting when informative clean samples are discarded. No prior method estimates the noise rate directly from the training data to dynamically guide the sample selection process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240101/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard noisy-label training set of RGB images with one-hot encoded (potentially corrupted) class labels, spanning resolutions from 32×32 (CIFAR-100, mini-ImageNet) to 256×256 (WebVision) and variable sizes (Clothing1M). Beyond the usual class prediction, the model outputs a scalar noise rate ε estimated per training epoch via a variational EM procedure. The estimated ε directly parameterises the sample selection curriculum R(t) = 1 − ε^(t), determining what fraction of the current mini-batch is treated as clean versus noisy at each step.

## Method

The paper introduces a probabilistic graphical model that factorises the noisy-label generation process into a clean-label classifier f_θy and a noisy-label classifier f_θŷ, coupled by a scalar noise rate ε that controls the mixture between the true label and a noise-corrupted prediction. Training proceeds via variational EM: the E-step approximates the posterior over latent clean labels, while the M-step jointly updates θy, θŷ, and ε by maximising the ELBO minus a sample-selection loss L(θy, ε^(t)). The key mechanism is that the dynamically estimated ε defines the selection curriculum R(t) = 1 − ε^(t), so the number of samples treated as clean at each epoch adapts to the data rather than following a fixed schedule. The approach integrates with existing SOTA methods (DivideMix, C2D, CC, InstanceGM, SSR, FINE) by using their classifier as f_θy and augmenting their training loop with the EM-based noise-rate estimation, requiring only a single additional scalar parameter and a warm-up phase.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with instance-dependent noise (IDN), integrating the proposed graphical model into DivideMix improves test accuracy from 58.61% to 64.02% at 0.5 IDN (+5.41%), and CC from 63.19% to 67.53% (+4.34%). On the real-world red mini-ImageNet at 0.8 noise rate, InstanceGM-Ours reaches 43.83% accuracy versus 39.62% for the original InstanceGM (+4.21%). On mini-WebVision, SSR-Ours achieves 81.68% Top-1 accuracy, surpassing the previous SOTA (BtR at 80.88%). The estimated noise rates closely track the true values (e.g., 0.53 estimated vs. 0.5 actual on CIFAR-100 at 0.5 IDN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms its base models across all noise rates on CIFAR-100 IDN: DivideMix gains +0.39 to +5.41%, C2D gains +0.41 to +2.09%, and CC gains +0.11 to +4.34%, with the largest improvements at the highest noise rate (0.5 IDN). InstanceGM-Ours slightly underperforms the original InstanceGM by 0.08% at 0.2 IDN but wins at 0.3, 0.4, and 0.5 IDN. On red mini-ImageNet, the improvements are more substantial: InstanceGM-Ours beats InstanceGM by +4.37, +3.44, and +4.21% at 0.4, 0.6, and 0.8 noise rates respectively, and even surpasses the self-supervised InstanceGM-SS baseline at 0.4 noise rate (56.61% vs. 56.37%). On Clothing1M, gains are modest (+0.07 to +0.13%) but consistent across all four integrated methods. On mini-WebVision, all four integrated methods (DivideMix, C2D, SSR, CC) achieve new SOTA Top-1 accuracy, with SSR-Ours reaching 81.68% and CC-Ours reaching 80.01%, both exceeding the previous best (BtR at 80.88% for SSR-Ours). The method also outperforms transition-matrix-based approaches (PartT, kMEIDTM) by large margins (e.g., 64.02% vs. 56.80% for PartT at 0.5 IDN), confirming the advantage of sample-selection-based strategies augmented with noise-rate estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own prior work InstanceGM [15] (WACV 2023), which introduced a graphical model for IDN noisy-label learning but did not model the underlying noise rate parameter. It departs from the broader sample-selection curriculum literature (Co-teaching, S2E, SSR, CC) where the selection rate R(t) is either hand-tuned, predefined as a function of epoch, or learned from a fixed set of basis functions—none of which ground the curriculum in a data-driven noise rate estimate. The key conceptual move is treating the noise rate ε as a learnable parameter within a variational EM framework and directly feeding it into the sample selection threshold, rather than relying on arbitrary clustering scores or fixed schedules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that simply fixing the correct noise rate in DivideMix's sample selection yields a 6% accuracy gain on CIFAR-100 at 0.5 IDN highlights that curriculum design is an underappreciated bottleneck in noisy-label learning, and the proposed plug-in offers a principled, low-cost way to address it across multiple SOTA methods. However, the actual improvements from the estimated (rather than ground-truth) noise rate are more modest (0.1–5%), and identifiability concerns remain when inferring clean labels from a single noisy annotation. Open questions include whether the approach benefits from different network architectures for clean vs. noisy label classifiers, how it behaves under IIN or mixed noise, and whether the modest gains on real-world datasets (Clothing1M, mini-WebVision) reflect a ceiling or simply the difficulty of those benchmarks.
