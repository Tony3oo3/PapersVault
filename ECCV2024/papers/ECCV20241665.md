## Problem

Registering photorealistic avatars to headset-mounted camera (HMC) images for VR facial animation is hindered by oblique viewing angles that obscure subtle lip and jaw movements, and by a large domain gap between monochrome infrared HMC images and RGB avatar renderings. Existing approaches either require identity-specific offline optimization taking hours to days, or use direct regression that fails to generalize across unseen identities. The paper demonstrates that the domain gap is a primary source of difficulty: a transformer-based refiner achieves near-offline accuracy on domain-consistent inputs but degrades sharply when the IR-to-RGB gap is re-introduced.

## Input / Output

The system takes multi-view monochrome infrared images (192×192) from a consumer VR headset (Meta Quest Pro), which provide a patchwork of non-overlapping oblique views of the upper and lower face, along with identity-specific multi-scale bias maps that parameterize the avatar decoder. It produces an expression code z ∈ R^256 and a 6-DoF head pose v ∈ R^6 in 0.4 seconds via feed-forward inference (no per-identity optimization), as well as a style-transferred RGB rendering R̂ that bridges the IR-to-avatar domain gap. The iterative refinement runs for T=3 steps, and the style transfer is conditioned on M=4 key reference expressions rendered from the avatar at the current pose estimate.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-module system that mutually reinforces: (1) a hybrid-transformer iterative refinement module F that encodes per-view alignment between HMC images, style-transferred images, and the current avatar rendering via a shared CNN, then applies a transformer over grid tokens from all camera views to predict residual corrections (Δz, Δv) added to the current estimate; and (2) a U-Net-based avatar-conditioned style transfer module S that transforms raw IR images into avatar-domain renderings, using sliding-window attention to fuse input and conditioning features, where conditioning images are rendered from the avatar at the current pose estimate plus M key reference expressions. The key insight is that the domain gap, not the oblique geometry alone, is the primary bottleneck: when domains match, the transformer refiner approaches offline optimization quality, and the two modules form a positive feedback loop where better registration yields better style-transfer conditioning and vice versa. Training is fully supervised with L1 image losses on rendered avatar comparisons, and the system generalizes to unseen identities without per-identity optimization.

## Results

On the 208-identity VR face dataset, the proposed method (F+S) with standard HMC cameras achieves a frontal image L1 error of 2.655, rotation error of 0.947°, and translation error of 0.886 mm in 0.4 s per frame. With augmented cameras, these improve to 2.399, 0.917°, and 0.845 mm respectively. The offline optimization baseline [30] achieves a lower frontal L1 of 1.713 but requires ~1 day per identity, while the direct regression baseline (MobileNetV3) runs in 7 ms but yields substantially worse errors (2.956 frontal L1, 2.850° rotation, 2.802 mm translation).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the offline optimization baseline [30] (which requires ~1 day per identity and an augmented camera rig), the proposed method trades a modest increase in frontal image L1 (2.655 vs. 1.713) for a ~2500× speedup (0.4 s vs. ~1 day), while achieving dramatically better head pose accuracy (0.947° rotation vs. 2.400°, 0.886 mm translation vs. 2.512 mm). The offline method's co-optimized style transfer can mask small pose errors in oblique views, inflating its pose metrics. Against the direct regression baseline (MobileNetV3, 7 ms), the proposed method wins decisively on all three metrics: frontal L1 (2.655 vs. 2.956), rotation error (0.947° vs. 2.850°), and translation error (0.886 mm vs. 2.802 mm). Notably, the regression baseline fails to improve even when given augmented cameras or domain-matched R_gt inputs, whereas the proposed method benefits significantly from both conditions (frontal L1 drops from 2.655 to 2.399 with augmented cameras). The regression baseline's poor head pose estimation is attributed to the lack of avatar conditioning, which is necessary to resolve the identity-dependent 3D transformation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VR face tracking line initiated by Schwartz et al. [30] and its predecessor [33], which achieved high-accuracy registration of photorealistic avatars to headset-mounted camera images but required person-specific offline optimization (hours to days) and augmented camera rigs. The key departure is making registration generic across identities and fast (feed-forward, ~0.4 s) by decoupling the problem into an avatar-conditioned style transfer module and a transformer-based iterative refinement module that mutually reinforce each other. The iterative refinement design draws on cascade regression architectures (e.g., Cascade Pose Regression [12]) but applies them to neural-rendering avatar models rather than parametric 3DMMs, and the style transfer is conditioned on the full personalized avatar decoder rather than a fixed domain pair as in CycleGAN-style approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical path toward real-time, high-fidelity VR face tracking on consumer headsets without the costly per-user offline optimization that prior methods required, and the publicly released 208-identity dataset (1M frames) with ground-truth correspondences is a valuable resource for the community. A key open question is whether the mutual-reinforcement loop generalizes beyond the specific avatar model [7] and headset configuration used here, and whether the approach can extend to body tracking or non-VR multi-view registration as the authors suggest. Limitations include the need for a pre-existing identity-specific avatar (from phone scan or lightstage) and known camera calibrations, which constrains deployment to users who have already undergone avatar creation.
