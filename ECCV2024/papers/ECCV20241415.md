## Problem

Standard fine-tuning of pre-trained models distorts the rich feature representations acquired during pre-training, and this distortion is counterintuitively worse for more complex downstream tasks. The paper identifies that the potential generalization benefit of preserving pre-trained features has been overlooked because conventional fine-tuning only pursues target-task accuracy, effectively turning the target task into 'the bed of Procrustes' and cutting off the broader utility of pre-trained features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241415/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pre-trained model (ResNet-18/50 or Swin-Transformer-Tiny) with a feature extractor and a classification head, taking RGB images of varying resolutions (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet) as input and producing class labels. Beyond standard classification accuracy, the evaluation framework measures feature preservation via linear-probing accuracy on the pre-training dataset and generalization via accuracy on held-out or domain-shifted test sets (CIFAR-10.1, STL-10, unseen CIFAR-100 subclasses), making the 'output' a multi-faceted assessment of both task performance and feature retention.

## Method

The paper proposes three plug-and-play fine-tuning strategies that create partial 'idling' of neurons to minimize feature distortion: (1) Linear Probing First (LPF) freezes the feature extractor and trains only the classification head for initial epochs before unfreezing everything, reducing the optimization pressure on pre-trained features; (2) Temporarily Neuron Inactivating (TNI) zeros out gradients closest to the per-layer mean at each update step (using a threshold δσ), so only neurons genuinely needing adjustment are modified; (3) Fine-tuning with an Assistant (AST) replicates the pre-trained model with frozen parameters, connects both feature extractors in parallel to a shared predictor, and trains them alternately, forcing the predictor to leverage pre-trained features directly. The key insight driving all three strategies is that feature forgetting correlates with the amount of new task-specific features to learn (the 'distance' to the target task), so reducing the workload on the feature extractor preserves more of the pre-trained knowledge base and improves out-of-distribution generalization without sacrificing target-task accuracy.

## Results

On CUB-200-2011 fine-grained classification, LPF achieves 79.63% accuracy at 100% sampling rate, outperforming standard fine-tuning (78.01%), L2SP (78.44%), DELTA (78.63%), and BSS (78.85%), though CoTuning remains the strongest baseline at 81.24%. For generalization, the AST+TNI combination improves CIFAR-10 accuracy from 51.85% to 60.04% and STL-10 from 44.28% to 48.62% when pre-training on CIFAR-10 and fine-tuning on CIFAR-100. On feature preservation (Table 1), AST+LPF retains 59.30% accuracy on the pre-training set versus 49.58% for standard fine-tuning, and achieves 51.62% on an unseen CIFAR-100 subset versus 43.78% baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CUB-200-2011, LPF outperforms standard fine-tuning (FT) by 1.62 points at 100% sampling (79.63% vs. 78.01%), and beats L2SP, DELTA, and BSS at 30% and 100% sampling rates. TNI similarly surpasses FT, L2SP, DELTA, and BSS across all four sampling rates, with the largest margin over FT at 15% sampling (46.38% vs. 45.25%). However, CoTuning [36] remains the strongest method across all sampling rates, achieving 81.24% at 100% sampling versus LPF's 79.63% and TNI's 79.25%. AST alone is the weakest of the three proposed strategies, underperforming FT at 30% and 100% sampling. The proposed strategies are positioned as plug-and-play alternatives that preserve pre-trained features and improve generalization rather than maximizing in-distribution accuracy, which explains why CoTuning (designed specifically for downstream accuracy) still leads on the target task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Kumar et al. [17] (ICLR 2022), which demonstrated that standard fine-tuning distorts pretrained features and degrades out-of-distribution performance, and whose linear probing protocol is adopted as the LPF strategy here. It departs from the broader parameter-efficient tuning (PET) literature by shifting the motivation from computational efficiency to generalization: rather than tuning fewer parameters to save cost, the proposed strategies (LPF, TNI, AST) temporarily idle a subset of neurons to preserve the rich feature space acquired during pre-training. The paper also positions itself against catastrophic-forgetting methods in continual learning, noting that transfer learning only requires target-task performance rather than equal retention of all previously learned tasks, making the setting less constrained.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The counterintuitive finding that more complex downstream tasks cause greater feature distortion (not less) reframes how practitioners think about fine-tuning: the goal is not merely to match domain similarity but to minimize the optimization distance the model must travel. The plug-and-play strategies offer a low-cost way to improve generalization without sacrificing target-task accuracy, which is practically appealing. However, the generalization evidence is limited to relatively small domain shifts (CIFAR-10 → CIFAR-10.1, STL-10), and the paper itself acknowledges that the scope of the feature-retention-to-generalization correlation, its behavior under domain adaptation or domain generalization, and the optimal combination of strategies remain open questions.
