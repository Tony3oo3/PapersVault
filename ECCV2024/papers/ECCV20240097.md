## Problem

Existing human-object interaction datasets and synthesis models are almost exclusively limited to single-object interactions, neglecting the ubiquitous manipulation of multiple objects in daily life. No prior dataset provides full-body 4D HOI sequences with multiple objects, fine-grained textual descriptions, and temporal segmentation labels, leaving text-driven multi-object HOI synthesis and timeline-controlled composition unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240097/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (single description for HIMO-Gen, or a sequence of atomic prompts for HIMO-SegGen), the initial SMPL-X pose of the human, initial 6-DoF poses of 2–3 objects, and object geometries encoded as 1,024-point Basis Point Sets. It outputs a synchronized 4D HOI sequence: SMPL-X parameters (52 joint positions, 52 joint rotations in 6D continuous format, and root translation) plus per-object relative rotation and global translation per frame. Sequences are capped at 300 frames (HIMO-Gen) or 100 frames (HIMO-SegGen), with text lengths of 40 and 15 tokens respectively; object order is randomly shuffled during training to prevent positional bias.

## Method

The core architecture is a dual-branch conditional diffusion model (built on the MDM framework) that denoises human motion and object motions in parallel, with a mutual interaction module of 8 Transformer blocks (4 heads) where each branch's cross-attention uses the other branch's hidden states as keys and values, enabling bidirectional feature fusion without weight sharing. A novel object-pairwise L2 loss constrains the relative distance between object geometries to follow physically plausible patterns (e.g., a knife approaching and resting on an apple). For HIMO-SegGen, an auto-regressive pipeline generates one HOI clip at a time, conditioning each new clip on the last 10 frames of the previously generated clip to ensure smooth transitions between temporally segmented actions. Text is encoded via CLIP, object geometry via BPS, and rotations use the continuous 6D representation to avoid discontinuities.

## Results

On the 2-objects partition of HIMO, HIMO-Gen achieves an FID of 1.48 and MM-Dist of 3.65, substantially outperforming all baselines (best baseline FID: 6.85 for MDM; best MM-Dist: 7.25 for priorMDM). HIMO-Gen also leads in R-Precision (0.637) and MultiModality (1.79). On the 3-objects partition, HIMO-Gen attains the best R-Precision (0.535) and MM-Dist (5.09), though MDM edges it slightly on FID (4.57 vs. 4.77). HIMO-SegGen further improves MM-Dist to 3.61 and MultiModality to 2.05 on the 2-objects split, demonstrating the benefit of fine-grained temporal text conditioning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HIMO-Gen dominates all three baselines on the 2-objects setting across every metric: it reduces FID from 6.85 (MDM) and 7.59 (IMoS) to 1.48, and cuts MM-Dist from 8.02 (MDM) and 8.74 (IMoS) to 3.65, approaching the ground-truth value of 3.57. On the 3-objects setting, HIMO-Gen still leads in R-Precision (0.535 vs. 0.503 for MDM and 0.466 for IMoS) and MM-Dist (5.09 vs. 6.31 for MDM and 7.77 for IMoS), but MDM achieves a marginally lower FID (4.57 vs. 4.77) and priorMDM scores higher on Diversity (9.34 vs. 8.95) and MModality (0.81 vs. 0.76). The ablation further confirms that the mutual interaction module is critical: removing it drops R-Precision from 0.637 to 0.471 and FID from 1.48 to 3.51, while the object-pairwise loss contributes a ~1.4-point FID improvement. Overall, the dual-branch diffusion architecture with mutual attention provides a clear and consistent advantage over single-branch or intent-driven baselines for multi-object HOI synthesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-driven HOI synthesis line (InterDiff, IMoS, HOI-Diff) from single-object to multi-object settings, and introduces temporal segmentation annotations to 4D HOIs for the first time. The dual-branch diffusion architecture with mutual interaction module draws from human-human interaction generation (InterGen, ReGenNet) and the MDM diffusion framework, while the auto-regressive generation pipeline for HIMO-SegGen adapts ideas from temporal action composition (TEACH, MultiAct, Perpetual Motion). The data acquisition pipeline builds on GRAB's optical MoCap approach but adds inertial gloves for finger capture and reflective-marker-based object tracking, filling a clear gap left by all prior full-body HOI datasets (GRAB, ARCTIC, CHAIRS, BEHAVE) which are restricted to single objects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HIMO is the first large-scale benchmark enabling text-driven synthesis of full-body human interactions with multiple objects, a setting that is ubiquitous in daily life but entirely absent from prior datasets. The temporal segmentation annotations open a new axis of fine-grained timeline control for HOI generation, allowing compositional synthesis of novel interaction sequences. Key limitations include the restriction to small household objects (no chairs, suitcases), the lack of natural RGB appearance due to MoCap suits and markers, and the absence of facial expressions. Open questions include whether the mutual interaction module and object-pairwise loss generalize to larger, articulated, or deformable objects, and whether the auto-regressive 10-frame conditioning strategy scales to longer multi-step compositions.
