## Problem

Existing all-in-one image restoration models rely on learned prompt embeddings or image-based degradation classifiers to guide a single network across multiple tasks, but none leverage the natural language instructions that users would actually provide. This paper asks whether free-form, human-written text instructions can serve as a more intuitive and expressive control mechanism for blind image restoration, replacing fixed degradation-specific prompts or learned guidance vectors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240942/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a degraded RGB image (256×256 patches during training) paired with a free-form English text instruction ranging from vague ('fix my image') to precise ('remove the noise from my picture'), and outputs a single restored high-quality image. At inference, the same model handles 5–7 degradation types (denoising, deblurring, dehazing, deraining, low-light enhancement, super-resolution, photo enhancement) selected purely by the instruction text, with no explicit degradation label or parameter required. Training prompts are GPT-4-generated (>10,000 samples), while evaluation includes real human-written instructions from amateur photographers.

## Method

InstructIR combines a frozen BGE-micro-v2 sentence encoder (17.3M params) with a NAFNet U-Net backbone (16M params), linked by a learned linear projection that maps 384-dim text embeddings to 256-dim vectors. The key architectural contribution is the Instruction Condition Block (ICB), which applies soft task routing: a per-channel sigmoid mask m_c = σ(W_c · e) is computed from the text embedding and element-wise multiplied with image features at each encoder/decoder level, followed by a residual NAFBlock, enabling the network to selectively activate channels relevant to the requested degradation. Training is end-to-end supervised with L1 reconstruction loss plus a cross-entropy intent-classification loss on the text embedding (a two-layer MLP head), which regularizes the projection to produce semantically meaningful clusters. Only ~100K parameters (the projection and classification head) are trained alongside the image backbone, keeping the model lightweight and trainable on a single GPU in about one day.

## Results

On the five-degradation (5D) benchmark, InstructIR-5D achieves an average PSNR of 29.55 dB and SSIM of 0.907, surpassing the previous best all-in-one method IDR (28.34 dB / 0.893) by +1.21 dB. The model uses only 15.8M parameters (16M image model + 100K text projection), comparable to NAFNet (17.1M) yet significantly outperforming it. In the three-degradation (3D) setting, InstructIR-3D reaches 32.43 dB / 0.913 average, edging out PromptIR (32.06 dB / 0.913). The 7D variant additionally handles super-resolution and photo enhancement, achieving 24.65 dB / 0.900 on MIT5K while maintaining competitive restoration scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InstructIR-5D outperforms the strongest prior all-in-one method IDR [89] by +1.21 dB average PSNR (29.55 vs. 28.34) and +0.014 SSIM, with the largest gains on deblurring (+1.53 dB) and low-light enhancement (+1.66 dB). Against its own backbone NAFNet [7] without text conditioning, the instruction guidance adds +1.79 dB, confirming the value of the Instruction Condition Block. In the 3D setting, InstructIR-3D (32.43 dB) beats PromptIR [54] (32.06 dB) and AirNet [36] (31.20 dB), though the margin over PromptIR is modest (+0.37 dB). On task-specific comparisons, InstructIR-7D (24.65 dB) trails the best photo-enhancement specialist 3DLUT [86] (25.21 dB) by 0.56 dB on MIT5K, and InstructIR (22.81 dB) is below IAGC [72] (24.53 dB) and MIRNet [84] (24.14 dB) on LOL low-light — expected gaps since InstructIR is a single multi-task model while those are dedicated single-task networks. Overall, InstructIR achieves the best all-in-one results across all five restoration tasks simultaneously with a compact 15.8M-parameter model.

## Relation to Prior Work

InstructIR sits at the intersection of two research threads: all-in-one image restoration (AirNet, IDR, PromptIR) and instruction-based image editing (InstructPix2Pix). Prior all-in-one methods guide a shared restoration network via learned prompt embeddings or auxiliary degradation classifiers, while InstructPix2Pix uses text instructions but targets generative editing rather than inverse problems. This paper bridges the gap by repurposing the task-routing mechanism of Strezoski et al. [63]—originally designed for binary task masks—into a soft, text-conditioned Instruction Condition Block, and by substituting CLIP with a compact sentence transformer (BGE-micro-v2) better suited to encoding degradation descriptions that carry little visual content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that free-form human instructions can replace learned guidance vectors for multi-task restoration, the paper opens a more natural and controllable interface for low-level vision, enabling users to chain instructions (e.g., derain then enhance) for fine-grained control. The 10K+ prompt dataset and evaluation protocol establish a new benchmark for text-guided restoration. Key limitations remain: the model struggles with compound degradations and ambiguous prompts, and the instruction sensitivity ablation (Table 4) shows a ~1 dB drop for vague instructions, suggesting that robustness to truly open-ended user language is still an open problem. Scaling to more realistic, multi-degradation scenes and larger model capacities are natural next steps.
