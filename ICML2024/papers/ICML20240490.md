## Problem

Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) are typically validated only on finite sets of sampled points, providing no guarantee that the learned solution satisfies the PDE across the entire continuous spatio-temporal domain. The paper demonstrates this is dangerously misleading: a Diffusion-Sorption PINN shows a maximum residual error of 1.1×10⁻³ with 10⁴ Monte Carlo samples but 21.09 with 10⁶ samples, revealing the network has failed to learn a valid continuous solution. The paper addresses this gap by establishing formal correctness conditions for PINNs and introducing a scalable post-training verification framework that certifies error bounds over the full domain.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained fully-connected PINN (with tanh activations), the symbolic PDE residual function (a nonlinear polynomial in the network's partial derivatives up to order R), and a box-constrained spatio-temporal domain C = [0,T]×D. It produces certified upper bounds on three quantities: the initial-condition error, the boundary-condition error, and the PDE residual error, each guaranteed to hold for every point in the continuous domain. The bounds are computed as linear functions of the input x via recursive back-substitution, then optimized in closed-form over the box constraint, with greedy input branching iteratively tightening the global bound by splitting sub-domains where the gap between empirical and certified values is largest.

## Method

∂-CROWN extends the CROWN/α-CROWN bound-propagation framework to handle partial derivatives of neural networks. For first-order derivatives, it derives a recursive expression (Lemma 1) and bounds each layer's contribution using a hybrid scheme: backpropagate to express ∂z^(k-1)z^(k) as a function of the pre-activation y^(k), then forward-substitute the pre-computed CROWN linear bounds on y^(k) rather than fully backpropagating, reducing complexity from O(L²) to O(L). Second-order derivatives follow an analogous recursion (Lemma 2) requiring relaxation of σ''(y^(k)). The nonlinear PDE residual f_θ is then bounded using McCormick envelopes over the product terms of the bounded derivatives, yielding linear upper/lower bound functions f_θ^U and f_θ^L in x. Finally, greedy input branching (Algorithm 1) adaptively splits the domain at N_b iterations, always choosing the sub-domain with the largest gap between Monte Carlo empirical estimates and certified bounds, to progressively tighten the global certificate.

## Results

Across four PDEs (Burgers', Schrödinger's, Allen-Cahn, Diffusion-Sorption), ∂-CROWN certified upper bounds closely track high-density Monte Carlo estimates, confirming tightness. For Burgers' equation, the certified residual bound is 1.03×10⁻¹ versus a 10⁶-sample MC estimate of 1.80×10⁻²; for Diffusion-Sorption, the certified bound of 21.34 matches the 10⁶ MC estimate of 21.09, while 10⁴ MC samples misleadingly report only 1.10×10⁻³. In the head-to-head efficiency comparison on Burgers' equation (Table 2), ∂-CROWN achieves a residual upper bound of 1.30×10¹, outperforming IBP (2.78×10³) by a factor of ~214× and LiRPA (1.78×10²) by ~14× in tightness, while using 1.3×10⁵ branches (vs. IBP's 5×10⁶ and LiRPA's 1.9×10⁴).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ∂-CROWN against two bound-propagation baselines: IBP (Gowal et al., 2018) and LiRPA (Xu et al., 2020a), evaluated on Burgers' equation under fixed runtime budgets. For initial and boundary condition bounds, ∂-CROWN and LiRPA produce nearly identical results (both reduce to standard CROWN in these cases), while IBP is 3–4 orders of magnitude looser (e.g., 4.12×10⁻³ vs. 2.63×10⁻⁶ for the initial condition). The critical comparison is on the residual bound: ∂-CROWN achieves 1.30×10¹, beating IBP's 2.78×10³ by a factor of ~214× and LiRPA's 1.78×10² by ~14×. In terms of branching efficiency, ∂-CROWN uses 1.3×10⁵ branches—far fewer than IBP's 5×10⁶ but more than LiRPA's 1.9×10⁴—reflecting a deliberate trade-off where the hybrid backpropagation/forward-substitution scheme (inspired by Shi et al., 2020) yields tighter per-branch bounds at O(L) cost rather than LiRPA's O(L²) full backpropagation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the CROWN/α-CROWN bound-propagation framework (Zhang et al., 2018; Xu et al., 2020b) from image classifier robustness verification to the novel setting of certifying PINN residuals over continuous spatio-temporal domains. The key technical extension is ∂-CROWN, which derives linear bounds on partial derivatives of the network using McCormick envelopes and a hybrid backpropagation/forward-substitution scheme (inspired by Shi et al., 2020) achieving O(L) complexity instead of the O(L²) of full backward propagation (LiRPA). Conceptually, the paper departs from prior PINN work that validates solutions only via point-wise empirical comparisons against numerical solvers, and from prior verification work that certifies local ℓp-ball robustness rather than global domain correctness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework providing certified (not merely empirical) error bounds for PINNs across their entire continuous applicability domain, addressing a critical gap for safety-sensitive deployment. The striking Diffusion-Sorption result—where 10⁴ Monte Carlo samples estimate a residual of 1.1×10⁻³ but 10⁶ samples reveal 21.09—demonstrates that standard empirical validation is fundamentally unreliable, motivating the need for formal certification. Key limitations include significant runtime (up to ~2.4×10⁶ s for residual verification), bound looseness for higher-order PDEs and higher-dimensional domains, and the absence of a formal guarantee connecting residual bounds to solution error. Open questions include whether tighter relaxations or smarter branching can scale the approach to the Navier-Stokes or Gray-Scott systems, and whether PIAT-style adversarial training can systematically reduce certified errors.
