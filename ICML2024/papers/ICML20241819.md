## Problem

Instruction-tuned LLMs are often poorly calibrated, yet existing calibration methods either require labeled data from the test task (as in temperature scaling), incur multiple forward passes at inference (MC dropout, prompt augmentation), or are prohibitively expensive for LLMs. The paper addresses the gap of calibrating LLMs on new, unlabeled tasks without retraining the LLM, while preserving accuracy and remaining computationally efficient.

## Input / Output

At test time, THERMOMETER requires only N* unlabeled prompts (questions without answers) from the new task; it computes the LLM's hidden features φ(x_n) for each prompt, passes them through a small MLP recognition network, and averages the outputs to obtain a single scalar temperature τ*. This temperature is then applied to the LLM's logits via standard temperature scaling to produce calibrated token probabilities. The method is designed so that the recognition network conditions exclusively on prompt features and never on completions, making it applicable to any task where only inputs are available.

## Method

THERMOMETER introduces a probabilistic model with task-specific latent temperatures τ_k and a shared recognition network ψ_θ (an MLP) that maps LLM hidden features to temperature predictions. Training uses variational inference: the variational posterior q(τ_k; θ) is parameterized as a product of Gaussians with fixed variance ε, which collapses to a point mass at the mean of ψ_θ(φ(x_n)) across the task's samples when ε is small. The training objective is an approximate ELBO that maximizes the likelihood of completions under the predicted temperature minus a Gamma prior penalty (mode at 1, encouraging temperatures near 1), optimized jointly across K labeled tasks. At inference, the temperature for an unseen task is simply the average of ψ_θ(φ(x_n)) over N* unlabeled prompts, requiring only a single forward pass through the LLM plus a negligible MLP evaluation (~0.5% overhead). Because the method builds on temperature scaling, it provably preserves greedy-decoded predictions and thus accuracy.

## Results

On MMLU (57 tasks) with LLaMA-2-Chat 7B, THERMOMETER achieves an average ECE of 0.078, reducing calibration error by 70% over the uncalibrated model (0.260) and approaching the temperature-scaling lower bound (0.062) that uses labeled test data. On BIG-bench (23 tasks), THERMOMETER reaches ECE 0.090 versus 0.233 for the vanilla model. On MRQA free-form QA (6 out-of-domain datasets), THERMOMETER achieves ECE 0.065 and NLL 0.551, outperforming all baselines except the labeled-data TS lower bound (ECE 0.029). THERMOMETER also transfers across model scales: temperatures predicted from LLaMA-2-Chat 7B improve calibration of LLaMA-2-Chat 70B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

THERMOMETER (ECE 0.078) substantially outperforms all unlabeled-data baselines on MMLU: MC-Augment (0.242), Elicitation (0.315), Elicitation-Ensemble (0.202), CAPE (0.196), and TS-CV (0.119). The closest competitor, TS-CV, which tunes a single global temperature on K−1 tasks, is beaten by 35% in ECE. Against the temperature-scaling lower bound (Guo et al., 2017; ECE 0.062), THERMOMETER is within 0.016 ECE despite using no labeled test-task data. On the harder BIG-bench benchmark, THERMOMETER (ECE 0.090) again leads all baselines, with TS-CV (0.087) being the nearest competitor. On free-form MRQA, THERMOMETER (ECE 0.065) edges out CAPE (0.067) and TS-CV (0.071), while MC-Augment degrades severely (0.335). MC-Dropout (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016), evaluated on FLAN-T5-XL, achieves ECE 0.107 versus THERMOMETER's 0.080, confirming that multi-forward-pass methods are both slower and less effective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends temperature scaling (Guo et al., 2017) from a single-task, labeled-data setting to a multi-task, unlabeled-data setting by learning a recognition network (in the Helmholtz machine tradition of Dayan et al., 1995) that predicts task-specific temperatures from unlabeled prompts. The variational parameterization draws on evidence accumulation (Bouchacourt et al., 2018), where per-sample Gaussian predictions are multiplied to yield a dataset-level posterior whose variance shrinks with sample size. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior multi-domain calibration work (Yu et al., 2022) by jointly learning a non-linear mapping across tasks rather than fitting a post-hoc linear regression, and from per-sample temperature approaches (Joy et al., 2023) by showing that dataset-level aggregation is more effective for LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: LLMs are routinely deployed on new tasks where no labeled calibration data exists, and this paper provides a principled, computationally cheap (~0.5% inference overhead) solution that preserves accuracy. The finding that a thermometer trained on a 7B model effectively calibrates a 70B model from the same family could dramatically reduce the cost of calibrating frontier-scale LLMs. Open questions include whether the approach extends to genuinely open-ended generation (summarization, translation) beyond the yes/no self-checking reformulation, whether it transfers across model families (not just scales within a family), and whether the confidence-calibration-only framing is sufficient for downstream decision-making that requires full probability distributions.
