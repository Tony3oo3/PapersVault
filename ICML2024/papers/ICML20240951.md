## Problem

Standard off-policy evaluation methods (reweighting, imputation, doubly-robust) all require overlap between the behavior and evaluation policies; without overlap, reweighting weights diverge and imputation models are only valid under strong well-specification assumptions. Existing no-overlap approaches either rely on a well-specified outcome model or optimize needlessly conservative bounds. This paper asks whether sharp (tight) partial identification bounds on the off-policy value can be obtained under only non-parametric smoothness assumptions on the conditional mean, without any model specification.

## Input / Output

The method takes logged data (X_i, A_i, Y_i) under a known behavior policy π_b, an evaluation policy π_e, a Lipschitz constant L, a metric d on the covariate space, and a fitted conditional mean μ̂ (estimated only on the overlap region). It outputs a partial identification interval [ψ̂⁻, ψ̂⁺] for the off-policy value, decomposed into an identifiable overlap contribution ψ̂₁ (via IPW) and bounded no-overlap contributions ψ̂₂⁻ and ψ̂₂⁺. The closed-form solution for the no-overlap bounds requires only O(n²) pairwise distance computations, and the multi-action case is handled by reducing to binary sub-problems per action.

## Method

The key insight is a 'no-interaction property': under L-Lipschitz constraints with a metric, the triangle inequality ensures that points in the no-overlap region never place tighter bounds on each other than those coming from the overlap region, so the linear program bounding the no-overlap contribution has a closed-form solution where each no-overlap point is set to the tightest bound from the nearest overlap point. The paper constructs a pair of LPs (upper and lower bounds) for the no-overlap contribution, proves the closed-form solution via the no-interaction property, and establishes consistency under mild assumptions (feasibility of μ̂ in the overlap region, consistency of μ̂, and no 'holes' in the covariate distribution). Rate analysis via a LeCam two-point argument shows the estimator achieves the minimax-optimal rate n^{-2/(d+2)} up to log factors, matching the rate for pointwise estimation of an L-Lipschitz function. The framework also composes naturally with boundedness assumptions (yielding Manski bounds as a special case) and can be approximated efficiently via nearest-neighbor search for large datasets.

## Results

On the Yeast dataset, the proposed method produces partial identification intervals that are 40–50% narrower than those of the Ben-Michael et al. (2021) baseline across all sample sizes (ratio 1.43–1.58). On the Yahoo! Front Page Today dataset, the proposed Lipschitz-based intervals are 73.5%, 79.1%, 83.7%, and 91.5% narrower than pure Manski bounds for T = 0.25, 0.3, 0.35, and 0.4 respectively (at L = 1). Coverage of the partial identification intervals approaches 100% as n grows for plausible Lipschitz constants (L ≥ 3), confirming the validity guarantee of Theorem 4.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Ben-Michael et al. (2021) baseline, the proposed method consistently produces narrower intervals by 40–50% across all sample sizes on the Yeast dataset (e.g., 4.37 vs. 6.57 at n=500; 1.68 vs. 2.41 at n=3000, both ×10³). The gap is largest at smaller sample sizes (ratio ≈ 1.58 at n=1000) and narrows slightly as n grows (ratio ≈ 1.43 at n=3000), but the proposed method wins at every setting. Against pure Manski bounds (L = ∞), the proposed Lipschitz-based intervals are dramatically tighter on the Yahoo! Front Page Today dataset: 73.5% narrower at T=0.25 and 91.5% narrower at T=0.4 (L=1), with the advantage shrinking as T approaches the overlap boundary at 0.5 where no partial identification is needed. The paper also notes that for L=1,2 on Yeast, the Lipschitz assumption is likely violated (Assumption 4.2 fails at large n), whereas L=4,5 produce coverage nearly matching the always-valid Manski bounds, suggesting those smoothness levels are plausible for the data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Manski (1990) boundedness framework for partial identification to Lipschitz smoothness assumptions in the no-overlap off-policy evaluation setting. It departs from prior no-overlap OPE work that either requires a well-specified outcome model (Mou et al. 2023, Jiang and Huang 2020) or optimizes potentially loose lower bounds (Ben-Michael et al. 2021, Higbee 2022), by providing sharp, minimax-optimal bounds under non-parametric smoothness. The key technical departure is the 'no-interaction property'—a structural result showing that the linear program bounding the no-overlap contribution has a closed-form solution because the triangle inequality makes constraints between no-overlap points redundant, a property that holds for Lipschitz but not Hölder or monotonicity assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first sharp partial identification bounds for OPE under smoothness (rather than just boundedness), with a computationally efficient O(n²) closed-form estimator and minimax optimality up to log factors, making no-overlap policy evaluation practical without strong model assumptions. Key limitations include slow convergence in high dimensions (rate n^{-2/(d+2)}), the need for a plausible Lipschitz constant L chosen via domain knowledge, and the fact that the no-interaction property fails for richer assumption classes like monotonicity or combined smoothness-plus-monotonicity, requiring numerical LP solving. Open questions include developing finite-sample confidence intervals under smoothness (beyond the asymptotic coverage guarantee), handling near-zero propensity scores, and extending to action-covariate-space smoothness.
