## Problem

Diffusion models require hundreds to thousands of denoising steps for a single sample, making inference prohibitively slow. Existing acceleration methods either apply handcrafted skipping rules (learning-free) or decouple training and inference schedules (learning-based), both of which risk discarding informative steps. No prior approach identifies and removes redundant steps *during* training in a principled, learned manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242498/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For image generation, the model takes standard Gaussian noise and produces 32×32 CIFAR-10 images using a U-Net backbone with T=1000 diffusion steps. For time series imputation, it takes partially observed multivariate time series (e.g., 35 ICU variables over 48 hours, or 36 air-quality stations over 36 timesteps) and outputs probabilistic imputations of missing entries via a CSDI architecture with T=50 steps. The learned mask vector m ∈ {0,1}^T determines which subset of the original T steps is actually executed at inference, reducing the effective step count to as few as 5 (images) or 10% of T (time series).

## Method

EDDPM assigns a binary mask m_t to each diffusion step t, where m_t=0 zeros out the injected noise variance β_t, effectively skipping that step. The discrete mask is continualized via probabilistic reparameterization: each m_t is drawn from a Bernoulli distribution with probability s_t, and the joint objective minimizes the DDPM denoising loss over sampled masks subject to an L1 sparsity constraint ‖s‖₁ ≤ K. Gradients with respect to model weights θ use standard backpropagation, while gradients with respect to s use a policy-gradient estimator. A gradually increasing masking rate (annealed from 1 to γ_f over training epochs) stabilizes optimization, and the simplex geometry of the constraint drives most s_t values to converge to 0 or 1, yielding a near-deterministic sparse mask that can be safely fixed at inference. This eliminates the need for handcrafted skipping rules and also accelerates late-stage training, since the model automatically focuses capacity on the most informative steps.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 image generation, EDDPM achieves a FID of 4.89 with only 5 denoising steps and 4.34 with 10 steps, substantially outperforming all learning-based and learning-free baselines at these low step counts (e.g., DPM-solver: 4.70 at 10 steps; GENIE: 5.28 at 10 steps; CTw/TDAS: 6.58 at 10 steps). At 1000 steps, EDDPM reaches 3.03 FID, matching DDPM (3.04) while using a learned sparse schedule. On time series imputation, EDDPM surpasses the full-step DDPM baseline (0.549 RMSE on Healthcare 10% missing) using only 50% of the denoising steps (0.505 RMSE), and achieves 18.242 RMSE on Air-quality with 40% steps versus the 19.212 baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDDPM decisively outperforms all baselines at 5 and 10 denoising steps on CIFAR-10: at 5 steps it achieves 4.89 FID versus 7.12 (CTw/TDAS), 11.2 (GENIE), and 13.77 (GGDM); at 10 steps it reaches 4.34 versus 4.70 (DPM-solver), 5.28 (GENIE), 6.58 (CTw/TDAS), and 7.03 (F-PNDM). At 25 steps, EDDPM (3.59) edges out GENIE (3.64) and GGDM (4.25), and at 100 steps it achieves 3.21, beating SN-DDIM (3.23) and CTw/TDAS (4.13). The only method that surpasses EDDPM is CTw/TDAS at 1000 steps (2.57 vs. 3.03), but the authors note this uses a different base model (NCSN++), making the comparison not directly apples-to-apples. On time series imputation, EDDPM dominates all acceleration methods (DDIM, SN-DDPM, NPR-DDIM, AnalyticDPM) across every masking rate on both Healthcare and Air-quality datasets, and notably exceeds the full-step DDPM baseline RMSE even when 50–80% of steps are removed. The paper also shows that EDDPM can identify optimal steps from a pretrained model in a single epoch of fine-tuning (1 min), achieving near-scratch-training quality (e.g., 11.142 vs. 9.566 MAE at 20% steps on Air-quality).

## Relation to Prior Work

EDDPM builds directly on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) by introducing a probabilistic masking module that multiplies each variance β_t with a learned Bernoulli mask, effectively making the forward and reverse processes jointly sparse. It departs from both learning-free acceleration methods (DDIM, Analytic-DPM, SN-DDPM) that rely on handcrafted uniform or quadratic skipping rules, and learning-based methods (GGDM, GENIE, DPM-solver, TDAS) that decouple training and inference schedules. The key conceptual departure is that EDDPM treats step selection as a discrete optimization problem solved via probabilistic reparameterization and policy gradients during training, rather than as a post-hoc scheduling decision. For time series, it builds on the CSDI conditional score-based diffusion architecture (Tashiro et al., 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in EDDPM's ability to identify informative denoising steps during training without handcrafted rules, and its finding that a single epoch of fine-tuning on a pretrained model suffices to locate the most critical steps—making it a viable tool for exploring large diffusion models under resource constraints. The convergence of masks to deterministic values eliminates inference-time randomness, and the automatic focus on informative steps in later training stages improves convergence speed. Limitations include evaluation only on CIFAR-10 (32×32) and small time series datasets, the requirement for retraining or fine-tuning (unlike learning-free methods), and the global sparsity constraint that does not capture per-step importance structure. Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-resolution image generation or large pretrained models like Stable Diffusion, and how it interacts with emerging consistency-based samplers.
