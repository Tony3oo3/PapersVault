## Problem

Surrogate-gradient-trained SNNs with LIF neurons suffer from temporal gradient vanishing: the product of the decay factor γ over timesteps drives gradients to zero, and the binary surrogate gradient causes the temporal gradient to become exactly zero whenever a neuron fires within the backpropagation window. This prevents SNNs from effectively exploiting temporal information, creating a persistent accuracy gap relative to ANNs with identical architecture.

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) or neuromorphic event streams (128×128 for DVS-CIFAR10 and DVS-Gesture) and outputs a class label. Static images are encoded into time series via direct encoding, where pixel values are fed to the input layer at each of T timesteps (typically 4–20); event streams are integrated into frames using the SpikingJelly framework. The neuron produces binary spike outputs at every timestep, and the final classification is computed from the accumulated membrane potential or spike count.

## Method

CLIF augments the standard LIF neuron with a complementary membrane potential m[t] that captures information lost to membrane potential decay. Specifically, m[t] decays by a sigmoid of the decayed membrane potential (so more membrane decay means less complementary decay), increments by 1 upon firing, and modifies the soft-reset to subtract an additional σ(m[t]) term, implementing a form of spike-frequency adaptation. During backpropagation through time, this complementary state introduces extra gradient paths (the T_M2 term) that decay more slowly than the standard LIF temporal gradients, compensating for the vanishing caused by the γ-product and the binary surrogate. The model adds zero learnable parameters and only two lines of code relative to LIF, making it a drop-in replacement across SNN architectures (ResNet, VGG, Transformer) without altering training hyperparameters.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, CLIF with ResNet-18 achieves 96.69% top-1 accuracy at T=8 (with data augmentation), slightly surpassing the ReLU-based ANN counterpart (96.65%). On CIFAR-100, CLIF reaches 80.89% at T=8, matching the ANN baseline (80.89%). On Tiny-ImageNet with VGG-13, CLIF achieves 64.13% at T=6, substantially outperforming the ANN baseline (59.77%) and all prior SNN methods. On neuromorphic datasets, CLIF achieves 97.92% on DVS-Gesture and 86.10% on CIFAR10-DVS, consistently outperforming its LIF counterpart by 0.35–1.20%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLIF outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and backbone tested. On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18, T=8), CLIF (95.68%) surpasses the closest SNN baselines DSR (95.40%, T=20), ASGL* (95.35%, T=4), and PSN* (95.32%, T=4), while also exceeding the ANN* baseline (96.65%) when data augmentation is applied. On Tiny-ImageNet (VGG-13), CLIF (64.13%, T=6) achieves a dramatic 7.32% improvement over the best prior SNN (ASGL, 56.81%) and 4.36% over the ANN (59.77%). On neuromorphic datasets, CLIF consistently beats its LIF counterpart: +0.35% on DVS-Gesture (97.92% vs 97.57%), +0.95% on CIFAR10-DVS with VGG11 (79.00% vs 78.05%), and +1.20% with VGGSNN2 (86.10% vs 84.90%). Notably, CLIF achieves these gains with zero additional learnable parameters and only two extra lines of code over vanilla LIF, whereas competing methods like PLIF, GLIF, and PSN introduce additional trainable parameters or architectural modifications. The only comparison where CLIF does not lead is CIFAR-100 at T=4 (77.00% vs DSR's 78.50% at T=20), but at matched or lower timesteps CLIF is competitive or superior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LIF neuron model (Wu et al., 2018) within the surrogate gradient (SG) training framework for SNNs, introducing a complementary membrane potential that creates additional temporal gradient paths during BPTT. It departs from prior neuron-enhancement approaches (PLIF, GLIF, KLIF) by adding zero learnable parameters, and from spiking (Conv)LSTM/GRU methods by preserving the binary spike output and reset mechanism while only adding two lines of code. The biological inspiration from spike frequency adaptation and GABAergic hyperpolarization positions CLIF as a phenomenological model rather than a mechanistic one, similar in spirit to the adaptive-threshold neuron of Bellec et al. (2020) but operating on reset strength rather than threshold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most valuable contribution is the clear analytical diagnosis of why LIF-based SNNs underperform ANNs: temporal gradients vanish both from the gamma decay factor and from the binary 0/1 spike-reset term, making additional timesteps counterproductive. CLIF's simplicity (hyperparameter-free, drop-in replacement) makes it broadly applicable across SNN architectures without retraining from scratch. However, the authors acknowledge that the mathematical complexity of CLIF's gradient expressions limits deeper theoretical understanding, and the performance gains over strong SNN baselines (1–2%) remain modest. Open questions include whether the complementary potential mechanism generalizes to even deeper networks or continuous-valued outputs, and whether the spike frequency adaptation it induces could be further exploited for neuromorphic hardware design.
