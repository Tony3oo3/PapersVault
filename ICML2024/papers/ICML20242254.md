## Problem

Universal morphology control requires transformer-scale models to achieve strong zero-shot generalization across diverse robot morphologies, but these models incur 67-160x higher inference FLOPs than a single-robot MLP, making them impractical for deployment on resource-constrained hardware. The paper asks whether one can retain transformer-level performance while reducing inference cost to MLP-level, and identifies that the core obstacle is the inability of a single monolithic network to decouple inter-task (cross-robot) knowledge from intra-task (per-robot) knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242254/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference time the policy consumes only limb-wise proprioceptive state vectors (joint angles, velocities, etc.) and outputs node-wise motor torques for each limb. The morphology context-a topology tree plus per-limb features (relative/absolute position, orientation, mass, joint type, gear ratio)-is fed into the hypernetwork exactly once before deployment to generate the full base MLP; after that the hypernetwork is discarded and only the small MLP runs. Because the base MLP's input/output dimensions vary with the number of limbs, the HN generates limb-wise parameter slices of fixed dimension rather than a single fixed-size weight matrix.

## Method

HyperDistill pairs a morphology-conditioned hypernetwork with a policy-distillation training procedure. The HN uses a transformer context encoder to produce per-limb embeddings, then generates limb-wise input/output layer parameters and shared hidden-layer parameters via linear output heads, yielding a compact robot-specific MLP that is order-invariant and free of redundant inter-task parameters. Training proceeds by behavior-cloning a pre-trained universal transformer teacher (ModuMorph) on 8M transitions collected from 1,000 mutated PD robots, minimizing KL divergence between teacher and student action distributions; direct RL training of the HN is found to be unstable and underperforming. The paper further identifies four algorithmic levers that control the teacher-to-student generalization gap: using a universal (rather than per-robot) teacher, aligning student and teacher architectures, increasing the number of PD robots to cover task space, and applying dropout to the context embedding as task-space regularization.

## Results

On the UNIMAL benchmark (100 training + 100 test robots across Flat Terrain, Variable Terrain, and Obstacle environments), HyperDistill matches the universal TF teacher (ModuMorph) performance on both training and unseen test robots while reducing model size by 6–14× and inference FLOPs by 67–160×. Specifically, in Flat Terrain, HyperDistill uses 0.12M parameters and 0.25M FLOPs versus 1.73M parameters and 39.86M FLOPs for the full ModuMorph teacher. In Variable Terrain and Obstacle, HyperDistill uses 0.30M parameters and 0.60M FLOPs versus ~2M parameters and ~40M FLOPs for the teacher.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ModuMorph (Xiong et al., 2023), HyperDistill matches the full teacher's locomotion performance on both training and test robots in all three environments, while the compressed ModuMorph (same parameter budget as HyperDistill's base MLP) still lags behind HyperDistill in both generalization and efficiency because its attention blocks cannot be decoupled. Against the standard TF architecture (Gupta et al., 2022, Metamorph), the compressed TF at a similar parameter count to HyperDistill's base MLP underperforms in all three environments, confirming that a monolithic TF cannot simultaneously achieve good performance and high inference efficiency without knowledge decoupling. HyperDistill also significantly outperforms a multi-robot MLP (same architecture as its base MLP) on test robots, where the MLP's order-invariance issue causes a larger generalization gap. Finally, training the HN directly via RL (HN-RL) performs poorly on both training and test robots, validating the necessity of the policy distillation training paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the universal morphology control line of work (MetaMorph, ModuMorph) that uses transformers to learn a single policy across diverse robot morphologies, and on hypernetworks (Ha et al., 2016) that generate base-network parameters from context. The key departure is repurposing HNs not to model intra-robot limb diversity (as ModuMorph does) but to enable knowledge decoupling: the HN encodes inter-task knowledge while the generated MLP encodes only intra-task knowledge, yielding MLP-level inference cost with TF-level performance. The training recipe via policy distillation extends prior RL distillation work (Rusu et al., 2015; Furuta et al., 2022) by systematically studying how teacher choice, architecture alignment, task coverage, and task-space regularization affect the student's zero-shot generalization gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 67–160× FLOPs reduction while matching a SOTA transformer teacher is a practically significant efficiency win for deploying universal robot controllers on constrained hardware, and the knowledge-decoupling principle is framed as generalizable to other domains where large models must be compressed for inference. A key limitation is that the HN must be invoked once per robot; if task context changes dynamically (e.g., language-conditioned control), the HN cannot be discarded and the efficiency advantage erodes. Open questions include whether the finding that attention modules may be unnecessary for universal morphology control extends to more complex manipulation tasks, and how to handle the HN overhead when context is non-stationary.
