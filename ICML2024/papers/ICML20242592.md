## Problem

Existing fine-tuning methods for vision-language pre-trained models (VL-PTMs) like CLIP address either OOD generalization under covariate shifts or open-set OOD detection of unseen classes, but not both simultaneously. The paper tackles the underexplored question of how to improve a VL-PTM's generalization to closed-set OOD data while effectively detecting open-set unseen classes during the same fine-tuning process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242592/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images and a fixed set of K closed-set class names as input, processed through CLIP's frozen image and text encoders followed by one-layer linear projection adapters. It produces two outputs: a class label prediction for known classes and an energy score (computed as the negative log-sum-exp of image-text inner products) used to flag open-set OOD instances. The framework operates in few-shot settings (1, 16, or 32 shots per class) and is evaluated under both same-dataset (ImageNet variants) and cross-dataset (PACS/VLCS vs. Caltech101/DTD/Food101) protocols.

## Method

CRoFT introduces an Energy Distribution Reshaping (EDR) loss that minimizes the squared gradient magnitude of energy scores on training data, which the paper proves (via Lemma 3.5 and Theorem 3.6) leads to domain-consistent Hessians of the classification loss—a known indicator of OOD generalization. This theoretical link enables a single loss to simultaneously improve open-set OOD detection and closed-set OOD generalization. Additionally, an adversarial generator (a one-layer linear projection) produces worst-case covariate-shifted OOD image features that preserve semantic information while maximizing distributional shift, and the model is trained to classify these robustly via a regularization term L_c. The final objective combines cross-entropy on ID features, the adversarial OOD classification loss, and EDR losses applied to both ID and generated OOD features, all optimized through lightweight adapter tuning with frozen CLIP encoders.

## Results

On Setup-I (ImageNet, 32-shot, CLIP ViT-B/16), CRoFT achieves 83.1% ID accuracy, 63.1% OOD accuracy, 86.5 AUROC, and 55.2 FPR95, outperforming all baselines on every metric. The headline improvement is a 25.3% reduction in FPR95 over zero-shot CLIP (77.3 → 55.2), while all competing fine-tuning methods actually degrade FPR95 relative to CLIP. On Setup-II (PACS/VLCS cross-dataset, 16-shot), CRoFT achieves 97.3% OOD ACC and 40.0 average FPR95 with ViT-B/16, surpassing the second-best FPR95 by ~11% and boosting OOD accuracy by 4.3% over CLIP-Adapter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRoFT decisively outperforms all seven baselines on FPR95 (the primary OOD detection metric), achieving 55.2% versus the next-best CoOp at 70.6% and zero-shot CLIP at 77.3%. Notably, every competing fine-tuning method (CoOp, CoCoOp, CLIP-Adapter, Tip-Adapter-F, DPLCLIP, Bayes-CAL) actually worsens FPR95 relative to the un-fine-tuned CLIP model, indicating that standard fine-tuning destroys OOD detection ability. On AUROC, CRoFT leads with 86.5%, well ahead of CoOp (79.1%) and DPLCLIP (77.8%). On OOD generalization (closed-set OOD accuracy), CRoFT achieves 63.1%, marginally beating Tip-Adapter-F (62.8%) and CoCoOp (61.8%). Tip-Adapter-F slightly edges CRoFT on ID accuracy (83.3% vs 83.1%) but is far worse on detection metrics (85.9% FPR95 vs 55.2%). The paper's central empirical claim—that concurrent optimization of OOD generalization and open-set detection is possible without sacrificing either—is validated by CRoFT simultaneously achieving the best OOD ACC and the best FPR95 across all shot settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: CLIP-based lightweight fine-tuning for OOD generalization (CoOp, CoCoOp, CLIP-Adapter, Tip-Adapter-F) and energy-based open-set OOD detection (Liu et al., 2020). The key conceptual departure is a theoretical link via Hessians: the proposed Energy Distribution Reshaping (EDR) loss, designed to minimize gradient magnitude of energy scores for OOD detection, is shown to induce domain-consistent Hessians of the classification loss, thereby also improving OOD generalization. Rather than treating these as competing objectives requiring separate regularization, the paper demonstrates they can be optimized concurrently within a single adapter-tuning framework, building on the Hessian-alignment literature (Rame et al., 2022; Hemati et al., 2023) but arriving at the Hessian consistency as a byproduct rather than a direct regularizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The concurrent optimization framing addresses a practical gap: real-world deployment of VL-PTMs requires both robustness to covariate shifts and the ability to flag truly novel classes, yet prior methods optimize one at the expense of the other. The finding that existing fine-tuning methods (CoOp, CoCoOp) actually *degrade* open-set detection relative to vanilla CLIP highlights an underappreciated tension in the field. Limitations include reliance on local optimality and small-epsilon assumptions in the theoretical bounds, evaluation only on CLIP with one-layer linear adapters, and FPR95 values that, while improved, remain in the 50–70% range. Open questions include whether the Hessian connection generalizes to deeper adapters or other VL-PTMs, and whether the adversarial worst-case feature generation scales to more complex distribution shifts.
