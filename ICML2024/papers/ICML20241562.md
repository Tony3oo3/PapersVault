## Problem

Deep RL agents are known to under-utilize their network parameters, with large fractions of neurons going dormant and performance degrading as networks are scaled up. While Graesser et al. (2022) showed that gradual magnitude pruning can improve DQN performance with a ResNet backbone, it remained unclear whether this benefit generalizes across architectures, agents, and training regimes, or whether the gains scale with network size.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241562/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For Atari experiments, the agent consumes 4 stacked 84×84 grayscale frames as input and outputs Q-values (or distributional Q-values for Rainbow/IQN) over the discrete action set. For MuJoCo continuous control, the input is a state vector and the output is a policy distribution. The key structural difference from standard RL is that the network's effective parameter count shrinks during training: at 95% sparsity, only 5% of the initial weights remain active, yet the output format and action space are unchanged.

## Method

The paper applies gradual magnitude pruning (Zhu & Gupta, 2017) as a drop-in training modification for value-based RL agents. A polynomial sparsity schedule (Equation 1) progressively zeros out the smallest-magnitude weights from 20% to 80% of training, reaching a target sparsity of 95%, after which the sparse topology is frozen for the remaining 20%. The critical finding is that while dense networks degrade monotonically as width increases, pruned networks improve monotonically with width, yielding ~60% (DQN) and ~50% (Rainbow) performance gains over unpruned baselines at 3× width. The paper attributes these gains to a combination of effects: reduced parameter norm, fewer dormant neurons, higher effective rank, and weaker gradient correlation (reduced gradient interference), which together preserve network plasticity in the non-stationary RL setting. The technique is validated across DQN, Rainbow, IQN, Munchausen-IQN, CQL, CQL+C51, DrQ(ε), DER, and SAC, and shown to outperform alternatives including lottery ticket retraining, RigL dynamic sparse training, weight decay, L2 normalization, network resets, and ReDo.

## Results

With the Impala-based ResNet architecture at 95% sparsity and width multiplier 3, gradual magnitude pruning yields approximately 60% performance improvement for DQN and 50% for Rainbow over the unpruned, unscaled baseline (measured as human-normalized IQM over 15 Atari games, 40M frames). At 90% and 95% sparsity with width 3, the improvement is 33%, while 99% sparsity maintains performance. Critically, while dense networks degrade monotonically as width increases (1→3→5), pruned networks improve monotonically, demonstrating that pruning enables effective network scaling in value-based RL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gradual magnitude pruning (GMP) at 95% sparsity outperforms all five compared baselines across width scales (Figure 12): periodic network resets (Nikishin et al., 2022), weight decay, ReDo dormant-neuron recycling (Sokar et al., 2023), and L2 parameter normalization (Kumar et al., 2022) all suffer performance degradation when network width increases, whereas GMP continues to improve. The lottery ticket hypothesis baseline (Frankle & Carbin, 2018), implemented by fixing the final pruned mask and retraining from dense initialization, underperforms both the GMP approach and the unpruned dense baseline (Figure 13). RigL dynamic sparse training (Evci et al., 2020) is effective but consistently fails to match GMP's performance at equivalent sparsity levels (Figure 22, Appendix G.3). In the offline RL setting, GMP also prevents performance collapse at width 1 for CQL+C51, a failure mode not addressed by the other baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse-training line of work in deep RL initiated by Graesser et al. (2022), who first showed that gradual magnitude pruning (GMP) from Zhu & Gupta (2017) improves DQN performance when paired with a ResNet backbone. While Graesser et al. demonstrated the effect for a single architecture comparison (ResNet vs. CNN), this paper systematically generalizes the finding across a wide range of value-based agents (DQN, Rainbow, IQN, M-IQN, CQL, CQL+C51, DrQ, DER), training regimes (online, offline, low-data), and network scales, positioning GMP as a broadly applicable parameter-efficiency technique rather than an architecture-specific curiosity. The paper also situates its contributions within the growing literature on RL pathologies—implicit underparameterization (Kumar et al., 2021a), dormant neurons (Sokar et al., 2023), and primacy bias (Nikishin et al., 2022)—showing that pruning simultaneously addresses multiple of these issues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is the scaling behavior: while dense RL networks degrade monotonically as width increases, pruned networks improve monotonically, suggesting a path toward scaling RL agents that mirrors (but inverts) the scaling laws of supervised learning. This matters as RL moves toward more complex, multi-task, and generalizable settings where larger networks will be necessary. Key limitations include the modest or absent gains for actor-critic methods (SAC, PPO) and the fact that the mechanistic explanation—reduced gradient correlation, lower parameter norms, fewer dormant neurons—remains correlational rather than causal. Open questions include whether the pruning schedule can be further optimized, how the technique interacts with multi-task and world-model-based agents, and whether hardware-level sparse acceleration will make the computational savings tangible.
