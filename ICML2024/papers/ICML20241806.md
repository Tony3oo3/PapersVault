## Problem

Deep weight space (DWS) models that process the weights of other neural networks suffer from severe overfitting, with 3D shape classification accuracy on ModelNet40 reaching only 16% compared to 90% for standard point-cloud methods. The paper identifies the root cause as a lack of diversity in DWS datasets: a single object can be represented by many different weight configurations (neural views), yet typical INR training sets use only one view per object, conflating generalization to new views with generalization to new objects. The paper asks what augmentation strategies can effectively increase weight-space diversity on-the-fly without the prohibitive cost of training additional INRs.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input the concatenated weight matrices and bias vectors of an INR MLP (e.g., a 5-layer 3→32→32→32→32→3 SIREN network for FMNIST, or a 5-layer 3→32→32→32→32→1 SIREN for ModelNet40 SDFs), and produces a class label (supervised) or a learned embedding (SSL). For MixUp variants, the input is a pair of such weight vectors, which are combined via a convex interpolation with a Beta-distributed mixing coefficient. A key structural constraint is that weight vectors carry permutation symmetries: two independently trained INRs representing the same object are rarely aligned, so naive interpolation of their weights does not yield a meaningful network.

## Method

The paper proposes three weight-space MixUp variants that address the alignment problem inherent to interpolating weight vectors. Direct MixUp performs a simple convex combination of two weight vectors; Randomized MixUp first applies random permutation matrices to the second vector before mixing; Alignment-based MixUp uses the Weight Matching algorithm (Re-Basin) to find approximate optimal permutations minimizing ‖v₁ − p·v₂‖₂ before interpolating, motivated by linear mode connectivity results showing that aligned interpolations preserve low loss. In addition, the paper introduces a novel family of weight-space-specific augmentations exploiting activation function symmetries: SIREN negation (negating a layer's weights/biases and the next layer's weights, justified by sin(−x) = −sin(x)), SIREN bias (shifting biases by kπ with sign flips), and ReLU scaling (rescaling via positive diagonal matrices). These augmentations are applied on-the-fly during training to DWS and GNN equivariant architectures, and the paper demonstrates that Alignment+MixUp with a single view achieves performance comparable to training with 5–10× more data, and yields 5–11% gains in a SimCLR contrastive pre-training setup.

## Results

On FMNIST-INR, Alignment+MixUp with a single view lifts DWS accuracy from 68.30% to 75.67% and GNN from 68.84% to 78.80%, matching or exceeding the performance of training with 10 views without augmentation (76.01% and 79.58% respectively). On ModelNet40-INR, the gains are even more dramatic: GNN improves from 8.82% to 26.88% (1 view) and DWS from 16.17% to 27.40%. On CIFAR10-INR, Alignment+MixUp achieves 42.76% (DWS) and 46.60% (GNN) with 1 view, surpassing the 10-view no-augmentation baselines of 40.64% and 46.52%. In the self-supervised setting, contrastive pre-training with the proposed augmentations yields 5–11% downstream accuracy gains over fine-tuning from scratch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates augmentations on two equivariant weight-space architectures: DWS (Navon et al., 2023a) and GNN (Zhang et al., 2023). Alignment+MixUp is the best augmentation for DWS in 5 of 6 dataset/view configurations, improving over no-augmentation DWS by up to 11.25 points (FMNIST 1-view: 68.30→75.67) and by 18.06 points on ModelNet40 1-view (8.82→26.88 for GNN). For GNN, direct MixUp is competitive with Alignment+MixUp in several settings (e.g., 80.18% vs. 78.80% on FMNIST 1-view), while the randomized-permutation MixUp variant is consistently weaker. In the non-invariant comparison (Table 4, Appendix E.2), the Transformer from Schürholt et al. (2021) achieves only 27.60–30.14% on FMNIST, far below DWS+Alignment+MixUp (75.67–79.41%) and GNN+Alignment+MixUp (78.80–82.94%), confirming that permutation-equivariant architectures remain essential even with strong augmentations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on equivariant weight space architectures (DWS by Navon et al. 2023a; GNN by Zhang et al. 2023) and identifies their persistent overfitting as a data-diversity problem rather than an architectural one. It adapts the MixUp augmentation paradigm (Zhang et al. 2017) to the weight space setting, leveraging weight alignment algorithms from the model-merging literature (Re-Basin by Ainsworth et al. 2022; Sinkhorn-based re-basin by Peña et al. 2023) to handle permutation symmetries that make naive interpolation meaningless. A novel contribution is the identification of activation-function-specific symmetries (SIREN negation, SIREN bias, ReLU scaling) as a new family of weight-space augmentations, complementing previously studied input-space and data-agnostic augmentations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practical, on-the-fly augmentation techniques that close part of the large performance gap between weight space models and direct methods (e.g., 16%→27% on ModelNet40 vs. ~90% for point cloud methods), and demonstrates that the overfitting in DWS stems from insufficient view diversity rather than model capacity—a finding that redirects future work toward data generation and alignment quality. A notable open question is why alignment-based MixUp only marginally outperforms direct MixUp (1–3%), suggesting current alignment algorithms (which ignore activation symmetries) are suboptimal; learning alignment from data (as in Navon et al. 2023b) is a promising direction. The techniques are also shown to improve self-supervised pre-training in weight space, broadening their applicability beyond supervised classification.
