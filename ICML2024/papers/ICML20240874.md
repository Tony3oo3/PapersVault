## Problem

Learning generalizable low-level control policies from sub-optimal demonstrations (e.g., contact-rich manipulations) remains challenging because such demos are inherently non-Markovian, noisy, discontinuous, and random, causing compounding errors in standard behavior cloning. Existing Transformer-based imitation learning methods treat the trajectory as a flat sequence without exploiting the natural multi-step subskill structure of the task, missing an opportunity for hierarchical planning that could improve both optimization and generalization.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a context window of T timesteps of interleaved state observations and actions (or PointNet-encoded point cloud features concatenated with proprioception), prepended with T learnable CoT prompt tokens. It outputs low-level continuous actions via a center-plus-offset decomposition: the action center is regressed from state token features, while the action offset is regressed from CoT token features. During training, the CoT decoder additionally predicts both the next subskill observation and the final (task-completion) observation for each CoT token, producing a 2q-dimensional target per token.

## Method

CoTPC introduces two key components. First, an observation-space-agnostic unsupervised subskill discovery step that segments each demo's action sequence into functionally similar groups using PELT change-point detection with cosine distance, yielding boundary observations that form the chain-of-thought (CoT) supervision. Second, a modified Behavior Transformer architecture where T learnable CoT prompt tokens are prepended to the state/action context under a hybrid masking scheme (CoT tokens attend all-to-all; state/action tokens attend causally plus to CoT tokens). The CoT tokens are coupled to action prediction: the same CoT token features that feed the CoT decoder (predicting next and last subskill) also feed the action offset decoder, while state tokens feed the action center decoder. Training minimizes a combined BC loss and auxiliary CoT MSE loss (λ=0.1), with random attention masking applied during training to prevent CoT tokens from trivially attending to future tokens. At inference, the CoT decoder is discarded and only the dynamically updated CoT features guide action offset prediction.

## Results

On ManiSkill2 (state observations), CoTPC achieves 86.0% SR on Stack Cube (unseen), 59.3% on Peg Insertion (0-shot), 51.2%/39.3% on Turn Faucet (unseen/0-shot), and 41.0%/41.0% on Push Chair (unseen/0-shot), consistently outperforming Behavior Transformer by 8–27 points across tasks. On Moving Maze, CoTPC reaches 44.0% SR versus 33.0% (BeT) and 23.0% (DT). On Franka Kitchen, CoTPC completes 2.1 sub-tasks per trajectory with 25.6% full-task SR, nearly doubling BeT's 14.4% SR. With point cloud observations, CoTPC also surpasses BeT on all five ManiSkill2 tasks (e.g., 81.0% vs 70.0% on Stack Cube unseen).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoTPC consistently outperforms Behavior Transformer (its direct architectural base) across all tasks and evaluation settings, with the largest gains on Peg Insertion 0-shot (59.3% vs 32.5%) and Stack Cube unseen (86.0% vs 73.0%). Against Decision Transformer, CoTPC wins by wide margins on every task (e.g., 86.0% vs 19.0% on Stack Cube, 59.3% vs 27.0% on Peg Insertion 0-shot). Against diffusion-based baselines, CoTPC is competitive but not uniformly dominant: Diffusion Policy edges out CoTPC on Turn Faucet unseen (54.0% vs 50.0%) and Push Chair unseen (54.0% vs 41.0%), while Decision Diffuser leads on Push Chair unseen (56.0% vs 41.0%); however, CoTPC clearly wins on all 0-shot settings (e.g., 59.3% vs 35.0% on Peg Insertion, 41.0% vs 38.0% on Push Chair), indicating stronger generalization to unseen object geometries. Vanilla BC is effectively non-functional on all ManiSkill2 tasks (0–1% SR), highlighting the difficulty of the sub-optimal demonstrations. On the simpler Moving Maze and Franka Kitchen benchmarks, CoTPC also leads all baselines (44.0% vs 33.0% SR on Maze; 25.6% vs 14.4% SR on Kitchen).

## Relation to Prior Work

CoTPC builds directly on the Behavior Transformer (BeT) architecture, extending it with learnable prompt tokens (borrowed from the vision-language prompting literature) and a hybrid masking strategy to jointly predict subskill-level chain-of-thought guidance alongside low-level actions. The subskill discovery step repurposes change-point detection (PELT) from time-series analysis, applying it to action sequences rather than observations, which makes it observation-space-agnostic. Conceptually, the paper bridges the chain-of-thought paradigm from LLMs (Wei et al., 2022) and the option/feudal RL hierarchy (Sutton et al., 1999; Dayan & Hinton, 1992) into a fully offline imitation learning setting, departing from Procedure Cloning (Yang et al., 2022) by not requiring access to the demonstrator's internal computations and from option-based HRL by not requiring online interaction or learned termination conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: most real-world robot demonstrations are sub-optimal (noisy, discontinuous, non-Markovian), yet existing BC/Transformer methods struggle to extract robust hierarchical structure from them. The observation-space-agnostic subskill discovery is a reusable component that could benefit other hierarchical IL methods. Key limitations include the purely offline setting (no online fine-tuning), the assumption that temporally close and functionally similar actions form coherent subskills (which breaks down for tasks with many micro-adjustments), and the fact that the CoT decoder is discarded at inference, making the hierarchy implicit rather than explicit. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-task foundation models for low-level control and whether online fine-tuning can further close the gap with optimal policies.
