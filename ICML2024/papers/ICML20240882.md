## Problem

Existing projection-free algorithms for stochastic constrained multi-level compositional optimization (e.g., LiNASA+ICG) suffer from three limitations: their SFO complexity of O(ε^{-2}) under the gradient mapping criterion does not match the optimal O(ε^{-1.5}) rate achievable for unconstrained problems, they provide no guarantees under the more widely used Frank-Wolfe gap criterion, and their analysis is restricted to non-convex objectives with no treatment of convex or strongly convex cases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240882/cropped_page6_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a K-level nested composition F(x) = f_K ∘ f_{K-1} ∘ … ∘ f_1(x) over a closed convex set X, accessing each f_i and ∇f_i only through noisy stochastic oracle samples. It requires two oracles per iteration: a Stochastic First-order Oracle (SFO) returning (f_i(x;ξ), ∇f_i(x;ξ)) and a Linear Minimization Oracle (LMO) solving argmin_{x∈X} ⟨x, d⟩, with the LMO replacing the expensive projection step. The output is a randomly selected iterate x_τ whose quality is certified under one of three criteria—Frank-Wolfe gap, gradient mapping, or optimal gap—depending on the convexity regime, with complexity measured in total SFO and LMO calls.

## Method

The core algorithm (PMVR) applies the STORM momentum-based variance reduction estimator directly to the overall nested gradient ∏∇f_i rather than estimating each level's gradient separately, which eliminates the need for per-level gradient clipping and uses only constant learning rate and momentum parameters. The Frank-Wolfe update x_{t+1} = x_t + η(z_t − x_t) with z_t = argmin_{x∈X} ⟨x, v_t⟩ replaces projection, where v_t is the variance-reduced gradient estimate. For the gradient mapping criterion, a modified sub-algorithm (PMVR-v2) runs N inner Frank-Wolfe loops on a quadratic surrogate g(w, x_t, v_t) to approximate the projection-free gradient mapping. For convex and strongly convex objectives, a stage-wise warm-start wrapper (Algorithm 3/4) halves the optimal gap per stage by decreasing α_s and η_s while increasing T_s, and for strong convexity the LMO direction incorporates a λ/2·(w_n − x_t) curvature term. Large batch sizes of O(ε^{-1}) or O(ε^{-0.5}) further reduce LMO complexity without degrading SFO rates.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is in Table 1, which summarizes SFO and LMO complexities under three criteria. For gradient mapping, PMVR-v2 achieves O(ε^{-1.5}) SFO and O(ε^{-2.5}) LMO with constant batch, improving over LiNASA+ICG's O(ε^{-2}) SFO and O(ε^{-3}) LMO; with a large batch of O(ε^{-0.5}), the LMO complexity further drops to O(ε^{-2}). For the Frank-Wolfe gap, PMVR achieves O(ε^{-3}) SFO and O(ε^{-3}) LMO (constant batch) or O(ε^{-3}) SFO and O(ε^{-2}) LMO (large batch), matching single-level projection-free rates. Empirically, on Industry-10 and Industry-12 portfolio optimization datasets, PMVR-v2 shows the fastest decrease in loss value, Frank-Wolfe gap, and gradient mapping across all three experimental tasks (2-level and 3-level compositional problems).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary multi-level competitor is LiNASA+ICG (Xiao et al., 2022), which achieves O(ε^{-2}) SFO and O(ε^{-3}) LMO under gradient mapping. PMVR-v2 strictly improves both: O(ε^{-1.5}) SFO (matching the unconstrained lower bound) and O(ε^{-2}) LMO with a batch of only O(ε^{-0.5}), versus LiNASA+ICG's O(ε^{-1}) batch requirement. For the Frank-Wolfe gap criterion, PMVR is the first multi-level projection-free method with theoretical guarantees, achieving rates that match the best single-level methods (1-SFW, SPIDER-FW). For convex and strongly convex objectives, PMVR's stage-wise variant achieves O(ε^{-2}) SFO/LMO (convex) and O(λ^{-1}ε^{-1}) SFO (strongly convex), matching single-level optima (1-SFW, SCGS) — results entirely absent from prior multi-level projection-free work. Empirically, on both Industry-10 and Industry-12 datasets across 2-level and 3-level portfolio optimization tasks, PMVR-v2 converges fastest in loss value, Frank-Wolfe gap, and gradient mapping, outperforming A-TSCGD, NLASG, Nested-SPIDER, SCSC, SMVR, and LiNASA+ICG in all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unconstrained multi-level compositional optimization literature (SMVR, NASA) to the constrained projection-free setting, building directly on the STORM variance reduction estimator (Cutkosky & Orabona, 2019) and the Frank-Wolfe update mechanism (Jaggi, 2013). It improves upon the only prior projection-free multi-level method, LiNASA+ICG (Xiao et al., 2022), by replacing the linearized NASA estimator with STORM applied directly to the overall gradient—avoiding the level-by-level gradient clipping required by SMVR—and by providing the first Frank-Wolfe gap guarantees and convex/strongly convex analyses for this problem class. The stage-wise warm-start design for convex objectives follows the standard multi-stage framework used in single-level projection-free methods (Lan & Zhou, 2016; Yurtsever et al., 2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work completes the theoretical picture for projection-free multi-level compositional optimization by providing matching rates across all three standard criteria (Frank-Wolfe gap, gradient mapping, optimal gap) and all three function classes (non-convex, convex, strongly convex), with SFO complexities that match the unconstrained lower bounds. For practitioners dealing with expensive projections (e.g., nuclear norm balls in matrix recovery, simplices in portfolio optimization), the LMO-based approach offers a practical alternative with now-guaranteed convergence. Open questions include whether level-independent rates are achievable in the projection-free multi-level setting, how these methods behave in decentralized or federated environments, and whether the constant batch size assumption can be relaxed further in practice.
