## Problem

Existing graph convolution operators—whether defined in the spatial domain (MPNNs limited to one-hop neighborhoods) or the spectral domain (polynomial approximations of the Laplacian)—are inflexible, not unified, and lack a general mechanism for controlling kernel support. Defining a truly general convolution on graphs is fundamentally harder than in Euclidean space due to the absence of canonical coordinates, irregular node degrees, and graph symmetries, which together prevent a straightforward port of continuous kernel convolution from signal processing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241379/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CKGCN consumes a graph with optional node features, edge features, and the adjacency matrix; it computes K-order Relative Random Walk Probabilities (RRWP) as pseudo-coordinates for every node pair, rescales them by the graph order N, and concatenates them with node/edge attributes before a linear stem projection. The kernel support is configurable per layer (K-hop neighborhood or the full graph), and the output is a per-node representation vector that is pooled (sum or mean) into a graph-level embedding for graph-level tasks, or read out directly for node-level tasks. The model is trained end-to-end with standard supervised losses (MSE for regression, cross-entropy for classification) under a fixed parameter budget (~100K–500K).

## Method

CKGConv parameterizes the convolution kernel ψ as a continuous function (a stack of ResNet-style MLP blocks with GELU activations and BatchNorm) of the relative positional encoding P_{i,j} between node i and node j, replacing the fixed discrete kernel of classical convolutions. Three design choices address graph-specific challenges: (1) RRWP provides pseudo-coordinates that encode multi-hop structural information without requiring canonical coordinates; (2) a scaled-convolution term (dividing by |supp_ψ(i)|) ensures numerical stability across nodes with vastly different neighborhood sizes; (3) an adaptive degree-scaler (x_i ⊙ θ₁ + d_i^{1/2}·x_i ⊙ θ₂) recovers degree information lost by mean aggregation. For multi-channel signals, the scalar kernel is extended via depthwise separable convolution, and the overall network stacks L CKGConv blocks (CKGConv layer + FFN + residual + BatchNorm). Theoretically, with globally supported kernels and generalized-distance pseudo-coordinates, CKGCN is proven to match the expressiveness of GD-WL (and hence SOTA graph transformers), while empirically it outperforms all convolutional GNN baselines and achieves performance comparable to GRIT across seven benchmarks.

## Results

On the five Benchmarking GNNs datasets, CKGCN achieves the best performance on 3 out of 5: ZINC (0.059 MAE, tied with GRIT), MNIST (98.42% accuracy), and PATTERN (88.66% weighted accuracy). On CIFAR-10 (72.79%) and CLUSTER (79.00%), CKGCN ranks second behind GRIT (76.47% and 80.03% respectively). On the Long-Range Graph Benchmark, CKGCN achieves 0.6952 AP on Peptides-func and 0.2477 MAE on Peptides-struct, both statistically indistinguishable from the best (GRIT: 0.6988 AP, 0.2460 MAE) but significantly better than the third-best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CKGCN outperforms the hybrid Graph Transformer GPS (Rampášek et al., 2022) on all five benchmarks, e.g., 0.059 vs 0.070 MAE on ZINC and 98.42% vs 98.05% on MNIST, demonstrating the advantage of continuous convolution over MPNN+attention. Against the SOTA Graph Transformer GRIT (Ma et al., 2023), CKGCN wins on MNIST (98.42% vs 98.11%) and PATTERN (88.66% vs 87.20%), ties on ZINC (0.059 vs 0.059), but trails on CIFAR-10 (72.79% vs 76.47%) and CLUSTER (79.00% vs 80.03%), suggesting dynamic attention and sharpening kernels have complementary strengths. CKGCN surpasses all convolutional GNNs (GCN, GIN, GAT, GatedGCN, PNA) on four of five datasets, with the only exception being CIFAR-10 where DGN (72.84%) marginally edges out CKGCN (72.79%). On the LRGB benchmarks, CKGCN (0.6952 AP on Peptides-func, 0.2477 MAE on Peptides-struct) is statistically indistinguishable from GRIT but significantly outperforms GPS (0.6535 AP, 0.2500 MAE) and all MPNN-based methods. The ablation in Table 3 further shows that constraining CKGCN to all-positive (blurring) kernels degrades ZINC MAE from 0.059 to 0.073, confirming the importance of flexible kernels with negative coefficients.
