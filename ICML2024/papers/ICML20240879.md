## Problem

High-level semantic concepts in large language models are empirically observed to be encoded linearly in representation space, but the theoretical origins of this linearity remain unexplained. The central question is whether this apparent linearity is illusory or arises from principled mechanisms in the training process. This paper asks specifically how the next-token prediction objective (softmax with cross-entropy) combined with the implicit bias of gradient descent promotes linear representations of latent concepts.

## Input / Output

The model takes a context sentence x, which is mapped via a deterministic function h_x to a partial binary concept vector d ∈ {◇,0,1}^m where ◇ denotes unknown concepts, and produces a probability distribution over next tokens y (equivalently, over full binary concept vectors c ∈ {0,1}^m). The key geometric outputs are steering vectors: for a concept C_i, the difference g(c_{(i→1)}) − g(c_{(i→0)}) in unembedding space and f(d_{(i→1)}) − f(d_{(i→0)}) in embedding space. The paper shows these steering vectors are parallel across all counterfactual pairs for the same concept (linearity) and nearly orthogonal across different concepts (orthogonality).

## Method

The paper introduces a latent conditional model where context sentences and next tokens share a latent space of m binary concept variables forming a Markov random field, with the context fixing a subset of 'core' concepts and leaving the rest to be inferred. The first theoretical result (Theorem 3.2) shows that if the learned log-odds match the true log-odds for a concept under all non-conditioning contexts, then all steering vectors for that concept are parallel in a projected subspace. The main technical contribution (Theorems 3.3–3.4) shows that even without perfect log-odds matching, the implicit bias of gradient descent on the exponential loss (a subproblem of softmax cross-entropy) aligns all unembedding steering vectors for a fixed concept in the limit, and when embeddings and unembeddings are trained jointly from orthogonal initializations, the embedding and unembedding steering vectors also align. A further result (Theorem 4.1) establishes that concepts separated in the MRF graph yield orthogonal representations, explaining the near-orthogonality of unrelated concept directions observed in LLaMA-2.

## Results

In simulated experiments with the complete set of conditionals, the latent conditional model produces highly linear representations: average cosine similarities among unembedding steering vectors range from 0.972 (m=3) to 0.997 (m=6), and between embedding and unembedding steering vectors range from 0.973 to 0.990. On LLaMA-2-7B, the French-Spanish language translation concept shows strong alignment with corresponding token concepts (e.g., French-Spanish unembedding steering vector aligns with the French-Spanish embedding steering vector) relative to unrelated concepts, as predicted by Theorem 3.4. Orthogonality between steering vectors of different concepts is also confirmed in both simulations and LLaMA-2, with near-zero cosine similarities across distinct concept directions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not compare against competing methods; instead, it validates its theoretical predictions (Theorems 3.2–3.4, 4.1–4.2) against both simulated data and the LLaMA-2-7B model. The key empirical validation is that the simple latent conditional model reproduces the linear and orthogonal representation geometry observed in LLaMA-2: steering vectors of the same concept are highly aligned (cosine similarity ≈ 0.97–0.99 in simulations) while steering vectors of different concepts are near-orthogonal. On LLaMA-2, the alignment between embedding and unembedding representations for matching concepts (e.g., French-Spanish) is nontrivial relative to unmatched concepts, confirming Theorem 3.4's prediction. The paper notes that final cosine similarities on LLaMA-2 are lower than in simulations, attributed to the complexity of natural language and the fact that abstract concepts may not lie in a strictly one-dimensional subspace.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work explaining linear representations in word embeddings (Mikolov et al., 2013; Arora et al., 2016; Ethayarajh et al., 2018) to large language models by introducing a latent conditional model with binary concept variables. While prior theoretical explanations relied on log-odds matching assumptions (which require specific distributional structure), this paper's key departure is showing that the implicit bias of gradient descent on exponential loss (building on Soudry et al., 2018) promotes linearity even when those assumptions fail. The definition of linearly encoded representation is adapted from Park et al. (2023), and the Markov random field structure generalizes the independent-concept case studied in earlier word embedding analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first theoretical account of why linear representations emerge in LLMs from the optimization dynamics themselves, rather than requiring specific data distribution assumptions. The finding that linearity is architecture-independent (a by-product of learning conditional probabilities via softmax cross-entropy) suggests the phenomenon is fundamental to the next-token prediction objective. Limitations include the injectivity assumption (one token per concept vector), the analysis of only subproblems of the full optimization, and the gap between simulated cosine similarities (~0.97–0.99) and LLaMA-2 results (lower, 'partial alignment'). Open questions include whether the theory extends to non-binary concepts, polysemous tokens, and whether the orthogonality of unrelated concepts holds without the matched-representation assumption.
