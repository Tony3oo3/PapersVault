## Problem

Large monolithic generative models face an exponential data-requirement bottleneck as the number of jointly modeled variables grows, and they exhibit poor compositionality on simple spatial and relational queries despite training on most of the Internet. This paper argues that complex generative systems should instead be built by composing smaller, separately trained generative models, which are more data-efficient, generalizable to unseen combinations, and programmable for new task distributions without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240466/cropped_page2_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a set of pre-trained generative models (EBMs, diffusion models, LLMs, video models, action models) each capturing a factor of the target distribution, and produces samples from their composed (product) distribution. Composition is specified by a structural graph or product expression over the models; for planning, the input is a start state and goal state combined with a trajectory dynamics model; for constraint satisfaction, it is a graph of low-dimensional constraint factors over decision variables. Outputs range from generated trajectories and images to hierarchical plans jointly specifying text, image, and action sequences.

## Method

The core mechanism is representing each generative model as an Energy-Based Model p_i(x) ∝ exp(−E_i(x)), so that the product of densities reduces to summing energy functions: p(x) ∝ exp(−(E_1(x)+E_2(x))). For continuous spaces, sampling from the composite EBM uses Langevin dynamics (ULA) with the gradient of the summed energy; for diffusion models, each denoising function ε(x,t) is reinterpreted as the score of an intermediate EBM, and compositional sampling is performed via annealed importance sampling that runs multiple reverse-diffusion (ULA) steps at each noise level on the composite score. For discrete spaces, one model serves as a proposal distribution while the remaining energy functions define a Metropolis-Hastings acceptance criterion. The paper also shows that autoregressive factorizations and raw diffusion score functions do not compose directly, motivating the EBM representation as the enabling algebraic structure.

## Results

The most concrete quantitative result is on the MATH dataset, where composing 5 instances of GPT-3.5 via multi-agent debate achieves 58.0±2.8% accuracy, outperforming the much larger GPT-4 model (55.0±2.9%). For trajectory modeling on Maze2D, the compositional (factorized) model achieves higher simulation accuracy at unseen start states with fewer agent episodes and requires substantially fewer training iterations than the monolithic joint model (Figure 5). The paper also establishes a theoretical data-efficiency argument: learning a joint distribution over 4 variables each with K values requires O(K⁴) samples, whereas factorized learning requires only O(K) per factor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary empirical comparison is against the monolithic trajectory generative model from Janner et al. (2022) on the Maze2D environment (4D state, 2D action). The compositional (factorized) version of the same architecture, representing trajectory chunksize 8, outperforms the monolithic model on two axes: (1) it achieves higher trajectory simulation accuracy at unseen start states when trained on a limited number of agent episodes (Figure 5a), and (2) it converges in substantially fewer training iterations (Figure 5b). This demonstrates that the factorization p(τ) ∝ ∏ᵢ p(sᵢ|sᵢ₋₁, a) is both more data-efficient and more computationally efficient than learning the full joint p(s₀,a₀,…,s_T,a_T) directly. The paper does not report a single unified benchmark table across all domains, as it is a position paper illustrating the compositional paradigm across multiple settings rather than a single controlled experiment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper synthesizes and advocates for a compositional generative modeling paradigm that has been demonstrated in a series of individual works by the authors and collaborators: EBM-based product-of-experts composition (Du & Mordatch 2019; Du et al. 2020a), compositional diffusion sampling via annealed importance sampling (Du et al. 2023a), trajectory planning through probability composition (Janner et al. 2022), multi-agent language model ensembling (Du et al. 2023b), and unsupervised discovery of compositional factors (Su et al. 2024; Comas et al. 2023; Liu et al. 2023). Conceptually, it roots the approach in Probabilistic Graphical Models (Koller & Friedman 2009) and the product-of-experts framework (Hinton 2002), while explicitly positioning itself against the dominant 'scale up monolithic models' paradigm exemplified by GPT-4, DALL-E 3, and similar systems. The paper does not introduce a new method but rather unifies these scattered contributions under a single architectural thesis and provides a practical implementation recipe (EBM + MCMC) for compositional sampling in both continuous and discrete spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper makes a compelling data-efficiency argument: joint distributions over D variables require O(K^D) samples while factorized models require only O(K) per factor, which is particularly relevant as multimodal models face exponentially growing combination spaces. However, the paper itself acknowledges two significant open questions: (1) current approaches assume a fixed, prespecified compositional structure, and automatically discovering the correct structure and per-model weightings remains unsolved; (2) real-world data often exhibits spurious correlations that violate the independence assumptions underlying factorization, and robust discovery methods incorporating prior knowledge or active intervention are needed. The broader implication is a potential shift in how AI systems are architected—toward modular, interpretable, and deployable collections of smaller models rather than ever-larger monoliths—but the evidence base is largely drawn from cited prior work rather than new controlled experiments, and the practical gap between the EBM+MCMC recipe and the efficiency of modern diffusion/autoregressive samplers remains substantial.
