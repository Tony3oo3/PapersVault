## Problem

Existing vision-language benchmarks test only one or two capabilities in isolation (e.g., recognition or OCR), failing to capture the integrated multi-capability reasoning that real-world multimodal tasks demand. Additionally, no unified evaluation metric existed for open-ended LMM outputs that vary widely in format—from single-word answers to hundred-word essays—making cross-task comparison difficult.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242381/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MM-Vet consists of 200 images paired with 218 open-ended questions, each tagged with the specific core capabilities (out of 6) and capability integrations (out of 16) required to answer it. The evaluation pipeline feeds each sample's question, ground truth annotation, and the LMM's free-form text prediction into a GPT-4 few-shot prompt, which returns a continuous correctness score in [0, 1]. Scores are then averaged per capability, per capability integration, and overall, with variance reported across 5 repeated GPT-4 evaluations to account for scoring stochasticity.

## Method

MM-Vet defines six core vision-language capabilities—Recognition, OCR, Knowledge, Language Generation, Spatial Awareness, and Math—and constructs 16 tasks from their pairwise and higher-order integrations, grounded in realistic scenarios (e.g., reading a floor plan, performing arithmetic, and identifying spatial relationships simultaneously). For evaluation, the paper proposes an LLM-as-judge metric: a GPT-4 prompt containing seven few-shot grading examples (covering fully correct, partially correct, and fully wrong answers of both short and long form) that maps a (question, ground truth, prediction) triple to a 0–1 score. The few-shot design lets GPT-4 infer scoring criteria in-context rather than requiring hand-coded rules per question type, and ablation studies confirm that including all seven examples minimizes the average absolute deviation from human-annotated scores (Δ̄ = 0.042 vs. 0.273 for keyword matching). The total benchmark score is the mean of per-sample scores, and per-capability or per-integration scores are computed by restricting the mean to the relevant subset of samples.

## Results

On MM-Vet (200 images, 218 open-ended questions), GPT-4V achieves the highest total score of 67.7%, substantially outperforming all open-source LMMs and tool-using agents. Among LLM-tool-using systems, MM-ReAct-GPT-4 scores 44.6%, with particularly strong performance in OCR (65.7%), spatial awareness (56.8%), and math (69.2%). Among end-to-end tuned LMMs, LLaVA-13B (LLaMA-2) leads with a total score of 32.9%, followed by LLaVA-13B (V1.3, 336px) at 32.5% and LLaMA-Adapter v2-7B at 31.4%. Even the best model (GPT-4V) scores only ~68%, indicating substantial room for improvement in integrated multimodal capabilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4V (67.7%) dominates all other systems by a wide margin, outperforming the second-best MM-ReAct-GPT-4 (44.6%) by over 23 points and the best open-source end-to-end model LLaVA-13B (LLaMA-2) (32.9%) by over 34 points. MM-ReAct-GPT-4 excels in tool-dependent capabilities—OCR (65.7%), spatial awareness (56.8%), and math (69.2%)—thanks to external Azure OCR, dense captioning, and PAL tools, but lags GPT-4V in recognition (33.1% vs. 67.5%) and knowledge (29.0% vs. 56.2%). Among end-to-end LMMs, LLaVA-13B (LLaMA-2) leads in recognition (39.2%) and OCR (22.7%), while LLaMA-Adapter v2-7B is strongest in knowledge (31.4%) and language generation (33.4%), benefiting from its large-scale tuning data. The 7B→13B upgrade consistently improves performance (e.g., LLaVA-7B at 23.8% vs. LLaVA-13B at 26.4%), and switching from Vicuna to LLaMA-2 as the LLM backbone yields an additional ~6.5% gain. Bard (53.5% on a 168-sample subset) outperforms all open-source models but trails GPT-4V, with MM-ReAct-GPT-4 still beating Bard in OCR, spatial awareness, and math.

## Relation to Prior Work

MM-Vet departs from classic VL benchmarks (VQA, COCO Captions, TextVQA) that test one or two isolated capabilities, instead framing evaluation around the *integration* of six core VL capabilities (recognition, OCR, knowledge, language generation, spatial awareness, math) into 16 compound tasks. For the evaluation metric, it extends the LLM-as-judge paradigm established in NLP (MT-Bench, GPTScore, GPT-Eval) to the multimodal setting, using few-shot GPT-4 prompts to score open-ended outputs uniformly. Among concurrent multimodal benchmarks (MME, MM-Bench, SEED-Bench), MM-Vet is distinguished by its explicit capability-integration taxonomy, which enables per-capability diagnostic insights rather than a single aggregate score.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even GPT-4V achieves only ~68%, exposing a substantial gap in integrated multimodal reasoning that no current open-source model approaches (best open-source: ~33%). The per-capability breakdown is actionable: it shows tool-augmented agents (MM-ReAct-GPT-4) can close specific gaps (OCR, math, spatial) but still lag on core recognition and language generation, pointing to complementary improvement paths. Key limitations include the image-text-only scope, the dependency on GPT-4 as evaluator (cost and access concerns), and the small sample size (218 questions) that limits statistical robustness. Open questions include whether the capability-integration framework generalizes to video/audio modalities and whether the LLM-as-judge approach can be made model-agnostic as open-source LLMs improve.
