## Problem

In multi-armed bandits with offline data, the offline and online reward distributions may differ due to distributional shift. The paper asks whether a policy can exploit offline data to beat vanilla UCB when the distributions are close, while still matching vanilla UCB's guarantee when they are far apart. It first proves an impossibility result showing no non-anticipatory policy can achieve this without additional information, then resolves the question by introducing a valid bias bound as auxiliary input.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives (i) an offline dataset S consisting of T_S(a) i.i.d. samples per arm drawn from potentially shifted distributions P^(off), (ii) a valid bias bound V where V(a) ≥ |μ^(off)(a) − μ^(on)(a)| for each arm (set to ∞ when no knowledge is available, reducing MIN-UCB to vanilla UCB), and (iii) a confidence parameter δ_t. At each online time step it outputs a single arm pull. The offline sample sizes T_S(a) are arbitrary non-negative integers and need not be equal across arms, a generality not required by prior work such as ARROW-CB.

## Method

MIN-UCB computes two upper confidence bounds per arm at each step: the vanilla UCB_t(a) using only online data, and a warm-start UCB_t^S(a) that pools online and offline sample means and adds a radius term containing both a stochastic uncertainty component and a bias-correction term (T_S(a)/(N_t(a)+T_S(a)))·V(a). The policy selects the arm maximizing min{UCB_t(a), UCB_t^S(a)}, thereby adaptively using offline data only when the bias bound is tight enough and the offline sample size is large enough to make the warm-start UCB tighter. The instance-dependent regret bound features a saving term Sav_0(a) = T_S(a)·Δ(a)·max{1−ω(a)/Δ(a), 0}², where ω(a) = V(a)+(μ^(off)(a)−μ^(on))(a) captures both the magnitude and direction of distributional shift. The instance-independent bound is governed by a novel linear program (LP) whose optimum τ_* characterizes the worst-case allocation of online pulls across arms with heterogeneous offline sample sizes, yielding a regret of O(min{√(KT log(T/δ)), (√(log(T/δ)/τ_*)+V_max)·T}).

## Results

The paper's main contributions are theoretical regret bounds. The instance-dependent bound (Theorem 4.2) gives E[Reg_T] = O(Σ_{a:Δ(a)>0} max{log(T)/Δ(a) − Sav_0(a), Δ(a)}), where Sav_0(a) = T_S(a)·Δ(a)·max{1−ω(a)/Δ(a), 0}² quantifies the regret saving from offline data. The instance-independent bound (Theorem 4.9) gives Reg_T = O(min{√(KT log(T/δ)), (√(log(T/δ)/τ_*) + V_max)·T}) with high probability, where τ_* is the optimum of a novel linear program. Numerical experiments (K=4, Gaussian rewards, T=T_S=10000) confirm that MIN-UCB consistently outperforms baselines across all tested V and T_S values, with the largest gains when V is small (precise bias bound) and T_S is large.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIN-UCB is compared against three baselines: PURE-UCB (vanilla UCB ignoring offline data), UCBS (always uses the warm-start UCB incorporating offline data), and HUCB (HUCB1 from Shivaswamy & Joachims, 2012, which always incorporates all offline data). MIN-UCB strictly outperforms PURE-UCB when the valid bias bound V is small (indicating offline and online distributions are close), achieving lower regret by leveraging the offline samples. Crucially, when V is large (loose bound, offline data potentially misleading), MIN-UCB gracefully degrades to match PURE-UCB's performance, while both UCBS and HUCB suffer significantly higher regret because they unconditionally incorporate the biased offline data. MIN-UCB also outperforms HUCB across all tested settings, with the gap widening as V increases. The experiments further show that increasing T_S (offline sample size) amplifies MIN-UCB's advantage over PURE-UCB when V is small, but has negligible effect when V is large, confirming the algorithm's adaptive behavior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on multi-armed bandits with offline data (HUCB1 by Shivaswamy & Joachims 2012, MonUCB by Banerjee et al. 2022), which assumes the offline and online reward distributions are identical. The key departure is allowing P^(off) ≠ P^(on) and introducing a 'valid bias bound' V as auxiliary input, which enables the MIN-UCB algorithm to adaptively decide whether to incorporate or ignore offline data at each time step. The paper also positions itself against ARROW-CB (Zhang et al. 2019) for contextual bandits and online learning with advice frameworks, showing that those approaches cannot deliver the tight instance-dependent regret bounds achieved here. The impossibility result in Section 3 formally establishes that without the auxiliary bound V, no non-anticipatory policy can outperform vanilla UCB (Auer et al. 2002), delineating the precise information needed to leverage biased offline data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The impossibility result provides a clean negative answer to a natural question (can offline data always help?), while the tight regret bounds (matching upper and lower bounds for both instance-dependent and instance-independent settings) establish fundamental limits on how much offline data can reduce exploration. The instance-independent bound's dependence on a novel linear program characterizing worst-case arm pull allocations is a new analytical tool. Open questions include extending the framework to linear bandits, MDPs, and alternative distributional drift metrics beyond the mean-gap bound V. The practical impact is currently limited to the stochastic K-armed bandit setting with Gaussian rewards and a known bias bound, which constrains real-world applicability.
