## Problem

Prior empirical studies observed that wider neural networks suffer less catastrophic forgetting in continual learning, but these experiments were limited to widths up to 2048 and lacked a theoretical characterization of the width–forgetting relationship. The exact functional form of how width affects continual learning error in finite-width, nonlinear, variable-depth feed-forward networks remained unexplored, leaving open whether simply scaling width is a viable strategy for mitigating forgetting.

## Input / Output

The model consumes flattened rotated images (28×28 for MNIST/Fashion MNIST, 32×32 for SVHN, 20×20 for GTSRB) where each dataset is partitioned into five sequential tasks corresponding to rotations of 0°, 22.5°, 45°, 67.5°, and 90°. The output is a class label for the corresponding task. During inference on task t, the intermediate layers from the final trained model are paired with the input/output layers trained specifically for task t, and only the rows designated active for that task participate in computation.

## Method

The paper establishes a theoretical framework proving that the continual learning error between models trained on tasks t and t′ is bounded by O((t′−t)·L·2^L·λ̄·χ·(∏L_l‖A_{t,l}‖₂)·γ·W^(−β)·α^((1−2β)/2)), where W is width, L is depth, α is the row-wise sparsity fraction, and γ, β are data-dependent constants. The key mechanism is that width acts as a functional regularizer: wider networks exhibit 'lazy training' (smaller distance from initialization), which the paper formalizes as Assumption 4.3 bounding the relative weight change by γW^(−β). This is combined with a perturbation analysis (building on Neyshabur et al.) to propagate per-layer weight differences through the network, yielding the W^(−β) decay that implies diminishing returns. The framework also introduces row-wise sparsity—selecting a random fraction α of hidden-layer rows to be active per task—which further reduces forgetting when (1−2β)/2 > 0. Empirically, the authors validate these predictions by scaling hidden dimensions to 2^16 (65,536), far beyond the 2048 limit of prior work, on four rotated-image benchmarks using both SGD and Adam, and additionally test Wide ResNet architectures to check generalizability beyond MLPs.

## Results

On Rotated MNIST (1-layer FFN, SGD), Average Forgetting decreases from 37.7% at width 32 to 26.7% at width 65536, but the improvement plateaus sharply after width ~2^10 (1024), where AF is already 32.6%. On Rotated Fashion MNIST, the effect is even more pronounced: AF barely moves from 46.0% (width 32) to 44.6% (width 65536), confirming the theoretical prediction of O(W^{-β}) diminishing returns. The theoretical bound predicts continual learning error scales as O(tW^{-β} α^{(1-2β)/2}), with empirically fitted β=0.311 for MNIST and β=0.12 for Fashion MNIST, explaining the much weaker width effect on the latter. With row-wise sparsity (α=0.1), AF drops dramatically to 0.2% at width 16384 on MNIST, corroborating the sparsity prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing continual learning methods; instead, it characterizes how a single architectural parameter (width) affects forgetting. The key comparison is against prior empirical work (Mirzadeh et al., 2022a,b; Ramasesh et al., 2021) which tested widths only up to 2048 and observed a roughly linear improvement in continual learning with width. By extending experiments to width 65536 (2^16), this paper reveals that the linear trend breaks down: AF plateaus well before 2^16, contradicting the extrapolation from prior studies. The paper also compares across model depths (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 layers), showing that deeper networks exhibit higher forgetting, consistent with the exponential 2^L factor in the theoretical bound. A Wide ResNet ablation (Table 2) confirms the diminishing returns pattern in a CNN architecture, with AF plateauing around 40–41% for both SVHN and GTSRB at 20x width multiplier. The sparsity experiment (α=0.1) demonstrates that row-wise sparsity can reduce AF from ~28% to ~0.2% on MNIST at width 16384, validating the theoretical α^{(1-2β)/2} term and suggesting sparsity is a more effective lever than width alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the empirical observations of Mirzadeh et al. (2022a,b) and Ramasesh et al. (2021), who noted a positive correlation between model width and continual learning performance but limited their experiments to widths up to 2048 and provided theoretical analysis only for two-layer linear networks. It also leverages the well-established 'lazy training' phenomenon from the NTK and overparameterization literature (Nagarajan & Kolter, 2019; Zou et al., 2020; Chizat et al., 2019), formalizing the observation that wider networks move less from initialization as a key assumption. The paper departs from prior work by providing one of the first theoretical frameworks for continual learning error in nonlinear, variable-depth FFNs, and by extending empirical validation to widths of 2^16, far beyond what had been previously explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that width yields diminishing returns for continual learning challenges the common heuristic that simply scaling model width is a straightforward remedy for catastrophic forgetting, suggesting that practitioners should consider complementary strategies (sparsity, architectural changes) rather than relying on width alone. The theoretical framework, while relying on an unproven assumption about distance from initialization and restricted to FFNs, provides the first provable guarantees connecting width, sparsity, depth, and number of tasks to continual learning error. Open questions include whether similar diminishing returns apply to depth, CNN channel counts, or LLM hidden dimensions, and whether combining width scaling with other functional regularization methods can overcome the plateau.
