## Problem

Despite many recent works on learning with 3D rotations, no comprehensive guide exists that answers which SO(3) representation is suitable for gradient-based neural network regression, considering both rotations in the model's input and output. The paper consolidates insights on how discontinuities, double cover, and metric choice in the mapping g: SO(3)→R affect learnability, and provides principled recommendations grounded in Lipschitz continuity analysis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240614/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For rotation estimation, inputs are point cloud pairs (3000 points from ModelNet airplane CAD models), 64×64 RGB cube images rendered in MuJoCo, or single RGB-D images (YCB-Video, 21 objects); outputs are d-dimensional rotation representation vectors (d∈{3,4,6,9}) that are projected to SO(3) via SVD or Gram-Schmidt orthonormalization. For feature prediction, the input is a rotation representation vector and the output is a rendered 64×64 image or a scalar Fourier-series value. Losses are computed either in representation space (MSE, MAE, cosine distance) or in SO(3) space (chordal or geodesic distance after mapping through f).

## Method

The paper's core theoretical contribution is showing that for dim(R)≤4, the map g: SO(3)→R must be discontinuous (SO(3) is not homeomorphic to any subset of R⁴), which makes the composed target function h*=g∘h̃ non-Lipschitz and causes gradient blow-up during training. It demonstrates that common fixes—distance-picking metrics, computing losses in SO(3), and half-space maps—do not eliminate this fundamental discontinuity, though half-space maps are valid for small-angle regimes. The paper then analyzes why R⁹+SVD outperforms R⁶+GSO: SVD distributes noise across all three column vectors (acting as an implicit ensemble) and maintains balanced gradient magnitudes, whereas GSO relies almost entirely on the first vector. Empirical validation across four tasks (point cloud alignment, cube rotation estimation, 6D pose estimation on YCB-Video, and Fourier-series feature prediction) confirms R⁹+SVD as the preferred default, with R⁶+GSO as a lighter alternative and quaternions with half-space maps viable only for small-angle or memory-constrained settings.

## Results

On the YCB-Video 6D pose estimation benchmark (21 objects), R⁶+GSO achieves 94.7% <2cm and 91.2% AUC, R⁹+SVD achieves 94.6% <2cm and 91.1% AUC, and quaternions (per-pixel) achieve 94.7% <2cm and 91.2% AUC, while Euler angles lag at 93.3% <2cm and 90.8% AUC. On point cloud alignment (ModelNet, 726 airplane models), R⁹+SVD performs best followed by R⁶+GSO, with low-dimensional representations (quaternions, Euler angles, exponential coordinates) showing notably larger errors. On the cube rotation estimation task, R⁹+SVD and R⁶+GSO outperform all 3D/4D representations, and for feature prediction (rotation→image), R⁹+SVD shows superior pixel-MSE performance over R⁶+GSO and augmented quaternions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four rotation representations across four experiments. On YCB-Video pose estimation, R⁶+GSO (Zhou et al., 2019) and R⁹+SVD (Levinson et al., 2020) perform nearly identically (94.7% vs 94.6% <2cm), both clearly outperforming Euler angles by ~1.4% <2cm. Quaternions with per-pixel prediction match the high-dimensional methods on YCB-Video, which the authors attribute to the dataset containing predominantly small-angle rotations where double-cover discontinuities are less impactful. On point cloud alignment (ModelNet) and cube rotation estimation, R⁹+SVD consistently outperforms R⁶+GSO, which in turn outperforms quaternions with half-space maps (Brégier, 2021) and all other low-dimensional representations. The paper's key finding is that R⁹+SVD should be the preferred default for rotation estimation, with R⁶+GSO as a viable alternative under memory constraints, while quaternions with half-space maps remain practical specifically for small-angle regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a unifying survey and empirical guide that consolidates the rotation-representation literature initiated by Zhou et al. (2019, R^6+GSO), Levinson et al. (2020, R^9+SVD analysis), Brégier (2021, manifold regression), and Pepe et al. (2022, geometric algebra/rotors). It departs from prior work by explicitly framing the analysis through Lipschitz continuity of the inverse map g: SO(3)→R and by extending the discussion to the underexplored case of rotations in the model's input (feature prediction), which most prior studies addressed only for rotation estimation. Experimentally, it reuses the DenseFusion architecture (Wang et al., 2019) as a testbed and adds gradient-ratio analyses that go beyond the loss-curve comparisons in earlier papers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a clear decision framework: use R^9+SVD or R^6+GSO as the default for gradient-based rotation learning, and reserve quaternions with half-space maps for small-angle regimes. A key practical insight is that changing the loss metric (distance-picking, SO(3)-space distances) does not resolve the fundamental discontinuities introduced by double cover in low-dimensional representations. Open questions include whether the recently proposed antipodal-symmetric quaternion distributions (Peretroukhin et al., 2020) and projective manifold gradient layers (Chen et al., 2022) can close the remaining gap to R^9+SVD, and whether the conclusions hold for very large-scale or multi-modal rotation estimation tasks beyond the relatively small networks and datasets tested here.
