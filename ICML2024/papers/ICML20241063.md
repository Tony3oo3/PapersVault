## Problem

In real-time reinforcement learning for non-stationary environments, the prevailing assumption is that continually updating the policy minimizes the temporal gap between data collection and deployment, thereby minimizing dynamic regret. This paper challenges that belief by showing that strategically pausing policy updates—holding the policy fixed for a non-zero duration—actually yields a sharper upper bound on dynamic regret by better managing aleatoric uncertainty inherent in the environment's intrinsic time-varying behavior.

## Input / Output

The agent receives environment states and collects one trajectory per unit time, while also tracking the time index to determine when to forecast and update. Before the first episode, the agent pre-specifies the number of update phases M, their timing {t_m}, and their durations {G_m}, with hold durations N_m determined residually. The output at each time step is an action sampled from the current policy; during update phases the policy is iteratively refined via Natural Policy Gradient on forecasted future Q-values, while during hold phases the policy remains frozen. The forecasting step produces a predicted Q-value at the next update time as a weighted linear combination of past Q-estimates, with weights constrained in l2 norm.

## Method

The paper proposes a forecasting online RL framework (Algorithm 1) that alternates between policy update phases and policy hold phases. During each update phase, the agent forecasts the future Q-value at the next update time using a least-squares linear estimator over past Q-estimates, then optimizes the policy via Natural Policy Gradient with entropy regularization conditioned on that forecast. The key theoretical contribution (Theorem 5.8) decomposes dynamic regret into three terms—policy optimization regret, Q-function forecasting regret, and non-stationarity regret—and shows that the non-stationarity regret, governed by exponential-order variation budgets, is minimized by a non-zero hold duration N_m*. The optimal ratio between update and hold durations is characterized by a balance equation involving the learning rate, entropy parameter, and environment uncertainty constants (α_1, α_2, B_1^max, B_2^max). Empirically, the approach is instantiated as Forecasting Soft Actor-Critic (FSAC) for high-dimensional Mujoco environments, where the update frequency γ_f < 1.0 consistently outperforms continuous updating (γ_f = 1.0).

## Results

In the goal switching cliffworld, the forecasting Q method (FQ) recovers an optimal policy shortly after step 15,000 following a goal switch at step 10,000, while the reactive Q-learning baseline fails to obtain an optimal policy for the remaining steps. In the Halfcheetah and Swimmer Mujoco environments, FSAC (Forecasting Soft Actor-Critic) yields a higher average return than SAC in most cases across 36 hyperparameter configurations. Critically, the experiments confirm that γ_f = 1.0 (continuous policy updating) does not produce the best average reward; instead, a non-zero policy hold duration (γ_f < 1.0) consistently achieves higher returns, validating the theoretical prediction that pausing policy learning reduces dynamic regret.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two baselines. First, in the goal switching cliffworld, the reactive Q-learning method (Watkins & Dayan, 1992) is outperformed by the forecasting Q method: after the goal switches at step 10,000, Q-learning never recovers an optimal policy, while FQ identifies the optimal policy by approximately step 15,000, yielding substantially higher cumulative reward in the post-switch phase. Second, in the Halfcheetah and Swimmer Mujoco environments, FSAC outperforms the standard SAC baseline (Haarnoja et al., 2018) in average reward across most update frequency settings (γ_f ∈ {0.1, ..., 1.0}) and prediction lengths (l_f ∈ {5, 15, 20}). Notably, FSAC with γ_f < 1.0 (i.e., strategic pausing of policy updates) achieves higher average returns than FSAC with γ_f = 1.0 (continuous updating), which in turn matches or exceeds SAC's performance—demonstrating that the pausing mechanism, not just the forecasting component, is the source of the performance gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the time-elapsing MDP formulation from Lee et al. (2023) and the Q-value forecasting approach of Chandak et al. (2020b), combining them with Natural Policy Gradient (Kakade, 2001) and Soft Actor-Critic (Haarnoja et al., 2018) to propose a forecasting online RL framework. It departs from prior non-stationary RL work (Mao et al., 2021; Fei et al., 2020; Chandak et al., 2020a) that assumed continuous policy updating is optimal, instead showing that strategically pausing updates reduces dynamic regret by managing aleatoric uncertainty. The key conceptual departure is treating the update/hold ratio as a design variable with a non-trivial optimum, rather than defaulting to maximum update frequency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a non-zero policy hold duration sharpens the dynamic regret bound challenges the prevailing assumption in online RL that more frequent adaptation is always better, offering a principled way to balance conservatism against environmental drift. However, the theoretical guarantees rely on exponential-order variation budget assumptions that may not hold in all real-world settings, and the empirical validation is limited to simple non-stationary patterns (sinusoidal reward changes in Mujoco, a single goal switch in cliffworld). Open questions include how to tighten the forecasting error bound (the dominant term in the regret), whether the optimal pause ratio generalizes to more complex or adversarial non-stationarity, and how the framework scales to high-dimensional continuous control with multiple simultaneous environmental shifts.
