## Problem

Local steps in federated learning empirically improve generalization, but no prior work has analyzed their generalization benefits in heterogeneous FL settings from a feature learning perspective. Existing theoretical results either focus on optimization complexity (Woodworth et al., Levy) or restrict to homogeneous data (Gu et al.), leaving open whether and why local steps help a network learn distinguishing features when clients have different data distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240115/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Theoretically, the model consumes structured inputs composed of P patches per sample: one feature patch encoding the client's pattern-related feature vector, K₀ feature noise patches carrying adversarial negative signals, and one pure Gaussian noise patch in the orthogonal complement. In experiments, 32×32 CIFAR-10 images are partitioned across 8 clients using a non-IID protocol that creates heterogeneous features while preserving identical binary label distributions. The output is a trained one-hidden-layer CNN (theory) or ResNet-18 (experiments) whose test accuracy reveals whether confounding features were learned or merely memorized as noise.

## Method

The paper introduces a heterogeneous data model with two forms of heterogeneity: feature heterogeneity (each client's label depends on a distinct feature vector) and adversarial heterogeneity (clients with confounding features share the same negative feature-noise components, while clients with normal features have individual noise). Using a one-hidden-layer CNN with cubic activation, the authors prove that GD with global updates shrinks the positive signal for confounding features by a factor of 1/K upon gradient averaging while the negative signal retains its scale, causing the network to memorize noise patches instead of learning those features—yielding ~50% test error on confounding clients despite zero training error. In contrast, GD with local updates preserves the full positive-to-negative signal ratio within each client, enabling the tensor power method to amplify the correct feature in polynomial time. Under a milder noise regime, they further show that Local GD with one-shot model averaging also learns all features, whereas global GD requires polynomially many communication rounds.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with heterogeneous features (h=0.25, with augmentation), Local SGD (I=16) achieves 91.21% test accuracy versus 90.17% for Parallel SGD, a ~1% improvement. Without data augmentation, the gap widens to ~3% (80.64% vs. 77.73% at h=0.25). All methods reach at least 99.9% training accuracy, confirming that the test accuracy gap is purely a generalization effect. In the feature noise experiment (Figure 5), Local SGD (I=128) outperforms Parallel SGD across all noise magnitudes ρ ∈ {0.03125, 0.0625, 0.125, 0.25}, with the gap increasing as ρ grows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Local SGD (with I=8, 16, or 32 local steps) against Parallel SGD (equivalent to Local SGD with I=1, i.e., global updates every round) on a modified CIFAR-10 task with heterogeneous features. Local SGD consistently outperforms Parallel SGD in all four settings (with/without augmentation × h=0.25/0.5), with the worst Local SGD result exceeding the best Parallel SGD result across random seeds. The advantage is most pronounced without data augmentation (~3% gap) and at higher heterogeneity (h=0.5, no augmentation: 81.27% vs. 77.73%). In the feature noise experiment, Local SGD (I=128) beats Parallel SGD at every noise magnitude ρ, and the gap widens monotonically with ρ, confirming that local steps provide a robustness benefit against adversarial feature noise. The theoretical results (Theorems 1–4) formalize this: global GD achieves near-random test error (~50%) on clients with confounding features, while local GD achieves near-zero test error in all clients.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature learning framework of Allen-Zhu & Li (2022a,b) to the federated learning setting, using a refined version of their multi-view data model to introduce a novel form of adversarial heterogeneity in feature noise structure across clients. It departs from the optimization-focused analyses of Woodworth et al. (2020a) and Levy (2023), which showed that local SGD offers no or limited optimization benefits over mini-batch SGD in heterogeneous settings, by instead proving a generalization advantage. It also extends the homogeneous-setting generalization results of Gu et al. (2023a,b), which attributed local SGD's benefit to reduced sharpness, to a heterogeneous regime where the mechanism is fundamentally different: local steps preserve the relative scale of positive feature signals that global averaging would otherwise shrink.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical proof that local steps improve generalization in heterogeneous FL, filling a gap left by prior work that either focused on optimization (where local SGD is at best neutral) or restricted to homogeneous data. The adversarial heterogeneity model—where feature noise structure differs across clients—provides a clean mechanism explaining why averaging gradients can actively hurt learning of confounding features, with practical implications for when and how to use local steps. Limitations include the reliance on a one-hidden-layer CNN with cubic activation, a synthetic data model with specific parameter regimes, and moderate over-parameterization; extending the analysis to deeper networks, vanilla Local SGD with model averaging at each round, and more realistic heterogeneity patterns remains open.
