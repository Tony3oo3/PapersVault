## Problem

Graph-based approximate nearest neighbor search (ANNS) relies on routing heuristics to decide which neighbors to evaluate with exact distance calculations, but these heuristics lack formal theoretical guarantees and can produce unbounded estimation errors that degrade recall. This paper asks whether a probabilistic guarantee can be established for the routing step—ensuring that any neighbor closer than a threshold is explored with probability at least 1−ε—while still being fast enough to yield practical speedups.

## Input / Output

The method takes a query vector q ∈ ℝ^d and a pre-built graph index (HNSW or NSSG) augmented with PEOs metadata: per-edge extreme-value IDs, projected vectors in L subspaces, and a quantile lookup table. During search, the routing test operates on edge residuals e = u − v (not raw vectors), producing a binary pass/fail decision per neighbor that gates whether the expensive exact distance computation is triggered. The final output is the top-K nearest neighbors, with the routing layer reducing exact distance calls by 70–80% compared to vanilla graph traversal.

## Method

PEOs (Partitioned Extreme Order Statistics) partitions the d-dimensional space into L orthogonal subspaces and decomposes each edge residual e into a regular part e_reg (aligned with the normalized sub-vector directions) and a residual part e_res. In each subspace, it applies the Reverse CEOs technique—selecting the projected vector that maximizes |e_i^T a_j|—to obtain a normal-distributed estimator of the angle between e and q, then aggregates across subspaces to reduce variance. A threshold T_r(e) is derived from the CDF of a worst-case normal distribution N_min^{e,x}, and the test passes iff H(e) ≥ T_r(e), where H(e) combines the weighted subspace projections. The paper proves (Theorem 6.3) that this yields a (δ, 1−ε)-routing guarantee for ε ≤ 0.5, and shows that RCEOs is the special case L=1. Implementation exploits pre-computed extreme-value IDs, a projection table built once per query, a quantile lookup table, and SIMD to process 16 edges simultaneously, avoiding costly random memory accesses to raw vectors.

## Results

PEOs integrated with HNSW and NSSG reduces exact distance calculations by 70–80%, yielding a 1.6×–2.5× throughput (QPS) improvement over the vanilla graph indexes across six million-scale datasets (GloVe200, GloVe300, DEEP10M, SIFT10M, Tiny5M, GIST) at K=100. Against the leading routing method FINGER, PEOs consistently achieves 1.1×–1.4× higher QPS while using less index space (e.g., 12.13 GB vs. 31.15 GB on DEEP10M). On the 100M-scale DEEP100M dataset, PEOs (with L=2, M=16) delivers ~30% QPS improvement over HNSW with only 3% additional space overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PEOs (HNSW+PEOs) dominates all six baselines on recall-QPS across every dataset. Against vanilla HNSW, PEOs achieves 1.6×–2.5× higher QPS at the same recall level; against vanilla NSSG, the gain is even larger on high-dimensional datasets (GloVe300, Tiny5M, GIST) where NSSG+PEOs clearly outperforms HNSW+PEOs. Compared to FINGER (the strongest routing competitor), PEOs is 1.1×–1.4× faster in QPS while requiring substantially less index space (e.g., 12.13 GB vs. 31.15 GB on DEEP10M, a 2.6× reduction). FINGER and Glass both suffer from unbounded estimation errors, dropping below 30% recall on GloVe200, whereas PEOs maintains high recall thanks to its probabilistic guarantee. ScaNN and IVFPQFS (Faiss) are consistently outperformed by PEOs on all six million-scale datasets. On the 100M-scale DEEP100M, PEOs with a compact configuration (L=2, M=16) still delivers ~30% QPS improvement over HNSW with only 3% extra space, confirming scalability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes two LSH primitives—SimHash (Charikar, 2002) and Reverse CEOs (Pham, 2021)—as routing tests within graph-based ANNS, where prior routing methods (TOGG-KMC, HCNNG, FINGER, Learn-to-route) relied on heuristics without formal guarantees. The key conceptual departure is formulating routing as a probabilistic problem with an explicit (δ, 1−ε) guarantee (Definition 3.2), then showing that space partitioning into L orthogonal subspaces (PEOs) reduces the variance of the angle estimator relative to both SimHash and single-subspace RCEOs. FINGER (Chen et al., 2023) is the closest prior work, also using SimHash-style collision counting for routing, but without the probabilistic guarantee or the multi-subspace variance reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The probabilistic guarantee is the main contribution: it ensures that promising neighbors are explored with high confidence, preventing the catastrophic recall degradation observed with heuristic routing (FINGER drops below 30% recall on GloVe200). The 70–80% reduction in exact distance calculations translates to 1.6–2.5× throughput gains on HNSW/NSSG, which is practically significant as vector search underpins RAG and semantic retrieval systems. Open questions include the MIPS extension (formulated but not empirically evaluated), the space overhead (~1–2× index size) for low-dimensional datasets, and whether the isotropic-distribution assumption for L selection holds on highly anisotropic real-world data beyond the dimension-permutation remedy.
