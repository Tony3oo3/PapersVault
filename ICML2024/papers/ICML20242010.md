## Problem

Standard convergence guarantees for variational inequality (VI) solvers rely on strong monotonicity and Lipschitz continuity of the operator, assumptions that fail in key ML applications such as GAN training and multi-agent games. This paper provides the first convergence analysis for the projection, Korpelevich (extragradient), and Popov (optimistic gradient) methods under the much weaker combination of α-symmetric generalized smoothness and p-quasi sharpness, using carefully designed adaptive stepsizes.

## Input / Output

The methods require only oracle evaluations of the operator F at iterates, a closed convex constraint set U, and an initial point; no prior knowledge of the Lipschitz constant or smoothness parameters is needed for the clipped/backtracking variant. The adaptive stepsizes are computed online from the operator norm ‖F(h_k)‖ at the current iterate, making them self-tuning. The output is a sequence converging to the solution set U*, with explicit rates: linear for p ≤ 2 and O(k^{-2/(p-2)}) for p > 2, where p is the quasi-sharpness exponent.

## Method

The core mechanism is a family of adaptive stepsizes of the form γ_k = min{1/(4μ), 1/(K·‖F(h_k)‖^α), ...} that normalize by the operator norm, exploiting the α-symmetric property to bound ‖F(h_k) − F(u_k)‖ using only ‖F(h_k)‖ and ‖h_k − u_k‖. For the Korpelevich and Popov methods, the p-quasi sharpness condition ensures the stepsizes are uniformly lower-bounded, enabling application of the Robbins-Siegmund lemma for asymptotic convergence and explicit rate bounds. A key practical contribution is a clipping-and-backtracking variant (Algorithm 1) that separates the normalization from a tunable parameter β_k, yielding convergence rates O(exp(−k)) for p ≤ 2 and O(k^{-2/(p-2)}) for p > 2 without requiring knowledge of p, α, L₀, or L₁. The resulting rates improve upon the O(k^{-1/(p-1)}) bound of Wei et al. (2021) under more restrictive monotonicity and Lipschitz assumptions.

## Results

The paper establishes the first convergence guarantees for projection, Korpelevich, and Popov methods on generalized smooth (α-symmetric) VIs with p-quasi sharp operators. For p ≤ 2, both Korpelevich and Popov achieve linear (exponential) convergence: D₀² exp(−μ/(B_u^(2−p)) γ̲ K) and R₁² exp(−μ/(B_h^(2−p)) γ̲ K) respectively. For p > 2, both methods achieve a rate of O(k^(−2/(p−2))), which strictly improves upon the O(k^(−1/(p−1))) rate from Wei et al. (2021) under more restrictive monotonicity and Lipschitz assumptions. The projection method achieves asymptotic convergence and linear convergence to a 1-neighborhood of the solution set.

| Korpelevich | D₀² exp(−μ/(B_u^(2−p)) γ̲ K) (Thm 5.3) | D₀²(1 + μ γ̲ (D₀²)^((p−2)/2) k)^(−2/(p−2)) (Thm 5.3) |
| Popov | R₁² exp(−μ/(B_h^(2−p)) γ̲ K) (Thm 6.3) | R₁²(1 + pC(R₁²)^((p−2)/2) k)^(−2/(p−2)) (Thm 6.3) |

Where γ̲ is a lower bound on adaptive stepsizes, B_u and B_h are upper bounds on dist(u_k, U*) and dist(h_k, U*), and C = 2^(−p/2) min{½(2R₁²)^(−(p−2)/2), 2^(−p+2) μ γ̲}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary empirical baseline is the Korpelevich method with golden-ratio stepsizes from Böhm (2023). Across all three tested settings — (α≈0.09, p=2.1), (α≈0.66, p=4.0), and (α≈0.86, p=8.0) — the paper's adaptive stepsizes for all three methods (projection, Korpelevich, Popov) converge faster than the golden-ratio baseline. The theoretical rate O(k^(−2/(p−2))) for p > 2 also improves upon the O(k^(−1/(p−1))) rate established by Wei et al. (2021), which required stronger assumptions (monotonicity plus Lipschitz continuity). In the clipping-and-backtracking experiment (Figure 2), the backtracking Korpelevich method with β=1.0 and q=0.75 further accelerates convergence, and increasing α and p no longer degrades performance. On the 2D ring GAN training task, the first-order method with clipping demonstrates practical advantage over standard approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work on variational inequalities: (i) the relaxation of Lipschitz continuity to generalized smoothness assumptions, specifically the α-symmetric operator class from Chen et al. (2023) which subsumes (L₀,L₁)-smoothness and Lipschitz continuity, and (ii) the relaxation of strong monotonicity to structured non-monotonicity conditions, specifically p-quasi sharpness from Vankov et al. (2023) which generalizes strong monotonicity, strong coherency, and SP-MS. The paper extends the classical Korpelevich (extragradient) and Popov (optimistic gradient) methods by introducing adaptive stepsizes that normalize by the operator value at the current iterate, avoiding the need to know Lipschitz-type constants a priori. This departs from prior adaptive-stepsize approaches for VIs (e.g., golden ratio stepsizes of Böhm 2023, clipping of Gorbunov et al. 2022b) by providing the first convergence guarantees under the simultaneous combination of α-symmetric smoothness and p-quasi sharpness, and by separating the clipping mechanism from the stepsize parameter to enable practical fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first convergence guarantees for first-order methods on VIs under the weakest known smoothness assumption (α-symmetric) combined with structured non-monotonicity (p-quasi sharp), which is directly relevant to GAN training and multi-agent games where operators are neither Lipschitz nor monotone. The improved rate of O(k^{-2/(p-2)}) for p>2 over the prior O(k^{-1/(p-1)}) under stronger assumptions is a meaningful theoretical advance. However, the practical impact is tempered by the fact that the adaptive stepsizes still require knowledge of problem parameters (p, α, L₀, L₁) in their theoretical form, and the backtracking variant, while parameter-free, has not been extensively validated on large-scale ML problems. Open questions include extending the analysis to stochastic VIs and weak Minty conditions, and whether the adaptive stepsizes transfer to higher-dimensional or non-smooth settings beyond the 2D toy operators used in experiments.
