## Problem

Existing spatio-temporal prediction models are trained end-to-end on specific datasets and fail to generalize when the test distribution differs from the training distribution, a common scenario in real-world urban computing where data comes from different cities, sensors, or time periods. The paper addresses this by asking how to efficiently adapt a pre-trained spatio-temporal model to a new, unseen dataset without retraining the entire backbone, while bridging the distribution gap between pre-training and downstream data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D spatio-temporal tensor X ∈ R^{R×T×F} (regions × time slots × features), a road-network adjacency matrix, and multi-resolution temporal features (hour-of-day and day-of-week vectors). The prompt network produces a compact embedding E_pro ∈ R^{R×F×d'} that is prepended to a frozen spatio-temporal backbone (e.g., STGCN, GWN, MTGNN, PDFormer), which then outputs predictions for the next P time slots across all regions and features. The framework is model-agnostic: the same prompt network plugs into any existing spatio-temporal predictor without architectural modification.

## Method

FlashST introduces a lightweight spatio-temporal prompt network with two sub-components: (i) a context distillation module that Z-Score normalizes the input, projects it via a learned embedding, concatenates temporal context (hour/day embeddings) and spatial context (the d_r smallest nontrivial Laplacian eigenvectors of the road-network graph, mapped through an MLP), and (ii) a dependency modeling module that applies a gated residual transformation for temporal encoding and a single-layer graph convolution with residual connection for spatial encoding. A unified distribution mapping mechanism regularizes the resulting prompt embeddings via an infoNCE-based uniformity loss, pushing embeddings of different regions apart on the hypersphere so that pre-training and downstream data occupy a shared distributional space. During pre-training (300 epochs on four PEMS datasets), both the prompt network and backbone are trained jointly with a combined regression + uniformity loss; during prompt-tuning (20 epochs on the target dataset), only the prompt network is updated while the backbone remains frozen, reducing training time by 20–80% relative to full fine-tuning.

## Results

FlashST achieves the best performance across all four downstream datasets (PEMS07(M), CA-D5, ChengDu-DIDI, NYC Citi Bike) on all three metrics (MAE, RMSE, MAPE). On PEMS07(M), FlashST reaches MAE 2.59 / RMSE 5.25 / MAPE 6.54%, outperforming the strongest baseline GWN (2.67 / 5.40 / 6.62%). On CA-D5, it achieves MAE 13.26 / RMSE 21.83 / MAPE 21.49% versus GWN's 13.63 / 22.30 / 24.68%. The framework also reduces training time by 20–80% compared to end-to-end training, with prompt-tuning requiring only 20 epochs versus 100 for full fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlashST outperforms all 13 baselines on every dataset and metric. The closest competitor is GWN, which FlashST beats by 0.08 MAE on PEMS07(M) (2.59 vs 2.67), 0.37 MAE on CA-D5 (13.26 vs 13.63), 0.04 MAE on ChengDu-DIDI (2.31 vs 2.35), and 0.02 MAE on NYC Citi Bike (1.79 vs 1.81). MTGNN is the second-strongest baseline, with FlashST improving MAE by 0.11 on PEMS07(M) and 0.02 on ChengDu-DIDI. The gap widens substantially against older or less adaptive methods: TGCN is outperformed by 2.46 MAE on PEMS07(M) and 2.07 MAE on CA-D5, while MSDR shows the largest degradation on CA-D5 (15.31 vs 13.26 MAE). The model-agnostic experiments (Table 4) further confirm that FlashST consistently improves upon both the frozen pre-trained model (w/o Finetune) and full-parameter fine-tuning (w/ Finetune) across STGCN, GWN, MTGNN, and PDFormer backbones, with the largest gains over the no-finetuning setting (e.g., 8.07→2.68 MAE for STGCN on PEMS07(M)).

## Relation to Prior Work

FlashST repurposes the prompt-tuning paradigm from NLP and vision (Brown et al., 2020; Zhou et al., 2022) into the spatio-temporal prediction domain, where prior work has focused almost exclusively on end-to-end training or full-parameter fine-tuning of GNN/Transformer backbones. It builds on the pre-training literature for spatio-temporal data (contrastive learning, masked autoencoders) but departs from it by introducing a lightweight prompt network that captures spatio-temporal context via Laplacian eigenvectors, multi-resolution temporal features, and graph-based message passing, rather than learning task-specific representations from scratch. The unified distribution mapping mechanism, grounded in infoNCE-based contrastive objectives, is a novel addition that explicitly aligns pre-training and downstream data distributions—a problem prior spatio-temporal pre-training work has not addressed directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, model-agnostic recipe for adapting pre-trained spatio-temporal models to new cities or modalities with only 20 tuning epochs, reducing training time by 20–80% compared to full fine-tuning while matching or exceeding end-to-end accuracy. This is particularly relevant as urban computing scales to dozens of cities with heterogeneous sensor networks, where retraining from scratch is infeasible. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer forecasting horizons, higher-dimensional feature spaces, or non-traffic spatio-temporal domains (e.g., energy, epidemiology), and whether the modest absolute accuracy gains over strong baselines (e.g., 2.59 vs. 2.67 MAE) will widen with larger pre-training corpora. The authors also flag the potential of integrating LLMs as knowledge guidance as a future direction.
