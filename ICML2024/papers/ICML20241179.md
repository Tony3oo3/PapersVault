## Problem

Prior provably efficient interactive imitation learning algorithms in the agnostic (non-realizable) setting either require convexity of the loss with respect to policy parameters or rely on a strong 'small separator' assumption that only holds for a few special policy classes. The fundamental question is whether computationally and statistically efficient imitation learning is possible for general policy classes without such restrictive structural assumptions.

## Input / Output

The learner interactively selects states by rolling out its current policy in the MDP, queries an expert for action annotations on those states, and additionally draws perturbation samples from a covering distribution d_0 (which must satisfy a smoothness condition relative to all policy visitation distributions). The output is a mixed policy formed by averaging E base policies from the ensemble, returned by selecting a random round's ensemble. In practice, the covering distribution is approximated by uniform states collected from prior DAGGER runs, and the perturbation sample size is fixed rather than drawn from a Poisson distribution.

## Method

MFTPL-P maintains an ensemble of E base policies, each trained by an offline classification oracle on the accumulated expert dataset unioned with Poisson-sampled perturbation examples drawn from the covering distribution d_0 with uniform random action labels. The key insight is that this ensemble approximates an in-expectation version of Follow the Perturbed Leader (FTPL), whose deterministic regret guarantee carries over to the online IL framework even though the sampling distribution at each round depends on the current policy (unlike standard smoothed online learning). This yields sublinear regret for general policy classes under a σ-smoothness assumption, translating to Õ(μ²H²√(A ln B)/(ε²√σ)) expert demonstrations. BOOTSTRAP-DAGGER replaces the covering-distribution perturbations with bootstrap resampling of the accumulated dataset, eliminating the need for d_0 access while retaining the ensemble-based data collection that empirically drives its advantage over DAGGER.

## Results

Theoretically, MFTPL-P achieves a regret bound of Õ(√N · (A(ln B)²/(σK²))^{1/4} + √N · (A ln B/σ)^{1/4} + √(N ln(1/δ))) for general policy classes, translating to Õ(μ²H²√(A ln B)/(ε²√σ)) expert demonstrations for EstGap ≤ ε. Empirically, across 4 continuous control tasks (Ant, Half-Cheetah, Hopper, Walker2D) in both realizable and non-realizable settings, MP-25(15) and BD-25 consistently outperform DAGGER and Behavior Cloning, with the performance gap most pronounced in non-realizable settings. The practical variant BD-5 achieves performance on par with MP-25(15) without requiring access to the covering distribution d₀, at only 2× the running time of DAGGER.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DAGGER (the primary online IL baseline), MP-25(15) and BD-25/BD-5 consistently outperform across all 4 continuous control tasks in both realizable and non-realizable settings, with the gap most pronounced in non-realizable settings where the expert MLP is not contained in the base policy class. Against Behavior Cloning (the offline IL baseline), MFTPL-P and BD variants also win, and the theoretical comparison shows a μ²H² vs. H⁴ dependence on horizon, reflecting the advantage of interactive data collection. Against Li and Zhang (2022), MFTPL-P matches the same μ²H²/ε² sample complexity but removes the restrictive 'small separator set' assumption on the policy class, extending guarantees to general policy classes. A key ablation (Section 5.4) reveals that BD-5's improvement over DAGGER stems from ensemble-based data collection (querying states from diverse ensemble members) rather than from ensemble averaging at test time: naive supervised learning on BD-5's collected data matches BD-5's performance, while Bagging on DAGGER's data only matches DAGGER.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online imitation learning reduction framework of Ross et al. (2011) by designing MFTPL-P, which approximates an in-expectation Follow the Perturbed Leader algorithm using Poisson sample-based perturbations from a covering distribution. It departs from prior agnostic IL algorithms (Li and Zhang, 2022; Cheng et al., 2019) that required either a 'small separator' assumption on the policy class or convexity of the loss function, by providing guarantees for general policy classes under only a smoothness/concentrability condition. The practical variant BOOTSTRAP-DAGGER further removes the need for explicit covering distribution access by replacing Poisson perturbations with bootstrap resampling, drawing conceptual inspiration from ensemble methods in safe IL (Menda et al., 2019) but with a fundamentally different mechanism for generating diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provably efficient agnostic interactive IL algorithm for general policy classes, removing restrictive structural assumptions that limited prior theory to a few special classes. However, the paper itself identifies a fundamental limitation: Proposition 7 shows that the reduction framework cannot guarantee global optimality in the agnostic setting, and the guarantees are runtime-dependent. The practical finding that a simple 5-ensemble bootstrap variant (BD-5) matches the theoretically-grounded MFTPL-P performance suggests the covering distribution assumption may be less critical in practice than theory implies. Open questions include designing agnostic IL algorithms with runtime-independent guarantees and global optimality, which the authors argue may be as hard as agnostic policy search RL.
