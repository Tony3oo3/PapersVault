## Problem

Existing GNN-based approaches for multi-agent dynamical systems suffer from error accumulation in long-term autoregressive rollouts, insufficient expressivity for complex governing rules, and poor generalization when system parameters shift between training and test distributions. No prior method explicitly disentangles object-level from system-level contexts to handle these challenges simultaneously within a continuous-time framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241354/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of observed graphs G^1:T_obs (each graph encoding object positions/velocities and spatial edges) plus known system parameters (e.g., temperature, viscosity, interaction strength), split into a conditional part for context extraction and a prediction part for supervision. Output is a continuous trajectory function: a decoder maps latent ODE states to positions and velocities (or 3D atomic coordinates) at any arbitrary timestamp, rather than discrete autoregressive steps, enabling evaluation at prediction lengths of 12, 24, or 36 timesteps.

## Method

PGODE first constructs a temporal graph over all observations and extracts object-level contexts via multi-layer message passing with temporal embeddings and attention-based summarization. A separate network produces system-level contexts, trained to maximize mutual information with known system parameters while minimizing mutual information with object-level contexts (Jensen-Shannon estimator, adversarial training), yielding disentangled representations invariant to system changes. These hierarchical contexts generate per-object gating weights over K learnable GNN prototypes (each with relation and aggregation FFNs), forming a mixture-of-experts graph ODE: dz_i/dt = Σ_k w_i^k · ψ_a^k(Σ_j ψ_r^k([z_i, z_j])) − z_i. The entire model is trained end-to-end via variational inference, maximizing the ELBO of future trajectory likelihood while regularizing with the system-parameter and disentanglement losses.

## Results

On physical dynamics (Springs and Charged), PGODE achieves MSE of 0.070 (q) and 0.262 (v) at prediction length 24 (ID) on Springs, and 2.037 (q) and 2.648 (v) on Charged, outperforming the strongest baseline HOPE by 47.40% (ID) and 48.57% (OOD) in average MSE. On molecular dynamics (5AWL and 2N5C), PGODE achieves MSE of 2.910/3.384/2.904 (qx/qy/qz) on 5AWL and 1.960/2.029/2.119 on 2N5C at prediction length 24 (ID), again leading all baselines. The advantage is particularly pronounced under OOD settings, where PGODE's disentangled context mechanism provides the largest gains over baselines that lack explicit system-parameter modeling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PGODE outperforms all seven main baselines (LSTM, GRU, NODE, LG-ODE, MPNODE, SocialODE, HOPE) across all four datasets and both ID/OOD settings. The largest relative gains are over HOPE, the strongest competing graph ODE method, with 47.40% average MSE reduction (ID) and 48.57% (OOD) on physical dynamics. ODE-based methods (LG-ODE, MPNODE, SocialODE, HOPE) consistently outperform discrete RNN baselines (LSTM, GRU) and the original NODE, validating the benefit of continuous-time modeling. On molecular dynamics (Table 2), PGODE leads HOPE by approximately 10–15% in MSE at prediction length 24 (ID), with the gap widening under OOD. In the extended comparison (Table 5), PGODE also surpasses NRI, AgentFormer, and I-GPODE on physical dynamics, with particularly large margins over I-GPODE (e.g., 0.070 vs. 0.746 MSE on Springs q at 24 ID). The ablation study confirms that both the prototype decomposition (removing it degrades Springs q from 0.070 to 0.164) and the disentanglement loss (removing it degrades OOD performance) are critical components.

## Relation to Prior Work

PGODE extends the coupled graph ODE framework (Huang et al., 2021) by introducing two orthogonal innovations: (1) hierarchical context discovery that disentangles object-level and system-level representations via mutual information minimization, and (2) a prototype decomposition that casts the graph ODE as a mixture-of-experts model where learnable GNN prototypes serve as experts and context-derived weights act as gating. This departs from prior graph ODE methods (NODE, LG-ODE, MPNODE, HOPE) that apply a single shared GNN to all nodes and rely on autoregressive rollouts, and from prior MoE work that uses black-box routing functions, by grounding the routing in physically interpretable, disentangled contexts. The variational inference formulation with ELBO optimization follows the standard VAE paradigm applied to trajectory prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in dynamical systems modeling: how to maintain prediction quality when system parameters (temperature, viscosity, interaction strengths) shift between training and deployment. The disentanglement mechanism provides a principled way to isolate system-level effects from object-level dynamics, which is directly applicable to scientific ML settings where physical constants vary. However, the evaluation is limited to small-scale simulations (10 particles, small proteins), and the authors acknowledge the absence of physical equivariance as a limitation. Open questions include whether the prototype decomposition scales to systems with hundreds or thousands of interacting agents, and whether the mutual information-based disentanglement remains effective when system parameters are partially unknown or high-dimensional.
