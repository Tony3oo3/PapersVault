## Problem

Existing visual reprogramming (VR) methods place learnable noise patterns using a single pre-defined binary mask shared across all target samples, which the paper shows limits generalization and can even increase training loss for individual samples. The authors demonstrate that optimal mask configurations vary significantly across images (e.g., different cat breeds prefer different mask sizes), revealing that a one-size-fits-all mask is a fundamental bottleneck for VR's approximation error.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240210/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target-domain RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR/SVHN/GTSRB, 128×128 for others), resizes it to the pre-trained model's input resolution (224×224 for ResNet, 384×384 for ViT), and produces a class label via a frozen pre-trained classifier followed by a non-parametric label mapping. The key intermediate artifact is a sample-specific three-channel mask of the same spatial dimensions as the resized image, generated on-the-fly by a lightweight CNN conditioned on the input image itself.

## Method

SMM replaces the fixed shared binary mask with a lightweight 5- or 6-layer CNN mask generator that takes the resized target image as input and outputs a three-channel, sample-specific mask indicating where the shared noise pattern δ should be applied. A patch-wise interpolation module then upscales the mask from the CNN's reduced resolution (H/2^l × W/2^l) back to full image size by replicating each pixel value across a 2^l × 2^l patch grid, avoiding backpropagation through interpolation. The element-wise product of δ and the generated mask is added to the resized image before feeding it into the frozen pre-trained model. Theoretically, the paper proves that SMM's hypothesis space is a strict superset of shared-mask VR's, guaranteeing lower approximation error (Proposition 4.3), while the mask generator adds less than 0.2% extra parameters relative to the pre-trained backbone. The three-channel design (rather than single-channel) allows per-channel noise modulation, which is particularly beneficial for datasets with limited color variation like GTSRB and Flowers102.

## Results

SMM achieves 52.53% average top-1 accuracy across 11 datasets with ResNet-18 (vs. 46.85% for the best shared-mask baseline, Full watermarking) and 56.35% with ResNet-50 (vs. 52.10%). On ViT-B32, SMM reaches 72.4% average accuracy, outperforming the best baseline (Medium watermarking at 65.2%) by 7.2 points. The largest gains appear on domain-shifted datasets: Flowers102 improves by +15.5% (ResNet-18) and +21.8% (ViT), Food101 by +15.4% (ViT), and SUN397 by +7.3% (ViT). SMM is the only method to exceed 80% on SVHN (84.4% ResNet-18) and 92% on EuroSAT (92.2% ResNet-18).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMM outperforms all four shared-mask baselines (Pad, Narrow, Medium, Full) on 10 of 11 datasets with ResNet-18, losing only on DTD where the padding-based Pad method (35.3%) edges out SMM (33.6%) due to texture-sensitive features being disrupted by watermarking noise. Against the Full watermarking baseline (Wang et al., 2022), SMM gains +5.7 points on average (ResNet-18) and +10.3 points on ViT-B32, with the largest margin on Flowers102 (+15.5% ResNet-18, +23.2% ViT). Compared to the Narrow and Medium watermarking variants (Bahng et al., 2022), SMM consistently leads, particularly on low-data or high-domain-shift tasks like GTSRB (+3.6% over Full) and EuroSAT (+7.9% over Full). In the appendix comparison with LoRA fine-tuning (Hu et al., 2021) on ViT-Large, SMM achieves 90.0% average on 32×32 inputs versus 77.9% for LoRA, while matching LoRA (83.5% vs. 83.4%) on 128×128 inputs, demonstrating VR's advantage when input resolution differs substantially from the pre-trained model's training resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual reprogramming (VR) lineage initiated by adversarial reprogramming (Elsayed et al., 2018) and developed through padding-based methods (Tsai et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2023) and watermarking (Bahng et al., 2022). The key departure is replacing the fixed, shared binary mask that all prior VR methods use with sample-specific three-channel masks generated by a lightweight CNN, combined with a patch-wise interpolation module for efficient resizing. The theoretical analysis draws on the PAC learning framework to formalize why the sample-specific hypothesis space strictly contains the shared-mask hypothesis space, yielding a lower approximation error guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single shared mask can cause individual samples' training loss to increase (Figure 2) provides a concrete diagnostic for a limitation that was previously only intuited, and the proposed fix is remarkably lightweight (<0.2% extra parameters) while delivering consistent gains across ResNet-18, ResNet-50, and ViT-B32 on 11 datasets. The method is model-agnostic and orthogonal to finetuning approaches, suggesting VR can be layered on top of LoRA or FC-layer finetuning for additional gains. However, VR remains ineffective on fine-grained tasks (StanfordCars, all methods <10%), and the paper acknowledges that when target tasks are substantially harder than the pre-trained model's domain, input-space modifications alone are insufficient. Open questions include how to more effectively combine VR with parameter-efficient finetuning and whether the sample-specific mask idea transfers to other modalities (text, speech) where reprogramming has also been explored.
