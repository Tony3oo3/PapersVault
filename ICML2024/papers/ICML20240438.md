## Problem

Spiking neural networks remain vulnerable to adversarial perturbations, limiting their deployment in safety-critical applications. Prior robustness work for SNNs focused on discrete spike outputs, weight constraints, or manual tuning of the leaky factor, without a principled dynamical-systems perspective on how perturbations propagate through continuous membrane potentials. This paper asks how to design stable neuronal dynamics that inherently resist perturbation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240438/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 32×32 RGB images from CIFAR-10/100 using direct (constant) encoding over T=8 time steps, and outputs a class label. During training, both the clean image and a perturbed version (Gaussian noise or RFGSM adversarial example) are fed simultaneously into the SNN so that the membrane potential perturbation at the last spiking layer can be computed and minimized. The network architectures are SNN versions of VGG11 and WideResNet-16-4 with DLIF neurons replacing standard LIF neurons.

## Method

The paper derives the membrane potential perturbation dynamics (MPPD), a simplified linear recurrence ε[t] = λ·ε[t−1] + W·Δs[t] that tracks how input perturbations propagate through each layer without the discontinuous spike-reset effect, and proves it satisfies finite-gain L₂ input-output stability with gain γ = √(1/(1−λ))·‖W‖. To reduce this gain, a Dynamic LIF (DLIF) neuron is introduced with a trainable temporal parameter a[t] multiplying the leaky term, providing an additional degree of freedom orthogonal to weight optimization. The training framework minimizes a composite loss: a mixup of clean and perturbed cross-entropy task losses plus ρ·MS-MPPD (the mean square of membrane potential perturbation summed over all neurons and time steps in the last spiking layer). Training uses spatio-temporal backpropagation (STBP) with a triangle surrogate gradient, and the framework is compatible with the SNN-RAT weight regularizer for further robustness gains.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the best configuration (DLIF, VGG11, AT+Reg, ρ=1.0) achieves 88.91% clean accuracy, 40.30% PGD-7 accuracy, 37.53% PGD-10 accuracy, and 39.85% APGD-10-DLR accuracy. On CIFAR-100, the same configuration reaches 66.33% clean accuracy, 24.25% PGD-7 accuracy, and 24.21% APGD-10-DLR accuracy. The WRN16 variant on CIFAR-10 with AT+Reg and ρ=1.0 achieves 38.78% PGD-7 and 37.39% APGD-10-DLR. Minimizing MS-MPPD (ρ=1 vs ρ=0) consistently improves robustness across all attack types, e.g., PGD-10 on CIFAR-10 VGG11 rises from 32.01% to 37.53%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior SNN robustness methods: HIRE-SNN (Kundu et al., ICCV 2021) and SNN-RAT (Ding et al., NeurIPS 2022). On CIFAR-10, the proposed DLIF+AT+Reg (ρ=1.0) achieves 40.30% PGD-7 accuracy, substantially outperforming SNN-RAT (21.16%) by ~19 points and HIRE-SNN (5.3%) by ~35 points. On CIFAR-100, the same configuration reaches 24.25% PGD-7, exceeding SNN-RAT (17.81%) by ~6.4 points and HIRE-SNN (2.9%) by ~21 points. The WRN16 variant further narrows the gap on CIFAR-100, with SNN-RAT's 17.81% PGD-7 vs. the paper's 19.22% (WRN16, AT+Reg, ρ=1.0). The paper also shows that the DLIF neuron alone (without MS-MPPD minimization) provides a modest robustness boost over vanilla LIF, and that combining adversarial training with the MS-MPPD loss (ρ=1) yields the largest gains over the ρ=0 ablation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior SNN robustness work (HIRE-SNN, SNN-RAT, Liang et al.) that focused on discrete spike outputs, weight constraints, or Lipschitz-based regularization, by instead analyzing the continuous membrane potential dynamics under perturbation. It draws on nonlinear control theory (Khalil's L2 input-output stability) to formalize how perturbations propagate through LIF neurons, and builds on the observation by Sharmin et al. and El-Allami et al. that the leaky factor governs robustness by making it a trainable per-timestep parameter in the proposed DLIF neuron (inspired by PLIF, Fang et al. 2021). The key conceptual shift is replacing spike-based distance metrics (TASAD, STD) with a continuous membrane potential perturbation metric (MS-MPPD) that provably satisfies L2 stability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first L2 stability guarantee for SNN perturbation dynamics, giving a principled theoretical lens for understanding and improving SNN robustness beyond empirical adversarial training. The MS-MPPD metric's demonstrated superiority over discrete spike-based metrics suggests that continuous internal-state supervision is a more effective robustness signal, which could inform future SNN training objectives. However, the L2 gain bound is acknowledged as loose, experiments are limited to CIFAR-10/100 with 8 timesteps, and it remains open whether the stability framework composes cleanly across many layers or scales to larger datasets and more complex tasks like video or control.
