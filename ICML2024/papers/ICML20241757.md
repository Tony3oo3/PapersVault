## Problem

Despite decades of work on GP-based Bayesian optimization, no algorithm with guaranteed order-optimal cumulative regret existed for the noise-free setting, leaving a COLT open problem unresolved. Additionally, all prevailing GP-based methods (GP-UCB, BPE, GP-ThreDS) require solving an expensive non-convex acquisition function optimization at every iteration, creating a significant computational bottleneck that worsens as the desired regret accuracy increases.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a black-box function f belonging to an RKHS with known kernel k over a compact domain X ⊂ R^d, and a total query budget T. At each step it observes either the exact function value (noise-free) or a value corrupted by R-sub-Gaussian noise. The key structural difference from standard GP-BO is that query points are drawn i.i.d. from a uniform distribution over the current active region rather than being chosen by maximizing an acquisition function, making the exploration schedule entirely non-adaptive and pre-determined by the epoch structure.

## Method

REDS (Random Exploration with Domain Shrinking) proceeds in epochs, sampling N_r points uniformly at random from a shrinking active region X_r at each epoch r, then using GP posterior UCB/LCB bounds to eliminate regions that cannot contain the global maximizer. The core theoretical contribution is a novel concentration bound showing that the spectrum of the sample covariance operator in an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space concentrates to that of the true covariance operator, establishing that n random points achieve worst-case posterior variance Õ(γ_n/n) (noisy) and Õ(n^{1−β}) (noise-free), matching the order-optimal rate of maximum posterior variance sampling. This spectral concentration is proven by separately controlling the head and tail of the spectrum using Matrix-Chernoff, non-commutative Khintchine, and Bernstein inequalities. Under noise-free feedback, REDS achieves regret Õ(max{T^{(3−β)/2}, 1}), closing the gap to the known lower bound and resolving the COLT open problem; under noisy feedback it achieves Õ(√(Tγ_T)). The non-adaptive random sampling eliminates the per-iteration acquisition function optimization, yielding a ~15× speedup over GP-ThreDS and ~100× over BPE in wall-clock time without sacrificing regret performance.

## Results

REDS achieves order-optimal cumulative regret of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\max\{T^{(3-\beta)/2}, 1\})$ in the noise-free setting (matching the lower bound of Tuo & Wang, 2020) and $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{T\gamma_T})$ in the noisy setting. Empirically, on three standard benchmark functions (Branin, Hartmann-4D, Hartmann-6D) with T=1000 queries, REDS achieves cumulative regret comparable to BPE and GP-ThreDS while running approximately 15× faster than GP-ThreDS and 100× faster than BPE (e.g., 0.32s vs. 4.37s vs. 29.84s on Branin).

## Baselines & Comparisons

REDS is compared against two state-of-the-art algorithms with order-optimal regret guarantees: BPE (Li & Scarlett, AISTATS 2022) and GP-ThreDS (Salgia et al., NeurIPS 2021). On cumulative regret (Figure 1), REDS performs comparably to both baselines across all three benchmark functions, confirming that non-adaptive random sampling matches the regret of adaptive acquisition-function-based methods. On runtime, REDS dominates decisively: it is ~15× faster than GP-ThreDS (0.32s vs. 4.37s on Branin; 1.19s vs. 19.33s on Hartmann-6D) and ~100× faster than BPE (0.32s vs. 29.84s on Branin; 1.19s vs. 119.71s on Hartmann-6D). The speedup stems from REDS bypassing the expensive non-convex acquisition function maximization required at each iteration by BPE and GP-ThreDS, replacing it with uniform random sampling from the current active region.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant GP-UCB/BPE/GP-ThreDS paradigm in which each query point is chosen by maximizing a non-convex acquisition function, replacing that adaptive step with non-adaptive random sampling from the current active region. It builds directly on the domain-shrinking framework of Li & Scarlett (2022) and Salgia et al. (2021), but the theoretical core—concentration bounds for the spectrum of infinite-dimensional sample covariance operators in RKHS—is distinct from the scattered-data interpolation techniques used to analyze maximum posterior variance sampling. The result positions random exploration as a universal alternative to MPV that holds over all compact domains without the regularity assumptions on domain boundaries required by Sobolev-space interpolation bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The order-optimal regret guarantee for REDS partially resolves a COLT open problem on noise-free kernel bandits, and the 15×–100× runtime speedup over BPE and GP-ThreDS is practically significant for BO practitioners who face the acquisition-function bottleneck at scale. Open limitations include Assumption 4.2 (bi-Lipschitz regularity of UCB level sets), the requirement to sample disjoint regions separately, and empirical validation restricted to three benchmark functions in 2–6 dimensions with 10 Monte Carlo runs. Whether the speedup advantage persists in higher-dimensional or batched settings, and whether Assumption 4.2 can be removed with refined analysis, remain open questions.
