## Problem

Unlearnable examples (UEs) add subtle, bounded perturbations to training images that drive model accuracy near random guessing, yet existing defenses are either computationally prohibitive (adversarial training) or lack universality across attack types (simple compressions like JPEG or grayscale). The paper addresses the need for a unified, efficient pre-training purification method that removes diverse UEs without requiring external clean data or any training-phase interventions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242379/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes unlearnable RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-subset) with their correct class labels as input, where perturbations are bounded by ℓ∞ (e.g., 8/255), ℓ2 (e.g., 1.0), or ℓ0 (e.g., 1 pixel) norms. It produces two outputs: purified training images ready for classifier training, and disentangled perturbation estimates p̂ that can be subtracted from the input. Notably, no ground-truth perturbations or external clean dataset is required; the D-VAE is trained entirely on the poisoned data in an unsupervised manner.

## Method

The core insight is that rate-constrained VAEs preferentially suppress UEs perturbations because the KL divergence bottleneck forces latents toward a standard normal, and Theorem 3.3 proves that features with high inter-class-to-intra-class variance ratio (i.e., highly predictive shortcuts) incur the largest estimation error under this constraint. Building on this, D-VAE extends a standard VAE with an auxiliary decoder D_θp and learnable class-wise embeddings u_y that jointly predict the perturbation p̂ = D_θp(u_y + z), trained by minimizing ‖(x − x̂) − p̂‖² alongside the reconstruction and rate-constraint losses. The two-stage purification framework first trains D-VAE with a small kld_target to aggressively push perturbations into the residual x − x̂, then subtracts p̂; a second stage with a larger kld_target refines the reconstruction to produce the final purified images, balancing perturbation removal against preservation of useful image content.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, D-VAE achieves a mean clean test accuracy of 90.01% across 8 attack methods (vs. 94.57% clean), outperforming all baselines by a wide margin. On CIFAR-100, the mean accuracy is 66.20% (vs. 77.61% clean), and on the 100-class ImageNet-subset it reaches 75.00% (vs. 80.52% clean), representing only a ~4% drop from clean-data performance. The method is particularly strong against OPS (one-pixel shortcut) attacks, where adversarial training collapses to 9.48% while D-VAE recovers 88.95%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D-VAE consistently outperforms all five baselines across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-subset. Against adversarial training (AT, Wen et al. 2023), D-VAE gains ~15 percentage points on CIFAR-10 mean (90.01% vs. 74.68%) and ~15 points on CIFAR-100 (66.20% vs. 51.32%), while also avoiding the heavy computational cost of AT. Against adversarial augmentations (AA, Qin et al. 2023), the margin is even larger at ~25 points on CIFAR-10. Among pre-training methods, D-VAE beats the best ISS compression (JPEG, Liu et al. 2023) by ~7 points on CIFAR-10 (90.01% vs. 82.64%) and ~11 points on ImageNet-subset (75.00% vs. 64.21%). Against AVATAR (Dolatabadi et al. 2023), which requires a diffusion model trained on clean data, D-VAE leads by ~5 points on CIFAR-10 and ~11 points on ImageNet-subset, while needing no external clean data. Against LFU (Sandoval-Segura et al. 2023), which is limited to class-wise linear perturbations, D-VAE achieves a dramatic ~27-point improvement on CIFAR-10 (90.01% vs. 63.19%), largely because D-VAE generalizes to sample-wise attacks (EM, TAP, REM, SEP) that LFU cannot handle.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pre-training purification line of work initiated by Liu et al. (2023) with Image Shortcut Squeezing (ISS), which showed that simple compressions like JPEG and grayscale can mitigate unlearnable example (UE) attacks. The key departure is the discovery that rate-constrained VAEs outperform JPEG at equivalent PSNR levels for perturbation removal, supported by a theoretical analysis linking KL-divergence constraints to hyperplane shifts in a Gaussian mixture model. The D-VAE architecture further departs from prior purification methods (ISS, AVATAR, LFU) by introducing an auxiliary decoder with learnable class-wise embeddings to explicitly disentangle perturbations, eliminating the need for external clean data required by diffusion-model-based defenses. The two-stage framework (aggressive denoising followed by quality-preserving reconstruction) is a practical synthesis of these insights into a unified pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method achieves near-clean-data performance (only ~4% drop on ImageNet-subset) across 8 diverse UE attack types without requiring external clean data or training-phase interventions, making it a practical and efficient defense for data-centric AI pipelines. The dual capability of D-VAE to both purify and generate UEs (from just 1% of poisoned samples) opens an interesting detection and augmentation angle for partial-poisoning scenarios. Limitations include incomplete disentanglement for adversarial-poisoning-based UEs (TAP, SEP), empirical sensitivity to kld_target selection, and the method being restricted to perturbative availability poisoning rather than broader data poisoning. The authors note future work on unsupervised learning scenarios, and the broader question of whether rate-constrained generative models can serve as a general-purpose 'poison filter' across modalities remains open.
