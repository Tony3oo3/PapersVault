## Problem

Adapting state-of-the-art black-box LLMs (GPT-3.5, Gemini) to specific tasks is infeasible with existing methods because they require access to model parameters, high-dimensional representations, or output token probabilities—none of which are available through modern API services. Fine-tuning APIs, the only available alternative, introduce transparency, privacy, and cost concerns. No prior method can adapt these black-box LLMs without any internal model access.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text query and interacts with a black-box LLM exclusively through its generation API (no parameter or logprob access), while a small adapter (DeBERTa-v3-base/large, 0.1B–0.3B params) scores the generated candidates. At inference, the LLM produces n sentence-level candidates per beam at each step, and the adapter selects the top-k highest-scoring chains to yield a final adapted response. The adapter is trained with ground-truth labels, AI feedback from a stronger LLM (GPT-4), or a combination, and once tuned it transfers plug-and-play to other black-box LLMs without retraining.

## Method

BBOX-ADAPTER frames black-box LLM adaptation as sampling from an energy-based model: p_θ(y|x) = p_LLM(y|x)·exp(g_θ(x,y))/Z_θ(x), where the black-box LLM is fixed and a small adapter g_θ is the trainable energy function. Training uses a ranking-based NCE loss that maximizes the posterior probability of target-domain (positive) samples over source-domain (negative) samples, with spectral normalization to stabilize gradients. An online adaptation framework iteratively samples from the current adapted inference, updates positive and negative sets via human or AI feedback, and refines adapter parameters over T iterations, enabling self-improvement without ground-truth data. At inference, the LLM acts as a proposal generator producing sentence-level candidates while the adapter performs beam search as an evaluator, selecting the highest-scoring reasoning path.

## Results

BBOX-ADAPTER (Combined) improves gpt-3.5-turbo by an average of 6.39% across four QA benchmarks, with the largest gain of +6.77% on GSM8K (74.28% vs. 67.51% base). The method also reduces training cost by 31.30× and inference cost by 1.84× compared to Azure-SFT fine-tuning. On the ToxiGen toxicity-mitigation task, BBOX-ADAPTER halves the toxic output rate of Mixtral-8x7B from 41.90% to 20.60%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unadapted gpt-3.5-turbo base model (CoT), BBOX-ADAPTER (Combined) wins on all four datasets, with gains ranging from +5.68% (StrategyQA) to +6.77% (GSM8K). Against Azure-SFT (the OpenAI fine-tuning API), BBOX-ADAPTER wins on GSM8K (74.28% vs. 69.94%) and ScienceQA (79.40% vs. 79.00%) but trails on StrategyQA (72.27% vs. 76.86%) and TruthfulQA (83.60% vs. 95.00%), where SFT's direct parameter access provides a substantial advantage. Against LoRA fine-tuning on Mixtral-8x7B (Table 6), SFT-LoRA achieves higher accuracy (73.80–75.98% vs. 65.26–66.08% for BBOX-ADAPTER) but requires 208 GiB of training VRAM versus only 105 GiB for BBOX-ADAPTER, making the latter more resource-efficient. The plug-and-play experiment (Table 3) further shows that an adapter trained on gpt-3.5-turbo transfers to davinci-002 (+6.85% average) and Mixtral-8x7B (+4.50% average) without retraining, a capability unavailable to SFT-based baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the grey-box LLM adaptation line (LMaaS, kNN-Adapter, CombLM, Proxy-Tuning) by removing the requirement for output token probabilities, which are inaccessible in modern black-box LLMs like GPT-3.5-turbo and Gemini. It repurposes ranking-based Noise Contrastive Estimation (originally from Ma & Collins, 2018, for unnormalized model training) as a loss that distinguishes target-domain data from source-domain generations, framing the entire adaptation process as sampling from an energy-based model where a small LM (DeBERTa) serves as the energy function. The online adaptation framework with AI feedback draws on the RLHF/RLAIF literature but applies it to iterative adapter refinement rather than policy optimization, and the plug-and-play property (an adapter trained on one LLM transferring to another) departs from all prior adaptation methods that are tightly coupled to a specific model's parameters or logits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BBOX-ADAPTER addresses a practical gap: adapting state-of-the-art black-box LLMs without uploading data to external APIs (privacy), without access to internal weights or token probabilities (transparency), and at a fraction of the cost of fine-tuning services (31× cheaper training). The plug-and-play transferability across architectures is a notable property with potential for reducing adaptation costs in multi-model deployments. However, a persistent performance gap versus SFT remains (e.g., 71.62 vs. 76.86 on StrategyQA), and the evaluation is limited to four QA-style datasets; whether the approach generalizes to open-ended generation, code, or multi-turn dialogue remains open. The toxicity-reduction experiment (halving toxic outputs on ToxiGen) hints at broader safety applications but is underexplored.
