## Problem

Deep learning architecture design is a resource-demanding process plagued by a vast combinatorial design space, long prototyping cycles, and high compute costs for at-scale validation. There is no principled pipeline to predict whether a new architecture will scale well, and it remains unclear whether small-scale synthetic tasks can serve as reliable proxies for compute-optimal scaling performance, or whether hybrid topologies combining heterogeneous computational primitives (attention, gated convolutions, recurrences) can outperform uniform Transformer designs.

## Input / Output

The MAD pipeline takes candidate architecture designs (combinations of sequence-mixing primitives such as attention, Hyena, Mamba, and GLA, paired with channel-mixers like SwiGLU or MoE, arranged in sequential, striped, or sparse-parallel topologies) and evaluates them on six synthetic token manipulation tasks at small scale (2-block, 4-layer models with width 128, iso-state normalized to 4096). The output is a per-task accuracy score and aggregate MAD ranking for each architecture. For validation, the top architectures are then trained as language models on The Pile (and DNA sequences) across 500+ configurations from 70M to 7B parameters, producing compute-optimal and state-optimal perplexity curves as a function of FLOP budget.

## Method

The paper introduces Mechanistic Architecture Design (MAD), a two-stage pipeline: (1) small-scale evaluation of 21 candidate architectures on six curated synthetic tasks (in-context recall, fuzzy recall, noisy recall, selective copying, compression, memorization) that probe specific token-manipulation capabilities, with iso-state normalization and hyperparameter sweeps to ensure fair comparison; and (2) large-scale compute-optimal and state-optimal scaling law analysis on The Pile to validate MAD rankings. The key architectural insight is that striped hybrid topologies—sequentially interleaving blocks of different computational primitives (e.g., 25% attention with 75% Hyena or Mamba)—outperform all non-striped architectures, with the optimal hybridization ratio and layer placement determined empirically. The paper further introduces a state-optimal scaling analysis (perplexity vs. total state dimension, P* ∝ M^{-0.28}) and demonstrates that aggregate MAD scores are linearly correlated with compute-optimal perplexity at scale, enabling fast architecture iteration without full pretraining runs.

## Results

Across 500+ models trained from 70M to 7B parameters on The Pile, StripedHyena (25% attention / 75% Hyena) achieves up to 20% lower compute-optimal perplexity than Transformer++ at the same FLOP budget. On MAD synthetic tasks, striped architectures yield an average +8.1% accuracy gain over non-striped baselines, head expansion adds +2.3%, and sparse MoE channel mixers add +1.7%. The state-optimal scaling exponent is c ≈ −0.28 (P* ∝ M^c), consistent across all architectures, and aggregate MAD scores are linearly rank-correlated with compute-optimal perplexity at scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Transformer++ (Llama 2-style), StripedHyena at the 25% attention ratio achieves lower perplexity at every IsoFLOP group from 4e18 to 2e21 FLOPs, with the gap widening at larger budgets; the off-frontier (overtrained) perplexity gap is also smaller for hybrids, making them more robust when training smaller models for longer. Against pure Hyena, adding attention layers (striping) and heads consistently improves both MAD accuracy and compute-optimal perplexity, confirming that Hyena's weakness on in-context recall is compensated by attention. Against Mamba, StripedMamba with interleaved attention outperforms the pure Mamba baseline in scaling, though Mamba retains an edge on the compression MAD task. Against Multi-Head Hyena, the full StripedHyena-Experts+MoE architecture (with sparse routing in both sequence and channel mixers) achieves the best perplexity, validating the MAD pipeline's iterative design. Against Gated Linear Attention, hybrid architectures that combine it with attention and Hyena outperform it on composite MAD metrics, particularly on recall and selective copying tasks where single-primitive models show specialization gaps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the compute-optimal scaling law framework of Kaplan et al. (2020) and Hoffmann et al. (2022) from Transformers to a diverse family of subquadratic architectures (Hyena, Mamba, Gated Linear Attention, and their striped hybrids), and introduces a new state-optimal scaling dimension. It builds on the mechanistic interpretability literature using synthetic token-manipulation tasks (associative recall, compression, copying) for interpretability or single-layer design (Fu et al. 2022, Poli et al. 2023, Arora et al. 2023), but repurposes these tasks as a systematic, end-to-end architecture design pipeline (MAD) rather than a diagnostic tool. The hybridization and sparsity ideas draw on mixture-of-experts (Shazeer et al. 2017) and block-state transformers (Fathi et al. 2023), while the state-expansion taxonomy (outer-product heads, mSISO) unifies design choices across Hyena, Mamba, and GLA variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MAD framework offers a practical path toward faster, cheaper architecture prototyping by showing that minutes-long synthetic task evaluations can rank architectures in a way that correlates with compute-optimal perplexity at scale — a finding validated across 500+ trained models and multiple IsoFLOP groups. The introduction of state-optimal scaling provides a new, inference-relevant axis for comparing architectures beyond FLOP cost. Key limitations include MAD's reliance on extrapolating from 2-block models to deeper architectures, its current restriction to perplexity as the target metric, and the fact that model rankings shift across domains (e.g., byte-level vs. sub-word tokenization, DNA vs. text). Open questions include whether MAD can be automated via search, whether it generalizes to downstream task performance, and whether the 25% optimal hybridization ratio holds for more complex topologies with three or more primitive types.
