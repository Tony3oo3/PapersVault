## Problem

Existing vision explainers, whether perturbation-based (LIME, GradCAM) or DNN-based (FastSHAP, ViT-Shapley), are constrained to explaining a single target model on a single task and cannot transfer across different models or downstream tasks without task-specific retraining. This makes deploying explanations in practical multi-task scenarios time- and resource-intensive, as a separate explainer must be trained for each task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242056/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TVE takes a 224×224 RGB image (or 32×32 for CIFAR-10) split into a 14×14 grid of 16×16-pixel patches, along with the task-specific linear classifier H_t of the target model. It produces a P×P (14×14) explanation heatmap in a single feed-forward pass, where each cell gives the attribution score of the corresponding patch to the predicted class. Internally, the explainer outputs two meta-attribution tensors of shape [P×P, D] (D=768 for ViT-Base, D=1024 for Swin-Base), which are then projected through H_t to yield the final per-patch scores.

## Method

TVE introduces meta-attribution: a task-independent tensor that encodes the importance of each input patch to every dimension of the backbone encoder's embedding vector, rather than to a specific task output. The explainer (Mask-AutoEncoder-Base backbone + 17 FFN head layers) is pre-trained on ImageNet to predict two tensors per patch—g_{k,z} (backbone output on the patch's 0/1/2-hop neighbors) and h_{k,z} (backbone output on all other patches)—via an L2 regression loss. At inference, a simple transfer rule adapts the meta-attribution to any downstream task by computing φ_{k,y,z} = log H_t(g_{k,z}; y) − log H_t(h_{k,z}; y), requiring no additional training on task-specific data. The paper provides a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) showing that minimizing the pre-training loss bounds the explanation error relative to the V-information-aligned attribution, with the bound scaling as 2ε/(1−ε) where ε shrinks as pre-training converges.

## Results

On the three downstream datasets (Cats-vs-dogs, Imagenette, CIFAR-10), TVE consistently achieves the highest Fidelity+ and lowest Fidelity- Sparsity-AUC across all three target architectures (ViT-Base, Swin-Base, DeiT-Base). For example, on Imagenette with ViT-Base, TVE reaches Fidelity+ of 0.33 and Fidelity- of 0.19, compared to ViT-Shapley's 0.25 and 0.25. On CIFAR-10 with Swin-Base, TVE achieves Fidelity+ of 0.31 and Fidelity- of 0.24 versus ViT-Shapley's 0.25 and 0.28. TVE also achieves ~100 image/s throughput, orders of magnitude faster than sampling-based baselines like KernelSHAP (~4.7 image/s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TVE outperforms all six baselines (LIME, IG, RISE, DeepLift, KernelSHAP/GradSHAP, and ViT-Shapley) on both Fidelity+ and Fidelity- across the majority of architecture-dataset combinations. ViT-Shapley, the closest DNN-based competitor, is consistently beaten: TVE gains +0.04 to +0.08 in Fidelity+ and reduces Fidelity- by 0.02 to 0.06 across all settings. Among sampling-based methods, LIME achieves competitive Fidelity+ on some settings (e.g., DeiT-Base on CIFAR-10) but performs poorly on Fidelity-, while KernelSHAP shows comparable Fidelity- to TVE but at a throughput of only ~4.7 image/s versus TVE's ~100 image/s. TVE's key advantage is that it requires no task-specific training data, whereas ViT-Shapley and the DNN-based baselines (FastSHAP, CORTX) must be trained per task. TVE also shows the smallest throughput degradation when scaling from ViT-Base to ViT-Large, since its latency depends on the explainer size rather than the target model size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the V-information framework (Xu et al., 2020; Hewitt et al., 2021) for defining feature attribution and the two-state approximation from Mitchell et al. (2022) for tractable computation, but departs from both local explainers (LIME, GradCAM, IG) and existing DNN-based explainers (FastSHAP, ViT-Shapley, CORTX) by introducing a task-agnostic 'meta-attribution' that attributes patches to backbone encoder embeddings rather than task-specific logits. The conceptual lineage draws directly from transfer learning in vision (frozen backbone + task-specific classifier), repurposing the architectural decomposition f_t = H_t ∘ G as the mechanism for attribution transfer. Unlike ViT-Shapley, which pre-trains on ImageNet but still requires task-specific classifiers aligned to the training distribution, TVE explicitly separates the reusable attribution knowledge (meta-attribution) from the task-specific projection (H_t), enabling zero-shot transfer to new downstream tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment: a single pre-trained explainer can explain multiple model architectures (ViT, Swin, DeiT) across diverse downstream tasks without any task-specific training, reducing the cost of generating explanations from O(tasks × models) to O(1) pre-training. The theoretical error bound (2ε/(1-ε)) provides a clean guarantee linking pre-training loss to explanation quality. However, the approach is constrained to tasks within the pre-training data distribution (ImageNet), and the MURA experiment shows that out-of-distribution domains still require full downstream training, limiting true zero-shot applicability. Open questions include whether the meta-attribution concept extends to non-vision modalities, whether it handles multi-label or open-vocabulary tasks, and how it performs when the backbone itself is not frozen during downstream fine-tuning at scale.
