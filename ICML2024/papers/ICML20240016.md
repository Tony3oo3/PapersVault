## Problem

Prior DP-OCO results either required smoothness of the loss functions (Agarwal et al., 2023) or achieved suboptimal dimension dependence in the privacy-dependent regret term. For non-smooth losses, the best known bound was √dT (Asi et al., 2023), missing the optimal √T leading-order term that is achievable in the non-private setting. This paper resolves both gaps simultaneously, delivering the first private regret bound with an optimal leading-order term for non-smooth losses and improving the dimension factor from d to √d in the privacy-dependent term.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a convex compact decision set K ⊂ R^d of diameter D, a sequence of G-Lipschitz convex loss functions l_t revealed adversarially but obliviously (independent of the iterates), and privacy parameters (ε, δ). It outputs a sequence of decision points x_t ∈ K, each sampled from a Gibbs distribution whose log-density includes both the cumulative loss and an L2 regularization term. In the lazy OCO variant, the algorithm additionally accepts a switching budget S and guarantees at most S expected switches while maintaining the same regret guarantee.

## Method

The core innovation is adding an L2 regularization term (λ/2)‖x‖² to the log-density of the Gibbs sampling distribution, making it strongly log-concave and satisfying the Log-Sobolev Inequality. This yields concentration of measure, so the stability of the algorithm—measured by the density ratio between consecutive Gibbs distributions—scales as O(√(β/λ)) rather than O(β) in the unregularized Private Shrinking Dartboard, enabling a significantly better dimension trade-off. The Wasserstein distance between consecutive Gibbs measures is bounded by G/λ via a projected Langevin diffusion coupling (Lemma 3.6), which underpins both the regret and privacy analyses. A rejection sampling mechanism (inspired by Geulen et al., 2010) limits the number of switches, and the privacy proof uses adaptive strong composition with per-step losses bounded through the density ratio and the Wasserstein bound. The regret analysis reformulates the problem in distribution space, applying the Gibbs variational principle to reduce the bound to a Follow-the-Regularized-Leader argument over the simplex of distributions on K.

## Results

The paper's main DP-OCO result (Theorem 4.4) achieves a regret bound of $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T} + \frac{\sqrt{d}\,T^{1/3}}{\varepsilon})$ for G-Lipschitz convex losses without any smoothness assumption, improving the previous best smooth-loss bound by a factor of $\sqrt{d}$ and matching the optimal $\sqrt{T}$ leading-order term for non-smooth losses for the first time. For Lazy OCO (Theorem 4.5), the algorithm achieves regret $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T} + \frac{\sqrt{d}\,T}{S})$ with at most S expected switches, again without smoothness or GLM restrictions. The key technical ingredient is sampling from a strongly log-concave Gibbs density with an added $\ell_2$-regularization term, which satisfies the Log-Sobolev Inequality and yields a stability ratio of $O(\sqrt{\beta/\lambda})$ instead of $O(\beta)$, enabling the improved dimension dependence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Agarwal et al. (2023), the prior best for smooth DP-OCO, this paper improves the dimension dependence in the privacy-dependent term from $d\,T^{1/3}/\varepsilon$ to $\sqrt{d}\,T^{1/3}/\varepsilon$ (a $\sqrt{d}$ factor) and extends the guarantee to non-smooth Lipschitz losses, where the previous result did not apply at all. Against Asi et al. (2023) (Private Shrinking Dartboard), which gave $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{dT})$ for non-smooth losses, this paper achieves the optimal $\sqrt{T}$ leading-order term for all $\varepsilon \geq d\,T^{-1/6}$ and improves the sub-leading term by a factor of $\sqrt{d}$ in intermediate $\varepsilon$ regimes. Against Kairouz et al. (2021), the earlier $d^{1/4}\sqrt{T}/\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ bound, this paper is strictly better for all $\varepsilon \geq d\,T^{-1/3}$. For Lazy OCO, the paper improves upon Sherman & Koren (2023) and Agarwal et al. (2023) by removing the smoothness requirement while matching their $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T} + \sqrt{d}\,T/S)$ rate, and improves upon Anava et al. (2015) and Chen et al. (2020) by achieving the optimal $\sqrt{T}$ leading-order term for sub-linear switching budgets where those methods only give $\sqrt{dT}$ or $T/\sqrt{S}$.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Private Shrinking Dartboard algorithm of Asi et al. (2023) and the authors' own prior FTPL-based DP-OCO work (Agarwal et al., 2023), but departs from both by replacing Follow-the-Perturbed-Leader with correlated rejection sampling from a Gibbs distribution augmented with an L2 regularization term. The key conceptual shift is borrowing the strongly log-concave sampling idea from private stochastic optimization (Gopi et al., 2022; Ganesh et al., 2023) and applying it to the online setting, where the L2 term makes the sampling density satisfy the Log-Sobolev Inequality, enabling concentration-of-measure arguments that decouple algorithmic stability from the temperature parameter β. The rejection-sampling mechanism for controlling switches follows Geulen et al. (2010), adapted here to the continuous domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first DP-OCO algorithm achieving the optimal √T leading-order regret term for non-smooth Lipschitz convex losses, and it improves the dimension dependence in the privacy-dependent term from d to √d, matching what was previously only achievable for the restricted GLM class. The same algorithm also yields improved lazy OCO bounds for non-smooth losses without any GLM restriction. Key open questions include whether the √d·T^{1/3}/ε rate can be further improved (e.g., to √d/ε as in the linear case), whether a separation between online and one-pass stochastic DP settings exists, and whether the techniques extend to strongly convex losses in the lazy setting.
