## Problem

Policies trained under constrained RL in a simulator often violate their constraints when deployed in a real environment due to model mismatch (sim-to-real gap, nonstationarity, adversarial perturbations). Existing robust constrained RL approaches are either heuristic without per-iteration guarantees (Russel et al., 2020; Mankowitz et al., 2020) or restricted to tabular MDPs without monotonic improvement (Wang et al., 2022), leaving a gap for scalable algorithms with theoretical guarantees on both reward improvement and constraint satisfaction under model uncertainty.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a state observation, an (s,a)-rectangular uncertainty set of transition kernels P, reward and utility functions r,c: S×A→[0,1], and a scalar constraint threshold d. For continuous state spaces, the transition kernel is parameterized via a Gaussian mixture model (or exponential-family parameterization for large discrete spaces) and the worst-case kernel is estimated by projected gradient descent within P. The output is a parameterized policy π_θ that maximizes the worst-case discounted reward over P subject to the worst-case discounted utility exceeding d, with per-iteration bounds on reward improvement and constraint violation.

## Method

RCPO is a two-step trust-region policy optimization that generalizes CPO to the robust constrained setting. Step 1 (robust policy improvement) uses a novel robust performance difference lemma (Lemma 4.1) that bounds the gap between robust value functions of two policies via their KL divergence, with a key approximation: the current policy's worst-case transition kernel p_k^r serves as a surrogate for the new policy's worst-case kernel, yielding a tractable local objective. Step 2 (projection) projects the intermediate policy onto the robust constraint set using a first-order utility advantage approximation, ensuring constraint satisfaction even when the current policy is infeasible. The theoretical analysis handles the non-stationarity of worst-case kernels across iterations via Lipschitz continuity of the robust value function and a change-of-measure argument, yielding explicit per-iteration bounds on worst-case reward improvement (Theorem 4.3) and constraint violation (Theorem 4.4) that scale with the trust-region radius δ.

## Results

The paper evaluates RCPO on four environments (Gambler, N-chain, Frozen-Lake, Point Gather) using convergence curves rather than a single summary table. In the tabular Gambler and Frozen-Lake problems, RCPO is the only method that consistently satisfies the worst-case utility constraint throughout training while achieving reward close to the unconstrained robust upper bound (RVI). In the deep Point Gather (Mujoco) task under Gaussian model perturbation, RCPO achieves substantially higher reward than all three non-robust baselines (CPO, PCPO, CUP) while maintaining constraint satisfaction; CUP violates the constraint so severely that its utility is not reported.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RCPO outperforms all five baselines on the key metric of worst-case constraint satisfaction: in the Gambler and Frozen-Lake problems, PCPO, R3C, CUP, and RVI all violate the utility constraint, while RCPO maintains satisfaction throughout training. On reward, RCPO matches the unconstrained robust upper bound (RVI) in the Gambler problem, demonstrating that the constraint does not significantly degrade performance there. In the N-chain problem, where the constraint is easy to satisfy for all methods, RCPO still converges to the RVI upper-bound reward while PCPO and CUP fall short, highlighting the benefit of robust policy improvement. In the deep Point Gather task under model perturbation, RCPO achieves the highest reward among all methods, with CPO, PCPO, and CUP all showing substantially lower reward; CUP's constraint violation is so severe that its utility curve is omitted entirely. The two-step design (improvement + projection) is critical: the direct CPO-style update (Eq. 8) can be infeasible when the current policy violates the constraint, whereas RCPO's separate projection step guarantees a feasible policy at every iteration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the constrained policy optimization (CPO) framework of Achiam et al. (2017) and the trust region method of Schulman et al. (2015) from the standard CMDP setting to the robust CMDP setting under model mismatch. The key conceptual departure is that under model uncertainty, the worst-case transition kernel is policy-dependent and differs for reward versus utility, making the standard CPO update intractable. The authors address this by introducing a robust performance difference lemma (generalizing Kakade & Langford, 2002) and a two-step algorithm—robust policy improvement followed by projection (inspired by PCPO, Yang et al., 2019)—that uses the current policy's worst-case kernel as a surrogate. Prior robust constrained RL work (Russel et al., 2020; Mankowitz et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2022) either used heuristic policy updates without guarantees or was restricted to tabular settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm for robust constrained RL applicable to continuous state spaces with per-iteration guarantees on both worst-case reward improvement and constraint violation, addressing a critical practical gap: policies trained in simulators that violate safety constraints when deployed in real environments. The robust performance difference lemma is a reusable theoretical tool for the broader robust RL community. Limitations include the need to specify or estimate the uncertainty set (which is non-trivial in practice), the additional computational cost of estimating worst-case transition kernels at each iteration, and the fact that the constraint violation bound depends on a ratio of visitation distributions that may be large. Open questions include how to construct uncertainty sets from data, whether the guarantees hold under partial observability, and how the method scales to multi-agent or high-dimensional continuous control settings.
