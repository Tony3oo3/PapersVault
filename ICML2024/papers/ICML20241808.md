## Problem

For bandit convex optimization with two-point feedback over cross-polytopes, state-of-the-art algorithms achieve a dimensionality dependence of O(√(d log d)) while the known lower bound is Ω(√(d/log d)), leaving a √(log d) gap. Existing approaches built on ℓ_∞-geometry and standard entropy-based mirror descent cannot close this gap because both the gradient estimator and the search strategy are individually optimal within that framework, making further improvement along the same line infeasible.

## Input / Output

Each round the algorithm receives exactly two function evaluations f(x_t + γΛ_t u_t) and f(x_t − γΛ_t u_t), where u_t is drawn uniformly from the ℓ_1 unit sphere and Λ_t is a diagonal matrix whose entries depend on the current iterate. The decision set K is any closed convex subset of the cross-polytope B_1(0,D), and the loss functions are L_1-Lipschitz convex. The output is a sequence of iterates with expected regret O(L_1√(dT)); in the nonconvex extension the algorithm outputs a (δ,ε)-stationary point measured in ℓ_∞ norm over an ℓ_1-ball neighborhood, found within O(dL_1²δ⁻¹ε⁻³) noisy evaluations.

## Method

The core contribution pairs an ℓ_1-ellipsoidal smoothing gradient estimator—whose per-coordinate variance scales as 1/(|x_i|+β)—with a mirror descent update using a symmetric, strictly convex function ψ that extends the negative ½-Tsallis entropy from the positive orthant to the full real line. The key insight, adapted from the Implicitly Normalised Forecaster, is that the decision-variable-dependent smoothing creates a variance structure that the Tsallis-entropy Bregman divergence naturally normalizes, eliminating the √(log d) factor and yielding O(√d) dimensionality dependence. The MD update x_{t+1} = argmin_{x∈K} ⟨g_t, x⟩ + α_t B_φ(x, x_t) with φ(x)=Σψ(x_i) is solved efficiently via a two-step procedure (inverse of ψ′ followed by projection onto the ℓ_1 sphere) with O(d log d) per-iteration cost. For the nonconvex and non-smooth extension, the same estimator and mirror descent are embedded within the online-to-nonconvex conversion framework of Cutkosky et al., producing the best-known O(dL_1²δ⁻¹ε⁻³) complexity for (δ,ε)-stationary points under L_1-Lipschitz assumptions.

## Results

For bandit convex optimisation with two-point feedback over cross-polytopes, the proposed algorithm achieves a regret upper bound of O(L₁√(dT)), improving the state-of-the-art O(√(Td log d)) by a factor of √(log d). The known lower bound is Ω(√(Td) / √(log d)), so a gap of (log d) remains. For stochastic nonconvex and non-smooth optimisation, the algorithm finds a (δ,ε)-stationary point in O(d L₁² δ⁻¹ ε⁻³) function evaluations, achieving linear dimensionality dependence that matches the best-known result of Kornowski & Shamir (2023) while operating under ℓ₁-Lipschitz geometry.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against Akhavan et al. (2022) and Shamir (2017) for bandit convex optimisation, where the proposed ℓ₁-ellipsoidal smoothing combined with symmetric Tsallis-entropy mirror descent removes the √(log d) factor present in both baselines, yielding O(√(dT)) versus O(√(Td log d)). Against the Duchi et al. (2015) lower bound of Ω(√(Td)/√(log d)), the proposed method still leaves a (log d) gap, indicating the bound is not yet tight. For nonconvex nonsmooth optimisation, the method improves over Lin et al. (2022) by reducing the dimension dependence from d^{3/2} to d and the ε-dependence from ε^{-4} to ε^{-3}, and over Chen et al. (2023a) by reducing d^{3/2} to d. Compared to Kornowski & Shamir (2023), which also achieves O(d L₂² δ⁻¹ ε⁻³), the proposed method matches the linear dimension dependence but operates under ℓ₁-Lipschitz geometry with ℓ_∞-stationarity rather than ℓ₂-Lipschitz geometry with ℓ₂-stationarity, making the comparison norm-dependent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws a direct conceptual bridge between the Implicitly Normalised Forecaster (INF) of Audibert & Bubeck (2010) for adversarial multi-armed bandits and the ℓ₁-ball smoothing gradient estimator of Akhavan et al. (2022) for bandit convex optimization over cross-polytopes. The key departure from prior work is replacing the (ℝ^d, ||·||∞) geometry with an ℓ₁-ellipsoidal smoothing whose per-coordinate variance is inversely proportional to the decision variable, paired with a symmetric negative 1/2-Tsallis entropy mirror map that normalises this variance. For the nonconvex non-smooth extension, the paper combines this mechanism with the online-to-nonconvex conversion framework of Cutkosky et al. (2023), positioning itself alongside Kornowski & Shamir (2023) but in the ℓ₁ geometry rather than the Euclidean one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The √log d improvement in dimensionality dependence for bandit convex optimization over cross-polytopes narrows the gap to the known lower bound and is the first to achieve O(√d) dependence in this setting, which is practically relevant for high-dimensional sparse-constrained problems in adversarial attacks and explainable AI. However, the paper is entirely theoretical with no empirical validation, and the authors acknowledge that the decision-variable-dependent smoothing makes it difficult to incorporate advanced variance reduction techniques like recursive gradients. For the nonconvex non-smooth case, the O(d) dimensionality dependence matches the best-known result of Kornowski & Shamir (2023) but under a different (ℓ₁-based) stationarity criterion, so the practical advantage over the Euclidean formulation remains unclear without experiments.
