## Problem

Training multiple differentially private models on the same data for different tasks (e.g., classification and synthetic data generation) compounds the privacy budget cost, making it impractical to serve diverse downstream needs. No existing DP method provides both discriminative (classification) and generative (synthetic data) capabilities in a single model, forcing practitioners to train and release separate models that each consume additional privacy budget.

## Input / Output

The method accepts tabular datasets with discrete, continuous, categorical, or mixed variable types (no preprocessing beyond a rank-and-project pipeline for hybrid domains) and produces a single DPSPN model. This one model simultaneously supports classification via approximate MPE inference (output: class label) and DP synthetic data generation via top-down sampling (output: new data points), with no retraining or adaptation required between tasks. The total privacy cost is bounded a priori by ε = ε'·t, where t is a user-specified cap on privacy-consuming operations.

## Method

DPSPNs are learned via a top-down recursive algorithm that alternates between private instance splitting (DP K-means using the Laplacian mechanism with sensitivity (dr+1)γ) and private variable splitting (binary random column splits drawn from a Beta distribution, which are data-independent and consume zero privacy budget). Leaf nodes are estimated with Laplacian-smoothed DP histograms using two-sided geometric noise. For classification, a root sum node is introduced whose children are per-class DPSPNs, with edge weights set to noisy class counts (geometric noise) representing class probabilities. The privacy guarantee ε = ε'·t follows from basic composition along each root-to-leaf path, with parallel composition across disjoint data subsets at each level, and the bound is computable before training begins.

## Results

On five classification datasets (adult, cancer, diabetes, german-credit, bank), DPSPN-generated synthetic data yields mean AUROC of 0.87 (adult), 0.83 (cancer), 0.66 (diabetes), 0.65 (german-credit), and 0.71 (bank) at ε=1, approaching the original-data baselines (0.89, 0.85, 0.76, 0.65, 0.86) and surpassing MST, PATE-GAN, and PrivBayes on most datasets. At ε=10, DPSPN reaches or exceeds the original-data AUROC on adult (0.88 vs 0.89), cancer (0.89 vs 0.85), and german-credit (0.67 vs 0.65). For density estimation on 20 binary datasets, DPSPN log-likelihood gaps to the non-private SPN baseline shrink from ~5–30% at ε=0.1 to ~1–5% at ε=10 (e.g., kdd: −2.61 vs −2.58; bnetflix: −59.23 vs −58.93).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPSPN outperforms MST (McKenna et al., 2021) on all five classification datasets at both ε=1 and ε=10, with the largest gaps on adult (0.87 vs 0.69 AUROC at ε=1) and bank (0.71 vs 0.56 at ε=1); MST is restricted to discrete-valued data and underperforms substantially on mixed/continuous datasets. Against PATE-GAN (Jordon et al., 2018), DPSPN wins on four of five datasets at ε=1 (e.g., cancer: 0.83 vs 0.56 AUROC; adult: 0.87 vs 0.73), with PATE-GAN only competitive on german-credit (0.64 vs 0.65). Against PrivBayes (Zhang et al., 2017), the comparison is more nuanced: PrivBayes slightly edges DPSPN on the fully discrete adult dataset at ε=1 (0.88 vs 0.87 AUROC) and ties on bank (0.71 vs 0.71), but DPSPN clearly wins on cancer (0.83 vs 0.72), diabetes (0.66 vs 0.59), and german-credit (0.65 vs 0.59). In terms of stability, DPSPN shows markedly lower variance across 50 training runs compared to PATE-GAN and MST, requiring only a single training run to achieve competitive performance. For density estimation, DPSPN log-likelihood approaches the non-private SPN baseline as ε increases, with gaps of only 0.03–0.3 nats at ε=10 on most binary datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SPN learning literature (Poon & Domingos, 2011; Gens & Domingos, 2013; Molina et al., 2018) by replacing each structure-learning step with a differentially private variant: DP K-means (Su et al., 2016) for instance splitting, a data-independent binary random column split for variable splitting, and Laplacian-smoothed histograms for leaf distributions. It departs from prior DP synthetic data generators (PATE-GAN, MST, PrivBayes) and DP classifiers (noisy SGD, PATE) by unifying both capabilities in a single probabilistic model, leveraging the Post-processing Theorem to avoid additional privacy cost at inference or generation time. The choice of random variable splitting over private independence tests is a deliberate simplification motivated by the high privacy cost of the latter and prior evidence that random SPN structures are competitive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that a single DPSPN model can serve as both a classifier and a synthetic data generator, halving the privacy budget consumption compared to training separate models for each task. The demonstrated stability (low variance across 50 training runs) addresses a well-known pain point in DP ML where multiple retraining runs are often needed. Limitations include restriction to ε-DP (not the more flexible (ε,δ)-DP), applicability only to tabular data, and the fact that the random variable splitting, while privacy-free, may underperform on datasets with strong variable dependencies. Open questions include uneven privacy budget allocation across network layers and extension to approximate DP.
