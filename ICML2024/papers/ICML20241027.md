## Problem

The add-remove model of differential privacy, which protects dataset size and is the standard in practical deployments, lacked a min-max optimal mean estimation algorithm. Prior approaches (independent Laplace noise on sum and count) achieved at best a factor-of-two suboptimal constant in the leading mean-squared-error term relative to the swap model. The paper asks whether this gap is inherent and shows it is not: the add-remove and swap models yield identical leading-order error for mean estimation.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a multiset D of real values in [ℓ, u] and a privacy budget ε > 0, and outputs an ε-differentially private estimate of the mean under the add-remove model. Unlike the swap model, the dataset size |D| is not public information, so the mechanism must privately estimate both the sum and the count simultaneously. The final estimate is clipped to [ℓ, u] to guarantee a valid mean, and the normalized MSE (|D|²·MSE) is the target metric, which for optimal algorithms scales as O(1) independent of |D|.

## Method

Algorithm 3 applies a linear transformation to the (sum, count) vector, mapping it to (s₁, s₂) where s₁ is the scaled sum and s₂ is the count minus the scaled sum, reducing the ℓ₁ sensitivity from 2 to 1 so that the enclosing ℓ₁ ball tightly fits the two-segment sensitivity space. In the high-privacy regime (small ε), independent Laplace noise on each coordinate achieves the optimal constant 2(u−ℓ)²/ε². In the low-privacy regime (large ε), the paper introduces the hourglass mechanism, a new two-dimensional noise distribution supported on diagonal lines x + y = kΔ whose marginal distributions exactly match the univariate staircase mechanism of Geng and Viswanath (2014). The hourglass density is piecewise exponential (proportional to e^{−kε} on region A_k and e^{−(2i+k)ε} on region B_k(i)), designed to protect the non-convex sensitivity space more precisely than the convex ℓ₁ ball, and can be sampled efficiently via a two-step procedure. Combined with an information-theoretic lower bound, this establishes min-max optimality with the same leading constant σ²(ε) as the swap model for all ε.

## Results

The paper proves that the min-max normalized MSE for mean estimation under the add-remove model is R_ar(ε, ℓ, u) = (u−ℓ)² · σ²(ε) · (1 ± o(1)), exactly matching the swap model optimal error. In the high-privacy regime (small ε), Algorithm 3 with Laplace noise achieves (u−ℓ)²/ε² · (1 ± o(1)), a factor-of-two improvement over the standard shifted-noise Algorithm 2 which achieves 4(u−ℓ)²/ε². In the low-privacy regime (large ε), the proposed hourglass mechanism matches the optimal σ²(ε) = Θ(e^{−2ε/3}) scaling, while the two-dimensional staircase mechanism only achieves Θ(e^{−ε/2}), a strictly worse exponential rate. Empirically, Algorithm 3 reduces MSE by at least a factor of two over commonly used baselines on synthetic datasets of 10,000 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary experimental baseline is the two-dimensional staircase mechanism of Geng et al. (2015), which is the natural extension of the univariate staircase to two dimensions. In the low-privacy regime (large ε), the hourglass mechanism matches the theoretical lower bound σ²(ε) indefinitely as ε grows, while the 2D staircase mechanism's error scales as Θ(e^{−ε/2}) versus the optimal Θ(e^{−2ε/3}), resulting in a growing gap. At a representative large-ε setting, the 2D staircase achieves a normalized MSE of ~5.86×10⁻³ while the hourglass achieves ~5.86×10⁻⁸, closely tracking the lower bound of ~5.73×10⁻⁸. In the high-privacy regime, Algorithm 3 with Laplace noise outperforms the standard shifted-noise Algorithm 2 by a factor of two, matching the theoretical ratio of their respective upper bounds (2/ε² vs. 4/ε²). The paper also notes that the prior conversion mechanism of Kamath et al. (2023) achieves a constant of 9, far from the optimal constant of 1 achieved here.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Geng and Viswanath's (2014) characterization of the optimal univariate staircase mechanism and the resulting min-max MSE σ²(ε) for mean estimation under the swap model. It departs from the standard add-remove approach of adding independent Laplace noise to the sum and count (Algorithms 1–2) by introducing a linear transformation that reduces the ℓ₁ sensitivity from 2 to 1, and by constructing a new two-dimensional hourglass mechanism whose marginals exactly recover the optimal univariate staircase. The key positioning is showing that the add-remove model, despite being strictly more conservative (it protects dataset size), achieves the same leading-constant min-max MSE as the swap model for scalar mean estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This resolves a natural open question about whether the add-remove model is inherently harder than the swap model for mean estimation, with direct practical relevance since the add-remove model is what production systems (McSherry 2009, Wilson 2019, Rogers 2020) actually deploy. The hourglass mechanism is a new two-dimensional noise distribution that may be useful beyond mean estimation for any query whose sensitivity space is a union of line segments rather than a convex set. Open questions include whether the add-remove/swap equivalence extends to higher-dimensional mean estimation or other statistical queries, and whether the hourglass mechanism can be adapted to non-convex sensitivity spaces in other contexts.
