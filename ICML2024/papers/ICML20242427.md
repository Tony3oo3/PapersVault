## Problem

Autoregressive SSL (GPT) and masked SSL (BERT) exhibit complementary downstream strengths—masked SSL excels at classification while autoregressive SSL excels at generation—but the theoretical mechanisms behind these differences remained unexplored. This paper provides the first unified theoretical comparison, establishing why each paradigm's design choices (fixed next-token targets vs. random multi-position targets; variable-length conditional inputs vs. fixed-length unmasked inputs) produce their respective advantages and limitations.

## Input / Output

For language, the model consumes text token sequences and predicts target tokens: the next word given a prefix (autoregressive) or randomly masked words given the remaining unmasked context (masked SSL). The diversity-enhanced autoregressive variant extends prediction to t future tokens simultaneously via group-based semi-autoregressive modeling, while the variable-length masked variant samples the mask ratio from a continuous range (e.g., [0.15, 0.3]) rather than fixing it. For vision, inputs are image patches and outputs are reconstructed patch tokens, with the same variable-length masking strategy applied to patch-level masking.

## Method

The paper reformulates generative SSL objectives as asymmetric matrix decomposition on co-occurrence matrices, then uses Eckart-Young theorem and spectral graph theory to derive downstream classification guarantees governed by singular values and labeling error. For classification, they prove (via a toy model) that masked SSL's multiple target positions yield smaller singular values—i.e., stronger inter-sample connectivity—than autoregressive SSL's single next-token target, explaining its superior clustering. For generation, they derive an upper bound showing masked SSL's error contains a length-misalignment term (w_k) absent from autoregressive SSL, formalizing why fixed-length unmasked pretraining inputs mismatch variable-length test inputs. To exploit each paradigm's strengths, they propose a diversity-enhanced autoregressive objective (predicting t future tokens via semi-autoregressive group modeling with two-stream attention from XLNet) and a variable-length masked objective (sampling mask ratio from a range), both validated on GLUE, PILE, C4, and ImageNet benchmarks.

## Results

On GLUE, the diversity-enhanced autoregressive objective improves the average score from 74.1 to 76.0 (+1.9%), with the largest gain on CoLA (30.9 → 34.2, +3.3%). On ImageNet-1K with ViT-Base, linear probing accuracy improves from 56.2% to 59.4% (+3.2%). For content generation, the variable-length masked objective reduces perplexity on PILE from 59.6 (fixed 15% mask) to 45.1, and on C4 from 71.2 to 59.4. On ImageNet reconstruction, variable-length masking reduces L2 loss from 0.127 (75% mask) to 0.072 and perceptual loss from 0.201 to 0.136 (~30% reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard autoregressive baseline (GPT-style next-token prediction), the proposed diversity-enhanced autoregressive objective consistently wins on classification: +1.9% average GLUE score, +3.2% linear probing on ImageNet-1K (ViT-B), and +4.1% few-shot LP accuracy. Against the standard masked baseline (BERT-style fixed 15% masking), the proposed variable-length masked objective wins decisively on generation: perplexity drops from 59.6 to 45.1 on PILE and from 71.2 to 59.4 on C4, outperforming even the fixed 30% mask (50.2 / 63.8). Against MAE (fixed 75% mask) on ImageNet reconstruction, variable-length masking ([0.5, 0.75]) reduces L2 loss by 43% (0.127 → 0.072) and perceptual loss by 32% (0.201 → 0.136). The paper also confirms that the original autoregressive model (GPT-2 medium) outperforms the masked model (BERT large) on perplexity (22.8 vs 34.1 on WikiText-2), while the masked model outperforms on GLUE (80.5 vs 72.8), validating the complementary strengths that motivate the two proposed objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spectral loss and matrix decomposition framework from contrastive SSL theory (HaoChen et al., 2021) to the generative SSL setting, establishing the first unified theoretical lens for comparing autoregressive (GPT-style) and masked (BERT-style) pretraining. It builds on the two-stream attention mechanism from XLNet (Yang et al., 2019) to implement semi-autoregressive prediction, and draws on group-autoregressive modeling from Wang et al. (2018b) for the diversity-enhanced objective. Prior work (Dong et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2022a) had only empirically observed that masked SSL excels at classification while autoregressive SSL excels at generation; this paper provides the first theoretical explanation for these complementary strengths.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers a principled, unified explanation for why two dominant pretraining paradigms exhibit complementary downstream strengths, reducing the choice between them from an empirical heuristic to a theoretically grounded trade-off. The proposed modifications—diversity-enhanced autoregressive prediction and variable-length masking—are simple, architecture-agnostic, and yield consistent gains across language and vision tasks, making them immediately adoptable. Open questions include whether the theoretical guarantees (which rely on linear attention for generation analysis and a simplified toy model for classification) hold for full Transformer architectures at scale, and whether the 'diversity' principle can be further generalized to other SSL paradigms such as contrastive or non-generative objectives.
