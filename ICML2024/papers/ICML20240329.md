## Problem

Existing science benchmarks evaluate only final answers on short contexts, failing to simulate real-life tutoring scenarios where LMs must process long textbook chapters, produce free-form explanations, and handle multidisciplinary STEM questions. No prior benchmark combined long-context understanding, free-form generation, and expert-annotated grading of the reasoning process, and no dialogue dataset existed for science-domain fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240329/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The LM tutor receives a textbook chapter (averaging 1,800 words, up to 6,100 words) concatenated with a student question as a single prompt within a 32K-token context window, and generates a free-form tutoring answer of up to 800 tokens. Evaluation is performed by GPT-4-Turbo, which grades both presentation and correctness (0–3 scale) against expert-written key points that sketch the essential content a good teacher should cover. The benchmark includes 834 questions across math, physics, CS, life sciences, and environmental science, with metadata tracking whether questions are closed-book, answer-in-chapter, misleading, or hard.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages: (1) long-context extension of Llemma-7B from 4K to 32K tokens by fine-tuning on 5B tokens of OpenWebMath with an increased RoPE base (1M), and (2) dialogue fine-tuning on TUTORCHAT, a dataset of 78K synthetic teacher-student conversations generated by GPT-3.5-Turbo and GPT-4-Turbo over 78,000 open-source textbook chapters scraped from libretexts.org. To simultaneously preserve math problem-solving, the authors introduce MathMix, which combines TUTORCHAT-STEM (40K science dialogues) with MetaMath (40K samples formed by concatenating 10 Q/A pairs each, ~3,000 tokens average). The key insight is that domain-specific science dialogues (TUTORCHAT-STEM) rather than general dialogues (UltraChat) are what enable strong math performance when mixed with MetaMath, yielding Llemma-7B-32K-MathMix and Llemma-34B-MathMix that rival Mistral-7B-V2 on TUTOREVAL while outperforming it on GSM8K and MATH.

## Results

On TUTOREVAL (834 long-context science questions), GPT-4-Turbo achieves 85.2 correctness, followed by Llama-3-70B (71.3) and Mixtral-8x7B (66.3). The paper's best open model, Llemma-34B-MathMix, scores 57.2 on TUTOREVAL and 57.6 on CLOSED-BOOK, while Llemma-7B-32K-MathMix achieves 50.5/46.6, rivaling Mistral-7B-V2 (50.9/53.8). On math problem-solving, Llemma-7B-32K-MathMix reaches 55.9 on GSM8K and 21.7 on MATH (avg 38.8), substantially outperforming Mistral-7B-V2 (38.9/9.0, avg 24.0). Fine-tuning with TUTORCHAT improves TUTOREVAL by 9 points over the general-purpose UltraChat dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Mistral-7B-V2 (50.9 TUTOREVAL), Llemma-7B-32K-MathMix (50.5) is nearly matched on TUTOREVAL but significantly outperforms it on math: 55.9 GSM8K / 21.7 MATH vs. 38.9 / 9.0, yielding a 38.8 vs. 24.0 average. The math-specialized baselines MAmmoTH and WizardMath score extremely poorly on TUTOREVAL-CLOSEDBOOK (1.5–28.2), confirming that training directly for GSM8K/MATH does not produce usable science tutoring models. Compared to Llama-2-7B-32k-MathMix (30.8) and CodeLlama-32k-MathMix (43.2), Llemma-7B-32K-MathMix (50.5) shows that continued pre-training on scientific text (OpenWebMath) is critical for TUTOREVAL performance. Among short-context generalist models, only Tulu-2-DPO-70B (59.5 CLOSED-BOOK) exceeds the paper's best 7B model, while Llama-3-8B (55.3) and Llama-3-70B (71.3) outperform the 7B Llemma model on TUTOREVAL, highlighting the gap between open 7B models and larger proprietary or Llama-3 systems. GPT-4 (85.2) remains the clear leader, with a ~35-point gap over the best open 7B model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LM-as-evaluator framework from AlpacaEval (Li et al., 2023a) by introducing expert-annotated key points as ground-truth guides for grading free-form scientific answers, and builds its model pipeline on Llemma (Azerbayev et al., 2023), an open-source math-specialized LM trained on OpenWebMath. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate research threads: (1) science LMs trained on large scientific corpora (Minerva, Galactica, Llemma) that target problem-solving benchmarks, and (2) dialogue/instruction-tuning datasets (UltraChat, OpenOrca, ShareGPT) that target general chat capabilities. The paper's key departure is arguing that neither thread alone produces useful science tutors, and that a dedicated long-context science dialogue dataset (TUTORCHAT) is needed to close the gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TUTOREVAL and TUTORCHAT fill a clear gap: no prior benchmark combined long-context processing, free-form generation, and multidisciplinary scientific knowledge in a realistic tutoring setting. The finding that math-specialized models (MAmmoTH, WizardMath) score near zero on science QA, while generalist dialogue models struggle with scientific accuracy, highlights that current training recipes do not transfer to real educational use. Limitations include a bias toward math and CS (362+205 of 834 questions), reliance on GPT-4 as evaluator (which may not be future-proof), and the absence of multi-turn evaluation. Open questions include whether the MathMix recipe generalizes beyond 7B/34B scale, whether TUTORCHAT's synthetic dialogues introduce systematic biases, and how to build harder benchmarks now that GPT-4 already scores 92% on TUTOREVAL.
