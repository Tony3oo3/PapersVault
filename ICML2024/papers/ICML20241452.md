## Problem

While the qualitative relationship between ROC and Precision-Recall curves was established by Davis & Goadrich (2006), no exact analytical bounds existed for AUC_PR at a fixed AUC_ROC (or vice versa). This gap is critical for imbalanced datasets, where a high AUC_ROC can coexist with a very low AUC_PR, yet practitioners lacked a way to quantify the full range of possible AUC_PR values for a given AUC_ROC.

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on binary classification scores paired with ground truth labels, parameterized by the class imbalance ratio a = N⁻/N⁺. Rather than producing per-sample predictions, the method yields closed-form analytical expressions: exact upper and lower bounds on AUC_PR for any fixed AUC_ROC, the inverse bounds on AUC_ROC for any fixed AUC_PR, and a point-wise coordinate transformation mapping any (TPR, FPR) pair to its (Recall, Precision) counterpart. These formulas require only the scalar a and the known AUC value as inputs.

## Method

The paper first derives an exact point-wise transformation between ROC and PR space: (x, y) → (y, y/(y + ax)), which subsumes and strengthens the dominance theorem of Davis & Goadrich. Using this, the problem of bounding AUC_PR at fixed AUC_ROC is cast as a constrained optimization over increasing functions with a fixed integral. A key lemma (via an infinity-norm continuity argument) justifies restricting the search to step functions, after which a first-order perturbation argument shows the maximizer is a two-level step function (zero then one) and the minimizer is a constant function, yielding the closed-form bounds AUC_PR^max = 1 − a·ln((1+a)/(1−c+a)) and AUC_PR^min = 1 − ac·ln((1+ac)/(ac)). The analysis is then inverted to recover AUC_ROC bounds at fixed AUC_PR, and a corollary shows that in many cases one can determine AUC_PR ordering from AUC_ROC values alone without computing the second metric.

## Results

The paper derives closed-form bounds for AUC_PR given a fixed AUC_ROC (Theorem 3.5) and vice versa (Theorem 3.9). For a highly imbalanced dataset (a=100) with AUC_ROC=0.875, the AUC_PR can range from 0.038 to 0.876, whereas for a balanced dataset (a=1) the range narrows to 0.725–0.935. Conversely, for a=100 and AUC_PR=0.7, the AUC_ROC ranges from 0.697 to 0.999, while for AUC_ROC=0.7 the AUC_PR ranges from 0.016 to 0.703. The global minimum of AUC_PR (Corollary 3.11) is shown to be a direct corollary of Theorem 3.5, recovering the Boyd et al. (2012) result.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no empirical model comparisons, so there are no traditional baselines. The key prior works it builds upon and surpasses are Davis & Goadrich (2006) and Boyd et al. (2012). Compared to Davis & Goadrich, the paper provides an exact analytical transformation formula (Eq. 1–3) that is a strictly stronger result than their Theorem 3.2, and it quantifies the full range of AUC_PR variability at fixed AUC_ROC rather than merely showing existence of a relationship. The paper demonstrates that the two example classifiers in Davis & Goadrich (a=100, AUC_ROC 0.813 vs 0.875) actually achieve the theoretical minimum AUC_PR bound from Theorem 3.5, confirming the tightness of the bound. Compared to Boyd et al. (2012), the global minimum of AUC_PR they derive is shown to be a simple corollary of the more general Theorem 3.5, which provides the minimum at any fixed AUC_ROC rather than only the global minimum.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Davis & Goadrich (2006), who established the qualitative dominance equivalence between ROC and PR curves, by deriving an exact analytical transformation formula between the two curve spaces and using it to compute tight bounds on AUC_PR for a fixed AUC_ROC (and vice versa). It also subsumes the global minimum of AUC_PR from Boyd et al. (2012) as a simple corollary of its main theorem, showing that the unachievable-region analysis is a special case of the more general fixed-AUC_ROC bound. The paper positions itself as providing a 'stronger version' of Davis & Goadrich's Theorem 3.2, replacing a qualitative dominance argument with a quantitative pointwise transformation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is clear: for highly imbalanced datasets (e.g., fraud detection, rare disease diagnosis), a classifier with AUC_ROC = 0.875 can have AUC_PR as low as 0.038, meaning the conventional reliance on AUC_ROC as a sole performance metric can be dangerously misleading. The corollary providing a shortcut for model comparison (avoiding one of the two AUC computations) offers a concrete efficiency gain in evaluation pipelines. Open questions include whether these bounds remain informative in multi-class or multi-label settings, and whether the analytical framework extends naturally to other curve pairs (e.g., F-measure curves). The results are purely theoretical and assume fixed class counts, so their applicability to streaming or concept-drifting data is unaddressed.
