## Problem

Existing Differentiable Causal Discovery (DCD) methods, which frame DAG search as continuous optimization, suffer from numerical instability in their acyclicity constraints (Power Series Trace penalties) that causes training to fail beyond tens of variables. The paper identifies this instability theoretically—showing PST constraints grow or vanish exponentially in the number of variables—and proposes a stable alternative constraint combined with a two-stage training procedure to enable DCD to scale to thousands of variables.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241520/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular data of n samples over d variables, optionally with intervention labels t_i ∈ {0,…,K} indicating which structural intervention was applied to each sample (0 = observational). Conditional distributions p_j(x_j | x_{-j}; θ, k) are parameterized by neural networks that take all other variables as input and output Gaussian mean and variance. The output is a directed acyclic graph represented as a thresholded adjacency matrix, with a final greedy DAG-trimming step (Algorithm 2) to guarantee acyclicity if the thresholded matrix still contains cycles.

## Method

SDCD replaces the unstable Power Series Trace acyclicity constraint with the spectral acyclicity constraint h_ρ(A) = |λ_d(A)| (the largest eigenvalue magnitude), which the paper proves is E-stable, V-stable, and D-stable, and computable in O(d²) via power iteration rather than O(d³). The optimization is a two-stage procedure: Stage 1 solves d independent variable-prediction problems without any acyclicity penalty (γ=0) to identify and mask out edges below a threshold τ₁, effectively performing feature selection that provably retains all true causal parents (Theorem 4.2). Stage 2 then optimizes the full DCD objective with the spectral penalty γ·h_ρ(A_θ) on the reduced edge set, incrementing γ linearly each epoch until the adjacency matrix is acyclic at the final threshold τ₂. The penalty method (rather than augmented Lagrangian) is used, and the spectral constraint's stability means γ need not be driven to machine-precision values, enabling reliable convergence even at d = 4000.

## Results

On observational data (s=4 parents per node), SDCD achieves the lowest SHD at every scale tested: 14.7±5.5 at d=10, 69.9±12.3 at d=50, 92.7±9.1 at d=100, and 621.7±10.7 at d=500. SDCD is the only method that scales beyond d=50 without failing; DCDI crashes at d>40, DAGMA and GIES fail at d≥30, and NOTEARS/NO-BEARS perform comparably to the trivial sortnregress baseline. On GPU, SDCD completes d=500 in under 15 minutes and scales to d=4,000 in under 2h45 (SHD 7567.0±343.7). On interventional data, SDCD is the only method to consistently improve as the fraction of intervened variables increases, achieving the best SHD on sparse graphs (edge density δ≤45%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDCD outperforms all ten baselines on SHD at every problem size where comparisons are possible. DCDI (Brouillard et al., 2020) is competitive at d=10–20 (e.g., 24.3 vs. 14.7 at d=10) but crashes with NaNs for d>40 due to E- and V-instability of its exponential acyclicity constraint. DCD-FG (Lopez et al., 2022) scales to d=500 but produces extremely dense graphs, yielding SHDs 10–100× worse than SDCD (e.g., 7252.7 vs. 621.7 at d=500). GIES (Hauser & Buhlmann, 2012) and DAGMA (Bello et al., 2022) both fail to converge or run at d≥30. NOTEARS (Zheng et al., 2018) and NO-BEARS (Lee et al., 2019) perform at or near the level of the trivial sortnregress baseline, confirming the findings of Reisach et al. (2021) that these methods do not meaningfully exploit causal structure. SCORE (Rolland et al., 2022) is competitive at d=10 (14.4 vs. 14.7) but fails to run at d≥100. On interventional data, SDCD is the only method whose SHD consistently decreases as the fraction of intervened variables increases from 0% to 100%, while DCDI, DCD-FG, and GIES show inconsistent or no improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDCD extends the Differentiable Causal Discovery (DCD) framework initiated by NO-TEARS (Zheng et al., 2018), which recast DAG search as continuous optimization over adjacency matrices. It adopts the spectral acyclicity constraint from Lee et al. (2019) and the penalty-based optimization from Ng et al. (2020), but provides the first systematic theoretical analysis showing that all Power Series Trace (PST) constraints used by NO-TEARS, DAGMA, and DCDI are inherently E-unstable and V-unstable, explaining their numerical failures at scale. The two-stage procedure (edge preselection followed by constrained DCD) is a new algorithmic contribution not present in prior DCD methods, with a proof that stage 1 recovers exactly the Markov boundary and thus never removes true causal parents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SDCD removes the primary barrier that limited DCD to tens of variables, scaling to 4,000+ variables with competitive accuracy, which opens the door to applying differentiable causal discovery to real-world datasets like Perturb-seq screens. The stability characterization of acyclicity constraints is a reusable theoretical tool that explains a class of failures across multiple methods. Open questions include a deeper understanding of how the spectral constraint's non-convexity affects gradient-based optimization, whether the two-stage procedure generalizes to other model classes (e.g., normalizing flows), and whether the theoretical guarantees (requiring infinite data and exact optimization) hold in practice with finite samples and approximate solvers.
