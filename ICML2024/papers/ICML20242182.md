## Problem

Existing LLM watermarks (KGW, DiP, ITS) are designed through heuristic arguments about the trade-off between identifiability and text quality, without a principled optimization framework. No prior work had formulated this test-text trade-off as a multi-objective optimization problem over a general class of watermarks, leaving open whether the proposed watermarks were actually optimal or merely reasonable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242182/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each token position t, the method consumes the LLM's conditional probability distribution p_t over the vocabulary, the text prefix v_{:t} (which via a keyed hash determines the pseudo-random green-red split G_t of size ⌊γN⌋), and a watermark key shared with the detector. It outputs a modified sampling distribution P̃[V_t|V_{:t}] that rescales all green-list token probabilities by a common factor and all red-list token probabilities by another common factor, preserving relative ordering within each list. The shift function Δ(p_t, G_t) is a scalar in [0, 1−Γ_t] that uniformly shifts the total green-list probability mass, making the perturbation minimal in the sense that no individual token's relative probability within its list is altered.

## Method

The paper defines a general class of watermarks parameterized by a shift function Δ(p_t, G_t) that uniformly rescales green-list and red-list probabilities, then formulates the test-text trade-off as a multi-objective optimization: maximize the expected number of green-list tokens ΔN_g while minimizing the expected log-perplexity increase Δ log PPL. The Pareto optimal solution (the OPT watermark) is a threshold rule: at each position t, if the expected damage B(p_t, G_t) ≤ β, the watermark is applied maximally (green-list token generated with probability 1); otherwise, no watermark is imposed. This means tokens whose promotion causes the least expected harm to text quality are watermarked first and most strongly, a greedy allocation that is provably Pareto optimal and decomposes token-by-token. The paper further shows that under mild binomiality assumptions, the same OPT watermark is Pareto optimal when test quality is measured by the power of a hypothesis test, and derives an alternative OPT′ watermark for a variance-aware text quality objective.

## Results

The OPT watermark achieves the Pareto optimal bound for the test-text trade-off (expected number of green-list tokens vs. expected log-perplexity) on C4-generated text with OPT-1.3B, outperforming KGW, DiP, and ITS across all tested configurations. When text quality is measured by ROUGE-1 on text summarization (CNN-DM, BART-large) and machine translation (WMT 2016, Multilingual BART), the KGW watermark outperforms OPT, demonstrating that optimality for one text-quality metric does not generalize to others. The OPT and OPT′ watermarks perform nearly identically on log-perplexity, while OPT′ marginally outperforms OPT on the between-token variance of log-perplexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KGW (Kirchenbauer et al., 2023), OPT dominates on the log-perplexity-based test-text trade-off (Figures 1–2), achieving more green-list tokens at the same perplexity cost or lower perplexity at the same identifiability, as expected from its Pareto optimality. However, on ROUGE-1 for summarization and translation tasks, KGW outperforms OPT, showing that the optimization objective matters. Against DiP (Wu et al., 2023), OPT clearly wins on identifiability (power of test and green-token count) while DiP retains its distortion-free property (ROUGE-1 close to the unwatermarked LLM) but at the cost of much weaker detectability. Against ITS (Kuditipudi et al., 2024), OPT outperforms on the power-of-test vs. log-perplexity trade-off; ITS additionally shows very poor ROUGE-1 scores because its deterministic sampling is incompatible with beam search used in the TS/MT experiments. Overall, OPT is the strongest watermark among the four on the primary log-perplexity metric but is not universally superior across all text-quality measures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the KGW watermark (Kirchenbauer et al., 2023) into a broad class of watermarks parameterized by a shift function Δ over a green-red vocabulary split, then reframes the test-text trade-off as a multi-objective optimization problem whose Pareto optimal solutions are derived in closed form. It departs from prior work (KGW, DiP, ITS) by replacing heuristic parameter choices (e.g., the logit shift δ in KGW) with an explicit optimization objective, and by showing that the resulting OPT watermark dominates these baselines on the log-perplexity metric. The class of watermarks studied is deliberately restricted to those that rescale green-list and red-list probabilities uniformly, excluding approaches that use external models or embedding-space similarity scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic, optimization-theoretic framework for the test-text trade-off in LLM watermarks, yielding a principled alternative to ad-hoc parameter tuning. However, three important caveats temper its impact: (1) Pareto optimality for log-perplexity does not transfer to other quality metrics (ROUGE-1 experiments show KGW outperforms OPT on summarization and translation), (2) the binomial independence assumption underlying the power-of-test analysis breaks down for stronger watermarks, and (3) OPT reduces n-gram diversity more than competing watermarks. Open questions include whether the framework extends to watermarks that couple adjacent tokens, whether the diversity reduction is perceptually meaningful, and how the approach behaves in low-entropy domains like code generation.
