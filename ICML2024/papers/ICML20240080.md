## Problem

Existing parameter-free stochastic optimization methods are only partially parameter-free, still requiring non-trivial knowledge such as bounds on stochastic gradient norms or distance to a minimizer. This paper asks the fundamental question of whether fully parameter-free methods exist in various stochastic optimization settings, and under what conditions they are possible or provably impossible.

## Input / Output

Rather than receiving exact problem parameters, algorithms are given crude ranges (lower and upper bounds) for each parameter—smoothness, Lipschitz constant, noise magnitude, distance to minimizer—along with the initialization point w₁, number of steps T, and failure probability δ. The method produces a single point w̄ ∈ ℝ^d with a high-probability suboptimality guarantee matching tuned SGD up to poly-logarithmic factors in the range widths. In the convex setting with function value access, the oracle provides both noisy function values and stochastic gradients; in the gradient-only setting, only stochastic gradient queries are available, which is where the fundamental limitations arise.

## Method

The paper's central insight is that a simple exponential grid search over step sizes (or projection diameters) combined with model selection via stochastic gradient norms (non-convex) or noisy function values (convex) yields fully parameter-free methods that outperform sophisticated adaptive algorithms like AdaSGD. For the convex gradient-only setting, Algorithm 1 runs AdaPSGD (projected SGD with AdaGrad-like dynamic stepsizes) across an exponential grid of diameters, using 'decorrelated' stepsizes and martingale concentration to achieve high-probability guarantees while bounding only the noise component rather than full gradient norms. A key lower bound (Theorem 6) shows that in this setting, full parameter-freeness is information-theoretically impossible: any method must incur an Ω(D·σ_max/T) penalty unless the noise or diameter range has width at most O(√T). The proposed method achieves this optimal rate, making it parameter-free up to the unavoidable lower-bound term, and extends to the convex smooth setting as the first parameter-free result in that regime.

## Results

In the non-convex smooth setting, the proposed hyperparameter-search method (Algorithm 2) achieves ‖∇f(w̄)‖² = Õ((β⋆F⋆ + σ⋆² log(1/δ))/T + σ⋆√(β⋆F⋆)/√T) · polylog(ηmax/ηmin, 1/δ), matching tuned SGD up to poly-log factors while being fully parameter-free. In the convex Lipschitz setting with noisy function values, Algorithm 3 achieves f(w̄) − f⋆ = Õ(D⋆√(G⋆²+σ⋆²)/√T + σ₀/√T) · polylog(ηmax/ηmin, 1/δ), also fully parameter-free under mild noise assumptions. In the convex setting with only stochastic gradient access, Algorithm 1 achieves f(w̄) − f⋆ = Õ(D⋆(G⋆+σ⋆)/√T + D⋆σmax/T), which is parameter-free when σmax/σmin = O(√T). A lower bound (Theorem 6) proves the D⋆σmax/T term is unavoidable, establishing that full parameter-freeness is infeasible without function value access.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against tuned SGD (the benchmark rate in Table 1), which all proposed methods match up to poly-logarithmic factors in the parameter range widths. Against existing adaptive methods (AdaSGD variants by Faw et al. 2022, Attia & Koren 2023, Liu et al. 2023), the hyperparameter-search approach in the non-convex setting has only logarithmic dependence on the stepsize range [ηmin, ηmax], whereas adaptive methods require tight knowledge of F⋆/β⋆ or suffer suboptimal polynomial dependence on problem parameters. Against existing parameter-free convex methods (Carmon & Hinder 2022, Ivgi et al. 2023), the proposed Algorithm 1 requires only a bound on the gradient *noise* σ⋆ rather than a bound on the full stochastic gradient norm, a distinction that is critical in the convex smooth case where gradients are unbounded. The lower bound (Theorem 6) shows that any method without function value access must incur an Ω(D⋆σmax/T) penalty, which the proposed method achieves optimally up to poly-log factors. The paper also notes that concurrent work by Carmon & Hinder (2024) and Khaled & Jin (2024) addresses similar questions, with the present paper providing a more detailed characterization of the cost of parameter-freeness and the first parameter-free result for convex smooth stochastic optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the two-phase SGD and success-amplification framework of Ghadimi & Lan (2013) and the decorrelated AdaGrad-like stepsizes of Attia & Koren (2023), but departs sharply from the prevailing trend of designing increasingly sophisticated adaptive or self-tuning algorithms. Instead, it demonstrates that a simple exponential grid search over fixed stepsizes (for non-convex and convex-with-function-values settings) or over projection diameters (for convex gradient-only settings) suffices to achieve fully parameter-free guarantees, outperforming the more complex AdaSGD analyses of Faw et al. (2022), Kavis et al. (2022), and Liu et al. (2023) that require tight knowledge of smoothness and suboptimality. The paper also positions itself against Carmon & Hinder (2022) and Ivgi et al. (2023), whose parameter-free convex methods require a bound on the full stochastic gradient norm, whereas this work's lower bound and algorithm depend only on the noise component, a distinction that becomes critical in the convex smooth case where gradients are unbounded.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean taxonomy of when full parameter-freeness is achievable in stochastic optimization: possible in non-convex smooth and convex-with-function-values settings, but fundamentally impossible in the convex gradient-only setting without at least O(√T)-tolerant knowledge of either the noise magnitude or the distance to a minimizer. This impossibility result (Theorem 6) resolves a long-standing ambiguity about why prior parameter-free methods always required some non-trivial parameter knowledge, and the accompanying upper bound shows the gap is essentially tight up to poly-log factors. The practical implication is that for practitioners who have access to noisy function values (as in most ML pipelines with validation sets), a simple stepsize grid search provides a theoretically justified, fully parameter-free alternative to hand-tuned SGD. Open questions include whether the O(√T) tolerance on the noise bound can be improved, whether parameter-free acceleration is possible in the convex smooth case, and whether the diameter tolerance (as opposed to gradient-noise tolerance) can be pushed further in online-to-batch reductions.
