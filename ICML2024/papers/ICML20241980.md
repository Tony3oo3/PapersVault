## Problem

GNN-based simulators for Lagrangian fluid dynamics suffer from pathological particle clustering during long rollouts, violating the ~1% compressibility requirement of weakly compressible SPH and producing unphysical dynamics. The paper identifies two root causes: (i) the network implicitly learns velocity correlations rather than true internal dynamics because external forces (e.g., gravity) are entangled in the acceleration target, and (ii) no mechanism exists to correct accumulating density errors during autoregressive rollout.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241980/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The GNN receives a time sequence of H past velocity vectors per particle (computed via finite differences of coordinates) plus an optional external force vector, and predicts per-particle accelerations. Crucially, the predicted acceleration excludes the accumulated external force contribution g_M^FD = (M·Δt_SPH)²g, which is added back analytically after the GNN forward pass. During inference, the SPH relaxation step consumes only particle coordinates (assuming zero initial velocities) and produces corrected positions, operating on a wider neighbor list (3× particle spacing vs. the GNN's 1.5×) with a Quintic spline kernel.

## Method

Neural SPH introduces two complementary corrections to existing GNN-based Lagrangian simulators (GNS, SEGNN) without requiring architectural changes. First, during training, the external force is removed from the acceleration target (operator splitting), forcing the network to model only pressure and viscous interactions; for spatially varying forces like reverse Poiseuille flow, the force function is smoothed via Gaussian convolution (or SPH kernel convolution) with width set to the velocity standard deviation to account for temporal coarsening over M steps. Second, during inference, 1–5 SPH relaxation steps are applied after each GNN prediction, using a pressure gradient term (and optionally a viscous term) with tuned hyperparameters α and β to redistribute particles and suppress density fluctuations. A novel density computation clips values to [0.98, 1.02]ρ_ref to handle free surfaces and walls with a single wall layer, and the generalized wall boundary condition of Adami et al. (2012) enforces impermeability. The relaxation operates entirely outside the differentiable framework, adding only 5–15% inference overhead per step.

## Results

On 400-step rollouts with GNS-10-128, Neural SPH-enhanced variants consistently outperform the baseline across all seven 2D/3D Lagrangian datasets. The most dramatic gains appear on 2D lid-driven cavity (Sinkhorn divergence improves ~1000×, from 3.1e-4 to 2.8e-7; kinetic energy MSE improves ~100×, from 1.1e-4 to 1.2e-6) and 2D dam break (position MSE improves 2.3×, from 1.9e-1 to 8.0e-2; kinetic energy MSE improves ~22×, from 4.6e-2 to 2.1e-3). On 2D reverse Poiseuille flow, the combined force-removal + pressure + viscous relaxation (GNS_{g,p,ν}) reduces kinetic energy MSE by ~30× (4.3e-3 → 1.4e-4) while matching the ground-truth velocity distribution. Inference overhead is modest: 1–5 SPH relaxation steps add only 1.05–1.15× rollout time in 2D and ~1.4× per step in 3D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two baseline GNN simulators: GNS (Sanchez-Gonzalez et al., 2020, non-equivariant) and SEGNN (Brandstetter et al., 2022, E(3)-equivariant). Against GNS, Neural SPH variants win on all seven datasets: the largest gains are on 2D LDC (Sinkhorn ~1000×, kinetic energy MSE ~100×) and 2D DAM (position MSE 2.3×, kinetic energy MSE ~22×), while 3D TGV and 3D LDC show more modest but consistent Sinkhorn improvements (4–18×). Against SEGNN, the pattern is similar but with more variance: SEGNN_p dramatically fixes the 2D LDC blowup (kinetic energy MSE drops from 9.1e+0 to 1.6e-5, a ~5.7×10⁵ improvement) and the 3D RPF blowup (kinetic energy MSE from 1.5e+3 to 5.7e-3, a ~260× improvement), but on 2D RPF the viscous term actually degrades kinetic energy MSE (7.3e-4 vs. 1.6e-4 for GNS_{g,p,ν}). The external force treatment (GNS_g) alone already yields a 5× kinetic energy improvement on 2D DAM without any relaxation, confirming that the GNS baseline primarily learns velocity correlations rather than true force dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LagrangeBench benchmarking suite (Toshev et al., 2024a) and its two state-of-the-art GNN-based simulators—GNS (Sanchez-Gonzalez et al., 2020) and SEGNN (Brandstetter et al., 2022a)—identifying a shared failure mode (particle clustering from tensile instabilities) that mirrors known artifacts in classical SPH solvers. Methodologically, it draws on SPH relaxation techniques (Litvinov et al., 2015; Fan et al., 2024) and generalized wall boundary conditions (Adami et al., 2012) to construct an inference-time correction routine, while the external force treatment is motivated by operator splitting of the Navier-Stokes momentum equation. Conceptually closest to PDE-Refiner (Lippe et al., 2024), which applies refinement steps to Eulerian solvers, but unlike hybrid models that embed differentiable solvers into training (Um et al., 2020; Toshev et al., 2023b), Neural SPH requires no retraining and operates purely at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Neural SPH offers a practical, low-overhead (1.05–1.15× per relaxation step) path to dramatically longer and more physically faithful rollouts of GNN-based fluid simulators without retraining, which is valuable for practitioners already invested in existing trained models. The identification that GNNs implicitly learn velocity correlations rather than true force dynamics (evidenced by the gravity bias being an order of magnitude too small) reframes how the community should think about what these models actually capture. Key open questions remain: the hyperparameter tuning procedure is dataset-specific and somewhat fragile, no physical accuracy thresholds analogous to chemical accuracy in computational chemistry have been established for Lagrangian fluid simulations, and whether the framework extends to more complex multi-phase or fluid-structure interaction scenarios is untested.
