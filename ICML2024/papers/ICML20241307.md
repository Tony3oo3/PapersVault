## Problem

Partial multi-view multi-label classification (PMvMLC) requires coupling multi-view consensus with multi-label task relevance when both views and labels are partially unavailable. Existing methods either handle only complete multi-view data or address only one type of incompleteness, and lack a principled mechanism to compress cross-view representations to maximize the proportion of shared (task-relevant) information while preserving feature validity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241307/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of available view features (from six feature types: GIST, HSV, DenseHue, DenseSift, RGB, LAB) where any subset of views may be missing, along with a partial label vector in which unknown tags are masked out. It produces a probability score for each of the c label categories via a sigmoid-activated neural network operating on the concatenated cross-view latent representation and sampled label prototype. The framework handles arbitrary combinations of missing views and missing labels simultaneously, with experiments using 50% missing rates for both.

## Method

SIP formulates cross-view representation learning as an information bottleneck problem: per-view stochastic encoders produce Gaussian posteriors that are fused via a Product-of-Experts (PoE) to yield a joint posterior p(z|{x}), and the IB loss balances a reconstruction term (preserving information from raw data) against a KL-divergence term (compressing view-private, non-shared information) with trade-off coefficient β. For multi-label classification, label prototypes are modeled as learnable Gaussian distributions in the latent space (initialized from one-hot vectors), sampled via the reparameterization trick, and pulled toward their corresponding samples through a prototype aggregation loss that implicitly captures label correlations in a data-driven manner. Classification is performed by a small fully-connected network on the concatenation of the sampled cross-view representation and each label prototype, trained with a masked cross-entropy loss that ignores unknown labels. The overall objective L = L_IB + α·L_PA + L_CE is optimized end-to-end, combining self-supervised shared-information learning with supervised label-prototype and classification objectives in a single joint framework.

## Results

On five multi-view multi-label datasets (Corel5k, Pascal07, ESPGame, IAPRTC12, MIRFLICKR) with 50% missing views and 50% missing labels, SIP achieves the best performance on all six evaluation metrics (1-RL, AP, 1-HL, AUC, 1-OE, 1-Cov) across every dataset, with an average rank (Ave.R) of 0 (i.e., first on all metrics). Headline AP values are 0.418 (Corel5k), 0.555 (Pascal07), 0.649 (ESPGame), 0.631 (IAPRTC12), and 0.649 (MIRFLICKR). On complete data (no missing views/labels), SIP also remains competitive, confirming generalizability beyond the incomplete setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIP outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across all five datasets. The closest competitor is DICNet (AAAI 2023), which also handles both missing views and partial labels; SIP improves AP by 3.7–9.7% over DICNet (e.g., 0.418 vs. 0.381 on Corel5k, 0.555 vs. 0.549 on Pascal07). NAIM3L (TPAMI 2022) is the second-strongest baseline, trailing SIP by 2.6–8.4% in AP. Methods designed only for single-view partial-label settings (C2AE, GLOCAL, DM2L) or complete multi-view settings (CDMM, LVSL) show substantially larger gaps, with AP values 10–20% below SIP, confirming that the ability to jointly handle missing views and missing labels is critical. iMVWL (IJCAI 2018), the earliest method supporting both incompleteness types, lags SIP by 6–11% in AP. On complete data (no missing views/labels), SIP remains competitive with methods designed specifically for the ideal case, demonstrating that the information-bottleneck shared-information learning does not sacrifice performance when all data is available.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIP sits at the intersection of three research threads: multi-view information bottleneck learning (Federici et al., 2020; Lee & Van der Schaar, 2021), product-of-experts fusion for multi-view posteriors (Wu & Goodman, 2018), and data-driven label prototype modeling for multi-label classification (Bai et al., 2022). Unlike prior incomplete multi-view multi-label methods (iMVWL, NAIM3L, DICNet) that rely on contrastive or graph-based strategies to handle missing views and labels, SIP reframes the shared-representation problem through an explicit information bottleneck objective that maximizes the proportion of cross-view shared information while compressing view-private noise. The label prototype component departs from statistical or pre-trained embedding approaches by learning stochastic label distributions directly from data, enabling flexible correlation modeling under partial label observation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded (information-theoretic) justification for why shared multi-view information should suffice for semantic prediction, and the β-balance analysis offers practical guidance on the compression-reconstruction trade-off. However, the core assumption—that shared information across views is always sufficient for accurate prediction—may break down for highly heterogeneous modalities (text, audio, image), as the authors themselves note in their limitations. Open questions include whether more sophisticated information capacity estimators could tighten the variational bounds, and whether the PoE fusion strategy generalizes to non-Gaussian or non-i.i.d. view encodings. The method's reliance on tuning β and α limits its plug-and-play applicability.
