## Problem

Data-free approximation of discrete target distributions (e.g., Boltzmann distributions in Combinatorial Optimization) has relied on exact-likelihood models like mean-field or autoregressive networks, because the standard reverse-KL training objective requires evaluating log q_θ(X). This precludes the use of highly expressive latent variable models such as diffusion models, which cannot compute exact marginal likelihoods. The discrete setting for data-free diffusion-based sampling had remained unexplored prior to this work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241765/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a CO problem instance as a graph with adjacency matrix Q ∈ ℝ^{N×N} (encoding the QUBO energy function) and produces a binary solution vector X ∈ {0,1}^N. The GNN-based diffusion model is conditioned on the graph structure and outputs a mean-field distribution (per-node Bernoulli probabilities) at each diffusion step. At inference, Conditional Expectation with Subgraph Tokenization (CE-ST) converts the mean-field output into a deterministic solution in O(N/k) sequential steps, where k is the token size, yielding a significant speedup over standard CE.

## Method

The core contribution is the Joint Variational Upper Bound (JVUB), which upper-bounds the intractable reverse KL D_KL(q_θ(X)‖p_B(X)) by the tractable joint KL D_KL(q_θ(X,Z)‖p(X,Z)), where Z is the latent variable (the diffusion trajectory X_{1:T}). This avoids any need to evaluate the marginal log-likelihood. The resulting loss decomposes into three θ-dependent terms: an entropy regularization encouraging exploration, a forward-reverse coupling term ensuring the reverse diffusion path is likely under the forward process, and the CO energy objective. A time-conditioned GNN parameterizes each reverse diffusion step q_θ(X_{t-1}|X_t), trained with variational annealing (linearly decreasing temperature) and RAdam. The paper further shows that EGN/EGN-Anneal are exact special cases of DiffUCO with T=1, and introduces two discrete noise distributions (Categorical and Annealed) with distinct training dynamics.

## Results

On MIS (RB-large), DiffUCO:CE-ST achieves an average independent set size of 38.87±0.13, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art LTFT (37.48) by 1.39 nodes while running ~26× faster (1:05 vs 4:22). On MDS (BA-large), DiffUCO:CE-ST achieves a dominating set size of 106.61±0.31, beating all other methods. On MaxCut (BA-large), DiffUCO:CE-ST reaches 2947.53±1.49, surpassing Gurobi's 2944.38±0.86 (300s time limit) in just 0:53 of evaluation time. Across all five CO benchmarks, DiffUCO variants achieve the best or near-best unsupervised results with evaluation times typically under 1 minute per graph.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffUCO:CE-ST outperforms the previous state-of-the-art unsupervised method LTFT (GFlowNets) on all four benchmarks where they overlap: MIS RB-large (38.87 vs 37.48), MDS BA-small (28.20 vs 28.61), MaxCl RB-small (16.30 vs 16.24), and MaxCut BA-large (2947.53 vs 2864). Against the mean-field baselines EGN and EGN-Anneal, DiffUCO achieves substantially better solution quality (e.g., 38.87 vs 30.68 on MIS RB-large) while also being faster at inference. On MaxCut BA-large, DiffUCO:CE-ST (2947.53) even surpasses Gurobi with a 300-second time limit (2944.38) in under 1 minute of evaluation. On MVC (RB-200), DiffUCO significantly outperforms VAG-CO, EGN, EGN-Anneal, and DB-Greedy across all hardness parameters p, and on hard instances (low p) achieves a lower approximation ratio than Gurobi. The supervised methods INTEL and DGL are outperformed on MIS by a wide margin (19.24 vs 18.47/17.36), and the heuristic baselines (Greedy, MFA) are consistently dominated by DiffUCO variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffUCO extends the Unsupervised Combinatorial Optimization (UCO) line of work by introducing the Joint Variational Upper Bound, a tractable loss that upper-bounds the reverse KL divergence without requiring exact sample likelihoods. This enables latent variable models—specifically discrete diffusion models (building on Austin et al.'s categorical noise and Ho et al.'s time-conditioned architecture)—to be applied in the data-free discrete setting, where prior UCO methods were restricted to exact-likelihood models like mean-field or autoregressive networks. The paper further unifies the landscape by proving that EGN (Karalias & Loukas, 2020) is mathematically equivalent to a one-step instance of the proposed framework, and adopts Subgraph Tokenization and variational annealing from Sanokowski et al. (2023) for efficient inference and training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the exact-likelihood requirement, this work opens the door to a broader class of expressive generative models (diffusion models, VAEs) for discrete, data-free optimization problems, which is a common bottleneck in statistical physics, Monte Carlo integration, and CO. The practical impact is demonstrated by SOTA results across five CO benchmarks with competitive inference times, particularly when combined with the proposed Tokenized Conditional Expectation. Key limitations remain: training on high-connectivity graphs (e.g., MaxCl on RB-large) is memory- and time-expensive, the learned distribution always carries residual bias (no unbiased sampling guarantee), and OOD generalization is not the design focus. Future directions include latent diffusion models and U-Net-style GNNs to reduce computational cost.
