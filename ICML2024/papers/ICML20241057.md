## Problem

Adversarial training, the de facto standard for robustness, requires access to the original training data, yet in privacy-sensitive domains (medical, biometric, proprietary) only pretrained model weights are publicly available. The paper demonstrates through a motivational study that even adversarial training on similar-domain or public datasets yields poor robustness, leaving no practical path to harden a pretrained model without its original data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241057/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires only the pretrained (non-robust) model's weights as input—no real images, labels, or auxiliary datasets. Synthetic samples are generated by optimizing random Gaussian noise against the teacher model's outputs and batch-normalization statistics (28×28 for medical, 32×32 for CIFAR/SVHN), producing 10,000–60,000 surrogate images. The output is an adversarially robust student model initialized from the teacher, evaluated under l∞ and l2 perturbation budgets (e.g., ε=8/255, 4/255, 128/255) against PGD and AutoAttack.

## Method

DataFreeShield combines three components. First, Diversified Sample Synthesis (DSS) generates synthetic data by directly optimizing each sample from N(0,1) noise, but dynamically resamples the weights of the synthesis loss terms (cross-entropy, feature-matching, pixel-prior) from U(0,1) per batch, breaking the clustering that fixed coefficients produce and yielding higher coverage and recall. Second, GradRefine aggregates gradients over B mini-batches, computes a per-parameter sign-agreement score A_k, and masks out parameters whose gradients fluctuate in sign (|A_k| < τ), retaining only consistently-directed updates to flatten the loss surface and mitigate the synthetic-to-real distribution gap. Third, the training objective L_DFShield replaces hard-label cross-entropy with three KL-divergence terms against the frozen teacher: clean accuracy KL(S(x̂), T(x̂)), robustness KL(S(x̂′), T(x̂)), and a smoothness term KL(S(x̂′), S(x̂)) that penalizes rapid output changes under perturbation, avoiding reliance on unreliable artificial labels.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with WRN-28-10 (l∞, ε=4/255), DataFreeShield achieves 43.73% AutoAttack accuracy, outperforming the best baseline AIT (9.47%) by over 34 percentage points. On SVHN with the same architecture, it reaches 62.66% A_AA versus 33.06% for AIT. On the privacy-sensitive medical datasets (Table 1), DataFreeShield consistently achieves the highest A_AA across all four datasets (Tissue, Blood, Path, OrganC) for both ResNet-18 and ResNet-50, e.g., 31.57% on Tissue (RN-18) and 59.86% on OrganC (RN-50), while baselines often drop below 10%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DataFreeShield decisively outperforms all four data-free baselines across every dataset and model. AIT (Choi et al., 2022) is the strongest baseline, reaching 9.47% A_AA on CIFAR-10 (WRN-28-10) and 33.06% on SVHN, but DataFreeShield exceeds it by 34.26 and 29.60 percentage points respectively. DaST (Zhou et al., 2020) and DFME (Truong et al., 2021) are consistently near or below random-guessing levels (e.g., 2.85% and 0.05% on CIFAR-10), while DFARD (Wang et al., 2023b) shows high clean accuracy but collapses to near 0% under AutoAttack, indicating it fails to learn transferable robustness. Among test-time defenses on medical datasets (Table 3), TTE (Pérez et al., 2021) achieves near-zero A_AA in most settings (e.g., 0.00% on Tissue RN-50), DAD (Nayak et al., 2022) peaks at 12.57% on OrganC, and DiffPure (Nie et al., 2022) reaches 34.38% on OrganC (RN-50) but degrades sharply on other datasets, whereas DataFreeShield maintains 25–60% A_AA consistently. The gap widens with model capacity: on CIFAR-10, DataFreeShield improves from 22.65% (ResNet-20) to 43.73% (WRN-28-10), while AIT actually degrades from 10.67% to 9.47%, suggesting baselines cannot exploit larger architectures due to limited synthetic data diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: adversarial training (Madry et al., 2018) and data-free learning via synthetic sample generation (Yin et al., 2020, DeepInversion). It departs from prior data-free learning (quantization, distillation, model extraction) by targeting adversarial robustness as the objective, and from prior adversarial robustness work by eliminating the assumption of access to real training data. The paper also positions itself against test-time defenses (DiffPure, DAD, TTE), arguing they either leak data, require domain-matched pretrained models, or provide negligible gains on non-robust models. Its three technical contributions—Diversified Sample Synthesis (dynamic coefficient sampling), GradRefine (gradient sign-agreement masking), and a KL-divergence-based training loss—each address a specific gap identified in the data-free adversarial setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: in privacy-sensitive domains (medical, biometric), pretrained models are shared but training data is not, leaving no clear path to adversarial robustness. The motivational study showing that even same-domain alternative datasets fail to transfer robustness is a useful negative result that justifies the data-free approach. Limitations include the quality ceiling imposed by the pretrained teacher, significant compute for synthetic data generation, and acknowledged privacy risks (membership inference) from generated samples. Open questions include scalability to larger architectures (e.g., ViTs, ImageNet-scale), whether the approach extends to non-CNN modalities, and whether the synthetic data can be further constrained to reduce privacy leakage.
