## Problem

Existing constrained policy optimization algorithms (CPO, PCPO) are designed for the discounted criterion and their policy improvement bounds trivialize (diverge to negative infinity) as the discount factor approaches 1, making them inapplicable to average-reward constrained MDPs. The average criterion is more suitable for long-term safety constraints, yet no practical trust-region-style policy optimization algorithm existed for average-constrained MDPs (ACMDPs) with function approximation.

## Input / Output

The method takes continuous state observations (dimensions ranging from R^9 for point-mass tasks to R^96 for the Grid traffic-light task) and produces continuous actions (R^2 to R^16) via a two-layer Gaussian neural network policy. The key distinction from discounted methods is that advantage estimation uses an average-reward bias function (estimated via a one-step TD backup with the average-reward GAE) rather than a discounted value function, and the optimization objective and constraints are expressed in terms of stationary-distribution expectations rather than discounted occupation measures.

## Method

ACPO derives new sensitivity bounds for average CMDPs by relating the total variation divergence of stationary distributions to policy divergence via eigenvalues of the transition matrix (parameter σ*), yielding a policy improvement bound (Proposition 3.4) and constraint violation bound (Corollary 3.5) that replace the inapplicable discounted CPO bound. The algorithm formulates a trust region optimization (Eq. 10) maximizing the average advantage surrogate subject to first-order cost constraint surrogates and a KL divergence step-size constraint, solved exactly via Lagrangian duality with a first-order Taylor expansion on the objective and a second-order expansion on the KL term. A novel recovery mechanism (Eq. 16) blends the cost-advantage gradient and reward-advantage gradient with a tunable parameter t to restore feasibility while minimizing reward regret, followed by a backtracking line search to enforce approximate constraint satisfaction. Theoretical guarantees (Theorem 3.6) provide worst-case performance degradation and constraint violation bounds that depend on the trust region size δ and the current constraint violation magnitude.

## Results

ACPO is evaluated on four OpenAI Gym environments (Point-Gather, Ant-Gather, Ant-Circle, Bottleneck, Grid) with state spaces up to 10^96, using 10^5 training steps over 5 runs. In Point-Gather, ACPO improves average reward by 52.5% over CPO and 36.1% over PCPO; in Ant-Gather, improvements are 41.1% and 61.5% respectively, while maintaining constraint satisfaction. ACPO is the only algorithm that achieves near-constraint-satisfying maximum average-reward policies across all tasks, with the tightest constraint tracking in Gather environments and still outperforming all baselines in the high-dimensional Grid and Bottleneck tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACPO outperforms all five baselines across all environments. Against CPO (Achiam et al., 2017) and PCPO (Yang et al., 2020), ACPO achieves 52.5% and 36.1% higher average reward in Point-Gather, and 41.1% and 61.5% higher in Ant-Gather, without additional constraint violation. Against Lagrangian ATRPO (Zhang et al., 2021) and Lagrangian PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), ACPO dominates in Ant-Circle, Ant-Gather, Bottleneck, and Grid, where both ATRPO and PPO yield the lowest rewards while failing to satisfy constraints even with tuned Lagrangian parameters. Against BVF-PPO (Satija et al., 2020), which converts cumulative cost constraints into state-based constraints, ACPO achieves both higher reward and better constraint satisfaction; BVF-PPO fails to meet constraints even in the moderately difficult Ant-Gather environment. The key differentiator is that ACPO is the only method designed natively for the average-reward CMDP setting, whereas all baselines are either discounted-criterion methods (CPO, PCPO, PPO) with large γ=0.999 or unconstrained average-reward methods (ATRPO, PPO) with ad-hoc Lagrangian constraint handling.

## Relation to Prior Work

ACPO sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: trust-region constrained policy optimization (CPO, PCPO) for discounted CMDPs, and average-reward policy optimization (ATRPO, Zhang & Ross) for unconstrained average MDPs. The critical departure is that the policy improvement bounds underpinning CPO trivialize (diverge to −∞) as the discount factor approaches 1, necessitating entirely new sensitivity bounds based on eigenvalues of the policy-induced transition matrix rather than the standard Kemeny's constant. The algorithm also introduces sublevel sets of cost constraints and a novel recovery mechanism that blends reward and cost gradients, neither of which appears in prior CPO or ATRPO formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first practical, theoretically grounded policy optimization algorithm for average-constrained MDPs, filling a gap where researchers had to resort to discounted approximations or ad-hoc Lagrangian adaptations of PPO/ATRPO. The sensitivity-based bounds (Corollary 3.5, Theorem 3.6) provide the first worst-case performance degradation and constraint violation guarantees in the average setting, which is important for safety-critical applications in robotics, safety-constrained RLHF for LLMs, and diffusion model generation. Limitations include testing only a single constraint in practice, evaluation on a limited set of OpenAI Gym environments (state spaces up to 10^96), and the recovery mechanism being designed for one constraint. Open questions include whether the eigenvalue-based sensitivity bound σ* remains tight in very high-dimensional continuous settings and whether the algorithm scales to multiple simultaneous constraints without the computational overhead the authors note.
