## Problem

LoRA-type parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods fail to recover full fine-tuning (FFT) accuracy on complex downstream tasks such as mathematical reasoning and code generation, because the low-rank prior cannot capture the full structure of FFT weight updates. The paper identifies that FFT updates are not purely low-rank but exhibit a low-rank plus sparse decomposition (analogous to Robust PCA), and asks whether a PEFT method can exploit this structure to close the accuracy gap while maintaining LoRA-level memory and compute budgets.

## Input / Output

The method takes frozen pretrained LLM weights (optionally 4-bit double-quantized), a small mask-generation subset (32 samples) from the downstream dataset, and the full training set. It produces two sets of adapter parameters per linear layer: a low-rank factorization (matrices B ∈ R^{m×r} and A ∈ R^{r×n}) and a sparse adapter stored in CSR format with a fixed top-k mask at densities as low as 0.15%. The sparse masks are task-adaptive, generated after a LoRA warm-up phase, and the entire adapted model fits within a single 24 GB GPU (or under 12 GB with quantization).

## Method

RoSA jointly trains a standard LoRA adapter and a highly-sparse adapter in parallel on top of frozen base weights, motivated by the observation that FFT updates decompose well into a low-rank component plus a sparse outlier component (Robust PCA). The training procedure has three phases: (1) a 64-batch LoRA warm-up, (2) sparse mask generation via top-k selection on accumulated squared gradients of the partially-adapted weights (W + Δ̃L), and (3) joint optimization of both adapters with the masks fixed. The sparse backward pass uses a custom SDDMM kernel that exploits the observed row/column clustering in the masks (≈50% of rows or columns are entirely zero), achieving 1.36× geometric mean speedup over Sputnik. The QRoSA variant extends this to 4-bit quantized base weights, yielding the first joint representation combining quantization, low-rank, and sparse approximations.

## Results

On LLaMA2-7B fine-tuned for a single epoch, RoSA (80M params, r=16, d=0.6%) achieves 32.2% on GSM8k, 95.2% on ViGGO, and 88.3% on SQL, outperforming LoRA (29.6/87.0/89.1) and SpA (21.9/45.8/74.2) at the same budget, while using only ~20.7 GB of memory versus >60 GB for full fine-tuning. In extended training (2–3 epochs), RoSA matches or exceeds FFT on GSM8k (38.6% vs 38.8%) and ViGGO (97.3% vs 95.0%). The quantized variant QRoSA (4-bit base weights) reaches 33.1% on GSM8k with under 12 GB of memory, surpassing both QLoRA (29.8%) and FFT (32.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA, RoSA consistently wins on GSM8k (e.g., 32.2 vs 29.6 at 80M, 32.8 vs 27.4 at 160M) and ViGGO (95.2 vs 87.0 at 80M), while SQL results are nearly tied (88.3 vs 89.1 at 80M, though RoSA at 40M actually beats LoRA at 160M: 89.7 vs 88.7). Against pure Sparse Adaptation (SpA), RoSA dominates across all tasks and budgets — the gap is especially large on ViGGO (95.2 vs 45.8 at 80M) and GSM8k (32.2 vs 21.9 at 80M). Against QLoRA (4-bit quantized base weights), QRoSA achieves 33.1% on GSM8k vs QLoRA's 29.8% and 95.4% on ViGGO vs 88.0%, while SQL is slightly behind (86.6 vs 89.0 for the best configs). Against IA3, RoSA wins decisively on GSM8k (32.2 vs 13.1) and ViGGO (95.2 vs 38.2), with SQL close (88.3 vs 84.5). Notably, in the single-epoch regime RoSA surpasses full fine-tuning on all three datasets, and in extended training it matches or exceeds FFT on GSM8k and ViGGO while using 40–100× fewer trainable parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoSA extends the LoRA family of low-rank adaptation methods by adding a jointly-trained sparse adapter, motivated by the observation that full fine-tuning updates of LLMs are better approximated by a low-rank plus sparse decomposition (inspired by Robust PCA) than by low-rank alone. It builds directly on LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) for the low-rank component, FISH Mask (Sung et al., 2021) and DSEE (Chen et al., 2021) for sparse/hybrid adaptation, and QLoRA (Dettmers et al., 2023a) for quantized base weights. The key departures from prior work are: (1) task-aware mask generation via gradient accumulation after a short LoRA warmup period, which substantially outperforms both FISH Mask's Fisher-based masks and DSEE's task-independent RPCA masks; and (2) a custom sparse GPU kernel (SDDMM) that exploits the clustered row/column structure of the learned masks, achieving 1.36× geometric mean speedup over Sputnik.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RoSA demonstrates that the accuracy gap between PEFT and full fine-tuning can be closed or even reversed (in single-epoch settings) using 40–100× fewer parameters, all within a single 24 GB GPU (or <12 GB with 4-bit quantization), making FFT-quality adaptation accessible in resource-constrained settings. However, the evidence is limited to LLaMA2-7B and three datasets, the method is 1.7–2× slower than LoRA due to sparse kernel overheads, and it does not improve over LoRA on simpler instruction-tuning tasks where LoRA already matches FFT. Open questions include whether the RPCA-inspired decomposition generalizes to other architectures and scales, whether theoretical guarantees can be established for the joint low-rank + sparse optimization, and whether the 2× runtime penalty can be eliminated with further kernel engineering.
