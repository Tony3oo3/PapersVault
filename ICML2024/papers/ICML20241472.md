## Problem

Prior work showed that one gradient step with a constant step size in a two-layer neural network learns only linear features, leaving the effects of larger, growing step sizes on non-linear feature learning unexplored. This paper asks: which polynomial features emerge when the step size scales as η ~ n^α, how do they manifest in the feature matrix spectrum, and precisely how much do they reduce training and test errors in the high-dimensional proportional regime?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241472/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. Gaussian inputs x_i ∈ R^d with single-index teacher labels y_i = σ_*(β_⊤x_i) + ε_i, a two-layer network of width N, and a step size η ~ n^α, under the proportional scaling d/n → φ and d/N → ψ. It produces a spectral decomposition of the updated feature matrix into the untrained bulk plus ℓ separated rank-one spikes aligned with polynomial features (x⊤β)^k for k ∈ [ℓ], along with exact limiting formulas for training loss improvement Δ_ℓ and test error improvement Λ_ℓ as functions of the Hermite coefficients of the teacher and activation functions.

## Method

The training procedure applies one gradient descent step of size η ~ n^α to the first-layer weights (initialized uniformly on the unit sphere), then fits the second layer via ridge regression on a held-out data split. The core technical result is a spectral decomposition proving that when (ℓ−1)/(2ℓ) < α < ℓ/(2ℓ+2), the updated feature matrix F equals the untrained F_0 plus ℓ rank-one spike terms c_1^k c_k η^k (X̃β)^∘k(a^∘k)^⊤, with the remainder having operator norm o(√n); Wedin's theorem then shows the top-ℓ left singular vectors are asymptotically aligned with the polynomial features (X̃β)^∘k. To derive error characterizations, the authors prove equivalence theorems establishing that replacing F with F_ℓ = F_0 + spikes leaves limiting training and test errors unchanged, then apply the Gaussian equivalence property to the untrained component F_0 to obtain closed-form expressions for the error improvements. The results reveal a staircase property: errors are constant within each α regime and jump at the boundaries, with the ℓ = 2 regime specifically capturing quadratic components of the teacher function that are inaccessible at smaller step sizes.

## Results

The paper proves that with step size η ≍ n^α where (ℓ−1)/(2ℓ) < α < ℓ/(2ℓ+2), the updated feature matrix acquires ℓ separated spectral spikes whose left singular vectors align with polynomial features (X̃β)^∘k for k ∈ [ℓ]. For ℓ=1, the training loss improves over untrained random features by Δ₁ = ψλc_{★,1}⁴m₂ / [φ(c_{★,1}² + φ(c_{★}² + σ_ε²))] ≥ 0, and for ℓ=2 an additional term 4ψλc_{★,1}⁴c_{★,2}²m₁ / [3φ(φ(c_{★}² + σ_ε²) + c_{★,1}²)²] captures quadratic feature learning. Numerical simulations (n=1000, N=500, d=300, shifted ReLU, λ=0.01) confirm two isolated spikes in the spectrum at η=n^0.29 and show training/test errors decreasing in log(η)/log(n) ∈ (0, 1/4) for the linear component, with a further drop in (1/4, 1/3) when a quadratic component is present in the teacher.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against untrained random features (F₀ = σ(X̃W₀ᵀ)), where the paper proves strict improvement in both training and test error for any α > 0. Against the constant-step-size regime of Ba et al. (2022) (α=0, ℓ=1 only), this paper shows that growing the step size to the ℓ=2 regime unlocks learning of the quadratic Hermite component c_{★,2}, which is provably impossible at constant step size. Against linear predictors and NTK/kernel methods, the paper inherits the result from Ba et al. (2022) that the one-step updated network can outperform them when the teacher has a non-zero first Hermite coefficient, and extends this to show the advantage grows with the strength of higher-order components. The staircase property (Section 4.3) shows that within each regime the error is asymptotically constant in the step-size constant c, with sharp jumps only at regime boundaries—contrasting with the smooth monotone decrease seen in mean-field or gradient-flow analyses (Abbe et al., 2021; Berthier et al., 2023). No direct numerical comparison against competing feature-learning methods is provided; the empirical validation is limited to confirming the spectral predictions and the qualitative error-curve shape.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-step feature learning analysis of Ba et al. (2022) and Damian et al. (2022), which showed that a constant step size only yields linear features, by considering step sizes that grow with sample size (η ∝ n^α). It builds on the Gaussian equivalence property (Goldt et al., 2022; Hu & Lu, 2023) and the spectral characterization of random feature matrices (Pennington & Worah, 2017), but must overcome the failure of Gaussian equivalence after large gradient steps by establishing new equivalence theorems. The analysis draws on spiked random matrix theory (Capitaine, 2014; Dozier & Silverstein, 2007) and Hermite polynomial expansions to characterize the emerging non-linear features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first rigorous characterization of which non-linear (polynomial) features emerge after a single gradient step with growing step size, filling a critical gap between the constant-step-size regime (linear only) and the very-large-step-size regime. The staircase property—where features are learned in discrete jumps as the step size crosses specific thresholds—offers a mechanistic explanation for hierarchical learning observed empirically in deeper training. Key open questions include the boundary case η ∝ n^((ℓ-1)/(2ℓ)), the α = 1/2 regime where the number of spikes diverges, and extending the analysis to same-data training without sample splitting.
