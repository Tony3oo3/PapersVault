## Problem

In multi-agent reinforcement learning with sparse rewards, existing exploration methods that add global intrinsic rewards to extrinsic rewards introduce non-stationarity into the training objective and complicate credit assignment among agents. Balancing these two reward streams also demands significant tuning without prior knowledge of the extrinsic reward structure. The paper asks whether exploration can be guided by individual agent contributions rather than a shared global intrinsic signal, thereby avoiding the credit assignment burden entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241139/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During centralized training, each agent's exploration policy receives both its local action-observation history τ^i and the privileged global observation s (e.g., a 720×1280×3 pixel frame in KAZ or a vector state in GRF/SMAC), while the exploitation policy and all execution-time inference use only local observations. The scaffolds network consumes global state transitions (s_t, u_t, s_{t+1}) to compute per-agent intrinsic signals. At execution, only the exploitation network is deployed, producing a joint action u ∈ U^n with no access to global state or the exploration policy.

## Method

ICES constructs per-agent intrinsic exploration scaffolds by estimating Bayesian surprise: the KL divergence between two CVAE priors, p_ψ(z_{t+1}|s_t, u_t) and p_φ(z_{t+1}|s_t, u_t^{-i}), which quantifies how much agent i's action shifts the latent state distribution relative to a counterfactual where that action is removed. The two priors share a decoder to align their latent spaces, preventing misalignment that would invalidate the KL measure. Exploration and exploitation policies are fully decoupled: the exploitation policy is trained with standard DQN/QMIX on extrinsic rewards, while the exploration policy is trained with REINFORCE-with-baseline to maximize the individual scaffold signal plus entropy regularization, using the global observation as input. During training, a behavior policy mixes exploration and exploitation actions with probability α; after training, only the exploitation network is retained for decentralized execution, so the scaffolds impose no runtime cost.

## Results

ICES is evaluated on three GRF scenarios (3_vs_1_with_keeper, corner, counterattack_hard) and five SMAC scenarios (3m, 8m, 2s3z, 2s_vs_1sc, 5m_vs_6m) under sparse reward settings, with metrics being average score (GRF: +1 win, 0 tie, −1 loss) and average win rate (SMAC). ICES consistently achieves faster convergence and higher final performance than all six baselines across both benchmarks. In the SMAC 2s_vs_1sc scenario, ICES begins observing winning episodes well before 2M timesteps, whereas none of the baselines have yet achieved a win at that point. Ablation studies confirm the importance of individual (vs. global) contributions, with ICES reaching >60% win rate in 3_vs_1_with_keeper versus only 40% for the global-contribution variant, and 60% vs. 20% when global observation is removed from the exploration policy in SMAC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICES (built on QMIX) outperforms all six baselines—QMIX, MAVEN, CDS, EMC-QPLEX, MASER, and ADER—on both GRF and SMAC in terms of convergence speed and final win rate/score. When paired with QPLEX as the exploitation backbone (ICES-QPLEX), it further surpasses the QPLEX-based baseline EMC-QPLEX, which nonetheless shows the strongest early-stage exploration among the baselines. On GRF, ICES handles environmental stochasticity and coordination demands more effectively than MAVEN (hierarchical latent exploration) and CDS (diversity-based exploration), achieving higher final scores across all three scenarios. On SMAC, ICES's compact latent-space scaffolds address the large state-space challenge more efficiently than ADER (adaptive entropy regularization) and MASER (subgoal generation from replay), with the gap being most pronounced in the harder 2s_vs_1sc and 5m_vs_6m scenarios. Overall, no baseline matches ICES on both convergence speed and final performance simultaneously; EMC-QPLEX is the closest competitor in early training but plateaus below ICES.

## Relation to Prior Work

ICES builds on the CTDE paradigm and value decomposition methods (QMIX, QPLEX) as its exploitation backbone, while departing from the dominant paradigm of global intrinsic rewards for multi-agent exploration (MAVEN, CDS, EMC, MASER). Its key conceptual departure is replacing global intrinsic rewards—which require credit assignment and introduce non-stationarity—with individual contributions estimated via Bayesian surprise in a CVAE latent space, drawing on single-agent curiosity literature (Itti & Baldi, 2005; Mazzaglia et al., 2022) and repurposing CVAE architecture (Sohn et al., 2015) for multi-agent credit-free exploration. The decoupling of exploration and exploitation policies, allowing the former to access privileged global observations during centralized training, is a novel architectural choice not present in prior MARL exploration methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ICES offers a practical and architecturally clean solution to sparse-reward MARL exploration that avoids the credit assignment complications of global intrinsic rewards, validated across GRF, SMAC, and pixel-based KAZ benchmarks. The ablation showing that removing global observation from the exploration policy drops SMAC performance from 60% to 20% winning rate underscores the value of privileged information in exploration, a finding with broader implications for CTDE-based methods. Key limitations include the additional training complexity from separate policy networks (scaling concerns for large agent teams) and the restriction to one-step latent transitions, which the authors acknowledge may be insufficient for more complex scenarios. Open questions include whether the approach extends to continuous action spaces, larger agent populations, and whether multi-step or time-abstracted scaffolds could further improve exploration in long-horizon tasks.
