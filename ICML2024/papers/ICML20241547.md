## Problem

Despite the widespread adoption of both RLHF and DPO for learning from human preferences, no prior work provides a unified statistical comparison of their sample complexity and convergence behavior. It remains unclear under what conditions—sample size, reward vs. policy dimensionality, regularization temperature—one paradigm is provably superior to the other, or when they are statistically comparable.

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on offline preference datasets of the form {(x, y^w, y^l)} drawn under a Bradley-Terry model, with linear reward features φ (dimension d_R, bounded norm) and loglinear policy features ψ (dimension d_P, bounded norm), a fixed reference policy μ, and a KL-regularization temperature β. The outputs are minimax suboptimality gap bounds and exponential convergence rates expressed as functions of d_R, d_P, n, β, and data-dependent covering numbers Λ_R and Λ_P, rather than a trained model. A key structural detail is that the bounds decompose into a statistical estimation term plus a regularization gap D(π) that captures the discrepancy between the unregularized and KL-regularized objectives.

## Method

The paper derives minimax bounds on the suboptimality gap for both RLHF and DPO by exploiting the smoothness and strong convexity of the log-sum-exp function that arises from loglinear policy parametrization. For RLHF, the reward learning phase yields a √(d_R/n) statistical term via MLE concentration, while the policy optimization phase uses a natural policy gradient update with O(e^{-t}/β) convergence; for DPO, the loss reduces to logistic regression in d_P+1 dimensions, and the PL condition gives O((1/β)(1−β/n)^t) convergence. The comparison reveals that RLHF's gap scales as Θ̃(√(d_R/n)) while DPO's scales as Θ(d_P/(βn)), so setting β = Θ(√(d_P/n)) for DPO matches RLHF's sample-size dependence, leaving the reward-vs-policy dimension gap as the decisive factor. The analysis is extended to non-realizable rewards (where RLHF incurs an irreducible O(ε_app) term but DPO can suppress it via β tuning) and to deterministic MDPs via a new DPO loss formulated over loglinear occupancy measures.

## Results

The paper derives minimax suboptimality gap bounds for RLHF and DPO under loglinear policies and linear rewards. Under realizable rewards with exact optimization, RLHF achieves Õ(√(d_R/n)) while DPO achieves Θ(d_P/(βn)); setting β = Θ(√(d_P/n)) makes DPO match RLHF's sample-size dependence. For non-realizable rewards, RLHF incurs an irreducible O(ε_app) constant error, whereas DPO can asymptotically eliminate this gap by tuning β = O(1/√n). In the approximate optimization setting, both paradigms exhibit exponential convergence: RLHF via natural policy gradient at O(e^{-t}/β) and DPO via gradient descent at O((1/β)(1−β/n)^t).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; the comparison is between the RLHF and DPO paradigms themselves. When d_R = d_P and n is small (n < d, typical for LLMs), RLHF has a smaller suboptimality gap due to its √(d_R/n) dependence versus DPO's d_P/(βn). For large n ≫ d, DPO asymptotically outperforms RLHF. When d_R ≪ d_P (common in MDPs where reward models are low-dimensional but policies are high-dimensional), RLHF is favored. Under non-realizable rewards, DPO has a structural advantage: by setting β = O(1/√n), its additional error term O(β·D_KL(π_θ*‖π_r*)) vanishes asymptotically, while RLHF's O(ε_app) term is a constant that cannot be reduced by more data. The MDP extension (Section 7) amplifies the dimension gap, further favoring RLHF in settings with simple reward structures but complex policies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as the first systematic theoretical comparison between RLHF (Ziegler et al., 2019) and DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), two paradigms that had previously been analyzed in isolation. It builds directly on Zhu et al. (2023) for RLHF reward-learning bounds, on the PL condition framework of Karimi et al. (2016) for convergence rates, and on Mei et al. (2020) for natural policy gradient analysis. The MDP extension draws on KL-regularized RL formulations from Nachum et al. (2019) and Lee et al. (2021), and is motivated by but distinct from Hejna et al. (2023)'s DPO-to-MDP extension, which assumed advantage-dependent preferences rather than deriving a new loss from deterministic transitions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a clear decision framework: RLHF is favored when reward dimension is small relative to policy dimension or when sample sizes are limited (n < d), while DPO asymptotically wins for large n with tuned β. The non-realizable reward result—that RLHF incurs an irreducible constant error while DPO can suppress its mismatch term via temperature—is a conceptually useful distinction. However, the analysis is confined to finite spaces, linear rewards, loglinear policies, and deterministic MDPs, leaving open whether these conclusions hold under general function approximation or stochastic dynamics. A large-scale empirical validation of the predicted dimensionality and sample-size regimes remains a significant gap.
