## Problem

The temperature parameter in softmax and contrastive losses critically controls probability concentration in LLMs and CLIP models, yet current practice relies on a single empirically chosen value that ignores data heterogeneity. While per-sample temperature optimization via KL-DRO has been shown to improve contrastive learning, it suffers from linearly growing memory costs, overfitting, and zero transferability to new data. The paper asks whether a small, generalizable neural network can predict a personalized temperature for any input, and provides a principled DRO-based framework to learn such a network.

## Input / Output

TempNet consumes the output of the foundation model rather than raw data: for LLMs it receives the full vocabulary logit vector (e.g., 32,000-dim for LLaMA2), and for CLIP it receives the normalized image or text embedding (e.g., 512-dim from ResNet-50/DistilBERT). A stop-gradient is applied to this input so TempNet training does not perturb the foundation model's representations. The output is a single scalar temperature τ constrained to [τ₀, τ_max] (e.g., [0.001, 2.0] for LLMs, [0.001, 0.05] for CLIP), which is plugged into the robust loss during training and into the softmax during inference.

## Method

The framework derives a KL-DRO robust loss whose Lagrangian dual yields a closed-form objective f_Θ(z, τ) = τ log(1/|C| Σ exp((L_k(z)−L_+(z))/τ)) + τρ, where τ is a per-instance temperature to be minimized. A variational-analysis lemma justifies replacing per-sample τ with a function τ(z) approximated by a neural network. TempNet itself has three components: (i) a transformation-projection block that reduces the high-dimensional logits/embedding to a small set of 'prototypical logits' via a feed-forward layer followed by a normalized matrix-vector product; (ii) a parameterized pooling layer that computes (attention-weighted mean − uniform mean) of the prototypical logits scaled by a learnable φ, directly mimicking the implicit optimal-τ formula from Lemma 5.1; and (iii) a sigmoid output layer mapping to [τ₀, τ_max]. The network is trained by backpropagation on the robust loss with stop-gradient on the foundation model's output, and can be trained from scratch, jointly with a pretrained model, or separately with a frozen model using only ~8M parameters for LLMs.

## Results

On contrastive learning (Table 3), TempNet achieves 46.17% IR@1 and 62.51% TR@1 on Flickr30K, 24.83% IR@1 and 34.50% TR@1 on MSCOCO, and 42.28% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K, outperforming all baselines. On instruction following (Table 1), TempNet-enabled LLaMA2 7B achieves a 5.74% length-controlled win rate on AlpacaEval 2.0 (vs. 5.35% baseline), and LLaMA2 70B reaches 15.83% (vs. 14.69%). On common-sense reasoning (Table 2), TempNet-enabled LLaMA2-7B improves HellaSwag accuracy from 57.0% to 61.7% and ARC-c from 42.7% to 45.2% over the fixed-τ=1.0 baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TempNet outperforms all four baselines across every metric in the contrastive learning experiments. Against CLIP (Radford et al., 2021), TempNet gains +5.19 points on Flickr30K IR@1 and +6.01 points on ImageNet-1K top-1 accuracy. Against CyCLIP (Goel et al., 2022), which uses cyclic temperature scheduling, TempNet leads by +3.71 on Flickr30K IR@1 and +5.53 on ImageNet-1K. Against SogCLR (Yuan et al., 2022), which uses a fixed tuned global temperature τ=0.01, TempNet improves by +2.85 on Flickr30K IR@1 and +4.82 on ImageNet-1K. Most notably, against iSogCLR (Qiu et al., 2023), which optimizes individualized temperatures per sample via Lagrangian duality, TempNet achieves consistent gains of 1.0–2.3 points (e.g., 46.17 vs. 44.36 on Flickr30K IR@1, 42.28 vs. 40.72 on ImageNet-1K), demonstrating that a small learned network generalizes better than per-sample temperature optimization while also being transferable to new datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Qiu et al. (2023), which introduced a KL-DRO-based robust contrastive loss with per-sample individualized temperatures optimized via Lagrangian duality (iSogCLR). The key departure is replacing non-scalable, per-sample temperature optimization with a small learned network (TempNet) that predicts temperatures from the foundation model's outputs, grounded in variational analysis (Lemma 4.1) and a theory-inspired parameterized pooling layer derived from the implicit optimal-temperature equation (Lemma 5.1). Prior temperature-prediction work (Wang et al. 2020, Zhang et al. 2021, Li et al. 2023b) used ad-hoc architectures and single-task settings; this paper unifies LLM softmax and CLIP contrastive losses under one DRO framework and designs TempNet to operate on both.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TempNet offers a practical, low-overhead mechanism (8.2M params for LLMs, 0.13M for CLIP) to inject input-dependent temperature into any pretrained LFM without retraining the base model, with demonstrated transferability across datasets and robustness to noisy captions. The interpretable correlation between predicted temperatures and text subjectivity or instruction type suggests a useful diagnostic for understanding when models should be creative versus factual. Open questions include whether the approach scales to frontier-scale LLMs trained on much larger corpora, whether it extends to other LFMs (e.g., diffusion models, audio), and whether the DRO constraint parameter ρ can be set automatically rather than tuned per task.
