## Problem

Existing Transformer-based and neural-field-based PDE solvers each lack at least one desirable property of an ideal surrogate: generalization to unseen PDE parameters, spatial and temporal zero-shot super-resolution, continuous temporal extrapolation, support across 1D/2D/3D, or efficient long-rollout inference. Standard Transformers additionally suffer from quadratic memory and time complexity, while neural fields (e.g., PINNs) process each spatial coordinate independently, wasting spatial dependencies and requiring separate forward passes per query point.

## Input / Output

The model receives a single-timestep initial condition (IC), a vector of PDE parameters, a scalar query time t, and an arbitrary set of s spatial coordinates (no regular-grid constraint required) as input, and outputs the PDE solution at all s queried spatial points for that time in one forward pass. Because the model is continuous in time, it can be queried at any t ∈ (0, T] and on any spatial grid (including finer grids than seen during training) without retraining. For 2D and 3D PDEs, the IC is encoded via multi-scale non-overlapping patches (sizes 4×4 and 16×16, or 4 and 16 in 1D) to capture dynamics at multiple spatial scales.

## Method

VCNeF extends conditional neural fields by vectorizing the spatial query: instead of processing each coordinate independently, it feeds all s spatial points through a shared architecture that uses linear self-attention to capture inter-point spatial dependencies. The IC is first encoded into a latent representation (via shared linear layers for 1D, or multi-scale 2D/3D convolutional patching for higher dimensions), then refined by a Linear Transformer encoder. The core mechanism is a stack of modulation blocks that condition the latent spatio-temporal coordinates on the IC using FiLM-style scaling (pointwise multiplication only, omitting the shift), combined with self-attention and an ELU+1 nonlinearity. This design makes the model non-autoregressive (predicting the full trajectory in one shot), time-continuous (enabling temporal zero-shot super-resolution), and parameter-conditioned (enabling generalization to unseen PDE parameter values), while linear attention keeps complexity O(s·N_t) rather than quadratic.

## Results

On the fixed-parameter PDEBench evaluation (Table 2), VCNeF-R (with randomized starting points) achieves the best nRMSE across all 1D PDEs: 0.0784 on Burgers (−20.6% vs FNO), 0.0113 on Advection (−40.5%), and 0.2029 on 1D CNS (−64.5%). For 2D CNS, VCNeF achieves nRMSE 0.1994 (−64.6% vs FNO) and bRMSE 0.0904 (−61.2%), and for 3D CNS it reaches nRMSE 0.7086 (−12.9% vs FNO). The model also demonstrates spatial and temporal zero-shot super-resolution with negligible error increase when tested at 4× higher spatial or temporal resolution than training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VCNeF/VCNeF-R outperforms the FNO baseline (the strongest single model) on nRMSE for all five PDEs, with the largest gains on 1D CNS (−64.5%) and 2D CNS (−64.6%). Against OFormer, VCNeF wins on nRMSE for 1D Burgers (0.0824 vs 0.1035), 1D Advection (0.0165 vs 0.0118 — OFormer slightly ahead), 1D CNS (0.2943 vs 0.4415), and 2D CNS (0.1994 vs not reported), while OFormer edges out VCNeF on 1D Burgers bRMSE (0.0215 vs 0.0228). VCNeF decisively beats Galerkin Transformer and CORAL on every PDE tested, with CORAL degrading severely on 1D CNS (nRMSE 0.5993) and 2D CNS (training diverged). UNet from PDEArena is competitive on 1D tasks (e.g., 0.0566 nRMSE on Burgers vs VCNeF's 0.0824) but fails on 2D CNS (nRMSE 1.4240 vs 0.1994) and is not evaluated on 3D CNS. MP-PDE, the GNN-based baseline, is only competitive on 1D Advection and is substantially worse on 1D Burgers (nRMSE 0.3046 vs 0.0784). For PDE parameter generalization (unseen parameters), VCNeF shows the tightest error distribution compared to cFNO and cOFormer, confirming the effectiveness of its parameter conditioning mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

VCNeF extends Conditional Neural Fields (CNeFs; Xie et al., 2021) by vectorizing spatial queries so that multiple spatio-temporal points are processed jointly in a single forward pass, with linear self-attention (Katharopoulos et al., 2020) modeling their interdependencies. The conditioning mechanism adapts FiLM scaling (Perez et al., 2018) to modulate coordinate representations based on initial conditions and PDE parameters. Conceptually, the paper positions VCNeF as a bridge between two families: neural fields/PINNs (time-continuous but spatially independent) and neural operators like FNO and OFormer (spatially joint but typically not time-continuous), claiming to inherit the strengths of both. The multi-scale patching for 2D/3D encoding draws from CrossViT (Chen et al., 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VCNeF simultaneously addresses five desiderata that no single prior method satisfies: spatial and temporal zero-shot super-resolution, PDE parameter generalization, IC generalization, 1D/2D/3D support, and efficient long-rollout inference. The near-zero degradation in error when quadrupling spatial resolution (e.g., 0.0824→0.0839 nRMSE on Burgers) or increasing temporal resolution 5× is practically significant for applications requiring high-fidelity output from low-resolution training. Key limitations include GPU memory constraints for parallel multi-timestep inference and restriction to regular grids for 2D/3D PDEs. Open questions include whether the approach extends to turbulent flows, whether adaptive time-stepping or physics-informed losses further improve accuracy, and how the training-time temporal discretization affects ZSSR quality.
