## Problem

Text-guided diffusion models have advanced text-to-audio generation but lack a flexible, precise mechanism for modifying specific events within an audio track while preserving unrelated content. Existing audio editing approaches either require end-to-end training on large editing-pair datasets or rely on full regeneration that disrupts the overall audio structure, and image-editing techniques based on spatial masks do not generalize to the temporal-spectral nature of audio.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242271/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an audio clip (encoded to mel-spectrogram latents via a VAE), a source text prompt describing the original audio, and a target text prompt specifying the desired edit, and produces a single edited audio waveform. No spatial or temporal masks are required; the edit is driven entirely by the textual prompt. The pipeline runs multiple denoising passes at different guidance scales in parallel and selects the final output via a CLAP-based relevance filter, so the user only specifies the target prompt and optionally a set of candidate guidance scales.

## Method

PPAE is a training-free plug-in module for latent diffusion models that performs precise audio editing by manipulating cross-attention maps during the denoising process. The source audio is first inverted into the model's noise space using edit-friendly DDPM inversion, then during each denoising step the source and target attention maps are blended via a Fuser module governed by a CosineAnnealing scheduler, which gradually transitions from the source map to the target map to avoid abrupt quality degradation. Three editing modes are supported: Replace (full attention-map swap), Refine (token-specific modification for style/attribute changes), and Reweight (scaled token modification for volume/emphasis control). A global guidance-bootstrapping strategy runs the full denoising pipeline across a set of guidance scales and uses a CLAP model to select the output most relevant to the target prompt, eliminating the need for manual scale tuning. The entire pipeline is compatible with any U-Net-based latent diffusion model (demonstrated on Tango) and requires no retraining.

## Results

On the Audio Replace task (FSD50K-based test set, 100 samples), PPAE achieves FAD 2.15, LSD 1.51, FD 27.53, KL 1.30, and CLAP 0.62, substantially outperforming the PTP baseline (FAD 2.95, FD 45.91, KL 4.42) and the regeneration baseline (FAD 4.93, FD 32.94). For Audio Refine, PPAE reaches FAD 6.86 and CLAP 0.63 versus 8.19/0.25 for unedited audio and 9.35/0.32 for PTP. In Audio Reweight, PPAE achieves a CLAP control range of 0.10–0.83 across scale factors c=−1 to c=2, compared to 0.32–0.79 for PTP, demonstrating stronger per-token control while keeping unrelated components stable (CLAP ~0.82–0.93).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the PTP (Prompt-to-Prompt) baseline adapted for audio, PPAE wins decisively on the Replace task: FAD drops from 2.95 to 2.15, FD from 45.91 to 27.53, and KL from 4.42 to 1.30, while CLAP improves from 0.57 to 0.62. On Refine, PPAE outperforms PTP on FAD (6.86 vs 9.35), LSD (1.55 vs 1.94), FD (43.31 vs 45.88), and CLAP (0.63 vs 0.32), though PTP achieves a lower KL (1.17 vs 1.92). For Reweight, PPAE provides a wider and more monotonic control range (CLAP 0.83 at c=2 down to 0.10 at c=−1) compared to PTP (0.79 to 0.32), indicating finer-grained per-token manipulation. Against Audit (Wang et al., 2023), the comparison is qualitative only since Audit is not open-source; the paper's case study (Fig. 4) shows that Audit regenerates the full audio and alters its structure, whereas PPAE preserves the original temporal structure while replacing only the targeted event. Subjective evaluation confirms PPAE's advantage: on Replace, PPAE scores 95.71 Relevance and 95.0 Consistency versus 89.28 and 81.42 for the regeneration baseline, with the gap widening on Reweight (99.28/94.28 vs 92.14/82.85).

## Relation to Prior Work

PPAE adapts the cross-attention map manipulation paradigm from image editing (Prompt-to-Prompt, Hertz et al. 2022) to the audio domain, combined with Null-text Inversion (Mokady et al. 2023) and Edit-friendly DDPM inversion (Huberman-Spiegelglas et al. 2023) for the inversion step. The key departure is the introduction of a Fuser module with a CosineAnnealing scheduler, motivated by the observation that direct attention map injection (as in PTP) causes abrupt quality degradation in audio due to the temporal-spectral nature of the signal. Unlike prior audio editing methods such as Audit (Wang et al. 2023) that require end-to-end training on editing pairs, PPAE is entirely training-free and functions as a plug-in controller on top of any pre-trained TTA diffusion model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PPAE offers a practical, training-free path to precise local audio editing that is compatible with existing diffusion backbones (Tango, AudioLDM, Make-An-Audio), lowering the barrier for fine-grained audio manipulation without collecting editing demonstration pairs. However, the approach is limited to attention-map-level modifications and does not guarantee structural-level precision for more complex tasks like audio fusion; processing time also precludes real-time use. Open questions include whether the Fuser mechanism generalizes across architectures beyond Tango, whether the guidance bootstrapping strategy scales to longer or more complex audio, and how to address the ethical risks of precise audio manipulation for generating misleading content.
