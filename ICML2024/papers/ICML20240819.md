## Problem

Existing online change detection (OCD) methods for dynamic systems assume perfect knowledge of system parameters (e.g., the measurement matrix in smart grids), an assumption that fails in practice due to estimation errors, environmental fluctuations, and system updates. Moreover, prior approaches are either centralized (incurring privacy and communication costs) or synchronously distributed (suffering from straggler delays). This paper is the first to address OCD with parameter uncertainties in a fully asynchronous distributed setting, providing certifiable robustness, provable optimality, and non-asymptotic convergence guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes time-varying observation vectors y^(t) ∈ R^M collected locally by L geographically dispersed sub-regions, along with a constraint-wise uncertainty set U for the system matrix H (rather than a single known matrix). At each time step, the algorithm solves a nested optimization to compute the CUSUM increment v_t, and the overall detector outputs a change declaration time T_G (when the recursive statistic V_J exceeds a threshold ζ) together with the estimated attack vector x^(t) orthogonal to the column space of H. The system is partitioned into sub-regions with partially overlapping state variables, so each worker only accesses its local measurements and communicates with a master node.

## Method

Triadic-OCD reformulates the generalized CUSUM increment computation as a bilevel optimization: the outer level minimizes a quadratic objective over the attack vector subject to box constraints and protection-function inequalities, while the inner level estimates the uncertain system matrix columns via D iterations of gradient descent on an augmented Lagrangian. The non-convex protection functions are handled by a cutting-plane approximation that is refreshed every w iterations (up to K₁ iterations), with new planes generated whenever the current iterate violates a constraint. Variable updates follow an asynchronous distributed protocol: workers update local variables (μ_l, r_l, p_l) using stale information from their last active iteration, and the master updates global variables (x, q_s, dual variables) only upon receiving updates from S active workers, with each worker guaranteed to communicate at least once every τ iterations. A regularized Lagrangian (following Xu et al., 2023) with decaying regularization coefficients c^k ∝ (k+1)^(-1/4) enables the non-asymptotic convergence proof, yielding an iteration complexity of O(1/ε²) to reach an ε-optimal point.

## Results

On the IEEE-14 bus system (4 sub-regions, 500 Monte Carlo runs), Triadic-OCD consistently achieves a smaller average detection delay than all three baselines for any given False Alarm Period (FAP), as shown in Figure 1. In the successful detection rate evaluation (Figure 3), Triadic-OCD outperforms both RGCUSUM and WCD across all upper bounds on detection delay when tested against 10³ randomly generated attack vectors. The method also demonstrates robustness across ellipsoid, D-norm, and polyhedron uncertainty sets (Figure 2), and converges significantly faster in asynchronous mode than its synchronous counterpart (Figure 4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Triadic-OCD outperforms all three baselines on the primary metric of average detection delay vs. FAP. Adaptive CUSUM (Huang et al., 2011) exhibits the worst performance, primarily because it presupposes a Gaussian distribution of the state variable θ^(t), which does not hold in the dynamic smart-grid setting. WCD (Li et al., 2014) and RGCUSUM (Zhang & Wang, 2021) both achieve commendable performance only when the system matrix H is precisely known; with the imperfect H estimates used in the experiment (uncertainty in the 4th sub-region), their detection performance degrades rapidly. Triadic-OCD, by contrast, explicitly handles the uncertainty in H via the constraint-wise uncertainty model and protection functions, maintaining superior detection delay and successful detection rate. The paper also shows that the asynchronous variant of Triadic-OCD converges significantly faster than the synchronous version under log-normal worker delays (LN(1, 0.5)), confirming the straggler-mitigation benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) CUSUM-based online change detection for dynamic systems (Huang et al. 2011, Li et al. 2014, Zhang & Wang 2021), which all assume perfect knowledge of the system matrix H; (2) robust optimization with constraint-wise uncertainty sets and protection functions (Yang et al. 2014), which provides the mathematical machinery to handle parameter uncertainty; and (3) asynchronous distributed bilevel optimization (Jiao et al. 2022, Zhang & Kwok 2014), which supplies the straggler-tolerant update mechanism. The key departure from prior OCD work is the simultaneous introduction of parameter uncertainty (via uncertainty sets on H) and an asynchronous distributed solving strategy, whereas previous methods either assume H is known exactly or operate in a centralized/synchronous setting. The cutting-plane approximation of the protection function constraints and the augmented Lagrangian inner-loop for the bilevel structure are borrowed from the robust optimization and bilevel optimization literatures respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to provide certifiable robustness, provable optimality, and non-asymptotic convergence guarantees for OCD under parameter uncertainty in an asynchronous distributed setting, which is directly relevant to practical smart-grid and MIMO security applications where perfect channel/state knowledge is unattainable. However, the experimental validation is limited to the IEEE-14 bus system (a small-scale benchmark) and a linear DC power flow model, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger, nonlinear grids or to settings with time-varying uncertainty sets. The constraint-wise (row-decoupled) uncertainty model, while versatile, may be conservative for correlated parameter uncertainties, and the fixed number of inner-loop gradient steps D introduces a robustness-accuracy tradeoff that is not systematically explored. Broader applicability beyond linear systems and the interaction between the cutting-plane refinement schedule and detection latency remain open questions.
