## Problem

SCALE-UP, the most advanced input-level backdoor detection method, relies on amplifying pixel values to detect poisoned samples, but pixel values are bounded in [0,255], causing benign samples with extreme pixels to remain unchanged and poisoned samples to lose their triggers when clipped to 255. This paper asks whether scaling model parameters—which are unbounded—can expose backdoors more reliably than scaling pixels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240764/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates in a white-box setting, requiring full access to the deployed model's BN parameters and the predicted confidence scores (not just labels, as in black-box SCALE-UP). It consumes a suspicious RGB image, the model's BN layer parameters (γ, β), and 100 local benign samples used for adaptive layer selection. It outputs a PSC score (average confidence across n=5 parameter-scaled models on the original model's predicted label) and a binary poisoned/benign decision via threshold T=0.9.

## Method

IBD-PSC exploits Parameter-oriented Scaling Consistency (PSC): when BN layer parameters (γ, β) are amplified by a factor ω=1.5, poisoned samples retain high prediction confidence while benign samples' confidence drops sharply. The method has two stages: (1) Model Amplification—starting from the last BN layer, it progressively amplifies more consecutive BN layers to create n=5 scaled models, with an adaptive algorithm selecting the starting layer k by monitoring when the error rate on local benign samples exceeds 60%; (2) Input Detection—it computes the PSC value as the mean confidence on the original model's predicted label across all scaled models and flags the sample as poisoned if PSC > T. Theoretically, Theorem 3.1 proves that under a Gaussian mixture assumption on features, amplifying BN parameters can drive feature norms arbitrarily large, and beyond a constant M the model always predicts the attacker's target class, explaining why poisoned samples remain consistent while benign ones do not.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, IBD-PSC achieves an average AUROC of 0.992 and F1 of 0.961 across seven representative backdoor attacks, substantially outperforming SCALE-UP (AUROC 0.731, F1 0.757), TeCo (AUROC 0.858, F1 0.834), and STRIP (AUROC 0.663, F1 0.494). On GTSRB and SubImageNet-200, IBD-PSC maintains average AUROC of 0.969 and 0.990 respectively, with F1 scores of 0.962 and 0.974. Notably, IBD-PSC achieves near-perfect detection (AUROC ≥ 0.97) on all seven attacks on CIFAR-10, while SCALE-UP and STRIP fail (AUROC < 0.7) on attacks involving subtle pixel-level alterations (Blend, IAD, WaNet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IBD-PSC decisively outperforms all three baselines across every dataset and attack. Against SCALE-UP (the closest prior IBD method), IBD-PSC improves average AUROC by 0.261 on CIFAR-10 (0.992 vs. 0.731) and 0.269 on GTSRB (0.969 vs. 0.700); the gap is most dramatic on Blend (0.998 vs. 0.644) and IAD (0.983 vs. 0.967 on CIFAR-10, but 0.970 vs. 0.309 on GTSRB), where pixel-value saturation causes SCALE-UP to fail. Against TeCo, IBD-PSC gains 0.134 average AUROC on CIFAR-10 (0.992 vs. 0.858), with the largest margin on Blend (0.998 vs. 0.675) and PhysicalBA (0.972 vs. 0.748). Against STRIP, the advantage is overwhelming: IBD-PSC achieves 0.992 vs. 0.663 average AUROC on CIFAR-10, while STRIP collapses to near-random performance (AUROC < 0.5) on Blend, WaNet, ISSBA, and BATT. IBD-PSC also reduces false positive rates substantially: on CIFAR-10, its FPR on target and benign classes is typically below 10%, compared to 20–94% for SCALE-UP (Table 4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SCALE-UP (Guo et al., ICLR 2023), which detected backdoors by observing prediction consistency under pixel-value amplification, by shifting the scaling operation from bounded pixel values to unbounded batch normalization parameters. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that pixel values are constrained to [0, 255], which causes SCALE-UP to fail when amplification saturates pixels and erases triggers, whereas BN parameters (γ, β) have no such upper bound. The theoretical analysis draws on neural collapse and Gaussian mixture assumptions to prove that sufficiently large feature norms (induced by BN parameter scaling) force classification toward the target class, providing a principled foundation absent in prior IBD methods like STRIP and TeCo that rely on implicit trigger assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IBD-PSC offers a practical, training-free 'firewall' for deployed models that achieves near-perfect detection (AUROC > 0.96) across 13 diverse attacks on three datasets, while remaining resistant to adaptive attacks that explicitly optimize against the defense. However, it only filters malicious inputs at inference time and cannot repair the backdoor, recover trigger patterns, or identify the true class of poisoned samples. An open question the authors flag is why scaled models cluster target-class benign samples into harder-to-learn classes rather than easier ones, suggesting deeper structural properties of backdoored feature spaces remain unexplored.
