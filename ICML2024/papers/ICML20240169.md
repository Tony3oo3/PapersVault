## Problem

Current evaluations of variational and Monte Carlo sampling methods rely on disparate performance measures and limited cross-method comparisons, making it difficult to assess progress or guide practitioner choices. The standard ELBO metric, based on reverse KL divergence, is inherently insensitive to mode collapse because its mode-seeking nature allows a model to achieve high ELBO while ignoring most modes of a multimodal target. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a unified benchmark with standardized tasks and a broad range of evaluation criteria, including novel metrics specifically designed to quantify mode collapse.

## Input / Output

Each sampling method receives a pointwise-evaluable unnormalized target density γ(x) and, when available, target samples, mode descriptors (partitioning R^d into M disjoint subsets), and the true log Z. The benchmark spans 12 target densities from 10 to 1600 dimensions, covering Bayesian logistic regression, random effects, time series, spatial statistics, synthetic mixtures, and image-based targets (14×14 MNIST, 28×28 Fashion MNIST). Outputs are structured as a matrix of performance criterion values (ELBO, EUBO, W₂, MMD, forward/reverse ESS, forward/reverse Δlog Z, EMC) per method per target, supplemented by sample visualizations and method rankings.

## Method

The benchmark evaluates 17 sampling methods across three categories—tractable density models (MFVI, GMMVI, NFVI), sequential importance sampling (SMC, AFT, CRAFT, FAB), and diffusion-based methods (MCD, LDVI, PIS, DIS, DDS, GBS, CMCD, ULA, UHA, GFN)—under a standardized protocol with 2000 particles/samples, 128 annealing or discretization steps, and 4 random seeds. The key technical contribution is a multi-criteria evaluation framework that pairs reverse-KL-based metrics (ELBO, reverse ESS, Δlog Z_r) with forward-KL-based metrics (EUBO, forward ESS, Δlog Z_f) and integral probability metrics (W₂, MMD), revealing that ELBO is blind to mode collapse while EUBO is sensitive to it. The paper further introduces Entropic Mode Coverage (EMC), a novel heuristic that partitions the target into M mode descriptors and computes the expected entropy of the induced auxiliary distribution over samples from the model, yielding a value in [0,1] where 0 indicates complete mode collapse and 1 indicates uniform coverage. Ablation studies isolate the effects of resampling, proposal support size, number of annealing steps, and learned base distributions on both ELBO and mode collapse severity.

## Results

Across 12 target densities and 17 sampling methods, no single method dominates all criteria. On real-world Bayesian targets (Table 5), GMMVI achieves the best ELBO on 6 of 7 tasks (e.g., Credit: −504.49, Cancer: +121.44, Sonar: −108.73), with FAB a close second (Credit: −504.50, Sonar: −108.59). On synthetic multimodal targets, FAB and GMMVI achieve the lowest Δlog Z_r and Δlog Z_f on Funnel, while diffusion-based methods (LDVI, MCD, GBS) achieve the highest Entropic Mode Coverage (EMC ≈ 0.62–0.95) on Digits and Fashion, indicating superior mode coverage but at the cost of lower ELBO. Mode collapse worsens sharply with dimensionality: for MoG/MoS at d=200, all methods except diffusion-based ones collapse to a single mode (EMC ≈ 0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMMVI and FAB are the strongest ELBO performers on real targets, with GMMVI edging out FAB on Credit (−504.49 vs −504.50) and Sonar (−108.73 vs −108.59), while FAB matches or slightly trails on Seeds and Brownian. Against SIS baselines (SMC, AFT, CRAFT), both GMMVI and FAB achieve substantially higher ELBOs—e.g., on Credit, GMMVI (−504.49) outperforms SMC (−580.94) and AFT (−584.77) by ~76–80 nats. Diffusion-based methods (MCD, LDVI, GBS, DDS) trade ELBO for mode coverage: on Fashion MNIST, GBS and DDS achieve EMC ≈ 0.62 versus 0.000 for MFVI and 0.217 for GMMVI, yet their ELBOs are often N/A or significantly lower. On the Funnel target, FAB and GMMVI achieve the best Δlog Z_r and Δlog Z_f, while diffusion methods produce visually reasonable samples but poor normalization estimates. MFVI, the simplest baseline, suffers complete mode collapse (EMC = 0.000) on both Digits and Fashion despite achieving a competitive ELBO on some real targets, illustrating the paper's key finding that ELBO alone is insufficient for evaluating sampling quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a unifying evaluation framework for the rapidly growing family of variational Monte Carlo sampling methods (AFT, CRAFT, FAB, MCD, PIS, DDS, GBS, etc.), which prior work has assessed using disparate and often incompatible performance criteria. It repurposes the forward KL divergence—previously used as a learning objective by Ji & Shen (2019)—as a diagnostic performance criterion (EUBO) sensitive to mode collapse, and introduces the Entropic Mode Coverage metric inspired by inception scores from generative modeling. The benchmark draws on the extended ELBO framework of Agakov & Barber (2004) and the variational Monte Carlo formulation of Wu et al. (2020a) and Doucet et al. (2022b), but shifts the focus from method development to standardized cross-method comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides the first standardized, multi-criteria reference for practitioners choosing among 17+ sampling methods across 12 diverse target densities, addressing a gap the authors identify as hindering progress in the field. The finding that ELBO and reverse log Z estimates are insensitive to mode collapse—while EUBO and the proposed EMC are—has direct implications for how the community should report and compare variational methods. Open questions include whether GMMVI's strong performance generalizes beyond the tested dimensionality range (it hits OOM on LGCP at d=1600), whether the exploration-exploitation trade-off in proposal support can be resolved automatically, and whether the EUBO's sensitivity to latent-variable looseness can be mitigated for fairer cross-category comparisons.
