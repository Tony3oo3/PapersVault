## Problem

Deep time series forecasting has operated under a one-model-per-dataset paradigm, preventing the transfer-learning benefits that large pre-trained models deliver in vision and language. Building a universal forecaster faces three unique challenges absent in other modalities: cross-frequency learning (different sampling rates induce different patterns), accommodating an arbitrary number of variates in multivariate series, and capturing the wide range of distributional properties across diverse datasets. No prior open-source model simultaneously addresses all three while being trained on sufficiently large-scale data.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a context window of length l (sampled up to 512 tokens after patching) and covariates spanning both the lookback and forecast horizons, with multivariate series flattened into a single sequence of arbitrary variate count (up to 128). Patch size is frequency-dependent (8 for yearly/quarterly up to 128 for second-level), so the same model handles minutely through yearly data. Output is a 4-component mixture distribution (Student's t, negative binomial, log-normal, low-variance normal) whose parameters are decoded from the masked encoder's output tokens, enabling both probabilistic sampling and deterministic point forecasts via the median.

## Method

MOIRAI is a masked encoder Transformer that introduces three architectural modifications for universal forecasting: (1) multi patch size projection layers that assign larger patches to high-frequency data and smaller patches to low-frequency data, learning one shared linear projection per patch size; (2) Any-variate Attention, which flattens all variates into a single sequence and encodes the time axis via Rotary Position Embeddings while disambiguating variates through two learnable scalar biases (same-variate vs. cross-variate) added to attention scores, ensuring permutation equivariance and invariance; and (3) a mixture-of-distributions head replacing a single parametric distribution. Training on LOTSA (27B observations, 9 domains) uses a task distribution that randomly samples context and prediction lengths, caps per-sub-dataset sampling to prevent domain dominance, and applies sequence packing to reduce padding waste from 61% to 0.4% of tokens.

## Results

As a zero-shot forecaster, MOIRAI_Base and MOIRAI_Large achieve best or second-best CRPS on 4 out of 6 probabilistic forecasting datasets (Electricity, Solar, Weather, Turkey Power), with MOIRAI_Large reaching CRPS of 0.036 on Turkey Power and 0.041 on Weather (via Base). On the long sequence forecasting benchmark, MOIRAI_Base achieves the best MSE on 5 of 7 datasets (ETTh2: 0.345, ETTm1: 0.381, ETTm2: 0.272, Electricity: 0.188, Weather: 0.238), outperforming full-shot baselines like iTransformer, TimesNet, and PatchTST without any dataset-specific training. On the Monash in-distribution benchmark, MOIRAI_Small achieves a normalized MAE of 0.655, outperforming all individual per-dataset baselines including N-BEATS, WaveNet, and Transformer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOIRAI (zero-shot) is compared against full-shot baselines that are individually trained and hyperparameter-tuned on each target dataset. On probabilistic forecasting, MOIRAI_Large wins on Solar (CRPS 0.406 vs. TiDE 0.420), Weather (MOIRAI_Base 0.041 vs. TFT 0.043), and Turkey Power (0.036 vs. TFT 0.039), while TiDE narrowly edges it on Electricity (0.048 vs. 0.050) and Walmart (0.077 vs. 0.098), and DeepAR leads on Istanbul Traffic (0.108 vs. 0.112). On long sequence forecasting, MOIRAI_Base achieves the best MSE on ETTh2 (0.345 vs. iTransformer 0.383), ETTm1 (0.381 vs. PatchTST 0.387), ETTm2 (0.272 vs. PatchTST 0.281), Electricity (0.188 vs. iTransformer 0.178—actually iTransformer wins here), and Weather (0.238 vs. iTransformer 0.258), while iTransformer leads on ETTh1 (0.454 vs. MOIRAI_Small 0.400—MOIRAI wins). Statistical baselines (AutoARIMA, Seasonal Naive) are consistently outperformed by large margins (e.g., CRPS 0.327 vs. 0.050 on Electricity). The LLM-based zero-shot baselines (GPT-3.5, LLaMA-2 via LLMTime) are substantially outperformed on the Monash benchmark, with MOIRAI_Small achieving normalized MAE 0.655 versus GPT-3.5's much higher per-dataset errors.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOIRAI extends the masked encoder pre-training architecture from Woo et al. (2023) by introducing three targeted modifications to handle time series heterogeneity: multi patch size projection layers for cross-frequency learning, Any-variate Attention (combining RoPE for the time axis and learned binary attention biases from TableFormer for the variate axis) for arbitrary-dimensionality inputs, and a mixture of parametric distributions for flexible probabilistic forecasting. It departs from prior universal forecasters (TimeGPT, TimesFM, Lag-Llama) by simultaneously addressing all three heterogeneity challenges—frequency, dimensionality, and distributional flexibility—rather than sidestepping them (e.g., per-frequency models or fixed distribution heads). The patch-based input representation follows PatchTST, while the Transformer backbone incorporates standard LLM improvements (RMSNorm, SwiGLU, pre-normalization).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LOTSA (27B observations across nine domains) is the largest open time series collection to date, removing a key data bottleneck for training Large Time Series Models and enabling reproducible pre-training research. The architecture components—particularly Any-variate Attention and multi patch size projections—are general and applicable beyond masked encoders, offering a reusable toolkit for the broader time series Transformer community. Open questions remain: the cross-frequency patch size assignment is heuristic, the architecture has limited support for very high-dimensional series, and the paper calls for proper neural scaling laws for LTMs. The finding that a single zero-shot model matches individually-tuned full-shot models validates the foundation model paradigm for time series, but the practical gap narrows on some datasets (Walmart, Istanbul Traffic), suggesting domain-specific fine-tuning may still be necessary for production use.
