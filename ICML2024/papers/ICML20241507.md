## Problem

Backpropagation-based training of deep networks is difficult to distribute across multiple processors due to backward locking, where earlier-layer devices sit idle awaiting the backward error signal. Additionally, there is no principled probabilistic mechanism for continual learning that naturally fuses information from sequential tasks without replay buffers or ad-hoc forgetting factors. The paper asks whether a fully probabilistic, message-passing training procedure can replace backpropagation while supporting distributed, asynchronous, and incremental learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes grayscale images (28×28 MNIST/FashionMNIST, 32×32 CIFAR-10) or noisy video frames (258×454 from DAVIS) and produces class labels, denoised pixel intensities, or posterior distributions over all model parameters. Unusually, parameters are random variables within the factor graph rather than fixed attributes of factors, so training and prediction are the same GBP inference procedure differing only in which nodes are observed. Training is single-epoch: each data point is seen once, its contribution is absorbed into the parameter posterior via Bayesian filtering, and it can then be discarded.

## Method

The approach constructs deep factor graphs whose architecture mirrors standard CNNs (convolutional, max-pooling, dense, upsampling layers), with all quantities—inputs, outputs, activations, and parameters—treated as random variables. Inter-layer factors enforce local consistency via quadratic energies of the form ‖x_{l-1} − f(x_l, Θ_l)‖²/(2σ²), analogous to predictive coding, while non-linear activation functions are handled by iterative linearisation around current variable estimates. Gaussian Belief Propagation (GBP) performs all inference: training is inference over parameters given observed inputs/outputs, and prediction is inference over outputs given parameters and inputs. Continual learning is achieved by Bayesian filtering—the parameter posterior after task t becomes the prior for task t+1—eliminating the need for replay buffers. Efficient message updates exploit the Woodbury identity to reduce factor-to-variable complexity from O(V³) to O(VM³), and the overall per-batch cost matches that of a forward/backward pass in the equivalent MLP.

## Results

On image classification, GBP Learning achieves 98.16±0.03% test accuracy on MNIST (single epoch, no replay), 88.2±0.1% on FashionMNIST, and 53.1±0.3% on CIFAR-10. On the full MNIST training set, this matches a CNN+Adam baseline with a 6×10³-example FIFO replay buffer, while comprehensively outperforming a linear classifier and a CNN without replay in the small-data regime. For video denoising on DAVIS, the five-layer continual-learning model achieves the highest PSNR (up to ~34.3 dB on frame 5), outperforming the single-layer model (~32.9 dB) and the hand-designed pairwise smoother (~31.1 dB). Asynchronous layer-wise training retains 98.11±0.04% accuracy on MNIST, nearly matching the synchronous 98.16±0.03%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary head-to-head comparison is with Lucibello et al. (2022), who train dense MLP-like factor graphs with GBP using analytically derived messages for binary weights and sign activations. GBP Learning outperforms them by 0.8% on MNIST (98.16% vs 97.40%) and by 11.8% on CIFAR-10 (53.1% vs 41.3%), while tying on FashionMNIST (88.2%). The CIFAR-10 gap is attributed to the convolutional architecture's translation equivariance benefiting more on that dataset. On MNIST, GBP Learning (single epoch, no replay) matches a CNN+Adam trained for one epoch with a 6×10³-example replay buffer, and substantially outperforms both a linear classifier and a CNN without replay, especially in the low-data regime. For video denoising, learnable factor graph models (both single-layer and five-layer) significantly outperform the hand-designed pairwise smoothing baseline in PSNR, with the deeper model and continual learning providing further gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Gaussian Belief Propagation (Davison & Ortiz, 2019) as its inference engine and on predictive coding (Millidge et al., 2022) for the inter-layer consistency factor design, but departs from both by treating network parameters as random variables to be inferred jointly with activations, rather than as fixed attributes of factors. The most direct prior work is Lucibello et al. (2022), who also train factor graphs with GBP but restrict to dense architectures with binary weights and sign activations, requiring analytically derived message updates. GBP Learning generalises this to arbitrary architectures (including convolutional layers) with continuous weights, using a generic linearisation scheme that avoids per-architecture rederivation, and adds continual learning via Bayesian filtering over the parameter posterior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that deep networks with non-linear factors can be trained end-to-end using only local, asynchronous message updates, sidestepping the backward-locking bottleneck that constrains distributed backpropagation. This opens a path toward training on heterogeneous, memory-local hardware (e.g., Graphcore IPUs, Cerebras) and toward truly incremental learning where data can be discarded after a single pass. However, the current implementation is roughly two orders of magnitude slower than backprop on MNIST, experiments are limited to small datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10), and the scalar-variable restriction prevents capturing rich correlation structure. Scaling to larger models and datasets, and developing tailored hardware/software stacks, remain the critical open questions before this approach can compete with optimised backprop pipelines.
