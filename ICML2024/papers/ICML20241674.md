## Problem

Deep learning models for fetal anatomical structure detection in ultrasound images suffer significant performance degradation when transferred across medical centers due to domain gaps from different equipment and scanning techniques. Unlike natural images where object relationships are chaotic, ultrasound images contain consistent topological (spatial relationships) and morphological (shape, texture) priors derived from human anatomy that existing UDA object detection methods fail to exploit.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241674/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes fetal ultrasound images (heart and head views) at varying resolutions (480p–1080p) from different equipment manufacturers (Samsung, Sonoscape, Philips) and gestational ages (20–34 weeks) as input. It produces bounding boxes and class labels for 16 anatomical regions: 9 cardiac structures (LA, RA, LV, RV, CR, R, VS, SP, DAO) and 7 head structures (T, LS, CP, CSP, BM, S, C). The source domain provides fully annotated ground truth while the target domain is unlabeled, with pseudo-labels generated during training.

## Method

ToMo-UDA builds on a shared ResNet-101 + FPN backbone with an FCOS detection head and introduces two domain-alignment modules. The Topology Knowledge Transfer (TKT) module constructs per-domain topology graphs from centroid features of each substructure (using memory banks for clustering), processes them through a GNN, and aligns source and target topological representations via Sinkhorn-based optimal transport. The Morphology Knowledge Transfer (MKT) module sparsely samples feature nodes from bounding boxes across FPN layers, processes them through a Morph-aware GNN (MAGNN) with cross-domain graphical attention to build per-substructure morphological representations, and aligns them via optimal transport. A Numerical Equilibrium (NE) component normalizes low-level feature distributions between domains to prevent mass imbalance from dominating the transport cost. The total loss combines supervised detection loss with TKT and MKT alignment losses (α=β=0.1).

## Results

On the primary FUSH² dataset, ToMo-UDA achieves 71.33% mAP (Center 1→2) and 88.71% mAP (Center 2→1) for fetal heart structure detection, surpassing the best UDA baseline (SIGMA++ at 57.83% and 82.50%) by 13.50 and 6.21 percentage points respectively. For fetal head detection, it reaches 81.30% and 70.27% mAP, outperforming the best baseline by 5.36 and 5.31 points. On cross-modal MMWHS (CT↔MRI), ToMo-UDA achieves 62.80% and 78.38% mAP, and on CardiacUDA it reaches 85.21% and 94.58% mAP, consistently outperforming all UDAOD baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ToMo-UDA outperforms all eight baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the strongest UDAOD baselines, SIGMA++ (Li et al., 2023b) and CMT (Cao et al., 2023), the gains are most pronounced on the harder Center 1→2 heart adaptation: 71.33% vs. 57.83% (SIGMA++) and 60.47% (CMT), a margin of 13.5 and 10.9 points. On the easier Center 2→1 direction, ToMo-UDA still leads by 6.2 points over SIGMA++ (88.71 vs. 82.50) and notably surpasses the fully supervised Target Only (84.78), suggesting the topological and morphological priors provide genuine generalization benefit. On the head task, ToMo-UDA beats CMT by 5.36 points (Center 1→2) and SIGMA++ by 5.31 points (Center 2→1). On cross-modal MMWHS, ToMo-UDA leads CMT by 5.83 points (CT→MRI) and AT (Li et al., 2022b) by 7.32 points (MRI→CT). The few-shot baselines AcroFOD and AsyFOD perform substantially worse than even Source Only on Center 1→2 heart (25.33% and 57.78% vs. 43.90%), indicating that limited target supervision is insufficient to bridge the domain gap in this dense-structure setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends UDA object detection from natural-image settings (adversarial training, self-training, image translation) to fetal ultrasound by introducing two domain-specific alignment modules—Topology Knowledge Transfer (TKT) and Morphology Knowledge Transfer (MKT)—that exploit anatomical invariants (fixed spatial relationships and consistent substructure morphology) as cross-domain priors. While prior medical UDAOD work (e.g., SMPT, source-free detection) addresses domain shift through generic feature or knowledge distillation, ToMo-UDA is the first to formalize topology and morphology as explicit graph-structured alignment targets using GNNs and optimal transport (Sinkhorn). The method builds on standard detection backbones (ResNet-101 + FPN + FCOS) and borrows the optimal-transport formulation from domain adaptation literature, but repurposes it for anatomical graph alignment rather than generic feature distribution matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of FUSH²—the first multi-center fetal ultrasound benchmark with 16 box-annotated anatomical regions across two hospitals and multiple equipment vendors—addresses a critical data gap that has limited UDAOD research in medical imaging. The finding that UDA can surpass fully supervised Target Only training (88.71% vs. 84.78% mAP on heart, center 2→1) suggests that anatomical priors can encode generalization beyond what target-domain supervision alone provides. However, the method assumes fixed structural topology (inapplicable to fundus/OCT), bounding boxes introduce irrelevant background into topology/morphology graphs, and the resolution disparity between centers (648×480 vs. 1280×872) remains an unresolved practical barrier that limits adaptation quality.
