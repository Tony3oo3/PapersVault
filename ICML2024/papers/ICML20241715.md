## Problem

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) frequently fail to converge to accurate PDE solutions, yet the root cause has been poorly understood. This paper identifies that the differential operator in the PINN residual term induces severe ill-conditioning in the loss landscape (condition numbers exceeding 10⁴), which causes first-order optimizers like Adam to converge extremely slowly and even Adam+L-BFGS to stall before reaching a critical point. The paper provides both a theoretical proof linking operator ill-conditioning to loss ill-conditioning and a practical optimizer that overcomes this barrier.

## Input / Output

The method takes a PDE specification (differential operator, boundary/initial conditions), 10,000 residual points sampled from a 255×100 interior grid, 257 initial-condition points, and 101 boundary points per boundary, along with a 3-hidden-layer tanh MLP (widths 50–400, Xavier initialization). It produces trained network weights approximating the continuous PDE solution, evaluated by L2RE on the full grid plus boundary/initial points. Notably, the loss must reach near-zero (interpolation) for the solution to be accurate—a loss of 10⁻³ yields L2RE ~10⁻¹, while 10⁻⁵ yields L2RE ~10⁻²—making high-precision optimization essential rather than optional.

## Method

The paper introduces NysNewton-CG (NNCG), a damped Newton optimizer that computes Newton steps via Nyström-preconditioned conjugate gradient (NyströmPCG) with Armijo line search, avoiding the O(p²) storage and O(p³) inversion of full Newton while remaining stable under the weaker Armijo conditions that cause L-BFGS to terminate prematurely. The recommended training pipeline is Adam (to escape saddle points) → L-BFGS (to reduce condition number by ~1000×) → NNCG (to drive loss below what L-BFGS can reach), with NNCG reducing loss by another factor of 10+ and L2RE by 2–4× over Adam+L-BFGS alone. Theoretically, the paper proves (Theorem 8.4) that polynomial spectral decay of the operator A∘K∞(w⋆) forces the PL*-condition number to grow as Ω(n_res^α), explaining why first-order methods are inherently slow, and proves (Theorem 8.5) that a hybrid gradient-descent-then-damped-Newton scheme achieves condition-number-independent linear convergence once near a minimizer.

## Results

Across convection, reaction, and wave PDEs, Adam+L-BFGS consistently achieves the lowest loss and L2RE among the three optimizers tested. On the convection PDE, Adam+L-BFGS attains a loss of 5.95e-6 and L2RE of 4.19e-3, a 14.2× improvement in L2RE over Adam (5.96e-2). On the wave PDE, Adam+L-BFGS achieves L2RE of 5.52e-2, a 6.07× improvement over L-BFGS (3.35e-1). Applying the proposed NNCG after Adam+L-BFGS further reduces loss by more than 10× (e.g., wave loss drops from 1.12e-3 to 6.13e-5) and L2RE by roughly 4× (wave: 5.52e-2 → 1.27e-2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Adam, L-BFGS, and their combination Adam+L-BFGS on three challenging PDEs (convection, reaction, wave) at multiple network widths. Adam+L-BFGS dominates both Adam and L-BFGS on nearly all metrics: it achieves the lowest loss and L2RE on convection (5.95e-6 / 4.19e-3 vs. Adam's 1.40e-4 / 5.96e-2 and L-BFGS's 1.51e-5 / 8.26e-3), reaction (3.26e-6 / 1.92e-2), and wave (1.12e-3 / 5.52e-2). The sole exception is the reaction ODE, where Adam slightly outperforms Adam+L-BFGS on loss at width 100 and L2RE at width 200, which the authors attribute to the reaction problem being less ill-conditioned (largest Hessian eigenvalue ~10³ vs. ~10⁴–10⁵ for the other PDEs). The proposed NNCG, applied after Adam+L-BFGS, further improves L2RE by 2–4× across all three PDEs (e.g., wave L2RE drops from 5.52e-2 to 1.27e-2), while gradient descent after Adam+L-BFGS provides no improvement, confirming the benefit of second-order methods on the ill-conditioned PINN loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work identifying ill-conditioning in PINN losses (Krishnapriyan et al., 2021; De Ryck et al., 2023) by providing a direct theoretical link between ill-conditioned differential operators and the Hessian condition number of the empirical PINN loss, without relying on lazy-training or NTK-constancy assumptions. It builds on the Nyström preconditioned conjugate gradient framework of Frangella et al. (2023) to construct the novel NNCG optimizer, and on the PL*-condition framework of Liu et al. (2022) to prove convergence of a hybrid gradient-then-Newton algorithm. Unlike prior work that studies conditioning through the NTK (requiring infinite-width assumptions), this paper analyzes finite-width PINN architectures via the Hessian spectral density.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, practical optimization recipe (Adam+L-BFGS+NNCG) that substantially outperforms standard PINN training pipelines, with the theoretical justification that first-order methods alone cannot achieve the near-zero loss required for accurate PDE solutions. The finding that L-BFGS often terminates prematurely without reaching a critical point—due to strong Wolfe line search failures—explains a common practical frustration and motivates the NNCG refinement. Open questions include whether these results extend to higher-dimensional PDEs, more complex architectures, and whether combining the right-preconditioning approach here with the left-preconditioning of Liu et al. (2024) yields further gains. The polynomial growth of condition number with residual point count (Theorem 8.4) suggests that simply adding more training points without second-order methods will not help.
