## Problem

Predicting nanomaterial morphology (shape and size) from synthesis parameters has no general computational solution, and prior ML efforts were confined to single-shape systems with ~100 samples. The boundaries of AI under realistic data constraints—particularly the potential of large language models for this domain—remained entirely unexplored, leaving experimentalists reliant on costly iterative SEM characterization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240476/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 10 continuous and 3 categorical synthesis parameters (temperature, reaction time, polymer concentration/molecular weight, solvent/surfactant identity, etc.) either as structured tabular features or as free-text descriptions generated from semantic templates. Outputs are multi-label shape predictions (5 categories: cubic, spherical, stick-shaped, flat, amorphous), combined shape-and-size labels (9 categories), or 128×128 pixel generated SEM-like images of individual nanoparticles. The LLM pipeline uses few-shot prompts containing 2–10 training examples in textual or tabular format, while the text-to-image system maps BERT-encoded text vectors through a linking autoencoder to a frozen VAE decoder.

## Method

The paper builds a 215-synthesis multimodal dataset of calcium carbonate nanoparticles (segmented into 46,800 individual SEM images) and applies three complementary AI approaches. First, binary-classification tree-based ensembles (Random Forest, XGBoost) are trained per shape/size category with hyperparameter optimization via 5-fold cross-validated grid search, achieving 0.80 average shape accuracy. Second, six state-of-the-art LLMs (GPT-4, GPT-4-turbo, GPT-3.5-turbo, Mistral medium/small/tiny) are evaluated under a systematic few-shot protocol varying the number of in-context examples, sampling strategy (target-class-only vs. mixed), and data format (textual vs. tabular); GPT-4 reaches 0.81 average accuracy, outperforming classical ML and showing particular strength on underrepresented classes. Third, a text-to-image prototype chains a pretrained BERT encoder, a shallow 4-layer 'linking' autoencoder (768-dim latent space), and a custom 4-block VAE decoder (4096-dim latent space) trained on augmented nanoparticle images, enabling in silico estimation of polydispersity from repeated generations. Statistical feature selection (Mann-Whitney U, Kruskal-Wallis, Fisher's exact, Chi-squared with Bonferroni correction) and SHAP-based importance analysis validate that the learned models capture the same synthesis–morphology associations identified by hypothesis testing.

## Results

For shape prediction across 5 nanoparticle morphologies, Random Forest achieves 0.80 ± 0.04 average accuracy and GPT-4 (few-shot, N=8, target-class sampling, textual format) achieves 0.81 ± 0.07 average accuracy. For the harder joint shape-and-size task (9 categories), XGBoost reaches 0.77 ± 0.10 average accuracy with 0.53 ± 0.08 F1. The text-to-image prototype (BERT + VAE + linking autoencoder) generates recognizable cubic nanoparticle images but remains limited by the small dataset (215 syntheses, 46,800 segmented images).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The three prior works compared in Table 10 (Shafaei & Khayati 2020, Iakovlev et al. 2019, Pellegrino et al. 2020) all predict only particle size within a single fixed shape (silver NPs, carbon nanotubes, TiO₂ NPs respectively), using 26–103 data points and reporting MAPE as their metric. The present work addresses a strictly harder task—joint shape and size classification across 5 morphologies with 215 syntheses—and reports accuracy/F1 instead, making direct numerical comparison inapplicable. However, the current work surpasses all three on scope: it handles multiple shapes simultaneously, provides a public dataset and code for reproducibility (none of the three do), and adds a generative text-to-image component. Among the LLMs evaluated, GPT-4 (0.81) marginally outperforms Random Forest (0.80) on shape prediction, while Mistral-small (0.80) matches RF at roughly 1/40th the inference cost of GPT-4, making it a pragmatic alternative for practitioners.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the established line of nanomaterial morphology prediction (previously limited to size estimation for single-shape systems like silver NPs or TiO2) to a multi-shape, multi-size classification task within a single chemical system (calcium carbonate). It departs from prior shape-prediction work (e.g., Timoshenko et al., which required XANES spectroscopy data) by using only synthesis parameters as input, and introduces LLMs (GPT-4, Mistral) as a novel prediction paradigm for this domain. The text-to-image prototype combines BERT feature extraction with a VAE and a shallow linking autoencoder, repurposing components from general-purpose generative AI for a highly specialized scientific imaging task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The publicly released 215-synthesis, multi-shape dataset (double the size of prior analogous studies) provides a much-needed benchmark for nanomaterial morphology prediction, and the finding that few-shot GPT-4 matches or slightly exceeds tree-based ensembles (0.81 vs. 0.80 accuracy) suggests LLMs could lower the barrier for experimentalists to access predictive tools without building ML pipelines. However, the single-chemical-system scope, small sample size per class (as few as 10 for sphere_large), and poor text-to-image quality limit immediate practical impact. Key open questions include whether LLM-based prediction generalizes across chemical systems, whether ensemble LLM methods can close the remaining gap, and whether a unified, publicly available nanomaterial database can be assembled to enable truly data-rich deep learning approaches.
