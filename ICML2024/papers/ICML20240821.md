## Problem

Mainstream semi-supervised learning methods additively combine a supervised loss on labeled data with a regularization term computed solely from unlabeled data, leaving the two data types largely disconnected in both representation and loss computation. This lack of direct labeled-unlabeled interaction prevents fully harnessing the potential of unlabeled data, and the effective design of representations and losses that exploit such interaction remains underexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240821/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 96×96 for STL-10, 112×112 for medical ultrasound) in a specific interdigitated batch layout where each labeled image is interleaved with μ=7 unlabeled images sharing the same weak-strong augmentation pair. The classifier outputs a C-dimensional probability vector over class labels, with the backbone (Wide ResNet or ViT) producing D-dimensional embeddings that are perturbed before the classification head. In the open-set medical setting, the unlabeled set contains out-of-distribution classes and feature distribution shift not present in the labeled set.

## Method

InterLUDE introduces two components that each promote direct labeled-unlabeled interaction. First, embedding fusion applies a circular-shift linear transformation in the embedding space, where each fused embedding z_i' = (1−α)z_i + αz_{i+1} blends a labeled embedding with its adjacent unlabeled neighbor (enforced by the interdigitated batch layout), improving representation diversity without requiring pseudo-labels. Second, the cross-instance delta consistency loss enforces that the change in predicted probabilities (Δ = p^w − p^s) between weak and strong augmentations is similar across labeled and unlabeled instances that share the same augmentation pair, extending consistency regularization from instance-wise to cross-instance. The total training objective combines standard cross-entropy on labeled data, FixMatch-style instance-wise consistency on unlabeled data, and the new delta consistency term, with an enhanced version (InterLUDE+) additionally incorporating self-adaptive thresholding and fairness regularization from FreeMatch.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 40 labels (CNN backbone), InterLUDE achieves 4.51% error rate and InterLUDE+ achieves 4.46%, surpassing the fully-supervised baseline of 4.57% and outperforming all 22 compared SSL methods. On STL-10 with only 40 labels (ViT backbone), InterLUDE achieves a striking 3.14% error rate versus 6.25% for the next-best method (FlatMatch), a 2x improvement. On the open-set Heart2Heart medical benchmark, InterLUDE+ achieves 3.25% error on TMED-2 and InterLUDE leads on cross-hospital generalization (13.75% on CAMUS, 4.86% on UNITY), outperforming all baselines including open-set-specific methods like OpenMatch and Fix-A-Step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InterLUDE/InterLUDE+ achieves the best or near-best error rate across nearly all 8 CNN settings in Table 1, winning outright on CIFAR-10 (40, 250 labels), CIFAR-100 (400 labels), and STL-10 (250, 1000 labels). The most dramatic margin is on STL-10 with 40 labels using ViT backbones (Table 2), where InterLUDE achieves 3.14% error versus 6.25% for FlatMatch and 14.91% for InstanT — a 2x improvement over the runner-up. On the open-set Heart2Heart benchmark (Table 3), InterLUDE outperforms all baselines including open-set-specific methods: on CAMUS it achieves 13.75% vs. 16.67% (FreeMatch) and 22.07% (OpenMatch), and on UNITY it reaches 4.86% vs. 5.20% (FlexMatch) and 7.17% (OpenMatch). FlatMatch, the closest concurrent work in spirit, is beaten by InterLUDE on most settings but shows much higher variance (e.g., 5.58±2.36 vs. 4.51±0.01 on CIFAR-10/40) and substantially underperforms on the medical benchmark (7.84% vs. 3.45% on TMED-2). InterLUDE+ (with SAT/SAF from FreeMatch) further improves over InterLUDE on low-label CIFAR settings and TMED-2, confirming the synergy between the two design choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

InterLUDE builds directly on the FixMatch paradigm (weak-strong augmentation with instance-wise consistency loss) and incorporates FreeMatch's self-adaptive thresholding and fairness regularization in its InterLUDE+ variant. The embedding fusion mechanism is derived from Yu et al. (2023a)'s circular-shift perturbation matrix, originally designed for supervised learning, which the authors repurpose for semi-supervised settings by enforcing an interdigitated batch layout that guarantees labeled-unlabeled pairing. The paper's core departure from prior work is the explicit argument that mainstream SSL's additive loss formulation (separate supervised and unlabeled terms) underutilizes unlabeled data by preventing direct labeled-unlabeled interaction in both representations and loss computation, positioning itself against FlatMatch which addresses the same disconnect but at ~8x higher computational cost via sharpness-aware minimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a remarkably simple embedding perturbation (circular shift with α=0.1) combined with a cross-instance delta-consistency loss yields consistent improvements across CNN and ViT backbones on standard benchmarks, and notably generalizes to open-set medical imaging (Heart2Heart) without being explicitly designed for that setting. The computational efficiency (~8x faster than FlatMatch) makes the approach practical for large-scale SSL. Key open questions include the theoretical understanding of why embedding-space perturbation aids SSL (the authors acknowledge this gap), whether the delta-consistency loss becomes vacuous when instance-wise consistency is already near-perfect, and whether the open-set generalization advantage is robust or an artifact of the specific medical benchmark.
