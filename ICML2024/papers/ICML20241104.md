## Problem

Two-sided matching markets with complementary preferences (firms requiring minimum quotas of multiple worker types) and unknown firm preferences that must be learned online remain unsolved: no implementable algorithm simultaneously guarantees stability, sublinear regret, and incentive compatibility under these combined constraints. Existing UCB-based competing bandit methods suffer linear regret in such settings because they cannot escape incorrect rankings once a worker is never re-matched, and prior work only handles single-type workers with unit quotas.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the platform samples mean rewards from Beta posteriors (α, β) per firm-worker-type triple, constructs estimated rankings, and runs a two-stage matching that first fills type-specific minimum quotas q_i^m in parallel via type-specific DA, then fills remaining total-quota positions Q_i − Σ_m q_i^m via standard type-agnostic DA. The output is a stable matching u_t^m(p_i) for every firm and type, from which each firm receives a noisy scalar reward y_{i,j}^m(t) = μ_{i,j}^m + ε(t) (sub-Gaussian), which updates the posterior. Workers' preferences π^m are known and fixed; only firm-to-worker preferences are learned.

## Method

MMTS (Multi-agent Multi-type Thompson Sampling) replaces UCB with posterior sampling for exploration, which avoids the linear-regret trap because sampled means can occasionally rank a previously under-explored worker higher, allowing the algorithm to correct stale rankings. The key architectural novelty is a double matching procedure: a first stage runs type-specific deferred-acceptance in parallel across all M worker types to satisfy minimum type quotas, then a second stage runs standard DA over the remaining unmatched workers and leftover quotas without type consideration, marginalizing the complementary preferences and guaranteeing a firm-optimal stable matching at every step. The regret analysis uses the eluder dimension to bound the number of times confidence-set widths exceed a threshold, yielding a Bayesian regret of Õ(Q√(K_max·T)) that is near-linear in total quota Q and depends only on √K_max (the largest single-type worker pool) rather than the total worker count, plus a constant NK/Q term from bad-event probabilities.

## Results

The main theoretical result is a Bayesian regret upper bound of $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(Q\sqrt{K_{\max}T})$, specifically $\Re(T) \leq 8Q\log(QT)\sqrt{K_{\max}T} + NK/Q$, which is near-linear in total quota $Q$ and depends only on $\sqrt{K_{\max}}$ (the maximum workers in a single type) rather than the total number of workers. Empirically, in the head-to-head comparison with centralized UCB (Liu et al., 2020) on a 3-firm, 3-worker, single-type setting, MMTS achieves a matching rate of 0.741 versus UCB's 0.031, and MMTS exhibits sublinear regret for all firms while UCB produces linear regret for firms 1 and 2. In the two multi-type simulation examples (N=2 firms, M=2 types), MMTS achieves sublinear regret for all firms and converges to the firm-optimal stable matching.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole direct baseline is centralized UCB from Liu et al. (2020), which addresses the simpler single-type, unit-quota competing matching problem. In the shared experimental setting (N=3 firms, K=3 workers, M=1 type, Q=1), MMTS decisively outperforms UCB: UCB suffers linear firm-optimal stable regret for firms 1 and 2 because once a firm's estimated ranking is slightly incorrect (e.g., firm p₃ ranks a₁ above a₃ by a margin of only 0.05), the UCB upper bound for the unexplored arm never shrinks, locking in the wrong ranking permanently. MMTS, by contrast, achieves sublinear regret in the same setting because Thompson Sampling's posterior draws occasionally produce alternative ranking patterns, allowing the algorithm to correct its initial misestimation. The matching rate gap (0.741 vs. 0.031) further illustrates that UCB's exploration failure leads to near-total matching breakdown, while MMTS maintains a healthy matching rate. The paper also notes that MMTS's regret bound depends on $\sqrt{K_{\max}}$ rather than $\sqrt{\sum_m K_m}$, a strict improvement over the single-type bounds in Liu et al. (2020) and Jagadeesan et al. (2021) when multiple worker types are present.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the competing bandit matching market framework of Liu et al. (2020) and Jagadeesan et al. (2021), which considered only a single worker type with unit quota per firm, to the more general setting of multi-type workers with complementary preferences (minimum type-specific quotas plus a total quota cap). The core algorithmic contribution is a 'double matching' technique that decomposes the problem into a type-specific DA stage and a type-agnostic DA stage, both built on the Gale-Shapley deferred acceptance algorithm. For the regret analysis, the paper adapts the eluder dimension framework of Russo & Van Roy (2013) to bound uncertainty widths in the multi-agent, multi-type setting, and uses Thompson Sampling (rather than UCB) as the exploration mechanism, motivated by a demonstrated failure mode of UCB in this context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm to simultaneously achieve stability and incentive compatibility in the complementary-preference matching setting, closing a gap where prior results (e.g., Dubins & Freedman, 1981) only applied to known-preference or single-type scenarios. The regret bound's dependence on √K_max (maximum workers in any single type) rather than √(total workers) is practically significant for large, heterogeneous markets. Open questions include extending to time-varying preferences, indifferent (non-strict) preferences, and incorporating real-world constraints like budget or geography. The observed negative-regret phenomenon—where a firm can temporarily benefit from estimation errors in a multi-agent setting—raises interesting questions about strategic behavior that remain only partially characterized.
