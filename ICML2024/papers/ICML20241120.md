## Problem

Score-based diffusion models require many score evaluations during sampling, and while training-free accelerated samplers like DPM-Solver show strong empirical speedups, no non-asymptotic convergence theory rigorously establishes that they are provably faster than vanilla DDIM or DDPM. This paper asks: can we design training-free deterministic and stochastic samplers with provably improved convergence rates over their unaccelerated counterparts?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241120/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The samplers consume only pre-trained score functions (available in practice as noise-prediction networks ε_t related to scores via s_t* = -ε_t*/√(1-ᾱ_t)) and initial Gaussian noise Y_T ~ N(0, I_d), requiring no additional training or Hessian access. They produce RGB image samples at dataset-specific resolutions (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 64×64 for ImageNet, 256×256 for LSUN and CelebA-HQ), with the number of function evaluations (NFEs) directly controlling sample quality. The deterministic sampler uses one score evaluation per step, while the stochastic sampler uses one score evaluation plus two noise draws per step.

## Method

The paper proposes two training-free accelerated samplers grounded in higher-order approximations. The deterministic sampler (accelerated DDIM) computes a mid-point prediction Y_t⁻ = Φ_t(Y_t) at each step, then incorporates a momentum correction term proportional to s_t(Y_t) − √(α_{t+1})·s_{t+1}(Y_t⁻) into the update, yielding O(1/T²) TV convergence versus O(1/T) for vanilla DDIM. This design mirrors second-order ODE solvers (DPM-Solver-2) by approximating the score along the probability flow ODE with a linear interpolation between consecutive steps. The stochastic sampler (accelerated DDPM) injects an intermediate noise step Y_t⁺ = Y_t + √((1−α_t)/2)·Z_t before the main denoising update, motivated by a higher-order Taylor expansion of the conditional density p_{X_{t-1}|X_t}, achieving O(1/T) convergence versus O(1/√T) for vanilla DDPM. Both samplers require only ℓ₂-accurate score estimates and make no log-concavity or smoothness assumptions on the target distribution.

## Results

Theoretically, the proposed accelerated DDIM-type sampler achieves a convergence rate of O(1/T²) in TV distance (improving over O(1/T) for vanilla DDIM), while the accelerated DDPM-type sampler achieves O(1/T) (improving over O(1/√T) for vanilla DDPM). Empirically, using pre-trained score functions, the accelerated DDIM sampler achieves FID scores that are approximately halved compared to vanilla DDIM at just 5 NFEs across LSUN-Churches, LSUN-Bedroom, CelebA-HQ, and CIFAR-10. The accelerated DDIM can produce high-quality images within 10 NFEs, whereas vanilla DDIM requires substantially more steps to reach comparable quality. For the stochastic setting, the accelerated DDPM sampler consistently outperforms the original DDPM on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-64 across all NFE budgets tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: the vanilla DDIM-type sampler (Song et al., 2020) and the vanilla DDPM-type sampler (Ho et al., 2020). Against DDIM, the proposed accelerated deterministic sampler wins decisively on FID across all four image datasets (LSUN-Churches, LSUN-Bedroom, CelebA-HQ, CIFAR-10), with the paper reporting that FID scores are roughly halved at 5 NFEs; visually, the accelerated sampler produces crisper, less noisy images at the same NFE budget. Against DDPM, the proposed accelerated stochastic sampler consistently achieves lower FID than the original DDPM on both CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-64 (Figure 4 in the appendix), though the gap is described as 'slight' relative to the larger gains seen in the deterministic setting. Both comparisons are conducted under identical pre-trained score functions and learning rate schedules, isolating the algorithmic contribution. The paper does not report any setting where the proposed methods underperform their respective baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three threads: (1) non-asymptotic convergence theory for DDIM (O(1/ε) iteration complexity, Li et al. 2023) and DDPM (O(1/ε²), Chen et al. 2022; Li et al. 2023), (2) training-free accelerated samplers like DPM-Solver-2 (Lu et al., 2022a) that empirically achieve few-step sampling via higher-order ODE approximation but lacked rigorous convergence guarantees, and (3) the probability flow ODE / reverse-time SDE framework (Song et al., 2021; Anderson, 1982). The key departure is providing the first provable acceleration: a momentum-like correction term derived from second-order ODE approximation improves the deterministic rate to O(1/√ε) and an intermediate noise injection improves the stochastic rate to O(1/ε), all without additional training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the theoretical backbone for why DPM-Solver-style higher-order ODE solvers work, filling a significant gap between empirical success and formal guarantees in diffusion sampling. The theory is notably general—requiring only ℓ₂ score accuracy and no log-concavity or smoothness on the target distribution—making it applicable to a broad range of practical settings. Open questions include the suboptimal dimension dependency (d⁶ for the deterministic sampler), whether third- or higher-order ODE/SDE extensions yield further provable gains, and whether the dimension bounds can be sharpened. For practitioners, the main takeaway is that the widely-used DPM-Solver-2 heuristic now has a convergence guarantee, and the proposed stochastic sampler offers a principled path to faster DDPM sampling.
