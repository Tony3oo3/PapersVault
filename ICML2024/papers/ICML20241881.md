## Problem

Designing functional enzymes that bind specific substrates remains a critical challenge in biocatalysis. Existing deep learning approaches for protein design are limited by missing fitness data for most enzyme families, unknown structures for many enzymes, the absence of substrate modeling in the design process, and the lack of a unified generative model applicable across all enzyme families.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241881/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives three conditioning signals: a BRENDA EC family tag (up to four hierarchical levels, e.g., 1.1.1.1), a small-molecule substrate with pre-computed 5-dimensional chemical features and fixed 3D atom coordinates, and a set of functionally important sites (residue types + Cα coordinates) automatically mined via ClustalW2 multiple sequence alignment at a 30% identity threshold. It jointly generates the full N-residue amino acid sequence (20 common amino acids), Cα coordinates for all non-conditioned residues, and a binary enzyme-substrate binding prediction. Non-conditioned residue coordinates are initialized on a spherical surface (r≈3.75 Å) around the preceding residue, and sequences are decoded greedily.

## Method

EnzyGen interleaves global Transformer attention with neighborhood equivariant sub-layers (NAELs) across 33 stacked layers, where each NAEL first computes full-sequence self-attention and then updates residue features and Cα coordinates using only K=30 nearest neighbors in 3D space, preserving SE(3) equivariance. A separate 3-layer neighborhood equivariant module encodes the substrate, and a binary classifier over pooled enzyme and substrate embeddings provides the binding constraint. Training minimizes a joint objective combining cross-entropy sequence loss, Gaussian coordinate reconstruction loss, and a binary enzyme-substrate interaction loss, with the first 200K steps using only the first two losses. The global attention sub-layers are initialized from the 650M-parameter ESM-2 checkpoint, yielding a 714M-parameter model trained for 1M steps on 8 A6000 GPUs. This interleaving design enables information exchange at both sequence-level (long-range) and spatial-level (local 3D) granularities simultaneously, which ablations confirm improves both folding quality (pLDDT) and substrate binding (ESP score) over simply concatenating ESM2 and EGNN.

## Results

Across 323 testing enzyme families (30 third-level BRENDA EC categories), EnzyGen achieves an average ESP score of 0.65, substrate binding affinity of −9.44 kcal/mol (Gnina), and AlphaFold2 pLDDT of 87.45. These are the best values among all four methods tested. For top-10 candidates, EnzyGen reaches an ESP score of 0.93, binding affinity of −10.12, and pLDDT of 90.52, indicating that the highest-probability designs are both well-folded and functionally effective.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EnzyGen outperforms all three baselines on every metric across all 30 third-level EC categories. On ESP score (enzyme-substrate interaction), EnzyGen (0.65 avg) beats ESM2+EGNN (0.61), PROTSEED (0.59), and RFDiffusion+ProteinMPNN (0.53). On substrate binding affinity (Gnina, lower = stronger binding), EnzyGen achieves −9.44 kcal/mol, improving by 0.87 kcal/mol over the best baseline RFDiffusion+ProteinMPNN (≈−8.57) and by a larger margin over PROTSEED and ESM2+EGNN. On AlphaFold2 pLDDT (folding confidence), EnzyGen (87.45) surpasses ESM2+EGNN (~85.5), RFDiffusion+ProteinMPNN (~83.5), and PROTSEED (~77.5). The 10.79% relative improvement in binding affinity over the best baseline is consistent across categories, with EnzyGen winning in all 30 third-level groups. Notably, ESM2+EGNN is the closest competitor on ESP and pLDDT, but EnzyGen's interleaving NAEL architecture and explicit substrate constraint provide a clear and consistent advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

EnzyGen synthesizes ideas from three prior lines of functional protein design—fitness-guided sequence generation, structure-to-sequence inverse folding, and joint backbone-sequence co-design (PROTSEED, RFDiffusion+ProteinMPNN)—but departs from all of them by introducing a unified model conditioned simultaneously on automatically mined functionally important sites (via ClustalW2 MSA) and small-molecule substrates. Architecturally, it interleaves Transformer-style global attention with K-nearest-neighbor equivariant message passing (NAEL), drawing on ESM-2 for initialization and EGNN for SE(3)-equivariant coordinate updates, but the joint training objective with an explicit enzyme-substrate interaction loss is new. The paper explicitly positions itself against the gap that no prior method provides a single model applicable across all enzyme families while also modeling the substrate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EnzyGen is the first unified generative model spanning 3,157 enzyme families, and EnzyBench provides the first comprehensive benchmark for enzyme design with three complementary metrics (ESP score, Gnina binding affinity, AlphaFold2 pLDDT), making it a practical resource for the field. Key limitations acknowledged by the authors include coverage of only PDB-confirmed structures (3,157 of 8,422 fourth-level EC categories) and a relatively weak substrate constraint (binary binding prediction rather than 3D complex pose design). Open questions include whether the approach scales to the full EC tree, whether explicit 3D substrate-binding geometry can be incorporated, and whether the zero-shot generalization to unseen substrates/categories (tested on only 2 each) holds more broadly.
