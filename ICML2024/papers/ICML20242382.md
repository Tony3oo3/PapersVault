## Problem

Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of language models produces delta parameters whose extreme redundancy has gone uncharacterized, and merging multiple task-specific LMs from the same backbone suffers from parameter interference that degrades performance. No prior work has demonstrated that the vast majority of SFT delta parameters can be eliminated without any retraining or GPUs, or that such sparsification can serve as a general-purpose preprocessing step to improve existing model-merging algorithms.

## Input / Output

The method operates purely on weight tensors: given a pre-trained backbone θ_PRE and K SFT models θ_SFT^t sharing that backbone, it computes per-model delta parameters δ^t = θ_SFT^t − θ_PRE, applies DARE to produce sparsified deltas, and outputs either a single sparsified model or a merged multi-task model. The sole hyperparameter is the drop rate p (typically 0.1–0.99), and the entire pipeline runs on CPUs with no training data or gradient computation. DARE is effective only when absolute delta values are small (≤ 0.002), which holds for standard SFT but breaks down after continuous pre-training where deltas reach ~0.03.

## Method

DARE (Drop And REscale) randomly zeros a fraction p of delta parameters via a Bernoulli mask, then rescales surviving entries by 1/(1−p) so that the expected linear transformation at each layer is preserved in expectation; the rescale step is critical, as dropping without it (DropOnly) causes embedding cosine similarity to collapse sharply as p grows. The paper proves that with γ = 1/(1−p), E[ĥ_i] = E[h_i], confirming that DARE preserves expected output embeddings, and empirically shows 90–99% of delta parameters can be removed with minimal performance loss, with tolerance for higher p increasing with model size. As a merging plug-in, DARE is applied independently to each source model's delta parameters before feeding them into established merging algorithms (Task Arithmetic, TIES-Merging, Fisher Merging, RegMean, Average Merging), reducing parameter interference by ensuring most delta entries are zero and thus non-conflicting. This yields consistent improvements over unsparsified merging and, in some cases, the merged model surpasses every individual source model.

## Results

DARE can eliminate 90% or even 99% of SFT delta parameters with minimal performance degradation, with tolerance increasing with model size (e.g., WizardMath-70B tolerates p=0.99 while 7B/13B variants fail). For model merging, Task Arithmetic + DARE on LM & Math & Code achieves 69.28 on AlpacaEval and 23.17 on HumanEval, surpassing the individual source models (67.20 and 36.59 respectively on their strongest tasks). The merged 7B model supermario_v2 attains 75.49 average on the Open LLM Leaderboard, ranking first among 7B models and beating both source models (75.29). All merging is performed on CPUs without retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2023), DARE consistently improves merging: for the 3-model LM&Math&Code merge, DARE lifts HumanEval from 18.29 to 23.17 and MBPP from 29.80 to 31.60, while AlpacaEval improves from 69.03 to 69.28. Against TIES-Merging (Yadav et al., 2023), DARE yields larger gains: the 3-model merge jumps from 65.91 to 72.50 on AlpacaEval (+6.59) and from 21.95 to 29.27 on HumanEval (+7.32), because DARE's random sparsification avoids TIES-Merging's harmful magnitude-based trimming step. Against Magnitude-based Pruning (Han et al., 2015), DARE outperforms in most cases, with the gap widening at higher drop rates (e.g., at p=0.7, MP drops GSM8K from 46.70 to 0.37 on 7B models while DARE maintains performance). The merged supermario_v2 (75.49 avg) surpasses both its source models WildMarcoroni-7B and WestSeverus-7B (75.29 avg each) on the Open LLM Leaderboard, demonstrating that DARE-enabled merging can produce a model stronger than any individual source. On encoder-based LMs (GLUE), DARE provides smaller but consistent improvements of 0.36%–0.84% across Average Merging, Task Arithmetic, Fisher Merging, RegMean, and TIES-Merging, though the merged model still struggles to surpass single-task models in some cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of network pruning and model merging, drawing conceptual inspiration from Dropout's drop-and-rescale mechanism but applying it to SFT delta parameters rather than activations. It builds directly on the model merging literature (Task Arithmetic, TIES-Merging, Fisher Merging, RegMean, Average Merging) by proposing DARE as a universal preprocessing step that sparsifies delta parameters before any merging method is applied. The key departure from prior pruning work is the focus on delta parameters (the SFT-to-pretrained difference) rather than fine-tuned parameters, combined with the rescaling factor 1/(1-p) that provably preserves embedding expectations in expectation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 90–99% of SFT delta parameters are redundant across architectures (BERT through 70B Llama 2) and tasks is a strong empirical discovery with immediate practical value: model merging becomes a CPU-only operation requiring no retraining or GPUs, and the merged model can occasionally surpass every individual source model. The technique has already been integrated into huggingface/peft and arceeai/mergekit, signaling broad adoption. Key limitations remain: DARE fails when delta parameters exceed ~0.002 in magnitude (e.g., after continuous pre-training), the upper bound of tolerable drop rate as a function of model size is not formally characterized, and the surpassing-source-models effect is configuration-dependent rather than guaranteed.
