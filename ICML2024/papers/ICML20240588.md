## Problem

Leaf sequencing in radiotherapy planning is predominantly solved by iterative optimization methods that are time-consuming and cannot leverage knowledge from large-scale training data. Additionally, the absence of a differentiable leaf sequencing module blocks end-to-end training of the full planning pipeline. This paper addresses both gaps by formulating leaf sequencing as a multi-agent reinforcement learning problem that executes in a single pass per control point.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240588/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 2D target fluence map (X×Y intensity grid) as input, which is first cropped to a bounding box around positive intensities to normalize heterogeneous patterns across patients. It produces a sequence of K control points, each specifying discrete leaf position changes (la ∈ ℤ², bounded by ±S) for X leaf pairs and a continuous monitor unit value (ma ∈ (R_b, R_e)) shared across all leaf pairs at that control point. The finite-horizon design means the model executes exactly once per control point rather than iterating to convergence.

## Method

RLS employs a two-level multi-agent PPO framework: X leaf agents (sharing a single policy network π_θl) predict discrete leaf movements, and a single MU agent (π_θm) predicts the monitor unit after observing the leaf positions, mirroring the clinical practice that MU depends on the aperture. The training uses a finite-horizon formulation (adapted from Pardo et al., 2018) where agents maximize expected return over a fixed episode length, enabling single-pass inference. Five reward components guide learning: approaching target fluence, penalizing overdosing, preventing leaf crossing, regularizing inter-CP movement smoothness (with tunable weight λ₄ for user preference), and regularizing aperture shape (area/perimeter ratio). A cropping strategy normalizes heterogeneous fluence patterns, and a post-processing step merges control points to handle variable sector lengths without retraining.

## Results

On the primary fluence reconstruction metric (MNSE↓), RLS outperforms the PORIx optimization sequencer across all five datasets: 0.149 vs. 0.219 (HNd), 0.165 vs. 0.257 (HNe1), 0.146 vs. 0.241 (HNe2), 0.042 vs. 0.079 (Pros), and 0.043 vs. 0.079 (Pros external). In terms of optimization convergence, RLS reduces the number of iterations needed to reach 97% and 99% of the planning cost (e.g., AIRE97% of 27.2 vs. higher PORIx values on HNd; AIRE99% of 50.1 on HNd). In the full-AI end-to-end VMAT pipeline, RLS achieves a Dose score MAE of 0.19 and DVH score MAE of 0.88, substantially better than the best OpenKBP dose-prediction entries (MAE 2.4 and 1.5 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the leaf sequencing optimizer built into PORIx (Varian's commercial VMAT optimization environment). RLS achieves lower MNSE than PORIx on every dataset, with the largest relative gains on head-and-neck data (e.g., 32% reduction on HNd: 0.149 vs. 0.219; 41% on HNe2: 0.146 vs. 0.241) and prostate data (47% on Pros: 0.042 vs. 0.079). RLS also reduces the iteration count for the outer optimization loop to reach 97%/99% cost thresholds, indicating faster convergence when integrated into PORIx. In the full-AI end-to-end pipeline (Table 3), RLS is compared against the best OpenKBP dose-prediction leaderboard entries (S1–S3) and the recent Teng et al. (2024) method; RLS achieves a Dose score MAE of 0.19 (VMAT, 8 CPs) versus 2.1–2.7 for those baselines, and a DVH score MAE of 0.88 versus 0.98–1.7, demonstrating that the leaf sequencing error introduced by RLS is well below the error scale of state-of-the-art dose prediction models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) as the core actor-critic algorithm and adapts finite-horizon RL (Pardo et al., 2018) to formulate leaf sequencing as a fixed-episode decision problem rather than an iterative optimization. It departs from prior RL work in radiotherapy (Hrinivich & Lee, 2020; Ganeshan, 2021), which operated in single-leaf-pair simulations, by introducing a two-level multi-agent architecture (X leaf agents sharing a policy + one MU agent) that handles the full practical output space. The key conceptual departure from the dominant optimization-based pipeline (RapidArc/PORIx) is replacing iterative refinement with a single-pass sequential policy, motivated by the desire to enable end-to-end differentiable AI planning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first MARL leaf sequencer demonstrated in a practical commercial planning environment (PORIx), opening a path toward fully learned, non-iterative radiotherapy planning pipelines. The controllable reward design (five tunable components) allows clinicians to encode preferences on leaf speed, aperture shape, and overdosing penalties, which is practically valuable. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to the first 100 optimization iterations, no joint end-to-end training of dose/fluence/sequencing modules, and dependence on a single proprietary platform for baseline comparison. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-resolution planning, different MLC geometries, and whether end-to-end training of the full pipeline can achieve globally optimal plans.
