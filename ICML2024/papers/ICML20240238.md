## Problem

Existing scribble-supervised semantic segmentation methods propagate label information from scribbled pixels to unlabeled regions but ignore the features of pixels that have already been correctly classified. These features could serve as strong guides for classifying boundary and ambiguous pixels, yet no prior scribble-supervised approach explicitly extracts and reuses them, leaving a gap between what the model has learned and how it refines its predictions.

## Input / Output

The method takes 512×512 RGB images with scribble annotations (partial pixel-level labels covering only a subset of pixels) as training input, and at inference additionally consumes multi-label classification results (from Q2L or MCAR) to determine which category prototypes to activate. It produces a full pixel-level semantic segmentation mask over 21 PASCAL VOC 2012 classes, with three internal prediction maps (initial, local-prototype-augmented, global-prototype-augmented) from which the best is selected.

## Method

The core mechanism extracts feature prototypes from the top-K highest-confidence pixels per category in the initial prediction, then fuses these prototypes back into the feature map via an attention-weighted augmenter (inspired by FeatMatch): features and prototypes are projected to an embedding space, their dot-product attention weights are computed with softmax, the weighted prototype is concatenated with the feature embedding, and a residual linear layer produces the augmented feature. Two prototype levels operate in tandem—local prototypes (per-image, per-batch) and global prototypes (a memory bank of n=5 slots per category, updated via cosine-similarity matching with momentum α=0.99)—to balance per-image specificity with cross-batch diversity. Training proceeds in three phases: a warm-up with only partial cross-entropy loss, a local-prototype phase adding a consistency loss between initial and local-augmented predictions, and a full phase adding a second consistency loss for global-prototype-augmented predictions. The backbone is SegFormer MiT-B1 (ImageNet-pretrained), and the entire pipeline is single-stage without CRF or auxiliary data.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2012 val set with scribble supervision, the proposed method (Ours-MiT-B1) achieves 77.9% mIoU, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art TEL (77.3%) by 0.6% while using a lighter backbone (MiT-B1 vs. ResNet101-based LTF). With ResNet101, the method reaches 76.2% mIoU, matching Seminar and A2GNN. The approach is single-stage, requires no extra supervised data, and does not use CRF, distinguishing it from most competing methods. On the fully supervised setting, MiT-B1 achieves 79.2% mIoU, meaning the scribble-supervised method recovers 98.4% of the fully supervised performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (Ours-MiT-B1, 77.9% mIoU) outperforms the previous scribble-supervised SOTA TEL (77.3%) by 0.6%, despite using a smaller backbone (MiT-B1 at 79.2% fully supervised vs. LTF-ResNet101 at 80.9%). Among single-stage scribble methods without extra data or CRF, the method leads by a clear margin over AGMM (76.4%), CDSP (75.9%), and GatedCRF (75.5%). With ResNet101, Ours-ResNet101 (76.2%) ties with Seminar and A2GNN (both 76.2%) but trails TEL (77.3%), indicating the method benefits more from the Transformer backbone. Compared to multi-stage or extra-data methods like SPML (76.1%, uses extra data + CRF) and BPG (76.0%, uses extra data), the proposed approach achieves higher mIoU with a simpler single-stage pipeline and no auxiliary supervision. The method also surpasses all point-supervised and bounding-box-supervised methods in Table 1, demonstrating the advantage of scribble-level annotation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the prototype-based feature augmentation mechanism from FeatMatch (Kuo et al., 2020), originally designed for semi-supervised image classification, to the pixel-level setting of scribble-supervised semantic segmentation. Within the scribble-supervised segmentation lineage (ScribbleSup → NormCut/KernelCut → GatedCRF → BPG/SPML → URSS/PSI → TEL → AGMM/CDSP), prior methods propagate label information via regularization losses, consistency constraints, pseudo-labels, or label diffusion, but none explicitly leverage the features of correctly classified pixels as prototypes to guide boundary-region classification. The key departure is the introduction of a dual local/global prototype system with a memory bank, where prototypes are extracted from high-confidence prediction regions rather than via clustering, and are used to augment features through attention-weighted fusion before a consistency loss regularizes the augmented predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method achieves a modest 0.6% mIoU improvement over the previous SOTA (TEL) on PASCAL VOC 2012 while operating as a single-stage framework without CRF or extra data, and the ablation confirms that prototype augmentation contributes a substantial ~10% gain over the baseline. However, a notable practical limitation is that inference requires external class labels (via Q2L or MCAR multi-label classifiers) to filter relevant prototypes, which adds a dependency not present in competing methods and limits direct deployment. The evaluation is confined to a single dataset (PASCAL VOC 2012) and a single backbone family, leaving open whether the prototype augmentation strategy generalizes to other datasets, larger-scale segmentation benchmarks, or different supervision granularities. Future work suggested by the authors includes extending the approach to multimodal tasks and refining prototype guidance to reduce the inference-time classifier dependency.
