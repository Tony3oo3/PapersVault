## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient finetuning (PEFT) methods like LoRA and OFT introduce many additional parameters and are highly sensitive to learning rate and hyperparameter choices, making large-scale deployment of individually adapted models costly. OFT in particular requires small learning rates, identity initialization, and incurs high computational overhead from matrix multiplications, while its Cayley parametrization excludes Householder reflections (det = −1) that could offer better parameter efficiency. The paper asks whether a transformation family can match or exceed these methods with 10–100× fewer parameters while being robust to learning rate choices and guaranteeing bounded deviation from the pretrained model.

## Input / Output

The method is applied to pretrained foundation models (Stable Diffusion v1.5, DeBERTaV3-base, Llama-2-7B) by multiplicatively transforming their weight matrices in attention (Q, K, V) and feedforward/projection layers. For image generation, inputs are text prompts optionally combined with control signals (semantic maps, face landmarks, Canny edge maps) producing 512×512 RGB images; for NLU, text inputs produce class labels or similarity scores; for instruction tuning, text prompts produce free-form text responses. Unlike additive methods (LoRA), the transformation is multiplicative and absorbed into weights at inference, adding no latency.

## Method

ETHER frames finetuning as a search for hyperplane normal vectors u, applying the Householder reflection H = I − 2uu^T to weight matrices, which by construction keeps ‖H − I‖_F = 2 constant, bounding perturbation and enabling high learning rates without model collapse. ETHER+ relaxes orthogonality via H⁺ = I − uu^T + vv^T with two unit vectors, allowing hyperplanes to interact and cancel (recovering identity when u = v) while still satisfying ‖H⁺ − I‖_F ≤ 2, applied on both sides of W for finer control. A block-diagonal formulation splits H into n independent blocks, reducing computation from O(d²f) to O(d²f/n) with full block-parallelism while keeping parameter count constant at O(Ld) for ETHER and O(L(d+f)) for ETHER+. The key insight is that OFT's Cayley parametrization only yields det = +1 orthogonal matrices, excluding the det = −1 Householder reflections that provide the parameter efficiency and learning-rate robustness. The paper further shows via a Naive (non-orthogonal OFT) control that hyperspherical energy retention is not the critical factor for stability; rather, the bounded Euclidean distance to identity is what prevents catastrophic deterioration.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark (DeBERTaV3-base), ETHER+ achieves the best average score of 90.10 with only 0.33M parameters, outperforming BOFT (89.89, 0.75M) and OFT (89.77, 0.79M). For instruction tuning of Llama-2-7B, ETHER+ reaches 44.87 MMLU, 46.50 ARC, 29.38 TruthfulQA-1, and 43.51 TruthfulQA-2 with 1.04M parameters, surpassing LoRA r=8 (43.61/46.16/28.76/42.21) with 4× fewer parameters. On controllable image synthesis (S2I on ADE20K), ETHER+ achieves 27.3 mIoU and 68.1% accuracy with 0.4M parameters, beating OFT (24.5 mIoU, 62.8% Acc) by a wide margin while using ~30× fewer parameters. ETHER alone matches OFT on S2I (24.6 vs 24.5 mIoU) with over 100× fewer parameters (0.1M vs 13.2M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA, ETHER+ dominates on instruction tuning (44.87 vs 43.61 MMLU, 46.50 vs 46.16 ARC) with 4× fewer parameters than LoRA r=8, and on GLUE ETHER+ (90.10 avg) beats LoRA r=18 (88.50 avg) by 1.6 points with ~4× fewer parameters. Against OFT, ETHER+ wins on every task: S2I mIoU 27.3 vs 24.5, GLUE avg 90.10 vs 89.77, and MMLU 44.87 vs 42.92, while using 30–100× fewer parameters. Against DreamBooth (full finetuning, 859.5M params), ETHER+ (0.4M params) achieves higher DINO (0.666 vs 0.644) and CLIP-I (0.800 vs 0.793) on subject-driven generation. Against VeRA, ETHER+ (1.04M) outperforms both VeRA r=64 (0.27M) and VeRA r=256 (1.05M) on all four instruction tuning metrics, with VeRA r=256 actually degrading in performance relative to r=64. Against AdaLoRA and BOFT on GLUE, ETHER+ (90.10) edges out both (89.46 and 89.89 respectively) while using fewer parameters than either. The paper also shows that ETHER's key advantage over OFT is learning-rate robustness: ETHER+ maintains strong performance across multiple learning-rate magnitudes, whereas OFT and Naive collapse at higher learning rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

ETHER builds directly on the multiplicative finetuning paradigm introduced by OFT (Qiu et al., 2023), which applies block-diagonal orthogonal transformations to pretrained weights to preserve hyperspherical energy. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that OFT's Cayley parametrization (Q = (I+S)(I-S)⁻¹) restricts to determinant +1 orthogonal matrices, thereby excluding Householder reflections (det = -1) — which are precisely the transformations that yield extreme parameter efficiency (a single unit vector per block) while maintaining bounded distance to the identity. ETHER+ further departs by relaxing orthogonality entirely, using two interacting hyperplane normals to allow finer-grained adaptation, and the paper's control experiments (OFT vs. its non-orthogonal 'Naive' variant) argue that the stability benefits attributed to HE preservation actually stem from the multiplicative structure and bounded perturbation rather than from orthogonality per se.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10–100× parameter reduction over LoRA and OFT, combined with the ability to use learning rates 10–100× higher without model collapse, makes ETHER/ETHER+ practically attractive for large-scale deployment where hyperparameter grid search is costly and many individual adaptations must be served. The challenge to the hyperspherical-energy narrative is an important conceptual correction, though the evidence is concentrated on a handful of diffusion and NLP tasks and would benefit from broader validation. A key open limitation is that multiplicative methods still incur higher training FLOPs than additive methods like LoRA (even with block-parallelism), and the expressive power of a single (or double) hyperplane reflection is harder to scale than LoRA's rank parameter, leaving a trade-off between parameter efficiency and approximation capacity.
