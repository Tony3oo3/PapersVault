## Problem

In RLHF, reward models trained on human preference data encode spurious correlations with response length, causing policies to exploit verbosity rather than improve actual content quality. Existing mitigations such as KL regularization, reward clipping, and length penalty require extensive hyperparameter tuning and fail to remove the root cause. The paper asks whether the length signal can be structurally disentangled from the reward model itself rather than patched at the RL optimization level.

## Input / Output

The reward model takes a text prompt and a text response and produces two scalar scores: a quality reward and a length reward computed from shared Transformer features via two separate linear projections. During RL fine-tuning, only the quality reward is fed to the policy (PPO or ReMax), while the length reward is discarded entirely. The policy outputs text responses evaluated via GPT-4 pairwise comparisons against the Vicuna-7B SFT baseline on LIMA prompts, with the Pareto front of win score versus average response length serving as the primary comparison metric.

## Method

ODIN extends the standard Bradley-Terry reward model by replacing the single linear projection with two weight-normalized linear heads (W_Q and W_L) operating on shared Transformer features. Training minimizes three losses jointly: a ranking loss on the sum of both rewards to preserve overall preference accuracy, a Pearson correlation loss that maximizes correlation between the length head and response length while minimizing it for the quality head (computed over the global minibatch), and an orthogonality loss on W_Q and W_L to push the two reward subspaces apart. Weight normalization on both heads prevents the degenerate solution where W_Q collapses to zero. During RL, only the quality head r^Q is used as the reward signal, structurally eliminating the length reward without introducing new RL hyperparameters, and the approach transfers across both PPO and ReMax.

## Results

ODIN reduces the Pearson correlation between reward and response length from 0.451 (baseline RM) to -0.03 (with both length and orthogonality losses), while maintaining validation accuracy at 69.2% vs. 70.1% for the baseline. On the LIMA evaluation (300 prompts, GPT-4 judge), ODIN-trained policies achieve a strictly higher Pareto front (Win Score vs. response length) than PPO*, ReMax*, and DPO baselines for all lengths ≥210 tokens. On benchmarks, ODIN policies maintain or slightly improve over SFT initialization (e.g., TruthfulQA: 34.64 vs. 32.68 at length 230; MMLU: 49.86 vs. 49.81), confirming no capability degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PPO with vanilla RM (and PPO* with reward clipping + length penalty), ODIN-PPO achieves a higher Pareto front on the LIMA GPT-4 Win Score at all response lengths ≥210 tokens, without requiring the extra hyperparameters (clipping threshold c, penalty α) that PPO* needs. Against ReMax with vanilla RM (and ReMax* with tricks), ODIN-ReMax similarly dominates the Pareto front, and the improvement is consistent across the β and η hyperparameter sweeps. Against DPO (re-implemented on the same OpenAssistant data and Vicuna-7B base), ODIN achieves a higher Win Score at comparable lengths in Figure 2. Against the length penalty approach of Singhal et al. (2023), ODIN produces a more significant Pareto front improvement (Figure 4) and is preferred in human evaluation at all three tested lengths (230, 245, 260 tokens; Figure 6), with the largest human-evaluation gap at length 260. The key advantage is that ODIN eliminates the need for reward clipping and length penalty entirely, removing two additional hyperparameters from the RL stage while achieving better results.

## Relation to Prior Work

ODIN builds on the standard RLHF pipeline (SFT → reward modeling → RL) and the growing literature on reward hacking mitigation, including length penalties (Singhal et al., 2023), KL regularization tuning, reward clipping, and weight-averaged reward models (WARM, Ramé et al., 2024). It departs from these algorithmic or ensemble-based approaches by attacking the problem at the representation level: a two-head reward model is trained with an explicit Pearson correlation loss and an orthogonality constraint on the projection weights, so that one head absorbs length signal and the other is forced to encode content quality. This draws on the disentanglement literature (Locatello et al., 2019) but applies it in a supervised, single-feature setting where the spurious variable (length) is directly observable, making the disentanglement tractable without the inductive-bias difficulties that plague unsupervised disentanglement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Length hacking is one of the most visible and practically damaging forms of reward over-optimization in deployed RLHF systems, and ODIN offers a drop-in fix that adds no new hyperparameters to the RL stage and works across both PPO and ReMax. The approach is limited in scope, however: it is validated only on a 7B model (Vicuna-7B) with a single preference dataset (OpenAssistant), and it addresses only the length dimension of spurious reward—other hacking patterns (formatting, repetition, specific token exploits) are not covered. Open questions include whether the linear two-head decomposition generalizes to non-linear or multi-dimensional spurious features, whether it holds at larger model scales where the RM has more capacity to encode length in non-linear ways, and whether the orthogonality constraint remains beneficial when the quality and length signals are more entangled in the data.
