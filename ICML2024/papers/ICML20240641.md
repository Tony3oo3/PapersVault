## Problem

Current state-of-the-art dynamical systems reconstruction (DSR) methods have been evaluated almost exclusively on monostable benchmarks or single-basin scenarios, leaving their ability to generalize to unobserved dynamical regimes in multistable systems entirely unexplored. This paper provides the first formal mathematical framework showing that black-box deep learning approaches, without adequate structural priors, fundamentally cannot achieve out-of-domain generalization across the full state space of a multistable system, and identifies precisely why and where they fail.

## Input / Output

The method takes time series trajectories sampled from one or more basins of attraction of a multistable ODE system (e.g., the bistable Duffing oscillator or a multistable Lorenz-like system) and produces a reconstructed vector field or flow operator. Evaluation is performed over a uniform grid of initial conditions spanning the entire state space (both training and OOD basins), using two novel error metrics: a statistical error based on the sliced Wasserstein-1 distance between occupation measures, and a topological error based on Lyapunov spectrum sign agreement, relative error in the maximum Lyapunov exponent, and Hausdorff distance between limit sets. Both metrics are provably sensitive to multistability (Theorem 3.3), unlike standard MSE which breaks down for chaotic trajectories and is insensitive to missing attractors.

## Method

The paper introduces a formal OODG framework for DSR grounded in ergodic theory and topology, defining strict learnability as zero training-domain error implying zero test-domain error for all models in the hypothesis class. Theoretically, it proves two contrasting results: SINDy with a correct function library is strictly learnable provided a training trajectory does not solve an algebraic equation in the library parameters (Theorem 4.1), while universal approximators (the full C¹ vector field class) are provably not strictly learnable for multistable, non-topologically-transitive flows, with an infinite family of VFs achieving zero training error but bounded-away-from-zero test error (Theorem 4.2). Empirically, the paper identifies three concrete mechanisms causing OODG failure in RNNs trained with SGD: (i) a simplicity bias in initialization that favors monostable dynamics, (ii) generalizing solutions corresponding to saddle points rather than minima in the single-basin loss landscape, and (iii) generalizing minima being sharp (narrow) rather than wide, making them less likely to be found by SGD. These findings are validated across shPLRNNs, Neural ODEs, and Reservoir Computing on the Duffing and multistable Lorenz-like systems.

## Results

The paper's central theoretical result (Theorem 4.2) proves that universal approximators (RNNs, N-ODEs, RCs) are not strictly learnable for multistable dynamical systems: an infinite family of models achieves zero training error on one basin yet incurs non-zero generalization error on the unobserved basin. Empirically, all three SOTA black-box methods (shPLRNN, RC, N-ODE) fail to generalize to the second basin of the Duffing system when trained on data from only one basin (Fig. 3), while SINDy with the correct function library generalizes perfectly from a single trajectory (Theorem 4.1). Retraining a generalizing model on just one basin causes an approximately 20-fold increase in reconstruction error on the unobserved basin (Fig. 5a), and generalizing minima are found to be *sharper* (smaller radius) than non-generalizing minima (Fig. 6), contrary to standard deep learning heuristics. When data from both basins are provided, all three black-box methods succeed with small statistical errors (shPLRNN ≈ 1.4×10⁻³, N-ODE ≈ 2.1×10⁻³, RC ≈ 2.7×10⁻³; Fig. A5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new DSR method but rather provides a theoretical framework and empirical diagnosis of why existing methods fail at OODG. Against SINDy (Brunton et al., 2016), the paper shows a sharp contrast: SINDy with the correct polynomial library is provably strictly learnable (Theorem 4.1) and generalizes from a single trajectory in one basin to the entire state space, whereas all three black-box baselines (shPLRNN, N-ODE, RC) provably cannot (Theorem 4.2) and empirically fail (Fig. 3). Against shPLRNN (Hess et al., 2023), the paper demonstrates that even with generalized teacher forcing and careful training, the model's simplicity bias in initialization (Fig. 4) and the saddle-point nature of generalizing solutions (Fig. 5b) prevent OODG on multistable systems. Against N-ODE (Chen et al., 2018) and RC (Patel & Ott, 2022), the same OODG failure is confirmed with identical eCDF distributions of high statistical and topological errors (Fig. 3b). The key finding is that the failure is not a sampling issue—increasing data from one basin does not help—but a fundamental identifiability problem in the hypothesis class, which only a strong structural prior (as in SINDy) can resolve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first comprehensive mathematical treatment of OODG in DSR, providing theorems that delineate what is and is not learnable, new evaluation metrics sensitive to multistability, and a diagnosis of why current training algorithms (SGD with standard initialization) actively unlearn multistable structure even from perfect initialization. The practical implication is significant: most real-world systems are multistable, yet the DSR field has overwhelmingly benchmarked on monostable systems, masking this fundamental limitation. Open questions include how to design training algorithms that explicitly encourage and preserve multistability, how to handle the common empirical scenario where the correct function library is unknown, and whether the theoretical impossibility results extend to higher-dimensional or partially observed systems.
