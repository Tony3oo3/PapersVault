## Problem

ANN-to-SNN conversion has only been theoretically grounded for ReLU networks via the IF neuron correspondence, restricting target architectures to ReLU-only nonlinearities and requiring data-dependent calibration for accuracy. This gap prevents conversion of modern DNNs employing GELU, layer normalization, or max pooling, and limits low-latency SNN inference performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241565/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained ANN model (ResNet, VGG, ConvNeXt, MLP-Mixer, ResMLP, RegNetX) with arbitrary elementwise or multi-operand nonlinearities and produces a converted SNN that classifies RGB images over T time steps (32–256). The SNN communicates via binary spike trains decoded with a signed schedule coding scheme, where each spike encodes the sign of a gradient update scaled by a time-varying learning rate η(t). Layer-wise normalization of ReLU activations is applied before conversion, and the learning rate schedule is empirically tuned per architecture (e.g., η(t)=5.0/(t+1) for ConvNeXt/MLP-Mixer, η(t)=0.15·0.965^t for others).

## Method

The paper first proves that discrete IF neuron dynamics with rate coding are mathematically equivalent to a subgradient method over a convex objective (Theorem 4.1), and that LIF neurons with EMA coding correspond to subgradient descent with constant step size (Theorem 4.2). It then replaces the subgradient optimizer with sign gradient descent (signGD), where a binary spike conveys only the sign of the gradient rather than its magnitude, widening the space of approximable nonlinear functions. The resulting signGD-based neuron (Definition 5.2) maintains two internal variables u(t) and v(t) with time-scheduled coefficients α_i(t), β_i(t), and a generalized learning rate schedule η(t), producing a spike s(t) via a Heaviside threshold on the gradient of a chosen objective L(y; x). Specific corollaries derive closed-form firing mechanisms for ReLU, LeakyReLU, GELU, max pooling (via a binary tree of pairwise max neurons), and layer normalization (decomposed into square and multiply-inverse-sqrt sub-neurons), enabling conversion of architectures previously inconvertible to SNNs.

## Results

On ImageNet, the signGD-based neuron achieves state-of-the-art ANN-to-SNN conversion accuracy: ResNet-34 reaches 75.34% top-1 at T=64 (vs. 75.65% ANN), VGG-16 reaches 75.32% at T=64 (vs. 75.35% ANN), and RegNetX-3.2GF reaches 77.74% at T=64 (vs. 81.19% ANN), outperforming all prior conversion methods by 3–7% at T=64. The method is the first to successfully convert ConvNext-B (81.07% at T=256), MLP-Mixer-B32 (72.97% at T=256), and ResMLP-S24 (78.04% at T=256), which require GELU and layer normalization support. On CIFAR-10, the converted SNN outperforms all existing works at T≥64 with a gap of <1% to the ANN, and also leads at T≤32 except for spike-aware activation techniques.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The signGD-based neuron outperforms all seven baselines at T≥64 across ResNet-34, VGG-16, and RegNetX on ImageNet. Against SNNC-AP (Li et al., 2021), the strongest prior method using calibration, the proposed method gains +4.2% on ResNet-34 (75.34 vs. 71.12) and +4.6% on VGG-16 (75.32 vs. 70.69) at T=64, and +6.8% on RegNetX (77.74 vs. 70.96). Spike-aware activation methods (QCFS, SlipReLU, SRP) achieve slightly higher accuracy at T≤32 (e.g., QCFS 69.37% vs. Ours 59.85% on ResNet-34 at T=32) but degrade significantly at T≥64, losing 1.97–5.71% to the proposed method. TSC (Han & Roy, 2020) and RTS (Deng & Gu, 2021) are far behind, with RTS achieving near-zero accuracy at T≤64 due to its reliance on temporal coding. SNM (Wang et al., 2022) is outperformed by +3.8% on VGG-16 at T=64. Crucially, the proposed method is the only one that converts architectures requiring GELU and layer normalization (ConvNext-B, MLP-Mixer, ResMLP), which no prior baseline can handle.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ANN-to-SNN conversion paradigm (Cao et al., 2015; Rueckauer et al., 2017), which established only a single theoretical correspondence between the IF neuron and clipped ReLU, into a general optimization-theoretic framework. The authors prove that discrete integrate-and-fire dynamics are exactly equivalent to the subgradient method (Boyd et al., 2003), then replace the subgradient optimizer with signGD (Bernstein et al., 2018) to unlock approximation of arbitrary nonlinearities. This departs from prior conversion techniques (TSC, RTS, SNNC-AP, QCFS, SlipReLU) that either require ReLU-only architectures, data-dependent calibration, or spike-aware activation substitutions, by providing a principled neuron design that preserves binary spike-based communication while supporting GELU, LeakyReLU, max pooling, and layer normalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper is the first to convert ConvNeXt, MLP-Mixer, and ResMLP to SNNs, opening a class of high-performance DNN architectures (with GELU and layer normalization) that were previously inconvertible. The optimization-theoretic framework provides a general recipe for designing new spiking neurons by choosing an optimizer form and learning rate schedule, which could accelerate future neuron design. Key limitations include a ~1.6× per-operation energy overhead over LIF neurons, slow convergence of layer normalization approximation (especially near σ→0), and the absence of hardware implementation. Open questions include extending the framework to continuous biophysical models (e.g., Hodgkin-Huxley), applying it to SNN training rather than conversion, and reducing the energy cost of the additional internal variables.
