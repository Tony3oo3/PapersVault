## Problem

Protein fitness optimization from low-fitness starting sequences is challenging because fitness landscapes are rugged with multiple peaks separated by low-fitness valleys, and existing methods (single-mutation search, Bayesian optimization, greedy rollout) struggle to escape local optima. The vast sequence space (20^L combinations) makes exhaustive search infeasible, and prior latent-space approaches either use corrupt-and-denoise autoencoders or lack an explicit mechanism for multi-step exploration across valleys.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of low-fitness protein sequences (below a specified percentile of a reference dataset) and a black-box fitness oracle (or in silico predictor) as input, operating in an active learning loop of E=15 rounds with 256 oracle calls per round. It produces 128 optimized protein sequences (length L=237 for GFP, L=28 for AAV) with high fitness and diversity. The latent representation has dimension R=32 (GFP) or R=16 (AAV), and each optimization step is constrained to at most m_step=3 mutations via a calibrating mechanism, with a total episode budget of m_total=15 mutations.

## Method

LatProtRL introduces a Variant Encoder-Decoder (VED) that encodes a protein sequence into a low-dimensional latent vector by subtracting the ESM-2 CLS embedding of a reference sequence from the mutant's CLS embedding, then projecting through a fully-connected layer with tanh activation; the decoder recovers the sequence via prompt-tuning, where the latent vector replaces the reference's CLS token and is passed through ESM-2 attention layers (first 4 fine-tuned). Optimization is formulated as a Markov decision process where a PPO policy perturbs the latent state by a continuous action vector (magnitude δ) over T_ep timesteps, with a sparse reward given only at episode end from the oracle. Three key components enable robust exploration: a frontier buffer (inspired by Go-Explore) that stores and samples from previously found high-fitness sequences to balance exploitation and exploration, calibrating steps that assign negative reward when a single action produces more than m_step mutations, and constrained decoding that applies only the top m_decode predicted mutations to the previous sequence. This multi-step MDP formulation allows the policy to learn trajectories that temporarily decrease fitness to cross valleys and reach higher peaks, which single-step or greedy methods cannot achieve.

## Results

On the GFP hard task, LatProtRL achieves a median fitness of 0.85 (vs. 0.75 for AdaLead, the strongest baseline) and a median distance to top-10% high-fitness sequences (d_high) of 2.0, compared to 19 for AdaLead. On the GFP medium task, LatProtRL matches AdaLead's fitness (0.93) while achieving a d_high of 1.0 versus AdaLead's 14. On AAV tasks, AdaLead edges out LatProtRL on raw fitness (0.74 vs. 0.71 medium; 0.72 vs. 0.66 hard), but LatProtRL substantially outperforms on d_high (2.4 vs. 4.4 medium; 2.0 vs. 6.4 hard), indicating its designs are closer to experimentally validated high-fitness regions. In in vitro validation, LatProtRL's top GFP designs reach fluorescence intensities of 5.00±0.16 and 5.01±0.12 (×10⁷), exceeding the wild-type (3.15±0.07), while only one of two AdaLead designs surpasses wild-type.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LatProtRL is compared against five baselines: PEX, AdaLead, Bayesian Optimization (BO), CMAES (one-hot), and CMAES-VED (using the paper's own VED encoder). On GFP, LatProtRL clearly outperforms all baselines: it matches AdaLead's fitness on the medium task (0.93) while achieving a d_high of 1.0 versus AdaLead's 14, and it surpasses AdaLead on the hard task in both fitness (0.85 vs. 0.75) and d_high (2.0 vs. 19). BO and CMAES (one-hot) fail catastrophically on GFP, with BO reaching only 0.25–0.33 fitness and CMAES producing negative fitness values. On AAV, AdaLead achieves slightly higher raw fitness (0.74 vs. 0.71 medium; 0.72 vs. 0.66 hard), but LatProtRL generates sequences much closer to experimentally validated high-fitness regions (d_high of 2.4 vs. 4.4 and 2.0 vs. 6.4). CMAES-VED, which uses the same VED representation but without RL, achieves 0.61/0.50 fitness on AAV medium/hard, confirming that the RL policy and frontier buffer contribute meaningfully beyond the representation alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

LatProtRL carves new ground at the intersection of latent-space protein optimization (Stanton et al., 2022; Gómez-Bombarelli et al., 2018) and reinforcement learning for sequence design (Angermueller et al., 2019; Wang et al., 2023), but departs from both in key ways. Unlike Stanton et al.'s denoising autoencoder approach, it uses a variant encoder-decoder that encodes mutations relative to a reference sequence with prompt-tuning, and unlike prior RL methods that act in sequence space, it formulates optimization as an episodic MDP in latent space with a frontier buffer (inspired by Go-Explore) and calibrating steps to control per-step mutation magnitude. The constrained decoding strategy and the use of a pre-trained protein language model (ESM-2) for both encoding and decoding represent a practical repurposing of pLMs for iterative optimization rather than one-shot generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The in vitro validation is the most compelling contribution: LatProtRL designs achieved higher GFP fluorescence than wild-type while AdaLead designs (ranked higher by the in silico oracle) did not, suggesting the method generates sequences closer to the true experimental distribution rather than overfitting to the surrogate. However, the decoder accuracy remains a bottleneck (40–62% top-1 on mutated positions), the VED does not handle insertions or deletions, and evaluation is limited to two protein families with relatively short sequences. Open questions include whether the MDP formulation scales to longer proteins or multi-objective settings, and whether combining the latent-space RL loop with AlphaFold2 structural feedback (mentioned as future work) can further improve designs.
