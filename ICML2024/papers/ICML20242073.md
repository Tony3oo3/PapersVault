## Problem

The generalized score function of Huang et al. (2018) for causal discovery in RKHS relies on a median heuristic to set the kernel bandwidth on the response variable, which has no optimality guarantee and can produce spurious causal edges by failing to capture the true data-dependent structure. This paper addresses the gap by proposing an automatic, data-driven kernel selection method that learns the optimal bandwidth jointly with the regression function, eliminating the need for manual heuristic tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes an observational dataset of n samples over q variables (continuous, discrete, mixed, or multi-dimensional) and outputs a recovered causal DAG (or its Markov equivalence class) along with per-variable learned kernel bandwidths. The kernel bandwidth is initialized with the standard median heuristic but then optimized via L-BFGS during the score evaluation at each GES step. The score function is evaluated per variable given its candidate parent set, and GES performs the discrete graph search over the combinatorial space of DAGs.

## Method

The core idea is to replace the fixed kernel on the response variable in the RKHS regression model with a trainable one and to model the generative process as a mixture of independent noise variables, so that the noise ε = k_x − f(PA) should be independent of PA. Minimizing the mutual information I(PA, ε̃) between the estimated noise and the parents is shown to be equivalent to maximizing the marginal likelihood of the joint distribution p(X, PA), where f is modeled as a Gaussian Process to prevent overfitting. The resulting score function (Eq. 17) differs from Huang et al.'s conditional marginal likelihood by an additional Jacobian determinant term that depends on the kernel bandwidth, which is precisely what provides the gradient signal to learn the optimal bandwidth. The paper proves the score is locally consistent (Lemma 4.2) via a Laplace-approximation/BIC argument, guaranteeing that GES with this score recovers the correct Markov equivalence class asymptotically.

## Results

On synthetic data (8 variables, n=200 and n=500, graph density 0.2–0.8), the proposed method achieves the highest F1 score across most density settings for continuous, mixed, and multi-dimensional data, with the largest margins over Marg and CV at high graph density (≥0.6). On real benchmarks SACH (11 variables) and CHILD (20 variables), the method's F1 score surpasses all baselines as sample size grows (n=200–2000), and its normalized SHD on CHILD is significantly lower than Marg and CV. Against continuous optimization methods (NOTEARS, NS-MLP, DAG-GNN, Dibs) on SACH and CHILD, the proposed method achieves substantially higher recall and F1, while the optimization-based methods retain higher precision but miss many true edges.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against seven baselines: PC (constraint-based with KCI), GP (vanilla GP marginal likelihood), CV and Marg (RKHS-based conditional likelihood from Huang et al. 2018), and four continuous optimization methods (NOTEARS, NS-MLP, DAG-GNN, Dibs). PC outperforms score-based GP on sparse real benchmarks but degrades sharply as graph density increases due to diminishing test power. GP is consistently outperformed by the RKHS-based methods (CV, Marg, and the proposed method), confirming the benefit of feature-space projection. The proposed method beats both CV and Marg on F1 and SHD across all synthetic data types, with the gap widening at high graph density where median-heuristic bandwidth selection fails most. Against the continuous optimization baselines (NOTEARS, NS-MLP, DAG-GNN, Dibs), the proposed method achieves markedly higher recall and F1 on SACH and CHILD, while those methods show high precision but miss many edges; Dibs is the weakest among them on both precision and recall. The authors attribute the F1 gap to the discrete search strategy (GES) exhaustively exploring the combinatorial space versus the continuous methods' reliance on acyclicity-constrained optimization over simpler local models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RKHS-based generalized score function of Huang et al. (2018), which used the conditional cross-covariance operator (Fukumizu et al., 2004) to transform conditional independence tests into regression model selection for causal discovery. The key departure is replacing the fixed, median-heuristic kernel bandwidth with a trainable kernel, and substituting the conditional likelihood score with a marginal likelihood of the joint distribution derived from a mutual information minimization perspective. This reframing models the causal relationship as a mixture of independent noise variables, providing a principled mechanism to simultaneously learn kernel parameters and the regression function without the degenerate solutions that arise when naively making the kernel trainable under the original conditional likelihood.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical but underappreciated weakness in kernel-based causal discovery: the median heuristic for bandwidth selection has no optimality guarantees and can produce spurious causal edges, as demonstrated in the motivating example. The proposed score function is locally consistent (proven via BIC equivalence), ensuring asymptotic recovery of the correct Markov equivalence class with GES. However, the method inherits the O(n³) bottleneck of Gaussian Processes and is only validated on small graphs (≤20 variables), leaving open the question of scalability to high-dimensional settings. The authors note future work on integrating the score with continuous optimization-based search methods (NOTEARS, DAG-GNN) to handle larger variable sets.
