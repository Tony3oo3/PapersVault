## Problem

LoRA fine-tuning of foundation models suffers from an inherent learning rate imbalance between its two factor matrices (A and B), requiring practitioners to use different learning rates (e.g., η_B/η_A = 2⁴) for stable training, and standard optimizers remain sensitive to hyperparameter choices. No prior work had exploited the low-rank matrix factorization structure of LoRA through Riemannian optimization to design a principled preconditioner that resolves this imbalance.

## Input / Output

The method takes frozen pretrained model weights (e.g., GPT-2 medium, Mistral 7B, Stable Diffusion V1.5) and a small fine-tuning dataset (e.g., 6 images for diffusion, E2E/GLUE for LLMs), producing only the low-rank LoRA parameters A ∈ ℝ^{m×r} and B ∈ ℝ^{r×n} with rank r typically 4–16. The preconditioner itself is an r×r matrix computed from the current A and B values, introducing negligible storage overhead. For diffusion models, the output is generated images conditioned on a text prompt; for LLMs, it is generated text or classification scores on NLU benchmarks.

## Method

The paper introduces a scaled gradient descent (scaled GD) update where the gradient of A is left-multiplied by (BᵀB + δI)⁻¹ and the gradient of B is right-multiplied by (AAᵀ + δI)⁻¹, derived from a novel Riemannian metric g_{[x]}(η,ξ) = ⟨η_A, ξ_A BᵀB⟩ + ⟨η_B, ξ_B AᵀA⟩ on the quotient manifold of fixed-rank matrices. The key insight is that this preconditioner acts as a gradient projector, projecting the full-matrix gradient onto the row space of A and column space of B, thereby better approximating full fine-tuning than unscaled gradient descent. Theoretically, the authors prove that LoRA trained with this preconditioned Adam achieves stable feature learning under infinite-width NN setting with a single shared learning rate η = Θ(1), eliminating the need for the separate η_A and η_B tuning required by unpreconditioned Adam. Empirically, the method requires only four lines of code change to existing AdamW/SGD implementations and is validated on GPT-2 (E2E NLG), Mistral 7B (GLUE), Stable Diffusion V1.5 (object generation), and Mix-of-Show (face generation), showing consistent improvements in convergence speed, final quality, and robustness to learning rate choices.

## Results

On the E2E NLG challenge with GPT-2 medium (r=4), scaled AdamW achieves the best scores across all metrics: BLEU 69.6, NIST 8.77, MET 46.6, ROUGE-L 71.8, CIDEr 2.52, outperforming unscaled AdamW (BLEU 68.9) and closing the gap between SGD and AdamW (scaled GD reaches BLEU 69.2 vs. SGD's 66.6). On the GLUE benchmark with Mistral 7B (r=16), scaled GD dramatically improves over SGD from 71.21 to 80.36 average score, while scaled AdamW improves AdamW from 88.28 to 89.01 average. The scaled optimizers outperform their unscaled counterparts on all evaluation metrics in both settings, with the largest gains observed for SGD-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares scaled (preconditioned) optimizers against their unscaled counterparts (SGD and AdamW) as baselines. Scaled GD dramatically outperforms plain SGD on GLUE, improving the average score from 71.21 to 80.36 (+9.15 points), with particularly large gains on RTE (50.90→54.15) and CoLA (55.89→68.17). On E2E, scaled GD (BLEU 69.2) nearly matches AdamW (BLEU 68.9) while using a simpler optimizer with less storage. Scaled AdamW consistently outperforms unscaled AdamW on every metric in both tables: on E2E it gains +0.7 BLEU (69.6 vs. 68.9) and on GLUE it gains +0.73 average (89.01 vs. 88.28). The key practical finding is that the preconditioner makes training more robust to learning rate choices, as demonstrated in diffusion model experiments where unscaled AdamW produces black images at large learning rates while scaled AdamW maintains quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges Riemannian optimization and parameter-efficient fine-tuning by importing a preconditioner derived from a Riemannian metric on the quotient manifold of low-rank matrices (Mishra & Sepulchre, 2016) into the LoRA training loop. The preconditioner was previously studied only for classical low-rank matrix problems (matrix sensing, robust PCA) with plain gradient descent (Tong et al., 2021b); here it is extended for the first time to Adam-family optimizers and to deep network fine-tuning. The work directly addresses the asymmetric learning-rate requirement (η_B ≫ η_A) identified by LoRA+ (Hayou et al., 2024), showing that the Riemannian preconditioner eliminates the need for separate per-parameter learning rates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is strong: the preconditioner is only r×r (typically r=4), requires a four-line code change, and introduces negligible runtime overhead, making it trivially adoptable in existing LoRA pipelines. Theoretically, it provides an elegant resolution to the learning-rate imbalance problem under infinite-width analysis, though the convergence guarantee (Section 7) is limited to a convexified two-layer ReLU surrogate rather than actual deep networks. Open questions include whether the preconditioner transfers to other PEFT paradigms (adapters, prompt tuning), whether it helps at very large LoRA ranks where the r×r inversion is no longer trivially cheap, and whether the infinite-width stability results carry over to the finite-width regime where most practical fine-tuning occurs.
