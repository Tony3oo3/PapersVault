## Problem

State-of-the-art stereo matching networks predict disparity by taking the expectation of a discrete categorical distribution over candidate disparities, which is equivalent to L2 risk minimization and is sensitive to multi-modal distributions, producing overly smooth predictions that miss high-frequency details. The paper asks how to model continuous scene disparity in a principled, differentiable manner that is robust to multi-modal probability distributions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of rectified RGB stereo images and outputs a per-pixel continuous (real-valued) disparity map. Internally, the network operates at 1/4 and 1/2 resolution: the coarse stage samples 192 uniform disparity hypotheses over the full range, while the refined stage samples 16 hypotheses in a local window around the coarse prediction. The final disparity is not restricted to the discrete hypothesis set but can be any real value within [d_min, d_max].

## Method

The key insight is to frame disparity prediction as L1 risk minimization: given a discrete categorical distribution over disparity hypotheses, the paper interpolates it into a continuous probability density via a Laplacian kernel, then finds the disparity y that minimizes the expected absolute error ∫|y−x|p(x)dx. Since no closed-form solution exists, a binary search (O(log N) complexity) locates the zero of the risk derivative, and the implicit function theorem provides the backward gradient dy/dp^m to enable end-to-end training through this non-differentiable search. The architecture is a two-stage cascade (inspired by CasMVSNet): a ResNet backbone with spatial pyramid pooling extracts multi-scale features, 3D cost volumes are built by concatenation, and stacked 3D hourglass networks aggregate matching costs before the L1-risk module produces the final continuous disparity at each stage.

## Results

On the SceneFlow test set, Stereo Risk achieves the best EPE of 0.43 px, reducing the >1px error from 5.00 (ACVNet) to 4.22 and the >0.5px error from 8.51 (IGEV) to 8.10, while using only 11.96M parameters. On KITTI 2012, the method attains the best non-occluded >2px error of 1.58 (vs. 1.69 for PCWNet) and >3px NOC of 1.00. In cross-domain evaluation (trained only on SceneFlow, no fine-tuning), Stereo Risk achieves the best >1px error on Middlebury (9.32 NOC, 12.63 ALL) and ETH3D (2.41 NOC, 2.71 ALL), outperforming IGEV by 1.19 and 1.34 px respectively on ETH3D >1px.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Stereo Risk outperforms all 12 compared baselines on the SceneFlow test set, achieving the lowest EPE (0.43 vs. 0.47 for ACVNet and IGEV) and the lowest >1px error (4.22 vs. 5.00 for ACVNet, 5.21 for IGEV, 5.44 for DLNR). On KITTI 2012, it achieves the best non-occluded >2px (1.58) and >3px (1.00) errors, beating PCWNet (1.69/1.04) and IGEV (1.71/1.12), though it trails PCWNet slightly on the ALL >3px metric (1.44 vs. 1.37). On KITTI 2015, it ties for the best NOC D1_ALL (1.48, shared with IGEV and LAC+GANet) and achieves the best NOC D1_BG (1.25). In cross-domain settings without fine-tuning, the advantage is most pronounced: on ETH3D it reduces >1px NOC from 3.60 (IGEV) to 2.41, and on Middlebury from 10.16 (DLNR) to 9.32. The method also improves upon ITSA-based baselines (PSMNet, GwcNet, CFNet) when applied at inference time, confirming the plug-and-play nature of the L1-risk module. CFNet and ACVNet, which use simpler architectures, show the largest absolute gaps, while the closest competitors (IGEV, PCWNet) are separated by 0.1–0.5 px on key metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the two-stage cascade cost volume architecture from CasMVSNet (Gu et al., 2020) and the stacked hourglass cost aggregation from PSMNet (Chang & Chen, 2018), but departs fundamentally from the standard expectation-based disparity decoding used across the field. It reframes continuous disparity prediction as an L1 risk minimization problem grounded in statistical decision theory (Vapnik, 1991; Lehmann & Casella, 1998), showing that the widely-used expectation is merely the L2-risk special case. The key methodological departure is using the implicit function theorem to backpropagate through the non-differentiable L1 optimal solution, enabling end-to-end training without closed-form solutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The L1 risk minimization module is a lightweight, parameter-free drop-in replacement for expectation-based decoding that consistently improves accuracy across multiple architectures (ACVNet, PCWNet, IGEV) even without retraining, suggesting the prediction head is a meaningful bottleneck in current stereo networks. The notably stronger cross-domain generalization (e.g., >1px error dropping from 4.05 to 2.71 on ETH3D) hints that L1 robustness to multi-modal distributions transfers better to unseen domains. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-view stereo or optical flow, and whether the binary search overhead becomes prohibitive at very high disparity ranges or real-time constraints.
