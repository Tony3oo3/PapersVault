## Problem

Transformer world models are expected to improve long-horizon policy gradients due to their short gradient paths, but the paper shows that state-conditioned transformers (both Markovian and history-conditioned) actually produce circuitous gradient paths through autoregressive state predictions, causing policy gradients to grow exponentially with horizon. This means the favorable gradient properties of transformers are lost when they are used in the standard state-conditioned world model formulation for model-based RL.

## Input / Output

The Action-conditioned World Model (AWM) takes a single initial state s_1 and a sequence of actions a_1, ..., a_t as input, and directly predicts the future state ŝ_{t+1} without conditioning on any intermediate predicted states. The policy π_θ maps a (stop-gradient) state to a continuous action, and the full BPO loop alternates between supervised training of the AWM on collected episodes and policy optimization via backpropagation through the unrolled AWM. Unlike standard world models that predict one-step transitions, the AWM must internally encode the entire dynamics to map an action sequence to a future state, which is what eliminates the circuitous paths.

## Method

The paper introduces Action-conditioned World Models (AWMs) of the form ŝ_{t+1} = g(s_1, a_1, ..., a_t), which condition solely on the initial state and action history rather than intermediate states. The key theoretical contribution is showing that AWMs directly inherit the gradient propagation properties of the underlying architecture: an RNN AWM reproduces the exponential gradient growth of Markovian models (formalizing their equivalence via Proposition 4.1), while a self-attention AWM yields only polynomial O(H³) gradient growth (Corollary 4.2), in contrast to the O(L_s^H) exponential growth of history-conditioned models (Theorem 3.1). The training procedure (BPO, Algorithm 1) alternates between collecting episodes, training the AWM with a standard state-prediction loss, and updating the policy by backpropagating the cumulative reward through the unrolled AWM. Empirically, transformer AWMs produce smoother optimization landscapes than even the true differentiable simulator in chaotic environments, and outperform Markovian models, HWMs, SAC, and Online Decision Transformers on long-horizon Myriad tasks.

## Results

On the Myriad testbed (8 long-horizon continuous-control tasks), BPO with transformer AWMs is the only world model to maintain strong performance as the horizon increases beyond 100 steps, while Markovian, RNN, and History-conditioned World Model (HWM) baselines all degrade sharply. On the chaotic double-pendulum environment, transformer AWMs produce stable gradients and outperform all other world models, including backpropagation through the differentiable simulator itself. In the one-bounce environment with non-differentiable dynamics, AWMs accurately predict the final reward landscape while Markovian and HWM models suffer catastrophic compounding errors. BPO with transformer AWMs is also significantly more sample-efficient than both model-free SAC and Online Decision Transformer on Myriad.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against model-free Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), BPO with transformer AWMs achieves higher final performance and reaches it with fewer environment steps on Myriad tasks at both 20 and 100-step horizons (Figure 6). Against Online Decision Transformer (Online-DT), BPO with transformer AWMs similarly outperforms in both final return and sample efficiency; Online-DT additionally struggles to scale with horizon, dropping sharply from 20 to 100 steps, and in a separate evaluation (Figure 9) performs worse than even simple one-step model-based methods on Myriad. Within the model-based BPO family, transformer AWMs dominate Markovian models, RNN world models, and transformer HWMs on all Myriad horizons beyond 100 steps, and on the double-pendulum they are the only method that successfully optimizes the policy, whereas HWMs and Markovian models fail entirely due to exploding or vanishing gradients from circuitous paths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the long line of backpropagation-based policy optimization (Werbos 1974; Heess et al. 2015; Hafner et al. 2021/2023) and the recent adoption of transformers as world models (Micheli et al. 2022; Chen et al. 2022; Robine et al. 2023). It departs from prior work by showing that the standard History-conditioned World Model formulation—predicting next state from full state-action history—creates 'circuitous gradient paths' that negate the transformer's favorable gradient propagation properties, a phenomenon not previously identified. The paper formally establishes (Proposition 4.1) that backpropagating through unrolled Markovian dynamics is equivalent to backpropagating through an RNN, a connection previously only informally noted, and uses this to introduce Action-conditioned World Models that restructure the gradient flow to be purely within the network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AWM framework provides a principled way to leverage any sequence model's gradient properties for policy optimization, with the theoretical guarantee that the policy gradient inherits the architecture's gradient behavior (polynomial for transformers vs. exponential for RNNs). The finding that a learned AWM can produce an easier-to-navigate optimization landscape than the true differentiable simulator is notable and suggests that model-based RL need not faithfully replicate environment dynamics to be effective. Key limitations include the deterministic, finite-horizon, low-dimensional experimental setting; scaling to high-dimensional POMDPs, stochastic environments, and infinite-horizon tasks remains open, though the paper provides preliminary evidence for stochastic robustness via intermediate-state rollouts.
