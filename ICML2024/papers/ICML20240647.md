## Problem

Path-based graph neural networks have achieved strong empirical results, but their expressive power has not been formally characterized. It was unclear how path aggregation relates to the k-WL hierarchy, whether paths can count substructures like cycles, and which graph families they can distinguish. This paper fills that gap by proposing a general color refinement framework for path-based GNNs and proving it forms a novel expressivity hierarchy incomparable to k-WL and other powerful GNNs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240647/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes undirected graphs with node features (and edge features for real-world molecular datasets) and produces node-level or graph-level embeddings via iterative path aggregation. A key structural detail is that at each iteration, every node aggregates the multiset of all paths up to a configurable length ℓ starting from that node, optionally augmented with shortest-path distance labels up to depth d ≤ ℓ. The theoretical analysis treats the output as a stable node coloring (for isomorphism testing), while the neural implementation PAIN outputs continuous embeddings suitable for regression or classification.

## Method

The paper introduces PATH-WL, a family of color refinement algorithms where each node's color is updated by hashing the multiset of all paths up to length ℓ (with optional shortest-path distance encoding of depth d), rather than aggregating only direct neighbors as in 1-WL. The corresponding neural architecture PAIN implements this by encoding each path with an LSTM (injective over sequences) and aggregating path embeddings via summation (injective over multisets), with a readout MLP for graph-level predictions. A critical design choice is that path length ℓ and the number of iterations are independent parameters, unlike pathNN where they must coincide, allowing shorter paths with more iterations to achieve the same expressivity at lower computational cost. Theoretically, the paper proves that 1-PATH-WL can count cycles of arbitrary length at the node level, is strictly more expressive than 1-WL for any ℓ > 1, and is incomparable to k-WL for all k ≥ 3, establishing a new expressivity hierarchy.

## Results

On the EXP expressivity benchmark, untrained 0-PAIN with path length 5 distinguishes all 600 non-isomorphic graph pairs (0 failures), matching 3-WL and pathNN. On the strongly regular graph (SR) dataset, 1-PAIN with path length 4 and distance encoding d=1 distinguishes over 50% of pairs, achieving a ~40% failure rate on SR(29,14,6,7) compared to pathNN's 80%. On ZINC, 1-PAIN achieves a MAE of 0.148±0.003, outperforming GIN (0.387), GCN (0.278), and GAT (0.384) but trailing CIN (0.079), ESAN (0.102), and pathNN (0.090). On OGBG-MOLHIV, 1-PAIN achieves a ROC-AUC of 78.50±1.4, beating GIN (75.58), GCN (76.06), and GSN (77.99) but falling short of CIN (80.94) and Graphormer (80.51).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the EXP expressivity benchmark, PAIN matches 3-WL and pathNN by distinguishing all 600 graph pairs, while GIN fails on all 600. On the SR dataset, 1-PAIN substantially outperforms pathNN, reducing the failure rate from ~80% to ~40% on SR(29,14,6,7). On ZINC, PAIN (MAE 0.148) clearly beats standard message-passing GNNs—GIN (0.387), GCN (0.278), and GAT (0.384)—but is outperformed by higher-order methods CIN (0.079), ESAN (0.102), and pathNN (0.090). On OGBG-MOLHIV, PAIN (ROC-AUC 78.50) surpasses GIN (75.58), GCN (76.06), and GSN (77.99) but trails CIN (80.94), Graphormer (80.51), and pathNN (79.17). The authors attribute PAIN's gap to state-of-the-art on real-world datasets to the complexity of aggregating all paths up to length ℓ simultaneously in each layer, whereas pathNN incrementally increases path length layer-wise, simplifying the learning task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the path-based GNN introduced by Michel et al. (2023, pathNN) and the GNN–WL expressivity equivalence framework of Xu et al. (2019). It generalizes pathNN by decoupling path length from the number of iterations and by aggregating all paths up to length ℓ simultaneously rather than layer-by-layer, yielding the PATH-WL color refinement family and the PAIN architecture. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of shortest-path distance encoding (parameter d) as an orthogonal axis of expressivity, which the authors show is critical for cycle counting and for distinguishing strongly regular graphs with shorter path lengths.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that path-based GNNs form a novel expressivity hierarchy incomparable to k-WL, Subgraph GNNs, Local 2-GNNs, and Folklore k-GNNs, and that they can count cycles of arbitrary length—properties important for molecular graph learning. However, the exponential dependence on path length limits practical applicability to sparse graphs, and PAIN does not outperform state-of-the-art methods (CIN, pathNN) on real-world benchmarks like ZINC and OGBG-MOLHIV. Open questions include characterizing graph classes where short path lengths suffice and developing approaches that aggregate only a relevant subset of paths to reduce computational cost.
