## Problem

Fragment-biased GNNs that incorporate substructures as inductive bias consistently outperform standard message-passing models on molecular tasks, yet no theoretical expressivity framework exists to analyze or compare them. Meanwhile, higher-order GNNs with strong theoretical guarantees (via k-WL) often underperform these fragment-biased models empirically and suffer from poor generalization to out-of-distribution data. This paper bridges the theory-practice gap by providing a unified expressivity measure for fragment-biased architectures and a model that achieves both high expressiveness and strong generalization.

## Input / Output

The model consumes molecular graphs with node features (atom types) and edge features (bond types), which are first decomposed via a rings-paths fragmentation into minimal cycles, paths formed by degree-2 chains, and junction nodes where three or more fragments meet. The fragmentation produces a higher-level graph whose nodes are the fragments, connected when they share a vertex in the original graph. Outputs include regression targets (penalized logP on ZINC, spatial lengths on Peptides-struct), multi-label classification (10 functional property classes on Peptides-func), and substructure count predictions for expressivity evaluation. The ordinal encoding of fragment types (class embedding plus size-scaled embedding) allows the model to handle an infinite vocabulary of unseen fragment sizes without retraining.

## Method

The paper introduces the Fragment-WL test, a hierarchy of WL variants (NF-WL < FR-WL < HLG-WL) that progressively incorporate fragment information as node features, learned fragment representations, and a higher-level graph of inter-fragment connections, establishing that higher-level abstraction strictly increases expressivity. Building on this, FragNet performs bidirectional message passing between the original molecular graph and the higher-level fragment graph: nodes receive messages from neighboring nodes and their containing fragments, while fragments receive messages from their constituent nodes and neighboring fragments. The rings-paths fragmentation decomposes the complete graph using only two substructure classes (rings and paths) plus junction nodes, achieving full coverage with a small vocabulary (18–100 entries depending on dataset). An ordinal encoding represents each fragment as a pair of learned embeddings—one for its class (path, cycle, junction) and one scaled proportionally to its size—enabling generalization to unseen fragment sizes. The model achieves HLG-WL expressivity (strictly more powerful than 3-WL for 5-cycle-recovering fragmentations) while maintaining linear complexity in the number of nodes and fragments.

## Results

On ZINC-10k (penalized logP, MAE↓), FragNet achieves 0.0775±0.005, matching CIN++ (0.077±0.004) and outperforming all other GNNs by at least 27% (vs. GSN at 0.115) and all fragment-biased models by 34% (vs. HIMP at 0.151). On the Long-Range Graph Benchmark Peptides-struct (MAE↓), FragNet achieves 0.2462±0.0021, the best GNN result, narrowly beating CIN++ (0.2523) and HIMP (0.2503), and on Peptides-func (AP↑) it reaches 0.6678±0.005, surpassing all GNNs and most transformers. For out-of-distribution generalization (7-ring held out), FragNet achieves a test MAE of 0.34 versus GRIT's 0.61 (1.8× lower error), and on zero-shot transfer to QM9 it reaches 1.12 MAE, outperforming GRIT (1.22) and HIMP (3.43).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FragNet outperforms all basic GNNs (GCN, GIN, GAT, GraphSAGE) by a wide margin on ZINC-10k (0.0775 vs. 0.367–0.526 MAE) and all higher-order GNNs (RingGNN 0.353, 3WLGNN 0.303). Among fragment-biased models, FragNet nearly halves the error of HIMP (0.0775 vs. 0.151) and outperforms GSN (0.115) and Autobahn (0.106) by 32–49%. Against topological GNNs, FragNet matches CIN++ on ZINC-10k (0.0775 vs. 0.077) and beats CIN-Small (0.094), while on Peptides-struct it achieves the best GNN result (0.2462 vs. CIN++ 0.2523). Among transformers, FragNet beats Graphormer (0.122), GT (0.226), SAN (0.139), and GPS (0.070 on ZINC-10k) but loses to GRIT (0.059) on ZINC-10k and Peptides-func (0.6988 vs. 0.6678 AP); however, FragNet substantially outperforms GRIT on out-of-distribution generalization (0.34 vs. 0.61 MAE with 7-rings held out) and zero-shot QM9 transfer (1.12 vs. 1.22 MAE), demonstrating that its inductive bias provides superior robustness despite slightly lower in-distribution accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the WL-test hierarchy (Morris et al., 2021) to fragment-biased GNNs by introducing the Fragment-WL test, which formalizes how the *manner* of incorporating substructure information (as node features, learned fragment representations, or higher-level graph connections) determines expressiveness. Architecturally, FragNet builds directly on HIMP's (Fey et al., 2020) higher-level junction tree message passing, but replaces HIMP's fixed ring-and-edge vocabulary with a novel rings-paths fragmentation that achieves an infinite vocabulary through ordinal encoding. The paper also unifies the expressiveness analysis of topological GNNs (CIN, CIN++, MPSN) under the same Fragment-WL framework, showing they are all bounded by HLG-WL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Fragment-WL hierarchy (2-WL < NF-WL < FR-WL < HLG-WL) provides the first unified theoretical lens for comparing fragment-biased and topological GNNs, resolving a gap where prior work lacked expressivity analysis for these practically important models. The practical finding that a linear-complexity GNN with inductive fragment biases can match or exceed quadratic-complexity transformers on molecular benchmarks—and generalize 1.8× better on out-of-distribution fragments—suggests that structured inductive biases may be more valuable than raw attention capacity for molecular tasks. Limitations include the fragmentation being tailored to molecules (not applicable to dense general graphs) and GRIT still performing slightly better on molecules with frequent fragments, leaving open whether the ordinal encoding can be further refined for that regime.
