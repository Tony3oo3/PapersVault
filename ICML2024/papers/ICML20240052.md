## Problem

Despite the widespread use of hyperbolic neural networks and the established connection between Euclidean ADMM and continuous dynamical systems (França et al., 2018), no theoretical work has linked hyperbolic optimizers to dynamical systems. This paper addresses that gap by deriving the continuous-limit ODE for a hyperbolic ADMM variant and analyzing the stability of its critical points using Lyapunov theory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240052/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely within the Poincaré ball model, taking two hyperbolically convex functions f(x) and g(z), a full-column-rank constraint matrix A, an initial point x₀ in the unit ball, and a penalty parameter ρ. The constraint is the non-linear hyperbolic relation z = exp₀(A(log₀ x)) rather than the Euclidean z = Ax, and all vector operations use Möbius addition/subtraction. The output is a non-linear first-order ODE (the continuous limit with time scale t = k/ρ) whose critical points correspond to the optimizer's fixed points, along with a Lyapunov-based stability characterization.

## Method

The paper redefines ADMM in the Poincaré ball by replacing Euclidean operations with Möbius addition, Möbius scalar multiplication, and the exponential/logarithmic maps at the origin, yielding the constraint z = exp₀(A(log₀ x)). Following the continuous-limit methodology of França et al. (2018), it takes δ → 0 with ρ = 1/δ and applies the Mean Value Theorem component-wise to derive a non-linear ODE of the form (AᵀA)⁻¹∇V(X) + (AᵀA)⁻¹Ω₁ + (AᵀA)⁻¹Ω₂X + ((AᵀA)⁻¹AΩ₃ + Ω₄X)X′ = 0, where the Ωᵢ terms arise from differentiating the hyperbolic exp/log composition and depend implicitly on X. For stability analysis, the ODE is linearized around a critical point X* by substituting X = X* + δX and neglecting higher-order terms, producing a linear system ÃX + B̃X′ = 0. Asymptotic stability of X* is then established via a Lyapunov function L(X) = XᵀPX when A is positive definite and ∇V(X) > 0 near X*.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no quantitative experimental results or benchmark evaluations. The main contributions are two theorems: Theorem 3.6 establishes that the continuous limit of the Hyperbolic ADMM flow (with time scale t = k/ρ) corresponds to a non-linear ODE of the form (AᵀA)⁻¹∇V(X) + (AᵀA)⁻¹Ω₁ + (AᵀA)⁻¹Ω₂X + ((AᵀA)⁻¹AΩ₃ + Ω₄X)X′ = 0, and Theorem 4.4 proves asymptotic stability of critical points under the conditions that A is positive definite, ∇V(X) > 0 near X*, and X(t) > 0 for all t > t₀. The authors explicitly defer numerical validation and empirical comparisons to future work.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include any empirical comparisons, baselines, or experimental benchmarks. It is entirely theoretical in nature, presenting a novel formulation of ADMM in the Poincaré ball model and proving its continuous-limit ODE and Lyapunov stability properties. The authors note in the conclusions that a numerical comparison with standard ADMM in gyrovector space is anticipated as future work, suggesting their hyperbolic version may offer potential superiority, but no such comparison is provided. The closest 'comparison' is conceptual: the paper extends the Euclidean ADMM continuous-limit result of França et al. (2018) to the hyperbolic setting, showing that the non-linear terms Ω₁ through Ω₄ (arising from the exponential and logarithmic maps) introduce additional complexity absent in the Euclidean case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous-limit analysis of ADMM from França et al. (2018) — which derived a linear ODE for the Euclidean case — to the hyperbolic setting within the Poincaré ball model, where the constraint z = Ax is replaced by z = exp₀(A(log₀ x)). The hyperbolic ADMM formulation itself builds on the classical ADMM framework of Boyd et al. (2011), adapted using Möbius addition and gyrovector-space operations. The authors explicitly note that no prior theoretical work has connected hyperbolic optimizers to dynamical systems, positioning this as the first such analysis. The stability analysis draws on classical Lyapunov theory (Hirsch et al., 2012) applied to the resulting non-linear ODE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretical foundation for understanding how hyperbolic ADMM evolves over time, which could inform practical implementation choices (e.g., step-size selection, convergence diagnostics) for hyperbolic neural networks. However, the stability guarantee (Theorem 4.4) requires A to be square and positive definite, and the ODE contains implicit Ω terms that depend on X, making the analysis difficult to apply concretely. No numerical experiments are provided, and the authors themselves flag that the non-linear ODE necessitates future numerical analysis. Open questions include extending the framework to arbitrary base points (beyond the origin), other hyperbolic models, and the impact of isometries on convergence.
