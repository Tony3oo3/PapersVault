## Problem

Existing methods for causal discovery with latent variables are predominantly constraint-based (e.g., FCI, tetrad tests, rank deficiency tests), which suffer from testing-order dependency, error propagation, and sensitivity to significance level selection. While score-based methods like GES are well-established for the causally sufficient case, formulating a score-based approach that identifies causal structures *among* latent variables (not just their effects on observed variables) with identifiability guarantees has been an open challenge due to the difficulty of characterizing degrees of freedom and constructing valid scoring functions in the presence of unmeasured confounders.

## Input / Output

The method consumes T i.i.d. samples of m measured variables and operates on the empirical covariance matrix S; latent variables are entirely unmeasured, so all inference is performed on the marginal distribution over observed variables. The output is a causal DAG over both observed and latent variables (edges among latent variables and from latent to observed), identified up to Markov equivalence under the 1-factor assumption or up to equivalence under specific graph operators (skeleton, minimal-graph, intra-atomic) under the hierarchical assumption. The user must specify an upper bound on the number of latent variables (⌊m/3⌋ for the 1-factor case, a hyperparameter for the hierarchical case).

## Method

SALAD introduces a scoring function score_dim(G, D) that assigns the model's degrees of freedom dim(G) if G can generate the empirical covariance (otherwise ∞), and proves it is score-equivalent and yields algebraic equivalence to the true structure in the large sample limit under generalized faithfulness. For finite samples, a BIC variant is used, where the likelihood is computed by minimizing the negative log-likelihood over the model parameters (B, C, Ω_X) subject to the structural support constraints. The key technical contribution is characterizing degrees of freedom under two structural assumptions: for the 1-factor model (each observed variable has one latent parent, each latent has ≥3 observed children), dim(G) = |G| + m; for latent hierarchical structures, dim(G) can be strictly less than |G| + m due to redundant edges among latent variables sharing the same parents and children, computed via an iterative reduction algorithm. For search, the paper provides both an exact enumeration procedure (iterating over all non-Markov-equivalent structures satisfying the assumption) and a continuous optimization formulation using Gumbel-Softmax relaxation of binary edge indicators with augmented Lagrangian for the structural constraints, unifying structure search and likelihood computation into a single differentiable problem.

## Results

On the 1-factor model, SALAD achieves F1 scores of 0.99–1.00 across sample sizes (100–10,000), while SALAD-CS reaches 0.97–0.99. On the hierarchical structure, SALAD achieves F1 scores of 0.92–0.98, substantially outperforming the best constraint-based baseline HUANG (0.57–0.88) and GIN (0.47–0.48). The advantage is most pronounced at small sample sizes: with 100 samples, SALAD scores 0.99 (1-factor) and 0.92 (hierarchical) versus 0.90 (FOFC, with 4 of 12 runs invalid) and 0.57 (HUANG) respectively. F1 scores approach 1.0 for both structural assumptions at large sample sizes, supporting the theoretical consistency guarantees.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SALAD (exact) outperforms all three constraint-based baselines on both structural assumptions across all sample sizes. Against HUANG (Huang et al., 2022), SALAD leads by 0.42–0.49 F1 on the 1-factor model at 100 samples and by 0.35–0.51 F1 on the hierarchical structure, with the gap narrowing but remaining at 0.07–0.10 at 10,000 samples. Against FOFC (Kummerfeld & Ramsey, 2016), SALAD achieves comparable F1 on the 1-factor model at large sample sizes (0.99 vs. 0.99), but FOFC suffers from reliability issues (only 8–12 of 12 runs valid at 100 samples, and 0 valid runs on the hierarchical structure). Against GIN (Xie et al., 2020), SALAD dominates by a wide margin (0.55–0.63 F1 on 1-factor, 0.44–0.50 F1 on hierarchical), as GIN's performance remains nearly flat across sample sizes. The continuous variant SALAD-CS matches SALAD within 0.01–0.02 F1 on the 1-factor model, confirming that the continuous relaxation preserves accuracy while improving computational efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the score-based causal discovery framework of GES (Chickering, 2002) from the causally sufficient setting to linear latent variable causal models, where prior work has been dominated by constraint-based methods (FCI, Silva et al.'s tetrad-based approach, Huang et al.'s rank-constraint method). The key conceptual departure is formulating a scoring function that achieves both score equivalence and algebraic equivalence for structures with causally-related latent variables, providing a unified view that subsumes several existing constraint-based procedures. The continuous search variant draws on Gumbel-Softmax relaxation and augmented Lagrangian methods from the differentiable causal discovery literature (Zheng et al., 2018; Ng et al., 2022), while the identifiability results build directly on the structural assumptions and equivalence notions from Silva et al. (2003; 2006) and Huang et al. (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first score-based method with identifiability guarantees for causal structures containing causally-related latent variables, addressing practical failure modes of constraint-based approaches (testing-order dependency, error propagation) that become severe at scale. The characterization of degrees of freedom for latent hierarchical structures—showing they are generally less than |G|+m due to edge redundancy within atomic covers—is a non-trivial algebraic result that enables correct model selection. Open questions include: (1) the exact search is computationally expensive and a greedy/scalable variant (analogous to GES) remains to be developed; (2) the theoretical justification for using BIC in the stratified exponential family setting is acknowledged as lacking, though empirically effective; (3) the tightness of the degrees-of-freedom upper bound in Algorithm 2 is conjectured but unproven.
