## Problem

Previous analyses of the edge-degree constrained subgraph (EDCS) sparsifier showed an approximation ratio of 2/3 − O(1/β) for maximum matching, suggesting that increasing β monotonically improves the ratio toward 2/3. However, these proofs are loose for small β, leaving the exact approximation ratio unknown for any specific parameter pair. This paper asks: what is the precise approximation ratio of a (β, β⁻)-EDCS for every valid choice of parameters, and does the monotone-improvement picture actually hold?

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a bipartite graph G on n vertices together with integer parameters β > β⁻ ≥ 1 that define the EDCS density constraint. The output is a sparse subgraph H ⊆ G with at most O(nβ) edges (the EDCS) and, crucially, the exact worst-case approximation ratio μ(G)/μ(H) for that parameter pair. The LP is instantiated and solved numerically via Gurobi for all 4,950 feasible (β, β⁻) pairs with 1 ≤ β⁻ < β ≤ 100, producing a complete lookup table of tight ratios.

## Method

The paper formulates a factor-revealing linear program whose variables correspond to vertex profiles (position relative to a Hall's witness, degree in H, matched status in the EDCS matching M and the optimal matching M*) and edge profiles (endpoint profiles, membership in H, M, or M*). Ten validity conditions—enforcing EDCS degree bounds, Hall's witness structure, and matching consistency—prune infeasible profiles before the LP is constructed. The LP constraints tie vertex counts to incident edge counts via degree equalities and normalize so that M-edges sum to 1, making the objective (sum of M*-edge variables) equal to the approximation ratio. The central theorem proves tightness in both directions: every concrete (G, H) instance maps to a feasible LP solution with objective μ(G)/μ(H), and every rational optimal LP solution maps back to a valid bipartite instance achieving that ratio. Solving the LP reveals that the best ratio is 0.6774 at (β, β⁻) = (6, 5), beating the long-believed 2/3 barrier, and that for even β the (β, β−1)-EDCS always exceeds 2/3 while converging to it from above as β grows.

## Results

The factor-revealing LP yields the exact approximation ratio of (β, β⁻)-EDCS for all parameter pairs. The headline result is that a (6,5)-EDCS achieves an approximation ratio of 0.6774, surpassing the previously believed 2/3 ≈ 0.6667 limit. For even β ≥ 6, the (β, β−1)-EDCS always exceeds 2/3 (e.g., 0.6756 for β=8, 0.6721 for β=10), converging to 2/3 from above as β grows. For odd β ≥ 7, the (β, β−1)-EDCS achieves exactly 2/3, while all other (β, β⁻) combinations yield ratios strictly below 2/3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's tight factor-revealing LP directly improves upon two prior analyses. First, the previous analytical bound (Bernstein & Stein 2015; Assadi & Bernstein 2019; Behnezhad 2021) gave only 2/3 − O(1/β), suggesting that increasing β monotonically improves the ratio toward 2/3 from below. The new LP reveals this is incorrect: for even β, the (β, β−1)-EDCS actually exceeds 2/3, with the peak at β=6 giving 0.6774. Second, the factor-revealing LP of Behnezhad & Khanna (2022) for hierarchical EDCS only guaranteed a 0.6 approximation for a (6,5)-EDCS, whereas the tight LP in this paper recovers the correct value of 0.6774 — a gap of 0.077. The Bernstein & Stein (2015) lower-bound example (Figure 1) showing 2/3 is tight for odd β is confirmed exactly by the LP (e.g., 0.6666 for β=7, 9, 11), but does not apply to even β, explaining the >2/3 results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the EDCS sparsifier introduced by Bernstein & Stein (2015) and the factor-revealing LP technique used by Behnezhad & Khanna (2022) for hierarchical EDCS. However, it departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) previous analyses (Bernstein & Stein 2015, Assadi & Bernstein 2019, Behnezhad 2021) used loose analytical arguments that suggested increasing β monotonically improves the approximation ratio toward 2/3, whereas this paper's tight LP reveals the opposite for even β; and (2) the LP of Behnezhad & Khanna (2022) was not tight (giving only 0.6 for a (6,5)-EDCS), while the LP here is proven exact via a bidirectional reduction between feasible solutions and graph instances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that a (6,5)-EDCS achieves a 0.6774 approximation—beating the long-believed 2/3 barrier—is significant because beating 2/3 for maximum matching is considered a difficult open problem in streaming, sublinear-time, and dynamic settings. Since EDCS can be constructed via a simple greedy algorithm in polynomial time, this result provides a concrete, practical path to better-than-2/3 matching in those settings. Open questions include whether the even-β advantage can be exploited in the various large-scale models where EDCS is deployed, and whether the tight LP framework can be extended to weighted or non-bipartite variants.
