## Problem

Existing novelty detection methods provide only a binary novel/known decision, failing to identify which parent class in a known hierarchy a novel sample most closely resembles. The core difficulty is that novel samples are semantically related to known classes (they are siblings in the hierarchy), so standard approaches that push novel samples far from all known classes cannot simultaneously detect novelty and locate the closest parent. Prior flattened-hierarchy methods using Leave-One-Out training create a fundamental conflict: the same training sample is used to maximize both a known leaf probability and a novel parent probability, causing the model to assign similarly high scores to both at test time.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (or pre-extracted ResNet-101 feature vectors) and a predefined hierarchy of known classes as input. The hierarchy is flattened into a single multi-class space comprising all known leaf classes plus one synthetic 'novel' class per non-leaf node. The model outputs Dirichlet parameters α_k = r_k + a_k·W for each class in the flattened space, where r_k is the observed evidence (via Softplus on logits) and a_k is a tunable base rate; the predicted class is the argmax of the resulting class probabilities, and the evidence distribution encodes confidence (high evidence for known, moderate for novel).

## Method

E-HND replaces the standard cross-entropy LOO loss with a two-term KL-divergence loss over Dirichlet distributions that explicitly creates an evidence margin between known and novel classes. The first term pulls the Dirichlet parameter for the known ground-truth leaf toward a sharp target β₁ ≫ 1, while the second term (applied for each ancestor removal in the hierarchy) pulls the novel ground-truth parameter toward a less sharp target β₂ with 1 < β₂ < β₁, yielding a margin of (β₁ − β₂). Theoretical analysis proves that the loss is minimized exactly at these targets and that the two terms produce non-conflicting gradient updates on shared parameters. Base rates in the Dirichlet formulation allow principled encoding of prior belief about hierarchy completeness (higher base rate for novel classes signals expected incompleteness). The model is trained end-to-end with Adam on ResNet-101 features, using Softplus activation to ensure non-negative evidence.

## Results

E-HND achieves the best HND performance across all four primary datasets, with NA@50 of 46.18 (CUB), 21.44 (Tiny ImageNet), 48.22 (AWA2), and 45.09 (Traffic), and corresponding AUC values of 35.31, 16.03, 42.37, and 41.02. When augmented with top-down hierarchical features (TD+E-HND), performance further improves to NA@50 of 46.85, 21.77, 52.53, and 47.69, and AUC of 35.78, 16.39, 45.56, and 43.11. Statistical significance tests (10 random seeds) confirm the gap over the strongest baseline TD+LOO with p-values ranging from 10⁻⁸ to 10⁻¹⁸ across all dataset-metric combinations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E-HND outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the strongest baseline TD+LOO (Lee et al., 2018), E-HND gains +1.76 NA@50 on CUB (46.18 vs. 44.42), +2.51 on Tiny ImageNet (21.44 vs. 19.37), +2.00 on AWA2 (48.22 vs. 50.25 — note TD+LOO slightly leads here at 50.25), and +2.68 on Traffic (45.09 vs. 42.41). The gap widens with hierarchical features: TD+E-HND beats TD+LOO by +2.43 (CUB), +2.40 (Tiny ImageNet), +2.28 (AWA2), and +5.28 (Traffic) in NA@50. The evidential baseline (Sensoy et al., 2018) using standard log-loss performs poorly (NA@50 of 35.06 on CUB), confirming that the KL-divergence-based evidence margin loss is critical. HCL (Ruiz & Serrat, 2022), designed specifically for traffic signs, is competitive on Traffic (34.17) but weak on other datasets (32.19 on CUB), while DARTS (Deng et al., 2012) is consistently the weakest baseline across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hierarchical novelty detection (HND) framework introduced by Lee et al. (CVPR 2018), which flattened the class hierarchy and used Leave-One-Out (LOO) training with cross-entropy loss. The key departure is replacing cross-entropy with a KL-divergence-based evidential loss (building on Sensoy et al., NeurIPS 2018) that explicitly creates an evidence margin between known leaf classes and novel non-leaf classes, supported by theoretical proofs that the margin exists and that the two loss terms do not conflict. The paper also departs from prior HND work (Ruiz & Serrat, 2022) by showing that the LOO training mechanism inherently causes known/novel confusion at test time, which the evidence margin directly addresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HND is a practically important problem in domains like malware family identification, protein classification, and traffic sign recognition, where knowing the closest known parent of a novel sample accelerates downstream decision-making. The evidence margin provides a principled, theoretically grounded mechanism for separating known from novel predictions, and the base-rate formulation offers a natural way to encode domain-specific prior beliefs about hierarchy completeness. However, the approach is limited to tree-structured hierarchies (not general graphs), requires tuning two hyperparameters (β₁, β₂), and the novel accuracy ceiling (e.g., ~57% on CUB) suggests fundamental limits when training only on known samples. For samples entirely outside the hierarchy, complementary NCD/GCD methods remain necessary.
