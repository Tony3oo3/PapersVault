## Problem

Long-term time series forecasting (LTSF) models typically require millions of parameters to capture extended temporal dependencies, limiting deployment in resource-constrained settings. While recent lightweight models like DLinear and FITS have reduced parameter counts to the 10k level, no prior work has achieved sub-1k parameters while maintaining state-of-the-art accuracy. The paper exploits the inherent periodicity in many real-world time series to decouple periodic and trend components, enabling extreme parameter compression without sacrificing predictive performance.

## Input / Output

The model takes a univariate historical window of length L (default 720) from a multivariate time series along with a known periodicity w (e.g., 24 for hourly data with daily cycles), and produces forecasted values over a horizon H (96–720 steps). The input is first mean-subtracted for instance normalization, then passed through a 1D convolution for sliding aggregation before being reshaped into w subsequences of length ⌊L/w⌋. The output is reconstructed by transposing and reshaping w predicted subsequences of length ⌊H/w⌋ back into a full sequence of length H, then adding the mean back.

## Method

SparseTSF's core mechanism is the Cross-Period Sparse Forecasting technique: the input sequence is reshaped into an n×w matrix (n = ⌊L/w⌋) and transposed to w×n, effectively creating w subsequences that each contain one data point per period. A single shared linear layer of size n×m (m = ⌊H/w⌋) then predicts across all w subsequences simultaneously, and the result is transposed and reshaped back to length H. A 1D convolution with kernel size 2×⌊w/2⌋+1 is applied before downsampling to perform sliding aggregation, mitigating information loss and outlier sensitivity from the sparse sampling. Because the periodic component becomes constant within each downsampled subsequence, the model's task reduces to predicting only trend variations, requiring just ⌊L/w⌋×⌊H/w⌋ + 2×⌊w/2⌋+1 total parameters (e.g., 924 for L=H=720, w=24). The model is trained with MSE loss under a channel-independent strategy, using Adam with a relatively high learning rate (0.02) to compensate for the tiny parameter count.

## Results

SparseTSF achieves the best MSE on ETTh1 (0.359/0.397/0.404/0.417 at horizons 96/192/336/720) and ETTh2 (0.267/0.314/0.312/0.370), outperforming all baselines including PatchTST and FITS. On Electricity, it matches or slightly trails PatchTST (e.g., 0.138 vs. 0.129 at horizon 96), while on Traffic it is competitive but behind PatchTST at longer horizons (0.445 vs. 0.432 at 720). Crucially, SparseTSF achieves these results with only 0.92K trainable parameters—roughly 11× fewer than FITS (10.5K), 528× fewer than DLinear (485K), and over 6,800× fewer than PatchTST (6.31M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SparseTSF (0.92K params) decisively outperforms FITS (10.5K params) on ETTh1 and ETTh2 across all horizons (e.g., 0.359 vs. 0.375 on ETTh1-96; 0.312 vs. 0.340 on ETTh2-336), and also on Electricity at horizons 96–336. Against DLinear (485K params), SparseTSF wins on ETTh1 (0.359 vs. 0.374 at 96) and ETTh2 (0.267 vs. 0.338 at 96) but is competitive on Electricity and Traffic. The strongest baseline, PatchTST (6.31M params), edges out SparseTSF on Electricity (0.129 vs. 0.138 at 96) and Traffic (0.360 vs. 0.382 at 96; 0.432 vs. 0.445 at 720), but SparseTSF achieves these near-parity results with ~6,800× fewer parameters. Among heavier Transformer-based models (Informer, Autoformer, FEDformer, FiLM), SparseTSF consistently achieves the lowest MSE on ETTh1 and ETTh2, with gaps of 0.01–0.05 in MSE. The paper also demonstrates that SparseTSF's cross-domain generalization (e.g., Electricity→ETTh1: 0.373 at 96) surpasses all baselines including PatchTST (0.400) and FITS (0.380), highlighting the robustness of its periodic-feature extraction.

## Relation to Prior Work

SparseTSF extends the lightweight LTSF trajectory established by DLinear (single linear layer) and FITS (frequency-domain filtering at 10k parameters) to its logical extreme, reducing parameters below 1k. Its core mechanism—Cross-Period Sparse Forecasting via downsampling/upsampling to decouple periodicity from trend—differs conceptually from FITS's Fourier-based approach and from classical seasonal-trend decomposition (OneShotSTL), instead exploiting the structural sparsity of periodic data through parameter-shared linear layers across downsampled subsequences. The paper also adopts the channel-independent strategy and instance normalization now standard in the field, positioning itself as a methodological contribution within an already-accepted task rather than a new problem formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a model with under 1k trainable parameters matching or exceeding 6M–18M parameter Transformers opens deployment on edge devices and in resource-constrained settings, and the demonstrated cross-domain generalization (Electricity→ETTh1) suggests utility for small-sample and low-quality data scenarios. Key limitations remain for ultra-long periods (>100) where excessive sparsity degrades performance, and for data with multiple intertwined periods or no clear periodicity (e.g., financial data). Open questions include whether denser sparse strategies or multi-period decomposition modules can close these gaps while preserving the extreme parameter efficiency, and whether the approach generalizes to non-stationary or irregularly sampled series.
