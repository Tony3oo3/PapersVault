## Problem

Off-policy learning from observational data inherits bias from the historical behavioral policy that generated the data, yet no neural framework existed for learning fair decision rules under this setting. Simply omitting the sensitive attribute is insufficient because other covariates can act as proxies, and prior fairness work either targeted prediction tasks rather than policy optimization or required strong assumptions like known causal structure. The paper fills this gap by introducing fairness notions (action fairness, envy-free fairness, max-min fairness) tailored to off-policy learning and providing a practical neural framework with theoretical guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes observational data tuples (X, S, A, Y) where X ∈ ℝ^d are non-sensitive covariates, S is a discrete sensitive attribute (e.g., gender), A ∈ {0,1} is a binary action, and Y ∈ ℝ is a continuous outcome. It produces a stochastic policy π: X → [0,1] (action probability) parameterized by a neural network operating on a learned fair representation Φ(X) ∈ ℝ^k, along with empirical policy value estimates under each fairness criterion. The policy class is restricted to action-fair policies via the representation, and value fairness is enforced through modified optimization objectives (penalized or min-max policy value).

## Method

FairPol is a two-step neural framework. In Step 1, it learns a fair representation Φ(X) via adversarial training with three feed-forward networks: a base representation network, an outcome prediction network (ensuring Φ remains predictive of Y), and a sensitive attribute network (ensuring Φ is non-predictive of S). The adversarial objective is the counterfactual domain confusion loss, where the representation network minimizes a confusion loss that forces the sensitive attribute network toward uniform predictions, while the sensitive attribute network simultaneously tries to classify S correctly. In Step 2, a policy network π_θ(Φ(X)) is trained on the frozen fair representation using one of three objectives: the standard empirical policy value (no value fairness), an envy-free objective V̂(π) − λ·max|V̂_s(π) − V̂_{s'}(π)|, or a max-min objective min_s V̂_s(π), each computed via DM, IPW, or doubly robust policy scores. The paper further provides generalization bounds showing 1/√n convergence rates under boundedness assumptions, with the fairness-specific bounds depending on the minimum group probability ν.

## Results

On simulated credit-lending data (n=3,000), FairPol with action fairness (DR score) achieves an overall policy value of 1.01±0.03, closely matching the oracle action-fair upper bound of 1.03±0.02, while reducing the action fairness gap from 2.42 (unrestricted) to 0.23. Under max-min fairness, FairPol equalizes subgroup policy values at 0.73±0.03 for both S=0 and S=1, with a zero action fairness gap. On the Oregon Health Insurance Experiment (n=24,646), FairPol with action fairness reduces the Spearman correlation between gender and policy predictions from 0.129 (unrestricted) to 0.015, at a modest cost of 0.007 in overall policy value (0.130 vs. 0.137).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper explicitly notes that no external baselines exist for fair off-policy learning over general (non-linear) policies, so comparisons are made against oracle reference points. The optimal unrestricted policy (with ground-truth outcome functions) achieves the highest overall policy value (1.24 on simulated data, 0.137 on OHIE) but exhibits substantial discrimination (action fairness gap of 2.42 / Spearman 0.129). The oracle action-fair policy provides an upper bound under the action fairness constraint (1.03 on simulated data); FairPol with action fairness (DR) reaches 1.01, within 0.02 of this bound, while reducing the fairness gap to 0.23. On the real-world OHIE data, FairPol with action fairness reduces the gender–policy Spearman correlation from 0.129 to 0.015 (an ~88% reduction) while retaining 95% of the unrestricted policy value. For max-min fairness, FairPol perfectly equalizes subgroup values (0.73 for both groups on simulated data) and improves the worst-case subgroup value relative to the unrestricted policy, confirming the theoretical prediction that max-min fairness without action fairness is trivially satisfied by the unrestricted optimum (Lemma 4.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts fair representation learning (Zemel et al., 2013; Madras et al., 2018) and the counterfactual domain confusion loss (Melnychuk et al., 2022) to the off-policy learning setting, where the goal is to learn decision rules from observational data collected under a potentially discriminatory behavioral policy. It builds on the standard off-policy learning framework (Kallus, 2018; Athey & Wager, 2021) with its three policy score estimators (DM, IPW, DR), and imports envy-free and max-min fairness notions from the resource allocation literature (Arnsperger, 1994; Bertsimas et al., 2011) into this causal decision-making context. The key departure from prior work is the two-step architecture: fair representation learning enforces action fairness (independence from the sensitive attribute), while modified policy value objectives enforce value fairness, a combination not present in either the fairness-in-predictions or the causal-SCM-based fairness literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first neural framework for fair off-policy learning, filling a gap where no prior method could handle arbitrary non-linear policies under formal fairness constraints from observational data. The theoretical contribution (generalization bounds with 1/√n convergence) and the practical demonstration on the Oregon Health Insurance Experiment show the framework is both principled and applicable to real-world settings like healthcare and lending. Key limitations include the inherent performance-fairness tradeoff, restriction to binary actions, and the requirement that the sensitive attribute is observed. Open questions include extending to sequential decision-making, multiple actions, and settings where the sensitive attribute is unobserved or where fairness must be enforced without explicit group labels.
