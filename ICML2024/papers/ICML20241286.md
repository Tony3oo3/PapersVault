## Problem

Existing federated representation learning (FRL) theory and algorithms assume the over-parameterized regime, where the shared representation is large enough to perfectly encode every client's ground-truth model. In practice, resource-constrained edge devices (smartphones, IoT) cannot store, compute, or communicate such large models, making the under-parameterized regime—where the representation dimension k is smaller than the rank r of the true model matrix—both more realistic and fundamentally harder: locally optimal representations may lie outside the globally optimal subspace, so naive averaging of local models fails to recover the correct shared representation.

## Input / Output

Each of M clients holds N local samples (x, y) with x drawn from a sub-Gaussian distribution in R^d and y = φ_i^T x + ξ (linear case) or a class label (general case). The method outputs a shared representation matrix B ∈ R^{d×k} and per-client head vectors w_i ∈ R^k (or linear head matrices H_i for the non-linear extension), jointly minimizing the federated empirical risk plus regularization. In the under-parameterized setting (k < r), the product BW cannot equal the ground-truth matrix Φ, so the objective is to find the best rank-k approximation; for the general CNN variant on CIFAR-10/100, the output is a softmax class prediction from a two-convolution-layer network with linear personalized heads.

## Method

FLUTE introduces two key innovations over existing FRL algorithms. First, it augments the standard federated empirical risk with two regularization terms: −γ₁‖BW‖_F² (which preserves the top-k singular components of the product, counteracting local over-fitting) and γ₂(‖B^T B‖_F² + ‖WW^T‖_F²) (which promotes the most significant components while suppressing less significant ones, guiding the server to distill the correct subspace). Second, unlike FedRep or FedPer where local heads are optimized exclusively on the client side, FLUTE performs a server-side update step that simultaneously optimizes both the shared representation and all local heads using the aggregated gradients plus the regularization gradients, leveraging cross-client head information to recover the global optimum. The algorithm uses a data-independent small random initialization (entries ~ N(0, α²)) and is analyzed via a low-rank matrix approximation framework, yielding a per-client sample complexity of Õ(max{d,M}/(Mε²)) and exponential convergence when N is sufficiently large.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (100×5 partition), FLUTE* achieves 78.26% test accuracy, outperforming FedRep* (75.30%), FedPer (73.67%), and FedRep (73.81%). On CIFAR-100 (100×40 partition, the most severely under-parameterized setting), FLUTE* reaches 48.17%, beating FedRoD (48.12%), FedCP (43.64%), and FedPer (44.69%). The performance gap between FLUTE and baselines widens as the level of under-parameterization increases (larger N and m), confirming the method's advantage in the under-parameterized regime. FLUTE also outperforms FedRep in synthetic linear experiments, particularly when both use random initialization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLUTE and FLUTE* consistently outperform all six baselines across all 8 partition settings on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. Against FedRep (the closest FRL competitor with theoretical guarantees), FLUTE* gains approximately 3–4% accuracy on CIFAR-10 (e.g., 78.26% vs. 75.30% at 100×5) and 0.7–1.5% on CIFAR-100 (e.g., 79.56% vs. 78.89% at 100×5). FedRoD is the strongest baseline on CIFAR-100, reaching 48.12% at 100×40 (nearly matching FLUTE's 48.17%), but FLUTE pulls ahead more clearly at 100×20 (59.71% vs. 57.28%). FedPer and FedCP trail FLUTE by 2–5% on CIFAR-10 and 1–3% on CIFAR-100. Fed-LG and FedAvg are substantially weaker, with Fed-LG dropping to 22.70% on CIFAR-100 100×40 versus FLUTE's 48.17%, highlighting the difficulty of learning a common head with local representations in the under-parameterized regime. The paper also shows that when FedRep is given the same random initialization as FLUTE (FedRep RI), FLUTE converges to a lower error in significantly fewer iterations, isolating the benefit of FLUTE's regularization design from initialization advantages.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the federated representation learning (FRL) framework established by FedPer (Arivazhagan et al., 2019) and FedRep (Collins et al., 2021) from the over-parameterized regime to the under-parameterized regime, where the shared representation is too small to perfectly express all local models. The algorithmic design draws on asymmetric low-rank matrix factorization theory (Chen et al., 2023), adapting its regularization and convergence analysis to the distributed, data-driven FL setting where the ground-truth matrix is not directly observable. A key departure from prior FRL work is the server-side joint optimization of both the representation and all local heads, which is unnecessary in the over-parameterized setting but essential here to distill the correct subspace from misaligned local representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The under-parameterized regime is arguably the more realistic setting for FL on resource-constrained edge devices (IoT, smartphones) that cannot store, compute, or communicate over-parameterized models, making this the first FRL algorithm with provable guarantees in that practically important regime. The linear sample complexity speedup in the number of clients M is a meaningful theoretical result, though the exponential sample requirement for convergence and the increased communication cost (transmitting local heads to the server) are notable practical limitations. Open questions include whether the theoretical guarantees extend to non-linear representations (the general FLUTE extension is empirical only), how the method behaves when the singular value gap Δ is very small, and whether the communication overhead can be reduced without sacrificing the subspace-distillation property.
