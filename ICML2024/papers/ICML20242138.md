## Problem

While benign overfitting—where interpolating noisy training data still yields good generalization—has been established for clean training of neural networks, it remained an open question whether this phenomenon extends to adversarial training. Chen et al. (2023) showed benign overfitting for adversarially trained linear models but explicitly left the neural network case unresolved. This paper answers that question affirmatively, providing the first convergence and generalization guarantees for adversarial training of two-layer neural networks in a non-realizable (noisy) setting.

## Input / Output

The method operates on input vectors x ∈ R^d drawn from a noisy mixture distribution where each sample is a label-dependent signal (±μ) plus log-concave noise, with labels independently flipped at rate β. The adversarial perturbation budget α is constrained to be at most ||μ|| (the signal magnitude). The trained network produces a binary prediction (+1 or −1) and the analysis yields three key quantities: robust training loss (driven to zero), clean test error (bounded by β plus an exponentially decaying term), and robust test error (also bounded by β plus an exponentially decaying term when α/||μ|| is sufficiently small relative to √(n||μ||²/d)).

## Method

The paper analyzes gradient descent-based adversarial training (PGD-style inner maximization) of two-layer networks f(x;W) = (1/√m)Σ a_s φ(w_s^T x) with symmetric initialization (w_{s+m/2} = w_s, a = [1,...,1,−1,...,−1]) that makes f(·;W⁰) ≡ 0. The key technical insight is that under a small initialization (ω_init√(md) ≤ η), the first gradient step dominates the trajectory, pushing the network beyond the lazy/NTK regime, and the adversarial training trajectory remains close to standard training when α ≤ ||μ||. The proof establishes that the network correctly classifies all training points after the first step (zero robust training error), then uses concentration inequalities on λ-strongly log-concave distributions to bound the normalized expected conditional margin, yielding exponential decay in the test error. The analysis handles both smooth (e.g., smoothed Leaky ReLU) and non-smooth (ReLU, Leaky ReLU) activations, with the non-smooth case requiring an additional width condition m ≥ C log(n/δ) to ensure sufficient active neurons throughout training.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) shows that after T ≥ T̄ iterations of adversarial training, the robust training loss satisfies L̂_rob(W^T) ≤ ε and the robust training error is exactly zero (100% robust training accuracy). The clean test error is bounded by β + 2exp(−cλn‖μ‖⁴/(C²d)), and the robust test error is bounded by β + 2exp(−cλ‖μ‖²((‖μ‖/C)√(n/d) − α/‖μ‖)²) for α/‖μ‖ ≤ (1/C)√(n‖μ‖²/d). Empirically, on synthetic data with β=0.1, the robust training loss reaches zero while the robust test error converges to the label noise rate of 0.1; on MNIST (β=0), both robust training loss and robust test error converge to zero. A phase transition is observed at dimension d ≈ O(‖μ‖⁴) for clean accuracy and d ≈ O(‖μ‖²) for robust accuracy, consistent with the theoretical predictions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is primarily a theoretical contribution and does not include a direct empirical comparison against competing adversarial training methods. Instead, it resolves an open question posed by Chen et al. (2023), who provided benign overfitting guarantees only for adversarially trained linear models and explicitly left the extension to neural networks as future work. The paper also distinguishes itself from prior adversarial training analyses (Gao et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2020; Li & Telgarsky, 2023) that are restricted to the lazy/NTK regime, which has been shown to be incompatible with adversarial robustness (Wang et al., 2022b). Unlike Mianjy & Arora (2023), which provides guarantees beyond NTK but only for robustly realizable distributions, this paper handles non-realizable (noisy) data, which is essential for establishing benign overfitting. The empirical validation on MNIST confirms that the theoretical phase-transition predictions hold even when the data deviates from the assumed Gaussian mixture generative model, suggesting the results are indicative of broader phenomena.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Chen et al. (2023), who showed benign overfitting for adversarially trained *linear* models, to two-layer neural networks with both smooth and non-smooth activations. It builds on the clean-setting benign overfitting analysis of Frei et al. (2022) and Xu & Gu (2023) for two-layer networks, adapting their trajectory-based proof techniques to the adversarial training loop. Unlike prior adversarial training convergence results (Gao et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2020; Li & Telgarsky, 2023), which are confined to the lazy/NTK regime or assume robust realizability, this work operates beyond the NTK regime on non-separable, noisy data, and unlike Mianjy & Arora (2023), it does not require the data to be robustly realizable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves an open question by showing that the 'robust overfitting' phenomenon observed empirically (Rice et al., 2020; Sanyal et al., 2020) is not fundamental but rather a consequence of specific data distributions and large perturbation budgets; under a log-concave mixture model with moderate α, adversarial training achieves near-optimal robust generalization while interpolating noisy labels. Key limitations include the restrictive data distribution assumption (product log-concave marginals), the requirement that α ≤ ‖μ‖, and the high-dimensional regime d ≫ n. Open questions include extending to moderate attack strengths (α/‖μ‖ of constant order), more general data distributions, and the regime where d and n are of the same scale.
