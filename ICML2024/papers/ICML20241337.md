## Problem

Existing text-driven motion generation methods produce only body-only motions, ignoring the fine-grained hand gestures and facial expressions essential for vivid whole-body animation. Additionally, their text-motion alignment supervision operates at the frame level (via CLIP or LLM embeddings), failing to capture sequence-level semantics such as direction and temporal ordering, which causes generated motions to misalign with textual descriptions.

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form English text description (e.g., 'The man is playing the ukulele happily') as input and produces a whole-body 3D motion sequence of L frames. Each frame is represented in an extended H3D-Format comprising root angular/linear velocities, root height, local joint positions and velocities for both body and hand joints, plus a 50-dimensional FLAME facial expression vector. The body-hand motion is downsampled by a factor of 2 (body) and 4 (hand) into discrete tokens, while facial motion uses a 4× downsampling rate with 2-level RVQ codes.

## Method

HumanTOMATO introduces a Holistic Hierarchical VQ-VAE (H²VQ) that learns two structured codebooks—one for hand and one for body—where hand tokens are fused into the body encoding via concatenation and 1D convolution before body quantization, creating a kinematically informed hierarchical representation at very low bit rates. A Hierarchical-GPT (18-layer transformer) then autoregressively predicts body tokens followed by hand tokens at each timestep, conditioned on a text embedding from a pre-trained Text-Motion Alignment (TMA) model. The TMA is itself trained via contrastive learning (InfoNCE) on the TEMOS VAE architecture, providing motion-aware language priors that replace CLIP embeddings. During Hierarchical-GPT training, an additional contrastive alignment loss computed by TMA on the decoded motion provides explicit sequence-level text-motion supervision beyond the standard cross-entropy token prediction loss. Facial motion is generated separately using a 2-level RVQ quantizer and a parallel autoregressive predictor, justified by the partial independence of facial expressions from body-hand dynamics.

## Results

On the Motion-X whole-body motion generation benchmark, HumanTOMATO achieves the best text-motion alignment across all alignment metrics: TMA-R-Precision(256) Top3 of 0.638 (vs. 0.584 for the best baseline MotionDiffuse, a 9.2% relative improvement), R-Precision(32) Top3 of 0.703, and TMA-Matching Score of 0.809. In terms of generation quality, HumanTOMATO reports an FID of 1.174, slightly behind MotionDiffuse (1.129) but well ahead of MDM (3.800) and MLD (3.407). On diversity, MDM leads with MModality 2.530 and Diversity 11.400, while HumanTOMATO scores 1.732 and 10.812 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HumanTOMATO outperforms all five baselines on every text-motion alignment metric. Against the strongest alignment competitor MotionDiffuse, it gains 9.2% on TMA-R-Precision(256) Top3 (0.638 vs. 0.584) and 1.2% on R-Precision(32) Top3 (0.703 vs. 0.695), while also achieving the lowest TMA-Matching Score (0.809 vs. 0.829). On generation quality (FID), HumanTOMATO (1.174) is competitive with MotionDiffuse (1.129) and substantially better than T2M-GPT (1.366), MDM (3.800), MLD (3.407), and TEMOS (9.147). However, HumanTOMATO trails MDM on diversity metrics (MModality 1.732 vs. 2.530; Diversity 10.812 vs. 11.400), suggesting the alignment supervision slightly constrains generation variety. TEMOS, the earliest VAE-based baseline, is clearly outperformed on all metrics, with an FID more than 7× worse and alignment scores roughly 30% lower.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discrete-representation text-to-motion generation pipeline (VQ-VAE + GPT-style autoregressive code prediction, as in T2M-GPT) to the whole-body setting by proposing H²VQ, which replaces a single flat codebook with two kinematically-structured codebooks (body and hand) motivated by their different amplitude and frequency characteristics. It departs from the prevailing use of CLIP or LLM text embeddings as language priors by introducing TMA, a motion-specific contrastive pre-training objective (building on TEMOS's VAE architecture and CLIP's InfoNCE paradigm) that produces motion-aware text embeddings. For facial motion, it adapts the RVQ technique from neural audio codecs (SoundStream) to expression-parameter quantization, completing a unified whole-body generation framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of text-driven whole-body motion generation (simultaneous face, hand, and body), a task that is critical for games, animation, and robotics but was previously unaddressed due to data scarcity and modeling complexity. The TMA pre-training and sequence-level alignment supervision address a fundamental gap: CLIP's image-text alignment cannot capture motion dynamics (e.g., clockwise vs. counter-clockwise walking), and the new TMA-R-Precision(256) metric provides a more discriminative evaluation protocol. Open questions include whether the autoregressive generation scales to longer sequences without error accumulation, whether the approach generalizes to multi-person or scene-interactive motions, and how to reduce the computational cost of sequential token prediction for real-time applications.
