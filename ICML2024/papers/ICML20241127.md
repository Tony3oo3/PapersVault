## Problem

GP-based methods for multi-region neural data can discover latent variables with explicit frequency and direction information but incur O(T³) inference cost, while LDS-based methods achieve O(T) cost but lack the kernel expressiveness to model oscillatory structure and phase delays. No prior work had established a conversion from a multi-output GP with a complex-valued, frequency-encoding kernel to an LDS, because the complex-valued spectral density is non-symmetric and the multi-output case requires kernel separability that was not previously addressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-region neural recordings (spike trains or LFPs) from P brain regions, each with n^p neurons sampled over T time steps, organized as y ∈ R^{N×T}. It produces M×P low-dimensional latent variables split into m_a across-region dimensions (shared oscillatory dynamics with region-specific phase delays) and m_w within-region dimensions (local activity), plus Z discrete switching states that modulate frequencies and delays over time. The latent-to-observation mapping is a block-diagonal factor analysis (y = C·Re[Gf] + d + ε), and the complex-valued latent dynamics f ∈ C^{MPk×T} are projected to real observations via a selection matrix G.

## Method

MRM-GP converts a multi-output GP with a complex-valued multi-region kernel (separable into a temporal exp(−τ²/2σ² + iητ) and a spatial Σ a_p^r a_p'^r exp(iηφ_pp') component) into a discrete-time LDS. For within-region variables, spectral factorization of the non-symmetric complex spectral density S(ω) is performed by approximating 1/S(ω) as a polynomial in iω (rather than ω, as in the real-valued case), solving for companion-matrix coefficients via QR decomposition, then discretizing through the matrix exponential. For across-region variables, the kernel's space-time separability allows the LDS to be constructed as a Kronecker product: the transition matrix is I_P ⊗ A_m^w and the measurement matrix is K_spatial^m ⊗ Q_m^w, embedding phase delays directly into the spatial covariance. The full model is block-diagonal across latent dimensions, and switching states (Switching MRM-GP) assign distinct transition and measurement matrices to each discrete state to capture time-varying frequencies and delays. Inference uses variational Laplace EM with all steps linear in T, and the complex dynamics are implemented in the real domain by doubling the state dimension.

## Results

On LFP recordings (V1–VISam, 8 Hz drifting grating), MRM-GP (Z=1) achieves test log-likelihood comparable to CSM-GPFA and higher than DLAG, while recovering a consistent V1→VISam phase delay of ~8.2 ms at ~7.5 Hz. On V1–V2 spike trains (6 Hz drifting grating), MRM-GP with Z=2 switching states achieves the highest test LL, exceeding both CSM-GPFA and DLAG, and disentangles communications into 6 Hz (small delay) and 1 Hz (large delay) bands. Inference time scales linearly with the number of time points for MRM-GP, whereas both CSM-GPFA and DLAG exhibit cubic growth (Figure 4F).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DLAG (Gokcen et al., 2022), MRM-GP achieves higher test log-likelihood on both the LFP and spike-train datasets, attributed to the multi-region kernel's explicit frequency modeling versus DLAG's frequency-agnostic Squared Exponential kernel; DLAG's across-region latent variable shows three spectral peaks while MRM-GP isolates a single dominant frequency per dimension. Against CSM-GPFA (Hultman et al., 2018), MRM-GP with Z=1 matches its test LL (as expected, since MRM-GP is an LDS approximation of the same GP), but MRM-GP with Z=2 switching states surpasses CSM-GPFA on the spike-train data, revealing time-varying frequencies and phase delays that the non-switching GP cannot capture. The most practical advantage over both baselines is computational: MRM-GP's inference cost is O(T) in time points, while CSM-GPFA and DLAG require O(T³), making MRM-GP substantially faster for longer recordings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate methodological families for multi-region neural data analysis: GP-based models (Hultman et al. 2018; Gokcen et al. 2022) that capture frequency and phase-delay structure through multi-output kernels, and LDS-based models (Glaser et al. 2020) that offer linear-time inference but lack kernel-level expressiveness. The key technical contribution is extending the GP-to-LDS conversion via spectral factorization (Hartikainen & Särkkä 2010; Solin & Särkkä 2014) to the complex-valued multi-output setting, where the spectral density is non-symmetric due to an explicit frequency parameter—a gap not addressed by prior single-output or real-valued conversions. The separability of the complex-valued multi-region kernel (Ulrich et al. 2015) is leveraged to factor the conversion into a temporal LDS (via spectral factorization) and a spatial component (via Kronecker product), and the switching mechanism from Fox et al. 2008 is integrated to accommodate time-varying frequencies and delays.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MRM-GP provides a practically important efficiency gain: it achieves linear inference cost over time points while retaining the multi-region kernel's ability to disentangle oscillatory communications into distinct frequency bands and estimate directional phase delays—capabilities that the GP-based CSM-GPFA baseline also has but at cubic cost. This makes it feasible to apply frequency-resolved connectivity analysis to long or high-density recordings that were previously prohibitive. The authors identify two clear limitations: the separability requirement restricts kernel choices, and the model cannot capture phase resetting or inter-trial phase variability, which are known phenomena in cortical oscillations. Open questions include whether the spectral factorization approximation (particularly at k=2) degrades for very short length scales or multi-frequency mixtures within a single latent dimension, and whether the approach extends to non-stationary or non-Gaussian neural signals.
