## Problem

Graph rationalization methods in federated learning face a unique shortcut problem: each client's non-IID data contains different spurious correlations, so simply aggregating locally-trained rationalization models fails to produce a globally faithful rationale. Existing de-shortcut methods (DIR, DisC, RGDA) were designed for centralized settings and do not account for the client-specific shortcut heterogeneity that arises in FL.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242393/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives a local partition of graph-label pairs (G, Y) where G has node features S ∈ R^{|V|×d_g} and edges, distributed via LDA to create heterogeneous shortcut patterns across N clients. The method outputs a class label prediction and a binary rationale mask M ∈ R^{|V|} (sampled via Gumbel-softmax) identifying the sparse subset of nodes (controlled by sparsity α) that constitutes the explanation. At inference, only the rationale representation h_r is used for prediction, with no augmentations applied.

## Method

FedGR introduces two anti-shortcut augmenters that generate shortcut-conflicted samples at each client. The complement-aware augmenter first enforces sufficiency and independence principles via a contrastive loss (pulling rationale h_r toward full-graph h_g while pushing it away from complement h_e), then randomly permutes complements across the batch and concatenates them with rationales to break original statistical dependencies. The difference-aware augmenter uses an MLP-based node feature masking (Gumbel-softmax) to perturb node features while preserving graph structure, trained to minimize the global model's loss while maximizing the local model's loss—effectively generating samples that cross the local model's decision boundary (where shortcuts reside) while remaining consistent with the global model's predictions. The overall objective combines the standard rationalization loss, the contrastive constraint, the complement-augmented prediction loss, and the difference-augmented prediction loss, trained via alternating local updates and weighted global aggregation over T communication rounds.

## Results

FedGR achieves the best performance across all seven evaluation metrics in the main comparison (Table 1, GIN backbone). On Spurious-Motif, FedGR reaches 46.10% / 55.38% / 49.77% ACC at bias levels 0.5/0.7/0.9, outperforming the strongest baseline DARE (44.83% / 48.91% / 42.88%) by up to 6.89 points at bias=0.9. On OGB molecular datasets, FedGR attains AUC of 0.7387 (MolHIV), 0.6316 (MolToxCast), 0.6690 (MolBBBP), and 0.6017 (MolSIDER), surpassing the best baseline RGDA (0.7246 / 0.6235 / 0.6605 / 0.5906) by 0.2–1.4 points. Ablation confirms both augmenters contribute: removing the difference-aware augmenter drops MolHIV AUC from 0.7387 to 0.7214, and removing the complement-aware augmenter drops it to 0.7321.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedGR outperforms all nine baselines on every metric in the main FL comparison. Against the strongest de-shortcut baselines, FedGR leads DARE by 1.27–6.89 ACC points on Spurious-Motif and by 0.2–1.3 AUC points on OGB; it leads RGDA (the best OGB baseline) by 1.41 AUC on MolHIV and 1.11 on MolSIDER. Notably, several centralized de-shortcut methods (DIR, CAL, InterRAT) perform comparably to or worse than the plain GIN backbone in FL, indicating that their counterfactual strategies do not transfer well to heterogeneous client distributions. Data-augmentation-based methods (DisC, RGDA) show intermediate performance, confirming that counterfactual augmentation helps in FL but is insufficient without the complement-aware and difference-aware mechanisms. The structural generalizability experiments (Table 2) further show that adding FedGR's difference-aware augmenter to DisC, RGDA, GSAT, InterRAT, or DARE yields consistent 0.27–1.67% AUC improvements, demonstrating that the framework is complementary to existing rationalization methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends centralized graph rationalization methods (DIR, DisC, CAL, GSAT, DARE, InterRAT, RGDA) into the federated learning setting, where each client holds a non-IID subset of graph data with potentially different shortcut structures. The complement-aware augmenter builds on the counterfactual augmentation paradigm already used in DisC and RGDA but adds a contrastive learning constraint to formally enforce the sufficiency and independence principles of rationalization. The difference-aware augmenter is the key departure from prior work: it exploits the FL-specific property that local models are more biased toward client-specific shortcuts than the aggregated global model (Assumption 3.3), generating anti-shortcut samples that cross the local model's decision boundary while preserving the global model's predictions. This FL-specific mechanism has no direct analogue in the centralized de-shortcut literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of de-shortcut graph rationalization under federated learning, filling a gap where prior rationalization methods were only validated on centralized data. The finding that centralized de-shortcut methods (DIR, CAL, InterRAT) degrade to vanilla GIN performance in FL underscores that shortcut mitigation strategies do not transfer across training paradigms, motivating FL-specific designs. Limitations include reliance on Assumption 3.3 (global model less biased than local), which may not hold under extreme heterogeneity or with very few communication rounds, and the modest 1–2% AUC gains on OGB datasets suggest the approach's benefit is most pronounced in high-bias synthetic settings. Open questions include whether the difference-aware augmenter generalizes to other FL tasks beyond rationalization and how performance scales with increasing client heterogeneity and communication constraints.
