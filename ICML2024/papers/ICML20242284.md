## Problem

Most causal representation learning methods assume that all latent causal variables are fully captured in every observation. This paper tackles the partially observed setting where each measurement only reveals an instance-dependent subset of the underlying causal state (e.g., objects entering or leaving a camera frame), and where observations are unpaired—there is no simultaneous multi-view data of the same system state. Prior work on partial observability required fixed subsets per domain or view, but here the subset varies dynamically per sample, making identifiability fundamentally harder.

## Input / Output

The method takes a collection of unpaired high-dimensional observations (rendered images or numerical vectors) generated by a mixing function applied to masked causal variables, where unmeasured latents are replaced by a fixed mask value (0 or an out-of-distribution constant). For the piecewise linear theorem, group labels indicating which partial observability pattern produced each observation are required at training time but not at test time. The output is a learned representation that identifies the masked causal variables up to permutation and element-wise invertible linear transformation, evaluated via mean coefficient of determination (MCC) against ground truth.

## Method

The core insight is that a sparsity constraint on the encoder output (L0 norm of g(X) ≤ L0 norm of Z) breaks rotational indeterminacies in the latent space, enabling element-wise identifiability. Two theorems are established: one for injective linear mixing functions requiring no parametric assumptions on the causal model (allowing arbitrary dependencies among latents), and one for piecewise linear mixing functions that additionally requires Gaussian latent variables, mask independence from latents, and known group assignments. Practically, the method trains an autoencoder (encoder g_ψ and decoder f̂_θ) by minimizing reconstruction loss subject to an L1 sparsity constraint (approximating L0), solved via Cooper with extra-gradient Adam. For the piecewise linear case, per-group Gaussianity is enforced through skewness and kurtosis regularization terms pushing each latent's third moment to 0 and fourth moment to 3 within each observability group.

## Results

On the numerical linear mixing experiments (Table 1), the method achieves MCC of 0.997–0.998 for n=5–10 causal variables with low causal graph degree (k=0–1) and 50% measured variables, degrading to 0.714 at n=40. On the Multiple Balls image dataset (Table 2), MCC remains above 0.90 for up to 8 balls in both the missing-ball and masked-position settings. On PartialCausal3DIdent (Table 3), the method achieves a mean MCC of 0.832±0.016 across all 7 object classes, with per-class values ranging from 0.804 to 0.858, demonstrating applicability to highly nonlinear, high-dimensional rendering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against competing causal representation learning methods, as it introduces a new problem setting (unpaired partial observations with instance-dependent masks) for which no prior baselines exist. The only quantitative comparison is between the proposed method and an oracle in the piecewise linear case (Fig. 2), where the oracle additionally knows the mask value y^i for each observation and uses it to set a low variance for masked variables in the skewness/kurtosis regularization. The oracle consistently outperforms the proposed method, particularly as the number of causal variables n or the number of layers m increases, indicating that the empirical difficulty lies in enforcing the Gaussianity constraint (Eq. 6) without direct mask access. On the image benchmarks (Multiple Balls, PartialCausal3DIdent), no external baselines are reported; the results serve as validation that the sparsity-based identifiability principle generalizes beyond the linear and piecewise linear theoretical guarantees to highly nonlinear rendering functions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparsity principle for disentanglement from Lachapelle et al. (2023), which applied sparsity to task-connection matrices in multi-task learning, to a fundamentally different setting: sparsity on the latent representation itself arising from instance-dependent partial observability. It also generalizes the identifiability results of Kivva et al. (2022) from non-degenerate to degenerate multivariate normals, a necessary extension because masking introduces singular covariance matrices. Compared to prior partial-observability CRL work (Yao et al. 2023 for paired multi-view, Sturma et al. 2023 for unpaired multi-domain), this paper is the first to handle instance-dependent (rather than fixed) observability patterns with unpaired observations, and the linear result (Thm. 3.1) requires no group information and no parametric assumptions on the causal model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first identifiability guarantees for causal representation learning under instance-dependent partial observability, a setting motivated by real-world scenarios like occlusion and objects entering/leaving a camera frame. However, the empirical gap between the proposed method and an oracle that knows the mask values (especially for piecewise linear mixing) reveals that the Gaussianity constraint is the practical bottleneck, and current skewness/kurtosis regularizers are insufficient. Key open questions include: removing the group-information requirement for the piecewise linear case, extending identifiability beyond piecewise linear mixing functions, and finding better empirical proxies for the Gaussianity condition that would close the oracle gap.
