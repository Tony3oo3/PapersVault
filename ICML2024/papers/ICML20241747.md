## Problem

Learning ordered orthogonal singular or eigenfunctions of high-dimensional operators with neural networks is hindered by prior methods' reliance on explicit orthonormality constraints: SpIN requires per-iteration Cholesky decomposition and bi-level optimization for unbiased gradients, while NeuralEF needs function normalization and suffers from biased gradient estimates. Both are restricted to self-adjoint operators and scale poorly with the number of modes L. The paper seeks an unconstrained optimization framework that implicitly enforces orthogonality, handles non-self-adjoint operators, and admits simple unbiased gradient estimation.

## Input / Output

The method requires only the ability to evaluate quadratic forms ⟨f|Tg⟩ and inner products ⟨f|f'⟩, which can be estimated via importance sampling from domain samples or paired samples (x,y) from a joint distribution. For analytical operators (e.g., differential operators in PDEs), the operator is given in closed form; for data-dependent operators (e.g., canonical dependence kernels), only paired samples are needed. The output consists of parametric neural-network functions representing the top-L singular/eigenfunctions (scaled by their singular values rather than normalized), with singular values recoverable from the product of function norms, and structured co-embeddings where coordinates are ordered by singular value magnitude.

## Method

NeuralSVD builds on Schmidt's low-rank approximation theorem, formulating an unconstrained LoRA objective whose global minimizer recovers the top-L singular subspace without explicit orthonormality constraints. To break the rotational symmetry and recover ordered singular functions, the paper introduces two nesting techniques: sequential nesting, which minimizes L_LoRA(f_{1:ℓ}, g_{1:ℓ}) for each ℓ while treating earlier modes as fixed (suitable for disjoint parameterization), and joint nesting, which minimizes a weighted sum of all nested LoRA objectives simultaneously (suitable for shared parameterization). The key insight is that the nested global optimum characterizes singular functions scaled by their singular values, enabling singular value estimation from function norms. Gradients are computed via simple matrix-vector products with masking vectors, yielding unbiased estimators at O(B²L + BL²) complexity per minibatch, and the framework directly handles non-self-adjoint and even non-compact operators.

## Results

On the zero-shot sketch-based image retrieval (ZS-SBIR) task with the Sketchy Extended dataset, NeuralSVD_jnt achieves P@100 of 0.670±0.010 and mAP of 0.581±0.008 (Split 1), and P@100 of 0.724±0.008 and mAP of 0.641±0.008 (Split 2), outperforming both baselines that require generative models. For the 2D hydrogen atom eigenvalue problem, NeuralSVD_seq achieves a subspace distance of 2.06×10⁻⁴ over 16 eigenmodes, outperforming SpIN and NeuralEF by roughly an order of magnitude in all reported fidelity measures. In the 2D harmonic oscillator experiment (non-compact operator), NeuralSVD_seq again significantly outperforms NeuralEF, and additionally correctly identifies non-positive eigenvalues by converging to zero-norm functions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SpIN (Pfau et al., 2019) and NeuralEF (Deng et al., 2022b) on the 2D hydrogen atom, NeuralSVD_seq and NeuralSVD_jnt outperform both by approximately an order of magnitude in subspace distance and eigenvalue error; SpIN additionally suffers from higher memory and time costs due to Cholesky decomposition and Jacobian storage. On the 2D harmonic oscillator (a non-compact operator), NeuralSVD_seq significantly outperforms NeuralEF, and uniquely converges to zero functions for non-positive eigenvalues, enabling correct spectral inference. On ZS-SBIR, NeuralSVD_jnt (P@100=0.670, mAP=0.581 on Split 1) beats both LCALE (P@100=0.583, mAP=0.476, which uses word embeddings) and IIAE (P@100=0.659, mAP=0.573, which requires a generative model), without needing either external knowledge or a generative model. Against the matrix-free LOBPCG solver (Knyazev, 2001) on the 2D hydrogen atom, NeuralSVD achieves comparable eigenvalue accuracy at substantially lower computational cost, with LOBPCG showing degraded accuracy for higher modes as grid resolution increases. Notably, NeuralSVD handles non-self-adjoint operators (e.g., the asymmetric CDK in cross-domain retrieval) where SpIN and NeuralEF are inapplicable by design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the constrained-optimization lineage of SpIN (Pfau et al., 2019) and NeuralEF (Deng et al., 2022b), which enforce orthonormality via Cholesky decomposition or function normalization and suffer from biased gradients or memory overhead. Instead, it grounds the optimization in Schmidt's (1907) low-rank approximation theorem, yielding an unconstrained objective whose global minimizer characterizes the top-L singular subspace, and introduces 'nesting' (sequential or joint) to break the permutation symmetry and recover ordered singular functions. A key conceptual extension is that the framework naturally handles non-self-adjoint operators (full SVD), whereas SpIN and NeuralEF are restricted to self-adjoint EVD. The joint nesting idea also connects to Xu & Zheng (2024) on structured feature decomposition via canonical dependence kernels, generalized here to arbitrary operators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unconstrained NestedLoRA formulation removes the need for Cholesky decomposition, explicit normalization, and bi-level optimization, making it straightforward to implement with off-the-shelf gradient-based optimizers and scalable to larger L. The ability to handle non-self-adjoint operators and non-compact operators (e.g., harmonic oscillator) broadens the applicable problem class beyond prior parametric eigenfunction methods. Open questions remain: convergence guarantees for the nested objective with finite-capacity networks, understanding when a given architecture can approximate the target eigenfunctions, and scalability to high-dimensional quantum chemistry or large-scale PDE problems where the current experiments use simple MLPs on 2D domains.
