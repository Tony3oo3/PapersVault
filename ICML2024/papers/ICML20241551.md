## Problem

When constraints are applied only at inference time (ITV) to modify a pre-trained model's outputs, it is unclear whether this ad-hoc modification degrades the model's generalization performance. Prior work (Nishino et al., 2022) provided generalization bounds only for PAC-learnable hypothesis classes, leaving the general case—especially non-PAC-learnable settings—uncharacterized. This paper asks: under what conditions does imposing constraints at inference time preserve the relative generalization error (the gap to the best possible constrained model)?

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a trained hypothesis h: X → R (binary) or h: X → R^K (multi-class) and a requirement function c: X × Y → {0,1} that specifies which output labels are feasible for each input. The constrained hypothesis h_c is constructed by setting scores to a large negative constant −M for infeasible labels. The output is a theoretical characterization: whether R(h_c) − R(f_c*) ≤ R(h) − R(g*) holds universally, where g* and f_c* are the unconstrained and constrained optimal hypotheses respectively. The analysis is distribution- and constraint-agnostic, requiring only that at least one feasible label exists per input.

## Method

The paper introduces the notion of relative error preservation and derives a necessary and sufficient condition (Theorem 4.3) for a multi-class loss to preserve it: (i) modifying the unconstrained optimum g* to satisfy constraints must yield the constrained optimum f_c*, and (ii) the relative error inequality must hold against this modified optimum. For binary classification, preservation always holds because constraints simply fix the output on a subset of inputs. For multi-class, the paper proves that softmax cross-entropy loss preserves relative error by showing the error difference reduces to a KL divergence (hence non-negative), that one-versus-rest loss preserves it via per-class linear decomposition, and that pairwise comparison loss preserves it with zero-one, sigmoid, or ramp surrogates because the optimal score ordering matches the probability ordering. Conversely, the multi-class margin loss fails condition (i) because its minimizer does not encode the full probability distribution among non-top labels, and a worst-case construction shows the relative error can degrade to at least 1/2.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical finding is that relative error (the gap between a hypothesis's generalization error and the optimal one) is always preserved under inference-time constraints for binary classification (Theorem 4.2). For multi-class classification, the paper identifies a necessary and sufficient condition (Theorem 4.3) and shows that softmax cross-entropy, one-versus-rest, and pairwise-comparison losses (with zero-one, sigmoid, or ramp binary surrogates) all preserve relative error. In contrast, the multi-class margin loss does not preserve relative error, and a worst-case analysis (Theorem 5.3) shows the relative error can degrade to at least 1/2 under adversarial constraint choices.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Rather than comparing empirical methods, the paper compares loss functions on the property of relative-error preservation under inference-time constraints. Softmax cross-entropy (CE) loss preserves relative error for all hypotheses, distributions, and constraints (Proposition 4.4), with the proof reducing the error difference to a KL divergence. One-versus-rest (OVR) loss combined with any binary surrogate also preserves relative error (Proposition 4.5), and pairwise-comparison (PC) loss does so when paired with zero-one, sigmoid, or ramp losses (Proposition 4.6). The multi-class margin loss, however, fails to preserve relative error for K>2 with zero-one, ramp, or sigmoid surrogates (Proposition 5.1), and PC loss with hinge or logistic surrogates also fails (Proposition 5.2). The worst-case bound of 1/2 relative error degradation for margin loss (Theorem 5.3) quantifies the practical risk of using the wrong loss function in the ITV setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inference-time verification (ITV) framework introduced by Nishino et al. (2022), which provided generalization bounds only for PAC-learnable hypothesis classes. While the prior work showed that constrained models can be bounded relative to other hypotheses in the class, it left open how the generalization error changes for general (non-PAC-learnable) cases and did not identify which loss functions are safe under ITV. The paper also positions itself against domain adaptation theory, arguing that the ITV setting is more restricted (no access to unlabeled data for domain distance estimation) and thus cannot directly inherit those bounds. The neuro-symbolic AI and constrained prediction literature (Ahmed et al., 2022; Giunchiglia et al., 2023) provides the practical motivation but no prior theoretical characterization of loss-function-dependent error preservation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that practitioners using pre-trained models with inference-time constraints should prefer softmax cross-entropy or one-versus-rest loss functions, as these provably preserve relative error, while margin-based losses can degrade performance by up to 1/2 in the worst case. A key open question the paper identifies is whether one can estimate the best hypothesis in the ITV setting without access to constraints during training, since a model with small unconstrained relative error might not be the best constrained model. The limitation that ITV may overlook hypotheses that perform much better under constraints (even if they were suboptimal without them) suggests a fundamental trade-off between the flexibility of ITV and the performance guarantees of learning-time verification.
