## Problem

Finite-horizon Wasserstein-2 distributionally robust regret-optimal (DR-RO) control requires solving semi-definite programs whose dimension scales with the time horizon, making them intractable for long or infinite horizons. Moreover, prior DR control methods either assume i.i.d. disturbances or collapse to overly conservative H∞/RO controllers at the extremes. This paper addresses the infinite-horizon setting where the optimal controller is provably non-rational (lacking any finite-order state-space realization), yet must be computable and implementable in real time.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the stabilizable/controllable/observable state-space tuple (A, Bu, Bw, C), a nominal zero-mean weakly stationary disturbance process characterized by its auto-covariance operator M_circ, and a Wasserstein-2 radius r (with r_T ∝ r√T scaling). The primary output is a causal LTI state-space controller (F̃, G̃, H̃, J̃) obtained via rational approximation of the non-rational optimal controller in the H∞ norm, along with the worst-case disturbance power spectrum N*(z) and the finite-dimensional parameter Γ* that fully characterizes the optimal non-rational solution at any frequency.

## Method

The DR-RO problem is reformulated as a concave-convex saddle-point problem over positive-definite Toeplitz operators using Fenchel duality and the Bures-Wasserstein distance, with the optimal causal controller derived via the Wiener-Hopf technique. A key structural result (Theorem 4.2) shows that although the optimal controller K*(z) is non-rational (involving a square root of a rational transfer function), it admits a finite-dimensional parametric form N*(z) = ¼(I + √(I + 4γ⁻¹Γ*(z⁻¹I−Ā)⁻*C̄*C̄(z⁻¹I−Ā)⁻¹Γ*))² parameterized by a single matrix Γ* and scalar γ*. An efficient Frank-Wolfe algorithm in the frequency domain (with DFT-based spectral factorization and bisection for γ) computes the saddle point with provable O(1/k) convergence. Finally, a novel convex feasibility problem using linear matrix inequalities (LMIs) from the trace parametrization of positive trigonometric polynomials yields the best rational approximation of N*(z) in the H∞ norm, from which a realizable state-space controller is constructed.

## Results

On the [AC15] aircraft system, the infinite-horizon DR-RO controller achieves the lowest worst-case expected regret across all tested ambiguity radii (r = 0.01 to 3), outperforming H2, H∞, and RO controllers (Figure 1b). The rational approximation of order 2 (RA(2)) closely matches the non-rational DR-RO controller, e.g., 303.33 vs. 302.08 at r=1 and 1318.98 vs. 1307.12 at r=3, while order 1 (RA(1)) degrades severely at larger r (33394.74 vs. 302.08 at r=1). In time-domain simulations over 210 steps with 1000 trials, the infinite-horizon DR-RO controller achieves the lowest average LQR cost under white noise, worst-case DR-RO disturbances, and worst-case DR-LQR disturbances, outperforming finite-horizon DR-RO and DR-LQR controllers (re-applied every 30 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The DR-RO controller dominates all four baselines in worst-case expected regret across the full range of r. Compared to the RO controller (Sabag et al., 2021), DR-RO achieves strictly lower regret for any finite r, converging to RO only as r→∞; at r=3, DR-RO achieves 1307.12 while RO is higher (Figure 1b). Against the H2 controller (Hassibi et al., 1999), DR-RO matches performance at small r (59.16 vs. ~59 at r=0.01) but pulls ahead substantially as r grows, since H2 does not account for distributional shifts. The H∞ controller (Zhou et al., 1996) is consistently outperformed by DR-RO across all r values, confirming that the Wasserstein ambiguity set provides a less conservative robustness guarantee than worst-case bounded-energy disturbances. In time-domain comparisons against finite-horizon DR-RO and DR-LQR (Yan et al., 2023), the infinite-horizon DR-RO controller achieves the lowest average cost in all four scenarios (white noise, worst-case DR-RO, worst-case DR-LQR), despite the finite-horizon controllers being re-applied every 30 steps and their worst-case disturbances being regenerated at the same interval.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Regret-Optimal (RO) control framework of Sabag et al. (2021) and the Wasserstein-2 distributionally robust (DR) extensions of Taha et al. (2023) and Hajar et al. (2023a) from finite-horizon to infinite-horizon settings. The key mathematical departure is the use of the Wiener-Hopf technique (Kailath et al., 2000) to handle causality in the saddle-point formulation, combined with a Frank-Wolfe algorithm in the frequency domain that replaces the SDP-based approach of prior finite-horizon work. The variational formula for the steady-state worst-case expected regret is inherited from Kargin et al. (2024), but this paper adds the finite-dimensional parametrization of the non-rational optimal solution and a convex LMI-based rational approximation method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical impact is eliminating the SDP bottleneck that made finite-horizon DR-RO infeasible for long horizons, while also providing a stabilizing controller (unlike finite-horizon counterparts) that handles time-correlated disturbances without iid assumptions. The non-rationality of the optimal controller is a notable structural finding, but the convex rational approximation via LMIs provides a practical path to real-time implementation. Open questions include extending to partially observable systems, multi-input rational approximation, and incorporating online adaptation as disturbance statistics are learned.
