## Problem

Risk-Sensitive Distributional RL (RS-DisRL) with static Lipschitz risk measures (LRM) lacks comprehensive theoretical guarantees on sample complexity, particularly under general function approximation. Prior results were confined to tabular MDPs or specific risk measures (CVaR, ERM), and the non-Markovian nature of optimal policies under static LRM—depending on the full reward history—prevented direct extension of existing Markovian policy learning analyses to infinite-state settings.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume episodic state-action-reward trajectories from an MDP with bounded rewards in [0,1], along with either a parametric model class Θ (model-based) or a distributionally Bellman-complete value function class Z (model-free), and a confidence radius β or γ. The output is a sequence of optimistic augmented-MDP policies (Markov in the augmented state space S† = S × [0,H]) that correspond to history-dependent policies in the original MDP, accompanied by confidence sets whose structural complexity (eluder dimension, witness rank, or Bellman eluder dimension) determines the regret bound. The regret is measured as the cumulative gap in LRM-defined risk values between the optimal and deployed policies over K episodes.

## Method

The paper introduces two meta-algorithms—RS-DisRL-M (model-based) and RS-DisRL-V (model-free)—that operate on augmented MDPs where the state is expanded to include the cumulative reward, converting the non-Markovian optimal policy into a Markov one. The key technical novelty lies in novel LSR and MLE estimation procedures tailored to the distributional Bellman equation over CDFs: for LSR, a mixed distribution function decomposes the augmented transition operator into the original transition model times a reward-weighted CDF, enabling least-squares regression on the transition kernel; for MLE, a new augmented simulation lemma connects the ℓ₁ TV distance between estimated and true transition kernels to the ℓ∞ distance between cumulative reward CDFs, bypassing the failure of standard simulation lemmas for non-Markovian policies. Both estimation approaches satisfy concentration and general elliptical potential conditions, yielding the first Õ(√K) regret upper bounds for RSRL with static LRM under general function approximation, with the structural dimension D capturing eluder dimension (LSR), witness rank (model-based MLE), or Bellman eluder dimension (model-free MLE).

## Results

The paper establishes the first Õ(√K) regret upper bounds for Risk-Sensitive Distributional RL (RS-DisRL) with static Lipschitz Risk Measures (LRM) under general function approximation. All four algorithm variants (model-based LSR, model-based MLE, model-free LSR, model-free MLE) achieve sublinear regret with √K dependency on the number of episodes. The model-based LSR bound is Õ(L∞(ρ)H·D₁√K) and the model-free LSR bound is Õ(L∞(ρ)H·D₃√K), where D captures structural complexity (eluder dimension, covering number, or witness rank). In the risk-neutral tabular special case, the bounds recover minimax-optimal √K rates matching prior results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so comparisons are against prior theoretical regret bounds rather than empirical baselines. Compared to Fei et al. (2020; 2021) on ERM, the LSR bound removes the problematic e^{|γ|H²} factor in the Lipschitz constant by leveraging distributional analysis instead of back-propagating Bellman error through the utility function. Compared to Bastani et al. (2022), which only covers static spectral risk in tabular MDPs, this work extends to the broader LRM class (encompassing ERM, CVaR, coherent risk, convex risk) and to general function approximation. In the risk-neutral setting, the model-based MLE result aligns with Liu et al. (2023)'s OMLE bound, and the model-free MLE result matches Jin et al. (2021)'s Bellman eluder dimension framework. The model-free LSR result recovers the optimal √K dependency of Wang et al. (2020) in the tabular risk-neutral case. The paper also notes that for low-rank MDPs with CVaR, the V-type witness rank analysis provides a more favorable dependence on the transition matrix rank d compared to Zhao et al. (2023).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends three research threads: risk-sensitive RL (RSRL), distributional RL (DisRL), and general function approximation. Prior RSRL theory was confined to tabular MDPs (Bastani et al., 2022 for spectral risk; Wang et al., 2023a for CVaR) or specific risk measures like ERM (Fei et al., 2020; 2021), while DisRL theory (Bellemare et al., 2017; Wang et al., 2023b; Wu et al., 2023) focused on risk-neutral objectives. The paper departs from these by formulating RSRL with the general class of static Lipschitz Risk Measures (LRM) under both model-based and model-free function approximation, using the augmented MDP framework (Bäuerle & Ott, 2011) combined with distributional Bellman equations. Technically, it adapts LSR (Jin et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2023) and MLE (Liu et al., 2023) estimation to the distributional/augmented setting, introducing a novel augmented simulation lemma to handle the non-Markovian optimal policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first statistically efficient framework for RS-DisRL with static LRM, achieving Õ(√K) regret that is minimax-optimal in the number of episodes for tabular cases. The general LRM formulation subsumes ERM, CVaR, spectral risk, coherent risk, and convex risk measures, providing a single theoretical lens for a broad class of risk-sensitive decision-making problems. A key open question is the computational tractability of the optimistic planning step (solving argmax over policies and models in the augmented MDP), which the paper acknowledges but does not fully resolve. Additionally, the practical scalability of the augmented state space (which includes cumulative reward) and whether tighter bounds exist for specific LRM subclasses remain important directions.
