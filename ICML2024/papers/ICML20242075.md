## Problem

Existing consistent complementary-label learning methods require either a uniform distribution assumption over complementary labels (too restrictive for real-world annotations) or an additional ordinary-label training set to estimate transition matrices (often unavailable). This paper addresses the gap by proposing a consistent approach that needs neither assumption, making complementary-label learning more practical for real-world annotation scenarios where labels are imbalanced and biased.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a complementary-label training set where each example is a feature vector paired with a subset of labels (0 to q-1 labels) indicating classes the example does NOT belong to, along with class prior probabilities π_k (estimable via Best Bin Estimation if unavailable). It outputs a single class label per test instance by taking argmax over q binary classifier scores. The complementary labels are generated class-wise under the SCAR assumption, meaning each class k is independently assigned as a complementary label with probability c_k when the true label is not k, allowing multiple complementary labels per example.

## Method

SCARCE introduces the Selected-Completely-at-Random (SCAR) assumption, where complementary labels are generated independently per class with class-specific probabilities c_k, and derives an unbiased risk estimator that rewrites the multi-class classification risk using only densities p(x|ȳ_k=1) and p(x|ȳ_k=0). Using the one-versus-rest strategy, the multi-class problem decomposes into q independent negative-unlabeled (NU) binary classification problems, each solved by minimizing a corrected risk estimator that wraps potentially negative terms with a non-negative function (e.g., absolute value) to prevent overfitting. The approach is shown to be calibrated to the 0-1 loss, with estimation error bounds converging at O_p(1/√n), and the corrected estimator is proven biased but consistent. The method is model-agnostic (works with any loss, architecture, or optimizer) and can be instantiated as a meta-algorithm plugging in any PU learning method per class.

## Results

On the real-world CLCIFAR-10 dataset, SCARCE achieves 42.04% (ResNet) and 44.41% (DenseNet) classification accuracy, outperforming the best baseline Phuber-CE (41.13%) and EXP (43.27%). On the more challenging CLCIFAR-20, SCARCE reaches 20.08% (ResNet) and 19.91% (DenseNet), dramatically surpassing the best baseline Phuber-CE (8.10%) and EXP (7.51%) by over 12 percentage points. Across 40 synthetic benchmark cases (MNIST, Kuzushiji-MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10 under uniform, biased, and SCAR generation settings), SCARCE achieves the best performance in 39 cases, with the sole exception being GA on CIFAR-10 under the uniform setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCARCE outperforms all 11 baselines in 39 out of 40 synthetic benchmark cases and all 4 real-world cases. On synthetic datasets, the closest competitor is GA (Ishida et al., 2019), which matches or slightly exceeds SCARCE only on CIFAR-10 under the uniform distribution setting; under biased and SCAR settings, SCARCE leads GA by 5–30 percentage points. Methods relying on the uniform distribution assumption (PC, NN, GA) degrade severely under biased and SCAR settings (e.g., PC drops to 11.00% on MNIST SCAR-a LeNet), while SCARCE remains robust. On real-world CLCIFAR-20, the gap is most dramatic: SCARCE achieves ~20% accuracy versus 5–8% for all baselines (CC, PRODEN, EXP, MAE, Phuber-CE, LWS, CAVL, IDGP, POP), with several baselines (CC, MAE, CAVL, IDGP, POP) collapsing to the chance level of 5.00%. The partial-label learning methods (LWS, CAVL, IDGP, POP) and multiple-complementary-label methods (EXP, MAE, Phuber-CE) all underperform SCARCE, confirming that the SCAR assumption and risk-correction approach provide a meaningful advantage over existing consistent and heuristic approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCARCE builds directly on the complementary-label learning literature (Ishida et al. 2017, 2019; Feng et al. 2020a; Gao & Zhang 2021; Liu et al. 2023) but departs from all prior consistent methods by replacing the uniform distribution assumption or the need for an ordinary-label anchor set with a Selected-Completely-at-Random (SCAR) assumption borrowed from PU learning (Elkan & Noto 2008; Coudray et al. 2023). The risk-correction mechanism follows Lu et al. (2020) and Wang et al. (2023b), wrapping potentially negative terms in a non-negative function to prevent overfitting. A key conceptual contribution is showing that under the OVR strategy, multi-class complementary-label learning decomposes into q independent negative-unlabeled (NU) binary classification problems (Niu et al. 2016), unifying the two weakly-supervised settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first consistent complementary-label learning method that requires neither the uniform distribution assumption nor an ordinary-label training set, making it applicable to realistic annotation scenarios where labels are imbalanced and anchor points are unavailable. The SCAR assumption is more flexible (allowing per-class sampling probabilities c_k) and aligns with how complementary labels are actually collected via iterative yes/no annotation rounds. Limitations include the instance-independent setting (complementary label generation depends only on the true label, not the feature), the need for class priors (estimable but requiring irreducibility), and the fact that the risk-correction introduces a small bias. Open questions include extending to instance-dependent complementary label generation and scaling to very large label spaces where the OVR decomposition becomes expensive.
