## Problem

Existing Boolean task composition for RL (Nangue Tasse et al., 2020) supports only reachability semantics: negating a task merely prevents the agent from terminating in that goal region, not from passing through it. This means regions cannot dynamically switch between being goals and obstacles at runtime, and no composable safety (avoidance) constraints are supported. The paper asks how to extend Boolean composition to enforce safety properties—avoiding undesired states on the path to a goal—without retraining when tasks are combined.

## Input / Output

At deployment, the method takes a Boolean formula over atomic propositions (e.g., T ∧ H ∧ ¬R) and a set of pre-trained extended Q-value functions, one per atomic task (plus dedicated negated-task policies for prioritized safety). The extended Q-value functions are indexed by (state, goal, action) and encode the optimal value for reaching every goal under the task's reward structure, not just the task's own goal. The output is a composed policy that selects actions at each step, producing trajectories that are pure paths when available, minimum-violation paths otherwise, or prioritized-safety paths that strictly avoid designated regions before minimizing remaining violations. Composition is entirely zero-shot: no gradient updates or retraining occur at runtime.

## Method

The paper introduces two safety semantics—minimum-violation (MV) and prioritized safety (PS)—and a penalty hierarchy parameterized by a multiplier η that orders bad behaviors so that incurring a lighter penalty η times is always preferable to incurring the next-worse penalty once. Positive tasks use a reward structure (Eq. 2) with step, bad-step, worst-step, bad-termination, and worst-termination penalties; negated tasks for PS use a modified structure (Eq. 3) that adds a worst-step penalty for passing through the negated region and swaps goal/bad-termination conditions. Boolean composition is performed on extended Q-value functions via max (disjunction), min (conjunction), and a subtraction-based negation operator, with correctness proofs showing the composed policy preserves the safety semantics of its components. For continuous action spaces, the paper shows the optimal composed action is always one of the individual policies' optimal actions, enabling closed-form composition without searching over the continuous action set. A key limitation for PS is that composing multiple negated policies can cause chattering, addressed by Assumption 4.1 (use at most one negated policy at a time, or train safety-extended value functions that maintain additional paths through allowed regions).

## Results

This paper is primarily a theoretical contribution with qualitative demonstrations rather than a quantitative benchmark study. The key empirical results are: (1) correct zero-shot Boolean composition of safety-aware policies is demonstrated across three environments (grid world with value iteration, grid world with DQN, and Bullet-Safety-Gym with TD3), showing that composed policies produce pure, MV, or PS paths as specified; (2) Figure 5 shows that learning extended value functions for composition requires exponentially fewer training steps than learning all individual Boolean combinations, with the gap widening as the number of tasks grows; and (3) the penalty hierarchy (Table 1) with multiplier η ensures correct path selection, with η set to exceed the longest shortest path in the environment. No standard accuracy or success-rate metrics are reported; correctness is verified by inspecting trajectories and policy maps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the reachability-only (RO) semantics of Nangue Tasse et al. (2020), which this paper extends with safety-aware (SA) semantics. Figure 2 directly compares the two: under RO, a policy for 'square ∧ ¬purple' passes through purple regions on the shortest path to a satisfying goal, while under SA the policy takes a longer detour to avoid purple regions entirely. The paper also compares its two safety notions (MV vs. PS): MV treats all non-satisfying regions as equally undesirable and minimizes the count of extraneous labels, while PS strictly avoids a designated subset of regions even at the cost of longer paths (Fig. 1c, Fig. 3). In terms of training efficiency (Fig. 5), the composition approach (learning a small number of base + negated policies) scales exponentially better than learning every Boolean combination individually, though the absolute training cost per policy is higher due to the penalty structure. The paper notes that PS composition can exhibit chattering (infinite loops) without Assumption 4.1, a failure mode not present in the RO baseline, making MV the more robust default for arbitrary compositions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Boolean task algebra of Nangue Tasse et al. (2020), which provided zero-shot composition of discrete Q-tables for reachability tasks, by introducing two safety semantics (minimum-violation and prioritized safety) and extending composition to continuous action spaces. It positions itself in contrast to safe RL literature (constrained policy optimization, shielding, control barrier functions), which focuses on training safe policies by constraining the MDP, whereas this work focuses on composing already-trained policies with safety guarantees at deployment time. The penalty-hierarchy approach with multiplier η draws on the structured reward design from temporal logic planning, where satisfying unintended formulae first leads to specification violations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to compose safety-aware policies zero-shot at runtime is practically significant for scalable RL deployment, where training a separate policy for every possible task combination is infeasible. However, the theoretical guarantees require deterministic MDPs, and PS semantics need Assumption 4.1 (single negated policy or bounded non-satisfying regions) to avoid chattering, which limits generality. Open questions include extending to stochastic and discounted settings, incorporating reward shaping to ease convergence under safety penalties, and developing planning methods for selecting which Boolean compositions to execute in complex task sequences.
