## Problem

Existing weakly supervised learning methods are either tailored to specific supervision forms (partial labels, aggregate counts, pairwise observations, unlabeled data) or, when attempting unification, rely on oversimplifying assumptions like conditional independence that limit scalability to long sequences. Moreover, even unified approaches incur quadratic or factorial computational complexity, making them impractical for realistic group sizes. This paper asks whether a single framework can handle arbitrary weak supervision with linear-time computation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images at standard resolutions (28×28 for MNIST/F-MNIST, 32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 96×96 for STL-10, 224×224 for ImageNet-100) paired with a weak supervision signal W that can take any of several forms: a set of candidate labels per instance, a count or existence flag over a group of L instances, a pairwise comparison/similarity/confidence score between two instances, or merely a class prior over unlabeled data. All inputs are treated as sequences of length L (L=1 for instance-level supervision), and the output is a class label per instance. For multi-class problems, the framework decomposes into K binary sub-problems (one-vs-rest) trained with binary cross-entropy.

## Method

GLWS formulates weakly supervised learning as an EM problem where the true label Y is a latent variable, decomposing the objective into an unsupervised consistency loss L_U (aligning per-instance predictions with the labeling distribution induced by W) and a supervised loss L_S (ensuring group predictions satisfy W). The key technical insight is representing W as a Non-deterministic Finite Automaton (NFA) whose states and transitions encode the constraints of the supervision (e.g., 2 states for MultiIns, m+1 states for LProp with m positives, 3 states for pairwise comparison). By taking the product of the classifier's prediction sequence (a linear chain) with the NFA, the authors construct a trellis on which a forward-backward algorithm computes both EM terms in O(|Q|·L) time, reducing complexity from quadratic (Count Loss) or factorial (UUM) to linear. The forward score accumulates probabilities of valid prefixes and the backward score of valid suffixes, yielding per-position joint probabilities p(y^j, w | x^{1:L}; θ^t) for the unsupervised term and the total accepting-state probability for the supervised term, all computed in log-space with re-scaling for numerical stability.

## Results

GLWS achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across all 11 weak supervision settings evaluated. On partial label learning (Table 1), GLWS leads on all four datasets, e.g., 94.31% on CIFAR-10 (ratio 0.50) and 82.66% on ImageNet-100 (ratio 0.01), surpassing the previous best RCR by up to 1.28%. On aggregate observations, GLWS improves over Count Loss by 12.93% on CIFAR-100 MultiIns (56.29% vs. 30.66%) and by 4.50% on CIFAR-100 LProp. On pairwise observations, GLWS gains 10.23% over the best baseline on CIFAR-100 PComp (83.15% vs. 78.90%) and 14.03% on CIFAR-100 SimConf (87.94% vs. 69.67%). On positive-unlabeled learning, GLWS outperforms the previous best CVIR by 3.02% on CIFAR-10 (500 pos) and 4.81% on CIFAR-100 (1000 pos).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLWS consistently outperforms all 17 baselines across the 11 weak supervision settings. In partial label learning, GLWS beats RCR (the strongest baseline) by 0.27–1.28% across datasets, and substantially outperforms LWS (by up to 27.68% on CIFAR-100 at ratio 0.10) and CC (by up to 9.52% on ImageNet-100). In aggregate observations, GLWS surpasses Count Loss by 1.46–12.93% on MultiIns and by 2.19–4.50% on LProp; UUM is infeasible for LProp with bag size >5 due to factorial complexity, while GLWS handles arbitrary bag sizes in linear time. In pairwise observations, GLWS improves over Rank Pruning by 1.17–4.25% on PComp, over UUM by 0.20–1.26% on PSim, over Sconf ReLU by 7.41–18.27% on SimConf, and over ConfDiff Unbiased by 5.31–14.21% on ConfDiff. In positive-unlabeled learning, GLWS outperforms CVIR (the previous best) by 3.02–4.81%, and dominates DistPU, NNPU, UPU, and VarPU by 5–30+ points on harder datasets. Notably, GLWS does not require class priors or additional loss functions (e.g., contrastive loss in PiCO, multiple augmentations in RCR), making it simpler to implement while achieving superior accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the EM framework (Dempster et al., 1977) and the forward-backward algorithm (Rabiner, 1989; Graves et al., 2006) from speech recognition, repurposing them for weakly supervised classification. It departs from recent unification attempts (Shukla et al., 2023; Wei et al., 2023) that either approximate the EM or rely on conditional independence assumptions, by introducing NFA modeling of weak supervision to solve the complete EM exactly in linear time. The key conceptual move is converting the combinatorial problem of enumerating all valid labelings into a finite-state machine problem, where the NFA's states and transitions are dictated by the weak supervision structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a single, scalable algorithm for 11 diverse weak supervision settings, eliminating the need for specialized solutions per setting and removing the conditional independence assumption that limits prior methods on long sequences. The linear-time complexity (vs. quadratic or factorial in prior work) makes aggregate observation learning practical for realistic group sizes. Open questions include handling noisy weak supervision (mentioned as future work), NFA minimization to further reduce state count, and whether the approach extends naturally to structured prediction or sequence labeling tasks beyond classification.
