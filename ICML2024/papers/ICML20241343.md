## Problem

Transformers are widely adopted as sequence backbones in Partially Observable RL, but it remains unclear whether they can fundamentally solve POMDPs even with unlimited data. This paper establishes that regular languages—which are directly reducible to POMDPs—lie outside the computational reach of Transformers due to their constant-depth parallel structure, challenging the prevailing assumption that Transformers are universally suitable for sequential decision-making under partial observability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241343/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The (SEQ, RL) pipeline consumes a temporal sequence of (observation, action, reward) tuples from a partially observable environment and produces a discrete or continuous action at each timestep. The SEQ component must compress the full observation history into a hidden state that implicitly reconstructs the true underlying Markov state, which the RL component then uses for policy computation. Tasks span discrete binary-string POMDPs (PARITY, SYM(5), EVEN PAIRS) with variable sequence lengths up to 57, continuous PyBullet locomotion with occluded velocity or position channels, and long-horizon memory tasks (Passive T-Maze, Passive Visual Match) requiring retention over hundreds of steps.

## Method

The paper first proves a reduction from regular language recognition to POMDP solving (Proposition 4.1), then shows via circuit complexity that log-precision Transformers cannot solve NC1-complete regular language POMDPs (Theorem 4.4) and cannot length-generalize on a broad class including PARITY (Theorem 4.6), because softmax attention dilutes per-position influence as O(1/n). To remedy this, the authors identify that pointwise-recurrence Transformer variants (FART, FWP, RWKV, RetNet) all reduce to the linear recurrence x_t = Λx_{t-1} + u_t, which is equivalent to a diagonalized linear RNN. They adopt LRU (Orvieto et al., 2023) as the concrete instantiation, which manages the eigenvalue range λ_i ∈ [0,1] to avoid gradient explosion/vanishing while preserving the recurrent inductive bias needed to reconstruct true states. Extensive experiments across regular-language POMDPs, PyBullet occlusion tasks, and long-term memory benchmarks confirm that LRU matches or exceeds LSTM on state reconstruction and outperforms GPT on all partially observable tasks, while GPT's inferiority disappears under full observability.

## Results

On the PyBullet occlusion benchmark (8 partially observable locomotion tasks), LRU achieves an average normalized score of 88.2, substantially outperforming GPT (39.3) and LSTM (74.6). LRU wins 5 out of 8 individual tasks, with the largest margins on Ant-V (29.8 vs. 9.4 for GPT) and Hopper-V (94.1 vs. 13.5 for GPT). On regular-language-derived POMDPs, GPT fails to recover true states on PARITY and SYM(5) (NC¹-complete), while LRU and LSTM successfully reconstruct them. On pure long-term memory tasks (Passive T-Maze, Passive Visual Match), LRU matches or exceeds GPT and significantly outperforms LSTM, with LRU beating GPT on the harder Passive Visual Match task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT (Transformer), LRU dominates across all three experimental categories: it achieves 88.2 vs. 39.3 average normalized score on PyBullet occlusion (a 2.2× margin), successfully reconstructs true states on PARITY and SYM(5) POMDPs where GPT's hidden states collapse to near-random clustering (silhouette scores 0.12/0.05 vs. −0.03/−0.13), and matches or beats GPT on long-term memory tasks. Against LSTM (RNN), LRU is competitive overall (88.2 vs. 74.6 on PyBullet) but wins decisively on tasks requiring long-range memory (Ant-V: 29.8 vs. 7.4; Walker-V: 61.7 vs. 12.2) while LSTM retains an edge on Cheetah and Hopper-P. A fully-observable ablation confirms all three models perform comparably when partial observability is removed, isolating GPT's deficit to its inability to model hidden state transitions. On the NC¹-complete SYM(5) task, scaling GPT (varying hidden size, layers, depth) yields no significant improvement, consistent with the theoretical upper bound of TC⁰ circuit depth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: the formal-language-theoretic limitations of Transformers (Hahn 2020; Merrill & Sabharwal 2023; Delétang et al. 2023) and the empirical observations that Transformers underperform RNNs in partially observable RL (Ni et al. 2022, 2023; Morad et al. 2023). The key conceptual contribution is a reduction from regular languages to POMDPs, providing the first theoretical explanation for why Transformers fail in these settings. On the solution side, the paper unifies pointwise-recurrence Transformer variants (FART, FWP, RWKV, RetNet) with linear RNNs, showing they converge to the same diagonalized recurrence form, and positions LRU as the practical instantiation of this unified view.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the prevailing assumption that Transformers are the default sequence backbone for RL, providing both a circuit-complexity-based impossibility result (under TC0 ≠ NC1) and a length-generalization argument that more data or scale cannot fix the fundamental limitation. Practically, it offers LRU as a well-validated alternative that balances long-term memory (Transformer strength) with state reconstruction (RNN strength). Open questions include whether LRU inherits theoretical guarantees for regular language recognition, how the difficulty classification extends to continuous-state POMDPs, and whether the analysis generalizes to the exploration side of RL.
