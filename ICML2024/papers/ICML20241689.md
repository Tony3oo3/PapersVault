## Problem

Existing safe RL algorithms prune the policy search space to a feasible region defined solely by safety constraints, but within that region many states cannot reach the goal in finite time, causing large amounts of wasted exploration. The paper asks how to further prune the feasible region by jointly encoding both safety feasibility and goal reachability, thereby accelerating convergence without sacrificing constraint satisfaction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a state vector from a deterministic MDP and outputs a continuous action, alongside a scalar feasible reachable (FR) function value in [-1, 1] (approximated by a tanh-activated MLP) that certifies whether the state lies in the feasible reachable region. The FR function is learned as an action-conditioned network G(x,u) = F(f(x,u)) in the SAC integration, and a binary mask m(x) derived from G's sign routes each state to either a reward-maximizing or FR-maximizing policy loss.

## Method

The core contribution is the feasible reachable (FR) function F^π(x), defined as γ^{N_g} if the goal is reached before a constraint violation, −γ^{N_c} if a violation occurs first, and 0 otherwise. This function provably satisfies a self-consistency condition and a 'risky Bellman equation' (a max-operator Bellman backup over F), both of which are contraction mappings under the uniform metric, guaranteeing a unique fixed point solvable by value iteration. The FRPI algorithm alternates three steps: (1) policy evaluation of the Q-function, (2) FR region identification via fixed-point iteration on the scenery network, and (3) region-wise policy improvement where states inside the FR region maximize Q subject to F(x')>0, while states outside maximize F to expand the region. Theoretically, the FR region is proven to monotonically expand across iterations, and the algorithm converges to the maximum feasible reachable region and optimal state-value function in finite iterations for finite state-action spaces. In practice, FRPI is integrated with SAC (FRPI-SAC) using a replay buffer, twin Q-networks, and a separate action-conditioned FR network with a slowly-updated target for stability.

## Results

On the four Safety Gym tasks, FRPI-SAC converges in 2.0K–8.6K iterations, achieving near-zero constraint violations while matching or exceeding the return of the unconstrained SAC baseline. Compared to the closest safe-RL baselines (FPI-SAC and RAC), FRPI-SAC is 2.4–3.1× faster on PointGoal/PointPush and 1.6–2.5× faster on CarGoal/CarPush. On the classical control tasks (ACC, Quadrotor), FRPI identifies the feasible region at 5K iterations versus 75K (ACC) and 150K (Quadrotor) for FPI, a 15–30× speedup in region discovery. Inference time is essentially identical to FPI-SAC (1.576 ms vs 1.573 ms), confirming no additional computational overhead from the scenery module.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FRPI-SAC outperforms all safe-RL baselines on convergence speed across all four Safety Gym tasks. Against FPI-SAC (the closest indirect-method baseline), FRPI-SAC converges 2.4–3.1× faster (e.g., 2.0K vs 6.2K on PointGoal, 8.6K vs 20.7K on CarPush) while also identifying a smaller feasible reachable region that enables further policy-space pruning. Against RAC (a reachability-constrained method), FRPI-SAC is 1.6–2.5× faster, with the gap largest on CarGoal (2.2K vs 5.6K). Against SAC-Lag (a Lagrangian direct method), the speedup is the most dramatic at 2.5–3.5×, and FRPI-SAC achieves near-zero constraint violations where SAC-Lag exhibits severe oscillations and non-convergent cost curves. Compared to the unconstrained SAC upper bound, FRPI-SAC matches or slightly beats SAC on convergence (2.0K vs 2.5K on PointGoal) while guaranteeing safety, whereas SAC achieves comparable returns only by sacrificing constraint satisfaction. On the classical control tasks, FRPI's region-expansion speed advantage over FPI is even more pronounced (15–30×), and both methods ultimately reach the same optimal cost and return, confirming that FRPI's speedup comes from smarter pruning rather than a different optimum.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the indirect safe RL lineage that uses safety certificates (CBFs, Lyapunov functions, HJ reachability) to constrain policy optimization, building most directly on FPI (Yang et al., 2023d), which identifies the maximum feasible region via fixed-point iteration. The key departure is augmenting feasibility with goal-reachability: while FPI prunes only infeasible states and RAC (Yu et al., 2022) handles reachability separately, the FR function unifies both into a single scalar that identifies the intersection of feasible and reachable states. The theoretical machinery (self-consistency condition, risky Bellman equation, contraction mapping) follows the standard Bellman-operator framework but is applied to this new combined criterion, and the algorithm is integrated with SAC in an off-policy setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a 3–5× reduction in convergence iterations for safe goal-reaching tasks, achieved by pruning the policy space more aggressively than feasibility alone allows, without sacrificing safety guarantees. A notable limitation is that for regulating tasks (e.g., ACC) without a natural goal set, the goal function must be hand-crafted, and its quality directly affects return variance and convergence. Open questions include whether the FR framework generalizes to stochastic MDPs (all proofs assume determinism), to tasks with ambiguous or learned goal definitions, and to high-dimensional continuous control beyond the 2D/3D environments tested here.
