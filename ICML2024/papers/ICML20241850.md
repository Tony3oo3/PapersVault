## Problem

Conventional data subset selection maximizes a data utility function under a budget constraint but ignores the possibility that selected data owners may later request deletion under GDPR's right to erasure, causing a significant drop in model utility that cannot be corrected until the next (expensive) data acquisition cycle. The paper asks whether a learner can proactively maximize the expected or worst-case post-deletion data utility during the initial selection, given only probabilistic anticipations of which owners will stay.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241850/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a feasible set M of data owners, a monotone submodular utility function u (e.g., nearest-neighbor or naive Bayes submodular functions), a budget k, and anticipated staying probabilities specified either as per-owner Bernoulli parameters s_j (O(|M|) parameters) or as a distribution over the number of owners staying out of k (O(k) parameters). It outputs a selected subset K of exactly k owners. For the risk-averse variant, an additional CVaR level α ∈ (0,1] controls sensitivity to worst-case deletion scenarios. The utility is evaluated via sample average approximation with n i.i.d. samples of staying subsets, with incremental updates to keep per-round cost at O(|M|) for functions like the diversity kernel.

## Method

The key technical contribution is showing that the expected post-deletion utility u_E(B) = E_{A~p_B}[u(A)] can be rewritten as E_{C~p_K}[u(B∩C)] — an expectation over a fixed distribution — which preserves monotone submodularity (Prop. 1–2) and enables the standard greedy algorithm with a (1−1/e) guarantee. Two objectives are proposed: EDADS (risk-neutral, maximizing expected utility) and RA_α-DADS (risk-averse, maximizing the α-level CVaR of post-deletion utility, optimized via greedy selection over B combined with Brent's method over the threshold τ). For dependent decisions, the paper prescribes a recursive procedure (Cor. 1) to compute the pmf for every subset size t < k from the learner-specified distribution over k, and proves that beta-binomial distributions satisfy this recursion. Efficient evaluation avoids the O(2^k) exact computation by either sample average approximation with incremental Cholesky updates or, for multilinear utilities like the NN submodular function, a closed-form expectation (Cor. 2) that reduces to evaluating u at the staying-probability vector.

## Results

The paper evaluates DADS (EDADS and RA_α-DADS) against conventional DS on four dataset/utility combinations (NN on Synthetic, Heart Disease, Fashion-MNIST; NB on Adults Income). When data owners stay according to the anticipated probability model, EDADS consistently outperforms DS on validation metrics (F1, accuracy, balanced accuracy), with the advantage being most pronounced when fewer owners are selected or the simulated staying probability s̄ is lower (e.g., s̄ = 0.2). DS achieves the best metric score with zero deletions but exhibits the steepest performance degradation as deletions increase, while EDADS degrades more gradually. RA_α-DADS with smaller α (e.g., α = 0.1) further improves worst-case metric scores under heavy deletions at the cost of slightly lower no-deletion performance, with the α-induced decrease being less drastic than the decrease from lowering the staying probability s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is conventional (non-anticipative) DS, which maximizes the same submodular utility function (NN or NB) without accounting for future deletions (Wei et al., 2015). EDADS wins on post-deletion validation metrics across all four experiments whenever the simulated staying probability matches the anticipated model: for example, on (NN-H) with k=200 and (NN-F) with k=600, EDADS maintains higher F1/accuracy than DS as the number of deletions grows, while DS drops sharply. However, EDADS loses to DS in the no-deletion regime (beige curves in Fig. 4), confirming a clear trade-off: anticipating deletions causes the learner to select more redundant, centrally-located data points that sacrifice peak accuracy for robustness. RA_α-DADS extends this by allowing the learner to tune α to improve worst-case (high-deletion) performance without recalibrating the staying probability, outperforming both DS and EDADS in the tail of the deletion distribution (Fig. 6). The paper also notes that optimizing for the worst case (e.g., setting very low staying probabilities) can actually reduce data utility when few or no deletions occur, highlighting that DADS's probabilistic formulation is preferable to adversarial robustness objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends stochastic submodular maximization (Asadpour et al., 2008) from independent Bernoulli variables to dependent deletion decisions, and repurposes the CVaR-based risk-aware submodular optimization framework of Zhou & Tokekar (2022) for a data selection context. It builds directly on the NN and NB submodular data utility functions from Wei et al. (2015), showing they can be wrapped into deletion-anticipative objectives while preserving monotone submodularity. The paper deliberately positions itself against deletion-robust submodular maximization (Krause et al., 2008; Mirzasoleiman et al., 2017), which handles adversarial or known deletion sets, by instead modeling probabilistic, unknown deletions and optimizing expected or CVaR utility rather than worst-case robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical GDPR compliance gap: learners with limited budgets who cannot reacquire data after deletions need a principled way to select training data that anticipates future erasure requests. The key practical insight is that deletion anticipation shifts selection toward more central, redundant data points, trading some no-deletion performance for substantially better post-deletion performance. Limitations include the reliance on sample average approximation (increasing computational cost by a factor of n) and the approximate nature of the RA_α-DADS solution. Open questions include extending DADS to other data selection algorithms (e.g., GLISTER, coreset methods), handling class imbalance in deletion probabilities, and exploring more advanced sampling techniques to reduce the SAA overhead.
