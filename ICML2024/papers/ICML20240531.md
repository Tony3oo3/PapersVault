## Problem

Alignment-based domain adaptive object detectors suffer from source bias, where the stronger supervised detection loss from labeled source data causes the model to acquire disproportionately more source-specific knowledge than domain-agnostic knowledge, impairing target-domain generalization. Additionally, these detectors exhibit exacerbated inconsistency between classification scores and localization quality in the target domain, causing NMS to suppress well-localized boxes in favor of poorly localized ones with higher classification confidence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240531/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes unlabeled target-domain RGB images at test time, while training requires labeled source images, unlabeled target images, and CycleGAN-transformed source2target images (224×224 crops for the classification-teacher). At inference, the detector produces bounding boxes with class labels, but the TROLN network additionally generates per-proposal localization scores (centerness, IoU, and target affinity weights) that are fused into a single scalar to rescale classification scores before NMS, yielding refined detection rankings.

## Method

The DSD framework trains a ResNet101 classification-teacher on source and CycleGAN source2target mixed data using supervised cross-entropy plus adversarial domain alignment (GRL), then distills its domain-agnostic logits into the detector's ROI features via an L1 loss applied only to source proposals with IoU > 0.8, supplemented by an auxiliary classification loss on the projected features. For the consistency issue, TROLN extends OLN with a pixel-level domain discriminator whose output serves as a target affinity score map; pixel-level (τ₁) and instance-level (τ₂) affinity weights reweight the centerness and IoU losses during training so the network emphasizes target-relevant localization cues. At test time, the DCE strategy computes a composite localization score s = √(4·c·b·τ₁·τ₂) from TROLN and refines each detection's classification score via a fourth-root softmax of cls × s, ensuring better-localized boxes receive higher ranking scores during NMS.

## Results

On Cityscapes→FoggyCityscapes, DSD-DA applied to DA-Faster achieves 44.2% mAP (VGG16), 45.2% (ResNet50), and 45.7% (ResNet101), improving over the DA-Faster baseline by +4.6%, +5.0%, and +4.6% respectively. When plugged into stronger baselines, AT+DSD-DA reaches 52.2% mAP and CMT+DSD-DA reaches 52.3% mAP (both VGG16). On Kitti→Cityscapes, the method achieves 46.9% AP50 (DA-Faster) and 49.3% (AT), and on SIM10k→Cityscapes it reaches 47.8% (DA-Faster) and 52.5% (AT), consistently outperforming all listed baselines in those settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Cityscapes→FoggyCityscapes (VGG16), DSD-DA on DA-Faster (44.2% mAP) outperforms all alignment-based baselines including SWDA (34.3%), TIA (42.3%), TDD (43.1%), MGA (43.6%), PT (42.7%), SCAN (42.1%), SIGMA (43.5%), and SIGMA++ (44.5%), but falls short of the self-training methods HT (50.4%), OADA (45.4%), AT (49.3%), and CMT (50.3%). However, when DSD-DA is added to AT and CMT, it pushes them to 52.2% and 52.3% respectively, surpassing all prior methods. On Kitti→Cityscapes, DA-Faster+DSD-DA (46.9% AP50) beats CADA (43.2%), MEGA (43.0%), SSAL (45.6%), KTNet (45.6%), and SIGMA (45.8%), while AT+DSD-DA (49.3%) exceeds AT (47.7%) by +1.6%. On SIM10k→Cityscapes, DA-Faster+DSD-DA (47.8%) surpasses all listed baselines (best prior: CFFA at 43.8%), and AT+DSD-DA (52.5%) improves over AT (51.4%) by +1.1%. The method is plug-and-play, consistently adding 2–5% mAP regardless of the base detector or backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the alignment-based DAOD lineage (DA-Faster, SIGMA, H2FA, AT, CMT) by identifying a previously underexplored failure mode: the supervised detection loss from source data dominates the adversarial alignment loss, causing the detector to acquire disproportionately source-specific knowledge. It departs from prior alignment methods by introducing a distillation loss from a domain-agnostic classification-teacher trained on CycleGAN-style-mixed data, and from prior localization-quality work (IoU-Net, FCOS centerness) by incorporating pixel- and instance-level target affinity weights into a novel localization score. The TROLN component builds directly on OLN (Kim et al., 2022), adding a pixel-level domain discriminator to reweight training losses toward target-relevant regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The source bias diagnosis provides a useful conceptual lens for understanding why alignment-based DAOD methods plateau on target domains, and the plug-and-play DSD+DCE framework consistently improves multiple strong baselines (DA-Faster, AT, CMT) by 4–5 mAP across three backbones and three adaptation scenarios. Limitations include the dependency on CycleGAN for generating the T′ domain (which may not generalize to arbitrary domain shifts), the restriction to two-stage detectors, and the finding that target data cannot be used for distillation due to noise. Open questions include whether the source bias analysis and distillation remedy extend to one-stage or transformer-based detectors, and whether the CycleGAN step can be replaced by more robust style-transfer or augmentation strategies.
