## Problem

Existing neural theorem provers for Coq are trained offline on a fixed corpus and cannot build representations for mathematical definitions encountered in unseen packages, forcing them to treat new concepts as unknown tokens. This paper shows that the locality property of formal math libraries—where physically proximate concepts are mutually relevant and share proof structures—can be exploited through online representation learning to dramatically improve proving in novel mathematical settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240164/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes subgraphs extracted from a single interconnected mono-graph of Coq's global environment (250M+ nodes), where each proof state or definition has a root node and the forward closure is taken until definition nodes are encountered, then pruned to 1024 nodes. Tactic arguments are restricted to local hypotheses or global definitions (not arbitrary terms), and the model outputs 256 scored tactic suggestions via beam search. At inference, the definition embedding table is dynamically updated in topological order as new definitions appear, so that component definitions are embedded before their dependents.

## Method

Graph2Tac is a message-passing GNN (8 hops of graph convolution + MLP + residual + layernorm, hidden dim 128) shared between two co-trained tasks: a definition task that computes hierarchical embeddings for definitions from their structure graphs (root nodes masked, component definitions referenced via the embedding table), and a prediction task that maps proof state graphs to base tactics and arguments. The definition task is the key novelty—it uses cosine similarity loss to align computed embeddings with learned ones, enabling the model to embed entirely new definitions at inference by processing their graphs in dependency order. Argument prediction uses a 2-layer RNN whose outputs feed separate global (inner product with the definition table, temperature-scaled) and local (inner product with GNN node embeddings) queries. The combined loss is L = 1000·L_def + L_tactic, trained with Adam (lr=0.0003) on batches of 512 definitions and 512 proof states. A complementary online k-NN solver learns from the 1000 most recent tactic scripts, and the two solvers are highly orthogonal, combining for a 1.27× improvement over either alone.

## Results

On the 10-minute benchmark (2000 theorems from unseen Coq packages), G2T-Anon-Update achieves a 26.1% pass rate, a 1.5× improvement over the G2T-NoDef-Frozen baseline (17.4%). The online k-NN solver reaches 25.8%, a 1.72× improvement over its offline counterpart (15.0%). Their combination (G2T-Anon-Update + kNN-online) achieves 33.2%, a 1.27× improvement over either solver alone. Both online solvers outperform CoqHammer combined (17.4%), the transformer baseline (10.5–14.8%), and firstorder auto (7.5%) by at least 1.48×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

G2T-Anon-Update (26.1%) and kNN-online (25.8%) both substantially outperform CoqHammer combined (17.4%), the strongest symbolic baseline, by roughly 8–9 percentage points. Against the transformer baseline, the gap is even larger: G2T-Anon-Update beats Transformer-GPU (14.8%) by 11.3 points and Transformer-CPU (10.5%) by 15.6 points. On the informal CoqGym comparison (Table 2), kNN-online achieves 28.4% total pass rate, surpassing ProverBot9001 (19.4%), Diva (19.3%), TacTok+Passport (12.9%), TacTok (11.0%), and ASTactic (10.5%). The G2T-NoDef-Frozen baseline (17.5%) performs comparably to CoqHammer, confirming that the definition embedding task is the key differentiator rather than the GNN architecture alone. The online k-NN's advantage over its offline version (25.8% vs. 15.0%) and G2T's advantage over its frozen-embedding version (26.1% vs. 17.4%) together demonstrate that online information—whether from recent proofs or new definitions—is the primary source of the performance gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Tactician platform and its graph-based encoding of Coq's formal knowledge base, extending the offline k-NN solver (Blaauwbroek et al., 2020a) with a novel graph neural network (Graph2Tac) that introduces a hierarchical definition embedding task. It departs from prior Coq solvers (ASTactic, TacTok, ProverBot9001, CoqHammer) by explicitly incorporating online information—new definitions and recent proofs—at inference time, rather than relying solely on a fixed training corpus. The GNN architecture draws on graph-based representations for theorem proving (Paliwal et al., 2020) but adds the co-trained definition task that aligns computed embeddings with learned ones, enabling the model to represent previously unseen mathematical concepts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that online learning is not merely beneficial but essential for theorem proving in new mathematical domains, with both the definition task (1.5× improvement) and online k-NN (1.72× improvement) showing large gains over their offline counterparts. The high complementarity between the two online approaches (1.27× combined improvement) suggests that future solvers should integrate both proof-level and definition-level online signals. Open questions include unifying the two paradigms into a single model, handling new tactics (not just new definitions), and whether real-time fine-tuning can further close the gap. A key limitation is that the G2T model cannot predict arbitrary term arguments, restricting it to single-definition or hypothesis arguments, and the package-level evaluation split introduces variability that complicates cross-paper comparisons.
