## Problem

Post-training pruning methods like SparseGPT and Wanda can reduce LLM sizes but suffer significant performance degradation even at moderate sparsity (50%), because they lack an efficient retraining stage to recover lost knowledge. Full fine-tuning with dynamic sparse masks is prohibitively expensive for LLMs, while existing PEFT methods like LoRA destroy the sparse structure upon weight merging, forcing a second pruning step that unpredictably alters performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241351/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SPP takes a post-training pruned LLM (e.g., LLaMA or LLaMA-2 pruned by SparseGPT or Wanda) with fixed binary masks at a given sparsity pattern (unstructured or N:M such as 2:4, 2:8) and a small instruction fine-tuning dataset (Stanford Alpaca, ~52K samples). It outputs fine-tuned sparse weights that share the exact same sparsity pattern and ratio as the input pruned model, with only ~1.5–3‰ of total parameters being trainable (e.g., 20M out of 6.8B for LLaMA-7B with r=16). The method is applied to specific linear layers (q_proj, k_proj, v_proj, o_proj, gate_proj, up_proj, down_proj, score) and requires no mask updates during training.

## Method

SPP introduces two lightweight learnable parameter sets per linear layer: W_α ∈ R^{r×n} (randomly initialized, applied to columns) and W_β ∈ R^{m×1} (zero-initialized, applied to rows), which are element-wise multiplied (Hadamard product) with the frozen sparse weights after being repeated to full matrix size. Because multiplication by zero preserves zeros, the sparsity pattern and ratio are exactly maintained throughout both training and weight-merging, unlike LoRA's additive low-rank update which densifies the matrix. The forward pass uses a residual formulation Y = F(X, W̃) + s·F(Dropout(X), W̃'), where W̃' = W̃ ⊙ Repeat(W_α) ⊙ Repeat(W_β), ensuring the initial network output matches the pruned model for stable training. A memory optimization restructures the computation into r block-wise multiplications following Megatron-LM's column-parallel scheme, eliminating the need to store the full repeated W_α matrix. With r=16, SPP trains only ~20M parameters for a 7B model, completing fine-tuning in a few hours on 8 A100 GPUs.

## Results

On LLaMA 7B with 2:4 structured sparsity, SPP improves average zero-shot accuracy from 49.74 (SparseGPT) to 54.60 and from 48.34 (Wanda) to 55.42, a gain of ~5–7 points. At 75% unstructured sparsity, SPP dramatically outperforms both Wanda (32.14) and DS/T (32.23), achieving 41.71 average accuracy on LLaMA 7B and reducing WikiText perplexity from 1285 to 21.8. For LLaMA 30B at 75% sparsity, SPP reaches 50.33 average accuracy (vs. 39.21 for Wanda) with a perplexity of 10.89. SPP requires only ~2.9‰ trainable parameters (e.g., 2.0×10⁷ for 7B models) while preserving the exact sparsity pattern throughout training and weight merging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SparseGPT, SPP yields consistent gains of 3.3–4.9 points in average zero-shot accuracy across LLaMA 7B–65B at 50% sparsity, with the largest improvements on 2:4 structured sparsity (e.g., +4.91 for 30B). Against Wanda, SPP provides similar or larger gains (up to +7.08 for 7B 2:4), and in two cases (30B Unstructured 50% with SparseGPT+SPP at 65.79, and 13B Unstructured 50% with Wanda+SPP at 61.55) the sparse model even exceeds its dense counterpart. At 75% sparsity, SPP dramatically outperforms the training-free DS/T method: on LLaMA 7B unstructured 75%, SPP achieves 41.71 average accuracy vs. 32.23 for DS/T (+9.5 points) and reduces WikiText perplexity from 646 to 21.8; on 30B, the gap widens to +12.6 points (50.33 vs. 37.77). Compared to LoRA* (LoRA training followed by re-applying Wanda masks), SPP wins on average accuracy at 75% sparsity for both 7B (41.71 vs. 39.11) and 30B (50.33 vs. 48.60), confirming that preserving sparsity during training is more effective than post-hoc re-pruning.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPP is conceptually inspired by LoRA (Hu et al., 2021), which introduces lightweight learnable matrices to frozen dense weights, but departs critically by replacing the additive low-rank update with element-wise (Hadamard) multiplication against the frozen sparse weights. This design choice ensures the sparsity pattern and ratio are preserved throughout training and weight-merging, unlike LoRA which produces a dense matrix upon merging. The method also builds on the post-training pruning literature (SparseGPT, Wanda) as its starting point, positioning itself as the missing retraining stage that these methods lack. The paper claims to be the first to systematically integrate parameter-efficient retraining with advanced post-training pruning for LLMs, carving out a niche between expensive full fine-tuning with dynamic masks and training-free approaches like DS/T.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPP addresses a practical gap: post-training pruning of LLMs causes significant performance degradation, yet full retraining is prohibitively expensive. By requiring only ~2% trainable parameters and a small instruction-tuning dataset, SPP makes sparse LLM recovery accessible. However, the evaluation is limited to instruction fine-tuning on Alpaca, and on harder benchmarks like MMLU, SPP-trained sparse models still show considerable gaps versus dense counterparts, suggesting that scaling training data would be necessary for full recovery. Open questions include whether iterative mask updating (as the authors suggest) could further close the gap, and whether the approach generalizes to other pruning paradigms or modalities beyond text LLMs.
