## Problem

Accurate 3D geometric shape assembly requires analyzing fine-grained local correspondences between mating surfaces of fractured parts, but existing high-order feature transforms (e.g., high-dimensional convolution) that capture such neighborhood match patterns incur quadratic complexity in input resolution, making them infeasible at the high spatial resolutions needed for precise geometric alignment. Prior assembly methods that avoid this cost by collapsing parts into global embeddings lose the local spatial detail necessary to localize and match mating surfaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241070/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of point clouds representing fractured parts (approximately 5,000 points total, sampled proportionally to each fracture's surface area) and predicts the relative SE(3) transformation (rotation and translation) between them, with the largest fracture serving as a fixed anchor. For multi-part assembly (up to 20 parts), pairwise relative transformations are computed and then jointly optimized via a pose graph using Shonan rotation averaging. The backbone produces features at three spatial resolutions, and the matching operates hierarchically from coarse (low-resolution, global attention) to fine (high-resolution, local/sparse attention with ~ε neighbors per point).

## Method

The core contribution is the Proxy Match Transform (PMT), a sub-quadratic high-order feature transform that replaces the memory-intensive pairwise correlation matrix F_X·F_Y^T with a low-dimensional shared proxy tensor P (D_proxy ≪ |X|,|Y|). Each PMT independently transforms one feature set via local attention weighted by its correlation with the proxy, and the dot product of the two PMT outputs provably equals the high-order convolution under orthonormality and zero-matrix constraints on the proxy tensors (enforced via auxiliary losses). The full PMTR architecture uses a KPConv-FPN backbone to extract features at three resolutions, applies two PMT layers at coarse level (global attention) to localize mating surfaces, then two PMT layers at fine level (sparse local attention) to refine correspondences, followed by SuperGlue-style optimal transport and relative transformation prediction. This reduces complexity from O(|X|·|Y|) to O(max(|X|,|Y|)·D_proxy), enabling fine-level matching at resolutions where vanilla high-order convolution causes out-of-memory errors.

## Results

On the Breaking Bad dataset (pairwise assembly), PMTR achieves CRD of 0.39×10⁻² and CD of 0.25×10⁻³ on the everyday subset, and CRD of 0.60×10⁻² and CD of 0.42×10⁻³ on the artifact subset, outperforming all baselines on every metric. The closest matching-based competitor, GeoTransformer, scores CRD 0.61/0.89 and CD 0.51/0.70 on everyday/artifact respectively, while regression-based methods (Global, LSTM, DGL, NSM, Wu et al.) are an order of magnitude worse (CRD > 19×10⁻²). PMTR also reduces RMSE(R) from 22.81° (GeoTransformer) to 17.14° on everyday, and from 33.23° to 23.28° on artifact.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PMTR outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across both everyday and artifact subsets. Against the strongest matching-based competitor GeoTransformer (Qin et al., 2022), PMTR reduces CRD by 36% (0.39 vs 0.61) and CD by 51% (0.25 vs 0.51) on everyday, and by 33% and 40% respectively on artifact, while also cutting RMSE(R) from 22.81° to 17.14° (everyday) and 33.23° to 23.28° (artifact). Jigsaw (Lu et al., 2023) is competitive only on RMSE(T) for everyday (2.73 vs 5.53) but is far worse on CRD (5.48 vs 0.39) and RMSE(R) (38.73° vs 17.14°). The five regression-based methods (Global, LSTM, DGL, NSM, Wu et al.) that predict absolute poses via global embeddings are an order of magnitude worse on CRD and CD, confirming that local correspondence analysis is essential for accurate assembly. PMTR also demonstrates superior efficiency: it requires ~21.5× fewer FLOPS and ~3.3× less peak memory than GeoTransformer during training, enabling fine-level matching at high resolutions where GeoTransformer and high-dimensional convolution cause out-of-memory errors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the high-order feature transform lineage initiated by Neighbourhood Consensus Networks (Rocco et al., 2018) and the theoretical equivalence between convolution and multi-head attention (Cordonnier et al., 2020), which together motivate reformulating 2nd-order convolution as a sum of attention-weighted correlation lookups. It departs from prior shape assembly methods (Global, LSTM, DGL, NSM) that collapse each part into a global embedding by retaining spatially-rich point cloud features throughout, and from prior high-order matching layers (GeoTransformer, HDC) that incur quadratic complexity by introducing a shared low-dimensional proxy tensor that mediates information exchange between two independent feature transforms. The overall PMTR framework also draws on CoFiNet-style coarse-to-fine correspondence refinement, SuperGlue's optimal transport layer, and Shonan rotation averaging for multi-part pose graph optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sub-quadratic PMT layer makes fine-grained (high-resolution) matching computationally feasible where quadratic methods simply run out of memory, which is a practical bottleneck for geometric assembly tasks requiring precise mating-surface alignment. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) that PMT with orthonormal/zero proxy constraints exactly expresses high-order convolution provides a principled design rationale rather than a heuristic approximation. Open questions include whether the proxy mechanism generalizes beyond pairwise point cloud matching to other correspondence tasks (e.g., image matching, semantic correspondence), whether performance degrades gracefully under very low overlap, and whether the approach transfers to applications like robotic assembly or artifact restoration without domain-specific retraining.
