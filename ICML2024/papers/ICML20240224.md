## Problem

Existing primal-dual algorithms for constrained online learning under adversarial inputs require two unrealistic assumptions: the decision maker must know the Slater feasibility parameter α in advance, and a strictly feasible policy must exist at every round. Both fail in practical settings like repeated ad auctions with ROI constraints, where the bidder may be priced out in some rounds and cannot pre-compute the feasibility margin. This paper asks whether these assumptions can be entirely bypassed while still achieving best-of-both-worlds no-regret guarantees.

## Input / Output

At each round the decision maker selects an action x_t from a finite or continuous action space (e.g., a bid amount) and receives only bandit feedback: the realized reward f_t(x_t) and cost c_t(x_t) for the chosen action. The budget B = Ω(T) is a hard constraint enforced deterministically (the algorithm switches to a void action once remaining budget drops below 1), while the ROI constraint is soft, permitting a vanishing cumulative violation. Crucially, the algorithm does not receive α or any bound on it as input; the only problem-specific parameters are T, B, and a confidence parameter δ.

## Method

The core mechanism is a 'dual-balancing' framework that replaces the standard sublinear-regret requirement on primal and dual minimizers with a weaker adaptivity requirement: both must guarantee sublinear regret on every sub-interval [t₁, t₂] ⊆ [T]. This interval-level no-regret property creates a self-regulating feedback loop: if the dual multiplier μ_t grows too large, the primal minimizer can 'fight back' by playing a safe policy on the interval where μ is large, forcing the dual to incur regret and thereby capping μ_t ≤ 2/α without any explicit projection onto a set depending on α. The dual uses online gradient descent with separate learning rates η_B = 1/(ρ√T) for the budget multiplier (projected onto [0, 1/ρ]) and η_R for the ROI multiplier (projected onto ℝ₊). The primal uses EXP3-SIX with a Fixed-Share weight update to handle bandit feedback in repeated first-price auctions. This yields Õ(√T) regret in the stochastic setting and an α/(1+α) competitive ratio in the adversarial setting, with vanishing ROI violation and strict budget satisfaction, under only the relaxed assumption that a safe policy exists frequently enough (every k rounds) rather than at every round.

## Results

In the stochastic setting, the framework achieves a tight $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ regret upper bound (Theorem 6.9) while guaranteeing vanishing cumulative ROI constraint violation ($1 + 2/(\eta_R \alpha)$) and strict budget satisfaction. In the adversarial setting, it achieves an $\alpha/(1+\alpha)$ constant-factor competitive ratio (Theorem 6.10) under the standard $B = \Omega(T)$ assumption. Crucially, both guarantees hold without prior knowledge of the Slater parameter $\alpha$ and without requiring a strictly feasible policy at every round — only that one exists 'frequently enough' (e.g., every $k = \log T$ steps). The Lagrange multiplier $\mu_t$ is shown to be bounded by $2/\alpha$ throughout the horizon as a byproduct of the weakly adaptive primal-dual interaction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or head-to-head algorithm comparisons. The comparisons are to prior theoretical guarantees: (1) Standard primal-dual frameworks (Immorlica et al. 2022; Balseiro et al. 2022; Castiglioni et al. 2022a) require exact knowledge of $\alpha$ and handle only packing constraints, making them inapplicable to ROI-constrained bidding. (2) Feng et al. (2023) handles ROI + budget constraints but relies on truthfulness of second-price auctions and stochastic inputs, so it cannot address first-price auctions or adversarial sequences. (3) Slivkins et al. (2023) achieves $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ when $\alpha$ is known but degrades to $\tilde{O}(T^{3/4})$ when $\alpha$ is unknown, and only in the stochastic setting. The paper's contribution is the first best-of-both-worlds framework that simultaneously removes both the $\alpha$-knowledge requirement and the per-round safe-policy assumption, unifying the stochastic and adversarial analyses under a single proof structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the primal-dual template for adversarial bandits with knapsacks (Immorlica et al., 2022; Castiglioni et al., 2022a) by replacing the standard no-regret requirement on both primal and dual players with a weaker adaptivity (interval regret) requirement. The key departure is that previous frameworks enforce boundedness of Lagrange multipliers via explicit projection onto a set defined by the Slater parameter α, which must be known in advance; here, boundedness emerges as a byproduct of the interaction between weakly adaptive primal and dual algorithms, eliminating the need for α. The primal regret minimizer for the auction application builds on EXP3-SIX (Neu, 2015) augmented with a Fixed-Share-style update to achieve the required interval regret guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result is practically significant for online ad bidding, where platforms have been transitioning from second-price to first-price auctions and advertisers face both budget and ROI constraints; prior work could only handle the truthful second-price case or required knowledge of feasibility margins that are unavailable in practice. The conceptual insight that dual-variable boundedness can be achieved 'for free' through the algorithmic interaction of adaptive regret minimizers, rather than through explicit projection, opens a path to handling other non-packing constraints in online optimization without infeasibility assumptions. Open questions include whether the α/(1+α) competitive ratio in the adversarial setting is tight, whether the per-round feasibility relaxation (Assumption 7.1) can be further weakened, and whether the framework extends to more complex constraint structures beyond budget and ROI.
