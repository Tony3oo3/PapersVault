## Problem

Existing data valuation methods define a data point's value by its improvement on a specific validation distribution, which is impractical in collaborative ML and data marketplaces where parties or buyers have heterogeneous, unknown validation distributions. This paper asks: how can we value data without a known or fixed validation distribution while still providing a worst-case performance guarantee for potential buyers?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241222/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a training dataset D_N (features x ∈ R^d with labels y), a kernel function K (for kernel ridge regression) or an NN architecture (for NTK-based computation), a reference sampling distribution P, and a robustness parameter ε defining the χ²-divergence uncertainty set Q. It produces per-data-point model deviation scores M_i(K, D_S) or M_i(Θ_0, D_S) that serve as data values. For NNs, the computation is training-free—only gradients of the model output with respect to initial parameters are needed—making it substantially cheaper than baselines requiring n+1 full training runs for LOO.

## Method

The paper replaces the standard validation-performance utility with the distributionally robust generalization error (DRGE), defined as the worst-case loss over all distributions within an ε-ball of P under χ²-divergence. Since DRGE has no closed form, the key insight is that only its marginal improvement is needed for data valuation, and model deviation ‖f_S − f_{S∪{i}}‖_H (the RKHS norm difference between models trained with and without point i) upper-bounds this marginal improvement (Theorem 3.3). For neural networks, the NTK theory (Arora et al., 2019) shows that a sufficiently wide NN's predictions approximate kernel regression with the NTK, so the NTK-based model deviation ‖g_S − g_{S∪{i}}‖_{H_Θ} serves as a proxy for DRGE's marginal improvement (Theorem 3.4). Practically, the theoretical NTK Θ is replaced by the NTK at initialization Θ_0 of a finite-width network, yielding a bounded approximation error (Theorem 3.5) and a training-free, closed-form computation via simple kernel matrix operations. The framework also identifies a 'uniqueness' notion—comprising scarcity (few same-label points) and dissimilarity (large RKHS distance to same-label points)—that characterizes low-value data.

## Results

The primary metric is DRGE (Distributionally Robust Generalization Error; lower is better), evaluated on 45% data subsets selected by each method. For kernel regression, Deviation achieves the lowest DRGE on UBER (361.81), MNIST (1.893), and CIFAR-10 (2.470), while LOO is slightly better on HOUSING (2.482 vs. 2.555) and DIABETES (0.908 vs. 1.057). For neural networks, Deviation achieves the lowest DRGE on HOUSING (3.416), UBER (265.65), DIABETES (0.794), and CIFAR-10 (0.122), with LOO marginally better on MNIST (0.222 vs. 0.224). All other baselines (TracIn, Influence, DAVINZ, CG, LAVA) are consistently outperformed by both Deviation and LOO across nearly all dataset–model combinations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LOO (the strongest baseline), Deviation wins on 3 of 5 KR datasets (UBER: 361.81 vs. 372.12; MNIST: 1.893 vs. 2.075; CIFAR-10: 2.470 vs. 2.479) and 4 of 5 NN datasets (HOUSING: 3.416 vs. 3.661; UBER: 265.65 vs. 269.05; DIABETES: 0.794 vs. 0.835; CIFAR-10: 0.122 vs. 0.122), with LOO edging ahead only on KR-HOUSING (2.482 vs. 2.555), KR-DIABETES (0.908 vs. 1.057), and NN-MNIST (0.222 vs. 0.224). TracIn and Influence, which trace gradient-based influence on a single validation distribution, are consistently worse than both Deviation and LOO across all datasets and model types. DAVINZ, an NTK-based training-free method, performs poorly on KR (e.g., 2.400 on MNIST vs. 1.893 for Deviation) and is competitive only on a few NN settings. CG and LAVA, which are restricted to image datasets, are outperformed by Deviation on both MNIST and CIFAR-10 for both KR and NN. The paper attributes the advantage of Deviation to its worst-case (distributionally robust) objective: baselines that optimize for a single validation distribution fail to capture data points that are critical under distributional shift, which becomes more pronounced as the heterogeneity parameter ε increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data valuation literature (Data Shapley, LOO, TracIn, Influence functions, DAVINZ, LAVA) by replacing the standard validation-performance utility function with a distributionally robust generalization error (DRGE), thereby removing the need for a known/fixed validation distribution. The theoretical backbone draws on NTK theory (Arora et al., 2019; Lee et al., 2019) to show that model deviation in the NTK-induced RKHS serves as a valid proxy for the marginal improvement of DRGE in neural networks, extending the kernel-methods result to deep learning. Conceptually, the paper repurposes distributionally robust optimization (χ²-divergence uncertainty sets) as a lens for data valuation, a connection not previously explored in the data valuation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine practical gap in data marketplaces and collaborative ML where parties cannot agree on a common validation distribution, and provides a training-free (no n+1 retrainings) method that is both theoretically grounded and empirically competitive. The identification of 'uniqueness' (scarcity + dissimilarity) as the driver of data value offers interpretability that prior proxy-based methods lack. Key limitations include the reliance on RKHS/NTK structure (limiting applicability to non-kernel, non-NTK models), the need to specify the reference distribution P and radius ε, and the assumption that all buyer distributions fall within the χ²-ball. Open questions include extending to other learning algorithms (e.g., boosting, tree-based methods) and relaxing the distributional proximity assumption for highly heterogeneous settings.
