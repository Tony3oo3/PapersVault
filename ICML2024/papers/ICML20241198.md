## Problem

Hotelling's deflation is the standard sequential algorithm for computing multiple principal components, yet all prior theoretical analyses assume the top-eigenvector sub-routine is solved exactly (t=∞). In practice, finite computation budgets and finite precision introduce numerical errors at each deflation step, and the sequential structure causes these errors to propagate and compound into subsequent eigenvector estimates. This paper is the first to mathematically characterize how such inexactness accumulates across deflation steps.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real symmetric positive-definite covariance matrix Σ ∈ ℝ^{d×d} with strictly decaying eigenvalues (λ₁*=1 > λ₂* > … > λ_d* > 0), a target rank K ≤ d, a sub-routine PCA(·,·) for the top eigenvector, and a fixed iteration count t. It outputs approximate top-K eigenvectors {v_k} along with explicit error bounds ‖v_k − u_k*‖₂ that are functions of the eigengap structure {T_j = λ_j* − λ_{j+1}*}, the per-step sub-routine errors ‖δ_k‖₂, and t. The bounds reveal that for slowly decaying spectra (e.g., power-law λ_j = 1/j), the sub-routine errors must be near-factorially small to maintain constant accuracy as K grows.

## Method

The analysis proceeds in two tiers. First, a sub-routine-agnostic bound (Theorem 3.1) uses Weyl's inequality and the Davis-Kahan sin Θ theorem to show that the deflation matrix error ‖Σ_k − Σ_k*‖_F satisfies a recurrence with multiplicative factor (3 + 2λ_j*/T_j) per step, yielding a near-factorial error growth for slowly decaying spectra. Second, when the sub-routine is fixed to power iteration (Theorem 4.1), the paper exploits the directional structure of the power iteration error—specifically that the component of δ_k along the j-th eigenvector decays as (λ_j*/λ_k*)^t—to track errors independently per eigenvector direction via a recursive bound on |v_k^⊤ u_j*|. This directional tracking, combined with a Neumann expansion to bound the alignment u_k^⊤ u_k*, replaces the eigengap-dependent product with a constant-base exponential (8^{K−k}), reducing the required iteration count from Ω(log(K/ε) + K log K) to Ω(log(K/ε) + K) for power-law spectra.

## Results

The paper provides two main theoretical error bounds for inexact Hotelling's deflation. In the sub-routine agnostic case (Theorem 3.1), the error ‖v_k − u_k*‖₂ grows with a multiplicative factor ∏(3 + 2λ_j*/T_j) per deflation step, which can be near-factorial for slowly decaying spectra (e.g., power-law λ_j = 1/j). When the sub-routine is fixed to power iteration (Theorem 4.1), this is improved to a constant-base exponential 8^(k−k′), reducing the required number of power iteration steps from t ≥ Ω(log(K/ε) + K log K) to t ≥ Ω(log(K/ε) + K), saving a factor of log K. Empirically, spectral clustering on MNIST (Figure 1) confirms that increasing power iteration steps monotonically improves mutual information, validating the theoretical error propagation analysis.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no standard empirical baselines to compare against. The key internal comparison is between the sub-routine agnostic bound (Theorem 3.1) and the power-iteration-specific bound (Theorem 4.1): the latter improves the per-step error amplification from ∏(3 + 2λ_j*/T_j) (which can grow near-factorially) to a constant 8 per step, yielding a log K savings in required iterations. Compared to prior work, the paper notes that Allen-Zhu & Li (2017) and Gemp et al. (2021) analyze error for specific algorithms (EigenGame, Oja++) but not for the deflation framework, and that all prior deflation analyses (Zhang 2006, Sriperumbudur et al. 2007, Saad 1988) assume exact PCA sub-routines (t = ∞). The paper positions itself as the first to characterize error propagation in the inexact deflation setting, with the power iteration result (Corollary 4.2) providing a tighter iteration complexity than what the agnostic analysis (Corollary 3.3) would suggest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical Hotelling's deflation framework (Hotelling, 1933) by providing the first rigorous characterization of how numerical errors from inexact eigenvector estimation propagate through sequential deflation steps. Prior analyses of deflation methods (Zhang 2006, Sriperumbudur 2007, Saad 1988) all assume exact PCA sub-routines, while Allen-Zhu & Li (2017) and Gemp et al. (2021) study error accumulation but only for specific algorithms like EigenGame and Oja++. The paper departs from these by offering a sub-routine-agnostic bound (Theorem 3.1) using Weyl's inequality and the Davis-Kahan sin Θ theorem, then tightening it via the directional structure of power iteration errors (Theorem 4.1), leveraging the Neumann expansion technique from Eldridge et al. (2017).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides practitioners with concrete lower bounds on the number of power iteration steps needed per deflation stage to achieve a target accuracy, which is directly actionable in high-dimensional ML pipelines where deflation is used for spectral clustering, dimensionality reduction, and feature extraction. The key theoretical insight—that power iteration's directional error structure saves a log K factor over the agnostic bound—gives a principled justification for why power iteration outperforms generic eigensolvers in sequential deflation. Open questions include extending the analysis to Lanczos methods, power iteration with momentum, repeated eigenvalues, and positive semi-definite (low-rank) matrices, all of which the authors identify as natural next steps.
