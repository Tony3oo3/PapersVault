## Problem

Private Everlasting Prediction (PEP) supports unbounded prediction queries under differential privacy, but suffers from three critical limitations: it provides no robustness against adversarial (out-of-distribution) queries that can poison the oracle, its privacy parameter δ is coupled to the time horizon T (making it not truly everlasting), and the generic construction is computationally inefficient with sample complexity quadratic in the VC dimension. This paper addresses all three gaps simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a labeled training set S of n examples from a domain X, then processes an endless adaptive stream of unlabeled query points x_r ∈ X, outputting a binary prediction ŷ_r for each. Crucially, the algorithm never publicly releases a hypothesis; instead it maintains an evolving black-box prediction oracle whose internal state updates after every query. Under the robustness requirement, only a γ fraction of queries need to be drawn from the target distribution D; the remaining (1−γ) fraction can be fully adversarial, and the algorithm only needs to ensure these do not degrade accuracy on legitimate queries.

## Method

The core construction (RectanglesPERP) for axis-aligned rectangles in d dimensions replaces the k independent hypotheses of the generic PEP construction with a single evolving hypothesis that tracks boundary points along each axis. It uses the RSC (Reorder-Slice-Compute) paradigm to adaptively select slices of the dataset, and a ChallengeBT subroutine (combining BetweenThresholds with the Sparse Vector Technique) to privately answer threshold queries about whether a point lies deep inside a boundary region. The key balancing insight is that whenever a query incurs a privacy loss by being classified as 'deep' inside a boundary dataset, that query point is added to the dataset in exchange, so the privacy budget is replenished over time. For the 'truly everlasting' property, the paper introduces a relaxed privacy definition where δ becomes a per-round function δ(i) with total sum δ*, decoupling sample complexity from the time horizon T. This yields sample complexity Õ(d/(α·ε²)·log²(1/γ)) for rectangles and Õ(log d/(α·ε²)·log²(1/γ)) for decision stumps, both computationally efficient and with only logarithmic dependence on the robustness parameter.

## Results

The paper presents theoretical sample complexity bounds for Private Everlasting Robust Prediction (PERP). For axis-aligned rectangles in d dimensions, the construction achieves sample complexity O(d/(α·ε²) · log²(1/γ)), which is linear in d (vs. quadratic in VC dimension for the generic construction). For d-dimensional decision-stumps, the bound is O(log d/(α·ε²) · log²(1/γ)). The generic robust extension to Naor et al.'s construction yields O(VC²(C)/(α²·γ·ε²)), with a 'price of robustness' of roughly 1/(αγ). A key conceptual contribution is a relaxed privacy definition (Definition 3.8) that disconnects the privacy parameter δ from the time horizon T, making the prediction truly everlasting with sample complexity independent of the number of queries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against the generic PEP construction of Naor et al. (2023), which requires sample complexity quadratic in the VC dimension and is computationally inefficient. The new rectangle construction improves this to linear in d (the dimension), matching the non-private sample complexity up to privacy factors, while also achieving strong robustness with only a log²(1/γ) dependence on the adversarial fraction. The decision-stump construction further reduces the dependence to log d, matching the known VC dimension of that class. The generic robust extension (Observation 1.3) pays a 1/(αγ) price over the non-robust PEP, which the specialized constructions avoid. Crucially, the new constructions use the relaxed privacy definition (Definition 3.8) that makes sample complexity independent of the time horizon T, whereas all prior constructions (including Naor et al.) have sample complexity growing as polylog(1/δ) where δ must be ≪ 1/T for meaningful privacy, effectively bounding the number of supported queries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Private Everlasting Prediction (PEP) framework introduced by Naor et al. (2023) in two conceptual directions: adding robustness against adversarial poisoning queries (yielding PERP) and introducing a time-varying privacy parameter δ that decouples sample complexity from the time horizon T. The constructions build on the Reorder-Slice-Compute (RSC) paradigm of Cohen et al. (2023) and the BetweenThresholds/Stopper algorithms of Bun et al. (2017) and Dwork et al. (2009), repurposing them to maintain a single evolving hypothesis rather than the multiple independent hypotheses required by Naor et al.'s generic 'sample and aggregate' approach. The paper departs from the standard (ε,δ)-DP definition by proposing a relaxed variant (Definition 3.8) where δ is a function of time, which is strictly weaker than standard DP but sufficient to prevent leakage attacks while enabling truly unbounded query support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that PEP can be made 'truly everlasting' (sample complexity independent of T) and robust to adversarial queries, addressing two fundamental limitations of the Naor et al. framework. It provides the first computationally efficient PEP constructions for axis-aligned rectangles and decision-stumps—classes where classical private learning is provably impossible over infinite domains—suggesting PEP may become a viable alternative to classical private learning. Open questions include whether a generic PERP construction can achieve sample complexity linear in 1/α (rather than 1/α²) or sub-linear in 1/γ, and whether the single-evolving-hypothesis approach extends beyond rectangles to broader concept classes.
