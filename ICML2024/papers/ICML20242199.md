## Problem

Neuron-level interpretability frameworks have been developed for vision (Network Dissection, MILAN, CLIP-Dissect) and language (ROME, Bills et al.) domains, but none exist for acoustic models. Existing vision tools rely on image-specific components like segmentation or CLIP image encoders, and audio-specific alternatives are hindered by the limited class coverage of sound event detection models and the immaturity of text-audio contrastive models. This leaves a gap in understanding what individual neurons in audio networks actually respond to.

## Input / Output

AND takes a target audio network (e.g., AST or BEATs), a probing dataset of audio clips (e.g., ESC50), and a predefined concept set as inputs. Audio clips are first converted to open-domain natural language descriptions via the SALMONN captioning model, so the entire dissection pipeline operates in text space rather than on raw waveforms or spectrograms. The three outputs are: a closed-set concept label selected from the concept set, a calibrated natural language summary of shared acoustic properties among top-K highly-activated clips, and a set of open-set acoustic concepts extracted from that summary via POS tagging and LLM-based filtering.

## Method

AND is an LLM-based pipeline with three specialized modules. Module A (closed-concept identification) computes, for each concept, a representative vector by taking CLIP text-encoder similarities between the concept and all audio descriptions, then matches this vector to the neuron's activation pattern using a similarity function (cosine, WPMI, etc.)—this 'description-based' (DB) approach avoids cross-modal audio-text alignment. Module B (summary calibration) uses Llama-2-chat-13B to generate summaries of top-K high- and low-activation audio descriptions, then removes sentences from the high-activation summary that are too similar (cosine > threshold) to any sentence in the low-activation summary, filtering out spurious or hallucinated concepts. Module C (open-concept identification) applies POS tagging to the calibrated summary and uses rule-based plus LLM-based filtering to retain only acoustically-relevant adjectives and nouns. The DB method achieves 100% top-1 last-layer dissection accuracy on AST, outperforming both in-context LLM selection and CLAP-based cross-modal retrieval.

## Results

On last-layer dissection (the primary quantitative evaluation), the Description-Based (DB) method achieves perfect 100% top-1 and top-5 accuracy with 1.00 cosine similarity on AST, 76% top-1 / 100% top-5 on BEATs-finetuned, and 58% top-1 / 82% top-5 on BEATs-frozen. In human evaluation of middle-layer neurons, the calibrated summary (Module B) achieves the highest quality with a mean rating of 3.49/5 and 0.72 cosine similarity to human-written descriptions. For audio machine unlearning, open-concept pruning (OCP, Module C) produces the largest target-vs-non-target confidence gap (ΔA−ΔR = 3.90 on AST, 1.49 on BEATs-finetuned), confirming its effectiveness for concept-specific neuron ablation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three closed-concept identification strategies within AND's Module A: ICL (querying Llama-2-chat-13B to select a concept from the summary), TAB (cross-modal retrieval via CLAP, analogous to CLIP-Dissect), and DB (similarity computation in CLIP text space between concept and audio descriptions). DB consistently outperforms both alternatives across all three target models and all metrics. On AST, DB achieves perfect 100% top-1 accuracy versus 96% for TAB and 72% for ICL. The gap widens on weaker models: on BEATs-frozen, DB reaches 58% top-1 while TAB manages only 46% and ICL drops to 16%. The authors attribute DB's advantage to operating entirely in text feature space, avoiding the cross-modal projection imperfections inherent in CLAP-based TAB. For middle-layer human evaluation, the calibrated summary (Module B) also outperforms both Module A variants, scoring 3.49/5 versus 3.24 (DB) and 2.73 (TAB) in human ratings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neuron-level interpretation lineage (Network Dissection, MILAN, CLIP-Dissect, FALCON) from vision and language modalities to audio, adapting CLIP-Dissect's core algorithm of matching neuron activation patterns to concept feature vectors. The key methodological departure is using LLM-generated audio captions (via SALMONN) as intermediate representations, enabling the description-based (DB) method to operate entirely in text space with CLIP's text encoder rather than relying on cross-modal audio-text models like CLAP. Two additional modules—summary calibration (removing spurious concepts via low-activation comparison) and open-concept identification (POS-tagging plus LLM filtering for acoustically relevant terms)—are novel components not present in prior dissection frameworks. The machine unlearning application extends concept-specific pruning from vision/language to audio, leveraging the open-concept module for neuron selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first neuron-level interpretability tool for acoustic models, filling a genuine gap where prior work was limited to input-specific explanations (LIME, LRP) or layer-wise correlation analysis. The finding that audio models discriminate via combinations of basic acoustic features (frequency, amplitude) rather than high-level abstract concepts contrasts with MILAN's vision observations and has practical implications for audio model design and editing. However, the scope is limited: experiments use only ESC50 (50 classes) and GTZAN, the human evaluation is author-based rather than crowd-sourced, and the reliance on SALMONN captioning quality introduces a potential bottleneck. Open questions include whether findings generalize to larger, more diverse audio datasets, whether the training-strategy effects on polysemanticity hold for speech models, and whether concept-pruning-based unlearning scales beyond single-class ablation.
