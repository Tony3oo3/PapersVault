## Problem

The dominant paradigm for efficient vision-language model construction projects image features into the input space of language models as visual prompts, then applies PEFT. While parameter-efficient, this approach significantly inflates input sequence length (e.g., 256 visual tokens vs. ~6 text tokens in VQAv2), causing 2.6×–4.8× increases in training and inference latency. The paper asks whether visual information can be injected into language models without extending the input sequence at all.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240886/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (224×224 for BART/T5 experiments via CLIP-ResNet-101; 336×336 for LLaMA experiments via CLIP-ViT-L/14 yielding 256 patch tokens) and a text question, producing a free-form text answer or caption. Crucially, the visual features are never prepended to the token sequence; instead they are projected to the language model's hidden dimension and concatenated into FFN weight matrices, so the input length seen by the transformer remains identical to a text-only model. For video tasks, 64 frames are encoded by CLIP-ViT-B/32 [CLS] tokens following the same memory-space injection.

## Method

MemVP exploits the finding that FFN layers in transformers function as key-value memory (Geva et al., 2021). A shared projector (one or two FC layers) maps visual features z_i to the language model's hidden dimension d, and learnable position embeddings p_i^k, p_i^v are added to form new key and value entries. These entries are concatenated to the existing FFN weight matrices W_1 and W_2, so the FFN output becomes the sum of the original D key-value lookups plus n visual key-value lookups. For SwiGLU-based models (LLaMA), the gate matrix W_3 is set to x/||x||² for visual entries, effectively omitting the second key and avoiding extra parameters. During fine-tuning, only the projector and position embeddings are updated (≈3.8–5.5M parameters), while the vision encoder and language model remain frozen. The per-layer FLOP overhead drops from 4nd(6d+n+2L) for input-space prompting to just 4ndL, making the added computation negligible when d≫n.

## Results

On ScienceQA, MemVP with LLaMA-7B achieves 93.07% average accuracy, surpassing LLaVA-LoRA (90.85%), LaVIN (89.41%), and LLaMA-Adapter (85.19%), and even outperforming fully fine-tuned LLaVA-13B with VL pre-training (90.92%). On VQAv2, GQA, and COCO Captions with BART-base and T5-base, MemVP achieves the best average scores (80.2 and 80.8) while using only 23%–44% of the FLOPs of input-space visual prompting baselines. In terms of speed, MemVP is 1.7× faster in training and 1.4× faster in inference than VL-PET on BART, and 1.75×/1.8× faster than LLaVA-LoRA on LLaMA-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MemVP outperforms all PEFT baselines across every evaluated benchmark. On ScienceQA with LLaMA-7B, MemVP (93.07%) beats LLaVA-LoRA (90.85%) by 2.2 points, LaVIN (89.41%) by 3.7 points, and LLaMA-Adapter (85.19%) by 7.9 points, while using a comparable number of trainable parameters (~3.9M). On BART-base and T5-base, MemVP surpasses the state-of-the-art VL-PET (80.2 vs. 79.8; 80.8 vs. 80.4 average) and also outperforms LoRA, Compacter, and VL-Adapter, while reducing language-model FLOPs to 23%–44% of those baselines. Notably, MemVP-7B without any VL pre-training exceeds fully fine-tuned LLaVA-13B with VL pre-training (93.07% vs. 90.92%) on ScienceQA, and also surpasses GPT-4 zero-shot (82.69%) by over 10 points. In training and inference speed, MemVP is 1.75× and 1.8× faster than LLaVA-LoRA on LLaMA-7B, and 1.4× faster than LaVIN during training despite using 256 visual tokens versus LaVIN's 6.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Geva et al.'s (EMNLP 2021) finding that FFN layers in language models function as key-value memories storing factual associations, and repurposes this mechanism for vision-language alignment. It departs from the dominant 'input-space visual prompting' paradigm (LLaVA, VL-Adapter, VL-PET) that projects image features into the language model's input sequence, and from cross-attention approaches (Flamingo, BLIP) that insert new attention layers, by instead concatenating position-embedded visual prompts with the FFN weight matrices themselves. The method is positioned as a PEFT framework where only a lightweight projector and position embeddings are trained, making it conceptually a vision-conditioned adapter embedded in the memory space rather than the input space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MemVP addresses a practical bottleneck in VL model deployment: input-space visual prompts (e.g., 256 tokens in LLaVA) inflate sequence length and multiply training/inference FLOPs, while cross-attention alternatives add hundreds of millions of parameters requiring expensive VL pre-training. The demonstrated 1.4–1.8× speedups with competitive or superior accuracy make this a compelling option for low-cost adaptation of new foundation models. However, the paper acknowledges that the inference advantage is concentrated in the prefill stage, so benefits diminish for long-text generation tasks, and the instruction-tuning appendix shows MemVP-7B still trails fully pre-trained LLaVA-1.5 on several benchmarks, suggesting the approach may not fully replace VL pre-training for the most demanding applications.
