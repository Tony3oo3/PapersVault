## Problem

Most automated sleep analysis methods rely on supervised learning tailored to a narrow task, failing to exploit the rich unlabeled physiological dynamics present across the full breadth of polysomnography (PSG) sensors. Joint multi-modal contrastive learning across brain activity signals (BAS), ECG, and respiratory channels had not been explored, leaving a gap in holistic representation learning for sleep. This paper introduces the first multi-modal foundation model for sleep, trained on over 100,000 hours of PSG data from 14,000 participants.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 30-second clips (the standard epoch duration in sleep scoring) from 19 channels across three modalities—10 BAS channels (multi-region EEG, EOG, EMG), 2 ECG channels, and 7 respiratory channels (chest/abdomen movement, pulse, nasal/oral flow)—all resampled to 256 Hz. Each modality is encoded independently into a fixed-dimensional embedding vector; downstream classifiers operate on the concatenated multi-modal embedding. The model is trained on 11,261 participants (~88,000 hours) and evaluated on a held-out test set of 1,401 participants, with external validation on the Physionet CinC 2018 dataset.

## Method

Three modality-specific 1D CNNs (rooted in EfficientNet/MobileNetV2 with atrous convolutions, depthwise separable convolutions, and inverted residual blocks) produce separate embeddings for BAS, ECG, and respiratory signals. The key novelty is a leave-one-out contrastive learning (CL) objective: for each modality i, its embedding is contrasted against the average embedding of all other modalities (x̄≠i), using an InfoNCE-style loss with a learnable temperature, rather than the standard pairwise CL that only aligns specific modality pairs. This encourages each embedding to capture semantics aligned with all other modalities simultaneously. After self-supervised pretraining (SGD, lr=0.001, 20 epochs, batch size 32), the frozen embeddings drive a simple logistic regression classifier for downstream tasks. Ablations confirm that 3-modality pretraining outperforms 2-modality or single-modality variants, and that ECG inclusion particularly enriches representations of other signals.

## Results

On sleep stage classification, SleepFM with leave-one-out CL achieves a macro AUROC of 0.906 and macro AUPRC of 0.685, substantially outperforming the end-to-end supervised CNN (0.842 AUROC, 0.579 AUPRC) despite using only 1,265 training participants versus 12,526 for the CNN. For SDB detection, leave-one-out CL reaches AUROC 0.941 and AUPRC 0.711 versus 0.843 and 0.555 for the supervised CNN. In cross-modal retrieval from 90,000 candidates, pairwise CL achieves 48% top-1 average accuracy (e.g., BAS→ECG Recall@10 of 0.74), exceeding random chance by over 500×. External validation on the Physionet CinC 2018 dataset confirms generalization, with SleepFM scoring macro AUROC 0.924 and F1 0.700 versus 0.843 and 0.363 for a CNN trained end-to-end on that external data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three approaches: the proposed leave-one-out CL, standard pairwise CL, and an end-to-end supervised 1D EfficientNet CNN. The supervised CNN is a strong baseline because it is trained on the full 12,526-participant dataset (pretrain + train), whereas the CL models' logistic regression classifiers see only 1,265 training participants. Despite this 10× data disadvantage, leave-one-out CL outperforms the supervised CNN by 6.4 points in macro AUROC (0.906 vs 0.842) and 10.6 points in macro AUPRC (0.685 vs 0.579) on sleep staging, and by 9.8 AUROC points (0.941 vs 0.843) and 15.6 AUPRC points (0.711 vs 0.555) on SDB detection. Leave-one-out CL also consistently beats pairwise CL across all tasks (e.g., 0.906 vs 0.876 macro AUROC for sleep stages; 0.941 vs 0.902 AUROC for SDB), with the gap widening on AUPRC metrics. The one area where pairwise CL edges out leave-one-out is cross-modal retrieval (e.g., BAS→ECG Recall@10 of 0.74 vs 0.58), which the authors attribute to pairwise CL's training objective directly matching the retrieval evaluation protocol. On external validation (Physionet CinC 2018), SleepFM's leave-one-out embeddings with logistic regression achieve macro AUROC 0.924 and F1 0.700, surpassing a CNN trained end-to-end on that external dataset (0.843 AUROC, 0.363 F1) by 8.1 AUROC and 33.7 F1 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-style multi-modal contrastive learning paradigm (Radford et al., 2021) from image-text pairs to physiological time-series signals, building on the 1D CNN architecture from Ouyang et al. (2022) for ECG classification. Prior sleep ML work has been overwhelmingly supervised (CNNs, RNNs, DNNs for staging and SDB detection), and prior contrastive efforts in medicine have focused on single modalities (3KG for ECG, CLOCS for cardiac signals) or ECG paired with structured EHR records (Raghu et al., 2022; Lalam et al., 2023). SleepFM departs from these by jointly modeling three physiological systems (brain, heart, lungs) across 19 channels and introducing a leave-one-out contrastive objective that contrasts each modality's embedding against the mean of the other modalities, rather than standard pairwise alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first multi-modal foundation model for sleep, SleepFM demonstrates that self-supervised contrastive pretraining on 100,000+ hours of PSG data yields embeddings that, with only a linear probe, substantially outperform end-to-end supervised CNNs trained on 9× more data, suggesting a scalable path toward reducing reliance on expensive manual sleep scoring. The external validation on Physionet CinC 2018 (macro AUROC 0.924 vs 0.843) is encouraging for cross-site deployment, though the study is limited to a single institution, two downstream tasks, and one CL variant. Open questions include whether the leave-one-out advantage persists with more than three modalities, how to handle missing channels in real-world PSG, and whether other SSL objectives (masked modeling, generative) might further improve representations for rarer events like arousals or periodic leg movements.
