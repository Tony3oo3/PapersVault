## Problem

Standard RL policies rely on Gaussian parameterization, limiting them to discovering a single behavior per context. While Mixture of Experts (MoE) policies can capture multi-modality, prior approaches like SVSL use linear experts with Gaussian per-expert context distributions that require hand-tuned penalty terms and prior knowledge of the environment's valid context bounds. This paper addresses how to learn diverse, non-linear skills with automatic curriculum learning without any assumptions about the environment's context space.

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous context vector c (e.g., 4-dim ball landing positions in table tennis, 5-dim obstacle/goal/position in box pushing) drawn from the environment's unknown distribution p(c) and produces motion primitive parameters θ (12–29 dimensional) that define a full trajectory via ProDMPs. During training, a batch of contexts is sampled by resetting the environment, and each expert selects its preferred subset via its energy-based context distribution. During inference, the gating distribution π(o|c) selects an expert, which then outputs a Gaussian over θ whose mean or sample is executed as a trajectory.

## Method

Di-Skill parameterizes each MoE expert π(θ|c,o) as a non-linear neural network outputting a Gaussian over motion primitive parameters, and each per-expert context distribution π(c|o) as an energy-based model π(c|o) = exp(φ_o(c))/Z, where φ_o is a learnable neural network. The intractable normalizing constant Z is approximated by summing exp(φ_o(c_i)) over a batch of contexts sampled from the environment's distribution p(c) via resets, which naturally restricts exploration to valid regions without requiring explicit bounds or penalty terms. Automatic curriculum learning emerges because each expert samples training contexts from its own EBM, focusing on sub-regions it favors, while the entropy bonus H[π(c|o)] and variational term π̃(o|c) encourage overlap and specialization respectively. Expert updates use trust-region constrained optimization (Eq. 7) to stabilize the bi-level optimization, while context distribution updates use a PPO-style objective (Eq. 8) with closed-form terms. The overall objective is a KL-regularized maximum entropy formulation that incentivizes diverse solutions while keeping the mixture's context distribution aligned with the environment's p(c).

## Results

Di-Skill is evaluated on six robot simulation tasks using IQM (interquantile mean) with 95% stratified bootstrap confidence intervals over 24 seeds. On the Box Pushing with Obstacle (BPO) task, Di-Skill achieves the highest success rate, outperforming BBRL which cannot capture the multi-modality introduced by the obstacle. On Robot Mini Golf (MG), Di-Skill shows a clear performance gap over BBRL, while on Table Tennis Hard (TT-H) Di-Skill achieves a slightly higher end success rate than BBRL. On Hopper Jump (HJ), Di-Skill performs on par with BBRL, and on 5-Link Reacher (5LR) Di-Skill outperforms BBRL by capturing multi-modal reaching behaviors (e.g., reaching from different sides).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BBRL (Otto et al., 2023), Di-Skill matches performance on Hopper Jump but wins on all tasks requiring multi-modal or non-linear policies: BPO (obstacle-induced multi-modality), 5LR (reaching from different sides), TT-H (slightly higher end success rate), and MG (clear gap, non-Markovian reward). BBRL converges slightly faster on HJ and TT, but Di-Skill's non-linear experts and automatic curriculum learning give it an edge where diversity matters. Against SVSL (Celik et al., 2022), Di-Skill clearly outperforms on TT: SVSL with 20 linear experts and hand-tuned Gaussian punishment terms needs significantly more samples to reach ~80% success, while Di-Skill with 5 non-linear experts and energy-based context distributions converges faster. Against PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), Di-Skill substantially outperforms on 5LR and BPO where time-sparse rewards make step-based exploration infeasible. The LinDi-Skill ablation (linear experts + Di-Skill's EBM) confirms that non-linear experts are essential for non-Markovian tasks (TT-H, MG) and that the EBM context distribution alone (without hand-tuned penalties) is sufficient for valid context sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-paced diverse skill learning framework of Celik et al. (2022, SVSL) by replacing its linear experts with non-linear neural network experts and its Gaussian per-expert context distributions with energy-based models (EBMs). The CEPS framework (Kupcsik et al., 2013) and trust-region layers (Otto et al., 2021) provide the foundational optimization machinery. The key conceptual departure is using EBMs to parameterize per-expert context distributions, which eliminates the need for environment-specific penalty terms to keep context samples within valid bounds—a practical limitation of prior Gaussian-based approaches that required hand-tuned punishment functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EBM-based context distribution is a clean, general solution to the problem of curriculum shaping in bounded, multi-modal context spaces without requiring prior knowledge of the environment's geometry. This makes the method applicable to complex robot tasks where context bounds are defined by physical constraints (e.g., table surfaces, obstacle positions) that are difficult to encode analytically. The main limitation acknowledged is the inability to replan mid-episode, causing failures on small collisions; the authors also note that sample efficiency could be improved with off-policy techniques. For the broader RL community, the work demonstrates that energy-based models can be practically integrated into policy gradient optimization for distribution-level (rather than action-level) modeling.
