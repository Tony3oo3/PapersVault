## Problem

Existing semi-supervised multi-view classification methods randomly select samples to label, producing unstable and suboptimal results, and they fail to properly decouple and separately exploit the shared and specific information (SSI) inherent in multi-view data. This paper addresses both issues by proposing a dynamic labeling strategy that prioritizes uncertain samples for annotation while leveraging decoupled SSI through dedicated classifiers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242042/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes V multi-view feature matrices (each with potentially different feature dimensions d_v) and a small initial labeled set (5% cold start). It iteratively selects B = 2.5% of remaining samples per round for labeling based on a dual-classifier uncertainty score, and generates pseudo-labels for high-confidence consistent predictions, continuing until 50% of samples are labeled. The final output is a predicted class label per sample, computed as the argmax over the element-wise maximum of the two classifier outputs.

## Method

The architecture employs V+1 four-layer autoencoders (one per view plus one for the concatenated feature) with shared middle-layer parameters to extract SSI, followed by a 3-layer view discriminator that decouples shared from specific information via KL-divergence losses (specific info must be classified to its source view; shared info must yield a uniform distribution). Two separate 3-layer classifiers are then trained on the decoupled specific and shared representations independently, since different samples may be more discriminable via one or the other. The uncertainty score for active labeling is 1 − (max-class confidence from specific classifier × max-class confidence from shared classifier), so samples hard to classify by both information sources are labeled first. The total loss combines reconstruction, view-discrimination, and dual-classification terms, and the model is retrained after each labeling round in an iterative loop.

## Results

On six benchmark datasets at 50% label ratio, DMVLS achieves 99.97% ACC on Handwritten, 97.47% on BDGP, 96.22% on Cora, 86.97% on CiteSeer, 75.63% on STL-10, and 100.00% on YTB10. The method outperforms the second-best baseline by 1.60%, 12.80%, 11.80%, 14.71%, 11.62%, and 0.08% respectively at this ratio. Notably, DMVLS achieves the lowest standard deviation across all datasets (e.g., ±0.05 on Handwritten vs. ±0.29 for DSRL), demonstrating substantially greater stability than random-labeling baselines. On large-scale YTB10 (38,654 samples), graph-based methods (MVAR, ERL-MVSC, DSRL) fail entirely due to out-of-memory errors, while DMVLS achieves perfect accuracy with linear O(n) complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMVLS outperforms all five baselines on every dataset at every label ratio tested (20%–50%). Against MVAR (Tao et al., 2017), the gap is largest on BDGP (97.47% vs. 84.67% at 50%) and CiteSeer (86.97% vs. 61.45%), while MVAR is the only baseline that runs on all six datasets. Co-GCN (Li et al., 2020) is consistently weak on Cora (48.40%) and STL-10 (31.48%), likely due to its reliance on combined Laplacian graph structure. ERL-MVSC (Huang et al., 2021) is the strongest competitor on Cora (84.42%) and CiteSeer (72.26%) but fails on YTB10 and performs poorly on BDGP (45.70%). DSRL (Wang et al., 2022b) is competitive on Handwritten (98.40%) but collapses on BDGP (20.08%) and CiteSeer (55.90%). IMvGCN (Wu et al., 2023a) is the closest competitor on YTB10 (99.92% vs. 100.00%) but trails by 11–15 points on CiteSeer and STL-10. Three of the five baselines (MVAR, ERL-MVSC, DSRL) cannot run on YTB10 (38,654 samples) due to quadratic-space graph operations, whereas DMVLS's autoencoder-based architecture scales linearly.

## Relation to Prior Work

DMVLS sits at the intersection of semi-supervised multi-view classification (SSMVC) and active learning, repurposing uncertainty sampling from the active learning literature into a multi-view setting where uncertainty is defined via two separate classifiers (one for shared information, one for view-specific information). The SSI extraction and decoupling mechanism draws on the view discriminator concept from Jia et al. (2020) and the shared-specific decomposition tradition in multi-view subspace clustering (Luo et al., 2018; Zhou et al., 2020), but wraps them in an autoencoder framework to ensure reconstruction quality. The key departure from existing SSMVC methods (MVAR, Co-GCN, ERL-MVSC, DSRL, IMvGCN) is the replacement of random sample selection with an iterative, confidence-driven labeling strategy, and the replacement of graph-based propagation (quadratic/cubic complexity) with a linear-complexity AE architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that in multi-view semi-supervised learning, *which* samples get labeled matters as much as *how* the model is trained, and that the shared vs. specific information decomposition is dataset-dependent (e.g., shared information dominates on STL10 while specific information dominates on CiteSeer). The linear time complexity makes the approach viable for large-scale multi-view data where graph-based competitors fail with out-of-memory errors. Open questions remain around the random cold-start initialization, the fixed per-round labeling budget (2.5%), and whether the two-classifier uncertainty metric generalizes beyond the product-of-max-probabilities formulation used here.
