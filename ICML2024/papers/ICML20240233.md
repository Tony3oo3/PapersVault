## Problem

Existing continual self-supervised learning (CSSL) methods, such as CaSSLe, combine a plasticity loss (learning new representations) with a stability loss (preserving old representations), but the stability term can impede the plasticity term because the two losses operate on disjoint sets of model outputs. This creates an unresolved tension: the regularization designed to prevent forgetting may simultaneously hinder the acquisition of better representations on new tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240233/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two augmented views of an unsupervised RGB image processed by both the current model (h_θ_t) and the frozen previous model (h_θ_{t-1}), yielding four feature vectors per sample. For MoCo-based variants, two additional queues (default size 65536) store pseudo-negative embeddings from the previous model across iterations. The output is a learned encoder whose quality is assessed via linear probing (training only a linear head on frozen features) across class-, data-, and domain-incremental scenarios, with stability and plasticity metrics reported alongside average accuracy.

## Method

PNR introduces pseudo-negatives—output features from the *other* model (previous for the plasticity loss, current for the stability loss)—into both loss terms of the CSSL objective. For InfoNCE-based contrastive methods (SimCLR, MoCo), the plasticity loss L1 adds previous-model embeddings to the denominator as pseudo-negatives, while the stability loss L2 (a contrastive distillation term) adds current-model embeddings as pseudo-negatives; the two denominators are identical except for the predictor g(·), yielding a natural plasticity-stability trade-off. For non-contrastive methods (BYOL, BarlowTwins, VICReg), where no explicit negatives exist, PNR instead adds a regularization term that maximizes the squared L2 distance between the predictor output g(z_t^A) and the previous model's output z_{t-1}^B for a differently-augmented view of the same image, effectively pushing the new representation away from the old model's alternative-view embedding. Gradient analysis shows the update direction decomposes into attraction toward a blend of current and previous positive embeddings and repulsion from a weighted center of mass over all negatives and pseudo-negatives, simultaneously enhancing discriminability (plasticity) and knowledge retention (stability).

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (Class-IL, 5T), PNR achieves A₅ of 62.36% with MoCo, 58.87% with SimCLR, 60.28% with Barlow, 63.13% with BYOL, and 56.47% with VICReg, consistently outperforming CaSSLe by 0.2–3.3%. On ImageNet-100 (Class-IL, 5T), the gains are larger: MoCo+PNR reaches 67.85% vs. 63.49% for MoCo+CaSSLe (+4.4%), and VICReg+PNR reaches 62.48% vs. 59.18% (+3.3%). On the large-scale ImageNet-1k (Class-IL, 10T), MoCo+PNR achieves 55.88% vs. 43.38% for MoCo+CaSSLe, a 12.5-point improvement. Stability (S↓) and plasticity (P↑) metrics confirm PNR's advantage: MoCo+PNR yields (S,P)=(1.23, 3.47) versus (2.80, 2.52) for MoCo+CaSSLe on ImageNet-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PNR is compared primarily against CaSSLe (Fini et al., 2022), the prior state-of-the-art CSSL method, and secondarily against EWC (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) and DER (Wu et al., 2019) in the CIFAR-100 5T setting. PNR wins against CaSSLe in nearly all configurations: on CIFAR-100 (5T) the gains range from +0.18% (Barlow) to +3.34% (VICReg), and on ImageNet-100 (5T) from +0.35% (SimCLR) to +4.36% (MoCo). The advantage is most dramatic on ImageNet-1k, where MoCo+PNR improves over MoCo+CaSSLe by 6.3 points (5T) and 12.5 points (10T). Against EWC and DER (both supervised-CL regularizers applied to SSL), PNR leads by 5–10 points on CIFAR-100, confirming that the pseudo-negative mechanism is substantially more effective than weight-decay or exemplar-replay strategies. The one notable exception is BYOL in Data-IL (5T), where PNR (66.08%) slightly trails CaSSLe (66.22%), which the authors attribute to the near-identical data distributions across tasks in that scenario.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends CaSSLe (Fini et al., 2022), which introduced the first dedicated regularization for Continual Self-Supervised Learning via a two-term loss (plasticity + stability). The key conceptual departure is that CaSSLe's stability loss only constrains the current model toward the previous model's outputs, while PNR injects pseudo-negatives from both the current and previous models into both loss terms, drawing on the InfoNCE loss structure (Oord et al., 2018) and contrastive distillation (Tian et al., 2019). The paper further generalizes the pseudo-negative idea to non-contrastive SSL methods (BYOL, BarlowTwins, VICReg) that do not natively employ negatives, by reinterpreting the cross-model, cross-augmentation output as a pseudo-negative to be repelled.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PNR provides a principled mechanism to balance the stability-plasticity tradeoff in CSSL that is applicable across both contrastive and non-contrastive SSL methods, achieving consistent 2–6% gains over the prior SOTA (CaSSLe) across Class-IL, Data-IL, and Domain-IL on multiple datasets. However, the work is limited to CNN-based architectures (ResNet-18/50) and the computer vision domain; the authors note that extension to ViT-based models and NLP remains open. Additionally, the paper acknowledges a 4–6% reproduction gap with CaSSLe's reported numbers on ImageNet-100/DomainNet, which tempers the absolute performance claims, though relative comparisons within the paper remain consistent.
