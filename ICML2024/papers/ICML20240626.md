## Problem

Deep Image Priors (DIPs) are highly overparameterized and inevitably overfit to noise when trained to convergence on corrupted images, requiring fragile early-stopping heuristics. Underparameterized alternatives like Deep Decoder prevent overfitting but sacrifice rich image-prior quality, failing to capture fine texture details. The paper asks whether a principled pruning strategy applied at random initialization—using only the corrupted measurement—can yield a sparse subnetwork that both resists overfitting and retains the expressive power of a full DIP.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single corrupted 512×512×3 RGB image (or undersampled k-space measurements for MRI), a randomly initialized 6-layer encoder-decoder Unet (~3M parameters, Kaiming init), and a fixed random Gaussian input tensor z of dimension H×W×32×3. It produces a sparse binary mask at a user-specified sparsity (e.g., 3–5%, yielding ~90K parameters—fewer than the 786K image pixels), which is applied to the initialized network to form a Sparse-DIP subnetwork. The subnetwork is then trained to convergence (40k iterations, Adam, lr=1e-2) to output the reconstructed image. Notably, the mask is learned entirely at initialization without any clean-image supervision and is transferable across images, datasets, and corruption types.

## Method

OES formulates mask selection as a discrete optimization over binary masks minimizing the reconstruction loss at random initialization, then relaxes it to a continuous problem by learning Bernoulli dropout probabilities p regularized by a KL divergence to a uniform prior Ber(p₀) with p₀ = s/d. The Gumbel-Softmax (Concrete) reparameterization trick makes the discrete mask sampling differentiable, allowing gradient descent to optimize p with a small temperature (T=0.2) and a very small KL weight (λ=1e-9). After convergence, the mask is obtained by ranking p values and thresholding to the target sparsity. The resulting Sparse-DIP subnetwork is underparameterized (fewer parameters than pixels), which inherently prevents overfitting to noise while the retained weights—concentrated in the decoder, the encoder-decoder junction, and the final convolution layer—preserve the low-frequency image prior. A key finding is that masking alone at initialization already produces a low-frequency approximation of the clean image, and the encoder is far more compressible than the decoder, explaining why LTH-based masks (which retain encoder weights based on post-training magnitudes) are suboptimal for reconstruction.

## Results

On the Set-14 dataset at σ=25 dB (Gaussian noise), Sparse-DIP (3% sparse, ~90K parameters) achieves an average PSNR of ~26.4 dB at convergence (40k epochs, no early stopping), outperforming Dense DIP (~22.1 dB), GP-DIP (~23.5 dB), and Deep Decoder (~25.9 dB). At 5% sparsity, OES masks outperform all competing pruning methods: e.g., on Pepper, OES reaches 27.45 dB vs. GraSP (22.22), SynFlow (22.07), IMP-(0.8)^14 (25.52), and IMP-(0.2)^3 (25.66). On the Face dataset, OES achieves up to 29.58 dB (Face-2) vs. 29.50 dB for Deep Decoder and 22.02 dB for Dense DIP. Sparse-DIP also extends to MRI reconstruction from 4× and 8× undersampled k-space, where it reduces overfitting artifacts compared to vanilla Dense DIP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Dense DIP (Ulyanov et al., 2018), Sparse-DIP wins by a large margin (4–6 dB PSNR) because Dense DIP overfits to noise at convergence due to its 3M overparameterized weights. Against GP-DIP (Cheng et al., 2019), which uses SGLD to delay overfitting, OES still wins by 1–3 dB on most images, as GP-DIP still eventually overfits. Against Deep Decoder (Heckel & Hand, 2018), the closest competitor, OES wins by ~0.3–1.1 dB on average; Deep Decoder slightly edges out on Monarch (27.87 vs 27.62) and Barbara at σ=12 dB, but OES recovers directional edges better due to its higher-frequency noise impedance. Against PAI methods GraSP (Wang et al., 2020) and SynFlow (Tanaka et al., 2020), OES wins by 3–5 dB at 5% sparsity, as both show clear overfitting when trained to convergence. Against LTH/IMP (Frankle & Carbin, 2018), OES wins by 1.5–2 dB (e.g., 27.45 vs 25.52–25.66 on Pepper), because IMP masks derived from converged weight magnitudes encode noise-correlated structure; even with clean-image training, IMP masks show worse cross-image transferability than OES masks learned at initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of neural network pruning and Deep Image Prior (DIP) for image reconstruction. It builds on the DIP framework (Ulyanov et al., 2018) as the base architecture and on the Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization trick (Maddison et al., 2016) to make discrete mask learning differentiable. Conceptually, it departs from the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (LTH/IMP), which prunes based on post-training weight magnitudes, by arguing that for overfitting-prone tasks like DIP, pruning at random initialization is strictly preferable. It also extends Pruning-at-Initialization (PAI) methods like GraSP and SynFlow—originally designed for classification—to the image reconstruction setting, and bridges the gap between overparameterized DIP (which overfits) and underparameterized Deep Decoder (which underperforms on fine details).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OES provides a principled, early-stopping-free mechanism to prevent overfitting in DIP by reducing network capacity through learned masks, with the added benefit that masks transfer across images, datasets, and corruption types. The conceptual finding that masking alone at random initialization induces a low-frequency image prior is a useful insight for understanding how architecture and sparsity interact in generative networks. Limitations include the computational overhead of Gumbel-Softmax mask learning, slight performance degradation in cross-domain transfer, and architecture-specificity for specialized tasks like MRI. Open questions include a theoretical explanation for why sparse subnetworks at initialization capture image structure, and whether these findings extend to diffusion-model-based generative frameworks.
