## Problem

Existing ranking bandit methods assume homogeneous position effects (identical across all items and users) and operate at the population level, making them unsuitable for personalized, contextual ranking on online platforms. Moreover, the action space of all possible K-permutations from N items grows exponentially, rendering direct application of standard UCB or Thompson sampling infeasible. This paper addresses both gaps by modeling heterogeneous, item-specific position effects within a contextual bandit framework and providing an efficient polynomial-time solution to the resulting combinatorial optimization.

## Input / Output

At each round the algorithm receives a user context vector X_t ∈ R^d (i.i.d. from a known distribution) and must jointly select K items from a pool of N unstructured candidates (no exogenous item features) and assign them to K ordered positions. The output is a permutation σ_t over the retrieved set s_t(X_t), and the algorithm observes itemwise user responses Y_{t,j,k} (e.g., watchtime, revenue, or click) for each displayed item-position pair. The reward is an additive (or generalized additive) aggregation of per-item satisfaction scores, with each item's response modeled by a GLM parameterized by an item-specific embedding β_j ∈ R^d and a scalar position effect α_j.

## Method

The UCR algorithm operates in two phases: a random initialization phase of T_0 rounds to ensure well-conditioned covariance matrices, followed by an adaptive UCB phase. In the UCB phase, item parameters (α_j, β_j) are estimated via MLE, and upper confidence bounds on per-item satisfaction are constructed as A'(θ̂_{t,j}^T z + ξ·||z||_{(V_j^{(t)})^{-1}}), where z = (position, X_t) is the augmented feature. The key technical insight is that maximizing the UCB over all K-permutations from N items can be reformulated as a maximum weight bipartite matching problem on a graph with N item nodes and K position nodes, where edge weights are g_k(μ̂^U_{t,j}(k, X_t)); this is solved in O(K² log K) time via the Hungarian algorithm, simultaneously determining both the retrieved set and the optimal ordering. The resulting cumulative regret bound is Õ(d√(NKT)), which is minimax-optimal in T and avoids the exponential dependence on the permutation space size C(N,K)·K!.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is a cumulative regret bound of Õ(d√(NKT)) for ranking K items from N candidates in a d-dimensional context space over T rounds (Theorem 4.1), which is minimax optimal in T and avoids the exponential dependence on the action space size C(N,K). In the special case N=K, the bound simplifies to Õ(d√T) (Corollary 4.2). Empirically, UCR consistently achieves lower cumulative regret than the G-MLE baseline across all simulated settings (N=7,K=5; N=10,K=5; N=K=5, over T=500 rounds) and lower relative regret on a real-world e-commerce click-through-rate dataset (13,717 samples, 436 items, K=3, over T=100 rounds).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is G-MLE (greedy Maximum Likelihood Estimation), which ranks items by their point-estimate MLE satisfaction scores without any exploration bonus. UCR outperforms G-MLE in every experimental setting: all three simulated configurations (N=7/K=5, N=10/K=5, N=K=5) and the real-world e-commerce CTR task. The advantage is most pronounced with well-chosen UCB tuning parameter ξ; with poorly chosen ξ (e.g., ξ=0.5 on the real-world data), UCR's regret curve approaches that of G-MLE at larger iterations, though it still maintains a slight edge. The paper notes that UCR's robustness to ξ variation is a practical advantage, as the algorithm outperforms G-MLE across all reasonably chosen ξ values. No other baselines (e.g., Thompson sampling, cascade bandits, or position-based model bandits) are included in the empirical comparison, which limits the breadth of the empirical validation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contextual bandit framework of Li et al. (2017) and the combinatorial bandit literature (Li et al., 2016) to the joint select-and-rank problem with heterogeneous position effects. Prior ranking bandit work (cascade models, position-based models) assumes homogeneous position bias across items and users, making optimal ranking trivial (sort by attractiveness); here, item-specific position parameters α_j break that structure. The key technical departure is reformulating the NP-hard ranking optimization over K! permutations as a bipartite maximum weight imperfect matching solvable in polynomial time via the Hungarian algorithm, enabled by the generalized additive reward structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(d√(NKT)) regret bound is minimax optimal in T and crucially avoids the exponential dependence on the action space cardinality (N!/(N-K)!), making adaptive ranking tractable where direct UCB or Thompson sampling over all permutations would be infeasible. The bipartite matching reformulation is a practical algorithmic contribution that could be adopted in production ranking systems. Limitations include the GLM assumption on individual item responses, the additive (or generalized additive) reward structure that may not capture inter-item interference, and the need for a random initialization phase T₀. Open questions include scalability to very large N, handling non-additive list-level rewards, and robustness to distributional shift beyond the i.i.d. context assumption.
