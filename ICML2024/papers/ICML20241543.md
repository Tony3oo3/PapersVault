## Problem

While transformers are known to encode causal structure (e.g., induction heads for Markov chains), the mechanism by which gradient descent discovers and encodes such structure during training from scratch remains unexplained. This paper asks: how do transformers learn causal structure with gradient descent, and can we prove that the learned attention weights correspond to the underlying causal graph?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241543/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a token sequence s_{1:T} of length T over an alphabet of size S, generated by sampling a random transition matrix π from a prior P_π and then drawing tokens according to a fixed but unknown directed acyclic graph G (each token depends only on its parent in G). The output is a probability distribution over the next token s_{T+1}. The transformer must estimate the in-context transition π(·|s_T) purely from the sequence, without knowing G or π a priori. The analysis assumes T ≫ S and that a non-vanishing fraction of nodes have parents.

## Method

The paper analyzes stage-wise gradient descent on a reduced two-layer disentangled transformer where only the position-position block A^(1) and the token-token block A^(2) are trainable. The key insight is that the gradient of A^(1) with respect to the cross-entropy loss computes the χ²-mutual information between pairs of tokens; by the data processing inequality, this mutual information is maximized along edges of the latent causal graph, so gradient descent drives A^(1) toward the adjacency matrix of G. In stage 2, A^(2) grows in the direction I_S − (1/S)·11ᵀ, making the second attention layer perform a hard comparison: the last token attends to all prior tokens whose parent token matches s_T, yielding the empirical transition estimate. The main theorem (Theorem 4.4) provides an end-to-end guarantee that the population loss converges to the Bayes-optimal entropy at rate O(log T / T_eff^{cγ}), and the learned attention pattern satisfies S(Â^(1))_{i,p(i)} ≥ 1 − O(1/T) for all non-root nodes.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4.4) shows that stage-wise gradient descent on a reduced two-layer disentangled transformer achieves population loss L(θ̂) − L* ≲ log(T)/T_eff^(cγ) while the first attention layer converges to the causal graph adjacency matrix, i.e. S(Â⁽¹⁾)_{i,p(i)} ≥ 1 − O(1/T) for all non-root nodes. Theorem 4.5 further provides an out-of-distribution generalization guarantee: for any transition matrix π̃ with lower-bounded entries, the trained model's prediction error is bounded by ≲ log(T)/T_eff^(cγ). Empirically, across 20 randomly generated causal graphs (T=20, S=3), the average first-layer attention weight from each node to its true parent (avgattn) achieves a mean of 0.837 with standard deviation 0.054, confirming that gradient descent recovers the latent causal structure.

This is primarily a theoretical paper and does not run head-to-head comparisons against competing methods; the 'compares_to' list is empty. Instead, the paper's empirical validation is a self-consistency check: the theoretical prediction that gradient descent encodes the causal graph adjacency matrix in the first attention layer is confirmed by training disentangled transformers on Task 2.4 and observing that the position-position block of Â⁽¹⁾ converges to the true adjacency matrix (Figures 2, 4, 7). Relative to prior work, the paper extends Bietti et al. (2023) and Edelman et al. (2024), who studied induction head formation on Markov chains, to arbitrary tree-structured causal graphs and provides an end-to-end population loss guarantee rather than a few-step analysis. It also generalizes the in-context learning setup of Garg et al. (2022) from a fixed pairing structure to arbitrary causal DAGs, and unlike prior works on transformer representational capacity (Akyürek et al., Bai et al., Von Oswald et al.), it proves that gradient descent actually learns the relevant construction from scratch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the induction head framework (Olsson et al., 2022) from Markov chains to arbitrary causal graphs, and generalizes the in-context function learning task of Garg et al. (2022) to a unified 'random sequences with causal structure' formulation. It uses the disentangled transformer architecture of Friedman et al. (2023) as its analytical vehicle, and departs from prior training-dynamics work (Lu et al., 2021; Snell et al., 2021; Bietti et al., 2023) by analyzing a two-layer model rather than a single attention head, and by assuming T ≫ S rather than S ≫ T. The key conceptual departure is showing that the gradient of the first attention layer computes χ²-mutual information between token pairs, so the data processing inequality provides a natural mechanism for causal edge recovery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end theoretical guarantee that gradient descent on a (simplified) two-layer transformer recovers latent causal structure, connecting optimization dynamics to information-theoretic principles in a clean way. The χ²-mutual-information interpretation of the attention gradient is a reusable analytical tool that could inform understanding of more complex transformer circuits. Open questions include: whether the multi-head construction for non-tree graphs (Section 6) can be proven to emerge from gradient descent rather than being hand-designed, whether the results extend to deeper or standard (non-disentangled) architectures, and whether the T ≫ S assumption is fundamental or merely a technical convenience.
