## Problem

LLMs remain weak at multi-step mathematical reasoning, and the most popular math instruction-tuning datasets (GSM8K, MATH) contain only ~7.5K training examples each, severely limiting the scale of supervised fine-tuning. Existing data-augmentation approaches (WizardMath, MetaMath) are inherently bounded by the number of manually designed operations applied to original examples, capping how much new data can be generated. Additionally, mathematical reasoning evaluation is fragmented across datasets with inconsistent protocols and a clear gap between elementary and competition-level problems.

## Input / Output

The MathScale pipeline consumes ~20K seed math word problems (text) from MWPBENCH's training set and produces 2 million synthetic question-answer pairs (MathScaleQA), where each answer is a step-by-step natural-language CoT solution ending with a final numeric or symbolic answer. All training examples are formatted in the Alpaca instruction template. At inference, the fine-tuned model receives a single math word problem as input and generates a full reasoning chain as output, evaluated via greedy decoding with fuzzy-match answer extraction.

## Method

MathScale is a three-stage synthetic data generation pipeline inspired by Tall's (2013) cognitive theory of mathematical learning. First, GPT-3.5 extracts high-level topics (~2K) and fine-grained knowledge points (~8K) from seed questions, decoupling the process from concrete problem instances (concept compression). Second, a concept graph is built with topics and KPs as nodes and log-smoothed co-occurrence counts as edge weights; a multi-step random walk over the topic, topic-KP, and KP sub-graphs samples diverse concept compositions (connection forging). Third, GPT-3.5 is prompted to generate new math questions conditioned on each sampled composition, with few-shot exemplars selected by Jaccard distance over knowledge-point sets. Because the number of possible concept combinations grows combinatorially with graph size rather than being bounded by a fixed set of augmentation operations, the pipeline scales to 2M unique examples, yielding near-logarithmic performance gains when fine-tuning LLaMA-2 or Mistral backbones.

## Results

On MWPBENCH (9 math word problem datasets spanning elementary to college level), MathScale-7B achieves 35.2% micro-average and 38.2% macro-average accuracy, surpassing the best same-size open-source baseline (MetaMath-7B at 24.7%/26.6%) by 42.8% and 43.6% respectively. MathScale-Mistral-7B reaches 39.1% micro and 41.7% macro, approaching GPT-3.5-Turbo (39.9%/41.9%). MathScale-13B achieves 37.2% micro and 39.6% macro, outperforming all LLaMA-2-13B-based baselines by a wide margin. On the fresh Fresh-GaokaoMath-2023 dataset (30 questions), MathScale-7B scores 30.0%, far exceeding WizardMath-7B (13.3%) and MetaMath-7B (16.6%), though still below GPT-3.5-Turbo (40.0%) and GPT-4 (43.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MathScale-7B decisively outperforms all LLaMA-2-7B-based baselines: it beats MetaMath (24.7%→35.2% micro, +42.5%), GAIR-Abel (18.7%→35.2%, +88%), WizardMath (16.3%→35.2%, +116%), and MAmmoTH-CoT (15.8%→35.2%, +123%) in micro-average accuracy. Under matched training size (96.5K examples), MathScale-7B achieves 22.7% micro vs. WizardMath's 16.3%; at ~300K examples, MathScale-7B reaches 27.1% micro vs. MetaMath's 24.7% at 395K, winning on 8 of 9 datasets despite using fewer examples. MathScale-Mistral-7B (39.1% micro) surpasses WizardMath v1.1 (35.5%) and MetaMath Mistral (32.9%), and nearly matches GPT-3.5-Turbo (39.9%) in micro-average. On the out-of-domain Fresh-GaokaoMath-2023 test, MathScale-7B (30.0%) more than doubles WizardMath-7B (13.3%) and MetaMath-7B (16.6%), confirming generalization beyond the training distribution. The gap to GPT-4 (52.0% micro) remains substantial, indicating room for further scaling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant augmentation-based paradigm for synthetic math data (WizardMath's evol-instruct operations, MetaMath's answer/question rephrasing) by introducing a concept-graph sampling strategy that decouples generation from specific seed questions. The method draws an explicit analogy to Tall's (2013) cognitive framework of concept compression and connection forging, repurposing graph random walks as a mechanism for composing novel topic–knowledge-point combinations. On the evaluation side, it extends the narrow GSM8K/MATH evaluation convention into a unified 9-dataset benchmark (MWPBENCH) that spans elementary through college-level problems, filling a previously unaddressed gap at the college tier with the new CollegeMath dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MWPBENCH and the 2M-example MathScaleQA dataset are immediately useful resources for the community, and the demonstrated logarithmic scaling curve suggests further gains are possible with more synthetic data. A notable open question is whether the approach transfers to GPT-4-based generation or to larger backbones (70B+), both of which the authors defer to future work. The finding that GPT-4 validation of GPT-3.5 solutions does not improve (and slightly degrades) downstream performance is counterintuitive but rests on a small 5K-example ablation, leaving the distillation-vs-accuracy tradeoff underexplored. Tool-integrated reasoning (e.g., code execution) is explicitly out of scope, limiting the method's applicability to problems requiring exact computation.
