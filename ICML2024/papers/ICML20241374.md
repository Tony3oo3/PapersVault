## Problem

Outliers in vision transformer activation distributions cause severe accuracy degradation under post-training quantization, yet existing PTQ methods focus on quantization functions or pre-training modifications while overlooking how the reconstruction granularity (number of jointly optimized blocks) interacts with these outliers. The paper identifies that the optimal granularity is architecture-dependent and governed by a property it terms 'transformer-like topological homogeneity,' which had not been previously analyzed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241374/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained vision transformer (ViT, DeiT, or Swin variants) and a small calibration set of 16 batches × 64 samples from ImageNet training data. It produces a low-bit (4-bit or 6-bit) quantized model with both weights and activations quantized, which then outputs standard ImageNet class labels. The granularity decision is architecture-driven: ViT/DeiT use a 3-block joint optimization scheme, while Swin uses per-module (self-attention, out-projection, MLP) optimization within each block.

## Method

The paper proves (Theorem 3.2) that under transformer-like topological homogeneity, coarser reconstruction granularity (jointly optimizing more consecutive blocks) yields strictly smaller quantization loss, and validates this empirically via sampling experiments. It then derives two rules: for homogeneous architectures (ViT, DeiT), a 3-block combination is optimal; for Swin, where downsample blocks break homogeneity and cause cross-stage gradient interference (shown via explicit gradient decomposition in Eq. 4 vs. Eq. 5), the finest granularity—optimizing three internal modules (self-attention, out-projection, MLP) independently—is preferred. The optimization itself combines Adaround-style adaptive weight rounding with QDrop-style random activation dropping, using STE for backpropagation through the non-differentiable rounding operation, with 20,000 iterations per block at a 50% activation drop probability.

## Results

On ImageNet with 4/4-bit quantization, the method achieves Top-1 accuracy of 72.88% on ViT-Small, 76.59% on ViT-Base, 66.31% on DeiT-Tiny, and 82.46% on Swin-Base, consistently outperforming all baselines by 4–9 percentage points. At 6/6-bit quantization, gains narrow but remain positive: 80.60% on ViT-Small, 83.81% on ViT-Base, 71.52% on DeiT-Tiny, and 84.91% on Swin-Base. The largest absolute improvements occur at 4-bit, where the method's outlier-aware granularity strategy most effectively mitigates the limited quantization fixed points. On the LLM extension (w2a16g128 on LLaMA-7B), the method combined with OmniQuant reduces WikiText2 perplexity from 11.06 (OmniQuant alone) to 8.80.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method decisively outperforms RepQ-ViT across all seven ViT/DeiT/Swin variants at 4/4-bit, with margins ranging from +3.22% (DeiT-B: 78.83 vs 75.61) to +8.88% (DeiT-Tiny: 66.31 vs 57.43), and maintains a consistent +0.17 to +0.34% lead at 6/6-bit. Against NoisyQuant (6/6-bit only), the method wins on six of seven models (e.g., ViT-S: 80.60 vs 78.65; Swin-B: 84.91 vs 84.68) but trails by a narrow 0.10% on Swin-Small (82.76 vs 82.86). APQ-ViT and PTQ4ViT are dominated by large margins at both bit widths, with PTQ4ViT particularly weak at 4-bit (e.g., 30.69% on ViT-B vs 76.59% for Ours). FQ-ViT is essentially non-functional at 4-bit (0.1% across all models) and remains competitive only on DeiT-Tiny at 6-bit (58.66%). PSAQ-ViT and Ranking are outperformed on all models where they report results at 6/6-bit. The paper's key claim is that the granularity-aware approach yields the largest gains precisely in the low-bit regime where outliers are most damaging, explaining the widening gap between 4-bit and 6-bit improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reconstruction granularity concept from MRECG (Ma et al., 2023), which addressed loss oscillations in CNNs, to vision transformers where the topological homogeneity assumption breaks down. It builds on the optimization machinery of Adaround (adaptive rounding) and QDrop (random activation dropping) for the reconstruction step, and generalizes BRECQ's block-by-block optimization to multi-block combinations. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of 'transformer-like topological homogeneity' as a criterion that determines whether coarser or finer granularity is optimal, revealing that Swin's multi-stage architecture with downsampling blocks violates this condition and thus requires the opposite granularity strategy from ViT/DeiT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The architecture-dependent granularity rule (coarse for homogeneous stacks, fine for heterogeneous multi-stage models) provides a principled, theoretically grounded decision procedure for PTQ practitioners rather than relying on ad-hoc hyperparameter tuning. The strong 4-bit results (e.g., 82.24% on Swin-B, 80.50% on ViT-S) demonstrate that granularity selection alone can close much of the gap to FP32 without architectural modifications. Limitations include the search space being restricted to at most 3 consecutive blocks, evaluation limited to ImageNet classification, and the LLM extension (Appendix E) being preliminary. Open questions include whether the granularity rules generalize to detection/segmentation transformers, to models with heterogeneous attention patterns (e.g., Mamba-based), and whether the approach composes with emerging outlier-suppression techniques like SmoothQuant.
