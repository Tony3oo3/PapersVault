## Problem

Sparse phase retrieval algorithms suffer from a statistical-to-computational gap: the information-theoretic sampling bound is m = Ω(k log n), yet practical initialization methods require m = Ω(k² log n) or, at best, m = Ω(max{k/s log n, √(k/s) log³ n}) as in the truncated power (TP) method. The log³ n term in TP arises from truncation arguments needed to handle the heavy-tailed (fourth-power Gaussian) spectrum, and no prior method eliminates it while avoiding hyperparameter tuning.

## Input / Output

ESP takes m phaseless measurements y_j = |a_j^* x| with complex Gaussian sampling vectors a_j ∈ C^n, the sampling matrix A, and the sparsity level k as input. It outputs a complex signal estimate z ∈ C^n rescaled to ||z|| = λ (where λ² = (1/m)Σy_j²), guaranteed to lie within a δ-neighborhood of x under the sampling condition m = Ω((||x||²/|x_max|²) k log n). Unlike TP, ESP requires no hyperparameters (η₁, η₂) for spectrum truncation, simplifying implementation.

## Method

ESP constructs an exponential-based spectrum L = (1/m)Σ(1/2 − exp(−y_j²/λ²)) a_j a_j^* and performs three sequential steps: (1) diagonal search to identify i_max as the index of the largest diagonal entry of L, (2) support recovery by selecting the k most significant entries in the i_max-th column of L, and (3) signal estimation via the principal eigenvector of the submatrix L_S rescaled to norm λ. The key insight is that the exponential kernel (1/2 − exp(−y_j²/λ²)) has a sub-exponential tail, enabling direct Bernstein-type concentration inequalities without the truncation arguments that force the log³ n overhead in TP. For theoretical analysis, a proxy spectrum L̃ (replacing λ² with ||x||² in the exponent) is introduced, whose expectation has the closed form xx*/(4||x||²), and the gap between L and L̃ is controlled via Lagrange mean-value bounds on the exponential difference.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical contribution is a sampling complexity bound of m = Ω((‖x‖²/|x_max|²) k log n) for δ-neighborhood initialization, eliminating the log³(n) factor present in the prior state-of-the-art TP method. Empirically, at a 30% sampling ratio (n=1000, k=10), ESP recovers 40% of support indices versus 24% for TP, 18% for SPARTA, and 9% for TP with unoptimized hyperparameters. In the phase transition plot (n=1000, m=1000, k=30), ESP achieves a noticeably higher successful recovery rate than TP, while the two methods perform comparably at very low sparsity (k=10). For sparse 0-1 signals where all nonzeros have equal magnitude, ESP's advantage over TP narrows but remains present in the high sampling-ratio regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESP consistently outperforms TP (Cai et al., 2022c) across all tested settings, with the largest gap appearing when the signal has non-uniform nonzero magnitudes (s = 1/√k) and at moderate sparsity levels in the phase transition plot. Against SPARTA (Wang et al., 2018) and CoPRAM (Jagatap & Hegde, 2019), ESP achieves substantially higher support recovery fractions (40% vs. ~18% at 30% sampling ratio) and lower relative error, though the gap narrows for sparse 0-1 signals where all nonzeros share the same magnitude. TP with unoptimized hyperparameters (TP-UD) performs worst among all methods, recovering only 9% support at 30% sampling ratio, highlighting the sensitivity of TP to its truncation parameters. The key theoretical advantage of ESP over TP is the elimination of the log³(n) term in the sampling complexity, which translates to fewer required samples when k < s·log⁴(n), i.e., in the highly sparse regime. ESP also requires no hyperparameter tuning (unlike TP's η₁, η₂), making it easier to deploy in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the exponential spectrum originally developed by Gao & Xu (2017) for generic (non-sparse) phase retrieval to the sparse setting, repurposing it as the core of a three-step initialization algorithm. It directly builds on the truncated power (TP) method of Cai et al. (2022c), which was the state-of-the-art for sparse phase retrieval initialization, and on the Hadamard WF of Wu & Rebeschini (2021). The key conceptual departure is replacing the heavy-tailed fourth-power spectrum (used by TP, SPARTA, CoPRAM) with a sub-exponential-tailed exponential spectrum, which eliminates the truncation argument and the resulting log³ n overhead in the sampling complexity bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ESP closes part of the statistical-to-computational gap in sparse phase retrieval by matching the information-theoretic Ω(k log n) bound when the signal has a dominant entry (|x_max| ~ ‖x‖), without requiring hyperparameter tuning. However, the gap is not fully closed: the bound still depends on the ratio ‖x‖²/|x_max|², and no practical algorithm is known to achieve the information-theoretic bound for arbitrary sparse signals. The paper also notes that the initialization error δ must be small enough (e.g., δ < 1/10 for SPARTA) for the subsequent iterative stage to succeed, and the dependence on δ⁻² in the sample complexity remains a practical consideration. Whether a polynomial-time algorithm can fully bridge the gap for all sparse signals remains an open question.
