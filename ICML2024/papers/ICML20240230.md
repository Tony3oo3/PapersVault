## Problem

Existing Large Multi-modality Models (LMMs) perform planning exclusively in language space, analogous to only using the left hemisphere of the brain. They lack vision-based associative reasoning—the ability to imagine and reason about future visual states—which limits their performance on tasks requiring prediction of subsequent events or spatial understanding beyond what text descriptions can capture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240230/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts text queries, single images, or video clips and internally ensures both a vision input I_O and a language input T_O are available (converting via L2V or V2L models when one modality is missing). The vision planning branch generates 1–3 future frames (via Stable Video Diffusion, DMVFN, or MCVD), while the language planning branch produces exactly 3 sub-questions. Final outputs are task-specific: multiple-choice answers for video Q&A (STAR, NExT-QA), free-form captions for BDD-X, continuous course/speed control signals for driving, and 3D gripper trajectories (X, Y, Z) for robotics.

## Method

VLP runs two parallel planning branches before a final LMM decision. The Vision Planning branch uses a video diffusion model (VPG) to generate future frames, then a two-stage Vision Planning Selector: a Coarse Selector (ChatGPT prompted with a yes/no question) decides whether future frames are relevant at all, and a Fine Selector (BLIP-2 with CLIP visual features and a Q-former text query) scores each frame and retains the top-K most informative ones. The Language Planning branch applies zero-shot chain-of-thought via ChatGPT to decompose the query into three sub-questions. The Decision Maker (LLAVA or GPT-4V) then performs a multi-round conversation: vanilla answering, sequential language-plan answering, vision-plan answering, and a final voting step where the LMM re-evaluates the vanilla answer against each plan-based answer before committing to the ultimate output.

## Results

On the STAR video Q&A benchmark, VLP achieves 50.5% average accuracy (using 4 input frames + 1 generated frame), outperforming the strongest LMM baseline LLAVA (47.4%) by 3.1 points and SEVILA (44.6%) by 5.9 points. On NExT-QA, VLP reaches 64.7% average accuracy, surpassing SEVILA (63.6%) and BLIP-2 voting (62.7%). On the BDD-X video captioning task, VLP attains BLEU-4 of 35.7, CIDEr of 256.7, and METEOR of 31.1, exceeding the prior state-of-the-art ADAPT (34.6 / 247.5 / 30.6). On BAIR robot gripper trajectory prediction, adding vision planning reduces RMSE from 19.85 cm to 19.36 cm (2+0 setting) and from 14.39 cm to 13.85 cm (1+2 planning setting).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLP outperforms all compared baselines across every evaluated benchmark. On STAR, VLP (50.5% avg.) beats the closest LMM-based competitor LLAVA (47.4%) by 3.1 points, SEVILA (44.6%) by 5.9 points, and BLIP-2 concat (42.2%) by 8.3 points; the gap is largest on the Prediction sub-category (50.8% vs. 45.5% for LLAVA), where vision planning is most beneficial. On NExT-QA, VLP (64.7% avg.) surpasses SEVILA (63.6%) by 1.1 points and BLIP-2 voting (62.7%) by 2.0 points, while substantially outperforming InternVideo (49.1%) and VFC (51.5%). On BDD-X video captioning, VLP improves BLEU-4 over ADAPT (35.7 vs. 34.6), SAA (31.8), WAA (32.3), and S2VT (30.2), with the largest CIDEr gain over ADAPT (256.7 vs. 247.5). On BAIR, vision planning consistently reduces gripper trajectory RMSE by 0.4–0.5 cm across all prediction and planning settings. The only notable caveat is that VLP's NExT-QA Temporal score (60.5) is slightly below SEVILA (61.3) and BLIP-2 voting (59.1 is actually lower), indicating the advantage is most pronounced on causal and descriptive questions rather than purely temporal ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LMM pipeline (LLaVA, BLIP-2, Flamingo) by adding a parallel vision planning branch that uses video diffusion models (Stable Video Diffusion, DMVFN, MCVD) to generate future frames, complementing the existing language planning (zero-shot CoT via ChatGPT) that prior LMMs already employ. It departs from prior robotics-focused video planning work (Du et al. 2023a,b; Ajay et al. 2023) by generalizing the concept of vision planning to open-domain video Q&A, captioning, and action prediction tasks. The framework is modular, plugging into existing decision makers (LLaVA, GPT-4V, BERT/ADAPT) rather than proposing a new end-to-end architecture, and introduces a Coarse/Fine Selector mechanism to filter generated frames before they reach the LMM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that combining vision and language planning yields consistent but modest gains (≈3 points on STAR, ≈1 BLEU on BDD-X) over strong LMM baselines, validating the 'two-hemisphere' cognitive analogy. However, the central limitation is stark: ground-truth future frames dramatically outperform generated ones (57.5 vs 50.0 on STAR Prediction), revealing that current video generation quality is the true bottleneck, not the planning architecture. Inference cost (60+ seconds per video generation) also limits practical deployment. The framework's value will likely grow substantially as video generation models mature, making it a useful template for future work once that bottleneck is resolved.
