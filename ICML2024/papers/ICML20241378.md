## Problem

Decision-focused learning has shown superior performance over prediction-focused approaches, but it remains unknown how to embed distributionally robust optimization (DRO) as a differentiable layer—specifically, how to differentiate decisions with respect to an ambiguity set. Additionally, existing differentiable optimization layers handle either purely continuous or purely discrete decisions, leaving the differentiation of mixed-integer decisions for generic mixed-integer convex optimization unsolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241378/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a covariate vector z (contextual information) and a dataset of historical (z_n, y_n) pairs. The learning model M_φ(z) outputs a finite-dimensional parameter θ that defines a parameterized second-order cone ambiguity set (e.g., mean and variance parameters). A projection layer then maps θ to θ^proj to guarantee the ambiguity set is non-empty by enforcing that a weighted empirical conditional distribution Q_z lies within it. The DRO layer ultimately produces a mixed-integer decision (x_d, x_c) where x_d ∈ {0,1}^{n₁} and x_c ∈ ℝ^{n₂}.

## Method

The core contribution is a dual-view methodology that handles mixed-integer decisions via two distinct principles: the continuous part x_c is differentiated through the cone program using implicit differentiation (via cvxpylayers), while the discrete part x_d is treated as a constraint in the bilevel learning problem and handled through an energy-based surrogate. Specifically, each feasible integer solution x_d is assigned an energy E(x_d, θ, λ) = exp(−f(x_d, x_c*(x_d,θ), θ)/λ), inducing a Boltzmann distribution p(x_d|θ,λ) over integer solutions. The surrogate value function R_λ(φ) is the expectation of the decision loss under this distribution, and its gradient is estimated via self-normalized importance sampling from a proposal distribution built from the T best integer solutions obtained by successive cutting-plane solves. The paper proves that R_λ(φ) epi-converges to the true value function R(φ) as λ→0⁺, and that the surrogate is differentiable, enabling end-to-end gradient-based training of the learning model.

## Results

On the 40-dimensional continuous portfolio management problem, the proposed decision-focused learning (DFL) method achieves 0.289 average percentage profit, outperforming both prediction-focused learning (PFL, 0.082) and the method of Costa & Iyengar (2023, 0.268). On mixed-integer portfolio management problems, DFL achieves 0.1561, 0.1763, and 0.2145 average percentage profit at dimensions 20, 40, and 60 respectively, yielding improvements of 167%, 178%, and 347% over PFL. On the multi-item newsvendor problem, DFL demonstrates average improvements of 21.4%, 18.7%, and 18.1% over the prediction-focused benchmark across three SOC ambiguity set types.

|---|---|---|---|

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary external baseline is Costa & Iyengar (2023), the only prior work on distributionally robust decision-focused learning. On the 40-dimensional continuous portfolio problem, the proposed DFL method (0.289) outperforms Costa & Iyengar (0.268) by approximately 7.8% in average percentage profit, attributed to the fact that Costa & Iyengar presumes a restrictive residual structure on the uncertainty distribution while the proposed method applies to general distributions. The internal comparison between DFL and PFL is the main methodological evaluation: DFL consistently dominates PFL across all problem settings, with the gap widening as problem dimension increases (from 167% improvement at 20 dimensions to 347% at 60 dimensions) and as the ambiguity set becomes more complex (from 113% with 15 SOC constraints to 347% with 60 constraints). On the newsvendor problem, DFL also outperforms the prediction-focused benchmark (which uses full knowledge of the conditional distribution) by 18–21% across three ambiguity set types, demonstrating that directly optimizing decision loss yields better decisions than optimizing prediction accuracy even with oracle-level distributional knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the differentiable optimization layer paradigm (Cvxpylayers, Agrawal et al. 2019a; cone program differentiation, Agrawal et al. 2019b) to the distributionally robust optimization setting, building on the parameterized SOC ambiguity set formulation from Bertsimas et al. (2019) and the conditional distribution construction from Bertsimas & Kallus (2020). It departs from prior differentiable optimization layers that handle only pure continuous or pure discrete decisions by introducing a dual-view methodology that treats continuous and integer components under distinct principles (automatic differentiation vs. constrained optimization). The closest prior work, Costa & Iyengar (2023), addresses DRO decision-focused learning but is restricted to continuous decisions in specific financial problems with a residual distributional structure, whereas this paper handles general distributions and mixed-integer decisions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first generic differentiable DRO layer supporting mixed-integer decisions, filling a gap that the authors explicitly identify as unsolved in the decision-focused learning literature. The dual-view methodology is stated to be applicable to any mixed-integer convex optimization layer, not just DRO, which broadens its potential impact. However, scalability remains a significant limitation: the pipeline relies on CPU-based solvers (Gurobi, Cvxpylayers), and the authors acknowledge that GPU acceleration would be needed for large-scale problems. Open questions include whether the energy-based surrogate with importance sampling remains tractable as the number of binary variables grows, and whether the epi-convergence guarantees translate into practical convergence rates for deep learning models.
