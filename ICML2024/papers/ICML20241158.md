## Problem

Existing adversarial robustness benchmarks such as RobustBench evaluate only in-distribution (ID) generalization, leaving it unknown how robustness transfers when the test distribution differs from training. This paper addresses three questions: how resilient are current robust models to distribution shift, can OOD robustness be predicted from ID robustness, and what methods can achieve OOD robustness beyond the limits of existing approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241158/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark evaluates models on RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10, 224×224 ImageNet) under 80 adversarial evaluations per model: 79 dataset-shift subsets (4 natural variants + 15 corruption types at 5 severity levels) plus 1 ID baseline, and 6 unforeseen threat-model attacks (larger ε, different p-norm, PPGD, LPA, ReColor, StAdv). The output is per-shift adversarial robustness (accuracy under attack) and an aggregated OOD score averaging dataset-shift and threat-shift results, with the MM5 minimum-margin attack used for evaluation (~32× faster than AutoAttack with comparable accuracy).

## Method

OODRobustBench constructs a two-axis evaluation framework: dataset shift (OOD_d) covering 23 naturalistic input distribution changes and threat shift (OOD_t) covering 6 unforeseen adversarial attacks. The paper performs a large-scale analysis of 706 robust models (60.7K adversarial evaluations) spanning diverse architectures, training methods, and set-ups, discovering a strong linear correlation between ID and OOD robustness that enables OOD prediction from ID performance, including in an unsupervised manner. By extrapolating this linear trend, the authors predict upper bounds on achievable OOD robustness (e.g., only 43% on ImageNet ℓ∞ under dataset shift), demonstrating that existing methods are unlikely to achieve high OOD robustness. They then systematically evaluate data augmentation, model scaling, unsupervised representation learning, and various adversarial training methods, identifying specific AT approaches (Hypersphere Embedding, MMA, Adaptive Smoothing, Variation Regularization) as the most promising for exceeding the predicted limits.

## Results

Across 706 robust models and 60.7K adversarial evaluations, OODRobustBench reveals that adversarial robustness degrades on average by 18% (CIFAR-10 ℓ∞), 31% (CIFAR-10 ℓ₂), and 24% (ImageNet ℓ∞) under distribution shifts. The predicted upper bound of OOD robustness under dataset shift is only 66%/71%/43% for CIFAR-10 ℓ∞, CIFAR-10 ℓ₂, and ImageNet ℓ∞ respectively, indicating existing methods are unlikely to achieve high OOD robustness. ID and OOD robustness exhibit strong linear correlation (R² > 0.9 for most shifts), enabling OOD prediction from ID performance, but the slope is low (e.g., ~0.1 for fog on ImageNet), meaning 10% ID improvement yields only ~1% OOD improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark reveals significant ranking discrepancies between ID and OOD robustness: AS (Bai et al., 2023) overtakes BDM (Wang et al., 2023) from 2nd to 1st in OOD ranking due to superior threat-shift robustness (46.7% vs 35.8%), while HAT (Rade & Moosavi-Dezfooli, 2022) catastrophically drops from 22nd to 57th due to a 46% robustness collapse under Gaussian noise. HE (Pang et al., 2020) jumps from 70th to 3rd in OOD ranking, achieving 46.2% OOD_t robustness despite only 53.8% ID robustness, making it the strongest threat-shift defense. Among adversarial training methods, VR (Dai et al., 2022), HE (Pang et al., 2020), MMA (Ding et al., 2020), and AS (Bai et al., 2023) achieve adversarial effective robustness (AER) of 1.7%, 16.22%, 10.74%, and 9.41% respectively under threat shift, far exceeding the PGD baseline (0.2%). OOD generalization methods (PLAT, CARD-Deck) and the combined MSD+REx approach provide no meaningful adversarial robustness gain, with PLAT and CARD-Deck achieving near-zero OOD adversarial accuracy. On ImageNet ℓ∞, the top model (Comprehensive/Swin-L, Liu et al., 2023) achieves only 26.74% OOD robustness, confirming the severe OOD generalization gap at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends RobustBench (Croce et al., 2021), which evaluates in-distribution adversarial robustness, to the out-of-distribution setting by introducing OODRobustBench with 23 dataset shifts and 6 threat shifts. It builds on the corruption benchmark (Hendrycks & Dietterich, 2019) for corruption-based shifts and adopts MM5 (Gao et al., 2022) for efficient large-scale adversarial evaluation. Conceptually, it extends the linear-correlation finding of Miller et al. (2021) from OOD accuracy to OOD adversarial robustness, and complements prior small-scale studies of unforeseen-attack robustness (Laidlaw et al., 2021; Dai et al., 2022) with a systematic 706-model analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that adversarial robustness degrades by 18–31% under distribution shift and that the predicted upper bound of OOD robustness from existing ℓp-based methods is only ~43% on ImageNet, implying that current approaches are fundamentally insufficient for real-world deployment. The finding that higher ID robustness paradoxically leads to greater OOD degradation challenges the assumption that continued ID improvements will naturally yield OOD robustness. Key open questions include: how to design training methods that break the observed linear trend and exceed the predicted ceiling, how to effectively combine adversarial training with OOD generalization techniques (the tested MSD+REx combination actually impaired performance), and whether the catastrophic noise-shift degradation observed in specific implementations (e.g., HAT) reflects a broader vulnerability class.
