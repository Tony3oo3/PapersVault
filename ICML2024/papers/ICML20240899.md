## Problem

Existing generative models on Riemannian manifolds either rely on intractable heat kernel approximations (diffusion/score-based models) or require expensive Riemannian divergence computation (CNF models), both of which restrict applicability to simple geometries and hinder scalability to high dimensions. Additionally, previous diffusion models lack geometric interpretability since their generative processes are parameterized by score functions without explicit geometric meaning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240899/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a random sample from a prior distribution on the manifold (typically uniform) along with precomputed manifold geometry: either the Riemannian metric for geodesic/exponential map computation (Logarithm Bridge) or the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of the Laplace-Beltrami operator for spectral distance (Spectral Bridge). It produces generated samples on the manifold via either stochastic SDE simulation or deterministic probability flow ODE integration, and computes log-likelihood estimates through the probability flow. The eigenfunctions are computed once before training and do not require on-the-fly computation during training or sampling.

## Method

The paper constructs a generative diffusion process as a mixture of bridge processes (diffusion conditioned to fixed endpoints) on the manifold, avoiding heat kernel estimation entirely. The bridge drift is designed as a weighted mean of tangent vectors pointing toward data points via the logarithm map (Logarithm Bridge) or spectral distance gradient (Spectral Bridge), giving the mixture process an explicit geometric interpretation as moving toward the most probable endpoint. Training uses a 'two-way bridge matching' objective: a simple regression on the drifts of forward and backward bridge processes that requires no divergence computation, with a two-way simulation strategy (simulating from the nearer endpoint) that avoids drift explosion near terminal time and reduces in-training simulation steps by up to 34.9×. A time-scaled importance sampling weight q(t) ∝ σ_t^{-2} stabilizes training and guarantees likelihood maximization, distinguishing it from uniform-time Flow Matching. The framework generalizes Riemannian Flow Matching as a special case (σ_t → 0) while showing that stochasticity is essential for accurate trajectory estimation on curved manifolds.

## Results

On the 2D sphere (earth/climate science), LogBM achieves the best NLL on Volcano (-9.52) and Fire (-2.47), outperforming the next-best RFM by 1.59 and 0.61 NLL respectively, while matching or slightly trailing baselines on Earthquake and Flood. On general closed manifolds (Stanford Bunny, Spot the Cow), SpecBM with only 15 in-training simulation steps outperforms RFM (300 steps) on both k=50 and k=100 eigenfunction targets, achieving a ×12.8 training speedup. On protein torus datasets, LogBM matches or marginally improves over RFM (e.g., RNA: -5.27 vs -5.20 NLL) while scaling to 10⁴ dimensions without degradation, unlike RSGM which suffers from high-variance divergence estimation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RFM (Chen & Lipman, 2024), the strongest baseline, LogBM wins decisively on Volcano (-9.52 vs -7.93) and Fire (-2.47 vs -1.86) on the sphere, and on general closed manifolds (Stanford Bunny k=100: 1.41 vs 1.53; Spot the Cow k=100: 0.97 vs 1.08) while using only 5% of the in-training simulation steps (15 vs 300). On protein tori, LogBM ties RFM on all 2D datasets and slightly edges it on 7D RNA (-5.27 vs -5.20 NLL). Against RSGM (Bortoli et al., 2022), LogBM outperforms on all four sphere datasets and scales to 10⁴ dimensions on high-dimensional tori where RSGM degrades due to stochastic divergence estimation variance. Against CNF baselines (RCNF, Moser Flow, CNFM), LogBM dominates on the sphere, with the largest gap on Volcano (-9.52 vs -2.38 for CNFM). On Earthquake and Flood, RDM and CNFM respectively achieve marginally better NLL than LogBM, though the differences are within or near statistical significance. The key differentiator is training efficiency: on general manifolds, LogBM achieves superior NLL with ×12.8 speedup over RFM, and on high-dimensional tori achieves ×5 speedup over RSGM's implicit score matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Euclidean diffusion mixture framework (Peluchetti, 2021; Liu et al., 2023) to Riemannian manifolds by constructing a generative process as a mixture of bridge processes whose drifts are designed as weighted means of tangent directions to data points. It departs from the dominant denoising paradigm of Riemannian score-based models (Bortoli et al., 2022; Huang et al., 2022), which require intractable heat kernel approximations or expensive divergence computation, and from deterministic CNF/flow-matching approaches (Mathieu & Nickel, 2020; Chen & Lipman, 2024), which also require divergence or many simulation steps. The paper formally shows that Riemannian Flow Matching (RFM) is a special case of its framework when the noise schedule vanishes, positioning the stochastic mixture process as a strict generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a divergence-free, heat-kernel-free training objective that scales to dimensions (up to 10^4) where prior manifold generative models fail, with practical applications to protein torsion angles, climate events, and molecular conformations. A key open question is scalability to complex manifolds where the logarithm map or Laplace-Beltrami eigenfunctions are expensive to compute, and the theoretical guarantees for non-compact manifolds remain incomplete. The finding that stochasticity is essential (not merely convenient) for accurate trajectory estimation on general manifolds challenges the broader trend toward deterministic flows and may influence how future manifold generative models are designed.
