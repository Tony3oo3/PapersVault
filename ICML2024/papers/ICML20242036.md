## Problem

Businesses keep model weights and training data proprietary as trade secrets, yet society demands algorithmic transparency (e.g., verifying no copyrighted content was used in training). Existing audit mechanisms—releasing data and weights, multi-party computation, or trusted third parties—are either impractical, prohibitively expensive, or require trust that contradicts the audit's purpose. Prior ZK-SNARK work handles only the forward pass of DNNs, making it impossible to prove that a model was actually trained on a specific dataset without revealing either.

## Input / Output

The model provider commits to a dataset (ordered lexicographically by hash) and to final model weights, while the architecture is public. A user submits an arbitrary computable audit function F(data, weights); the provider then returns the output of F alongside a ZK-SNARK certifying correct execution on the committed data and weights. The protocol is non-interactive: any third party can verify the proofs after the fact without further interaction, and the only information revealed beyond F's output is the model architecture.

## Method

ZKAUDIT operates in two phases: ZKAUDIT-T proves honest training by having the provider compute ZK-SNARKs of each SGD step (forward pass of frozen layers, forward and backward pass of updated layers, and weight updates) over a verified-random traversal ordering of committed data; ZKAUDIT-I proves correct execution of the user-specified audit function on the same committed weights and data. The core technical contribution is enabling full gradient descent inside ZK-SNARKs, which requires three new components: (1) a backward pass expressed as structurally similar computations to the forward pass, (2) rounded division via polynomial constraints in finite fields (improving training accuracy by up to 11% over floor division), and (3) a high-performance softmax that handles underflow, precision loss, and numeric range in fixed-point arithmetic by rescaling exponentials and subtracting the maximum before exponentiation. All operations use variable-precision fixed-point arithmetic with lookup tables for non-linearities, compiled into the halo2 AIR format with KZG commitments, achieving proving times of 2–328 s per SGD step on commodity hardware.

## Results

ZKAUDIT-T achieves near-parity accuracy with full fp32 training: dermnet 38.5% vs 39.0% (−0.5%), flowers-102 79.7% vs 80.4% (−0.7%), and cars 49.8% vs 50.4% (−0.6%). A single SGD step on MobileNet v2 (1.0, 224) is proven in 328 s (scale factor 2^15) with 10 ms verification, while the recommender system requires only 24 s per step. End-to-end audit costs are as low as $10 for a recommender censorship audit and $108 for a copyright audit on flowers-102 (~10 cents per image).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZKAUDIT is at least 95× faster than the closest prior ZK-SNARK inference method (pvCNN, 31,011 s) and up to 610× faster than Zen (200,000 s), while performing a full SGD step (forward + backward + weight update) rather than just the forward pass that all three baselines support. Critically, none of the prior methods (Zen, vCNN, pvCNN) implement the softmax or backward pass, making them infeasible for training proofs. Against the secure-training baseline of Rathee et al. (2023) (semi-honest MPC for CIFAR-10 HiNet), ZKAUDIT-T is 196× cheaper ($2,417 vs. $475,234) while additionally providing malicious-adversary security and audit capabilities that the MPC approach lacks. The paper also shows that replacing rounded division with standard integer division degrades ImageNet top-5 accuracy by up to 11.3%, underscoring that the fixed-point optimizations are essential for competitive downstream fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Kang et al. (2022), which optimized the forward pass of DNNs for ZK-SNARK-based inference, by adding the backward pass, softmax, and weight updates needed to prove full stochastic gradient descent. It departs from the broader secure ML literature (MPC, FHE, prior ZK-SNARK inference work) by being the first to handle training against malicious adversaries for realistic architectures, and from prior ZK-training work (Garg et al. 2023, Sun et al. 2023) by implementing high-precision softmax and achieving near-fp32 accuracy on ImageNet-scale models. The ZKAUDIT protocol itself is a new framing that combines commitments, verified randomness, and per-step ZK-SNARKs into a two-phase audit protocol with no trusted third party.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that trustless, non-interactive audits of ML training are technically feasible at practical cost ($10–$108 per audit), opening a path toward regulatory compliance and transparency without sacrificing trade-secret protection. Key limitations remain: the protocol does not protect against data poisoning, reveals model architecture, and has only been demonstrated on relatively small models (MobileNet v2, a simple DLRM), leaving open whether the approach scales to transformer-based or LLM-scale models. The 196× cost reduction over MPC-based secure training and the 95× speedup over prior ZK-SNARK arithmetic suggest a fundamentally more efficient route, but real-world adoption will depend on whether proving times remain tractable as model size grows.
