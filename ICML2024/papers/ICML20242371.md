## Problem

When service providers collect real user instructions to align LLMs, those instructions often contain sensitive personal information that is exposed to human annotators during the labeling process. Standard differentially private fine-tuning only protects against model memorization at deployment but offers no protection during the annotation stage. This paper is the first to identify and address the annotation-stage privacy risk in the LLM alignment pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242371/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of real user instructions (e.g., ~200K from LMSYS-Chat-1M after deduplication and filtering) and a publicly pre-trained LLM (LLaMA 7B or 13B) as inputs, and produces a set of synthetic instructions with formal (ε,δ)-DP guarantees that can replace real instructions in both supervised fine-tuning and RLHF pipelines. The synthetic instructions are generated by sampling from a privately fine-tuned generator and then filtered to match the real instruction distribution in embedding space, yielding a final set of ~180K instructions. Because the synthetic data is privately released, downstream fine-tuning can use standard (non-DP) optimizers via the post-processing property of DP.

## Method

The framework has two stages. Stage 1 privately fine-tunes a pre-trained LLaMA model on user instructions using DP-Adam with LoRA (rank 8, attention weights only), then samples one million synthetic instructions via nucleus sampling (top-p=0.95, T=1.0). Stage 2 introduces a novel DP histogram resampling algorithm: synthetic instructions are clustered into K=1000 groups via k-means on Sentence-T5 embeddings, a histogram of real instructions is built by assigning each to its nearest cluster centroid, Gaussian noise (σ=10) is added to privatize the histogram, and synthetic instructions are then resampled to match the noisy histogram. The key insight is that the histogram resampling incurs a much smaller privacy cost than the fine-tuning step (ε₂ is small relative to ε₁), yet it substantially closes the distributional gap between synthetic and real instructions, improving MAUVE scores by large margins and translating into 8.6% relative win-rate gains in supervised fine-tuning.

## Results

On supervised fine-tuning with LLaMA 7B, the best DP synthetic instruction model (300K samples, ε=5.98) achieves a 68.1% win-rate against a FLAN-finetuned baseline on Chatbot Arena instructions, closing the gap to non-private Chatbot Arena training (68.9%) to within 0.8 percentage points. For LLaMA 13B, the best DP model reaches 74.5% win-rate, surpassing Vicuna-v1.3 (72.8%). The DP histogram filtering (Algorithm 2) yields an 8.6% relative improvement in win-rate over unfiltered synthetic instructions and boosts MAUVE scores from 0.942 to 0.961 (unigram) at ε≈6. In RLHF with PPO on Phi-1.5, private synthetic instructions produce reward and win-rate curves closely matching those from real instructions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Vicuna-v1.3 (Chiang et al., 2023), the proposed DP synthetic instruction approach wins on Chatbot Arena instructions: the 7B DP model (68.1%) outperforms Vicuna-v1.3 (64.1%) by 4.0 percentage points, and the 13B DP model (74.5%) beats Vicuna-v1.3 (72.8%) by 1.7 points. However, on the AlpacaEval benchmark, the best 7B DP model (71.6%) underperforms Vicuna-v1.3 (76.8%) by 5.2 points, while the 13B DP model (80.6%) trails Vicuna-v1.3 (82.1%) by 1.5 points. Against LLaMA2-Chat (Touvron et al., 2023b), the 7B DP model (71.6%) is essentially tied with LLaMA2-Chat (71.4%) on AlpacaEval, and the 13B DP model (80.6%) is within 0.5 points of LLaMA2-Chat (81.1%). The paper also shows that DP training on real instructions (Chatbot Arena, ε=5.94) yields only 60.7% for 7B, confirming that synthetic instructions with standard optimizers outperform direct DP fine-tuning on sensitive data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DP fine-tuning literature for language models (Yu et al. 2022; Yue et al. 2022; Kurakin et al. 2023) and the DP synthetic text generation paradigm (Augenstein et al. 2019; Mattern et al. 2022), but departs from both by targeting instruction-following alignment rather than simpler text generation tasks. The key conceptual departure is identifying the human-annotation privacy risk as distinct from model memorization, which prior private alignment work (Wu et al. 2024a) does not address. The histogram-based resampling algorithm (Algorithm 2) is inspired by MAUVE's cluster-and-histogram distributional comparison (Pillutla et al. 2022) but repurposed as a private filtering mechanism, and the overall two-stage framework composes DP fine-tuning with a low-cost private histogram release to close the distributional gap between synthetic and real instructions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formally address the annotation-stage privacy risk in LLM alignment, providing a practical two-stage pipeline that simultaneously protects against both human exposure and model memorization while maintaining near-parity utility with non-private training. The finding that filtered DP synthetic instructions (ε≈6) achieve win-rates within ~1% of non-private real instructions is practically significant for service providers who must handle sensitive user data. Limitations include the text-only scope, the moderate privacy budget (ε≈6), and the requirement to access real instructions (albeit only for embedding-based clustering) during the resampling stage. Open questions include extension to multi-modal instructions, whether the histogram approach generalizes to larger cluster counts or different embedding spaces, and how the framework performs under stricter privacy budgets (ε<3).
