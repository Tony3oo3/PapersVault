## Problem

The seminal min-max (MM) neural network architecture guarantees monotonicity and asymptotic approximation of any monotone function, but in practice training often gets stuck in undesired local optima because the hard max/min operations produce zero partial derivatives, rendering most neurons 'silent' and causing severe underfitting with very coarse piecewise-linear approximations. This paper identifies the silent-neuron problem as the root cause and proposes a minimal architectural fix that preserves the theoretical guarantees while eliminating the training pathology.

## Input / Output

The SMM module takes a numerical feature vector x ∈ R^d and outputs a scalar value that is guaranteed monotonic (non-decreasing or non-increasing) in a user-specified subset of input variables. For partial monotonicity, only weights connecting to constrained variables are restricted to be non-negative (via exponential encoding), while weights for unconstrained variables are free; an auxiliary neural network can process unconstrained inputs to increase expressiveness. The module is designed as a drop-in component within larger deep learning systems trained end-to-end, and the output can be interpreted as a regression target or a classification logit.

## Method

The SMM module replaces the hard max (within each group of linear neurons) and hard min (across groups) of the original MM architecture with smooth LogSumExp (LSE) functions: LSE_β for the max and LSE_{-β} for the min, where β > 0 is an additional learnable parameter encoded via the exponential to ensure positivity. Because LSE is strictly increasing and differentiable everywhere, every neuron contributes a non-zero gradient to the output, eliminating the silent-neuron problem that plagues the original MM network. The paper proves (Corollary 1) that SMM inherits the asymptotic approximation guarantee of MM: for any continuous bounded monotone function with bounded partial derivatives on [0,1]^d, an SMM network can approximate it to arbitrary precision by choosing β sufficiently large. The module is trained by unconstrained gradient-based optimization (Rprop in experiments) with no additional hyperparameters beyond the architecture size K and group sizes h_k, and the default setting K = h_1 = ... = h_K = 6 was used across all experiments without tuning.

## Results

On univariate monotonic regression (f_sq, f_sqrt, f_sig), SMM achieves median test MSE of 0.01, 0.02, and 0.01 (×10⁻³), outperforming all baselines with statistically significant margins (p < 0.001). On multivariate tasks (d = 2, 4, 6), SMM achieves 0.00, 0.01, and 0.02 (×10⁻³) MSE, again beating all competitors. On the real-world benchmark suite from Nolte et al. (2022), SMM₆₄ achieves 69.5% accuracy on COMPAS, 0.154 RMSE on BlogFeedback, 65.47% on LoanDefaulter, 70.1% on ChestXRay, 91.3% on Heart Disease, and 7.51 MSE on Auto MPG, matching or exceeding all previously reported methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMM decisively outperforms the original MM architecture (Sill, 1997) on all univariate tasks (e.g., 0.01 vs. 0.10 MSE on f_sq), attributed to MM's silent-neuron problem where only ~2.8 of 36 neurons remain active after training versus >31 for SMM. Against XGBoost, SMM wins on all synthetic univariate and multivariate tasks but loses on 3 of 4 UCI partial-monotone tasks (e.g., 0.25 vs. 0.22 on Energy Y₁), where tree ensembles benefit from the unconstrained features. SMM outperforms HLL (Yanagisawa et al., 2022) on univariate tasks (0.01 vs. 0.04) and most multivariate settings, while also being >30× faster to train (9.7s vs. 329s for d=2). Against LMN (Nolte et al., 2022), SMM achieves lower MSE on univariate (0.01 vs. 0.37 for LMNˢ on f_sq) and multivariate tasks, with the exception of d=6 where differences are not statistically significant. On the real-world benchmark suite (Table 3), SMM₆₄ matches or beats Certified (Liu et al., 2020), COMET (Sivaraman et al., 2020), Crystal (Milani Fard et al., 2016), and CMNN (Runje & Shankaranarayana, 2023) across all seven tasks, e.g., 69.5% vs. 68.8% on COMPAS and 0.154 vs. 0.158 RMSE on BlogFeedback.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Sill's (1997) min-max (MM) network architecture by replacing the hard min/max operations with smooth LogSumExp approximations, diagnosing the original MM's failure mode as 'silent neurons' caused by zero partial derivatives. It positions itself against a spectrum of more complex monotonic modelling approaches—lattice layers (which suffer L^d parameter scaling), certified monotonic networks (requiring NP-hard SMT/MILP solving), Lipschitz monotonic networks (requiring a manually chosen Lipschitz constant), and constrained monotonic neural networks (using three activation functions per layer)—arguing that all are unnecessarily complex relative to the simple SMM module. The theoretical foundation builds on the asymptotic approximation theorem for MM networks (Sill, 1997; Daniels & Velikova, 2010), which the SMM inherits via a straightforward perturbation argument bounding the LogSumExp approximation error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SMM module offers a conceptually simple, computationally efficient, and theoretically grounded drop-in replacement for monotonicity constraints in deep learning systems, with no curse of dimensionality and no additional hyperparameters beyond the architecture size. The smoothness property is highlighted as scientifically important for inter- and extrapolation behavior, distinguishing SMM from piecewise-constant approaches like XGBoost and isotonic regression. However, on complex real-world UCI tasks with many unconstrained features, XGBoost still outperforms SMM, and the paper acknowledges that the overall system architecture's inductive bias can overshadow the monotonic module's contribution. Open questions include how SMM scales to very high-dimensional inputs, whether the single-layer SMM module suffices for highly non-linear real-world targets, and how it compares on larger-scale benchmarks beyond the small UCI sets tested here.
