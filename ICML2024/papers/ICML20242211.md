## Problem

Medical VQA models suffer from modal interference: they treat all instruction tokens equally, fail to trace answers back to specific visual evidence, and lack dynamic, instruction-specific feature alignment during training. Existing approaches either rely on manual annotation for attention steering or apply generic data augmentation that ignores the semantic relationship between a question's core intent and the relevant image regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242211/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a medical image (X-ray, CT, or MRI) and a natural language question as input, and produces a text answer accompanied by a chain-of-thought visual explanation in which specific phrases are tagged with <core_visual_explanation> markers indicating the supporting visual evidence. The question itself is annotated with 'real_intent' labels highlighting the key tokens the model should focus on. The IAI-Med VQA dataset augments VQA-RAD with these dual annotations (real-intent labels on questions, core-visual-explanation labels on answers), and the frozen PMC-CLIP backbone processes images while PMC-LLaMA handles the language side.

## Method

Uni-Med comprises three tightly coupled modules. First, the Instruct-to-Answer Clues Interpreter (IAI) uses GPT-4V guided by a Universal-Navigator Prompt (UNP) to generate visual explanations and label the 'real intent' tokens in each instruction, producing the IAI-Med VQA dataset. Second, Task-guided Token-Level Cut-Mix (TC-Mix) decomposes images into patches, computes bidirectional cosine similarity between patch features and marked answer-token embeddings via projection heads, selects top-N semantically aligned patches, and performs element-wise cut-mixing between image pairs within the same organ+problem group to enforce task-relevant feature learning. Third, Intention-guided Attention (IGA) applies a projection to the multi-head attention scores in the LLM, scaling down attention for tokens outside the 'real intent' set by a factor β<1 with renormalization, thereby suppressing non-core instruction interference. Training minimizes a combined cross-entropy loss on the text answer and an L2 loss on the visual explanation, with the PMC-CLIP visual encoder kept frozen throughout.

## Results

On the SLAKE dataset, Uni-Med (7B) achieves 87.52% closed-ended accuracy, 85.34% open-ended accuracy, and 86.12% overall accuracy, surpassing the previous SOTA (MedVInT-PMC-VQA) by 1.22%, 0.84%, and 0.92% respectively. On VQA-RAD, Uni-Med reaches 87.22% closed and 74.21% open accuracy, exceeding LLaVA-Med (which used 1M pre-trained images) by 3.03% and 12.69% on closed and open questions respectively. The model uses only 140K pre-trained images (PMC-VQA), achieving these gains with substantially less pretraining data than LLaVA-Med's 1M images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Uni-Med outperforms the previous SOTA, MedVInT-PMC-VQA, on every reported metric: +0.42% closed and +0.51% open on VQA-RAD, and +1.22% closed, +0.84% open, and +0.92% overall on SLAKE, while using the same 140K pre-trained images. Against LLaVA-Med (7B, 1M pre-trained images), Uni-Med gains +3.03% closed and +12.69% open on VQA-RAD, and +2.18% closed and +2.26% open on SLAKE, demonstrating that instruction-to-answer alignment is more effective than simply scaling pretraining data. Compared to LLaVA-Med (13B), Uni-Med (7B) still leads by +1.94% closed and +0.37% open on SLAKE. Against earlier pretrain-finetuning methods (M3AE, MTL, MUMC), Uni-Med achieves the highest overall accuracy on both datasets (82.05% VQA-RAD, 86.12% SLAKE). On VQA-RAD, Uni-Med also surpasses RAMM (78.27% overall) by 3.78% and MUMC (79.2%) by 2.85%, confirming consistent gains across the full range of baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PMC-VQA framework (Zhang et al., 2023b), which uses PMC-CLIP as a frozen visual backbone and PMC-LLaMA as the LLM, by adding three targeted modules: an MLLM-based data construction pipeline (IAI) that generates visual explanations and 'real intent' labels, a task-guided adaptation of CutMix (Yun et al., 2019) that aligns text tokens with image patches via semantic similarity, and an intention-guided attention mechanism that suppresses non-core instruction tokens. Conceptually, it departs from prior Med-VQA work that treats text and image modalities symmetrically, instead explicitly modeling the instruction-to-answer interaction to reduce modal interference. The IAI module repurposes GPT-4V as a zero-shot annotation tool, positioning the paper at the intersection of MLLM-based data augmentation and attention-guided training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The publicly released IAI-Med VQA dataset, with its 'real intent' and 'core visual explanation' annotations, provides a useful resource for the Med-VQA community studying interpretability and instruction-following. However, the ablation (Table 4) reveals that the IAI module contributes only ~0.3% over the baseline on SLAKE overall, and the full model's GPU throughput is slightly lower than the baseline, raising questions about the cost-benefit of the added complexity. Open questions include whether the GPT-4V-generated visual explanations are clinically reliable at scale, whether the approach generalizes beyond radiology to other medical imaging modalities, and whether the modest gains (≤1.2%) justify the additional pipeline overhead in deployment settings.
