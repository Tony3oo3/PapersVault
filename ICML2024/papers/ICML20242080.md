## Problem

Existing self-supervised gaze estimation methods are restricted to eye-image input: contrastive approaches fail to generalize to full-face settings due to the lack of suitable data augmentation, while generative autoencoder approaches produce trivial, indistinguishable features when expanded to full-face images because vanilla MSE loss optimizes only reconstruction quality without any explicit semantic regularization on the learned gaze representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242080/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 full-face RGB images normalized via detected facial landmarks and produces a 2D gaze direction angle. Two augmented views (color jitter + grayscale) of each image are fed through the encoder; the resulting feature is simultaneously used for contrastive regularization, eye-region reconstruction via a decoder, and a frozen linear probing head for final gaze prediction. The decoder target is the eye-region patch (not the full face), and no head-pose labels are required.

## Method

BeCa decomposes the autoencoder likelihood p(y|x) = ∫ p(y|z) p(z|x) dz and shows (Proposition 1) that maximizing it is equivalent to jointly minimizing an InfoNCE contrastive loss between the input and its latent feature plus a weighted MSE reconstruction loss on the eye region. This introduces explicit global semantic regularization that prevents the trivial-solution collapse of vanilla autoencoders on full-face images. BeCa-InfoMSE further replaces the MSE term with an Informative Mean Square Error that estimates mutual information between the input and an unknown high-level label distribution via a batch-level softmax over predicted-vs-ground-truth eye patches, thereby decoupling representation quality from pixel-level reconstruction fidelity. The framework is backbone-agnostic (ResNet-18/50, ViT-tiny) and is trained end-to-end for 200 epochs with AdamW and cosine LR decay.

## Results

Under 100-shot linear probing, BeCa-InfoMSE achieves 6.03° gaze error on Columbia, 5.17° on MPII, and 15.27° on Gaze360 using ViT-tiny, outperforming AutoEncoder(ERC) (7.0°/6.77°/24.25°) by 14–37% and SimCLR (12.08°/10.22°/37.01°) by 50–59%. With ResNet-18, BeCa-InfoMSE reaches 6.12°/5.44°/21.75° on the same datasets. In whole-dataset fine-tuning on MPII, BeCa-InfoMSE achieves 3.83° gaze error, surpassing the best supervised baseline L2CS-Net (3.92°).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BeCa-InfoMSE consistently outperforms all self-supervised baselines across every dataset and backbone. Against the generative baseline AutoEncoder(ERC), BeCa-InfoMSE reduces gaze error by 12–37% (e.g., 5.17° vs 6.77° on MPII with ViT-tiny; 15.27° vs 24.25° on Gaze360). Against contrastive baselines SimCLR and Barlow Twins, the improvement is even more dramatic (50–59% lower error), confirming that pure contrastive learning is ineffective for gaze estimation. Compared to the state-of-the-art eye-image method CE (Sun et al., 2021), BeCa-InfoMSE achieves 5.44° vs 7.2° on MPII and 6.12° vs 6.4° on Columbia without requiring head pose labels. Against the full-face contrastive method GazeCLR (Jindal & Manduchi, 2023), BeCa-InfoMSE wins on Columbia (6.12° vs 6.6°), MPII (5.44° vs 6.5°), and Gaze360 (21.75° vs 37.07°). In whole-dataset fine-tuning on MPII, BeCa-InfoMSE (3.83°) even surpasses supervised methods including L2CS-Net (3.92°), AGE-Net (4.09°), and FAR-Net (4.3°).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the generative self-supervised gaze estimation line (Yu & Odobez, 2020; Sun et al., 2021), which uses an autoencoder to reconstruct eye regions as a pretext task, and departs from it by (1) extending input from eye patches to full-face images and (2) injecting contrastive regularization (InfoNCE) into the autoencoder's latent space to prevent trivial solutions. It also draws on the broader contrastive pre-training literature (SimCLR, Barlow Twins, BYOL) but identifies a fundamental incompatibility: gaze estimation lacks the classification-oriented data augmentations that make contrastive learning effective. The InfoMSE loss is a novel contribution that reframes the reconstruction objective through an information-theoretic lens, estimating mutual information between input and an unknown 'high-level' label distribution rather than minimizing pixel-level MSE directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to achieve competitive self-supervised full-face gaze estimation without requiring head pose labels or multi-view video, making it more practical than prior eye-image-only methods (CE, GazeCLR). The finding that reconstruction quality and semantic representation quality are not positively correlated—evidenced by MSE loss rising while gaze error falls during BeCa-InfoMSE training—challenges a common assumption in generative self-supervised learning and could inform other reconstruction-based pre-training tasks. Limitations include the continued need for eye landmark detection and the reliance on ETH-Xgaze pre-training for small datasets like Columbia; fully label-free full-face gaze estimation remains an open problem.
