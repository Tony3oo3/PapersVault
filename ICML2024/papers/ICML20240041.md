## Problem

Current Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) evaluation protocols assume an offline setting where the data stream waits for the model to finish adapting before revealing the next sample, ignoring the computational overhead that slower methods incur. In real-world deployment, data arrives at a constant speed, so slower TTA methods inevitably miss incoming samples they could use for adaptation, yet this effect is never accounted for in benchmarking. This paper identifies that the standard offline protocol systematically favors sophisticated but slow methods over simpler but fast ones, misrepresenting their real-world applicability.

## Input / Output

The TTA methods under evaluation consume unlabeled RGB images in batches of size 64 (single images for MEMO) arriving from a constant-speed stream, along with pre-trained model parameters (ResNet-50-BN or ViT trained on ImageNet). The key operational detail is that the stream reveals data at a fixed rate r, and the method's relative adaptation speed C(g) = ⌈r / R(g(x))⌉ determines which samples the method actually adapts to versus which are handled by the base model f_θ without adaptation. Output is a class label per image, with performance measured as error rate across 15 corruption types on ImageNet-C, CIFAR-10-C, ImageNet-R, and ImageNet-3DCC.

## Method

The paper proposes a Realistic TTA evaluation protocol that simulates a constant-speed data stream and intrinsically penalizes slower methods by granting them fewer adaptation opportunities. The core mechanism introduces the relative adaptation speed C(g), computed online per sample as the ceiling of the ratio between stream speed and the method's processing speed, accounting for both hardware noise and input-dependent variability (e.g., methods that optionally skip adaptation). At each time step t, the method g adapts only if (t mod C(g)) = 0; otherwise, the most recently adapted base model f_θ generates predictions for the skipped samples. This protocol is applied to 15 TTA methods (spanning BN adaptation, entropy minimization, distillation, pseudo-labeling, and diffusion-based approaches) across four evaluation scenarios: episodic, continual, variable stream speed, and practical TTA with label imbalance. The results reveal that under realistic constraints, simple fast methods like AdaBN and SHOT outperform state-of-the-art but slower methods like SAR and DDA, while sample-rejection methods like EATA maintain the best speed-accuracy trade-off.

## Results

Under the proposed realistic (online) evaluation protocol on ImageNet-C (severity 5, ResNet-50-BN), EATA achieves the lowest average error rate at 55.6%, followed by SHOT at 59.1% and TENT at 61.6%. In contrast, under the standard offline protocol, SAR (56.2%) and EATA (52.0%) lead, with SHOT at 59.9%. The realistic protocol penalizes slower methods substantially: DDA degrades by +17.6% (64.4%→82.0%), TTAC-NQ by +7.5% (59.0%→66.5%), and SAR by +7.2% (56.2%→63.4%), while fast methods with C(g)=1 (AdaBN, BN, LAME) show zero degradation. Notably, SHOT (2020) actually improves by 0.8% under realistic evaluation, and outperforms the state-of-the-art SAR (2023) by 4.3% in this setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under the realistic protocol, EATA (55.6%) remains the top performer, benefiting from its fast forward-pass-based sample rejection that limits its C(g) to 3. SHOT (59.1%) surpasses SAR (63.4%) by 4.3% and TENT (61.6%) by 2.5%, reversing the offline ranking where SAR (56.2%) beat SHOT (59.9%). The simple BN method (66.7%, C(g)=1) outperforms the more sophisticated SAR (63.4%) by only 3.3% but with zero computational overhead, and AdaBN (68.5%) beats DDA (82.0%) by 13.5% despite being from 2016 vs. 2022. Data-dependent methods MEMO (81.9%) and DDA (82.0%) degrade to near-random performance because their extreme C(g) values (54 and 810) mean they adapt to only 1–2% of the stream. In the continual setting, methods without sample rejection (SHOT, TENT, TTAC) that overfit in the offline setup actually benefit from the realistic protocol's forced skipping, closing the gap with SAR and EATA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not propose a new TTA method but instead introduces a realistic online evaluation protocol that re-examines 15 existing TTA methods (AdaBN, SHOT, TENT, EATA, SAR, CoTTA, DDA, MEMO, etc.) under a constant-speed data stream. It draws conceptual inspiration from Online Learning (Cai et al., 2021; Shalev-Shwartz et al., 2012), where the learner must process data as it arrives at a fixed rate, and repurposes this framing to expose a critical blind spot in the current offline TTA evaluation: the implicit assumption that the stream waits for the model to finish adapting. The paper carves new ground by formalizing the 'relative adaptation speed' C(g) as the ratio of stream speed to method speed, turning inference latency into an explicit evaluation variable rather than an ignored implementation detail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that a 2017 BN-statistics method (AdaBN) outperforms the 2023 SOTA (SAR) by 13% under realistic evaluation, and that SHOT (2020) beats SAR—challenges the field's implicit assumption that more sophisticated adaptation mechanisms are always superior. This reframes the research agenda toward developing TTA methods that are both accurate and computationally lightweight, a dimension previously overlooked. Open questions include whether the protocol generalizes to non-image tasks (video, audio, language), how to handle scenarios where the base model cannot process skipped samples in real-time (the single-model variant in Appendix C shows even more dramatic degradation), and whether the findings hold for streaming settings with temporal correlation. A limitation is that the protocol normalizes stream speed to the base model's forward-pass time, which may not reflect all deployment constraints (e.g., edge devices with heterogeneous hardware).
