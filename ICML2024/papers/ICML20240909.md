## Problem

Existing zero-shot TTS systems struggle to simultaneously achieve high speech quality, speaker similarity, and natural prosody because speech intrinsically couples multiple attributes (content, prosody, timbre, acoustic details) that are difficult to model jointly. Prior neural codecs using residual vector quantization decompose speech hierarchically but fail to effectively disentangle these attributes across quantization levels, leaving the generative model to handle complex coupled information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240909/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes phoneme-level text (derived from grapheme-to-phoneme conversion) and a 3-second speech prompt from the target speaker. The prompt is factorized by FACodec into attribute-specific sub-prompts (content, prosody, acoustic details codes plus a global timbre vector), which condition separate diffusion modules. Output is a 16 kHz speech waveform reconstructed by the codec decoder from the generated discrete codes and the prompt's timbre embedding, requiring 60 forward passes (4 diffusion iterations with classifier-free guidance on most attributes).

## Method

NaturalSpeech 3 introduces FACodec, a neural codec with factorized vector quantization (FVQ) that disentangles speech into content (2 quantizers), prosody (1 quantizer), acoustic details (3 quantizers), and a global timbre vector (via Transformer + temporal pooling), using information bottleneck (8-dim projection before quantization), supervised auxiliary losses (F0, phoneme labels, speaker ID), gradient reversal layers, and detail dropout to enforce disentanglement. A factorized discrete diffusion model then generates attributes sequentially—duration, prosody, content, acoustic details—each conditioned on its own attribute prompt and previously generated attributes, while timbre is directly inherited from the prompt rather than generated. The discrete diffusion uses a mask-based forward/reverse process (inspired by MaskGIT) with confidence-based unmasking and classifier-free guidance (scale 1.0, p_cfg=0.15). This divide-and-conquer design reduces per-attribute modeling complexity, enables independent attribute control via different prompts, and avoids the generative model needing to learn timbre from scratch.

## Results

On LibriSpeech test-clean, NaturalSpeech 3 achieves Sim-O 0.67, Sim-R 0.76, WER 1.81, CMOS 0.00, and SMOS 4.01, reaching near ground-truth quality (CMOS 0.00 vs. +0.08 for recordings) and surpassing all baselines on every metric. On RAVDESS, it achieves the best average MCD (4.28) and MCD-Acc (0.52) for prosody similarity, with CMOS 0.00 (vs. +0.17 for ground truth) and SMOS 4.72 (vs. 4.42 for ground truth). The system achieves a 15.27× speedup over VALL-E (RTF 0.296 vs. 4.520) while outperforming it on all quality and similarity metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NaturalSpeech 3 outperforms all seven zero-shot TTS baselines on every reported metric. The largest margins are over VALL-E (Sim-O 0.67 vs. 0.47; WER 1.81 vs. 6.11; CMOS 0.00 vs. −0.60) and StyleTTS 2 (Sim-O 0.67 vs. 0.38; SMOS 4.01 vs. 3.07). Against the closest competitor Voicebox, NaturalSpeech 3 gains +0.03 Sim-O, −0.22 WER, +0.23 CMOS, and +0.32 SMOS. On RAVDESS prosody evaluation, NaturalSpeech 3 achieves the best average MCD (4.28 vs. 4.44 for Mega-TTS 2, 4.50 for StyleTTS 2) and MCD-Acc (0.52 vs. 0.40 for StyleTTS 2), with SMOS 4.72 exceeding even ground truth (4.42). For codec reconstruction (Table 4/11), FACodec at 4.8 kbps surpasses SoundStream at the same bandwidth by 0.44 PESQ and 0.79 MCD, and matches SoundStream at double bandwidth (9.6 kbps) while using half the bits. For zero-shot voice conversion (Table 12), FACodec achieves Sim-O 0.86 and WER 3.46, beating UniAudio (WER 4.80) and LM-VC (WER 4.91) on intelligibility while matching their speaker similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in zero-shot TTS that treats speech as a monolithic signal (mel-spectrogram or RVQ token sequence) by explicitly factorizing it into attribute-specific subspaces (content, prosody, timbre, acoustic details) via a novel factorized vector quantization codec (FACodec). It builds on discrete diffusion models (MaskGIT, DiffuSeq, SoundStorm) for generation but applies them independently within each factorized subspace, and on neural codec architectures (SoundStream, DAC) for the encoder/decoder backbone. The disentanglement techniques (information bottleneck, gradient reversal, detail dropout) extend prior speech disentanglement work (NANSY, SpeechTokenizer, Disen-TF-Codec) to more attributes and stronger decoupling, while the overall system positions itself as a divide-and-conquer alternative to VALL-E, NaturalSpeech 2, and Voicebox.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NaturalSpeech 3 achieves human-level naturalness on the multi-speaker LibriSpeech test set in a zero-shot setting, a milestone following NaturalSpeech 1's single-speaker result, and the factorization paradigm is shown to be extensible beyond the proposed diffusion model (e.g., improving VALL-E). The ability to independently manipulate timbre, prosody, and duration via attribute prompts opens practical controllability that monolithic TTS systems lack. Key limitations remain: English-only training data, dependence on phoneme transcription for content supervision, inability to handle background sounds, and disentanglement validated primarily on TTS rather than broader tasks. Open questions include whether unsupervised disentanglement can match the supervised approach and whether the factorization framework generalizes to multilingual and more diverse speaker populations.
