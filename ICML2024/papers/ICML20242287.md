## Problem

Inferring the posterior distribution of inducing points in Deep Gaussian Processes (DGPs) is challenging: mean-field Gaussian variational inference (DSVI) is overly restrictive and introduces bias with nonlinear likelihoods, while Implicit Posterior VI (IPVI) relies on adversarial Nash-equilibrium optimization that is unstable and also biased. No existing method provides both a flexible, non-Gaussian posterior approximation and an explicit, tractable variational lower bound for the DGP marginal likelihood.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard tabular or image training data (features and targets) along with a fixed set of 128 inducing points per layer (initialized as Gaussian random variables) and DGP hyperparameters (depth 2–5, RBF kernel). It produces posterior samples of the inducing variables via a T-step denoising diffusion SDE, which are then propagated through the DGP layers to yield predicted function values, class probabilities, or reconstructed images. For the Frey Faces reconstruction task, input images have 75% of pixels removed, and the model recovers the full 20×28 image from a 20-dimensional latent code.

## Method

DDVI parameterizes the posterior of inducing points as the terminal distribution of a time-reversed denoising diffusion SDE, where the intractable score function ∇ln p_t is approximated by a neural network trained via score matching. The key technical contribution is a bridge process trick: an auxiliary forward SDE initialized at the fixed prior p_fix (rather than the true posterior q) is constructed so that the KL divergence between the approximate and true path measures becomes computable, yielding an explicit variational lower bound (ELBO) for log p(y) that combines the data likelihood, the prior on inducing variables, a score-matching penalty, and a KL term between p_fix and a known Gaussian. Training uses stochastic mini-batch variational inference with reparameterization for both the Euler-discretized diffusion transitions and the DGP layer conditionals, enabling efficient gradient backpropagation through the entire diffusion network and DGP stack via Adam SGD.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, DDVI achieves 95.56% test accuracy (4-layer DGP), outperforming the best baseline IPVI (93.52%) by over 2 percentage points, while also training ~1.1x faster per iteration. On MNIST and Fashion-MNIST, DDVI reaches 99.01% and 90.85% respectively, exceeding all baselines by 1.2–3.5 points. On large-scale classification (SUSY, HIGGS), DDVI attains AUC of 0.887 and 0.857 at L=4/5, consistently leading DSVI, IPVI, and SGHMC. On the Frey Faces reconstruction task, DDVI achieves the lowest RMSE (7.64) and NLL (1.17) among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDVI outperforms all three baselines (DSVI, IPVI, SGHMC) across every evaluated dataset and task. On image classification, the margin is most pronounced on CIFAR-10, where DDVI (95.56%) beats IPVI (93.52%) by 2.04 points and DSVI (91.79%) by 3.77 points at 4 layers; on Fashion-MNIST the gap over IPVI is 1.95 points. On large-scale AUC benchmarks, DDVI leads by 0.3–1.7 points over DSVI and by 0.1–0.5 points over the closest competitors (IPVI/SGHMC) on SUSY and HIGGS. On the Frey Faces reconstruction task, DDVI achieves the lowest RMSE (7.64 vs. 7.91 for IPVI) and NLL (1.17 vs. 1.33 for IPVI). Notably, DDVI also matches or exceeds the per-iteration speed of DSVI and IPVI while being substantially faster than SGHMC (e.g., 0.69s vs. 8.61s per iteration on CIFAR-10 at 4 layers), making it both more accurate and more efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of sparse Gaussian process inference and denoising diffusion models. It builds directly on the DGP framework (Damianou & Lawrence, 2013) and the sparse inducing-point machinery (Titsias, 2009), departing from DSVI's restrictive mean-field Gaussian posterior and IPVI's adversarial Nash-equilibrium training by instead parameterizing the inducing-point posterior as the terminal distribution of a reverse denoising diffusion SDE. The technical core draws on the classical time-reversal theorem for diffusions (Anderson, 1982; Haussmann & Pardoux, 1986) and score matching (Vincent, 2011), with a novel bridge-process construction that converts the intractable KL(P^φ‖P) into an explicit, optimizable evidence lower bound—something neither DSVI nor IPVI provides in closed form.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By yielding an explicit ELBO amenable to standard backpropagation and mini-batch SGD, DDVI removes the adversarial-training instability that plagues IPVI and the expressiveness bottleneck of DSVI's Gaussian assumption, while remaining computationally comparable to DSVI per iteration. The consistent gains across regression, classification, and reconstruction tasks (including 2M-point Airline and 11M-point Higgs datasets) suggest the diffusion-based posterior is a practical upgrade for DGP practitioners. Open questions include the cost of simulating T SDE steps at inference time, whether the flexibility of the score network is truly necessary versus a simpler non-Gaussian family, and how the method scales to very deep (L > 5) or high-dimensional DGP architectures.
