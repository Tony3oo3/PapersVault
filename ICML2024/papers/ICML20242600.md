## Problem

Homomorphic encryption schemes like CKKS can only evaluate polynomial circuits on encrypted data, yet transformers rely on inherently non-polynomial operations—Softmax (involving exponentiation and division) in self-attention and LayerNorm (involving square root)—that resist straightforward polynomial approximation. Prior HE-based secure inference work has been limited to CNNs with simpler activations like ReLU and GELU, leaving transformers, the dominant architecture for both NLP and vision, entirely outside the scope of non-interactive HE inference.

## Input / Output

A data owner encrypts a text sequence (up to 1024 tokens) or an RGB image using CKKS and transmits the ciphertext to an untrusted model owner, who runs the polynomial transformer entirely on encrypted data without any further interaction. The model returns an encrypted prediction (next-token logits for language modeling or class scores for image classification), which the data owner decrypts locally. The HE parameters constrain the computation to a multiplication depth of 12 with 42 fractional and 18 integer precision bits at 128-bit security, meaning every operation in the network must be expressible as a low-degree polynomial within these limits.

## Method

The paper introduces a three-stage pipeline to produce a fully polynomial transformer. First, the architecture is modified: Softmax attention is replaced with pointwise σ-attention (an element-wise activation applied to QK^T/√d_k) stabilized by a length-scaling factor S(L) applied post-activation, and LayerNorm is either swapped for BatchNorm (vision) or retained but constrained via a novel variance-minimization loss (NLP). Second, a supplementary training phase adds range-minimization objectives (Eq. 13) that shrink the input domain of every non-polynomial layer—reducing LayerNorm variance from ~3300 to ~300 and activation ranges from ~70 to ~20—so that low-degree polynomial approximations become accurate. Third, each remaining non-polynomial component is replaced by its polynomial surrogate: Remez-derived polynomials for GELU, Lee et al. compositions for ReLU, and Panda's piecewise Newton-Raphson polynomials for the inverse square root in LayerNorm. For vision, additional stabilizers (BatchNorm2D on the attention matrix and an extra BatchNorm in the MLP) close the accuracy gap with the original Softmax+LayerNorm model.

## Results

On Wikitext-103, the 6-layer polynomial BERT-like transformer achieves a perplexity of 19.89 versus 18.98 for the original Softmax-based transformer (gap of 0.91), while the 12-layer model reaches 18.91 versus 16.89 (gap of 2.02). On vision tasks, the polynomial ViT on CIFAR-100 achieves 70.8% accuracy versus 73.4% for the original ViT (gap of 2.6%), and the polynomial Swin on Tiny-ImageNet reaches 58.9% versus 59.4% (gap of 0.5%). Under full homomorphic encryption (HEaaN, 128-bit security), the polynomial models reproduce plaintext predictions in 99.5% of test cases, with secure inference over 128 tokens taking 211 seconds on an A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external polynomial or HE-based transformer methods, as it is the first to demonstrate secure inference over HE with full transformers. The primary comparison is internal: the final polynomial model is benchmarked against the original vanilla Softmax-based transformer of identical size and architecture. The performance gap is small — 0.91 perplexity points (6-layer) and 2.02 (12-layer) on Wikitext-103, and 2.6% accuracy (ViT/CIFAR-100) and 0.5% (Swin/Tiny-ImageNet) on vision tasks. The authors note that at least 80% of the NLP gap is attributable to the final polynomial approximation stage, suggesting that higher-degree polynomials could close most of the remaining difference. On the smaller text-8 and enwik8 benchmarks, the gap shrinks to 0.02–0.07 perplexity points, indicating the method scales well with task difficulty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of HE-friendly polynomial CNN work (Baruch et al. 2023 for ResNet/ConvNeXt, Lee et al. 2021 for polynomial ReLU) to the transformer architecture, which had been excluded from polynomial HE due to Softmax and LayerNorm. The key methodological departure is replacing Softmax-based self-attention with a pointwise σ-attention mechanism stabilized by length scaling, and extending the range-minimization training loss from Baruch et al. 2023 from activation inputs to LayerNorm variance (Eq. 12). For polynomial approximations, it reuses established tools: Lee et al. 2021 for ReLU, Remez for GELU, and Panda 2022 for inverse square root, but applies them in a transformer context where the input domains are far more challenging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that full transformer architectures (BERT-like LMs and ViT/Swin) can be deployed for non-interactive secure inference over HE, closing a significant gap in the PPML landscape where prior secure transformer protocols required interactive MPC or client-aided computation. The performance gap is modest (<4% accuracy for vision, ~1–2 perplexity for NLP), suggesting the approach is viable. However, the 211-second inference time for 128 tokens and the reliance on an alternative attention mechanism (rather than approximating Softmax directly) raise open questions about scalability to larger models, longer contexts, and additional modalities. Whether σ-attention is a universal drop-in replacement for Softmax across all transformer variants remains untested.
