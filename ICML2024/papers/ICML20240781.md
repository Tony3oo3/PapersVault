## Problem

Standard robustness notions treat data corruption as a uniform threat, but in practice corruption is often localized to specific subpopulations (e.g., biased survey responses from one demographic group). The paper shows that classic algorithms like ERM can fail catastrophically at the group level: a tiny number of corrupted labels in one group can flip predictions for an entirely unrelated group. This motivates a fine-grained 'multigroup robustness' guarantee where each subpopulation's prediction stability degrades only with the corruption *within* that subpopulation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a binary-labeled dataset S = {(x_i, y_i)} from a finite domain X (which may be adversarially corrupted and need not be i.i.d.), a subpopulation class C ⊆ 2^X (potentially containing overlapping groups), and a black-box base learning algorithm A. It outputs a probability predictor p: X → [0,1] that is simultaneously multigroup robust and multiaccurate. The robustness bound for each group C adapts to the label-flip distance and multiset symmetric difference restricted to C, meaning groups with no corruption receive near-zero degradation regardless of how heavily other groups are attacked.

## Method

The core contribution is an iterative post-processing algorithm (Algorithm 1) that augments any base classifier to achieve empirical multiaccuracy: at each step it identifies a group C where the predictor's mean signed error exceeds ε, then shifts predictions for all points in C by ε in the error-reducing direction, clipping to [0,1]. The key theoretical insight is that empirical multiaccuracy combined with uniform convergence over the output predictor class is sufficient for multigroup robustness against strong data-dependent adversaries (label flips, additions, deletions). Unlike standard multiaccuracy algorithms that sample fresh data per iteration, this approach reuses the same (potentially corrupted) dataset throughout, which is essential for the robustness guarantee. The paper further proves a lower bound showing multiaccuracy is necessary for any non-trivial multigroup robust algorithm, and demonstrates that the post-processing step increases ℓ₂ loss by at most 2ε relative to the base predictor.

## Results

The paper's main empirical results (Figures 2–3) show that under label-flipping attacks on the White male subgroup of the Folktables-Income dataset, base classifiers (Logistic Regression, MLP) exhibit increasing multi-accuracy error (MA-err) across all other subgroups as the noise rate grows, while the post-processed CLF-PP predictors maintain consistently low MA-err with comparable accuracy. Under data-addition attacks targeting the White female subgroup (using only White male data points), CLF-PP again sustains low MA-err and high accuracy even at low noise levels where base models already degrade. The robust linear regression baseline (Paudice et al., 2019) achieves multiaccuracy performance an order of magnitude worse than the proposed method across all tested datasets (Folktables-Income, Employment, Public Coverage, Bank, Law School).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against label sanitization (Feng et al., 2014), the proposed CLF-PP post-processing is superior at moderate-to-high noise rates, where sanitization's reliance on neighboring labels causes it to degrade, while CLF-PP maintains low MA-err; however, sanitization performs comparably or slightly better at very small noise levels. Against the robust logistic regression baseline (Paudice et al., 2019), the paper reports that its multiaccuracy performance was an order of magnitude worse than the proposed method, indicating that general-purpose robust regression does not translate to fine-grained subgroup-level robustness guarantees. The base classifiers (LR, MLP, DT, kNN, XGB) without post-processing all fail to preserve multigroup robustness under both label-change and data-addition attacks, confirming the theoretical vulnerability of standard ERM-style algorithms identified in Section 1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on multiaccuracy and multicalibration algorithms (Hébert-Johnson et al., 2018; Kim et al., 2019), repurposing their iterative post-processing framework to achieve a new robustness guarantee rather than a fairness guarantee. It departs from prior robust learning work (Kearns & Li, 1988; Bshouty et al., 2002) by not bounding the adversary's total corruption budget, instead providing adaptive per-group guarantees that scale with the corruption localized to each subgroup. It also distinguishes itself from prior fairness-under-poisoning work (Jagielski et al., 2021; Jin & Lai, 2023) by tracking *where* corruption occurs relative to the group structure, rather than treating the dataset as a monolithic object. The key conceptual departure is establishing that multiaccuracy is not merely a sufficient but a *necessary* condition for any non-trivial multigroup robust algorithm, creating a formal bridge between algorithmic fairness and robustness that had not been previously identified.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, data-aware robustness notion that is more meaningful than population-level guarantees when corruption is heterogeneous across subpopulations—a realistic scenario in survey data, web-scraped corpora, and other practical settings. The lower bound showing multiaccuracy is necessary for non-trivial multigroup robustness is a clean theoretical result that unifies two previously separate literatures. However, the practical applicability is currently limited: the post-processing algorithm requires the predictor to come from a finite class, the analysis is restricted to binary labels, and it is unclear whether multigroup robustness of the predictor transfers to post-processed optimal actions in the omniprediction setting. Extending to multi-class problems and infinite predictor families (e.g., neural networks) are natural open directions.
