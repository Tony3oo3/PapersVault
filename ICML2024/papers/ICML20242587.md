## Problem

Data privacy and security for 3D point clouds remain unexplored, and directly extending 2D availability attacks (EM, AP) to point clouds under Chamfer distance regularization provably degenerates: the bi-level optimization's update directions become out of control, forcing the optimal perturbation toward zero and rendering poisons ineffective. This paper identifies this degeneracy theoretically and proposes a fix.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242587/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D point cloud training dataset of N labeled samples, each represented as an unordered set of 1024 points in R^3, and produces a poisoned dataset where each point cloud carries an imperceptible perturbation δ. Perturbations are constrained by Chamfer distance (not ℓp norm, which is unsuitable for unstructured point sets), with a balancing hyperparameter β controlling the trade-off between poisoning efficacy and geometric fidelity. The poisoned dataset, when used to train a victim classifier, yields dramatically reduced clean test accuracy while preserving visual structure.

## Method

FC-EM (Feature Collision Error-Minimization) modifies the inner optimization of the bi-level min-min problem by replacing the cross-entropy loss with a novel class-wise feature collision loss L_fc, which uses cosine similarity in the network's feature space to encourage intra-class feature concentration and inter-class dispersion. This creates shortcuts in feature space rather than logit space, inducing update directions distinct from the outer θ-optimization and thereby breaking the degeneracy equilibrium proven in Theorem 4.1. Theoretically, Theorem 5.1 guarantees a non-trivial lower bound on ‖δ*‖₂ when the gap Δ = L₂(θ*,0) − L₂(θ*,δ*) is substantial, and Theorem 5.3 shows FC-EM achieves stronger linear separability of the poisoned dataset because it reduces cosine similarity between last-layer weight rows. The algorithm alternates θ-updates (minimizing cross-entropy + Chamfer distance) with δ-updates (minimizing feature collision loss + Chamfer distance) over 200 epochs with a batch size of 128.

## Results

On ModelNet40, FC-EM reduces test accuracy to 27.67% (PointNet), 31.12% (PointNet++), and 32.46% (DGCNN), compared to clean accuracies of 90.70%, 93.06%, and 92.64% respectively, while maintaining Chamfer distances of ~8.4×10⁻⁴ and Hausdorff distances of ~13.5×10⁻³. On the medical IntrA dataset, FC-EM drives F1-scores to 0.000 for PointNet++ and DGCNN (complete prediction collapse). On the BFM-2017 face dataset, FC-EM reduces accuracy from 98.1% to 11.0%, and on OmniObject3D it achieves 20.1% accuracy, consistently outperforming all baselines across every dataset and model architecture tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FC-EM dominates all baselines on every dataset and model. Compared to EM (Unlearnable Examples, Huang et al. 2020), FC-EM reduces PointNet accuracy on ModelNet40 from 82.98% to 27.67% while simultaneously lowering Chamfer distance from 29.7×10⁻⁴ to 8.4×10⁻⁴, meaning FC-EM is both a stronger attack and more imperceptible. Compared to AP (Adversarial Poisons, Fowl et al. 2021), FC-EM achieves 27.67% vs. 69.37% accuracy on PointNet and 31.12% vs. 89.26% on PointNet++, with comparable or lower Chamfer distances. The regularized variants REG-EM and REG-AP(-T) achieve good imperceptibility (Chamfer ~1.7–7.8×10⁻⁴) but fail as attacks, retaining test accuracies above 70%, whereas FC-EM maintains similar imperceptibility (8.4×10⁻⁴) while driving accuracy below 33%. On the medical IntrA dataset, FC-EM achieves F1-scores of 0.000 (PointNet++/DGCNN) versus 0.288–0.545 for all baselines, and on BFM-2017 it reduces accuracy to 11.0% versus 46.9–91.1% for baselines. FC-EM also outperforms all methods under every defense tested (PGD, TRADES, Mixup, Rsmix, IF-Defense) and shows strong transferability with all cross-model accuracies below 40%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2D availability attack literature (Unlearnable Examples by Huang et al. 2020 and Adversarial Poisons by Fowl et al. 2021) to 3D point clouds, identifying a fundamental obstacle: the unordered, unstructured nature of point clouds necessitates Chamfer distance regularization rather than ℓ_p norm constraints, and this substitution causes the bi-level optimization to degenerate (Theorem 4.1), rendering poisons ineffective. The proposed FC-EM method departs from prior work by replacing the cross-entropy loss in the inner optimization with a feature collision loss inspired by contrastive learning (Chen et al. 2020, SimCLR), creating shortcuts in feature space rather than logit space to induce divergent update directions that break the degeneracy equilibrium.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of data privacy and security for 3D point cloud deep learning, establishing a baseline for a previously unexplored problem area with practical implications for medical imaging, face recognition, and autonomous driving. The theoretical degeneracy result (that distance regularization in bi-level optimization collapses poisons to zero) is a non-trivial finding that cautions against naive cross-domain transfer of optimization-based attacks. Key open questions include developing defensive strategies against 3D availability attacks, improving imperceptibility (the authors note residual clustering artifacts), and extending the approach to handle partial data leakage scenarios where only a subset of training data is poisoned.
