## Problem

While generative models are increasingly evaluated for statistical accuracy (closeness to the true distribution), no prior work provides a mathematical guarantee that the learned distribution deviates from the empirical distribution of the training set—i.e., that it does not merely replicate observed examples. This paper addresses the missing theoretical link between the creativity/diversity of generated samples and the structural properties of the generator, showing that left-invertibility of the push-forward map is sufficient to ensure maximal deviation from the empirical distribution without sacrificing minimax-optimal precision.

## Input / Output

The method takes a latent vector u drawn uniformly from [0,1]^d (where d is the latent dimension, assumed much smaller than the ambient dimension D) and produces a generated sample g(u) in [0,1]^D via a smooth push-forward map. Training data consist of n i.i.d. points in [0,1]^D. Experiments cover both low-dimensional point data (Swiss Roll, d=2, D=2) and images (MNIST at 28×28 grayscale, CIFAR-10 at 32×32 RGB), with the latent dimension d chosen well below the pixel dimension. A key structural constraint is that the generator g must admit a smooth left inverse h: [0,1]^D → [0,1]^d, enforced either as a hard constraint (λ=∞) or via a soft penalty with tuning parameter λ.

## Method

The paper introduces LIPERM, a penalized variant of WGAN whose objective is W1(g#U_d, P_{n,X}) + λ·pen_H(g), where the left-inverse penalty pen_H(g) = min_{h∈H} ||h∘g − Id_d||_{L_q}^q encourages the push-forward map to be nearly invertible with a Lipschitz-continuous inverse. The central theoretical result (Proposition 1) proves that any generator with an L_H-Lipschitz left inverse satisfies W1(g#U_d, Q) ≥ 1/(2L_H(1+(2V_d n)^{1/d})) for any distribution Q concentrated on the n training points, establishing a lower bound of order n^{-1/d} on deviation from the empirical distribution. A companion upper bound (Theorem 2) shows the IPM distance to the true distribution is at most O(n^{-1/d}) under a manifold assumption, confirming minimax-rate optimality independent of the ambient dimension D. In practice, LIPERM is implemented as a standard WGAN-GP with an additional inverse network H_φ trained jointly with the generator to minimize ||H_φ(G_θ(u)) − u||², and experiments on Swiss Roll, MNIST, and CIFAR-10 confirm that increasing λ preserves generation quality while driving the left-inverse penalty toward zero.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution establishes that a generator with a smooth left inverse (LIPERM with λ=∞) is separated from any distribution concentrated on the n training points by at least W₁ ≥ 1/(2L_H(1+(2V_d n)^{1/d})), which is of order n^{-1/d}. The precision upper bound (Theorem 2) shows E[d_F(ĝ#U_d, P*)] ≤ inf{d_F + λpen} + 4σ* + ε + cL*√d/n^{1/d}, which is minimax-rate-optimal and independent of the ambient dimension D. Empirically, on MNIST and CIFAR-10, increasing λ from 0 to 8 does not degrade the Inception Score or generator loss, while the left-inverse penalty is effectively minimized (generator becomes nearly left-invertible). On Swiss Roll, training instability persists regardless of λ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not perform a head-to-head quantitative comparison against other generative methods; the implicit baseline is vanilla WGAN (λ=0). On MNIST, generated digits for λ=1, 4, 8 are visually indistinguishable in quality from the λ=0 WGAN output, while the left-inverse penalty drops to near zero, confirming the generator is nearly left-invertible. On CIFAR-10, Inception Scores and generator losses across λ∈{0,1,4,8} are nearly identical, with λ=8 yielding the smallest left-inverse penalty (closest to exact invertibility). The key differentiator is theoretical: no prior work provides a finite-sample lower bound on the Wasserstein-1 distance between the learned distribution and the empirical distribution, which LIPERM achieves at the minimax-optimal rate n^{-1/d}. The paper notes that on standard datasets with the architectures tested, replication is not an observed issue, so the creativity benefit of the penalty is not empirically demonstrated beyond the theoretical guarantee.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the WGAN framework (Arjovsky et al., 2017; Gulrajani et al., 2017) by introducing a left-inverse penalty on the generator's push-forward map, drawing conceptual inspiration from the inverse-consistency losses in variational autoencoders (Kingma and Welling, 2014) and Cycle-GAN (Zhu et al., 2017). Unlike prior work on GAN diversity that targets mode collapse (ensuring coverage of all modes of the true distribution), this paper addresses a different axis of diversity: guaranteeing that the learned distribution deviates from the *empirical* distribution of training data, thereby preventing replication. The theoretical analysis builds on minimax optimality results for adversarial distribution estimation (Schreuder et al., 2021; Tang and Yang, 2023) and classical Wasserstein convergence rates (Dudley, 1969; Boissard and Gouic, 2014), showing that the left-invertibility constraint is compatible with rate-optimal learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first finite-sample lower bound quantifying how far a generative distribution must be from the empirical distribution when the generator has a smooth left inverse, and shows this bound is tight (order n^{-1/d}) while simultaneously achieving minimax-optimal precision. This resolves a conceptual tension: the constraint that prevents overfitting to training examples does not sacrifice statistical efficiency. However, the empirical validation is limited — on standard datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, Swiss Roll), the authors could not demonstrate visible creativity gains because replication was not an issue with the chosen architectures, and Swiss Roll training remained unstable. Open questions include extending the analysis to diffusion models, higher-smoothness function classes, non-IPM distances (e.g., Sinkhorn divergence), and understanding the interplay between latent dimension choice and the Lipschitz constant.
