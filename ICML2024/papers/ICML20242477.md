## Problem

Despite LLMs' demonstrated ability to perform arithmetic calculations, the internal mechanisms by which they execute these computations remain largely unexplained. No prior work has identified which specific transformer components (attention heads, MLPs) are causally responsible for arithmetic, characterized their operational behavior, or leveraged this understanding for targeted model improvement without degrading general capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242477/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes arithmetic calculation sentences (e.g., 'The addition of 3 and 5 equals to _') with single-digit operands (since LLaMA2 tokenizes each digit individually) as input to pre-trained LLaMA2-7B/13B models, paired with counterfactual sentences that preserve grammatical structure but remove calculation logic. It produces three types of outputs: (1) a sparse set of key attention heads (<5% of all heads) and MLPs ranked by causal effect on the answer logit, (2) attention pattern and embedding similarity analyses revealing operand/operator focus and layer-by-layer computation, and (3) a fine-tuned model where only ~32 heads' parameters (0.07B out of 6.7B) are updated, yielding improved math accuracy while preserving MMLU/CSQA performance.

## Method

The paper proposes a three-stage 'identify-analyze-finetune' pipeline. First, path patching (a causal intervention technique) measures each head's and MLP's effect on the correct-answer logit by replacing its activation with the counterfactual-data activation while freezing all other components; heads causing >5% logit change are designated key heads, validated via mean ablation knockout showing ~70% accuracy drop. Second, attention pattern analysis reveals key heads selectively attend to operand and operator tokens, while MLP probing via cosine similarity with unembedding vectors shows a two-phase computation: layers 12–17 collect operand information, then layers 17–28 progressively refine the answer in a coarse-to-fine, layer-by-layer manner analogous to binary addition. Third, precise SFT updates only the W_Q, W_K, W_V, W_O matrices of the top-32 key heads (with gradient rescaled by H/h), achieving math improvements comparable to or exceeding full-model SFT (e.g., +12.8% on GSM8K vs +10.0%) while maintaining general task performance and reducing training time by over 3×.

## Results

On LLaMA2-7B, Precise SFT (tuning only 32 attention heads, 0.07B parameters) achieves 27.4% on GSM8K, 55.8% on SVAMP, and 69.7% on SingleEq, matching or surpassing Full SFT (6.7B parameters) which scores 24.6%, 50.3%, and 68.2% respectively. Crucially, Precise SFT preserves generic task performance (46.4% MMLU, 59.6% CSQA) while Full SFT degrades them by ~5.5–5.8%. Training speed is 50 samples/sec for Precise SFT versus 15 samples/sec for Full SFT, a 3× speedup. Similar trends hold for LLaMA2-13B, where Precise SFT reaches 46.3% GSM8K and 66.6% SVAMP while maintaining 55.0% MMLU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between Precise SFT (tuning 32 identified key attention heads) and Full SFT (tuning all parameters) under identical training settings. Precise SFT outperforms Full SFT on GSM8K by +2.8% (7B) and +1.7% (13B), and on SVAMP by +5.5% (7B) and +3.8% (13B), while also leading on SingleEq by +1.5% (7B) and +2.4% (13B). On AddSub, Full SFT edges out Precise SFT by +3.1% (7B) and +1.1% (13B), the only task where the full-parameter approach wins. The critical advantage of Precise SFT is on generic tasks: it maintains MMLU and CSQA scores within 0.2–0.4% of the base model, whereas Full SFT causes drops of 4.6–5.8% on both benchmarks. Additionally, Precise SFT trains 3× faster (50 vs. 15 sam./sec on 7B) while updating less than 1% of total parameters, making it a strictly more efficient alternative for boosting mathematical capability without collateral damage to general language abilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the causal intervention toolkit (path patching and mean ablation) established by Wang et al. (2023a) and Goldowsky-Dill et al. (2023) to the specific domain of arithmetic calculation in LLMs, building on the residual-stream framework of Elhage et al. (2021) and the MLP-as-additive-update analysis of Geva et al. (2022). It departs from prior math-interpretability work (Stolfo et al. 2023, Hanna et al. 2023, Wu et al. 2023) by providing a complete identify-analyze-finetune pipeline rather than stopping at component identification, and by demonstrating cross-dataset transferability of the discovered circuits. The precise fine-tuning strategy also distinguishes itself from Panigrahi et al. (2023), which locates sparse parameters via L1-regularized optimization on an already-fine-tuned model, whereas this paper identifies task-relevant components via causal effect measurement on the pre-trained model and then fine-tunes them directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that tuning only 32 attention heads (0.07B of 6.7B parameters) matches or surpasses full-model SFT on math benchmarks while preserving general capabilities offers a practical, parameter-efficient recipe for targeted capability enhancement. The mechanistic narrative—sparse heads attend to operands/operators, then downstream MLPs perform a coarse-to-fine layer-by-layer computation—provides a human-understandable account of how LLMs execute arithmetic, which is a step toward reliable and auditable mathematical reasoning. Open questions include whether this sparse-circuit structure generalizes to multi-step word problems (the paper shows partial overlap with GSM8K but identifies additional text-attending heads), whether it holds across architectures beyond the LLaMA2/Mistral family, and how to extend precise tuning to MLPs without the 15% efficiency penalty observed per additional MLP.
