## Problem

Deep time series models suffer severe performance degradation in data-scarce scenarios, yet the field lacks the large-scale pre-training infrastructure, unified data format, and generative architecture that enabled large language models to achieve few-shot generalization and task versatility. Existing pre-trained time series models are constrained to small scales, single tasks (typically forecasting), and encoder-only architectures that do not scale well for autoregressive generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241313/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Timer consumes heterogeneous time series (univariate, multivariate, irregularly sampled) converted into the S3 single-series sequence format, where each token is a segment of S consecutive time points (e.g., S=96) and the context length is N tokens (e.g., N=15, yielding up to 1440 time points). For forecasting, the model iteratively generates next tokens autoregressively to produce arbitrary-length predictions; for imputation, masked segments are replaced with special tokens and the model generates reconstructions via denoising autoencoding; for anomaly detection, the model predicts the next segment from observed context and flags segments whose prediction error exceeds a quantile threshold. All three tasks share the same next-token prediction objective, unifying them under a single generative framework.

## Method

Timer is a decoder-only Transformer pre-trained with GPT-style next-token prediction on the Unified Time Series Dataset (UTSD), a curated corpus of up to 1 billion time points spanning seven domains with hierarchical difficulty (UTSD-1G through UTSD-12G). The key architectural choice is the S3 format, which normalizes and pools single-variate series across datasets, then uniformly samples fixed-length windows—extending channel independence by removing the time-alignment requirement and enabling cross-dataset, cross-domain pre-training. The model uses segment-level tokens (consecutive time points of length S) with independent encode/decode projections and optional timestamp embeddings, supervised by MSE loss on each predicted segment. Empirically, the decoder-only architecture outperforms encoder-only, MLP, CNN, and RNN backbones in downstream generalization after pre-training, and the autoregressive generation enables flexible context lengths, iterative multi-step forecasting, and on-the-fly anomaly detection without task-specific retraining.

## Results

On forecasting, Timer fine-tuned with only 1% of ETTh1 samples, 5% of Traffic, and 3% of PEMS03 already matches or surpasses SOTA small deep forecasters (PatchTST/iTransformer) trained on full data. Pre-training on UTSD-12G reduces prediction error from 0.165→0.154 (Weather), 0.126→0.118 (PEMS03), and 0.125→0.107 (PEMS04) versus training from scratch at full data. In segment-level imputation, Timer outperforms TimesNet in 100% of 44 scenarios at 5% data scarcity and 86.4% at 20%. Under few-shot forecasting with scaled-up models (50M params, UTSD-12G), Timer reduces PEMS prediction error to 0.138, surpassing iTransformer's full-data result of 0.139.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Timer (decoder-only, pre-trained on UTSD-12G) outperforms the encoder-only Transformer in nearly all downstream forecasting scenarios after pre-training (Table 1), confirming that the GPT-style architecture generalizes better than BERT-style for time series. Against SOTA small deep forecasters, Timer with only 1–5% of training data matches or beats PatchTST and iTransformer trained on 100% of data on ETTh1, Traffic, and PEMS03. In imputation, Timer surpasses TimesNet in 100% of 44 scenarios at 5% data and 86.4% at 20%, while in anomaly detection on the UCR Anomaly Archive (250 tasks), Timer achieves a lower average quantile than both TimesNet and Anomaly Transformer. In the backbone comparison (Figure 12), the decoder-only Transformer achieves lower validation loss than TiDE (MLP), TCN (CNN), and LSTM, validating Transformer as the scalable architecture for LTSMs. In zero-shot forecasting across seven held-out datasets, Timer ranks among the top three LTSMs alongside Moirai and TimesFM, outperforming Chronos and MOMENT, though the correlation between pre-training scale and performance remains weak across all models.

## Relation to Prior Work

Timer directly extends the Channel Independence (CI) concept from PatchTST into the S3 single-series sequence format, but departs fundamentally from the encoder-only, non-autoregressive paradigm that dominates time series forecasting by adopting a decoder-only, GPT-style autoregressive architecture. The generative pre-training objective (next-token prediction) is borrowed from the LLM literature (Radford et al., 2018), while the denoising autoencoding adaptation for imputation follows T5 (Raffel et al., 2020). Unlike prior time series pre-training efforts that rely on masked modeling or contrastive learning and focus on in-dataset transfer, Timer unifies heterogeneous time series into a single format and pre-trains at scale (up to 1B time points) for cross-domain, cross-task generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first comprehensive framework for large time series models, unifying forecasting, imputation, and anomaly detection under a single generative objective, and releases UTSD (1B time points) as critical infrastructure for the field. The finding that decoder-only Transformers outperform the dominant encoder-only architecture after pre-training challenges a prevailing assumption in time series modeling and aligns with LLM evidence. Open questions remain around probabilistic forecasting, longer context lengths, and the weak correlation between pre-training scale and zero-shot performance, suggesting that data quality and model-data co-scaling are still unsolved. The zero-shot LTSM benchmark is a timely contribution but will need continuous expansion as new models emerge.
