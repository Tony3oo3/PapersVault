## Problem

Standard rested bandits (arms evolve only when pulled) and restless bandits (all arms evolve every round) are too rigid for scenarios where pulling one arm triggers the evolution of only a subset of other arms. This paper introduces Graph-Triggered Rising Bandits (GTRBs), a generalization where an undirected graph over the arms encodes which pairs of arms trigger each other's reward evolution, with rested and restless bandits recovered as degenerate cases (identity and all-ones adjacency matrices, respectively).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240615/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a binary symmetric adjacency matrix G (with 1s on the diagonal) defining arm interactions, a subgaussianity bound σ, a sliding-window parameter ε ∈ (0, 1/2), and a time horizon T. The output is an arm-selection policy producing sublinear regret bounds whose form depends on the graph's clique structure (number and sizes of cliques). Notably, the stochastic algorithm R-□-UCB does not require G as input—it plays as if the graph were the identity matrix—while the graph structure appears only in the regret analysis via a minimal block-diagonal super-matrix.

## Method

The core mechanism is a biased estimator that propagates each arm's reward function to the current round by extrapolating from its last two (deterministic) or last 2h (stochastic) observations using the concept of internal time (the number of triggers an arm has undergone). For block-diagonal adjacency matrices (disjoint cliques), the optimal policy admits a closed form: play greedily within the clique with maximum cumulative reward, and the regret decomposes into a rested component (identifying the best clique) and a restless component (identifying the best arm within it), with clique sizes acting as multiplicative constants. For general graphs, computing the optimal policy is NP-hard, so DR-G-UB uses a maximal block-diagonal sub-matrix as a computationally efficient proxy. In the stochastic setting, R-□-UCB achieves sublinear regret for any graph without knowing it, with the regret bound interpolating between the rested and restless corner cases through the minimal super-matrix's clique structure.

## Results

The paper establishes that computing the optimal policy in GTRBs with arbitrary graphs is NP-hard (Theorem 1), while for block-diagonal adjacency matrices the optimal policy admits a closed-form greedy solution within the best cumulative-reward clique (Theorem 2). For the stochastic setting, R-□-UCB achieves a regret bound of Õ(min_q {(σT)^(2/3) + k̄₁·T^q·Υ_ν(⌈T/k̄₁⌉,q) + T^(2q/(1+q))·Σ|C_m|·Υ_ν(⌈T/|C_m|⌉,q)^(1/(1+q))}), where the three terms correspond to variance, rested bias, and restless bias contributions respectively. In the deterministic setting, DR-BG-UB achieves a regret of Õ(inf_q {T^q·Σ|C_m|·Υ_ν(⌈Ñ_{C_m,T}/|C_m|⌉,q) + Σ|C_m|·Ñ_{C_m,T}^(q/(1+q))·Υ_ν(⌈Ñ_{C_m,T}/|C_m|⌉,q)^(1/(1+q))}), with the two terms capturing rested and restless bias. The bounds recover the known state-of-the-art results for both rested and restless rising bandits as special cases.

The key novelty is that the GTRB bounds interpolate between these two extremes based on the graph's clique structure, with the number and sizes of cliques controlling the relative weight of rested versus restless contributions. The paper also notes that in the stochastic setting, R-□-UCB does not require knowledge of the graph structure, whereas in the deterministic setting, DR-G-UB must compute a maximal block-diagonal sub-matrix of G, introducing additional computational overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces Graph-Triggered Rising Bandits (GTRBs) as a principled generalization of both rested and restless stochastic rising bandits, unifying them as degenerate cases of a graph-structured model (identity matrix for rested, all-ones matrix for restless). The theoretical machinery builds directly on the R-□-UCB algorithm and regret analysis from Metelli et al. (2022) for stochastic rising bandits, as well as the optimal policy characterization from Heidari et al. (2016) for improving/decaying bandits. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of an adjacency matrix governing which arms' expected rewards evolve when another arm is pulled, a structure absent from both prior rested and restless formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a clean unifying lens for understanding how inter-arm dependencies affect learning complexity, with regret bounds that explicitly interpolate between the rested and restless corner cases based on graph topology (clique sizes, number of connected components). A practically important finding is that R-□-UCB achieves sublinear regret for arbitrary graph structures without requiring knowledge of the graph, avoiding the NP-hard optimal policy computation. Open questions include extending the framework to rotting bandits (where the paper notes a crucial asymmetry with rising bandits), handling more general (non-block-diagonal) graph structures with tighter bounds, and investigating whether the concavity assumption can be relaxed.
