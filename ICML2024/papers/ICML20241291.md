## Problem

Existing non-asymptotic sample complexity bounds for SAA under heavy-tailed assumptions still depend on complexity measures of the feasible region (e.g., covering numbers or generic chaining functionals), which grow polynomially with dimensionality d. This paper asks whether SAA and its regularized variant can achieve sample complexity bounds completely free from any such feasible-region complexity measure under standard regularity conditions comparable to the typical SAA literature.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an i.i.d. sample ξ₁,…,ξ_N of size N from the distribution of ξ, a convex feasible region X ⊆ R^d, and a deterministic cost function f(x,ξ); for the regularized variant (RSAA), it additionally requires hyperparameters λ₀, a norm index q' ∈ (1,2], and an initial guess x⁰. The output is the optimal solution x̂ to the SAA or RSAA formulation together with a sample complexity bound specifying the minimum N needed to guarantee F(x̂) − F(x*) ≤ ε with probability at least 1−β. Notably, the heavy-tailed assumption only requires bounded variance of ∇f(·,ξ) (Assumption 4.1), which is weaker than the sub-Gaussian or finite fourth-moment conditions in prior work.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a proof strategy based on 'average-replace-one (average-RO) stability': for each j, an alternative SAA formulation F_N^(j) is constructed by replacing the j-th sample with an independent i.i.d. copy ξ_j', and the expected squared q-norm distance between the solutions of the original and modified formulations is bounded. This stability bound, combined with the composite objective decomposition (Lipschitz-gradient component F₁ + Lipschitz component F₂) and the bounded gradient variance assumption, yields dimension-free sample complexity rates without any uniform convergence argument (no ε-nets, no generic chaining). For strongly convex SP, the bound is O(max{L/μ, (σ²ₚ+M²)/(μεβ)}); for general convex SP with Tikhonov regularization, it is O(V_{q'}(x*)/(q'−1) · max{L/ε, (σ²ₚ+M²)/(ε²β)}). The approach exploits the strong convexity of f(·,ξ) (or the (q'−1)-strong convexity of the regularized objective) to convert the stability bound into a suboptimality gap, and uses Young's inequality with carefully chosen parameters to balance the smoothness and Lipschitz terms.

## Results

The paper establishes that SAA for strongly convex SP achieves a sample complexity of N ≥ O(max{L/μ, (σ_p² + M²)/(μεβ)}) and RSAA for general convex SP achieves N ≥ O(R*/(q'−1) · max{L/ε, (σ_p² + M²)/(ε²β)}), both completely free from any complexity measure of the feasible region. In the most favorable regime (M=0, small ε), the bounds simplify to O(σ_p²/(μεβ)) for strongly convex and O(V_{q'}(x*)/(q'−1) · σ_p²/(ε²β)) for general convex SP. Under component-wise moment bounds, σ_p² scales as d^{2/p}, so for p > 2 the dimension dependence is sub-polynomial, and for p ≥ c·ln d the bound becomes fully dimension-free.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's bounds are compared against three families of prior results. Against the light-tailed benchmark of Shapiro et al. (2021), which requires sub-Gaussian tails and incurs a covering-number term Γ_ε(X) growing polynomially in d, this paper removes the complexity measure entirely and relaxes the tail assumption to bounded variance. Against the heavy-tailed benchmark of Oliveira & Thompson (2023), which uses generic chaining γ(X*,ε) ≤ O(√d · D*) and requires finite fourth moments, this paper achieves dimension-free (or sub-polynomial) dependence while only requiring a bounded second moment of the gradient. Against stability-based results (Bousquet & Elisseeff, 2002; Shalev-Shwartz et al., 2010; Hu et al., 2020) that are already dimension-free, this paper works under the weaker composite-objective regularity of Assumption 1.1 (Lipschitz gradient + bounded gradient) rather than the uniform Lipschitz condition |f(x,ξ)−f(y,ξ)| ≤ M‖x−y‖ with M independent of ξ, which can be unbounded in many practical SP problems. The proof technique (average-RO stability) is novel for SAA analysis and differentiates from both uniform convergence (ε-net, generic chaining) and uniform stability approaches used in prior work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant SAA analysis paradigm that relies on uniform convergence over the feasible region (via covering numbers or generic chaining functionals) by instead leveraging average-RO stability, a concept from learning theory (Shalev-Shwartz et al., 2010) that had not previously been applied to non-asymptotic SAA sample complexity. It also works under a bounded-variance condition on the gradient (Assumption 4.1) that is weaker than the sub-Gaussian/sub-exponential tail assumptions in Shapiro et al. (2021) or the finite higher-moment conditions in Oliveira & Thompson (2023), while maintaining the same composite smooth-plus-Lipschitz objective structure standard in the SP literature. The regularized SAA (RSAA) formulation for general convex problems follows the Tikhonov-regularization approach of Hu et al. (2020) and Feldman & Vondrak (2019), but achieves bounds free of feasible-region complexity measures under less restrictive tail assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that SAA sample complexity can be provably free from any feasible-region complexity measure under standard regularity conditions challenges a long-standing assumption in the SAA literature that such measures are unavoidable, potentially reducing the predicted dimension dependence from polynomial to sub-polynomial or even dimension-free (when p ≥ c ln d). The average-RO stability proof technique is identified as potentially of independent interest and may extend to nonconvex problems or stochastic constraints. Key limitations include the requirement of deterministic constraints, convexity of f(·,ξ) a.s., and bounded gradient variance; the authors note (Remark 4.3) that extending to a more flexible variance condition of the form C‖x−x*‖²_q + σ²_p remains open, as does the nonconvex and stochastic-constraint setting.
