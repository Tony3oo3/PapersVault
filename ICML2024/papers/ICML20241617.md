## Problem

Prior work on optimal ridge regularization characterized the sign of the optimal penalty and the monotonicity of optimal risk only for in-distribution prediction or restricted to non-negative regularization. This paper asks: under arbitrary covariate or regression shift, how does the optimal ridge penalty change (can it become negative even in the underparameterized regime?), and does the optimally tuned risk remain monotonic in the data aspect ratio? These questions are motivated by empirical observations that negative ridge penalties can be optimal on real datasets and by the practical prevalence of distribution shift.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a training dataset (X, y) with feature covariance Σ, L₂ projection signal β, and conditional variance σ², along with a test distribution parameterized by (Σ₀, β₀, σ₀²), and a regularization level λ in the range (λ_min(φ), ∞) where λ_min(φ) is a tight negative lower bound derived from the smallest eigenvalue of the sample Gram matrix. Under proportional asymptotics (p/n → φ), the method produces deterministic equivalents for the OOD prediction risk decomposed into bias, variance, regression-shift bias, and irreducible error, from which the optimal penalty λ* and optimal risk are characterized. The key unusual aspect is that λ is allowed to be negative, with the lower bound λ_min(φ) significantly tighter than the naive bound used in prior work.

## Method

The paper establishes a deterministic equivalent for OOD ridge risk (Proposition 2.4) under only moment-bounded response assumptions and arbitrary feature covariance, extending prior IND results to the full range of negative regularization. It then derives 'general alignment' conditions between the signal vector and the feature covariance spectrum that determine the sign of λ* under covariate shift (Theorem 3.3) and regression shift (Theorem 3.4), revealing that negative optimal regularization can occur even in the underparameterized regime (φ < 1) under regression shift—a phenomenon absent in the IND setting. For risk monotonicity, the authors extend the subsampling-ridge equivalence framework of Patil & Du (2023) to accommodate negative regularization (Theorem 4.4), proving that the optimally tuned OOD risk is monotonic in both φ and SNR under arbitrary distribution shift (Theorem 4.2), while suboptimally tuned risk is generally non-monotonic (Theorem 4.3). The fixed-point equations governing the risk are analyzed over the complex half-plane to handle the negative regularization regime rigorously.

## Results

The paper establishes that the optimal ridge penalty λ* can be negative under both covariate shift and regression shift, even in the underparameterized regime (φ < 1) where it is always non-negative in the in-distribution setting. On MNIST, as the test distribution is progressively shifted by excluding labels, the optimal penalty transitions from positive to negative: λ* = 1.03 (no shift) → 0.48 → 0.00 → −0.48 → −1.44 (excluding labels {1,2,3,4}). The optimally tuned OOD risk is proven to be monotonically increasing in the data aspect ratio φ and SNR, even when optimizing over negative regularization levels, while suboptimally tuned risk is shown to be non-monotonic.

| Excluded test labels | λ* |
|---|---|
| ∅ (no shift) | 1.03 |
| {4} | 0.48 |
| {3, 4} | 0.00 |
| {2, 3, 4} | −0.48 |
| {1, 2, 3, 4} | −1.44 |

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper, so comparisons are with prior analytical results rather than empirical baselines. Compared to Dobriban & Wager (2018), which shows λ* = φ/SNR > 0 for isotropic signals in the IND setting, this paper shows that covariate shift with anisotropic signals can make λ* < 0 even when training features are isotropic (Theorem 3.3). Compared to Wu & Xu (2020), which requires strict alignment between signal and covariance spectrum and only covers the overparameterized regime, this paper provides general alignment conditions that hold in both under- and overparameterized regimes and allow arbitrary response models. Compared to Patil & Du (2023), which proves monotonic optimal risk only for IND and positive regularization, this paper extends the monotonicity guarantee to the OOD setting and the full range of negative regularization (Theorem 4.2). The MNIST experiment (Table 2) provides empirical validation that distribution shift drives λ* negative, a phenomenon not captured by any prior IND-only theory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on optimal ridge regularization from the in-distribution (IND) setting to the out-of-distribution (OOD) setting, building directly on the risk asymptotics framework of Dobriban & Wager (2018) and the monotonicity results of Patil & Du (2023). It generalizes the strict alignment conditions of Wu & Xu (2020) and Richards et al. (2021) for negative optimal regularization into broader 'general alignment' conditions that apply to arbitrary covariance and signal structures. The subsampling-ridge equivalences from Du et al. (2023) and Patil & Du (2023) are extended to accommodate negative regularization and OOD risks, with the tighter negative-penalty lower bound from LeJeune et al. (2024) enabling analysis over the full feasible range.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a unified theoretical framework showing that distribution shift can fundamentally alter the optimal regularization landscape—most notably, that negative regularization becomes optimal even in the underparameterized regime under regression shift, a regime where it is provably never optimal in the IND setting. The monotonicity of optimally tuned OOD risk in the aspect ratio φ, holding under arbitrary covariate and regression shift, gives practitioners confidence that optimal tuning avoids double-descent pathologies even under distribution shift. Open questions include data-dependent tuning methods for OOD settings (the paper only characterizes oracle-optimal λ*), extension to kernel ridge regression and lasso, and understanding whether the alignment conditions identified here can be estimated from data without knowledge of the test distribution.
