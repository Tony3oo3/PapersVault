## Problem

Standard multi-task learning (MTL) gradient aggregation methods treat each task's gradient as a single deterministic vector, ignoring the fact that different dimensions of the gradient have different sensitivities to change. Some dimensions are more lenient while others are more restrictive, but this per-dimension uncertainty is lost when only a point gradient is used. The paper asks: can we recover this uncertainty information and use it to derive a better shared update direction?

## Input / Output

The method operates on a shared feature extractor with linear task-specific heads, consuming inputs ranging from molecular graphs (QM9), RGB images (CIFAR-100, UTKFace), to X-ray images (ChestX-ray14), and producing regression values, binary labels, or multi-class labels depending on the task. Crucially, the Bayesian inference is applied only to the last task-specific linear layer, and gradient aggregation is performed in the space of the last shared hidden representation (typically low-dimensional) rather than over all shared parameters, keeping the computation tractable. The method assigns a unique weight to each gradient dimension for each task and each datum in the batch, a finer granularity than existing per-task or per-dimension weighting schemes.

## Method

BayesAgg-MTL places a Bayesian posterior over the last task-specific linear layer weights, which induces a distribution over the per-task gradients w.r.t. the last shared hidden representation. For regression tasks, the posterior is Gaussian in closed form and gradient moments are derived analytically via moment matching; for classification tasks, a second-order Taylor expansion (retaining the first-order term, unlike prior Laplace approximations) yields a Gaussian posterior, and gradient moments are estimated via Monte Carlo sampling from the posterior. The aggregation step finds an update direction that maximizes the product of per-task Gaussian likelihoods, solved in closed form under a diagonal covariance assumption, yielding per-dimension, per-datum, per-task precision-weighted averages of the gradient means. An informative prior—computed from the full-dataset posterior at the end of each epoch—stabilizes the batch-level posterior estimation, and a scaling hyperparameter s ∈ (0,1] down-weights the precision to mitigate model misspecification.

## Results

On QM9 (11 regression tasks), BayesAgg-MTL achieves 53.2±7.1% Δm, outperforming the next-best method Nash-MTL (62.0±1.4%) by nearly 9 points. On CIFAR-MTL (20 binary classification tasks), it reaches 59.97±0.4% accuracy, surpassing MGDA (59.74%) and RLW (59.30%). On UTKFace (mixed regression + classification), it achieves −2.23±0.76% Δm, the best among all 13 compared methods, with the only other method to beat the single-task baseline on the regression task being MGDA (−1.39%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BayesAgg-MTL outperforms all 13 baselines on QM9, beating the strongest competitor Nash-MTL (Navon et al., 2022) by 8.8 points in Δm% (53.2 vs. 62.0) and IGBv2 (Dai et al., 2023) by 14.5 points. On CIFAR-MTL, it achieves the highest accuracy (59.97%) over MGDA (59.74%) and RLW (Lin et al., 2022; 59.30%), with a clear margin over loss-based methods like DWA (Liu et al., 2019a; 58.44%) and PCGrad (Yu et al., 2020; 56.32%). On UTKFace, it wins the overall Δm% (−2.23%) against MGDA (−1.39%), CAGrad (Liu et al., 2021; −0.58%), and IMTL-G (Liu et al., 2020; −0.50%), and is the only method alongside MGDA to improve upon single-task learning on the age regression task. On ChestX-ray14, the Δm% (−14.96%) is competitive but not the best—IGBv2 (−2.82%) and SI (−10.94%) achieve lower values—though BayesAgg-MTL attains the highest per-task AUC-ROC on most of the 14 disease classes. Against deep ensembles (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017), BayesAgg-MTL substantially outperforms both a 1024-head shared-backbone ensemble (161.4% on QM9) and a 10-network full ensemble (144.5%) while requiring far less computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: Bayesian last-layer inference for deep networks (Snoek et al., 2015; Wilson et al., 2016a) and gradient-based multi-task optimization (PCGrad, Nash-MTL, MGDA, CAGrad). The key departure is treating task-specific parameters as random variables during back-propagation, which induces a distribution over gradients rather than a single deterministic vector. The aggregation rule (Eq. 5) is shown to be a Nash bargaining solution with Gaussian likelihood, connecting it conceptually to Nash-MTL, but the critical difference is that BayesAgg-MTL assigns unique weights per task, per gradient dimension, and per datum in the batch, rather than a single scalar weight per task. For classification, the paper adapts the Laplace approximation with a generalized Gauss-Newton correction to handle the non-Gaussian likelihood, and uses Monte Carlo sampling for moment estimation since closed-form solutions are unavailable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The per-dimension, per-datum uncertainty weighting represents a meaningful resolution improvement over existing MTL methods that assign a single weight per task, and the consistent SOTA across four diverse benchmarks (molecular graphs, CIFAR-100 superclasses, chest X-rays, and mixed regression/classification on faces) suggests the approach generalizes well. However, the method is currently restricted to linear task-specific heads, which limits its applicability to architectures with non-linear task towers; the authors acknowledge this as the primary future direction. The informative prior strategy (computing a full-dataset posterior once per epoch) is a practical but somewhat ad-hoc solution to the batch-only posterior problem, and the diagonal covariance assumption, while necessary for numerical stability, may understate correlations between gradient dimensions. The calibration results (Appendix C.1) hint at a broader benefit of the Bayesian layer beyond gradient aggregation, suggesting potential for improved uncertainty estimation in MTL settings.
