## Problem

Replica exchange SGLD (reSGLD) effectively navigates non-convex landscapes but suffers from over-exploration in high-temperature chains, where particles delve too deeply into distribution tails, causing exploding losses in deep learning or suboptimal regret in Thompson sampling. Existing constrained sampling techniques (reflected Langevin, proximal methods) have not been integrated with replica exchange to simultaneously enable multi-temperature exploration and prevent tail over-exploration within a bounded domain.

## Input / Output

The method consumes mini-batch stochastic gradients of the potential U, initial parameter vectors for P chains (typically 2 or 4), an explicit bounded domain Ω (e.g., a box [-4,4]^d, flower-shaped, heart, or polygon boundary), and a temperature schedule {τ₁,…,τ_P}. It produces posterior parameter samples for analytical use, empirical posterior distributions over the constrained domain, identified dynamical system parameters (e.g., σ, ρ, β for Lorenz), and trained neural networks evaluated via Bayesian model averaging accuracy, NLL, and Brier score. The reflection operator R(·) is applied after every gradient step, and the swap decision uses a corrected intensity that subtracts a noise-variance term to account for stochastic gradient estimation.

## Method

r2SGLD runs parallel Langevin diffusions at distinct temperatures within a compact domain Ω, where each chain is updated via stochastic gradient descent plus injected noise, followed by a reflection step that maps out-of-boundary samples back into Ω using a tangent-line construction at the nearest boundary point. A Metropolis-style swap between chains is accepted with probability governed by a corrected swapping intensity that compensates for stochastic gradient noise. The key theoretical insight is that the Poincaré and Log-Sobolev constants scale as O(1/diam(Ω)²), so reducing the domain diameter quadratically accelerates mixing in both χ²-divergence and W₂-distance. For multi-chain deep learning (P=4), the algorithm adopts the deterministic even-odd (DEO) non-reversible swapping scheme with an adaptive correction term to maintain a target swap rate, enabling near-linear round-trip paths across the temperature ladder.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet20, r2SGHMC achieves 75.38% BMA accuracy, 8489 NLL, and 2.46‰ Brier score, outperforming reSGHMC (75.01%, 8552, 2.50) and cycSGHMC (73.49%, 8913, 2.65). With ResNet56, r2SGHMC reaches 79.39% accuracy, 7155 NLL, and 2.89‰ Brier, surpassing reSGHMC (78.87%, 7406, 2.94) and SGDM (74.40%, 9724, 3.59). The reflection mechanism also enables stable training at learning rates >10.0 where non-reflected baselines fail entirely (test accuracy <10% or infinite loss).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares r2SGHMC against three main families: SGLD/SGDM (Welling & Teh, 2011), cyclical SGLD/SGHMC (Zhang et al., 2020), and replica exchange SGLD/SGHMC (Deng et al., 2020a), each with and without reflection. r2SGHMC consistently outperforms reSGHMC by ~0.4% accuracy and ~250 NLL on ResNet20, and by ~0.5% accuracy and ~250 NLL on ResNet56, confirming that the reflection step adds measurable benefit on top of replica exchange. Against cycSGHMC, r2SGHMC gains ~1.9% accuracy (ResNet20) and ~1.4% (ResNet56), showing that the dual-chain swapping mechanism is more effective than cyclical learning rates alone. Compared to the simplest baseline SGDM, r2SGHMC improves accuracy by 3.25% (ResNet20) and 4.99% (ResNet56) while reducing NLL by over 1200 points. In the multi-modal simulation experiments, r2SGLD achieves the lowest KL divergence among all nine tested methods, outperforming R-SGLD, R-cycSGLD, and penalized variants, with the gap widening as the number of modes increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends replica exchange SGLD (reSGLD, Deng et al. 2020) by incorporating reflection at domain boundaries to prevent over-exploration in high-temperature chains, drawing on the constrained Langevin Monte Carlo framework of Bubeck et al. (2018) and the continuous-time reLD analysis of Chen et al. (2019). The key conceptual departure is that prior constrained samplers (projected Langevin, proximal Langevin, mirrored Langevin) operated on single chains, while here the reflection is applied within a multi-temperature parallel tempering scheme, creating a new interaction between the swap mechanism and the boundary dynamics. The theoretical analysis extends the Poincaré and Log-Sobolev inequality framework from unbounded to bounded non-convex domains, yielding an explicit O(1/diam(Ω)²) scaling for the convergence rate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quadratic dependence of mixing rate on domain diameter provides a principled argument for incorporating prior knowledge (physical constraints, positivity, stability conditions) into the sampling domain, which is broadly applicable beyond the specific experiments shown. The first application of constrained gradient Langevin dynamics to dynamical system identification (Lorenz, Lotka-Volterra) opens a methodological avenue for physics-informed Bayesian inference. Open questions include the spectral gap analysis for r2SGLD in genuinely non-convex domains (acknowledged as beyond current scope), the discretization analysis being limited to convex domains, and whether the large-learning-rate advantage observed on CIFAR-100 persists at ImageNet scale with more chains and longer training.
