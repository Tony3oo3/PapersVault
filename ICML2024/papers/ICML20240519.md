## Problem

Existing DRL-based constructive solvers for routing problems (TSP, CVRP) are typically trained on small-scale, uniform-distribution instances and fail to generalize to larger scales or different node distributions. The paper identifies two root causes: embedding aliasing, where the Lipschitz constraint of neural encoders prevents distinguishing closely-spaced nodes at larger scales, and interference from irrelevant nodes, where attention-based encoders accumulate non-negligible influence from far-away nodes as instance size grows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240519/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 2D Euclidean node coordinates (and per-node demands plus a fixed vehicle capacity for CVRP) as input, all scaled to the unit square. Rather than processing the full graph, it dynamically constructs nested k-nearest-neighbor subgraphs centered on the last visited node at each autoregressive step, restricting the action space to the smallest view (e.g., 15-NN) while larger views (35-NN, 50-NN) provide contextual state information. The output is a complete node visit sequence (tour) generated step-by-step, with the depot node always available as a candidate in CVRP.

## Method

INViT is a Transformer-based autoregressive solver that processes multiple nested local views in parallel. Each view is a k-NN subgraph (e.g., k=50, 35, 15) around the last visited node, processed by an independent single-view encoder (2-layer MHA + FFN) after an invariant normalization layer that min-max normalizes coordinates relative to the local neighborhood, making embeddings scale- and distribution-invariant. The multi-view decoder channel-wise concatenates the per-view embeddings for shared nodes (candidates, last visited, first visited/depot), then uses the last and first visited nodes as query and the candidate nodes as key/value in a final attention layer to produce action probabilities. Training uses REINFORCE with POMO-style data augmentation (rotation, reflection, normalization, and varied starting points across 8 augmented copies per instance), with a greedy baseline model for variance reduction. The key design insight is that restricting both the action space (to k-NN candidates) and the state space (to nested local views) simultaneously mitigates embedding aliasing and irrelevant-node interference, enabling cross-size and cross-distribution generalization from training on only size-100 uniform instances.

## Results

On the MSVDRP dataset, INViT-3V achieves average gaps of 7.97% (TSP-5000), 7.52% (TSP-10000), and 5.63% (CVRP-5000) across four distributions, substantially outperforming all baselines on large-scale instances. On TSPLIB, INViT-3V maintains gaps of 8.61% (1001–10000 nodes) and 9.11% (>10000 nodes), while baselines like POMO degrade to 60.05% and 94.27% respectively. On CVRPLIB Set-X, INViT-3V achieves 10.21% gap on instances >500 nodes, compared to 58.82% for POMO and 12.21% for ELG-v1. Inference time remains competitive at ~10 minutes for TSP-10000 and ~4 minutes for CVRP-5000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

INViT-3V decisively outperforms all six baselines on large-scale instances (TSP-5000/10000, CVRP-5000), where POMO and PointerFormer degrade catastrophically (gaps >50–100%) and even the strongest generalization-focused baselines (Omni-TSP/VRP, ELG-v1) still show gaps of 20–70%. On TSPLIB instances >10000 nodes, INViT-3V achieves 9.11% gap versus 26.73% (ELG-v1), 43.61% (LEHD), and 94.27% (POMO). On CVRPLIB Set-X (>500 nodes), INViT-3V (10.21%) beats ELG-v1 (12.21%), Omni-VRP (11.21%), and BQ-NCO (11.18%), though the margin is narrower. On small-scale instances (TSP-100, CVRP-50), INViT is competitive but not always best (e.g., LEHD achieves 0.57% on TSP-100 uniform vs. INViT-3V's 0.95%), which the authors attribute to the training scale being close to the evaluation scale where embedding aliasing is less severe. The key differentiator is that INViT is trained only on uniform-distribution size-100 instances, whereas Omni-TSP/VRP is trained on sizes 50–200 with diverse distributions, yet INViT still outperforms it on all large-scale benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

INViT builds on the autoregressive solver framework of Kool et al. (2019) and the POMO data augmentation strategy of Kwon et al. (2020), but departs from prior work by identifying two specific failure modes—embedding aliasing and interference from irrelevant nodes—and addressing them through a nested-view encoder with an invariant normalization layer. It is most closely related to ELG (Gao et al., 2023), which also uses k-NN local views, but INViT learns to aggregate views in embedding space, uses nested (rather than parallel) views, and omits a global encoder based on statistical evidence that global context is detrimental. Compared to Omni-TSP/VRP (Zhou et al., 2023), which tackles both cross-size and cross-distribution generalization via meta-RL, INViT achieves comparable or better generalization while being trained only on uniform small-scale instances, avoiding the need for diverse training distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a model trained solely on TSP-100/CVRP-50 uniform instances can generalize to TSP-10000/CVRP-5000 with gaps of only 6–9%, while all compared baselines degrade to 20–100%+, offering a practical path to deploying a single small model across scales and distributions without retraining. The MSVDRP dataset provides a standardized multi-scale, multi-distribution benchmark that the field lacked. Limitations include modest performance on small/medium-scale instances (close to training size) where the generalization mechanisms are less critical, and inference time that, while competitive, is higher than the simplest baselines. Open questions include whether the nested-view + invariance principle transfers to other combinatorial optimization problems beyond routing, and whether adaptive (rather than fixed) k-NN sizes could further improve performance.
