## Problem

Standard diffusion models rely on score matching theory that is well-established for continuous data but has not yielded competitive results on discrete domains like natural language. Existing discrete diffusion approaches (mean prediction, ratio matching, concrete score matching) either use incompatible loss functions (e.g., an ℓ² loss for inherently positive probability ratios) or require intractable computations, resulting in models that significantly underperform autoregressive baselines on both likelihood and generation quality.

## Input / Output

The model operates on sequences of discrete tokens from a vocabulary of size N (e.g., 50257 for GPT-2's BPE tokenizer), with each token position perturbed independently by a sparse transition matrix (uniform or absorbing). During training, the input is a clean token sequence x₀ and a sampled time t, and the network outputs a score tensor s_θ(x_t, t) ∈ ℝ^{d×N} giving the ratio p_t(y)/p_t(x) for every single-token substitution at each position. For generation, the model starts from a base distribution (uniform over tokens or a MASK absorbing state) and iteratively applies τ-leaping updates to produce a full text sequence; for conditional generation, prompt tokens at arbitrary positions (beginning, end, or middle) are held fixed throughout the reverse process.

## Method

SEDD parameterizes the reverse discrete diffusion process by the concrete score (ratios of the data distribution) and introduces score entropy, a loss built on the Bregman divergence with F = −log, which naturally enforces positivity of the ratios through a log-barrier gradient structure—addressing the fundamental incompatibility between the ℓ² loss in concrete score matching and the positivity constraint on probability ratios. The denoising score entropy (DSE) variant replaces the unknown marginal ratio p(y)/p(x) with the known forward transition ratio p(y|x₀)/p(x|x₀), making Monte Carlo estimation tractable with a single network evaluation per sample. The architecture is a diffusion transformer (encoder-only with adaLN-zero time conditioning and rotary positional encoding) that maps a noisy sequence to per-position score vectors. For sampling, the paper derives a Tweedie τ-leaping strategy that is provably optimal among all independent token-update strategies, and an ELBO for likelihood evaluation is obtained via Dynkin's formula, enabling direct perplexity-based training and evaluation without requiring distribution annealing.

## Results

On GPT-2 zero-shot perplexity tasks, SEDD Absorb (medium) beats GPT-2 medium on 4 of 5 datasets (e.g., WikiText-103: ≤29.98 vs 31.39; PTB: ≤87.12 vs 123.14; WikiText-2: ≤31.04 vs 31.80), and SEDD Absorb (small) beats GPT-2 small on 3 of 5. On One Billion Words, SEDD Absorb achieves ≤32.79 perplexity, within 1 point of the autoregressive Transformer (31.98) while reducing perplexity by 50–75% over all prior diffusion baselines (D3PM ≤77.50, DiffusionBert ≤63.78). For unconditional generation, SEDD matches GPT-2 quality using 32× fewer network evaluations and outperforms un-annealed GPT-2 by 6–8× in generative perplexity at full 2048 steps. On conditional generation (MAUVE), SEDD Standard prompting (no annealing) scores 0.957, beating GPT-2 with nucleus sampling (0.955) and SSD-LM with logit thresholding (0.919).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEDD Absorb outperforms GPT-2 (Radford et al., 2019) on 3–4 of 5 zero-shot perplexity tasks at both small and medium scale, with the largest gap on PTB (≤87.12 vs 123.14 at medium). Against prior discrete diffusion models, SEDD reduces perplexity by 50–75%: D3PM (Austin et al., 2021) scores ≤77.50 on 1BW vs SEDD's ≤32.79, and DiffusionBert (He et al., 2022) scores ≤63.78. The continuous diffusion baseline PLAID (Gulrajani & Hashimoto, 2023) is beaten by SEDD Absorb on WikiText-2, PTB, and WikiText-103 at both scales. On text8, SEDD Absorb (≤1.39 BPC) matches AR Argmax Flow (1.39) and beats D3PM Absorb (≤1.45), BFN (≤1.41), and MAC (≤1.40), trailing only the autoregressive Transformer and Discrete Flow (both 1.23). For conditional generation, SEDD Standard (MAUVE 0.957, no annealing) edges out GPT-2 with nucleus-0.95 (0.955) and substantially outperforms SSD-LM (Han et al., 2022) with logit thresholding (0.919) and un-annealed SSD-LM (0.312). SEDD also enables infilling (MAUVE 0.942) without specialized training, a capability not available to the autoregressive baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEDD builds directly on the discrete diffusion framework of D3PM (Austin et al., 2021) and the continuous-time formulation of Campbell et al. (2022), but departs fundamentally from their training objectives. Where D3PM uses mean prediction and Concrete Score Matching (Meng et al., 2022) uses an l2 loss on probability ratios, SEDD introduces score entropy—a Bregman divergence-based loss that naturally enforces positivity of the concrete score and reduces to a tractable denoising variant (analogous to denoising score matching in the continuous case). The network architecture follows the diffusion transformer (Peebles & Xie, 2023), and the ELBO for likelihood evaluation extends Dynkin's formula as formalized in Campbell et al. (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a non-autoregressive discrete diffusion model can match or exceed GPT-2 on zero-shot perplexity benchmarks, challenging the assumption that sequential generation is necessary for competitive language modeling. The ablation showing that replacing score entropy with l2 concrete score matching degrades perplexity by ~10,000× isolates the loss function as the critical ingredient, providing a clear theoretical prescription for future discrete diffusion work. Limitations include evaluation only at GPT-2 scale (no hyperparameter search), reliance on upper-bound likelihoods rather than exact ones, and a gap with modern LLMs that the authors acknowledge. Open questions include whether score entropy scales to much larger models, whether continuous-diffusion heuristics (classifier-free guidance, self-conditioning) can be adapted, and whether the compute-quality tradeoff (32× fewer evaluations for similar quality) holds at scale.
