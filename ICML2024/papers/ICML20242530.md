## Problem

Existing single-cell pre-trained language models (Geneformer, scGPT, scBERT) are trained exclusively on transcriptomic data via self-supervised masked modeling, capturing gene co-expression but lacking any understanding of cell identity semantics (cell type, disease, pathway) that human experts encode in natural language. Consequently, they cannot perform zero-shot cell identity understanding and require costly labeled fine-tuning data that is often unavailable for novel cell types or emerging diseases.

## Input / Output

The model takes scRNA-seq count matrices (normalized and converted to rank-ordered gene sequences of up to 2048 tokens) and multi-perspective text descriptions generated from OBO Foundry ontologies covering 8 cell identity aspects (assay, cell type, developmental stage, tissue, organ, disease, donor sex, ethnicity). At inference, it produces cell embeddings in a shared latent space with text embeddings, cell-text matching scores via cross-attention, and downstream predictions such as cell type labels, pathway multi-labels, or batch-integrated embeddings. Zero-shot classification combines cosine similarity scores and cross-attention matching scores with a tunable weight α (default 0.2), and the number of top-k candidates passed to the slower matching module is adjustable for speed-accuracy tradeoff.

## Method

LangCell pairs a Geneformer-initialized cell encoder (transformer with [CLS] token) with a PubMedBERT-initialized text encoder that operates in two modes: unimodal (standard BERT) and multimodal (BLIP-style pluggable cross-attention after each self-attention layer). Pre-training jointly optimizes four losses: Masked Gene Modeling for gene reconstruction, Cell-Cell contrastive learning using dropout-based positive pairs with a momentum encoder, Cell-Text contrastive learning (CLIP-style bidirectional InfoNCE) to align cell and text embeddings, and Cell-Text Matching (binary cross-entropy via the multimodal cross-attention head) for fine-grained cell-text correspondence. Training is two-stage: first unimodal (MGM + C-C) to establish strong single-cell representations, then multimodal (all four losses) to inject text-based cell identity knowledge. The key insight is using OBO Foundry-derived text as distant supervision during pre-training, enabling the cell encoder to internalize cell identity semantics without any task-specific labels, which is what makes true zero-shot cell type annotation possible.

## Results

LangCell is the only single-cell PLM capable of zero-shot cell type annotation, achieving 86.5% accuracy and 89.6% macro F1 on PBMC10K and 83.9% accuracy and 82.6% macro F1 on PBMC3&68K without any fine-tuning. In zero-shot NSCLC subtype classification, LangCell reaches 93.5% accuracy, surpassing Geneformer fine-tuned with 10-shot learning (73.1%) by approximately 20 percentage points. For cell batch integration, LangCell achieves the best S_final score on both PBMC10K (87.6) and Perirhinal Cortex (95.4), outperforming scVI, scGPT, and Geneformer. In fine-tuning cell type annotation, LangCell-CE attains SOTA on all three benchmarks (e.g., 98.3% Acc on PBMC10K, 85.4% Acc on Zheng68K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LangCell's zero-shot performance (86.5% Acc / 89.6% F1 on PBMC10K) exceeds the 9-shot results of Geneformer (88.0% / 78.6%), scGPT (86.7% / 75.8%), and scBERT (81.9% / 62.6%), demonstrating that no amount of labeled data compensates for the lack of cross-modal text knowledge in existing single-cell PLMs. On the more challenging PBMC3&68K dataset, LangCell zero-shot (83.9% / 82.6%) similarly surpasses Geneformer 9-shot (83.3% / 74.1%) and scGPT 9-shot (85.7% / 76.9%) in F1. For cell-text retrieval on Tabula Sapiens (161 cell types), LangCell zero-shot outperforms BioTranslator trained on up to 30% of cell types (48 types), achieving 28.65% Recall@1 versus BioTranslator's 13.71%. In fine-tuning cell type annotation, LangCell-CE achieves SOTA across PBMC10K (98.3% Acc), LiverCross (50.4% Acc), and Zheng68K (85.4% Acc), beating Geneformer by 0.5–3.7% accuracy. For batch integration, LangCell leads all baselines on S_final (87.6 on PBMC10K vs. 84.7 for Geneformer; 95.4 on Perirhinal Cortex vs. 88.0 for Geneformer), and in pathway identification, LangCell-CE improves AUROC from 82.8 to 89.3 and AUPRC from 23.9 to 31.4 over Geneformer.

## Relation to Prior Work

LangCell extends the single-cell pre-trained language model lineage (scBERT, scGPT, Geneformer, xTrimoGene) by introducing a genuine cross-modal pre-training framework that aligns scRNA-seq data with natural language descriptions. It builds directly on Geneformer for the cell encoder and PubMedBERT for the text encoder, while adapting the BLIP-style cross-attention architecture and InfoNCE contrastive objectives from the vision-language literature. The key departure from BioTranslator, the closest prior work, is that LangCell performs full transformer-based pre-training on 27.5M scRNA-seq entries rather than relying on a naive MLP for cell encoding, and it jointly optimizes four complementary loss functions (masked gene modeling, cell-cell contrastive, cell-text contrastive, and cell-text matching) rather than treating the modalities separately.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LangCell addresses a critical practical bottleneck in single-cell genomics: the need for labeled data to annotate every new cell type or disease subtype, which is prohibitively expensive for rare conditions or novel subtypes. The demonstrated zero-shot capability (surpassing few-shot baselines by 20-40% in some settings) suggests a paradigm shift toward annotation-free cell identity understanding. However, limitations remain: pre-training texts are drawn exclusively from OBO Foundry (limiting semantic diversity), the model cannot yet handle other omics modalities like scATAC-seq, and the zero-shot evaluation partially overlaps with pre-training descriptions. Open questions include whether richer, free-form textual sources (e.g., literature abstracts) would further improve generalization, and whether the approach transfers to spatial transcriptomics or multi-omics settings.
