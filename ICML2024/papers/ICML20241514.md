## Problem

Existing time series generation methods ignore the rich paired heterogeneous metadata (categorical, continuous, and time-varying) that accompanies real-world datasets, making them unable to generate time series for specific conditions. Additionally, standard evaluation metrics like FID-based scores only measure marginal distributional similarity and fail to penalize conditional generators for poor specificity in reproducing metadata-specific features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241514/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes heterogeneous metadata conditions as input: categorical features (e.g., 71 heart disease statements, 11 weather descriptions, 12 monitoring stations) and continuous features (e.g., temperature, rain levels, wind speed), each potentially time-varying across the horizon L. It produces a multivariate time series sample of shape L×F (e.g., 96×6 for air quality, 1000×8 for ECG) that is conditioned on the specific metadata. The metadata domain is N^{L×K_cat} × R^{L×K_cont}, allowing arbitrary combinations of static and per-timestep conditions.

## Method

TIME WEAVER is a diffusion model that conditions the denoising process on heterogeneous metadata via a preprocessing module: categorical features are one-hot encoded and projected through FC layers, continuous features are projected through separate FC layers, and the resulting embeddings are fused by a self-attention layer to produce a metadata embedding z fed into the denoiser at every diffusion step. Two denoiser backbones are used—CSDI (feature and temporal self-attention transformers) and SSSD (structured state-space layers)—both modified from imputation architectures to accept the metadata embedding as an additional input. The paper also introduces J-FTSD, a new evaluation metric that computes the Frechet Distance over joint embeddings of time series and metadata, where the two feature extractors (Informer encoders) are jointly trained via CLIP-style contrastive learning to capture the correlation between time series and their paired metadata, enabling the metric to penalize generators that fail to reproduce metadata-specific features.

## Results

TIME WEAVER-CSDI achieves the best J-FTSD on Air Quality (2.2), Traffic (0.53), and Electricity (0.6), while TIME WEAVER-SSSD achieves the best J-FTSD on ECG (5.43) and Traffic (0.36). On the TSTR metric (downstream classification AUC), TIME WEAVER-SSSD reaches 0.85 on ECG and TIME WEAVER-CSDI reaches 0.77 on Air Quality, 0.83 on ECG, and 0.78 on Electricity. The best TIME WEAVER variant outperforms the best GAN baseline by roughly 6× on Air Quality, 1.75× on ECG, 4× on Electricity, and over 40× on Traffic in J-FTSD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIME WEAVER consistently outperforms both GAN baselines (WaveGAN and Pulse2Pulse) on all four datasets for both J-FTSD and TSTR. On Air Quality, TIME WEAVER-CSDI achieves J-FTSD of 2.2 versus 14.25 (WaveGAN) and 22.07 (Pulse2Pulse), a roughly 6× improvement over the best GAN. On Traffic, TIME WEAVER-SSSD achieves J-FTSD of 0.36 versus 17.70 (Pulse2Pulse) and 25.69 (WaveGAN), a >40× improvement. On ECG, TIME WEAVER-SSSD achieves J-FTSD of 5.43 and TSTR of 0.85, beating WaveGAN (9.55 / 0.65) and Pulse2Pulse (13.49 / 0.63). On Electricity, TIME WEAVER-CSDI achieves J-FTSD of 0.6 and TSTR of 0.78, outperforming Pulse2Pulse (2.8 / 0.71) and WaveGAN (7.82 / 0.57). Against the U-Net 1D diffusion baseline (Table 9), the best TIME WEAVER variant improves TSTR by ~9% and J-FTSD by ~17% on average, confirming that time-series-specific denoiser architectures (CSDI, SSSD) are necessary over generic image-domain backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIME WEAVER extends the diffusion-based time series generation line initiated by CSDI (Tashiro et al., 2021) and SSSD (Alcaraz & Strodthoff, 2022), repurposing their denoiser backbones—originally designed for imputation and forecasting—into metadata-conditioned generators by adding a heterogeneous metadata preprocessing module (categorical tokenizers, continuous tokenizers, and a self-attention conditioning layer). The closest prior work, Alcaraz & Strodthoff (2023), incorporated only categorical ECG statements as conditions; this paper generalizes to arbitrary combinations of categorical, continuous, and time-varying metadata. On the evaluation side, the paper departs from FID/Context-FID-style metrics that marginalize over conditions, introducing J-FTSD which jointly embeds time series and metadata via contrastively trained feature extractors (Informer-based), drawing an analogy to CLIP-style training but adapted to time series patches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world time series datasets (energy, traffic, air quality, medical) ship with rich paired metadata, yet prior generation methods ignored it, limiting applications like stress-testing, data augmentation, and what-if analysis. The J-FTSD metric is a useful contribution because it exposes a blind spot in existing evaluation—models can score well on distributional similarity while failing to reproduce metadata-specific features—and the contrastive joint training of feature extractors is a clean solution. Limitations include slower inference than GANs (200 diffusion steps), and the causal-relationship analysis is acknowledged as qualitative rather than rigorous. Open directions include progressive distillation for faster sampling and extending heterogeneous conditioning to forecasting and anomaly detection.
