## Problem

While convergence rates of MLE in Gaussian MoE models (regression setting) have been studied, the analogous analysis for classification—specifically the softmax gating multinomial logistic MoE—had remained missing from the literature. Moreover, the paper reveals that when expert parameters vanish, an intrinsic PDE interaction between the softmax gating numerator and the expert function causes parameter estimation rates to degrade below any polynomial order, a phenomenon not previously identified.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a covariate vector X ∈ R^d (from a compact subset) and produces conditional class probabilities g_G(Y=s|X) for s ∈ {1,…,K} via a mixture of k* multinomial logistic experts weighted by softmax gates. The MLE operates in an over-specified regime (fitting k > k* experts) and outputs a mixing measure Ĝ_n; a Voronoi cell decomposition then partitions true parameters into exact-specified (one fitted component) and over-specified (multiple fitted components) groups, which receive different estimation rates.

## Method

The paper constructs a generic Voronoi-based metric D_r that captures the distinct convergence behaviors of exact-specified versus over-specified parameters, and proves Hellinger lower bounds E_X[h(g_G, g_G*)] ≳ D_2(G, G_*) under Regime 1 (no vanishing expert parameters), yielding parametric rates. Under Regime 2 (some b*_iℓ = 0), a PDE links ∂u/∂β₁ to ∂u/∂b, creating linearly dependent Taylor terms that break the standard decomposition; the authors then establish a minimax lower bound showing rates slower than any polynomial. To resolve this, they propose a modified softmax gating function that applies a bounded transformation M(X) (e.g., tanh, normalization, sigmoid) to the input before the softmax, ensuring linear independence between X and M(X) up to second order and thereby eliminating the PDE interaction for all parameter settings, restoring stable polynomial rates.

## Results

The paper establishes that under the standard softmax gating multinomial logistic MoE, the density estimation rate is parametric at Õ(n^{-1/2}) in all regimes. For parameter estimation, Regime 1 (no expert parameter collapse) yields Õ(n^{-1/2}) for exact-specified parameters and Õ(n^{-1/4}) for over-specified parameters, while Regime 2 (expert parameter collapse) produces rates slower than any polynomial Õ(n^{-1/2r}) for over-specified parameters. The proposed modified softmax gating function restores the favorable Õ(n^{-1/2}) / Õ(n^{-1/4}) rates for exact-specified / over-specified parameters regardless of the expert parameter configuration.

| Gating | Parameter Setting | Density | Exact-specified Parameters | Over-specified Parameters |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Standard Softmax | Regime 1 | Õ(n^{-1/2}) | Õ(n^{-1/2}) | Õ(n^{-1/4}) |

| Modified Softmax | Any regime | Õ(n^{-1/2}) | Õ(n^{-1/2}) | Õ(n^{-1/4}) |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between the standard softmax gating function and the proposed modified softmax gating function (which applies a bounded transformation M(X) before the softmax). Under Regime 1 (no expert parameter collapse), both gating functions achieve identical rates: Õ(n^{-1/2}) for exact-specified parameters and Õ(n^{-1/4}) for over-specified parameters. Under Regime 2 (expert parameter collapse, where b_{iℓ}*=0 for all ℓ), the standard softmax gating suffers a PDE-induced interaction that degrades over-specified parameter rates to slower than any polynomial (potentially O(1/log n)), while the modified softmax gating maintains the Õ(n^{-1/4}) rate. The modified gating thus strictly dominates the standard gating in the worst case without sacrificing performance in the favorable case. The paper also contrasts its results with prior Gaussian MoE works (Ho et al. 2022; Nguyen et al. 2023, 2024b), noting that the over-specified rates here (Õ(n^{-1/4})) are substantially faster than the monotonically decreasing rates O(n^{-1/2r̄}) with r̄≥4 observed in those settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis framework for MLE in mixture-of-experts models from the regression setting (Gaussian MoE, studied in Ho et al. 2022, Nguyen et al. 2023/2024b/2024c) to the classification setting (multinomial logistic MoE), which the authors explicitly note has remained unaddressed in the literature. It adapts the Voronoi-based loss function from Manole & Ho (2022) to capture distinct estimation rates for exact-specified versus over-specified parameters, and carries forward the PDE-based interaction analysis between gating and expert parameters that was identified in prior Gaussian MoE work. The key departure is the proposal of a modified softmax gating function that transforms the input before routing, which eliminates the parameter interaction and was not present in any prior MoE convergence analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical characterization of MLE convergence in softmax gating multinomial logistic MoE, providing practitioners with a concrete diagnostic: if expert parameters collapse (some b_iℓ* = 0), the standard softmax gate can cause convergence rates slower than any polynomial, potentially O(1/log(n)). The proposed modified softmax gating function (e.g., M(X) = X/||X|| or M(X) = tanh(X)) is a simple, drop-in fix that restores polynomial rates regardless of parameter settings, with direct implications for training stability in large-scale MoE systems. Open questions include the misspecified setting (where the true data-generating process is not a multinomial logistic MoE), the effect of temperature parameters on convergence, and whether these theoretical findings translate to the deep, over-parameterized MoE architectures used in practice.
