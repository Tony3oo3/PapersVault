## Problem

Existing methods for incorporating high-level verbal feedback into LLMs (e.g., supervised context distillation, RLHF, in-context prompting) suffer from severe overgeneralization: the model applies the feedback to prompts where it is irrelevant, degrading behavior in unrelated contexts. The paper asks whether a model can be adapted to selectively follow a single sentence of context-dependent feedback while preserving its original behavior everywhere else, without requiring extensive human annotation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241898/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single natural-language feedback sentence (e.g., 'Use London gangster vernacular when texting my friend Peter') and a base instruction-tuned LLM (Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2) as input. GPT-4 is used as a data-generation oracle to produce 960 in-scope prompts, 960 near-scope prompts, and 960 out-of-scope prompts (drawn from the OIG dataset) per feedback, along with baseline and revised model completions. The output is a set of LoRA adapter weights (rank 64, alpha 128) that, when applied to the base model, yield context-aware responses that adhere to the feedback only for in-scope inputs while remaining unchanged for out-of-scope and near-scope inputs.

## Method

C3PO (Contextualized Critiques with Constrained Preference Optimization) operates in two stages. First, GPT-4 translates the verbal feedback into a synthetic preference dataset: it generates K topical categories, then in-scope prompts (where the feedback applies), near-scope prompts (superficially related but out of scope), and out-of-scope prompts (completely unrelated); the base model then produces baseline responses y⁻ and feedback-revised responses y⁺ for in-scope prompts, forming preference pairs where y⁺ is always preferred. Second, the model is fine-tuned with a combined objective L_C3PO = L_DPO(D_in-scope) + λ₁·L_SFT(D_out-of-scope) + λ₂·L_SFT(D_near-scope), where DPO applies the feedback on in-scope prompts while the SFT regularization terms anchor the model's behavior toward the original policy on irrelevant prompts. A key theoretical contribution shows that this synthetic two-policy preference data satisfies the Bradley-Terry model with a closed-form reward r*(x,y) = log(π⁺(y|x)/π⁻(y|x)), yielding an optimal policy that interpolates between the baseline and feedback-revised policies depending on the KL-regularization coefficient β.

## Results

On 100 pieces of verbal feedback (50 human-written, 50 GPT-4-generated), C3PO achieves the best overall score S_overall = 0.7065 ± 0.024, combining in-scope feedback adherence (S_in = 0.563 ± 0.031) with out-of-scope behavior preservation (S_out = 0.150 ± 0.014). C3PO reduces overgeneralization by 30% compared to the best baseline while maintaining in-scope adherence comparable to in-context prompting. General task performance is preserved, with GPT-4-rated quality dropping only from 4.497 to 4.470 (out of 5) on 500 out-of-scope prompts after C3PO fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C3PO outperforms SCD (Askell et al., 2021) on all three metrics: S_overall 0.7065 vs. 0.6005 (+10.6%), S_in 0.563 vs. 0.470 (+9.3%), and S_out 0.150 vs. 0.269 (44% less out-of-scope behavior change). Against DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) applied without regularization, C3PO achieves a dramatically better S_overall of 0.7065 vs. 0.4045, with S_out reduced from 0.517 to 0.150 (a 71% reduction in overgeneralization) while also improving in-scope adherence from 0.326 to 0.563. The SCD + Negatives variant (which adds the same regularization C3PO uses) achieves the lowest S_out (0.133) but at a severe cost to in-scope adherence (S_in = 0.367), yielding a lower S_overall (0.617) than C3PO. In-context prompting achieves the highest raw in-scope adherence (S_in = 0.677) but suffers the worst overgeneralization (S_out = 0.503), confirming that simply providing feedback in the prompt does not solve the contextualization problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

C3PO synthesizes two established alignment tools—Direct Preference Optimization (Rafailov et al., 2023) for in-scope behavior change and Supervised Context Distillation (Askell et al., 2021) for out-of-scope regularization—into a single joint objective, with the key departure being the synthetic data generation scheme that explicitly constructs near-scope and out-of-scope prompts to encode where feedback should and should not apply. This contrasts with Constitutional AI (Bai et al., 2022) and context distillation, which target universal behavioral rules, and with model editing (Mitchell et al., 2021; Meng et al., 2022), which studies localized edits for specific error types rather than general behavioral preferences. The theoretical contribution showing that two-policy preference data satisfies the Bradley-Terry model with a closed-form optimal policy extends the DPO analysis to a setting where preference labels are generated by two distinct policies rather than a black-box annotator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30% reduction in overgeneralization while maintaining in-scope adherence addresses a practical bottleneck for deploying personalized LLMs (personal assistants, industry-specific models) where accumulating verbal feedback in prompts becomes unwieldy. The LoRA-mixing result—showing that separately learned feedback adapters can be simply summed with virtually no degradation—opens a path toward incremental, mix-and-match personalization without retraining. Open questions include whether this composable approach scales beyond two feedbacks, the proper 'strictness' of the out-of-scope constraint (the finding that full KL distillation is worse than simple SFT is unresolved), and how feedback complexity interacts with base model capability.
