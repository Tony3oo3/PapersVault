## Problem

Existing fast approximation algorithms for k-means with outliers have running time that depends on the aspect ratio Δ (the ratio between maximum and minimum pairwise distances), which can be arbitrarily large in practice and limits scalability. The paper addresses this by designing sampling-based algorithms with near-linear running time in the data size that are independent of Δ, while still discarding only (1+ε)z outliers rather than a larger fraction.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a dataset X ⊂ R^d of size n, a target number of clusters k, a number of outliers z, and dimension d. It outputs a set of clustering centers and a partition of X into inliers and outliers. A key design choice is that the Fast-Sampling stage opens O(k/ε) centers (more than k) to achieve a 4-approximation, and a subsequent Center-Reduction step recovers exactly k centers at the cost of an O(1/ε) approximation. The number of outliers discarded is (1+ε)z, where ε is a tunable parameter controlling the trade-off between approximation quality and the number of extra points removed.

## Method

The core contribution is the Fast-Sampling algorithm, which uses a two-stage sampling strategy to pick inliers close to optimal centers without guessing the optimal clustering cost (the source of Δ-dependency in prior work). In the probability-boosting stage, an oversampling factor is estimated via a fast OSE subroutine (Algorithm 2) that uses linear selection to find the furthest εz points iteratively, boosting distance-based sampling probabilities so that Θ((1+ε)z) points are sampled independently in expectation, guaranteeing inliers constitute at least ε/(1+ε) of the sample. In the probability-normalization stage, the boosted probabilities are normalized so that exactly one inlier close to some optimal center is picked with probability Ω(ε) per iteration. Repeating for O(k/ε) rounds yields a 4-approximation with O(k/ε) centers. The Center-Reduction algorithm then recalls mistakenly discarded inliers by guessing subsets of the outlier set and calling a weighted (O(1/ε), 1+ε)-approximation subroutine, reducing the solution to exactly k centers with O(1/ε) approximation in time O(ndk log log n / ε² + d·poly(k, 1/ε)·log(nΔ)).

## Results

On 8 real-world datasets (Skin, SUSY, SIFT, Shuttle, KDDFULL with varying outlier fractions), the proposed TIKmeans achieves the best clustering cost on most datasets, reducing cost by at least 4.43% over RobustKmeans++ and 21.16% over NKmeans on Skin-5 and Shuttle respectively. The faster IKmeans variant is at least 20% faster than state-of-the-art on average across all 8 datasets, and reduces running time by over 50% on the 100M-point SIFT dataset compared to RobustKmeans++ (9,635s vs. 92,840s). LS++ (Grunau & Rozhoň, 2022) times out (>24h) on SUSY and SIFT, while NKmeans (Im et al., 2020) times out (>72h) on SIFT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RobustKmeans++ (Deshpande et al., 2020), TIKmeans achieves lower clustering cost on 6 of 8 datasets (e.g., 68,049 vs. 71,210 on Skin-5, a 4.4% reduction), while IKmeans is consistently faster (0.59s vs. 0.66s on Skin-5; 9,635s vs. 92,840s on SIFT-5, a ~90% speedup). Against NKmeans (Im et al., 2020), TIKmeans reduces cost by 21.16% on Shuttle and 8.4% on Skin-5, and both methods are far faster than NKmeans (which requires >72h on SIFT). Against LS++ (Grunau & Rozhoň, 2022), the proposed methods are dramatically faster (1.4s vs. 2,374s on Skin-5) and LS++ simply times out on SUSY and SIFT; however, LS++ achieves slightly lower cost on Skin-5 (70,614 vs. 68,049 for TIKmeans is actually TIKmeans winning, but on Skin-10 LS++ gets 75,520 vs. TIKmeans 72,448). Overall, TIKmeans wins on clustering quality for most datasets while IKmeans wins on speed, and both avoid the aspect-ratio-dependent running time that limits LS++ and NKmeans on large-scale data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sampling-based approach to k-means with outliers initiated by Bhaskara et al. (2019) and Grunau & Rozhoň (2022), both of which require a guess for the optimal clustering cost that introduces dependence on the aspect ratio Δ. The key departure is the Fast-Sampling algorithm's two-stage strategy (probability boosting followed by normalization), which eliminates the need for this guess and thus removes Δ-dependency from the running time entirely. The Center-Reduction algorithm then leverages the weighted approximation subroutine from Grunau & Rozhoň (2022) to reduce the number of opened centers from O(k/ε) to exactly k, achieving the same O(1/ε)-approximation guarantee as LS++ but with a running time that is independent of Δ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first near-linear time approximation algorithm for k-means with outliers whose running time is independent of the aspect ratio, which is significant because Δ can be arbitrarily large in practice and was a scalability bottleneck for all prior sampling-based and local-search methods. The (1+ε)z outliers discarded is provably the best possible given known lower bounds, making the result tight in that dimension. Open questions include whether the 4-approximation (with relaxed centers) can be tightened, whether a lower bound exists for the (1+ε)z setting, and whether the approach extends to other clustering objectives (k-median, k-center) with outliers. The practical impact is demonstrated on datasets up to 100M points, where the method is 50%+ faster than the next-best baseline.
