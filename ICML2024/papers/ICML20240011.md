## Problem

Despite over 20 years of coreset research, the optimal coreset size for the geometric median (1-median) problem in low-dimensional Euclidean space remained unresolved. Prior upper bounds of Õ(ε^{-2d/(d+1)}) and Õ(√d·ε^{-1}) lacked matching lower bounds, leaving open whether the sublinear-in-ε^{-1} regime was truly achievable and what the tight dependence on dimension and precision should be.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a point set P ⊆ R^d of n points and a precision parameter ε > 0, and produces a weighted subset S ⊆ P of size Õ(2^{ηd}·ε^{-d/(d+1)}) with nonnegative weights. The guarantee is that for every query point c ∈ R^d, the weighted sum of distances from c to S approximates the unweighted sum from c to P within a (1±ε) multiplicative factor. The coreset is a strict subset of the input (no synthetic points), and the construction is deterministic.

## Method

The upper bound combines a polynomial technique with a modified quad-tree. The polynomial technique (Lemma 2.1) uses Taylor expansion of Euclidean distances to show that for a point set in a unit cube, one can assign weights in [-1,1] (with at least 1/3 equal to +1) so that the weighted distance sum from any far query point decays as O(|P|·||q||^{-c}) for arbitrary constant c; this is achieved by zeroing out a polynomial whose coefficients are linear in the weights via a subspace-face intersection argument. The quad-tree organizes points into dense, sparse, and ordinary leaves; for ordinary leaves the polynomial technique reduces the coreset fraction, while the low-dimensional packing property (Lemma 3.3) ensures that far cells contribute negligible additive error. A bootstrapping procedure iteratively compresses the base coreset to reach the final size. The matching lower bound adapts Alexander's (1990) analytic discrepancy theory for halfspaces, connecting coreset additive error to discrepancy via a signed mass distribution and the integral identity linking pairwise distances to squared halfspace discrepancies.

## Results

The paper establishes matching upper and lower bounds on the size of ε-median coresets in low-dimensional Euclidean space. The upper bound (Theorem 1.1) gives a deterministic construction of size $\tilde{O}(2^{\eta d}\varepsilon^{-d/(d+1)})$, while the lower bound (Theorem 1.2) shows that any coreset using input points must have size at least $\Omega(\varepsilon^{-d/(d+1)})$. For the line metric (d=1), this recovers the optimal $\tilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-1/2})$ bound, and for the plane (d=2) yields $\tilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-2/3})$, improving over the prior $\tilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-1})$ of Huang et al. (2023).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's $\tilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-d/(d+1)})$ bound strictly improves over all prior low-dimensional constructions. Compared to Braverman et al. (2022), which gave $\tilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-2d/(d+1)})$, the new result halves the exponent on $\varepsilon^{-1}$ (e.g., from $\varepsilon^{-4/3}$ to $\varepsilon^{-2/3}$ in the plane). Compared to Huang et al. (2023), which gave $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{d}\cdot\varepsilon^{-1})$, the new bound is sublinear in $\varepsilon^{-1}$ for every fixed d, whereas Huang et al.'s bound is linear. The lower bound of $\Omega(\varepsilon^{-d/(d+1)})$ (Theorem 1.2), proven via Alexander's discrepancy techniques, confirms the upper bound is tight up to polylogarithmic factors. For the line metric (d=1), the paper recovers the $\tilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-1/2})$ bound that was previously only known as a lower bound (Baker et al., 2020) and an upper bound (Huang et al., 2023), thereby settling that case completely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper settles the coreset size for the geometric median (1-median / Fermat-Weber) problem in low-dimensional Euclidean space, building directly on the progression from Cohen-Addad et al.'s high-dimensional O~(ε^{-2}) bound, through Braverman et al.'s low-dimensional O~(ε^{-2d/(d+1)}) construction, to Huang et al.'s O~(√d·ε^{-1}) result. The upper bound combines a quad-tree spatial partitioning with a novel polynomial/Taylor-expansion argument (Lemma 2.1) that assigns signed weights to cancel low-order distance terms, while the matching lower bound repurposes Alexander's (1990) analytical discrepancy theory for halfspaces — a connection between coreset lower bounds and discrepancy that had not been previously exploited in this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the first tight characterization (up to polylog factors) of the geometric median coreset size in low dimensions, showing the optimal rate is ε^{-d/(d+1)} — strictly sublinear in ε^{-1} for all d ≥ 1, a property previously known only for the line metric. Two open questions remain: whether the lower bound extends to coresets not restricted to input points (all known constructions use input points, yet squared-distance 0-coresets of O(1) size exist without this restriction), and whether the exponential-in-d dependency can be reconciled with the high-dimensional O~(ε^{-2}) bound to yield a 'best of both worlds' guarantee. For practitioners, the sublinear-in-ε^{-1} rate means that in low dimensions, coreset-based streaming and distributed algorithms for geometric median can be significantly more space-efficient than the high-dimensional worst case suggests.
