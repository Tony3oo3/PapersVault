## Problem

Detecting periodic patterns in noisy time series without labels is challenging because self-supervised methods require task-specific augmentations that are difficult to design for quasi-periodic signals and can cause model collapse under strong augmentations. Existing unsupervised approaches either impose random variance constraints on batches (an overly optimistic assumption without labels) or rely on contrastive pairs that may decorrelate representations of the same underlying periodicity. This paper asks whether periodicity can be detected purely through spectral regularizers that avoid both augmentation design and batch-statistics assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes fixed-length uniformly sampled time series segments (8-second windows with 2-second shifts, resampled to 25 Hz for PPG; 10-second segments for ECG; 15 Hz accelerometer magnitude for step counting) and produces a same-length learned representation from which the dominant periodic frequency is extracted via argmax of the Fourier transform magnitude. No labels, augmentations, or positive/negative pair construction are required during training; the only task-specific hyperparameter is the bandlimited frequency range of interest (e.g., 30–210 bpm for heart rate, 5–40 rpm for respiration, 40–140 spm for steps).

## Method

The core contribution is a two-term spectral loss: (1) a spectral-entropy minimization term that provably maximizes periodicity in the time domain (Proposition 2.1), and (2) a KL-divergence term between the normalized spectra of the original input and the learned representation that prevents the degenerate constant-output collapse (Proposition 2.2). A third bandpass term suppresses power outside the frequency range of interest. The encoder is a 1D U-Net (no LSTM or dilated convolutions, which the authors found to destroy frequency information), trained with Adam at lr=0.001 and batch size 512 using the combined loss without any projection head. The key insight is that anchoring each representation to its source sample's spectrum—rather than enforcing batch-level diversity or contrastive pair structure—guarantees non-collapse while requiring no augmentation pipeline, making the method directly applicable to noisy, multi-periodic, non-stationary time series.

## Results

Across 8 datasets spanning three tasks (HR from ECG/PPG, respiratory rate, step counting), the proposed unsupervised method achieves up to 45–50% error reduction over prior state-of-the-art. On ECG-based HR estimation (Table 1), it reaches MAE of 3.75 (PTB-XL), 1.29 (DaLiA_ECG), and 2.13 (WESAD), outperforming the closest unsupervised baseline SiNC and all self-supervised methods. On PPG-based HR (Table 4), it achieves MAE of 9.30 on IEEE SPC12 and 10.27 on IEEE SPC22, roughly halving the error of SiNC (19.34 and 21.93). The method also leads on respiratory rate (MAE 3.40 on CapnoBase-64s) and regular step counting (MAPE 5.95 on Clemson), though it underperforms supervised methods on the noisiest settings (DaLiA_PPG, Clemson semi-regular).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms the closest unsupervised baseline SiNC across nearly all datasets: MAE 3.75 vs 3.97 (PTB-XL), 1.29 vs 1.72 (DaLiA_ECG), 9.30 vs 19.34 (IEEE SPC12), and 5.95 vs 22.92 MAPE (Clemson Regular), with the largest gap (~52% reduction) on PPG-based HR. Against self-supervised methods (SimCLR, BYOL, TS-TCC, TS2Vec, VICReg, Barlow Twins, SimPer), the proposed approach wins on all ECG and PPG HR datasets and on respiratory rate, while SimPer—despite being designed for periodicity—underperforms other SSL methods on noisy signals due to spectral overlap from its augmentations. The method also surpasses supervised baselines (DCL, CNN) on most datasets, e.g., MAE 3.75 vs 6.08 on PTB-XL and 9.30 vs 10.05 on IEEE SPC12. The two notable exceptions are DaLiA_PPG (MAE 27.41 vs CNN 7.35) and Clemson semi-regular (MAPE 35.18 vs FCN 17.22), where the authors attribute the gap to extremely low SNR that hides the target periodicity, making unsupervised learning insufficient without label guidance. Traditional heuristic methods (Fourier, Autocorrelation, RobustPeriod) are substantially outperformed, with RobustPeriod failing catastrophically on PTB-XL (MAE 72.79) and IEEE SPC12 (MAE 19.56).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant self-supervised learning paradigm for time series (SimCLR, BYOL, TS-TCC, TS2Vec, SimPer) that relies on task-specific data augmentations to generate positive/negative pairs, arguing that such augmentations are particularly ill-suited for noisy quasi-periodic signals where strong transforms cause collapse and weak ones hinder convergence. Conceptually, it is closest to SiNC (Speth et al., 2023), which also operates without labels for periodicity detection, but replaces SiNC's batch variance constraint with a KL-divergence regularizer between input and output spectra. The theoretical framing draws on the Wiener-Khintchine theorem and information-theoretic properties of spectral entropy for Gaussian versus periodic processes, providing a principled loss design rather than an ad-hoc pretext task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method eliminates the need for hand-crafted augmentations and batch-level diversity constraints, offering a single fixed loss (spectral entropy + KL divergence + bandpass penalty) that transfers across tasks and datasets without re-tuning. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that when training data has very low SNR, the unsupervised learner can converge to noise patterns rather than the desired periodicity, causing it to underperform supervised and self-supervised baselines on DaLiA_PPG and Clemson semi-regular. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-periodic or non-stationary signals where the frequency of interest shifts over time, and whether the U-Net architecture is truly necessary or whether lighter backbones suffice.
