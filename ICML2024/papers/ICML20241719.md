## Problem

DPO and related preference optimization methods assume that observed preference pairs follow the Bradley-Terry-Luce model without error, yet real-world human feedback is inherently noisy with flipped or ambiguous labels. While practitioners have proposed heuristics such as label smoothing (cDPO) to mitigate noise, no theoretical guarantees exist for these methods or for DPO itself, leaving a gap between empirical practice and provable performance bounds.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dataset of noisy preference triplets (prompt s, observed-chosen a_w, observed-rejected a_l) sampled from an SFT policy, along with a known flip rate ε ∈ (0, 1/2) that is tuned via cross-validation in practice. It outputs a parameterized policy π_θ (log-linear or neural) that generates text responses, with the guarantee that the sub-optimality gap relative to the optimal policy scales as O(1/(1−2ε) · √(d/n)) under good feature coverage of the SFT policy.

## Method

The core contribution is the rDPO loss, which de-biases the standard DPO binary cross-entropy loss under random preference flips. The key insight is that the log-odds of the noisy preference probability differ from the clean one, introducing bias; the paper instead defines un-normalized preference probabilities σ(βh)^(1−ε) / σ(−βh)^ε that preserve the original logits, yielding the loss [(1−ε)L(θ; a_w, a_l) − εL(θ; a_l, a_w)] / (1−2ε), which is an unbiased estimator of the clean DPO loss (Lemma 3.1). In gradient form, rDPO upweights updates when the implicit reward model misorders the observed pair (scaled by the no-flip probability) and downweights them when it orders correctly (scaled by the flip probability), making parameter updates more aggressive in the correct direction on average than DPO or cDPO. The paper proves the first estimation-error and sub-optimality-gap bounds for DPO-family policies, showing the cost of noise is a multiplicative factor of 1/(1−2ε), and extends the framework to PPO-based reward training, SLiC, IPO, and Plackett-Luce preference models.

## Results

On the IMDb sentiment generation task with 40% preference noise (ε=0.4), rDPO achieves a mean reward of 0.99 ± 0.00 at 1000 training steps, matching the clean-data DPO upper bound (0.99 ± 0.02) while all other methods degrade substantially. In the PPO family, rPPO reaches 0.94 ± 0.23 versus 0.78 ± 0.41 for vanilla PPO under the same noise level. On the Anthropic Helpful-Harmless dialogue task, rDPO (ε=0.1) improves win-rate over the SFT policy by 24.32% (gpt2-large) and 51.20% (Llama-2-7b), outperforming both DPO and cDPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

rDPO decisively outperforms vanilla DPO under 40% noise: at 1000 steps, rDPO achieves 0.99 ± 0.00 reward versus DPO's 0.88 ± 0.32, and DPO's reward actually drops to 0.72 ± 0.43 at 400 steps before partially recovering. Against cDPO (Mitchell's label-smoothing heuristic), rDPO wins by a wide margin (0.99 vs. 0.83 at 1000 steps), confirming the paper's theoretical argument that cDPO's gradient weights are systematically lower than rDPO's (Lemma 3.2). IPO (0.82) and SLiC (0.90) also degrade significantly under noise, with rDPO surpassing both by 0.17 and 0.09 reward respectively. In the PPO family, rPPO (0.94 ± 0.23) beats both vanilla PPO (0.78 ± 0.41) and cPPO (0.87 ± 0.33) on the same noisy IMDb data. On the Anthropic Helpful-Harmless task, rDPO (ε=0.1) improves win-rate over SFT by 24.32% (gpt2-large) and 51.20% (Llama-2-7b), exceeding DPO's 22.20%/45.78% and cDPO's 18.34%/39.16%, demonstrating that the advantage generalizes to larger models and real-world noisy preference data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2023) by adapting the binary cross-entropy loss to handle random preference flips, drawing directly on the unbiased estimation techniques from the noisy-label classification literature (Natarajan et al., 2013). It departs from the conservative DPO (cDPO) heuristic of Mitchell (2023) and the reward-modeling heuristics of Wang et al. (2024), which both introduce bias into the DPO loss, by constructing a loss that is provably an unbiased estimator of the clean-data DPO loss. The theoretical analysis also connects to the offline RL literature on policy estimation error (Agarwal et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2020) through the use of feature coverage assumptions and relative condition numbers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical performance guarantees for the widely-used DPO algorithm (as a byproduct of setting ε=0), resolving a notable gap in understanding practical preference optimization. The multiplicative 1/(1-2ε) factor cleanly quantifies the sample-efficiency cost of noisy preferences, giving practitioners a concrete tradeoff. Open questions include how rDPO compares to the more complex heuristics in Wang et al. (2024) (e.g., adaptive margins, label flipping), whether the known-ε assumption (practically handled via cross-validation) holds in real annotation pipelines, and whether the results extend to the much larger models and more structured noise patterns encountered in production RLHF.
