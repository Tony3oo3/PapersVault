## Problem

While sparse MoE and dense softmax gating have received theoretical analysis of their parameter estimation rates, the dense-to-sparse gate—which uses a softmax temperature to progressively control expert sparsity during training—lacks any comprehensive convergence analysis. The central question is whether the temperature parameter is sample-efficient: does it admit polynomial-rate estimation, or do its interactions with gating and expert parameters degrade the rates to sub-polynomial orders?

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. pairs (X_i, Y_i) ∈ R^d × R drawn from a Gaussian MoE with a temperature-scaled softmax gate, and produces the maximum likelihood estimate of the mixing measure G (i.e., all gating weights β_0, gating directions β_1, temperature τ, and expert parameters a, b, ν). The theoretical output is not a point estimate but a set of minimax lower and upper bounds on the convergence rate of each parameter group under both exact-specified (k = k*) and over-specified (k > k*) settings, expressed via Voronoi-cell-based loss functions.

## Method

The paper first proves that in the standard dense-to-sparse gate, the softmax temperature interacts with gating parameters β_1 and expert parameters (a, b) through two partial differential equations (∂F/∂τ = (1/τ)β_1^⊤ ∂F/∂β_1 and ∂²F/∂τ∂b = (1/τ²)β_1^⊤ ∂F/∂a), which induce linearly dependent Taylor terms and force estimation rates for β_1 and τ to be slower than any polynomial (as slow as 1/log n). To break these interactions, the authors propose an activation dense-to-sparse gate that routes the linear layer output through an activation function σ before the softmax, i.e., Softmax((σ(β_1^⊤X) + β_0)/τ). By imposing first-order (exact-specified) or second-order (over-specified) linear independence conditions on σ and its derivatives—satisfied by sigmoid and GELU—the PDE interactions vanish, and all parameter estimation rates improve to parametric O~(n^{-1/2}) under exact specification and O~(n^{-1/4}) for β_1, τ, a under over-specification. The analysis is validated via EM-based simulations on synthetic data across 20 sample sizes from 10⁴ to 10⁵.

## Results

Under the standard dense-to-sparse gate, the density estimation rate is parametric at Õ(n^{-1/2}), but parameter estimation rates for β₁j* and τ* are slower than any polynomial rate Õ(n^{-1/(2r)}) for all r≥1, potentially as slow as O(1/log(n)), due to PDE-based interactions between temperature and other parameters. The proposed activation dense-to-sparse gate (using sigmoid or GELU) eliminates these interactions, improving all parameter estimation rates to Õ(n^{-1/2}) under exact-specified settings and Õ(n^{-1/4}) for β₁j*, τ*, a_j* under over-specified settings. Empirical experiments with k*=2 true experts and k=3 estimated experts (n ranging from 10⁴ to 10⁵) confirm these theoretical rates via log-log convergence plots.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between the standard dense-to-sparse gate (Nie et al., 2022) and the proposed activation dense-to-sparse gate. Under the standard gate, the temperature parameter τ* and gating weights β₁j* suffer from sub-polynomial convergence rates (potentially O(1/log(n))) due to two PDE-based interactions: ∂F/∂τ = (1/τ)·β₁ᵀ(∂F/∂β₁) and ∂²F/∂τ∂b = (1/τ²)·β₁ᵀ(∂F/∂a). The activation dense-to-sparse gate, which inserts a nonlinear activation σ(·) (e.g., sigmoid, GELU) between the linear layer and softmax, breaks these PDE interactions under first-order (exact-specified) or second-order (over-specified) independence conditions on σ. This yields a dramatic improvement: all parameter rates become Õ(n^{-1/2}) under exact-specified settings (vs. sub-polynomial), and Õ(n^{-1/4}) for β₁j*, τ*, a_j* under over-specified settings (vs. sub-polynomial). The practical implication is that selecting important experts requires only polynomial data (≈ε^{-η̄}) with the activation gate versus exponential data (≈exp(1/ε^η)) with the standard gate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence rate analysis of Gaussian MoE parameter estimation initiated by Ho et al. (2022) for input-independent gating, Nguyen et al. (2023) for dense softmax gating, and Nguyen et al. (2024b) for Top-K sparse gating, to the dense-to-sparse gating mechanism introduced by Nie et al. (2022). The key departure is identifying that the softmax temperature in dense-to-sparse gating creates partial differential equation (PDE) interactions between temperature and both gating and expert parameters that are substantially more severe than any previously characterized interaction. The proposed activation dense-to-sparse gate (inserting sigmoid/GELU before softmax) is a novel architectural modification that eliminates these PDE interactions, building on the weakly identifiable mixture framework of Ho & Nguyen (2016) for over-specified settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that temperature in dense-to-sparse gating induces sub-polynomial (potentially O(1/log(n))) parameter estimation rates is a significant negative result with direct practical implications: selecting important experts in MoE models using standard dense-to-sparse gates requires exponential data, which is infeasible in practice. The activation dense-to-sparse gate provides a simple, principled fix that restores parametric rates, offering concrete guidance for MoE architecture design. Open questions include extending the analysis to misspecified settings (where data don't come from the model), deterministic expert settings with least-squares loss, and the non-convex parameter space challenges that arise in those extensions.
