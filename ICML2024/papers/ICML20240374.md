## Problem

Despite Fourier analysis being a cornerstone of signal processing and time series analysis, its application to score-based diffusion models for time series generation remained largely unexplored. This paper asks whether representing time series in the frequency domain via the DFT constitutes a useful inductive bias for diffusion models, and if so, how to formally translate the SDE-based diffusion framework to the complex-valued frequency domain.

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series of shape N×M (N time steps, M features) and, for the frequency variant, first applies a DFT to obtain a complex-valued representation constrained by mirror symmetry (x̃_κ = x̃*_{N−κ}), reducing it to N real independent components via a coordinate chart φ. The score network (a 3.2M-parameter transformer encoder) consumes the noisy truncated frequency-domain vector in R^{d_X} along with the diffusion time t, and outputs the score. Generated samples are produced by integrating the reverse SDE for T=1000 steps and applying the inverse DFT to recover real-valued time series.

## Method

The key theoretical contribution is showing that applying the DFT operator to the forward SDE transforms standard Brownian motions into 'mirrored Brownian motions'—stochastic processes whose components obey mirror symmetries and decompose into independent real and complex Brownian motions. The forward diffusion in the frequency domain becomes d x̃ = f̃(x̃,t)dt + g(t)dṽ, and the reverse diffusion introduces a diagonal scaling matrix Λ (with entries 1 for DC/Nyquist components and 1/√2 otherwise) that corrects for the differing variances of real versus complex frequency components. Denoising score matching is adapted by proving (Proposition 3.4) that minimizing the frequency-domain score matching loss is exactly equivalent to minimizing the loss for an auxiliary time-domain score, connecting the two formulations. Practically, the model operates entirely in R^{d_X} on the truncated non-redundant components, using a VP-SDE with linear noise scheduling (β_min=0.1, β_max=20), a transformer encoder backbone with learnable positional and diffusion-time encodings, and AdamW with cosine LR scheduling over 200 epochs.

## Results

Frequency diffusion models consistently achieve lower sliced Wasserstein distances (SW↓) than time diffusion models across all 6 datasets, in both the time and frequency evaluation domains. The most dramatic improvement is on US-Droughts, where frequency diffusion reduces the time-domain SW from 2.913 to 0.738 (a ~4x reduction) and the frequency-domain SW from 2.849 to 0.633. On MIMIC-III, the time-domain SW drops from 0.211 to 0.152, and on NASDAQ-2019 from 60.512 to 43.602. The paper further shows that artificially delocalizing the spectral representation (via Gaussian smoothing in frequency) closes the gap, with the time model eventually outperforming the frequency model at σ≈7, confirming that spectral localization drives the advantage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the canonical time-domain score-based diffusion model (VP-SDE with linear noise schedule, following Song et al. 2020), using an identical transformer encoder architecture (10 layers, 12 heads, d_model=72, 3.2M parameters) and training protocol. Frequency diffusion wins on every dataset in both evaluation domains: for example, on US-Droughts the time-domain SW drops from 2.913 (time diffusion) to 0.738 (frequency diffusion), a 4x improvement, and on NASDAQ-2019 from 60.512 to 43.602. The advantage is most pronounced on datasets with strong low-frequency concentration (US-Droughts, NASDAQ-2019) and smallest on ECG, which has the least spectral localization. The paper confirms the result is architecture-agnostic by repeating the experiment with LSTM backbones (Appendix D) and with marginal Wasserstein distances (Appendix B.4), both yielding the same conclusion. A key ablation shows that when spectral localization is artificially removed via Gaussian smoothing in the frequency domain (σ≈7), the time model overtakes the frequency model, validating the localization-based explanation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the score-based SDE diffusion framework of Song et al. (2020) from the time domain to the frequency domain, introducing the concept of mirrored Brownian motions to handle the complex-valued, mirror-symmetric structure of the DFT. It is conceptually adjacent to FourierFlow (Alaa et al., 2021), which applied normalizing flows to Fourier representations, and to Phillips et al. (2022), who studied diffusion on functional spaces including Fourier bases, but neither of those works provides the explicit SDE translation with the mirrored Brownian motion prescription. The denoising score matching objective is adapted from Hyvärinen & Dayan (2005) and Song & Ermon (2019), with the key modification being the Λ² weighting that accounts for the non-uniform variance structure of the frequency-domain noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded recipe for implementing diffusion in the frequency domain, which is practically important because it avoids the pitfall of generating complex-valued time series and shows no additional computational cost. The spectral localization explanation, while intuitive, is validated with a clean delocalization experiment that identifies when frequency diffusion should *not* be preferred, giving practitioners a concrete diagnostic. Open questions include whether the advantage persists for time-localized signals (e.g., sparse event data), whether multiresolution (wavelet) representations could further improve generation, and whether the findings generalize beyond the six relatively small datasets studied. The paper explicitly notes that localization is not the sole explanation, leaving room for further mechanistic understanding.
