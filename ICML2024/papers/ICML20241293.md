## Problem

Existing deep generative model families—VAEs, GANs, autoregressive models, and (latent) diffusion models—each excel in only a subset of the three core capabilities (generation, reconstruction, and compact representation) and struggle across different data types (discrete sequences vs. continuous images). No single framework unifies all three capabilities while remaining flexible enough to handle text, images, and protein sequences within one training objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241293/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts variable-length text sequences, fixed-resolution RGB images (128×128 for FFHQ, 64×64 for CelebA), or amino-acid protein sequences, and produces generated samples, high-fidelity reconstructions, 128-dimensional latent vectors, interpolated or edited samples, and optimized protein sequences. A key design choice is that the decoder can be a pretrained autoregressive language model (GPT2-xl) for text, a pixel-level diffusion model for images, or convolutional layers for proteins, making the reconstruction loss modality-specific (cross-entropy vs. MSE). The encoder is a BERT-small transformer for text, a UNet for images, or a simple transformer for proteins, all mapping to a shared low-dimensional latent space.

## Method

EDDPMs replace the first Gaussian noising step of standard DDPM with a parameterized neural encoder q_λ(x₁|x₀) and the corresponding denoising step with a parameterized decoder p_φ(x₀|x₁), while retaining standard Gaussian noising-denoising for all subsequent steps t=2,…,T. The resulting variational objective decomposes into four terms: the standard diffusion denoising loss L_{t-1}, a KL alignment loss L_align that matches the encoder's latent output to the diffusion model's denoised estimate at step 1, a reconstruction loss L_rec from the decoder, and a prior KL L_T. All parameters (encoder λ, decoder φ, diffusion θ) are trained jointly end-to-end using the well-established DDPM training recipe with a single hyperparameter w balancing reconstruction against diffusion losses. This unified training—unlike latent diffusion models that train a VAE separately—yields a semantically richer latent space that supports interpolation, latent arithmetic editing, and optimization without the generation-reconstruction trade-off seen in VAEs.

## Results

On the Yelp text domain, EDDPM achieves 92.1 BLEU for reconstruction content preservation (vs. 87.6 for LatentOps and 86.1 for Optimus-DAAE), 16.4 perplexity for generation fluency (vs. 68.1 LatentOps, 94.1 Optimus-DAAE, 337.6 GENIE), and 0.977 MAUVE for distribution match (vs. 0.240 LatentOps, 0.006 Optimus-DAAE). On images (FFHQ 128, T=50), EDDPM achieves 12.26 generation FID, 5.48 reconstruction rFID, and 16.98 interpolation FID (α=0.4), outperforming DiffAE (12.57/5.93/22.17) and LDM (12.90/4.87/75.13). For protein representation on Gifford, EDDPM's latent-space regressor achieves superior MSE, L1, Pearson, and Spearman correlations over ReLSO and vanilla VAEs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDDPM dominates all text diffusion baselines (GENIE, AR-Diffusion) by a wide margin on every metric: generation perplexity is 16.4 vs. 337.6 (GENIE) and 157.8 (AR-Diffusion), and MAUVE distribution match is 0.977 vs. 0.013 and 0.007 respectively. Against autoencoder-based baselines (LatentOps, Optimus-DAAE), EDDPM achieves substantially better generation quality (perplexity 16.4 vs. 68.1 and 94.1; MAUVE 0.977 vs. 0.240 and 0.006) while maintaining competitive reconstruction (92.1 vs. 87.6 and 86.1 BLEU) and interpolation performance. EDDPM is on par with LatentOps on latent arithmetic accuracy (57.1 vs. 57.3) and slightly behind on interpolation fluency (30.8 vs. 32.5), but far surpasses both on generation distribution match. Compared to GPT4 (20-shot), EDDPM achieves lower generation perplexity (16.4 vs. 25.7) and dramatically better distribution match (0.977 vs. 0.007), though GPT4 retains an edge on diversity (0.87 vs. 0.79). On images, EDDPM consistently outperforms DiffAE across all four datasets (e.g., FFHQ generation FID 12.26 vs. 12.57, reconstruction rFID 5.48 vs. 5.93) and substantially outperforms LDM on interpolation (16.98 vs. 75.13 FID at α=0.4), while matching or exceeding NVAE and StyleGAN-XL on aggregate performance. For protein sequences, EDDPM's latent representations yield better fitness prediction (lower MSE, higher Pearson/Spearman) than ReLSO and vanilla VAEs, and its optimization produces more diverse high-fitness proteins than ReLSO's pseudo-convex convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

EDDPM generalizes the DDPM variational inference formulation (Ho et al., 2020; Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015) by replacing the fixed Gaussian noising at the first diffusion step with a learnable encoder-decoder pair, effectively making the model a VAE (Kingma & Welling, 2014) whose prior is a learned diffusion process rather than a fixed Gaussian. This distinguishes it from latent diffusion models (Rombach et al., 2021) that train the VAE and diffusion separately, and from DiffAE (Preechakul et al., 2022) and LSGM (Vahdat et al., 2021) that use approximations for joint training; EDDPM derives an exact ELBO that allows stable end-to-end optimization. The framework also departs from prior text diffusion models (GENIE, AR-Diffusion, Diffusion-LM) by accommodating pretrained autoregressive LMs as decoders, which those approaches are architecturally incompatible with.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying generation, reconstruction, and representation in a single jointly-trained model that handles both discrete (text, protein) and continuous (image) data, EDDPM offers a practical recipe for building multi-capability generative models without the trade-offs inherent in VAEs or the architectural constraints of standard diffusion. The ability to plug in pretrained LMs as decoders is particularly impactful for text, where prior diffusion approaches lagged far behind autoregressive models. Key open questions include whether the single-step encoding generalizes to hierarchical latent representations, how the approach scales to video/audio/multimodal settings, and whether the unified framework can serve as a foundation for world models or reasoning systems as the authors speculate.
