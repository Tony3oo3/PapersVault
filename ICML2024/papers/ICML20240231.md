## Problem

In safe reinforcement learning, sparse binary cost signals make constraint estimation significantly harder than reward estimation, causing non-smooth value functions and large estimation noise that degrade policy performance. Existing representation learning objectives like value consistency fail to improve safety-aware embeddings because the cost value function is too rough to serve as effective supervision. The paper asks whether a smoother, more learnable safety metric can be used to guide representation learning for better constraint estimation.

## Input / Output

The method takes raw state vectors (low-dimensional continuous states) or RGB images (high-dimensional observations) from Safety-Gymnasium environments and produces a 64-dim (vector) or 128-dim (image) safety-aware embedding z. The embedding is concatenated with the raw state and fed to the RL policy and value functions, while a separate prediction head outputs a discrete distribution (63 bins) over the feasibility score. The feasibility score itself is defined as the expected maximum discounted cost along a trajectory, making it a trajectory-level safety indicator rather than a per-step value.

## Method

FCSRL introduces a two-component self-supervised representation learning objective: (1) a SimSiam-style dynamics consistency loss that trains an encoder g and transition model h to predict next-state embeddings from current embeddings and actions, using a momentum (EMA) target encoder to prevent collapse; and (2) a feasibility consistency loss that supervises a prediction head to match the bootstrap-estimated feasibility score F^π(s,a) = E[max_t γ^t c(s_t,a_t)], computed via a Bellman operator with max over cost and expectation over next action. The key theoretical contribution is proving that the feasibility score is temporally smoother than the cost value function (Proposition 4.3) and that 1−F^π(s,a) equals the probability of all future state-action pairs being safe (Proposition 4.1), making it a more learnable supervision signal. The feasibility prediction uses discrete regression (distributional RL) to handle the action-marginalized distribution of feasibility values. The learned target embedding z^(m) is then used as input to any standard safe RL algorithm (PPO-Lag, TD3-Lag), making the framework modular and compatible with existing constrained optimization methods.

## Results

On the Safety-Gymnasium benchmark (cost threshold ε=10), FCSRL (built on TD3-Lag) achieves the highest reward on all six vector-state tasks while satisfying the safety constraint: 24.4±1.4 on PointGoal1, 10.5±2.3 on PointButton1, 11.8±4.5 on PointPush1, 13.5±2.6 on PointGoal2, 27.6±2.0 on CarGoal1, and 3.6±1.3 on CarButton1. All cost returns remain at or below the threshold (≤11), whereas baselines like FOCOPS violate constraints severely (e.g., cost 73.6 on PointGoal2). On image-based tasks, FCSRL also outperforms CURL, SPR, and the value-consistent model in both reward and constraint satisfaction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FCSRL (TD3-Lag) dominates all four safe RL baselines (CPO, PPO-Lag, FOCOPS, CVPO) on every Safety-Gymnasium task in reward while maintaining constraint satisfaction. The margin is largest on PointGoal1 (24.4 vs. 13.4 for PPO-Lag) and CarGoal1 (27.6 vs. 15.0 for FOCOPS). FOCOPS and PPO-Lag frequently violate the cost constraint (e.g., FOCOPS reaches cost 73.6 on PointGoal2 vs. threshold 10), whereas FCSRL keeps all costs at or below 11. Against representation-learning baselines (SALE, value-consistent model, TCL, forward raw/latent), FCSRL's advantage grows as constraints tighten: at ε=10 on PointPush1, FCSRL achieves 11.6 reward vs. 8.6 for SALE and VC, and the gap widens further at ε=5. On image-based tasks, CURL fails to learn useful temporal features (near-zero reward on CarGoal1), SPR violates constraints on PointButton1 and PointGoal2, and FCSRL outperforms the value-consistent model by leveraging the smoother feasibility signal for embedding supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of self-supervised representation learning for RL (SimSiam-style dynamics consistency from Chen & He 2021, SALE from Fujimoto et al. 2023) and safe RL via Lagrangian primal-dual methods (Ray et al. 2019, Stooke et al. 2020). Its key departure from prior representation learning work is replacing value consistency—which the authors show fails for sparse cost signals—with a feasibility score (maximum discounted cost) inspired by Hamilton-Jacobi reachability from control theory. The feasibility score is combined with dynamics consistency in a unified self-supervised objective, and distributional RL (Bellemare et al. 2017) is borrowed to handle the action-marginalized feasibility prediction. The framework is designed to be a drop-in module compatible with existing model-free safe RL algorithms like PPO-Lag and TD3-Lag.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in safe RL: sparse cost signals make constraint estimation noisier and less learnable than reward estimation, leading to suboptimal reward-cost trade-offs. By providing a theoretically smoother safety supervision signal and demonstrating consistent gains across vector-state and image-based tasks under varying constraint tightness, FCSRL offers a modular improvement that practitioners can plug into existing safe RL pipelines. Open questions include whether the feasibility score can serve as a direct auxiliary signal for policy learning to achieve state-wise safety (mentioned as future work), how the approach scales to more complex or partially observable environments, and whether the advantage persists in offline safe RL settings where data distribution shift compounds estimation difficulty.
