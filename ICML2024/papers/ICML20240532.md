## Problem

Existing chain-of-thought distillation methods treat all rationale tokens equally during training, causing student models to fail at precisely mimicking the keypoint tokens that are critical for correct reasoning. Additionally, these methods distill all reasoning steps simultaneously without distinguishing their learning order, diverging from the human cognitive pattern of progressing from easier to harder tasks and leading to sub-optimal student performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240532/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reasoning question as text input and produces a step-by-step rationale followed by a final answer. The teacher LLM (GPT-3.5-Turbo) generates rationales via zero-shot CoT prompting, which are then distilled into smaller student models (LLaMA-7B, FlanT5-XL, FlanT5-Large) trained with LoRA. During progressive distillation, the student is given the question plus a prefix of preceding rationale steps as input and must generate only the remaining steps, with the number of provided prefix steps decreasing over training epochs until the student generates the full rationale from the question alone.

## Method

KPOD introduces two coupled components: (1) a rationale token weighting module that learns per-token significance via Gumbel-Softmax mask learning, optimized by an answer prediction loss (predicting the answer from the question plus masked rationale prefixes) and a mask ratio loss (maximizing the fraction of masked tokens), so that the unmasked probability serves as a distillation weight emphasizing keypoint tokens; and (2) an in-rationale progressive distillation strategy that schedules learning from the last few steps toward the full rationale, where step difficulty is computed via a token-weighted generation loss (using softmax-normalized significance weights to suppress irrelevant tokens) and a monotone submodular value function—solved approximately by the FTGP algorithm—selects diverse questions (via K-means clustering on GloVe embeddings) to escalate difficulty at each epoch under a knapsack constraint. The final distillation loss multiplies each token's log-probability by its learned significance weight, simultaneously enforcing precise keypoint mimicry and easy-to-hard step ordering.

## Results

KPOD is evaluated on four reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, ASDiv, SVAMP, CommonsenseQA) using accuracy (%) as the metric, with GPT-3.5-Turbo as the teacher and three student models of varying sizes (LLaMA-7B, FlanT5-XL, FlanT5-Large). With LLaMA-7B, KPOD achieves 46.74% on GSM8K, 71.02% on ASDiv, 68.67% on SVAMP, and 77.89% on CommonsenseQA, outperforming the strongest baseline (MCC-KD) by 5.16%, 5.26%, 4.00%, and 1.48% respectively. The gains are even more pronounced for smaller students: FlanT5-Large (760M) improves from 20.03% to 22.46% on GSM8K and from 78.13% to 81.41% on CommonsenseQA over the best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KPOD consistently outperforms all four baselines (SCoTD, Specialized KD, SCOTT, MCC-KD) across every dataset and student model configuration. With LLaMA-7B on GSM8K, KPOD reaches 46.74% accuracy, surpassing MCC-KD (41.58%, the strongest baseline) by 5.16 points and SCOTT (40.97%) by 5.77 points. On CommonsenseQA with FlanT5-XL, KPOD achieves 88.04%, a substantial 4.42-point margin over SCOTT (83.62%), which was the best baseline on that particular dataset. For the smallest student (FlanT5-Large, 760M), KPOD leads by 2.43 points on GSM8K (22.46% vs. Specialized KD's 20.03%) and by 3.28 points on CommonsenseQA (81.41% vs. MCC-KD's 78.13%), indicating that the keypoint-weighted progressive strategy is especially beneficial when the student has limited capacity. No baseline is competitive on any of the four benchmarks with any student model.

## Relation to Prior Work

KPOD extends the CoT distillation line (SCoTD, Specialized KD, SCOTT, MCC-KD) by addressing two gaps those methods share: treating all rationale tokens equally and ignoring the learning order of reasoning steps. It repurposes mask learning (via Gumbel-Softmax) to produce per-token significance weights, and adapts curriculum learning principles to operate *within* a single rationale rather than across the dataset, departing from prior CL methods (Adaptive CL, SPL, ICL) that schedule at the sample or token-sequence level without accounting for step-level reasoning difficulty. The submodular optimization (FTGP) for question selection is borrowed from combinatorial optimization and applied here for the first time in a distillation scheduling context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a small set of 'keypoint' tokens (digits, operators, key nouns) carries most of the reasoning signal, and that a student benefits from generating later rationale steps before earlier ones, offers a concrete and implementable recipe for improving CoT distillation without architectural changes. Limitations include reliance on a single teacher (GPT-3.5-Turbo), evaluation on only four relatively small benchmarks, and the added training overhead of the mask-learning module and per-stage submodular optimization. Open questions include whether the token-weighting signal transfers across teacher models or to longer, multi-paragraph rationales, and whether the progressive schedule generalizes to non-mathematical reasoning where step boundaries are less clear.
