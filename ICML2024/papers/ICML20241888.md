## Problem

Estimating the intrinsic dimension (ID) of high-dimensional data manifolds remains challenging: traditional statistical estimators (PCA, MLE, nearest-neighbor) fail on complex, high-ID data, and normalizing-flow-based methods suffer from a stability-versus-expressivity trade-off that limits their reliability. Diffusion models, despite being powerful generative models that implicitly capture data geometry, had not been exploited for ID estimation. This paper proves that diffusion models encode the data manifold's normal bundle and uses this to build the first diffusion-based ID estimator.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241888/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires a pre-trained score network s_θ(x,t) and a set of data points x₀ from the dataset. For each x₀, it samples K=4d perturbed points from N(x₀, σ²_{t₀}I) at a small diffusion time t₀, evaluates the score at each, and assembles them into a d×K matrix. The output is a score spectrum (singular values from SVD) and a pointwise ID estimate k̂(x₀) = d − argmax(sᵢ − sᵢ₊₁); the final ID is the maximum over many randomly chosen x₀ points to account for local geometric and approximation errors.

## Method

The key theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) proves that as diffusion time t→0, the score ∇ₓ ln pₜ(x) at any point x near the manifold points directly at its orthogonal projection π(x) on the manifold, so the score lies in the normal space N_{π(x)}M. The proof uses tubular neighborhoods and Morse theory to show the tangential component vanishes relative to the normal component. Practically, one samples K=4d Gaussian-perturbed copies of a data point at small t₀, evaluates the trained score network at each, and performs SVD on the resulting score matrix: the number of large singular values equals the normal space dimension (d−k), while k singular values collapse near zero, yielding the intrinsic dimension. This avoids the architectural constraints of normalizing flows and leverages the training stability of diffusion models, making it robust to high-ID manifolds where flow-based methods fail.

## Results

On controlled Euclidean and image manifolds with known intrinsic dimension, the proposed diffusion-based method achieves estimates within 1–2 of ground truth across all settings (e.g., 11 vs. 10 for the 10-sphere, 100 vs. 100 for the 100-dim Squares manifold, 98 vs. 100 for the 100-dim Gaussian blobs manifold). On the 100-dimensional Gaussian blobs manifold — the most challenging setting — the method estimates 98 while ID-NF drops to 56.3, MLE to ~35–40, Local PCA to 18, and PPCA overestimates to 985. On MNIST (unknown true ID), the method estimates 152, which aligns with the auto-encoder reconstruction error plateau, whereas MLE estimates only ~13–14 and PPCA overestimates to 706.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed diffusion-based method outperforms or matches all six baselines on every dataset. Against ID-NF (the strongest neural-network-based competitor), the method wins decisively on high-intrinsic-dimension settings: on the 100-dim Gaussian blobs manifold it estimates 98 vs. ID-NF's 56.3, and on the 100-dim Squares manifold it hits 100 vs. 94.2. The MLE estimator (Levina & Bickel, Haro et al.) consistently underestimates, dropping to 35–40 on 100-dim manifolds and only ~13–14 on MNIST, suggesting it systematically underestimates manifold dimension. Local PCA fails catastrophically on non-linear manifolds (32 on the 1-dim Spaghetti line, 18 on the 100-dim Gaussian blobs), while PPCA overestimates severely on non-linear data (98 on the Spaghetti line, 985 on the 100-dim Gaussian blobs, 706 on MNIST). On the simpler linear-structure datasets (spheres, low-dim Squares), all methods perform comparably, confirming that the advantage of the diffusion-based approach emerges specifically for complex, high-dimensional, non-linear manifolds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the continuous-time score-based diffusion framework of Song et al. (2020), repurposing the learned score function as a geometric probe of the data manifold's normal bundle. It departs from prior ID estimation literature (PCA-based, nearest-neighbor MLE, and normalizing-flow methods) by introducing the first diffusion-model-based estimator, leveraging the fact that at low noise levels the score vector becomes orthogonal to the manifold. The theoretical analysis draws on differential geometry tools (tubular neighborhoods, Morse theory) to rigorously establish the score-to-normal-bundle correspondence, going beyond the heuristic manifold-hypothesis connections explored in Pidstrigach (2022) and Oko et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that diffusion models implicitly encode the normal bundle of the data manifold is a fundamental geometric insight with implications beyond ID estimation, potentially informing how generative models capture low-dimensional structure. The empirical finding that MLE-based estimates (as used in Pope et al., 2021) dramatically underestimate MNIST's intrinsic dimension (~14 vs. ~152) challenges widely-cited numbers in the literature. Open questions include whether the method extends to non-compact or non-smooth manifolds, how it scales to very high ambient dimensions (e.g., ImageNet-scale), and whether the normal-bundle structure can be exploited for other geometric tasks such as tangent-space estimation or manifold reconstruction.
