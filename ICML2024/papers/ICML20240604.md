## Problem

Over-parameterized neural networks memorize training data, including mislabeled and long-tailed samples, but existing memorization metrics like Feldman-Zhang scores require training thousands of models per dataset, making them prohibitively expensive. This paper asks whether a single training run can yield a reliable per-sample memorization score, and whether the curvature of the loss landscape around each input can serve that role.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240604/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB training images (28×28 for MNIST/FashionMNIST, 32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) with their ground-truth labels, processed through a ResNet18 trained to near-100% training accuracy. For each sample, the network is perturbed along n random Rademacher directions at each epoch, and the resulting per-sample curvature score (trace of the squared input-Hessian of cross-entropy loss) is averaged over all T epochs. The output is a scalar memorization score per training sample, enabling ranking, AUROC-based mislabel detection, and qualitative inspection of the highest-curvature examples.

## Method

The core metric is Tr(H²) of the loss with respect to the input x, estimated via Hutchinson's trace estimator using Rademacher vectors v: Curv(X) = (1/nT) Σ_t Σ_i ||∂(L_t(x+hv)−L_t(x))/∂x||², where h is a small perturbation step and the Hessian-vector product is computed via a finite-difference approximation. Two design choices distinguish this from prior curvature work (Garg & Roy 2023; Moosavi-Dezfooli et al. 2019): (1) random Rademacher directions replace adversarial directions for more reliable estimation, and (2) curvature is averaged across all training epochs rather than computed only at the end, accounting for the shifting decision boundaries during learning. The computational overhead is O(nT) backward passes versus O(T) for vanilla training, with n typically 5–20, and no explicit Hessian or second-order gradients are ever formed. The paper validates the metric three ways: high cosine similarity (0.82–0.90) with FZ scores on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet, state-of-the-art AUROC for detecting synthetically mislabeled samples, and qualitative identification of mislabeled, long-tailed, and duplicated-with-conflicting-labels samples that FZ scores miss.

## Results

The proposed curvature metric achieves a cosine similarity of 0.82 (CIFAR-100) and 0.72 (ImageNet) with Feldman-Zhang memorization scores using only a single training run, rising to 0.90 and 0.87 on the top-K most memorized samples. For synthetic mislabeling detection, curvature achieves 100.0% AUROC on MNIST (1% corruption) and 97.4% on CIFAR-10 (1% corruption), outperforming all individual baselines. The combined Curv^SSFT method reaches 96.0% AUROC on CIFAR-100 at 1% corruption, surpassing SSFT alone (94.8%) and curvature alone (90.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Feldman-Zhang (FZ) memorization scores, the proposed curvature achieves a cosine similarity of 0.82 on CIFAR-100 and 0.72 on ImageNet (with weight decay), requiring only one training run versus thousands of models for FZ. The prior curvature method from Garg & Roy (2023) drops to 0.17 cosine similarity (with WD) on CIFAR-100, confirming that averaging over epochs and using Rademacher vectors are essential. For mislabeled data detection, curvature outperforms Inconfidence, Confident Learning, and Learning Time on all three datasets and all corruption levels. On MNIST and CIFAR-10, curvature alone beats SSFT at every corruption level (e.g., 97.4% vs 94.5% at 1% on CIFAR-10); on CIFAR-100, SSFT alone edges out curvature (94.8% vs 90.9% at 1%), but the Curv^SSFT combination surpasses both (96.0%). In duplicate detection (Table 10), curvature identifies 80/250 synthetic duplicates in the top-100 ranking versus 73 for Adversarial Distance and 0 for Learning Time or SSFT, and achieves 0.82 cosine similarity with FZ scores compared to 0.57 for Adversarial Distance and 0.51 for SSFT (Table 11).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes input-space loss curvature—previously studied for adversarial robustness (Moosavi-Dezfooli et al., 2019) and coreset selection (Garg & Roy, 2023)—as a per-sample memorization metric. The key methodological departures from Garg & Roy (2023) are replacing adversarial perturbation directions with Hutchinson's trace estimator using Rademacher vectors, and averaging curvature over all training epochs rather than computing it at the end of training; the authors show both changes are essential for reliable memorization scores. The metric is validated against Feldman & Zhang (2020) influence-based memorization scores, which require training thousands of models, whereas this approach needs only a single training run.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary practical impact is a ~3-orders-of-magnitude reduction in compute for measuring per-sample memorization, making dataset auditing feasible at scale. The discovery of duplicated images with conflicting labels in CIFAR100 and ImageNet—a failure mode missed by FZ scores and prior duplicate-detection tools—demonstrates that curvature analysis can surface dataset integrity issues invisible to other methods. Limitations include the requirement to overfit (100% training accuracy) for the metric to be most informative, the non-trivial curvature dynamics (increase-then-decrease) that necessitate epoch averaging, and the finding that curvature alone underperforms SSFT on CIFAR100, suggesting it works best as a complementary signal. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-vision modalities, whether the discovered failure modes extend to other large-scale benchmarks, and whether curvature-based early stopping (explored briefly in the appendix) can be made into a principled regularization strategy.
