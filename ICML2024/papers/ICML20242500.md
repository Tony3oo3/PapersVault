## Problem

Reward-driven alignment of diffusion models suffers from overoptimization—overfitting to imperfect reward models that degrades true generation quality—but the underlying causes remain unclear. Existing methods compute rewards exclusively on final generated images, creating a mismatch with the sequential multi-step denoising process that constitutes the model's inherent temporal inductive bias. Additionally, primacy bias in the critic network's active neurons further exacerbates overfitting to early training experiences, yet this mechanism had not been identified or addressed in prior work.

## Input / Output

The method takes text prompts as conditioning signals and produces LoRA-adapted Stable Diffusion v1.4 parameters that generate aligned images. During training, the temporal critic consumes intermediate noisy latents at every denoising timestep (not just the final clean image), using the encoder of an off-the-shelf reward model (CLIP-based aesthetic predictor, PickScore, or HPSv2) plus a 5-layer MLP to predict per-timestep reward residuals anchored to the final-image reward. Evaluation is conducted via a novel cross-reward generalization metric, measuring out-of-domain reward scores after finetuning on a specific reward function.

## Method

TDPO-R reformulates diffusion model alignment as a multi-step MDP with timestep-dependent rewards, where a lightweight temporal critic (reward model encoder + 5-layer MLP) predicts per-timestep reward residuals anchored to the final-image reward. The policy (LoRA-adapted U-Net) and critic are updated simultaneously via per-timestep gradient descent rather than per-batch updates, matching the temporal granularity of the denoising process and improving sample efficiency. To counteract primacy bias, TDPO-R periodically resets only the active neurons in the critic (identified by activation score thresholding) every F epochs, deliberately preserving dormant neurons that the authors discover act as an implicit regularization against overoptimization. This combination of temporally structured rewards and selective active-neuron reset yields superior cross-reward generalization and sample efficiency compared to DDPO and AlignProp.

## Results

The paper evaluates TDPO and TDPO-R primarily through line plots rather than a single numerical table. On sample efficiency (Figure 4), both TDPO and TDPO-R consistently reach target reward levels (e.g., Aesthetic Score ~7±0.1) with fewer reward queries than DDPO-2 and DDPO-100 across all three training rewards (PickScore, HPSv2, Aesthetic Score). On cross-reward generalization (Figure 3), TDPO and TDPO-R maintain higher out-of-domain evaluation scores (ImageReward, PickScore, HPSv2) as the training reward increases, whereas DDPO and AlignProp show declining or plateauing out-of-domain scores, indicating less overoptimization. The neuron reset ablation (Figure 6) confirms that resetting active critic neurons (TDPO-R) yields the best cross-reward generalization, outperforming no reset (TDPO), dormant neuron reset, all-neuron reset, and KL regularization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TDPO and TDPO-R outperform DDPO (Black et al., 2024) on both sample efficiency and cross-reward generalization across all three reward functions. Critically, even when DDPO is re-implemented at the same 100-updates-per-epoch frequency as TDPO (DDPO-100), TDPO still achieves faster reward convergence and better out-of-domain generalization, showing the advantage stems from the temporal reward structure rather than merely higher update frequency. Against AlignProp (Prabhudesai et al., 2023), TDPO/TDPO-R demonstrate superior cross-reward generalization and, in qualitative comparisons (Figures 2, 7, 13), produce images with greater stylistic diversity and higher fidelity at matched reward levels, whereas AlignProp and DDPO exhibit noticeable saturation in style, background, and lighting. The pre-trained Stable Diffusion v1.4 serves as a reference point; all fine-tuned methods improve the target reward but at varying degrees of overoptimization, with TDPO-R showing the mildest degradation in out-of-domain metrics. The neuron reset ablation further shows that TDPO-R's active-neuron reset strategy outperforms KL regularization (used in DPOK, Fan et al. 2023) for mitigating overoptimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-based diffusion model alignment line initiated by DPOK (Fan et al., 2023) and DDPO (Black et al., 2024), which map the denoising process to a multi-step MDP but assign a single timestep-independent reward to the final image. It departs from this formulation by introducing timestep-dependent temporal rewards via a learned critic, aligning the reward granularity with the per-timestep gradient updates. It also builds on the primacy bias literature (Nikishin et al., 2022; Sokar et al., 2023) but reaches a counterintuitive conclusion: rather than resetting dormant neurons as prior work recommends, the paper finds that resetting *active* neurons in the critic mitigates overoptimization while resetting dormant ones worsens it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reward overoptimization is a practical bottleneck for deploying reward-aligned diffusion models, and this paper provides both a mechanistic diagnosis (inductive bias mismatch + primacy bias in active neurons) and a simple, low-overhead mitigation (temporal rewards + periodic active-neuron reset). The cross-reward generalization metric it introduces offers a more principled evaluation than single-reward curves. Key limitations include reliance on LoRA (precluding full-model neuron analysis), evaluation on a single base model (SD v1.4), and the unexplored territory of multi-reward learning, which the authors flag as an important open direction.
