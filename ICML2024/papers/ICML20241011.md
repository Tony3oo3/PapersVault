## Problem

Deep reinforcement learning policies are known to be vulnerable to imperceptible adversarial perturbations, yet no systematic diagnostic method exists to understand the spatial and temporal structure of these vulnerabilities in the policy's decision boundary. Existing adversarial training techniques (e.g., SA-DDQN) claim to certify robustness, but it remains unclear whether they eliminate correlated non-robust representations or merely relocate them. The paper asks how to precisely identify, visualize, and quantify the directions of instability in deep neural policies and how these directions transform under adversarial attacks, distributional shifts, and different training regimes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained DRL Q-network and a set of MDP state observations (RGB frames from ALE games collected over 10 episodes), along with adversarial perturbation hyperparameters (e.g., ε for Nesterov momentum, κ for Carlini-Wagner). The principal output is a vector of the same spatial dimensions as the state observation, directly renderable as an image to visualize where non-robust features concentrate. A scalar feature correlation quotient in [0,1] quantifies how much the correlated non-robust features shift under a given transformation, and per-state gradient norms are tracked over time to measure temporal instability.

## Method

RA-NLD defines an ε-non-Lipschitz direction at each state as the unit vector that maximizes the gap between the maximum Q-value at the perturbed state and the Q-value of the previously optimal action, approximated via the gradient of the softmax cross-entropy loss at the base state. By collecting these gradient vectors across a set of visited states and forming their empirical covariance matrix L(S), the principal non-Lipschitz direction is obtained as the top eigenvector of L(S) (Proposition 3.3), yielding a single spatially interpretable direction capturing the dominant correlated vulnerability. The feature correlation quotient Λ(S',S) = G_{S'}^T L(S) G_{S'} / G_S^T L(S) G_S (bounded in [0,1] by Proposition 3.6) then quantifies how much the non-robust feature structure changes under adversarial perturbations, natural distributional shifts, or independent stochastic runs. The method is applied to both vanilla Double DQN and certified adversarially trained SA-DDQN policies across four ALE games, with Fourier analysis of the principal direction revealing that adversarial training produces smoother, more evenly spread spatial frequency signatures compared to vanilla training.

## Results

The feature correlation quotient Λ(S^Ψ, S) quantifies how much adversarial perturbations remold correlated non-robust features. Under Carlini&Wagner, the quotient drops to 0.041±0.041 on Pong and 0.284±0.023 on BankHeist, indicating near-complete reorganization of vulnerable directions, while Nesterov Momentum preserves more structure (0.344±0.020 on Pong, 0.341±0.024 on BankHeist). For natural distributional shifts, blurring causes the largest drop (0.085±0.008 on Pong, 0.095±0.013 on BankHeist), while compression artifacts preserve the most correlation (0.493±0.019 on Pong). Adversarially trained (SA-DDQN) policies exhibit significantly higher variance in gradient norms ‖∇J‖² across states compared to vanilla-trained policies, indicating spikier temporal instability in their non-robust representations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a diagnostic/analysis paper rather than a method comparison, so it does not benchmark RA-NLD against competing interpretability tools. Instead, it compares the effects of four adversarial attack formulations (Carlini&Wagner, Nesterov Momentum, DeepFool, Elastic-Net) on the same policies, revealing that C&W most aggressively reorganizes non-robust features (lowest Λ on Pong: 0.041) while Nesterov Momentum preserves the most structure (highest Λ on Pong: 0.344). The paper also contrasts vanilla-trained (Double DQN) versus certified adversarially-trained (SA-DDQN) policies, finding that SA-DDQN does not eliminate non-robust features but relocates them to disjoint spatial coordinates with dramatically higher temporal variance in gradient norms. Across five natural distributional shifts (brightness/contrast, compression, rotation, perspective, blurring), blurring and rotation cause the largest disruption to correlated non-robust features (Λ < 0.15 on Pong), while compression artifacts preserve the most (Λ ≈ 0.49 on Pong), consistent with the qualitative visualizations of the principal non-Lipschitz direction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes standard spectral analysis (PCA on gradient outer products) and adversarial perturbation formulations (Carlini-Wagner, Nesterov momentum, DeepFool, elastic-net) into a unified diagnostic framework for visualizing correlated non-robust features in deep RL policies. It extends the line of work by Korkmaz (2022, 2023) on shared adversarial directions and the limitations of certified adversarial training in RL, shifting the focus from proposing new attacks or defenses to systematically *understanding* the spatial and temporal structure of policy vulnerabilities. The conceptual bridge to visual-illusion research in neuroscience (Hubel & Wiesel, Grunewald & Lankheet) is novel but primarily serves as an interpretive analogy rather than a methodological dependency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that state-of-the-art certified adversarial training (SA-DDQN) does not eliminate non-robust features but instead produces more spatially concentrated, temporally spiky vulnerabilities challenges the assumption that certified robustness guarantees safety in sequential decision-making. The RA-NLD framework provides a practical diagnostic tool for algorithm developers to inspect how design choices (architecture, training method, state representation) shape policy vulnerabilities before deployment. Limitations include evaluation restricted to ALE games with DDQN, and the method's reliance on gradient access to the Q-network, which may not transfer directly to model-free or black-box policy settings. Open questions include whether the observed disjoint non-robust patterns under adversarial training generalize to continuous-control or multi-agent settings.
