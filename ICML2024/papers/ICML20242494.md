## Problem

CLIP relies on a single contrastive supervision per image-text pair, leaving substantial uni-modal and cross-modal information untapped, and operates exclusively in the spatial domain where the human visual system's sensitivity to high-frequency variations (textures, edges, noise) is underexploited. Additionally, non-informative tokens in the ViT image encoder inflate computational cost without contributing meaningful signal, slowing training and hindering cross-modal alignment.

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images (split into h×w patches) and natural-language text captions as input, plus a 64×64 low-resolution image counterpart fed to a lightweight DeiT-Tiny Guide module. The image encoder produces two distinct instance-level embeddings (frequency and spatial) alongside token-level representations, all aligned against the text encoder's embedding via a combined loss. Downstream, these embeddings support zero-shot/linear-probe classification (11 datasets) and zero-shot image-text retrieval (Flickr30K, MS-COCO).

## Method

MLIP splits the ViT image encoder into a Frequency Stage (4 Fourier Blocks applying 2D DFT, a Lego-Filter that modulates the power spectrum with piece-wise cosine filters, and IFFT reconstruction) and a Spatial Stage (remaining MHSA blocks with inserted Acceleration Blocks). At the instance level, both frequency and spatial embeddings are contrastively aligned with text embeddings via InfoNCE. At the token level, frequency tokens use a loose one-to-many cosine-similarity matching with text tokens (following FILIP), while spatial tokens use a strict one-to-one bipartite matching solved by the Kuhn-Munkres algorithm to avoid assignment conflicts. For acceleration, a controllable-compression-rate token merging strategy selects the least important tokens (ranked by attention scores from a fine class token) and merges them, guided by cross-attention with a coarse class token from the pre-trained Guide that injects global semantic context before merging decisions are made.

## Results

On ImageNet zero-shot classification with ViT-B/16, MLIP achieves 46.3% top-1 accuracy, surpassing CLIP (39.0%) and SLIP (43.2%) but trailing DeCLIP (47.9%) by 1.6 points; with label smoothing, MLIP reaches 48.6%, exceeding DeCLIP. On zero-shot image-text retrieval (Flickr30K), MLIP-ViT-B/32 achieves 53.1% I2T R@1 and 35.2% T2I R@1, outperforming all baselines including DeCLIP (51.4/34.3) and UniCLIP (52.3/34.8). In terms of computational efficiency, MLIP achieves the best ZS-top-1/GFLOPs ratio (0.94) compared to CLIP (0.82), SLIP (0.74), and DeCLIP (0.71), while using fewer GPU hours (370) than SLIP (488) and DeCLIP (545).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MLIP clearly outperforms CLIP and SLIP across all classification and retrieval benchmarks: on ImageNet ZS (ViT-B/16), MLIP gains +7.3 points over CLIP and +3.1 over SLIP. Against FILIP, MLIP achieves higher ZS accuracy (41.1 vs. 39.5 on ViT-B/32) and additionally reports LP results. Against DeCLIP, MLIP trails by 1.6–2.1 points on ZS ImageNet without label smoothing, but closes and reverses the gap when label smoothing is applied (48.6 vs. 47.9 on ViT-B/16), and matches or exceeds DeCLIP on LP (70.2 vs. 70.4 on B/32). In zero-shot retrieval, MLIP dominates all baselines: on Flickr30K I2T R@1, MLIP-ViT-B/32 reaches 53.1% vs. DeCLIP 51.4%, UniCLIP 52.3%, and CLIP 34.9%; on MS-COCO T2I R@1, MLIP-ViT-B/16 achieves 24.5% vs. UniCLIP 20.2% and DeCLIP 18.4%. In computational efficiency, MLIP achieves the best performance-per-FLOP ratio (0.94) and the best performance-per-GPU-hour ratio (1.59), indicating superior overall efficiency despite slightly lower peak accuracy than DeCLIP.

## Relation to Prior Work

MLIP extends the CLIP paradigm by introducing frequency-domain supervision (via DFT and a Lego-Filter) alongside the standard spatial-domain contrastive loss, a combination not previously explored in vision-language pretraining. It builds on the data-efficiency line of work (SLIP, DeCLIP, FILIP) that expands supervision beyond a single InfoNCE term, and on token-level alignment methods (FILIP, TokenFlow), but departs by operating in both spatial and frequency token spaces with distinct matching strategies (one-to-many for frequency tokens, one-to-one via Kuhn-Munkres for spatial tokens). For acceleration, it adapts the token merging idea from ToMe (Bolya et al., 2022) to the CLIP setting for the first time, augmented by a lightweight Guide module that injects global semantics from a low-resolution ViT to make merging decisions more reliable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical pain points in CLIP training—underutilized per-pair information and high ViT compute cost—within a single unified framework, and demonstrates that frequency-domain features (textures, scene complexity) provide genuinely complementary supervision to spatial features, particularly on texture- and scene-heavy benchmarks like DTD and Flowers. However, the authors acknowledge that MLIP does not surpass SOTA (DeCLIP) without additional label-smoothing tricks, and that token merging complicates transfer to dense prediction tasks. Open questions include whether the frequency-stage design scales to ViT-Large or beyond, whether the Guide module can be replaced by learned alternatives, and whether the multi-domain supervision principle generalizes to video-language or audio-language pretraining.
