## Problem

State-of-the-art Tree-of-Thoughts (ToT) methods rely on the LLM to assign pointwise scores to intermediate thoughts for selection, but LLM scores are noisy and unreliable, potentially misleading the search toward suboptimal reasoning paths. This paper identifies this widespread yet understudied noise problem and asks whether a more robust evaluation mechanism can replace pointwise scoring without requiring additional models or extensive ensembling.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query (e.g., an arithmetic word problem, a set of four numbers for Game of 24, or a partially filled Sudoku grid) and iteratively generates task-specific intermediate thoughts: short text snippets for QA, single arithmetic operations for Game of 24, or partial grid fillings for Sudoku. After T rounds of generation and pairwise comparison, it outputs K most promising chains of thoughts, from which a final answer is determined by majority voting over an accumulated answer list.

## Method

C-ToT replaces the pointwise scoring step in ToT with a knockout-style pairwise comparison: in each round, candidate intermediate thoughts are randomly paired and the LLM is prompted to select the more promising one from each pair, halving the candidate set until K thoughts remain. Two variants handle comparison noise differently: Standard Mode performs n independent comparisons per pair with majority voting, while Dueling Mode implements the Knockout algorithm from dueling bandits, adaptively allocating comparison budgets per pair based on empirical win-probability confidence intervals (with bias and confidence parameters scheduled across knockout rounds). A key design choice is maintaining a 'remain list' of previously unselected thoughts that are re-included in subsequent rounds' comparisons, enabling the algorithm to recover potentially mis-evaluated thoughts without relying on fixed score thresholds. Theoretical analysis via the (ε,δ)-PAC framework guarantees that the ε-maximum thought is identified with probability at least 1−δ using O(γ²|Z|/ε² · log(1/δ)) comparisons per round.

## Results

Across six reasoning benchmarks, C-ToT (Duel.) achieves the highest accuracy on all tasks: 73.0% on GSM8K, 70.4% on Coin Flip (OOD), 80.0% on BBH, 63.0% on AQuA, 41.0% on Game of 24, and 75.4% on Sudoku. The Standard variant (C-ToT Stand.) is competitive, reaching 71.3%, 66.2%, 75.7%, 61.4%, 40.0%, and 74.4% respectively, indicating that a single pairwise comparison often suffices. The largest gains over the SToT baseline appear on Game of 24 (+6.7 pp) and Sudoku (+15.4 pp), where sequential reasoning makes noisy pointwise scoring most damaging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-ToT (Duel.) outperforms all six baselines on every dataset. Against the closest competitor SToT (Yao et al., 2023), the Dueling variant gains +13.7 pp on GSM8K (73.0 vs 59.3), +8.3 pp on BBH (80.0 vs 69.6), +5.9 pp on AQuA (63.0 vs 57.1), +6.7 pp on Game of 24 (41.0 vs 34.3), and +15.4 pp on Sudoku (75.4 vs 60.0); on Coin Flip (OOD) it leads by +8.3 pp (70.4 vs 62.1). The SC-CoT self-consistency baseline (Wang et al., 2022) is outperformed on all tasks, with the largest margin on Game of 24 (41.0 vs 8.0). PoT (Chen et al., 2023) and Self-Refine (Madaan et al., 2023) are both dominated by C-ToT across all six benchmarks. GoT (Besta et al., 2023) is not directly comparable due to its subtask-decomposition paradigm, but a preliminary C-GoT variant on the sorting task matches S-GoT accuracy (90.5% vs 90.4% for 32 elements) while using more prompt tokens, suggesting the comparison mechanism transfers to graph-based settings as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the score-based Tree-of-Thoughts (S-ToT) framework of Yao et al. (2023) by replacing pointwise LLM scoring with pairwise comparison for selecting intermediate thoughts, motivated by Vapnik's principle that the easier sub-problem (comparison) suffices when the harder one (accurate scoring) is unreliable. The Dueling variant directly instantiates the Knockout algorithm from Falahatgar et al. (2017) on best-arm identification with dueling feedback, adapting its bias/confidence scheduling to the CoT generation setting. The paper also positions itself against self-consistency ensembling (SC-CoT, Wang et al. 2022) and graph-of-thoughts (Besta et al. 2023), arguing that pairwise comparison offers a more token-efficient and structurally natural alternative to repeated scoring or graph-level merging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and widespread issue—noisy LLM feedback in iterative reasoning—that prior ToT methods largely ignored, and provides both a simple mechanism (pairwise comparison) and a theoretically grounded variant (dueling bandits) to mitigate it. However, all experiments use only GPT-3.5-turbo, leaving open whether the advantage of comparison over scoring persists with more capable models that may score more reliably. The token cost of the Dueling variant can be substantially higher (e.g., ~6× S-ToT on QA), and the modest accuracy gains (2–6 points) raise questions about the cost-benefit trade-off in practice. A key open question is whether the pairwise mechanism generalizes to settings where the LLM's comparison ability is itself limited, such as very long or highly abstract intermediate thoughts.
