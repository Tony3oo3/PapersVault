## Problem

Sequential Bayesian optimal experimental design requires optimizing the Expected Information Gain (EIG) at each step, which in turn demands sampling from a sequence of intractable posterior distributions. A fundamental tension exists: EIG maximization seeks designs that maximize the KL distance between successive posteriors, which is precisely the regime where standard SMC samplers degrade. No prior method combined stochastic optimization for design with principled SMC-based posterior inference, and existing alternatives (variational approximations, reinforcement learning) either lack quality guarantees on the posterior or bypass posterior estimation entirely.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a Bayesian model with a closed-form likelihood p(y|θ,ξ) and prior p(θ), a continuous design space E ⊂ R^d, and the running history of previous designs and observations D_{k-1}. At each sequential step it outputs an optimal design ξ*_k (a point in R^d), a weighted particle approximation of the posterior p(θ|D_k) using M = N(L+1) particles partitioned into L+1 independent subsets of N particles each, and a stochastic gradient estimate of the PCE contrastive bound. The design parameter is continuous and differentiable, and the likelihood must admit a reparametrization T_{θ_0}^ξ(U) for gradient computation.

## Method

PASOA alternates two procedures: (1) adaptive tempered SMC (Algorithm 2) that propagates particles from p(θ|D_{k-1}) to p(θ|D_k) along a geometric tempering path, with the number of intermediate steps chosen adaptively to maintain a user-specified effective sample size threshold; and (2) stochastic gradient ascent on the Prior Contrastive Estimation (PCE) lower bound of the EIG (Algorithm 1/3), where the L+1 contrastive variables are sampled from the product-form particle approximation ⊗_{ℓ=0}^L P_{k,ℓ}^N rather than from a single joint SMC estimate. The product-form construction is key: it averages over N^{L+1} tuples instead of N, reducing variance without additional particles, and the paper proves L2 convergence of this estimator with a rate of (L+1)/N. The main theoretical result (Proposition 2) establishes that the resulting design estimators are consistent, converging in probability to the true EIG maximizers under compactness and isolated-maximum assumptions.

## Results

On the Source Location benchmark (K=30 experiments, L=200 contrastive variables, N=100 particles per contrastive variable), PASOA achieves a 30% improvement over RL-BOED on the SPCE lower bound and substantially higher SNMC upper bounds, while the L2 Wasserstein distance to the true source locations is two orders of magnitude lower than competing methods. PASOA also outperforms non-tempered SMC with far fewer particles (5×10³ vs 10⁶) at comparable computation time (~26s vs ~130s on a V100 GPU). On the CES benchmark (K=10 experiments), PASOA's [SPCE, SNMC] intervals are distinctly above all other methods, confirming the advantage of accurate tempered posterior estimation for design optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PASOA outperforms RL-BOED (Blau et al., 2022) on both SPCE and SNMC information gain metrics for Source Location, with a 30% SPCE improvement, despite RL-BOED being a non-myopic (long-sighted) approach that requires ~10–20 hours of training. The L2 Wasserstein distance for PASOA is two orders of magnitude lower, indicating far more accurate posterior estimation. Against VPCE (Foster et al., 2020), which uses variational inference for posterior approximation, PASOA achieves higher information gains and much lower Wasserstein distances, suggesting that accurate SMC-based posterior estimation is more critical than the variational approximation quality. PASOA also beats its own non-tempered SMC variant, demonstrating that tempering is essential: with only 5×10³ particles, tempered PASOA matches or exceeds SMC with 10⁶ particles. On the CES benchmark, both SMC-based methods (PASOA and non-tempered SMC) outperform RL-BOED and VPCE in terms of design sequence quality and posterior accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

PASOA unifies two previously separate threads in Bayesian optimal experimental design: stochastic gradient optimization of the PCE contrastive bound (Foster et al., 2020) for design selection, and tempered SMC (Del Moral et al., 2006; Neal, 2001) for sequential posterior inference. Prior SMC-based design work (Drovandi et al., 2014; Kuck et al., 2006) was restricted to discrete or static designs, while recent stochastic-optimization approaches (Foster et al., 2019–2021; Blau et al., 2022) either used variational posteriors or bypassed posterior estimation entirely via reinforcement learning. The paper's key departure is the joint use of adaptive tempered SMC (Jasra et al., 2011) to supply high-quality posterior particles that feed directly into the stochastic gradient, combined with a product-form estimator that exploits conditional independence across contrastive samples for variance reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first consistency guarantee for sequential design estimators in the BOED literature and identifies a fundamental tension: maximizing EIG actively increases the KL distance between successive posteriors, which is precisely the regime where standard SMC degrades. This makes tempering not merely a convenience but a necessity for accurate inference in sequential design. Open questions include extending to high-dimensional parameters where posterior inference becomes harder, incorporating amortized simulation-based inference for likelihood-free models, and exploring policy-based (non-myopic) extensions that could leverage the accurate posteriors PASOA already produces. The requirement for a tractable closed-form likelihood limits immediate applicability to simulation-only models.
