## Problem

Existing federated SAM-based methods (FedSAM, MoFedSAM, FedGAMMA, FedSMOO) compute perturbations from local client gradients, but under data heterogeneity the local loss landscape does not reflect the global one, so minimizing local sharpness fails to guide the aggregated model toward a global flat minimum. Moreover, all these methods require two backpropagations per local step (one for the perturbation, one for the update), imposing significant computational overhead on resource-constrained clients.

## Input / Output

Each client receives the current global model w^t and retains the global model from its last active round w_i^old; the perturbation direction is computed as the normalized difference w_i^old − w^t, requiring no gradient computation. The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for OfficeHome/DomainNet) and outputs a class prediction from the aggregated global model. Because the perturbation is fixed throughout all E local iterations within a round (unlike FedSAM's per-step local gradient), only one backpropagation is needed per local step, halving the per-iteration compute cost relative to prior SAM-based federated methods.

## Method

FedLESAM replaces the local-gradient perturbation of FedSAM with a locally estimated global perturbation: δ_{g,k}^t = ρ(w_i^old − w^t)/‖w_i^old − w^t‖, where w_i^old is the global model from the client's previous active round. The key insight is that the global update direction between consecutive rounds approximates the global gradient direction (the same principle Scaffold uses for variance reduction), so the perturbation can be inferred without any extra forward or backward pass. The local update then becomes w_{i,k+1}^t = w_{i,k}^t − η_l ∇F_i(w_{i,k}^t + ρδ_{g,k}^t), performing a single backpropagation per step. Two variants—FedLESAM-S (Scaffold-based) and FedLESAM-D (FedDyn-based)—incorporate variance reduction and dynamic regularization to improve estimation accuracy when the global model changes rapidly or client drift is large. Theoretically, the consistent perturbation across local steps eliminates the Δ = 120L²ρ²/(CET²) + 2L²ρ²/(CT) term present in FedSAM's convergence bound, yielding a tighter guarantee under the same assumptions.

## Results

On averaged test accuracy across four datasets, FedLESAM-S achieves 82.63% on CIFAR-10, 48.00% on CIFAR-100, 79.80% on OfficeHome, and 73.37% on DomainNet, outperforming FedAvg (75.96/41.69/77.99/70.25) by 3.83%, 3.79%, 0.87%, and 1.75% respectively. On CIFAR-100 under Dirichlet (β=0.1, 100 clients, 10% active), FedLESAM-S surpasses all baselines with ~6% improvement over FedAvg and ~1% over the best competing method. On OfficeHome's unseen 'Painting' domain, FedLESAM gains 2.39% over the best baseline and 7.22% over FedAvg. In terms of efficiency, FedLESAM-D reaches 74% CIFAR-10 accuracy in only 0.31× the communication rounds and 0.30× the computation time of FedAvg.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedLESAM-S consistently outperforms all SAM-based baselines (FedSAM, MoFedSAM, FedGAMMA, FedSMOO) on CIFAR-10/100 under both Dirichlet and Pathological splits, with the largest gap on CIFAR-100 where it gains ~6% over FedAvg and ~1% over the best baseline. On OfficeHome and DomainNet (leave-one-domain-out), FedLESAM outperforms all baselines on every target domain, while FedDyn and FedSMOO suffer from overfitting on the unseen domain. Compared to the non-SAM baselines Scaffold and FedDyn, FedLESAM-S and FedLESAM-D add 3.83% and 3.05% on CIFAR-10 respectively, confirming that global perturbation estimation is orthogonal to and complementary with variance reduction. In efficiency, FedLESAM-D requires only 0.30× the computation time and 0.31× the communication rounds of FedAvg to reach 74% CIFAR-10 accuracy, whereas FedSAM requires 1.56× computation time and MoFedSAM requires 1.16×, highlighting that FedLESAM's single-backpropagation design eliminates the extra forward pass that all other SAM-based methods incur.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of federated sharpness-aware minimization (FedSAM, MoFedSAM, FedGAMMA, FedSMOO) by identifying a fundamental misalignment: all prior methods compute perturbations from local gradients, which under data heterogeneity point toward local flat minima rather than the global flat minimum. The key conceptual departure is repurposing Scaffold's global-update estimation (the difference between consecutive global models) to approximate the global perturbation direction at each client, eliminating the need for a second forward-backward pass. The method is built on FedAvg as its base algorithm, with variants incorporating Scaffold's variance reduction (FedLESAM-S) and FedDyn's dynamic regularization (FedLESAM-D) to further reduce client drift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is twofold: FedLESAM achieves tighter convergence (eliminating the Δ term in the bound) while halving per-iteration computation compared to standard SAM-based federated methods, making it attractive for resource-constrained clients in medical or autonomous driving applications. Open questions include how the estimation degrades under extreme straggler conditions (many rounds without participation), whether the approach composes with other SAM variants (ASAM, VaSSO, mSAM), and whether the global-update-as-perturbation-direction heuristic holds for non-convex landscapes with multiple sharp basins. The paper acknowledges that ρ selection remains a trade-off between generalization and convergence that is not fully resolved theoretically.
