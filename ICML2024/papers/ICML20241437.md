## Problem

State-of-the-art text-to-3D generators rely on Score Distillation Sampling (SDS), which requires tens of thousands of 2D generator evaluations, is slow (hours per asset), unstable, and prone to artifacts like saturated colors and the Janus problem. The fundamental issue is that off-the-shelf 2D image generators are not 3D-aware, so SDS must slowly force different views to agree. This paper asks whether sufficiently high-quality multi-view generation can eliminate the need for SDS and large reconstruction networks entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241437/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a reference image (generated by the Emu T2I model) and produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting model along with a 16-frame turntable video at 512×512 resolution. The video represents a 360° camera orbit at fixed distance with a random elevation in [0, π/4]. The 3D model is initialized with 5000 Gaussians and optimized for 1200 iterations, with the video diffusion model invoked twice (10 DPM++ steps each) during fitting for iterative refinement. Total generation time is approximately 3 minutes.

## Method

IM-3D fine-tunes the Emu Video text-to-video diffusion model on 100k synthetic 3D turntable videos, training only the temporal convolutional and attention layers while freezing spatial layers, so the model learns to generate 16 consistent high-resolution views of an object from a single reference image and text prompt. Instead of SDS, the 16 generated frames are used to directly optimize a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation via differentiable rendering with image-level losses (LPIPS, MS-SSIM, and a segmentation mask loss), which robustly bridges small multi-view inconsistencies without requiring a learned reconstruction network. An iterative refinement loop then renders the fitted 3D model, adds noise to the rendered frames, and feeds them back into the video diffusion model for denoising, repeating this two times to progressively resolve residual inconsistencies. The use of a fast DPM++ sampler reduces the total number of 2D generator evaluations to roughly 40–80, a 10–100× reduction over SDS, while the efficiency of Gaussian Splatting's differentiable renderer enables full-resolution image-level losses at each iteration.

## Results

IM-3D achieves the highest CLIP(Image) faithfulness among all compared methods, scoring 91.40±5.5 on re-rendered views and 92.38±5.1 on synthesized views, while also leading in CLIP(Text) at 31.66±1.7 (re-rendered) and 31.92±1.6 (synthesized). The method completes generation in just 3 minutes, compared to 72 minutes for MVDream and 120 minutes for ImageDream. In human evaluation, annotators preferred IM-3D over all competitors on both image alignment and 3D quality criteria.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IM-3D outperforms all nine baselines on both CLIP(Text) and CLIP(Image) metrics for re-rendered 3D views. The largest gains are over MVDream (+14.5 CLIP(Image), 24× faster) and Zero123XL (+10.1 CLIP(Image)), which rely on image-based multi-view generators rather than video. Against the closest competitor ImageDream (120 min), IM-3D improves CLIP(Image) by 7.6 points while being 40× faster. Among the fast reconstruction-based methods (OpenLRM at 0.17 min, One2345++ at 0.75 min), IM-3D still leads by ~8 CLIP(Image) points, demonstrating that the video-based multi-view generation quality advantage outweighs the speed benefit of amortized reconstruction networks. In the human study, IM-3D was preferred over all competitors on both image alignment and 3D quality, with the paper noting the margins were often large.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant SDS-based text-to-3D paradigm (DreamFusion and its many variants) by arguing that sufficiently consistent multi-view generation can eliminate the need for score distillation entirely. It builds on the multi-view generation line (Zero-1-to-3, MVDream, ImageDream) but replaces image-based generators with a fine-tuned video diffusion model (Emu Video), and on the direct-reconstruction line (Instant3D, One-2-3-45) but replaces large learned reconstruction networks with a straightforward Gaussian Splatting fit using image-level losses (LPIPS, MS-SSIM). The iterative refinement loop—rendering the 3D object, re-noising, and re-denoising through the video model 2–3 times—occupies a novel middle ground between one-shot reconstruction and the tens-of-thousands-of-iterations SDS optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10–100× reduction in 2D generator evaluations (80 vs. 20,000–320,000) and the 3-minute total runtime make high-quality text-to-3D generation practically accessible, sidestepping well-known SDS pathologies (Janus problem, color saturation, mode collapse). The key open question is whether the approach generalizes beyond the 100k synthetic-asset training set to truly open-world prompts, and whether the video model's residual inconsistencies (e.g., spurious animation for dynamic subjects) can be further reduced. More broadly, the result suggests that the design space for text-to-3D should shift investment toward improving 2D/3D-aware generation quality rather than developing ever-more-robust distillation or reconstruction networks.
