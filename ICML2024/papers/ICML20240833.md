## Problem

Existing inference-time watermarking methods such as KGW apply a uniform splitting ratio and watermark logit to every token, creating an inherent tension where stronger watermarks improve detectability but degrade semantic coherence, and vice versa. This paper addresses the gap by making both parameters token-specific and jointly optimizing them so that detectability and semantic quality are improved simultaneously rather than traded off.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240833/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the LLM's raw logits and the embedding of the preceding token, which feeds two lightweight MLPs (γ-generator and δ-generator) to produce a token-specific splitting ratio γ_t ∈ (0,1) and watermark logit δ_t ∈ ℝ⁺. The preceding token's hash seeds a Bernoulli(γ_t) distribution over the vocabulary to partition tokens into green and red lists, with Gumbel-Softmax providing a differentiable surrogate for the non-differentiable Bernoulli sampling. Detection requires only the LLM's embedding matrix (to recover γ_t per token) and the shared hash function, using a modified one-sided z-score that accounts for the varying γ_t values via a Poisson binomial approximation.

## Method

Two lightweight two-layer MLPs (hidden dim 64, LeakyReLU) are trained to output context-dependent watermarking parameters: the γ-generator produces a splitting ratio used with Gumbel-Softmax to differentiably assign each vocabulary token to the green or red list, and the δ-generator produces a watermark logit that additively biases green-list token logits before softmax sampling. A differentiable surrogate for the z-score replaces the non-differentiable green-token count |s|_G with the sum of per-step green-token selection probabilities under the modified logits, enabling gradient-based optimization of detectability; semantic coherence is captured by the negative cosine similarity of SimCSE (RoBERTa-base) sentence embeddings between watermarked and non-watermarked generations. The Multiple-Gradient Descent Algorithm (MGDA) computes a convex combination of the two loss gradients at each training step, dynamically balancing the competing objectives to converge toward Pareto optimal solutions without a manually tuned trade-off weight. The learned parameters exhibit interpretable behavior: weaker watermarks (lower γ and δ) are assigned after adjectives and determiners where the next token is strongly constrained, while stronger watermarks are applied after punctuation where the next token is less constrained.

## Results

On OPT-1.3B with Top-k=50 sampling, the proposed method achieves 100% TPR at both 0% and 1% FPR with a SimCSE semantic similarity of 0.713, outperforming EXP-edit (TPR@0%=0.922, SimCSE=0.655) and EXP-edit with Top-k=50 (TPR@0%=0.968, SimCSE=0.677). The method improves the Pareto frontier over KGW, SWEET, MultiBit, and SIR across all tested LLMs (OPT-1.3B, LLAMA2-7B/13B/70B). Generation and detection speeds (3.95s and 0.17s for 200 tokens) are comparable to KGW (3.83s, 0.07s) and far faster than EXP-edit (24.7s, 155.0s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KGW (Kirchenbauer et al., 2023a), the proposed method achieves a strictly superior Pareto frontier in TPR-vs-SimCSE at both 0% and 1% FPR, attributed to token-specific γ and δ values and multi-objective optimization; KGW uses uniform values and cannot jointly optimize both objectives. Against SWEET (Lee et al., 2023), the method is notably better at 0% FPR where SWEET fails to reach 100% TPR (only ~7 of 200 tokens are high-entropy and watermarkable), while performance is similar at 1% FPR; SWEET_NoPrompt further degrades, confirming prompt dependence. Against MultiBit (Wang et al., 2023), the method achieves a superior Pareto frontier, as MultiBit's multi-bit message embedding inherently reduces text quality despite its Balance-Marking strategy. Against SIR (Liu et al., 2023), the method improves the Pareto frontier, and SIR_NoPrompt shows significant performance degradation without prompts. Against EXP-edit (Kuditipudi et al., 2023), the method wins on both TPR (1.000 vs. 0.922/0.968) and SimCSE (0.713 vs. 0.655/0.677), and additionally supports beam search and greedy decoding, which EXP-edit cannot extend to due to its reliance on pseudo-random sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the KGW inference-time watermarking framework (Kirchenbauer et al., 2023a), which uses a fixed splitting ratio and watermark logit to bias token selection toward a green list. The key departure is replacing these uniform hyperparameters with token-specific values generated by two lightweight MLPs (γ-generator and δ-generator), and jointly optimizing them via a multi-objective optimization (MGDA) framework that simultaneously maximizes a differentiable z-score surrogate and SimCSE-based semantic similarity. This contrasts with prior adaptive approaches like SWEET (selective high-entropy watermarking), SIR (CompositionalBERT-based semantic invariance), and Wouters (closed-form logit optimization), which each address the detectability-semantics trade-off through single-objective heuristics or restrictive assumptions rather than explicit Pareto optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, end-to-end trainable solution to the core tension in LLM watermarking: stronger watermarks improve detection but degrade text quality. The generalizability finding—that networks trained on OPT-1.3B transfer to LLAMA2 7B/13B/70B without retraining—suggests the learned token-specific parameters capture language-level regularities rather than model-specific artifacts, which is practically valuable. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-turn dialogue or streaming generation, how it performs under more aggressive adversarial attacks (e.g., token-level substitution at scale), and whether the SimCSE-based semantic loss is sufficient for longer passages where local coherence may mask global semantic drift.
