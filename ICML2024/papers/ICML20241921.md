## Problem

Existing audio-visual LLMs either ignore speech entirely or rely on external ASR systems that discard paralinguistic and speaker information, making them unable to perform speech-dependent video understanding tasks like audiovisual speech recognition or speech-content-based QA. The core difficulty is that speech requires temporally fine-grained modeling (at the syllable/word level) while other video elements operate at coarser scales, and integrating both in a single end-to-end model without modality or frame dominance remains unsolved.

## Input / Output

The model consumes video frames sampled at 2 Hz (one frame every 0.5 s), a continuous audio stream processed at 50 Hz spectrogram frame rate (split into speech via Whisper and non-speech via BEATs), and a natural-language instruction prompt. Audio and visual features are temporally synchronized at each video frame with zero-padding for missing modalities, and the MRC Q-Former handles variable-length inputs via sliding windows so that both short clips and longer videos are processed without fixed-length constraints. The output is a free-form text response (multiple-choice QA answer, open-ended caption, or speech transcription) generated autoregressively by the LLM backbone.

## Method

The central architectural contribution is the Multi-Resolution Causal (MRC) Q-Former, which divides the synchronized audio-visual feature stream into fixed-length sliding windows at two temporal resolutions (0.5 s and 5 s), applies resolution-specific learnable query vectors via cross-attention at each level, and enforces a block-wise triangular causal mask so that each frame's encoding incorporates all prior frames in an autoregressive manner. A diversity loss penalizes cosine similarity among output query vectors within each window to prevent the model from repeatedly encoding the same salient frame, and an unpaired audio-visual mixed training scheme augments the training set with independently sampled audio and video pairs to force balanced cross-modal attention. The full system uses frozen Whisper (speech), BEATs (non-speech audio), and InstructBLIP (visual) encoders feeding into the MRC Q-Former, whose outputs are projected and concatenated with the text prompt before a Vicuna-13B LLM fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 32, ~0.4% of parameters) generates the response.

## Results

On the SAVE benchmark, video-SALMONN 13B achieves 2.6% WER on ASR (LibriSpeech test-clean), 49.6% accuracy on Video QA (NExT-QA), and 89.6 CIDEr on image captioning (Flickr30k). On audio-visual tasks, it achieves 7.7% WER on AVSR (How2), 49.8% and 70.5% accuracy on AVQA (Ego4D-QA and Presentation-QA respectively), 47.6% on AVSSD (VGGSS), and 79.7% on AVM (SpokenCOCO+VGGSS). The headline improvements are over 25% absolute accuracy on Video QA versus the fine-tuned InstructBLIP baseline and over 30% absolute accuracy on AVQA versus Video-LLaMA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Whisper large-v2 (audio-only), video-SALMONN 13B achieves a lower ASR WER of 2.6% vs. 2.9% and a 7.2% relative WER reduction on AVSR (7.7% vs. 8.3%), confirming that visual input improves speech recognition. Against InstructBLIP 13B (visual-only, fine-tuned on the same data), video-SALMONN 13B gains 24.9 percentage points on Video QA (49.6% vs. 24.7%), 5.1 CIDEr on image captioning (89.6 vs. 84.5), and 1.3 points on OCR (37.8% vs. 36.5%), while matching on VQA (44.8% vs. 45.6%). Against Video-LLaMA 7B (the closest multimodal baseline), video-SALMONN 13B achieves over 30 percentage-point gains on both AVQA subsets (49.8% vs. 18.2% on Ego4D-QA; 70.5% vs. 21.3% on Presentation-QA) and 27.4 points on AVM (79.7% vs. 52.3%), while slightly trailing on AVSSD (47.6% vs. 41.9% is actually a win of 5.7 points). On the MUSIC-AVQA zero-shot benchmark, video-SALMONN outperforms AV-LLM (Shu et al., 2023) by 7.4 points (52.6% vs. 45.2%) and Video-LLaMA by 16 points (52.6% vs. 36.6%). Overall, video-SALMONN wins or ties on every task where a direct comparison is possible, with the largest margins on speech-dependent audio-visual tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SALMONN (Tang et al., 2023), an audio-only LLM with generic hearing abilities, by incorporating a visual encoder and the Q-Former alignment mechanism from BLIP-2 (Li et al., 2023a) to handle full video inputs. It departs from prior av-LLMs (Video-LLaMA, Macaw-LLM, X-LLM) that either ignore speech entirely or rely on external ASR systems to feed transcriptions as text, by treating speech as a first-class modality with native paralinguistic and speaker-attribute understanding. The key architectural departure is the MRC Q-Former, which operates at multiple temporal resolutions to accommodate speech's fine-grained temporal structure alongside coarser visual and audio-event information, combined with a causal self-attention module to enforce temporal ordering across independently extracted frames.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

video-SALMONN addresses a genuine gap in multimodal LLMs: no prior single model could natively understand speech (including tone, speaker identity, and paralinguistic cues) alongside non-speech audio and visual content in an end-to-end fashion. The SAVE benchmark provides a useful unified evaluation framework, and the MRC Q-Former's multi-resolution design offers a generalizable principle for handling modalities with different temporal granularity. Limitations include reliance on frozen pre-trained encoders, spatial resolution constraints on static images (partially addressed by the spotlight extension), and qualitative-only evidence for emergent co-reasoning abilities. Open questions include scalability to longer videos, whether the multi-resolution trade-off generalizes beyond the 0.5s/5s configuration, and whether the mixed training strategy remains effective as paired audio-visual data scales up.
