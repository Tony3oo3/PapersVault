## Problem

Deploying large-scale models in federated multimodal learning (MFL) imposes prohibitive storage and computation costs on resource-constrained clients, while full model deployment raises intellectual property concerns. Existing efficient fine-tuning strategies in FL still incur unavoidable storage overhead and have not been adapted to the cross-client modality heterogeneity inherent in multimodal settings, leaving a significant gap in practically applying large-scale models within MFL.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242466/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client holds M unimodal datasets (video clips of 16 frames at 224×224, audio as MFCC features, images at 224×224, or tokenized text) with a shared label. During training, all modalities of a client must be co-present for multimodal fusion on the server, but at inference each device independently classifies its own unimodal input. The U-shaped split ensures raw data and labels never leave the client; only intermediate 'smashed' features are transmitted to the main server.

## Method

M²FedSA combines U-shaped Split Learning with a Dual Adaptive Fine-tuning Strategy (DAFS) and a Dual Knowledge Transfer Strategy (DKTS). The model is split into four modules: embedding and low-level encoder on the client, high-level encoder on the main server, and classifier on the client, with the large-scale backbone frozen. Two lightweight bottleneck adapters are inserted: a Task Adapter in residual connection within the server-side high-level encoder (capturing task-discriminative semantics) and a Modality Adapter in serial connection within the client-side low-level encoder (amplifying modality-specific features). DKTS transfers multimodal knowledge to unimodal clients at both the feature level (cosine-similarity alignment) and decision level (normalized KL divergence between unimodal and multimodal logits). Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 decouples unimodal and multimodal optimization until the fusion module stabilizes (detected via coefficient of variation of loss differences), then Stage 2 freezes the fusion module and jointly optimizes with knowledge-transfer losses, using intra-modality aggregation for MAs/classifiers and inter-modality aggregation for TAs.

## Results

M²FedSA achieves the best average performance across all four multimodal benchmarks: 40.51% ACC on Epic-Kitchens-100, 43.64% ACC on MiT, 63.74% AUC on Hateful-Memes, and 54.33% UAR on MELD. These represent improvements of 0.84%, 1.66%, 1.39%, and 1.24% over the second-best method (CreamFL) on each dataset, respectively. Crucially, M²FedSA achieves these gains with dramatically fewer trainable parameters per client (0.12M on Epic-Kitchens/MiT, 62.49K on Hateful-Memes, 69.79K on MELD) compared to CreamFL (26.04M, 60.85M, 63.16M) and FDARN (6.11M, 6.43M, 6.09M), reducing storage by over 91% versus FedMSplit on Epic-Kitchens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M²FedSA outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset. Against the model-homogeneity group (FedAvg, FedProx, SCAFFOLD, FedRS, FedOpt), the gains are substantial: e.g., on Epic-Kitchens, M²FedSA (40.51%) beats the best homogeneous method FedOpt (35.63%) by nearly 5 points, and on Hateful-Memes it surpasses FedOpt (58.61%) by 5.13 AUC points. Against the model-heterogeneity group, M²FedSA edges out the strongest competitor CreamFL by 0.84% ACC (Epic-Kitchens), 1.66% ACC (MiT), 1.39% AUC (Hateful-Memes), and 1.24% UAR (MELD), while using 99.5% fewer trainable parameters on Hateful-Memes (62.49K vs. 60.85M) and 99.5% fewer on Epic-Kitchens/MiT (0.12M vs. 26.04M). Compared to FedMSplit, M²FedSA improves Epic-Kitchens accuracy by 4.48% while reducing per-client trainable parameters by 91.18% (0.12M vs. 1.36M). FDARN is consistently outperformed as well, with M²FedSA gaining 3.84% ACC on Epic-Kitchens and 2.31% ACC on MiT over FDARN, again with far fewer parameters (0.12M vs. 6.11M).

## Relation to Prior Work

M²FedSA unifies three previously separate research threads: U-shaped Split Learning (Vepakomma et al., 2018; Thapa et al., 2022) for privacy-preserving model partitioning, bottleneck adapter-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning (Houlsby et al., 2019) for lightweight adaptation of frozen large-scale models, and adaptive knowledge distillation (Wu et al., 2022) for cross-model knowledge transfer. Prior Split Federated Learning work was restricted to unimodal settings, while prior Multimodal Federated Learning methods either deployed full large-scale models on clients (incurring prohibitive storage costs) or avoided large-scale models altogether. The paper's core departure is the first systematic integration of all three components into a single multimodal FL framework, with a novel dual-adapter design (task adapter in residual connection on the server, modality adapter in serial connection on the client) and a two-stage training protocol that decouples multimodal fusion learning from unimodal adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying large-scale models under federated constraints: it reduces client-side trainable parameters by over 98% relative to full fine-tuning while matching or exceeding its accuracy, and cuts local storage by 88%+ compared to the next most efficient baseline. This makes federated multimodal learning with modern backbones (Swin-Transformer, RoBERTa, Whisper) feasible on resource-constrained edge devices. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is the requirement for complete multimodal data during the training phase, which constrains applicability in settings where modalities are intermittently available. Open questions include whether the two-stage protocol generalizes to more than three modalities, how the method behaves under asynchronous or partially-participating client regimes, and whether the knowledge transfer gains persist when scaling to foundation-model-sized backbones.
