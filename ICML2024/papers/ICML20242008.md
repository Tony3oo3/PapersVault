## Problem

Exact solutions to neural network learning dynamics exist only for simple architectures and do not transfer across model families, making it difficult to build a unified theory of representation learning. The paper asks whether a universal, architecture-agnostic effective theory can capture the common representational learning behaviors shared by diverse deep networks, particularly in the expressive regime where parametrization constraints are weak.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242008/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The theory takes as input two datapoints (input vectors x₁, x₂ and target outputs y₁, y₂), two effective learning rates (1/τ_h for the encoder and 1/τ_y for the decoder), and an initial weight scale that determines the starting representational distance. It produces a closed-form 3-dimensional ODE system governing the representational distance ||dh||², the prediction difference ||dy||², and an output alignment variable w, from which one can read off the training loss curve and a closed-form expression for the final representational distance (Equation 10) that depends on input/output geometry and the initial weight regime.

## Method

The paper splits an arbitrary deep network into a smooth encoder map h_φ and decoder map y_ψ, then models the interaction between two nearby datapoints by linearizing both maps around their representational midpoint. Under the assumption of high expressivity (large, complex architecture), gradient descent is applied directly to the linearization parameters (Jacobian matrices D_h, D_y and output mean ȳ), yielding an effective 3-dimensional ODE system for ||dh||², ||dy||², and w that is self-contained and architecture-independent. The only architecture-specific quantities are two effective learning rates, which are fit empirically. Fixed-point analysis of the reduced 2D system reveals a single stable attractor whose value transitions between a random-initialization-dominated regime (large weights, 'lazy') and a data-structure-dominated regime (small weights, 'rich'), closely mirroring the known rich/lazy learning dichotomy. The theory is validated across architectures varying in depth, width, nonlinearity (leaky ReLU, ReLU, ELU, tanh, Swish, linear), connectivity (skip connections, convolution), and regularization (dropout), as well as on XOR, Gaussian blob feature-collapse, and MNIST tasks.

## Results

The effective theory, requiring only two fitted effective learning rates (1/τ_h and 1/τ_y), matches the representational dynamics of 15+ architecture variants (varying depth, width, nonlinearity, connectivity) on a two-point dataset. On MNIST, the predicted pairwise representational distances achieve a Pearson correlation of 0.703 with the trained network (0.512 excluding diagonal blocks). The theory's specific functional form is validated: four altered versions of the ODE system (adding a square, a factor of 2, removing a sign, removing a term) increase the squared fit loss from 0.92 to 2.99–16.61, confirming the dynamics are not merely a two-parameter fit to arbitrary curves.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing methods in the traditional sense; instead it validates the effective theory against (a) modified versions of its own ODE system and (b) different network architectures. Against four alternative dynamics (Equation 51), the effective theory achieves a squared fit loss of 0.92 versus 15.35 (squared w), 16.61 (factor of 2), 2.99 (sign flip), and 5.64 (removed term), confirming the specific functional form matters. Across architectures (20-layer leaky ReLU, convolutional, skip connections, narrow, dropout, linear, ReLU, ELU, tanh, Swish), the theory matches all trajectories with only two fitted constants. The theory correctly predicts feature collapse along task-irrelevant directions at small initial weights, matching observations from Flesch et al. (2022) on a 2-layer ReLU network, and extends this prediction to a 5-layer tanh architecture. However, the theory fails for a 2-layer network on the XOR task (predicting full merging of same-output pairs when only one pair merges), highlighting that the high-expressivity assumption is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the local elasticity phenomenon (He & Su, 2020; Zhang et al., 2021) to justify reducing full-dataset learning dynamics to pairwise interactions, and draws on mean-field and physics-inspired approaches to interacting particles. It departs from the neural tangent kernel literature, which provides universal solutions but only in the lazy (high-weight) regime where the dataset does not shape representations, by targeting the feature-learning regime where data structure controls the representation. Unlike exact-solution approaches for specific architectures (Saxe et al., 2014; Saad & Solla, 1995), which require redoing the analysis for each architecture, the paper's effective theory abstracts architecture into two effective learning rates, leveraging only the smoothness of the encoder and decoder maps as guaranteed by universal approximation theorems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a conceptual explanation for the rich-versus-lazy learning distinction rooted in smoothness constraints rather than architecture-specific inductive biases, suggesting that gradient descent on sufficiently expressive networks naturally produces structured representations at small initial weights. A significant limitation is the two-point interaction framework, which has no clear path to extension to larger datasets, and the theory does not account for inductive biases (convolutional structure, attention, etc.) that interact with the modeled dynamics. Open questions include how the pairwise predictions compose into full-dataset geometry, whether the framework extends to modern optimizers like Adam (partially addressed), and how architecture-specific biases modify the universal predictions.
