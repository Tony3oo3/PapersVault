## Problem

Multi-objective alignment of foundation models is critical for accommodating diverse, often conflicting human preferences (e.g., helpfulness vs. harmlessness), but existing MORLHF approaches require training M separate models via expensive and unstable RL, one per preference combination. The paper asks whether a single model trained exclusively with supervised fine-tuning can achieve Pareto-optimal multi-objective alignment while supporting dynamic, inference-time preference adjustment at negligible additional cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242322/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For text generation, the model consumes a prompt augmented with normalized reward values (one decimal place) embedded as text markers (e.g., <R1>0.5<R2>0.8) and produces a text response; for text-to-image, it takes a caption with reward conditions and outputs 512×512 images. At inference, user preference weights w are converted to desired reward values via a closed-form preference-to-reward mapping before being inserted into the prompt, enabling continuous adjustment without retraining. Rewards are normalized to zero mean and unit standard deviation over the training set, and extreme values are clipped using a ±4σ rule from a sampled normal distribution.

## Method

RiC operates in three stages: (1) offline multi-reward conditional SFT, where prompts are relabeled with scores from N reward models and the base model is fine-tuned with standard cross-entropy loss; (2) online training, where the model generates responses targeting rewards near the Pareto front and multi-objective rejection sampling (MORS) filters out dominated samples to augment the dataset for a second SFT pass; (3) inference-time adaptation, where user preferences are mapped to desired rewards via a closed-form solution of a convex optimization problem with ℓp-norm regularization (Theorem 3.1). For the practical p=∞ case, rewards with preference ≥ 1/N are set to their maximum while lower-preference rewards scale linearly, yielding a simple piecewise mapping. The entire pipeline requires only SFT on a single model, avoiding RL entirely, and achieves a superior empirical Pareto front at roughly 10% of the GPU hours of MORLHF.

## Results

On the Helpful Assistant task (HH-RLHF, 160k prompts) and Reddit Summary task (14.9k posts), RiC achieves a superior empirical Pareto front over MORLHF and Rewarded Soups across all preference settings, as measured by average multi-dimensional test rewards. In terms of computational cost, RiC with two online iterations requires only 153.2 GPU hours, compared to 1,477.1 for MORLHF (10.4%) and 622.7 for Rewarded Soups (24.6%). For text-to-image generation with Stable Diffusion v1.5 on LAION-5B, RiC effectively balances aesthetic and compressible rewards at inference time, producing images that adapt to user-specified preference weights without retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MORLHF, RiC achieves a strictly superior empirical Pareto front on both the Helpful Assistant and Reddit Summary tasks while requiring only ~10% of the GPU hours (153.2 vs. 1,477.1); MORLHF also lacks inference-time adaptability, requiring a separate RL run per preference. Against Rewarded Soups, RiC produces a better Pareto front in all tested settings (two- and three-objective), and the paper notes that linear interpolation of LLM weights in Rewarded Soups can actually decrease performance relative to MORLHF. Against MODPO (Appendix C.3), RiC outperforms on both the Helpful Assistant and Reddit Summary tasks; notably, MODPO fails to align the two objectives in the Reddit Summary task, indicating a limitation in its versatility. RiC also retains base-model capabilities (e.g., 'faithful' reward in Reddit Summary) that SFT-based baselines lose due to catastrophic forgetting, giving it an additional advantage in multi-objective settings where one objective is already well-served by the pretrained model.

## Relation to Prior Work

RiC extends the reward-conditional supervised fine-tuning paradigm (Hu et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2021) from single-reward to multi-reward alignment, replacing the RL-based PPO training used in MORLHF and the weight-interpolation strategy of Rewarded Soups with a single SFT-trained model. The key conceptual departure is the theoretically grounded preference-to-reward mapping derived from a convex optimization formulation, which enables dynamic inference-time adaptation without retraining. Unlike MODPO, which requires structured preference data and a modified DPO loss, RiC operates with standard SFT loss and off-the-shelf reward models, positioning itself as a simpler and more scalable alternative within the multi-objective alignment literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~10× reduction in GPU hours relative to MORLHF, combined with the ability to adapt to arbitrary preference vectors at inference time from a single trained model, makes multi-objective alignment substantially more accessible for practitioners who cannot afford to train M separate RL policies. However, the method's failure on positively correlated rewards (Appendix C.5) and the acknowledged safety risk of prompt manipulation for reward injection remain open challenges. Future work on context-dependent inference-time adaptations and handling correlated reward dimensions would strengthen the approach's generality.
