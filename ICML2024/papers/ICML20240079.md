## Problem

GNNs suffer from over-squashing (information bottleneck at low-curvature edges) and over-smoothing (feature homogenization at high-curvature edges), and existing rewiring methods that mitigate these issues require the original graph structure, scale poorly with node count, and depend on dataset-specific hyperparameters. Crucially, many real-world applications (document classification, physics simulation, NLP entity representation) provide only node features without an accessible graph topology, rendering all structural rewiring methods inapplicable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only node feature vectors (no graph structure required) and produces a Delaunay triangulation graph whose edges define the message-passing topology for a downstream GNN. Features are first projected to 2D via UMAP to avoid high-dimensional distance degradation, then triangulated; the resulting graph has a mean degree of approximately 6 and a maximum clique size of 3. The final output is node class labels obtained by running a standard GNN (GCN or GAT) on the Delaunay graph with the original node features.

## Method

Delaunay Rewiring (DR) discards the original graph entirely and reconstructs connectivity from node features via 2D Delaunay triangulation, which guarantees that no point lies inside the circumcircle of any triangle. The key insight is that this construction simultaneously eliminates strongly negatively curved edges (bottlenecks causing over-squashing) and caps the maximum clique at size 3 (preventing over-smoothing from large cliques), while sparsifying the graph to a mean degree of ~6. For datasets where raw features lack expressiveness, the method first passes features through a GCN to obtain learned embeddings before UMAP reduction and triangulation. The approach runs in O(N log N) time with zero tunable hyperparameters, and the Delaunay graph is used as the fixed topology for a standard 2-layer GNN trained with supervised node classification.

## Results

Using GCN as backbone, Delaunay Rewiring (DR) achieves the best accuracy on all 10 datasets, with an average improvement of 21.8% over the base GCN. On heterophilic datasets, DR leads by large margins: 74.28% on Chameleon (vs. 68.03% for GTR), 65.25% on Squirrel (vs. 53.32% for GTR), and 67.22% on Cornell (vs. 47.57% for GTR). On homophilic datasets, DR also wins but with smaller gaps: 91.39% on Cora, 81.14% on Citeseer, and 88.69% on Pubmed. The method's complexity is O(N log N) with no hyperparameters, compared to O(N²) or higher for most structural rewiring baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DR outperforms all six structural rewiring baselines on every dataset. FA (Alon & Yahav, 2021) is consistently the weakest method, often scoring below the base GCN (e.g., 26.34% on Chameleon vs. 65.35% base), and fails with OOM on Roman-Empire and Pubmed. DIGL (Klicpera et al., 2019) is competitive on homophilic graphs (88.31% on Cora) but degrades sharply on heterophilic ones (40.53% on Squirrel). SDRF (Topping et al., 2022) and FOSR (Karhadkar et al., 2023) show moderate gains on heterophilic datasets but remain 5–20 points behind DR (e.g., SDRF 58.49% vs. DR 70.98% on Wisconsin). BORF (Nguyen et al., 2023) is the strongest structural baseline on Roman-Empire (58.58%) but still trails DR by 3.4 points, and it times out (>24h) on Squirrel. GTR (Black et al., 2023) is the closest competitor on Chameleon (68.03% vs. 74.28%) and Pubmed (88.44% vs. 88.69%), yet DR maintains a clear lead across the board, with the largest gaps on the most heterophilic graphs (Cornell: +19.65 pp, Texas: +9.11 pp, Wisconsin: +12.49 pp).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of structural graph rewiring (SDRF, FOSR, BORF, GTR, DIGL), which all require and modify the input graph, by instead reconstructing the graph entirely from node features via Delaunay triangulation in a 2D UMAP embedding. It leverages the curvature-based understanding of over-squashing (Topping et al., 2022) and over-smoothing (Nguyen et al., 2023) to motivate why the resulting triangulated graph's structural properties—bounded clique size, absence of strongly negative/positive curvature edges, and reduced resistance—should mitigate both phenomena. The approach also positions itself as a principled alternative to k-NN graph construction for non-graph data, offering O(N log N) complexity versus O(N²) and eliminating the k hyperparameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method's ability to construct a useful graph from features alone (without any input graph structure) opens a practical pathway for graph learning in domains where only tabular or feature data is available, such as document classification or entity representation. However, the method's dependence on feature quality is a significant limitation—triangulation on raw features can underperform the original graph (Table 3), and the learned-feature variant requires a preliminary GNN pass. Open questions include applicability to long-range graph benchmarks, scalability beyond node classification, and whether the 2D UMAP projection is always sufficient or whether adaptive dimensionality selection would help.
