## Problem

Cardinality sketches such as HyperLogLog provide strong error guarantees for non-adaptive queries, but their behavior under adaptive inputs—where queries depend on prior responses—was previously uncharacterized. This paper asks: how efficiently can an adversary construct an input set that causes the sketch estimator to produce grossly incorrect cardinality estimates, and is this vulnerability inherent to the sketch structure itself?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240023/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attacker fixes a ground set N of n keys (with n = Ω(k log(kr)/α)) and the fixed sketch randomness ρ, then issues r queries that are random subsets of N (each key included independently with probability 1/2 or a sampled rate q). For standard estimators, the attacker receives the sufficient statistic T(S_ρ(U)) per query and outputs an adversarial prefix set U_α of size αn whose sketch is biased up by a factor Ω(1/α). For general estimators, the attacker receives Bernoulli responses Z to a soft threshold problem and outputs a small mask set M of size Õ(k) such that S_ρ(M ∪ U) ≈ S_ρ(M) for any large U, rendering all estimators ineffective.

## Method

The core attack (Algorithm 1) is a single-batch procedure: issue r = O(k/α²) random subsets of a fixed ground set, score each key by the average sufficient statistic T(S_ρ(U)) over queries containing it, and take the lowest-scoring prefix as the adversarial set. The key insight is that scores are correlated with key priorities—low-priority keys systematically receive lower scores because their presence reduces the sketch statistic—so the sorted order separates low-rank keys from the bulk with high probability via Chebyshev's inequality. For general query response algorithms, the attack (Algorithms 3–4) targets a simplified soft threshold problem and uses Õ(k²) queries; it works even when the QR algorithm knows the full attack state and query distribution. The analysis uses a rank-domain representation of sketches that factors out hash values, showing the score gap between low-rank and transparent keys is Ω(1/k) per step, which accumulates to a separation of Õ(r/k) after r steps. The quadratic query complexity for general estimators matches the known upper bound from robustness wrappers, establishing inherent vulnerability of the sketch structures.

## Results

The single-batch attack on standard estimators (Algorithm 1) uses only O(k) queries and empirically achieves 40% overestimation or underestimation of cardinality with just 4k queries against HLL++ (the most widely deployed cardinality sketch). For k=104 (ε=0.1) and k=416 (ε=0.05) with a ground set of 5000 keys, the attack consistently produces adversarial inputs where the estimator's error far exceeds the guaranteed ε. The general attack on arbitrary query response algorithms (Algorithm 4) requires Õ(k²) adaptive queries, matching the generic quadratic upper bound from Hassidim et al. (2020), and produces a small mask set M (|M| < αn) that poisons larger sets U such that S_ρ(M∪U) ≈ S_ρ(M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not present a traditional baseline comparison table, as it introduces attacks rather than a new estimator. The key comparisons are against theoretical bounds and prior work: (1) The linear O(k) attack on standard estimators matches the straightforward upper bound of using disjoint sketch components, and is more efficient than prior streaming-based attacks by Reviriego & Ting (2020) and Paterson & Raynal (2021), which required many dependent queries. (2) The quadratic Õ(k²) attack on general estimators matches the generic upper bound construction of Hassidim et al. (2020), establishing that the vulnerability is inherent to the sketch structure itself. (3) Unlike prior attacks that assumed a streaming setting with sequential insertions, this work demonstrates that a single batch of non-adaptive queries (with only post-processing of responses) suffices to construct adversarial inputs, suggesting the vulnerability is exploitable even by observing a normal workload.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adaptive data analysis and robust streaming literature (Hardt & Ullman 2014; Hassidim et al. 2020) to the specific and widely-deployed class of composable cardinality sketches (MinHash, bottom-k, k-partition, domain sampling). Prior attacks on HLL (Reviriego & Ting 2020; Paterson & Raynal 2021) were restricted to streaming settings with many dependent queries, whereas this paper constructs single-batch attacks against standard estimators and multi-batch attacks against arbitrary estimators. The attack structure also draws on the rank-domain representation and sufficient statistics framework from the sketching literature, and the quadratic upper bound for general estimators matches the robustness wrapper of Hassidim et al. (2020), establishing tightness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the vulnerability is inherent to the sketch structures themselves—rather than a fixable flaw in specific estimators—has broad implications for any system relying on cardinality sketches (BigQuery, Apache DataSketches, distributed analytics). The paper leaves open whether the vulnerability extends to all composable sketch structures and suggests that restricting per-key query participation to sublinear/subquadratic bounds could restore robustness. For practitioners, the practical takeaway is that even non-malicious adaptive workloads (optimization loops, control systems) can inadvertently select biased key sets, and the 40% estimation error achievable with only 4k queries is well within the range of normal system operation.
