## Problem

In long-tail visual recognition, models suffer from two intertwined failures: inter-class bias (confusion between visually similar classes) and intra-class variance (loose, spread-out feature clusters). Existing contrastive and metric learning objectives like SupCon address intra-class variance but fail to impose a significant penalty on inter-class bias, and none provide a principled mechanism to jointly handle both while naturally accommodating class imbalance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241397/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images at dataset-specific resolutions (32×32 for CIFAR, 28×28 for MedMNIST, 1920×1080 for IDD) and produces class labels for classification or bounding boxes with class labels for detection. Unusually, the input batch is reformulated as a ground set partitioned into per-class subsets, and the loss operates on pairwise cosine similarities aggregated through set-based submodular functions rather than on individual sample pairs, meaning the objective's value depends on the combinatorial structure of the entire class set rather than on any single anchor-positive-negative triple.

## Method

SCoRe introduces a family of combinatorial loss functions built on submodular set functions (Facility Location, Graph-Cut, Log-Determinant) applied to per-class sets of feature embeddings. Two formulations are defined: Total Information (S_f), which minimizes intra-cluster volume to enforce compactness, and Total Correlation (C_f), which additionally maximizes diversity across the full ground set to enforce inter-cluster separation. The Facility Location variant (SCoRe-FL) inherently balances classes because its summation scales with the complement set rather than the class size, giving tail classes proportionally larger gradients. The framework generalizes SupCon, N-pairs, and OPL as special instances, and reformulates Triplet and SNN losses into submodular variants that outperform their originals. Training follows a two-stage protocol: the CNN backbone is trained with the combinatorial objective in stage 1, then a linear classifier is trained with cross-entropy on frozen features in stage 2.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-LT (IF=100), GLMC+SCoRe-FL achieves 92.33% Top-1 accuracy, outperforming GLMC (88.50%) by 3.83% and PaCo (85.11%) by 7.22%. On CIFAR-100-LT (IF=100), the same combination reaches 61.33%, a 3.33% gain over GLMC and 9.33% over PaCo. On ImageNet-LT, GLMC+SCoRe-FL achieves 59.3% overall Top-1 accuracy (vs. 56.1% for GLMC and 55.6% for PaCo). For object detection, Faster-RCNN+SCoRe-FL achieves 36.3 mAP on IDD (+5.9 over Faster-RCNN+FPN) and 19.1 mAP on LVIS (+4.9 over Faster-RCNN+FPN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCoRe objectives consistently outperform all 27 baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest SoTA long-tail classifiers, GLMC+SCoRe-FL beats GLMC by 3.83% on CIFAR-10-LT (IF=100) and 3.33% on CIFAR-100-LT (IF=100), while PaCo+SCoRe-FL surpasses PaCo by 0.5–1.0% on CIFAR-10-LT and 1.7–0.9% on CIFAR-100-LT. On ImageNet-LT, GLMC+SCoRe-FL achieves 59.3% overall accuracy, exceeding GLMC (56.1%) by 3.2% and PaCo (55.6%) by 3.7%, with the largest gains on the 'Few' (tail) category (+6.2% over GLMC). In object detection, SCoRe-FL improves mAP by 5.9 points over Faster-RCNN+FPN on IDD (36.3 vs. 30.4) and 4.9 points on LVIS (19.1 vs. 14.2), while also surpassing the SupCon-augmented detector by 5.1 and 4.7 mAP points respectively. Among metric/contrastive learners, SCoRe-FL outperforms SupCon by 1.84% on CIFAR-10-LT (IF=10) and 1.65% on the pathological step distribution, and by 1.5% on DermaMNIST; the submodular reformulations (Submod-Triplet, Submod-SNN, SupCon-Var) each beat their non-submodular originals by 0.3–4.1%. Notably, SCoRe-FL's inherent class-balancing property yields the largest relative gains under severe imbalance (IF=100) and on tail classes, while the advantage narrows in more balanced settings (IF=10).

## Relation to Prior Work

SCoRe builds on the generalized submodular information measures framework of Iyer et al. (2022) to introduce a set-based combinatorial viewpoint to representation learning, departing from the pairwise similarity paradigm that underlies most contrastive and metric learning losses. The paper positions itself as a unifying framework, showing that SupCon, N-pairs, and OPL are special instances of its S_f formulation, while Triplet and SNN losses are non-submodular and require algebraic modification to fit. Practically, SCoRe objectives are plugged into existing long-tail architectures (PaCo, GLMC) as auxiliary supervision heads, inheriting their training pipelines while replacing or augmenting their contrastive objectives with submodular combinatorial ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combinatorial framing offers a principled explanation for why certain contrastive losses work and reveals a structural class-balancing property in SCoRe-FL (inverse scaling with set size) that is absent in standard contrastive objectives, making it particularly relevant for long-tail and class-imbalanced settings. The 19.4% relative improvement in object detection on LVIS and the unification of multiple losses under one framework have practical value for practitioners. However, the ImageNet-LT results reveal a trade-off: SCoRe-GC degrades sharply on few-shot classes (16.7% vs. 34.9% for PaCo), suggesting the objectives can overfit to tail classes at the expense of rare ones. Open questions include whether the combinatorial viewpoint extends to self-supervised or multimodal settings, and whether the class-balancing property holds at extreme imbalance ratios beyond IF=100.
