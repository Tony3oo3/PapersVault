## Problem

Audio-visual learning has been split into two disconnected branches: representation learning (contrastive or masked autoencoder approaches on raw signals) and generative modeling (autoregressive or diffusion-based video-to-audio synthesis). No unified framework existed that could simultaneously learn semantic audio-visual representations and generate high-quality audio from silent video, largely because raw video frames and audio spectrograms are too high-dimensional for joint modeling, and generative models typically require multi-stage pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241904/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

VAB consumes pre-extracted latent representations rather than raw signals: 10 frame-level eva-CLIP embeddings (768-dim, 1 fps) for a 10-second video and 500 discrete audio tokens (from DAC's 12 codebooks or Encodec's 4 codebooks at 50 Hz). For generation, the model outputs audio tokens that are decoded by the corresponding neural codec into a 16 kHz waveform; for understanding tasks, it produces mean-pooled audio and visual embeddings for retrieval or feeds into a linear head for multi-label event classification. The 10:500 ratio of visual to audio tokens is a key structural constraint that motivates the modal-specific expert design.

## Method

VAB is a multi-way transformer encoder (24 layers, 1024-dim) where the first 12 layers use modality-specific feed-forward networks (acting as audio or visual experts) and the remaining 12 layers use shared FFNs for cross-modal fusion, all with shared bidirectional self-attention. Pre-training performs visual-conditioned masked audio token prediction: audio tokens are masked at a variable ratio (truncated Gaussian, mean 0.55) and the model predicts the masked tokens given unmasked audio tokens and visual features, minimizing cross-entropy over all four codebook levels. For zero-shot generation, VAB uses MaskGIT-style iterative parallel decoding with confidence-scored re-masking (cosine schedule), temperature-annealed Gumbel noise, and classifier-free guidance, reducing inference to ~16 steps versus hundreds for autoregressive baselines. When using DAC's 12 codebooks, a separate coarse-to-fine transformer generates the remaining 8 fine-level tokens conditioned on the 4 coarse tokens and visual features. After pre-training, the first 12 expert layers are fine-tuned with contrastive loss for retrieval or with a linear classifier for audio-visual and audio-only classification.

## Results

On VGGSound video-to-audio generation, VAB-Encodec achieves FAD 2.69 and KLD 2.58 (best KLD among all methods) while generating 10-second audio in 0.4s — a 17× speedup over the best autoregressive baseline FoleyGen (6.9s). VAB-Encodec also attains the highest MOS (2.86) in human evaluation. On cross-modal retrieval, VAB-Encodec achieves R@1 of 39.5 (V→A) and 37.5 (A→V) on AudioSet, roughly doubling the best baseline (ImageBind: 22.1/20.8). For audio-visual classification, VAB-Encodec reaches 65.2% accuracy (V+A) on VGGSound and 33.5% mAP (V-only) on AudioSet-2M, outperforming all prior methods on the visual-only setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In video-to-audio generation, VAB-Encodec slightly trails FoleyGen on FAD (2.69 vs. 2.59) but surpasses it on KLD (2.58 vs. 2.89) and inference speed (0.4s vs. 6.9s, a 17× speedup), while also winning the MOS human evaluation (2.86 vs. 2.76). All other baselines (SpecVQGAN, IM2WAV, Diff-Foley) are substantially outperformed on both FAD and KLD. In cross-modal retrieval, VAB-Encodec achieves roughly 2× the R@1 of the strongest baselines (ImageBind 22.1, CAV-MAE 18.8) on AudioSet V→A retrieval, and similarly dominates on VGGSound (33.5 vs. 21.6/14.8). For audio-visual classification, VAB-Encodec is competitive but not always best: it trails MAViL on VGGSound V+A (65.2 vs. 67.1) and AudioSet-2M V+A (47.7 vs. 53.3), yet clearly wins on visual-only classification across all three datasets (e.g., 55.1% vs. 51.2% for MBT on VGGSound). On audio-only classification (ESC-50, SPC-1), VAB-Encodec (91.4/96.1) remains competitive but falls short of the dedicated audio models Aud-MAE (94.1/96.9) and MAViL (94.4/97.4), which the authors attribute to the lossy compression inherent in audio tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

VAB unifies two previously disconnected research threads—audio-visual representation learning (CAV-MAE, MAViL) and video-to-audio generation (FoleyGen, Diff-Foley, IM2WAV)—into a single framework by operating in latent space rather than on raw signals. Architecturally, it draws on the multi-way transformer with modal-specific experts from VLMO (Bao et al., 2022), the confidence-score-based parallel decoding from MaskGIT (Chang et al., 2022), and the two-stage coarse-to-fine token generation from VampNet (Garcia et al., 2023). The key conceptual departure is using visual-conditioned masked audio token prediction as a single pre-training objective that simultaneously enables representation learning and generative modeling, whereas prior works required separate deterministic learners for understanding and autoregressive or diffusion models for generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VAB demonstrates that a single 403M-parameter model can serve as both a competitive audio-visual encoder (retrieval, classification) and a fast video-to-audio generator (17× speedup over FoleyGen), reducing the need for separate specialized pipelines. The finding that the simpler Encodec (4 codebooks) outperforms the more complex DAC (12 codebooks) in generation quality is mildly counterintuitive and suggests that coarse-level token information plays a critical role in cross-modal learning. Open questions include whether higher visual frame rates (beyond 1 fps) and spatial features from CLIP could close the remaining gap in audio-only classification and enable audio-visual localization, and whether the unified approach extends to audio-to-video generation where the paper notes significant challenges with noisy training data.
