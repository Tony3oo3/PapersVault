## Problem

Automated red teaming methods for LLMs have proliferated rapidly, but prior work uses at least 9 distinct, non-overlapping evaluation setups with unstandardized parameters (e.g., token generation count can shift ASR by up to 30%), rendering cross-paper comparisons effectively meaningless. The field also lacks evaluations with sufficient behavioral breadth, robust classifiers resistant to gaming, and a mechanism to enable codevelopment of attacks and defenses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241431/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

HarmBench takes 510 curated harmful behaviors (200 standard, 100 contextual with accompanying context strings, 100 copyright, 110 multimodal with images) and a red teaming method, which generates test cases fed to target LLMs using greedy decoding with a standardized T=512 tokens. Completions are scored by a fine-tuned Llama 2 13B classifier (93.2% human agreement) for non-copyright behaviors or a MinHash-based classifier for copyright, yielding per-behavior ASR. The R2D2 defense takes a base LLM (Mistral 7B) plus a pool of 180 persistent GCG-optimized test cases and produces an adversarially trained model that preserves general capability (MT-Bench 6.0 vs. 6.5 for Mistral 7B Instruct).

## Method

HarmBench standardizes the evaluation pipeline around three design principles: breadth (novel functional categories including contextual and multimodal behaviors with 0% searchability), comparability (fixed T=512 tokens, greedy decoding, open-source classifiers), and robust metrics (prequalification tests for classifier gaming, held-out validation/test split of behaviors and classifiers). The R2D2 adversarial training method draws on fast adversarial training by maintaining a persistent pool of test cases that are continually re-optimized with GCG (m=5 steps per iteration) rather than regenerated from scratch. Each training step combines three losses: an away loss that directly opposes the GCG objective, a toward loss that trains the model to emit a fixed refusal string, and a standard SFT loss on UltraChat to preserve utility. Every L=50 model updates, K=20% of the test case pool is reset to maintain adversarial diversity, and the full 500-step training completes in 16 hours on 8×A100, achieving 4× lower GCG ASR than Llama 2 13B Chat.

## Results

Across 18 red teaming methods and 33 LLMs, no single attack or defense is uniformly effective: every attack achieves low ASR on at least one model, and every model is vulnerable to at least one attack. The proposed R2D2 adversarial training method (Zephyr 7B + R2D2) achieves 5.9% ASR under GCG, a 4× improvement over Llama 2 13B Chat (30.0%) and a 5.5× improvement over Llama 2 7B Chat (32.5%), while preserving general capability (MT-Bench score of 6.0 vs. 6.5 for Mistral 7B Instruct v0.2). Robustness is found to be independent of model size within families (7B–70B), with training procedures and data being far more predictive of jailbreak resistance than parameter count.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R2D2 (Zephyr 7B + R2D2) dramatically outperforms Llama 2 7B Chat (32.5%→5.9%) and Llama 2 13B Chat (30.0%→5.9%) on GCG ASR, and is the strongest defense on all three GCG variants (GCG, GCG-Multi, GCG-Transfer). Compared to its base model Zephyr 7B without R2D2, adversarial training uniformly improves robustness across all 16 attacks, though gains are less pronounced for dissimilar attacks like PAIR, TAP, and Stochastic Few-Shot. Against the broader field, the paper shows that no prior defense (including Llama 2's multi-round manual red teaming, Baichuan 2's RL harmlessness training, or Qwen's safety annotations) is robust to all attacks, and no prior attack (including GCG, PAIR, TAP, AutoDAN, or Human Jailbreaks) breaks all models. The HarmBench classifier (fine-tuned Llama 2 13B) achieves 93.19% agreement with human labels, outperforming GPT-4 (88.37%), Llama-Guard (66.94%), GPTFuzz (75.42%), and AdvBench substring matching (69.93%). On MT-Bench, R2D2 scores 6.0 versus 6.5 for Mistral 7B Instruct v0.2, indicating only a modest utility trade-off for the large robustness gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GCG adversarial attack framework (Zou et al., 2023) as its core adversary and adapts the persistent test case mechanism from fast adversarial training (Shafahi et al., 2019) to the LLM red teaming setting. It departs from prior automated red teaming evaluations by identifying three previously unaddressed desiderata—breadth, comparability, and robust metrics—and systematically designing HarmBench to satisfy them, in contrast to the at least 9 disparate evaluation pipelines catalogued in prior work. The R2D2 adversarial training method extends the Zephyr fine-tuning pipeline (Tunstall et al., 2023) with a dynamic GCG-based adversary, positioning itself between static-dataset adversarial training (Jain et al., 2023) and multi-round approaches (Ge et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HarmBench is likely to become the de facto standard for comparing LLM jailbreak attacks and defenses, given its scale (18 methods × 33 models × 510 behaviors) and its careful attention to evaluation pitfalls like token-count sensitivity and classifier gaming. The finding that robustness is independent of model size within families challenges the prior assumption that scaling alone improves safety, redirecting attention to training data and procedures. A key open question is whether adversarial training against a single attack family (GCG) can generalize to dissimilar attacks (PAIR, TAP), which the paper shows it only partially does, suggesting multi-attack training is necessary but computationally expensive. The observation that contextual behaviors—those most differentially harmful—paradoxically yield higher ASR than standard behaviors raises urgent concerns about the safety of LLMs in realistic deployment settings.
