## Problem

Standard IR pipelines require separate models for retrieval (dual-encoder) and reranking (cross-encoder), increasing development and maintenance complexity. The paper asks whether a single Transformer encoder can simultaneously produce the relevance scores needed at each stage, and whether such a unified model can serve as a more effective distillation teacher than a vanilla cross-encoder by providing individual query and document embeddings for alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240982/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The unified encoder consumes query and document text in three input configurations: concatenated with special tokens (CE/reranker mode), independently padded to a fixed length (DE/retriever mode), or independently encoded with token-level interaction (late-interaction mode). Beyond a scalar relevance score, the model outputs separate query and document embedding vectors via dual-pooling in CE mode or direct pooling in DE mode, which are the key additional outputs enabling embedding-matching distillation to smaller student models.

## Method

USTAD trains a single BERT-base encoder with a joint objective that combines task-specific losses (e.g., ListMLE) for each mode plus a score-matching term that aligns scores across modes (Eq. 8). In CE mode, the encoder processes the concatenated query-document sequence and applies dual-pooling—separately pooling token embeddings at query and document positions—to recover individual embeddings from the joint representation, avoiding the degenerate embeddings that standard CE models produce. For distillation, EmbedDistill adds explicit L2 embedding-alignment losses between teacher and student query (and optionally document) embeddings on top of standard score-matching, grounded in a theoretical bound showing the teacher-student generalization gap decomposes into embedding misalignment terms. The asymmetric DE student inherits the teacher's frozen document encoder and trains only a small query encoder (e.g., BERT-mini at 11M params vs. 110M teacher), which provably reduces the uniform deviation term and eliminates document embedding misalignment by construction, achieving 95–97% of teacher performance at 1/10th the trainable parameters.

## Results

On the NQ test set (relaxed recall), USTAD in DE mode (110M params) achieves R@20=85.3 and R@100=89.9, matching AR2-g (220M, R@20=85.4) and YONO (165M, R@20=85.2) with roughly half the parameters. The EmbedDistill-trained DistilBERT student (68M) reaches R@20=85.1 and R@100=89.8, within 0.3 points of the 220M-parameter baselines. On BEIR (zero-shot, 14 datasets), the 6-layer EmbedDistill student achieves nDCG@10=44.0, outperforming other 6-layer baselines (TAS-B: 42.8, GenQ: 42.5) while remaining competitive with 12-layer models like Contriever (46.6) and SentenceBERT (45.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

USTAD DE mode (110M) essentially ties AR2-g (220M) on NQ retrieval (R@20: 85.3 vs 85.4) and slightly edges out YONO (165M, R@20: 85.2), while using roughly half the parameters of both. The EmbedDistill-trained DistilBERT student (68M) achieves R@20=85.1, within 0.3 points of AR2-g and 1.3 points of RocketQA (82.7), demonstrating that a 3x smaller model can match 220M-parameter retrievers. The 11M BERT-mini student (R@20=81.2) trails the 220M baselines by 4–7 points but still surpasses DPR (78.4) and R2D2 (80.6). On BEIR zero-shot evaluation, the 6-layer EmbedDistill student (nDCG@10=44.0) outperforms all other 6-layer baselines—TAS-B (42.8), GenQ (42.5), and Wang & Lyu (40.6)—but remains behind the strongest 12-layer models: Contriever (46.6), SentenceBERT (45.7), and Jina (44.5). For reranking on NQ test, USTAD CE mode (110M) achieves R@20=87.3, surpassing YONO (220M, R@20=86.7) and R2-D2 (110M, R@20=84.5), though it trails AR2-d (330M, R@5=81.5) on R@5 (79.9 vs 81.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior unified IR approaches (YONO, RocketQA v2) that jointly train separate retrieval and reranking models, instead proposing a single Transformer encoder that switches between cross-encoder, dual-encoder, and late-interaction scoring via different input configurations and pooling strategies. For distillation, it extends the standard score-matching paradigm (Lu et al., 2020; Hofstätter et al., 2020) by adding explicit embedding alignment losses, drawing on representation-alignment ideas from non-IR distillation (FitNets, DistilBERT) but grounding them in a novel theoretical bound on the teacher-student generalization gap specific to dual-encoder IR models. The asymmetric student configuration—freezing the teacher's document encoder and training only a small query encoder—is a new architectural choice not present in prior IR distillation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

USTAD simplifies IR pipeline maintenance by replacing multiple specialized models with one, and the EmbedDistill + asymmetric DE recipe offers a practical path to 95–97% teacher performance with only 1/10th the trainable parameters, which is significant for latency-constrained deployments. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 5.1) provides the first rigorous justification for why embedding alignment reduces the teacher-student gap in IR, going beyond the empirical heuristics common in the field. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger encoders (beyond BERT-base), whether it extends to decoder-based retrieval (e.g., DSI), and whether the late-interaction mode can be fully integrated without the current performance trade-offs observed in ablations.
