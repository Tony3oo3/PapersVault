## Problem

Existing object-centric models for sequential inputs rely on RNN-based encoders that sequentially update slots over time, leading to training instability on longer sequences due to gradient vanishing/exploding, poor capacity for long-range temporal dependencies, and training time complexity linear in sequence length. This paper introduces the first temporally-parallelizable slot learning architecture that eliminates recurrence entirely, enabling stable and fast training on long video sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241859/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For 2D unposed videos, each RGB frame is encoded by a CNN into L feature vectors per frame, which are then processed across T frames to produce N slot vectors per time-step. For 3D posed multi-camera scenes, each time-step provides K posed views (9 channels: RGB + ray origin + ray direction) that are first compressed into a Set Latent Scene Representation (SLSR) via a per-view CNN and cross-view transformer, then fed to PSB. The slots are decoded into reconstructed frames (2D) or novel-view pixel colors via viewpoint-conditioned decoders (NeRF or SlotMixer), with a fraction of input views held out during training to enforce geometric understanding.

## Method

PSB replaces the recurrent slot update with M stacked PSB blocks that refine initial slots in parallel across all time-steps. Each block interleaves three operations: (i) bottom-up cross-attention where slots attend to input features using causal masking, inverted attention with renormalization (to induce slot competition), and relative positional bias (for length generalization); (ii) slot interaction via two decoupled self-attentions—one along the time axis (same slot index across frames, with causal masking) and one along the object axis (all slots at the same time-step)—which together enforce temporal tracking and spatial allocation; and (iii) a 2-layer MLP with GELU. All operations use residual connections and layer normalization, making the block suitable for deep stacking. The architecture is trained end-to-end as an encoder within a self-supervised auto-encoding framework, paired with interchangeable decoders (alpha-mixture, autoregressive image transformer, NeRF, or SlotMixer), and achieves 1.6× faster training with stable convergence on longer sequences compared to the recurrent SAVi baseline.

## Results

On 2D unposed videos (MOVi-A/B with alpha-mixture decoder), PSB achieves 14.7–26.8% higher video-level FG-ARI and 2.9–7.6% higher reconstruction PSNR compared to the recurrent baseline SAVi. On dynamic 3D scenes (CLEVR-Simple and CLEVR-Natural-Ego), PSB improves slot linear-probing by 7.3–121% and novel-view-synthesis PSNR by 4–8% over SAVi and static 3D baselines. Training is 1.6× faster than SAVi, and PSB maintains stable training at episode lengths where SAVi becomes unstable (e.g., T=12). On visually complex 2D videos (MOVi-C/D/E with autoregressive transformer decoder), PSB is slightly behind STEVE in FG-ARI but remains competitive in PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAVi (Kipf et al., ICLR 2022), the recurrent state-of-the-art for sequential slot learning, PSB consistently wins on all reported metrics: FG-ARI, reconstruction PSNR, slot linear-probing accuracy, and novel-view-synthesis PSNR, while also training 1.6× faster and remaining stable at longer episode lengths where SAVi degrades. Against STEVE (Singh et al., NeurIPS 2022), which pairs the same recurrent encoder with an autoregressive image-transformer decoder, PSB is slightly behind in FG-ARI on the visually complex MOVi-C/D/E datasets but competitive in PSNR, making it a preferred option when training efficiency and stability are prioritized. Against the static 3D scene models uORF (Yu et al., 2021) and OSRT (Sajjadi et al., NeurIPS 2022), which apply Slot Attention independently per time-step, PSB achieves substantially better linear-probing scores (up to 121% improvement) and higher novel-view PSNR, demonstrating that temporal modeling via parallelizable attention outperforms both the recurrent and the per-frame static approaches on dynamic 3D scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant RNN-based paradigm in object-centric video learning (SAVi, STEVE) by introducing the first temporally-parallelizable slot learning architecture, PSB, which replaces sequential recurrence with causal attention over all time-steps simultaneously. It builds directly on Slot Attention's inverted-attention and renormalization mechanism for competitive slot specialization, adopts the SLSR backbone from OSRT for 3D multi-view encoding, and operates within the auto-encoding frameworks established by SAVi and STEVE. The key conceptual shift is treating the temporal axis as a parallelizable attention dimension rather than a sequential recurrence, analogous to how Transformers replaced RNNs in language modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PSB addresses a fundamental scalability bottleneck in object-centric learning: RNN-based encoders suffer from training instability on longer sequences and linear time complexity, limiting their applicability to real-world video lengths. The 1.6× speedup and stable training on longer episodes make object-centric representation learning more practical, while the demonstrated compatibility with diverse decoders (alpha-mixture, autoregressive transformer, NeRF, SlotMixer) positions PSB as a general-purpose encoder module. Key open questions include the quadratic memory cost in sequence length (addressable via SSMs), inference complexity of O(W²) per new frame versus O(1) for RNNs, and whether the approach scales to real-world, longer-horizon scenes beyond synthetic CLEVR-style benchmarks.
