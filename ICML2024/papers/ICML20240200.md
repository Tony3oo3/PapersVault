## Problem

While the training dynamics of logistic regression on separable data are well understood, the specific structure of associative memory modules—single linear layers with fixed input/output embeddings trained under cross-entropy—remains poorly characterized. In particular, how correlated embeddings and imbalanced token frequencies interact to produce oscillatory transients, benign loss spikes, and (in underparameterized regimes) suboptimal memorization has not been formally analyzed. This paper provides a fine-grained theoretical and empirical account of these phenomena.

## Input / Output

The model takes N input tokens with fixed embeddings e_x ∈ R^d, M output classes with fixed embeddings u_y ∈ R^d, and a data distribution p(x) over tokens; the sole learnable parameter is a weight matrix W ∈ R^{d×d}. The output is a discrete class label f_W(x) = argmax_y ⟨u_y, We_x⟩, and the paper tracks the scalar margins m_i(x) = ⟨u_{f*(x)} − u_i, We_x⟩ as the primary diagnostic of learning progress. The analysis is conducted in both overparameterized (d ≥ N) and underparameterized (d < N) regimes, with the two-particle case (N=2, M=2) solved in closed form.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a reduction of all gradient dynamics (flow, descent, stochastic variants) to a non-linear system of interacting particles, where each particle w_ij = ⟨W, u_j ⊗ e_i⟩ represents one input-output association and interactions are governed by embedding correlation parameters α_ij = ⟨e_i, e_j⟩ and β_ijk = ⟨u_i, u_j − u_k⟩. For orthogonal embeddings in the overparameterized regime, the dynamics decouple and margins grow logarithmically in time, with the exact solution expressed via the product logarithm. When embeddings are correlated, the two-particle system is solved to show that γ_2 (the max-margin direction) grows as log(t) while γ_1 (the orthogonal direction) converges to ½ log(p₁/p₂), and that large learning rates amplify a transitory oscillatory regime producing arbitrarily large loss spikes (Proposition 5). In the underparameterized regime (d < N), the paper demonstrates that minimizing cross-entropy does not minimize 0-1 loss, leading to catastrophic forgetting of frequent associations. The findings are validated on a simplified two-layer Transformer performing an induction-head bigram copying task, where the same margin growth, oscillation, and loss-spike behaviors are observed.

## Results

In the overparameterized regime (d ≥ N) with orthogonal embeddings, the paper proves that classification margins grow logarithmically: m_t(x) = log(c_x(t − t_0)) − h(c_x(t − t_0)), yielding a loss bound L(W_t) ≤ (1/tη) Σ p(x)/c_x. For the two-particle interacting case with correlated embeddings (correlation α) and class imbalance (p_1 ≥ p_2), the max-margin direction grows as γ_2(t) = log(c_t t + 1) + O(log t / t) while the orthogonal direction converges to γ_1(t) = ½ log(p_1/p_2) + O(1/t). A single gradient step with learning rate η can produce a loss spike of at least η(αp_1 − p_2)p_2, which is arbitrarily large when αp_1 ≫ p_2. In the underparameterized regime (d < N), the paper demonstrates that minimizing cross-entropy does not always minimize 0-1 loss, with the minimizer of L potentially 'forgetting' the most frequent association.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without direct method-vs-method benchmark comparisons; it does not propose a new architecture or training algorithm to be evaluated against baselines. Instead, it positions its contributions relative to prior theoretical work: Theorem 2 (binary orthogonal case) is identified as a special case of the logistic regression separable-data dynamics of Soudry et al. (2018) and Wu et al. (2023), extending their O(1/t) loss rate with explicit dependence on the learning rate η and data-dependent constants c_x. The two-particle interacting results (Theorem 4) are noted as consistent with Wu et al. (2023; 2024) but provide finer-grained dependence on the correlation parameter α, class imbalance ratio p_1/p_2, and step-size η. The oscillatory and loss-spike phenomena are connected to the edge-of-stability literature (Cohen et al., 2020; Rosenfeld & Risteski, 2023), but the paper attributes them specifically to correlated embeddings and token frequency imbalance rather than to sharpness of the loss landscape alone. Empirically, the paper validates its predictions on a simplified two-layer Transformer (following Bietti et al., 2023) trained on a bigram induction task, confirming that large learning rates produce loss spikes and margin oscillations while accelerating final convergence, and that larger embedding dimensions reduce interference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training dynamics literature on logistic/cross-entropy loss (Soudry et al., 2018; Wu et al., 2023) to the specific structured setting of associative memories with fixed token embeddings, where the dynamics reduce to a non-linear interacting particle system. It builds directly on the recent line connecting Transformer layers to Hopfield-style associative memories (Bietti et al., 2023; Cabannes et al., 2024) by providing the first fine-grained gradient-flow and gradient-descent analysis of a single such memory module. The particle system framework departs from prior logistic regression analyses by explicitly incorporating correlated embeddings and imbalanced token frequencies as sources of memory interference, connecting to edge-of-stability phenomena (Cohen et al., 2020; Rosenfeld & Risteski, 2023) through a new mechanistic lens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical explanation for empirically observed training instabilities (loss spikes, oscillations) in terms of memory competition driven by embedding correlation and class imbalance, and shows that large learning rates, despite causing these transients, accelerate asymptotic convergence to max-margin solutions. A notable negative result is that cross-entropy minimization can lead to suboptimal memorization (even forgetting the most frequent association) in underparameterized regimes, suggesting the loss is not well-calibrated for capacity-limited associative memories. Key limitations include the restriction to a single linear layer with fixed embeddings, no normalization or adaptive optimizers, and small-scale Transformer validation; open questions include how these dynamics compose across layers, interact with factorized parameterizations, and scale to realistic vocabulary sizes and sequence lengths.
