## Problem

Existing tensorized graph-based incomplete multi-view clustering methods rely on observed data as the dictionary for subspace recovery, requiring sufficient noise-free samples, suffer from cubic computational complexity at scale, use the Tensor Nuclear Norm which uniformly scales all singular values (failing to differentiate valuable large singular values from noise-related small ones), and neglect local geometric manifold structure within views. EDISON addresses all four limitations simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240658/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes m incomplete view matrices {X^v ∈ R^{d_v × n}} where missing instances are zero-filled, along with binary indicator matrices M^v ∈ R^{n_u^v × n} marking which instances are absent in each view. It produces cluster labels by applying k-means to the left singular vectors of the consensus anchor graph Z̄ = (1/√m)[Z^1, …, Z^m] ∈ R^{n×tm}, where each view-specific anchor graph Z^v ∈ R^{n×t} encodes data-to-anchor similarity with t ≪ n anchor points, ensuring sub-cubic scaling.

## Method

EDISON unifies three innovations into a single ADMM-optimized objective: (1) Enhanced Dictionary Representation (EDR) combines missing-data imputation (learnable P^v M^v term), hidden-data effects captured via a W^v X^v term derived from LatLRR's skinny-SVD duality, and anchor graph construction with orthogonality constraint A^v(A^v)^T = I to reduce complexity; (2) Gaussian Error Rank (GER) replaces TNN with a non-convex surrogate ‖Z‖_GER = (1/n_3)Σ_k Σ_i (2/√π)∫₀^{δσ} e^{-l²}dl that heavily penalizes small singular values while saturating near 1 for large ones, solved via a Moreau-Yosida operator in the t-SVD domain; (3) Hyper-anchor graph Laplacian Regularization (HLR) adds Tr(Z^v L_h^v Z^v) where L_h^v is a hypergraph Laplacian over anchor points, capturing high-order local geometric structure. The full problem is solved by ADMM decomposed into seven closed-form sub-problems with guaranteed convergence to a KKT point.

## Results

On NGs and BBCSport, EDISON achieves 100% ACC, NMI, and PUR at 10% missing rate. On BBCSport at 30% missing rate, EDISON surpasses the second-best IMSR by 12.76% (ACC), 21.93% (NMI), and 12.76% (PUR). On the large-scale ALOI-100 dataset (10,800 samples), EDISON completes clustering in 193.28 s, compared to 1,169.70 s for HCLS-CGL and 2,112.20 s for IMSR. Across all six datasets and three missing rates (10%, 30%, 50%), EDISON consistently ranks first or second on ACC, NMI, and PUR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDISON outperforms the strongest graph-based baseline IMSR on most datasets: on BBCSport at 30% missing rate, EDISON leads by 12.76% ACC, 21.93% NMI, and 12.76% PUR; on HW and Scene15, EDISON achieves substantially higher scores (e.g., 87.78% vs. 37.28% ACC on HW at 10% missing). Against the SOTA tensorized method HCLS-CGL, EDISON wins on HW (87.78% vs. 37.07% ACC), Scene15, MSRCV1, and ALOI-100, while HCLS-CGL retains a slight edge on NGs at 10% (90.87% vs. 87.78% ACC in the table, though the paper reports 100% for EDISON under its best configuration). Both deep-learning baselines (CDIMC and DSIMVC) are consistently outperformed across all six datasets and all missing rates, with EDISON achieving 20–60 percentage-point ACC advantages in several settings. In terms of runtime, EDISON (193 s on ALOI-100) is roughly 6× faster than HCLS-CGL (1,170 s) and 11× faster than IMSR (2,112 s), while remaining competitive with the simplest baselines (BSV: 4.25 s, Concat: 1.95 s) that sacrifice accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tensorized graph-based incomplete multi-view clustering (TGIMC) framework by addressing four specific limitations of prior methods: reliance on observed data as dictionary, cubic computational complexity, uniform singular-value scaling via Tensor Nuclear Norm, and neglect of local manifold structure. It draws on LatLRR's dual nuclear norm formulation to introduce an Enhanced Dictionary Representation (EDR) that handles missing data and insufficient sampling, replaces TNN with a non-convex Gaussian Error Rank (GER) for differentiated singular-value penalties, and incorporates a hypergraph Laplacian regularization (HLR) adapted from Zhou et al.'s hypergraph learning. The overall architecture follows the ADMM-based optimization paradigm established in low-rank representation literature, with the Moreau-Yosida operator providing the proximal update for the GER term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EDISON provides a practical, scalable solution for incomplete multi-view clustering that simultaneously handles missing instances, under-sampled features, and large-scale data through anchor graph construction, making it applicable to real-world scenarios where data is rarely complete. The GER offers a principled non-convex alternative to TNN that better preserves informative large singular values while penalizing noise-related small ones, a design choice that could inform other tensor-based learning tasks. However, the method remains a shallow approach limited to linear anchor representations, and the need to tune multiple trade-off parameters (α, β, γ, δ) and the anchor count limits its ease of deployment. Open questions include whether the GER generalizes to higher-order tensors or non-tensorial structures, and whether the hypergraph Laplacian can be replaced by learned adjacency structures for greater flexibility.
