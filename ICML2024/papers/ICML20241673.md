## Problem

Weighted squared loss (as in iALS/WRMF) achieves strong ranking performance in implicit-feedback recommendation, yet no theoretical connection to ranking metrics like DCG has been established, leaving its success unexplained. Meanwhile, the softmax loss is proven DCG-consistent but its non-linear exponential operations prevent closed-form or higher-order optimization over the full item corpus. The paper bridges this gap by deriving a squared-form surrogate of the softmax loss that retains DCG-consistency while enabling efficient ALS optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a binary user-item interaction matrix R ∈ {0,1}^{M×N} (98–99%+ sparse) representing implicit feedback, where 1 indicates an interaction. It produces a full-corpus preference score vector o^(u) ∈ ℝ^N for each user u, from which a ranked item list is extracted for top-K evaluation (K=10). Unlike sampling-based baselines that only score a subset of negatives, RG² scores all N items per user, making it a non-sampling method. The model is a linear MF (P·Qᵀ) or a 3-layer LightGCN with 64-dimensional embeddings.

## Method

The RG² loss is derived by performing a second-order Taylor expansion of the DCG-consistent softmax loss at the origin, yielding the upper-bound form log N − o_i^(u) + (1/2N)‖o^(u)+1_N‖². This is algebraically rewritten as a weighted squared loss with per-pair targets S_ui = r_ui·N/|I_u| − 1 and weights W_ui = |I_u|/(2N|D|), which pushes positive scores toward N/|I_u| − 1 and negative scores toward −1. The paper proves DCG-consistency (Theorem 3.13) via a Cauchy–Schwarz bound linking the surrogate gap to the DCG gap, and establishes a generalization upper bound (Theorem 3.20) using pseudo-dimension of the low-rank hypothesis space. Optimization uses a Weighted ALS algorithm (Algorithm 1) with closed-form updates for P and Q, avoiding SGD's variance and enabling convergence in few iterations. The squared structure also allows direct plug-in to NewtonCG for non-linear encoders without the O(MN) approximation bias that plagues other losses.

## Results

On MovieLens-10M, RG² achieves the best MRR@10 of 0.4723 and NDCG@10 of 0.2963, outperforming Softmax (0.4487/0.2849) and WRMF (0.4475/0.2797). On Amazon Electronics, RG² matches Softmax on NDCG@10 (0.0212) and slightly edges it on MRR@10 (0.0173 vs 0.0172). On Steam, RG² is within 0.2% of Softmax on both metrics (MRR@10: 0.0492 vs 0.0493; NDCG@10: 0.0575 vs 0.0579) while converging faster due to ALS optimization. Overall, RG² delivers ranking performance on par with or surpassing the DCG-consistent Softmax loss while eliminating the need for stochastic gradient descent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RG² clearly dominates all sampling-based baselines (BPR, BCE, S-Softmax, UIB, SML) across all three datasets, with Softmax achieving 20.49% and 20.19% average relative improvements over the best sampling method on MRR@K and NDCG@K respectively, and RG² extending this lead further. Against WRMF (the closest non-sampling squared-loss competitor using ALS), RG² wins on every dataset and metric, confirming that the Taylor-derived target values (S_ui) align better with ranking than WRMF's fixed 0/1 targets. Against Softmax (the DCG-consistent gold standard optimized with SGD), RG² achieves a 4.63% average relative improvement on MovieLens, a 0.31% improvement on Electronics, and a negligible 0.70% decrease on Steam, while converging in fewer epochs due to closed-form ALS updates. Sparsemax, a sparse variant of Softmax, performs worst among non-sampling methods due to underfitting of inactive users and cold items. The paper also validates RG² on a LightGCN backbone (Table 5), where it again outperforms Softmax on MovieLens (MRR@10: 0.4652 vs 0.4414) and Electronics (MRR@10: 0.0176 vs 0.0171), though it trails slightly on Steam.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected lines of work in implicit-feedback recommendation: the theoretically-grounded softmax loss (proven DCG-consistent via Bregman divergence by Bruch et al., 2019 and Yang & Koyejo, 2020) and the practically-efficient weighted squared loss used in WRMF/iALS (Hu et al., 2008; Rendle et al., 2021, 2022). The key conceptual move is applying a second-order Taylor expansion of the softmax loss at the origin, revealing that the resulting squared-form surrogate (RG²) inherits DCG-consistency while remaining amenable to closed-form ALS optimization. This departs from prior work, which treated the squared loss's strong ranking performance as an empirical observation without theoretical justification, and from the softmax line, which was limited to SGD-based optimization due to its non-linear structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the missing theoretical link explaining why squared losses (used in iALS for over a decade) achieve competitive ranking performance, which has been a long-standing gap in the recommendation literature. Practically, RG² offers a drop-in replacement for WRMF that matches or slightly exceeds softmax-level ranking quality while converging faster via ALS, and it eliminates the manual hyperparameter α. Limitations include the Taylor expansion upper bound relying on an assumption that logits remain near zero (validated only for extreme cases), a generalization bound that is loose due to pseudo-dimension dependence, and NewtonCG experiments that are exploratory and do not clearly outperform ALS. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-linear encoders beyond MF/LightGCN and whether the DCG-consistency guarantee holds under the relaxed assumptions of real-world training dynamics.
