## Problem

Prior width-scaling parameterization theory (Yang & Hu 2021; Yang & Littwin 2023) assumes parameters become fully aligned with data during training, which excludes standard and NTK parameterizations from the feature-learning regime regardless of learning rate choice. This paper asks what happens when alignment is treated as a continuous, layer-specific quantity rather than a binary assumption, and whether the resulting broader space of stable parameterizations yields better practical prescriptions. Additionally, the epsilon hyperparameter in adaptive optimizers was overlooked as a component of the parameterization that breaks scale invariance at realistic model widths.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a model width n, a parameterization choice (Standard, NTK, muP, or Mean-Field), and an optimizer family (SGD, Adam, or Adafactor) as inputs, and produces per-layer learning rate exponents c_l and empirically-tuned constant multiplicative factors γ_l for each of the embedding, hidden, and readout layers. The per-layer learning rate is structured as η_l = β_n · γ_l · (n/b)^{-c_l}, where the exponent c_l is theoretically determined by the alignment assumption and the constant γ_l is tuned at a base model dimension b=1024 and transferred across all larger widths. The alignment ratio metric A_l^t = log_n(||z_{l-1}^t W_l^t|| / (||z_{l-1}^t|| ||W_l^t||)) is computed per layer per training step to empirically measure how alignment evolves, and the epsilon scaling prescription ε_l = base_ε · (n/b)^{-g_l} is derived from the gradient scale exponent g_l specific to each parameterization and layer.

## Method

The paper defines a general alignment space with three exponents (α_l, ω_l, u_l) quantifying the alignment contribution from the ΔW_l·z_{l-1}, W_l·Δz_{l-1}, and ΔW_l·Δz_{l-1} terms in the activation expansion, then derives stability and nontriviality constraints as functions of these variables for SGD, Adam, and Adafactor update rules. Under two specific assumptions—full alignment (α=u=1) and no alignment (α=u=1/2), both with ω=1/2—the constraints yield maximum stable per-layer learning rate exponents for all four parameterizations × three optimizers, recovering prior tensor program results as a special case when ω_{L+1}=1. Empirically, the paper trains tens of thousands of decoder-only Transformer models (NanoDO architecture, 8 layers, up to 26.8B parameters) on C4 across all parameterization-optimizer combinations, showing that per-layer learning rates with tuned constants outperform global learning rates for all parameterizations, that standard parameterization with per-layer rates outperforms muP, and that the no-alignment exponents are empirically preferable for most configurations. Finally, the paper proposes Adam-atan2, a one-line modification replacing the Adam update's division with an arctangent function, which eliminates the epsilon hyperparameter entirely, restores exact scale invariance, and closes the performance gap between theoretically equivalent parameterization pairs (Standard+NTK, muP+MFP) that was previously caused by epsilon underflow.

## Results

Across tens of thousands of experiments on decoder-only Transformers (up to 26.8B parameters, 14 model sizes, C4 dataset), the paper shows that standard parameterization with theoretically motivated per-layer learning rates and tuned constant factors substantially outperforms muP: the second-largest standard model (15.3B params) achieves lower eval loss than the largest muP model (26.8B params) despite having only 57% as many parameters. The best-performing configuration overall is Adam + parameter scaling + NTK + no-alignment exponents. For epsilon underflow, all three mitigations (small constant ε=1e-15, per-layer ε scaling, and the proposed Adam-atan2) yield substantial eval loss improvements for mean-field parameterization, with the gap increasing with model size; after epsilon correction, theoretically equivalent parameterization pairs (Standard+NTK, muP+MFP) show approximately equal performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against muP (Yang & Hu, 2021), the paper's key finding is that standard parameterization with per-layer learning rates and optimal constant factors substantially outperforms muP under a fair comparison (both using per-layer LRs and tuned constants), with the second-largest standard model beating the largest muP model despite 43% fewer parameters. Against the NTK parameterization (Jacot et al., 2018), NTK + Adam + parameter scaling + no-alignment exponents emerges as the single best-performing configuration across all parameterizations and optimizers tested. Against mean-field parameterization (Bordelon & Pehlevan, 2022), MFP is shown to be most sensitive to epsilon underflow due to its hidden-layer gradients scaling as 1/D^1.5, and all three epsilon mitigations (small constant from Wortsman et al. 2023, per-layer scaling from Yang & Littwin 2023, and the proposed Adam-atan2) close the performance gap, after which MFP matches muP as expected from their theoretical equivalence. Against the standard parameterization baseline (Glorot & Bengio, 2010), the paper demonstrates that adding theoretically motivated per-layer learning rate exponents and tuned constant factors transforms standard parameterization from a global-LR baseline into a configuration that beats muP, challenging the prior narrative that muP is uniquely suited for hyperparameter transfer. The paper also shows that prior alignment assumptions (ω_{L+1}=1) are overly conservative: relaxing to ω_{L+1}=1/2 allows standard and NTK to achieve feature learning, and empirically the no-alignment exponents (α=u=1/2) outperform full-alignment exponents (α=u=1) for most parameterizations with Adam and Adam+parameter scaling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the width-scaling parameterization framework of Yang & Hu (2021) and Yang & Littwin (2023) by replacing their specific alignment assumptions (notably ω_{L+1}=1) with a general alignment-general space parameterized by three alignment variables (α, ω, u), recovering prior results as a special case. It broadens the optimizer coverage from SGD and Adam to include Adafactor (adaptive optimizers with parameter scaling), and builds on the hyperparameter transfer framework of Yang et al. (2022) while challenging its central claim that muP is uniquely suited for transfer. The epsilon underflow analysis connects to Wortsman et al. (2023) and Yang & Littwin (2023), who independently identified the issue, but this paper provides the first systematic empirical validation across parameterizations and proposes Adam-atan2 as a complete fix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard parameterization with theoretically motivated per-layer learning rates and tuned constants outperforms muP substantially challenges the prevailing narrative in the scaling literature and suggests that the choice of parameterization matters less than the learning rate prescription and practical details like epsilon. Adam-atan2 offers a one-line code change that eliminates a confounding hyperparameter, making parameterization comparisons cleaner and potentially improving training stability for large models. Open questions include whether alignment-aware learning rate schedules or optimizers can further close the gap, how these results extend to depth and batch-size scaling, and whether the compute-optimal setting (as opposed to fixed-step) requires sharper exponent decay as the authors note.
