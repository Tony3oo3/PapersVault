## Problem

Post-hoc conceptual explanations for Vision Transformers lack a unified framework that simultaneously satisfies faithfulness, stability, sparsity, multi-level structure (dataset/image/patch), and parsimony. Existing feature-attribution methods (SHAP, LIME, Saliency) and conceptual methods (CRAFT) either provide only image-level explanations, lack stability under perturbation, or are constrained to the ViT's hidden dimension (768 concepts), making them neither faithful nor parsimonious.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242113/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PACE consumes a ViT's internal representations: J patch embeddings (each d-dimensional, d=768 for ViT-Base), the predicted class label, and per-patch attention weights averaged across H heads (used as virtual counts so that a_mj=0.2 means patch j contributes 0.2J samples). A perturbed version of the image (generated via standard augmentation) is also required for the stability term. The output is a three-level explanation: K dataset-level Gaussian distributions (μ_k, Σ_k), one image-level Dirichlet-parameterized concept vector θ_m ∈ R^K, and J patch-level Categorical concept assignments φ_mj ∈ R^K, with K=100 chosen for parsimony.

## Method

PACE is a variational Bayesian framework that treats ViT patch embeddings as observed variables in a hierarchical generative model: K dataset-level Gaussian components (concepts) emit patch embeddings, with image-level topic proportions drawn from a Dirichlet prior and patch-level one-hot assignments drawn from a Categorical distribution. The ELBO decomposes into three terms—L_e (Gaussian embedding likelihood weighted by attention), L_f (log-likelihood of the ViT's predicted label given inferred concepts via a GLM, enforcing faithfulness), and L_s (a contrastive binary label distinguishing the perturbed image's concepts from in-batch negatives, enforcing stability). Inference alternates closed-form coordinate updates for patch-level φ_mjk (involving the digamma function, Mahalanobis distance to concept means, and gradient contributions from L_f and L_s) and image-level γ_mk, while learning updates the dataset-level μ_k and Σ_k via weighted means and covariances—structurally analogous to LDA's EM but with attention-weighted observations and additional faithfulness/stability objectives.

## Results

PACE achieves the best average scores across all four datasets on all three quantitative desiderata: faithfulness (0.72), stability (0.11, lower is better), and sparsity (0.64). On the synthetic Color dataset, PACE attains perfect faithfulness (1.00) and the best stability (0.20). On real-world datasets, PACE leads in faithfulness (0.80 on Flower, 0.50 on Cars, 0.56 on CUB) and stability (0.12, 0.05, 0.05 respectively), while also providing the only Full three-level (dataset/image/patch) conceptual structure with K=100 concepts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PACE outperforms all five baselines on every quantitative metric across all four datasets. Against SHAP, PACE improves faithfulness by 0.28 (0.72 vs 0.44) and stability by 1.91 (0.11 vs 2.02). Against Saliency (the strongest non-conceptual baseline), PACE gains 0.08 in faithfulness (0.72 vs 0.64) and 0.32 in stability (0.11 vs 0.43), while dramatically improving sparsity (0.64 vs 0.00). Against CRAFT (the closest conceptual competitor), PACE achieves 4.8× higher faithfulness (0.72 vs 0.15) and 16.8× better stability (0.11 vs 1.85). PACE is also the only method providing Full three-level explanations (dataset, image, patch) and uses K=100 concepts versus K=768 for SHAP, LIME, Saliency, and AGI, satisfying the parsimony desideratum.

## Relation to Prior Work

PACE adapts the algorithmic structure of Latent Dirichlet Allocation (Dirichlet prior on image-level topic proportions, Categorical patch-level assignments, Gaussian emissions) to the setting of ViT patch embeddings, drawing on hierarchical Bayesian deep learning (Wang & Yeung, 2016; 2020) for its top-down generative process. It departs from existing post-hoc explanation methods (SHAP, LIME, Saliency, AGI) that assign per-patch importance scores, and from CRAFT which performs recursive low-rank factorization, by instead modeling the full distribution of patch embeddings as a Gaussian mixture and inferring latent concepts via variational inference. The key conceptual departure is treating attention weights as virtual patch counts within the generative model, enabling a unified probabilistic treatment of faithfulness, stability, and sparsity through a single ELBO objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PACE is the first method to simultaneously satisfy all five proposed desiderata (faithfulness, stability, sparsity, multi-level structure, parsimony) for ViT explanations, and its dataset-level Gaussian concept centers provide a principled bridge between image-level and patch-level explanations that prior methods lack. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.2) connecting the ELBO to mutual information terms gives a clean information-theoretic interpretation of what the learned concepts capture. Key limitations include the fixed number of concepts K (no automatic determination), the requirement for access to ViT hidden states and attention weights, and evaluation primarily on fine-grained datasets (102–200 classes) rather than large-scale benchmarks like ImageNet. Future work on non-parametric concept discovery and scaling to foundation models like CLIP would strengthen the practical impact.
