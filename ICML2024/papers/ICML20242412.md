## Problem

Differentiable annealed importance sampling (DAIS) was proposed to optimize the initial distribution of AIS, but the specific divergence it minimizes was not characterized. This gap matters because standard VI minimizes the reverse KL (mode-seeking, underestimates variance) while Markovian Score Climbing minimizes the forward KL (mass-covering but unstable in high dimensions), leaving unclear whether a symmetrized objective could combine the strengths of both. The paper also asks whether the learned initial distribution q₀ is useful as a standalone compact posterior approximation at inference time, without running expensive AIS steps.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unnormalized target distribution f(z) (e.g., a joint p(z,x) with fixed observed data x) and produces a fully factorized normal variational distribution q₀(z) with learned means and variances. At training time, N particles are drawn from q₀ and propagated through K uncorrected HMC transitions along an annealing schedule; at inference time (DAIS₀), only q₀ is used, yielding posterior mean and standard deviation estimates directly from its parameters without any MCMC sampling. The method also learns the annealing schedule β₁,…,β_{K−1}, the HMC mass matrix, and step widths as part of the optimization.

## Method

The core theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) shows that for equally spaced annealing steps and perfect transitions, the gap between log Z and the AIS ELBO with N=1 particles equals (1/K) times the Jensen-Shannon divergence between q₀ and f/Z, up to O(1/K³) corrections. The proof proceeds by expressing the gap as a sum of KL divergences between consecutive annealing distributions, applying a Taylor expansion to the log-partition function along the annealing path, and showing that only boundary terms survive in the large-K limit, yielding half the forward KL plus half the reverse KL. Practically, DAIS₀ trains by maximizing the DAIS ELBO using reparameterization gradients through uncorrected HMC transitions (dropping the Metropolis-Hastings acceptance step for differentiability), then at inference time simply reads off the mean-field parameters of q₀. This makes DAIS₀ a form of variational inference that implicitly optimizes a symmetrized objective, expected to be less mode-seeking than VI and more stable than MSC in higher dimensions.

## Results

On Bayesian logistic regression across five real-world datasets (sonar, ionosphere, heart disease, heart attack, loan), DAIS₀(16) achieves the best standard deviation MAE on 3 out of 5 datasets, e.g., 4.27×10⁻² on sonar (d=61) vs. 6.23×10⁻² for full DAIS and 7.95×10⁻² for IWVI. On Gaussian process regression (N=16, K=16), DAIS₀(16) achieves the best std MAE among compact methods in most settings, e.g., 2.51×10⁻² on RBF₁ d=10 vs. 5.16×10⁻² for IWVI. For mean estimation, IWVI and IWVI_SIR generally outperform DAIS₀, while DAIS₀ remains competitive. The compact DAIS₀ representation also outperforms the full sampling-based DAIS on std MAE in several cases (e.g., ionosphere: 3.25×10⁻² vs. 7.64×10⁻²), attributed to the high sampling complexity of DAIS in moderate dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to IWVI (reverse KL / mode-seeking), DAIS₀ consistently provides more accurate uncertainty estimates (std MAE), particularly in higher dimensions: on sonar (d=61) DAIS₀ achieves 4.27×10⁻² vs. 7.95×10⁻² for IWVI, and on ionosphere (d=35) 3.25×10⁻² vs. 4.73×10⁻². However, IWVI outperforms DAIS₀ on mean estimation for most datasets (e.g., loan: 1.67×10⁻² vs. 2.02×10⁻²). Compared to full DAIS (sampling-based), DAIS₀ wins on std MAE by a significant margin on sonar (4.27×10⁻² vs. 6.23×10⁻²) and ionosphere (3.25×10⁻² vs. 7.64×10⁻²), which the authors attribute to the high sampling complexity of DAIS in moderate dimensions. Compared to MSC₈c (forward KL / mass-covering), DAIS₀ is more stable and achieves lower std MAE on all five datasets, while MSC₈c suffers from high variance and inconsistent centering of the variational distribution. On GP regression (Table 1), DAIS₀(16) achieves the best std MAE among compact methods in most configurations (e.g., RBF₁ d=25: 2.61×10⁻² vs. 4.03×10⁻² for IWVI), confirming the theoretical prediction that the Jensen-Shannon divergence yields more mass-covering approximations than the reverse KL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides a theoretical characterization of DAIS (Geffner & Domke, 2021; Zhang et al., 2021) by showing that, in the limit of many annealing transitions, it minimizes the Jensen-Shannon divergence between its initial distribution q₀ and the target. This positions DAIS₀ (using only q₀ at inference) as a natural middle ground between standard VI (reverse KL, mode-seeking) and Markovian Score Climbing (forward KL, mass-covering), and connects to Ruiz & Titsias (2019) who showed a related contrastive divergence converges to the same quantity. The proof technique draws on thermodynamic integration results from Grosse et al. (2013) and Crooks (2007), and the variance-reduction framing builds on the IWVI analysis of Domke & Sheldon (2018).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result gives practitioners a principled reason to use DAIS's initial distribution as a compact variational posterior: it implicitly optimizes a symmetrized objective that avoids the mode-seeking pathology of VI and the high-dimensional optimization instability of forward-KL methods. However, the theorem requires K→∞ and N=1, while practical experiments use moderate K (≤16) and N=16, so the theoretical guarantee is only approximate. Open questions include whether the JS-divergence advantage persists in very high-dimensional settings (beyond d=25 tested here), whether the compact q₀ representation can be enriched (e.g., with correlations) to close the remaining gap to full DAIS, and how the result extends to non-geometric annealing schedules.
