## Problem

Depth-based early-exit approaches for federated learning with heterogeneous clients suffer from three interrelated issues: multiple per-exit classifiers create competing optimization objectives, deeper sub-models are trained on only a subset of client data, and knowledge distillation relies on hand-picked teacher sub-models that may be suboptimal for a given client's data distribution. This paper addresses how to jointly exploit multi-layer features for prediction and improve deeper-layer representations without the overhead of separate classifiers or static teacher selection.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (resized to 224×224) or speech audio clips, processed by a pre-trained DeiT-S (or DeiT-B) vision transformer backbone. Each client is assigned a maximum resource budget r_i that determines how many transformer blocks it can run, so the effective input is consumed by a variable-depth sub-model. The output is a single class label produced by one shared classifier that operates on a fused representation combining the modulated meta-class token with the current layer's class token, rather than separate classifiers at each exit.

## Method

ReeFL introduces a lightweight shared transformer block (Ree) applied recurrently at every early exit. At exit l, Ree takes a queue of all prior class tokens [z_meta, z_cls^1, ..., z_cls^l] plus positional embeddings and produces two outputs: a modulated meta-class token m_0^l added to z_cls^l for the shared classifier (exploitation), and a modulated latest class token m_l^l that replaces z_cls^l as input to the next backbone block (exploration/feature modulation). For knowledge distillation, instead of a fixed teacher, ReeFL dynamically selects the best-performing sub-model per client using a running estimate of per-exit training cross-entropy loss, and computes KL divergence from that teacher to all other exits. The entire approach uses a single shared classifier trained on the full dataset across all exits, eliminating the partial-data training problem of deeper classifiers and the gradient conflicts of multiple competing classifiers.

## Results

On the 4-exit setting, ReeFL achieves a mean accuracy of 76.42% on CIFAR-100 (α=1000, full fine-tuning), outperforming the strongest baseline DepthFL (57.52%) by ~19 points. Under the more challenging 12-exit setting, ReeFL reaches 66.83% on CIFAR-100 (α=1000, full fine-tuning) versus 46.66% for DepthFL. In the frozen-backbone regime on SpeechCommands, ReeFL achieves 66.09% mean accuracy compared to 24.37% for DepthFL and 26.53% for InclusiveFL, demonstrating the critical role of feature modulation when the backbone cannot adapt. Communication cost is comparable to baselines (~54 MB per round for full fine-tuning vs. ~53 MB for DepthFL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReeFL consistently outperforms all three baselines across nearly all fine-tuning methods, datasets, and heterogeneity levels. Against DepthFL (Kim et al., ICLR 2023), the closest competitor, ReeFL gains ~19 points on CIFAR-100 (α=1000, full fine-tuning: 76.42 vs. 57.52) and ~42 points on SpeechCommands (frozen: 66.09 vs. 24.37); the only scenario where DepthFL edges out ReeFL is SA on CIFAR-100 with IID data (72.63 vs. 72.15). Against InclusiveFL (Liu et al., KDD 2022), ReeFL achieves substantially higher accuracy in the frozen and PEFT regimes (e.g., 67.52 vs. 53.99 on CIFAR-100 α=1000 frozen; 73.9 vs. 69.37 with LoRA), while InclusiveFL's FedAdam aggregation gives it a slight edge over its own FedAvg variant but still falls well short of ReeFL. ScaleFL (Ilhan et al., CVPR 2023) is the weakest baseline, often failing to beat the naive ExclusiveFL exclusion strategy, particularly under frozen backbones and PEFT methods (e.g., 28.5 vs. 48.02 on CIFAR-100 frozen, 45.68 vs. 67.46 with LoRA), due to its reliance on width-based channel pruning that requires extensive retraining of pruned channels with limited data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the depth-based scaling line of work for federated learning with heterogeneous clients (DepthFL, InclusiveFL, ScaleFL) by replacing the standard multi-classifier early-exit design with a single shared classifier fed by a recurrent transformer module (Ree) that fuses class tokens from all preceding layers. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) the recurrent module simultaneously performs feature exploitation (for prediction) and exploration (modulating backbone features for deeper layers), and (2) knowledge distillation uses a dynamic per-client best-exit selection rather than hand-picked teacher-student pairs. The approach also integrates naturally with parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, adapters, SSF), extending the evaluation beyond full fine-tuning to a broader set of training paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReeFL addresses a practical deployment bottleneck in cross-device FL where resource-constrained clients are either excluded or forced to use degraded sub-models. The shared-classifier design eliminates the competing-gradient problem of multi-exit classifiers and ensures the classifier is trained on the full dataset, while dynamic teacher selection avoids the sub-optimality of fixed distillation hierarchies. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-uniform state-size architectures (e.g., ResNets), whether it composes with differential privacy, and whether the recurrent module generalizes to language or other modalities. The paper's evaluation is limited to DeiT-S/DeiT-B backbones and three benchmarks, leaving scalability to larger models and more diverse tasks untested.
