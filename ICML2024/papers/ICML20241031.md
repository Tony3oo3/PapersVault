## Problem

In interactive decision-making with a fixed latent context per episode, existing theory covers two extremes: fully observed context (contextual MDPs) and fully hidden context (LMDPs with exponential lower bounds). This paper studies the intermediate regime where the agent receives weakly revealing prospective side information at the start of each episode, showing that this setting is not captured by standard weakly-revealing POMDP frameworks and fundamentally requires K^{2/3} regret rather than the expected √K.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241031/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At the start of each episode, the agent receives a prospective side information value ι drawn from a context-dependent emission matrix I, which weakly reveals the latent context (quantified by an α-weakly revealing condition on total variation distance between posterior beliefs). During the episode, the agent observes instantaneous observations o_t and takes actions a_t over H steps. The output is a history-dependent policy π: I × (A×O×S)* → Δ(A) that conditions on ι, along with a model estimate θ̂. A key structural constraint is that ι is fixed throughout the episode, creating non-trivial correlations between observations across time steps that violate the standard POMDP conditional independence assumption.

## Method

The paper introduces a two-phase explore-then-exploit algorithm. Phase 1 (pure exploration) executes blind policies (ignoring ι) selected to maximize cumulative trajectory bonuses defined via a PSR-based reparameterization using the emission matrix I and its left-inverse I†. The critical insight is that exploration restricted to blind policies suffices to identify the optimal policy in the larger class that exploits ι, established through a key inequality bounding the TV distance between any model in the confidence set and the true model under the full policy class by the expected cumulative bonus under blind policies. Phase 2 exploits the learned model. The confidence set is constructed via MLE with a log(K|Θ|/δ) penalty, and the termination criterion uses the explicit bonus bound from Huang et al. (2023) rather than indirect TV-distance arguments. This yields a matching K^{2/3} regret upper bound with polynomial dependence on M, H, S, A, and α^{-1}, avoiding the exponential A^{Ω(M)} dependence of general LMDPs.

## Results

The paper establishes a matching upper and lower bound of poly(A, α⁻¹, M) K^(2/3) regret for learning in LMDP-Ψ environments, where α quantifies the weakly revealing nature of the prospective side information. This K^(2/3) rate is fundamentally worse than the standard √K rate achievable in weakly revealing POMDPs, but crucially avoids the exponential A^Ω(M) dependence on the number of latent contexts that plagues general LMDPs. The lower bound (Corollary 4.3) shows that any algorithm must suffer at least min(A^Ω(M)√K, (A/α²)^(1/3) K^(2/3)) regret, confirming the K^(2/3) rate is tight up to polynomial factors in problem parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are made at the level of sample complexity across POMDP classes. The LMDP-Ψ setting is shown to be strictly harder than weakly revealing POMDPs (which achieve √K regret) but strictly easier than general LMDPs (which have an A^Ω(M)√K lower bound). The key negative result is that LMDP-Ψ is NOT contained within the weakly revealing POMDP class: the standard conditional independence assumption on observations is violated because the prospective side information ι is fixed across all time steps within an episode, creating non-trivial correlations. The K^(2/3) upper bound (Theorem 4.4) is achieved via a pure exploration algorithm using only blind policies (Π_blind) during exploration, followed by exploitation with the full policy class Π that leverages prospective side information. This represents an exponential improvement in sample complexity over the LMDP lower bound from Kwon et al. (2021) when prospective information is absent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces LMDP-Ψ, a new class of decision problems that interpolates between fully observed contextual MDPs and fully unobserved Latent MDPs, where the agent receives weakly revealing prospective side information at the start of each episode. Algorithmically, it builds on the pure exploration framework of Huang et al. (2023) for PSRs and the Optimistic MLE machinery of Liu et al. (2022, 2023), while the lower bound construction reuses the hard instance family from Kwon et al. (2021). The key conceptual departure is that LMDP-Ψ violates the standard POMDP conditional independence assumption (observations are correlated across time steps due to the fixed prospective information), placing it outside the weakly revealing POMDP class despite superficial similarities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that LMDP-Ψ requires Ω(K^{2/3}) regret rather than the expected O(√K) is a clean negative result that exposes a fundamental gap in how the POMDP literature handles temporally correlated observations. The matching upper bound shows the problem is still efficiently learnable (polynomial in all parameters), providing an exponential improvement over the general LMDP lower bound. Open questions include whether the K^{2/3} rate is tight for broader classes of POMDPs with non-trivial observation correlations, and whether the pure exploration approach can be extended to settings where the prospective information is itself partially observed or noisy.
