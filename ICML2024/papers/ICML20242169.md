## Problem

Characterizing the asymptotic distribution of the minimum empirical risk (MER) has traditionally required strong regularity conditions—Euclidean parameter spaces, IID data, differentiable risks, and unique minimizers with non-singular Hessians. These assumptions are violated in modern ML settings with non-smooth losses, dependent data, functional parameter spaces, and multiple or connected minima. This paper provides a unified asymptotic theory for the √n-blowup of the MER under minimal abstract assumptions, enabling inference, hypothesis testing, and model selection in far broader settings.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a sequence of (potentially dependent) data points Z_i, a loss function l(x,Z) indexed by an arbitrary parameter space X (not necessarily Euclidean or finite-dimensional), and a sequence of model complexity indices for model selection. The primary output is the weak limit of τ_n(ψ̂_n − ψ*), which is generally a non-Gaussian functional of a Gaussian process (an infimum over the minimizer set). Practically, the method produces bootstrap-based confidence intervals for the MER, size-α hypothesis test decisions comparing two models, and a consistent estimator k* of the optimal model complexity via a penalized criterion.

## Method

The core mechanism combines a Danskin-type theorem establishing Hadamard directional differentiability of the infimum functional on classes of bounded (not necessarily continuous) functions with a directionally differentiable delta method, transferring a uniform central limit theorem for the empirical risk to the MER. This yields the limiting distribution as lim_{ε↓0} inf_{x∈S^ε} F(x), where F is a tight Gaussian process, without requiring continuity, differentiability, or Euclidean structure. For practical inference, the paper deploys modified bootstrap procedures (Fang–Santos, Hong–Li, and a Firpo-style variant) that consistently approximate the non-Gaussian limit even when the standard bootstrap fails, including a moving block bootstrap for β-mixing data. A penalized model selection criterion (SWIC) with penalties of order dim(X_k)·log(n)/√n is shown to consistently recover the least complex optimal model, generalizing AIC/BIC theory beyond Euclidean and uniquely-minimized settings. The framework is further extended to incremental hypothesis spaces where the parameter space grows with sample size, via a sequence-level delta method.

## Results

In the Gaussian mixture of experts model selection experiment (n=2000, 50 replications, true k*=2), the proposed Sin-White Information Criterion (SWIC) correctly identifies the optimal complexity in 100% of replications, while BIC achieves 72% accuracy and AIC always underestimates complexity (0% correct). In the neural network experiment, all three bootstrap procedures (standard nonparametric, Fang-Santos modified, and Firpo-based) achieve nominal 90% coverage for the minimum classification loss confidence intervals at moderate sample sizes, with the Firpo-based method being considerably faster due to not requiring refits of the network for each bootstrap resample.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed SWIC penalty against AIC and BIC for model selection in a Gaussian mixture of experts setting with dependent (8-dependent) data. SWIC dominates both baselines: it achieves 100% correct model selection across all 50 replications, while BIC correctly selects k*=2 in only 72% of cases (frequently overestimating complexity) and AIC always underestimates the true complexity. The key theoretical reason is that AIC and BIC penalties do not satisfy the F2 condition required for consistency under the paper's framework, whereas SWIC's penalty P_{k,n} = dim(X_k)·log(n)/√n does. For the bootstrap comparison, the standard nonparametric bootstrap has minimal theoretical support in this setting (it requires full Hadamard differentiability of the inf map, which is very restrictive), while the two modified procedures (Fang-Santos and Firpo-based) are shown to be consistent under the paper's weaker directional differentiability assumptions. All three methods achieve nominal 90% coverage empirically, but the Firpo-based method is substantially faster computationally since it avoids refitting the neural network for each bootstrap resample.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the √n-blowup asymptotic theory for minimum empirical risks initiated by Shapiro (1991) and subsequent works, which required Euclidean parameter spaces, IID data, and Lipschitz continuity. The key methodological advance is combining recent Danskin-type theorems for infima over bounded (not necessarily continuous) functions (Römisch 2014, Carcamo et al. 2020, Firpo et al. 2023) with uniform central limit theorems for Donsker classes, thereby relaxing assumptions to allow non-Euclidean and functional parameter spaces, dependent (β-mixing) data, and discontinuous loss functions. For statistical inference, the paper builds on the modified bootstrap framework of Fang & Santos (2019) and Hong & Li (2020) for directionally differentiable functionals, and generalizes the model selection consistency theory of Sin & White (1996) and Baudry (2015) beyond Euclidean spaces with unique minimizers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides a unified theoretical framework that subsumes many previously bespoke analyses of MER asymptotics (e.g., for mixture models, nonparametric curve registration) under a single set of abstract assumptions, making it easier to verify conditions in new applications. The practical tools—consistent bootstrap confidence sets, hypothesis tests, and the SWIC model selection criterion—fill a gap for settings where the classical n-blowup (Wilks-type) theory fails due to non-differentiability, multiple minima, or non-Euclidean parameters. Open questions include better characterizing the limiting distributions (which are generally non-Gaussian infima of Gaussian processes), understanding when standard vs. modified bootstraps are valid, and extending the incremental hypothesis space results to random or data-dependent parameter spaces.
