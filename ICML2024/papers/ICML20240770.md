## Problem

Large transformer-based models suffer from an outlier inefficiency problem: the softmax function in attention cannot assign zero probability to any token, forcing attention to be allocated to low-information 'no-op' tokens (delimiters, punctuation, background patches). These outliers grow during training, waste GPU memory, and cause severe performance degradation under quantization (e.g., W8A8). Existing fixes like Clipped Softmax and Gated Attention are ad-hoc, require hyperparameter tuning, or introduce extra parameters, and none address the root cause from a principled associative-memory perspective.

## Input / Output

The OutEffHop layer takes a raw query matrix R and a raw memory matrix Y (text tokens, image patches, or time-series windows) along with learnable projection matrices W_Q, W_K, W_V, and produces an attention-like output Z = Softmax_1(β R W_Q W_K^T Y^T) Y W_K W_V. The critical structural difference from standard attention is that Softmax_1 adds 1 to the denominator, so the attention weights sum to strictly less than 1, allowing the head to collectively 'abstain' by assigning near-zero probability to all positions simultaneously rather than being forced to concentrate mass on a spurious token. In self-attention mode R = Y; in pooling mode Y is the sole input with a learnable static query.

## Method

The core contribution is the Outlier-Efficient Modern Hopfield Model, which augments the standard modern Hopfield energy function with a refined log-sum-exponential (lse_1) that adds +1 to the log-sum-exp term, creating a zero-energy fixed point corresponding to a 'no-op' memory class. This is motivated by a 'no-op classification' mechanism that appends one extra dimension to the pattern space: outlier patterns are mapped to a unique vector Ω with zero inner product with any query, so they contribute exp(0)=1 to the energy rather than a large exponential. The retrieval dynamics derived via the CCCP procedure is T_OutEff(x) = Ξ Softmax_1(β Ξ^T x), which monotonically minimizes the energy and, when applied as a single iteration, exactly reproduces the Softmax_1 attention mechanism. Theoretically, the model retains fixed-point convergence and exponential memory capacity while achieving a strictly tighter retrieval error bound and a larger capacity lower bound than the original modern Hopfield model, plus a generalization bound scaling as Õ(N^{-1/2} log N) in sample size.

## Results

Across BERT, OPT, ViT, and STanHop-Net, OutEffHop achieves an average reduction of ~22% in average kurtosis and ~26% in maximum infinity norm compared to vanilla attention. The most dramatic improvement is in OPT, where W8A8 perplexity drops from 42.012 (vanilla) to 16.429 (OutEffHop), and average kurtosis falls from 23,341 to 21.5. In the STanHop-Net time-series benchmark (Table 2), OutEffHop ranks in the top two for outlier efficiency in 25 out of 30 prediction-horizon/dataset settings, with 3–4% reductions in max infinity norm and average kurtosis over the best STanHop-Net variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla attention, OutEffHop delivers massive outlier suppression: BERT kurtosis drops from 418.7 to 26.6, OPT kurtosis from 23,341 to 21.5, and OPT W8A8 perplexity from 42.0 to 16.4. Compared to Clipped Softmax (Bondarenko et al., 2023), OutEffHop is competitive on kurtosis in BERT (26.6 vs. 14.2) and ViT (31.6 vs. 33.9), and dramatically superior in OPT (21.5 vs. 9,731); combining the two (Clipped OutEffHop) further improves kurtosis in BERT (11.8) and ViT (24.6) but underperforms in OPT, consistent with the known weakness of Clipped Softmax on that architecture. Against Gated Attention, OutEffHop alone is slightly weaker on kurtosis in BERT (26.6 vs. 17.8) but Gated OutEffHop achieves the best combined kurtosis (15.6) and infinity norm (32.8) in BERT, and the best overall in OPT (11.4 kurtosis, 7.6 inf. norm). In the STanHop-Net time-series comparison (Table 2), OutEffHop outperforms the original Hopfield (Ramsauer et al., 2020), SparseHopfield (Hu et al., 2023), and STanHop-Net GSH (Wu et al., 2024b) baselines, achieving 3–4% lower max infinity norm and average kurtosis than the best STanHop-Net variant, and ranking top-two in 25 of 30 evaluation settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the modern Hopfield model framework (Ramsauer et al., 2020; Hu et al., 2023) by introducing a 'no-op classification' dimension into the energy function, yielding a refined log-sum-exponential (lse_1) that allocates outlier patterns to a zero-energy point. The key conceptual departure is showing that the resulting retrieval dynamics naturally subsumes Softmax_1 (Miller, 2023) as a single-iteration special case, thereby providing a physics-based (energy minimization) interpretation of what was previously a heuristic attention modification. It also positions itself against the outlier-reduction methods of Bondarenko et al. (2023) (Clipped Softmax, Gated Attention) by offering a principled alternative that requires no additional hyperparameters and combines additively with those methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper elevates Softmax_1 from a blog-post-level trick to a principled associative memory model with guaranteed convergence, exponential storage capacity, and a generalization bound where sequence length T does not degrade performance—making Hopfield layers a theoretically grounded alternative to transformer attention. Practically, OutEffHop delivers meaningful quantization gains (W8A8 perplexity/accuracy improvements) with negligible parameter overhead, and its compatibility with existing outlier-reduction methods (Clipped Softmax, Gated Attention) suggests it can be layered into current pipelines. A stated limitation is that it does not address LayerNorm-induced outliers, which arise from a different mechanism, leaving the full outlier picture incomplete. Open questions include whether the no-op classification can be learned end-to-end without explicit outlier identification, and whether the approach scales to the 10B+ parameter regime where outliers are most costly.
