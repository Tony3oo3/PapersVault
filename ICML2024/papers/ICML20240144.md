## Problem

Existing MARL methods promote behavioral diversity by adding intrinsic rewards or auxiliary loss terms, which modifies the learning objective and provides no principled way to measure or control the resulting diversity to a specific value. No prior approach could constrain a multi-agent system's diversity to an exact, quantified target while leaving the original RL objective intact.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240144/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes each agent's local observation (continuous vector) and a scalar desired diversity value SND_des, and produces per-agent action distributions (deterministic means or stochastic normal parameters) whose pairwise Wasserstein-2 distances, averaged over a set of observations sampled from training batches, exactly equal SND_des. The diversity is evaluated over a dynamically constructed observation set O (unified observations from all agents and timesteps in a batch), and a soft-update coefficient τ stabilizes the scaling factor across training steps. At deployment, the scaling factor is frozen, so inference cost equals that of a standard heterogeneous network (n+1 forward passes).

## Method

DiCo represents each agent policy as the sum of a parameter-shared homogeneous component π_h(o) and a per-agent heterogeneous deviation π_{h,i}(o), then rescales the deviations by the ratio SND_des / SND̂, where SND̂ is the System Neural Diversity of the unscaled deviations computed over a batch of observations. This architectural constraint—applied directly to the actor network output—guarantees (via a proven theorem for deterministic, homogeneous-variance, and heterogeneous-variance Gaussian policies) that the resulting system diversity equals the target exactly, without altering the RL objective. Because it is purely structural, DiCo is compatible with any actor-critic MARL algorithm (IPPO, MADDPG, IDDPG) with continuous actions. A soft-update mechanism (τ-weighted exponential moving average) stabilizes the scaling factor during training, and an optional auxiliary loss penalizes action means that fall outside bounded action domains to prevent constraint bypass.

## Results

Across all five VMAS tasks, DiCo agents empirically match their desired diversity target (SND_des) throughout training, confirming the theoretical guarantee of Theorem 5.1. In the Tag task, constraining red agents to SND_des=0.6 nearly doubles the tagging reward compared to both homogeneous (SND_des=0) and unconstrained heterogeneous agents (which settle at ~SND=0.3), while also producing emergent strategies (ambush, pinching, man-to-man marking). In Dispersion, DiCo at SND_des=6 converges to the optimal one-agent-per-food policy faster than unconstrained heterogeneous agents, which remain suboptimal at training's end. In Reverse Transport, low-diversity DiCo constraints (SND_des=0, 0.1) reach optimal reward faster than unconstrained heterogeneous policies, demonstrating that constraining diversity downward also improves sample efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three policy configurations across all tasks: (1) homogeneous agents (SND_des=0, equivalent to parameter sharing), (2) unconstrained heterogeneous agents (Eq. 3, sum of shared + per-agent components with no scaling), and (3) DiCo-constrained agents at various SND_des values. In tasks where diversity is beneficial (Dispersion, Sampling, Tag), DiCo at a higher diversity level outperforms both baselines: in Dispersion, DiCo (SND_des=6) reaches the optimal assignment faster than unconstrained agents that still have two agents pursuing the same food; in Sampling, DiCo (SND_des=5) enforces a wider spatial spread than unconstrained agents that revisit cells; in Tag, DiCo (SND_des=0.6) nearly doubles reward over both homogeneous and unconstrained agents. In tasks where homogeneity is optimal (Reverse Transport, same-goal Navigation), DiCo at low diversity (SND_des=0 or 0.1) converges faster than unconstrained heterogeneous agents, which learn excessive diversity and converge more slowly. The unconstrained heterogeneous baseline consistently shows the largest policy search space, leading to slower convergence and more instability in both directions (too little or too much diversity). DiCo's architectural constraint thus acts as a regularizer on the policy search space, improving sample efficiency regardless of whether the task requires more or less diversity than the unconstrained default.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the dominant paradigm of promoting diversity in MARL via additional objectives (intrinsic rewards in Jaques et al., Wang et al., Jiang & Lu; auxiliary losses in MAVEN, ROMA). Instead of modifying the learning objective, DiCo applies a purely architectural constraint—representing policies as a shared component plus dynamically rescaled per-agent deviations—to control diversity to an exact target value without altering the RL objective. The method builds directly on the System Neural Diversity (SND) metric (Bettini et al., 2023c) for its closed-form Wasserstein-based computation, and on Fixed Integral Neural Networks (Kortvelesy, 2023) for an analytical variant that enforces constraints over the entire observation space. The shared-plus-deviation policy decomposition also bridges the long-standing parameter-sharing vs. independent-policies tradeoff in MARL, offering a continuous spectrum between the two extremes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiCo provides the first principled, theoretically guaranteed mechanism to set behavioral diversity to a precise value, opening a new axis of control for MARL practitioners who can now treat diversity as a design parameter rather than an emergent side effect. The emergent strategies in the Tag task (ambush, pinching, man-to-man marking) suggest that diversity constraints can unlock qualitatively different tactical behaviors, but all experiments are confined to simple 2D VMAS environments with 2–4 agents, leaving open whether the approach scales to complex, high-dimensional tasks like StarCraft or real-world multi-robot systems. The requirement for domain knowledge to select SND_des remains a practical barrier, and the proposed PID closed-loop optimizer is only sketched, not empirically validated. Future work on automatic diversity selection and validation in complex environments will be critical for broader adoption.
