## Problem

Oversampled Fourier phase retrieval of multichannel color images suffers from a trivial 180° rotation ambiguity that causes per-channel orientation misalignment when using traditional iterative solvers like RAAR, producing severely degraded reconstructions. Existing diffusion-based approaches such as DPS fail on this highly nonconvex problem, and supervised learning cannot handle the inherent ambiguity. The paper asks whether a pretrained diffusion model can be integrated with a traditional iterative engine to automatically resolve the channel alignment without any retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a Fourier magnitude measurement y = |F(zpad(x₀))| of size rh×rw×3 (where r² is the oversampling ratio, tested at 2.0 and 4.0) and a pretrained unconditional diffusion model (e.g., on FFHQ or ImageNet at 256×256) as input, and outputs a reconstructed h×w×3 color image. No retraining or fine-tuning of the diffusion model is required for different oversampling ratios or noise levels. The zero-padding support and nonnegativity constraint are the only structural priors on the image itself.

## Method

DiffFPR interleaves a DDIM sampling loop with a short RAAR inner loop at each denoising step. At step t, Tweedie's formula predicts x̂₀(x_t) on the clean image manifold M₀; RAAR then runs T_RAAR iterations (typically 1–4) on this prediction to enforce measurement consistency, yielding x̂₀'(x_t). A damped update x̂₀' ← (1−√α_t)·x̂₀ + √α_t·x̂₀' blends the diffusion prior with the measurement-guided estimate, where the coefficient √α_t is small at early (noisy) steps and approaches 1 near the end, so the strong color-image prior dominates early to prevent channel misalignment while measurement fidelity takes over later. The updated point is then pushed back to the less-noisy manifold M_{t−1} via the standard DDIM step. This design performs measurement guidance in the clean manifold rather than in the noisy one (unlike DPS's gradient-descent guidance), avoiding the back-propagation through the score network and the failure mode caused by the nonconvex likelihood p(y|x₀).

## Results

On the 1K-image FFHQ and ImageNet benchmarks (256×256, oversampling ratio r²=4), DiffFPR achieves 37.24 dB PSNR / 0.902 SSIM / 0.087 LPIPS on FFHQ and 31.76 dB PSNR / 0.751 SSIM / 0.204 LPIPS on ImageNet under noiseless measurement, substantially outperforming RAAR (22.50 / 21.80 dB) and DPS* (24.85 / 22.89 dB). Under Gaussian noise (σ_y=0.01), DiffFPR still leads with 29.17 dB (FFHQ) and 25.94 dB (ImageNet), while RAAR drops to ~12.7 dB. Notably, DiffFPR achieves a 100% success rate (PSNR > 20 dB) across all 1K test images, compared to 52.4%/48.2% for RAAR and only 14.5%/4.0% for DPS on FFHQ/ImageNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffFPR decisively outperforms all five baselines across every noise level and dataset. Against RAAR (Luke 2004), the primary traditional iterative engine, DiffFPR gains ~14.7 dB PSNR on noiseless FFHQ (37.24 vs. 22.50) and ~16.4 dB on noisy (σ_y=0.01) FFHQ (29.17 vs. 12.74), with the gap widening as noise increases because RAAR suffers from channel-orientation misalignment. Against DPS (Chung 2022a), the gradient-descent diffusion guidance approach, DiffFPR leads by 12.4 dB on noiseless FFHQ and 3.3 dB on noisy FFHQ; DPS also has a very high failure rate (only 14.5% of FFHQ images exceed 20 dB PSNR vs. 100% for DiffFPR). Against HIO (Bauschke 2002), the older projection method, DiffFPR-RAAR outperforms DiffFPR-HIO by 1.0 dB (noiseless) and 6.2 dB (σ_y=0.01) on FFHQ, confirming RAAR's superior noise handling as the inner engine. Against neural-representation baselines DIP (Ulyanov 2018) and SIREN (Sitzmann 2020) on the 128×128 FFHQ subset, DiffFPR achieves 37.59 dB PSNR versus 16.92 dB (DIP) and 15.54 dB (SIREN), a gap of over 20 dB, as these methods cannot resolve the 180° rotation ambiguity inherent to Fourier phase retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: traditional projection-based iterative solvers for phase retrieval (RAAR, HIO, DRS) and diffusion models as priors for inverse problems (DPS, DDNM, DDRM). It departs from the dominant diffusion-based inverse problem paradigm, which uses gradient descent through the score network for measurement guidance, by instead plugging a traditional iterative engine (RAAR) directly into the diffusion sampling loop as the measurement-guidance step. The key conceptual move is performing this guidance in the clean image manifold via Tweedie's formula rather than in the noisy manifold, and applying a damped update scaled by √α_t to counteract the high prediction variance at early sampling steps. This is positioned as the first method to solve multi-channel (color) Fourier phase retrieval by combining a pretrained diffusion prior with a classical iterative solver.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: color image phase retrieval, where the 180° rotation ambiguity causes channel misalignment that no existing method handles automatically. The 100% success rate (vs. 52.4% for RAAR and 14.5% for DPS on FFHQ) demonstrates that the diffusion prior effectively resolves the orientation problem. However, limitations remain: performance degrades significantly under noise (PSNR drops from 37.2 to 22.2 as σ_y goes from 0 to 0.05), the method is ~15× slower than RAAR, and all experiments use synthetic data. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to other nonconvex problems with trivial ambiguities, how to reduce noise artifacts in the final output, and whether real-world measurements (with structured noise) behave similarly.
