## Problem

Despite the empirical success of knowledge distillation, the statistical mechanism behind its effectiveness remains poorly understood, and no principled method exists for quantifying the uncertainty of the distilled student model. The paper addresses both gaps by recasting KD within a Bayesian framework, showing that the KD loss is exactly the negative log-posterior of a model with a teacher-informed prior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240517/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard classification images (28×28 grayscale for MNIST/Fashion-MNIST, 32×32 RGB for CIFAR-10/100) along with their true labels, plus the teacher model's predicted class probability vectors for every training point. Beyond the usual class label and predicted probabilities, BKD outputs a per-sample mean deviance as an uncertainty score and an empirical credible interval [0, τ̂ᵢ] on the deviance, enabling coverage-rate evaluation against ground-truth labels without needing true class probabilities.

## Method

BKD formulates the student model's parameters θ as random variables with a teacher-informed prior π_θ(θ;{pᵢ}) that factorizes as a product of Dirichlet densities Dir(1_K + λpᵢ) over the student's output probability vectors, where λ controls trust in the teacher. The cross-entropy with true labels serves as the likelihood, and the paper proves (Theorem 4.3) that minimizing the standard KD loss is equivalent to finding the posterior mode, giving a transparent Bayesian interpretation of KD. For full posterior inference, SGLD is applied with mini-batch gradients of the KD loss plus Gaussian noise to generate Monte Carlo samples of θ. Prediction uncertainty is then quantified via the expected deviance Δ(q) = −2Σ q_k log q_k averaged over posterior samples, and credible intervals are constructed by solving for the threshold τᵢ that achieves the target coverage level empirically.

## Results

On four benchmark datasets, BKD achieves classification accuracy competitive with the original KD method while providing calibrated uncertainty estimates. On Fashion MNIST, BKD reaches 90.5% accuracy, surpassing both the teacher model (90.1%) and original KD (90.2%). On CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, BKD achieves 96.4% and 84.2% accuracy respectively, marginally outperforming original KD (96.3%, 84.1%) and matching Dropout. Under increasing image perturbation on MNIST, BKD's mean deviance (uncertainty) rises monotonically, whereas original KD, BNN, and Dropout all exhibit overconfident predictions at high perturbation levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BKD is compared against three baselines: original KD (Hinton et al., 2015), KD + Bayesian Neural Network (Blundell et al., 2015), and KD + Monte Carlo Dropout (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016). In terms of classification accuracy, BKD ties or marginally beats original KD on CIFAR-10 (96.4% vs. 96.3%) and CIFAR-100 (84.2% vs. 84.1%), and clearly outperforms it on Fashion MNIST (90.5% vs. 90.2%), though it trails slightly on MNIST (98.4% vs. 98.6%). BKD substantially outperforms the BNN baseline across all datasets (e.g., 96.4% vs. 90.6% on CIFAR-10, 84.2% vs. 78.7% on CIFAR-100). Against Dropout, BKD matches or slightly exceeds accuracy on all four datasets. The most distinguishing advantage of BKD lies in uncertainty quantification: under perturbed inputs, BKD's mean deviance increases steadily with perturbation level, while original KD, BNN, and Dropout all show decreasing deviance (overconfidence) at high perturbation levels, indicating BKD provides more reliable uncertainty estimates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the original knowledge distillation framework of Hinton et al. (2015) and the statistical perspective of Menon et al. (2021), which interpreted teacher predictions as approximating Bayes probabilities. It departs from Korattikara Balan et al. (2015) (Bayesian Dark Knowledge), which required access to the teacher's full posterior predictive distribution—unavailable for large pre-trained models—by instead constructing a Dirichlet teacher-informed prior from the teacher's point predictions alone. The equivalence proof between KD loss minimization and posterior mode estimation provides a clean Bayesian reinterpretation that prior statistical analyses of KD had not explicitly established, and the SGLD-based posterior sampling for uncertainty quantification extends the KD pipeline beyond point estimation into a full Bayesian inference framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The posterior-mode equivalence offers a transparent theoretical explanation for why KD regularizes the student model, potentially guiding future improvements to distillation objectives. The uncertainty quantification capability (mean deviance, credible intervals) is practically valuable for deployment scenarios requiring calibrated confidence, such as outlier detection or safety-critical applications. However, SGLD is computationally expensive compared to alternatives like MC dropout, and the method is currently limited to classification with a Dirichlet prior; extensions to regression, generative models, and restricted student architectures (e.g., LDA for analytical posteriors) remain open. The paper also notes that the Dirichlet prior may be suboptimal near simplex extrema, motivating exploration of alternatives like the continuous categorical distribution.
