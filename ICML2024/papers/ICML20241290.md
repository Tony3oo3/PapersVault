## Problem

Existing LLM-based molecular generation suffers from sample inefficiency: beam search cannot terminate early on failing sequences or steer toward superior pharmaceutical properties, while MCTS-based planners like PG-TD over-exploit high-probability tokens and support only a single reward objective. The paper asks how to improve the exploration–exploitation balance in MCTS-guided Transformer decoding so that generated molecules simultaneously satisfy multiple drug-quality criteria (docking, druglikeness, solubility, synthesizability).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241290/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained GPT-2-like Transformer (124M parameters, trained on ~10.7M ZINC SMILES strings) and a target protein binding site (3CLPro PDB 7BQY or RTCB PDB 4DWQ) as the optimization context. It outputs complete SMILES molecule strings, each scored by a multi-critic ensemble (druglikeness, solubility, synthesizability, docking via a BERT surrogate) whose min-max-normalized scores are summed into a single reward. In the code-generation setting, the same framework takes a programming prompt and produces candidate programs evaluated by pass rate and strict accuracy on private test cases.

## Method

ERP replaces PG-TD's P-UCT selection with PH-UCT, which augments the UCB exploration bonus with two Transformer-derived signals: the next-token probability π_τ(a|s_t) and an e-step forward entropy term (1/e)Σ H(π_τ(·|s_{t+i})), so the planner preferentially explores subtrees where the model is uncertain rather than merely revisiting high-probability tokens. During expansion, a combined TOP-PK filter (cumulative probability threshold p and maximum child count k) restricts candidate tokens to the Transformer's most plausible continuations, pruning the action space and reducing invalid-molecule generation. Partial molecules are evaluated by running the Transformer's beam search to completion and scoring the resulting full molecules with the multi-critic normalized reward, which is then backpropagated via max-update to ancestor nodes. The e-step entropy mechanism is the key differentiator: by looking ahead e steps and measuring the average policy entropy over that horizon, ERP identifies branches where the pretrained model lacks experience and directs additional rollouts there, achieving a principled exploration–exploitation tradeoff that PG-TD's visitation-only bonus cannot capture.

## Results

On the 3CLPro (SARS-CoV-2) benchmark, ERP achieves a best normalized reward of 3.24 (+13.71% over Sampling) and an average top-10% normalized reward of 3.07 (+12.25%), outperforming PG-TD (3.20 and 2.99 respectively) by 1–5% across key metrics. On the RTCB (human cancer) benchmark, ERP reaches a best normalized reward of 3.24 (+4.52%) and average valid normalized reward of 2.61 (+5.61%), again surpassing PG-TD (3.10 and 2.53). ERP also generates more unique valid molecules (2575 on 3CLPro, 1343 on RTCB) than all baselines, and improves docking scores by up to 12% over Sampling. On code generation (APPS), ERP outperforms PG-TD on pass rate and strict accuracy across GPT-2 and GPT-Neo models, e.g., 29.711% vs 28.142% pass@20 on APPS Intro with GPT-2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PG-TD (the prior state-of-the-art MCTS-guided Transformer decoding method), ERP consistently wins on both drug discovery benchmarks: on 3CLPro, ERP improves best normalized reward from 3.20 to 3.24, average top-10% reward from 2.99 to 3.07, and docking score from −8.37 to −9.13; on RTCB, ERP lifts best normalized reward from 3.10 to 3.24 and average valid reward from 2.53 to 2.61. ERP also generates more unique valid molecules than PG-TD (2575 vs 2549 on 3CLPro; 1343 vs 1266 on RTCB), and its advantage grows with more rollouts, whereas PG-TD's performance degrades beyond 256 rollouts. Against UCT (vanilla MCTS without Transformer guidance), ERP wins decisively on all quality metrics (e.g., best normalized reward 3.24 vs 3.10 on 3CLPro; 3.24 vs 2.97 on RTCB), though UCT produces more unique molecules on 3CLPro (3013 vs 2575) at the cost of lower average quality. On code generation (APPS), ERP outperforms PG-TD on pass rate across most settings (e.g., 29.711% vs 28.142% pass@20 on APPS Intro with GPT-2; 30.916% vs 29.209% with GPT-Neo), with the gap widening at larger k values, confirming that the entropy-reinforced selection mechanism generalizes beyond drug discovery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MCTS-guided Transformer decoding framework introduced by PG-TD (Zhang et al., 2023a) from single-reward code generation to multi-objective drug discovery. The core methodological departure is the PH-UCT selection rule, which augments the P-UCT formula (itself derived from AlphaGo's policy-weighted UCB) with an e-step forward entropy measurement of the Transformer's next-token distribution, explicitly encoding uncertainty about future token sequences into the exploration bonus. For expansion, ERP replaces PG-TD's random token sampling with a TOP-PK filter, and for evaluation it uses the Transformer's beam search to score partial molecules, thereby integrating the language model's prior knowledge at every stage of the tree search rather than only at selection time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The entropy-reinforced planning mechanism offers a principled way to balance exploration and exploitation in sequence generation for multi-property optimization, which is directly relevant to practical drug discovery where no single objective dominates. However, the evaluation relies on a surrogate docking model rather than physics-based docking, uses only 124M-parameter GPT-2-scale Transformers, and the improvements over PG-TD are modest (1–5%), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger LLMs or more complex molecular representations (e.g., SELFIES, graphs). The finding that PG-TD's performance degrades with increased rollouts while ERP improves monotonically suggests a genuine limitation in prior planning-guided decoding, but the practical significance of the 1–5% gap in a drug discovery pipeline remains to be validated in wet-lab settings.
