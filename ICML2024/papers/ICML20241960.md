## Problem

Existing sparse training methods reduce FLOPs but typically underperform dense baselines, requiring 2–5× longer training schedules to close the accuracy gap. The paper asks whether sparsity can instead be leveraged to *improve* accuracy while maintaining exactly the same FLOP budget as a dense model, rather than merely trading compute for performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241960/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Sparse-IFT transformations are drop-in replacements for dense layers: they accept the same input feature dimensions and emit the same output dimensions as the original feedforward function, so no architectural rewiring is needed. For vision, inputs are standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet, 32×32 for CIFAR-100) producing class labels; for NLP, text sequences with a 2048-token context window produce next-token predictions. The first and last layers (input convolution and output linear) are kept dense to preserve gradient flow, with the resulting FLOP delta absorbed by slightly increasing sparsity in intermediate layers.

## Method

Sparse-IFT introduces four FLOP-equivalent transformations—Sparse Wide, Sparse Parallel, Sparse Factorized, and Sparse Doped—that replace dense layers by widening input/output channels, adding parallel non-linear branches, factorizing into sparse sub-matrices, or combining a low-rank component with an unstructured sparse matrix, respectively. Each is parameterized solely by a sparsity level s, with the widening or branching factor derived analytically to guarantee Iso-FLOP equivalence (e.g., Sparse Wide uses k_sw = √(1/(1-s))). The resulting expansion of the sparse mask-weight search space (scaling with width, parallel branches, or hidden dimension) enables dynamic sparse training methods such as RigL to discover superior sparse subnetworks within a single training run without any hyperparameter changes. A spectral analysis using Ramanujan graph properties (Ramanujan Gap Δr and Weighted Spectral Gap λ) reveals that DST iteratively optimizes connectivity topology and weight magnitudes in a manner correlated with final accuracy, with Sparse Wide achieving the largest gains due to its highest search-space cardinality.

## Results

On ImageNet, Sparse Wide IFT ResNet-18 at 90% sparsity achieves 74.4% top-1 accuracy, a +3.5% improvement over the dense ResNet-18 baseline (70.9%) while using the same training FLOPs. Sparse Wide IFT ResNet-34 reaches 76.8% (+2.6% over dense 74.2%), and BotNet-50 reaches 78.6% (+1.1% over dense 77.5%). For language modeling, Sparse Wide IFT GPT-3 Small at 75% sparsity improves average Open LLM Leaderboard accuracy by 0.9% (34.7% vs. 33.8% dense). Transfer learning yields +5.2% mAP on COCO object detection and +2.4% mIoU on CityScapes segmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18 (Sparse Wide IFT), all Pruning-at-Initialization methods underperform the dense baseline at higher sparsity: SNIP drops to 75.8% at 90% sparsity (vs. 77.0% dense), GraSP to 76.5%, and FORCE to 75.4%. DST methods (SET, RigL, GraNet) consistently outperform PaI methods, with RigL achieving 80.1% at 90% sparsity. Critically, Sparse-IFT with RigL at 200 epochs (Iso-FLOP) achieves 80.1%, outperforming the same method with extended schedules (200·1/(1-s) epochs) which only reach 76.4–78.9%, demonstrating that the expanded search space—not longer training—drives the gains. Unstructured sparsity (RigL) also outperforms N:M structured sparsity (Hubara et al.) by 1.0–2.0% across sparsity levels (e.g., 80.1% vs. 78.1% at ~90% sparsity). The headline result is that Sparse-IFT + DST beats the dense baseline at identical FLOPs, whereas all compared sparse training baselines either match or fall below dense accuracy under the same FLOP budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant framing of sparse training as a FLOP-reduction technique (Lottery Ticket Hypothesis, DST methods like RigL/SET/GraNet) by repurposing sparsity as an accuracy-improving mechanism at constant FLOPs. Conceptually, it bridges overparameterization methods (ExpandNets, DoConv, ensembling) and sparse training: rather than adding parameters or layers to increase capacity, it widens or parallelizes layers while sparsifying them to maintain the original FLOP budget. The Ramanujan graph spectral analysis draws directly on Hoang et al. (2023a,b), extending their connectivity metrics to characterize how DST navigates the expanded search space created by Sparse-IFT transformations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a single-hyperparameter, drop-in layer replacement that consistently improves accuracy across CNNs and transformers without any training schedule changes, which lowers the barrier to adoption significantly. However, the wall-clock benefits depend on hardware with unstructured sparsity support (Cerebras CS-2, DeepSparse on AVX-512 CPUs), which remains limited; on standard GPUs, the Iso-FLOP guarantee does not translate to speedup. Open questions include whether the accuracy gains persist at much larger scales (beyond GPT-3 Small / ResNet-34), how Sparse-IFT interacts with modern scaling laws, and whether the spectral properties identified (Ramanujan gap, spectral gap) can be used as training-time diagnostics or early-stopping criteria.
