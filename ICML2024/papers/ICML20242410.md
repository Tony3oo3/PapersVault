## Problem

Existing long-horizon event sequence forecasting methods for temporal point processes remain autoregressive, generating events one at a time and accumulating error over the prediction horizon. No prior approach directly models the joint distribution of both event types and inter-arrival times for multiple future events simultaneously, missing the complex dependencies between these two data types across the sequence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242410/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a variable-length historical context sequence of (inter-arrival time, event type) pairs, encoded by a continuous-time Transformer into a fixed-dimensional representation. It outputs a fixed-length sequence of N future events (typically N=20), where inter-arrival times are strictly positive continuous values and event types are categorical. At inference, multiple samples are drawn from the learned distribution; final predictions are formed by averaging the time vectors and majority-voting the event types across samples.

## Method

CDiff employs two coupled denoising diffusion processes: a Gaussian diffusion on Box-Cox-transformed inter-arrival times and a multinomial (categorical) diffusion on event types. The key architectural innovation is that the two denoising networks interact — the time denoiser conditions on the current event-type estimate and the type denoiser conditions on the current time estimate — enabling the model to learn the joint distribution rather than treating the two modalities as conditionally independent. The forward process adds independent Gaussian and uniform categorical noise, while the reverse process learns coupled denoising distributions parameterized by a transformer with cross-attention to the context encoding. Training minimizes a decomposed ELBO with standard DDPM epsilon-prediction for times and direct e_0 prediction for types, sampling one random diffusion timestep per training example. Inference uses DDIM acceleration for the time process and step-skipping for the categorical process to reduce sampling cost.

## Results

On N=20 event sequence forecasting across six real-world datasets, CDiff achieves the best OTD on 5/6 datasets (e.g., 21.01 on Taxi, 41.25 on StackOverflow, 37.73 on Amazon) and the best RMSE_e on all six (e.g., 1.131 on Taxi, 2.653 on Taobao, 1.095 on MOOC). For time-interval prediction, CDiff attains the lowest RMSE_x+ on all datasets (0.351 on Taxi, 0.551 on Taobao, 1.199 on StackOverflow) and the lowest sMAPE on 5/6 datasets. The advantage over the strongest baseline (HYPRO) grows with prediction horizon: Wilcoxon p-values for per-step error differences drop from ~10⁻³ at step 1 to ~10⁻⁷ at step 20 on Taobao.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDiff outperforms all seven baselines on the majority of metrics across all six real-world datasets. The closest competitor is HYPRO (Xue et al., 2022), which uses AttNHP as its base model with a sequence-selection module; CDiff beats HYPRO on OTD for 5/6 datasets and on RMSE_e for all six, with the gap widening at longer horizons (p-values dropping to ~10⁻⁷ at step 20 on Taobao). AttNHP (Yang et al., 2022), the SOTA single-event forecaster, is consistently outperformed by CDiff on both time and type metrics, confirming that autoregressive generation accumulates error over N=20 steps. TCDDM (Lin et al., 2022), which models time and type as conditionally independent, is beaten by CDiff on RMSE_e across all datasets, validating the benefit of the cross-diffusion interaction. Dual-TPP (Deshpande et al., 2021) and NHP (Mei & Eisner, 2017) are outperformed on nearly every metric. LNM (Shchur et al., 2020) is competitive on time metrics (RMSE_x+) but lags on event-type prediction. The modified Add-and-Thin baseline (Lüdke et al., 2023, augmented with a naive type predictor) is outperformed by CDiff on both OTD and time metrics on all four tested datasets, further confirming the importance of jointly modeling type and time.

## Relation to Prior Work

CDiff extends the diffusion generative modeling framework (DDPM, multinomial diffusion) to the temporal point process (TPP) forecasting setting, where prior work has been dominated by intensity-based autoregressive models (Hawkes, NHP, AttNHP) or non-autoregressive methods that assume conditional independence between event types and inter-arrival times (TCDDM, LNM). The key departure is the 'cross-diffusion' architecture: two coupled denoising processes (Gaussian for times, categorical for types) that condition on each other's intermediate states, enabling joint sequence-level modeling without the error accumulation inherent in autoregressive baselines like HYPRO and Dual-TPP. The paper also builds on DDIM for accelerated sampling and uses a Box-Cox transformation to handle the strictly-positive domain of inter-arrival times within a Gaussian diffusion framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By generating an entire N-event sequence non-autoregressively, CDiff avoids the compounding error that plagues sequential TPP forecasters, yielding consistent and statistically significant improvements on long-horizon tasks across six real-world datasets. The ablation confirming that the cross-interaction (joint vs. independent modeling) is necessary for peak performance validates the core architectural choice. A key limitation is the requirement to fix N in advance, which becomes problematic for data with highly variable inter-arrival times; extending the framework to variable-length or interval-based generation without this constraint remains an open direction. The cross-diffusion paradigm for mixed-type (continuous + categorical) sequences could also be applicable beyond TPPs to other structured sequence generation tasks.
