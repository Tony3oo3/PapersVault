## Problem

Coordinate-based neural representations (CNRs) for spherical signals—using sinusoidal encodings, spherical harmonics, or spectral embeddings—lack the expressive power to capture highly nonlinear signals at their finest details. While hybrid neural representations (feature-grid + MLP) resolved this limitation for Euclidean data, extending them to the sphere is non-trivial because existing approaches rely on Euclidean-specific grid structures and distance-based interpolation that are redundant or ill-defined on a sphere.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240961/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes spherical coordinates (latitude ψ ∈ [−π/2, π/2], longitude φ ∈ [0, 2π)) as input and predicts continuous signal values (geopotential in m²/s², temperature in K, or CMB temperature in μK_CMB) at arbitrary points on the sphere, enabling evaluation at any resolution. For weather data, the native grid is equirectangular with non-uniform cell areas concentrated at the poles, requiring latitude-weighted evaluation metrics; for CMB data, the native grid is HEALPix with uniform-area cells at N_side=2048 (~5 arcmin, ~50M points). For temporal interpolation, a timestep scalar is concatenated to the positional feature; for compression, the network is trained per-image to encode the full spatial field.

## Method

HNeR-S constructs positional features by assigning learnable D-dimensional parameters to grid points across L hierarchical levels (coarse-to-fine) of a spherical feature-grid, then interpolating the four nearest neighbors via bilinear spherical interpolation in latitude and longitude. Two grid structures are used: an equirectangular grid (with scaling factor γ=1.5 per level) for weather/climate data, and a HEALPix grid (resolution doubling per level via N_side=2^{ℓ−1}) for CMB data, each aligned with the native data distribution. The interpolation handles longitude periodicity by wrapping intervals across the prime meridian and resolves pole singularity by merging all grid points at a pole into a single shared parameter. The concatenated multi-level positional feature Z(x_in) ∈ R^{D×L} is fed into a 4-layer MLP (256 hidden units, ReLU) to predict the target signal. This design inherits the high-frequency capacity of hybrid representations while respecting spherical geometry through the grid topology and interpolation scheme.

## Results

HNeR-S achieves the best weighted PSNR across all nine task-dataset combinations in Table 1. On geopotential regression, it reaches 66.83 dB vs. the best baseline WIRE at 65.24 dB; on temperature regression, 60.87 dB vs. WIRE's 56.18 dB. For CMB regression, HNeR-S attains 38.08 dB, a substantial 8.17 dB margin over the next-best WIRE (29.91 dB). In super-resolution, the advantage is even more pronounced: e.g., 67.51 dB on Geo. ×2 SR vs. 59.09 dB for ReLU+P.E., and 28.84 dB on CMB ×2 SR vs. 26.24 dB for WIRE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HNeR-S outperforms all six coordinate-based neural representation baselines (ReLU+P.E./NeRF, SIREN, SHINR, WIRE, FFN, HashGrid) on every task and dataset. The largest margins appear on CMB regression, where HNeR-S (38.08 dB) exceeds WIRE (29.91 dB) by over 8 dB, and on Geo. ×2 super-resolution, where it beats ReLU+P.E. (59.09 dB) by 8.4 dB. Among baselines, WIRE (Gabor wavelet activations) is consistently the strongest competitor on CMB and temperature tasks, while ReLU+P.E. (NeRF-style sinusoidal encoding) leads on geopotential super-resolution. HashGrid (multiresolution hash encoding) performs worst across nearly all settings, confirming that Euclidean hash grids are ill-suited for spherical data. For compression (Table 4), HNeR-S as a spatial compressor achieves WRMSE of 144.8 at 0.057 BPP, comparable to the spatiotemporal FFN-T (73.6 at 0.111 BPP) while using fewer bits, and substantially outperforms SIREN (669.5) and WIRE (2617.2) at similar BPP ranges.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the hybrid neural representation paradigm—learnable feature-grid parameters combined with bilinear interpolation and an MLP decoder, established for Euclidean data by Müller et al. (HashGrid) and Martel et al. (ACORN)—to the spherical domain. Prior CNR work on spherical signals (SHINR, spherical-harmonic encodings, Cartesian-lifted MLPs) relies solely on coordinate-based positional encodings and lacks the expressive power of feature grids. The key technical departure is designing spherical feature-grids (equirectangular for weather data, HEALPix for CMB data) with a bilinear spherical interpolation that respects the non-Euclidean geometry, pole singularity, and periodicity of the sphere, rather than naively transplanting Euclidean voxel grids.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Spherical signals underpin critical scientific applications—global weather forecasting and CMB cosmology—where high-fidelity interpolation and super-resolution are essential but under-served by existing CNR methods. The paper demonstrates that a relatively simple architectural change (spherical feature grids + bilinear interpolation) yields large gains over all coordinate-based baselines, and notably operates at full Planck resolution (N_side=2048, ~50M points) where prior CMB works typically downgrade to N_side=64. Open questions include whether adaptive or geodesic-aware interpolation could further improve performance, and whether the approach extends to other non-Euclidean manifolds beyond the sphere.
