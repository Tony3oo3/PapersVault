## Problem

State-of-the-art LLM compression methods (quantization, pruning) are evaluated almost exclusively on benign performance metrics like perplexity and MMLU, leaving their impact on safety-critical trustworthiness dimensions—fairness, ethics, toxicity, privacy, adversarial robustness—largely unexplored. This paper asks: which compression method and rate best preserves (or even improves) trustworthiness while achieving efficiency, and what hidden risks emerge at extreme compression rates that benign benchmarks cannot reveal?

## Input / Output

The evaluation takes three 13b pre-trained LLMs (LLAMA2 13b, LLAMA2 13b Chat, Vicuna 13b Chat) and applies training-free compression: GPTQ or AWQ quantization at 3/4/8-bit precision, or 2:4 semi-structured pruning via SparseGPT, Wanda, or magnitude-based selection, each calibrated on 128 samples from C4. Outputs are 0–100 normalized scores across eight DecodingTrust dimensions (Stereotype, Privacy, Toxicity, Fairness, AdvGLUE++, OOD Robustness, Adversarial Demonstration, Ethics), MMLU accuracy over 57 tasks, and per-scenario refusal rates. The authors modify DecodingTrust's refusal handling—counting refusals as negative responses in Ethics and including all predictions in Fairness—to reduce bias from the high refusal rates typical of open-source 13b models.

## Method

The paper conducts the first systematic empirical study of how post-training compression affects multi-dimensional trustworthiness, structured around three questions: (1) pre-training a 7b model vs. compressing a 13b model, (2) the optimal compression rate for trustworthiness, and (3) risks at extreme 3-bit compression. They evaluate five SoTA compression methods across three diverse 13b models on DecodingTrust (33 test cases, 8 dimensions) plus MMLU, with repeated runs over random calibration seeds to quantify variance. Key mechanistic findings include: 4-bit quantization acts as a sweet spot that preserves all trust dimensions within 5 points and can unexpectedly improve Fairness and Ethics (e.g., Ethics jumps from 54.1 to 76.3 with GPTQ 4-bit) by activating latent moral knowledge; 3-bit GPTQ catastrophically degrades Toxicity and OOD robustness (up to 50-point drops) due to loss of instruction-following ability, while AWQ's activation-aware scaling mitigates this; and 2:4 pruning produces inconsistent, model-dependent trust degradation unlike the more predictable quantization. The authors further dissect sub-scenarios (evasion, jailbreak, few-shot fairness, PII leakage) to trace the causal mechanisms behind compression-induced trust shifts.

## Results

4-bit GPTQ quantization of LLAMA2 13b Chat preserves MMLU within 1.5 points (53.6→52.1) while unexpectedly improving Ethics from 62.3 to 76.2 and Fairness from 82.2 to 88.0. 8-bit quantization incurs negligible drops (<3 points) across all 8 trust dimensions. At 3-bit, GPTQ suffers catastrophic trustworthiness degradation (up to ~50-point drops in Toxicity and ~30-point in OOD), while AWQ 3-bit remains more stable but still shows significant drops in Adv Demo and Fairness. Pruning at 50% (2:4 sparsity) causes >5-point losses on at least 3 trust dimensions, making it less reliable than quantization at the same compression rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPTQ, AWQ is substantially more reliable at extreme 3-bit compression: AWQ 3-bit drops MMLU by only ~3 points while GPTQ 3-bit drops ~8 points, and GPTQ 3-bit suffers catastrophic ~50-point and ~30-point drops in Toxicity and OOD respectively, whereas AWQ avoids these failures. Against pruning methods (SparseGPT, Wanda, Magnitude at 2:4 sparsity), quantization is consistently more reliable: pruning causes >5-point trustworthiness losses on at least 3 dimensions with inconsistent results across models, while 4-bit quantization stays within 5 points on all dimensions. Compared to pre-trained 7b models, 4-bit compressed 13b models are more efficient and more accurate on MMLU, but 7b models outperform in 3-4 trust dimensions (OOD, AdvGLUE++, Fairness) by over 5 points. The 8-bit quantized 13b model is nearly indistinguishable from the dense 13b model across all metrics (<3-point difference), confirming that moderate quantization is a safe compression choice. Across all three base models (LLAMA2 13b, LLAMA2 13b Chat, Vicuna 13b Chat), the 4-bit quantization-induced gains in Ethics and Fairness are general, not model-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-established lines of work: post-training LLM compression (GPTQ, AWQ, SparseGPT, Wanda, magnitude pruning) and LLM trustworthiness evaluation (DecodingTrust, TrustGPT, TrustLLM). Prior compression evaluations (Jaiswal et al., 2023a; Namburi et al., 2023) focused on benign performance or parametric knowledge, while prior trustworthiness benchmarks evaluated only dense, uncompressed models. This work is the first to systematically apply a multi-dimensional trustworthiness benchmark (8 dimensions) to a broad spectrum of compressed models, bridging the gap between efficiency-focused and safety-focused evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides actionable guidance for practitioners deploying compressed LLMs: 4-bit quantization is a safe sweet spot that preserves (and sometimes improves) trustworthiness, while 3-bit GPTQ poses genuine safety risks invisible to standard MMLU benchmarks. Limitations include evaluation on only three 13b-parameter models, a restricted set of five post-training methods, and N:M-only pruning patterns, leaving open whether findings generalize to other architectures, training-based compression, or newer model generations. The non-monotonic trust improvement at moderate quantization raises the open question of whether compression can be deliberately leveraged as a lightweight alignment tool.
