## Problem

Faithfully attributing the full reasoning process of transformer models—both at the input level and across all latent neurons—while maintaining computational efficiency comparable to a single backward pass remains unsolved. Existing LRP variants fail on non-linear attention components (softmax, bilinear matrix multiplication, normalization), producing numerical instabilities or low faithfulness, while attention-rollout methods yield checkerboard artifacts and cannot explain hidden representations, and gradient-based methods suffer from severe noise in deep architectures.

## Input / Output

Given a trained transformer (LLM or ViT), an input sequence (text tokens or image patches), and a target output (predicted token or class), AttnLRP produces relevance scores for every input feature and every latent neuron at every layer in a single backward pass (or two forward + one backward with gradient checkpointing, scaling memory as O(√N_L)). Unlike input-only attribution methods, the layer-wise relevance values for intermediate neurons are a direct by-product of the propagation, enabling ranking of influential neurons and their manipulation to alter model outputs.

## Method

AttnLRP derives novel propagation rules within the Deep Taylor Decomposition framework for the three non-linear operations that standard LRP cannot handle: (1) softmax is linearized at x with a bias term (justified because softmax(0)=1/N acts as a virtual bias), yielding R_i^{l-1} = x_i(R_i^l − s_i Σ_j R_j^l); (2) bilinear matrix multiplication is decomposed by sequentially applying the ε-rule to the summation and a uniform 1/N rule (derived from both Taylor and Shapley decompositions) to each product term, giving R_ji^{l-1} = Σ_p A_ji V_ip R_jp^l / (2O_jp + ε) while preserving strict conservation; (3) LayerNorm/RMSNorm reduces to the identity rule via Taylor decomposition at the zero reference point. For Vision Transformers, the γ-rule replaces the ε-rule in linear layers to suppress gradient shattering noise, a modification not needed for LLMs due to the discrete nature of the text domain.

## Results

AttnLRP achieves the highest faithfulness score (area between perturbation curves) across all evaluated models: 6.19 on ViT-B-16/ImageNet, 2.50 on LLaMA 2-7b/IMDB, 10.93 on LLaMA 2-7b/Wikipedia, 0.96 top-1 accuracy (0.72 IoU) on Mixtral 8x7b/SQuAD v2, and 0.94 (0.84) on Flan-T5-XL/SQuAD v2. The most dramatic gain is on the MoE model Mixtral 8x7b, where AttnLRP improves top-1 accuracy by 46% over CP-LRP (0.96 vs. 0.50). On ViT-B-16, AttnLRP (6.19) narrowly edges out the enhanced CP-LRP with γ-rule (6.06) while substantially outperforming Grad×AttnRoll (2.60) and KernelSHAP (4.71).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AttnLRP outperforms all nine baselines on faithfulness across every model and dataset. The closest competitor is CP-LRP (Ali et al., 2022): on Mixtral 8x7b, AttnLRP achieves 0.96 top-1 accuracy versus CP-LRP's 0.50 (a 46% relative gain), while on Flan-T5-XL the gap narrows to 0.94 vs. 0.91. On ViT-B-16/ImageNet, AttnLRP (6.19) slightly surpasses the γ-enhanced CP-LRP (6.06) and substantially beats Grad×AttnRoll (2.60) and KernelSHAP (4.71). Gradient-based methods (Input×Grad, Integrated Gradients, SmoothGrad) consistently underperform due to gradient shattering, with SmoothGrad even producing negative faithfulness scores on Wikipedia. AtMan and Grad-CAM achieve competitive plausibility on SQuAD v2 QA (0.86 and 0.82 top-1, respectively) but fail on unstructured tasks like next-word prediction and classification, where AttnLRP dominates. Attention Rollout and its gradient-weighted variant produce the weakest attributions overall, with Attn Rollout scoring near or below random on several benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP) framework and its Deep Taylor Decomposition (DTD) foundation to handle the non-linear operations unique to transformer architectures—specifically softmax, bilinear matrix multiplication, and normalization layers. Prior LRP-for-transformers attempts (Ding et al., Voita et al., Chefer et al., Ali et al.) either reused standard LRP rules that violate conservation or stopped relevance flow through softmax/normalization, sacrificing faithfulness and latent attribution. AttnLRP departs by deriving novel, DTD-grounded rules for each non-linear component that simultaneously ensure numerical stability, strict conservation, and the ability to attribute both input tokens and internal latent neurons (including query/key/value paths) in a single backward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AttnLRP is the first method to provide faithful, holistic attributions for both input and latent representations of transformers at the cost of a single backward pass, enabling practical debugging, bias detection, and concept-based explanations for models with billions of parameters. The demonstrated ability to identify and manipulate specific knowledge neurons (e.g., shifting 'Arctic' to 'Desert' by toggling one neuron) opens a path toward targeted model editing and safety interventions. Open questions remain around γ-parameter tuning for ViTs, the impact of quantization on attribution quality, and whether the softmax rule's reliance on non-vanishing gradients (mitigated by temperature scaling) generalizes to all classification settings.
