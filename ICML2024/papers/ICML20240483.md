## Problem

Deep Ensembles remain the most reliable uncertainty estimation method but require 5× training and inference cost, while alternatives like MC-Dropout or sampling-free approaches sacrifice reliability or demand architectural modifications. Iterative neural networks, which feed their own output back as input for successive refinement, are widely used for performance gains but their convergence dynamics have never been exploited as a free uncertainty signal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240483/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard input (aerial image, 2D airfoil contour, 3D triangulated mesh, or MNIST digit) plus the previous iteration's prediction as an additional input channel, producing N successive outputs y_1 through y_N. The final prediction y_N is the primary output (distance map, lift-to-drag/drag scalar, or class label), while the per-pixel, per-node, or per-sample variance across {y_1,...,y_N} yields the uncertainty estimate. No additional forward passes beyond the N iterations of a single network are needed, unlike ensembles or MC-Dropout which require 5× passes.

## Method

The key insight is that the iterative update y_i = f_Θ(x, y_{i-1}) can be interpreted as gradient ascent on log p(y_i|x), so the convergence speed of the sequence directly reflects prediction certainty: in-distribution, low-noise inputs converge quickly (low variance), while out-of-distribution or noisy inputs converge slowly or erratically (high variance). The uncertainty estimate is simply the variance U^i = Var({y_1^i, ..., y_N^i}) at each spatial location, requiring no architectural change or additional training. To ensure intermediate predictions remain well-calibrated, training minimizes the sum of losses over all N iterations rather than only the final one. The approach is demonstrated with a Recurrent U-Net (dual-gated recurrent unit in the bridge) for road delineation and iterative GMM-layer GNNs for aerodynamic shape optimization, where the uncertainty feeds a Bayesian Optimization acquisition function (UCB) to select promising shapes for simulation.

## Results

On road delineation, the iterative U-Net achieves the best accuracy across both datasets: F1=24.5 and APLS=77.21 on RoadTracer, and F1=15.4 and APLS=78.04 on Massachusetts, outperforming Deep Ensembles (F1=22.1/14.1) and MC-Dropout (F1=20.4/13.6). For uncertainty quality, the method achieves rAULC=79.27 and Pearson correlation=87.46 on Massachusetts (beating Deep Ensembles' 78.65/76.39) and rAULC=69.23/corr=74.73 on RoadTracer (comparable to Deep Ensembles' 72.19/79.42). On MNIST classification, the method reaches 99.0% accuracy with ECE=0.013, matching or exceeding Deep Ensembles (98.8%, ECE=0.018). Crucially, the method requires only 2.8× training and 2.7× inference time relative to a single model, versus 5× for Deep Ensembles.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MC-Dropout (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016), the proposed method consistently wins on both accuracy and uncertainty quality across all datasets, e.g., F1 improves from 20.4 to 24.5 on RoadTracer and rAULC from 30.18 to 69.23. Against Deep Ensembles (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017), the method achieves comparable or better uncertainty (rAULC 79.27 vs 78.65 on Massachusetts; PR-AUC 0.88 vs 0.86 on airfoils) while delivering higher accuracy (F1 24.5 vs 22.1 on RoadTracer) at roughly half the computational cost (2.8× vs 5× training time). Against the standard U-Net (Ronneberger et al., 2015), the iterative version improves F1 by 3.4 points on RoadTracer and 1.6 points on Massachusetts, with a much larger APLS gain (77.21 vs 65.04). Against Kriging (Laurenceau et al., 2010) in the aerodynamic OOD detection task, the method achieves higher ROC-AUC (0.87 vs 0.79) and PR-AUC (0.88 vs 0.78) on airfoils. Against the standard GNN baseline (Baqué et al., 2018), the iterative GNN outperforms it in both prediction accuracy (Fig. 5 left) and Bayesian optimization performance (Fig. 5 right), with the gap being most pronounced when few training samples are available.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the iterative refinement mechanism already used in recurrent U-Nets, stacked hourglass networks, and iterative GNNs for performance gains, reframing the convergence behavior of successive outputs as a free uncertainty signal. The theoretical grounding draws on denoising autoencoder reconstruction error theory (Bengio et al., Alain & Bengio), where the update step is interpreted as gradient ascent on log-probability, linking convergence speed to in-distribution confidence. It positions itself against Deep Ensembles (the gold standard but 5× cost), MC-Dropout (cheaper but less calibrated), and sampling-free methods that require architecture modifications, offering a middle ground that needs no architectural changes and only ~2.7× inference cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is that any existing iterative network immediately gains a calibrated uncertainty estimate with no retraining or architectural surgery, making it broadly deployable across segmentation, shape optimization, and other iterative-refinement tasks. The demonstration across two structurally different domains (2D aerial road delineation and 3D aerodynamic shape optimization via Bayesian Optimization) strengthens the generality claim. Open questions include how the variance-based proxy behaves when iterations do not fully converge, whether it captures aleatoric vs. epistemic uncertainty separately, and whether the approach extends to iterative architectures with more than a few iterations where variance may plateau.
