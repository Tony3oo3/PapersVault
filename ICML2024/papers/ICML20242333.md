## Problem

Linear attention Transformers offer linear-time inference but generally underperform softmax attention in language modeling, partly because existing variants rely on data-independent decay factors rather than the data-dependent gating mechanisms known to be crucial in RNNs. Additionally, current linear attention implementations are not I/O-aware, making them slower in practice than highly optimized softmax attention (FlashAttention-2) even at moderate sequence lengths (~1K–4K).

## Input / Output

The model consumes tokenized text sequences (Mistral tokenizer) and produces next-token predictions via a standard autoregressive objective. The GLA layer uses separate key (d_k = d/2) and value (d_v = d) dimensions split across H heads, with per-head LayerNorm, a Swish output gate, and a final projection back to d. Training is conducted on a 100B-token subset of SlimPajama at 340M and 1.3B parameter scales, with sequence lengths up to 24K via truncated BPTT over 2K segments.

## Method

The paper introduces FlashLinearAttention, an I/O-aware chunkwise algorithm that tiles Q, K, V blocks into SRAM to minimize HBM traffic and exploit tensor cores, offering both a memory-efficient non-materialization version and a sequence-parallel materialization version (with recomputation to control memory). It then generalizes this to Gated Linear Attention (GLA), which replaces the fixed decay with a data-dependent gate G_t = α_t^T 1, where α_t = σ(x_t W_α^1 W_α^2 + b_α)^{1/τ} uses a low-rank (d×16, 16×d_k) parameterization with temperature τ=16 to encourage slower forgetting. A key technical challenge is that the parallel form requires log-space computation for numerical stability, preventing direct use of half-precision tensor-core matmuls; the paper resolves this via secondary-level chunking, where inter-sub-chunk interactions are computed as half-precision matmuls while only the small intra-sub-chunk portion uses full-precision log-space arithmetic. A closed-form gradient formula for d log α_t (via reverse cumulative sum of q_t ⊙ dq_t − k_t ⊙ dk_t) avoids materializing all L hidden states in HBM during backpropagation.

## Results

At the 1.3B/100B-token scale, GLA achieves an average zero-shot accuracy of 51.0%, matching Transformer++ (50.9%) and outperforming Mamba (50.0%) and RetNet (48.9%). On perplexity, GLA reaches 17.22 on WikiText and 14.47 on LAMBADA, slightly behind Transformer++ (16.85/13.44) but well ahead of RetNet (18.64/17.27). On recall-intensive tasks (FDA, SWDE, SQuAD), GLA outperforms both Mamba and RetNet at both scales (e.g., 42.6 on SQuAD vs. 35.2 for Mamba at 1.3B). GLA also demonstrates strong length extrapolation, generalizing from 2K training length to over 20K test sequences with minimal perplexity degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Transformer++ (LLaMA architecture), GLA is competitive: at 1.3B scale it achieves a marginally higher average accuracy (51.0 vs. 50.9) but slightly worse perplexity (17.22 vs. 16.85 WikiText). Against RetNet, GLA consistently wins on all metrics at both scales, with the gap widening at 1.3B (e.g., 14.47 vs. 17.27 LAMBADA ppl; 51.0 vs. 48.9 avg. acc), confirming the benefit of data-dependent gating over a fixed decay. Against Mamba, GLA trades off: it wins on HellaSwag (49.8 vs. 40.1) and LAMBADA accuracy (46.9 vs. 46.2) at 1.3B, while Mamba wins on PIQA (72.2 vs. 71.8) and ARC-challenge (28.2 vs. 26.6). On recall-intensive tasks (FDA, SWDE, SQuAD), GLA outperforms both Mamba and RetNet at both scales (e.g., 50.6 vs. 41.4 on SWDE at 1.3B), attributed to its larger recurrent state and selection mechanism. On the synthetic MQAR task, GLA outperforms RetNet, Mamba, Hyena, and RWKV-4, with matrix-valued-state models collectively beating convolutional/SSM baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear attention formulation of Katharopoulos et al. (2020) by introducing a data-dependent gating mechanism (G_t = α_t^T 1) that sits between the scalar gates of Mamba-2/RetNet and the full low-rank gates of DFW, while adapting the I/O-aware tiling principles of FlashAttention (Dao et al., 2022b; Dao, 2023) to the chunkwise parallel form of linear attention. The key departure from RetNet (Sun et al., 2023a) is replacing its data-independent decay factor with a data-dependent, per-feature gate, and the key departure from Mamba (Gu & Dao, 2023) is using a matrix-multiply-compatible parameterization that enables tensor core utilization and larger hidden states. A secondary-level chunking technique is introduced to recover half-precision matmul efficiency despite the log-space computations required for numerical stability in the gated case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FlashLinearAttention implementation provides a practical, open-source building block that makes linear attention competitive with optimized softmax attention in wall-clock time, removing a key engineering barrier to adopting subquadratic architectures. The competitive performance against LLaMA-architecture Transformers and Mamba at 340M/1.3B scale, combined with strong length generalization (2K→20K+), suggests GLA is a viable alternative for long-context modeling. However, the authors acknowledge that experiments are limited to moderate scale (≤1.3B params, ≤100B tokens), and it remains open whether the throughput and recall advantages over Mamba persist at larger scales (>7B) where tensor parallelism and other system-level optimizations become critical. The paper also notes the potential for applying GLA to other modalities with long-range dependencies.
