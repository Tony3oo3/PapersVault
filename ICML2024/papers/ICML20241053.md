## Problem

Existing online test-time adaptation (OTTA) methods rely on regularization toward the source model to prevent catastrophic forgetting, but this continuous source-model dependency prevents them from accumulating and leveraging target domain information across multiple domains. As a result, performance plateaus or degrades as the number of target domains increases, because newly learned target weights from prior domains are neither incorporated nor effectively utilized for subsequent adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241053/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a stream of unlabeled target images (batch size 64) and a pre-trained source model (ViT, Swin Transformer, or data2vec base variants), with no access to source data or ground-truth labels. It produces a point-estimated latent weight vector φ̂_t via plug-in approximation, which is injected back into the SGD-based OTTA procedure as the initialization for the next time step. Notably, only model weights are stored (no target data), inherently safeguarding privacy, and the additional computation is minimal since the gradient g_t is already computed by the SGD optimizer.

## Method

SLWI introduces a latent weight φ_t that is recursively updated via a Kalman-filter-style Bayesian filtering framework, where the transition model (Eq. 5) pulls the latent weight toward the source weight with parameter a, and the emission model (Eq. 6) tracks the noisy target weight observations with a backtracking coefficient c_t. A stationary score (Eq. 13), computed from the posterior predictive distribution of target weights, detects local non-stationarity (outlier weight changes) and triggers backtracking by reducing c_t to 1/α, thereby pulling the observation back toward the predicted mean and preventing error propagation from unreliable unsupervised updates. The refined latent posterior is transferred to the OTTA procedure through a plug-in approximation (Eq. 15) using a time-independent Kalman gain b, replacing the standard source-model regularization term. This design allows SLWI to be seamlessly integrated with any existing OTTA objective (TENT, EATA, ROID) with only 2–5% additional computational overhead.

## Results

On ImageNet-C (covariate shifts, 15 corruption domains), SLWI achieves a 42.5% average error rate, outperforming the strongest baseline ROID (44.8%) by 2.3 percentage points. On D109 (natural distribution shifts across 5 domains), SLWI reaches 41.8% average error versus ROID's 44.2% (2.4% improvement). Under the most challenging label-shift setting (γ=0.0) on ImageNet-C, SLWI attains 13.6% average error compared to ROID's 17.4%, a 3.8% gain. Across all three backbone architectures (ViT, Swin, D2V), SLWI consistently outperforms ROID by 0.5–2.9% depending on scenario.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLWI outperforms all six baselines across every evaluated scenario and dataset. Against the strongest competitor ROID (weight ensembling), SLWI improves average error by 2.3% on ImageNet-C covariate shifts, 2.4% on D109 natural shifts, and 3.8% under the most extreme label shift (γ=0.0). Against TENT (entropy minimization), the gap is 10.8% on ImageNet-C, and against LAME (parameter-free adaptation) it reaches 28.2%, with LAME suffering catastrophic degradation on blur and weather corruptions. RoTTA, SAR, and EATA all cluster around 51–53% average error on ImageNet-C, while SLWI achieves 42.5%, indicating that the Bayesian filtering and backtracking mechanism provides substantially better target-domain information accumulation than simple regularization or reset strategies. The improvement is consistent across ViT, Swin, and D2V backbones (Table 5), and SLWI adds only 2.0–5.0% computational overhead when integrated with existing OTTA methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OTTA framework established by TENT (entropy minimization) and the source-regularization strategies of EATA, SAR, and ROID, which all rely on explicit regularization terms (Fisher information, weight ensembling, or reset mechanisms) to anchor target weights to the source model. SLWI departs from this lineage by replacing the fixed regularization with a Bayesian filtering (Kalman filter) state-space model that tracks a latent weight accumulating information from both source and target domains over time. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a backtracking mechanism that detects non-stationary (outlier) target weights via a normalized log-posterior predictive score and pulls them back toward the expected trajectory, rather than simply constraining them to the source.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SLWI addresses a fundamental limitation of existing OTTA methods: they only reference the immediately previous time step's weights and the fixed source weights, failing to accumulate and refine target-domain information across the adaptation sequence. The Bayesian filtering formulation provides a principled, noise-robust way to integrate this information, and the plug-in approximation keeps computational overhead to 2–5%, making it a practical drop-in for existing OTTA pipelines. The main open question is the reliance on manually tuned hyperparameters (ζ, α) for backtracking; the authors acknowledge this and plan to explore implicit identification. Broader implications include whether the state-space modeling of weight dynamics can be extended to other continual or streaming adaptation settings beyond OTTA.
