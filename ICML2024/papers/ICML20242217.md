## Problem

Applying RL to complex multi-step reasoning faces a fundamental tension: outcome supervision provides only sparse final-answer rewards, making it difficult for the model to identify which intermediate reasoning steps caused errors, while process supervision offers dense step-level signals but requires expensive manual annotation of every reasoning step. This paper asks whether the benefits of process supervision can be achieved using only outcome supervision, without any per-step annotations or reward model training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242217/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reasoning problem (text prompt) paired with at least one correct demonstration (a full reasoning path to the golden answer) as training input. During RL, the model does not always start from the prompt; instead, it begins generation from an intermediate state sampled within the demonstration, producing only the remaining portion of the reasoning chain. The output is a step-by-step reasoning chain in either natural language (CoT) or executable Python code (P-CoT), terminated by a final answer that is checked against the golden label to compute the outcome reward.

## Method

R³ constructs a reverse curriculum by sampling M intermediate states (typically 5–6, at line breaks or uniformly) from each correct demonstration and using them as starting points for RL rollouts. The model first learns to complete reasoning from states near the end of the demonstration (short, easy rollouts) and progressively slides the start state toward the beginning, so that at each stage the model faces a tractable exploration problem where it has already learned most of the remaining steps. Crucially, only an outcome-based reward function (correct/incorrect final answer, with a small partial reward ε for numeric-but-wrong answers) is used, yet the shortened rollouts effectively convert this sparse signal into approximately step-level supervision. To prevent overfitting to easy stages and ensure smooth generalization, R³ mixes all curriculum stages into a single training set rather than training them sequentially. The policy is optimized with PPO, a KL penalty against the SFT-initialized policy, and GAE for advantage estimation.

## Results

On eight CoT reasoning tasks (GSM8K, SVAMP, BGQA-main, BGQA-conflict, MNLI, SNLI, race@High, race@Middle), R³ achieves an average accuracy of 65.62%, outperforming SFT (60.19%) by 5.4 points and the RL baseline (61.48%) by 4.1 points. On program-based reasoning (P-CoT) on GSM8K, Codellama-7B+R³ reaches 74.2%, exceeding SFT by 11.4 points and RL by 4.2 points. The method also delivers more stable training dynamics, with smoother reward curves and consistent test-set improvements compared to vanilla RL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On P-CoT GSM8K, Codellama-7B+R³ (74.2%) surpasses MAmmoTH-Coder-7B (59.4%) by 14.8 points and Tora-7B (68.8%) by 5.4 points, both of which rely on large-scale extra annotated data (260k and 16k samples respectively). It also beats Tora-code-7B (72.6%) by 1.6 points and Codex (71.6%) by 2.6 points, while remaining competitive with GPT-3.5-Turbo (78.0%) at a fraction of the model size. Against GPT-4 (97.0%), R³ still trails by 22.8 points, reflecting the gap between 7B open models and the largest closed-source system. On the CoT reasoning suite, R³ consistently wins over the SFT and RL baselines across all eight tasks, with the largest gains on MNLI (+6.9 over SFT) and race@High (+8.0 over SFT), confirming that the reverse curriculum mechanism provides effective step-level supervision without requiring process annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants reverse curriculum learning—a technique well-established in robotics and game-playing RL (Florensa et al., 2017; Salimans & Chen, 2018)—into the LLM reasoning setting, combining it with the exploration-from-demonstration paradigm (Subramanian et al., 2016). Its key conceptual contribution is showing that by progressively sliding the exploration start state from the end of a correct demonstration back to its beginning, outcome supervision (which requires only a final-answer reward) can approximate the dense, step-level signals of process supervision without any per-step annotation. The method is built on PPO with KL regularization, consistent with the standard RLHF pipeline, and positions itself as a practical middle ground between the two dominant supervision paradigms in LLM reasoning (outcome vs. process).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

R³ offers a low-cost path to step-level reasoning supervision that avoids the expensive human annotation required by process supervision, making it attractive for practitioners who have access to correct demonstrations but not step-by-step labels. The finding that a 7B model with R³ approaches GPT-3.5-Turbo on program-based math reasoning (74.2 vs. 78.0) suggests the method could narrow the gap between open and closed models at modest scale. However, the method is limited to settings where at least one correct demonstration is available per problem, and all experiments are conducted at 7B scale, leaving open whether the curriculum benefits persist or diminish at larger model sizes where exploration is already easier. The authors also note that data scale and diversity remain important, particularly for math tasks.
