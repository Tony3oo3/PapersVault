## Problem

Existing TEE-based model obfuscation schemes for on-device DNN inference share a common vulnerability called 'fragility of randomness': they either modify a fixed small subset of weights (creating detectable distributional anomalies) or apply lightweight shuffle-and-scale transformations that preserve the statistical fingerprint of convolution kernels. This allows a statistical-analysis adversary to recover approximately 97% of the secret model's functionality, rendering the obfuscation nearly as weak as no protection at all.

## Input / Output

The system takes a pre-trained CNN (e.g., AlexNet, ResNet18/50, VGG16) as the secret model and standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 96×96 for STL-10) as inference inputs. During offline preprocessing, the TEE generates an obfuscated weight matrix and recovery parameters (inverse transformation matrices and a permutation) that remain confined within the TEE. At inference time, the input image is masked with a one-time padding vector before offloading to the GPU; the GPU returns the obfuscated layer output, which the TEE un-masks and applies the inverse transformation to recover the true classification result.

## Method

GROUPCOVER introduces two mechanisms to achieve sufficient randomization at the convolution-kernel level. First, an inverse clustering algorithm (Algorithm 1) groups g=4 kernels with the greatest pairwise cosine-similarity distance into each cluster, ensuring that the most statistically distinguishable kernels are mixed together. Second, within each cluster, a randomly generated 4×4 invertible matrix A performs a linear combination of the four kernels (mutual covering), followed by a global permutation of the weight matrix, so that no individual kernel retains its original statistical signature. During online inference, the GPU computes Conv(W′, X+R) on the obfuscated weights with one-time padding R, and the TEE reconstructs the true output via Y = A⁻¹ P_π⁻¹ (Y′ − Conv(W′,R)), verified with Freivalds' algorithm. The entire preprocessing involves only addition and subtraction operations, taking seconds rather than hours, and introduces less than 19% throughput overhead and ~0.01% accuracy loss relative to the unprotected model.

## Results

Under a statistical-analysis-based model-stealing attack, GROUPCOVER limits the adversary's recovered accuracy to 1.03× the black-box baseline (1.00×), whereas existing obfuscation schemes (NNSplitter, Magnitude, SOTER, ShadowNet) allow the adversary to recover 2.82×–3.09× the black-box baseline, approaching the no-shield level (3.16×). In terms of efficiency, GROUPCOVER achieves 5,556 samples/sec throughput on CIFAR-100 (AlexNet), losing only 19% compared to Slalom (4,437 samples/sec) while adding model IP protection. Accuracy loss is negligible at an average of 0.013% across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and STL-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GROUPCOVER decisively outperforms all four existing TEE-based obfuscation schemes on the model-stealing attack metric: NNSplitter (3.09×), Magnitude (2.91×), SOTER (2.90×), and ShadowNet (2.82×) all allow the adversary to recover roughly 97% of the secret model's functionality, while GROUPCOVER limits recovery to 1.03× the black-box baseline (Orekondy et al., 2019), a ~3× improvement. Against the no-shield baseline (3.16×), GROUPCOVER reduces attack effectiveness by over 3×. On efficiency, GROUPCOVER (5,556 samples/sec on C100/AlexNet) outperforms Slalom (4,437 samples/sec) by ~25% while adding model IP protection that Slalom lacks; Slalom leaves model weights fully exposed to the GPU. In terms of accuracy, GROUPCOVER matches the no-shield baseline within 0.013% average loss, whereas schemes like TEESlice require model retraining and introduce 4–6× inference slowdown. The Wilcoxon signed-rank test (p=0.10) confirms no statistically significant difference between GROUPCOVER and the black-box defense across varying query budgets (10–300 per class).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the TEE+GPU secure inference paradigm established by Slalom (ICLR 2019) and Darknight (MICRO 2021), which offload linear computations to untrusted GPUs while keeping sensitive data in the TEE. It specifically targets the model obfuscation sub-line (NNSplitter, Magnitude, SOTER, ShadowNet) that was added to protect model IP during GPU outsourcing. The key departure is identifying a shared vulnerability—'fragility of randomness'—across all four prior obfuscation schemes and proposing inverse clustering with mutual kernel covering as a fundamentally different obfuscation strategy, rather than the fixed-strategy or per-kernel shuffle/scale approaches used previously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that all four prior obfuscation schemes (published at ICML, TDSC, ATC, S&P) leak ~97% of the secret model under a practical statistical attack is a significant finding for the TEE-based model protection community, as it undermines the security guarantees these systems were designed to provide. GROUPCOVER's achievement of near black-box security with only 19% overhead and negligible accuracy loss makes it a practical drop-in replacement. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-CNN architectures (transformers, ViTs), how it interacts with TEE side-channel attacks explicitly excluded from the threat model, and whether the 67.3% communication overhead can be reduced with hardware-level TEE-GPU interconnects.
