## Problem

GNNs are poor at estimating predictive uncertainty, which limits deployment in safety-critical applications. Existing graph uncertainty methods either require intractable Bayesian posteriors or pre-specify fixed-form prior distributions for posterior updates, compromising both flexibility and empirical performance. This paper tackles the gap by formulating uncertainty estimation as a stochastic dynamical system on graphs, eliminating the need for any specific prior form.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241226/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{|V|×d} and the graph adjacency structure, producing node class predictions alongside explicit aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty estimates. Uncertainty is obtained by drawing multiple samples from the final representation distribution P(H(T)) of the stochastic diffusion process; each sample yields a distinct node embedding, from which aleatoric uncertainty is the expected entropy of class predictions and epistemic uncertainty is the variance of the final embeddings. The stochastic forcing network incorporates the graph Laplacian with added self-connections (L+I) so that epistemic uncertainty propagates along edges under the homophily assumption.

## Method

GNSD models node representation evolution as a graph stochastic diffusion equation dH(t) = f(H(t))dt + g(H(t))dW(t), where the drift term f is a continuous-depth GNN with multi-head self-attention (analogous to GRAND) ensuring accurate prediction, and the stochastic forcing term g captures epistemic uncertainty by parameterizing the variance of a Q-Wiener process defined on the graph domain. The key novelty is a new Q-Wiener process on graphs derived via graph Fourier transform, for which the authors provide a first-order Taylor approximation (√Λ ≈ I + Λ) that replaces costly eigendecomposition with a simple multiplication by L+I. Training uses a distributional uncertainty loss (expected cross-entropy over sampled final representations) rather than point-estimate classification loss. The stochastic forcing network's use of the graph Laplacian with self-connections gives it two properties: transitivity (connected nodes share similar epistemic uncertainty) and computational efficiency (no dense matrix operations per discretization step). The paper proves existence and uniqueness of the mild solution under Lipschitz conditions on g and semigroup generation by the drift operator.

## Results

On Amazon-Computers OOD detection (Table 1), GNSD achieves AUROC of 94.06 (label leave-out) and 95.95 (feature perturbation), outperforming the previous SOTA GNNsafe (90.50 / 89.46) and GPN (88.76 / 87.92). GNSD reduces FPR95 by 48.3% on average across both OOD scenarios while maintaining competitive in-distribution accuracy (ID ACC 89.64 / 87.99). For misclassification detection (Table 2), GNSD achieves AUROC of 83.08, 88.04, and 76.04 on Amazon-Computers, Cora, and CiteSeer respectively, improving over GPN by 6.5% average AUROC and reducing FPR95 by 12.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GNSD outperforms all 12 baselines across both OOD detection scenarios on Amazon-Computers. Against the strongest OOD detection baseline GNNsafe (Wu et al., 2023), GNSD improves AUROC by 3.56 points (94.06 vs 90.50) and reduces FPR95 by 16.78 points (31.47 vs 48.25) under label leave-out, and by 6.49 AUROC and 59.93 FPR95 under feature perturbation. Against the SOTA uncertainty estimation method GPN (Stadler et al., 2021), GNSD gains 5.30 AUROC and reduces FPR95 by 10.61 points on label leave-out. For misclassification detection, GNSD beats GPN by 2.85 AUROC on Amazon-Computers (83.08 vs 80.23) and by 11.90 AUROC on Cora (88.04 vs 76.14), while also improving AUPR err by up to 14.0% on average. Standard GNN baselines (GCN, GAT, GRAND, GREAD) and ensemble/Bayesian methods (GCN-Ensemble, BGCN) consistently underperform GNSD by 5–15 AUROC points, confirming that the stochastic diffusion formulation provides substantially better uncertainty quantification than deterministic or ensemble approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graph diffusion PDE line (GRAND, GREAD) by introducing a stochastic forcing term driven by a Q-Wiener process, transforming the deterministic diffusion equation into a graph stochastic differential equation. The Q-Wiener process is originally defined in separable Hilbert spaces (Lord et al., 2014); here it is novelly extended to graph domain via graph Fourier transform, with a practical first-order Taylor approximation of √Λ that avoids eigendecomposition. Compared to prior graph uncertainty methods (GPN, GKDE, BGCN, ensembles), GNSD departs from the requirement of pre-specified prior distributions by letting the stochastic process itself encode epistemic uncertainty, while the drift network retains the continuous-depth GNN structure from GRAND for accurate prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SPDE-based formulation offers a principled, prior-free route to uncertainty estimation on graphs that naturally captures both aleatoric and epistemic components through a single stochastic process, avoiding the computational overhead of ensembles or the rigidity of fixed-form posteriors. The graph-domain Q-Wiener process and its efficient approximation are reusable mathematical tools beyond this specific architecture. Open questions include scalability to very large graphs (OGBN-Arxiv already shows memory issues with exact sampling), applicability under heterophily (the current design assumes graph homophily), and whether the first-order Taylor approximation degrades on graphs with highly non-uniform spectral properties.
