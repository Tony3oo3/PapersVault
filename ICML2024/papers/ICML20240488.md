## Problem

Existing post-training quantization methods for LLMs (GPTQ, SpQR, QuIP/QuIP#) suffer significant accuracy degradation at extreme compression (2–3 bits per parameter), and none achieve Pareto-optimality below 3 bits—meaning a smaller model at higher bitwidth often outperforms a larger model at 2 bits. The paper asks whether multi-codebook quantization techniques from information retrieval can be adapted to close this gap and make extreme LLM compression practically competitive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240488/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM linear-layer weight matrices (W ∈ R^{d_out × d_in}) together with calibration input activations (X ∈ R^{d_in × n} from RedPajama, 4096-token sequences). The output is a homogeneous quantized representation per layer: M learned codebooks (each 2^B vectors of dimension g), discrete one-hot codes selecting one vector per codebook per weight group, and a single per-output-unit scale—totaling M·B bits per group of g weights plus small codebook/scale overhead, with no separate sparse outlier storage.

## Method

AQLM extends classic Additive Quantization by (1) reformulating the code-selection objective as a MAP inference problem in a fully-connected discrete MRF whose unary and pairwise potentials are weighted by the calibration Gram matrix XX^T, so that codes are chosen to minimize layer-output MSE rather than raw weight error; codes are optimized via beam search while codebooks are updated with Adam on the same MSE. (2) After layer-wise quantization, a block-level fine-tuning phase backpropagates through the entire transformer block (4–8 linear layers) to jointly adjust codebooks, scales, and non-quantized parameters (RMSNorm) against the original block outputs, compensating for cross-layer error interactions. Initialization uses residual K-means to seed both codes and codebooks, and the overall procedure alternates beam-search code updates and Adam codebook updates until convergence, followed by the block fine-tuning step.

## Results

AQLM achieves the lowest perplexity across all model sizes in the extreme 2-bit compression regime: on LLAMA 2 7B/13B/70B at ~2 bits, AQLM reaches WikiText-2 PPL of 6.59/5.60/3.94, compared to 8.22/6.06/4.16 for QuIP# and 13.48/5.90 for QuIP on 13B/70B. At 3 bits, AQLM achieves WikiText-2 PPL of 5.46/4.82/3.36 on 7B/13B/70B, outperforming GPTQ (8.06/5.85/4.40) and SpQR (6.20/5.28/3.85). AQLM is the first method to achieve Pareto-optimality below 3 bits per parameter, with the optimal bitwidth around 2.5 bits for 13B models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AQLM outperforms QuIP# (Tseng et al., 2024) across all model sizes at 2-bit: WikiText-2 PPL of 6.59 vs 8.22 on 7B (−1.63), 5.60 vs 6.06 on 13B (−0.46), and 3.94 vs 4.16 on 70B (−0.22), with the largest gap on the smallest model. Against QuIP (Chee et al., 2023), AQLM's advantage is dramatic at 2-bit: 5.60 vs 13.48 PPL on 13B and 3.94 vs 5.90 on 70B, indicating QuIP's fixed-lattice approach degrades severely at extreme compression. At 3-bit, AQLM beats GPTQ (Frantar et al., 2022a) by 2.60/1.03/1.04 PPL on 7B/13B/70B respectively, and SpQR (Dettmers et al., 2023b) by 0.74/0.46/0.49 PPL. On Mixtral 8×7B at 2-bit, AQLM also edges out QuIP# (4.61 vs 4.75 Wiki2 PPL; 67.68 vs 66.34 avg accuracy), with a +3-point gain on ARC-Challenge. After end-to-end fine-tuning, AQLM★ and QuIP#★ reach comparable accuracy, with AQLM★ slightly ahead on 7B (6.14 vs 6.19 Wiki2 PPL) and 13B (5.33 vs 5.35).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Additive Quantization (AQ) from the approximate nearest neighbor search literature (Babenko & Lempitsky 2014; Martinez et al. 2016, 2018) to LLM weight compression, introducing two key departures from both the ANN and LLM quantization lineages: (1) the MAP-MRF optimization is made input-adaptive by incorporating calibration activations, and (2) codebooks are jointly fine-tuned across transformer blocks rather than optimized per-layer. It contrasts with prior LLM quantization methods (GPTQ, SpQR, QuIP/QuIP#) that use direct rounding, sparse outlier handling, or fixed lattice codebooks, instead learning additive codebooks end-to-end on calibration data. The paper is the first to successfully scale multi-codebook quantization to billion-parameter LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AQLM is the first method to achieve Pareto optimality below 3 bits per parameter, a regime where prior approaches were dominated by simply using a smaller model at higher bit-widths. The practical impact is significant: quantized models can match or exceed FP16 inference speed (up to 4× on CPU) while reducing memory by up to 8×, enabling accurate LLM execution on commodity hardware. Limitations include substantially longer calibration time than GPTQ/RTN (days for 70B models) and the fact that end-to-end fine-tuning (borrowed from QuIP#) is needed for the best 2-bit results. Open directions include more systematic fine-tuning strategies and generalization to other modalities or attention-cache compression.
