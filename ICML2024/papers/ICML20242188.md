## Problem

Despite the widespread use of stochastic natural gradient variational inference (NGVI) across probabilistic models, no non-asymptotic convergence rate existed for the stochastic setting, even for simple conjugate likelihoods. Existing analyses relied on assumptions—such as the Fisher information matrix being uniformly bounded below or the KL divergence being strongly convex—that provably fail in practice, leaving the practical convergence behavior of NGVI theoretically unexplained.

## Input / Output

The method takes a prior distribution, a likelihood model (conjugate or non-conjugate), and training data (features and labels) as input, and produces an approximate posterior distribution parameterized by the expectation parameter of an exponential family (e.g., a Gaussian). A critical practical constraint is that the stochastic gradient must respect the natural parameter domain (e.g., the covariance component must remain negative definite), which the paper shows is guaranteed by the Bonnet-Price gradient estimator but not by standard automatic differentiation. The convergence guarantee applies to a weighted average of iterates with step size γ_t = 2/(2+t), yielding an O(1/T) bound on the KL divergence to the true posterior.

## Method

The paper establishes the first O(1/T) non-asymptotic convergence rate for stochastic NGVI by proving an exact equivalence between natural gradient descent and mirror descent in the expectation parameter space, then showing that for conjugate likelihoods the negative ELBO is simultaneously 1-smooth and 1-strongly convex relative to the convex conjugate A* of the log-partition function. This relative well-conditionedness, combined with a norm-independent gradient variance definition (necessary because A* is not strongly convex w.r.t. any norm), enables a clean convergence proof via stochastic mirror descent techniques. The rate is independent of the objective's condition number and the initialization-to-optimum distance, with the constant depending only on the stochastic gradient variance. For non-conjugate likelihoods, the paper shows the ELBO is non-convex in the expectation parameter even for log-concave likelihoods (via logistic and Poisson regression counterexamples), implying that an O(1/T) rate would require new structural properties like the Polyak-Lojasiewicz inequality.

## Results

The paper proves the first O(1/T) non-asymptotic convergence rate for stochastic NGVI with conjugate likelihoods: E[ℓ(ω̄_{T+1})] − min ℓ(ω) ≤ V/(T+2), where V is the stochastic gradient variance. On the Bike dataset (n=17,389) for Bayesian linear regression, both SGD and stochastic NGD exhibit O(1/T) convergence in KL divergence to the true posterior on a log-log scale, confirming the theoretical rate. However, stochastic NGD converges dramatically faster in early iterations—SGD requires thousands of iterations to match the progress NGD makes in its first few steps—indicating a substantially better constant factor in the big-O bound. For non-conjugate likelihoods (Mushroom, n=8124; MNIST, n≈13,000), stochastic NGD remains faster than SGD but the improvement is less pronounced.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is stochastic gradient descent (SGD), i.e., black-box variational inference (Ranganath et al., 2014). On the conjugate Bayesian linear regression task (Bike dataset), both methods achieve the same O(1/T) asymptotic rate, but stochastic NGD has a dramatically better constant: it reaches a low KL divergence in just a handful of iterations while SGD needs thousands to catch up. This is consistent with the theory, since the NGD convergence rate in Theorem 1 is independent of the objective's condition number and the initialization-to-optimum distance, whereas SGD's rate depends on both. On non-conjugate Bayesian logistic regression (Mushroom and MNIST), stochastic NGD still outperforms SGD in convergence speed, but the gap is narrower, consistent with the paper's finding that the ELBO in the expectation parameter is non-convex for non-conjugate likelihoods. The paper also notes that the Price-theorem stochastic gradient (Eq. 11) is a higher-quality estimator than the reparameterization trick, yielding lower variance and further improving NGD's practical speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the NGD = Mirror Descent equivalence established by Raskutti & Mukherjee (2015) and the stochastic mirror descent convergence theory for relatively smooth/strongly convex functions by Lu et al. (2018) and Hanzely & Richtárik (2021), applying them to the exponential family setting where the distance generating function A* is non-strongly convex and non-smooth. It departs from prior convergence arguments (Hoffman et al., 2013; Khan et al., 2016) that relied on assumptions provably violated in practice, instead proving the first O(1/T) rate under minimal, verifiable conditions. The non-convexity result for non-conjugate likelihoods contrasts with Domke (2020), who showed convexity of the ELBO under the mean-square-root parameterization, revealing that the canonical NGD parameterization (expectation parameter) fundamentally changes the optimization landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(1/T) rate for conjugate likelihoods, being independent of the condition number and initialization distance, provides the first clean theoretical guarantee for stochastic NGVI applicable to practical settings like SVGP and large-scale Bayesian linear regression. The non-convexity finding for non-conjugate likelihoods explains a long-standing gap in the literature: proving an O(1/T) rate in that setting would require new structural properties of the ELBO (e.g., Polyak-Lojasiewicz inequality) that have not yet been established. This reframes the open question from 'why hasn't anyone proved convergence?' to 'what new ELBO properties are needed?', giving the community a concrete direction for future theoretical work.
