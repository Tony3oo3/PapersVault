## Problem

Vision large language models (VLLMs) are substantially more vulnerable to jailbreak attacks than their underlying LLMs, because vision-language instruction fine-tuning causes the LLM to forget its prior safety alignment, and the fine-tuning data itself contains harmful content. No existing safety fine-tuning dataset or method was available specifically for VLLMs, making it impossible to restore or maintain safety alignment after multi-modal training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242604/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image paired with a text instruction as input and produces a text response that is either a helpful answer (for safe queries) or a refusal with an explanation of why the request is inappropriate (for harmful queries). The VLGuard dataset covers 4 main harm categories (Privacy, Risky Behavior, Deception, Discrimination) with 9 subcategories, and is structured so that safe images are paired with both a safe and an unsafe instruction, while harmful images receive a single instruction requiring refusal. The test set is partitioned into Safe-Safe (helpfulness), Safe-Unsafe (text-side safety), and Unsafe (vision-side safety) subsets for multi-faceted evaluation.

## Method

The paper introduces VLGuard, the first public vision-language safety dataset, constructed by sourcing images from five existing harmful-content datasets and using GPT-4V to generate instruction-response pairs that either answer safe questions or refuse harmful ones with a 100–200 word rationale. Two fine-tuning strategies are proposed: post-hoc fine-tuning (applying VLGuard plus ~5,000 helpfulness samples to an already-trained VLLM) and mixed fine-tuning (appending VLGuard to the original VLLM training data, constituting only 0.1–0.3% of total data). Both strategies work with full parameter updates or LoRA, and the key finding is that even this small amount of safety data reduces attack success rates from ~80% to near zero on black-box attacks (AdvBench, FigStep, TAP) while preserving or slightly improving helpfulness on benchmarks like MMLU, ScienceQA, and VizWiz. The paper also demonstrates that removing harmful samples from existing training data only partially restores safety, and that text-only safety data (Safety LLaMA) is insufficient for vision-language safety, validating the necessity of a multi-modal safety dataset.

## Results

Post-hoc fine-tuning on VLGuard reduces the AdvBench Vanilla attack success rate (ASR) of LLaVA-v1.5-7B from 6.45% to 0.00%, and the FigStep jailbreak ASR from 90.40% to 0.00%. On the VLGuard test set, the Safe-Unsafe ASR drops from 87.46% to 0.90% and the Unsafe ASR from 72.62% to 0.23%, while helpfulness (Safe-Safe winning rate vs. GPT4V) is maintained or slightly improved (18.82% → 18.96%). Mixed fine-tuning, where VLGuard constitutes only 0.3% of total training data, achieves comparable safety gains (e.g., LLaVA-13B AdvBench Suffix ASR: 74.23% → 8.46%) with no helpfulness degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against their base LLMs (Vicuna-v1.5-7B/13B, Llama-2-7B-Chat), all evaluated VLLMs are substantially less safe: LLaVA-v1.5-7B has 6.45% vs. 3.28% AdvBench Vanilla ASR and 78.27% vs. 69.42% Suffix ASR, confirming that VLM fine-tuning degrades LLM safety alignment. After VLGuard post-hoc fine-tuning, LLaVA-v1.5-7B surpasses its base LLM Vicuna-v1.5-7B on AdvBench Vanilla (0.00% vs. 3.28%) and approaches it on Suffix Injection (13.08% vs. 69.42%). Compared to the text-only Safety LLaMA dataset (Bianchi et al., 2024), VLGuard fine-tuning is dramatically more effective on vision-language attacks: FigStep ASR drops to 0.00% (vs. 87.00% for Safety LLaMA) and VLGuard Unsafe ASR drops to 0.23% (vs. 56.57%), while both achieve 0.00% on AdvBench Vanilla. Among the 10 VLLMs benchmarked on the VLGuard test set (Table 12), all exhibit high safety risks (Safe-Unsafe ASR ranging from 61.83% to 98.92%), with no model achieving below 60% refusal on unsafe instructions. Removing harmful training data ("Clean" retraining) only partially restores safety (e.g., LLaVA-7B AdvBench Vanilla: 6.45% → 5.77%), whereas VLGuard fine-tuning reduces it to 0.00%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM safety alignment literature (RLHF, Safety LLaMA by Bianchi et al. 2024) to the vision-language domain, where no prior safeguarding strategy existed. It builds on the observation by Qi et al. (2023b) that fine-tuning aligned LLMs with even small amounts of text data compromises safety, and demonstrates this effect is amplified in VLLM training due to both harmful content in instruction datasets and the additional visual attack surface. Methodologically, it repurposes standard supervised instruction fine-tuning (full or LoRA) with a curated safety dataset, rather than introducing a new training paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VLGuard dataset (2,000 images, ~3,000 instruction-response pairs) provides the first practical benchmark and training resource for VLLM safety, and its small size makes it a plug-and-play addition to existing VLLM training pipelines. The finding that VLLM fine-tuning systematically degrades the safety alignment of the underlying LLM — and that simply removing harmful training data does not fully restore it — is an important diagnostic for the field. Key limitations include the dataset's modest scale, the inability to defend against sophisticated white-box gradient-based attacks, and coverage of only a subset of real-world harm categories, leaving open questions about scaling and robustness to novel attack vectors.
