## Problem

Gradient-based meta-solving can train neural networks to generate solver hyperparameters (e.g., initial guesses) by backpropagating through the solver, but this requires the solver to be differentiable. In practice, most production solvers (PETSc, OpenFOAM, commercial codes) are legacy non-differentiable black boxes, making state-of-the-art joint training inapplicable without costly code modification or reimplementation. This paper asks: how can one train a meta-solver jointly with an unmodified legacy solver?

## Input / Output

The meta-solver Ψ takes the PDE right-hand side vector b_τ (e.g., the source term of a discretized Poisson equation or the body force in a 3D elasticity problem) and outputs an initial guess θ for the iterative solver, parameterized as sine-basis coefficients for the 1D Poisson case or a dense vector for higher-dimensional problems. The legacy solver f (PETSc Jacobi, geometric multigrid, or algebraic multigrid) is called as a black box twice per training step with parameters θ and θ+εv to form a finite-difference directional derivative. Training is self-supervised: the loss is a surrogate for iteration count built from the relative residual ‖b_τ − Ax_k‖/‖b_τ‖, requiring no pre-computed reference solutions.

## Method

The core contribution is the control variate forward gradient h_v(θ) = g_v(θ) − ĝ_v(θ) + ∇f̂(θ), where g_v is the forward gradient of the legacy solver estimated by two finite-difference evaluations, and ĝ_v is the forward gradient of an adaptive neural-network surrogate f̂ computed via forward-mode automatic differentiation. This estimator is unbiased (under Rademacher v) and its variance scales as (d−1)‖∇f − ∇f̂‖² rather than (d−1)‖∇f‖², so a well-fitted surrogate dramatically reduces variance. The surrogate f̂ is trained online with a directional-derivative-matching loss ‖(∇f·v) − (∇f̂·v)‖², adapting to the shifting distribution of θ as the meta-solver improves. The meta-solver Ψ is updated via a modified backpropagation that replaces ∇_θ f with h_v, requiring no modification to the legacy code. Theoretical analysis shows that with an adaptive surrogate whose gradient error decays geometrically, the convergence rate becomes independent of dimension d, unlike the original forward gradient whose rate degrades as 1/(Ld).

## Results

On the 1D Poisson equation with the Jacobi solver in PETSc, NI-GBMS (h_{v,ε}) reduces the average iterations to converge from 173.00 (zero initial guess) to 45.15, a 74% reduction, and from 175.71 (supervised learning baseline) to 45.15. For the geometric multigrid solver, iterations drop from 90.06 to 43.06 (52% reduction). On the more advanced 2D biharmonic and 3D linear elasticity problems solved with algebraic multigrid in PETSc, NI-GBMS reduces iterations by 57% (73.74 → 31.54) and 71% (88.71 → 25.61) respectively, demonstrating strong generalization to higher-dimensional PDEs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the oracle baseline (GBMS with true gradient ∇f from Arisaka & Li, 2023), NI-GBMS achieves comparable or even slightly better results: 45.15 vs. 53.64 iterations for Jacobi (NI-GBMS wins) and 43.06 vs. 39.29 for multigrid (∇f wins by ~9%). Against the forward gradient baseline (Belouze, 2022), NI-GBMS substantially outperforms g_{v,ε}, reducing iterations from 66.85 to 45.15 (Jacobi) and from 59.09 to 43.06 (multigrid), a 32–27% improvement. Against the surrogate-model gradient baseline (Jacovi et al., 2019, ∇f̂), NI-GBMS is dramatically better: 45.15 vs. 106.65 for Jacobi (58% fewer iterations) and 43.06 vs. 52.59 for multigrid. The supervised learning baseline Ψ_SL, which ignores solver behavior entirely, performs at or below the trivial zero-guess baseline, confirming that joint training with solver feedback is essential. On the advanced 2D/3D problems (Table 3), NI-GBMS achieves 57% and 71% iteration reductions over the default PETSc zero initial guess, with no direct ∇f comparison available since PETSc does not support automatic differentiation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient-based meta-solving (GBMS) framework of Arisaka & Li (2023), which jointly trains neural networks with differentiable numerical solvers, to handle non-automatic-differentiable legacy codes (e.g., PETSc, OpenFOAM, commercial software) without any modification. The key technical ingredient is a control variate forward gradient: it builds on the forward gradient of Belouze (2022) as an unbiased, backpropagation-free estimator, but reduces its high variance by subtracting the forward gradient of an adaptively trained surrogate model. This contrasts with the biased 'Estimate and Replace' approach of Jacovi et al. (2019) and the score-function-based control variates of Grathwohl et al. (2017), which are inapplicable to deterministic black-box functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The non-intrusive design removes the primary barrier preventing practitioners from applying ML-based solver acceleration to real-world legacy and commercial codes, making the technique immediately deployable in large-scale scientific computing. The demonstrated 57–74% reduction in solver iterations for PETSc's Jacobi, multigrid, and algebraic multigrid methods on Poisson, biharmonic, and elasticity problems validates the approach across problem types and solver families. Open questions include scaling to higher-dimensional parameter spaces (e.g., learning full preconditioner matrices rather than scalar shifts), designing surrogate architectures that exploit solver structure, and extending to nonlinear or time-dependent problems where the solver's behavior is more complex.
