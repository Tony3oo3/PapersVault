## Problem

While numerous mechanisms have been proposed to integrate Evolutionary Algorithms and Reinforcement Learning for policy search, it remains unclear which mechanisms are complementary and can be fully combined. Previous works lack systematic cross-mechanism analysis and predominantly evaluate on MUJOCO tasks with dense rewards, limiting the generalizability of their conclusions to exploration-heavy or low-quality-reward settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes environment states (continuous vectors for MuJoCo/maze/manipulation tasks, or discrete frame sequences for MinAtar) and outputs a single action per timestep from the RL policy. A population of policy networks simultaneously interacts with the environment, and their cumulative rewards (or surrogate Q-value estimates via Critic H-step bootstrap) serve as fitness signals for evolutionary selection. Two variants are produced: EvoRainbow for general tasks and EvoRainbow-Exp for exploration tasks with sparse or deceptive rewards.

## Method

The paper systematically decouples prior ERL algorithms into five orthogonal design perspectives—Interaction Mode, Individual Architecture, EA/Operator choice, EA-to-RL impact mechanism, and Fitness Surrogate usage—and compares each option under controlled conditions across dense, deceptive, and sparse reward tasks. The key finding is that the optimal mechanism combination is task-dependent: EvoRainbow (Parallel Mode + Shared Architecture + CEM + Genetic Soft Update + Critic H-step Bootstrap with Generation Control) excels on general tasks, while EvoRainbow-Exp (Parallel Mode + Private Architecture + CEM + Genetic Soft Update, no surrogate) is superior on exploration tasks. The shared architecture reduces the policy search space via a common state encoder trained to maximize Q-values across all individuals, while the private architecture preserves independent representations that resist collapse under low-quality reward signals. Genetic Soft Update directly blends elite population parameters into the RL policy (θ_RL = τ'θ_Elite + (1−τ')θ_RL), enhancing policy plasticity and enabling escape from suboptimal basins.

## Results

EvoRainbow and EvoRainbow-Exp are evaluated on 20 tasks spanning five domains (MUJOCO locomotion, MinAtar games, D4RL maze/trap tasks, and MetaWorld manipulation), with results reported as learning curves (mean ± 95% CI over 5 seeds, up to 1M environment steps). EvoRainbow achieves the best or near-best performance on general tasks with dense or moderate rewards (Humanoid, Ant, Walker, Swimmer, Breakout, Asterix, Freeway, Space Invaders, and most MetaWorld tasks), while EvoRainbow-Exp significantly outperforms all baselines on exploration-heavy tasks with sparse or deceptive rewards (Trap Maze, Wall Task, Large Maze, Ant Maze). The paper reports that EvoRainbow-Exp is the only method to successfully solve the Trap Maze and Wall Task, where all other ERL baselines and pure RL methods (SAC, TD3) get trapped in local optima.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvoRainbow outperforms all ERL baselines (ERL, PDERL, CERL, CEM-RL, PGPS, ERL-Re²) on locomotion and manipulation tasks, with the largest gaps observed on Humanoid and MetaWorld tasks where the Shared Architecture + CEM + Genetic Soft Update combination provides clear advantages. On exploration tasks (Trap Maze, Wall Task, Large Maze), EvoRainbow-Exp (Private Architecture + CEM + Genetic Soft Update, no surrogate) dominates all baselines, while EvoRainbow's surrogate-based variant actually degrades due to inaccurate value estimates under deceptive/sparse rewards. Among pure RL baselines, SAC fails entirely on all four maze tasks, TD3 underperforms on locomotion compared to EvoRainbow, and DQN is competitive only on MinAtar where EvoRainbow still leads. CEM-RL (EA-Master mode) consistently underperforms both EvoRainbow variants across all task types, confirming that the EA-dominant interaction mode is inferior. ERL-Re², the closest prior method sharing the Shared Architecture, is outperformed by EvoRainbow on locomotion and manipulation tasks, with the gap attributed to the addition of Genetic Soft Update and the Critic(H-Step) surrogate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a systematic re-examination of the ERL (Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning) literature, building directly on ERL (Khadka & Tumer, 2018), CEM-RL (Pourchot & Sigaud, 2019), ERL-Re² (Hao et al., 2023), and Supe-RL (Marchesini et al., 2021). Rather than proposing a single new mechanism, it decouples prior algorithms into five orthogonal design perspectives (interaction mode, architecture, EA choice, EA-to-RL impact, fitness surrogate) and compares them under controlled conditions across dense, deceptive, and sparse reward tasks. The resulting EvoRainbow and EvoRainbow-Exp are assembled from the empirically best mechanism at each perspective, making the contribution primarily one of systematic empirical synthesis rather than a novel algorithmic primitive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with actionable guidance on which ERL design choices suit which reward regimes—e.g., shared architecture for dense rewards versus private architecture for sparse/deceptive rewards, and the counterintuitive finding that fitness surrogates improve general tasks but degrade exploration tasks. This is valuable because prior ERL work was largely validated only on MuJoCo locomotion tasks, leaving generalizability unclear. Key limitations include the step-by-step (rather than exhaustive) mechanism comparison, which may miss cross-perspective interactions, and the absence of a single unified framework—users must choose between EvoRainbow and EvoRainbow-Exp based on task characteristics. Open questions include developing reward-robust shared representations, exploring more advanced EAs beyond CEM/GA, and designing surrogates that do not harm exploration.
