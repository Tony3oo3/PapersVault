## Problem

Existing nonparametric sequential change detection methods, such as the BCS-Detector, require constructing confidence sequences in both forward and backward directions, implicitly demanding independent observations and limiting applicability to dependent data streams. This paper seeks a simpler reduction from change detection to sequential estimation that relies only on forward confidence sequences, works under minimal structural assumptions (including martingale dependence), and provides stronger false-alarm control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241816/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of observations X₁, X₂, … from a general space 𝒳 with a prespecified (possibly infinite-dimensional) parameter functional θ, without requiring i.i.d. sampling or a known pre-change distribution. It produces a stopping time τ adapted to the natural filtration, declaring a change has occurred. The scheme handles both non-partitioned settings (unknown P₀, P₁ in a common class 𝒫) and partitioned settings (known pre-change parameter class Θ₀), and the post-change distribution P₁ may depend on the pre-change observations.

## Method

The RCS-Detector initializes a new level-(1−α) forward confidence sequence at every time step and stops as soon as the intersection of all active CSs becomes empty, requiring only forward-direction CS construction. This eliminates the backward-CS requirement of the BCS-Detector, removing the implicit independence assumption and enabling application to dependent (e.g., Markov) data streams. The ARL is controlled at 1/α via a proof that attaches an e-process to each CS and constructs an e-detector whose threshold crossing stochastically dominates the RCS-Detector's stopping time, yielding a tighter bound than the BCS-Detector's 1/(2α)−3/2. Detection delay is bounded in terms of the CS width function, matching BCS-Detector guarantees, and for bounded-mean change detection the delay is O(log(1/αK)/K) where K is a KL-divergence quantity. The paper further establishes a one-to-one correspondence between RCS-Detector and Lorden's classical reduction from SCD to repeated sequential testing, unifying the two frameworks via the CS–sequential-test duality.

## Results

The RCS-Detector achieves an ARL lower bound of 1/α, improving upon the BCS-Detector's bound of 1/(2α) − 3/2 by approximately a factor of 2, while matching its detection delay guarantee of O(log(1/αK)/K) for bounded mean detection (where K is a KL-divergence quantity). In numerical experiments on Beta-distributed observations with Δ ∈ {0.05, ..., 0.175} and α = 0.001, both schemes show comparable detection delays on independent data, with RCS-Detector slightly better at smaller Δ and BCS-Detector slightly better at larger Δ. The RCS-Detector additionally handles dependent data streams (e.g., Markov-switching distributions) where the BCS-Detector is inapplicable due to its backward-CS requirement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the BCS-Detector (Shekhar & Ramdas, 2023), the RCS-Detector strictly improves the ARL guarantee from 1/(2α) − 3/2 to 1/α (a factor-of-2 improvement) while exactly matching the detection delay bound O(log(1/αK)/K). The RCS-Detector also removes the independence requirement: it only needs forward confidence sequences, whereas the BCS-Detector requires constructing CSs in both forward and backward directions, which is typically only possible for i.i.d. data. In numerical experiments (Figures 1–2), both schemes show comparable empirical ARL and detection delay on independent Beta-distributed data, with RCS-Detector exhibiting slightly higher ARL variability at small α. Against CuSum (Page, 1954), which requires exact knowledge of both pre- and post-change distributions, the RCS-Detector is significantly worse in detection delay (Figure 3, left panel) but operates without any parametric knowledge of the distributions. The paper also establishes a one-to-one correspondence (Proposition 3.1) between the RCS-Detector and Lorden's (1971) reduction from SCD to repeated sequential testing, showing that RCS-Detector is a nonparametric generalization of Lorden's scheme.

## Relation to Prior Work

The RCS-Detector is a direct simplification of the BCS-Detector (Shekhar & Ramdas, 2023), replacing its pair of forward and backward confidence sequences with a stream of forward-only CSs, thereby eliminating the implicit independence assumption required to construct martingales in both temporal directions. The paper also establishes a one-to-one correspondence between the RCS-Detector and Lorden's (1971) classic reduction from sequential change detection to repeated sequential probability ratio tests, via the duality between confidence sequences and power-one sequential tests, thereby providing a nonparametric generalization of that result. The ARL proof additionally leverages the e-process/e-detector framework of Shin et al. (2024), positioning the scheme within the broader anytime-valid inference literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, general recipe that immediately converts any existing confidence sequence construction into a change-detection scheme with non-asymptotic ARL control, making the rapidly growing CS literature directly applicable to nonparametric and dependent-data change detection problems. The factor-of-2 improvement in the ARL lower bound over BCS-Detector, combined with the removal of the bidirectional CS requirement, makes the scheme strictly more general under weaker assumptions. Key open questions include the quadratic per-step computational cost (mitigated only heuristically via windowing), the lack of a data-driven window-size selection strategy, and the gap between the theoretical detection-delay guarantees and the empirical performance observed on independent data, where RCS- and BCS-Detector perform comparably.
