## Problem

Deep ensembles provide strong OOD detection but incur linear computational and memory overhead with ensemble size, while single-model OOD-aware methods like outlier exposure require external OOD proxy data that is often unavailable in practice. The paper addresses the gap of achieving ensemble-level OOD detection and uncertainty calibration without extra OOD data or additional inference cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240295/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet) and produces two outputs: a class label via argmax over concatenated ID-class logits from all submodels, and an OOD detection score computed as p(y|z) = p(y|z,f_i) × p(f_i|z), where the second factor is 1 minus the OOD-class softmax probability of the responsible submodel. Each submodel internally outputs K_i+1 logits (its ID classes plus a dedicated OOD sink class), but only the ID logits are exposed to the final ensemble decision.

## Method

The core idea is to split an N-class classification task into n complementary subtasks (grouped by semantic similarity), where each subtask treats its K_i classes as in-distribution and all remaining N−K_i classes as OOD, enabling OOD-aware training without external data. Each submodel is trained with a class-balanced BCE loss using an outlier-exposure-inspired uniform target for the OOD class (Equation 1) to prevent overconfidence. To keep inference at single-model cost, the architecture is a tree-like Split-Ensemble: submodels share early backbone layers and iteratively branch apart at layers where SNIP-based sensitivity masks show low IoU correlation (below a Minimal Cutting Threshold), followed by Hessian-aware global structural pruning to remove redundancy. The splitting and pruning are performed alternately during training until the FLOPs budget is met, yielding a single forward pass through a shared-then-divergent tree rather than n independent networks.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, Split-Ensemble achieves 77.7% top-1 accuracy (ResNet-18) at 1× FLOPs, surpassing the single-model baseline (75.9%) by 1.8% while matching the 4× Naive Ensemble (80.1%) within 2.4%. On Tiny-ImageNet, it reaches 51.6% accuracy versus 26.1% for a single model and 44.6% for a 4× Naive Ensemble. For OOD detection, Split-Ensemble improves mean AUROC over the single-model baseline by 2.2% (CIFAR-10), 8.1% (CIFAR-100), and 29.6% (Tiny-ImageNet), while also outperforming the 4× Naive Ensemble on all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Split-Ensemble outperforms the single-model baseline on both accuracy and OOD detection at identical 1× FLOPs, gaining 0.8–1.8% accuracy on CIFAR-10/100 and up to 29.6% AUROC on Tiny-ImageNet OOD detection. Against the 4× Naive Ensemble, Split-Ensemble achieves comparable CIFAR-10 accuracy (95.5% vs 95.7%) and slightly lower CIFAR-100 accuracy (77.7% vs 80.1%), but at only 1/4 the computational cost, while decisively winning on OOD detection (e.g., 93.7% vs 91.1% mean AUROC on CIFAR-10). Among parameter-efficient ensemble methods, Split-Ensemble dominates: it beats FilmEnsemble (the strongest efficient baseline) by 7.7% on CIFAR-10 accuracy (95.5% vs 87.8%) and by 2.2% AUROC on CIFAR-10 OOD detection, all at 1× vs 4× FLOPs. MC-Dropout, MIMO, MaskEnsemble, and BatchEnsemble are all substantially outperformed on both accuracy and OOD metrics, with MIMO being the weakest (54.9% CIFAR-100 accuracy). On the more challenging Tiny-ImageNet and ImageNet-1K, Split-Ensemble (51.6% and 70.9%) surpasses both the single model and the 4× Naive Ensemble, demonstrating that the task-splitting objective helps convergence on harder tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: outlier-exposure OOD-aware training (Hendrycks et al., 2018), parameter-efficient deep ensembles (MIMO, BatchEnsemble, FilmEnsemble), and multi-task pruning (SNIP, Hessian-aware global pruning). The key conceptual departure from prior efficient ensembles is that instead of multiple submodels learning the same task with shared parameters, the original classification task is split into complementary subtasks where each submodel treats a subset of classes as in-distribution and the remainder as OOD. This reframing eliminates the need for external OOD proxy data while preserving the OOD-aware training signal that outlier exposure requires. The iterative splitting-and-pruning algorithm, guided by sensitivity-mask IoU and Hessian importance, is a novel architectural search procedure not present in prior ensemble or multi-task pruning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that Split-Ensemble achieves ensemble-level OOD detection (beating a 4× naive ensemble on AUROC) at single-model inference cost, removing the primary barrier to deploying calibrated uncertainty in resource-constrained settings. The task-splitting idea is broadly applicable in principle, but the paper is limited to image classification with ResNet backbones and requires semantic class groupings, which may not be available in all domains. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-classification tasks (detection, segmentation), whether the number of splits and grouping can be fully automated, and whether the method scales to the large language model setting the authors mention as future work. The reliance on a manually tuned MCT threshold and the sensitivity of performance to the number of splits (Table 8) suggest the method is not yet fully plug-and-play.
