## Problem

Computing a TMECor in adversarial team games is hard because the team's coordinated strategy space is exponential in the game tree size due to asymmetric information. While the TB-DAG provides a compact representation of this space, directly applying Column Generation to the TB-DAG is infeasible because the TB-DAG itself remains exponential, making the Best Response Oracle unscalable. This paper bridges the gap by proposing a novel framework that computes the best response in the original (smaller) game tree and then transforms it into TB-DAG form.

## Input / Output

The method takes a zero-sum extensive-form game tree with a team of |T|≥2 players (each with their own information sets) and an opponent, and produces a TMECor consisting of a team coordinated strategy in TB-DAG form (a probability distribution over prescriptions at belief nodes) and an opponent sequence-form strategy. The restricted game maintains TB-DAG form variables while the BRO operates over the original game tree's normal-form coordinated strategies, connected by a transformation step that expands only the beliefs and actions reachable under the best response.

## Method

DCG alternates between solving a restricted game in TB-DAG form and computing a pure coordinated best response in the original game tree via a novel pure BRO, then transforming that response into TB-DAG form before adding it to the restricted game. The pure BRO exploits a correlation plan ξ over a carefully defined set of relevant joint sequences Σ_T^⋈ (joint sequences at game tree nodes plus single-player sequences), with polynomial-sized constraints O(|H∪Z||T|) that encode the team's correlation property while avoiding the exponential constraints of the full von Stengel-Forges polytope. The key insight enabling faster convergence is that the TB-DAG induced by a set of transformed strategies can represent new coordinated strategies not in the convex hull of the originals (due to new combinations of beliefs and actions), so DCG converges in O*((b(p+1))^w) iterations—exponentially fewer than normal-form CG's 2^|Π₁|·|Π_T|/|Π₁| worst-case bound.

## Results

On Leduc Poker with deep game trees (e.g., ³L₁₃ with 457 sequences per player), DCG_pure converges to a TMECor in 40s with 82 iterations, while the state-of-the-art CG_random requires 933s and 1132 iterations, and CG_2random runs out of memory. On Kuhn Poker with wide game trees (e.g., ³K⁴₈ with 1113 sequences), DCG_pure solves in 642s whereas CG_random exceeds 10 hours and CG_2random exhausts memory. The number of iterations for DCG_pure is consistently 5–15× fewer than normal-form CG algorithms (e.g., 82 vs. 1149 on ³L₁₃), confirming the theoretical exponential convergence advantage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CG_random (Zhang et al. 2021 / Farina et al. 2021), DCG_pure is at least two orders of magnitude faster in deep/wide games: 40s vs. 933s on ³L₁₃, and 642s vs. >10h on ³K⁴₈, while requiring 82 vs. 1132 iterations. Against CG_2random (Zhang et al. 2022b, two-sided CG), DCG_pure consistently wins; CG_2random frequently runs out of memory in large games (e.g., ³L₁₄, ³L₁₅, ³K⁴₈) due to adding |Σᵢ| extra variables per iteration. Against the DAG baseline (Zhang et al. 2022c, direct LP on the full TB-DAG), DCG_pure is slower on very narrow games (e.g., 0.75s for DAG vs. 40s for DCG_pure on ³L₁₃) but DAG runs out of memory on all wider games (³L₁₇, ³L₀₂₁₀, ³L₀₂₁₃) where DCG_pure still converges. Against CG_dag (Bosansky et al. 2014, sequence-form double oracle on the full TB-DAG), DCG_pure wins in every game tested: 40s vs. 30m on ³L₁₃, and CG_dag exceeds 10 hours on all other Leduc instances. In shallow/narrow games (e.g., ³K¹₅), normal-form CG variants are slightly faster than DCG_pure (0.46s vs. 0.86s), but this gap reverses as game depth and width increase.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work on computing TMECor in adversarial team games: (1) normal-form Column Generation (CG) algorithms that iteratively expand the team's exponential coordinated strategy space via a Best Response Oracle (BRO) exploiting the team's correlation property (Celli & Gatti, 2018; Zhang et al., 2021; Farina et al., 2021), and (2) the Team Belief DAG (TB-DAG) representation (Zhang et al., 2022c; Carminati et al., 2022) that provides a compact sequence-form encoding of the team's strategy space. The key departure is that DCG uses *different* strategy representations in the restricted game (TB-DAG form) and the BRO (normal-form coordinated strategy), connected by a novel transformation step, whereas prior frameworks use the same representation in both. The pure BRO further departs from the state-of-the-art randomized BRO (Farina et al., 2021) by restricting the von Stengel-Forges polytope to a polynomial-sized set of relevant joint sequences, avoiding the exponential constraint blowup that arises when extending it to multiplayer pure strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first efficient TB-DAG-based CG algorithm, resolving a practical gap where TB-DAG methods only outperformed CG on small games and direct TB-DAG CG was infeasible due to the exponential BRO. The exponential convergence speedup over normal-form CG (Theorem 3, Corollary 5) is theoretically clean and empirically validated by two-orders-of-magnitude runtime improvements on deep/wide game trees, enabling previously unsolvable instances. Limitations include evaluation restricted to Kuhn and Leduc poker (standard but limited in structural diversity), and the transformation step still incurs non-trivial cost in wide games, as seen when DCG_pure^linrelax degrades relative to DCG_pure. The authors identify applying multiagent learning frameworks (policy-spaced response oracles) with deep learning for the transformation and BRO as a promising path to very large-scale games, which remains open.
