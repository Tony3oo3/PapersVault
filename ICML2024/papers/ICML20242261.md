## Problem

Diffusion models encode complex, multimodal data distributions into a single unimodal Gaussian prior, forcing the generative ODE to be highly nonlinear with strong curvature. This makes learning the noise-to-data mapping unnecessarily difficult, particularly at high noise levels where denoising is most ambiguous. The paper asks whether introducing a small set of learnable discrete latent variables can partition this complex mapping into simpler sub-mappings, reducing ODE curvature and improving generation quality without relying on domain-specific pre-trained encoders.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242261/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For image synthesis, the model takes a class label (and optionally a text prompt) as conditioning and produces 64×64 or 128×128 RGB images; during training, a ViT encoder infers m=10 discrete latents (each with codebook size k=100) from clean images, while at inference these are sampled from a 12-layer autoregressive Transformer. For molecular docking, the input is a protein structure and ligand graph, and the output is a 3D ligand pose; here the discrete latents take values over the nodes of the protein-ligand joint graph, effectively indicating key atoms involved in the binding interaction. The discrete latents are deliberately kept few and low-cardinality so that the autoregressive prior over them remains trivially learnable.

## Method

DisCo-Diff augments a standard diffusion model with m categorical latent variables, each drawn from a codebook of size k, inferred by a ViT encoder and injected into the U-Net denoiser via cross-attention layers (analogous to text conditioning in text-to-image models). Training is end-to-end in two stages: first, the denoiser and encoder are jointly optimized by extending the denoising score matching objective to condition on the discrete latents, with Gumbel-Softmax relaxation enabling gradient flow through the discrete sampling; second, a lightweight autoregressive Transformer is trained to model the marginal distribution of the discrete latents. The key mechanism is that the discrete latents learn to capture global multimodal structure (e.g., style, color patterns, or binding-site atoms), partitioning the single complex noise-to-data ODE into a set of simpler, lower-curvature conditional ODEs. Classifier-free guidance is applied with respect to the discrete latents (with 10% dropout during training), amplifying their conditioning effect at inference. The framework requires no pre-trained encoders and is validated on ImageNet-64/128 (achieving SOTA FID of 1.65 with ODE sampler), 2D toy distributions, and molecular docking, demonstrating universal applicability across modalities.

## Results

On class-conditioned ImageNet-64 with an ODE sampler, DisCo-Diff achieves a state-of-the-art FID of 1.65 (78 NFE), improving over EDM (2.36) and PFGM++ (2.32). In the unconditional setting, DisCo-Diff reaches FID 3.70, surpassing SCDM (3.94) and EDM (6.20). On ImageNet-128 with an ODE sampler, DisCo-Diff achieves FID 1.98 (114 NFE) versus VDM++'s 2.29. For molecular docking on PDBBind, DisCo-DiffDock-S improves the success rate from 32.9% to 35.4% (full set) and from 13.9% to 18.5% (unseen proteins) over DiffDock-S.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DisCo-Diff achieves new state-of-the-art FID scores across all primary image generation settings it competes in. On ImageNet-64 class-conditioned ODE, it reduces FID from 2.36 (EDM) and 2.32 (PFGM++) to 1.65; on ImageNet-128 ODE, from 2.29 (VDM++) to 1.98. In the unconditional setting, DisCo-Diff (3.70) outperforms SCDM (3.94), which relies on pre-trained MoCo features, and EDM (6.20). With the Restart stochastic sampler on ImageNet-64, DisCo-Diff (1.22) narrowly beats RIN (1.23) and substantially improves over EDM w/ Restart (1.36) and VDM++ (1.43). For molecular docking, DisCo-DiffDock-S improves over its direct baseline DiffDock-S by 2.5 points (full) and 4.6 points (unseen proteins), though the larger DiffDock model (38.2%/20.8%) still outperforms it, reflecting the capacity difference. DisCo-Diff does not compete in the adversarial-objective setting (where StyleGAN-XL achieves 1.51 FID), as it is a pure diffusion-based method without a GAN discriminator.

## Relation to Prior Work

DisCo-Diff extends the score-based diffusion model framework (Song et al., 2021; Karras et al., 2022) by augmenting the continuous Gaussian prior with a small set of learnable discrete latent variables, inferred end-to-end via a ViT encoder and injected through cross-attention. This departs fundamentally from latent diffusion models (Rombach et al., 2022) that compress data into continuous latents, and from VQ-VAE-style approaches (Esser et al., 2021; Ramesh et al., 2021) that use large spatial grids of discrete tokens with large codebooks. It also differs from prior conditioning works (Bao et al., 2022; Harvey & Wood, 2023; Hu et al., 2023) that rely on pre-trained encoders (CLIP, MoCo, DINO), by learning the discrete latents jointly with the denoiser objective, making the framework universally applicable beyond images. The conceptual contribution is showing that a handful of global discrete latents with small codebooks can partition the noise-to-data mapping into simpler sub-mappings with reduced ODE curvature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DisCo-Diff provides a simple, universal plug-in that consistently improves diffusion model quality across modalities (images, molecules, toy data) without requiring pre-trained encoders, achieving SOTA FID on class-conditioned ImageNet-64/128 with ODE samplers. The curvature-reduction analysis offers a principled explanation for why discrete latents help, and the finding that only 10 latents with codebook size 100 suffice suggests the approach is computationally lightweight. Open questions include whether the framework extends to text-to-image generation (where text already provides strong conditioning), whether the two-stage training can be unified end-to-end, and whether the observed degradation with stochastic samplers on ImageNet-128 reflects a fundamental limitation of discrete latents at small noise levels. The group hierarchical design, where different latents capture shape vs. color at different U-Net resolutions, hints at broader interpretability benefits for generative models.
