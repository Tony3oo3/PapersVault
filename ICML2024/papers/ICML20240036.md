## Problem

Existing Gaussian process models on graphs are limited to dyadic (pairwise) interactions between vertices and cannot represent polyadic relations involving edges, faces, or higher-order cells. While cellular complexes provide the topological structure to capture such higher-order interactions, no GP framework has been developed on them that supports directed signals, uncertainty quantification, and joint modeling across different cell types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240036/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite oriented cellular complex (with incidence matrices defining boundaries between cells), noisy observations on a subset of cells at each skeleton level, and optional cell weights. It produces predictive means and covariances on all cells simultaneously, with the distinctive property that edge predictions carry directional information (sign relative to a fixed orientation) and that the reaction-diffusion kernel induces cross-covariances between cochains of different orders (e.g., vertices and edges), enabling inference on one cell type to inform predictions on another.

## Method

The paper defines a GP on a cellular complex as a direct sum of Gaussian random cochains, characterized by a mean cochain and a symmetric positive-semidefinite bilinear kernel on chains. Two kernels are constructed via spectral decompositions: (1) the cellular Matérn kernel, defined as the solution to (2ν/ℓ² + Δ_k)^ν κ(c,·) = c^♭ where Δ_k is the Hodge Laplacian, generalizing the graph Matérn kernel by replacing the graph Laplacian with the Hodge Laplacian on k-cochains; and (2) the reaction-diffusion kernel, defined via the Dirac matrix D (a formal square root of the super-Laplacian) as K = U(rI − cΛ + dΛ²)^{-ν}U^⊤, where the off-diagonal blocks of the precision matrix (proportional to the incidence matrix B_1) create probabilistic coupling between cochains of adjacent orders. The Dirac term is essential for propagating information across cell types, as setting c=0 recovers independent per-skeleton Matérn GPs with no cross-cell mixing.

## Results

On the ocean current interpolation task, the proposed Edge Matérn GP achieves MSE 0.029 and NLL −703.42, outperforming the Graph Matérn baseline (MSE 0.030, NLL −684.54) on uncertainty calibration while matching on point accuracy. On the synthetic signal-mixing experiment, the reaction-diffusion (RD) GP substantially outperforms the CC-Matérn GP across all cell types: vertices (MSE 0.076 vs 0.165), edges (MSE 0.200 vs 0.335), and triangles (MSE 0.166 vs 0.272), with correspondingly lower NLL scores. On the electromagnetic field experiment, the RD GP yields slightly better MSE and NLL for the electric and magnetic fields (e.g., B-field MSE 0.178 vs 0.190) but is marginally worse on the scalar potential MSE (0.11 vs 0.102).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The only external baseline is the Graph Matérn GP (Borovitskiy et al., 2021), compared on the ocean current magnitude prediction task. The proposed Edge Matérn GP achieves comparable MSE (0.029 vs 0.030) but a substantially better NLL (−703.42 vs −684.54), indicating better-calibrated predictive uncertainties and the benefit of the topological inductive bias from modelling signals directly on edges. Internally, the reaction-diffusion (RD) GP is compared against the CC-Matérn GP (which uses a block-diagonal super-Laplacian and thus cannot mix information across cell types). On the synthetic signal-mixing experiment, the RD GP wins decisively on both MSE and NLL across all three cell types, with vertex MSE improving by more than 50% (0.076 vs 0.165). On the electromagnetic field experiment, the RD GP outperforms the CC-Matérn GP on 8 out of 10 random seeds for the electric and magnetic fields, though the margin is smaller than in the synthetic setting where correlations were directly imposed. The CC-Matérn GP is slightly better on the scalar potential MSE (0.102 vs 0.11), suggesting that mixing is most beneficial when inter-field correlations are strong.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on Gaussian processes defined on topological structures—starting from graph Matérn GPs (Borovitskiy et al., 2021) and vector-valued GPs on Riemannian manifolds (Hutchinson et al., 2021)—to the more general setting of cellular complexes, which capture polyadic (higher-than-dyadic) interactions. It draws on the Dirac operator from topological signal processing (Bianconi, 2021; Calmon et al., 2023) to construct a novel reaction-diffusion kernel that mixes information across cell types, and on the Hodge Laplacian to generalise the graph Matérn kernel to arbitrary cell orders. The paper positions itself against concurrent work on edge GPs (Yang et al., 2024) and hypergraph GPs (Pinder et al., 2021), distinguishing itself by making inferences on the cells themselves rather than only on vertices, and by handling cell orientation natively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, uncertainty-aware tool for regression on higher-order networks where signals live on edges, faces, or volumes—settings common in physics (ocean currents, electromagnetic fields) and biology (group interactions, neuronal dynamics) that standard graph GPs cannot address. The reaction-diffusion kernel's ability to propagate information across cell types via the Dirac operator is a natural and effective mechanism for joint multi-signal modelling. Key limitations include the computational cost of eigendecomposition (restricting to networks of hundreds of thousands of cells) and the limited flexibility of only three hyperparameters (r, c, d) in the RD kernel; the authors identify Hodge decomposition as a promising direction for added expressiveness. Extending to manifolds with boundary conditions and more complex geometries remains open.
