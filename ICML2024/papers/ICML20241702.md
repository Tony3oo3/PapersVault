## Problem

Decomposable submodular maximization with millions of private component functions is computationally prohibitive under centralized sequential greedy, and existing distributed approaches either require sharing gradient information or lack the privacy guarantees needed for sensitive applications. No federated algorithm existed that could maximize a weighted sum of private submodular functions under matroid constraints while achieving the optimal (1−1/e) approximation factor with minimal communication rounds.

## Input / Output

The method takes a matroid polytope P (encoding constraints like cardinality), N private client submodular functions f_i, a ground set E of size n, and client weights p_i summing to 1. Each client communicates only its greedy direction v_i — an indicator vector of a matroid base with r ones — requiring O(r log n) bits per round, rather than the full O(n)-dimensional gradient. The output is a fractional solution x^(T) in P, rounded via oblivious schemes (pipage, swap, or greedy rounding) to yield an independent set S achieving (1−1/e)·OPT minus a small additive term.

## Method

FEDCG adapts the Continuous Greedy algorithm to a federated architecture: each client computes a local greedy direction by maximizing a linear function over the matroid polytope using its private gradient, and these directions are aggregated via single-server Secure Aggregation (SecAgg) so the server never sees individual updates. A key technical challenge is that direction vectors carry no magnitude information, so the analysis introduces a bounded gradient dissimilarity assumption (|∇f̂_i − ∇F̂|_∞ ≤ γ_t) rather than full gradient bounds, yielding the (1−1/e) guarantee with additive error O(rD/T + r²m_F/T). To handle partial participation, an unbiased client sampling scheme selects K clients per round with replacement, and a practical variant (FEDCG+) interleaves τ local steps between communication rounds to reduce rounds from T to T/τ, at the cost of an additional additive term from local divergence. A separate discrete algorithm (Algorithm 3) uses randomized response with importance-weighted participation to solve Facility Location and Maximum Coverage directly in the discrete domain, achieving (1−1/e−ε) under cardinality constraints.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments. The main quantitative results are approximation guarantees: FEDCG (full participation, Theorem 3.2) achieves $(1-1/e)\text{OPT} \leq \hat{F}(\mathbf{x}^{(T)}) + rD/T + r^2 m_F / 2T$; with partial participation (Theorem 3.5), an additional $6rD/\sqrt{TK\delta}$ term appears; FEDCG+ with $\tau$ local steps (Theorem 4.3) adds terms $2\sigma r$ and $2\eta r^{1.5}\sum L_t$ for gradient estimation and local-step divergence. The discrete federated greedy (Theorem 5.1) achieves $(1/2-\varepsilon)\text{OPT}$ for general matroids and $(1-1/e-\varepsilon)\text{OPT}$ under cardinality constraints, with expected $\tilde{O}(rn^2/\varepsilon^2)$ participating clients per round independent of $N$.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper contains no empirical comparisons or baselines; all results are theoretical. In terms of theoretical positioning, FEDCG matches the optimal $(1-1/e)$ multiplicative approximation factor of the centralized Continuous Greedy algorithm (Calinescu et al., 2011) while operating under a federated privacy model (SecAgg) and sublinear per-client communication ($\tilde{O}(r)$ bits per round). Unlike the Decentralized Continuous Greedy of Mokhtari et al. (2018b), which requires clients to share gradient information with neighboring nodes in a graph, FEDCG only transmits matroid base indicator vectors through a secure aggregator, fundamentally reducing the information exposed. The discrete algorithm (Algorithm 3) builds on the sparsification framework of Rafiey & Yoshida (2022) and Kenneth & Krauthgamer (2023), adapting their centralized sparsification to the federated setting via randomized response, achieving a $(1/2-\varepsilon)$ guarantee for general matroids and the near-optimal $(1-1/e-\varepsilon)$ for cardinality constraints without any client sharing its full function.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Continuous Greedy algorithm (Calinescu et al., 2011) for submodular maximization under matroid constraints into the federated learning framework (McMahan et al., 2017), where each client holds a private component function and only communicates compressed direction vectors via secure aggregation. It departs from prior decentralized submodular maximization work (Mokhtari et al., 2018b) which requires sharing gradient information between clients or with neighbors, by instead having clients compute local argmax directions over the matroid polytope and averaging them — a fundamentally different information flow that necessitates the novel bounded gradient dissimilarity assumption (Assumption 3.1). The discrete algorithm (Algorithm 3) repurposes the sparsification techniques of Rafiey & Yoshida (2022) and Kenneth & Krauthgamer (2023) for the federated setting, introducing a randomized-response mechanism for privacy-preserving marginal contribution reporting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical framework for decomposable submodular maximization in a federated setting with privacy guarantees, achieving the optimal (1−1/e) approximation factor even under practical constraints like partial client participation, local computation steps, and stochastic gradient estimation. The bounded gradient dissimilarity assumption (Assumption 3.1) is more relaxed than standard bounded-gradient-norm assumptions and is naturally satisfied in many applications (e.g., γ=1 for Max Coverage), making the results broadly applicable. Open questions include extending to differential privacy guarantees, establishing lower bounds on bit complexity, and tightening the additive error terms that grow with the number of local steps τ. The discrete federated algorithm opens a new direction for applying submodular sparsification in privacy-preserving combinatorial optimization.
