## Problem

Existing multimodal LLMs are largely confined to input-side perception (understanding images, video, audio) without the ability to generate content in those same modalities. Pipeline-style systems that combine LLMs with external generation tools suffer from error propagation through discrete text intermediates and lack end-to-end training, limiting their ability to interpret complex, implicit user instructions. This paper addresses the gap by building a unified, end-to-end any-to-any MM-LLM that can both perceive and generate across text, image, video, and audio in arbitrary combinations.

## Input / Output

The system accepts any combination of text, image, video, and audio as input, encoded via ImageBind into a shared embedding space. For output, the LLM directly generates text tokens, while for non-text modalities it emits learned modality signal tokens (IMG_i, AUD_i, VID_i) that are projected and fed as soft conditioning into frozen diffusion decoders (Stable Diffusion v1.5 for images, Zeroscope v2-576w for video, AudioLDM 1-full for audio). The signal tokens carry richer modality-specific information than textual captions, enabling the diffusion models to better interpret spatial relations, numeration, and other visual-semantic details that are hard to express in language.

## Method

NExT-GPT connects a frozen Vicuna-7B LLM with a unified ImageBind encoder (via a multi-stage grouping mechanism that hierarchically aggregates patch-level features into learnable concept tokens) and three frozen diffusion decoders (via Transformer-based output projection layers). Training proceeds in three stages: (1) encoding-side alignment using X-to-text captioning on CC3M, WebVid-2M, and AudioCaps; (2) decoding-side instruction-following alignment that minimizes L2 distance between projected signal token representations and diffusion text-encoder representations alongside conditional denoising loss; and (3) end-to-end instruction tuning with LoRA on a curated 5K-sample MosIT dataset featuring multi-turn, modality-switching dialogues. Only ~1% of total parameters (155M out of ~12.4B) are updated, keeping all encoders and diffusion backbones frozen, which enables low-cost training and easy expansion to additional modalities.

## Results

On image understanding, NExT-GPT achieves CIDEr scores of 123.7 (NoCaps), 84.5 (Flickr 30K), and 124.9 (COCO), outperforming InstructBLIP (123.1/82.4/102.2) and mPLUG-Owl (117.0/80.3/119.3). For image QA, it reaches 66.7% on VQA v2, 48.4% on VizWiz, and 52.1% on OKVQA, surpassing DREAMLLM (56.6/45.8/44.3) and Emu (40.0/35.4/34.7). On comprehensive benchmarks, NExT-GPT scores 58.0 on MMBench and 57.5 on SEED-Bench. For multimodal generation, NExT-GPT achieves FID 10.07 (image), FAD 1.68 (audio), and CLIP-SIM 31.97 (video), all state-of-the-art among compared systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NExT-GPT outperforms all LLM-centric baselines (InstructBLIP, LLaVA, mPLUG-Owl, Emu, DREAMLLM) on image captioning, image QA, and comprehensive benchmarks, with the largest margins on COCO captioning (+5.6 over mPLUG-Owl) and OKVQA (+7.8 over DREAMLLM). However, Video-LLaVA surpasses NExT-GPT on VQA v2 (74.7 vs. 66.7) and MMBench (60.9 vs. 58.0), likely due to its specialized video-visual alignment. For multimodal generation, NExT-GPT achieves the best FID of 10.07 on MS-COCO, beating SD-1.5 (11.21), CoDi (11.26), GILL-8B (12.20), Emu-13B (11.66), and UIO-2XXL (13.39); it also leads on audio FAD (1.68 vs. CoDi's 1.80 and AudioLDM-L's 1.96) and video CLIP-SIM (31.97 vs. CoDi's 28.90). In zero-shot generation, NExT-GPT still maintains competitive performance (FID 11.18, FAD 1.74, CLIP-SIM 30.96), confirming strong generalization. On video QA, NExT-GPT achieves 64.5% on MSVD-QA and 61.4% on MSRVTT-QA, outperforming Video-LLaMA (51.6/–) and Emu (32.4/14.0), though Video-LLaVA leads on MSVD-QA (70.7). For audio captioning, NExT-GPT reaches CIDEr 81.3 on AudioCaps, surpassing CoDi (78.9) and UIO-2XXL (48.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

NExT-GPT sits at the intersection of two prior research threads: (1) MM-LLMs that align multimodal encoders to a frozen LLM for input-side understanding (BLIP-2, Flamingo, PandaGPT), and (2) LLM-as-controller pipeline systems that delegate generation to external tools via textual instructions (Visual-ChatGPT, HuggingGPT). It departs from both by introducing an end-to-end trainable architecture where the LLM (Vicuna-7B) directly emits modality-specific signal tokens (borrowed from GILL) that condition frozen diffusion decoders (Stable Diffusion, Zeroscope, AudioLDM), with ImageBind serving as a unified encoder across all non-text modalities. The key architectural departure from pipeline systems is the use of soft signal-token representations rather than discrete text captions as the inter-module interface, and the key departure from CoDi is the LLM-centric reasoning layer that enables instruction-following and implicit intent understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NExT-GPT demonstrates that a unified any-to-any multimodal agent is feasible with only ~1% of parameters trainable, which is practically important for resource-constrained deployment and for modularly adding new modalities without retraining the full stack. However, generation quality remains bounded by the underlying diffusion models, the MosIT instruction dataset is small (5K dialogues), and the system is limited to four modalities. Open questions include whether the signal-token mechanism scales gracefully to higher-fidelity or longer-horizon generation (e.g., multi-minute video), whether the grouping mechanism generalizes to modalities with very different token structures, and whether end-to-end tuning can be further reduced or replaced by adapter-only approaches as diffusion models improve.
