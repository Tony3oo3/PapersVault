## Problem

Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) pretrained on massive datasets achieve strong downstream performance but are too computationally expensive for resource-constrained deployment. The paper tackles the underexplored question of how to transfer knowledge from a large VFM across both task and architecture boundaries to train a small task-specific model with limited labeled data, going beyond standard knowledge distillation (architecture-only) and transfer learning (task-only).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242016/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes RGB images at 256×256 resolution for classification tasks and 512×512 for segmentation, producing class labels or pixel-wise segmentation masks (150 classes for ADE20K). The method operates in two stages: a pretraining stage that matches the target model's task predictions to the adapted VFM's predictions on an unlabeled transfer set, followed by a finetuning stage on limited labeled target task data (as few as 5–10 images per class). The VFM itself is first adapted to the target task using a task-specific head (linear classifier or DeepLabV3) before serving as the teacher.

## Method

The proposed task-oriented knowledge transfer approach proceeds in three steps: (a) finetune the VFM on the target task using limited labeled data and a task-specific head, (b) pretrain the small target model by matching its task predictions (logits) to the adapted VFM's predictions on a large unlabeled transfer set via KL divergence, and (c) finetune the target model on the limited labeled data. The key insight is that transferring task-oriented knowledge (matching task outputs) substantially outperforms task-agnostic feature matching because small models cannot inherit the vast general knowledge stored in large VFMs. When a large task-related transfer set is unavailable, the paper introduces a retrieval-augmented curation strategy using query-balanced crop-level image retrieval from web-scale galleries (YFCC15M or DataComp-1B), which increases diversity by giving equal weight to all query images and enables extraction of relevant regions from out-of-domain images.

## Results

The proposed task-oriented knowledge transfer from VFMs (DINOv2 or OpenCLIP) to small target models (FastViT or MobileViT-V2) outperforms all competing pretraining strategies across five target tasks (HAM10K, EuroSAT, Places365, ImageNet, ADE20K) under limited labeled data. Performance gains over task-agnostic VFM distillation, CLIP pretraining, ImageNet pretraining, and DINO pretraining reach up to 11.6%, 22.1%, 13.7%, and 29.8% respectively (using generic CC3M transfer set). The approach also achieves up to 9×, 4×, and 15× reduction in pretraining compute (A100 GPU hours) compared to task-agnostic distillation, ImageNet pretraining, and DINO pretraining while outperforming them. For example, on EuroSAT with 10 labeled images per class, task-oriented transfer from DINOv2 with a task-related transfer set achieves 94.71% accuracy vs. 84.38% for CLIP pretraining and 84.83% for DINO pretraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed task-oriented knowledge transfer consistently outperforms all three compared baselines across all five target tasks. Against CLIP pretraining (Radford et al., 2021), the method achieves gains of 2.89–22.1% depending on task and transfer set, with the largest margin on ImageNet (up to 22.1% with task-related transfer) and ADE20K (up to 20.9% with CC3M). Against DINO self-supervised pretraining (Caron et al., 2021), gains are even larger, reaching up to 29.8% on ImageNet with CC3M transfer and 21.7% with task-related transfer, while also requiring up to 15× less pretraining compute. Against pseudo-labeling-based semi-supervised learning (Lee et al., 2013), the proposed method achieves 91.0% on EuroSAT (5 img/cls) vs. 87.8% after three rounds of pseudo-labeling, and 86.3% on HAM10K (500 img/cls) vs. 82.4% after two rounds, demonstrating that knowledge transfer from VFMs is more effective than iterative pseudo-labeling even when the latter has access to the same unlabeled data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: knowledge distillation (which transfers knowledge between model architectures, e.g., Hinton et al. 2015, Tian et al. 2020) and transfer learning (which transfers knowledge between tasks, e.g., Lu et al. 2021). The key departure from prior VFM distillation work (DINOv2, GSD) is the explicit task-oriented step of first adapting the VFM to the target task before distilling, rather than performing task-agnostic feature matching. The retrieval-augmented transfer set curation draws on recent retrieval-based data curation for VLM training (Liu et al. 2023, Xu et al. 2023) but repurposes it for curating distillation data rather than training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides a practical, well-evidenced recipe for deploying small models in resource-constrained settings (autonomous driving, medical imaging, industrial automation) where large VFMs are infeasible, with up to 15× compute reduction while outperforming standard pretraining strategies. The finding that task-oriented transfer substantially outperforms task-agnostic transfer challenges the implicit assumption that a VFM's general-purpose knowledge is the most valuable thing to distill. Open questions include whether leveraging large-scale unlabeled domain data to better adapt the VFM (rather than just small labeled sets) would further improve transfer, and whether the approach extends to specialized domains poorly represented in web data (e.g., industrial automation).
