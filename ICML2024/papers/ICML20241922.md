## Problem

Fourier features based positional encoding (PE) requires manual, empirical tuning of the number and values of frequency components for each task, and its stationary encoding struggles to efficiently learn high-frequency functions—especially in data-limited settings like few-view NeRF or speech synthesis. The paper identifies that neither empirically tuned stationary features nor directly trainable Fourier features (as in APE) adequately resolve this: the former cannot transfer across tasks, and the latter face an ill-posed 1D-to-2D learning problem in unbounded space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241922/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SPE is a drop-in replacement for PE that consumes the same inputs as the base model: 3D coordinates and viewing direction for NeRF, phoneme sequences for FastSpeech, or 1D points for NTK regression. The only hyperparameter is L (number of PE components), which should scale with network size (L ∝ N_para); the paper shows that setting L=10 for spatial coordinates and L=4 for direction in NeRF works universally without per-object tuning. Outputs are standard for each task: rendered RGB novel views, audio spectra, or scalar function values.

## Method

SPE is defined as sin(ω·PE(x)), where PE(x) is the standard sinusoidal positional encoding and ω is a trainable weight vector applied as a sinusoidal activation on the PE output. This design makes the effective frequency features adaptive to the input while constraining the search to a bounded space (since PE(x) ∈ [-1,1]), which the paper proves yields more stable and faster training than directly learning sin(ωx) in unbounded space. Theoretical analysis shows SPE can approximate PE with optimally tuned Fourier features (Theorem 3.2), that PE is a special case of SPE in the small-ω limit (Theorem 3.4), and that L controls the approximation resolution (Theorem 3.1). In practice, SPE is implemented by simply replacing the first activation function after PE with a sinusoidal activation—no architectural changes or additional hyperparameter tuning are required—and it can be combined with adversarial (GAN) training to further improve high-frequency fidelity.

## Results

On the 8-view Blender NeRF benchmark, FreeNeRF+SPE+GAN achieves PSNR 25.202 and SSIM 0.910, representing a ~3% PSNR improvement and 1.7% SSIM gain over the SOTA FreeNeRF baseline (24.259 / 0.883). In text-to-speech synthesis with FastSpeech, SPE consistently outperforms both PE and vanilla FastSpeech across all five test cases, with up to 4.6% PSNR improvement (e.g., 103.9 vs. 99.3 in Case 4). In 1D NTK regression, SPE achieves 2× faster convergence than PE across all frequency parameters p, with training loss two orders of magnitude smaller at p=0.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPE (as FreeNeRF+SPE+GAN) outperforms all PE-based baselines: it beats FreeNeRF by 0.94 dB PSNR and 0.027 SSIM, MipNeRF by 1.86 dB, DietNeRF by 2.06 dB, and original NeRF by 10.2 dB. Against hash-encoding methods, SPE's advantage is dramatic—InstantNeRF and NeRFfacto score below 15 dB PSNR, roughly 10 dB worse than SPE, confirming that hash encoding struggles severely with few views. The only other adaptive PE method, APENeRF (Gao et al., 2023), scores 23.067 PSNR, still 2.1 dB below FreeNeRF+SPE+GAN, and produces visibly blurred high-frequency patterns. In speech synthesis, SPE improves over vanilla FastSpeech by 2.7–4.6 dB PSNR and over PE-augmented FastSpeech by 0.8–1.7 dB across all five cases. In 1D NTK regression, SPE converges in ~20,000 iterations versus ~40,000 for PE, and maintains competitive test loss even at p=0.5 where PE overfits.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPE sits at the intersection of two prior threads: Fourier-feature positional encoding (Tancik et al., 2020) and periodic-activation implicit representations (SIREN, Sitzmann et al., 2020). Rather than learning sin(ωx) directly as in Adaptive PE (Gao et al., 2023) — which the authors show is an ill-posed 1D-to-2D problem in unbounded space — SPE composes a sinusoidal activation on top of the standard PE, yielding sin(ω·PE(x)) and thereby bounding the frequency search. This departs from hash-encoding methods (InstantNeRF, NeRFfacto) that perform Monte Carlo-style discretization, which the paper argues requires far more data to resolve collisions and generalize to unseen views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is a drop-in, single-activation-function replacement that removes the need for per-task empirical tuning of Fourier-feature hyperparameters, demonstrated across NeRF, TTS, and NTK regression. The theoretical result that PE is a small-ω limit of SPE (Theorem 3.4) gives a clean justification for why SPE subsumes existing pipelines without degrading them. Open questions include whether the benefit persists at scale (large-scene NeRF, long-form TTS), whether the GAN component is truly necessary or merely complementary, and whether the L ∝ N_para guideline holds for architectures beyond standard MLPs.
