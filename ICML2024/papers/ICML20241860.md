## Problem

Training diffusion-based generative models with nonlinear inference processes is intractable: the DSM objective requires the score of the full transition kernel q(y_t|y_0), which is unavailable for nonlinear drifts, while the ISM objective requires expensive divergence computation via the Hutchinson trace estimator. No automated, derivation-free training procedure existed for nonlinear Langevin processes with non-Gaussian stationary distributions, limiting generative modeling to linear priors and score estimation for physical systems to computationally prohibitive ISM-based approaches.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a data sample x, a nonlinear SDE specified by drift f(y,t) and diffusion coefficient g(t), a model prior π_θ (e.g., mixture of Gaussians or Logistic), and a scheduler s_λ(t) that sets the local linearization window. It outputs a trained score network s_θ, generated samples via the probability-flow ODE, and a differentiable local-DSM ELBO estimate. For image experiments the score network is a U-Net parameterized in noise form; for low-dimensional physics experiments it is a 3-layer feedforward network with antisymmetry constraints.

## Method

The key insight is to replace the intractable full transition score ∇log q(y_t|y_0) with a local transition score ∇log q(y_t|y_s) where s is close to t, then approximate this local kernel via a first-order Taylor expansion of the drift around (y_s, s) or (y_s, t), yielding a locally linear (affine) process with a Gaussian transition kernel whose mean and covariance are computed in closed form using matrix factorization. A scheduled pair s_λ(t) is designed so that ∫_s^t g²(τ)dτ = λ, keeping the Taylor approximation error bounded uniformly across t rather than growing with the time gap. The resulting local-DSM ELBO (Theorem 1) is a valid variational lower bound that requires no divergence computation, enabling faster and more stable training than ISM. For image generation, a perceptually weighted variant reparameterizes the score as s_θ = γ^{-1}(t,s)·ε_θ to give uniform weight across timesteps.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with a non-Gaussian Langevin inference process, local-DSM achieves BPDs of ≤3.496±0.11 (MOG prior) and ≤3.568±0.07 (Logistic prior), outperforming the ISM ELBO baseline by 0.14 and 0.17 BPDs respectively. The perceptually-weighted variant (local-DSM PW) further improves to ≤3.561±0.09 for the Logistic prior. On the 2D checkerboard, local-DSM converges significantly faster than ISM within 20k gradient steps. For non-equilibrium stochastic dynamics and interacting particle systems, local-DSM trained models produce samples nearly identical to the ground-truth inference process, while ISM-trained models diverge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares local-DSM against the ISM ELBO (Huang et al., 2021) as the primary baseline, which is the standard objective for training score models with nonlinear inference processes. On CIFAR-10, local-DSM achieves substantially better BPDs: 3.496 vs. 3.637 for the MOG prior and 3.568 vs. 3.741 for the Logistic prior, a gap of roughly 0.14–0.17 BPDs at equal compute. On the 2D checkerboard, local-DSM produces visibly better samples after only 20k steps compared to ISM. For the non-equilibrium dynamics system (eq. 22) and the interacting particle system (eq. 24) from Boffi & Vanden-Eijnden (2023b), local-DSM trained models generate samples that are near-identical to the ground-truth SDE, whereas ISM-trained models diverge rapidly, as confirmed by MMD comparisons in the appendix. The paper also shows that increasing the scheduler parameter λ (widening the gap between s and t) degrades local-DSM toward the ISM level, confirming the approximation error is controlled by the schedule.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the automated DSM training framework of Singhal et al. (2023), which handled only linear inference processes, to the nonlinear regime by introducing a local-DSM objective built from short-horizon transition kernels q(y_t|y_s) and approximating their scores via first-order Taylor linearization of the drift. The variational ELBO structure follows Huang et al. (2021) and Song & Ermon (2020), while the mean/covariance ODE solutions and matrix factorization technique for solving them are inherited from Särkkä & Solin (2019). The key departure from prior work is that ISM (used by Boffi & Vanden-Eijnden for nonlinear processes) requires the Hutchinson trace estimator for the score divergence, whereas local-DSM avoids this entirely by reducing the problem to sampling from a locally Gaussian transition kernel, making training both faster and less noisy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work removes a significant practical barrier: training diffusion models with non-Gaussian priors (mixture of Gaussians, Logistic) or estimating scores for physically-motivated nonlinear SDEs previously required either intractable PDE solutions or the noisy ISM objective. The scheduled-pair design s_λ(t) that controls Taylor approximation error is a practical contribution that makes the method robust across time scales. Open questions include whether the approach extends to state-dependent diffusion coefficients g(y,t), how the Taylor error scales in high dimensions, and whether the local-DSM framework can be combined with the latent-space or flow-based approaches (Kim et al., Bartosh et al.) for even more flexible inference processes.
