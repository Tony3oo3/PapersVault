## Problem

Inferring a clean 3D scene from a single corrupted image (e.g., with rain, fog, floaters, or unknown camera parameters) is difficult because existing approaches either require knowing the corruption family in advance or rely on ad-hoc regularization. The paper identifies a fundamental issue: standard MAP (point-estimate) inference over joint scene and corruption parameters provably yields 'billboard' solutions where the corruption NeRF absorbs the scene signal, and shows that full posterior inference avoids this pathology.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 128×128 corrupted RGB image as input and jointly infers a clean 3D scene NeRF, a corruption NeRF, and (optionally) camera intrinsics such as field-of-view. Outputs include a depth map (via the 95th-percentile ray-scattering distance), a clean RGB reconstruction rendered from the scene NeRF alone, and the corruption NeRF parameters. The scene is represented either as triplane grids (ShapeNet) or as 512 set-latent vectors (MultiShapeNet), and the corruption is a separate NeRF with an improper uniform prior, so no corruption family must be specified beforehand.

## Method

RIG formulates scene recovery as full posterior inference p(x,c|y) over scene NeRF parameters x and corruption NeRF parameters c, with a strong scene prior p(x) and a uniform corruption prior p(c). For normalizing-flow scene priors (ProbNeRF/RealNVP), inference uses a mean-field variational guide optimized via the ELBO with the path-derivative estimator. For diffusion scene priors (SSDNeRF), the paper introduces ReGAL, which alternates between reconstruction-guidance denoising steps on the diffusion latent z and Langevin updates on the corruption latent c, with an importance-sampling generalization (K weighted particles) providing convergence guarantees. The key theoretical contribution is a proof that MAP solutions necessarily include billboard configurations (Proposition 2.1), while the full posterior concentrates on high-probability-mass regions where the corruption does not fully obscure the scene.

## Results

On ShapeNet, SSDNeRF ReGAL (K=8) achieves the best VSD across all corruption types: 0.27 (Clean), 0.25 (Rain), 0.41 (Cloud), and 0.34 (FOV), substantially outperforming the DPT baseline (0.30, 0.63, 0.58, 0.49 respectively) on corrupted conditions. Full posterior inference (ProbNeRF VI, SSDNeRF ReGAL) consistently outperforms MAP point estimation (ProbNeRF MAP) on corrupted scenes, e.g. 0.32 vs 0.43 VSD for Rain. On MultiShapeNet, DPT achieves lower VSD (0.90–0.92) than RIG (0.87–0.95), which the authors attribute to the scene prior lacking sufficient fidelity for the more complex scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is DPT (Ranftl et al., 2021), a powerful feedforward depth estimator trained on diverse indoor/outdoor scenes. On ShapeNet, RIG methods decisively outperform DPT on all corrupted conditions: SSDNeRF ReGAL (K=8) achieves 0.25 VSD for Rain vs DPT's 0.63, and 0.34 for FOV vs DPT's 0.49, while matching DPT on clean images (0.27 vs 0.30). On MultiShapeNet, however, DPT outperforms RIG across all conditions (e.g., 0.90 vs 0.88 VSD for Rain), which the authors attribute to the scene prior not capturing fine details of the more complex MSN scenes. Internally, full posterior inference (ProbNeRF VI, SSDNeRF ReGAL) consistently beats MAP point estimation (ProbNeRF MAP) on corrupted ShapeNet scenes, confirming the paper's theoretical argument that MAP leads to 'billboard' solutions where the corruption NeRF absorbs the scene signal. The importance sampling variant (K=8) provides the largest gains for FOV estimation (0.34 vs 0.49 for K=1), while gains for Rain and Cloud are marginal.

## Relation to Prior Work

RIG builds on ProbNeRF (a RealNVP prior over NeRFs) and SSDNeRF (a diffusion-based NeRF prior) as scene priors, and extends reconstruction-guidance conditioning (Ho et al., 2022) into ReGAL, a diffusion conditioning algorithm that handles auxiliary latent variables (the corruption NeRF) not covered by the diffusion prior. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant paradigm of domain randomization or robust-loss training for corruption-robust perception, instead framing the problem as full Bayesian posterior inference over jointly unknown scene and corruption parameters. The key theoretical departure is the proof that MAP inference (equivalent to standard fine-tuning) is inherently susceptible to 'billboard' solutions where the corruption absorbs the entire observation, a failure mode absent from full posterior inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that scene priors—previously used primarily for generation—can serve as the backbone of robust perception systems that generalize to unseen corruption types without retraining, a capability that domain randomization fundamentally lacks. ReGAL is positioned as a general-purpose diffusion conditioning tool for latent variable models with auxiliary variables, potentially applicable beyond 3D graphics. However, two major limitations constrain near-term impact: the method requires a 3D prior over real-world scenes (currently only available for synthetic datasets), and inference takes 7.5–9.5 minutes per image, making real-time deployment infeasible without amortized inference or faster samplers. The MSN results, where the prior's limited fidelity prevents clear gains over DPT, underscore that prior quality is the bottleneck for broader applicability.
