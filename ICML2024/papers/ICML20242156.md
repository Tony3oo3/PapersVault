## Problem

LLM safety alignment is empirically brittle—small modifications like non-malicious fine-tuning or adversarial prompts can jailbreak well-aligned models—yet the structural reason for this fragility remains unclear. This paper asks whether safety-critical regions in model weights are sparse and disentangled from utility regions, which would explain why safety is so easily compromised while general capabilities persist.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242156/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two separate calibration datasets—a safety set (AdvBench harmful instructions paired with the model's refusal responses) and a utility set (Alpaca-Cleaned general instruction pairs)—to compute per-neuron or per-rank importance scores on each. It produces a sparse set of safety-critical neurons (~3% of weights) or safety-critical ranks (~2.5% of total ranks), and when these are removed, the model's attack success rate jumps from 0 to over 90% across three threat models (vanilla, adversarial suffix, adversarial decoding) while zero-shot utility accuracy on six benchmark tasks remains above 0.5. The attribution operates block-wise across all linear layers (self-attention Q/K/V/O and MLP up/gate/down) using output-wise sparsity per matrix row.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary attribution pipelines. At the neuron level, it computes importance scores via SNIP (gradient-based loss sensitivity) or Wanda (activation-weighted magnitude), then applies a set-difference operation: safety-critical neurons are those in the top-q% safety scores but *not* in the top-p% utility scores, isolating regions exclusively responsible for safety. At the rank level, it introduces ActSVD, which performs SVD on the product WX_in (weight matrix times stacked response activations) to identify behavior-specific rank subspaces, then applies orthogonal projection ΔW = (I − Π^u)Π^s W to extract safety-critical ranks that are orthogonal to utility-critical ranks. The key finding is that these isolated regions are remarkably sparse—removing fewer than 3% of neurons or fewer than 100 of 4096 ranks destroys safety while preserving utility—suggesting safety alignment is encoded in a thin, fragile layer of the model. The paper further shows that freezing these identified safety-critical neurons during fine-tuning does not prevent jailbreaking, as fine-tuning can create alternative pathways that bypass the original safety mechanisms.

## Results

On Llama2-7B-chat, removing safety-critical neurons identified via set difference (SNIP scores) at <3% sparsity drives ASR from 0 to >0.9 across all three attack settings (Vanilla, Adv-Suffix, Adv-Decoding) while preserving average zero-shot accuracy above 0.5. At the rank level, removing just 2.5% of ranks (fewer than 100 out of 4096) via orthogonal projection (ActSVD) achieves ASR_Vanilla=0.71, ASR_Adv-Suffix=0.97, ASR_Adv-Decoding=0.91 with 0.58 accuracy. In contrast, removing top-safety neurons without disentanglement drops accuracy to ~0.35, confirming that safety and utility regions overlap and must be separated. Freezing up to 67% of safety-critical neurons during fine-tuning still fails to prevent jailbreaking (ASR remains 0.85–0.94 for n≥50 Alpaca examples).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its disentanglement methods (set difference for neurons, orthogonal projection for ranks) against four baselines: SNIP-top, Wanda-top, attention-head probing, and ActSVD-top. Set difference with SNIP consistently outperforms the attention-head probing baseline, achieving higher ASR at the same accuracy level across both Llama2-7B-chat and Llama2-13B-chat (Figure 2a). Removing top-safety neurons (SNIP-top or Wanda-top) without disentanglement also achieves high ASR but at the cost of dropping average accuracy from 0.58 to ~0.35, whereas set difference maintains accuracy above 0.51. At the rank level, orthogonal projection (ActSVD with projection) outperforms ActSVD-top: removing just the top-1 safety rank drops accuracy to 0.38, while the orthogonally-projected removal of 6 safety ranks (keeping 96 utility ranks) achieves ASR_Adv-Suffix=0.97 with 0.58 accuracy. The probing baseline, despite over half of attention heads achieving >0.95 accuracy in distinguishing harmful from harmless instructions, fails to isolate safety-critical regions as effectively as the neuron-level set difference approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes established pruning and low-rank compression techniques (SNIP, Wanda, SVD-based methods) for a new purpose: identifying and isolating safety-critical regions in aligned LLMs. The key methodological departure from prior attribution work is the explicit disentanglement of safety from utility via set difference (neuron level) and orthogonal projection (rank level), addressing the entanglement problem that makes naive top-scoring approaches destroy utility alongside safety. The paper positions itself against attention-head probing (Hewitt & Liang, 2019) as insufficient for this task, and against fine-tuning attack literature (Qi et al., 2024b; Zhan et al., 2023) by providing a mechanistic explanation for why those attacks succeed. The ActSVD method extends prior activation-aware SVD approaches (ASVD, FWSVD) by performing SVD directly on the output matrix WX_in rather than a weighted version of W.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that safety is encoded in only ~3% of parameters and ~2.5% of ranks provides a concrete, model-intrinsic metric for assessing alignment brittleness that complements red-teaming. The failure of the intuitive defense—freezing safety-critical neurons to block fine-tuning attacks—reveals that adversaries can create entirely new pathways, pointing to a fundamental limitation of parameter-level defenses. The observation that removing least-safety-relevant neurons can *improve* robustness opens a promising but underexplored direction for safety-enhancing pruning. Key limitations include restriction to the Llama2-chat family and the question of whether these findings generalize to models aligned with different strategies (e.g., DPO, Constitutional AI).
