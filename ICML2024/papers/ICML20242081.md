## Problem

Value Iteration Networks (VINs) fail at long-term planning: success rates drop below 10% when shortest path lengths exceed ~120 steps, and training deeper VINs (needed to cover longer horizons) collapses due to vanishing/exploding gradients. Standard deep-learning remedies like highway or residual connections have not transferred effectively to VINs because the planning module's RL-derived inductive bias (the Bellman optimality operator) interacts poorly with naive skip connections.

## Input / Output

For 2D maze tasks, the input is a 4×m×m tensor encoding the maze map plus start and goal positions across four orientations; for 3D ViZDoom, RGB first-person-view images are first processed by a CNN to predict the top-down maze map before feeding the planning module. The output is a sequence of discrete navigation actions (move forward, turn left, turn right) that the agent executes to reach the goal within a step budget. The planning module operates on a learned latent MDP (reward and transition tensors) mapped from the observation, not directly on the raw input.

## Method

The highway VIN replaces the standard stacked VI modules in the planning module with highway blocks that implement the highway value iteration operator. Each block contains: (1) a standard VI module (Bellman optimality step), (2) N_p parallel Value Exploration (VE) modules stacked for N_b−1 layers, where each VE module computes a Bellman expectation under an ε-greedy embedded policy rather than a max, thereby diversifying spatial information and gradient flow, and (3) an aggregate gate (softmax-weighted combination across depths and parallel branches) plus a filter gate (element-wise max against the base VI output) that together form a skip-connection architecture while discarding harmful exploratory activations. The full network of N_B highway blocks (yielding hundreds of total layers) is trained end-to-end via standard backpropagation with imitation learning, requiring only a small number of additional learnable parameters (softmax temperatures per block) over the original VIN.

## Results

On the 25×25 maze with shortest path lengths (SPL) in [130,230], Highway VIN at depth 300 achieves a 90.06% success rate, while VIN (N=30) drops to 2.96%, GPPN (N=30) to 2.76%, and Highway network (N=60) to 17.46%. On the 15×15 maze with SPL [60,100], Highway VIN (N=200) reaches 98.20% versus 76.16% for VIN (N=40) and 65.17% for GPPN (N=40). On 3D ViZDoom navigation with SPL [60,100], Highway VIN achieves 96.98% compared to 69.37% (VIN) and 44.42% (GPPN). Highway VIN is the only method that maintains >90% success rate on the hardest 25×25 maze tasks, demonstrating that depth scaling (up to 300 layers) is effective only with the proposed highway architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Highway VIN decisively outperforms all three baselines on long-horizon planning. On the hardest 25×25 maze tasks (SPL [130,230]), Highway VIN (N=300) achieves 90.06% success rate versus 2.96% for VIN, 1.32% for GPPN, and 17.46% for Highway network — a gap of 87+ percentage points over VIN and GPPN. On the 15×15 maze (SPL [60,100]), Highway VIN reaches 98.20% compared to 76.16% (VIN), 65.17% (GPPN), and 75.86% (Highway network). On short-horizon tasks (SPL [1,30] or [1,60]), all methods perform comparably at 97–99%, indicating the advantage is specifically in long-term credit assignment. Notably, VIN and Highway network collapse to near 0% when depth exceeds ~150–200 layers, while Highway VIN improves monotonically with depth up to 300 layers. GPPN is the most robust baseline across depths but plateaus and never exceeds ~29% on the hardest 25×25 tasks regardless of depth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper embeds the highway value iteration algorithm (Wang et al., 2024), a credit-assignment method from RL, into the planning module of Value Iteration Networks (Tamar et al., 2016), drawing architectural inspiration from highway networks (Srivastava et al., 2015) and ResNets (He et al., 2016). Unlike prior VIN variants that address training stability (GPPN) or generalization (Transfer VIN, AVIN), this work specifically targets the depth-scaling bottleneck for long-term planning by introducing a value exploration module, an aggregate gate, and a filter gate. A key conceptual contribution is revealing that highway RL and highway networks—developed independently for different purposes—share a common architectural principle of gated multi-path information flow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Highway VINs enable training 300-layer planning networks with standard backpropagation, achieving 90%+ success on 25×25 mazes with shortest path lengths of 130–230 where all baselines (VIN, GPPN, highway networks) drop to near 0%. The ablation showing that skip connections alone do not improve planning—only the combination of diverse exploration (VE module) and safe filtering (filter gate) does—challenges the assumption that depth-scaling techniques transfer directly from classification to planning. Limitations include evaluation restricted to maze navigation (2D and 3D ViZDoom) with imitation learning; open questions include scaling to larger, real-world planning domains and whether multiple parallel VE modules with heterogeneous policies can further improve performance.
