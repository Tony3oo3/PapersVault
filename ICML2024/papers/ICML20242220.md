## Problem

Previous coreset selection methods fix the coreset size in advance and optimize only model performance, leaving practitioners without a principled way to find the smallest coreset that still performs comparably to full data. This paper introduces refined coreset selection (RCS), which jointly minimizes model performance and coreset size under a strict priority order (performance first, size second), and shows that simple modifications to existing methods—such as weighted objective combinations—fail to respect this hierarchy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled dataset of n samples, a neural network architecture, a predefined upper-bound coreset size k, and a non-negative performance compromise parameter ε that caps how much the primary objective f1 may degrade in exchange for a smaller coreset. It outputs a binary mask m ∈ {0,1}^n (where m_i=1 selects sample i) whose L0 norm is typically smaller than the initial k, yielding a refined coreset subset. The mask is initialized at size k and progressively shrunk during the outer-loop search; in practice, examples can be grouped so that group members share a single mask bit to reduce search space on large datasets.

## Method

LBCS formulates RCS as a cardinality-constrained bilevel optimization with lexicographic preferences: the inner loop trains the network to convergence on the current coreset, while the outer loop updates the binary mask via a randomized direct search (LexiFlow) that compares candidate masks using lexicographic relations over the ordered objective list [f1, f2]. The lexicographic comparison first checks whether f1 (full-data loss of the coreset-trained model) improves; only when f1 falls within the ε-compromise region M1* does the algorithm optimize f2 (coreset size). This avoids the intractable weight-tuning of scalarized objectives and provably reaches ε-convergence under progressable and stable moving conditions (Theorem 2). Practical acceleration tricks include warm-starting the inner loop across mask updates, exploiting model sparsity, and grouping examples to shrink the effective search dimension.

## Results

On SVHN, LBCS consistently achieves the best test accuracy across all predefined coreset sizes (e.g., 70.6% at k=1000, 84.6% at k=4000) while simultaneously reducing the actual coreset size (e.g., 970 vs. 1000, 3804 vs. 4000). On F-MNIST, LBCS reaches 84.5% at k=4000 with only 3745 samples, outperforming Probabilistic (84.2% at full 4000) and Moderate (82.4%). On CIFAR-10, LBCS achieves 69.5% at k=3000 with 2914 samples, beating Probabilistic (68.7%) and Moderate (69.2%). On ImageNet-1k, LBCS attains 90.84% top-5 accuracy at 80% predefined ratio while optimizing the actual ratio down to 77.82%, surpassing all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LBCS dominates on SVHN across all coreset sizes, beating the strongest baseline Probabilistic by 2.8 points at k=1000 (70.6% vs. 67.8%) and by 0.3–1.7 points at larger k, while also reducing the actual coreset size by 3–4%. On F-MNIST, LBCS wins at k=2000–4000 (e.g., 84.5% vs. 84.2% for Probabilistic at k=4000) but trails Probabilistic slightly at k=1000 (79.7% vs. 80.3%). On CIFAR-10, LBCS leads at k=1000–3000 (e.g., 69.5% vs. 68.7% for Probabilistic at k=3000) but is marginally behind Moderate at k=4000 (73.4% vs. 73.9%). EL2N and GraNd are consistently the weakest baselines, trailing LBCS by 8–12 points on SVHN and 10–15 points on CIFAR-10. The critical differentiator is that LBCS achieves these gains with a smaller actual coreset size (e.g., 957 vs. 1000 on F-MNIST, 2914 vs. 3000 on CIFAR-10), directly fulfilling the refined coreset selection objective of minimizing size under performance constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bilevel coreset selection framework introduced by Borsos et al. (NeurIPS 2020) and Zhou et al. (ICML 2022), which optimize only model performance under a fixed coreset size. The key departure is the introduction of a second, lower-priority objective—minimizing coreset size—formalized through lexicographic preferences rather than the intractable weighted combination of objectives. For the outer-loop optimization, the authors adapt the LexiFlow randomized direct search algorithm from Zhang et al. (ICLR 2023), originally designed for hyperparameter optimization with lexicographic preferences, repurposing it for binary mask selection in coreset construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RCS formulation addresses a practical gap: practitioners rarely have a fixed coreset size in mind and instead want the smallest subset that still performs well. The lexicographic framework avoids the scale-mismatch problem that makes weighted multi-objective optimization intractable here, and the ε-compromise mechanism provides a useful regularizer against overfitting during coreset selection, yielding robustness gains under label noise and class imbalance. Open questions include the unknown convergence rate, the computational cost of bilevel optimization (O(TK) per selection), and whether the approach can be extended to non-bilevel coreset methods or scaled to accelerate pre-training of large foundation models.
