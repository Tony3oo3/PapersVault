## Problem

Diffusion models produce high-quality images but require hundreds to thousands of sampling steps, making them impractical for real-time applications. Existing acceleration strategies—fast ODE solvers, knowledge distillation from pretrained teachers, and consistency models—either degrade sharply at one step, impose architectural constraints, or add significant training overhead. The paper asks whether a diffusion model can be trained to directly predict the clean image in a single forward pass while still supporting multi-step refinement, without any ODE solver or distillation pipeline.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a random Gaussian noise vector x_T ~ N(0,I) (and a class label for ImageNet 64×64) and produces a 32×32 or 64×64 RGB image. Unusually, the network also consumes an estimated target x_0^(n) as a conditioning input via cross-attention at 16×16 and 8×8 resolutions; during one-step sampling this is simply another N(0,I) draw, while multi-step sampling feeds the previous iteration's output back as the next conditioning. Training additionally requires the ground-truth image x_0 and a randomly sampled timestep t to construct the noisy input x_t.

## Method

DDDM replaces the standard PF-ODE solver with a single neural network F_θ that directly maps (x_0^(n), x_t, t) to the denoised estimate via f_θ = x_t − F_θ, eliminating all numerical integration. Training is iterative: at epoch n the network is conditioned on the previous epoch's prediction x_0^(n) for each sample, and the loss (Pseudo-LPIPS = √(LPIPS + c²) − c) is computed between the new prediction and the ground-truth x_0, so the estimate progressively converges toward real data. Sampling mirrors this loop: starting from x_0^(0) ~ N(0,I) and x_T ~ N(0,I), the model iterates x_0^(n+1) = x_T − F_θ(x_0^(n), x_T, T) for s steps, yielding one-step generation at s=1 or higher-quality multi-step output at s>1. The architecture is the ADM U-Net (128 or 192 base channels, 3 residual blocks per stage) with cross-attention modules at two resolutions to inject the conditioning target, and a deeper variant (DDDM-deep) doubles blocks per resolution. The paper provides convergence theorems showing that as the iterative loss vanishes, the pushforward distribution converges uniformly to the data distribution under Lipschitz and boundedness conditions.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, DDDM-deep (T=1000) achieves FID 2.57 and 2.33 for one-step and two-step sampling respectively, surpassing StyleGAN2-ADA (FID 2.92) and iCT (FID 2.83/2.46). Extending to 1000 sampling steps reduces FID to 1.79, matching EDM-G++ (1.77 at 35 NFE). On ImageNet 64×64, DDDM-deep achieves FID 3.47 (1-step) and 3.08 (2-step), outperforming iCT (4.02/3.20) but trailing iCT-deep (3.25/2.77), which benefits from 4× larger batch size and 1.6× more training iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR-10, DDDM-deep (1-step FID 2.57) outperforms all GAN baselines (StyleGAN2-ADA: 2.92, BigGAN: 14.7) and most distillation-based methods (CD: 3.55, TRACT: 3.78, DFNO: 3.78), and also beats iCT (2.83) by 0.26 FID. However, iCT-deep (2.51) retains a slight edge over DDDM-deep (2.57) at 1-step, and at 2-step iCT-deep (2.24) still leads DDDM-deep (2.33). At 1000 steps, DDDM-deep (1.79) is competitive with EDM-G++ (1.77 at only 35 NFE), and DDDM with T=8000 achieves 1.74, setting a new state-of-the-art. On ImageNet 64×64, DDDM-deep (3.47/3.08) beats iCT (4.02/3.20) but trails iCT-deep (3.25/2.77), which the authors attribute to iCT-deep's 4× larger batch size and 1.6× more training iterations. DDDM also improves precision and recall over iCT on ImageNet, indicating better mode coverage despite the FID gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

DDDM builds directly on the DDPM noise schedule (Ho et al., 2020) and the probability flow ODE formulation from score-based SDEs (Song et al., 2020b), but departs from both the ODE-solver-based samplers (DDIM, DPM-Solver, DEIS) and the distillation-based approaches (consistency models, knowledge distillation) that dominate the few-step generation literature. Instead of numerically integrating the PF ODE or compressing a teacher model's trajectory, DDDM parameterizes the ODE solution itself with a neural network and recovers x_0 via a simple iterative fixed-point update, unifying training and inference in a single framework. The Pseudo-LPIPS loss is a direct analogue of the Pseudo-Huber loss introduced in improved consistency training (Song & Dhariwal, 2023), adapted to the perceptual metric domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical appeal is eliminating the need for a pretrained teacher model and hand-crafted ODE solvers, simplifying the training pipeline for few-step diffusion generation. However, the approach carries a non-trivial memory overhead (tracking x_0^(n) per training sample: 29.5 GB for ImageNet 64×64), and the authors themselves flag a potential evaluation bias since LPIPS and the Inception network both rely on ImageNet features, which could inflate FID scores. On ImageNet 64×64, DDDM still trails iCT-deep by a meaningful margin (FID 2.11 vs 2.77 at 1000 steps), which the authors attribute to compute disparities rather than methodological shortcomings. Open questions include whether the iterative scheme scales to higher resolutions or video, whether the memory cost can be mitigated, and whether the evaluation bias concern materially affects the reported numbers.
