## Problem

Weightless neural networks (WNNs) using binary lookup tables offer extreme inference efficiency but have been restricted to single-layer architectures because the discrete LUT structure makes multi-layer gradient-based training infeasible. The closest prior work, DiffLogicNet, enables multi-layer training but scales double-exponentially O(2^{2^n}) with LUT size, requiring 18.4 quintillion parameters for a single LUT-6, and both approaches rely on random connections, costly popcount circuits, and lack effective regularization for binary tables.

## Input / Output

Real-valued inputs (grayscale images, audio features, or tabular columns) are binarized via Distributive Thermometer encoding into ordered unary bit vectors, which are then grouped into fixed-size tuples to form binary addresses for the first LUT layer. No arithmetic operations occur between layers—each layer's binary outputs simply form the addresses for the next layer's LUTs. The final output is a class score per class, obtained either by summing the last layer's binary outputs (popcount) or, for ultra-low-cost chip deployments, by a learned pyramidal LUT reduction that directly produces the class decision.

## Method

The DWN chains multiple layers of binary lookup tables (LUT-n) and trains them end-to-end via a novel Extended Finite Difference (EFD) technique that approximates partial derivatives of the LUT addressing function by summing contributions from all 2^n table positions weighted by inverse Hamming distance, reducing training complexity from O(2^{2^n}) to O(2^n) per LUT. Learnable Mapping introduces a weight matrix that selects which input bits feed each LUT via argmax during training, then is discarded at inference so connections become fixed with zero overhead. Learnable Reduction replaces the final popcount with a decreasing pyramidal stack of LUTs that learns the class decision, shrinking circuit area for tiny models. Spectral Regularization penalizes the L2 norm of the Fourier coefficients of each LUT's pseudo-Boolean function, providing a WNN-specific overfitting control that conventional L1/L2 penalties cannot offer for binary tables.

## Results

On FPGA (Xilinx Zynq Z-7045), DWNs with n=6 LUTs achieve geometric-average improvements of 20.7× latency, 12.3× throughput, 121.6× energy per sample, and 11.7× area versus FINN BNNs, and 3.3×, 2.3×, 19.0×, and 22.7× versus ULEEN, while matching or exceeding accuracy on all datasets except CIFAR-10 (where FINN's CNN architecture gives it a 22.7-point accuracy edge). On the Elegoo Nano microcontroller, throughput-optimized DWNs achieve 1.2% higher average accuracy than XGBoost with a 15% speedup; accuracy-optimized DWNs gain 5.4% at the cost of 8.3× slower inference. For ultra-low-cost chips, DWNs reduce NAND2 circuit area by up to 42.8× versus Tiny Classifiers and 310× versus DiffLogicNet while maintaining or improving accuracy. On tabular data, DWNs achieve an average rank of 2.5 (vs. 3.4 for XGBoost, 3.6 for TabNet) and an average L1 distance of 0.005 from the best model per dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FINN (BNN), DWNs win decisively on all hardware metrics (20.7× lower latency, 121.6× lower energy, 11.7× smaller area on geometric average) while matching accuracy on MNIST, FashionMNIST, KWS, and ToyADMOS; FINN's only advantage is CIFAR-10 accuracy (80.1% vs. 57.4%) due to its support for convolution. Against ULEEN (prior SOTA WNN), DWNs improve latency by 3.3×, energy by 19.0×, and area by 22.7× while achieving equal or better accuracy on all non-CIFAR datasets. Against DiffLogicNet, DWNs match or exceed accuracy on every dataset (e.g., 71.52% vs. 64.18% on KWS, 89.01% vs. 87.44% on FashionMNIST) while using 4–5× fewer LUTs and 2–3× less energy. Against other LUT-based networks (LogicNets, PolyLUT, NeuraLUT) on the xcvu9p FPGA, DWNs achieve higher accuracy with dramatically fewer LUTs (e.g., 2,092 vs. 54,798 for NeuraLUT on MNIST) and 67.8× lower area-delay product on JSC. On the Elegoo Nano microcontroller, DWNs outperform XGBoost by 1.2% average accuracy with 15% higher throughput (unpacked) or 5.4% higher accuracy (bit-packed). For ultra-low-cost chips, DWNs beat Tiny Classifiers on both accuracy and NAND2 area (e.g., 98.9% at 88 NAND vs. 93% at 300 NAND on skin-seg) and beat DiffLogicNet by up to 310× in circuit size. On tabular data, DWNs achieve the best average rank (2.5) among all compared models, surpassing XGBoost (3.4), CatBoost (3.6), LightGBM (4.5), TabNN (3.6), and TabNet (7.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weightless neural network (WNN) lineage—exemplified by ULEEN's single-layer FPGA deployment—into the multi-layer regime by introducing Extended Finite Difference (EFD), which reduces the training complexity of LUT-based backpropagation from O(2^2^n) (as in DiffLogicNet) to O(2^n). It departs from both WNNs and DiffLogicNet by replacing their fixed random inter-layer connections with Learnable Mapping, substituting the area-expensive popcount output with Learnable Reduction, and adding a WNN-specific Spectral Regularization. The architecture is designed to natively align with FPGA LUT-6 resources, a hardware-software co-design consideration absent in prior WNN work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DWNs demonstrate that multiplication-free, LUT-based networks can be trained to multi-layer depth and deployed across a wide hardware spectrum (FPGA, $1.50 microcontroller, sub-100-NAND ASIC) with competitive accuracy, opening a practical path for ultra-low-power edge inference. A notable limitation is the significant accuracy gap on CIFAR-10 (57% vs. 80% for FINN's convolutional BNN), suggesting the approach may struggle with complex spatial hierarchies without convolutional or attentional structure. Open questions include whether EFD-based training scales to deeper networks or larger LUTs, and whether the architecture can be extended to CNN or Transformer blocks for broader vision and language tasks.
