## Problem

Pre-trained vision models such as CLIP suffer significant performance degradation under Out-of-Distribution (OOD) scenarios, and standard fine-tuning to recover robustness requires costly human annotation. While Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) possess strong visual understanding, they struggle with vision classification tasks due to the fundamental incompatibility between text generation and visual recognition, making them unreliable as direct classifiers. The paper asks whether MLLMs can be effectively leveraged to diagnose and rectify vision model errors in a nearly unsupervised manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240804/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled OOD images, a pre-trained vision model (e.g., CLIP ViT-L or EVA ViT-g), and a small confidence-sorted support set (ρ examples per class with clean labels) used to estimate a noise transition matrix and retrieve in-context exemplars. Rather than asking the MLLM to classify among all C classes, it is posed a binary True/False question per candidate class using only a positive exemplar (correct image-class pair) and a negative exemplar (image from the most confusing class paired with the query class name). The outputs are rectified pseudo-labels for fine-tuning, a fine-tuned vision model deployable at inference time, and a detection score Δ (averaging MLLM and vision-model confidence) usable for OOD/open-class detection.

## Method

Machine Vision Therapy (MVT) operates in three stages. First, a noise transition matrix T is estimated by uniformly sampling high-confidence OOD examples per class to form a support set, capturing the probability that one true class is mispredicted as another. Second, Denoising In-Context Learning (DICL) performs two sub-steps: Diagnosing, which asks the MLLM whether the vision model's top prediction is correct by presenting a positive exemplar from the predicted class and a negative exemplar from the highest-noise-probability class, and Therapy, which traverses the top-N noisy classes (guided by T) asking the MLLM a binary question for each, selecting the class with the highest 'True' probability. A detection score Δ combining MLLM and vision-model confidence gates whether therapy is needed. Third, the rectified MLLM predictions serve as pseudo-labels to fine-tune the vision model via cross-entropy loss. Theoretically, under distribution consistency and concept distinguishability assumptions, DICL is guaranteed to recover the ground-truth class as the number of in-context exemplars grows.

## Results

On the primary OOD benchmark ImageNet-V, MVT+FT with ViT-L backbone achieves 68.8% accuracy, surpassing CLIP ViT-L (51.8%) by 17% and EVA ViT-g (64.9%) by 6%. On ImageNet-A, MVT+FT reaches 75.1% (ViT-L) and 75.5% (ViT-g), improving over the respective baselines by 5.8% and 3.6%. On iWildCam, MVT alone (without fine-tuning) boosts accuracy from 13.4% (CLIP) to 25.0%, a near-doubling. Across DomainBed datasets (VLCS, PACS, OfficeHome, DomainNet), MVT+FT achieves an average of 84.0% (ViT-L) and 84.4% (ViT-g), outperforming CLIP/EVA baselines by 3.4–3.8% on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVT+FT consistently outperforms both CLIP ViT-L and EVA ViT-g baselines across all OOD datasets. On ImageNet-V, the improvement is most dramatic: 68.8% (ViT-L+FT) vs. 51.8% (CLIP) and 70.6% (ViT-g+FT) vs. 64.9% (EVA), representing gains of 17% and 6% respectively. On ImageNet-A, MVT+FT achieves 75.1% (ViT-L) and 75.5% (ViT-g), beating CLIP by 5.8% and EVA by 3.6%. On iWildCam, MVT alone (without fine-tuning) already doubles the CLIP baseline from 13.4% to 25.0%, though fine-tuning is omitted here due to high error rates in the vision model predictions. MVT also surpasses the Vanilla FT baseline (which uses only the small support set for fine-tuning) on nearly all OOD datasets, confirming that the MLLM-rectified supervision provides more effective training signals than the limited clean labels alone. Direct VQA prompting of MLLMs underperforms the vision model baselines on most ID datasets, validating the paper's motivation for using MLLMs as a therapy signal rather than a direct classifier.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: learning with noisy labels (transition matrix estimation à la Natarajan et al., 2013) and in-context learning theory (Xie et al., 2021). It departs from prior MLLM-for-vision work (Flamingo, Otter, MMICL) that attempts to use MLLMs as direct classifiers, instead repurposing them as a diagnostic/rectification tool for vision model errors. The Denoising In-Context Learning strategy is a novel twist on standard ICL: rather than presenting all class exemplars, it frames the problem as a binary true/false judgment using only a positive and a negative exemplar pair, grounded in the noisy-label transition matrix to select which pair to present.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical pathway to improve OOD robustness without large-scale human annotation, which is a significant cost barrier in deployment. However, it still requires a small labeled support set (ρ per class) for transition matrix estimation, and MLLM inference is slow and memory-intensive, limiting real-time applicability. Open questions include whether the method extends to truly open-set scenarios without any class candidates, how it scales as MLLMs improve, and whether the transition matrix assumption (class-conditional noise) holds under severe distribution shifts. The finding that support set distribution shift does not degrade performance is encouraging but was tested on a limited set of domain pairs.
