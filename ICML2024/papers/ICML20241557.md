## Problem

Protein-conditioned RNA design is challenging because RNA's conformational flexibility means a single static structure output bottlenecks downstream sequence generation, and fine-tuning large structure prediction networks (e.g., RF2NA) for generative tasks is computationally prohibitive. No existing method simultaneously generates RNA sequences and structures conditioned on a target protein while accounting for RNA dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241557/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a protein backbone atom structure (N, Cα, C coordinates per residue) and protein sequence as fixed conditions, and generates an RNA nucleotide sequence (A/C/G/U tokens) along with its 3D backbone structure (P, C4', N1/N9 coordinates per nucleotide). At inference, a 'pose guess' is obtained by folding the protein MSA with a mock all-adenine RNA of the target length, which orients the protein relative to the RNA centroid before the 5-step flow matching refinement loop begins.

## Method

RNAFlow uses conditional flow matching where the score predictor is a composite of a GVP-GNN inverse folding model (Noise-to-Seq) and a frozen RF2NA network: given a noised RNA backbone, Noise-to-Seq autoregressively predicts an RNA sequence, and RF2NA folds it into the denoised structure. Training minimizes MSE on backbone coordinates (after Kabsch alignment) plus cross-entropy on nucleotide identity, with Gumbel-Softmax enabling gradient flow through the discrete sequence prediction. The key architectural innovation is the Traj-to-Seq module, a multi-graph GNN that conditions on the final few structures from the flow matching trajectory as an approximate conformational ensemble, improving sequence recovery by capturing RNA dynamics that a single structure cannot represent. The inverse folding model is pre-trained on clean protein-RNA complexes before fine-tuning on the flow matching objective, avoiding the cost of training RF2NA from scratch.

## Results

On the RF2NA pre-training split, RNAFlow-Base achieves 12.85±0.63 RMSD and 0.51±0.01 lDDT for structure generation, outperforming Conditional MMDiff (14.82±1.01 RMSD, 0.34±0.02 lDDT) by 28% in RMSD and 56% in lDDT. For sequence generation, RNAFlow-Base reaches 0.30±0.02 native sequence recovery on the RF2NA split and 0.30±0.01 on the sequence similarity split, surpassing MMDiff (0.24, 0.22) and the LSTM baseline (0.27, 0.24). In the GRK2 motif-scaffolded aptamer design task, RNAFlow-Trajectory achieves 7.09 RMSD and 0.54 recovery, improving over MMDiff (9.19 RMSD, 0.32 recovery) by 22.85% in RMSD and 68.75% in recovery.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RNAFlow outperforms both baselines across all evaluated metrics. Against Conditional MMDiff (Morehead et al., 2023), RNAFlow-Base reduces RMSD by 28% (12.85 vs 14.82) and increases lDDT by 56% (0.51 vs 0.34) on the RF2NA split, with similar margins on the sequence similarity split (16% RMSD reduction, 50% lDDT increase). For sequence recovery, RNAFlow-Base achieves 0.30 vs MMDiff's 0.24 on the RF2NA split and 0.30 vs 0.22 on the sequence similarity split. Against the LSTM baseline (Im et al., 2019), RNAFlow-Base improves sequence recovery from 0.27 to 0.30 on the RF2NA split and from 0.24 to 0.30 on the sequence similarity split; the LSTM also degrades on the harder sequence similarity split while RNAFlow remains stable. In the GRK2 aptamer design task, RNAFlow-Trajectory achieves 0.54 recovery vs MMDiff's 0.32 and LSTM's 0.29, with a 22.85% RMSD improvement over MMDiff (7.09 vs 9.19). The paper notes a remaining gap to the RF2NA upper bound (4.67 RMSD), reflecting the inherent difficulty of de novo design versus folding.

## Relation to Prior Work

RNAFlow adapts the flow matching framework established for protein backbone generation (Yim et al., 2023a; Bose et al., 2023) to the RNA domain, but makes a key architectural departure from RFDiffusion (Watson et al., 2023): rather than fine-tuning a large structure prediction network, it keeps RF2NA frozen and trains only an RNA inverse folding model (Noise-to-Seq) as the score predictor, with RF2NA serving as a fixed folding oracle. The inverse folding component builds on multi-state RNA design (Joshi et al., 2023), repurposing flow matching inference trajectories as a proxy for conformational ensembles. Compared to MMDiff (Morehead et al., 2023), which performs unconditional SE(3)-discrete diffusion for nucleic acid sequence-structure generation, RNAFlow introduces protein conditioning and replaces diffusion with flow matching for computational efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RNAFlow is the first generative model to jointly design protein-conditioned RNA sequences and structures, addressing a gap where prior methods handled either sequence-only design (LSTM/VAE on SELEX data) or unconditional structure generation. The trajectory-as-ensemble strategy for capturing RNA dynamics is a creative but unvalidated approximation; the paper acknowledges a large performance gap to the RF2NA folding upper bound and notes that RNAFlow underperforms random baselines on conformationally diverse single-stranded RNAs. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer or more dynamic RNAs, whether the frozen-RF2NA assumption holds as better protein-RNA predictors emerge, and whether any designed molecules achieve functional binding experimentally.
