## Problem

While numerous watermarking schemes have been proposed for generative models, it was previously unknown whether 'strong' watermarking—where a computationally bounded attacker cannot erase the watermark without significant quality degradation—is theoretically achievable at all. This paper proves a general impossibility result: under natural assumptions about the existence of quality and perturbation oracles, no strong watermarking scheme (even in the secret-key setting) can be secure against a generic random-walk attack.

## Input / Output

The attack takes a prompt x and a watermarked output y (text of ~200 tokens or a generated image), plus oracle access to a quality function Q(x,y) and a perturbation oracle P(x,y). It produces a de-watermarked output y' whose detection p-value exceeds 0.05 (standard no-false-positive threshold) while maintaining quality as judged by GPT-4. For text, the perturbation oracle resamples masked token spans using a weaker open-source masked language model; for images, it inpaints random square patches using a weaker diffusion model—both significantly less capable than the watermarked model being attacked.

## Method

The core attack is a quality-preserving random walk via rejection sampling on the space of high-quality outputs for a given prompt. Starting from the watermarked output y, the adversary repeatedly samples a perturbed candidate y_t from the perturbation oracle P and accepts it (updating y' := y_t) only if the quality oracle confirms Q(x, y_t) ≥ Q(x, y), thereby restricting the walk to the high-quality subgraph. After O(t_max/ε_pert) steps—where t_max is the mixing time of the induced random walk and ε_pert is the per-step quality-preservation probability—the distribution of y' converges to the stationary distribution, which is independent of the watermarking secret key k. This guarantees Detect_k(x, y') = 0 with probability approaching 1 − ε_pos (the scheme's false positive rate) while quality is preserved by construction. The attack is universal and scheme-agnostic: it requires no knowledge of the watermarking algorithm, no access to the unwatermarked model, and only black-box access to the watermarked model plus a weaker perturbation model.

## Results

On C4 Real News with Llama2-7B, the quality-preserving random walk attack degrades all three LM watermark schemes to p-values above the 0.05 threshold (KGW: 0.002→0.187; Unigram: 4.563e-11→0.208; EXP: <1/5000→0.312), while GPT-4 judge scores remain near zero (−0.067 to −0.088), indicating negligible quality degradation. For image watermarks on Stable Diffusion XL, both Invisible Watermark (p-value 1.793e-5→0.206) and Stable Signature (p-value 5.9404e-6→0.0589) are similarly defeated with CLIP scores dropping by less than 1 point. The attack requires only black-box access to the watermarked model and a weaker open-source model for perturbation, with no knowledge of the secret key or scheme details.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's attack is a generic, scheme-agnostic method that successfully removes watermarks from all five tested schemes without requiring knowledge of the secret key or scheme internals. For LM watermarks, KGW (Kirchenbauer et al., 2023a) is reduced from a z-score of 6.236 to 1.628, Unigram (Zhao et al., 2023a) from 8.210 to 1.456, and EXP (Kuditipudi et al., 2023) from 3.540 to 0.745 — all crossing the p-value > 0.05 threshold that corresponds to a 5% false positive rate. For image watermarks, Invisible Watermark (Mountain, 2021) on SDXL drops from p-value 1.793e-5 to 0.206, and Stable Signature (Fernandez et al., 2023) on SDXL-Turbo drops from 5.9404e-6 to 0.0589, with CLIP scores degrading by only 0.13 and 0.51 points respectively. In all cases, GPT-4 judge scores remain within −0.07 to −0.14 of zero, confirming that the de-watermarked outputs are of comparable quality to the originals. The attack's key advantage over prior paraphrasing-based attacks is that it does not need to preserve semantic content, only prompt-fitness quality, making it strictly more general.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior watermark-removal attacks (paraphrasing, diffusion purification, optimization-based) by introducing a generic random-walk attack that requires no knowledge of the specific watermarking scheme or its secret key. Conceptually, it extends the impossibility result of Zhao et al. (2023b) for classical post-hoc image watermarks to the generative-model watermarking setting, where the watermark is embedded in the sampling process. The key methodological departure is the formulation of security with respect to prompt-conditioned quality (fitness to the prompt) rather than edit distance or semantic similarity, which is what makes the impossibility result hold in generality. The random-walk-plus-rejection-sampling framework draws on Markov chain mixing theory, applied here to the space of high-quality outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This impossibility result fundamentally reframes the watermarking debate: under natural and increasingly satisfied assumptions (quality oracle, perturbation oracle), no strong watermarking scheme—public or secret-key—can resist a determined attacker. The practical implication is that watermarking should be viewed as a weak, best-effort provenance tool rather than a robust anti-misuse mechanism, which has direct consequences for policy (e.g., the EU AI Act, Executive Order 14110) and for model developers investing in watermarking infrastructure. Open questions include whether weak watermarking (resisting only bounded-edit attacks) remains viable, whether the perturbation-oracle assumption truly holds for all prompt types, and whether any cryptographic or architectural approach can circumvent the impossibility. The paper also leaves open whether more efficient, scheme-specific attacks exist beyond this generic (but less efficient) random-walk approach.
