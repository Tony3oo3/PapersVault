## Problem

LLMs face both explicit context-length limits and degraded performance on long inputs even within those limits, making them unable to robustly consume documents that are orders of magnitude longer than their context window. The paper identifies a fundamental mismatch: LLMs passively process text word-by-word, whereas humans retain fuzzy gist memories of what they've read and interactively look up specific details only when needed for a task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241054/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a long document (ranging from ~4K words in QuALITY to ~344K words in NarrativeQA) and a task prompt (multiple-choice question or free-form instruction). It produces a compressed gist memory (ordered page-tagged summaries) and a final task answer. The gist memory is the key intermediate artifact: it is contextualized with page numbers so the LLM can selectively retrieve raw pages, and it must fit within the LLM's context window, which drives the compression rate (85–97% across datasets).

## Method

ReadAgent is a zero-shot prompting system with three stages that mimic human reading. First, episode pagination prompts the LLM to choose natural pause points (scene transitions, dialogue ends) between paragraphs, segmenting the document into variable-length 'pages' bounded by min_words and max_words. Second, memory gisting compresses each page into a short gist using a 'shorten' prompt (rather than 'summarize' to preserve narrative flow), then concatenates all gists with page tags into an ordered gist memory. Third, interactive look-up prompts the LLM to decide which page(s) to re-read given the task, replacing the corresponding gists with raw text before generating the final answer. Two look-up strategies are studied: parallel (ReadAgent-P, requesting multiple pages at once) and sequential (ReadAgent-S, requesting one page at a time with access to previously expanded pages). The entire pipeline requires no training or architectural changes, scaling the effective context window by 3.5–20× while outperforming full-text, retrieval, and gist-only baselines.

## Results

On QuALITY (dev, 230 docs / 2086 questions), ReadAgent-S (1–6 page look-up) achieves 87.17% accuracy, surpassing full raw content (85.83%) while compressing the context by 72.17% (~3.5× effective context length). On NarrativeQA Gutenberg test (avg. 71k words, max 343k words), ReadAgent-S (1–3 pgs) reaches 60.55% LLM Rating-1 and 0.219 ROUGE-L, improving over the best retrieval baseline (BM25 Top-4: 53.60% LR-1, 0.197 ROUGE-L) by ~13% and ~32% respectively, while extending effective context length by ~20×. On QMSum test, ReadAgent-S (1–6 pgs) attains 49.58% LR-1 and 21.50 ROUGE-L, outperforming all baselines. On Mind2Web cross-task, ReadAgent-P (1–5 snippets) achieves 33.7% element accuracy and 29.2% step success rate, beating MindAct (PaLM 2-L + trained Rank LM) on every metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BM25 retrieval (Robertson et al., 2009), ReadAgent wins on every dataset and metric: on QuALITY, ReadAgent-S (87.17%) beats BM25 Top-4 (84.42%) by 2.75 points; on NarrativeQA Gutenberg test, ReadAgent-S achieves 60.55% LR-1 vs. BM25 Top-4's 53.60% (+6.95 pts) and 0.219 ROUGE-L vs. 0.197 (+11%); on QMSum test, ReadAgent-S reaches 49.58% LR-1 vs. BM25 Top-6's 40.45% (+9.13 pts). The paper also shows that ReadAgent's prompt-based page selection outperforms neural retrieval (Gemini API embeddings) on QuALITY (84.13% vs. 82.65% for 1-page look-up). Against MindAct (Deng et al., 2023) on Mind2Web, ReadAgent-P (1–5 snippets) surpasses MindAct (PaLM 2-L + supervisedly trained Rank LM) on all four metrics in the cross-task split (33.7% vs. 29.8% element accuracy; 72.5% vs. 61.9% operation F1; 29.2% vs. 24.4% step SR; 2.8% vs. 1.2% episode SR), despite using no domain-trained retrieval model. ReadAgent also outperforms MindAct (GPT-4 + Rank LM) on step success rate in the cross-website split (31.1% vs. 30.1%) while using a smaller base model (PaLM 2-L vs. GPT-4).

## Relation to Prior Work

ReadAgent carves new ground at the intersection of LLM agents, retrieval-augmented generation, and cognitive science (fuzzy-trace theory). Unlike architectural long-context approaches (Longformer, BigBird, LongT5) that require training or fine-tuning, ReadAgent is a pure prompting system that requires no model modifications. It departs from conventional RAG by replacing BM25 or embedding-based retrieval with LLM-driven reasoning over a flat gist memory, and from hierarchical summary-tree agents like MemWalker by using a single-level compressed representation that preserves narrative flow and allows reasoning over distant information at the same granularity. The LLM-driven episode pagination (choosing natural pause points rather than fixed-length chunks) is a distinctive design choice validated by ablation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReadAgent demonstrates that effective context length can be extended 3.5–20× with a simple prompting pipeline, and that compressed gist memory plus selective look-up can outperform feeding the full original text to the LLM — a counterintuitive result suggesting that reducing distracting information is as important as increasing available context. The method is practical (no training, works with any instruction-tuned LLM) and generalizes beyond reading comprehension to web navigation. Key limitations remain: the gist memory itself is bounded by the LLM's context window, so it cannot handle arbitrarily long inputs; the paper acknowledges a plausible hallucination risk when the model must fill in details elided by gisting; and iterative gisting for very long conversation histories is left as future work.
