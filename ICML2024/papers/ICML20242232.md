## Problem

SGD-based adversarial training suffers from robust overfitting, where robust test accuracy degrades over epochs while robust training accuracy keeps improving. Prior uniform stability analysis revealed this is caused by the non-smoothness of the adversarial loss (from the max operation), which introduces an additional O(T^q·ε) term in the stability bound that grows with training iterations even as n→∞. No existing algorithm achieves uniform stability for the weakly-convex, non-smooth problems that arise in adversarial training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard supervised training set of RGB images with class labels (e.g., CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, SVHN at 32×32), a neural network architecture (e.g., WideResNet-28×10), and a perturbation intensity ε (e.g., 8/255 for ℓ∞-PGD). It outputs uniformly stable network parameters w that are obtained by alternating between inner minimization over w and outer gradient descent over an auxiliary variable u, with the outer step size following a diminishing schedule α_t = 1/(pt) where p > l (the weak convexity parameter). The final model is the inner minimizer w(u^T; S) evaluated at the last outer iterate.

## Method

ME-A reformulates the original non-smooth minimization min_w R_S(w) as an equivalent min-min problem min_u min_w [h(w;z) + (p/2)‖w−u‖²] using the Moreau envelope applied to the empirical loss. This decomposition separates the non-strong convexity (handled by the outer problem, which is p-smooth and convex) from the non-smoothness (handled by the inner problem, which is (p−l)-strongly convex but non-smooth). The algorithm alternates N steps of a first-order algorithm A (e.g., SGD) on the inner problem with one gradient descent step on the outer problem, where the outer gradient is ∇_u M(u;S) = p(u − w(u;S)). Crucially, applying the Moreau envelope to the empirical loss (rather than per-sample as in Moreau-Yosida Smoothing) avoids the O(n^4.5) iteration complexity and memory overhead of storing n separate networks. This yields O(T^q/n) uniform stability for weakly-convex non-smooth problems—eliminating the O(T^q·ε) robust overfitting term—without additional computational cost beyond the base algorithm A.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with TRADES loss and AutoAttack evaluation, ME-SGD achieves 55.23% robust test accuracy versus 51.91% for standard SGD adversarial training, a ~3.3% improvement. When combined with pseudo-labeled data (Carmon et al., 2019), ME-SGD+data reaches 62.76% versus 59.45% for SGD+data. On the adversarial loss (AT Loss), ME-SGD achieves 51.66% vs. 50.80% for SGD, with the combined ME-SGD+data reaching 59.14% vs. 58.41%. The paper demonstrates that the robust overfitting term O(T^q·ε) is eliminated by ME-A, leaving only the sample complexity term O(T^q/n), and that the two improvements (reducing overfitting and adding data) are additive.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard SGD adversarial training baseline (Gowal et al., 2020), ME-SGD consistently outperforms: on TRADES loss it gains ~3.3% (55.23% vs. 51.91%), and on AT loss it gains ~0.9% (51.66% vs. 50.80%). When pseudo-labeled data from Carmon et al. (2019) is added, both methods improve by ~8%, but ME-SGD+data still leads by ~3.3% on TRADES (62.76% vs. 59.45%), confirming the additive decomposition of robust overfitting and sample complexity. Compared to SWA (Izmailov et al., 2018), which the paper shows is a special case of ME-A as p→0, ME-A achieves comparable practical robust accuracy (e.g., 63.17% for SWA/p=0 vs. 63.86% for p=1 in ablation Table 6) but provides the first provable O(T^q/n) uniform stability guarantee for weakly-convex non-smooth problems, whereas SWA lacks any theoretical stability bound. The paper also notes that PPM (τ=0) performs similarly to plain SGD (56.86% in ablation), and MYS is computationally intractable in practice due to memory requirements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the uniform stability framework of Hardt et al. (2016) for smooth losses and the adversarial training stability analysis of Xiao et al. (2022b), which identified an additional O(T^q·ε) term arising from the non-smoothness of the adversarial loss. The key conceptual departure is repurposing the Moreau envelope function (Moreau, 1965) — a tool previously used in PPM and Moreau-Yosida Smoothing to smooth the loss — instead to *separate* non-strong convexity from non-smoothness into a min-min structure, yielding an inner strongly-convex non-smooth problem and an outer convex smooth problem. This positioning makes ME-A the first algorithm with provable O(T^q/n) uniform stability for weakly-convex non-smooth problems, a regime where PPM, MYS, and ERM all fail. The paper also reveals that Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA), a widely-used practical technique, is the p→0 limit of ME-A, providing the first theoretical grounding for SWA's empirical success in adversarial training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The additive decomposition of robust generalization into a robust-overfitting term O(T^q·ε) and a sample-complexity term O(T^q/n) gives practitioners a clear diagnostic: ME-A (or SWA) addresses the former, while additional data addresses the latter, and the two improvements compose. The main limitation is the weak-convexity assumption (l-gradient Lipschitz of the standard loss), which is reasonable for neural networks but requires estimating l, and the practical gains are modest (1–3% on CIFAR-10) because ME-A closely approximates SWA in practice. An open question is whether uniformly stable algorithms can be designed for genuinely non-convex non-smooth problems beyond the weakly-convex regime, which would remove the l-estimation burden and broaden applicability.
