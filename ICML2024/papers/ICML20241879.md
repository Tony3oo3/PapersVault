## Problem

Prior theoretical work on rich-observation RL has been limited to discrete (tabular) or linear latent dynamics, leaving a gap for the continuous, nonlinear settings that dominate real-world control. The central algorithmic challenge is that representation learning and exploration must be interleaved, yet standard representation learning objectives (contrastive learning, multistep inverse kinematics) do not extend to continuous dynamics because their optimal predictors are not guaranteed to be Lipschitz functions of the latent state. This paper asks: how can we design representation learning schemes that gracefully compose with exploration under Lipschitz continuous latent dynamics?

## Input / Output

The method consumes high-dimensional observations (e.g., 100×100 pixel images in the maze environments, or MuJoCo camera frames in the D4RL benchmark) paired with continuous actions, and produces a learned decoder mapping observations to a low-dimensional continuous latent state space (dim_S = 2 in experiments) along with a nonstationary policy. The latent state space and action space are assumed to be metric spaces with finite covering dimension, and the latent dynamics must satisfy a TV-Lipschitz continuity assumption. In the offline evaluation, the learned decoder is frozen and fed into TD3-BC for policy optimization, so the representation is the primary output of interest.

## Method

The core contribution is BCRL.C, a representation learning objective that minimizes a min-max-min problem: the decoder φ is chosen to minimize, over a class of discriminators f, the Bellman backup error where the prediction head g is constrained to be 1-Lipschitz with respect to the latent state-action metric. This Lipschitz constraint on g (enforced via spectral normalization in practice) is the key novelty over prior schemes—it ensures the learned latent space respects the continuity structure of the true dynamics, which is what prior objectives like contrastive learning and multistep inverse kinematics fail to guarantee. For online RL, BCRL.C is interleaved with CRIEE, which uses count-based exploration bonuses over discretized balls in the learned latent space and performs optimistic dynamic programming with a function class derived from the discretized representation. The analysis requires a new pseudobackup operator and a careful treatment of discretization error, since the pseudobackup is neither linear nor monotone in the continuous setting.

## Results

On the visual D4RL benchmark (walker and cheetah environments), BCRL.C-trained decoders used with TD3-BC achieve reward curves competitive with PCLaSt (Koul et al., 2023) and significantly outperform a randomly initialized decoder with the same architecture. In the maze environments, BCRL.C qualitatively learns representations that respect wall boundaries and local dynamics structure, while a baseline without the Lipschitz constraint on prediction heads fails to capture these structures. The paper presents results as learning curves (Figure 2) rather than single-point metrics, with BCRL.C tracking PCLaSt closely across training epochs in both locomotion environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares BCRL.C against two baselines on the visual D4RL benchmark (walker and cheetah): PCLaSt (Koul et al., 2023), a recent method for discovering plannable continuous latent states, and a randomly initialized decoder with the same network architecture. BCRL.C is competitive with PCLaSt in both environments, achieving similar TD3-BC reward trajectories across training epochs, while significantly outperforming the random decoder. On the maze environments, BCRL.C is compared against a variant of itself without the Lipschitz constraint on prediction heads (no spectral normalization); the constrained version correctly captures wall and boundary structure in the learned latent space, while the unconstrained baseline produces representations that ignore these geometric constraints. The paper notes that PCLaSt was chosen as the representative baseline because it outperforms other representation learning methods (contrastive learning, multistep inverse kinematics) in the visual D4RL benchmark with exogenous noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rich-observation RL framework (Block MDPs, low-rank MDPs) from discrete/tabular latent states to continuous latent dynamics governed by Lipschitz continuity, building directly on the BCRL/RepLearn representation learning objective from Modi et al. (2024), Zhang et al. (2022), and Mhammedi et al. (2023a). It also draws on the GOLF algorithm (Jin et al., 2021) and coverability (Xie et al., 2023) for the statistical analysis, introducing approximate coverability and the DBR technique (Amortila et al., 2024) to handle the misspecification introduced by discretizing continuous state spaces. A key departure from prior work is the demonstration that standard representation learning objectives—contrastive learning (Misra et al., 2020) and multistep inverse kinematics (Lamb et al., 2023)—do not naturally extend to continuous dynamics because their optimal predictors need not be Lipschitz in the latent state.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical framework providing provably efficient algorithms for RL with both rich observations and continuous latent dynamics, a combination that captures realistic robotic control settings. The separation result showing that Q*/V*-Lipschitzness (sufficient in the classical Lipschitz MDP setting) is insufficient with rich observations is a meaningful negative result that clarifies the boundary of what continuity assumptions are needed. Open questions include whether the quadratic dependence on effective state space size is necessary, whether Wasserstein-continuity assumptions suffice, how to handle exogenous noise, and whether adaptive (instance-dependent) guarantees are achievable. The empirical validation is proof-of-concept (small mazes, two MuJoCo environments), so practical scalability remains untested.
