## Problem

Existing algorithms for constrained nonconvex nonsmooth optimization only provide asymptotic convergence guarantees, and the standard convergence criteria (gradient mapping and Frank-Wolfe gap) are ill-defined in the nonsmooth setting because they require a well-defined gradient at every feasible point. This paper fills the gap by introducing non-asymptotic convergence analysis for zeroth-order stochastic methods on such problems, requiring new approximate stationarity notions that are both well-defined and amenable to finite-time analysis.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume only function evaluations of a stochastic component f(x;ξ) via a Function Query Oracle (FQO) and, for the projection-free variant, a Linear Maximization Oracle (LMO) over the convex compact set Ω. No gradient or subgradient access is required. The output is a point x_R (uniformly sampled from the iterates) that is a (γ,δ,ε)-generalized Goldstein stationary point or a (δ,ε)-Goldstein Frank-Wolfe stationary point, where δ>0 is a fixed smoothing radius tied to the Goldstein δ-subdifferential rather than a vanishing hyperparameter.

## Method

The key insight is to replace the Clarke subdifferential (which yields no finite-time guarantee) with the Goldstein δ-subdifferential—the convex hull of all Clarke subgradients in a δ-neighborhood—and build the generalized gradient mapping and δ-Frank-Wolfe gap on top of it. The paper proves (Theorem 4.7) that the δ=0 Clarke-based criteria are intractable for any local-oracle algorithm, while the δ>0 Goldstein-based criteria admit finite-time convergence. For gradient estimation, the methods use randomized smoothing (averaging f over a δ-ball) combined with a two-point zeroth-order estimator, yielding an unbiased estimate of ∇F_δ(x) that provably lies in ∂_δ F(x). Two algorithm families are proposed: a projected gradient descent variant (ZOSPGD) and a Frank-Wolfe variant (ZOSFW), each available in a minibatch (MB) and variance-reduced (VR) version, achieving O(d^{3/2} δ^{-1} ε^{-4}) and O(d^{3/2} δ^{-1} ε^{-3}) FQO complexity respectively.

## Results

The paper establishes non-asymptotic convergence rates for four zeroth-order stochastic algorithms solving constrained nonconvex nonsmooth optimization. The minibatch variants (MB-ZOSPGD, MB-ZOSFW) require O(d^{3/2} δ^{-1} ε^{-4}) function query oracle calls, while the variance-reduced variants (VR-ZOSPGD, VR-ZOSFW) improve this to O(d^{3/2} δ^{-1} ε^{-3}) calls. In experiments on a robust low-rank matrix recovery problem (synthetic d=3000–5000 and MovieLens-1M), the projection-free ZOSFW methods converge substantially faster in wall-clock time than the projection-based ZOSPGD methods, while VR-ZOSPGD paradoxically converges slower than MB-ZOSPGD due to the expensive nuclear-norm projection step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external baselines but instead evaluates four algorithmic variants it proposes: MB-ZOSPGD, VR-ZOSPGD, MB-ZOSFW, and VR-ZOSFW. On both synthetic (d=3000–5000) and MovieLens-1M datasets, the projection-free ZOSFW methods (MB-ZOSFW, VR-ZOSFW) converge faster in processed time than the projection-based ZOSPGD methods, confirming the practical advantage of avoiding the expensive nuclear-norm projection (which requires full SVD, O(d^3) per step). Surprisingly, VR-ZOSPGD converges slower than MB-ZOSPGD despite its better theoretical rate (ε^{-3} vs ε^{-4}), which the authors attribute to the costly projection operation undermining the efficiency gains from variance reduction. On MovieLens-1M, the gap between ZOSFW and ZOSPGD methods is even more pronounced, with VR-ZOSPGD performing worst overall. The theoretical rates match the best known unconstrained results (Lin et al. 2022 for ε^{-4}, Chen et al. 2023 for ε^{-3}), confirming that the constrained setting does not worsen the oracle complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Goldstein δ-subdifferential framework from Zhang et al. (2020) and the gradient-free methods of Lin et al. (2022) from the unconstrained to the constrained nonconvex nonsmooth setting. Prior work on constrained nonconvex nonsmooth optimization (Curtis & Overton, 2012; Hare et al., 2016; Tang et al., 2014) relied on reformulating the problem as unconstrained via penalty or filter methods and provided only asymptotic convergence guarantees. The key conceptual departure is showing that the natural Clarke-based generalizations of gradient mapping and Frank-Wolfe gap (GCSP, CFWSP) are intractable in finite time (Theorem 4.7), necessitating the Goldstein-based alternatives (GGSP, GFWSP) that the paper introduces and analyzes for zeroth-order stochastic algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first non-asymptotic convergence analysis for zeroth-order stochastic methods on constrained nonconvex nonsmooth problems, filling a gap that has persisted since the asymptotic-only results of the 2010s. The impossibility result for Clarke-based stationarity is a clean negative result that guides future work toward Goldstein-type criteria. Open questions include whether the O(d^{3/2} δ^{-1} ε^{-3}) complexity is tight (no lower bound is established) and whether the empirical observation that variance-reduced ZOSPGD underperforms minibatch ZOSPGD in practice—despite a better theoretical rate—reflects a fundamental tension between projection cost and gradient estimation quality.
