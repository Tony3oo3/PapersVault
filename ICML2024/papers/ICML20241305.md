## Problem

In semi-supervised multi-label learning, previous methods either treat each label independently or attempt to directly estimate label correlation from minimal labeled data, which is unreliable. This failure to capture inter-label dependencies means the pseudo-labels generated for unlabeled instances do not respect the co-occurrence or mutual-exclusion structure inherent in multi-label problems, limiting model performance when labeled data is scarce.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241305/cropped_page7_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from large-scale multi-label benchmarks (Pascal-VOC-2012, MS-COCO-2014, NUS-WIDE), where only a small fraction p ∈ {0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20} of samples carry complete multi-label annotations and the remainder are unlabeled. The predictive model (ResNet-50 pre-trained on ImageNet) outputs a binary label vector of length c for each image, while the pseudo-label generator S produces soft labels in [0,1]^c for unlabeled instances that are constrained by the learned correlated label prior.

## Method

PCLP constructs a correlated label prior probability distribution using a nonlinear Structural Causal Model (SCM) over a DAG of labels, where edges encode causal dependencies between classes and exogenous Gaussian noise captures unexplained variation. This SCM prior replaces the standard factorized Gaussian prior in a variational label enhancement framework (comprising a pseudo-label generator S, encoder E, and sample generator G), so that generated pseudo-labels are constrained to conform to the learned inter-label correlations. Because the SCM makes the joint distribution implicit, the paper employs a GAN-based discriminator to adversarially estimate gradients for all components (S, E, G, and the SCM parameters β). The predictive model is then trained jointly on labeled instances and unlabeled instances assigned with the correlation-aware pseudo-labels, with theoretical guarantees showing that incorporating label correlation in the prior yields more accurate pseudo-labels and a bounded generalization error.

## Results

PCLP achieves state-of-the-art mAP across all three benchmark datasets at every labeled proportion (p = 0.05–0.20). On Pascal-VOC-2012, PCLP reaches 77.25 mAP at p=0.05 and 84.59 mAP at p=0.20, surpassing the strongest baseline CAP by 1.35 and 0.27 mAP respectively. On MS-COCO-2014, PCLP attains 64.43 mAP (p=0.05) and 71.52 mAP (p=0.20), outperforming CAP by 1.55 and 1.09 mAP. On NUS-WIDE, PCLP scores 46.39 mAP (p=0.05) and 52.45 mAP (p=0.20), leading CAP by 1.41 and 1.08 mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCLP outperforms all 13 baselines across every dataset and labeled proportion. The closest competitor is CAP (Xie et al., 2023), which uses class-specific thresholds based on estimated class prior probabilities; PCLP beats CAP by 1.35–0.27 mAP on VOC, 1.55–1.09 mAP on COCO, and 1.41–1.08 mAP on NUS, with the largest gaps at the lowest labeled proportion (p=0.05). Among instance-based pseudo-labeling methods, IAT-1 (Xie et al., 2023) is the strongest on VOC (75.34 at p=0.05) but PCLP still leads by 1.91 mAP; on COCO and NUS, IAT variants are less competitive. The dual-classifier SSMLL method DRML (Wang et al., 2020) performs worst among deep methods (61.75 mAP on VOC at p=0.05), likely because it relies on a limited number of labeled instances to construct label correlation directly. The MLML methods (LL-R/CT/CP, PLC) and SSL methods (ADSH, FREEMATCH) all trail PCLP by 1–3 mAP, confirming that explicitly modeling joint label correlation via SCM yields meaningfully better pseudo-labels than threshold-based or consistency-based strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Variational Label Enhancement framework (Xu et al., 2020/2021/2023) by replacing its standard factorized Gaussian prior over pseudo-labels with a Structural Causal Model (SCM) that captures inter-label dependencies. It departs from prior SSMLL methods (CAP, DRML, IAT) that either treat each label independently or directly estimate a correlation matrix from limited labeled data, instead learning a generative model of the joint label prior from both labeled and unlabeled instances. The SCM formulation draws on DAG-based causal modeling (Yu et al., 2019), while the adversarial gradient estimation for optimization follows the bidirectional generative modeling approach of Shen et al. (2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical guarantee that a factorized label prior provably yields suboptimal pseudo-labels (Theorem 5.2) while an SCM-based prior recovers Bayesian-informed generators (Theorem 5.3) provides a principled justification for the design choice, going beyond empirical validation. However, the method's complexity (jointly training a GAN discriminator, variational encoder/generator, and SCM) raises practical adoption barriers, and the fixed DAG structure (ordered by chain rule) rather than learned causal structure limits expressiveness. Open questions include whether the SCM topology could be learned end-to-end, whether the approach extends to non-image modalities or extreme multi-label settings, and whether the GAN-based optimization could be replaced with simpler alternatives.
