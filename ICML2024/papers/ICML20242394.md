## Problem

Existing offline imitation learning methods that leverage imperfect demonstrations select data based on state-action similarity to expert demonstrations, which confines training to the narrow expert state manifold and ignores potentially abundant diverse behaviors that deviate from expert actions. This similarity-based criterion neglects dynamics information, leaving the learned policy brittle to covariate shift when it encounters states outside expert coverage. The paper asks: how can one extract positive behaviors from noisy data without resorting to complex reward learning or adversarial optimization?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of expert trajectories (as few as 1 for MuJoCo/AntMaze, 10 for Adroit/FrankaKitchen, 25 for vision-based tasks) and a large set of imperfect trajectories (1000) from unknown, potentially highly suboptimal policies. Observations are either low-dimensional state vectors (11–59 dims) or 84×84 RGB images for vision-based tasks. The output is a single imitation policy (a 2-layer MLP or 2-layer CNN mapping states to actions) trained via weighted behavior cloning, with no reward signal or environment interaction required at any point.

## Method

ILID introduces a resultant-state-based data selection principle: instead of matching state-actions to expert demonstrations, it identifies states within imperfect data that fall on the expert state manifold (via a state-only discriminator) and rolls back K steps to extract the causal state-actions that led to those states, thereby capturing diverse beneficial behaviors that explicitly exploit dynamics. The selected complementary dataset is then combined with expert data in a two-term weighted BC objective: the first term applies importance-sampling weights (from a state-action discriminator) to the union dataset to enhance expert support, while the second term clones only the selected behaviors that lie outside the expert state manifold (gated by β(s,a)), preventing suboptimal actions from interfering with expert imitation. Theoretically, the approach provably reduces the suboptimality bound from O(|S|H/n_e) for standard BC to O(min{|S|/ε², |S|H/ε}), eliminating the linear horizon dependence and enabling recovery from out-of-distribution states.

## Results

ILID achieves state-of-the-art normalized scores on 20 out of 21 offline IL benchmarks, typically outperforming the best baseline by 2–5×. For example, on antmaze-medium ILID scores 64.6 vs. 6.4 (ISWBC, the next best), on hammer 51.0 vs. 15.3 (CSIL), and on lift-img 90.4 vs. 56.9 (ISWBC). The sole exception is hopper, where DWBC edges out ILID (78.3 vs. 68.9). Runtime remains comparable to plain BC (~40 min vs. ~30 min on an RTX 4090 for 250k steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ILID outperforms all five baselines on 20 of 21 tasks. BCE and BCU (plain BC on expert-only or union data) fail on most tasks (scores near 0) because limited expert state coverage leaves the policy unable to recover from covariate shift. DWBC and ISWBC, which select data via state-action similarity to expert demonstrations, show moderate success in MuJoCo locomotion (e.g., DWBC 78.3 on hopper, the one task where ILID trails) but collapse in long-horizon manipulation (Adroit, FrankaKitchen) and maze navigation (AntMaze), where their narrow state coverage causes error compounding. CSIL (model-free IRL) and MLIRL (model-based IRL) struggle in high-dimensional environments due to reward extrapolation and world-model estimation errors, scoring near 0 on most Adroit and vision-based tasks. ILID's dynamics-aware data selection (identifying actions whose resultant states fall in the expert manifold) enables it to stitch trajectory fragments and recover from mistakes, yielding 2–5× gains over the best baseline in most settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

ILID departs from the dominant paradigm in offline IL with imperfect demonstrations (DWBC, ISWBC) that selects behaviors based on state-action similarity to expert data, instead proposing a dynamics-aware criterion: a behavior is positive if its resultant state falls within the expert state manifold. This is complemented by a lightweight weighted BC objective that combines importance-sampling reweighting (as in ISWBC) with a Dirichlet-style mask to prevent suboptimal actions in expert states from interfering with expert imitation. The approach also provides a theoretical suboptimality bound (Theorem 4.2) showing provable alleviation of error compounding, in contrast to the IRL-based alternatives (CSIL, MLIRL) that rely on reward learning and world models but struggle with extrapolation in high-dimensional settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2–5× improvement over six strong baselines across 21 diverse benchmarks (continuous control, dexterous manipulation, vision-based tasks) with BC-comparable runtime makes ILID a practical drop-in upgrade for offline IL pipelines where expert data is scarce. The key limitation is the assumption of state overlap between expert and imperfect data; if no imperfect trajectory ever reaches an expert state, the selection criterion has no signal. The authors identify multi-quality demonstrations and incorporating prior information about data quality as natural future directions, and the theoretical analysis is restricted to deterministic dynamics, leaving the stochastic case open.
