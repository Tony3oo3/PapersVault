## Problem

In Interaction-Grounded Learning (IGL), the agent must infer a latent binary reward from noisy feedback to optimize its policy, but existing methods (e.g., E2G) rely on strict conditional independence and well-separated feedback distributions that break down under realistic noise. This paper asks whether an information-theoretic objective can robustly enforce the conditional independence assumption even when feedback is significantly corrupted.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240774/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a batch of (context image, action, feedback image) tuples collected offline by a behavior policy, where the context is a 28×28 MNIST digit, the action is a predicted digit 0–9, and the feedback is a digit image (0 or 1) that may be replaced by noisy images from EMNIST or shifted digits depending on the noise type. The reward decoder (a 2-layer FC network) maps the feedback image to a scalar probability in [c, 1−c], and the final output is a linear contextual bandit policy mapping context images to action distributions, evaluated by prediction accuracy on a held-out test set.

## Method

The core contribution is an information-bottleneck-style objective that minimizes the conditional mutual information I(Y; X,A | R_ψ) (enforcing the conditional independence assumption) minus a regularization term β·I(X,A; R_ψ) (preventing the decoder from overfitting to noisy feedback). Because both terms involve continuous random variables, the paper applies the Donsker–Varadhan variational representation of KL-divergence to convert each MI into a sup/inf over neural function classes G and T, yielding a three-level min-max optimization (min over decoder ψ, max over G, min over T) solvable by alternating gradient descent-ascent. The framework is further generalized to arbitrary f-divergences (f-VI-IGL) by replacing the exponential in the variational bound with the Fenchel conjugate f*. After training the reward decoder, a standard offline contextual bandit oracle recovers the final policy, and a data-driven tie-breaking step selects between ψ and 1−ψ based on decoded return.

## Results

On the number-guessing task (MNIST), VI-IGL (β=10) achieves 81.6±7.9% policy accuracy in the noiseless setting, comparable to the E2G baseline (82.2±4.3%). Under noisy feedback at level 0.1, VI-IGL substantially outperforms E2G across all four noise types: 71.0% vs. 25.0% (independent), 73.6% vs. 21.6% (action-inclusive), 72.1% vs. 15.4% (context-inclusive), and 65.2% vs. 20.6% (context-action-inclusive). The advantage persists at higher noise levels (0.2 and 0.3), where VI-IGL maintains 42–69% accuracy while E2G drops to 15–25%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is E2G (Xie et al., 2021b), the original Interaction-Grounded Learning algorithm that jointly trains a policy and reward decoder by maximizing the decoded return gap between a learned and a 'bad' policy. In the noiseless setting, VI-IGL (81.6%) is essentially on par with E2G (82.2%), confirming that the information-theoretic objective does not sacrifice performance when the full conditional independence assumption holds cleanly. However, under any form of noisy feedback, VI-IGL dramatically outperforms E2G by 40–57 percentage points at noise level 0.1 (e.g., 72.1% vs. 15.4% for context-inclusive noise). The gap narrows somewhat at noise level 0.3 (e.g., 51.4% vs. 24.6% for context-inclusive), but VI-IGL still maintains a 25–35 point advantage. The paper attributes E2G's failure to its reliance on well-separated feedback distributions conditioned on the latent reward (Assumption 2 in Xie et al., 2021b), which breaks down when noise makes the two distributions overlap significantly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Interaction-Grounded Learning (IGL) framework of Xie et al. (2021b) by replacing the contrastive decoder objective with an information-theoretic one grounded in the information bottleneck principle (Tishby et al., 2000). The key technical enabler is the Donsker-Varadhan variational representation of KL divergence (and its f-divergence generalization from Nguyen et al., 2010), which converts the intractable mutual information estimation over continuous variables into a tractable min-max optimization. The paper departs from prior IGL work by introducing a regularization term I(X,A;R_ψ) that was absent in the original E2G algorithm, and by extending the objective to general f-divergences, yielding a family of algorithms (f-VI-IGL) rather than a single method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in IGL-based RL: the original E2G algorithm degrades severely under noisy feedback (dropping from ~82% to ~20% accuracy), a regime common in BCI and recommender system applications. The regularization term and variational formulation provide a principled path to robustness, and the f-divergence extension offers flexibility for different noise structures. However, empirical validation is limited to a single number-guessing task with MNIST/EMNIST images, results exhibit high variance (acknowledged by the authors), and the sample complexity bounds contain exponential factors that are impractical for deep networks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to richer environments, whether non-information-theoretic dependence measures (MMD, Wasserstein) could replace MI, and how to tighten the generalization analysis to account for gradient-based optimization.
