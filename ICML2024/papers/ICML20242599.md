## Problem

Standard blocking techniques for learning from β-mixing data deflate the effective sample size by the block length k, yielding worst-case convergence rates as if every observation were repeated k times with perfect dependence. This paper asks whether, for the square loss, one can avoid this multiplicative deflation so that mixing effects appear only in additive higher-order terms, and shows that this is possible under a weakly sub-Gaussian condition on the hypothesis class.

## Input / Output

The method takes stationary β-mixing data pairs (X_i, Y_i) with X_i in a normed space and Y_i in a Hilbert space, along with a hypothesis class F that is either convex or realizable and satisfies the weakly sub-Gaussian condition ‖f‖_{Ψ_p} ≤ L‖f‖_{L²}^η. It produces an empirical risk minimizer f̂ with an excess risk bound of the form ‖f̂ − f⋆‖²_{L²} ≲ r⋆² + V_{2q}(F⋆ ∩ r⋆S_{L²})·log(1/δ)/n, where r⋆ is a critical radius solving a fixed-point equation involving the local γ₂-complexity and the noise-class interaction variance. The burn-in conditions (n_quad, n_mult, k_mix) encode when mixing effects become negligible, and for geometrically ergodic processes these reduce to polynomial requirements in log(n/δ).

## Method

The core mechanism combines three ingredients: (1) a Ψ_p-Bernstein MGF bound (Maurer & Pontil, 2021) that controls the Laplace transform of centered sums using only a 2q-moment and a Ψ_p-norm rather than boundedness; (2) mixed-tail generic chaining (Dirksen, 2015) to make this pointwise bound uniform over the localized class F⋆ ∩ rS_{L²}, splitting the tail into an L^{2q} (variance) component and a Ψ_p (boundedness) component; and (3) the weakly sub-Gaussian class condition, which ensures that at the localization scale r⋆ the Ψ_p component is of strictly smaller order than the L^{2q} component, so all dependence on the block length k is confined to the additive higher-order term. The analysis is first carried out for k-wise independent data and then ported to β-mixing via Yu's blocking/decoupling argument, where the mixing coefficient β(k) appears only in the failure probability and the burn-in condition k·β^{-1}(k) ≥ n/δ. This yields a 'near mixing-free' rate where the leading term depends only on second-order statistics and local class complexity, matching the iid rate up to the variance proxy.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 3.1) shows that for β-mixing data, any ERM over a convex or realizable weakly sub-Gaussian class achieves ‖f̂ − f⋆‖²_{L²} ≲ r⋆² + V_{2q}(F⋆ ∩ r⋆S_{L²}) · log(1/δ)/n with probability 1−4δ, where r⋆ is a critical radius depending on the γ₂-functional and the noise-class interaction variance. Crucially, the leading term is free of any multiplicative dependence on the mixing time or block length k; mixing appears only in additive higher-order burn-in terms. For d-dimensional parametric classes (Corollary 3.1), the rate simplifies to V_{2q} × (d + log(1/δ))/n, recovering the optimal parametric rate scaled by the appropriate noise level. In the special case of realizable bounded linear regression (Corollary 3.2), the variance term reduces to V(W) and the burn-in requires only n/k ≳ d + log(1/δ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's theoretical guarantee improves upon three prior paradigms for learning with dependent data. First, standard blocking approaches (Mohri & Rostamizadeh 2008; Kuznetsov & Mohri 2017; Roy et al. 2021) incur a multiplicative deflation of the effective sample size by the block length k in the leading rate term; this paper eliminates that deflation, relegating k to additive higher-order terms. Second, martingale-difference approaches (Simchowitz et al. 2018; Kowshik et al. 2021; Ziemann & Tu 2022) achieve mixing-free rates but require a strong realizability assumption (the regression function must lie in the hypothesis class); this paper removes that requirement, working in the agnostic/misspecified setting. Third, the prior dependent linear regression result of Ziemann et al. (2023b) achieves a similar mixing-free rate but is restricted to linear classes via the Fuk-Nagaev inequality; this paper extends to general weakly sub-Gaussian classes (smoothness classes, parametric classes, finite classes) using mixed-tail generic chaining. Compared to the iid sub-Gaussian class framework of Lecué & Mendelson (2013), the present work handles dependent data and a strictly broader class of hypotheses (η < 1 topological equivalence rather than η = 1 norm equivalence), at the cost of a slightly degraded variance proxy (2q-moment instead of 2nd moment when p < ∞).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the blocking-based analysis of Ziemann et al. (2023b) for dependent linear regression to general hypothesis classes, and generalizes the sub-Gaussian class framework of Lecué & Mendelson (2013) from the iid setting to β-mixing data. The key technical departure is the use of mixed-tail generic chaining (Dirksen, 2015) combined with a Ψ_p-norm refinement of Bernstein's inequality (Maurer & Pontil, 2021), which replaces the need for boundedness or strict norm equivalence with a weaker 'weakly sub-Gaussian' topological condition (η < 1). The localization and critical-radius structure follows Mendelson's (2014) learning-without-concentration framework, while the β-mixing-to-k-wise-independence reduction uses the classical blocking/decoupling technique of Yu (1994).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result resolves a long-standing gap: prior nonasymptotic bounds for learning with dependent data either required strong realizability (martingale-difference assumptions) or suffered an irreducible multiplicative deflation of the effective sample size by the mixing time. By showing that this deflation can be pushed into additive higher-order terms for a broad class of problems (smoothness classes, parametric classes, sub-Gaussian linear regression, finite classes), the paper provides the first sharp, instance-optimal rates in the agnostic (non-realizable) dependent-data setting. Open questions include whether the weakly sub-Gaussian condition can be further relaxed, whether a unified analysis covering both the martingale (mixing-free) and blocking (mixing-dependent) regimes is possible, and whether the burn-in requirements can be tightened for high-dimensional smoothness classes where the curse of dimensionality makes them exponentially large.
