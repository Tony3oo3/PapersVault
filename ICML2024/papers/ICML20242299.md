## Problem

The three-phase LLM training paradigm (Pretraining, SFT, RLHF) is understood only at the behavioral level—Pretraining encodes knowledge, SFT aligns formats, RLHF refines outputs—but this does not explain how distinct internal capabilities develop or interact. The paper asks whether 'cognitive' capability (information encoded in hidden representations) and 'expressive' capability (token-level output quality) can be separately quantified, and whether they follow different developmental trajectories across training phases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242299/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes single-choice questions (one question plus four answer options) from benchmarks like ARC, RACE, OpenbookQA, and CommonSenseQA. For cognitive measurement, each (question, choice_i) pair is wrapped in Template A to extract the last-token hidden state vector c ∈ R^m from every Transformer layer; PCA directions are fit on a training split and accuracy is computed by projecting test embeddings onto those directions. For expressive measurement, the full (question, {choice_1..4}) tuple is wrapped in Template B and the model's direct token-level answer accuracy is recorded. The output is a per-dataset, per-checkpoint, per-layer capability profile, plus a single scalar (max over layers) summarizing cognitive capability.

## Method

The paper defines cognitive capability as the linear separability of correct vs. incorrect answer embeddings in the hidden space, quantified via unsupervised PCA (adapted from Representation Engineering) on last-token vectors extracted at each Transformer layer, and expressive capability as direct token-level answer accuracy. The key empirical finding is an asynchronous development pattern: cognitive capability plateaus during Pretraining (around 2.4T tokens for Baichuan-33B) while expressive capability lags far behind until SFT and RLHF close the gap, with SFT playing the dominant role. Theoretically, the gap is attributed to the superior linear separability of the high-dimensional hidden space R^m over the discrete token space T, demonstrated by a linear SVM on hidden states achieving ~87% accuracy on HalluQA while direct token generation achieves only ~6% at Pretraining. The paper further shows that the vocabulary linear layer undergoes the largest KL-divergence shifts during SFT/RLHF, linking expressive capability gains to this final projection. Finally, optimization-free strategies—few-shot prompting, 8× repeated sampling, and a 'magical' additional prompt—partially or fully bridge the cognitive-expressive gap, with repeated sampling matching cognitive accuracy and confirming that cognition sets an upper bound on expression.

## Results

On RACE, cognitive capability (PCA-based) stabilizes at 74–80% accuracy across SFT epochs and PPO, while expressive capability (direct token output) rises from 68.7% (SFT epoch 2) to 78.9% (SFT epoch 4) and 77.0% (PPO), consistently remaining below the cognitive ceiling. Across all five benchmark datasets (ARC-Challenge, ARC-Easy, CommonSenseQA, OpenBookQA, RACE), the hypothesis test for independence between cognitive and expressive accuracy yields p < 0.01% in nearly all settings, confirming a strong statistical correlation. On HalluQA, a linear SVM on hidden-space representations achieves 86.8% accuracy throughout all training stages, while direct token generation improves from 6.1% (Pretrain) to 56.3% (PPO), illustrating the persistent gap between the two capability measures. Repeated sampling (8 draws) closes the gap entirely, matching expressive accuracy to cognitive accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the lm-evaluation-harness framework (Gao et al., 2023), which uses greedy search to evaluate each option's probability. During Pretraining, the PCA-based cognitive capability measurement consistently outperforms the lm-evaluation-harness scores (Figures 13–14), with the gap narrowing substantially after SFT and RLHF. A second key comparison is between a linear SVM classifier on hidden-space representations and direct token generation on HalluQA: the SVM maintains a stable 86.8% accuracy across all stages (Pretrain through PPO), while direct token generation starts at only 6.1% and reaches 56.3% after PPO—a 30-point gap that persists even after full training. This demonstrates that the hidden space offers superior linear separability over the token-level space, and that SFT/RLHF primarily improves the vocabulary linear layer's mapping rather than the underlying cognitive representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the linear representation hypothesis (Park et al., 2023) and Representation Engineering (Zou et al., 2023) into a concrete two-axis framework: 'cognitive capability' measured via PCA on hidden-layer activations versus 'expressive capability' measured via direct token output. It connects the previously qualitative notion of Alignment Tax (Lightman et al., 2023; Ouyang et al., 2022) to a mechanistic explanation grounded in linear separability differences between the hidden space and the token vocabulary space, and further ties the SFT/RLHF bridging of this gap to vocabulary-layer weight dynamics tracked via KL divergence. The residual-connection and pre-LayerNorm analysis explaining the layer-wise plateau curve extends the interpretability literature by linking architectural inductive biases to observable redundancy in intermediate layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a measurable diagnostic for separating what a model 'knows' from what it can 'say,' with practical implications for training pipeline design (e.g., knowing that cognitive capacity is largely fixed after pretraining, while SFT/RLHF primarily shapes expression). The finding that 8 repeated samples can close the cognitive-expressive gap offers a cheap, optimization-free inference-time strategy. Limitations include evaluation restricted to the Baichuan model family, strong independence assumptions in the theoretical analysis (Claim C.4–C.5), and the somewhat ad hoc choice of PCA direction for cognitive quantification. Open questions include whether the plateau/redundancy phenomenon generalizes across architectures and whether the vocabulary-layer KL-divergence signal can be exploited for targeted fine-tuning.
