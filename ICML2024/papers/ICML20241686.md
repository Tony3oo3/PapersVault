## Problem

Federated fine-tuning of billion-sized LLMs on end devices is currently limited to parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods, which may underperform full-parameter tuning especially under non-IID data. Full-parameter tuning with federated learning is impractical because backpropagation-based approaches require prohibitive memory (20+ GB for a 1.3B model) and all existing ZOO-based FL methods either transmit full model parameters each round (O(d) communication) or require clients to replicate an ever-growing number of update steps to synchronize (O(τrm) computation). No prior work makes federated full-parameter tuning of billion-sized LLMs feasible on devices with bounded communication and memory.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a pre-trained LLM (w^0) and a private local text dataset; the server holds no model parameters, only K integer seeds and a scalar gradient accumulator of K floats. Per round, the server transmits the seed pool (a single 4-byte integer encoding all K seeds) and the accumulator (~K × 4 bytes), totaling under 18 KB. Clients reconstruct the latest global model locally via w = w^0 − η·Σ(a_j·z_j), perform τ steps of local training, and upload (seed_index, scalar_gradient) pairs. The final output is a full-parameter fine-tuned LLM, reconstructed from w^0 plus the accumulated scalar-gradient-weighted perturbations.

## Method

FedKSeed exploits the fact that a zeroth-order (MeZO) update step is fully determined by a random seed (regenerating the perturbation z_j) and a scalar gradient ρ̂_j, so the server need only track K scalar accumulators rather than d-dimensional parameters. By restricting perturbation sampling to a fixed pool of K seeds and accumulating scalar gradients per seed, each client reconstructs the latest global model in at most K forward passes regardless of the number of rounds, reducing synchronization cost from O(τrm) to O(d). Theoretical analysis (Lemma 1, Theorem 2) shows the gradient estimation error from seed reuse is bounded by 2L‖z‖‖ε‖ + L‖ε‖², where ε (the mean shift of sampled perturbations) vanishes as K and total steps Γ grow. FedKSeed-Pro further assigns non-uniform sampling probabilities to seeds via softmax over min-max-normalized scalar-gradient amplitudes, concentrating updates on high-impact perturbations and reducing the required K from 4096 to 1024–2048 without accuracy loss. The choice of K is guided by intrinsic dimension theory, with empirical sweet spots around 1024–4096 for 1B–3B parameter models.

## Results

On six scenarios (DataJuicer-1.3B and LLaMA-3B across Natural Instructions and Dolly-15K with α=0.5 and α=5.0), FedKSeed-Pro achieves the highest Rouge-L among practical methods in 5 of 6 settings, reaching 36.29 on Dolly-15K (α=0.5) with LLaMA-3B and 30.19 on Natural Instructions with LLaMA-3B. The average relative improvement over the best practical baseline (FedIT) is 7.26% in Rouge-L. Per-round communication cost is 17,988 bytes (~18 KB) for FedKSeed and 9,796 bytes for FedKSeed-Pro, compared to 5.01–12.76 GB for FedAvg and 12–20 MB for FedIT, representing roughly a 1000× reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FedIT (Zhang et al., 2024, the strongest practical PEFT-based baseline using LoRA+Adam), FedKSeed-Pro outperforms by up to 3.06 Rouge-L points on Dolly-15K (α=0.5) with LLaMA-3B (36.29 vs. 33.23) and by 2.20 on Natural Instructions with LLaMA-3B (30.19 vs. 28.13); FedKSeed also edges out FedIT in 5 of 6 scenarios. Against FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017), FedKSeed matches or slightly exceeds accuracy in most settings (e.g., 29.77 vs. 27.88 on NI/LLaMA-3B) while reducing per-round communication from 12.76 GB to 18 KB and peak memory from 39.1 GB to 7.8 GB. Against FedZO (Fang et al., 2022), FedKSeed achieves comparable or marginally lower accuracy (e.g., 35.64 vs. 36.34 on Dolly-15K α=0.5/LLaMA-3B) but with the same ~1000× communication reduction. Against FedPTuning and FedPrompt (Kuang et al., 2023), FedKSeed-Pro dominates by large margins (e.g., 23.50 vs. 19.61 for FedPTuning and 6.04 for FedPrompt on NI/DataJuicer-1.3B), confirming that full-parameter tuning via ZOO substantially outperforms PEFT in federated settings with non-IID data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ZOO-based federated learning line (FedZO, FedMeZO) by introducing a finite seed-pool paradigm that decouples communication cost from model size while keeping client-side computation constant across rounds. Prior ZOO-FL methods using shared randomness (Zelikman et al., 2023; Feng et al., 2023; Maritan et al., 2023) either still require distributing full model parameters each round or demand ever-growing replication of all past update steps; FedKSeed's key departure is restricting perturbations to K reusable seeds with accumulated scalar gradients, reducing both communication to O(1) and synchronization computation to O(d). The approach also builds on the two-point gradient estimator from MeZO (Malladi et al., 2023) and connects the seed count K to the intrinsic dimension of the loss landscape (Aghajanyan et al., 2021), providing a theoretical rationale for why a small finite set of random directions suffices for full-parameter updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedKSeed is the first method to make federated full-parameter tuning of billion-sized LLMs practically feasible on end devices, with per-round communication under 18 KB and memory equivalent to inference—addressing a gap where prior FL-LLM work was confined to PEFT. The non-uniform seed sampling (FedKSeed-Pro) further reduces the required seed count while improving accuracy, suggesting that perturbation importance is highly non-uniform in practice. Limitations include evaluation only up to 3B parameters, heuristic K selection guided by intrinsic dimension estimates, and an informal convergence argument (Assumption 1) linking the two-point estimator to the one-point FedZO analysis. Open questions include scaling to 7B+ models, interaction with quantization, and whether the finite-seed paradigm extends to decentralized or asynchronous FL settings where the server-side accumulator model breaks down.
