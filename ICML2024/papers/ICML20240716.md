## Problem

Distributed bilevel optimization requires each worker to transmit full-dimensional stochastic gradients to a central server every iteration, creating prohibitive communication overhead that limits scalability. While communication compression has been extensively studied for single-level optimization, no algorithms existed for bilevel problems because the nested structure introduces bias in hypergradient estimation that standard compression techniques (error feedback, multi-step compression) cannot tolerate, as they fundamentally require unbiased gradient estimates.

## Input / Output

Each of n workers privately holds an upper-level cost function f_i, a lower-level cost function g_i, and local data distributions D_fi, D_gi, along with an upper-level ω_u-unbiased compressor C_i^u and a lower-level ω_l-unbiased compressor C_i^l (e.g., scaled rand-K sparsification). Workers compute local stochastic gradient/Jacobian estimates, compress them, and transmit only the compressed tensors to the server; the server aggregates and broadcasts updated global variables (x, y, z). The output is an ε-accurate solution x̂ satisfying E[||∇Φ(x̂)||²] ≤ ε, with the paper showing a recommended compressor ratio (1+ω_u)/(1+ω_l) = Θ(√(d_x/d_y)) to minimize total communicated bits.

## Method

The paper builds a family of compressed bilevel algorithms starting from SOBA, a single-loop framework that simultaneously updates upper-level x, lower-level y, and an auxiliary variable z (estimating the Hessian-inverse-vector product) to avoid explicit Hessian inversion. C-SOBA directly applies unbiased compression to SOBA's communications, achieving linear speedup but requiring a strong bounded-gradient assumption. CM-SOBA adds a moving-average momentum step on the upper-level direction, relaxing the requirement to bounded gradient dissimilarity. EF-SOBA incorporates error feedback, with a critical design choice: the lower-level compressors use a refined mechanism D̂_y^k = m_y^k + (1/n)ΣC_i^l(D_{y,i}^k − m_{y,i}^k) to preserve unbiasedness of y and z updates (since the hypergradient depends on their accuracy), while the upper-level uses standard EF recursions; this eliminates all data-heterogeneity assumptions. Finally, multi-step compression variants (CM-SOBA-MSC, EF-SOBA-MSC) match the optimal complexities of the best single-level compressed algorithms, and experiments on MNIST/CIFAR-10 demonstrate 10× communication reduction without significant accuracy loss.

## Results

The paper establishes the first family of distributed bilevel algorithms with communication compression, achieving a 10× reduction in communicated bits compared to non-compressed distributed SOBA (NC-SOBA) without substantial performance degradation on MNIST (MLP) and CIFAR-10 (CNN) tasks. Theoretically, C-SOBA achieves O(1/√(nK)) linear speedup with O(ω/(nε²)) communication rounds, while EF-SOBA-MSC matches the optimal O(σ²/(nε²)) computational and O((1+ω)/ε) communication complexities of the best single-level compressed algorithm (EF21-SGDM). Under heterogeneous data (Dirichlet α=0.1), EF-SOBA maintains convergence and test accuracy close to NC-SOBA, whereas C-SOBA and CM-SOBA deteriorate significantly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is NC-SOBA (non-compressed distributed SOBA). Under homogeneous data, all compressed variants (C-SOBA, CM-SOBA, EF-SOBA) achieve a 10× reduction in communicated bits with test accuracy and convergence curves closely matching NC-SOBA. Under heterogeneous data (Dirichlet α=0.1), C-SOBA and CM-SOBA show clear performance degradation, while EF-SOBA's convergence in iterations and final test accuracy remain close to NC-SOBA, confirming its robustness to data heterogeneity. Theoretically, EF-SOBA-MSC and CM-SOBA-MSC achieve the same asymptotic computational and communication complexities as the best-known single-level compressed algorithm EF21-SGDM, closing the gap that the nested bilevel structure previously imposed. The paper also notes that its algorithms are limited to unbiased compressors (e.g., rand-K), excluding biased but contractive compressors like Top-K, which is identified as future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SOBA single-loop bilevel framework (Dagréou et al., 2022) to the distributed setting and, for the first time, incorporates communication compression into bilevel optimization. It draws on three distinct threads: momentum-based bilevel methods (Chen et al., 2023b) to relax bounded-gradient assumptions, error feedback with momentum (Fatkhullin et al., 2023) to handle data heterogeneity in the upper level, and multi-step compression (Huang et al., 2022) to match single-level optimal complexities. The key departure from prior distributed bilevel work (Tarzanagh et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2022) is that the nested structure introduces a bias in hypergradient estimation that standard single-level compression techniques—designed for unbiased gradients—cannot directly handle, necessitating new algorithmic mechanisms and analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of communication compression for distributed bilevel optimization, a setting that arises in meta-learning, hyperparameter optimization, and adversarial training where communication bandwidth is a practical bottleneck. The 10× reduction in communicated bits without significant accuracy loss is practically meaningful for federated bilevel learning at scale. However, the methods are restricted to unbiased compressors (excluding widely-used Top-K), experiments are limited to MNIST and CIFAR-10 with small models, and the theoretical guarantees for the most advanced variants (EF-SOBA-MSC) involve logarithmic factors that obscure practical constants. Open questions include extending to contractive compressors, validating on larger-scale problems, and understanding whether the upper/lower-level asymmetric compression treatment generalizes to deeper nesting structures.
