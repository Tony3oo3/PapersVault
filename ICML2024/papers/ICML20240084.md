## Problem

Naive (zero or mean) imputation is widely used in practice but has been criticized for distorting data distributions. While recent work showed it can be consistent for prediction in high-dimensional linear models under MCAR, no complete picture existed across all dimension regimes, no rigorous framework tracked how the imputation bias scales with the number of observed features relative to the latent structure, and no finite-sample learning bounds were available for SGD trained on imputed data.

## Input / Output

The method operates on tabular data where each of d input features is observed with probability ρ under an MCAR (or MNAR) mechanism, producing an incomplete matrix X̃ = P ⊙ X alongside the always-observed scalar output Y. The analysis treats d as a free parameter relative to a fixed latent dimension p, so the same underlying model is evaluated as d ranges from well below to well above p. Outputs include the learned linear parameter vector θ (via one-pass SGD with Polyak-Ruppert averaging), the predicted scalar value X̃ᵀθ, and closed-form or bounded expressions for the Bayes risk, the imputation-specific excess risk Δ_imp/miss(d), and the total excess risk Δ_imp^(∞)(d).

## Method

The paper introduces a unified random-features framework in which d observed features are random projections of a p-dimensional latent variable Z, allowing the input dimension d to vary while the data-generating model (Y = Zᵀβ⋆ + ε) stays fixed. Within this framework, the authors derive exact Bayes risk formulas for complete, missing, and zero-imputed data, proving that the imputation bias is negligible relative to the missing-data bias when d ≪ p and that both vanish when d ≫ p. For learning, they apply one-pass SGD with Polyak-Ruppert averaging to the zero-imputed data and establish finite-sample excess-risk bounds showing a 1/√n rate in both very low and very high dimensions, with a dimension-free regime when d > ρ(1−ρ)√n. The analysis is then extended to an infinite-dimensional latent space via an RKHS, where the imputation excess risk is controlled by a kernel-ridge bias with penalization constant L²/ρ, and finally to MNAR missingness mechanisms (e.g., logistic models on the latent variable), where the same high-dimensional consistency result holds.

## Results

The paper establishes that in the low-dimensional regime (d ≪ p), the imputation bias Δ_imp/miss(d) is bounded by (ρ(d−1)/(p − ρ(d−1) − 2)) · Δ_miss(d), meaning naive zero-imputation adds negligible error relative to the error already caused by missing data. In the high-dimensional regime (d ≥ p), the combined error satisfies Δ_imp/miss(d) + Δ_miss(d) ≤ p/(ρd + (1−ρ)p) · ‖β*‖², which vanishes as 1/d. For SGD with Polyak-Ruppert averaging, the excess risk over the Bayes risk decays at rate 1/√n in both very low dimensions (d < p−1) and high dimensions (d > (1−ρ)√n/ρ), with the bound becoming dimension-free in the latter regime. The results extend to infinite-dimensional latent spaces, where the imputation bias is controlled by a kernel-ridge-type penalty of order L²/(ρd) · ‖f*‖²_ν.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or benchmark comparisons. The 'comparisons' are between different risk quantities within the same model: (i) complete-data Bayes risk R*(d), (ii) missing-data Bayes risk R*_miss(d), and (iii) zero-imputation Bayes risk R*_imp(d), ordered as R*(d) ≤ R*_miss(d) ≤ R*_imp(d). The key finding is that the gap R*_imp(d) − R*_miss(d) (the cost of naive imputation over the optimal pattern-aware predictor) is negligible in both d ≪ p and d ≫ p regimes. The paper also contrasts its results with prior work: Le Morvan et al. (2020) showed zero-imputation is generally inconsistent for linear models, while Ayme et al. (2023) proved consistency under a low-rank assumption; this paper extends the consistency result to a random-features framework without requiring explicit low-rank structure, and further extends to MNAR missingness patterns (Theorem 4.1) where the MCAR assumption is relaxed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the random features framework of Rahimi & Recht (2007) to study the impact of zero-imputation on linear prediction, building directly on Ayme et al. (2023) who showed consistency of zero-imputation under a low-rank input structure. The key departure is replacing the low-rank assumption with a (possibly infinite-dimensional) latent space, yielding a unified analysis across all input dimensions. The SGD generalization bounds leverage the non-strongly-convex stochastic approximation theory of Bach & Moulines (2013), while the missing-data decomposition follows Le Morvan et al. (2020). The paper further extends its MCAR results to MNAR scenarios, going beyond the standard missing-data assumption in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a rigorous, unified theoretical account of when naive imputation is harmless, yielding a clear practical recommendation: zero-imputation combined with a single-pass SGD is competitive and should be tried before resorting to more complex imputation methods. The finding that naive imputation is also relevant in very low dimensions (d ≪ p) is a new regime not previously identified in the literature. Open questions include obtaining fast (non-1/√n) convergence rates in the high-dimensional regime and deriving analogous SGD guarantees under MNAR missingness, both of which the authors explicitly flag as future work.
