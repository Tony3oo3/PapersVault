## Problem

Flow matching (FM) trains continuous normalizing flows by regressing a velocity model to a target velocity field, but on constrained domains (e.g., pixel values in [0,255]^d), the learned velocity field can drive trajectories outside the domain, producing collapsed or oversaturated samples. Existing constrained alternatives like reflected diffusion models require domain-dependent conditional score approximations (sums of Gaussians, Laplacian eigenfunctions) that introduce bias and limit practicality to simple geometries.

## Input / Output

The method takes a prior sample drawn from a distribution supported on the constrained domain Ω (e.g., a truncated standard Gaussian over [-1,1]^{3×m×m} for images) and, for conditional generation, a class label c. It outputs a sample guaranteed to remain within Ω at all times, with zero constraint violation by construction. For image generation, inputs are rescaled to the hypercube [-1,1]^{3×32×32} (CIFAR-10) or [-1,1]^{3×64×64} (ImageNet), and the generated images inherently respect the pixel-value bounds without post-hoc clipping.

## Method

RFM augments the standard CNF ODE with a reflection term dL_t that neutralizes outward-normal velocity at the boundary ∂Ω, yielding reflected CNFs whose trajectories provably stay within Ω. Training uses a conditional reflected flow matching (CRFM) objective that regresses the velocity model to analytical conditional velocity fields (e.g., an OT-style field with a (1−σ_min) coefficient on the data point to preserve convexity), which are derived directly from conditional flows confined to Ω—eliminating the biased score approximations required by reflected diffusion models. The paper proves existence/uniqueness of the reflected ODE solution, equivalence between the marginal and conditional RFM objectives, and a Wasserstein-2 bound on the generated distribution. Sampling employs standard ODE solvers (Dormand-Prince, Heun) with an iterative reflection procedure at the boundary, or a closed-form formula for hypercubes. A flow-guidance scheme analogous to classifier-free guidance is also provided for conditional generation under high guidance weights.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (32×32) unconditional generation, RFM achieves an FID of 4.76 with 139 NFE, outperforming vanilla FM (6.35 at 142 NFE) and substantially outperforming RDM at comparable NFE (44.40 at 200 NFE), though RDM with 2000 NFE reaches 2.72. On ImageNet (64×64) conditional generation with guidance weight w=15, RFM achieves FID 26.67 (723 NFE) versus FM's 31.83 (769 NFE) and RDM(ODE)'s 66.74 (871 NFE). A key advantage is that RFM produces zero constraint violation across all tested domains and guidance weights, whereas FM violates the [−1,1]³ domain in 51–65% of pixels on ImageNet and up to 92.8% of samples on 10D simplex.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla FM, RFM achieves a lower FID on CIFAR-10 (4.76 vs. 6.35) and ImageNet conditional generation (26.67 vs. 31.83), while maintaining zero constraint violation compared to FM's 51–65% pixel-level violation on ImageNet and 3–93% sample-level violation on low-dimensional domains. Against RDM, RFM is dramatically better at low NFE (4.76 at 139 NFE vs. 44.40 at 200 NFE on CIFAR-10; 26.67 at 723 NFE vs. 66.74 at 871 NFE on ImageNet), though RDM with 2000 NFE still achieves a lower CIFAR-10 FID (2.72). Against unconstrained score-based methods (NCSN++, DDPM++, Subspace NCSN++), RFM is competitive at far fewer NFE (4.76 at 139 NFE vs. 2.17–2.41 at 2000 NFE) while guaranteeing domain adherence. On low-dimensional toy tasks, RFM consistently achieves the lowest KL divergence across all four domains (hypercube, simplex, half annulus, cup), outperforming both FM and RDM, with RDM degrading severely in 10D (KL 0.48–0.76 vs. RFM's 0.024–0.045).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2023) to constrained domains by adding a reflection term to the underlying ODE, creating reflected CNFs. The reflection mechanism is inspired by Reflected Diffusion Models (Lou & Ermon, 2023), but the key departure is moving from reflected SDEs (which require domain-dependent conditional score approximations via sum-of-Gaussians or Laplacian eigenfunctions) to reflected ODEs with analytically available conditional velocity fields, preserving the simulation-free training property of vanilla FM. The framework also inherits the CNF/Neural ODE foundation (Chen et al., 2018) and the classifier-free guidance paradigm, adapting it to the velocity-field setting via a simple linear combination of conditioned and unconditioned velocities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RFM provides a clean, simulation-free training recipe for generative modeling on constrained domains that avoids the biased conditional score approximations plaguing reflected diffusion models, and guarantees zero constraint violation by construction. This is practically important for tasks like image generation where pixel values are bounded, and for high-guidance-weight conditional generation where vanilla FM produces 50–65% boundary violations. The main limitations are that the domain must have a regular shape admitting a designable conditional flow, the boundary must have an analytical form for computing reflections, and the current experiments are limited to 32×32 and 64×64 images; scaling to higher resolutions, videos, or more complex non-convex domains remains open.
