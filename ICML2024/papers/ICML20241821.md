## Problem

Column generation suffers from heavy oscillation of dual values across iterations, causing slow convergence and redundant column generation. While stabilization techniques mitigate this, they still leave a significant gap in obtaining accurate dual values at early stages. This paper addresses that gap by combining ML-based prediction of the optimal dual solution with an adaptive stabilization mechanism that dynamically adjusts its influence as CG progresses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241821/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph G(V,E), an initial set of maximal independent sets (warm-started from a greedy+local-search heuristic), and a one-shot ML prediction ŷ of the optimal dual solution. The ML model receives either hand-crafted statistical features per vertex (FFNN) or the full graph adjacency structure (GCN). Output is the converged set of dual values π* (giving the LP relaxation bound) and the column set S* of MISs; the adaptive penalty ε is recomputed each iteration from the pricing problem's minimum reduced cost c_ε*, requiring no external oracle.

## Method

The core contribution is a generalized restricted dual problem (G-RDP) that augments the standard RDP with auxiliary variables w_i^+, w_i^- measuring deviation of each dual variable π_i from its ML prediction ŷ_i, penalized by a parameter ε in the objective. Two ML predictors are trained via supervised regression (MSE loss) on optimal dual solutions: a 3-layer FFNN over statistical features (vertex frequency, MIS cardinality/degree statistics, graph density) and a 20-layer GCN that learns variable interrelationships directly from graph topology. The key adaptive rule sets ε = c_ε*/(c_ε*−1) when c_ε*<0 and ε=0 otherwise, which the paper proves is always upper-bounded by the Lagrangian gap, ensuring the attraction strength naturally decays as dual values improve and eventually surpass the ML prediction. This guarantees ε→0 in finite iterations, at which point ASCG-ML reduces to standard CG and inherits its convergence guarantee. The 'basin-in-attraction' effect is strongest early (large ε pulls oscillating duals toward the prediction) and weakens as CG refines the solution, addressing the mismatch between fixed-penalty stabilization and the evolving quality of dual iterates.

## Results

On the Matilda benchmark (258 test graphs), ASCG-FFNN achieves a 52% reduction in CG iterations (163.0 vs. 341.1 for classic CG) and a 43% reduction in computation time (1103s vs. 1921s). On the out-of-distribution Graph Coloring Benchmarks (GCB), ASCG-FFNN reduces iterations by 44.2% (86.6 vs. 155.1) and computation time by 9.6% (16.1s vs. 17.8s) under exact pricing. The adaptive stabilization provides a further 9% iteration reduction and 19% time reduction over the constant-penalty SCG-FFNN on Matilda, confirming the benefit of the adaptive penalty schedule.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against classic CG (Mehrotra & Trick, 1996), ASCG-FFNN reduces iterations by 52% and time by 43% on Matilda, and by 44.2% in iterations on GCB, making it the only stabilization method that also improves wall-clock time over CG on GCB. Against SCG (Du Merle et al., 1999), which uses a constant penalty of ε=1 on the previous dual iterate, ASCG-FFNN achieves 52% vs. 15% iteration reduction and 43% vs. 12% time reduction on Matilda, demonstrating that ML-based prediction plus adaptive scheduling substantially outperforms simple smoothing. Against SCG-ML (Kraul et al., 2023), which applies a constant penalty of ε=0.1 with an ML prediction, ASCG-FFNN gains a further 9% iteration reduction (163.0 vs. 194.3) and 19% time reduction (1103s vs. 1450s) on Matilda, confirming that the adaptive penalty schedule is critical for fully exploiting ML predictions. On GCB, ASCG-FFNN also dominates SCG-FFNN (86.6 vs. 112.4 iterations, 44.2% vs. 27.5% reduction), while ASCG-GCN underperforms ASCG-FFNN on GCB (122.9 vs. 86.6 iterations) due to poorer out-of-distribution generalization of the GCN's learned variable interrelationships.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the penalization-based stabilized column generation framework of Du Merle et al. (1999) by replacing the backward-looking reference (previous dual iterate) with a forward-looking ML prediction of the optimal dual solution, and by making the penalty parameter adaptive rather than fixed. It also builds on Kraul et al. (2023), who first demonstrated ML-based dual prediction for the cutting stock problem using a feedforward network with basic problem attributes; here the authors show that modeling variable interrelationships (via statistical features or a GCN) is essential for the graph coloring problem, and introduce an adaptive penalty schedule bounded by the Lagrangian gap. The theoretical grounding in Lagrangian duality (Theorem 3.2, Lemma 3.5) distinguishes the adaptive mechanism from prior constant-penalty approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 52% iteration reduction and 43% time reduction on Matilda, plus strong out-of-distribution generalization to GCB, demonstrate that ML-guided stabilization can be a practical acceleration tool for CG in combinatorial optimization. A notable open question is the GCN generalization failure: the GCN achieves lower MSE on the training distribution (Matilda) but substantially higher MSE on GCB, leading to worse CG performance than the simpler FFNN—highlighting that richer structural modeling does not automatically translate to better out-of-distribution generalization in optimization contexts. The authors identify future directions including extending ASCG-ML to other CG applications via generic GNN formulations, aligning the training objective with the optimization goal (e.g., bilevel optimization), and integrating with other stabilization techniques.
