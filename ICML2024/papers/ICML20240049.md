## Problem

Standard Gaussian process regression assumes i.i.d. Gaussian observation noise to maintain conjugacy, making it highly susceptible to outliers and heteroscedasticity. Existing robust GP methods either replace the Gaussian likelihood with heavy-tailed distributions (breaking conjugacy and requiring expensive MCMC or variational approximations) or perform costly outlier detection before fitting. This paper resolves the robustness-versus-computation trade-off by achieving provable Huber robustness while retaining exact closed-form conjugate posteriors at the same O(n³) cost as standard GPs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes covariates x ∈ ℝ^d, noisy observations y ∈ ℝ, kernel hyperparameters θ, and noise variance σ², plus a soft outlier threshold c chosen as the (1−ε)-quantile of |y_i − m(x_i)| (default ε=0.05). Outputs are the closed-form posterior N(f; μ^R, Σ^R) over the latent function, the posterior predictive N(f_⋆; μ_⋆^R, Σ_⋆^R) at new points, and RCGP-specific UCB/PI acquisition functions for Bayesian optimisation. Unlike standard GPs, the noise term σ²J_w is a data-dependent diagonal matrix (not σ²I_n), and the mean is shifted by a shrinkage term m_w that depends on the prior mean m(x_i) and the weighting function w(x_i, y_i).

## Method

RCGP replaces the standard log-likelihood in the GP posterior with a weighted score matching loss L_n^w derived from a generalised Bayesian framework, where the weighting function w(x,y) down-weights observations that deviate from the prior mean m(x). The key insight is that for a Gaussian model, this loss is quadratic in f, so the resulting posterior p^w(f|y,x) ∝ p(f) exp(−nL_n^w) remains Gaussian with closed-form mean and covariance that mirror the standard GP but replace σ²I_n with the diagonal matrix σ²J_w = σ² diag(σ²/2 · w^{-2}) and m with the shrinkage term m_w = m + σ²∇_y log(w²). The authors advocate the Inverse Multi-Quadratic kernel w_IMQ(x,y) = β(1+(y−m(x))²/c²)^{−1/2} for w, which satisfies the boundedness conditions (sup w < ∞ and sup y·w² < ∞) needed for a bounded posterior influence function, yielding provable Huber robustness. Hyperparameters are selected via an O(n³) leave-one-out cross-validation objective computed analytically via block-matrix inversion, avoiding the ill-posedness of maximising the unnormalised pseudo-marginal likelihood. The framework extends directly to sparse variational GPs (RCSVGP, Proposition 4.1) and Bayesian optimisation (Proposition 4.2) with the same closed-form structure.

## Results

On four benchmark datasets (Synthetic, Boston, Energy, Yacht) under four outlier regimes, RCGP matches standard GP when no outliers are present (e.g., Synthetic MAE 0.09 vs 0.09) while substantially outperforming GP under corruption: under asymmetric outliers, RCGP achieves MAE 0.63 vs GP's 1.14 on Synthetic and 0.35 vs 0.54 on Yacht. Under focused outliers, RCGP reduces MAE from 0.26 to 0.10 on Yacht and from 0.19 to 0.15 on Synthetic. Training time is comparable to standard GP (O(n³)), e.g., 1.2s vs 1.5s on Synthetic and 4.6s vs 3.8s on Energy, while t-GP and m-GP are 5–8× slower (30.7s and 16.7s on Boston respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard GP, RCGP matches performance when no outliers are present but substantially outperforms it under corruption: under asymmetric outliers, RCGP cuts MAE by ~45% on Synthetic (0.63 vs 1.14) and ~35% on Yacht (0.35 vs 0.54); under focused outliers it reduces MAE by ~60% on Yacht (0.10 vs 0.26). Against t-GP (Student-t likelihood), RCGP wins or ties on focused and asymmetric outliers across most datasets, while t-GP wins under uniform outliers (e.g., Boston 0.30 vs 0.51, Yacht 0.16 vs 0.29) because the symmetric t-distribution better matches the symmetric uniform corruption; however, t-GP is 5–8× slower (30.7s vs 5.1s on Boston). Against m-GP (mixture GP), RCGP is consistently superior under outlier settings and dramatically better when no outliers exist (e.g., Synthetic no-outlier MAE 0.09 vs 0.33), while m-GP is also significantly slower. In the sparse variational setting (Figure 6), RCSVGP achieves substantially lower RMSE than SVGP at comparable runtime, confirming the robustness benefit extends to the O(nm²) regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generalised Bayesian inference framework (Bissiri et al., 2016; Knoblauch et al., 2022) to GP regression by introducing a weighted score-matching loss (building on Barp et al., 2019 and Altamirano et al., 2023) that down-weights outliers while preserving the exact conjugate structure of standard GPs. It departs from the two dominant prior approaches to robust GPs—heavy-tailed noise models (Student-t, Laplace, Huber) that break conjugacy and require variational/MCMC approximations, and outlier-removal preprocessing—by instead modifying how information is assimilated through a robust loss rather than the noise model itself. The method also generalises Knoblauch's (2019) earlier robust deep GP, which lacked closed-form solutions and was restricted to variational deep GPs, by providing exact conjugate posteriors applicable to standard GPs, SVGPs, and Bayesian optimisation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RCGP resolves a long-standing trade-off in GP regression: robustness and conjugacy (with its O(n³) closed-form updates) were previously mutually exclusive, forcing practitioners to choose between reliable inference under outliers and computational tractability. The paper demonstrates that RCGP can serve as a drop-in replacement for standard GPs with no performance penalty in clean settings, raising the prospect of it becoming a default choice. Key limitations include dependence on a well-specified prior mean (mis-specified means can cause correct observations to be down-weighted) and the inability to place a posterior over kernel hyperparameters due to the generalised Bayesian formulation. Open questions include whether the quantile-based threshold selection generalises to high-dimensional or non-stationary settings, and whether the approach extends cleanly to fully Bayesian hyperparameter treatment.
