## Problem

Random Network Distillation (RND), a widely used intrinsic-reward exploration method, suffers from a 'bonus inconsistency' issue: before training, a single randomly initialized target network produces highly uneven bonuses across states, and after training, the predictor converges to a representation that cannot distinguish frequently visited states from rarely visited ones. This limits RND's ability to support deep exploration in sparse-reward environments and to serve as a reliable anti-exploration penalty in offline RL.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242328/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

In the online setting, the method takes a state observation (RGB frames for Atari, low-dimensional state vectors for Adroit/Fetch/MuJoCo) as input; in the offline setting, it takes a state-action pair. The predictor network outputs a k-dimensional embedding, from which a scalar intrinsic reward (exploration bonus) or anti-exploration penalty is computed via two terms: a deviation-from-mean term and a pseudo-count term. The bonus is combined with the environment reward to train a policy (PPO for online, SAC for offline), and the target networks remain frozen throughout training, so no additional backpropagation is required beyond the standard predictor update.

## Method

DRND replaces RND's single fixed target network with N randomly initialized, permanently frozen target networks. The predictor is trained with MSE loss against a uniformly sampled target from the N networks, and the exploration bonus combines two components: b₁ = ‖f_θ(x) − μ(x)‖², which measures deviation from the mean of the target distribution and addresses initial bonus inconsistency (its variance scales as (1+1/N)‖x‖²Σ, provably smaller than RND's single-network case), and b₂ = √(([f_θ(x)]² − [μ(x)]²)/(B₂(x) − [μ(x)]²)), which the paper proves (Lemma 4.3) is an unbiased, consistent estimator of 1/n (the inverse visitation count), thereby addressing final bonus inconsistency. The total bonus b(x) = α·b₁ + (1−α)·b₂ uses a fixed α (0.9 in practice), exploiting the natural dynamic where b₁ dominates early in training and b₂ dominates later as the pseudo-count estimate stabilizes. RND (N=1, α=1) and the Coin Flip Network pseudo-count method (α=0, coin-flip target distribution) are shown to be special cases of DRND, unifying curiosity-driven and count-based exploration within a single framework.

## Results

On D4RL MuJoCo tasks, SAC-DRND achieves an average normalized score of 86.0, outperforming SAC-RND (82.6), ReBRAC (81.2), CQL (73.6), IQL (72.9), TD3+BC (67.5), and SAC (16.2). On AntMaze tasks, SAC-DRND reaches 76.0, comparable to ReBRAC (76.8) and substantially ahead of SAC-RND (60.9), IQL (58.3), and CQL (50.6). In online exploration benchmarks (Atari, Adroit, Fetch), DRND-PPO consistently outperforms PPO, RND, CFN, and ICM, with the largest gains in deep-exploration environments like Montezuma's Revenge and Adroit Hammer/Relocate. The bonus distribution analysis shows DRND reduces KL divergence to the uniform distribution by ~5.4× before training (0.0070 vs 0.0377) and to the 1/√n distribution by ~2× after training (0.0476 vs 0.0946) compared to RND.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAC-DRND achieves the best average MuJoCo score (86.0) among all ensemble-free methods, beating SAC-RND (82.6) by 3.4 points and ReBRAC (81.2) by 4.8 points, with notably lower variance (e.g., hopper-random: 32.7±0.4 vs SAC-RND's 19.6±12.4). On AntMaze, SAC-DRND (76.0) is within 0.8 of ReBRAC (76.8) but substantially outperforms SAC-RND (60.9) by 15.1 points, particularly on medium-diverse (83.0 vs 74.7) and medium-play (86.2 vs 38.5). In online exploration, DRND-PPO outperforms RND, CFN, and ICM across Atari (Montezuma's Revenge, Gravitar, Venture), Adroit (Hammer, Relocate), and Fetch tasks, with the largest margins in deep-exploration environments. Compared to ensemble-based methods (SAC-N, EDAC, RORL, MSG) in the appendix, SAC-DRND matches or exceeds them on most MuJoCo tasks (86.0 vs 85.7 for RORL) while remaining ensemble-free. The method also outperforms the vanilla PPO baseline by a wide margin in all online settings, confirming that the distributional bonus provides meaningful exploration signal beyond the base algorithm.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRND extends Random Network Distillation (RND, Burda et al. 2018) by replacing its single fixed target network with N randomly initialized target networks drawn from a distribution, and by deriving a second bonus term that implicitly recovers pseudo-counts. The paper demonstrates that both RND (α=1, N=1) and the Coin Flip Network pseudo-count method (CFN, Lobel et al. 2023) are special cases of DRND, thereby unifying curiosity-driven and count-based exploration under a single framework. For offline RL, DRND builds on the anti-exploration formulation of SAC-RND (Nikulin et al. 2023) but replaces the single-network RND penalty with the distributional variant to better capture dataset distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded fix for a concrete and previously underappreciated limitation of RND (bonus inconsistency), with the elegant observation that the predictor network's weights implicitly encode visitation frequencies without requiring an auxiliary counting mechanism. The dual-bonus design (b₁ dominating early, b₂ dominating late) emerges naturally from a fixed α without dynamic scheduling, and the method incurs negligible computational overhead. Open questions include whether the pseudo-count interpretation holds reliably for very deep or non-linear architectures, whether the method's gains persist in extremely high-dimensional continuous control beyond the tested Adroit/Fetch tasks, and whether the offline anti-exploration benefit generalizes to model-based offline RL settings.
