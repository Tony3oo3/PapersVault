## Problem

Constraint-based causal discovery algorithms (PC, GS) require conditional independence tests, but no sample complexity guarantees existed for these tests with non-Gaussian, continuous variables under non-linear models. Prior theoretical results either assumed Gaussianity, linearity, or discreteness, or relied on a perfect CI oracle. This paper closes that gap by providing the first sample complexity bounds for causal discovery with non-linear models and non-Gaussian continuous variables.

## Input / Output

The method takes n i.i.d. samples of a continuous random vector (X, Y, Z) where X and Y are one-dimensional and Z is d_Z-dimensional, all with compact support and Hölder smoothness of order β. The data is split in half: the first half builds a kernel density estimate, and the second half evaluates the Von Mises entropy sum. The output is a binary CI decision (reject or fail to reject H₀: I(X;Y|Z)=0), and when embedded in PC or GS, the final output is the Markov equivalence class of the underlying causal DAG.

## Method

The core contribution is an exponential concentration inequality for the Von Mises entropy estimator built on a kernel density estimate (KDE) with Legendre kernels, which is tighter than what a standard Markov-inequality argument yields. The CI test VM-CI estimates conditional mutual information I(X;Y|Z) = H(X,Z)+H(Y,Z)−H(Z)−H(X,Y,Z) via four Von Mises entropy estimates and rejects H₀ when the estimate exceeds I_min/2. Under smoothness β > 1 + d_Z/2, the estimator achieves the parametric rate O(n^{-1/2}) with a dimension-free exponential tail, giving a sample complexity of n ≥ Ω(I_min^{-2} log²(1/α)) per test. This translates to n ≥ Ω(((Δ+1)/I_min · log(m/α))²) for PC and n ≥ Ω(I_min^{-1} log²((m²+mΓ²2^Γ)/α)) for GS. Computationally, VM-CI runs in O(dn²) time, avoiding the O(n³) cost of kernel ridge regression–based alternatives.

## Results

VM-CI achieves the optimal parametric convergence rate O(n^{-1/2}) for conditional mutual information estimation under Hölder smoothness (β > 1 + d_Z/2), yielding a sample complexity of n ≥ Ω((1/I_min²)·log²(1/α)) for the CI test itself. For the PC algorithm, the sample complexity to recover the Markov equivalence class is n ≥ Ω(((Δ+1)/I_min · log(m/α))²), and for GS it is n ≥ Ω((1/I_min · log((m²+mΓ²·2^Γ)/α))²) — the first such guarantees for non-linear, non-Gaussian continuous variables. Empirically, VM-CI (KDE-based) outperforms all tested baselines in type I+II error except HSIC, while being significantly faster (O(dn²) vs. O(n³) for kernel ridge regression–based methods). MINE performs near-randomly (error ≈ 1) at the sample sizes tested, as it requires >2×10⁶ samples to begin converging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the KNN+Von Mises estimator (Poczos & Schneider, 2011), VM-CI achieves lower type I+II error at all sample sizes and is substantially faster, since KDE avoids the slow convergence of KNN-based density estimation. Against MINE (Belghazi et al., 2018), VM-CI is overwhelmingly superior: MINE's total error remains close to 1 across all tested n (up to ~1000), because MINE's sample complexity bound scales as Ω(d·log d/ε²) and in practice requires over 2×10⁶ samples. Against the HSIC-based CI test (Zhang et al., 2012), HSIC achieves slightly lower error for small n (<1500), but VM-CI becomes competitive at n ≥ 1500 while being dramatically faster — HSIC's kernel ridge regression step involves inverting an n×n matrix (O(n³)), whereas VM-CI runs in O(dn²). Against the OT-based method (Akbari et al., 2023), VM-CI edges out the OT approach in both error and runtime, and additionally provides rigorous theoretical guarantees (exponential concentration, sample complexity bounds) that the OT method lacks (consistency is not proved). In the full causal discovery experiments (PC and GS), VM-CI outperforms all baselines in overall graph loss except HSIC, and its computational advantage translates into substantially shorter end-to-end runtimes for both algorithms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Von Mises entropy estimator framework of Kandasamy et al. (2015) and Krishnamurthy et al. (2014) by establishing the first exponential concentration inequality for the estimator when combined with kernel density estimation. It departs from the dominant kernel ridge regression + HSIC pipeline (Zhang et al., 2012) by replacing the O(n³) regression step with a direct KDE-based mutual information estimate, achieving O(dn²) time complexity. The plug-in entropy estimators of Liu et al. (2012) and Singh & Poczos (2016) are superseded by the Von Mises variant, which converges at better rates and is simpler to compute. The paper also contrasts with MINE (Belghazi et al., 2018), whose dimension-dependent bounds and massive sample requirements make it impractical, and with the OT-based approach of Akbari et al. (2023), which lacks consistency guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first sample complexity guarantee for constraint-based causal discovery (PC, GS) under non-linear models with non-Gaussian continuous variables, filling a significant theoretical gap where prior guarantees required Gaussianity, linearity, or discreteness. The exponential concentration result is tighter than what Markov's inequality yields, leading to substantially better sample complexity bounds (e.g., polynomial in log(m/α) rather than exponential in m^Δ). Limitations include the requirement of Hölder smoothness with β > 1 + d_Z/2, compact support, and a positive lower density bound, which may not hold in all practical settings. Open questions include whether the smoothness assumptions can be relaxed, how the method behaves in very high dimensions, and whether the gap to HSIC in error rates can be closed with better bandwidth selection.
