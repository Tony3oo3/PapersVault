## Problem

Decision Transformer (DT) relies on a manually chosen return-to-go prompt to guide action selection, which fails to stitch sub-optimal trajectories from offline data because the return-to-go only provides a lower bound on achievable value and does not propagate information backward along trajectories. The paper asks whether a transformer-based decision model can learn to automatically generate its own prompts, enabling optimal policy extraction from sub-optimal offline datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241370/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes state vectors and trajectory history from offline D4RL datasets (MuJoCo, AntMaze, FrankaKitchen). A learned high-level policy generates a prompt at each timestep—either a scalar in-sample optimal value (V-ADT) or a k-step-ahead sub-goal state (G-ADT)—which is concatenated with the current state vector before tokenization. The low-level causal transformer then autoregressively predicts the action conditioned on this prompt-state token and the preceding trajectory context, with sequence lengths of 20 (V-ADT) or 10 (G-ADT) timesteps.

## Method

The paper introduces a hierarchical policy decomposition π(a|s) = ∫ π^h(p|s)·π^l(a|s,p) dp, showing DT is a special case where the high-level policy is a fixed return-to-go counter. Two instantiations are proposed: V-ADT uses IQL to learn the in-sample optimal value V*_D(s) as the prompt, and G-ADT uses HIQL to learn k-step sub-goal predictions as prompts. The low-level transformer policy is trained with advantage-weighted regression (AWR) rather than DT's standard sequence prediction loss, weighting each transition by exp(A/α) where A is the advantage computed from learned Q and V functions. This joint optimization of prompt generation and action prediction is the key mechanism that enables stitching: the value prompt provides precise per-state guidance on maximum achievable return, while AWR ensures the policy preferentially imitates high-advantage actions, together overcoming DT's inability to combine partial trajectories.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark suite (18 environments spanning MuJoCo locomotion, AntMaze navigation, and Franka Kitchen manipulation), V-ADT achieves an average normalized score of 65.0±2.9, substantially outperforming DT (43.8±7.3) and QLDT (45.4±5.3) while approaching IQL (68.3). On the stitching-critical AntMaze tasks, V-ADT scores 52.4±2.9 versus DT's 16.0±2.1 and QLDT's 21.6±0.7. G-ADT achieves 72.6±2.6 average, outperforming Waypoint Transformer (66.0±4.4) and RvS (53.7±6.5) but trailing HIQL (77.1±7.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

V-ADT decisively outperforms both DT and QLDT across all three environment families, with the largest gaps on AntMaze (52.4 vs. 16.0 for DT, 21.6 for QLDT) where stitching sub-optimal trajectories is essential. Against value-based offline RL baselines, V-ADT surpasses TD3+BC (65.0 vs. 52.7) and CQL (65.0 vs. 63.7) on average, and matches or exceeds IQL on MuJoCo (78.7 vs. 76.9) while remaining slightly below IQL on AntMaze (52.4 vs. 63.0) and Kitchen (49.3 vs. 53.3). EDT is competitive on MuJoCo replay tasks (89.0 on hopper-medium-replay) but is not evaluated on AntMaze or Kitchen. For the goal-conditioned variant, G-ADT outperforms Waypoint Transformer (72.6 vs. 66.0) and RvS (72.6 vs. 53.7) on average, but falls short of HIQL (72.6 vs. 77.1), particularly on AntMaze-large tasks. The paper also shows that manual prompt tuning of DT (varying target return) cannot close the gap to V-ADT, confirming that learned hierarchical prompting is the key differentiator rather than prompt value selection alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes Decision Transformer (DT) as a special case of a hierarchical policy where a 'dummy' high-level policy manually sets a return-to-go prompt, and shows that DT's stitching failure stems from this fixed, non-learned prompt. It builds on IQL for in-sample optimal value estimation and HIQL for goal-conditioned value learning, then introduces joint optimization of both the high-level prompt generator and the low-level transformer policy via advantage-weighted regression. This departs from prior DT improvements (QLDT, EDT, HDT, Waypoint Transformer) that modify the prompt or trajectory representation but do not jointly optimize a learned high-level policy with the low-level actor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled HRL-based framework that explains why DT fails on stitching tasks and offers a concrete fix (learned value or subgoal prompts with AWR-based low-level training), yielding substantial gains over DT on AntMaze and Kitchen benchmarks. However, ADT still falls short of IQL and HIQL, and the authors attribute this gap to the difficulty of optimizing transformers as function approximators compared to MLPs, leaving open whether transformer-based decision models can match or surpass classical offline RL methods. Future directions include extending to skills and options as latent actions, unifying value- and goal-prompted variants, and applying ADT to multi-task, multi-modal foundation decision-making models where transformer architectures may have a clearer advantage.
