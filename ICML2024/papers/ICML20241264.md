## Problem

Time-series forecasting models trained via empirical risk minimization fail to generalize when future test data exhibits distribution shifts relative to historical training data. Applying invariant learning (IL) to TSF is non-trivial because unobserved exogenous variables (e.g., epidemics, policy changes) break IL's sufficiency and invariance assumptions, and TS datasets lack the explicit environment labels that most IL methods require.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a lookback window of shape l × d_in (e.g., 96 steps for hourly/daily data, 36 for weekly) and outputs a forecast of shape h × d_out over a configurable horizon (4–720 steps depending on the dataset). It is model-agnostic: any deep TSF backbone (Informer, Crossformer, PatchTST) is wrapped with an additional regressor head, and the framework operates on univariate targets with covariates (d_out=1, d_in≥1). During inference, only the invariant learning module is used; the environment inference module is discarded.

## Method

FOIL targets the sufficiently predictable component Y^suf of the target rather than raw Y, making invariant learning feasible despite unobserved variables Z. A Label Decomposing Component introduces Instance Residual Normalization (IRN), a surrogate loss that aligns the mean and variance of predictions with ground truth to absorb Z's additive/multiplicative effect without requiring explicit Z observations. A Time-Series Environment Inference Module performs EM-based clustering in the representation space via a multi-head network, where each head is an environment-specific regressor; the E-step reassigns labels by nearest regressor loss and then applies temporal label propagation (majority voting over a radius-r neighborhood) to preserve adjacency structure. A Time-Series Invariant Learning Module optimizes a combined objective (Eq. 10) that includes per-environment surrogate loss, an ERM term on raw Y, and a variance-of-loss penalty across environments to enforce invariance. The two modules are jointly trained via alternating updates, and the entire framework is model-agnostic, plugging into any off-the-shelf deep TSF backbone.

## Results

FOIL consistently outperforms all 12 distribution-shift baselines across four datasets (Exchange, ILI, ETTh1, ETTh2) on both MSE and MAE, with average improvements of over 10% on MSE and 5.5% on MAE relative to the best baseline. When applied as a plug-in to existing backbones, FOIL yields up to 85.38% MSE reduction for Informer on ETTh1 and up to 30.60% for the state-of-the-art PatchTST on Exchange. On the ILI dataset, which contains severe OOD shifts (unseen COVID-19 period), FOIL achieves the largest gains, e.g., reducing Informer's MSE from 4.187 to 1.047 at horizon 16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOIL outperforms all 12 baselines on every dataset and metric. General OOD methods requiring environment labels (GroupDRO, IRM, VREx, SD, IB-ERM) perform poorly, often matching or even falling below plain ERM, confirming that directly applying invariant learning to TSF without addressing unobserved variables is ineffective. Among label-free OOD methods, EIIL is the strongest general OOD baseline (e.g., 0.540 MSE on Exchange vs. 0.782 for ERM), but FOIL still surpasses it by a wide margin (0.136 vs. 0.540 MSE on Exchange). Temporal-shift-specific methods RevIN and NST are the most competitive baselines, yet FOIL exceeds them by roughly 10–19% on MSE. The hybrid methods (IRM+RevIN, EIIL+RevIN) combine OOD and temporal-shift handling and are the closest competitors, but FOIL still outperforms them by over 11% on average MSE, demonstrating that the proposed surrogate loss (IRN) is not replaceable by standard instance normalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

FOIL extends the invariant learning (IL) literature—rooted in IRM, VREx, and EIIL—from image and tabular domains to time-series forecasting, where it had not previously been applied. It departs from prior IL work by identifying that unobserved core variables (e.g., epidemics, policy shifts) violate the standard sufficiency and invariance assumptions, and by replacing explicit environment labels with an EM-based inference module that preserves temporal adjacency. On the TSF side, it is model-agnostic and wraps existing backbones (Informer, Crossformer, PatchTST), positioning itself as complementary to rather than competitive with temporal-shift methods like RevIN and NST, which only address marginal input distribution shifts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formal treatment of TSF as an OOD generalization problem via invariant learning, and the identification of unobserved variables as a fundamental blocker for IL in time-series settings is a useful conceptual contribution that explains why naive IL transfers fail. The surrogate loss (Instance Residual Normalization) offers a practical, data-free workaround for the unobserved-variable problem, and the model-agnostic design makes the framework broadly applicable. Limitations include evaluation on only four datasets, restriction to univariate forecasting with covariates, and reliance on a simplified additive-multiplicative assumption for unobserved Z; extending to multivariate settings, more complex Z interactions, and fairness across subgroups remain open.
