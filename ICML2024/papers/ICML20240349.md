## Problem

Over-squashing in GNNs causes long-range information to be compressed into fixed-width node representations, degrading expressivity. Existing remedies rely on graph rewiring (adding or removing edges) to improve signal propagation, but this inevitably alters the original topology, which can violate domain-specific semantics (e.g., breaking chemical bonds in molecular graphs or distorting community structure in social networks). The paper asks whether over-squashing can be mitigated by selectively expanding node widths instead of modifying connectivity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph with p-dimensional node features and a chosen centrality metric (degree, betweenness, closeness, PageRank, or load) to identify the top-k nodes whose hidden dimension is expanded to p_high. Throughout all intermediate layers, low-dimensional and high-dimensional nodes maintain their distinct dimensionalities; only at the final layer are all node representations projected back to a common dimension for downstream tasks (graph classification, node classification, or regression). The four edge types (low↔low, high↔high, low→high, high→low) each use separate learnable aggregation parameters, making the architecture a relational message-passing scheme over the original, unmodified graph.

## Method

PANDA partitions nodes into low-dimensional and high-dimensional subsets based on a centrality score, then defines four distinct message-passing aggregators corresponding to the four edge-type combinations. For low-to-high edges, a linear projection f(·) up-projects the low-dimensional sender's features into the expanded space; for high-to-low edges, a learned dimension selector g(·) computes a per-message score vector via a small neural network and gathers only the top-p most significant dimensions from the high-dimensional sender. The theoretical justification comes from the sensitivity bound (Di Giovanni et al., 2023): increasing width p to p_high raises the upper bound on the Jacobian norm, directly improving feature sensitivity at bottleneck nodes without touching the graph topology. Because no edges are added, PANDA avoids the over-smoothing trade-off that plagues rewiring methods, as confirmed by higher Dirichlet energy of the learned representations. The framework is backbone-agnostic and is instantiated for GCN, GIN, R-GCN, and R-GIN, with the only architectural change being the replacement of the standard neighbor aggregator with the four width-aware aggregators.

## Results

On graph classification (TUDataset), PANDA-GIN achieves 91.06% accuracy on REDDIT-BINARY, 88.75% on MUTAG, and 75.11% on COLLAB, while PANDA-GCN reaches 80.69% on REDDIT-BINARY (a 22.3% absolute improvement over the GCN baseline of 68.26%). On node classification, PANDA-GCN achieves 55.4% on TEXAS and 58.2% on WISCONSIN, outperforming all rewiring baselines. On the LRGB PEPTIDES-STRUCT regression task, PANDA reduces MAE from 0.3496 (GCN) to 0.3272, a 6.4% improvement, while on PEPTIDES-FUNC it raises AP from 59.30 to 60.28 under the Dwivedi et al. setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PANDA outperforms all seven rewiring baselines (DIGL, FA, SDRF, FoSR, BORF, GTR, CT-Layer) on nearly every dataset for both GCN and GIN backbones. The most striking gap is on REDDIT-BINARY with GCN, where PANDA (80.69%) surpasses the best rewiring method FoSR (70.33%) by over 10 points, and on COLLAB where PANDA-GCN (68.40%) nearly doubles the baseline (33.78%) while most rewiring methods remain near baseline. For GIN, PANDA edges out the strongest competitor (Last Layer FA at 90.22% on REDDIT-BINARY) by 0.84 points and leads by 5.3 points on MUTAG (88.75% vs. 83.45%). PANDA-GCN also substantially outperforms R-GCN (49.85% vs. 80.69% on REDDIT-BINARY), confirming that its relational structure is not merely a reparameterization of R-GCN. On node classification, PANDA wins on TEXAS (55.4% vs. 53.6% DIGL) and WISCONSIN (58.2% vs. 51.7% DIGL), though BORF remains competitive on CORNELL (50.8% vs. 47.5%). On LRGB PEPTIDES-STRUCT, PANDA achieves the lowest MAE (0.3272) compared to FoSR (0.3473) and GCN (0.3496), a 6.4% improvement over the baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

PANDA departs from the dominant line of over-squashing research that modifies graph topology via rewiring (SDRF, FoSR, BORF, GTR, DIGL, CT-Layer) by instead altering the message passing mechanism itself. Its theoretical motivation draws directly on the sensitivity bound theorem of Di Giovanni et al. (2023), which shows that wider hidden dimensions increase the upper bound on feature sensitivity, but PANDA applies this insight selectively to high-centrality nodes rather than globally, addressing the generalization concern raised in that work. Conceptually, PANDA can be viewed as a specialized relational GNN (R-GCN-like) where the 'relations' are determined by node width, but it is explicitly designed to mitigate over-squashing rather than model heterogeneous edge types.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PANDA offers a topology-preserving alternative to rewiring, which is particularly important for domain-specific graphs (molecular, social) where adding edges can violate semantic constraints. The method also avoids the over-smoothing trade-off inherent in rewiring, as confirmed by Dirichlet energy analysis. However, the evidence is limited to relatively small graph datasets (MUTAG: 188 graphs, ENZYMES: 600), the computational overhead is substantial (3–4× forward-pass time, and centrality computation can be prohibitive on large graphs like COLLAB), and the theoretical justification remains an informal proposition rather than a full theorem. Open questions include whether the approach scales to large-scale graphs, whether learned (rather than centrality-based) node selection could improve efficiency, and whether the benefits persist on larger, more heterogeneous benchmarks.
