## Problem

Sim-to-real RL has achieved state-of-the-art robotic behaviors, but scaling to new tasks requires extensive manual, task-specific engineering of the training environment—designing rewards, observations, actions, curricula, initial states, and termination conditions—collectively termed environment shaping. Existing benchmarks hide this bottleneck by shipping pre-shaped environments, so the community has no systematic understanding of how much human effort is actually required or how to automate it. This position paper argues that automating environment shaping, not improving the RL algorithm, is the critical next frontier for generalizing RL to diverse robotic tasks.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a set of unshaped reference environments (sample instances drawn from the oracle environment distribution, e.g., multiple dishwashers with varying object configurations) and a task specification (extrinsic reward or objective), and produces a shaped environment via a transformation function f plus a trained policy π. The key structural constraint is a bilevel optimization: the inner loop runs PPO on the shaped environment, while the outer loop (human reflection or automated search) updates f based on policy performance on strictly held-out test environments. The shaped environment is a modification of the reference environment—never the test environment—so that evaluation remains unbiased.

## Method

The paper formalizes environment shaping as a bilevel optimization problem where the outer level searches over shaping functions f (reward, observation space, action space, initial/goal state distributions, terminal conditions) and the inner level solves the standard RL objective on the resulting shaped environment. Empirically, the authors ablate each shaping component individually on three IsaacGymEnvs tasks (AllegroHand, Anymal, Humanoid), showing that removing any single component—sparse reward, unshaped action space, unshaped observation space, no early termination, or single initial state—can reduce performance by 100% in the worst case (e.g., Humanoid with unshaped observations drops to 0). A hill-climbing analysis over the joint shaping space reveals multiple local optima differing in two to four shaping dimensions simultaneously, demonstrating the non-convexity of the problem. Finally, the authors extend the LLM-based Eureka framework from reward shaping to observation and action space design, finding that GPT-4 can match or exceed human-designed components individually (e.g., 0.986 vs. 0.973 for reward) but performance collapses when tasked with jointly optimizing multiple components (e.g., 0.196 for reward × observation), identifying joint multi-component shaping as the key open problem.

## Results

On the Anymal task from IsaacGymEnvs, Eureka (GPT-4-based LLM search) matches or slightly exceeds human-designed shaping when optimizing a single component: reward (0.986 vs. 0.973, +0.013), action space (0.982 vs. 0.973, +0.009), and observation space (0.967 vs. 0.973, −0.006). However, when tasked with jointly optimizing multiple components, performance collapses: reward × observation drops to 0.196 (−0.777) and reward × action drops to 0.536 (−0.437) relative to the human baseline. Ablation experiments across AllegroHand, Humanoid, and Anymal show that removing any single shaping component (e.g., unshaped observation space for Humanoid) can reduce reward from 7554 to 0, confirming that environment shaping is the dominant bottleneck rather than the RL algorithm itself.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Eureka (Ma et al., 2023), an LLM-based bilevel search method, against the human-designed environment configurations from IsaacGymEnvs (Makoviychuk et al., 2021) on the Anymal quadruped locomotion task. For single-component shaping, Eureka slightly outperforms the human baseline on reward (+0.013) and action space (+0.009), and is marginally below on observation space (−0.006), indicating that GPT-4 can match expert-level design for isolated components. The critical finding is that joint optimization of multiple components causes catastrophic degradation: reward × observation shaping drops from 0.973 to 0.196, and reward × action drops to 0.536, revealing that the non-convex coupling between shaping dimensions remains unsolved by current LLM-based search. GPT-4 also failed entirely to generate working code for the three-way joint optimization (reward × observation × action), marked as N/A. The ablation in Table 1 further contextualizes these results by showing that the human-designed shaping is essential: removing the shaped observation space alone reduces Humanoid reward from 7554 to 0, and removing early termination drops AllegroHand from 38777 to 0.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concept of 'environment shaping' from Co-Reyes et al. (2020), which focused on ecological design choices like stochasticity and early termination, into a unified framework encompassing reward, observation space, action space, initial/goal states, and terminal conditions as a single bilevel optimization problem. It builds empirically on the IsaacGymEnvs suite (Makoviychuk et al., 2021) and the LLM-based reward design paradigm of Eureka (Ma et al., 2023), but departs from both by arguing that the field's focus on algorithmic improvements (e.g., better PPO variants) misidentifies the primary bottleneck, and by demonstrating that existing automation efforts are too narrowly scoped to reward alone. The paper positions itself as a call to redirect RL research toward automating the full shaping pipeline rather than refining the inner-loop policy optimizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that manual environment shaping, not algorithmic sophistication, is the primary bottleneck for scaling RL to diverse robotic tasks—challenges the community's default assumption that better policy optimization algorithms are the key to generalization. The empirical evidence (dramatic performance collapses in Table 1, non-convex joint shaping landscape in Figure 5, and Eureka's catastrophic failure at multi-component optimization in Table 2) provides concrete motivation for this reframing. Key open questions include: whether joint shaping can be decomposed into tractable sub-problems, whether foundation models can serve as effective priors for shaping design, and whether the proposed unshaped-environment benchmark will gain community adoption. The paper's limitations are that it is a position paper with evidence from only three IsaacGymEnvs tasks, and the bilevel formulation, while elegant, has no proposed solver that scales beyond the small search spaces tested.
