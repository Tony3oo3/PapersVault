## Problem

Data-driven PDE solvers based on graph neural networks struggle with out-of-domain generalization to unseen spatial geometries and routinely violate physical conservation laws and similarity symmetries (translation, rotation, reflection, scaling). Prior work has addressed conservation or equivariance in isolation, but no GNN-based solver simultaneously enforces local conservation, full similarity-equivariance, and generalization to arbitrary irregular domains within a single architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240755/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes irregular mesh geometry (cell volumes, face areas, face normal vectors, centroid positions), initial conditions for the physical fields, boundary conditions (Dirichlet or Neumann), and material properties (velocity field, diffusion coefficient, viscosity, density) as graph-structured input. It autoregressively produces time series of velocity, pressure, and volume fraction fields on the same mesh, with the conserved quantity being the volume-weighted field V_i·h_i rather than h_i itself. Experiments use coarsened meshes (Δx=1/20) interpolated from fine OpenFOAM solutions (Δx=1/320) to stress-test the model against numerical diffusion, and evaluation includes temporal extrapolation beyond the training horizon (up to t=3.2 vs. training to t=1.6).

## Method

FluxGNN is built on a theoretical characterization (Theorem 3.1) showing that an MPNN is locally conservative if and only if messages are antisymmetric (m_ij = −m_ji) and the update function is linear, which forces all nonlinearity into the message/flux function. The architecture mirrors the finite volume method: each cell's volume-weighted state V_i·h_i is updated by summing learned face fluxes S_ij·n_ij·F_ML(h_i, h_j, h_ij), where F_ML must be permutation-invariant in its first two arguments (Theorem 3.4) to guarantee antisymmetry. The encode-process-decode wrapper requires a linear encoder and a pseudoinverse decoder (Theorem 3.3) to preserve conservation through the embedding. Similarity-equivariant MLPs of the form MLP(‖h/σ‖)h use local geometric quantities (Δx, Δt) for scale normalization σ, naturally encoding the Courant number and avoiding global min/max operations. Temporal bundling is adapted to use only linear 1D-CNN layers for conservation, and the neural nonlinear solver (Barzilai-Borwein steps) and boundary encoders from PENN are incorporated without modification since they operate outside the flux MLPs.

## Results

On the convection-diffusion equation, FluxGNN achieves an MSE loss of 3.08×10⁻⁵, a 457× improvement over the FVM baseline (1408.47×10⁻⁵), while maintaining a conservation error (1.75×10⁻⁸) comparable to FVM (1.63×10⁻⁸). On the Navier-Stokes equations with mixture (test dataset), FluxGNN achieves Loss_α = 0.349×10⁻¹ with a conservation error of 0.06×10⁻⁵, compared to MP-PDE (Loss_α = 1.021×10⁻¹, conservation error 1001.95×10⁻⁵) and PENN (Loss_α = 0.358×10⁻¹, conservation error 1356.62×10⁻⁵). Critically, on the taller (unseen shape) dataset, FVM diverges entirely (NaN), PENN degrades substantially, and MP-PDE degrades, while FluxGNN maintains near-test-dataset accuracy (Loss_α = 0.337×10⁻¹) with conservation error of 0.02×10⁻⁵.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MP-PDE (Brandstetter et al., 2022), FluxGNN achieves conservation errors 4–5 orders of magnitude lower (e.g., 0.06×10⁻⁵ vs. 1001.95×10⁻⁵ on the test dataset) and substantially lower loss on the scaling and taller datasets, where MP-PDE degrades significantly (Loss_α jumps from 1.021 to 5.604 on scaling and 2.823 on taller). Against PENN (Horie & Mitsume, 2022), FluxGNN again dominates on conservation (0.06×10⁻⁵ vs. 1356.62×10⁻⁵) and is the only method that does not diverge on the scaling dataset (PENN outputs NaN) or the taller dataset (PENN's conservation error explodes to 4799.89×10⁻⁵). On the test dataset itself, PENN achieves slightly lower Loss_u (0.619 vs. 1.202) and Loss_p (0.598 vs. 1.143), but FluxGNN matches PENN on Loss_α (0.349 vs. 0.358) while being 4 orders of magnitude more conservative. Against the FVM baseline, FluxGNN achieves dramatically lower loss on both problems (e.g., 3.08×10⁻⁵ vs. 1408.47×10⁻⁵ MSE on convection-diffusion), while FVM diverges entirely on the taller Navier-Stokes dataset. The key differentiator is that FluxGNN's built-in conservation and similarity-equivariance enable robust spatial extrapolation to unseen shapes, scales, and orientations without accuracy degradation, a property neither baseline possesses.

## Relation to Prior Work

FluxGNN sits at the intersection of classical finite volume methods (FVM) and graph neural networks, showing that the FVM discretization is a special case of a locally conservative GNN (Theorem 3.4) and that the neural flux function generalizes the classical numerical flux. It directly extends PENN (Horie & Mitsume, 2022) by adding conservation guarantees and scale equivariance, and adapts temporal bundling from MP-PDE (Brandstetter et al., 2022) with a linearity constraint to preserve conservation. The E(n)-equivariant MLP from Horie et al. (2021) and the scale-equivariant formulation from Wang et al. (2021) are combined into a single similarity-equivariant layer, with the key departure being the use of local geometric quantities (Δx, Δt) rather than global min/max for the scale parameter, yielding a direct connection to the Courant number.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that conservation and similarity equivariance together are necessary for spatial out-of-domain generalization in GNN-based PDE solvers: on the taller dataset, FVM diverges, PENN (with E(n)-equivariance) degrades, and MP-PDE degrades, while FluxGNN maintains near-test accuracy. The conservation error gap is orders of magnitude (FluxGNN ~0.02–0.06 vs. MP-PDE ~1000–16000, PENN ~1300–4800), making the physical reliability argument concrete. Open questions include long-term temporal stability (the model becomes unstable after t≈7 in extended rollouts), whether the approach scales to 3D or more complex PDE classes, and how it compares against well-optimized classical solvers rather than a shared-implementation FVM baseline.
