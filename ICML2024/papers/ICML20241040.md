## Problem

Vision Transformers represent images as non-overlapping rectangular patch tokens, trading spatial granularity for embedding dimensionality and producing semantically rich but spatially coarsely quantized feature maps. This fixed spatial quantization limits performance on fine-grained dense prediction tasks, yet increasing token count incurs quadratic computational cost. The paper addresses how to recover sub-token spatial detail from pre-trained ViTs at inference time without modifying model weights, architecture, or forward pass.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image of resolution N×M and a pre-trained ViT with token size n×m (e.g., 16×16 or 8×8). It applies t sub-token translations (constrained to at most m/2 × n/2 pixels) to the input, runs each through the ViT, upsamples the resulting (N/n)×(M/m)×C feature maps to full N×M resolution, inverse-warps them to align with the original frame, and aggregates along the perturbation dimension to produce a full-resolution N×M×C feature field. This high-resolution output can be used directly for visualization or average-pooled back to (N/n)×(M/m)×C to feed into any downstream head (segmentation, depth, classification) without architectural changes.

## Method

SRT applies the stochastic resonance principle to the spatial domain: instead of adding noise to signal values, it shifts the input image by deterministic sub-token translations, extracts ViT features from each shifted version, upsamples to pixel resolution, inverse-warps to a common coordinate frame, and aggregates (mean or median) across all perturbations to yield a sub-token-resolved embedding. Translation is preferred over rotation or zoom because it preserves object scale, operates on integer pixel grids without interpolation artifacts, and admits an efficient implementation that bypasses explicit upsampling when average pooling is used. The aggregation occurs at the intermediate feature level rather than the output level, making it a feature-level ensemble compatible with any ViT layer and any downstream task. Formally, the process is cast as a closed-loop observer (moving average in higher resolution) where each perturbed measurement updates a full-resolution estimate. An optional self-distillation step (minimizing ‖φ_w(x) − SRT(x, w₀)‖) can bake the ensemble into the ViT weights, restoring single-pass inference cost while retaining the performance gain.

## Results

On DAVIS-2017 video object segmentation, SRT improves F&J score by 4.1% (ViT-S/16: 0.617→0.642), 1.3% (ViT-B/16: 0.622→0.630), and 2.0% (ViT-S/8: 0.706→0.720) over DINO baselines without any fine-tuning. On NYU-V2 monocular depth estimation, SRT achieves up to 14.9% relative improvement on RMSE_log and 4.7% on RMSE with linear heads, and 11.0% / 3.6% with DPT heads. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, mIoU improves by up to 1.7% (e.g., ViT-L/14 linear: 47.79→48.62). SRT also yields 2.6% relative improvement on Oxford image retrieval and 1.0% on VOC07 unsupervised object detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRT outperforms the overlapping-tokens approach of Amir et al. (2021) by a wide margin on DAVIS-2017: F&J 0.642 vs. 0.591 for ViT-S/16, a 5.1-point gap, while also being more computationally efficient (linear vs. quadratic in token count). A naive color-jitter ensemble is dramatically worse (F&J 0.477), confirming that spatial sub-token translation is the critical perturbation, not generic data augmentation. On NYU-V2 depth, output-space ensemble (OE) using the same perturbations but aggregating final predictions instead of intermediate features is often detrimental (e.g., ViT-S/14 linear RMSE worsens from 0.471 to 0.486), whereas SRT's feature-level aggregation consistently improves all metrics. Simple bilinear or bicubic interpolation of ViT features to full resolution is also far worse than baseline (e.g., ViT-L RMSE 0.311→0.732), validating that SRT's multi-pass ensemble captures information not recoverable by interpolation alone. The self-distillation variant (Eq. 4) recovers part of the gain (F&J 0.625) at single-forward-pass cost, though it trails full SRT (0.642).

## Relation to Prior Work

SRT extends the classical stochastic resonance principle (Benzi et al., 1981), originally formulated for overcoming quantization thresholds via additive noise, to the spatial domain of ViT inputs by applying sub-token translations. It departs from prior dense ViT feature extraction work (Amir et al., 2021), which uses overlapping tokens in a single forward pass, by instead performing multiple perturbed forward passes and aggregating at the feature level. The method is positioned as distinct from both super-resolution (which hallucinates missing detail via priors) and traditional anti-aliasing (which is inherently lossy), and from standard test-time augmentation that ensembles at the output level rather than at intermediate feature layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SRT offers a training-free, architecture-agnostic plug-in that consistently improves dense prediction tasks across multiple ViT backbones and layers, with the strongest gains (up to 14.9% on RMSE_log) on depth estimation where outlier sensitivity amplifies the benefit of smoother features. The self-distillation route for recovering SRT gains at single-forward-pass cost is promising but preliminary (recovering only ~1.3% of the 4.1% gain), leaving open how to efficiently compress ensemble information into a single model. Extending the perturbation group beyond translation to scale and domain-size transformations, and integrating stochastic resonance directly into ViT training, are identified as natural next steps.
