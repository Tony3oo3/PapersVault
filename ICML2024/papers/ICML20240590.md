## Problem

Prior work on inter-silo record-level differential privacy (ISRL-DP) federated learning characterized optimal excess risk bounds only for homogeneous (i.i.d.) silo data, leaving two open questions: whether the same optimal bounds hold under heterogeneous (non-i.i.d.) data distributions, and whether they can be achieved with fewer communication rounds. The state-of-the-art heterogeneous bound was suboptimal by a polynomial factor in the privacy and dimension parameters, and the communication complexity was significantly worse than the non-private lower bound.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes N silo datasets of n samples each drawn from arbitrary (potentially distinct) distributions, a convex L-Lipschitz loss function (smooth or non-smooth), a closed convex constraint set W of diameter D, and privacy parameters (ε, δ). It outputs a global model w_τ whose expected excess population risk is optimal up to logarithmic factors, along with explicit communication and gradient complexity guarantees. A key structural requirement is that each silo's full message transcript satisfies item-level DP, meaning the privacy guarantee holds even if the server or other silos collude to decode a given silo's data.

## Method

The core algorithm (Algorithm 1) iteratively solves a sequence of regularized ERM subproblems over geometrically shrinking data batches, using ISRL-DP Accelerated MB-SGD as the per-phase solver and a localization schedule (increasing regularization λ_i, decreasing batch size n_i) to enforce stability and generalization. The key insight enabling optimal heterogeneous bounds is that the stability and generalization guarantee for strongly convex regularized ERM does not require identically distributed data across silos, so the non-i.i.d. case is handled without additional loss. For non-smooth losses, the paper provides two variants: one using Nesterov (Moreau-Yosida) smoothing to reduce to the smooth case with improved communication complexity, and another using an ISRL-DP minibatch subgradient method for improved gradient complexity. For smooth losses with M=N, the communication complexity matches the non-private lower bound of Woodworth et al. (2020) in the high-heterogeneity regime, and the gradient complexity improves over prior state-of-the-art even in the centralized N=1 case.

## Results

The paper's headline result is achieving the minimax-optimal excess risk bound Õ(LD/√N · (1/√n + √(d log(1/δ))/(εn))) for heterogeneous (non-i.i.d.) ISRL-DP federated learning, resolving an open question from Lowy & Razaviyayn (2023a). For smooth losses, the communication complexity improves from Õ(Nn) (SOTA for i.i.d.) to Õ(N^(1/4)n^(1/4)), matching the non-private lower bound of Woodworth et al. (2020) in the high-heterogeneity regime. For non-smooth losses, communication complexity improves to Õ(√(Nn)). On MNIST binary logistic regression (N=25 silos, d=50), the localized ISRL-DP MB-SGD consistently outperforms the one-pass baseline across all privacy parameters ε in both reliable (M=N) and unreliable (M=18) communication settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Lowy & Razaviyayn (2023a) [LR'23], the paper's Algorithm 1 achieves the optimal excess risk for non-i.i.d. silo data, whereas LR'23's Algorithm 1 was suboptimal by a factor of Õ((√d/(εn√N))^(1/5)) in the smooth case. Communication complexity improves substantially: from N^(1/5)n^(1/5) to N^(1/4)n^(1/4) for smooth losses, and from N^(1/3)n^(1/3) to N^(1/2)n^(1/2) for non-smooth losses (via Nesterov smoothing). In the N=1 (centralized DP SCO) special case, the communication complexity is the square root of the prior SOTA bound from Zhang et al. (2022). In the MNIST experiments, the localized ISRL-DP MB-SGD (a practical non-accelerated variant of Algorithm 1) outperforms the one-pass ISRL-DP MB-SGD of Lowy et al. (2023a) across all tested privacy parameters ε in both reliable and unreliable communication settings, with lower average test error and comparable variance across 5 trials.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ISRL-DP federated learning framework of Lowy & Razaviyayn (2023a), which achieved optimal excess risk only for homogeneous (i.i.d.) silo data and left the heterogeneous case as an open question. The key technical departure is combining the localization technique of Feldman et al. (2020) with an algorithmic stability argument that does not require identically distributed data, applied to the multi-stage ISRL-DP Accelerated MB-SGD (a distributed version of AC-SA from Ghadimi & Lan, 2012). For non-smooth losses, the paper further incorporates Nesterov smoothing and convolutional smoothing (Kulkarni et al., 2021) to handle the lack of differentiability while preserving the optimal rate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work resolves a concrete open question from the ISRL-DP FL literature by showing that the minimax-optimal excess risk bound for i.i.d. data extends to arbitrarily heterogeneous silo data, and does so with communication complexity that matches the non-private lower bound of Woodworth et al. (2020) in the high-heterogeneity regime. The practical significance is that cross-silo FL (hospitals, banks) inherently involves heterogeneous data, so prior suboptimal bounds were not merely theoretical gaps but practical limitations. Open questions remain: a rigorous ISRL-DP communication lower bound, whether O(nN) gradient complexity is achievable, whether optimal communication and gradient complexity can be attained simultaneously, and extension beyond convex losses.
