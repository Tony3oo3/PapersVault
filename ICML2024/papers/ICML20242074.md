## Problem

Machine learning models face a fundamental tension: rapid adaptation to new tasks requires fast parameter changes, while retaining knowledge of previously learned tasks requires parameter stability. Existing continual learning methods (EWC, replay, architecture-based) address forgetting but do not achieve the rapid zero-shot transfer to novel environments that biological agents like rodents demonstrate in the Morris Water Maze. This paper introduces the sequential Morris Water Maze (sWM) benchmark and a biologically-inspired architecture that simultaneously achieves rapid learning and catastrophic-forgetting-free retention.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242074/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a partial observation of wall markings (120° field of view) from a 30×30 grid environment with unique noise-added step-function patterns, plus a velocity/action input for path integration. The output is a discrete movement action among four cardinal directions (N/S/E/W). The environment contains a hidden platform (goal) that the agent must reach within 100 steps; the goal location differs across the five sequential environments, and starting positions are randomized each episode.

## Method

The proposed vHSN architecture combines three components: (1) Vector-HaSH, a content-addressable memory modeling the entorhinal-hippocampal circuit, which maps sensory observations to grid cell codes via Hebbian-trained place-to-grid projections and pseudoinverse-trained sensory-to-place weights, with remapping (orthogonalization of internal representations) enabling separate storage per environment; (2) a randomly initialized, fixed CNN that computes a spatially invariant displacement representation between the current and goal grid codes, so the policy generalizes across environments without retraining; and (3) an attention module that stores key-value pairs associating displacement representations with actions, enabling zero-shot action retrieval in new environments. Training occurs in a single environment: the agent explores, learns the observation-to-grid-code mapping, stores the goal grid code, and builds displacement-to-action associations. In subsequent environments, only the Vector-HaSH sensory-to-place weights are updated (via online pseudoinverse), while the CNN and attention policy remain frozen, yielding near-perfect success rates (~99%) across all five environments without catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

On the sequential Morris Water Maze (sWM) task with 5 environments, vHSN achieves a 99.2% average success rate across all environments (ranging from 98.8% to 99.5% per environment), demonstrating near-perfect navigation in every environment simultaneously. The best continual-learning baseline, EWC, achieves only 23.2% average success rate, and the next-best, Fine-Tuning, reaches just 19.5%. The metric is the percentage of trials in which the agent successfully reaches the hidden platform within 100 steps, evaluated after training on all five environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

vHSN decisively outperforms all five continual-learning baselines on the sWM task, achieving 99.2% average success rate versus a best baseline (EWC) of only 23.2%—a gap of over 76 percentage points. EWC and Fine-Tuning retain near-perfect performance only on the most recently trained environment (Env 5: 100% and 99.9% respectively) but collapse to near-zero on earlier environments, confirming catastrophic forgetting. Replay-based methods (DER at 2.05%, DER++ at 3.39%, A-GEM at 7.31%, ER at 16.92%) all fail to maintain proficiency across environments, with most scoring 0% on at least one environment. The authors attribute EWC's failure to input similarity across environments causing conflicting action mappings, and replay methods' failure to a few trajectories disproportionately disrupting the network due to similar observations mapping to different actions. Notably, vHSN requires policy training in only the first environment and achieves zero-shot transfer to the remaining four, whereas all baselines require retraining or fine-tuning in each environment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Vector-HaSH content-addressable memory architecture (Chandra et al., 2023), which models the neocortical-entorhinal-hippocampal circuit, and repurposes it for spatial navigation in a sequential learning setting. It departs from the standard continual learning literature (EWC, DER, A-GEM, Experience Replay) by replacing unstructured parameter regularization or replay with structured, biologically-motivated inductive biases: grid-cell-based spatial representations, a fixed spatially-invariant CNN, and remapping to orthogonalize environment-specific memories. The paper explicitly positions itself against the common ML finding that adding constraints hurts performance, arguing instead that the right biological constraints (low-dimensional grid codes, spatial invariance, content-addressable storage) simultaneously enable rapid adaptation and long-term retention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that two goals typically in tension in continual learning—rapid plasticity for new tasks and stability for old ones—can be reconciled through architectural inductive biases inspired by the entorhinal-hippocampal system, achieving near-perfect retention across all environments after training on just one. However, the evaluation is limited to a simple 2D grid world with four discrete actions and only five environments, and the baselines are general-purpose continual learning methods not specifically designed for spatial navigation, so the large performance gap may partly reflect task-method mismatch rather than a fundamental advantage. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional or continuous action spaces, whether the fixed random CNN is truly necessary versus other spatial invariance mechanisms, and how the system behaves with many more environments or with intra-environment goal changes (partially addressed in the dsWM appendix).
