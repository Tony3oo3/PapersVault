## Problem

Individual feature attribution methods produce inconsistent, unstable, and hyperparameter-sensitive explanations that frequently disagree with one another, undermining their reliability for model interpretability. While the idea of combining multiple attributions has been explored informally (e.g., simple averaging), no theoretically grounded strategy existed for deriving optimal convex combinations that provably improve specific quality criteria such as robustness or faithfulness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240409/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given k feature attribution vectors (each d-dimensional, normalized to [0,1]) computed for the same input image x and model prediction f(x), the method consumes a small set of m_agg=50 metric evaluation samples (input perturbations ε for robustness, or binary feature-removal masks I for faithfulness) to solve for convex weights ω on the k-simplex. The output is the aggregated attribution φ^ω = Σωᵢφⁱ(x), which is then evaluated on m_eval=200 unseen metric samples to verify generalization. All experiments use 224×224 RGB images from ImageNet or 32×32/224×224 images from CIFAR-10 and MedMNIST variants.

## Method

The key insight is that common explanation quality metrics (Average-Sensitivity, Infidelity, and others) can be unified under a 'generalized L2 metric' form Q(φ(x)) = E[‖γ₁φ(x) − γ₂‖²], where γ₁ is a linear query and γ₂ a target. Under this formulation, minimizing Q over convex aggregation weights reduces to a constrained quadratic program on the simplex, solvable in closed form via standard QP solvers (CVXPY). The paper proves two theoretical guarantees: (1) an error-ambiguity decomposition (Theorem 4.2) showing the aggregated explanation is always at least as good as the weighted average of individual qualities, with the gain depending on inter-method diversity; and (2) a Rademacher-complexity-based generalization bound (Theorem 4.3) showing that weights estimated from m samples achieve near-optimal quality with O(1/√m) convergence. Three aggregation strategies are instantiated: AGG_robust (minimizes SENS_AVG), AGG_faith (minimizes INFD), and AGG_opt (jointly minimizes both with Frobenius-norm normalization), all requiring only 50 metric samples for weight optimization.

## Results

On ImageNet with VGG16, AGG_robust achieves SENS_AVG of 0.424 and SENS_MAX of 0.543, outperforming the best individual method (Guided BP: 0.515/0.650) and the best prior aggregation (AGG_Var: 0.582/0.700). For faithfulness on DenseNet121, AGG_faith achieves INFD of 2.390 and FCOR of 0.406, beating the best individual method (SmoothGrad: 2.604/0.388) and AGG_Mean (2.661/0.370). AGG_opt, which jointly optimizes both robustness and faithfulness, is consistently second-best or comparable across all metrics and model architectures (VGG16, AlexNet, ResNet18, MobileNetV2, MLPMixer, DenseNet121, DeiT, SwinT). Results generalize to CIFAR-10 and three medical imaging datasets (BloodMNIST, DermaMNIST, PathMNIST) with the same ranking pattern.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All eleven individual attribution methods (Saliency, Guided BP, DeepLift, IntGrad, InputxGrad, SmoothGrad, VarGrad, GradSHAP, GradCAM, GradCAM++, EigenCAM) are consistently outperformed by the proposed aggregation strategies on both robustness and faithfulness metrics across all model architectures. For robustness, AGG_robust improves SENS_AVG by 17–57% over the best individual method depending on architecture (e.g., 0.424 vs. 0.515 for Guided BP on VGG16; 0.410 vs. 0.483 for Guided BP on ResNet18). For faithfulness, AGG_faith reduces INFD by 8–15% relative to the best individual method (e.g., 2.390 vs. 2.604 for SmoothGrad on DenseNet121; 0.335 vs. 0.385 for GradCAM on DeiT) and increases FCOR by 4–19%. The two prior aggregation baselines from Rieger & Hansen (2019), AGG_Mean and AGG_Var, produce nearly identical results to each other and are consistently outperformed by AGG_robust, AGG_opt, and AGG_faith on every metric and model tested. Notably, AGG_faith is suboptimal for robustness (e.g., SENS_AVG of 0.644 on VGG16 vs. 0.424 for AGG_robust), confirming that optimizing for one quality criterion does not automatically transfer to another, and motivating the joint AGG_opt strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on aggregating multiple feature attribution results, building directly on the simple averaging and variance-weighted baselines of Rieger & Hansen (2019) and the neighbor-based Shapley aggregation of Bhatt et al. (2021). The key conceptual departure is framing optimal aggregation as a constrained quadratic program over a unified class of generalized L2 quality metrics, which enables provable improvement guarantees. The provable improvement theorem (Theorem 4.2) adapts the error-ambiguity decomposition from Krogh & Vedelsby (1994) in ensemble learning, while the generalization bound (Theorem 4.3) leverages a vector-contraction inequality for Rademacher complexities from Maurer (2016). The motivation is grounded in the 'no-free lunch' theorem for explanations (Han et al., 2022), which establishes that no single attribution method can universally approximate all models faithfully.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretically grounded framework for optimally combining feature attributions, providing practitioners with a principled alternative to ad-hoc averaging. The provable guarantee that aggregation never degrades quality (Theorem 4.2) and the O(1/√m) generalization bound (Theorem 4.3) give strong justification for the approach even with limited evaluation samples. Key limitations include increased inference time (up to ~38s for DenseNet121), reliance on the quality of individual methods, and restriction to convex combinations. Open questions include extending beyond convex weighting to voting or ensemble methods, incorporating heterogeneous explanation types (concept-based, counterfactual), and understanding whether the approach transfers to non-image modalities.
