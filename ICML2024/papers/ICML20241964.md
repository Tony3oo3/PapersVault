## Problem

Standard Branch-and-Bound (BnB) algorithms for ℓ₀-regularized optimization require solving a convex relaxation at every node of the decision tree to evaluate pruning tests, making this step the dominant computational bottleneck. This paper addresses the gap by showing that pruning tests for multiple regions can be evaluated simultaneously at negligible cost, eliminating the need to solve separate convex problems for each candidate region.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a data matrix A ∈ R^{m×n}, a target vector y ∈ R^m, a proper closed convex loss f, a proper closed convex regularization h (with h(0)=0 and 0 as an accumulation point of dom(h)), and a sparsity parameter λ > 0. It returns a sparse minimizer x of the ℓ₀-regularized problem to machine precision along with the optimal objective value. The BnB tree partitions the feasible space into regions defined by which coordinates are forced zero (S₀), forced non-zero (S₁), or free (S_•), and the method's pruning tests operate on these set partitions rather than on individual points.

## Method

The key insight is that the Fenchel-Rockafellar dual objective functions at a BnB node ν and all its successors share a common term D^ν(u), so evaluating the dual-based lower bound for all 2|S_•| direct successors of ν requires only a single evaluation of this shared term plus O(|S_•|) additional scalar computations, reducing the per-node cost from O(mnκ) (solving a convex relaxation) to O(m+n). The dual point u is chosen from −∂f(Ax̂) where x̂ is the current iterate of the first-order method solving the relaxation, making the inner products {a_i^T u} already available at no extra cost. When multiple direct successors pass the pruning test simultaneously, Algorithm 1 recursively prunes entire subtrees without ever solving the relaxations at those pruned nodes. The approach is embedded in a standard depth-first BnB implementation with coordinate descent for the relaxations, and the simultaneous pruning is checked at every iteration of the relaxation solver, allowing early termination as soon as any successor is prunable.

## Results

On synthetic data (m=500, n=1000, k=5), E10ps solves all 100 instances within a time budget where no competing solver (Mosek, Cplex, Gurobi, L0bnb) has solved a single instance, representing at least a one-order-of-magnitude acceleration. On six real-world feature-selection datasets (dimensions ranging from 38×7129 to 536×17322), the method outperforms all baselines across all loss/regularization combinations, with time savings reaching up to four orders of magnitude versus off-the-shelf MIP solvers and up to two orders of magnitude versus the specialized L0bnb solver. A sensitivity study isolating the simultaneous pruning component shows a minimum 5× speedup over the standard BnB strategy alone, with gains increasing with problem dimension n and feature correlation ρ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares E10ps against two categories of baselines: (1) off-the-shelf MIP solvers (Mosek, Cplex, Gurobi) and (2) the specialized L0bnb solver (Hazimeh et al., 2022). Against the MIP solvers, E10ps achieves up to four orders of magnitude speedup on real-world datasets, and on synthetic data solves all instances in a time window where MIP solvers solve none. Against L0bnb, E10ps achieves up to two orders of magnitude improvement in the best-case scenarios. Notably, the BnB implementation choices of E10ps (depth-first exploration, coordinate descent with working-set strategy) are deliberately aligned with those of L0bnb, so the observed speedup is attributed primarily to the proposed simultaneous pruning strategy rather than implementation differences. Cplex and Gurobi cannot handle the logistic loss instances, and L0bnb is restricted to least-squares loss with quadratic regularization, further limiting the comparison scope.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the duality-based node-screening approach of Atamturk & Gomez (2020) and Guyard et al. (2022), which were restricted to specific ℓ₀-regularized instances, to a general framework covering a broad family of problems under mild convexity hypotheses (H1–H4). The key departure is the observation that the Fenchel-Rockafellar dual objective at a BnB node and all its successors share a common structure (Proposition 2), enabling simultaneous pruning tests at O(m+n) cost rather than the O(mnκ) cost of solving a separate convex relaxation per node. The simultaneous tree expansion (Algorithm 1), where multiple child regions are pruned in one pass, is a new algorithmic contribution not present in prior BnB implementations for ℓ₀ problems such as L0bnb (Hazimeh et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: solving times improve by 1–4 orders of magnitude over state-of-the-art MIP solvers and specialized BnB methods, making previously intractable ℓ₀-regularized feature selection and sparse regression problems computationally accessible. A key limitation is that the duality-based lower bound (13) can be looser than the standard convex relaxation (8), so the method complements rather than replaces the relaxation-based pruning; its effectiveness depends on the duality gap being small enough to trigger premature stopping. Open questions include whether the framework extends to non-convex loss functions (violating H1), how the gains scale in the high-dimensional regime where m ≫ n, and whether the simultaneous pruning idea can be adapted to other combinatorial structures beyond coordinate-wise ℓ₀ sparsity.
