## Problem

While differentially-private (DP) synthetic data that preserves low-order marginals is an attractive alternative to training-based DP-ML (since the synthetic data can be generated once and reused for unlimited downstream tasks without additional privacy cost), it remained unclear whether such data can provably yield low excess risk for downstream ML models. Prior analyses either required strong distributional assumptions (e.g., the data must be representable as a low-degree Bayesian network) or produced error bounds growing exponentially with dimension, leaving a gap in distribution-free utility guarantees connecting marginal preservation quality to model performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real tabular dataset of n samples with m discrete features (normalized to [-1,1]) and binary labels in {-1,1}, along with privacy parameters (ε, δ) and a marginal order d. It produces a DP synthetic dataset of the same size n that approximately preserves all marginals up to order d, which can then be used to train any linear model (e.g., logistic regression via LBFGS) without injecting additional noise during training. The theoretical output is a bound on the excess empirical risk of the synthetic-trained model relative to the real-data-trained model, parameterized by the ℓ₁ marginal distance ν, dimension m, and marginal order d.

## Method

The core technique approximates the loss function φ with its degree-(d-1) Bernstein polynomial, which expands the empirical risk into a sum of monomials each involving at most d attributes, making each term directly computable from the corresponding d-th order marginal. The upper bound (Theorem 3.1) shows that if all marginals up to order d have ℓ₁ distance at most ν between real and synthetic data, the excess risk is O(1/√(d-1) + (3m)^(d-1)·ν/n) for general K-Lipschitz losses, with a tighter O(1/(d-1) + (2m)^(d-1)·ν/n) for logistic regression (Theorem 3.2) via a sharper Bernstein approximation for functions with continuous first derivatives. For the end-to-end DP guarantee, the mechanism adds Gaussian noise to all marginals up to order d and then finds synthetic data minimizing the maximum ℓ₁ deviation from the noisy marginals, yielding a privacy-utility trade-off where excess risk is dominated by O(1/√(d-1)) as n→∞. The lower bound (Theorem 4.1) leverages non-adaptive statistical query lower bounds from Dagan & Feldman (2020) to show that for certain parameter regimes (m = O(ln⁶(n)), d = O(ln(n)/ln ln(n))), no synthetic data algorithm can achieve excess risk below Ω(1/ln³(n)), nearly matching the upper bound of O(√(ln(ln(n))/ln(n))).

## Results

Using the AIM mechanism with (2, 1/n²)-DP, the accuracy of logistic regression models trained on marginal-preserving synthetic data drops by less than 1% compared to real-data models on five of six datasets (Adult: 0.842 vs 0.843, Compas: 0.666 vs 0.671, Churn: 0.757 vs 0.766, Dutch: 0.817 vs 0.817, Law: 0.896 vs 0.901), with excess empirical risk below 0.02. The Heart dataset (n=303) is the exception, showing a 2.2% accuracy drop (0.780 vs 0.802) and 0.032 excess empirical risk, attributed to its small sample size. Theoretically, the excess risk is upper-bounded by O(1/√(d−1)) for general Lipschitz losses and O(1/(d−1)) for logistic regression as n→∞, with a matching lower bound of Ω(1/ln³(n)) for worst-case distributions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the Pre-DPML approach (AIM marginal-preserving synthetic data) against two Training-DPML baselines: DP-SGD (Abadi et al., 2016) and PATE (Papernot et al., 2016). At ε=2, the AIM synthetic data approach achieves within 1% accuracy of the non-private real-data model on five of six datasets, with the sole exception being Heart (2.2% drop due to its small n=303). The paper argues a structural advantage over both DP-SGD and PATE: the synthetic data is generated once and can be reused for any number of downstream models without consuming additional privacy budget, whereas DP-SGD's privacy budget grows with the number of training runs and PATE requires public unlabeled data plus multiple teacher models. The paper also demonstrates model-agnosticism: the same AIM synthetic dataset can train classifiers with different target labels without re-generation, a flexibility not available to DP-SGD or PATE which are tied to a specific training objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions Pre-DPML (training on DP synthetic data) as an alternative to Training-DPML (DP-SGD, PATE), arguing that synthetic data generation decouples privacy from downstream computation. It builds on the marginal-preserving synthetic data generation literature (PrivBayes, Private-PGM, AIM) and the lower-bound techniques of Dagan & Feldman (2020) on non-adaptive statistical query learning. The key departure from prior utility analysis (Li et al., 2023) is the use of Bernstein polynomial approximation to bound excess risk for arbitrary continuous Lipschitz losses without requiring Bayesian network structure or incurring exponential dependence on dimension.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first distribution-free upper and lower bounds on excess empirical risk for linear models trained on marginal-preserving DP synthetic data, establishing that the approach has fundamental polylog(n) information-theoretic limits. Practically, it validates that with ε=2 DP, accuracy drops are under 1% on most datasets, supporting the 'generate once, train many times' paradigm that avoids privacy budget accumulation. Open questions include extending to non-linear models (neural networks, SVMs), handling continuous or high-cardinality attributes, and identifying data distribution assumptions that bypass the lower bound.
