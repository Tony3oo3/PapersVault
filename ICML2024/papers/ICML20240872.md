## Problem

Existing neural SBI methods (NPE, NLE, NRE) rely on normalizing flows or classifiers to model the full posterior or likelihood, and all can produce biased posteriors under limited simulation budgets with no cheap post-hoc fix. Quantile regression, a well-studied tool in statistics, had not been explored as a basis for SBI, and no prior method offered a guaranteed-unbiased calibration step that avoids MCMC sampling or expensive repeated posterior evaluations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes observation data x (tabular vectors or 2D projected density fields, e.g. 128×128 cosmological maps) together with a prior distribution over the parameter vector θ, and produces posterior samples drawn sequentially via inverse transform sampling from per-dimension interpolated CDFs. A key structural detail is that each 1D conditional quantile network is conditioned on both x and all previously sampled θ dimensions, making the output an autoregressive chain of 1D distributions rather than a single joint density. The method also outputs a Quantile Mapping Credible Region (QMCR) whose rank is computed analytically from a χ² distribution, requiring only O(N_o) network calls versus O(N_o·N_r) for the standard HPDR.

## Method

NQE trains one MLP per posterior dimension to predict n_bin equally-spaced conditional quantiles (default 16) using a weighted pinball (quantile) loss, with the i-th dimension conditioned on x and all earlier θ dimensions; the network outputs are parameterized through a softmax to enforce monotonicity. Predicted quantiles are interpolated into a continuous CDF via a modified PCHIP scheme (PCHIP-ET) that replaces polynomial segments with Gaussian tails in low-density bins and uses double-exponential fits in bins between multimodal peaks, preserving monotonicity and first-derivative continuity. A smoothness regularization term penalizes bins whose local PDF exceeds the interpolated value from neighboring bins, activated only when necessary to avoid suppressing legitimate multimodal valleys. For calibration, a single global broadening factor is solved by fixing all conditional medians and scaling the distance to every other quantile, ensuring the empirical q-coverage matches the target credibility level with ≲10³ validation simulations at negligible additional cost. An optional quantile-shifting calibration further removes per-quantile, per-dimension bias for known model misspecification without over-broadening the posterior.

## Results

NQE is evaluated on six sbibm benchmark problems (Two Moons, SLCPD, BGLMR, Gaussian Mixture, SIR, Lotka-Volterra) using C2ST as the primary metric (lower is better; 0.5 = perfect posterior, 1.0 = complete failure). Uncalibrated NQE achieves state-of-the-art C2ST across all six problems, matching or surpassing SNPE, SNLE, SNRE, and ABC baselines. After broadening calibration, NQE consistently produces unbiased posteriors (q-coverage matching the target credibility levels) using only ~10³ validation simulations with O(N_o) network calls, compared to O(N_i·N_o·N_r·N_m) for competing methods. In the cosmology application, quantile shifting calibration eliminates bias from known model misspecification without over-broadening the posterior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NQE outperforms all five baselines (SNPE, SNLE, SNRE, NPE, and ABC) on C2ST across all six benchmark problems, as shown in Figure 4 where NQE curves are at or near the bottom (best) for every problem. The most significant practical advantage over SNPE, SNLE, and SNRE is in posterior calibration: NQE's q-coverage evaluation requires only O(N_o) network calls, while all other methods require O(N_i·N_o·N_r·N_m) calls due to the need for MCMC sampling and PDF ranking, making calibration for competing methods computationally prohibitive in higher dimensions. Unlike NPE which has no known broadening technique that avoids MCMC, NQE's quantile-based representation enables a simple global broadening transform that preserves the autoregressive sampling structure. The paper notes that after global broadening calibration, C2ST is generally similar or slightly worse than uncalibrated NQE (Figure 16), reflecting the C2ST metric's tendency to penalize conservative posteriors similarly to biased ones. For the cosmology application with model misspecification, quantile shifting calibration achieves near-diagonal empirical coverage while global broadening is over-conservative, demonstrating the value of more advanced calibration schemes.

## Relation to Prior Work

NQE introduces a fourth family of neural SBI methods alongside NPE, NRE, and NLE, replacing the dominant Normalizing Flow density estimators with autoregressive 1D conditional quantile regression (Koenker & Bassett Jr, 1978) and PCHIP-ET spline interpolation. The autoregressive structure is conceptually similar to FMPE (Dax et al., 2023) and autoregressive NRE (Montel et al., 2023), but the quantile-based parameterization and the QMCR credible region (building on Talts et al., 2018) are novel contributions to the SBI framework. The post-processing broadening calibration directly addresses the bias problem identified by Hermans et al. (2021), while the idea of interpolating predicted quantiles draws from time-series forecasting (Gasthaus et al., 2019; Sun et al., 2023) but is, to the authors' knowledge, the first application in SBI.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The guaranteed unbiasedness via broadening calibration with only ~10³ validation simulations—regardless of posterior dimensionality—provides a practical safety net that NF-based methods lack, particularly valuable in applications like cosmology where model misspecification from baryonic physics is a known concern. The QMCR's O(N_o) evaluation cost versus O(N_o·N_r) for HPDR makes coverage-based calibration computationally feasible where it was previously prohibitive. Open questions include whether sequential NQE (SNQE) can match the gains of SNPE/SNRE, whether higher-order interpolation can eliminate the PCHIP-ET's discontinuous PDF derivatives, and whether more sophisticated per-quantile calibration (beyond global broadening) can remove bias without over-conservatism in high-dimensional settings.
