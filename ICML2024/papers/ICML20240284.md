## Problem

Performative prediction requires minimizing a risk that depends on the data distribution induced by the deployed model, but existing methods rely on assumptions that are easily violated in practice: convexity of the performative risk in the model parameter, prior knowledge of the distribution map, and availability of gradient information. This paper initiates the study of performative prediction under bandit feedback where the distribution map is unknown, the performative risk is non-convex in the model parameter, and only sample-level loss observations are available.

## Input / Output

The learner interacts with the environment over T_total deployment steps, each time deploying a model θ and receiving n_τ i.i.d. samples from the induced distribution D(θ) along with the corresponding loss values—no gradients or distribution-map queries are available. The algorithm outputs both an optimal model parameter θ* and an optimal distribution parameter φ*, with the regret measured over the total number of performative samples N rather than just the number of deployment rounds, reflecting the cumulative cost of deploying suboptimal models on real decision subjects.

## Method

The core idea is a reparameterization that rewrites the non-convex performative risk PR(θ) as a convex function PR†(φ) over the induced distribution parameter φ = φ(θ), under the assumption that PR† is convex and Lipschitz in φ. The outer algorithm (MinimizePR) performs two-point zeroth-order gradient descent in the distribution parameter space, but since the learner cannot directly evaluate PR†(φ), it calls an inner subroutine (LearnModel) at each iteration to find a model θ that approximately induces the target φ. LearnModel itself runs a two-point zeroth-order optimization minimizing the KL divergence between the target distribution and the model-induced distribution, using an off-the-shelf KL estimation oracle. The resulting two-level procedure achieves a sublinear regret bound of Õ((d_Θ + d_Φ) · N_KL^{1/6} · N^{5/6}) in the total number of performative samples, polynomial in the parameter dimensions.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is a sublinear regret bound: after N performative samples, the algorithm achieves regret Õ((d_Θ + d_Φ) · N_KL^(1/6) · N^(5/6) · √(log(1/p))) with probability at least 1−p (Theorem 4), where N_KL is the sample complexity of the KL-divergence estimator. The outer algorithm (MinimizePR) achieves O(√(d_Φ T) + √(ε_LM d_Φ)·T + √(T log(1/p))) regret in T steps (Theorem 2), and the inner algorithm (LearnModel) achieves O(√(d_Θ S) + √(ε_KL d_Θ)·S + √(S log(1/p))) regret in S steps (Theorem 3). Empirically, on a toy Bernoulli example (Example 3), the reparameterization method converges noticeably faster than directly optimizing PR(θ) in model-parameter space, confirming the theoretical advantage when PR is convex in φ but non-convex in θ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include a formal multi-baseline comparison table; the empirical evaluation is limited to a single toy example (Bernoulli distribution with φ(θ)=θ²) where the proposed reparameterization method is compared against a baseline that directly minimizes PR(θ) in model-parameter space using the same two-point zeroth-order scheme. The reparameterization method (orange curve) demonstrates a faster average convergence rate over multiple runs, while both methods ultimately converge. Theoretically, the paper positions itself against prior performative prediction work (Perdomo et al. 2020; Miller et al. 2021; Jagadeesan et al. 2022) by removing three standard assumptions: convexity of PR in θ, known distribution map, and availability of first-order information. The key distinction from Miller et al. (2021) is that the convexity condition here only needs to hold for each individual data parameter φ, rather than requiring the stronger 'mixture dominance assumption' over all triples (θ, θ′, θ₀) ∈ Θ³, making the condition strictly weaker and more broadly applicable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the performative prediction framework of Perdomo et al. (2020) by simultaneously removing three standard assumptions: convexity of the performative risk in the model parameter, knowledge of the distribution map, and availability of first-order information. The core algorithmic building block is the two-point zeroth-order convex optimization technique from Agarwal et al. (2010) and Shamir (2017), adapted here to a two-level structure where the outer loop optimizes over distribution parameters and the inner loop (LearnModel) finds a model inducing a target distribution via KL-divergence minimization. The reparameterization idea—expressing PR as a function of the induced distribution parameter rather than the model parameter—draws conceptual parallels to learning from revealed preferences (Roth et al., 2016; Dong et al., 2018), though the problem setting is distinct.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first provable sublinear regret guarantee for performative prediction under bandit feedback without requiring convexity in the model parameter or a known distribution map, which are conditions frequently violated in real-world social prediction (recommendation systems, criminal prediction, education). However, the practical impact is currently limited: the double-loop zeroth-order optimization is expected to converge slowly, the empirical validation is restricted to a toy example, and the key assumption that PR†(φ) is convex in the distribution parameter, while milder than convexity in θ, still restricts the class of applicable problems. Open questions include whether the N^(5/6) regret rate can be improved, whether parallelization can make the double-loop practical at scale, and whether the convexity-in-φ condition holds broadly in real performative settings.
