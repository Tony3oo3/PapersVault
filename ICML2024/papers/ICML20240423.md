## Problem

Interpretable policy learning forces a tradeoff between accuracy and interpretability: black-box recurrent models capture complex sequential decision-making but resist explanation, while constrained parametric models (decision trees, belief-state summaries) remain interpretable but sacrifice performance. The root cause is that existing methods compress the decision process into a single universal observation-to-action mapping, ignoring that human decisions are dynamic and shift drastically under different clinical contexts. CPR addresses this by reframing policy recovery as multi-task learning of simple context-specific policies rather than one complex universal policy.

## Input / Output

CPR consumes partially observable treatment trajectories (no reward signal) consisting of per-timestep patient observations (e.g., 7 vitals for MIMIC antibiotics, CDR-SB and hippocampal volume for ADNI), the full history of prior observations and binary actions, and optional static patient covariates (age, gender) encoded into the initial hidden state. The output at each patient-timestep pair is not merely a predicted action probability but a full interpretable linear decision model: a vector of logistic regression coefficients θ_t that directly quantifies each current observation's log-odds contribution toward the action, generated on-demand by the context encoder. A global variant (CPR-global) additionally decomposes the prediction as a telescoping linear combination of all historical features and actions, yielding exact per-feature attribution across the entire trajectory.

## Method

CPR factorizes the sequential policy P(a_t | x_1, a_1, …, x_t) into a black-box context encoder g (vanilla RNN or LSTM) that maps the full observation-action history to a parameter vector θ_t, and a glass-box observation-to-action function f (logistic regression with lasso regularization) that maps current observations x_t to action probabilities using θ_t. This hypernetwork-style architecture means the interpretable component is a simple linear map whose coefficients are directly readable as feature importances, while the black-box encoder retains full representational capacity over context. Training is supervised behavioral cloning with binary cross-entropy loss over all timesteps and patients, jointly optimizing g and θ with early stopping. The framework is modular: g can be any differentiable recurrent model and f can be any differentiable interpretable model, and a global-interpretability variant introduces a second encoder h and a telescoping bias update μ_t = α⟨β_{t-1}, [x_{t-1}, a_{t-1}]⟩ + (1−α)μ_{t-1} so that every historical feature retains an exact linear effect in the final prediction.

## Results

On MIMIC-III antibiotic prescription, CPR-RNN/LSTM achieves 0.82 AUROC and 0.90 AUPRC, substantially outperforming the best interpretable baselines (POETREE: 0.68 AUROC; INTERPOLE: 0.65 AUROC) while matching black-box RNN/LSTM (0.83–0.84 AUROC). On ADNI MRI prescription, CPR achieves 0.72 AUROC and 0.88 AUPRC, outperforming POETREE (0.62 AUROC) and INTERPOLE (0.60 AUROC), and tying the black-box RNN (0.72 AUROC). CPR also achieves the lowest Brier scores among interpretable methods on both tasks (0.14 on MIMIC, 0.15 on ADNI), indicating well-calibrated predictions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPR decisively outperforms both interpretable baselines on both datasets. On MIMIC-III, CPR achieves 0.82 AUROC versus POETREE's 0.68 (+14 absolute points) and INTERPOLE's 0.65 (+17 absolute points), closing the gap to black-box LSTM (0.84) to within 2 points. On ADNI, CPR reaches 0.72 AUROC versus POETREE's 0.62 (+10 points) and INTERPOLE's 0.60 (+12 points), matching the black-box RNN exactly. Notably, CPR also achieves the best Brier score among all interpretable methods on both tasks, indicating superior calibration. The black-box RNN/LSTM baselines edge out CPR by only 1–2 AUROC points on MIMIC and tie on ADNI, confirming that CPR's interpretable linear policy representation incurs negligible accuracy cost relative to fully black-box sequential models.

## Relation to Prior Work

CPR builds directly on the contextualized modeling framework (Lengerich et al., 2023), which decomposes a complex prediction problem into a rich context encoder generating parameters for a simple parametric model, and on the hypernetwork architecture (Ha et al., 2016) where one network produces the weights of another. The key departure from prior interpretable policy learning (INTERPOLE, POETREE) is rejecting the universal-policy paradigm: rather than constraining a single observation-to-action mapping to be interpretable (via decision trees or belief states), CPR reframes policy recovery as multi-task learning where each patient context yields a distinct, simple linear policy. This allows the black-box recurrent encoder to absorb all representational complexity while the per-context logistic regression head remains directly interpretable without post-processing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CPR closes the long-standing accuracy-interpretability gap in offline policy learning, matching black-box RNN/LSTM performance while providing sample-specific linear decision models that reveal treatment heterogeneity invisible to universal policies. The framework is modular (any recurrent encoder, any differentiable interpretable head) and portable to online settings, making it a general-purpose platform for interpretable sequential decision-making beyond the two medical tasks studied. Open questions include whether the assumption that recent observations dominate decision-making holds in longer-horizon or multi-agent clinical settings, whether the global-interpretability variant's error accumulation limits its utility over extended trajectories, and whether the approach scales to high-dimensional observation spaces (e.g., imaging) where linear per-context policies may be insufficient.
