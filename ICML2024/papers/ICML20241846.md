## Problem

TabNet's learned attention masks are dense and heavily overlapping across decision steps, making it difficult for practitioners to identify which specific features drive each prediction. Existing post-hoc methods like SHAP distribute importance across many features, and attention weights in transformers are similarly diffuse, leaving a gap between predictive accuracy and actionable interpretability for tabular data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241846/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a tabular matrix X ∈ R^{N×D} (with both categorical and continuous features, label-encoded) and class labels Y during training, and produces a class prediction at inference. Beyond the prediction, it outputs K sequential sparse feature importance masks (one per decision step), where each mask assigns near-one-hot weights to only 2–3 salient features per sample, ensuring mutual exclusivity across steps. A final output is a GPT-4-generated dictionary mapping each mask to a natural-language explanation of the selected features' interdependencies, plus an aggregate summary across all masks.

## Method

InterpreTabNet reformulates TabNet's encoder as a conditional variational autoencoder where each attention mask m_k is treated as a latent variable sampled from a Gumbel-Softmax distribution parameterized by the TabNet Transformer's output. The key architectural change is a KL-divergence sparsity regularizer r_M that maximizes the KL divergence between the Gumbel-Softmax distributions of adjacent masks, forcing the model to select mutually exclusive, high-salience features at each decision step rather than reusing the same features. An adaptive recursive search algorithm tunes r_M to balance mask sparsity (2–3 features per mask, each capturing 20–25% importance) against classification accuracy. Post-hoc, the sparse masks are formatted into structured prompts with few-shot in-context examples and fed to GPT-4, which generates per-mask and aggregate natural-language interpretations that capture feature interdependencies beyond what the raw masks convey.

## Results

InterpreTabNet achieves the best test accuracy on 2 out of 7 datasets: Adult Census Income (87.42%) and Forest Cover Type (94.75%), while remaining within a few percentage points of the best baseline on most others. On the Higgs dataset (11M samples), it attains 53.08% accuracy, slightly above Original TabNet (52.94%) but well below tree-based methods (~72.7%). The paper's primary quantitative claim is interpretability: in a blind human evaluation survey, 65% of respondents preferred InterpreTabNet's feature masks for identifying salient features, compared to 15% for TabNet and 5% each for XGBoost and LightGBM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Original TabNet, InterpreTabNet wins on Adult Census (+1.87 pp), Forest Cover (+0.57 pp), Poker Hand (+0.50 pp), and Higgs (+0.14 pp), but loses on Mushroom (−3.32 pp), Blastchar (−3.26 pp), and Diabetes (−1.54 pp), reflecting the accuracy cost of the KL-divergence sparsity regularizer. Against tree-based baselines (XGBoost, LightGBM), InterpreTabNet is substantially outperformed on Higgs (53.08% vs. ~72.7%), Diabetes (55.37% vs. ~61%), and Blastchar (72.96% vs. ~77–78%), but dominates on Poker Hand (99.50% vs. 75.57%/78.47%) and Forest Cover (94.75% vs. 92.30%/86.38%). Against TabTransformer, InterpreTabNet leads on Adult Census (+2.33 pp), Forest Cover (+12.20 pp), Diabetes (+10.92 pp), and Higgs (+1.11 pp), while trailing slightly on Poker Hand (−0.31 pp) and Mushroom (−3.38 pp). Against MLP, InterpreTabNet is consistently stronger on Adult Census (+7.66 pp) and Forest Cover (+9.86 pp) but weaker on Higgs (−10.09 pp). The paper's central argument is that the modest accuracy tradeoff (typically <3 pp on most datasets) is justified by a large interpretability gain: the sparse, mutually exclusive feature masks produced by the Gumbel-Softmax + KL-divergence formulation are rated significantly more interpretable than the dense attention masks of TabNet or the distributed SHAP values of XGBoost/LightGBM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reformulates TabNet's sequential attention masks as latent variables in a conditional VAE, sampling them from a Gumbel-Softmax distribution to enable the reparameterization trick. The key methodological departure from TabNet is replacing its entropy-based sparsity regularizer with a KL divergence regularizer that explicitly maximizes distributional diversity between adjacent masks, drawing on variational inference tools (Kingma & Welling; Jang et al.). The post-hoc GPT-4 prompting pipeline for generating natural-language interpretations of the learned masks is a novel combination of LLM-based explanation with intrinsic model interpretability, building on the broader trend of using language models as domain-knowledge priors (Choi et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: TabNet's dense, overlapping attention masks are difficult for practitioners to translate into actionable feature-level insights, and post-hoc methods like SHAP distribute importance too diffusely across features. The KL-divergence sparsity regularizer is a clean, minimal architectural change that yields visually interpretable masks, and the GPT-4 layer adds a natural-language bridge for non-technical stakeholders. However, the accuracy cost is non-trivial on several datasets (e.g., Mushroom drops from 99.94% to 96.62%, Blastchar from 76.22% to 72.96%), the r_M hyperparameter spans an enormous range (9 to 10^12) requiring a dedicated search algorithm, and the human evaluations are limited to 20 respondents. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to very high-dimensional or continuous-feature settings, whether the LLM interpretation adds genuine analytical value beyond the sparse masks themselves, and whether the KL-divergence formulation can be extended to other sequential-attention architectures beyond TabNet.
