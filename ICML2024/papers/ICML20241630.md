## Problem

Conventional knowledge distillation aligns student and teacher predictive distributions via logit matching, but the capacity gap between models makes this alignment sub-optimal and can even destabilize student training. Existing remedies (e.g., teacher assistants, student-friendly teachers) require complex or student-specific training schemes that hurt practicality. The paper asks whether the student's weaker capacity can instead be compensated by equipping it with a stronger ability to model the predictive distribution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet) alongside a frozen pre-trained teacher model whose intermediate features are cached in a MoCo-style queue of size L≪M for negative/positive sampling. At inference, the student produces a class label via a nonparametric Bayes rule—argmax over cosine similarity between the student feature and learned class prototypes—rather than a parametric softmax layer, and the feature representation itself is the primary transferable artifact for downstream tasks like linear probing and object detection.

## Method

AuxKD introduces instance membership s_i as a label-related auxiliary variable and reformulates the conventional KD objective via Bayes' rule, decomposing p^S(k|x_i) into p^S(s_i|x_i)·p^S(k|x_i,s_i)/p^S(s_i|x_i,k). Each factor is parameterized differently: p^S(s_i|x_i) as a contrastive instance-discrimination ratio over teacher negatives, p^S(x_i|s_i) as a positive-candidate matching ratio over same-class teacher features, and p^S(x_i|k) as a von Mises–Fisher class-conditional density centered on learned prototypes. The resulting single objective (Eqn. 10) unifies logit-level and feature-level distillation: Theorem 3.5 shows the first term is an upper bound on the MINE mutual-information estimator between student and teacher features, while Theorem 3.6 shows the second term is an ELBO on the marginal log-likelihood that shapes the student feature space toward a vMF mixture. The parametric softmax is replaced by a prototype-based classifier, and the gradient structure (Appendix D) induces implicit hard positive/negative mining, so hard examples automatically dominate optimization without explicit mining heuristics.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with homogeneous architectures, AuxKD achieves 77.50% Top-1 accuracy (WRN-40-2→WRN-16-2), 73.21% (ResNet56→ResNet20), and 77.47% (ResNet32x4→ResNet8x4), consistently outperforming all logit-based, feature-based, and combined baselines. On ImageNet-1K, AuxKD reaches 73.04% Top-1 (ResNet34→ResNet18) and 73.90% Top-1 (ResNet50→MobileNetV1), surpassing the prior best DiffKD by 0.82% and 0.28% respectively. For transfer learning, linear probing yields 74.4% on STL-10 and 39.0% on Tiny-ImageNet, while fine-tuned object detection achieves 56.1 AP on PASCAL-VOC and 41.9 AP on MS-COCO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AuxKD outperforms all 19 baselines across every student-teacher pair on CIFAR-100. Against the strongest logit-based method MLD, AuxKD gains +0.87% (WRN-40-2→WRN-16-2: 77.50 vs 76.63) and +1.02% (ResNet56→ResNet20: 73.21 vs 72.19). Against the best feature-based method ReviewKD, the margin is +1.38% (77.50 vs 76.12) on WRN-40-2→WRN-16-2. Against the strongest combined method SSKD, AuxKD leads by +1.46% (77.50 vs 76.04) and +1.72% (73.21 vs 71.49) on the respective pairs. On ImageNet-1K, AuxKD beats DiffKD by 0.82% Top-1 (73.04 vs 72.22) for ResNet34→ResNet18 and by 0.28% (73.90 vs 73.62) for ResNet50→MobileNetV1, while also surpassing DIST (72.07/73.24) and MLD (71.90/73.01). In the self-distillation setting on CIFAR-100, AuxKD exceeds IPWD by 1.24% (82.84 vs 81.60) with DenseNet121, and in transfer learning it outperforms SSKD by 2.0% on STL-10 linear probing (74.4 vs 72.4) and by 1.7 AP on PASCAL-VOC detection (56.1 vs 54.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reformulates conventional knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) by introducing instance membership as a latent auxiliary variable, drawing on contrastive clustering ideas (Shen et al., 2021; Tsai et al., 2021) to give the student a stronger distribution-modeling capacity. Rather than following the student-friendly-teacher line (TAKD, DGKD, SFTN) that requires complex training schemes, it addresses the capacity gap from the student side. The parameterization borrows the MoCo-style queue (He et al., 2020) for negative sampling and the vMF distribution (Ming et al., 2022) for class-conditional density, ultimately unifying logit matching and feature matching as special cases of a single probabilistic objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical unification showing that logit matching and feature matching both optimize the same underlying KD objective provides a principled lens for understanding why hybrid methods work, and the removal of the parametric softmax in favor of a vMF-based Bayes rule yields more transferable representations (confirmed by linear probing and detection experiments). The emergent hard positive/negative mining property from the gradient structure is a useful side effect that may inform future contrastive distillation designs. However, the paper explores only one auxiliary variable (instance membership) and one parameterization scheme, leaving open whether other label-related variables or distributional assumptions could yield further gains, and the moderate empirical margins (0.5–1.5%) over strong baselines suggest the practical ceiling of this particular formulation.
