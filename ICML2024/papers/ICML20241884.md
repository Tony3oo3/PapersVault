## Problem

While large transformer-based generalist agents trained via behavioral cloning (BC) have shown impressive multi-task performance, they require high-quality expert demonstration data that is scarce and expensive in robotics. It remained unclear whether offline actor-critic RL methods—which can learn from sub-optimal or self-generated data—could scale to large transformer models, and whether they would follow the same power-law scaling relationships observed in supervised learning. This paper provides the first systematic recipe and empirical evidence that offline actor-critic RL scales to ~1B-parameter models and outperforms BC baselines, particularly on low-quality data.

## Input / Output

The model consumes up to 2,634 input tokens per timestep: 223 proprioceptive values (each expanded to 8 tokens via a multi-scale normalizer), 8 RGB images at 80×80 pixels (each encoded to 100 spatial tokens via a ResNet), up to 50 SentencePiece text tokens for task instructions, and up to 3 goal images. Outputs are 38-dimensional continuous actions discretized into 101 categorical bins per dimension, plus a distributional Q-value estimate over 101 bins. The Perceiver bottleneck compresses all inputs into 32 latent vectors, enabling the 988M-parameter model to run at 20 Hz on a single RTX 3090 for real-robot control.

## Method

PAC combines a KL-regularized offline actor-critic objective with a Perceiver-IO architecture. The algorithm optimizes a policy that improves over a target policy via exp(Q/η)·π̃ weighting, with a BC regularization term (controlled by α) that stabilizes training and enables smooth interpolation from pure BC (α=1) to pure RL (α=0). The Q-function is learned distributionally (categorical over binned values) using a distributional TD target, and the action is encoded as a cross-attention query at the decoding stage rather than as an input, allowing the action-independent latent z_M to be cached for efficient multi-action Q-value evaluation. The Perceiver backbone uses a single cross-attention layer to compress 2,634 input tokens into 32 learnable latents, followed by M self-attention blocks on the latents (reducing quadratic cost from O(N²) to O(N_Z²)), and finally dedicated cross-attention decoders for policy and Q-value outputs. A novel multi-scale normalizer (tanh with logarithmically spaced gains) encodes continuous proprioceptive and action values to handle arbitrary input scales without saturation.

## Results

On the 32-task Control Suite benchmark, PAC (α=0.75) achieves 87.7% of expert performance, substantially outperforming Gato's 63.6% and the BC+Q baseline's 84.6%; α-PAC (per-dataset α tuning) further boosts this to 92.1%. On the CHEF stacking task with severely sub-optimal data (28% data success rate), PAC reaches 55.0% success while BC+Q manages only 11.0% and FilteredBC only 17.0%. On the real Sawyer robot, α-PAC achieves 69.8% success versus 7.1% for BC+Q, and iterative RL fine-tuning with self-generated data pushes this to 93.2%. Scaling analysis shows the RL objective benefits more from additional parameters (scaling exponent a=0.920) than pure BC (a=0.828), with data scaling exponents nearly identical (b≈0.266 for both).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAC decisively outperforms Gato (Reed et al., 2022) on the 32-task Control Suite benchmark, reaching 87.7% of expert performance versus Gato's 63.6%, with α-PAC further improving to 92.1%. Against RoboCat (Bousmalis et al., 2023), PAC wins on the Tower task (69.3% vs 61.0%) and Insertion task (80.3% vs 71.3%, with α-PAC reaching 89.3%), while roughly matching on Pyramid (63.5% vs 64.5%) where data quality is already near-expert (>75%). The most dramatic comparison is on the CHEF stacking task with only 28% data success rate: PAC achieves 55.0% success while BC+Q manages just 11.0% and FilteredBC only 17.0%, demonstrating that offline RL can extract substantially more from sub-optimal data than behavioral cloning. In the real-robot setting (Table 2), α-PAC achieves 69.8% success versus 7.1% for BC+Q, and iterative RL fine-tuning with self-generated data reaches 93.2% mastery. The Q-Transformer baseline (Chebotar et al., 2023), compared in the appendix, does not outperform BC in aggregate and fails on higher-dimensional domains, whereas PAC consistently improves over BC across all tested scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Perceiver-IO architecture (Jaegle et al., 2021, 2022) to the actor-critic setting, introducing a key modification where the action is encoded as a cross-attention query for Q-value decoding rather than as an input token. The training objective is a simplified variant of MPO/DIME (Abdolmaleki et al., 2018, 2022) combined with distributional RL (Bellemare et al., 2017) and BC regularization (Fujimoto & Gu, 2021), all unified under a single KL-based loss. While large generalist agents (Gato, RoboCat, RT-1/2, Octo) rely exclusively on behavioral cloning, and prior offline RL work (Q-Transformer, CQL) operates on much smaller models, this paper is the first to provide a complete recipe for scaling offline actor-critic RL to ~1B parameter transformers and to demonstrate that it follows Chinchilla-style scaling laws.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that offline RL follows power-law scaling relations analogous to supervised learning is a foundational result that gives practitioners a principled framework for allocating compute to RL training. The practical finding that a simple BC-to-RL interpolation (via α) enables safe, stable training of large models before transitioning to full RL optimization removes a major barrier to deploying RL in expensive multi-task settings. Key limitations include the requirement for reward annotations (which are costly in robotics) and the absence of strong cross-task transfer, suggesting that dataset composition and overlap will be critical for future scaling. Open directions include extending to multi-billion parameter models, integrating with pretrained vision-language models, and developing universal or unsupervised reward functions to reduce annotation burden.
