## Problem

Existing replay-based continual learning methods either apply uniform weights to all previous task gradients (experience replay) or only weight gradients that conflict with the current task (gradient alignment), failing to model the intricate interdependencies among previously learned tasks. This limited flexibility prevents the method from capturing the optimally integrated knowledge across past tasks, leading to sub-optimal performance and insufficient mitigation of catastrophic forgetting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242210/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each streaming step, the method consumes a batch of RGB images from the current task (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet) and samples drawn from a fixed-size memory buffer (0.6k–5k examples) that stores examples from all previously seen tasks. It outputs updated network parameters (Reduced ResNet-18 or a 3-layer CNN) and class predictions. The setting is online class-incremental: each task is trained for exactly one epoch, no task identity is available at inference, and the buffer is updated with current-task samples after each step.

## Method

POCL reformulates all replay-based CL methods as a hierarchical gradient aggregation framework u = g_n + Σ λ_i g_i^m, then replaces the fixed or conflict-based weighting with Pareto optimization: the weights λ* are found by minimizing ½‖Σ λ_i g_i^m‖² over the simplex, which maximizes the minimum inner product between the aggregated previous-task gradient and every individual task gradient (solved via Frank-Wolfe). To stabilize the optimization and reduce conflicts between the current-task gradient and the Pareto-optimized previous-task gradient, each gradient g_i^m and g_n is replaced by a hyper-gradient g_j^HD computed via a bi-level (Reptile-style) update that aligns each task's gradient with the overall buffer gradient. The final update direction is u* = g_n^HD + Σ λ_i* g_i^HD, ensuring the step benefits both the current and all previously learned tasks simultaneously.

## Results

On Split CIFAR-10 (N=5, |M|=1k), POCL achieves 66.23 AAA and 58.50 Acc, outperforming the strongest baselines CBA (65.47/52.08) and CLSER (63.27/53.06). On the harder Split CIFAR-100 (N=20, |M|=5k), POCL reaches 36.34 AAA and 33.36 Acc, substantially ahead of ER-ACE (24.93/20.58) and CLSER (23.25/16.42). On Split TinyImageNet (N=20, |M|=5k), POCL attains 25.48 AAA and 19.40 Acc, surpassing ER-ACE (21.16/17.22) and CBA (18.98/10.98). POCL also achieves the best Forgetting Measure across all datasets, e.g., −16.85 on Split CIFAR-10 vs. −27.31 for CBA and −31.38 for CLSER.

## Baselines & Comparisons

POCL consistently outperforms all 10 baselines across all three datasets and both AAA and Acc metrics. On Split CIFAR-10 (|M|=1k), POCL leads CBA by 6.42 Acc points (58.50 vs. 52.08) and CLSER by 5.44 Acc points (58.50 vs. 53.06), while also surpassing DER++ (50.31) and ER-ACE (51.17). The advantage is most dramatic on Split CIFAR-100 (|M|=5k), where POCL achieves 33.36 Acc versus 20.58 for ER-ACE (the next best), a 12.78-point gap, and 16.42 for CLSER. On Split TinyImageNet (|M|=5k), POCL reaches 19.40 Acc, beating ER-ACE (17.22) by 2.18 points and CBA (10.98) by 8.42 points. In terms of Forgetting Measure, POCL achieves −16.85 on Split CIFAR-10, −17.55 on Split CIFAR-100 (|M|=5k), and −20.22 on Split TinyImageNet (|M|=5k), all the best values, indicating substantially less forgetting than any baseline. Gradient alignment methods (GEM, AGEM, MER, La-MAML) and regularization (EWC) are consistently far behind, confirming that the Pareto-optimized gradient weighting with hyper-gradients provides a meaningful improvement over both simple replay and prior alignment strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the two major families of replay-based continual learning methods—experience replay (ER, DER++, CLSER, CBA) and gradient alignment (GEM, AGEM, MER, La-MAML)—under a single 'hierarchical gradient aggregation' framework, showing that all reduce to assigning weights λ_i to previous-task gradients. It then departs from prior work by introducing Pareto optimization (borrowed from multi-task learning, Lin et al. 2019; Momma et al. 2022) to jointly optimize these weights so that all previous tasks benefit simultaneously, rather than merely avoiding conflict with the current task. The hyper-gradient bi-level formulation from MER/La-MAML is repurposed to stabilize the Pareto-optimized direction, making the overall approach a synthesis of gradient alignment, meta-learning, and multi-objective optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification framework gives the CL community a cleaner lens for comparing and extending replay-based methods, and the Pareto formulation addresses a genuine gap: existing methods treat previous tasks as independent constraints relative to the current task, ignoring inter-task dependencies that can be exploited for collective improvement. However, the evaluation is limited to relatively small-scale benchmarks (CIFAR-10/100, TinyImageNet) with a single primary backbone (Reduced ResNet-18), and the Frank-Wolfe solver adds per-step overhead that may become prohibitive with many tasks. Open questions include whether the Pareto weighting strategy transfers to task-free or domain-incremental settings, how it interacts with parameter-isolation or regularization approaches, and whether the gains persist at ImageNet scale or with larger memory budgets.
