## Problem

Enforcing geometric constraints such as convexity, star-shape, path-connectedness, symmetry, or periodicity on pixel-wise segmentation masks leads to constraints that grow quadratically in the number of pixels and are typically NP-hard to optimize. This paper shows that implicit (coordinate-based) neural representations can provably satisfy these constraints by construction, simply through appropriate architecture choices, making them tractable to include in both variational and learning-based segmentation pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241783/cropped_page2_idx7.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes spatial coordinates (x, y) or spatio-temporal coordinates (x, y, t) as input to the implicit representation, rather than operating on the pixel grid directly; the RGB image, scribbles, or semantic features serve as supervision signals for training. The output is a continuous scalar function whose sub-level set (e.g., {x | G(x) ≤ 0.5}) defines the segmented region, which can be evaluated at arbitrary resolution or at intermediate time steps for video, enabling super-resolved tracking of object centroids. For the path-connected constraint, the implicit representation comprises ~39K parameters per image versus ~3M values for a 1000×1000 RGB image, making the storage overhead negligible.

## Method

The core mechanism is to parameterize the segmentation as a coordinate-to-scalar neural network whose architecture provably guarantees a geometric property: input convex neural networks (ICNNs) with non-negative inter-layer weights ensure convexity; a polar-coordinate decomposition with radial convexity and a negative output at the origin ensures star-shape; applying periodic or sign-flip transformations to the angular component enforces rotational or mirror symmetry; and composing a Real NVP diffeomorphism with an ICNN yields path-connected regions of genus zero (Proposition 2.3). For periodicity, a superposition of learnable sine waves is used. Training proceeds either sequentially (fitting the constrained implicit representation to a pre-trained network's unaries via stochastic optimization) or jointly (adding a distance-to-constrained-set penalty β·dist(N_θ(f), F_ν) to the standard segmentation loss, Eq. 12), with the joint approach consistently outperforming the sequential one in the experiments.

## Results

On the convexity dataset (51 scribble-based images), joint training with an FCN and RGB+semantic features achieves the best IoU of 0.894 for the convex projection (vs. 0.635 for the unconstrained network), while joint CNN+semantic reaches 0.899. Sequential convex fitting to a pre-trained CNN with semantic features yields 0.843 IoU (vs. 0.726 baseline). On the FBMS-59 motion segmentation benchmark (18 sequences), the path-connected prior provides a ~1% IoU gain (sequential) and ~2% IoU gain (joint) over the multicut+UNet baseline. Under image corruptions, the convex projection of SAM unaries consistently improves IoU (e.g., 0.5504 vs. 0.5330 under impulse noise, 0.6357 vs. 0.6246 under Gaussian noise).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Dröge & Moeller (2021) CNN/FCN baselines, the convex projection consistently improves IoU, with the largest gains (up to +26 points) occurring when the network lacks spatial input information, where the unconstrained FCN produces highly scattered segmentations. Compared to SAM (Kirillov et al., 2023), the convex projection of SAM's unaries yields small but systematic IoU improvements under all tested corruptions (e.g., +1.7 points under impulse noise, +1.1 under Gaussian noise) and resolves occlusion failures where SAM splits a single object. Against the Kardoost & Keuper (2021) multicut+UNet motion segmentation pipeline, the path-connected prior adds ~1% IoU (sequential) or ~2% IoU (joint) on average across 18 FBMS-59 sequences, with the largest gains on car sequences (e.g., cars3: 0.779→0.880 sequential, 0.885→0.887 joint). In a direct qualitative comparison with Chen et al. (2021)'s elastica-geodesic convexity prior, the paper's method produces provably convex segmentations while Chen et al.'s approach yields visible non-convex violations, motivating the exclusion of that method from quantitative comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the implicit neural representation paradigm (Sitzmann et al., 2020; Tancik et al., 2020) — previously used for image synthesis and scene representation — as a segmentation tool where architectural choices provably enforce geometric constraints. It builds directly on Input Convex Neural Networks (Amos et al., 2017) for convexity and star-shape, and on Real NVP normalizing flows (Dinh et al., 2017) as the diffeomorphism component for path-connectedness. The key conceptual departure from prior constrained-segmentation work (graph cuts, level-set topology preservation, orientation-based lifting) is that constraints are no longer added as complex regularizers or NP-hard combinatorial conditions on a discrete grid, but are instead baked into the continuous function's architecture, making them automatically satisfied by construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first provably correct, architecture-level enforcement of multiple geometric constraints (convexity, star-shape, path-connectedness, symmetry, periodicity) within a single unified implicit-representation framework, which is attractive for applications with scarce data or heavy occlusions where data-driven models fail. However, empirical gains are modest (1–2% IoU for path-connectedness, larger but setting-dependent for convexity), and the approach is limited to a single object per image, requires careful weight-decay tuning to avoid degenerate thin connections, and can underperform when ground truth annotations themselves violate the assumed constraint. Open questions include scaling to multi-object scenes, integration with foundation models like SAM at scale, and whether the discrete-evaluation gap (continuous path-connectedness vs. pixel-grid visualization) can be closed without sacrificing the provable guarantee.
