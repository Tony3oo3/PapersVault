## Problem

Traditional face obfuscation methods (pixelation, blurring) are no longer secure because blind face restoration (BFR) models can recover high-quality, identity-preserving faces from degraded images. Existing adversarial defenses against face recognition do not transfer well across different BFR architectures, leaving a gap in practical, model-agnostic privacy protection for obfuscated faces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242507/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-quality RGB face image (512×512 from FFHQ) and a degradation transformation T (e.g., pixelation via 32×32 nearest-neighbor downscale-then-upscale), producing an adversarially obfuscated face image x_a = T(z_a) that remains visually consistent with the original obfuscated image x = T(z). The perturbation is constrained by perceptual distance in AlexNet feature space (max ε = 1.5), and the final output is a pixelated face image that, when fed to any BFR model, yields a structurally disordered and identity-ambiguous restoration.

## Method

The core insight is that all BFR models, regardless of architecture, approximate an inverse mapping of the degradation transformation T, so they share a common functional property. DomCo exploits this by optimizing the perturbation in the high-quality domain Z (i.e., finding z_a such that x_a = T(z_a)) rather than directly in the obfuscated image space, which projects gradients back into the domain D_T and neutralizes model-specific non-robust gradient components. Optimization uses Perceptual PGD with a face-structure loss based on a face parsing model to explicitly disrupt facial geometry. To further enhance transferability, a domain-consistent data augmentation strategy adds noise to HQ images before applying T during surrogate model training, making the surrogate more robust to neighborhood perturbations and stabilizing the gradient transformation across models.

## Results

Using RestoreFormer as the surrogate model, DomCo achieves FID scores of 74–165 and IDS of 0.23–0.35 across five BFR attackers, substantially outperforming PPGD (FID 7–179, IDS 0.19–0.55) in transferability. The most striking gap appears against CodeFormer, where PPGD's FID collapses to 7.07 (nearly identical to the unprotected pixelated baseline of 6.47) while DomCo maintains FID of 74.35 and IDS of 0.32. Against the white-box RestoreFormer, PPGD slightly edges DomCo (FID 179 vs 143, IDS 0.19 vs 0.23), but DomCo's consistent performance across all black-box models demonstrates its superior generalization. Face structure metrics (mIoU, mPR) confirm DomCo disrupts facial anatomy far more effectively: e.g., mIoU drops to 0.247 against HIFaceGAN vs 0.607 for PPGD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DomCo against two baselines: plain pixelation (no adversarial perturbation) and PPGD (Perceptual PGD, Laidlaw et al. 2020) applied in a domain-inconsistent manner. DomCo decisively outperforms PPGD in transferability: while PPGD achieves strong white-box defense against RestoreFormer (FID 179, IDS 0.19), its performance collapses against CodeFormer (FID 7.07, IDS 0.63 — nearly identical to unprotected pixelation at FID 6.47), whereas DomCo maintains FID 74.35 and IDS 0.32 on the same model. Against HIFaceGAN, DomCo reduces IDS from 0.54 (PPGD) to 0.25 and mIoU from 0.607 to 0.247, indicating far more effective identity and structure disruption. The only scenario where PPGD slightly outperforms DomCo is the white-box RestoreFormer setting (FID 179 vs 143, IDS 0.19 vs 0.23), which is expected since PPGD overfits to the surrogate. Both methods substantially outperform plain pixelation across all attackers, confirming that adversarial perturbations are necessary to counter BFR models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends adversarial-attack-based face privacy protection (Cherepanova et al., 2020; Shan et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2021b) from defending against face recognition models to defending against blind face restoration (BFR) models, which can undo common obfuscation like pixelation. The optimization framework builds directly on Perceptual PGD (Laidlaw et al., 2020), but the key conceptual departure is computing perturbations in the high-quality domain and projecting them through the degradation operator, ensuring domain consistency. The core insight—that all BFR models approximate an inverse mapping of the same degradation transformation—reframes the transferability problem as one of aligning perturbations with the shared input-output structure rather than with any specific model's parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first study to systematically demonstrate that widely deployed anonymization techniques (pixelation) are effectively nullified by BFR models, and to provide a principled defense. The domain-consistency principle is elegant and potentially generalizable to other image-to-image restoration tasks, as the authors discuss in their appendix. Key limitations include the trade-off between transferability and perturbation perceptibility, evaluation restricted to pixelation as the sole degradation, and the black-box assumption that may not hold if BFR models incorporate adversarial training. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more complex degradations (e.g., heavy blur, occlusion) and whether the face structure loss generalizes beyond the 19-class parsing taxonomy.
