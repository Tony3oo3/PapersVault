## Problem

Plane-wave (PW) basis, a cornerstone of DFT solvers that naturally satisfies periodic boundary conditions, has not been explored within the neural operator paradigm for electron density prediction. While GTO-based neural operators (e.g., InfGCN) capture high-frequency features near atoms, and Fourier neural operators approximate low-frequency structure, neither respects the periodic boundary condition of the molecular lattice. The challenge is that a moderately-sized PW basis alone cannot represent the high-frequency components of electron density near nuclei, yet scaling to DFT-level PW sizes (up to 10^7) is infeasible for neural operators.

## Input / Output

The method takes a molecule represented by atom positions, atomic features (one-hot encoded atomic numbers), and a unit cell lattice L = [a₁, a₂, a₃]ᵀ defining periodicity, along with arbitrary query points in continuous 3D space. The output is the electron density scalar field ρ_pred(x) evaluated at those query points, decomposed as ρ_PW(x) + ρ_GTO(x). Unlike grid-based methods, query points can be placed at any location in continuous space without re-computing the probe-node features, and for aperiodic molecules the framework uses a large zero-padded unit cell to emulate periodicity.

## Method

GPWNO decomposes electron density prediction into two complementary branches: a PW-based component for low-frequency, far-from-atom regions and a GTO-based component for high-frequency, near-atom regions. The PW branch discretizes the signal via probe nodes uniformly placed on the lattice, then applies PW neural operator layers that perform convolution in reciprocal space (FT → frequency-wise linear transform → IFT), followed by message-passing aggregation from nearby probe nodes to query points. The GTO branch uses TFN-based equivariant message passing to learn atom-centered GTO coefficients (spherical harmonics up to degree l=3 or 4, with 16 Gaussian radial bases). A high-frequency masking scheme zeros the PW prediction within a cutoff radius of each atom, forcing the two branches to specialize without adding parameters. The entire architecture is SE(3)-equivariant and satisfies the periodic boundary condition by construction, since the PW basis aligns with the lattice and the GTO branch uses a periodic crystal graph.

## Results

On the aperiodic QM9 dataset, GPWNO achieves 0.73% NMAE, outperforming the second-best InfGCN (0.93%) by ~20% and DeepDFT2 (1.03%) by ~29%. On the MD dataset (six molecules), GPWNO reduces NMAE by approximately 50% relative to InfGCN, e.g., 2.45% vs 5.11% on Benzene and 5.32% vs 10.34% on Malonaldehyde. On the newly curated Materials Project dataset spanning seven crystal families, GPWNO consistently achieves the lowest NMAE (3.92–4.92%) versus InfGCN (4.36–5.21%), with the largest gap on Triclinic (3.92% vs 4.36%) and Cubic (4.32% vs 4.93%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPWNO outperforms all ten baselines on every dataset and crystal family tested. The closest competitor is InfGCN (Cheng & Peng, 2023), the GTO-based equivariant GNN; GPWNO reduces NMAE by ~20% on QM9 (0.73% vs 0.93%) and by ~50% across the six MD molecules (e.g., 2.45% vs 5.11% on Benzene). On the periodic Materials Project benchmark, GPWNO beats InfGCN in all seven crystal families, with the largest margin on Cubic (4.32% vs 4.93%) and Triclinic (3.92% vs 4.36%). The Fourier-based FNO baseline performs poorly (28.83% NMAE on QM9, 20–43% on MD) despite sharing the Fourier-transform mechanism with GPWNO, which the authors attribute to FNO's inability to capture high-frequency density features at practical basis sizes. Graph-based baselines (DimeNet, DimeNet++, EGNN) cluster around 12–19% NMAE on MD, while neural-operator baselines (GNO, LNO) are substantially worse (26–84%), confirming that the GTO+PW decomposition with high-frequency masking is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

GPWNO extends the neural operator paradigm for electron density prediction (Cheng & Peng, 2023; Li et al., 2020a) by introducing the plane-wave basis, which had been absent from prior neural operator approaches to this task. The GTO-based component directly follows the multicentric expansion scheme of InfGCN (Cheng & Peng, 2023), while the PW-based component adapts the Fourier neural operator's convolution-in-reciprocal-space mechanism to respect periodic boundary conditions via lattice-aligned wavevectors. The complementary PW/GTO decomposition is conceptually inspired by established DFT solver techniques—pseudopotentials, linearized augmented plane-wave, and mixed density fitting—that decouple atomic vicinity from interstitial regions, but is here realized within a learnable neural operator framework rather than a fixed numerical scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Electron density prediction is a bottleneck in DFT-based simulations for drug design, battery materials, and solar cell development, and GPWNO's ~50% NMAE reduction over the prior best (InfGCN) on MD datasets and consistent gains across seven crystal families on the newly curated Materials Project benchmark make it a practically relevant advance. The newly released MP benchmark spanning all seven crystal families fills a gap in periodic-systems evaluation. A key open question the authors acknowledge is that NMAE is not fully aligned with downstream applications (e.g., exchange-correlation energy errors), and validating against expensive DFT-based energy metrics remains future work. The finding that PW alone fails catastrophically (NMAE ~48%) while GTO alone is already competitive (NMAE ~5%) underscores that the PW component's value is specifically in capturing low-frequency interstitial structure, not the overall density shape.
