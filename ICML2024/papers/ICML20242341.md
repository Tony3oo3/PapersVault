## Problem

Multi-step look-ahead acquisition functions in Bayesian Optimization require nested Monte Carlo approximations whose computational complexity grows exponentially with the number of look-ahead steps, limiting their practical use. Standard nested MC achieves only sub-canonical convergence rates (O(ε^{-4}) for one nested operation versus the canonical O(ε^{-2})), making high-accuracy approximation prohibitively expensive. This paper is the first to apply the multilevel Monte Carlo (MLMC) framework to BO, reducing the complexity of nested look-ahead acquisition functions back to the canonical MC rate.

## Input / Output

The method takes the current GP posterior (mean and covariance functions) at observation set D_n and a target accuracy ε, and produces the next observation point x_{n+1} via a multilevel estimator optimized with L-BFGS and multi-start initialization. The MLMC estimator is a telescoping sum of fine-minus-coarse acquisition function differences across L levels, where the number of outer samples N_l and inner samples M_l at each level are determined by ε through closed-form formulas (Theorem 1). The method can be tuned to either minimize computational cost for a target accuracy or maximize accuracy for a fixed budget, with the gain increasing as the budget or target accuracy grows.

## Method

The core contribution is constructing an MLMC estimator for the nested MC approximation of two- and three-step look-ahead acquisition functions, where the acquisition function involves an outer expectation over GP samples and an inner expectation after a hypothetical observation. The estimator α_ML(x) = Σ_l Δα_{N_l,M_l}(x) uses a telescoping sum where each increment is the difference between a fine (M_l inner samples) and coarse (M_{l-1} inner samples) approximation sharing the same outer samples, reducing total cost from O(ε^{-4}) to O(ε^{-2}·|log ε|³). An antithetic coupling variant splits the M_l inner samples into two batches of M_{l-1} for the coarse estimator, improving the variance decay rate from β=1 to β≈1.5 and achieving the full canonical O(ε^{-2}) complexity. The theoretical analysis establishes convergence rates for both the acquisition function (Theorem 1) and its maximizer (Theorem 2) under standard SAA regularity assumptions, with the optimizer result requiring an additional assumption linking AF increments to optimizer increments that is verified numerically but deferred to future work for a full proof.

## Results

On the four benchmark functions from Lam et al. (2016) with k=15 function evaluations and 40 realizations, the proposed 2-step look-ahead AF (1EI+2EI) computed via MLMC achieves the best GAP (higher is better, 1 = optimum found) in 7 out of 8 mean/median cases: Branin-Hoo (0.992 mean, 0.997 median), Goldstein-Price (0.987, 0.998), Griewank (0.973 mean), and 6-hump Camel (0.942, 0.979). It is second-best only on Griewank median (0.975 vs. PES 0.987). In the full BO wall-time experiments (Figure 3) across six functions (1D Toy, Ackley, DropWave, Branin, Hartmann6, Cosine8), MLMC achieves the same normalized MSE as standard MC with substantially less computational cost, recovering the canonical O(ε⁻²) complexity rate instead of the sub-canonical O(ε⁻⁴) of nested MC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed MLMC-accelerated 2-step look-ahead AF (1EI+2EI) is compared against five myopic methods (PI, EI, UCB, PES), one non-myopic method (GLASSES), and four rollout-based look-ahead methods (R-4-9, R-4-10, R-5-9, R-5-10) from Lam et al. (2016). The proposed method dominates all baselines on mean GAP for every function: it outperforms the best myopic method (EI on Branin-Hoo at 0.818, PES on Goldstein-Price at 0.819) by 0.17–0.32 GAP points, and outperforms the best rollout method (R-4-9) by 0.09–0.13 GAP points. On median GAP, the proposed method wins 7 of 8 cases; the sole exception is Griewank where PES (0.987) edges out the proposed method (0.975) by 0.012. The rollout methods (R-4-9, R-4-10, R-5-9, R-5-10) consistently underperform the proposed method, confirming that the MLMC acceleration enables a more accurate look-ahead approximation within the same evaluation budget. In the wall-time BO experiments (Figure 3), MLMC achieves the same NMSE as standard MC at roughly half the computational cost across all six test functions, validating the theoretical complexity improvement from O(ε⁻⁴) to O(ε⁻²).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to apply the Multilevel Monte Carlo (MLMC) framework to Bayesian Optimization, specifically to accelerate multi-step look-ahead acquisition functions that involve nested expectations and maximizations. It builds directly on the foundational MLMC theory for nested expectations (Giles, 2015) and the Sample Average Approximation (SAA) framework from BoTorch (Balandat et al., 2020), which enables deterministic optimization of the acquisition function. Prior MLMC applications to nested MC problems (Giles & Haji-Ali, 2019; Giles & Goda, 2019; Hironaka et al., 2020) addressed risk estimation and expected value of sample information, but none addressed the specific structure of look-ahead BO where the integrand contains nested argmax operations. The paper also positions itself relative to the broader look-ahead BO literature (Lam et al., 2016; Wu & Frazier, 2019; Jiang et al., 2020a,b), which has traditionally relied on plain MC or very rough approximations for more than two steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is reducing the computational complexity of approximating two-step look-ahead acquisition functions from O(ε^{-4}) (standard nested MC) to O(ε^{-2}) (canonical MC rate), with the antithetic coupling variant achieving this without logarithmic penalties. This makes look-ahead BO substantially more tractable within a fixed computational budget, enabling higher-accuracy acquisition function maximization that directly improves the outer BO trajectory. However, several open questions remain: the main theorem (Theorem 2) relies on an unproven Assumption 3 linking AF increments to optimizer increments, the theory covers only two- and three-step look-ahead, and the authors acknowledge that MLMC may fail to achieve the canonical rate for deeper nesting due to curse-of-dimensionality effects, pointing toward multi-index MC as a necessary extension. The practical tuning of level counts and sample allocations remains heuristic, and the worst case degenerates to standard MC.
