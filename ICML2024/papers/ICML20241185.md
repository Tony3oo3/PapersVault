## Problem

Existing doubly robust (DR) estimators for debiased collaborative filtering are unbiased only when pseudo-labelings are exactly equal to true labels or when learned propensities are exactly correct. In practice, pseudo-labelings inevitably carry structured biases (user-specific, item-specific, or both), and DR estimators become severely biased when propensities are even slightly inaccurate. The paper asks whether the unbiasedness condition on pseudo-labelings can be relaxed to tolerate arbitrary user- or item-specific inductive biases without sacrificing the double-robustness guarantee.

## Input / Output

The method consumes user-item features, observed binary ratings, and a base propensity model (trained via standard binary classification on observation indicators) as input. It outputs debiased rating predictions and a reconstructed propensity model that satisfies per-user and per-item balancing constraints. Notably, the reconstruction operates on inverse propensities (1/p̃) rather than propensities themselves, which makes the optimization convex and aligns with the theoretical structure where DR bias scales with 1/p and variance with 1/p².

## Method

The paper proposes three new DR estimators (User-DR, Item-DR, User-Item-DR) that remain unbiased when pseudo-labelings deviate from true labels by an arbitrary user-specific function f(b_u), item-specific function g(b_i), or both, rather than requiring exact equality. This is achieved by imposing per-user and per-item constraints on the propensity model: the weighted sum of inverse-propensity residuals times the prediction-error difference must vanish for each user (or item), which algebraically cancels the structured bias term in the estimator's bias decomposition. To bound the variance increase from modifying propensities, a propensity reconstruction step finds the nearest constrained propensity to the original one via a convex optimization in 1/p̃, with constraint weights adaptively learned through an attention mechanism over user and item latent vectors. Training alternates among propensity reconstruction, pseudo-labeling model updates, and prediction model updates, with a trade-off hyperparameter γ balancing reconstruction fidelity against constraint satisfaction.

## Results

On the three real-world datasets, the proposed User-Item-DR (UIDR) achieves AUC 0.702 / N@5 0.799 / F1@5 0.332 on COAT, AUC 0.713 / N@5 0.752 / F1@5 0.382 on MUSIC, and AUC 0.804 / N@50 0.804 / F1@50 0.610 on KuaiRec, significantly outperforming all baselines (p < 0.05). In the semi-synthetic ML-100K experiments, UIDR reduces the relative error of loss estimation to as low as 0.002–0.010 under combined user-item inductive bias, compared to 0.015–0.109 for the standard DR estimator. The ablation on KuaiRec confirms that the full UDR method (AUC 0.802) improves over DR-JL (AUC 0.759) by +4.3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three proposed estimators (UDR, IDR, UIDR) significantly outperform every baseline on all three real-world datasets with p < 0.05. The strongest baselines—ESCM²-DR, MRDR-JL, and TDR—achieve AUC around 0.685–0.690 on MUSIC and 0.775–0.779 on KuaiRec, while UIDR reaches 0.713 and 0.804 respectively, a gap of roughly 2–3 AUC points. In the semi-synthetic ML-100K experiments, the advantage is most dramatic under combined user-item inductive bias: UIDR achieves relative errors of 0.002–0.012 versus 0.015–0.109 for DR and 0.031–0.052 for IPS/SNIPS, confirming the theoretical robustness to arbitrary user- and item-specific biases. IPS and SNIPS remain competitive when inductive bias is large (e.g., FIVE setting) because they do not rely on pseudo-labelings, but they are consistently outperformed by UIDR across all bias types. The ablation (Table 4) shows that removing either the reconstruction loss or the constraint loss degrades KuaiRec AUC from 0.802 to 0.754 or 0.787, respectively, confirming both components are necessary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the doubly robust (DR) estimation framework for debiased collaborative filtering, building directly on the DR-JL estimator of Wang et al. (ICML 2019) and its many variants (MRDR, ESCM²-DR, Stable-DR, etc.). The key departure is a theoretical relaxation: while all prior DR methods require pseudo-labelings to be exactly equal to true labels for unbiasedness, the proposed User-DR, Item-DR, and User-Item-DR estimators only require pseudo-labelings to deviate by an arbitrary user-specific, item-specific, or combined inductive bias. The propensity reconstruction learning approach draws on the covariate balancing propensity score literature (Imai & Ratkovic, 2014) to justify the constraint structure, and introduces an attention mechanism to adaptively weight per-user and per-item constraints, reducing the dual problem's parameter count.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized practical weakness: DR methods, despite their double robustness guarantee, are highly sensitive to even small pseudo-labeling errors when propensities are slightly misspecified. By showing that structured (user/item-specific) imputation biases are tolerable, the work provides a more realistic and actionable robustness guarantee for practitioners deploying debiased recommenders. The main limitation acknowledged is that the alternating training procedure cannot guarantee all constraints hold strictly due to computational burden, leaving a gap between theory and practice. Open questions include whether the user/item-specific bias structure captures the dominant real-world imputation errors, and whether the approach extends naturally to more complex bias structures (e.g., interaction-level biases).
