## Problem

In learning from aggregate responses (LAR), the learner only observes mean responses per bag rather than individual labels, raising the question of how to curate bags to maximize model quality under a minimum bag-size constraint k. Prior work has largely relied on random bagging, which introduces a non-vanishing bias in the estimator. This paper asks: what bagging configuration minimizes estimation error, and can the learner adaptively improve bag quality over multiple rounds using only aggregate feedback?

## Input / Output

The learner receives n unlabeled feature vectors X ∈ R^{n×d} partitioned into m non-overlapping bags of size at least k, along with the mean response of each bag (never individual labels). For label differential privacy, Laplace noise with scale B/(εk) is added to each aggregate response before the learner sees it. The output is a model parameter vector θ (for linear or GLM families) that predicts individual responses on future data; the minimum bag size k simultaneously enforces k-anonymity and controls the noise scale in the DP mechanism.

## Method

The core insight is that minimizing the event-level estimation error over bag configurations reduces to a one-dimensional size-constrained k-means clustering problem on prior-predicted responses ỹᵢ = E[yᵢ|xᵢ], with a sorting structure (Lemma 2.3) that enables an O(nk + n log n) dynamic-programming solver. PriorBoost is an adaptive T-stage algorithm: the first stage uses random bagging to obtain an initial model, and each subsequent stage uses the previous model's predictions to sort the next data slice and solve the constrained k-means problem, forming increasingly homogeneous bags. Theoretically, the paper proves that curated bags drive the estimator bias to zero asymptotically while random bags retain a (1−1/k)²‖θ*‖² bias floor, and extends the reduction to GLMs (logistic, Poisson) where the variance term can be dropped under mild conditions on m/d.

## Results

On synthetic linear regression (n=2^20, d=8, σ=0.1) and logistic regression (λ=10), PriorBoost converges to the optimal k=1 model quality for all bag sizes k∈{1,2,4,8,16,32,64}, while OneShot (random bagging) steadily degrades as k increases. Under ε-label differential privacy with Laplace noise, PriorBoost with k=64 nearly achieves its non-private test log loss for ε≥0.3, whereas OneShot's loss worsens with larger k. Theoretically, the estimation bias under random bags is lower bounded by (1−1/k)²‖θ*‖² (nonvanishing), while under PriorBoost's size-constrained k-means bags the bias vanishes asymptotically as n→∞.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PriorBoost against two baselines: OneShot (non-adaptive random bagging on all data) and PBPrefix (adaptive but trains on all accumulated data). PriorBoost decisively outperforms both on test MSE (linear regression) and test log loss (logistic regression): it converges to the k=1 optimal loss for every bag size, while OneShot's loss monotonically worsens as k grows and PBPrefix converges more slowly and to suboptimal solutions (particularly for k≥16 in logistic regression). In the differential privacy setting, PriorBoost uniquely benefits from larger bag sizes k because it simultaneously reduces Laplace noise scale by a factor of k, maintains low bias through homogeneous bag construction, and averages over more samples; OneShot, by contrast, suffers increasing bias that outweighs its variance reduction. The theoretical separation (Theorems 4.4 vs. 4.5) confirms that PriorBoost's asymptotic risk converges to zero while random bagging retains a nonvanishing bias of order (1−1/k)²‖θ*‖².

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learning-from-aggregate-responses (LAR) framework, which has been studied since the 1990s in group testing and label-proportion settings, by focusing specifically on the bag curation problem. While Chen et al. (2023) also study curated bags, they group samples by common features; here the authors instead cluster by predicted response values, reducing the optimal bagging problem to a one-dimensional size-constrained k-means clustering solved via dynamic programming (building on Wang & Song, 2011). The key departure from prior LAR work is the adaptive, multi-stage PriorBoost algorithm that bootstraps its own prior from aggregate data, eliminating the need for a public model, and the explicit connection to label differential privacy via the Laplace mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The provable separation between curated and random bags (vanishing vs. non-vanishing bias) provides a clear theoretical justification for adaptive bagging in privacy-preserving ML pipelines, and the counterintuitive DP tradeoff—where larger bag sizes help PriorBoost but hurt random bagging—gives practitioners concrete guidance for choosing k under a privacy budget. Limitations include the restriction to linear and GLM models, synthetic experiments with small feature dimensions (d ≤ 64), and the assumption of a single oracle round per sample. Open questions include whether the 1D k-means reduction and adaptive boosting extend to deep or nonlinear models, and how the method performs on real-world aggregate data with heterogeneous bag sizes.
