## Problem

Existing sliced Wasserstein variants face a fundamental trade-off: optimization-based methods (DSW, Max-SW) are discriminative but require expensive non-convex optimization, while energy-based methods (EBSW) are optimization-free but necessitate biased sampling via MCMC or importance weighting. The paper addresses the absence of a slicing distribution that is simultaneously discriminative, continuous, optimization-free, and amenable to fast i.i.d. parallel sampling.

## Input / Output

The method takes two probability measures (empirical distributions over data points in gradient flow experiments, or forward and reverse transition distributions in diffusion model training) and produces a sliced Wasserstein distance value. In the diffusion model setting, the input is a noisy image at a given time step and the output is a generated image. A key practical property is that sampling a projecting direction requires only one draw from each input measure plus one draw from a vMF/PS distribution, making the procedure trivially parallelizable across all L projections without any iterative or sequential dependency.

## Method

The core contribution is the random-path projecting direction (RPD): sample X~μ and Y~ν, form the normalized difference (X−Y)/‖X−Y‖₂, then draw the projecting direction from a von Mises-Fisher or Power Spherical distribution centered at that normalized vector. This yields the random-path slicing distribution (RPSD), which is guaranteed continuous (via the perturbation) and discriminative (since the random path captures directions where the two measures differ). From RPSD, the authors derive RPSW (replacing the uniform distribution in SW with RPSD) and IWRPSW (importance-weighting L RPDs by their projected distances through an increasing energy function, analogous to EBSW but with unbiased Monte Carlo estimation). For diffusion model training, they introduce the Augmented Generalized Mini-batch Energy (AGME) distance, which uses an SW variant as a kernel and augments it with a learned time-conditional non-linear discriminator to overcome the weak training signal of the standard GME distance.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 unconditional generation, IWRPSW-DD achieves the best FID of 2.70 with only 4 NFE and 0.21s generation time, outperforming DDGAN (FID 3.64) by 0.94 FID at the same speed. RPSW-DD achieves FID 2.82, also surpassing DDGAN. In gradient flow experiments on MNIST (digit 1→0), RPSW and IWRPSW reduce Wasserstein-2 distance faster than SW and EBSW while being significantly faster than optimization-based Max-SW and DSW.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among SW variants on CIFAR-10, IWRPSW-DD (FID 2.70) and RPSW-DD (FID 2.82) outperform SW-DD (2.90), Max-SW-DD (2.99), DSW-DD (2.88), and EBSW-DD (2.87), with IWRPSW-DD achieving the best FID by 0.12–0.29 over other SW variants. Compared to DDGAN (the closest same-speed baseline at 4 NFE), both RPSW-DD and IWRPSW-DD reduce FID by 0.82 and 0.94 respectively while maintaining identical generation time (0.21s). In gradient flow on MNIST, RPSW and IWRPSW converge to lower Wasserstein-2 distances faster than SW and EBSW, while Max-SW and DSW are both slower (due to iterative optimization) and achieve worse final distances. The key advantage over Max-SW and DSW is computational: RPSW/IWRPSW require only i.i.d. sampling (O(Ldn) for path construction) versus T gradient ascent iterations, making them 2×+ faster in practice. Against high-NFE diffusion models (DDPM, Score SDE, DDIM, LSGM), the proposed methods trade a small FID increase (2.70 vs. 2.10 for LSGM) for a 37–500× reduction in NFE and generation time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sliced Wasserstein (SW) distance framework by introducing a new slicing distribution — the random-path slicing distribution (RPSD) — constructed from the normalized difference between two random samples drawn from the input measures. It departs from prior discriminative slicing distributions (DSW, Max-SW) that require expensive iterative optimization, and from EBSW which, while optimization-free, necessitates MCMC or importance sampling due to its unnormalized density. The RPD approach achieves discriminativeness through a simple i.i.d. sampling procedure (sample from both measures, normalize the difference, add a vMF/PS perturbation), making it both optimization-free and trivially parallelizable. For the diffusion model application, the paper builds on DDGAN's implicit denoising architecture and replaces the adversarial loss with an augmented generalized mini-batch energy distance using SW variants as kernels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RPSW and IWRPSW variants offer a practical, drop-in replacement for SW that is faster than optimization-based alternatives (DSW, Max-SW) and simpler than MCMC-based EBSW, while achieving better discriminative performance in gradient flow and diffusion model training. A notable open question is the lack of a full triangle inequality proof (only a quasi-triangle inequality is established), which limits the theoretical guarantees compared to standard SW. The authors identify future extensions to spherical and hyperbolic manifolds where random paths become geodesics rather than straight lines. The AGME training framework for diffusion models, while showing modest FID improvements over DDGAN, introduces a new loss formulation that could be explored more broadly.
