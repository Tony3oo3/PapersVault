## Problem

Consistency Models (CMs) achieve state-of-the-art one-step generation quality empirically, yet no formal convergence guarantee existed to explain why they work. This paper provides the first theoretical analysis proving that CMs can generate samples with bounded Wasserstein-2 error to the true data distribution in a single step, under minimal assumptions (L2-accurate score and consistency functions, Lipschitz score, bounded second moment) that do not require log-concavity, log-Sobolev inequalities, or dissipativity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard Gaussian noise vector ξ ~ N(0, I_d) as input and applies the learned consistency model f_θ(·, T) to produce a single data sample in R^d. The theoretical output is a W2 error bound whose terms scale as (√d ∨ m) L_f e^{-T} + T²(ε_cm + L_f ε_sc) + T(ε_cm + L_f ε_sc + L_f L_s^{3/2} d^{1/2} h) + (√d ∨ m)√δ, with each parameter tunable to achieve any target ε. For TV guarantees, an additional forward OU smoothing step (no extra NN evaluations) or O(√d/ε) underdamped Langevin corrector steps are applied to the CM output.

## Method

The analysis decomposes the CM output error into consistency error (f_θ vs. exact backward mapping f^ex), score estimation error (L2 accuracy of s_φ), and ODE discretization error from the exponential integrator, then bounds the accumulated error along the probability flow ODE trajectory using a two-stage discretization schedule (uniform steps from T to h, then geometrically shrinking steps from h to δ) to control the singularity near t=0. For multistep sampling, the paper derives a recursive W2 bound with an exponentially decaying multiplier e^{-t_{n_k}}, proving that additional denoise-renoise steps asymptotically reduce the error below the one-step bound and eliminate the linear T-dependency. TV guarantees are obtained by either applying a forward OU process for δ time (no additional NN evaluations) or running an underdamped Langevin corrector with the score model for O(√d/ε) steps, both building on results from Chen et al. (2023a).

## Results

The main result (Corollary 3.9) shows that one-step CM sampling achieves W₂(f_{θ,T}#N(0,I_d), p_data) ≲ (√d ∨ m)·L_f·e^{-T} + T²(ε_cm + L_f·ε_sc) + T(ε_cm + L_f·ε_sc + L_f·L_s^{3/2}·d^{1/2}·h) + (√d ∨ m)·√δ, where ε_cm and ε_sc are the consistency and score estimation errors. The multistep sampling result (Corollary 3.11) replaces the linear T-dependency with log(L_f), yielding W₂(q_k, p_data) ≲ (log²(L_f) + 2^{-k}T²)(ε_cm + L_f·ε_sc) + (log(L_f) + 2^{-k}T)(ε_cm + L_f·ε_sc + L_f·L_s^{3/2}·d^{1/2}·h) + 2^{-k}(√d ∨ m)L_f·e^{-T} + (√d ∨ m)√δ. The required discretization complexity is N = O(L_f·L_s^{3/2}·d^{1/2}/ε), matching state-of-the-art for ODE-type SGMs. TV error O(ε) is achievable via an OU smoothing step (no extra NN evaluations) or an underdamped Langevin corrector requiring O(√d/ε) additional steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines; comparisons are made at the level of convergence guarantees. The W₂ error bound and discretization complexity N = O(L_f·L_s^{3/2}·d^{1/2}/ε) match the state-of-the-art for ODE-type SGMs established by Chen et al. (2023a) and Chen et al. (2023b), confirming the CM framework inherits the same asymptotic efficiency. Unlike Lee et al. (2022a), the results do not require a log-Sobolev inequality or log-concavity, covering arbitrarily non-log-concave and multimodal data distributions. For bounded-support distributions (including those on lower-dimensional submanifolds), the W₂ convergence matches the methodology of Bortoli (2022), Lee et al. (2022b), and Chen et al. (2022), though with polynomial rather than exponential dependence on the support radius. The key advantage over one-step SGM sampling is that multistep CM sampling reduces the linear T-dependency in the error to log(L_f), requiring only T ≥ max(log(2L_f)+δ, L_s^{-1}) rather than T = O(log(L_f(√d∨R)/ε)), which translates to lower training complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis framework for score-based generative models (SGMs) to Consistency Models (CMs), the one-step generative architecture introduced by Song et al. (2023). It directly builds on the minimal-assumption SGM convergence results of Chen et al. (2022) and Lee et al. (2022b) for Wasserstein bounds, and on Chen et al. (2023a) for the score perturbation lemma and underdamped Langevin corrector used in the TV error analysis. The key departure is that CMs replace the iterative ODE integration with a single learned mapping f_θ, requiring a new consistency error assumption (Assumption 3.4) alongside the standard L²-accurate score assumption, and a Lipschitz-like condition on the consistency model itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical guarantee showing that CMs can match the W₂ convergence rate of SGMs in a single step under the same minimal data assumptions (no log-Sobolev, no log-concavity), which provides the missing mathematical justification for the strong empirical performance of CMs in practice. The multistep sampling analysis offers the first theoretical evidence that additional denoise-inject steps reduce error, supporting the original CM paper's design choice. Key open questions remain: the Lipschitz assumption on f_θ is acknowledged as unrealistic, the TV bound requires an additional smoothing or Langevin correction step, and the paper does not address when the score and consistency functions can actually be learned well enough by neural networks — an end-to-end guarantee would require resolving these gaps.
