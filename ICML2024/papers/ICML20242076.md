## Problem

Existing variable importance measures for categorical responses either rely on parametric model assumptions, are tied to a specific trained ML model, or lack rigorous statistical inference guarantees. This paper introduces the Expected Total Variation (ETV) as a model-free, intuitive VIM for categorical Y and provides the first lower confidence bound for it, filling a gap where no VIM with valid inference existed for general categorical outcomes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242076/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires i.i.d. samples (X_i, Y_i, Z_i) plus the ability to draw from the conditional distribution L(X|Z), which is the key design-based/model-X assumption (naturally satisfied in randomized experiments like conjoint analysis, or estimable from unlabeled (X,Z) data). The classifier f must be trained on a held-out or cross-validated subset to preserve validity, and a buffer parameter c controls how aggressively samples near the decision boundary are discarded. The output is a single scalar lower confidence bound on ETV(X,Y,Z) at user-specified level 1−α, normalized to [0,1].

## Method

The core insight is that ETV equals the optimal binary classification accuracy between the true joint distribution L(X,Y,Z) and the 'independent' product distribution L(Y|Z)×L(X|Z)×L(Z), converting VIM inference into a classification problem. For each data point, J synthetic copies X^(j) are drawn from L(X|Z=Z_i) and labeled as 'resampled' (class 0), while the original (X_i, Y_i, Z_i) is labeled 'original' (class 1); any classifier f on these labeled samples yields a lower bound on the optimal accuracy, which is a rescaled ETV. A CLT-based lower confidence bound on the sample classification accuracy then produces a valid lower confidence bound on ETV (the 'floodgate' structure: a confidence bound on a lower bound). The classifier is motivated by the oracle f*(x,y,z)=I(p(y|x,z)>p(y|z)), approximated in practice by comparing fitted models p_θ1(y|x,z) and p_θ2(y|z) with a buffer c to discard uncertain samples. Cross-validation (Algorithm 2) with k folds ensures every data point contributes to inference while maintaining asymptotic validity under standard conditions.

## Results

On the US General Election conjoint data (n=7,190), the Total Variation Floodgate with HierNet classifiers and 10-fold cross-validation yields an ETV estimate of approximately 0.1 and a lower confidence bound of approximately 0.08, against a case-specific theoretical upper bound of ~0.365 (derived from the ideal distribution in Table 1 with q≈0.27). In synthetic simulations (1536 independent experiments, p=4 or 10, n=100p), the oracle classifier produces the highest lower confidence bound, the logistic model is a close second, and a generic random forest still performs reasonably. For the threshold parameter c, the cross-validation (CV) selection strategy outperforms the naive c=0 choice and closely approaches the oracle c selection across all tested configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not conduct a head-to-head numerical comparison against other VIM methods (e.g., Shapley-value-based VIMs, mMSE, MACM gap). Instead, it compares internal design choices: (1) different classifier families for the labeling function f — oracle, logistic regression, and random forest — where the oracle achieves the tightest lower bound, logistic is a close second, and random forest is competitive but weaker; (2) different strategies for choosing the buffer parameter c in equation (5) — Naive (c=0), CV (10-fold cross-validation), CV_oracle, and true Oracle — where CV outperforms Naive and is quite close to the oracle. The paper establishes a formal equivalence between its Algorithm 1 and the MACM gap inference of Zhang & Janson (2020) for binary Y, and notes the shared construction idea with Naf et al. (2022)'s λ̂_bayes^ρ, but the key distinction is the problem setting (conditional VIM vs. two-sample TV estimation) rather than a direct performance comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Floodgate framework of Zhang & Janson (2020) from non-categorical and binary responses to general categorical Y by introducing the Expected Total Variation (ETV) as a model-free variable importance measure. The core algorithmic idea—converting TV distance inference into a binary classification problem—shares its construction with Naf et al. (2022), who studied lower bounds on TV(P,Q) from two-sample data, but here the 'two samples' are generated via the model-X resampling mechanism rather than observed directly. The cross-validated variant (Algorithm 2) borrows CLT results for cross-validation from Bayle et al. (2020) and Austern & Zhou (2020) to ensure validity without discarding data points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first model-free VIM with rigorous lower confidence bound guarantees for general categorical responses, filling a gap where prior work (Zhang & Janson 2020, Williamson et al. 2021) only covered non-categorical or binary Y. Theorem 2.5 provides a notable impossibility result: no generic upper confidence bound on ETV can avoid covering the theoretical maximum even under ideal conditions, fundamentally limiting what can be certified. A key limitation is the model-X assumption (ability to sample from L(X|Z)), which is natural in randomized experiments like conjoint analysis but restrictive for observational data; relaxing this assumption remains an open problem. The sensitivity analysis connection (B ≥ 1 + 2(1−1/|Y|)·ETV) offers a practical interpretation for low-signal settings like GWAS.
