## Problem

Standard deep RL algorithms (DQN, Rainbow) enforce parameter-space equivalence (θ = w) between online and target networks via hard copies or Polyak updates, which the paper argues is an overkill: the true requirement is only function-space equivalence (v_θ = v_w) to ensure a single value function is learned. This parameter constraint excludes a large set of valid solutions, particularly in overparameterized settings, and the paper formalizes this gap between the solution sets F_pair and F_value.

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB image observations from Atari games (84×84 pixels) paired with discrete actions, producing distributional Q-value estimates over a set of return atoms (C51-style) rather than scalar values. The target network is updated by minimizing cross-entropy between the output distributions of the online and target networks over batches sampled from the replay buffer, rather than by copying or interpolating parameters. Two variants are explored: sampling state-action pairs directly, or sampling states and minimizing over all actions per state (the latter, 'all-action', performs better).

## Method

The Lookahead-Replicate (LR) algorithm replaces the standard target-network update with two alternating gradient-based operations per iteration. The Lookahead step performs K_L gradient descent steps on the target parameter w to minimize the Bellman residual H(θ,w) = ‖v_w − T^π v_θ‖²_D, which is the standard TD bootstrapping objective. The Replicate step performs K_R gradient descent steps on the online parameter θ to minimize the function-space discrepancy G(θ,w) = ‖v_θ − v_w‖²_D (or cross-entropy in the distributional setting), directly enforcing value equivalence without requiring θ = w. The paper proves convergence to a pair (θ*, w*) ∈ F_value under strong convexity and Lipschitz assumptions, and shows empirically that LR-augmented Rainbow outperforms standard Rainbow on all 55 Atari games, with an intermediate K_R (≈0.1·K_L) yielding the best trade-off between the two constraints.

## Results

On the full Atari benchmark (55 games, 5 seeds), LR outperforms Rainbow in both the aggregate learning curve (human-normalized median score vs. frames) and the final asymptotic performance comparison. On a subset of 6 games, both LR variants (one-action and all-action) outperform Rainbow with both frequency-based and Polyak target updates on all but one game. An intermediate value of K_R (the number of Replicate gradient steps) yields the best performance, with K_R = 800 (= 0.1·K_L) used for the full 55-game evaluation. Even under a compute-fair comparison where LR has experienced 9% less environmental data than Rainbow (due to the additional target-network gradient steps), LR still outperforms Rainbow.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Rainbow (Hessel et al., 2018), tested with both its default hard frequency-based target update and a soft Polyak update (τ = 0.005). LR (all-action variant, K_R = 800) outperforms Rainbow on all 55 Atari games in both the learning-curve comparison and the asymptotic score comparison (Figures 6 and 7). On the 6-game subset, LR beats both Rainbow variants on 5 out of 6 games, with the all-action variant consistently outperforming the one-action variant. The Polyak update is found to be roughly as effective as the frequency-based update for Rainbow, suggesting the improvement from LR is not simply due to a better target-update schedule. Notably, in the gradient-step-fair comparison, Rainbow actually sees 9% more data than LR at any given point, yet LR still achieves higher performance, indicating the function-space Replicate update provides a genuine algorithmic advantage beyond mere compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard target-network update paradigm in deep RL (hard frequency-based copying in DQN/Rainbow, or soft Polyak averaging in DDPG/SAC) by arguing that parameter-space equivalence (θ=w) is an overkill constraint. Instead, it reformulates value-function approximation to require only function-space equivalence (v_θ = v_w), and introduces the Lookahead-Replicate (LR) algorithm where the target network is updated via gradient descent on the value-difference loss rather than parameter copying. The Lookahead step is essentially standard TD bootstrapping, while the Replicate step is the novel component; the distributional loss used in experiments follows C51, and the base agent is Rainbow. Theoretically, the convergence analysis builds on recent TD convergence work (Asadi et al., 2023b; Lee & He, 2019) but extends it to the two-parameter, function-space-equivalence setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reformulation from parameter-space to function-space equivalence is a clean conceptual contribution that opens the door to using different hypothesis spaces for online and target networks, a possibility that TD-like algorithms fundamentally cannot exploit. The paper notes that the gap between the solution sets F_pair and F_value grows with the expressiveness of the function approximator, suggesting LR may be more conducive to scaling than existing algorithms, though this remains a conjecture. Open questions include how LR interacts with the deadly triad (bootstrapping + function approximation + off-policy), whether different parameter spaces for the two networks are practically beneficial, and whether the convergence guarantees (which require strong convexity) hold in the overparameterized deep network regime. The empirical gains over Rainbow on Atari are consistent but moderate, and the additional gradient steps for the target network introduce a small computational overhead.
