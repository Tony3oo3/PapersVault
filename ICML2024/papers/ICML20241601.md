## Problem

Traditional machine learning assumes data reside in linear vector spaces, and while geometric deep learning and graph representation learning have matured for pairwise relational data, higher-order topological structure (multi-way interactions, loops, voids across scales) remains largely unexplored in deep learning. This position paper argues that topological deep learning (TDL) is the natural next frontier for relational learning but identifies that the field critically lacks theoretical foundations, standardized benchmarks, scalable software, and compelling real-world applications that would justify practitioner adoption over standard GNNs.

## Input / Output

TDL methods consume higher-order topological domains—cell complexes, simplicial complexes, hypergraphs, cellular sheaves, and combinatorial complexes—where the input structure itself encodes multi-scale topology (connected components, cycles, voids) rather than mere pairwise adjacency. Outputs span class labels, learned embeddings, topological descriptors such as persistent homology barcodes, and generated samples for non-Euclidean domains (3D meshes, molecules, point clouds). A distinguishing feature is that the input topology is not a fixed graph but a flexible combinatorial structure whose cells at different dimensions carry distinct feature vectors and message-passing semantics.

## Method

As a position paper, the core contribution is a structured taxonomy of eleven open problems organized across six dimensions: applications and benchmarks, software infrastructure, complexity and scalability, explainability/generalization/fairness, and theoretical foundations covering TDL advantages, topological representation learning, and transformers for topological domains. The paper articulates four practical advantages of TDL over GRL/GDL: topology-informed architecture selection (e.g., knotted data in R³ cannot be embedded by a single-layer MLP to R²), modeling of multi-way interactions beyond pairwise edges, capture of manifold regularities such as remeshing symmetry, and topological equivariances under homeomorphism groups rather than standard permutation or Euclidean groups. Specific research directions include expressivity proofs via Weisfeiler-Lehman generalizations to cellular and simplicial complexes, sheaf-theoretic bridges connecting category theory with persistent homology, latent topological inference for automated higher-order structure discovery during training, and the development of a unified 'topological transformer' architecture. The paper also proposes concrete infrastructure needs: higher-order benchmark suites with stability metrics beyond predictive accuracy, graph-lifting and rewiring algorithms for synthetic topological data generation, and hardware-friendly TDL implementations to compete with GPU-optimized transformers.

## Results

This is a position paper that does not present new experimental results or quantitative benchmarks. Instead, it identifies 11 open problems and corresponding research directions across six thematic areas (applications/datasets/benchmarks, software, complexity/scalability, explainability/generalization/fairness, and theoretical foundations). The paper cites prior empirical successes of TDL methods — notably victories in the D3R Grand Challenges and SARS-CoV-2 variant forecasting — as motivation but does not report its own metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

No baselines or head-to-head comparisons are presented, as this is a position/survey paper rather than a method paper. The authors do discuss relative advantages of TDL over graph representation learning (GRL) and geometric deep learning (GDL) in qualitative terms — e.g., TDL captures multi-way interactions, remeshing symmetry, and topological equivariances that standard GNNs cannot express — but no quantitative comparison table is provided. The paper explicitly frames its contribution as an invitation to the community to address the identified open problems rather than as a benchmarking study.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper unifies and repositions several previously separate research threads—topological data analysis (persistent homology, Morse-Smale complexes), geometric deep learning (GNNs, equivariant networks), and higher-order signal processing (Hodge Laplacians on simplicial and cell complexes)—under the umbrella of 'topological deep learning' (TDL). It departs from the traditional TDA paradigm, where topology serves as a feature extractor feeding into standard DNNs, by arguing that topological structures should instead define the computational domain on which neural networks operate. The paper builds directly on recent architectural contributions (CW networks, simplicial attention networks, neural sheaf diffusion, combinatorial complex networks) and on the geometric deep learning framework of Bronstein et al., extending it from pairwise graph relations to multi-way topological interactions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies 11 concrete open problems spanning datasets, software, scalability, explainability, fairness, and theoretical foundations, effectively serving as a community roadmap for TDL. A central unresolved question is the cost-benefit trade-off: when does the added complexity of higher-order topological structures actually yield downstream gains over well-tuned GNNs or graph transformers, and can this be characterized theoretically? The scarcity of higher-order benchmarks, the lack of a unified 'topological transformer' architecture, and the absence of formal expressivity comparisons between TDL and GRL mean that TDL's practical superiority remains largely unproven outside a handful of domain-specific applications (D3R challenges, SARS-CoV-2 forecasting). For practitioners, the paper's main value is clarifying where TDL is likely to add value (multi-way interactions, remeshing symmetry, topological equivariances) versus where standard GNNs may suffice.
