## Problem

Fourier neural operators (FNOs) perform global convolutions in Fourier space, making them prone to over-smoothing and unable to capture local details critical for hyperbolic PDEs and differential operators. While CNNs provide local inductive bias, they are locked to a single resolution and require explicit up/downsampling that loses high-frequency content. The paper addresses the gap of incorporating principled local operations—both differential and integral—into neural operators while preserving discretization-agnosticism.

## Input / Output

The method accepts discretized PDE state functions on regular grids (e.g., 128×128 or 256×256) or unstructured meshes (e.g., flow past a cylinder) and produces time-evolved solutions, forcing functions, or velocity/pressure fields. A key property is that the local layers operate at any resolution without explicit resampling: the differential kernels scale their values by 1/h, and the integral kernels maintain a fixed physical receptive field via DISCO convolutions, enabling zero-shot super-resolution evaluation at 2× or 4× the training resolution.

## Method

The paper introduces two local layers added as parallel branches to FNO/SFNO architectures. The differential layer constrains a standard 3×3 convolutional kernel by subtracting its mean and scaling by 1/h, provably converging to a first-order differential operator as the grid width h→0 (analogous to finite-difference stencils). The local integral kernel layer uses DISCO convolutions: the kernel is parameterized as a learnable linear combination of piecewise-linear hat functions with compact support, evaluated continuously at quadrature points so the receptive field remains fixed in physical space regardless of resolution. On the sphere, the group action is restricted to SO(3)/SO(2) to keep outputs on S². Together, these branches provide local inductive bias alongside the global Fourier operations, reducing relative L²-error by 34–72% across five PDE benchmarks including turbulent Navier-Stokes, spherical shallow water, and Darcy flow.

## Results

The proposed local neural operator (FNO/SFNO augmented with differential and/or local integral kernels) reduces relative L²-error by 34–72% over the corresponding (S)FNO baselines across five PDE benchmarks. On the 2D Navier-Stokes (Kolmogorov flow, Re=5000), the best model (FNO + local integral + differential) achieves 9.02×10⁻² relative L²-error after 1 step and 1.96×10⁻¹ after 5 autoregressive steps, versus 1.38×10⁻¹ and 2.36×10⁻¹ for the FNO baseline. On the spherical shallow water equations, SFNO + local integral kernel achieves 2.62×10⁻⁴ (1 step) and 5.39×10⁻⁴ (5 steps), a 72% reduction over SFNO. On the 2D diffusion-reaction equation (PDEBench), the combined model reaches 3.1×10⁻² relative L²-error, a 63% improvement over FNO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the FNO baseline (Li et al., 2021), the proposed method wins on all planar problems: 87% lower relative L²-error on Darcy flow (7.36×10⁻³ vs 5.87×10⁻²), 34% lower on Navier-Stokes (9.02×10⁻² vs 1.38×10⁻¹), and 63% lower on the diffusion-reaction equation (3.1×10⁻² vs 8.3×10⁻²). Against the U-Net baseline (Gupta & Brandstetter, 2022), the proposed method is competitive on Darcy flow (7.36×10⁻³ vs 1.38×10⁻²) but substantially outperforms it on Navier-Stokes (9.02×10⁻² vs 1.67×10⁻¹) and shallow water (2.62×10⁻⁴ vs 1.34×10⁻³), while also being discretization-agnostic unlike the U-Net. Against the SFNO baseline (Bonev et al., 2023), the local integral kernel yields a 72% reduction in 1-step error (2.62×10⁻⁴ vs 9.22×10⁻⁴) and a 5.8× reduction in 5-step error (5.39×10⁻⁴ vs 3.19×10⁻³) on the sphere. Against the PDEBench baselines (Takamoto et al., 2022), the proposed FNO+local kernels improve the FNO result from 8.3×10⁻² to 3.1×10⁻² relative L²-error with fewer parameters. On the unstructured-mesh cylinder flow problem, the proposed FNO+local integral kernel (2.88×10⁻³ MSE) outperforms the best baseline GNN (5.00×10⁻³) by 42% and the GINO (2.09×10⁻²) by 86%, using fewer parameters than GINO and ViT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FNO/SFNO architecture by adding principled local operations, building on three threads: (1) finite-difference stencils, from which the authors derive a provable convergence result showing that a mean-centered, 1/h-scaled CNN kernel yields a differential operator; (2) DISCO convolutions (Ocampo et al., 2022), which are adapted here for the first time to the operator-learning setting to provide discretization-agnostic local integral kernels on general geometries including the sphere; and (3) prior work combining FNOs with standard CNN layers (Ye et al., 2022; Wen et al., 2022; Raonić et al., 2023), which this paper departs from by eliminating the need for explicit up/downsampling and extending to unstructured meshes. The key conceptual departure is that previous local-operation approaches were tied to a fixed grid resolution, whereas here both kernel types are formulated as continuous operators that can be evaluated at arbitrary resolutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 34–72% relative L2-error reductions across five PDE benchmarks (including turbulent Navier-Stokes and spherical shallow water) demonstrate that local inductive bias is a critical missing ingredient in spectral neural operators, particularly for capturing fine-scale and high-frequency features that global Fourier convolutions over-smooth. The practical impact is significant: the proposed layers are drop-in additions to existing FNO/SFNO codebases (available in the neuraloperator library), work on unstructured meshes, and preserve the discretization-agnostic property central to the neural operator paradigm. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 3D problems, how to handle the discretization sensitivity of differential layers at insufficient training resolution (noted in the super-resolution experiments), and whether the framework extends to other types of local operators (e.g., fractional derivatives).
