## Problem

State-of-the-art DICE methods for offline Learning from Observations (LfO) are restricted to f-divergences (KL, χ²) that ignore the geometric structure of state distributions, or to Wasserstein distance via Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality, which forces the underlying metric to be Euclidean. The paper demonstrates empirically that the choice of distance metric is critical for Wasserstein-based performance, yet no prior DICE method permits arbitrary or learned metrics. PW-DICE fills this gap by optimizing the primal Wasserstein distance, decoupling the metric choice from the optimization formulation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two offline datasets: expert state-only trajectories (no actions recorded) and task-agnostic state-action trajectories of unknown optimality. It produces a policy π(a|s) via weighted behavior cloning, where the per-sample weights are exponential functions of the optimized dual variables (Bellman residuals). The distance metric itself is learned online: a discriminator h(s) estimates the expert-to-non-expert occupancy ratio R(s), and a contrastive embedding g(s) (32-dim, trained with InfoNCE on consecutive state pairs from the task-agnostic data) captures reachability topology. A 3-head neural network parameterizes the |S|-dimensional dual variables for continuous state spaces.

## Method

PW-DICE formulates a joint minimization over the Wasserstein matching variable Π and the learner's state-action occupancy d_sa^π, subject to Bellman flow constraints and marginal constraints, with two KL regularizers (ε₁D_f(Π‖U) and ε₂D_f(d_sa^π‖d_sa^I)) that enforce offline pessimism. Applying Fenchel duality yields a single-level convex objective over 3|S| dual variables (value function, loading cost, unloading cost) with logsumexp terms, solvable by gradient descent. The key design choice is the distance metric c(s_i,s_j) = R(s_i) + β‖g(s_i)−g(s_j)‖², which combines a discriminator-based state importance score with a contrastively learned reachability embedding, enabling arbitrary non-Euclidean geometry. The policy is then extracted by weighted behavior cloning with weights proportional to exp((−γλ_{s'}+λ_s−λ_{s+|S|})/ε₂). Theoretically, PW-DICE generalizes SMODICE: setting ε₁→0, ε₂=1, and c(s,s')=−R(s) recovers the SMODICE objective exactly, unifying f-divergence and Wasserstein minimization under one framework.

## Results

On the tabular MDP (20 states, 4 actions, γ=0.95), PW-DICE (both LP and regularized variants) achieves the lowest regret across all combinations of expert dataset sizes {10, 100, 1000, 10000}, task-agnostic dataset sizes {10, 100, 1000, 10000}, and noise levels η ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 1}, outperforming SMODICE-KL, SMODICE-χ², and LobsDICE. On the MuJoCo testbed (hopper, halfcheetah, ant, walker2d, antmaze, kitchen), PW-DICE achieves the highest normalized average reward among all seven baselines on all four locomotion environments, with the gap over SMODICE-KL and LobsDICE being most pronounced on halfcheetah and ant. The method is also shown to be more robust than SMODICE under distorted state representations and mismatched expert dynamics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PW-DICE outperforms SMODICE (both KL and χ² variants) on all MuJoCo locomotion environments in normalized reward, and achieves lower regret on the tabular MDP; the χ² variant of SMODICE underperforms its KL counterpart in both papers. LobsDICE, which minimizes state-pair occupancy divergence, is consistently outperformed by PW-DICE on both the tabular and MuJoCo settings, though it was the best at minimizing divergence to the (suboptimal) softmax expert in the tabular experiment. OTR (Luo et al., 2023), despite also using the primal Wasserstein distance, struggles on most MuJoCo environments because its trajectory-level reward assignment fails to differentiate good from bad trajectories. ORIL (Zolna et al., 2020) and DWBC (Xu et al., 2022, which has extra access to expert actions) are both outperformed by PW-DICE on MuJoCo. ReCOIL (Sikchi et al., 2024) is outperformed as well, though its numbers are reported from the original paper since no code was available. Notably, PW-DICE is the only method to achieve negative regret (i.e., outperform the expert) in the tabular experiment with a highly stochastic softmax expert, when the task-agnostic dataset is large enough for accurate dynamics estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PW-DICE extends the DICE family of offline imitation learning methods (SMODICE, LobsDICE) by replacing f-divergence minimization with primal Wasserstein distance minimization, while retaining the Bellman flow constraint structure and dual-space optimization. It departs from prior Wasserstein-based IL work (SoftDICE, OTR, PWIL) that relies on Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality—which restricts the underlying metric to Euclidean—by optimizing the primal transport plan directly, enabling arbitrary and even learned distance metrics. The contrastive learning component for the distance metric draws on FIST and CURL, but repurposes InfoNCE to encode state reachability rather than visual similarity, and the theoretical result that SMODICE is a special case (ε₁→0, ε₂=1, c(s,s')=−R(s)) provides the first unified framework for f-divergence and Wasserstein DICE methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of f-divergence and Wasserstein minimization under a single convex optimization framework gives practitioners a principled way to choose between divergence types and, more importantly, to select or learn the underlying distance metric—a factor the paper empirically shows to be critical for Wasserstein-based performance. The removal of the data-coverage assumption (via the α-mixed denominator in R(s)) makes the method applicable in high-dimensional settings where expert and non-expert supports may not overlap. Open questions include whether the logsumexp-based objective remains stable in substantially harder environments (the authors acknowledge this as a limitation), whether the contrastively learned metric generalizes beyond locomotion tasks, and whether the approach extends to multi-task or hierarchical settings.
