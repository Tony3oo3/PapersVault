## Problem

Current state-of-the-art structure-based drug design methods rely on SE(3)-equivariant diffusion models over point clouds, which are complex to train, slow to sample from, and constrained by the expressivity limits of message-passing architectures. This paper asks whether a simpler, more expressive voxel-based score model—prioritizing representational power over equivariance inductive bias—can match or exceed these methods while being substantially faster to sample.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241656/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a protein binding pocket as input, voxelized into a 4×64×64×64 grid (channels for C, O, N, S) at 0.25 Å resolution over a 16³ Å³ volume centered on the ligand's center of mass. It outputs a 3D ligand as atomic coordinates, recovered by peak-detection from a 7×64×64×64 voxel grid (channels for C, O, N, S, F, Cl, P) followed by OpenBabel bond assignment. Hydrogens are modeled implicitly, and the same grid geometry and centering convention is applied to both ligand and pocket.

## Method

VoxBind extends the Neural Empirical Bayes (NEB) framework to the pocket-conditional setting, proving that the conditional Bayes estimator admits the closed form x̂(y|ξ) = y + σ²∇_y log p(y|ξ), which links a single denoising network to both the score function and the clean-molecule estimator. The architecture encodes the noisy ligand and pocket with separate 3D convolutional encoders, sums their embeddings, and passes the result through a 3D U-Net to predict the clean ligand, trained with a fixed-noise MSE denoising loss. Sampling uses conditional walk-jump sampling (cWJS): an underdamped Langevin MCMC chain draws noisy samples from the Gaussian-smoothed conditional p(y|ξ), and at arbitrary steps the denoiser performs a single-step 'jump' to estimate the clean molecule. Because the noise process is isotropic Gaussian smoothing (not coordinate displacement), steric clashes with the pocket are naturally avoided, and the single fixed noise level eliminates the multi-step diffusion schedule, making both training and inference simpler and faster.

## Results

On CrossDocked2020, VoxBind (σ=0.9) achieves a median VinaScore of −7.11 and VinaDock of −8.41, outperforming the previous best (DecompDiff: −6.04 and −8.43) on binding affinity. It also leads on high-affinity percentage (71.5% vs. 71.0% for DecompDiff), QED (0.58 vs. 0.43), and diversity (0.74 vs. 0.68). Sampling is ~5–12× faster than baselines: 492 s for 100 molecules on one A100 GPU versus 2,544 s (Pocket2Mol), 3,428 s (TargetDiff), and 6,189 s (DecompDiff).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VoxBind (σ=0.9) outperforms all five baselines on VinaScore (−7.11 vs. best baseline −6.30 for TargetDiff), VinaMin (−7.55 vs. −7.09 for DecompDiff), and high-affinity percentage (71.5% vs. 71.0% for DecompDiff). Against the closest competitor DecompDiff, VoxBind wins on VinaScore, VinaMin, QED (0.58 vs. 0.43), and diversity (0.74 vs. 0.68), while DecompDiff retains a slight edge on VinaDock (−8.43 vs. −8.41) and SA (0.60 vs. 0.69 — actually VoxBind wins here too). Compared to TargetDiff, VoxBind improves VinaScore by 0.81 and VinaDock by 0.50, while also generating molecules with fewer steric clashes (5.1 vs. 10.8 mean) and lower strain energy (161.9 vs. ~200+ kcal/mol). Against the autoregressive baselines (AR, Pocket2Mol), VoxBind dominates on all binding-affinity metrics and matches or exceeds them on drug-likeness (QED, SA), while generating molecules with atom counts closer to the reference distribution (24.0 vs. 15–16 for AR/Pocket2Mol). VoxBind is also 5–12× faster at sampling than all point-cloud baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

VoxBind extends the Neural Empirical Bayes (NEB) framework and walk-jump sampling scheme from Saremi & Hyvärinen (2019) and VoxMol (Pinheiro et al., 2023) to the pocket-conditional setting, introducing a conditional score function and a two-step walk-jump (cWJS) sampling procedure. It deliberately departs from the dominant point-cloud + SE(3)-equivariant diffusion paradigm (EDM, TargetDiff, DecompDiff) by representing molecules as 3D voxel grids and using a 3D U-Net architecture borrowed from conditional image generation, prioritizing expressivity over equivariance inductive bias. The noise process (Gaussian smoothing without spatial displacement) is fundamentally different from diffusion, providing a natural mechanism to avoid steric clashes with the pocket.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simpler, non-equivariant voxel-based CNN can outperform state-of-the-art equivariant point-cloud diffusion models on SBDD while being an order of magnitude faster to sample from, challenging the field's assumption that SE(3) equivariance is essential for 3D molecular generation. Key limitations include GPU-memory-bounded grid size (restricting applicability to drug-like molecules rather than nucleic acids or proteins), lack of wet-lab validation, and the need for OpenBabel post-processing for bond assignment. Open questions include scaling to larger biomolecules, better modeling of synthetic accessibility, and integrating pocket dynamics into the generation process.
