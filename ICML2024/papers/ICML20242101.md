## Problem

Existing theoretical guarantees for Sample Average Approximation (SAA) of Conditional Stochastic Optimization (CSO) rely critically on the IID assumption for training samples, yet many key applications—reinforcement learning, time series, optimal control—naturally produce dependent data. This paper fills the gap by establishing that SAA for CSO retains asymptotic consistency, finite-sample guarantees, and the same O(d/ε⁴) sample complexity when samples form a β-mixing sequence.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N outer samples {ξ_i} drawn from P(ξ) and M_i conditional samples {η_ij} drawn from P(η|ξ_i) per outer sample, where the joint sequence is stationary and β-mixing rather than IID. It also requires the loss functions f_ξ: R^m→R and g_η: R^d→R^m to be Lipschitz and bounded, and a compact decision set X⊂R^d. The output is an ε-optimal decision x̂_{N,M}*, the corresponding optimal value F̂_{N,M}*, and a 1−α confidence bound on the out-of-sample risk, with the deviation bound holding uniformly over the entire decision set.

## Method

The core technical contribution combines two tools: a covariance inequality for β-mixing sequences (|Cov(h(Z_j), h(Z_{j+k}))| ≤ 4‖h‖²β(k)) to control the inner conditional expectation error, and the independent block sampling technique of Yu (1993) to decompose the dependent outer sequence into nearly-IID blocks for which a Laplace-transform concentration inequality applies. The proof proceeds by constructing a ν-net over X to reduce the uniform supremum to a finite union, bounding the bias of the inner average via the covariance inequality, and then applying the block-based exponential tail bound to the centered outer sum. The resulting deviation bound is exponential in Nε²/(d_N M_f²(1+Σβ(i))), yielding the same O(d/ε⁴) sample complexity as the IID case, which drops to O(d/ε²) when inner and outer randomness are independent.

## Results

The paper establishes that SAA for CSO with β-mixing dependent data retains a sample complexity of O(d/ε⁴), matching the IID case, and O(d/ε²) when inner and outer randomness are independent. The exponential deviation bound takes the form P(sup|F̂−F|>ε) ≤ O(1)(4L_f L_g D_X/ε)^d exp(−Nε²/(32d_N M_f²(1+Σβ(i)))). In the P2P robust regression experiment, the allocation N=O(T^{1/2}) yields the smallest bias and highest probability guarantee P(x̂∈B_{0.5}(x⋆)), which increases exponentially with sample size. In the risk-averse portfolio experiment on the Kenneth R. French Data Library (Feb 2014–Feb 2022), all 6 sliding windows achieve positive mean returns, with cumulative returns increasing significantly over time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing optimization algorithms; instead, it compares the dependent (β-mixing) sampling scheme against an independent sampling scheme (where η_ij = η_1j for all i>1) in the P2P robust regression experiment. The independent scheme achieves a smaller in-sample risk, but the gap narrows as sample size increases, consistent with the theoretical prediction that dependence does not degrade asymptotic performance. Among three sample allocation strategies (N=O(T^{1/2}), O(T^{1/3}), O(T^{1/4})), the N=O(T^{1/2}) allocation dominates in bias and probability guarantee, validating the O(d/ε⁴) sample complexity result. The key theoretical comparison is with the IID setting of Hu et al. (2020a): the paper shows the same O(d/ε⁴) order is preserved under β-mixing, confirming that the dependent structure affects learning efficiency (captured by the mixing coefficient β(k)) but not the fundamental sample complexity rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAA framework for Conditional Stochastic Optimization (CSO) from the standard IID setting (Hu et al., 2020a; Dai et al., 2017) to the case where training samples form a β-mixing sequence. It draws on two key prior threads: (1) the SAA theory for classical Stochastic Optimization under dependent data (Wang et al., 2022; Pan, 2023), which established consistency and finite-sample guarantees for the simpler single-expectation case, and (2) the independent block sampling technique of Yu (1993), which is repurposed here to handle the nested conditional structure of CSO. The paper explicitly positions itself as filling a gap it identifies as 'remarkably sparse' in the literature: no prior work had established asymptotic consistency, finite-sample guarantees, or sample complexity for SAA-CSO under dependence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that the sample complexity remains O(d/ε⁴) under β-mixing—matching the IID rate—provides theoretical justification for applying SAA-CSO in inherently dependent settings (time series, RL trajectories, autoregressive processes) without sacrificing statistical efficiency. The conditional structure of CSO is shown to be the key driver of the ε⁻⁴ rate, which drops to ε⁻² when inner and outer randomness are independent, offering a clear diagnostic for practitioners. Open questions include how the mixing coefficient β(k) affects performance in specific applications, how to estimate β(k) from data in practice, and whether the results extend to other mixing conditions (e.g., φ-mixing) or non-stationary settings. The practical impact is currently limited to theoretical validation; the experiments confirm the theory but do not demonstrate a new algorithmic capability.
