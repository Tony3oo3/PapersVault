## Problem

Existing multivariate time series (MTS) pre-training methods serve only as model initializers and cannot execute downstream tasks without parameter or architecture modification, while task-specific methods require retraining from scratch for each task and setting (e.g., changing the forecasting length). No prior framework provides a single pre-trained model that can immediately produce sensible solutions across forecasting, imputation, and anomaly detection with frozen parameters, and then be further refined for a specific task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242452/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{T×C} and, depending on the task, either a past window (forecasting), an observed series with a binary missing mask (imputation), or a full observed series (anomaly detection). In IR mode the model processes each channel independently as a univariate sequence, so the same frozen weights handle any number of channels and any window length without retraining. In FT mode the full multivariate instance is processed jointly, and the output is a forecasted future window, an imputed series, or a per-timestamp anomaly score (MSE between original and reconstructed patches), respectively.

## Method

UP2ME follows a univariate-to-multivariate paradigm motivated by the heterogeneity of temporal and cross-channel dependencies. During univariate pre-training, variable window length (randomly sampled patch count per batch) and channel decoupling (independently sampling timestamp and channel index) generate univariate instances for MAE, deliberately discarding cross-channel structure to focus the encoder on temporal patterns. The pre-trained encoder-decoder then handles downstream tasks in IR mode by formulating each as a specific mask-reconstruction problem (future patches masked for forecasting, missing patches masked for imputation, each patch iteratively masked for anomaly scoring) with no parameter changes. In multivariate fine-tuning, the pre-trained encoder and decoder are frozen, and learnable Temporal-Channel (TC) layers are inserted between them; each TC layer applies a standard Transformer over time and a Graph Transformer over a sparse cross-channel dependency graph. That graph is constructed by max-pooling the frozen encoder's latent tokens per channel, computing pairwise cosine similarity, and retaining the intersection of the top-rC entries and r-nearest neighbors per channel, yielding at most rC edges and O(CN²) complexity per TC layer.

## Results

On forecasting (averaged over 4 prediction lengths), UP2ME(FT) achieves the best mSE on all 8 datasets, e.g., 0.341 on ETTm1, 0.221 on Weather, 0.155 on Electricity, and 1.413 on GECCO. Notably, UP2ME(IR) with frozen pre-trained parameters and no fine-tuning already matches or approaches SOTA task-specific methods on ETTm1 (0.360 vs. PatchTST's 0.353), Electricity (0.165 vs. 0.164), and Traffic (0.401 vs. 0.395). For imputation, UP2ME(FT) achieves the best mSE on 7 of 8 datasets (e.g., 0.128 on ETTm1, 0.079 on Weather, 0.097 on Electricity). For anomaly detection, UP2ME(FT) approaches task-specific SOTA on SMD (F1=83.31 vs. AnomalyTrans 90.98) and PSM (F1=97.16 vs. DCdetector 97.50), but substantially outperforms all methods on GECCO (F1=63.39 vs. DCdetector 31.71).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UP2ME(FT) outperforms all 13 baselines on forecasting across all 8 datasets, with the largest margins over pre-training methods (SimMTM, TS2Vec) and general-purpose TimesNet. The closest competitor is PatchTST, which UP2ME(FT) beats by 0.012 mSE on ETTm1 (0.341 vs. 0.353) and by 0.045 on GECCO (1.413 vs. 1.656). UP2ME(IR) without any fine-tuning is already competitive with PatchTST on ETTm1 (0.360 vs. 0.353) and Electricity (0.165 vs. 0.164), demonstrating the strength of the univariate pre-training. For imputation, UP2ME(FT) dominates over task-specific SAITS and GRIN by large margins (e.g., 0.128 vs. 0.201 mSE on ETTm1; 0.079 vs. 0.103 on Weather), while traditional interpolation methods (LI, SI) are far behind. For anomaly detection, UP2ME(FT) is slightly behind the two task-specific SOTA methods (DCdetector, AnomalyTrans) on SMD and PSM but substantially outperforms them on the challenging GECCO dataset (F1=63.39 vs. 31.71/34.45), and beats all pre-training and general-purpose baselines across all four anomaly detection datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

UP2ME adapts the Masked Autoencoder (MAE) pre-training paradigm from computer vision to multivariate time series, but introduces two key departures from both MAE and prior MTS pre-training methods (TS2Vec, SimMTM): variable window length and channel decoupling during instance generation. The univariate-to-multivariate paradigm is the central conceptual departure—prior MTS pre-training methods either process all channels jointly (retaining cross-channel dependency throughout) or use channel independence (processing channels separately but co-occurring), whereas UP2ME completely discards cross-channel information during pre-training and reintroduces it via a sparse dependency graph constructed from the frozen encoder during fine-tuning. Unlike general-purpose methods like TimesNet that require training from scratch per task, UP2ME's IR mode provides task-agnostic solutions with frozen parameters, a capability no prior MTS pre-training method possessed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IR mode—providing reasonable forecasting, imputation, and anomaly detection solutions with zero parameter modification after pre-training—addresses a practical pain point: practitioners no longer need to select, configure, and retrain a different model for each task or setting change. The univariate-to-multivariate paradigm also offers a natural path toward multi-dataset foundation models for MTS, since univariate pre-training is agnostic to the number of channels. Key limitations include the inability to handle classification tasks in IR mode, the single-dataset pre-training scope (unlike NLP foundation models), and the fact that anomaly detection performance, while competitive, still trails the best task-specific methods on some datasets. Building a true cross-dataset MTS foundation model remains an open question the authors explicitly flag for future work.
