## Problem

Existing RMAB literature assumes stochastic (stationary) rewards, while adversarial MDP and CMDP works do not handle the instantaneous activation constraint that at most B arms can be active per epoch. No prior algorithm achieves Õ(√T) regret for adversarial RMAB with both unknown transition functions and bandit feedback, where only rewards of visited state-action pairs are revealed. This paper is the first to close this gap, providing a provably efficient RL algorithm for this harder, more realistic setting.

## Input / Output

The decision maker observes the state of all N arms at each of H decision epochs within T episodes, with only the state space S and binary action space A={0,1} known in advance; transition kernels are unknown and adversarial rewards are revealed solely for the state-action pairs actually visited (bandit feedback). The output is a per-arm binary action (activate or passive) at each epoch that must satisfy the hard constraint of at most B active arms, implemented via a reward-maximizing index (RMI) that ranks arms and activates the top B. Each episode resets all arms to fixed initial states, and the algorithm must adapt its policy across episodes as rewards change arbitrarily.

## Method

UCMD-ARMAB combines four key components: (1) Hoeffding-based confidence sets that contain the true transition kernels with high probability; (2) Online Mirror Descent (OMD) applied to a relaxed ARMAB formulated over a time-dependent occupancy measure (necessary because no stationary policy exists for finite-horizon RMAB with an activation constraint, unlike adversarial MDP); (3) a novel biased overestimated adversarial reward estimator built from inverse importance-weighted counts with a bonus term, which is upper-bounded by 1 and encourages exploration without relying on a stationary occupancy measure; and (4) a low-complexity RMI index policy that converts the relaxed OMD solution into a feasible policy satisfying the hard per-epoch activation constraint by ranking arms on the probability of activation under the optimal occupancy measure. The regret is decomposed into three terms—OMD optimization gap, bandit-feedback estimation error, and index-policy implementation gap—each bounded to yield the overall Õ(H√T) regret guarantee.

## Results

The paper establishes a $\tilde{O}(H\sqrt{T})$ regret bound for UCMD-ARMAB, where $T$ is the number of episodes and $H$ is the episode length, making it the first algorithm to achieve $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T})$ regret for adversarial RMAB with bandit feedback and unknown transition functions. In numerical experiments on CPAP adherence (50 episodes × 50 steps) and a deadline scheduling problem (100 episodes × 100 steps), UCMD-ARMAB achieves significantly higher accumulated rewards and provably sublinear regret compared to the RMAB-UCRL baseline, whose regret grows exponentially under adversarial reward settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares UCMD-ARMAB against RMAB-UCRL (Xiong et al., 2022c), a state-of-the-art index-based RL algorithm designed for stochastic RMAB. In both the CPAP adherence and deadline scheduling case studies under adversarial rewards, UCMD-ARMAB significantly outperforms RMAB-UCRL in accumulated reward (Figures 2a, 3a) and exhibits provably sublinear regret (Figures 2b, 3b), whereas RMAB-UCRL's regret increases exponentially under adversarial settings. Theoretically, UCMD-ARMAB is the first to achieve $\tilde{O}(H\sqrt{T})$ regret for adversarial RMAB with bandit feedback and unknown transitions, matching the regret rate of adversarial MDP (Jin et al., 2020) and CMDP (Qiu et al., 2020) while additionally handling the hard instantaneous activation constraint via a low-complexity RMI index policy. Compared to stochastic RMAB (Xiong et al., 2022a;c) which achieves $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{HT})$, the adversarial setting introduces a $\sqrt{H}$ gap, reflecting the additional difficulty of arbitrary reward changes and partial (bandit) feedback.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads: adversarial MDP/CMDP (Rosenberg & Mansour 2019b; Jin et al. 2020; Qiu et al. 2020), which use OMD over occupancy measures for online regret minimization; stochastic RMAB (Xiong et al. 2022a;c; Wang et al. 2020), which use UCRL confidence sets and index policies for restless bandits with hard activation constraints; and Whittle index theory (Whittle 1988; Weber & Weiss 1990), which provides the asymptotic optimality foundation for index policies. The key departure is that adversarial MDP/CMDP rely on stationary occupancy measures (valid because stationary policies exist), whereas finite-horizon RMAB with instantaneous activation constraints admits no such stationary policy, forcing the use of time-dependent occupancy measures and a novel biased reward estimator that cannot leverage stationary visitation frequencies. The RMI index policy adapts the stochastic RMAB index framework to the adversarial setting, where rewards change arbitrarily across episodes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provably efficient algorithm for adversarial RMAB with bandit feedback and unknown transitions, closing a gap where no prior method could handle the combination of adversarial rewards, partial feedback, and hard activation constraints simultaneously. The Õ(H√T) regret matches the best known rates for adversarial MDP and CMDP, but the √H gap relative to stochastic RMAB (which achieves Õ(√(HT))) remains an open question—whether it is inherent to the adversarial setting or an artifact of the current analysis is unclear. The PSPACE-hardness of the offline problem makes the relaxed LP + index policy approach practically important, and the applications to CPAP adherence and EV charging scheduling demonstrate real-world relevance. Future work could explore whether the regret gap can be closed, whether the approach extends to adversarial transitions, or whether function approximation can handle larger state spaces.
