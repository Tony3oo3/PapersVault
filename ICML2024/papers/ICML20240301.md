## Problem

Existing unlearnable examples suffer from two critical limitations in real-world deployment: they lack cross-dataset transferability (perturbations degrade when applied to non-target datasets) and assume label-consistent scenarios (data owners and adversaries share the same labels). No prior method addresses both issues simultaneously, as transferable approaches require class labels and label-agnostic approaches rely on unstable clustering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240301/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The generator takes a 224×224 RGB image as input, extracts its CLIP image embedding as concept guidance, and outputs a perturbed image x' = x + δ with an L∞ norm constraint of δ < 8/255 to ensure imperceptibility. Because the generator is trained once on ImageNet, it can process images from any domain or resolution (with a resizing module for non-224×224 inputs) without retraining, enabling zero-shot generation of unlearnable examples for arbitrary data.

## Method

The 14A generator is a universal encoder-decoder network that takes both the original image and its CLIP image embedding as input, producing a sample-specific perturbation. Instead of using a surrogate adversary model, it directly aligns the perturbed image's CLIP embedding with target text embeddings in the shared multi-modal space: it pushes the embedding away from the top-k most similar text concepts (intra-concept separation) and toward the most opposite concept (inter-concept attraction) via a concept-wise discriminant loss. This loss uses an additive form rather than the multiplicative form of prior class-wise losses to handle the widely dispersed scale of concept distances, and selects only top-k associated concepts to reduce computational cost. The multi-modal embedding eliminates the need for explicit labels or clustering, making the generator inherently label-agnostic and cross-dataset transferable by operating on underlying concepts rather than dataset-specific features.

## Results

In the cross-dataset and label-agnostic setting (the paper's primary evaluation), 14A achieves the lowest testing accuracy across nearly all datasets and backbones, indicating the strongest unlearnability. On ResNet18, 14A reduces testing accuracy to 7.24% (Pets), 29.58% (Flowers), 19.21% (Cars), 19.19% (Food), and 28.74% (Sun), compared to 19.51–62.34% for the best baseline (LaUE). On large-scale backbones (ResNet50, VGG16, ViT), 14A's average improvement over baselines reaches 41.91%, versus 27.01% for smaller models, suggesting larger models are more vulnerable to the concept-transfer shortcut. Against attacks (Mixup, Gaussian smoothing, OPA), post-attack accuracy remains low (average 16.91%, max 34.10%), confirming robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

14A consistently outperforms both CP (Contrastive Poisoning, He et al. 2023) and LaUE (Label-agnostic Unlearnable Examples, Zhang et al. 2023) across nearly all datasets in the cross-dataset + label-agnostic setting. On ResNet18, 14A reduces Flowers accuracy to 29.58% versus 55.86% for CP and 62.34% for LaUE — a gap of 26–33 percentage points. On ViT, 14A achieves 10.63% on Flowers versus 45.84% (CP) and 31.05% (LaUE), a 20–35 point margin. The advantage is most pronounced on large backbones (average 41.91% improvement over baselines vs. 27.01% on small models), suggesting CP and LaUE's gradient-based or cluster-based perturbations are less effective against stronger feature extractors. The one exception is Cars, where 14A (19.21%) is comparable to LaUE (19.98%) and CP (20.68%), attributed to limited car images in ImageNet and resizing artifacts. TUE (Transferable Unlearnable Examples, Ren et al. 2023), also compared in the tables, performs similarly to CP and is consistently outperformed by 14A.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generator-based unlearnable examples framework introduced by Zhang et al. (LaUE, CVPR 2023), which used one autoencoder generator per cluster to produce label-agnostic perturbations. The key departure is replacing cluster-specific generators with a single universal generator that takes CLIP image embeddings as concept-conditioned input, and replacing the class-wise discriminant loss (which requires labels) with a concept-wise discriminant loss that aligns image embeddings toward opposite text concepts while pushing away from similar ones. This eliminates both the need for a surrogate model and the instability of k-means clustering, while simultaneously addressing cross-dataset transferability and label-agnostic utility—two challenges that prior methods (TUE for transferability, LaUE for label-agnosticism) handled only separately.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'once-trained, multiple-used' universal generator offers a practical efficiency advantage over gradient-based and multi-generator approaches, making unlearnable example generation feasible in dynamic or streaming data scenarios. However, the method's dependence on ImageNet-scale training and its performance degradation on low-resolution inputs (CIFAR-10/100) limit its true universality, and the authors acknowledge the need for adaptive resizing modules. Open questions include extending concept unlearnability beyond images to audio, text, and video modalities, and whether the CLIP-based concept alignment generalizes to domains where text descriptions are less informative. The observation that larger backbones (ViT, VGG16) are more vulnerable to the perturbations than smaller ones is a useful practical insight for defenders.
