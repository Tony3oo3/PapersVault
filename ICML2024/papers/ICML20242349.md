## Problem

Existing continual learning paradigms suffer catastrophic forgetting of expert classes when acquiring new knowledge, while federated learning is highly sensitive to data heterogeneity and lacks directionality in preserving individual agent strengths. The paper asks whether multi-agent systems can learn new abilities through socialized interaction—acquiring broader general knowledge while directionally maintaining superior performance on each agent's original expert classes—without relying on large-scale data or costly parameter synchronization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242349/cropped_page1_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent receives 32×32 RGB images from CIFAR-10 or CIFAR-100, partitioned so that every agent holds a disjoint subset of classes as its expert set (e.g., 2 of 10 classes in CIFAR10-5-2, or 25 of 100 in CIFAR100-4-25). At inference, the shared student backbone extracts features that are fed simultaneously to a general classifier and a per-agent expert classifier; the final class label is produced by whichever classifier yields the higher Helmholtz free energy, effectively routing each sample to the most confident head.

## Method

MASC (Multi-Agent Socialized Collaboration) operates in two phases. First, collective collaboration trains a student agent via collaborative distillation from N teacher agents, combining cross-entropy loss, per-teacher knowledge distillation loss, and an energy-alignment loss that constrains each classifier's Helmholtz free energy around a fixed anchor Δ; this grows the student from a vanilla agent into a generalist covering all classes. Second, reciprocal altruism freezes the student backbone and trains a new per-teacher classifier under the original teacher's supervision using the same three-term loss, so each teacher gains general-class ability while retaining expert-class strength. At inference, the classifier with the highest HFE is selected per sample, implementing a divide-and-conquer strategy that directionally preserves expert accuracy while enabling versatile general-class prediction. The paper further provides an information-theoretic analysis showing that sociability information (conditional mutual information between agents' inputs and the target) bounds the improvement in Bayes error from multi-agent collaboration over single-agent learning.

## Results

On CIFAR10-5-2, MASC achieves 93.40% expert-class accuracy, 81.04% general-class accuracy, and 87.22% average accuracy after growth, outperforming the best CL method (PODNet: 80.15/66.64/73.39) and the best FL method (FedDecorr: 91.85/75.54/83.69) on all three metrics. On CIFAR100-4-25, MASC reaches 65.40% expert, 58.64% general, and 62.02% average accuracy, surpassing the best CL baseline (DER: 54.04/54.53/54.29) and best FL baseline (FedDecorr: 60.16/56.93/58.55). The key advantage is directionality: MASC maintains a clear expert-over-general gap (e.g., +12.36 on CIFAR10) while CL methods suffer catastrophic forgetting (expert drops to 40–80%) and FL methods show near-zero directionality (expert ≈ general).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MASC outperforms all 16 baselines on every metric (expert, general, and average accuracy) on both CIFAR10 and CIFAR100. Among CL methods, PODNet is the strongest (73.39% avg on CIFAR10) but still trails MASC by 13.83 points, and most CL methods (EWC, iCaRL, LwF) suffer severe catastrophic forgetting with expert accuracy dropping below 50%. Among FL methods, FedDecorr is the closest competitor (83.69% avg on CIFAR10, 58.55% on CIFAR100), yet MASC leads by 3.53 and 3.47 points respectively; critically, FL methods show near-zero directionality (expert ≈ general), whereas MASC maintains a clear expert advantage. GLFC (federated CL) performs worst overall (51.56% avg on CIFAR10), indicating that naively combining FL and CL paradigms is ineffective. The ablation confirms that neither collective collaboration alone (63.60% avg) nor reciprocal altruism alone (58.38% avg) suffices—only their combination achieves the directional trade-off that MASC targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of continual learning (CL) and federated learning (FL), arguing that both are insufficient: CL is single-agent and suffers catastrophic forgetting, while FL is multi-agent but non-directional and sensitive to heterogeneity. It introduces 'socialized learning' (SL) as a new paradigm inspired by cultural evolution and population genetics, where multiple agents with disjoint expert classes collaboratively grow into generalists while directionally preserving their original expertise. Methodologically, MASC builds on Helmholtz free energy–based classifier selection (Liu et al., 2020) for inference and on knowledge distillation for the collective collaboration module, but departs from both CL and FL by treating expert and general classes as two sides of the same coin within a unified multi-agent framework rather than as a sequential stability-plasticity trade-off.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The directional maintenance of expert-class accuracy while acquiring new general classes is a practically relevant requirement (e.g., a medical AI that must not degrade on its specialty while learning adjacent domains), and the SL framing provides a clean conceptual vocabulary for it. However, the evidence is confined to CIFAR-10/100 with 4–5 agents and a fixed class partition, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger agent populations, dynamic class arrivals, or real-world heterogeneous data. The data-driven knowledge distillation component (generating synthetic samples from teacher statistics) is a promising direction for reducing communication overhead, but its practical impact in bandwidth-constrained settings remains untested. Future work on multi-modal agents and dynamic inter-agent relationships, as the authors note, would be needed to validate the paradigm beyond toy benchmarks.
