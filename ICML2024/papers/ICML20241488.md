## Problem

Prior theoretical work (Jayakumar et al., 2020) proposed a hierarchy of multiplicative interactions in which gating sits at the bottom and full weight prediction (HyperNetworks) at the top, implying kernel prediction is strictly more expressive. This paper identifies that the hierarchy overlooks fixed-weight layers that are virtually always present adjacent to gating layers in practice, and shows that once these are accounted for, gating and weight prediction are on equal footing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241488/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the practical image burst denoising application, the network takes a burst of T noisy grayscale frames (H×W×T) and produces a single denoised image (H×W), using 5×5 spatial kernels. The theoretical framework operates on feature vectors of length n_i (up to 128 in experiments) or 3D feature maps (C×H×W), producing either a predicted weight matrix (HyperNetwork) or an element-wise multiplier vector of size n_k (gated double layer). In the KPN variant, the hidden layer channel count M (64 or 128) controls the rank of the kernel approximation, with the largest intermediate tensor being 1.5–2.1× smaller than in the original KPN.

## Method

The core contribution is a constructive equivalence (Theorem 3.1) showing that a gated double layer — a fixed linear projection, an element-wise multiplication by a predicted vector, and a second fixed linear projection — exactly reproduces any HyperNetwork layer's output when the hidden dimension satisfies n_k ≥ n_j·n_i. The fixed layers serve as indexing matrices that map each entry of the predicted multiplier to a specific position in the weight matrix, making the element-wise product functionally identical to a full matrix-vector product with predicted weights. This extends to convolutional kernel prediction (Corollary 3.2) and to Factorization Machines (Theorem 3.3). Empirically, the authors train GDLs with Adagrad to emulate both random and CIFAR-10-trained HyperNetwork layers, showing they converge to lower error than equally-sized MLPs, and demonstrate that a low-rank GDL (M=128) replacing the kernel prediction in a KPN achieves 32.8 dB PSNR (vs. 32.6 dB for the original) while reducing peak memory by 1.5×.

## Results

On the 8-frame image burst denoising task (McMaster dataset), the proposed Gated-128 variant achieves 32.8 dB test PSNR, slightly outperforming the original Kernel Prediction Network (32.6 dB) while reducing the maximum feature map size from 200×H×W to 128×H×W (a 1.5× memory saving). The more aggressive Gated-64 variant (64×H×W) degrades to 31.9 dB PSNR, indicating a lower bound on the hidden layer size. In the random and CIFAR-10 trained network emulation experiments, the gated double layer (GDL) consistently outperforms an equally-sized MLP baseline in relative L2 error across all tested hidden layer scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the original Kernel Prediction Network (KPN) of Mildenhall et al. (2018), which predicts a full 5×5 denoising kernel per pixel. The proposed Gated-128 variant (replacing kernel prediction with a convolutional gated double layer) achieves 32.8 dB PSNR versus 32.6 dB for the original KPN, a marginal quality improvement, while reducing the widest layer's channel count by 1.5× (from 200 to 128). The Gated-64 variant, which further compresses the hidden layer, drops to 31.9 dB, showing that the low-rank approximation has a practical lower bound. In the auxiliary emulation experiments (Sec. 4.1–4.2), the GDL also outperforms an equally-FLOP-sized MLP baseline at all hidden layer scales for both random and CIFAR-10-trained HyperNetwork layers, with the trained-network case showing faster convergence at lower scales due to the low-rank structure of learned predicted weights.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multiplicative-interaction hierarchy of Jayakumar et al. (2020), which placed gating at the bottom and full weight prediction (HyperNetworks) at the top, by showing the hierarchy is flat once the fixed-weight layers that are virtually always adjacent to gating in practice are accounted for. It also connects to the Factorization Machine literature (Rendle, 2010) by proving that low-rank linear HyperNetwork layers are FMs for binary inputs, and provides the missing theoretical justification for the empirical success of gating-based alternatives to attention and kernel prediction in gMLP (Liu et al., 2021), NAFNet (Chen et al., 2022), and modulated periodic activations (Mehta et al., 2021). The empirical application builds directly on the Kernel Prediction Network of Mildenhall et al. (2018) for burst denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The equivalence result gives practitioners a principled reason to prefer gating over full kernel prediction when memory is constrained, and the KPN experiment demonstrates a concrete 1.5× memory reduction with no quality loss. However, the paper acknowledges two important limitations: there is no guarantee that SGD will find the GDL solution (only existence is proven), and generalization to multi-headed attention architectures is non-trivial because constructing a useful low-rank basis for MHA is itself an open problem. The broader implication is that the recent popularity of gating-based architectures (gMLP, NAFNet) is not an accident but a theoretically grounded rank-constrained approximation of weight prediction.
