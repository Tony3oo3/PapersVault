## Problem

Large language models have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in natural language processing but have not been directly adapted to single-cell transcriptomics, where traditional methods rely on specialized architectures (VAEs, diffusion models, gene-embedding transformers) that cannot leverage LLMs' pretrained linguistic knowledge. This paper addresses the gap by asking whether gene expression data can be reformulated as text so that standard LLM fine-tuning pipelines become applicable to biological generation, annotation, and interpretation tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes single-cell RNA-seq count matrices (filtered to ≥200 expressed genes per cell and ≥200 cells per gene, log-normalized to 10,000 UMI) and converts them into 'cell sentences'—sequences of gene names sorted by descending expression rank. For GPT-2 experiments, sentences are truncated to the top 100 genes (~350 tokens); Pythia-160m operates on full-length sentences up to 9,200 tokens. Outputs include generated cell sentences (convertible back to expression vectors via a per-dataset linear regression on log-rank), combinatorial text labels (cell type × perturbation × exposure), and free-form natural language abstracts. The rank-to-expression mapping achieves R² ≈ 0.815 on the immune tissue dataset, preserving over 81% of expression variance.

## Method

Cell2Sentence (C2S) encodes each cell as a sequence of gene names rank-ordered by transcript abundance, exploiting the well-known inverse-rank (Zipf-like) frequency structure of gene expression so that a simple linear regression in log-log space (e_i = a_d × r_i + b_d) can recover approximate expression values from rank alone. Standard causal language modeling (next-token prediction) is then used to fine-tune GPT-2 (small/medium/large) and Pythia-160m on these cell sentences, with natural language prompts (e.g., 'Cell type: CD4 T cell. Genes: …') embedded via 20 template variants. A critical finding is that natural language pretraining is essential: models trained from random initialization on cell sentences alone achieve only ~29% cell-type prediction accuracy versus ~70–74% for NL-pretrained models, confirming that the LLM's linguistic embeddings provide meaningful structure for conditioning. The framework is fully compatible with Hugging Face transformers and flash attention, requiring no custom architecture—only a BPE tokenizer trained on the gene-name vocabulary (9,609 tokens) and standard fine-tuning hyperparameters (AdamW, cosine schedule, 16× gradient accumulation).

## Results

On conditional cell generation (immune tissue dataset), C2S (Pythia-160m) achieves the best k-NN accuracy of 0.2746 (k=10) and the lowest Gromov-Wasserstein distance of 54.30, outperforming scDiffusion (0.2368, 72.02), scVI (0.2425, 302.13), scGen (0.2377, 315.95), and scGPT (0.1811, 2989.81). On perturbed cell generation, C2S achieves Pearson R of 0.9241 and Spearman R of 0.6210 on unseen cytokine stimulation conditions, substantially exceeding scGen (0.6805 / 0.5654) and scGPT (0.0041 / −0.0002). For combinatorial cell label classification, C2S (GPT-2 Large) achieves the highest partial-label accuracy across all three benchmarks: 0.639 (Cytokine Stimulation), 0.631 (L1000), and 0.575 (GTEx), and the highest full-label accuracy of 0.202 on L1000. In abstract summary generation, only C2S produces statistically significant alignment with ground-truth abstracts (T-test p=0.003, KS-test p=0.023), while all LLM baselines (GPT-3.5, Mixtral, Mistral) fail to reach significance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C2S (Pythia-160m) outperforms all four single-cell generative baselines on conditional cell generation: it beats scDiffusion (the strongest non-LLM baseline) by ~3.8% k-NN accuracy at k=10 and by 24.7 GW distance, and surpasses scGPT by ~9.4% k-NN accuracy and by a factor of ~55 in GW distance. On perturbed cell generation, C2S achieves Pearson R of 0.9241 versus scGen's 0.6805 and scGPT's near-zero 0.0041, indicating that the language model's semantic understanding of combinatorial labels (cell type + cytokine + exposure) provides a substantial advantage over non-language architectures. For combinatorial label classification, C2S (GPT-2 Large) outperforms Geneformer (0.600/0.419/0.500 partial ACC) and scGPT (0.419/0.334/0.270) on all three datasets (Cytokine Stimulation, L1000, GTEx), and also beats classical baselines k-NN and XGBoost, demonstrating that natural language pretraining transfers to out-of-distribution bulk RNA-seq data. In abstract summary generation, C2S (GPT-2 small) is the only method achieving statistically significant correlation with ground-truth abstracts (T-test p=0.003), while GPT-3.5-Turbo (p=0.220), Mixtral-8x7B (p=0.233), and Mistral-7B (p=0.384) all fail to reach significance, and C2S's generated embeddings are 50% closer to ground truth in MMD distance than the best LLM baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes standard pretrained LLMs (GPT-2, Pythia-160m) for single-cell transcriptomics by introducing a rank-order gene encoding (Cell2Sentence) that converts expression matrices into text sequences, leveraging the well-known inverse-rank frequency structure of gene expression. It departs from the dominant single-cell foundation model paradigm (scGPT, Geneformer) that uses specialized tokenization and masked language modeling over numeric gene values, and from generative architectures like scVI, scGen, and scDiffusion that operate directly in expression space. The conceptual bridge is the observation that a simple linear model in log-log space can reversibly map rank back to expression, making the text encoding information-preserving enough for downstream biological tasks while enabling direct use of Hugging Face tooling and causal LM objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical accessibility of C2S—requiring only standard LLM fine-tuning pipelines and no custom architectures—lowers the barrier for biologists to leverage pretrained language models on transcriptomic data. A key open question is whether the approach scales: experiments are limited to 100-gene truncations for GPT-2 and 160M-parameter Pythia, and the seen-vs-unseen prompt gap (Table 10) suggests fragility at this scale. The paper also leaves open whether the rank-order encoding generalizes to other omics modalities (proteomics, epigenomics) or to longer gene panels, and whether the modest model sizes used here would benefit from the emergent capabilities observed at larger LLM scales.
