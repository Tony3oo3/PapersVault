## Problem

Existing GP-based uncertainty estimation for Transformers (e.g., SGPA) applies symmetric-kernel SVGPs to the attention kernel, ignoring that self-attention is inherently asymmetric (W_q ≠ W_k), which creates a nontrivial gap in capturing the intrinsic structure. Additionally, the matrix inversion in SVGP posteriors scales as O(s³), limiting efficiency for large-scale sequence data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240285/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard image patches (32×32 CIFAR-10/100) or text sequences (IMDB sentiment, CoLA acceptability) as input and produces class predictions accompanied by calibrated posterior uncertainty over the attention outputs. For fixed-length vision inputs, a concatenation merging scheme combines the two SVGP branches; for variable-length language inputs, an addition scheme is used. The final attention output is projected to the standard d_v dimensions via learned linear layers, preserving compatibility with downstream Transformer blocks.

## Method

KEP-SVGP replaces the self-attention layer with a pair of SVGPs whose inducing variables are formulated from the adjoint eigenfunctions of the asymmetric attention kernel, obtained via Kernel SVD (KSVD). The left and right singular vectors of K_att serve as kernel-eigen features for two symmetric kernels (K_att K_att^T and K_att^T K_att), fully characterizing the asymmetry in the posterior. Because the inducing-variable covariance is the diagonal singular-value matrix Λ, the posterior inversion reduces from O(s³) to O(s), yielding an overall time complexity of O(Ns²) for the addition scheme versus O(N²s) for SGPA. A tailored ELBO is derived to jointly optimize variational parameters (m_u, S_uu) and network weights (W_e, W_r, Λ), with a KSVD regularization loss ensuring the projections remain valid kernel-eigen features. The approximate posterior leverages the low-rank property of attention matrices (via Eckart-Young) to drop the residual term, further simplifying computation.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, KEP-SVGP achieves 84.70% accuracy with AURC of 35.15, outperforming MSP (83.50%, AURC 42.60) and all other single-model baselines. On IMDB, it reaches 89.01% accuracy with AURC 30.69, surpassing SGPA (88.36%, AURC 33.14) while being ~50× faster in training (32.30 vs 1662.36 s/epoch). KEP-SVGP Ensembles outperforms Deep Ensembles on all four benchmarks, e.g., 87.62% vs 86.43% ACC on CIFAR-10 and 31.02 vs 27.35 MCC on CoLA. On OOD detection (Table 3), KEP-SVGP Ensembles achieves the best AUROC in nearly all settings, e.g., 84.16% on CIFAR-100→SVHN vs 79.49% for Deep Ensembles.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KEP-SVGP consistently outperforms MSP on accuracy across all four datasets (e.g., 84.70% vs 83.50% on CIFAR-10, 30.54 vs 26.93 MCC on CoLA) while substantially reducing AURC (35.15 vs 42.60 on CIFAR-10). Against the closest Transformer-specific baseline SGPA, KEP-SVGP wins on every metric for both IMDB and CoLA (e.g., 89.01% vs 88.36% ACC and 30.69 vs 33.14 AURC on IMDB) and is ~50× faster in training (32.30 vs 1662.36 s/epoch on IMDB). KFLLLA achieves the best ECE on CIFAR-10 (1.51%) and CoLA (2.51%) but is unstable across tasks (worst AURC on CIFAR-100 at 263.59) and does not improve failure-prediction metrics. SV-DKL shows competitive ECE on CIFAR-100 (1.59%) but has the lowest ACC among single-model methods on CIFAR-10-C (68.95%) and poor AURC on IMDB (59.84). KEP-SVGP Ensembles surpasses Deep Ensembles on all four benchmarks in accuracy and most uncertainty metrics, with the largest gap on CoLA (31.02 vs 27.35 MCC) and CIFAR-100 (62.45 vs 59.65% ACC), and achieves the best OOD AUROC in nearly all Table 3 settings (e.g., 84.16% on CIFAR-100→SVHN vs 79.49% for Deep Ensembles).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Sparse Variational Gaussian Process (SVGP) framework of Titsias (2009) by replacing standard inducing points with kernel-eigen features derived from the asymmetric Kernel SVD (KSVD) of the attention matrix, building directly on the Primal-Attention formulation of Chen et al. (2023). The most direct predecessor, SGPA (Chen & Li, 2023), applies a single SVGP to attention outputs but treats the attention kernel as symmetric; KEP-SVGP departs by decomposing the inherently asymmetric kernel into two symmetric sub-kernels (K_att K_att^T and K_att^T K_att) and modeling each with a separate SVGP branch. The kernel-eigen feature technique itself traces back to inter-domain GPs (Lázaro-Gredilla & Figueiras-Vidal, 2009), but its application to large-architecture models like Transformers is new here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first variational inference approach for Transformers that explicitly accounts for the asymmetry of the self-attention kernel, closing a theoretical gap where prior GP-based attention methods implicitly assumed symmetry. The reduction of posterior inversion from O(s³) to O(s) via the diagonal singular-value matrix is a clean and practical efficiency gain, making Bayesian attention feasible at longer sequence lengths. However, experiments are limited to small-scale benchmarks (CIFAR-10/100, IMDB, CoLA) and the low-rank approximation (Remark 3.1) that enables the simplified posterior may not hold for shallow layers or very long sequences, leaving open whether the approach scales to ImageNet or long-context language modelling. The concatenation merging scheme still retains O(N²s) complexity, partially offsetting the gains.
