## Problem

Existing DPO-style preference alignment methods for text-to-image diffusion models treat the entire reverse denoising chain as a single action under a contextual bandit assumption, ignoring the inherently sequential generation process. This sparse-reward formulation—where feedback is only available after the full trajectory—is known to cause high gradient variance and low sample efficiency. The paper asks whether a finer-grained dense-reward perspective, specifically one that emphasizes the initial denoising steps responsible for establishing image structure, can improve both the efficacy and efficiency of preference alignment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt together with pairs of generated trajectories (preferred vs. non-preferred, ranked by open-source scorers such as ImageReward or HPSv2) as the training signal, and produces 512×512 RGB images via a LoRA-adapted Stable Diffusion 1.5 U-net. A practical distinction from prior DPO-style T2I work is that the full 50-step reverse chain (all intermediate latents in bfloat16) must be stored rather than only the final image, roughly doubling CPU memory usage. Preference is derived from binary pairwise comparisons of generated images, so no explicit scalar reward model is trained or required at inference time.

## Method

The core contribution is reformulating T2I preference alignment as an MDP with a latent dense reward at each denoising step and introducing a temporal discount factor γ < 1 into the DPO-style loss to break the temporal symmetry that treats all timesteps equally. By proving that reward-shaping invariance (optimal policy is unchanged under state-dependent reward shifts) allows the intractable partition functions in the Bradley-Terry likelihood to be bounded, the authors derive a tractable negative-log-likelihood loss in which each timestep's log-likelihood ratio is weighted by γ^t, giving earlier steps—where image outlines and high-level composition are established—disproportionately larger gradient updates. Training is off-policy: trajectories are periodically collected from the current policy, and LoRA parameters are optimized on mini-batches of stored trajectory pairs with KL regularization toward the frozen pretrained model to avoid generating unnatural images. Setting γ = 1 recovers the classical trajectory-level DPO objective, making the proposed loss a strict generalization that reduces to the bandit assumption when no temporal asymmetry is desired.

## Results

On the HPSv2 multiple-prompt benchmark (3200 test prompts), the proposed method achieves an average HPSv2 score of 30.09 and Aesthetic score of 6.31, outperforming the best prior baseline Dreamlike Photoreal 2.0 (27.86 HPSv2) by over 2.2 points and the trajectory-level reward variant (γ=1, i.e., classical DPO-style) by 1.05 points. On the single-prompt DPOK setting, the method improves both ImageReward and Aesthetic scores over DPOK and the trajectory-level reward baseline. Human evaluation confirms a 76.8% win rate over SD1.5, 68.3% over Dreamlike Photoreal 2.0, and 65.1% over the trajectory-level reward variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (γ=0.9) outperforms all baselines on both HPSv2 and Aesthetic scores in the multiple-prompt experiment. Against Stable Diffusion v1.5 (the base model), it gains +2.97 HPSv2 average and +0.69 Aesthetic. Against DALL·E2, the gain is +3.14 HPSv2. Against the strongest pre-trained checkpoint Dreamlike Photoreal 2.0, it leads by +2.23 HPSv2. Most critically, against the trajectory-level reward variant (γ=1, equivalent to classical DPO-style alignment), the dense-reward method with temporal discounting achieves +1.05 HPSv2 and +0.37 Aesthetic, validating the core hypothesis that emphasizing early denoising steps improves alignment. In the single-prompt DPOK setting, the method also surpasses DPOK on both ImageReward and Aesthetic metrics, and human evaluation confirms a 65.1% win rate over the trajectory-level reward baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO-style explicit-reward-free alignment framework from LLMs (Rafailov et al., 2023) and its T2I adaptation (Wallace et al., 2023a) by replacing the trajectory-level bandit reward assumption with a dense, per-step reward perspective. The key conceptual departure is introducing a temporal discounting factor γ < 1 into the DPO-style loss, which breaks the temporal symmetry inherent in prior trajectory-level formulations and aligns the training signal with the hierarchical generation process of diffusion models (outlines first, details later). The theoretical derivation draws on classical RL tools—reward shaping (Ng et al., 1999), off-policy optimization, and KL-regularized policy search—to obtain a tractable lower bound on the Bradley-Terry likelihood that cancels intractable partition functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, explicit-reward-free method for T2I preference alignment that only requires binary image comparisons, avoiding the need to train or backpropagate through an explicit reward model. The finding that even a small break from temporal symmetry (γ=0.99) improves training efficiency over the classical γ=1 DPO-style loss suggests the dense-reward perspective is broadly applicable. Limitations include the need to store full generation latents (roughly 2× CPU memory overhead) and the off-policy iteration between data collection and training. Open questions include extending the method to noisy preference labels, text-to-video generation, and whether the temporal discounting insight transfers to other sequential generative processes.
