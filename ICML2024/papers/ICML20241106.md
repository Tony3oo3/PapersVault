## Problem

In PU learning, the straightforward disambiguation-free approach of treating all unlabeled instances as pseudo-negatives suffers from a boundary deviation phenomenon where the learned decision boundary shifts toward the positive side, degrading performance. This paper identifies a missing theoretical link: how the probability boundary of the asymmetric disambiguation-free expected risk relates to its asymmetric penalty, and leverages this to design a principled method for controlling where the boundary is placed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of labeled positive image instances (1,000 per dataset) and a large pool of unlabeled images (ranging from ~5,000 to ~104,500 depending on the dataset, at resolutions from 28×28 to 224×224). Beyond the final binary class prediction, the method produces an intermediate stochastic label for each unlabeled instance—a continuous value in (0,1) representing its estimated class posterior probability—generated by locating the instance within the probability boundary fence formed by multiple weak classifiers.

## Method

PUL-CPBF is a two-stage method grounded in Theorem 2.1, which proves that the probability boundary α of the asymmetric disambiguation-free expected risk is a monotonic function of the asymmetric penalty β (α = βπ(1−π̂)/((1−β)π̂)). In stage 1, the method trains m weak binary classifiers (sharing a frozen pre-trained backbone, each with its own classification layer) by minimizing asymmetric disambiguation-free empirical risks at m different β values corresponding to α ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9}; these classifiers form a probability boundary fence, and each unlabeled instance's posterior probability is bracketed by the pair of adjacent classifiers that disagree on its prediction, from which a stochastic label is drawn uniformly. In stage 2, a strong classifier is trained in a self-training manner over the labeled positives and all unlabeled instances with their stochastic labels, using cross-entropy loss with iterative label sharpening (temperature-scaled prediction blending) and FixMatch-style consistency regularization with data augmentation.

## Results

On the six benchmark PU datasets (F-MNIST-1/2, CIFAR-10-1/2, STL-10-1/2), PUL-CPBF achieves classification accuracy of 96.7%, 96.5%, 91.4%, 91.0%, 83.4%, and 85.4% respectively, consistently outperforming all PU learning baselines. On the real-world Alzheimer dataset, PUL-CPBF attains 73.4% accuracy, 75.3% F1, and 81.1% AUC. The performance gap to the fully supervised PN learning upper bound is only 0.5–2.6% across all datasets, and PUL-CPBF is more time-efficient than HolisticPU (e.g., 213s vs. 415s on F-MNIST-1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PUL-CPBF outperforms all 10 PU learning baselines across all 6 benchmark datasets. The closest competitor is HolisticPU, which PUL-CPBF beats by 0.5–1.4% accuracy (e.g., 96.7 vs. 96.2 on F-MNIST-1, 83.4 vs. 82.5 on STL-10-1). Against Dist-PU, the gap is larger at 2.7–10.2% (e.g., 91.4 vs. 87.8 on CIFAR-10-1, 91.0 vs. 80.8 on CIFAR-10-2). P³Mix-C, the strongest mixup-based baseline, trails by 2.5–4.5% (e.g., 96.7 vs. 92.8 on F-MNIST-1). On the Alzheimer dataset, PUL-CPBF achieves the best F1 (75.3), accuracy (73.4), precision (71.4), and AUC (81.1) among all PU methods, surpassing HolisticPU (F1 74.5, AUC 77.1) and Dist-PU (F1 73.7, AUC 77.1). In terms of efficiency, PUL-CPBF runs in ~213s on F-MNIST-1 versus ~415s for HolisticPU and ~48s for Dist-PU, making it roughly 2× faster than HolisticPU while only ~4.5× slower than Dist-PU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the disambiguation-free expected risk framework for PU learning (du Plessis et al., 2014) by introducing an asymmetric penalty and proving that the resulting Bayes optimal classifier's probability boundary is analytically controlled by that penalty (Theorem 2.1). Conceptually, PUL-CPBF bridges the two dominant branches of PU learning—cost-sensitive risk correction (uPU, nnPU, Dist-PU) and pseudo-labeling/self-training (PULNS, HolisticPU)—by using the asymmetric risk to train a set of weak classifiers at different probability thresholds, which collectively form a 'probability boundary fence' for generating stochastic labels. The self-training stage then borrows techniques from semi-supervised learning (FixMatch-style consistency regularization, temperature sharpening), positioning the method as a hybrid that leverages theoretical guarantees from one branch and practical training tricks from the other.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical result (Theorem 2.1) provides a principled, interpretable mechanism for controlling the classification boundary in PU learning, directly addressing the well-known disambiguation-free boundary deviation problem. Practically, PUL-CPBF narrows the gap to fully supervised PN learning to just 0.5–2.6% accuracy across diverse benchmarks, and its two-stage design is modular—any standard semi-supervised trick can be plugged into the second stage. Open questions include whether the probability boundary fence concept generalizes to multi-class or multi-label PU settings, whether the method's dependence on accurate π estimation (via KM2) limits its applicability in highly imbalanced or noisy real-world scenarios, and whether the stochastic label refinement loop converges reliably in deeper network regimes.
