## Problem

Contextual inverse linear programming (CILP) requires learning a model that predicts unknown LP cost vectors from contextual features so that solving the LP with predicted costs reproduces known optimal decisions. The core obstacle is that LP solutions are piecewise-constant in the cost vector, making the decision non-differentiable and precluding standard auto-differentiation. Prior methods (e.g., Sun et al. 2023) provided convergence guarantees only under strong assumptions of LP non-degeneracy and data interpolation, limiting practical applicability.

## Input / Output

The method takes contextual features z (RGB Warcraft tileset images for shortest path, grayscale MNIST digit grids for perfect matching) along with known LP constraint matrices (A, b) and known optimal decisions x*, and produces a predicted cost vector c (edge weights) via a ResNet-18 backbone followed by a softplus activation to enforce non-negativity. The predicted cost vector is then fed into the LP solver to obtain a decision x̂(c), and training minimizes the discrepancy between x̂(c) and x*. Unlike predict-and-optimize baselines such as SPO+, the method does not require access to ground-truth cost vectors c* during training.

## Method

The key insight is that for a linear prediction model, the KKT optimality conditions define a convex (affine) set C of cost vectors that reproduce x*, and the set F of cost vectors realizable by the model is also convex, reducing CILP to finding a point in C∩F. Algorithm 1 solves this via alternating projections (POCS) between C and F, achieving linear convergence without requiring LP non-degeneracy or data interpolation. For scalability, the feasibility problem is further reduced to empirical risk minimization on a smooth convex loss h(θ) = (1/2N)Σᵢ min_{q∈Cᵢ} ‖f_θ(zᵢ) − q‖² that satisfies the Polyak-Lojasiewicz condition, enabling efficient first-order methods (SGD, Adam, AdaGrad) with provable convergence rates. The paper also introduces a scale-invariant sub-optimality metric Γ that uses the projection of the predicted cost onto C as a proxy for the unknown ground-truth cost, avoiding the degenerate behavior of prior metrics where arbitrarily small cost vectors artificially reduce the gap.

## Results

On the real-world Warcraft Shortest Path (12×12 and 18×18) and Perfect Matching (12×12 MNIST) tasks, the proposed method (using ResNet-18 + softplus, trained with Adam/AdaGrad for 50 epochs) consistently outperforms all baselines by a considerable margin on decision-loss (Eq. 17) across both tasks. On estimate-loss (Eq. 16), the method outperforms ST, BB, and MOM, and is comparable to SPO+ (which has access to the ground-truth cost vector c*). On synthetic shortest path and fractional knapsack problems, the method significantly outperforms ST, QPTL, BB, and MOM on both metrics, while remaining comparable to SPO+. The POCS and ERM variants show negligible performance differences, validating the theoretical equivalence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines: ST (Sahoo et al., 2022), MOM (Sun et al., 2023), BB (Vlastelica et al., 2019), QPTL (Wilder et al., 2019), and SPO+ (Bertsimas & Kallus, 2020). The proposed method wins decisively on decision-loss against all baselines on both real-world tasks, with the gap being most pronounced against ST and BB. On estimate-loss, the method beats ST, BB, and MOM but is comparable to SPO+ on Shortest Path; notably, SPO+ requires access to the ground-truth cost vector c*, which the proposed method does not. MOM's reported performance is actually overestimated because its implementation uses ground-truth c* to compute the basis, yet the proposed method still outperforms it. QPTL is excluded from real-world experiments due to prohibitive computational cost (it requires solving a QP per gradient step via implicit differentiation). The proposed method also matches or exceeds MOM's convergence guarantees without requiring the non-degeneracy or interpolation assumptions that MOM depends on.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the contextual inverse linear programming (CILP) framework established by Sun et al. (2023) and Besbes et al. (2023), but departs from both by using KKT optimality conditions rather than reduced-cost conditions to define the feasible set of cost vectors. Unlike prior differentiable-optimization approaches (BB, ST, QPTL, OptNet) that attempt to backpropagate through the LP or regularize it, the paper circumvents the non-differentiability entirely by reducing CILP to a convex feasibility problem (solved via alternating projections) and then to ERM on a smooth convex loss satisfying the Polyak-Lojasiewicz condition. This positioning avoids the non-degeneracy and interpolation assumptions required by Sun et al. (2023) and the realizability assumption of Besbes et al. (2023), while also avoiding the hyperparameter sensitivity of regularization-based methods like QPTL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction to ERM with PL guarantees enables the use of scalable stochastic first-order methods (SGD, Adam, AdaGrad) for large-scale CILP problems, which is a practical advantage over exact projection-based methods that require full-dataset passes. The proposed scale-invariant sub-optimality metric addresses a genuine gap in how CILP performance is measured, since prior metrics (Γ₁, Γ₂) are either gameable or require ground-truth costs. Open questions include extending the framework to unknown constraints, handling non-linear convex objectives more broadly, and reducing the computational cost of the per-iteration QP projection, which remains the bottleneck for very large problems.
