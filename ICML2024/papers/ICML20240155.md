## Problem

Smoothed online quadratic optimization (SOQO) has been studied almost exclusively from an adversarial perspective, leaving the stochastic setting entirely unexplored. This paper asks two foundational questions: (1) what is the structure of the online-optimal policy when the hitting-cost minimizers follow a stochastic process, and (2) can a single algorithm achieve near-optimal performance in both stochastic and adversarial environments simultaneously?

## Input / Output

At each round t, the algorithm observes the current minimizer v_t ∈ R^d (revealed online), the known positive definite matrix A, and the previous action x_{t-1}, then outputs an action x_t ∈ R^d. The action space is unbounded (R^d), which distinguishes SOQO from standard OCO and rules out bounded-diameter assumptions. Performance is measured as dynamic regret relative to the online-optimal LAI benchmark in the stochastic (martingale) setting, and as competitive ratio relative to the hindsight-optimal sequence in the adversarial setting — a deliberate choice of different metrics reflecting the fundamentally different worst-case behaviors of the two environments.

## Method

The paper derives the online-optimal stochastic algorithm (Lazy Adaptive Interpolation, LAI) via dynamic programming, obtaining a closed-form interpolation x_t = C_t x_{t-1} + (I - C_t) v_t where the matrix sequence {C_t} satisfies the recursion C_t^{-1} = 2I + A - C_{t+1} with terminal condition C_T = (I+A)^{-1}. This recursion is the key structural insight: it makes the optimal policy distribution-agnostic (depending only on A and T) and yields O(T) total cost under martingale minimizers. The paper then shows the adversarial-optimal ROBD algorithm suffers Ω(T) regret in stochastic settings because its fixed matrix C_ROBD cannot match the time-varying {C_t}. Finally, LAI(γ) is constructed by modifying the terminal condition to interpolate between LAI (γ=0) and a fixed interpolation with C = C_L (γ=1), achieving constant stochastic regret and a competitive ratio of O(√(κ(A)/λ_min^A)) in the adversarial regime, thus providing best-of-both-worlds guarantees without knowing the environment type in advance.

## Results

The paper establishes that LAI (Lazy Adaptive Interpolation) is the unique online-optimal algorithm in the stochastic (martingale) setting, achieving total cost O(T) that is insensitive to the distribution beyond variance. In contrast, the adversarial-optimal ROBD algorithm suffers Ω(T) linear regret relative to LAI in the martingale setting. The best-of-both-worlds algorithm LAI(γ) with γ=1 achieves a competitive ratio of 1 + √(κ(A)/λ_min^A) in the adversarial regime (λ_min^A ≪ 1), which is near-optimal compared to ROBD's 1 + O(1/√λ_min^A), while simultaneously maintaining constant regret (bounded by σ²/4 · ((1+4/λ_min^A)^{1/2}−1)/(λ_min^A+2)) in the stochastic setting. Numerical experiments confirm LAI(1) has virtually zero regret across light-tail and heavy-tail distributions, and outperforms ROBD until approximately 20% adversarial contamination in mixed environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is ROBD (Goel et al., 2019), the adversarially optimal algorithm for smoothed online optimization. In the stochastic (martingale) setting, LAI(1) decisively outperforms ROBD: LAI(1) achieves constant (near-zero) regret while ROBD incurs Ω(T) linear regret, as confirmed by Theorem 3.5 and the regret plots in Figure 1 across uniform, normal, Laplace, logistic, Gumbel, log-normal, and Pareto distributions. In the adversarial setting, LAI(1) with γ=1 achieves a competitive ratio of 1 + √(κ(A)/λ_min^A), which is within a factor of √κ(A) of ROBD's optimal 1 + O(1/√λ_min^A) for well-conditioned A matrices (Corollary 3.12). In mixed adversarial-stochastic experiments (Figure 2), LAI(1) maintains superior performance over ROBD until roughly 20% adversarial contamination, after which ROBD takes over. The paper also shows that static algorithms (a common benchmark in OCO) are fundamentally inadequate, incurring Θ(T²) cost versus LAI's O(T), motivating the use of the dynamic online-optimal LAI as the stochastic benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the smoothed online optimization (SOO) literature, which has been studied almost exclusively from an adversarial perspective (Bansal et al. 2015; Chen et al. 2018; Goel et al. 2019), by introducing the first stochastic analysis of the SOQO problem class. The adversarial-optimal ROBD algorithm (Goel et al. 2019) serves as the primary baseline, and the best-of-both-worlds framing is inspired by Bubeck & Slivkins (2012) in multi-arm bandits, though the SOQO setting differs fundamentally in its unbounded continuous action space and dynamic (rather than static) performance benchmark. The dynamic programming machinery used to characterize the stochastic optimal algorithm departs from the potential-function and receding-horizon techniques that dominate the adversarial SOO literature, and the authors note that such closed-form DP solutions are generally intractable for non-quadratic cost functions (Lin et al. 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a fundamental structural incompatibility between adversarial and stochastic optimality in SOQO: the adversarial-optimal ROBD suffers linear regret under martingale inputs, while the stochastic-optimal LAI is competitively suboptimal against adversarial inputs. This resolves a long-standing average-case vs. worst-case trade-off question for this problem class and provides a practical best-of-both-worlds algorithm (LAI(γ)) that practitioners can deploy without knowing the environment's nature. Open questions highlighted include extending the analysis beyond quadratic costs (where DP solutions become intractable), multi-agent settings with coupled costs, and learning the cost matrix A online. The results are confined to the quadratic setting and finite-variance martingale assumptions, limiting direct transfer to more general stochastic or non-convex environments.
