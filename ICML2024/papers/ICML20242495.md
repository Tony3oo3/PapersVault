## Problem

Lightweight image super-resolution methods focus on architecture design but neglect to further reduce network redundancy through pruning. Existing compression techniques—neural architecture search, knowledge distillation, and channel pruning—either demand substantial extra computational resources (large teacher networks, search budgets) or only prune channels while leaving redundant kernel weights intact. This paper addresses the gap of simultaneously pruning both channels and weights without requiring pretrained models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242495/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image and produces a high-resolution super-resolved image at ×2, ×3, or ×4 scale. During training, 64×64 LR color patches are processed in batches of 16 with standard data augmentation (90° rotations and horizontal flips). Evaluation is performed on the Y channel of YCbCr space using PSNR and SSIM, with FLOPs computed at a 3×1280×720 output size for fair comparison.

## Method

FMP introduces a hypernetwork whose parameters serve as meta-data for the SR backbone (LSRB or EDSR). Each backbone layer is assigned a channel vector (controlling output/input channel counts for structured pruning) and a weight indicator tensor (a continuous mask reflecting per-weight strength for unstructured pruning). The hypernetwork computes a grid from the outer product of current and previous-layer channel vectors, applies two linear transformations to each grid element, reshapes the result into kernel weights, and multiplies element-wise by the weight indicator. Channel vectors are optimized via proximal gradient descent with L1 soft-thresholding, while weight indicators and hypernetwork parameters are updated by SGD with L1 sparsity regularization. The pruning stage halts when a target compression ratio is reached, after which channels and weights are sparsified and the corresponding backbone parameters are removed, yielding a flexible mix of structured and unstructured sparsity.

## Results

FMP achieves the best PSNR and SSIM on all five benchmark datasets (Set5, Set14, B100, Urban100, Manga109) across all three upscaling factors (×2, ×3, ×4). At ×2, FMP reaches 38.17 dB / 0.9615 SSIM on Set5 and 32.71 dB / 0.9360 SSIM on Urban100, outperforming the second-best ASSLN by 0.05 dB and 0.0051 SSIM respectively. The model maintains a compact footprint of ~694K parameters and 153.7G FLOPs at ×2, slightly fewer FLOPs than IMDN (158.8G) and ASSLN (159.1G) while delivering higher quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FMP outperforms all 16 compared baselines on every dataset and scale. Against the closest competitor ASSLN (channel pruning from a pretrained model), FMP gains 0.05–0.30 dB PSNR depending on dataset and scale, with the largest margin on Urban100 (e.g., 32.71 vs 32.41 dB at ×2). Compared to NAS-based methods (MoreMNAS-A, FALSR-A) and knowledge distillation (CARN+KD), FMP achieves higher PSNR with fewer parameters (~694K vs ~1,021–1,592K) and lower FLOPs (153.7G vs 222.8–238.6G at ×2), without requiring a teacher network or architecture search. In the flexible-vs-channel-pruning ablation (Table 4), FMP consistently beats DHP (channel-only pruning) by 0.02–0.09 dB across 20–60% pruning ratios on EDSR-8-128, confirming the benefit of jointly pruning weights. FMP also matches or exceeds LatticeNet and DIPNet in PSNR while using fewer FLOPs (153.7G vs 169.5G and 153.7G vs comparable at ×2). The LSRB backbone itself runs 7–33% faster in inference than RLFN (12 ms vs 19 ms on Urban100) at comparable PSNR, validating the architectural simplification before pruning is applied.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DHP (Li et al., 2020a), which used a hypernetwork to predict backbone weights for differentiable channel pruning in SR, by adding a second input (weight indicators) that enables simultaneous unstructured weight pruning alongside structured channel pruning. The lightweight baseline LSRB is derived from RLFN (Kong et al., 2022) by simplifying its residual blocks and increasing the use of enhanced spatial attention. Conceptually, the work unifies two previously separate compression paradigms—structured and unstructured pruning—within a single hypernetwork framework, departing from prior SR pruning methods (ASSLN, SRP) that require pretrained models and from NAS/KD approaches that demand large search budgets or teacher networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint structured-and-unstructured pruning via a hypernetwork offers a practical path to deploying SR models on resource-constrained devices without the overhead of NAS search or KD teacher training, and the LSRB baseline demonstrates that simpler architectures can outperform more complex ones in inference speed. However, the method has only been validated on EDSR and LSRB (both CNN-based), leaving open whether it generalizes to Transformer-based SR models like SwinIR. The authors also note the absence of standardized benchmarks specifically for pruning in SR, and the plug-and-play applicability of the technique to arbitrary pre-trained SR networks remains unexplored.
