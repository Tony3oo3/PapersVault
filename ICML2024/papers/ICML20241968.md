## Problem

Nearest centroid classifier (NCC) losses, widely used in cross-domain few-shot classification, produce representations where samples from different classes retain undesirably high similarities, inducing uncertainty and misclassification. Existing methods implicitly leverage label information via class centroids but lack a principled mechanism to simultaneously maximize intra-class and minimize inter-class similarities. The paper identifies that kernel HSIC measures can capture this dependence structure but may fail to do so accurately without proper kernel tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241968/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a vary-way vary-shot task sampled from an unseen domain, the method receives a support set of a few labeled images (variable number of classes and shots per class) and a query set of unlabeled images. A pre-trained ResNet-18 backbone (distilled from 8 domain-specific backbones via URL) extracts embeddings, which are then transformed by a lightweight linear head initialized as identity. The method outputs class labels for query samples by measuring similarity to class prototypes, with the linear head optimized per episode using Adadelta.

## Method

MOKD formulates a bi-level optimization over optimized kernel HSIC measures. The inner level selects the Gaussian kernel bandwidth via grid search to maximize test power (HSIC/√variance), making the dependence measure more sensitive to actual statistical dependence. The outer level then minimizes a surrogate loss that maximizes HSIC(Z,Y) (dependence between representations and labels, encouraging class-specific structure) while penalizing HSIC(Z,Z) (dependence among all representations, acting as a regularizer against overfitting). The key theoretical contribution shows that the NCC-based loss is lower-bounded by a combination of HSIC(Z,Y) and HSIC(Z,Z), providing a principled bridge from prototype-based classification to dependence optimization. This yields class-specific representations where intra-class similarities are maximized and inter-class similarities are minimized, without increasing computational cost over the base URL pipeline.

## Results

Under the 'train on ImageNet only' setting, MOKD achieves an average accuracy of 67.3% across all 13 Meta-Dataset domains, outperforming the closest baseline URL (65.9%) by 1.4% and SSL-HSIC (62.0%) by 5.3%. On unseen domains specifically, MOKD reaches 68.1% average accuracy versus 66.6% for URL, demonstrating stronger cross-domain generalization. Under the 'train on all datasets' setting, MOKD achieves 76.3% average accuracy (rank 2.2), surpassing URL (75.8%) and 2LM (75.7%). MOKD wins on 10 out of 13 datasets in both settings, with the largest gains on Quick Draw (+3.3% over URL) and MSCOCO (+2.3% over URL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOKD is built on top of URL (Li et al., ICCV 2021), which distills a universal backbone from 8 domain-specific ResNet-18 models and fine-tunes a linear head per episode. MOKD replaces URL's NCC-based loss with a bi-level HSIC objective, yielding consistent gains: +1.4% average over URL in the ImageNet-only setting and +0.5% in the all-datasets setting, with the largest per-dataset improvements on Quick Draw (+3.3%) and MSCOCO (+2.3%). Against SSL-HSIC (Li et al., NeurIPS 2021), which shares a similar HSIC-based outer objective but lacks test power maximization, MOKD outperforms by 5.3% (ImageNet-only) and 1.7% (all-datasets), confirming that optimizing kernel bandwidth for test power is critical. MOKD also surpasses the strongest meta-learning baselines—ProtoNets-large (53.7% avg), ALFA+FP-MAML (52.8%), and FLUTE (46.9%)—by 13–20 points in the ImageNet-only setting. In the all-datasets setting, MOKD edges out 2LM (Qin et al., CVPR 2023) by 0.6% and URT (Liu et al., ICLR 2021) by 4.5% on average, though it trails URT on Quick Draw (82.2% vs. 82.4%) where URT's attention head captures fine-grained doodle features better. The only notable failure case is Fungi under the all-datasets setting, where MOKD (68.6%) slightly underperforms URL (68.7%), likely due to overfitting on this visually complex dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOKD builds directly on the URL pipeline (Li et al., 2021a), which distills a universal backbone from domain-specific pre-trained models and adapts a linear head per task, and on SSL-HSIC (Li et al., 2021b), which optimizes kernel dependence for representation learning. The key conceptual departure is reframing the NCC-based loss as a lower bound expressible in terms of HSIC(Z,Y) and HSIC(Z,Z), then introducing a bi-level optimization where the inner loop maximizes the test power of the Gaussian kernel (following Jitkrittum et al., 2016) before the outer loop optimizes dependence. Unlike SSL-HSIC, which is derived from unsupervised contrastive learning, MOKD is explicitly supervised and targets class-specific cluster structures indicated by labels in few-shot tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical bridge between NCC loss and HSIC provides a principled justification for using dependence measures in few-shot adaptation, and the test power maximization step addresses a practical weakness of kernel methods—insufficient sensitivity to dependence with small support sets. However, improvements over URL are modest (~1.4–1.5% on unseen domains) and the method still inherits URL's reliance on a pre-trained ResNet-18 backbone, limiting its applicability to settings where such pre-training is available. Open questions include whether the bi-level structure can be made more computationally efficient for larger support sets, whether test power maximization generalizes beyond the Meta-Dataset benchmark, and whether the approach extends to other meta-learning paradigms (e.g., gradient-based methods like MAML).
