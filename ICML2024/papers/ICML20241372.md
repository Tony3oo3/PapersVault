## Problem

Cross-modal transfer leverages pretrained models (e.g., vision transformers) for tasks in entirely different modalities (audio, ECG, PDEs), but it remains unclear what knowledge actually transfers and why some modality pairs benefit while others do not. The paper identifies that the degree of modality semantic knowledge discrepancy—formalized as divergence between conditional distributions P(Y|X) across modalities—determines whether source knowledge is effectively reused or actively distorted during finetuning, a factor previously overlooked by the universal-encoder assumption.

## Input / Output

The method consumes target modality data in either 2D form (images, spectrograms, PDE grids at 224×224 or 128×128) or 1D form (EMG signals, ECG traces, genetic sequences) along with pretrained source model weights (Swin Transformer or RoBERTa). A modality-specific embedder projects raw inputs into a shared token space (128-dim for 2D, 768-dim for 1D) before the frozen-initialized transformer encoder processes them. The outer-loop meta objective additionally requires a small labeled source dataset (CIFAR-10 or CoNLL-2003) to evaluate source discriminability, and outputs are either class logits or dense spatial predictions (PDE solutions, protein distances, cosmic-ray masks) depending on the task.

## Method

MoNA is a two-stage meta-learning framework. In Stage 1, a bi-level optimization (MAML-style single-step inner loop) trains only the target embedder: the inner loop virtually finetunes the full model on target data, while the outer loop measures how well the resulting encoder preserves source-modality discriminability via alignment-and-uniformity loss on source features, combined with the target task loss through a trade-off parameter λ. The theoretical analysis (Appendix A.3) shows this objective maximizes the dot product between source and target loss gradients with respect to the encoder, effectively aligning their update directions so that target adaptation does not destroy source knowledge. In Stage 2, the learned embedder initializes a standard full finetuning pass over all parameters. This contrasts with prior two-stage methods (ORCA, Embedder warmup) that use optimal-transport or task-loss-only objectives in the first stage, which the paper shows are less effective at reducing knowledge misalignment.

## Results

On NAS-Bench-360, MoNA achieves the best or near-best performance on all 10 tasks, with headline improvements over ORCA including 27.13% error on Spherical (vs. 29.85%), 0.99 MAE on PSICOV (vs. 1.91), and 0.121 AUROC on Cosmic (vs. 0.152). On PDEBench, MoNA achieves state-of-the-art nRMSE on 4 out of 8 PDE tasks, outperforming ORCA on 7 tasks with notable gains on Advection (0.0088 vs. 0.0098) and Navier-Stokes (0.054 vs. 0.062). On additional benchmarks, MoNA reaches 9.64% error on ESC50 (vs. 12.75% for ORCA) and 13.45% error on UCF101 (vs. 16.86% for ORCA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoNA outperforms ORCA (Shen et al., 2023), the strongest prior cross-modal transfer method, on all 10 NAS-Bench-360 tasks, with the largest gains on PSICOV (0.99 vs. 1.91 MAE, ~48% reduction) and Spherical (27.13% vs. 29.85% error). Against FPT (Lu et al., 2022) and Perceiver IO (Jaegle et al., 2022), MoNA achieves substantially lower errors across nearly all tasks (e.g., 6.48% vs. 10.11% and 70.04% on CIFAR-100). The Frozen-encoder approach (Zhang et al., 2023b) performs poorly on cross-modal tasks (35.20% on NinaPro), confirming that freezing the encoder is ineffective when modality knowledge is misaligned. On PDEBench, MoNA beats PINN (Raissi et al., 2019) on all 8 tasks and is competitive with the specialized FNO (Li et al., 2020), achieving lower nRMSE on 5 of 8 equations. U-Net (Ronneberger et al., 2015) is outperformed by MoNA on all PDEBench tasks where both are reported. MoNA also surpasses DASH (Shen et al., 2022) and the NAS-Bench-360 baseline (Tu et al., 2022) on most tasks, though DASH retains a slight edge on FSD50K mAP (0.60 vs. 0.55).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-modal transfer pipeline established by ORCA (Shen et al., 2023) and FPT (Lu et al., 2022), which treat pretrained transformers as universal encoders adaptable to any target modality via modality-specific embedders and predictors. The key departure is a diagnostic: the authors empirically show that the 'universal encoder' assumption is modality-dependent, formalizing the failure mode as a divergence between source and target conditional distributions P(Y|X). Methodologically, MoNA repurposes MAML-style bi-level optimization (Finn et al., 2017) to learn the target embedder in a first stage, using the alignment-and-uniformity loss (Wang & Isola, 2020) on source data as the outer-loop objective—replacing ORCA's optimal-transport alignment with a meta-learning signal that directly optimizes for source knowledge preservation during subsequent finetuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The formalization of modality semantic knowledge discrepancy provides a principled lens for understanding when and why cross-modal transfer succeeds or fails, moving the field beyond purely empirical trial-and-error. This is practically relevant for data-scarce scientific domains (PDEs, ECG, protein structure) where leveraging vision or language pretrained models is the only viable path. However, the approach inherits ORCA's reliance on surrogate source datasets (CIFAR-10, CoNLL-2003) for the outer-loop loss, and the single-step inner-loop simplification limits the meta-learning signal; the paper itself flags evaluating different source modalities and pretrained models as future work. The modest but consistent gains (1–9% relative over ORCA) suggest the framework is sound but not yet transformative.
