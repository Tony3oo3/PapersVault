## Problem

Existing algorithms for empirical Group Distributionally Robust Optimization (GDRO) either suffer from a quadratic dependence on the target accuracy ε (e.g., SMD at O(m ln m / ε²)) or fail to exploit the two-level finite-sum structure of the objective, incurring an unnecessary √m factor in their complexity (e.g., MPVR at O(mn̄ + m√(mn̄ ln m)/ε)). This paper closes that gap by deriving an O(m√(n̄ ln m)/ε) computation complexity, improving the state of the art by a factor of √m, and extends the result to the empirical Minimax Excess Risk Optimization (MERO) problem.

## Input / Output

The method consumes m groups of training samples {ξ_ij} with group sizes n_i, a per-sample loss function ℓ(w; ξ) assumed L-smooth and G-Lipschitz, and algorithmic hyperparameters (epoch count S, per-epoch iterations K_s, learning rates η_k^s, and weights α_k^s). It outputs a model parameter vector w* minimizing the maximal empirical risk across all m groups, a dual variable q* on the (m−1)-simplex representing the worst-case group distribution, and—when solving MERO—estimated minimal empirical risks R̂_i* for each group. The two-level structure (m outer groups, n_i inner samples) is the key structural assumption that the algorithm exploits.

## Method

ALEG is a double-looped stochastic primal-dual algorithm built on a modified mirror prox routine with variance reduction. Its central innovation is a group sampling strategy that draws exactly one sample from each of the m groups per iteration, which eliminates the randomness at the outer summation level and reduces the Lipschitz constant of the stochastic gradient by a factor of 1/m compared to uniform or importance sampling. Within each group, an SVRG-style correction (stochastic gradient at the current point minus stochastic gradient at the snapshot plus full gradient at the snapshot) provides variance reduction. The algorithm further employs 'negative momentum' in the dual space via one-index-shifted weighted averages to construct snapshot points and Lyapunov functions, enabling support for non-constant learning rates. For the MERO problem, ALEM applies ALEG in two stages: first running m independent ALEG instances to estimate each group's minimal empirical risk R_i*, then solving the resulting approximated GDRO problem with ALEG again, achieving a near-matching Õ(m√(n̄ ln m)/ε) complexity.

## Results

ALEG achieves an O(m√(n̄ ln m)/ε) computation complexity for empirical GDRO, improving the state-of-the-art MPVR rate O(mn̄ + m√(mn̄ ln m)/ε) by a factor of √m. For empirical MERO, ALEM attains Õ(m√(n̄ ln m)/ε), matching the GDRO rate up to logarithmic factors. On CIFAR-100 (m=100 groups, linear softmax classifier), ALEG converges to a lower max empirical risk than SMD, MPVR, and AL-SVRE in both training and test settings, with notably faster convergence in terms of stochastic gradient evaluations. ALEM similarly outperforms ERMEG and TSA on both synthetic and CIFAR-100 MERO tasks in convergence speed and stability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALEG outperforms all four GDRO baselines in computation complexity. Compared to SMD, ALEG eliminates the quadratic ε-dependency, winning whenever ε ≤ O(√(ln m/n̄)). Against MPVR, ALEG removes the √m factor via its group sampling strategy that reduces the stochastic gradient Lipschitz constant by 1/m. AL-SVRE is worse by a factor of √m·ln(m/ε), and BROO-KX is less favorable under the common regime m ≤ O(n̄). For empirical MERO, ALEM (Õ(m√(n̄ ln m)/ε)) dominates both ERMEG (O(mn̄ ln(mn̄)/ε²)) and TSA (O(m ln m/ε²)) by removing the quadratic ε-dependency. In the CIFAR-100 experiments, ALEG achieves lower max empirical risk with fewer gradient evaluations than all competitors, and ALEM converges more rapidly and stably than ERMEG and TSA on both training and test sets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mirror Prox with Variance Reduction (MPVR) framework of Alacaoglu & Malitsky (2022) by exploiting the two-level finite-sum structure specific to empirical GDRO, which prior methods (MPVR, AL-SVRE) treat as a generic one-level finite-sum problem. The key departure is a group sampling strategy that draws one sample per group per iteration, reducing the stochastic gradient's Lipschitz constant by a factor of 1/m and yielding a √m complexity improvement. The alterable-hyperparameter mechanism via one-index-shifted weighted averages is a practical departure from the fixed-parameter analyses in prior variance-reduced mirror prox methods. For empirical MERO, the paper builds on the two-stage schema of Zhang et al. (2024) but replaces their stochastic approximation with the faster ALEG solver.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The √m complexity improvement is practically significant for group-robust learning scenarios with many groups (federated learning with many clients, robust classification with many categories), where m can be large. The alterable hyperparameters make ALEG more adaptable to real training dynamics, a feature absent from prior theoretical analyses of variance-reduced saddle-point methods. However, the results are confined to the convex-concave setting with linear models in experiments, leaving open whether the group sampling trick transfers to non-convex neural network training where most practical DRO applications live. The near-matching complexity for MERO over GDRO suggests the two-stage estimation overhead is essentially free, but this relies on the convexity assumption that may not hold in deep learning.
