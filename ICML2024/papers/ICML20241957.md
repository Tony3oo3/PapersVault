## Problem

Existing asymmetric kernel SVD (KSVD) formulations only support finite-dimensional feature mappings, have unbounded variational objectives, and lack empirical validation of their practical utility. Moreover, the Nyström method for accelerating kernel computations has only been formalized for symmetric Mercer kernels, leaving no principled way to scale KSVD to large datasets. The paper asks how to learn feature-space directions in an asymmetric setting while controlling computational complexity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241957/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two sample sets {x_i} from space X and {z_j} from space Z, which may have different dimensions; a compatibility matrix C (via PCA projection, random projection, pseudoinverse, or learnable parameters) aligns them so that a kernel κ(x,z) can be evaluated. The output consists of left and right singular vectors B_φ ∈ R^{n×r} and B_ψ ∈ R^{m×r} that parameterize r directions in a (possibly infinite-dimensional) feature space H, yielding projected feature scores for new data points. Notably, even when a symmetric kernel function like RBF is used, the resulting matrix G remains asymmetric because it is evaluated on two distinct input sets, and the feature maps are never explicitly computed.

## Method

The paper introduces the Coupled Covariances Eigenproblem (CCE), which seeks directions W_φ and W_ψ in a common feature space H satisfying Σ_φ W_ψ = Λ W_φ and Σ_ψ W_φ = Λ W_ψ, where Σ_φ and Σ_ψ are empirical covariance operators from two feature maps φ and ψ. By expressing the directions through projection operators and their adjoints, the CCE reduces exactly to the SVD of the asymmetric kernel matrix G with entries G_ij = ⟨φ(x_i), ψ(z_j)⟩/√(nm), providing a covariance-operator interpretation of KSVD that supports infinite-dimensional feature spaces. For efficiency, the paper formally derives an asymmetric Nyström method from the integral equations governing a pair of adjoint eigenfunctions (Schmidt, 1907), introducing scaling factors to match the orthonormal SVD solution and enabling out-of-sample extension via subsampling. This asymmetric Nyström method is shown to be the fastest solver among truncated SVD, randomized SVD, and symmetric Nyström applied to GG^T and G^T G, with speedups up to 32× on large problems.

## Results

On directed graph node classification, KSVD achieves the best Micro-F1 across all five datasets (e.g., 0.792 on Cora, 0.712 on TwitchPT, 0.710 on BlogCatalog), consistently outperforming SVD, KPCA, DeepWalk, HOPE, and DiGAE. For graph reconstruction, KSVD dramatically reduces the ℓ₁ error compared to SVD (e.g., 14.0 vs. 622.0 on Cora; 756.0 vs. 1839.0 on TwitchPT), showing that nonlinearity is critical. In biclustering, KSVD achieves the best NMI on ACM (0.68) and Wiki (0.48), matching or exceeding specialized methods BCOT and EBC. The asymmetric Nyström solver achieves up to 32.51× speedup over randomized SVD on PubMed (19,717 nodes) at tolerance ε=10⁻¹.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KSVD consistently outperforms both SVD and KPCA on node classification across all five directed graph datasets, confirming that the combination of nonlinearity (vs. SVD) and asymmetry (vs. KPCA) is essential; for example, on TwitchPT, KSVD achieves 0.712 Micro-F1 vs. 0.694 (SVD) and 0.681 (KPCA). On graph reconstruction, KSVD's ℓ₁ error is orders of magnitude lower than SVD (e.g., 14.0 vs. 622.0 on Cora), while KPCA sometimes matches or slightly beats KSVD on ℓ₂ (e.g., 13.3 vs. 14.3 on Citeseer), indicating that asymmetry alone is insufficient without nonlinearity. Against graph-specific methods, KSVD is competitive with HOPE and DiGAE on most datasets and clearly outperforms DeepWalk, despite being a simpler unsupervised method requiring only the adjacency matrix. In biclustering, KSVD achieves the best NMI on ACM (0.68) and Wiki (0.48), surpassing the specialized optimal-transport method BCOT (0.38, 0.48) and ensemble method EBC (0.62, 0.47) on ACM. For the asymmetric Nyström solver, the proposed method is the fastest across all tested problem sizes, achieving 1.71× speedup over RSVD on Cora (N=2708) and 32.51× on PubMed (N=19717), with the advantage growing with problem size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Suykens' (2016) variational formulation of Kernel SVD (KSVD) by grounding it in a coupled covariance eigenproblem (CCE) over covariance operators, thereby enabling infinite-dimensional feature maps that the original formulation could not handle. The asymmetric Nyström method is derived from the integral equations of adjoint eigenfunctions (Schmidt, 1907), providing a formal counterpart to the symmetric Nyström method of Williams & Seeger (2000) that was previously restricted to Mercer kernels. The CCE framework also clarifies the relationship between KSVD and symmetric approaches like KPCA and KCCA, showing that KSVD couples two feature mappings through a single asymmetric kernel rather than treating them independently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first empirical validation of KSVD's practical utility, filling a gap left by Suykens (2016) which provided no experiments. The asymmetric Nyström method achieves up to 32× speedup over randomized SVD on large-scale problems, making asymmetric kernel feature learning tractable for datasets with millions of samples. Open questions remain about when asymmetry genuinely outperforms symmetrization (the paper acknowledges this is problem-dependent), and the compatibility matrix C for non-square data, while practical, lacks a principled selection criterion beyond heuristics. The framework is primarily unsupervised; how CCE integrates with end-to-end supervised training beyond the learnable-C variant remains underexplored.
