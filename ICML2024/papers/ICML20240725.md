## Problem

Fast convolution algorithms (Winograd, FFT) and model quantization are individually effective for reducing inference cost, but combining them causes severe accuracy degradation: Winograd's ill-conditioned Vandermonde matrices amplify rounding errors, and FFT's irrational coefficients introduce significant quantization noise under low-precision formats. No existing method simultaneously achieves low arithmetic complexity, compatibility with low-precision (int8 or below) quantization, and maintained model accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard CNN convolution inputs: spatial-domain feature map tiles (e.g., 7×7) and 3×3 (or 5×5, 7×7) filter weights, producing the corresponding linear convolution output tile. A key design choice is that the 7×7 tile size aligns with ImageNet's factor-of-7 feature map dimensions, eliminating zero-padding waste. In the quantized pipeline, spatial-domain data is stored as int8 while transformation-domain data is quantized to int4, and the division by N in the inverse transform is fused into the model's floating-point scaling factors rather than computed at inference time.

## Method

SFC (Symbolic Fourier Convolution) extends the DFT with symbolic computing: complex roots of unity (e.g., e^{jπ/3}) are represented as first-order polynomials with integer coefficients (s² = s−1 for DFT-6), so the entire forward transform reduces to additions only, with transformation matrices containing exclusively 0, 1, and −1 entries. Element-wise multiplication in the transform domain is performed on these polynomial pairs using a 3-multiplication scheme (vs. 4 for naive polynomial multiply). A second key contribution introduces correction terms that convert the invalid wrap-around outputs of circular convolution into valid linear convolution results, recovering up to 73% of multiplications for 3×3 kernels (3.68× reduction vs. Winograd's 2.25×) while keeping the condition number κ(Aᵀ) near 3.3 (vs. 20–31 for Winograd). The paper further derives a frequency-wise quantization strategy, grouping scaling factors by DFT frequency bin to exploit the energy concentration in low frequencies, which recovers accuracy at int6 and int4 bitwidths.

## Results

On ImageNet with post-training quantization, SFC-6(7×7,3×3) achieves 69.59% Top-1 on ResNet-18 (Δ=-0.17%), 73.14% on ResNet-34 (Δ=-0.16%), and 76.02% on ResNet-50 (Δ=-0.12%) at int8, with only 0.6–1.0% degradation at int6. The algorithm delivers a 3.68× multiplication reduction for 3×3 convolutions (vs. 2.25× for Winograd F(2×2,3×3)) and 1.6×–2.5× BOPs reduction compared to both Winograd F(4×4,3×3) and direct convolution at equivalent accuracy. On FPGA, the SFC accelerator achieves 10.08 GOPs/DSPs/Clock, nearly 2× the throughput-per-resource of the best Winograd baseline (5.64).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Winograd F(2×2,3×3) (Lavin & Gray, 2016), SFC-6(6×6,3×3) achieves 1.64× lower arithmetic complexity (27.16% vs. 44.4%) with comparable MSE (2.4 vs. 2.2), and for 5×5 and 7×7 kernels the gap widens dramatically (SFC MSE 3.6 vs. Winograd 10.5 and 28.1). Against the two state-of-the-art Winograd quantization methods—Channel Balancing (Chikin & Kryzhanovskiy, 2022) and Full Quantization (Tianqi et al., 2023)—SFC-6(7×7,3×3) at int8 outperforms both by 1.4–2.0% Top-1 on ResNet-18 (69.59 vs. 67.54 and 68.16) and by 3.5–4.5% at int6 (68.80 vs. 64.34). On FPGA throughput-per-resource (GOPs/DSPs/Clock), SFC achieves 10.08, surpassing the Winograd accelerator of Liang et al. (2020) at 5.64, the NTT accelerator of Prasetiyo et al. (2023) at 3.48, and the direct-convolution accelerator of Huang et al. (2022) at 1.96, while using fewer DSPs (1056) than the NTT design (4100). Overall, SFC simultaneously wins on both accuracy and computational efficiency across all six baselines, with the largest margins appearing at lower bit-widths (int6/int4) where Winograd-based methods degrade most severely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Fourier-based fast convolution framework (Mathieu et al., 2014) by replacing numerical DFT computation with symbolic polynomial arithmetic, thereby eliminating irrational coefficients that cause rounding errors under low-precision formats. It departs from the Winograd algorithm (Lavin & Gray, 2016) in both the number field (complex vs. real) and the transformation construction (symbolic DFT vs. Vandermonde interpolation), while retaining the same three-stage compute structure. The correction-term technique for recovering valid linear convolution from circular convolution outputs is a novel contribution not present in prior FFT- or Winograd-based methods. The paper also provides the first systematic numerical error analysis comparing direct convolution, Winograd, and Fourier-based fast convolution under quantization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SFC resolves a long-standing conflict between fast convolution and model quantization by achieving 3.68× multiplication reduction (vs. Winograd's 2.25×) at comparable numerical error, with FPGA results showing 10.08 GOPs/DSPs/Clock versus 5.64 for Winograd-based accelerators. The approach is limited to small kernel sizes (3×3, 5×5, 7×7) and relies on the specific algebraic structure of 4- and 6-point DFTs; for very large kernels, the authors acknowledge FFT becomes more advantageous. Open questions include whether the symbolic approach generalizes to higher-order DFT points without introducing higher-degree polynomial terms, and whether the frequency-wise quantization strategy transfers to other fast-convolution paradigms.
