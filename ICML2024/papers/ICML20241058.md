## Problem

State-of-the-art trajectory-matching dataset distillation methods (e.g., MTT) lose effectiveness as images-per-class (IPC) increases, sometimes underperforming random sample selection. The paper identifies the root cause: these methods fail to incorporate the rare, complex features of harder samples into the synthetic dataset even with increased IPC, producing a persistent coverage gap between easy and hard test samples that limits generalization at larger scales.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241058/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a full training dataset (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, Tiny ImageNet at 64×64), pre-computed per-sample difficulty scores (C-score, Forgetting score, or EL2N), and expert training trajectories from a ConvNet-BN network trained on the full dataset. It produces a synthetic dataset of IPC images per class structured as a union of two subsets: a frozen portion D_select of real images (fraction 1−α, harder samples) and a distilled portion D_distill of optimized synthetic images (fraction α, easier samples). At evaluation time, the synthetic dataset is used to train a ResNet18 with a combined augmentation strategy—DSA on D_distill and simple random crop/flip on D_select.

## Method

SelMatch combines two mechanisms to manage the difficulty level of the synthetic dataset as a function of IPC. First, selection-based initialization uses a sliding-window algorithm: training samples are sorted by difficulty score, and a window of size r = (IPC × C / |D_real|) × 100% is slid across the sorted list to find the starting point β that maximizes test accuracy, yielding an initial subset D_initial at an IPC-appropriate difficulty level. Second, partial updates partition D_initial into D_select (the harder 1−α fraction, kept frozen) and D_distill (the easier α fraction, updated via MTT trajectory matching). During each distillation iteration, only D_distill is updated to minimize the MTT matching loss computed over the full synthetic set D_select ∪ D_distill, encouraging D_distill to condense knowledge not already present in D_select. The hyperparameter α decreases with IPC (e.g., 0.8 at IPC=25 to 0.1 at IPC=1000 on CIFAR-10), preserving more hard samples as the synthetic set grows. Distillation uses ConvNet-BN with extended max start epoch T⁺=80, and the final synthetic dataset is evaluated on ResNet18.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with IPC=50 (10% subset ratio), SelMatch achieves 54.5% test accuracy with ResNet18, a 3.5% absolute gain over the strongest baseline DATM (51.0%). Across all tested settings (5%–30% subset ratios on CIFAR-10/100 and 10%–20% on Tiny ImageNet), SelMatch consistently outperforms both selection-only and distillation-only baselines. On CIFAR-10 at IPC=1500 (30%), SelMatch reaches 91.3% versus 88.3% for random selection and 88.6% for MTT, approaching the full-dataset accuracy of 93.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SelMatch outperforms all nine baselines (Random, Forgetting, Glister, Oracle window, DSA, DM, MTT, FTD, DATM) across every tested dataset and subset ratio. The most notable margin is on CIFAR-100 at IPC=50, where SelMatch (54.5%) surpasses DATM (51.0%) by 3.5% and MTT (51.3%) by 3.2%. Oracle window selection (the best selection-only baseline) outperforms all distillation methods except SelMatch, confirming that difficulty-aware initialization is critical; SelMatch's additional gain over Oracle window (e.g., 54.5% vs. 50.0% on CIFAR-100 IPC=50) comes from the partial-update distillation step. On simpler architectures (ConvNet, VGG-11), SelMatch underperforms DATM by ~1–2% but still beats MTT, as the distilled dataset's harder features are less learnable by small networks. Forgetting and Glister (score-based and optimization-based selection) perform poorly at low IPC (e.g., 9.5% and 21.7% on CIFAR-100 IPC=25), while distillation methods like DSA and DM saturate or underperform random selection at higher IPC, validating the paper's motivation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SelMatch sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: trajectory-matching dataset distillation (MTT, DATM) and difficulty-score-based sample selection (Forgetting, C-score, Glister). It builds directly on MTT's trajectory-matching objective and DATM's observation that the relevant trajectory window shifts with IPC, but departs from both by introducing a selection-based initialization step (sliding-window over difficulty-ranked samples) and a partial-update mechanism that freezes a fraction of the synthetic set during distillation. The paper also demonstrates that these two components are transferable to other distillation backbones (DSA, SRe2L), positioning SelMatch as a general wrapper rather than a standalone method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-documented failure mode—distillation methods underperforming random selection at moderate-to-large IPC—and provides a principled, well-ablated fix that is practically useful for the 5–30% subset regime where pure distillation breaks down. Two limitations temper its impact: it requires pre-computed difficulty scores (C-score or Forgetting, both computationally expensive) and two hyperparameters (α, β) that must be tuned per dataset and IPC, with no closed-form estimate available. An open question the authors flag is whether optimal α and β can be predicted without running the full distillation loop, which would remove the main practical barrier to adoption.
