## Problem

Existing semi-supervised partial label learning (SSPLL) methods handle partial labels and unlabeled data as separate sub-problems, composing a PLL disambiguation step with an SSL pseudo-labeling step in a 'divide and conquer' fashion. This composite design leads to incremental solutions that merely replicate existing methods without establishing a principled connection between label disambiguation and pseudo-label assignment. The paper asks whether a single unified framework can treat both label redundancy (partial labels) and label insufficiency (unlabeled data) simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two types of training data: partial-label images where each instance is associated with a candidate label set S_i (a proper subset of all c classes containing the true label) and unlabeled images with no label information. During training, each instance maintains a dynamic pseudo candidate label set that is expanded and condensed across epochs, constrained to size [1, c-1]. The model outputs a c-dimensional class score vector, and the final prediction is the argmax class label. Experiments use standard image resolutions: 28×28 for Fashion-MNIST and SVHN, 32×32 for CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100.

## Method

SPMI introduces a mutual-information-based label channel that uniformly treats all data by maintaining a dynamic pseudo candidate label set per instance, regardless of whether it originated from partial-label or unlabeled data. Label expansion adds a label k to the candidate set when the model's posterior q(k|z_i) exceeds the class prior p(k), derived from maximizing I(Z;Y) via the Information Bottleneck approximation. Label condensation removes the single least-relevant label by minimizing a KL-divergence-based information score G(x_i, S_i, k) that quantifies information loss upon removal, with a threshold τ gating when condensation is triggered. An initialization step exploits the convexity of cross-entropy to seed unlabeled instances with high-confidence candidate labels after a warm-up period. Training uses a weighted cross-entropy loss over the current candidate set, where weights are proportional to model outputs within the set, and the class prior is updated via the mean model output each epoch.

## Results

On the SSPLL benchmark, SPMI achieves the best test accuracy across most settings. On CIFAR-100 (l=5000, p=0.05), SPMI reaches 66.43%, substantially outperforming the strongest SSL baseline FreeMatch (67.81% is actually slightly higher, but SPMI beats FixMatch at 52.06% and MixMatch at 36.44%). On CIFAR-10 (l=4000, p=0.3), SPMI attains 92.81%, surpassing FreeMatch (92.73%) and FlexMatch (92.32%). On SVHN (l=1000, p=0.3), SPMI achieves 96.59%, the highest among all methods. The method shows the largest gains over PLL-only baselines on CIFAR-100, where SPMI (66.43%) exceeds PLCR (27.23%) by nearly 40 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPMI outperforms all three PLL baselines (PRODEN, PLCR, POP) combined with FixMatch across nearly all settings, with the most dramatic gains on CIFAR-100 where SPMI (66.43%) exceeds PLCR (27.23%) by ~39 points and POP (43.04%) by ~23 points. Against SSL baselines combined with PRODEN, SPMI beats MixMatch on every configuration (e.g., 90.41% vs 40.03% on CIFAR-10 l=1000) and FixMatch on most settings (e.g., 92.81% vs 92.19% on CIFAR-10 l=4000). SPMI edges out FlexMatch and FreeMatch on F-MNIST, CIFAR-100 (l=10000), and SVHN, but trails them slightly in the most extreme low-data regimes: CIFAR-10 l=1000 (SPMI 90.41% vs FreeMatch 91.20%, FlexMatch 91.08%) and CIFAR-100 l=5000 (SPMI 66.43% vs FreeMatch 67.81%), which the authors note are settings specifically targeted by those SSL methods. On the PCLL extension (Table 4), SPMI also surpasses all CLL baselines (Forward, L-W, NLL) combined with PRODEN, achieving 94.55% on CIFAR-10 and 78.67% on CIFAR-100.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'divide and conquer' paradigm in semi-supervised partial label learning (SSPLL), where prior methods (SSPL, PARM) simply concatenate a PLL disambiguation module with an SSL pseudo-labeling module. Instead, SPMI draws on the Information Bottleneck / mutual information framework (Alemi et al., 2017) to treat label redundancy (partial labels) and label insufficiency (unlabeled data) as two ends of a single spectrum, operating them through a unified label channel with expansion and condensation operations. Conceptually, it subsumes PLL, CLL, and SSL as special cases of the same candidate-label-set manipulation, rather than treating them as separate sub-problems to be stitched together.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The uniform treatment offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc composition of weakly-supervised methods, and the mutual-information-based label channel provides a theoretically grounded mechanism for progressive label purification that avoids the error accumulation inherent in threshold-based pseudo-labeling. However, all experiments are confined to small-scale benchmarks (F-MNIST, CIFAR-10/100, SVHN) with at most 10,000 labeled samples, leaving open whether the approach scales to ImageNet-level datasets or benefits from the large unlabeled pools typical of modern SSL. The framework also introduces several interacting hyperparameters (threshold τ, warm-up T_w, class-prior updates) whose joint sensitivity is only partially characterized, and the method's reliance on per-epoch candidate set manipulation may be computationally costly at scale.
