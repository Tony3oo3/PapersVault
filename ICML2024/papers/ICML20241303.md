## Problem

Distributionally Robust Optimization (DRO) methods suffer from over-pessimism in practice—producing low-confidence predictions, unstable parameter estimates, and poor generalization. The paper identifies a root cause: DRO's worst-case distributions excessively upweight noisy samples (which have high prediction errors) alongside the intended minor sub-populations, and existing outlier-robust regression methods cannot resolve this because both noisy samples and minor sub-populations exhibit high errors simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes tabular covariate vectors and target values, plus a kNN graph G_N constructed once at initialization to encode data manifold geometry (built via NN-Descent for near-linear O(N^1.14) complexity on large datasets). It outputs regression predictions and worst-case sample weights q that are transported along graph edges via gradient flow, implemented as message propagation through the DGL library with GPU parallelization. The graph structure is the key geometric input that distinguishes this method from standard DRO, and no target information is leaked into the graph construction.

## Method

GC-DRO augments the Geometric Wasserstein DRO risk objective with two calibration terms: a graph total variation term Σ w_ij q_i q_j (ℓ_i − ℓ_j)² that penalizes distributions concentrating density on locally non-smooth (noisy) samples while leaving smooth minor sub-population clusters unpenalized, and a negative entropy term that acts as a non-linear graph Laplacian enforcing weight smoothness along the manifold. The paper establishes that this risk objective equals the negative Helmholtz free energy (interaction + potential + entropy), unifying KL-DRO, χ²-DRO, MMD-DRO, and GDRO under a common physical framework where the interaction energy term is the novel component. Optimization proceeds via time-discretized gradient flow in the geometric Wasserstein space, with a proven approximation error ratio of e^{−CT_in} after T_in inner-loop steps, and a sensitivity analysis (Proposition 3.3) showing that for sufficiently large label noise δ, GC-DRO's sample weight sensitivity is strictly lower than GDRO, χ²-DRO, and KL-DRO.

## Results

On simulation data with sub-population shifts and label noise, GCDRO achieves the best TestMean RMSE of 0.482 (weak noise, 0.5%) and 0.540 (strong noise, 5%), along with the lowest TestStd (0.070 and 0.044) and Parameter Estimation Error (0.190 and 0.268). Under strong label noise, GCDRO reduces TestMean RMSE by 19% compared to GDRO (0.540 vs. 0.677) and by 48% compared to KL-DRO (0.540 vs. 1.037). On three real-world regression datasets (bike-sharing, house price, temperature) with natural distribution shifts, GCDRO consistently outperforms all baselines with the most stable cross-sub-population performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCDRO outperforms all six baselines across every metric under both weak (0.5%) and strong (5%) label noise. Against GDRO (the closest baseline sharing the same Geometric Wasserstein uncertainty set), GCDRO's calibration terms reduce TestMean RMSE from 0.605 to 0.482 (weak noise) and from 0.677 to 0.540 (strong noise), while also cutting Parameter Estimation Error from 0.421 to 0.268 under strong noise. KL-DRO and χ²-DRO suffer the most severe over-pessimism, with KL-DRO's TestMean RMSE reaching 1.037 under strong noise—nearly double GCDRO's 0.540. DORO, designed specifically for outlier robustness, still underperforms GCDRO (0.611 vs. 0.540 TestMean under strong noise) because it relies on the assumption that noisy samples always have the largest prediction errors, which does not hold when minor sub-populations coexist. HRDRO (0.801) and WDRO (0.600) are also clearly outperformed, confirming that neither holistic robust regression nor standard Wasserstein DRO adequately handles the joint challenge of sub-population shifts and label noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GDRO (Liu et al., 2022b), which uses a discrete Geometric Wasserstein distance to construct the DRO uncertainty set, by adding two calibration terms (graph total variation and entropy) to the risk objective to counteract over-pessimism from noisy samples. The optimization algorithm is derived from the Fokker-Planck gradient flow framework on graphs (Chow et al., 2017), and the risk objective is shown to correspond to Helmholtz free energy, providing a unifying physical lens under which KL-DRO, χ²-DRO, MMD-DRO, and GDRO can be compared by their interaction, potential, and entropy components. The paper explicitly departs from prior outlier-robust regression methods (Diakonikolas & Kane, 2018; Klivans et al., 2018) by arguing they are inapplicable when noisy samples and minor sub-populations coexist, and from DORO (Zhai et al., 2021) by not relying on the assumption that noisy samples always have the largest prediction errors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The free-energy interpretation offers a principled design space for future DRO calibration terms (any quadratic interaction energy is admissible), and the gradient-flow algorithm with a bounded e^{-CT_in} error ratio makes the approach practical for deep models. However, the method is currently limited to regression on tabular data with a kNN graph, and the paper acknowledges that model selection without domain information in validation remains an open problem. Extending GC-DRO to classification, image, and language data, and to more sophisticated manifold representations beyond kNN, are natural next steps that the authors leave for future work.
