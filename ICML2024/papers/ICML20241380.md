## Problem

Sparse and delayed rewards in long-horizon RL tasks hinder effective exploration and gradient-based learning. Existing reward shaping methods either depend on expensive human demonstrations or introduce additional intrinsic objectives (e.g., exploration bonuses, curiosity) that risk trapping agents in local optima and deviating from the original optimal policy. No prior method achieves self-learning dense reward generation that avoids both external knowledge and extraneous optimization goals while preserving policy consistency.

## Input / Output

The reward agent receives the environment state and the policy agent's action as a joint observation (s, a) and outputs a continuous suggested reward r^S bounded in [R_min, R_max]. The policy agent receives the environment state and outputs a control action, while its effective reward is the sum of the sparse environmental reward and the β-scaled suggested reward (β ∈ (0,1]). Notably, the reward agent's true state includes the policy agent's network parameters θ, making its interaction a POMDP where θ is only implicitly observable through the (s,a) pair.

## Method

ReLara introduces a cooperative dual-agent architecture where a Reward Agent (A_R) and a Policy Agent (A_P) are each solved as independent off-policy actor-critic problems (SAC-based with double Q-networks) using separate replay buffers. The reward agent is formulated as a POMDP interacting with the combined (Environment, Policy Agent) entity, and its Q-function is trained solely on environmental rewards, ensuring it learns to predict true task value rather than its own suggested signals. The key mechanism is that the untrained reward agent initially emits random rewards, which naturally diversify the policy agent's optimization directions and encourage exploration; as the reward agent converges, its outputs evolve into informative, future-oriented dense signals that shift the policy agent toward exploitation—achieving a self-adaptive exploration-exploitation balance without any hand-crafted schedule or additional objectives. The suggested reward is scaled by a hyperparameter β (default 0.2) to prevent it from overwhelming the environmental feedback.

## Results

ReLara achieves the highest average episodic return on 8 out of 10 sparse-reward continuous control tasks. Headline improvements include AntStand (28.66 vs. 15.93 for SAC, a 79% gain), AntFar (67.77 vs. 9.64 for SAC, a 7× gain), and RobotPush (58.71 vs. 0.55 for SAC, where all other baselines score near zero). On the two tasks where ReLara does not lead—RobotReach (103.56 vs. PPO's 110.44) and MountainCar (0.89 vs. RND's 0.94)—the gap is small and attributable to ReLara's burn-in phase delaying initial convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReLara outperforms all six baselines on 8 of 10 environments. Against the closest reward-shaping competitor ROSA, ReLara achieves 7–100× higher returns (e.g., 67.77 vs. 4.71 on AntFar; 58.71 vs. 0.00 on RobotPush). Compared to the exploration-based ExploRS, ReLara dominates on long-horizon tasks (AntVeryFar: 64.07 vs. 1.67; RobotPush: 58.71 vs. 0.20) while matching it on HumanKeep (160.31 vs. 158.09). Versus the general-purpose RL baselines SAC, TD3, RND, and PPO, ReLara's largest margins appear on tasks where all baselines fail to learn (AntSpeed, RobotPush, AntVeryFar), while on HumanKeep and MountainCar the gap to RND is within one standard error (160.31 vs. 159.79; 0.89 vs. 0.94). The only clear losses are RobotReach, where PPO's on-policy exploration edge (110.44 vs. 103.56) slightly exceeds ReLara, and MountainCar, where RND's fixed-network novelty bonus (0.94) marginally beats ReLara (0.89), both attributable to ReLara's burn-in phase delaying early convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReLara builds on the reward shaping literature (Ng et al., 1999) and the actor-critic paradigm (SAC as the policy backbone), but departs from both demonstration-based RS (inverse RL, imitation learning) and intrinsic-motivation-based RS (exploration bonuses, curiosity) by requiring no external knowledge and introducing no auxiliary objectives. Its closest structural relative is ROSA (Mguni et al., 2023), which also employs a two-agent reward-shaping architecture, but ReLara reframes the interaction as a cooperative POMDP rather than a zero-sum Markov game, and optimizes both agents concurrently rather than alternately. The key conceptual departure is treating the reward agent's 'action' as a continuous suggested reward signal learned end-to-end, so that the exploration-to-exploitation transition emerges organically from the reward agent's own learning progression rather than being explicitly scheduled.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReLara offers a practical, self-contained solution to the sparse-reward challenge in continuous control (robotics, physical simulation) where hand-crafted reward functions are infeasible and exploration bonuses risk trapping agents in local optima. The self-adaptive exploration-exploitation balance—where random early rewards expand the policy's search space and evolved rewards sharpen exploitation—is a clean mechanism that avoids the common failure mode of novelty-driven methods. However, the framework's benefit is contingent on the environment actually providing sparse/delayed rewards; in dense-reward settings the auxiliary agent may be redundant or harmful. Open questions include formal convergence guarantees for the coupled two-agent optimization, adaptation to dense-reward or partially-observable real-world tasks, and whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional or multi-task settings.
