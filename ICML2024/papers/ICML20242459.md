## Problem

Existing prompt tuning methods for vision-language models (e.g., CoOp) perform uniform cross-modal alignment without distinguishing spurious from invariant visual features, causing text embeddings to be pulled toward spurious correlations when training data is imbalanced. This leads to severe OOD generalization degradation, where the model relies on background or co-occurring attributes rather than the target object. No prior work has addressed spurious correlation specifically within the prompt tuning framework for VLMs, where both encoders are frozen and conventional decoupling strategies are inapplicable.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a set of class names as input. The image is encoded by a frozen CLIP image encoder (ResNet-50 or ViT-B/32), and its output is decomposed into two sub-embeddings via two lightweight linear projection layers. The class names are combined with a learnable soft prompt (4 context tokens) and encoded by the frozen text encoder. The final prediction is a class label obtained by computing cosine similarity between the invariant image sub-embedding and the text class embeddings, with the spurious sub-embedding explicitly excluded from the prediction path.

## Method

CoOPood introduces two lightweight projection layers (φ and ψ) after the frozen image encoder to decompose each image feature into an invariant component z_u and a spurious component z_s. The alignment is then split into two independent contrastive phases: a cross-entropy loss (L_ce^u) pulls text embeddings toward the invariant image embedding, while a KL-divergence loss (L_s) pushes text embeddings away from the spurious image embedding by regularizing the spurious predictive distribution toward uniform. A conditional mutual information regularization (L_c), estimated via a class-conditional inner-product metric, enforces that the two sub-embeddings are conditionally independent given the label, ensuring a valid decomposition. The theoretical justification is grounded in a structured causal model (SCM) that proves the conditional independence signatures z_u ⊥ z_s | Y and z_s ⊥ t | Y. The final objective is L = L_ce^u + αL_s + βL_c, and the framework is orthogonal to existing prompt architectures, demonstrated by plug-and-play integration with CoCoOp.

## Results

On Waterbirds (ResNet-50), CoOPood achieves 82.38% average and 60.28% worst-group accuracy, outperforming CoOp (78.98%/48.91%) and ERM (81.50%/57.17%). On ImageNet-1K (baby pacifier), CoOPood reaches 93.05%/86.08%, a substantial gain over CoOp (88.55%/78.87%). With group information, *Group CoOPood attains 89.71%/85.64% on Waterbirds and 97.68%/95.36% on ImageNet-1K, surpassing *Group DRO on all three datasets. On the larger ViT-L-14 backbone, *Group CoOPood achieves 93.49%/88.81% on Waterbirds, outperforming all five additional baselines including *Group DFR (93.32%/85.71%) and *Group DRO (91.49%/86.15%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoOPood consistently outperforms CoOp and ERM on Waterbirds and ImageNet-1K across both backbones, with the largest gains on worst-group accuracy (e.g., +11.37 points over CoOp on Waterbirds worst-group with ResNet-50). On CelebA, CoOPood improves worst-group accuracy over CoOp (31.11% vs. 26.11%) but still trails pre-trained CLIP (67.78%), which the authors attribute to the extremely small worst-group proportion (1.1%) in the training split. With group information, *Group CoOPood surpasses *Group DRO on all three datasets (e.g., 85.64% vs. 83.33% worst-group on Waterbirds). On the ViT-L-14 Waterbirds benchmark, *Group CoOPood (93.49%/88.81%) beats *Group DFR (93.32%/85.71%), *Group PDE (88.66%/76.24%), *Group SDM (88.16%/80.14%), TextSpan (79.90%/45.20%), and RobotShot (84.13%/72.90%). The plug-and-play experiment shows CoCoOp+CoOPood improves over CoCoOp alone by 2.1–9.5% on Waterbirds and CelebA, confirming orthogonality with existing prompt tuning mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CoOp/CoCoOp prompt tuning lineage for CLIP by introducing a decoupled alignment mechanism that separates invariant and spurious image features via two lightweight projection layers before performing contrastive learning. It departs from prior prompt tuning methods (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe, PLOT) that apply a single uniform cross-modal alignment, and from traditional spurious-correlation mitigation methods (Group DRO, DFR, PDE) that operate on unimodal classifiers or require retraining the backbone. The theoretical grounding via structured causal models and conditional independence signatures is borrowed from the invariant-representation literature (Ahuja et al., 2021; Jiang & Veitch, 2022) but is, to the authors' claim, the first application to the cross-modal prompt tuning setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is practically attractive because it adds only ~2% parameters and 0.02% FLOPS over CoOp while being plug-and-play with existing prompt tuning architectures (demonstrated with CoCoOp). However, the CelebA results reveal a limitation: without group information, the worst-group accuracy does not improve over zero-shot CLIP, suggesting the linear-projection decoupling may be insufficient when the minority group is extremely underrepresented (1.1%). Open questions include whether the SCM-based conditional independence assumptions hold for more complex or multi-attribute spurious correlations, and whether the approach generalizes beyond binary or few-class settings to the full 1000-class ImageNet without hand-crafted group definitions.
