## Problem

Despite SWA's widespread empirical success in improving generalization, its theoretical analysis has been limited to the idealized setting of convex loss functions with sampling with replacement. The two most practical scenarios—non-convex objectives and sampling without replacement—remain unexplored, with two compounding analytical obstacles: SWA's averaging makes every historical gradient relevant (unlike SGD where only the current step matters), and the (1+αβ)-expansion property in non-convex cases creates nested dependencies across all prior steps.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a training set S of n i.i.d. examples, a loss function g(w;z) that is L-Lipschitz and β-smooth (bounded in [0,1] for non-convex theorems), and a step-size schedule (fixed α ≤ 2/β for convex; non-increasing α = c/t for non-convex). It produces closed-form uniform stability bounds ε_gen and corresponding generalization error bounds for the SWA-averaged model w̄_T, with the without-replacement theorems requiring T ≤ n. Experiments verify the bounds on LeNet/VGG16 (MNIST, CIFAR-10) and MLP (Adult) by measuring parameter distance and generalization error between models trained on two datasets differing in one sample.

## Method

The core technique is a mathematical-induction-based recursive representation that bounds each gradient difference ‖∇g(w_i, z_{i+1}) − ∇g(w'_i, z_{i+1})‖ in terms of the previous step's parameter gap, converting the nested (1+αβ)-expansion problem into a tractable finite geometric sum. For convex cases, the non-expansive property (α ≤ 2/β) collapses the recursion, yielding O(T/n) with replacement and O(ln T/n) without replacement; for non-convex cases, the key inequality (T−t)/(tT) ≤ ½ log(T/t) controls the exponential growth, producing O(T^{cβ/(cβ+2)}/n). Sampling without replacement is handled by casting the event of selecting the distinguished sample pair as a combinatorial probability problem, splitting into three cases (occurs at step T+1, after T+1, or in prior steps) with probabilities 1/n, (n−T−1)/n, and T/n. The fundamental insight is that SWA's 1/T averaging coefficient effectively transforms SGD's (1+αβ)-expansive update into a (1+αβ/T)-expansive one, which is the structural reason SWA achieves uniformly sharper generalization bounds than vanilla SGD across all four settings.

## Results

The paper establishes stability-based generalization bounds for SWA that are uniformly sharper than SGD across all four settings (convex/non-convex × with/without replacement). The most striking improvement is in the convex, sampling-without-replacement setting, where SWA achieves O(ln T/n) versus SGD's O(T/n) — a logarithmic improvement over linear. In the non-convex settings, SWA's exponent improves from cβ/(1+cβ) (SGD) to cβ/(2+cβ) (SWA), reflecting that SWA transforms the (1+αβ)-expansive property into a (1+αβ/T)-expansive one. Empirically, on MNIST (LeNet), CIFAR-10 (VGG-16), and Adult (MLP), SWA consistently yields smaller parameter differences and lower generalization errors than vanilla SGD under both sampling strategies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SWA against two SGD baselines: Hardt et al. (2016) for sampling-with-replacement settings and Kuzborskij & Lampert (2018) for sampling-without-replacement settings. SWA strictly dominates SGD in all four settings. Against Hardt et al. (2016), SWA halves the convex with-replacement bound (O(T/n) vs O(2T/n)) and improves the non-convex exponent from cβ/(1+cβ) to cβ/(2+cβ). Against Kuzborskij & Lampert (2018), the improvement is most dramatic in the convex without-replacement case, where SWA achieves O(ln T/n) versus SGD's O(T/n), and in the non-convex without-replacement case where SWA's bound depends only on the smoothness constant β rather than the data-dependent Lipschitz parameter γ used by Kuzborskij & Lampert. Empirically, SWA consistently shows smaller parameter distances and lower generalization errors than SGD on all three datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, Adult) under both sampling strategies, confirming the theoretical predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the uniform-stability framework of Hardt et al. (2016) from SGD to Stochastic Weight Averaging, and from the restricted convex + sampling-with-replacement setting to all four combinations of convexity and sampling strategy. It builds on Kuzborskij & Lampert (2018), who analyzed SGD under without-replacement sampling, by transferring that combinatorial-probability treatment to the more complex SWA case where historical gradient terms are nested. The key technical departure is a mathematical-induction-based recursive bounding scheme that handles the (1+αβ)-expansive property's step-to-step dependency in non-convex SWA, a difficulty absent in the single-step SGD analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first complete theoretical picture of SWA's generalization across convex/non-convex and with/without-replacement settings, rigorously confirming the empirical superiority of SWA over SGD that practitioners have relied on for years. The O(ln T) vs. O(T) improvement in the convex without-replacement case and the (1+αβ/T) effective expansion factor offer a clean mechanistic explanation for why averaging helps. Limitations include the reliance on L-Lipschitz and β-smooth assumptions, batch-size-1 theory, and the T ≤ n constraint for without-replacement results; open questions include removing smoothness assumptions and connecting the stability analysis to loss-landscape geometry for practical model selection.
