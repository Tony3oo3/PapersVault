## Problem

MAML-based few-shot learning methods rely on simple inner-loop gradient descent that lacks adaptability to task-specific dynamics, leading to slow convergence and poor generalization under distribution shifts between training and test tasks. Existing optimizer variants (Adagrad, Adam, RMS) offer no convergence guarantees in non-convex settings, and the manual tuning of PID controller parameters remains an open challenge when applied to neural network training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242374/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes N-way K-shot RGB images (84×84 for mini-ImageNet/tiered-ImageNet, 32×32 for CIFAR-FS) with K=1 or 5 labeled support samples per class, plus 15 query samples per class per iteration. Beyond the standard class label output, the hyperparameter generator network f_ζ produces per-layer PID gains (K_P, K_I, K_D) conditioned on the mean, variance, and gradient of each backbone layer's parameters, enabling layer-wise adaptive optimization within the MAML inner loop.

## Method

LA-PID augments SGD-Momentum with a differential term φ_{t+1} that predicts future gradient changes, forming a full PID-like update rule where the proportional, integral, and derivative components correspond to the current gradient, exponentially weighted historical gradient accumulation, and gradient difference, respectively. A 2-layer MLP generator network f_ζ adaptively produces the three PID gains per layer from layer parameter statistics (mean, variance, gradient), eliminating manual tuning. The paper derives a closed-loop transfer function via Laplace transform, proving the optimizer behaves as a second-order control system and establishing the stability condition 0 < (K_P+1)/(2√(K_I·K_D)) < 1 for underdamped convergence. Optimal initialization of K_D is derived in closed form using the Ziegler-Nichols rule, and the entire optimizer operates in the MAML inner loop while the outer loop updates the generator parameters via standard gradient descent.

## Results

On mini-ImageNet with ResNet12, LA-PID+MAML achieves 63.29% (1-shot) and 79.18% (5-shot) 5-way classification accuracy, outperforming MAML (58.37%/69.76%) by ~5 and ~9.4 percentage points respectively. On tiered-ImageNet with ResNet12, LA-PID+MAML reaches 64.77% (1-shot) and 82.59% (5-shot), surpassing ALFA+MAML (64.62%/82.48%) and MAML (58.58%/71.24%). In the cross-domain setting (mini-ImageNet→CUB), LA-PID+MAML with ResNet12 achieves 65.93% 5-way 5-shot accuracy, exceeding the ResNet18 baseline (65.57%) and ALFA+MAML (61.22%). Notably, LA-PID converges in fewer inner-loop steps: a single inner-loop update (70.21%) already surpasses MAML's five-step result (63.11%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MAML (Finn et al., 2017), LA-PID+MAML consistently wins across all datasets and backbones: on mini-ImageNet with ResNet12, it gains +4.92 pp (1-shot) and +9.42 pp (5-shot); with 4-CONV, the gains are +0.34 pp (1-shot) and +3.95 pp (5-shot). Against ALFA (Baik et al., 2023), LA-PID+MAML leads on mini-ImageNet with ResNet12 by +3.55 pp (1-shot) and +1.22 pp (5-shot), and on tiered-ImageNet by +0.15 pp (1-shot) and +0.11 pp (5-shot); however, with the 4-CONV backbone on mini-ImageNet, ALFA actually outperforms LA-PID (50.58% vs. 48.70% 1-shot; 69.12% vs. 63.11% 5-shot), suggesting LA-PID's advantage is more pronounced with deeper backbones. Against L2TT (Chen et al., 2019), LA-PID+MAML wins on mini-ImageNet with ResNet12 by +2.47 pp (1-shot) and +1.02 pp (5-shot), and with 4-CONV by +1.00 pp (1-shot) and −1.64 pp (5-shot, where L2TT slightly edges ahead). In the cross-domain mini-ImageNet→CUB evaluation, LA-PID+MAML (ResNet12) achieves 65.93%, beating ALFA+MAML (61.22%) by +4.71 pp and the ResNet18 baseline (65.57%) by +0.36 pp, while MAML alone drops to 53.83%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of meta-learning (MAML) and classical control theory, extending the SGD-Momentum optimizer by adding a derivative term to form a full PID-like update rule within the inner loop. While prior PID-based optimizers (Wang et al., 2020a; Weng et al., 2022) applied fixed gain coefficients, LA-PID introduces a generated network that adaptively tunes K_P, K_I, and K_D per layer and per task, drawing on the hyperparameter-generator design from ALFA (Baik et al., 2023). The paper also departs from the dominant MAML research direction—improving initialization—by instead targeting the inner-loop optimizer itself, and provides a first-principles convergence analysis via second-order control system dynamics that prior PID optimizers lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical convergence analysis (mapping the optimizer to a second-order system with explicit damping ratio, overshoot, and settling time) offers a principled way to initialize and interpret PID gains in neural network training, which could inform optimizer design beyond few-shot learning. However, the paper's own ablation reveals that only a few layers receive non-zero PID parameters, suggesting the generation network underutilizes the full architecture—a limitation that constrains the generality of the layer-adaptive claim. Open questions include whether the PID analogy holds for deeper or transformer-based backbones, whether the convergence guarantees transfer to non-convex loss landscapes beyond the linearized analysis, and whether the modest accuracy gains (typically 2–5 points over ALFA) justify the additional hyperparameter-generation overhead in practice.
