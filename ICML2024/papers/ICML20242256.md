## Problem

Prior work on online capacitated coverage maximization (OCCM) under KIID arrivals provided only a non-adaptive sampling policy with a parameter-free competitive ratio of 0.580, leaving open how the ratio depends on the two natural structural parameters: the uniform task capacity b and the maximum number Δ of workers covering any single skill. No adaptive policy incorporating real-time boostings had been designed or analyzed for this coverage-maximization setting, and no hardness results existed as a function of (τ, b) where τ = 1 − e^{−Δ}.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a bipartite graph G = (I, J, E) with binary skill vectors χ_i, χ_j ∈ {0,1}^K, non-negative task-skill weights w_λ, integer task capacities b_i, and a known KIID arrival distribution over T rounds (with integral rates, WLOG r_j = 1 so T = |J|). It produces a feasible worker-to-task allocation X and, as the main theoretical output, a competitive ratio function η(τ, b) expressed as an integral over [0,1] involving a function h(z) defined by a second-order ODE, together with the non-adaptive counterpart κ(τ, b) and the adaptivity gap AG = η − κ. The analysis is asymptotic in T → ∞ and assumes T ≫ b ≥ 1.

## Method

The paper proposes two LP-guided sampling policies: a non-adaptive SM-A that draws tasks from the static distribution {x*_{ij}} of the benchmark LP, and an adaptive SM-B that adds real-time boostings (RTB) — sampling only from a worker's currently safe neighbors with a renormalized distribution — plus two simulation-based attenuations (ATT-I and ATT-II) guided by auxiliary sequences {φ_t, ψ_t} defined recursively in Eq. (11). The sequences are computed in the T → ∞ limit via a second-order ODE h'' = (h')³ h^{b−1} e^{−bh−1} b^b/(b−1)! with h(0)=0, h'(0)=1, yielding closed-form solutions for small b (e.g., b=1,2). Competitive analysis reduces each task-skill pair to a balls-and-bins model where type-A balls (covering the skill) and type-B balls (not covering it) are sampled with time-varying probabilities ψ_t·p/T and ψ_t·q/T, and the coverage probability is lower-bounded by the integral η(τ,b). The key result is η(τ,b) ≥ 0.602 for all (τ,b), recovering 1−1/e at (τ,b)=(1,∞) and exceeding it whenever Δ < ∞ or b < ∞, with a matching upper bound η̄(b) established for the special case Δ=1.

## Results

The adaptive policy SM-B achieves a competitive ratio η(τ,b) ≥ 0.602 for all parameter combinations (τ,b), strictly improving over the 0.580 of the prior non-adaptive result. In the unbounded setting (Δ,τ)=(∞,1), b=∞, both SM-A and SM-B recover the optimal 1−1/e ≈ 0.632 barrier. For finite parameters, SM-B exceeds this barrier: η(τ,1) ≈ 0.692 when b=1, and η(1−1/e,∞) ≈ 0.741 when (Δ,τ)=(1,1−1/e) with b→∞. The adaptivity gap AG = η−κ ranges from 0.060 at b=1 down to 0 at b=∞, quantifying the benefit of real-time boostings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Kapralov et al. (2013), who showed Greedy achieves the optimal 1−1/e ≈ 0.632 for online submodular welfare maximization with unbounded offline capacity, this paper matches that bound in the (Δ,τ)=(∞,1), b=∞ corner but strictly exceeds it whenever either Δ<∞ or b<∞ (e.g., 0.692 at b=1, 0.741 at τ=1−1/e with b→∞). Against Xu (2023b), which studied the exact same OCCM model with a non-adaptive policy achieving CR 0.580, this paper recovers that 0.580 as a special case of its analysis and improves it to η(τ,b)≥0.602 via the adaptive SM-B with real-time boostings and two-layer attenuations. Against Dickerson et al. (2019b), which considered a different objective (a single submodular function over all edges with unit capacities, CR 0.399), the present work addresses a more general per-agent coverage objective with arbitrary capacities, making direct numerical comparison inapplicable but demonstrating a substantially stronger guarantee in the overlapping coverage-maximization setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Xu (2023b), which introduced the Online Capacitated Coverage Maximization (OCCM) model and a non-adaptive LP-guided sampling policy achieving a parameter-free CR of 0.580. The key departures are: (1) a parameter-dependent competitive analysis expressing the CR as a function of τ = 1−e^{−Δ} (maximum skill overlap) and b (uniform task capacity), and (2) a new adaptive policy SM-B equipped with real-time boostings (RTB) and two layers of simulation-guided attenuations, analyzed via second-order ODEs—a technique absent from prior work in this line. The RTB idea has appeared as a heuristic in ride-hailing and crowdsourcing systems (Dickerson et al., 2019a, 2024) and was formally analyzed for fairness maximization with unit capacities (Ma et al., 2023), but this is the first competitive analysis of RTB for coverage maximization with non-unit capacities and multi-layer attenuations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the classical 1−1/e competitive ratio barrier for online matching is not universal: it can be strictly beaten (up to ~0.741) whenever either the skill-overlap parameter Δ is finite or the task capacity b is finite, which are the typical real-world regimes. The quantified adaptivity gap (η − κ, up to 0.060) provides the first theoretical characterization of how much real-time boostings help over static LP-guided sampling in coverage maximization. Open questions include extending the analysis to general monotone submodular valuations, deriving a universal hardness bound as a function of (τ, b), and validating the policies on real crowdsourcing datasets. The ODE-based analytical framework may also transfer to other online stochastic optimization problems with capacity constraints.
