## Problem

Existing deep learning-based SBDD methods and their standard docking score evaluation metric both overlook molecular specificity: generated molecules achieve high docking scores not only on their intended target but also on nearly all other protein pockets, indicating promiscuous binding. The paper shows that despite chronological improvements in absolute docking scores, models underperform reference ligands on a specificity-aware metric, revealing that progress has primarily improved the unconditional component p(x) rather than the conditional binding probability p(y|x).

## Input / Output

The method takes a protein pocket represented as 3D atom coordinates and one-hot atom types, with the protein center of mass shifted to the origin for SE(3)-invariance. It outputs a 3D ligand molecule (atom coordinates and types) whose atom count is sampled from a prior conditioned on pocket size. For evaluation, generated molecules are converted to 1D/2D representations, re-docked via Glide against both the original target and a randomly sampled decoy pocket to compute the Delta Score, which measures the difference between target and off-target docking scores.

## Method

SBE-Diff combines a contrastively trained Specific Binding Energy (SBE) model with an energy-guided SE(3)-equivariant diffusion process. The SBE model uses two SE(3)-equivariant GNN encoders (one for molecules, one for pockets) and computes binding energy as the negative cosine similarity of their embeddings; it is trained with a symmetric in-batch softmax loss that treats ligands from other pockets as negative examples, directly optimizing the log conditional binding probability. During reverse diffusion, the SBE gradient with respect to noisy atom coordinates and types is injected at each timestep to steer generation toward molecules with high specificity for the target pocket. The diffusion backbone follows TargetDiff's architecture (Gaussian noise on coordinates, categorical noise on atom types), but the SBE guidance is the key differentiator, with empirical coefficients w₁=0.1 and w₂=1e-4 controlling gradient strength.

## Results

On CrossDocked2020, SBE-Diff achieves the best Delta Score (0.552 mean, 0.250 median) and the highest ratio of generated molecules outperforming reference ligands (0.300 mean, 0.216 median) among all generative methods, while maintaining a competitive absolute docking score of 6.815 (vs. 7.102 for DecompDiff and 6.632 for reference ligands). The reference ligand achieves a Delta Score of 1.158, indicating that even the best generative method still has a substantial specificity gap. SBE-Diff improves the Delta Score by 64.8% over TargetDiff (0.552 vs. 0.335) while only marginally affecting QED (−1.2%), SA (−0.0%), and diversity (−0.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SBE-Diff outperforms all five baselines on the Delta Score metric, the paper's proposed specificity measure: it beats DecompDiff (0.354), DrugGPS (0.490), Pocket2Mol (0.437), AR (0.393), and TargetDiff (0.335) by margins of 55%, 12%, 26%, 41%, and 65% respectively. On the ratio of molecules surpassing reference ligands, SBE-Diff (0.300) also leads all baselines, exceeding the next-best DecompDiff (0.274) by 9.5%. In terms of absolute docking score, SBE-Diff (6.815) trails only DecompDiff (7.102) but exceeds TargetDiff (6.665), DrugGPS (6.554), Pocket2Mol (5.946), and AR (5.737). The closest comparison is with TargetDiff (the base diffusion architecture): SBE-Diff improves the Delta Score by 64.8% and the ratio by 15.8% while sacrificing only 1.2% QED and maintaining identical SA and diversity scores. Notably, even SBE-Diff's best Delta Score (0.552) remains well below the reference ligand (1.158), highlighting that specificity remains an open challenge across all current generative methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the TargetDiff architecture (Guan et al., 2023a) for SE(3)-equivariant molecular diffusion and the energy-guided SDE framework of Bao et al. (2022), but departs conceptually by reframing the SBDD objective from maximizing the joint probability p(x,y) to maximizing the conditional binding probability p(y|x). It introduces the Delta Score as a new specificity metric derived from the Boltzmann distribution, and trains a Specific Binding Energy (SBE) function via in-batch contrastive learning (inspired by CLIP-style training) to guide the diffusion process. The paper positions itself as both a critical re-examination of how SBDD is evaluated (arguing docking scores conflate unconditional and conditional binding) and a methodological fix that explicitly models selectivity during generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that ~30% of randomly sampled training molecules match or exceed reference ligands in docking score, and that only 2/20 best-scoring generated molecules rank their true target highest across all test pockets, challenges a core assumption in SBDD benchmarking and motivates the Delta Score as a more meaningful evaluation metric. The SBE-Diff method demonstrates that specificity can be improved (64.8% delta score gain over TargetDiff) without sacrificing drug-likeness (QED, SA) or diversity, offering a practical recipe for future SBDD pipelines. However, the evaluation is confined to CrossDocked2020 with Glide scoring, the absolute delta scores remain far below reference ligands (0.55 vs 1.16), and it remains open whether the specificity gains translate to wet-lab binding selectivity or whether the contrastive in-batch negatives adequately capture the full pocket distribution.
