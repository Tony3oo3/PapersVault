## Problem

Continual audio-video pre-training faces two underexplored challenges: the spatio-temporal correlation between audio and video is highly sparse (only a few sound-source regions are truly correlated), and the model suffers from multimodal correlation overwriting, where learning new audio-video semantics erases previously learned cross-modal associations. No prior work addresses continual pre-training of audio-video models in a task-free setting without human annotations or task boundary information.

## Input / Output

The method consumes audio spectrogram patches (time × frequency, M patches per sample) and video clip patches (frames × spatial, N patches per sample) from 10-second clips. Rather than processing all patches, it selects a subset controlled by sampling ratios ρ_a and ρ_v (default 0.5), producing a reduced input (X̂_a, X̂_v) for the masked autoencoder backbone. The AVM module outputs per-patch importance scores and correlation scores, while STELLA+ additionally stores these selected patches (rather than raw data) in rehearsal memory, reducing memory footprint by ~45%.

## Method

STELLA introduces a probabilistic patch selection framework built on two components. First, a lightweight Audio-Video Matching (AVM) module computes cross-attention maps between audio and video tokens, from which importance scores are derived via softmax and mean-pooling, identifying the sparse set of spatio-temporally aligned patches. Second, Replay-guided Correlation Assessment compares cross-attention maps activated by current queries against those from past queries (stored in rehearsal memory); a softmax over the concatenated current-and-past attention yields correlation scores that flag patches more strongly associated with past data. Selection proceeds by applying a Bernoulli distribution to correlation scores to zero out high-past-correlation patches, then a multinomial distribution over remaining importance scores to sample informative patches; audio patches are selected in time chunks to preserve temporal continuity. The selected patches feed a DER++-based masked autoencoder with reconstruction, contrastive, and penalty losses, and the AVM module is trained with a binary audio-video matching objective without backpropagating through the backbone.

## Results

On Continual-VS zero-shot retrieval, STELLA+ achieves 15.44% average recall (A→V) and 15.53% (V→A), outperforming the strongest baseline ESMER (13.58% / 13.33%) by 1.86%p and 2.20%p respectively. On the larger Continual-AS benchmark, STELLA+ reaches 15.26% (A→V) and 14.47% (V→A), a 3.69%p gain over ESMER on V→A retrieval. In terms of efficiency, STELLA+ reduces GPU memory from 30.95 GB (DER++) to 17.15 GB (~45% reduction) while simultaneously improving throughput. On downstream tasks, STELLA+ achieves 58.54% accuracy on audiovisual classification and 57.26 MIoU on audiovisual segmentation, both surpassing all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STELLA+ outperforms all seven task-free continual learning baselines (ER, MIR, DER++, GMED, CLS-ER, LUMP, ESMER) on both Continual-VS and Continual-AS zero-shot retrieval, with the largest margin over ESMER (the strongest baseline) of 3.69%p on Continual-AS V→A retrieval (14.47% vs. 10.78% avg recall). On Continual-VS, STELLA+ leads ESMER by 1.86%p (A→V) and 2.20%p (V→A), while also achieving the lowest forgetting scores (3.89 F on V→A vs. 5.79 for ESMER). DER++, the backbone continual learning framework used by STELLA, is surpassed by 2.92%p on Continual-VS A→V and 4.82%p on Continual-AS V→A. CLS-ER and ESMER, which maintain additional model copies, consume 32.50 GB and 31.45 GB GPU memory respectively, whereas STELLA+ uses only 17.15 GB (~45% less). MATS, an adaptive patch selection method for video pre-training, achieves 12.91% A→V on Continual-VS, well below STELLA's 14.16%, confirming that generic patch selection without audio-video correlation awareness is insufficient. LUMP, which mixes past and current data, shows the weakest performance among memory-based methods (10.88% A→V on Continual-VS), indicating that simple data mixing fails to capture sparse audio-video alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the masked autoencoder-based audiovisual pre-training line (TVLT, CAV) and the task-free continual learning literature (DER++, ESMER, LUMP), but departs from prior multimodal continual learning (AV-CIL, CIGN) by eliminating the need for supervised labels and task boundary information. Its core methodological contribution is repurposing cross-attention maps from a lightweight Audio-Video Matching module as a patch-selection signal, combining localized importance scoring with replay-guided correlation assessment—neither of which has a direct analogue in prior continual pre-training work (which typically operates at the sample or feature level rather than the patch level). The approach is grounded in the DER++ framework, extending it with a probabilistic patch selection mechanism that addresses the sparsity of audio-video correlations and the risk of multimodal correlation overwriting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formalize and solve continual audio-video pre-training in a task-free, label-free setting, opening a practically important direction as real-world audio-video models inevitably face distribution shifts (e.g., new sound sources, emerging semantics). The simultaneous improvement in retrieval performance (~3.7%p over the strongest baseline) and ~45% reduction in GPU memory consumption makes the approach attractive for deployment scenarios with limited compute. Open questions include whether the patch-level selection strategy generalizes to longer-form video, additional modalities (e.g., text), or larger-scale datasets, and whether the rehearsal memory requirement (even in the STELLA+ variant storing patches rather than raw data) can be further reduced or eliminated. The authors also note privacy concerns inherent to storing any subset of training data in rehearsal memory.
