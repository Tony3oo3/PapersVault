## Problem

Modern vision pipelines handle large images by downsampling or cropping, both of which discard either high-frequency detail or global context. No existing framework allows vision backbones trained on small images to model very large images end-to-end on contemporary GPUs while preserving both local and global information without quadratic memory growth.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240678/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

xT accepts large images of varying scale (up to 29,400 × 24,400 pixels for SAR satellite imagery, 800 × 800 for iNaturalist, 1024 × 2048 for Cityscapes) and produces task-specific outputs: class labels, dense segmentation masks, or detection boxes. The input is consumed in a nested, hierarchical fashion: the image is first subdivided into non-overlapping square regions (e.g., 256×256), each of which is further patchified internally by the region encoder. Region features are concatenated in row-major order and streamed through the context encoder, which may process them in chunks when the full sequence exceeds the context window.

## Method

xT is a streaming, two-stage architecture built on nested tokenization. In Stage 1, a myopic vision backbone (Swin, Hiera) independently encodes each region of the large image in batches, producing a sequence of local feature vectors. In Stage 2, a lightweight context encoder with near-linear complexity—either a Transformer-XL derivative using HyperAttention (for cross-chunk recurrence) or Mamba (for single-pass long-sequence modeling)—integrates global context across all local features, effectively extending the receptive field of the vision backbone to the entire image. The context encoder is deliberately shallow (1–2 layers) to keep parameter overhead at 5–10%, and uses stop-gradient recurrence or selective state-space dynamics to propagate information across chunks without full backpropagation through the entire sequence. The resulting contextualized feature sequence is then fed to a task-specific decoder (classification head, UNet-style segmentation decoder, or DetINO detection head).

## Results

On iNaturalist 2018 Reptilia classification, xT improves top-1 accuracy by up to 8.6% over the corresponding base model (e.g., Hiera-B 48.60% → Hiera-B<xT>Hyper 57.20%; Swin-T 53.76% → Swin-T<xT>Mamba 61.97%). On xView3-SAR segmentation (images ~29,400×24,400 px), xT with Transformer-XL boosts aggregate F1 by up to 13.4 points (Swin-T: 67.8 → 81.2) and close-to-shore F1 by up to 6.0 points. On Cityscapes detection, Swin-B-DetINO runs out of memory at 2048 px input, while Swin-B-DetINO<xT>XL achieves 43.0 mAP at 2048/512.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Swin Transformer v2 (Liu et al., 2022), xT consistently wins across all model sizes on iNaturalist Reptilia: Swin-T gains +8.2% (53.76→61.97), Swin-S gains +5.2% (58.45→63.62), Swin-B gains +5.5% (58.57→64.08), and Swin-L gains +4.7% (68.78→73.47), while using equal or fewer parameters and often less memory. Against Hiera (Ryali et al., 2023), the largest relative gain is observed: Hiera-B improves from 48.60% to 57.20% (+8.6%) with xT, the paper's headline classification result. Against DetINO (Zhang et al., 2022) on Cityscapes detection, the base Swin-B-DetINO backbone simply runs out of memory at 2048 px input, whereas Swin-B-DetINO<xT>XL achieves 43.0 mAP at 2048/512, making direct numerical comparison impossible but demonstrating xT's ability to handle inputs that are infeasible for the baseline. On xView3-SAR segmentation, xT variants outperform their non-context counterparts by up to 13.4 F1 points (Swin-T) and 11.1 F1 points (Swin-S), with the gap narrowing for Swin-B (+5.1), suggesting diminishing returns at larger backbone sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

xT positions itself as a lightweight, plug-in framework that repurposes long-context NLP models (Transformer-XL, Mamba, HyperAttention) as context encoders on top of existing hierarchical vision backbones (Swin, Hiera), rather than designing a new architecture from scratch. This departs from prior large-image approaches that rely on multi-pass cascading (Gadermayr et al., 2019) or sliding windows with suppression (Dalal & Triggs, 2005), by introducing a streaming two-stage pipeline where a myopic region encoder extracts local features and a near-linear context encoder integrates global semantics. The nested tokenization scheme (region-level subdivision followed by patch-level encoding within each region) is the key architectural contribution, enabling the context encoder to operate on a compressed sequence of region features rather than raw pixels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck—standard vision models cannot process images beyond a few megapixels without downsampling or cropping—and provides a simple, backbone-agnostic solution that scales to 29,000×29,000 pixel satellite imagery with near-constant memory. A notable limitation is that the nested tokenization introduces convolution-like aliasing artifacts in the effective receptive field, and Mamba currently lacks an efficient XL-style recurrence kernel, restricting its use in the cross-chunk setting. Open questions include whether the framework extends to video or 3D data, whether deeper context encoders yield diminishing returns, and whether the approach can be applied to generative or diffusion-based pipelines for large images.
