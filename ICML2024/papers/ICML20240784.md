## Problem

Existing dense model inversion methods reconstruct the entire image area, making them extremely inefficient when inverting high-resolution images from large-scale Vision Transformers. The paper identifies two underlying causes: redundant inversion of noisy backgrounds that contribute minimally to classification loss, and unintended inversion of spurious foreground-background correlations (termed "hallucination" in model inversion) that are improperly memorized by pre-trained models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240784/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on 224×224 RGB images inverted from pre-trained ViTs (DeiT/16-Tiny, DeiT/16-Base, CLIP ViT/32-Base) given a target class label and a randomly initialized input. The output is a sparsely inverted surrogate image at approximately 77% sparsity, where only semantically meaningful foreground patches are retained and background patches are permanently discarded (represented as black blocks). For downstream use, only the retained foreground patches are fed forward through the model, reducing token count and eliminating noisy backgrounds and potential spurious correlations.

## Method

SMI is a plug-and-play extension to existing dense inversion methods (e.g., DeepInversion) that requires no modification to their original loss functions. It comprises two components: (1) semantic patch identification, which uses the CLS token's attention weights from the final ViT layer (averaged across all attention heads) in the preceding iteration to rank patches by their contribution to label-relevant information; and (2) early inversion stopping, which progressively prunes the lowest-attention-weight patches at scheduled iterations (e.g., 30% at iterations 50, 100, 200, 300), permanently excluding them from both forward propagation and backward gradient computation. The multi-stage stopping strategy is conservative in early iterations when images are predominantly noisy and becomes more aggressive as semantic structure emerges. Theoretically, the paper demonstrates that sparsely inverted data increases the fraction of label-relevant patches (α) and decreases the noise level (τ), reducing both the required number of training samples and iterations for convergence in downstream ViT training.

## Results

On DeiT/16-Base (224×224, ImageNet), SMI achieves a 3.79× throughput speedup (4.51 vs. 1.19 its/s) and 75.62% FLOPs reduction (1578.97G vs. 6475.63G) over dense DeepInversion, while improving W4/A8 quantization Top-1 from 75.99% to 77.51% and W8/A8 from 78.58% to 79.63%. On DeiT/16-Tiny, the speedup is 2.57× with a 74.09% FLOPs reduction, and quantization accuracy is comparable (64.04% vs. 64.28% for W4/A8). For data-free knowledge transfer on CIFAR-10, sparsely inverted data dramatically outperforms dense inversion: 90.08% vs. 69.51% (DeiT/16-Tiny) and 95.10% vs. 90.02% (DeiT/16-Base), approaching the teacher's own accuracy (90.23% and 95.36% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DeepInversion (Dense) [Yin et al., CVPR 2020], SMI achieves 2.57–3.79× inversion speedup and 74–76% FLOPs reduction across both model scales. On DeiT/16-Base quantization, SMI improves W4/A8 Top-1 by +1.52 pp (77.51 vs. 75.99) and W8/A8 by +1.05 pp (79.63 vs. 78.58), while on DeiT/16-Tiny the drop is marginal (−0.24 pp for W4/A8, −0.14 pp for W8/A8). For knowledge transfer on CIFAR-10, SMI dramatically outperforms dense DeepInversion: 90.08% vs. 69.51% (DeiT/16-Tiny, +20.57 pp) and 95.10% vs. 90.02% (DeiT/16-Base, +5.08 pp), with the dense method even failing to converge for the Tiny model. Against PSAQ-ViT (Dense) [Li et al., ECCV 2022], SMI is 25× faster on DeiT/16-Tiny (18.82 vs. 0.74 its/s) and 9.8× faster on DeiT/16-Base (4.51 vs. 0.46 its/s), while also improving DeiT/16-Base W4/A8 accuracy from 76.73% to 77.51%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends dense model inversion methods (primarily DeepInversion and PSAQ-ViT) by introducing a sparse inversion strategy that selectively inverts only semantic foreground patches while progressively discarding noisy backgrounds. It draws on the token sparsification literature (DynamicViT, AdaViT, etc.) for the observation that background patches consume bandwidth but contribute little to predictions, but is the first to apply this principle to the inversion process itself. The paper also connects to the spurious correlation literature, identifying a novel 'hallucination' phenomenon where pre-trained models' memorized foreground-background correlations are unintentionally reconstructed during inversion. As a plug-and-play extension requiring no modification to existing loss functions, it positions itself as a drop-in accelerator for the broader data-free learning ecosystem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.79× inversion speedup with reduced FLOPs and memory is practically valuable for data-free applications involving large ViTs, where dense inversion is prohibitively expensive. The conceptual contribution—identifying that dense inversion inadvertently reconstructs spurious correlations ('hallucination')—opens a new line of inquiry into what information is actually being extracted from pre-trained models. However, the evaluation is limited to DeiT architectures at 224×224 resolution and two downstream tasks; whether the approach generalizes to other ViT variants (Swin, ConvNeXt-V2), higher resolutions, or tasks like detection and segmentation remains open. The fixed stopping schedule (30% at iterations 50/100/200/300) is empirically tuned and may not transfer across model scales or datasets without re-tuning.
