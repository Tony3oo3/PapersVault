## Problem

No sample-efficient, theoretically grounded risk-sensitive RL algorithm existed for partially observable environments. Prior theoretical work on risk-sensitive POMDPs addressed only the existence of optimal policies or planning with fully known transition probabilities, leaving the online learning and sample complexity problem entirely open. The combination of partial observability and non-linear entropic risk measures creates compounding technical obstacles for both model estimation and exploration design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242421/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During each episode of H steps, the agent receives only partial observations (not hidden states) from a tabular POMDP, but at episode end it reviews the hidden states that actually occurred (hindsight observation), which is used to update the empirical transition and emission matrices. The risk sensitivity parameter γ ≠ 0 governs the entropic risk measure, with γ > 0 indicating risk-seeking and γ < 0 risk-averse behavior. The algorithm outputs a sequence of risk-sensitive policies and a polynomial regret bound that explicitly decomposes into risk-awareness, statistical error, and history-dependency factors.

## Method

BVVI (Beta Vector Value Iteration) is a UCB-style model-based algorithm that applies a change-of-measure transformation to convert the POMDP into a 'reference model' where observations are independent of hidden states, decoupling transition and emission processes for tractable analysis. The key analytical innovation is the beta vector, a novel surrogate for value functions that enables simplified Bellman equations (where V_{H+1} is non-zero, necessitating a belief propagation phase before value iteration) and a risk-sensitive bonus function. The bonus scales with |e^{γ(H-h+1)} − 1| and combines transition and emission error residues to encourage exploration while respecting the agent's risk sensitivity. Risk belief vectors (forward-evolving) and conjugate beliefs (backward-evolving) jointly expose the dynamic programming structure hidden within the entropic risk objective, and the empirical beta vectors incorporate an optimism term to guarantee over-estimation of value functions. The resulting regret bound is polynomial in all parameters and improves upon or matches existing bounds in the risk-neutral and fully observable degenerate cases.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 6.1) establishes a regret upper bound of Õ((e^{|γ|H}−1)/(|γ|H) · H² · √(KS²OA) · √(H ln(KHSOA/δ))) for the BVVI algorithm, which is polynomial in all problem parameters. The corresponding sample complexity (Corollary 6.2) requires K ≳ (1/(ε²δ²)) · ((e^{|γ|H}−1)/(|γ|H))² · H⁵S²OA · ln(KHSOA/δ) episodes. Empirically, BVVI achieves sublinear regret growing at approximately O(√K) in both MDP and POMDP settings, and regret increases monotonically with |γ|, consistent with the theoretical prediction that the risk-awareness factor (e^{|γ|H}−1)/(|γ|H) amplifies the bound as risk sensitivity grows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the risk-neutral POMDP setting (γ→0), this paper's bound improves upon Lee et al. (2023) in the order of S, A, and H, attributed to a refined analysis that avoids the H⁴S²A(1+lnK) term present in the prior work. In the fully observable risk-sensitive MDP setting, BVVI degenerates to the algorithm of Fei et al. (2021a) and matches their upper bound Õ((e^{|γ|H}−1)/(|γ|H) · √(KH⁴S²A)). The regret also nearly reaches the information-theoretic lower bound for risk-sensitive RL from Fei et al. (2020), with the order of H only slightly higher (H² vs. H^{3/2}). Notably, the bound is independent of the minimum singular value α of the emission matrices, unlike prior POMDP learning results that require structural observability assumptions, thereby extending applicability to emission kernels lacking full column rank.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: risk-sensitive RL in fully observable MDPs (Fei et al. 2020, 2021a), sample-efficient POMDP learning with hindsight observations (Lee et al. 2023), and risk-sensitive POMDP planning with known models (James et al. 1994; Bäauerle & Rieder 2017). The change-of-measure technique, risk beliefs, and conjugate beliefs are inherited from the planning literature (James et al. 1994; Cavazos-Cadena & Hernandez-Hernandez 2005), while the UCB-style bonus design and regret analysis framework follow Fei et al. 2021a. The key departure is that prior risk-sensitive POMDP work assumed fully known transition and emission matrices and addressed only planning, whereas this paper tackles the learning problem with an unknown non-stationary model, introducing the beta vector as a new analytical tool to bridge the gap between the nonlinear risk measure and the partially observable structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provably sample-efficient algorithm for risk-sensitive POMDP learning, filling a gap where prior work either assumed known models (planning) or ignored risk (risk-neutral POMDP learning). The regret bound being independent of the emission matrix's minimum singular value α broadens applicability to POMDPs with rank-deficient emission kernels, a class excluded by most prior POMDP learning results. Open questions include extending to function approximation for large or infinite state spaces, and generalizing to other risk measures such as CVaR or coherent risk measures. The hindsight observation assumption, while common in practice (data center scheduling, sim-to-real transfer), remains a significant structural requirement that limits the result's generality.
