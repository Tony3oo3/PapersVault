## Problem

Multi-layer hierarchical generative models (e.g., NVAE, HVAE) assume a Gaussian prior that only models inter-layer dependencies, creating a 'prior hole' where the prior fails to match the aggregated generator posterior. While energy-based model (EBM) priors can bridge this gap, learning a single marginal EBM over multi-layer latent variables is intractable in practice: the posterior is highly multi-modal and the latent layers operate at very different scales, making MCMC sampling (e.g., Langevin dynamics) fail to mix between modes.

## Input / Output

At sampling time, the model starts from standard Gaussian noise in a uni-scale u-space and iteratively reverses T diffusion steps (typically T=3) via Langevin dynamics, then transforms back to the multi-layer z-space and passes through the frozen hierarchical generator (NVAE with 30 layers or HVAE with 3 layers) to produce RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR-10), 256×256 (CelebA-HQ), or 64×64 (LSUN-Church, CelebA-64). For controllable generation, a label or multi-attribute vector is coupled into the energy function at the top layers (t=0 step), conditioning the reverse sampling. For OOD detection, the model outputs per-layer energy scores of inferred latent samples, with top-layer scores serving as the decision function.

## Method

The paper replaces the single marginal EBM prior with a sequence of conditional EBMs learned within a diffusion probabilistic framework, where each conditional EBM at step t only needs to match slightly perturbed samples (u_t given u_{t+1}), making the energy landscape far less multi-modal and the MCMC sampling tractable. Crucially, the forward and reverse diffusion processes operate in a uni-scale u-space obtained by an invertible reparameterization of the Gaussian prior (z_i = μ(z_{i+1}) + σ(z_{i+1})·u_i), which preserves the hierarchical inter-layer dependency structure during noise addition and ensures that the final noisy state corresponds exactly to the Gaussian prior. The energy function F_ω(T_{β>0}(u_t), t) = Σ_i f_{ω_i}(z_i, t) couples all layers simultaneously, capturing both inter-layer and intra-layer relations. For each conditional EBM, Langevin dynamics is initialized at the given u_{t+1} rather than random noise, localizing the search to nearby modes and avoiding the mixing problem of marginal EBM sampling. Training uses a two-stage scheme: the hierarchical generator is fixed, and the EBM parameters are optimized via a score-matching gradient that contrasts forward-trajectory samples with conditional-EBM prior samples at randomly selected diffusion steps.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the proposed diffusion-learned EBM prior (T=3) achieves FID 8.93 and IS 9.03, outperforming the direct baseline Joint-EBM (FID 11.34) and DRL-EBM (FID 9.58). On CelebA-HQ-256 and LSUN-Church-64, the method achieves FID 8.78 and 7.34 respectively, surpassing Joint-EBM (9.89, 8.38) and NCP-VAE (24.79). The method remains competitive with strong GAN baselines (StyleGANv2: FID 9.9 on CIFAR-10; PGGAN: FID 8.03 on CelebA-HQ-256) but trails the best diffusion models (DDPM: FID 3.17; LSGM: FID 2.10 on CIFAR-10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method clearly outperforms all hierarchical VAE baselines with Gaussian priors: NVAE (FID 37.73), HVAE (81.44), and BIVA (66.37) on CIFAR-10, confirming that the EBM prior effectively addresses the prior hole problem. Against the direct EBM-prior baselines, the method beats Joint-EBM by 2.41 FID on CIFAR-10 (8.93 vs 11.34) and by 1.11 FID on CelebA-HQ-256 (8.78 vs 9.89), and outperforms NCP-VAE by a large margin (8.93 vs 24.08). The method also edges out DRL-EBM on CIFAR-10 (8.93 vs 9.58) but is slightly worse on LSUN-Church-64 (7.34 vs 7.04). Compared to other EBM approaches, the method beats Dual-MCMC (9.26), VAEBM (12.19), and Adv-EBM (13.21) on CIFAR-10 FID. However, the method still trails the strongest diffusion and GAN baselines: DDPM achieves FID 3.17 and LSGM 2.10 on CIFAR-10, while PGGAN reaches FID 8.03 on CelebA-HQ-256 and 6.42 on LSUN-Church-64, indicating a remaining gap to state-of-the-art generative models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) joint EBM priors for multi-layer hierarchical generators (Cui et al., 2023a), which learn a single marginal EBM but suffer from difficult MCMC sampling over multi-scale latent spaces; (2) diffusion recovery likelihood (Gao et al., 2020), which replaces a single marginal EBM with a sequence of conditional EBMs to ease sampling, but operates on data space or single-layer latent space; and (3) the uni-scale u-space transformation (Xiao et al., 2020; Cui et al., 2023a), which reparameterizes multi-scale latent variables into a uniform Gaussian space. The key conceptual move is combining all three: applying the diffusion-recovery conditional EBM scheme within the u-space of a multi-layer hierarchical generator, so that the forward diffusion process preserves inter-layer conditional dependencies while the reverse process only needs to match locally perturbed samples at each step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled recipe for bridging the prior hole in deep hierarchical VAEs (NVAE, HVAE) without retraining the generator backbone, and the u-space formulation is a clean solution to the problem of diffusion destroying hierarchical structure. However, the method remains clearly behind modern diffusion models (FID 8.93 vs. 3.17 for DDPM on CIFAR-10) and requires iterative Langevin sampling at each reverse step, which the authors acknowledge as a bottleneck. Open questions include whether the Langevin sampler can be amortized or replaced (as in diffusion-amortized MCMC), whether the approach scales to even deeper hierarchies, and whether the two-stage training scheme can be made end-to-end without sacrificing the efficiency gains that motivated it.
