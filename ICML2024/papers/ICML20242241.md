## Problem

AdamW with decoupled weight decay empirically outperforms Adam with ℓ2 regularization in optimization and generalization, yet it is unclear what objective AdamW actually optimizes. While Adam with ℓ2 regularization provably converges to stationary points of the regularized loss, AdamW's decoupled weight decay does not correspond to any obvious regularized objective, leaving its implicit bias completely uncharacterized.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a continuously differentiable loss L: ℝ^d → ℝ, an initialization x₀, a non-increasing learning rate schedule {η_t} with divergent partial sum (∑η_t = ∞), and a weight decay coefficient λ, under the condition β₁ ≤ β₂ < 1. The output is a characterization of the limit point: if AdamW converges to x_∞, then x_∞ must be a KKT point of min_{‖x‖∞ ≤ 1/λ} L(x), and for convex L, x_∞ is the constrained minimizer. The β₁ ≤ β₂ condition is necessary; a counterexample in Appendix B.3 shows AdamW can converge outside the ℓ∞ ball when β₁ > β₂.

## Method

The proof proceeds in two stages. First, the paper shows that normalized steepest descent with weight decay (NSD-WD) for any norm is exactly equivalent to Frank-Wolfe over the corresponding norm ball of radius 1/λ (Theorem 3.2), and that NSD-WD must converge to KKT points of the norm-constrained problem (Theorem 3.7). Second, it establishes that AdamW asymptotically behaves like SignGD with weight decay (i.e., NSD-WD w.r.t. ℓ∞ norm), since Adam is a smoothed version of SignGD that reduces correlation between numerator and denominator via moving averages. The key technical difficulty is that individual Adam updates ‖m_t/√v_t‖ can exceed 1, unlike NSD-WD where updates are normalized by construction; this is resolved by a novel tight upper bound on the average update size of Adam (Lemma 4.2), which holds even in stochastic settings and scales only logarithmically in 1/ε rather than linearly. The bound also yields practical guidance: for constant learning rate, the ℓ∞ norm of iterates is bounded by 1/λ when β₁ ≈ β₂ or λη ≪ 1 − β₂ < 1 − β₁, a prediction verified on PTB language modeling.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1.1) shows that AdamW with β₁ ≤ β₂ < 1 and a non-increasing learning rate schedule with divergent partial sum must converge to a KKT point of min_{‖x‖∞ ≤ 1/λ} L(x), and to the constrained minimizer if L is convex. On the PTB language modeling task (two-layer transformer, full-batch, 12800 epochs, η = √10 × 10⁻³), the ℓ∞ norm of AdamW iterates is empirically bounded by 1/λ (i.e., ≤ 1 for λ=1 and ≤ 0.5 for λ=2) when β₁ = β₂ ∈ {0.99, 0.999} or when β₁=0.9, β₂=0.95, while Adam's ℓ∞ norm keeps increasing without bound. On a 100-dimensional synthetic quadratic loss g(x) = Σ(xᵢ - xᵢ*)²/i², normalized steepest descent w.r.t. ℓ∞ norm with weight decay converges substantially faster than its ℓ₂ counterpart, consistent with the theoretical prediction that the smoothness constant H is bounded by π²/3 for ℓ∞ but equals 2 for ℓ₂ while the ℓ₂ norm of x* can be √d times larger.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary empirical comparison is between Adam and AdamW on the PTB language modeling task. Adam's ℓ∞ norm of parameters increases monotonically throughout training, while AdamW's ℓ∞ norm is constrained below 1/λ for both λ=1 and λ=2, directly verifying Theorem 1.1's prediction. This constraint holds when β₁ = β₂ (e.g., both 0.99 or 0.999) or when λη ≪ 1 - β₂ (e.g., β₁=0.9, β₂=0.95), but fails under the default setting β₁=0.9, β₂=0.999 where 1-β₂ < λη < 1-β₁, confirming the necessity of the β₁ ≤ β₂ condition. On the synthetic 100-dimensional problem, steepest descent w.r.t. ℓ∞ norm (both normalized and unnormalized) consistently outperforms the ℓ₂ norm counterpart in training loss, and adding weight decay accelerates convergence for both norms, supporting the conjecture that language modeling losses have better geometric properties under ℓ∞ than under ℓ₂.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the implicit bias literature—previously dominated by analyses of SGD and GD variants (Soudry et al., 2018; Gunasekar et al., 2018; Wilson et al., 2017)—to the adaptive optimizer AdamW, which had no prior theoretical characterization of its convergence target. The key technical bridge is the observation that normalized steepest descent with weight decay (NSD-WD) is exactly equivalent to Frank-Wolfe (Frank & Wolfe, 1956) over the corresponding norm ball, a connection also recently noted for the Lion optimizer (Chen et al., 2023). The paper then shows AdamW is a smoothed version of SignGD (normalized steepest descent w.r.t. ℓ∞ norm), formalizing the empirical observation by Kunstner et al. (2022) that SignGD closes the gap between Adam and SGD in the full-batch regime. Unlike Chen et al. (2023), whose Lion-K framework cannot capture AdamW's second-momentum dependence, this paper introduces a novel upper bound on Adam's average update size (Lemma 4.2) to handle the additional complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical explanation for why AdamW outperforms Adam with ℓ2 regularization: AdamW implicitly enforces an ℓ∞ norm constraint rather than an ℓ2 penalty, and the ℓ∞ geometry may be better suited to language modeling loss landscapes (smaller smoothness, as shown in the synthetic example). The result is limited to the full-batch deterministic setting and requires the condition β1 ≤ β2, leaving the stochastic regime and the default β1=0.9, β2=0.999 setting theoretically uncharacterized. Open questions include whether the ℓ∞ geometry advantage generalizes to real-world LLM training, whether non-asymptotic convergence rates can be established for AdamW, and how gradient noise interacts with the 1/λ norm bound when β2 is close to 1.
