## Problem

Solving Dec-POMDPs via single-player reformulation requires optimizing over joint decision rules of all players simultaneously at each stage, yielding double-exponential complexity that limits global methods to two or three players. Under hierarchical information sharing (HIS)—where each player knows its subordinate's actions and observations—this entanglement is unnecessary, yet no prior work showed how to exploit HIS to disentangle per-player decisions while preserving optimality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241640/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a finite-horizon Dec-POMDP tuple (states, per-player actions and observations, transition/reward models) together with an HIS chain specifying that player i observes player i−1's action and observation at every stage. It outputs a joint policy as a tuple of per-player, per-stage decision rules (each mapping a private history to an action) along with point-based value function estimates at selected occupancy states. The per-player decomposition means the planner prescribes one decision rule at a time rather than a monolithic joint rule, and the value function is represented as a finite collection of linear functions over occupancy states.

## Method

The key insight is that under HIS, a single-stage subgame can be decomposed into a perfect-information extensive-form game in which a sequential-move central planner acts for player 1, then player 2, …, up to player n, where each player's information set includes all subordinate histories. The paper proves (Theorem 2.2) that backward induction on this game yields the same optimal joint decision rule as the original simultaneous-move subgame, reducing backup complexity from double-exponential O(|O*|^|U*|^n) to linear O(n|Σ||U*|). To keep the game tree tractable, the authors introduce nested-occupancy states—sufficient statistics combining a nested belief state, the player's private history, and subordinate actions—that aggregate equivalent histories and further compress the tree. The resulting hPBVI algorithm plugs this extensive-form backup into the standard point-based value iteration loop, with an error bound matching the infinite-horizon POMDP bound of Pineau et al. (2003) as the horizon grows.

## Results

The proposed hPBVI algorithm achieves the best or near-best value across all 6 benchmarks (tiger, recycling, mabc, grid3x3, mars, boxpushing) while scaling to 10 players. On mabc(10) at horizon ℓ=30, hPBVI completes point-based backups in 139.82 s per backup with a value of 27.75, whereas PBVI^enum times out at 4 players and PBVI^milp at 5 players. Backup time for hPBVI on grid3x3(2) is 0.61 s versus 1329.33 s for PBVI^milp, a ~2000× speedup. On the largest tested domain recycling(8), hPBVI reaches a value of 600.00 compared to 126.19 for A2C and −244.02 for IQL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PBVI^enum (brute-force enumeration), hPBVI wins on every benchmark where both complete: it achieves equal or higher values while being orders of magnitude faster (e.g., 0.61 s vs. timeout on grid3x3(2)). Against PBVI^milp (MILP-based implicit enumeration), hPBVI matches values on small 2-player domains (e.g., recycling(2): both 93.73) but pulls ahead as team size grows, reaching values of 600.00 on recycling(8) and 27.75 on mabc(10) where PBVI^milp times out entirely. Against the local methods A2C and IQL, hPBVI achieves strictly higher values on all benchmarks except mabc(4) (24.94 vs. 24.36) and grid3x3(3) (28.16 vs. 27.92), where the local methods happen to find near-global optima due to weak coupling. The gap widens dramatically with team size: on recycling(8), hPBVI's value of 600.00 exceeds A2C's 126.19 by a factor of ~4.8, while IQL produces a negative value (−244.02). Overall, hPBVI is the only method that simultaneously maintains global optimality guarantees and scales beyond 5 players.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the single-player reformulation of Dec-POMDPs via occupancy states (Dibangoye et al., 2013; 2016) and the point-based value iteration framework (Pineau et al., 2003), extending both to exploit hierarchical information sharing (HIS). It generalizes the two-player one-sided information-sharing result of Xie et al. (2020) to arbitrary n-player hierarchies by proving that single-stage subgames can be decomposed into perfect-information extensive-form games, reducing point-based backup complexity from double-exponential to linear in the number of players. The introduction of nested-occupancy states as sufficient statistics departs from prior compact occupancy state approaches (Dibangoye et al., 2016) by using more concise nested belief states for more aggressive history compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work removes the primary scalability barrier for global Dec-POMDP solvers under a very natural information structure (corporate hierarchies, military command, vehicle platooning), enabling optimal or near-optimal planning for teams of 10+ players where prior methods fail at 4–5. Open questions include extending the analysis to tree-structured hierarchies, understanding how HIS interacts with other exploitable structures (dynamics independence, weak-separability), and whether the sequential-move reformulation transfers to general-sum or learning-based settings. The empirical observation that local methods (A2C, IQL) can match global methods on medium-sized teams with weakly coupled players is intriguing but the authors rightly caution against overgeneralizing from limited benchmarks.
