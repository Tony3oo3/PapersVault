## Problem

Temperature-conditional GFlowNets, which learn a family of generative policies p(x|β) ∝ R(x)^β to control exploration-exploitation, suffer from severe training instability because different temperatures produce vastly different gradient profiles and logit magnitudes. Prior approaches that condition on β by concatenating temperature embeddings into network layers (Layer-GFN) only partially alleviate this, and the empirical understanding of why and how temperature conditioning destabilizes training remained limited.

## Input / Output

The model takes a partial compositional state (a molecule graph built from building blocks or a biological sequence) and a scalar inverse temperature β, and outputs a forward policy distribution over the next action (prepend/append a token). The key design choice is that β enters the policy not as a concatenated embedding but as a scalar that is mapped through a small learned network into a softmax temperature T, which directly rescales the policy logits before normalization. The partition function Z_θ(β) is likewise parameterized as a learned function of β rather than a constant.

## Method

Logit-GFN introduces a logit-scaling network f_θ: ℝ → ℝ (a two-layer MLP with Softplus output) that maps the inverse temperature β to a softmax temperature T, and divides the β-independent policy logits α_θ(s,s′) by T before the softmax in the forward policy. This creates a direct skip-connection pathway from β to the output distribution, providing an inductive bias that lets the network adapt its decision sharpness without reparameterizing the entire policy. Training minimizes the trajectory balance loss with β sampled from P_train(β), and the partition function is conditioned on β via a separate scalar-to-scalar MLP. Optionally, the encoder output of f_θ can be concatenated with state embeddings to recover full layer-conditioning expressiveness. For online mode-seeking, the method samples β from a dynamic control policy P_exp(β) (e.g., simulated annealing) during exploration, marginalizing over multiple tempered distributions to discover diverse high-reward modes without training separate GFNs per temperature.

## Results

On the combinatorial optimization tasks (Table 3), Logit-GFN(DB) achieves 19.20±0.07 on MIS(Small) vs. 17.93±0.69 for GFN(DB), 35.65±0.61 on MIS(Large) vs. 34.19±0.37, 16.54±0.20 on MC(Small) vs. 15.56±0.11, and 34.08±3.15 on MC(Large) vs. 29.36±0.01. On the four biochemical tasks (QM9, sEH, TFBind8, RNA-Binding), Logit-GFN consistently discovers more modes than all baselines (unconditional GFN, MARS, A2C, Soft Q-Learning, PPO) over 2,000–5,000 online training rounds, while also exhibiting stable loss convergence at extreme inverse temperatures (β=5,000) where unconditional GFN diverges. In offline generalization, Logit-GFN generates samples whose 25th-percentile and median rewards surpass the offline dataset when queried with out-of-distribution high β values, whereas unconditional GFN trained at the same high β degrades in performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Logit-GFN outperforms all five baselines across every evaluated task. Against unconditional GFN (trained at a fixed β), Logit-GFN wins decisively on training stability (smooth loss at β=5,000 vs. extreme instability), offline generalization (higher 25th-percentile and median rewards at out-of-distribution β), and online mode discovery (more modes found in fewer rounds on all four biochemical tasks). Against Layer-GFN (the prior temperature-conditional architecture), Logit-GFN shows superior training stability and more modes discovered, while Layer-GFN occasionally matches Logit-GFN on offline generalization at high β but fails to adaptively shift its reward distribution across temperatures. Against RL baselines (A2C with entropy regularization, Soft Q-Learning, PPO) and the MCMC-based MARS method, Logit-GFN discovers more diverse high-reward modes in online mode-seeking on QM9, sEH, TFBind8, and RNA-Binding, demonstrating that temperature-conditional GFlowNets with logit scaling provide a more effective exploration-exploitation balance than standard RL or MCMC approaches in these compositional generation tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GFlowNet framework (Bengio et al., 2021) and the trajectory balance training objective (Malkin et al., 2022a), extending the temperature-conditional GFlowNet line of work initiated by Zhang et al. (2022c) for scheduling and Zhou et al. (2023) for phylogenetic inference. The key departure is architectural: rather than the standard layer-conditioning approach (concatenating temperature embeddings into network layers, as in diffusion models and prior Topoformer-based GFlowNets), Logit-GFN introduces a simple skip connection that scales the policy's logits by a learned scalar function of β. This reframes temperature conditioning as a direct modulation of softmax sharpness rather than a change in network parameters, providing a stronger inductive bias for the tempered reward landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first generic, task-agnostic architecture for stable temperature-conditional GFlowNet training, removing the need for task-specific customization that plagued prior approaches. The online discovery algorithm with dynamic β sampling (particularly simulated annealing) offers a practical recipe for balancing exploration and exploitation in scientific discovery tasks like molecular design. However, the evaluation is confined to four biochemical tasks and small combinatorial optimization problems; the authors acknowledge that effectiveness in very high-dimensional or highly complex generative tasks remains unexplored. An open question is whether the logit-scaling principle generalizes to other conditional generative frameworks beyond GFlowNets.
