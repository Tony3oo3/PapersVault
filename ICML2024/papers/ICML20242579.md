## Problem

Existing gradient-based attribution methods (IG, AGI, BIG, GIG) rely on fixed reference points, adversarial sample quality, or specific gradient constraints, resulting in limited exploration of attribution integration paths and inconsistent interpretability. The paper addresses the gap of how to systematically and iteratively search the feature space to identify the most faithful features without depending on a single linear path or a single adversarial sample.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242579/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224×3 RGB image and a trained DNN (Inception-v3, ResNet-50, VGG16, or ViT-B/16) as input. It produces a per-pixel attribution map where each feature receives a normalized importance score in [0,1] per iteration, with the final attribution A* being the sum across all iterations. Features are processed in batches of k=5000 per iteration (≈30 iterations total for a full image), and removed features are clipped to zero so the model treats them as unseen in subsequent iterations.

## Method

ISA performs iterative attribution by alternately applying gradient ascent (to identify features whose removal disrupts the model) and gradient descent (to identify features whose removal weakens the model's decision), using sign(∂L/∂x) to ensure fair, equal-magnitude feature perturbations across all dimensions. At each iteration, the k features with the lowest combined normalized attribution (A̅ = A̅_a + A̅_d, where each is divided by its respective total loss change ΔL) are clipped to zero and assigned their importance scores. A scale parameter S ∈ [1,2) is applied so that attribution values from later iterations are always strictly greater than those from earlier ones (a̅_γ = a̅_γ·S + γ), enforcing the principle that features removed later are more important. The method is proven to satisfy both Sensitivity and Implementation Invariance axioms, and Theorem 4.1 establishes that the sign-based perturbation maximizes loss change under the fairness constraint, making it the optimal direction for feature exploration.

## Results

On ImageNet (1000 samples), ISA achieves the highest Insertion score across all three evaluated models: 0.7335 on Inception-v3, 0.6073 on ResNet-50, and 0.5085 on VGG16, outperforming the strongest baseline AGI by margins of 0.109, 0.094, and 0.078 respectively. ISA also attains the lowest INFD (faithfulness) score on all three models (4.82, 0.83, and 0.56), confirming superior attribution fidelity. The average Insertion score improvement over all other baselines is 0.3206 (Inception-v3), 0.3538 (ResNet-50), and 0.3074 (VGG16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ISA dominates all seven gradient-based baselines on the primary Insertion score metric across Inception-v3, ResNet-50, and VGG16. The closest competitor is AGI (Pan et al., IJCAI 2021), which ISA outperforms by 0.109 (Inception-v3), 0.094 (ResNet-50), and 0.078 (VGG16) in Insertion score; on INFD faithfulness, ISA edges out AGI by 0.16 on Inception-v3 (4.82 vs 4.98) and by a large margin on VGG16 (0.56 vs 3.36). GIG (Kapishnikov et al., CVPR 2021) achieves the lowest Deletion scores (e.g., 0.0363 on Inception-v3) but trails ISA substantially on Insertion (0.3194 vs 0.7335), which the authors argue is the more reliable metric. BIG (Wang et al., ICML 2022) and SM (Simonyan et al., 2013) are outperformed by ISA by 0.25 and 0.20 Insertion points respectively on Inception-v3. In the NLP/LSTM experiment (Table 4), ISA achieves the best Insertion score (0.8316) over IG (0.6825), DeepLIFT (0.6658), and AGI (0.6691). Against Grad-CAM (Selvaraju et al., ICCV 2017), ISA wins on both Insertion (0.7293 vs 0.5798) and Deletion (0.0745 vs 0.1594) on Inception-v3. On ViT-B/16 (non-CNN), ISA achieves the best Insertion score (0.6715) over AGI (0.5014) and IG (0.3782), though its Deletion score (0.1153) is slightly higher than IG's (0.068).

## Relation to Prior Work

ISA synthesizes ideas from several distinct lineages within gradient-based attribution: the axiomatic framework of Integrated Gradients (Sundararajan et al., 2017), the adversarial-path search of AGI (Pan et al., 2021), and the sign-function fairness principle from adversarial attacks (Goodfellow et al., 2014). Its key conceptual departure is the iterative, autoregressive feature-clipping mechanism inspired by diffusion models, which replaces the single linear or adversarial integration path with a multi-round search that progressively removes unimportant features. The paper also introduces a formal 'fairness' axiom and a scale parameter to enforce monotonic importance across iterations, neither of which appears in prior attribution work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ISA achieves the highest Insertion scores and lowest INFD scores across three CNN architectures and a ViT, offering practitioners a more faithful attribution tool than existing gradient-based methods. However, the method's Deletion scores are not consistently the best (e.g., GIG substantially outperforms ISA on ResNet-50), and the authors acknowledge the limitation of removing features only in equal-sized batches. Open questions include whether unequal feature removal, adaptive step sizes, or extension to non-image modalities (beyond the single LSTM experiment) can further improve performance, and whether the iterative overhead (~480 propagations vs. AGI's ~400) remains acceptable at larger scales.
