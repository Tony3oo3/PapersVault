## Problem

Despite the widespread use of normalizing flows for density estimation, a rigorous understanding of their expressive power has been lacking. Existing universality proofs for coupling-based flows rely on ill-conditioned neural networks or only guarantee convergence on bounded subspaces, while volume-preserving flows (NICE, GIN, NHF) are commonly assumed to be universal. This paper closes both gaps by proving volume-preserving flows are fundamentally not universal in KL divergence and by providing a new, well-conditioned, globally convergent universality proof for affine coupling-based flows.

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework analyzes what class of target distributions p(x) on R^D a stack of coupling blocks can represent. The flow maps data x ∈ R^D to latent codes z ∈ R^D distributed as a standard normal, yielding the density p_θ(x) = p(z=f_θ(x))|f_θ'(x)| via the change-of-variables formula. The paper's key analytical object is the latent pushforward distribution p_θ(z) = (f_θ#p)(z), which is compared to the target standard normal to define a convergence metric Δ_affine that quantifies how much a single additional coupling block can still reduce the KL loss.

## Method

The paper establishes two complementary results. First, it proves (Theorem 4.1–4.2) that volume-preserving flows with constant Jacobian determinant cannot achieve zero KL divergence for all targets; instead they converge to a rotationally symmetric distribution p*(z) constructed by radially sorting the input density, and a single one-dimensional non-volume-preserving layer suffices to restore universality. Second, it proves (Theorem 5.2) that an affine coupling block has a unique fixed point: if no further loss reduction is possible, the latent distribution must already be the standard normal. Building on this, Theorem 5.4 shows that iteratively appending L-bi-Lipschitz affine coupling blocks—each trained to standardize the conditional mean and variance of the active dimensions given the passive ones—converges the latent distribution to N(0,I) globally, yielding a constructive, well-conditioned universality proof that avoids the ill-conditioned step-function translations and bounded-support restrictions of prior work.

## Results

The paper establishes two main theoretical results: (1) volume-preserving normalizing flows are provably NOT universal in KL divergence (Theorem 4.2), with a concrete lower bound on achievable KL divergence given by Theorem 4.1; and (2) affine coupling-based flows (e.g., RealNVP) ARE distributional universal approximators under a well-conditioned, global convergence criterion (Theorem 5.4). The key quantitative comparison is in Table 1, which shows that the new construction (Theorem 5.4) is the only one that simultaneously uses well-conditioned coupling blocks, allows variable volume change, and considers the full support of the distribution. Empirically, the iterative layer-by-layer construction converges in KL divergence on a topologically challenging toy distribution (Figure 1, Figure 5 in Appendix E.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so comparisons are between universality proof constructions rather than empirical baselines. The paper's construction (Theorem 5.4) overcomes all three limitations of prior work: Teshima et al. (2020a) use ill-conditioned coupling blocks and only guarantee convergence on a bounded subspace with a single active dimension; Koehler et al. (2021) also use ill-conditioned blocks (translations approximating step functions) and only guarantee Wasserstein convergence on a compact set. Critically, the paper shows that Koehler et al.'s three-block construction is actually volume-preserving (Jacobian determinant = (ε′ε″²)^(D/2), independent of input), making it non-universal in KL divergence by Theorem 4.2. The new proof uses L-bi-Lipschitz coupling blocks, allows variable volume change via the rescaling term in Equation (28), and converges globally over the full support of p(x). The paper also demonstrates that more expressive coupling functions (e.g., rational-quadratic splines) can reduce an additional loss component (conditional negentropy J(b)) that affine couplings cannot, leading to faster convergence with the same number of blocks (Corollary 5.5, Equation 36).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on distributional universality of coupling-based normalizing flows initiated by Teshima et al. (2020) and Koehler et al. (2021), building directly on Draxler et al.'s (2020) characterization of the maximal loss improvement by a single affine coupling block. It departs from prior constructions by showing they rely on ill-conditioned networks and, critically, that Koehler et al.'s three-block construction is actually volume-preserving and therefore not universal in KL divergence. The paper also carves new ground by establishing the first non-universality result for volume-preserving flows (NICE, GIN, Neural Hamiltonian Flows), a class previously assumed to be expressively sufficient for its intended applications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The non-universality of volume-preserving flows is a fundamental limitation that affects any application relying on NICE, GIN, or NHF (disentanglement, Boltzmann generators, physical state-space modeling), and the paper provides both a characterization of the biased distribution these flows actually learn and a simple one-dimensional fix. The new universality proof for affine coupling flows (RealNVP) is constructive and well-conditioned, bridging a gap between the empirical success of these architectures and their theoretical justification. Open questions include the relationship between the proposed convergence metric Δ_affine and KL divergence, the number of blocks needed for a given accuracy, and whether the volume-preserving fix fully removes all biases in augmented settings.
