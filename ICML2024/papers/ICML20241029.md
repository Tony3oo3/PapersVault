## Problem

S-rectangular robust MDPs capture correlated uncertainties across actions within each state, making them more realistic than sa-rectangular settings, but existing solution methods are either prohibitively slow (LP/SDP with O(S^5.5) complexity) or inapplicable to moderately large state spaces (R² Bellman operators with contraction factor exceeding γ). Additionally, while it was known that optimal robust policies in this setting must be stochastic, nothing was understood about the structure of that stochasticity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a nominal transition kernel P₀, nominal reward R₀, per-state L_p uncertainty radii (α_s for rewards, β_s for kernels), and discount factor γ, with the constraint that kernel noise satisfies a simplex condition (row sums equal zero) to preserve valid probability distributions. It outputs the optimal robust value function v*_U(s) for all states, the optimal robust threshold policy π*(a|s) which is proportional to [Q(s,a) − v*(s)]^{p−1} over actions with positive advantage, and the evaluated robust Bellman operator at each state. For p=1,2,∞ the Bellman operator is computed in closed form or in O(A) steps; for general p it requires binary search over a narrow interval [max_a Q(s,a) − σ, max_a Q(s,a)].

## Method

The paper derives explicit closed-form robust Bellman operators for s-rectangular L_p uncertainty sets by decomposing the minimax problem into a nominal Bellman term minus a penalty involving a novel p-variance function κ_p(v) (measuring spread of the value function) and a policy q-norm ||π_s||_q (capturing the s-rectangular coupling across actions). The optimal robust Bellman operator is characterized as the solution to a generalized water-filling equation: find x such that [Σ_a (Q(s,a)−x)^p · 1(Q(s,a)≥x)]^{1/p} = σ, where σ = α_s + γβ_s κ_q(v). This yields a threshold optimal policy π*(a|s) ∝ [Q(s,a)−v*(s)]^{p−1}·1(Q(s,a)≥v*(s)) that plays only the top few actions proportionally to their advantage, contrasting with softmax policies. The resulting value iteration achieves O(S²A + SA log A) per iteration for p=1,2 (vs. O(S^5.5) for LP), with contraction factor exactly γ, enabling efficient computation in large state spaces.

## Results

The proposed method evaluates the optimal s-rectangular L_p robust Bellman operator in O(S²A) time for p=1,2,∞ (closed form or Algorithm 1) and O(S²A + SA·log(A/ε)) for general p (binary search), achieving a contraction factor of γ identical to non-robust MDPs. Empirically, for p=1,2,∞ the robust value iteration incurs only 1.1–2.8× the runtime of non-robust value iteration across state spaces up to S=100, A=20, while LP-based methods require 1,438×–4,904,004× and become infeasible beyond S=50 for s-rectangular cases. The method also yields the first explicit characterization of optimal robust policies as threshold policies proportional to [Q(s,a)−v*(s)]^{p−1}·1(Q(s,a)≥v*(s)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the LP baseline, the proposed method is faster by a factor of at least S (O(S²A) vs. O(S^{5.5}A^{4.5})), and empirically outperforms it by 1,000×–4,900,000× in wall-clock time; LP also only supports polyhedral (L₁) uncertainty sets and yields only approximate solutions. Against Wiesemann et al.'s SDP approach (O(S^{5.5}+S³A), approximate), the proposed method is faster by a factor of S^{3.5} for p=1,2 and provides exact solutions with a valid-kernel guarantee. Against Ho et al.'s bisection method (O(S³A), exact but L₁-only), the proposed method is faster by a factor of S and extends to all L_p norms. Against Derman et al.'s R² Bellman operators (gradient-based, approximate), the proposed method is the only one applicable to large state spaces: Derman's contraction factor γ(1+√S·β*) rapidly approaches 1, making convergence impractical, whereas the proposed method maintains the standard γ contraction. The proposed method is also the only approach that provides an explicit optimal policy characterization (threshold policies), which all four baselines lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sa-rectangular robust MDP framework of Wang & Zou (2021, 2022) to the more general s-rectangular setting, where action-level uncertainties within a state are coupled. It builds on the foundational s-rectangular robust MDP formulation of Wiesemann et al. (2013) and the exact L₁ Bellman operator evaluation of Ho et al. (2020), generalizing both to arbitrary L_p norms. The key methodological departure from Derman et al. (2021) is imposing a simplex condition on kernel noise, which yields a clean γ contraction factor (rather than γ(1+√S·β*)) and replaces their q-norm regularizer with a p-variance function κ_p(v). The policy improvement step generalizes the water-filling lemma of Anava & Levy (2016) from L₂ to L_p.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(S²A) closed-form evaluation of the optimal robust Bellman operator is a significant computational advance over prior LP/SDP methods (O(S^5.5)), making s-rectangular robust MDPs tractable for large state spaces. The threshold policy characterization resolves a long-standing open question about the stochasticity of optimal robust policies in s-rectangular MDPs, revealing a structure fundamentally different from softmax policies in entropy-regularized MDPs. The connection between value-function variance and robustness provides a practical regularizer for designing more resilient policies. Open questions include extending the closed-form results to general norms (beyond L_p) in the s-rectangular case, and developing model-free convergence guarantees for the online algorithm.
