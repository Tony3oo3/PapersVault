## Problem

Hindsight experience replay and goal relabeling are empirically successful in sparse-reward RL, yet no prior work provides a theoretical explanation for why they improve sample efficiency or a provably efficient algorithm that incorporates goal relabeling. The paper asks: does goal-conditioned value functions provably reduce sample complexity, how does the relabeling mechanism achieve this, and can we design an algorithm with matching guarantees?

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a goal-conditioned episodic MDP (state space S, action space A, goal space G, horizon H) and a hypothesis class F of goal-conditioned Q-functions, and produces an ε-optimal policy for the target goal g*. The key operational detail is that during the elimination phase, the algorithm evaluates Bellman errors across all goals in G (via a max operator) rather than only g*, which is the goal relabeling step. The sample complexity bound is expressed in terms of the new GOAL-BE dimension d, yielding Õ(H³d²/ε²) episodes, with an additional log-covering-number factor for the goal space.

## Method

GOALIVE is a modification of OLIVE (Jiang et al., 2017) that replaces single-goal Bellman error evaluation with a max-over-goals criterion during hypothesis elimination: at each step, it computes max_{g∈G} |E[f_h(s,a,g) − r_h(s,a,g) − max_{a'} f_{h+1}(s',a',g)]| and eliminates all hypotheses whose error exceeds a threshold. The key insight is that this denser feedback (nonzero Bellman errors under relabeled goals even when the target-goal reward is sparse) allows eliminating exponentially more hypotheses per trajectory. To analyze the algorithm, the paper introduces the GOAL-BE dimension, a goal-conditioned variant of the Bellman-Eluder dimension that is provably ≤ the original BE dimension and exponentially smaller in the best case (1 vs. 2^d−1 in their constructed example). The paper also shows that standard hindsight relabeling (using achieved states as goals) is a computationally efficient approximation of the optimal max-over-goals relabeling, and that in deterministic settings the two are exactly equivalent.

## Results

The paper's central quantitative result (Lemma 3.2) shows that in a constructed MDP with horizon H, any model-free algorithm without multiple goals requires Ω(2^H) trajectories to find a near-optimal policy, while the goal-conditioned algorithm GOALIVE achieves an ε-optimal policy using only poly(H, 1/ε, log(1/δ)) trajectories — an exponential improvement. The GOAL-BE dimension (Lemma 5.5) is shown to be 1 in this example, compared to the original BE dimension of at least 2^d − 1, confirming the exponential gap. Theorem 5.9 establishes that GOALIVE's sample complexity is Õ(H³d²/ε²) episodes, where d is the GOAL-BE dimension, with the only additional cost being a log-covering number of the goal space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines, so comparisons are made at the level of algorithms and complexity measures. GOALIVE is a direct modification of OLIVE (Jiang et al., 2017), with the sole difference being that Bellman error is evaluated over all goals g ∈ G rather than only the target goal g*, enabling denser feedback and better hypothesis elimination. The new GOAL-BE dimension is provably no larger than the original Bellman-Eluder dimension (Lemma 5.4) and can be exponentially smaller in the best case (Lemma 5.5: 1 vs. 2^d − 1). Compared to multitask RL results (Lu et al., 2022), GOALIVE targets suboptimality on a single target task rather than all tasks, and uses a complexity measure that exploits inter-goal structure. Compared to reward-free exploration (Jin et al., 2020a), GOALIVE is model-free rather than model-based, avoiding the error accumulation from imperfect dynamics estimation. The paper also notes that existing hindsight experience replay methods (Andrychowicz et al., 2017; Li et al., 2020; Pitis et al., 2020) can be viewed as computationally efficient modifications of GOALIVE that sacrifice some sample efficiency for tractability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OLIVE hypothesis-elimination framework (Jiang et al., 2017) by incorporating goal-conditioned value functions into the Bellman error evaluation, and generalizes the Bellman-Eluder dimension (Jin et al., 2021a) to a goal-conditioned variant (GOAL-BE). It also builds on the F-profile definition from Sun et al. (2019) to formalize what it means for a model-free algorithm to access states only through goal-conditioned value functions. The paper positions itself as the first to provide a theoretical explanation for the empirical success of Hindsight Experience Replay and goal relabeling, showing that existing relabeling methods (Andrychowicz et al., 2017; Li et al., 2020; Pitis et al., 2020) are computationally efficient modifications of the proposed GOALIVE algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first information-theoretic proof that goal relabeling can exponentially improve sample efficiency in the best case, resolving a long-standing gap between the empirical success of HER and the lack of theoretical guarantees. The GOAL-BE dimension offers a principled complexity measure that is provably no larger than the original BE dimension, giving practitioners a tool to identify problems where goal-conditioned approaches will be most beneficial. Open questions include whether the exponential improvement holds beyond the constructed example, how the theory extends to stochastic dynamics and continuous goal spaces, and whether the realizability assumption can be relaxed. The paper's focus on model-free algorithms (rather than model-based) aligns with empirical practice but limits the generality of the guarantees.
