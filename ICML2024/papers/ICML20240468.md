## Problem

Energy-based models for reasoning (e.g., IREM) require backpropagating through the entire iterative optimization process during training, making them slow and unstable, and they struggle to generalize to harder problem instances beyond the training distribution. The paper asks how to learn energy functions for reasoning that train stably without unrolling optimization and that can adaptively allocate more computation at test time to solve harder problems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240468/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts diverse structured inputs—20×20 matrices (encoded as 400-d vectors), partially filled 9×9 Sudoku boards (one-hot or MNIST digit images), and graph adjacency matrices with start/goal node embeddings—and produces the corresponding solution (completed matrix, filled board, connectivity matrix, or [T,N] action sequence). A key design choice is that at inference the number of gradient-descent steps T per energy landscape is a free parameter: increasing T trades speed for accuracy, enabling the model to tackle inputs that are larger or more ill-conditioned than anything seen during training (e.g., 18-node graphs vs. 12-node training graphs, or Sudoku with 17–34 clues vs. 31–42).

## Method

IRED learns a sequence of K annealed energy landscapes E_θ^k(x,y) (k=1,…,10), where earlier landscapes are smoother (higher Gaussian noise σ_k) and later ones are sharper, mirroring a diffusion noise schedule. Training combines two losses that avoid backpropagating through the optimization loop: (1) a denoising/score-function MSE loss that supervises ∇_y E_θ^k to predict the injected noise ε, and (2) a contrastive loss that pushes the energy of the ground-truth label below that of a noise-corrupted negative, shaping the global energy minima. At inference, the model initializes y from Gaussian noise and sequentially runs T gradient-descent steps on each landscape (from smooth to sharp), scaling the solution between landscapes by √(1−σ_k²)/√(1−σ_{k−1}²); a line-search check ensures each step decreases energy. This annealed multi-step optimization prevents the error accumulation that plagues single-step diffusion sampling and lets the model adaptively spend more computation on harder instances.

## Results

On the harder Sudoku dataset (17–34 given entries vs. 31–42 in training), IRED achieves 62.1% accuracy, substantially outperforming IREM (24.6%), Diffusion (10.3%), SAT-Net (3.2%), and RRN (28.6%). On harder graph connectivity (18 nodes vs. 12 in training), IRED reaches 93.8% vs. IREM's 89.8% and Diffusion's 61.3%. On continuous tasks, IRED achieves the lowest MSE on all three operations (e.g., 0.2063 on harder matrix inverse vs. 0.2083 for IREM and 0.2132 for Diffusion), and on harder shortest-path planning (25-node graphs vs. 15 in training), IRED achieves 91.9% vs. 88.4% for IREM and 46.9% for Diffusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IRED consistently outperforms IREM (Du et al., 2022) across all three domains, with the largest gaps on harder instances: 62.1% vs. 24.6% on harder Sudoku, 93.8% vs. 89.8% on harder connectivity, and 0.2063 vs. 0.2083 MSE on harder matrix inverse. Against the direct Diffusion baseline (Ho et al., 2020), IRED achieves substantially higher accuracy on harder tasks (e.g., 62.1% vs. 10.3% on Sudoku, 91.9% vs. 46.9% on planning), demonstrating that iterative energy minimization is critical over single-pass denoising. On the domain-specific SAT-Net baseline (Wang et al., 2019), IRED dramatically outperforms on harder Sudoku (62.1% vs. 3.2%) and Visual Sudoku (46.6% vs. 0.0%), while SAT-Net slightly edges IRED on in-distribution Sudoku (98.3% vs. 99.4% is actually IRED winning). Against RRN (Palm et al., 2018), IRED more than doubles the harder-Sudoku accuracy (62.1% vs. 28.6%) and also outperforms on Visual Sudoku harder instances (46.6% vs. 28.6%), though RRN matches IRED on in-distribution Sudoku (99.8% vs. 99.4%). Overall, IRED wins or ties on every harder-difficulty evaluation across all domains, with the advantage growing as problem difficulty increases beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

IRED directly extends IREM (Du et al., 2022), which formulated reasoning as optimization over a single learned energy landscape but suffered from slow, unstable training due to backpropagation through the full optimization chain. Drawing on the EBM–diffusion connection (Ho et al., 2020; Du et al., 2023), IRED replaces the single landscape with a sequence of annealed energy landscapes and replaces backprop-through-optimization with a combination of score-function (denoising) supervision and contrastive energy-landscape shaping, both of which are single-step losses. This positions IRED as a practical, stable training recipe for the broader 'reasoning as energy minimization' paradigm, while also distinguishing itself from standard diffusion models by running multiple gradient-descent steps at each landscape rather than a single learned transition kernel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical takeaway is that formulating reasoning as energy optimization enables adaptive computation at inference time: allocating more gradient steps to harder instances yields substantial out-of-distribution generalization (e.g., 62% vs 3% on harder Sudoku), a property that is difficult to achieve with fixed-depth or single-pass architectures. Open questions include whether the fixed Gaussian noise schedule can be learned, whether amortized initializers or guided optimizers can reduce the many gradient steps currently required, and whether the framework can be extended with explicit memory to handle tasks analogous to chain-of-thought reasoning. The uniform applicability across continuous, discrete, and planning domains with only an encoder swap is a strong argument for the generality of the energy-optimization formulation.
