## Problem

Gaussian process latent variable models (GPLVMs) suffer from model collapse—producing vague, homogeneous, or distorted latent representations—due to two underexplored factors: improper selection of the projection variance and inadequate kernel flexibility. Existing scalable GPLVM variants (BGPLVM, GPLVM-SVI) rely on inducing points that add optimization burden and limit kernel choices to simple forms like RBF, while the role of the projection variance in triggering collapse has not been theoretically characterized.

## Input / Output

The method takes an observed data matrix Y ∈ R^{N×M} (or an incomplete Y_obs with a specified fraction of missing entries for imputation) and produces a low-dimensional latent representation X ∈ R^{N×Q} with Q ≪ M, learned as a variational Gaussian distribution q(X) = ∏ᵢ N(μᵢ, Sᵢ). It simultaneously learns the SM kernel hyperparameters (mixture weights, means, and variances for m components) and the projection variance σ². For imputation, missing entries are recovered via their posterior mean E[Y_miss | X, Y_obs], and the latent dimensionality Q is a user-specified hyperparameter.

## Method

advisedRFLVM embeds a spectral mixture (SM) kernel into the GPLVM and approximates it via a differentiable random Fourier feature (RFF) map: rather than sampling spectral points from the full Gaussian mixture, it applies the vanilla RFF independently to each of the m mixture components (using the reparameterization trick) and stacks the resulting feature maps, yielding an unbiased, differentiable kernel estimator. This differentiability is the key enabler—it allows the ELBO to be optimized end-to-end with off-the-shelf automatic differentiation (Adam) over kernel hyperparameters, projection variance, and variational parameters, without introducing inducing points. The ELBO's data-reconstruction term is estimated via Monte Carlo sampling from q(X) and p(W), while the KL regularization term is evaluated analytically; the Woodbury matrix identity reduces per-iteration cost to O(N(mL)²) when N ≫ mL. Theoretically, the paper proves via the linear GPLVM (DPPCA) that fixing σ² above the smallest eigenvalue drives the latent to zero (homogeneity), while setting σ² to its MLE guarantees the global optimum, motivating joint learning of σ² within the variational framework.

## Results

advisedRFLVM achieves the highest KNN classification accuracy across all six real-world datasets, with headline results of 79.5% on MNIST, 76.5% on YALE, 78.9% on MONTREAL, and 84.6% on BRIDGES. For missing data imputation on MNIST, advisedRFLVM attains the lowest test MSE at every missing-data proportion (e.g., 0.025 at 0% missing vs. 0.035 for VAE, 0.048 for BGPLVM, 0.066 for RFLVM), and maintains a KNN accuracy of 63.6% even at 60% missing data where competitors drop to 27–59%. On the synthetic S-shaped manifold task, advisedRFLVM and RFLVM consistently outperform BGPLVM and GPLVM-SVI in R² score, with the gap widening when the ground-truth kernel is a hybrid RBF + periodic kernel.

## Baselines & Comparisons

advisedRFLVM outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset for KNN accuracy. Against the closest GPLVM competitor RFLVM, gains are consistent but moderate (e.g., MNIST 79.5% vs. 60.2%, YALE 76.5% vs. 65.3%, MONTREAL 78.9% vs. 76.9%), which the authors attribute to RFLVM's Dirichlet-process prior causing 'rich-get-richer' weight concentration and its MCMC inference being less efficient than ELBO optimization. Against inducing-point-based GPLVMs (BGPLVM, GPLVM-SVI), the advantage is large on MNIST (79.5% vs. 56.7% and 34.4%) and YALE (76.5% vs. 55.3% and 52.1%), reflecting the benefit of the flexible SM kernel over fixed RBF kernels. Against VAEs, advisedRFLVM leads by a wide margin on MNIST (79.5% vs. 64.3% for VAE) and YALE (76.5% vs. 61.1%), while the gap narrows on BRIDGES (84.6% vs. 75.1%) and CIFAR-10 (29.0% vs. 26.6%). Classic linear/nonlinear methods (PCA, LDA, Isomap, HPF) are consistently the weakest, confirming that model flexibility is critical. For missing-data imputation (Table 2), advisedRFLVM achieves the lowest MSE at all missing proportions on both MNIST and BRENDAN, with the advantage growing as the missing fraction increases (e.g., at 60% missing on MNIST: 0.068 MSE vs. 0.098 for BGPLVM and 0.120 for RFLVM).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GPLVM framework (Lawrence, 2005) by integrating the spectral mixture kernel (Wilson & Adams, 2013) with a differentiable random Fourier feature approximation (building on Rahimi & Recht, 2007, and Jung et al., 2022) within a variational inference setting. It departs from inducing-point-based sparse GPLVMs (BGPLVM, GPLVM-SVI) that add optimization burden and limit kernel flexibility, and from RFLVM (Gundersen et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2023) which uses a Dirichlet process mixture with a 'rich-get-richer' bias. The key conceptual departure is a theoretical analysis via the linear GPLVM (DPPCA) that formally characterizes how improper projection variance selection causes model collapse, complementing the practical fix of learning σ² jointly with kernel hyperparameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal theoretical characterization of model collapse in GPLVMs, linking it to two concrete, actionable factors (projection variance and kernel flexibility), which gives practitioners clear diagnostic criteria. The advisedRFLVM method avoids the optimization burden of inducing points while maintaining O(N(mL)²) complexity, making it a lightweight alternative for probabilistic dimensionality reduction. However, the method cannot handle out-of-distribution data without an explicit encoder, and training time may still be prohibitive for very large datasets, leaving open the question of how to scale this approach to massive data while preserving the GP's implicit regularization benefits.
