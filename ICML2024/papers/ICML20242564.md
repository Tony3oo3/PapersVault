## Problem

Realistic pedestrian attribute recognition (PAR) datasets are profoundly label-imbalanced, with most attributes occurring at frequencies well below 0.5, causing models to over-predict majority labels and overfit on under-represented attributes. Existing remedies such as loss re-weighting or image re-sampling cannot achieve true per-attribute label balance because attributes co-occur within the same images, making independent balancing infeasible in pixel space. This paper identifies label imbalance as the primary, under-addressed bottleneck for PAR performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242564/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single pedestrian RGB image resized to 256×192 and produces a vector of C binary attribute predictions (26 for PA100k, 35 for PETA, 51 for RAPv1). At inference, the pipeline is a standard ConvNeXt-base backbone followed by a fully-connected attribute-decomposition layer and per-attribute linear classifiers, incurring no additional computational cost over a baseline model. Training uses only horizontal flip and random crop as image augmentations, with a batch size of 64 and Adam optimizer.

## Method

The framework has two complementary components. First, Feature Re-sampled Decoupled Learning (FRDL) is a two-stage pipeline: Stage 1 trains the full model with instance-balanced sampling, then Stage 2 freezes the feature extractor, caches per-attribute features into binary label banks, and re-trains only the classifier by drawing features with equal probability from positive and negative banks—achieving true label balance without the intractable image-level re-sampling that attribute co-occurrence makes impossible. Second, Gradient-Oriented Augment Translating (GOAT) addresses the overfitting and feature noise introduced by over-sampling minority features: it translates each cached feature along its local loss-gradient direction toward the batch loss centroid, implemented as a short stochastic gradient descent trajectory of the feature extractor around its converged solution. This yields a heterogeneous, in-distribution Bayesian sampling cloud that introduces genuine semantic novelty (unlike homogeneous Gaussian augmentation methods such as ISDA, which the paper proves are equivalent to fixed-margin optimizers) while simultaneously regularizing spurious feature-label pairs.

## Results

On PA100k, FRDL+GOAT (ConvNeXt-base) achieves 89.44% mean accuracy (mA), surpassing the strongest baseline PARFormer-B (Swin Transformer) by 5.49 points. On RAPv1, it reaches 87.72% mA, outperforming the best prior method DAFL (83.72%) by 4.00 points. On PETA, the method scores 88.59% mA, slightly below DRFormer (89.96%), though the authors attribute this to known data leakage in PETA's train/test split; on the leakage-free PETAzs variant, FRDL+GOAT leads with 79.10% mA versus 76.16% for PARFormer. The method also achieves the best F1 on PETA (89.03%) while using a lighter backbone (ConvNeXt-base) than most competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FRDL+GOAT decisively outperforms all 11 compared PAR methods on PA100k mA (89.44% vs. best prior 83.95% by PARFormer-B) and RAPv1 mA (87.72% vs. best prior 83.84% by PARFormer-B), with margins of 5.5 and 3.9 points respectively. On PETA, DRFormer (89.96% mA) edges out FRDL+GOAT (88.59%), but the authors argue PETA's random train/test split causes identity overlap that inflates prior scores; on the zero-shot PETAzs variant, FRDL+GOAT leads by 2.94 points (79.10% vs. 76.16%). Against loss re-weighting baselines (Li et al. 2015, Tan et al. 2020, Zhang et al. 2021), FRDL alone gains 3.8–4.8 mA points, demonstrating that true label-balanced feature re-sampling substantially exceeds re-weighting. On the open-set UPAR* benchmark, the method achieves 79.40% mA, surpassing PARFormer (72.58%) by 6.82 points. The method's F1 on RAPv1 (79.16%) is lower than several baselines, a discrepancy the authors attribute to the inherent tension between label-balanced training (which boosts mA) and instance-based precision metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the two-stage Decoupled Learning pipeline (Kang et al., 2019) from long-tailed multi-class recognition to the multi-label PAR setting, but with a critical modification: Stage#2 re-samples cached features rather than images, sidestepping the intractable label-balanced image re-sampling problem caused by attribute co-occurrence. It departs from the dominant PAR literature (attention mechanisms, graph-based attribute correlation, multi-task architectures) by arguing that label imbalance, not spatial localization or inter-attribute dependencies, is the fundamental bottleneck. The GOAT component explicitly critiques ISDA and MetaSAug, proving their homogeneous Gaussian augmentation is mathematically equivalent to a large-margin optimizer with static margins, and replaces it with a gradient-oriented, heterogeneous Bayesian sampling procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of label imbalance as the 'grey rhino' bottleneck for PAR, combined with a lightweight plug-and-play solution that requires no architectural changes, offers a practical path for the field to stop chasing increasingly complex models while ignoring a simpler data-level fix. The theoretical equivalence between homogeneous feature augmentation and large-margin optimization is a useful negative result that redirects future augmentation research toward genuinely heterogeneous methods. However, the notable F1-score weakness (e.g., 79.16 on RAPv1 vs. 82.30 baseline) and the reliance on a specific evaluation protocol that excludes highly imbalanced attributes limit the generality of the claims. Open questions include whether FRDL+GOAT transfers cleanly to other multi-label domains (e.g., scene classification, medical imaging) and whether the F1–mA gap can be closed without sacrificing the label-balancing benefit.
