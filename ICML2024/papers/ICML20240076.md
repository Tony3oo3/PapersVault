## Problem

Transformer neural processes (TNPs) have not incorporated translation equivariance, a natural inductive bias for stationary spatio-temporal data where translating the input should translate the prediction correspondingly. While other NP families such as ConvCNP and RCNP achieve this through convolutions or relational encodings, no TNP variant has done so, leaving a gap between the expressiveness of transformer-based attention and the geometric structure of the data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240076/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes off-the-grid context points (continuous spatial/temporal coordinates paired with scalar or vector observations) and target input coordinates, with no requirement that inputs lie on a regular grid. The output is a factorized posterior predictive distribution over target outputs, parameterized as a Gaussian with heterogeneous noise (mean and pre-softplus variance per target point). This off-the-grid formulation distinguishes TE-TNPs from ConvCNP, which requires discretization and is restricted to low-dimensional input domains.

## Method

The core contribution is the translation equivariant multi-head self-attention (TE-MHSA) and cross-attention (TE-MHCA) operations, which replace standard attention by making the attention score depend on the pairwise input difference x_n − x_m through a learnable MLP, rather than on absolute positions. Initial token embeddings depend only on observations (not coordinates), and input locations are dynamically updated at each layer via a translation-equivariant, permutation-invariant function that reuses the attention weights. To reduce quadratic complexity, pseudo-tokens with corresponding pseudo-locations are introduced (TE-PT-TNP), where pseudo-locations are initialized as a weighted average of input coordinates with weights from a standard attention mechanism. Theoretically, the paper proves that stationarity of the ground-truth stochastic process is equivalent to translation equivariance of the prediction map, and establishes a generalization bound showing that a translation-equivariant model with bounded receptive field that is accurate on a local region is accurate on any larger region.

## Results

On the synthetic 1-D regression task, TE-TNP maintains a test log-likelihood of −0.47 across all input shift amounts (Δ = 0 to 1.0), matching ConvCNP (−0.46) while TNP degrades from −0.48 to −0.57 and CNP from −0.69 to −1.08. On the Kolmogorov Flow PDE task, TE-PT-TNP-M64 achieves 0.92 LL, substantially outperforming PT-TNP-M128 (0.74), Multi-task GP (0.48), and CNP (−0.97). On the real-world ERA5 environmental dataset, TE-PT-TNP achieves 1.27–1.44 LL across central, western, and northern Europe, while CNP collapses to −5.70 LL on western Europe. On image completion, TE-PT-TNP-M128 reaches 1.25 LL on T-MNIST and 1.51 on T-CIFAR-10, surpassing ConvCNP (1.20 and 1.44 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CNP, TE-PT-TNP dominates across all four tasks: on synthetic 1-D regression at Δ=1.0, TE-PT-TNP-M32 (−0.46) outperforms CNP (−1.08) by over 0.6 LL; on Kolmogorov Flow, TE-PT-TNP-M64 (0.92) beats CNP (−0.97) by nearly 2 LL; and on ERA5 western Europe, TE-PT-TNP (1.27) exceeds CNP (−5.70) by over 6 LL. Against RCNP, TE-PT-TNP wins decisively on Kolmogorov Flow (0.92 vs 0.16) and ERA5 (1.27–1.44 vs −0.09 to 0.34), though RCNP matches TE-TNP on the flat synthetic 1-D task (−0.58 vs −0.47, where RCNP is actually slightly worse). Against ConvCNP, TE-PT-TNP matches or exceeds it on synthetic 1-D (−0.46 vs −0.46) and image completion (1.25 vs 1.20 on MNIST; 1.51 vs 1.44 on CIFAR-10), while ConvCNP cannot be applied to the 3-D Kolmogorov or 4-D ERA5 tasks due to convolutional limitations. Against the non-equivariant TNP/PT-TNP, TE-TNP and TE-PT-TNP show identical performance at Δ=0 but maintain flat performance as Δ increases, whereas TNP degrades by 0.09 LL and PT-TNP-M32 by 0.15 LL at Δ=1.0.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Transformer Neural Process (TNP) family by replacing standard multi-head attention with translation-equivariant variants (TE-MHSA and TE-MHCA), where attention scores depend on pairwise input-location differences rather than absolute positions. The input-location update mechanism borrows from E(n)-equivariant GNNs (Satorras et al., 2021), and the TE-MHSA is shown to be a special case of the LieTransformer's LieSelfAttention operation (Hutchinson et al., 2021). Prior translation-equivariant NPs—ConvCNP and RCNP—achieved equivariance through CNNs or relational encoders, but none had incorporated it into the transformer-based TNP architecture. The paper also draws a connection to relative positional encodings (e.g., RoPE) in LLMs, framing TE-attention as a continuous-domain generalization of that idea.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides both theoretical guarantees (Theorems 2.1–2.2) and extensive empirical evidence that translation equivariance is a critical inductive bias for spatio-temporal neural processes, enabling generalization to input regions outside the training distribution. The environmental data results are particularly striking: non-equivariant models (CNP, PT-TNP) fail catastrophically on shifted test regions while TE-PT-TNP maintains strong performance, validating the theoretical prediction. Key limitations include the computational overhead of passing pairwise differences through MLPs and the need for pseudo-token counts to scale with data size, both flagged for future work. Open questions include whether sliding-window attention could provide explicit receptive-field control to tighten the generalization guarantees in practice.
