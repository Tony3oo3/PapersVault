## Problem

No existing method can reconstruct 3D scenes from raw event camera streams in a generalizable, feedforward manner. Prior event-based 3D approaches (EventNeRF, Ev-NeRF) require per-scene optimization and suffer from blurred edges and soft fogs due to NeRF's continuous implicit representation, while generalizable 3DGS frameworks have only been applied to RGB cameras. Additionally, the critical subtasks of event-based depth estimation and intensity recovery have never been jointly optimized with 3D reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 360-degree event stream encoded as spatial-temporal voxel grids (B=5 temporal bins, trilinear interpolation of polarities) from two consecutive moments, along with known camera pose and intrinsics. Because event cameras only detect log-intensity changes without color, the output is a grayscale intensity value per Gaussian rather than spherical harmonics coefficients. The feedforward pipeline produces per-pixel 3D Gaussian parameters (position via depth unprojection, rotation as quaternion, scale, opacity, intensity), a dense depth map, a foreground mask, and a reconstructed intensity image, enabling novel view synthesis without any per-scene retraining.

## Method

EvGGS cascades three modules—depth/mask UNet, intensity UNet, and a residual-block Gaussian regressor—hierarchically in both feature and output spaces so that gradients backpropagate through the entire pipeline. The depth module's 32-channel feature volume is concatenated into the intensity module's input, and the intensity module's features plus predicted depth feed the Gaussian regressor, injecting geometric priors into appearance recovery and vice versa. The key insight is that 3D-aware collaborative training makes the subtasks mutually beneficial: improved geometry provides spatial context for texture, and improved texture supplies detail cues for depth. Training uses a joint loss (λ=0.2/0.2/0.6) combining L1, L2, and perceptual losses on source-view depth/intensity and target-view rendered images, with the depth module pretrained for 60k steps before joint fine-tuning at a reduced learning rate. The framework is the first to combine 3DGS with pure event-based reconstruction in a generalizable setting, and the authors also introduce the Ev3DS synthetic and Ev3D-R real-world datasets with calibrated depth, intensity, and silhouette ground truths.

## Results

On the Ev3DS synthetic dataset, EvGGS achieves 27.95 PSNR, 0.968 SSIM, and 0.045 LPIPS for novel view synthesis, outperforming all baselines by a large margin (next best: EventNeRF at 24.62 PSNR). For intensity reconstruction, the jointly trained module (EvGGSj) reaches 29.18 PSNR and 0.969 SSIM, surpassing E2VID (27.78 PSNR) and EVSNN (27.91 PSNR). For depth estimation, EvGGSj achieves 1.95 RMSE and 39.4 Abs.rel (×1000), beating EReFormer (2.12 RMSE, 46.2 Abs.rel) and ASNet (2.87 RMSE, 52.4 Abs.rel). On the realistic Ev3D-R dataset, EvGGS-f (fine-tuned) reaches 27.84 PSNR vs. 23.87 for E-ENeRF, demonstrating the smallest sim-to-real gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvGGS outperforms EventNeRF (Rudnev et al., CVPR 2023) by 3.33 dB PSNR (27.95 vs. 24.62) while also being generalizable and real-time, whereas EventNeRF requires per-scene optimization and renders at only 0.045 FPS. Against the E2VID+ENeRF pipeline (Lin et al., SIGGRAPH Asia 2022), EvGGS gains 4.09 dB PSNR, attributed to collaborative training improving depth and intensity quality beyond what E2VID achieves standalone. The E2VID+3DGS baseline (Kerbl et al., ACM TOG 2023) lags significantly at 19.19 PSNR because its reconstruction quality is entirely bounded by the upstream intensity recovery. For intensity reconstruction, EvGGSj (29.18 PSNR) beats E2VID (Rebecq et al., 27.78), FireNet (Scheerlinck et al., 25.56), and EVSNN (Barchid et al., 27.91), with the joint training strategy adding ~2.2 dB over the independently trained version. For depth estimation, EvGGSj (1.95 RMSE, 39.4 Abs.rel) surpasses EReFormer (Liu et al., 2.12/46.2), E2Dpt (Hidalgo-Carrió et al., 2.86/54.3), and ASNet (Jianguo et al., 2.87/52.4), achieving at least 14.7% improvement in Abs.rel over all baselines. On MipNeRF 360 large scenes, EvGGS (22.75 PSNR on Bicycle) outperforms GrayNeRF (Mildenhall et al., 19.84) and E-ENeRF (18.98), confirming generalization to unbounded scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: event-based 3D reconstruction (EventNeRF, Ev-NeRF, E-NeRF), generalizable neural rendering (ENeRF, MVSNeRF, GPS-Gaussian), and 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023). Prior event-based 3D methods all require per-scene optimization, while prior generalizable methods operate exclusively on RGB frames. EvGGS departs from both by being the first to reconstruct 3D Gaussians from raw event streams in a feedforward manner, and it introduces a cascading collaborative training scheme where depth, intensity, and Gaussian parameter regression modules are jointly optimized through a differentiable rendering pipeline rather than trained in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a pure event-based pipeline can achieve generalizable, real-time 3D reconstruction with a small sim-to-real gap, this work opens a practical path toward deploying event cameras in robotics and AR/AR where RGB cameras fail (extreme lighting, high-speed motion). The new Ev3DS/Ev3D-R datasets fill a notable gap in the community. Key limitations remain: the replacement of spherical harmonics with a single intensity channel precludes modeling view-dependent effects, and specular metals are not handled. Whether the collaborative training paradigm generalizes beyond this specific three-module cascade to more complex multi-task 3D pipelines is an open question.
