## Problem

Existing asymptotic analyses of deep random feature networks assume unstructured, independent random weights, yet trained neural networks have fundamentally structured weights whose statistics encode learned information. This paper addresses the gap by providing a tight asymptotic characterization of the test error for learning with Gaussian rainbow networks—deep nonlinear networks with structured random weights whose row-wise covariances may depend on previous layer weights—extending prior results that were limited to isotropic, layer-independent weights.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241787/cropped_page6_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a deep structured feature map defined by deterministic weight matrices W_ℓ and Lipschitz activation functions, the three population (cross-)covariance matrices Ω, Φ, Ψ (which for Gaussian rainbow networks admit closed-form linear recursions in the weight matrices), training data (x_i, y_i), and a ridge regularization parameter λ. It produces a deterministic equivalent test error formula E_gen^rmt(λ) that is anisotropic—depending on the eigenvectors of Ω and their overlap with eigenvectors of Φ and Ψ, not merely eigenvalues—and a deterministic equivalent M(λ) of the resolvent G(λ) = (XX^T/p + λ)^{-1} satisfying a self-consistent equation involving the scalar m(λ).

## Method

The core technical contribution is an anisotropic Marchenko-Pastur law for the resolvent G(λ), yielding the deterministic equivalent M(λ) = (λ + (n/p)λm(λ)Ω)^{-1} with error measured in Frobenius norm of the observable, which streamlines the proof compared to prior spectral-norm-based approaches. The key novelty lies in multi-resolvent deterministic equivalents: products like G·(XZ^T/n) acquire an additional factor of mλ beyond the naive substitution G≈M and (XZ^T/n)≈Φ, requiring careful leave-one-out identity expansions and stability analysis of self-consistent equations. For Gaussian rainbow networks, the population covariances Ω, Φ, Ψ are shown to admit closed-form linear recursions (Definition 4.2) in terms of the weight matrices, with coefficients κ_ℓ^1 = E[φ_ℓ'(N_ℓ)] and κ_ℓ^* capturing the linear and residual (noise) components of each activation. The rigorous proof of the two-layer linearization (Theorem 4.4) combines Wiener chaos expansion to decompose nonlinear functions of Gaussian vectors into uncorrelated Hermite components, with Stein's method to establish quantitative CLTs for the multi-layer quantities. The framework is validated empirically on both synthetic Gaussian rainbow networks and trained finite-width networks on MNIST, where the linearized formulae capture learning curves of networks trained with Adam in the lazy regime.

## Results

The paper's main result (Theorem 3.1) provides a tight asymptotic formula for the generalization error of ridge regression on Lipschitz feature maps in the proportional n~p~k regime, expressed entirely in terms of the population covariances Ω, Φ, Ψ and a scalar self-consistent equation for m(λ). For Gaussian rainbow networks, Theorem 4.4 rigorously proves that the population covariances are well-approximated by simple linear recursions (Def. 4.2) involving products of weight matrices. Empirically, the theoretical predictions (solid lines) match numerical simulations (dots) closely across all tested settings: a 4-layer tanh network with correlated weights on synthetic data (Fig. 1), a 3-layer ReLU network trained with Adam on Gaussian data (Fig. 2), and a 3-layer ReLU network on MNIST (Fig. 3). On MNIST, the theory captures the learning curves for multiple regularization strengths and training times, with the trained feature map consistently outperforming both raw-input ridge regression and untrained random features.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing methods in the traditional sense; instead, it validates its theoretical predictions against numerical simulations and compares different feature-map configurations. In Figure 2, the trained 3-layer ReLU network's feature map (blue) outperforms both ridge regression on raw inputs (red) and ridge regression on the untrained random feature map (green) across the full range of sample sizes, demonstrating that training induces a beneficial inductive bias even in the lazy regime. In Figure 1, the theory accurately captures the test error of a 4-layer network with inter-layer weight correlations (W₁=W₂) and target/student correlations, settings where previous unstructured-weight analyses (Schröder et al., 2023; Bosch et al., 2023a) do not apply. The anisotropic deterministic equivalent (Theorem 3.1) generalizes prior isotropic results (Mei & Montanari, 2022; Mei et al., 2022) by handling structured noise covariance Σ≠I and the general misspecified case Z≠X, which previous approaches could not address without restrictive symmetry assumptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the asymptotic analysis of deep random feature models (Schröder et al. 2023; Bosch et al. 2023a; Cui et al. 2023) from unstructured i.i.d. weights to structured, potentially correlated weights via the Gaussian rainbow network framework of Guth et al. 2023. Technically, it builds on anisotropic Marchenko-Pastur laws (Knowles & Yin 2017) and Wiener chaos/Stein's method (Nourdin & Peccati 2012) to derive multi-resolvent deterministic equivalents that handle the cross-covariance structure between learner and target feature maps. The linearization of population covariances generalizes the recursive formulae of Fan & Wang 2020 and Schröder et al. 2023 to the correlated-weight setting, while the effective linear model interpretation connects to the lazy training and implicit bias literature (Jacot et al. 2020; Ba et al. 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first tight asymptotic characterization of test error for deep networks with structured random weights, bridging a gap between random network theory and the statistics of trained networks. The effective linear model (W_eff, C_eff) offers a concrete mechanism for how training induces anisotropic inductive bias even in the lazy regime, with empirical validation on MNIST. Key open questions include the boundaries of the Gaussian rainbow framework's applicability to non-lazy training, whether the linearization holds beyond the two-layer case proven rigorously, and how the theory extends to more complex architectures or non-Gaussian weight ensembles.
