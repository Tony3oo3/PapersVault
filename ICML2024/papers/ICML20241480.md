## Problem

Existing uncertainty quantification methods for Neural Operators (e.g., BayesianNO, MC-Dropout, VarianceNO) provide well-calibrated uncertainty estimates in-domain but fail to produce error-correlated uncertainty for even moderately out-of-domain PDE parameter inputs, while the only method that works well OOD—full ensembles of NOs—is computationally expensive. This limits the practical deployment of NOs in scientific applications where test-time PDE parameters are typically outside the training range.

## Input / Output

The input is a PDE parameter (diffusivity k, PME degree m, Stefan shock value u*, or advection velocity β) encoded as a constant scalar field over the spatiotemporal domain, paired with fixed initial and boundary conditions; the OOD shift is a change in the parameter's value range relative to training. The output is the PDE solution u(x,t) on a discrete grid at multiple time steps, accompanied by per-point uncertainty estimates (mean and variance) computed across M=10 prediction heads. In the OPERATOR-PROBCONSERV variant, the final output is a corrected solution that additionally satisfies a known linear conservation constraint (e.g., ∫u dx = b) via a closed-form projection.

## Method

DIVERSENO modifies the last feed-forward layer of a Fourier Neural Operator to have M=10 output heads instead of one, with all heads sharing parameters up to the penultimate layer. A diversity regularization term in the training objective maximizes the pairwise L2 distance between the last-layer weight matrices of different heads, encouraging the heads to produce functionally diverse predictions that mimic the multi-mode posterior exploration of a full ensemble at a fraction of the computational cost. The key insight, validated empirically, is that diversity in the last layer alone suffices for well-correlated OOD uncertainty estimates, since in-domain the last layer collapses diversity to a common ground truth while OOD the diversity persists in the outputs. OPERATOR-PROBCONSERV then feeds the per-point variance from DIVERSENO into the PROBCONSERV constrained least-squares update (μ̃ = μ − ΣGᵀ(GΣGᵀ)⁻¹(Gμ−b)), which projects the prediction onto the conservation-law subspace with larger corrections in high-variance (high-error) regions.

## Results

On the 1-d heat equation with large OOD shift (k_train ∈ [1,5], k_test ∈ [7,8]), DIVERSENO achieves n-MeRCI of 0.03, matching EnsembleNO (0.03) and dramatically outperforming BayesianNO (0.80), VarianceNO (1.70), and MC-DropoutNO (0.86). DIVERSENO also achieves the lowest MSE (8.8e-05) among all methods, beating EnsembleNO (1.1e-04) by ~20%. Across all PDEs and OOD shifts, DIVERSENO achieves 2× to 70× improvement in n-MeRCI over other cheap UQ methods, and is 49–80% better in MSE than EnsembleNO at comparable FLOPs. OPERATOR-PROBCONSERV further improves OOD accuracy by up to 34% over the unconstrained DIVERSENO prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIVERSENO decisively outperforms all three cheaper UQ baselines on the key n-MeRCI metric (which measures correlation between uncertainty and error): on the large OOD heat equation, DIVERSENO achieves 0.03 vs. BayesianNO's 0.80, VarianceNO's 1.70, and MC-DropoutNO's 0.86 — a 2× to 70× improvement across all PDEs and shifts. Against the expensive EnsembleNO baseline (10 independently trained FNOs), DIVERSENO matches n-MeRCI (0.03 vs. 0.03) while achieving lower MSE (8.8e-05 vs. 1.1e-04) and being 49–80% more efficient in FLOPs. BayesianNO (Laplace approximation) fails most dramatically OOD, producing narrow, uncalibrated uncertainty bounds that do not contain the true solution. VarianceNO (mean-variance NLL training) is the worst baseline, with n-MeRCI exceeding 1.0 (indicating anti-correlated uncertainty) and the highest MSE. MC-DropoutNO (variational inference via dropout) performs slightly better than BayesianNO but still produces n-MeRCI values near 0.86, far from the near-zero values of DIVERSENO and EnsembleNO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two threads: DeepEnsembles (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017) for UQ via multiple independently trained models, and PROBCONSERV (Hansen et al., 2023) for projecting predictions onto physically constrained subspaces using covariance information. The key departure is identifying that existing UQ methods for neural operators (BayesianNO, MC-Dropout, VarianceNO) fail specifically under OOD shifts in PDE parameters, and that the functional diversity of ensemble members—not their individual accuracy—is the mechanism enabling good OOD uncertainty. DIVERSENO repurposes the ensemble insight into a single multi-head architecture with a weight-diversity regularization, and OPERATOR-PROBCONSERV is the first application of the PROBCONSERV framework to neural operators (previously instantiated only with Attentive Neural Processes).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NOs are increasingly deployed as fast surrogates for PDE solvers, but their OOD fragility limits practical use when test-time parameters fall outside the training range; this paper provides a simple, computationally cheap mechanism (last-layer diversity) to detect and partially correct such failures. The finding that PROBCONSERV can drastically degrade predictions (up to 1300% MSE increase) when fed uncalibrated uncertainty estimates underscores that error-correlated UQ is a prerequisite, not a nice-to-have, for physics-constrained correction. Limitations include evaluation primarily on 1D PDEs with a single operator architecture (FNO), reliance on global (not local) conservation constraints, and residual high errors under large OOD shifts—suggesting that local physical constraints and broader architectural validation remain open.
