## Problem

Existing model-free RL exploration methods treat all action dimensions uniformly, aggregating uncertainty across the entire action space without recognizing that different primitive behaviors (e.g., vertical arm movement vs. grasping torque) have varying significance at different stages of policy learning. This uniform treatment causes the agent to waste interactions on low-impact actions while under-exploring critical ones, leading to poor sample efficiency that is especially pronounced on challenging manipulation and sparse-reward tasks.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes state vectors (dim S) and outputs continuous action vectors (dim A) through a Gaussian policy network. Beyond the standard policy output, the method maintains a causal weight matrix B_{a→r|s} ∈ R^{dim A × 1} computed every I environment steps from a local buffer of N_c fresh transitions via DirectLiNGAM, quantifying each action dimension's causal influence on reward. A gradient dormancy degree α_τ is also computed from per-neuron weight gradient L2 norms across all network layers, serving as the adaptive signal for the reset mechanism.

## Method

ACE builds on SAC by replacing the standard policy entropy with a causality-aware entropy H_c that weights each action dimension's entropy by its causal weight B_{a_i→r|s}, so dimensions with greater causal impact on reward receive proportionally stronger exploration pressure within the modified Bellman operator T_c^π. Causal weights are estimated via DirectLiNGAM on a local buffer of fresh transitions at fixed intervals, with identifiability of the causal structure guaranteed under the global Markov and faithfulness assumptions. To counteract overfitting to specific behaviors, ACE introduces a gradient-dormancy-guided soft reset: neurons whose gradient L2 norm falls below threshold τ relative to the layer mean are classified as gradient-dormant, and the resulting dormancy degree α_τ directly sets the reset factor η = clip(α_τ, 0, η_max) for periodically perturbing both policy and Q-network weights. The paper proves that the modified Bellman operator satisfies contraction, policy improvement, and policy iteration convergence properties in tabular MDPs, and validates the approach across 29 continuous control tasks spanning 7 domains with a single hyperparameter set.

## Results

Across 29 continuous control tasks spanning 7 domains, ACE achieves performance improvements of 2.1× on manipulator tasks, 1.1× on locomotion tasks, 2.2× on dexterous hand tasks, and 3.7× on sparse-reward tasks compared to model-free baselines. On MetaWorld's 14 dense-reward manipulation tasks, ACE leads by over 70% on very hard tasks and exceeds 30% on hard/medium tasks, reaching 100% success rate on previously unsolved tasks (pick-place-wall, stick-push, disassemble). On 6 sparse-reward tasks, ACE outperforms the dedicated exploration method SAC+RND by a significant margin, particularly excelling where baselines completely fail to learn.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACE consistently outperforms SAC and TD3 across all 29 tasks with a single hyperparameter set, with the largest gaps on manipulation (2.1×) and sparse-reward (3.7×) tasks. Against the dedicated exploration bonus SAC+RND on sparse-reward tasks, ACE still wins by a significant margin, demonstrating that causality-aware entropy provides superior exploration guidance beyond novelty-based bonuses. In the BAC comparison (Appendix F.1, F.4), ACE surpasses BAC on challenging dexterous hand tasks, and the ad-hoc ACE-BAC variant further outperforms vanilla BAC, confirming the plug-and-play nature of the components. When integrated into REDQ (high-UTD method), ACE significantly improves sample efficiency and final performance on Walker2d and CheetahRun. In the visual RL setting (Appendix J), ACE+DrM outperforms vanilla DrM by replacing the dormant-ratio schedule with the gradient-dormancy degree and adding causal-weight-guided action sampling. On locomotion tasks specifically, the gap between ACE and CausalSAC (without reset) narrows, indicating the reset mechanism contributes most on longer-horizon manipulation tasks where gradient dormancy persists.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the maximum entropy RL framework (SAC) by replacing the uniform entropy regularization with a causality-weighted entropy that differentially prioritizes exploration across action dimensions based on their discovered causal impact on rewards. The causal discovery component leverages DirectLiNGAM, a well-established linear non-Gaussian structural equation model, applied here in a novel way to quantify per-dimension action-to-reward causality conditioned on state. The gradient-dormancy-guided reset mechanism departs from prior dormant neuron work (Sokar et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2023), which defined dormancy via activation statistics, by instead measuring dormancy through gradient L2 norms—a distinction the authors argue is more informative for state-based RL. The paper positions itself within the causal RL literature but specifically targets the underexplored problem of discovering shifting causal effects between action dimensions and rewards during training, rather than modeling state-transition causality or requiring explicit causal structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, data-driven mechanism for addressing the sample efficiency bottleneck in off-policy RL by identifying which primitive behaviors matter most at each training stage, rather than treating all action dimensions symmetrically. The plug-and-play nature (demonstrated with SAC, BAC, REDQ, CQL, and DrM backbones) suggests broad applicability, and the 100% success rate on very hard MetaWorld tasks where baselines fail is practically significant for robotics. However, the method relies on DirectLiNGAM's linearity and non-Gaussianity assumptions, adds computational overhead from periodic causal discovery, and the reset mechanism's benefit is task-dependent (less pronounced in locomotion). Open questions include whether the causal weighting generalizes to highly non-linear reward structures, how it interacts with model-based RL, and whether the gradient dormancy signal remains informative in very high-dimensional or visual settings.
