## Problem

Despite the promise of variational learning for providing weight uncertainty in deep networks, no method could train large models (e.g., LLMs with hundreds of millions of parameters) from scratch at a cost comparable to Adam while also yielding useful uncertainty estimates. Prior variational approaches suffered from high-variance gradients, expensive Hessian computations, or implementation complexity that prevented them from scaling, leading to a widespread belief in an inherent accuracy-uncertainty trade-off.

## Input / Output

IVON consumes standard inputs—text token sequences for language modeling (GPT-2 on OpenWebText) and RGB images for classification (ImageNet, CIFAR-10/100, TinyImageNet)—and produces standard outputs (next-token predictions, class probabilities) alongside a learned posterior over all network weights parameterized by a mean vector m and standard deviation vector σ. This posterior enables downstream applications not available from point-estimate optimizers: predictive uncertainty via posterior sampling, Hessian-based model merging, leave-one-out generalization estimation, and per-example data sensitivity scores.

## Method

IVON adapts the Bayesian Learning Rule (Lin et al., 2020) into an Adam-like optimizer that directly minimizes the variational objective L(q) = λE_q[l̄(θ)] + D_KL(q‖p). The key mechanism is a reparameterization-trick-based Hessian estimator ĥ = ĝ·(θ−m)/σ² that requires only a single element-wise multiplication with the minibatch gradient (no per-example gradient squares), combined with a Riemannian update rule that guarantees positivity of the running Hessian average even when individual estimates are negative. The resulting update (Alg. 1) mirrors Adam's structure—momentum on the gradient, exponential moving average on the Hessian, and a preconditioned step—but uses a Newton-like division by (h+δ) rather than Adam's square-root scaling, and includes a sampling step θ~q(θ) at each iteration. Practical tricks include using weight decay δ as the prior precision, initializing the Hessian with a constant h₀ (typically 0.01–1), and element-wise gradient clipping for transformer training. The computational overhead over Adam is negligible for small-to-medium models and modest (~20%) for GPT-2-scale models, while the learned σ² provides calibrated uncertainty without any post-hoc approximation.

## Results

On GPT-2 (773M params) pretrained on OpenWebText, IVON reduces validation perplexity from 13.0 (AdamW) to 12.6, with similar gains at 355M (14.1 vs 14.5) and 125M (17.9 vs 18.1). On ImageNet with ResNet-50, IVON achieves 77.46% top-1 accuracy (vs 75.16% AdamW and 76.63% SGD) with substantially better calibration (ECE 0.022 vs 0.066 for AdamW). On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-20, IVON attains 92.71% accuracy, NLL 0.219, ECE 0.008, and Brier 0.108, outperforming all non-ensemble baselines; Multi-IVON (5-model mixture) further reaches 94.37% accuracy and NLL 0.179, surpassing a 5-model deep ensemble (93.57%, 0.198).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IVON consistently outperforms AdamW on both accuracy and calibration: on ImageNet ResNet-50 it gains +2.3% top-1 accuracy and reduces ECE from 0.066 to 0.022, while on GPT-2 it lowers perplexity by 0.2–0.4 points across all three model sizes. Against SGD, IVON wins on accuracy and all uncertainty metrics (NLL, ECE, Brier) across every dataset tested, and notably avoids the severe overfitting that AdamW exhibits on smaller datasets (e.g., 47.3% vs 62.7% on TinyImageNet ResNet-18). Among Bayesian deep learning baselines on CIFAR-10, IVON achieves the best accuracy (92.71%) and lowest NLL (0.219) among all non-ensemble methods, beating SWAG (92.45%, 0.230), VOGN (92.37%, 0.226), MC-Dropout (91.85%, 0.242), Laplace (91.52%, 0.284), and BBB (91.09%, 0.289). Multi-IVON (a 5-model mixture) surpasses a 5-model deep ensemble on accuracy (94.37% vs 93.57%) and NLL (0.179 vs 0.198), though the ensemble retains a slight edge on ECE (0.014 vs 0.029). For model merging, IVON's Hessian estimate matches the squared-gradient method of Daheim et al. (2024) in quality (94.0% vs 93.9% average accuracy) while eliminating the 100% post-training overhead. In the NeurIPS 2021 approximate inference competition, Multi-IVON placed first, outperforming Multi-SWAG (2nd) and SAE (3rd) on predictive agreement and total variation distance.

## Relation to Prior Work

IVON directly extends the Variational Online Newton (VON) method of Khan et al. (2018) and the positive-definite-constraint handling of Lin et al. (2020), which itself builds on the Bayesian Learning Rule (Khan & Rue, 2021). The key departure from prior natural-gradient variational methods (e.g., VOGN) is replacing the expensive per-example Gauss-Newton Hessian estimate with a single reparameterization-trick vector multiplication, yielding an Adam-like update that is a true second-order optimizer at first-order cost. The paper positions itself against the growing consensus (Trippe & Turner, 2017; Foong et al., 2020; Coker et al., 2022) that variational learning cannot match standard optimizers at scale, and against post-hoc uncertainty methods (MC-Dropout, SWAG, Laplace) that do not directly optimize the variational objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a variational optimizer can train LLMs (GPT-2, up to 773M parameters) from scratch at Adam-like cost while providing calibrated weight uncertainty, challenging the widely-held belief in an inherent accuracy-uncertainty trade-off. The practical impact is significant: IVON is a drop-in Adam replacement that yields uncertainty for free, enabling new use cases like zero-overhead Hessian-based model merging, faithful generalization prediction during training, and data sensitivity estimation. Key limitations include incompatibility with batch normalization, the irreducible cost of MC sampling, and the need for careful Hessian initialization; open questions include whether flexible posterior forms (beyond diagonal Gaussian) further improve results and whether deterministic variants of the variational objective can eliminate sampling overhead.
