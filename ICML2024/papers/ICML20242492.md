## Problem

Quantum optimization research has focused on single-function (block-box) settings, leaving the finite-sum structure ubiquitous in machine learning (SVMs, regression, Lasso) entirely unexplored in the quantum regime. This paper initiates the study of quantum algorithms for finite-sum optimization, providing the first quantum speedup over the classical tight bound and establishing matching quantum lower bounds for four convex variants plus a nonconvex critical-point variant.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a quantum finite-sum oracle O_F that maps |x⟩⊗|i⟩⊗|0⟩ → |x⟩⊗|i⟩⊗|∇f_i(x)⟩, enabling queries to all n sub-function gradients simultaneously in quantum superposition over the index register. It also requires smoothness ℓ, strong convexity μ (or Lipschitz constant L), and target precision ε as parameters. The output is a point x* ∈ R^d satisfying E[F(x*)] ≤ inf F + ε (convex cases) or E‖∇f(x*)‖ ≤ ε (nonconvex case), with query complexity depending on n, d, and the condition number ℓ/μ.

## Method

The core technique replaces the classical variance-reduction step in Katyusha (convex) and SPIDER (nonconvex) with a quantum variance-reduced gradient (QVRG) subroutine that leverages unbiased quantum mean estimation via multi-level Monte Carlo (Sidford & Zhang 2023, building on Cornelissen et al. 2022). This yields a quadratic speedup in the variance term—reducing the per-iteration cost from O(σ²/ε²) classical samples to Õ(σ/ε) quantum queries—while the unbiasedness is critical because bias would accumulate across the iterative algorithm's convergence. For lower bounds, the paper adapts the Woodworth–Srebro hard instance (randomly chosen orthonormal vectors forming chains) and proves that finding an ε-optimal point requires information about every vector in each sub-function chain, then reduces this to a multi-chain matrix detection problem solved via the non-negative quantum adversary method (Zhang 2005), yielding Ω(n + n^{3/4}(ℓ/μ)^{1/4}) queries.

## Results

For the smooth strongly-convex case (Case 1), the quantum algorithm achieves query complexity Õ(n + √d + √(ℓ/μ)(n^{1/3}d^{1/3} + n^{-2/3}d^{5/6})), improving the classical tight bound Õ(n + √(nℓ/μ)) by reducing the n-dependence from n^{1/2} to n^{1/3} (when d is small). The matching quantum lower bound is Õ(n + n^{3/4}(ℓ/μ)^{1/4}), confirming the speedup is at most polynomial. For the nonconvex critical-point problem, the quantum algorithm uses Õ(n + ℓ(d^{1/3}n^{1/3} + √d)/ε²) queries, improving over the classical SPIDER bound O(n + n^{1/2}/ε²) when d < √n.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the classical Katyusha algorithm (Allen-Zhu 2017), the quantum algorithm achieves a strictly better query complexity whenever the dimension d is relatively small: the n-dependence drops from n^{1/2} (classical) to n^{1/3} (quantum) in the leading term, at the cost of an additional √d factor. Against the classical lower bound of Woodworth & Srebro (2016), the new quantum lower bound is weaker (n^{3/4} vs. n^{1/2} in the non-trivial term), confirming that quantum computing provides a polynomial but not exponential speedup for finite-sum optimization. For the nonconvex critical-point problem, the quantum algorithm (based on SPIDER) achieves Õ(n + ℓ(d^{1/3}n^{1/3} + √d)/ε²) queries versus the classical O(n + n^{1/2}/ε²), yielding a speedup precisely when d < √n. The paper also notes that prior quantum work by Ozgul et al. (2023) on non-logconcave sampling has a dimension factor of at least Ω(d), making the present result the first quantum algorithm with a genuine speedup for finite-sum convex optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper initiates the quantum analogue of finite-sum optimization, a problem that has been extensively studied classically (Katyusha, SVRG, SPIDER) but for which no quantum algorithm with provable speedup existed. The core technical contribution is transplanting the unbiased quantum variance reduction subroutine from Sidford & Zhang (2023) into the iterative frameworks of Katyusha (convex) and SPIDER (nonconvex), replacing classical stochastic gradient sampling with quantum superposition queries over all sub-functions simultaneously. For lower bounds, the paper departs from the standard hybrid-argument approach used in prior quantum optimization lower bounds (Garg et al., Zhang & Li) because quantum superposition over sub-functions invalidates the sequential-access assumption; instead, it adapts the Woodworth–Srebro hard instance and applies the non-boolean quantum adversary method of Zhang (2005) via a reduction to a multi-chain query problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish both quantum upper and lower bounds for finite-sum optimization, confirming that quantum speedup is polynomial (in n) but not exponential, and delineating the precise regime where quantum algorithms outperform classical ones (small d relative to n). The gap between the quantum upper bound (n^{1/3} dependence) and lower bound (n^{3/4} dependence) in the n-parameter leaves room for either improved algorithms or tighter lower bounds. Open questions include whether quantum speedup extends to second-order stationary point finding in nonconvex finite-sum problems, whether the multi-chain reduction is tight, and whether these techniques yield practical speedups for concrete ML tasks like SVM or regression once fault-tolerant hardware is available.
