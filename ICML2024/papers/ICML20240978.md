## Problem

Existing molecular generation frameworks require large datasets to learn the target molecule distribution, yet task-relevant molecules in practical settings (e.g., drug discovery for a specific target) are often extremely scarce due to expensive experimental validation. This data-efficiency gap has been largely overlooked in deep molecular generation, with prior low-shot methods limited to structurally similar molecules like monomers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240978/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of N molecules (as few as 30) represented as SMILES strings, all sharing a common chemical concept (e.g., HIV activity, blood-brain barrier permeability). It outputs novel SMILES strings that lie in the target distribution, with an optional post-hoc modification algorithm that corrects invalid SMILES patterns (branch/ring/valency errors) to achieve 100% validity without additional training. The frozen MolT5-Large-Caption2Smiles model is never updated; only the learned token embeddings are optimized.

## Method

HI-Mol adapts textual inversion from the image domain to molecules by replacing the single shared token with a three-level hierarchy: a shared token [S*] capturing the common concept, K intermediate tokens [I_k*] capturing cluster-wise features, and N detail tokens [D_n*] capturing molecule-specific features. Training optimizes these token embeddings via cross-entropy loss on SMILES strings using a frozen MolT5 model, with the intermediate token assignment for each molecule determined unsupervised by minimizing the loss over all K clusters. For sampling, the method interpolates the intermediate and detail token embeddings of two randomly selected molecules with a coefficient λ drawn from a uniform prior, then feeds the interpolated embeddings alongside [S*] into the frozen model to generate a novel molecule. This multi-level design is critical because molecules sharing a chemical concept often have entirely different structures (unlike visually similar images), making a single token insufficient to capture the distribution.

## Results

On the MoleculeNet benchmark, HI-Mol consistently outperforms all baselines on Active ratio, FCD, and NSPDK across HIV, BBBP, and BACE. On HIV, HI-Mol achieves an Active ratio of 11.4% (vs. 1.6% for STGG), FCD of 16.6 (vs. 20.2), and NSPDK of 0.019 (vs. 0.033). On QM9, HI-Mol trained on only 2% of the data achieves FCD 0.430, outperforming STGG (0.585) trained on the full dataset — a 50× data-efficiency gain. In low-shot property prediction (16/32-shot), HI-Mol yields the highest Δ ROC-AUC across all three MoleculeNet tasks (e.g., +3.53 on BACE 16-shot).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HI-Mol outperforms the strongest SMILES-based baseline STGG on all three MoleculeNet datasets: Active ratio improves from 1.6→11.4 (HIV), 89.1→94.6 (BBBP), and 42.9→80.4 (BACE), while FCD drops from 20.2→16.6, 14.4→10.7, and 17.6→14.0 respectively. Against fragment-based methods (MiCaM, PS-VAE, JT-VAE), HI-Mol achieves substantially lower FCD and NSPDK (e.g., 16.6 vs. 20.4 FCD on HIV vs. MiCaM) and higher Active ratios. Graph-based methods (DiGress, GDSS) suffer from very low validity (17.8% and 69.4% on HIV) and poor FCD; HI-Mol achieves 100% validity (with grammar correction) and far better distributional fidelity. On QM9, HI-Mol trained on only 2% of data (FCD 0.430) beats all full-data baselines including STGG (0.585), GDSS (2.900), GSDM (2.650), and GraphAF (5.268). In the extremely limited regime (30 samples on HIV), HI-Mol improves FCD from 39.2 (DEG) to 34.8, while STGG and CRNN degrade to 41.5 and 40.0. For low-shot property prediction, HI-Mol consistently yields the highest Δ ROC-AUC (e.g., +3.53 on BACE 16-shot vs. +2.34 for STGG and −0.60 for DiGress), confirming that generated molecules carry useful signal for downstream classifiers.

## Relation to Prior Work

HI-Mol adapts textual inversion (Gal et al., 2022) from the text-to-image domain to molecular generation by operating within a frozen pre-trained molecular language model (MolT5, Edwards et al., 2022). The key conceptual departure is replacing the single shared token of image-domain inversion with a three-level hierarchy of tokens (shared, intermediate/cluster, detail/molecule-specific), motivated by the chemical prior that molecular datasets admit hierarchical clustering. This positions the work at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning of large language models and structure-aware molecular generation, departing from both graph-based and fragment-based generative approaches that train from scratch on large datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Data-efficient molecular generation is critical for practical drug discovery, where task-specific active molecules are inherently scarce and expensive to acquire experimentally. The demonstrated 50× data efficiency (outperforming STGG on QM9 with only 2% of training data) and consistent gains on MoleculeNet benchmarks make this practically relevant for scenarios with limited labeled data. However, the framework's ceiling is tied to the quality of the underlying molecular language model, which is itself a very recent and rapidly evolving area; the authors note that future advances in molecular LMs (analogous to the diffusion model breakthroughs in vision) will unlock more capabilities such as compositionality. Open questions include whether the hierarchical token design generalizes to 3D molecular representations and whether the interpolation-based sampling can be made more controllable for targeted property optimization.
