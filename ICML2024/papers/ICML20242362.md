## Problem

Accelerated algorithms for minimax optimization and fixed-point problems with matching upper and lower bounds were recently established using the anchoring mechanism (e.g., OHM, FEG), leading to the assumption that anchoring is the unique optimal acceleration. This paper challenges that assumption by showing the optimal acceleration mechanism is not unique: it introduces dual algorithms that achieve exactly the same worst-case convergence rates while using a fundamentally different acceleration mechanism, reframing optimal acceleration as a family of methods rather than a singular one.

## Input / Output

For fixed-point problems, the method takes a nonexpansive operator T: R^d → R^d, an initial point y_0, and a pre-specified iteration count N, producing an approximate fixed point y_{N-1} with residual norm bounded by 4‖y_0 - y_★‖²/N². For minimax problems, it takes an L-smooth convex-concave function, step size α ≤ 1/L, and N, producing a saddle point approximation with gradient norm bounded by 4‖x_0 - x_★‖²/(α²N²). A key distinction from the primal algorithms is that the dual methods require N (or terminal time T in continuous time) to be specified in advance, and they introduce an auxiliary variable z that accumulates operator evaluations with time-varying coefficients (N-k-1)/(N-k) rather than anchoring to the initial point.

## Method

The core mechanism is H-duality: representing any fixed-step-size algorithm via a lower-triangular H-matrix of coefficients, the H-dual is obtained by anti-diagonal transpose (H^A)_{k,j} = (H)_{N-j,N-k}. Applying this to OHM yields Dual-OHM, which replaces the anchoring-to-y_0 update with a momentum-like rule y_{k+1} = y_k + (N-k-1)/(N-k)(Ty_k - Ty_{k-1}), and applying it to FEG yields Dual-FEG, which uses a z-variable accumulating gradient differences with coefficients (N-k-1)/(N-k). The convergence proof for both dual algorithms uses a Lyapunov function V_k involving ‖z_k + 2Ãx_N‖² that is shown nonincreasing via monotonicity, yielding the identical O(1/N²) rate. In continuous time, the Dual-Anchor ODE replaces the 1/t anchoring coefficient with 1/(T-t), and the paper further shows that an (N-2)-dimensional continuous family of exact optimal algorithms interpolates between OHM and Dual-OHM, with both corresponding to boundary points of this family.

## Results

The paper establishes that Dual-OHM achieves the exact optimal rate ‖y_{N-1} − Ty_{N-1}‖² ≤ 4‖y_0 − y_⋆‖²/N² for nonexpansive fixed-point problems, matching the known lower bound with no constant-factor gap. Dual-FEG achieves ‖∇L(x_N)‖² ≤ 4‖x_0 − x_⋆‖²/(α²N²) for L-smooth convex-concave minimax, matching FEG's rate and the lower bound up to a constant factor of 16. Additionally, the paper proves the existence of a continuous (N−2)-dimensional family of exact optimal fixed-point algorithms interpolating between OHM and Dual-OHM. Empirically, Dual-FEG reaches terminal accuracy significantly earlier than FEG on strongly-convex-strongly-concave bilinear problems, while both algorithms produce identical terminal iterates for linear operators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between each new dual algorithm and its primal counterpart. Dual-OHM ties OHM exactly on worst-case convergence rate (both achieve 4‖y₀−y⋆‖²/N², matching the lower bound from Park & Ryu 2022), but uses a fundamentally different mechanism: OHM pulls iterates toward the initial point y₀ (anchoring), while Dual-OHM applies momentum to successive T-evaluations. Similarly, Dual-FEG ties FEG on the minimax rate 4‖x₀−x⋆‖²/(α²N²), and both outperform the unaccelerated Extragradient (EG) method of Korpelevich (1976) by an order of k in the rate (O(1/k²) vs. O(1/k)). Empirically (Figures 2b, 3), Dual-FEG reaches the terminal accuracy much earlier than FEG on strongly-convex-strongly-concave bilinear problems, suggesting a practical advantage for strongly monotone operators. However, for linear operators the terminal iterates of FEG and Dual-FEG are provably identical (Appendix I.3), while for nonlinear operators (Figure 1b) the terminal iterates diverge. The paper also notes a practical trade-off: dual algorithms require the total iteration count N (or terminal time T) to be specified in advance, whereas primal algorithms do not.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the line of work establishing optimal accelerated algorithms for fixed-point and minimax problems: OHM (Lieder, 2021) and FEG (Lee & Kim, 2021) were previously believed to be the unique optimal mechanisms based on the anchoring/Halpern-type update. The authors repurpose the H-duality framework from convex minimization (Kim et al., 2023a) to fixed-point and minimax settings, using the anti-diagonal transpose of H-matrices to systematically derive Dual-OHM and Dual-FEG as counterparts with identical worst-case rates. The continuous-time analysis extends the Anchor ODE (Ryu et al., 2019) by introducing a Dual-Anchor ODE with a terminal-time singularity, and the Lyapunov analysis draws on monotone operator theory (Eckstein & Bertsekas, 1992) to establish convergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that exact optimal acceleration is a family rather than a singleton—reframes the study of optimal first-order methods from searching for 'the' algorithm to characterizing a space of equally optimal mechanisms with different practical behaviors (e.g., Dual-FEG converges faster in early iterations for strongly monotone operators). The H-duality correspondence provides a principled recipe for discovering new optimal algorithms by dualizing known ones, potentially applicable beyond the fixed-point/minimax setting. Open questions include: the complete characterization of all exact optimal algorithms (Theorem 4.1 gives only a subset), whether H-duality extends to other problem classes (e.g., nonconvex-nonconcave minimax), and what additional criteria beyond worst-case rate should guide algorithm selection in practice.
