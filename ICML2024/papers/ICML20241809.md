## Problem

Standard Multi-Armed Bandit assumes arms are perpetually available, but in real-world settings like online advertising and crowdsourcing, arms (advertisers, workers) will exit the game if continuously neglected. This paper formalizes the MAB with Impatient Arms setting where each arm has a patience threshold and leaves after being ignored for that many consecutive steps, then shows that existing algorithms (UCB, SE) can incur near-linear regret when the optimal arm is impatient, and derives a minimax lower bound proving fundamental hardness when the load factor exceeds 1.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a known patience vector m (positive integers per arm), time horizon T, and stochastic 1-sub-Gaussian reward observations. It outputs an arm pull sequence that guarantees no arm departs before being identified as sub-optimal. Unusually, the regret is always measured against the global optimal arm k* even after it has left the game, reflecting the cost of failing to keep it available. The patience vector is assumed known in advance, and the algorithm must construct a pulling schedule (feasible cycle) that respects all patience constraints before any learning-based elimination occurs.

## Method

FC-SE replaces the Round-Robin schedule in Successive Elimination with a 'feasible cycle'—a cyclic arm sequence where the gap between consecutive pulls of any arm never exceeds its patience. When a feasible cycle covers all K arms, FC-SE achieves Õ(n + √(nT)) regret where n is the cycle length. When only a subset of N < K arms can form a feasible cycle, the algorithm divides time into segments of length c, uses checkpoints to pull remaining arms before their patience expires, and randomly drops arms from the cycle to make room. The shortest feasible cycle is constructed via an Almost Uniform Schedule (AUS) optimization borrowed from the Age of Information literature, solved by dynamic programming. FC-Entry extends the framework to new entering arms with unknown patience by reserving two virtual arm slots in the cycle, achieving Õ(K²T^{2/3}n^{1/3}) dynamic regret under sparse entry assumptions.

## Results

FC-SE achieves an expected regret of Õ(n + √(nT)) when a feasible cycle covers all K arms (N=K), and Õ(K^{4/3} T^{2/3} n^{1/3}) when only a subset of N < K arms fits in the initial cycle. FC-Entry extends this to the setting with new entering arms, achieving dynamic regret Õ(K² T^{2/3} n^{1/3}). In simulations with T = 10⁵, FC-SE and FC-Entry exhibit sub-linear regret growth while UCB and SE baselines show linear or near-linear regret in the impatient-arm configurations tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares FC-SE and FC-Entry against two standard baselines: UCB and Successive Elimination (SE). UCB is shown to incur nearly linear regret Ω(Δ_min T^{1−γ} / (C(T) ln T)) when the optimal arm has patience m_{k*} = O((ln T)^θ) for any θ ∈ (0,1), even when the load factor l(K,m) → 0 (Theorem 3.5). SE, which uses a Round-Robin schedule, provably incurs full linear regret R_T = Δ̄·T whenever any arm has patience m_k < K (Proposition 3.6). FC-SE outperforms both baselines in the intermediate regime (some m_k < K but a feasible cycle exists for a subset of arms), achieving sub-linear regret where both UCB and SE fail. In the simulations (Fig. 2b–d), FC-SE and FC-Entry maintain sub-linear regret curves while UCB and SE exhibit linear growth in the impatient configurations. When all arms are sufficiently patient (m_k ≥ K), SE and UCB remain competitive, and FC-SE's advantage is not needed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces the Multi-Armed Bandit with Impatient Arms setting, where arm availability is determined endogenously by the algorithm's own past actions (an arm exits after m_k consecutive ignored pulls), distinguishing it from sleeping bandits where availability is stochastic or adversarial. The regret definition also departs from sleeping bandit literature by always comparing against the global optimal arm k*, even after it has departed. For the algorithmic core, FC-SE replaces the Round-Robin schedule of Successive Elimination (Even-Dar et al., 2006) with a feasible cycle, and the cycle construction borrows the Almost Uniform Schedule (AUS) framework from the Age of Information scheduling literature (Li et al., 2021, 2023a), creating a non-trivial bridge between bandit theory and real-time scheduling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a clean hardness threshold: when the load factor l(K,m) > 1, no algorithm can achieve sub-linear regret, and it shows that standard UCB and SE incur (nearly) linear regret in broad intermediate regimes. This clarifies the boundary between tractable and intractable instances and motivates the feasible-cycle approach. Open questions include the computational complexity of finding optimal feasible cycles for arbitrary patience vectors, whether the gap between the l(K,m) ≤ ln 2 sufficient condition and l(K,m) > 1 impossibility can be tightened, and how these ideas extend to combinatorial or contextual variants. The FC-Entry extension to dynamic arm entry with unknown patience is a natural but important step toward realistic deployment in advertising and crowdsourcing.
