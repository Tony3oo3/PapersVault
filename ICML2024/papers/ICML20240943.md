## Problem

Existing finite-time analyses of neural TD and Q-learning under Markovian sampling only guarantee Õ(ε⁻²) sample complexity, despite the fact that Õ(ε⁻¹) is achievable by double-loop methods like fitted Q-iteration. The paper asks whether the single-loop neural TD/Q-learning algorithm can be analyzed to achieve the optimal Õ(ε⁻¹) rate under general multi-layer network approximation, closing the gap between theory and the expected sample efficiency.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a stream of state-action-reward-transition tuples (s_t, a_t, r_t, s_{t+1}, a_{t+1}) generated online by a fixed learning policy π under non-i.i.d. Markovian sampling, along with an L-layer Q network of width m initialized with Gaussian weights. It outputs parameters θ^T such that the expected squared error E[(Q(s,a;θ^T) − Q*(s,a))²] ≤ ε + ε_F, where ε_F is the irreducible approximation error of the local linearization function class. The analysis requires the network width m ≥ m* (sufficiently wide) and a normalized feature map ‖φ(s,a)‖ = 1, and notably only assumes positive semi-definiteness of the feature covariance matrix Σ_π rather than the stronger positive definiteness used in prior work.

## Method

The core contribution is a new subspace analysis technique that decomposes the parameter space into the range space R(Σ_π) and kernel space K(Σ_π) of the feature covariance matrix. Proposition 3.4 shows that for any iterate θ^t, there exists a fixed-point parameter θ_*^t such that θ^t − θ_*^t ∈ R(Σ_π), meaning kernel-space components do not affect Q-function values. Combined with the non-singularity of Σ_π on R(Σ_π) (Proposition 3.5), this yields a fast O(1/T) convergence rate for ‖θ^t − θ_*^t‖², which translates directly to Õ(ε⁻¹) sample complexity for the projected stochastic semi-gradient algorithm minimizing the MSPBE objective. The algorithm uses a general L-layer network with smooth activations (ELU, GeLU, Sigmoid) and a decaying learning rate η_t = 1/(2(1−γ)λ₀(t+1)), with the same technique extended to neural Q-learning and minimax Q-learning for two-player zero-sum Markov games.

## Results

The paper establishes an improved Õ(ε⁻¹) sample complexity for neural TD learning and Q-learning with general L-layer networks under Markovian sampling, improving upon the prior best Õ(ε⁻²) bound. The same Õ(ε⁻¹) complexity is extended to minimax neural Q-learning for two-player zero-sum Markov games. Empirically, on OpenAI Gym tasks (both discrete and continuous state spaces), the TD algorithm shows faster convergence and smaller final TD errors as network width m increases, and the condition number ratio r = σ_max/σ_min of Σ_π stabilizes to a constant for large m, validating the key regularity assumption (Assumption 3.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's Õ(ε⁻¹) sample complexity strictly improves over all existing neural TD/Q-learning baselines, which achieve at best Õ(ε⁻²). Specifically, Cai et al. (2023) and Xu & Gu (2020) both require Ω(1/ε⁴) or Ω(1/ε⁶) network width and achieve only O(1/ε²) complexity; Sun et al. (2022) achieves O(1/ε^(2/(2−α))) which degrades to O(1/ε²) without additional sparsity assumptions (α=1); Tian et al. (2022) also achieves O(1/ε²) with ELU/GeLU activations. The only method matching the O(1/ε) rate is Bhandari et al. (2018), but that result is for linear (non-neural) function approximation. The key technical advance is a new subspace analysis that decomposes the parameter space into the range and kernel of the feature covariance matrix Σ_π, enabling faster convergence in the range subspace while the kernel component is shown to be irrelevant to Q-function values. The paper also relaxes the strict positive definiteness assumption (≻0) on Σ_π to positive semi-definiteness (⪰0) on its nonzero singular values, which is a weaker and more natural condition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the line of finite-time analyses for neural TD and Q-learning by Xu & Gu (2020), Cai et al. (2023), Sun et al. (2022), and Tian et al. (2022), all of which achieved Õ(ε⁻²) sample complexity under Markovian sampling. The key departure is a novel subspace decomposition technique that splits the parameter space into the range and kernel of the feature covariance matrix Σ_π, allowing the analysis to track convergence only in the range subspace where the linearized Q-function is non-singular. This avoids the positive-definiteness assumption on Σ_π required by prior work (only positive semi-definiteness is needed), and is the mechanism that upgrades the complexity to Õ(ε⁻¹), matching what double-loop methods like FQI and Gauss-Newton achieve but in a more practical single-loop setting. The paper also extends the analysis to minimax Q-learning for two-player zero-sum Markov games, which had no prior neural-network finite-time analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Õ(ε⁻¹) sample complexity closes a gap between single-loop neural TD methods and the more computationally expensive double-loop alternatives (FQI, Gauss-Newton), making the theoretically optimal rate accessible in the standard online RL setting. The subspace analysis technique is of independent interest and the authors note it could potentially extend to linear Q-learning and actor-critic algorithms without requiring positive definiteness of the feature covariance matrix. However, the empirical validation is limited to basic OpenAI Gym experiments with two-layer networks, and the width requirement of Ω(1/ε²) remains substantial. Open questions include whether the technique generalizes to actor-critic methods, whether it holds under i.i.d. sampling, and whether the over-parameterization requirement can be further reduced.
