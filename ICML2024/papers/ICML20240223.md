## Problem

Prior work on Reinforcement Learning with Aggregate Bandit Feedback (RL-ABF), where the agent only observes the sum of rewards at episode end rather than each individual reward, was limited to tabular MDPs with small state spaces. In practical applications the state space is enormous and function approximation is essential, yet ABF under function approximation cannot be reduced to a linear bandits problem without incurring regret that scales with the number of states. This paper is the first to develop efficient algorithms with near-optimal regret for ABF in linear MDPs.

## Input / Output

At each episode the agent specifies a stochastic Markov policy, observes the full trajectory of state-action pairs (x_h, a_h) for h ∈ [H], and receives only the scalar aggregate reward v^k = Σ_h r_h^k rather than the H individual rewards. The state-action features φ(x,a) ∈ R^d are known and bounded (‖φ‖ ≤ 1), and the algorithm must produce a policy per episode while achieving regret Õ(poly(dH)√K) over K episodes, where d is the feature dimension and H is the horizon. Notably, the regret bounds are independent of the state space size |X|.

## Method

The paper introduces two algorithms built around a novel ensemble randomization technique: RE-LSVI (value-based) and REPO (policy optimization). The core idea is to draw m ≈ log(K/δ) independent Gaussian noise vectors ζ^{k,i} ~ N(0, β_r²(Λ^k)^{-1}) and compute m separate Q-function estimates, each perturbed by a different noise vector; the agent then follows the policy corresponding to the most optimistic value estimate, which is provably optimistic with high probability via an anti-concentration argument. A loose symmetric clipping mechanism keeps value estimates bounded without introducing bias into the random walk induced by the noise. For REPO, a Multiplicative Weights hedging scheme arbitrates over the ensemble members, enabling the first policy optimization algorithm for ABF (which did not exist even in the tabular setting), combined with a reward-free warm-up phase and an epoch schedule that keeps perturbations fixed within each epoch to preserve Gaussianity of their sum.

## Results

The paper establishes near-optimal regret bounds for two algorithms in linear MDPs with aggregate bandit feedback. RE-LSVI (Theorem 1) achieves Õ(√(d⁵H⁷K)) regret, while REPO (Theorem 2) achieves Õ(√(d⁵H⁹K log⁹(|A|/δ))) regret. Both bounds have the optimal √K dependence on the number of episodes, and the results are independent of the state space size |X|. RE-LSVI has a tighter dependence on H (H⁷ vs. H⁹), while REPO is the first policy optimization algorithm for ABF, a setting where no PO algorithm existed even in the tabular case prior to this work.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The key theoretical comparisons are: (1) Efroni et al. (2021) solved ABF only in tabular MDPs using a complex Thompson Sampling analysis; this paper extends to linear MDPs with a simpler optimism-based analysis via the ensemble randomization technique. (2) Wu & Sun (2023) solved the closely related Preference-based RL in linear MDPs using randomization, but their approach requires a complex TS analysis and yields worse dependence on d and H. (3) Sherman et al. (2023) proved √K regret for PO in linear MDPs with standard reward feedback; REPO adapts their reward-free warm-up and indicator mechanism to the ABF setting, which is substantially harder since individual rewards are unobserved. The paper notes that formulating ABF as a linear bandit problem (as in the tabular case) would lead to regret scaling with the number of states, which the proposed algorithms avoid.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Aggregate Bandit Feedback (ABF) model from tabular MDPs (Efroni et al., 2021) to linear MDPs with function approximation, building directly on the LSVI-UCB framework of Jin et al. (2020b) for standard reward feedback. The key methodological departure is replacing the complex Thompson Sampling analysis of Efroni et al. with a randomized ensemble technique: drawing m independent Gaussian perturbations of the reward estimate and selecting the maximally optimistic value function, which yields a straightforward optimism-based regret proof. For the policy optimization algorithm (REPO), the paper builds on the reward-free warm-up and indicator mechanism of Sherman et al. (2023) and Wagenmaker et al. (2022), introducing a novel multiplicative-weights hedging scheme over the ensemble that enables the first PO algorithm for ABF in any setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to achieve near-optimal √K regret for ABF under function approximation, closing a gap that limited prior theory to small state spaces. The ensemble randomization technique is a clean, reusable alternative to Thompson Sampling that may apply to other settings where aggregate or delayed feedback breaks per-step credit assignment. Open questions include whether the epoch schedule in REPO is truly necessary (the authors speculate a Berry-Esseen-type argument might eliminate it), whether the tabular PO analysis (which avoids the warm-up) can be extended to linear MDPs, and whether the H^7/H^9 dependence can be tightened. The paper is purely theoretical with no empirical validation, limiting immediate practical impact.
