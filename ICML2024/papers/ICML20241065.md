## Problem

Text-guided image compression methods that use text to steer a generative decoder achieve high perceptual quality but suffer 3–5 dB PSNR degradation relative to standard neural codecs. It remains unclear whether text can improve perceptual fidelity at conventional bitrates (≥0.1 bpp) without substantially sacrificing pixel-wise accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241065/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At encoding time the method consumes an RGB image (256×256 crops for training; full-resolution for CLIC/Kodak evaluation) together with a text caption of up to 38 tokens. At decoding time only the compressed bitstream is needed—no text is required—producing a reconstructed image. For datasets without human captions (CLIC, Kodak), captions are generated by OFA; the method is shown to be robust to caption source (human, OFA, BLIP-2, GPT-4).

## Method

TACO augments the PSNR-oriented ELIC autoencoder backbone with a frozen CLIP text encoder and a six-layer text adapter (three linear layers interleaved with three cross-attention layers: Text→Image, Image→Text, and Text→Image at a downsampled resolution) that injects caption semantics into the encoder's latent features. The full network is trained from scratch with a joint loss combining rate, MSE, LPIPS, and a novel joint image-text loss that applies CLIP contrastive loss between the caption and the reconstruction plus a CLIP embedding distance between the original and reconstructed image. Crucially, text is used only at encoding (to guide what perceptually meaningful information to preserve in the latent code) rather than at decoding, avoiding the high generative diversity of diffusion- or GAN-based decoders that causes PSNR loss. No adversarial loss is employed, keeping the decoder a standard autoencoder that produces accurate reconstructions with minimal generative variance.

## Results

On MS-COCO 30k, TACO achieves 0.025 LPIPS at 0.226 bpp, less than half the best baseline (HiFiC at 0.053 at 0.233 bpp), while maintaining PSNR within ~1 dB of ELIC and LIC-TCM. This LPIPS advantage holds across all three evaluation datasets (MS-COCO 30k, CLIC, Kodak), where TACO outperforms every baseline in perceptual fidelity. On MS-COCO 40k, TACO also surpasses the text-guided decoding baseline (Qin 2023) in both LPIPS and FID while keeping PSNR competitive. The added computational overhead is modest: ~10% encoding latency increase over ELIC (78.6 ms vs. 71.4 ms at 256×256), and the total model size (101.75M params) is ~80% smaller than perception-oriented codecs like MS-ILLM and HiFiC (181.72M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PSNR-oriented codecs (ELIC, LIC-TCM), TACO matches pixel-level fidelity within ~1 dB while dramatically improving LPIPS (e.g., 0.025 vs. ~0.05+ on MS-COCO 30k). Against perception-oriented codecs (HiFiC, MS-ILLM), TACO achieves substantially lower LPIPS (0.025 vs. 0.053 for HiFiC) while recovering most of the PSNR gap that these methods typically sacrifice. Against the text-guided decoding baselines (TGIC/Jiang 2023 and Qin 2023), TACO outperforms in both LPIPS and FID on MS-COCO 40k, which the authors note is somewhat surprising since text-guided encoding was primarily motivated by preserving PSNR. Against PerCo (Agustsson et al., CVPR 2023), TACO wins on both PSNR and LPIPS despite PerCo's impressive FID. In text similarity (Table 1), TACO at 0.2146 bpp achieves BLEU-4 of 0.84 and CIDEr-D of 8.21, outperforming LIC-TCM (0.76/7.44) and ELIC (0.78/7.58) while slightly trailing MS-ILLM (0.85/8.39) which uses more bits.

## Relation to Prior Work

TACO builds on the ELIC autoencoder-based neural image compression backbone but departs fundamentally from the dominant text-guided image compression paradigm, which uses text to guide the decoding process (via diffusion models, GANs, or plug-in decoder modules). Instead, TACO repurposes text exclusively for the encoding side, injecting CLIP text features into the encoder through a cross-attention adapter and training with a joint image-text contrastive loss. This inverts the lineage established by Pan et al. (2022), Jiang et al. (2023), and Qin et al. (2023), all of which treat text as a decoding-side signal, and aligns more closely with the original ELIC architecture's PSNR-oriented design philosophy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that at standard bitrates (≥0.1 bpp), the primary value of text in image compression lies in its alignment with human perception rather than in its information-theoretic efficiency as a compact code, challenging the textual transform coding motivation. This opens a practical path to combining pixel-level and perceptual fidelity without the 3–5 dB PSNR penalty typical of diffusion-based codecs. Key limitations include the quadratic scaling of cross-attention with caption length (problematic for video transcripts), the dependency on image-text training pairs, and the need for a caption at inference time; whether the approach extends to video or very long-text settings remains open.
