## Problem

The statistical properties of fitted Q-evaluation (FQE) for off-policy evaluation remain incompletely understood, particularly under nonparametric Q-function models. Three fundamental questions are largely open: whether the optimal n^{-1/2} convergence rate is achievable for policy value estimation under nonparametric models with fixed horizon, how error bounds scale with growing horizon T (previous parametric results give T^2/√n while tabular MIS achieves T/√n), and what role probability ratio functions play in sharpening FQE convergence beyond the tabular setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n i.i.d. offline trajectories {S_t, A_t, R_t}_{t=1}^T generated by a fixed behavior policy in a finite-horizon time-inhomogeneous MDP with continuous state space S ⊂ R^d and finite action space A, along with a specified target policy π. It produces a plug-in policy value estimate ν̂(π) = ∫ Q̂_1^π(s,a) ρ_1^π(s,a) d(s,a) and establishes high-probability finite-sample error bounds. In the nonparametric setting, the number of sieve basis functions K is allowed to grow with n (e.g., K ∝ n^{1/(1+2β)}), and the analysis tracks how the interplay between K, n, and T determines the dominant error term.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a three-term error decomposition of the FQE value estimator (E1: first-order stochastic term, E2: Bellman operator estimation error, E3: approximation bias) that enables separate, sharp bounding of each component. By exploiting the connection between FQE and marginal importance sampling, they show that the variance of E1 decomposes as a sum of T conditional variances (via a martingale-type argument), yielding a T^{1.5}/√n first-order rate instead of the T^2/√n in prior parametric work. Under an additional realizability assumption that probability ratio functions w_t^π lie in the same sieve space as Q-functions, the first-order term improves to T/√n, matching the sharpest tabular MIS bound. For nonparametric Q-functions in Hölder spaces, they show that the optimal n^{-1/2} rate is achievable when Q-functions are sufficiently smooth (β_Q > 1) or when ratio functions are realizable (removing the β_Q > 1 requirement), with the approximation bias rendered asymptotically negligible by appropriate choice of K.

## Results

The paper establishes that under the completeness assumption for Q-functions, FQE estimators achieve the optimal n^{-1/2} convergence rate for policy value estimation in both parametric and nonparametric settings (the latter requiring Q-functions to be sufficiently smooth, i.e., β_Q > 1). For the horizon dependence, the first-order error term scales as T^{1.5}/√n (improving upon the T²/√n bound in Duan et al., 2020 and Zhang et al., 2022), and further improves to T/√n when the probability ratio functions satisfy a realizability condition, matching the sharpest known tabular bound from Yin & Wang (2020). In the nonparametric setting without ratio realizability, the rate is n^{-β_Q/2} (slower than optimal) for 1/2 < β_Q ≤ 1, but recovers n^{-1/2} when β_Q > 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Duan et al. (2020) and Zhang et al. (2022), which establish a T²/√n first-order bound under linear/differentiable function approximation, this paper improves the horizon dependence to T^{1.5}/√n using the same realizability and completeness assumptions, by exploiting a variance decomposition that separates T individual conditional variances. Compared to Nguyen-Tang et al. (2021) and Ji et al. (2022) in the nonparametric setting, the paper achieves a strictly faster n-dependence: n^{-1/2} (when β_Q > 1) versus n^{-α/(2α+2D)} or n^{-α/(2α+d)} respectively, and a milder T-dependence (T^{1.5} vs. T² or T^{2−α/(2α+2D)}). The key new result is that under an additional realizability assumption on the probability ratio functions (Assumption 3.4 or 3.11), the first-order bound improves to T/√n in both parametric and nonparametric settings, matching the tabular MIS bound of Yin & Wang (2020) without requiring explicit estimation of the ratio functions. This 'double-rate robustness' property means FQE implicitly benefits from smooth ratio functions even though they are not directly modeled.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical analysis of Fitted Q-evaluation (FQE) for off-policy evaluation by unifying parametric (linear) and nonparametric (linear sieve with B-spline/wavelet bases) modeling of Q-functions under a time-inhomogeneous MDP framework. It builds on Duan et al. (2020) and Zhang et al. (2022) for parametric FQE, and on Nguyen-Tang et al. (2021) and Ji et al. (2022) for nonparametric FQE, but departs from both by using a different error decomposition that separates the first-order (variance) term from the bias-induced term, enabling sharper horizon dependence. A key conceptual departure is the explicit connection to marginal importance sampling (MIS) estimators (Yin & Wang, 2020; Xie et al., 2019), which the authors leverage to show that linear horizon dependence (T/√n) is achievable for FQE beyond the tabular setting when probability ratio functions satisfy smoothness conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves three open questions in the FQE literature: it confirms that the optimal n^{-1/2} rate is achievable for nonparametric FQE under mild smoothness (Hölder) conditions on Q-functions, improves the first-order horizon dependence from T² to T^{1.5} (and to T with ratio-function realizability), and identifies a 'double-rate robustness' property where explicit estimation of ratio functions is unnecessary. A notable open question is whether the cubic T dependence in the higher-order term can be reduced to match the T^{3/2} rate in the tabular setting of Yin & Wang (2020). The practical impact is primarily theoretical—guiding practitioners on when nonparametric FQE is statistically efficient and what smoothness conditions on the environment are needed—but the simulation study is limited to a simple 1D MDP, leaving open questions about performance in high-dimensional or complex continuous-state settings.
