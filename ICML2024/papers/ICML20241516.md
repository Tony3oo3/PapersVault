## Problem

Despite numerous off-policy RL algorithms reporting state-of-the-art results through various regularization techniques, the specific mechanisms driving improvements remain poorly understood because most methods are tested in narrow settings—typically a single benchmark with limited task diversity. This paper asks whether general neural network regularizers can outperform RL-specific algorithmic improvements, and how overestimation, overfitting, and plasticity loss interact to affect agent performance across diverse environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241516/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The agents consume proprioceptive state vectors from 14 continuous-control tasks spanning locomotion (hopper, humanoid, dog, quadruped) and manipulation (MetaWorld) under two replay ratio regimes (RR=2 and RR=16), producing continuous action vectors. A secondary evaluation extends to RGB image inputs via the DrQ architecture on 6 DMC tasks. All agents share a three-layer MLP (256×256 hidden units, ReLU), with all regularizations applied exclusively to the critic network.

## Method

The paper systematically implements 64+ configurations by combining one method from each of three regularization families—critic regularization (CDQ, TOP, GPL), network regularization (Layer Norm, Spectral Norm, Weight Decay), and plasticity regularization (full-parameter resets, CRLU, SAM)—within the Soft Actor-Critic framework, evaluated over 10 seeds per configuration. Performance is dissected through first-, second-, and third-order marginalization to isolate individual effects and cross-family interactions. The central finding is that general network regularizers, particularly layer norm paired with full-parameter resets, consistently outperform RL-specific critic regularizations and enable model-free agents to solve previously unsolvable Dog-domain tasks. Correlation analysis of proxy metrics (overestimation, overfitting, dormant neurons, gradient norm) reveals that interventions targeting one problem (e.g., resets for plasticity) often more strongly affect metrics associated with other problems (e.g., overestimation), indicating a multifaceted learning challenge rather than independent failure modes.

## Results

Across 14 tasks from DMC and MetaWorld under two replay ratio regimes (RR=2 and RR=16), the paper finds that a simple SAC agent with Layer Normalization (LN) and full-parameter resets reliably achieves state-of-the-art model-free performance on Dog-Run and Dog-Trot—tasks previously unsolved by model-free agents using proprioceptive states—approaching the performance of model-based methods (Hansen et al., 2022). Spearman correlation analysis reveals that overestimation exhibits the strongest negative correlation with agent returns on both benchmarks, while critic gradient norm is the dominant predictor in Dog environments. Network regularization (LN, SN) reduces critic approximation error more effectively than purpose-built critic regularization (CDQ, TOP, GPL), and combining LN with resets in the high-replay regime (RR=16) yields the most robust performance across DMC tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is the model-based TD-MPC2 agent (Hansen et al., 2022) on Dog-Run and Dog-Trot. The paper's model-free SAC with LN + full-parameter resets over 4M steps achieves performance 'very close to' the model-based approach, with the model-based agent still slightly ahead—marking the first time a model-free agent with proprioceptive states finds a better-performing policy within the training regime on these tasks. Internally, the paper systematically compares 60+ regularization configurations and finds that critic regularization methods (CDQ, TOP, GPL) are consistently outperformed by network + plasticity regularization combinations; in particular, adding critic regularization on top of LN or resets often degrades performance. Layer norm dominates on DMC (especially Dog environments), while spectral norm is more robust on MetaWorld. Full-parameter resets significantly outperform other plasticity-inducing methods (CRLU, SAM, ReDo) in both robustness and peak performance, particularly in the high-replay regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Soft Actor-Critic as a unified framework to systematically compare regularization techniques that have been proposed in isolation across recent off-policy RL literature: critic regularization (CDQ, TOP, GPL), network regularization (Layer Norm, Spectral Norm, Weight Decay), and plasticity regularization (full-parameter resets, CRLU, SAM). Prior work typically validated each technique on a single benchmark or task family, making cross-technique comparisons difficult. The paper's positioning follows Sutton's 'Bitter Lesson' thesis, hypothesizing that general neural network regularizers motivated by gradient-based learning stability may outperform RL-specific algorithmic improvements, and it tests this hypothesis across 60+ configurations on 14 tasks from two benchmarks under two replay ratio regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is a clear, well-evidenced recipe: replacing the ubiquitous Clipped Double Q-learning with Layer Norm (for DMC) or Spectral Norm (for MetaWorld) plus full-parameter resets (for high replay ratios) yields robust, superior performance and enables model-free SAC to solve previously unsolvable Dog locomotion tasks. Limitations include evaluation restricted to proprioceptive states, a single algorithm (SAC), and two benchmarks; the image-based DrQ experiments were inconclusive, leaving open whether these findings transfer to pixel-based settings. A broader open question is whether the observed environment-dependent effectiveness of different regularizers reflects fundamental differences in loss landscape geometry that could be exploited for automatic regularization selection.
