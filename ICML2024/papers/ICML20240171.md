## Problem

Deep learning models are known to memorize spurious features in training data, yet no rigorous theoretical framework exists to quantify this phenomenon. Existing generalization theory (benign overfitting, double descent) models noise in labels rather than in input features, so it cannot capture how irrelevant input components become correlated with labels through overfitting. Moreover, the role of the model architecture and activation function in governing the degree of memorization remained uncharacterized.

## Input / Output

The method takes training samples z = [x, y] where x is the informative core feature and y is an uninformative spurious component (e.g., a noise background), with labels g(x) depending only on x. The trained model is then queried on a spurious sample z^s = [x', y_i] where the core feature is replaced by an independent draw (or set to zero in image experiments), and the memorization is measured as the covariance between f(z^s, θ*) and the original label g_i. The theoretical output is a bound Cov(f(z^s, θ*), g) ≤ γ·√(R_{Z-1})·√(Var(g)), where γ is an explicit constant depending on the feature map and activation, and R_{Z-1} is the generalization error of the model trained without sample z_1.

## Method

The key insight is that memorization of spurious features decomposes into two multiplicative terms: the leave-one-out stability S_{z_1} (which equals the generalization error of the model trained without z_1) and a novel feature alignment F_φ(z^s, z_1) measuring the similarity between the spurious and full samples in the model's feature space. Lemma 4.1 establishes this decomposition via the projector P_{Φ_{-1}}^⊥ over the span of the remaining training features, showing S_{z_1}(z) = F_φ(z, z_1)·S_{z_1}(z_1). For random features, Theorem 5.4 proves F_RF concentrates to γ_RF bounded below by Σ_{l≥2} μ_l² α^l / Σ_{l≥1} μ_l², where μ_l are Hermite coefficients of the activation and α = d_y/d is the spurious fraction. For NTK, Theorem 6.3 yields the closed-form γ_NTK = α·Σ_{l≥1} μ'_l² α^l / Σ_{l≥1} μ'_l², making the dependence on the activation derivative explicit. The proofs rely on high-dimensional concentration (Hanson-Wright inequality) and Hermite polynomial expansions to show that the feature alignment is governed by the non-linear (higher-order) components of the activation, with higher-order Hermite coefficients suppressing memorization.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical contribution is a closed-form characterization of the feature alignment constant γ that governs spurious memorization. For RF models (Theorem 5.4), γ_RF is bounded below by (Σ_{l≥2} μ_l² α^l)/(Σ_{l≥1} μ_l²), where μ_l are Hermite coefficients of the activation and α = d_y/d is the spurious fraction. For NTK models (Theorem 6.3), γ_NTK = α · (Σ_{l≥1} μ_l'² α^l)/(Σ_{l≥1} μ_l'²) is given in exact closed form. The memorization covariance is bounded as Cov(f(z₁ˢ,θ*), g₁) ≤ γ_φ √(ℛ_{Z_{-1}}) √(Var(g₁)), showing memorization scales with generalization error. Experiments on MNIST and CIFAR-10 confirm that spurious accuracy decreases as training set size N grows, increases with α, and is reduced by activations with dominant high-order Hermite coefficients.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without a direct method-vs-baseline comparison; the `compares_to` list is empty. Instead, the experiments compare different activation functions and architectures under a unified spurious-accuracy protocol. For RF models on synthetic Gaussian data (Figure 2), the activation φ₂ = h₁ + h₂ (dominant low-order Hermite coefficients) yields higher spurious accuracy than φ₄ = h₁ + h₄, consistent with the theoretical prediction that lower-order coefficients increase γ_RF. For NTK models on MNIST (digits 1 vs 7) and CIFAR-10 (cats vs ships) (Figure 3), the activation with φ'₂ = h₀ + h₁ produces more memorization than φ'₈ = h₀ + h₇, matching the closed-form γ_NTK prediction. Across architectures (fully-connected, small CNN, and two ResNet variants in Figures 4–5), the qualitative trend holds: test accuracy rises and spurious accuracy falls with increasing N, and the spurious accuracy at any fixed N is above the random-guessing baseline (0.5 for binary, 0.1 for 10-class), confirming that memorization persists even in feature-learning networks beyond the lazy RF/NTK regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected lines of work: the classical stability-generalization literature (Bousquet & Elisseeff, 2002) and the empirical study of spurious feature memorization (Yang et al., 2022; Hermann & Lampinen, 2020). While prior theoretical analyses of over-parameterized models (Mei & Montanari, 2022; Montanari & Zhong, 2022) characterize in-distribution generalization for RF and NTK, they model noise in the labels rather than in the input, leaving spurious feature memorization unaddressed. The paper's key conceptual contribution is the 'feature alignment' F(z^s, z), a novel quantity that decomposes memorization into a stability term (well-established) and a geometric overlap term (new), then proves concentration of this alignment to a closed-form constant for both RF and NTK feature maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous theoretical framework that quantifies spurious feature memorization and explicitly links it to generalization error, providing practitioners with a principled handle: the degree of memorization is governed by the activation function's Hermite coefficients and the fraction of input occupied by the spurious feature. The finding that higher-order Hermite coefficients in the activation reduce memorization offers a concrete design principle. Limitations include the focus on generalized linear regression (RF/NTK) in the theory, though experiments on CNNs and ResNets suggest the framework transfers; open questions include extending to correlated spurious features, test-time novel spurious features, and incorporating simplicity bias dynamics.
