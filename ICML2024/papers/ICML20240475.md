## Problem

Detecting whether copyrighted content was used to train a language model is critical for accountability and legal compliance, yet training data is typically undisclosed. Existing membership inference methods either require access to token-level probabilities (inapplicable to fully black-box models like ChatGPT or Claude) or rely on direct extraction prompts that increasingly trigger safety refusals. The paper addresses the gap of a detection method that works on any LLM regardless of logit availability while identifying a substantial volume of potentially copyrighted content.

## Input / Output

The method takes a verbatim text passage (approximately 64, 128, or 256 tokens) from a book or arXiv paper, generates three paraphrased variants via Claude 2.0, and constructs all 24 permutations of a 4-option multiple-choice question. The target LLM is queried on all 24 permutations per passage, yielding 720 binary responses per book (30 passages × 24 permutations). The output is a per-book accuracy score compared against a baseline computed from books published after the model's training cutoff; for models with logit access, label probabilities are additionally calibrated before argmax selection.

## Method

DE-COP frames training-data membership detection as a multiple-choice question-answering task: the model must identify the verbatim passage among three AI-generated paraphrases. The core premise is that a model trained on a given text will select the verbatim option significantly more often than chance, whereas for unseen text performance remains near random. To mitigate selection bias (models prefer certain answer positions), all 24 permutations of the 4 options are evaluated and averaged. A baseline is established by running the same procedure on books published after the model's knowledge cutoff, and a suspect book is flagged if its accuracy significantly exceeds this baseline. For models with logit access, a calibration step computes per-label probability adjustments from a held-out set of 30 clean books and subtracts them before selecting the most probable answer, further reducing position bias.

## Results

On BookTection-128 with logits-accessible models, DE-COP achieves an average AUC of 0.921, surpassing the best baseline (Lowercase, 0.900) and the prior best method Min-K%-Prob (0.840) by 9.6 percentage points. On fully black-box models (ChatGPT, Claude 2.1), DE-COP reaches 72.7% average accuracy on suspect books, compared to only 4.7% for prefix probing (32/50-token) and 34.9% for the Name Cloze task. On the arXivTection proof-of-concept dataset, DE-COP achieves AUC scores of 0.908 (Claude 2.1), 0.726 (LLaMA-2 70B), and 0.736 (Mixtral 8x7B), all with p-values below 10⁻⁶.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DE-COP outperforms all four baseline families across every model tested. Against the reference-free methods from Carlini et al. (2020) — Perplexity (avg. AUC 0.820), Zlib (0.689), and Lowercase (0.900) — DE-COP (0.921) wins on every model except GPT-3, where the experiment was incomplete due to API costs. Against Min-K%-Prob (Shi et al., 2023), the strongest logits-based baseline at 0.840 average AUC, DE-COP achieves a 9.6-point improvement, with the gap widening on larger models (e.g., 0.972 vs. 0.895 on LLaMA-2 70B). For fully black-box models, DE-COP's 72.7% average accuracy dramatically exceeds prefix probing from Liu et al. (2023) at 4.7% (32-token) and 2.2% (50-token), and also surpasses the modified Name Cloze task from Chang et al. (2023) at 34.9%, more than doubling its detection rate. The only setting where DE-COP does not clearly win is GPT-3 in Table 2 (0.863 vs. Lowercase's 0.957), though the authors note this is an incomplete evaluation and likely under-represents DE-COP's true performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

DE-COP repurposes the counterfactual memorization framework (Feldman 2021; Zhang et al. 2023a) into a practical black-box detection protocol by operationalizing it as a multiple-choice question-answering task over verbatim and paraphrased passages. It departs from logit-based methods like Min-K%-Prob (Shi et al. 2023) and perplexity-based baselines (Carlini et al. 2020) by requiring no token probability access, and from prompting-based extraction methods (Karamolegkou et al. 2023; Liu et al. 2023) by avoiding the fragility of exact-completion prompts that increasingly trigger refusal in chat-aligned models. A concurrent work (Golchin & Surdeanu 2024b) also frames detection as MCQA, but DE-COP adds a logit-calibration debiasing step and a permutation-averaging strategy to mitigate selection bias, and evaluates on a substantially larger and more diverse benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 72% average accuracy on fully black-box models (vs. ~4% for prior methods) makes copyright detection practically feasible for production LLMs where logit access is unavailable, directly relevant to ongoing litigation (NYT v. OpenAI) and regulatory pressure. Key limitations temper the findings: the 'suspect' book group consists of bestsellers whose widespread web presence may inflate memorization signals beyond direct training inclusion, and the method cannot distinguish between a book being in the training corpus versus being indirectly seen through forums, blogs, or Project Gutenberg. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-English content, whether the paraphrase-generation model's identity (the 7% AUC drop when Claude evaluates ChatGPT-generated paraphrases) can be controlled in adversarial settings, and whether the method's time cost (24 permutations per passage) can be reduced without sacrificing the selection-bias mitigation.
