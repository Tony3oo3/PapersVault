## Problem

Existing adaptive federated optimization methods (FedAdam, FedAMS) require synchronous aggregation, forcing the server to wait for the slowest client before each global update, which severely limits practical deployment in heterogeneous systems with stragglers. While asynchronous FL strategies like FedBuff and FedAsync address this for SGD-based methods, the impact of asynchronous delays on nonlinear adaptive gradient operations (e.g., the second-moment tracking in Adam/AMSGrad) remains unexplored, leaving no provable convergence guarantees for adaptive FL under asynchrony.

## Input / Output

The method consumes local client data (partitioned via Dirichlet distribution with heterogeneity parameter α), an initial global model, and asynchronous client updates arriving at variable delays. A concurrency-buffer framework controls the pipeline: M_c clients train locally in parallel, and the server accumulates M updates before triggering a single global adaptive step. The output is the updated global model parameter vector, with the adaptive optimizer state (momentum m_t, second-moment v_t, and running max v̂_t) maintained across rounds on the server.

## Method

FADAS replaces the synchronous aggregation step in adaptive FL with a buffered asynchronous scheme: clients perform K local SGD steps at their own pace, and the server accumulates M asynchronous updates Δ_t before applying an AMSGrad-style adaptive update (tracking momentum m_t, second-moment v_t, and running maximum v̂_t) to the global model. The key novelty is a delay-adaptive learning rate that tracks per-client gradient delays via timestamps and, when the maximum delay τ_t^max in a round exceeds a threshold τ_c, scales the learning rate down to min{η, 1/τ_t^max}, preventing stale updates from destabilizing convergence. Theoretically, this reduces the convergence rate's dependence from the worst-case delay τ_max to the median delay τ_median, yielding O(1/√(TM)) convergence with only O(τ_avg/T) delay overhead rather than O(τ_max·τ_avg/T). The approach is validated on ResNet-18/CIFAR-10/100 and BERT fine-tuning (with LoRA) on GLUE tasks, showing 8× wall-clock speedup over FedAvg and superior accuracy over FedBuff and FedAsync under large worst-case delays.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with large worst-case delay (τ_max=127) and Dir(0.1) heterogeneity, delay-adaptive FADAS achieves 73.96% test accuracy, substantially outperforming FedAsync (50.92%) and FedBuff (38.68%). On CIFAR-100 under the same setting, FADAS_da reaches 57.18% (Dir(0.3)) versus 38.55% for FedAsync and 18.63% for FedBuff. On GLUE language tasks (mild delay), FADAS achieves 64.26% on RTE, 83.33% on MRPC, and 90.76% on SST-2, consistently surpassing both asynchronous baselines. In wall-clock time, FADAS achieves an 8× speedup over FedAvg and >2.5× speedup over FedAMS on CIFAR-10 to reach 75% accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FADAS and its delay-adaptive variant consistently outperform both asynchronous baselines (FedAsync and FedBuff) across all five datasets. The advantage is most dramatic under large worst-case delays: on CIFAR-100 with Dir(0.3), FADAS_da achieves 57.18% versus 38.55% (FedAsync) and 18.63% (FedBuff), a gap of ~19 and ~39 percentage points respectively. On GLUE RTE, FADAS_da reaches 65.10% versus 49.46% (FedAsync) and 61.61% (FedBuff); on MRPC, FADAS achieves 83.33% versus 69.71% (FedAsync) and 76.80% (FedBuff). Against the synchronous baselines, FADAS does not directly compare accuracy but demonstrates significant wall-clock efficiency: it reaches 75% CIFAR-10 accuracy in 228 time units versus 2257.7 for FedAvg (8× speedup) and 648.7 for FedAMS (2.8× speedup). FedAsync shows severe accuracy fluctuation (±5–7 std) under large delays, while FADAS maintains much lower variance (±0.5–1.4 std), confirming the stability benefit of the adaptive optimizer combined with buffered aggregation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FADAS sits at the intersection of two established but previously separate research threads in federated learning: adaptive global optimization (FedAdam, FedAMS) and asynchronous buffered aggregation (FedBuff, FedAsync). It inherits FedBuff's concurrency-and-buffer architecture for handling stragglers and FedAMS's AMSGrad-based pseudo-gradient update for the global step, but is the first to combine them with a convergence analysis that explicitly accounts for asynchronous delay in the adaptive optimizer's second-moment tracking. The delay-adaptive learning rate mechanism draws a conceptual parallel to FedAsync's hinge-based weighting but applies it to the adaptive step size rather than the model interpolation coefficient, and is the first such mechanism analyzed within an adaptive FL convergence proof.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As adaptive optimizers become the default for training large models (BERT, Llama, ViT), the inability of synchronous adaptive FL to tolerate stragglers is a practical bottleneck that this paper directly addresses. The theoretical result that delay-adaptive learning rates replace the worst-case delay τ_max with the median delay τ_median in the convergence bound is a meaningful relaxation for heterogeneous deployments. Open questions include whether the bounded-delay and uniform-arrival assumptions hold in truly unbounded real-world settings, whether the approach extends to more complex adaptive schedules (e.g., per-parameter or per-client adaptive rates), and how the method behaves under communication compression or differential privacy constraints that are common in production FL systems.
