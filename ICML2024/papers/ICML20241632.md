## Problem

Conventional e-commerce models are task-specific and fail to generalize to new users and products (cold start), while existing LLM-based e-commerce efforts rely on synthetic data or cover only narrow task sets. This paper addresses the gap by constructing the first open-sourced, large-scale, high-quality instruction dataset for real-world e-commerce tasks and using it to develop generalist e-commerce LLMs that outperform both GPT-4 and state-of-the-art task-specific models.

## Input / Output

Each training and test sample pairs an instruction (one of 6 diverse phrasings per task, with 1 held out as unseen) and a task-specific input (product metadata, user query, review text, or interaction history) with a structured output such as an attribute value with its source location, a multiple-choice label, a ranked product list, or a free-form generated answer. For sequential recommendation, the input is a chronological list of up to 50 product title-category-brand strings and the output is a single letter selecting the correct next product from 20 candidates. OOD evaluation holds out entire product categories (e.g., Tools, Cells) so that all test products are unseen during training.

## Method

The paper introduces ECInstruct, a 116,528-sample instruction dataset covering 10 real e-commerce tasks across 4 categories (product understanding, user understanding, query-product matching, product QA), with diverse instructions generated via GPT-4 from human-written seeds and rigorous multi-stage quality control. Six general-purpose LLMs (Flan-T5 XXL 11B, Llama-2 13B/7B-chat, Mistral-7B, Flan-T5 XL 3B, Phi-2 3B) are instruction-tuned on ECInstruct using LoRA (α=16, rank=16 on all projection layers and LM head) with a cosine scheduler, 5% warmup, learning rate 1e-4, batch size 128, and 3 epochs. A key design choice is training all 10 tasks jointly (generalist) rather than per-task, which yields comparable or better performance and enables cross-task knowledge transfer. The diverse-instruction design is validated by showing that models trained on 5 varied instructions generalize well to a 6th unseen instruction, and scaling experiments confirm that larger training sets (1K→92K) substantially improve performance, especially on OOD products.

## Results

In in-domain (IND) evaluation across 10 e-commerce tasks, eCeLLM models achieve an average improvement of 10.7% over the best baselines, with eCeLLM-M reaching 0.662 F1* on AVE, 0.542 HR@1 on SR, and eCeLLM-L reaching 0.648 Macro F1 on SA and 0.851 F1 on AP. In out-of-domain (OOD) evaluation on unseen products, eCeLLM models show a 9.3% average improvement over the best baselines, with eCeLLM-L achieving 0.273 HR@1 on SR (vs. 0.081 for the best SoTA task-specific model) and 0.629 Macro F1 on SA. eCeLLM-L also demonstrates strong generalizability to unseen instructions, with only a 1.4% performance drop on SA compared to seen instructions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against general-purpose LLMs, eCeLLM-L achieves a 39.6% average improvement over GPT-4 Turbo across all 10 tasks, with particularly large gaps on SR (0.526 vs. 0.387 HR@1) and AVE (0.582 vs. 0.495 F1*). eCeLLM also substantially outperforms Gemini Pro, Claude 2.1, Llama-2 13B-chat, and Mistral-7B Instruct on most tasks, confirming that domain-specific instruction tuning is critical. Against the existing e-commerce LLM EcomGPT, eCeLLM-L wins on all 10 tasks, with a 244.6% improvement on SA (0.648 vs. 0.188 Macro F1) and 53.5% on PM (0.995 vs. 0.648 F1), attributed to ECInstruct's use of real-world data versus EcomInstruct's synthetic tasks. Against SoTA task-specific models, eCeLLM wins on 7 of 10 IND tasks: eCeLLM-M beats SUOpenTag/AVEQA on AVE (0.662 vs. 0.546 F1*), eCeLLM-L beats RGCN/DeBERTaV3 on PRP (0.611 vs. 0.588 Macro F1), eCeLLM-L beats BERTweet/P5 on SA (0.648 vs. 0.573), and eCeLLM-M beats gSASRec/Recformer on SR (0.542 vs. 0.265 HR@1). On PM, MPC, and AG, eCeLLM ties or is within ~2% of the best baselines (e.g., 0.995 F1 matching DeBERTaV3 on PM; 0.841 F_BERT vs. 0.858 for GPT-4 Turbo on AG). In OOD evaluation, eCeLLM-L outperforms the best SoTA task-specific model on 5 of 6 tasks, with a 41.4% improvement on SR (0.273 vs. 0.081 HR@1) and 14.1% on SA (0.629 vs. 0.567 Macro F1), though it trails slightly on AVE (-10.5%) and AG (-2.2%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the well-established instruction-tuning paradigm (LoRA fine-tuning of general-purpose LLMs) to e-commerce, building on the broader instruction-tuning literature (FLAN, T0, Flan Collection) and domain-specific adaptations (Platypus for STEM, Mammoth for math). It departs from the two closest e-commerce LLM efforts—LLaMA-E (focused on authoring tasks) and EcomGPT (relying heavily on synthetic/manipulated tasks)—by constructing ECInstruct entirely from real-world e-commerce data across 10 diverse tasks, and by providing the first systematic IND and OOD evaluation protocol for e-commerce LLMs. The method itself (LoRA instruction tuning) is standard; the contribution lies in the dataset design, task coverage, and evaluation rigor rather than a novel training technique.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ECInstruct is the first open-sourced, large-scale, high-quality e-commerce instruction benchmark, filling a gap that limits reproducibility and comparison in this area. The demonstrated OOD generalizability to unseen products (9.3% average improvement over best baselines) directly addresses the cold-start problem, a long-standing practical pain point in e-commerce platforms. Limitations include the 10K-per-task sample cap driven by compute constraints, absence of user-level OOD evaluation (due to anonymous user identifiers), and English-only scope. Open questions include whether scaling ECInstruct (the authors note ~4.8M additional AVE samples are available) further improves OOD performance, and whether the generalist multi-task framing extends to newer e-commerce tasks like explanation generation.
