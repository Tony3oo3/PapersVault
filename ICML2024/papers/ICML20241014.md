## Problem

Existing privacy analyses for private selection mechanisms (Report Noisy Max, PrivateTuning) rely on Rényi DP accounting, which is inherently lossy when (ε,δ)-DP guarantees are ultimately required. No prior work provided direct (ε,δ)-privacy profile bounds that use the point-wise privacy profiles of the base mechanisms as building blocks, leaving a gap between the tightness of modern numerical privacy accounting and the available selection-theoretic guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241014/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only point-wise information about the (ε,δ)-privacy profile δ(ε) of the base mechanism (e.g., from a numerical accountant for the subsampled Gaussian mechanism), along with the expected number of candidates m and the distribution parameters for the random stopping variable K (truncated negative binomial parameters η, γ or binomial parameters n, p). It outputs a closed-form (ε,δ)-DP bound for the end-to-end selection algorithm, with the ε-value expressible as a function of m, δ, and the base mechanism's privacy profile. A key practical detail is that the bound can be further optimized over the choice of intermediate threshold ε₁ in the privacy profile, and when tuning hyperparameters that affect privacy (e.g., noise scale σ), the training duration T can be jointly adjusted to stay within the derived thresholds.

## Method

The core result (Theorem 4.1) bounds the f-divergence between the output distributions of a private selection algorithm on neighboring datasets by a sum over quality scores involving the PGF derivative of the random number of evaluations K, generalizing the RDP analysis of Papernot & Steinke (2022) to arbitrary convex f. Specializing to the hockey-stick divergence f(z)=[z−e^ε]_+ yields direct (ε,δ)-DP bounds that are simpler than the RDP route (no optimization over RDP orders) and tighter in all regimes of interest. The paper then derives distribution-specific bounds: for K ~ truncated negative binomial (Theorem 5.2), the bound is m·δ(ε̂) where ε̂ = ε − (η+1)log(e^{ε₁} + (1−γ)/γ · δ(ε₁)); for K ~ Bin(n,p) (Theorem 5.7), the bound generalizes the Poisson case and allows a more concentrated K at the same privacy cost. The binomial result subsumes the Poisson case as p→0 and recovers the Liu & Talwar 3ε-bound as a special case when η=1 and the base mechanism is pure ε-DP.

## Results

The paper's headline quantitative result is that the hockey-stick divergence bound (Thm. 5.2) allows evaluating approximately 3× as many candidate models as the RDP bound (Thm. 5.6) for a Poisson subsampled Gaussian mechanism with q=16384/50000, σ=21.1, T=250 (Fig. 7). For the Report Noisy Max mechanism with m candidates and Gaussian noise σ=4, the hockey-stick bound (Thm. 3.2) yields tighter (ε,δ)-curves than the RDP-based bound (Thm. 3.1) across all δ values tested (Fig. 1). In the linear regression experiment on UCI datasets, tuning OPS-PTR via the private selection algorithm with the paper's Thm. 5.2 outperforms all non-adaptive baselines in the privacy-utility trade-off (Fig. 5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Papernot & Steinke (2022) RDP bounds, the paper's hockey-stick bounds are strictly tighter across all regimes of interest: Fig. 7 shows ~3× more models evaluable at the same (ε,δ) budget, and Figs. 2–4 confirm the advantage for both geometric and binomial/Poisson distributions of K. Against Liu & Talwar (2019), the paper recovers their 3ε pure-DP bound as a special case (Cor. 5.3) but provides substantially tighter (ε,δ)-DP bounds; in the linear regression experiment (Fig. 5), the paper's Thm. 5.2 yields a much better privacy-utility trade-off than Liu & Talwar's Thm. 3.5. Against Chaudhuri et al. (2011) output perturbation and Wang (2018) OPS-Balanced, the paper's Gen-PTR + private selection approach dominates both non-adaptive baselines on the UCI linear regression tasks (Fig. 5). Against Zhu & Wang (2022) RDP bound for RNM, the paper's Thm. 3.2 gives tighter (ε,δ)-curves (Fig. 1), with the ε-growth rate O((1/σ)√(log(m/δ))) preserved.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the private selection line of work initiated by Liu & Talwar (2019) and advanced by Papernot & Steinke (2022), who provided Rényi DP (RDP) bounds for black-box tuning algorithms with geometric, truncated negative binomial, and Poisson stopping distributions. The key departure is replacing the RDP-based accounting—which is known to be lossy when converting to (ε,δ)-DP—with direct hockey-stick divergence (privacy profile) bounds that use only point-wise (ε,δ) information about the base mechanism. The general f-divergence bound (Thm 4.1) subsumes the RDP results as a special case, and the introduction of the binomial distribution for the number of evaluations K is a new practical option that strictly generalizes the Poisson case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for DP practitioners: in DP-SGD hyperparameter tuning, the tighter bounds allow evaluating approximately 3× as many candidate models at the same privacy budget, and the binomial distribution yields more concentrated K (fewer wasted evaluations) for free. The approach also enables tighter analysis of Generalized PTR for unbounded-sensitivity queries like linear regression, where RDP-based methods are inapplicable. Open questions include whether the bounds can be further tightened for randomized candidate counts, how to handle data-adaptive selection (e.g., Bayesian optimization), and finding tight worst-case bounds for unbounded queries under additive noise.
