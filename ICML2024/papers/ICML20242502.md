## Problem

Despite extensive algorithmic efforts, OOD generalization with neural networks remains poorly understood: existing explanations attribute failure to spurious correlations, and theoretical analyses rely on linear or unstructured models that ignore the inductive biases of SGD-trained networks. This paper identifies a fundamentally different and previously unexplored failure mode—feature contamination—where neural networks learn uncorrelated background features alongside predictive core features, causing OOD generalization failure even when background features have zero correlation with the label.

## Input / Output

In the theoretical model, inputs are feature vectors x ∈ R^d generated as linear combinations of d_0 orthogonal unit-norm feature vectors (core features scaled by label y and background features scaled by label-independent weights), and the model outputs a real-valued score for binary classification in {−1, 1}. In the empirical setting, RGB images from ImageNet-based, WILDS, and DomainNet benchmarks are encoded by CLIP-based encoders (pre-trained or distilled) into fixed-dimensional representations, on which linear probes are trained for ID and OOD evaluation. The theoretical analysis tracks the OOD risk defined as the worst-case expected hinge loss over a family of test distributions where background feature weights shift from [0,1] to [−1,0].

## Method

The paper combines an empirical distillation study with a theoretical analysis of two-layer ReLU networks trained by SGD with hinge loss and weight decay. Empirically, a student encoder is trained to match CLIP teacher representations via Euclidean distance minimization, then evaluated with linear probes, showing that even explicit access to good representations is insufficient for OOD generalization. Theoretically, the key mechanism is activation asymmetry: ReLU neurons develop class-conditional activation probabilities that are near 1 for one class and near 0 for the other, which makes the expected gradient projection onto every background feature non-zero regardless of its label correlation. This causes neurons to accumulate both core and uncorrelated background features in their weight vectors (feature contamination), coupling them in the pre-activation so that OOD shifts in background features suppress neuron activation and degrade predictions. The paper proves that linear networks are provably free from this effect, establishing a separation between linear and non-linear models, and connects the theory to deep network behavior via activation asymmetry histograms, neuron selectivity metrics, and Grad-CAM visualizations.

## Results

The paper's main empirical finding (Figure 1) shows that linear probes on distilled representations (student networks trained to match CLIP teacher representations) achieve similar ID accuracy to pre-trained CLIP models but still lag significantly in OOD performance. Specifically, distilled models close only about half of the average effective robustness gap between standard models and pre-trained CLIP models on ImageNet-based OOD benchmarks, with even worse performance on iWildCam and DomainNet. Theoretically, the paper proves that a two-layer ReLU network trained by SGD achieves ID risk o(1) while OOD risk remains Θ̃(1) (Theorem 4.3), and that this failure is caused by feature contamination — neurons accumulate both core and uncorrelated background features (Theorem 4.2). Linear networks are provably free from this effect (Theorem 4.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three model families across six pre-trained models and eight OOD benchmarks: (i) linear probes on pre-trained CLIP representations (teacher), (ii) linear probes on distilled representations (student trained via representation distillation), and (iii) standard models trained from scratch on ID data. The distilled student models consistently outperform standard models in effective robustness (OOD advantage over standard baselines), confirming that access to good representations helps. However, distilled models still fall far short of pre-trained CLIP models in OOD performance — closing only ~50% of the robustness gap on ImageNet-based datasets and performing even worse on iWildCam and DomainNet. This gap cannot be explained by spurious correlations (since both teacher and student representations would be similarly affected) or data leakage (ruled out by Mayilvahanan et al., 2024). The paper attributes the residual gap to feature contamination: the student network's non-linear optimization dynamics cause it to couple core and background features in neuron pre-activations, a failure mode absent in the pre-trained teacher due to its different training trajectory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the prevailing OOD generalization narrative that attributes failure to spurious correlations (Arjovsky et al., 2019; Nagarajan et al., 2021) by identifying a fundamentally different mechanism—feature contamination—where non-linear networks learn uncorrelated background features alongside predictive core features. Theoretically, it extends the line of work analyzing two-layer ReLU networks under SGD (Allen-Zhu & Li, 2021; Karp et al., 2021) to an OOD setting, filling a gap where prior analyses either restricted to linear models or ignored optimization dynamics. Empirically, it builds on representation distillation (Hinton et al., 2014; Tian et al., 2020) and CLIP pre-training (Radford et al., 2021) to demonstrate that even explicit access to generalizable representations is insufficient for a student network to generalize OOD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes the OOD generalization problem by showing that the inductive biases of non-linear optimization (specifically, activation asymmetry in ReLU networks) create a failure mode orthogonal to spurious correlations, implying that existing algorithmic approaches targeting spurious correlations may be addressing the wrong problem. The conjecture that pre-training linearizes uncorrelated features—thereby mitigating feature contamination—is a compelling but unproven hypothesis that, if confirmed, would explain why diverse pre-training is the most effective OOD strategy to date. Key limitations include the restriction to binary classification, two-layer networks, and a specific structured data model; extensions to deeper architectures, multi-class settings, and rigorous analysis of pre-training dynamics remain open.
