## Problem

Large pre-trained diffusion models capture rich structure over a vast probability space, but real-world tasks (user customization, medical imaging, domain-specific generation) target a much smaller sub-distribution with far fewer samples. Existing fine-tuning approaches like ControlNet and T2I-Adapter lack a principled probabilistic formulation for how additional conditions should modify the denoising trajectory, and they do not systematically differentiate the strength of intervention across hierarchical feature levels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240801/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

BPS takes a frozen Stable Diffusion V1.5 backbone and accepts task-specific conditions as 512×512 image-type inputs (sketch/edge maps, layout bounding boxes encoded as 512×512×2 with object location in channel 1 and overlap count in channel 2, segmentation maps, or concatenated line-drawing + style-reference images) alongside a text prompt. The text prompt is sampled stochastically from three levels (generic quality descriptor, object list, or BLIP-generated detailed caption) with equal probability to reduce prompt dependence. The output is a 512×512 RGB image sampled via DDIM in 50 steps.

## Method

The paper derives a Bayesian decomposition via Tweedie's formula and Bayes' theorem showing that the posterior denoise function equals the pretrained (prior) denoise function plus a time-dependent 'steering gear' M that encodes the gradient of the log-likelihood of the additional condition. BPS realizes M in feature space with a head-heavy, foot-light U-Net consisting of a contracting path (8 residual blocks with scale-shift-norm for temporal conditioning), a transition path (2 residual blocks), and an expansive path (12 lightweight residual blocks), producing 21 condition features added to the 21 functional units of the frozen SD UNet. Differentiated weights (w_i = scale/4 for encoder stages, w_1 for the middle block, w_i = 1 for decoder stages) ensure earlier-stage features exert stronger directional influence on the denoising trajectory. Zero-convolution layers initialize all residual outputs to zero for stable training, and only the BPS parameters φ are optimized while the backbone θ̂ remains frozen, using the standard ε-prediction loss.

## Results

On the sketch-to-image task (COCO17), BPS achieves an FID of 10.49 at 10 epochs and 10.04 at 15 epochs, substantially outperforming T2I-Adapter (18.39), ControlNet (19.41), and the original SD (20.59). On segmentation-to-image (ADE20K), BPS attains an mIoU of 0.366, exceeding the ControlNet-reported score of 0.35. In user preference studies, BPS scores 2.38/3 for result quality and 2.52/3 for condition fidelity, compared to 1.87/1.95 (T2I-Adapter) and 1.77/1.53 (ControlNet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BPS decisively outperforms all three baselines on FID for sketch-to-image: 10.49 vs. T2I-Adapter's 18.39 (−43%), ControlNet's 19.41 (−46%), and SD's 20.59 (−49%). On CLIP Score, BPS (0.2614) is marginally below SD (0.2647) and T2I-Adapter (0.2642) but well above ControlNet (0.2361), a gap the authors attribute to their stochastic multilevel textual control. For segmentation-to-image on ADE20K, BPS achieves 0.366 mIoU, surpassing ControlNet's reported 0.35. In the 20-user preference study, BPS is ranked highest on both result quality (2.38 vs. 1.87 and 1.77) and condition fidelity (2.52 vs. 1.95 and 1.53), confirming that the FID advantage translates to perceptual quality. BPS also demonstrates superior robustness to textual errors, maintaining generation quality where SD, ControlNet, and T2I-Adapter degrade noticeably.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the conditional-control paradigm established by ControlNet (Zhang et al., 2023) and T2I-Adapter (Mou et al., 2023), both of which attach learnable modules to a frozen Stable Diffusion backbone to inject task-specific conditions. The key departure is a formal Bayesian reframing: using Tweedie's formula and Bayes' theorem, the authors decompose the posterior denoise function into the pretrained prior score plus a time-dependent 'steering gear' term, providing a probabilistic interpretation of what these modules actually compute. Architecturally, BPS adopts the EMD integration mode (encoder + middle + decoder) rather than ControlNet's ME-D or T2I-Adapter's EM, and introduces a head-heavy/foot-light weighting scheme that differentiates intervention strength across U-Net levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Bayesian formulation gives practitioners a principled lens for understanding and designing conditional-control modules, moving beyond the empirical 'zero-conv + residual block' recipe. The substantial FID gap (10.49 vs. 19.41 for ControlNet on sketch-to-image) and strong data-efficiency results (usable generation from as few as 40 samples) make BPS a practically attractive alternative for low-data customization scenarios. Open questions the authors flag include how to handle multiple heterogeneous conditional inputs with per-condition intervention control, and whether the fixed weight schedule can be made adaptive for extended-domain settings.
