## Problem

Prior object-centric relational abstraction methods (e.g., OCRA) achieved strong systematic generalization on multi-object visual reasoning but were limited to single-rule problems and suffered O(N⁴) computational complexity, preventing scaling to scenes with many objects. Meanwhile, Abstractors—a Transformer extension implementing a relational bottleneck via relational cross-attention—offered O(N²) complexity and multi-head relational modeling but had not been applied to multi-object visual inputs. This paper bridges both gaps by combining slot-based object discovery with Abstractor layers into a single scalable architecture.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images at task-specific resolutions (128×128 for ART/SVRT/CLEVR-ART, 80×80 per panel for PGM, 224×224 for V-PROM), where matrix-based tasks (PGM, V-PROM) present a 3×3 grid of panels and each candidate answer is inserted into the missing cell before processing. Slot attention extracts K object-centric embeddings per panel (up to 16 slots per panel, yielding up to 144 objects for PGM or 90 for V-PROM). Output is a per-choice score vector for multi-choice tasks (softmax + cross-entropy) or a single sigmoid score for binary same/different tasks.

## Method

Slot Abstractors combine two components: (1) Slot Attention, pre-trained via a reconstruction autoencoder to extract unsupervised object-centric representations, and (2) a stack of L Abstractor layers implementing multi-head relational cross-attention (RCA). The key architectural insight is a factorized slot representation: position-specific embeddings initialize the relation vectors s⁰, while feature-specific embeddings generate queries and keys in RCA, so that values (and thus downstream processing) depend only on pairwise inner products of features—a relational bottleneck that disentangles relations from object identity. Each Abstractor layer interleaves RCA, self-attention, and feedforward sub-layers with residual connections, enabling modeling of higher-order relations while retaining O(K²) complexity. For real-world images (V-PROM), slot attention is applied on top of frozen DINO ViT features (DINOSAUR), and the slot parameters are additionally fine-tuned during reasoning-task training.

## Results

On the ART dataset, the Slot Abstractor achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on three of four tasks (96.36% same/different, 91.64% relational-match-to-sample, 95.22% distribution-of-3), beating the previous best (OCRA) by up to 9%. On CLEVR-ART, it reaches 96.34% (RMTS) and 91.61% (identity rules), surpassing OCRA by up to 14%. On PGM, it achieves up to 21% improvement over PredRNet on OOD regimes (e.g., 39.3% extrapolation vs. 19.7%), and on V-PROM it reaches 67.7% test accuracy versus 51.2% for the previous best model (Relation Net).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Slot Abstractor decisively outperforms all slot-based relational baselines (Slot-RN, Slot-IN, Slot-CorelNet, Slot-ESBN, Slot-GAMR) across ART, SVRT, and CLEVR-ART, with margins of 10–70% depending on the task. Against the previous state-of-the-art OCRA, it wins on ART (by up to 9%) and CLEVR-ART (by up to 14%), is comparable on SVRT (marginal gains on same/different, slight loss on spatial relations to GAMR), and critically could not be trained on PGM at all due to its O(N⁴) complexity. On PGM, the Slot Abstractor beats PredRNet by up to 21% on OOD regimes (extrapolation: 39.3% vs. 19.7%; interpolation: 91.6% vs. 70.5%) and ARII by up to 14% (H.O.T.P.: 78.3% vs. 64.1%), though STSN retains an edge on the in-distribution neutral regime (98.2% vs. 91.5%). However, with only 20% of training data, the Slot Abstractor (71.25%) substantially outperforms STSN (53.35%), demonstrating superior sample efficiency. On V-PROM with real-world images, it achieves 67.7% versus 51.2% for Relation Net, a 16.5-point improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously separate lines of work: Slot Attention (Locatello et al., 2020) for unsupervised object-centric representation learning, and Abstractors (Altabaa et al., 2023), a Transformer extension implementing the relational bottleneck via relational cross-attention. The direct predecessor, OCRA (Webb et al., 2023b), already paired slot attention with a relational bottleneck but suffered from O(N⁴) complexity and single-relation limitations, while Abstractors had the desired O(N²) complexity and multi-head architecture but had never been applied to multi-object visual inputs. The novel contribution is the specific architectural integration—factorized feature/position slot representations and a particular interleaving of relational cross-attention with self-attention and feedforward layers—validated through targeted ablations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Slot Abstractor's quadratic complexity enables it to process 144-object PGM problems where the previous state-of-the-art (OCRA) literally could not be trained due to memory constraints, and it achieves large margins (up to 21%) on out-of-distribution PGM regimes. Extending systematic generalization to real-world images (V-PROM, 67.7% vs. 51.2% prior best) broadens the applicability of relational inductive biases beyond synthetic benchmarks. Key open questions remain: performance on some OOD regimes (e.g., extrapolation) is still well below human levels, the fixed slot count limits flexibility for variable object counts, and the quadratic complexity, while improved, could be further reduced with efficient attention mechanisms.
