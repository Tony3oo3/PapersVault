## Problem

Property-guided diffusion models for molecular and protein design rely on guidance models trained on scarce, biased labeled data. When the denoising process explores out-of-distribution regions of chemical or sequence space, these guidance models produce overconfident and incorrect gradient signals that steer generation toward false-positive regions. Existing approaches focus on modifying the diffusion sampling process itself rather than improving the guidance model's behavior under distribution shift.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained diffusion model (graph-structured, E(3)-equivariant, or categorical), a small labeled dataset with property values, a larger unlabeled context set of molecules, and a target property y. It produces novel molecules, protein sequences, or polycyclic aromatic systems with improved target properties. The context set is consumed only during guidance model training via a forward pass through the embedding trunk with fixed random parameters; at sampling time, the method is a drop-in replacement for the standard guidance function with zero additional computational overhead.

## Method

CGD introduces a data- and timestep-dependent Mahalanobis distance regularizer added to the standard supervised guidance model loss. The regularizer constructs a covariance matrix K_t from inner products of model embeddings of unlabeled context points (using randomly initialized embedding parameters), and specifies a mean function m_t that reverts predictions to the training label mean with high target variance on OOD inputs. This encourages smooth gradients between similar context points and high predictive uncertainty in regions distant from labeled data. The timestep dependence is critical: the diagonal offset τ_t increases with noise level to penalize overconfidence as inputs approach the reference distribution, while the covariance scale σ_t decreases to relax smoothness constraints where the in/out-of-distribution distinction becomes less meaningful. The approach is architecture-agnostic, requires no changes to the diffusion model, and is compatible with latent, equivariant, Riemannian, and constrained diffusion processes.

## Results

Across five protein targets (PARP1, FA7, 5HT1B, BRAF, JAK2) in the small molecule generation task, CGD consistently achieves the highest normalized docking scores (top 5% of generated molecules, normalized so 1 = best validation set score) compared to all baselines, with the largest gains on targets where the training data is most biased. For equivariant diffusion on polycyclic aromatic systems, CGD produces significantly lower (better) composite electronic property objectives across all guidance scales (0, 2, 4) compared to weight decay and L2 regularization. For discrete protein sequence optimization, CGD Pareto fronts dominate those of standard guidance as samples enter the out-of-distribution regime, yielding better properties at every level of naturalness. The method adds no sampling-time overhead and requires only two extra forward passes per training iteration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGD outperforms the weight decay baseline from Lee et al. (2023) across all five protein targets in the small molecule task, with the gap widening as the OOD challenge increases. Deep ensembles (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017) and self-supervised denoising pre-training (Zaidi et al., 2023) perform best on in-distribution evaluation but fail to match CGD on the out-of-distribution high-property validation set, confirming that uncertainty estimation and pre-training alone are insufficient for reliable OOD generation. The three unsupervised domain adaptation methods—DeepCoral (Sun & Saenko, 2016), DANN (Ganin et al., 2016), and domain confusion (Tzeng et al., 2015)—all underperform CGD, as they align embeddings rather than directly shaping prediction behavior. For the materials task, CGD significantly beats the standard guidance from Weiss et al. (2023) across all guidance scales, and for protein sequences it dominates the Pareto front of Gruver et al. (2023) in the OOD regime. The paper notes that CGD is orthogonal to ensembling and pre-training and can be combined with them for further gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the function-space regularization framework of Rudner et al. (2023) from image classification to property-guided molecular generation, introducing two key adaptations: a factorized Mahalanobis regularizer suitable for multi-output regression (mean + variance) and an explicit timestep-dependence that accounts for the diffusion noising schedule. It is positioned as orthogonal to prior work on guided diffusion for molecular design (Lee et al. 2023, Weiss et al. 2023, Gruver et al. 2023), which primarily modified the unconditional sampling process or the diffusion SDE itself, whereas CGD instead regularizes the guidance model training to improve its OOD behavior. The method draws on the SDE-based diffusion framework of Song et al. (2020) and the classifier-guidance paradigm of Sohl-Dickstein et al. (2015), but departs from standard domain adaptation and semi-supervised techniques by directly constraining the guidance model's predictions on unlabeled context points rather than aligning embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies the guidance model—not the diffusion model—as the primary bottleneck for OOD generalization in property-guided molecular design, and shows that a simple, plug-and-play regularizer on the guidance model consistently outperforms more sophisticated alternatives (ensembles, domain adaptation, self-supervised pre-training) across three distinct diffusion architectures and application domains. Practical limitations include the need for abundant unlabeled context data, two additional hyperparameters (σ_t, τ_t), and domain expertise for context set selection. Open questions raised by the authors include encoding more complex OOD behavior (e.g., reverting to physics-based predictions rather than high uncertainty), active learning strategies for context set construction, and integration with multi-task or meta-learning frameworks.
