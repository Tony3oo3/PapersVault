## Problem

Existing LLM interpretability methods either require extensive labeled data for training (linear probes) and are limited to a small predefined set of concepts, or produce only short one-to-few-token predictions (LogitLens, TunedLens) that cannot explain complex, open-world concepts in detail. There is no training-free method that can generate rich natural language explanations of what information is encoded in arbitrary hidden embeddings across all layers of a transformer LLM.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240287/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a hidden embedding extracted from a specific layer ℓ* and token position i* in a transformer LLM's original forward pass, combined with a user-defined interpretation prompt containing a placeholder token (e.g., "[X] Please repeat the previous message"). It produces a free-form natural language description of the embedding's content, annotated with per-token relevancy scores computed as treatment effects (the probability difference of generating each token with versus without the embedding injection). For the control variants, the output is modified parameters for a single transformer layer, enabling targeted behavioral changes without full-model retraining.

## Method

SelfIE exploits the observation that LLMs can already summarize or repeat passages in context by repurposing this capability for embedding interpretation: during a separate 'interpretation forward pass,' the placeholder token embedding at a chosen layer k is replaced with the target hidden embedding, and standard autoregressive decoding generates a natural language explanation with no training or data collection required. The key architectural insight is that the residual structure of transformers creates a unified representation space across layers, allowing an embedding from layer ℓ* to be faithfully interpreted when injected at a different layer k (typically k < 20). For control, Supervised Control minimizes MSE between a SelfIE-identified target embedding and a single layer's output, requiring gradient computation on only that one layer. Reinforcement Control extends RLHF to the embedding level by converting SelfIE's text interpretations into non-differentiable reward signals from a human or LLM evaluator, enabling unsupervised erasure of harmful knowledge from a single layer with only a few gradient updates.

## Results

On the Counterfact fact-editing benchmark, SelfIE-based Supervised Control achieves the best harmonic mean of 59.77%, surpassing ROME (56.92%), FT (26.98%), and RepE (6.80%), with the highest paraphrase generalization (36.12%) and competitive efficacy (58.40%). On TextWorld, SelfIE's zero-shot interpretation matches the accuracy of a 100-shot supervised linear probe for eliciting implicit world states. Reinforcement Control reduces prompt injection's success rate in eliciting harmful responses by 84.66% (from 89.06% to 4.4%) while preserving 95.85% of the model's fact-answering capability. Supervised Control achieves a 95% effective rate in overriding user-specified ethical preferences in prompts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ROME, SelfIE-based Supervised Control wins on harmonic mean (59.77% vs. 56.92%), efficacy (58.40% vs. 50.95%), and paraphrase (36.12% vs. 33.29%), while ROME retains a slight edge on specificity (94.94% vs. 90.43%); SelfIE also requires only O(1) gradient computation versus ROME's O(L). Against FT, SelfIE dominates on harmonic mean (59.77% vs. 26.98%), paraphrase (36.12% vs. 12.40%), and specificity (90.43% vs. 54.39%), though FT achieves higher raw efficacy (82.12% vs. 58.40%) at the cost of poor generalization. Against RepE, SelfIE wins decisively on all metrics except specificity (90.43% vs. 98.01%), with a 8.8× improvement in harmonic mean (59.77% vs. 6.80%). Against MEND, the comparison is qualitative (Table 1): SelfIE requires O(1) gradient computation and supports open-ended concept editing, whereas MEND requires O(L) gradients and is limited to simple fact associations. On TextWorld, SelfIE's zero-shot interpretation matches the 100-shot linear probe of Li et al. (2021), demonstrating that no training data is needed to elicit implicit world-state knowledge from embeddings.

## Relation to Prior Work

SelfIE repurposes an LLM's existing ability to summarize or repeat a passage for a new purpose: interpreting its own hidden embeddings by injecting them into a separate forward pass. This departs from prior interpretability methods (linear probes, LogitLens, TunedLens) that require training data or produce only one-to-few-token explanations, and from model-editing methods (ROME, MEND, RepE) that operate at the output level or require O(L) gradient computation. The paper also extends RLHF from output tokens to hidden embeddings, enabling reward-based control without supervised targets, building on the RLHF framework of Ouyang et al. (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SelfIE provides a training-free, open-world tool for inspecting and controlling LLM internal reasoning, with practical safety implications demonstrated by an 84.66% reduction in prompt-injection success from editing a single layer. Key open questions include whether the unified-representation hypothesis (that embeddings from any layer can be faithfully interpreted at a fixed insertion layer) holds across architectures, whether the circularity of using the same model for both encoding and interpretation limits faithfulness guarantees, and whether the control methods generalize beyond LLaMA-2-70B. The finding that harmful knowledge persists in intermediate embeddings despite safety alignment, and that editing one concept generalizes across 388 diverse harmful behaviors, suggests concentrated rather than distributed storage of such knowledge.
