## Problem

Inner product decoders, ubiquitous in graph autoencoders and GNNs, are empirically known to have limited expressiveness for graph reconstruction, yet no prior work provided a theoretical explanation for this pervasive failure. This paper formalizes the limitation via sign rank, proving that real inner product decoders impose a uniform acute-angle connectivity constraint that provably depletes latent degrees of freedom, and establishes concrete lower bounds on the minimum latent dimension required for faithful graph reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unweighted undirected graph adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{N×N} and a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×d} (often the identity for featureless graphs) as input. The adjacency is treated not as strictly binary but as a sign pattern (positive entries = connected, non-positive = disconnected), placing it in an equivalence class of uncountable cardinality. Output is a reconstructed adjacency via either deterministic sign decoding sign(ZCZ^T) or probabilistic Bernoulli sampling B∘σ(ZCZ^T), along with the latent embedding Z ∈ R^{N×f} where f is the tunable latent dimension.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution shows that the real sign rank of a graph's adjacency provides a lower bound on latent dimension: an (N−1)-star graph requires at least ⌈(N+1)/2⌉ real dimensions but only 1 complex dimension, and planar grid graphs with more than 4 nodes cannot be rank 2. To overcome these limitations while staying within the inner product framework, the paper introduces learnable cutoffs—diagonal matrices C that transform the decoder from sign(ZZ^T) to sign(Z₁Z₁^T − Z₂Z₂^T), converting uniform planar classifiers into node-specific conic classifiers with individual connectivity angles θᵢ. This is equivalent to complex latent embeddings where Re(ZZ^T) = Z_rZ_r^T − Z_iZ_i^T naturally separates real and imaginary components. The resulting architectures (DGAE, CGAE, mGAE) are trained self-supervised with a BCE + Frobenius-norm loss, and empirically achieve faithful reconstruction of molecular graphs (QM-9, Zinc, TU-Enzymes, TU-Protein) in ≤10 latent dimensions where the vanilla GAE fails even at 80 dimensions.

## Results

On molecular benchmarks (QM-9, Zinc, TU-Enzymes, TU-Protein; 35,118 total graphs), the proposed DGAE achieves faithful graph reconstruction at h₂=10 latent dimensions, while the standard GAE mostly fails even at h₂=80. On Cora (2,708 nodes), the complex CVAE achieves the lowest reconstruction error of 22±5 (×10⁴) at h₂=640, compared to 337±32 for the vanilla VAE and 167±127 for the cutoff-augmented DVAE. On CiteSeer (3,327 nodes), the CGAE reaches 69±44 (×10⁴) error at h₂=640 versus 744±8 for the GAE baseline. On pedagogical grid and chain graphs, all cutoff-augmented architectures achieve perfect deterministic (sign) reconstruction for h₂≥8, while the GAE struggles significantly even at very high latent dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the Kipf & Welling (2016) Graph Autoencoder (GAE/VAE), which uses a standard inner-product decoder σ(ZZᵀ) without cutoffs or complexification. The proposed cutoff-augmented DGAE/DVAE consistently outperforms the GAE on molecular datasets: all 35,118 graphs across QM-9, Zinc, TU-Enzymes, and TU-Protein are faithfully embedded at h₂=10, whereas the GAE mostly fails up to h₂=80. On Cora and CiteSeer at high latent dimensions (h₂≥40), the complex CVAE/CGAE achieves substantially lower reconstruction error than both the vanilla VAE/GAE and the cutoff DVAE/DGAE (e.g., 22 vs. 337 vs. 167 ×10⁴ on Cora at h₂=640). At low latent dimensions (h₂≤10), the DVAE/DGAE and CVAE/CGAE show higher error than the VAE/GAE, suggesting the augmented architectures require more capacity to converge but ultimately reach much lower error ceilings. On grid and chain graph memorization tasks, the GAE produces entangled, erroneous reconstructions even at h₂=597, while all augmented architectures achieve perfect sign-decoded reconstruction at h₂=8.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Kipf & Welling's (2016) Graph Autoencoder (GAE), which popularized the inner product decoder for graph reconstruction, and on the broader GCN literature that has empirically noted but never theoretically explained the decoder's expressiveness limitations. The key conceptual departure is importing sign rank from combinatorics and complexity theory to formalize *why* the inner product decoder is fundamentally constrained, rather than merely observing that it underperforms. The proposed fix—learnable cutoffs that transform planar classifiers into conic classifiers, and complex latent embeddings—repurposes the algebraic structure of complex inner products (Re(ZZ^T) = Z_r Z_r^T − Z_i Z_i^T) as a practical decoder augmentation, connecting it to the eigendecomposition perspective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical explanation for a pervasive empirical failure mode in GNNs, giving practitioners a principled reason to suspect inner product decoders and a simple, low-cost modification (cutoffs) to mitigate the issue without abandoning the inner product framework. The sign-rank lower bounds (e.g., star graphs require Ω(N) real dimensions but only 1 complex dimension) provide a clean theoretical lens for understanding when and why low-dimensional embeddings fail. Open questions include whether the cutoff mechanism generalizes to inductive or semi-supervised settings, whether the theoretical rank bounds are tight for realistic graph distributions, and whether the gains observed on memorization tasks (grids, chains, small molecules) persist in more complex downstream tasks like link prediction or node classification at scale.
