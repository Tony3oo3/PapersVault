## Problem

Prior online learning frameworks for individual fairness (Gillen et al. 2018; Bechavod et al. 2020; Bechavod & Roth 2023) restrict auditing to a single auditor or simple majority voting, and their oracle-efficient algorithms require T optimization oracle calls per round with suboptimal regret/fairness bounds of O(T^{7/9}) and O(T^{41/45}). This paper asks whether a richer class of multi-auditor aggregation schemes can be handled while simultaneously improving both the convergence rates and the per-round computational cost.

## Input / Output

Each round the learner receives k feature vectors x̄^t ∈ X^k and (in the full-information setting) their labels ȳ^t ∈ {0,1}^k, or (in the partial-information setting) only labels for individuals receiving a positive prediction. A panel of m_t auditors, each with an implicit distance function d^j: X×X→[0,1], reports a k×k binary objection matrix, which is aggregated by a monotone function f^t into a single violation pair or Null. The learner must maintain a randomized policy π^t ∈ ΔH but only has sampling access to it (via an offline ERM oracle), so it must draw R samples to form an empirical approximation π̃^t for querying auditors. The output is a sequence of policies achieving O(T^{3/4}) (full info) or O(T^{5/6}) (partial info) bounds on both regret against fair-in-hindsight comparators and cumulative fairness violations.

## Method

The core contribution is a Lagrangian loss L^t(π,λ) = (1/k)·Error^t(π) + λ·Ūnfair^t_{π̃,α,β}(π) that is linear in π, enabling a reduction to Context-FTPL (or Context-Semi-Bandit-FTPL for one-sided feedback). A key technical distinction separates constraint elicitation from objective minimization: the learner approximates π^t with R = Õ(ε^{-2}) oracle samples to form π̃^t, queries auditors at a slightly tighter sensitivity α−ε/2, and uses the resulting violation to build the Lagrangian loss fed to the FTPL subroutine. The monotone aggregation framework is characterized by Lemma 2.9, which proves that for any monotone scheme and fixed pair of individuals, the aggregate decision is always equivalent to a single threshold auditor j^{i*}, making the fairness constraint effectively a single Lipschitz-type inequality per pair. The dual variable λ^t = T^{1/4} (or T^{1/6} in the partial-information case) is set to balance the accuracy and fairness terms, yielding the improved O(T^{3/4}) and O(T^{5/6}) simultaneous bounds while reducing per-round oracle calls from T to Õ(ε^{-2}) (or Õ(ε^{-2}+k^2 T^{1/3})).

## Results

In the full-information setting, the paper's oracle-efficient algorithm (Algorithm 2) achieves simultaneous bounds of O(s^{3/4} k^{7/4} T^{3/4} log^{1/2}|H|) for regret and O((1/ε) s^{3/4} k^{3/4} T^{3/4} log^{1/2}|H|) for fairness violations, while requiring only Õ(ε^{-2}) oracle calls per round. In the partial-information (one-sided label feedback) setting, Algorithm 4 achieves O(s^{3/4} k^{11/4} T^{5/6} log^{1/2}|H|) regret and O((1/ε) s^{3/4} k^{7/4} T^{5/6} log^{1/2}|H|) fairness violations with Õ(ε^{-2} + k^2 T^{1/3}) oracle calls per round. Both settings improve on the best previously known oracle-efficient bounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper improves on two prior oracle-efficient baselines. Against Bechavod et al. (2020) in the full-information setting, the new O(T^{3/4}) bound strictly dominates the previous O(T^{7/9}) bound (since 3/4 < 7/9), and the per-round oracle complexity drops from O(T) calls to Õ(ε^{-2}) calls — a dramatic reduction from linear to constant (in T) oracle queries. Against Bechavod & Roth (2023) in the partial-information setting, the new O(T^{5/6}) bound improves on the previous O(T^{41/45}) bound (since 5/6 ≈ 0.833 < 41/45 ≈ 0.911), again with oracle complexity reduced from O(T) to Õ(ε^{-2} + k^2 T^{1/3}). The key technical enabler is the Lagrangian formulation combined with a separation between constraint elicitation (using an approximate policy π̃) and objective minimization (using the implicit Context-FTPL policy), which avoids the need to approximate the full policy with T oracle calls each round. The monotone auditing framework also generalizes the single-auditor and majority-vote schemes of prior work, subsuming them as special cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on online learning with individual fairness initiated by Gillen et al. (2018) and developed by Bechavod et al. (2020) and Bechavod & Roth (2023), which progressively relaxed assumptions on the fairness metric and feedback structure. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of monotone aggregation functions, which generalize from single-auditor or majority-vote schemes to arbitrary monotone combinations of multiple auditors, with a clean characterization (Lemma 2.9) showing any such scheme reduces to a single threshold auditor. Methodologically, the paper replaces the 'fake datapoint' reduction of Bechavod et al. (2020) with a direct Lagrangian formulation, reducing the problem to Context-FTPL (Syrgkanis et al., 2016) and its semi-bandit variant, which yields both tighter convergence rates and substantially lower per-round oracle complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The oracle complexity reduction from T calls per round to Õ(ε^{-2}) is practically significant, making individual fairness constraints tractable for large hypothesis classes where previous algorithms were computationally prohibitive. The monotone aggregation framework provides a principled way to incorporate diverse, potentially conflicting human judgments without requiring auditors to articulate explicit metrics, which is important for real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the relaxed baseline (competing against (α−ε)-fair policies rather than α-fair ones) can be tightened, whether non-trivial lower bounds exist for this setting, and whether the separating-set requirement can be relaxed for broader hypothesis classes.
