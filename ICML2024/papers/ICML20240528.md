## Problem

Existing data-driven FWI methods either jointly train encoder-decoder networks requiring paired data for all components (InversionNet) or only partially decouple them while still needing paired data for the decoder and relying on fixed integral kernels with poor noise robustness (InvLINT). This paper asks whether both the encoder and decoder can be trained entirely from their own domains via self-supervised learning, with paired data needed only for a simple linear mapping between the two latent spaces.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D image-like representations: seismic data as 1000×70 arrays (receiver traces over time, log-normalized to [-1,1]) and velocity maps as 70×70 spatial grids. It produces velocity maps for the inverse problem and seismic data for the forward problem, both at the same fixed grid resolution. For the EM inversion variant, inputs are surface electromagnetic measurements and outputs are conductivity maps in the range [0, 0.65].

## Method

The Auto-Linear framework trains two independent Masked Autoencoders (MAEs) with transformer backbones—one on seismic data and one on velocity maps—using a 75% mask ratio, with no paired data required. After self-supervised pre-training, both encoders and decoders are frozen, and a low-rank linear converter (two linear layers with a 128-dimensional bottleneck) is trained on paired data to map between the frozen latent spaces. The key insight is the Auto-Linear Phenomenon: the independently learned latent representations from two physically related domains are automatically linearly correlated, so a simple linear mapping suffices to connect them. This single linear converter enables both inverse modeling (seismic encoder → velocity decoder) and forward modeling (velocity encoder → seismic decoder) simultaneously, and the same frozen encoder/decoder pair can be shared across datasets with different subsurface structures by retraining only the linear layer.

## Results

On the 11 OpenFWI 2D datasets, Auto-Linear achieves MAE values ranging from 0.0060 (Kimberlina-CO₂) to 0.1820 (CurveVel-B), with an average MAE of 0.0719 across all datasets. The method uses only 12.3M parameters (half of InversionNet's 24.4M) and requires supervised training of only a linear layer. On the forward process (Fault Family), Auto-Linear achieves MAE of 0.0099–0.0253, outperforming InversionNet by 2–3× (e.g., 0.0132 vs. 0.0373 on CurveFault-A). On the EM inversion task (Kimberlina-Reservoir), Auto-Linear achieves MAE 0.00438 and SSIM 0.9700, beating both baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against InversionNet (Wu & Lin, 2019; joint encoder–decoder training, 24.4M params), Auto-Linear (12.3M params) achieves comparable MAE on most datasets but wins outright on FlatVel-A (0.0081 vs. 0.0131), Style-B (0.0638 vs. 0.0689), and Kimberlina-CO₂ (0.0060 vs. 0.0061), while InversionNet retains a small edge on the harder B-variants (e.g., CurveVel-B: 0.1497 vs. 0.1820). Against InvLINT (Feng et al., 2022; kernel-based separate training), Auto-Linear dominates on every metric and every dataset, with the gap especially large on complex structures: MAE 0.0277 vs. 0.0853 on CurveFault-A and 0.0738 vs. 0.0981 on CurveVel-A. On the forward process, Auto-Linear outperforms InversionNet by 2–3× in MAE (e.g., 0.0132 vs. 0.0373 on CurveFault-A). On elastic FWI (E^FWI), Auto-Linear matches the supervised ElasticNet benchmark within ~0.004 MAE on Vp for FlatVel-A (0.0330 vs. 0.0308). In few-shot settings (1/10 or 1/20 paired data), Auto-Linear consistently beats InversionNet by 0.01–0.03 MAE across all five tested datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends InvLINT (Feng et al., ICML 2022), which demonstrated a linear mapping between fixed integral-transform embeddings of seismic data and velocity maps but still required paired data to train the decoder. The key departure is replacing hand-crafted kernel-based encoders with self-supervised Masked Autoencoders, fully decoupling both encoder and decoder training from paired data and restricting supervised learning to a single linear converter. The framework also positions itself against the dominant joint-training paradigm (InversionNet) and contrasts with generative-model-based inverse solvers (diffusion models), arguing that the linear-connector approach enables single-step inference and simultaneous forward/inverse modeling without requiring an explicit forward operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Auto-Linear phenomenon — that independently self-supervised autoencoders from two physically coupled domains automatically develop linearly correlated latent spaces — offers a principled simplification of subsurface inversion from a complex image-to-image translation to a rank-constrained linear problem. Practically, this yields strong few-shot performance, improved noise robustness, and the ability to share encoders/decoders across structurally diverse datasets while only retraining a small linear layer. Key limitations include the continued need for paired data (albeit minimal) for the linear converter, evaluation exclusively on simulated data due to the absence of open real-world seismic datasets, and a fixed-grid formulation that contrasts with continuous physics-driven methods. Open questions include whether the phenomenon generalizes to elastic (multi-parameter) FWI and whether the linear converter can be trained on purely unpaired data.
