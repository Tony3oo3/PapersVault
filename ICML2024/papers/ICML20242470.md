## Problem

POMDPs provide the correct framework for reinforcement learning under partial observability, but planning in them is PSPACE-complete and learning has exponential sample complexity in the horizon. Existing structured POMDP algorithms (exploiting L-decodability or γ-observability) reduce statistical complexity but still rely on intractable oracles—belief-space integration, infinite-dimensional operations, or nonlinear value function backups—making them impractical. The paper asks whether a representation-based perspective can yield both computationally tractable planning and provable sample efficiency for structured POMDPs.

## Input / Output

The agent receives 64×64×3 RGB images as raw observations, which are first compressed by a VAE into compact vector features; these features, together with the preceding L−1 action-observation pairs (L=3 in experiments), form the L-step history window x_h that serves as the effective state for representation learning. The output is a continuous action produced by a Soft Actor-Critic policy whose critic is parameterized by the learned latent variable representation. Unlike standard MDP RL that conditions on a single state, the method explicitly consumes a sliding window of observations and actions to capture the non-Markovian structure induced by partial observability.

## Method

The core contribution is the Multi-step Latent Variable Representation (μLV-Rep), which reveals that in an L-decodable POMDP the Q-function admits an exact linear representation Q_h^π(x_h, a_h) = ⟨p(·|x_h, a_h), w^π(·)⟩_{L₂(μ)}, where p is a learned latent variable distribution over a continuous latent z. The key mechanism is the moment matching policy ν_π, which conditions on a sufficient latent variable to generate the same expected observation dynamics while being independent of history older than L steps, thereby eliminating the overlap dependence between x_h and x_{h+L} that previously forced nonlinear representations. Training proceeds by maximizing the ELBO to learn p(z|x_h, a_h) and p(o_{h+1}|z), approximating the Q-function via random feature quadrature (Monte-Carlo sampling from p(z|x_h) with RKHS random features), and planning with SAC using an ellipsoid exploration bonus for optimism in the face of uncertainty. This replaces the intractable belief-space Bellman backup with a simple least-squares regression, yielding a PAC sample complexity of poly(C, H, |A|^L, L, ε, log(|M|/δ)) episodes.

## Results

On the Meta-world benchmark (50 robotic manipulation tasks with 64×64×3 visual observations), μLV-Rep achieves better than 90% success rate on 33 out of 50 tasks after 1M environment steps. It outperforms or matches (within 10%) the best of DreamerV2 and MWM on 41 out of 50 tasks. In terms of computational efficiency, μLV-Rep trains at 21.3 steps per second, nearly 2.6× faster than MWM (8.1 steps/sec), while using a simpler architecture (no ViT). The method uses L=3 history window and a VAE for image encoding, with SAC as the planner.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two model-based baselines: DreamerV2 (Hafner et al., 2021), which learns latent representations via a recurrent world model, and Masked World Model / MWM (Seo et al., 2023), which uses a ViT-based autoencoder for masked feature reconstruction. On the four representative Meta-world tasks shown in Figure 1, μLV-Rep demonstrates faster convergence and higher final success rate than both baselines. Across all 50 tasks (Figure 2), μLV-Rep surpasses or is within 10% of the best baseline on 41 tasks, with the remaining 9 tasks showing a gap. MWM achieves comparable sample efficiency on certain tasks but incurs significantly higher computational cost due to its ViT component (8.1 vs 21.3 steps/sec). DreamerV2 generally lags behind both μLV-Rep and MWM in final performance, particularly on tasks requiring longer-horizon planning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear MDP representation framework (Jin et al., 2020b; Yang & Wang, 2020) to the POMDP setting by showing that L-decodability induces a linear value function representation, a result that was previously blocked by the nonlinear overlap between consecutive observation windows. The key conceptual leap is repurposing the moment matching policy from Efroni et al. (2022)—previously used only as a proof technique—to enable tractable algorithm design via least-squares regression, bypassing explicit belief computation entirely. The variational learning and random feature quadrature machinery is adapted from LV-Rep (Ren et al., 2023b), and the ellipsoid exploration bonus follows Uehara et al. (2021). The paper explicitly contrasts with PSR (Littman & Sutton, 2001), noting that PSR's Bayes-rule update introduces a nonlinear operation that makes planning intractable, whereas the proposed representation avoids this.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm for structured POMDPs that simultaneously achieves (i) sub-exponential sample complexity, (ii) computationally tractable planning via simple least-squares optimization, and (iii) a practical exploration mechanism—closing a gap where prior theoretical results relied on infeasible oracles. The linear representation insight is broadly applicable: any future POMDP algorithm that can exploit L-decodability can potentially inherit this tractable planning structure. Limitations include the reliance on L-decodability (or γ-observability), evaluation only on Meta-world and MuJoCo without classic POMDP benchmarks like RockSample, and the finite candidate class assumption in the theory. Open questions include sensitivity to the decodability assumption in practice, scaling to very long horizons, and whether the linear structure persists under model misspecification.
