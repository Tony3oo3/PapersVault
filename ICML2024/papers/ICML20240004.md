## Problem

Existing one-shot subset selection methods rely on either diversity (feature-space coverage) or difficulty (label-side informativeness) alone, failing to represent the joint data distribution P(x,y) that both components jointly define. Moreover, the selection criterion is applied independently of the subset size, even though the optimal balance between representative and difficult samples shifts fundamentally as the budget changes. No principled mechanism exists to adaptively balance these two objectives given a specific subset size.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full training dataset (features extracted from the second-last layer of a ResNet, plus one-hot labels), a target subset size |S|, and an initial model trained for 10 epochs on the full set to compute per-sample EL2N difficulty scores and feature vectors. It outputs a fixed subset selected in a class-balanced fashion, which is then used to train a newly initialized model from scratch. A hard cutoff rate β prunes the most extreme (potentially noisy) samples before greedy selection begins, and the Beta-distribution parameters a and b are tuned per dataset based on |S| and the mean difficulty score D̄.

## Method

BOSS derives a balanced core-set loss bound (Theorem 1) that decomposes the full-set loss into a feature-similarity term λ^η‖x_i − x_j‖ and a label-variability term λ^y‖y_i − y_j‖, proving that both must be jointly minimized for a tight bound. Theorem 2 then establishes that the EL2N score lower-bounds the label-variability term in dense, high-variability regions near the decision boundary, providing a practical surrogate for the inaccessible label term. To adapt the balance to subset size, the authors design a Beta-distribution importance function I(x_j, y_j) = Beta(D_j | a, b) where a = 1 + D̄ + c_a|S| and b = 2 + c_b|S|, so that the mode shifts toward higher difficulty and the variance shrinks as |S| grows—yielding diverse selection for small budgets and difficulty-focused selection for larger ones. The final selection objective F(S) = Σ_{i∈V} max_{j∈S} Sim(x_i, x_j) · I(x_j, y_j) is a submodular facility-location function weighted by the importance scores, optimized via a lazy greedy algorithm with a cutoff mechanism to exclude the hardest (noisiest) samples.

## Results

BOSS achieves top-1 test accuracy of 78.27% on CIFAR-10 (10% subset), 46.54% on CIFAR-100 (10%), 32.54% on Tiny-ImageNet (10%), and 88.83% on SVHN (8%), consistently outperforming all baselines. The advantage is most pronounced in the low-budget regime: at 10% subset size on CIFAR-100, BOSS gains +6.3 points over the strongest baseline CCS (40.26%) and +7.9 over CRAIG (38.67%). As subset size increases to 50%, the gap narrows (e.g., 73.93% vs 71.53% for CCS on CIFAR-100), reflecting diminishing room for improvement. On the large-scale ImageNet-1k dataset, BOSS reaches 68.53% at 10% subset, surpassing CCS (59.10%) by 9.4 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BOSS outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and subset size tested. The largest margins appear at small subset sizes: on CIFAR-100 at 10%, BOSS (46.54%) beats CCS (40.26%) by 6.3 points, CRAIG (38.67%) by 7.9 points, and GradMatch (36.68%) by 9.9 points. Among gradient-based methods (CRAIG, GradMatch, Adacore, LCMAT), performance is tightly clustered within 1–2 points of each other, all substantially below BOSS, confirming that gradient matching alone cannot capture the joint feature-label distribution. CCS, the strongest single baseline, benefits from coverage-centric sampling but still lags BOSS by 2–6 points depending on dataset and budget, as it does not explicitly balance diversity and difficulty in a size-aware manner. YOCO, which prioritizes easy samples on already-condensed data, is outperformed by BOSS on both CIFAR-100 (e.g., 46.54% vs 38.86% at 10%) and ImageNet-1k (68.53% vs 54.93% at 10%). The ablation confirms that neither diversity alone nor difficulty alone suffices: difficulty-only selection (EL2N) collapses to 7.11% on CIFAR-100 at 10%, while diversity-only reaches 36.69%, versus 46.54% for the full BOSS method.

## Relation to Prior Work

BOSS unifies two previously separate lines of subset selection—diversity-based methods (CRAIG, GradMatch, Adacore, LCMAT) that cover feature/gradient space, and difficulty-based methods (EL2N, Forgetting, CCS, Moderate) that target hard samples—under a single balanced core-set loss bound derived from Sener & Savarese's (2018) active-learning formulation. The key departure is that prior work treats diversity and difficulty as competing paradigms, whereas the paper's Theorem 1 shows both terms are jointly necessary and that their relative contribution shifts with subset size. The practical mechanism (a Beta-distribution importance function parameterized by subset size and mean difficulty) is novel, but the underlying ingredients—facility-location submodular optimization, EL2N scoring, and lazy-greedy selection—are all established tools repurposed into a size-aware balance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The size-awareness insight (small budgets should prioritize representativeness; larger budgets should increasingly target difficult regions) gives practitioners a principled, interpretable knob rather than a fixed heuristic, and the consistent 2–6 point gains over CCS across four datasets and multiple architectures (ResNet, EfficientNet, ViT) make it a practical upgrade for data-efficient training pipelines. Limitations include the need for ~10 epochs of full-set initial training to compute difficulty scores, the unknown Lipschitz constants that prevent direct minimization of the theoretical bound, and the one-shot nature that precludes adaptation during training. Open questions include whether the Beta-parameterization generalizes to non-image modalities, whether the initial-training cost can be reduced via proxy models, and whether the submodular facility-location surrogate remains tight for very deep or non-convex architectures.
