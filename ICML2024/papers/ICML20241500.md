## Problem

Stochastic Rising Bandits (SRBs) model settings where arm rewards increase with each pull (e.g., online best model selection in AutoML), but prior BAI work either assumed deterministic rewards, required a known parametric form for the reward function, or only addressed regret minimization. This paper is the first to provide fixed-budget BAI algorithms with theoretical guarantees for the general stochastic SRB setting under only non-decreasing and concave expected reward assumptions, without requiring knowledge of the reward function's specific form.

## Input / Output

The algorithms operate on a rested bandit with K arms whose expected rewards are non-decreasing and concave in the number of pulls, with σ²-subgaussian noise. R-UCBE additionally requires an exploration parameter a whose theoretically sound value depends on the problem complexity index H_{1,η}(T), while R-SR needs only T, K, and the window size ε. The output is a single recommended arm at the end of the budget, where 'optimal' is defined as the arm with the largest expected reward after T pulls (which can change with T), accompanied by explicit bounds on misidentification probability and simple regret.

## Method

The core technical contribution is the design of two arm-dependent estimators with adaptive window size h(n) = ⌊εn⌋: a pessimistic estimator (average of the last h samples, assuming the reward stays constant, yielding O(n^{-1/2}) concentration) and an optimistic estimator (adding a concavity-based linear growth projection to time T, yielding O(Tn^{-3/2}) concentration). R-UCBE uses the optimistic estimator plus a UCB-style exploration bonus to select arms, while R-SR uses the pessimistic estimator in a phase-based successive-rejects scheme that eliminates one arm per phase with phase lengths from Audibert et al. (2010). The paper proves error probability bounds of the form exp(-T/H(T)) for both algorithms, derives a matching lower bound for R-SR, and shows that a minimum time budget is unavoidable for identifiability, with the complexity governed by a two-regime behavior at β = 3/2 (where bias and variance terms in the concentration balance).

## Results

On a synthetic Gaussian SRB with K=5 arms and σ=0.01, evaluated over time budgets T∈[100,3200], R-UCBE achieves the lowest empirical error probability for small budgets (T<185), outperforming all baselines by a large margin, while R-SR reaches an error rate below 0.05 for T>1000. On empirical simple regret, R-SR consistently outperforms R-UCBE and all baselines across the entire budget range. The optimal arm changes at T=185 in the synthetic setting, making the problem hardest near that threshold where all algorithms show elevated error rates (>0.2). A sensitivity analysis confirms R-UCBE is robust to misspecification of its exploration parameter a for T>185.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Both R-UCBE and R-SR significantly outperform all seven baselines (RR, RR-SW, UCB-E, SR, Prob-1, ETC, Rest-Sure) on empirical error probability across the evaluated budget range. The stationary BAI algorithms (UCB-E and SR from Audibert et al., 2010) and the adversarial BAI algorithm (Prob-1 from Abbasi-Yadkori et al., 2018) perform poorly because they assume fixed expected rewards and cannot track the rising nature of arm payoffs. Rest-Sure (Cella et al., 2021), the closest competitor as it also handles non-stationary rewards, is outperformed by both proposed methods but requires prior knowledge of the parametric form ρ of the reward function, which R-SR does not need. On simple regret, R-SR dominates R-UCBE, which suffers from over-exploitation of suboptimal arms leading to fewer pulls of the optimal arm. The round-robin baselines (RR, RR-SW) serve as weak references and are consistently the worst performers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fixed-budget Best Arm Identification framework of Audibert et al. (2010) to the Stochastic Rising Bandits setting introduced by Metelli et al. (2022), where arm rewards increase (in expectation) with each pull. It departs from prior CASH/BAI work by Li et al. (2020), which assumes deterministic rewards, and Cella et al. (2021), which requires a known parametric reward form, by operating under only a concavity assumption on expected rewards. The key methodological contribution is designing new pessimistic and optimistic estimators that account for the non-stationary, increasing nature of rewards, then plugging them into UCB-based and Successive Rejects-based algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SRB setting captures important real-world problems like Combined Algorithm Selection and Hyperparameter optimization (CASH) and online best model selection, where the optimal option can shift as the budget changes. The lower bound showing that a minimum time budget is unavoidable for identifiability is a fundamental characterization of the problem's difficulty, and R-SR matching this bound up to constants provides a clean optimality story. Open questions include designing algorithms that balance the error probability against the minimum budget requirement, extending to the restless setting, and handling non-concave reward functions where learnability may break down.
