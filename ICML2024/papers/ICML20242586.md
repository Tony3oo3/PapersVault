## Problem

Spikes in the training loss during SGD are a common but poorly understood phenomenon, and their relationship to generalization has remained unclear. While catapult dynamics were previously characterized for full-batch GD with large learning rates, it was unknown whether SGD loss spikes share the same mechanism or how they connect to feature learning and test performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242586/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis consumes a neural network's training trajectory, computing the NTK matrix at each iteration on the current mini-batch and decomposing the prediction residual into eigendirections of that kernel. The per-batch critical learning rate is approximated as b/||K_batch||_2, and a catapult event is flagged whenever the algorithmic learning rate exceeds this threshold. Outputs include the loss split into top-s and remaining eigenspace components, the NTK spectral norm trajectory, AGOP alignment (cosine similarity between the trained network's and the true model's AGOP), and test loss or classification error.

## Method

The paper proves that SGD loss spikes are catapults by verifying three signatures: (i) the spike is confined to the top eigenspace of the tangent kernel while the complementary eigenspace loss decreases monotonically, (ii) each spike is accompanied by a drop in the NTK spectral norm, and (iii) spikes occur precisely when η > η_crit(X_batch) for the sampled batch (match rates of 94–97% across architectures). Smaller batch sizes increase the variance of batch-level NTK eigenvalues, producing more catapults and thus more opportunities for feature learning. The generalization mechanism is quantified via AGOP alignment: each catapult increases the cosine similarity between the trained network's AGOP and the true model's AGOP, and test error correlates strongly with this alignment across optimizers (GD, SGD, Adam, Adagrad, etc.). Multiple catapults in GD are induced by repeatedly raising the learning rate after each catapult, since the decreasing ||K||_2 raises η_crit and permits further excursions above it.

## Results

The paper's central quantitative finding (Table 1, synthetic rank-2 regression) shows that decreasing SGD batch size from 2000 (GD) to 5 increases AGOP alignment from 0.81 to 0.95 while reducing test loss from 0.74 to 0.42. The match rate between loss spikes (ΔL > 0) and the condition η > η_crit(X_batch) is 94.7–97.3% across shallow, 5-layer FCN, and 5-layer CNN architectures (Table 2), confirming that SGD loss spikes are catapults. Across seven datasets (synthetic rank-2/3/4 regression, SVHN, CelebA, FashionMNIST, USPS) and multiple architectures (FCN, CNN, Wide ResNet 10-10, ViT-4), the number of catapults monotonically increases as batch size decreases, and AGOP alignment strongly correlates with test performance across optimizers including GD, SGD, SGD+Momentum, RMSprop, Adagrad, Adadelta, and Adam.

| Batch size | AGOP alignment | Test loss |
|---|---|---|
| 2000 (GD) | 0.81 | 0.74 |
| 50 | 0.84 | 0.71 |
| 10 | 0.89 | 0.59 |
| 5 | **0.95** | **0.42** |

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an analysis paper rather than a method paper, so comparisons are structured around optimization configurations rather than competing architectures. The primary comparison is across batch sizes: batch size 5 achieves AGOP alignment 0.95 and test loss 0.42, substantially outperforming full-batch GD (batch 2000) at 0.81 alignment and 0.74 test loss. A critical control experiment (Fig. 10) shows that when the learning rate is set small (η_crit/40), all batch sizes yield similar test performance, confirming that the benefit of small batches is specifically mediated by catapults rather than noise alone. Across optimizers (Fig. 11), AGOP alignment remains a strong predictor of test performance: for example, on the rank-2 task, Adam (alignment ~0.82) underperforms SGD (alignment ~0.90) on test loss, consistent with the catapult mechanism. The paper also compares NTK parameterization against PyTorch default parameterization (Figs. 21, 30) and finds the catapult–AGOP–generalization relationship holds in both settings, though absolute alignment values differ.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously studied but disconnected phenomena: loss spikes in SGD (observed empirically for years), catapult dynamics in GD with large learning rates (Lewkowycz et al., 2020), and the well-known generalization advantage of small-batch SGD. It extends the catapult concept from the full-batch GD setting to minibatch SGD by showing that batch-dependent critical learning rates trigger the same top-eigenspace dynamics. For the generalization mechanism, it departs from the 'flatter minima' explanation proposed by Lewkowycz et al. (2020) and instead invokes AGOP alignment (Radhakrishnan et al., 2024a) as the feature-learning pathway through which catapults improve test performance, building on the NTK framework of Jacot et al. (2018) and the quadratic-model analysis of Zhu et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic, testable explanation for why small-batch SGD generalizes better than large-batch or GD — a long-standing empirical observation previously attributed to flatness of minima — by showing that smaller batches increase the frequency of catapults, which in turn improve AGOP alignment. This reframing has practical implications for learning-rate scheduling (e.g., cyclical schedules that induce catapults) and for understanding when and why loss spikes are beneficial rather than harmful. Open questions include whether the AGOP-alignment mechanism holds for non-polynomial or high-rank target functions (the paper shows it fails for full-rank tasks), whether the top-eigenspace characterization survives in the feature-learning regime far from NTK, and whether the catapult-AGOP connection can be made rigorous beyond the empirical evidence presented here.
