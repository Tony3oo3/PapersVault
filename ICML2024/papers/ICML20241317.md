## Problem

Existing cross-domain recommendation methods either rely on overlapped users/items as bridges for knowledge transfer or assume full accessibility of intra-domain data, both of which are impractical under strict privacy regulations. This paper tackles the more challenging Privacy-Preserving Cross-Domain Recommendation (PPCDR) setting where users and items are entirely non-overlapped across domains and raw intra-domain data (ratings, reviews) cannot be shared, while also addressing the negative transfer problem caused by aligning dissimilar items during domain adaptation.

## Input / Output

Each domain provides its own user-item rating matrix (binarized at threshold 4), user-item review texts (encoded via BERT), and one-hot user/item IDs; no raw data crosses domain boundaries. The model outputs top-K item recommendations evaluated by HR@10/20 and NDCG@10/20, along with user and item embeddings in a shared D=128-dimensional latent space. The only cross-domain artifacts exchanged are the random projection matrix Z and the differentially private projected item embeddings, while noise matrices remain local to each domain.

## Method

RidCDR combines a standard rating prediction module (collaborative filtering with BERT-encoded reviews and binary cross-entropy loss) with a novel private-robust embedding alignment module built on the DPRA method. DPRA first applies a differentially private projection (random matrix Z plus Gaussian noise) to item embeddings, guaranteeing (ε,δ)-differential privacy so that cross-domain transfer cannot leak original item information. It then performs robust reweighted sample adaptation via Unbalanced Optimal Transport: a Sample Weight Adjustment Mechanism (SWAM) optimizes the Fenchel-Lagrange dual of UOT using FISTA with c-transform to increase weights of similar cross-domain items and suppress dissimilar ones, followed by a Sample Reweighting Optimal Transport (SROT) step that solves a classic OT problem on the reweighted marginals via the Reweighting Discrete Kantorovich Functional. The total loss is the sum of intra-domain rating prediction loss and a weighted RDKF discrepancy term, jointly optimized end-to-end.

## Results

On the Amazon dataset, RidCDR achieves HR@10 of 0.4088 (CD→Movie), 0.4672 (Movie→CD), and 0.4283 (Video→Game), with corresponding NDCG@10 values of 0.2228, 0.2477, and 0.2340. On the Douban dataset, RidCDR reaches HR@10 of 0.3359 (Book→Music), 0.4384 (Music→Book), and 0.3627 (Movie→Book). Across all six domain pairs, RidCDR consistently outperforms the strongest baseline MOTKD by 2–17% in HR@10 and 3–17% in NDCG@10, with the largest gains observed on the CD→Movie transfer (6.0% HR@10, 17.1% NDCG@10 over MOTKD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RidCDR outperforms all 15 baselines across every domain pair on both Amazon and Douban. The closest competitor is MOTKD (balanced optimal transport for feature alignment), which RidCDR surpasses by 6.0% HR@10 on Amazon CD→Movie (0.4088 vs. 0.3856), 5.4% on Movie→CD (0.4672 vs. 0.4431), and 1.9% on Video→Game (0.4283 vs. 0.4204). Adversarial-training-based methods (TDAR, Rec-DAN) and Gromov-Wasserstein-based GWCDR trail RidCDR by 8–15% in HR@10, while single-domain models (NeuMF, DeepCoNN, NARRE) lag by 25–40%, confirming that cross-domain knowledge sharing is essential under the no-overlap setting. On Douban, RidCDR leads MOTKD by 7.7% HR@10 on Book→Music (0.3359 vs. 0.3120) and 3.1% on Music→Book (0.4384 vs. 0.4253). The ablation variants further show that removing the robust reweighting (RidCDR-D, using DP-Sliced OT) or replacing UOT with entropic regularization (RidCDR-U) degrades performance by 3–8% HR@10, validating the necessity of the full DPRA pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: non-overlapped cross-domain recommendation (CDR), privacy-preserving CDR, and optimal transport for domain adaptation. It builds directly on unbalanced optimal transport (Benamou, 2003) as the transport mechanism, the c-transform strategy (An et al., 2022) for efficient dual optimization, and differentially private random projection (Rakotomamonjy & Liva, 2021) for the privacy guarantee. The key departure from prior PPCDR work (Chen et al., 2022; Liao et al., 2023) is the elimination of the overlapped-user/item requirement, and from prior non-overlapped CDR (MOTKD, SER) is the addition of a formal differential privacy guarantee on the cross-domain signal. The FISTA-based optimization of the UOT dual (SWAM) and the subsequent Reweighting Discrete Kantorovich Functional (SROT) replace the entropic Sinkhorn iterations that prior OT-based CDR methods rely on, motivated by numerical instability concerns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a more realistic deployment scenario where neither user identities nor item identities are shared across domains, a setting that prior PPCDR methods cannot handle. The combination of differential privacy with unbalanced OT provides a principled way to share domain-invariant item structure while formally bounding information leakage, which is practically important for regulated industries. However, the DP guarantee applies only to the item embedding projection step, and the method's computational cost (iterative FISTA for both SWAM and SROT at each training step) may limit scalability to very large domains. Open questions include whether the approach extends naturally to multi-source settings, whether the privacy budget can be tightened without significant performance loss, and whether the item-only alignment (vs. the marginal gain from user alignment in RidCDR++) reflects a fundamental asymmetry or a limitation of the current formulation.
