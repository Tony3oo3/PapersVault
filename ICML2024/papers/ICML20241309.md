## Problem

In-context learning (ICL) in LLMs is uneven in effectiveness, highly sensitive to template and demonstration choices, difficult to quantitatively control, and consumes valuable context window space. The underlying mechanism—shifting latent states via self-attention—is opaque, making it hard to adjust task strength or combine multiple tasks. This paper asks whether the task information encoded in ICL demonstrations can be extracted into a compact, controllable representation that bypasses the need for demonstrations in the prompt entirely.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of demonstration examples (paired x→y or unpaired sets of x's and y's) and a single query text, with no templates or prompt instructions required for the demonstrations. It produces generated text for the query. The demonstrations are consumed via forward passes to extract last-token latent states across all transformer layers, which are concatenated into a single high-dimensional vector; the resulting ICV is a single direction vector (the first principal component of latent-state differences) scaled by a user-specified hyperparameter λ. Because demonstrations are compressed into one vector rather than prepended to the prompt, the approach is not constrained by context length and can incorporate arbitrarily many examples.

## Method

ICV operates in two stages. First (task summary), each demonstration x and y is passed through the LLM to obtain last-token latent states h(x) and h(y) concatenated across all L layers; the ICV is computed as the first principal direction of the set {h(y_i) − h(x_i)} via PCA (for paired demonstrations) or as the gradient of a contrastive loss (for unpaired demonstrations). Second (feature shifting), during inference on the query, the ICV is added to the latent states at every layer l and every token position t as h̃_{t,l} = h_{t,l} + λ·h_ICV^l, followed by ℓ2-norm normalization to preserve the original representation magnitude. The key insight is that ICL's effect through self-attention is equivalent to a position-wise shift of latent features, and ICV makes this shift explicit, direct, and controllable via λ. The method requires no fine-tuning, introduces no new parameters, and supports task arithmetic—adding or negating ICVs to combine or invert multiple tasks simultaneously.

## Results

On ParaDetox language detoxification, ICV (λ=0.1) reduces toxicity to 34.77% on Falcon-7B and 39.54% on Llama-7B, compared to 73.09%/66.81% for ICL and 52.78%/48.94% for LoRA fine-tuning, while maintaining competitive ROUGE-1 (65.76/65.97) and BERT scores (92.88/92.73). On style transfer (GYAFC formality, Yelp sentiment), ICV achieves 48.30% formality accuracy and 75.28% positivity accuracy, outperforming ICL (32.96%/63.42%) and LoRA FT (21.99%/65.92%). For Shakespeare role-playing, ICV achieves the highest win rate among the three methods on both Llama-7B and Llama-13B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA fine-tuning (Hu et al., 2021), ICV achieves substantially lower toxicity: 34.77% vs. 52.78% on Falcon-7B and 39.54% vs. 48.94% on Llama-7B, while also preserving better semantic similarity (ROUGE-1 65.76 vs. 61.35 on Falcon; 65.97 vs. 57.32 on Llama). On style transfer, ICV dominates LoRA FT by a wide margin—48.30% vs. 21.99% formality accuracy and 75.28% vs. 65.92% positivity accuracy—suggesting LoRA overfits to the small demonstration set. Against the concurrent Task Vector method (Hendel et al., 2023), ICV reduces toxicity from 53.36% to 34.77% on Falcon-7B (a ~19-point improvement) while also achieving higher ROUGE-1 (65.76 vs. 62.37), partly because ICV adds the vector across all layers rather than replacing latent states at a single selected layer. ICV also requires no training or gradient updates, whereas LoRA FT needs 20 epochs of fine-tuning and Task Vector requires a development set for layer selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes in-context learning (ICL) as an implicit latent-space shifting operation mediated by self-attention, then makes that shift explicit and controllable via a single PCA-derived vector added across all transformer layers. It is most closely related to the concurrent work of Hendel et al. (2023), which also extracts a 'task vector' from latent states but replaces them at a single selected layer; ICV instead adds the vector to every layer, preserving original information and eliminating layer-selection. The task-arithmetic properties (addition, negation, composition of ICVs) parallel Ilharco et al.'s task vectors, but ICV achieves them without any fine-tuning. The activation-steering literature (Turner et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2023; Burns et al., 2022) provides the broader conceptual backdrop of modifying internal representations to steer behavior, but ICV is specifically grounded in the ICL setting with demonstration examples rather than hand-specified concepts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ICV offers a practical, training-free alternative to ICL that removes context-window constraints and provides a simple scalar (λ) for controlling task strength, making it attractive for open-source LLM deployment. However, the jail-breaking experiments (99% attack success rate on Vicuna-7B with only 5 demonstrations and seconds of compute) raise a serious safety concern: safety alignment may be far more fragile than assumed, and the same mechanism that enables beneficial steering can be weaponized. A key limitation is the white-box requirement—ICV needs direct access to intermediate latent states, ruling out API-only models. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger models (beyond 13B), whether the single-PCA-direction assumption holds for more complex or multi-step tasks, and how to defend against adversarial ICV-based attacks.
