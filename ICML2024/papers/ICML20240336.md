## Problem

Safety mechanisms deployed in text-to-image diffusion models (concept removal, negative prompts, safety guidance) have only been evaluated on limited safe-prompting benchmarks, leaving their reliability against diverse, adversarially crafted prompts largely unexplored. No automated, scalable red-teaming tool existed for systematically uncovering safety-evasive prompts in T2I diffusion models, and manual discovery of problematic prompts is hard to scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240336/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an original text prompt P (known to produce inappropriate content with an unconstrained T2I model), along with white-box access to both the unconstrained model G and the safe model G' (e.g., ESD, SLD, SD-NEGP). It produces a discrete, legible hard prompt P* (16 tokens for P4D-N, or variable-length for P4D-K) that, when fed to G', bypasses its safety mechanism and generates an image containing the target inappropriate concept. Optimization runs for 3000 gradient steps in the latent space of the diffusion model with 25 inference steps and guidance scale 7.5.

## Method

P4D optimizes a continuous soft prompt embedding P_cont* in the latent space of the diffusion model by minimizing the L2 distance between the noise predictions of the unconstrained model G (conditioned on the original prompt P) and the safe model G' (conditioned on the optimized prompt) at a shared noisy latent vector z_t. Following PEZ, the continuous embedding is projected to discrete tokens via nearest-neighbor vocabulary lookup, and gradients are passed directly through this non-differentiable quantization step to update P_cont*. Two variants are explored: P4D-N initializes N=16 tokens from random vocabulary embeddings, while P4D-K inserts learnable tokens after every K=3 tokens of the original prompt's CLIP embedding, preserving interpretability. A key empirical finding is that guidance-based safety filters (in SLD and SD-NEGP) act as an 'information obfuscation' layer that constrains the explorable embedding space; deactivating them during prompt optimization (while keeping them active at inference) reveals substantially more problematic prompts, analogous to obfuscated gradients in adversarial robustness.

## Results

P4D-UNION (the union of P4D-N and P4D-K prompts) achieves the highest failure rate (FR) across all safe T2I models and categories: 66.58% on ESD, 52.66% on SLD-MAX, 55.29% on SLD-STRONG, and 40.98% on SD-NEGP for the nudity category, and 72.74% on ESD for French-horn. This means roughly half of prompts originally deemed 'safe' by deployed safety mechanisms can be manipulated to produce inappropriate content. P4D-N and P4D-K individually achieve comparable performance (e.g., 50.65% vs 47.19% FR on ESD for nudity), with P4D-K offering better prompt interpretability since it preserves the original prompt as a substring.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Textual Inversion (Gal et al., 2022), P4D-UNION substantially outperforms on the nudity category: 66.58% FR on ESD vs. 11.91% for Text-Inv, and 52.66% vs. 13.73% on SLD-MAX. Against PEZ (Wen et al., 2024), P4D-UNION again leads decisively—66.58% vs. 12.47% (PEZ-Orig) and 26.59% (PEZ-PInv) on ESD, and 55.29% vs. 28.57% and 22.32% on SLD-STRONG—demonstrating that jointly leveraging both the unconstrained and safe T2I models is more effective than either alone. Against Advprompt (Maus et al., 2023), a black-box gradient-free method, P4D-N achieves 90% FR on ESD for nudity versus only 10% for Advprompt, and 100% vs. 50% on Car, with P4D also being 6–10× faster (20–30 min vs. 3–5 hours per prompt). Across all three external baselines, P4D consistently wins on every category and model, with the largest margins observed on ESD (finetuning-based safety) and the smallest on SD-NEGP (guidance-based safety), where the text filter constrains the explorable embedding space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt engineering paradigm from NLP (specifically the soft-to-hard projection and gradient-through-quantization update rule from PEZ) into the domain of red-teaming text-to-image diffusion models. While prior red-teaming efforts have focused almost exclusively on language models (e.g., Perez et al. 2022, Wichers et al. 2024), P4D is the first automated tool targeting T2I diffusion models with deployed safety mechanisms (negative prompts, SLD, ESD). It also draws a conceptual parallel between the 'information obfuscation' of safety text filters and the 'obfuscated gradients' phenomenon identified by Athalye et al. (2018) in adversarial robustness, repurposing that insight for generative model safety.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that roughly half of prompts deemed safe by existing benchmarks can be manipulated to bypass deployed safety mechanisms undermines the reliability of current evaluation protocols and suggests a false sense of security in deployed T2I systems. The 'information obfuscation' result—that disabling safety filters during debugging actually reveals more vulnerabilities—challenges a natural assumption and has direct implications for how safety researchers should structure their testing pipelines. Key limitations include the white-box requirement (access to both unconstrained and safe models), which restricts applicability to closed-source APIs, and the observation that optimized prompts often lack linguistic coherence, raising questions about whether the discovered vulnerabilities reflect realistic user behavior. Open questions include adapting the methodology to black-box/closed-source models and whether the findings generalize beyond the Stable Diffusion model family.
