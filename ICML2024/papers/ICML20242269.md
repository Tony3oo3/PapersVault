## Problem

Most Vision Transformer research focuses on modifying the self-attention module while leaving the feedforward network (FFN) in its original form, with little theoretical understanding of what the FFN actually contributes. This paper identifies that the FFN module is fundamentally a non-linearity generator whose degree is determined by the number of hidden dimensions, and asks whether this non-linearity can be amplified through the activation function itself to reduce the hidden dimension and thus computational cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242269/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The IFFN module is a drop-in replacement for the FFN layer in any ViT block, operating on a feature tensor of shape N×C (number of patches × channel dimension) and producing the same shape output. For classification on ImageNet-1k, the full pipeline takes 224×224 RGB images and outputs a 1000-way class label; for COCO detection it outputs bounding boxes with class scores; for ADE20K segmentation it outputs per-pixel class maps over 150 categories. The module requires no changes to the surrounding architecture, training schedule, or input preprocessing of the baseline model.

## Method

The paper first proves (Corollary 3.1) that each output element of the FFN is a linear combination of C′ distinct nonlinear functions of the corresponding input element, establishing the FFN as a non-linearity generator of degree C′. To exploit this, the authors introduce AGeLU — an arbitrary GeLU with learnable pre- and post-scaling (α, β) and bias (γ, θ) — and concatenate two independently parameterized AGeLU functions sharing a single linear projection of half the original hidden width, thereby halving FLOPs while preserving the same C′-degree non-linearity. A spatial-wise enhancement (depthwise convolution + BatchNorm + GeLU) is appended to further enrich non-linearity using local spatial context, drawing on the observation that a DW Conv after a nonlinearity is equivalent to a series-informed activation. The final IFFN module is validated as a plug-in across DeiT, Swin, PoolFormer, and LVT, reducing 10–21% FLOPs and parameters without accuracy loss, and a Lipschitz-bound theorem is provided to characterize the module's stability.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k, replacing the original FFN with the proposed IFFN module reduces parameters and FLOPs by 10–21% across all tested baselines while maintaining or slightly improving top-1 accuracy. For example, DeiT-Ti goes from 72.2% to 72.6% top-1 with 12.6% fewer parameters and 12.7% fewer FLOPs; PoolFormer-S24 improves from 80.3% to 80.7% with 19.8% fewer parameters and 20.0% fewer FLOPs. On COCO 2017 object detection, Swin-Ti+IFFN matches the baseline box AP of 46.0 while saving 4M parameters and 16G FLOPs. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, Swin-Ti+IFFN achieves 45.0 mIoU (vs. 44.5 baseline) with 4M fewer parameters and 17G fewer FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DeiT (Touvron et al., 2021), IFFN matches or slightly exceeds top-1 accuracy (e.g., DeiT-Ti: 72.2→72.6%, DeiT-S: 79.9→80.0%) while cutting parameters and FLOPs by ~12–15%. Against Swin (Liu et al., 2021), IFFN achieves equal or marginally better accuracy (Swin-Ti: 81.2→81.5%, Swin-S: 83.2→83.2%) with ~14% reductions in both parameters and FLOPs. Against PoolFormer (Yu et al., 2022), the gains are most pronounced because PoolFormer's computation is dominated by the FFN: IFFN saves 18–21% in parameters and FLOPs while matching or improving accuracy (e.g., PoolFormer-S24: 80.3→80.7%). Against LVT (Yang et al., 2022), IFFN achieves comparable accuracy (within ±0.2%) with ~8–10% reductions in parameters and FLOPs. In all four baselines, the method never degrades accuracy by more than 0.2% (LVT-R2: 74.8→74.6%), confirming that the IFFN module is a drop-in replacement that consistently trades computation for equal or better performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the observation that prior ViT modifications (CMT, CoAtNet, PVTv2) altered the FFN module alongside other architectural changes, leaving the isolated effect of FFN modification unclear. It departs from this by providing a theoretical decomposition (Corollary 3.1) showing the FFN is fundamentally a non-linearity generator whose degree equals the hidden dimension, then leveraging this to motivate a specific reparameterization: replacing GeLU with the more flexible AGeLU and concatenating two instances to halve the hidden dimension while preserving non-linearity degree. The spatial-wise enhancement draws conceptually from VanillaNet's series-informed activation, reframed as depthwise convolution after non-linearity to exploit spatial context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IFFN module offers a practical plug-and-play efficiency gain (10–20% FLOPs/parameter reduction) across diverse ViT backbones without accuracy loss, which is directly useful for deployment-constrained settings. The theoretical framing—FFN as a non-linearity generator—gives practitioners a principled lens for future FFN design beyond ad-hoc architectural swaps. However, the gains are moderate and the method's benefit is most pronounced in architectures where FFN dominates computation (e.g., PoolFormer); for attention-heavy models the savings are smaller. Open questions include whether the non-linearity-degree analysis transfers to NLP transformers or to settings where the FFN interacts with more complex attention mechanisms.
