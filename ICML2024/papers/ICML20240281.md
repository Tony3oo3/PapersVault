## Problem

Existing nonlinear ICA methods for learning temporal causal representations assume the mixing function from latent variables to observations is invertible, a condition violated by common real-world phenomena such as 3D-to-2D projection, object occlusion, and persistence of vision. This paper establishes the first identifiability theory that accommodates a nonlinear, non-invertible generation process and proposes a principled method to recover latent causal variables under this relaxed assumption.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a temporal window of μ+1 observations x_{t:t-μ} (e.g., video frames or time-series vectors) and produces the current latent causal representation ẑ_t ∈ R^n, identifiable up to permutation and component-wise invertible transformation. The key structural difference from standard step-to-step encoders is the sequence-to-step mapping: the encoder must integrate historical observations to compensate for information lost in the non-invertible mixing function g, which maps r+1 adjacent latent variables to a single observation. In the real-world SUTD-TrafficQA setting, inputs are ResNet-101 frame features from traffic videos, and the learned representation feeds into an HCRN cross-modality matching module for video question answering.

## Method

CaRiNG extends Sequential VAE with two architectural modifications: a sequence-to-step encoder (SeqEnc) that maps the observation window x_{t:t-μ} to the current latent variable ẑ_t, recovering information lost due to non-invertibility, and a transition prior module that uses a normalizing flow to parameterize the prior p(ẑ_t | ẑ_{t-1:t-τ}) as a product of independent Gaussian noises, enforcing the conditional independence assumption required by the identifiability theorem. Training minimizes the ELBO objective combining a reconstruction loss (MSE) and a KL divergence between the encoder posterior and the flow-based prior. The theoretical contribution includes Theorem 1, which proves identifiability under non-invertible mixing with stationary transitions, and Lemmas 1–2, which close the gap between the Jacobian sparsity condition and true identifiability by adding path-connectedness and non-degeneracy assumptions to guarantee permutation invariance in nonlinear settings.

## Results

On the synthetic NG (non-invertible generation) benchmark, CaRiNG achieves an MCC of 0.933±0.010, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline TDRL (0.627±0.009) and Sequential VAE (0.750±0.035). On the more challenging NG-TDMP (time-delayed mixing process) setting, CaRiNG scores 0.921±0.010 versus TDRL's 0.837±0.068. On the real-world SUTD-TrafficQA VideoQA benchmark, CaRiNG reaches 41.22% accuracy, surpassing the previous best method CMCIR (38.58%) by 2.64 points and TDRL (37.32%) by 3.9 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CaRiNG outperforms all 15 baselines across both synthetic and real-world evaluations. On the NG setting, the gap over TDRL (the strongest ICA-based baseline assuming invertibility) is 0.306 MCC, and over Sequential VAE (the best non-ICA temporal method) is 0.183 MCC, confirming that both temporal context and the conditional-independence KL constraint are critical. On NG-TDMP, CaRiNG still leads TDRL by 0.084 and Sequential VAE by 0.074, though the margin narrows as the time-delayed mixing adds complexity. On SUTD-TrafficQA, CaRiNG (41.22%) beats TDRL (37.32%) by 3.9 points, CMCIR (38.58%) by 2.64 points, and HCRN (36.26%) by 4.96 points, despite using a simpler ResNet-101 backbone compared to CMCIR's Swin-Transformer-L. Notably, TDRL's MCC drops to 0.03 on the lost latent dimension under non-invertibility, while CaRiNG maintains near-perfect recovery, directly validating the identifiability theory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Sequential VAE framework (Chung et al., 2015; Li & Mandt, 2018) and the nonlinear ICA identifiability theory (Hyvarinen & Morioka, 2017; Hyvarinen et al., 2019) to a setting that prior temporal causal representation methods (TDRL, LEAP) explicitly exclude: non-invertible generation processes. The key architectural departure is replacing the step-to-step encoder with a sequence-to-step encoder that ingests temporal context, and introducing a normalizing-flow-based transition prior module to enforce conditional independence. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as the first to prove identifiability when the mixing function loses information, complementing the existing body of nonlinear ICA theory that assumes invertibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identifiability guarantee under non-invertible generation is a meaningful theoretical advance, since real-world perception (3D-to-2D projection, occlusion, motion blur) is inherently non-invertible, making prior methods' invertibility assumption practically restrictive. However, the theory still requires the absence of instantaneous causal relations and a fixed number of latent variables across time steps, limiting applicability to dynamic scenes with entering/exiting agents. The real-world validation on SUTD-TrafficQA is encouraging but rests on a single dataset, and the authors acknowledge a gap between synthetic experiments and real data, calling for a dedicated benchmark with ground-truth latent variables generated via game engines.
