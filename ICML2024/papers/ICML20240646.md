## Problem

Most C-TDG methods based on message passing, RNNs, or self-attention fail to propagate information over long spatial distances (beyond a few hops) or long temporal distances (many events in the past), due to over-squashing in static DGNs and vanishing gradients in RNNs. No prior framework explicitly models and guarantees non-dissipative propagation of information across both the spatial and temporal dimensions of a C-TDG, leaving long-range spatio-temporal dependencies unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of irregularly sampled events, each a tuple of (timestamp, event type, source node, destination node, node features, edge features), processed sequentially to update node states. Between events, the ODE integrates node representations over a fixed propagation interval [t_s, t_e], where t_e (determined by step size ε times number of layers L) controls how far information spreads spatially. A 2-layer MLP readout maps the final node embedding to a prediction (binary class, multi-class label, or link score), and the initial condition ψ (e.g., tanh of concatenation of previous state and current input) ensures historical context is carried forward across events.

## Method

CTAN models node state evolution as a non-dissipative ODE whose weight matrix is constrained to be anti-symmetric (W_ℓ − W_ℓᵀ), guaranteeing that the Jacobian's eigenvalues are purely imaginary and thus information is neither amplified nor attenuated during propagation. The ODE is discretized via forward Euler with a small γI subtraction on the diagonal to maintain numerical stability, yielding a layer-wise update where the number of discretization steps equals the number of graph convolutional layers. The aggregation function Φ uses attention-based message passing over the temporal neighborhood, incorporating elapsed-time embeddings to handle irregular sampling. Crucially, the initial condition ψ (e.g., tanh(h^{i−1}(t_e) ‖ x(i))) preserves the node's accumulated history into each new event's propagation, enabling non-dissipative information flow over time as well as space. The terminal time t_e acts as a tunable propagation radius: fewer layers yield localized updates, while more layers extend the reach, and even with few layers, successive events relay information incrementally across the graph.

## Results

On the synthetic T-PathGraph long-range task, CTAN achieves 88.93% accuracy at path length n=20, while all baselines collapse to chance level (~50%); at n=9, CTAN scores 99.93% versus 47–53% for every other method. On the Temporal PascalVOC edge-classification task (sc=30, 1 GCL), CTAN reaches 9.98 F1, a 65% improvement over the second-best method (TGAT at 6.04). On real-world future link prediction, CTAN ranks first on all four datasets (Wikipedia 97.55, Reddit 98.61, LastFM 83.81, MOOC 92.47 AUC), averaging 4.7% above the best baseline. On the TGB benchmark, CTAN achieves an average rank of 3.25 across four datasets, tied with DyGFormer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CTAN decisively outperforms all six baselines on the T-PathGraph long-range task: at n=20 it retains 88.93% accuracy while memory-based methods (JODIE, TGN) and memory-less methods (TGAT, DyGFormer, GraphMixer, DyRep) all degrade to near-chance (~50%), with JODIE being the closest competitor (100% up to n=9, then 50.87% at n=20). On Temporal PascalVOC (sc=30, 1 GCL), CTAN (9.98 F1) surpasses TGAT (6.04) by 65%, though TGAT nearly matches CTAN at 5 GCLs (10.38 vs. 10.41), suggesting self-attention can approximate long-range propagation with sufficient depth. On the four real-world link prediction datasets, CTAN achieves the highest AUC on every dataset, beating TGN (the strongest baseline) by 1.95 (Wikipedia), 0.38 (Reddit), 4.63 (LastFM), and 1.73 (MOOC) points. On the TGB benchmark, CTAN ties DyGFormer on average rank (3.25) but significantly outperforms it on tgbl-review-v2 (40.52 vs. 22.39 MRR), where the high surprise index (0.987) limits the benefit of long node histories that DyGFormer exploits. EdgeBank (a non-parametric memorization baseline) surpasses CTAN on LastFM when given an infinite time window, but this advantage stems from accessing the full temporal adjacency matrix rather than learned propagation.

## Relation to Prior Work

CTAN extends the anti-symmetric DGN (A-DGN) framework from static graphs to Continuous-Time Dynamic Graphs, combining the non-dissipative ODE formulation of Gravina et al. (2023) with the event-stream processing paradigm of the TGN architecture (Rossi et al., 2020). The theoretical foundation draws on anti-symmetric RNNs (Chang et al., 2019) and graph neural ODEs (Poli et al., 2019), but the key departure is introducing non-dissipativeness over time as a distinct property from non-dissipativeness over space—a requirement unique to C-TDGs that cannot be addressed by static-graph ODE methods alone. The long-range evaluation tasks adapt the static Long Range Graph Benchmark (Dwivedi et al., 2022) and the sequence classification setup from anti-symmetric RNNs into the temporal graph domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to provide a theoretically grounded solution to long-range propagation in C-TDGs, and the new synthetic benchmarks (T-PathGraph, T-PascalVOC) fill a gap in the evaluation landscape by explicitly testing spatio-temporal information propagation. The 'truncated non-dissipative propagation' mechanism—where information accumulates across successive events even with few layers—offers a practical path to scaling propagation radius without increasing model depth. Open questions include whether the forward Euler discretization is optimal (the authors note adaptive multi-step schemes as future work), how CTAN scales to very large graphs, and whether the moderate real-world improvements (4.7% average AUC) will grow as long-range dependencies become more explicit in larger or noisier datasets.
