## Problem

Hybrid SSM-attention models suffer from two practical bottlenecks: linear attention's theoretical O(L) complexity is negated by intensive GPU memory access and cumsum operations in causal settings, making it slower than SSMs in wall-clock time; and long convolutions (as SSM replacements) are unstable without hand-crafted regularizations because a single kernel cannot simultaneously capture low- and high-frequency patterns. These issues prevent hybrid designs from achieving both true linear speed and competitive accuracy.

## Input / Output

CHELA consumes variable-length sequences (1K–16K tokens) in three modalities: tokenized text, raw 16000-sample speech waveforms (no MFCC preprocessing), and flattened pixel sequences from 32×32 images. For classification tasks (LRA, sCIFAR, Speech Commands) it outputs a class label via mean-pooling over the output sequence followed by a linear head; for autoregressive language modeling (WikiText-103, enwik8) it outputs next-token logits with adaptive softmax over a 260K vocabulary. The short convolution kernel sizes are input-dependent: one is fixed at 3, the other scales as 2·log₁₀(L)+1.

## Method

CHELA pairs two hardware-aware modules. First, a tiling-based linear attention implementation (inspired by FlashAttention and GLA) exploits the HBM–SRAM bandwidth gap via a divide-and-conquer loop: right-multiplication is used across blocks while left-multiplication handles intra-block causal masking, keeping intermediate accumulations in SRAM and achieving near-real linear wall-clock scaling. Second, short-long convolutions prepend two parallel short kernels (sizes 3 and 2·log₁₀(L)+1) with SiLU activation before a single long FFT convolution, distributing multi-frequency learning pressure and stabilizing the long kernel; at inference, Structural Reparameterization fuses the short kernels into the long one for a single-pass convolution. The convolution output Z is injected into the linear attention through a GRU/GAU-style gating mechanism: Z modulates Q and K via learned scalers/offsets, produces an element-wise attention gate G_a, and drives an output gate G_o that blends the attention output with the residual in a gated fashion. Each block stacks this token mixer with a two-layer FFN under pre-layer normalization and residual connections.

## Results

On the Long Range Arena (LRA) benchmark, CHELA achieves an average accuracy of 88.19% across six tasks (ListOps, Text, Retrieval, Image, Pathfinder, Path-X), outperforming the next-best hybrid model SPADE-chunk (87.40%) and SSM baselines like S4D (85.50%) and S4 (80.50%). On WikiText-103, CHELA attains a perplexity of 16.97 with 258M parameters, beating Mega-chunk (18.07) and Transformer-XL (18.30), while running at 53k tokens/s (nearly 10× faster than the vanilla Transformer at 5.6k t/s). On enwik8, CHELA achieves 0.96 bits-per-byte with only 48M parameters, surpassing Transformer-VQ (0.99) and Mega (1.02). On sCIFAR pixel-level classification, CHELA reaches 94.02% accuracy, exceeding the previous best convolutional model MultiresNet (93.15%) and SSM-based Liquid-S4 (92.02%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHELA outperforms all 30 baselines across every evaluated task. On LRA, it leads the closest hybrid competitor SPADE-chunk by 0.79 points in average accuracy (88.19 vs. 87.40) and Mega-chunk by 2.53 points (88.19 vs. 85.66), while also being slightly faster (5.8× vs. 5.5× speedup over Transformer). Among pure SSMs, CHELA surpasses S4D (85.50) by 2.69 points and S4 (80.50) by 7.69 points on LRA average; Mamba (66.59) trails significantly, particularly on ListOps (38.02) and Path-X (67.32). On language modeling, CHELA achieves the lowest perplexity on WikiText-103 (16.97 vs. Mega-chunk's 18.07 and Transformer-XL's 18.30) and the lowest BPC on enwik8 (0.96 vs. Transformer-VQ's 0.99 and Mega's 1.02), while using fewer parameters than Transformer-VQ (48M vs. 190M). On sCIFAR, CHELA (94.02%) beats the strongest convolutional baseline MultiresNet (93.15%) by nearly a full point and all SSM baselines (S4: 91.80%, S5: 90.10%, Liquid-S4: 92.02%). On Speech Commands, CHELA (97.98%) edges out S4 (97.50%) and substantially outperforms Mega-chunk (96.03%), with a comparable parameter count (493K vs. 476K).

## Relation to Prior Work

CHELA sits at the intersection of three research threads: hardware-efficient attention (FlashAttention, GLA), structured sequence modeling (S4, long convolutions from Fu et al.), and hybrid SSM-attention architectures (MEGA, SPADE). It departs from prior hybrid models by replacing SSMs with short-long convolutions stabilized via structural reparameterization, and by implementing linear attention with a FlashAttention-style tiling strategy borrowed from GLA to achieve true wall-clock linear speed. The gating mechanism draws from GAU and GRU, while the multi-frequency short kernels are inspired by multiresolution convolutional designs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CHELA is the first hybrid SSM-attention model to simultaneously improve performance over prior linear hybrids (MEGA-chunk, SPADE-chunk) while maintaining genuine linear wall-clock complexity, addressing a practical bottleneck (GPU memory access and cumsum) that had made linear attention's theoretical O(L) advantage illusory. The short-long convolution design offers a simple, reparameterizable stabilizer for long convolutions that could be adopted broadly. Open questions include whether dynamic short-convolutional configurations or time-varying SSMs could further improve the structured component, and whether the hardware-efficient tiling strategy generalizes to other linear attention variants beyond the kernel-based formulation used here.
