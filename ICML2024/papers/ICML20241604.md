## Problem

Standard weighted-sum scalarization in multi-objective RL offers no direct control over individual objective values, potentially yielding unfair solutions where one objective is severely sacrificed. The max-min formulation guarantees fairness but is non-linear and has no known model-free solution method. This paper bridges that gap by deriving a convex optimization theory for max-min MORL and proposing the first practical model-free algorithm that directly optimizes the max-min objective.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes environment state vectors (e.g., 37-dimensional traffic state or grid-based maze observations) and produces a stochastic policy via softmax over soft Q-values, along with a learned weight vector w on the (K−1)-simplex that implicitly encodes the fairness trade-off. Unlike deterministic argmax policies from standard DQN, the output policy is inherently stochastic due to entropy regularization, and the weight vector is jointly optimized rather than fixed a priori. The scalarized reward at each step is the weighted sum ∑w_k r^(k)(s,a) with the learned w.

## Method

The paper formulates max-min MORL as a linear program over state-action visitation frequencies, then derives its dual as a joint optimization over a value function v and a weight vector w on the simplex (P1). To resolve the indeterminacy where the dual solution doesn't uniquely recover the optimal policy, entropy regularization is added, yielding a convex problem (P1') whose solution explicitly gives the optimal stochastic policy as a softmax of soft Q-values. The model-free algorithm (Algorithm 1) alternates between soft Q-learning updates for a fixed w and weight updates via a novel Gaussian-smoothing-based gradient estimator: N Gaussian-perturbed copies of the Q-network are each updated one step, their resulting soft values are fit by linear regression, and the regression coefficient serves as the gradient for projected gradient descent on the simplex. Theoretical results establish convexity, continuous differentiability, and Lipschitz continuity of the soft value function in w, ensuring the gradient-based weight optimization converges to the max-min optimum.

## Results

On the Four-Room environment, the proposed method achieves a min-of-returns value of 0.96 (Type 1: 0.96, Type 2: 2.88), Pareto-dominating both baselines: MDQN (min 0.60) and Utilitarian DQN (min 0.76). On the traffic light control (SUMO-RL) environment with four directional objectives, the proposed method achieves a minimum return of -1455, substantially outperforming MDQN (-1837) and Utilitarian DQN (-2397). The learned weights in the traffic task (N: 0.43, S: 0.30, E: 0.15, W: 0.12) adapt to the asymmetric traffic flow, reducing the standard deviation across dimensions from 937.2 (Utilitarian) to 174.8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two value-based baselines: (1) Utilitarian, a standard DQN using uniformly averaged rewards (1/K weighting), and (2) MDQN (Fair Min-DQN from Siddique et al., 2020), which applies a min operation inside the Bellman backup. On Four-Room, the proposed method Pareto-dominates both baselines, achieving 0.96 min-return vs. 0.76 (Utilitarian) and 0.60 (MDQN), while also collecting more Type 2 elements (2.88 vs. 2.56 and 0.60). On the traffic light control task, the proposed method achieves -1455 min-return, a 39% improvement over Utilitarian (-2397) and a 21% improvement over MDQN (-1837). The paper attributes MDQN's poor performance to a cold-start issue: its min-inside-Bellman update only fires when all objectives are simultaneously non-zero, making early learning extremely slow. The Utilitarian baseline, while competitive on individual dimensions, exhibits a large gap between high-traffic (N/S) and low-traffic (E/W) directions due to its fixed uniform weighting, whereas the proposed method's learned weights adapt to the asymmetric traffic flow.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear programming formulation of RL (Puterman, 1994) to the multi-objective setting, showing that max-min MORL admits a dual LP whose variables are a value function and a weight vector on the simplex. It builds directly on the soft Bellman operator and soft Q-learning framework (Haarnoja et al., 2017) to resolve a key indeterminacy: the unregularized dual solution yields the correct max-min value but not a unique optimal policy. The gradient estimation for the weight optimization leverages Gaussian smoothing theory (Nesterov & Spokoiny, 2017), applied here via linear regression over perturbed weight samples. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant weighted-sum scalarization paradigm in MORL (Roijers et al., 2013) and from Fair-DQN-style methods (Siddique et al., 2020) that optimize E[min] rather than min(E), positioning itself as a value-based method that explicitly optimizes the egalitarian welfare function over expected returns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The entropy regularization insight—that adding a concave entropy term to the primal visitation-frequency problem makes the dual-to-primal policy mapping explicit and unique—is a clean theoretical contribution that resolves a subtle gap in the LP duality argument for max-min MORL. The resulting alternating algorithm (soft Q-update + Gaussian-smoothing gradient step on weights) is practical and outperforms both DQN-based and Fair-DQN baselines on the tested environments. Open questions include convergence guarantees for the alternating scheme in the model-free setting, scalability to continuous high-dimensional action spaces, and whether the approach extends to non-convex or non-additive reward structures. The paper's experiments, while clear, are limited to small-scale discrete-action environments, leaving open whether the method's advantages persist in larger, more realistic MORL benchmarks.
