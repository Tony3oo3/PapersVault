## Problem

Despite widespread adoption of prompting and prefix-tuning, it remained unclear whether these parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods could universally approximate arbitrary sequence-to-sequence functions, and if so, how long the prefix must be. Prior universality results for transformers either trained all parameters or relied on memorization with exponential depth in the target precision ε, leaving open whether a fixed pretrained model could be reprogrammed purely through its prefix.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of T input vectors constrained to the unit hypersphere S^m (as produced by LayerNorm in practice) and a learned prefix of N vectors in R^d, with all model parameters (attention matrices H, W_V, and MLP weights) held fixed. The output is a sequence of T vectors approximating the target function at each position. The key complexity parameter is the prefix length N, which scales as O(ε^{-(10+14m+4m²)}) for scalar functions on S^m, and the required depth is only T+2 attention layers for general sequence-to-sequence functions, independent of ε.

## Method

The central insight is that a single attention head with fixed parameters can approximate any continuous function on the hypersphere by exploiting the fact that exp(λ⟨x, p_k⟩) terms act as von Mises-Fisher kernels when inputs are restricted to S^m, where the dot product equals cosine similarity. The proof proceeds in three steps: (1) showing that sums of exp(λ⟨x, p_k^α⟩)p_k^β terms (core attention) are dense in C(S^m) via spherical convolution with the von Mises-Fisher kernel, yielding a Jackson-type bound on N; (2) showing the normalization denominator is approximately constant for uniformly distributed control points, recovering split attention; and (3) embedding the split attention into a classical attention head of dimension 3(m+1) by placing x, p_k^α, and p_k^β in orthogonal subspaces with a specific block-structured H and W_V. For general sequence-to-sequence functions, a Schmidt-Hieber variant of the Kolmogorov-Arnold theorem decomposes the target into element-wise Lipschitz maps (approximated by one attention head via Corollary 3) and a summation (approximated by one more head), requiring only T+2 layers total.

## Results

The paper establishes that prefix-tuning a single attention head with hidden dimension 3(m+1) is a universal approximator for any continuous function on the hypersphere S^m, with a Jackson-type bound of N = O(ε^(-10-14m-4m²)) prefix positions needed to achieve precision ε. For general sequence-to-sequence functions, only T+2 attention layers (linear in sequence length T, independent of ε) are required. This contrasts sharply with the prior result of Wang et al. (2023), which requires exponential depth O(Tε^(-m)) and relies on memorization of all function-input pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary theoretical comparison is with Wang et al. (2023), who also prove prefix-tuning is a universal approximator but via a fundamentally different (memorization-based) construction. Wang et al. require model depth O(Tε^(-m)) that grows with approximation precision ε, whereas this paper needs only T+2 layers independent of ε. Wang et al. use a fixed prefix length for all functions, meaning a constant function and a highly nonsmooth function require the same prefix; this paper's prefix length N scales with the smoothness of the target (smaller L and C_H yield shorter prefixes). Additionally, Wang et al. effectively hard-code all possible function-input-output pairs, explaining their exponential size, while this paper shows attention heads are naturally suited for approximation without memorization. The paper also builds on Petrov et al. (2024), which showed prefix-tuning cannot change relative attention patterns over input tokens, by demonstrating that a 'universal' attention head structure (specific H and W_V matrices) can emulate any new function defined purely through the prefix.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the universal approximation theory for transformers from the full-training setting (Yun et al., 2019; Jiang and Li, 2023) to the parameter-efficient prefix-tuning setting, where all model weights are frozen and only the prefix is learned. It builds directly on Ng and Kwong's (2022) density result for approximation on the hypersphere, upgrading it to a Jackson-type bound with explicit prefix-length guarantees, and leverages Schmidt-Hieber's (2021) reformulation of the Kolmogorov-Arnold theorem for the sequence-to-sequence extension. The closest prior work is Wang et al. (2023), who showed prefix-tuning universality via memorization with exponential depth; this paper departs by showing that memorization is unnecessary and that T+2 layers with prefix length depending only on smoothness suffice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a single attention head with fixed pretrained parameters can universally approximate any continuous function on the hypersphere fundamentally reframes how we think about the expressivity of the attention mechanism and the power of prefix-tuning. The Jackson-type bound on prefix length (O(ε^(-10-14m-4m²))) reveals that prefix-tuning is asymptotically less efficient than full training, an important caveat for practitioners. A major open question is whether these results transfer to real-world pretrained models, which almost certainly do not possess the specific H and W_V structure required; deriving an inverse (Bernstein-type) bound for arbitrary pretrained transformers remains unsolved. The safety implications are significant: if any function can be encoded in a prefix, it raises fundamental questions about whether transformer-based systems can be made robustly safe against adversarial prompting.
