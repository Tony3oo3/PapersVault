## Problem

Existing reward models in RLHF and direct preference optimization (DPO, C-RLFT) produce point-estimate rewards that ignore the inherent ambiguity in human preference annotations, where responses with identical reward scores can exhibit vastly different uncertainty levels. This unmodeled uncertainty leads to unreliable data curation, suboptimal training curricula, and degraded instruction-following performance, particularly when preference gaps are small or annotator disagreement is high.

## Input / Output

The URM consumes preference pairs (instruction, chosen response, rejected response) or single SFT responses (instruction, response, class label) and outputs both a scalar reward score and a multi-dimensional uncertainty vector comprising epistemic uncertainty, aleatoric uncertainty, and exponentiated Balanced Entropy U ∈ [0, e]. The uncertainty estimates are not final outputs but are consumed downstream as curriculum ordering signals, loss-weighting coefficients in UDPO, and regularization terms in UCPO, ultimately producing an aligned language model. A key design constraint is that the sigmoid transformation of reward differences precludes the standard beta-distribution assumption, requiring a novel numerical formulation for Balanced Entropy.

## Method

The core contribution is an Uncertainty-aware Reward Model (URM) built on a pre-trained LM with Monte Carlo dropout for Bayesian approximation, trained with a standard Bradley-Terry-style negative log-likelihood loss over preference data. The key insight is converting the reward gap R_c − R_r into a sigmoid probability P_{y_c ≻ y_r} = σ(R_c − R_r), which reframes preference uncertainty as binary classification uncertainty amenable to information-theoretic decomposition into epistemic and aleatoric components. Because the sigmoid transformation invalidates the beta-distribution assumption underlying standard Balanced Entropy, the authors derive a new sigmoid-regime formulation using numerical integration of the posterior differential entropy. The resulting exponentiated Balanced Entropy U is then deployed in three ways: (1) as a curriculum signal that orders DPO training data by decreasing U or increasing aleatoric uncertainty, (2) as a per-sample loss weight C_u = (e−U)/E[e−U] in UDPO that downweights ambiguous preferences, and (3) as a certainty regularization term γ·(1−Ũ) added to the class-conditioned SFT loss in UCPO, extending the framework to single-response mixed-quality datasets.

## Results

On Vicuna-Bench pair scores, UDPO outperforms DPO by approximately 7 points on average across Pythia 1.4B and 6.9B models (e.g., 115.0 vs. 105.0 on OpenAI-Summarize with Pythia 1.4B; 111.3 vs. 103.8 on Anthropic-Helpful with Pythia 6.9B). On MT-Bench pair scores, UDPO surpasses DPO by more than 1 point on average (e.g., 102.5 vs. 98.8 on Anthropic-Helpful with Pythia 1.4B). For the SFT-based setting, UCPO improves instruction-following over C-RLFT by more than 5 points on average on both Vicuna-Bench and MT-Bench. The URM achieves 67.8–84.6% preference prediction accuracy across held-out datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), UDPO wins on 3 of 4 Vicuna-Bench settings (107.5 vs. 100.0 on Anthropic-Helpful, 115.0 vs. 105.0 on OpenAI-Summarize, 111.3 vs. 103.8 on Pythia 6.9B Anthropic-Helpful) and narrowly loses on Synthetic-GPT-J (120.0 vs. 121.3), though the authors note UDPO still beats DPO in direct head-to-head comparison (117.5). On MT-Bench, UDPO wins or ties in 3 of 4 settings, with the largest gain being +3.7 points on Anthropic-Helpful (102.5 vs. 98.8). Against C-RLFT (Wang et al., 2023), UCPO consistently outperforms by more than 5 points on average across both Vicuna-Bench and MT-Bench for Pythia 1.4B and 6.9B on curated Open-Orca and Dolly datasets. The paper also demonstrates that uncertainty-guided curriculum ordering (e.g., decreasing Balanced Entropy) improves MT-Bench single scores to 6.12 vs. 5.73 for random ordering with Llama 2 7B, and increasing aleatoric uncertainty boosts Vicuna-Bench pair scores to 171.3 vs. 146.3 for random.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of RLHF-style reward modeling and Bayesian uncertainty estimation, extending the DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) and C-RLFT (Wang et al., 2023) training pipelines by introducing an Uncertainty-aware Reward Model (URM) that outputs reward distributions via Monte Carlo dropout rather than scalar scores. The key conceptual bridge is the sigmoid transformation of reward gaps into binary preference probabilities, which allows the authors to apply Balanced Entropy (Woo, 2023) in a new sigmoid regime where the standard Beta-distribution approximation breaks down. Unlike prior uncertainty-in-RLHF work that uses reward model ensembles for exploration bonuses or regularization during PPO, this paper deploys uncertainty in the direct policy optimization setting (DPO, C-RLFT) where no explicit reward model is used at training time, and further leverages it for curriculum ordering of preference data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that uncertainty in preference annotations is not merely noise to be averaged out but carries actionable signal: ordering DPO training data by decreasing Balanced Entropy or increasing aleatoric uncertainty yields consistent improvements over random ordering, and weighting the DPO loss by inverse uncertainty (UDPO) or adding an uncertainty-regularized term to C-RLFT (UCPO) improves instruction-following by several points across multiple model scales. The demonstration that a poorly ordered curriculum can cause a model to effectively 'forget' previously learned capabilities (Fig. 5) is a practically important warning for practitioners running DPO. Open questions include whether the separately trained URM scales efficiently to much larger models, whether the MC dropout approximation remains reliable at scale, and whether the sigmoid-regime Balanced Entropy formulation generalizes beyond binary preference to multi-way ranking settings.
