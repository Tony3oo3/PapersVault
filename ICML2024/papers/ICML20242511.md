## Problem

While LIMA and AlpaGasus represent state-of-the-art approaches to selecting high-quality instruction fine-tuning (IFT) data via manual curation or GPT-3.5-Turbo scoring, the distinguishing features that make IFT demonstrations effective remain unclear. This paper asks whether a trivially simple selection criterion—response length—can match or surpass these expensive, sophisticated data-selection pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242511/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes existing IFT datasets (Alpaca-52k, Evol-Instruct-70k) as instruction-response pairs and selects the 1,000 examples with the longest responses as the training subset. An optional refinement step feeds each selected pair to GPT-3.5-Turbo via an introspection prompt that first critiques the original response, then regenerates an improved version. The output is a fine-tuned LLM (Llama-2-7B, Llama-2-13B, or Mistral-7B-v0.1) that produces text responses at inference time, evaluated via head-to-head LLM-as-judge comparisons, AlpacaEval 2.0, MT-Bench, and Open LLM factual-knowledge benchmarks.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage data-selection-and-refinement pipeline for supervised fine-tuning. First, the 1,000 longest responses are extracted from a larger IFT dataset, operating on the intuition that longer responses contain more learnable signal, are harder to memorize (reducing overfitting), and force the model to capture long-distance semantic structure. Second, an introspection prompting procedure uses GPT-3.5-Turbo to review and regenerate each selected response, improving style, structure, and detail without requiring human annotation. The refined 1k dataset is then used for standard SFT, optionally combined with NEFTune noise augmentation on token embeddings. Across multiple base architectures and source datasets, this simple pipeline consistently outperforms AlpaGasus-1k, LIMA-1k, and full-dataset fine-tuning on instruction-following evaluations, and with NEFTune achieves the second-highest AlpacaEval 2.0 score among Llama-2-7B-based models using only 1,000 examples and no preference data.

## Results

On AlpacaEval 2.0, Refined-Alpaca-1k-longest + NEFTune achieves a 7.88% win rate with Llama-2-7B (2nd highest Llama-2-7B-based model on the leaderboard), surpassing Llama-2-Chat-7B (4.96%) and approaching Tulu-2-DPO-7B (8.20%), while training on only 1,000 examples and no preference data. In head-to-head GPT-4 comparisons, Alpaca-1k-longest achieves a 46.3% average win rate against LIMA-1k (with only 28.3% losses). On MT-Bench, Refined-Alpaca-1k-longest + NEFTune scores 4.18 (Llama-2-7B), 6.09 (Llama-2-13B), and 6.00 (Mistral-7B-v0.1), consistently outperforming Alpaca-52k, AlpaGasus-1k, and LIMA-1k baselines. On the Open LLM benchmark, Refined-Alpaca-1k-longest achieves 56.4% average (Llama-2-7B), surpassing LIMA-1k (55.9%) and the base model (53.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Alpaca-52k (Taori et al.), Alpaca-1k-longest wins on AlpacaEval 2.0 (3.16 vs. 2.74), MT-Bench (3.96 vs. 3.74 for Llama-2-7B), and head-to-head GPT-4 comparisons across all five evaluation sets. Against AlpaGasus-1k (Chen et al.), Alpaca-1k-longest wins on AlpacaEval 2.0 (3.16 vs. 2.69) and head-to-head comparisons, despite the two datasets having minimal overlap (most 1k-longest examples score only 3.5 from GPT-3.5-Turbo). Against LIMA-1k (Zhou et al.), the plain Alpaca-1k-longest achieves a 46.3% win rate in head-to-head GPT-4 comparisons, while Refined-Alpaca-1k-longest achieves a dominant 59.9% average win rate across three architectures (vs. 20.2% for LIMA). Against Evol-Instruct-70k (Xu et al.), Evol-Instruct-1k-longest wins on AlpacaEval 2.0 (4.09 vs. 3.44) and head-to-head comparisons. Against Llama-2-Chat-7B (Touvron et al., 27k SFT + 3M preference pairs), Refined-Alpaca-1k-longest surpasses it on AlpacaEval 2.0 (6.00 vs. 4.96) using only 1k SFT examples and no preference data. Against Tulu-2-DPO-7B (Ivison et al., 326k SFT + 64k DPO), Refined-Alpaca-1k-longest + NEFTune (7.88) is close but slightly below (8.20). Against Vicuna-7B (Chiang et al.), the plain Alpaca-1k-longest (3.16) underperforms (4.16), but the refined + NEFTune variant (7.88) clearly surpasses it. Against Reflection-tuning (Li et al.), Refined-Alpaca-1k-longest matches Recycled-Alpaca-52k on AlpacaEval 2.0 (6.00 vs. 5.93) while outperforming it on the Open LLM benchmark (61.17 vs. 59.30 average) using 52× fewer training examples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the dominant paradigm in IFT data selection, where quality is defined either by LLM-based scoring (AlpaGasus, using GPT-3.5-Turbo grades) or by expensive manual curation (LIMA). It departs from both by proposing response length as the sole selection criterion, motivated by prior observations that RLHF and DPO implicitly encourage longer outputs (Singhal et al., Yuan et al.) and by the intuition that longer responses are harder to memorize and contain more learnable structure. The refinement step draws on introspection prompting (inspired by Chain-of-Thought) and combines with NEFTune noise augmentation, but the core contribution is the data selection heuristic itself, which the authors show is nearly orthogonal to LLM-judged quality scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides an extremely cheap, reproducible baseline (select the 1k longest responses) that should be the default comparison for any future IFT data selection work, and it challenges the field's working assumption that 'high quality' IFT data is what LLM judges or human annotators score highly. It also partially challenges the Superficial Alignment Hypothesis by showing that IFT on carefully selected long examples can improve factual-knowledge benchmarks (Open LLM), not just instruction-following style. Open questions remain: what specific properties of long responses drive the effect (information density, long-range coherence, task complexity?), whether the approach transfers to safety-critical alignment, and whether the gains persist as base models scale. The paper explicitly notes it does not address safety or ethical alignment.
