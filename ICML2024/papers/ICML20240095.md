## Problem

While stochastic recursive gradient methods (SPIDER, PAGE) achieve O((n+κ√n)log(1/ε)) IFO complexity for finite-sum optimization under the Polyak-Lojasiewicz (PL) condition, no matching lower bound existed to confirm optimality. In the decentralized setting, where n agents on a connected network each hold one local function, tight lower bounds on communication rounds, time cost, and local oracle calls for PL minimization were also absent, leaving the fundamental limits of distributed first-order methods under PL unclear.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume local function-value and gradient pairs (f_i(x), ∇f_i(x)) accessible only to agent i, along with the network topology encoded as a symmetric doubly-stochastic mixing matrix W with spectral gap γ. In the decentralized setting, agents communicate only with neighbors per round (cost τ per round), and complexity is tracked along three separate axes—communication rounds, wall-clock time (which accounts for parallel local computation), and total LFO calls—rather than a single oracle count. The output is an ε-suboptimal point x̂ satisfying f(x̂)−f* ≤ ε, obtained by uniformly sampling from the agents' final iterates.

## Method

The lower-bound construction adapts the zero-chain function g_{T,t} from Yue et al. (2023) to the finite-sum PL setting via orthogonal decomposition (Lemma 3.2) and scaling (Lemma 3.3), yielding Ω(n+κ√n log(1/ε)) IFO calls. For the decentralized case, g_{T,t} is split into three components q₁, q₂, r assigned to disjoint agent subsets at graph distance σ apart, forcing any DFO algorithm to perform Ω(κ/√γ log(1/ε)) communication rounds and Ω(κ(1+τ/√γ)log(1/ε)) time to propagate information across the network. The proposed DRONE algorithm combines a PAGE-style stochastic recursive gradient estimator (with Bernoulli(p) full-recompute and Multinomial(b,q1) partial-update sampling) with gradient tracking and Chebyshev-accelerated gossip (AccGossip), achieving matching upper bounds in expectation by using a larger stepsize η=Θ(1/L) than prior decentralized nonconvex methods when n=Ω(κ²).

## Results

The paper establishes a nearly tight IFO lower bound of Ω(n + κ√n log(1/ε)) for finite-sum PL optimization, matching the upper bound O((n + κ√n) log(1/ε)) of SPIDER/PAGE up to logarithmic factors. In the decentralized setting, the paper proves lower bounds of Ω(κ/√γ log(1/ε)) communication rounds, Ω(κ(1 + τ/√γ) log(1/ε)) time cost, and Ω(n + κ√n log(1/ε)) LFO calls. The proposed DRONE algorithm achieves Õ(κ/√γ log(1/ε)) communication, Õ(κ(1 + τ/√γ) log(1/ε)) time, and O((n + κ√n) log(1/ε)) LFO complexity in expectation, nearly matching all three lower bounds simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRONE is compared against DGD-GT (decentralized gradient descent with gradient tracking) and centralized gradient descent (CGD) in both theory and experiments. In theory, DGD-GT matches the communication and time lower bounds but has a suboptimal LFO complexity of O(κn log(1/ε)), which is worse than the Ω(n + κ√n log(1/ε)) lower bound when n = Ω(κ²). DRONE improves the LFO bound to O((n + κ√n) log(1/ε)) by incorporating a stochastic recursive gradient estimator (inspired by PAGE), while maintaining the same communication and time complexity as DGD-GT. In numerical experiments on a hard instance, linear regression (DrivFace), and logistic regression (RCV1) with n=32 agents on a linear graph, DRONE requires significantly fewer LFO calls than both DGD-GT and CGD. CGD achieves fewer communication rounds and less running time than both decentralized methods, which the authors attribute to the absence of consensus error in the client-server architecture. DGD-GT and DRONE have comparable communication rounds and running times across all three problems, consistent with their matching theoretical communication complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three well-developed threads: (1) the zero-chain function construction of Yue et al. (2023) for PL lower bounds, which is extended here from full-batch to incremental oracle settings; (2) the stochastic recursive gradient estimators (SPIDER, PAGE) that achieve O((n+κ√n)log(1/ε)) IFO complexity for single-machine PL optimization; and (3) decentralized gradient tracking with Chebyshev acceleration (EXTRA, AccGossip) for networked optimization. The key departure is combining these threads to establish the first tight IFO lower bound for finite-sum PL optimization and the first simultaneous lower bounds on communication, time, and LFO complexity in the decentralized PL setting, then constructing DRONE to match all three.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By confirming that existing recursive gradient methods (SPIDER, PAGE) are optimal up to logarithmic factors for PL finite-sum optimization, this paper closes a long-standing open problem and provides a clear stopping criterion for algorithm design in this regime. The decentralized lower bounds and the DRONE algorithm offer the first complete complexity characterization (communication, time, computation) for PL optimization over networks, which is directly relevant to federated and distributed learning. Open questions include extending the analysis to stochastic (expectation-form) objectives and to the more general Kurdyka-Lojasiewicz inequality, both of which the authors identify as future directions.
