## Problem

Offline RL methods (AWAC, IQL, TD3+BC) perform well on single-task datasets but paradoxically degrade significantly—sometimes to near-zero performance—when data from related but different tasks is simply concatenated into a single buffer. This failure mode is critical because realistic deployment scenarios involve heterogeneous data sources (multiple tasks, exploration data, diverse behaviors), yet no prior work systematically identified why or how to fix this. The paper introduces MOOD, a new testbed on the DeepMind Control suite, to expose and study this data-diversity failure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240232/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes offline trajectory buffers (state, action, reward, next-state tuples) that may be merged from multiple behavior policies trained on different objectives (e.g., walk, run, stand, plus RND exploration data) and relabeled for a single target task. The output is a parameterized Gaussian policy over continuous actions, evaluated by cumulative return normalized to the best trajectory in the dataset. A key detail is that mixed-objective datasets are strict supersets of their same-objective counterparts—no subsampling occurs—making the performance drop purely a function of added data diversity rather than data quality.

## Method

The paper's central finding is that targeted scaling of the policy network (actor) is the dominant factor resolving the diverse-data failure, more so than algorithmic modifications. Specifically, increasing the actor from 2×256 to 5×1024 hidden units (with 3×256 critics) bridges the performance gap on MOOD mixed-objective datasets for all three candidate algorithms (TD3+BC, AWAC, IQL), and the same scaled AWAC/IQL surpass state-of-the-art on D4RL locomotion and antmaze. The mechanism is attributed to plasticity loss in the actor: the non-stationary offline optimization target (the advantage-weighted distribution π*_B) shifts substantially over training, and small actors saturate early and cannot track it. Secondary contributions include ASAC (replacing biased weighted importance sampling with direct sampling from the advantage-weighted distribution via a logsumexp-tree) and evaluation sampling (selecting the highest-Q action among M samples at test time), both of which provide marginal gains on top of scaling.

## Results

On the D4RL Locomotion-v2 benchmark, AWAC with a large modern architecture achieves a total score of 813.1, surpassing DQL (790.7), XQL (752.9), CQL (698.5), and DT (672.6). On D4RL Antmaze-v0, IQL with the large modern architecture reaches 463.1, outperforming XQL (420.9), DQL (417.8), CQL (303.6), and DT (112.2). On the MOOD mixed-objective testbed, scaling the actor from 2×256 to 5×1024 units bridges the performance gap: IQL improves from 284.8 to 373.6 and AWAC from 276.4 to 367.3 (total across four environments), matching same-objective performance. The paper demonstrates that this simple scaling of the policy network is more impactful than algorithmic modifications like ensemble critics, evaluation sampling, or advantage sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DT, CQL, XQL, and DQL on D4RL, the paper's scaled AWAC and IQL consistently outperform all four baselines on Locomotion-v2 (813.1 and 810.7 vs. best baseline DQL at 790.7) and Antmaze-v0 (IQL 463.1 vs. best baseline XQL at 420.9). TD3+BC also surpasses all baselines on Locomotion-v2 (815.0) but lags on Antmaze-v0 (338.4 vs. XQL 420.9), suggesting TD3+BC's BC regularization is less effective in high-diversity settings. On the unbalanced Locomotion-0.1-v2 datasets, IQL with the large architecture (500.5) matches or exceeds the trajectory-weighting strategy of Hong et al. (2023a) (478.1 for CQL) without requiring any modified sampling distribution. The key takeaway is that the performance gains come almost entirely from increasing the policy network size (5 hidden layers × 1024 units), while algorithmic additions like 5-critic ensembles, evaluation sampling, and the ASAC variant provide only marginal or inconsistent improvements on top of scaling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the policy-constrained offline RL lineage (TD3+BC, AWAC, IQL), all of which modify TD3's actor-critic framework with varying degrees of conservatism. It departs from the prevailing trend of proposing increasingly sophisticated algorithms (CQL, XQL, DQL, Decision Transformer) by demonstrating that these methods' failures on diverse data stem not from algorithmic deficiencies but from insufficient policy network capacity. The ASAC contribution repurposes the sum-tree data structure from prioritized replay to enable direct sampling from the advantage-weighted target distribution, avoiding the biased weighted importance sampling estimator used in AWAC. The connection to plasticity loss literature (Nikishin et al., 2022; Lyle et al., 2023) is notable in that it identifies the actor network—not the critic—as the component suffering from early saturation in the offline setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that targeted policy scaling (5 hidden layers × 1024 units) is the dominant factor—outperforming algorithmic refinements like ensemble critics, evaluation sampling, or advantage sampling—challenges the field's assumption that offline RL requires ever-more-complex algorithms. Practically, this means existing simple methods (AWAC, IQL) can immediately match or surpass state-of-the-art on D4RL with minimal implementation changes. Open questions remain about why actor scale specifically matters (the plasticity loss hypothesis is supported but not fully mechanistic), whether the finding generalizes beyond continuous control, and whether the MOOD-style diverse-data evaluation will become standard. The paper's 50,000+ experiments provide strong evidence, though the analysis is limited to four DMC environments and two D4RL suites.
