## Problem

Large vision-language models (LVLMs) suffer from object hallucination (OH) across three types—existence, attribute, and relationship—yet existing mitigation methods either target only existence hallucination, require external powerful LVLMs or additional training data, or operate as post-hoc corrections. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a unified, training-free decoding strategy that simultaneously reduces all three OH types while preserving generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240307/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

HALC takes an RGB image and a text query as input and produces a textual response (caption or VQA answer) with reduced object hallucination. It operates as a plug-and-play decoding module integrated into any LVLM (MiniGPT-4, LLaVA-1.5, mPLUG-Owl2) without retraining. Internally, it consumes a zero-shot detector (Grounding DINO or OWLv2) to retrieve per-token visual context windows and a BLIP model to compute global image-text matching scores for beam selection.

## Method

HALC operates at two levels during autoregressive decoding. Locally, it identifies object-related tokens via POS tagging, retrieves a visual context window using a zero-shot detector, samples n field-of-view (FOV) windows via exponential expansion, computes pairwise Jensen-Shannon Divergences between the token probability distributions decoded from each FOV, and applies bi-directional contrastive decoding on the top-m most contrasting FOV pairs to redistribute token probabilities and recover hallucinated tokens. Globally, it employs a matching-based beam search that scores candidate sequences using BLIP image-text similarity to select the top-k most visually faithful responses. The key insight, validated by a brute-force oracle study showing 84.5% hallucination elimination, is that token-specific optimal visual contexts exist and can be approximated by selecting FOV pairs with maximum contrast in decoding distributions. A theoretical guarantee (Theorem 5.1) certifies that the FOV sampling strategy converges to the optimal visual context as sample size grows.

## Results

On MSCOCO (CHAIR), HALC achieves CHAIR_S of 17.80 / 13.80 / 17.33 and CHAIR_I of 8.10 / 5.50 / 7.43 on MiniGPT-4, LLaVA-1.5, and mPLUG-Owl2 respectively, reducing sentence-level hallucination by 10–13 points over the best prior method (LURE) while maintaining or improving BLEU scores. On the MME benchmark, HALC outperforms all baselines by an average margin of +10.7% (existence), +18.3% (position), +19.4% (color), and +20.2% (count). HALC also shows notably lower standard deviations than non-OH-specific baselines, indicating stronger robustness across random seeds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HALC consistently outperforms all five baselines on CHAIR_S and CHAIR_I across all three LVLM backbones. Against the strongest OH-specific baseline LURE, HALC reduces CHAIR_S by 10.08 points on MiniGPT-4 (17.80 vs. 27.88), 5.68 points on LLaVA-1.5 (13.80 vs. 19.48), and 3.94 points on mPLUG-Owl2 (17.33 vs. 21.27). DoLa and OPERA, which are not specifically designed for OH, show minimal or even negative gains over greedy decoding on CHAIR metrics, while VCD performs worst among OH-specific methods on LLaVA-1.5 (CHAIR_S 23.33) and mPLUG-Owl2 (CHAIR_S 27.27). Woodpecker achieves the highest BLEU (17.05 on LLaVA-1.5) but at the cost of higher hallucination (CHAIR_S 23.85), partly because it leverages ChatGPT for post-hoc correction. On MME, HALC leads all methods on all four sub-tasks, with the largest margins on count (+20.2%) and color (+19.4%), confirming gains across existence, attribute, and relationship hallucination types.

## Relation to Prior Work

HALC extends the contrastive decoding framework of Li et al. (2022b) from text-only expert/amateur pairs to pairs of visual context windows (FOVs), introducing an adaptive focal-contrast mechanism that samples and selects the most contrasting FOVs via Jensen-Shannon Divergence. It departs from prior OH mitigation methods (DoLa, OPERA, VCD) that contrast model layers or add noise, and from post-hoc/self-correction pipelines (Woodpecker, LURE) that require external LLMs or additional training, by operating entirely at the decoding level with a zero-shot detector (Grounding DINO/OWLv2) for initialization and a BLIP-based visual matching score for global beam selection. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 5.1) providing a formal convergence guarantee for FOV sampling toward an optimal visual context is a novel contribution absent from prior decoding-based OH work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HALC's plug-and-play design (no extra training, no external LLM) makes it immediately deployable across open-source LVLMs, and its simultaneous handling of all three OH types (existence, attribute, relationship) addresses a gap where prior methods focused primarily on object existence. The proposed OPOPE metric offers a more stable offline evaluation alternative to POPE for post-hoc methods. Key limitations include the ~2.4× computational overhead without parallelization, dependence on an external detector for FOV initialization, and the finding that beam size k=1 is optimal (larger k degrades performance), suggesting the global search component is underutilized. Open questions include scalability to video or multi-image inputs, whether the detector dependency can be internalized, and whether the approach transfers to non-English or low-resource settings.
