## Problem

Despite the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis showing that sparse subnetworks matching dense model accuracy exist at initialization, all practical attempts to find them without training the full network (pruning at initialization) have failed to match post-training pruning methods at high sparsities. This paper asks why: what fundamental barrier prevents data-agnostic or lightly data-dependent mask selection from producing trainable sparse subnetworks, and why do data-dependent methods like IMP succeed where initialization-based methods fail?

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework takes a p-parameter function class with mask-weight structure (m, w) where m ∈ {0,1}^p and w ∈ R^p, along with n i.i.d. input-output pairs in R^d × [-1,1], and produces a lower bound on the Lipschitz constant of any learned function that fits below the noise level σ². The bound is expressed in terms of an effective parameter count p_eff = I(m^W; D) + E[||m||_1]·log(1+60WJε⁻¹), which can be substantially larger than the raw number of unmasked parameters. Empirically, the paper tracks two proxies for p_eff—memorization capacity (train accuracy on noisy labels) and correlation with fixed label noise—across sparsity levels for MLPs and convNets on Gaussian, FashionMNIST, and CIFAR-10 data.

## Method

The paper proves a modified Law of Robustness (extending Bubeck & Sellke, 2023) in which the standard parameter count p is replaced by an effective parameter count p_eff that includes the mutual information between the sparsity mask and the training data. The key proof technique replaces the standard union bound over the function class with an information-theoretic concentration inequality (Xu & Raginsky, 2017), showing that fitting below the noise level forces the learned function to correlate with noise, which in turn forces high mutual information between the mask and data. The central implication is that IMP-derived masks, which are selected after training on the full dataset, carry O(p·log(1/(1-γ))) bits of mutual information with the data, making them effectively far less sparse than data-agnostic masks (e.g., SynFlow) at the same raw sparsity. Experiments confirm this by showing IMP and magnitude-after-training subnetworks memorize more noisy training points and correlate more strongly with label noise than SNIP, GraSP, or SynFlow at matched sparsity, and that corrupting gradients eliminates this correlation, verifying that the information enters through data-dependent training.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 5.6) establishes that a sparse network fitting below the noise level σ² must have Lipschitz constant at least Ω(ε√(nd/p_eff)), where the effective parameter count p_eff = I(m^W; D) + E[||m||₁]·log(1+60WJε⁻¹) replaces the usual parameter count. Experiments on Gaussian data (n=30, d=30), noisy FashionMNIST, and noisy CIFAR-10 confirm that at the same sparsity level, IMP and magnitude-after-pruning subnetworks memorize a significantly larger fraction of noisy training points than SNIP, GraSP, or SynFlow subnetworks. On a toy 21-parameter MLP, exact mutual information computation shows IMP and magnitude-after-pruning masks carry substantially more information about the dataset than SNIP or GraSP masks, while SynFlow (data-agnostic) has identically zero MI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's theoretical framework explains why IMP (Frankle & Carbin, 2018) outperforms all initialization pruning methods: IMP's mask is derived after training the full network, giving it high mutual information I(m^W; D) and thus a much larger effective parameter count p_eff. In memorization capacity experiments (Figures 1a–c), IMP and magnitude-after-pruning (Han et al., 2015) consistently achieve higher train accuracy on noisy data at every sparsity level compared to SNIP (Lee et al., 2018), GraSP (Wang et al., 2020), and SynFlow (Tanaka et al., 2020), with the gap widening as sparsity increases. SynFlow, being entirely data-agnostic, has zero mutual information and performs closest to random pruning. On the toy model with exact MI computation (Figure 3), IMP and magnitude-after-pruning show the highest mask-data mutual information, while SNIP and GraSP show lower (but nonzero) MI, and SynFlow shows exactly zero. The paper does not propose a new pruning algorithm but rather provides the information-theoretic explanation for why all initialization pruning methods (SNIP, GraSP, SynFlow) fail at high sparsity in a similar way, as observed empirically by Frankle et al. (2020b).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Law of Robustness of Bubeck & Sellke (2023) by replacing the raw parameter count p with a data-dependent 'effective parameter count' p_eff that incorporates the mutual information between the sparsity mask and the dataset, drawing on the information-theoretic concentration bound of Xu & Raginsky (2017). It positions itself as a theoretical explanation for the well-documented empirical failure of initialization pruning methods (SNIP, SynFlow, GraSP) to match the performance of lottery tickets found via iterative magnitude pruning (Frankle & Carbin, 2018; Frankle et al., 2020b). The conceptual departure from prior information-theoretic generalization bounds is that mutual information is used not to certify good generalization when it is low, but to show that fitting below the noise level *requires* high mutual information, thereby making the mask itself a source of effective capacity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled information-theoretic explanation for why lottery tickets exist but cannot be found without training the dense model, reframing the long-standing empirical puzzle of initialization pruning failure as a fundamental parameter-information tradeoff. The effective parameter count concept generalizes beyond the Law of Robustness to VC-dimension and Rademacher-complexity bounds (Appendix C), suggesting a broader principle. Key limitations include the non-constructive nature of the results (necessary but not sufficient conditions), inapplicability to the online training regime of LLMs, and small-scale experiments that rely on proxies (memorization capacity, noise correlation) rather than direct mutual information estimation. Open questions include whether analogous barriers hold for LLMs and whether one can design pruning algorithms that operate at the optimal information-parameter tradeoff boundary.
