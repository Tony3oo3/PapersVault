## Problem

LLM inference is prohibitively expensive for continuous stream processing (e.g., processing one million 8K-token documents per hour would require ~1,000 A100 servers at >$30K/hour). Existing cost-reduction strategies—knowledge distillation and static model cascades—assume a pre-collected labeled training set, making them inapplicable to streaming settings where queries arrive online and no human annotations are available. The paper asks: can we learn a cost-aware cascade of models entirely online, using only the LLM's own outputs as supervision?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241544/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes an infinite stream of text queries (movie reviews, forum posts, emotion sentences, or fact-checking claims) one at a time and emits a discrete class label per query. Internally, each query traverses an N-level cascade (logistic regression → BERT-base → LLM) where a learned deferral function at each level decides whether to output a prediction or pass the query to the next, more expensive model. The LLM at the top level always produces a label that serves as the ground-truth annotation for updating all smaller models and the deferral policy, so no external human labels are ever required.

## Method

The paper formulates online cascade learning as an episodic MDP whose cost function jointly penalizes prediction loss and per-level deferral overhead (weighted by a tunable constant μ), then solves it via a DAgger-style imitation learning algorithm: smaller models are updated by online gradient descent on LLM-collected trajectories, while deferral functions are calibrated post-hoc with an MLP trained to predict whether the current model's prediction is wrong. A decaying probability β_t periodically forces queries to the LLM (analogous to DAgger's on-policy sampling) to accelerate convergence. The key theoretical result is a no-regret guarantee (average regret → 0 as T → ∞) under OGD with learning rate t^{-1/2}, proven by reducing the cascade's dynamic deferral probabilities to the static ensemble case and showing they converge to the optimal binary deferral rule. In practice, the system starts by routing all queries to the LLM and gradually shifts workload to cheaper models, achieving up to 90% inference cost reduction while matching or exceeding LLM accuracy across four benchmarks.

## Results

On four benchmarks (IMDB, HateSpeech, ISEAR, FEVER), Online Cascade Learning (OCL) achieves accuracy within 1–2% of the LLM at moderate cost budgets and in several cases *exceeds* the LLM at higher budgets (e.g., FEVER: 78.49% vs. GPT-3.5 Turbo's 70.34%; HateSpeech accuracy: 85.35% vs. 83.34%). The system reduces LLM inference calls by up to 90% (HateSpeech, N=507 out of ~5,352 queries) while maintaining accuracy of 82.66% and recall of 82.36%, closely matching GPT-3.5 Turbo (83.34%/83.28%). With Llama 2 70B Chat as the expert, OCL reaches 92.63% on IMDB and 77.73% on FEVER, matching or surpassing the LLM (93.33% and 68.23% respectively) at a ~60% reduction in LLM calls.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-3.5 Turbo (Brown et al., 2020 family), OCL at the highest budget (N=5200/4900/2700/2800) exceeds the LLM on FEVER (78.49% vs. 70.34%) and HateSpeech accuracy (85.35% vs. 83.34%), while falling slightly short on IMDB (93.01% vs. 94.15%) and ISEAR (81.03% vs. 83.28%). Against Llama 2 70B Chat (Touvron et al., 2023b), OCL surpasses the LLM on FEVER (77.73% vs. 68.23%) and HateSpeech accuracy (78.32% vs. 77.81%), and is nearly tied on ISEAR (82.03% vs. 82.19%), while trailing slightly on IMDB (92.63% vs. 93.33%). In all cases, OCL achieves these results using only a fraction of the LLM calls (e.g., ~5,200 calls for 12,500 IMDB queries, a 60% reduction; ~507 calls for ~5,352 HateSpeech queries, a 90% reduction). OCL also consistently outperforms the knowledge-distillation baselines (Distilled LR and Distilled BERT-base) and the Online Ensemble Learning ablation across all four datasets, confirming the benefit of the learned deferral policy over fixed-weight ensembles.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three existing threads—DAgger-style imitation learning (Ross et al., 2011), static model cascading with deferral policies (Varshney & Baral, 2022; Chen et al., 2023), and online convex optimization with no-regret guarantees (Zinkevich, 2003)—into a unified online cascade learning framework. The key departure from prior cascade work is the elimination of any pre-labeled training set: smaller models learn continuously from LLM demonstrations in a streaming setting, and the deferral policy is co-optimized online via OGD rather than trained offline on a fixed dataset. The confidence calibration component builds on the observation (Jitkrittum et al., 2023) that static confidence thresholds are inadequate when model capabilities are dynamically evolving, replacing them with a learned MLP-based deferral function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 90% inference cost reduction while matching LLM accuracy across four diverse benchmarks (sentiment, hate speech, emotion, fact-checking) and two LLM backends makes this practically significant for any streaming NLP deployment where LLM costs are prohibitive. The no-regret guarantee provides a clean theoretical foundation, though it relies on the strong assumption that the LLM's output is always correct (treated as ground truth), which may not hold in practice with noisy or hallucinating LLMs. Open questions include how the framework behaves when the LLM expert is imperfect, whether the uniform-inference-cost assumption holds for variable-length generation tasks, and how the approach scales to cascades with more than four models or to multi-turn conversational settings.
