## Problem

Despite molecular surfaces encoding the most direct information about protein-protein interactions, existing self-supervised protein representation learning has focused almost exclusively on amino acid sequences and 3D structural elements, leaving surface-based pretraining largely unexplored. Surface point clouds present additional challenges: they are sparse, inherently disordered (lacking sequential arrangement), and standard masked auto-encoder approaches impose no constraint on the latent space between encoder and decoder, limiting the semantic quality of learned representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes protein 3D structures (atomic coordinates with element types) and generates oriented surface point clouds via SDF-based gradient descent sampling at a 1.05 Å radius, enriched with residue-level chemical features from a geometric aggregation network. The surface is partitioned into irregular patches via FPS+KNN (with ρK=1 to balance coverage and overlap), ordered by Morton code to impose a pseudo-sequential structure. Downstream, the learned patch embeddings are fine-tuned to produce binding scores (classification), binding affinity ΔG (regression), or mutant effect ΔΔG (regression).

## Method

Surface-VQMAE employs a hierarchical backbone: quasi-geodesic convolution layers capture local point-level geometry, followed by SurfFormer, a Transformer whose attention mechanism incorporates radial-basis-function-encoded geodesic distances as geometric structural embeddings alongside standard QKV projections and sinusoidal positional encodings derived from Morton ordering. The pretraining objective extends MAE by replacing the shared learnable mask token with a vector-quantized code embedding sampled via Gumbel-Softmax relaxation over a learned codebook, enforcing a discrete (categorical) posterior on latent variables to yield more condensed semantics. Two lightweight MLP decoders predict masked patch center coordinates (Chamfer distance loss) and pseudo-curvatures computed from local covariance eigenvalues (RMSE loss), with a KL divergence term regularizing the latent distribution toward a uniform prior. The curvature target is provably roto-translation invariant, addressing a key limitation of coordinate-only reconstruction.

## Results

On protein interface scoring (Table 1), Surface-VQMAE achieves an AUROC of 94.78% and balanced accuracy of 87.16%, outperforming the next-best method PIsToN (93.55% AUROC) by 1.23 percentage points. On antibody-antigen binding affinity prediction (Table 2), it reaches a Pearson correlation of 0.411 on crystal structures and 0.358 on ZDock-docked structures, surpassing the best DL baseline NERE (0.340/0.234) by 7.1% and 52.6% respectively. On mutant effect prediction (Table 3), Surface-VQMAE attains the highest per-structure Pearson (0.4694) and Spearman (0.4324) correlations, along with the best overall AUROC (0.7469), setting new SOTA on nearly all metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On protein interface scoring, Surface-VQMAE decisively outperforms all 10 baselines: it beats the strongest DL method PIsToN (Stebliankin et al., 2023) by 1.23% AUROC and 1.91% BAcc, and surpasses the previous surface-based method dMaSIF (Sverrisson et al., 2021) by ~5% AUROC. All energy-based (FireDock, RosettaDock, PyDock, ZRANK2) and potential-based (SIPPER, AP-PISA, CP-PIE) methods trail by 5–20+ AUROC points. On binding affinity prediction, Surface-VQMAE (Pearson 0.411 crystal / 0.358 ZDock) outperforms NERE (Jin et al., 2023) by 7.1% and 52.6% respectively, and exceeds all physics-based potentials (best: AP-PISA at 0.323 crystal). On mutant effect prediction, Surface-VQMAE achieves the best per-structure Pearson (0.4694) and Spearman (0.4324), edging out RDE-Net (Luo et al., 2023) by 0.025 and 0.032, and PPIFormer (Bushuiev et al., 2023) by 0.041 and 0.033; it also attains the highest overall AUROC (0.7469 vs. RDE-Net's 0.7454). The method is competitive but not always best on overall Pearson (0.6482 vs. DDGPred's 0.6580) and MAE (1.1271 vs. RDE-Net's 1.1123), indicating slight trade-offs in aggregate regression metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked auto-encoder paradigm (He et al., 2022; Pang et al., 2022) to molecular surfaces by combining it with vector quantization, replacing the standard straight-through estimator with a Gumbel-Softmax relaxation inspired by dVAE (Ramesh et al., 2021) to allow probabilistic codebook sampling. The hierarchical backbone pairs quasi-geodesic convolution (Sverrisson et al., 2021) for local point-level features with a novel SurfFormer that augments self-attention with radial-basis-function-encoded geodesic distances for global patch-level interactions. The Morton curve ordering strategy is borrowed from PointGPT (Chen et al., 2023) to impose sequential structure on inherently unordered point clouds. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior protein SSL work (ESM, PPIFormer, RDE-Net) by operating exclusively on surface geometry rather than sequences or full 3D structures, positioning molecular surfaces as a first-class pretraining modality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to demonstrate that purely surface-based self-supervised pretraining can match or exceed structure- and sequence-based methods across multiple PPI tasks, validating molecular surfaces as an independent and information-rich representation for protein function. The robustness to docking error in binding affinity prediction (0.358 vs. NERE's 0.234 on ZDock structures) suggests surface representations may be more forgiving of structural uncertainty, which is practically important for drug discovery pipelines. Open questions include whether the FPS-based partitioning is optimal, how the method handles noisy or incomplete surface data, and whether the gains generalize beyond the specific PDB-REDO pretraining corpus and the SKEMPI/SAbDab evaluation sets. The authors acknowledge these limitations and note that exploring alternative downsampling strategies and broader dataset coverage are natural next steps.
