## Problem

Existing truthfulness-enhancement methods for LLMs rely on in-domain annotated data (e.g., TruthfulQA) or costly human labels, making them impractical for the broad spectrum of out-of-domain questions encountered in deployment. The paper asks whether OOD queries can be leveraged to improve model truthfulness in a self-supervised manner, without any human-annotated answers to those questions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240282/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained 7B LLM (Llama2-Chat-7B or Zephyr), a set of OOD questions from ARC-Challenge (~1092 after format filtering), and six in-domain few-shot demonstrations from TruthfulQA. The model itself generates both a 'correct' and an 'incorrect' answer for each question via few-shot prompting, producing pairwise training data without external annotation. The output is a LoRA-fine-tuned model evaluated on TruthfulQA MC1/MC2 (truthfulness) and ARC, HellaSwag, MMLU (core capabilities), with the key constraint that core-capability performance must be preserved.

## Method

GRATH operates in three stages: (1) the pretrained model is prompted with few-shot demonstrations to generate a plausible correct answer and a plausible incorrect answer for each OOD question, forming pairwise truthfulness data; (2) the model is fine-tuned via DPO on these pairs, increasing the likelihood of correct answers while decreasing that of incorrect ones (self-truthifying); (3) the now-improved model regenerates the 'correct' answers (leaving incorrect answers fixed), widening the distributional distance between the two, and DPO is applied again (gradual self-truthifying). The key empirical finding is that DPO's truthfulness gains depend on both a small domain gap between training and testing data and a large distributional distance between correct and incorrect answers within the pairs; the iterative refinement naturally increases this distance (mean pairwise distance shifts from 65.94% to 87.80%), explaining why the second DPO pass yields substantially higher MC1/MC2 accuracy. The approach uses LoRA (rank 8) for parameter-efficient training with 1000 DPO steps per execution, and a single iteration (T=1) suffices for SOTA TruthfulQA performance.

## Results

GRATH_Llama2 (7B) achieves state-of-the-art TruthfulQA performance with MC1 accuracy of 54.71% and MC2 accuracy of 69.10%, surpassing even 70B-parameter models (e.g., Xwin-LM v0.1 70B: MC1 40.27%, MC2 59.86%). Compared to its base model Llama2-Chat-7B (MC1 30.23%, MC2 45.32%), GRATH yields improvements of +24.48% and +23.78% respectively. Core capabilities are largely preserved: ARC improves from 52.73% to 57.76%, HellaSwag from 78.50% to 79.63%, while MMLU drops only slightly from 48.14% to 46.88%. GRATH_Zephyr similarly improves MC1 from 42.23% to 53.86% and MC2 from 57.83% to 66.73%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against open models of all sizes (7B–70B), GRATH_Llama2 achieves the highest TruthfulQA MC1 (54.71%) and MC2 (69.10%) scores, exceeding the best 70B model (Xwin-LM v0.1) by 14.44% on MC1 and 9.44% on MC2. Among fine-tuning baselines applied to the same Llama2-Chat-7B base, GRATH outperforms RepE (the strongest truthfulness-specific method) by 11.26% on MC1 (54.71% vs. 43.45%) and 8.65% on MC2 (69.10% vs. 60.45%). Standard DPO with OOD data yields only 36.72% MC1, a 17.99% gap behind GRATH, while SFT and RLHF actually degrade truthfulness (MC1 drops to ~22.4–22.6%). ITI shows no improvement over the base model (identical MC1/MC2 scores), confirming that inference-time interventions without training are insufficient. GRATH also generalizes across DAP approaches: replacing DPO with IPO or RSO still yields substantial truthfulness gains (MC1 44.43% and 39.90% respectively after two DPO executions), though DPO remains the strongest variant.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRATH builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) as its optimization backbone, repurposing a human-preference alignment technique for truthfulness learning without any human annotations. It departs from prior truthfulness-enhancement methods (RepE, ITI) that rely on in-domain data or internal representation manipulation, and from standard DPO/RLHF pipelines that require costly annotated preference pairs. The iterative self-training loop draws conceptual parallels to SPIN and ReST^EM, but GRATH's specific focus on relative truthfulness differences in self-generated answer pairs—rather than absolute quality scoring—distinguishes it from those self-improvement approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GRATH demonstrates that a 7B model can surpass 70B models on TruthfulQA MC tasks using only OOD questions and self-generated data, offering a practical, annotation-free post-processing recipe for improving LLM truthfulness. The finding that DPO is vulnerable to domain gap (OOD data degrades performance) is a useful caution for practitioners applying preference optimization across domains. Open questions remain: the method's benefit on the generative TruthfulQA task is inconsistent (degrades with more iterations when using the current base model as reference), overfitting limits the number of useful iterations, and generalization beyond the ARC-Challenge OOD source and 7B/13B scale is untested.
