## Problem

Existing physical reasoning benchmarks (CLEVRER, Physion, ComPhy) are limited to simple rigid-body primitives with fixed physical parameters and narrow question types, leaving a critical gap in evaluating whether AI models can infer diverse continuum physical properties (density, elasticity, tension, plasticity) and reason about soft-body and fluid dynamics. The paper asks whether current models possess the physical commonsense to handle coupled soft-rigid interactions, counterfactual dynamics, and goal-driven planning across heterogeneous materials.

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides 2,000 Unity-rendered videos (Full HD 1920×1080, 120–250 frames per scenario) paired with 6,500 template-generated questions across four scenarios (fluid, rope, cloth, ball). Each video carries one property question and two dynamics questions (counterfactual, goal-driven, or predictive), answered as multiple-choice with 2–4 options or yes/no. The ContPRO oracle model additionally consumes point-cloud and particle-level simulation data, while MLLM baselines receive only 10 downsampled frames per video.

## Method

ContPRO is a four-module neural-symbolic oracle that decomposes video QA into perception, simulation, parsing, and execution. A fine-tuned Mask R-CNN (ResNet-101-FPN) detects objects and static attributes per frame; a physical simulator predicts dynamics using DPI-Net (a GNN) for rope/cloth and a differentiable Material Point Method (MPM) for fluid/ball, where physical properties (density, viscosity, Young's modulus, yield stress) are inferred by gradient descent minimizing Chamfer loss between predicted and ground-truth particle positions. An LLM (ChatGPT/GPT-4) acts as a program parser, translating the natural-language question into executable Python code against a predefined SoftScene API. A symbolic executor then runs the generated code with the perceived object states and simulated dynamics to produce the final answer, combining the interpretability of LLM reasoning with the precision of particle-based physics.

## Results

On the ContPhy benchmark (6,500 questions across 4 scenarios), the oracle model ContPRO achieves the best overall performance, reaching 78.0% on Fluid Property, 90.1% on Fluid Predictive (per-option), 92.4% on Ball Predictive (per-option), and 88.6% on Ball Predictive (per-question). Notably, ContPRO surpasses human performance on several predictive tasks (e.g., 90.1% vs. 73.9% on Fluid P-Opt; 88.6% vs. 58.8% on Ball P-Ques). Among non-oracle models, ALPRO (visual) and PhyDNet (physical) are the strongest baselines, while goal-driven questions remain extremely challenging for all machine models, with per-question accuracy often below 20%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ContPRO (oracle) decisively outperforms all non-oracle baselines on most question types, with the largest margins on property inference (e.g., 78.0% vs. 51.3% for PhyDNet on Fluid Property) and predictive questions (e.g., 90.1% vs. 53.8% for ALPRO on Fluid P-Opt). Among visual models, ALPRO benefits from large-scale video-text pre-training and leads its family, while MAC and HCRN show competitive but inconsistent results. Physical models PhyDNet and PIP excel in specific settings (PhyDNet on rope/ball counterfactual; PIP on fluid goal-driven) but struggle with cloth and fluid property questions. MLLMs (GPT-4V, Gemini) perform best on rope and ball scenarios where objects (cubes, spheres) are more familiar, but drop sharply on fluid and cloth (e.g., GPT-4V scores only 25.0% on Fluid Property vs. 78.0% for ContPRO). Goal-driven questions are the hardest for all machine models—per-question accuracy rarely exceeds 60% even for ContPRO—while humans achieve 64–92%, highlighting a significant gap in reverse physical reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the physical reasoning benchmark lineage (CLEVRER, Physion, ComPhy, CRIPP-VQA) by shifting from rigid-body primitives with fixed parameters to continuum objects—fluids, deformable cloths, ropes, and plastoelastic balls—whose physical properties (density, elasticity, plasticity, tension) vary as free variables. The ContPRO oracle model synthesizes two previously separate threads: particle-based dynamic simulation (MPM, DPI-Net) and LLM-as-program-parser (following ViperGPT), creating a neuro-symbolic pipeline where ChatGPT translates natural-language queries into executable code over a physical simulator. Unlike prior benchmarks that fix object parameters or ask only binary contact-prediction questions, ContPhy introduces property-inference, counterfactual, goal-driven, and predictive question types over a unified continuum setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a concrete and large gap: even GPT-4V and Gemini perform near-random on fluid and cloth property/dynamics questions, while humans score 75–94%, indicating that current foundation models lack physical intuition for deformable and fluid materials specifically. The ContPRO oracle demonstrates that marrying LLMs with differentiable particle-based simulators (MPM) can close much of this gap, offering a blueprint for future physical-reasoning architectures. Key limitations include restricted language diversity (template-generated questions), only four scenario types, and the oracle's dependence on privileged particle-level access, leaving open whether end-to-end models can achieve comparable performance from raw video alone.
