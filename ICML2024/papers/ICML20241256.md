## Problem

Image Aesthetics Assessment (IAA) datasets exhibit severe long-tailed distributions that bias models toward majority (mid-score) samples, yet existing long-tail solutions from classification tasks are inapplicable due to IAA's lack of well-defined categories, feature-label mismatch from annotation subjectivity, and characteristically smooth predicted distributions that defeat standard confidence-based pseudo-labeling. This is the first work to systematically address long-tail imbalance specifically for aesthetics-oriented models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, processed by a Swin Transformer V2 backbone, and outputs a probability mass function (PMF) over C discrete aesthetic quality levels (e.g., 1–10 for AVA/TAD66K, 1–5 for AADB/PARA). The scalar Mean Opinion Score is derived as the expected value of this distribution. The method is designed as a plug-and-play enhancer that can be attached to any existing IAA backbone without architectural modification.

## Method

ELTA comprises three loosely-coupled modules. (1) Tail Features Augmentation (TFA) performs feature-level mixup at an intermediate network layer using a two-stage sampling strategy: the first stage biases sample selection toward minority instances (large score deviation from batch mean) via a softmax over |s_i − s̄|, and the second stage pairs aesthetically similar samples; the mixing factor λ is score-proportional rather than drawn from a Beta distribution. (2) Feature-Label Similarity Alignment (FLSA) computes a cosine-similarity matrix over batch features and a label-similarity matrix from normalized score differences, then adds an MSE alignment loss as a regularizer to the supervised EMD loss, pulling feature geometry toward label geometry. (3) Adaptive Probability Distribution Sharpening (APDS) applies per-sample temperature scaling where τ_i = exp(−β|ŝ_i − s̄|) exponentially sharpens minority logits while leaving majority logits smooth, combined with a pseudo-label threshold t*; both β and t* are jointly optimized via grid search minimizing MAE on a small labeled validation subset. Training proceeds in a self-training loop: supervised pre-training, pseudo-labeling of unlabeled data via APDS, then joint optimization with labeled and pseudo-labeled losses.

## Results

On AVA, ELTA achieves PLCC 0.777 and SRCC 0.764, with minority MAE of 0.438 (low) and 0.426 (high), outperforming all 7 IAA baselines. On PARA, ELTA reaches PLCC 0.943 and SRCC 0.912 with the lowest MAE across all segments (L=0.327, M=0.251, H=0.290). On TAD66K, ELTA slightly trails EAT in overall correlation (PLCC 0.539 vs. 0.546) due to lacking theme awareness, but still achieves the best minority MAE (L=1.457, H=1.162). Across all four datasets, ELTA consistently achieves the lowest MAE in both the low and high score segments, confirming effective mitigation of long-tail bias.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELTA outperforms all seven IAA baselines (NIMA, HGCN, BIAA, TANet, MaxViT, MUSIQ, EAT) on PLCC and SRCC across AVA, AADB, and PARA, with the largest margins over NIMA (e.g., +0.141 PLCC on AVA) and HGCN (+0.085 PLCC on AVA). The closest competitor is EAT, which slightly edges ELTA on TAD66K overall correlation (PLCC 0.546 vs. 0.539, SRCC 0.517 vs. 0.496) due to its theme-aware design, yet ELTA still achieves substantially lower minority MAE on TAD66K (L: 1.457 vs. 1.591; H: 1.162 vs. 1.175). Against the two deep imbalanced regression (DIR) baselines, ELTA dominates across all four datasets: on AVA it improves PLCC by +0.025 over L&FDS (0.777 vs. 0.752) and +0.018 over RankSim (0.777 vs. 0.759), while reducing low-segment MAE from 0.588/0.542 to 0.438. The plug-and-play experiments (Table 3) further show that adding ELTA's modules to any of the seven IAA methods consistently improves PLCC, SRCC, and minority MAE, confirming its generality as an enhancement layer rather than a standalone model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the mixup augmentation technique (Zhang et al., 2017) from pixel-level to feature-level interpolation, introducing a score-deviation-guided sampling strategy and a minority-weighted mixing factor to address long-tailed IAA. It draws on the feature-label similarity alignment principle from RankSim (Gong et al., 2022) and repurposes temperature scaling from knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) for adaptive distribution sharpening in pseudo-label selection. The paper positions itself as the first work to systematically address long-tail imbalance in IAA, arguing that standard long-tail solutions (re-sampling, re-weighting, pixel-level augmentation) are fundamentally inapplicable due to IAA's lack of well-defined categories, feature-label mismatch, and smooth output distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying three IAA-specific obstacles to long-tail mitigation (ambiguous category boundaries, feature-label confusion, and smooth predicted distributions), the paper provides a conceptual framework that explains why existing imbalanced-learning methods fail in aesthetics and guides future work. The plug-and-play design, validated across seven architectures and four datasets, makes ELTA practically adoptable for any IAA pipeline. A key limitation acknowledged is the slight trade-off in majority performance, and the paper leaves open the question of whether minority gains can be achieved without any majority degradation. The work also highlights that annotator bias toward mid-range scores is an inherent, dataset-agnostic source of long-tail that cannot be eliminated at the data level.
