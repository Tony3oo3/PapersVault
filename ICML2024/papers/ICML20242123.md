## Problem

Existing non-asymptotic analyses of actor-critic (AC) and natural actor-critic (NAC) with fixed function approximation incur a non-diminishing error term ε_critic, and the best single-loop NAC analysis achieves only O(ε^{-6}) sample complexity (tabular case) versus O(ε^{-3}) for nested-loop variants. This paper eliminates ε_critic entirely by using compatible function approximation while closing the single-loop NAC gap to O(ε^{-3}), all under the challenging setting of a single Markovian sample trajectory without i.i.d. or nested-loop assumptions.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a single online Markovian trajectory from a general MDP and a parameterized policy class π_ω, with the critic using k-step TD and eligibility traces over the policy-dependent compatible feature φ_ω = ∇_ω log π_ω(a|s). The actor and critic are updated simultaneously in a single loop with two-timescale stepsizes (γ ≥ α ≥ β), and the critic parameter is projected onto a bounded ball. The primary outputs are tight non-asymptotic convergence bounds: AC reaches an ε-stationary point in O(ε^{-2}) samples, and NAC reaches an ε + √ε_actor neighborhood of the globally optimal policy in O(ε^{-3}) samples, both without the ε_critic term present in prior work.

## Method

The core technique is compatible function approximation, where the critic's linear feature is set to the policy score function φ_ω = ∇_ω log π_ω(a|s), making the feature time-varying as the actor updates ω. This eliminates the critic's approximation error and, for NAC, causes the Fisher information matrix to cancel algebraically in the natural gradient, removing the need for its expensive estimation. The major technical challenge is bounding the critic's tracking error when both the feature function and the sampling distribution change at every step. The authors develop a novel auxiliary Markov chain decomposition that separates the tracking error into three components: error from the time-varying compatible feature (bounded via Lipschitz continuity), error from the time-varying sampling policy, and error from the gap between the actual and stationary distributions (bounded via uniform ergodicity). They then bound the tracking error as a function of the policy gradient norm (AC) or the optimality gap (NAC), and conversely bound the gradient or optimality gap as a function of the tracking error, applying these bounds recursively to obtain the tightest possible convergence rates without the constant ε_critic floor.

## Results

For the AC algorithm with compatible function approximation, the paper proves convergence to an ε-stationary point with sample complexity O(ε^{-2}) in the single-loop, single-Markovian-trajectory setting, eliminating the non-diminishing ε_critic term present in all prior bounds. For the NAC algorithm, the paper achieves convergence to an ε+√ε_actor neighborhood of the globally optimal policy with sample complexity O(ε^{-3}), also in the single-loop setting—closing the O(ε^{-3}) gap that previously separated single-loop NAC (best O(ε^{-6})) from nested-loop NAC (O(ε^{-3})). Both results match the best known sample complexities while strictly improving the error bounds by removing ε_critic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary advantage over all 18 compared baselines is the elimination of the non-diminishing ε_critic term from the error bound, which is present in every prior work using fixed (policy-independent) function approximation in the critic. For AC, the paper matches the best known single-loop sample complexity of O(ε^{-2}) (achieved by Olshevsky & Gharesifard 2023 and Chen et al. 2021) but with a strictly tighter error of ε versus ε+ε_critic. For NAC, the paper closes a significant gap: the best prior single-loop NAC result (Khodadadian et al. 2022, tabular case) required O(ε^{-6}) samples, while nested-loop methods (Xu et al. 2020a, Chen et al. 2022) achieved O(ε^{-3}) but with error ε+ε_critic+√ε_actor; this paper achieves O(ε^{-3}) in the single-loop setting with the reduced error ε+√ε_actor. Additionally, for NAC, compatible function approximation eliminates the need to estimate and invert the Fisher information matrix (Proposition 2), providing a computational advantage over all baselines that require explicit Fisher matrix estimation. The paper also does not require the smoothness assumption on the parameterized policy (Assumptions 4–5 in Assumption 2) for the NAC analysis, unlike Chen et al. 2021 and Olshevsky & Gharesifard 2023.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the compatible function approximation framework of Sutton et al. (1999) and the natural policy gradient of Kakade (2001), combined with the two-timescale single-loop AC/NAC structure of Konda & Tsitsiklis (2003). It departs from all prior non-asymptotic analyses of AC/NAC (Xu et al. 2020, Chen et al. 2021, Olshevsky & Gharesifard 2023, etc.) which use fixed feature functions in the critic and consequently incur a non-vanishing ε_critic term. The key technical departure is handling the time-varying, policy-dependent feature φ_ω in the critic, which prior analyses could not address because the feature class changes at every actor update, requiring a novel auxiliary Markov chain decomposition to bound the tracking error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a significant gap in the RL theory literature by showing that single-loop NAC with a single Markovian trajectory achieves the same O(ε^{-3}) sample complexity as nested-loop variants, while eliminating the non-diminishing ε_critic from the error bound. Practically, compatible function approximation also removes the need to estimate and invert the Fisher information matrix in NAC, reducing computational overhead. Limitations include the restriction to average-reward MDPs, linear compatible features, and a single trajectory; extending to discounted settings, nonlinear function classes, or multi-trajectory settings remains open. The analysis also requires the policy to be sufficiently exploratory (Assumption 3), which may not hold in practice without additional regularization.
