## Problem

Fine-tuning pre-trained foundation models for semi-supervised learning introduces two previously unexplored pathologies: aggregated biases, where the model's inherent class imbalances from pre-training are amplified during pseudo-label selection, and cognitive deviation, where the model's confidence scores fail to reflect true learning difficulty or distinguish correct from incorrect predictions. While foundation models have been applied to SSL, no prior work identified or addressed these specific failure modes that degrade pseudo-label quality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of labeled RGB images (1–100 per class depending on dataset) and a large pool of unlabeled images, producing a single class label per image. During training, each unlabeled image is processed through both weak and strong augmentation pipelines to compute consistency losses, and the auxiliary classifier outputs a continuous pseudo-label vector used to compute per-sample importance weights. The backbone is a frozen CLIP ViT with only 50 learnable prompt tokens per layer (VPT-deep) plus a linear classifier, keeping trainable parameters minimal.

## Method

FINESSL introduces two core mechanisms on top of a VPT-fine-tuned CLIP ViT. First, the Balanced Margin Softmax (BMS) replaces standard cross-entropy with a loss that imposes class-specific positive margins on non-target logits, where each margin Δ_k^t = 1 − σ_t(k)/max_k σ_t(k) is computed from the learning pace (count of confident pseudo-labels per class) and scaled by a dynamically adjusted α_t that grows with the spread of normalized learning paces. Second, Decoupled Label Smoothing (DLS) trains a separate auxiliary linear classifier with label smoothing (λ=0.5) whose gradients are detached from the shared feature extractor, preventing smoothing from corrupting representations; this auxiliary branch produces calibrated confidence scores that reweight all unlabeled samples in the main branch's consistency loss, replacing hard thresholding with soft importance weighting. The total loss combines BMS-based supervised and reweighted consistency losses for the main branch with standard supervised and smoothed consistency losses for the auxiliary branch.

## Results

FINESSL achieves state-of-the-art top-1 accuracy across five datasets. On CIFAR-10 with only 1 labeled sample per class (N1), FINESSL reaches 96.15%, outperforming the next-best method DebiasPL (78.30%) by nearly 18 points. On CIFAR-100 N4, FINESSL scores 80.44% vs. 79.57% for DebiasPL and 77.10% for FixMatch. Training cost is reduced by over 6× (9,630 s vs. 55,574 s for FixMatch with Wide ResNet from scratch).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FINESSL dominates in extreme low-label regimes: on CIFAR-10 N1 it reaches 96.15% vs. 78.30% for DebiasPL (the closest competitor) and ~60% for FixMatch/FlexMatch/FreeMatch/SoftMatch. On CIFAR-100 N4, FINESSL (80.44%) beats DebiasPL (79.57%) by 0.87% and FixMatch (77.10%) by 3.34%. On FOOD-101 N2, FINESSL (87.04%) surpasses DebiasPL (86.51%) by 0.53% and FlexMatch (80.96%) by 6.08%. On the harder Semi-Aves dataset with OOD samples, FINESSL (61.12%) edges out DebiasPL (60.85%) and FixMatch (60.15%). On ImageNet 1% labeled, FINESSL (74.22%) leads DebiasPL (73.58%) by 0.64%. The one near-tie is CIFAR-10 N4 where FINESSL (97.39%) is essentially matched by FixMatch (97.43%) and FlexMatch (97.41%), which the authors attribute to the task being too simple for the bias issues to manifest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: modern pseudo-label-based SSL (FixMatch, FlexMatch, FreeMatch, SoftMatch) and parameter-efficient fine-tuning of foundation models (VPT, LoRA, Adapter). It departs from prior SSL methods that handle class imbalance by dynamically adjusting confidence thresholds (FlexMatch, FreeMatch, SoftMatch) by instead modifying the loss function directly via a balanced margin softmax, drawing on class-imbalanced learning techniques (logit adjustment, label-distribution-aware margin loss). The closest prior work, DebiasPL, also leverages CLIP for SSL but only uses it for pseudo-labeling discarded samples rather than as the primary backbone, and does not address the overconfidence problem. The paper's key conceptual contribution is identifying two distinct failure modes—aggregated biases (amplified class imbalance) and cognitive deviation (unreliable confidence)—that arise specifically when fine-tuning foundation models for SSL, rather than training from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6× reduction in training cost (30 epochs × 500 steps vs. 1024×1024 steps) makes SSL with foundation models practically deployable in resource-constrained settings, and the framework's modularity (swappable PEFT strategies and SSL algorithms) lowers the barrier for practitioners. However, the paper only uses CLIP's image encoder, leaving open whether the text encoder or other pre-training paradigms (SimCLR, MoCo) would yield different bias patterns. The finding that decoupled label smoothing improves performance while *worsening* calibration (ECE) suggests that confidence calibration is not the right lens for pseudo-label quality, an open question that could reshape how SSL practitioners think about threshold selection.
