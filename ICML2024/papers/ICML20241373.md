## Problem

Existing federated learning methods address in-federation data heterogeneity but fail catastrophically when deployed on out-of-federation (OOF) clients whose distributions were never seen during training. Recent OOF generalization approaches—client augmentation and adversarial distribution alignment—either incur prohibitive communication or computation costs at scale or sacrifice generalization quality, revealing a fundamental tradeoff between OOF robustness and scalability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241373/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes distributed client data (EHR medication records, brain MRI scans, monthly temperature series, or RGB images) and the resulting model weights, which serve a dual role as both trainable parameters and node embeddings for topology inference. A key design choice is that model weights are shared among clients for topology learning, avoiding the need to centralize raw data and preserving privacy. The output is a single global model producing task-specific predictions—classification accuracy, Dice Similarity Coefficient, or MSE—evaluated on clients outside the training federation.

## Method

TFL alternates between two steps: Client Topology Learning (CTL) and Learning on Client Topology (LCT). CTL constructs an ε-graph over clients by computing pairwise model-weight similarity (dot product, cosine, ℓ₁, or ℓ₂) and applying hard thresholding for sparsity, requiring only model sharing rather than data centralization. LCT computes betweenness centrality on the learned graph to derive a topological prior p, then solves a constrained minimax optimization that balances worst-case risk against the influential-client prior via a distributional distance constraint D(λ‖p) ≤ τ. The constrained problem is converted to an unconstrained primal-dual form using a Lagrange multiplier q, solved by alternating gradient descent on θ and projected gradient ascent on λ over the probability simplex. FedAvg (uniform prior, τ=0) and DRFL (τ→∞) emerge as special cases, positioning TFL as a principled interpolation between average-case and worst-case robustness that prioritizes both the worst-case and the most representative clients.

## Results

On PACS (20 clients, leave-one-domain-out), TFL achieves 69.95% average OOF accuracy, outperforming FedAvg (67.40%), FedProx (68.23%), DRFA (68.41%), and FedSR (68.16%). On the eICU healthcare dataset (72 hospitals), TFL reaches 58.41% ROC-AUC in just 2 communication rounds with 10.44M communicated parameters, surpassing DRFA (57.20%) and FedSR (57.25%) while matching their communication efficiency. On FeTS (21 institutions), TFL achieves 74.29% DSC, a 2.17-point improvement over FedSR (72.85%) and a 3.14-point improvement over FedAvg (71.45%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TFL consistently outperforms all federated baselines across every evaluated dataset. Against DRFA (the strongest FL robust optimization baseline), TFL gains 2.25% on PACS, 2.11% ROC-AUC on eICU, 1.98% DSC on FeTS, and 2.2% MSE improvement on TPT-48. Against FedSR (the most recent OOF-specific FL method), TFL gains 2.63% on PACS, 2.03% on eICU, and 3.00% on FeTS. TFL also improves in-federation performance, beating DRFA by 2.5% on CIFAR-10 (72.24% vs 70.51% at 25 clients) and 0.81% on CIFAR-100 (38.85% vs 38.04%). On the scalability dimension, TFL matches DRFA's communication efficiency (2 rounds, 10.44M params on eICU) while achieving superior accuracy, and its wall-clock time on PACS is comparable to FedAvg and FedSR. Centralized methods (DGER, DIRT-GAN, Mixup, CORAL) still outperform all FL methods, as expected, but TFL narrows the gap most effectively among federated approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

TFL extends the distributionally robust federated learning (DRFL) framework of Deng et al. (2020) by introducing a topology-based constraint that prioritizes not only worst-case clients but also 'influential' clients identified via graph centrality. It departs from prior OOF generalization methods (FedDG, FedADG, FedSR) that rely on client augmentation or adversarial alignment, which the paper argues suffer from communication or computation bottlenecks at scale. The client topology learning module repurposes graph signal processing ideas (epsilon-graph construction, betweenness centrality) in a privacy-preserving way by using model weights as node embeddings rather than raw data, a connection to graph topology learning literature that had not been explored in the federated setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: FL models that perform well in-federation can fail catastrophically on unseen clients, a problem especially acute in healthcare and other multi-institutional deployments. The scalability result—that TFL achieves the best OOF accuracy with the fewest communication rounds (2 vs. 6–8 for baselines)—is a notable practical contribution, suggesting the topology constraint acts as a useful inductive bias that accelerates convergence. Open questions include how the O(N²) topology learning scales to cross-device settings with millions of clients (partially addressed via clustering), whether the model-similarity-to-data-similarity assumption holds for foundation models, and how the framework handles adversarial clients that deliberately corrupt their model updates.
