## Problem

Existing multivariate time series forecasting methods rely on untrainable moving average kernels for trend-seasonal decomposition, which apply uniform weights and fail to capture non-linear or noisy trends in real-world data. Additionally, prior approaches either model inter-series dependencies (correlations across variables) or intra-series variations (temporal patterns within each series) but struggle to do both simultaneously without introducing information loss through patching or point-wise attention.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{N×T} (N variates, T=96 look-back steps) and produces future F steps Ŷ ∈ R^{N×F} for F ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720}. Before decomposition, the input is linearly projected to an embedding dimension D with positional encoding, and the decomposition kernel operates on this embedded representation rather than raw values. The final prediction is the element-wise sum of a linearly projected trend component and a dual-attention-processed seasonal component.

## Method

Leddam introduces two key components. First, a Learnable Decomposition Module replaces the standard moving average kernel with a trainable 1D convolutional kernel (size K=25, stride 1) initialized via a Gaussian distribution (σ=1.0) so the center position receives maximum weight; this kernel adapts during training to capture non-linear trend structures. Second, a Dual Attention Module processes the seasonal component through two parallel branches: (a) channel-wise self-attention that embeds each entire variate as a single token (following iTransformer) to model inter-series dependencies, and (b) auto-regressive self-attention that generates tokens by iteratively cutting a length-L prefix and concatenating it to the end of the sequence, preserving all original information while simulating temporal dynamics; the raw sequence serves as Q while the full auto-regressive tokens serve as K and V. The trend component is projected by a simple linear layer, and the final forecast is the sum of the trend and seasonal outputs.

## Results

Leddam achieves the best MSE on 7 out of 8 datasets and best MAE on 6 out of 8, with the sole MSE loss on Traffic (0.467 vs. iTransformer's 0.428). Headline results include MSE of 0.373 on ETTh2, 0.169 on Electricity, 0.281 on ETTm2, and 0.242 on Weather (all averaged over F∈{96,192,336,720}, T=96). The method also demonstrates strong generalizability: plugging the learnable decomposition module into other architectures yields 11.87%–48.56% MSE reduction, with LSTM showing up to 80.28% improvement on ETTm2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Leddam outperforms all 12 baselines on MSE across 7 of 8 datasets, with the only loss being Traffic where iTransformer achieves 0.428 vs. Leddam's 0.467. Against the closest competitor iTransformer (Liu et al., 2024), Leddam wins on ETTh1 (0.431 vs. 0.454), ETTh2 (0.373 vs. 0.383), ETTm1 (0.386 vs. 0.407), ETTm2 (0.281 vs. 0.288), Electricity (0.169 vs. 0.178), Solar-Energy (0.230 vs. 0.233), and Weather (0.242 vs. 0.258). Against PatchTST (Nie et al., 2023), Leddam wins on all 8 datasets, with the largest margin on Electricity (0.169 vs. 0.216) and ETTh2 (0.373 vs. 0.387). Against DLinear (Zeng et al., 2023), Leddam wins on all 8 datasets, with particularly large gaps on ETTh2 (0.373 vs. 0.559) and ETTm2 (0.281 vs. 0.350). Crossformer (Zhang & Yan, 2023) and SCINet (Liu et al., 2022) are consistently the weakest baselines, with Crossformer failing catastrophically on ETTm2 (MSE 1.219) and ETTh2 (0.942). In the generalization study (Table 6), the learnable decomposition module plugged into LSTM yields 48.56% average MSE reduction, into Informer 31.72%, into Transformer 26.27%, into SCINet 23.15%, and into LightTS 11.87%, confirming broad applicability beyond the full Leddam architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the trend-seasonal decomposition paradigm introduced by Autoformer (Wu et al., 2021) by replacing the fixed moving average kernel with a learnable 1D convolutional kernel initialized with a Gaussian distribution, and builds on iTransformer's (Liu et al., 2024) channel-wise self-attention for inter-series dependency modeling. It departs from patch-based approaches (PatchTST, Crossformer) that sacrifice information through segmentation, and from point-wise attention methods that lack temporal semantics, by proposing an auto-regressive token generation scheme that preserves the full sequence while simulating dynamic temporal variations. The dual attention module unifies two previously separate modeling concerns—inter-series dependencies and intra-series variations—into a single architecture, addressing a gap where iTransformer handles the former but not the latter, and PatchTST handles the latter but not the former.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The learnable decomposition module's plug-and-play nature (yielding 11.87%–48.56% MSE reduction when inserted into heterogeneous architectures like LSTM, SCINet, and Informer) makes it a broadly applicable component for the time series forecasting community, potentially simplifying the design of future models. However, the main SOTA improvements over iTransformer remain modest (e.g., 0.431 vs. 0.454 MSE on ETTh1), and the model still loses to iTransformer on the Traffic dataset, suggesting the dual attention module's advantage is dataset-dependent. Open questions include whether the Gaussian initialization is truly essential versus other smooth priors, how the auto-regressive token generation scales to very long look-back windows, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the standard 8-dataset benchmark to truly non-stationary or high-frequency industrial data.
