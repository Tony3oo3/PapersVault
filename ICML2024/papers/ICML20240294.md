## Problem

While LLMs frequently produce factual errors, the mechanistic reasons behind hallucinations remain poorly understood from the perspective of inner representations. Existing mitigation approaches (retrieval-augmented generation, fine-tuning, self-evaluation) rely on external knowledge bases or additional training, limiting their applicability to domain-specific or resource-constrained settings. This paper asks whether the hidden states of in-context tokens already encode a signal that distinguishes factually correct from incorrect generations, and whether that signal can be exploited at inference time without any external resources.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt (e.g., a question) as input and produces a generated text answer, operating entirely at inference time with no training or fine-tuning. A key implementation detail is that contextual entropy values are pre-computed for all ~32,000 vocabulary tokens against the prompt tokens before generation begins, yielding a fixed entropy vector that is looked up during decoding rather than recomputed per step. The method is applied to both open-ended generation (TriviaQA, HotpotQA, NQ, TruthfulQA) and multiple-choice settings, and is compatible with any downstream decoding algorithm (greedy, beam search).

## Method

The core insight is that candidate tokens corresponding to correct answers exhibit significantly sharper (lower-entropy) activation patterns across in-context token positions at intermediate transformer layers (e.g., layer 26 of 32 in LLaMA-2-7B) compared to incorrect tokens. Formally, for each candidate token v_p, the method computes its activation score s(i, v_p) = softmax(W x_i^l)_{v_p} at every in-context position i using the LM head projection at a selected informative layer l, normalizes these scores into a probability distribution via softmax, and computes the resulting entropy as the contextual entropy E(v_p, v_{1:t}). During decoding, the original next-token probability is multiplied by exp(-λ E(v_p, v_{1:t})), where λ ∈ [0,1] controls the strength of the entropy penalty, thereby boosting tokens with sharper in-context activations. A filtering step (threshold 0.1 on the original probability) restricts adjustment to high-probability candidates, and only prompt tokens (not previously generated tokens) are used in the entropy computation to avoid deductive hallucination. This pre-computation strategy reduces inference overhead to a 23.4% latency increase over greedy decoding while outperforming DoLa by 7.3% in speed.

## Results

On TruthfulQA (open-ended), Activation Decoding achieves the best Truth*Info scores across all model sizes: 59.1% (LLaMA2-7B-chat, +3.3 over greedy), 62.3% (13B, +4.8), and 55.7% (70B, +8.6). On knowledge-seeking benchmarks, the method yields F1 improvements of up to 4.8 points (TriviaQA-70B), 4.7 points (HotpotQA-13B), and 3.7 points (NQ-70B) over greedy decoding. The method also reduces inference time by 7.3% compared to DoLa while adding only 23.4% latency over greedy decoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DoLa (Chuang et al., 2024), Activation Decoding achieves higher Truth*Info on TruthfulQA for all three model sizes (59.1 vs. 58.5 for 7B; 62.3 vs. 60.0 for 13B; 55.7 vs. 54.3 for 70B) and also outperforms DoLa on F1 for knowledge-seeking tasks (e.g., 30.1 vs. 29.0 on HotpotQA-70B, 37.8 vs. 36.2 on NQ-70B). Against ITI (Li et al., 2023a), the proposed method consistently wins on Truth*Info and F1, while ITI shows virtually no improvement over greedy decoding on the 13B and 70B models (identical scores in Table 3). Against self-evaluation (Kadavath et al., 2023), the logit+entropy metric achieves the highest AUROC (up to 0.76) for distinguishing correct from incorrect predictions, surpassing self-eval by several points. The hybrid Ours+DoLa combination further improves Truth*Info (e.g., 59.7 for 7B, 61.0 for 13B, 55.2 for 70B), confirming the two methods capture complementary hallucination signals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the mechanistic interpretability line of work showing that LLM hidden states encode factual knowledge (Geva et al., 2023; Ram et al., 2023a) and on constrained decoding methods for factuality (DoLa, ITI, Repe). It departs from DoLa's layer-contrast approach and ITI's direction-vector intervention by instead exploiting the *spatial* pattern of a candidate token's activations across all in-context positions, quantified via entropy. The key conceptual shift is treating in-context sharpness as a factuality signal rather than relying on inter-layer logit differences or trained classifiers, and the pre-computation strategy makes it more efficient than DoLa's per-step layer comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a training-free, plug-and-play decoding strategy that is complementary to DoLa (their combination outperforms either alone), making it a practical tool for hallucination mitigation in deployed LLMs. However, the authors acknowledge it only addresses model-internal hallucinations where knowledge exists in hidden states but fails to be elicited, and cannot fix errors from missing or outdated training data. Open questions include whether the in-context sharpness signal generalizes to much larger models, different architectures, or long-context settings, and whether the performance ceiling observed (correcting some errors introduces new ones) can be broken.
