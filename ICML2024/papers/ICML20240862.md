## Problem

In domain incremental learning, experience replay mitigates catastrophic forgetting but does not explicitly address the abrupt representation drift that occurs at task boundaries, where previously learned feature clusters shift as the model adapts to new domains. This drift is directly correlated with accuracy drops on old tasks, yet existing drift-mitigation methods from class-incremental learning (e.g., Caccia et al., 2022) rely on separating seen and new classes, making them inapplicable to DIL where all tasks share the same class set.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (64×64 for DN4IL, 32×32 for iCIFAR-20) and outputs class labels over a fixed set of 100 (DN4IL) or 20 (iCIFAR-20) classes that remain constant across all tasks. A bounded memory buffer stores a subset of past samples along with their labels and the logits from both classifiers, enabling logit-based distillation of 'dark knowledge' during subsequent task training. The buffer is populated via Intermediary Reservoir Sampling, which biases storage toward intermediate training epochs to capture more informative logits.

## Method

DARE employs a ResNet-18 encoder with two linear classifiers trained with complementary objectives—cross-entropy on f₁ and supervised contrastive loss on f₂—to enforce diverse decision mechanisms. For each new task, training cycles through three stages: (1) Divergence freezes the encoder and maximizes the discrepancy between the two classifiers' predictions on new-task samples, tightening decision boundaries around old-task representations; (2) Adaptation freezes the classifiers and minimizes their discrepancy, forcing the encoder to project new-task representations into the subspace spanned by old-task features; (3) Refinement unfreezes all parameters and trains with standard classification losses plus a consistency loss on buffered samples. This adversarial-style alternating procedure gradually assimilates new domain information without disrupting existing representations, and the Intermediary Reservoir Sampling strategy populates the buffer with logits from intermediate training stages to maximize distilled dark knowledge.

## Results

On the challenging DN4IL dataset (6 domains, 100 classes) with a buffer size of 50, DARE achieves 32.32% last accuracy and −22.98 BWT, outperforming DER++ (28.08%, −29.05) by 15.1% in accuracy and ER (24.24%, −36.11) by 33.3%. With a larger buffer of 200, DARE reaches 40.59% last accuracy and −14.69 BWT on DN4IL, and 58.53% / −30.22 on iCIFAR-20. The extended DARE++ variant (with EMA semantic memory) achieves 44.11% on DN4IL and 65.79% on iCIFAR-20 at buffer 200, matching or exceeding multi-memory baselines CLS-ER and DUCA while using fewer parameters (22.38M vs. 33.57M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DARE consistently outperforms the single-model rehearsal baselines ER and DER++ across all buffer sizes and both datasets. On DN4IL with buffer 50, DARE gains +8.08% last accuracy over ER and +4.24% over DER++, while also improving BWT by 13.13 and 6.07 points respectively. Against the multi-memory baselines CLS-ER and DUCA, the single-model DARE trails by a few points (e.g., 32.32 vs. 37.90/38.91 on DN4IL buffer 50), but DARE++ (with EMA) closes or reverses this gap—achieving 40.51% on DN4IL buffer 50 (beating both CLS-ER and DUCA) and 44.11% at buffer 200 (nearly matching DUCA's 44.45%) while using only 22.38M parameters versus 33.57M. In the extended comparison (Table 4), DARE also surpasses regularization-based methods (oEWC, SI) and other replay-based methods (A-GEM, FDR) by a wide margin on both datasets. Notably, DARE with buffer 50 on DN4IL (32.32%) already exceeds DER++ with buffer 100 (32.06%), indicating that the three-stage training process is more sample-efficient than simply increasing replay capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

DARE repurposes the dual-classifier maximum classifier discrepancy framework from unsupervised domain adaptation (Saito et al., 2018) into a continual learning context, orchestrating it into a three-stage (Divergence–Adaptation–Refinement) training loop that gradually pulls new-domain representations into the subspace spanned by old-domain representations. The discrepancy loss formulation is taken from Tan et al. (2022), the second classifier uses supervised contrastive loss (Khosla et al., 2020), and the buffer mechanism storing logits alongside exemplars follows DER++ (Buzzega et al., 2020). The key departure from prior work is the explicit targeting of representation drift in DIL—where classes are shared across tasks—rather than in CIL where class separation enables simpler isolation strategies (Caccia et al., 2022), and the introduction of Intermediary Reservoir Sampling to bias buffer storage toward intermediate-training checkpoints that carry more 'dark knowledge.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic analysis of representation drift specifically in the DIL setting, showing that abrupt drift at task boundaries directly correlates with accuracy drops on old tasks, and demonstrates that a three-stage adversarial-style training loop can suppress this drift without requiring task-id at inference. The practical relevance is strong for safety-critical applications (autonomous driving, robotics) where forgetting old domain knowledge is unacceptable, and the single-model DARE++ matching multi-memory systems (CLS-ER, DUCA) with roughly half the parameters is a meaningful efficiency gain. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond ResNet-18 and two datasets, whether the task-id dependency in IRS can be removed (as the authors acknowledge), and whether the three-stage schedule transfers to other continual learning paradigms such as class-incremental or task-free settings.
