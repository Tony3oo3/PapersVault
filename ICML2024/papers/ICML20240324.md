## Problem

Deep reinforcement learning agents frequently hit training bottlenecks where performance plateaus at a local optimum, particularly in sparse-reward environments. Existing refining strategies either overfit by resetting exclusively to identified critical states (StateMask-R) or rely on random exploration frontiers that cannot guarantee positive returns (JSRL), and retraining from scratch is prohibitively expensive for complex tasks.

## Input / Output

The method requires a pre-trained DRL policy (trained by any algorithm, e.g., PPO or SAC), an environment simulator that supports state reset, and a trained mask network that scores state importance. It produces a refined policy with improved expected reward, evaluated via final episode reward (dense-reward tasks) or success probability (e.g., malware evasion rate). The pre-trained policy need not be PPO-based; the paper demonstrates generality by refining a SAC agent via a GAIL-approximated policy network.

## Method

RICE combines three components: (1) a simplified StateMask alternative that reformulates the mask-training objective from min|η(π)−η(π̄)| to max η(π̄) with an additive blinding bonus α·a_t^m, enabling vanilla PPO training instead of the original prime-dual optimization while preserving theoretical guarantees; (2) a mixed initial state distribution μ(s) = β·d_ρ^π̂(s) + (1−β)·ρ(s) that blends explanation-identified critical states with default initial states, preventing the overfitting observed when resetting solely to critical states; and (3) Random Network Distillation (RND) as an intrinsic exploration bonus added to the task reward during PPO-based refining, encouraging the agent to expand state coverage from the mixed frontiers. The theoretical analysis shows that the mask-based sampling is equivalent to sampling from a better policy π̂, and that the mixed distribution yields a tighter sub-optimality bound O(ε/(1−γ)² · ‖d_ρ^{π*}/d_ρ^π‖_∞) compared to random-visited-state baselines.

## Results

RICE achieves the highest final reward across all eight evaluated applications after refining. On MuJoCo games, RICE improves Hopper from 3559.44 to 3663.91, Walker2d from 3768.79 to 3982.79, and HalfCheetah from 2024.09 to 2138.89. On real-world applications, the gains are more dramatic: Selfish Mining improves from 14.36 to 16.56 (+15.3%), CAGE Challenge 2 from -23.64 to -20.02, Autonomous Driving from 10.30 to 17.03 (+65.3%), and Malware Mutation evasion probability from 42.20% to 57.53% (+36.3%). The mask network also trains 16.8% faster than the original StateMask while maintaining comparable fidelity scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RICE outperforms all four refining baselines (PPO fine-tuning, JSRL, StateMask-R, and the original StateMask refining) across all eight applications. PPO fine-tuning provides only marginal gains (e.g., Hopper +79.31 vs. RICE's +104.47) because it cannot escape local optima. StateMask-R (fine-tuning solely from critical states) sometimes *degrades* performance (e.g., Auto Driving drops from 10.30 to 7.62) due to overfitting, while RICE's mixed initial distribution avoids this. JSRL performs competitively on MuJoCo but lags significantly on real-world tasks (e.g., Auto Driving: 11.26 vs. RICE's 17.03). On the explanation side, RICE's mask network matches or slightly exceeds the original StateMask fidelity while training 16.8% faster, and both substantially outperform random explanation (e.g., Malware Mutation: 57.53 vs. 48.60 for random). RICE also beats Self-Imitation Learning (SIL) on all four MuJoCo tasks (Table 5) and outperforms Integrated Gradients and AIRS as explanation backends (Table 6).

## Relation to Prior Work

RICE builds directly on StateMask (Cheng et al., 2023), a step-level explanation method that identifies critical states in DRL trajectories, but departs from its original refining strategy (StateMask-R) which fine-tunes exclusively from critical states and suffers from overfitting. The paper also draws on Random Network Distillation (Burda et al., 2018) as an exploration bonus mechanism, and positions itself against JSRL (Uchendu et al., 2023) and PPO++ (Chang et al., 2023), which use random or uninformative state selections for exploration frontiers. The key conceptual departure is combining explanation-informed critical state identification with a mixed initial distribution and intrinsic-motivation-based exploration, rather than relying on fine-tuning or random curriculum selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RICE offers a practical, theoretically grounded approach to improving pre-trained DRL agents without the prohibitive cost of retraining from scratch, which is particularly relevant for safety-critical applications (autonomous driving, cyber defense) where agents are developed in simulators. The paper's main limitation is its warm-start requirement: the method assumes the pre-trained policy already covers the optimal policy's state distribution, and it degrades to plain RND in cold-start settings. Open questions include how to filter critical states where the agent has already converged (reducing wasted computation), whether the approach extends to non-simulator environments where exact state reset is infeasible, and whether the mixed-distribution principle generalizes to multi-agent or hierarchical RL settings.
