## Problem

Nonsmooth sparsity constrained optimization (minimizing f(x)+h(Ax−b) subject to ‖x‖₀≤s) is NP-hard and arises in robust regression, SVMs, and sparse logistic regression. Existing methods suffer from three limitations: they cannot handle general nonsmooth objectives (PALM requires closed-form proximals, IHT methods assume smoothness, PSGD requires Lipschitz continuity), they yield only weaker Lipschitz stationary points rather than stronger block-k stationary points, and they lack comprehensive convergence analysis to approximate global optima.

## Input / Output

The method requires a matrix A∈R^{m×n}, vector b∈R^m, sparsity level s, a smooth convex function f, a convex nonsmooth function h whose proximal operator P_μ(c) is efficiently computable, and an initial feasible point x¹ with ‖x¹‖₀≤s. It produces a sparse solution vector x with ‖x‖₀≤s that approximates the global optimum, with a convergence rate of Õ(1/t) plus an irreducible error term L_F‖x̄‖ that depends on the Lipschitz constant of the objective and the ℓ₂ norm of the true optimum. The smoothing parameter μ is either held constant or decayed as η/(t+t₀) to balance constraint satisfaction against optimization progress.

## Method

SPGM reformulates the constrained problem by introducing an auxiliary variable y and a smoothing parameter μ that penalizes the squared constraint violation ‖Ax−b−y‖²/(2μ), yielding a smooth objective J(x,y;μ) amenable to proximal alternating linearized minimization. The x-subproblem is solved via one of two strategies: SPGM-IHT uses a majorized quadratic upper bound with a scalar Hessian H^t = A_s²/μ^t + M_s + θ and projects onto the ℓ₀ ball via hard thresholding, while SPGM-BCD uses a matrix Hessian H^t = (AᵀA+θ₁I)/μ^t + M̃ + θ₂I and performs a local combinatorial search over a randomly selected working set of k coordinates, solving 2^k linear equations to identify the best block update. The y-subproblem is solved exactly via the proximal operator P_μ(Ax^{t+1}−b). The key theoretical contribution is proving that SPGM-BCD converges to block-k stationary points (strictly stronger than Lipschitz stationary points) and establishing Õ(1/t) convergence rates to approximate global optima for both variants, with irreducible error terms matching the best-known bounds from PSGD.

## Results

The paper establishes that both SPGM-IHT and SPGM-BCD converge to approximate global optimal solutions at a rate of Õ(1/t) + L_F‖x̄‖ (for IHT) or Õ(1/t) + 3L_F‖x̄‖ (for BCD), where L_F is the Lipschitz constant and ‖x̄‖ is the ℓ₂ norm of the global optimum. The irreducible error term L_F‖x̄‖ for SPGM-IHT precisely matches the best-known PSGD bound, while SPGM-BCD's 3L_F‖x̄‖ is three times that bound. Both methods find an ε-approximate stationary point in O(ε⁻¹) iterations. Numerically, SPGM-IHT performs comparably to existing IHT-style methods, while SPGM-BCD consistently delivers the best performance across two nonsmooth sparsity constrained optimization tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PSGD (Liu et al., 2019), SPGM-IHT achieves the same irreducible error bound L_F‖x̄‖, matching the best-known rate for this nonconvex NP-hard problem, while additionally handling general nonsmooth h(·) without requiring Lipschitz continuity of h. Against DIHT (Yuan et al., 2020b), SPGM-IHT provides a full primal convergence rate (Õ(1/t) + L_F‖x̄‖) where DIHT only establishes a primal-dual gap rate O(1/β) without addressing primal convergence. Against ADMM-IHT (He & Yuan, 2012), CVX-ℓ₁ (Candes & Tao, 2005), and NCVX-ℓₚ (Xu et al., 2012), SPGM-BCD consistently delivers superior numerical performance, attributed to its ability to identify block-k stationary points—a strictly stronger optimality condition than the Lipschitz stationary points targeted by IHT-based methods. The paper's key advantage over all baselines is the combination of general nonsmooth handling (via smoothing), stronger optimality guarantees (block-k for BCD), and comprehensive global convergence analysis, which no single prior method provides simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads in sparse optimization: smoothing techniques (Chen, 2012; Moreau-Yosida approximation), proximal alternating minimization (PALM, Bolte et al., 2014), and sparse subproblem solvers (IHT from Blumensath & Davies, 2008; Block Coordinate Decomposition from Yuan et al., 2020a). It positions itself against a landscape where each existing method addresses only a subset of the problem: PALM handles smooth nonconvex objectives, PDM handles nonsmooth but lacks convergence rates, PSGD handles nonsmooth but requires Lipschitz continuity, and DIHT handles smooth problems only. SPGM's key conceptual departure is combining a smoothing parameter μ→0 with proximal gradient steps to handle general nonsmooth h(·) while retaining the IHT or BCD sparsity-enforcement mechanism, thereby simultaneously addressing the three identified gaps (generality, optimality strength, and convergence analysis).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first comprehensive convergence-to-approximate-global-optimum analysis for nonsmooth sparsity constrained optimization, with irreducible error terms matching the best-known PSGD bounds and leveraging the inherent sparsity (‖x̄‖ ≤ √s·‖x̄‖∞). The block-k stationary point guarantee for SPGM-BCD is a meaningful strengthening over the Lipschitz stationary points achieved by IHT-based methods, with practical confirmation that BCD consistently outperforms IHT-style baselines. Open questions include: the 3× larger irreducible error constant for SPGM-BCD versus PSGD, the reliance on restricted strong convexity/smoothness (Assumption 2.2) that may not hold in all practical settings, and whether the combinatorial search cost of BCD (solving 2^k subproblems per iteration) remains tractable for larger k. The framework could extend to group sparsity or structured sparsity constraints.
