## Problem

A/B testing in industry (ridesharing, healthcare, tech) often suffers from limited experimental sample sizes and small effect sizes, motivating the use of historical control data to improve ATE estimation. However, distributional shifts—particularly reward shift between the experimental and historical control arms—introduce bias that existing methods handle poorly: they either assume zero shift (achieving efficiency only under that assumption) or require the shift to be large enough for clear detection. This paper addresses the full spectrum of reward shift magnitudes (zero through large) with estimators that are simultaneously efficient, robust, and oracle-optimal across all regimes.

## Input / Output

The method takes two offline datasets: an experimental dataset of i.i.d. context-action-reward triplets (binary action: control vs. target) and a historical dataset of i.i.d. context-reward pairs generated solely under the control policy. In the sequential extension, these become sequences of state-action-reward triplets and state-reward pairs over T time steps under a time-varying MDP. The output is a weighted ATE estimate τ̂_w = w·τ̂_e + (1−w)·τ̂_h with w ∈ [0,1], a Wald-type confidence interval, and the optimal combination weight itself. The weight is determined by minimizing estimated MSE (non-pessimistic) or a pessimistic upper bound on MSE (pessimistic), with the latter incorporating an uncertainty quantifier U from concentration inequalities.

## Method

The core mechanism constructs two doubly robust base estimators—τ̂_e (experimental-data-only, using DR/IS/direct methods) and τ̂_h (using experimental data for the target policy value and historical data for the control policy value, with a density ratio μ to correct for covariate shift)—then forms a linear combination with weight w optimized to minimize MSE. The non-pessimistic estimator plugs in the unbiased bias estimate b̂_h = τ̂_e − τ̂_h into the closed-form MSE expression, while the pessimistic estimator replaces b_h² with (|b̂_h| + U)², an upper bound valid with probability 1−α. The key theoretical insight is that the pessimistic estimator's excess MSE depends only on the oracle weight w* rather than the estimated weight ŵ, making it robust in the moderate reward-shift regime where the non-pessimistic estimator can fail. Non-asymptotic bounds establish that both estimators achieve the efficiency bound (plus a small spurious estimation error) when b_h is small, and the oracle property when b_h is large, with the pessimistic variant extending oracle optimality down to the moderate regime. The framework extends to sequential decision making by replacing the DR estimator with the double RL estimator and inflating error bounds by a factor of T².

## Results

The paper evaluates MSE of ATE estimators across three reward-shift regimes (small, moderate, large b_h) using non-dynamic and sequential simulations. In the small b_h regime, the proposed non-pessimistic estimator's MSE closely approximates the semi-parametric efficiency bound (SPE), while in the moderate and large b_h regimes, the pessimistic estimator achieves the oracle property (MSE asymptotically equal to the oracle estimator). Empirically, the pessimistic estimator maintains stable MSE as b_h increases from 0 to 1.5, whereas the non-pessimistic and Lasso estimators show significant MSE degradation in the moderate regime. Confidence interval experiments confirm that the pessimistic estimator achieves nominal 95% coverage with narrower intervals than EDO, while Lasso exhibits significant undercoverage.

**Table 1: Summary of the properties of MSEs of the proposed estimators across reward shift scenarios.**

| Reward shift | Non-pessimistic estimator | Pessimistic estimator |
|---|---|---|
| Zero | Close to efficiency bound | Same order to oracle MSE |
| Small | Close to oracle MSE | Same order to oracle MSE |
| Moderate | May suffer a large MSE | Oracle property |
| Large | Oracle property | Oracle property |

*The oracle MSE denotes the MSE of the oracle estimator that uses the best weight to combine historical and experimental data; the efficiency bound is the smallest achievable MSE among a broad class of regular estimators (Tsiatis, 2006).*

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Lasso baseline (Cheng & Cai, 2021), the proposed NonPessi estimator outperforms Lasso in most b_h settings, and the two perform comparably only when b_h is large. The Pessi estimator demonstrates clear robustness advantages: as b_h increases, Lasso's MSE rises sharply while Pessi's increases only slightly, and Lasso's performance is highly sensitive to its ℓ₁ tuning parameter (tested values 0.8–4), whereas Pessi requires no such tuning. Against the SPE baseline (Li et al., 2023), which assumes zero reward shift, SPE is the top performer in the small b_h regime but degrades substantially as b_h grows; the proposed NonPessi estimator tracks SPE closely in this regime, while Pessi maintains stable performance across all regimes. In the sequential ridesharing simulation (30-day switchback design, 24-hour horizon), Pessi consistently achieves the lowest or near-lowest MSE across treatment effect ratios of 5% and 10%, outperforming EDO, Lasso, and NonPessi particularly in the moderate b_h range.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data-integration framework for ATE estimation by relaxing the zero-reward-shift assumption of Li et al. (2023), whose semi-parametrically efficient (SPE) estimator achieves the efficiency bound only when historical and experimental control rewards are identical. It also builds on the double RL estimator of Kallus & Uehara (2020) for the sequential decision-making extension. The key conceptual departure is from prior combination methods (Cheng & Cai 2021; Han et al. 2021; Dahabreh et al. 2023) that either require the reward shift to be zero or sufficiently large for detection; here the authors handle the full spectrum including the challenging moderate regime, and import the pessimistic principle from offline RL to obtain robustness in that regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners running A/B tests in ridesharing, e-commerce, or healthcare, the paper provides a principled, plug-in method (the pessimistic weighted estimator) that safely borrows from historical data without requiring prior knowledge of the magnitude of distributional shift. The clean three-regime theoretical characterization (small/moderate/large b_h) gives actionable guidance on when each estimator variant is optimal. Open questions include: extending the hybrid procedure to settings without prior regime knowledge, handling unmeasured confounding in the historical data, and whether the pessimistic principle can be further tightened to close the small gap between the pessimistic and oracle MSE in the moderate regime.
