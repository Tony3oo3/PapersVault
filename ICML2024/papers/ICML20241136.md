## Problem

Backdoor poisoning attacks (BPAs) can compromise neural networks with only a small fraction of poisoned training data, yet the theoretical reasons why these attacks succeed remain largely unexplored. Prior theoretical work studied BPA from the perspective of hypothesis class properties (memorization capacity, adaptability), but no prior work analyzed the learning dynamics within a convolutional neural network to explain how and when the backdoor is embedded.

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework operates on a multi-view data model where each input is a matrix of P non-overlapped patches of dimension d, containing one feature patch (aligned with one of K orthogonal unit-norm feature vectors), one main noise patch, and remaining background noise patches. The trigger vector v replaces one background patch in poisoned data, and the targeted label y^p is user-specified. The output is a binary class label via sign(F(x)), and the theoretical results provide explicit sufficient conditions (involving K, the poisoning ratio n_po/n, and the norm ratio ||v||/||u||) under which the backdoored network simultaneously achieves high clean accuracy and high attack success rate.

## Method

The paper analyzes a two-layer patch-wise CNN with C channels, a smoothed symmetrized ReLU activation (q=3), logistic loss, and gradient descent, under a multi-view data model with K orthogonal features and Gaussian noise. The key technical contribution is decomposing the weight update into three directional components—feature vectors, the trigger vector, and noise—and showing that the trigger vector is captured at time T_v = Õ(n/(n_po·||v||³·σ₀)) while feature vectors are captured at T_u = Õ(K/(ησ₀)), with T_v ≪ T_u when ||v||³/||u||³ ≫ n/(n_po·K). A second key insight is that the dirty-label mechanism (flipping labels of poisoned data) creates a persistent asymmetry: even in the late stage when both feature and trigger projections reach Ω(1), the trigger vector's contribution to the network output remains strictly larger, preventing the feature vectors from overtaking the backdoor signal. The main theorem formalizes that under mutual orthogonality of {u^k}∪{v}, over-parameterization (nP²K ≤ o(√d)), and Ω(1) ≤ n_po ≤ o(n), the backdoored network achieves both high clean accuracy and high attack success rate with high probability.

## Results

The paper's main contribution is theoretical: Theorem 5.4 proves that under a multi-view data model, a backdoored two-layer CNN trained on a poisoned set achieves both high clean accuracy (≥ 1 − O(nP²KC/poly(d))) and high attack success rate (≥ 1 − O(nP²KC/poly(d))) once the trigger vector is captured at time T_v = Θ̃(n/(η·n_po·‖v‖₂³·σ₀)). Empirically, on MNIST with the BadNets attack, the attack success rate (ASR) exceeds 95% at a poisoning rate of just 0.02 when the training set has 10,000 samples, but requires a poisoning rate of 0.1 for only 2,000 samples. Clean accuracy remains above 99% across all settings, confirming that the backdoor is effectively hidden. The time T* to reach ASR > 95% decreases as poisoning rate increases (e.g., from 28 epochs at rate 0.03 to 13 epochs at rate 0.1 for 6,000 samples).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is a theoretical analysis rather than a method comparison, so it does not benchmark against competing defense or attack algorithms. Instead, it validates its theoretical predictions (Theorems 5.3 and 5.4) using two well-known dirty-label attacks—BadNets (Gu et al., 2017) and the four-corner attack (Turner et al., 2019)—on MNIST and CIFAR-10. The key empirical finding is that the minimum poisoning rate needed for ASR > 95% decreases monotonically with training set size (from 0.10 at n=2000 to 0.02 at n=10000 on MNIST), directly confirming the theoretical dependence on n_po/n. Clean accuracy remains essentially unchanged (≥99%) across all poisoning rates, supporting the theory that n_po ≤ o(n) keeps the harmful effect of label-flipped data negligible. The paper also shows via SVD and T-SNE analysis that poisoned data representations align with the negative direction of the top singular vector, consistent with the theoretical prediction that the trigger vector dominates the network output in the late training stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior theoretical analyses of backdoor poisoning attacks (Manoj & Blum, 2021; Xian et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2023a), which study BPA effectiveness from the hypothesis-class or statistical-risk perspective, by instead analyzing the gradient-descent learning dynamics within a two-layer CNN. It directly adopts the multi-view data model and patch-wise CNN architecture from Shen et al. (2022) on data augmentation, repurposing their feature-learning framework to characterize how trigger vectors and main features are captured at different timescales. The analytical techniques (ODE reduction, early/late-stage decomposition) follow the feature-learning literature of Allen-Zhu & Li (2021) and Shen et al. (2022), but are applied here to a poisoned training set with flipped labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to theoretically characterize why dirty-label backdoor attacks succeed by tracking the learning dynamics of a CNN, identifying three concrete success conditions (number of features K, norm ratio ‖v‖/‖u‖, and poisoning rate n_po/n) that practitioners can use to reason about attack robustness. However, the analysis is confined to a two-layer CNN with binary classification, a specific smoothed-ReLU activation, and strong orthogonality assumptions on feature and trigger vectors, leaving open whether the conclusions extend to deeper architectures, multi-class settings, or non-orthogonal triggers. A key open question is whether the identified 'race' between trigger and feature learning persists under realistic data distributions and whether the theoretical conditions can inform practical detection or defense strategies.
