## Problem

Existing human-AI interaction datasets for text-to-image models capture single-shot prompts without a fixed goal, making it impossible to study how users iteratively refine prompts or to quantify how easily a model can be steered toward a desired output. This paper addresses both gaps by creating a controlled interactive game that records sequential prompt iterations toward a target image and by proposing a quantitative steerability metric based on Markov-chain stopping time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242029/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Players receive a 512×512 target image and submit positive and negative text prompts; the system generates a 512×512 image using Stable Diffusion v2.1 with a fixed seed and 20 DPM-Solver steps, then returns a CLIP-embedding-based similarity score (0–100) with per-target normalization to equalize difficulty. The primary outputs are the ArtWhisperer dataset (51,026 interactions from 2,250 players across 191 targets) and a steerability score per target image, computed as the expected number of interactions to reach the top score bin [81,100] via Monte Carlo simulation of an empirical Markov chain.

## Method

The paper designs an online game where users iteratively refine prompts to match a target image, with all model hyperparameters (seed, DPM-Solver scheduler, 20 iterations) fixed so that prompt quality is the sole variable. Target images are selected from Wikipedia captions and AI-generated prompts using a two-stage CLIP-embedding seed-selection procedure that guarantees the target is achievable under the fixed parameters. The steerability metric bins scores into five intervals, constructs an empirical transition matrix from all users' score sequences for each target (with a uniform prior regularizer ε), and estimates the expected stopping time to reach the top bin via Monte Carlo simulation. The authors validate the metric against human ratings from a controlled Prolific study, compare steerability across image categories and between SD v2.1 and v1.5, and evaluate GPT-4 and Gemini as automated players to assess vision-language model feedback utilization.

## Results

The ArtWhisperer dataset contains 51,026 interactions from 2,250 players across 191 target images, with an average of 9.29 prompts per player and an average score of 58.93/100. Users improve their score from an average initial 56.7 to a final 73.0 (average improvement of 16.3 points), with each iterative prompt update improving the score 40–60% of the time (mean 48.6%). The proposed steerability metric (expected stopping time to reach the [81,100] score bin) shows that city and nature images are most steerable, while fantasy and AI-generated art images are least steerable. GPT-4, when given feedback, consistently improves its score and outperforms human users in steerability across all image categories, whereas Gemini shows no improvement with feedback.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three baselines. Against GPT-4 (OpenAI, 2023), the paper finds that GPT-4 is actually *better* at steering Stable Diffusion than human users: SD is at least as steerable with respect to GPT-4 as with humans across all image categories, even after normalizing for initial score, indicating GPT-4 adapts to feedback more effectively than humans. Against Gemini (Team et al., 2023), GPT-4 clearly wins: GPT-4's score trajectory improves steadily with feedback (plateauing after ~15 rounds), while Gemini shows no improvement with feedback, likely because the evaluated Gemini model was not trained for multi-turn conversation. Against SDv1.5 (Rombach et al., 2022a), SDv2.1 is more steerable for nature, sci-fi/space, and real images (the only category with a difference exceeding the standard deviation of the mean is 'Nature'), while steerability is similar across the two models for other categories such as AI-generated art and fantasy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the human-AI interaction literature by introducing the first public dataset of *sequential*, goal-directed interactions between humans and a text-to-image model. Existing text-to-image interaction datasets (e.g., DiffusionDB, Pick-a-Pic, Simulacra) are scraped from online repositories, capture single-shot interactions, and lack a predefined target, making it impossible to study iterative prompt refinement. The steerability metric draws on Markov chain theory applied to empirical score transitions, a formulation not previously used for evaluating generative model usability. The synthetic prompter proof-of-concept connects to Promptist (Hao et al., 2022) and collaborative editing work (Zhu et al., 2023), but differs in objective: rather than optimizing a single prompt, it learns to generate human-like prompt *trajectories*.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ArtWhisperer dataset and steerability metric provide the field with a concrete, computable way to quantify how 'easy' a generative model is to use iteratively, filling a gap where prior evaluation relied on static benchmarks or qualitative user studies. The finding that GPT-4 matches or exceeds human steerability (after normalizing for initial prompt quality) suggests VLMs could serve as scalable synthetic evaluators, potentially automating steerability measurement without human annotation. Key limitations include the modest target-image diversity (191 images), the CLIP-based scoring function's only moderate correlation with human ratings (r≈0.58), and the restriction to a single primary model (SD v2.1). Open questions include whether steerability can be incorporated directly into diffusion model training objectives, and whether the findings generalize beyond Stable Diffusion to other architectures.
