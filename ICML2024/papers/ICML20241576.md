## Problem

As LoRA adapters proliferate across online hubs, trained independently by different users, there is no principled way to (1) build a compact, high-performing library of adapters from multi-task data that encourages cross-task transfer, and (2) route new inputs to the most relevant adapters in a zero-shot fashion without joint training of the router and experts. Standard mixture-of-experts approaches assume the router and experts are trained together, which is incompatible with the decentralized, asynchronous reality of adapter sharing.

## Input / Output

The system takes a base LLM (Phi-2 2.8B, Mistral 7B, or StableLM 3B) and a multi-task training set (256 Flan-v2 tasks) to produce a library of K LoRA adapters (K=10 for MBC, K=256 for Private). At inference, a text query is processed token-by-token and layer-by-layer: each hidden state is routed to the top-4 most relevant adapters via Arrow, and the selected LoRAs are linearly combined (weighted sum of A and B matrices) to modulate the attention layers. LoRA rank is fixed at 4 with only attention layers and their output projections patched, keeping the per-adapter parameter count minimal.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary techniques. For library construction, Model-Based Clustering (MBC) is a two-stage procedure: first, private LoRAs are trained per task; then the flattened LoRA weight vectors are reduced via SVD, pairwise cosine similarities are computed, and K-Means clusters the tasks. One adapter is then trained per cluster on the union of member tasks' data, matching the total compute budget of baselines. The key empirical finding motivating MBC is a positive correlation between LoRA weight similarity and the performance delta of joint vs. private training. For zero-shot reuse, Arrow routing computes each expert's prototype as the first right singular vector of its AB^T matrix (the direction of maximum variance in hidden-state space). At inference, each token's hidden state at each layer is scored against all prototypes via absolute dot product, and the top-k adapters are selected and linearly combined. Arrow requires no access to training data, no gradient-based router training, and routes differently per token and per layer, making it suitable for decentralized, asynchronously trained adapter libraries.

## Results

On 10 held-out zero-shot tasks with Phi-2 (2.8B), MBC-μ achieves 67.1% mean accuracy, outperforming FullFT (65.6%) by 1.5 points and the strongest library baseline MHR-μ (65.9%) by 1.2 points. With Arrow routing, MBC-↑ reaches 67.4%, and even the independently trained Private-↑ (66.6%) surpasses FullFT. On Mistral 7B, MBC-μ achieves 64.6% mean accuracy, outperforming Shared (63.1%) and Private-μ (61.9%). For supervised adaptation on 12 SNI tasks (Phi-2), MBC-Poly achieves 78.8 Rouge-L (100% data) and 68.2 (10% data), consistently topping all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Poly (Ponti et al., 2023), MBC-μ outperforms Poly-μ by 1.4 points on Phi-2 zero-shot (67.1% vs. 65.7%) and by 5.4 Rouge-L on supervised adaptation (78.8 vs. 73.4). Against MHR (Caccia et al., 2023), MBC-μ leads by 1.2 points on Phi-2 zero-shot (67.1% vs. 65.9%), and the paper notes MHR-μ was already competitive with FullFT. Against π-tuning (Wu et al., 2023), MBC-Poly achieves 78.8 Rouge-L versus 76.7 for π-tuning on 100% supervised data, and 68.2 vs. 64.6 on 10% data. Against LoraHub (Huang et al., 2024), the gap is dramatic: MBC-Poly scores 78.8 Rouge-L while LoraHub scores only 45.3 on the same 100% supervised setting, which the authors attribute to LoraHub's gradient-free optimization that does not fine-tune expert weights. Notably, even the independently trained Private library with Arrow routing (66.6%) surpasses FullFT (65.6%) on Phi-2, a result the authors highlight as surprising given that experts are trained in isolation and routing is learned post-hoc.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adapter-composition line of work (Polytropon/Poly, MHR, LoraHub, AdapterSoup) by addressing two underexplored sub-problems: (1) how to build a compact adapter library that maximizes cross-task transfer, and (2) how to route new inputs to independently trained adapters without joint training of router and experts. MBC builds on Zhou et al. (2022)'s observation that adapter parameters serve as task embeddings, but applies it to cluster tasks before training rather than post-hoc. Arrow departs from standard MoE routing (Fedus et al., 2022) and from data-dependent routers (TP, CM) by deriving expert prototypes purely from the SVD of LoRA weight matrices, requiring no training data or gradient updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is that adapter hubs (e.g., HuggingFace) could support a decentralized ecosystem where users train LoRAs asynchronously on private data and a post-hoc router selects relevant adapters for new inputs without any coordination. Key limitations include restriction to LoRA on attention layers only, a fixed set of 256 Flan tasks, and modest absolute gains (1–2 points) over strong baselines like MHR-μ. Open questions include whether the approach scales to heterogeneous adapter types (MLPs, prompts), larger model families, and whether Arrow's per-token, per-layer routing remains advantageous as library size grows beyond 256 experts.
