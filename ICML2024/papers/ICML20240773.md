## Problem

Applying high-capacity sequence-modeling architectures like Decision Transformers to multi-task offline RL introduces severe gradient conflicts that worsen with increasing task count, causing negative transfer and performance degradation below task-specific baselines. Existing remedies—shared structures, task-conditioned representations, and post-hoc gradient surgery—address symptoms rather than the root cause of indiscriminate parameter sharing. The paper asks whether an optimal 'harmony subspace' of parameters can be identified per task to enable effective sharing without conflict.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a short trajectory prompt (K* steps of state-action-return-to-go tuples), the most recent K-step history, a target return, and a task ID, and outputs a continuous 4-dimensional action (3D end-effector position + gripper openness) at each timestep. Uniquely, a learned binary task-specific mask (sparsity ~0.2) gates which subset of the shared Transformer's 1.47M parameters is active for each task, so the effective per-task model is a sparse sub-network of the unified policy. For unseen tasks, a voting threshold over all training-task masks constructs a generalized mask.

## Method

HarmoDT formulates harmony-subspace discovery as a bi-level optimization within a meta-learning framework: the outer loop periodically updates task-specific binary masks via a Harmony Score that balances an Agreement Score (element-wise product of masked task gradient and mean masked gradient) with an Importance Score (parameter magnitude or Fisher information), while the inner loop performs standard gradient descent on the masked parameters. Each mask update cycle masks the α_t most conflicting weights (ArgBtmK of the Harmony Score) and recovers an equal number of previously masked weights that have since aligned (ArgTopK of the unmasked gradient product), with α_t controlled by cosine annealing. Masks are initialized via the Erdős-Rényi Kernel (ERK) to ensure layer-appropriate sparsity, and the entire procedure is built on the Prompt-DT architecture, so the only architectural addition is the learned per-task binary mask applied element-wise to the Transformer weights during both training and inference.

## Results

On Meta-World 50 tasks (MT50-rand), HarmoDT-F achieves 82.20±0.40% average success rate under the near-optimal setting and 57.20±0.68% under the sub-optimal setting, outperforming the strongest baseline Prompt-DT* (69.33% and 48.40%) by 12.9% and 8.8% respectively. Even the fixed-mask variant HarmoDT-R (75.39% / 53.80%) surpasses all baselines, confirming that task-specific parameter subspaces alone yield substantial gains. On unseen tasks (Cheetah-dir, Cheetah-vel, Ant-dir), HarmoDT-F attains an average cumulative reward of 396.8, exceeding Prompt-DT (362.1) by 9.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HarmoDT-F decisively outperforms all offline baselines on Meta-World 50 tasks: it beats Prompt-DT* (the strongest offline baseline) by 12.9% (near-optimal) and 8.8% (sub-optimal), MTDIFF-P-ONEHOT by 20.9% and 8.3%, and MTDT* by 16.4% and 14.9%. Against online MTRL methods, HarmoDT-F exceeds D2R (63.53%) by 18.7% in the near-optimal setting, and PaCo (54.31%) by 27.9%. On unseen-task generalization (Cheetah-dir, Cheetah-vel, Ant-dir), HarmoDT-F surpasses Prompt-DT by 9.6% in average cumulative reward (396.8 vs. 362.1) and MTDT by 80.5% (396.8 vs. 219.2), with the largest relative gain on Cheetah-vel where it reduces the negative reward from −127.7 to −66.5. Notably, even HarmoDT-R, which uses fixed random masks without the meta-learning update, already outperforms every baseline, indicating that the mere introduction of task-specific parameter subspaces is the dominant source of improvement, while the learned mask refinement provides an additional 3–7% gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

HarmoDT builds directly on the Prompt-DT architecture (Xu et al., 2022) and the Decision Transformer sequence-modeling framework (Chen et al., 2021), adding a meta-learning layer that learns task-specific binary masks to define a 'harmony subspace' of parameters for each task. It departs from prior gradient-conflict mitigation strategies (PCGrad, GradDrop, CAGrad) that adjust gradients post hoc, instead intervening at the parameter level by selectively activating subsets of weights per task. The mask initialization borrows from ERK (Evci et al., 2020), and the bi-level optimization framing connects to meta-learning literature, but the specific formulation—using agreement and importance scores to dynamically reassign parameters between tasks—is novel to this work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental bottleneck in multi-task offline RL: gradient conflicts that degrade performance as task count grows. The finding that even random frozen masks substantially outperform unmasked baselines suggests that the mere existence of parameter sparsity per task is a critical, previously underappreciated factor. However, the method's reliance on a fixed sparsity ratio across all tasks (acknowledged as a limitation) and its evaluation confined to Meta-World manipulation tasks leave open whether the harmony subspace concept generalizes to heterogeneous domains or tasks of vastly different complexity. The 36-hour training cost per seed on a single GPU also raises practical scalability questions for larger task suites.
