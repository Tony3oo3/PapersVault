## Problem

In causal representation learning, prior identifiability results require parametric assumptions (linearity, Gaussianity), graphical restrictions, or interventional data. This paper tackles the fundamental question of what can be recovered about latent causal variables and their structure in the most general, completely nonparametric setting from purely observational multi-domain data, without assuming hard interventions or specific functional forms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242484/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes d-dimensional observed variables X (d ≥ n) sampled from m different distributions (domains) Θ = {θ^(1),...,θ^(m)}, where each domain corresponds to a different configuration of latent causal mechanism parameters θ_i. It produces recovered latent variables Ẑ, a causal adjacency matrix Â encoding the DAG structure, and the moralized graph (Markov network) of the underlying causal DAG. Recovery is up to specific indeterminacies: each Ẑ_π(i) is a function of Z_i and its 'intimate neighbors' Ψ_{Z_i} in the Markov network, which may be empty in many cases, yielding component-wise identifiability.

## Method

Theoretically, the paper exploits the equivalence between pairwise conditional independence and zero cross-derivatives of the log-density (Lin 1997), combined with a sparsity constraint on the recovered Markov network and sufficient change conditions across domains (requiring 2n + |M_Z| + 1 domains). This yields identifiability of the Markov network up to isomorphism (Theorem 2) and of latent variables up to intimate-neighbor mixtures (Theorem 3), with the moralized graph recovered under two new weaker faithfulness assumptions (SAF and SUCF). Practically, the Change Encoding Network (CEN) is built on a VAE framework with two prior implementations: a nonparametric version using normalizing flows conditioned on domain index u to model the latent SEM, and a parametric version assuming linear scaling/shifting mechanisms. An L1 regularization on the adjacency matrix Â approximates the sparsity constraint on the Markov network edges, which is the key ingredient enabling identifiability beyond the trivial fully-connected solution.

## Results

The paper's primary contributions are theoretical identifiability guarantees rather than quantitative benchmark results. The key results are: (1) Theorem 2 shows the recovered latent Markov network is isomorphic to the true one under sparsity and sufficient-change assumptions; (2) Theorem 3 shows each recovered latent variable is a function of at most itself and its 'intimate neighbors' in the Markov network, with Remark 1 noting that if each neighbor is non-adjacent to at least one other neighbor, the variable is recoverable up to component-wise transformation; (3) Proposition 2 establishes that the Markov network equals the moralized graph of the true DAG if and only if the proposed SAF and SUCF faithfulness relaxations hold. Simulation experiments on 4-variable Y-structure and chain-structure DAGs (with both Gaussian and Laplace noise, modular changes across domains) confirm that the nonparametric and parametric implementations recover a strong one-to-one correspondence between estimated and true latent components and correctly identify the causal adjacency structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not include direct empirical comparisons against prior causal representation learning methods (e.g., iVAE, ICE-Beem, or intervention-based approaches). The evaluation is limited to simulation studies where ground-truth latent variables and causal structures are known, validating the theoretical identifiability claims. The two proposed implementations (nonparametric normalizing-flow prior and parametric linear-SEM prior) are compared against each other in Figures 3 and 4, both showing successful recovery of latent variables and causal structure under Y-structure and chain-structure settings with modular domain changes. The paper positions its contribution as establishing what is identifiable in the most general nonparametric setting without parametric or interventional assumptions, thereby clarifying the unique value added by stronger assumptions used in prior works (e.g., linearity, hard interventions, or specific functional constraints).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the nonlinear ICA identifiability framework (Hyvärinen et al., 2019) and the VAE-based disentanglement approach (Khemakhem et al., 2020) to the general causal setting where latent variables are not independent but follow a structural equation model. The key technical ingredient—the equivalence between conditional independence and zero cross-derivatives of the log-density (Lin, 1997)—is repurposed to handle causal dependencies among latents. Unlike prior causal representation learning work that requires parametric assumptions (linearity, Gaussianity) or interventional data, this paper works in a completely nonparametric, purely observational setting with only distributional changes across domains, and introduces weaker faithfulness variants (SAF, SUCF) to connect the recovered Markov network to the moralized graph of the true DAG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper provides a fundamental answer to what is recoverable in the most general causal representation learning setting: the moralized graph (not the full DAG) and latent variables up to 'intimate neighbor' indeterminacies, without any parametric or interventional assumptions. The impossibility result (Corollary 1)—that independent components cannot exist when causal relations are present—clarifies a boundary that prior ICA-based methods implicitly assumed away. Open questions include what minimal additional assumptions (beyond sparsity and sufficient change) yield full component-wise identifiability, and how the theory extends when only a subset of causal mechanisms change. The practical validation is limited to 4-variable simulated DAGs, leaving open whether the sparsity constraint and sufficient-change assumption are tractable in high-dimensional real-world settings.
