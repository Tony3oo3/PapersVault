## Problem

While training data extraction has been well-studied for LLMs and diffusion models, it remains unclear whether Document-Based VQA models memorize and regurgitate sensitive training data (including PII appearing only once) when the answer is visually removed from the input document. No prior work has quantified this extractability, distinguished it from legitimate generalization, or proposed effective countermeasures for the VQA setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241657/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The extraction probe takes a partial document image I^{-a} (the original DocVQA page with the answer region blanked via Tesseract OCR bounding boxes) paired with the original training question Q, and checks whether the model still outputs the correct answer a. The canary set comprises ~4,654 manually curated samples (~1,200 unique images) from DocVQA's ~40K training set, filtered to exclude OCR failures and trivially inferable answers. Models are evaluated at multiple training resolutions (e.g., Donut from 2560×1920 down to lower resolutions; Pix2Struct at ~1.2M and ~0.8M pixels; PaLI-3 at ~1.1M pixels), and outputs include extractability counts, PII counts, and per-sample memorization/simplicity scores.

## Method

The paper introduces a generalization baseline f_G (same architecture, trained on D^{tr} minus the canary set) to attribute extractable answers to memorization vs. generalization: |M| = |E − G| captures answers extractable only by the full model. To validate this attribution, the authors adapt Feldman's counterfactual memorization score and Zhang et al.'s simplicity score to the partial-context setting, defining an Extractable Memorization score M_E and Extractable Simplicity score S_E estimated empirically over K=50 random training splits (each canary included/excluded exactly 25 times). Ablations probe how extractability depends on context: removing all text, paraphrasing the question via PaLM2, applying brightness/rotation/translation perturbations, and shuffling question-image pairs. As a defense, Extraction Blocking (EB) augments training with (I^{-a}, Q, 'ANSWER NOT PRESENT') pairs, which reduces extractable PII to zero across all models and attack variants while slightly improving ANLS test performance.

## Results

On DocVQA, Donut at its highest training resolution (2560×1920) has |M|=63 memorized extractable samples, which grows to 756 at the lowest resolution; Pix2Struct-Base shows a similar pattern with substantial memorization. PaLI-3 at high resolution exhibits minimal memorization, with most extractable answers attributable to generalization rather than memorization. The proposed Extraction Blocking (EB) defense reduces extractable memorized samples to 0 for all models while simultaneously improving ANLS (e.g., +3.4 for Pix2Struct-Base, +1.2 for Donut), outperforming alternative defenses like Inference-Time Paraphrasing (−12.5 to −13.8 ANLS) and Random String prepending/appending (−1.9 to −3.7 ANLS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three Document-Based VQA models—Donut (176M params), Pix2Struct (282M/1.3B params), and PaLI-3 (5B params)—on their susceptibility to training data extraction. Donut and Pix2Struct both exhibit substantial memorization: at their highest resolutions, they retain 63 and ~94 memorized extractable samples respectively, with unique PIIs (names, serial numbers, travel destinations) among the extracted data. PaLI-3, benefiting from web-scale pretraining, shows the least memorization at high resolution, with most of its extractable answers explained by generalization (e.g., resolving acronyms, identifying chemical substances) rather than memorized training pairs. In terms of utility, PaLI-3 also achieves the best test ANLS (87.6) compared to Pix2Struct (76.6) and Donut (67.5), making it both the most private and the most accurate model in this comparison. The key differentiator is training resolution: lowering it increases memorization dramatically across all models (Donut |M| goes from 63 to 756), while PaLI-3's strong pretraining provides partial resilience even at lower resolutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training data extraction literature from LLMs (Carlini et al., 2021, 2023b) and diffusion models (Carlini et al., 2023a; Somepalli et al., 2023b) to the Document-Based VQA setting, where the attacker has a partial document image with the answer removed rather than a text prefix. It adapts the counterfactual memorization and simplicity scores of Feldman (2019) and Zhang et al. (2021a) to the partial-context extraction scenario, and uses the two-model comparison heuristic from Carlini et al. (2021) and Guo et al. (2023) to disentangle memorization from generalization. The proposed Extraction Blocking defense is inspired by the V-CSS counterfactual sample synthesis of Chen et al. (2020), repurposed here as a privacy countermeasure rather than a robustness technique.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that even properly regularized, non-overfitting document VQA models can leak PII that appears only once in ~40K training samples, posing a concrete privacy risk for any deployment handling sensitive documents. The finding that lower training resolution dramatically amplifies memorization (from 63 to 756 extractable samples for Donut) gives practitioners a clear, actionable design guideline. The Extraction Blocking defense is notable for simultaneously eliminating extractable PII and improving ANLS, making it a low-cost mitigation. Open questions include whether EB introduces a membership-inference side-channel (the model's 'ANSWER NOT PRESENT' response may signal training-set membership), whether the findings generalize beyond DocVQA to reasoning-heavy document tasks, and whether differentially private training can be scaled to VLMs without prohibitive utility loss.
