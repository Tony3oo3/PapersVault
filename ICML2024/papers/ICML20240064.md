## Problem

Scalable SDP solvers like CGAL with sketching achieve memory efficiency but suffer from iteration-dependent parameter schedules that slow convergence as problem size grows and prevent effective warm-starting. Spectral bundle methods offer low per-iteration cost and fast convergence but were previously restricted to either equality-only or inequality-only constraints and lacked practical implementations for massive instances. This paper unifies these approaches into a single algorithm that handles general SDPs with both constraint types while supporting warm-start initialization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an SDP specified by a (typically sparse) cost matrix C ∈ S^n, a linear operator A defined by matrices A_i, a constraint vector b, and an index set I partitioning equality from inequality constraints, along with user-set parameters (α, ρ, β, k_c, k_p) and an optional warm-start (X_0, y_0, V_0). With the Nyström sketching option, the primal iterate is never stored explicitly as an n×n matrix; instead, a rank-r sketch P_t = X̄_tΨ is maintained via low-rank updates, reducing memory from O(n²) to O(nr + nk + m + k⁴). The output is a primal-dual pair (X_T, y_T) satisfying certified bounds on objective gap, primal feasibility, and dual feasibility.

## Method

USBS applies the proximal bundle method to the penalized dual objective f(y) = α[max(λ_max(C−A*y), 0)] + ⟨b,y⟩ + ι_Y(y), constructing a model over a low-dimensional subspace X̂_t parameterized by k orthonormal vectors V_t (split into k_c current eigenvectors and k_p past spectral directions) and a trace-bounded matrix X̄_t that accumulates past spectral information. The candidate iterate is obtained by solving a small quadratic SDP over the k-dimensional subspace using a custom primal-dual interior point method, and the model is updated by eigendecomposing the subspace matrix to separate current from past spectral components. A key design choice is that all parameters (ρ, β, k_c, k_p) are fixed across iterations, eliminating the iteration-dependent schedules that plague CGAL and enabling reliable warm-starting. When augmented with a Nyström sketch, the primal is tracked via a random projection Ψ with provably constant (non-compounding) approximation error, and the sketch is updated in O(nk) time per iteration via low-rank modifications.

## Results

USBS achieves a 500x speedup over CGAL (the state-of-the-art scalable SDP solver) on a QAP instance with 2 billion decision variables, and solves an SDP with over 10^13 decision variables (333SP MaxCut) to a quality solution while CGAL fails to reach an accurate solution within 72 hours. On the 10 DIMACS10 MaxCut instances, CGAL fails to achieve an ε-approximate solution on 7 out of 10 instances within 72 hours, whereas USBS solves all 10 in 28 hours or less without warm-starting. Warm-starting USBS yields more than a 100x speedup in convergence compared to cold-starting, while CGAL often cannot reliably exploit a warm-start initialization due to its iteration-dependent parameter schedules.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is CGAL with sketching (Yurtsever et al., 2021), the previous state-of-the-art scalable SDP solver. USBS decisively outperforms CGAL across all three problem families. On MaxCut (DIMACS10), CGAL fails to reach an ε=10^{-1} approximate solution on 7 of 10 instances within 72 hours, while USBS solves all 10 in under 28 hours cold-started; the largest instance (333SP, n=3.7M, >10^13 variables) is solved by USBS but intractable for CGAL. On QAP (TSPLIB pr144, n=144), USBS achieves a lower relative gap faster and, critically, can leverage a warm-start initialization to accelerate convergence, whereas CGAL's iteration-dependent parameters prevent reliable warm-starting. On interactive entity resolution (PubMed, QIAN, SCAD-zbMATH), the performance gap between USBS and CGAL grows as the number of ∃-constraints (and thus problem size) increases, and USBS consistently benefits from warm-starting while CGAL does not. In summary, USBS wins on every comparison across all metrics (convergence time, relative gap, cumulative solve time) and all datasets, with the advantage widening as problem scale grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

USBS unifies and extends the spectral bundle method lineage initiated by Helmberg & Rendl (2000) for equality-constrained SDPs and refined by Ding & Grimmer (2023) with convergence guarantees, by handling both equality and inequality constraints simultaneously and introducing flexible k_c/k_p model parameters. It incorporates the Nyström sketching technique from Tropp et al. (2017) and Yurtsever et al. (2021) to achieve memory-efficient scalability, a capability absent from prior spectral bundle methods. The convergence analysis builds directly on the proximal bundle convergence rates of Díaz & Grimmer (2023). Unlike the state-of-the-art scalable solver CGAL, USBS uses no iteration-dependent parameter schedules, which is the key structural difference enabling reliable warm-starting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 500x speedup over CGAL on 2-billion-variable instances and the ability to solve 10^13-variable SDPs where CGAL fails within 72 hours make USBS a practically important tool for large-scale combinatorial optimization, neural network verification, and interactive entity resolution. The pure JAX implementation enables GPU/TPP acceleration, broadening accessibility beyond CPU-bound solvers. However, USBS is explicitly limited to SDPs with sparse cost matrices, m = o(n²) constraints, and low-rank solutions, leaving dense or highly-constrained SDPs (e.g., sum-of-squares) unaddressed. An open question is whether the warm-start advantage persists when the problem structure changes substantially between iterations rather than growing incrementally.
