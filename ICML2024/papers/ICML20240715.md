## Problem

Existing length extrapolation methods for Transformers treat sequences as flat token arrays, ignoring the intrinsic modular segmentation of text (sentences, code lines, proof steps) where per-segment token counts are bounded while segment counts grow linearly with sequence length. No prior positional encoding scheme explicitly models this two-level structure, leaving a gap between how text is actually organized and how positional information is encoded.

## Input / Output

The method takes a tokenized text sequence and a segmentation strategy (e.g., full stop '.', newline '\n', or equals sign '=' for arithmetic) that partitions the sequence into non-overlapping segments. For each token, BiPE produces two positional signals: a learnable absolute embedding added to the input token embedding (intra-segment, indexed 1..segment_length) and a relative encoding applied within the attention module (inter-segment, indexed by segment number). The intra-segment index resets at every segment boundary, so a 50-token sentence always uses positions 1–50 regardless of where it appears in a 120k-token document.

## Method

BiPE assigns each token a pair of encodings: an intra-segment absolute positional encoding (APE) that identifies the token's local position within its segment, and an inter-segment relative positional encoding (RPE) that captures the distance between the token's segment and other segments. Because per-segment token counts are empirically bounded, APE is sufficient at the intra-segment level, while RPE (instantiated as RoPE or ALiBi) handles the unbounded inter-segment dimension. In BiPE-RoPE, rotation matrices are indexed by segment number rather than token index, so the attention score depends on segment distance n−m; in BiPE-ALiBi, a linear bias r|n−m| is added to the attention logits. A theoretical analysis using a two-level finite automaton (Bi-NFA) shows that a Transformer with BiPE requires O(k² + Σ|Qᵢ|²) embedding dimensions to represent hierarchical automata, versus O(T²) for flat APE-based Transformers, confirming the parameter-efficiency advantage of the bilevel design.

## Results

On the SCROLLS long-context benchmark, BiPE-RoPE achieves an average score of 22.36, outperforming RoPE (18.38) by 3.98 points and all other baselines by over 3 points. For length extrapolation on PG-19, BiPE-ALiBi reduces perplexity from 28.59 (ALiBi) to 25.24 at 8192 sequence length, and BiPE-RoPE achieves 19.67 perplexity at 4096 length versus 158 for RoPE. On the Arithmetic capacity task, BiPE-ALiBi reaches 97% accuracy at 87K parameters while all other positional encodings fall below 70%. On in-distribution benchmarks (RACE, WinoGrande, TruthfulQA, PIQA, HellaSwag, MMLU), BiPE methods perform on par with baselines, confirming no degradation at normal sequence lengths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiPE-RoPE consistently outperforms RoPE across all major evaluations: by 3.98 points on SCROLLS average (22.36 vs 18.38), by a massive margin on PG-19 perplexity at 4096 length (19.67 vs 158), and by 1.52 points on SCROLLS when combined with YaRN fine-tuning (24.53 vs 23.01). BiPE-ALiBi similarly beats ALiBi on PG-19 perplexity at 8192 length (25.24 vs 28.59) and on SCROLLS average (18.34 vs 17.62), though the gap is smaller than for the RoPE variant. Against Sinusoidal PE, Randomized RoPE, and XPOS, BiPE methods dominate on all length extrapolation and long-context tasks. On in-distribution benchmarks (Table 2), BiPE-RoPE and BiPE-ALiBi achieve comparable scores to RoPE and ALiBi (e.g., 28.04 vs 29.00 on RACE, 26.91 vs 26.43 on MMLU), confirming that the bilevel design does not sacrifice standard-length performance. The paper also demonstrates compatibility with YaRN positional interpolation, where BiPE-RoPE+YaRN maintains low perplexity up to 20k sequence length while RoPE+YaRN degrades significantly beyond 11k.

## Relation to Prior Work

BiPE builds on the length extrapolation literature (Press et al., 2022; Chi et al., 2022; Su et al., 2021) but departs from all prior positional encoding methods by exploiting the intrinsic modular segmentation of text (sentences, code lines, proof steps). Rather than treating a sequence as a flat token array, BiPE disentangles position into an intra-segment absolute encoding (bounded, so APE suffices) and an inter-segment relative encoding (unbounded, so RPE like RoPE or ALiBi is used). The theoretical analysis draws on hierarchical finite automata (Alur et al., 1999) and the NFA-representation framework of Feng et al. (2023) to formalize why this bilevel decomposition is more parameter-efficient. The method is explicitly orthogonal to existing extrapolation techniques (YaRN, Position Interpolation, XPOS) and is shown to compose with them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BiPE offers a principled, drop-in modification to positional encoding that consistently improves length extrapolation across text modalities (natural language, code, math) without sacrificing in-distribution performance, and it composes with existing fine-tuning strategies like YaRN. The Bi-NFA theoretical framework provides a formal justification for why bilevel encoding reduces embedding dimensionality from O(N²) to O(N^{3/2}), giving practitioners confidence in the design. Open questions include extending the bilevel design to deeper hierarchies (sentences → paragraphs → documents) and applying it to data without natural segmentation boundaries (time series, protein sequences), which the authors identify as future directions.
