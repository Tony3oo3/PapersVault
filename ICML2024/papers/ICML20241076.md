## Problem

Behavior Transformers (BeT) discretize continuous actions via k-means clustering to capture multimodal behavior, but k-means does not scale to high-dimensional or temporally extended action spaces and provides no gradient signal, limiting long-range action modeling. The paper asks whether a learned, differentiable discretization can replace k-means while preserving the transformer's ability to model multimodal, long-horizon behavior.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of h observation tokens (state vectors or 112×112 RGB images encoded via ResNet18/HPR) and, in the conditional variant, appends g future observation tokens as goal conditioning. It outputs a continuous action chunk of length n by decoding the sum of predicted residual VQ codebook vectors through a learned decoder, then adding a per-observation offset. For the nuScenes driving task, variable-length object detections are serialized into fixed token sequences (mission, ego-state, trajectory history, and per-object tokens) to handle the non-uniform observation dimensionality.

## Method

VQ-BeT replaces BeT's k-means tokenizer with a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 trains a Residual VQ-VAE (N_q=2 layers) that maps each action chunk to a primary code (coarse clustering) and a secondary code (fine-grained residual), using L1 reconstruction loss plus a VQ commitment objective with moving-average codebook updates. Stage 2 trains a GPT-like transformer to predict the primary and secondary codes autoregressively from the observation sequence, using a weighted focal loss where the secondary-code loss is scaled by β<1 to reflect the hierarchical prior. A lightweight offset head adds a continuous correction to the decoded action for full fidelity. Because the transformer predicts discrete tokens in a single forward pass, inference is 5× faster than diffusion-based baselines in simulation and 25× faster on the real robot, while still capturing multiple behavior modes.

## Results

On unconditional behavior generation across seven simulated environments, VQ-BeT achieves the best performance in 5/7 tasks, outperforming DiffusionPolicy-T (the strongest baseline) on PushT (0.78 vs 0.74 Final IoU), Image PushT (0.68 vs 0.45), Kitchen (3.66 vs 3.44 goals), and Multimodal Ant (3.22 vs 2.90 goals). On goal-conditional generation, VQ-BeT wins 6/7 environments, achieving 0.39 Final IoU on PushT (vs 0.30 for C-BESO) and 3.78/4 goals on Kitchen (vs 3.75 for C-BESO). VQ-BeT also achieves a 5× inference speedup over diffusion-based methods (15.1 ms vs ~100 ms per step) while matching or exceeding their quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VQ-BeT decisively outperforms MLP-based BC and the original BeT (k-means tokenization) across nearly all environments, with BeT particularly struggling on image-based tasks (e.g., 0.01 on Image PushT vs VQ-BeT's 0.68). Against DiffusionPolicy-T (transformer variant), VQ-BeT wins on PushT, Image PushT, Kitchen, and Multimodal Ant, but loses on BlockPush (1.79 vs 1.93) and Image Kitchen (2.98 vs 3.01). Against DiffusionPolicy-C (convolutional variant), VQ-BeT wins on PushT, Image PushT, Kitchen, and Multimodal Ant, while DiffPolicy-C edges ahead on Image Kitchen (3.11 vs 2.98). In the conditional setting, VQ-BeT dominates C-BeT and GCBC across all tasks, and beats C-BESO and CFG-BESO on 6/7 environments (losing only on BlockPush where C-BESO achieves 0.93 vs VQ-BeT's 0.87). On nuScenes autonomous driving, VQ-BeT achieves the lowest L2 trajectory error (0.73 m) among partial-information methods, matching the best full-information model (Agent-Driver, 0.74 m) while using less environmental information.

## Relation to Prior Work

VQ-BeT directly extends Behavior Transformers (BeT) by replacing its k-means-based action tokenization with a learned Residual VQ-VAE, addressing k-means' inability to scale to high-dimensional or temporally extended action spaces. The residual quantization mechanism is borrowed from the audio codec literature (Soundstream), following the broader trend of using VQ-VAE tokenization as a discretization bottleneck in generative models for audio, video, and images. The conditional formulation extends C-BeT's goal-conditioned architecture, while the overall GPT-like transformer backbone and focal-loss training objective are retained from the BeT lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-pass inference (5× faster than diffusion policies in simulation, 25× on real robots) makes VQ-BeT practically attractive for real-time deployment on resource-constrained hardware, and the 73% improvement on long-horizon real-robot tasks suggests better robustness to compounding errors. The finding that receding-horizon control completely fails (0/30) on a low-cost mobile manipulator while closed-loop works challenges the common assumption that action chunking is universally beneficial. Open questions include scaling to orders-of-magnitude larger behavior datasets, learning shared latent action spaces across embodiments for policy transfer, and whether the discrete action space can accelerate real-world RL.
