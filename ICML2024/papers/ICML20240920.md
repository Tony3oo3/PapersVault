## Problem

Unsupervised domain adaptation methods operate exclusively in pixel-space and struggle with challenging domain shifts such as geographic disparities or ego-to-exo video perspective changes, where accurately characterizing and bridging distribution gaps from images alone becomes intractable. The paper identifies that text descriptions, which are ubiquitously available as metadata or generatable via captioning models, exhibit significantly smaller domain gaps than images (9.5% vs 17.1% transfer drop on GeoNet), yet remain largely unexploited for cross-domain transfer.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes labeled source images with associated text descriptions (alt-text, hashtags, captions, or action narrations) and unlabeled target images with their own text descriptions; at inference, only the image or video clip is needed, producing a class label (or action label for videos, or an outlier flag in open-set settings). Text descriptions are required only during training and incur zero test-time overhead. For GeoNet, text comes from public metadata; for DomainNet, captions are generated by BLIP-2; for Ego2Exo, atomic action descriptions from EgoExo4D serve as text supervision.

## Method

LaGTran trains a DistilBERT sentence classifier on source-domain (text, label) pairs, then freezes it to generate pseudo-labels for all target-domain text descriptions via argmax over class probabilities. These pseudo-labels are used to jointly train a vision backbone (ViT-B for GeoNet, Swin-B for DomainNet, Omnivore features for Ego2Exo) alongside the labeled source images, with equal sampling from both domains per mini-batch to balance dataset sizes. The core mechanism is a simple cross-modal supervision transfer: discriminative knowledge learned in the lower-dimensional, more domain-robust text space is distilled into the image classifier without any adversarial alignment, MMD, or self-training loop. For open-world transfer, a maximum softmax probability threshold (τ=0.75) on the text classifier assigns an outlier class to target samples, enabling joint closed-set and open-set classification.

## Results

On GeoNet (600-class GeoImnet and 205-class GeoPlaces), LaGTran achieves 60.24% average Top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best prior UDA method PMTrans (50.22%) by ~10% and the source-only baseline (46.53%) by ~14%. On DomainNet-345, LaGTran sets a new SOTA with 72.41% average accuracy across 12 transfer settings, surpassing PMTrans (69.63%) and CLIP zero-shot (70.38%). On the open-world GeoUniDA benchmark, LaGTran achieves 61.16% H-score, exceeding OVANet (47.26%) by ~14%. On the proposed Ego2Exo video benchmark, LaGTran reaches 21.55% average Top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best video UDA method TransVAE (17.70%) by ~4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaGTran wins across all 19 baselines on every dataset. On GeoNet, it outperforms the strongest UDA method PMTrans by ~10% (60.24% vs 50.22%), CLIP zero-shot with domain-aware prompting by ~10% (60.24% vs 50.36%), and the text-only TextMatch baseline by ~8% (60.24% vs 51.92%), confirming that source-trained text classification is superior to both image-based adaptation and direct text-label matching. On DomainNet, LaGTran surpasses PMTrans by ~3% (72.41% vs 69.63%) and CLIP by ~2% (72.41% vs 70.38%), while older UDA methods like MCD (34.01%), MDD (47.11%), and SCDA (48.55%) are far behind. On GeoUniDA (open-world), LaGTran's 61.16% H-score exceeds DANCE (51.75%) by ~9% and OVANet (47.26%) by ~14%, with the source-only MSP baseline at 50.20%. On Ego2Exo, LaGTran (21.55%) beats TransVAE (17.70%) by ~4%, TA3N (17.44%) by ~4%, and zero-shot video models EgoVLP (12.62%) and LaVILA (14.51%) by 7–9%, though it remains below the target-supervised upper bound (25.55%).

## Relation to Prior Work

LaGTran departs from the dominant UDA paradigm of pixel-space domain alignment (adversarial, MMD-based, self-training, patch-mixing) by instead exploiting the lower-dimensional, more domain-invariant text modality as an intermediary for knowledge transfer. Conceptually, it builds on the observation that text descriptions (from metadata, captioning models, or narrations) are ubiquitously available and carry discriminative semantic content, but repurposes a simple source-trained BERT sentence classifier as a cross-modal pseudo-label generator rather than using text for representation alignment or zero-shot prompting as in CLIP-based or language-guided domain generalization methods. The closest prior work (Goyal et al., 2023) uses only label names as text, whereas LaGTran leverages free-form captions or atomic action descriptions, and unlike ego-exo alignment methods that require paired videos, it operates in a standard unsupervised transfer setting with unpaired data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a conceptually trivial pipeline (train a text classifier, generate pseudo-labels, jointly train an image classifier) can outperform years of sophisticated pixel-space adaptation by 10%+ on challenging geographic transfer, suggesting that the field may have over-invested in complex domain alignment when a simpler cross-modal signal suffices. The Ego2Exo benchmark fills a notable gap in video domain adaptation by providing the first unsupervised ego-to-exo transfer setting with fine-grained action labels and text supervision. Key limitations include dependence on external VLMs (e.g., BLIP-2) for text generation when metadata is absent, residual domain gaps within the text modality itself, and the requirement that text descriptions be available at training time—open questions include whether text-adaptation mechanisms could further close the remaining gap and whether the approach extends to settings where text quality is very low.
