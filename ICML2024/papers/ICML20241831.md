## Problem

Empirical studies have shown that larger language models are more sensitive to noise in in-context learning prompts than smaller models, yet the underlying mechanism remains unexplained. Existing theoretical analyses of ICL focus on approximation power or gradient-descent simulation but do not characterize how model scale (rank, number of heads) changes which hidden features the attention mechanism attends to, nor do they formally explain the resulting robustness gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241831/cropped_page7_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The theoretical analysis takes a token covariance matrix Λ ∈ R^{d×d}, a rank constraint r (linear setting) or head count m (parity setting), and an evaluation prompt with M noisy input-label pairs, and produces closed-form optimal attention weight matrices U* and V* that specify exactly which eigenvector directions the model uses for prediction. The behavior-difference theorems then output a decomposition of evaluation loss into signal, input-noise, and label-noise terms, quantifying how the loss gap between a rank-r and rank-r' model scales as (r'−r)/M · (‖s‖²_D + σ²). Empirically, the method consumes 16 in-context exemplars with a controllable fraction of flipped labels on GLUE/SST-2/Subj tasks and produces accuracy curves across Llama 3B/7B/13B/70B models.

## Method

The paper derives closed-form optimal solutions for two stylized ICL settings: (1) a one-layer single-head linear self-attention transformer pretrained on linear regression, where model scale is measured by the rank r of the key-query matrix U, and (2) a two-layer multi-head transformer with ReLU activations pretrained on sparse parity classification, where scale is the number of heads m. In both cases, the optimal solution is a truncated version of the full-capacity solution: the rank-r model retains only the top-r eigenvector directions of the token covariance (or, in the parity case, only the neurons covering the important task set S₁), while the full model covers all directions. The key behavioral consequence is a bias-variance trade-off: increasing r reduces the irreducible error from uncovered residual features (‖ξ‖²_D) but increases the finite-sample error from capturing additional noisy directions, yielding a loss gap of (r'−r)/M · (‖s‖²_D + σ²) between the two models. This is validated empirically by showing that Llama-70B attention maps attend to both relevant and irrelevant sentences while Llama-13B focuses only on relevant ones, and that larger models degrade more sharply under label-flip noise.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: it derives closed-form optimal solutions showing that a rank-r linear self-attention model retains only the top-r eigenvectors of the token covariance matrix (Theorem 4.1), and that the evaluation loss gap between a rank-r′ and rank-r model scales as (r′−r)/M · (‖s‖²_D + σ²) when the task signal lies entirely in the top-r directions (Theorem 4.3). Empirically, on five NLP tasks (SST-2, Subj, and three GLUE tasks) using Llama 3B/7B/13B/70B with M=16 in-context exemplars, larger models show significantly steeper accuracy degradation as the label-flip percentage increases, while smaller models remain near their 0%-flip performance. An attention-magnitude ablation (Figure 3) confirms that Llama-2-70B attends to both relevant and irrelevant sentences, whereas Llama-2-13B focuses predominantly on relevant content.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not propose a new method to benchmark against external baselines; instead, it compares model scales (Llama 3B, 7B, 13B, 70B) against each other under increasing label noise. At 0% label flips, larger models consistently achieve higher in-context accuracy across all five datasets, confirming the standard scaling trend. However, as the fraction of flipped labels grows (up to 100%), the 70B model's accuracy drops sharply—often approaching or falling below random guessing—while the 3B model's accuracy remains relatively flat, consistent with the theoretical prediction that smaller models ignore noise in less-important feature directions. The same pattern holds for both chat (instruction-tuned) and base (non-instruction-tuned) model families, though base models show an even stronger robustness gap. The attention ablation further shows that the 70B model allocates comparable attention mass to irrelevant inserted sentences (from GSM-IC) as to the original SST-2 input, whereas the 13B model largely ignores the irrelevant content, directly illustrating the 'distractibility' mechanism predicted by Theorems 4.2–4.3 and 5.2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear self-attention ICL framework of Zhang et al. (2023b) and Wu et al. (2024) by introducing rank constraints on attention matrices to model scale, and further generalizes to a non-linear setting using the two-layer multi-head transformer architecture from Li et al. (2023b) on sparse parity data (following Daniely & Malach, 2020). While prior ICL theory focused on approximation power (constructing transformers that can perform ICL) or characterized single-layer single-head solutions, this paper's key departure is providing closed-form optimal solutions under rank/head constraints and rigorously analyzing how the resulting attention patterns differ across scales, thereby explaining the empirically observed noise-sensitivity gap between large and small models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first clean theoretical mechanism explaining why larger LLMs are more vulnerable to label and input noise during ICL: rank-constrained optimal solutions focus on top-variance feature directions, so smaller models inherently ignore noisy low-variance directions while larger models attend to them. This has practical implications for prompt engineering and noise injection strategies when deploying LLMs. However, the analysis is limited to stylized settings (one-layer linear attention on linear regression, two-layer multi-head on sparse parity) and does not extend to real multi-layer transformers or study optimization trajectories, leaving open whether the same mechanism governs behavior in practical architectures.
