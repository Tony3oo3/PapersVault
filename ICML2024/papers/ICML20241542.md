## Problem

Reward-free RL (Jin et al., 2020) provides a single exploration phase that yields near-optimal policies for any reward function in the risk-neutral setting, but no analogous efficient exploration strategy exists for risk-sensitive RL. As RL is increasingly deployed in safety-critical domains where tail-risk matters, the paper asks whether a provably efficient exploration algorithm can be designed for CVaR-based reward-free RL that works uniformly across all reward functions and risk tolerance levels.

## Input / Output

During exploration, the algorithm interacts with a tabular MDP (S states, A actions, horizon H) without any reward signal, using only the risk tolerance τ and accuracy ε as parameters. During planning, a specific deterministic reward function r: S×A→[0,1] is supplied, and the algorithm outputs a near-optimal deterministic Markovian policy ρ̂ over the augmented state space S×[0,H] along with an initial budget b̂∈[0,H]. The practical variant CVaR-VI-DISC additionally requires a discretization precision η=ετ/3H, restricting the budget to a finite grid of multiples of η, and guarantees the optimization error stays within ε/3.

## Method

The framework has two phases. In exploration, CVaR-RF-UCRL maintains upper confidence bounds E_h^t(s,a) on the Q-function estimation error that are independent of the policy, initial budget, and reward function; the agent follows a greedy policy selecting the action with the largest E at each state, and stops when E_1^t(s_1,·) drops below ετ/3, yielding Õ(S²AH⁴/ε²τ²) trajectories. In planning, the CVaR objective is reformulated over an augmented MDP with state (s,b) where b tracks the residual budget, converting the inherently history-dependent CVaR problem into a Markovian one solvable by dynamic programming. CVaR-VI performs exact value iteration over the continuous budget [0,H], while CVaR-VI-DISC discretizes rewards to a grid of precision η, reducing the DP to a finite computation with runtime O(S²AH³/ε²τ²). A matching lower bound of Ω(S²AH²/ε²τ) is established by reducing the risk-neutral reward-free lower bound to the CVaR setting via a constructed MDP that maps cumulative reward to CVaR.

## Results

The paper establishes a sample complexity upper bound of Õ(S²AH⁴/(ε²τ²)) for the CVaR-RF-UCRL exploration algorithm and a matching lower bound of Ω(S²AH²/(ε²τ)) for any CVaR-RF exploration algorithm, demonstrating near-optimality in S, A, and ε (with a gap of H²/τ). In numerical experiments on a 21×21 grid-world with H=20 and 30,000 transitions, the reward-free approach achieves CVaR errors of 0.05 (τ=0.05) and 0.006 (τ=0.95) under Reward Setup 1, and 0.023 (τ=0.05) and 0.003 (τ=0.95) under Reward Setup 2, all within the ε=0.1 threshold. The CVaR-VI-DISC planning algorithm has a runtime of O(S²AH³/(ε²τ²)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against other reward-free or risk-sensitive RL algorithms in a head-to-head benchmark. Instead, the primary validation is against an oracle baseline that uses the true transition probabilities P to compute the optimal CVaR policy, versus the proposed reward-free pipeline (CVaR-RF-UCRL exploration + CVaR-VI-DISC planning) that uses only the empirical P̂ estimated from reward-free data. In both reward setups, the reward-free approach achieves errors well within the theoretical ε=0.1 guarantee: 0.05 and 0.006 for Setup 1, and 0.023 and 0.003 for Setup 2. The visitation patterns of the reward-free policy closely match the oracle policy, confirming that the single exploration phase captures sufficient environment information. The theoretical contribution is positioned relative to the risk-neutral reward-free lower bound of Jin et al. (2020), showing an additional 1/τ factor is necessary due to CVaR's focus on tail outcomes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: reward-free RL (Jin et al., 2020; Kaufmann et al., 2021; Ménard et al., 2021), which decouples exploration from a specific reward function, and CVaR-based risk-sensitive RL (Chow & Ghavamzadeh, 2014; Tamar et al., 2015a,b; Wang et al., 2023), which replaces the expected-return objective with a tail-risk measure. The exploration algorithm CVaR-RF-UCRL adapts the UCRL-style upper-confidence-bound framework of Fiechter (1994) and Kaufmann et al. (2021) to the augmented MDP formulation of Bauerle & Ott (2011) and Wang et al. (2023), where the CVaR objective is recast as a minimization over a budget-augmented state space. The planning phase reuses the discretization technique of Bastani et al. (2022) and Wang et al. (2023) to make value iteration computationally tractable over the continuous budget dimension.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provably efficient framework that handles CVaR RL in a reward-free setting, filling a gap where prior risk-sensitive RL work assumed a known reward function and prior reward-free work was restricted to risk-neutral objectives. The near-optimal sample complexity (upper bound Õ(S²AH⁴/ε²τ²) vs. lower bound Ω(S²AH²/ε²τ)) and the adaptability result (exploration at tolerance τ suffices for all τ′ ≥ τ) make the framework practically useful for safety-critical applications where the reward structure may change. The remaining H² and 1/τ gap between upper and lower bounds, along with the restriction to tabular MDPs, leaves open the question of whether tighter bounds are achievable via empirical Bernstein inequalities or whether the framework extends to function-approximation settings.
