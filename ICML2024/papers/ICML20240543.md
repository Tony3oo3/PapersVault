## Problem

Forecasting SARS-CoV-2 hospitalizations requires integrating hundreds of predictors (409) from surveillance, weather, and electronic health records, yet the available time series is short (~586 days), creating a high-dimensional, sample-limited setting. Prior work identified elastic-net penalized regression as the best performer, but it is purely linear and cannot capture the non-linear temporal dynamics of epidemic waves. Reservoir Computing, which naturally models such dynamics through a random recurrent reservoir, had not been explored in this high-dimensional, short-series regime.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 409 daily predictors spanning department-level COVID-19 surveillance (Santé Publique France), NOAA weather variables, hospital EHR counts (BUH), and 1st/2nd-order derivatives computed over a 7-day window; all features are smoothed via local polynomial regression (span 21 days) and max-abs scaled to [−1, 1]. The target is the 14-day-ahead hospitalization count, but training is performed on the *change* in hospitalizations (h_{t+14} − h_t) rather than the raw level, which the authors found easier for ML models to learn. Evaluation uses a rolling one-day-over-two protocol with a one-year sliding training window, and the final prediction is the median of 40 independently initialized RC instances.

## Method

The proposed RC-GA method couples an Echo State Network (500 recurrent units, with both the input layer and reservoir connected to a linear ridge-regression readout) with a Genetic Algorithm that simultaneously optimizes RC hyperparameters (ridge, spectral radius, leaking rate, input scaling) and performs per-feature binary selection. The GA uses barycentric crossover, tournament selection, and Gaussian mutation over a population of 200 individuals for 30 generations, treating each of the 409 features as a categorical (include/exclude) hyperparameter. The key finding is that RC without feature selection performs poorly (MAE 17.59) in this high-dimensional regime, but GA-driven selection of ~200 features combined with the reservoir's non-linear temporal mixing reduces MAE to 15.27, outperforming elastic-net, XGBoost, LSTM, Transformers, and other baselines. Increasing the reservoir to 2000 units further improves MAE to 14.66, and aggregating 20 reservoirs during hyperparameter optimization (rather than 3) helps the GA avoid a suboptimal leaking-rate local optimum.

## Results

On the 14-day SARS-CoV-2 hospitalization forecast task (409 predictors, 586 days), the best RC-GA variant (RC-GA with 20 reservoirs for hyperparameter optimization and monthly update) achieves a MAE of 14.70, while RC-(GA-500)-2k (2000 neurons, no monthly update) reaches 14.66 MAE. The standard RC-GA (500 units, no monthly update) achieves MAE 15.27, MAEB −3.31, MRE 0.24, and MREB 0.84. All RC-GA variants substantially outperform Transformer-based methods (MAE 18.34–19.28) and Prophet (MAE 21.30), and modestly outperform XGBoost (15.45) and Elastic-net (15.83).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RC-GA (MAE 15.27) outperforms Elastic-net from the prior Ferté et al. (2022) work (MAE 15.83) by 0.56, confirming that non-linear temporal interactions captured by the reservoir improve over the best linear model previously identified. Against LSTM (Gers et al. 2000, MAE 15.74 with PCA dimension reduction), RC-GA wins by 0.47 MAE, and the gap widens to 4.01 MAE against the Transformer implementation based on Zeng et al. (2023) (MAE 19.28). RC-GA also beats XGBoost (15.45) by a narrow 0.18 MAE margin, and substantially outperforms all other Transformer variants (Informer 18.70, PatchTST 18.34) and Prophet (21.30). Notably, RC without feature selection (RC-GA no FS, MAE 17.59) or with random search instead of GA (RC-RS, MAE 18.45) degrades significantly, demonstrating that the GA-driven feature selection is critical for RC to succeed in this high-dimensional, sample-limited setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Reservoir Computing (Jaeger 2001; Maass 2002; Lukoševičius & Jaeger 2009) into a regime where prior RC applications to epidemic forecasting (Ghosh et al. 2021; Ray et al. 2021; Liu et al. 2023) used only a single time series as input, and where prior high-dimensional RC work (Arcomano et al. 2020; Weddell et al. 2021) relied on orders of magnitude more observations (10⁴–10⁵). The key departure is coupling RC with a Genetic Algorithm for simultaneous feature selection and hyperparameter optimization, addressing the 409-predictor / 586-observation regime that the authors' own prior work (Ferté et al. 2022) identified as a high-dimensional challenge best handled by elastic-net. The GA component draws on metaheuristic optimization literature (Ezugwu et al. 2020) and the barycentric crossover / tournament selection framework from classical evolutionary computation (Goldberg 1989; Holland 1975).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that non-linear temporal interactions (captured by the reservoir) are essential for anticipating epidemic shifts that linear models like elastic-net miss, and that the reservoir layer's relative importance to the output layer increases precisely during epidemic surges — a mechanistic insight with practical value for hospital planning. However, the evidence is confined to a single French hospital over one pandemic, monthly hyperparameter updates proved counterproductive (GA converging to a suboptimal high leaking rate), and the performance gain over the previous best (elastic-net) is modest (~3.5% MAE reduction). Open questions include generalizability to other pathogens or healthcare settings, whether the approach benefits from more aggressive GA exploration to avoid local optima, and whether parallelizing reservoirs with shared output layers could capture multiple epidemic regimes simultaneously without proportional compute cost.
