## Problem

Existing SOTA unlearning algorithms for LLMs (e.g., Gradient Ascent) judge success by the average memorization score across the entire forget set, but this aggregate metric masks a non-uniform, long-tailed distribution in which some sequences remain highly memorized and trivially extractable while others are over-forgotten and also become extractable via the Streisand effect. The paper argues that unlearning success must be assessed at the per-sequence level and that no existing algorithm provides the fine-grained, per-example memorization control needed to close these privacy loopholes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained causal language model (GPT-Neo at 125M, 1.3B, or 2.7B parameters) and a forget set of 200-token textual sequences drawn from the Pile corpora, operating without access to the full pretraining dataset. It produces modified model weights in which each forget-set sequence has its exact memorization score (the fraction of tokens the model predicts verbatim) brought within an empirically calibrated band (15%–60%) that matches the memorization profile of unseen text. Utility is then evaluated on four dialogue generation benchmarks (WoW, WoI, BST, ED) and nine NLP classification tasks (ARC, COPA, Lambada, Winogrande, PubmedQA, Piqa, Hellaswag, MathQA).

## Method

The paper introduces two algorithms that provide per-sequence memorization control. Selective Gradient Ascent (SGA) performs a two-step optimization: it first maximizes the causal LM loss only on sequences whose memorization score exceeds a threshold γ (recomputing the target subset each epoch), then switches to maximizing loss on the top-k most memorized sequences until the forget-set average meets the forgetting threshold. Task Arithmetic for Unlearning (TAU) first runs SGA for a few epochs to standardize the memorization distribution, then subtracts a scaled drift vector—obtained by fine-tuning on the forget set—from the SGA-adjusted weights, with a hyperparameter λ controlling the subtraction magnitude. The paper also contributes a new privacy metric that counts sequences whose post-unlearning memorization falls outside [15%, 60%], and a loss-based Membership Inference Attack (MIAU) that compares a sequence's loss to the average loss of its masked-token neighbors, empirically demonstrating that over-forgotten sequences become more extractable (the Streisand effect in LLMs) and that SOTA aggregate-based methods are vulnerable to this attack.

## Results

Across GPT-Neo models (125M, 1.3B, 2.7B) unlearning 16 textual sequences, SGA achieves the strongest privacy with only 1.8, 1.0, and 0.8 extractable outliers respectively, compared to 5.0, 4.6, and 7.0 for the SOTA GA baseline. For utility, TA and TAU preserve text generation quality best in the 125M model (F1=7.18 and 7.37 vs. GA's 2.28), while SGA matches or exceeds the default model's F1 in the 1.3B (12.63 vs. 12.86) and 2.7B (10.94 vs. 11.23) models. TAU achieves the best cross-entropy in the 2.7B model (3.27, matching GA and the default), and SGA achieves the highest classification accuracy in the 2.7B model (57.22% vs. GA's 56.25%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GA (Jang et al., 2022), the SOTA unlearning baseline, SGA reduces extractable outliers by 64–89% across model sizes (e.g., 1.0 vs. 4.6 on 1.3B; 0.8 vs. 7.0 on 2.7B) while matching or exceeding GA's F1 in the 1.3B and 2.7B models. TA (Ilharco et al., 2022, adapted for unlearning) dramatically outperforms GA on utility in the 125M model (F1 7.18 vs. 2.28) but degrades in larger models where SGA and TAU take over. DPD (Majmudar et al., 2022) achieves competitive extractable counts in the 125M model (8.8) but its F1 drops to 6.27–6.52 across all sizes, and it does not modify model weights, leaving it vulnerable to white-box attacks. GA+Mismatch (Yao et al., 2024, evaluated in Appendix D) is never the top performer: it is always worse than GA on F1, CE, and ACC, and while it slightly improves extractable counts over GA, it still lags behind SGA and TA on all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two distinct lines of work: Gradient Ascent (GA) for unlearning in LLMs (Jang et al., 2022) and Task Arithmetic for editing/detoxifying LLMs (Ilharco et al., 2022). The key conceptual departure is shifting the unlearning success criterion from aggregate (average) memorization across the forget set to per-example memorization control, motivated by the observation that SOTA methods like GA leave highly-memorized outliers that remain extractable. SGA introduces a two-step selective optimization on top of GA, while TAU repurposes Task Arithmetic—previously used only for detoxification and bias removal—for the first time in the unlearning setting, preceded by an SGA warm-up to standardize the memorization distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that the Streisand effect (over-unlearning making data *more* extractable via membership inference) occurs in LLMs, with a 50% increase in MIA success after GA, is a concrete privacy vulnerability in the current SOTA. The finding that no single unlearning method dominates across model sizes and forget-set cardinalities provides practitioners with a practical decision map (TA/TAU for small models, SGA for large models). However, the evaluation is limited to GPT-Neo (125M–2.7B) and verbatim memorization; open questions include whether per-example control scales to 10B+ models, whether it addresses higher-level factual associations (e.g., entity-PII links), and whether the empirically chosen thresholds (lb=15%, ub=60%) generalize across training corpora.
