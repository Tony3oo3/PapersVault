## Problem

Existing approaches to enrich GNNs with pattern counts—subgraph counts and homomorphism counts—are incomparable in expressiveness: each can distinguish graph pairs the other cannot. The paper asks what information can be injected that strictly subsumes both approaches, and shows the answer is the homomorphism basis (Spasm) of the target pattern, which yields strictly more expressive architectures at no additional computational cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes a featured graph plus precomputed homomorphism counts of all graphs in the Spasm (or anchored Spasm* for node-level) of a chosen target pattern, encoded via a 2-layer MLP or sinusoidal positional encoding. Counts are injected either at node-level (one vector per vertex, summing to the global count) or graph-level (a single vector fed to the final readout MLP). Outputs are task-dependent: node embeddings for classification, pooled graph embeddings for regression (ZINC, QM9), or node-pair scores for link prediction (COLLAB).

## Method

The core contribution is a theoretical and practical framework showing that injecting the homomorphism counts of all quotient graphs (Spasm) of a target pattern F strictly dominates injecting either Sub(F,·) or Hom(F,·) alone (Theorem 4.1), even for higher-order GNNs. This is computationally free because the most efficient algorithm for computing Sub(F,G) already proceeds by computing the Spasm homomorphism counts via tree-decomposition dynamic programming. The framework extends to node-level via anchored Spasm* (Theorem 4.5), avoids redundancy by only injecting basis terms whose treewidth exceeds the GNN's expressiveness level, and generalizes beyond fixed patterns to arbitrary graph motif parameters including induced subgraph counts and first-order logic satisfiability counts (Proposition 4.6).

## Results

On ZINC-12K graph regression, injecting Spasm* homomorphism counts yields the best MAE across all architectures: GAT 0.147, GCN 0.165, GIN 0.146, and BasePlanE 0.100, substantially outperforming both subgraph counts (Sub) and single-pattern homomorphism counts (Hom). On QM9, R-GCN augmented with graph-level homomorphism counts of Ω≤5^con ∪ {C6} reduces MAE by 40–85% across all 13 quantum-chemical properties (e.g., ZPVE from 17.40 to 3.03). On the BREC expressiveness benchmark, PPGN + Hom(Ω≤5^con) achieves 76.25% total graph-pair distinguishability, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art I²-GNN (70.2%) and 2-WL (67.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Bouritsas et al.'s subgraph counting (Sub), the proposed Spasm/Spasm* approach clearly wins on GAT (0.147 vs 0.210 MAE) and GCN (0.165 vs 0.206), while matching GIN (0.146 vs 0.147) and BasePlanE (0.100 vs 0.108). Against Barcelo et al.'s single-pattern homomorphism counts (Hom), Spasm* outperforms on every architecture, with the largest gap on GAT (0.147 vs 0.269) and GIN (0.146 vs 0.208). On BREC, PPGN + Hom(Ω≤5^con) achieves 76.25% total distinguishability, beating I²-GNN (70.2%), GSN (63.5%), and 2-WL (67.5%), with the largest gain in the Regular category (85.7% vs 35.7% for PPGN alone). On COLLAB link prediction, injecting Spasm(P6) homomorphism counts with GAT reaches 51.62% Hits@50, outperforming the previously studied clique-count injection (49.75% for K5). On QM9, R-GCN + Hom(Ω≤5^con ∪ {C6}) reduces MAE by 40–85% over the R-GCN baseline across all 13 properties, confirming that graph-level homomorphism basis injection is effective even without node-level features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and supersedes two previously incomparable approaches to enhancing GNN expressiveness: subgraph counting (Bouritsas et al., 2023; Bevilacqua et al., 2022) and homomorphism counting (Barcelo et al., 2021). It builds on the graph-theoretic result of Curticapean et al. (2017) that any graph motif parameter decomposes into a linear combination of homomorphism counts over its Spasm (quotient graphs), and shows that injecting the full homomorphism basis strictly subsumes both prior approaches without additional computational cost. The work also connects to the k-WL expressiveness hierarchy (Dvorák, 2010; Neuen, 2023) and recent characterizations of which homomorphism counts are already expressible by specific GNN architectures (Zhang et al., 2024), enabling redundancy-aware feature selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, fine-grained recipe for targeted expressivity gains: identify the target pattern, compute its Spasm, and inject those homomorphism counts — a procedure that is both strictly more expressive and computationally no harder than prior methods. Open questions remain around encoding large homomorphism count values in large graphs (where positional encoding proved critical), selecting appropriate bases for domain-specific tasks, and extending the framework to logical formulas beyond fixed pattern counting. The finding that even planar-graph-complete architectures like BasePlanE benefit from basis injection suggests that distinguishing graphs is a weaker requirement than capturing motif parameters, a distinction that may inform future architecture design.
