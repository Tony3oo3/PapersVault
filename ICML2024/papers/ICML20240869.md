## Problem

RL-based alignment (e.g., PPO) suffers from high variance in policy gradient estimation, impeding efficient convergence, while DPO—though avoiding RL—was shown to implicitly minimize the forward KL divergence against the optimal policy, yielding a mean-seeking approximation that fails to capture the principal modes of the target distribution. The paper identifies that the KL-regularized reward maximization objective is in fact equivalent to minimizing the reverse KL (mode-seeking) against the optimal policy, but this probability-matching equivalence had not been exploited for practical optimization.

## Input / Output

EXO consumes a text prompt x, K i.i.d. completions y_{1:K} sampled from the frozen SFT policy π_sft(·|x), and either a reward model score r_φ(x,y_i) for each completion or a binary preference label (chosen y_w vs. rejected y_l) that is smoothed into a soft distribution with hyperparameter ε. The output is an updated autoregressive policy π_θ that, at inference time, generates text completions exhibiting mode-seeking behavior—concentrating probability mass on the principal modes of the optimal policy rather than spreading mass across the mean of all modes. The method requires no reward model at inference and operates entirely through standard gradient backpropagation on the empirical KL loss.

## Method

The key theoretical insight is proving that maximizing the KL-regularized reward objective J_lhf^β(π_θ) is equivalent to minimizing the expected reverse KL D_KL(π_θ^β_π ‖ π_β_r^*) against the analytic optimal policy, where π_θ^β_π is a tempered version of the parametrized policy. EXO then realizes this reverse KL by drawing K samples from π_sft and computing the KL between two empirical distributions: p_{f_θ} (self-normalized exponentials of the log-ratio log π_θ/π_sft raised to β_π) and p_{r_φ} (self-normalized exponentials of reward/β_r), thereby avoiding the intractable partition function Z_β. Theorem 3.2 guarantees that the gradient of the EXO loss aligns exactly with the gradient of the alignment objective as K→∞, for arbitrary policy parametrization. In contrast, the paper proves (Theorem 3.3) that DPO's general form minimizes the forward KL D_KL(π_β^* ‖ π_θ), which under finite model capacity produces a mean-seeking policy that overestimates the long tail of π_sft and fails to concentrate on the optimal policy's modes—a gap empirically confirmed via density-ratio visualizations and win-rate benchmarks on summarization, dialogue, and instruction-following tasks.

## Results

On the TL;DR summarization task, EXO_rw achieves 97.3% win rate vs SFT and 76.4% vs Chosen under the reward model, and 88.5% vs SFT and 64.0% vs Chosen under GPT-4 evaluation, outperforming all computationally efficient baselines. On the Anthropic-HH dialogue task, EXO_rw reaches 85.6% vs SFT and 87.2% vs Chosen (reward model) and 83.5% vs SFT and 60.0% vs Chosen (GPT-4). In the controlled IMDB experiment, EXO achieves the most efficient reward-vs-KL frontier, consistently attaining higher oracle reward than DPO and PPO at the same KL divergence. EXO is the only practically efficient method to exceed 60% GPT-4 win rate against chosen responses on both public datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EXO outperforms DPO across all settings and metrics. On TL;DR, EXO_pref beats DPO_pref by 24.2 points (92.5 vs 68.3) in reward-model win rate vs SFT and by 36.4 points (60.1 vs 23.7) vs Chosen; EXO_rw beats DPO_rw by 14.6 points (97.3 vs 82.7) vs SFT and 36.6 points (76.4 vs 39.8) vs Chosen under the reward model. Against PPO, EXO_rw leads by 4.1 points (97.3 vs 93.2) in reward-model win rate vs SFT and by 12.0 points (64.0 vs 52.0) in GPT-4 win rate vs Chosen on TL;DR; on Anthropic-HH, EXO_rw beats PPO by 10.6 points (85.6 vs 75.0) in reward-model win rate vs SFT and 17.0 points (83.5 vs 66.5) in GPT-4 win rate vs SFT. The Best-of-N baseline achieves comparable or slightly higher reward-model scores but suffers the largest GPT-4 win-rate drop (e.g., 83.5% vs SFT on TL;DR), indicating over-exploitation of the imperfect reward model. The paper also shows in the controlled IMDB experiment that DPO_rw's frontier degrades as K increases (from 4 to 8), consistent with its mean-seeking forward-KL behavior, while EXO_rw's frontier continues to improve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the RLHF alignment framework of Ziegler et al. (2019), which formulated alignment as KL-regularized reward maximization, and on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), which eliminated the need for RL by exploiting the analytic optimal policy. The key departure is a theoretical reframing: the authors prove that the alignment objective is equivalent to minimizing the *reverse* KL divergence to the optimal policy, and that DPO in its general form actually minimizes the *forward* KL — a mean-seeking objective that fails to capture the modes of the target distribution under finite model capacity. This connects to the probability-matching perspective discussed in maxent RL analysis (Eysenbach & Levine, 2019; Korbak et al., 2022) but is the first to derive a practical optimization procedure from it, and to the weighted-regression family in offline RL (Peters & Schaal, 2007; Peng et al., 2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that DPO is a mean-seeking approximation rather than an exact optimization of the alignment objective challenges a widely-adopted method and provides a principled explanation for its empirical shortcomings. EXO offers a drop-in replacement with clean theoretical guarantees (asymptotic gradient equivalence to the RL objective) and consistent empirical gains across summarization, dialogue, and instruction-following at multiple model scales. Open questions remain: the method still suffers from reward over-optimization when the reward model is imperfect, scaling beyond 6B parameters is untested, and the authors note that dynamic reward model improvement and uncertainty-aware regularization could further close the gap to true human preferences.
