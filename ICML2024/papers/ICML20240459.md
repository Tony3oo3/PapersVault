## Problem

Prior 4-bit LLM quantization has relied on integer formats (INT4) or normal-distribution-based lookup tables (NF4), but it is unclear what distribution LLM weights and activations actually follow or what the theoretically optimal 4-bit grid should be. The paper addresses this by profiling 30+ DNNs to identify the underlying distribution and then systematically mapping the accuracy-versus-hardware-efficiency tradeoff across 11 candidate datatypes, including previously unexplored FP4 variants and their supernormal extensions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240459/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw weight and activation tensors from 30+ DNNs (LLMs, BERT-like transformers, CNNs) to fit distribution parameters, and produces 16-value 4-bit datatype definitions: lookup tables for SF4 and NF4, or hardware-efficient format specifications for E2M1, E2M1+SR, E2M1+SP, APoT4, and APoT4+SP. Quantized models use symmetric sub-channel quantization with block size 128 and optional MSE clipping, evaluated on LAMBADA, WikiText-2, and five zero-shot benchmarks (HellaSwag, Winogrande, PIQA, BoolQ, ARC-c). Hardware costs are obtained by synthesizing SystemVerilog MAC units (multiplier + 256-term accumulator) at TSMC 28nm.

## Method

The key insight is that LLM weight and activation distributions are best approximated by Student's t-distributions with single-digit degrees of freedom (ν≈5), not normal distributions, which shifts the optimal quantization grid toward the tails. SF4 is derived by equalizing probability mass through the t-distribution quantile function Q_S(p;ν) with ν fixed at 5, directly generalizing NF4's normal-quantile construction. Using SF4 as a high-accuracy reference, the paper identifies that standard E2M1 wastes one of its 16 representable values on negative zero and proposes two supernormal extensions: super-range (SR) adds a single point at the distribution edge, while super-precision (SP) inserts an extra value within the distribution to better match SF4's symmetry. The paper then synthesizes MAC units for all 11 datatypes and maps the Pareto frontier, finding that E2M1+SP achieves near-SF4 accuracy with only 3.6% estimated system chip area overhead over INT4, while INT5 would add 17.7% overhead due to memory scaling.

## Results

On LLaMA2-7B weight-only quantization, SF4 (ν=5) achieves a mean relative accuracy drop of −0.22% from FP32 across six zero-shot benchmarks, compared to −1.10% for NF4 and −1.17% for INT4. E2M1 with super-precision (SP) reduces the average accuracy loss to −0.80% with only 3.6% MAC area overhead, while E2M1+SP increases Phi-2 accuracy by up to 2.19% with 1.22% estimated chip area overhead. The Pareto frontier spans INT4 (lowest area, −1.17% accuracy drop) through E2M1 (−0.85%, 0.6% overhead) to E2M1+SP (−0.80%, 3.6% overhead), with E2M1 reducing the average accuracy drop by 7.34% relative to INT4 at near-negligible system cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SF4 consistently outperforms NF4 (QLoRA, Dettmers et al. 2023) across all seven evaluated LLMs: on LLaMA2-7B, SF4 achieves 73.20% LAMBADA accuracy vs. NF4's 72.35%, and the mean Δ% improves from −1.10% to −0.22%. The Intel E2M1 variant (Shen et al. 2023) and bitsandbytes E2M1 variant (Dettmers et al. 2022) both underperform the standard E2M1 baseline on every model, with Δ% of −1.30% and −1.28% respectively on LLaMA2-7B, attributed to their subnormal values clustering too close to zero. APoT4 (Li et al. 2020) performs competitively with E2M1 (Δ% = −0.86% vs. −0.85% on LLaMA2-7B), and adding super-precision to APoT4 narrows the gap to SF4 (−0.39% vs. −0.22%), though APoT4 requires additional conversion logic from FP32/BF16 in practice. The super-range (SR) variant of E2M1 is consistently worse than the baseline (−3.49% on LLaMA2-7B), while super-precision (SP) provides a small but consistent improvement (−0.80% vs. −0.85%). Overall, the paper establishes E2M1+SP as the best hardware-efficient option, achieving near-SF4 quality with a dedicated MAC unit and only 3.6% system area overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NF4 (Dettmers et al., 2023) lookup-based quantization paradigm by replacing the assumed normal distribution with a Student's t-distribution, motivated by a large-scale profiling study across 30+ DNNs. It also builds on the APoT (Li et al., 2020) and FP8 (Kuzmin et al., 2022; Micikevicius et al., 2022) lines of work showing that non-integer formats outperform integers at low precision, and on FLIQS/MoFQ for systematic format comparisons. The key conceptual departure is using the t-distribution's degree-of-freedom parameter to analytically derive a theoretically optimal 4-bit datatype (SF4) and then using it as a reference to design hardware-efficient supernormal extensions to E2M1 and APoT4, bridging the gap between lookup-based accuracy and native MAC-unit efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first comprehensive Pareto frontier mapping 4-bit datatypes across both model accuracy and synthesized chip area (TSMC 28nm), giving hardware designers a clear tradeoff curve from INT4 to E2M1+SP. The practical finding that E2M1 with super-precision support recovers most of NF4's accuracy advantage over INT4 with only ~3.6% system area overhead is directly actionable for next-generation accelerator design. Open questions include whether the ν=5 choice generalizes to future model architectures, whether these trends persist at 3-bit or below, and whether the lookup-based SF4 can be practically deployed for activation quantization (requiring product quantization or similar). The super-range variant's consistent accuracy degradation across models is a useful negative result that constrains future format design.
