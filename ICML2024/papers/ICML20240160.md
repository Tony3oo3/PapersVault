## Problem

Contrastive image-text pre-training (e.g., CLIP) learns coarse-grained representations that discard fine-grained visual details, performing poorly on localization, counting, and spatial reasoning tasks. Existing fine-grained objectives (FILIP, GLoRIA, MGCA, PACL) address this but are computationally and memory expensive, unstable during training, and/or depend on pretrained encoders, making fair comparison and scaling to large batch sizes difficult.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240160/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images resized to 224×224 (split into P patches by a ViT encoder) and text captions tokenized with a 32k-vocabulary SentencePiece tokenizer (max 55 tokens). Beyond standard global image and text embeddings (512-dim), SPARC produces per-token language-grouped vision embeddings — alignment-weighted sums of image patches — and per-patch vision embeddings, all in a shared 512-dim space. The fine-grained loss operates within individual image-text pairs, requiring no cross-batch negatives, which is the key structural difference from prior fine-grained methods.

## Method

SPARC computes a similarity matrix between patch and token embeddings for each image-text pair, then sparsifies it with a threshold σ=1/P and min-max normalizes the surviving entries to obtain alignment weights. For each text token, a language-grouped vision embedding is formed as the weighted sum of patches using these alignment weights, enabling a flexible one-to-many mapping (e.g., all patches of a dog align to the token 'dog'). A sequence-wise contrastive loss contrasts each token embedding with its corresponding language-grouped vision embedding using only other tokens in the same caption as negatives, avoiding the expensive all-patch-by-all-token cross-batch computation. This fine-grained loss is combined with a standard global CLIP-style contrastive loss (weighted sum with λ_g and λ_f). Critically, SPARC avoids softmax for alignment weights, sidestepping the winner-takes-all dynamics that force one-to-one patch-token mappings and cause gradient vanishing at initialization.

## Results

On zero-shot ImageNet classification (ViT-B/16), SPARC achieves 74.7% top-1 accuracy, outperforming CLIP (73.9%) and MGCA (72.7%). On MSCOCO retrieval, SPARC reaches 57.6/43.0 R@1 for image-to-text/text-to-image, surpassing CLIP (56.2/42.4) and MGCA (59.8/43.0 on i2t but lower on other metrics). On fine-grained tasks, SPARC achieves 27.36 mIoU on Pascal VOC segmentation (vs. 23.02 for CLIP) and 25.1 AP on LVIS rare classes (vs. 22.0 for CLIP), demonstrating improved localization. SPARC adds only +0.55% compute over CLIP at batch size 16384.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPARC consistently outperforms all five baselines across both coarse-grained and fine-grained tasks. Against CLIP, SPARC gains +0.8% on ImageNet, +2.3% on ImageNet-R, +0.9% on ImageNet-C, and +1.4/+0.6 R@1 on MSCOCO i2t/t2i retrieval, while adding negligible compute overhead (+0.55%). Against MGCA (the strongest competing fine-grained method), SPARC improves ImageNet by +2.0%, ImageNet-R by +3.5%, and ImageNet-C by +2.9%, though MGCA slightly edges SPARC on MSCOCO i2t (59.8 vs. 57.6). GLoRIA underperforms CLIP on most metrics in the from-scratch pretraining setting (70.4 vs. 73.9 on ImageNet), while FILIP and PACL suffer severe degradation (61.4 and 63.3 on ImageNet respectively) due to training instability and reliance on pretrained components. On fine-grained localization tasks, SPARC's advantage is most pronounced: +4.34 mIoU over the next best method on Pascal VOC segmentation and +3.1 AP over CLIP on LVIS rare classes, confirming that the sparse alignment mechanism learns genuinely more informative local representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPARC builds directly on CLIP's global contrastive loss as one component of its objective, while departing from prior fine-grained alignment methods (FILIP, GLoRIA, MGCA, PACL) in three key ways: it replaces softmax-based attention weights with a sparse min-max normalized similarity to enable flexible one-to-many patch-to-token mappings, it computes the fine-grained loss within individual image-text pairs rather than across the full batch (reducing compute and memory), and it trains both encoders from scratch rather than relying on pretrained components. The paper explicitly positions itself as a like-for-like comparison that isolates the benefit of the sparse alignment objective by holding architecture, data, and training setup constant across all methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPARC's practical significance lies in achieving consistent improvements on both coarse-grained (classification, retrieval) and fine-grained (detection, segmentation, faithfulness) tasks with only +0.55% additional compute over CLIP, making it a drop-in replacement for standard contrastive pretraining. The ablation confirming that softmax's winner-takes-all dynamics specifically harm many-to-one alignment provides a useful design principle for future cross-modal attention mechanisms. Open questions include whether alternative sparsification strategies, richer captions, or additional supervision signals (bounding boxes, segmentation masks) could further improve the learned representations, and whether the approach extends to video or other modalities.
