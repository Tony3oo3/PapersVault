## Problem

Existing low-rank bandit algorithms either assume restrictive arm-set geometry (unit Frobenius ball, bilinear structure) or rely on computationally intractable sub-procedures, and lack a principled understanding of how arm-set geometry governs regret. Separately, the classical nuclear-norm-penalized least squares estimator for low-rank trace regression is suboptimal in several regimes, and no efficient experimental design criterion tailored specifically to low-rank matrix recovery has been proposed.

## Input / Output

The estimator consumes noisy linear measurements (X_i, Y_i) with Y_i = ⟨Θ*, X_i⟩ + η_i, the known population covariance matrix Q(π) of the measurement distribution, and a rank bound r; it outputs a rank-≤r matrix estimate Θ̂ with operator-norm error Õ(σ√(B(Q)/n₀)). In the bandit setting, the arm set A must be operator-norm-bounded (‖A‖_op ≤ 1), and the algorithm additionally requires solving a convex optimization to find the B-minimizing sampling distribution π* over A before exploration begins. A bootstrapped variant (Warm-LowPopArt) removes the need for a user-supplied pilot estimator by first running a coarse LowPopArt pass.

## Method

LowPopArt is a three-stage pipeline: (1) construct one-sample unbiased estimators Θ̃_i = Q(π)^{-1}(Y_i − ⟨Θ₀, X_i⟩)vec(X_i) using the known population covariance; (2) aggregate them via Minsker's Catoni-based robust matrix mean estimator, which symmetrizes each sample through a dilation operator and applies a log-quadratic transform to eigenvalues to control heavy tails; (3) perform SVD on the resulting matrix and hard-threshold singular values below a data-dependent cutoff to enforce rank-r structure. The recovery guarantee depends on a new quantity B(Q) — the maximum eigenvalue of block-diagonal slices of Q^{-1} — which is provably tighter than the 1/λ_min(Q) term appearing in nuclear-norm-penalized bounds. The paper further introduces a convex experimental design objective min_π B(Q(π)) that is computationally tractable (solvable via cvxpy) and yields arm-set-adaptive regret bounds through two bandit algorithms: LPA-ETC (explore-then-commit) achieving Õ((R_max r² B_min(A) T²)^{1/3}) regret, and LPA-ESTR (explore-subspace-then-refine) achieving Õ(σ√(S_*²/S_r² · B_min(A) d^{0.5} T)) regret under a well-conditioned Θ* assumption.

## Results

The paper's two proposed algorithms achieve arm-set-adaptive regret bounds: LPA-ETC (Algorithm 3) attains $\tilde{O}((R_{\max}r^2 B_{\min}(\mathcal{A})T^2)^{1/3})$ regret, which for the unit operator norm ball simplifies to $\tilde{O}(r^{2/3}d^{2/3}T^{2/3})$, and LPA-ESTR (Algorithm 4) attains $\tilde{O}(d^{1/4}\sqrt{B_{\min}(\mathcal{A})T(S_*/\lambda_r)})$ regret, simplifying to $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{d^{5/2}T})$ for the unit operator norm ball. The underlying LowPopArt estimator achieves operator norm recovery error $\tilde{O}(\sigma\sqrt{B(Q)/n_0})$, which is always tighter than the classical nuclear norm bound $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{r/(n\lambda_{\min}(Q)^2)})$ since $B(Q)\leq d/\lambda_{\min}(Q)$. Empirically, B_min-optimal exploration outperforms E-optimal design, and LowPopArt-based algorithms show lower and more stable cumulative regret than nuclear-norm-based and OFUL-based baselines in both ETC and ESTR settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LPA-ESTR strictly dominates LowESTR (Lu et al., 2021) in all cases: it removes the $\sqrt{r}$ factor and replaces the arm-set-dependent quantity $1/\lambda_{\min}(Q(\pi))^2$ with $B_{\min}(\mathcal{A})$, which satisfies $B_{\min}(\mathcal{A})\leq 1/\lambda_{\min}(Q(\pi))^2$ for all distributions. For the hard arm set $\mathcal{A}_{\text{hard}}$, this yields a $d$-factor improvement in the arm-set dependence (from $d^{13/2}$ to $d^{7/2}$). LPA-ETC achieves a $T^{2/3}$ regret rate that beats the $\sqrt{T}$ rate of LowLOC and rO-UCB when $T\ll O(d^9/(B_{\min}(\mathcal{A})^2 r))$, and unlike those methods it is computationally efficient. Compared to G-ESTT (Kang et al., 2022), LPA-ESTR removes the unknown arm-set constant $M$ (which can be as large as $d_1d_2$) in favor of the optimizable $B_{\min}(\mathcal{A})$. Empirically (Figures 2–3), LowPopArt with B_min-optimal design achieves lower nuclear norm recovery error than nuclear norm regularization, and both LPA-ETC and LPA-ESTR show lower and more stable cumulative regret than ESTR, rO-UCB, LowESTR, and vanilla OFUL in the respective experimental settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

LowPopArt adapts the one-sample estimator + Catoni robust mean + hard thresholding pipeline from PopArt (Jang et al., 2022) for sparse linear regression to the low-rank matrix setting, replacing coordinate-wise thresholding with SVD-based singular value truncation. The robust matrix mean estimation step relies on Minsker's (2018) Catoni-based estimator for heavy-tailed random matrices, while the bandit algorithms build on the Explore-Subspace-Then-Refine (ESTR) framework of Jun et al. (2019) and the LowOFUL subroutine. The key departure from prior work is the introduction of B(Q), a new experimental design criterion that directly characterizes low-rank estimation hardness, replacing the classical E-optimal design (maximizing λ_min(Q)) that underlies nuclear norm penalized least squares guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first computationally tractable experimental design criterion specifically tailored to low-rank matrix estimation, showing that the 'right' geometry quantity B_min(A) can yield d^{3/2}-factor tighter recovery guarantees than the classical 1/λ_min(Q)² dependence on adversarial arm sets. Both proposed bandit algorithms (LPA-ETC and LPA-ESTR) are computationally efficient and handle general arm sets, unlike prior methods that either require bilinear structure or are computationally intractable. Open questions include closing the (rd)^{1/3} gap between upper and lower regret bounds, matching the guarantees of specialized algorithms (e.g., Lattimore & Hao, 2021) for specific arm sets while maintaining generality, and extending LowPopArt to settings where the population covariance must itself be estimated from data.
