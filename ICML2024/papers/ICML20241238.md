## Problem

Existing LLM watermarking methods face a fundamental trade-off: fixed green/red list approaches (KGW-0) are robust but vulnerable to spoofing attacks, token-hash approaches (KGW-1/2) add security but lose robustness under paraphrasing, and distortion-free methods (EXP-edit) preserve text quality but lack robustness. No prior method simultaneously guarantees robustness, security against forgery, and text quality comparable to un-watermarked output while enabling model-agnostic detection without access to the original LLM or prompt.

## Input / Output

During generation, the method consumes the LLM's raw logits at each autoregressive step along with the previously generated token sequence, and produces a watermarked token sampled from a temperature-scaled distribution. During detection, a candidate text sequence is processed by a small auxiliary Measurement Model (e.g., GPT-2 or OPT-350M) for entropy estimation and a Sentence Embedding Model (all-mpnet-base-v2) for semantic extraction, yielding a scalar detection score (mean of δ·v̂ over identified watermarked tokens) without requiring the original LLM or prompt. A secret opening sentence of M=50 tokens is prepended to stabilize early-generation watermarking.

## Method

The Adaptive Watermark comprises three components. (1) AWTI uses a small auxiliary Measurement Model to compute Shannon entropy of each token distribution and only perturbs those exceeding a threshold α, leaving low-entropy (deterministic) tokens untouched to minimize quality degradation. (2) SLSVE extracts a per-step logits scaling vector by passing the Sentence-BERT embedding of the preceding text through a trained feedforward Semantic Mapping Model (two residual blocks), which is optimized with a multi-term loss enforcing semantic smoothness, uniform positive/negative split, and unbiased token preference, then binarized to a 0/1 vector. (3) AWTS applies the watermark via multiplicative temperature scaling—each logit is multiplied by (1 + δ·v̂_k)—so the perturbation magnitude is proportional to the original logit rather than a fixed additive bias, preserving distribution shape. Detection approximates a likelihood ratio test by averaging δ·v̂_k over AWTI-identified watermarked tokens, making the process fully model-agnostic.

## Results

Under GPT-3.5 paraphrase attack on OPT-6.7B, the proposed Adaptive Watermark achieves ROC-AUC of 0.981 and Best F1 of 0.932, outperforming KGW-0 (0.964/0.904), KGW-1 (0.874/0.815), KGW-2 (0.769/0.728), and EXP-edit (0.881/0.801). Under DIPPER paraphrase on the same model, it reaches ROC-AUC 0.964 and F1 0.903, again leading all baselines. In terms of text quality, the watermarked text perplexity is comparable to un-watermarked text and significantly lower than KGW-0 and KGW-1. For security, the decryption rate under a strengthened spoofing attack is 0.571, well below KGW-0 (0.963), KGW-1 (0.912), and KGW-2 (0.723).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four main baselines: KGW-0 (fixed green/red list, Zhao et al. 2023), KGW-1/KGW-2 (hash-based green/red list, Kirchenbauer et al. 2023a), and EXP-edit (distortion-free sampling watermark, Kuditipudi et al. 2023), plus two model-non-agnostic methods (SWEET and SIR) in the appendix. Under paraphrase attacks, the proposed method consistently outperforms all baselines in both ROC-AUC and F1 across OPT-2.7B, OPT-6.7B, and GPT-J-6B; the largest margin is against KGW-2 (e.g., 0.981 vs 0.769 ROC-AUC on OPT-6.7 under GPT-3.5). Against KGW-0, which is the most robust baseline, the advantage is smaller but consistent (0.981 vs 0.964 on OPT-6.7/GPT-3.5). EXP-edit, the distortion-free method, shows competitive robustness on GPT-J-6B under DIPPER (0.960 vs 0.963) but lags on other settings. On text quality (perplexity), the proposed method matches un-watermarked text and is clearly better than KGW-0/KGW-1, while being comparable to EXP-edit. On security (decryption rate under spoofing attack), the proposed method achieves 0.571 versus 0.963 for KGW-0, 0.912 for KGW-1, and 0.723 for KGW-2, demonstrating substantially stronger resistance to forgery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the KGW family of logit-manipulation watermarks (Kirchenbauer et al. 2023a; Zhao et al. 2023) and the distortion-free sampling approach of Kuditipudi et al. (2023), but departs from them in three key ways. First, it replaces hash-based or fixed green/red lists with a trained semantic mapping network (SLSVE) that derives the perturbation from sentence embeddings, directly addressing the spoofing vulnerability identified by Sadasivan et al. (2023). Second, it introduces an auxiliary measurement model for entropy-based token selection (AWTI), which generalizes the high-entropy perturbation idea from Lee et al. (2023) by removing the dependency on the original LLM and prompt. Third, it substitutes additive logit bias with multiplicative temperature scaling (AWTS), a perturbation mechanism not used in prior watermarking work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles the practical trilemma of robustness, security, and text quality that prior watermarking methods addressed in isolation, and its model-agnostic detection (no original LLM or prompt needed) makes it more deployable than SWEET or SIR. However, the approach adds substantial overhead—three auxiliary models (measurement, sentence-embedding, semantic-mapping) plus per-model tuning of δ and α—which limits plug-and-play adoption. Open questions include whether the semantic mapping model's robustness to paraphrase degrades under more aggressive adversarial edits, whether the entropy threshold generalizes across domains without re-tuning, and the lack of a theoretical guarantee on detection power under distribution shift.
