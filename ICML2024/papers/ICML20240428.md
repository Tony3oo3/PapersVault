## Problem

Distributed stochastic gradient tracking (DSGT) is the state-of-the-art decentralized SGD method, but its convergence rate degrades severely on general communication topologies because the parameter C_W in its complexity bound is not a constant. The paper asks whether a single-loop decentralized SGD algorithm can achieve lower iteration complexity than DSGT for all network topologies without requiring inner communication loops.

## Input / Output

Each of m agents holds a private non-IID dataset D^(i) and a local objective f_i(x); the only inter-agent communication is neighbor-to-neighbor governed by a mixing matrix W (doubly stochastic, possibly asymmetric for SS_DSGT or symmetric positive semi-definite for ASS_DSGT). The algorithm outputs an ε-suboptimal solution x̄_T to the aggregate objective f(x) = (1/m)Σf_i(x), with the number of communication rounds T scaling as O(σ̄²/(μmε) + √L·σ̄/(μ(1−λ₂(W))√ε)) for SS_DSGT and O(σ̄²/(μmε) + √L·σ̄/(μ√(1−λ₂(W))√ε)) for ASS_DSGT.

## Method

SS_DSGT extends the snap-shot gradient tracking idea to decentralized SGD by introducing a snapshot variable q_t that records a past position of x_t and is refreshed with probability p=θ. Instead of updating the gradient-tracking variable s_t with the full stochastic gradient at every iteration (as DSGT does), SS_DSGT updates s_t only when ζ_t=1, so that s_t tracks ∇F(q_t, ξ_τ) rather than ∇F(x_t, ξ_t); the x-update then uses the correction s_t + ∇F(x_t,ξ_t) − ∇F(q_t,ξ_τ) whose average equals the true stochastic gradient. This single modification reduces the topology dependence from (1−λ₂(W))^(−3/2) to (1−λ₂(W))^(−1). ASS_DSGT further wraps SS_DSGT in a loopless Chebyshev acceleration by replacing W with a 2m×2m augmented matrix W̃ that embeds a heavy-ball momentum, yielding a contraction rate of √(1−λ₂(W)) and matching the best-known (1−λ₂(W))^(−1/2) complexity while using a decaying step size that permits a larger initial learning rate.

## Results

The paper's primary contributions are theoretical: SS_DSGT achieves an iteration complexity of O(σ̄²/(μmε) + √Lσ̄/(μ(1−λ₂(W))√ε) + L/((1−λ₂(W))με^{1/3})), improving upon DSGT's O(σ̄²/(μmε) + √Lσ̄/(μ(1−λ₂(W))^{3/2}√ε)) by reducing the topology dependence from (1−λ₂(W))^{−3/2} to (1−λ₂(W))^{−1}. ASS_DSGT further achieves O(σ̄²/(μmε) + √Lσ̄/(μ√(1−λ₂(W))√ε) + L/(√(1−λ₂(W))με^{1/3})), matching DSGT's best-case rate without the C_W parameter. Empirically, on three datasets (banknote, a9a, ijcnn1) with m=20 agents, SS_DSGT converges faster than DSGT on the asymmetric topology W^asy (θ=0.0761), and ASS_DSGT shows the fastest practical convergence on the symmetric topology W^sy (θ=0.024).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is DSGT (Pu & Nedić, 2021). On the asymmetric mixing matrix W^asy (θ=0.0761), SS_DSGT consistently outperforms DSGT in terms of ‖x̄_t − x*‖² versus communication rounds across all three datasets (banknote, a9a, ijcnn1), with the most pronounced gap on a9a, validating the theoretical improvement from (1−λ₂(W))^{−3/2} to (1−λ₂(W))^{−1} dependence. On the symmetric mixing matrix W^sy (θ=0.024), ASS_DSGT achieves the fastest convergence among all three methods (SS_DSGT, ASS_DSGT, DSGT), despite sharing the same theoretical iteration complexity as DSGT, likely due to the loopless Chebyshev acceleration reducing the effective condition number. Notably, ASS_DSGT's theoretical rate of O(√Lσ̄/(μ√(1−λ₂(W))√ε)) is independent of the C_W parameter that appears in DSGT's improved analysis (Koloskova et al., 2021), making it robust across all network topologies rather than only those where C_W is a constant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the snap-shot gradient tracking (SS_GT) idea from Song et al. (2023), originally developed for deterministic decentralized optimization, to the stochastic setting, yielding SS_DSGT. It also builds directly on DSGT (Pu & Nedić, 2021) as the primary baseline, sharing its single-loop communication strategy while replacing the per-iteration gradient tracking update with a probabilistic snap-shot mechanism inspired by SVRG and loopless Katyusha. For the accelerated variant ASS_DSGT, the paper incorporates loopless Chebyshev acceleration (Arioli & Scott, 2014; Scaman et al., 2019) via an augmented 2m×2m mixing matrix, a technique previously applied to heavy-ball methods but not to decentralized stochastic gradient tracking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a concrete open question from Koloskova et al. (2021) by showing that the C_W parameter—which governed DSGT's worst-case complexity and was unknown to be tight—can be entirely eliminated, yielding a clean O((1−λ₂(W))⁻¹) dependence for SS_DSGT across all topologies. However, the practical validation is limited to small-scale l₂-regularized logistic regression with 20 agents on three tabular datasets, and the theoretical guarantees require strong convexity and smoothness, restricting applicability to modern deep learning objectives. Open questions include whether the snap-shot mechanism extends to non-convex or non-smooth settings, and whether the C_W-free bound is truly optimal for decentralized SGD.
