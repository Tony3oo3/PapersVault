## Problem

Existing latent representations learned via VAEs or inverse dynamics (e.g., ACRO) capture low-level position information but ignore the reachability geometry of the environment—states separated by obstacles are clustered together, violating the assumption that nearby latent states are mutually reachable. This inadequacy produces infeasible plans and degrades sample efficiency in goal-conditioned planning and policy learning. The paper asks: can we learn a representation where ℓ₂ distance in latent space faithfully reflects the number of transitions between states?

## Input / Output

The method consumes high-dimensional observation images (100×100 single-channel grids for 2D mazes, 84×84 RGB for Sawyer-Reach, or 4×4 grids of images with exogenous noise for MuJoCo tasks) along with a goal observation image, and produces a 256-dimensional continuous latent state, an action sequence for reaching the goal, and multi-level discrete state abstractions. The encoder φ and PCLAST map ψ are trained offline on transition data (500K random-policy samples for mazes, 20K for Sawyer-Reach), after which the representation is frozen for downstream planning or policy learning. The PCLAST map ψ specifically transforms the ACRO latent state into a metric space where Euclidean distance approximates transition count, enabling both k-means-based abstraction and CEM-based trajectory optimization.

## Method

PCLAST is a two-stage representation: first, an encoder φ is trained jointly with a multi-step inverse dynamics model f_AC (ACRO) to filter exogenous noise and capture the endogenous state; second, a separate map ψ is trained with a temporal contrastive loss derived from the likelihood of a Gaussian random walk, where positive pairs are states d steps apart (d ≤ d_m) and negative pairs are randomly sampled states. The contrastive objective P_k(y=1|s_t, s_{t+k}) = σ(e^α − e^β‖ψ(s_t) − ψ(s_{t+k})‖²) is fitted via binary cross-entropy, and critically, gradients from this loss do not backpropagate into φ to prevent it from re-acquiring exogenous information. A one-step forward model δ is learned in the ACRO latent space for planning. For goal-conditioned planning, the PCLAST embedding is discretized at n granularity levels via k-means, each yielding a discrete transition graph; Dijkstra's algorithm searches these graphs hierarchically from coarsest to finest to generate waypoints, and a low-level CEM trajectory optimizer (minimizing ‖ψ(s_t) − ψ(s*)‖² subject to δ dynamics) computes the action sequence to reach the nearest waypoint.

## Results

The n-Level Planner with PCLAST achieves the highest goal-reaching success rates across all maze environments: 97.8% on Hallway, 89.5% on Rooms, and 89.1% on Spiral, substantially outperforming the low-level-only planner (86.7%, 69.3%, 50.0%) and all model-free baselines. In reward-based PPO (dense), PCLAST representation yields 66.7% success on Hallway versus 10.0% for ACRO and 6.7% for raw images. In the exogenous-noise Cheetah-Run offline RL setting, PCLAST also outperforms ACRO, DRIML, HOMER, CURL, and 1-step inverse model baselines. Multi-level planning (n=5) provides a 3.8× computational speedup over n=2 (0.07 ms vs. 0.265 ms) without sacrificing plan quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCLAST decisively outperforms ACRO (Islam et al., 2022) in maze environments: under PPO with dense reward, PCLAST achieves 66.7% on Hallway vs. ACRO's 10.0%, and 61.7% on Spiral vs. 23.3%. For CQL (sparse), PCLAST wins on Hallway (40.0% vs. 15.0%) but ACRO edges ahead on Rooms (33.3% vs. 23.3%). Against raw image representations, PCLAST provides dramatic gains in mazes (e.g., 66.7% vs. 6.7% on Hallway with PPO-dense) but both perform comparably on Sawyer-Reach where the absence of obstacles makes the task easier. In the reward-free RIG setting, ACRO slightly outperforms PCLAST on Hallway (15.0% vs. 10.0%) and Spiral (12.0% vs. 10.0%), while both are far below the n-Level Planner. In exogenous-noise Cheetah-Run, PCLAST surpasses DRIML, HOMER, CURL, and 1-step inverse model baselines, confirming its advantage in non-linear dynamics with distractor noise. The n-Level Planner with PCLAST (97.8% Hallway) substantially exceeds the low-level-only planner (86.7%) and all model-free methods, validating the hierarchical abstraction approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

PCLAST builds directly on the ACRO framework (Islam et al., 2022) for learning endogenous state representations via multi-step inverse dynamics, and on the EX-BMDP theoretical foundation (Efroni et al., 2021) for filtering exogenous noise. The key conceptual departure is that while ACRO and related methods (HOMER, DRIML, CURL) focus on removing distractor information, they do not enforce the reachability geometry of the state space — states separated by walls can end up close in the learned embedding. PCLAST addresses this by learning an additional map ψ via a temporal contrastive objective derived from a Gaussian random walk likelihood, making ℓ₂ distance in the embedding approximate transition count. For planning, the paper combines this representation with Dijkstra's algorithm over k-means cluster abstractions and CEM for local trajectory optimization, extending the model-based planning literature (Finn & Levine, 2017; Hafner et al., 2019b) with a multi-level hierarchical structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete failure mode of noise-filtering representations — loss of reachability structure — and shows that fixing it yields substantial gains in goal-conditioned planning success rates, particularly in environments with obstacles. The hierarchical n-level planner provides a practical recipe for zero-shot planning to ad-hoc goals with 3.8× computational speedup over single-level planning. However, the approach requires domain knowledge to set K_max and cluster counts, is primarily validated in relatively simple 2D mazes and a single robotic arm, and the authors note it is less effective in linear-dynamics environments where standard methods already perform well. Open questions include whether the reachability-aware representation generalizes to high-dimensional continuous control with complex dynamics, and whether the contrastive objective can be made fully automatic without manual hyperparameter tuning.
