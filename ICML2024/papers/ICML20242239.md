## Problem

Existing defenses against model poisoning in federated learning rely on cross-client or global information (e.g., statistical outlier detection, Hessian-based consistency checks), which degrades severely under high non-IID data distributions and/or when a large fraction of clients are malicious. No prior defense can identify malicious clients based solely on the individual model update itself, without assuming similar data distributions across benign clients or a predominant benign majority.

## Input / Output

For each participating client, the method consumes the client's model update vector g_i^t, the current global model w^{t-1}, and a compact distilled local knowledge K_i (10 data-label pairs, ~2% of local data) that is maintained and refined across rounds. It outputs a scalar reconstruction error e_i^t per client, which is compared against a fixed threshold γ=0.6 to assign a benign/malicious label; clients exceeding the threshold are permanently expelled from the FL system. The remaining updates are aggregated (e.g., FedAVG) into the next global model, so the final output is both a clean global model and a set of permanently flagged malicious clients.

## Method

FedREDefense exploits the observation that model updates from genuine training can be approximately reconstructed by training a virtual network with a small set of distilled local knowledge, whereas hand-crafted poisoning updates (e.g., inverted gradients, scaled backdoor updates) cannot. The reconstruction is computed via a bi-level optimization: the inner loop trains a virtual network initialized at w^{t-1} using the distilled knowledge K_i for N=5 SGD steps, and the outer loop optimizes K_i and the virtual learning rate α_i over M iterations to minimize the normalized L2 distance between the virtual update and the actual client update. The resulting reconstruction error e_i^t is the ratio ‖ŵ_N − ŵ_0 − g_i^t‖²/‖g_i^t‖², which empirically separates benign clients (0–0.3) from malicious ones (0.7–1.0). Three acceleration strategies reduce overhead: compact parameterization (10 pairs), maintaining K_i across rounds so only a few refinement iterations are needed after the first participation, and dynamic early stopping that halts optimization once e_i^t < γ, yielding an average of ~2.4 outer-loop iterations per client per round versus ~60 for fixed-iteration baselines.

## Results

FedREDefense achieves 100% Detection Accuracy (DACC), 0% False Positive Rate (FPR), and 0% False Negative Rate (FNR) across all seven model poisoning attacks on all three datasets (FashionMNIST, CIFAR-10, CINIC), with an Averaged Aggregated Rounds (AAR) of 0, meaning malicious clients are filtered out immediately upon first participation. In terms of end-to-end testing accuracy (TACC), FedREDefense maintains near-clean performance under attack: 85.5–85.7% on FashionMNIST (vs. 86.04% no-attack), 61.0–61.2% on CIFAR-10 (vs. 61.69% no-attack), and 50.8–51.0% on CINIC (vs. 52.20% no-attack). For targeted attacks, the Attack Success Rate (ASR) is kept below 4.5% in all scenarios, compared to 75–98% ASR for undefended FedAVG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedREDefense outperforms all eight compared defenses across every dataset and attack scenario. FLDetector, the closest detection-based baseline, completely fails (100% FNR, AAR=500) on LIE, Min-Max, Min-Sum, and MPAF attacks, and still shows 60% AAR on Fang/DBA/Scaling, meaning malicious clients remain aggregated for 60 rounds before detection. FLAME and FLTrust suffer from high False Positive Rates (27–28% for FLAME, 12–18% for FLTrust), incorrectly excluding benign clients and degrading TACC by 3–15 points relative to FedREDefense. Cross-client statistical methods (Krum, Median, Trimmed Mean) degrade severely under non-IID settings: Krum drops to 3.04% TACC on CIFAR-10 under Fang attack (vs. 61.06% for FedREDefense), and Trimmed Mean falls to 23.64%. FLCert, which relies on Median as its base aggregation, inherits Median's weaknesses and achieves only 36.04% TACC on CIFAR-10 under Fang. FedREDefense's key advantage is its per-client reconstruction-error mechanism, which is independent of cross-client or global distribution assumptions, enabling 100% DACC even with 72% malicious clients and extreme non-IID (β=0.05).

## Relation to Prior Work

FedREDefense draws its core mechanism from the dataset distillation literature (Pi et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2022; Cazenavette et al., 2022), which shows that a small set of distilled data-label pairs can approximate the effect of full local training. However, it departs fundamentally from the dominant line of FL poisoning defenses (Krum, Median, Trimmed Mean, FLAME, FLTrust, FLDetector, FLCert), which all rely on cross-client statistical comparisons or global reference information to flag outliers. By reframing detection as a per-client reconstruction problem—asking whether an update is consistent with genuine training rather than whether it deviates from peers—the paper eliminates the dependency on distributional similarity across clients that limits all prior approaches under non-IID data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated gap: existing FL defenses degrade sharply under high non-IID degrees and large fractions of malicious clients, conditions that are common in real deployments. The 100% detection accuracy across seven attacks, three datasets, and varying attack fractions is a strong practical result. Key limitations include inability to detect data poisoning attacks (which produce updates from genuine training on poisoned data), reliance on relatively small CNNs, and non-trivial server-side computational overhead for the bi-level optimization. Open questions include whether the reconstruction-error gap holds for larger, deeper architectures, whether the approach extends to semi-federated or asynchronous settings, and how to handle adaptive attacks that more cleverly mimic genuine training dynamics.
