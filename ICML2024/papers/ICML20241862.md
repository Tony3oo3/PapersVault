## Problem

Transformer-based in-context RL models such as Algorithm Distillation (AD) and DPT are architecturally locked to a fixed action space: their final linear layer has a predetermined output dimension, and the classifier head associates each dimension with a specific action meaning. Changing the action set size, order, or semantics therefore requires retraining or yields degraded performance, preventing large-scale pretraining across environments with heterogeneous action spaces.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a multi-episode interaction history (observations, actions, rewards) where actions are encoded as randomly generated orthonormal vectors (regenerated each training step), plus a prepended prompt listing the embeddings of all currently available actions. The output is a single predicted action embedding vector of fixed dimensionality, which is converted to a discrete action either by nearest-neighbor lookup or by sampling from a categorical distribution whose logits are dot-product similarities between the prediction and the action set embeddings. Because the prediction is a continuous vector rather than a categorical logit vector, the model is agnostic to the number, identity, and ordering of actions at inference time.

## Method

Headless-AD removes AD's final linear classification head and instead directly predicts an action embedding, trained with an InfoNCE contrastive loss that pulls the prediction toward the ground-truth action embedding while pushing it away from all other actions in the set. Actions are encoded via a dynamic mapping that generates a fresh set of orthonormal random vectors on the unit sphere at every training step, eliminating any prior knowledge of action identity and forcing the model to infer action semantics purely from the interaction context. To compensate for the lost structural knowledge, the full set of action embeddings is prepended to the input sequence as a prompt, giving the model explicit awareness of which actions are available. At inference, a single set of orthonormal embeddings is generated once and used throughout evaluation, and the selected action is determined by either argmax similarity or probabilistic sampling over the similarity scores. The orthonormality constraint is critical: it ensures that aligning the prediction with one action embedding does not inadvertently assign probability mass to other actions, a failure mode that occurs with standard-normal embeddings.

## Results

On the Bernoulli Bandit, Headless-AD achieves 0.98±0.02 normalized performance (where 1.0 = Thompson Sampling) across 4–50 arms, matching or exceeding Thompson Sampling on larger arm counts (20–50) without any retraining. On the Darkroom gridworld, Headless-AD reaches 0.87±0.03 success rate while trained only on 50 actions, maintaining stable performance across all five action-space variants (including 125-action sets 2.5× larger than training). On the Contextual Bandit, Headless-AD matches LinUCB regret across 4–20 arms, whereas AD fails to converge on larger action spaces.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Algorithm Distillation (AD, Laskin et al. 2022), Headless-AD wins decisively on all variable-action-space settings: AD's performance drops sharply on permuted train actions and sliced test actions (Figure 3), and it cannot handle larger action sets without full retraining. Even when AD is retrained from scratch for each new action set size, Headless-AD outperforms it on the larger sets (50 arms in Bernoulli Bandit, 125 actions in Darkroom). Against LinUCB (Li et al. 2010) on the Contextual Bandit, Headless-AD matches its regret curve across all arm counts (4–20), while AD also matches LinUCB on smaller sets but fails to converge on larger ones. On the Bernoulli Bandit, Headless-AD approaches Thompson Sampling's regret under both shifted (even-arm) and uniform reward distributions, confirming its in-context learning capability for novel task configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Algorithm Distillation (AD) by Laskin et al. (2022), a transformer-based in-context RL model that predicts the next action via a fixed-size linear output head, making it architecturally locked to a specific action space. The key architectural departure—removing the output head and directly predicting action embeddings—draws from Headless-LLM (Godey et al., 2023) and Wolpertinger (Dulac-Arnold et al., 2015), while the use of random orthonormal action embeddings follows the data-augmentation strategy of Kirsch et al. (2023) and Lu et al. (2023), though those works target continuous action spaces. The paper positions itself as the first to systematically study variable discrete action spaces in the in-context RL setting, filling a gap where prior ICL-RL models (AD, DPT) assumed a fixed action set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Headless-AD provides a clean architectural recipe (headless output + random orthonormal embeddings + InfoNCE loss + action-set prompt) that could be grafted onto other transformer-based RL models like DPT, enabling foundation-model-style pretraining across environments with heterogeneous action spaces. However, the evaluation is confined to relatively simple environments (Bernoulli/contextual bandits, a gridworld), and the orthonormality constraint caps the number of representable actions at the embedding dimensionality, which becomes a practical bottleneck for very large action spaces. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more complex, high-dimensional environments and whether the contrastive-loss design choice generalizes beyond the tested settings.
