## Problem

Inverse reinforcement learning reduces imitation to repeatedly solving an RL problem, forcing the learner to explore the entire state space to find reward under adversarially chosen metrics—often wasting most interactions on policies dissimilar to the expert. Prior work (Swamy et al., 2023) curtailed this exploration by resetting the learner to expert states, but requires generative model access to the environment, which is unrealistic in many real-world settings. The paper asks: how can we focus IRL policy search on expert-similar policies without assuming the ability to reset to arbitrary states?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241725/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes expert demonstration trajectories (state-action sequences) as offline data and collects additional online data through environment interaction during training. No explicit reward signal is provided; instead, a discriminator network is trained adversarially to distinguish expert from learner trajectories, and the discriminator's output serves as the reward/cost function for policy optimization. The output is a single trained policy (or a mixture of policies) that is guaranteed to compete with the expert under the ground-truth reward function, evaluated on continuous control tasks with state-vector observations.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution is a reduction from IRL to expert-competitive RL via the Expert-Relative Regret Oracle (ERROr) property: the inner policy search need only guarantee that the learner's policy competes with the expert (not the optimal policy) under each adversarially chosen reward, which is sufficient to recover a policy that competes with the expert under the true reward. This is instantiated with two algorithms: HyPE (model-free) runs Soft Actor-Critic with expert demonstrations mixed into the replay buffer during the inner loop, and HyPER (model-based) fits a dynamics model on a mixture of learner and expert data, then performs policy optimization with resets to expert states *within the learned model* rather than the real environment. The key practical insight is that hybrid training on expert data focuses the learner's exploration on states the expert visits, dramatically reducing the interaction needed per inner-loop iteration while preserving the robustness-to-covariate-shift benefits of interactive IRL.

## Results

On the MuJoCo locomotion benchmark (Ant, Hopper, Humanoid, Walker2d), HyPE and HyPER converge to expert performance significantly faster than all baselines, with the performance gap widening as environments become more difficult. HyPER achieves this using only 150k environment steps compared to 1M for all model-free methods. On D4RL antmaze-large, all interactive baselines (including FILTER) achieve zero reward, while HyPER matches behavioral cloning performance without requiring resets in the real environment, using roughly 1/10th the online interaction that HyPE+Resets requires.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BC (behavioral cloning), HyPE and HyPER clearly outperform on MuJoCo locomotion tasks, converging to expert-level reward much faster; on antmaze-large, HyPER matches BC performance without needing real-environment resets, while HyPE alone falls short of BC unless augmented with resets. Against IQLearn, both HyPE and HyPER substantially outperform, with IQLearn failing to match even BC on some MuJoCo environments and exhibiting unstable training. Against MM (moment-matching IRL) and BC-Reg, HyPE and HyPER show consistent and growing advantages as task difficulty increases across the MuJoCo suite. Against FILTER (the prior state-of-the-art efficient IRL method requiring expert-state resets), HyPE and HyPER are empirically more sample efficient on MuJoCo, which the authors attribute to using both expert states and actions rather than states alone; on antmaze-large, FILTER achieves zero reward while HyPER matches BC, demonstrating a decisive advantage in hard-exploration settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: hybrid RL (which mixes offline expert data with online learner data to reduce exploration, as in HyQ and LAMPS) and efficient inverse RL (which reduces the interaction cost of the IRL inner loop, as in FILTER's use of expert-state resets). The key conceptual departure is the Expert-Relative Regret Oracle (ERROr) reduction, which shows that the IRL inner loop only needs to produce policies that compete with the expert rather than the globally optimal policy. This generalizes the reset-based argument of Swamy et al. (2023) to hybrid-training algorithms that do not require generative model access to the environment, yielding both a model-free algorithm (HyPE) and a model-based one (HyPER).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ERROr reduction provides a clean theoretical justification for why hybrid training is a valid policy-search subroutine in IRL, removing the need for the often-unavailable ability to reset to arbitrary states. Empirically, HyPER achieves the highest IRL performance on antmaze-large (where all other interactive baselines score zero) without requiring real-environment resets, suggesting practical applicability to robotics where resets are infeasible. Open questions include how sensitive HyPER is to model accuracy in the low-data regime (where it degrades notably), whether the complementary combination of HyPE/HyPER with FILTER can be formalized, and whether the ERROr framework extends to settings with stochastic or partially observable dynamics.
