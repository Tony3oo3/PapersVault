## Problem

Existing knowledge graph embedding models that use orthogonal relation transformations are each confined to a single geometry (Euclidean or hyperbolic) and a fixed low dimension (2D or 3D), making them unable to simultaneously capture cyclical and hierarchical topologies. No prior approach breaks both the dimensional rigidity and the geometric homogeneity constraint for relational orthogonalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241124/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

GoldE consumes knowledge graph triples (h, r, t) and learns entity embeddings in a product manifold D^k composed of m elliptic and m hyperbolic component spaces, each of dimension k_i (summing to total dimension k). For each triple, the head entity embedding is transformed by a relation-specific k-dimensional orthogonal matrix (elliptic Householder product or hyperbolic boost + Euclidean rotation) and scored against the tail embedding via a component-wise distance (Mahalanobis for elliptic, geodesic for hyperbolic). The output is a plausibility score per triple used for link prediction, with all transformations differentiable and bijective so standard Euclidean optimization applies.

## Method

The core contribution is a universal orthogonal parameterization built on a generalized Householder reflection H(u, w) = I − 2·uu^T·diag(w)/(u^T·diag(w)·u) defined in a quadratic inner product space with weighting vector w. Setting w to 1, p (positive), or q = (−1,1,…,1) recovers Euclidean, elliptic, and hyperbolic reflections respectively, and the product of k such reflections (the Orth mapping) provably covers the entire generalized orthogonal group O_w(k) via the Cartan-Dieudonné theorem. For the hyperbolic case, a polar decomposition restricts the transformation to the positive subgroup O_q^+(k) (first element ≥ 1) so that transformed embeddings remain on the upper sheet of the hyperboloid, expressed as a block product of a (k−1)-dimensional Euclidean Householder product and a hyperbolic boost matrix. GoldE integrates elliptic and hyperbolic parameterizations across a product manifold to handle topological heterogeneity, and trains with a self-adversarial negative sampling margin loss. Efficient computation replaces O(k³) matrix-vector multiplications with O(k²) vector-vector operations via the Householder update formula and an equivalent boost parameterization.

## Results

GoldE achieves state-of-the-art link prediction performance across all three benchmarks. On WN18RR, it reaches MRR 0.525 and H@10 0.615; on FB15k-237, MRR 0.370 and H@10 0.558; and on YAGO3-10, MRR 0.588 and H@10 0.723. Compared to the closest Euclidean baseline HousE, GoldE gains an average of 2.5% absolute MRR, and even surpasses the projection-enhanced HousE-pro by 1.3% absolute MRR. Under the restricted 32-dimensional setting, GoldE still leads all baselines (e.g., MRR 0.482 on WN18RR vs. 0.472 for RotH).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GoldE outperforms all 13 baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the most directly comparable Euclidean Householder model HousE, GoldE improves MRR by +2.9 pts (WN18RR: .525 vs .496), +2.2 pts (FB15k-237: .370 vs .348), and +2.3 pts (YAGO3-10: .588 vs .565). It also surpasses the stronger HousE-pro variant by +1.4, +0.9, and +1.7 MRR points respectively. Among hyperbolic baselines, GoldE leads RotH by +2.9, +2.6, and +1.8 MRR points and AttH by +3.9, +2.2, and +2.0 MRR points across the three datasets. The non-orthogonal baselines (TransE, DistMult, ComplEx, ConvE, MuRP) trail GoldE by 4–30 MRR points depending on the dataset, confirming the advantage of the orthogonal parameterization. Under the 32-dim restriction (Table 5), GoldE still leads the best baseline AttH by +1.6 MRR on WN18RR and +2.3 on YAGO3-10.

## Relation to Prior Work

GoldE unifies two previously separate research threads in knowledge graph embedding: the 'Dimension Branch' (RotatE → QuatE → HousE) that extends Euclidean orthogonal transformations to higher dimensions, and the 'Geometry Branch' (RefH, RotH, AttH) that leverages hyperbolic isometries for hierarchical structures. The core mathematical contribution is a generalized Householder reflection in a quadratic inner product space, which subsumes Euclidean, elliptic, and hyperbolic orthogonal transformations as special cases, with the Cartan-Dieudonné theorem guaranteeing full coverage of each orthogonal group. By composing these via a product manifold, GoldE is the first framework to simultaneously break the dimensional rigidity and geometric homogeneity that constrained all prior orthogonal KGE models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The universal orthogonal parameterization provides a clean theoretical template that could extend beyond KGE to other settings requiring structured orthogonal transformations (RNNs, GNNs, Transformers), as the authors note. However, the product manifold configuration is simplified (uniform dimensions per geometry type, no learned curvature signature), and the practical gains over strong baselines like HousE-pro are moderate (~1.3% MRR). Open questions include whether the geometric unification scales to hyper-relational or temporal KGs, and whether a learned signature estimator for the product space could further improve the geometry-structure matching.
