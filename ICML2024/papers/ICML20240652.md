## Problem

Standard MCTS and its AlphaZero variants are frequentist methods that ignore uncertainty in neural network value estimates, leading to inefficient exploration when search budgets are limited and predictions are inaccurate. The paper asks whether Bayesian uncertainty quantification over Q-value posteriors can be exploited to guide tree search more effectively, and provides the first finite-time Bayesian regret bound for a Thompson sampling tree search algorithm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240652/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The planner receives an RGB image of the current environment state (from ProcGen Maze or Leaper) and a deterministic simulator model for tree expansion. A neural network (Impala architecture) outputs a Gaussian posterior N(μ_θ(s,a), σ_θ²(s,a)) for each state-action pair, where σ_θ is obtained either via an MLE-trained log-variance head or an ensemble of K=5 networks. The search runs for T iterations (budget) over a tree of depth H, and the output is a committed action at the root selected via one of several strategies (MCTS-style max-backup, quantile-based, or stochastic SoftMax), along with the full posterior Q-value distributions at the root.

## Method

The paper formulates tree search as a sequential discovery problem where each edge expansion reveals a leaf reward, and proposes two Bayesian exploration algorithms. Thompson Sampling Tree Search (TSTS) samples a leaf from the posterior probability of being on the optimal branch using forward sampling (sequentially sampling Q values and greedily selecting actions) combined with a max-backup that propagates the distribution of the maximum of independent posteriors up the tree. Bayes-UCB Tree Search (BTS) replaces posterior sampling with a quantile-based selection rule α(s) = 1 − (1−α₀)·e^{−(N(s)−1)/β}, which the authors find empirically superior. The key theoretical result is a Bayesian regret bound of O(HR_max·√(|Z|·H(z*)·T)), showing that informative priors (low entropy) reduce regret. The neural network is trained via self-play in an AlphaZero-style loop, with Q-value targets from the search and uncertainty estimated via MLE or ensembles.

## Results

On the ProcGen Maze test domains, all Bayesian planners (TSTS, BTS, B-UCT2, B-UCB) with ground-truth uncertainty estimates significantly outperform N-MCTS across all search budgets, while on training domains all methods are comparable. On the ProcGen Leaper test set, BTS with ground-truth uncertainty achieves a success rate above 85%, and even without GT estimates BTS significantly outperforms N-MCTS. An ablation shows that BTS retains its advantage over N-MCTS even when up to 20% noise is added to the ground-truth uncertainty estimates. Among the Bayesian variants, the ordering on test domains is BTS > B-UCT2 > B-UCB > TSTS, indicating that quantile-based exploration bonuses outperform pure posterior sampling in the tree search setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against N-MCTS (Anthony et al., 2017), the Bayesian methods with ground-truth uncertainty achieve significantly higher success rates on both Maze and Leaper test domains, while matching N-MCTS on training domains where the network is more accurate. SH-N-MCTS (Danihelka et al., 2021), which replaces root exploration with sequential halving, performs significantly worse than all other methods in the Maze domain. DNG-MCTS (Bai et al., 2013) is comparable to standard N-MCTS overall but degrades substantially at small search budgets, and is significantly outperformed by TSTS with ground-truth uncertainty. W-MCTS (Dam et al., 2023), which propagates uncertainty via Wasserstein barycenters, is comparable to TSTS without GT uncertainty but is significantly outperformed by TSTS with GT uncertainty across all search budgets, which the authors attribute to their Max-Backup being more naturally suited to the deterministic tree structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revives and formalizes Bayesian approaches to MCTS that were explored in the 1990s meta-reasoning literature (Russell & Wefald, 1991b; Dearden et al., 1998) and by Tesauro et al. (2010), connecting them to the modern AlphaZero-style deep learning planning paradigm. It provides the first finite-time Bayesian regret bound for Thompson sampling tree search, adapting the information-theoretic analysis of Russo & Van Roy (2016) from bandits to trees, and formally establishes that Tesauro et al.'s max-backup is a concrete instance of Thompson sampling under independent posteriors. The Bayes-UCB tree search (BTS) variant extends Kaufmann et al. (2012) from MABs to tree search with a novel quantile schedule, and the paper positions itself against recent Bayesian MCTS work by Bai et al. (2013) and Dam et al. (2023) by applying Thompson sampling to branches rather than individual nodes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central finding is a mixed result: with ground-truth uncertainty, Bayesian planners dramatically outperform MCTS on zero-shot generalization tasks, but standard learned uncertainty estimates (MLE and ensembles) are not accurate enough to translate into planning gains. This identifies uncertainty estimation accuracy as the critical bottleneck for practical Bayesian planning, pointing toward conformal prediction and epistemic neural networks as promising directions. The theoretical contribution (first finite-time regret bound for Bayesian tree search) provides a principled foundation, while the empirical finding that Bayes-UCB-style exploration bonuses outperform Thompson sampling in tree search (contrary to MAB intuition) offers a practical algorithmic insight. Open questions include whether richer posteriors (e.g., transformer-based) that capture dependencies across the search history can close the gap.
