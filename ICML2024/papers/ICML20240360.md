## Problem

Second-order optimisers like K-FAC depend heavily on stabilising heuristics (Levenberg-Marquardt damping, adaptive learning rate selection) for viability, yet it remains unclear whether these heuristics or the underlying curvature model are primarily responsible for their documented benefits. The paper asks whether transplanting K-FAC's adaptive heuristics onto a first-order method like Adam could capture similar stability and performance gains without the full computational cost of second-order curvature.

## Input / Output

At each iteration the method consumes the current gradient, Adam's running momentum and variance buffers, the scalar loss values at consecutive parameter points (to compute the reduction ratio ρ), and the Fisher-curvature-vector product (C+λI)d_t obtained via one additional backward pass using Jacobian-vector products. It outputs updated model parameters, a per-iteration learning rate α selected by minimising a local quadratic model, and an adaptively rescaled damping parameter λ. The Fisher matrix is never explicitly stored; only its action on the update direction vector is computed, keeping memory overhead to roughly four times SGD.

## Method

AdamQLR retains Adam's update direction d_t = m̂_t / (√v̂_t + ε) but replaces its fixed learning rate with a quadratic-model selection: it constructs a damped second-order model M(θ) = f(θ_{t-1}) + (θ−θ_{t-1})ᵀg_t + ½(θ−θ_{t-1})ᵀ(C+λI)(θ−θ_{t-1}) using the true Fisher matrix C, then sets α = g_tᵀd_t / (d_tᵀ(C+λI)d_t) to minimise M along the Adam direction. A Levenberg-Marquardt damping parameter λ is adapted each step by comparing the model-predicted loss change to the actual observed change via a reduction ratio ρ, increasing λ when the model over-predicts improvement and decreasing it when the model is too conservative. The key conceptual move is interpreting Adam's 1/√v̂_t rescaling as a diagonal Fisher-curvature transformation, which motivates layering K-FAC's trust-region damping and step-size logic on top of Adam's direction. The result is an optimiser whose only remaining hyperparameters are Adam's β₁, β₂, ε and an initial damping λ₀, with the learning rate fully encapsulated by the quadratic model.

## Results

Across six benchmark tasks, AdamQLR's performance relative to Adam and K-FAC is notably variable. On SVHN (ResNet-18), AdamQLR achieves 89.6% test accuracy, outperforming Adam (88.4%) and K-FAC (86.3%); on UCI Protein, AdamQLR's test loss of 0.237 beats Adam (0.248) but trails K-FAC (0.230). On CIFAR-10, Adam (82.4%) and Adam Untuned (84.2%) both outperform AdamQLR (79.3%) and K-FAC (79.6%), while on Fashion-MNIST all three methods are within ~0.3% test accuracy. The untuned AdamQLR configuration (batch size 3200, λ₀=0.001) is broadly competitive with tuned baselines, supporting the paper's claim of hyperparameter robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against **Adam** (Kingma & Ba, 2015), AdamQLR wins on SVHN (89.6% vs 88.4% test accuracy) and UCI Protein (0.237 vs 0.248 test loss), is roughly tied on Fashion-MNIST (87.0% vs 87.3%), and loses on CIFAR-10 (79.3% vs 82.4%). Against **K-FAC** (Martens & Grosse, 2015), AdamQLR wins on SVHN (89.6% vs 86.3%) and Fashion-MNIST (87.0% vs 86.9%, where K-FAC overfits severely with a 0.367 generalisation gap vs AdamQLR's 0.105), is roughly tied on CIFAR-10 (79.3% vs 79.6%), and loses on UCI Protein (0.237 vs 0.230). The paper's central empirical finding is that K-FAC's adaptive heuristics (damping + quadratic-model learning rate) provide variable standalone benefit: they improve Adam on some tasks (SVHN, UCI Protein) but offer no consistent advantage over tuned Adam or full K-FAC, and the full K-FAC method itself suffers from overfitting and instability that AdamQLR partially avoids.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines of work: adaptive first-order optimizers (Adam, RMSprop, Adagrad) and second-order curvature-based methods (K-FAC, quasi-Newton, natural gradient). Its conceptual contribution is reframing Adam's second-moment buffer v̂_t as a diagonal Fisher approximation, then transplanting K-FAC's Levenberg-Marquardt damping and quadratic-model learning rate selection onto Adam's update directions to produce AdamQLR. The paper explicitly positions itself as an empirical dissection of whether K-FAC's stabilizing heuristics (as opposed to its Kronecker-factored curvature model) are the primary drivers of second-order methods' documented benefits, a question that prior work has not isolated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that K-FAC's heuristics show variable standalone effectiveness—sometimes outperforming full K-FAC, sometimes diverging—raises a fundamental open question about what actually makes second-order methods work in practice and whether the curvature model and its heuristics are separable. Practically, the untuned AdamQLR configuration (fixed batch size 3200, initial damping 0.001, ω_dec=0.5, ω_inc=2.0) achieving comparable performance to tuned Adam and SGD baselines across multiple tasks suggests a path toward reducing hyperparameter sensitivity, though the method's dependence on large batch sizes and additional forward/backward passes limits its immediate adoption. Open questions include why full K-FAC sometimes underperforms its own heuristics applied to Adam, whether transformer-specific defaults would close the Penn Treebank gap, and whether the CIFAR-10 instability at scale reflects a fundamental limitation of the quadratic-model approach.
