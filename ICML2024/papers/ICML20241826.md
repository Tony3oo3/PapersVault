## Problem

Existing hypergradient methods for lower-level constrained bilevel optimization (LCBO) rely on restrictive differentiability and invertibility assumptions on the optimality conditions, lack non-asymptotic convergence rate analysis, and typically require double-loop or two-timescale updates. This paper addresses the open challenge of designing a single-loop single-timescale method with convergence guarantees for LCBO without these restrictive assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method takes an upper-level objective f(x,y), a lower-level objective g(x,y) that is strongly convex in y, and a convex constraint set Y ⊆ ℝ^{d₂}, along with initial parameters x₁ and y₁. It outputs a (δ,ε)-stationary point x_r (uniformly sampled from the iterate sequence), where δ controls the neighborhood radius for the δ-approximation generalized Jacobian of the projection operator and ε is the stationarity tolerance. The (δ,ε) formulation is necessary because exact ε-stationary points cannot be found in finite time for nonsmooth nonconvex problems.

## Method

The key insight is to derive the hypergradient via Clarke's nonsmooth implicit function theorem and generalized Jacobian, avoiding the differentiability assumptions on y*(x) and the projection operator required by prior work. The non-smoothness of the projection is handled by randomized smoothing (averaging over a δ-ball), which yields a differentiable surrogate whose gradient belongs to the δ-approximation generalized Jacobian. The matrix inverse in the hypergradient is approximated via a Neumann series truncated at Q terms, with an unbiased stochastic estimator for the smoothed projection Jacobian computed in parallel over d₂ dimensions. The resulting single-loop single-timescale algorithm (DMLCBO) updates x and y simultaneously using double-momentum gradient estimators with Adam-style adaptive step sizes, and is proven to reach a (δ,ε)-stationary point in Õ(d₂²ε⁻⁴) iterations.

## Results

On the meta-learning task, DMLCBO achieves 99.97% test accuracy on Omniglot 5-way 5-shot and 85.25% on FC100 5-way 5-shot, outperforming all baselines by 4–19 percentage points. On the data hyper-cleaning task, DMLCBO reaches 94.24% on MNIST 6vs9 and 56.33% on Madelon, consistently leading or matching the best baseline. The method achieves a (δ,ε)-stationary point with Õ(d₂²ε⁻⁴) iterations, matching the best-known rate for unconstrained bilevel optimization while handling lower-level convex constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMLCBO outperforms all three baselines on 7 out of 8 evaluated dataset-problem combinations. The largest margins appear on FC100, where DMLCBO achieves 85.25% (5-way) and 86.93% (10-way), surpassing V-PBGD by 7.1 and 19.8 points respectively, and Approx by 11.1 and 17.8 points. On Omniglot, DMLCBO leads by 4.3 points over V-PBGD (99.97% vs 95.67%) in the 5-way setting, while RMD-PCD is competitive (99.69%). The sole exception is FashionMNIST 1vs7 in data hyper-cleaning, where RMD-PCD narrowly edges out DMLCBO (88.93% vs 88.85%), though both substantially outperform V-PBGD (85.95%) and Approx (86.03%). The paper attributes DMLCBO's advantage to its single-loop design not depending on accurate lower-level solutions, whereas RMD-PCD, Approx, and V-PBGD degrade when the inner problem is only approximately solved.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bilevel optimization literature from unconstrained lower-level problems to the constrained setting by replacing the restrictive differentiability and invertibility assumptions on projection operators (used in Blondel et al., 2022; Bertrand et al., 2022) with the nonsmooth implicit function theorem from Clarke (1990) and Bolte et al. (2021). Algorithmically, it builds on the double-momentum and adaptive step-size framework of Khanduri et al. (2021) and Huang & Huang (2021), but adapts it to the constrained case via randomized smoothing (Lin et al., 2022) and Neumann series approximation of the matrix inverse. The key departure is that prior LCBO methods (Table 1) were all double-loop or lacked convergence rate guarantees, whereas this work provides the first single-loop single-timescale algorithm with a non-asymptotic convergence bound for the constrained setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work removes a significant practical barrier: prior hypergradient methods for constrained bilevel problems required the projection operator to be differentiable at the solution, which fails for common constraint sets like ℓ₁ balls or norm balls. The (δ,ε)-stationary point framework provides a theoretically sound optimality criterion given the impossibility of finding exact ε-stationary points in finite time for nonsmooth nonconvex problems. Limitations include the strong convexity requirement on the lower-level objective, the d₂² dependence in the iteration complexity, and validation only on relatively small-scale problems (binary classification, few-shot learning with small networks). Open questions include whether the framework extends to non-convex lower-level problems and whether the dimension dependence can be improved.
