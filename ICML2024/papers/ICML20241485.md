## Problem

Existing methods for identifying interpretable Building Blocks (BBs) in multi-state time-series data either assume identical BB structure across states, force orthogonality, or cannot handle real-world irregularities such as variable trial durations, missing samples, and differing trial counts per state. No prior approach simultaneously recovers sparse, non-orthogonal BBs with per-trial temporal activity while accommodating subtle cross-state structural adaptations and both within-state and between-state variability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241485/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a collection of D multi-trial sets {Y_m^d}, where each state d contains M_d trials of potentially different durations T_m^d and sampling rates, with N channels per trial. Optionally, numerical or categorical state labels are provided to construct a supervised state-similarity graph. The output consists of sparse BB matrices A^d ∈ R^{N×p} per state (where each channel can sparsely belong to multiple BBs with varying membership weights) and per-trial temporal activity traces Φ_m^d ∈ R^{T_m^d × p}, with the vector ν enabling identification of both state-invariant background components and state-specific emerging components.

## Method

SiBBlInGS performs alternating minimization between BB updates and temporal trace updates, driven by two similarity graphs: a per-state channel graph H^d (built from concatenated within-state observations via a Gaussian kernel, then kNN-thresholded and row-normalized) and a state-similarity graph P (either data-driven via Procrustes/DTW distances or supervised via label distances). The BB update for each state d solves a re-weighted ℓ1 graph filtering problem (Eq. 1) where the re-weighting (Eq. 2) incorporates H^d to promote grouping of temporally similar channels into the same BB, while a cross-state regularization term weighted by P and per-BB weights ν enforces controlled structural similarity between states. The temporal trace update (Eq. 3) is a per-trial regularized non-negative least-squares with terms for data fidelity, activity bounding, iteration continuity, temporal diffusion, and inter-BB decorrelation. This graph-driven re-weighting mechanism is the key distinction from standard dictionary learning or tensor factorization, as it uses data-driven spatial and state relationships to shape the sparsity pattern rather than applying uniform ℓ1 penalties.

## Results

On synthetic data (D=3 states, p=10 BBs, N=100 channels), SiBBlInGS recovers ground-truth temporal traces with correlation close to 1 and achieves high Jaccard index values for BB composition, outperforming all 8 baselines (Fig. 2E, 6F). On the monkey somatosensory cortex dataset (8 hand-direction states, 18 trials each), all 4 identified BBs exhibit within/between-state correlation ratios >1, and multi-class logistic regression on the temporal traces alone accurately predicts the hand direction (Fig. 4C, E). SiBBlInGS remains robust under noise up to σ_signal/σ_noise > 3 and degrades gracefully with increasing missing samples (Fig. 2F–G). On the epilepsy EEG data, SiBBlInGS uniquely identifies a localized BB emerging in the 8 seconds preceding seizures around electrode F8, a pattern not recovered by any baseline method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SiBBlInGS outperforms all 8 baselines (PARAFAC, Tucker, PCA global/local, Sparse PCA global/local, demixed PCA, mTDR, NONFAT/NNDTN) on the synthetic recovery task, achieving higher trace correlation and Jaccard index for BB composition (Fig. 2E, 6F, 7–8). On the Google Trends experiment, SiBBlInGS with p=5 produces clearly theme-specific BBs (e.g., 'College', 'Passover', 'COVID'), whereas PARAFAC and Tucker yield empty or heavily blended components, and PCA/SPCA produce less interpretable mixtures (Fig. 10). On the neural reaching task, SiBBlInGS is the only method that simultaneously captures state-specific temporal modulation, subtle cross-state structural adaptation in BB composition, and neurons belonging to multiple clusters; dPCA and mTDR cannot handle the varying trial structure, and SRM enforces orthogonality that is biologically implausible. On the epilepsy EEG data, SiBBlInGS uniquely discovers a pre-seizure BB localized around F8 that no baseline method identifies, attributed to the ν parameter enabling state-specific emerging components. The key structural advantage over tensor methods (PARAFAC, Tucker, HOSVD) is that SiBBlInGS does not require fixed trial duration or identical sampling rates, and over dictionary-learning methods (RWL1-GF/Graft) it extends to multi-state data with cross-state regularization via the state-similarity graph P.

## Relation to Prior Work

SiBBlInGS extends the re-weighted ℓ1 graph filtering (RWL1-GF/Graft) framework from Charles et al. (2022), which was limited to single-trial calcium imaging data with a single pixel-space graph, into a multi-state, multi-trial setting with two complementary graphs: a per-state channel-similarity graph and a cross-state similarity graph. The re-weighted ℓ1 sparsity mechanism from Candes et al. (2008) and the broader dictionary learning paradigm from Olshausen & Field (1996) provide the foundational sparse coding machinery. The paper deliberately departs from tensor factorization methods (PARAFAC, Tucker, NONFAT), which assume fixed BB structure across states or fixed temporal traces, and from deep learning approaches (VAEs, transformers, CEBRA), which lack sparse interpretability and cannot handle variable trial durations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SiBBlInGS fills a practical gap for multi-state time series analysis by simultaneously accommodating variable trial durations, missing samples, differing sampling rates, and per-state trial counts—challenges that tensor methods, dPCA, and TDR/mTDR cannot handle. The ν parameter enabling discovery of both state-invariant background and state-specific emerging components (demonstrated by the pre-seizure BB in epilepsy data) is a useful mechanism for clinical and scientific discovery. Key limitations include the linearity assumption in BB-to-observation mapping, Gaussian noise assumption, inability to handle missing channels or cross-trial channel identity changes, and the absence of directional connectivity modeling—all flagged as future work. The framework's applicability spans neuroscience, social science, and environmental domains, but validation remains limited to relatively small-scale datasets.
