## Problem

Existing deep Safe RL algorithms (CPO, CUP, PDO, APPO) uniformly apply infinite-horizon Advantage-based Estimation (ABE) to all constraints, yet practical Safe RL benchmarks use finite-horizon non-discounted cumulative cost constraints (Σc_t ≤ b over T steps). This fundamental mismatch causes ABE to produce catastrophic estimation errors with relative magnitudes exceeding 1.0, leading to safety-violation policy updates. No prior deep Safe RL algorithm can accurately estimate how finite-horizon constraints change under a policy update.

## Input / Output

The method consumes environment state vectors and differentiable transition dynamics (e.g., from the Brax physics engine) along with reward/cost functions and scalar constraint thresholds over a finite horizon T, and produces updated policy network parameters and corresponding actions. A critical requirement is access to first-order gradients of both the objective and constraint functions via backpropagation along trajectories, which necessitates either a differentiable simulator or a trained world model. The constraint is specifically a non-discounted cumulative sum compared against a scalar threshold, matching the format used in Safety-Gym, Bullet-Safety-Gym, and Safety-Gymnasium benchmarks.

## Method

The core contribution is Gradient-based Estimation (GBE), which replaces ABE's infinite-horizon advantage function with a first-order Taylor expansion J_f(θ₀+δ) ≈ J_f(θ₀) + δᵀ∇_θ J_f(θ₀), yielding an estimation error bounded by ½ε‖δ‖₂². Building on GBE, CGPO formulates a constrained surrogate sub-problem within a trust region at each iteration: maximize the linearized objective subject to the linearized constraint and a quadratic trust region bound. The sub-problem is solved analytically via KKT conditions across three geometric scenarios (entirely infeasible, entirely feasible, or partially feasible trust region), with the partially feasible case yielding a closed-form update θ_{k+1} = θ_k + (g_k − ν*_k q_k)/λ*_k. An adaptive trust region radius mechanism adjusts step size based on a reduction ratio (objective consistency) and toleration ratio (constraint error tolerance), keeping estimation errors within acceptable bounds. Gradients are computed via SHAC-style short-horizon backpropagation through the differentiable dynamics, segmenting trajectories into sub-windows of length h to mitigate gradient vanishing/exploding while preserving finite-horizon constraint structure.

## Results

On four differentiable Safe RL tasks (CartPole, Reacher, HalfCheetah, Ant), CGPO achieves the lowest convergence steps (1.15M–8.52M) and the lowest constraint violation ratio (3.96%–14.29%) across all tasks, outperforming all baselines on both metrics. CGPO requires less than 28.8%–91.7% of the environmental steps at convergence compared to baselines, demonstrating substantially higher sample efficiency. In the ablation on estimation accuracy (Figure 5), GBE maintains relative errors below 0.15 across all training stages, while ABE produces relative errors exceeding 1.0, confirming that the infinite-horizon advantage-based estimation is fundamentally inapplicable to finite-horizon non-discounted constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGPO dominates all four traditional Safe RL baselines on both convergence speed and constraint violation ratio across every task. Against PDO (Lagrangian), CGPO reduces convergence steps by 64.7%–65.5% and cuts violation ratios from 12.01%–74.58% down to 3.96%–14.29%. Against APPO (augmented Lagrangian), CGPO is 70.4%–76.2% faster to converge and reduces violations by 40%–75%. Against CPO (primal trust-region), CGPO is 73.7%–76.2% faster and achieves lower violations on all tasks where CPO succeeds (CPO fails entirely on HalfCheetah). Against CUP (projection-based), CGPO is 74.1%–85.2% faster and reduces violations by 62%–84%. The differentiable baselines BPTT-Lag and SHAC-Lag, while sample-efficient, suffer from inherent Lagrangian oscillation near constraint boundaries, yielding violation ratios of 24%–68% versus CGPO's 4%–14%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the constrained surrogate problem structure from CPO (Achiam et al., 2017) and the trust region framework from TRPO (Schulman et al., 2015), but replaces the Advantage-based Estimation (ABE) used by all existing deep Safe RL algorithms with a Gradient-based Estimation (GBE) derived from first-order Taylor expansion along differentiable trajectories. The gradient computation leverages the short-horizon sub-window approach from SHAC (Jie Xu et al., 2022) within differentiable simulators like Brax. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that ABE's infinite-horizon discounted formulation is fundamentally incompatible with the finite-horizon non-discounted constraints that dominate Safe RL benchmarks, and that analytical gradients from differentiable dynamics provide a natural and theoretically bounded alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that ABE produces relative errors exceeding 1.0 for finite-horizon constraints — the standard formulation in Safety-Gym, BulletSafety-Gym, Safety-Gymnasium, and OmniSafe — reveals a fundamental mismatch that has affected all prior deep Safe RL algorithms, potentially explaining their poor constraint satisfaction in practice. CGPO's theoretical guarantees (bounded worst-case performance degradation and constraint violation) and adaptive trust region radius provide practical stability. However, the method's reliance on environmental differentiability (or world models as a workaround) limits its immediate applicability to non-differentiable real-world systems, and the world model augmentation shown in the paper uses a simple two-layer MLP yielding low-precision gradients, leaving the gap between differentiable and non-differentiable settings as an open challenge.
