## Problem

While Lipschitz constants of neural networks are well-studied for adversarial robustness, provable upper bounds on the second derivative (curvature) of general sequential networks remained largely unexplored. The only prior method (Singla & Feizi, 2020) was restricted to scalar-valued, non-residual architectures and required iterative numerical optimization for certificates. This paper addresses the gap by developing a general, scalable framework for curvature bounds on vector-valued sequential networks in any ℓ_p norm, yielding closed-form robustness and attack certificates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240499/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a trained neural network (layer weights and architecture) and a specific input image, the method computes a layer-by-layer upper bound on the Lipschitz constant of the network's Jacobian. From this curvature bound and the local gradient at the input, it produces closed-form certified robustness radii (lower bounds on distance to the nearest decision boundary) and attack certificate radii (upper bounds on the minimum perturbation needed to provably misclassify). The curvature bound is differentiable with respect to network parameters, enabling its direct use as a training regularizer without additional forward passes.

## Method

The core algorithm recursively propagates curvature bounds through a sequential network using a compositional theorem (Theorem 3.1) that relates the Jacobian Lipschitz constant of a composition to the individual Lipschitz and curvature constants of its components. For each residual block, the Jacobian's Lipschitz constant is bounded either directly via matrix norms or through a vectorized Jacobian representation (Lemma 3.6) that enables tighter estimates via LipSDP; subnetwork Lipschitz constants are computed using LipLT (loop transformation) to avoid exponential growth from naive product bounds. The paper introduces the 'anchored Lipschitz constant,' which fixes one point in the Lipschitz definition to the point of interest, significantly reducing conservatism in local certificates. During training, the curvature bound serves as a differentiable regularizer, and the resulting certified radius has a closed-form quadratic solution that provably improves upon Lipschitz-only certificates when curvature is sufficiently small (Proposition 2.2).

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the proposed CCRC method achieves 52.09% certified accuracy at ε=36/255 on the 6F architecture (4M params), outperforming the CRM baseline (42.73%) by over 9 percentage points. On the 6C2F architecture (0.7M params), CCRC reaches 49.53% certified accuracy at ε=36/255 versus 36.25% for CRM. For the 1-Lipschitz Lip-3C1F model, adding CCRC regularization to SLL improves certified accuracy from 45.0% to 46.6% at ε=36/255 and from 26.5% to 31.6% at ε=108/255, while also achieving higher clean accuracy (62.1% vs 60.63% for 6F).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CRM (Fazlyab et al., 2023), CCRC consistently wins on certified accuracy across all perturbation budgets: on 6C2F, CCRC achieves 49.53% vs 36.25% at ε=36/255 (a 13.3-point gain), and on 6F, 52.09% vs 42.73% (a 9.4-point gain), while also matching or exceeding clean accuracy. Against SLL (Araujo et al., 2022), adding CCRC regularization to the 1-Lipschitz Lip-3C1F architecture improves certified accuracy at every ε tested (46.6% vs 45.0% at ε=36/255; 31.6% vs 26.5% at ε=108/255), though at a small cost in clean accuracy (53.2% vs 57.2%). Against CRC (Singla & Feizi, 2020), the compositional curvature estimation (CCRC) produces tighter certified radii per sample on a 6-layer MNIST network, as shown in Figure 3, because it handles vector-valued outputs and general sequential architectures rather than being restricted to scalar-valued non-residual networks. Overall, the paper's method dominates all three baselines on certified robustness metrics while maintaining competitive clean accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the curvature-based robustness certification framework of Singla & Feizi (2020), which was limited to scalar-valued networks with affine-then-activation architectures, to general vector-valued sequential networks (including residual and convolutional blocks) in any ℓp norm. It builds directly on the loop transformation (LipLT) and SDP-based (LipSDP) Lipschitz estimation techniques of Fazlyab et al. (2019, 2023), repurposing them to bound the Jacobian's Lipschitz constant rather than the network's own Lipschitz constant. The introduction of the 'anchored Lipschitz constant'—fixing one point in the Lipschitz definition to the point of interest—is a new conceptual tool that reduces conservatism in both first- and second-order bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The compositional curvature bound provides a scalable, layer-by-layer algorithm that is differentiable and can serve as a training regularizer, making curvature control practical for deep networks rather than a post-hoc analysis tool. The attack certificates (Proposition 2.4) are a novel contribution that narrows the certification gap, giving practitioners tighter bounds on actual robust accuracy without running expensive attacks. Key limitations include the conservatism of the bound for deep networks (the paper acknowledges future work to tighten the gap), evaluation only on small-scale datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10), and the requirement of slope-restricted monotone activations. Open questions include whether the approach scales to ImageNet-sized models and whether the curvature regularizer can be combined with other robustness techniques (e.g., randomized smoothing) for complementary guarantees.
