## Problem

When two learning agents (e.g., a recommender system and a user) interact sequentially with misaligned utilities in a decentralized setting, it is unclear what performance guarantees are achievable for both agents simultaneously. This paper shows that the natural Stackelberg equilibrium benchmark is fundamentally unachievable—any pair of algorithms incurs linear regret for at least one player—motivating the need for relaxed benchmarks and new algorithmic designs that account for the other agent's learning errors.

## Input / Output

Each player receives their own action set (A for the leader, B for the follower), a time horizon T, and a history of previously observed actions and stochastic Gaussian rewards (mean in [0,1], unit variance). Critically, the leader only observes their own reward and (in the StrongDSG variant) not even the follower's chosen action, while the follower observes the leader's action and their own reward. The output is a distribution over actions at each time step, with the guarantee expressed as worst-case regret bounds over all utility functions v₁, v₂ on the given action sets.

## Method

The paper introduces γ-tolerant benchmarks that take an infimum over ε ≤ γ of an ε-relaxed Stackelberg utility (max over one player's actions, min over the other player's ε-best-response set) plus an ε-regularizer, making the benchmark tolerant to the other agent's suboptimality during learning. For the leader, the key algorithm ExploreThenUCB first explores each arm E times (discarding all history) to let the follower partially converge, then runs UCB with confidence bounds calibrated to the follower's high-probability instantaneous regret. This yields tight Õ(T^{2/3}) regret for both players, matching a matching lower bound. Under a Lipschitz continuity condition on utilities (LipschitzUCB) or with weaker self-γ-tolerant benchmarks (PhasedUCB), the leader can start learning immediately, achieving Õ(√T) regret.

## Results

The paper establishes that the standard Stackelberg equilibrium benchmarks are fundamentally unachievable, yielding linear regret Ω(T) for at least one player (Theorem 3.1). Against the proposed γ-tolerant benchmarks, both players achieve Õ(T^{2/3}) regret via the ExploreThenUCB algorithm (Theorem 4.4), which is near-optimal given the matching Ω(T^{2/3}·|B|^{1/3}) lower bound (Theorem 4.5). Under a Lipschitz continuity condition on utilities, faster Õ(√T) regret is achievable with respect to the original Stackelberg benchmarks (Theorem 5.1), and under self-γ-tolerant benchmarks, Õ(√(|A|·|B|·T·log T)) regret is achievable (Theorem 5.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or dataset comparisons. The key theoretical comparisons are: (1) the paper shows that out-of-the-box ExploreThenCommit for both players leads to linear regret even against the relaxed γ-tolerant benchmarks (Proposition 4.3), motivating the need for the leader to wait for the follower to partially converge; (2) the Õ(T^{2/3}) upper bound (Theorem 4.4) is shown to be near-tight via a matching Ω(T^{2/3}·|B|^{1/3}) lower bound (Theorem 4.5), with a small gap in the |A| dependence; (3) under a Lipschitz continuity condition (L* bounded), the paper recovers the standard Õ(√T) bandit rate even against the original Stackelberg benchmarks (Theorem 5.1), which is stronger than the γ-tolerant benchmark result; (4) the self-γ-tolerant benchmark (Theorem 5.3) achieves Õ(√T) regret with a matching Ω(√(T·(|A|-1)·|B|)) lower bound (Proposition C.5), confirming near-optimality in that relaxed setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on learning in Stackelberg games (Bai et al. 2021; Gan et al. 2023; Camara et al. 2020; Collina et al. 2023b) by moving from centralized or alignment-assuming settings to a fully decentralized environment where each player only observes their own stochastic rewards and no alignment between utilities is assumed. It builds on standard multi-armed bandit tools (UCB from Auer et al. 2002; divergence-based lower bound techniques from Lattimore and Szepesvári 2020) but repurposes them for the two-player sequential setting where the leader's environment is non-stationary due to the follower's evolving behavior. A key departure from prior work is the introduction of γ-tolerant benchmarks that replace the infeasible Stackelberg equilibrium, and the simultaneous analysis of both players' regret rather than focusing solely on the leader.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the natural Stackelberg equilibrium benchmark is fundamentally unachievable in decentralized learning (linear regret for at least one player) and that T^{2/3} regret is a tight lower bound, providing a clear theoretical ceiling for what any pair of algorithms can guarantee. This has direct implications for designing recommender systems and chatbots where both the algorithm and the human user are learning: it shows that misalignment between their objectives creates an irreducible cost that cannot be eliminated by better algorithms alone. Open questions include closing the gap in |A| dependence between upper and lower bounds, extending the self-γ-tolerant benchmark results to allow richer follower algorithm classes, and incorporating application-specific structure (e.g., linear utilities, specific learning rates in advertising auctions).
