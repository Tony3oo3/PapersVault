## Problem

Existing arbitrary-scale super-resolution methods rely on MLP-based implicit neural representations that suffer from spectral bias, producing over-diffused predictions that fail to capture fine-scale high-frequency structures in scientific data (e.g., small-scale vortices in turbulence). Additionally, per-pixel L1/L2 losses bias reconstruction toward averaged outputs, and deep encoder features often lack sufficient spatial resolution for dense prediction tasks. The paper addresses these gaps by reformulating SR as operator learning between function spaces, enabling resolution-invariant inference.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a low-resolution discretization of a continuous function (e.g., a 32×32 turbulence snapshot or 720×1440 weather field) and produces a super-resolved output at any arbitrary target resolution, with no fixed upsampling factor. During training, upsampling ratios are sampled uniformly from U[1,4], yet the model generalizes to extrapolation ratios up to ×32 at inference. The input is treated as point evaluations of an underlying continuous function rather than a fixed pixel grid, and the parameter-free sampler renders encoder features at the target resolution via patch-based interpolation before the decoder produces the final output.

## Method

HiNOTE composes three components: a hybrid upsampling encoder (combining convolution and Fourier-domain upsampling, placed at the network onset rather than the end), a parameter-free sampler that renders features at arbitrary resolutions using area-normalized patch ensembling, and a hierarchical U-Net decoder built on a neural operator with Galerkin-type self-attention. The hierarchical architecture uses sinc-based bandlimited upsampling/downsampling to avoid aliasing errors across K resolution levels, while the Galerkin-type attention reduces kernel integral complexity from O(m²d_h) to O(md_h²) by interpreting Q/K/V columns as learned basis functions and applying Monte-Carlo approximation. A learnable frequency-aware loss prior, computed as the absolute difference between the model prediction and a spectral-resizing of the input, is min-max normalized and exponentiated to produce per-pixel weights that redistribute gradient contributions toward high-frequency regions the model struggles to fit.

## Results

On the arbitrary-scale SR benchmark (turbulence flow data), HiNOTE achieves the best MSE across all tested upsampling ratios: 7.121e-6 (×4.6), 8.906e-5 (×8.2), 7.361e-4 (×15.7), and 2.041e-3 (×32), yielding an average 20.11% MSE reduction over the second-best method (SRNO). On the single-scale ×4 task, HiNOTE attains PSNR of 51.903 dB on turbulence, 46.424 dB on temperature, 36.995 dB on kinetic energy, and 44.501 dB on water vapor, outperforming all 10 baselines. The model uses only 1.5M parameters, fewer than SwinIR (2.1M), while the Galerkin-type self-attention reduces FLOPs from 28.062G (vanilla) to 2.708G per sample.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiNOTE outperforms all 10 baselines (5 single-scale: SRCNN, ESPCN, EDSR, WDSR, SwinIR; 5 arbitrary-scale: MetaSR, LIIF, LTE, DFNO, SRNO) across all four datasets and all tested upsampling ratios. The closest competitor is SRNO, which HiNOTE surpasses by an average 20.11% in MSE reduction; at ×8.2 the gap is particularly large (8.906e-5 vs. 1.634e-4 MSE). LTE is the second-best at ×4.6 (7.860e-6 vs. 7.121e-6 MSE) but degrades more sharply at higher scales. On the single-scale ×4 turbulence task, CNN-based EDSR and WDSR outperform the transformer-based SwinIR, yet HiNOTE still leads all methods (6.112e-6 vs. 6.270e-6 for WDSR). Notably, at the extreme ×32 ratio, bilinear interpolation (1.703e-3 MSE) surpasses several deep learning baselines (MetaSR 2.716e-3, LIIF 2.283e-3), suggesting the reliable extrapolation range for these models is approximately ×16 given training on ratios up to ×4.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiNOTE sits at the intersection of neural operator learning (Kovachki et al., 2023) and arbitrary-scale super-resolution, replacing the MLP-based implicit neural representations used by LIIF, LTE, and LINF with a hierarchical transformer decoder that learns mappings between function spaces. The core attention mechanism is Galerkin-type self-attention (Cao, 2021), which reduces kernel integral complexity from quadratic to linear, while the encoder borrows the bandlimited sinc-filter upsampling strategy from alias-free GANs (Karras et al., 2021) and the Fourier upsampling module from Yu et al. (2022). The paper departs from prior arbitrary-scale SR work primarily in two ways: (1) treating the SR problem as operator learning rather than coordinate-based implicit function evaluation, and (2) introducing a model-agnostic, spectral-resizing-based frequency-aware loss prior that reweights pixel contributions during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency-aware loss prior is a simple, model-agnostic mechanism that could be adopted by other SR or reconstruction methods to better capture high-frequency details, and its 21.78% MSE reduction on Kinetic Energy (the most challenging dataset) demonstrates practical value for scientific data. A notable practical finding is that deep learning SR models degrade to interpolation-level performance at extreme extrapolation ratios (×32), suggesting a reliable extrapolation ceiling of roughly 16× given training on up to 4×; extending this range would require incorporating larger upsampling factors during training. Open questions include whether the operator-learning formulation and Galerkin attention generalize to 3D volumetric data (e.g., volumetric MRI or 3D weather fields), and whether the hierarchical sinc-filter design can be made fully resolution-independent without the fixed K-scale hierarchy.
