## Problem

Existing provable algorithms for multi-task linear representation learning (AltGDmin, FedRep) solve the non-convex low-rank matrix problem only in centralized or attack-free federated settings. This paper addresses the missing Byzantine-resilient federated solution, where up to τL nodes (τ < 0.4) can collude and send arbitrary messages. A key technical obstacle is that the spectral initialization step operates on subspaces (Stiefel manifold), which cannot be aggregated with standard geometric median since U and −U denote the same subspace.

## Input / Output

Each of L federated nodes holds a disjoint subset of rows (m̃ = m/L) from the q-task data matrix, with per-task feature vectors X_k ∈ R^{m×n} and scalar outputs y_k ∈ R^m. The algorithm outputs an n×r representation matrix U (column span approximating U*) and per-task regression vectors b_k ∈ R^r, enabling few-shot prediction on a new task using only m_new > r samples via b_new = (X_new U)† y_new. The constraint r ≪ min(n,q) is essential: without the learned representation, the n-dimensional regression is intractable in the data-scarce regime.

## Method

Byz-AltGDmin makes the alternating GD-minimization algorithm Byzantine-resilient in two stages. For initialization, the paper introduces Subspace Median (and its Median-of-Means variant): each node computes the top-r singular vectors of its local matrix (Θ₀)_ℓ, the center orthonormalizes all received estimates, computes projection matrices P_ℓ = U_ℓ U_ℓ^⊤, takes the geometric median of the vectorized projections, and selects the node whose projection is closest to this GM — circumventing the fact that subspaces do not live in a Euclidean space. For the GD iterations, each node computes a local partial gradient ∇f_ℓ using its own b_k estimate, and the center aggregates via geometric median (or GM of means over mini-batches of ρ = L/L̃ nodes) before taking a projected GD step with QR re-orthonormalization. Sample-splitting across T = Cκ² log(1/ε) iterations yields a final guarantee of SD_F(U*, U) ≤ ε with per-node communication cost O(nr log(n/ε)) and sample complexity m̃q ≥ Cκ⁴μ²(n+q)r² log(1/ε)(L̃/L).

## Results

In the resilient federated PCA setting (Table 1), the proposed Subspace Median (SubsMed) achieves a sample complexity of nrL/ε² and per-node communication cost of nr, compared to n²L/ε² and n² for SVD-ResCovEst, and max(n²r², n/ε²)·L and nr·(σ_r*/Δ)·log(n/ε) for ResPowMeth. In the numerical few-shot learning experiments (L=18 nodes), the GMoM-based initialization achieves a max SD_F error of 0.477 (mean 0.457) with 1 Byzantine node, substantially lower than the GM-based initialization's 0.716 (mean 0.665). The complete Byz-AltGDmin algorithm guarantees SD_F(U*, U) ≤ ε after T = Cκ²log(1/ε) iterations with per-node communication cost O(nr·log(n/ε)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed Subspace Median (SubsMed) against two natural Byzantine-resilient baselines for federated PCA: SVD-ResCovEst (geometric median of covariance matrices, Minsker 2015) and ResPowMeth (GM-based modification of the power method, building on Hardt & Price 2014). SubsMed dominates both on sample complexity: it requires only nrL/ε² samples versus n²L/ε² for SVD-ResCovEst (a factor of n improvement) and max(n²r², n/ε²)·L for ResPowMeth. On communication cost, SubsMed needs only nr per node versus n² for SVD-ResCovEst and nr·(σ_r*/Δ)·log(n/ε) for ResPowMeth, making it the most communication-efficient resilient option. In the numerical few-shot learning experiments, the GMoM variant of the proposed algorithm achieves roughly 33% lower initialization error (0.477 vs 0.716 max SD_F) compared to the plain GM variant under 1 Byzantine node, and both outperform the non-resilient mean-aggregation baseline in the no-attack setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AltGDmin algorithm (Nayer & Vaswani, 2023) and the FedRep federated framework (Collins et al., 2021) to a Byzantine-attack federated setting, where up to 40% of nodes can be adversarial. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of Subspace Median and Subspace Median of Means: because subspaces live on the Stiefel manifold rather than a Euclidean space, standard geometric median cannot be applied directly to basis matrices, so the authors instead compute the GM of vectorized projection matrices and select the node whose subspace is closest to the GM. For the GD iterations, the paper borrows the geometric-median-of-means aggregation strategy from the Byzantine-resilient SGD literature (Chen et al., 2017; Yin et al., 2018; Alistarh et al., 2018), but notes the analysis is non-trivial because the GD step here is not a standard projected gradient step for a fixed cost function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provably Byzantine-resilient solution for multi-task linear representation learning and few-shot learning in a federated setting, with explicit sample, time, and communication complexity guarantees. The Subspace Median tool is of independent interest for a broad class of federated subspace learning problems (online PCA, subspace tracking, phase retrieval, low-rank recovery), as the authors explicitly note. However, the empirical validation is very limited (a single figure with L=18 nodes and 1–2 Byzantine nodes), the noise case (σ_v²≠0) is only sketched rather than fully proven, and no real-world data experiments are included. Open questions include whether the guarantees hold under dynamic (non-fixed) Byzantine sets, how the approach scales to larger node counts, and whether the linear representation assumption is sufficient for practical few-shot tasks.
