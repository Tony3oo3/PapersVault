## Problem

Deterministic diffusion samplers like DDIM are widely used in practice but lack a purely optimization-theoretic analysis with convergence guarantees. While diffusion models are typically framed probabilistically, the deterministic nature of popular samplers suggests a deterministic interpretation is possible, yet no prior work provides convergence bounds or uses such analysis to guide sampler design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241642/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The sampler takes a pretrained denoiser ε_θ (trained on CIFAR-10 32×32, CelebA 64×64, or Stable Diffusion latent space) and a log-linear noise schedule {σ_t}, starting from x_N ~ N(0,I). For text-to-image generation with Stable Diffusion, a text prompt conditions the denoiser. The output is a single generated image x_0 after N denoiser evaluations (as few as 5–10 steps), with the key practical detail that the schedule uses a smaller initial σ_N (e.g., 40 vs. 157 for standard DDIM) to concentrate steps at lower noise levels.

## Method

The paper formalizes denoising as approximate projection onto the data manifold via a relative-error model: ‖x − σ ε_θ(x,σ) − proj_K(x)‖ ≤ η·dist_K(x) when √n·σ ≈ dist_K(x). Under this model, DDIM is shown to be gradient descent on f(x) = ½·dist_K(x)² with step-size β_t = 1 − σ_{t-1}/σ_t, yielding explicit convergence bounds. The proposed gradient-estimation sampler (Algorithm 2) exploits the invariance of ∇dist_K(x) along line segments to the manifold: since denoiser outputs at nearby points should be similar, it combines the current and previous denoiser outputs via ε̄_t = 2ε_θ(x_t,σ_t) − ε_θ(x_{t+1},σ_{t+1}) to cancel correlated estimation error. This second-order update, paired with a log-linear (geometric) σ schedule derived from the admissibility conditions, achieves state-of-the-art FID scores with only 5–10 function evaluations.

## Results

The proposed gradient-estimation sampler achieves state-of-the-art FID scores on pretrained CIFAR-10 and CelebA models in as few as 5–10 function evaluations. On CIFAR-10, it reaches FID 3.79 at N=10 (vs. 16.86 for DDIM and 3.87 for UniPC) and 3.32 at N=20 (vs. 3.48 for DPM-Solver). On CelebA, it achieves FID 4.41 at N=10 (vs. 18.08 for DDIM and 5.83 for DPM-Solver). The sampler also produces high-quality text-to-image samples on Stable Diffusion with N=10 steps, performing comparably to other commonly used samplers on MS-COCO 30K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed sampler outperforms DDIM by a large margin across all settings (e.g., FID 3.79 vs. 16.86 on CIFAR-10 at N=10; 4.41 vs. 18.08 on CelebA at N=10). It also beats PNDM, DEIS, and A-DDIM on both datasets at all comparable step counts. On CIFAR-10, it edges out UniPC (3.79 vs. 3.87 at N=10) and DPM-Solver (3.32 vs. 3.48 at N=20; 3.41 vs. 3.48 at N=50). On CelebA, it wins at N=5 (10.76 vs. 11.3 for PNDM) and N=10 (4.41 vs. 5.83 for DPM-Solver), but DPM-Solver retains a slight advantage at N=20 (2.82 vs. 3.19) and N=50 (2.71 vs. 3.04). The EDM schedule comparison (Table 1) shows the log-linear schedule alone improves DDIM from 16.86 to 13.25 on CIFAR-10 at N=10, confirming the schedule design contributes independently of the second-order update.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDIM/DDPM diffusion framework (Song et al., 2020a; Ho et al., 2020) by providing a deterministic optimization-theoretic interpretation that complements the standard probabilistic/SDE view. It makes rigorous the projection interpretation of denoising first suggested by Rick Chang et al. (2017), formalizing it as a relative-error model and showing DDIM is equivalent to gradient descent on the squared Euclidean distance function with bounded relative error. The gradient-estimation sampler (Algorithm 2) draws on linear multistep methods and accelerated gradient descent (Scieur et al., 2017), but differs from ODE solvers (Karras et al., 2022; Lu et al., 2022a; Zhao et al., 2023) in that it minimizes gradient-estimation error rather than discretization error. The invariance property of the distance gradient along projection line-segments provides an alternative justification for consistency models (Song et al., 2023) and connects to score distillation sampling (Poole et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The convergence analysis of DDIM under the (η,ν) relative-error model is the first of its kind, providing explicit distance bounds and characterizing admissible noise schedules—insights that directly inform practical schedule design (log-linear with geometric rate). The gradient-estimation sampler achieves competitive FID with very few steps (3.79 on CIFAR-10 at N=10 vs. DDIM's 16.86), though the ablation reveals the schedule choice contributes substantially. A key limitation is that the error model's parameters (η, ν) are difficult to estimate a priori, and Theorem 4.4 shows final error is bounded but need not vanish, motivating the heuristic full-step at the last iteration. The broader framework opens paths to incorporating arbitrary constraints and objectives into generative models via the projection/distance-function lens, though this remains largely speculative.
