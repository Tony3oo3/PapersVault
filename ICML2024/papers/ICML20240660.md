## Problem

Existing knowledge distillation methods for diffusion models either require expensive offline generation of synthetic training targets by running the full teacher sampler, or depend on access to the original real training data to simulate intermediate diffusion states (as in Consistency Models and TRACT). This is a critical limitation for large-scale text-to-image models whose training corpora consist of billions of pairs with unknown preprocessing, making data-dependent distillation infeasible. BOOT addresses this gap by enabling single-step distillation that requires no real or synthetic data at all.

## Input / Output

The distilled student model always takes pure Gaussian noise ε ~ N(0, I) as its spatial input, combined with a target timestep t and an optional condition (text prompt or class label), and produces a single generated image in one forward pass (1 NFE). Unlike consistency models that must consume intermediate noisy states x_t derived from real data, BOOT's input is always the noise prior, which is what makes the procedure data-free. For text-to-image, prompts are drawn from DiffusionDB (14M user-generated prompts), and the model supports classifier-free guidance with a learnable weight w, producing outputs at resolutions from 32×32 (CIFAR-10) up to 512×512 (StableDiffusion) or 1024×1024 (DeepFloyd IF).

## Method

BOOT introduces a Signal-ODE, a continuous-time ODE defined over the 'signal' component y_t = (x_t − σ_t ε)/α_t rather than the full noisy state x_t, so that the fixed noise ε cancels out and the network always operates in a low-frequency signal space that is easier to learn. The training objective is a bootstrapping loss derived via finite differences of the Signal-ODE: the student at an earlier step s is trained to match a one-step teacher-corrected version of itself at a later step t, with a stop-gradient on the target to ensure stability. Crucially, no EMA copy of the student is needed (unlike Consistency Models) because the incremental improvement term in the target is generically non-zero, preventing collapse. An auxiliary boundary loss at t_max enforces that the student's initial denoising output matches the teacher's, which is essential to avoid severe mode collapse and color saturation. The student architecture mirrors the teacher U-Net with additional conditioning modules for the target timestep t (and optionally CFG weight w), initialized from the teacher weights with zero-initialized new modules.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (unconditional, 1 NFE), BOOT with LPIPS loss achieves FID 4.38, competitive with CM (3.55) and DFNO (4.12) which both require real or synthetic data, while substantially outperforming PD (8.34) and Direct distillation (9.36). On FFHQ 64×64, BOOT achieves FID 9.0 in 1 step, improving over 10-step DDIM (18.3) by 2× while remaining 10× faster than 50-step DDIM (7.6). On class-conditional ImageNet 64×64, BOOT reaches FID 12.3 in 1 step, beating 50-step DDIM (13.7) and 10-step DDIM (18.3). For text-to-image (StableDiffusion), BOOT achieves a zero-shot CLIP score of 0.254 on COCO2017 (vs. 0.262 for 50-step DDIM) and FID 30.21 on DiffusionDB, far surpassing LCM (111.55).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among 1-NFE distillation methods on CIFAR-10, BOOT (LPIPS, FID 4.38) is the only fully data-free approach and outperforms PD (8.34), Direct distillation (9.36), and ReFlow (6.18) by large margins. It trails CM (3.55) and DFNO (4.12) by roughly 0.2–0.8 FID, but both of those require real training data or large-scale synthetic data generation, which BOOT explicitly avoids. On FFHQ 64×64, BOOT (FID 9.0, 1 step) beats 10-step DDIM (18.3) and 1-step DDIM (225) decisively, though 50-step DDIM (7.6) remains slightly better at 50× the inference cost. On ImageNet 64×64, BOOT (12.3) actually surpasses 50-step DDIM (13.7) while being 50× faster. For text-to-image, BOOT's FID of 30.21 on DiffusionDB dramatically outperforms LCM (111.55), and its CLIP score (0.254) is within 0.008 of the 50-step DDIM teacher (0.262) at 2 orders of magnitude faster inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

BOOT sits at the intersection of diffusion model distillation and ODE-based generative modeling. It builds directly on the DDIM/PF-ODE framework (Song et al., 2021; Song et al., 2020) to derive a new 'Signal-ODE' that operates in a reparameterized signal space where the noise term cancels out. Conceptually, it departs from Consistency Models (Song et al., 2023) and TRACT (Berthelot et al., 2023), which bootstrap from real data by mapping intermediate noisy states x_t back to x_0, by instead bootstrapping forward from pure Gaussian noise ε through all timesteps — making the student's input distribution fixed and data-free. The bootstrapping objective resembles Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) in structure (DE loss + boundary loss), but applied to high-dimensional generative modeling rather than low-dimensional PDEs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that BOOT enables single-step distillation of large text-to-image models (Stable Diffusion, DeepFloyd IF) without access to their training corpora, which may consist of billions of pairs with unknown preprocessing — a critical constraint for proprietary or privacy-sensitive models. The quality gap versus data-dependent methods (e.g., FID 4.38 vs. 3.55 on CIFAR-10 vs. CM) remains a limitation, and the current design is restricted to single-step generation, precluding the multi-step quality improvements available in CM. Open questions include whether BOOT can be combined with data-dependent objectives for further gains, whether multi-step variants with restart sampling can close the quality gap, and whether the Signal-ODE framework generalizes to training single-step diffusion models from scratch without a pre-trained teacher.
