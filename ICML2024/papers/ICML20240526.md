## Problem

Existing video MLLMs struggle with complex video reasoning due to two under-explored bottlenecks: fine-grained pixel-level spatial-temporal perception and cognitive-level scene comprehension leveraging commonsense. While Chain-of-Thought prompting has proven effective for language tasks, no CoT-based reasoning framework specifically tailored for video input with video MLLMs had been developed, leaving a gap between shallow perceptual video understanding and human-like multi-step video reasoning.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a video clip (uniformly sampled at 8 fps) and a text question, producing a final QA answer (multiple-choice or open-ended) along with intermediate STSG expressions that serve as grounding rationale. The STSG output encodes per-frame object proposals with bounding boxes, CLIP-based neural embeddings, category labels, predicate triplets, and cross-frame temporal coreference edges linking the same objects over time. For open-ended questions, the system first generates four candidate answers before ranking them, unifying all QA into a multi-choice format.

## Method

MotionEpic is a video MLLM built on Vicuna-7B with a ViT-L/14 video encoder, Q-Former projector, and a 6-layer recurrent Graph Transformer (768-d hidden) that encodes multi-frame STSGs with temporal coreference edges; it is trained via grounding-aware tuning with five objectives (pairing, full STSG generation, partial STSG from action descriptions, action description from objects, and bbox-to-label) on Action Genome and WebVid-10M video-STSG pairs, using LoRA for parameter-efficient updates. On top of MotionEpic, the Video-of-Thought (VoT) framework decomposes video QA into five sequential steps: target identification, STSG-based object tracking, action analysis integrating neighbor scenes and commonsense, candidate answer scoring and ranking (1–10 scale with rationale), and dual-perspective verification checking both pixel-grounding consistency and commonsense coherence. This explicit multi-step decomposition from low-level pixel grounding to high-level cognitive interpretation distinguishes VoT from vanilla CoT's single 'think step by step' prompt, and the STSG-based grounding mitigates hallucination by anchoring reasoning to structured visual evidence.

## Results

On the fine-tuning video QA benchmarks, MotionEpic with VoT achieves 73.4% on VLEP, 71.5/72.6/66.6/62.7% on STAR (Int./Seq./Pre./Fea.), 70.8% on IntentQA, and 72.8/45.0% on Social-IQ (2-Way/4-Way), setting new state-of-the-art across all metrics. On Causal-VidQA, VoT+MotionEpic reaches 81.2% (Description), 83.0% (Explanation), 54.7% (Prediction AR), and 58.6% (Counterfactual AR). On NExT-QA, it achieves 76.0% overall accuracy (75.8% Causal, 74.6% Temporal, 83.3% Descriptive). In zero-shot settings, VoT+MotionEpic averages 62.3% across MSR-VTT, ActivityNet, NExT-QA, and STAR, outperforming the best zero-shot baseline (VideoChat2 at 56.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionEpic+VoT consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across every reported metric. Against the strongest non-MLLM baselines (VLAP, SeViLA, LLaMA-VQA), VoT gains 3.8–8.3 points on VLEP/STAR and 8.3 points on IntentQA. Among video MLLM baselines, the largest margins appear on IntentQA (+8.3 over Video-LLaVA) and Social-IQ 4-Way (+5.8 over Video-LLaVA), indicating VoT's advantage grows with reasoning complexity. On Causal-VidQA, VoT+MotionEpic beats the best MLLM baseline (Video-LLaVA) by 7.5 points on Description and 7.1 on Explanation, and on NExT-QA it surpasses VLAP by 0.5 points overall and 2.3 on Temporal. In zero-shot settings, VoT+MotionEpic leads VideoChat2 (the strongest zero-shot baseline) by 6.3 points on average, with the gap widening on cognitively demanding benchmarks (NExT-QA +4.8, STAR +2.7) versus perceptual ones (MSR-VTT +2.1, ActivityNet +5.5). The paper also shows that simply adding CoT to Video-LLaVA yields only marginal gains (e.g., +1.4 on VLEP), while the full VoT decomposition provides substantially larger improvements, confirming that structured multi-step reasoning from perception to cognition is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Chain-of-Thought prompting paradigm (Wei et al., 2022) from text to video by proposing a structured 5-step decomposition (VoT) that progresses from pixel-level grounding to cognitive verification, rather than relying on a single 'think step by step' prompt. It builds on the spatial-temporal scene graph representation from Action Genome (Ji et al., 2020), retrofitting it with temporal coreference edges and encoding it via a recurrent Graph Transformer (Dwivedi & Bresson, 2020) to enable fine-grained video grounding within a multimodal LLM. The key departure from existing video MLLMs (Video-LLaMA, Video-ChatGPT, Video-LLaVA) is the implicit integration of STSG as a native input/output modality of the LLM, rather than treating scene graphs as an external auxiliary signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that structured multi-step video reasoning (VoT) yields substantially larger gains than vanilla CoT prompting, suggesting that the decomposition strategy matters as much as the model's raw capability. The STSG-based grounding mechanism offers a principled way to mitigate hallucination in video MLLMs, which is a persistent practical concern. Open questions include whether the 5-step pipeline generalizes to longer videos or streaming settings, the computational overhead of multi-step inference, and whether the approach transfers to tasks beyond multiple-choice QA. The paper also acknowledges energy and misuse concerns inherent in deploying powerful video reasoning systems.
