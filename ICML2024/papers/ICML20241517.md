## Problem

Two fundamental questions about lifted multicut polytopes had remained open: which lower box inequalities 0≤x_e with e∈F define facets, and which cut inequalities define facets. While Hornákova et al. (2017) and Andres et al. (2023) characterized facets induced by cycle, path, upper box, and lower box (e∈E) inequalities, the remaining classes were unresolved, leaving the canonical facet analysis of these polytopes incomplete.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241517/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a connected graph G=(V,E), an augmentation Ĝ=(V,E∪F) with E∩F=∅, and a specific edge uw∈E∪F (for lower box facets) or an f-cut δ of G (for cut facets). For lower box facets, it outputs two graph-theoretic conditions—absence of certain paths and cycles of uw-separators in Ĝ—that are necessary, sufficient, and decidable in polynomial time. For cut facets, it outputs an NP-hardness result, with the intermediate characterization (existence of f_d-paths for all d∈δ) serving as a certificate in the |F|=1 special case.

## Method

For lower box facets, the paper proves that 0≤x_uw is facet-defining iff (i) no simple path in Ĝ of uw-separators connects two uw-cut-nodes, and (ii) no simple cycle in Ĝ consists entirely of uw-separators. The necessity proof constructs a bijection between even and odd edges of any violating path/cycle that are set to 1, revealing a hidden alternating-sum equality; a key structural lemma uses Menger's theorem to show that any cycle of uw-separators avoiding uw-cut-nodes must be even. The sufficiency proof builds |E∪F|−1 linearly independent vectors in the difference space via a layered induction over edge sets H_j. For cut facets, the paper reduces 3-SAT to deciding whether f_d-paths exist for all d∈δ in a constructed graph where variable layers and clause layers are connected by a cut δ encoding literal negation, so that satisfying assignments correspond exactly to valid f_d-paths.

## Results

The paper establishes two main theoretical results. First, Theorem 4.1 provides necessary, sufficient, and efficiently decidable conditions (involving the absence of certain paths and cycles of uw-separators in the augmented graph) for a lower box inequality 0 ≤ x_uw to define a facet of the lifted multicut polytope Ξ_{GĜ}. Second, Theorem 5.4 proves that deciding whether a cut inequality (4) defines a facet is NP-hard, even in the special case |F| = 1, via a reduction from 3-SAT. These results complete the characterization of all canonical facet classes of lifted multicut polytopes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments, baselines, or quantitative comparisons. The contributions are mathematical: a complete facet characterization for lower box inequalities (extending prior partial results by Hornákova et al. 2017 and Andres et al. 2023) and an NP-hardness result for cut inequality facets. The paper positions its results as completing the polyhedral analysis of lifted multicut polytopes, filling gaps left by prior work that had only established necessary conditions for these two facet classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper completes the polyhedral characterization of lifted multicut polytopes initiated by Hornákova et al. (2017), who established the dimension, cycle/path inequality facets, upper box facets, and lower box facets for edges in E, and Andres et al. (2023), who added half-chorded odd cycle and intersection inequality facets along with necessary conditions for cut and lower box (e∈F) facets. The present work fills the two remaining gaps: it gives a full (necessary, sufficient, efficiently decidable) characterization of lower box facets for all edges in E∪F using Menger's theorem, and proves NP-hardness of deciding cut inequality facet-definingness. The proof technique for the lower box result—constructing an auxiliary graph by removing cut-nodes and applying Menger's theorem to show cycles of separators must be even—is a clean application of classical graph theory to a polyhedral question.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The NP-hardness result for cut inequality facets (even in the minimal case |F|=1) is a fundamental limitation for branch-and-cut algorithms: one cannot efficiently identify which cut inequalities are facets, complicating the design of separation routines. The paper suggests that strengthened inequalities beyond the canonical cut inequalities may be needed for practical cutting-plane methods, and identifies the 'paths crossing a cut with a chord' structure as the source of complexity. The lower box facet characterization, by contrast, is directly usable in algorithm design since the conditions are efficiently decidable. Open questions include whether non-local connectedness with respect to separators (rather than cuts) yields tractable facet characterizations, and whether the hardness result extends to related polytopes like the clique partitioning polytope.
