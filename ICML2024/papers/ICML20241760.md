## Problem

Large language models frequently fail to adhere to their prompts, exhibiting hallucination, meandering, and degradation, yet existing remedies like instruction-finetuning and reinforcement learning require substantial data and compute. Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) has proven effective for prompt adherence in text-to-image generation but has never been applied to autoregressive language modeling, where prompts are complex, multipart, and outputs are unbounded rather than fixed-size.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (conditioning c), an optional negative prompt (c̄), and a scalar guidance weight γ, and produces autoregressive text by reweighting the next-token logit distribution at every generation step. The unconditional distribution P(w) is obtained simply by dropping the prefix c from the model's context window, leveraging the fact that LLMs are trained on finite context windows and naturally handle both conditional and unconditional generation. The optimal γ is typically small (<2) for most language-modeling tasks, in contrast to the γ∈3–5 range common in text-to-image diffusion.

## Method

The paper adapts CFG's logit extrapolation formula from diffusion models to autoregressive language modeling: at each token step i, the guided logit is computed as log P(w_i|w_{<i}) + γ·[log P(w_i|w_{<i},c) − log P(w_i|w_{<i})], requiring two forward passes (conditional and unconditional) per token. Unlike diffusion models that require conditioning dropout during training, this formulation works out-of-the-box on any pretrained LLM because dropping the prefix is a natural operation within the model's finite context window. The approach extends to negative prompting by replacing the unconditional term with a negative conditioning c̄, enabling granular control over assistant-style system prompts. The method is architecture-agnostic, operates purely in logit space without network editing, and can be stacked with other inference-time techniques such as Chain-of-Thought and Self-Consistency.

## Results

LLaMA-7B with CFG (γ=1.5) achieves 81.3% zero-shot accuracy on LAMBADA, surpassing PaLM-540B (77.9%) and establishing SOTA. Across GPT-2, Pythia, and LLaMA families, CFG consistently improves performance on 7 of 9 standard NLP benchmarks (e.g., SciQ: 95.1→96.6% for LLaMA-65B; HellaSwag: 84.1→86.3% for LLaMA-65B). On HumanEval code generation, CodeGen-2B-mono with γ=1.5 improves pass@1 from 19.5% to 20.9%. In a blind human evaluation with 611 votes, 75% of raters preferred CFG-guided outputs for system-prompt adherence (γ=3) without degrading user-prompt relevance (52% preference).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FUDGE (Yang & Klein, 2021), a classifier-guidance method for language modeling, CFG achieves substantially greater steering: 0.312 vs. 0.065 for sentiment control (IMDB) and 0.523 vs. 0.045 for toxicity control, while running 100× faster since FUDGE requires an external discriminator at every timestep. Against the compute-matched baseline of simply using a model twice the size (same inference FLOPs), ANCOVA analysis shows statistically insignificant performance differences on 5 of 9 benchmarks (p>0.01), with CFG clearly winning on LAMBADA (p=0.000) and SciQ (p=0.008), vanilla winning on WinoGrande (p=0.003) and TriviaQA (p=0.008), and the rest mixed. Against PaLM-540B on LAMBADA zero-shot, LLaMA-7B with CFG (81.3%) outperforms by 3.4 points despite having ~77× fewer parameters. Within the paper's own ablation, CFG at γ=1.5 consistently outperforms the γ=1 baseline across model families, with the largest gains on LAMBADA (+7.7 to +12.0 points) and SciQ (+6.1 to +9.1 points), while showing slight degradation on WinoGrande and TriviaQA for larger LLaMA models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly adapts the Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) logit-extrapolation formula from diffusion-based text-to-image generation (Ho & Salimans, 2021; Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) to autoregressive language modeling, extending it with negative prompting (Du et al., 2020) for granular assistant control. A key departure from the diffusion setting is that autoregressive LMs naturally produce both conditional and unconditional next-token distributions from finite context windows, eliminating the need for the conditioning-dropout training that diffusion models require. The work positions itself against prior NLP controlled-generation methods (FUDGE, PPLM, GEDI, Contrastive Decoding) that rely on external classifiers or discriminators, and against concurrent CFG-in-NLP efforts (Malkin et al., 2022; Pei et al., 2023; Shi et al., 2023) that were limited to narrow tasks like toxicity or summarization, by demonstrating broad applicability across zero-shot, CoT, code, and chatbot prompting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CFG is a plug-and-play, training-free inference technique already integrated into Huggingface and llama.cpp, making it immediately accessible; the finding that a smaller model with CFG matches a 2×-sized vanilla model at equivalent inference FLOPs offers a concrete path to reducing training and VRAM costs. Limitations include the need for per-context γ tuning, the doubling of kv-cache memory (which becomes prohibitive at long sequence lengths), and acknowledged risks of CFG being exploited for prompt injection or alignment overriding. Open questions include the precise mechanism by which CFG reorders the vocabulary distribution (distinct from instruction-tuning), optimal γ scheduling across different parts of a CoT chain, and memory-reduction strategies for the doubled kv-cache.
