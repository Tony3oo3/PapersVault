## Problem

Learning with expert advice (full information) and multi-armed bandit (no side information) are two classic online decision problems with well-understood minimax regret bounds, but the natural interpolation—where arms are partitioned into groups and pulling one arm reveals losses of all arms in the same group—lacked tight bounds. Existing results for bandit with graph feedback, while general, yield suboptimal regret for this specific clique-union structure, leaving a gap between the known upper and lower bounds.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a group structure vector m = (m_1, …, m_K) specifying K groups of sizes m_k (total N = Σm_k arms) and, at each round, observes the losses of all arms in the pulled arm's group (losses in [0,1]). For m-MAB, the output is an arm pulled each round to minimize cumulative regret over T rounds; for m-BAI, the output is a single arm whose mean loss is within ε of the optimal arm's mean, achieved with probability at least 0.95. The algorithm knows m and T in advance, and the BAI version uses a reduction from MAB that samples from the empirical pull-frequency distribution after T* rounds.

## Method

The paper designs a two-stage online stochastic mirror descent (OSMD) algorithm that maintains a distribution Y^(t) over K groups (updated via Tsallis entropy ψ(y) = -2Σ√y(i)) and within-group distributions X_k^(t) (updated via negative entropy). The key technical contribution is a novel analysis that views the discrete algorithm as a discretization of an auxiliary piecewise continuous process governed by ODEs, then bounds the accumulated discretization error. This yields a regret decomposition where the Y^(t)(k) weighting cancels the 1/Y^(t)(k) factor inherent in the loss estimator, producing the tight bound O(√(T·Σ log(m_k+1))) rather than the suboptimal O(√(KT·log N)) from prior approaches. For the matching lower bound, the paper proves that the all-Ber(1/2) instance is a universal hard instance via a 'direct sum' reduction: any (ε,0.05)-PAC algorithm must simultaneously identify that each group consists of Ber(1/2) arms, and the per-group lower bound is established using the Bretagnolle-Huber inequality on a Gaussian-to-Bernoulli reduction.

## Results

The paper establishes tight minimax regret bounds for m-MAB: Θ(√(T · Σ_{k=1}^K log(m_k+1))), which interpolates between the classical MAB bound Θ(√(NT)) (when all m_k=1) and the expert advice bound Θ(√(T log N)) (when K=1, m_1=N). For the pure exploration version m-BAI, the paper proves a matching upper and lower bound of Θ((1/ε²) · Σ_{k=1}^K log(m_k+1)) rounds for an (ε, 0.05)-PAC algorithm. These bounds are extended to general bandit with graph feedback via clique cover parameters, yielding improved bounds for several families of feedback graphs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main improvement is over the disjoint union of K cliques setting (m-MAB), where the previous best upper bound was O(√(KT) log(NT)) (Alon et al., 2017) and the lower bound was Ω(√(KT)). The new bound Θ(√(T Σ log(m_k+1))) is strictly tighter: for example, when K=⌊log N⌋ and m=(1,1,...,1,N−K+1), the true minimax regret is Θ(√(T log N)) while both Eldowa et al. (2024) with their independence-number-based bound O(√(TK log(N/K))) and Foster et al. (2020) with their two-stage algorithm bound O(√(TK max_k log m_k)) only achieve O(√(T) log N). For general strongly observable graphs, the new clique-cover-based upper bound O(√(T Σ log m_k)) replaces the older O(√(αT) log(NT)) (Alon et al., 2015) and is always superior since it accounts for the actual clique sizes rather than just the independence number α. For weakly observable graphs, the new lower bound (Theorem 1.7) confirms a conjecture from He & Zhang (2023) and implies the optimality of several previously established regret bounds, including for disjoint unions of loopless complete bipartite graphs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the two-stage online stochastic mirror descent (OSMD) framework of He & Zhang (2023) for bandit with graph feedback, but introduces a novel analysis via piecewise continuous processes that yields tight bounds where the prior framework gave suboptimal ones. The m-MAB problem is positioned as a natural interpolation between multi-armed bandit (Audibert & Bubeck, 2009) and learning with expert advice (Freund & Schapire, 1997), and as a structured special case of bandit with graph feedback (Alon et al., 2015; Chen et al., 2021). The paper departs from prior graph-feedback work by showing that clique-cover-based parameters (Σ log(m_k+1)) are the correct quantity, improving upon independence-number-based bounds (Eldowa et al., 2024) and two-stage algorithm analyses (Foster et al., 2020) that were suboptimal for this structured setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first tight minimax regret and PAC sample complexity bounds for the m-MAB/m-BAI family, resolving the exact dependence on group structure and confirming that the 'all Ber(1/2)' instance is a universal hard instance for BAI. The generalization to bandit with graph feedback via clique covers and t-packing independent sets confirms a conjecture from He & Zhang (2023) and tightens bounds for several graph families. Open questions include whether the piecewise continuous process analysis technique extends to other two-stage algorithms, and whether the instance-specific BAI lower bound methodology (using mixture distributions rather than pairwise comparisons) can sharpen logarithmic factors in other exploration problems.
