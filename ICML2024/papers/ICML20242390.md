## Problem

Traditional data augmentation pipelines hard-code specific parametric transformations (rotations, color shifts, style transfer) and cannot simulate arbitrary environmental interventions—such as turning a cartoon into a photo or changing a scene's weather—without manual engineering. The paper asks whether modern text-to-image generators can serve as general-purpose interventional data augmentation mechanisms, using natural language prompts to simulate arbitrary domain shifts and thereby improve classifier robustness under covariate shift and spurious correlations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242390/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 source-domain images paired with interventional text prompts (e.g., 'a sketch of an elephant') and produces augmented images that mimic a target domain while preserving the class label. For each training image, k augmented samples are pre-generated (k=3 for PACS/Office-Home, k=5 for NICO++/DomainNet, k=4 for RRSF tasks), and one is randomly sampled per training step. The augmented images are concatenated with the originals to train standard classifiers (ResNet-18/50, ConvNeXt-L) via cross-entropy minimization.

## Method

The core mechanism uses Stable Diffusion v1.5 (pre-trained on LAION-Aesthetics) with SDEdit to edit source images conditioned on interventional prompts that describe the target environmental domain. The paper systematically ablates three axes: (1) prompting strategies—minimal ('a sketch of an elephant'), handcrafted domain-expert prompts, and T5-generated language-enhanced prompts (conservative and diverse variants); (2) conditioning mechanisms—SDEdit (image-conditioned editing), Text2Image (text-only generation from noise), ControlNet (Canny-edge spatial guidance), InstructPix2Pix (instruction-following editing), and direct retrieval from the generator's training corpus; and (3) post-hoc CLIP-based filtering of low-quality outputs. The key finding is that the conditioning mechanism has the largest impact on performance (Text2Image often outperforms image-conditioned editing), prompting strategy matters second, and CLIP filtering does not consistently help. Across SDG and RRSF benchmarks, T2I-based augmentation outperforms all traditional baselines (PixMix, AugMix, ACVC, L2D, etc.) regardless of configuration.

## Results

On the primary SDG evaluation (ResNet-50, average accuracy across PACS, Office-Home, NICO++, and DomainNet), SDEdit with minimal prompting (SDEdit(M)) achieves 61.04%, and the best prompting variant SDEdit(LEC) reaches 61.24%. This represents a ~5% improvement over the strongest traditional baseline PixMix (55.89%), which itself outperforms ERM (54.78%) by only 1.10%. On the largest benchmark DomainNet, SDEdit(M) delivers a 5.48% absolute boost over ERM (31.94% vs. 25.26%), while all traditional augmentation methods show negligible gains. For RRSF, SDEdit enables substantial improvements over ERM across all three spurious-correlation settings (background, texture, and demographic bias).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All four SDEdit prompting variants (M, H, LEC, LEM) outperform every traditional augmentation baseline across all four SDG benchmarks, with the gap ranging from ~5% (DomainNet) to ~11% (PACS). PixMix is the strongest baseline overall (55.89% avg), followed by AugMix (55.56%) and RandAugment (55.42%); notably, no single baseline consistently dominates across benchmarks, reflecting that each encodes different invariance assumptions. On PACS specifically, SDEdit(H) achieves 77.87% versus PixMix's 67.12% (a 10.75% gap), while on DomainNet SDEdit(M) reaches 31.94% versus ERM's 25.26%—a 5.48% gain that no baseline replicates. For RRSF, SDEdit substantially outperforms ERM in all three bias settings (background, texture, demographic), whereas baselines like PixMix can even worsen demographic bias relative to ERM. Interestingly, Text2Image(M) (generating from scratch without an input image) outperforms SDEdit on PACS (83.72% vs. 76.43%) and DomainNet (40.77% vs. 31.94%), suggesting that conditioning on the source image can sometimes constrain the generative process.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the causal intervention framework for domain generalization (Ilse et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2022b) by replacing hand-crafted parametric augmentations (AugMix, PixMix, ACVC, etc.) with natural-language-conditioned T2I generators (Stable Diffusion via SDEdit, ControlNet, InstructPix2Pix, or Text2Image). It extends prior work on T2I-generated synthetic data (Bansal & Grover, 2023; He et al., 2023; Azizi et al., 2023), which focused on pre-training data synthesis or limited augmentation contexts, by systematically studying T2I generators as a general-purpose interventional augmentation mechanism across multiple conditioning mechanisms, prompting strategies, and post-hoc filtering techniques. The key conceptual departure is treating the T2I generator as an off-the-shelf intervention simulator that requires only a text description of the desired environmental change, rather than hard-coding pixel-space transformations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a clear, well-ablated recipe for using off-the-shelf T2I generators to improve classifier robustness, showing 5+ point gains over the strongest traditional augmentation baselines across four SDG benchmarks and three RRSF settings. Key open questions include: whether the approach extends to detection/segmentation tasks (which would require spatial conditioning beyond text prompts), how it behaves for target domains genuinely outside the T2I generator's training distribution, and whether the computational cost of pre-generating N×k images remains prohibitive at scale. The finding that retrieval from the generator's training data can match or exceed generation in some settings also raises questions about the fundamental value of the generative process versus simple nearest-neighbor lookup.
