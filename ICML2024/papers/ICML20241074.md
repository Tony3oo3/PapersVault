## Problem

Batch selection in expensive multi-objective combinatorial optimization (MOCO) requires solving a cardinality-constrained subset selection problem over an exponentially large search space O(|X|^n). Prior approaches either optimize batch acquisition in a continuous latent space (introducing discrepancies with the actual combinatorial space) or select candidates by individual acquisition scores without modeling interdependencies. Greedy algorithms decompose the problem into n smaller subproblems but are inherently sequential, preventing parallelization since each subproblem depends on the previous one's solution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained surrogate model (deterministic or statistical), a batch acquisition function (HVI, EHVI, NEHVI, or UCB-HVI), a cardinality constraint n, and operates over a combinatorial space such as amino-acid sequences or molecular graphs. The set-conditioned policy consumes the current partial batch B via its surrogate predictions f̃(x) ∈ R^m (or UCB variant) as the conditioning set, combined with the current construction state, to output an action distribution over the next candidate. The final output is a proposal batch of n candidates for oracle evaluation; in the multi-round active learning loop, the accumulated non-dominated solutions form the approximate Pareto set.

## Method

The core contribution is a single set-conditioned policy π_θ^set trained via REINFORCE to solve all n greedy subproblems concurrently, amortizing the sequential burden. The architecture uses a deep set encoder over the surrogate predictions of the conditioning set B (justified by Lemma 3.7, which shows marginal gains depend only on f̃(B)), concatenated with a state encoder and decoded by an action head (optionally augmented with an MLM for sequence tasks). Training uses a novel 'partial derivative step' update rule where the policy maximizes the expected marginal gain Δ_a(x|B) with B sampled from its own greedy sampling (self-play), and the behavior policy is refreshed every N_t steps to enable parallel subset sampling. The greedy policy is formally defined as a fixed point of the expected gain function J(θ,θ'), and convergence is guaranteed under smoothness and strong concavity conditions (Theorem 3.5). The paper further extends approximated greedy bounds to near-submodular functions via the submodularity ratio (Theorem 3.9) and to sum-dispersion diversity objectives (Theorem 3.10).

## Results

On the RFP multi-round active learning benchmark, the proposed method achieves the same Hypervolume indicator as the best baseline (MBGA) while requiring 1.69× fewer oracle queries. In the first-round batch BO setting with NEHVI, the method achieves 0.779 on RFP, 16.444 on 3-Bigram, and 0.698 on Small Molecules, outperforming LaMBO (0.591, 9.922, 0.545) and MBGA (0.654, 12.376, 0.483). On single-round synthetic subset selection tasks, the method matches exact greedy on the 2-Bigram task (HV = 0.630 at n=16) and consistently outperforms all RL and heuristic baselines across 3- and 4-Bigram tasks and the DNA aptamer design task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all baselines across every evaluated task. Against LaMBO (latent space optimization), the method achieves 32% higher NEHVI on RFP (0.779 vs. 0.591) and 66% higher on 3-Bigram (16.444 vs. 9.922). Against MBGA (model-based genetic algorithm), the gains are 19% on RFP and 33% on 3-Bigram. Against preference-conditioned RL baselines (PC-RL with Chebyshev or weight scalarization), the method achieves higher Hypervolume Indicator on all bigram tasks and the DNA aptamer task; for example, on 4-Bigram at n=256, the method scores 0.094 vs. 0.082 (PC-RL-TS) and 0.032 (PC-RL-WS). On the 2-Bigram task, the method exactly matches the exact greedy solution (HV = 0.630 at n=16), while on larger 4-Bigram tasks it approaches but slightly trails exact greedy (0.094 vs. 0.106 at n=256), confirming that the learned greedy policy approximates the optimal sequential construction with diminishing gaps as search space grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the approximated greedy algorithm for submodular set function maximization (Nemhauser et al., 1978; Goundan & Schulz, 2007) by replacing the generic per-step maximization algorithm with a single learned set-conditioned policy trained via REINFORCE, amortizing all n greedy subproblems into one training objective. The architecture directly adapts preference-conditioned RL policies (Xi Lin, 2022; Jain et al., 2023) by substituting the preference encoder with a deep set encoder over surrogate model predictions. Theoretically, it extends the submodularity-ratio bound of Das & Kempe (2018) to incorporate an approximation factor α, and generalizes the diversification bound of Borodin et al. (2012) to the approximated greedy setting. Conceptually, it departs from latent-space optimization (LaMBO) and individual-score sampling (AL-MOGFN) by directly optimizing the batch acquisition function on the combinatorial space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1.69× query reduction on the RFP task is practically meaningful for expensive evaluation scenarios (protein design, molecular optimization) where each query is costly. The theoretical extension to near-submodular and sum-dispersion functions broadens the applicability of greedy guarantees beyond the strict submodularity assumption that rarely holds in practice with MC-estimated acquisition functions. However, the convergence proof requires strong concavity and smoothness conditions on the expected gain that are unlikely to hold for the actual policy gradient objective, and the multi-round results are demonstrated primarily on a single task (RFP). Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large combinatorial spaces (e.g., full protein sequences of length 100+) and whether the policy training cost is amortized efficiently across many active learning rounds.
