## Problem

Bayesian Flow Networks (BFNs) iteratively refine distribution parameters rather than samples, offering a differentiable framework for both continuous and discrete data, but their exact relationship to diffusion models (DMs) remained unclear. Without an explicit SDE formulation, BFNs lacked access to the fast sampling techniques (e.g., high-order ODE solvers) that have dramatically accelerated DMs, leaving the original BFN sampler as a basic first-order discretization with poor sample quality at low NFEs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242294/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained BFN network (noise-prediction for continuous data, data-prediction for discrete data) and initializes from an isotropic Gaussian at a truncated time 1−η (with η typically 10⁻³–10⁻⁵). For continuous data (CIFAR-10, 32×32 RGB), the solver iterates over the mean parameter μ(t) and outputs a generated image. For discrete data (text8, 256-character sequences over 27 classes), the solver operates on latent variables z(t) that the original BFN formulation marginalizes out, and the final step maps z to a categorical distribution via softmax before taking argmax to produce the discrete sample.

## Method

The paper proves that BFN's noise-adding process uniquely solves a linear SDE (Theorem 4.1 for continuous data on μ(t), Theorem 5.1 for discrete data on latent z(t)), that BFN's regression losses are equivalent to denoising score matching w.r.t. the SDE variables, and that the original BFN sampler is a first-order discretization of the corresponding reverse-time SDE. Building on this, they derive the probability flow ODEs for BFNs and propose BFN-Solvers that exploit the semi-linear structure via a change-of-variable to λ(t), yielding closed-form integral solutions that are then discretized with Taylor expansions and finite differences. For continuous data, BFN-Solver++1/++2 and SDE-BFN-Solver++2 are obtained by directly plugging BFN's noise schedule α(t)=γ(t), σ²(t)=γ(t)(1−γ(t)) into the DPM-Solver++ framework; for discrete data, the solvers are derived from scratch since the SDE has no linear drift term. Empirically, BFN-Solver++2 achieves FID 55.87 at 10 NFE on CIFAR-10 (vs. 253.23 for BFN), and SDE-BFN-Solver2 reaches SA 86.82 at 50 NFE on text8 (vs. 84.88 for BFN), corresponding to a 5–20× speedup at matched quality.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (continuous data), BFN-Solver++2 achieves FID 55.87 at 10 NFE, substantially outperforming the original BFN sampler (FID 253.23 at 10 NFE) and matching BFN at 50 NFE (FID 74.81) with a 5× speedup. On text8 (discrete data), SDE-BFN-Solver2 reaches SA 86.82 at 50 NFE, surpassing BFN at 1000 NFE (SA 86.69) with a 20× speedup. At 20 NFE, BFN-Solver2 achieves SA 0.84, outperforming both D3PM (BPC 1.52 at 50 NFE) and OA-ARDM (BPC 1.52 at 20 NFE) in indirect comparison.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original BFN sampler, the proposed BFN-Solvers achieve dramatic improvements at low NFEs: on CIFAR-10, BFN-Solver++2 at 10 NFE (FID 55.87) beats BFN at 50 NFE (FID 74.81), a 5× speedup; on text8, SDE-BFN-Solver2 at 50 NFE (SA 86.82) exceeds BFN at 1000 NFE (SA 86.69), a 20× speedup. Against D3PM (Austin et al., 2023), BFN-Solver2 at 20 NFE achieves SA 0.84, which is better than D3PM's best configuration at 50 NFE (BPC 1.52), as bridged through the original BFN sampler (25 NFE, BPC 1.52, SA 0.80). Against OA-ARDM (Hoogeboom et al., 2022), BFN-Solver2 at 20 NFE (SA 0.84) outperforms OA-ARDM at 20 NFE (BPC 1.52), again via the BFN bridge. At very high NFEs (>500), SDE-based samplers slightly outperform ODE-based ones, consistent with known diffusion model behavior, but the ODE-based BFN-Solvers remain superior at the practically important low-NFE regime (≤50).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges Bayesian Flow Networks (BFNs) and score-based diffusion models (DMs) by showing that BFNs' noise-adding processes are unique solutions of linear SDEs, their regression losses are equivalent to denoising score matching, and their original sampler is a first-order discretization of the corresponding reverse-time SDE. The fast sampling methodology is directly inherited from DPM-Solver/DPM-Solver++ (Lu et al., 2022b;c), adapted to BFNs' specific semi-linear structure and noise schedule. For discrete data, the SDE operates on latent variables z that the original BFN formulation marginalizes out, a non-trivial departure from the parameter-space view of BFNs. The work positions BFNs not as a fundamentally new generative paradigm but as a special case of diffusion models with a particular noise schedule, unifying two previously separate lines of research.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical unification provides a principled framework for analyzing and improving BFNs by leveraging the extensive solver and training literature from DMs, yielding a 5–20× speedup in sampling with minimal NFEs (e.g., 10 steps). The discrete-data result is particularly impactful: BFNs on categorical data solve a linear SDE and train via DSM, making them significantly simpler and more scalable than prior discrete diffusion approaches (D3PM, OA-ARDM, simplex SDEs) that struggle with intractability or information loss. Key limitations include evaluation on small datasets (CIFAR-10, text8) with pre-trained BFN models, absence of likelihood evaluation, and the need for manual tuning of the truncation parameter η. Open questions include scaling to ImageNet-scale benchmarks, developing predictor-corrector and likelihood-evaluation methods for BFNs, and systematic comparison of noise schedules across BFN and DM formulations.
