## Problem

Axis-aligned splitting is a critical inductive bias in decision tree ensembles that significantly affects generalization on tabular data, yet its theoretical properties remain largely unexplored due to the discrete, non-differentiable nature of hard tree splits. Prior NTK analyses of soft tree ensembles assumed all input features participate in every split (oblique boundaries), making them unable to capture the axis-aligned constraint. This paper bridges that gap by deriving closed-form NTKs for axis-aligned soft tree ensembles, enabling analytical characterization of training dynamics under the axis-aligned constraint.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard tabular feature vectors (F features per sample, N samples) and produces a scalar regression prediction or, via SVM with the derived NTK, a class label. The NTK is computed in closed form at initialization from the input data and tree architecture specification, yielding an N×N kernel matrix that fully determines the training trajectory under lazy training. Both continuous and categorical features are supported through one-hot or target encoding, and the kernel formulation naturally handles feature subsets of varying orders (up to third-order interactions in experiments).

## Method

The paper formulates axis-aligned splitting in soft trees by zeroing all but one element of each split-node weight vector, then derives the limiting NTK (Theorem 3.1) for infinitely many such trees under two regimes: AAA (zero-initialized parameters remain frozen throughout training) and AAI (axis-aligned only at initialization, with free gradient updates thereafter). The key structural result (Proposition 3.1) shows the NTK of a mixed-architecture ensemble decomposes as the sum of NTKs from each sub-architecture, enabling simultaneous handling of diverse tree structures. Proposition 4.1 further proves that any axis-aligned non-oblivious tree ensemble (including non-perfect binary trees) can be converted into a set of axis-aligned oblivious tree ensembles inducing the identical limiting NTK up to a constant factor, drastically reducing the architecture search space. Empirically, Multiple Kernel Learning via EasyMKL is applied over kernels from all feature-subset tree architectures to learn optimal architecture weights, revealing that AAA produces concentrated, data-dependent feature selections while AAI yields near-uniform weights.

## Results

On the tic-tac-toe dataset (9 features, 958 instances, 4-fold cross-validation), the paper demonstrates that AAA (Always Axis-Aligned) with MKL or Optimal weights outperforms AAA (Benchmark) across all values of α ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 4.0}, while AAI (Axis-Aligned at Initialization) shows comparable performance between Optimal and Benchmark weights. Under optimal hyperparameters, the ranking is AAA > AAI > Oblique, confirming that stronger axis-aligned inductive bias yields better generalization. Notably, even AAA (Benchmark) without explicit feature selection outperforms Random Forest and GBDT (1000 trees), suggesting gradient-descent-based training is more effective than greedy search for capturing third-order feature interactions. Across 15 UCI binary classification datasets, AAA consistently produces more concentrated MKL weights (higher KL divergence from uniform) compared to AAI, indicating AAA is more sensitive to data structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three NTK-based SVM classifiers (AAA, AAI, and Oblique from Kanoh & Sugiyama 2023) against Random Forest and GBDT on the tic-tac-toe dataset. AAA with MKL or Optimal weights achieves the highest classification accuracy, outperforming both AAI and the Oblique kernel (Theorem 2.1 from Kanoh & Sugiyama 2023) across all α values, with the gap being most pronounced at higher α where the soft splitting approaches a hard step function. AAI performs intermediately, with its Optimal and Benchmark weights yielding similar accuracy, consistent with the observation that AAI's MKL weights are near-uniform. Both AAA (Benchmark) and AAI (Benchmark) outperform RF and GBDT even without feature selection, and this advantage persists even when RF/GBDT hyperparameters (max_depth, n_estimators) are tuned (Figure A.9). The key insight is that the gradient-descent training paradigm inherent to the NTK framework captures higher-order interactions (third-order in tic-tac-toe) that greedy tree-growing methods miss, rather than the axis-aligned constraint alone being the sole source of improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NTK analysis of soft tree ensembles established by Kanoh & Sugiyama (2022, 2023), which assumed oblique splitting (all features active at each node), to the axis-aligned setting where only a single feature is used per split — the standard inductive bias of practical tree ensembles like CatBoost and NODE. The soft tree formulation itself builds on Kontschieder et al. (2015), and the NTK framework on Jacot et al. (2018). The key departures are: (1) two distinct axis-aligned training regimes (AAA vs. AAI) that yield different closed-form kernels, (2) a decomposition theorem (Proposition 3.1) enabling simultaneous analysis of heterogeneous tree architectures, and (3) a sufficiency result (Proposition 4.1) showing oblivious trees span the same NTK space as arbitrary axis-aligned trees, dramatically reducing the architecture search space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first theoretical explanation for why the axis-aligned constraint — empirically shown to be critical for generalization on tabular data (Grinsztajn et al., 2022) — matters, by revealing that it breaks rotational invariance in the NTK and induces feature-specific kernel structure. The oblivious-tree sufficiency result has direct practical impact: it reduces architecture search from an exponential space to combinations of oblivious trees, making MKL-based Neural Architecture Search tractable. However, the analysis is confined to the lazy-training regime, positive definiteness is not guaranteed for all feature configurations, and the empirical validation relies heavily on small datasets (tic-tac-toe, UCI benchmarks with <1000 samples), leaving open whether these insights transfer to large-scale tabular learning. The finding that gradient descent training of axis-aligned models outperforms greedy RF/GBDT on interaction-heavy tasks suggests a broader question about when optimization method matters more than model architecture.
