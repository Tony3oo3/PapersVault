## Problem

Existing pre-training data selection methods for language models rely on simple heuristics—rule-based filters, perplexity thresholds, or domain-level proxies like Wikipedia—rather than capturing the abstract, subjective qualities (writing style, factual density, educational value, required expertise) that humans intuitively perceive. The paper asks whether LLMs can reliably discern these qualities at scale and whether the resulting signals improve downstream model capabilities when used for data selection.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes pairs of text snippets (up to 512 Llama tokens each) drawn from the 260B-token SlimPajama corpus and produces, per document and per criterion, a scalar quality rating via a fine-tuned QuRater model. These ratings are then used as unnormalized logits in a temperature-controlled softmax to sample a 30B-token training subset without replacement, or to define a curriculum ordering over the full corpus. The four criteria are evaluated independently, and the ratings are normalized to unit variance before sampling.

## Method

QuRating first elicits pairwise quality judgments from GPT-3.5-turbo on 250K text pairs per criterion (with both orderings and multiple generations to counter positional bias), then trains a Sheared-Llama-1.3B model with four linear heads using a Bradley-Terry binary cross-entropy loss to map individual documents to scalar ratings. The key insight is that LLMs produce substantially more reliable judgments in pairwise comparison (Kendall τ = 0.79) than in individual scoring (τ = 0.61), making pairwise the preferred elicitation format. For data selection, quality ratings are treated as logits in a temperature-parameterized softmax (p(d_i) ∝ exp(s_i/τ)), sampled without replacement via the Gumbel top-k trick, where τ interpolates between top-k selection (τ→0) and uniform sampling (τ→∞). The paper proves this sampling scheme is mathematically equivalent to pre-training on the full corpus followed by RLHF with quality ratings as input-independent rewards, with τ corresponding to the KL-divergence weight. A secondary use of the ratings is curriculum learning: training in reverse sampling order (low→high expertise) improves ICL performance by 0.6% over random ordering on the same data.

## Results

On 10 in-context learning tasks (5 reading comprehension, 3 commonsense reasoning, 2 world knowledge), the best QuRating method (Educational Value, τ=2.0) achieves 46.7% average ICL accuracy, improving over uniform sampling (44.9%) by 1.8% and matching a uniform baseline trained with 50% more data (46.8%). Validation perplexity improves from 8.96 (uniform) to 8.91. All four QuRating criteria with τ=2.0 outperform uniform sampling on average ICL, while top-k selection (τ=0) consistently worsens perplexity (10.5–11.5) and produces uneven task performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QuRating substantially outperforms both DSIR (Xie et al., 2023b) and perplexity filtering (Wenzek et al., 2020; Marion et al., 2023) on both perplexity and ICL. DSIR with Wikipedia degrades average ICL from 44.9% to 42.9% (−2.0%) and worsens perplexity from 8.96 to 10.67; DSIR with Book similarly drops ICL to 43.8%. Perplexity filtering (both lowest and highest) also underperforms uniform sampling, with the highest-perplexity variant dropping ICL to 41.7% (−3.2%). In contrast, the best QuRating criterion (Educational Value, τ=2.0) improves average ICL to 46.7% (+1.8%) while slightly reducing perplexity to 8.91, matching the performance of a uniform model trained with 50% more compute (46.8%). The paper notes that perplexity is a poor proxy for ICL capability: Writing Style achieves the lowest perplexity (8.90) but only a marginal ICL gain (+0.1%), while Educational Value achieves comparable perplexity with a much larger ICL improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

QuRating extends the data-selection-for-pretraining literature by replacing hand-crafted heuristics (C4/Gopher filters) and proxy-domain methods (bigram discriminators, DSIR importance resampling) with LLM-derived pairwise quality judgments formalized through the Bradley-Terry model. The closest prior work is Gunasekar et al. (2023), which filters web data using GPT-4's single-criterion educational-value judgments; QuRating generalizes this to four criteria, uses pairwise comparisons for more stable rankings, and connects the resulting exponential sampling to RLHF reward-weighted regression. The without-replacement sampling strategy is borrowed from DSIR (Xie et al., 2023b), and the QuRater training objective parallels reward-model training in RLHF (Ouyang et al., 2022) without conditioning on user input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of QuRatedPajama (260B tokens annotated across four quality dimensions) and the QuRater model provides a reusable infrastructure for data exploration and curriculum design that goes beyond a single selection experiment. The finding that educational-value-based selection at τ=2.0 matches a uniform-sampling baseline trained for 50% more steps is practically significant for resource-constrained training, and the curriculum-learning result (ordering by expertise improves ICL without changing the dataset) opens a new axis for leveraging quality signals. Key open questions include whether results transfer to 7B+ models, how to mitigate the documented social and linguistic biases in the ratings, and how to optimally combine multiple criteria rather than the ad-hoc 25%-each mix tested here.
