## Problem

Streaming algorithms for high-dimensional geometric problems such as ℓ_p subspace approximation lacked practical, near-optimal solutions: prior methods either required exponential space in k, relied on SDP-based approaches infeasible in streaming, or had exponential dependence in p. This paper provides the first deterministic single-pass streaming algorithm that constructs a strong coreset (valid for all k-dimensional subspaces simultaneously) with near-tight distortion, and extends it to general ℓ_p with only polynomial dependence in p.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes an insertion-only stream of n rows a_1, …, a_n ∈ ℝ^d one at a time, along with a rank parameter k ≤ d and (for the ℓ_p variant) a norm parameter p ≥ 1. It outputs a coreset S ⊆ [n] of size O(k log²(nκ)) storing only the selected rows, plus an approximate k-dimensional subspace V̂ computed via SVD of the coreset. For ℓ_p, each coreset point also receives a non-negative weight w_i derived from the exponential scaling. The coreset is 'strong' in that the distortion guarantee holds uniformly over all k-dimensional subspaces, not just the optimal one, and the algorithm requires only O(d·k log²(nκ)) bits of space.

## Method

The core mechanism (Algorithm 1) is a deterministic greedy selection rule driven by online rank-k ridge leverage scores: a new row a_t is added to the coreset S if its leverage score with respect to the current coreset matrix A_S (regularized by λ = ‖A_S − [A_S]_k‖²_F / k) exceeds the threshold 1/(1+1/k). This criterion guarantees distortion at most √|S| for all k-dimensional subspaces, while a bound on the sum of online ridge leverage scores (O(k log²(kκ))) caps |S| at O(k log²(nκ)), yielding overall distortion O(√k log(nκ)). For ℓ_p subspace approximation, the paper exploits the min-stability property of exponential random variables to reduce the problem to ℓ_∞: each point is scaled by an independent e_i^{-1/p} before being fed to Algorithm 1, and the resulting coreset with weights approximates the ℓ_p cost within a factor of k^{1/2+2/p} poly(log^{1+3/p}(nκ)). A fast implementation computes the SVD of the coreset only when S changes (at most O(k log²(nκ)) times) and uses a Gaussian sketch to approximate each row's leverage score in O(nnz(a_i) log n) time, giving total running time O(nnz(A) log n + d·poly(k, log nκ)).

## Results

The paper's main theoretical result is a deterministic streaming algorithm (Algorithm 1) that constructs a strong coreset of size O(k log²(nκ)) with distortion O(√k log(nκ)) for ℓ_∞ subspace approximation, where κ is the online rank-k condition number. A matching lower bound shows that any algorithm achieving distortion O(√(k/log n)) requires Ω(n) bits of space, establishing near-optimality up to polylog factors. Empirically, on a synthetic dataset (n=40,000, d=10,000, k=20), the coreset has only 28 rows with distortion ≤1.34 for the true low-rank subspace and ≤1.05 for random subspaces. On real grayscale images (1836×3264 chessboard, k=150; 4690×6000 Pinwheel Galaxy, k=200), the coreset retains ~17% of rows with distortion ≤1.09 and ≤1.12 respectively, and width estimation distortion is ≤1.005 and ≤1.03.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper without a head-to-head empirical comparison table, but it positions its guarantees against several prior methods. Compared to Tukan et al. (2022), which gives a coreset of size Õ(k^(3k)) for ℓ_∞ subspace approximation, this paper's coreset of O(k log²(nκ)) removes the exponential dependence on k, making it practical for moderate k. Compared to Feldman et al. (2010) for ℓ_1 subspace approximation, whose coreset requires Õ(d(k·2^(O(√log n))/ε²)^poly(k)) points for a (1+ε)-approximation, this paper achieves poly(k, log(nκ)) approximations using only poly(k, log(nκ)) points. For width estimation, the paper improves upon Woodruff and Yasuda (2022), which gives an O(√(d log n)) approximation, by achieving O(√k log(nκ)) multiplicative error (plus an additive Δ term) when the data is nearly low-rank, a significant improvement when k ≪ d. The lower bound (Theorem 4.1) shows the distortion is near-optimal: any algorithm with distortion O(√(k/log n)) requires Ω(n) space, while this paper achieves O(√k log(nκ)) with O(k log²(nκ)) space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the online ridge leverage score framework of Braverman et al. (FOCS 2020) and the offline ridge leverage scores of Cohen et al. (SODA 2017), repurposing them as a row-selection mechanism for constructing strong coresets in the streaming setting. It extends the high-dimensional geometric streaming framework of Woodruff and Yasuda (FOCS 2022) by showing that when data is nearly low-rank, their ℓ∞ subspace embedding can be replaced by a much smaller coreset with comparable distortion. The paper also departs from prior ℓp subspace approximation work (Deshpande et al., Feldman et al.) by being the first to use exponential random variables (via min-stability) in the subspace approximation context, removing the exponential-in-p dependence of Woodruff and Yasuda (2023) at the cost of only achieving poly(k, log nκ) approximation rather than (1+ε).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-tight lower bound (Theorem 1.2) establishes that the O(√k log nκ) distortion is essentially optimal for any sublinear-space streaming algorithm, closing a gap in understanding what is achievable. Practically, the algorithm is simple to implement and runs in O(nnz(A) log n + d·poly(k, log nκ)) time, making it viable for large-scale data where SDP-based or exponential-in-k approaches are infeasible. The main limitation is the poly(k, log nκ) approximation factor rather than (1+ε), and the condition number κ can be as large as n^{O(k)} in the worst case without structural assumptions on the data. Open questions include whether the gap between the lower bound and the algorithm's distortion can be closed, and whether the exponential random variable trick can be refined to yield better approximation factors for ℓp.
