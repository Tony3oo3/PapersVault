## Problem

Standard low-rank matrix completion theory assumes entries are observed uniformly at random, independent of their values, but real-world data often exhibits entry-dependent missingness (e.g., only positive entries are recorded). This paper tackles symmetric PSD matrix completion under ReLU sampling—where only non-negative entries are observed—and shows that, unlike the uniform-sampling case, the nonconvex objective has a non-benign global landscape with spurious critical points, yet still admits exact recovery under mild structural assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a partially observed symmetric PSD matrix M ∈ R^{n×n} where the observation set Ω = {(i,j) : m_ij ≥ 0} is determined deterministically by the entry values (ReLU sampling), along with the known rank r. The matrix is modeled as M = U*U*^T + Δ, where U* ∈ R^{n×r} is the planted factor and Δ is optional noise. The output is the completed matrix M* = U*U*^T, recovered via the low-rank factor U ∈ R^{n×r} obtained by optimizing the nonconvex factorization objective.

## Method

The paper formulates MC as min_U (1/4)||UU^T − M||_Ω^2 and proves that while the global landscape contains spurious local minima (refuting the benign-landscape conjecture from uniform sampling), the objective is geodesically strongly convex on the quotient manifold R^{n×r}/O^r in a neighborhood of the planted solution [U*]. This local convexity is established under two key assumptions: (i) rows of U* partitioned into 2^r orthant blocks are each full-rank, and (ii) adjacent orthant blocks share enough observed entries to span R^{r×r}. To exploit this local landscape, the authors design a tailored SVD initialization that imputes missing entries as −|q_ij| (where Q = YY^T from a random Gaussian Y) before applying truncated SVD, placing GD in the strongly convex basin. The framework extends to noisy settings and general threshold-based deterministic sampling via a connectivity assumption on fully-observed diagonal blocks, with all assumptions verified to hold with high probability for i.i.d. Gaussian U* when r ≤ O(log n).

## Results

In the noiseless ReLU sampling setting (n=200, r=5), GD with the paper's tailored initialization achieves a completion error of (7.6±0.7)·10⁻¹¹ and gradient norm of (9.7±0.16)·10⁻⁷, effectively recovering the ground truth, while random-imputation (RI) and random spectral (RS) initializations stall at completion errors of ~0.5 and ~0.45 respectively. In the noisy case (σ=10⁻⁴), the tailored initialization converges to an O(σ)-neighborhood of the ground truth (completion error (4.4±0.13)·10⁻⁵), whereas RI and RS initializations remain far from optimal (completion error ~0.51). In the larger-scale comparison (n=1000, r=20), the proposed GD method achieves completion error comparable to all five baselines while being substantially faster than ADMM and GNMR, and as fast as or slightly faster than PAM, ScaledGD, and MPAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its GD method with five baselines on MC with ReLU sampling (n=1000, r=20): ADMM (convex nuclear-norm formulation), ScaledGD, PAM (Saul 2022 formulation), MPAM (Seraghiti et al. 2023), and GNMR. In terms of recovery performance, all methods achieve comparable completion error in both noiseless and noisy (σ=10⁻²) settings, meaning the proposed method neither clearly wins nor loses on accuracy. On computational efficiency, the proposed GD method is substantially faster than ADMM and GNMR, as fast as MPAM, and slightly faster than PAM and ScaledGD. The key differentiator is the tailored initialization: without it (using random or random-imputation spectral initialization), GD converges to spurious critical points with completion error ~0.5, while with the proposed ReLU-aware initialization it converges to the global optimum. The paper notes that direct nuclear-norm minimization (a common convex baseline) does not work well under ReLU sampling, consistent with prior empirical findings by Naik et al. (2022).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the nonconvex matrix completion landscape analysis (Ge et al. 2016; Sun & Luo 2016) from the standard uniform-sampling regime to deterministic entry-dependent (ReLU) sampling, where only non-negative entries are observed. It builds on Bishop & Yu (2014) for deterministic PSD matrix completion and on empirical ReLU-sampling studies (Naik et al. 2022; Saul 2022; Seraghiti et al. 2023), but departs from them by providing the first theoretical optimization-landscape characterization (geodesic strong convexity on a quotient manifold) for this setting. The key conceptual departure is showing that, unlike uniform sampling where the global landscape is benign, ReLU sampling produces spurious local minima, necessitating a tailored SVD-based initialization rather than random initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a genuine gap: entry-dependent missingness is pervasive in practice (surveys, sensor saturation, rating skew), yet prior theory either assumed uniform sampling or gave impractical guarantees. The geodesic strong convexity result provides the first landscape guarantee for deterministic entry-dependent MC, and the tailored initialization makes GD practically viable. Key open questions include generalizing to rectangular matrices and unknown rank, weakening the connectivity assumption (3.6d), and closing the large gap between the theoretical rank bound (r ≤ 0.5 log n) and the empirically observed limit (r ≈ 45 for n = 200). Extending the analysis to broader entry-dependent sampling regimes beyond ReLU and thresholding is identified as a natural next step.
