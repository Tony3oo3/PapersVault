## Problem

Current multimodal survival prediction methods tokenize whole-slide images into >10⁴ patches and transcriptomics into gene groups, then fuse them with Transformers, incurring prohibitive memory costs for attention and making post-hoc interpretability intractable. The small cohort sizes typical of cancer genomics (a few hundred patients) compound this into a Large-p, Small-n problem, and the thousands of interacting tokens obscure which morphological or molecular features drive patient-level predictions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes gigapixel WSIs tessellated into 256×256-pixel patches at 20× magnification (0.5 μm/pixel, typically >10⁴ patches per slide) encoded by a pretrained UNI (DINOv2-based ViT-L) patch encoder, alongside log-2 TPM bulk RNA-seq profiles (~3×10⁴ genes) organized into 50 MSigDB Hallmark pathways. The WSI is compressed to 16–32 morphological prototype tokens (≥300× reduction) and transcriptomics to 50 pathway tokens (≥20× reduction) before fusion. Outputs include a patient-level Cox risk score, per-prototype spatial heatmaps on the WSI, and bi-directional cross-modal attention maps linking specific morphologies to specific pathways.

## Method

MMP constructs an unsupervised, compact multimodal representation by (1) fitting a Gaussian Mixture Model (initialized via K-means on training-set patch embeddings) to each WSI's patch embeddings, where the GMM parameters (mixture weights, means, diagonal covariances) per component form a fixed-length slide summary of 16–32 morphological prototypes, and (2) projecting gene expression onto 50 binary Hallmark pathway membership vectors to form pathway prototypes. These two small token sets are then fused via either a full Transformer (joint self- and cross-attention) or an entropic-regularized Optimal Transport cross-alignment (Sinkhorn), both operating on the condensed token set without approximations. The paper proves (Lemma 3.1) that the OT transport plan is equivalent to the Transformer cross-attention matrix up to a multiplicative factor under unbalanced entropic-regularized OT with negative dot-product cost, unifying the two fusion strategies. Prototype-specific learnable encodings and per-prototype feedforward networks exploit the fact that prototype identities are consistent across patients, and the reduced token count enables batch-level Cox proportional hazards training (batch size 64) rather than the single-patient NLL loss required by non-prototype baselines.

## Results

On six TCGA cancer cohorts, MMP (both Transformer and OT variants) achieves an average C-Index of 0.665, outperforming the next-best multimodal baseline MOTCat (0.631) by +5.4% and the best unimodal model by +7.8%. MMP ranks in the top-2 for 5 out of 6 cancer types, with particularly strong gains on BRCA (0.753) and KIRC (0.748). The framework also achieves at least 5× fewer GFLOPs per patient compared to cross-attention baselines (0.334 vs. 2.10 GFLOPs on LUAD), and MMP significantly stratifies high- vs. low-risk groups (log-rank p < 0.05) for all six cancer types, whereas MOTCat is significant for only three.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMP (both OT and Transformer variants, avg C-Index 0.665) outperforms all four multimodal baselines: MOTCat (0.631, +5.4%), CMTA (0.623, +7.1%), SurvPath (0.629, +5.7%), and MCAT (0.610, +9.2%). Among unimodal histology baselines, MMP's unimodal variant (0.611) beats ABMIL (0.599), TransMIL (0.575), AttnMISL (0.605), IB-MIL (0.569), and ILRA (0.589), confirming the value of GMM-based morphological prototyping even without transcriptomics. MMP_OT and MMP_Trans are essentially tied (both 0.665), empirically confirming the theoretical equivalence between OT cross-alignment and Transformer cross-attention shown in Lemma 3.1. Notably, on CRC, unimodal histology baselines (e.g., AttnMISL at 0.728) outperform all multimodal methods, suggesting residual challenges in multimodal training dynamics for that cancer type. MMP also achieves ≥5× fewer GFLOPs than cross-attention baselines (0.334 vs. 2.10 on LUAD; 0.864 vs. 5.49 on KIRC), making it the most computationally efficient method in the comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

MMP extends PANTHER (Song et al., 2024a), which used morphological prototypes for unimodal WSI summarization, into a multimodal setting by pairing GMM-based histology prototypes with fixed biological pathway prototypes from MSigDB Hallmark gene sets. The GMM aggregation mechanism builds on differentiable EM for set representation (Kim, 2022), while the pathway tokenization follows SurvPath (Jaume et al., 2024). A key conceptual departure is the formal proof that Transformer cross-attention and entropic-regularized OT cross-alignment are equivalent up to a multiplicative factor, unifying two previously treated-as-distinct fusion paradigms under a single framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By compressing >10⁴ histology patches into 16–32 prototype tokens, MMP enables exact (non-approximate) multimodal Transformer fusion at 5× lower FLOPs, and unlocks bidirectional interpretability (histology→pathway and pathway→histology) that prior methods like MCAT and MOTCat cannot provide due to their unidirectional cross-attention. The theoretical unification of Transformer and OT fusion offers a principled platform for future multimodal architecture design. Open questions include whether the fixed prototype count generalizes across cancer types, whether the approach holds on external cohorts beyond TCGA, and whether single-cell foundation models can replace the shallow MLP used for transcriptomics.
