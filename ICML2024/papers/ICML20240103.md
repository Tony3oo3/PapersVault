## Problem

Reconstruction-based representation learning (autoencoders, masked autoencoders) produces visually compelling reconstructions yet yields latent features that are suboptimal for downstream perception tasks, requiring long training schedules and fine-tuning to compete with self-supervised methods. This paper identifies and theoretically explains three root causes of this failure: (R1) the subspace explaining most pixel variance is least informative for classification, (R2) perception-relevant features are learned last due to spectral bias, and (R3) the same reconstruction error can correspond to drastically different perception performance, making the problem ill-posed.

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on a data matrix X ∈ R^{D×N} (flattened images) and a target matrix Y ∈ R^{C×N} (class labels), producing closed-form optimal encoder V*, decoder Z*, and classifier W* parameters, plus a continuous alignment metric in [0,1] that measures the fraction of Y^T Y energy captured by the top-k eigenspace of X^T X. The metric is computed via a single SVD of X and a cumulative sum, making it O(DN·min(D,N)) rather than requiring a D×D eigendecomposition. Empirically, the framework is validated on ResNet-9 and ResNet-34 autoencoders over CIFAR-10/100, Tiny-ImageNet, ImageNet-10, and ArabicDigits, with images projected onto top or bottom subspaces of the data covariance before training.

## Method

The paper derives a closed-form solution (Theorem 1) for the joint reconstruction-plus-classification loss, showing the optimal encoder spans a generalized eigenspace that continuously interpolates between OLS (λ→0) and PCA (λ→∞). A key result (Theorem 2) proves this linear-regime analysis is valid for arbitrary high-capacity nonlinear encoders at initialization and throughout training when the decoder is under-parametrized, grounding the theory in practical deep network settings. The paper then extends the framework to denoising autoencoders (Theorem 3), obtaining a closed-form alignment metric under arbitrary noise distributions, and proves that masking (as in MAE) is beneficial for alignment while additive Gaussian noise is provably inert (Corollary 3.1) because it preserves the eigenvalue ordering of the data covariance. Empirically, projecting Tiny-ImageNet onto the top 90% variance subspace yields only 45% top-1 accuracy versus 55% from the bottom 20% subspace, and two ResNet-34 autoencoders with identical reconstruction loss can differ by up to 40% in classification accuracy.

## Results

On TinyImageNet, a ResNet-9 trained and evaluated on images projected onto the top subspace explaining 90% of pixel variance achieves only 45% top-1 accuracy, while the bottom subspace explaining just 20% of pixel variance reaches 55% top-1 accuracy, demonstrating that reconstruction-optimal features are anti-correlated with perception. On ImageNet-10, two ResNet-34 autoencoders with identical train and test reconstruction loss can exhibit top-1 accuracy ranging from 50% to nearly 90%, confirming the ill-posedness of reconstruction as a proxy for perception. The paper further shows that MAE's masking strategy improves ImageNet top-1 accuracy from ~50% to 74%, while proving that additive Gaussian noise provides zero benefit regardless of the downstream task (Corollary 3.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an analysis paper without a traditional method-vs-baseline comparison; instead, it contrasts internal settings to isolate the misalignment. The primary empirical contrast is between top-subspace (reconstruction-optimal) and bottom-subspace (perception-relevant) projections: across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, TinyImageNet, and ArabicDigits, the bottom subspace consistently yields higher classification accuracy, with the gap widening as background complexity and class count increase. A second contrast pairs ResNet-34 autoencoders trained with reconstruction-only loss versus reconstruction-plus-supervised-loss: the paired models achieve identical reconstruction error but differ by up to 40 percentage points in top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-10. Finally, the paper contrasts denoising strategies, showing that random masking (as in MAE) provably increases the alignment metric between reconstruction and classification, while additive isotropic Gaussian noise is provably invariant to the supervised task for all noise levels σ ≥ 0.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the denoising autoencoder framework (Vincent et al., 2010) and the PCA solution for linear autoencoders (Wold et al., 1987), extending them with a closed-form alignment metric that quantifies how well reconstruction features serve downstream perception tasks. It provides the first theoretical justification for observations previously noted empirically by Zamir et al. (2018, Taskonomy) and Locatello et al. (2019) that reconstruction-based representations are misaligned with supervised learning. The analysis connects to the spectral bias literature (Rahaman et al., 2019; Chakrabarty & Maji, 2019) to explain why perception-relevant features are learned last, and directly addresses the MAE framework (He et al., 2022) by proving why its masking strategy is beneficial while other noise types are not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper provides the first principled explanation for why reconstruction-based methods require long training schedules and fine-tuning to compete with SSL, a gap that was 'common knowledge' but lacked theoretical grounding. The provable ineffectiveness of additive Gaussian noise (Corollary 3.1) and the demonstrated benefit of masking give practitioners concrete guidance for designing denoising strategies, opening a new research direction toward task-aware noise distributions. Key limitations include the reliance on linear-regime analysis (though Theorem 2 argues transferability to nonlinear settings) and experiments on relatively small datasets (Tiny-ImageNet, CIFAR). The open question of how to select optimal noise strategies without knowledge of the downstream task remains unresolved, though the paper provides a detection mechanism for provably useless strategies.
