## Problem

Cross-modal fine-tuning—adapting a pretrained vision or language model to an entirely different modality such as protein sequences, PDE fields, or cosmic ray data—faces two compounding challenges: a large modality gap (different feature spaces, label spaces, and distributions) and severe data scarcity in the target modality. The prior state-of-the-art, ORCA, uses a two-stage align-then-fine-tune procedure that reduces but does not eliminate the modality gap, leading to training instability (prone to unfavorable local optima) and suboptimal transfer performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two streams of data per training step: target modality samples of arbitrary input dimension (2D images, 1D sequences, PDE solution fields, or tabular rows) and source modality samples (natural images from CIFAR-10/Tiny-ImageNet for 2D tasks, or text from CoNLL-2003 for 1D tasks). Both are projected into a shared N×D embedding space via modality-specific embedders before patch-level mixing. The output is task-dependent: classification logits over K classes, dense prediction maps matching the input spatial resolution, or per-index regression values, with a single unified transformer backbone (Swin-Transformer for 2D, RoBERTa for 1D) producing all of these.

## Method

PaRe is an end-to-end cross-modal fine-tuning framework built around a modality-agnostic Patch Replacement mechanism. A lightweight gate network (single FC layer + sigmoid) scores every patch in both the source and target embeddings; the top-k highest-scoring source patches are then used to replace the bottom-k lowest-scoring target patches, with Gumbel-Softmax ensuring differentiable selection. The replacement count k decreases linearly from an initial value (e.g., 3000) to 0 across training epochs, implementing a curriculum that transitions the intermediate modality from source-like (easy, high transferability) to target-like (hard, task-specific). The training loss combines a pure target loss with a mixed loss L_mix = (1−λ)L_tar + λL_src where λ = k/N, so the source supervision weight naturally fades as the intermediate modality approaches the target. This single-stage procedure replaces ORCA's two-stage OTDD alignment + full fine-tuning, yielding a smoother loss landscape and consistent gains across 48 datasets spanning 10+ modalities.

## Results

On NAS-Bench-360 (10 diverse tasks spanning 2D classification, 2D dense prediction, and 1D tasks), PaRe achieves the best result on all 10 tasks, e.g., 6.25% error on CIFAR-100, 25.55% on Spherical, 0.99 MAE on PSICOV, and 0.121 AUROC on Cosmic. On PDEBench (8 PDE tasks), PaRe surpasses U-Net and PINN on all tasks and outperforms ORCA in 6/8 tasks (e.g., 2.70E-03 nRMSE on Advection 1D vs. ORCA's 9.80E-03). On OpenML-CC18 (30 tabular datasets), PaRe achieves the highest average AUROC of 0.9030, winning on 15/30 datasets, compared to ORCA (0.8946) and XGBoost (0.8909).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaRe outperforms ORCA (the primary cross-modal fine-tuning baseline) on 9 out of 10 NAS-Bench-360 tasks, with the largest gains on PSICOV (0.99 vs. 1.91 MAE, a 48% reduction) and Spherical (25.55% vs. 29.85% error). Against task-specific hand-designed models and the AutoML method DASH, PaRe achieves superior results on most tasks, notably on Spherical (25.55% vs. 67.41% and 71.28%) and PSICOV (0.99 vs. 3.35 and 3.30). The general-purpose Perceiver IO is substantially outperformed across all 10 tasks. On PDEBench, PaRe beats PINN and U-Net on all 8 tasks and matches or slightly trails FNO (e.g., 2.70E-03 vs. 1.10E-02 on Advection 1D, but 8.06E-02 vs. 2.20E-01 on Darcy-Flow 2D). On OpenML-CC18, PaRe's average AUROC of 0.9030 exceeds all tabular baselines including XGBoost (0.8909), AutoGluon (0.8947), and TabPFN (0.8943), winning on 15/30 datasets versus 7/30 for ORCA and AutoGluon.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ORCA (Shen et al., 2023), which first established cross-modal fine-tuning via a two-stage pipeline (OTDD-based alignment followed by full fine-tuning). PaRe departs from this by replacing the two-stage design with an end-to-end approach that progressively constructs intermediate modalities through modality-agnostic patch replacement in a shared embedding space. The method draws conceptual inspiration from Mixup/CutMix (adapted to the cross-modal, semantically unpaired setting), curriculum learning (the gradual k-decrease schedules easy-to-hard intermediate modalities), and domain adaptation (intermediate domain construction to bridge gaps), while distinguishing itself from VLMixer by handling modalities with no semantic correlation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PaRe offers a practical, modality-agnostic recipe for transferring vision or language pretrained models to highly specialized domains (PDEs, protein sequences, cosmic rays, tabular data) where labeled data is scarce and the modality gap is large. The end-to-end design avoids the computationally expensive OTDD computation required by ORCA, improving scalability. Key open questions remain: how to automatically select the best proxy source dataset for a given target modality, whether a truly modality-agnostic data augmentation strategy can complement the patch replacement, and how to leverage unlabeled target-modality data to further alleviate data scarcity.
