## Problem

Existing raw image compression methods (e.g., RIC) directly encode noisy raw images, wasting bits on physically unavoidable sensor noise that degrades both bitrate efficiency and reconstruction quality. While joint denoising-and-compression strategies have been proposed for RGB images, they require clean paired data for training, which is impractical for raw images due to camera-specific noise distributions and the difficulty of capturing clean references. No prior method achieves self-supervised raw image compression that explicitly separates and discards noise from the bitstream.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy Bayer CFA raw image (4-channel RGGB, cropped to 512×512 patches) along with pre-calibrated camera noise model parameters (system gain k, read noise σ_read, per-row noise σ_row, and fixed pattern noise n_fp) as input. It produces a compressed bitstream via a hyperprior VAE encoder and a reconstructed clean raw image at the decoder. A single model handles a continuous range of variable rates (0.0018–0.18 λ_D) using the QARV quantization-error-aware framework, eliminating the need for multiple rate-specific models.

## Method

The core insight is that real sensor noise cannot survive in the low-dimensional latent space of a rate-constrained VAE, so the encoder naturally disentangles noise from signal without clean references. A U-Net noise extractor F_n predicts the noise component, which is then regularized by a chain of bijective transformations (inspired by normalizing flows) that map the complex multi-component noise distribution—fixed pattern, row, and heteroscedastic Gaussian (shot+read)—into a standard Gaussian latent space where a negative log-likelihood loss can be computed. Fixed pattern noise is subtracted before the extractor, row noise is removed via per-row mean subtraction, and the remaining heteroscedastic Gaussian noise is standardized by the estimated variance σ_hg² = σ_read² + x̂·k. The total training loss combines the hyperprior rate term, an MSE distortion term between the reconstruction and the input minus predicted noise, and a noise divergence term (NLL + covariance loss for spatial independence), all optimized self-supervisedly without any clean image supervision.

## Results

On the SID dataset, the proposed method achieves 41.17 dB PSNR at 0.030 bpp, surpassing RIC (41.03 dB at 1.606 bpp) by more than 8 dB in PSNR while using ~54× fewer bits. On the FDRIC dataset, it reaches 49.14 dB at 0.144 bpp versus RIC's 44.89 dB at 0.200 bpp, a 4.25 dB gain at lower bitrate. Overall, the paper reports improvements of +2 to +10 dB and bit savings of 2 to 50× across all evaluated SNR ranges and camera models. In the supervised comparison (Table 3), the self-supervised method achieves 8.35% lower BD-rate than SID+RIC and 59.50% lower than ELD+RIC on the SID dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary one-stage baseline RIC, the proposed method achieves a ~54× bitrate reduction (0.030 vs. 1.606 bpp) with a +0.14 dB PSNR gain on SID, and a 4.25 dB PSNR gain at lower bitrate on FDRIC. Among two-stage denoiser+compressor baselines, the method outperforms BM3D+RIC by +1.09 dB at 4.5× lower bpp on SID and +4.28 dB at 1.28× lower bpp on FDRIC; it beats Ne2Ne+RIC by +0.68 dB at 2.8× lower bpp on SID and +4.26 dB at 3.1× lower bpp on FDRIC; and it surpasses LGBPN+RIC by a large margin (+7.25 dB on SID, +10.58 dB on FDRIC). Against the supervised baselines (Table 3), the self-supervised method achieves 8.35% lower BD-rate than SID+RIC and 59.50% lower than ELD+RIC, demonstrating that paired clean images are not necessary. In the QARV compressor ablation (Fig. 7), the framework generalizes beyond the VAE backbone, consistently outperforming QARV and all two-stage baselines. In terms of efficiency (Table 4), the method is 500× faster than BM3D+RIC (0.567 s vs. 290.6 s latency) and uses 64% fewer FLOPs than Ne2Ne+RIC while achieving 317.69% BD-rate improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads: learned VAE-based image compression (Ballé et al. 2018; RIC/Li et al. 2022a), physics-based raw image noise modeling (ELD/Wei et al. 2020; PMN/Feng et al. 2022), and joint denoising-compression optimization (Cheng et al. 2022). The key departure from Cheng et al.'s supervised joint framework is replacing the clean-image reconstruction constraint with a self-supervised noise regularization loss, where a bijective normalizing-flow-style transformation (inspired by Rezende & Mohamed 2015) maps the predicted noise into a known Gaussian latent space. The U-Net noise extractor follows ELD's architecture, and the compression backbone reuses RIC's VAE with a scale hyperprior, positioning the contribution as a loss-function and training-paradigm innovation rather than an architectural one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for raw image workflows: eliminating noise from the bitstream yields 2–50× bitrate savings and +2 to +10 dB PSNR gains, while the self-supervised formulation removes the need for expensive clean paired captures that are impractical to collect at scale across camera models. The FDRIC dataset (549 training images, 0.1–10⁵ lux) fills a clear gap, as prior raw denoising datasets (SID, ELD) cover only low-light conditions and cannot train compressors for daytime SNR. Open questions include generalization to cameras with uncalibrated or unknown noise parameters, whether the approach extends to video raw streams, and how sensitive performance is to miscalibrated noise model coefficients in the field.
