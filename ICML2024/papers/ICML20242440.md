## Problem

Representation learning research has focused almost exclusively on classification, overlooking the fundamentally different topology of regression representations: classification yields disconnected clusters while regression requires continuous, ordinal feature spaces. The paper asks what topology a regression feature space should have for effectiveness, and how that topology connects to the Information Bottleneck principle, which had previously been formalized only for classification.

## Input / Output

The regularizer operates on the last hidden layer's feature representations (z ∈ R^d) and their corresponding target labels (y) within a training batch, computing persistent homology on subsets of size n_m from both spaces. For synthetic coordinate prediction, input is a 100-dimensional vector (4 signal dimensions + 96 noise dimensions) and output is a 3D coordinate. For real-world tasks, inputs are RGB images (NYU-v2 depth maps, DIV2K super-resolution patches, AgeDB face crops) and outputs are per-pixel depth values, high-resolution images, or scalar age estimates respectively.

## Method

PH-Reg is a two-term regularizer added to the standard regression loss. The intrinsic dimension term L_d estimates the feature space ID via the slope of log E(Z_n) vs. log n (Birdal's persistent homology estimator) but normalizes by the target space's E(Y_n), so the loss is minimized when feature and target IDs match rather than simply driving ID to zero. The topology term L_t (from the Topology Autoencoder) preserves 0-dimensional topologically relevant distances between feature and target spaces by matching edge lengths in their respective minimum spanning trees. The theoretical justification comes from three results: Theorem 1 reduces the IB tradeoff to minimizing H(Y|Z) + H(Z|Y); Theorem 2 shows H(Z|Y) upper-bounds the generalization error; and Theorem 3 bounds H(Z|Y) by the intrinsic dimension of the feature manifold, while Proposition 2 shows the optimal representation is homeomorphic to the target space.

## Results

On the synthetic coordinate prediction task, PH-Reg (Ld+Lt) reduces MSE by over 90% on Circle (0.013 vs. 0.154 baseline) and Swiss Roll (0.61 vs. 2.99), and by ~77% on Mammoth (49 vs. 211). On real-world depth estimation (NYU-Depth-v2), PH-Reg improves δ1 from 0.792 to 0.807, reduces REL from 0.153 to 0.144, and lowers RMS from 0.512 to 0.481. On age estimation (AgeDB), the combined regularizer achieves 7.32 MAE (ALL) and 12.18 (Few), outperforming the baseline by 0.48 and 1.45 respectively. On super-resolution, PH-Reg gains 0.096 dB PSNR on Urban100 (26.179 vs. 26.083).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PH-Reg (Ld+Lt) outperforms all four baselines across every synthetic and real-world task. Against Information Dropout (InfDrop), PH-Reg is substantially better: on Swiss Roll, InfDrop actually worsens MSE (4.15 vs. 2.99 baseline), while PH-Reg achieves 0.61. Against Ordinal Entropy (OE), PH-Reg wins on all four synthetic shapes and all three real-world tasks (e.g., 7.32 vs. 7.65 MAE on AgeDB ALL). Against Birdal's regularizer (Ld′), which naively minimizes intrinsic dimension without considering the target space, PH-Reg's Ld term that normalizes by the target's persistent homology yields large gains (e.g., 0.61 vs. 2.74 on Swiss Roll, 49 vs. 141 on Mammoth), confirming that over-compressing below the target's ID is harmful. Against the Topology Autoencoder alone (Lt), PH-Reg's addition of the ID-matching term Ld provides further improvements on Swiss Roll (0.61 vs. 1.83) and Torus (0.61 vs. 0.95), while Lt alone already captures much of the topological benefit on Circle (0.036) and Mammoth (80). Overall, the combination of both terms is consistently superior, validating the paper's two theoretical connections independently.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the information bottleneck framework from classification to regression, establishing that the IB tradeoff can be expressed purely in terms of H(Z|Y) and H(Y|Z) for regression. It builds on Birdal et al.'s (2021) persistent homology-based intrinsic dimension estimation and Moor et al.'s (2020) topological autoencoder, but departs from both by incorporating the target space's topology and intrinsic dimension as constraints rather than purely minimizing feature-space complexity. The homeomorphism result (Proposition 2) extends Achille & Soatto's (2018) optimal representation definition from a classification context to a topological characterization specific to regression, where the target space is a continuous metric space rather than a discrete set of classes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical justification for why lower intrinsic dimension improves generalization in regression, connecting it through conditional entropy to the IB principle—a gap that prior work (Ansuini et al., 2019; Brown et al., 2022b) left unexplained. However, the practical impact is limited: real-world improvements are modest (0.48 MAE on age estimation, 0.096 dB PSNR on super-resolution), the regularizer adds O(n_m² log n_m) training cost, and the theoretical bounds rely on strong assumptions (Lipschitz continuity, specific distributional forms for Q). Open questions include whether the 0-dimensional topological features suffice for complex target geometries, whether the ID-matching requirement generalizes to high-dimensional target spaces, and whether the approach scales to tasks where the target space topology is non-trivial (e.g., multi-modal depth distributions).
