## Problem

All existing reinforcement learning methods for Parameterized Action Markov Decision Processes (PAMDPs) are model-free, despite model-based RL demonstrating superior sample efficiency in standard discrete and continuous action spaces. No prior work has successfully applied model-based RL to the PAMDP setting, and no theoretical performance guarantees existed for PAMDP RL algorithms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242432/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a state vector observation and outputs a parameterized action consisting of a discrete action k and its corresponding continuous parameter z_k. During planning, the dynamics model is conditioned on the full parameterized action (s_t, k_t, z_{k_t}), and the PAMDP-specific MPPI maintains a separate multivariate Gaussian distribution over continuous parameters for each discrete action, updated iteratively over E planning iterations. Only the first action from the selected trajectory is executed (receding-horizon MPC), and the dynamics model is trained with stochastic Gaussian outputs using the reparameterization trick.

## Method

DLPA learns a parameterized-action-conditioned dynamics model (transition, reward, and continuation predictors) trained with an H-step prediction loss that feeds only the initial state and a sequence of parameterized actions, forcing the model to predict the entire trajectory and enabling gradients to flow back through time. Three inference architectures are proposed for handling the entangled discrete-continuous action structure, with a sequential decoupling approach (predict a latent embedding from state+discrete action, then refine with continuous parameters) performing best. Planning uses a PAMDP-specific MPPI that maintains separate continuous-parameter distributions per discrete action and updates them via return-weighted reweighting over E iterations, preserving the dependency between discrete and continuous components. Two separate reward predictors conditioned on the termination flag improve performance in harder domains. The paper also provides the first theoretical regret bound for PAMDP RL via Lipschitz continuity, quantifying how discrete action mismatches, continuous parameter distribution errors, and model estimation errors compound over the planning horizon.

## Results

On 8 standard PAMDP benchmarks, DLPA achieves the best asymptotic cumulative return on 6 out of 8 tasks (Hard Goal: 28.38, Catch Point: 7.56, Hard Move 4/6/8/10: 6.29/8.48/7.80/6.35), while trailing HyAR on Platform (0.92 vs. 0.98) and Goal (28.75 vs. 34.23). The performance gap over HyAR widens dramatically as the parameterized action space grows: on Hard Move(10) with 2^10 discrete actions, DLPA scores 6.35 while HyAR drops to −7.05. DLPA also achieves approximately 30× higher sample efficiency than the best model-free baseline across all scenarios, and extends its advantage to the more complex Half-Field-Offense (HFO) domain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DLPA outperforms all five model-free PAMDP baselines (HyAR, HPPO, P-DQN, PATD3) on 6 of 8 benchmarks in asymptotic return, with the largest margins on the Hard Move family where the action space is 2^n. Against HyAR (the strongest baseline, which learns a latent action embedding), DLPA wins on Hard Goal (+1.97), Catch Point (+2.36), and all four Hard Move variants, with the gap growing from +0.20 at n=4 to +13.40 at n=10; HyAR retains an edge on Platform (+0.06) and Goal (+5.48). HPPO and P-DQN fail catastrophically on Hard Move (scores below −15 for n≥6), while PATD3 is consistently the weakest baseline. On the additional HFO domain (10× longer horizon, larger state/action spaces), DLPA again surpasses all model-free baselines. In terms of sample efficiency, DLPA converges in roughly 6,000–23,000 environment steps depending on action-space size, compared to orders of magnitude more for the model-free methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to apply model-based reinforcement learning to Parameterized Action MDPs (PAMDPs), a setting where all prior work (PADDPG, HPPO, P-DQN, HyAR) has been exclusively model-free. The planning component adapts Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI, Williams et al. 2015) by maintaining separate continuous-parameter distributions conditioned on each discrete action, preserving the entangled structure of parameterized actions. The dynamics learning draws on multi-step prediction losses from model-based RL (PlaNet, TD-MPC) but introduces PAMDP-specific inference architectures and separate reward predictors conditioned on termination. The theoretical analysis extends Lipschitz continuity performance bounds from standard MDPs (Asadi et al. 2018) to the PAMDP setting via Wasserstein metric decomposition over discrete and continuous action components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DLPA fills a clear gap by bringing the sample-efficiency advantages of model-based RL (well-established in continuous/discrete-only MDPs) to the PAMDP setting, achieving ~30× better sample efficiency than the best model-free PAMDP baselines. The finding that DLPA succeeds in Hard Move (n≥6) without learning an action embedding space—where HyAR's original paper showed no standard PAMDP algorithm could—challenges the assumption that latent action compression is necessary for large parameterized action spaces. Limitations include evaluation primarily on low-dimensional 2D control benchmarks (though HFO adds some complexity), and the method's reliance on accurate learned dynamics may limit applicability to highly stochastic or partially observable real-world domains. Open questions include scaling to higher-dimensional continuous parameter spaces and integration with representation learning for high-dimensional observations.
