## Problem

Existing fairness guarantees in federated learning, such as core-stability from CoreFed, require concave agent utility functions, rendering them inapplicable to practical deep neural networks. The paper proves that even approximate proportionality (guaranteeing any fraction of maximum utility) is infeasible for non-concave utilities, motivating a shift from cardinal utility-based to ordinal rank-based fairness guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes n agents' local data (e.g., rotated MNIST or CIFAR-10 images) and a feasible model set P (e.g., the parameter space of a CNN or VGG11), where agent utilities are defined as 1 minus cross-entropy loss on local test data. It outputs a single model θ* that is PVC-stable, meaning no coalition of agents can unanimously prefer more than a (1−|T|/n) fraction of models over θ*, which implies each individual agent ranks θ* in the top (1−1/n) fraction of all feasible models. The only assumption required is Lebesgue-measurability of utility functions, which holds for any piecewise-continuous function including standard deep networks.

## Method

The key insight is replacing cardinal utility comparisons with ordinal ranks: a model θ has rank r for agent i if i weakly prefers θ to an r-fraction of all models. The paper adapts the proportional veto core (PVC) from social choice theory to this rank-based setting, proving PVC-stable models always exist under merely Lebesgue-measurable utilities (Theorem 1). The practical algorithm Rank-Core-Fed operates in two phases: first, it runs FedAvg to obtain an initial model θ₀, then each agent samples J models along its utility gradient direction at θ₀ to form a finite representative set P; second, it performs iterative elimination where each agent in turn removes its least preferred |P|/n−1 models from P, and the remaining model with highest utilitarian social welfare is selected. This finite-set procedure provably yields a model in the (n/m)-PVC of the representative set, where m=|P|, and the approximation error vanishes as m grows.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (100 agents), Rank-Core-Fed achieves the highest rankwise proportionality (β) across all heterogeneity levels: 0.85 (I_high), 0.91 (I_mid), and 0.95 (I_low), compared to 0.79–0.88 for FedAvg, 0.81–0.89 for CoreFed, and 0.80–0.91 for q-FFL. On MNIST (10 agents), the gains are smaller but consistent, with β ranging from 0.89 to 0.97. Utilitarian social welfare (sum of agent utilities) remains comparable across all methods, with Rank-Core-Fed matching or slightly exceeding baselines (e.g., 37.41 vs. 37.29–37.34 on CIFAR-10 I_low).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Rank-Core-Fed outperforms all five baselines on the fairness metric (β) in every setting. Against FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017), the gap is most pronounced on CIFAR-10 I_high, where Rank-Core-Fed achieves β=0.85 versus 0.79 for FedAvg (a 6-point improvement). Against CoreFed (Chaudhury et al., 2022), the closest prior method with core-stability guarantees, Rank-Core-Fed still leads by 2–4 points on CIFAR-10 (e.g., 0.95 vs. 0.89 on I_low). Against q-FFL (Li et al., 2020), the advantage is 2–5 points on CIFAR-10. In the appendix comparisons, Rank-Core-Fed also beats PropFair (Zhang & Sabuncu, 2018) by 3 points (0.97 vs. 0.94) and TERM (Li et al., 2021) by 5 points (0.97 vs. 0.92) on MNIST I_low. Social welfare is essentially matched across all methods, confirming that the fairness gains come without sacrificing overall model quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the proportional veto core (PVC) from Moulin's (1981) social choice theory to federated learning, replacing the cardinal utility-based core-stability of CoreFed (Chaudhury et al., 2022) with an ordinal rank-based fairness notion. The key conceptual departure is shifting from cardinal utilities (requiring concavity for existence guarantees) to ordinal ranks (requiring only Lebesgue measurability), which the authors show is both necessary and sufficient: they prove that even approximate cardinal proportionality is infeasible for non-concave utilities (Example 1), while PVC-stable models exist under essentially unrestrictive assumptions. The algorithm Rank-Core-Fed builds on standard FL training (FedAvg) for initial model selection, then applies iterative veto-based elimination over a finite representative set of models, distinguishing it from welfare-maximization approaches like q-FFL or egalitarian methods like Mohri et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The assumption relaxation from concavity to Lebesgue measurability is practically significant: it means PVC-stability guarantees now hold for deep neural networks and other non-convex model classes where CoreFed's guarantees were inapplicable. Two open questions remain: (1) whether provably efficient algorithms exist for computing PVC-stable models over continuous model spaces (the paper provides an FPRAS only for linear utilities over polyhedra), and (2) whether a natural objective function (analogous to the Nash product for core-stability) can directly optimize toward PVC guarantees. The empirical gains over baselines are modest (a few percentage points in rankwise proportionality), suggesting the primary contribution is theoretical rather than algorithmic.
