## Problem

While large language models have demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization across diverse NLP tasks, no equivalent foundation model existed for time-series forecasting that could deliver near state-of-the-art accuracy on previously unseen datasets without task-specific training. The challenge is compounded by the absence of a fixed vocabulary or grammar for time-series, the need to support arbitrary context lengths, prediction horizons, and temporal granularities, and the lack of sufficiently large public time-series corpora for pretraining.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a univariate time-series context of variable length (up to 512 time-points) and produces point forecasts for an arbitrary future horizon via autoregressive patch generation. Input is segmented into non-overlapping patches of length 32, each mapped to a 1280-dim token via a residual block plus positional encoding; a binary padding mask handles variable-length inputs. Output patches are longer (128 time-points) than input patches, so a 256-step forecast requires only 2 autoregressive steps rather than 8, and the model is normalized using the mean and standard deviation of the first input patch (reversible instance normalization).

## Method

TimesFM is a 200M-parameter decoder-only transformer (20 layers, 16 heads, 1280 model dim) trained with causal self-attention to predict the next output patch from all preceding input patches, analogous to next-token prediction in LLMs. Two key architectural departures from standard patch-based forecasting (e.g., PatchTST) are: (1) decoder-only training, which naturally supports variable context lengths and enables parallel training over the full window, and (2) asymmetric patch lengths (input=32, output=128) that reduce autoregressive decoding steps while still accommodating short series. A random patch-masking strategy during training—masking a random fraction r∈[0,31] of the first patch—ensures the model sees all context lengths from 1 to 512 without requiring separate encoder-decoder windows. The model is pretrained on ~100B time-points drawn from Google Trends, Wikipedia pageviews, synthetic ARMA/seasonal/trend series, and several public benchmarks (M4, Electricity, Traffic, Weather, LibCity, Favorita Sales), using a mixture distribution that balances granularities and allocates 80% real / 20% synthetic data.

## Results

On the Monash archive (18 datasets), TimesFM achieves a Scaled MAE (Geometric Mean) of 0.6846, the best among all 15 baselines, outperforming N-BEATS (0.7005), FFNN (0.7044), and the zero-shot llmtime/GPT-3 (0.9715) by 25%. On the Darts benchmark (8 datasets), TimesFM achieves a Scaled MAE (GM) of 0.5767, within statistical significance of the best methods (ARIMA at 0.5219 and llmtime at 0.4882). On the ETT long-horizon forecasting tasks (4 datasets × 2 horizons), TimesFM achieves an average MAE of 0.36, matching or slightly beating supervised PatchTST (0.37) and outperforming FEDFormer (0.53), AutoFormer (0.53), and Informer (0.99) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimesFM clearly outperforms the zero-shot llmtime/GPT-3 baseline across all three benchmarks: by 25% on Monash (0.6846 vs. 0.9715 Scaled MAE GM), by ~15% on Darts (0.5767 vs. 0.4882, though within significance), and by 20% on ETT (0.36 vs. 0.45 avg MAE). On Monash, TimesFM beats all supervised baselines including N-BEATS (0.7005), FFNN (0.7044), DeepAR (0.7477), and WaveNet (0.9384), despite never having seen these datasets during training. On ETT, TimesFM matches or slightly beats the supervised PatchTST (0.36 vs. 0.37) and substantially outperforms other long-horizon transformers like FEDFormer (0.53), AutoFormer (0.53), and Informer (0.99). On Darts, TimesFM is within statistical significance of the best methods (seasonal ARIMA at 0.5219 and llmtime at 0.4882), though the 8-dataset evaluation yields wide confidence intervals. The pretrained PatchTST(ZS) ablation (same architecture but encoder-decoder) performs poorly on Monash (1.0557) due to context-length mismatch, confirming the advantage of the decoder-only design for zero-shot generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

TimesFM builds directly on PatchTST's (Nie et al., 2022) patching strategy for time-series but departs fundamentally by adopting a decoder-only, causal-attention training paradigm borrowed from LLMs, rather than PatchTST's encoder-decoder design. It positions itself against both supervised per-dataset forecasters (DeepAR, N-BEATS, Informer-family models) and LLM-based zero-shot forecasters (llmtime/GPT-3), arguing that a purpose-built time-series architecture trained from scratch on a large temporal corpus can outperform repurposed language models at a tiny fraction of the parameter and compute cost. The key conceptual departure from NLP is the handling of variable context/horizon lengths and granularities through patch masking and asymmetric input/output patch sizes, since time-series lacks the fixed vocabulary and grammar structure that makes LLM pretraining straightforward.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that a 200M-parameter model pretrained on ~100B timepoints can achieve near-SOTA zero-shot forecasting across diverse domains, granularities, and horizons without any dataset-specific training, significantly lowering the barrier for downstream forecasting users. Open questions remain around probabilistic forecasting, covariate integration, prompt-tuning analogues for time-series, and interpretability of the learned temporal representations. The practical release of model weights on HuggingFace positions TimesFM as a likely reference baseline for future time-series foundation model research, while the ablations (synthetic data necessity, output patch length, scaling) provide actionable design guidance for the community.
