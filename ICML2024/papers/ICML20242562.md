## Problem

Multi-objective reinforcement learning (MORL) with potentially conflicting reward signals is pervasive in real-world applications, yet no prior work provides finite-time Pareto-stationary convergence or sample complexity guarantees for actor-critic MORL algorithms. The core difficulty is that standard MGDA-type multi-objective optimization analyses do not transfer to the actor-critic setting due to the intricate coupling between actor and critic components and the cumulative estimation biases introduced by stochastic gradient samples, which can cause divergence if untreated.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a finite-state MOMDP where each transition yields an M-dimensional reward vector (one scalar per objective) and a state observation. Value functions for all objectives share a common linear feature mapping φ(s) ∈ R^{d₂} with d₂ < |S|, and the policy is parameterized by θ ∈ R^{d₁}. The output is a policy parameter θ̂_T (uniformly sampled from the iterate sequence) that defines a Pareto-stationary policy, with the guarantee that the weighted gradient norm ‖∇_θ J(θ̂_T) λ*‖² is bounded by ε + O(ζ_approx), where ζ_approx is the linear function approximation error.

## Method

MOAC alternates between a critic step (mini-batch TD-learning with N inner iterations and batch size D to update M value function parameters w^i) and an actor step (B Markovian samples to compute individual policy gradients g^i_t for each objective). The key mechanism is an MGDA-style common descent direction: a QP (Eq. 9) finds the optimal weight vector λ̂*_t minimizing the norm of the weighted gradient sum, and a momentum update λ_t = (1−η_t)λ_{t−1} + η_t λ̂*_t (Eq. 10) smooths the weights across iterations. This momentum is the critical innovation: it makes the cumulative change of λ_t quantifiable and controllable, yielding convergence rates and sample complexity of O(ε^{-2} log ε^{-1}) that are independent of the number of objectives M—sharply contrasting with general MOO where rates scale linearly or cubically in M. With η_t = t^{-1} or t^{-2}, the algorithm converges at O(T^{-1} log T) or O(T^{-1}) respectively, and the first batch of environment samples automatically initializes the gradient weights without manual specification.

## Results

On the Kuaishou real-world recommendation dataset (7 reward objectives), MOAC achieves NCIS scores of 0.541 (Click), 1.312 (Like×10⁻²), 5.070 (Follow×10⁻⁴), 3.266 (Comment×10⁻³), 1.066 (Forward×10⁻³), 1.486 (Dislike×10⁻⁴), and 1.307 (WatchTime), yielding positive improvements over Behavior-Clone in 6 of 7 objectives. In synthetic experiments on resource-gathering-v0 and fishwood-v0, MOAC with η_t = t⁻¹ achieves simultaneous improvement across all objectives with converging policy gradients, and the O(ε⁻² log ε⁻¹) sample complexity bound is independent of the number of objectives M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOAC outperforms PDPG (Chen et al., 2021) in 5 of 7 objectives (Click, Like, Follow, Comment, Dislike), with the largest margin on Follow (+10.0% vs +4.78% over Behavior-Clone) and Like (+6.57% vs −0.26%), while PDPG edges ahead only on WatchTime (1.308 vs 1.307) and Forward (0.919 vs 1.066 is actually MOAC winning; PDPG wins on Dislike at 1.140 vs 1.486). Against TSCAC (Cai et al., 2023), MOAC wins substantially on Like (+6.57% vs +3.09%), Follow (+10.0% vs −1.57%), Comment (+1.27% vs −3.92%), and Forward (−4.76% vs −10.0%), while TSCAC leads on Click (+1.49% vs +1.30%), Dislike (−41.8% vs −35.5%), and WatchTime (+3.43% vs +1.71%). The ablation with fixed gradient weights (initialized by MOAC's first-iteration QP solution) confirms that the dynamic momentum-based MGDA update contributes meaningful gains over static scalarization, outperforming it in 6 of 7 objectives. Overall, MOAC is the only method achieving positive improvements over Behavior-Clone in 6 of 7 objectives simultaneously, demonstrating balanced multi-objective optimization rather than prioritizing a single dominant reward signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate literatures: MGDA-style multi-objective optimization (Désidéri, 2012) and actor-critic policy-gradient RL (Xu et al., 2020; Qiu et al., 2021). While MGDA has been analyzed for finite-time convergence in general MOO settings (Liu & Vicente, 2021; Fernando et al., 2022), and actor-critic methods have well-established convergence results for single-objective RL, no prior work had combined these to yield a multi-objective actor-critic algorithm with provable Pareto-stationary convergence. The key methodological departure is the introduction of a momentum mechanism on the gradient-weight vector λ_t, which simultaneously mitigates cumulative estimation bias from stochastic MGDA updates and enables automatic initialization of objective weights from environment samples, distinguishing MOAC from both fixed-weight scalarization approaches and prior MORL methods (PDPG, TSCAC) that lacked any convergence guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first paper to establish finite-time Pareto-stationary convergence and sample complexity (O(1/ε²)) for actor-critic MORL, providing a theoretical foundation that was entirely absent from the MORL literature. The M-independence of the convergence rate (contrasted with O(M) or O(M³) in general MOO) is a practically important property for applications with many objectives. However, the analysis is restricted to linear value function approximation, finite state/action spaces, and Pareto-stationarity (a necessary but not sufficient condition for Pareto-optimality), leaving open questions about nonlinear function approximation, infinite-horizon continuous spaces, and whether the guarantees extend to multi-agent or decentralized MORL settings.
