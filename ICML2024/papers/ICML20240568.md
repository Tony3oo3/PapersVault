## Problem

Transformers trained for in-context learning exhibit prolonged learning plateaus—periods of minimal improvement followed by abrupt transitions—making training computationally expensive. The mechanism behind these plateaus was unexplored, and the standard remedy of scaling model size is resource-intensive. This paper asks whether the plateaus can be understood and shortened without increasing model scale.

## Input / Output

In the primary synthetic task, the Transformer receives a sequence of context image-label pairs (images from Shape3D tokenized by a frozen VAE into latent vectors) followed by a prompt image, and must predict the prompt's label. The label is determined by a hidden factor (one of six: object color, shape, scale, background color, floor color, pose) and a mapping function m, so the model must both decode factor values from the image (weights component) and infer which factor is hidden from the context (context component). For NLP validation, the model processes text sequences (SST sentiment reviews or four-word selection prompts) with in-context examples and outputs a class label or selected word.

## Method

The paper conceptually decomposes the Transformer's internal representation into a weights component g_weights(x) (information about the input's factor values, independent of context) and a context component g_context(s_c) (information about which factor is hidden, derived from context examples), then uses linear probes to measure each component's quality during training. The key empirical finding is that learning plateaus coincide with dysfunction of the weights component—its probe score drops below or stagnates at the initialization value—while the context component improves normally. To break plateaus, three strategies are proposed: (1) weights warm-up, where the model is first trained on a simplified D_fix setting (single fixed mapping function) before switching to the full D_rnd setting, (2) mixed training that interleaves D_fix and D_rnd data throughout training, and (3) an extra weights loss that adds auxiliary supervision predicting all six factor values from the internal representation. A constructive analysis further shows that if the weights component is perfect at some layer, only three additional Transformer layers suffice to achieve strong in-context learning, confirming the causal role of the weights component.

## Results

The paper's central quantitative finding is that a 6.69M-parameter Transformer trained with mixed D_fix∧rnd data for only 20 epochs achieves in-context learning accuracy comparable to a 13.88M-parameter model trained on D_rnd for 50 epochs, demonstrating that mitigating learning plateaus reduces required model scale by ~2x. On the Shapes3D synthetic task, the weights component score drops below its initialization value under D_rnd training (confirming dysfunction), and the learning plateau duration exhibits an approximately linear correlation with the weights component score after 50 epochs. Adding an extra weights loss (λ=0.1) significantly shortens plateaus, while an extra context loss yields only marginal benefit. On SimpleFunction tasks, weights component dysfunction (measured by MSE_p) emerges when input dimension d > 30, and the extra loss technique is effective only in this dysfunction regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods but rather evaluates three proposed strategies against the standard D_rnd→D_rnd training baseline on the same synthetic and NLP tasks. The weights warm-up method (D_fix→D_rnd→D_rnd) eliminates the initial learning plateau with as few as 2 warm-up epochs, though the weights component still degrades after the switch to D_rnd. The mixed training method (D_fix∧rnd→D_rnd) is the strongest intervention: it sustains a healthy weights component throughout training and enables a 6.69M model (20 epochs) to match a 13.88M model (50 epochs) on D_rnd, effectively halving the required model size. The extra weights loss method (adding L_w with λ=0.1) provides a data-free alternative that significantly accelerates learning, whereas the analogous extra context loss (L_c) produces negligible improvement, confirming that the weights component—not the context component—is the bottleneck. On NLP tasks (SST-ICL, WordSelection), the same qualitative pattern holds, though the SST-ICL task requires more than 30 epochs before the D_fix∧rnd advantage over D_rnd becomes visible, reflecting the greater difficulty of improving the weights component in natural language.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a complement to the extensive literature on the abrupt emergence (transition) of in-context learning (Olsson et al., 2022; Michaud et al., 2023; Kirsch et al., 2022), shifting focus to the preceding learning plateaus. Its conceptual decomposition into weights and context components is related to but explicitly distinguished from the in-weights vs. in-context learning distinction of Chan et al. (2022a,b): here both components coexist within a single ICL prediction, whereas in-weights learning is recast as standard supervised learning. The probe-based evaluation methodology draws on interpretability tools (Alain & Bengio, 2016; Voita & Titov, 2020), and the synthetic task design builds on Shapes3D (Kim & Mnih, 2018) and simple function tasks (Garg et al., 2022), but the paper's core contribution is the diagnostic framework linking weights-component dysfunction to plateau duration, which no prior work had isolated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical pathway to reduce the compute cost of training Transformers for in-context learning without scaling model size: a 6.69M model with mixed training matches a 13.88M model after fewer epochs, and the extra-loss technique requires no auxiliary dataset. However, the methods are somewhat task-structured (the extra loss requires knowing factor decompositions), and the NLP experiments reveal that the benefits are delayed and can be negative in early training, limiting immediate generalizability. The theoretical explanation for *why* the weights component degrades remains incomplete—the constructive analysis in Appendix E is a weaker substitute for a full training-dynamics proof, and the authors acknowledge the framework cannot explain plateaus in supervised learning (e.g., grokking). Open questions include whether the weights/context decomposition generalizes to tasks without clean factor structure and whether the identified mechanisms hold at much larger scales.
