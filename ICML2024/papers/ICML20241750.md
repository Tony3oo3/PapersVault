## Problem

While extensive research has focused on deriving efficient SDE/ODE solvers for diffusion models, the sampling schedule—the discretization of the integration interval into noise levels—has been treated as a minor hyperparameter relying on hand-crafted heuristics such as polynomials and cosine functions. The paper demonstrates that the optimal schedule depends on the specific dataset, trained model, and solver, and that suboptimal schedules cause significant quality degradation, particularly in the few-step synthesis regime where discretization errors are largest.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241750/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained denoiser network, a small subset of dataset samples (8192 points), a choice of stochastic SDE solver, and a target number of steps N, and produces an optimized sequence of N+1 noise levels that can be plugged directly into any compatible solver at inference time. The schedule is optimized hierarchically (10→20→40 steps) and then log-linearly interpolated to arbitrary step counts, requiring at most ~300 optimization iterations on 4–16 GPUs. The resulting schedules are dataset- and solver-specific but empirically generalize across different classifier-free guidance values and even to deterministic ODE solvers like DDIM and DPM-Solver++(2M).

## Method

The core insight is that every stochastic SDE solver can be reinterpreted as exactly solving a solver-specific linearized SDE on each sub-interval, and the mismatch between this linearized SDE and the true generative SDE is bounded by a KL-divergence upper bound (KLUB) derived via Girsanov's theorem. This KLUB decomposes additively over sub-intervals, turning schedule optimization into a coordinate-wise minimization problem over the intermediate timesteps. To estimate the KLUB with low variance, the authors use importance sampling over time with a distribution derived from Gaussian data assumptions, which handles the ~3-orders-of-magnitude variation in integrand values within a single interval. The actual optimization uses a zeroth-order (derivative-free) approach: for each schedule point, 11 candidate values are evaluated in a local neighborhood and the one minimizing KLUB is selected, avoiding the instability of gradient-based optimization. A hierarchical strategy (optimize 10 steps → subdivide → optimize 20 → subdivide → optimize 40) with early stopping at the first stage accelerates convergence and prevents over-optimization of the upper bound, which the paper proves does not coincide with the true KL-optimal schedule.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, AYS-optimized schedules consistently outperform hand-crafted baselines across all solvers, with the largest gains in the low-NFE regime. For SDE-DPM-Solver++(2M) at NFE=10, AYS achieves FID 6.11 vs. 8.48 (EDM) and 8.47 (time-uniform); for DPM-Solver++(2M) at NFE=10, AYS reaches FID 4.31 vs. 4.82 (LogSNR). The paper reports that a 20-step AYS schedule matches the quality of a 30-step default schedule, yielding a 1.5× speedup. In user studies on Stable Diffusion 1.5 and Stable Video Diffusion, AYS receives clear preference (58% vs. 42% for video) over the default EDM schedule.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AYS outperforms the EDM schedule (Karras et al., 2022) across nearly all solvers and NFE settings on ImageNet, with the most dramatic gain for SDE-DPM-Solver++(2M) at NFE=10 (FID 6.11 vs. 8.48, a 28% reduction). Against the LogSNR schedule (Lu et al., 2022) used with DPM-Solver++(2M), AYS achieves FID 4.31 vs. 4.82 at NFE=10 and 4.70 vs. 4.81 at NFE=20. For the ER-SDE-Solvers (Cui et al., 2023), AYS consistently beats both EDM and time-uniform schedules, e.g., ER-SDE-Solver 1 at NFE=10 drops from 17.78 (EDM) to 8.36 (AYS). Against DDIM with time-uniform schedule (Song et al., 2020), AYS improves FID from 7.57 to 6.96 at NFE=10. The paper notes that Watson et al. (2022), who learned schedules by differentiating through KID, is limited to image models and first-order DDIM; AYS is more versatile, working across data modalities (2D, image, video) and higher-order solvers. Gains diminish at higher NFE (30 steps) where discretization error is already small, confirming the method's primary value in the few-step regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of work on accelerating diffusion model sampling, which has focused on deriving more efficient SDE/ODE solvers (DPM-Solver++, DDIM, ER-SDE-Solvers) while treating the sampling schedule as a fixed hand-crafted hyperparameter (EDM, LogSNR, time-uniform). It builds on the Girsanov's theorem framework from Chen et al. (2022) to derive a KL-divergence upper bound (KLUB) between the true generative SDE and the linearized SDE solved by a discretized solver, and on the Karras et al. (2022) SDE formulation for the noising schedule notation. Prior attempts at schedule optimization (Watson et al. 2021 via ELBO, Watson et al. 2022 via KID differentiation) are acknowledged but positioned as limited to specific objectives and image-only settings, whereas AYS provides a solver-agnostic, modality-agnostic framework grounded in stochastic calculus.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is immediate: the paper provides plug-and-play optimized schedules for Stable Diffusion 1.5, SDXL, DeepFloyd-IF, and Stable Video Diffusion that practitioners can adopt without retraining, yielding measurable quality gains especially in the few-step (10 NFE) regime where most real-time applications operate. A key limitation is that the KLUB objective is an upper bound on discretization error rather than the error itself, necessitating early stopping to avoid over-optimizing intermediate path alignment at the expense of final output quality. Open questions include extending the framework to label- or text-conditional schedule optimization, single-step higher-order ODE solvers (Heun, Runge-Kutta), and integration with flow matching and stochastic interpolant formulations, which the authors note are compatible in principle.
