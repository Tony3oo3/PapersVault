## Problem

Recent heatmap-guided MCTS methods for large-scale TSPs rely on ML models trained with heuristic surrogate losses that do not directly optimize the TSP objective, creating a fundamental train-test mismatch since MCTS is absent during training. Additionally, no prior work has conducted a fair, resource-matched comparison between these ML-guided MCTS pipelines and the established LKH-3 heuristic, leaving the practical value of the paradigm unverified.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242224/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a 2D Euclidean TSP instance with n vertices in [0,1]², represented as a coordinate tensor of shape (n, 2). The heatmap generation stage produces an n×n matrix Φ where Φ_{i,j} encodes the suitability of edge (i,j) in the optimal tour; for SoftDist this is computed as a row-wise softmax of the negated distance matrix scaled by a single temperature τ. The heatmap then conditions MCTS, which outputs a final TSP tour as a vertex permutation π of length n.

## Method

The paper introduces SoftDist, a baseline that applies softmax to the TSP distance matrix with a single tunable temperature parameter τ (e.g., 0.0066 for TSP-500, 0.0018 for TSP-10000), found via grid search on the original TSP objective. Because SoftDist has only one parameter and no learned weights, it directly optimizes the true TSP loss rather than a surrogate, aligning training and test phases and eliminating the need for supervision. The paper also proposes the Score metric, defined as the ratio of LKH-3's gap to MCTS's gap over the optimal solution length, enabling fair comparison under identical CPU hardware and time budgets. Experiments across TSP-500/1000/10000 show SoftDist outperforms or matches all ML-based heatmap generators (ATT-GCN, DIMES, UTSP, DIFUSCO) in solution quality with negligible inference time, while all heatmap-guided MCTS methods score below 34% relative to LKH-3.

## Results

On TSP-500/1000/10000, SoftDist (softmax on the distance matrix) achieves performance gaps of 1.44%, 2.20%, and 3.13% respectively, outperforming ATT-GCN (1.64%/2.37%/3.80%), DIMES (1.77%/2.44%/3.23%), and UTSP (3.41%/4.40%/N/A) while generating heatmaps in <0.1 s versus minutes for ML-based methods. DIFUSCO achieves slightly lower gaps (0.51%/1.18%/2.77%) but requires 3.61–28.51 min for heatmap generation. Under the proposed Score metric (relative efficiency vs. LKH-3), all heatmap-guided MCTS methods score below 34% (SoftDist: 0.84%/4.17%/29.88%), confirming that even the best MCTS variant is far less efficient than LKH-3 under identical CPU resources.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SoftDist outperforms ATT-GCN, DIMES, and UTSP on both solution quality (lower gap) and heatmap inference time across all three TSP scales; for example, on TSP-1000 SoftDist achieves a 2.20% gap with near-zero heatmap time versus ATT-GCN's 2.37% (0.73 min), DIMES's 2.44% (2.08 min), and UTSP's 4.40% (3.35 min). DIFUSCO is the sole method that beats SoftDist on gap (e.g., 1.18% vs. 2.20% on TSP-1000), but at the cost of 11.86 min heatmap generation (≈3.6× the MCTS time) and requires fully supervised labels at each scale plus 8×V100 GPUs. Against the LKH-3 heuristic, all heatmap-guided MCTS methods lose decisively: the Score metric shows SoftDist at only 0.84% (TSP-500), 4.17% (TSP-1000), and 29.88% (TSP-10000) of LKH-3's efficiency, meaning MCTS solutions are 10–100× further from optimal than LKH-3 under identical CPU time budgets. Even with Min et al.'s tuned MCTS parameters (Table 3), the best Score reaches only 13.93% (DIFUSCO, TSP-1000), and a zero-heatmap baseline (Zeros) performs nearly identically to SoftDist, indicating the heatmap contributes minimally relative to the MCTS search itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not build on a specific prior method but instead critically evaluates the entire heatmap-guided MCTS paradigm (ATT-GCN, DIMES, UTSP, DIFUSCO) that has dominated large-scale TSP research since 2021. It departs from this lineage by questioning two foundational assumptions: that ML-generated heatmaps are necessary for effective MCTS guidance, and that the MCTS paradigm itself offers practical value over established heuristics. The SoftDist baseline repurposes the simplest possible distance-based scoring (softmax on the distance matrix) as a counterpoint to complex GNN/diffusion/RL-based heatmap generators, while the Score metric introduces a controlled comparison framework against LKH-3 that prior ML-TSP papers avoided due to hardware and language mismatches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a parameter-free softmax on the distance matrix matches or exceeds complex ML heatmaps challenges the core premise that learned representations add value in this pipeline, suggesting the field's investment in surrogate loss design may be misdirected. The Score metric reveals that even the best heatmap-guided MCTS methods achieve less than 30% of LKH-3's efficiency, raising the question of whether post-hoc search-based ML approaches can ever compete with decades of hand-crafted heuristic engineering. Open questions include whether end-to-end solution generation (bypassing MCTS entirely) can close this gap, and whether theoretically grounded loss functions could make ML heatmaps genuinely informative rather than redundant. The paper's limitations include its focus on 2D Euclidean TSP and the fact that LKH-3 benefits from extensive problem-specific tuning that ML methods lack by design.
