## Problem

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is widely used to improve LLM reliability, yet no theoretical framework exists to certify or bound the generation risks of RAG models. In particular, it remains unexplored whether RAG provably reduces generation risks compared to vanilla LLMs, how to provide finite-sample risk guarantees, and whether such guarantees survive test-time distribution shifts. Existing conformal risk control methods assume data exchangeability and are limited to monotonic risk functions, leaving a gap for general bounded risk functions under distribution drift.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user query, an external knowledge base of 6M+ documents, a calibration set, and a RAG configuration vector λ = [N_rag, λ_g, λ_s] (number of retrieved examples, generation set size, and diversity threshold). Rather than producing a single generation, the constrained protocol outputs a set of diverse generations whose size and mutual similarity are controlled by λ_g and λ_s. The certified outputs are (1) a conformal generation risk upper bound α̂_λ for a given configuration, and (2) a valid configuration set Λ̂_α guaranteeing risk below a user-specified level α, both computed from calibration statistics via Hoeffding-Bentkus inequalities.

## Method

C-RAG introduces a constrained generation protocol that retrieves N_rag in-context examples via KNN search, samples multiple generations from a one-layer self-attention transformer, and rejects outputs exceeding a similarity threshold to enforce diversity. Conformal risk analysis is then adapted to this protocol: Prop. 1 derives a high-probability risk upper bound from calibration empirical risk, and Prop. 2 uses Bonferroni FWER control to identify configurations whose risk is provably below a target level. The theoretical core models the transformer with a (d⁺, Φ_M)-transformer assumption and the retriever as a V_rag-retrieval model (bounded contrastive-loss variance), proving in Thm. 1 that RAG achieves strictly lower conformal generation risk than a single LLM with high probability. Thm. 2 extends the guarantee to test-time distribution shifts measured by bounded Hellinger distance ρ, providing the first conformal risk bound for general bounded risk functions under drift, and Thm. 3 shows RAG's advantage persists under such shifts with a retrieval-quality decay factor m(ρ).

## Results

Across four NLP datasets (AESLC, CommonGen, DART, E2E) using Llama-2-7b with 1-ROUGE-L as the risk function and δ=0.1, C-RAG's conformal generation risk upper bounds the empirical risk with gaps narrowing to the scale of 1e-3, confirming both soundness and tightness. RAG consistently achieves lower conformal generation risk than a vanilla LLM (N_rag=0) across all four retrieval models, and the risk decreases monotonically as the number of retrieved examples N_rag increases. Under distribution shifts (Hellinger distance ρ up to 0.2), the conformal generation risk increases linearly with ρ while remaining valid. Among retrieval models, Biencoder-SFT and OpenAI/ada (text-embedding-ada-002) achieve the lowest conformal generation risks, outperforming BM25 and BAAI/bge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four retrieval models: BM25 (token-level matching), BAAI/bge (SOTA open-source embedding in MTEB), OpenAI/ada (text-embedding-ada-002), and Biencoder-SFT (in-domain fine-tuned biencoder). Biencoder-SFT consistently achieves the lowest conformal generation risk across all four datasets, benefiting from in-domain training data. OpenAI/ada, trained on a large open corpus, also demonstrates low conformal generation risks and is competitive with Biencoder-SFT, particularly under distribution shifts where both show lower risks at varying Hellinger distances. BM25 and BAAI/bge achieve higher conformal generation risks, with BM25 (sparse token matching) generally performing worst. All four RAG configurations outperform the vanilla LLM baseline (N_rag=0), empirically validating Theorem 1. The paper also shows that increasing N_rag reduces risk more effectively than adjusting the generation set size λ_g, and that the conformal risk bounds remain tight (gap ~1e-3) regardless of which retrieval model is used.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conformal risk control framework of Bates et al. (2021) and Angelopoulos et al. (2021, 2022) from classification/regression to text generation, adapting their Hoeffding-Bentkus concentration bounds to a constrained RAG generation protocol. It departs from prior distribution-shift conformal results (Angelopoulos et al. 2022; Farinhas et al. 2023), which are limited to monotonic risk functions, by proving the first guarantee for general bounded risk functions under Hellinger-bounded shifts, building on the Grammian generalization bound of Weber et al. (2022). The theoretical analysis of RAG's risk-reduction benefit draws on single-layer transformer formulations from Von Oswald et al. (2023) and Zhang et al. (2023b), but provides the first proof that retrieval-augmented generation provably lowers conformal generation risk relative to a vanilla LLM under explicit conditions on retrieval and transformer quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

C-RAG provides the first provable framework for certifying that RAG reduces generation risk, giving practitioners a principled way to choose RAG configurations (number of retrieved examples, generation set size, diversity threshold) that guarantee risk below a target level. The extension to distribution shifts for general bounded risk functions broadens the applicability of conformal guarantees beyond the exchangeability assumption that has limited prior work. Key limitations include the need for in-distribution calibration data (challenging for time-varying user queries), the high-probability (not deterministic) nature of the guarantee, and the trade-off between knowledge base size and inference efficiency. Open questions include adaptive conformal risk calibration for sequential/time-series deployments and whether the single-token-per-instance simplification in the theoretical analysis transfers to multi-token generation settings.
