## Problem

Training a unified model on heterogeneous data from different domains or tasks suffers from severe gradient conflicts that suppress the benefits of large-scale data. Existing multi-task learning methods either share all parameters (hard parameter sharing, which lacks capacity for task-specific features) or introduce full task-specific branches (soft parameter sharing / MoE, which causes parameter explosion and poor scalability). The paper asks how to efficiently mitigate these heterogeneity conflicts while keeping the parameter overhead controllable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242577/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on three heterogeneous data types: domain heterogeneity (same classification task across domains like VLCS/Office-Home), multi-input task heterogeneity (different medical image modalities and body regions in RadImageNet/MedMNISTv2), and single-input task heterogeneity (same RGB-D input in NYUv2 producing segmentation masks, depth maps, and surface normal maps simultaneously). A key practical distinction is that MoLA-Grad requires task identifiers at inference time to select the correct adapter, while MoLA-Router operates without them by dynamically routing through a learned gate, making it applicable to target-agnostic deployment scenarios.

## Method

MoLA attaches E low-rank adapters (each a pair of matrices B_i ∈ R^{C_out·k × r·k} and A_i ∈ R^{r·k × C_in·k}) in parallel to a shared backbone W_0, so the convolution becomes g_t = (W_0 + Σ α_i B_i A_i) h_t. Two variants handle different inference scenarios: MoLA-Grad sets E=T and uses one-hot task identifiers to assign each sample exclusively to its adapter (explicit gradient separation, implemented efficiently as a group convolution), while MoLA-Router learns a shared router that outputs soft mixing weights α via a gate function, supervised by a novel Task-wise Decorrelation (TwD) loss that encourages homogeneous weights within a task and heterogeneous weights across tasks (implicit gradient separation). The critical design choice is training MoLA end-to-end from scratch alongside the backbone (unlike LoRA fine-tuning), where the primary-secondary rank discrepancy between the full-rank backbone and low-rank adapters encourages the model to disentangle shared knowledge into W_0 and complementary task-specific knowledge into the adapters, as confirmed by principal-component and eigenvalue-distribution analyses showing MoLA reduces dominant eigenvalues and increases the proportion of active eigenvectors.

## Results

On NYUv2 (single-input task heterogeneity), MoLA-Grad achieves 52.79 mIoU for segmentation and a task-weighted performance drop of −9.17% relative to single-task baselines, outperforming the best MTL baseline MTAN (52.07 mIoU, −6.56%) and the hard-parameter-sharing HSP (45.21 mIoU, +4.72%). On RadImageNet (multi-input task heterogeneity), MoLA-Grad reaches 56.47% average accuracy across 11 medical imaging tasks, surpassing the best baseline HSP (55.81%) and single-task (54.91%). On domain heterogeneity benchmarks, MoLA-Grad attains 86.41% on VLCS and 84.02% on Office-Home, exceeding all MTL and domain-adaptation baselines by 0.8–1.3 points on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoLA-Grad and MoLA-Router consistently outperform all 22 baselines across all five datasets and three heterogeneous scenarios. On NYUv2, MoLA-Grad (−9.17% Δm) beats the strongest SPS/MoE baselines MTAN (−6.56%) and CGC (−6.55%) by ~2.6 points, and the gradient-optimization methods (PCGrad 3.17%, CAGrad 0.13%, GradVac 3.75%) by a wide margin. On RadImageNet, MoLA-Grad (56.47% avg) surpasses HSP (55.81%), LTB (55.72%), and MGDA (55.08%), while MoLA-Router (56.03%) edges out HSP by 0.22 points. On Office-Home, MoLA-Grad (84.02%) outperforms the best domain-adaptation method EQRM (83.73%) and all MTL baselines (Cross-Stitch 76.94%, MMoE 77.70%, CGC 78.76%). On VLCS, both MoLA variants (~86.4%) exceed Cross-Stitch (85.59%) and DSelect-K (85.04%). Notably, MoLA-Grad requires task identifiers at inference, while MoLA-Router operates without them yet achieves within 0.1–0.5 points of MoLA-Grad, making it the more deployable variant for target-agnostic settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) from its standard role in parameter-efficient fine-tuning of pre-trained models to an end-to-end training paradigm where low-rank adapters are jointly trained with a shared backbone from scratch. It draws structural inspiration from Mixture of Experts (MMoE, DSelect-K) but replaces full expert networks with low-rank adapters to achieve controllable parameter growth, and introduces a Task-wise Decorrelation (TwD) loss to guide the router in the target-agnostic variant. Conceptually, the paper positions itself at the intersection of MTL (hard/soft parameter sharing, gradient surgery methods) and adapter-based adaptation, arguing that the primary-secondary rank discrepancy between backbone and adapters naturally encourages disentanglement of shared versus task-specific knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoLA offers a parameter-efficient alternative to soft parameter sharing and MoE-based MTL methods for heterogeneous data training, with the practical advantage that parameter count grows slowly with rank r and number of adapters. The eigenvalue analysis revealing that MoLA suppresses dominant eigenvalues (the opposite of LoRA's amplification effect) provides a mechanistic explanation for why gradient conflicts are reduced. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large numbers of tasks or to foundation-model-scale backbones, whether the optimal rank and adapter count can be determined automatically rather than by grid search, and whether the TwD loss generalizes to scenarios where task boundaries are ambiguous or overlapping.
