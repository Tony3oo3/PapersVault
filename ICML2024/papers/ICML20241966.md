## Problem

Estimating risk measures (VaR, CVaR, variance) of the infinite-horizon discounted cost in a Markov Cost Process had no known sample complexity guarantees beyond the mean. Prior work (Metelli et al., 2023) established only an Ω(1/ε) lower bound for mean estimation, and no lower or upper bounds existed for any risk measure beyond the mean in a Markovian setting. This paper is the first to provide matching minimax lower and upper bounds of order 1/ε² for VaR, CVaR, and variance estimation.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a sample path of N state transitions from an unknown MCP, where at each step the agent observes the current state, a reset decision (0 or 1), and a single-stage cost drawn from the state's cost distribution. The MCP can be restarted from the initial state distribution ν as many times as needed. The N transitions are partitioned into m = ⌈N/T⌉ independent trajectories of length T (the truncation parameter), and the output is a point estimate of the target risk measure (VaR, CVaR, variance, or a general Lipschitz risk measure) of the infinite-horizon discounted cost, with explicit error bounds in both expected and high-probability senses.

## Method

The lower bounds are established via two distinct constructions: (1) for deterministic costs, a two-state Markov chain where one state's cost diverges as ε→0, leading to a constrained max-min optimization over transition and initial-state probabilities that yields exp(−nε² ln(1/α) ln(1/γ)); and (2) for stochastic costs, a single-state MCP with Gaussian single-stage costs, where a novel reduction maps n cost observations to 2n i.i.d. Gaussian samples of the limiting distribution via a linear combination aZ + bZ', enabling application of total-variation and KL-divergence arguments to obtain exp(−2√(Knε²)). The upper bounds use a truncation scheme: the infinite-horizon cost D is approximated by the T-horizon cost D_T with error bounded by γ^T K/(1−γ), and standard i.i.d. concentration inequalities (Wang & Gao for CVaR, Prashanth et al. for VaR, Hoeffding for variance) are applied to the m truncated samples. A general extension covers any Lipschitz-continuous risk measure (spectral risk, utility-based shortfall risk) via the 1-Wasserstein distance, yielding matching Õ(1/ε²) bounds.

## Results

The paper establishes a minimax lower bound of Ω(1/ε²) for estimating VaR, CVaR, mean, and variance of the infinite-horizon discounted cost in a Markov Cost Process, in both expected and high-probability senses. Matching upper bounds of Õ(1/ε²) are derived via a truncation-based estimator for CVaR, variance, and a broad class of Lipschitz risk measures (including spectral risk and utility-based shortfall risk). In the high-probability setting, the lower bound takes the form exp(−c√(Nε²)), implying N = Ω(ε⁻² ln(1/δ)) samples are necessary, while the upper bound achieves the same rate with a truncation parameter T = O(log(1/ε)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole direct comparison is with Metelli et al. (2023), which derived a minimax lower bound of Ω(1/ε) (i.e., Ω(ε⁻¹ ln(1/δ)) samples) for mean estimation in a discounted MCP using a two-state Markov chain with {0,1} rewards. This paper's lower bound of Ω(1/ε²) strictly improves upon that result: Remark 3.3 and Remark A.1 show that the Metelli et al. bound, when the ε-dependence of their variance parameter σ_f² = ε(1−ε) is properly accounted for, actually degrades to Ω(1/n) in the high-probability sense, whereas the present paper achieves Ω(1/√n) in expectation and Ω(ε⁻² ln(1/δ)) in high probability. Beyond the mean, this paper is the first to provide any lower or upper bound for risk measures (VaR, CVaR, variance) in a Markovian setting, filling a gap that Metelli et al. did not address. The upper bounds match the lower bounds up to logarithmic factors, confirming near-optimal sample complexity for the proposed truncation-based estimator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MCP mean-estimation lower bound framework of Metelli et al. (2023) from the expected value to risk measures (VaR, CVaR, variance), replacing their unconstrained optimization with a constrained one that accounts for the quantile structure of risk measures. For upper bounds, it adapts i.i.d. concentration inequalities for CVaR (Wang & Gao, 2010) and VaR (Prashanth et al., 2020) to the sequential MCP setting via a truncation-and-reset scheme, and leverages the Wasserstein-distance concentration framework of Prashanth & Bhat (2022) to cover a broad class of Lipschitz risk measures including spectral and utility-based shortfall risk. The key conceptual departure is that prior MCP estimation theory only addressed the mean, while risk measure estimation was confined to i.i.d. sampling; this paper bridges the two for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish matching (up to log factors) minimax lower and upper bounds for risk measure estimation in a Markovian setting, providing a clear sample-complexity target for risk-sensitive policy evaluation and gradient algorithms. A notable byproduct is the correction of Metelli et al.'s claimed Ω(1/√n) mean-estimation lower bound, which the authors show is actually only Ω(1/n) once the ε-dependence in their proof is properly accounted for. Open questions include closing the δ-dependence gap between upper (√log(1/δ)) and lower (log(1/δ)) bounds, extending upper bounds to sub-Gaussian cost distributions, and incorporating Markov chain mixing time into the lower bounds.
