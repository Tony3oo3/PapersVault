## Problem

Estimating parameters of directed graphical models with latent variables from incomplete data is intractable under standard likelihood-based approaches (EM, VI) because the posterior over hidden variables is generally intractable, requiring either restrictive structural assumptions (mean-field, conjugate families) or variational approximations that treat parameter estimation as a by-product of posterior inference. This paper asks whether one can learn parameters of *any* DAG with hidden nodes without explicitly solving inference or assuming structural independencies, by replacing the KL-divergence-based MLE objective with a Wasserstein distance objective from optimal transport.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a known DAG structure (with observed and hidden nodes), a batch of observed data samples (documents for LDA, time-series observations for HMM, or images for discrete representation learning), and a prior distribution over exogenous noise variables. It produces point estimates of all model parameters simultaneously—topic-word matrices γ and concentration α for LDA, Poisson rate parameters λ for HMM, and codebook means, covariances, mixing weights, and decoder network weights for discrete representation learning. The backward maps are parameterized by neural networks (LSTM + linear layers for text, 3-layer MLP for time series, deep networks for images), and the exogenous noise is sampled directly from its prior rather than learned, reducing computational cost.

## Method

OTP-DAG reformulates parameter estimation as minimizing the Wasserstein distance between the empirical data distribution and the model distribution over observed nodes, rather than maximizing likelihood. The key theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) shows this Wasserstein objective equals the infimum over stochastic 'backward maps' φ_i (one per observed node) of the expected reconstruction cost c(X_O, ψ_θ(PA_{X_O})), where ψ_θ is the deterministic forward generative mapping and the backward maps must satisfy push-forward constraints φ_i#P_d(X_i) = P_θ(PA_{X_i}, U_i). In practice, the hard push-forward constraint is relaxed into a divergence penalty η·D(P_φ, P_θ), yielding a tractable objective optimized by alternating SGD updates on the backward map parameters (neural networks) and the model parameters θ. Discrete latent variables are handled via Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization and continuous ones via the standard Gaussian reparameterization trick, enabling end-to-end differentiable training. The framework generalizes Wasserstein Auto-Encoders (WAE) from two-node models to arbitrary DAGs, and inherits WAE's resistance to posterior collapse because the push-forward divergence penalizes the *marginal* distribution of latent codes rather than per-sample KL, and the reconstruction term prevents the backward map from ignoring input information.

## Results

On discrete representation learning (K=512), OTP-DAG consistently outperforms VQ-VAE across all four image datasets: on CIFAR-10 it achieves SSIM 0.80 vs 0.70, PSNR 25.40 vs 23.14, LPIPS 0.23 vs 0.35, and rFID 56.5 vs 77.3; on SVHN the gap widens to PSNR 32.56 vs 26.94 and rFID 25.2 vs 38.5. On the Poisson HMM task, OTP-DAG matches EM in parameter estimation accuracy (mean absolute error 0.040–0.148 vs 0.022–0.166) while running ~6.7× faster (≈3 min vs ≈20 min for 50 steps). On LDA parameter recovery, OTP-DAG achieves the best Wasserstein distance (0.027, 0.010, 0.005 across three settings) and Hellinger distance, though EM and SVI remain slightly better on KL divergence (their native objective).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EM (Dempster et al., 1977), OTP-DAG achieves comparable or better parameter estimation accuracy on the Poisson HMM (mean absolute error 0.040–0.148 vs 0.022–0.166) while being ~6.7× faster (≈3 min vs ≈20 min per 50 steps). On LDA, OTP-DAG wins on Hellinger and Wasserstein distances (e.g., WS 0.027 vs 0.058 at K=10) but trails EM by a small margin on KL divergence (1.701 vs 1.634), which is the metric EM directly optimizes. Against SVI (Hoffman et al., 2013), OTP-DAG similarly dominates on Hellinger and Wasserstein while being slightly worse on KL; on real-world topic inference (Table 2), OTP-DAG achieves substantially higher coherence (e.g., 10.45% vs 5.90% on 20 News Group) while maintaining competitive diversity. Against ProdLDA (Srivastava & Sutton, 2017), OTP-DAG wins on all three fidelity metrics in LDA and achieves much higher topic coherence (10.45% vs 4.78% on 20 News Group; 7.66% vs 2.91% on DBLP). Against VQ-VAE (Van Den Oord et al., 2017), OTP-DAG wins on every reconstruction metric across all four image datasets, with the largest gains on CIFAR-10 (+0.10 SSIM, +2.26 PSNR) and SVHN (+0.06 SSIM, +5.62 PSNR), attributed to VQ-VAE's codebook collapse issue that OTP-DAG's OT-based objective mitigates.

## Relation to Prior Work

OTP-DAG generalizes the Wasserstein Auto-Encoder (WAE) from a two-node latent-variable model to arbitrary directed acyclic graphs, replacing the MLE/KL-divergence objective of EM and variational inference with a Wasserstein distance minimization. The theoretical core (Theorem 4.1) leverages the gluing lemma from optimal transport to show that the global OT cost decomposes into local backward maps from each observed node to its parents, connecting to the push-forward measure formalism. Practically, the method inherits the reparameterization and amortized inference machinery from VAE/VI (Gumbel-Softmax for discrete variables, Gaussian reparameterization for continuous ones) but replaces the ELBO with a reconstruction-plus-divergence objective, positioning it between the analytic-derivation extreme (EM, SVI) and the black-box extreme (ProdLDA, VQ-VAE).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a unified, assumption-light approach to parameter estimation in any DAG with latent variables, avoiding the intractable posterior integrals that limit EM and the structural assumptions that constrain mean-field VI. The demonstrated mitigation of codebook collapse in discrete representation learning (perplexity ~500 vs ~70 for VQ-VAE on CIFAR-10) suggests the OT formulation may be a more natural objective for discrete latent spaces than KL-based ELBOs. Open questions include scalability to very large graphs where ancestral sampling for parent marginals becomes expensive, the choice of divergence D for the push-forward constraint (WS, MMD, JSD each have trade-offs), and whether the framework extends to undirected models or structure learning.
