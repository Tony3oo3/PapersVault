## Problem

Fine-tuned vision-language models (e.g., CLIP with prompt tuning) suffer from miscalibration in open-vocabulary settings: they become underconfident on base classes yet overconfident on novel classes. Existing post-hoc calibration methods (temperature scaling, density-ratio calibration, binning) can fix base-class miscalibration but fail to transfer to unseen classes, leaving a critical reliability gap for real-world deployment in high-stakes applications.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a set of text class labels (including both base and novel classes) as input. It computes a per-class Textual Deviation (TD) score using only the l2-normalized text embeddings from the pre-trained and tuned CLIP text encoders, requiring K=5 nearest-neighbor lookups among base-class text features. The output is a set of rectified logits that, after softmax, yield calibrated class probabilities; for base classes the TD score is set to 1, so the method reduces to standard temperature scaling and does not alter base-class calibration.

## Method

Distance-Aware Calibration (DAC) is a post-hoc, training-free method that scales the CLIP temperature τ by a class-dependent factor γ(ĉ) derived from the Textual Deviation score. The TD score is defined as the ratio of the proximity of a novel class's text embedding to base-class text embeddings in the tuned model versus the pre-trained model: γ(c_i) = P(w'_i, W') / P(w_i, W), where P uses the exponential of the negative mean L2 distance to K nearest base-class text features. The key insight is that prompt tuning widens the textual distribution gap, and classes farther from the base-class text manifold receive artificially high confidence; DAC counteracts this by applying a larger temperature (lowering confidence) to classes with lower TD scores. The calibrated logit is L_c^dac(x) = γ(ĉ)·τ·sim(φ(x), ψ(t'_c)), which is algorithm-agnostic, requires no hyperparameter tuning, and adds negligible inference cost (0.13 s vs. 0.12 s baseline on ImageNet-1K).

## Results

Across 11 downstream datasets, DAC reduces the average ECE from 13.84% to 7.00% for CoOp (a 6.84-point reduction), from 5.77% to 4.61% for MaPLe, and from 4.22% to 3.74% for ProGrad. The improvement is most pronounced for high-confidence predictions (bins 0.6–0.9), where CoOp's ECE drops from 41.60 to 11.37 in the 0.9-confidence bin. DAC adds negligible inference overhead (0.13 s vs. 0.12 s baseline on ImageNet-1K) and requires no hyperparameter tuning beyond a fixed K=5 nearest-neighbor count.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAC is compared against six post-hoc calibration baselines (Temperature Scaling, Density-Ratio Calibration, Histogram Binning, Isotonic Regression, Multi-Isotonic Regression) and the uncalibrated tuned model. On new classes, Temperature Scaling and Density-Ratio Calibration—both trained on base-class data—actually worsen calibration (e.g., TS increases CoOp's ECE from 1.59% to 3.90% on ImageNet new classes), while bin-based methods are entirely inapplicable to zero-shot novel-class prediction. DAC outperforms DEN on new-class ECE (5.67% vs. 8.08% for CoOp on ImageNet) and is ~120× faster at inference (0.13 s vs. 15.88 s). Against the uncalibrated tuned model, DAC consistently reduces ECE across all seven tuning methods (CoOp, CoCoOp, ProDA, KgCoOp, MaPLe, ProGrad, PromptSRC), with the largest gains for CoOp (−6.84 pts) and MaPLe (−1.16 pts). DAC also complements DEN: combining both yields 3.29% ECE on ImageNet new classes, the best result reported.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the post-hoc calibration literature (temperature scaling, density-ratio calibration, binning-based methods) to the open-vocabulary setting for fine-tuned vision-language models. It builds directly on the proximity-based calibration framework of Xiong et al. (2023) by repurposing the proximity definition to compute a Textual Deviation score over class text embeddings rather than image features. The key departure from prior calibration work (Oh et al., 2023; Tu et al., 2023) is the recognition that calibration learned on base classes does not transfer to novel classes, motivating a class-dependent temperature scaling that is agnostic to the specific prompt-tuning method used.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard temperature scaling—while fixing base-class miscalibration—actively worsens calibration on novel classes is a practical warning for anyone deploying fine-tuned CLIP in open-vocabulary scenarios. DAC's plug-and-play design (no hyperparameter tuning, negligible inference overhead) makes it immediately adoptable alongside any existing prompt-tuning pipeline. Open questions remain about whether the textual-deviation signal generalizes to larger VLMs (e.g., Flamingo, LLaVA), to non-CLIP architectures, and to settings where the base-to-novel split is not cleanly defined (e.g., truly open-ended generation).
