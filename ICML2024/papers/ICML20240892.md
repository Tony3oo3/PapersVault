## Problem

Existing multimodal LLMs predominantly operate on image-text data, leaving video adaptation underexplored due to the challenge of efficiently encoding spatiotemporal dynamics. 2D frame-level encoders miss temporal motion information, while 3D video tokenizers produce prohibitively long token sequences (e.g., 1280 tokens for a 2.2s clip), making large-scale video-language pre-training computationally infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240892/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For video input, clips are downsampled to 6 fps with 24 consecutive frames per segment; keyframes are resized to 224×224 and motion vectors (extracted via MPEG-4 I/P-frame decomposition at 16×16 macroblock granularity) are normalized to [-1,1] and resized to 20×36. The LLM consumes a unified 1D token sequence of up to 2048 tokens, where each video shot yields ~90 visual tokens plus 135 motion tokens delimited by [MOV]/[/MOV] special tokens. Outputs include text responses for understanding tasks, generated video clips (up to 1024×576 or 768×768 resolution, 24 frames per decoded segment), and generated images at 224×224.

## Method

Video-LaVIT decomposes each video into alternating keyframes and motion vectors extracted during MPEG-4 decoding, then tokenizes them separately: keyframes use an off-the-shelf LaVIT image tokenizer (ViT-G/14, 16384-entry codebook, ~90 tokens), while motion vectors pass through a VQ-VAE-based spatiotemporal encoder (12 transformer blocks with interleaved spatial and temporal attention, 1024-entry codebook with EMA updates, 135 tokens). A 3D U-Net detokenizer reconstructs video from the LLM's generated tokens, using enhanced motion conditioning that combines channel-wise concatenation of motion vectors with cross-attention from a dedicated motion feature encoder. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) tokenizer/detokenizer training on WebVid-10M, (2) unified autoregressive generative pre-training of a LLaMA 2 7B (initialized from LaVIT) on mixed video-image-text data, and (3) instruction tuning. For long video generation, an explicit noise constraint via DDIM inversion of the previous clip's last frame ensures temporal consistency across sequentially decoded segments.

## Results

On image understanding, Video-LaVIT (7B) achieves state-of-the-art results across 8 benchmarks, including 80.3 on VQA v2, 64.4 on GQA, 56.0 on VizWiz, and 1551.8 on MME, consistently outperforming LLaVA-1.5 (78.5, 62.0, 50.0, 1510.7) with the same base model. On zero-shot video question answering, it reaches 73.2% on MSVD-QA, 59.3% on MSRVTT-QA, and 50.1% on ActivityNet-QA, setting new SOTA on all three. For text-to-video generation on MSR-VTT, it achieves 188.36 FVD and 11.27 FID with only 10M public training clips, outperforming CogVideo (1294 FVD) and VideoPoet (213 FVD, 270M proprietary clips). On long video generation (64 frames), it achieves the best KVD score of 4.94, surpassing FreeNoise (6.07).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest same-size baseline LLaVA-1.5 (same LLaMA-2 7B backbone and instruction data), Video-LaVIT wins on all 8 image benchmarks, with the largest margins on VizWiz (+6.0), SEED (+5.4), and SQA (+3.2). Among video-language models, it outperforms Video-LLaVA by 2.5% on MSVD-QA (73.2 vs 70.7) and by 4.8% on ActivityNet-QA (50.1 vs 45.3), where explicit motion tokenization is most beneficial for recognizing diverse human actions. On text-to-video generation, Video-LaVIT (10M public clips) achieves 188.36 FVD on MSR-VTT, beating CogVideo (1294 FVD, 5.4M clips) and VideoPoet (213 FVD, 270M proprietary clips), while remaining competitive with SVD (242.02 FVD on UCF-101, 152M proprietary clips). The only metric where Video-LaVIT does not lead is the GPT-scored relative score on MSRVTT-QA (3.3 vs Video-LLaVA's 3.5), a 0.2-point gap. On long video generation, it achieves the best KVD (4.94) among all baselines including FreeNoise (6.07) and Gen-L-Video (21.06), validating the explicit noise constraint for temporal consistency across clips.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LaVIT framework for unified image-language pre-training to the video domain by introducing a decomposed video representation: keyframes (tokenized by LaVIT's existing image tokenizer) and motion vectors (tokenized by a new VQ-VAE-based spatiotemporal encoder). It departs from prior video-language models (Video-LLaVA, LLaMA-VID) that encode frames independently with 2D encoders, and from VideoPoet's 3D video tokenizer that produces prohibitively long token sequences (1280 tokens for 2.2s). The key conceptual departure is repurposing MPEG-4 motion vectors—normally a lossy compression byproduct—as the temporal signal for LLM pre-training, avoiding both expensive optical flow computation and the token explosion of 3D encoders. The detokenizer inherits the 3D U-Net architecture from Stable Video Diffusion, augmented with enhanced motion conditioning via cross-attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The keyframe-plus-motion-vector decomposition offers a practical path to video-language pre-training that is >90% more token-efficient than 3D video encoders while preserving temporal dynamics, making it feasible to process longer videos within standard LLM context windows. The fact that competitive results are achieved with only 10M public video clips (WebVid-10M) is notable given that competing methods like VideoPoet use 270M proprietary clips. However, the paper acknowledges significant limitations: the 4096 context window and short WebVid clips (~15s) constrain long-video generation, and the training cost (128 A100s, 60h for the LLM stage) remains prohibitive for web-scale video data. Open questions include whether the motion-vector approach generalizes to high-motion or scene-transition-heavy content, and whether joint exploitation of spatial and temporal redundancies can further reduce the token budget.
