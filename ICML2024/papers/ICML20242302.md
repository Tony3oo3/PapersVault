## Problem

Existing bandit LQR control algorithms assume a uniform strong convexity lower bound across all cost functions, requiring the learner to know the minimum curvature α_min before initialization. This assumption is hard to satisfy in practice and prevents adaptation to true per-round curvatures, causing significant performance degradation (e.g., from Õ(√T) to Õ(T^{2/3}) for smooth costs) when even a fraction of costs are merely convex rather than quadratic. The paper asks whether a single algorithm can be adaptive to heterogeneous curvatures while remaining robust to corrupted or decaying quadraticity.

## Input / Output

At each round, the learner receives a partial state observation y_t (not the full state x_t), a single scalar cost value c_t(y_t, u_t) (bandit feedback, no gradient), and the curvature α_t of the current cost function. The system matrices A, B, C are known in advance. The algorithm outputs a control action u_t via a Disturbance-Response Policy and achieves interpolated regret bounds that depend on the cumulative curvature α_{1:t} rather than a fixed minimum, recovering Õ(T^{3/4}) for purely convex Lipschitz costs, Õ(α^{-1/3}T^{2/3}) for quadratic Lipschitz costs, and Õ(α^{-1/2}√T) for quadratic smooth costs.

## Method

The method reduces bandit LQR to BCO with memory via a lossless 'with-history' reduction that integrates the full action history into the cost function, avoiding the hard-to-control truncation errors that self-concordant barriers (needed for bandit feedback with heterogeneous curvatures) would otherwise introduce. The reduced problem is solved by FTRL with self-concordant barriers in a lifted domain, using adaptive regularization coefficients λ_t that balance exploration and bias. The key technical contribution is a novel half-page stability analysis of the term ||w̄_t − w̄_{t+1}||_{H_t} using Newton decrement from interior-point methods, which simultaneously controls both the regret and the switching-cost (memory) terms. A lazy-update scheme (randomly selecting rounds where all H recent policies coincide) enables gradient estimation from the single scalar feedback, and the non-oblivious adversary introduced by the with-history reduction is handled because the comparator policy is deterministic under oblivious cost selection.

## Results

The paper achieves interpolated regret bounds for bandit LQR control that are adaptive to heterogeneous cost curvatures. For Lipschitz costs, the method recovers O~(T^{3/4}) for convex functions and achieves a new O~(T^{2/3}) for quadratic functions; for smooth costs, it recovers O~(T^{2/3}) for convex and O~(√T) for quadratic. Notably, the O~(√T) smooth bound is maintained even when O(T^{3/4}) cost functions are non-quadratic (corrupted quadraticity), and the O~(T^{2/3}) Lipschitz bound holds even when O(T^{8/9}) functions are convex. A single algorithm (Algorithm 2) handles all four curvature regimes without modification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three prior works in Table 1. Against Gradu et al. (2020), the method matches the O~(T^{3/4}) Lipschitz-convex bound but extends it to a new O~(T^{2/3}) Lipschitz-quadratic regime that was previously unaddressed. Against Cassel and Koren (2020), it matches the O~(T^{2/3}) smooth-convex bound and the O~(T^{3/4}) Lipschitz-convex bound, while additionally handling corrupted and decaying quadraticity. Against Sun et al. (2023), it matches the O~(√T) smooth-quadratic bound in T-dependence but improves the curvature dependence from O~(α^{-1}√T) to O~(α^{-1/2}√T), and crucially maintains the O~(√T) rate even when a large fraction (up to O(T^{3/4})) of cost functions lose their quadraticity — a robustness guarantee none of the baselines provide. The key differentiator is that a single algorithm with one set of hyperparameters (3.5) or (3.6) handles all four curvature regimes, whereas prior methods require separate algorithms for convex vs. quadratic costs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bandit LQR control literature (Gradu et al., 2020; Cassel and Koren, 2020; Sun et al., 2023) from homogeneous to heterogeneous cost curvatures, building directly on the FTRL-with-self-concordant-barriers algorithm of Luo et al. (2022) for adaptive BCO. The key methodological departure is replacing the truncation-based reduction (used in prior control-to-OCO reductions) with the lossless 'with-history' reduction from Sun et al. (2023), which is necessary because self-concordant barriers are unbounded near the domain boundary and would make truncation errors uncontrollable. The paper's central technical contribution is a simplified stability analysis using Newton decrement from interior-point methods, replacing a two-page proof-by-contradiction in Luo et al. (2022) with a half-page argument.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper removes a practical barrier in bandit LQR: prior algorithms require knowledge of the minimum curvature α_min before initialization, which is often unavailable and prevents adaptation to true curvatures. The interpolated guarantees also provide robustness to corrupted or decaying quadraticity (e.g., maintaining Õ(√T) regret even when O(T^{3/4}) costs are merely convex), which is relevant for real-world systems where cost structure may degrade over time. The techniques—particularly the Newton decrement stability analysis and the with-history reduction—are explicitly noted as general beyond this specific problem. An important open direction identified is handling homogeneous but unknown curvatures with bandit feedback, which remains unsolved even in pure BCO.
