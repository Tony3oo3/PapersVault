## Problem

Traditional ASR systems in voice assistants are trained on isolated single-turn utterances and cannot adapt to conversational context or leverage implicit user feedback (e.g., reformulations after misrecognition). Existing context-aware approaches—fine-tuning, model personalization, and contextual biasing—require additional supervised data, per-user parameter storage, or context at inference time, introducing latency overhead and data-labeling costs that are often unjustified by marginal gains.

## Input / Output

The teacher model ingests a target utterance's audio features (30 ms frame rate) alongside past/future audio context (preceding and succeeding user queries) and past/future text context (ASR one-best hypotheses and assistant responses), producing a word-piece token transcript via a non-streaming Conformer transducer. The distilled student model consumes only the single target utterance's audio with causal (past-only) attention, producing the same word-piece output at minimal latency. Context is available exclusively during teacher training and distillation label generation, never at student inference time.

## Method

The framework has two stages. First, a context-aware teacher (200M or 1B Conformer transducer) is fine-tuned with three complementary signals: explicit audio context via feature concatenation or multi-head self-attention over context-encoded embeddings, explicit text context via learned sentence-piece embeddings or BERT summary vectors, and implicit context via the PF-CLC contrastive objective augmented by Ohm—a novel online hard-negative mining procedure that uses BIRCH clustering over a streaming buffer and reservoir sampling to construct semantically coherent local batches under restricted per-GPU batch sizes (≤16). Second, the teacher's context-informed hypotheses on reformulation dialogues form a semi-supervised single-utterance dataset (SSRD, ~25K hours) used to distill knowledge into a streaming student model, transferring context-awareness into a context-free architecture with no additional inference compute. Reformulation up-sampling (1:5 ratio) further emphasizes implicit feedback during training.

## Results

On the OD3 dataset, the full model (context + CLC + Ohm) achieves 8.73 WER (26.2% relative WERR) overall and 13.21 WER (30.8% WERR) on repeat/rephrase utterances, compared to a baseline of 11.90 and 19.09 WER respectively. On the closed-source real-world dialog data, the best 200M teacher model (feature-concatenation audio context + learned text embeddings + CLC/Ohm) achieves 6.91% WERR on the ALL set and 9.58% WERR on the reformulation (REF) set. After distillation to a context-free 1B student model, the system retains up to 31.94% distillation efficiency on the REF set, achieving 3.06% WERR without any runtime context.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed 200M model outperforms all same-scale or smaller baselines on OD3: it beats Whisper S (200M) by 2.51 WER points overall (8.73 vs 11.24) and by 0.96 points on repeat/rephrase (13.21 vs 14.17), and surpasses the CLC (200M) baseline by 0.26 WER overall and 0.60 on repeat/rephrase. Against much larger models, the 200M system is within 0.22 WER of Whisper L (1.3B) overall (8.73 vs 8.51) but trails by 0.84 WER on repeat/rephrase (13.21 vs 12.37), suggesting the larger model still has an edge on the hardest subset. The gap over Conformer (100M) and Wav2Vec 2 (433M) is dramatic—over 10 WER points—highlighting the benefit of dialog-context training over generic pre-training. On the closed-source real-world data, the context-aware teacher achieves up to 9.58% WERR on reformulation utterances, and the distilled student retains ~4% relative WER improvement without any runtime context, confirming that training-time context learning transfers to context-free inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PF-CLC contrastive learning framework from Chan et al. (2024) by introducing Ohm, an online hard-negative mining procedure that makes contrastive losses practical under the small per-GPU batch sizes typical of production ASR training. It combines this implicit context learning with explicit audio/text context modeling (feature concatenation, learned embeddings) in a Conformer-transducer teacher, then distills the resulting context-aware knowledge into a context-free streaming student via semi-supervised training on reformulation dialogues. The approach departs from prior contextual biasing and model personalization methods by requiring no additional context at inference time, and from prior distillation work (Masumura et al., 2021) by incorporating both audio and text context plus contrastive self-supervision rather than text-only context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap in production ASR: the ability to learn from conversational context and user reformulations without incurring inference-time latency or storage costs for context. The finding that a 200M context-trained model outperforms a 1B non-context model, and that context knowledge survives distillation into a context-free student (~4% WERR retained), suggests that training-time context is a more efficient path to robustness than model scaling. Open questions include the observed negative transfer on low-diversity domains (home automation, -22.68% WERR), where context over-reliance degrades performance, and how to dynamically gate context usage. The OD3 results (26% WERR) are on semi-synthetic data, so real-world generalization of the full pipeline remains to be validated at scale.
