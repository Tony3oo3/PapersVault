## Problem

Existing sequential disentanglement models condition both static and dynamic latent codes on the entire input sequence, causing information leakage where dynamic vectors encode static content (or vice versa). Prior fixes—drastically reducing dynamic dimension or adding mutual information loss terms—introduce tuning difficulties, limit expressivity for complex dynamics, and require domain-specific data augmentation. The paper asks whether a biased architecture with a simpler loss can eliminate leakage without these complications.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a sequence x_{1:T} of length T (64×64 RGB video frames, multivariate time-series windows, or 20-frame audio spectrograms) and outputs a single static code s, a sequence of dynamic codes d_{1:T}, and a reconstruction of the input. Unusually, the static code is extracted from only one element (x_1) via a non-recurrent encoder, while the dynamic path processes the remaining elements through an LSTM. The encoder is explicitly non-recurrent—each x_t is processed independently—so temporal structure enters only through the LSTM on the dynamic branch.

## Method

The key architectural insight is a subtraction inductive bias: the non-recurrent encoder produces per-element representations g_{1:T}, and the dynamic LSTM receives {g_1, g_{2:T} − g_1}, so static features present in g_{2:T} are explicitly removed before the recurrent module can encode them. The static posterior q(s|x_1; φ_s) is a simple MLP (linear + tanh) applied to g_1, decoupled from the sequence-level dynamics. The training objective contains only two hyperparameters (α weighting the x_1 reconstruction term and β scaling the KL regularizers) and no mutual information or contrastive loss terms, making it substantially simpler than baselines like C-DSVAE or SPYL. The dynamic prior is a recurrent Gaussian (LSTM-based), and both static and dynamic codes are sampled via the reparametrization trick before being concatenated and fed to a modality-specific decoder.

## Results

On the MUG video dataset, the method achieves state-of-the-art disentanglement with 86.90% dynamic accuracy, IS=5.598, H(y|x)=0.041, and H(y)=1.782, surpassing the previous best (SPYL: 85.71%, 5.548, 0.066, 1.779). On PhysioNet mortality prediction, it achieves AUPRC=0.473 and AUROC=0.858, outperforming all baselines including the raw-feature random forest (0.446/0.802). On ETTh1 temperature forecasting, it reaches MAE=11.21, and on TIMIT speaker verification it obtains a disentanglement gap of 31.11% (static EER 3.50%, dynamic EER 34.62%), both SOTA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is compared against 11 baselines spanning VAE-based (DSVAE, C-DSVAE, SPYL, S3VAE), GAN-based (MoCoGAN), Wasserstein-based (R-WAE), Koopman-based (SKD), and time-series-specific (GP-VAE, GLR) approaches. On MUG, it outperforms the strongest baseline SPYL by 1.19% in accuracy (86.90% vs 85.71%), 0.05 in IS, and 0.025 in H(y|x), while on Sprites it ties SPYL at 100% accuracy. On PhysioNet, it surpasses SPYL (AUPRC 0.473 vs 0.367) and even the raw-feature random forest baseline (0.473 vs 0.446), a notable result since prior disentanglement methods underperformed raw features. On ETTh1, it achieves the lowest MAE (11.21) versus SPYL (12.22) and GLR (12.27). On TIMIT, it improves the disentanglement gap over SPYL from 29.81% to 31.11%. On the challenging Jesters gesture dataset, it more than doubles SPYL's dynamic classification score (68.2% vs 27%). In the information leakage analysis (Table 2), it achieves the best leakage gaps (78.57% static, 84.01% dynamic) over SPYL (71.8%, 82.08%), confirming superior separation of static and dynamic information.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sequential disentanglement VAE lineage initiated by DSVAE (Li & Mandt, 2018) and its successors (S3VAE, C-DSVAE, SPYL), which all condition the static posterior on the full input sequence and rely on mutual information loss terms or dimension constraints to combat information leakage. The key departure is an architectural inductive bias: conditioning the static code on a single sequence element and subtracting its representation from the dynamic path, which eliminates the need for MI regularization, contrastive estimation, or restrictive latent dimensions. This positions the work as a simplification of the variational framework rather than a new modeling paradigm, drawing on the broader principle that tailored architectures (CNNs, attention) can encode inductive biases more effectively than loss-function engineering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is a training objective with only two hyperparameters (α, β) and no data augmentation, making the method easier to deploy across modalities without domain-specific tuning. The assumption that the static factor must be present in every sequence element is a limitation that the authors acknowledge but that the current literature shares entirely. Open questions include whether the single-sample conditioning generalizes to settings with missing or corrupted frames, whether the subtraction bias transfers to diffusion-based backbones (which the authors flag as future work), and whether the approach scales to multi-factor disentanglement beyond the single static + single dynamic decomposition.
