## Problem

Existing policy gradient methods for robust MDPs with s-rectangular ambiguity sets, such as the double-loop DRPG, require an inner loop that solves a sequence of nonconcave subproblems over the ambiguity set with increasing accuracy, imposing a heavy computational burden and worse-than-O(1/ε⁴) convergence complexity. No single-loop policy gradient method with a global optimality guarantee had been established for this setting, and the convergence analysis of the underlying nonconvex-nonconcave min-max problem under gradient dominance (as opposed to concavity or KL conditions) remained unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method operates on tabular MDPs with finite state and action spaces, taking as input an s-rectangular ambiguity set (a Cartesian product of per-state convex sets over transition probability matrices), a bounded cost function, a discount factor γ ∈ (0,1), and an initial state distribution ρ. It outputs a robust policy π that minimizes the worst-case expected discounted cost over the ambiguity set, along with the corresponding worst-case transition kernel p. The s-rectangular structure allows nature to choose the transition kernel for each state independently of the action, which is less conservative than the (s,a)-rectangular assumption while preserving tractability.

## Method

SRPG is a single-loop primal-dual algorithm that alternates gradient descent on the policy π and gradient ascent on the transition kernel p using a regularized objective χ(π, p, π̄, p̄) = J_ρ(π,p) + (r₁/2)‖π−π̄‖² − (r₂/2)‖p−p̄‖², where π̄ and p̄ are auxiliary variables updated via exponential averaging. The Moreau-Yosida smoothing via the quadratic terms simultaneously stabilizes both the primal and dual updates, avoiding the oscillation and divergence issues of vanilla gradient descent-ascent in the nonconvex-nonconcave regime. The key theoretical contribution is establishing O(1/ε⁴) convergence to a globally optimal policy under the gradient dominance property of the dual function—a condition that is weaker than concavity or the KL property and is shown to hold for RMDPs with s-rectangular ambiguity sets. The analysis also reveals a regional exponential growth property of the objective, explaining the empirically observed faster initial convergence that degenerates to O(1/ε⁴) near the optimum.

## Results

SRPG achieves the same theoretical convergence rate of O(1/ε⁴) as DRPG but with significantly lower per-iteration computational cost, since it avoids the nested inner-loop optimization over the ambiguity set. In the parameterized inventory management setting (Table 2), SRPG reduces runtime by 4–15× compared to DRPG: for example, at S=500, A=20, SRPG takes 3,737 s versus DRPG's 35,109 s, and at S=300, A=50, SRPG takes 2,114 s versus DRPG's 32,343 s. On GARNET MDPs and the inventory management problem, SRPG converges consistently faster and more smoothly than DRPG across all 8 tasks, while DRPG exhibits noticeable oscillation particularly in the (s,a)-rectangular setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole experimental baseline is DRPG (Wang et al., 2023), a double-loop robust policy gradient method that requires solving a nonconcave inner maximization over the ambiguity set at each outer iteration. SRPG outperforms DRPG on every tested configuration: on GARNET MDPs (both s-rectangular and (s,a)-rectangular ambiguity sets, GARNET(5,6,3) and GARNET(10,5,10)), SRPG reaches lower worst-case expected cost φ(πₖ) at every iteration count and converges without the oscillation pattern visible in DRPG, especially pronounced in the (s,a)-rectangular case. On the inventory management problem with transition-kernel-only parameterization, SRPG again converges faster and more stably. In the full parameterization setting (both policy and transition kernel), SRPG achieves the same objective value as DRPG in 4–15× less wall-clock time (Table 2), with the largest speedup (15.3×) at S=300, A=50. The computational advantage stems from SRPG's single-loop design: its projection cost scales as O(ε⁻⁴(M_π + M_p)) versus DRPG's O(ε⁻⁴(M_π + M_p·ε⁻²)), since DRPG must repeatedly solve the inner problem to increasing precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Moreau-Yosida smoothing framework from single-loop min-max optimization (Zhang et al., 2020; Zheng et al., 2023) to the RMDP setting, replacing the double-loop DRPG of Wang et al. (2023) with a single-loop primal-dual scheme. The key theoretical departure is proving convergence for a nonconvex-nonconcave min-max problem using the gradient dominance property of the dual function, rather than relying on the KL property or concavity of the max component as in prior min-max analyses. The paper also establishes that the Moreau envelope of a weakly convex, gradient-dominated function inherits the gradient dominance property (Theorem 3.2), extending results from Yu et al. (2022) on Bregman envelopes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SRPG provides the first single-loop policy gradient method for s-rectangular RMDPs with a global optimality guarantee, eliminating the computationally expensive inner-loop subproblem solving that plagues DRPG. The convergence analysis for nonconvex-nonconcave min-max via gradient dominance is of independent interest beyond RMDPs. However, the iteration complexity remains O(1/ε⁴), matching DRPG, and the method is restricted to tabular, s-rectangular settings; extending to function approximation, noisy gradients, and non-rectangular ambiguity sets remains open. The regional exponential growth property offers a useful explanation for the observed fast initial convergence but does not improve the worst-case rate.
