## Problem

CLIP is extraordinarily vulnerable to targeted data poisoning and backdoor attacks during pre-training, with as little as 0.0001% poisoned data sufficient for successful attacks—four orders of magnitude less than supervised models. The only existing pre-training defense, RoCLIP, causes a ~10% drop in downstream performance, while other proposed methods target fine-tuning rather than pre-training. This paper addresses the gap of protecting CLIP during pre-training against strong attacks without sacrificing model quality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes large-scale image-caption pairs (RGB images at 224×224 with natural language captions) that may contain a small fraction of poisoned examples (up to 0.05% for TDPA, 0.15% for BA). During training, the pipeline partitions the data into safe and risky subsets based on a GMM fit to image-caption cosine similarities, applying different losses to each. The output is a pre-trained CLIP model (ResNet-50 or ViT-B/32 image encoder + transformer text encoder) evaluated via zero-shot classification (cosine similarity between image and text-prompt embeddings) and linear-probe classification on 10 downstream datasets.

## Method

SAFECLIP operates in three phases: (1) a 5-epoch unimodal contrastive learning warmup on image and text modalities separately, using a nearest-neighbor pool to find positive pairs, which clusters similar images/captions and pushes poisoned pairs apart before cross-modal association; (2) a single pass of CLIP loss over all examples at a very low learning rate (5e-6) to weakly associate image-caption representations while preserving the separation created by warmup; (3) iterative pre-training where a two-component GMM fit to cosine similarities partitions data into a safe set (probability > 0.9 of belonging to the high-similarity component) and a risky set, with CLIP loss applied only to the augmented safe set and unimodal CL applied to the risky set, while the safe set grows by 1% per epoch. This reduces the effective poison rate in the safe set from 0.05% to as low as 0.000375%, breaking the attack while preserving downstream performance.

## Results

SAFECLIP reduces the attack success rate (ASR) of targeted data poisoning attacks from 93.75% to 0% and backdoor attacks from up to 100% to 0% across CC1M, CC3M, MSCOCO, and Visual Genome, using a single set of hyperparameters (s=1%, r=5, lr_low=5e-6, t=0.9). On downstream performance (CC3M pre-training), SAFECLIP achieves +0.9% average zero-shot and +0.5% average linear-probe accuracy over vanilla CLIP across 10 datasets, while RoCLIP suffers a ~10% drop in both metrics. Even at higher poison rates (0.1%–0.5%), SAFECLIP maintains 0% ASR on MSCOCO for both TDPA and BadNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to vanilla CLIP, SAFECLIP eliminates virtually all attack success: TDPA ASR drops from 93.75% to 0% (CC1M/CC3M) and 62.5% to 0% (MSCOCO/VG), while backdoor ASR drops from up to 100% to 0–5%. Against RoCLIP, the only prior pre-training defense, SAFECLIP is strictly more consistent: RoCLIP still leaks 37.5% ASR on VG TDPA and 9.6% on VG Blended, whereas SAFECLIP achieves 0% and 5% respectively. Critically, SAFECLIP preserves downstream performance (average +0.9% zero-shot, +0.5% linear-probe over CLIP on 10 datasets), while RoCLIP degrades performance by approximately 10% on both zero-shot and linear-probe evaluations. SAFECLIP also generalizes to higher poison rates (0.1%–0.5%) and multi-trigger backdoor attacks (HTBA, PBA All2One, PBA All2All) with 0% ASR, scenarios not covered by RoCLIP.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAFECLIP extends CLIP's pre-training pipeline (Radford et al., 2021) by inserting unimodal contrastive learning warmup and a GMM-based safe/risky data partition before the main CLIP loss training. It draws on nearest-neighbor contrastive learning (Dwibedi et al., 2021) to enrich unimodal representations and prevent poisoned examples from clustering. Conceptually, it departs from RoCLIP (Yang & Mirzasoleiman, 2023), the only prior pre-training defense, which attempts to disassociate poisoned pairs via random caption matching at the cost of ~10% downstream performance, and from post-hoc cleaning methods (Bansal et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2023) that require a clean reference model or additional large-scale data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that merely 0.0001% of CLIP pre-training data suffices for a successful targeted poisoning attack (four orders of magnitude less than supervised learning) exposes a critical vulnerability in web-scale vision-language pre-training that is trivially exploitable in practice. SAFECLIP is the first pre-training defense that neutralizes both targeted poisoning and multiple backdoor attack variants to 0% ASR while preserving CLIP's downstream accuracy, making it practically deployable. Open questions include whether the GMM threshold and 1% safe-set increment generalize to the 400M+ pair scale of production CLIP, how the method behaves under adaptive attacks that specifically target the unimodal CL warmup, and whether the approach extends to other contrastive pre-training frameworks beyond CLIP.
