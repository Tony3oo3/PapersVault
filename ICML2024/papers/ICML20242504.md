## Problem

Traditional economic models offer limited foresight into policy outcomes, and human policymakers often have incentives misaligned with the public interest. While AI-based approaches to economic simulation exist (e.g., the AI Economist, Human-Centered Mechanism Design), no general framework unifies voting on policy objectives with the design of a full multi-agent economic environment, rather than a single-shot mechanism. This paper proposes Social Environment Design as a research agenda to bridge this gap, connecting Reinforcement Learning, EconCS, and Computational Social Choice communities.

## Input / Output

The framework takes as input latent type vectors from n agents (representing their values/preferences) and the principal's own type, which are aggregated via a social choice function into a social welfare objective. The principal then outputs a parameterized POMG (the Induced Economy) by choosing an action φ from a constrained action space, where the constraint D(φ₀, φ) ≤ δ limits how far the new environment can deviate from the previous round, and a continuity constraint forces the new POMG's initial state to equal the terminal state of the prior round. In the Apple Picking example, the principal's output is a vector of tax rates per bracket, and the objective value is an interpolation between utilitarian (total apples) and egalitarian (minimum apples) welfare.

## Method

The Social Environment Design Game is formalized as a one-leader, n-follower online Stackelberg-Markov Game played over repeated rounds. Each round consists of a Voting Mechanism (a social choice function f mapping type profiles to a welfare function w) followed by a Stackelberg Game where the principal commits to an action φ that induces a parameterized POMG via a policy implementation map P, and n follower agents then play strategically within that POMG to maximize their own discounted returns. The principal's optimization is a constrained Stackelberg problem (maximize w subject to divergence and continuity constraints), which can be reparameterized into an unconstrained problem over a reparameterized space. The framework is instantiated as an Apple Picking Game built on Melting Pot 2.0, where PPO-trained player agents with parameter sharing and GAE interact in a sequential social dilemma, while a PPO-trained principal adjusts tax brackets over 50-timestep tax periods, with a two-phase curriculum using tax annealing to stabilize training.

## Results

This is a position paper proposing a new theoretical framework (Social Environment Design) and does not report quantitative experimental results. The authors explicitly state: 'We do not include experimental results here, as the primary purpose of this paper is to propose a future research agenda and illustrate open problems.' The only concrete artifact is a Sequential Social Dilemma Environment (Apple Picking Game) built in Melting Pot 2.0 with 7 agents, 64 initial apples, 3 tax brackets, and a 50-timestep tax period, but no performance metrics are reported.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare its framework against any baselines or competing methods, as it is a position paper laying out a research agenda rather than an empirical contribution. The authors position their work relative to prior research (e.g., Human-Centered Mechanism Design by Koster et al. 2022, the AI Economist by Zheng et al. 2022, and Stackelberg-POMDP by Brero et al. 2022) but only in terms of scope and generality, not quantitative performance. The key differentiators claimed are: (1) a fully general POMG environment rather than a restricted public-goods setting, (2) voting over Principal objectives rather than over mechanisms, and (3) iterative environment redesign rather than a single-shot mechanism. No head-to-head comparisons or benchmark evaluations are provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-agent environment design framework of Zhang et al. (2009) into a multi-agent, sequential setting by combining Stackelberg-Markov games (building on Brero et al.'s Stackelberg-POMDP and Gerstgrasser & Parkes' meta-learning approach) with a voting mechanism drawn from computational social choice. It explicitly differentiates from Human Centered Mechanism Design (Koster et al., 2022) by operating over general POMG environments rather than single-shot linear public goods mechanisms, and by voting on welfare objectives rather than on candidate mechanisms. The AI Economist (Zheng et al., 2022) is the closest prior system, but SED adds democratic objective selection and iterative rounds with continuity constraints, positioning itself as a strict generalization of both mechanism design and the AI Economist's fixed-objective two-level MARL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a position paper, its primary contribution is a formal research agenda at the intersection of MARL, mechanism design, and computational social choice, with a concrete (if small-scale) Apple Picking implementation to ground the abstractions. The most critical open problems it identifies—convergence guarantees for iterative Stackelberg-POMG equilibria, scaling to millions of agents, and incorporating bounded rationality and cognitive biases into follower agents—remain entirely unresolved and would require substantial algorithmic advances. The framework's practical impact is currently speculative: no empirical validation of policy quality is presented, and the gap between a 7-agent toy environment and real-world economic simulation is vast, making the long-term feasibility of the agenda uncertain.
