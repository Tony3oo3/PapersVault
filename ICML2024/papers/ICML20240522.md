## Problem

In federated learning with highly heterogeneous (non-IID) data, client drift and biased local updates—especially in the final classification layer—severely degrade convergence speed and accuracy, a problem that is particularly acute in cross-device settings with thousands of clients. Existing optimization-based FL methods (FedAvg, Scaffold, FedAvgM) struggle with destructive interference during aggregation and require many communication rounds, while prior closed-form approaches like Nearest Class Mean classifiers underperform on complex datasets. The paper asks whether a principled, exact-aggregation classifier can be trained in a federated setting that is provably immune to statistical heterogeneity and dramatically more efficient than gradient-based alternatives.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (resized to 224×224) that are passed through a frozen, ImageNet-pretrained MobileNetV2 feature extractor to produce fixed-dimensional feature vectors; the classifier operates on these feature maps rather than raw pixels. Output is a class label predicted by a one-vs-rest Ridge Regression linear classifier (or its random-features kernelized variant), with per-class weight vectors normalized by their L2 norm to handle class imbalance. In the FED3R+FT variant, the RR solution is converted into a softmax classifier initialization (with temperature calibration) before joint fine-tuning, ultimately producing a standard neural network classifier.

## Method

FED3R repurposes the online/recursive formulation of Ridge Regression for the federated setting: each client computes two local statistics from its pre-trained feature maps—the covariance matrix A_k = Z_k^T Z_k and the cross-term b_k = Z_k^T Y_k—and uploads them to the server, which sums them across all clients and solves the closed-form system (A + λI)^{-1} b to obtain the global classifier. Because the aggregation is an exact sum, the solution is mathematically identical to the centralized RR solution regardless of how data is partitioned across clients, making it invariant to statistical heterogeneity and sampling order; each client needs to be sampled only once, requiring exactly ⌈K/κ⌉ rounds. FED3R-RF extends this by first mapping features through a random-features approximation of an RBF kernel to handle non-linear separability, at the cost of larger statistics (D² vs d²). FED3R+FT then uses the RR solution as a stable softmax initialization and fine-tunes either the full model, only the classifier, or only the feature extractor (keeping the RR classifier fixed) with a standard FL optimizer, which the paper shows reduces destructive interference and yields more discriminative features in heterogeneous cross-device settings.

## Results

On Landmarks, FED3R-RF (10k random features) achieves 56.6% top-1 accuracy, and with fine-tuning via Scaffold (FED3R+FT), the best result reaches 67.4 ± 0.3%. On iNaturalist, FED3R-RF achieves 47.6% and FED3R+FTFEAT (FedAvgM) reaches 51.5 ± 0.2%. In terms of efficiency, FED3R-RF (10k) requires only 27.3 rounds to reach 40% accuracy on Landmarks, compared to 528.7 rounds for FedAvg (×19.3 speedup) and 2251.3 for FedAvg-LP (×82.4 speedup), while using up to two orders of magnitude less communication and computation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FED3R and FED3R-RF consistently outperform FedAvg, FedAvgM, and Scaffold (and their LP variants) in convergence speed, communication cost, and computation cost across both Landmarks and iNaturalist, with speedups ranging from ×10.5 (vs. Scaffold) to ×82.4 (vs. FedAvg-LP) in rounds to reach a target accuracy. On Landmarks, FED3R-RF (10k) surpasses Scaffold-LP in accuracy while requiring two orders of magnitude less communication and computation. On iNaturalist, FED3R-RF surpasses all LP and full-training baselines by a substantial margin across every evaluation criterion. Against FedNCM (the closest prior closed-form classifier), FED3R-RF wins by up to 20 accuracy points (56.6% vs. 36.2% on Landmarks; 47.6% vs. 32.2% on iNaturalist). In the fine-tuning stage (FED3R+FT), the FED3R-initialized variants outperform the same FL algorithms without FED3R initialization, with the FTFEAT variant (fixed classifier, fine-tuned features) being most robust in the high-heterogeneity iNaturalist setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the online/recursive formulation of Ridge Regression (Rifkin et al., 2003; Kailath et al., 2000) for the federated setting, exploiting the associative property of the sufficient statistics (A and b matrices) to show that exact federated aggregation recovers the centralized closed-form solution. It departs from the dominant gradient-based FL paradigm (FedAvg, Scaffold, FedAvgM) by eliminating iterative local optimization entirely, and from prior classifier-calibration approaches (Luo et al., 2021; Legate et al., 2023a) by training the classifier in a distributed manner from the start rather than retraining it post-hoc. The kernelized variant (FED3R-RF) builds on Random Features (Rahimi & Recht, 2007) to approximate KRR without the O(n²) kernel matrix bottleneck.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-orders-of-magnitude reduction in communication and computation costs, combined with exact convergence in ⌈K/κ⌉ rounds and provable immunity to statistical heterogeneity, makes FED3R a compelling lightweight alternative for cross-device FL where gradient-based methods struggle. The finding that fixing the RR classifier while fine-tuning only the feature extractor (FED3R+FTFEAT) yields better feature quality than full fine-tuning in highly heterogeneous settings challenges the default practice of updating all layers jointly. Limitations include reliance on pre-trained features, restriction to linear (or kernelized) classifiers, and the fact that the fine-tuning stage still requires a standard FL algorithm; open questions include extension to streaming data, personalized FL, and non-convex classifier objectives.
