## Problem

While Linear Mode Connectivity (LMC) and Layerwise Linear Feature Connectivity (LLFC) have been established for models trained on the same task via the spawning method, it remained unknown whether models finetuned on *different* downstream tasks from a common pretrained checkpoint exhibit any linear structure. The paper addresses this gap by discovering that although LMC fails in the pretraining-finetuning paradigm (loss landscapes are not connected), the internal features are in fact approximately linearly connected across tasks — a stronger property than previously known LLFC.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained checkpoint θ_PT and a set of finetuned models {θ_i} obtained by finetuning θ_PT on distinct downstream tasks (e.g., different rotation angles of MNIST, disjoint CIFAR-100 class splits, or different ImageNet/T5 tasks). For each pair (or set) of finetuned models, it computes per-layer cosine similarity and projection-coefficient metrics between features of the weight-interpolated model and the linear interpolation of individual model features, evaluated on the union of the corresponding task datasets. The output is a quantitative verification of CTL at every layer, plus derived feature-space decompositions that explain model averaging and task arithmetic operations.

## Method

The paper formalizes Cross-Task Linearity (CTL): for finetuned models θ_i and θ_j sharing a pretrained initialization, f^(ℓ)(αθ_i + (1−α)θ_j) ≈ αf^(ℓ)(θ_i) + (1−α)f^(ℓ)(θ_j) at every layer ℓ, which is a strict strengthening of LLFC (proportionality → approximate equality). Empirical validation spans MLPs on Rotated MNIST, ResNet-18 on Split CIFAR-100, ViT-B/32 and ViT-L/14 on ImageNet/CIFAR-10, and T5 on text tasks, confirming CTL holds across architectures and modalities. Building on CTL and its conjectured transitivity, the paper derives Theorem 4.2 (multi-model generalization explaining model averaging as feature averaging) and Theorem 4.3 (connecting CTL to weight disentanglement, explaining task arithmetic addition and negation as feature-space aggregation and subtraction). A theoretical bound (Theorem 5.1) shows the CTL deviation is O(α(1−α)λ_max‖θ_i−θ_j‖²/2), linking emergence to landscape flatness and weight proximity, while ablation experiments identify pretraining duration and pretraining–finetuning task similarity as the key factors promoting CTL.

## Results

The paper provides empirical evidence that Cross-Task Linearity (CTL) consistently holds for finetuned models across diverse settings (MLP/ResNet-18 on Rotated MNIST and Split CIFAR-100; ViT-B/32 and T5 on ImageNet and text datasets). Specifically, the cosine similarity between features in the weight-interpolated model and the linear interpolation of features in two finetuned models approaches 1 (i.e., 1−cosine approaches 0) across layers and interpolation coefficients α ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 0.75}, while the coefficient ratio (measuring feature length agreement) concentrates around 1. The paper further validates that model averaging features approximate the average of individual model features (Equation 1), and that task arithmetic addition/negation in parameter space translates to corresponding operations in feature space (Equations 2–4). Root-cause analysis shows that increasing pretraining epochs and higher pretraining–finetuning task similarity both promote CTL emergence, while the number of finetuning epochs and inter-task similarity have minimal effect.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a discovery/analysis paper rather than a method paper, so it does not benchmark a proposed model against competing baselines on standard accuracy metrics. Instead, it positions CTL relative to two prior connectivity notions: LMC (Frankle et al., 2020) and LLFC (Zhou et al., 2023). LMC requires models trained on the same task with approximately equal loss, and the paper confirms LMC fails in the pretraining-finetuning paradigm when tasks differ. LLFC (Zhou et al., 2023) establishes proportionality of features under weight interpolation for same-task models; CTL extends this to cross-task settings and strengthens the relation from proportionality to approximate equality. The paper also connects to task arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2023) and model averaging (Wortsman et al., 2022a), showing that CTL provides a feature-space explanation for both: model averaging accuracy linearly correlates with logits ensemble accuracy (Figure 4), and the stitched-model experiments (Figures 9–10) confirm that feature-space addition/negation reproduces the multi-task and forgetting behaviors observed in parameter-space task arithmetic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Linear Mode Connectivity (LMC) and Layerwise Linear Feature Connectivity (LLFC) framework from Zhou et al. (2023) and Frankle et al. (2020) from same-task to cross-task settings, discovering that while LMC fails for finetuned models on different tasks, a stronger feature-space property (CTL) holds. It also builds on the task vector formalism from Ilharco et al. (2023) and the weight-averaging framework from Wortsman et al. (2022), providing unified feature-space explanations for both task arithmetic and model averaging. Notably, the paper departs from the linearization hypothesis rejected by Ortiz-Jimenez et al. (2023) by establishing an *approximate* linearity (CTL) rather than strict linearity, and connects CTL to the Weight Disentanglement condition proposed in that work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that pretrained-then-finetuned networks act as approximate linear maps from parameter space to feature space provides a unified mechanistic explanation for two widely-used model merging techniques (model averaging and task arithmetic), bridging a gap between parameter-space operations and their feature-space effects. Key open questions include whether CTL extends to Large Language Models (explicitly deferred by the authors), whether the transitivity conjecture (Conjecture 4.1) can be formally proven, and whether the preliminary Taylor-expansion bound (Theorem 5.1) can be tightened into a rigorous proof. The practical implication is that feature-space reasoning may guide better merging strategies, though the paper is primarily diagnostic rather than proposing new algorithms.
