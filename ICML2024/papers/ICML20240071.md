## Problem

While prior work characterized the SGD time complexity for single-sample updates (n_b=1) in terms of the target's information exponent ℓ, the finite batch size regime—how n_b interacts with the learning rate and target hardness to determine training iterations—remained largely unexplored. This paper reveals a fundamental limitation of one-pass SGD: beyond a critical batch size of order d^(ℓ/2), negative self-interaction feedback terms dominate the dynamics and prevent any further reduction in training time, a regime that was previously uncharacterized.

## Input / Output

The method consumes high-dimensional Gaussian covariates z ~ N(0, I_d) with labels y = h*(W*z) + √Δ·ξ in a strict one-pass, sample-splitting protocol where each batch of size n_b = n_0·d^μ is used exactly once with learning rate γ = γ_0·d^(−δ). The output is a complete theoretical characterization: the iteration count T = T_0·d^θ for weak recovery as a function of the exponents (μ, δ, ℓ), a phase diagram delineating SGD-possible, SGD-impossible, and Correlation-loss-possible regimes, and a closed-form system of low-dimensional ODEs governing the exact asymptotic training dynamics. The analysis is conducted in the limit d → ∞ with k = O(1) relevant target directions.

## Method

The core technique tracks training dynamics through sufficient statistics—neuron-target overlaps M_t and neuron norms Q_t—which by Gaussian concentration reduce the infinite-dimensional projected SGD process to a finite system of deterministic ODEs. The key mechanistic insight is that the spherical gradient update decomposes into a signal component and a self-interaction term ⟨g_t, w_t⟩ acting as random weight decay; when n_b ≫ d^(ℓ/2), this term becomes extensive and the dynamics enter a 'self-interaction regime' where standard SGD cannot achieve weak recovery at the optimal rate. To overcome this, the paper introduces Correlation loss SGD, replacing the MSE loss with ℓ̃ = (1/n_b)Σ(1 − y^ν f(z^ν)), which suppresses the self-interaction term and extends the beneficial batch size range to n_b = O(d^(ℓ−1)), reducing iterations to polylog(d). Proofs combine Hermite polynomial expansions of the activation and target, Orlicz-space concentration for noise control, and a one-step recovery argument for the large learning rate regime, with the ODE description showing that batch-size effects enter only through intra-batch correlation terms that vanish asymptotically.

## Results

The paper establishes that for one-pass projected SGD on two-layer networks, the optimal batch size for minimizing iteration time is n_b = O(d^{ℓ/2}), where ℓ is the information exponent of the target. Below this threshold, the number of iterations scales as T = I(ℓ)/n_b (e.g., T = O(d^{ℓ-1}/n_b) for ℓ > 2), while beyond it, vanilla SGD enters a 'self-interaction regime' where it cannot achieve weak recovery at all. Correlation Loss SGD overcomes this limitation, restoring T = O(d^{ℓ-1}/n_b) for batch sizes up to n_b = O(d^{ℓ-1}), and the one-step giant-step protocol achieves recovery in T = 1 with n_b = O(d^ℓ) samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four training protocols across batch size regimes: vanilla projected SGD, Correlation Loss SGD, and one-step giant-step GD. For ℓ > 2 and batch sizes in the self-interaction regime (d^{ℓ/2} ≪ n_b ≤ d^{ℓ-1}), vanilla SGD fundamentally fails to achieve weak recovery, while Correlation Loss SGD succeeds with T = O(d^{ℓ-1}/n_b) iterations and the same total sample complexity N = O(d^{ℓ-1}) as the n_b = 1 case. The one-step protocol (Dandi et al., 2023) achieves the fastest recovery (T = 1) but requires the largest batch n_b = O(d^ℓ), making it impractical for moderate dimensions. For ℓ ≤ 2, the self-interaction regime does not exist, so Correlation Loss SGD provides no advantage over vanilla SGD. The paper also validates these predictions numerically (Fig. 2), showing that Correlation Loss SGD consistently achieves faster weak recovery than vanilla SGD for ℓ = 3 across multiple batch sizes and dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the information-exponent framework of Ben Arous et al. (2021) from the single-sample-per-batch case (n_b=1) to finite batch sizes, and the leap-complexity analysis of Abbe et al. (2023) to the one-pass SGD regime with explicit batch-size scaling. It builds on the summary-statistics ODE methodology of Saad and Solla (1995) and Veiga et al. (2022) to derive exact asymptotic dynamics for arbitrary batch sizes, and adopts the Correlation Loss SGD protocol from Damian et al. (2024) as a remedy for a newly identified self-interaction bottleneck. The key departure from prior work is the identification of a fundamental ceiling on batch-size benefits for one-pass SGD at n_b ~ d^{ℓ/2}, a regime not addressed by the n_b=1 or n_b=O(d) analyses in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that one-pass SGD cannot benefit from batch sizes beyond d^{ℓ/2} without incurring a sample-complexity penalty is practically relevant for large-scale single-pass training (e.g., LLM pretraining, federated learning) where batch size is a key hyperparameter. The Correlation Loss SGD workaround and the sharp ODE characterization provide actionable guidance for practitioners choosing between batch size, learning rate, and loss function. Limitations include the restriction to two-layer networks, primarily single-index targets, and cold-start initialization; the paper explicitly leaves open whether the same phenomenology holds for deeper architectures, warm starts, or full (beyond weak) recovery. The self-interaction regime's dependence on the second-layer initialization scale (a_0) suggests that practical training protocols with non-vanishing readout weights may exhibit qualitatively different behavior than the vanishing-a_0 assumption common in theory.
