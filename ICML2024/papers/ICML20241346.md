## Problem

Existing diffusion transformers like DiT are trained on fixed-resolution images (e.g., 256×256) with random cropping and resizing, which introduces spatial biases and prevents generalization to other resolutions or aspect ratios. When evaluated outside their training resolution, transformer-based diffusion models exhibit severe quality degradation, while CNN-based methods retain some flexibility due to local receptive fields. The paper asks whether a transformer architecture can natively support arbitrary resolutions and aspect ratios without architectural compromises.

## Input / Output

During training, FiT accepts images of any aspect ratio, resizing only high-resolution images to fit within a maximum pixel budget of HW ≤ 256² while preserving the original aspect ratio (no cropping). During inference, the user specifies a target resolution and aspect ratio (e.g., 224×448 or 160×480), and the model generates a corresponding image by defining a position map and sampling noisy latent tokens of the appropriate sequence length. The output is a decoded RGB image at the requested resolution, with token length scaling proportionally to the spatial dimensions.

## Method

FiT introduces three key design changes over DiT: (1) a flexible training pipeline that treats images as variable-length token sequences, pads them to a maximum length of 256 tokens, and computes loss only on valid tokens, eliminating cropping bias; (2) an architecture that replaces absolute positional embeddings with 2D Rotary Positional Embedding (applying separate 1D RoPE to height and width coordinates), swaps MLP for SwiGLU, and uses Masked Multi-Head Self-Attention to prevent padding tokens from interacting with content tokens; (3) training-free resolution extrapolation via VisionNTK and VisionYaRN, which decouple the rotary base/frequency scaling into independent height and width scale factors (s_h, s_w), enabling stable generation at resolutions and aspect ratios beyond the training distribution without any fine-tuning.

## Results

FiT-XL/2 (trained for only 1.8M steps on ImageNet-256) achieves FID-50K of 5.42 at 320×320, 7.90 at 224×448, and 15.72 at 160×480 — all out-of-distribution resolutions — using VisionNTK extrapolation. At in-distribution non-square resolutions, FiT-XL/2 reaches FID 5.74 at 160×320 and 16.81 at 128×384, substantially outperforming all prior diffusion and GAN baselines. At the standard 256×256 resolution, FiT-XL/2 records FID 4.27, slightly behind DiT-XL/2 (2.27) which was trained for 7M steps, but FiT surpasses DiT at every other tested resolution and aspect ratio.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FiT-XL/2 (1.8M steps) decisively outperforms DiT-XL/2 (7M steps) at every non-square resolution: FID drops from 20.14→5.74 at 160×320, 107.2→16.81 at 128×384, 94.94→7.90 at 224×448, and 140.2→15.72 at 160×480. Among transformer-based diffusion models, U-ViT and MDT collapse at non-square out-of-distribution resolutions (FID 67–383), while FiT maintains FID below 16. Among CNN-based baselines, LDM-4-G is the closest competitor at 224×448 (FID 8.55 vs. FiT's 7.90) and at 160×480 (19.24 vs. 15.72), but FiT still wins on both FID and IS. At the standard 256×256 resolution, FiT-XL/2 (FID 4.27) trails DiT-XL/2 (2.27), StyleGAN-XL (2.30), and MDT (1.79), reflecting its shorter training budget. The text-to-image experiment on CC3M/MSCOCO confirms the advantage: FiT-XL/2 achieves FID-30K of 28.21 at 160×320 and 36.57 at 128×384, versus DiT-XL/2's 47.75 and 119.34 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

FiT builds directly on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023) and the Latent Diffusion Model paradigm (Rombach et al., 2022), but departs from the fixed-resolution training assumption by conceptualizing images as variable-length token sequences. It adapts three key ideas from the LLM literature—2D Rotary Positional Embedding (from 1D RoPE in Su et al., 2024), SwiGLU feed-forward layers (from LLaMA), and training-free length extrapolation via NTK-aware interpolation and YaRN—into the 2D vision setting, introducing decoupled VisionNTK and VisionYaRN that handle height and width scale factors independently. The flexible training pipeline is conceptually related to NaViT's 'Patch n' Pack' for perception, but is the first to apply such variable-resolution training to a generative diffusion transformer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FiT addresses a fundamental practical limitation of diffusion transformers: the inability to generate images at resolutions or aspect ratios outside the training distribution. The training-free VisionNTK/VisionYaRN extrapolation methods offer a lightweight, plug-and-play solution that avoids retraining, making them immediately applicable to existing DiT-based systems. Key limitations include the restriction to a maximum token length of 256 during training (limiting extrapolation to roughly 512×512), evaluation only on ImageNet class-conditional generation, and exploration of only training-free extrapolation. Open questions include whether these techniques scale to video generation, higher-resolution pretraining, and whether fine-tuned extrapolation methods (e.g., YaRN with additional training) could push the resolution ceiling further.
