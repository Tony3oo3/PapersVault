## Problem

Autoregressive pretraining for visual representation learning (iGPT) has been largely abandoned in favor of BERT-style masked image modeling, despite iGPT's initial competitive results. The core limitation is that iGPT predicts raw pixels—noisy and semantically impoverished—and relies solely on next-token prediction, missing the richer supervisory signal available from semantic representations. This paper demonstrates that two simple modifications can make autoregressive pretraining a strong, scalable alternative to masked image modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241726/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images (512×512 for segmentation) and divides them into 4 non-overlapping spatial clusters (each 112×112, containing 7×7 patches of 16×16). A frozen CLIP model converts each cluster into a semantic token, which serves as the prediction target. The encoder's output features are used directly for downstream tasks: ImageNet-1K classification (224×224 input, linear probe or fine-tuning), ADE20K semantic segmentation (512×512, UperNet head), and zero-shot classification via fine-tuning on LAION-400M with an OpenCLIP text tower.

## Method

D-iGPT makes two modifications to iGPT: (1) it replaces raw-pixel prediction with semantic-token prediction using cosine similarity loss, where tokens are produced by a frozen discriminatively trained model (CLIP); and (2) it adds a discriminative decoder D alongside the generative decoder G, so the model is supervised to predict both the next unseen token (autoregressive) and the visible tokens (distillation-style). The ViT encoder processes randomly permuted cluster sequences, and both decoders are lightweight 2-layer Transformer blocks that attend to encoder outputs via [Gen]/[Dis] query tokens. This dual-supervision design disentangles autoregressive and distillation objectives, and the paper shows it works best when semantic tokens come from discriminatively trained models (CLIP) rather than generative ones (VQVAE, DINO), achieving 86.2% top-1 on ImageNet-1K with ViT-B in only 300 epochs and 90.0% with ViT-H pretrained on ImageNet-21K.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, D-iGPT with ViT-B achieves 86.2% top-1 accuracy (the first ViT-B to surpass 86% at 224×224 input), and with ViT-L reaches 87.8%. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, D-iGPT attains 53.8 mIoU (ViT-B) and 57.3 mIoU (ViT-L), setting new benchmarks for their respective model sizes. When scaled to ImageNet-21K pretraining with ViT-H, D-iGPT achieves 90.0% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K, surpassing all existing solutions trained on public datasets. In zero-shot classification on ImageNet-1K, D-iGPT with ViT-L/14 fine-tuned on 1B LAION-400M samples reaches 77.1% top-1, outperforming OpenCLIP by 1.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D-iGPT (ViT-B, 300 epochs) outperforms the supervised baseline DeiT by +5.0%, the dominant MIM method MAE by +2.6%, and the closest prior arts MILAN/DeepMIM by +0.6% on ImageNet-1K classification. Against EVA (same CLIP teacher, 800 epochs), D-iGPT gains +0.7% while using only 37.5% of the training epochs. At ViT-L scale, D-iGPT surpasses MAE by +1.9% and BEiT-v2 by +0.5% on classification, and leads on ADE20K segmentation by +3.7 mIoU over MAE. On ImageNet-21K pretraining, D-iGPT-H (632M params, 14M images) reaches 90.0% top-1, beating EVA (1B params, 30M images, 89.6%), BEiT-3 (1.9B params, 89.5%), and One-Peace (4B params, 89.8%) while using substantially smaller model and data. On robustness benchmarks (ImageNet-A, -R, -C, -S, V2, Real, Hard), D-iGPT consistently outperforms both supervised (DeiT) and self-supervised (MAE, BEiT-v2) baselines, with the largest gains on challenging sets like IN-Rendition (+10.2% over BEiT-v2 at ViT-L) and IN-Sketch (+6.6%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits iGPT (Chen et al., 2020), the pioneering autoregressive image model that predicted next raw pixels, and repositions it against the dominant BERT-style masked pretraining paradigm (MAE, BEiT, BEiT-v2) that has largely displaced autoregressive approaches in vision. Two modifications anchor the new method: (1) replacing pixel-level prediction with semantic tokens from a discriminatively trained model (CLIP), following BEiT's tokenization philosophy, and (2) augmenting the generative autoregressive decoder with a separate discriminative decoder that predicts visible-cluster tokens, effectively introducing a knowledge-distillation-style supervision. The random cluster permutation strategy is borrowed from RandSac (Hua et al., 2022) and XLNet to decouple performance from a fixed spatial ordering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a 300-epoch autoregressive pipeline with CLIP tokens matches or exceeds 1600-epoch masked-image-modeling baselines (MAE, BEiT-v2, MILAN) challenges the field's working assumption that BERT-style pretraining is inherently superior for visual representation learning. The 90.0% top-1 on ImageNet-1K with a vanilla ViT-H trained solely on public ImageNet-21K data is a concrete, reproducible milestone that narrows the gap with private-data models. Open questions include whether the autoregressive + discriminative dual-decoder formulation generalizes beyond ImageNet-scale data, whether the CLIP-token dependency limits applicability when no strong pretrained teacher is available, and whether the approach scales to truly large autoregressive image models (e.g., 1B+ parameters) where sequence-length costs become prohibitive.
