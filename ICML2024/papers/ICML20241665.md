## Problem

Most first-order convergence guarantees for stochastic optimization require data sampling to satisfy properties beyond recurrence—such as independence, exponential mixing, or reshuffling. This paper asks whether there exists a class of algorithms for which recurrence alone (every data point visited infinitely often with sufficient frequency) is sufficient to guarantee optimal O(n^{-1/2}) first-order convergence for non-convex, possibly non-smooth objectives with constraints.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes an arbitrary recurrent data sample sequence (v_n) with no independence or Markovian structure required, along with per-data-point loss functions f^v, a convex feasible set Θ, and data weights π(v) summing to one. It outputs a parameter θ_N whose expected optimality gap O_f(θ_N) converges at rate O(n^{-1/2}), with the implied constant depending explicitly on the sampling process's 'target time' t_⊙ (averaged expected return time) or 'hitting time' t_hit (supremized expected return time), rather than on mixing parameters.

## Method

The paper proposes RMISO (Regularized Minimization by Incremental Surrogate Optimization), which extends MISO by maintaining a list of convex majorizing surrogates g_n^v for each data point v, updating only the surrogate for the newly sampled point v_n each iteration, and minimizing the weighted average of all surrogates plus a regularization term. Three variants are analyzed: constant proximal regularization (rate depends only on the potentially much smaller target time t_⊙), dynamic proximal regularization where the regularization parameter adapts to the maximum time any vertex has been unvisited (depends on both t_⊙ and t_hit), and diminishing radius restriction (gives almost sure convergence to stationarity). The key analytical insight is that the regularization enforces iterate stability (summability of squared step sizes), which combined with the zero-gradient property of surrogates at their construction points and the recurrence assumption, allows bounding the surrogate error gradient without any mixing or independence arguments—yielding O(n^{-1/2}) rates under purely recurrent sampling.

## Results

The paper establishes O(n^{-1/2}) convergence rates for the expected optimality gap under general recurrent data sampling for RMISO with constant or dynamic proximal regularization (Cases 3.5–3.6), and O(n^{-1/2} log n) for the diminishing radius variant (Case 3.7). The key theoretical contribution is that the implied constant depends on the target time t_⊙ (an averaged hitting time) rather than the worst-case hitting time t_hit, which can be orders of magnitude smaller (e.g., O(|V|) vs O(|V|²) on the 'lonely' graph). Empirically, on MNIST distributed NMF, RMISO outperforms ONMF and is competitive with AdaGrad, with cyclic sampling yielding a dramatic improvement over random walk. On the a9a logistic regression task, RMISO-CPR and RMISO-DR maintain stable performance across graph topologies while MCSAG and RMISO-DPR degrade on the lonely graph.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original MISO (Mairal, 2015, recovered by setting ρ=0), RMISO with proximal regularization achieves the same O(n^{-1/2}) rate but with a better constant: the dependence shifts from linear in L·t_⊙/√μ (strongly convex surrogates) to √(L·t_⊙) (with optimal ρ), and crucially extends the guarantee from i.i.d. sampling to arbitrary recurrent sampling. On MNIST distributed NMF, RMISO outperforms ONMF (Mairal et al., 2010) across both random walk and cyclic sampling, and shows competitive reconstruction error against AdaGrad (Duchi et al., 2011), with cyclic sampling producing a dramatic improvement over random walk consistent with the theory. On a9a logistic regression, RMISO-CPR and RMISO-DR outperform MCSAG (Even, 2023) on the lonely graph topology because MCSAG's convergence depends on t_hit (O(|V|²)) while RMISO-CPR/DR depend only on t_⊙ (O(|V|)); however, AdaGrad and SGD-HB converge faster in wall-clock iterations but exhibit significantly less stable trajectories, which the authors note could be a disadvantage when the objective is expensive to evaluate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MISO (Mairal, 2015), originally analyzed only under i.i.d. sampling, by introducing proximal regularization (RMISO) that enables convergence guarantees under the much weaker assumption of arbitrary recurrent data sampling. It departs from the mixing-time analysis framework used for SGD and SAG under Markovian data (Sun et al., 2018; Even, 2023; Lyu, 2023) by developing a novel proof technique based on iterate stability and last-passage times, avoiding the need for exponential mixing or conditional unbiasedness. The connection to SAG is close—both store per-data-point information and average over the full dataset—but RMISO's constant-weight surrogates (vs. SAG's decreasing weights) and the use of majorization-minimization rather than gradient steps require fundamentally different analytical tools.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that convergence constants depend on the target time t_⊙ (an average over data points) rather than the hitting time t_hit (a worst-case), which can differ by orders of magnitude on sparse graphs—enabling better sampling scheme design for decentralized optimization. Open questions include proving asymptotic convergence for the simplest constant-regularization variant (Case 3.5) and almost-sure convergence for the dynamic variant (Case 3.6), as well as extending the framework to non-convex constraint sets or adaptive surrogate construction. The framework's generality (no Markovian, independence, or aperiodicity assumptions) makes it applicable to novel sampling strategies like greedy selection or graph-aware traversal that lack stationary distributions.
