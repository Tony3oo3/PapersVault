## Problem

Existing graph distillation methods rely on GNN-specific information (gradients, representations, trajectories) as supervision, which introduces two critical limitations: (1) the distillation GNN's filtering function biases the synthetic graph's spectrum toward certain eigenvalues (spectrum bias), and (2) different GNN architectures produce different synthetic graphs, requiring costly traversal over architectures to find the optimal one.

## Input / Output

GDEM takes a real graph with adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{N×N}, node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×d}, and one-hot labels Y, and produces a synthetic graph with N' ≪ N nodes. Rather than directly learning the synthetic adjacency matrix, the method learns a synthetic eigenbasis U'_K ∈ R^{N'×K} (where K = K₁ + K₂ ≤ N') and synthetic node features X' ∈ R^{N'×d}, then constructs the final adjacency matrix A' = Σₖ(1−λₖ)u'_k u'_kᵀ by combining the learned eigenbasis with the real graph's spectrum. Labels Y' are predefined one-hot vectors scaled proportionally to the real graph's class distribution.

## Method

GDEM decomposes the normalized graph Laplacian into eigenvalues and eigenbasis, then matches only the K₁ smallest and K₂ largest eigenvectors (principal eigenbasis) between real and synthetic graphs, avoiding dependence on any specific GNN. The training objective combines three terms: an eigenbasis matching loss (L_e) that aligns the subspaces Xᵀu_k u_kᵀX between graphs, an orthogonality regularization (L_o) preserving the orthonormal structure of the learned eigenbasis, and a discrimination constraint (L_d) that enforces cosine similarity on category-level representations to capture downstream task information. After optimization, the synthetic adjacency matrix is constructed by directly replicating the real graph's spectrum with the learned synthetic eigenbasis, ensuring spectral fidelity without GNN influence. Theoretically, GDEM is proven to produce restricted ε-spectral approximations of the real graph, and empirically achieves superior cross-architecture generalization (2–6× lower variance across GNNs) with ~40× lower time overhead compared to traversal-based methods.

## Results

GDEM achieves state-of-the-art node classification accuracy on 6 out of 7 datasets, e.g., 72.6% on Citeseer (1.80% ratio), 78.4% on Pubmed (0.15%), 93.1% on Reddit (0.10%), and 60.8% on Gamers (0.25%), consistently outperforming gradient-matching (GCOND, SGDD) and trajectory-matching (SFGC) baselines. On Ogbn-arxiv (0.25%), GDEM (63.8%) is slightly outperformed by SFGC (66.1%) and SGDD (65.8%), attributed to insufficient eigenvectors at extreme compression. In cross-architecture generalization (Table 3), GDEM achieves the lowest standard deviation across six GNNs (e.g., 0.51 on Citeseer vs. 2.54 for GCOND), and its distillation time is 1.79 s versus 68.75 s (GCOND) and 74.76 s (SGDD) when traversing all architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GDEM outperforms all three graph distillation baselines (GCOND, SFGC, SGDD) on 6 of 7 datasets, with the largest margins on Reddit (+3.5% over SGDD) and Squirrel (+1.3% over SFGC). On Ogbn-arxiv, GDEM (63.8%) underperforms SFGC (66.1%) and SGDD (65.8%), which the authors attribute to the limited number of eigenvectors available at 0.25% compression being insufficient to capture the widely distributed spectral structure of that graph. Against traditional reduction methods (Random, Coarsening, Herding, K-Center), GDEM leads by 5–30+ points across all datasets, confirming that GNN-informed distillation substantially outperforms heuristic structural sampling. In cross-architecture generalization, GDEM reduces the standard deviation across six GNNs by 2–6× compared to GCOND (e.g., 0.51 vs. 2.54 on Citeseer; 1.35 vs. 8.35 on Reddit), demonstrating that eigenbasis matching produces synthetic graphs that are agnostic to the evaluation GNN. In terms of efficiency, GDEM's single distillation run (1.79 s) is ~38× faster than GCOND (68.75 s) and ~42× faster than SGDD (74.76 s) when the latter must traverse all six GNN architectures to find the best distillation model.

## Relation to Prior Work

GDEM departs from the dominant paradigm in graph distillation (GCOND, SGDD, SFGC) that relies on GNN-related supervision signals—gradients, representations, or training trajectories—to synthesize small graphs. Instead, it draws on the spectral coarsening literature (Jin et al., AISTATS 2020) and the upper-bound decomposition of the gradient-matching objective (Jin et al., ICLR 2022) to isolate eigenbasis matching as a GNN-independent distillation criterion. The key conceptual shift is that by matching eigenvectors and directly replicating the real graph's spectrum, GDEM eliminates the spectrum bias and architecture-traversal overhead that plague prior methods, positioning itself as a spectral-domain alternative to the spatial-domain GNN-centric approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 40× distillation speedup (1.79 s vs. ~70 s) and the near-elimination of cross-architecture variance (e.g., 0.51 vs. 2.54 std on Citeseer) make GDEM a practically attractive option for teams that need a single synthetic graph usable across multiple GNN backbones. The theoretical guarantee of restricted spectral similarity provides a principled foundation absent in prior GD work. However, limitations remain: the O(KN²) eigen-decomposition cost is non-trivial for very large graphs, performance degrades at extreme compression ratios (notably on Ogbn-arxiv where only hundreds of eigenvectors are available), and the discrimination constraint introduces a low-frequency bias that must be carefully balanced. The authors identify eigenbasis matching without explicit eigenvalue decomposition as a promising future direction.
