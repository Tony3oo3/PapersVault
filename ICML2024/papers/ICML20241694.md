## Problem

While prior work establishes that neural networks preferentially learn simpler spurious features over complex core features, the precise impact of the *relative* complexity gap between spurious and core features on core feature convergence dynamics remains unexplored. Additionally, the common assumption that spurious and core feature learning occur in cleanly separable phases—which underpins popular debiasing algorithms like Just Train Twice—has not been rigorously validated, and the persistence of spurious features after core features are learned lacks theoretical explanation.

## Input / Output

The synthetic dataset takes a boolean vector x ∈ {+1,−1}^n decomposed into core coordinates x_c ∈ {+1,−1}^c, spurious coordinates x_s ∈ {+1,−1}^s, and noise coordinates x_u ∈ {+1,−1}^u, and produces a binary label y = f_c(x_c). The spurious correlation is controlled by a confounder strength λ ∈ [0,1], where λ=0.5 corresponds to no correlation and λ→1 to near-perfect correlation. Two function families are used: parity functions (exponentially hard in degree) and threshold staircase functions (polynomially hard in degree), enabling independent control over the complexity of each feature. For real-data validation, standard RGB images from WaterBirds, Color-MNIST, and Domino Image are used with ResNet-50 backbones.

## Method

The paper introduces a boolean-function-based synthetic dataset framework where spurious and core features are represented as parity or staircase functions of differing degrees, with their correlation controlled via a mixture distribution parameterized by λ. A two-layer ReLU network (width 100) is trained with batch SGD under cross-entropy loss, and feature learning is tracked via core/spurious correlation and decoded correlation (after last-layer logistic regression on group-balanced data). The theoretical analysis leverages Fourier analysis of boolean functions to derive the population gradient gap at initialization, showing the spurious feature's gradient advantage scales as (λ−1/2)(ξ_{s−1}−ξ_{s+1}) versus (1/2)(ξ_{c−1}−ξ_{c+1}) for the core feature, enabling layer-wise learning of the spurious feature first. After the spurious subnetwork reaches Bayes optimality, the remaining network's gradient toward the core feature is reduced by a factor of 4λ(1−λ), explaining the convergence slowdown. The paper further shows that spurious neurons become 'dead' to core coordinates (zero gradient) and that the optimal spurious margin γ_s* remains positive for any finite core confidence γ_c, providing a theoretical account for spurious feature persistence and justifying why Last Layer Retraining on balanced data reduces spurious subnetwork weights.

## Results

The paper's key quantitative findings are: (1) simpler spurious features (lower-degree parity or staircase) substantially increase the number of epochs needed to reach 0.95 core correlation, with the effect becoming pronounced when confounder strength λ exceeds a threshold (~0.7–0.8); (2) the population gradient for core features is reduced by a factor of 4λ(1−λ) once the spurious feature is learned (e.g., ~0.25× at λ=0.9), explaining the convergence slowdown; (3) debiasing algorithms JTT and Spare achieve Jaccard scores below 0.5 on staircase and Domino-vision datasets where spurious and core features have comparable complexity, versus successful minority-group identification on parity-style datasets with large complexity gaps; (4) Last Layer Retraining (LLR) on group-balanced data consistently improves worst-group accuracy, with the largest gains occurring early in training when core-feature confidence is low.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates three existing approaches against its boolean spurious features framework. **Just Train Twice (JTT, Liu et al. 2021)** and **Spare (Yang et al. 2023)** both rely on the assumption that spurious features are learned in a clearly separated early phase, enabling minority-group identification from an early-stopped model. On parity-style datasets where the spurious feature is much simpler (e.g., degree 4 vs. 10), both methods successfully identify the minority group (high Jaccard scores). However, on staircase datasets (degree 7 vs. 14) and the more challenging Domino-vision dataset, where spurious and core features have comparable complexity and are learned concurrently, both methods fail with Jaccard scores below 0.5, and worst-group accuracy does not improve after upsampling. **Last Layer Retraining (LLR, Kirichenko et al. 2023)** is the approach the paper provides theoretical justification for: the authors show that LLR works by reducing the ratio of last-layer weights connected to the spurious subnetwork relative to the core subnetwork (Table 5), and that the improvement is most pronounced when the core feature has low confidence (early in training) and confounder strength λ is high. The paper does not propose a new debiasing algorithm but rather explains when and why existing methods succeed or fail, positioning LLR as the more robust option across the complexity spectrum.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the theoretical machinery of Barak et al. (2023) for learning parity functions with two-layer ReLU networks (Fourier gap analysis, layer-wise training, initialization schemes) and extends it to the spurious correlation setting by introducing a controlled boolean-function dataset. It also draws on Abbe et al. (2022, 2023) for staircase functions as a second function class with hierarchical learning dynamics. Conceptually, it departs from prior spurious-correlation studies (Waterbirds, Color-MNIST, Domino) that use ad-hoc feature constructions by providing a principled, parameterized framework where relative complexity and correlation strength are independently tunable, and it challenges the separability assumption underlying debiasing algorithms like Just Train Twice and Spare.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that spurious and core feature learning phases are not cleanly separable (especially for staircase functions) directly undermines the core assumption of a family of widely-used debiasing algorithms, suggesting they may fail in realistic settings where spurious features are not dramatically simpler than core features. The persistence of spurious feature weights even after core features are fully learned, and the theoretical explanation for why Last Layer Retraining works by reducing spurious subnetwork weights, provide actionable insight for practitioners. Open questions include whether the boolean-function framework generalizes to richer feature spaces, whether the subnetwork disentanglement holds in deeper architectures, and whether end-to-end theoretical guarantees can be established without the current phase-separation assumptions.
