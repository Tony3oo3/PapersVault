## Problem

Existing spiking neural networks rely on binary {0,1} spikes that cause severe information loss, making them incapable of handling general language tasks with competitive accuracy. Prior language-oriented SNNs (SpikeBERT, SpikeGPT) either only addressed discriminative tasks or retained ANN matrix multiplications within transformer blocks, leaving a large performance gap (up to 28%) between SNNs and ANNs in language modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242251/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard text token sequences (up to 128 tokens for GLUE, longer for summarization/translation) and produces class labels, regression scores, or generated text depending on the task. Unusually, all internal matrix multiplications in the transformer are replaced by spike-driven accumulate operations over T time steps, with spikes taking ternary levels {-α, 0, +α}; only the final regression head remains a standard ANN layer. The spike firing rate is controllable via a hyperparameter k, enabling a tunable performance–energy trade-off.

## Method

SpikeLM introduces an elastic bi-spiking mechanism that generalizes spike encoding along three axes while preserving the pure-addition property of SNNs: (i) bi-directional encoding replaces binary {0,1} spikes with ternary {-1,0,1} levels, doubling per-spike information entropy; (ii) distribution-aware frequency encoding scales membrane potentials by a layer-wise factor α(t) = (k/n)Σ|m_i(t)| computed from the first training batch, yielding a controllable firing rate; (iii) amplitude encoding assigns spikes magnitude α(t) so that the expected spike value matches the membrane potential, with α mergeable into weights post-training. Backpropagation uses a straight-through estimator derived from the expectation of stochastic spike variables. The authors prove via dynamical isometry that the elastic bi-spiking Jacobian is closer to identity than ReLU's, ensuring superior gradient stability. For discriminative tasks the model is pretrained self-supervised (BERT-style) and finetuned; for generative tasks it is initialized and distilled from pretrained BART/mBART teachers.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark (12-layer BERT architecture), SpikeLM (T=4) achieves 76.5% average, reducing the performance gap to BERT_base (83.2%) from 28.3% (LIF-BERT) to just 6.7%, while saving 3.7× energy (13.74 mJ vs. 51.41 mJ). With T=1, SpikeLM reaches 75.7% average at only 3.98 mJ (12.9× energy savings). On generative tasks, SpikeLM (T=4) achieves ROUGE-L 32.92 on XSUM and 29.06 on CNN-DailyMail, dropping only 1.80% and 2.69% from BART_base, and BLEU 22.95 on WMT16 En-Ro (vs. 26.82 for mBART_large).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpikeLM (T=4, 76.5% avg) outperforms all prior SNN baselines: SpikeBERT (59.7%, +16.8%), SpikingFormer T=4 (68.9%, +7.6%), LIF-BERT (54.9%, +21.6%), and PSN-BERT* (34.7%, +41.8%). Against ultra-low-bit quantized BERTs, SpikeLM (T=1, 75.4% avg in Table 4) surpasses BiPFT (70.8%), BiT (71.0%), BiBERT (63.2%), BinaryBERT (53.7%), TernaryBERT (45.5%), and Q2BERT (47.7%), despite using ternary activations rather than binary. On generative tasks, SpikeLM (T=4) exceeds LIF-BART (T=4) by 4.60% ROUGE-L on XSUM and 1.01% on CNN-DailyMail, and by 3.91 BLEU on WMT16. Compared to the ANN baselines, SpikeLM narrows the gap to BERT_base from 28.3% to 6.7% on GLUE, and to BART_base from 6.40% to 1.80% ROUGE-L on XSUM. Notably, SpikeLM (T=1) already outperforms LIF-BART (T=4) on WMT16 (20.93 vs. 19.04 BLEU), showing that improved spike encoding is more effective than simply increasing time steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LIF neuron framework (Gerstner et al., 2014) by replacing binary {0,1} spike encoding with a three-part elastic bi-spiking mechanism: bidirectional ternary levels, distribution-aware frequency scaling, and layerwise amplitude encoding. It departs from prior language-oriented SNNs (SpikeBERT, SpikeGPT) that either relied on distillation, used only discriminative tasks, or retained partial ANN blocks, by achieving fully spike-driven computation across both discriminative and generative language tasks. The theoretical grounding via dynamical isometry (Chen et al., 2020) connects the spike encoding to optimization stability, positioning the work at the intersection of SNN theory and transformer architecture design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that fully spike-driven models can handle general language tasks (both NLU and generation) with competitive accuracy, narrowing the SNN-ANN gap from ~28% to ~7% on GLUE while maintaining 12.9× energy savings at T=1. The elastic bi-spiking mechanism is architecturally simple (a threshold change plus amplitude scaling) yet yields large gains, suggesting that prior SNN underperformance in language was largely an encoding bottleneck rather than a fundamental limitation of spiking computation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly large LLMs (the paper only tests up to 680M mBART), whether weight quantization can be incorporated alongside activation spiking, and whether the distribution-aware frequency encoding generalizes to non-Gaussian input distributions in deeper or more heterogeneous architectures.
