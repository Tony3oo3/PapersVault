## Problem

Finite-time convergence guarantees for decentralized nonsmooth nonconvex stochastic optimization were largely missing: prior work either required weak-convexity assumptions, provided only asymptotic results, or achieved suboptimal rates. Specifically, no first-order finite-time guarantee existed for the general nonsmooth nonconvex setting, and the zero-order decentralized setting required variance reduction to match centralized complexity. This paper closes these gaps by establishing the optimal O(δ⁻¹ε⁻³) sample complexity in both first-order and zero-order decentralized settings without weak-convexity or variance reduction.

## Input / Output

Each of n agents accesses only its local stochastic function f^i(x) = E_ξ[F^i(x,ξ)] through either a first-order oracle (unbiased stochastic gradient ∇F^i(x,ξ) with bounded second moment G²) or a zero-order oracle (noisy function value F^i(x,ξ) with bounded Lipschitz constant). Agents communicate exclusively with neighbors defined by a fixed symmetric doubly stochastic matrix P over a connected graph. The algorithm outputs a single point w^out drawn uniformly from K epoch-level candidates, each being the average of nT local iterates within one epoch, guaranteed to be a (δ,ε)-stationary point of the global objective f(x) = (1/n)Σf^i(x) in expectation.

## Method

ME-DOL (Multi Epoch Decentralized Online Learning) runs K epochs of length T, where each epoch executes a decentralized online gradient descent algorithm (Shahrampour & Jadbabaie, 2018) that generates bounded actions Δ_{t,i}^k for each agent. At each iteration, agents form a random interpolation point w_{t,i}^k = y_{t-1,i}^k + s·Δ_{t,i}^k, query their local oracle (with randomized smoothing parameter δ' = δ/2 for nonsmooth objectives), and perform a weighted neighborhood average to update y_{t,i}^k. The key technical insight is an online-to-nonconvex conversion adapted to the decentralized setting: the function-value telescoping is decomposed into a regret term (bounded via decentralized online learning), a noise term, and a discrepancy term arising from the gap between the global gradient and the average of local gradients. For nonsmooth objectives, randomized smoothing with the geometric lemma of Kornowski & Shamir (2024) splits the stationarity radius δ into δ/2 for smoothing and δ/2 for the Goldstein subdifferential ball, controlling the smoothness parameter L₁ = O(δ⁻¹) to achieve the optimal O(δ⁻¹ε⁻³) rate rather than the suboptimal O(δ⁻¹ε⁻⁴) from naive smoothing. In the zero-order setting, a two-point gradient estimator yields O(d) dimension dependence, improving over the O(d^{3/2}) of prior decentralized methods.

## Results

The paper establishes a sample complexity of O(δ⁻¹ε⁻³) for finding (δ,ε)-stationary points of the global objective in three settings: smooth first-order, nonsmooth first-order, and nonsmooth zero-order. This matches the optimal centralized rate (Cutkosky et al., 2023) and, for the first-order nonsmooth case, provides the first finite-time guarantee for decentralized nonsmooth nonconvex stochastic optimization without weak-convexity assumptions. In the zero-order setting, ME-DOL achieves O(dδ⁻¹ε⁻³) complexity, improving the dimension dependence from O(d^{3/2}) (DGFM, DGFM+) to O(d), matching the centralized optimal rate. Empirically, on a nonconvex penalized SVM with capped-ℓ₁ regularizer across six datasets (IJCNN, RCV1, SUSY, a9a, HIGGS, covtype), ME-DOL demonstrates faster gradient norm decay and higher test classification accuracy than baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the zero-order setting, ME-DOL is compared against DGFM (Lin et al., 2024) on a9a, HIGGS, and covtype; ME-DOL dominates DGFM in test classification accuracy across all three datasets (Fig. 5), while also improving the theoretical dimension dependence from O(d^{3/2}) to O(d) without requiring variance reduction. In the first-order setting, ME-DOL is compared against DPSGD (Lian et al., 2017) on the same three datasets; ME-DOL achieves better test classification accuracy than DPSGD (Fig. 6), which was originally designed for smooth problems and only analyzed for nonsmooth settings via projection (DPSM, Chen et al., 2021). Theoretically, ME-DOL matches the optimal centralized rate O(δ⁻¹ε⁻³) in all three oracle settings, whereas prior decentralized work (DGFM) achieved only O(d^{3/2}δ⁻¹ε⁻⁴) without variance reduction, and no finite-time guarantee existed for the first-order nonsmooth decentralized case before this work. The network connectivity experiments (ring graphs with 7–13 neighbors, Erdős–Rényi random graphs) confirm that better connectivity (smaller ρ) yields faster convergence, consistent with the O((1−ρ)⁻²) dependence in the theorems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three distinct threads: the online-to-nonconvex conversion technique of Cutkosky et al. (2023) for centralized nonsmooth optimization, the decentralized online learning framework of Shahrampour & Jadbabaie (2018), and the geometric lemma on Goldstein subdifferential sets from Kornowski & Shamir (2024). The key conceptual departure is extending the centralized online-to-nonconvex reduction to the decentralized setting, where the discrepancy between local and global variables introduces additional terms that must be controlled via the geometric lemma. Unlike prior decentralized nonsmooth work (Chen et al., 2021) that relied on weak-convexity and Moreau envelopes, ME-DOL uses randomized smoothing to handle general Lipschitz nonsmooth objectives, and unlike Lin et al. (2024) for zero-order, it achieves the O(δ⁻¹ε⁻³) rate without variance reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first finite-time convergence guarantee for decentralized nonsmooth nonconvex stochastic optimization in the first-order setting without weak-convexity assumptions, filling a notable gap between the well-understood smooth decentralized case and the previously unaddressed nonsmooth first-order decentralized case. The matching of centralized rates (O(δ⁻¹ε⁻³)) suggests that decentralization does not fundamentally increase the sample complexity for this class of problems. However, the (1−ρ)⁻² network dependence is suboptimal compared to the (1−ρ)⁻¹ᐟ² lower bound known for the smooth case, and no lower bound exists for the nonsmooth decentralized setting, leaving the optimal communication complexity open. Future directions include high-probability bounds, closing the ρ-dependence gap, and extending to the deterministic regime.
