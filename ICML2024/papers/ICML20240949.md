## Problem

Hyperparameter tuning for stochastic optimization is prohibitively expensive at scale, yet it remains unclear whether algorithms can self-tune to match optimally-tuned SGD. This paper formalizes the notion of 'tuning-free' algorithms—those that match optimally-tuned SGD up to polylogarithmic factors given only loose lower/upper bounds on problem parameters—and establishes a fundamental separation: tuning-free optimization is impossible for convex problems over unbounded domains, but possible under bounded domains or sufficiently benign noise distributions.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a stochastic first-order oracle returning unbiased function values and gradient estimates with bounded (or sub-gaussian) noise, along with loose hints (lower and upper bounds) on the smoothness constant L, Lipschitz constant G, noise magnitude σ, and initial distance to the optimum D*. The output is a point x_out whose suboptimality matches the best known high-probability SGD rate multiplied by a polylogarithmic factor in the hint ratios and T. Crucially, the hints may be very loose—only polylogarithmic degradation is permitted, not polynomial—making this a much weaker assumption than knowing the true parameters.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a combination of impossibility theorems and conditional positive results. For impossibility, the authors construct pairs of 1D quadratic (or absolute-value) functions with indistinguishable stochastic oracles, showing no algorithm can simultaneously satisfy the tuning-free guarantee for both (Theorems 2, 3, 5). For positive results, they show DoG and DoWG are tuning-free under bounded domains (Theorem 1), and that T-DoG/T-DoWG augmented with a variance-estimation procedure achieve tuning-free matching when the signal-to-noise ratio K_snr = σ/R is bounded away from zero (Theorem 4). For nonconvex smooth objectives, they give a restarted SGD algorithm using a doubling trick over logarithmically many stepsizes combined with a FindLeader subsampling procedure to select the best iterate, achieving the best-known high-probability SGD rate at only a polylogarithmic cost (Theorem 6).

## Results

The paper establishes a complete landscape of tuning-free stochastic optimization. In the bounded domain, DoG and DoWG match optimally-tuned SGD up to polylogarithmic factors (Theorem 1). In the unbounded stochastic convex setting, no algorithm can match tuned SGD up to any polylogarithmic factor (Theorems 2–3), but under a high signal-to-noise ratio (K_snr bounded away from zero), T-DoG/T-DoWG with variance estimation recover tuning-free guarantees at only a logarithmic minibatch cost (Theorem 4). For nonconvex smooth objectives, a restarted SGD with a FindLeader procedure matches the best-known high-probability SGD rate up to polylog factors (Theorem 6), while matching the in-expectation rate is provably impossible (Theorem 5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline throughout is optimally-tuned SGD, whose convergence rates (Equations 2, 5, 9, 10) serve as the target for tuning-free algorithms. In the bounded convex setting, DoG and DoWG match tuned SGD's rates up to a polylogarithmic factor in the hint ratios (D̲/D̄, σ̲/σ̄, T, δ⁻¹), with no prior knowledge of whether f is smooth or Lipschitz. In the unbounded stochastic convex setting, the impossibility theorems (2–3) show that no algorithm—regardless of design—can achieve even a polylogarithmic price of adaptivity, fundamentally separating the stochastic case from the deterministic setting where bisection, Polyak stepsizes, and T-DoG/T-DoWG all succeed. Under benign noise (K_snr bounded away from zero), T-DoG/T-DoWG with a variance-estimation minibatch of size b ≈ O(log(dT/δ)) recover tuning-free guarantees, paying only a logarithmic overhead in total gradient accesses. For nonconvex optimization, the restarted SGD (Algorithm 1) matches the high-probability rate of Liu et al. (2023) up to polylog factors, but Theorem 5 rules out matching the tighter in-expectation rate of Ghadimi & Lan (2013), establishing a gap between what is achievable in expectation versus with high probability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper formalizes a precise notion of 'tuning-free' optimization (Definition 1.1) that is deliberately distinct from the existing parameter-free optimization literature: it requires matching tuned SGD up to only polylogarithmic degradation given loose hints on problem parameters, rather than achieving a universal rate. It builds directly on the DoG and DoWG parameter-free stepsize schedules (Ivgi et al., 2023; Khaled et al., 2023) and the FindLeader subsampling procedure (Madden et al., 2020), extending their analyses to establish tuning-free guarantees or prove their impossibility. The paper also draws on deterministic tuning-free methods (Adaptive Polyak, bisection search, line search) to contrast with the fundamentally harder stochastic setting, and on online learning lower bounds (Cutkosky & Boahen) to position its impossibility results.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The impossibility results for unbounded stochastic convex optimization (Theorems 2–3) are a significant negative contribution: they show that no algorithm, regardless of design, can match tuned SGD up to polylog factors when the domain is unbounded and noise is merely bounded almost surely. The identification of the signal-to-noise ratio K_snr as the key condition enabling tuning-free optimization (Theorem 4) provides a clear, actionable characterization for practitioners choosing between DoG/DoWG and tuned SGD. Open questions include whether the noise assumptions (sub-gaussian norm) can be relaxed to expected smoothness, whether tuning-free optimization is possible in the finite-sum setting, and how these results extend to the highly nonsmooth objectives common in deep learning.
