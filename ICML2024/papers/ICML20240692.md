## Problem

Riemannian optimization on matrix manifolds requires full retraction at every iteration to maintain feasibility, with costs scaling as O(n³) for constraints like orthogonality or positive definiteness. While coordinate descent (CD) in Euclidean space avoids this by updating one coordinate at a time, designing valid CD updates on manifolds is inherently difficult because the tangent space structure and retraction must be respected. Prior work has developed CD only for a few specific manifolds (orthogonal, Stiefel, SPD with affine-invariant metric), leaving no general framework that unifies efficient CD across the broader family of matrix manifolds encountered in machine learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a differentiable objective f defined on a matrix manifold M (Stiefel, Grassmann, hyperbolic, symplectic, SPD/SPSD, doubly stochastic, or multinomial), an initial feasible point X₀ ∈ M, and hyperparameters (stepsize η, outer iterations K, inner iterations S). The key computational detail is that the coordinate derivative θ = ⟨∇f(X), B_ℓ⟩ is computed from the Euclidean gradient rather than the Riemannian gradient, making it independent of the chosen metric. The RCDlin variant further reduces cost by anchoring the gradient at X_k for all S inner iterations, so only one gradient evaluation per outer loop is needed. Output is the optimized matrix X on M, with convergence tracked via optimality gap or Riemannian gradient norm.

## Method

The core framework selects a basis {B_ℓ} spanning the tangent space such that the retraction along each basis direction admits a closed-form, low-cost update: Givens rotations for Stiefel/Grassmann (O(p) per update), hyperbolic rotations (Lorentz boosts) for the hyperbolic manifold, exponential row/column scaling for the symplectic manifold, and coordinate Sinkhorn (a 2×2 balancing) for the doubly stochastic manifold. The coordinate derivative is computed as θ = ⟨∇f(X), B_ℓ⟩ using only the Euclidean gradient, bypassing the need for the Riemannian gradient and its metric-dependent projection. Two algorithms are proposed: RCD, which recomputes the gradient at each inner iteration, and RCDlin, which linearizes the objective at X_k and reuses a single anchored gradient across all S inner updates. Convergence is established at rate O(|I|L/K) for randomized coordinate selection and O(|I|²L/K) for cyclic selection, with the linearized variant incurring only additional constants from the gradient staleness.

## Results

The paper evaluates RCD and RCDlin across five problem settings (Procrustes, PCA, distillation, nearest symplectic matrix, Lorentz embeddings, and SPSD matrix completion). On the Procrustes problem (n=200, p=150), RCD achieves lower flop counts than all baselines (TSD, RGD-QR, RGD-Cayley, RGD-EXP) while remaining competitive in runtime. On the SPSD weighted least squares problem with sparse A (n=500, p=100, 70% observed entries), RCDlin shows a clear advantage over RGD in convergence speed. On Lorentz embedding learning (WordNet mammals, n=5), RCDlin with time-cyclic basis selection converges at a rate comparable to RGD in terms of runtime, using MAP and MR as evaluation metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five categories of baselines. (1) TSD (Gutman & Ho-Nguyen, 2023), a column-wise CD method on Stiefel: RCD wins on flop counts (O(p) vs O(np) per update) and on PCA, TSD converges slowly due to non-invariance to basis change, while RCD's row-wise Givens updates are well-defined on Grassmann. (2) RGD with QR, Cayley, and exponential retractions: RCD achieves lower per-iteration cost and competitive convergence; RCDlin further reduces gradient computations by anchoring the gradient at the outer iteration. (3) Infeasible methods (PLAM, PCAL, PenCF, ExPen, Landing) on the Procrustes problem: RCD performs competitively, while infeasible methods require careful tuning of multiple regularization parameters. (4) RGD with Cayley, quasi-geodesic, and SR retractions on the symplectic manifold: RCD with block coordinate updates shows clear advantage in both flop counts and runtime. (5) On the SPSD manifold with sparse A, RCDlin significantly outperforms RGD, while on dense SPD (n=p=500) the two are comparable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends the line of coordinate descent (CD) work on specific manifolds: Givens-rotation-based CD for the orthogonal group (Shalit & Chechik, 2014; Massart & Abrol, 2022), tangent subspace descent for the Stiefel manifold (Gutman & Ho-Nguyen, 2023), and Cholesky-based CD for the SPD manifold (Darmwal & Rajawat, 2023). The key conceptual departure is identifying a common algebraic template—choosing a basis of the tangent space parameterized by skew-symmetric or symmetric matrices, paired with an exponential retraction that reduces to a 2×2 rotation—so that the same CD construction applies uniformly across Stiefel, Grassmann, hyperbolic, symplectic, doubly stochastic, and SPSD manifolds. The RCDlin variant, which anchors the gradient at the outer-iteration point to amortize gradient computations, is a practical extension not present in prior manifold CD work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework reduces per-iteration cost from O(n³) (full retraction) to O(n) or O(p) for most manifolds considered, making CD competitive with full-gradient Riemannian methods while being significantly cheaper per step. The general template (basis choice → coordinate derivative via Euclidean inner product → 2×2 retraction) provides a clear recipe for extending CD to new matrix manifolds, lowering the barrier for future work. Open questions include whether the convergence analysis can be tightened beyond the compact-neighbourhood assumptions, whether the framework extends to non-matrix or infinite-dimensional manifolds, and how the methods behave for non-smooth or composite objectives where the Euclidean-gradient shortcut (Eq. 3) may not apply directly.
