## Problem

The Information Maximization (InfoMax) greedy heuristic is the standard approach for sequential binary test selection, yet existing theoretical guarantees on its performance are often vacuous—loose by orders of magnitude compared to its empirical efficiency. This paper establishes the first tight, non-vacuous performance bounds for InfoMax by introducing a δ-unpredictability assumption on the test set, showing that the expected number of tests needed is at most a small multiplicative factor of the entropy of Y.

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite set of binary tests T, a discrete target variable Y with known distribution, and a δ-unpredictability parameter δ ∈ [0, 1/2] (with an optional noise level α for the BSC model). It produces a sequence of adaptively selected tests via greedy mutual information maximization, terminating when Y is identified (or predicted with confidence γ in the noisy case), and yields a closed-form upper bound on the expected number of tests: H(Y)/h(1/2+δ) for oracle tests and H(Y)/(h(1/2+δ)−h(α)) for noisy tests. The δ-unpredictability condition requires that at every iteration, some available test has conditional probability of being 'true' within 1/2 ± δ of the current active set.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a two-part performance analysis under a δ-unpredictability assumption. For oracle tests (deterministic functions of Y), the paper proves InfoMax requires at most H(Y)/h(1/2+δ) tests by showing each non-terminal iteration extracts at least h(1/2+δ) bits of conditional entropy, then summing over iterations and bounding the total by H(Y). This bound is shown to be tight via an explicit construction using a geometrically-decaying distribution over Y with singleton tests. For noisy tests corrupted by a Binary Symmetric Channel with crossover probability α, the analysis is extended by showing (via Lemma 5.1) that InfoMax still selects the test whose true (de-noised) outcome most closely bisects the active set, and the additional h(α) term in the denominator accounts for the irreducible noise entropy that persists even after Y is determined. The paper validates the δ-unpredictability assumption empirically on CUB-200-2011 (δ=0.22) and AwA2 (δ=0.17), demonstrating the new bound is roughly an order of magnitude tighter than prior bounds from Loveland (1985) and Dasgupta (2004).

## Results

The paper establishes that for a δ-unpredictable set of oracle tests, InfoMax requires at most H(Y)/h(1/2+δ) tests on average to identify Y, and for BSC-noisy tests with crossover probability α, at most H(Y)/(h(1/2+δ)−h(α)) tests. Empirically, on CUB-200-2011 (δ=0.22), the proposed bound B_Oracle=8.93 closely tracks the actual InfoMax performance of 7.70 (ratio ≈1.16), while on AwA2 (δ=0.17), B_Oracle=6.17 vs. InfoMax=5.73 (ratio ≈1.08). The paper further proves the bound is tight by constructing an explicit distribution and test set where InfoMax achieves exactly H(Y)/h(1/2+δ) tests.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's proposed bound B_Oracle uniformly dominates both prior bounds across all δ values. Compared to Loveland's bound (B_Lov, 1985), the paper's bound is roughly 2× tighter: 8.93 vs. 17.44 on CUB-200 and 6.17 vs. 12.69 on AwA2. Compared to Dasgupta's bound (B_Das, 2004), which makes no assumption on the test set and uses opt(T,Y) (proxied by H(Y)), the improvement is dramatic—approximately 18× tighter on CUB-200 (8.93 vs. 161.86) and 14× tighter on AwA2 (6.17 vs. 88.31). In non-uniform prior experiments (200 Dirichlet-sampled priors per dataset), the ratio B_Oracle/InfoMax remains about an order of magnitude smaller than both B_Lov/InfoMax and B_Das/InfoMax, with the gap widening as H(Y) decreases. The paper also proves tightness: for any δ, there exists a δ-unpredictable test set where InfoMax achieves exactly H(Y)/h(1/2+δ), so no tighter universal bound is possible.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fifty-year lineage of analysis on the greedy Information Maximization (InfoMax) strategy for sequential binary test selection, building on foundational work by Shannon (1948), Garey & Graham (1974), Loveland (1985), and Dasgupta (2004). It departs from prior work by introducing the δ-unpredictability assumption on the test set, which yields the first performance bound that scales with H(Y) rather than log|Y| or the intractable opt(T,Y), and by proving the bound is tight via an explicit construction. For the noisy case, it extends beyond Nowak (2008) and Golovin et al. (2010) by analyzing a BSC noise model without assuming tests are repeatable, a setting closer to practical applications like medical diagnosis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The InfoMax greedy strategy is one of the most widely deployed heuristics in active testing (medical diagnosis, experimental design, group testing), yet prior theoretical guarantees were often vacuous in practice. The tight, entropy-scaled bounds here provide practitioners with a concrete, interpretable performance guarantee under a mild and empirically validated assumption (δ-unpredictability with δ ≈ 0.17–0.22 on real datasets). Open questions remain: how to verify δ-unpredictability a priori for a given test set, whether the BSC i.i.d. noise assumption generalizes to correlated or Y-dependent noise, and how to incorporate the confidence parameter γ more explicitly into the bounds.
