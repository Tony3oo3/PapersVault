## Problem

High-fidelity fMRI-to-image reconstruction has been demonstrated, but all prior approaches require single-subject models trained on dozens of hours of expensive fMRI data, making them impractical for clinical assessment or brain-computer interface applications. No existing method can produce quality reconstructions from a single MRI visit (~1 hour of scanning) or generalize across subjects with different brain geometries and functional organizations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241794/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Input is a single-trial fMRI sample: flattened spatial patterns of 13,000–18,000 voxels from the 'nsdgeneral' posterior cortical region in 1.8-mm native volume space, Z-scored per voxel using training-set statistics. The primary output is a 768×768 reconstructed image produced by a two-stage diffusion pipeline (SDXL unCLIP followed by base SDXL image-to-image refinement guided by predicted captions, then 4:1 weighted averaging with a low-level VAE output). Secondary outputs include a natural-language image caption (via a frozen GIT model) and an OpenCLIP ViT-bigG/14 embedding for nearest-neighbor image/brain retrieval.

## Method

The key architectural innovation is a shared-subject functional alignment step: each subject has its own linear ridge-regression layer (with weight decay) mapping native-space fMRI voxels to a common 4096-dim latent, after which a single shared MLP backbone (4 residual blocks) and a diffusion prior trained from scratch map to OpenCLIP ViT-bigG/14 image token embeddings (256×1664). The model is pretrained end-to-end on 7 subjects (30–40 hours each) with equal sampling per subject, then fine-tuned on a held-out 8th subject using as little as 1 hour of data. Three jointly optimized losses drive training: a diffusion-prior loss aligning fMRI latents to CLIP image space, a contrastive BiMixCo/SoftCLIP retrieval loss on a separate MLP projector, and a low-level loss combining L1 on SD VAE latents with a VICRegL SoftCLIP auxiliary term. At inference, the diffusion prior's CLIP embeddings feed a fine-tuned SDXL unCLIP model (cross-attention layers retrained to accept image embeddings instead of text), whose output is refined via base SDXL image-to-image using predicted captions, and finally blended with the low-level submodule's VAE reconstruction.

## Results

On the full Natural Scenes Dataset (40 sessions), MindEye2 achieves state-of-the-art performance across nearly all reconstruction and retrieval metrics: 0.322 PixCorr, 0.431 SSIM, 96.1% Alex(2), 95.4% Inception, 93.0% CLIP, and 98.8%/98.3% image/brain retrieval. Critically, with only 1 hour of fine-tuning data (2.5% of the full dataset), MindEye2 still achieves 0.419 SSIM, 90.6% Alex(5), 81.2% Inception, and 79.0% image retrieval—competitive with single-subject models trained on 40× more data. Human preference experiments confirm refined reconstructions are preferred 71.94% of the time over unrefined ones, and correct reconstructions are identified 97.82% of the time in two-alternative forced-choice judgments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MindEye2 outperforms MindEye1 (its direct predecessor) on nearly all metrics with full data: SSIM improves from 0.360 to 0.431, Alex(2) from 92.8% to 96.1%, and image retrieval from 90.0% to 98.8%, while CLIP identification is slightly lower (93.0% vs. 93.3%). Against Brain Diffuser (Ozcelik & VanRullen, 2023), MindEye2 achieves dramatically better retrieval (98.8% vs. 18.8% image retrieval) and higher reconstruction quality across all low- and high-level metrics. Compared to Takagi & Nishimoto (2023), MindEye2 leads on every reported metric, with particularly large gaps in Alex(2) (96.1% vs. 78.9%) and Inception (95.4% vs. 83.8%). The most striking result is the 1-hour setting: MindEye2 fine-tuned on just 1 hour of data (79.0% image retrieval, 90.6% Alex(5)) substantially outperforms Brain Diffuser trained on the same 1 hour (57.4% brain retrieval in Table 3 context), demonstrating that cross-subject pretraining is the key enabler for data-efficient decoding. For captioning, MindEye2 outperforms both UniBrain (Mai & Zhang, 2023) and Ferrante et al. (2023) on METEOR (0.248 vs. 0.170) and ROUGE-L (0.326 vs. 0.225) with COCO captions, and on METEOR (0.344 vs. 0.305) with GIT captions.

## Relation to Prior Work

MindEye2 extends MindEye1 (Scotti et al., NeurIPS 2023) by replacing its per-subject training with a shared-subject architecture that uses subject-specific ridge regression to map fMRI voxels into a common 4096-dim latent space, followed by a shared MLP backbone, diffusion prior (following DALL-E 2's approach), and submodules. It upgrades the target embedding space from CLIP ViT-L/14 to OpenCLIP ViT-bigG/14 and replaces Versatile Diffusion with a custom SDXL unCLIP model fine-tuned to accept image embeddings. The key conceptual departure from prior cross-subject work (e.g., Ferrante et al., 2023a) is that the simple linear alignment does not require subjects to view shared stimuli, enabling pretraining on entirely unique per-subject image sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reducing required fMRI scanning from ~40 hours to 1 hour makes brain-to-image decoding practically feasible for clinical and BCI applications, including potential communication for locked-in patients. However, the approach is currently limited to natural scene images (COCO distribution), fMRI's sensitivity to head movement constrains real-time use, and generalization to mental imagery or other image domains remains untested. An open question is whether the simple ridge-regression alignment generalizes beyond the NSD's specific experimental protocol, and whether the 1-hour result holds for subjects with atypical cortical organization.
