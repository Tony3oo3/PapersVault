## Problem

The robot learning community has increasingly invested in scaling physics simulators (MuJoCo, Isaac Gym, Brax) and sim2real transfer techniques as the primary route to generalizable manipulation, yet this paradigm is misaligned with the actual requirements of in-the-wild deployment. The paper argues that no amount of simulator scaling can bridge the gap to diverse, contact-rich real-world scenes or encode human-preference compliance, and that the community risks mistaking simulation benchmarks for meaningful progress toward useful robots.

## Input / Output

As a position paper, this work does not propose a new algorithmic pipeline but instead specifies the desiderata for the target system: a manipulation agent that accepts a language task specification and RGB visual observations and directly executes a compliant action trajectory with zero task-specific fine-tuning or exploratory interaction. The critical output constraint is not merely task completion but preference compliance—e.g., repositioning a laptop before grasping a full cup—implying the policy must internalize soft, context-dependent behavioral norms rather than optimize a fixed reward.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a structured two-part critique. For the 'not sufficient' claim, it argues that (a) simulators cannot faithfully model the high-dimensional contact dynamics of everyday manipulation (pouring, chopping, folding), (b) sim2real transfer requires solving a harder forward-dynamics problem than the manipulation policy itself, and (c) simulation success rates show weak correlation with real-world performance (citing Dasari et al. 2023, Pumacay et al. 2024). For the 'not necessary' claim, it identifies three scalable alternatives: passive video pretraining for visual and action priors (R3M, VIP, Track2Act), democratized real-world data collection via wearable tools (UMI) and teleoperation (ALOHA) with embodiment-agnostic action spaces (Open-X), and preference-based fine-tuning with on-arm safety constraints for compliance. The argument is grounded in concrete manipulation examples and a taxonomy of sim2real techniques (domain randomization, domain adaptation, reality checkpoints, sensor calibration) to show why each falls short at the diversity scale required for in-the-wild deployment.

## Results

This is a position paper with no quantitative experiments or results tables. The paper's central claim is that scaling robotic simulators is neither necessary nor sufficient for achieving zero-shot deployable, human-preference-compliant manipulation in diverse real-world settings. The argument is supported by conceptual reasoning and citations of prior work (e.g., Dasari et al., 2023 showing poor correlation between simulated and real-world success rates; Pumacay et al., 2024 showing scaling in simulation does not straightforwardly translate to real-world applicability), rather than by new empirical benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a position paper, this work does not propose a new method or run controlled experiments against baselines. Instead, it critiques the simulation-based paradigm by contrasting it with alternative data sources and training strategies. The paper argues that passive web video pre-training (e.g., R3M, VIP, Track2Act) and scalable real-world data collection tools (e.g., ALOHA teleoperation, DexCap, Universal Manipulation Interface) offer more tractable and higher-fidelity routes to generalizable manipulation than scaling simulators. It further contends that safety and human-preference compliance are better addressed through real-world constraints, preference-based optimization, and human-in-the-loop fine-tuning rather than through simulated trial-and-error. The paper does not claim to outperform any specific baseline; rather, it reframes the research agenda away from simulation scaling toward real-world data and deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper departs from the dominant sim2real paradigm in robot manipulation (exemplified by domain randomization in MuJoCo, Isaac Gym, and related frameworks) by systematically arguing that scaling simulation is neither necessary nor sufficient for in-the-wild manipulation. Rather than proposing a new method, it repurposes existing threads—passive video pretraining (R3M, VIP, Track2Act), human preference learning, and scalable real-world data collection (ALOHA, Open-X Embodiment)—as a more principled alternative to simulation-centric training. It also draws on the broader ML success narrative (GPT-4, Segment Anything) to highlight how manipulation lags behind vision and NLP in zero-shot generalization, framing the gap as a methodological choice rather than an inherent difficulty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a significant allocation of community resources toward simulator engineering and domain randomization, redirecting attention toward real-world data collection, video-based pretraining, and human-preference compliance as more tractable paths to generalizable manipulation. Key open questions include whether the proposed alternatives (passive video, teleoperation at scale) can match the parallelism and cost advantages of simulation, and whether the sim2real gap will narrow as simulators improve. As a position paper with conceptual arguments and citations rather than new empirical results, its impact depends on whether the community finds the framing persuasive enough to shift research priorities.
