## Problem

Existing meta-learning Bayesian optimization methods rely on GP surrogate models that scale poorly, are sensitive to varying observation scales and noise types across tasks, and treat task similarity deterministically, leading to unreliable adaptation when a new task differs significantly or has few observations. The paper addresses how to meta-learn BO that is scalable, robust to heterogeneous scales and noise, and explicitly accounts for task uncertainty.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241587/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes configuration-objective pairs from T related tasks (meta-data) and sequentially collected evaluations on the target task, operating on tabular search spaces (discrete and continuous hyperparameters or architecture parameters). It outputs the next query point by maximizing a composite acquisition function that combines a meta-learned probabilistic classifier (via Thompson sampling over the task embedding posterior) with a gradient boosting residual model. The task embedding posterior, approximated via Laplace's method, is an intermediate output that drives both exploration and the Bayesian adaptation step.

## Method

MALIBO extends likelihood-free BO (LFBO) with meta-learning by replacing the surrogate model with a structured neural network: a shared ResFFN feature mapping φ, a task-agnostic mean prediction layer m that identifies promising regions across tasks, and a task-specific linear layer h_zt parameterized by a low-dimensional task embedding z_t. During meta-training, the LFBO classification loss is augmented with a regularization term that matches the empirical distribution of task embeddings to a Gaussian prior via marginal CDF matching (KS-test style) and covariance matching, enabling tractable Bayesian inference at adaptation time. For a new task, the task embedding posterior is approximated with a Laplace fit around the MAP estimate, and Thompson sampling over this posterior provides uncertainty-aware exploration. A gradient boosting residual model is added after a few initial iterations to correct errors from the meta-learned model, ensuring robust performance even when meta-learning fails or the target task deviates from the meta-data distribution.

## Results

MALIBO is evaluated on three AutoML benchmarks (NASBench201, HPOBench, HPO-B) and synthetic function ensembles (Hartmann3D, Forrester, Branin) using normalized regret as the primary metric, reported as mean ± standard error over 100 random runs. MALIBO consistently achieves the best or near-best anytime performance across all benchmarks, with particularly large margins on HPOBench where GP-based methods struggle due to abrupt value changes and varying scales across tasks. On the Hartmann3D noise robustness experiment (multiplicative noise with ε ∈ {0, 0.1, 1.0}), MALIBO maintains strong performance across all noise levels while GP-based methods (especially RGPE) degrade significantly. Runtime analysis shows MALIBO is the second-fastest meta-learning method, with only negligible overhead over LFBO due to the Laplace approximation and latent feature computation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MALIBO outperforms all 13 baselines on the aggregated normalized regret across NASBench201, HPOBench, and HPO-B. Against GP (Snoek et al.) and BaNNER, MALIBO achieves substantially lower regret, particularly on HPOBench where the regression objective with varying scales violates GP smoothness assumptions; PFN is comparable to GP except on NASBench201. Against ABLR, RGPE, and FSBO, MALIBO is clearly superior on NASBench201 and HPOBench (limited meta-data settings), while these GP-based meta-learning methods only recover on the larger HPO-B benchmark. Against LFBO (non-meta-learning), MALIBO provides much stronger warm-starting and lower early regret, while LFBO+BB matches MALIBO's initial performance but ultimately converges to worse final regret due to search-space pruning. Against MetaBO, MALIBO is more robust: MetaBO shows strong warm-starting on HPOBench but fails on NASBench201 due to higher task diversity. On the Hartmann3D multiplicative noise experiment, MALIBO and LFBO remain stable across all noise levels, while RGPE and ABLR degrade sharply as noise increases, and GC3P's quantile transformation does not translate to a well-known noise model.

## Relation to Prior Work

MALIBO sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: likelihood-free Bayesian optimization (LFBO, Song et al. 2022), which bypasses surrogate models by directly learning acquisition functions via weighted classification, and meta-learning for BO (ABLR, BaNNER, GC3P), which uses neural networks to share knowledge across related tasks. The key conceptual departure is combining these: rather than meta-learning a surrogate model (which inherits GP scalability limits and noise/scale assumptions), MALIBO meta-learns the acquisition function itself in a task-agnostic feature space, with a probabilistic task embedding regularized toward a Gaussian prior (following Saseendran et al., 2021). Thompson sampling over the task embedding posterior and a gradient boosting residual model are added to handle exploration and distribution shift, respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners running HPO or NAS with limited evaluation budgets, MALIBO offers a scalable, assumption-light alternative to GP-based meta-learning that handles heterogeneous scales and noise types across tasks without normalization or copula fitting. The gradient boosting residual model provides a practical safety net when meta-data is unrepresentative, though its interaction with the meta-learned model (sequential error correction rather than explicit weighting) remains somewhat ad-hoc. Open questions include whether the uni-modal Gaussian prior is sufficient for highly diverse task collections, how the exploration-exploitation threshold τ should be adapted dynamically, and whether the Laplace approximation's speed advantage over HMC/SVI holds in higher-dimensional embedding spaces.
