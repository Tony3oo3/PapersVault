## Problem

One-shot structured pruning of large vision and language models remains challenging because existing post-training methods (e.g., ZipLM) are computationally expensive, memory-intensive, and cannot scale beyond ~340M parameters. The paper addresses this by asking whether a combinatorial optimization framework can achieve high-quality structured pruning at scale (up to 30B parameters) without any retraining, while being both time- and memory-efficient on a single GPU.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained model (ResNet, MobileNet, or OPT family) and a small calibration set (500 image samples for vision models; 128 segments of 2048 tokens from C4 for LLMs) and produces a structured pruned model with specific channels, neurons, or attention heads removed. Pruning is applied layer-by-layer sequentially, where each layer's objective uses the dense model's feature maps as targets. The output model achieves a user-specified speedup ratio (e.g., 2×, 3×) with no further fine-tuning, and the entire pruning process runs on a single 32GB V100 GPU by loading one layer at a time.

## Method

OSSCAR formulates structured pruning as a Mixed Integer Quadratic Program (MIQP) with a layer-wise reconstruction objective, then exploits the fact that groups of weights (channels, heads) share identical quadratic coefficients to reformulate the Hessian from hundreds of millions of entries down to a manageable size (e.g., 27k×27k for OPT-30B). By fixing the binary pruning variables, the continuous subproblem has a closed-form solution, reducing the problem to a pure combinatorial search over which groups to prune. The key algorithmic contribution is a local search that uses low-rank matrix updates (Proposition 4.1) to compute the objective change in O(t·d₁(d₁+d₂)) time when adding or removing t rows, rather than re-solving the full quadratic. The algorithm iteratively expands the pruned set while swapping in higher-impact groups, balancing greedy pruning (large p̂) with local exploration (small p̂), and achieves 6–8× speedup over ZipLM with substantially lower perplexity.

## Results

On OPT-2.7B at 2× inference speedup, OSSCAR achieves a WikiText2 perplexity of 29.48, which is 125× lower than ZipLM (3611) and 407× lower than MP (12793). On ResNet50 (ImageNet) at 1.9× speedup, OSSCAR achieves 38.45% top-1 accuracy versus 29.23% (Lasso) and 28.87% (ThiNet), a ~10 percentage-point improvement. OSSCAR is 6–8× faster than ZipLM in pruning time (e.g., 2842s vs. 22839s for OPT-2.7B) and scales to OPT-30B on a single 32GB V100 GPU, a model size 100× larger than what prior structured pruning methods handle.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSSCAR dominates all baselines at moderate-to-high speedup ratios. Against ZipLM (the prior state-of-the-art for one-shot LLM pruning), OSSCAR achieves 125× lower perplexity on OPT-2.7B at 2× speedup (29.48 vs. 3611) while being 6–8× faster in pruning time; ZipLM also cannot run on models ≥6.7B due to memory constraints. Against MP and MP+, the gap widens dramatically with speedup: at 2× on OPT-2.7B, OSSCAR's perplexity is 29.48 versus 12793 (MP) and 12003 (MP+). On vision models, OSSCAR outperforms Lasso and ThiNet by ~10 percentage points on ResNet50 at 1.9× speedup (38.45% vs. 29.23%/28.87%), and by 15–30% on ResNet20 and MobileNetV1 at higher speedups. CHIP and FPGM perform poorly across all vision settings (often below 20% accuracy at 1.4× on ResNet50), as they do not directly minimize the layer-wise reconstruction objective that OSSCAR optimizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the layer-wise reconstruction objective introduced by He et al. (2017) and Luo et al. (2017) for CNN channel pruning, and extends it to the one-shot (post-training) setting for LLMs following ZipLM (Kurtić et al., 2023). The key departure is formulating structured pruning as a Mixed Integer Quadratic Program (MIQP) and solving it via a novel local combinatorial search that exploits low-rank matrix updates, drawing on the discrete optimization literature for sparse linear models (Hazimeh & Mazumder, 2020; Hazimeh et al., 2023). Unlike prior one-shot methods (Kwon et al., 2022; Kurtić et al., 2023) that relied on Hessian-based local models or sensitivity coefficients and were limited to models under 340M parameters, OSSCAR's reformulation exploits shared quadratic coefficients across variable groups to reduce the effective Hessian size, enabling scaling to 30B-parameter models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OSSCAR is the first structured pruning framework to handle models up to 30 billion parameters (100× larger than prior work) using a single 32GB GPU, making one-shot structured pruning of large LLMs practically accessible. The 6–8× speedup over ZipLM while achieving substantially better perplexity at higher sparsity levels (e.g., 29.5 vs. 3611 at 2× speedup for OPT-2.7B) demonstrates that the combinatorial optimization approach is both more accurate and more efficient than sensitivity-based heuristics. Open questions include whether the layer-wise greedy strategy can be improved by joint cross-layer optimization, whether the approach extends to other architectures (e.g., LLaMA, Mistral), and whether the calibration data requirements (128–2048 sequences) can be further reduced without significant quality loss.
