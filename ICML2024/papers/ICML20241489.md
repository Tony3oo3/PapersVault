## Problem

Existing primal-dual algorithms for constrained MDPs only guarantee sublinear weak regret, which permits error cancellations where a constraint violation in one episode is offset by over-satisfaction in another, leaving the agent unsafe during learning. Efroni et al. (2020) posed the open question of whether any primal-dual method can achieve sublinear strong regret (summing only positive error terms) in an unknown CMDP. This paper provides the first affirmative answer.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on a tabular finite-horizon CMDP with S states, A actions, H steps, and I safety constraints, requiring no prior knowledge of transitions, rewards, or constraint rewards. Each episode the agent selects a non-stationary policy, plays it for H steps, and observes stochastic reward, constraint reward, and next-state samples. The output is a per-episode policy and dual variables (Lagrange multipliers), with the guarantee that both the objective suboptimality and the maximum constraint violation sum to at most Õ(K^0.93) over K episodes, where the constants depend polynomially on S, A, H, I, and the Slater gap Ξ.

## Method

The core mechanism is a regularized primal-dual ascent-descent scheme that adds entropy regularization to the policy (primal) and a quadratic term to the Lagrange multipliers (dual), inducing strict concavity in the state-action occupancy measure and strong convexity in the dual. This enables last-iterate convergence to the regularized saddle point, which is then shown to approximate the unregularized optimum by tuning the regularization parameter τ and dual domain diameter λ_max. The key technical advance over Ding et al. (2023) is a mirror descent lemma with local norms that handles the unbounded surrogate gradients of the entropy-regularized Q-values without restricting policies to a bounded simplex, yielding closed-form policy updates. For the online setting, the scheme is combined with optimistic exploration: the agent maintains empirical estimates of rewards, constraint rewards, and transitions, adds confidence bonuses, and also optimistically estimates the entropy term ψ_k to preserve the regularized structure under model uncertainty. The resulting Algorithm 1 achieves Õ(K^0.93) strong regret with η = (H²I)⁻¹ΞK^(−5/7), τ = K^(−1/7), and λ_max = HΞ⁻¹K^(1/14).

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 5.1) establishes that the proposed regularized primal-dual algorithm achieves sublinear strong regret of $\tilde{O}(K^{0.93})$ for both the objective suboptimality and the constraint violation, with hyperparameters $\tau = K^{-1/7}$, $\eta = (H^2 I)^{-1}\Xi K^{-5/7}$, and $\lambda_{\max} = H\Xi^{-1}K^{1/14}$. This is the first primal-dual algorithm to provably achieve sublinear regret without allowing error cancellations. In numerical experiments (S=A=H=5, one constraint), the regularized algorithm exhibits sublinear strong regret while the vanilla primal-dual and dual algorithms of Efroni et al. (2020) suffer linear strong regret due to persistent oscillations, despite achieving sublinear (even constant) weak regret.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its regularized primal-dual algorithm (Algorithm 1) against the vanilla primal-dual algorithm of Efroni et al. (2020) (Equations 3–4 with optimistic exploration) and the vanilla dual algorithm of Efroni et al. (2020). On the strong regret metric (which disallows error cancellations), the regularized algorithms clearly outperform both vanilla baselines: the vanilla methods exhibit linear strong regret due to persistent oscillations between safe and unsafe episodes, while the regularized methods achieve sublinear strong regret. On the weaker (standard) regret metric, the vanilla algorithms actually perform better, with the vanilla primal-dual even showing near-constant suboptimality regret. The key distinction is that the vanilla algorithms' weak regret can be misleadingly low (or even zero) while the policies remain unsafe in roughly half of all episodes, whereas the regularized algorithm guarantees that the fraction of episodes violating constraints by at least $\varepsilon$ vanishes as $K \to \infty$. The regularized dual algorithm (Equations 11–12) shows similar numerical performance to the regularized primal-dual but at higher computational cost due to requiring a planning subroutine rather than just policy evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on two threads: the regularization-based last-iterate convergence framework of Ding et al. (2023), which was limited to a single constraint and a known CMDP, and the optimistic exploration paradigm of Efroni et al. (2020), which established weak regret bounds for primal-dual CMDP algorithms but explicitly left the strong regret question open. The key technical departure from Ding et al. (2023) is a modified mirror-descent analysis using local norms that avoids the need for Bregman projections onto a restricted simplex, yielding closed-form policy updates and enabling generalization to multiple constraints. The online extension further requires incorporating model uncertainty into the regularized primal-dual analysis, a step Ding et al. (2023) left as an open question.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first primal-dual algorithm with a provable sublinear strong regret guarantee for CMDPs, resolving an open question posed by Efroni et al. (2020) and providing a theoretical foundation for the safety properties of the algorithm class most widely used in practice. However, the achieved rate of Õ(K^0.93) is far from the optimal Õ(K^0.5) known for LP-based methods and for weak regret, leaving substantial room for improvement. Open questions include tightening the analysis, extending to function approximation and infinite-horizon settings, and understanding whether the regularization overhead is fundamentally necessary or an artifact of the current proof technique. The simulation confirming that vanilla primal-dual suffers linear strong regret in practice validates that error cancellations are not merely a theoretical artifact.
