## Problem

Most existing SNN compression methods perform unstructured, connection-level pruning, which produces irregular sparsity patterns that are difficult to deploy on neuromorphic hardware. This paper addresses the need for structured, channel-level pruning in deep SNNs that can adaptively discover lightweight architectures during training, inspired by biological synaptic plasticity mechanisms where connections are dynamically established and eliminated.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241168/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny ImageNet) encoded into spike sequences over 4 time steps, or neuromorphic event data (DVS-CIFAR10) integrated into frames over 20 time steps. During training, the framework outputs a binary mask tracking which convolutional channels are active; after training, pruned channels are physically removed to yield a compact SNN that still produces class labels. Two hyperparameters control the process: a target sparsity ratio p% and a per-iteration mask update ratio q%.

## Method

The SCA framework jointly optimizes network weights and topology during supervised training using surrogate gradient backpropagation with L1 regularization. Channel importance is scored by the mean L1 norm of membrane potentials across the training set (Eq. 4), so channels with low spiking activity are deemed redundant. Each iteration prunes an additional q% of the lowest-scoring channels while regrowing q% of previously pruned channels selected by the magnitude of their Batch Normalization gamma gradients, maintaining a constant overall sparsity of p%. A binary mask records structural state, and after convergence the masked-out channels are permanently removed, yielding a regular, hardware-friendly architecture. The framework is architecture-agnostic, supporting both post-activation (VGG, ResNet) and pre-activation (Pre-ResNet) SNN topologies.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with VGG16, SCA achieves 91.14% accuracy at 9.31% remaining parameters (only −0.88% loss) and even improves accuracy by +0.53% at 28.39% parameters. On CIFAR-100, SCA reaches 64.89% accuracy at 23.52% parameters with a +0.64% improvement over the unpruned baseline. On DVS-CIFAR10, SCA compresses to 6.95% parameters with less than 1% accuracy drop, and improves accuracy by +0.90% at 21.73% parameters. On Tiny-ImageNet, SCA maintains 49.33% accuracy at 30.60% parameters with only −0.19% loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among structured (channel-level) baselines, SCA outperforms PAC-based (Chowdhury et al., 2021) on CIFAR-10 at comparable compression: SCA achieves 91.14% at 9.31% connectivity (−0.88% loss) versus PAC's 90.10% at 7.00% (−1.06% loss). On CIFAR-100, however, PAC-based retains higher accuracy (68.10% at 10.70% connectivity) than SCA (64.89% at 23.52%), though SCA uses a deeper VGG16 backbone. Against unstructured (weight-level) methods, SCA trades some compression ratio for hardware-friendly regularity: Grad R (Chen et al., 2021) and Dendritic-based (Chen et al., 2022) reach higher accuracy at lower connectivity (e.g., 92.84% at 5.08% and 0.75% respectively), but their irregular sparsity is less amenable to hardware deployment. On DVS-CIFAR10, SCA achieves a +0.90% accuracy gain at 21.73% connectivity, while ESL-SNNs (Shen et al., 2023) shows only −0.28% at 10% connectivity but uses a heavier VGG8 architecture. On Tiny-ImageNet, SCA matches Attention-based (Kundu et al., 2021) within 0.19% accuracy at similar compression levels (30.60% vs. 40.00% connectivity), confirming generalizability to complex datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of structured pruning (well-studied in ANNs) and SNN compression (dominated by unstructured, weight-level methods like Grad R, Spike-Thrift, and ADMM-based approaches). It repurposes the biological concept of synaptic plasticity—where connections are dynamically established and eliminated—into a training-time mechanism that jointly prunes and regrows convolutional channels, using spiking activity (membrane potential magnitude) as the importance signal rather than weight magnitude. The dynamic regrowth criterion, based on BN gamma gradients, draws on the observation that pruned channels with high gradient signals may still be useful, distinguishing it from one-shot structured pruning methods like PAC-based channel selection (Chowdhury et al., 2021) or SD-SNN (Han et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Structured channel-level pruning produces regular, hardware-friendly network topologies that are far more deployable on neuromorphic chips than the irregular sparse masks produced by unstructured weight pruning—a critical practical gap for the SNN community. The finding that moderate compression (20–30% parameters) can actually improve accuracy suggests that standard deep SNN architectures contain genuinely redundant channels, and that activity-based pruning identifies them more effectively than weight-norm heuristics. Open questions include scalability to much larger networks (e.g., ImageNet-scale), applicability to non-convolutional SNN architectures (transformer-based SNNs), and whether the BN-gamma regrowth criterion generalizes beyond the specific architectures tested. The paper does not provide hardware validation on actual neuromorphic chips, leaving the deployment claims somewhat aspirational.
