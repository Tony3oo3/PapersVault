## Problem

Standard softmax self-attention in transformers suffers from two fundamental issues: (1) the output representation collapses to rank 1 as depth increases, severely limiting representational capacity, and (2) the steady-state solution is highly sensitive to input perturbations, making models vulnerable to adversarial attacks and noise. The paper identifies the root cause by showing that self-attention is a discretization of an autonomous state-space model that implicitly minimizes nonlocal total variation, thereby promoting smoothness and erasing high-frequency detail.

## Input / Output

For vision tasks, the model consumes standard RGB images (ImageNet at 224×224, ADE20K at 518×518) split into 16×16 patches, producing class labels or per-pixel segmentation masks. For language modeling, it takes tokenized text sequences (WikiText-103, context length 256) and outputs next-token predictions. A distinctive aspect is that the PID-attention mechanism internally references the value embeddings from the first layer (scaled by a hyperparameter β ∈ (0,1]) as a 'reference signal' against which each subsequent layer's output is compared, and it accumulates error terms across all preceding layers within a single forward pass.

## Method

The paper reinterprets self-attention as a one-step Euler discretization of an autonomous state-space model (SSM) that performs gradient flow on a nonlocal total variation functional, proving via Jordan decomposition that the steady-state solution has rank 1 and depends on the initial condition. To counteract this, a PID closed-loop controller is integrated into the SSM with a reference signal f(x) = βv(x,0) (the scaled first-layer value embedding). The resulting PID-attention (Definition 1) augments standard softmax attention with three correction terms: a proportional term λ_P·e^ℓ (immediate error from reference), an integral term λ_I·Σe^m (accumulated past errors), and a derivative term λ_D·(e^ℓ − e^{ℓ−1}) (rate of error change). Theoretically, the P-term alone guarantees the steady-state solution retains the full rank of the input and bounds perturbation error by β·ε̄, the D-term further damps eigenvalues by a factor 1/(1+λ_D) to suppress rapid oscillations, and the I-term ensures asymptotic stability for any positive gains. The P- and PI-controlled SSMs are further shown to correspond to gradient descent and Bregman iteration, respectively, on a regularized energy functional E(v,f) = J(v) + G(v,f) that balances smoothness against data fidelity to the reference.

## Results

On ImageNet classification, PID DeiT achieves 73.13% top-1 accuracy on clean data (vs. 72.17% for Softmax DeiT) and substantially improves robustness under adversarial attacks: 38.52% under FGSM (+4.88 pts), 15.08% under PGD (+3.06 pts), and 70.87% under noise (+1.67 pts). On ADE20K segmentation, PID DeiT backbone yields 37.42% SS MIoU and 38.28% MS MIoU (vs. 35.72/36.68 for DeiT). On WikiText-103 language modeling, PIDformer reduces test perplexity to 33.45 (vs. 34.29 for the softmax transformer baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PID DeiT consistently outperforms the Softmax DeiT baseline (Touvron et al., 2021) across all evaluated settings: +0.96 pts clean top-1, +4.88 pts under FGSM, +3.06 pts under PGD, and +2.88 pts under SPSA, with similar gains on OOD benchmarks (ImageNet-A, -R, -C, -O). Against the FAN robust vision transformer (Zhou et al., 2022), PID FAN improves clean top-1 from 77.11% to 77.40% and FGSM top-1 from 38.27% to 39.61%, demonstrating that PID control is complementary to existing robustness techniques. On WikiText-103, PIDformer beats the Nyströmformer-based softmax transformer (Xiong et al., 2021) by 0.71 in valid PPL (32.44 vs. 33.15) and 0.84 in test PPL (33.45 vs. 34.29). The paper also reports comparisons against FeatScale (Wang et al., 2022) in the appendix, where PIDformer shows competitive or superior robustness while maintaining clean accuracy. Overall, PIDformer wins on every reported metric across all four baselines and all three task domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the standard transformer's self-attention mechanism (Vaswani et al., 2017) as a discretization of an autonomous state-space model that performs gradient descent on nonlocal total variation (Gilboa & Osher, 2008), thereby inheriting the smoothness-promoting dynamics that cause rank collapse. Prior work on over-smoothing (Shi et al., 2022; Dong et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2022) identified the rank-collapse pathology from graph-theoretic and Fourier-domain perspectives, while control-theoretic approaches to deep learning (Chen et al., 2021b; Shao et al., 2020) applied feedback to other architectures. PIDformer departs from both lines by integrating a full PID controller directly into the attention computation, with the P-component corresponding to gradient descent on a regularized functional and the PI-component corresponding to Bregman iteration (Yin et al., 2008), yielding a unified control-theoretic explanation for both robustness and representation capacity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SSM interpretation provides a principled, provable mechanism for why softmax attention degrades with depth and is fragile to perturbations, and the PID modification offers a drop-in architectural fix with theoretical guarantees of stability and rank preservation for any positive gain values. Empirically, the approach is orthogonal to and composable with existing anti-oversmoothing methods (e.g., FeatScale) and robustness techniques (e.g., FAN), suggesting broad applicability. Open questions include the sensitivity to the derivative gain λ_D (higher values degrade performance), the lack of analysis for privacy-preserving properties, and whether the control-theoretic framing extends naturally to other attention variants (linear, sparse, or multi-head with cross-attention).
