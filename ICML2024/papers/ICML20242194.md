## Problem

Prior hallucination research in LVLMs has focused almost exclusively on object hallucinations, which can be partially mitigated by bolting on object detectors. Hallucinations in inter-object relationships—such as incorrectly asserting a spatial or action relationship between two present objects—remain under-explored despite being more indicative of true visual scene comprehension. No comprehensive, leakage-free benchmark existed to systematically evaluate and analyze this type of hallucination.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242194/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark presents binary yes/no questions in two tiers: image-level questions ask whether a relationship exists anywhere in the image, while instance-level questions attach colored bounding boxes or masks to specific objects and ask about the relationship between those referenced objects. All questions are grounded in the NoCaps validation set (4,034 images from OpenImage) to prevent overlap with the COCO/Visual Genome data used in LVLM pretraining and instruction tuning. Evaluation follows the POPE protocol, reporting accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score, and yes-ratio over 5 random 1:1 pos-neg subsets.

## Method

R-Bench is constructed via a multi-stage pipeline: COCO captions are parsed into relationship triplets by a scene graph parser to build a comprehensive relationship set; for each NoCaps image, its captions are parsed into triplets and matched against the relationship set to form relationship seeds; GroundingDINO localizes significant objects with bounding boxes, and SAM generates corresponding masks; Llama2-chat-13B then generates yes/no questions conditioned on the captions, seeds, and boxes, followed by ~two weeks of manual filtering (yielding 12,655 questions: 7,883 image-level and 3,768 instance-level). The paper's analytical contribution identifies three co-occurrence patterns driving hallucinations—relationship-relationship, subject-relationship, and relationship-object—by computing conditional probability matrices and showing that high-frequency co-occurring pairs correlate with elevated hallucination rates. Additional experiments on web-collected counterfactual and illusion images reveal that LVLMs frequently ignore visual content in favor of LLM common-sense priors and fail at context-based spatial reasoning.

## Results

On R-Bench image-level questions, Qwen-VL achieves the highest accuracy (79.19%) and F1 score (80.48), followed by mPLUG-Owl2 (73.66% acc, 77.88 F1), LLaVA-1.5 (71.23% acc, 77.28 F1), and InstructBLIP (69.31% acc, 76.04 F1). Performance drops sharply on instance-level questions: the best model (Qwen-VL) reaches only 58.82% accuracy with bounding boxes and 59.84% with masks, while InstructBLIP falls to 51.95% (box) and 55.63% (mask). Compared to object hallucination (Table 4), relationship hallucinations are consistently more severe across all models, with Qwen-VL dropping from 86.57% (object) to 79.19% (relationship) and InstructBLIP from 78.77% to 69.31%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen-VL consistently outperforms the other three LVLMs across all settings, achieving the highest accuracy on image-level (79.19%), box (58.82%), and mask (59.84%) questions, though its advantage narrows on mask-based instance-level questions where mPLUG-Owl2 (55.80%) and InstructBLIP (55.63%) are closer. LLaVA-1.5 and InstructBLIP show the weakest instance-level performance (53.15% and 51.95% box accuracy respectively), indicating poor local visual relationship comprehension despite adequate reference-object recognition (Table 1 shows 71–76% box accuracy for object identification). All four models exhibit a large accuracy gap between image-level and instance-level questions (roughly 15–20 points), revealing that LVLMs can judge whether a relationship exists globally but fail to verify it for specific referenced objects. In the counterfactual and illusion hallucination tests (Table 6), even the best model (Qwen-VL) reaches only 50% counterfactual accuracy and 0% illusion accuracy, confirming that all baselines heavily rely on LLM common-sense priors rather than actual visual content.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the POPE object hallucination evaluation framework (Li et al., 2023e) from object presence to inter-object relationship existence, filling a gap the authors identify in prior LVLM hallucination research. It departs from earlier relationship-hallucination attempts (Chen et al., 2023c; Villa et al., 2023) by constructing the benchmark on the NoCaps validation set to avoid data leakage from Visual Genome/COCO pretraining, and by introducing instance-level questions with colored bounding boxes and masks that test local visual comprehension. The construction pipeline repurposes GroundingDINO for object localization, SAM for mask generation, and Llama2-chat-13B for question generation, combining tools from the LVLM ecosystem into a unified evaluation protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that relationship hallucinations are systematically worse than object hallucinations across all evaluated models, and that LVLMs rely on LLM common sense rather than visual evidence (counterfactual accuracy ≤50%), provides a concrete diagnostic for why current LVLMs fail at scene comprehension. The long-tail distribution between object words (3.6 per caption) and relationship words (1.3 per caption) in training data offers a plausible mechanistic explanation. Open questions include whether fine-grained image-text alignment can close the image-level/instance-level gap, how to mitigate the LLM common-sense override, and whether the findings generalize to stronger closed-source models like GPT-4V that were not evaluated due to budget constraints.
