## Problem

During autoregressive inference, transformer LLMs must store key-value representations for all past tokens in a KV cache that grows linearly with sequence length and batch size, quickly becoming memory-prohibitive for long contexts or large serving batches. Existing mitigations—Grouped Query Attention (GQA) requires architectural changes at pre-training time, while cache eviction policies (H2O, TOVA) cause severe downstream performance degradation at high compression ratios. The paper asks whether a pretrained LLM can be retrofitted to adaptively compress its own KV cache at inference time without adding parameters and without losing downstream accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM checkpoint (Llama 2 7B, 13B, or 70B) and a negligible fraction of its original pre-training data (~2% for 2× compression, ~4% for 4×) as continued pre-training input, producing a DMC-transformed model with no additional parameters. At inference, the model consumes a text sequence and generates tokens autoregressively while maintaining a sub-linearly growing KV cache whose length per head is determined by learned append/accumulate decisions. The first dimension of each query and key vector is repurposed to carry the decision and importance scores, so the effective attention dimensionality is reduced by one per head.

## Method

DMC introduces a per-head, per-layer binary decision at each time step: whether to append the current key-value pair to the cache or to accumulate it (via a weighted average) into the last cached element, with the weight given by a learned importance score. Both the decision α and importance ω are extracted from the first dimension of k_t and q_t respectively, avoiding any new parameters. Training uses a Gumbel-sigmoid relaxation of the discrete decision, a continuous partial-accumulation recurrence (Eq. 9) that generalizes the inference-time update to α∈[0,1], and an additive attention mask that blocks queries from attending to intermediate KV states that would be discarded at inference, thereby preventing train-inference mismatch. A one-sided compression-ratio loss (Eq. 10) is added to the language modeling objective to push the global CR toward a target that is linearly annealed during continued pre-training, followed by a short solidifying phase at fixed CR with cosine LR decay. The result is a model where each head and layer learns its own compression ratio, with deeper layers tending to compress more aggressively, and the overall cache growing sub-linearly rather than linearly with sequence length.

## Results

DMC 4× on Llama 2 7B retains MMLU 43.9 (vs. 44.6 original), CS-QA 70.2 (vs. 70.5), and even improves HumanEval to 16.5 (vs. 14.0). At 13B, DMC 4× achieves MMLU 54.2, CS-QA 73.2, and HumanEval 22.0, all within 0.3 points of the original. For Llama 2 70B (GQA 8× + DMC 2× = 16× total compression), performance is essentially unchanged: MMLU 68.8, CS-QA 77.9, HumanEval 29.9. In practice, DMC 4× yields a 3.4×–3.7× throughput increase on NVIDIA H100/A100 GPUs by enabling larger batch sizes within the same memory budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GQA (Ainslie et al., 2023), DMC wins on every metric at both 2× and 4× CR for 7B and 13B; the MMLU gap widens with compression (e.g., +9.2 at 4× for 7B: 43.9 vs. 34.7), and DMC also outperforms GQA in sample efficiency, reaching target performance in roughly half the fine-tuning steps. Against H2O (Zhang et al., 2023), DMC dominates at 4× CR across all scales—most dramatically on HumanEval (16.5 vs. 4.9 for 7B; 22.0 vs. 8.5 for 13B)—while at 2× CR the two are roughly comparable on MMLU and CS-QA. Against TOVA (Oren et al., 2024), DMC again leads at 4× CR on all metrics (e.g., CS-QA 70.2 vs. 64.2 for 7B; HumanEval 22.0 vs. 2.4 for 13B), and at 70B with 16× total compression DMC matches the original model exactly while TOVA drops 0.7 on MMLU. A key practical advantage is that H2O and TOVA require materializing the full n² attention matrix, making them incompatible with FlashAttention, whereas DMC remains compatible with efficient attention kernels.

## Relation to Prior Work

DMC extends the authors' prior work on Dynamic Token Pooling (Nawrot et al., 2023), which used a learnable boundary predictor to merge token representations in intermediate Transformer layers, by applying the same segmentation-and-merging idea to the KV cache during autoregressive inference and introducing a Gumbel-sigmoid continuous relaxation for end-to-end training. It departs from the dominant GQA paradigm (fixed compression via shared KV heads) and from heuristic eviction policies (H2O, TOVA) by learning an adaptive, per-head, per-layer compression ratio that merges rather than discards tokens. Conceptually, DMC positions itself between vanilla Transformers (linearly growing KV cache) and State Space Models (constant memory), achieving sub-linear memory growth without architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: 3.4–3.7× throughput gains on H100/A100 GPUs with negligible performance loss at 4× compression make DMC a drop-in replacement for KV caching in production LLM serving, enabling longer contexts and larger batches within fixed memory budgets. The finding that DMC and GQA compose multiplicatively (16× total compression on Llama 2 70B with no degradation) broadens its applicability to already-deployed models. Key limitations include negative results when pre-training DMC from scratch (suggesting a chicken-and-egg dependency between representation quality and segmentation decisions), and the requirement for PagedAttention-style variable-length storage. Open questions include whether DMC can be extended to sub-linear (rather than piecewise-constant) cache growth and whether the learned compression schemata can inform architectural design.
