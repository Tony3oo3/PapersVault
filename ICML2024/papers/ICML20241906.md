## Problem

Large Language Models frequently generate toxic language, and existing mitigation strategies—fine-tuning, pre-prompting, and post-processing—suffer from high computational cost, vulnerability to adversarial jail-breaking, or degradation of other model capabilities. While prior work identified 'expert neurons' responsible for encoding specific concepts, the natural intervention of zeroing their activations proved highly sensitive to the number of experts selected, making it impractical across different model architectures and scales. The paper asks whether a hyperparameter-free, soft neural intervention can mitigate toxicity while preserving perplexity and zero-shot reasoning abilities.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LLM (any transformer architecture from 1.5B to 40B parameters), a small toxicity-labeled sentence dataset (500 toxic and 2000 non-toxic sentences from Jigsaw 2018), and standard text prompts at inference time. It produces a permanently modified set of LLM weights—specifically, scaled rows in the weight matrices and bias vectors of linear layers outside attention blocks—yielding a detoxified model deployable with no changes to the inference pipeline. The intervention is static: once weights are modified, the model serves detoxified completions with zero additional computational overhead.

## Method

AURA (AUROC Adaptation) identifies toxicity expert neurons by treating each neuron's maximum pre-activation response across labeled sentences as a binary classifier, then ranking neurons by their AUROC. All neurons with AUROC > 0.5 (above chance) are selected as experts, eliminating the need to tune a cutoff k. Each expert's activation is then dampened by a per-neuron factor α_m = 1 − 2(AUROC_m − 0.5), derived from the Gini coefficient, so that the strongest experts are nearly muted (α_m → 0) while marginal experts are barely affected (α_m → 1). Concretely, the m-th row of the weight matrix W and the corresponding bias entry b in each relevant linear layer are multiplied by α_m, making the intervention a one-time, permanent weight modification. This design removes both hyperparameters (k and α) that plagued earlier strategies—Det_zero (zeroing) and DAMP (uniform dampening)—while achieving comparable or superior toxicity reduction (up to 2.2× on Mistral-7B) with only a 0.72 perplexity increase.

## Results

AURA achieves up to 2.2× reduction in RTP toxicity on Mistral-7B with only a 0.72 perplexity increase, and 2.1× on Falcon-7B with 0.52 PPL increase. Across all seven models (1.5B–40B parameters), AURA consistently outperforms Det_zero and DExperts on toxicity reduction while preserving or even improving perplexity. Zero-shot common-sense reasoning degrades by at most 2 points (Falcon-7B), and AURA shifts toxic data modes to OOD (median +193.46 PPL on toxic corpora) while keeping non-toxic PPL nearly unchanged (+0.59 mean).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AURA outperforms DExperts on GPT2-XL, achieving 1.3× RTP toxicity reduction with a 0.96 PPL *decrease*, while DExperts achieves only 1.2× reduction at a 1.48 PPL increase and requires additional memory for its expert/anti-expert models. Against Det_zero, AURA is more effective on most models (e.g., 2.1× vs. 1.1× on Falcon-7B, 1.8× vs. 1.1× on MPT-7B) and is hyperparameter-free, whereas Det_zero requires a model-specific sweep over k and can catastrophically degrade perplexity when too many neurons are zeroed. The one exception is Mistral-7B, where Det_zero achieves a larger RTP reduction (3.7× vs. 2.2×) due to its SwiGLU architecture, but at the cost of a 4-point drop in zero-shot common-sense performance. CTRL is unable to reduce toxicity while preserving perplexity (PPL jumps to 176.9). Overall, AURA provides the best toxicity-perplexity trade-off across all seven models without any model-dependent hyperparameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Suau et al. (2022), who discovered that expert neurons in LLMs can be manipulated to *induce* concepts in generated text, by showing the same neurons can be *suppressed* to mitigate toxic language. It departs from the dominant toxicity-mitigation paradigm (fine-tuning, RLHF, pre-prompting, output filtering) by intervening on internal neuron activations rather than external quantities, and from the prior hard intervention (Det_zero) by introducing proportional, AUROC-weighted dampening (AURA) that eliminates the need for model-specific hyperparameter search. The method is positioned within the mechanistic-interpretability tradition of expert neurons (Radford et al., 2017) while targeting a practical deployment goal: a one-time weight modification that can be served without altering inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AURA offers a deployment-ready, zero-inference-cost toxicity mitigation that works across model scales (1.5B–40B) and architectures, and notably counteracts adversarial pre-prompts that increase toxicity by ~1.6×, returning the model below its original toxicity level. Key limitations include: the method is validated only for toxicity (not other harmful concepts), it considers only linear layers outside attention blocks, and it is a static intervention that cannot be dynamically toggled. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other concepts, how architectural differences (e.g., SwiGLU in Mistral/Llama) affect expert-neuron interventions, and whether dynamic, context-dependent dampening could further preserve model abilities.
