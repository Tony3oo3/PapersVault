## Problem

Existing plug-and-play diffusion guidance methods (classifier guidance, Diffusion Posterior Sampling) require the conditioning loss to be differentiable, but many practical musical rules—such as note density (involving indicator functions) and chord progression (involving black-box analysis APIs)—are non-differentiable or opaque to backpropagation. This makes it impossible to guide a pre-trained diffusion model toward rule-compliant music without retraining or training surrogate models, which are shown to be unreliable for these rules.

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes Gaussian noise in a 4×16×16 latent space (per 128-step segment) plus optional rule targets (e.g., a 16-dim note density vector, an 8-dim chord progression sequence, or a 12-dim pitch histogram) and produces a 3-channel piano roll at 10 ms resolution (128 pitches × 1024 time steps ≈ 10.24 s) encoding velocity (0–127), binary onsets, and binary sustain pedal. For editing, it additionally accepts a source piano roll and a binary mask specifying which time regions to preserve versus regenerate, with a tunable noise level controlling the degree of modification. Arbitrary-length generation is achieved by concatenating latent codes and applying DiffCollage.

## Method

The core contribution is Stochastic Control Guidance (SCG), which reframes rule-guided sampling as a stochastic optimal control problem over the reverse diffusion SDE. Using path integral control theory, the authors derive that the optimal control policy equals the gradient of the log-likelihood of the rule given the current state; in practice, this is approximated by sampling n candidate next steps at each DDPM iteration, estimating the corresponding clean sample via Tweedie's formula, and selecting the candidate that minimizes the rule loss—requiring only forward evaluation of the (potentially non-differentiable) rule function. The underlying architecture is a latent diffusion model: a VAE compresses 3×128×128 piano-roll segments into 4×16×16 latent codes, and a DiT-XL transformer with rotary position embeddings learns the joint distribution over concatenated latent sequences, trained with classifier-free guidance for dataset-level conditioning (classical performance, classical sheet music, pop). SCG is applied after step 250 of a 1000-step DDPM schedule, and can be combined with gradient-based guidance (classifier or DPS) for composite rules, where the gradient provides a preliminary direction and SCG selects the best stochastic realization along it.

## Results

On unconditional generation, SCG achieves the highest average Overlapping Area (OA) across all three datasets: Maestro (0.943), Muscore (0.934), and Pop (0.939), outperforming all baselines. For individual rule guidance, SCG achieves the lowest loss on both non-differentiable rules—note density (0.131 vs. 0.698 for Classifier, 1.261 for DPS-NN) and chord progression (0.273 vs. 0.723 for Classifier, 0.414 for DPS-NN)—without requiring any surrogate model training. In composite rule guidance, the Classifier+SCG combination achieves the best simultaneous controllability (PH: 0.003, ND: 0.148, CP: 0.284) with a high OA of 0.864.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCG decisively outperforms all gradient-based guidance methods on non-differentiable rules: it reduces note density loss by 5.3× over Classifier (0.131 vs. 0.698) and 9.6× over DPS-NN (0.131 vs. 1.261), and chord progression loss by 2.6× over Classifier (0.273 vs. 0.723) and 1.5× over DPS-NN (0.273 vs. 0.414). DPS-Rule, which directly uses the rule gradient, completely fails on note density (loss 2.508, no improvement over no guidance) because the indicator-based density function has zero gradient almost everywhere, and cannot be applied to chord progression at all due to the black-box music21 API. For the differentiable pitch histogram rule, DPS-NN achieves the lowest loss (0.001) since the surrogate model learns well, while SCG is competitive (0.003). Among unconditional generation baselines, MusicTr is strongest on classical (Maestro 0.903, Muscore 0.901) but weakest on Pop (0.845); CPW is the reverse (Pop 0.899, Maestro 0.801); SCG achieves the best OA on all three datasets. In composite rule guidance, Figaro-expert-learned achieves a slightly higher OA (0.883 vs. 0.874) but requires a source music input (style transfer), making it not directly comparable; MuseCoCo shows poor fine-grained controllability (ND loss 2.734, CP loss 0.821). For editing, SCG achieves lower error than MuseMorphose on note density (27.87% vs. 29.34%) and dramatically lower error than PolyDiffusion on chord progression (12.62% vs. 70.48%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the score-based SDE diffusion framework (Song et al., 2021b) and DDPM sampling (Ho et al., 2020), but departs fundamentally from the gradient-based guidance paradigm established by classifier guidance (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) and Diffusion Posterior Sampling (Chung et al., 2023), both of which require differentiable loss functions. The key conceptual departure is importing path integral control theory (Theodorou et al., 2010) into the diffusion sampling loop, yielding a guidance algorithm (SCG) that only requires forward evaluation of rule functions. The paper also connects to the emerging line of work linking stochastic optimal control to generative modeling (Berner et al., 2022; Vargas et al., 2023), and leverages SDEdit (Meng et al., 2022) for editing, while introducing a DiT-based latent diffusion architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023) tailored to 10ms-resolution piano rolls.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCG is the first training-free, plug-and-play guidance method that handles non-differentiable and black-box rule functions in diffusion models, removing a key barrier for practical controllable generation in domains where constraints are naturally discrete or API-based (e.g., chord progression via music21, note density via thresholding). The stochastic control unification (Proposition 4.2) provides a clean theoretical lens under which classifier guidance, DPS, and SCG are all instances of the same optimal control, which may guide future method design. Limitations include significant computational overhead (multiple forward passes per step; ~20 min for 4 samples at n=16) and a controllability-vs-quality trade-off that requires tuning. The authors note the approach extends beyond music to protein design and physics-simulator-guided imaging, suggesting broader applicability, though these remain untested.
