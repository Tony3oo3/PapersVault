## Problem

Existing methods for improving VLM generalization (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe, PromptSRC) rely on increasingly intricate algorithmic designs for a single model, yet their performance gains have plateaued (e.g., 70.43% → 70.73% on ImageNet new classes). This paper identifies that the knowledge of a single model has been fully exploited and asks whether much weaker VLMs can be leveraged collaboratively to push generalization beyond this ceiling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241336/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a set of text prompts (category names embedded in a template like 'a photo of a {class}') as input, producing a fused classification logit vector over K categories. Four pre-trained CLIP models (RN50, RN101, ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16) each independently process the image and generate their own logits via cosine similarity with text embeddings; the ensemble then combines these logits. For the tuning ensemble, the concatenated image features from all four vision encoders (not the logits) are additionally fed to the weight generator to produce sample-specific weights.

## Method

The paper proposes three ensemble strategies tailored to different resource constraints. (1) Zero-shot Ensemble (ZS_En) assigns a fixed weight of 1.0 to the strongest model (ViT-B/16) and dynamically weights the remaining models by their prediction confidence (max softmax probability) via a softmax-normalized scheme, requiring no training data or compute. (2) Training-free Ensemble (TF_En) performs a greedy search over a discrete weight grid {0.1, …, 1.0} for each weaker model to maximize accuracy on a few-shot base-class training set, again keeping ViT-B/16 at weight 1.0. (3) Tuning Ensemble (T_En) introduces a Sample-Aware Weight Generator (SWIG), a two-layer MLP (f_dim → f_dim/32 → num_weight) that takes concatenated image features from all vision encoders as input and outputs per-sample ensemble weights, trained for 5 epochs on few-shot data. A key design choice is using image features rather than logits as SWIG input to avoid bias toward base-class text embeddings, and the 'weak helps strong' principle is preserved by maintaining ViT-B/16's dominance in the first two strategies.

## Results

On zero-shot generalization across 11 datasets, the proposed ZS_En achieves 67.88% average accuracy, surpassing the best single model CLIP-ViT-B/16 (65.27%) by +2.61%. For base-to-new generalization, PromptSRC+TEn attains 81.11% harmonic mean (HM) accuracy on average, outperforming the previous best PromptSRC (79.97%) by 1.14%. On cross-dataset evaluation, TEn achieves 67.46% average accuracy, beating MaPLe (66.30%) and PromptSRC (65.81%). In domain generalization, CoCoOp+TEn reaches 61.66% average across ImageNet variants, exceeding PromptSRC (60.65%) by 1.01%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against seven prior VLM adaptation methods (CLIP, CoOp, CoCoOp, ProDA, MaPLe, SHIP, PromptSRC) plus BLIP/BLIP-2 in the appendix. PromptSRC+TEn achieves the best average HM (81.11%) on base-to-new generalization, beating PromptSRC (79.97%) by 1.14%, SHIP (79.94%) by 1.17%, and MaPLe (78.55%) by 2.56%. CoCoOp+TEn (79.20%) also surpasses CoCoOp (75.83%) by 3.37% and MaPLe by 0.65%. On cross-dataset evaluation, TEn (67.46% avg) outperforms all baselines, with the largest margin over CoOp (63.88%) at +3.58%. In domain generalization, CoCoOp+TEn (61.66% avg) beats PromptSRC (60.65%) by 1.01% and MaPLe (60.28%) by 1.38%, while uniquely maintaining strong source-domain accuracy (73.25% on ImageNet) unlike CoOp which degrades on targets. The standalone TEn (75.05% HM) already exceeds CoCoOp (75.83%) on new-class accuracy (77.72% vs. 71.69%), demonstrating that ensemble knowledge from weaker models compensates for the lack of prompt tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of work on VLM adaptation (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe, PromptSRC, SHIP), which all focus on extracting maximum generalization from a single CLIP model (typically ViT-B/16) through increasingly sophisticated prompt learning or constraint mechanisms. The authors argue that single-model knowledge has been 'fully explored,' and instead repurpose the well-established ensemble learning paradigm (logit fusion, confidence weighting, meta-learning) to combine multiple pre-trained CLIP variants with different architectures (ResNet-50/101, ViT-B/32/16). The connection to prior ensemble literature (bagging, boosting, stacking) is conceptual rather than technical; the specific contribution is designing three lightweight fusion strategies tailored to different resource constraints in the VLM generalization setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'weak helps strong' finding—that significantly weaker CLIP variants (e.g., RN50 at 58.71% vs. ViT-B/16 at 65.27%) consistently improve the strongest model—challenges the implicit assumption in prior VLM adaptation work that only the best single model matters. The three strategies (zero-shot, training-free, tuning) offer practical flexibility for practitioners with varying compute budgets. However, the approach is limited to CLIP-family models with shared text encoders, the SWIG is a minimal 2-layer MLP, and the ensemble inherently multiplies inference cost. Open questions include whether the strategy generalizes to heterogeneous VLMs (e.g., combining CLIP with LLaVA or DINOv2-based models), whether it scales to larger model families, and whether the gains persist as individual models become stronger.
