## Problem

Existing linear attention methods (Performer, Linear Transformer, RetNet, etc.) require extensive retraining from scratch to achieve competitive performance, which is prohibitively expensive for large language models (e.g., LLaMA-7B requires ~82,432 GPU-hours). The paper identifies that the primary source of approximation error in these methods stems from Monte Carlo sampling of kernel feature maps, and asks whether a pre-trained vanilla Transformer can be converted to a linear-complexity model with minimal fine-tuning cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240274/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained Transformer checkpoint (Pythia, OPT, TinyLLaMA, or LLaMA2) and a small fine-tuning corpus (The Pile, 825 GiB) as input, and produces a linear-complexity language model that generates text tokens autoregressively. Crucially, the DCT-based frequency domain transformation is applied along the hidden dimension d (not the sequence length n), which preserves autoregressive causality and enables O(nd log d) complexity rather than the O(n log n · d) that token-axis transforms would require. A learned gating mechanism (adapted from RetNet) is incorporated to support efficient streaming inference where memory and throughput remain constant regardless of sequence length.

## Method

DiJiang reformulates softmax attention as a Gaussian kernel approximation and applies Bochner's Theorem to express it as an integral over frequency domain, then replaces the Monte Carlo sampling used by Performer with a weighted Quasi-Monte Carlo scheme (Positive Fixed Features / Weighted Positive Fixed Features) that achieves O(1/m) approximation error versus O(1/√m) for random sampling. The frequency domain mapping is concretely implemented via Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) along the hidden dimension, yielding the Weighted Discrete Cosine Features kernel φ_WDCF(x) = D·e^(T·C·x^T), where C is the DCT coefficient matrix, T is a fixed random diagonal matrix from the inverse CDF, and D is a learnable per-dimension weight. This transforms Attention(Q,K,V) = softmax(QK^T)V into FKA(Q,K,V) = φ_WDCF(Q)·φ_WDCF(K)^T·V, reducing complexity from O(n²d) to O(nd log d). The entire conversion requires only lightweight fine-tuning of D and T per layer (plus the gating parameters), achieving comparable benchmark accuracy to the original Transformer at roughly 1/16 to 1/50 of the original training cost and up to ~10× faster inference.

## Results

On Pythia-410M, DiJiang achieves an average benchmark score of 0.4567, slightly exceeding the original Pythia-410M (0.454) while requiring only ~1/16 of the training cost (6.6 days vs 105.8 days on A800). At the 7B scale, DiJiang-7B reaches an average of 0.557 across 11 benchmarks, nearly matching LLaMA2-7B (0.565) while using only 40B training tokens versus LLaMA2's 2000B (~1/50). Inference throughput improves substantially, e.g., from 203 to 787 tokens/s on the 410M model and from 34 to 284 tokens/s on the 2.8B model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against other linear attention methods fine-tuned on Pythia-410M, DiJiang (0.457 avg) substantially outperforms all competitors: Linformer (0.398), Cosformer (0.405), Performer (0.418), RetNet (0.384), and even the unweighted PFF kernel (0.426), with the largest gaps over RetNet (+7.3%) and Linformer (+5.9%). DiJiang also slightly exceeds the original vanilla Pythia-410M (0.454), indicating that the DCT-based kernelization introduces negligible approximation loss. At the 7B scale, DiJiang-7B (0.557 avg) trails LLaMA2-7B (0.565) by only 0.8% on average while using roughly 1/50 of the training tokens (40B vs 2000B), with notable wins on Race-Middle (0.618 vs 0.402) and COPA (0.730 vs 0.670) but losses on SIQA (0.346 vs 0.485) and BoolQ (0.626 vs 0.749). Compared to the original Pythia models across scales, DiJiang matches or slightly exceeds performance at 70M–410M parameters but shows small degradations at 1B (0.440 vs 0.456) and 2.8B (0.473 vs 0.478), suggesting the approximation gap widens modestly with model size.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiJiang extends the kernel-based linear attention lineage (Katharopoulos et al. 2020; Choromanski et al. 2020/Performer) by replacing Monte Carlo random feature sampling with weighted Quasi-Monte Carlo sampling, grounded in Bochner's Theorem and spherical coordinate analysis from Lyu (2017) and Yang et al. (2014). The key practical departure is the use of Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) to implement the frequency-domain feature map, which reduces the mapping cost to O(d log d) and operates in the real domain for hardware efficiency. Unlike prior linear attention methods (Performer, RetNet, RWKV, Mamba) that require training from scratch, DiJiang is explicitly designed as a post-hoc conversion of pre-trained vanilla Transformers, borrowing a gating mechanism from RetNet (Sun et al. 2023) to enable efficient autoregressive inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated 1/50 training cost reduction for LLaMA2-7B (40B vs. 2T tokens) and ~10x inference speedup address a critical practical barrier to deploying efficient attention in existing large models, where retraining is economically and environmentally prohibitive. However, the 'comparable performance' claim warrants scrutiny: individual benchmark drops are non-trivial (e.g., SIQA 0.346 vs. 0.485, BoolQ 0.626 vs. 0.749, MMLU 0.407 vs. 0.468 for LLaMA2-7B), and the method's reliance on a borrowed gating mechanism from RetNet raises questions about its self-containedness. Open questions include whether the approximation quality degrades further at larger scales (beyond 7B), whether the m=d constraint limits expressiveness for tasks requiring fine-grained attention patterns, and whether the approach generalizes to multi-modal or non-autoregressive settings.
