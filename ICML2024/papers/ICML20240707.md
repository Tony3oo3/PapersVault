## Problem

Standard dot-product self-attention is provably not globally Lipschitz, yet it powers virtually all production transformers. Existing approaches either modify the attention mechanism to enforce Lipschitzness (yielding architectures unused in practice) or rely on local Lipschitz bounds that are too conservative to produce non-vacuous certified robustness. This paper asks: can we derive tight, interpretable local sensitivity bounds for the *unmodified* dot-product self-attention that are both tractable and sufficient for certified ℓ₂ robustness of practical vision transformers?

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained ViT (or BERT) model, an input image (32×32 CIFAR-10/SVHN) or sentence (SST), and a perturbation radius ε. The input is consumed as a sequence of n patch embeddings (n×d matrix) or token embeddings, and the bound δ(X,ε) is computed per data point via power iterations on Kronecker-product matrices. The output is a deterministic certified ℓ₂ robustness guarantee: if the prediction margin exceeds √2·δ(X,ε), the predicted class is certified invariant to all perturbations of Frobenius norm ≤ ε. Unlike randomized smoothing, the guarantee is per-sample and exact (not probabilistic).

## Method

LoFAST decomposes the attention output change into two terms: Δ₁ (linear in the perturbation, involving the fixed attention matrix P_l(X)) and Δ₂ (involving the change in the softmax attention weights). For Δ₁, the paper derives an exact closed-form solution via a Kronecker-product identity, yielding the key sensitivity metric ζ(X) = ‖H⊗I + Σ P_l(X)⊗(W_l^V W_l^O)ᵀ‖, which is the dominant term and computable by power iteration. For Δ₂, the 1-Lipschitz property of softmax is exploited to bound the attention weight change, producing two levels of tightness (δ₂⁽¹⁾ using the exact matrix M_l(X), and δ₂⁽²⁾ using a cheaper spectral-norm relaxation). The bound is propagated recursively through multi-layer networks, with LayerNorm replaced by a 1-Lipschitz LayerProject and all feed-forward/patch-embedding layers constrained to be 1-Lipschitz via SDP-based Lipschitz Layer (SLL) parameterizations, so that the per-layer expansion factor remains controlled. The resulting end-to-end bound is tight enough to yield non-vacuous certified robust accuracy on CIFAR-10 and SVHN, and is complementary to general-purpose verifiers like α,β-CROWN (achieving up to 3× speedup when used as a first-pass filter).

## Results

On CIFAR-10, LoFAST achieves certified ℓ₂ robust accuracy of 29.1% at ε=0.02, 16.0% at ε=0.05, and 3.0% at ε=0.1 for a 3-layer ViT, while α,β-CROWN achieves 41.2%, 14.1%, and fails (overflow) respectively. LoFAST runs at 0.055–0.094 sec/sample versus 6.7–20.4 sec/sample for CROWN, a >100× speedup. When combined (CROWN+LoFAST), certified accuracy at ε=0.05 improves from 14.35% to 20.03% on CIFAR-10 and from 27.45% to 29.51% on SVHN, demonstrating the two verifiers are complementary. Against the local-Lipschitz bound from SpecFormer, LoFAST's upper bound is tighter by an order of magnitude (e.g., 0.0291 vs. 16.901 at ε=0.01), making SpecFormer too conservative for non-vacuous certification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against α,β-CROWN (Zhang et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2021), LoFAST is dramatically faster (0.055–0.094 vs. 6.7–20.4 sec/sample) but gives looser certified accuracy at small perturbations (29.1% vs. 41.2% at ε=0.02 for ViT-3). However, CROWN suffers overflow failures at ε≥0.05 for 4-layer and ε≥0.05 for 5-layer ViTs, while LoFAST still produces non-vacuous bounds. When used in a cascade (LoFAST first, then CROWN on unverified samples), the combination improves certified accuracy at ε=0.05 from 14.35% to 20.03% on CIFAR-10 and from 27.45% to 29.51% on SVHN, confirming the two verifiers are truly complementary. Against SpecFormer's local-Lipschitz bound (Xixu, 2023), LoFAST is tighter by roughly three orders of magnitude (0.0291 vs. 16.901 at ε=0.01), and the SpecFormer bound is so loose that it yields vacuous certified robustness on CIFAR-10, whereas LoFAST's bound closely tracks the PGD empirical lower bound (0.0291 vs. 0.0286).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of work that modifies dot-product self-attention to enforce global Lipschitzness (Kim et al. 2021; Qi et al. 2023; Dasoulas et al. 2021) by instead deriving tight analytical bounds on the *local* sensitivity of the unmodified mechanism. It builds on the margin-based certification framework of Tsuzuku et al. (2018) for connecting sensitivity bounds to certified robustness, and leverages SDP-based Lipschitz Layer parameterizations (Araujo et al., 2023) to control weight norms during training. In contrast to general-purpose verifiers like CROWN/AutoLiRPA that struggle with scalability on transformers, and to local-Lipschitz approaches like SpecFormer (Xixu, 2023) that are shown to be an order of magnitude too conservative, LoFAST provides a fine-grained, interpretable, and computationally tractable bound specific to the attention operation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that non-Lipschitz dot-product self-attention can still yield non-vacuous certified ℓ₂-robustness for practical ViTs, resolving a tension between the known non-Lipschitzness and the empirical robustness of transformers. The interpretable key sensitivity metric ζ(X) gives concrete design guidance (control input norm via layer projection, constrain W^V and W^O norms) that practitioners can directly apply. However, the validation is limited to small ViTs (3–5 layers, 128–384 dim) on CIFAR-10 and SVHN, and the bounds degrade for large input norms; scaling to ImageNet-scale models or deeper architectures remains open. For NLP, the analysis is restricted to word-embedding-space perturbations rather than token-level attacks, a limitation shared with prior work.
