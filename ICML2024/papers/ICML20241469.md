## Problem

Existing methods for explaining unsupervised representation learning either produce low-quality explanations or require computationally expensive per-sample optimization (e.g., RELAX takes ~2 seconds per image). There is a need for a self-explaining system that generates high-quality occlusion-based explanations in parallel with representation computation, enabling real-time use in video processing and interactive applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241469/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a frozen representation model (ResNet50 or Vision Transformer trained via supervised, SimCLR, SwAV, or DINO pre-training) as input. It produces a pixel-wise occlusion mask of the same spatial dimensions as the input image with values in [0,1], generated in a single forward pass alongside the feature embedding. The mask is a set-of-features explanation (unordered subset of important pixels) rather than an additive per-pixel attribution, and is validated by checking that the masked image's representation remains close to the original in latent space.

## Method

NEM-U augments a frozen model Φ with a U-Net decoder Ψ, where Φ's intermediate feature maps serve as the U-Net encoder via skip connections, so only the decoder (~9–10M parameters) is trained. The loss combines three terms: a preservation term (negative cosine similarity between original and masked representations), an L1 sparsity penalty on the mask, and a binary-encouraging regularizer B(x) = −N_d·P_d that penalizes solutions where the masked and inverse-masked images are close in latent space, pushing the mask toward binary values. Training uses pixel scaling (multiplying pixels by mask values) with the Prodigy optimizer for 10 epochs on COCO or VOC data. At inference, a single forward pass through the frozen model and the masking network yields both the embedding and the explanation mask, making NEM-U approximately 1000× faster than RELAX while matching or exceeding its locality scores.

## Results

NEM-U achieves the best Relevancy Mass across all four models and both datasets (e.g., 0.823 on VOC and 0.697 on COCO for the supervised ResNet50), and the lowest Complexity in most settings (e.g., 9.390 on VOC for supervised). In terms of speed, NEM-U generates explanations in 0.002 seconds per image, approximately 1000× faster than RELAX (2.048 s) and 3.5× faster than the next-best method (Saliency at 0.007 s). NEM-U wins 12 out of 16 metric-dataset-model comparisons against U-RELAX, while RELAX retains the highest Relevancy Rank scores (e.g., 0.719 vs. 0.691 on VOC for supervised).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RELAX (the prior state-of-the-art for representation explainability), NEM-U wins decisively on speed (0.002 s vs. 2.048 s per image, ~1000× faster), Relevancy Mass (e.g., 0.823 vs. 0.599 on VOC for supervised), Complexity (9.390 vs. 10.626), and Sparseness (0.740 vs. 0.336), but loses on Relevancy Rank (0.691 vs. 0.719 on VOC for supervised), which the authors attribute to NEM-U producing set-of-features rather than additive per-pixel explanations. Against U-RELAX, NEM-U similarly dominates on Relevancy Mass, Complexity, Sparseness, and speed, while U-RELAX retains a slight edge on Relevancy Rank in most settings. Against the gradient-based baselines (Integrated Gradients, Gradient SHAP, Saliency), NEM-U outperforms all three on every metric across all models and datasets, with the largest gaps on Relevancy Mass (e.g., 0.823 vs. 0.569 for Integrated Gradients on VOC supervised) and Complexity. The authors note that the Relevancy Rank gap with RELAX is partly an artifact of comparing set-of-features explanations to additive feature explanations, and that no fair metric exists to directly compare the two paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the occlusion-based explanation paradigm established by RELAX (Wickström et al., 2023a) for unsupervised representation learning, but departs from its per-sample iterative optimization by learning a masking network that predicts explanations in a single forward pass. The NEM-U architecture repurposes the U-Net encoder-decoder structure from semantic segmentation, with the frozen representation model serving as the encoder and a lightweight trained decoder generating the occlusion mask. The loss function is shown to subsume the rate-distortion objective of MacDonald et al. (2019) as a special case, connecting the work to the broader preservation-game literature on meaningful perturbations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1000× speedup over RELAX (0.002s vs. 2.048s per image) while maintaining comparable locality makes large-scale explanation generation (e.g., video processing, interactive applications) practically feasible for the first time in the unsupervised setting. The paper's framing of NEM-U as a set-of-features explanation rather than an additive per-pixel attribution raises an open question about which explanation paradigm is more appropriate for representation learning, and no fair metric currently exists to compare them. Limitations include the need for a training phase (making it less suitable for one-off explanations), dataset-dependent mask density, and weaker pixel-ranking ability compared to RELAX.
