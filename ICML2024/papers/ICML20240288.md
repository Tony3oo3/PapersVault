## Problem

Existing decentralized bilevel optimization (DSBO) algorithms all rely on nested-loops of consensus iterations to estimate the hypergradient, which causes the cumulative differential-privacy budget to explode when standard noise injection is applied. Even for the simpler single-level decentralized optimization problem, prior DP solutions must sacrifice convergence accuracy for privacy. This paper is the first to simultaneously achieve rigorous local differential privacy and accurate convergence in decentralized bilevel optimization.

## Input / Output

Each of m agents holds local upper-level and lower-level loss functions with data arriving sequentially (one sample per time step) under heterogeneous distributions, and communicates only with neighbors defined by a symmetric weight matrix W. The algorithm outputs optimized upper-level parameters x (e.g., hyperparameters) and lower-level parameters y (e.g., model weights) that converge to the global optimum, while each agent's cumulative privacy budget remains finite regardless of the number of iterations. The serial data arrival and cross-agent heterogeneity are central design constraints that the single-loop consensus must handle without nested communication rounds.

## Method

The core contribution is a single-loop decentralized algorithm (Algorithm 2) that eliminates all nested consensus iterations by integrating the Hessian-inverse-vector product estimation (Algorithm 1) directly into the main update loop. At each iteration, every agent performs three coupled steps: a lower-level y-update with neighbor consensus, a z-update estimating the Hessian-inverse-vector product via a local gradient step plus consensus, and an upper-level x-update using a locally computed hypergradient estimate that avoids full Hessian/Jacobian computation. Differential privacy is achieved by injecting Laplace noise into each consensus term with variance decaying as (t+1)^(-ς); the algorithm's structure ensures the sensitivity also decays as (t+1)^(-(1+v)), so the cumulative privacy budget converges to a finite value even as T→∞. Convergence rates are established for nonconvex, convex, and strongly convex upper-level objectives, with the privacy-accuracy tradeoff quantified as O(T^(-(1-v_x))/ε_i²) in the nonconvex case.

## Results

The paper's primary quantitative contribution is in Table 1, which shows LDP-DSBO achieves a computational complexity of O(max{p,q}·δ^{-2.6}) for a δ-solution under nonconvex F, with a bounded O(1) cumulative privacy budget — the only algorithm in the comparison that supports differential privacy. Under strongly convex F, the convergence rate is O(T^{-β₁}) with β₁ up to 0.4 (yielding O(max{p,q}·δ^{-2.5})); under convex/nonconvex F, the rate is O(T^{-(1-v_x)}) with 1-v_x ≈ 0.23–0.385. Experimentally, on MNIST and CIFAR-10 under LDP constraints, Algorithm 2 achieves substantially lower training/test loss and higher test accuracy than MA-DSBO and GBDSBO, with GBDSBO and MA-DSBO failing to converge (losses diverging to infinity) on MNIST and CIFAR-10 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MA-DSBO (Chen et al., 2023), Algorithm 2 achieves lower training loss and higher test accuracy on all three experimental settings (synthetic, MNIST, CIFAR-10) under LDP constraints; on CIFAR-10 meta-learning, MA-DSBO fails entirely with training and test losses diverging to infinity. Against GBDSBO (Yang et al., 2022), Algorithm 2 outperforms on synthetic data and MNIST; on MNIST, GBDSBO also fails to train with both training and test losses tending to infinity. Against VRDSBO (Gao et al., 2023), Algorithm 2 outperforms on MNIST both with and without DP noise, because VRDSBO implicitly assumes homogeneous lower-level functions and is not applicable to general heterogeneous DSBO. In the absence of DP noise, Algorithm 2 achieves test accuracy comparable to MA-DSBO on synthetic data and higher accuracy than GBDSBO, while on MNIST it achieves lower training loss and higher test accuracy than both. The key differentiator is that LDP-DSBO is the only algorithm achieving a bounded O(1) cumulative privacy budget while maintaining accurate convergence, whereas all baselines either lack DP support or would require exploding privacy budgets if standard Laplace noise were naively added to their nested-loop consensus iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of decentralized stochastic bilevel optimization (DSBO) and local differential privacy (LDP). It builds on the Hessian-inverse-vector product estimation framework of Chen et al. (2023, MA-DSBO) and the diminishing Laplace noise mechanism from Wang & Nedić (2023) and Chen & Wang (2023) for private decentralized optimization. The key departure is that all prior DSBO algorithms (MA-DSBO, GBDSBO, DSBO-JHIP, SPDB) rely on nested-loops of consensus iterations, which causes the cumulative privacy budget to explode under standard DP noise injection. By designing a single-loop algorithm that avoids nested consensus, the authors achieve a diminishing sensitivity that keeps the cumulative privacy budget finite even as iterations go to infinity — a property no prior DSBO algorithm possesses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result to simultaneously guarantee rigorous local differential privacy and accurate convergence in decentralized bilevel optimization, a setting that encompasses meta-learning, hyperparameter optimization, and neural architecture search in privacy-sensitive domains. The single-loop design is of independent interest even without privacy, as it avoids the steady-state errors inherent in nested-loop approaches under heterogeneous data. Open questions include whether the O(T^{-(1-v_x)}/ε²) privacy-accuracy tradeoff is fundamental or can be tightened, whether the approach scales to larger networks and deeper models, and whether the LDP framework (which is more conservative than central DP) can be relaxed without sacrificing the finite-budget guarantee. The experimental validation is limited to 10-agent ring topologies and relatively small datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10), leaving scalability untested.
