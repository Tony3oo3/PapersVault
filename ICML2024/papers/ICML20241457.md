## Problem

Outlier detection methods suffer severe performance degradation under adversarial attacks, and existing robust approaches that combine Outlier Exposure (OE) with adversarial training rely on fixed, distant outlier datasets (e.g., Tiny ImageNet) that perform poorly when the inlier distribution differs significantly from the OE set. The paper identifies that OE effectiveness is highly sensitive to the distance between exposed outliers and the inlier distribution, and that effective adversarial training for outlier detection requires OE samples that are simultaneously diverse, near-distribution, and conceptually distinguishable from inliers.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images at varying resolutions (32×32 for low-res benchmarks like CIFAR-10/MNIST, higher resolution for medical/industrial datasets like MVTec-AD and Head-CT) along with the inlier class label text (e.g., 'screw'). At test time, the trained classifier outputs a scalar anomaly score (the (K+1)-th logit corresponding to the auxiliary outlier class), which is thresholded to produce a binary inlier/outlier decision. Notably, the method requires access to inlier class names as semantic descriptors and uses a smaller CLIP variant trained on noisy images (67M samples) rather than the standard noise-free CLIP, since the diffusion guidance operates on intermediate noisy representations.

## Method

RODEO generates adaptive near-distribution outliers through a three-stage pipeline. First, it extracts near-outlier labels by finding semantically close words to inlier class labels via Word2Vec, filtering with a CLIP-based threshold to ensure conceptual distinguishability, and augmenting with negative-attribute texts (e.g., 'broken screw') to cover both semantic-level and pixel-level outliers. Second, it conditions a pretrained diffusion model (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) on inlier images by starting the reverse denoising process from a random intermediate timestep t₀ ~ U(0.3T, 0.6T) rather than pure Gaussian noise, while guiding each denoising step with CLIP gradients that push the generated image toward the extracted outlier labels in the shared text-image embedding space; a CLIP-score threshold then filters out any generated samples that remain in-distribution. Third, it adversarially trains a standard classifier (ResNet-18 or Wide ResNet) with cross-entropy loss using PGD-10 on the combined inlier and generated OE dataset. The key differentiator from prior OE methods is operating in pixel space (not embedding space as in Dream-OOD) and leveraging both text and image conditioning to produce outliers that are simultaneously diverse and near-distribution, supported by theoretical analysis showing that near-distribution OE reduces adversarial error and diverse OE minimizes worst-case detection risk.

## Results

In the Novelty Detection (ND) setting across 11 datasets, RODEO achieves a mean AUROC of 83.7% (clean) and 63.5% (PGD-1000), with 63.8%/62.6% under AutoAttack/Adaptive AutoAttack. This represents a dramatic improvement over the next-best robust method (PrincipaLS at 31.7% PGD) and a ~50% relative gain over EXOE (0.3% PGD). In the OSR setting, RODEO reaches 97.2%/85.0% (clean/PGD) on MNIST and 87.7%/65.3% on Fashion-MNIST, while in OOD detection it achieves 93.2%/69.5% (CIFAR10 in-distribution) and 88.1%/64.7% (CIFAR100 in-distribution) under PGD-1000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RODEO's primary advantage is adversarial robustness: under PGD-1000 in the ND setting, it achieves 63.5% mean AUROC versus PrincipaLS (31.7%), OCSDF (23.7%), APAE (9.5%), and all non-robust methods (≤3.7%), representing a 2–50× improvement. In clean settings, RODEO (83.7%) is competitive but trails EXOE (86.5%), Transformaly (90.1%), and MSAD (88.6%), reflecting the well-documented clean-vs-robust tradeoff. In OSR, RODEO outperforms ATD (68.7/56.5 on MNIST) and ALOE (79.5/37.3) on both clean and PGD, while PLP (88.3/0.4) and ViT-MSP (92.4/2.9) maintain higher clean scores but collapse under attack. In OOD detection, RODEO (93.2/69.5 on CIFAR10) matches ATD (94.3/69.1) under PGD while substantially outperforming ALOE (97.8/5.0) and PLP (98.4/0.1) in adversarial settings. The ablation (Table 2) further confirms that RODEO's adaptive OE pipeline outperforms Dream-OOD (62.4/22.7), GOE (64.2/29.4), FITYMI (56.3/20.6), and baseline OE with ImageNet (76.6/36.5) in both detection AUROC and the FDC diversity/closeness metric (3.325 vs. ≤1.971).

## Relation to Prior Work

RODEO extends the Outlier Exposure (OE) paradigm introduced by Hendrycks et al. (2018) and refined by robust detection methods like ALOE, ATD, and ATOM, which all rely on a fixed auxiliary outlier dataset (e.g., Tiny ImageNet). The key departure is making OE adaptive: rather than using a static, potentially distant dataset, RODEO generates near-distribution outliers by conditioning a pixel-space diffusion model on inlier images and guiding the denoising process with CLIP text-image alignment toward semantically related but distinct labels. This contrasts with Dream-OOD, which operates in embedding space and inherits biases from its large-scale pretraining, and with FITYMI, which leverages only the image domain. The theoretical analysis (Theorems 3.1 and 3.3) provides a formal justification for why both near-distribution and diversity of OE samples are necessary for adversarial robustness, a connection not previously established in the OE literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Robust outlier detection under adversarial attacks remains a critical gap in open-world ML safety, with prior methods often performing near-random under strong attacks. RODEO's data-centric approach—requiring only inlier class labels and a pretrained diffusion model—offers a broadly applicable recipe that could be plugged into existing detection pipelines without architectural changes. However, the clean-vs-robust trade-off persists (clean AUROC of ~84% vs. ~87% for EXOE), and the method's dependence on text labels and a diffusion backbone limits its applicability to label-free or non-image settings. Open questions include whether the pixel-space generation advantage over embedding-space methods (Dream-OOD) holds at scale, and whether the approach extends to video, audio, or tabular outlier detection.
