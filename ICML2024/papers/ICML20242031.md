## Problem

Convex message passing algorithms (e.g., max-sum diffusion, TRW-S) minimize LP/Lagrangian upper bounds on MAP inference via coordinate descent, but their convergence properties were incompletely understood. While prior work showed iterates converge to a set defined by local consistency of active constraints, it remained an open conjecture whether the iterates converge to a single fixed point at all, and no convergence rate was known.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph structure (V, E) with unary and pairwise weights θ, an initial message vector δ, and a precision threshold ε. It iteratively updates individual messages (or pairs of subproblem weights in the Lagrangian decomposition variant) until the maximum coordinate change η falls below ε. The output is a converged message/weight vector satisfying the fixed-point equations (e.g., arc consistency for max-sum diffusion), which yields a tight upper bound on the MAP objective value.

## Method

The paper proves convergence by first analyzing a general coordinate descent method for minimizing the pointwise maximum of affine functions, where non-unique coordinate minimizers are resolved by ignoring affine functions independent of the current variable. The key technical tool is a novel energy function E_k(y) = Σ k^i · sort(y)_i (with k = 1 + C/c) that strictly decreases by at least c|x_j − x_j*| in every non-trivial update, ruling out cycles. Under boundedness of the energy (verified for MAP inference via a cancellation argument), this yields convergence to a fixed point in O(1/ε) iterations. Max-sum diffusion and pairwise max-marginal averaging are then shown to be special cases of this general method, transferring the convergence guarantee. As a negative result, the paper also shows that a mid-point rule variant of coordinate descent can cycle on both constrained and unconstrained problems.

## Results

The paper proves that coordinate descent on the pointwise maximum of affine functions (with the 'ignore constant functions' rule) converges to a fixed point in O(1/ε) iterations for any precision ε > 0 (Theorem 2.9). This is achieved via a novel energy function E_{1+C/c}(Ax+b) that strictly decreases by at least c|x_j − x_j*| in every non-trivial update (Proposition 2.7). The result is transferred to max-sum diffusion (Theorem 3.3) and max-marginal averaging (Theorem 3.5), proving the long-standing conjecture that these algorithms converge to a fixed point. Additionally, the paper shows that a mid-point rule variant of coordinate descent can cycle (Proposition 4.1), providing a negative result for that alternative selection strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The key comparison is against prior theoretical results: previous work (Schlesinger & Antoniuk 2011 for max-sum diffusion; Kolmogorov 2006 for TRW-S) showed only that limit points satisfy local consistency (arc consistency or weak tree consistency), i.e., convergence to a *set* of points, without establishing convergence to a single fixed point or any rate. This paper strengthens those results by proving convergence to a single fixed point and providing the first O(1/ε) iteration complexity bound. The authors note that the O(1/ε) bound involves a constant that depends exponentially on problem size, and they are uncertain whether this is an artifact of the analysis or inherent to the method. The mid-point rule cycling result (Proposition 4.1) contrasts with the relative interior rule (Werner et al. 2020), which was shown to be 'not worse' than other rules but for which cycling was not previously demonstrated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper resolves a convergence conjecture for convex message passing algorithms that dates back to the original max-sum diffusion formulation by Kovalevsky & Koval (1975) and was explicitly stated by Schlesinger & Antoniuk (2011) and Werner (2007). Prior work established only weaker guarantees: Kolmogorov (2006) showed limit points of TRW-S satisfy weak tree consistency, and Schlesinger & Antoniuk (2011) proved convergence to the arc-consistency set (but not to a single point). The paper's key technical novelty is a weighted-sum energy function over sorted affine-function values that strictly decreases per iteration, enabling both the fixed-point convergence proof and the first O(1/ε) rate bound. It also connects to the broader Lagrangian decomposition framework of Guignard & Kim (1987) and Johnson et al. (2007), showing max-marginal averaging is a special case of the general piecewise-affine coordinate descent scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the first rigorous convergence guarantee (to a single fixed point, not just a set) and the first convergence rate bound for a family of widely-used dual coordinate descent methods in MAP inference and combinatorial optimization. However, the O(1/ε) bound carries a constant that grows exponentially with problem size, and the authors note they have never observed such exponential dependence empirically, leaving open whether the bound is tight or an artifact of the analysis. The cycling result for the mid-point rule variant is a useful negative finding that delineates the boundary of when coordinate descent on non-smooth convex problems is well-behaved. The authors also note the proof likely extends to other dual BCD methods (e.g., TRW-S) but not to SRMP, which by design only converges to a locally consistent set.
