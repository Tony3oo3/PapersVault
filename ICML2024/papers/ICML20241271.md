## Problem

Resource-constrained scheduling is NP-hard, and existing exact solvers (CPLEX, Gurobi, CP-SAT) suffer from poor scalability on large, dense graphs while heuristics yield suboptimal solutions. Moreover, these solvers are designed for single-threaded CPU execution and cannot exploit GPU parallelism. The paper addresses the gap of making SDC-based scheduling fully differentiable so that gradient descent on parallel hardware can optimize the schedule without training data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataflow DAG G(V,E) with per-node resource costs, weighted edges encoding dependency constraints in SDC form (s_i − s_j ≤ c_ij), and a latency bound L that caps the number of scheduling stages. Each node's schedule variable is vectorized as a probability vector in R^L, and the output is a one-hot assignment of every node to a stage in [0, L−1]. The optimization is model-free and dataless: no labeled examples or reward signals are required, and the objective (peak memory + inter-stage communication) is directly differentiable through the sampling process.

## Method

The core contribution is a fully differentiable SDC scheduling framework built on Gumbel-Softmax sampling. Each node's discrete stage assignment is represented as a one-hot vector in R^L, and a novel 'constrained Gumbel Trick' encodes inequality constraints via a cumsum-based transformation T_≤ that restricts the feasible sampling space for each node based on its predecessors' sampled values, guaranteeing constraint satisfaction at every draw. The loss combines an entropy-based memory term (encouraging even distribution of nodes across stages) and a normalized inter-stage communication cost, both computed on the hard one-hot schedule. Optimization proceeds by gradient descent on the underlying probability vectors through the Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization, with the temperature τ annealed to sharpen discrete decisions. The entire pipeline is implemented in PyTorch, enabling GPU-parallel evaluation of the loss and backpropagation across all nodes simultaneously.

## Results

The method achieves convergence to near-optimal scheduling solutions within the first 360 seconds across all tested workloads (12 random workloads with 810–8193 nodes and 6 EPFL designs with 1221–20058 nodes), while CPLEX and Gurobi barely initiate optimization on the largest designs (Square: 18,742 nodes; Voter: 15,761 nodes) within the full 3600-second timeout. CPLEX and Gurobi exhibit approximately a 3× performance degradation when workload scale increases from ~5,000 to ~10,000 nodes, and roughly a 2× degradation on technology-mapped (dense) EPFL designs versus their original sparse counterparts. Loss function convergence is observed within the first 30–75 epochs, with a brief warm-up instability in the first ~10 epochs attributable to the Gumbel-Softmax mechanism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CPLEX and Gurobi (both solving the same SDC+LP formulation), the proposed differentiable method wins decisively on dense and large-scale workloads: it converges within 360 s while CPLEX/Gurobi show ~3× slower progress at 10k-node scale and fail to produce meaningful optimization within 3600 s on the largest EPFL designs (Square, Voter). On small, sparse EPFL designs (Adder, i2c controller), CPLEX and Gurobi remain competitive, and the paper acknowledges that for trivially solvable instances the method offers little advantage. Against CP-SAT, the comparison is more nuanced: CP-SAT matches or slightly outperforms the proposed method on the smallest and sparsest workloads (e.g., RW1–3, Adder, i2c controller), but its performance degrades sharply and it fails to produce any optimization on larger, denser workloads (RW7–12, Square, Voter). Overall, the proposed method's advantage grows with graph density and scale, consistent with its GPU-parallelizable gradient-descent formulation versus the sequential search strategies of the three solvers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-established threads: the Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization trick for differentiable discrete sampling (Jang et al., 2016) and the System of Difference Constraints (SDC) formulation for encoding scheduling dependencies as linear inequalities (Cong & Zhang, 2006). The key conceptual departure is the 'constrained Gumbel Trick,' which composes Gumbel-Softmax sampling with SDC inequality constraints via a cumulative-sum transformation, enabling differentiable optimization over discrete schedule variables without any training data. This contrasts with prior ML-based schedulers (imitation learning, RL) that require labeled data or reward signals, and with exact solvers (CPLEX, Gurobi, CP-SAT) that solve the same SDC+LP formulation but are limited to single-threaded CPU execution and struggle with scalability on dense, large graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a training-free, GPU-parallel differentiable approach can match or exceed heavily optimized commercial solvers on large, dense scheduling instances, opening a path to leveraging modern accelerators for combinatorial optimization problems that were previously confined to CPU-bound exact or heuristic methods. However, the authors acknowledge that the method underperforms on small or sparse graphs, exhibits overfitting in loss convergence beyond certain epochs, and offers no advantage on problems trivially solvable by LP/CP solvers. Open questions include whether the constrained Gumbel Trick generalizes beyond SDC-form (i.e., totally unimodular) constraints to arbitrary disjunctive or non-linear constraints, and whether adaptive regularization or early-stopping strategies can mitigate the observed overfitting.
