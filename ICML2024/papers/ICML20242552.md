## Problem

Sparse adversarial perturbations bounded by the l0 norm are common in physical-world attacks (broken pixels, adversarial stickers), but the non-convexity of the l0 budget makes standard gradient-based attacks like PGD ineffective and existing sparse attacks either unable to strictly enforce the l0 constraint or too computationally expensive for adversarial training. This paper addresses both the attack side (efficient l0-bounded perturbation generation) and the defense side (adversarial training against such perturbations).

## Input / Output

The attack takes a clean RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-100/GTSRB), model parameters θ, and a sparsity budget k, and produces a sparse perturbation δ = p ⊙ m where m is a binary pixel-level mask with at most k non-zero entries (a pixel counts as perturbed if any of its channels is modified) and p is the per-channel magnitude tensor constrained to keep x+δ in [0,1]. For adversarial training, the 20-iteration sPGD attack is called per mini-batch to generate training perturbations, and the output is a robust CNN (PreActResNet-18 or ResNet-34) that maintains high accuracy under l0-bounded attacks.

## Method

sPGD decomposes the perturbation into a magnitude tensor p and a binary sparsity mask m, updating p via PGD-style sign-gradient ascent (analogous to the l∞ case) and m via a continuous surrogate m̃ updated by normalized gradient ascent followed by top-k projection after a sigmoid. Two key mechanisms distinguish sPGD from prior decomposition-based attacks: (1) an unprojected gradient that replaces the binary mask m with σ(m̃) when backpropagating to p, avoiding the sparse coordinate-descent bottleneck and enabling dense exploration, and (2) a random reinitialization of m̃ when the projected mask remains unchanged for t consecutive iterations, preventing local optima. The paper further constructs sparse-AutoAttack (sAA) as a cascade ensemble of sPGD (both projected and unprojected variants) and the black-box Sparse-RS attack for comprehensive evaluation, and incorporates sPGD into adversarial training (sAT, sTRADES) with a random backward-function selection per batch, a multi-k strategy (training with 6× the test sparsity), and a higher reinitialization tolerance to favor exploitation over exploration under the limited 20-iteration budget.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with k=20, the proposed sTRADES model achieves 89.8% clean accuracy and 61.7% robust accuracy under the full sAA evaluation, dramatically outperforming PGD0-T (90.0% clean, 4.5% sAA) and PGD0-A (77.5% clean, 2.4% sAA). Among white-box attacks, sPGD consistently achieves the lowest robust accuracy (i.e., strongest attack) across all model types, e.g., 18.8% vs. 39.8% (PGD0) and 55.6% (SAIF) on the Fast-EG-l1 model. On ImageNet-100 (k=200), sAT achieves 86.2% clean accuracy and 61.2% sAA robust accuracy, compared to PGD0-A's 76.0% clean and 1.8% sAA. sPGD also demonstrates superior efficiency, completing 10000 iterations in 42–45 minutes versus 59 min (RS) and 604 min (CornerSearch) on 1000 test instances.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PGD0 (Croce & Hein, 2019), sPGD achieves substantially lower robust accuracy across all models, e.g., 18.8% vs. 39.8% on Fast-EG-l1 and 81.7% vs. 61.1% on sTRADES (lower = stronger attack), while PGD0-based adversarial training (PGD0-A/T) yields only 2.4%/4.5% sAA robustness versus 36.2%/61.7% for sAT/sTRADES. Against Sparse-RS (Croce et al., 2022), sPGD outperforms RS on l1- and l2-trained models (e.g., 18.8% vs. 25.0% on Fast-EG-l1) but loses to RS on l∞- and l0-trained models due to gradient masking (e.g., 81.7% vs. 61.8% on sTRADES); the sAA ensemble combines both to achieve the best overall performance. Against SparseFool (Modas et al., 2018) and SAIF (Imtiaz et al., 2022), sPGD dominates by large margins in every setting (e.g., 18.8% vs. 65.4% SF and 55.6% SAIF on Fast-EG-l1). Against l1-APGD (Croce & Hein, 2021) and Fast-EG-l1 (Jiang et al., 2023) trained models, sTRADES achieves 61.7% sAA robustness versus 16.9% and 16.8% respectively, confirming that l0-specific adversarial training with sPGD yields significantly stronger sparse-attack robustness than the closest convex-relaxation baselines. CornerSearch (Croce & Hein, 2019) achieves competitive robust accuracy but requires ~14× more runtime (604 min vs. 42 min for sPGDproj), making it impractical for large-scale evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the PGD iterative attack framework (Madry et al., 2017) to the non-convex l0-bounded setting, where vanilla PGD0 (Croce & Hein, 2019b) is known to trap in local maxima. It builds directly on SAIF's (Imtiaz et al., 2022) decomposition of the perturbation into a magnitude tensor and binary sparsity mask, but replaces SAIF's Frank-Wolfe updates with gradient ascent on a continuous mask relaxation plus an unprojected-gradient trick borrowed from the lottery-ticket/pruning literature. The evaluation framework (sAA) mirrors AutoAttack's (Croce & Hein, 2020) ensemble-of-diverse-attacks philosophy, and the adversarial training pipeline follows the TRADES (Zhang et al., 2019b) formulation, with the key departure being the use of a 20-iteration sPGD attack that is efficient enough for per-batch generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first l0 attack efficient enough to serve as the inner-loop adversary in adversarial training, closing a practical gap that prior l0 methods (PGD0, SAIF) could not fill due to their high per-iteration cost or weak performance under limited iterations. The resulting sTRADES model achieves substantially higher robust accuracy than all prior l0-trained baselines across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet-100, and GTSRB, and the sAA benchmark gives the community a standardized, parameter-free evaluation protocol for sparse robustness. The authors acknowledge that 20-iteration sPGD remains computationally heavy, making scaling to very large datasets an open problem, and note that gradient masking (where the black-box RS attack outperforms sPGD on l∞/l0-trained models with unlimited queries) is an inherent consequence of the non-convex budget that their method mitigates but does not eliminate.
