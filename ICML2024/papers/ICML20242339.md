## Problem

While pre-trained Vision Transformers have surpassed CNNs on vision tasks, no prior work has used self-attention as the exclusive building block to simultaneously model the three levels of relationships critical for few-shot learning: patch-patch (building block), sample-sample (intra-task), and task-task (inter-task). CNN-based meta-learning suffers from computationally expensive building-block correspondence and relies on image-level task embeddings that introduce background noise, making it difficult to separate dissimilar tasks in high-dimensional spaces.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N-way K-shot episodic tasks where each episode provides K labeled support images and M unlabeled query images per task, all at 224×224 resolution (360×360 for SMKD-MetaFormer). Images are split into 16×16 patches (ViT-Small) or 7×7 windows (Swin-Tiny) and processed in a single feedforward pass. Output is a class label per query sample computed via patch-wise cosine similarity against per-class support prototypes, with a temperature-scaled softmax. The autoregressive variant (MetaFormer-A) additionally feeds previously predicted queries back as weighted auxiliary support, and inference-time augmentation shuffles sample order s=30 times to select the most confident logits.

## Method

MetaFormer extends a pre-trained ViT with two lightweight attention modules inserted every other layer starting from layer 6. Masked Sample Attention (MSA) decouples patch and sample attention: after standard patch-level MHSA, tokens are reshaped to compute sample attention at each patch location, reducing complexity from O((NK+M)²L²) to O(L(NK+M)²+(NK+M)L²). An adaptive causal mask restricts support samples to attend only to themselves (enforcing intra/inter-class discriminative consistency) while query samples attend to support samples; label embeddings are infused via elementwise addition. Patch-grained Task Attention (PTA) learns T task-specific probe vectors that aggregate discriminative patch patterns via attention, maintains a dynamic knowledge pool (Z=50 components) consolidated across meta-training episodes, retrieves relevant historical knowledge via cosine-similarity-weighted summation, and fuses it back into support patch tokens through a second attention operation. Training is two-stage: IBOT self-supervised pre-training followed by episodic meta-tuning with SGD, and the framework supports both inductive and transductive protocols by switching the sample causal mask.

## Results

On miniImageNet (5-way), MetaFormer-I achieves 75.78% (1-shot) and 90.02% (5-shot) accuracy with a ViT-Small backbone, outperforming the previous best ViT-based inductive method FewTURE by 7.76% and 5.51% respectively. In the transductive setting, MetaFormer-A reaches 84.78% (1-shot) and 91.39% (5-shot) on miniImageNet, surpassing protoLP by 0.46% and 1.37%. On tieredImageNet, MetaFormer-I attains 79.05% (1-shot) and 90.40% (5-shot), while the combined SMKD+MetaFormer-I pipeline pushes tieredImageNet 5-shot to 92.42%. In cross-domain evaluation, MetaFormer-I improves over FewTURE by up to 8.77% and PMF by up to 16.86% across 8 target domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaFormer-I decisively outperforms all prior ViT-based inductive methods: it exceeds FewTURE (68.02%) by 7.76 points on miniImageNet 1-shot and PMF (71.01%) by 4.77 points, while using fewer parameters than the 3×ViT-Small HCT Transformers (63M vs. 24.5M). Against CNN-based baselines, the gap is even larger—MetaFormer-I surpasses COSOC (69.28%) by 6.50 points and MetaQDA (67.83%) by 7.95 points on miniImageNet 1-shot. In the transductive setting, MetaFormer-A (84.78% mini 1-shot) edges out protoLP (84.32%) and EASY (84.04%), establishing itself as the first pure-transformer transductive few-shot classifier. The combined SMKD+MetaFormer-I pipeline achieves the best inductive results overall (76.54% mini 1-shot, 92.42% tiered 5-shot), demonstrating that MetaFormer is complementary to self-distillation training. In cross-domain transfer, MetaFormer-I leads FewTURE by up to 8.77% (CUB: 56.98% vs. 48.21%) and PMF by up to 16.86% (CUB: 56.98% vs. 40.12%), while MetaFormer-A dominates on EuroSAT (79.44% vs. protoLP's 77.89%) and CropDisease (87.36% vs. 87.13%). On foundation models, Tip-Adapter-F+MetaFormer-I achieves 88.83% on EuroSAT and 45.96% on ISIC, surpassing Tip-Adapter-F alone by 16.82% and 13.69% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ViT-based few-shot learning line initiated by FewTURE (Hiller et al., 2022) and PMF (Hu et al., 2022), which demonstrated that meta-learning can augment pre-trained transformers, but which confined sample-level attention to the top classifier. MetaFormer departs by embedding sample and task relationships throughout the network at multiple scales using exclusively attention mechanisms, drawing the decoupled patch-sample attention design from video transformers (Bertasius et al., 2021) and the autoregressive query interaction from transformer neural processes (Nguyen & Grover, 2022). It also positions itself as orthogonal to self-distillation training methods like SMKD (Lin et al., 2023), showing that the two can be combined for further gains. Conceptually, it replaces the CNN-era reliance on task embeddings and FiLM conditioning for inter-task relationships with explicit patch-grained attention over a dynamic knowledge pool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MetaFormer provides a practical, drop-in module that can be attached to off-the-shelf pre-trained ViTs (including CLIP) to substantially improve few-shot classification without inference-time fine-tuning, making it attractive for resource-constrained deployment. The unified adaptive mask that switches between inductive and transductive protocols is an elegant design choice that simplifies the training/inference pipeline. However, the method is limited to image classification, and the knowledge pool's consolidation strategy (cosine-similarity threshold of 0.5, fixed pool size of 50) remains somewhat heuristic, raising open questions about scalability to very large task distributions or continual learning settings. Whether the decoupled attention approximation degrades gracefully at higher resolutions or for dense prediction tasks (segmentation, detection) remains unexplored.
