## Problem

On-policy RL algorithms like PPO and TRPO optimize the policy using only the current state-action pair's advantage, ignoring the direct influence of subsequent state-action pairs in the trajectory. This limits the agent's ability to 'reflect' on its actions based on what follows, leading to sample inefficiency. The paper asks whether explicitly incorporating the relationship between consecutive state-action pairs into the policy objective can accelerate convergence while preserving monotonic improvement guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes on-policy trajectories of (state, action, reward) tuples and specifically extracts consecutive pairs (s, a, s', a') from each trajectory. The key input detail is that it requires the advantage A^{π̂}(s', a') of the *subsequent* pair alongside the importance ratios r(a|s) and r(a'|s'), forming a product of two ratios weighted by the next-step advantage. The output is an updated policy network θ, with the total objective combining the standard PPO clipped term (k=1) and a weighted reflective term (k=2) scaled by a hyperparameter β.

## Method

RPO derives a generalized surrogate objective by recursively expanding TRPO's policy improvement identity (Lemma 3.1), yielding a finite sum over k subsequent state-action pairs (Theorem 3.2). The critical modification is replacing the product of (r_t − 1) terms with a product of r_t terms (Eqn. 3), which eliminates a sign-confusion problem where a negative advantage combined with a negative ratio difference would incorrectly increase the probability of a bad action. The practical algorithm (Algorithm 1) combines the standard PPO clipped objective L̂₀^clip with a reflective term L̂₁^clip = E[min[r·r'·A(s',a'), clip(r)·clip(r')·A(s',a')]], where each ratio is clipped *separately* rather than jointly to maintain stability. Theoretically, RPO guarantees monotonic policy improvement (Theorem 4.1) and contracts the solution space (Ψ₂ ⊆ Ψ₁, Theorem 4.2), meaning the reflective constraint restricts the set of admissible policy updates, accelerating convergence.

## Results

On MuJoCo (6 continuous control tasks, 10 seeds), RPO achieves the best mean return in all environments: HalfCheetah 3495, Hopper 3262, Reacher −4.9, Walker2d 4025, Swimmer 157, and Humanoid 5793. On Atari 2600 (54 games, 3 seeds, 50M timesteps), RPO outperforms PPO in 26 of 54 games (vs. PPO's 11), with an average normalized improvement of at least 70% over PPO. In the CliffWalking toy environment, RPO significantly reduces cliff-fall frequency and shortens steps-to-goal compared to PPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RPO outperforms all six baselines across every MuJoCo environment. Against PPO (the primary baseline), RPO gains +1087 on HalfCheetah (3495 vs 2408), +467 on Hopper (3262 vs 2795), +382 on Walker2d (4025 vs 3643), +23 on Swimmer (157 vs 134), and +185 on Humanoid (5793 vs 5608). Against TRPO, RPO leads by +633 on HalfCheetah, +1266 on Hopper, +827 on Walker2d, and +368 on Humanoid. The closest competitor is GePPO, which RPO surpasses on HalfCheetah (+342), Hopper (+92), Walker2d (+335), and Humanoid (+1693), while tying on Swimmer (157) and slightly ahead on Reacher (−4.9 vs −8.1). TayPO performs poorly (negative returns on most tasks) due to the '1' term issue identified in the paper's analysis. On Atari, RPO wins 26/54 games versus PPO's 11/54, with notable margins on games like Gopher (9489 vs 2165), Phoenix (43073 vs 27074), and UpNDown (494588 vs 344972), though PPO retains an edge on a few games such as Hero and Seaquest.

## Relation to Prior Work

RPO extends the TRPO/PPO lineage by generalizing the policy improvement identity to explicitly incorporate subsequent state-action pairs into the surrogate objective, rather than relying solely on the value function to implicitly encode future information. It departs from TayPO (Tang et al., 2020) by identifying and removing a problematic '1' term in the product of importance ratios that can mislead policy updates, and it distinguishes itself from multi-step RL methods by directly optimizing the policy rather than adjusting value function targets. The practical algorithm is a minimal modification of PPO: a single additional clipped term weighted by β is added to the standard PPO objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The solution-space contraction theorem (Ψ₂ ⊆ Ψ₁) provides a clean theoretical explanation for why incorporating subsequent trajectory information accelerates convergence, complementing the standard monotonic improvement guarantee. However, the empirical gains over PPO are moderate and the method has only been validated on MuJoCo and Atari with limited hyperparameter tuning; the authors themselves note that k=2 is used primarily for simplicity rather than optimality. Open questions include whether the reflective mechanism transfers to off-policy settings, maximum-entropy frameworks, or domains with longer relevant horizons where k=2 may be insufficient.
