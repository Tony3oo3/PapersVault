## Problem

Existing evaluations of image watermark robustness suffer from inconsistent metrics, limited attack taxonomies, small datasets, and no joint assessment of detection performance alongside image quality degradation, making it impossible to fairly compare watermarking algorithms or rank attacks. This lack of standardization has obscured the true vulnerabilities of modern watermarks in realistic deployment scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240059/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes watermarked images produced by three algorithms (Stable Signature, Tree-Ring, StegaStamp) and applies 26 attacks at five strength levels each, using 5,000 reference images from DiffusionDB, MS-COCO, and DALL-E3 as the non-watermarked comparison pool. Detection performance is reported as TPR@0.1%FPR (a stricter threshold than the 1% FPR used in prior work), while image quality is captured by 8 metrics (PSNR, SSIM, NMI, FID, CLIP-FID, LPIPS, aesthetics, artifacts) that are normalized to a common [0.1, 0.9] scale via 10th/90th quantiles and averaged into a single Normalized Quality Degradation score. The final output is a set of unified Performance-vs-Quality 2D plots per watermark, enabling simultaneous ranking of watermarks by robustness and attacks by potency.

## Method

WAVES introduces a standardized stress-testing protocol with three key innovations. First, it defines a comprehensive attack taxonomy of 26 attacks spanning distortions (8 single + 4 combined), regeneration (single diffusion/VAE plus novel 'rinsing' multi-cycle regeneration), and adversarial attacks (grey-box and black-box embedding attacks via PGD on off-the-shelf encoders like KL-VAE, CLIP, ResNet18, and SDXL-VAE, plus surrogate detector attacks trained on watermarked images). Second, it replaces p-value and AUROC metrics with TPR@0.1%FPR for detection and multi-class accuracy for user identification, ensuring fairness across watermark methods with different message spaces and statistical tests. Third, it normalizes 8 heterogeneous quality metrics using quantile-based scaling over all attacked images, aggregates them into a unified degradation score, and plots detection performance against this score across attack strengths, producing the Performance-vs-Quality 2D plots that serve as the basis for both watermark robustness radar charts and attack leaderboards.

## Results

WAVES evaluates three watermarking methods (Stable Signature, Tree-Ring, StegaStamp) under 26 attacks across three datasets (DiffusionDB, MS-COCO, DALL-E3), measuring detection performance via TPR@0.1%FPR and image quality via 8 aggregated metrics. StegaStamp demonstrates the highest overall robustness, maintaining TPR@0.1%FPR > 0.94 under most attacks. Tree-Ring is critically vulnerable to adversarial attacks: AdvCls-UnWM&WM and AdvCls-WM1&WM2 achieve Q@0.95P = -inf (i.e., they destroy the watermark with near-zero quality degradation), and AdvEmbG-KLVAE8 drops TPR to near zero. Stable Signature is completely destroyed by single regeneration attacks (Regen-Diff, Regen-DiffP), with Avg P dropping to 0.001.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StegaStamp (Tancik et al., 2020) emerges as the most robust watermark, occupying the largest area in the robustness radar plot and maintaining TPR@0.1%FPR > 0.94 under all adversarial attacks; however, it is still affected by regeneration attacks (Regen-Diff drops Avg P to 0.943) and geometric distortions (Dist-Rotation drops Avg P to 0.357). Stable Signature (Fernandez et al., 2023) is the least robust overall, being completely destroyed by single regeneration attacks (Regen-Diff and Regen-DiffP reduce Avg P to 0.001) and rinsing regenerations, though it is remarkably resistant to all adversarial attacks (Avg P ≥ 0.997 under all AdvEmb and AdvCls attacks). Tree-Ring (Wen et al., 2023) sits in between: it is highly vulnerable to the novel adversarial attacks introduced in WAVES (AdvCls-UnWM&WM, AdvCls-WM1&WM2, and AdvEmbG-KLVAE8 all achieve Q@0.95P = -inf, meaning they remove the watermark with negligible quality loss), but maintains reasonable robustness against distortions and single regenerations. DWT-DCT (Al-Haj, 2007) and MBRS (Jia et al., 2021) are evaluated in Appendix G.5 as additional watermarks and are found to be significantly less robust than the three primary methods, consistent with their known vulnerabilities to purification and regeneration attacks documented in prior work.

## Relation to Prior Work

WAVES builds on the line of watermark robustness evaluation established by individual watermark papers (StegaStamp, Stable Signature, Tree-Ring) and attack studies (Zhao et al. 2023a on regeneration, Saberi et al. 2023 on surrogate detectors, Lukas et al. 2023 on adaptive attacks), but departs from them by unifying evaluation under a single standardized protocol with stricter metrics (TPR@0.1%FPR vs. prior TPR@1%FPR or AUROC), joint performance-vs-quality 2D plots, and a taxonomy of 26 attacks spanning distortions, regenerations, and adversarial methods. It introduces novel attack categories absent from prior work—adversarial embedding attacks targeting off-the-shelf encoders (grey-box and black-box) and multi-setting surrogate detector attacks—while also extending regeneration attacks with 'rinsing' (multi-cycle diffusion) and prompted variants. The benchmark explicitly addresses the inconsistency problem identified across prior evaluations, where different papers used different metrics, datasets, and attack strengths, making cross-method comparison impossible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WAVES reveals that widely deployed watermarking systems have critical, previously undetected vulnerabilities: Tree-Ring's reliance on a public KL-VAE encoder makes it nearly fully removable via grey-box adversarial embedding with minimal visual distortion, and Stable Signature's decoder-rooted design is bypassed entirely by regeneration through an alternate VAE. The practical implication that DALL-E3's use of an open-source KL-VAE creates a security gap is a concrete, actionable finding for production AI services. Open questions include whether hybrid watermarking (combining methods with complementary vulnerabilities) can achieve broad robustness, whether more accurate DDIM inversion or trainable restoration networks can fix Tree-Ring's fundamental weakness, and whether the findings generalize beyond the three tested watermarks to newer or proprietary schemes.
