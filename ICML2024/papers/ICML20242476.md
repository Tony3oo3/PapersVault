## Problem

Autoregressive generation models like BART incur heavy inference costs that impede real-time deployment, yet existing efficiency methods either produce a single fixed-size compressed model or rely on confidence-based early exiting limited to classification tasks. No prior work provides a principled, input-dependent mechanism to dynamically allocate computation across the full encoder-decoder stack of a generation model while provably preserving output quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242476/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard text inputs (source documents for summarization, concatenated question+context for QA, sentence pairs for classification) and produces both the generated output (summary, answer span, or class label) and a per-instance skipping plan specifying which attention layers, feed-forward layers, and input tokens to bypass. The skipping decisions are derived from the first encoder layer's hidden representation via a lightweight one-layer MLP policy network, so no additional input features are required beyond what the backbone already computes.

## Method

The switchable decision (SD) network defines a discrete decision space over three candidate types—attention layers, feed-forward layers, and input tokens (with six token-skipping strategies)—and parameterizes per-layer Bernoulli and categorical distributions via a one-layer MLP policy network. A reinforcement learning agent trained with policy gradient and a self-critical baseline learns input-specific skipping strategies that maximize a reward combining negative loss (quality) and estimated FLOPs (computation). The key optimization insight is a lexicographic constrained update rule: quality (loss) is the primary objective, and computation is minimized only to the extent it does not impede quality descent, with the Lagrange multiplier λ computed analytically from the gradient inner product rather than manually tuned. During inference, decisions are made greedily (argmax over the policy distributions) rather than sampled, yielding a deterministic and reproducible inference path per input.

## Results

On CNN/DailyMail summarization, SD achieves ROUGE-1/2/L of 44.31/21.18/41.01 at only 61.1% FLOPs, slightly exceeding BART-large (44.16/21.28/40.90 at 100% FLOPs). On XSum, SD reaches 45.20/22.16/37.30 at 81.9% FLOPs, matching BART's quality. For question answering, SD attains EM/F1 of 88.7/94.5 on SQuAD 1.1 (80.5% FLOPs) and 86.0/89.3 on SQuAD 2.0 (83.3% FLOPs), essentially matching BART. On GLUE classification, SD matches or marginally exceeds BART accuracy (e.g., 87.2% vs 87.0% on RTE) while using only ~81–84% FLOPs, and inference time drops from 10.3s to 5.1s per iteration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SD matches or slightly exceeds BART-large on all quality metrics while reducing FLOPs by 18–39% (e.g., ROUGE-L 41.01 vs 40.90 on CNN/DM at 61.1% FLOPs; EM/F1 88.7/94.5 vs 88.8/94.6 on SQuAD 1.1 at 80.5% FLOPs). Against other efficiency methods on CNN/DM (Table 3), SD achieves the same ROUGE-L as BART-student and Pegasus (41.01) but at 61.1% FLOPs versus their 93.1%, and outperforms CALM (40.54) and FREE (40.69) in both quality and efficiency. DQ-Bart achieves much lower FLOPs (18.2%) but at a significant quality cost (ROUGE-L 40.05 vs 41.01). On classification, SD matches BART on SST (96.6%) and slightly exceeds it on RTE (87.2% vs 87.0%) at ~81–84% FLOPs, while being marginally below RoBERTa on MNLI (89.7/90.0 vs 90.2/90.2). The method generalizes across architectures: applied to GPT-2 and T5, SD improves or matches their ROUGE while cutting FLOPs to 74.5%, confirming task- and model-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dynamic-network and early-exiting literature (RTJ, DeeBERT, FastBERT, BlockDrop, SkipNet) from classification and vision settings to autoregressive encoder-decoder generation models, where prior work has been largely absent. It departs from confidence-based early exiting by using a reinforcement learning agent with a deliberately designed candidate space (attention layers, FFN layers, and input tokens) rather than a single exit criterion. The optimization strategy also differs from prior RL-based dynamic networks by replacing the ad-hoc linear combination of quality and computation with a lexicographic constrained optimization that automatically balances the two objectives without manual tuning of a trade-off coefficient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~40% inference FLOPs reduction with negligible (or slightly positive) quality impact is practically significant for deploying large generation models on resource-constrained devices, and the method's generality across BART, GPT-2, T5, and LLaMA architectures suggests broad applicability. However, the approach adds a small training overhead (2.7% GPU memory, 1.6% step time) and the RL agent requires ~5K iterations to converge, which may be non-trivial for very large models. Open questions include combining switchable decisions with quantization or distillation (explicitly deferred to future work), scaling to much larger models (e.g., 70B+), and whether the first-layer-based decision heuristic remains effective as model depth increases substantially.
