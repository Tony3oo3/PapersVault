## Problem

Standard conformal prediction methods like APS accumulate sorted softmax probabilities to compute non-conformity scores, but softmax outputs are typically miscalibrated and long-tailed, causing tail classes to be easily included and inflating prediction set sizes. The paper asks whether the actual probability values are necessary at all, or whether label ranking information alone suffices for effective conformal prediction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the softmax output vector (K probability values) from any pre-trained classifier on an RGB image, but only retains the maximum softmax probability (MSP) and the relative ranking of labels, discarding all other probability magnitudes. It outputs a set-valued prediction of class labels with a finite-sample marginal coverage guarantee of 1−α, where the set size adapts to instance difficulty via the MSP and rank of the ground-truth label.

## Method

SAPS (Sorted Adaptive Prediction Sets) defines a non-conformity score that, for the top-ranked label, equals u·π_max(x), and for all remaining labels equals π_max(x) + (rank − 2 + u)·λ, where λ is a tunable weight on ranking information and u ~ U[0,1]. This replaces the accumulated softmax values in APS with a single calibrated confidence (MSP) plus a constant-weighted rank offset, minimizing dependence on unreliable tail probabilities while preserving instance-wise uncertainty. Theoretically, the paper proves that APS without any probability values yields prediction set sizes consistent with model top-k accuracy, and that SAPS with λ→∞ asymptotically matches this bound. The method inherits the standard conformal calibration procedure and marginal coverage guarantee, and requires only a single hyperparameter λ tuned on a validation split.

## Results

On ImageNet (α=0.1), SAPS reduces the average prediction set size from 20.95 (APS) to 2.98, a roughly 7× reduction, while maintaining the target 90% marginal coverage. Compared to RAPS (3.29), SAPS further shrinks the set to 2.98 and achieves a lower Each-Size Coverage Violation (ESCV) of 0.40 vs. 0.53. On CIFAR-100 (α=0.1), SAPS achieves a set size of 2.67 versus 7.88 for APS and 2.99 for RAPS. Under distribution shift (ImageNet-V2), SAPS still produces the smallest sets, confirming robustness beyond the calibration distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against APS (Romano et al., 2020), SAPS achieves a dramatic reduction in prediction set size: on ImageNet (α=0.1), the average set drops from 20.95 to 2.98 (≈7× smaller), and on CIFAR-100 from 7.88 to 2.67. Against RAPS (Angelopoulos et al., 2021), SAPS consistently produces smaller sets across all three datasets (e.g., 2.98 vs. 3.29 on ImageNet, 2.67 vs. 2.99 on CIFAR-100) and achieves a substantially lower ESCV (0.40 vs. 0.53 on ImageNet; 0.30 vs. 0.37 on CIFAR-100), indicating better conditional coverage. In the closest comparison—SAPS vs. RAPS(k_r=1) on CIFAR-10 (α=0.1)—set sizes are nearly identical (1.63 vs. 1.62), but SAPS's ESCV is 0.089 versus 0.138 for RAPS, more than 1.5× lower. On the distribution-shift benchmark ImageNet-V2, SAPS again yields the smallest sets, confirming that the advantage over both baselines persists when the test distribution differs from the calibration distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Adaptive Prediction Sets (APS, Romano et al., 2020) and Regularized APS (RAPS, Angelopoulos et al., 2021) frameworks for conformal classification, which both construct non-conformity scores by accumulating sorted softmax probabilities. The key conceptual departure is the observation that the actual probability values are largely redundant: only the label ranking and the maximum softmax probability (MSP) carry useful information for conformal prediction. SAPS replaces all non-maximum probability values with a constant, positioning itself between the fully value-dependent APS and the fully value-free ranking-only variant, while retaining MSP to preserve instance-wise adaptivity. The method also connects to the calibration literature (temperature scaling) and the MSP-as-confidence literature (Hendrycks & Gimpel, 2016).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: SAPS reduces average prediction set sizes by roughly 7× on ImageNet (20.95 → 2.98) while maintaining the desired marginal coverage, and it improves conditional coverage (ESCV) over RAPS across models and datasets. The conceptual finding that probability values are unnecessary for conformal prediction challenges a foundational assumption in the APS/RAPS line of work and suggests that future score-function design should prioritize ranking structure over probability magnitudes. Open questions include whether the hyperparameter λ can be eliminated or learned end-to-end, and whether the conditional coverage gap on large-scale datasets (e.g., ImageNet) can be further closed. The method is post-hoc and architecture-agnostic, making it straightforward to adopt alongside any pre-trained classifier.
