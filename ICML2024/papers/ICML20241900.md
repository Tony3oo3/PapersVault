## Problem

Online learning in constrained MDPs (CMDPs) with long-term constraints has only been studied under the assumption that constraints are sampled from a fixed stochastic distribution. No prior work provides guarantees when constraints are chosen adversarially, and existing primal-dual methods require the learner to know the Slater feasibility parameter ρ a priori—an unreasonable assumption in practice. This paper is the first to provide best-of-both-worlds guarantees for CMDPs that handle both stochastic and adversarial rewards and constraints simultaneously, without requiring knowledge of ρ.

## Input / Output

At each episode, the learner receives a reward vector r_t ∈ [0,1]^{|X×A|} and a constraint matrix G_t ∈ [-1,1]^{|X×A|×m} (each either sampled from a fixed distribution or chosen adversarially), along with a trajectory of state-action pairs from the unknown transition function. The learner outputs a policy π_t: X×A→[0,1] per episode. The transition function P is never known; the algorithm must estimate it online via confidence sets. The algorithm requires no knowledge of the Slater parameter ρ, the reward/constraint distributions, or whether the setting is stochastic or adversarial.

## Method

PDGD-OPS instantiates a zero-sum Lagrangian game between a primal player (optimizing over occupancy measures) and a dual player (optimizing over Lagrange multipliers in [0, T^{1/4}]^m). The primal player uses UC-O-GDPS, a novel upper-confidence online gradient descent policy search that replaces the mirror descent update of UC-O-REPS with projected gradient descent, enabling a weak no-interval regret property that is essential for bounding dual variables without knowing ρ. An adaptive learning rate η_t = 1/(ℓ̄_t · C√T) removes the quadratic dependence on ‖λ‖_1 from the primal regret bound. The key technical insight is that both the primal and dual players satisfy weak no-interval regret, which allows a contradiction argument to show that Lagrange multipliers are automatically bounded by ζ = 20mL²/ρ² whenever Condition 2.5 holds, yielding Õ(√T) regret and violation; when it does not hold, the bounded dual space [0, T^{1/4}]^m guarantees Õ(T^{3/4}) bounds in all cases.

## Results

In the stochastic constraint setting with Condition 2.5 (ρ ≥ T^{-1/8}L√(20m)) holding, PDGD-OPS achieves Õ(√T) cumulative regret and Õ(√T) cumulative constraint violation, matching state-of-the-art bounds. When Condition 2.5 does not hold, the algorithm still guarantees Õ(T^{3/4}) regret and violation. In the adversarial constraint setting, the algorithm achieves no-α-regret with α = ρ/(1+ρ) (i.e., it collects at least a ρ/(1+ρ) fraction of optimal reward) while maintaining Õ(ζ√T) sublinear constraint violation, where ζ = 20mL²/ρ². This is the first algorithm to provide guarantees for adversarially-selected constraints in CMDPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDGD-OPS is the only method in Table 1 that simultaneously handles adversarial rewards, adversarial constraints, unknown ρ, absence of Slater's condition, and the MDP setting. Efroni et al. (2020) handles stochastic rewards and constraints in MDPs with unknown ρ but cannot handle adversarial losses at all. Qiu et al. (2020) extends to adversarial rewards but still requires stochastic constraints and Slater's condition. Castiglioni et al. (2022b) handles adversarial rewards and constraints but operates in non-sequential (non-MDP) settings and critically requires the learner to know ρ in advance. Wei et al. (2023) handles MDPs with unknown ρ but only under bounded non-stationarity assumptions, not general adversarial constraints. The key differentiator of PDGD-OPS is the combination of a primal-dual framework with the weak no-interval regret property, which automatically bounds dual variables without requiring prior knowledge of ρ.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the UC-O-REPS framework of Rosenberg & Mansour (2019b) for adversarial MDPs by replacing mirror descent with online gradient descent, enabling a weak no-interval regret property that had never been studied in episodic adversarial MDPs. It adapts the best-of-both-worlds primal-dual paradigm from Balseiro et al. (2023) and Castiglioni et al. (2022b) to the CMDP setting, while critically removing the assumption that the Slater parameter ρ must be known a priori. The key departure from all prior CMDP online learning work (Efroni et al. 2020, Qiu et al. 2020, Wei et al. 2023) is the treatment of adversarially-selected constraints, which had been entirely unaddressed in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm to provide best-of-both-worlds guarantees for CMDPs, handling both stochastic and adversarial constraints without requiring knowledge of the underlying process or the Slater parameter ρ. The no-α-regret result (α = ρ/(1+ρ)) under adversarial constraints is the best possible given the impossibility result of Mannor et al. (2009), establishing a fundamental limit. Open questions include whether the T^(3/4) bound in the degenerate (small ρ) regime can be tightened, whether the loop-free MDP assumption can be relaxed, and whether the theoretical guarantees translate to practical performance in real-world constrained RL applications like autonomous driving or budget-constrained bidding.
