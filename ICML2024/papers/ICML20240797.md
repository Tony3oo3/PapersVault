## Problem

ML weather forecasting models like GraphCast cannot operate independently because they rely on ERA5 reanalysis data produced by traditional, computationally expensive data assimilation systems tightly coupled to specific NWP codes. Prior diffusion-based data assimilation methods were limited to small-scale or low-resolution problems and could not match the 0.25° horizontal resolution required for global ML forecast models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240797/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 48-hour GraphCast forecast (13 vertical levels × 10 variables at 0.25° resolution, ~7.4M grid values) and sparse column observations (as few as 1,000 columns, <0.4% of total grid points) along with a 2D hard mask (721×1440) marking observation locations. It produces a fully gridded assimilated atmospheric state at the same 0.25° resolution, ready as initial conditions for downstream forecast models. Observations are simulated by sampling random columns from ERA5, and the mask is broadcast across all variable and level dimensions during the soft-mask mixing step.

## Method

DiffDA adapts the pretrained GraphCast GNN as the denoising network ε_θ in a DDPM framework, exploiting the architectural match between a two-time-step forecast model and a denoiser that takes (noisy state, predicted state, timestep). Conditioning on the predicted state is achieved by concatenating it with the noisy state in feature channels during training. Sparse observation conditioning is applied exclusively at inference time via an inpainting-style soft mask: a hard observation mask is expanded into a soft mask through 'softbleed' (a (max,×)-convolution with a Gaussian kernel of σ_G=2.5), observations are interpolated via universal kriging to fill the soft mask support, and at each denoising step the known and unknown regions are blended using the soft mask. This inference-only observation conditioning avoids the combinatorial explosion of training with variable-length sparse inputs, and the skip connection from the predicted state to the de-normalized output ensures the assimilated field stays close to the forecast.

## Results

On single-step data assimilation at 0.25° resolution, DiffDA reduces RMSE below the 48-hour GraphCast forecast error for all three key variables (z500, t850, t2m) with as few as 4,000 observed columns (<0.4% of total data). With 40,000 columns (<3.9%), z500 RMSE drops to 19.5 m and t2m to 0.51 K, both below the 6-hour forecast error. When assimilated data is used as input for a 48-hour GraphCast forecast, the resulting errors with 10,000 columns are lower than 72-hour ERA5-based forecast errors, implying a lead-time loss of at most 24 hours. The method achieves the highest resolution (0.25°) among all ML-based data assimilation approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is GraphCast forecast error at varying lead times (6h–96h), which serves as a skill reference rather than a competing assimilation method. In single-step assimilation, DiffDA with 4,000 columns already beats the 48-hour forecast error for z500 (65.9 vs. 93.2 m), t850 (0.92 vs. 1.00 K), and t2m (1.02 vs. 1.03 K). At 40,000 columns, z500 (19.5 m) and t2m (0.51 K) surpass even the 6-hour forecast, while t850 (0.43 K) is marginally above the 6-hour value (0.40 K). In the downstream forecasting experiment, 48-hour forecasts initialized from DiffDA-assimilated data with 10,000 columns produce errors below the 72-hour ERA5-based forecast for all three variables, confirming a lead-time penalty of ≤24 hours. The paper does not compare against traditional 4D-Var or ensemble Kalman filter methods numerically, citing their computational intractability at 0.25° resolution, but notes that DiffDA runs in ~15 minutes on a single A100 GPU versus large compute clusters for traditional methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the pretrained GraphCast weather forecasting model as the backbone of a denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM), exploiting the architectural similarity between a forecast model (mapping two time steps to a future state) and a denoising step (mapping a noisy state to a cleaner one). For conditioning on sparse observations, it adapts the Repaint inpainting framework with a key practical modification: replacing the hard observation mask with a soft mask generated via (max,×)-convolution and filling the expanded support region with kriging interpolation, since hard masking fails due to spatial pooling in encoder-decoder architectures. Prior ML data assimilation work (Rozet & Louppe, Finn et al., Andry et al.) was limited to small-scale or low-resolution problems, while traditional variational and ensemble Kalman filter methods remain computationally expensive and tightly coupled to specific NWP codes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiffDA is the first ML data assimilation method to reach 0.25° global resolution (ERA5 level), which is critical because current ML forecast models are all trained on ERA5 data produced by traditional DA, creating a circular dependency that prevents them from being truly self-contained. The 15-minute single-GPU inference time (vs. large compute clusters for traditional methods) opens the door to assimilating more observations that are currently discarded, and to generating reanalysis datasets autoregressively. Key open questions include: error accumulation in autoregressive cycles (especially for surface variables like t2m), the absence of 4D assimilation and observation quality control, and the extension to non-point observations such as satellite imagery and radar soundings.
