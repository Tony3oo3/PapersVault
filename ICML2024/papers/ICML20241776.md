## Problem

In repeated principal-agent interactions, the principal can only influence the agent's action through monetary incentives, yet both the agent's reward vector and the principal's own reward distributions are unknown. Existing mechanism design approaches assume preferences are known a priori, and the only prior learning-based algorithm (Dogan et al., 2023) suffers from exponential dependence on the number of arms and requires careful hyperparameter tuning. This paper asks whether the principal can learn an optimal incentive policy with near-minimax regret in both multi-armed and contextual bandit settings.

## Input / Output

At each round the principal observes the available action set (a finite set of arms in the multi-armed case, or a compact subset of the unit ball in R^d in the contextual case) and the agent's chosen action plus her stochastic reward. The principal's only decision is which arm to incentivize and the scalar incentive amount (or, in the contextual case, an upper semi-continuous incentive function with finite support). The agent's rewards are deterministic and known to the agent but hidden from the principal; the principal's rewards are i.i.d. draws from unknown distributions. The output is a per-round incentive policy, and the theoretical guarantee is a cumulative regret bound of O(√(TK) log T) for the multi-armed case and O(d√T log T) for the contextual case.

## Method

IPA decouples the problem into two phases: first, a per-arm binary search (K⌈log₂T⌉ rounds total) estimates the infimal incentive π_a* needed to make the agent select arm a, and second, a standard bandit subroutine (e.g., UCB) is run in black-box fashion on the shifted reward instance where each arm's mean is reduced by the estimated incentive. For the contextual setting, Contextual IPA replaces the per-arm binary search with a multidimensional binary search over a sequence of confidence sets S_t ⊇ s* (the agent's reward vector), using the Projected Volume subroutine of Lobel et al. (2018) to handle adversarially chosen search directions dictated by the non-stationary action sets. Once the projected diameter of S_t falls below 1/T, the algorithm switches to a corruption-robust contextual bandit subroutine (CW-OFUL), treating the non-stationary gap between estimated and true incentives as bounded adversarial corruption (total budget ≤ 4). This two-component decomposition avoids the simultaneous exploration/exploitation difficulty of prior work and yields regret bounds that match, up to logarithmic factors, the minimax lower bounds for the underlying bandit problems.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is a regret bound of O(d√T log T) for Contextual IPA in the linear contextual setting (Corollary 2), matching the minimax lower bound Ω(d√T) for stochastic linear bandits up to logarithmic factors. In the multi-armed setting, IPA achieves nearly optimal distribution-free and instance-dependent regret bounds (Corollary 1), recovering standard UCB-style guarantees on a shifted bandit instance. Numerically, on a 5-arm instance with T=10,000, IPA's cumulative regret is nearly indistinguishable from a UCB Oracle that has full knowledge of the agent's reward vector, confirming that the binary-search cost for estimating optimal incentives is negligible.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares IPA against two baselines in its experiments (Figure 2, 5 arms, T=10,000, 100 runs). First, the Principal's ε-Greedy algorithm of Dogan et al. (2023b) exhibits clearly suboptimal cumulative regret and requires careful tuning of a hyperparameter m controlling exploration (m=500 was best; below m=500 the algorithm under-explores and incurs linear regret on some runs, while above m=500 it over-explores). Second, a UCB Oracle baseline that knows the agent's reward vector s and simply runs UCB on the shifted arm means μ_a = θ_a + s_a − max s_a' serves as an upper bound on achievable performance. IPA's regret curve is nearly equal to the UCB Oracle's, demonstrating that the additional K⌈log₂T⌉ binary-search rounds needed to estimate the agent's preferences impose negligible overhead. Unlike Dogan et al.'s method, IPA requires no hyperparameter tuning and avoids the simultaneous exploration/exploitation of both the principal's and agent's rewards.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the principal-agent bandit framework of Dogan et al. (2023a;b), which studied the same repeated game but relied on an ε-Greedy algorithm with suboptimal regret and a sensitive exploration hyperparameter. The key methodological departure is a clean two-phase decoupling: a binary search phase to estimate the agent's incentive thresholds, followed by a black-box bandit subroutine for the principal's own reward estimation. For the contextual extension, the paper repurposes the Projected Volume subroutine from Lobel et al. (2018) for multidimensional binary search and frames the non-stationary incentive shift as adversarial corruption, leveraging CW-OFUL from He et al. (2022) as the robust bandit subroutine.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm for incentivized learning in a contextual bandit setting, achieving O(d√T log T) regret that matches the minimax lower bound for stochastic linear bandits up to logarithmic factors. The practical relevance spans healthcare, ecological taxation, and any domain where a principal must guide a self-interested agent through monetary incentives while learning both parties' reward structures. Open questions include extending to strategic (non-greedy) agents who may exploit information rent, learning agents with non-stationary behavior, and MDP settings where the principal faces additional sequential decision complexity.
