## Problem

Existing self-supervised methods for EEG time series suffer from three interrelated gaps: (1) EEG lacks well-defined semantic units analogous to words in NLP, (2) reconstruction-based approaches (e.g., MAE) are unstable when applied to noisy, corrupted EEG signals, and (3) the inherent periodicity of physiological signals is ignored, causing small time shifts to produce spurious token distinctions. These limitations prevent robust contextual representation learning at scale.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multivariate EEG clips (19 channels, 12-second or 60-second segments) which are partitioned into non-overlapping patches of 250 samples along the time axis; each channel is processed independently through the encoder to preserve heterogeneous physiological dynamics. At fine-tuning, a [CLS] token representation is extracted per channel and fused via a 1×1 pointwise convolution before a single-layer FFN produces the final prediction (binary seizure detection or 4-class seizure type). The architecture naturally handles variable-length sequences since the [CLS] token provides a fixed-size sequence-level summary.

## Method

VQ-MTM is a BERT-style masked prediction model whose label branch consists of two parameter-free modules: a random-projection quantizer that maps each patch through a fixed Gaussian projection matrix and assigns it the index of the nearest codebook vector under cosine similarity (theoretically grounded in a generalization of the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma from Euclidean distance to inner products), and a phase-aligning module that applies the Time-Phase-Shift Equivariance of the Fourier Transform to subtract a frequency-dependent phase offset (k·arg(ẑ₁)) from each Fourier coefficient before inverse-transforming, thereby collapsing time-shifted variants of a periodic pattern into a single canonical form. The encoder branch is a 2-layer Transformer (dim 256, FFN 1024) operating on channel-separated patch embeddings, trained to predict the quantized pseudo-labels of randomly masked tokens (30% mask ratio) via a K-way softmax (K=1024). Because the quantizer and codebook are fixed after initialization, the total model complexity matches a vanilla Transformer, enabling straightforward scaling to large corpora like TUSZ (79.4 GB) and TUAB (58.6 GB).

## Results

On the TUSZ dataset, VQ-MTM achieves the best seizure detection AUROC of 0.904 (60s clips) and 0.887 (12s clips), surpassing the strongest baseline PatchTST (0.834 and 0.866) by 8.39% and 2.42% respectively. For seizure classification, VQ-MTM attains a weighted F1-score of 0.615 (60s) and 0.620 (12s), outperforming the best baseline (MAE at 0.585 for 60s) by 5.13%. On the TUAB dataset, VQ-MTM achieves AUROC of 0.871 (60s) and 0.868 (12s), edging out BIOT (0.865) and TimesNet (0.863) by small margins. On smaller UCR/UEA datasets (Epilepsy, ECG200, ECG5000), VQ-MTM achieves the highest accuracy (0.964, 0.860, 0.942) and F1-score (0.963, 0.857, 0.938) across all three.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VQ-MTM consistently outperforms all ten baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest large-dataset baselines, VQ-MTM beats PatchTST (Nie et al., ICLR 2023) by 8.39% AUROC on 60s TUSZ detection (0.904 vs 0.834) and 5.13% F1 on 60s classification (0.615 vs 0.554); it surpasses TimesNet (Wu et al., ICLR 2023) by 19.1% AUROC on 60s detection (0.904 vs 0.713) and MAE (He et al., CVPR 2022) by 21.1% AUROC on 60s detection (0.904 vs 0.747). SimMTM (Dong et al., NeurIPS 2023) performs worst on the large TUSZ/TUAB datasets (AUROC 0.637–0.653), which the authors attribute to its memory-intensive positive-pair generation being incompatible with large-scale data. On TUAB, VQ-MTM narrowly edges out BIOT (Yang et al., NeurIPS 2023) by 0.69% AUROC on 60s clips (0.871 vs 0.865). On the smaller UCR/UEA datasets, VQ-MTM dominates Informer (Zhou et al., AAAI 2021), Autoformer (Wu et al., NeurIPS 2021), FEDformer (Zhou et al., ICML 2022), and iTransformer (Liu et al., ICLR 2024), achieving 0.964 accuracy on Epilepsy vs. 0.862 (Informer) and 0.939 (FEDformer), and 0.942 accuracy on ECG5000 matching iTransformer's 0.942 while yielding a higher F1 (0.938 vs 0.932). DCRNN (Tang et al., ICLR 2022) is outperformed by 13.1% AUROC on 60s TUSZ detection (0.904 vs 0.753), confirming that the GNN-based self-supervised approach is less effective than the proposed random-projection quantization strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

VQ-MTM builds on the BERT-style masked token prediction paradigm and the random-projection quantization strategy from speech pretraining (USM, Chiu et al. 2022), but departs in two key ways: it replaces the ℓ2-norm nearest-neighbor lookup with a cosine-similarity-based quantizer backed by a generalized Johnson-Lindenstrauss theorem for inner products, and it introduces a phase-alignment module grounded in the Time-Phase-Shift Equivariance of the Fourier Transform to handle periodic signal variants. Unlike MAE-style reconstruction or contrastive-learning approaches for time series, VQ-MTM generates discrete pseudo-labels from a parameter-free codebook, and unlike the concurrent LaBraM (Jiang et al. 2024a) which uses a learnable VQ-VAE quantizer, VQ-MTM's quantizer is fixed after initialization, making it more parameter- and compute-efficient. The channel-separation strategy (encode each channel independently, fuse via 1×1 conv at fine-tuning) aligns with the inductive bias adopted in PatchTST for multivariate forecasting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses three concrete gaps in EEG self-supervised learning—absence of well-defined semantic units, sensitivity to acquisition noise, and neglect of inherent periodicity—within a single coherent framework, and validates the approach on two of the largest publicly available EEG corpora (TUSZ at 79.4 GB, TUAB at 58.6 GB). The phase-alignment module is a genuinely novel theoretical contribution (leveraging Fourier equivariance to collapse time-shifted variants into a single token) that could transfer to other periodic physiological or industrial signals. Open questions include whether the fixed random-projection codebook generalizes well to non-EEG domains without re-tuning, whether the 2-layer/256-dim Transformer is near-optimal or whether deeper encoders would benefit more from the quantized labels, and how the method scales to even longer recordings (hours-long continuous EEG) where the patch-level periodicity assumption may break down.
