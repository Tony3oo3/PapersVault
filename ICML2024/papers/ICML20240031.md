## Problem

Prediction-based NAS relies on encoding neural network architectures into vectors that feed an accuracy predictor, yet the field has fragmented into many encoding paradigms—structural (adjacency/path), score-based (zero-cost proxies), unsupervised learned (Arch2Vec, CATE), and supervised learned (GNN internal representations)—without a systematic comparison of their relative effectiveness. Moreover, no prior work provides a unified encoding scheme that enables a single predictor to transfer across heterogeneous NAS search spaces with only a handful of accuracy samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240031/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The primary input is a cell-based architecture graph (adjacency matrix plus per-edge operation labels), which is optionally concatenated with a zero-cost proxy vector (13 scalar measurements), an Arch2Vec latent vector, or a CATE latent vector before the prediction head. For cross-space transfer, a unique numerical search-space index is appended to each operation, creating a unified operation vocabulary across all 13 NAS spaces. The output is a scalar predicted accuracy (trained with pairwise hinge ranking loss) used to rank the entire search space for iterative sampling-based NAS.

## Method

FLAN ensembles two graph-flow modules—Dense Graph Flow (DGF) with residual connections to counteract over-smoothing, and Graph Attention (GAT) with pairwise node interactions—repeated 5 times, and adds an iterative operation-embedding update mechanism where a backward GNN pass over T timesteps refines the operation embedding table before prediction. The key empirical finding is that supervised learned GNN encodings outperform all other categories, and concatenating supplemental encodings (especially ZCP) before the 3-layer MLP head yields up to 15% additional Kendall-τ improvement. For transfer, FLAN uses unified encodings that index operations by (search-space, operation) pairs, allowing a predictor pre-trained on 1024 samples from a source space to be fine-tuned on 4–16 samples of a target space, achieving 8× sample-efficiency gains over from-scratch training. The full pipeline trains in under 10 minutes on a single GPU and evaluates ~160 architectures per second at inference.

## Results

FLAN_ZCP achieves the best Kendall-τ correlation across all NAS spaces, reaching 0.831 on NASBench-101 (10% samples), 0.788 on NASBench-201 (1% samples), and 0.721 on NASBench-301 (5% samples), outperforming the prior best (TA-GATES) by up to 15% in Kendall-τ. In end-to-end NAS on NASBench-101, FLAN_CAZ achieves 94.22% test accuracy with only 8 trained models, a 2.12× reduction over Zero-Cost NAS (50 models). Cross-search-space transfer with unified encodings yields at least 8× sample efficiency improvement over from-scratch predictors, and FLAN^T with ZCP supplementary encoding achieves 0.622 Kendall-τ on DARTS with just 16 samples versus CDP's 0.531 with 100 samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLAN_ZCP consistently outperforms TA-GATES (the prior state-of-the-art) by 3–5% Kendall-τ across NASBench-101, 201, and 301, with the largest gap on NB101 at 1% samples (0.698 vs. 0.668). Against structural encodings (ADJ, Path from White et al.), FLAN_ZCP achieves roughly 2× higher Kendall-τ, confirming that learned graph encodings with supplementary signals substantially surpass fixed structural representations. Among unsupervised learned encodings, CATE is the strongest standalone method (0.571 on NB201 at 1%) but still trails FLAN_ZCP (0.788) by a wide margin; Arch2Vec performs worst overall. In cross-domain transfer, FLAN^T with ZCP beats GENNAPE's ensemble after 50-sample fine-tuning (0.944 vs. 0.910 Spearman-ρ on NB201), though GENNAPE retains a zero-shot advantage (0.815 vs. 0.702). Against CDP, FLAN^T with ZCP achieves 0.622 Kendall-τ on DARTS with only 16 samples versus CDP's 0.531 with 100 samples, a 5× sample-efficiency gain. In end-to-end NAS (Table 8), FLAN_CAZ matches the 94.22% test accuracy of BONAS (1000 models), Aging Evo (418 models), BRP-NAS (140 models), and Zero-Cost NAS (50 models) using only 8 trained models, a 2.12× improvement over the best prior sample-based method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the encoding taxonomy introduced by White et al. (2020) by broadening the definition of 'encoding' to encompass supervised learned representations (e.g., GNN intermediate activations) and unsupervised latent encodings (Arch2Vec, CATE), unifying them under a single comparative framework. Architecturally, FLAN builds directly on TA-GATES' iterative operation embedding mechanism and the Dense Graph Flow (DGF) module from Ning et al. (2023), adding a parallel GAT-based attention flow and residual connections to form a dual graph-flow ensemble. The unified encoding strategy for cross-search-space transfer extends the concept from Multi-Predict and GENNAPE by appending search-space indices to operation vectors, enabling a single predictor to generalize across 13 distinct NAS spaces. The paper also adopts the BRP-NAS iterative sampling algorithm for end-to-end NAS evaluation, positioning FLAN as a drop-in predictor replacement rather than a new search strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The open-sourcing of structural, score-based, and learned encodings for over 1.5 million architectures across 13 NAS spaces is a significant community resource that removes a major barrier to reproducible predictor research. The demonstrated 46× sample efficiency gain from cross-domain transfer (using only 4–16 target-space accuracy samples) offers a concrete path toward reusing NAS knowledge across design spaces, reducing the computational and carbon cost of NAS. However, the study is limited to cell-based search spaces; whether unified encodings and transfer generalize to macro-level or non-cell-based architectures (e.g., BLOX, NAS-Bench-ASR) remains an open question. Additionally, the finding that few-shot fine-tuning can sometimes degrade predictor performance (Table 3) suggests that the optimal transfer protocol is non-trivial and warrants further investigation.
