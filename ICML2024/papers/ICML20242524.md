## Problem

Existing long-tailed learning strategies exhibit an inherent trade-off: improving tail-class accuracy typically degrades head-class performance, and no single strategy achieves balanced performance across all frequency strata. The paper formalizes this trade-off theoretically and reframes long-tailed learning as a multi-objective optimization problem over head, medium, and tail class performance, then asks how to fuse heterogeneous strategies to approach the Pareto frontier.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100-LT, 224×224 for ImageNet-LT and iNaturalist 2018) and outputs a single class label per image. During training, each input image is processed through a shared backbone (ResNet-32 or ResNet-50) and then routed to K task-specific heads, each implementing a different long-tailed strategy (e.g., CE, Balanced Softmax, KPS, BCL). At inference, the EOSS module selects the per-class best-performing strategy head to produce the final prediction, so the output is still a single class label but the decision is made by whichever fused strategy historically performed best on that class.

## Method

MOOSF fuses multiple heterogeneous long-tailed learning strategies within a multi-task learning framework where all strategies share a feature extraction backbone but retain their own loss functions and classifier heads. Three modules resolve the conflicts inherent in such fusion: (1) HICA dynamically computes per-strategy weights via softmax over cosine similarities between each strategy's per-class accuracy vector and the mean accuracy vector, adapting the loss weighting as training progresses; (2) GHOP orthogonalizes each strategy's gradient by projecting out components along all other strategies' gradients, eliminating linear gradient conflicts before the update step; (3) EOSS tracks per-class accuracy across strategies and, at test time, routes each sample to the strategy that historically achieved the highest accuracy on its true class. The paper proves that under standard smoothness and learning-rate assumptions, the fused parameters converge to a Pareto-optimal solution, and that if the strategy set satisfies a heterogeneous attention property, the fused strategy can match or exceed the best individual strategy on every frequency stratum simultaneously.

## Results

On CIFAR-100-LT (IR=100), MOOSF(CE+BS) achieves 52.1% overall accuracy, surpassing the best single-strategy baselines BCL (44.2%), RIDE (47.5%), and SHIKE (46.9%) by 7.9, 4.6, and 5.2 points respectively. On ImageNet-LT, MOOSF(KPS+BCL) reaches 57.4% overall accuracy, outperforming BCL (55.6%) and RIDE (53.6%). On iNaturalist 2018, MOOSF(KPS+BCL) achieves 72.7% overall accuracy, exceeding BCL (71.8%) and RIDE (71.6%). Notably, fusing two simple strategies (CE+BS) outperforms all complex single strategies across all three datasets and imbalance ratios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOOSF consistently outperforms all individual baselines across all three datasets. On CIFAR-100-LT (IR=100), MOOSF(CE+BS) achieves 52.1% overall accuracy, beating CE alone (38.1%) by 14.0 points, BS alone (42.8%) by 9.3 points, KPS (42.2%) by 9.9 points, BCL (44.2%) by 7.9 points, RIDE (47.5%) by 4.6 points, and SHIKE (46.9%) by 5.2 points. On ImageNet-LT, MOOSF(KPS+BCL) reaches 57.4%, surpassing BCL (55.6%) by 1.8 points, RIDE (53.6%) by 3.8 points, KPS (52.3%) by 5.1 points, and cRT (47.3%) by 10.1 points. On iNaturalist 2018, MOOSF(KPS+BCL) achieves 72.7%, exceeding BCL (71.8%) by 0.9 points and RIDE (71.6%) by 1.1 points. The key finding is that fusing two simple strategies (e.g., CE+BS) surpasses all complex single strategies (BCL, SHIKE, RIDE), demonstrating that the multi-objective fusion framework extracts complementary strengths that no single strategy achieves alone. MOOSF also consistently outperforms simple MTL fusion (e.g., 52.1% vs. 48.0% for CE+BS at IR=100), confirming the benefit of the HICA, GHOP, and EOSS conflict-resolution modules.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the multi-task learning as multi-objective optimization framework (Sener & Koltun, NeurIPS 2018), adapting it to the long-tailed learning setting by treating head, medium, and tail class performance as competing objectives. It departs from the dominant paradigm of designing increasingly complex single strategies (e.g., SHIKE, BCL, RIDE) by instead fusing existing heterogeneous strategies through a shared backbone with task-specific heads, augmented by three novel conflict-resolution modules (HICA, GHOP, EOSS). The theoretical contribution formalizes the head-tail trade-off via a generalization error bound (Proposition 3.1) and proves that the fused model converges to a Pareto optimal solution (Proposition 3.7), providing a principled justification that prior heuristic fusion attempts lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical and conceptually clean alternative to the arms race of ever-more-complex single long-tailed strategies: fusing two simple strategies (e.g., CE+BS) with MOOSF can match or exceed state-of-the-art complex methods at lower computational cost, which is attractive for practitioners. However, the approach introduces additional parameters and training overhead, and performance gains diminish (or even reverse) when fusing more than two strategies due to escalating gradient conflicts and Pareto frontier complexity. Open questions include how to handle asynchronous optimization dynamics during fusion, how to scale to more than a handful of strategies without degradation, and whether the framework generalizes beyond classification to other long-tailed tasks like detection or segmentation.
