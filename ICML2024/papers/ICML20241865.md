## Problem

Large-scale nonconvex optimization with nonnegativity constraints (e.g., nonnegative least-squares, NNMF, L1-regularised problems) is poorly served by existing methods: first-order projected gradient is slow on ill-conditioned problems and stalls near saddle points, while second-order alternatives (projected Newton, interior point) introduce computationally challenging subproblems that do not exploit the simplicity of the nonnegative orthant projection. The two-metric projection framework of Bertsekas (1982) elegantly blends projected gradient and Newton steps but had no established complexity guarantees in nonconvex settings and required explicit Hessian inversion, making it impractical at scale.

## Input / Output

The method requires access to a twice continuously differentiable (possibly nonconvex) objective f: R^d → R subject to x ≥ 0, consuming only gradient evaluations and Hessian-vector products (computable via one extra backpropagation pass) rather than the full Hessian. It terminates at an ε-FO point satisfying three approximate optimality conditions: gradient non-negativity on the active set, a weighted gradient norm bound on the active set, and a gradient norm bound on the inactive set. The active/inactive split is controlled by a tolerance δ_k that scales as ε_g^{1/2}, and the MINRES inexactness tolerance η can be fixed (Algorithm 1) or scaled with ε_k (Algorithm 2).

## Method

The core mechanism splits the index set into an active set (components near the boundary, x_k^i ≤ δ_k) and an inactive set (interior components), then applies a projected gradient step on the active set and an inexact Newton-MR step on the inactive set simultaneously, with feasibility restored by a single projection onto R_+^d. The Newton subproblem is solved inexactly via MINRES rather than CG, which naturally handles indefinite Hessians: a simple scalar condition on the MINRES iterates (⟨r, Hr⟩ ≤ 0) certifies a non-positive curvature direction, triggering a forward-tracking line search that takes large steps through flat regions. Two variants are provided: Algorithm 1 requires only Lipschitz gradient and boundedness, achieving O(ε_g^{-2}) iterations, while Algorithm 2 adds Lipschitz Hessian and curvature regularity assumptions to achieve the state-of-the-art O(ε_g^{-3/2}) rate via Type II steps that zero out the active-set component when active optimality is already met. Under non-degeneracy and second-order sufficiency, the method identifies the true active set in finite iterations and then reduces to unconstrained Newton-MR, yielding superlinear or quadratic local convergence.

## Results

The paper evaluates convergence (objective value vs. number of oracle calls) to an ε_g = 10⁻⁸ first-order optimality tolerance across five problems of varying dimensionality (d = 785 to 385,220). On convex multinomial regression (MNIST, CIFAR-10), FISTA achieves the fastest convergence, while the proposed Newton-MR TMP (MR) is competitive with projected Newton-CG (CG). On nonconvex problems (two-layer neural network on Fashion MNIST, and both NNMF tasks on 20 Newsgroup and Olivetti Faces), MR consistently outperforms all baselines, reaching the target tolerance in fewer oracle calls. The paper also demonstrates fast local (superlinear/quadratic) convergence once the active set is identified.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against projected Newton-CG (Xie & Wright, 2023), the proposed MR method achieves faster convergence on all nonconvex problems (neural network training and both NNMF tasks), while being competitive on convex regression. Against projected gradient (Beck, 2017), MR dominates across all five problems, particularly on ill-conditioned and high-dimensional instances where PG stagnates. Against FISTA (Beck & Teboulle, 2009), the paper acknowledges that FISTA clearly outperforms MR on the two convex multinomial regression problems (MNIST and CIFAR-10), though MR remains competitive. Against proximal gradient with momentum (Lin et al., 2020), MR achieves superior convergence on the nonconvex neural network and NNMF problems. Overall, MR wins or ties on 4 out of 5 problem instances, with the sole loss being to FISTA on convex settings where first-order accelerated methods are naturally strongest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Bertsekas's (1982) two-metric projection (TMP) framework, which blends projected gradient steps on the active set with Newton steps on the inactive set, by replacing the exact Newton subproblem with an inexact MINRES-based solver and incorporating non-positive curvature directions. The inexact Newton machinery is drawn directly from the Newton-MR framework of Liu & Roosta (2022a,b), which established MINRES's suitability for nonconvex settings through its natural NPC detection and monotonicity properties. A key departure from the closest concurrent work, Xie & Wright's (2023) projected Newton-CG, is the simultaneous application of gradient and Newton-MR steps within a single iteration, rather than the sequential approach (gradient steps until active-set optimality, then Newton-CG) that Xie & Wright employ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first paper to establish global iteration complexity guarantees for TMP-type algorithms in nonconvex settings, with Algorithm 2 achieving the state-of-the-art O(ε^{-3/2}) rate under Lipschitz Hessian assumptions. The Hessian-free design (requiring only Hessian-vector products) makes the method practical for large-scale ML problems like NNMF and sparse regression, where forming or inverting the Hessian is infeasible. Open questions include extensions to general box constraints, stochastic variants, and whether the strong curvature assumptions (Assumptions 3.6–3.7) needed for the improved rate can be weakened. The practical advantage over projected Newton-CG is modest in the experiments, and FISTA still dominates on convex problems, suggesting the method's primary value is in nonconvex, ill-conditioned regimes where first-order methods stagnate.
