## Problem

Existing audio-visual speech separation (AVSS) methods fuse auditory and visual features at only a single temporal scale and lack explicit selective attention for speaker selection, in contrast to the brain which integrates cross-modal information at multiple cortical levels and employs attention to focus on relevant speech. This limits their ability to exploit semantic associations across scales and to efficiently distinguish the target speaker from interferers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241174/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 2-second noisy speech waveform at 16 kHz and synchronized 88×88 grayscale lip frames at 25 FPS as input. The audio is encoded by a single 1D convolution (kernel 16, stride 8) into embeddings, while the video is encoded by a pre-trained 3D-convolution + ResNet-18 lip-reading model (CTCNet-Lip). The separation network outputs a soft mask of shape N_a × T_a' that is element-wise multiplied with the noisy speech embedding, and a 1D transposed convolution decodes the result back to a clean single-speaker waveform.

## Method

IIANet comprises two parallel unimodal CNN networks (audio and video) connected by two types of attention blocks: IntraA (top-down attention within each modality, using global and local variants) and InterA (cross-modal selective attention at three hierarchical levels: top, middle, and bottom). The InterA blocks use sigmoid-gated element-wise products rather than similarity-matrix attention, enabling efficient cross-modal modulation at each temporal scale. The network iterates N_F=4 audio-visual fusion cycles followed by N_S=12 audio-only refinement cycles with shared parameters, progressively refining the separation mask. This multi-scale, multi-level attention design is inspired by the brain's hierarchical cross-modal integration (thalamus → primary cortices → association areas) and top-down attentional modulation, achieving state-of-the-art separation with only 11% of CTCNet's MACs.

## Results

On the three standard AVSS benchmarks, IIANet achieves SI-SNRi of 16.0 dB (LRS2), 18.3 dB (LRS3), and 13.6 dB (VoxCeleb2), outperforming the previous SOTA CTCNet by 1.7, 0.9, and 1.7 dB respectively. The lightweight IIANet-fast variant (reducing audio-only cycles from 12 to 6) still surpasses CTCNet on all datasets while using only 7% of CTCNet's MACs and running 40% faster on CPU. In terms of efficiency, IIANet uses 11% of CTCNet's MACs, 44% of its parameters, and 16% of its GPU memory while delivering superior separation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IIANet outperforms all six baselines across all three datasets on every reported metric. Against the previous SOTA CTCNet (Li et al., 2022), IIANet gains +1.7 dB SI-SNRi on LRS2, +0.9 dB on LRS3, and +1.7 dB on VoxCeleb2, while using only 11% of CTCNet's MACs (18.6G vs. 167.1G) and 44% of its parameters (3.1M vs. 7.0M). The fast variant IIANet-fast still beats CTCNet by +0.8 dB SI-SNRi on LRS2 and +0.7 dB on VoxCeleb2, while being 40% faster on CPU (0.52s vs. 1.26s). Against the Transformer-based AVLiT-8 (Martel et al., 2023), IIANet leads by 3.2–4.8 dB SI-SNRi depending on dataset. The CNN-based baselines (AVConvTasNet, VisualVoice, CaffNet-C, LWTNet) trail IIANet by 3.5–8.4 dB SI-SNRi, with the largest gaps on LRS3 where IIANet's multi-scale attention provides the most benefit over single-scale fusion approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

IIANet extends the top-down intra-modal attention mechanism from TDANet (Li et al., 2023), originally designed for audio-only speech separation, into the audio-visual domain by introducing inter-attention (InterA) blocks that perform cross-modal selective fusion at multiple temporal scales (top, middle, bottom). This departs from prior AVSS methods like CTCNet and VisualVoice, which fuse audio-visual features at only a single temporal scale, and from Transformer-based approaches (CaffNet, VOViT) that use attention as the backbone rather than as a lightweight selective fusion mechanism. The key architectural choice—replacing similarity-matrix attention with sigmoid-gated element-wise products—draws on the efficiency of the TDANet design while distributing cross-modal interaction across the hierarchy, mirroring the multi-level neural integration observed in the brain's auditory and visual pathways.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the dramatic efficiency-quality tradeoff: IIANet-fast achieves better separation than the previous SOTA (CTCNet) with only 7% of its MACs and 40% faster CPU inference, making high-quality AVSS viable on resource-constrained hardware. The finding that the specific video encoder matters little (<0.3 dB across three encoders) while the fusion strategy dominates suggests future work should focus on fusion architectures rather than visual feature extraction. Open questions include scalability beyond 4 speakers, robustness in streaming/real-time settings, and whether the multi-scale attention principle transfers to other multimodal separation or enhancement tasks. The paper also acknowledges that side-profile visual cues degrade performance (though less than baselines), leaving room for more robust visual feature handling.
