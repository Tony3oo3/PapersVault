## Problem

Multi-timestep simulation in Spiking Neural Networks simultaneously inflates training memory to O(L×T) (storing activations across all timesteps) and increases inference energy to O(T) (repeated input processing). Existing training optimizations such as OTTT, SLTT, and S-RevSNN reduce one of these costs but not both, leaving a fundamental trade-off between cheap training and low-power inference unresolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240786/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For static image classification (ImageNet-1K at 224×224), the input is encoded only once and then partitioned into T groups, each fed to an independent sub-network, rather than being repeated T times as in standard SNNs. For neuromorphic event-stream datasets (CIFAR10-DVS, DVS128 Gesture at 128×128), the input is encoded at each timestep following conventional practice. The output is a single class label; only the last timestep's prediction is used for the cross-entropy loss, with no averaging across timesteps.

## Method

T-RevSNN turns off the temporal dynamics of all spiking neurons except the last layer of each stage, so that most neurons perform a simple spatial forward pass (V = W·S) with no membrane potential recurrence. At the remaining 'turn-on' neurons, the LIF dynamics are made temporally reversible (Eq. 9), meaning only the final-timestep membrane potential must be stored to reconstruct all earlier states, reducing training memory to O(L). To achieve O(1) inference, the encoded input features and the entire network are split into T independent sub-networks that share parameters only at the key spiking neurons, eliminating the need to process the input T times. Multi-level temporal-reversible connections (Eq. 10) inject high-level features from deeper layers of the previous timestep into the current one, and the basic block follows a ConvNeXt-style design with depth-wise separable convolutions, no batch normalization, and ReZero-scaled membrane shortcuts for stable training.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, T-RevSNN (ResNet-18, 512 channels, 29.8M params, T=4) achieves 73.2% top-1 accuracy with only 85.7 MB/img training memory, 9.1 min/ep training time, and 2.8 mJ inference energy. This represents an 8.6× memory reduction, 2.0× training speedup, and 1.6× energy reduction compared to the state-of-the-art Spike-driven Transformer (74.6%, 730.0 MB/img, 18.8 min/ep, 4.5 mJ). On neuromorphic datasets, T-RevSNN (T=16) reaches 79.2% on CIFAR10-DVS and 97.9% on DVS128 Gesture with 18.9 MB memory, matching or exceeding most baselines while using roughly 3× less memory than Spike-driven Transformer (51.4 MB) and S-RevSNN (25.0 MB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against spiking ResNet baselines, T-RevSNN (512) dominates across all metrics: 73.2% accuracy vs. 67.8% (SEW-ResNet-50) and 69.4% (MS-ResNet-34), with 7× lower memory (85.7 vs. 596.9 MB/img) and lower inference energy (2.8 vs. 4.9/5.1 mJ). Against spiking Transformers, T-RevSNN (512) achieves 73.2% vs. 74.6% for Spike-driven Transformer (a 1.4% gap attributed to CNN vs. Transformer architecture), but with 8.6× less training memory (85.7 vs. 730.0 MB/img), 2.0× faster training (9.1 vs. 18.8 min/ep), and 1.6× lower inference energy (2.8 vs. 4.5 mJ); it also outperforms SpikFormer (73.4%) in memory and energy while matching accuracy. Among training-optimization methods, T-RevSNN surpasses OTTT (66.2%) and SLTT (67.0%) in accuracy while using comparable or lower memory (57.5–85.7 vs. 71.7–117.3 MB/img) and significantly less training time (6.1–9.1 vs. 18.1–23.4 min/ep). On neuromorphic datasets, T-RevSNN (T=16) matches SEW-ResNet and SpikFormer on Gesture (97.9%) and approaches Spike-driven Transformer on CIFAR10-DVS (79.2% vs. 80.0%), all with 2.7–5× lower memory than those baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two threads: the reversible neural network paradigm (Gomez et al., 2017), which eliminates the need to store intermediate activations during backpropagation, and the observation from OTTT (Xiao et al., 2022) and SLTT (Meng et al., 2023) that temporal gradients in SNNs contribute minimally to final weight updates. The key departure is that prior training-optimization methods (OTTT, SLTT, S-RevSNN) only modify the backward pass while leaving the forward propagation unchanged, so inference cost remains O(T). T-RevSNN instead alters the forward pass itself—turning off temporal dynamics for most neurons and splitting the network into T independent sub-networks—thereby coupling training efficiency with O(1) inference. Unlike S-RevSNN (Zhang & Zhang, 2024), which is spatially reversible, this work is temporally reversible, exploiting the natural invertibility of the LIF membrane potential update.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The simultaneous achievement of O(L) training memory and O(1) inference energy addresses a fundamental scaling bottleneck for SNNs: prior methods could reduce one cost but not both, making large-scale SNN training prohibitively expensive. The 8.6× memory reduction and 2× training speedup make ImageNet-scale SNN training feasible on standard hardware, potentially accelerating the transition from SNN research to practical neuromorphic deployment. However, the approach is currently limited to CNN-based blocks (the authors report that spiking Transformer blocks fail to converge under this architecture), and a 1.4% accuracy gap remains versus the best Transformer-based SNN. Open questions include whether temporal reversibility can be extended to attention-based architectures, whether it generalizes to long-sequence or video tasks, and whether the O(1) inference advantage holds on neuromorphic hardware with the proposed sparse-addition implementation.
