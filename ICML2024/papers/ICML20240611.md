## Problem

Existing opponent exploitation methods in imperfect information games either guarantee safety against deceptive opponents or robust exploitation under modeling errors, but rarely both simultaneously. Moreover, traditional safety is benchmarked against Nash Equilibrium, which is computationally infeasible in large games where all practical strategies are inherently exploitable, making the safety criterion overly restrictive and limiting potential profit.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240611/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a precomputed blueprint strategy (solved offline via Monte Carlo CFR), an opponent model providing estimated reach probabilities at the top infosets of the current subgame, and the game state defining which subgame to solve. It produces a refined subgame strategy for the acting player that maximizes exploitation of the modeled opponent while satisfying per-infoset counterfactual value safety constraints, computed in real-time by solving a constructed gadget game with CFR. The gadget game's structure depends on the number of opponent infosets k and a tunable parameter β that controls the safety-exploitation tradeoff.

## Method

OX-Search introduces 'adaptation safety'—requiring the exploitation strategy to be no more exploitable than the blueprint—replacing the impractical NE-based safety benchmark. It formulates a constrained optimization that maximizes the weighted improvement over the estimated opponent distribution subject to non-negative CBV improvement at every opponent infoset in the subgame. To solve this efficiently in real-time, the paper constructs a gadget game by duplicating the subgame, shifting utilities by the blueprint's counterfactual values, and adding a chance node that routes to either an exploitation branch (weighted by estimated reach probabilities) or a safety-verification branch (with option nodes allowing the opponent to opt out). The NE of this gadget game, computed via CFR, yields the optimal exploitation strategy, and the paper proves that the resulting strategy is adaptation-safe (Theorem 4.3) and guarantees a worst-case profit increase of (1−ε)δ even under relative modeling error ε (Theorem 4.4).

## Results

OX-Search is evaluated on exploitability (lower is better) and head-to-head utility (higher is better) across Leduc Hold'em, Flop Hold'em Poker (FHP), Liar's Dice, and Goofspiel. OX-Search consistently achieves lower exploitability than the blueprint strategy in both Leduc Hold'em and FHP, and its exploitability remains insensitive to increasing modeling error, unlike RNR which degrades. In head-to-head evaluations against evolving opponents (Pr_shuffle=0.2 in Leduc, 0.5 in FHP), OX-Search outperforms both SES and Real-time RNR in both games. Against static opponents with modeling noise, OX-Search matches SES in Leduc Hold'em and outperforms both SES and RNR in FHP, while RNR's performance deteriorates sharply as estimation error grows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SES (Liu et al., 2022), OX-Search achieves lower exploitability in Leduc Hold'em (where SES is less effective due to no abstraction) and comparable exploitability in FHP; OX-Search also wins on head-to-head utility against evolving opponents in both games and maintains robustness under modeling errors in FHP where SES degrades. Against Real-time RNR (Johanson et al., 2007), OX-Search clearly wins on exploitability safety—RNR's exploitability increases with modeling error while OX-Search's stays flat—and on robust exploitation in FHP; RNR can show slightly better raw exploitation in Leduc Hold'em under very small errors but deteriorates significantly as error grows. Against MaxMargin subgame solving (Brown & Sandholm, 2017), OX-Search achieves comparable or better exploitability while exploiting opponents more aggressively, since MaxMargin maximizes the minimum improvement (a conservative objective) whereas OX-Search only requires non-negative improvement, allowing it to redirect gains toward high-reach-probability infosets. In Liar's Dice, OX-Search generalizes well, though increasing β may further improve performance; in Goofspiel, exploitability remains consistent regardless of estimation error, likely due to the game's hidden-action information structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the safe subgame solving framework (Brown & Sandholm, 2017) and the gadget-game construction technique for constrained strategy solving (Davis et al., 2019), while adopting the inductive proof structure from subgame decomposition (Burch et al., 2014). Its key conceptual departure is redefining safety relative to a precomputed blueprint strategy rather than the Nash Equilibrium, which prior methods (Ganzfried & Sandholm, 2015b; Liu et al., 2022) used as the safety benchmark. The OX-Search framework unifies real-time search with this 'adaptation safety' criterion, and its gadget game is a novel combination of MaxMargin and Subgame Resolving constructions that encodes both the exploitation objective and the per-infoset safety constraints into a single equilibrium-finding problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The adaptation safety notion provides a pragmatic safety guarantee for large-scale games where computing an exact NE is infeasible, and the theoretical results (Theorems 4.3–4.6) give clean bounds on both exploitability and worst-case profit under modeling error—something prior methods like SES and RNR could not simultaneously guarantee. Limitations include the restriction to two-player zero-sum games, reliance on reach-probability models rather than predictions of future opponent actions, and the need for a precomputed blueprint. Open questions include whether the framework extends to multiplayer settings, whether incorporating opponent action predictions preserves adaptation safety, and how the approach scales to games with even larger state spaces.
