## Problem

Existing text-to-avatar methods suffer from oversaturation (exaggerated geometry and color contrast) and low visual quality, while general text-to-3D methods produce static objects that cannot be animated. The fundamental tension is that Score Distillation Sampling enforces per-view similarity to a 2D diffusion model, which is inherently multi-view inconsistent, driving the optimizer toward over-saturated solutions when applied to a single precise 3D representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241375/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single free-form text prompt (e.g., a character name) and produces a fully animatable 3D avatar parameterized by SMPL-X vertex offsets (10,475 vertices), a 2048×2048 albedo texture map, and pose parameters. During optimization, rendered images are 800×800 pixels, and the final avatar can be posed arbitrarily via linear blend skinning, making it directly usable in conventional graphics pipelines for animation and virtual try-on.

## Method

X-Oscar follows a progressive Geometry → Texture → Animation pipeline, optimizing SMPL-X vertex offsets, then an albedo map, then refining both under varied poses from VPoser. Two key modules address oversaturation: (1) Adaptive Variational Parameter (AVP) represents the avatar as a Gaussian distribution over vertex offsets and albedo, using the parameters' own standard deviations as perturbation weights so the model learns from easy (low-variance) to hard (high-variance) automatically; (2) Avatar-aware Score Distillation Sampling (ASDS) replaces the standard isotropic Gaussian noise in SDS with a composite noise whose variance scales with the current geometry and appearance standard deviations, making the denoising difficulty adapt to the avatar's learned state and preventing the diffusion model from over-correcting toward saturated outputs.

## Results

In user studies with 52 participants rating 40 prompts on a 1–10 scale, X-Oscar achieves the highest scores across all three criteria: geometry quality (8.85), texture quality (8.91), and text consistency (9.22), outperforming the next-best methods (DreamWaltz at 6.38 for geometry, TADA at 6.95 for texture, and TADA at 7.62 for consistency). On CLIP similarity scores (excluding AvatarCLIP, which uses CLIP as its training signal), X-Oscar attains the best scores on ViT-B/32 (31.70) and ViT-B/16 (31.97), and is second on ViT-L/14 (28.10 vs. TADA's 27.72). On OpenCLIP, X-Oscar ranks second on ViT-B/16 (30.28) and is competitive on the other backbones. Training time is 172 minutes on an RTX 3090, the shortest among mesh-based methods using Stable Diffusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

X-Oscar decisively outperforms all nine baselines on the user-study metrics, with margins of 2.47 over DreamWaltz (6.38) on geometry quality, 1.96 over TADA (6.95) on texture quality, and 1.60 over TADA (7.62) on text consistency. Among text-to-3D methods (DreamFusion, Magic3D, Fantasia3D, ProlificDreamer), the gap is even larger, with X-Oscar scoring 8.85 vs. a maximum of 4.21 (Magic3D) on geometry quality. On CLIP similarity (excluding AvatarCLIP, which is trained with CLIP supervision), X-Oscar leads on ViT-B/32 (31.70 vs. TADA's 31.09) and ViT-B/16 (31.97 vs. DreamWaltz's 31.20), and is narrowly second on ViT-L/14 (28.10 vs. TADA's 27.72). On OpenCLIP, X-Oscar is competitive but not always first: it trails AvatarCLIP (32.77) and DreamFusion (31.57) on ViT-B/32, and is second to AvatarCLIP (31.20) on ViT-B/16. In training time, X-Oscar (172 min) is the fastest mesh-based method using Stable Diffusion, beating TADA (175 min) and AvatarCraft (298 min), though slower than NeRF-based DreamWaltz (105 min) and Gaussian-splatting-based HumanGaussian (69 min).

## Relation to Prior Work

X-Oscar sits at the intersection of two research threads: SDS-based text-to-3D generation (DreamFusion, ProlificDreamer) and SMPL-X-parameterized text-to-avatar methods (TADA, DreamWaltz, AvatarCLIP, HumanGaussian). It departs from prior avatar methods by decomposing the joint optimization into a progressive Geometry → Texture → Animation pipeline, and from standard SDS by replacing the fixed Gaussian noise with an avatar-aware noise term (ASDS) whose variance adapts to the current state of the 3D representation. The Adaptive Variational Parameter (AVP) mechanism, which uses the learned standard deviation of vertex offsets and albedo as the perturbation scale, is a novel twist on variational score distillation (ProlificDreamer) tailored to the oversaturation problem specific to human avatars.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The progressive decomposition and AVP/ASDS modules offer practical, drop-in improvements for anyone working on SDS-based 3D generation who struggles with oversaturation and multi-view inconsistency. However, the method remains tied to the SMPL-X human body prior, limiting applicability to non-human or clothed characters beyond what the mesh can represent, and the ~172-minute training time on a single GPU is still impractical for interactive use. Open questions include whether the adaptive noise scheduling generalizes to other 3D representations (NeRF, Gaussian Splatting) and whether the progressive paradigm can be extended to multi-character or scene-level generation.
