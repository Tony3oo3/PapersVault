## Problem

Existing post-hoc concept-removal methods (INLP, LEACE, RLACE) are overzealous: when spurious and main-task features are correlated, the concept classifier used to identify the spurious subspace inadvertently incorporates main-task directions, so projecting out the spurious subspace also removes vital main-task information. This degrades OOD generalization and produces misleading interpretability results, particularly in the high-correlation regime where spurious features are most likely to be exploited by the DNN.

## Input / Output

The method operates post-hoc on frozen DNN embeddings (ResNet-50 last-layer for Waterbirds/CelebA, BERT [CLS] for MultiNLI), reduced to d=300 or d=100 via PCA for computational efficiency. It requires paired binary labels: a main-task label (e.g., bird type) and a spurious concept label (e.g., background type) for each sample. The output is a set of orthonormal basis vectors spanning the estimated spurious subspace; the transformed embeddings are obtained by projecting onto the orthogonal complement of this subspace, after which a standard linear classifier predicts the main-task label.

## Method

JSE jointly estimates two low-dimensional orthogonal subspaces (spurious and main-task) via a nested iterative procedure. At each step, two logistic regressions are trained simultaneously on the current embedding subspace, subject to an orthogonality constraint between their weight vectors, so that the spurious direction is discouraged from using main-task features and vice versa. The inner loop iteratively projects out estimated main-task directions before re-estimating the spurious direction, while the outer loop projects out confirmed spurious directions before estimating the next one. Two statistical tests (t-statistics on weighted BCE differences) govern the stopping criteria: the first checks whether a candidate direction is significantly predictive of the spurious label, and the second (novel) checks whether it is more predictive of the spurious label than of the main-task label, preventing the method from attributing main-task directions to the spurious subspace. The final transformation projects embeddings onto the orthogonal complement of the estimated spurious subspace, retaining the main-task subspace intact.

## Results

On the Waterbirds dataset, JSE achieves 87.8% worst-group OOD accuracy at the strongest spurious correlation (p_train = 0.9), outperforming INLP (71.8%), LEACE (61.1%), RLACE (57.4%), and ADV (70.2%). On CelebA, JSE reaches 74.4% worst-group accuracy at p_train = 0.9 versus 55.3–59.6% for all baselines. On the Toy dataset (ρ = 0.9), JSE maintains 80.3% worst-group accuracy while INLP drops to 47.1%, LEACE to 51.0%, and RLACE to 52.3%. On MultiNLI, JSE (63.4%) is competitive with LEACE (67.6%) but trails it slightly at the strongest correlation, while still outperforming INLP (48.1%) and RLACE (61.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

JSE decisively outperforms INLP on all four datasets at high spurious correlation: on Waterbirds (87.8% vs. 71.8%), CelebA (74.4% vs. 57.4%), and Toy (80.3% vs. 47.1%), the gap widens as the spurious correlation strengthens, confirming that INLP's over-removal of main-task features becomes more severe. LEACE is the closest competitor, matching or slightly exceeding JSE on MultiNLI (67.6% vs. 63.4% at p_train = 0.9), but JSE dominates on both vision datasets (Waterbirds: 87.8% vs. 61.1%; CelebA: 74.4% vs. 59.6%) and the Toy dataset (80.3% vs. 51.0%). RLACE and ADV consistently underperform JSE by 10–30 percentage points on the vision benchmarks, with RLACE showing the largest degradation (57.4% on Waterbirds at p_train = 0.9). The paper also shows via Grad-CAM that JSE's model relies on bird features rather than background, whereas INLP, LEACE, and RLACE continue to exploit the spurious background cue. On the 'clean BERT' experiment (Section 4.6), only JSE preserves main-task performance after concept removal, while all other methods degrade as spurious correlation increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear-subspace concept-removal lineage (Bolukbasi et al. 2016; INLP by Ravfogel et al. 2020; RLACE by Ravfogel et al. 2022a; LEACE by Belrose et al. 2023) by introducing a key conceptual departure: rather than estimating only the spurious-concept subspace to project out, JSE jointly estimates both the spurious and main-task subspaces under an orthogonality constraint. The individual direction vectors are still obtained via the TCAV-style normalized logistic-regression coefficients (Kim et al. 2018), but the joint optimization and iterative nested projection procedure are new. The paper also positions JSE against adversarial removal (ADV) and instance-reweighting methods (GDRO, JTT), showing it is the first concept-removal method competitive with the latter while retaining interpretability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The over-removal problem—where existing methods strip main-task features alongside spurious ones—is a fundamental barrier to deploying concept-removal in the most relevant settings (strong spurious correlations). JSE's joint estimation with statistical stopping criteria addresses this directly, and the Grad-CAM and regression analyses provide concrete evidence of selective removal. Key open questions include: whether the orthogonality assumption can be relaxed or tested a priori, how the method behaves when concepts are non-linearly encoded (as appears to happen in BERT's [CLS] embeddings), and whether the finite-sample sensitivity at high correlation can be mitigated. The paper also highlights the broader need to disentangle concepts in large language model representations.
