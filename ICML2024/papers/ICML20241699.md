## Problem

Existing Inverse Constrained Reinforcement Learning (ICRL) algorithms rely on online interaction with the environment to infer safety constraints, which is infeasible in safety-critical domains where data collection is dangerous or expensive. Moreover, offline ICRL is inherently ill-posed because the ground-truth constraint is not identifiable from a limited dataset, and the set of superior distributions that define feasible constraints is computationally intractable to construct directly.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline dataset of (state, action, reward) tuples partitioned into expert trajectories and suboptimal data, with reward signals assumed observable. In the HighD driving experiments, the constraint network receives raw RGB images as input via a CNN backbone rather than low-dimensional state vectors. The output is a sparse per-state-action cost function (zero on expert-visited states, positive on constraint-violating regions), a safe control policy extracted via advantage-weighted regression, and the intermediate superior visitation distributions used to drive cost updates.

## Method

ICSDICE reformulates offline ICRL as an alternating two-stage optimization: a forward stage solves a regularized dual CMDP problem (via DICE with chi-square f-divergence) to estimate a superior distribution that exceeds expert reward, and an inverse stage updates the cost function by assigning positive costs to the support of accumulated superior distributions while enforcing zero cost on expert-visited states. The chi-square divergence introduces a clipping mechanism (d* = 0 when δ_V ≤ −2ξ_r) that controls sparsity of the learned constraint, and a discounted visitation distribution replaces the empirical one to provide an unbiased Lagrangian multiplier estimate, particularly in absorbing states. Policy extraction uses advantage-weighted regression with max-clipping on the temporal difference for numerical stability. The algorithm iterates forward/inverse steps until the estimated superior distribution matches the expert distribution, with convergence guaranteed in finite MDPs.

## Results

On MuJoCo environments (100% expert data), ICSDICE achieves near-zero constraint violation (cost 0.00–0.05) while maintaining competitive cumulative rewards (e.g., 3,073 on Ant(Blocked), 1,587 on Walker(LS)). In the HighD driving dataset, ICSDICE achieves a return of 10.7±1.5 with 6.5% cost violation and 0.81 AUC for constraint identification, substantially outperforming OptiDICE-c (return −1.6±3.7, 12% cost, 0.76 AUC). For constraint recovery accuracy (AUC), ICSDICE reaches 0.91–0.98 across MuJoCo tasks, compared to 0.54–0.97 for OptiDICE-c, demonstrating superior cost function identification even when policy performance is comparable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OptiDICE-c (the closest competitor), ICSDICE matches or slightly trails on raw policy reward in some MuJoCo tasks (e.g., 1,340 vs. 1,368 on Ant(LS); 1,017 vs. 1,255 on Hopper(Blocked)) but achieves significantly higher AUC for constraint identification (0.91–0.98 vs. 0.54–0.97), indicating that OptiDICE-c's comparable policy performance comes at the cost of a dense, inaccurate learned constraint. On the HighD driving benchmark, ICSDICE clearly wins: return 10.7 vs. −1.6, cost 6.5% vs. 12%, and AUC 0.81 vs. 0.76. Against SMODICE (offline imitation via state-occupancy matching), ICSDICE achieves substantially lower cost (0.00–0.05 vs. 0.06–0.52) while maintaining comparable or higher rewards, confirming that imitation-only methods cannot enforce safety constraints. Against LS-IQ (inverse Q-learning), ICSDICE dominates on both cost (0.00–0.05 vs. 0.02–0.40) and reward, as LS-IQ was not designed for constraint recovery and produces highly variable, often unsafe policies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Distribution Correction Estimation (DICE) framework (Nachum et al., 2019; Lee et al., 2021; Sikchi et al., 2024) from offline policy optimization to the problem of offline constraint inference. While prior ICRL methods (Malik et al., 2021; Scobee & Sastry, 2020; Gaurav et al., 2023) operate in an online paradigm requiring interactive environment access, ICSDICE reformulates the problem as a two-stage optimization: a forward stage solving a regularized dual CRL problem to estimate 'superior distributions' (those exceeding expert reward), and an inverse stage updating the cost function to penalize those distributions. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of superior distributions as an intermediate object that bridges policy learning and constraint inference, replacing the intractable task of characterizing the full feasible set with an iterative distribution-matching procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ICSDICE addresses a practical gap: in safety-critical domains (autonomous driving, robotics, healthcare), online exploration for constraint learning is infeasible or dangerous, yet prior ICRL methods all require it. The finding that policy performance and constraint accuracy can diverge sharply (OptiDICE-Constraint matches ICSDICE in return but has far worse ROC for cost identification) is practically important, as it means a policy can appear safe while the underlying constraint model is unreliable and non-transferable. Open questions include extending to soft constraints (ε > 0), handling distributional uncertainty in the offline dataset, and establishing convergence rate and sample complexity guarantees beyond the finite-MDP setting.
