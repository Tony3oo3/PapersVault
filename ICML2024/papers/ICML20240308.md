## Problem

Optimization proxies provide near-optimal feasible solutions to parametric optimization problems, but quality guarantees have remained largely empirical. The only prior provable worst-case guarantee (Nellikkath & Chatzivasileiadis, 2021) relies on a bilevel formulation whose single-level reformulation introduces many auxiliary variables and complementarity constraints, and is restricted to convex problems. This paper addresses both limitations by proposing an exact compact formulation that applies to non-convex problems and is substantially more tractable.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained optimization proxy (a ReLU-based neural network followed by differentiable feasibility repair layers), a bounded input parameter domain defined by perturbation ranges on the problem's parameters (e.g., ±u% scaling and noise on load vectors for DC-OPF), and the underlying parametric optimization problem. It produces the worst-case optimality gap over the domain, the adversarial input point achieving it, and primal/dual objective bounds. The verification is cast as a Mixed-Integer Linear Program solved with Gurobi, where the neural network and feasibility layers are encoded via binary variables and linear constraints.

## Method

The core contribution is a compact optimality verification formulation that replaces the inner optimization of the bilevel problem with a simple feasibility constraint y ∈ Y(x), which the paper proves is an exact high-point relaxation (Theorem 4.1). This eliminates KKT conditions and complementarity constraints, naturally supports non-convex lower-level problems, and reduces model size (e.g., 7281→1353 binary variables on the 1354-bus system). As a primal heuristic, the paper introduces a Projected Gradient Attack with Value Function Approximation (PGA-VFA) that uses a piecewise-linear outer approximation of the convex value function—constructed from dual solutions of 50,000 historical instances—to compute gradients without solving nested optimization problems. PGA-VFA is parallelized across multiple starting points and input subregions. The approach is demonstrated on DC-OPF (convex, up to 1354 buses) and the knapsack problem (non-convex, binary decisions), with novel MILP encodings of the hypersimplex projection and sorting/selection feasibility layers.

## Results

The compact formulation reduces binary variables by up to 81% (1354-bus: 1,353 vs. 7,281) and achieves consistent solving-time speedups over the bilevel formulation, reaching up to 209.5% on the 300-bus system with 2% input perturbation. On open (unsolved) instances, the compact formulation finds substantially better primal bounds, with gains up to 626.4% on the 1354-bus system at 20% perturbation (359.72 K$ vs. 49.52 K$). The proposed PGA-VFA heuristic finds near-optimal adversarial solutions in seconds (e.g., 7.09s on 300-bus u=1%) versus 114.79s for the compact MIP to reach the same quality. The compact formulation also enables verification of non-convex knapsack proxies, where the bilevel approach is inapplicable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the bilevel formulation of Nellikkath & Chatzivasileiadis (2021), which reformulates the lower-level optimization via KKT conditions and complementarity constraints. The compact formulation wins on every metric: it reduces binary variables by 26–81% (most dramatic on the 1354-bus system: 1,353 vs. 7,281), achieves consistent speedups of 29–210% on solved instances, and produces dramatically tighter primal bounds on unsolved instances (up to 626% improvement). On the dual side, the compact formulation is slightly better (2.6–14.1% tighter dual bounds). Critically, the compact formulation extends to non-convex problems (demonstrated on knapsack), where the bilevel KKT-based reformulation is entirely inapplicable since KKT conditions are not sufficient for optimality in the non-convex case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Nellikkath & Chatzivasileiadis (2021), who first formalized optimality verification of optimization proxies as a bilevel program and reformulated it via KKT conditions into a single-level MILP. The key departure is the observation that the high-point relaxation (Moore & Bard, 1990) of the bilevel problem is exact (Theorem 4.1), yielding a compact single-level formulation that eliminates complementarity constraints and auxiliary dual variables. This compact formulation also extends the task to non-convex parametric problems, where KKT-based reformulation is inapplicable. The PGA-VFA heuristic further draws on the piecewise-linear outer approximation of convex value functions (Boyd & Vandenberghe, 2004) and connects to adversarial example search in neural network verification, though with larger, structured input perturbations rather than small ℓp-norm balls.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Optimization proxies are being deployed in safety-critical infrastructure (power grids, supply chains) yet have lacked formal worst-case quality guarantees; this paper provides the first tractable exact verification scheme that also covers non-convex problems, filling a critical gap between empirical performance and provable guarantees. The substantial computational savings (up to 209% speedup, 637% better primal bounds on unsolved instances) make verification practical for industrial-scale systems like the 1354-bus Pegase network. Key limitations remain: the approach still relies on MIP solvers that struggle with deep networks and large input domains, and the method is currently limited to ReLU-based architectures. Open questions include coupling with spatial branch-and-bound or α,β-CROWN for scalability, extending to auto-regressive and Transformer-based proxies, and handling nonlinear activations.
