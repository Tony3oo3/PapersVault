## Problem

Existing adversarial training methods for deep metric learning in image retrieval suffer from two overlooked limitations: a weak adversary (simultaneously perturbing all triplet components dilutes the embedding shift per sample) and model collapse (stronger adversaries cause the embedding space to shrink and entangle). Additionally, the standard robustness metric ERS has inconsistencies in similarity measures and ignores initial-state variation, making fair comparison difficult.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241967/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images from fine-grained retrieval datasets (CUB-200-2011, Cars196, SOP) using a pre-trained ResNet-18 backbone with a 512-dimensional embedding head. During training, inputs are triplets (anchor, positive, negative) sampled via semi-hard sampling; during evaluation, a query image is embedded and ranked against a gallery, producing retrieval metrics (R@1, R@2, mAP, NMI) and adversarial resistance scores under eight attack types (CA±, QA±, ES:R, LTM, GTM, GTT).

## Method

CA-TRIDE decouples the adversarial perturbation into two alternating mini-batch phases: Candidate Perturbation (CAP), which perturbs positives and negatives while fixing the anchor, and Anchor Perturbation (ANP), which perturbs only the anchor while fixing candidates. This spatial decoupling doubles the per-sample embedding shift compared to simultaneous perturbation, yielding a stronger adversary. To prevent the resulting model collapse, a novel metric called collapseness (C) is introduced, which applies exponential weighting to anchor-proximity samples to proactively detect impending collapse; the adversarial loss is clipped at C≥0 to cap perturbation strength. A top-rank pair (Δ_TR + L_TR) further reinforces local robustness against recall attacks by targeting the closest half of positives and negatives. The method also proposes a new evaluation metric, Adversarial Resistance Score (ARS), which normalizes attack impact relative to the attack's intended goal rather than raw post-attack results.

## Results

On the three standard image retrieval benchmarks (CUB-200-2011, Cars196, SOP), CA-TRIDE achieves overall ARS scores of 51.6%, 57.2%, and 81.3% respectively, outperforming the prior SOTA method HM (47.2%, 52.9%, 77.7%) by 4–5% and ACT (40.3%, 41.4%, 54.9%) by 11–26%. Under the conventional ERS metric, CA-TRIDE similarly leads with 38.6%, 47.7%, and 62.4% versus HM's 36.0%, 46.1%, and 61.6%. Notably, CA-TRIDE achieves these gains using only 16 PGD steps (half of the 32 used by ACT and HM) while maintaining comparable clean R@1 (e.g., 34.9% on CUB vs. HM's 34.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CA-TRIDE is compared against two prior adversarial-training defenses for deep metric learning: ACT (Zhou et al., 2021b) and HM (Zhou & Patel, 2022). Against ACT, CA-TRIDE achieves a large margin of 11–26% higher overall ARS across all three datasets (e.g., 51.6% vs. 40.3% on CUB), while also recovering most of the clean R@1 that ACT sacrifices (34.9% vs. 27.5% on CUB). Against the stronger HM baseline, CA-TRIDE wins by a consistent 4–5% in overall ARS (51.6% vs. 47.2% on CUB; 57.2% vs. 52.9% on CARS; 81.3% vs. 77.7% on SOP) and by 1.6–2.6% in ERS, all while using only half the PGD steps (16 vs. 32). The one notable exception is the GTM attack, where CA-TRIDE scores lower than HM on CUB (59.0% vs. 78.2%) and CARS (66.9% vs. 76.4%), which the authors attribute to a design conflict between their top-rank pair (prioritizing top-half negatives) and GTM's mechanism of pushing toward the single closest negative. Clean R@1 is essentially matched with HM on CUB (34.9% vs. 34.9%) and CARS (60.7% vs. 60.2%), with a slight 1.5% drop on SOP (48.3% vs. 46.8% — actually a gain), confirming that the robustness gains come at negligible accuracy cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial training paradigm for deep metric learning established by Zhou et al. (2021b, ACT) and Zhou & Patel (2022, Hardness Manipulation), which adapted PGD-based adversarial training from image classification to triplet-based retrieval. It departs from these methods by identifying that simultaneous perturbation of all triplet components (anchors, positives, negatives) inherently weakens the adversary due to geometric dilution of embedding shifts, and by introducing a proactive 'collapseness' metric to replace the reactive hardness signal. The triplet decoupling (TRIDE) mechanism and the collapse-aware optimization are novel contributions layered on top of the existing AT-in-DML framework, while the proposed ARS metric revises the ERS evaluation protocol from prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, plug-and-play defense for DML-based image retrieval that simultaneously improves robustness (4–5% over SOTA) and reduces computational cost by half, making stronger adversarial training more accessible. The collapseness metric offers a general-purpose tool for monitoring model collapse in any triplet-based DML training, not just adversarial settings. Open questions remain around the dataset-dependent attention factor λ (which currently requires manual tuning based on entanglement levels) and the globality-locality trade-off exposed by the GTM attack, where the top-rank pair design conflicts with certain attack mechanisms. The approach is limited to triplet-based DML and ResNet-18-scale models, leaving open whether the geometric insights generalize to larger architectures or non-triplet losses.
