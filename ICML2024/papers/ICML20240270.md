## Problem

Continuous Pareto front approximation in multi-task learning via linear combinations of base networks (PaMaL) requires a full separate network per task, causing parameter counts to scale linearly with the number of tasks and preventing inter-task knowledge sharing. This makes the approach impractical for problems with many tasks (e.g., 20 or 40 objectives), where PaMaL fails to converge and produces poor hypervolume.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240270/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a preference vector α on the m-dimensional simplex (sampled from a Dirichlet distribution during training) and multi-task mini-batch data as input, and produces the corresponding Pareto-optimal shared-bottom network parameters θ(α) = θ₀ + s·Σᵢ αᵢ BᵢAᵢ for each layer. At inference time, any user-specified α yields a point on the learned Pareto front without retraining. The low-rank factorization (rank r ≪ d, k) means each task-specific component contributes only d·r + r·k parameters per layer instead of d·k.

## Method

LORPMAN replaces PaMaL's m full base networks with a single main network θ₀ plus m low-rank matrices BᵢAᵢ per layer, motivated by the observation that PaMaL's base networks develop high cosine similarity during training, making their pairwise differences low-rank. An orthogonal regularization term ‖(WᵀW − I)‖² is applied to the flattened, normalized low-rank matrices to encourage disentanglement and reduce redundancy, with stochastic approximation (sampling 3 tasks) to keep complexity O(1) in m. Training minimizes the expected linear scalarization loss over Dirichlet-sampled preference vectors, augmented with multi-forward regularization for correct PF ordering, and uses a two-phase schedule where the main network is frozen after a set number of epochs to force task-specific learning into the low-rank components. A universal approximation theorem guarantees the structure can approximate any continuous PF mapping to arbitrary precision.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (20 tasks), LORPMAN achieves a hypervolume of 0.887×10⁻², outperforming PaMaL (0.0583×10⁻²) by 15× while using only 8.9% of the parameters (26.4M vs. 296.5M). On CelebA (40 tasks), LORPMAN reaches HV 1.167×10⁻² versus PaMaL's 0.472×10⁻² and COSMOS's 0.783×10⁻², using only 21% of PaMaL's parameters. On smaller-scale datasets (MultiMNIST, Census, UTKFace), LORPMAN consistently achieves the best HV (0.918, 0.656, 0.314 respectively) with fewer parameters than PaMaL. On CityScapes, LORPMAN attains 72.13 mIoU and 92.57 pixel accuracy for segmentation, surpassing all single-solution baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LORPMAN outperforms all continuous PF baselines across every dataset. Against PaMaL (the closest competitor), LORPMAN wins on all six datasets, with the gap widening dramatically at scale: on CIFAR-100, PaMaL's HV collapses to 0.0583×10⁻² due to slow convergence of 20 jointly-trained networks, while LORPMAN reaches 0.887×10⁻². COSMOS and FiLM consistently underperform due to their limited parameter space (e.g., COSMOS HV 0.344×10⁻² vs. LORPMAN 0.887×10⁻² on CIFAR-100). PHN and PHN-HVI are only evaluated on MultiMNIST and Census due to hypernetwork size constraints, where LORPMAN still achieves higher HV (0.918 vs. 0.906) with far fewer parameters (0.046M vs. 2.793M). PHN-Chunking on CelebA (0.663–0.681×10⁻²) also trails LORPMAN (1.167×10⁻²). Against discrete PF methods (EPO, PMTL, MOO-SVGD, PNG, GMOOAR) on MultiMNIST, LORPMAN's continuous PF dominates all discrete solution sets. On CityScapes, LORPMAN (72.13 mIoU) beats the best single-solution method Nash-MTL (71.13 mIoU) and all gradient-manipulation baselines (PCGrad 70.02, CAGrad 69.23, Graddrop 70.07, etc.), though MGDA matches LORPMAN on depth absolute error (0.0141 vs. 0.0135) at the cost of much worse segmentation (66.45 mIoU).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends PaMaL (Dimitriadis et al., 2023), which learns m full base networks whose convex combination yields Pareto-optimal solutions, by replacing those m networks with a single shared main network plus m low-rank adaptation matrices per layer. The low-rank decomposition is motivated by observed high cosine similarity between PaMaL's base networks during training, and draws on the LoRA literature (Hu et al., 2021) for the parameter-efficient structure. Orthogonal regularization, previously applied to individual weight matrices (Xie et al., 2017; Bansal et al., 2018), is repurposed here to enforce disentanglement among the low-rank matrices rather than within a single network. The paper also provides a universal approximation theorem generalizing PaMaL's two-task result to m tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is making continuous Pareto front learning viable for large task counts (20–40 tasks) where PaMaL's parameter count becomes prohibitive and training fails to converge. The 8.9% parameter usage on CIFAR-100 and 21% on CelebA relative to PaMaL, combined with substantially better hypervolume, suggests this low-rank-plus-shared-backbone pattern could become a default architecture for scalable multi-task learning. A noted limitation is the uniform rank across all layers; adaptive or layer-specific rank allocation is left as future work. The approach also assumes a shared-bottom architecture, and its applicability to more complex topologies (e.g., mixture-of-experts or cross-attention sharing) remains unexplored.
