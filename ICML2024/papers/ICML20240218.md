## Problem

Existing dynamic multimodal fusion methods lack theoretical guarantees and tend to fall into suboptimal, unstable fusion because they estimate modality quality heuristically (e.g., via uncertainty scores or learned gating) without a principled link to generalization performance. No prior work had derived, from the generalization error upper bound, the specific covariance conditions that fusion weights must satisfy to provably reduce the bound in a multimodal system.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240218/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts paired multimodal inputs (image+text, RGB+depth, image+audio, or three-view facial images) and produces a final class label via a weighted sum of unimodal predictions. Unusually, the per-modality fusion weight is not a learned scalar or a softmax over logits but a Calibrated Co-Belief computed at inference time from a lightweight confidence predictor that outputs p̂_true (predicted true-class probability) for each modality, combined with a relative calibration factor derived from the softmax output distribution uniformity of the other modalities.

## Method

Starting from the Rademacher-complexity generalization error bound, the paper proves that reducing the bound requires (i) negative covariance between a modality's fusion weight and its own loss (Mono-Covariance) and (ii) positive covariance between that weight and every other modality's loss (Holo-Covariance). Rather than predicting loss directly (which is numerically unstable near convergence), the authors exploit the identity ℓ = −log p_true to substitute the predicted true-class probability p̂_true as a stable proxy, yielding Mono-Confidence (own p̂_true) and Holo-Confidence (ratio of other modalities' losses to total losses, rewritten in p_true form). The sum, Co-Belief, provably satisfies both covariance conditions simultaneously. To handle residual prediction uncertainty in open environments, an asymmetric Relative Calibration multiplies the Co-Belief by a Distribution-Uniformity ratio (DU^m / DU^n) only when the modality is more uncertain than its counterpart, leaving confident modalities unpenalized. The final fusion weight is softmax(Calibrated Co-Belief) applied to unimodal predictions, trained end-to-end with a multi-task loss combining cross-entropy classification and MSE-based p_true prediction.

## Results

On four multimodal classification benchmarks (MVSA, UPMC FOOD101, NYU Depth V2, CREMA-D) with Gaussian noise applied to 50% of modalities, PDF achieves the best average and worst accuracy across all noise levels (ε = 0, 5, 10). At the highest noise (ε=10), PDF reaches 63.09% avg / 60.31% worst on MVSA, 62.83% / 62.03% on FOOD101, 62.56% / 60.25% on NYU Depth V2, and 47.84% / 44.62% on CREMA-D, outperforming the strongest baseline (QMF) by 1.8–5.5 points in average accuracy. The performance gap widens with increasing noise, e.g., on CREMA-D at ε=10 PDF gains 5.45 points over DYNMM in average accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDF outperforms all three dynamic fusion baselines—TMC (Han et al., 2022), QMF (Zhang et al., 2023), and DYNMM (Xue & Marculescu, 2023)—on every dataset and noise level. QMF is the closest competitor, with PDF leading by 0.4–1.8 points in average accuracy at ε=0 and by 1.8–5.5 points at ε=10 (e.g., 63.09 vs. 61.28 on MVSA, 47.84 vs. 41.60 on CREMA-D). DYNMM degrades sharply under noise (e.g., 59.21→56.65 on MVSA from ε=5 to ε=10), while PDF maintains a more stable decline. For the uncertainty-metric comparison in Table 4, the proposed Distribution Uniformity (DU) used in Relative Calibration surpasses Energy (Liu et al., 2020), Entropy (Shannon, 1948), Evidential (Sensoy et al., 2018), and MCP (Hendrycks & Gimpel, 2016) by 0.5–2.4 points in average accuracy on MVSA at ε=10 (63.09 vs. 60.94–61.93). Static baselines (Concat, Late Fusion) are consistently outperformed, with the gap widening substantially under noise, confirming the benefit of dynamic, theory-guided fusion weights.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generalization error bound framework from Rademacher complexity theory (Mohri et al., 2018) to the multimodal fusion setting, deriving a bound that explicitly decomposes into Mono-Covariance (weight vs. own loss) and Holo-Covariance (weight vs. other modalities' losses) terms. It departs from prior dynamic fusion methods (TMC, QMF, DynMM) that estimate modality quality via uncertainty or learned architectures without theoretical guarantees, and specifically contrasts with QMF (Zhang et al., 2023), which also invokes generalization bounds but from a unimodal perspective that overlooks cross-modal interplay. The key methodological departure is transforming loss prediction into true-class probability (p_true) prediction for stability, and introducing a relative calibration via Distribution Uniformity rather than standard uncertainty measures (energy, entropy, evidential).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first provable reduction of the generalization error upper bound for decision-level multimodal fusion through a concrete, implementable weight construction (Co-Belief), addressing a gap where prior dynamic fusion methods operated without theoretical grounding. However, the relative calibration component remains an empirical heuristic without theoretical guarantees, and the confidence predictor is a simple linear module, leaving open questions about whether more principled uncertainty estimation or richer predictor architectures could further tighten the bound. The framework is currently validated on classification tasks with two or three modalities; extension to regression, generation, or higher-order fusion remains unexplored.
