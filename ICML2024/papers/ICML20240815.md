## Problem

Existing polynomial graph filters (ChebNet, BernNet, JacobiConv, etc.) rely on predefined polynomial bases that remain fixed across all graphs, ignoring the varying heterophily degrees that fundamentally determine which signal frequencies are relevant for node classification. This mismatch leads to suboptimal spectral filtering, and the resulting fixed-basis propagation also exacerbates over-smoothing and over-squashing in deep GNNs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240815/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the graph adjacency matrix, a d-dimensional node feature matrix, and an estimated homophily ratio ĥ computed from the training split (validated to be accurate within ~2% of the true ratio even at 10% training data). It produces node class labels via a softmax over an MLP head, and as a secondary output, the learned weight vector w over the UniBasis spectrum reveals the frequency distribution of graph signals, enabling spectral graph explanation. A hyperparameter τ ∈ [0,1] controls the blend between homophily and heterophily bases and is selected per dataset based on its homophily ratio.

## Method

UniBasis integrates two components: the standard homophily basis {x, Px, …, P^K x} and a novel adaptive heterophily basis {u_0, …, u_K} whose pairwise angles are set to θ = (1−h)π/2, directly encoding the graph's heterophily degree. The heterophily basis is constructed iteratively (Algorithm 1) by building an orthonormal sequence via three-term recurrence, then adjusting each new vector's direction so that all pairs maintain the prescribed angle θ, with a closed-form coefficient t_k ensuring the constraint. UniFilter computes z = Σ_k w_k(τP^k x + (1−τ)u_k) and feeds z into an MLP; the convolutional matrix τP^k + (1−τ)P_{θ,k} simultaneously propagates and rotates node features. Theoretically, the rotation component guarantees that Dirichlet energy converges to (1−τ)E(G,X) rather than zero (preventing over-smoothing), and the Jacobian |∂z_u^(k)/∂x_v| is independent of propagation step k (mitigating over-squashing).

## Results

On the six standard node-classification benchmarks, UniFilter achieves the highest accuracy on 5 of 6 datasets in the polynomial-filter comparison (Table 1), with standout gains on heterophily graphs: 75.75% on Chameleon (+1.03% over Specformer) and 67.40% on Squirrel (+2.76% over Specformer). Against model-optimized methods (Table 2), UniFilter leads on all six datasets, e.g., 89.12% on Cora vs. 86.98% for GCN and 73.66% on Chameleon vs. 71.21% for GloGNN++. On three large-scale graphs (Table 8), UniFilter attains the best accuracy on Genius (90.94%) and Ogbn-arxiv (73.70%, +1.38% over ChebNetII), while trailing ChebNetII by 0.40% on Penn94.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniFilter outperforms all 11 polynomial-filter baselines on Cora (89.49% vs. 88.98% for JacobiConv), Pubmed (91.44% vs. 90.87% for EvenNet), Chameleon (75.75% vs. 74.72% for Specformer), and Squirrel (67.40% vs. 64.64% for Specformer). It narrowly trails Specformer on Citeseer (81.39% vs. 81.49%) and OptBasisGNN on Actor (40.84% vs. 42.39%), the two losses the authors acknowledge. Against the nine model-optimized methods (Table 2), UniFilter wins on all six datasets, with the largest margins on Chameleon (+2.45% over GloGNN++) and Squirrel (+2.45% over LINKX). On the three large-scale graphs (Table 8), UniFilter leads on Genius (90.94% vs. 90.47% BernNet) and Ogbn-arxiv (73.70% vs. 72.32% ChebNetII) but falls slightly short of ChebNetII on Penn94 (84.46% vs. 84.86%). The ablation (Figure 3) further shows that the combined UniBasis consistently outperforms using only the homophily basis (HomFilter), only the heterophily basis (HetFilter), or a fixed orthonormal basis (OrtFilter) across the full range of synthetic homophily ratios from 0.13 to 0.81.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the polynomial graph filter lineage (ChebNet, BernNet, JacobiConv, OptBasisGNN, Specformer) by identifying a critical gap: all existing polynomial bases are fixed and ignore the heterophily degree of the underlying graph. Building on the three-term recurrence construction from OptBasisGNN (Guo & Wei, 2023) for orthonormal basis generation, the authors introduce an adaptive heterophily basis whose pairwise angles are set to (1-h)·π/2, then blend it with the standard homophily basis via a hyperparameter τ. The theoretical framing connects to the over-smoothing and over-squashing literature by recasting the heterophily basis as a rotation matrix, yielding clean guarantees that Dirichlet energy remains bounded and Jacobian sensitivity is propagation-step-independent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded answer to why fixed polynomial bases underperform on heterophily graphs and offers a universal basis that adapts to any homophily ratio without re-deriving polynomials. However, the practical gains over strong baselines (Specformer, JacobiConv, GloGNN++) are modest (1–3% on heterophily datasets), and the τ hyperparameter still requires per-dataset tuning, limiting full automation. Open questions include whether τ can be learned end-to-end, how the approach scales to graphs with millions of nodes, and whether the rotation-matrix interpretation generalizes to multi-layer or attention-based GNN architectures beyond single-step polynomial filters.
