## Problem

Non-greedy layer-wise local learning trains each module with an isolated local error, but the reconciliation among these local errors has never been rigorously studied. The paper identifies that when the local gradient of a layer w.r.t. its input is discordant with the local gradient of the previous layer w.r.t. its output, convergence of the final-layer loss cannot be guaranteed — a fundamental defect that global back-propagation is naturally immune to.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) and outputs class labels. The network is partitioned into K gradient-isolated local modules; each module receives the detached output of the previous module as input and is trained with its own local classifier head. In the BP-free variant, the classifier is a fixed equiangular tight frame (ETF) matrix rather than a learnable head, so no back-propagation occurs within any module.

## Method

The paper derives a convergence bound for a two-layer model showing that the last-layer loss reduction per iteration is bounded by a term proportional to the squared distance between the local gradient of the second layer w.r.t. its input and the local gradient of the first layer w.r.t. its output (the 'discordance' ε). To remedy this, Successive Gradient Reconciliation (SGR) stores the gradient δx_{k-1}^{(L_{k-1})} after updating layer k−1, then at the update of layer k adds an MSE regularizer L_k^{SGR} = ‖δx_{k-1}^{(L_k)} − δx_{k-1}^{(L_{k-1})}‖² to the local classification loss, steering θ_k so that the input change induced by the previous layer also reduces L_k. The process is applied layer-by-layer in a forward sweep without breaking gradient isolation or introducing any learnable parameters. A proposition shows that when L_k^{SGR}=0 for all layers, local updates become exactly equivalent to global BP. The method is integrated into both local-BP training (with multi-layer auxiliary classifiers) and BP-free training (with fixed ETF classifiers), achieving competitive accuracy with global BP while saving over 40% memory on CNN and Transformer architectures.

## Results

On ImageNet, SGR achieves competitive top-1 accuracy with global BP while saving 28–46% memory: ResNet-101 (K=3) reaches 77.02% (vs. 77.97% BP) at 18.18 GB (↓42.6%), ViT-small (K=3) reaches 78.65% (vs. 79.40% BP) at 11.73 GB (↓43.3%), and Swin-small (K=3) reaches 81.64% (vs. 83.02% BP) at 22.83 GB (↓46.4%). On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-32 (K=2), SGR achieves 92.91% top-1, slightly exceeding global BP (92.82%), and on CIFAR-100 (K=2) reaches 74.63% (vs. 74.78% BP). In the BP-free setting on CIFAR-10, SGR attains 72.40% with PlainNet, outperforming PEPITA (56.33%) and DRTP (50.53%) by over 16%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against global BP, SGR closes most of the accuracy gap (≤1.5% on Transformers, ≤1% on ResNets) while saving 28–46% memory, making it the strongest local-learning alternative tested. Against InfoPro (Wang et al., 2021), SGR consistently achieves higher top-1 accuracy (e.g., 78.65% vs. 78.15% on ViT-small; 80.05% vs. 79.38% on Swin-tiny) and saves substantially more memory (43.3% vs. 27.7% on ViT-small; 40.9% vs. 12.9% on Swin-tiny). On CIFAR-10/100 with ResNet-32, SGR outperforms Greedy layer-wise training (Belilovsky et al., 2019) by up to 12.85% (K=16, CIFAR-10: 85.65% vs. 76.96%), DGL (Belilovsky et al., 2020) by up to 1.30% (K=2, CIFAR-10: 92.91% vs. 91.05%), and InfoPro by up to 1.17% (K=2, CIFAR-10: 92.91% vs. 91.74%). In the BP-free setting, SGR (72.40% CIFAR-10, 49.41% CIFAR-100) dramatically surpasses DRTP (50.53%/20.14%), PEPITA (56.33%/27.56%), and FA (57.51%/27.15%), confirming that gradient reconciliation is the key missing ingredient in prior BP-free methods. In self-supervised learning (Barlow Twins, ResNet-50), SGR matches Siddiqui et al. (2023) in accuracy (70.30% vs. 70.48%) while using 51% less memory (34.86 GB vs. 70.53 GB).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the non-greedy layer-wise local learning framework established by Belilovsky et al. (2019) and Wang et al. (2021, InfoPro) by identifying a previously unaddressed defect: the discordance between neighboring local gradients that impedes convergence. While InfoPro adds a reconstruction loss to each local module and Jaderberg et al. (2017) learns synthetic gradients using the global BP signal (breaking gradient isolation), SGR introduces a parameter-free MSE regularizer between the input-gradient of the current layer's loss and the stored output-gradient of the previous layer's loss. For the BP-free variant, the paper builds on DRTP (Frenkel et al., 2021) and ETF classifier structures (Yang et al., 2022a), replacing random projection matrices with equiangular tight frames to better induce class separability at each local layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical convergence bound (Theorem 3.3) provides the first rigorous explanation for why non-greedy local learning degrades with depth, framing the issue as a gradient reconciliation problem rather than an inherent limitation of local errors. Practically, the >40% memory savings on ImageNet for both CNN and Transformer architectures while maintaining within ~1.5% of global BP accuracy makes local training a viable alternative for memory-constrained settings. Open questions include whether SGR transfers to finetuning pretrained large models (the authors explicitly flag this as future work), whether the remaining gap to global BP can be closed further, and whether the method's training-time overhead (longer than global BP due to the second-order gradient computation) limits its adoption at scale.
