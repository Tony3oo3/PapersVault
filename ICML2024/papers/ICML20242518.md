## Problem

Existing trust-region on-policy RL algorithms such as TRPO guarantee monotonic improvement only in expected performance, leaving worst-case performance outcomes uncontrolled. This is critical in safety-sensitive deployments (autonomous driving, robot manipulation) where even rare poor performance samples can cause catastrophic failures. The paper addresses the unexplored problem of guaranteeing monotonic improvement of the lower probability bound of performance with high confidence within a single fixed environment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state observations (continuous state vectors for Mujoco/GUARD/Gymnasium Robotics tasks, or RAM observations for Atari) and outputs actions from a parameterized policy π_θ. The key internal quantity is the performance sample R_π(s_0) — the discounted return from a given initial state — whose distribution over s_0 ~ μ is decomposed into a MeanVariance component (expected within-state variance) and a VarianceMean component (between-state variance of expected returns). The probability factor k and the trust-region step size δ are the primary hyperparameters controlling the trade-off between exploration and worst-case protection.

## Method

APO introduces the objective max_π J(π) − k·V(π), where Selberg's inequality guarantees that B_k(π) = J(π) − k·V(π) is a lower probability bound of performance with confidence p_k^ψ = 1 − 1/(k²ψ+1). The variance V(π) is decomposed into MeanVariance (expected variance of returns over start states) and VarianceMean (variance of expected returns over start states), each bounded via KL-divergence-based surrogate terms involving the advantage function, the variance of action values Ω_π, and a bounded term |H(s,a,s')|_max. The resulting optimization (Eq. 4) maximizes a lower bound on J(π) minus upper bounds on both variance components, subject to a KL trust-region constraint, and Theorem 1 proves monotonic non-decrease of B_k at every iteration. The practical variant PAPO replaces the explicit trust-region constraint with early stopping on mean KL divergence (analogous to PPO's clipping), enabling multiple gradient steps per update and yielding substantial gains on high-dimensional continuous tasks like Humanoid and HandReach.

## Results

Across 62 Atari games, APO achieves the highest expected reward in 29/62 games (last 100 epochs) and 26/62 games (all epochs), compared to PPO (17 and 22), TRPO (12 and 10), and A2C (3 and 3). On the 12 GUARD continuous control tasks, APO achieves the highest expected reward in 9/12 tasks for both all-epoch and last-20-epoch metrics, versus PPO (3 and 2), TRPO (0 and 1), and A2C (0 and 0). For worst-case performance, APO leads in 27/62 Atari games (last 100 epochs) and 9/12 GUARD tasks (last 20 epochs), substantially outperforming all baselines. PAPO (the clipped variant) further improves over APO on high-dimensional Mujoco/Gymnasium tasks (Humanoid, HandReach, HandManipulate).

## Baselines & Comparisons

APO dominates TRPO across nearly all metrics: on GUARD, APO wins 9/12 tasks for expected performance (vs. TRPO's 0–1), and 8–9/12 for worst-case (vs. TRPO's 1–2). Against PPO, APO wins 29 vs. 17 Atari games (last 100 epochs) for expected performance and 9 vs. 2 GUARD tasks, though PPO remains competitive on Atari worst-case (24 vs. 26 all-epoch). A2C is consistently the weakest baseline, winning at most 3 Atari games and 0–1 GUARD tasks. On continuous tasks, APO also outperforms AlphaPPO, ESPO, and V-MPO in convergence speed and final reward, with PPO-family methods (including PPO, ESPO) showing more erratic behavior on complex PUSH and CHASE tasks. PAPO (the early-stopping variant) further surpasses APO on super-high-dimensional Mujoco/Gymnasium tasks (HumanoidStandup, HandReach), where trust-region methods struggle, while maintaining comparable computational cost to PPO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the trust-region policy optimization lineage (TRPO, PPO) by adding a variance-penalty term to the surrogate objective, grounded in Selberg's inequality to guarantee monotonic improvement of the lower probability bound of performance rather than merely expected performance. It explicitly positions itself apart from distributional RL (which models full return distributions without worst-case guarantees), risk-sensitive RL (which uses Lagrangian formulations for cost constraints), and robust RL (which addresses model discrepancies across environments). The policy improvement lower bound from CPO (Achiam et al., 2017) is directly reused as a component of the APO surrogate, and PAPO inherits the early-stopping mechanism from PPO's clipped surrogate objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The guarantee of monotonic improvement in worst-case performance with high confidence addresses a genuine gap for safety-critical deployments (autonomous driving, robot manipulation) where occasional catastrophic episodes are unacceptable. The practical finding that APO/PAPO achieves this with essentially the same computational footprint as TRPO (less than 5% additional wall-clock time) makes it a viable drop-in replacement. Key limitations include the on-policy requirement (poor sample efficiency for costly environments) and the need to tune the probability factor k, where both too-small and too-large values degrade performance. Open questions include whether the trust-region variance-penalty framework extends to off-policy or model-based settings, and whether the theoretical guarantees hold under the practical approximations (treating H_max as a hyperparameter, using averages instead of maxima).
