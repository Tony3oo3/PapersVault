## Problem

Linear bandit algorithms require a priori knowledge of the noise level, and their performance degrades when the specified noise parameter is much larger than the actual noise. For bounded noise, the prior variance-adaptive algorithm SAVE achieves optimal regret but relies on the numerically poor SupLinRel-style (wasting samples across disjoint buckets) and assumes the conditional variance is fixed deterministically before the game starts, severely limiting practical applicability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240911/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithms receive at each round an arm set X_t ⊂ {x ∈ R^d : ||x||₂ ≤ 1}, a norm bound S on ||θ*||, and a noise parameter (σ₀² for sub-Gaussian or R for bounded noise); LOFAV additionally requires the time horizon n and number of levels L = ⌈½ log₂(n/d)⌉. The output is an arm selection via optimistic maximization over the confidence set, yielding a confidence ellipsoid (or intersection of L ellipsoids) and a cumulative regret guarantee. In the Bayesian optimization application, the continuous search space is discretized into 512 arms mapped through 128-dimensional random Fourier features, and the output is the selected point or neural architecture.

## Method

Both confidence sets are derived from the FTRL regret equality (Orabona, 2023, Lemma 7.1), which links the estimation error to an online variance estimator A_t = Σ f_s(θ̂_{s-1})·D_s². For LOSAN, the key insight is to retain A_t in the confidence set rather than bounding it loosely, combined with a weighted ridge regression estimator (weights w_t = 1 ∧ 1/||x_t||_{Σ_{t-1}^{-1}} ensuring D_t² ≤ ½) so that the confidence width scales as √(dσ*² + σ₀²) instead of √(dσ₀²), yielding a √d regret improvement when σ*² ≪ σ₀². For LOFAV, the confidence set is an intersection of L ellipsoids centered at secondary estimators θ̄_{t,ℓ} (minimizing an augmented loss K_{t,ℓ}), with an exponential cover over ρ_ℓ = 2^{-ℓ} to adapt to arbitrary conditional variances without the fixed-variance assumption of SAVE. The regret analysis for LOFAV uses a peeling-based argument with the elliptical potential count lemma to handle the absence of explicit arm stratification, achieving the optimal Õ(d√(R² + Σσ_t²)) regret with OFUL-level computational complexity.

## Results

Theoretically, LOSAN achieves a regret bound of Õ(σ₀√(dn) + σ*d√n), improving upon the state-of-the-art Õ(σ₀d√n) by a factor of √d when the true noise σ*² is much smaller than the specified σ₀². LOFAV matches the optimal variance-adaptive regret bound Õ(d√(R² + Σσ_t²)) of SAVE while using an optimistic approach with all samples rather than a small subset. In the confidence set comparison (Figure 1, n=500,000, d=2, L=9, σ_t²=0.1), the proposed method yields an upper confidence bound of 1.01 on the mean reward versus 7.66 for Zhao et al. (2023a) and 1.05 for SNCS. Empirically, LOSAN and LOFAV show lower cumulative regret than OFUL in synthetic experiments and better or comparable simple regret in Bayesian optimization benchmarks (Beale, Branin, Three-Hump Camel, Zakharov 4D) and NATS-Bench (CIFAR-100, ImageNet-16 120).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OFUL (Abbasi-Yadkori et al., 2011), both LOSAN and LOFAV consistently achieve lower cumulative regret in synthetic experiments (Figure 2) and lower simple regret across all four benchmark functions in Bayesian optimization (Figures 3–4), with the gap most pronounced when the noise is over-specified. Against GP-UCB (Srinivas et al., 2010) and GP-EI (Jones et al., 1998) with linear-kernel Gaussian process regression, the proposed methods show better or comparable simple regret on the benchmark functions, though the advantage is modest. On NATS-Bench (CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-16 120, Figure 5), no single method dominates: LOSAN shows comparable results to OFUL and the discrete Bayesian optimization strategy of Garrido-Merchán & Hernández-Lobato (2020) for sub-Gaussian noise, while LOFAV is competitive under bounded noise. The key practical advantage over SAVE (Zhao et al., 2023a) is numerical tightness — the confidence set is dramatically narrower (1.01 vs. 7.66 in Figure 1) — and the removal of the restrictive assumption that conditional variance is fixed deterministically before the game starts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the optimistic linear bandit framework of OFUL (Abbasi-Yadkori et al., 2011) and the variance-adaptive SAVE algorithm (Zhao et al., 2023a), but departs from both in critical ways. For the sub-Gaussian noise setting, it leverages the FTRL regret equality (Orabona, 2023) directly—keeping the online variance estimator term A_t unbounded rather than loosely upper-bounding it as in Dekel et al. (2012) or Jun et al. (2017)—to obtain a confidence set whose width scales with the true noise σ*² rather than the over-specified σ₀². For the bounded noise setting, it converts the SupLinRel-style SAVE algorithm (which uses disjoint sample buckets and assumes variance is fixed before the game) into a fully optimistic algorithm that uses all samples and adapts to arbitrary conditional variances, employing the peeling-based regret analysis and elliptical potential count lemma from Kim et al. (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The √d regret improvement when the sub-Gaussian parameter is over-specified is practically significant for high-dimensional linear bandits where noise levels are typically specified conservatively. LOFAV is positioned as the first practical (optimistic-style, polynomial-time) variance-adaptive linear bandit algorithm, removing both the SupLinRel sample-wastefulness and the restrictive deterministic-variance assumption of SAVE. Open questions include extending variance-adaptive worst-case bounds to more general hypothesis classes, finding batch counterparts to the sequential weights, and developing a Thompson sampling variant with O(d|X_t|) per-round complexity. The Bayesian optimization experiments on NATS-Bench suggest practical utility, though no single method dominates across all benchmarks.
