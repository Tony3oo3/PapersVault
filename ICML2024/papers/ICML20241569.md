## Problem

Most variance reduction methods for finite-sum non-convex optimization require periodic full gradient computations, which become a severe bottleneck in distributed and federated settings due to synchronization costs. Existing single-loop alternatives (e.g., ZeroSARAH) achieve optimal gradient complexity but lack the versatility of full-gradient methods: they cannot provably find second-order stationary points, do not achieve exponential convergence under the PL condition, and cannot exploit Hessian heterogeneity for further speedup. The paper asks whether a single-loop estimator can simultaneously deliver all four of these properties.

## Input / Output

SILVER takes a finite-sum objective {f_i}_{i=1}^n, an initial point x^0, a minibatch size b, step size η, and a noise radius r, and maintains O(nd) memory by storing a per-component auxiliary variable y_i^t for each f_i. It outputs ε-first-order stationary points in expectation, (ε,δ)-second-order stationary points with high probability (by adding small noise of radius r), and ε-solutions under the PL condition in expectation. The federated extension FL-SILVER additionally takes the number of clients P, client sample size p, and local update steps K, and requires each client to store one auxiliary variable; the communication complexity is O(P + ζ√P/ε²) for first-order and O(Δ(L/K + ζ)(1/ε² + ρ²/δ⁴)) for second-order.

## Method

SILVER's core mechanism decomposes SAGA's approximation target into a sum of adjacent-step gradient differences ∇f_i(x^s) − ∇f_i(x^{s−1}), mirroring SARAH's telescoping structure, but implements this via per-component auxiliary variables y_i^t that are updated in O(bd) time using only minibatch gradients. The key analytical result (Lemma 1) shows that the estimator error from each past step decays exponentially with factor (1 − b/4n), preventing the error accumulation that plagues SAGA while avoiding the full-gradient refreshes that SARAH requires. This yields the optimal O(n + ζ√n/ε²) gradient complexity with explicit Hessian-heterogeneity dependence, matching a matching lower bound. For FL-SILVER, SILVER operates at the server level to control client sampling error via a SARAH-type recursive estimator, while each client runs a local SARAH-type update to correct local-update drift; adding small noise to the iterate enables saddle-point escape without degrading communication rounds.

## Results

SILVER achieves the optimal gradient complexity of O(n + ζ√n/ε²) for finding ε-first-order stationary points in nonconvex finite-sum optimization, matching SARAH and SPIDER while being the first single-loop method to do so. It is also the first single-loop algorithm to provably find (ε,δ)-second-order stationary points (with complexity Õ(n + LΔ(1/ε² + ρ²/δ⁴)b)) and to achieve exponential convergence under the PL condition. For federated learning, FL-SILVER achieves the best communication rounds of O((1/K + ζ)(1/ε²)) while allowing client sampling with p ≳ √P, yielding a communication complexity of Õ(P + ζ√P/ε²) that is strictly better than BVR-L-SGD. On EMNIST, FL-SILVER achieves the smallest gradient norm and locally linear convergence, performing comparably to BVR-L-SGD (its full-participation special case) while being ~10× more communication-efficient.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAGA (the prior single-loop baseline), SILVER achieves the optimal O(n + ζ√n/ε²) gradient complexity versus SAGA's suboptimal O(n + Ln^(2/3)/ε²), confirmed empirically by Figure 1 showing SILVER's estimator discrepancy is close to SARAH's and far below SAGA's. Against SARAH, SILVER matches its optimal complexity and versatility (SOSP, PL convergence, heterogeneity speedup) while eliminating periodic full-gradient computations, making it ~10% faster in practice (Figure 6). Against ZeroSARAH (the only prior single-loop method with optimal complexity), SILVER uniquely provides second-order optimality, exponential PL convergence, and explicit ζ-dependent speedup—properties ZeroSARAH lacks entirely. In federated learning, FL-SILVER matches BVR-L-SGD's best communication rounds O((1/K+ζ)/ε²) while allowing client sampling (p ≳ √P), improving communication complexity from O(P/ε²) to Õ(P + ζ√P/ε²); it also matches BVR-L-PSGD's SOSP rounds while permitting sampling, unlike BVR-L-PSGD which requires full participation. Against MimeMVR, FL-SILVER avoids the additional intra-client Hessian heterogeneity and σ_c assumptions, and against FedAvg/SCAFFOLD, FL-SILVER achieves locally linear convergence (Figure 3) that the baselines do not exhibit.

## Relation to Prior Work

SILVER is constructed as a deliberate fusion of SAGA's single-loop structure (no periodic full gradient computation) and SARAH's recursive estimator design (optimal complexity and versatility). Prior single-loop methods like ZeroSARAH achieved optimal gradient complexity but could not simultaneously provide second-order optimality, exponential convergence under the PL condition, or heterogeneity-dependent speedup; the paper shows this is a structural limitation of ZeroSARAH's estimator, not merely an analytical gap. On the federated learning side, FL-SILVER extends BVR-L-SGD (which requires full client participation) and MimeMVR (which requires increasingly large client samples as heterogeneity decreases) by enabling client sampling at p ≈ √P while preserving the best communication rounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a fundamental tension in variance reduction: single-loop methods avoid the synchronization bottleneck of periodic full gradients in distributed settings, but previously could not match the versatility of full-gradient methods. By achieving all four desiderata in a single-loop framework, SILVER provides a new base for designing communication-efficient FL algorithms that handle client sampling, saddle-point avoidance, and local convexity simultaneously. Open questions include combining SILVER with gradient compression (which the authors note operates in an orthogonal direction), the storage cost of per-component auxiliary variables in very large-scale settings, and whether the theoretical advantages translate to gains on larger, more realistic FL workloads beyond the small-scale EMNIST experiments presented here.
