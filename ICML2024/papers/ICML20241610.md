## Problem

SignSGD with majority voting (signSGD-MV) reduces communication costs in distributed learning through one-bit gradient quantization, but its convergence rate degrades significantly as the number of adversarial workers increases. The paper asks whether the convergence rate can remain invariant to the number of adversarial workers, and shows that it can—provided honest workers outnumber adversarial ones—by exploiting rather than discarding the gradient information sent by attackers.

## Input / Output

Each of M workers transmits a one-bit sign vector of length N (the sign of its locally computed stochastic gradient) per iteration, and the server broadcasts a single one-bit sign vector back to all workers for the model update. The unusual aspect is that the server does not discard compromised workers' signs; instead, it assigns them negative LLR weights so their flipped signs actively contribute to the correct decoding. Communication cost per worker per iteration is 2N bits (N uplink + N downlink), roughly 30× less than full-precision SGD with Multi-Krum.

## Method

signSGD-FD replaces the simple majority-vote aggregation of signSGD-MV with a weighted majority voting (WMV) decoder whose weights are log-likelihood ratios (LLRs) estimated online. The server models each worker's one-bit sign as a transmission over a binary symmetric channel (BSC) and estimates each worker's cross-over probability by counting sign disagreements with the aggregated sign over past iterations. These estimated probabilities yield LLR weights that are updated recursively; when a worker's estimated error probability exceeds 1/2 (indicating an adversarial worker under sign-flip attacks), its LLR weight automatically becomes negative, so its flipped signs are used constructively in the weighted sum. The paper proves a unified convergence bound showing that under sign-inversion attacks (r=1), the decoding error exponent is identical to the no-attack case, and under stochastic sign-flip attacks (r<1), the effective worker count degrades gracefully from M to M−L rather than M−2rL as in signSGD-MV.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 with M=15 workers and L=6 compromised workers under SIA (r=1), signSGD-FD maintains test accuracy nearly identical to the no-attack case, while signSGD-MV degrades significantly as L increases. Under SSFA with r=1/2, signSGD-FD still outperforms signSGD-MV but both algorithms degrade gradually with increasing L. In terms of communication efficiency, signSGD-type algorithms reduce total communication costs by approximately 30× compared to Multi-Krum on SGD, and by 10× compared to Multi-Krum on Top-K SGD, while achieving equal or superior test accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against signSGD-MV (Bernstein et al., 2018), signSGD-FD clearly wins under SIA (r=1): signSGD-FD maintains near-attack-free accuracy for L < M/2, while signSGD-MV's accuracy drops sharply as L grows; under SSFA (r=1/2), signSGD-FD still achieves higher accuracy but both degrade. Against Election-signSGD (Sohn et al., 2020), Sto-signSGD (Jin et al., 2020), and Noisy signSGD (Chen et al., 2020), signSGD-FD achieves the highest test accuracy in Figure 3(a) under SIA with L=6, and is notably less sensitive to hyperparameter tuning. The key differentiator from Sto-signSGD is that signSGD-FD assigns negative LLR weights to compromised workers (exploiting their gradient information) rather than zeroing them out. Against Multi-Krum on full-precision SGD (Blanchard et al., 2017), signSGD-FD matches or exceeds test accuracy while requiring ~30× less communication; against Multi-Krum on Top-K SGD (Stich et al., 2018), the gap narrows to ~10× in communication savings. Under Gaussian Byzantine attacks (Figure 3b), signSGD-MV surprisingly matches signSGD-FD in accuracy, suggesting the advantage of federated defense is most pronounced under sign-flipping attacks rather than magnitude-based attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends signSGD-MV (Bernstein et al., 2018), the standard one-bit gradient quantization scheme with majority voting, by replacing uniform voting with a weighted majority voting (WMV) decoder derived from the log-likelihood ratio framework for parallel binary symmetric channels (Jeon et al., 2018). It departs from the dominant Byzantine-robust aggregation paradigm (coordinate-wise median, geometric median, center clipping) that seeks to exclude or downweight adversarial workers, instead proposing to *harness* their gradient signs with dynamically learned (potentially negative) LLR weights. The coding-theoretical reinterpretation of signSGD as M parallel BSCs is the conceptual bridge that makes this repurposing of adversarial information possible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The counter-intuitive theoretical guarantee—that convergence rate is invariant to the number of sign-inverting adversarial workers as long as honest workers outnumber them—challenges the field's default assumption that Byzantine information must be discarded. Practically, this means signSGD-FD can maintain communication efficiency (one bit per coordinate) while achieving robustness without the overhead of worker scoring or exclusion mechanisms used by Multi-Krum or median-based methods. Limitations include the requirement that honest workers strictly outnumber adversarial ones, the worst-case degradation at r=1/2 (random sign flipping), and the current restriction to sign-flipping attack models; open questions include whether the federated defense principle extends to more sophisticated attacks (backdoor, data poisoning) and to asynchronous or heterogeneous-data settings.
