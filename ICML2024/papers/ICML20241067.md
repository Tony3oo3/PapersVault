## Problem

Supervised matrix factorization (SMF) jointly performs low-dimensional feature extraction and classification, but its training involves a non-convex, factor-wise constrained optimization problem with three or four parameter blocks whose landscape was largely uncharacterized. This lack of understanding limited both the theoretical guarantees available for iterative solvers and the ability to determine how much regularization is needed for robust parameter estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241067/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-dimensional data matrix X ∈ R^{p×n}, optional low-dimensional auxiliary covariates X_aux ∈ R^{q×n}, and class labels y ∈ {0,…,κ}^{1×n}, with each factor constrained to a closed convex compact set (typically the bounded nonnegative orthant). It produces a factor matrix W ∈ R^{p×r}, a code matrix H ∈ R^{r×n}, and classification coefficients β ∈ R^{r×κ} and Γ ∈ R^{q×κ}, simultaneously yielding a rank-r reconstruction of X and a logistic/multinomial classifier. A tuning parameter ξ controls the trade-off between reconstruction fidelity and classification accuracy, and the two SMF variants (SMF-W and SMF-H) differ in whether the classifier operates on the filtered features W^T x_i or the code h_i.

## Method

The paper explicitly computes the 4×4 block Hessian of the SMF objective and derives eigenvalue bounds on its diagonal blocks, which naturally yield a block coordinate descent algorithm with adaptive step sizes and an O(ε^{-1}(log ε^{-1})²) iteration complexity for reaching an ε-stationary point. A key insight is that minimum L2-regularization is needed on only one factor (W in the high-dimensional regime p≫n, or H in the large-sample regime n≫p) to restore local strong convexity, enabling a local estimation guarantee of O(1/√n) for the corresponding factor under a generative SMF model. For practical GPU acceleration, the authors propose a neural implementation consisting of two coupled two-layer networks—a reconstructor (W, H with MSE loss) and a classifier (W, β with cross-entropy loss)—that share the first-layer weight W and synchronize it via a convex combination after each backpropagation step, approximating the BCD update in a single forward-backward pass.

## Results

On the CuMiDa cancer microarray datasets, SMF-W(BCD) achieves the best breast cancer classification accuracy of 0.924 (±0.01), outperforming all other methods including SMF-W(LPGD) (0.894), FFNN (0.890), and CNN (0.854). On pancreatic cancer, SMF-H(LPGD) leads at 0.885 (±0.07), while SMF-W(BCD) achieves 0.869 (±0.02), both substantially above PCA-LR (0.747) and SVM (0.746). The BCD algorithm converges to an ε-stationary point within O(ε⁻¹(log ε⁻¹)²) iterations, verified empirically on MNIST and EMSCAD datasets. On the EMSCAD job-posting dataset, SMF-W with nonnegative constraints achieves the best F-score for fake-posting detection by tilting learned topics toward the 5% minority class.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the LPGD method of Lee et al. (2023), SMF-W(BCD) ties on pancreatic cancer (0.869 vs 0.869) but clearly wins on breast cancer (0.924 vs 0.894, a 3-point gap); SMF-H(LPGD) edges out SMF-W(BCD) on pancreatic (0.885 vs 0.869) but loses on breast (0.875 vs 0.924). Against NMF (Lee & Seung, 2000) and the GSM neural topic model (Miao et al., 2017) on the EMSCAD job-posting dataset, SMF-W with nonnegative constraints achieves the best F-score for fake-posting detection, while both unsupervised baselines learn topics skewed toward the 95% majority class and yield poor classification. The Pareto analysis (Figure 4) shows SMF-W dominates the reconstruction-accuracy trade-off frontier, outperforming both pure logistic regression and NMF+LR pipelines across all tested ξ values. The neural GPU implementation of SMF-W achieves competitive accuracy (0.854/0.881) with significantly faster wall-clock training on large datasets compared to the CPU-based BCD loop.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Lee et al. (2023), which reformulated SMF as a low-rank matrix estimation problem via a 'double-lifting' trick and showed exponential convergence of projected gradient descent under well-conditioning. However, that approach cannot handle factor-wise constraints (e.g., nonnegativity), so this paper instead performs a direct local landscape analysis of the original 4-block constrained problem, computing the full 4×4 Hessian structure and deriving minimum L2-regularization for local strong convexity. The BCD algorithm is grounded in the block majorization-minimization framework of Lyu & Li (2023), while the local consistency guarantee extends the non-concave penalized MLE theory of Fan & Li (2001) to the non-identifiable SMF setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous local landscape characterization of SMF, filling a notable gap given that even the 2-block NMF landscape was not fully understood until recently. The practical BCD algorithm with adaptive step sizes and the GPU-friendly neural implementation make SMF more accessible to applied ML practitioners, and the cancer classification and topic modeling experiments demonstrate competitive performance against deep learning baselines. Key limitations include the requirement for convex (not Grassmannian) constraints, bounded activations, and the fact that local consistency is established for only one factor at a time depending on the regime. Open questions include whether the landscape analysis extends to non-convex constraints or to settings where the rank r is unknown.
