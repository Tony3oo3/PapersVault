## Problem

Quantum state classification via machine learning has relied exclusively on fully supervised training, requiring extensive labeled measurement data whose acquisition cost grows exponentially with system size. Semi-supervised learning, which leverages abundant unlabeled data alongside sparse labels, had not been applied to quantum measurement data, leaving a critical gap for practical quantum computing where labeling is prohibitively expensive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241952/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes M random single-qubit Pauli measurements (each specifying a basis from {X, Y, Z} and a binary outcome) on an L-qubit state, converted into a shadow embedding matrix of shape M×8L by concatenating the real and imaginary parts of per-qubit density matrices. The output is a discrete class label (e.g., 6 classes defined by coupling strength phase × evolution time for the Heisenberg model, or binary image class for VQC). A key constraint is that the M measurements are inherently unordered, so the model must be permutation-invariant along the measurement axis.

## Method

SSL4Q introduces two architectural components: (1) a shadow embedding that encodes both the sampled Pauli operator and its binary outcome into a 2×2 density matrix via ρ_i = 3|ψ_i⟩⟨ψ_i| − I, then flattens real and imaginary parts into an 8-element vector per qubit, preserving operator information that prior methods discard; and (2) a Permutation Equivalence Attention (PEA) layer, a multi-head transformer decoder without positional encoding or dropout, followed by average pooling to enforce permutation invariance over the M measurements. Training follows the Mean Teacher semi-supervised paradigm: a student model is optimized with a combined supervised cross-entropy loss (on the labeled fraction) and an L2 consistency loss between student and teacher predictions on Gaussian-noise-augmented inputs, while the teacher is maintained as an exponential moving average of the student. The consistency weight λ is ramped up exponentially over the first 10 epochs to stabilize early training before unlabeled data dominates the gradient signal.

## Results

On the Heisenberg model (η=0.9, only 10% labeled), SSL4Q achieves 71.97% accuracy at M=16, L=20 and 93.83% at M=128, L=20, consistently outperforming all baselines across all system sizes (L=20–50) and measurement counts (M=16–128). On the VQC model (MNIST digits 0/1, M=512), SSL4Q reaches 99.20–99.24% accuracy across mask rates η=0.6–0.95, surpassing QCNN (95.74–97.40%) and CNN (86.10–94.50%). Under depolarizing gate noise at p=0.1, SSL4Q retains 97.68% accuracy, still exceeding QCNN's noiseless performance of 96.38%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSL4Q outperforms all five baselines across every configuration tested. On the Heisenberg model, the strongest baseline LLM4QPE (a transformer with LSTM embedding) trails SSL4Q by 8.07 points at M=16, L=20 (63.90% vs. 71.97%) and by 5.13 points at M=128, L=20 (88.70% vs. 93.83%). The RNN baseline (GRU-based, from Carrasquilla et al. 2019) is consistently 10–15 points below SSL4Q, while kernel methods (Gaussian Kernel and NTK from Huang et al. 2022) are the weakest, reaching only 53–66%. On the VQC/MNIST task, SSL4Q (99.20–99.24%) exceeds QCNN (Cong et al. 2019, 95.74–97.40%) by 2–3.5 points and CNN (He et al. 2016, 86.10–94.50%) by 5–13 points. Notably, even SSL4Q's fully supervised variant (SSL4Q sup.) surpasses LLM4QPE and RNN, indicating that the PEA layer architecture itself provides a structural advantage over ordered-sequence approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

SSL4Q adapts the Mean Teacher semi-supervised framework (Tarvainen & Valpola, 2017) to quantum data by introducing two quantum-specific components: a shadow embedding that converts random Pauli measurement records into real-valued vectors using the classical shadow reconstruction formula (Huang et al., 2020), and a Permutation Equivalence Attention (PEA) layer that enforces permutation invariance over unordered measurements. It departs from prior quantum state classification methods (Carrasquilla et al., 2019; Tang et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2022), which are all fully supervised and either treat measurements as ordered sequences (RNN, LLM4QPE) or average them away, by explicitly preserving the unordered nature of random measurements and exploiting unlabeled data through consistency regularization. The paper also distinguishes itself from Zhang et al. (2023), which claimed semi-supervised quantum state classification but required quantum states directly as inputs—an impractical requirement since states must first be measured to obtain classical data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first practical semi-supervised learning framework for quantum measurement data, addressing the exponential labeling cost that makes fully supervised quantum state classification infeasible for large systems. The demonstrated 2–5% accuracy gains over supervised baselines at 90% label masking, along with strong noise robustness (97.68% at depolarizing noise p=0.1 vs. QCNN's 72.34%), suggest the approach could reduce experimental overhead in quantum device certification. Limitations include confinement to consistency regularization (no pseudo-labeling or generative SSL explored), restriction to classification (no regression for property estimation), and validation only on two model families up to 50 qubits. Open questions include whether more advanced SSL techniques transfer to quantum data, how the method scales beyond 50 qubits, and whether the PEA layer generalizes to higher-dimensional or multi-qubit measurement operators.
