## Problem

Current multi-modal learning paradigms optimize all modalities synchronously, causing modality competition where the dominant modality's gradient suppresses updates to weaker modalities. Existing remedies (G-Blending, OGM, PMR, UMT) still operate within the joint-learning framework and achieve only limited improvements. The paper asks whether a fundamentally different learning paradigm—alternating modality updates—can achieve true modality reconciliation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240789/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes modality-specific raw inputs (audio spectrograms of 257×1004 or 257×299, single or multi-frame RGB images at 224×224, 3D object front/rear views, or pre-extracted BERT/facial/acoustic features via MMSA-FET) and produces a final class label via decision-level fusion (sum of modality-specific logit vectors). For retrieval tasks, it outputs cosine-similarity scores between modality-specific latent features. Unlike feature-level fusion baselines, ReconBoost operates purely at the decision level, making it composable with arbitrary fusion strategies (QMF, TMC, learnable weighting).

## Method

ReconBoost alternates updates across modality-specific learners: in each round, one modality's parameters are optimized while all others are frozen, naturally disentangling gradients and eliminating modality competition. A KL-divergence-based reconcilement regularization term is added to the per-modality loss, dynamically encouraging the updated learner to capture complementary information beyond what other modalities already provide. The paper proves (Theorem 3.1) that with KL reconcilement, this objective is mathematically equivalent to gradient boosting—the updated learner fits a pseudo-label derived from the residual of the other modalities—while discarding historical learners to avoid overfitting (an 'alternating-boosting' strategy). Two enhancement schemes are added: a Memory Consolidation Regularization (MCR) that penalizes excessive divergence from the previous modality's predictions, and a Global Rectification Scheme (GRS) that performs joint fine-tuning of all modalities after each alternating update to escape poor local minima.

## Results

ReconBoost achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across all six multi-modal benchmarks. On CREMA-D (audio-visual emotion recognition), it reaches 79.82% accuracy, surpassing the strongest baseline UMT (70.97%) by nearly 9 points. On AVE (audio-visual event localization), it achieves 71.35% vs. UMT's 67.71%, and on ModelNet40 (3D object classification) it reaches 91.78% vs. UMT's 90.07%. On tri-modal sentiment datasets (MOSEI, MOSI, CH-SIMS), ReconBoost leads by 1.57%, 1.51%, and 2.11% over the best competitor respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReconBoost consistently outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and metric. The closest competitor is UMT (Du et al., 2023), which uses uni-modal knowledge distillation; ReconBoost beats it by 3.64% on AVE, 8.85% on CREMA-D, 1.71% on MN40, 1.57% on MOSEI, 2.16% on MOSI, and 2.33% on CH-SIMS. PMR (Fan et al., 2023) and OGM-GE (Peng et al., 2022) are further behind, with ReconBoost leading by 7.15% and 8.42% respectively on CREMA-D. G-Blending (Wang et al., 2020) and UME (Du et al., 2023) show the largest gaps, particularly on CREMA-D where ReconBoost exceeds them by 16.01% and 11.41%. On the retrieval task (mAP), ReconBoost also surpasses CMCL (Jing et al., 2021) and HSR (Jiang et al., 2023) by 7.21% and 5.30% overall mAP on CREMA-D, confirming that the modality-competition problem persists in retrieval and is effectively addressed by the alternating-boosting strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant synchronous joint-learning paradigm in multi-modal learning (G-Blending, OGM-GE, PMR, UMT) by proposing an alternating modality-update strategy, and grounds it theoretically in Friedman's gradient boosting. The key conceptual move is showing that a KL-divergence-based reconcilement regularization makes the alternating update equivalent to a gradient-boosting step, where each modality learner fits the residual of the others. Unlike classical GB that ensembles all weak learners, ReconBoost discards historical models and retains only the newest per modality, forming an 'alternating-boosting' strategy suited to over-parameterized DNNs. The memory consolidation and global rectification schemes further adapt the boosting framework to the deep learning setting, drawing on GrowNet-style rectification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded alternative to ad-hoc gradient-modulation tricks for mitigating modality competition, and the alternating-boosting formulation is modular enough to combine with diverse fusion strategies (QMF, TMC, MMTM). However, all experiments use ResNet-18 or small MLP/LSTM backbones on relatively small datasets (≤23K samples), leaving open whether the approach scales to large pre-trained encoders or very high-dimensional modalities. The fixed cyclic modality ordering (rather than adaptive selection) is a simplification that the ablation (Sec. D.5) shows is actually better than loss-guided selection, but the reason for this is not fully explained. Broader applicability to tasks beyond classification and retrieval (e.g., generation, segmentation) remains untested.
