## Problem

Existing diffusion models for crystal structure prediction fail to enforce two critical symmetries: lattice permutation equivariance (permuting lattice bases must induce a corresponding transformation of fractional coordinates) and rigorous periodic translation equivariance throughout the diffusion training process. The score-matching objective used in prior work like DiffCSP cannot guarantee a periodic-translation-invariant score because the training dataset does not contain all periodic translations of each structure, breaking the invariance of the learned score field.

## Input / Output

The model takes a one-hot encoded atom-type vector A (chemical composition) as the sole conditioning input and jointly generates a 3×2 lattice parameter matrix C (three positive lengths and three angles in (0,π)) and a 3×n fractional coordinate matrix F with entries in [0,1). Lattice parameters are preprocessed via log-length and tan(φ−π/2) transformations to map their constrained domains onto ℝ for DDPM diffusion, while fractional coordinates are diffused under a wrapped-normal score-matching framework with a predictor-corrector sampler. One structure sample is generated per test composition and evaluated via pymatgen's StructureMatcher.

## Method

EquiCSP jointly diffuses lattice parameters (via DDPM) and fractional coordinates (via score-matching with wrapped normal) using a GNN denoising network with Fourier-transformed relative fractional coordinates for periodicity. Lattice permutation equivariance is enforced not architecturally but through additional permutation-consistency loss terms (L_pC, L_ps, L_pF) that penalize the model for violating equivariance under random S₃ permutations, which the authors show is more efficient than Frame Average. The key novelty is Periodic CoM-free Noising: a noise conversion function m(·) built from the directional-statistics 'mean angle' that maps all periodic-translation-equivalent fractional coordinate matrices to a unique CoM-free representation, guaranteeing the training score is periodic-translation-invariant. A Von Mises distribution approximates the marginal PDF of the CoM-free noise to compute an accurate score, and a chain-rule-based probabilistic modeling step (Eq. 17–18) rigorously connects the CoM-free score back to the original coordinate score while preserving invariance (Proposition 4.7).

## Results

On the stable structure prediction task, EquiCSP achieves the best RMSE across all three datasets: 0.0707 on Perov-5, 0.0510 on MP-20, and 0.1169 on MPTS-52. In terms of match rate, EquiCSP reaches 57.59% on MP-20 and 14.85% on MPTS-52, surpassing the previous best diffusion method DiffCSP (51.49% and 12.19% respectively), while tying with DiffCSP on Perov-5 (52.02%). The RMSE improvement over DiffCSP is particularly pronounced on MPTS-52 (0.1169 vs. 0.1786, a 34.5% reduction), indicating that the equivariant formulation effectively reduces redundancy in the solution space for larger unit cells.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EquiCSP is compared against four categories of baselines: optimization-based methods (RS, BO, PSO), a conditional generative SchNet variant (P-cG-SchNet), a VAE-based crystal generator (CDVAE), and the closest diffusion-based competitor (DiffCSP). Against DiffCSP, EquiCSP wins on MP-20 (57.59% vs. 51.49% match rate; 0.0510 vs. 0.0631 RMSE) and MPTS-52 (14.85% vs. 12.19% match rate; 0.1169 vs. 0.1786 RMSE), while tying on Perov-5 match rate (52.02%) but achieving a lower RMSE (0.0707 vs. 0.0760). Against CDVAE, EquiCSP substantially outperforms on all three datasets, with the largest gap on MP-20 (57.59% vs. 33.90% match rate). The optimization-based baselines (RS, BO, PSO) and P-cG-SchNet are clearly inferior, with match rates below 56% on Perov-5 and below 16% on MP-20, confirming that generative diffusion models with proper symmetry handling are the most effective approach for CSP.

## Relation to Prior Work

EquiCSP extends the DiffCSP framework (Jiao et al., 2023), which jointly diffuses lattice parameters via DDPM and fractional coordinates via score-matching with wrapped normal distributions, by adding two previously missing symmetry guarantees: lattice permutation equivariance and rigorous periodic translation equivariance. The periodic CoM-free noising mechanism adapts the center-of-mass-free paradigm from GeoDiff (Xu et al., 2022) for molecular conformation generation to the periodic setting of crystals, using the 'mean angle' concept from directional statistics. The lattice parameter representation C=[l,φ] and the probabilistic modeling process are borrowed from SymMat (Luo et al., 2023), while the Fourier-transform-based message passing for periodicity follows DiffCSP. A key departure from prior equivariant generation work is the use of soft loss-based equivariance enforcement rather than architectural hard constraints (e.g., Frame Average), motivated by the computational infeasibility of finite group averaging in iterative diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying and enforcing lattice permutation equivariance—a symmetry overlooked by all prior crystal diffusion models—EquiCSP reduces redundancy in the solution space and improves both match rate and RMSE across datasets of increasing complexity. The periodic CoM-free noising technique is a general-purpose tool for any score-based generative model operating on periodic data, potentially applicable beyond crystals. However, the Von Mises approximation for the marginal distribution of the CoM-free noise and the acknowledged non-rigorous connection between the CoM-free score and the true score remain open theoretical gaps. The method is validated only on inorganic crystals with up to 52 atoms per cell, and whether the equivariance losses scale efficiently to much larger unit cells or to organic/molecular crystals remains untested.
