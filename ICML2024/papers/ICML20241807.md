## Problem

A/B testing relies on device identifiers to consistently assign treatments, but privacy regulations (GDPR, cookie deprecation) fragment user identity across devices, violating SUTVA since treatment is assigned per-device while outcomes depend on user-level exposure. Existing interference-aware estimators require exact network knowledge, multiple trials, or linear outcome models, leaving no method that handles uncertain network structure with non-linear outcomes in a single experimental run.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241807/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-device binary treatment assignments, observed outcomes, device-level covariates, and approximate neighbourhoods M(i) that are guaranteed supersets of the true device-sharing neighbourhoods (obtained from IP, geo-location, or probabilistic identity-linking models). It produces a GATE estimate as the mean difference between all-treatment and all-control potential outcomes, along with the fitted counterfactual mean outcome functions. A key structural constraint is that at GATE prediction time the latent exposure is exactly determined (all-0 or all-1 assignments), so no graph knowledge is needed at inference.

## Method

HIFIVE reframes treatment effect estimation under uncertain interference as a latent variable regression problem: the exposure computed under the assumed superset graph M(i) decomposes into the true exposure plus an independent error term (by the superset assumption A2), reducing the problem to classical measurement-error regression. The outcome model is a GLM parameterized by neural networks (MLPs with one hidden layer and leaky ReLU) for the functions c₀, c₁, g, and w in the exposure mapping. Training uses a K-sample importance-weighted ELBO with the DReG gradient estimator, treating the discrete exposure as a continuous latent variable with a Gaussian variational posterior. Identifiability of the conditional mean function is established by adapting the Schennach–Hu measurement-error identification theorem, requiring only that the outcome function be continuously differentiable with non-vanishing derivative. The approach requires no repeated trials, no exact graph, and no linearity in outcomes.

## Results

On synthetic Erdős-Rényi graphs, HIFIVE produces unbiased GATE estimates across all interference strengths (r), while all baselines (Poly, ReFeX, PERC/DWR, DM/SUTVA) exhibit strong bias as interference increases. On the AirBnB simulator, HIFIVE achieves bias and RMSE comparable to the Oracle Exposure model (which has access to the true graph), despite using only approximate neighbourhoods. On the US Power Plant Emissions observational dataset, HIFIVE's GATE estimate (with 95% CI) is consistent with the EXP and ReFeX estimators that require exact neighbourhoods. A key limitation is that estimation variance grows with the size of the assumed neighbourhood M(i), creating a bias-variance tradeoff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HIFIVE outperforms ReFeX [36] and PERC/DWR [117] on both synthetic and AirBnB experiments: these baselines require oracle access to the true interference graph and still produce biased estimates under non-linear outcomes, whereas HIFIVE uses only approximate (superset) neighbourhoods and remains unbiased. On the AirBnB simulator, HIFIVE matches the Oracle Exposure model from Brennan et al. [14] in both absolute relative bias and relative RMSE, despite the ground-truth outcome model not being an exposure model — demonstrating robustness to misspecification of assumption (A1). On the US Power Plant Emissions dataset, HIFIVE produces GATE estimates with 95% confidence intervals overlapping those of the EXP estimator [4] and ReFeX [36], both of which require exact neighbourhood information that HIFIVE does not. The SUTVA/DM estimator (used as a simple baseline throughout) is consistently the most biased method whenever interference is present, confirming the necessity of interference-aware estimation in this setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

HIFIVE sits at the intersection of network interference causal inference and measurement-error identification. It builds directly on the Schennach–Hu [74] result for nonparametric identification of classical measurement-error models without side information, repurposing it to show that the true exposure is identifiable when only a noisy (superset-based) exposure is observed. The variational training objective draws on the importance-weighted ELBO of Burda et al. [15] with the DReG gradient estimator. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant network-interference literature (Hudgens & Halloran, Aronow et al., Eckles et al.) by dropping the requirement for exact graph knowledge, and from multiple-trial methods (Cortez et al., Yu et al.) by operating on a single randomized or observational trial, at the cost of introducing a latent-variable model for the unobserved true exposure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Identity fragmentation driven by privacy regulation (GDPR, cookie deprecation) is making device-level A/B testing increasingly unreliable, and HIFIVE offers a principled, single-trial solution that only requires a superset of each user's devices—a condition easily met via IP, geo-location, or probabilistic device-linking. The identifiability guarantee under the superset assumption is a genuine theoretical contribution that was not previously established for this setting. Key limitations include the variance growing with neighbourhood size (requiring a bias-variance trade-off in practice) and the reliance on the exposure-mapping assumption (A1), which the AirBnB experiments show can be violated in realistic settings. Open directions flagged by the authors include temporal/longitudinal extensions and tighter control of the variance penalty from large superset neighbourhoods.
