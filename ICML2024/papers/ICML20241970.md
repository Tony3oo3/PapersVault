## Problem

Client selection in federated learning must balance model accuracy, training speed, and energy consumption across heterogeneous devices, yet existing heuristic-based methods generalize poorly to unseen deployment configurations and RL-based methods suffer from cold-start and sample inefficiency. Additionally, prior learning-based approaches score each device in isolation (pointwise), failing to directly optimize the relative ordering that determines which devices are actually selected.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241970/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-device state vectors obtained via an 'early exit' probing scheme: after only the first local training epoch, each device reports scalar values (computation/communication latency, computation/communication energy, training loss, and data size) to the central server, avoiding the full multi-epoch training overhead. The output is a Q-value ranking over all N available devices from which the top-K are selected for the current round; the Q-network uses a Value Decomposition Network architecture with a three-layer MLP per device, and the final selection is determined by relative Q-value ordering rather than absolute thresholds.

## Method

FedRank combines three mechanisms: (1) offline imitation learning pre-training via behavioral cloning, where the Q-network is trained to replicate decisions of state-of-the-art analytical policies (Harmony, Oort, FedMarl) on collected state-action episodes, eliminating the RL cold-start problem; (2) a pairwise ranking loss (RankNet-style BCE) that replaces pointwise Q-value regression, training the network to correctly order device pairs so selected devices score significantly higher than non-selected ones; (3) online DQN fine-tuning with a joint loss combining the standard temporal-difference RL objective and the pairwise ranking term, allowing the model to surpass the analytical expert. The reward function jointly penalizes stragglers and high-energy devices via developer-specified factors α and β, and the probing-based early exit reduces per-round latency by 10.6% and energy by 25.2% compared to full local training.

## Results

FedRank achieves the best test accuracy across all four datasets under both IID and Non-IID settings, reaching 99% on MNIST-LeNet5 (ID) in 32 rounds, 87.69% on CIFAR-10-ResNet18, 68.58% on CINIC-10-VGG16, and 43.54% on TinyImageNet-ShuffleNet (all OOD, IID). It accelerates training convergence by up to 2.01× (CINIC-10) and reduces per-round energy consumption by up to 40.1% (MNIST) relative to the FedAvg baseline. Under Non-IID conditions, FedRank improves accuracy by up to 56.9% over FedAvg on the ID dataset and by up to 17.08% on OOD datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedRank outperforms all seven baselines on every metric (accuracy, speed, energy) across all four datasets in both IID and Non-IID settings. On CIFAR-10 (IID), FedRank reaches 87.69% accuracy versus the best baseline FedMarl at 86.37% (+1.32 pp) and Oort at 85.69% (+2.00 pp), while also achieving the fastest convergence at 1.67× speed and lowest energy at 44.4%. On the harder CINIC-10 dataset, FedRank's 68.58% accuracy surpasses TiFL (67.32%, the best baseline) by 1.26 pp and delivers the top speed of 2.01× versus FedMarl's 1.86×. On TinyImageNet, the gap widens: FedRank achieves 43.54% versus Oort's 40.57% (+2.97 pp), the largest accuracy margin among OOD datasets. Under Non-IID conditions, FedRank maintains its lead (e.g., 59.11% on CIFAR-10 vs. FedMarl's 56.73%), confirming that the imitation-learning pre-training and pairwise ranking loss provide robustness to data heterogeneity that pure RL baselines (Favor, FedMarl) lack due to their cold-start vulnerability.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedRank sits at the intersection of three research threads in federated learning: heuristic-based client selection (Oort, TiFL, AFL, Harmony), RL-based selection (Favor, FedMarl, AutoFL), and learning-to-rank from information retrieval. It departs from prior RL-based approaches by introducing an offline imitation-learning pre-training phase that uses analytical heuristics as expert demonstrations, directly addressing the cold-start problem that has limited real-world adoption of RL in FL. The key conceptual departure is reframing device selection from a pointwise scoring problem to a pairwise ranking problem (via RankNet-style BCE loss), borrowing the LTR paradigm to emphasize relative ordering over absolute value prediction. The early-exit probing mechanism for state acquisition builds on the probing idea in Oort and FedMarl but formalizes it as a latency/energy reduction strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cold-start problem has been a practical barrier to deploying RL-based client selection in real FL systems, and the IL pre-training strategy here offers a concrete, well-motivated solution that could be adopted by other RL-based FL schedulers. However, the experimental scale (100 devices, 10 selected, 50 rounds) is modest for a systems paper, and the convergence analysis is relatively standard. Open questions include how well the IL pre-training transfers when the device pool changes substantially, whether the pairwise ranking advantage persists at much larger scale (thousands of devices), and how sensitive the method is to the quality of the expert heuristics used for pre-training. The 56.9% Non-IID accuracy gain is partly inflated by the extremely weak FedAvg baseline (36.7%), so the practical improvement over the strongest baselines (Oort, FedMarl) is more modest (3–7%).
