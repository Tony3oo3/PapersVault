## Problem

While super-linear convergence of EM for two-component Mixed Linear Regression (2MLR) has been established in noiseless and high-SNR settings, the exponent of convergence has never been theoretically estimated and the geometric structure of the EM iteration trajectory remains uncharacterized. Additionally, prior convergence analyses assumed balanced mixing weights, leaving the unbalanced case unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241355/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled covariate-response pairs (x_i, y_i) drawn from a symmetric 2MLR model with Gaussian covariates x ~ N(0, I_d) and additive Gaussian noise of known variance σ², along with an initial parameter guess (θ⁰, π⁰). It outputs the regression parameter vector θ ∈ R^d and mixing weights π = {π(1), π(2)} at each EM iteration. A key structural property is that all population-level EM updates for θ lie in the two-dimensional plane span{θ⁰, θ*}, reducing the trajectory analysis to a single angular parameter φ^t regardless of ambient dimension d.

## Method

The paper derives explicit closed-form expressions for population EM updates using modified Bessel functions K₀ and K₁, valid across all SNR regimes and for both balanced and unbalanced mixing weights (parameterized via ν = log(π(1)/π(2))/2). In the noiseless limit (SNR → ∞), the authors show that the EM update for θ depends only on the sub-optimality angle φ^t = π/2 − arccos|ρ^t|, yielding a simple recurrence tan φ^t = tan φ^{t−1} + φ^{t−1}(tan²φ^{t−1} + 1). This recurrence implies that all iterations trace a cycloid with parameter ‖θ*‖/π on the plane span{θ⁰, θ*}, and that convergence is quadratic in the angle φ once φ exceeds arctan(1.5), with the convergence rate independent of mixing weights. The error in mixing weights is shown to be exactly proportional to the residual angle (π/2 − φ^{t−1}), and a finite-sample analysis via coupling with the population EM yields a tighter statistical error bound of O(√(d/n) ∨ log(1/δ)/n ∨ √(log(1/δ)/n)) compared to prior O(√(d/n)·log(n/δ)) results.

## Results

In the noiseless setting, the paper proves that EM iterations for 2MLR follow a cycloid trajectory in the plane span{θ⁰, θ*}, with a quadratic convergence rate (exponent ≈ 2) for the sub-optimality angle φᵗ, validated empirically with slopes consistently at or above 2 across SNR = 10⁶, 10⁷, 10⁸. The statistical error bound for regression parameters is tightened to O(√(d/n) ∨ log(1/δ)/n ∨ √(log(1/δ)/n)), improving upon the prior O(√(d/n)·log(n/δ)) bound. The quadratic convergence region is extended from ‖θ−θ*‖/‖θ*‖ ≤ 1/10 (Kwon et al., 2021) to ≈ 0.22 in the noiseless limit. Total iterations to convergence scale as T = O(log(n/log(1/δ))) with Easy EM initialization, or T′ = O(log(n/d) ∧ log(n/log(1/δ))) with a good initialization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Balakrishnan et al. (2017), this paper extends the population EM update from balanced mixing weights (π = {1/2, 1/2}) to the general unbalanced case via the ν parameterization, and provides a tighter finite-sample statistical error bound by exploiting rotational invariance of Gaussians to rewrite the ℓ₂ error as a geometric mean of two chi-square distributions. Against Ghosh & Kannan (2020), who proved super-linear convergence of Alternating Minimization (hard-label EM variant) only within a region dependent on min(π*(1), π*(2)), this paper establishes quadratic convergence for full soft-label EM without any restriction on mixing weights, and shows the regression-parameter error is independent of π*. Relative to Kwon et al. (2021), the quadratic convergence region is expanded from ‖θ−θ*‖/‖θ*‖ ≤ 1/10 to ≈ 0.22 in the noiseless limit, and the statistical error bound is improved from a multiplicative log factor O(√(d/n)·log(n/δ)) to an additive one O(√(d/n) ∨ log(1/δ)/n ∨ √(log(1/δ)/n)). The cycloid trajectory characterization (Proposition 4.4) is entirely new and provides a geometric explanation for the convergence behavior that prior analyses lacked.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis of EM for two-component Mixed Linear Regression (2MLR) initiated by Balakrishnan et al. (2017) and refined by Kwon et al. (2019, 2021) and Ghosh & Kannan (2020). While prior works established global convergence and super-linear rates under restrictive conditions (balanced mixing weights, specific SNR regimes, or bounded initialization), this paper departs by deriving explicit Bessel-function closed-forms for EM updates across all SNR regimes and by introducing a trajectory-based geometric analysis. The cycloid characterization of iterations and the recurrence relation for the sub-optimality angle represent a new analytical lens not present in the prior EM-for-MLR literature, which had focused on norm-based error tracking rather than the geometric path of iterates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cycloid trajectory provides a clean geometric explanation for why EM converges quadratically in the noiseless 2MLR setting, and the result that the convergence exponent is independent of mixing weights resolves a gap left by prior analyses that assumed balanced weights. The tighter finite-sample statistical error bound (removing a multiplicative log factor) is practically useful for determining sample complexity. However, the main results are confined to the noiseless (infinite SNR) limit and the two-component case; extending the cycloid framework to finite SNR, multiple components, or non-Gaussian covariates remains open, as do the authors' own suggestions for analyzing the weakly separated (SNR→0) regime via the Corollary 3.2 recurrence.
