## Problem

Equilibria of standard Markov games are highly sensitive to small environmental perturbations, and while distributionally robust RL has been well studied in single-agent settings, the multi-agent case remains largely open. The core question this paper tackles is whether one can simultaneously achieve robustness against worst-case distributional shifts and near-optimal sample efficiency when learning equilibrium policies in robust Markov games (RMGs), where each agent must hedge against its own prescribed uncertainty set while strategically interacting with other agents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241829/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives N i.i.d. samples per (state, joint-action, time-step) tuple drawn non-adaptively from a generative model of the nominal environment, along with the known reward function and per-agent TV-distance uncertainty radii σ_i ∈ (0,1]. It outputs an ε-approximate robust equilibrium joint policy (selectable as robust NE, CE, or CCE via a subroutine), together with the estimated robust Q-functions and value functions for all agents at all time steps. The non-adaptive generative-model sampling is a key structural assumption that enables the finite-sample analysis, and the agent-wise (s,a)-rectangularity condition on the uncertainty sets is required for the robust Bellman recursion to hold.

## Method

DR-NVI is a two-stage model-based algorithm: it first constructs an empirical nominal transition kernel via empirical frequencies, then performs backward value iteration from h=H to h=1. At each time step, the robust Q-function for each agent is computed by solving the dual of the TV-ball DRO subproblem (via strong duality, reducing an S-dimensional simplex optimization to a one-dimensional clipped-value maximization over a scalar α), and the equilibrium at each state is obtained by solving the corresponding matrix game with the robust Q-functions as payoffs. The agent-wise (s,a)-rectangularity condition is what decouples the uncertainty across agents, states, and time steps, yielding a tractable robust Bellman equation. The paper proves a near-optimal upper bound of Õ(SH³∏A_i/ε² · min{H, 1/min σ_i}) samples and, crucially, establishes the first information-theoretic lower bound for RMGs, confirming optimality in S, H, ε, and the uncertainty levels.

## Results

DR-NVI achieves an ε-approximate robust {NE, CCE, CE} for finite-horizon RMGs with sample complexity Õ(SH³∏Aᵢ/ε² · min{H, 1/min σᵢ}), which is near-optimal given the established information-theoretic lower bound of Õ(SH³ max Aᵢ/ε² · min{H, 1/min σᵢ}). The gap between upper and lower bounds is only a factor of ∏Aᵢ/max Aᵢ (the curse of multiple agents). In the single-agent RMDP special case (A₂=…=Aₙ=1), DR-NVI achieves the minimax-optimal rate Õ(SA₁H³/ε² · min{H, 1/σ₁}), the first such result in the finite-horizon setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DR-NVI improves upon the prior state-of-the-art for robust MGs (Blanchet et al., 2023) by a factor of Õ(S³(∏Aᵢ)²), reducing the state-space dependence from S⁴ to S and the action-space dependence from (∏Aᵢ)³ to ∏Aᵢ. Compared to the standard MG baseline (NVI), DR-NVI achieves the same H⁴ scaling when uncertainty is small (min σᵢ ≲ 1/H), but improves to H³ scaling when min σᵢ ≳ 1/H, yielding a factor of H·min σᵢ improvement (up to H when σᵢ = O(1)). In the single-agent RMDP limit, DR-NVI matches the minimax-optimal lower bound, establishing the first finite-horizon minimax-optimal sample complexity for robust MDPs. The paper also provides the first information-theoretic lower bound for RMGs, confirming near-optimality with respect to S, H, ε, and {σᵢ}.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Nash value iteration (Liu et al., 2021) for standard Markov games to the distributionally robust setting, leveraging the (s,a)-rectangularity condition and strong duality from robust dynamic programming (Iyengar, 2005) to handle the nonlinear worst-case payoffs. It extends the single-agent robust RL literature (Shi et al., 2023; Panaganti & Kalathil, 2022) to the multi-agent regime, where game-theoretic interactions introduce statistical dependencies absent in the single-agent case. Compared to the closest prior work on robust MARL (Blanchet et al., 2023; Ma et al., 2023), DR-NVI improves the sample complexity by a factor of Õ(S³(∏A_i)²) and is the first to match an information-theoretic lower bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first near-optimal finite-sample guarantee for learning robust equilibria in Markov games, closing a gap that left practitioners without a clear understanding of the sample cost of robustness in multi-agent settings. The finding that robust MARL requires no more samples than standard MARL (when σ_i ≳ 1/H) is practically reassuring for sim-to-real deployment. Open questions include taming the curse of multi-agents (the ∏A_i factor), extending to other divergence metrics, and moving beyond non-adaptive generative-model sampling to online or offline settings.
