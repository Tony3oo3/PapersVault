## Problem

Standard DRL policies trained in simple scenarios fail to transfer to significantly more complex scenarios with the same task logic (e.g., larger maps, more obstacles), and existing few-shot generalization methods require expensive reward function design in the test environment. This paper introduces 'cognitive generalization'—the ability to deploy a simple-scenario policy to a complex scenario via reward-agnostic fine-tuning—and proposes a general framework to unlock this capability in mainstream DRL algorithms.

## Input / Output

The method takes state observations (64×64×3 RGB frames converted to 128-dim vectors via a pre-trained MLP for ProcGen, or raw state vectors for 2DMAZE) and actions (discrete 15-dim for ProcGen, continuous for 2DMAZE) as input. In the complex test scenario, no reward signal is available; instead, the system outputs a policy for action selection along with approximated Q-values and rewards computed from weighted k-nearest neighbors in the cognitive latent space. The VAE latent space dimension is 5 for 2DMAZE and 15 for ProcGen, and the GBR segmentation produces an adaptive number of granular balls (subregions) without any pre-specified class count.

## Method

GBRL operates in two stages. Stage 1 trains a standard DRL policy (DDPG, TD3, or PPO) in a simple scenario while simultaneously building a cognitive latent space via a VAE whose decoder uses a cascaded prediction head to model state-transition residuals (environmental dynamics), ensuring that samples with similar environmental impact are spatially close in the latent space. The latent space is then segmented using Granular Ball Representation (GBR), which adaptively splits and merges granular balls based on a Distribution Measure (DM) criterion, requiring no predefined number of clusters. Stage 2 deploys the policy in the complex scenario without reward feedback: each new state-action pair is encoded into the latent space, assigned to a granular ball via a distance-radius ratio criterion, and its Q-value and reward are approximated as a similarity-weighted average of k nearest neighbors within that ball. The policy is then fine-tuned via standard TD-learning using these approximated values, and the VAE is periodically retrained on accumulated complex-scenario samples to refine the latent space.

## Results

On seven ProcGen tasks, GBRL-TD3 achieves the highest episode score on 4 out of 7 tasks (Ninja 6.17, Heist 5.63, Leaper 5.24, Miner 12.38), outperforming the strongest few-shot baseline ExpGen by up to 0.72 points on Miner. When combined with DDPG, TD3, or PPO, GBRL brings policies trained only on simple scenarios to performance comparable to (or exceeding) policies trained directly on complex scenarios across six test tasks (Figure 3). In the clustering ablation (Appendix D.3), GBR outperforms K-means, DBSCAN, and DPC by approximately 15% on average policy score across three ProcGen tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the zero-shot baseline M2TD3, GBRL-TD3 wins on all seven ProcGen tasks, with the largest margin on Miner (+5.22 points), confirming that reward-agnostic fine-tuning substantially outperforms no-fine-tuning approaches. Against the few-shot baseline IDAAC, GBRL-TD3 wins on five of seven tasks (Ninja, Heist, Leaper, Miner, CaveFlyer) but trails on Chaser (3.25 vs 3.48) and is close on CaveFlyer (6.13 vs 6.16). Against the strongest few-shot baseline ExpGen, GBRL-TD3 wins on four tasks (Ninja, Heist, Leaper, Miner) but loses on Chaser (3.25 vs 3.48), CaveFlyer (6.13 vs 6.16), and Dodgeball (4.88 vs 5.49). In the clustering comparison (Appendix D.3), GBR outperforms K-means, DBSCAN, and DPC by roughly 15% on average policy score, with the largest gap on Chaser (3.27 vs 2.86 for K-means, 2.53 for DBSCAN). Notably, GBRL-TD3 achieves these results without any reward signal in the complex scenario, whereas IDAAC and ExpGen both require dense reward feedback during fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the domain adaptation paradigm in RL (Eysenbach et al., 2020), which assumes source-domain experience can transfer to a target domain with the same task logic, and extends it by eliminating the need for reward feedback in the target domain. Technically, it combines deep representation learning for RL (Li et al., 2021; Chandak et al., 2019) with Granular Ball Representation (Xia et al., 2019, 2023), an adaptive multi-granularity clustering method previously applied to small-dataset classification but not to reinforcement learning. The paper departs from both zero-shot generalization (which forbids any test-scenario interaction) and few-shot generalization (which requires reward signals in the test scenario) by proposing an intermediate 'cognitive generalization' setting: a single simple training scenario, a single more complex test scenario sharing the same task logic, and reward-agnostic fine-tuning using only raw observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is reducing the cost of designing reward functions for complex scenarios (e.g., game NPC behavior, robot control), where manual reward engineering scales poorly with environment complexity. Key limitations include the restrictive assumption that training and test scenarios share identical task logic, action space, and observation space; the need for per-task GBR hyperparameter tuning (η, k); and evaluation limited to relatively simple 2D environments (2DMAZE, ProcGen). Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-dimensional continuous control (e.g., robotics), how sensitive it is to distributional shifts beyond 'same task logic,' and whether the VAE latent space remains meaningful as scenario complexity grows substantially.
