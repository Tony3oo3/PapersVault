## Problem

Path-based knowledge graph reasoning methods suffer from missing paths and information over-squashing inherent to message-passing neural networks, while existing transformer-based approaches rely on textual descriptions via pretrained language models, introducing ambiguity and domain-specific knowledge dependencies while neglecting structural information. This paper asks whether the transformer attention mechanism can be redefined to operate directly on graph structure—capturing all-pair entity interactions—without any textual input.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241280/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a knowledge graph G = {V, E, R} and a query triplet (h, r_q, ?), where entity features X are initialized as all-zero vectors and relation features R are randomly initialized learnable parameters. The attention mechanism computes interactions over all entity pairs (not just connected paths), producing updated entity representations that are scored via an MLP + sigmoid to rank all candidate tail entities. The kernel function uses Frobenius-norm-normalized projections and a first-order Taylor approximation of the exponential, reducing attention computation from O(|V|²) to O(|V|) per layer.

## Method

KNOWFORMER redefines self-attention as a weighted aggregation over all entity pairs, where attention scores are determined by the plausibility of entity pairs as query prototypes (entities sharing the same query relation). The query function (Q-RMPNN) and value function (V-RMPNN) are relational message-passing networks that propagate DistMult-based relational messages over k-hop neighborhoods, with the value function additionally incorporating head-entity labeling for link prediction. The kernel function approximates the exponential kernel via a first-order Taylor expansion around zero with Frobenius norm normalization, enabling linear-complexity attention computation. The overall architecture stacks L transformer layers (custom attention + FFN + skip connections + layer normalization), trained end-to-end with a negative sampling loss. The paper proves the attention layer achieves at least RA-WL₂ expressivity on an extended graph, and that the kernel approximation error is bounded by O(e^γ/2) independent of graph size.

## Results

On transductive knowledge graph reasoning, KNOWFORMER achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four benchmark datasets: MRR of 0.430 (H@1: 34.3, H@10: 60.8) on FB15k-237, 0.579 (H@1: 52.8, H@10: 68.7) on WN18RR, 0.566 (H@1: 50.2, H@10: 67.5) on NELL-995, and 0.615 (H@1: 54.7, H@10: 73.4) on YAGO3-10. The most notable margin is on YAGO3-10, where KNOWFORMER outperforms the best path-based baseline AdaProp by 4.2 MRR points (0.615 vs. 0.573). In the inductive setting, KNOWFORMER also achieves the best or near-best results across 12 dataset splits, with the largest gain on NELL-995 v1 (MRR 0.827 vs. 0.697 for INGRAM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KNOWFORMER outperforms all path-based baselines (NBFNet, RED-GNN, A*Net, AdaProp, ULTRA) on every transductive dataset and metric, with the largest margins on YAGO3-10 (MRR 0.615 vs. 0.573 for AdaProp, +4.2) and FB15k-237 (MRR 0.430 vs. 0.417 for AdaProp, +1.3). Against embedding-based methods (TransE, DistMult, RotatE, HousE), KNOWFORMER leads by 6–10 MRR points on FB15k-237 and 4–12 points on YAGO3-10. Among text-based methods, SimKGC is the sole baseline that beats KNOWFORMER, achieving MRR 0.666 on WN18RR (vs. 0.579) by leveraging pretrained language model knowledge over the WordNet lexicon; however, SimKGC collapses on YAGO3-10 (MRR 0.211) and underperforms KNOWFORMER on FB15k-237 and NELL-995. In the inductive setting, KNOWFORMER surpasses all baselines on the majority of 12 splits, with particularly strong gains on NELL-995 v1 (MRR 0.827 vs. 0.697 for INGRAM) and FB15k-237 v2 (MRR 0.532 vs. 0.514 for NBFNet). On homogeneous graph link prediction (Cora, Citeseer, PubMed), KNOWFORMER achieves the best AUROC and AP on Cora (0.961/0.965) and PubMed (0.987/0.988), and competitive results on Citeseer (0.941/0.950 vs. S-VGAE's 0.947/0.952).

## Relation to Prior Work

KNOWFORMER repositions the transformer architecture for knowledge graph reasoning by treating self-attention as a weighted aggregation over all entity pairs, rather than the dominant paradigm of encoding textual descriptions via pretrained language models (KG-BERT, SimKGC, KGT5). Its attention mechanism is directly inspired by the kernel-based reformulation in SAT (Chen et al., 2022), while the relational message-passing sub-modules (Q-RMPNN, V-RMPNN) inherit their architecture from NBFNet (Zhu et al., 2021) and use DistMult (Yang et al., 2015) as the underlying score function. The key conceptual departure is the 'query prototype' definition, which reduces the attention computation from O(|R|·|V|²) to O(|V|²) by conditioning pairwise interactions on the plausibility of entity pairs under a specific query relation, combined with a first-order Taylor approximation of the exponential kernel to achieve linear complexity in the number of entities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KNOWFORMER addresses two well-recognized limitations of path-based KG reasoning methods—missing paths and information over-squashing—by enabling all-pair entity interactions through attention, and does so without relying on textual descriptions, making it more robust to domain-specific KGs and scalable to large graphs. The theoretical expressivity guarantee (matching RA-WL₂ on an extended graph) and the bounded approximation error for the kernel provide principled justification for the design choices. Open questions include whether the method can be adapted to full-inductive settings with unseen relations (the paper only handles semi-inductive with invariant relation sets), whether it can benefit from a pre-training/fine-tuning paradigm akin to ULTRA, and whether the all-pair attention mechanism remains effective on extremely sparse or heterogeneous KGs beyond the four standard benchmarks.
