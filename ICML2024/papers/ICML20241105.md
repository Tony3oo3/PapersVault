## Problem

Existing generative models for protein design focus primarily on backbone frames in SE(3) and do not address the full-atom, target-specific peptide design problem, where side-chain torsion dynamics, sequence-structure consistency, and explicit conditioning on a binding pocket are all critical. Additionally, no comprehensive benchmark existed to systematically evaluate generative models on peptide design tasks.

## Input / Output

The model takes the full-atom structure of a target protein receptor (residue types, backbone frames, and side-chain angles) as conditioning context and generates a bound-state peptide of 3–25 residues. For partial-sampling tasks (fix-backbone sequence design or side-chain packing), the peptide backbone or sequence is held fixed while only the desired modality is sampled. Outputs are full-atom coordinates reconstructed from predicted positions, orientations, torsion angles, and discrete residue types, with 64 samples generated per target.

## Method

PepFlow factorizes the conditional joint distribution p(peptide | receptor) into four modalities—position (ℝ³), orientation (SO(3)), torsion angles (T⁵), and residue type (simplex Δ¹⁹)—and learns a separate conditional flow on each manifold using geodesic interpolation (linear in Euclidean/logit space, exponential-map-based on SO(3) and the torus). A SE(3)-equivariant encoder processes the target protein, and an Invariant Point Attention decoder regresses the target-state descriptors at t=1 (reparameterized CFM), from which vector fields are derived for Euler-step sampling. The multi-modal CFM loss is a weighted sum over all four modalities plus auxiliary backbone and torsion reconstruction losses. Partial sampling is achieved by fixing ground-truth values for undesired modalities during both training and inference, enabling the same architecture to serve sequence-structure co-design, fix-backbone inverse folding, and side-chain packing.

## Results

On the primary sequence-structure co-design task, PepFlow w/Bb+Seq+Ang achieves the best RMSD (2.07 Å), binding site ratio (86.89%), and binding affinity (21.37%) among all methods, substantially outperforming RFDiffusion (RMSD 4.17 Å, BSR 26.71%) and ProteinGenerator (RMSD 4.35 Å, BSR 24.62%). In fix-backbone sequence design, PepFlow w/Bb+Seq achieves the highest amino acid recovery rate (56.40%) and diversity (23.38), while the full-atom variant leads in designability (65.48%). For side-chain packing, PepFlow attains the lowest MSE on all four torsion angles (χ1: 17.38°, χ2: 24.71°, χ3: 33.63°, χ4: 58.49°) and the highest correct-prediction rate (62.79%), surpassing DiffPack and all energy-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In sequence-structure co-design, PepFlow w/Bb+Seq+Ang dominates on structural fidelity (RMSD 2.07 Å vs. 3.28–4.35 Å for baselines) and binding-site accuracy (BSR 86.89% vs. 24.62–49.83%), but trails RFDiffusion (78.52%) and ProteinGenerator (71.82%) on designability, which the authors attribute to baselines being trained on the full PDB and biased toward stable motifs. In fix-backbone sequence design, PepFlow w/Bb+Seq outperforms ProteinMPNN (AAR 56.40% vs. 53.28%) and ESM-IF (43.51%) on recovery rate and diversity (23.38 vs. 15.33/13.76), while the full-atom variant achieves the best designability (65.48% vs. 60.55% and 53.76%). In side-chain packing, PepFlow w/Bb+Seq+Ang achieves the lowest MSE on all four torsion angles and the highest correct rate (62.79%), beating the strongest learning-based baseline DiffPack (χ1: 17.92°, χ2: 26.08°, χ3: 36.20°, Correct 62.58%) and all energy-based methods (Rosetta, SCWRL4) by wide margins. Overall, PepFlow consistently wins on geometry and binding metrics across all three tasks, with the main trade-off being slightly lower diversity and stability compared to backbone-only baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

PepFlow extends the Conditional Flow Matching framework (Lipman et al., 2022) and its Riemannian generalization (Chen & Lipman, 2023) to a multi-modal setting for protein-peptide design, constructing analytical flows on four distinct manifolds: Euclidean space for positions, SO(3) for orientations, a flat torus for torsion angles, and the logit space for residue types. It departs from the dominant diffusion-based paradigm in protein design (RFDiffusion, ProteinGenerator) by using deterministic ODE-based flows, and extends prior backbone-only generation to full-atom modeling by explicitly incorporating side-chain conformation as a learned modality. The architecture leverages Invariant Point Attention from AlphaFold as the decoder, and the paper introduces a new benchmark dataset and evaluation metrics absent from prior peptide design literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a concrete gap in computational peptide design: prior methods model only backbone frames and sequences, ignoring the side-chain dynamics that govern actual binding interactions. The multi-modal flow matching formulation provides a principled way to jointly model heterogeneous data types (continuous, rotational, angular, categorical) on their natural manifolds, and the partial sampling mechanism elegantly unifies co-design, inverse folding, and side-chain packing under one framework. Key limitations include reduced diversity inherent to deterministic ODE flows and the absence of property-guided generation, both of which constrain practical drug-design applications. The new benchmark and metrics fill a notable void in the field, though the modest performance gaps over strong baselines (and the fact that baselines outperform on Stability and Designability) suggest the full-atom advantage, while real, is not yet transformative.
