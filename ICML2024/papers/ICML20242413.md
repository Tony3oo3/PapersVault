## Problem

Prior learning-augmented dictionary structures (Lin et al. 2022; Cao et al. 2023) achieve static optimality with accurate frequency predictions but degrade to O(n) robustness under adversarial predictions. The paper asks whether a single data structure can simultaneously achieve optimal consistency (O(1) factor over entropy) and optimal robustness (O(log n) worst-case per operation) without restrictive assumptions like random-order rank.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242413/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an online stream of m dictionary operations (search, insert, delete, successor, predecessor, rank, select, range queries) over n keys from universe [n], together with a known predicted frequency vector f̂ (a probability distribution over keys); the actual frequency vector f is unknown. Performance is measured by the number of comparisons per operation, and the structure must support all standard ordering-based secondary queries without shuffling keys or requiring the random-order rank assumption that limits prior learned treaps.

## Method

RobustSL is a skip list whose replication strategy combines a deterministic class-base with a stochastic geometric component. Items are classified into K = O(log log n) classes by predicted frequency ranges (parameter θ), and each class c receives a minimum of D(K) − D(c) replicas (the class-base), where D is a recursively defined increasing function ensuring higher-frequency items occupy higher levels. On top of the class-base, each item receives additional replicas drawn from a geometric distribution with parameter p, mimicking the random replication of classic skip lists within each class. The deterministic classification guarantees that under perfect predictions the per-item access cost is O(log f_i), yielding O(1) consistency, while the stochastic component caps the maximum search cost at O(log n) regardless of prediction quality, yielding optimal robustness. The structure is dynamic (reconstructed when n changes by a factor of 4) and supports secondary queries via standard skip-list augmentations such as deep pointers and skip-count fields.

## Results

Under random frequency ordering with perfect predictions (n=2000, dynamic setting), RobustSL incurs only 9.5% more comparisons per query than learning-augmented treaps, confirming near-optimal consistency. Under adversarial frequency ordering with noisy predictions, RobustSL achieves 40.0× lower comparisons per query than learned treaps in the dynamic setting and 45.1× lower in the static setting. For larger dictionaries (n=10,000) under adversarial predictions, learned treaps degrade to 788 comparisons per query while RobustSL remains at O(log n). The theoretical consistency bound is −4/(p·log p) ≈ O(1) with p=0.368, and robustness is O(log n) for all prediction quality levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against learning-augmented treaps (Lin et al., 2022), RobustSL is within 7–10% under perfect predictions but achieves 40–45× fewer comparisons under adversarial predictions, where learned treaps degrade to near-linear cost (788 comparisons/query at n=10,000). Against learning-augmented B-Trees (Cao et al., 2023), RobustSL similarly outperforms under adversarial conditions, as Cao et al.'s structure is proven to have O(n) robustness (Proposition 2.3) while RobustSL maintains O(log n). Against splay trees, RobustSL avoids the constant-factor pointer-update overhead that makes splay trees impractical, while matching or exceeding their static optimality under good predictions. Against balanced BSTs (pessimistic BST / Knuth's optimal BST), RobustSL achieves comparable worst-case O(log n) robustness but significantly better average-case performance when predictions are accurate, since balanced BSTs do not exploit frequency information at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the learning-augmented dictionary line initiated by Lin et al. (ICML 2022), who replaced random treap priorities with frequency predictions under a random-order rank assumption, and Cao et al. (arXiv 2023), who removed that assumption via a log-log priority transformation but lost robustness. RobustSL departs from both by switching the underlying structure from a treap/B-tree to a skip list, where a deterministic class-based replication (driven by predicted frequency) ensures consistency while an independent geometric random variable per item ensures robustness — a two-factor design that neither prior treap-based approach could replicate. The skip list's multi-level architecture is the key structural departure, as it naturally separates the 'how many levels' decision (deterministic, frequency-driven) from the 'which specific levels' decision (stochastic), avoiding the adversarial vulnerability of priority-based trees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first learning-augmented dictionary to simultaneously achieve optimal consistency (static optimality) and optimal robustness (O(log n) worst-case per operation) without restrictive assumptions like random-order rank, resolving a trade-off that prior work treated as inherent. The practical significance is that RobustSL supports dynamic insertions/deletions and secondary queries (predecessor, successor, rank, select, range) with the same guarantees, making it a viable drop-in replacement for balanced BSTs in database and systems applications. Open questions include whether the guarantees extend to partial predictions (only a subset of items have frequency estimates) and whether any learning-augmented BST (not just skip lists) can achieve both properties, which the authors leave for future work.
