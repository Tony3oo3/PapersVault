## Problem

While diffusion models provably enable fast (polynomial-time) unconditional sampling from well-modeled distributions, no algorithm is known to efficiently sample from the posterior p(x|y) given a noisy linear measurement. This paper proves that efficient posterior sampling is fundamentally impossible: under the standard cryptographic assumption that one-way functions exist, every algorithm requires superpolynomial time on some instances, even though unconditional sampling on the same distribution is provably fast.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy linear measurement y = Ax + η where A ∈ R^{m×d} is a known matrix with m < d, η ~ βN(0, I_m) is Gaussian noise, and approximate smoothed scores of the prior p(x) that are representable by a polynomial-size neural network (the 'well-modeled' condition). It produces samples from the posterior p(x|y). The lower bound instance uses m ≥ d^{0.01} measurements with noise β = Θ(1/log²d), and the hardness is measured in terms of the number of measurements m rather than the ambient dimension d.

## Method

The core construction encodes a one-way function f into the phase of discretized Gaussians: the first d coordinates of the distribution are Gaussians centered at ±R·s_i (encoding the input s), while the last d' coordinates are discretized Gaussians whose phase (0 or ε/2) is determined by f(s)_i. At large smoothing σ ≫ ε√(log d), the two phase-shifted discretized Gaussians become indistinguishable, so the distribution resembles a product distribution with easily computable scores; at small σ, the phase information is preserved. Posterior sampling from this distribution would reveal f(s) from the observed last d' coordinates, thereby inverting f. The authors prove the distribution is well-modeled by constructing polynomial-size ReLU networks that approximate the σ-smoothed scores at all relevant smoothing levels (using piecewise-linear approximations for product distributions and orthant-identification gadgets for small σ), establishing that the hardness is genuinely in posterior sampling rather than in score representation.

## Results

The paper establishes that posterior sampling from diffusion models is computationally intractable under standard cryptographic assumptions. The main lower bound (Theorem 1.8) shows that for m > d^0.01 measurements, (1/10, 1/10)-posterior sampling requires superpolynomial time in d, even though unconditional sampling is provably polynomial-time. Under the stronger assumption that some one-way function requires exponential time to invert (Theorem 1.9), posterior sampling takes 2^Ω(m) time. The matching upper bound (Theorem 1.7) shows rejection sampling achieves a (1/d^C, δ)-posterior sampler in poly(d)·(O(1)/(β√δ))^m time, making it essentially optimal up to a log log d factor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines. The central comparison is between the upper bound (rejection sampling, Theorem 1.7) and the lower bound (Theorems 1.8–1.9). Rejection sampling runs in poly(d)·(O(1)/(β√δ))^m time, while the lower bound shows that under one-way functions, no algorithm can do better than superpolynomial time in d (for m > d^0.01), and under stronger assumptions, no algorithm can beat 2^Ω(m). The paper also implicitly rules out several existing approaches: posterior score approximation methods (Chung et al. 2023, Kawar et al. 2021, Song et al. 2023) are approximations that fail to sample from the true posterior; particle filtering methods (Trippe et al. 2023, Dou & Song 2024) require superpolynomially many particles under one-way functions, and exponentially many under stronger assumptions. The lower bound distribution is specifically constructed to be well-modeled (scores representable by polynomial-size ReLU networks), so the hardness is not an artifact of score representation difficulty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the diffusion model sampling theory of Chen et al. (2023), which establishes polynomial-time unconditional sampling for well-modeled distributions, and the posterior sampling problem formulation from Jalal et al. (2021a) for noisy linear inverse problems. It departs fundamentally from the large body of posterior sampling algorithms (score approximation, replacement methods, particle filtering) by proving a computational lower bound: under the assumption that one-way functions exist, no polynomial-time posterior sampler can exist in general, even when the smoothed scores are well-approximated by a polynomial-size ReLU network. The proof technique of encoding a one-way function into the phase of a discretized Gaussian is a novel construction that bridges cryptography and generative modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This result establishes a fundamental limit: the natural extension from fast unconditional sampling to fast posterior sampling is impossible in general, meaning all existing posterior sampling algorithms (Chung et al., Kawar et al., Trippe et al., Dou & Song) must either approximate the posterior or take superpolynomial time on some inputs. The paper shows rejection sampling's exponential-in-m runtime is essentially optimal, closing the gap between upper and lower bounds up to a log-log factor. The key open question is identifying distributional properties (beyond well-modeledness) under which posterior sampling becomes tractable, which will guide future algorithm design. Practitioners running posterior sampling heuristics daily should understand that their methods' empirical success relies on implicit structure in natural data that the lower-bound distribution lacks.
