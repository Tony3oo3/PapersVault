## Problem

Pixel-wise regression models (monocular depth estimation, optical flow estimation) are widely deployed in security-critical applications like autonomous driving, yet their adversarial robustness under black-box, query-based attacks remains unexplored. Prior adversarial patch attacks on these tasks assume white-box access, and existing black-box patch methods designed for low-resolution classification images (≤32×32) face an exponential scalability barrier when adapted to the high-resolution inputs (1242×375) typical of regression tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of training and validation RGB images (100 and 5 scenes from KITTI, resized to 320×1024 or 384×1280) plus iterative queries to a black-box model that returns pixel-wise regression outputs (1-channel depth or 2-channel flow). It produces a single universal adversarial patch of size 3×h×h (covering 1–3% of the image) that, when attached at a fixed location to any unseen image, degrades the model's per-pixel prediction. The patch is scene-independent and location-independent, requiring only a one-time generation cost of ~50K–300K queries.

## Method

BADPART introduces a square-based patch optimization framework that alternates between probabilistic square sampling and score-based gradient estimation to overcome the high-dimensional search space. In each iteration, a small square sub-area within the patch is selected via a probability distribution derived from the current pixel-wise error map (higher-error regions are sampled more frequently), with a coarse-to-fine size schedule. For the selected square, a batch of b binary noises (±ε) is generated; each noise's impact on attack performance is scored, then scores are normalized and adaptively scaled (dividing by the count of positive/negative elements rather than subtracting the mean, to preserve sign in regression settings). The weighted average of noises, normalized by its l2-norm, yields the gradient estimate used by an Adam optimizer to update the square. This square-level gradient estimation is far more efficient than pixel-by-pixel zeroth-order methods, and the probabilistic sampling focuses computation on the most vulnerable regions of the patch.

## Results

On the 2% (80×80) patch setting, BADPART achieves the best black-box attack performance across all 7 target models: 89.75 DEE (meters) on Monodepth2, 70.38 on DepthHints, 54.14 on SQLDepth, 46.85 on PlaneDepth, 1212.13 EPE (pixels) on FlowNetC, 32.42 on FlowNet2, and 5.26 on PWC-Net. These results approach white-box attack performance on several models (e.g., 89.75 vs. 90.99 DEE on Monodepth2). On the Google 3D Portrait API, BADPART causes a 43.5% relative depth estimation error (reducing mean depth from 0.431 cm to 0.243 cm) using only 50K queries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BADPART outperforms all three primary baselines (GenAttack, HardBeat, Patch-RS) on every model and patch size. Patch-RS (Croce et al., 2022) is the strongest baseline, achieving 78.47 DEE on Monodepth2 and 447.60 EPE on FlowNetC at 2% patch, but BADPART extends these to 89.75 and 1212.13 respectively. HardBeat (Tao et al., 2023) shows moderate performance (19.64 DEE on Monodepth2) but plateaus quickly in query efficiency, being surpassed by BADPART around 120K queries on FlowNet2. GenAttack (Alzantot et al., 2019) is nearly ineffective, producing errors close to zero on most MDE models (e.g., 0.01 DEE on Monodepth2). In the supplementary single-image comparison (Table 9), HPA (Fawzi & Frossard, 2016) and Adv-watermark (Jia et al., 2020) are also substantially outperformed: at 30K queries, BADPART achieves 24.24 DEE on Monodepth2 versus 1.39 for HPA and 2.18 for Adv-watermark, with the latter showing virtually no improvement with additional queries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the square-based perturbation framework from SquareAttack (Andriushchenko et al., 2020) and the score-based gradient estimation ideas from HardBeat (Tao et al., 2023) to the fundamentally different setting of pixel-wise regression tasks, where outputs are dense continuous values rather than discrete class labels and input resolutions are orders of magnitude higher. The key conceptual departure is replacing the random-search paradigm of prior black-box patch attacks with a structured, gradient-estimated optimization over square sub-areas, guided by the pixel-wise error distribution as a domain-specific prior. The paper also draws on the vertex-constrained noise insight from Moon et al. (2019) and the adaptive score scaling from Tao et al. (2023), but re-engineers both for the regression output structure where subtracting mean scores can flip signs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of black-box adversarial patch attacks on pixel-wise regression models, a gap that is practically significant given the security-critical deployment of MDE and OFE in autonomous driving and AR. The demonstration on Google's 3D Portrait API (43.5% relative depth error with 50K queries) and the finding that the SOTA query-based defense Blacklight achieves 0% detection even at 800K queries suggest that current defenses are fundamentally inadequate against universal patch attacks that inject per-sample randomness. Open questions include extending the attack to physical-world settings (viewing angles, lighting, printing), generating stealthy patches suitable for privacy preservation, and developing defenses that can handle the dense, continuous output structure of regression models without degrading benign performance.
