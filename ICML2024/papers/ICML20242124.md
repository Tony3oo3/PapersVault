## Problem

Existing RL-based multiplier design methods train separate agents for fixed scalarized preferences, preventing knowledge sharing across related optimization problems and failing to efficiently cover the entire preference space. The exponentially growing design space and the high cost of synthesis-in-the-loop reward signals make sample efficiency a critical bottleneck for discovering Pareto-optimal circuit designs across all possible area-delay trade-offs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242124/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multiplier specification (input bit width of 8/16/32/64 bits and PPG encoding type: AND-gate or Booth), a compressor tree state encoded as a 3D image tensor of shape K×(Mb+Nb)×ST, and a dynamically generated preference (a 2D weight vector over area/delay plus a synthesis delay constraint). It outputs a set of compressor tree designs approximating the Pareto front across the full preference space, which are converted to Verilog via the EasyMAC encoder and evaluated through OpenROAD synthesis with the Nangate 45nm library.

## Method

HAVE is a two-level hierarchical framework: a Pareto-driven meta-agent (trained with REINFORCE) generates N diverse, complementary preferences per step by tracking the evolving Pareto frontier as state and acting over a decomposed preference space (weight vector + delay constraint), while an adaptive multi-task agent optimizes multipliers conditioned on those dynamic preferences. The multi-task agent uses a shared conditioned encoder (ResNet-18 for the 3D state image concatenated with a normalized preference embedding) feeding into a multi-head MLP decoder, one head per preference, with asynchronous Q-learning over a shared replay memory and a data-relabeling mechanism to transfer transitions across tasks. A key efficiency gain comes from a factored Q-value representation that decomposes the 4×Nc action space into independent column and type sub-actions combined by summation, allowing one sample to update Q-values for multiple compositional actions; the paper proves this factored Bellman operator is a contraction and bounds the approximation error to 2R/(1−γ)². A restore-then-explore strategy (elite-pool state restoration plus random network distillation bonus) further promotes efficient exploration of the large design space.

## Results

On eight multiplier design problems (8/16/32/64-bit, AND-gate and Booth-encoding PPGs), HAVE consistently Pareto-dominates all five baselines, achieving up to 28.08% larger hypervolume over the SOTA RL-MUL and up to 89.05% over Wallace. HAVE also achieves a maximum delay reduction of 10% and a maximum area reduction of 7.93% on individual multiplier designs. When deployed into PE arrays (large-scale DNN accelerator circuits), the gains persist: up to 28.84% hypervolume improvement over RL-MUL and 10.26% delay reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAVE Pareto-dominates all five baselines (Wallace, GOMIL, RL-MUL, Random, RL-Scalar) across all eight multiplier design problems. Against the classical Wallace tree, HAVE achieves up to 89.05% hypervolume improvement (16-bit Booth) and 84.79% on PE arrays. Against the SOTA RL-based method RL-MUL (Zuo et al., 2023), HAVE improves hypervolume by up to 28.08% on multipliers and 28.84% on PE arrays, with maximum individual delay reductions of 10% and area reductions of 7.93%. The GOMIL integer-programming baseline is also consistently dominated in the Pareto front visualizations (Figure 2). The ablation confirms that each component (restore-then-explore, factored Q, multi-task learning, hierarchical meta-agent) contributes incrementally, with the largest single gain coming from the factored Q-value update (FR over R) and the hierarchical meta-agent (HAVE over MFR).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends RL-MUL (Zuo et al., 2023), which applies DQN to compressor tree optimization with synthesis-in-the-loop, by introducing a two-level hierarchical framework: a meta-agent that adaptively generates diverse preferences (weight vectors + delay constraints) and a lower-level adaptive multi-task agent that jointly optimizes across those preferences. It departs from prior RL-based circuit design methods (Roy et al., 2021; Zuo et al., 2023) that train separate agents for fixed scalarized objectives, and from standard MORL approaches that use random preference sampling, by formulating preference generation as an MDP and circuit optimization as an adaptive multi-task problem with shared encoders and a data-relabeling mechanism. The factored Q-value update, which decomposes the flat action space into column and type sub-actions, draws on factored MDP theory (Guestrin et al., 2001; Szita & Lorincz, 2008) but is the first to provide a convergence guarantee for the factored Q-learning variant in this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HAVE is the first framework to approximate Pareto-optimal multiplier designs across the entire preference space in a single training run with high sample efficiency, achieving up to 28% hypervolume improvement over the SOTA RL-MUL and generalizing to large-scale PE arrays used in DNN accelerators. The theoretical error bound for factored Q-learning (Theorem 4.1) provides the first convergence guarantee for this representation, though the factored Q-function space is a proper subspace, implying inherent approximation loss. Open questions include whether the hierarchical meta-agent approach scales to more than two objectives or to other EDA tasks (placement, floorplanning), and whether the simplified single-step bandit formulation of the meta-agent limits its ability to capture long-horizon preference dynamics.
