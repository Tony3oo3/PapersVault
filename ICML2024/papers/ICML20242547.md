## Problem

Prepending safety prompts to LLM inputs is a widely deployed safeguarding practice, yet the underlying working mechanisms of these prompts remain unexplained, preventing their automatic optimization. The paper asks: do safety prompts help models recognize harmfulness, or do they simply increase overall refusal probability? Answering this question from a representation-space perspective is the prerequisite for designing principled prompt optimization methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242547/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a basic textual safety prompt (e.g., LLaMA-2's or Mistral's official prompt) and a small set of 200 controlled harmful/harmless queries as anchor data, and produces an optimized continuous safety prompt of length L (a matrix of trainable embeddings in R^{n×L}) initialized from the token embeddings of the basic prompt. At inference time, the optimized continuous prompt is prepended to the user query, and the frozen LLM generates a response that is either a refusal or a compliance. The optimization operates on the hidden state of the last input token from the top model layer, projected into a 4-dimensional PCA space.

## Method

DRO (Directed Representation Optimization) first anchors a low-dimensional representation space via PCA on hidden states of anchor data (harmful/harmless queries with and without k basic safety prompts), then fits a logistic regression on empirical refusal probabilities to estimate a 'refusal direction' vector w_r in that space. The core optimization treats the safety prompt as continuous trainable embeddings and uses a binary cross-entropy loss that contrasts the refusal logit f_r(x_θ) of the optimized prompt against the initial prompt f_r(x_0), pushing harmful queries' representations along w_r and harmless queries' opposite. A second loss L_h maintains the model's natural harmfulness-recognition capability, and a regularization term L_U (computed via the orthogonal complement of the PCA basis) prevents information loss in the n−m unoptimized dimensions, preserving general generation quality. The key empirical finding motivating this design is that safety prompts do not improve harmfulness distinguishability but uniformly shift representations in a higher-refusal direction, so DRO makes that shift selective based on query harmfulness.

## Results

On the default safety prompt setting, DRO reduces average compliance with harmful queries from 9.8% (basic prompt) to 1.6% on MaliciousInstruct and from 10.3% to 1.4% on AdvBench, while also reducing false refusals on harmless queries from 7.1% to 2.0%. DRO maintains general performance at 63.5% AlpacaEval win rate (vs. 62.5% for the basic prompt and 56.3% for vPT). Under GCG jailbreak attack on AdvBench, DRO further reduces compliance from 13.3% (basic prompt) to 3.1%, while vPT only achieves 9.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DRO against three baselines: no safety prompt, the human-crafted basic safety prompt (default), and vanilla Prompt-Tuning (vPT), which trains continuous prompts via standard cross-entropy on model-generated responses. DRO substantially outperforms vPT on both out-of-domain safety benchmarks (1.6% vs. 5.0% on MaliciousInstruct; 1.4% vs. 6.8% on AdvBench), indicating that vPT's sequence-likelihood objective does not generalize well to unseen harmful queries. DRO also matches or exceeds the basic prompt on AlpacaEval (63.5% vs. 62.5%), whereas vPT degrades general performance to 56.3%, likely due to overfitting to task-specific training data. On the jailbreak setting (GCG attack on AdvBench), DRO achieves 3.1% average compliance versus 9.4% for vPT and 13.3% for the basic prompt, confirming that representation-directed optimization transfers to adversarial perturbations. The ablation further shows that removing the refusal-direction loss L_r causes a large safety drop (7.8% on MaliciousInstruct), while removing the regularization L_U degrades AlpacaEval from 63.5% to 52.6%, validating both components.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the continuous prompt-tuning paradigm (Lester et al., 2021; Li & Liang, 2021) but departs fundamentally from its optimization objective: rather than maximizing sequence likelihood on labeled responses, DRO optimizes in a low-dimensional PCA-anchored representation space along an estimated 'refusal direction.' The empirical analysis in §2 is novel in treating safety prompts as black-box interventions and probing their mechanistic effect on hidden states, a perspective absent from prior LLM safety work that typically focuses on training data or RLHF. The method also connects to the broader jailbreak-defense literature (GCG, AutoDAN) by showing that representation-space optimization generalizes to adversarial suffix attacks, whereas vanilla prompt-tuning does not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that safety prompts do not improve harmfulness recognition but merely shift representations toward higher refusal (causing false refusals on benign queries) reframes the design problem: the bottleneck is not recognition but the refusal decision boundary. DRO's practical appeal—requiring only 200 synthetic queries and no large safety dataset—makes it accessible for practitioners who cannot afford massive alignment training. Key open questions include whether the refusal direction generalizes to ambiguous or persuasive queries (as the authors note), whether the approach scales beyond 7B models, and how it interacts with ongoing safety fine-tuning. The authors themselves caution that safety prompts remain a lightweight complement, not a substitute, for comprehensive alignment.
