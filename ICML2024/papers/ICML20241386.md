## Problem

UNet skip connections, while critical for stable training of diffusion models, act as architectural shortcuts that limit the network's expressivity for the push-forward transformation from Gaussian noise to the data distribution. As sampling steps decrease, the UNet's role converges to this direct mapping, and the skip connections become a bottleneck that no existing ODE sampler can overcome regardless of step count. This paper identifies this architectural limitation and proposes a simple training-free remedy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241386/cropped_page4_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on any pretrained UNet-based diffusion model (EDM, LDM, UViT, Stable Diffusion) by scaling skip vectors during inference, requiring no retraining or architectural modification. It takes standard Gaussian noise (plus a class label for ImageNet or a text prompt for Stable Diffusion) and produces synthetic images at 64×64, 256×256, or 768×768 resolution. The only hyperparameter is the skip coefficient ρ, which is linearly interpolated from a bottom-layer value (e.g., 0.5–0.83) to 1.0 at the top layer, and can optionally vary over the sampling time axis.

## Method

Skip-Tuning introduces per-layer coefficients ρ_i < 1 that scale the down-sampling skip vectors d_i before concatenation with up-sampling vectors u_i, effectively reducing the shortcut signal and forcing the decoder to perform more complex transformations. The ρ values follow a linear interpolation from ρ_bottom to ρ_top=1.0 across the 16 UNet layers, mimicking the reduced skip-to-up norm ratios observed in distilled models. The key insight is that diminishing skip connections monotonically increases the network's gradient norm (a proxy for transformation complexity), which is precisely what the push-forward mapping requires. Empirically, Skip-Tuning improves score-matching losses in discriminative feature spaces (Inception-V3, ResNet-101, CLIP) especially at intermediate noise levels, and reduces the MMD between inverted pseudo-noise and true Gaussian, explaining the FID gains. The method is training-free, orthogonal to any ODE/SDE sampler, and generalizes across architectures and modalities, breaking the ODE-sampling FID limit (1.75 at 19 NFEs vs. 2.2 baseline) and even surpassing EDM-2 (1.57 vs. 1.58) at 39 NFEs.

## Results

Skip-Tuning breaks the ODE sampling limit on ImageNet 64: with only 19 NFEs, Skip-Tuned EDM (ρ: 0.82→1.0) achieves FID 1.75, surpassing the previously established ODE limit of ~2.2. At 39 NFEs, Skip-Tuned EDM reaches FID 1.57, beating the heavily optimized EDM-2-S (FID 1.58) while using fewer NFEs (39 vs. 63) and fewer parameters (296M vs. 280M). In the extreme few-step regime (9 NFEs), Skip-Tuning nearly halves the FID from 5.88 to 2.92 with UniPC sampling. The method generalizes to LDM (FID 4.91→4.67 at 10 steps), UViT (FID 2.32→2.21 at 50 steps), and distilled one-step models (CD: 6.85→5.56; DI: 4.16→3.98).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original EDM baseline, Skip-Tuning consistently improves FID across all tested NFEs: at 19 NFEs (UniPC), FID drops from 2.60 to 1.75; at 39 NFEs, from 2.21 to 1.57. The headline result is that Skip-Tuned EDM (39 NFE, FID 1.57) surpasses EDM-2-S (63 NFE, FID 1.58), a heavily retrained and optimized model, using fewer sampling steps and the same architecture. For LDM on 256×256 ImageNet, Skip-Tuning yields modest but consistent gains (e.g., 4.91→4.67 at 10 steps, 4.25→4.13 at 20 steps), while UViT improves from 2.32 to 2.21 at 50 steps. In the one-step distilled setting, Skip-Tuning improves CD-EDM from 6.85 to 5.56 and DI-EDM from 4.16 to 3.98 (reproduction baselines), confirming that the benefit persists even when skip connections are already implicitly reduced by distillation. Compared to FreeU, which operates in frequency space via wavelet transforms, Skip-Tuning is simpler (no Fourier operations) and produces object/semantic enrichment rather than primarily altering contrast and aesthetics, as shown in the Stable Diffusion 2 text-to-image comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the diffusion probabilistic model framework (Song et al., 2020b) and the UNet architecture (Ronneberger et al., 2015), but departs from the dominant line of work on improving diffusion sampling through better ODE solvers, distillation, or time-step selection. Instead, it targets the network architecture itself, specifically the skip connections that have been considered essential for UNet performance in diffusion training. The closest prior work is FreeU (Si et al., 2023), which also modifies skip connection strengths but from a frequency-decomposition perspective; the paper explicitly contrasts its approach, showing that Skip-Tuning's gains are not explained by frequency-space score matching. Unlike SCedit (Jiang et al., 2023), which adds learned nonlinear projections to skip connections, Skip-Tuning is a purely training-free scalar scaling, making it orthogonal to and composable with any existing sampler.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Skip-Tuning breaks the known ODE-sampling limit for EDM on ImageNet 64 (FID 1.75 at 19 NFEs vs. the ~2.2 ceiling) and even surpasses the heavily optimized EDM-2 (FID 1.57 vs. 1.58) with fewer NFEs, all without any retraining. The finding that pixel-space score matching worsens while feature-space score matching improves—and that naive fine-tuning on the feature-space loss actually degrades performance—challenges the assumption that better pixel-level denoising is the right objective for sampling quality. Open questions include a more complete theoretical explanation for why reducing skip strength increases effective network complexity in a beneficial way, whether the optimal ρ schedule can be learned automatically, and whether the technique extends to non-UNet architectures (e.g., pure transformer backbones without skip connections).
