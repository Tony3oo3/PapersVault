## Problem

In partially-observable decision-making, the optimal policy often requires complex information-gathering strategies (e.g., checking traffic before choosing a route), yet existing POMDP methods that leverage privileged information at training time focus on solving the task and fail to learn *how* to gather the needed information. Prior approaches either imitate an omniscient expert without recovering its information-gathering behavior or rely on generic RL objectives that struggle to discover multi-step exploration strategies, especially when the hidden state changes within an episode.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes a history of observations, actions, and rewards h_t, processed by an LSTM (hidden dim 64) to produce a history embedding. During training, it additionally receives a privileged information vector i_t, which can be the full hidden state, future transitions τ_{t+1:T}, or other designer-specified signals; in the Construction environment, observations are 80×60 RGB images embedded by a 3-layer CNN. The policy outputs a discrete action (e.g., directional movement, bus ride, object interaction) via a recurrent double-deep Q-network conditioned on both the history embedding and a latent encoding z.

## Method

PROBE extends the DREAM meta-RL algorithm to general POMDPs by introducing an information-bottlenecked encoder F_ψ(z|i) that distills task-relevant features from privileged information, a policy π_θ(a|h,z) conditioned on the encoding, and a decoder q_ω(z|h) that reconstructs the encoding from observation history. The core innovation is a per-timestep exploration bonus r_t^PROBE = ‖f_ψ(i_{t+1}) − g_ω(h_t)‖² − ‖f_ψ(i_{t+1}) − g_ω(h_{t+1})‖², derived from a telescoping decomposition of the mutual information between the trajectory and the hidden-state encoding, which rewards the agent for reducing uncertainty about the next hidden state. The total reward r_t + α·r_t^PROBE is optimized via double deep Q-learning, jointly training the policy, encoder, and decoder. The information bottleneck (an ℓ₂ penalty on the encoder output) ensures only task-relevant information drives exploration, making the method robust to excess or noisy privileged features. A variant, PROBE-FUTURE, uses the agent's own future transitions as privileged information, eliminating the need for any external privileged signal while retaining comparable performance.

## Results

PROBE achieves the highest achievable return in all three benchmark POMDP domains (Tiger Door, Light-Dark, Map) after 100K training episodes, matching DREAM and outperforming IMPORT, ELF, and a recurrent DDQN baseline. In the more challenging Non-stationary Map environment requiring repeated information gathering, PROBE achieves a final return of 0.41, compared to 0.33 for IMPORT and 0.29 for ELF. The PROBE-FUTURE variant, which uses future transitions instead of explicit privileged information, performs comparably to PROBE in Tiger Door and even slightly improves learning efficiency. In the Construction vision-based task, PROBE uniquely learns a hedging behavior (waiting before committing to a route) that neither DREAM nor other baselines discover.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DREAM (Liu et al., 2021), PROBE matches performance on the three stationary benchmark domains but substantially outperforms it on all three complex exploration environments. DREAM's single-phase exploration design means it either stops exploring too early (failing when the environment changes in Non-stationary Map and Overcooked) or never stops exploring (failing to make task progress). In Construction, DREAM reads the sign but immediately commits to a route, while PROBE learns to wait and hedge, saving time if the route status changes. Against IMPORT (Kamienny et al., 2020), PROBE wins decisively: IMPORT never learns to gather information and instead makes random guesses in Tiger Door/Light-Dark or takes the slower walking route in Map, achieving only 0.33 in Non-stationary Map versus PROBE's 0.41. Against ELF (Walsman et al., 2022), PROBE also wins: ELF learns quickly initially due to expert action labels but fails to reliably explore, achieving 0.29 in Non-stationary Map. The recurrent DDQN baseline (no privileged information) eventually learns optimal behavior in the simple benchmarks but does so much less efficiently than PROBE or DREAM.

## Relation to Prior Work

PROBE extends the DREAM meta-RL algorithm (Liu et al., 2021), which decouples exploration and exploitation for hidden-parameter MDPs where the unobserved state is fixed within an episode, to the general POMDP setting where the hidden state can change at any timestep. It adopts the informed POMDP formalism (Lambrechts et al., 2023) for incorporating privileged information during training. The key departure from prior POMDP methods (IMPORT, ELF, asymmetric actor-critic) is that those approaches use privileged information to simplify task learning but do not explicitly learn information-gathering strategies; PROBE adds a mutual-information-based exploration bonus on a bottlenecked representation of the privileged information, enabling the agent to discover when and how to explore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a concrete gap: prior POMDP algorithms with privileged information can solve tasks but fail to learn the exploration behaviors needed to recover that information at test time, which is critical in dynamic or non-stationary environments. The PROBE-FUTURE variant, which uses only future transitions (available at no extra cost) as privileged information, is practically appealing since it removes the need for ground-truth hidden states or expert demonstrations. Open questions include scaling to continuous or very large hidden state spaces, the sensitivity to the exploration bonus weight α (which requires manual tuning), and whether the tabular regret bound extends to function-approximation settings.
