## Problem

Reference-model-free online batch selection methods (e.g., train loss, gradient norm) score and select samples independently in a sample-wise manner, which overlooks inter-sample correlations and produces redundant, low-diversity subsets that degrade training performance. While reference-model-based methods (RHO-LOSS, Bayesian) achieve better results, they require costly auxiliary models that are impractical for large-scale pre-training. The paper addresses how to select diverse and representative subsets without any reference model while preserving a principled optimization guarantee.

## Input / Output

At each training step, the method receives a large batch B of N_B samples, the current model f_θ, and a fixed budget N_S (typically 10–30% of N_B). Selection features are the per-sample gradients at the final layer of the network, which are task-agnostic and cheap to compute. The output is a selected mini-batch S ⊂ B of exactly N_S samples used for the SGD update, after which the next large batch is pre-sampled without replacement within the same epoch.

## Method

DivBS defines a group-wise orthogonalized representativeness objective r(S,B,θ) that evaluates the selected subset as a whole: it maximizes the sum of projections of all batch features onto an orthonormal basis of the subspace spanned by the selected samples, thereby subtracting redundant components already captured by other selected samples. Proposition 3.1 shows this objective is invariant to the choice of orthonormal basis, reducing it to a computable form involving only the norm of the projected batch-sum. The auxiliary function r′ (which drops the |E| factor) is proven submodular, normalized, and monotone, so a greedy sequential selection achieves a (1−e^{−1}) approximation guarantee (Proposition 3.3). Algorithm 2 further streamlines the greedy step by orthogonalizing only the running Sum vector rather than all N_B candidates, cutting selection overhead to under 5% of total epoch time while maintaining comparable accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 10% budget, DivBS achieves 94.65% top-1 accuracy, surpassing full-data training (95.50%) by only 0.85% while using 70% fewer iterations. On CIFAR-100 (10% budget), DivBS reaches 73.11% accuracy versus 70.61% for uniform sampling and 77.28% for full-data training. On Tiny ImageNet (10% budget), DivBS achieves 50.84% accuracy, outperforming the next-best baseline (CCS-BS at 49.18%) by 1.66%. DivBS also accelerates convergence, reaching 62% target accuracy on CIFAR-100 in 132 epochs (10% budget) versus 150–174 epochs for all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DivBS consistently outperforms all seven reference-model-free baselines across every dataset and budget ratio. The gap is most dramatic against sample-wise methods: Train Loss (Kawaguchi & Lu, AISTATS 2020) drops to 65.12% on CIFAR-100 (10%) versus DivBS's 73.11%, and SVP (Coleman et al., ICLR 2020) collapses to 31.23% on CIFAR-100 and 19.34% on Tiny ImageNet. Grad Norm (Katharopoulos & Fleuret, ICML 2018) is particularly unstable, achieving only 65.23% on CIFAR-10 (10%), while its importance-sampling variant (Grad Norm IS) recovers to 92.51% but still trails DivBS by 2.14%. The two diversity-aware coreset baselines, Moderate-BS (Xia et al., ICLR 2023) and CCS-BS (Zheng et al., ICLR 2023), are the closest competitors, yet DivBS leads by 2.0–4.4% on CIFAR-100 and 1.7–1.9% on Tiny ImageNet at 10% budget. Against reference-model-based methods (RHO-LOSS, Mindermann et al. ICML 2022; Bayesian, Deng et al. NeurIPS 2023), DivBS significantly narrows the gap on CIFAR-10*/100* without requiring any auxiliary model. InfoBatch (Qin et al., ICLR 2024) performs far below uniform sampling (43.56% vs 70.61% on CIFAR-100 at 10%), attributed to its loss-rescaling instability under small budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

DivBS sits at the intersection of online batch selection (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2015) and submodular set optimization (Nemhauser et al., 1978), departing from prior reference-model-free methods (Train Loss, Grad Norm, SVP) that score samples independently and ignore inter-sample redundancy. It also extends the diversity-aware coreset selection ideas of Moderate (Xia et al., 2023) and CCS (Zheng et al., 2023) from the one-shot offline setting into the online batch selection paradigm, but replaces their heuristic diversity criteria with a principled orthogonalized representativeness objective. The key conceptual departure is treating the selected subset as a whole (group-wise) rather than aggregating independent per-sample scores, and using Gram-Schmidt-style orthogonalization to eliminate redundant contributions before measuring representativeness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DivBS offers a lightweight, reference-model-free selection strategy that consistently narrows the gap to full-data training across five task types (classification, imbalanced learning, segmentation, cross-modal retrieval, LM fine-tuning) with under 5% overhead over uniform sampling, making it practically deployable in large-scale training pipelines. The 1-e^{-1} approximation guarantee provides theoretical grounding that heuristic diversity methods lack. Open questions include whether the additional forward pass can be eliminated via hardware acceleration or feature reuse, whether the method extends to very large batch sizes where the O(N_S · dim) orthogonalization cost becomes non-trivial, and whether the orthogonalization objective generalizes to non-gradient selection features (e.g., activations, embeddings).
