## Problem

Selecting a small representative subset of data for efficient model training or fine-tuning remains challenging: existing coreset-based approaches rely on k-center objectives that are highly sensitive to outliers, provide weak theoretical guarantees under strong distributional assumptions, and are restricted to classification tasks. Moreover, model-based scoring methods (margin, entropy) require expensive inference on the entire dataset, making them impractical for large foundation models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240083/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataset with pre-computed embedding vectors (e.g., from BERT or the model's own layers), oracle access to a loss function, and clustering parameters (number of clusters k, accuracy ε, and per-cluster Hölder constants Λ). It produces a weighted subset S of size O(ε^{-2}) with per-element weights w such that the weighted loss on S is an unbiased estimator of the total loss on D, while making only k queries to the loss function (one per cluster center). The weighted sample can directly replace the full dataset in gradient-based optimization for fine-tuning or regression.

## Method

The core algorithm computes a k-means (or more generally (k,z)-clustering) on the embedding space, queries the loss function only at the k cluster centers, and then samples each data point with probability proportional to a proxy loss defined as the center's loss plus a Hölder-continuous distance term scaled by the per-cluster constant Λ_i. Under the assumption that the loss is (z,Λ)-Hölder continuous within each cluster—a weaker condition than the Lipschitz assumption of prior work—sensitivity sampling yields a coreset whose average loss approximates the full-dataset average within a (1±ε) multiplicative factor plus an additive term proportional to the (k,z)-clustering cost. This additive dependence on clustering cost makes the method inherently robust to outliers, in contrast to k-center-based approaches. For linear regression, the method interprets rows of the design matrix as points, clusters them via k-median, computes the regression solution on the weighted centers, and samples proportionally to clustering distance plus regression residual, achieving performance competitive with leverage score sampling at a fraction of the computational cost.

## Results

On image classification with k=2000 selected points, the proposed sensitivity sampling (Algorithm 1) achieves 92.07% accuracy on MNIST, 81.40% on Fashion-MNIST, and 45.98% on CIFAR-10 (gradient-based variant), outperforming uniform sampling (91.30%, 80.91%, 45.87%) and the k-center coreset of Sener & Savarese (91.34%, 76.92%, 44.91%). For LLM fine-tuning on WMT T2T EnDe (~1% of data), the method improves accuracy from 0.59 (random baseline) to 0.60, covering 9% of the headroom to the full-dataset accuracy of 0.70. On the gas sensor regression task, the clustering-based sampling matches leverage score sampling in R² quality while running 2–3× faster (e.g., 858.5s vs. 1890.7s at n=20,000).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the k-center coreset method of Sener & Savarese (2018). The proposed sensitivity sampling wins decisively on Fashion-MNIST (81.40% vs. 76.92%, a +4.5 pp gain) and on CIFAR-10 (45.98% vs. 44.91%, +1.1 pp), while achieving a modest +0.7 pp on MNIST (92.07% vs. 91.34%). Against uniform sampling, the method also wins on all three datasets, with the largest margin again on Fashion-MNIST (+0.5 pp over uniform, but +4.5 pp over the Sener & Savarese coreset). For LLM fine-tuning on WMT T2T EnDe, the method outperforms both the diversity-based variant of Sener & Savarese and uniform sampling in both accuracy and BLEU score, with results consistent across BERT and SentenceT5 embeddings. On linear regression (gas sensor dataset), the clustering-based sampling matches leverage score sampling in R² score while being substantially faster—k-medoids runs in O(nnz + n) time versus O(nnz + n^ω) for leverage scores, yielding a 2–3× speedup at n=20,000–30,000.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Sener & Savarese (2018), who introduced a k-center-based coreset approach for active learning in CNNs, and on Feldman & Langberg's (2011) sensitivity sampling framework for clustering coresets. The key departures are: replacing the outlier-sensitive k-center objective with k-means/k-median, relaxing the Lipschitz continuity assumption to the weaker per-cluster Hölder continuity condition, and generalizing from classification to arbitrary loss functions including regression and LLM fine-tuning. The sensitivity sampling distribution from the clustering coreset literature is repurposed as the core data-selection mechanism, with the clustering cost providing the additive error term in the guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first data-selection guarantees that require only sublinear model queries (k queries to the loss function) under a natural and empirically validated Hölder continuity assumption, rather than requiring the model to be evaluated on the entire dataset. The practical implication is significant for fine-tuning foundation models, where running inference on millions of data points is prohibitively expensive. Open questions include how the method behaves when embeddings are poor proxies for loss structure, whether the Hölder condition holds for very deep or multimodal models, and whether the modest empirical gains over uniform sampling (e.g., 0.59→0.60 on WMT) grow with model scale or dataset size. The additive error term depending on clustering cost also means the guarantee degrades for highly non-clusterable data.
