## Problem

Online RL with sparse rewards suffers from extremely low sample efficiency, and prior approaches that leverage offline data (e.g., behavior priors, skill extraction) are inherently limited by the fixed nature of the offline dataset. Existing diffusion-based data augmentation methods like SynthER generate only individual transitions rather than full trajectories, under-utilizing the generative capacity of diffusion models and missing the richer temporal structure that full trajectories provide for learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242332/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a current state (proprioceptive vector or 84×84 rendered image) and an optional task prompt (language string or one-hot vector) as conditioning signals, and produces a full synthetic trajectory of variable length containing states, actions, and rewards. For image-level generation, k consecutive frames are concatenated into a single 2D image for the diffusion model, and a two-component decoder (diffusion-layer features + MLP key-point networks) recovers the underlying state, action, and reward from the generated frames. The trajectory length is not fixed at generation time but is determined post-hoc by a length estimator and a non-parametric pruning step.

## Method

ATraDiff fine-tunes a pretrained Stable Diffusion model (or trains a U-Net from scratch for state-level generation) on offline trajectories, with the first frame serving as the generation condition so that synthesized trajectories are relevant to the current learning context. To handle variable trajectory lengths, it employs a coarse-to-precise strategy: multiple diffusion models are trained at different fixed lengths (k=5,10,15,…), a lightweight length estimator selects the appropriate model, and a non-parametric pruner identifies the optimal ending position by maximizing the difference between prefix and suffix averages of adjacent-frame similarity. Training data is preprocessed by appending duplicate final frames to create redundant segments, enabling the pruner to detect where meaningful content ends. During online RL, the diffuser is periodically adapted on newly collected transitions selected via an importance indicator (TD-error or cumulative reward) with a random-dropping pick-up strategy, while a frozen copy of the original model guards against catastrophic forgetting. The entire pipeline is orthogonal to the RL algorithm: it simply augments the replay buffer with synthesized transitions at a controlled ratio ρ, leaving the underlying policy learning untouched.

## Results

On the D4RL Locomotion offline RL benchmark (Table 1), ATraDiff consistently outperforms both SynthER and S4RL across 12 task-dataset combinations for both TD3+BC and IQL. Notable gains include IQL on hopper-replay (84.6 → 103.6) and hopper-expert (106.2 → 113.6), and TD3+BC on hopper-expert (101.1 → 111.2). In the offline-to-online setting (Table 5), ATraDiff achieves 102.3 normalized return on Locomotion (vs. SynthER's 94.6), 0.77 on Meta-World Multitask (vs. 0.57), and 0.81 on Meta-World Sequencetask (vs. 0.62), demonstrating particularly large improvements in complex, distribution-shift-heavy environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SynthER (Lu et al., 2023), the closest prior diffusion-based augmentation method, ATraDiff wins on nearly all tasks: in Table 5, it improves Locomotion from 94.6 to 102.3, AntMaze from 97.3 to 98.7, Meta-World Multitask from 0.57 to 0.77, and Sequencetask from 0.62 to 0.81. Against S4RL (Sinha et al., 2021), ATraDiff outperforms on 10 of 12 D4RL Locomotion tasks for both TD3+BC and IQL, with the largest gap on hopper-replay (IQL: 103.6 vs. 95.6). In the online RL setting (Figure 3), ATraDiff improves both SAC and REDQ across all three locomotion environments, achieving comparable final performance in fewer steps (improved sample efficiency). In the offline-to-online setting (Figures 4–5), ATraDiff boosts all three baselines—SPiRL, PARROT, and RLPD—with the best result in every environment always achieved by a method combined with ATraDiff. On the V-D4RL pixel-based benchmark (Table 2), ATraDiff improves BC by 1.2–17.9 points across walker and cheetah datasets, confirming applicability to visual RL.

## Relation to Prior Work

ATraDiff extends the diffusion-based RL data augmentation paradigm introduced by SynthER (Lu et al., 2023), which generates individual transitions, to the trajectory level, synthesizing full state-action-reward sequences that provide richer temporal context for learning. Architecturally, the image-level variant is built on Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022), fine-tuned to generate multi-frame trajectory images conditioned on the current state and task, with a custom decoder recovering proprioceptive states. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior offline-to-online RL methods (SPiRL, PARROT, RLPD) that extract fixed skills or behavior priors from offline data, instead treating the offline dataset as training data for a generative model that can produce novel, task-relevant experiences during online training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design—augmenting a replay buffer without modifying the RL algorithm—makes ATraDiff broadly applicable across value-based, policy-based, and offline-to-online methods, addressing the long-standing sample-efficiency bottleneck in sparse-reward RL. A key open question is the computational cost: training multiple diffusion models at different lengths roughly doubles training time (e.g., 7h → 16h for SAC), and the authors acknowledge that a single variable-length diffuser would be preferable. The approach also raises questions about the reliability of generated trajectories in safety-critical deployments, and whether the benefits persist in truly open-ended or non-stationary environments beyond the curated D4RL and Meta-World benchmarks.
