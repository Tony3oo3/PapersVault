## Problem

Previous selective classification methods focus exclusively on modifying the classification layer (e.g., adding abstention heads or selection logits) to estimate prediction confidence, leaving the feature representation unoptimized. This paper provides a PAC-Bayesian generalization bound showing that intra-class variance of feature embeddings directly affects selective classification performance, and is the first to explicitly optimize the feature layer for this task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242201/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet and CelebA) and outputs either a class prediction or an abstention decision, with the confidence score computed as the maximum softmax logit (SR). A threshold on SR determines whether the model predicts or abstains, and the selective risk is evaluated at various coverage rates from 10% to 100%. No data augmentation is used for generating contrastive positives, distinguishing it from typical contrastive learning pipelines.

## Method

CCL-SC redefines contrastive positive and negative samples based on prediction correctness rather than label identity: a positive sample is one correctly classified into the anchor's class, while a negative sample is one misclassified into that class. A Confidence-aware Supervised Contrastive (CSC) loss, built on InfoNCE, pulls the anchor's normalized feature embedding toward positives and away from negatives, with the loss magnitude weighted by the model's SR so that high-confidence samples receive stronger gradient signals. Two MoCo-style queues (one for positives, one for negatives) maintained by a momentum encoder provide a large pool of contrastive samples across batches. Training is one-stage: after an initial warm-up period with cross-entropy loss only, the combined loss (w·L_CSC + L_CE) is backpropagated through both the feature embedding layer and the classification layer simultaneously, with the momentum encoder updated via exponential moving average but never directly optimized.

## Results

On CelebA, CCL-SC achieves the lowest selective risk across all 13 coverage rates, e.g., 18.71% at 100% coverage and 4.93% at 50% coverage, outperforming the best baseline SAT+EM+SR (19.04% and 5.51% respectively). On CIFAR-100, CCL-SC achieves the best selective risk at coverage ≥50% (e.g., 26.55% at 100% coverage vs. 26.96% for SAT+EM+SR; 3.95% at 50% vs. 4.08%), with only marginal losses to SAT+SR in the 20%–40% range. On ImageNet, CCL-SC consistently outperforms SAT+EM+SR and SAT across coverages 20%–100% (e.g., 26.26% vs. 27.27% at full coverage; 4.79% vs. 5.57% at 50%), and even a 100-epoch checkpoint (CCL-SC*) remains competitive at 20%–80% coverage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCL-SC outperforms the vanilla SR baseline (Hendrycks & Gimpel, 2017) on all four datasets: 14/0/0 (win/tie/lose) on CelebA, 9/4/0 on CIFAR-100, and consistently lower risk on ImageNet (e.g., 26.26% vs. 27.41% at 100% coverage). Against SAT (Huang et al., 2020) and its entropy-minimization variant SAT+EM (Feng et al., 2023), CCL-SC wins on CelebA (12/2/0 and 7/6/0 respectively) and CIFAR-100 (8/5/0 and 9/4/0), with the gap widening at higher coverages (e.g., 6.83% vs. 7.83% at 60% on CIFAR-100). Deep Gamblers (Liu et al., 2019) is the weakest selective-head baseline, with CCL-SC winning 12/0/0 on CIFAR-100 and 11/3/0 on CelebA. On ImageNet, CCL-SC beats both SAT+EM+SR and SAT at every coverage from 20% to 100%, with the largest gap at 50% coverage (4.79% vs. 5.57% and 6.82%). Against calibration-based methods (Focal Loss, Adaptive Focal, Soft AvUC, Soft ECE, MMCE) and the Human-AI collaboration AUCOC loss (Sangalli et al., 2023), CCL-SC achieves the lowest selective risk at all coverage rates on CIFAR-100 (Table 8), e.g., 6.83% vs. 7.26% (AUCOC) at 60% coverage. The only notable losses are to SAT+SR on CIFAR-100 at 30% and 40% coverage, where the gap is small (≤0.3%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of selective classification work (SelectNet, Deep Gamblers, SAT, SAT+EM) that modifies the classification layer by adding selection heads or abstention logits, and instead optimizes the feature representation layer for the first time. The theoretical motivation comes from a PAC-Bayesian generalization bound (building on Neyshabur et al., 2018) that reveals intra-class feature variance as a key determinant of selective classification generalization. Methodologically, it adapts the MoCo queue-and-momentum-encoder architecture (He et al., 2020) and the InfoNCE contrastive loss (Oord et al., 2018), but redefines positive/negative samples based on prediction correctness rather than label identity, and weights the loss by the model's softmax confidence (SR), distinguishing it from standard supervised contrastive learning (Khosla et al., 2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical justification for why feature-level optimization (not just classification-head design) improves selective classification, and demonstrates that the two perspectives are complementary—CCL-SC can be stacked on top of SAT or EM for further gains. A practical limitation is that the method requires a momentum encoder and two queues, adding training overhead, and the improvements on saturated datasets like CIFAR-10 are marginal. Open questions include whether the intra-class variance bound is tight, whether the approach extends to non-image modalities or out-of-distribution detection, and whether the confidence-weighted contrastive objective can be made more sample-efficient for very large class spaces.
