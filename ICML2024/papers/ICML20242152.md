## Problem

Existing plug-and-play (PnP) methods guarantee convergence only under strong Lipschitz constraints on the denoiser (non-expansiveness, averagedness, or firmly non-expansiveness), which empirically degrade denoising performance. This paper identifies pseudo-contractiveness as a strictly weaker condition that still admits convergent fixed-point algorithms, and provides a practical training strategy to enforce it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242152/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The DRUNet-based denoiser takes a noisy image (64×64 patches during training, full images at test time) and a scalar noise level σ ∈ [0, 60] as input, and outputs a denoised image of the same size. In the PnP restoration loop, the degraded observation (blurred image with known kernel, downsampled image with scale factor s ∈ {2,4}, or noisy image) is iteratively refined by composing the denoiser with a data-fidelity step (proximal or gradient). For the traffic data completion task, the input is a 3D tensor with missing entries and the output is the completed tensor, solved via PnPI-HQS on mode-k unfoldings.

## Method

The key insight is that pseudo-contractiveness of a denoiser D is equivalent to the symmetric part S of its Jacobian J having all eigenvalues ≤ 1, which is a much weaker spectral constraint than the unit-disk or smaller regions required by non-expansiveness or averagedness. To enforce this during training, the paper applies the holomorphic map f(z) = z/(z−2), which bijectively maps the half-plane {Re(z) ≤ 1} to the unit disk, and then constrains the spectral norm ‖f(S)‖* ≤ 1 via a modified power iteration that avoids explicit matrix inversion. For k-strictly pseudo-contractive denoisers, a simpler spectral regularization ‖kI + (1−k)J‖* ≤ 1 is used. At inference, the PnP algorithms (PnPI-GD, PnPI-HQS, PnPI-FBS) employ the Ishikawa two-step iteration with decreasing step sizes to converge strongly to a fixed point of the composite operator, with convergence theorems established under the pseudo-contractive assumption.

## Results

On CBSD68 deblurring with Levin's 8 kernels, PnPI-HQS achieves the best average PSNR of 27.75 dB and SSIM of 0.7797 at σ=12.75, and 26.77 dB / 0.7386 at σ=17.85, outperforming all convergent baselines and even the non-convergent DPIR (27.65 / 0.7738). For super-resolution (s=2, σ=2.55), PnPI-HQS reaches 27.09 dB PSNR and 0.7602 SSIM, again the best among all methods. In standalone denoising, the pseudo-contractive PC-DRUNet matches unconstrained DRUNet (34.14 vs 34.14 dB at σ=15) while the more restrictive MMO and NE-DRUNet drop to 32.74 and 32.97 dB respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PnPI-HQS (using SPC-DRUNet with k=1/2) outperforms all convergent PnP baselines by a clear margin: it beats MMO-FBS by +1.40 dB PSNR, NE-PGD by +1.17 dB, and Prox-DRS by +1.11 dB on deblurring at σ=12.75. It also edges out the non-convergent DPIR by +0.10 dB PSNR and +0.006 SSIM, while providing a guaranteed convergence. PnPI-FBS is competitive with convergent methods (beating NE-PGD by +0.25 dB and Prox-DRS by +0.19 dB) but trails PnPI-HQS by ~0.9 dB. PnPI-GD is the weakest of the three proposed variants, underperforming most baselines on deblurring. In denoising, PC-DRUNet and SPC-DRUNet match unconstrained DRUNet (34.14 vs 34.14 dB at σ=15), while the more restrictive MMO (32.74), NE-DRUNet (32.97), and Prox-DRUNet (33.18) all lose 1–1.4 dB, confirming that pseudo-contractiveness is a much less harmful constraint than non-expansiveness or firm non-expansiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the plug-and-play (PnP) framework for inverse problems by replacing the strong Lipschitz-type assumptions on the denoiser (firmly non-expansive, non-expansive, averaged, contractive) with the strictly weaker pseudo-contractiveness condition, building on Ishikawa's 1974 fixed-point iteration for pseudo-contractive mappings. The training strategy adapts the spectral regularization idea from Pesquet et al. (2021) but applies a novel holomorphic transformation f(z)=z/(z−2) and functional calculi to map the pseudo-contractive half-plane constraint into a tractable spectral-norm constraint on the symmetric part of the Jacobian. The resulting PnPI-GD, PnPI-HQS, and PnPI-FBS algorithms are direct Ishikawa-process extensions of the standard PnP-GD, PnP-HQS, and PnP-FBS iterations, with convergence theorems that require only k-strict pseudo-contractiveness of the denoiser rather than non-expansiveness or averagedness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that pseudo-contractive DRUNet achieves essentially identical denoising PSNR to unconstrained DRUNet (34.14 vs 34.14 dB at σ=15 on CBSD68), while methods enforcing stronger constraints (MMO, NE-DRUNet) lose 1–1.5 dB, suggesting that convergence guarantees need not come at a meaningful performance cost. However, the convergence theorems require the denoiser to map a compact convex set to itself, which is a restrictive assumption not addressed in the experiments; the step-size sequences for Ishikawa iteration (α_n=(n+1)^−a, β_n=(n+1)^−b) are also somewhat rigid in practice. Open questions include whether the holomorphic-training strategy generalizes to architectures beyond DRUNet (e.g., UNet, diffusion models), whether the compactness assumption can be relaxed to closed convex sets, and whether the approach scales to larger training sets and higher-resolution images.
