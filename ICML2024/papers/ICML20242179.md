## Problem

Prior federated offline Q-learning methods (e.g., FedAsynQ) achieve linear speedup but lack pessimism, leaving them vulnerable to overestimation on state-action pairs insufficiently covered by individual agents. It remained unknown whether the principle of pessimism could be implemented in a federated offline RL setting to eliminate overestimation while fully exploiting collaborative coverage across agents, without sharing raw datasets or model estimates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242179/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on M agents, each holding K offline episodes (trajectories of length H) collected by its own behavior policy in a finite-horizon tabular MDP with S states and A actions. Agents never share raw data; they only exchange local Q-tables, value estimates, and visitation counters with a central server at scheduled synchronization points. The output is a global Q-function estimate and an ε-optimal deterministic policy, with the guarantee that individual agents need not cover the full state-action space—only their datasets collectively must cover the optimal policy's trajectory (finite average single-policy concentrability C_avg*).

## Method

FedLCB-Q alternates between local Q-learning updates at agents and pessimistic global aggregation at a server. Three key mechanisms control uncertainty: (1) learning rate rescaling, where rates decay slowly with a global visitation counter but rapidly during local updates, limiting drift of local Q-estimates between synchronizations; (2) importance averaging at the server, where each agent's Q-value is weighted by a function of both its local and the global visitation count, down-weighting rarely-updated (high-uncertainty) local estimates; and (3) a global Hoeffding-style penalty computed from the aggregated visitation counter across all agents, which is less conservative than per-agent penalties and prevents overestimation without restricting the policy to any single agent's behavior distribution. Under an exponential synchronization schedule with rate γ=2/H, the algorithm requires only Õ(H) communication rounds while achieving a sample complexity of Õ(H⁷SC_avg*/(Mε²)), yielding linear speedup in the number of agents.

## Results

FedLCB-Q achieves a sample complexity of Õ(H⁷SC*_avg/(Mε²)) to learn an ε-optimal policy in finite-horizon tabular MDPs, demonstrating linear speedup in the number of agents M. Under exponential synchronization (rate γ=2/H), the algorithm requires only Õ(H) communication rounds, nearly independent of state-action space size and number of agents. The bound nearly matches the single-agent pessimistic Q-learning sample complexity (Shi et al., 2022) up to a factor of H, as if all data were centrally stored, while requiring only collaborative coverage of the optimal policy rather than individual agent coverage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to FedAsynQ (Woo et al., 2023), the closest federated model-free baseline, FedLCB-Q achieves a significantly lower communication cost: Õ(H) rounds versus Õ(HM/d_avg), which grows with both the number of agents and the inverse of the minimum occupancy probability. FedLCB-Q also relaxes the coverage requirement from uniform coverage of the entire state-action space (requiring d_avg > 0) to merely collaborative coverage of the optimal policy's trajectory (requiring C*_avg < ∞), enabling agents to use sub-optimal behavior policies. In terms of sample complexity, FedLCB-Q has a slightly worse H-dependency (H⁷ vs. H⁶ for FedAsynQ) but introduces the single-policy concentrability coefficient C*_avg, which can be much smaller than 1/d_avg when the optimal policy is concentrated. Compared to single-agent pessimistic Q-learning (LCB-Q, Shi et al., 2022), FedLCB-Q achieves linear speedup in M with only an additional factor of H, approaching the minimax lower bound Õ(H⁴SC*_avg/(Mε²)) for centrally-processed data up to a polynomial factor of H³.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: pessimistic Q-learning for offline RL (LCB-Q, Shi et al. 2022) and federated Q-learning (FedAsynQ, Woo et al. 2023). While LCB-Q achieves near-optimal sample complexity in the single-agent offline setting via a Hoeffding-style penalty, and FedAsynQ achieves linear speedup in the federated setting, the latter lacks pessimism and thus risks overestimation on under-covered state-action pairs. The key departure here is showing that pessimism *can* be transplanted into the federated offline setting without sharing raw data or model estimates, by introducing learning-rate rescaling at agents and importance-weighted averaging at the server to control the additional uncertainty from asynchronous local updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result resolves an open question—whether pessimism is compatible with federated offline RL under collaborative (rather than individual) coverage—and provides the first model-free federated offline RL algorithm with a pessimism guarantee and Õ(H) communication rounds. The practical implication is that agents with individually suboptimal, non-expert datasets can still collaboratively learn a near-optimal policy, which is relevant for privacy-constrained multi-agent settings. Limitations include the H^7 sample complexity (vs. H^4 minimax lower bound), restriction to finite-horizon tabular MDPs, and the absence of function approximation or robustness to perturbed environments, all flagged as future work.
