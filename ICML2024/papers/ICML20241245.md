## Problem

High-resolution salient object detection (HRSOD) faces two coupled challenges: fine detail regions (e.g., butterfly tentacles, hair) occupy a tiny fraction of the image and are easily lost during training and undetected by standard metrics, while processing full-resolution images directly incurs prohibitive computational cost. Existing patch-based two-stage methods introduce inter-patch discontinuities, and end-to-end models waste computation on global features at full resolution while atrous convolutions degrade detail fidelity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single high-resolution RGB image (tested at 1280×1280) and produces a full-resolution saliency map at 49 FPS. Internally, the input is consumed at two resolutions: the first stage operates on a downsampled 352×352 version for global localization, while the second stage extracts only two residual-block-level shallow features from the original HR image for lightweight detail refinement, avoiding full backbone processing at high resolution.

## Method

ESNet decouples HRSOD into an evolution stage (LrLM) and a succession stage (HrRM). The evolution stage applies supervision evolution—progressively max-pooling the ground truth to create layer-specific labels that increase the relative proportion of detail regions at higher levels—and feature evolution, where each decoder layer uses the previous layer's saliency prediction as spatial attention and an evolutionary variation loss constrains inter-layer feature differences to match label changes, enforcing coarse-to-fine learning. The succession stage introduces a High-Resolution Trigger that extracts only two ResNet residual blocks from the HR image, applies a Saliency Feature Enhancement Module (SFEM) using prototype-based attention restricted to boundary regions, and injects Sobel-extracted boundary features into the inherited encoder features via residual connections. A Mutually Exclusive Supervision (MES) loss ensures refinement only adds or removes pixels in detail areas without disturbing the object body. The paper also proposes MAE_BD, a boundary- and detail-aware metric that weights errors in inflated boundary masks and high-perimeter-to-area local patches to better discriminate detail quality.

## Results

On HRSOD-TE, ESNet (ResNet-50 backbone) achieves MAE 0.021, F-measure 0.926, S-measure 0.932, MAE_BD 0.166, and FNR 0.053, outperforming all 16 compared methods across all five metrics. The Swin Transformer variant (OURS_swin) further improves to MAE 0.019, F-measure 0.937, and MAE_BD 0.157 on HRSOD-TE. ESNet runs at 49 FPS on 1280×1280 input, substantially faster than HQSOD (<5 FPS at 1024×1024) and PGNet (~30 FPS at 1024×1024). On UHRSD-TE, ESNet achieves MAE 0.038 and F-measure 0.915, while OURS_swin reaches MAE 0.027 and F-measure 0.935.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESNet outperforms the strongest CNN-based HRSOD method HQSOD by 2.1% in F-measure, 12.5% in MAE, and 10.2% in MAE_BD on HRSOD-TE, and by 1.4% in F-measure and 10.2% in MAE_BD on UHRSD-TE. Against the best Transformer-based HRSOD method PGNet, the Swin variant (OURS_swin) achieves a 2.4% MAE_BD gain on HRSOD-TE and a 5.2% MAE_BD gain on UHRSD-TE, while also improving F-measure by 0.9% and 2.1% respectively. Compared to the best NRSOD method PFSNet, ESNet gains 3.3% in F-measure and 36.3% in MAE on HRSOD-TE, confirming that HRSOD-specific architectures are necessary for high-resolution inputs. In inference speed, ESNet reaches 49 FPS at 1280×1280 resolution, roughly 10× faster than HQSOD (<5 FPS at 1024×1024) and ~1.6× faster than PGNet (~30 FPS at 1024×1024), while simultaneously achieving better detection quality. On normal-resolution datasets (DUTS-TE, HKU-IS), ESNet also surpasses all NRSOD and HRSOD baselines, with 3.1% MAE and 1.0% F-measure gains over the second-best model on DUTS-TE, demonstrating strong generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

ESNet builds directly on CTDNet as its base encoder-decoder architecture for the evolution stage, but departs from both dominant HRSOD paradigms: patch-based two-stage models (Zeng et al., 2019; Tang et al., 2021) that crop HR images into patches for local refinement, and end-to-end models (Zhang et al., 2021b; Xie et al., 2022) that process full-resolution images through the entire backbone. Instead, ESNet decouples the task into low-resolution localization (evolution stage) and lightweight high-resolution refinement (succession stage), where both stages process the full image without patch-dividing. The supervision evolution (progressive max-pooling of ground truth) and feature evolution (evolutionary variation loss between adjacent decoder layers) are novel mechanisms that go beyond standard side supervision, while the High-Resolution Trigger reuses only two ResNet residual blocks for shallow HR feature extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 49 FPS inference speed at 1280×1280 represents a substantial practical efficiency gain over prior HRSOD methods (HQSOD <5 FPS, PGNet ~30 FPS), making real-time high-resolution saliency detection feasible. The proposed MAE_BD metric addresses a genuine evaluation gap—traditional metrics like MAE and F-measure are insensitive to the small detail regions that define HRSOD quality. However, the paper acknowledges limitations: performance degrades on skeletonized internal structures, and the approach still depends on high-quality HR annotations. Open questions include whether the two-stage decoupling generalizes to other dense prediction tasks at extreme resolutions, and whether the evolution strategy can be adapted to reduce annotation cost.
