## Problem

The dominant pre-train-then-fine-tune paradigm assumes that once a model is aligned or personalized via LoRA fine-tuning, the original pre-fine-tuning (Pre-FT) weights are irrecoverable. This paper shows that assumption is false: when multiple LoRA fine-tuned variants of the same base model are publicly available (as on Hugging Face or civitai), the exact Pre-FT weights can be recovered without any training data or model inference, undermining safety alignment and personalization guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input n merged LoRA fine-tuned weight matrices (W'_i = W_P + BA, with the low-rank factors A and B not available to the attacker) all originating from the same source model, plus the LoRA rank r (which can be estimated via a linear program over pairwise rank differences). It outputs the recovered Pre-FT weight matrices W_P for each fine-tuned layer, operating independently per layer. The entire procedure is data-free, unsupervised, and requires no forward or backward pass through the model, making it trivially parallelizable across layers and GPUs.

## Method

Spectral DeTuning formulates Pre-FT weight recovery as a non-convex optimization: find a shared matrix W and n low-rank residuals M_i (rank ≤ r) minimizing the sum of squared reconstruction errors across all fine-tuned models. It solves this via iterative coordinate descent with closed-form sub-solutions: the M-step computes each M_i* as the rank-r SVD truncation of (W'_i − W*), and the W-step sets W* to the mean of (W'_i − M_i*). A rank scheduler progressively increases the effective rank from 1 to r using an increase-on-plateau strategy, which the authors show dramatically accelerates convergence and reduces inter-layer variance. A separate linear-programming heuristic estimates unknown or heterogeneous LoRA ranks from pairwise weight differences, and a full-rank detection step identifies and removes LoRAs originating from a different base model.

## Results

Spectral DeTuning recovers pre-fine-tuning weights with dramatically lower error than baselines across all three tested models. On Mistral-7B DPO (n=8 LoRAs), it achieves a W-Error of −22.06 ± 1.18 and SBERT distance of −14.71 ± 3.12, compared to −9.90 and −3.06 for the LoRA FT baseline. On Stable Diffusion 1.5 (n=5 in-the-wild LoRAs from civitai), it reduces LPIPS from 0.514 (LoRA FT) to 0.009 ± 0.006, indicating near-perfect image generation recovery. On ViT (n=5 LoRAs), the activation distance drops from 1e-1 (LoRA FT) to 1e-6 ± 3e-6, demonstrating almost exact weight recovery.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Spectral DeTuning against two baselines: (1) LoRA FT, which uses a single fine-tuned LoRA model as-is, and (2) Mean LoRA, which averages the weights across all fine-tuned LoRA models (motivated by the assumption that the mean of low-rank residuals is zero). Spectral DeTuning outperforms both baselines by a wide margin on every model and metric. On Mistral DPO, it improves W-Error by over 11 points (−22.06 vs. −10.76 for Mean LoRA) and SBERT by over 11 points (−14.71 vs. −3.46). On Stable Diffusion, it reduces LPIPS from 0.482 (Mean LoRA) to 0.009, a 54× improvement in perceptual image quality. On ViT, it reduces activation distance from 5e-2 (Mean LoRA) to 1e-6, a 50,000× improvement. The Mean LoRA baseline consistently provides only marginal gains over a single LoRA model, confirming that simple weight averaging is insufficient to undo the fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LoRA (Hu et al., 2021), whose low-rank update structure W' = W_P + BA is the mathematical foundation of the entire attack. It departs from prior model-extraction work (Tramer et al., 2016; Shafran et al., 2023), which targets models hidden behind APIs, and from red-teaming/adversarial attacks on aligned LLMs (Carlini et al., 2023; Wei et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2023), which attempt to recover pre-fine-tuning *capabilities* through inference. Spectral DeTuning instead recovers the exact pre-fine-tuning *weights* in a data-free, inference-free manner using iterative SVD-based low-rank factorization with coordinate descent, a classical optimization toolkit repurposed for this new security problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work challenges a widely-held security assumption: that publishing only LoRA fine-tuned variants of a model effectively hides the original (potentially unsafe) pre-fine-tuning weights. Given the growing ecosystem of public LoRA marketplaces (HuggingFace, civitai) where multiple flavors of the same base model are routinely released, the practical exposure is immediate and broad. The paper acknowledges no known defense exists and that once a vulnerability is identified in publicly available models, retraction is impossible. Open questions include extending the attack to full fine-tuning (not just LoRA), reducing the number of required LoRA variants, and developing principled defenses such as training protocols that make recovery infeasible.
