## Problem

Numerical simulation of PDEs on fine unstructured grids is computationally prohibitive, especially when multiple parameter sweeps are needed. Traditional upscaling methods coarsen grids based solely on coefficient averaging without leveraging modelled data, while reduced-order modelling approaches require specific data structures (eigenvectors, temporal frequencies) and do not directly optimize grid geometry. No prior method provides an end-to-end differentiable pipeline that directly optimizes unstructured grid point locations to minimize simulation misfit in a self-supervised manner.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D point cloud of N site points with associated discrete permeability values and a polygonal domain boundary as input, along with fixed source and measurement point locations. It produces a coarsened point cloud of n < N optimized site locations (with averaged permeabilities) that, when Voronoi-tessellated, yields a coarser unstructured grid. Source and measurement points are held fixed during optimization; only the remaining site coordinates are updated. The degree of reduction r = n/N is a user-specified hyperparameter, and the method has been demonstrated at reductions up to 90× (90,000 → 1,000 points).

## Method

The core contribution is making the entire simulation pipeline differentiable so that gradients from a loss function propagate back to grid point positions. A differentiable Voronoi tessellation separates the non-differentiable Delaunay triangulation (which provides only adjacency indexing) from the differentiable computation of geometric parameters (edge lengths, cell areas via the Shoelace formula and circumcenter formulas). The finite volume solver is implemented as a message-passing graph neural network (PyTorch Geometric), where edge weights encode the FVM system matrix entries and the explicit Euler update is a differentiable tensor operation. Coarsening begins with k-means clustering plus mean pooling as an initialization, then stochastic gradient descent (Adam) iteratively optimizes site coordinates to minimize the MSE between pressure time series at measurement points on the fine and coarse grids. The approach is self-supervised: the fine-grid simulation itself provides the target, requiring no external training data.

## Results

The method reduces the number of grid points by up to 90× (90,000 → 1,000) while achieving a 34× simulation speedup. After 60 optimization epochs, the coarsened grid achieves an RMSE of ≈0.042 at the measurement point, compared to 0.057 for the k-means + average pooling baseline. The approach generalizes to both parabolic (Darcy/porous media flow) and hyperbolic (wave equation) PDEs, and in a real-world petroleum engineering scenario with 4 measurement points, the proxy model accurately predicts pressure beyond the training time horizon.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is k-means clustering combined with arithmetic mean pooling, which serves as the initial grid coarsening before optimization. The proposed differentiable optimization pipeline consistently outperforms this baseline: for the 90,000 → 1,000 reduction, the optimized grid achieves RMSE ≈ 0.042 versus 0.057 for k-means + average pooling (a ~26% relative improvement). Across multiple reduction degrees (r = 0.75, 0.5, 0.25), the optimized method produces pressure time series that track the fine-grid ground truth more closely than the unoptimized k-means baseline. The paper does not compare against other reduced-order modeling techniques (POD, DMD) or mesh adaptation methods (Fidkowski & Chen, 2021) quantitatively, positioning its contribution as the first autodifferentiation-based unstructured grid coarsening rather than a direct benchmark against existing ROM or upscaling approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of differentiable physics simulation and mesh coarsening. It departs from traditional upscaling (which averages coefficients without using modelled data) and from ROM methods like POD/DMD (which use modelled data but don't modify the grid geometry). The closest prior work is Fidkowski & Chen (2021), which uses neural networks for mesh anisotropy optimization but requires Hessian matrices and external training data. The authors' key conceptual departure is making the entire pipeline—Voronoi tessellation, finite volume solver, and loss—end-to-end differentiable, enabling gradient-based optimization of point locations without any external supervision or Hessian evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for reservoir and groundwater modeling where grids contain millions of cells and repeated simulations are standard practice; a 34× speedup with modest accuracy loss is directly useful. However, the method is currently limited to 2D, explicit time-stepping (with its associated stability restrictions), and finite volume discretization, leaving open whether it extends to 3D, implicit schemes, or non-linear PDEs. The self-supervised formulation (no external training data) is a strong property, but the optimization is sensitive to the number of epochs and the choice of measurement points, and the paper does not provide a systematic study of when coarsening fails or degrades.
