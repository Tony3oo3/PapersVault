## Problem

Weighted low rank approximation (WLRA) is NP-hard, and prior approaches rely on heuristics, bicriteria guarantees, or fixed-parameter tractable algorithms that are computationally expensive. No simple algorithm with provable relative-error guarantees existed for the natural case where the weight matrix has low rank, and the communication complexity of distributed WLRA was entirely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes an input matrix A ∈ R^{n×d}, a non-negative weight matrix W ∈ R^{n×d} of rank r, and a target rank k. The output is a matrix Ã that is not itself low rank but can be stored in O((n+d)rk) parameters as the entrywise product of W^(∘-1) and a rank-rk matrix. For structured weight matrices (low-rank plus sparse, diagonal, block diagonal, banded, or monotone missing-data patterns), the entrywise inverse can be applied in time proportional to the structure size rather than the full matrix dimension.

## Method

The core insight is that if rank(W) ≤ r and rank(A') ≤ k, then rank(W∘A') ≤ rk (Lemma 2.1), so the optimal rank-k WLRA solution, when entrywise-weighted by W, has rank at most rk. The algorithm simply computes a rank-rk approximation of W∘A (via SVD or randomized SVD) and then divides entrywise by W to produce Ã = W^(∘-1) ∘ (W∘A)_rk, yielding a κ-approximate WLRA solution with κ = 1+ε in near-linear time. This reweighting approach also enables column subset selection for weighted feature selection and yields nearly tight Θ(rsk) communication complexity bounds for distributed WLRA, establishing that the rank of W parameterizes the communication cost. For practical model compression, the Fisher information matrix serves as W and is empirically near rank-1, making the algorithm extremely efficient.

## Results

On the model compression task (Fisher-weighted WLRA at rank 20), the proposed svd_w algorithm achieves weighted Frobenius losses of 0.0157 (MNIST), 0.0689 (Fashion-MNIST), 0.0023 (SmallNORB), and 0.0092 (Colorectal Histology), nearly matching the best-performing EM baseline (0.0149, 0.0627, 0.0019, 0.0090) while running 20–28× faster (e.g., 1.06 s vs. 26.1 s on SmallNORB; 7.2 s vs. 206.1 s on Colorectal Histology). The Fisher weight matrices are empirically near-rank-1, with 95–99.9% of Frobenius mass in the first singular value across all four datasets, validating the low-rank W assumption.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the EM algorithm of Srebro & Jaakkola (2003), svd_w achieves within ~5% loss on MNIST/Fashion-MNIST and within ~20% on SmallNORB, but at 20–28× lower running time (e.g., 1.06 s vs. 26.1 s on SmallNORB); the paper further shows that initializing EM with the svd_w solution yields the best loss–time trade-off. Against the greedy basis pursuit of Bhaskara et al. (2021), svd_w wins on all four datasets in loss (e.g., 0.0023 vs. 0.0131 on SmallNORB) while also being faster. Against the row-norm sampling method of Dai (2023), svd_w dominates by a wide margin, particularly on SmallNORB where the loss gap is 0.0023 vs. 0.3978 (a ~170× difference). The unweighted SVD baseline is consistently worse in loss, confirming that incorporating the Fisher weights is essential for model compression quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the fixed-parameter-tractable algorithms for WLRA by Razenshteyn et al. (2016) and Ban et al. (2019), which showed that the rank r of the weight matrix W parameterizes computational complexity. The key departure is a new relaxation: rather than requiring the output to be low rank (which is NP-hard to approximate), the paper outputs a 'reweighted' matrix W^{∘-1}∘(W∘A)_{rk} that is not necessarily low rank but is storable in O((n+d)rk) parameters. The communication complexity results build on column subset selection techniques from Boutsidis et al. (2016), and the empirical model compression setting follows Hsu et al. (2022) and Hua et al. (2022), who proposed using Fisher information matrices as weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that the rank of the weight matrix W tightly parameterizes the communication complexity of WLRA (Θ(rsk) bits), providing the first distributed complexity characterization for this problem. The algorithm's extreme simplicity—compute an SVD of W∘A and divide entrywise by W—makes it immediately deployable, and the empirical validation that Fisher information matrices in model compression are nearly rank-1 (95–99.9% mass in the top singular value) gives strong practical justification for the low-rank W assumption. Open questions include extending guarantees to general (non-low-rank) weight matrices beyond the structured families covered, and whether the reweighted solution can be further compressed to true low-rank form without losing the approximation guarantee. The empirical evaluation is limited to small MLPs on image classification, leaving open whether the approach scales to the large LLMs that motivated the application.
