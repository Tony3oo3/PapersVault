## Problem

Prior work demonstrated that diffusion models can compose known attributes in novel combinations, but it remained unexplored whether they can interpolate between extreme values of a continuous latent factor that were never observed during training. This paper asks whether a DDPM trained exclusively on clearly smiling and clearly non-smiling faces can generate mildly smiling faces without any reference images, going beyond the compositional support of the training distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240421/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes Gaussian noise as input and produces 64×64 (or 128×128) RGB images whose attribute values fall in the unobserved intermediate range. During sampling, the model consumes scores from multiple guidance classifiers simultaneously (e.g., both the 'smiling' and 'non-smiling' classifiers), rather than a single class score as in standard classifier guidance. Training data is pre-filtered to contain only extreme examples (e.g., the top and bottom 5k–60k samples by classifier confidence), creating a clear gap in the latent attribute space that the model must bridge at inference time.

## Method

The core contribution is a sampling procedure called multi-guidance, which generalizes standard classifier guidance by summing the scores of multiple classifiers (one per class) weighted by guidance strengths λ_km, effectively sampling from a product distribution p_Π(x) ∝ p(x)∏ p_k(y_m|x)^λ_km. The DDPM itself is trained with the standard Nichol & Dhariwal recipe (U-Net, cosine noise schedule, learned variance, L_hybrid objective) on the filtered extreme-only dataset, with no architectural or training modifications. At inference, using the scores from both the positive and negative class classifiers simultaneously steers the denoising trajectory toward the middle of the attribute spectrum, producing samples with intermediate values. The authors validate this across architectures (U-Net, diffusion transformer), guidance schemes (classifier and classifier-free), attributes (smile, age, hair color, object size), and datasets (CelebA, ImageNet, synthetic), and show that spectral normalization on the guidance classifier smooths predictions in the unseen region, improving interpolation accuracy from ~78% to >90% on a controlled synthetic task.

## Results

On CelebA smile interpolation, multi-guidance sampling from a DDPM trained exclusively on extreme (clearly smiling/non-smiling) examples achieves an MSE of 0.13 and KLD of 0.61 against a uniform distribution, compared to 0.56/3.17 for the training distribution and 0.64/3.5 for unconditional sampling. The interpolation ability persists with as few as 5k training examples (MSE 0.279, KLD 1.185) and is robust to guidance strength λ ∈ [3.5, 100]. On a controlled synthetic dataset, multi-guidance achieves 78% ± 8.7% accuracy in generating samples in the unobserved middle region, rising above 90% with spectral normalization on the classifier.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is an empirical study that does not benchmark against competing interpolation or generation methods; instead, it compares three internal conditions: the extreme training distribution, unconditional DDPM sampling, and the proposed multi-guidance sampling. Multi-guidance dramatically outperforms both the training distribution (MSE 0.13 vs. 0.56; KLD 0.61 vs. 3.17) and unconditional sampling (MSE 0.13 vs. 0.64; KLD 0.61 vs. 3.5) in producing samples with intermediate attribute values. The paper also verifies that the interpolation is not due to memorization (via CLIP nearest-neighbor checks) or data leakage (via ensemble filtering with estimated error probability < 0.09 for ≥10 leaked samples). Results generalize across attributes (smile, age, hair color), architectures (U-Net and diffusion transformer), and sampling schemes (classifier guidance and classifier-free guidance), confirming the phenomenon is not an artifact of a specific setup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the compositional generalization framework of Wiedemer et al. (2023) and Okawa et al. (2023), which studied whether diffusion models can compose attributes in novel configurations, by demonstrating a strictly stronger capability: zero-shot interpolation beyond the compositional support of the training data. The multi-guidance sampling procedure generalizes standard classifier guidance (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) by summing scores from multiple classifiers simultaneously, drawing on the product-distribution score decomposition from energy-based model literature (Liu et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2023a). The base model follows the training recipe of Nichol & Dhariwal (2021), and the paper explicitly positions its contribution as distinct from prior interpolation work (Yue et al., 2024; Kim et al., 2022; Wang & Golland, 2023), which did not constrain the training distribution to extreme examples only.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that DDPMs can generate intermediate-attribute samples without ever observing them during training has direct implications for fairness (generating underrepresented attribute values), medical imaging (rare pathologies), and understanding the inductive biases of diffusion models. Key limitations include the ill-defined nature of interpolation for some attributes (e.g., blond-to-black hair), the restriction to 64×64 images, and the reliance on classifier-based proxies rather than ground-truth continuous labels. Open questions include whether spectral normalization and other regularization can be systematically leveraged, whether zero-shot extrapolation beyond the training support is achievable, and whether these findings extend to text-conditioned and higher-resolution models.
