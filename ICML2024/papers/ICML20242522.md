## Problem

Theoretical understanding of IRL lags far behind standard RL: no provably sample-efficient algorithms existed for IRL in standard offline or online settings, and no appropriate performance metric existed for measuring the quality of recovered reward sets. The inherent non-uniqueness of IRL solutions (zero reward is always feasible for any expert policy) makes the problem structurally different from standard RL, raising the open question of whether IRL is fundamentally harder. This paper provides the first line of results showing IRL is 'not much harder than standard RL' in a precise sense.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline dataset of K trajectories where each trajectory provides state-action pairs from a behavior policy π^b plus expert feedback (either the expert's sampled action or a binary indicator of whether the behavior action lies in the expert's support), along with a parameter set Θ ⊂ V̄ × Ā. It produces an estimated reward mapping R̂: (V,A) → r that serves as a 'generating function' for the feasible reward set, satisfying both a small D_Θ^π distance to the ground truth mapping and a pointwise monotonicity condition (R̂(V,A) ≤ R*(V,A) for all (h,s,a)). The reward mapping parameterization means the output is not a single reward but a structured family indexed by value and advantage functions.

## Method

The core technical contribution is seamlessly adapting the pessimism principle from offline RL to IRL. The RLP algorithm estimates the transition kernel and expert policy empirically, then constructs the estimated reward by subtracting a Bernstein-based bonus from the empirical reward formula, which guarantees pointwise monotonicity (estimated reward ≤ true reward) with high probability. This monotonicity, combined with a small d^π distance under a near-optimal policy, yields planning guarantees via a new proposition (Proposition 6) showing that learning with the estimated reward incurs bounded suboptimality. For the online setting, RLE reduces to a reward-free exploration subroutine (from Li et al., 2023) that finds a behavior policy with uniform concentrability, followed by RLP on the collected data. The paper also introduces new performance metrics (d^π and D_Θ^π) based on value-function differences under visitation distributions, which are provably stronger than existing Hausdorff-based metrics and better suited to offline/online settings where not all states are visitable.

## Results

The paper establishes the first provably sample-efficient IRL algorithm in the offline setting (RLP), requiring K ≥ Õ(H⁴SC* log N / ε² + H²SC*η/ε) episodes to achieve D_Θ^{π^eval}(R*, R̂) ≤ ε with monotonicity. In the special case π^eval = π^E, the rate improves to Õ(H³SC* log N / ε²). For the online setting, RLE achieves the stronger D_Θ^all metric with K + NH ≥ Õ(H⁴SA log N / ε² + H²SAη/ε) episodes. Information-theoretic lower bounds (Theorems 10, 12) show these rates are nearly tight up to H and polylog factors when S ≤ A.

| Setting | Metric | Upper Bound (episodes) | Lower Bound (episodes) | Tightness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Offline (general π^eval) | D_Θ^{π^eval} | Õ(H⁴SC* log N / ε²) | Ω(H²SC* min{S,A} / ε²) | Tight up to H², log, S vs min{S,A} |
| Offline (π^eval = π^E) | D_Θ^{π^E} | Õ(H³SC* log N / ε²) | — | — |
| Online (RLE) | D_Θ^all | Õ(H⁴SA log N / ε²) | Ω(H³SA min{S,A} / ε²) | Tight up to H, log, S vs min{S,A} |

*Here C* is the single-policy concentrability coefficient, log N = log N(Θ; ε/H), η = Δ⁻¹·1{option 1}, and Õ hides polylog(H,S,A,1/δ) factors.*

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is with Lindner et al. (2023) in the online IRL setting. Lindner et al. achieve Õ(H⁴S²A/ε² + H²SAη/ε) episodes in their D^L metric, while RLE achieves Õ(H⁴SA log N/ε² + H²SAη/ε) in the strictly stronger D_Θ^all metric (Theorem 7 shows D_Θ^all ≥ D^L, with the inequality being strict). For Θ = V̄ × Ā, RLE's rate is Õ(H⁴SA²/ε²) versus Lindner's Õ(H⁴S²A/ε²), so RLE is better when A < S and comparable otherwise, but crucially in a stronger metric. In the offline setting, RLP is the first algorithm with any sample complexity guarantee; no prior work (Metelli et al. 2021, 2023) provides results for the standard offline setting as their guarantees critically rely on simulator access. The paper also shows that the pessimism principle from offline RL transfers seamlessly to IRL, yielding rates that mirror standard offline RL up to a log N factor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the pessimism principle from offline RL (Jin et al., 2021) to the IRL setting, showing that the same Bernstein-based bonus construction that yields near-optimal policies in offline RL also yields monotone, accurate reward mappings. The reward mapping formalism (Definition 1) is a direct restatement of the classical Ng & Russell (2000) characterization of feasible rewards, but the paper elevates it to a first-class learning target with a new metric (D_Θ^π) that is provably stronger than the Hausdorff-based metrics used by Metelli et al. (2021, 2023) and Lindner et al. (2023). For the online setting, the algorithm reduces to reward-free exploration (Li et al., 2023) followed by the offline RLP subroutine, a clean compositional design that prior IRL theory had not exploited.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provably sample-efficient IRL algorithm in the standard offline setting, resolving a gap that had persisted since the simulator-based results of Metelli et al. The conceptual takeaway—that IRL is 'not much harder than standard RL' in the tabular episodic setting—reframes how the community thinks about the difficulty of reward recovery. Key limitations include the restriction to tabular MDPs, the need for a concentrability assumption, and the min{S,A} gap between upper and lower bounds. Open questions the authors flag include extension to function approximation, empirical validation, and whether the lower-bound gap can be closed.
