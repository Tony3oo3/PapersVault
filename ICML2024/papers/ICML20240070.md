## Problem

Offline learning agents trained on limited demonstrations suffer from causal confusion: they pick up spurious correlations between independent environment entities and fail catastrophically when those correlations break at test time. Existing counterfactual augmentation methods either require online environment interaction, rely on heuristic causal discovery (e.g., transformer attention weights), or depend on model rollouts that compound approximation errors. The paper asks whether a principled, fully offline method can generate dynamically feasible counterfactual samples that break spurious correlations without additional environment access.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed offline trajectory dataset with a known state-space factorization into N entity subspaces (e.g., 51-dim Franka-Kitchen states decomposed into robot joints, kitchen items, and goals) and produces an augmented dataset of counterfactual transitions (s, a, s') where action-unaffected entity states are swapped from other trajectories. The augmented data is consumed by any downstream policy learning algorithm (LMP, TD3, TD3+BC) at a configurable ratio of observed-to-counterfactual samples per batch. A key constraint is that the entity factorization must be provided a priori, and the method assumes no direct object-to-object causal edges in the local causal graph.

## Method

CAIAC identifies action-unaffected entities via Causal Action Influence (CAI), a state-conditioned mutual information measure computed as the KL divergence between the conditional next-state distribution P(S'_j | S=s, A=a) and the marginal P(S'_j | S=s), estimated using a Gaussian MLP transition model and K=64 sampled actions. Entities with CAI below a threshold θ form the uncontrollable set U_s, and counterfactuals are generated by swapping entity transitions (s_i, s'_i) between two trajectories that share at least one uncontrollable entity. The critical inductive bias is that object-object interactions are assumed sparse, reducing the intractable full causal discovery problem to the tractable action-influence detection problem. This contrasts with CODA, which uses transformer attention weights as a heuristic proxy for influence, and with model-based approaches that require autoregressive rollouts. The method operates as a plug-in preprocessing module: counterfactuals are precomputed offline and mixed with real data during training, requiring no modification to the downstream learning algorithm.

## Results

On the Franka-Kitchen benchmark with OOD initial configurations, CAIAC achieves 0.81 success rate on Kettle and 0.75 on Microwave, substantially outperforming CoDA (0.18, 0.07), CoDA-action (0.16, 0.0), and No-Augm. (0.07, 0.01). On Fetch-Pick&Lift with 4 cubes (spurious correlations in training, random positions at test), CAIAC reaches a high success rate while all baselines exhibit subpar performance. In the Fetch-Push low-data regime (20% of data), CAIAC significantly outperforms No-Augm. and all counterfactual baselines, which actually degrade performance due to infeasible sample generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAIAC decisively outperforms CoDA (Pitis et al., 2020) across all Franka-Kitchen tasks, with the largest gap on Microwave (0.75 vs. 0.07) and Kettle (0.81 vs. 0.18); the paper attributes CoDA's failure to transformer attention weights being unable to recover the correct local causal graph in high-dimensional settings. CoDA-action, which restricts influence detection to action-to-object edges only, performs even worse than full CoDA on most tasks (e.g., 0.0 vs. 0.07 on Microwave), as its heuristic misestimates influence and generates dynamically infeasible counterfactuals. On Fetch-Pick&Lift, CAIAC also beats RSC (Ding et al., 2023), whose causally-uninformed state perturbation heuristics break true cause-effect relationships and whose learned dynamics model lacks theoretical causal guarantees. In the model-based comparison (Appendix B.2), CAIAC+MBPO outperforms MBPO on Fetch-Pick&Lift but underperforms on Fetch-Push, suggesting that the small fraction of infeasible counterfactuals introduces compounding errors in model rollouts. Overall, CAIAC wins on 6 out of 7 Franka-Kitchen tasks and on both Fetch benchmarks, with the only unsolved task (Hinge cabinet) being unsolved by all methods due to missing demonstrations from the initial configuration.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAIAC builds directly on the Local Causal Model (LCM) framework of Pitis et al. (2020, CODA), which introduced counterfactual data augmentation by swapping entity states across trajectories based on a locally factored causal graph. The key departure is replacing CODA's heuristic reliance on transformer attention weights for causal influence detection with a principled explicit measure—state-conditioned mutual information (CAI) from Seitzer et al. (2021)—and further simplifying the problem by assuming sparse object-object interactions, thereby reducing full local causal discovery to action-influence detection alone. This positions CAIAC as a theoretically grounded, offline-only module that avoids both environment interaction and model-based counterfactual rollouts, distinguishing it from prior causal RL work that operates in online or interventional settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that principled causal influence detection can substantially outperform heuristic alternatives (transformer attention weights) for creating dynamically feasible counterfactuals, directly addressing causal confusion in offline robot learning. However, the core assumption of negligible object-object interactions limits applicability to settings where the agent is the sole causal driver of entity states, and the method requires a known state-space factorization into entities. Open questions include extending the approach to settings with significant inter-object dynamics, automatically learning entity factorizations, and rebalancing data distributions to counteract imbalances—directions the authors explicitly flag for future work.
