## Problem

Existing Crowdsourced Multi-Label Inference (CMLI) methods require crowdsourced labels on both training and testing sets, focus on inferring ground-truth labels rather than learning a generalizable predictor, and provide no theoretical guarantees. This paper tackles the underexplored Crowdsourced Multi-Label Learning (CMLL) problem: how to train a robust multi-label classifier end-to-end from unreliable crowdsourced annotations alone, with provable risk consistency and generalization bounds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242219/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes d-dimensional image feature vectors paired with M crowdsourced binary label vectors (one per annotator, each of length K) as training input; ground-truth labels are never accessible. At test time, only the feature vector is provided and the model outputs a K-dimensional multi-label prediction. A key practical detail is that the M per-annotator labels are first aggregated into a single averaged label vector s_k = (1/M)Σ_m ỹ_mk, which serves as the noisy supervision signal throughout training.

## Method

CLEAR establishes the first unbiased risk estimator for CMLL by inverting class-dependent, instance-independent transition matrices that model how each annotator corrupts ground-truth labels. A key theoretical result (Theorem 2) shows that aggregating M annotator labels yields a single aggregated transition matrix per class (a weighted average of individual matrices), reducing the estimation burden from M×K to K matrices. The architecture is a decoupled autoencoder with two VAEs: a feature VAE encoding x and a label VAE encoding progressively denoised labels s′, both decoding through Multivariate Probit Models to reconstruct the aggregated label s under the unbiased loss. Label correlations are exploited by distilling the label VAE's latent Gaussian into the feature VAE via KL divergence and MSE losses. Transition matrices are estimated using an anchor-point strategy that averages the model-predicted probabilities of the top-C most confident samples per class, and a Rademacher-based generalization bound (Theorem 3) guarantees convergence of the empirical risk minimizer.

## Results

Under the asymmetric noise setting (T̄₀₁ᵏ=0.2, T̄₁₀ᵏ=0.5), CLEAR achieves 0.7267 Example-F1 on Mirflickr, 0.6055 on Scene, and 0.1968 on NUS-WIDE, outperforming the best baseline by 28.64%, 24.0%, and 137% respectively. Across all three noise scenarios and five datasets, CLEAR improves over the naive BCE baseline by an average of 12.49% (Example-F1), 11.89% (Micro-F1), and 10.03% (Macro-F1). CLEAR also consistently outperforms all baselines as the number of annotators increases from 3 to 15, while most baselines degrade.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLEAR outperforms all six baselines on the majority of dataset-metric combinations across all three noise scenarios. Against the naive BCE baseline, CLEAR gains an average of ~12% on Example-F1, confirming the value of the unbiased risk estimator over direct training on noisy labels. The crowdsourcing-based methods MV and DoctorNet are particularly outperformed under heavy noise (T̄₁₀ᵏ=0.5): on Mirflickr, CLEAR achieves 0.7267 Example-F1 versus 0.4403 for DoctorNet and 0.4344 for MV, because these methods naively trust majority-voting labels or overfit to error labels. Against the MLL baselines, CLEAR beats MPVAE by a large margin (e.g., 0.6055 vs. 0.3656 on Scene Example-F1) and ML-KNN by an even wider gap, demonstrating robustness to unreliable crowdsourced information. The closest competitor is PML-NI (partial multi-label learning), which CLEAR surpasses on most datasets but narrowly trails on NUS-WIDE Macro-F1 under the T̄₀₁ᵏ=0.5, T̄₁₀ᵏ=0.2 setting (0.0491 vs. 0.0584), suggesting PML-NI's redundant-label modeling provides a slight edge in that specific regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLEAR extends the unbiased risk estimator framework from single-label noisy label learning (Patrini et al., 2017; Xia et al., 2019) to the multi-label crowdsourcing setting, where prior work (Zhang & Wu, 2018; Li et al., 2019) only addressed label inference rather than predictor learning. The decoupled autoencoder architecture draws directly from MPVAE (Bai et al., 2020) for capturing label correlations via multivariate probit models, while the transition matrix estimation follows the anchor-point paradigm. The key conceptual departure is the aggregated transition matrix formulation (Theorem 2), which reduces M×K individual matrices to K aggregated ones by modeling annotator contributions as a linear mixture, enabling a practical unbiased objective without per-annotator modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide a theoretically grounded (risk-consistent, generalization-bounded) method for learning multi-label predictors directly from crowdsourced annotations, addressing a practical gap where prior CMLI methods required test-time crowdsourced labels. The monotonic improvement with annotator count (while baselines degrade) suggests the approach scales favorably in real crowdsourcing pipelines. Limitations include the instance-independent transition matrix assumption, uniform annotator contribution approximation, and reliance on the anchor-point assumption for transition matrix estimation, all of which may not hold in highly heterogeneous annotation settings. Open questions include extending to instance-dependent noise, non-uniform annotator reliability, and very large label spaces where the per-class decomposition becomes costly.
