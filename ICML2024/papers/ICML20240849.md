## Problem

Ecological rationality posits that human cognition is adapted to the statistical structure of real-world environments, but testing this requires both ecologically valid tasks and rational models for them. Prior meta-learned cognitive models relied on hand-engineered synthetic environments whose statistics (e.g., zero feature correlations, full linearity, near-perfect predictability) diverged sharply from real data, leaving a gap between the tasks models were trained on and the tasks humans actually encounter.

## Input / Output

At inference time, the frozen transformer receives a sequence of stimulus-category pairs (each stimulus is a 3-, 4-, or 6-dimensional numerical feature vector, each category is binary) and predicts the category probability for the current stimulus conditioned on all preceding pairs via causal self-attention. No gradient updates occur during evaluation; learning is entirely in-context through the attention mechanism over the causal mask. The model is evaluated on binary classification tasks with 100–616 data points, and on the OpenML-CC18 benchmark it is given 30 training pairs and tested on 70 held-out pairs per task.

## Method

The approach has two stages. First, CLAUDE-V2 is prompted in a two-step process to synthesize ~10,000 binary category learning tasks (feature names, category labels, and 100–616 data points per task) whose distributional statistics—feature correlations, sparsity (Gini coefficient), linearity, and logistic-regression learning curves—closely match 28 real-world OpenML-CC18 tasks, unlike synthetic Bayesian logistic-regression or Bayesian neural-network priors. Second, a 6-layer transformer decoder (64 model dimension, 8 attention heads, 256 FFN units, sinusoidal positional encoding, causal attention mask) is meta-trained for 500,000 episodes by maximizing the log-likelihood of category labels given preceding stimulus-category pairs across sampled tasks (Equation 1), using ADAM at lr 1e-4. The resulting model, ERMI, approximates the Bayes-optimal sequential inference algorithm for the ecological task distribution and, unlike its synthetic-data counterpart MI, captures human-like difficulty ordering, a shift from prototype- to exemplar-based strategies, and human-like generalization to unseen stimuli.

## Results

On the OpenML-CC18 classification benchmark (23 binary tasks, 4 features, 100 data points), ERMI achieves the best mean accuracy of 70.95% ± 0.54 and best mean rank of 2.26 ± 0.22, with an exceedance probability of 0.66. On the Badham et al. (2017) replication of Shepard's category learning paradigm, ERMI explains human trial-by-trial choices best 43% of the time (posterior model frequency 0.43 ± 0.05), outperforming the Rational Model of Categorization (0.41 ± 0.05) and all other baselines. On the Devraj et al. (2021) replication of Smith & Minda, ERMI again leads with a posterior model frequency of 0.32 ± 0.06, followed by GCM (0.24 ± 0.05) and the rule-based model (0.20 ± 0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ERMI outperforms all seven cognitive-model baselines on both human experiments. On the Badham et al. (2017) study, ERMI (posterior model frequency 0.43) narrowly beats RMC (0.41), while MI (0.10), PFN (0.05), GCM, PM, Rule, and Rulex all fall below 10%. On the Devraj et al. (2021) study, ERMI (0.32) leads GCM (0.24) and the rule-based model (0.20). Against CLAUDE-V2 used directly as a cognitive model, ERMI achieves a lower MSE (0.05 vs. 0.18) and a lower BIC (38,722 vs. 39,817), indicating a better fit to human choices. On the OpenML-CC18 ML benchmark, ERMI (70.95% accuracy, rank 2.26) surpasses TabPFN (70.51%, rank 2.85), XGBoost (70.17%, rank 2.61), and SVM (69.29%, rank 2.76), with a 3.5-point accuracy gain over the parameter-matched PFN and a 10-point gain over MI.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the meta-learned models of cognition framework (Binz et al., 2022a; Binz et al., 2023), which previously relied on hand-engineered or synthetically-generated task distributions (Bayesian logistic regression for MI, Bayesian neural networks for PFN), by replacing the synthetic task prior with LLM-generated ecologically valid category learning tasks. The transformer decoder architecture with causal attention mask is identical to prior MI/PFN work; the sole change is the training data distribution. The paper also builds on recent evidence that LLMs produce realistic tabular data (Borisov et al., 2022) and match human priors (Griffiths et al., 2023), but is the first to use LLM-generated tasks as the training distribution for meta-learned cognitive models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a general methodology for testing ecological rationality: prompt an LLM to generate large quantities of ecologically valid tasks, then meta-learn on them to obtain rational agents adapted to real-world statistics. This overcomes the long-standing obstacle that insufficient real-world datasets preclude meta-learning. Key limitations include ERMI learning faster than humans (suggesting bounded rationality constraints are needed), LLM-generated data being US/western-centric, and uncertainty about scaling to higher-dimensional or multi-class problems. The approach is explicitly framed as extensible to decision-making, reinforcement learning, and function learning, making it a potentially broad methodological contribution to cognitive science.
