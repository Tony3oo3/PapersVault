## Problem

Previous evolutionary reinforcement learning (ERL) frameworks predominantly combine evolutionary algorithms with policy-based RL, overlooking value-based RL. Applying existing ERL frameworks to value-based RL is problematic because cumulative-reward fitness misaligns with the value iteration objective, and uniform interaction of all population members injects low-quality samples that degrade RL optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241116/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes state observations (low-resolution pixel frames for MinAtar/Atari tasks, continuous state vectors for MUJOCO) and produces Q-value estimates from which actions are selected via argmax. Unusually, the population fitness is computed entirely from replay buffer samples (TD error over M=5120 or 1024 transitions) rather than from environment interaction, making evaluation sample-free. Each individual in the population is a pair of Q-network and target network, and the final output is an improved value function that yields a better policy than the underlying RL algorithm alone.

## Method

VEB-RL maintains a population of Q-networks (with corresponding target networks) and uses the negative Temporal Difference error computed over replay buffer samples as the fitness metric, aligning evolution with the value iteration objective without requiring environment interaction. An Elite Interaction mechanism restricts environment interaction to only the top-N highest-fitness individuals, ensuring that only high-quality samples enter the shared replay buffer for RL gradient optimization. The optimized RL Q-network is injected into the population each generation, and target networks are hard-updated every H generations for stability. Two evolutionary variants are implemented: GA-based (tournament selection, k-point crossover, Gaussian mutation) and CEM-based (Gaussian distribution update over the top half of the population). The framework is integrated with DQN, Rainbow, SPR, and TD3, demonstrating generality across value-based and actor-critic algorithms.

## Results

On MinAtar, both GA-based and CEM-based VEB-RL significantly improve DQN, achieving approximately 100% performance gains on Asterix and Seaquest and about 50% on Breakout and Space Invaders over all ERL baselines. On Atari 100k, VEB-SPR improves SPR by approximately 40% (e.g., 4099.1 vs. 3678.4 on Qbert, 15505.0 vs. 9190.0 on Battle Zone) and outperforms CURL, OTRainbow, and SimPLe on 7 out of 9 tasks. On MUJOCO, VEB-TD3 achieves 7356 (Ant), 6940 (Humanoid), and 5490 (Walker), surpassing TD3, ERL, CEM-RL, and PDERL across all three tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VEB-RL consistently outperforms all ERL baselines (ERL, CEM-RL, ERL-Re²) on MinAtar, with ERL-Re² being the strongest prior ERL method and still trailing by ~50–100% on several tasks. Compared to the base RL algorithms, VEB-RL brings substantial gains: ~100% improvement over DQN on Asterix/Seaquest and ~40% over SPR on Atari 100k. On Atari 100k, VEB-SPR dominates CURL, OTRainbow, and SimPLe on 7 of 9 tasks (losing only on Alien where SimPLe scores 616.9 vs. VEB-SPR's 882.7, and on Pong where SimPLe's 12.8 slightly edges VEB-SPR's 10.3). On MUJOCO, VEB-TD3 beats TD3 by 18% (Ant: 7356 vs. 6219), 9% (Humanoid: 6940 vs. 6362), and 14% (Walker: 5490 vs. 4806), and also surpasses ERL, CEM-RL, and PDERL on all three tasks. The paper also shows VEB-RL outperforms a population-based pure RL baseline (DQN-multiple) on MinAtar, confirming gains come from the EA mechanisms rather than simply maintaining multiple Q-networks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Evolutionary Reinforcement Learning (ERL) framework—originally developed for policy-based RL (ERL, CEM-RL, ERL-Re², PDERL)—to the value-based RL setting, where the population consists of Q-networks rather than actor networks. The key conceptual departure is replacing the standard cumulative-reward fitness with the negative Temporal Difference error, which aligns population evolution with the Bellman-equation optimization objective of value iteration. The concurrent EA+RL architecture (shared replay buffer, RL injection into population, population samples feeding RL) is inherited directly from ERL, while the Elite Interaction mechanism is a new design that restricts environment interaction to top-ranked individuals to avoid low-quality sample contamination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VEB-RL fills a notable gap in the ERL literature by demonstrating that evolutionary search over value functions (rather than policies) is both principled and effective, with the negative TD error providing a sample-efficient, interaction-free fitness signal. The practical impact is significant for practitioners using DQN, Rainbow, or SPR who want to improve sample efficiency without switching to policy-based methods. However, the framework lacks theoretical guarantees, incurs a ~67% time overhead from population evaluation, and the authors acknowledge that the per-individual target network architecture and random-sampling-based fitness evaluation are suboptimal designs with room for improvement. Open questions include whether the TD-error fitness generalizes to distributional or model-based value functions, and whether the distributional-shift collapse observed when removing RL interaction can be mitigated.
