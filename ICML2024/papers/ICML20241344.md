## Problem

Existing unsupervised domain adaptation methods for multivariate time series extract domain-invariant features directly from shared extractors, implicitly aligning distributions without probing the underlying causal structure among variables. This leads to capturing coarse-grained shortcut features (e.g., background, biases, domain-specific styles) rather than true domain-invariant representations, and the consistency of intra-class causal rationales across domains remains entirely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241344/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multivariate time series of shape K×τ_max (K variables, τ_max time steps) from a labeled source domain and an unlabeled target domain, where each variable is a univariate time series. Two augmented views (query and key) are generated per sample via history crop, historical cutout, channel dropout, and Gaussian noise. At inference, only the query branch of the target domain is used to predict a class label (probability distribution over N_c classes) for non-augmented multivariate time series.

## Method

CauDiTS introduces an Adaptive Rationale Disentangler (ARAD) that first captures inter-variable spatio-temporal relationships via an attention module, then applies a learnable binary causal mask (thresholded sigmoid) to partition the relationship matrix into a domain-common causal subgraph A and a domain-specific non-causal subgraph B. A causally augmented GCN (GCN_c) aggregates node representations using only causal edges to produce domain-invariant features Z_c for classification, while a parallel GCN_o processes non-causal edges for adversarial domain discrimination via gradient reversal. Two additional learning strategies enforce cross-domain consistency: (1) causal prototype consistency, which computes per-class prototypes via SVD on source node representations and pulls target representations toward their nearest prototype, and (2) domain intervention causality invariance, which applies backdoor adjustment by intervening on Z_c and conditioning on sampled domain-specific correlations G_o, with Pearson-correlation-based reweighting to encourage statistical independence between Z_c and Z_o.

## Results

CauDiTS achieves an average Macro-F1 improvement of 11.81% over the best baseline across four benchmark datasets. On Boiler, it reaches 0.7683 avg Macro-F1 (vs. CLUDA's 0.680, +12.99%), with a remarkable 45.31% gain on the 2→3 pair. On WISDM, CauDiTS attains 0.7362 avg Macro-F1 (vs. CLUDA's 0.607, +21.29%), showing notably more stable performance across pairs. On HAR, it achieves 0.9502 avg Macro-F1 (vs. RAINCOAT's 0.905, +4.99%), and on HHAR it reaches 0.7191 (vs. CLUDA's 0.666, +7.97%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CauDiTS outperforms all 13 baselines on every dataset and every source→target pair. The closest competitors are CLUDA (contrastive learning-based UDA for time series) and RAINCOAT (feature/label shift alignment), which are the second-best methods on most pairs. On Boiler, CauDiTS beats CLUDA by 12.99% in average Macro-F1 (0.7683 vs. 0.680), with the largest single-pair gain of 45.31% on 2→3 (0.7251 vs. 0.499). On WISDM, the improvement over CLUDA is 21.29% (0.7362 vs. 0.607), and CauDiTS shows notably more stable performance across pairs where baselines fluctuate widely. On HAR, CauDiTS surpasses RAINCOAT by 4.99% (0.9502 vs. 0.905), a meaningful gain given the near-saturation of existing methods. On HHAR, the average improvement over CLUDA is 7.97% (0.7191 vs. 0.666). General-purpose UDA methods (CAN, CDAN, DDC, DeepCORAL, DSAN, HoMM, MMDA) consistently underperform the time-series-specific baselines, confirming the importance of temporal structure in domain adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

CauDiTS extends the causal disentanglement framework for domain adaptation established by DSR (Cai et al., 2019) and iMSDA (Kong et al., 2022), which model cross-domain data generation via domain-invariant and domain-variant latent representations. The key departure is introducing a deeper causal layer: rather than directly disentangling Zc from Zo, CauDiTS first disentangles domain-common causal rationales (Gc) from domain-specific non-causal correlations (Go) among multivariate time series variables, using a learnable binary mask over a variable interrelation attention graph. This positions the work at the intersection of causal representation learning, graph neural networks, and adversarial domain adaptation, going beyond prior time-series UDA methods (VRADA, CoDATS, CLUDA, RAINCOAT) that align features without explicitly modeling causal structure among variables.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The causal perspective offers a more principled explanation for why naive feature alignment in multivariate time series UDA captures spurious shortcut features rather than true domain-invariant signals, and the backdoor adjustment strategy provides a concrete mechanism to block confounding paths. However, the approach relies on strong assumptions (Assumption 3.1: conditional distributions shift while causal rationales remain fixed), the causal mask is binary and learned end-to-end without external validation of the discovered structure, and evaluation is limited to four relatively small-scale datasets. Open questions include whether the discovered causal rationales are genuinely interpretable and stable in complex real-world systems, whether the binary mask is sufficient for partially causal relationships, and how the method scales to high-dimensional multivariate settings with hundreds of variables.
