## Problem

Offline goal-conditioned RL methods that rely on value functions suffer from compounding estimation errors under sparse binary rewards, while existing diffusion-based RL methods require iterative denoising at every environment step, making them 10–15× slower. The paper asks whether one can frame goal-reaching as a single-step reverse diffusion in state space, eliminating both the value function and the multi-step denoising bottleneck.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240851/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy network (3-layer MLP, 256 units, ReLU) takes the current state, the desired goal, and a scalar time horizon h as input; the horizon is encoded via 32-dimensional sinusoidal positional embeddings with maximum period T=50. The output is a diagonal Gaussian over actions: the mean is bounded by tanh scaled to the action-space maximum, and the standard deviation is enforced non-negative via softplus. For pixel observations, a 3-layer CNN (32/64/64 channels) first maps images to a 256-d latent vector before the MLP policy.

## Method

Merlin constructs a forward diffusion process in state space by moving trajectories away from goal states (via reverse play of offline data, a learned CVAE reverse policy + GRU reverse dynamics model, or contrastive-learned latent-space trajectory stitching with ball-tree nearest-neighbor search). The goal-conditioned policy is then trained by behavior cloning to reverse this diffusion, which Theorem 3.1 proves equivalent to maximizing a lower bound on the log-likelihood of reaching the goal under the reverse process. Crucially, diffusion operates in state space rather than action space, so only one 'denoising' step is needed per environment step, and no value function is learned. The policy additionally conditions on the time horizon separating the current state from the goal, analogous to the timestep conditioning in denoising diffusion models, and learns both the action mean and variance to capture uncertainty at different distances from the goal.

## Results

On the 10-task offline GCRL benchmark (state-space input), Merlin-NP achieves the best average rank of 1.7 in both expert and random settings, followed by Merlin-P (2.4/1.9) and base Merlin (3.5/3.8). On the hardest task HandReach (random dataset), Merlin-NP scores 20.06 discounted return versus 8.36 for the next-best method (BESO) and 0.08 for GoFAR. Training and inference are roughly 10–15× faster than diffusion-based baselines (DD, g-DQL, BESO) since Merlin requires only one denoising iteration per environment step rather than an iterative reverse diffusion chain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Merlin-NP (trajectory stitching) and Merlin-P (parametric reverse model) outperform all seven baselines on the majority of tasks, with the largest margins on the hardest tasks: on HandReach (expert), Merlin-NP scores 24.93 vs. GoFAR 15.39, BESO 15.44, and GCSL 0.15; on FetchSlide (expert), Merlin-NP scores 5.19 vs. BESO 3.40 and GoFAR 3.34. Among the non-diffusion baselines, GoFAR is the strongest (avg. rank 5.4), while GCSL and WGCSL (which rely on behavior cloning + hindsight relabeling without variance learning or time-horizon conditioning) rank last (8.6 and 5.5). Among diffusion-based baselines, BESO is the best (avg. rank 5.4) but still trails Merlin-NP by a wide margin, and DD and g-DQL perform poorly on harder tasks (e.g., g-DQL scores 0.00 on HandReach). The key efficiency advantage is that Merlin requires only one policy evaluation per environment step, making training and inference ~10–15× faster than DD, g-DQL, and BESO, which must run an iterative denoising chain at every step.

## Relation to Prior Work

Merlin reframes offline goal-conditioned RL through the lens of denoising diffusion models, but departs fundamentally from prior diffusion-based RL methods (Decision Diffuser, Diffusion-QL, BESO) that perform iterative denoising in action or trajectory space. Instead, Merlin constructs a forward diffusion process in state space by reversing trajectories away from goals, requiring only a single 'denoising' step per environment step. The method is closest in spirit to GCSL (behavior cloning with hindsight relabeling), extending it with learned policy variance, time-horizon conditioning, and a principled diffusion-theoretic justification (Theorem 3.1) showing that behavior cloning maximizes a lower bound on goal-state log-likelihood. The parametric and non-parametric forward process constructions draw on reverse model-based imagination (Wang et al., 2021) and contrastive representation learning, respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10–15× computational efficiency over other diffusion-based RL methods makes diffusion-inspired policies practical for settings where iterative denoising is prohibitive, and the elimination of a value function sidesteps the compounding estimation errors that plague offline actor-critic methods. The trajectory stitching technique (Merlin-NP) is a general-purpose data augmentation tool applicable beyond Merlin to other offline RL algorithms. However, the evaluation is limited to relatively low-dimensional control tasks (up to 25-dim state spaces) with maximum 50-step horizons, and the performance gains over the closest baseline (GCSL) are modest on many tasks. Open questions include whether the state-space diffusion framing scales to high-dimensional continuous control or language-grounded tasks, and whether the forward process construction can be made more principled rather than relying on ad hoc choices among three options.
