## Problem

Existing 2D human pose estimation methods estimate keypoint confidence using ad-hoc heuristics (e.g., heatmap maximum or regression variance) that are systematically misaligned with the actual pose accuracy metric (OKS). The paper shows through theoretical analysis that this miscalibration arises because the heuristic confidences ignore instance scale and per-keypoint falloff constants that are baked into OKS, leading to unreliable confidence rankings that hurt mAP and downstream safety-critical applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240657/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CCNet consumes penultimate features from a frozen pose estimation network: for heatmap-based methods, a 256-channel spatial feature map after deconvolution layers; for regression-based methods, a 2048-dimensional vector after global average pooling. It outputs per-keypoint calibrated confidence scores in [0,1] and per-keypoint visibility predictions in [0,1], which are aggregated (weighted by predicted visibility) into a single instance-level confidence. The module adds only ~0.08M parameters and negligible inference latency, and the backbone remains frozen so mAR is unchanged.

## Method

The paper first derives the expected OKS under a Gaussian keypoint model, showing it takes the form l²/(σ²+l²), which depends on instance area and per-keypoint falloff constants—quantities absent from the standard heatmap-max or RLE-variance confidence formulations. To close the gap beyond closed-form rescoring, CCNet is a lightweight post-hoc branch (one 1×1 convolution for detection methods, one FC layer for regression methods) that explicitly regresses per-keypoint confidence toward the per-keypoint OKS value using an MSE loss, and predicts visibility via binary cross-entropy to replace the brittle thresholding in instance aggregation. Only CCNet parameters are trained for ~12K steps with Adam (lr=1e-3), leaving the backbone untouched. The resulting instance confidence is a visibility-weighted sum of keypoint confidences, directly linking the predicted score to the OKS ranking that mAP depends on.

## Results

On the COCO validation set, CCNet consistently improves mAP by 0.8–1.4 points across all tested pose estimation backbones and paradigms (detection and regression), while leaving mAR unchanged since the backbone is frozen. The largest gains are observed for RLE with ResNet-50 (+1.4 mAP, 72.2→73.6) and IPR with heatmap confidence (+1.3 mAP, 69.5→70.8). On COCO-WholeBody, CCNet improves whole-body mAP by +2.3 (61.0→63.3), and on MPII it improves mAP by +1.2 (RLE) and +0.4 (SBL). The additional parameters are negligible (e.g., 34.00M→34.08M for SBL ResNet-50), and training requires only ~12K steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCNet is a plug-and-play calibration module that augments (rather than competes with) existing pose estimators, and it improves every baseline tested. For heatmap-based detection methods, SBL (Xiao et al., ECCV 2018) gains +0.9 mAP (ResNet-50) and +0.8 (ResNet-152); HRNet (Sun et al., CVPR 2019) gains +1.0 (W32) and +0.9 (W48); and ViTPose (Xu et al., NeurIPS 2022) gains +0.8 mAP. For regression-based methods, RLE (Li et al., ICCV 2021) shows the largest improvement at +1.4 mAP with ResNet-50 and +0.8 to +0.9 with larger backbones; Poseur (Mao et al., ECCV 2022) gains +0.9 mAP; and IPR (Sun et al., ECCV 2018) gains +1.3 mAP when using its heatmap confidence variant. In the downstream 3D mesh recovery task on 3DPW, SPIN (Kolotouros et al., ICCV 2019) combined with CCNet-calibrated 2D poses reduces PA-MPJPE from 58.8 to 57.8 and MPJPE from 100.5 to 99.7, confirming that better-calibrated confidences directly benefit downstream optimization. Across all comparisons, mAR remains identical, confirming that CCNet improves only the confidence ranking without altering pose accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of pose estimation and confidence calibration. While prior pose estimation work (SBL, HRNet, RLE, Poseur) focused almost exclusively on keypoint accuracy and estimated confidence via heuristics (heatmap maximum, 1−σ), and prior uncertainty-in-pose work (Bramlage et al., Pierzchlewicz et al.) addressed distribution calibration at the cost of accuracy, this paper is the first to derive the ideal confidence form directly from the OKS evaluation metric and show the systematic mismatch. It repurposes the post-hoc calibration paradigm from classification (Guo et al., 2017) into a lightweight CCNet add-on that operates on frozen penultimate features, avoiding the accuracy trade-off of retraining-based uncertainty methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Well-calibrated pose confidence is safety-critical for downstream applications like robotics, autonomous driving, and 3D mesh recovery, where discarding or weighting low-confidence predictions directly affects system reliability. The paper's clean decomposition—showing that the mAP–mAR gap quantifies miscalibration and that a single-layer post-hoc module closes most of it—gives practitioners a practical, framework-agnostic tool. Key limitations include the post-hoc constraint (bounded by frozen features), the restriction to top-down single-person 2D settings, and the open question of how to hybridize post-hoc calibration with train-time uncertainty modeling without sacrificing accuracy. Extending to 3D pose and bottom-up multi-person association remains nontrivial.
