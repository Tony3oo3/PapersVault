## Problem

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) still suffers from hallucinations when retrieved evidence conflicts with the LLM's parametric knowledge, as the model may ignore the provided context and rely on its internal memory. Prior work addresses this via supervised fine-tuning or prompt engineering, but it remains unexplored whether reinforcement learning can align RAG LLMs to a trustworthy status—responding solely from contextual evidence—without explicit supervision on how to generate the response.

## Input / Output

The method takes a question q and a retrieved context c, formatted together via a prompting function f(q,c) into a natural-language instruction, and produces a free-form text response grounded in the context. Training data is constructed by entity substitution: the original answer in the context is replaced with a same-type entity, creating a counterfactual context that conflicts with the model's parametric memory. The reward signal is sentence-level, applied only at the terminal token, and the model is evaluated on both free-form QA (exact match) and multi-choice QA (accuracy) formats.

## Method

TRUSTWORTHY-ALIGNMENT formulates LLM autoregressive generation as a Markov Decision Process and trains with PPO using a three-component reward: a trust reward that gives +α₀ if the substituted (contextual) answer appears in the response, −α₀ if the original (parametric) answer appears, and −α₁ if neither appears; a KL-divergence penalty against the SFT reference policy; and a collapse penalty that detects and penalizes repetitive degenerate outputs. A key theoretical contribution shows, via potential-based reward shaping, that the KL and collapse terms are expressible as differences of a state potential function, so the optimal policy under the full reward is consistent with the optimal policy under the trust reward alone when the discount factor γ is sufficiently close to 1. An annealing exploration strategy linearly decays the sampling temperature from 2.0 to 0.0 over training to balance exploration of new response patterns against exploitation of high-reward trajectories. The approach is implemented with LoRA adapters on top of the DeepSpeed-Chat framework, requiring no human-annotated response data—only the counterfactual question-context pairs and the reward function.

## Results

On Natural Questions, TRUSTWORTHY-ALIGNMENT achieves 94.92% EM and 0.70% memorization ratio (M_R) with Llama-2-7b-chat, surpassing SFT (92.42% EM, 1.24% M_R) and all prompting baselines. On the out-of-domain ConflictQA-StrategyQA, the method reaches 77.06% accuracy with 24.19% M_R (Vicuna-7b-v1.5), outperforming SFT (71.73% Acc, 29.97% M_R) by a large margin on both metrics. On ConflictQA-PopQA, SFT retains an edge in accuracy (e.g., 90.74% vs. 66.14% for Vicuna), but TRUSTWORTHY-ALIGNMENT achieves lower M_R, indicating stronger suppression of parametric memory. The method also shows minimal alignment tax, with Vicuna-7b-v1.5 dropping less than 1% on Open LLM Leaderboard tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4 (zero-shot), TRUSTWORTHY-ALIGNMENT on Llama-2-7b-chat achieves 94.92% EM with only 0.70% M_R on Natural Questions, dramatically outperforming GPT-4's Opinion prompting (89.71% EM but 84.14% M_R), showing that the RL-aligned open-source model is far more trustworthy despite lower raw capability. Against Llama-2-7b (base), TRUSTWORTHY-ALIGNMENT underperforms SFT on Natural Questions EM (82.93% vs. 92.34%) due to weaker instruction-following, but wins on ConflictQA-StrategyQA (69.17% vs. 67.38% Acc; 37.32% vs. 39.76% M_R), demonstrating better generalization. For Llama-2-7b-chat, the method surpasses SFT on Natural Questions (94.92% vs. 92.42% EM) and achieves the lowest M_R (0.70% vs. 1.24%). Against Vicuna-7b-v1.5, SFT edges out on Natural Questions EM (93.14% vs. 91.22%) and ConflictQA-PopQA accuracy (90.74% vs. 66.14%), but TRUSTWORTHY-ALIGNMENT clearly wins on ConflictQA-StrategyQA (77.06% vs. 71.73% Acc; 24.19% vs. 29.97% M_R), indicating superior suppression of parametric memory in out-of-domain settings. Overall, the method trades a small amount of in-domain accuracy for substantially better trustworthiness and generalization to unseen conflict scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLHF alignment paradigm (Ouyang et al., 2022) from human-preference alignment to a new objective: making retrieval-augmented LLMs trust contextual evidence over parametric memory. Prior work on knowledge conflicts (Longpre et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2022; Neeman et al., 2023) addressed this via supervised fine-tuning or prompt engineering, but this is the first to frame trustworthiness as an RL problem with a dedicated reward function and provide a potential-based reward shaping guarantee (Ng et al., 1999) that the shaped reward preserves the optimal policy. The method reuses the PPO + KL-penalty machinery from standard RLHF, adding a collapse penalty and an annealing exploration schedule specific to the LLM generation setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that RAG LLMs possess an inherent capacity to follow contextual evidence and that RL can 'unlock' this without explicit supervision on response format, broadening alignment beyond human preference to agent trustworthiness. Key limitations include evaluation only on 7B-scale models, a single training dataset (Natural Questions), and an assumption that the retriever returns correct evidence; the modified-reward experiment (Section D.2) hints at handling imperfect retrievers but remains preliminary. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models, generalizes beyond QA to other RAG tasks, and how to balance trustworthiness with the model's ability to flag when retrieved evidence is unreliable.
