## Problem

Learning implicit signed distance functions from sparse, noisy, unoriented point clouds in an unsupervised setting suffers from severe overfitting: the training loss decreases while validation error (Chamfer distance to ground truth) increases early, producing hallucinated, wavy, or fragmented shapes. Existing work regularizes with smoothness priors but leaves the training query sampling strategy unexplored, despite the fact that sparse inputs make the nearest-point pseudo-labels inherently noisy and cause the network to overfit on easy, noise-dominated samples while informative hard samples are drowned out.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single sparse (1024 points) or dense, noisy, unoriented 3D point cloud with no normals or labels. Query points are sampled from locally-adapted Gaussian distributions centered at each input point, with standard deviations set to the maximum distance to the 51 nearest neighbors. The output is an MLP-parameterized SDF whose zero level set is extracted as an explicit triangle mesh via Marching Cubes; the SDF can also be rendered directly through ray marching.

## Method

Building on the NeuralPull projection loss (pulling spatial queries onto the SDF zero level set via gradient descent), the paper augments training with adversarial query samples computed as first-order Taylor perturbations δ̂ = ρ_q · ∇_q L / ‖∇_q L‖, where the perturbation radius ρ_q is a local fraction (10⁻²) of the nearest input point's local standard deviation σ_p, making the adversarial search spatially adaptive to input density. This local hard-sample mining is framed as a tractable special case of distributionally robust optimization. The original and adversarial losses are combined with learnable weights following the Liebel & Körner multi-task balancing strategy, ensuring stable convergence. The adversarial queries are generated in the vicinity of original queries within these locally adapted radii, so they remain close to the same nearest input point and provide informative supervision without introducing spurious pseudo-labels.

## Results

On ShapeNet (1024 sparse noisy points), the method achieves CD1=0.76, CD2=0.020, NC=0.87, and FS=0.83, outperforming all baselines across every metric. On FAUST, it reaches CD1=0.220 and FS=0.981, surpassing even supervised methods like SAC (CD1=0.261) and NKSR (CD1=0.274). On the 3D Scene benchmark (100 pts/m²), the mean CD1 drops to 0.041 versus 0.077 for NTPS and 0.097 for the NeuralPull baseline. On SRB, the method achieves CD1=0.49 (sparse) and 0.19 (dense), matching the best dense result (DIGS at 0.19) while substantially outperforming NP (0.58/0.23).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 14 baselines on the primary ShapeNet benchmark: it beats its direct baseline NeuralPull (NP) by 34% in CD1 (0.76 vs 1.16) and 11% in FS (0.83 vs 0.75), and surpasses the closest sparse-specific competitor NTPS by 32% in CD1 (0.76 vs 1.11). On FAUST, it achieves the best CD1 (0.220) and FS (0.981) among all methods, including supervised approaches like SAC (CD1=0.261) and NKSR (CD1=0.274), while also outperforming the prior-based On-Surf (CD1=0.584) and the grid-based GP (CD1=0.495). On 3D Scene, the method achieves the lowest mean CD1 (0.041) and CD2 (0.004), beating NTPS (0.077/0.010) and NP (0.097/0.016), though NTPS retains a slight edge in NC (0.897 vs 0.881). On SRB dense inputs, the method ties DIGS at CD1=0.19 and beats OG-INR (0.20) and NP (0.23), while in the sparse setting it leads all methods (0.49 vs 0.58 for NP and 0.68 for DIGS). The classical Poisson reconstruction SPSR is consistently the weakest deep-learning competitor, with CD1 values 2–3× higher than the proposed method across all datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NeuralPull (Ma et al., 2021) framework for unsupervised SDF learning from point clouds by introducing locally adversarial query sampling as a regularization mechanism. While prior work in this space relies on smoothness priors (Eikonal, divergence, Lipschitz constraints) to combat overfitting under sparse inputs, this paper instead draws from distributionally robust optimization and adversarial training (Madry et al., 2017; Staib & Jegelka, 2017) to identify and exploit hard spatial samples near the input cloud. The multi-task loss weighting strategy from Liebel & Körner (2018) is repurposed to stabilize the combined original-plus-adversarial objective, and the local radius design ties adversarial perturbation magnitude to the input point cloud's local density.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the sampling strategy for spatial queries—not just the loss function or smoothness prior—significantly affects overfitting in unsupervised implicit shape learning, opening a new axis of regularization for this problem. The consistent improvements across synthetic objects, articulated human shapes, and large-scale scenes (with the method also benefiting dense settings) suggest the adversarial query idea is broadly applicable within the NeuralPull family. However, the approach remains tied to the specific projection-based loss of NeuralPull, and the authors acknowledge persistent difficulties with very thin or convoluted geometry, leaving open whether adversarial sampling can be combined with other priors or extended to more general implicit representation learning settings.
