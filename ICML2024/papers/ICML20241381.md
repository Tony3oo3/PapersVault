## Problem

LLMs possess broad scientific knowledge and reasoning ability but lack the computational machinery to simulate physical phenomena and ground their hypotheses in quantitative observational feedback. Existing LLM-based discovery methods (FunSearch, Eureka, OPRO) are confined to the LLM's own inference capacity and cannot perform the numerical simulation or gradient-based parameter fitting that physical science demands. The paper asks whether LLMs and differentiable simulations can be combined into a unified, domain-agnostic framework that mirrors how human scientists iteratively propose hypotheses and test them experimentally.

## Input / Output

For constitutive law discovery, the method takes a ground-truth particle position trajectory (T timesteps) and an initial PyTorch nn.Module code snippet (e.g., a linear elastic material) as the starting point; it outputs a new nn.Module whose __init__ defines trainable continuous parameters (e.g., Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio) and whose forward encodes the symbolic constitutive equation producing Kirchhoff stress or corrected deformation gradient. For molecular design, the input is a target quantum-mechanical property value (HOMO, LUMO, or HOMO-LUMO gap, normalized on QM9) and the output is a SMILES string (≥8 atoms, no hydrogens) paired with an (N,3) coordinate matrix. In both settings the LLM consumes a top-K heap of past (code, loss-curve, particle-trajectory, intermediate-optimization) tuples as context, and the simulation returns compact observational feedback z (e.g., recorded particle trajectories) plus the inner-optimization loss curve o.

## Method

SGA formulates scientific discovery as a bilevel optimization: the outer level uses an LLM (GPT-4-turbo) to perform search-based optimization over discrete symbolic variables (constitutive equations, SMILES strings) and to propose the continuous parameterization Θ, while the inner level runs a differentiable simulator (Warp-based MPM for materials; RDKit + UniMol for molecules) to solve for optimal continuous parameters θ via gradient descent. The LLM is prompted in a structured three-part format (analysis of past iterations, step-by-step plan, code output) and receives simulation feedback including loss curves and auxiliary recordings. An exploit-and-explore strategy splits offspring generation into low-temperature (T=0.5, 4 offspring) and high-temperature (T=1.0, 12 offspring) groups, maintaining a 1:3 ratio to balance conservative refinement against bold exploration. A top-K heap stores ranked (expression, optimized-parameter) pairs across iterations, enabling evolutionary crossover where the LLM draws on multiple high-performing 'breeds' rather than only the single best solution. The key architectural insight is that the LLM handles what to simulate (discrete, symbolic search) while the simulation handles how well it works (continuous, gradient-based fitting), with the two levels exchanging information through the bilevel constraint θ̂ ∈ argmin_{θ∈Θ} L(y(θ; Φ, E)).

## Results

On the 8-task benchmark (4 constitutive law search + 4 molecule design), SGA achieves losses orders of magnitude lower than all LLM-based baselines: e.g., 5.2e-5 vs. 128.0 (Eureka) on task (a), and 1.3e-4 vs. 1.1 (FunSearch) on molecule design task (e). On the symbolic regression reformulation (Table 2), SGA attains R²=0.9990 and MAE=86.4, surpassing the best traditional method Operon (R²=0.9988, MAE=92.4). On the imaginary constitutive law task designed to prevent memorization, SGA achieves a loss of 1.3e-3 while all baselines remain above 89, confirming genuine generalization rather than recall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGA dominates all four LLM-based baselines (CoT, FunSearch, Eureka, OPRO) by 2–4 orders of magnitude on every task in Table 1; for instance, on the hardest constitutive law task (b) (von Mises plasticity from purely elastic), SGA reaches 2.1e-1 while the best baseline Eureka scores 531.0. Against traditional symbolic regression methods (Table 2), SGA achieves the best R² (0.9990) and lowest MAE (86.4), narrowly beating Operon (R²=0.9988, MAE=92.4) and substantially outperforming data-driven pre-trained models like SymbolicGPT (R²=0.5233). On molecule design, SGA outperforms the population-based GhemGE by 37× on task (e) (1.3e-4 vs. 4.8e-3) and 16× on task (f) (1.1e-1 vs. 1.8), though GhemGE retains a slight edge on task (h) (9.8e-5 vs. 3.6e-5). The imaginary constitutive law experiment (Table 4) is decisive: SGA achieves 1.3e-3 while FunSearch, Eureka, and OPRO all remain above 89, demonstrating that SGA's gains stem from genuine optimization rather than memorization of known material laws.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of LLM-as-optimizer work (FunSearch, Eureka, OPRO) by addressing their fundamental limitation: LLMs are confined to their own computational capability for tasks requiring numerical simulation. It also builds directly on differentiable simulation for constitutive law discovery (Ma et al., 2023), repurposing it as the inner-level gradient-based optimizer. The bilevel structure is conceptually borrowed from neural architecture search (DARTS, ProxylessNAS), where discrete architecture search is nested over continuous weight optimization, but here the outer level is an LLM performing open-ended symbolic search rather than a predefined architecture space, and the inner level is a physics simulator rather than a neural network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, domain-agnostic template for LLM-augmented scientific discovery that could be adopted across physics, chemistry, and engineering with minimal prompt changes. Key limitations include the ~$10-per-task GPT-4 cost that scales with iterations, the dependency on differentiability of LLM-generated code (zeroth-order optimizers are suggested as a fallback), and the safety risk of executing unfiltered LLM-generated code. Open questions include incorporating domain-specific constraints and human feedback for regularization, and whether KV-cache reuse can reduce the computational overhead of the iterative LLM calls.
