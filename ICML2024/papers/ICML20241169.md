## Problem

Existing federated binarization methods (SignSGD and its variants) compress model updates in a post-training manner, meaning binarization errors are introduced only after local training and cannot be optimized. Additionally, the step size α is treated as a fixed hyperparameter, requiring manual tuning and often yielding suboptimal performance. This paper asks whether binary model updates and their step size can be learned directly during local training to inherently reduce approximation errors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241169/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives the global model parameters from the server and trains on local image data (28×28 for FMNIST, 32×32 for SVHN/CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100). The client maintains two parameter copies: the base model and a separate update tensor m initialized to zero. The output uploaded per client is a binary model update (each element is ±α) plus a per-layer scalar step size α, which the server aggregates layer-wise to form the next global model. The per-layer step size is the key structural difference from standard SignSGD, where a single global α is used.

## Method

FedBAT introduces a learnable binarization operator S(x, α) applied during forward propagation of local training, so the loss is computed on binarized updates and gradients flow back to both the update tensor m and the step size α. The operator clips values outside [−α, α] to ±α and applies stochastic binarization with uniform noise inside the interval; a Straight-Through Estimator makes the floor function differentiable. The step size is parameterized as α = α′·e^(ρ·α_e) to enforce positivity, with α_e initialized to zero and ρ controlling optimization pace. Local training proceeds in two stages: a full-precision warm-up phase (fraction φ of local steps) to obtain meaningful update magnitudes, followed by binarization-aware training where both m and α are jointly optimized. Convergence is proven under both strongly convex and non-convex objectives, showing the same O(1/T) rate as uncompressed FedAvg.

## Results

FedBAT achieves test accuracy comparable to or exceeding FedAvg across all four datasets (FMNIST, SVHN, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100) under both IID and Non-IID data distributions with 30 and 100 clients. On the most challenging setting (CIFAR-100, Non-IID-2, 100 clients), FedBAT reaches 49.2% accuracy, matching FedAvg (49.0%) while SignSGD drops to 32.5% and EF-SignSGD to 40.2%. The largest gains over SignSGD exceed 24 percentage points (e.g., SVHN Non-IID-2: 89.2% vs. 64.7%). FedBAT also outperforms all other binarization baselines (EF-SignSGD, Noisy-SignSGD, Stoc-SignSGD) and the sparsity-based ZeroFL by 1–9% across settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedBAT matches or slightly exceeds FedAvg (the uncompressed baseline) in most settings, e.g., 89.2% vs. 89.3% on CIFAR-10 IID and 49.2% vs. 49.0% on CIFAR-100 Non-IID-2 (N=100), while compressing uplink communication by 32×. Against SignSGD, FedBAT achieves the largest gains under Non-IID-2: +8.5% on CIFAR-10 (81.0% vs. 76.6%), +16.7% on CIFAR-100 (49.2% vs. 32.5%), and +24.5% on SVHN (89.2% vs. 64.7%). Among the improved binarization baselines, FedBAT outperforms EF-SignSGD by 1–9% (e.g., 49.2% vs. 40.2% on CIFAR-100 Non-IID-2), Noisy-SignSGD by 2–9%, and Stoc-SignSGD by 1–9%. ZeroFL (97% sparsity) is consistently outperformed by FedBAT by 1–4% across all datasets. In the supplementary comparison (Table 5), FedBAT matches FedPAQ at 3–4 bits/parameter on CIFAR-10 (e.g., 89.2% vs. 88.8% IID) while using only 1 bit, and substantially outperforms BiFL (61.8% vs. 89.2% on CIFAR-10 IID) because BiFL binarizes weights rather than updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedBAT builds directly on the SignSGD family of binarization methods (SignSGD, EF-SignSGD, Noisy-SignSGD, Stoc-SignSGD) for compressing uplink communication in federated learning, and uses FedAvg as its aggregation backbone. The key conceptual departure is that all prior methods apply binarization in a post-training manner—compressing updates only after local training is complete—whereas FedBAT integrates a learnable binarization operator into the local training loop itself, making both the binary updates and the per-layer step size differentiable parameters optimized via a Straight-Through Estimator. This draws an analogy to quantization-aware training (PACT, LSQ) in centralized deep learning, but adapts it to the short-horizon local training phase of FL, where a warm-up stage and a temperature-scaled step-size parameter (ρ) are needed to stabilize learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: FedBAT achieves 32× uplink compression (1-bit per parameter) while matching or occasionally exceeding full-precision FedAvg accuracy across four datasets, two architectures, and three data distributions, addressing a core bottleneck in real-world FL deployments. The theoretical analysis provides convergence guarantees comparable to FedAvg under both convex and non-convex settings, lending confidence to the approach. Key limitations include the need for two copies of model parameters on each client (increasing memory overhead) and the fact that the convergence proof assumes a fixed step size (α = ‖m‖∞) rather than the learned step size used in practice. Open questions include whether the learnable-binarization paradigm extends naturally to multi-bit quantization, asynchronous FL, or personalized FL settings.
