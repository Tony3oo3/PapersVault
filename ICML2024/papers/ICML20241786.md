## Problem

MCTS and AlphaZero-family algorithms struggle with long-horizon exploration in continuous state spaces because they do not distinguish states by spatial novelty, and progressive widening produces short, heavily-branched trees that over-explore the starting region. Sampling-based motion planners like RRT explore efficiently via Voronoi bias but make strong assumptions (known deterministic dynamics, path-planning objective) and lack a mechanism for reward-guided search. The paper bridges these two paradigms by showing both are approximate solutions to a state occupancy measure regularization objective, enabling MCTS to inherit SBMP's exploration efficiency without its restrictive assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a continuous-state, continuous-action MDP with deterministic dynamics, receiving the current state, a goal region, and a neural network policy for sampling new actions during expansion. It maintains a k-d tree over visited states that serves dual purposes: computing Voronoi-region volumes for the exploration term and providing value estimates by averaging node values at mid-depth k-d tree nodes (a bias-variance tradeoff). The output is a growing search tree with per-node value estimates and a traversed path to the goal, with the k-d tree updated via backpropagation after each expansion.

## Method

Volume-MCTS formulates exploration as maximizing expected node return minus an f-divergence (reverse KL) regularization on the state occupancy density, where the density is estimated via a partition density estimator (k-d tree Voronoi regions). The key theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves that for search trees specifically, any convex loss on the state occupancy measure can be minimized by independently optimizing the policy at each node, yielding a closed-form optimal policy π*(a|n) = λ·SubtreeVol(a) / (α − γ^d(n)·P(n reached)·Q(a|n)) that balances reward (via Q) against spatial coverage (via subtree volume). The algorithm traverses the tree by sampling from this policy until selecting a 'stay' move, then expands the node by sampling a new action from a neural network policy, executing it, and backpropagating the k-d tree value estimate. The paper further shows that RRT's Voronoi bias emerges as the λ→∞ limit and that count-based exploration (Never Give Up) emerges from the Hellinger-divergence empirical decision rule, unifying both under the same regularized objective.

## Results

On geometric mazes (5000 rollouts/episode), Volume-MCTS achieves 40.0±1.0 average reward at maze size 5 after training, while AlphaZero and AlphaZero w/CBE both drop to 0.0. At maze size 9, Volume-MCTS still achieves 26.0±4.0 while all baselines score 0.0. On the Dubins car maze (more challenging dynamics), Volume-MCTS reaches 34.0±1.0 at size 5 after training versus 0.0 for AlphaZero. On the quadcopter navigation task, Volume-MCTS outperforms SAC+HER, AlphaZero, POLY-HOOT, and SST at longer search times, returning shorter paths than SST on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Volume-MCTS decisively outperforms AlphaZero-Continuous (Moerland et al., 2018) on all maze sizes ≥4, where AlphaZero's reward drops to 0.0 while Volume-MCTS maintains 38–45. AlphaZero with CBE (adapted from Never Give Up) is the strongest baseline but still falls to 0.0 by maze size 5–6, whereas Volume-MCTS retains 22–38 reward. On the quadcopter task, Volume-MCTS beats SAC+HER (Andrychowicz et al., 2018), AlphaZero (Silver et al., 2018), and POLY-HOOT (Mao et al., 2020) on reward as a function of total environmental interactions. Against SST (Li et al., 2016), Volume-MCTS finds the goal at a similar frequency but returns shorter paths on average, and surpasses SST when longer search times are permitted. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that reward guidance is essential: removing rewards reduces Volume-MCTS to Kinodynamic-RRT performance, with significantly longer paths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Grill et al. (2020), who showed that MCTS's UCB selection rule can be derived as a solution to a regularized policy optimization objective, and extends this framework by regularizing the state occupancy measure rather than the policy itself. It also draws on Hazan et al. (2019) for the state occupancy entropy maximization objective and on the SBMP literature (RRT, SST) for the Voronoi bias. The key conceptual departure is showing that both Voronoi bias and count-based exploration emerge as (approximate) solutions to the same state occupancy f-divergence regularization, unifying two previously disconnected exploration strategies under a single objective. A critical structural result is that for trees (unlike general MDPs), convex functions of state occupancy can be optimized by independently solving for the optimal policy at each node, making the otherwise intractable state occupancy objective tractable in the MCTS setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first high-probability, non-asymptotic exploration efficiency bound for an MCTS-family algorithm, showing that any region on a δ-controllable trajectory is reached in O(t²) steps with probability > 0.5, independent of the discount factor γ. This is practically significant for long-horizon problems where γ is close to 1 and standard regret bounds degrade. Limitations include the assumption of deterministic dynamics and state-dependent rewards (extensions are sketched but not fully analyzed), the k-d tree value estimator's consistency is only conjectured, and experiments are confined to navigation tasks. Open questions include whether the tree-specific convexity result can be extended to general MDPs or DAGs, whether the state occupancy regularization framework yields useful exploration signals in non-navigation domains (e.g., manipulation, game playing), and whether the k-d tree approximation to the optimal policy introduces meaningful performance gaps at scale.
