## Problem

Multi-instance point cloud registration requires estimating multiple rigid transformations aligning a source model to all its copies in a target scene. Existing deep-learning approaches rely on global spatial consistency, which becomes unreliable when instances overlap, and on spectral clustering of high-dimensional correspondence features, which is computationally expensive. The paper addresses both limitations by introducing a local-consistency-based feature encoder and a maximal-clique-driven pose estimation pipeline that clusters low-dimensional transformation vectors instead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242193/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a set of M putative correspondences (6D vectors pairing source and target points) produced by a pre-trained Predator matcher, with point clouds downsampled at 0.05 m voxel resolution. It outputs K rigid transformations, each represented as a 7D vector (quaternion rotation + 3D translation) or 6D vector (Euler angles + translation), one per detected instance. The number of instances K is not given a priori and is inferred from the clustering step.

## Method

PointMC operates in four stages. First, a graph-based correspondence embedding module built from stacked spatial-consistency-aware self-attention (SCASA) layers encodes each putative correspondence using local spatial consistency (distance invariance restricted to a local neighborhood via farthest-point sampling), producing discriminative features that a three-layer MLP head classifies as inlier or outlier under binary focal loss. Second, the surviving correspondences form a weighted compatibility graph whose edge weights combine first-order distance invariance with a second-order compatibility measure (M_s = M_f · (M_f × M_f)) for sparsity and robustness. Third, the improved Bron-Kerbosch algorithm extracts all maximal cliques; each clique's compatible correspondences are fed to SVD to yield a candidate rigid transformation, and a weight-based filtering step assigns each node to its highest-weight clique to reduce redundancy. Fourth, the resulting set of low-dimensional pose vectors is clustered (DBSCAN or Chameleon) into K groups, and the transformation minimizing the reprojection error over its associated correspondences is selected as the final pose for each instance.

## Results

On the real-world Scan2CAD dataset, PointMC achieves 84.65% mean recall, 76.36% mean precision, and 80.29% mean F-score with a runtime of 0.07s, outperforming the closest deep-learning baseline PointCLM (77.15% MF) by 3.14% in MF. On the synthetic ModelNet40 dataset, PointMC reaches 97.24% MF with 0.04s runtime, surpassing PointCLM (96.29% MF) by 0.95%. The method also substantially outperforms the multi-model fitting baselines (CONSAC, RansaCov, T-linkage), which achieve at most 56.22% MF on Scan2CAD and 3.51% MF on ModelNet40.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PointMC outperforms all five baselines on both datasets across all three metrics (MR, MP, MF). Against the strongest deep-learning competitor PointCLM (Yuan et al., ECCV 2022), PointMC gains +3.39% MR and +3.14% MF on Scan2CAD while being 0.02s faster; on ModelNet40 the margin is smaller (+0.95% MF) but PointMC is still faster (0.04s vs 0.05s). Against ECC (Tang & Zou, CVPR 2022), which relies on global spatial consistency and spectral clustering, PointMC improves MF by 11.19% on Scan2CAD and 8.43% on ModelNet40, with the gap widening in scenes with overlapping instances. The three multi-model fitting methods (CONSAC, RansaCov, T-linkage) are far behind: CONSAC achieves 56.22% MF on Scan2CAD (24.07% below PointMC) and only 3.51% MF on ModelNet40, while T-linkage and RansaCov score below 40% MF on both datasets. PointMC also matches or beats CONSAC in runtime (0.07s vs 0.06s on Scan2CAD) while delivering 24% higher MF, and is dramatically faster than T-linkage (0.07s vs 5.59s).

## Relation to Prior Work

PointMC adapts the maximal clique search strategy from MAC (Zhang et al., 2023a), which was designed for pairwise registration, to the multi-instance setting by treating each clique as a consensus set for one instance. It replaces the global spatial consistency used by ECC and PointCLM with a local spatial consistency module (SCASA from Qin et al., 2023) to better handle overlapping instances, and incorporates a second-order compatibility measure (Chen et al., 2022) for sparser graph construction. The key conceptual departure from prior multi-instance methods is shifting the clustering burden from high-dimensional correspondence features (requiring spectral clustering) to low-dimensional 6D/7D transformation vectors (amenable to DBSCAN or Chameleon), eliminating iterative pose refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-instance point cloud registration remains underexplored, and PointMC demonstrates that local spatial consistency is meaningfully superior to global spatial consistency when instances overlap—a practical scenario in indoor scenes. The efficiency gain (0.04s vs. 0.05s for PointCLM) is modest, but the architectural insight of clustering transformations rather than features offers a cleaner pipeline that avoids expensive spectral clustering. Open questions include the sensitivity of the clustering step to hyperparameters (acknowledged by the authors), the scalability of maximal clique search to very large correspondence sets, and whether the approach generalizes to non-rigid or partially occluded instances beyond the current rigid, moderate-overlap setting.
