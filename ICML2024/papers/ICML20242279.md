## Problem

Existing contextual pricing models either assume a fixed price elasticity (linear demand camp) or homoscedastic valuation noise (linear valuation camp), and neither captures how product features modulate price sensitivity. This paper introduces a unified model where the elasticity coefficient is linearly dependent on the context vector, equivalently yielding a heteroscedastic (feature-scaled) valuation, and asks whether near-optimal regret is achievable under this more general structure with only binary (Bernoulli) demand feedback.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the algorithm receives a d-dimensional context vector x_t (bounded in a unit ball) and a known non-increasing link function S, then outputs a scalar price p_t = J(x_t^T θ_t, x_t^T η_t) + Δ_t where Δ_t is a Rademacher perturbation of magnitude Δ. The seller observes only a binary purchase indicator 1_t ∈ {0,1} (Boolean-censored feedback), not the underlying valuation. Parameters θ, η ∈ R^d are constrained to unit balls with the additional requirement that x^T η > C_β > 0 for all feasible x, η, ensuring the elasticity coefficient stays bounded away from zero.

## Method

The PwP algorithm computes a greedy price from current parameter estimates, adds a random ±Δ perturbation to explore, and updates parameters via a single Online Newton Step (ONS) on the negative log-likelihood. The key technical insight is that the Rademacher perturbation on the price injects variance into the expected Hessian of the log-likelihood, transforming it from a rank-deficient (a−b)² form into a (a²+b²) form that is 'strongly convex enough' to serve as a valid surrogate loss—something the plain log-likelihood cannot do when elasticity is feature-dependent. This yields an O(√(dT log T)) regret upper bound for both i.i.d. and adversarial context sequences, with a matching Ω(√(dT)) lower bound. The ONS update uses a Woodbury rank-1 matrix inversion for O(d²) per-step cost, and the perturbation magnitude Δ = (d log T / T)^{1/4} balances exploration reward loss (TΔ²) against estimation error (d log T / Δ²).

## Results

PwP achieves an O(√(dT log T)) expected regret for both stochastic and adversarial context sequences, with a matching Ω(√(dT)) lower bound establishing optimality up to log factors. In numerical experiments (d=2, T=2^16), PwP achieves ~O(T^0.56) regret on stochastic inputs and ~O(T^0.513) on adversarial inputs, closely matching the theoretical rate. Under model misspecification (fully-linear heteroscedastic valuation), PwP still achieves ~O(T^0.536) regret, while the baseline RMLP-2 with the correct model degrades to a slope of 0.681.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is a modified RMLP-2 algorithm (from Javanmard & Nazerzadeh, 2019) adapted to estimate both θ* and η*. On stochastic context sequences, both PwP and modified RMLP-2 achieve comparable ~O(T^0.56) regret, consistent with the theoretical O(√(T log T)) rate. However, under an adversarial context sequence designed to exploit RMLP-2's fixed pure-exploration schedule, PwP maintains ~O(T^0.513) regret while RMLP-2 suffers linear regret, demonstrating PwP's robustness to adversarial inputs. In the model-misspecification experiment (fully-linear heteroscedastic valuation y_t = x_t^T θ* + x_t^T η* · N_t), PwP with its linear-fractional model and expanded context achieves ~O(T^0.536) regret, outperforming RMLP-2 with the correct model (slope 0.681), suggesting PwP's ONS-based convex optimization framework is more robust than non-convex MLE approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate camps in contextual dynamic pricing—the 'linear demand' model (Qiang & Bayati 2016; Ban & Keskin 2021) and the 'linear valuation' model (Javanmard & Nazerzadeh 2019; Xu & Wang 2021)—by showing they are equivalent under a shared link function, and then extending both by making the price elasticity coefficient feature-dependent (x_t^T η*), which is equivalent to heteroscedastic valuation noise. Technically, it builds directly on Xu & Wang (2021)'s ONS-based framework for the fixed-elasticity case, but overcomes the key obstacle that the negative log-likelihood is no longer 'convex enough' as a surrogate loss when elasticity varies with context, by introducing a Rademacher price perturbation that injects the necessary Fisher information. The adversarial robustness is achieved through the ONS update (Hazan 2016) rather than the epoch-based exploration of RMLP-2, which the paper shows is vulnerable to oblivious adversaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of the linear-demand and linear-valuation perspectives, together with the matching O(√(dT log T)) upper and Ω(√(dT)) lower bounds, provides a clean theoretical resolution to a modeling question that had fragmented the literature. The perturbation-as-information-gain tradeoff (losing O(Δ²) in reward to gain O(Δ²)·I in Fisher information) is a reusable conceptual tool for other online decision problems where the natural surrogate loss lacks sufficient curvature. Open questions include: removing the C_β > 0 lower bound on elasticity (which forces Ω(1/C_β) regret), extending to unknown noise distributions (where the problem becomes substantially harder and bandit-style methods are needed), and handling simultaneous nonstationarity and heteroscedasticity. The model-misspecification experiments suggest practical robustness but lack rigorous guarantees.
