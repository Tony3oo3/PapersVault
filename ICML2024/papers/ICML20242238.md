## Problem

Existing proximal causal inference methods for estimating causal effects under unmeasured confounding rely on background knowledge to justify the validity of proxy variables (NCE and NCO), and prior data-driven selection work (Kummerfeld et al., 2022) is restricted to single-treatment settings with a single unmeasured confounder. This paper addresses the more general and practically relevant scenario of multiple treatments, a single outcome, and multiple unmeasured confounders, where proxy variables may also have direct effects on the outcome.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242238/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a dataset of p observed treatments and one outcome variable, along with the known number q of unmeasured confounders (estimable via factor model significance tests). For each causal relationship X_k → Y, it outputs a valid set of q Negative Control Exposures and q Negative Control Outcomes, together with an unbiased point estimate of the total causal effect. If no valid proxy sets exist for a given relationship, the algorithm outputs NA rather than a biased estimate.

## Method

The paper first extends the Kuroki–Pearl proxy estimator from one confounder to q confounders via a determinant-based formula (Proposition 2). It then establishes two complementary identifiability routes: (1) a second-order route using rank-deficiency constraints on cross-covariance matrices, which requires either a Quadruple-disconnected NC (Assumption 2) or q+1 valid NCEs and q+1 valid NCOs (Assumption 3), yielding the Proxy-Rank algorithm; and (2) a higher-order route using the Generalized Independent Noise (GIN) constraint under non-Gaussian noise (LiNGAM), which only requires the minimal Assumption 1, yielding the Proxy-Gin algorithm. Both algorithms exhaustively search over candidate subsets of treatments, test the relevant rank or GIN conditions, and plug the selected proxies into the extended estimator. Theoretical proofs establish correctness (consistency) of both algorithms under rank-faithfulness, and experiments on synthetic and real gene-expression data confirm that Proxy-Rank and Proxy-GIN outperform naive regression and the prior FindNC method.

## Results

On synthetic Gaussian data (Figure 3 graph, normal noise, sample sizes 1k–5k), Proxy-Rank achieves near-zero bias for all three target causal effects (β_{X₂→Y}, β_{X₅→Y}, β_{X₆→Y}), outperforming NAIVE regression and FindNC (Kummerfeld et al., 2022) across all sample sizes. On non-Gaussian data (exponential noise, X₃ removed), Proxy-GIN outperforms both NAIVE and FindNC for β_{X₂→Y} and β_{X₅→Y} at all sample sizes, while Proxy-Rank fails on these two effects because no valid NCE/NCO exist in the ground-truth graph. On the real-world Mouse Obesity Dataset (17 gene expressions, 227 mice, 1 latent confounder), the method recovers effects consistent with Miao et al. (2022): Gstm2, Sirpa, and 2010002N04Rik show positive significant effects on body weight, while Dscam shows a negative effect.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: NAIVE (ordinary least-squares regression of Y on X_k) and FindNC (the DANCE algorithm of Kummerfeld et al., 2022 combined with the standard single-confounder proxy estimator). Proxy-Rank dominates both baselines in the Gaussian setting, showing consistently lower bias for all three causal effects at every sample size (1k, 3k, 5k). In the non-Gaussian setting, Proxy-GIN clearly beats both NAIVE and FindNC for the two estimable effects (β_{X₂→Y}, β_{X₅→Y}), while FindNC underperforms because it requires three Quadruple-disconnected NCs per causal relationship—a condition not met in the test graph. NAIVE is expected to be biased in all settings due to unmeasured confounding U. The paper notes that Proxy-Rank cannot estimate β_{X₂→Y} and β_{X₅→Y} in the non-Gaussian case because the ground-truth graph lacks valid NCE/NCO pairs, so it outputs NA rather than a biased estimate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Kuroki-Pearl (2014) proxy variable estimator from a single unmeasured confounder to multiple confounders via a determinant-based formulation, and generalizes the data-driven proxy selection framework of Kummerfeld et al. (2022) from single-treatment to multi-treatment settings. The proximal criteria (NCE/NCO definitions) are inherited from Miao et al. (2018a), while the higher-order identification route leverages the Generalized Independent Noise (GIN) constraint from Xie et al. (2020). A key departure from Kummerfeld et al. is that the proxy variables here are not restricted to being conditionally independent of both treatment and outcome given the confounder, and the required assumptions (one Quadruple-disconnected NC under Assumption 2) are substantially milder than the three required in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical bottleneck for proximal causal inference has been that valid NCE/NCO are typically justified by domain expertise rather than discovered from data; this paper provides the first data-driven selection algorithms for the multi-treatment, multi-confounder setting, making the method more broadly applicable. However, the approach is confined to linear causal models with a known number of confounders, and the factorial computational complexity in the number of treatments limits scalability to high-dimensional settings. Open questions include extending the framework to non-linear models, handling unknown confounder dimensionality, and relaxing the rank-faithfulness or non-Gaussianity assumptions that underpin identifiability.
