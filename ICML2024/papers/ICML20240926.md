## Problem

Federated conformal prediction (FCP) provides rigorous coverage guarantees for distributed uncertainty quantification, but is vulnerable to Byzantine failures where even a small fraction of malicious clients reporting arbitrary conformity score statistics can severely degrade the marginal coverage below the target level. No prior work addresses this vulnerability with certified robustness guarantees in the setting where malicious clients can submit arbitrary score statistics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240926/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client computes conformity scores (e.g., LAC or APS scores in [0,1]) on its local calibration set using the globally trained classifier, then sketches them into an H-dimensional histogram characterization vector (a probability vector over H subintervals of the score range) and reports it along with its local sample size to the server. The server outputs a conformal prediction set for a new test point with certified lower and upper coverage bounds, the identified set of malicious clients, and—when the malicious count is unknown—an EM-based estimate of the number of malicious clients.

## Method

Rob-FCP detects Byzantine clients in the characterization-vector space by computing pairwise ℓp distances and defining a maliciousness score as the average distance to the K_b−1 nearest neighbors; the K_b clients with the lowest scores are retained for quantile estimation. The key theoretical result (Theorem 1) certifies that the coverage lower bound approaches the desired 1−α level as the minimal benign sample size grows, with explicit penalties for Byzantine ratio, client heterogeneity (σ), and data-sketching error. When the number of malicious clients is unknown, an EM-based estimator (Theorem 2) iteratively refines K_b by maximizing the Gaussian likelihood of the top-ranked characterization vectors while minimizing the likelihood of the remainder, with a precision guarantee that improves as the malicious vectors' distance from the benign mean increases.

## Results

Under 40% malicious clients, FCP's marginal coverage drops to 0.805–0.837 under coverage attack and inflates to 1.000 (with set sizes of 10.00–200.0) under efficiency attack across all five datasets. Rob-FCP maintains marginal coverage of 0.892–0.904 and average set sizes close to benign levels (e.g., 1.344–1.804 on MNIST/SHHS/PathMNIST) under all three Byzantine attacks. Under Gaussian attack, Rob-FCP achieves coverage of 0.900–0.928 with set sizes 1.359–54.91, compared to FCP's 0.937–1.000 coverage and 1.609–61.50 set sizes. The theoretical coverage bounds from Theorem 1 are validated empirically on Tiny-ImageNet, confirming both validity and tightness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is FCP (Lu et al., 2023), the standard federated conformal prediction method without Byzantine defenses. Rob-FCP decisively outperforms FCP on every dataset and under every attack type. Under coverage attack (40% malicious clients), FCP's coverage drops by 5–9 percentage points below the 0.9 target (e.g., 0.805 on MNIST, 0.825 on Tiny-ImageNet), while Rob-FCP stays within 0.1–0.8 points of target (0.899–0.903). Under efficiency attack, FCP's set size explodes to 10.00–200.0 (complete loss of efficiency), whereas Rob-FCP keeps set sizes within 1.3–44, close to benign levels. Under Gaussian attack, FCP over-covers (up to 1.000 on PathMNIST) with inflated set sizes, while Rob-FCP maintains coverage near 0.9 with tighter sets. Rob-FCP also demonstrates robustness across varying data heterogeneity (Dirichlet β from 0.1 to 0.9) and against mimic attacks, consistently outperforming FCP in all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Federated Conformal Prediction (FCP) by Lu et al. (2023) to the Byzantine setting, where malicious clients can report arbitrary conformity score statistics. While Byzantine-robust aggregation methods (Blanchard et al. 2017, Yin et al. 2018) provide detection metrics for distributed SGD, those metrics are designed for optimization convergence and cannot be directly applied to conformal calibration to yield coverage guarantees. Rob-FCP introduces a histogram-based characterization of local score distributions and a nearest-neighbor-distance maliciousness score tailored to the conformal prediction context, filling the gap that no prior FCP work addresses Byzantine robustness with certified coverage bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that even 10–40% malicious clients can degrade FCP coverage from the target 0.9 to roughly 0.80–0.83, a vulnerability that is critical for safety-sensitive applications like medical diagnosis. Rob-FCP recovers near-benign coverage with provable bounds, and the EM-based estimator for unknown attacker counts makes the framework practical. Open questions include extending to adversarial settings where clients manipulate raw data rather than reported statistics, incorporating differential privacy into the characterization vector upload, and analyzing the breakpoint under partial (rather than full) score manipulation budgets.
