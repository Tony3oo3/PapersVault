## Problem

Existing DP accountants (moment accountant, f-DP, RDP) are designed to track privacy budget expenditure after training, but cannot a priori select DP-SGD parameters to maximize utility under a fixed (ε,δ) budget. The moment accountant of Abadi et al. (2016) leaves its constants c₁, c₂ unspecified and provides no tight closed-form relationship between noise σ, rounds T, and the privacy budget, making it impossible to directly optimize the privacy-utility-implementation tradeoff before running an experiment.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a local training dataset of size N, a target privacy budget (ε, δ) with δ typically set to 1/N, a clipping constant C, and a desired test accuracy threshold. It outputs a complete DP-SGD configuration: the noise standard deviation σ (constrained by the closed-form σ = √(2(ε+ln(1/δ)))/ε), the number of communication rounds T ≈ γθ²k²/ε, the mini-batch size s = K/T, and a diminishing step-size schedule. A key intermediate artifact is the utility graph, which pre-computes the best-case accuracy as a function of σ and C without performing any private training, allowing the user to select σ before committing to a full DP-SGD run.

## Method

The paper non-trivially improves the moment accountant analysis by making the previously unknown constants c₁ and c₂ explicit functions of T and other parameters, yielding the closed-form guarantee: DP-SGD is (ε,δ)-DP if σ = √(2(ε+ln(1/δ)))/ε, δ ≤ 1/N, ε < 0.5, (2/e)²k² ≥ 1/2 + ln(1/δ), and T ≥ γθ²k²/ε where k = K/N measures total gradient computations in epochs. Tightness is established via the f-DP framework, showing T cannot be reduced by more than a constant factor ≈ 4 without violating the DP guarantee. The proactive DP framework then combines three tools: (1) the tight closed-form DP formula linking all parameters, (2) a utility graph that estimates the accuracy-vs-σ tradeoff by adding noise to a pre-trained non-private model, and (3) a DP calculator that, given (C, σ, k), solves for ε, γ, T, and s to produce a complete experiment setup. The implementation flow sets δ = 1/N, searches the utility graph for the largest σ meeting the accuracy target, computes ε from the closed form, verifies ε < 0.5, and sets T to the lower bound to minimize noise aggregation rounds while maximizing accuracy.

## Results

On the strongly convex LIBSVM problem, the proactive DP framework achieves 86% test accuracy at ε=0.04945 (vs. 93% without DP, a 7% drop) and 91% at ε=0.1, using σ≈19.2 and C=0.1. On the non-convex MNIST image classification task with LeNet, ε=0.15 yields ~93% test accuracy compared to 98% without differential privacy (a 5% drop), with σ≤12 and C=0.025. The framework demonstrates that (ε=0.05, δ=1/N)-DP is achievable on LIBSVM while maintaining good accuracy, and the closed-form relation σ=√(2(ε+ln(1/δ))/ε) enables a priori parameter selection without running private training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Abadi et al. (2016), at ε=2 and k=7 epochs both methods achieve ~60% test accuracy, but the paper's method incurs only a 14.14% accuracy deduction (using AlexNet with 74.74% non-DP baseline) versus Abadi's 26% deduction (86% non-DP baseline with a simpler architecture), indicating a tighter privacy-utility tradeoff. Against the state-of-the-art scale-based method of De et al. (2022), which achieves 65.9% on CIFAR-10 using WRN-40-4 (98% non-DP, a 32.1% deduction), the paper's method achieves ~60% for ε∈[0.5,3] with a much simpler network. At ε=1 specifically, the paper reports better test accuracy than De et al.'s 56.8%, while also supporting smaller ε values (down to 0.05 on LIBSVM and 0.15 on MNIST) that De et al. does not report. The paper's advantage is thus in achieving comparable or better accuracy at smaller privacy budgets and with simpler model architectures, though the absolute accuracy numbers are lower due to the use of smaller networks (LeNet, AlexNet) rather than wide ResNets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DP-SGD framework and moment accountant method of Abadi et al. (2016), which provided only loose bounds with unknown constants c₁ and c₂. The authors non-trivially refine the moment accountant analysis to make these constants explicit as functions of T and other parameters, yielding a tight closed-form (ε,δ)-DP guarantee. The tightness result is validated against the f-DP framework of Dong et al. (2021), which is known to be the most general DP accounting framework but yields only computer-evaluable (non-closed-form) expressions. The asynchronous SGD setting follows the Hogwild! philosophy (Recht et al., 2011), extending the analysis to varying sample size sequences and client-adaptive computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The proactive DP framework inverts the traditional DP accounting paradigm: instead of tracking privacy expenditure after the fact, it enables a priori selection of all DP-SGD parameters (σ, ε, δ, T, s, step-size scheme) to simultaneously satisfy a privacy budget and a utility target. The tightness result (T ≈ 2k²/ε cannot be improved by more than a factor of ~4) is the first such characterization for the moment accountant method, giving practitioners confidence that the recommended round count is near-optimal. However, experimental validation is limited to simple problems (logistic regression on LIBSVM, LeNet on MNIST) and the utility graph is explicitly a heuristic that does not guarantee the true DP-SGD accuracy will match predictions. Open questions include whether the framework scales to large architectures (ResNet, Transformer) and complex datasets (ImageNet, CIFAR-100) where the non-convex landscape and larger N may alter the σ-accuracy trade-off.
