## Problem

Zeroth-order (ZO) optimization methods like MeZO enable memory-efficient LM fine-tuning using only forward passes, but they suffer from instability at smaller batch sizes, a significant convergence gap to first-order methods in non-prompted settings, and a reliance on carefully crafted task prompts. To the best of the authors' knowledge, variance-reduction techniques had not been explored for ZO optimization in the context of LM fine-tuning, leaving a gap between ZO methods' practical performance and that of standard first-order fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained LM parameters (masked models up to RoBERTa-large at 355M params, autoregressive models up to OPT-6.7B at 6.7B params) and small training sets of 512 text samples from GLUE/SuperGLUE classification tasks. Gradients are estimated exclusively via forward passes: two perturbed forward evaluations per SPSA step replace backpropagation, meaning the method requires no gradient computation and is compatible with non-differentiable objectives or black-box API access. The output is fine-tuned model parameters, recoverable from a stored random seed and a sequence of loss-difference scalars without re-running forward passes.

## Method

MeZO-SVRG is a memory-efficient variant of ZO-SVRG that periodically (every q steps) computes a full-batch SPSA gradient estimator and blends it with a minibatch estimator via the control-variate update θ^(t+1) = θ^(t) − η[∇̄f_I(θ^(t)) − ∇̄f_I(θ̄) + ∇̄f(θ̄)]. Two key implementation choices distinguish it from prior ZO-SVRG: (1) all samples in a batch are perturbed along a single shared random direction z, enabling data-parallel forward passes and eliminating the memory/speed trade-off of per-sample perturbations; (2) in-place parameter updates and a seed-based resampling trick reduce the memory footprint to 3× inference (vs. 5× for a naive ZO-SVRG implementation). The method uses two independent learning rates (η₁ > η₂ for full-batch vs. minibatch steps) and a simple loss-feedback annealing schedule. A convergence guarantee of O(d/T + 1(b<n)/b) is established for the single-perturbation-vector estimators, confirming that larger batch sizes reduce iteration complexity.

## Results

On masked LMs (DistilBERT, RoBERTa-large) and autoregressive LMs (GPT-2, OPT-2.7B, OPT-6.7B) across GLUE/SuperGLUE tasks, MeZO-SVRG achieves up to 20% higher test accuracy than MeZO (e.g., 84% vs 56% on RoBERTa-large SST-2 full-parameter FT; 53% vs 41% on GPT-2 MNLI). MeZO-SVRG also surpasses MeZO's peak test accuracy in roughly half the GPU-hours (e.g., 5.6 vs 19.4 GPU-hrs on GPT-2 SST-2). In terms of memory, MeZO-SVRG requires at least 2× less peak GPU memory than FO-SGD for autoregressive models (e.g., 4.72 GB vs 18.55 GB for RoBERTa-large at batch size 64, context length 128), with savings reaching 76% at larger batch sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MeZO (Malladi et al., 2023), MeZO-SVRG wins on every task and model tested, with improvements ranging from 4 to 20 percentage points in test accuracy (e.g., +28 pts on RoBERTa-large SST-2 Full FT: 84% vs 56%; +12 pts on GPT-2 MNLI: 53% vs 41%). MeZO-SVRG also achieves these gains in roughly half the GPU-hours (Table 4: e.g., 5.6 vs 19.4 GPU-hrs on GPT-2 SST-2). Against FO-SGD (Robbins & Monro, 1951), MeZO-SVRG narrows the gap substantially but does not match it in full-parameter settings (e.g., 84% vs 96% on RoBERTa-large SST-2); however, in partial-parameter fine-tuning, MeZO-SVRG often reaches within 5% of FO-SGD (e.g., 74% vs 85% on DistilBERT SST-2 Partial FT). Against FO-Adam (Kingma & Ba, 2015), the comparison is limited to memory profiling: MeZO-SVRG uses 35–79% less peak GPU memory than FO-Adam across batch sizes and context lengths (Table 3), while FO-Adam cannot even fit OPT-6.7B on a 40 GB GPU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously separate lines of work: MeZO (Malladi et al., 2023), which made zeroth-order SGD memory-efficient for LM fine-tuning via in-place perturbation, and ZO-SVRG (Liu et al., 2018), which introduced variance reduction for zeroth-order optimization. The authors identify that MeZO suffers from instability at small batch sizes and a significant convergence gap to first-order methods in non-prompted settings, then address both by transplanting SVRG's control-variate mechanism into MeZO's memory-efficient framework. A key engineering departure from the original ZO-SVRG is the use of single-perturbation SPSA estimators (perturbing the entire batch in one direction) to exploit data parallelism while maintaining a 3×-inference memory footprint, versus the 5× required by a naive ZO-SVRG implementation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners fine-tuning LMs under tight GPU memory budgets, MeZO-SVRG offers a practical drop-in improvement over MeZO: up to 20% higher test accuracy with half the GPU-hours, and at least 2× less memory than first-order SGD with savings that grow with batch size and context length. The method also removes the reliance on carefully crafted task prompts, broadening applicability to standard fine-tuning pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 7B parameters, how it interacts with PEFT methods like LoRA, and whether it can be applied to black-box API-only models where even forward-pass gradients are restricted. The residual gap to first-order methods, though substantially narrowed, remains a limitation for tasks where peak accuracy is critical.
