## Problem

Adversarial energy-based models (EBMs) avoid expensive MCMC sampling but suffer from three persistent limitations: unstable minimax training on complex multi-modal distributions, reliance on asymmetric KL divergence that inadequately captures the energy distribution, and an intractable entropy term that creates a trade-off between generation quality and density estimation. While recent work has combined EBMs with diffusion processes (DRL, DDGAN), these methods either lack a useful density estimate or still require MCMC, leaving a significant gap between adversarial EBMs and mainstream generative models like GANs and diffusion models.

## Input / Output

The generator network takes a noisy image x_t, a d-dimensional latent variable z drawn from N(0,I), and a discrete time step t as inputs, producing a denoised estimate x_{t-1} through the reparameterized conditional q(x_{t-1}|x_t, x_0=G_φ(x_t,z,t)). The energy function takes x_t and t and outputs a scalar energy value (negative ℓ₂ norm between input and network output), serving as an unnormalized log-likelihood for density estimation. Experiments span 2D toy data, MNIST (28×28), CIFAR-10 (32×32), CelebA (64×64), and LSUN church (128×128), all scaled to [-1,1], with T=4 diffusion steps and NFE=4 for sampling.

## Method

DDAEBM embeds a sequence of adversarial EBMs into each denoising step of a VP-SDE diffusion process, so each EBM learns a simpler conditional distribution q(x_{t-1}|x_t) rather than the full marginal. The generated denoising distribution is defined via reparameterization as p_φ(x_{t-1}|x_t) = ∫ p(z) q(x_{t-1}|x_t, G_φ(x_t,z,t)) dz, where G_φ is a z-conditioned NCSN++/DDPM++ generator. To address the asymmetric KL limitation, the generator is trained with a symmetric Jeffrey divergence (forward KL + reverse KL), and the intractable entropy of the generated distribution is handled by introducing a variational Gaussian posterior q_ψ(z|x_{t-1},x_t) parameterized by a CNN encoder. The reverse KL term is bounded using an ELBO with an importance ratio inspired by ACT, and the energy function is regularized with a gradient penalty to stabilize the minimax game. This yields an MCMC-free training and sampling procedure that simultaneously produces high-quality generations and a usable energy function for density estimation and OOD detection.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, DDAEBM achieves FID 4.82 and IS 8.86 with only 4 NFE, outperforming all MCMC-free adversarial EBMs by a large margin (next best: BiDVL at FID 20.75) and approaching MCMC-based methods like DRL (FID 9.58) and CLEL-large (FID 8.61). On CelebA 64×64, DDAEBM obtains FID 10.29, and on 128×128 LSUN church it reaches FID 13.80, substantially outperforming DRL (FID 26.69 under the same evaluation protocol). The energy function also provides competitive OOD detection (AUROC 0.83 on SVHN) and achieves the best mode coverage on StackedMNIST (KL 0.033, 1000/1000 modes).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDAEBM dramatically outperforms all MCMC-free adversarial EBMs on CIFAR-10: it reduces FID from 20.75 (BiDVL, the previous best) to 4.82, a 4.3× improvement, while also reporting IS (8.86) where most baselines do not. Among MCMC-based EBMs, DDAEBM (FID 4.82, NFE 4) surpasses DRL (FID 9.58, NFE 180) and CLEL-large (FID 8.61, NFE 1200) in both quality and sampling efficiency. Against non-EBM generative models, DDAEBM is close to DDGAN (FID 3.75) and can match it (FID 3.73) with one additional energy-guided denoising step, but trails Score SDE (FID 2.20, NFE 2000) and StyleGAN2 (FID 2.92, NFE 1) which do not provide density estimates. On OOD detection, DDAEBM ties VAEBM and VERA on SVHN (AUROC 0.83) but lags slightly on CelebA (0.70 vs. 0.77 for VAEBM/BiDVL). On StackedMNIST mode counting, DDAEBM achieves the lowest KL divergence (0.033) among all compared methods including DDGAN (0.071) and MEG (0.042), confirming superior mode coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

DDAEBM sits at the intersection of adversarial EBMs (VERA, EBM-BB, BiDVL) and denoising diffusion models (DDPM, Score SDE). Prior work like DRL (Gao et al., 2021) embedded a single EBM into a diffusion process but still required MCMC for training, while DDGAN (Xiao et al., 2022) combined GANs with diffusion for fast sampling but sacrificed density estimation. This paper departs from both by placing a full adversarial EBM (generator + energy function minimax game) at every denoising step, using a symmetric Jeffreys divergence and a variational posterior to address the three known instabilities of adversarial EBM training (unbalanced minimax, asymmetric KL, intractable entropy). The reparameterized denoising distribution with latent variable z is borrowed from DDGAN, but the EBM structure and Jeffreys-based objective are the key differentiators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a substantial gap between adversarial EBMs and mainstream generative models (FID 4.82 vs. 20+ for prior adversarial EBMs on CIFAR-10) while retaining the ability to provide an unnormalized density estimate — something GAN-based competitors like DDGAN cannot do. This makes the energy function directly useful for downstream tasks like OOD detection and density estimation without additional MCMC. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 128×128 images, whether the energy function's 'heavier workload' compared to a GAN discriminator limits further quality gains, and whether the T=4 time-step choice generalizes or requires careful tuning per dataset. The reliance on a single extra denoising step to match DDGAN's FID also suggests the base model is not yet fully competitive on pure generation quality.
