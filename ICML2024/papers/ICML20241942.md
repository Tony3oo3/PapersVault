## Problem

Pseudo-labeling-based semi-supervised learning suffers from model overconfidence: with limited labeled data, the teacher model confidently assigns incorrect pseudo-labels, and the standard cross-entropy loss then forces the student to learn representations in the wrong direction. Existing methods focus on improving pseudo-label quality (thresholding, filtering) but ignore the inter-class semantic relationships that could regularize training when a pseudo-label is wrong.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241942/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs of weakly and strongly augmented views of each unlabeled sample (RGB images at 32×32 for CIFAR/STL-10, 224×224 for ImageNet, or text reviews for language experiments). The teacher model processes the weak view to produce a confidence-weighted pseudo-label, while the student processes the strong view to produce a full class probability vector. The output is a standard class label prediction; the novel component is a K×K cost matrix bootstrapped from the classification head weights that modulates how the student's probability distribution is penalized relative to the pseudo-label.

## Method

OTMatch reinterprets pseudo-labeling consistency as matching two semantic distributions via inverse optimal transport, then adds an OT-based unsupervised loss (L_un3) on top of FreeMatch. The key mechanism is bootstrapping the OT cost matrix from the model's own classification head: after normalizing each class weight vector v_k, the cost is set to C_kk' = 1 − ⟨v_k, v_k'⟩ and updated with momentum (m=0.999) throughout training, so the cost evolves with the model's learned inter-class geometry. A lemma shows that when the pseudo-label is a point mass and the student prediction is a distribution, the OT distance reduces to a simple weighted sum over K classes, giving O(K) computation per sample. The final training objective is L_OTMatch = L_FreeMatch + λ·L_un3, where λ is tuned per dataset (0.01–0.5), and the OT loss can also be interpreted as aligning a self-attention-style aggregation of class prototypes with the pseudo-label's head vector.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 10 labels per class, OTMatch achieves 4.89% error rate, substantially outperforming FreeMatch (8.07%) and FixMatch (24.79%). On STL-10 with 40 labels, OTMatch reaches 12.10% error versus FreeMatch's 15.56%. On ImageNet (100 labels/class), OTMatch achieves 39.29% top-1 error and 17.77% top-5 error, improving over FreeMatch (40.57% / 18.77%). On language datasets (Amazon/Yelp Review), OTMatch integrated with FlexMatch achieves the best error rates among compared methods, e.g., 43.81% on Amazon Review with 250 labels vs. FlexMatch's 45.75%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OTMatch outperforms FreeMatch (its direct baseline) across all vision benchmarks: CIFAR-10 (10/40/250 labels), CIFAR-100 (400/2500 labels), STL-10 (40/1000 labels), and ImageNet (100 labels/class). The largest gains appear in the most label-scarce settings—CIFAR-10 with 10 labels (4.89% vs. 8.07%, a 3.18-point reduction) and STL-10 with 40 labels (12.10% vs. 15.56%, a 3.46-point reduction)—consistent with the paper's motivation of alleviating overconfidence under limited supervision. OTMatch also surpasses FixMatch, FlexMatch, and all earlier methods (MixMatch, ReMixMatch, UDA, Dash, MPL) on every vision dataset. On language datasets, OTMatch (integrated with FlexMatch) beats FlexMatch, CoMatch, AdaMatch, and SimMatch on both Amazon and Yelp Review, though Dash retains a lead on Yelp Review (35.10% vs. 43.61% at 250 labels). The per-iteration overhead is minimal: 0.25 s vs. 0.23 s for FreeMatch on CIFAR-10.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pseudo-labeling-based semi-supervised learning lineage (FixMatch → FlexMatch → FreeMatch) by adding an optimal transport loss that incorporates inter-class semantic relationships, rather than the standard cross-entropy alignment with a single pseudo-label. Conceptually, it builds on the inverse optimal transport (IOT) framework of Shi et al. (2023), which showed that cross-entropy and contrastive losses can be recovered as special cases of OT, and extends this lens to the semi-supervised setting to derive FreeMatch's thresholding mechanism from first principles. The key departure from prior work is that the OT cost matrix is bootstrapped from the model's own classification head weights (learning dynamics) rather than from external knowledge like word embeddings, and the resulting loss is computable in O(K) time via a structural lemma.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a unifying theoretical framework that reframes pseudo-labeling methods as distribution-matching problems, which could guide future algorithm design beyond ad-hoc thresholding heuristics. The O(K) computational overhead and the fact that the cost is self-learned (no external data needed) make the approach broadly applicable and easy to integrate with existing pipelines. However, the empirical gains are most pronounced in extreme low-label regimes (e.g., CIFAR-10 with 10 labels) and become marginal at higher label budgets, suggesting the OT regularization is most valuable when the model is most overconfident. Open questions include whether the cost-bootstrapping mechanism generalizes to non-CNN backbones or very large class spaces, and whether the IOT perspective can yield new algorithmic insights beyond the current OTMatch formulation.
