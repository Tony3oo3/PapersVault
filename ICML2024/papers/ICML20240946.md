## Problem

All prior work on inexact first-order oracles analyzes or adapts specific algorithms (e.g., gradient descent, proximal gradient) to handle inexact information, with no algorithm-agnostic guarantee. This paper asks whether one can take any first-order algorithm designed for exact oracles and, without knowledge of its internal logic, make it work with inexact oracle responses in a universal black-box manner.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a black-box first-order algorithm A (with a known error guarantee err(T,M,R) for exact oracles) and an η-approximate first-order oracle for f (and optionally an η_C-approximate separation oracle for a convex constraint set C). It produces a feasible solution x̄ ∈ X (or C ∩ Z) after T iterations with the guarantee f(x̄) ≤ OPT(f) + err(T, M+η/(2R), R) + 4ηT. The algorithm A is consumed purely as a query-response oracle: the procedure intercepts A's queries, translates inexact oracle responses into consistent exact first-order pairs, and feeds them back to A without ever inspecting A's internal state or update rules.

## Method

The core mechanism is an online construction of a modified convex function F that is pointwise close to the true f (‖F−f‖∞ ≤ 2ηT) and for which the inexact oracle responses, after a small modification, serve as exact first-order information. For the nonsmooth case, F_t is maintained as the pointwise maximum of affine functions built from shifted oracle responses, with a minimal downward shift s* chosen at each step to preserve convexity and compatibility with previously reported pairs. For the smooth case, a localized randomized smoothing (uniform-ball averaging) is applied to these piecewise-linear functions, with a novel 'ball protection' invariant ensuring that the smoothed values and gradients at previously queried points remain unchanged across iterations. A key technical ingredient is a new smoothing lemma showing that when the base function is already close to an α-smooth function, the smoothed version inherits a significantly tighter smoothness bound (α√d(4√(5(T+1))+3) rather than the standard M√d/r), and a maximal coupling argument to control gradient Lipschitzness. The entire construction is online and requires only O(T²d) operations, with no access to A's internals.

## Results

The paper establishes universal transfer theorems: any first-order algorithm with error err(T,M,R) under exact oracles can be run with η-approximate oracles, incurring additional error 4ηT (nonsmooth, Theorem 2.2) or 5η(T+2) with a degraded smoothness constant α′=α√d(4√(5(T+1))+3) (smooth, Theorem 2.3). Applied to subgradient descent, this yields error O((MR+η)/√T)+2Tη; applied to Nesterov's accelerated method, it yields O(αR²√d/T^1.5+ηT), preserving a superlinear rate over the nonsmooth O(1/√T) baseline. For cutting-plane methods, the error becomes O((MR+η/2)exp(−T/poly(d)))+4ηT, retaining exponential convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison (Appendix C) is with Devolder et al. (2014), which analyzes specific algorithms (gradient descent, accelerated gradient descent) under inexact (δ,L)-oracles. For nonsmooth M-Lipschitz functions, running subgradient descent directly with an η-approximate oracle requires η=O(1/T^0.5) for O(1/√T) error, whereas the universal black-box wrapper requires η=O(1/T^1.5) for the same rate. For L-smooth functions with Nesterov acceleration, Devolder et al. need δ=O(1/T^3) (i.e., η=O(1/T^3)) to recover the O(1/T^2) rate, while this paper achieves only O(√d/T^1.5) with η=O(1/T^2.5), losing both a √d factor and the full acceleration. The paper explicitly acknowledges that algorithm-specific analyses yield tighter bounds; the trade-off is that the universal result applies to any first-order algorithm (cutting-plane, bundle, projection-free, mixed-integer) without knowledge of its internals, and is the first to handle inexact oracles for structured nonconvex ground sets like mixed-integer constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

Prior work on inexact first-order oracles (Devolder et al. 2014, Schmidt et al. 2011, Lan 2009) has been algorithm-specific, analyzing how particular methods like (accelerated) gradient descent or proximal gradient behave under noisy subgradients. This paper departs by providing a universal, black-box transfer theorem: any first-order algorithm for convex optimization can be wrapped to work with inexact oracles without knowledge of its internals. The technical core builds on Nesterov's smoothing framework (2005) and Yousefian et al.'s smoothness guarantees for randomized smoothing (2012), but introduces a novel online construction of Lipschitz/smooth convex extensions from inexact first-order pairs—a non-trivial step since inexact information does not directly admit a convex extension (as illustrated in Figure 1).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The universality is the key practical contribution: it immediately applies to projection-free methods, cutting-plane methods, bundle methods, and mixed-integer convex optimization algorithms (the first such analysis under inexact oracles), without requiring algorithm-specific re-derivation. A notable limitation is that the smooth case (Theorem 2.3) does not fully recover Nesterov's O(1/T²) acceleration, yielding only O(1/T^1.5) even with optimally tuned oracle accuracy, due to the √d factor introduced by the smoothing step. Open questions include whether the smooth-case gap can be closed, whether strongly convex variants yield tighter bounds, and whether the O(ηT) accumulation term is fundamental or an artifact of the black-box construction.
