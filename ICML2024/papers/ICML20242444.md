## Problem

First-order optimizers like SGD and Adam require back-propagation, which incurs substantial memory overhead (e.g., 12× inference memory for OPT-13B) during LLM fine-tuning. While MeZO (Malladi et al., 2023) demonstrated that ZO-SGD can fine-tune LLMs without BP, the broader landscape of ZO optimization methods—beyond the basic ZO-SGD—remained unexplored, leaving open whether other ZO techniques offer better accuracy-efficiency trade-offs for LLM fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes pre-trained LLMs (Roberta-Large, OPT-1.3B/13B, LLaMA2-7B, Vicuna-7B, Mistral-7B) and downstream task data (SST2, RTE, COPA, WinoGrande, MultiRC) as input, producing fine-tuned model parameters evaluated on test accuracy, peak GPU memory, GPU count, and per-iteration runtime. ZO methods consume only function values via forward passes with a configurable query budget q (default q=1), while FO baselines require full back-propagation; task alignment via prompt templates (e.g., converting classification to next-token prediction) is applied before optimization begins.

## Method

The paper benchmarks six BP-free optimizers—ZO-SGD, ZO-SGD-Sign, ZO-SGD-MMT, ZO-SGD-Cons, ZO-Adam, and Forward-Grad—across five LLM families, four tasks of increasing complexity, and five fine-tuning schemes (full-tuning, LoRA, prefix-tuning, prompt-tuning), using the randomized gradient estimator (RGE) with central differences along random Gaussian directions. Key empirical findings include the critical role of task alignment (without it, ZO methods lose ~10% accuracy), Forward-Grad as a competitive BP-free baseline that approaches FO performance at high query budgets, and the scalability bottleneck where FO methods pull ahead on harder tasks. Three novel enhancements are proposed: block-wise ZO descent (splitting parameters into p blocks for lower-variance estimation, outperforming full-model RGE at equal query cost), hybrid ZO-FO training (applying ZO to shallow layers and FO to deep layers, achieving near-FO accuracy with ~10% memory reduction), and sparsity-induced ZO gradient estimation (applying random sparse masks to RGE perturbations, where moderate sparsity ~20-30% improves accuracy over vanilla ZO-SGD).

## Results

On COPA with LoRA fine-tuning, ZO methods are competitive with FO baselines: ZO-Adam matches FO-SGD at 89% on OPT-13B, and ZO-SGD matches FO-SGD at 90% on Mistral-7B. On the harder WinoGrande task, a consistent 5–6% accuracy gap persists between FO and ZO methods (e.g., FO-Adam 68.9% vs. ZO-Adam 64.0% on OPT-13B). In terms of memory efficiency, ZO-SGD requires only 64 GB (1×A100) versus 245 GB (4×A100) for FO-Adam when full-fine-tuning OPT-13B, and reduces per-iteration runtime by ~42% compared to FO-SGD. The proposed block-wise ZO variant improves SST2 accuracy from 90.83% (MeZO) to 93.69% at the same query budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FO-SGD and FO-Adam, ZO methods are within 1–2% on COPA (e.g., ZO-Adam 89% vs. FO-SGD 88% on OPT-13B; ZO-SGD 90% vs. FO-SGD 90% on Mistral-7B) but trail by 5–6% on WinoGrande, revealing a scalability bottleneck for ZO on complex reasoning tasks. Forward-Grad, the BP-free baseline using forward-mode AD, is competitive with ZO methods on COPA (89% on OPT-13B, matching ZO-Adam) but underperforms on WinoGrande (62.9% vs. 64.0% for ZO-Adam), and its advantage over ZO only emerges at high query budgets (q>500). Among ZO variants, ZO-Adam is the strongest on SST2 (best in 4/8 PEFT settings) but at the cost of 158 GB memory vs. 64 GB for ZO-SGD; ZO-SGD-Cons and ZO-SGD-MMT offer stable intermediate performance, while ZO-SGD-Sign is the weakest (52.5% on Roberta-Large FT SST2). ZO-SGD (MeZO) does not consistently dominate other ZO methods, confirming that the simplest ZO approach is not always optimal. The proposed block-wise ZO variant outperforms both MeZO (q=1) and full-model ZO-SGD (q=26) on both SST2 (93.69% vs. 90.83%/91.28%) and WinoGrande (57.2% vs. 55.5%/55.7%), demonstrating that structured gradient estimation reduces variance more effectively than simply increasing the query budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Malladi et al. (2023), which introduced ZO-SGD (MeZO) for BP-free LLM fine-tuning, by systematically benchmarking five additional ZO/FP-free optimizers (ZO-Adam, ZO-SGD-Sign, ZO-SGD-MMT, ZO-SGD-Cons, Forward-Grad) across five LLM families, three task complexities, and five fine-tuning schemes. It draws on the broader ZO optimization literature (Ghadimi & Lan 2013; Nesterov & Spokoiny 2017; Duchi et al. 2015) and the forward-mode AD literature (Baydin et al. 2022; Ren et al. 2022) to position Forward-Grad as an overlooked BP-free baseline. The paper departs from prior work by proposing three novel ZO enhancements—block-wise descent, hybrid ZO-FO layer splitting, and sparsity-induced perturbation—that go beyond the single-method scope of MeZO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides the first practical guidance for selecting among ZO optimizers for LLM fine-tuning, revealing that no single ZO method dominates across all settings and that task alignment is disproportionately critical for ZO (≈10% accuracy drop) versus FO (≈1–2%). The memory analysis showing ZO-SGD at 64 GB versus FO-Adam at 245 GB for OPT-13B full fine-tuning is a concrete, actionable efficiency win for on-device and resource-constrained deployment. Open questions remain: the 5–6% accuracy gap on complex reasoning tasks (WinoGrande) suggests ZO methods hit a scalability ceiling, and whether the proposed enhancements (block-wise, hybrid, sparsity) close this gap at larger scales (70B+) is untested. The paper also leaves open whether Forward-Grad's theoretical unbiasedness advantage can be practically exploited without prohibitive query budgets.
