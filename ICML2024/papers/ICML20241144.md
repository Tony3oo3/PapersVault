## Problem

Existing evaluations of quantized LLMs are fragmented: prior work either focuses solely on Weight and Activation quantization for language modeling tasks, or examines only a narrow subset of emergent abilities. No comprehensive study has systematically assessed how quantizing different tensor types (Weight, Activation, KV Cache) at various bit-widths affects performance across diverse task categories (basic NLP, emergent abilities, trustworthiness, dialogue, long-context) and model families of varying sizes, leaving practitioners without principled guidance for selecting quantization configurations.

## Input / Output

The evaluation pipeline takes FP16 pre-trained LLMs spanning 11 families (OPT, LLaMA2, Falcon, Bloomz, Mistral, ChatGLM, Vicuna, LongChat, StableLM, Gemma, Mamba) from 125M to 180B parameters, applies uniform quantization at bit-widths from 2 to 8 bits on Weight, Activation, and/or KV Cache tensors, and measures performance on 18 benchmarks. Outputs include task-specific metrics (accuracy, perplexity, GPT-4 dialogue scores), tensor-level statistics (kurtosis, AbsMax, standard deviation) that explain quantization sensitivity, and per-task-type bit-width recommendations bounded by a 2% accuracy loss threshold. The quantization formats follow standard practice: asymmetric group-wise for weights and KV Cache (group size matching head dimension, typically 128), symmetric per-token for activations.

## Method

The paper conducts a systematic empirical evaluation across four dimensions: tensor type (Weight-only, Weight-Activation, KV Cache), model family and size, task category, and quantization bit-width. For each configuration, the authors apply uniform quantization with task-appropriate granularity and measure performance degradation relative to the FP16 baseline. A key analytical contribution is the use of tensor statistics—particularly kurtosis—to explain why larger models tolerate Weight and KV Cache quantization better (lower kurtosis, fewer outliers) while being more sensitive to Activation quantization (higher kurtosis with more outliers). The study also evaluates SOTA recovery methods (AWQ for Weight-only, SmoothQuant for Weight-Activation) at extreme bit-widths (W2, W4A4) and analyzes failure modes qualitatively, including attention map changes in ethics tasks and error taxonomy for multi-step reasoning. Findings are organized into tensor-level, model-level, and task-level insights, culminating in a structured bit-width recommendation table per task type.

## Results

Across 11 model families (125M–180B) and 18 benchmarks, W4, W4A8, and KV4 quantization preserves >98% of FP16 performance on basic NLP tasks for most LLMs, while W3 causes significant degradation for models <13B and W2 leads to near-complete performance collapse. For emergent abilities, Multi-Step Reasoning and Self-Calibration are notably more sensitive than In-Context Learning and Instruction-Following, with W3 LLaMA2-7B losing nearly all Self-Calibration ability. On dialogue (MT-bench), W8/W8A8/KV4 maintain <2% GPT-4 score loss, but W4 causes >0.3 point drops (e.g., LLaMA2-70B drops from 6.26 to 5.91). For long-context tasks (≥4K tokens), KV Cache quantization is more damaging than Weight quantization, with KV8 already causing notable degradation in the LongChat family.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the simple Round-to-Nearest (RTN) quantization baseline against two SOTA PTQ methods: AWQ (Lin et al., 2023) for Weight-only quantization and SmoothQuant (Xiao et al., 2023) for Weight-Activation quantization. AWQ provides meaningful recovery at W3 (e.g., LLaMA2-7B improves from 66.41% to 69.63% on LAMBADA; LLaMA2-70B from 76.46% to 78.73%), but cannot rescue W2 where RTN already collapses to 0%. SmoothQuant partially recovers W4A4 performance (LLaMA2-7B: 4.31% → 25.56%; LLaMA2-70B: 0.04% → 38.11%), yet remains far below FP16 (73.32% and 78.96% respectively). On MT-bench dialogue, SmoothQuant at W4A4 lifts LLaMA2-13B from 1.00 to 2.34 (Turn 1) and LLaMA2-70B from 1.00 to 2.09, still well below FP16 scores of 5.72 and 6.26. Overall, neither AWQ nor SmoothQuant can restore FP16-level performance at extreme low bit-widths (W2, W4A4), confirming that further research on quantization schemes or QAT is needed for those regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of quantization evaluation work initiated by Yao et al. (2023), who studied Weight and Activation quantization on language modeling tasks, and Liu et al. (2023b), who focused on emergent abilities of quantized LLMs. It carves new ground by being the first to systematically evaluate all three tensor types (Weight, Activation, KV Cache) across 11 model families (125M–180B parameters) and five task categories (basic NLP, emergent abilities, trustworthiness, dialogue, long-context), filling the gap where prior work either ignored KV Cache quantization or covered only a narrow slice of tasks. The paper evaluates SOTA quantization methods (AWQ, SmoothQuant, GPTQ) as baselines but does not propose a new quantization algorithm; its contribution is the comprehensive empirical framework and the resulting design guidelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides actionable bit-width recommendations (e.g., W4/KV4 for most tasks, KV8 for long-context ≥4K) that directly inform deployment decisions, and reveals several non-obvious scaling behaviors: larger models are *less* tolerant of Activation quantization (due to increasing kurtosis) while being *more* tolerant of Weight quantization, and MoE models like Mixtral-8x7B are more quantization-sensitive than dense models of comparable effective size. Key limitations include the restriction to PTQ (no QAT), the absence of ablations on group size, and the caveat that specific bit-width recommendations may not generalize to new architectures or tasks. Open questions include the cumulative effect of quantization over multi-turn dialogues, whether mix-precision strategies (different bit-widths per layer) can further close the gap, and how QAT might rescue extreme low-bit regimes (W2, W4A4) where current PTQ methods fail.
