## Problem

Supervised domain adaptation from a public source to a private target domain requires differentially private algorithms, but prior work (Bassily et al., 2022) only addressed the unsupervised setting with squared-loss regression and weaker theoretical guarantees. This paper fills the gap by providing (ε,δ)-DP supervised adaptation algorithms with formal convergence guarantees for both convex (linear regression) and non-convex (Lipschitz-smooth loss) settings, grounded in the stronger optimization framework of Awasthi et al. (2024).

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a public labeled sample S^Pub of size m (drawn i.i.d. from the source distribution) and a private labeled sample S^Priv of size n (drawn i.i.d. from the target distribution), with bounded inputs (‖x‖₂ ≤ r) and bounded outputs (|y| ≤ 1 for regression). It produces a predictor h (parameterized by w with ‖w‖₂ ≤ Λ) that is (ε,δ)-differentially private with respect to the private target sample, along with reparameterized weight variables u that encode how much each sample's loss is emphasized. The privacy guarantee is with respect to single-entry changes in S^Priv only; the public sample S^Pub is treated as fixed and non-sensitive.

## Method

The core insight is a reparameterization u_i = 1/q_i of the per-sample loss weights, which (i) transforms the jointly non-convex optimization over (w, q) into a jointly convex quadratic-over-linear problem in (w, u) for the linear regression case, and (ii) reduces the ℓ₂-sensitivity of private-data gradients from Ω(1) to O(1/n) in the general non-convex case. CnvxAdap solves the convex formulation via noisy projected gradient descent with Gaussian noise calibrated to the reduced sensitivity, while NCnvxAdap handles general G-Lipschitz, β-smooth losses by additionally replacing the non-smooth ‖q‖_∞ term with a μ-softmax approximation and converging to stationary points via the gradient-mapping criterion. The empirical discrepancy is privately estimated upfront using a Laplace mechanism (ε/2 budget), and the remaining ε/2 budget is spent on noisy gradient updates. The resulting optimization error for CnvxAdap scales as O(GΛ√(d log(1/δ))/(nε)), matching the known lower bound for private convex ERM.

## Results

On regression (Table 1), the non-private convex algorithm CnvxAdap∞ matches or beats SBest on 4 of 5 UCI datasets, with the largest gain on Gas (MSE 0.342 vs. 0.374, a ~8.6% relative improvement). On classification (Table 2), the non-private non-convex algorithm NCnvxAdap∞ outperforms SBest on 3 of 7 datasets (Adult: 91.38% vs. 91.17%, German: 77.96% vs. 77.78%, Accent: 93.95% vs. 93.61%) and is within 0.5% on the remaining four (CIFAR-100, CIFAR-10, SVHN, ImageNet). For the private variants, performance approaches the non-private solution as ε and n increase; at ε=10–15 and n=10,000, the gap to the non-private baseline is negligible on both Gas (regression) and Adult/CIFAR-10 (classification).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SBest (Awasthi et al., 2024), the paper's non-private algorithms are competitive: CnvxAdap∞ wins on 3/5 regression datasets (notably Gas, MSE 0.342 vs. 0.374) and ties on Wind, while NCnvxAdap∞ wins on 3/7 classification datasets and trails by ≤0.5% on the rest. Against KMM (Huang et al., 2006) and DM (Cortes & Mohri, 2014), both non-private algorithms clearly outperform these older unsupervised/discrepancy-minimization baselines (Appendix Tables 3 and 6). Against noisy minibatch SGD (Bassily et al., 2019), CnvxAdap outperforms the purely private target-only baseline, confirming the benefit of leveraging public source data. Against private-DM (Bassily et al., 2022), CnvxAdap outperforms the prior private adaptation method even at low ε and high n, validating the stronger theoretical foundation from the Awasthi et al. optimization. Overall, the paper's methods match or exceed state-of-the-art non-private adaptation while being the first to provide (ε,δ)-DP guarantees with near-optimal convergence rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the general optimization framework and learning bounds of Awasthi, Cortes, and Mohri (2024) for supervised domain adaptation, which established that jointly optimizing the predictor and sample reweighting yields the strongest theoretical guarantees among reweighting-based methods. It departs from the closest prior private adaptation work (Bassily et al., 2022) in three key ways: it addresses supervised rather than unsupervised adaptation, covers both convex and non-convex loss functions rather than only squared loss, and leverages the stronger optimization formulation of Awasthi et al. (2024) rather than discrepancy minimization. The central technical contribution is a reparameterization (u_i = 1/q_i) that transforms a non-jointly-convex optimization into a jointly convex one, enabling noisy projected gradient descent with formal (ε,δ)-DP guarantees and essentially optimal convergence rates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide (ε,δ)-differentially private supervised domain adaptation algorithms with provable learning guarantees for both convex (linear regression) and non-convex (e.g., neural network classification) settings, filling a gap between private learning theory and practical domain adaptation. The reparameterization technique is broadly applicable and could inform other private optimization problems involving sample weights. Open questions highlighted by the authors include the reverse scenario (private source, public target), handling distinct feature spaces between domains, and extending to unsupervised adaptation via unlabeled discrepancy. The empirical validation, while covering multiple datasets, uses relatively modest sample sizes and the privacy-utility tradeoff remains significant at small ε values.
