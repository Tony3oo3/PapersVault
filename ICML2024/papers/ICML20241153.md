## Problem

Class-incremental learning (CIL) requires a single model to absorb new classes sequentially without catastrophic forgetting, yet existing architecture-based methods either over-parameterize or rely on expanding-and-pruning heuristics whose expansion criteria lack theoretical guarantees. The paper identifies that the convergence property of learning sequential mappings via a universal approximation theorem remains under-explored in the CIL community, and that network growth should be commensurate with the intrinsic complexity of each arriving task rather than driven by arbitrary loss-based triggers.

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes labeled image batches per task (784-dim vectors for MNIST/FashionMNIST, 32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet-R) and optionally features extracted from a frozen or fine-tuned pre-trained backbone (ResNet-18 or ViT-B/16). At inference, it receives batches of test instances from any previously trained task or class without task identifiers, and outputs both a predicted class label and an implicit task identity inferred through activation-threshold matching. The model is rehearsal-free, requiring no exemplar buffer, and the final classifier layer is parameterized by per-task activation thresholds alongside the output weights.

## Method

AutoActivator is a connectionist model that tailors neural unit dynamics for CIL through three coupled mechanisms. First, a supervisory mechanism (formalized in Theorem 4.2 via a universal approximation theorem) randomly generates candidate node batches and recruits only those satisfying an inequality that guarantees a minimum residual-error reduction, ensuring the network grows compactly in proportion to task complexity. Second, output weights are updated via a closed-form pseudoinverse of a partitioned matrix (Eq. 8–9), avoiding backpropagation and yielding the least-squares optimal solution at each expansion step. Third, at inference, per-task activation thresholds (mean of max softmax probabilities during training) are compared against test-instance predictions to partially or entirely reactivate the relevant neural units while leaving others inactivated, enabling task-agnostic decision-making without task descriptors. The convergence of the sequential learning process is theoretically guaranteed by showing the residual error norm decreases geometrically under the supervisory condition.

## Results

On CIFAR-100/10 (10 tasks × 10 classes), AutoActivator achieves 69.65% average classification accuracy (ACA) with a backward transfer (BWT) of −0.01, outperforming the second-best method (FS-DGPM, 67.54%) by 2.11% while maintaining the most compact scale ratio (114.26%). On CIFAR-100/25, it reaches 70.16% ACA with BWT of −0.03. On the small-scale MNIST-10/5 benchmark, it attains 97.32% ACA with essentially zero forgetting (BWT = 0.00) and a rehearsal-free memory budget of only 2.04 MB. On ImageNet-R-200/10 with a pre-trained ViT-B/16, the dual-prompt variant (Ours††) achieves 70.32% ACA, surpassing CODA-Prompt (69.01%) and DualPrompt (68.13%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoActivator consistently outperforms all 34 compared baselines across every dataset. On CIFAR-100/10, it beats the strongest rehearsal-based method FS-DGPM (67.54%) by 2.11% and the architecture-based DER (65.29%) by 4.36%, while also achieving the lowest scale ratio (114.26% vs. >200% for NISPA, RPS-Net, Co²L, and DER). On MNIST-10/5, it surpasses the best rehearsal-free competitor CRNet (94.49%) by 2.83% and the best rehearsal-based method IL2M (95.51%) by 1.81%, with a BWT of 0.00 versus −0.02 to −0.15 for all others. On ImageNet-R with ViT-B/16, the dual-prompt variant (Ours††, 70.32%) exceeds CODA-Prompt (69.01%) by 1.31% and DualPrompt (68.13%) by 2.19%, while the single-ViT variant (Ours†, 65.45%) trails both prompt-based methods but still beats L2P (61.57%) by 3.88%. Notably, regularization-based methods (MAS, OLEWC, SI) and rehearsal-based methods (BiC, IL2M, Co²L) all exhibit substantially larger forgetting (BWT ≤ −0.09) compared to AutoActivator's near-zero BWT, and architecture-based methods that grow the network (RPS-Net, Co²L, DER) incur scale ratios exceeding 200%, whereas AutoActivator stays at 114.26%.

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoActivator bridges two previously separate lines of work: constructive/randomized neural networks (Kwok & Yeung, 1997; Chen & Wan, 1999) and architecture-based class-incremental learning (DER, FOSTER, RPS-Net). It departs from prior architecture-based CIL methods by replacing loss-driven expansion criteria with a supervisory mechanism grounded in a universal approximation theorem for sequential mappings, and from prior randomized network work by embedding the random-node paradigm into a multi-task incremental setting with activation-threshold-based reactivation. The pseudoinverse-based stepwise weight update (from Chen & Wan, 1999) is repurposed to avoid backpropagation during node expansion, while the biologically motivated reactivation of neural units draws on cognitive neuroscience literature on memory consolidation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The rehearsal-free, minimal-expansion property makes AutoActivator practically relevant for privacy-sensitive and resource-constrained CIL deployments where storing exemplars is infeasible. The theoretical convergence guarantee via a universal approximation theorem for sequential mappings fills a gap the authors note is 'under-explored in the CIL community,' providing a principled alternative to heuristic expansion rules. However, the authors themselves characterize the method as 'theoretical and embryonic,' and open questions remain: the assumption of known task boundaries during training limits real-world applicability, the reliance on pre-trained backbones for strong results on larger datasets is a common but non-trivial dependency, and the method has not yet been tested on class-imbalance sequences or truly boundary-free streams.
