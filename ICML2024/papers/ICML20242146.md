## Problem

Universal domain adaptation (UniDA) must simultaneously handle domain shift and category shift, where source and target domains share only a subset of classes with no prior knowledge of which are common versus private. A critical but underexplored challenge is preventing target samples from being misclassified into source-private categories during feature alignment, while also effectively aligning distributions for the common categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242146/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from a labeled source domain and an unlabeled target domain, processed through a ResNet-50 backbone pretrained on ImageNet. The classifier produces a (C^s + 1)-dimensional probability vector per sample, where the extra dimension corresponds to the unknown class; target samples are either assigned to one of the C^s source classes (if common) or flagged as unknown (if target-private). Evaluation uses H-score, the harmonic mean of accuracy on common classes and accuracy on the unknown class.

## Method

BSP-WSA combines three components: (1) an adversarial classifier with C+1 outputs that simultaneously detects target unknown classes and aligns feature distributions via a min-max game between classifier and feature generator; (2) Batch Singular Value Polarization (BSP), which performs SVD on a memory-bank-smoothed predicted label matrix and minimizes the sum of the r smallest singular values while maximizing the r largest, thereby steepening the singular value curve to reduce predicted class diversity and push target samples away from source-private classes; (3) Weighted Semantic Augmentation (WSA), which reformulates the semantic augmentation loss with a class-specific weight γ_j proportional to the ratio of target samples assigned to each source class, so that common classes receive stronger augmentation while source-private classes are down-weighted. The BSP module is plug-and-play and can be attached to other UniDA frameworks, as demonstrated by its integration into GLC.

## Results

Under the OPDA setting (both source and target have private classes), BSP-WSA achieves average H-scores of 88.3% on Office-31 (10/10/11), 77.0% on Office-Home (10/5/50), and 57.1% on VisDA-2017 (6/3/3), outperforming the second-best method GATE by 0.7%, 1.4%, and 0.7% respectively. Under PDA (source private only), BSP-WSA reaches 95.0%, 74.6%, and 78.0% on the three datasets, improving over GATE by 1.3%, 0.7%, and 2.4%. Under ODA (target private only), gains are smaller (91.2%, 70.4%, 71.1%) since the method's core contribution targets source-private-class misassignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BSP-WSA outperforms all six baselines (UAN, CMU, DANCE, DCC, OVANet, GATE) across all three datasets in the OPDA setting, with the closest competitor being GATE (CVPR'22), which it surpasses by 0.7–1.4% in average H-score. The gap over OVANet (ICCV'21) is more pronounced, reaching 2.1% on Office-31 and 4.3% on Office-Home. In the PDA setting, BSP-WSA leads GATE by up to 2.4% on VisDA-2017, while in ODA the advantage narrows (e.g., 91.2% vs. 89.5% on Office-31) because the absence of source private classes removes the primary scenario BSP addresses. The ablation confirms that BSP contributes more than WSA individually (e.g., +15.8% on Office-31 OPDA vs. +9.6% for WSA alone), and the plug-and-play experiment shows BSP can boost GLC (CVPR'23) by 0.9% on Office-31 and 3.2% on VisDA-2017 in OPDA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on three distinct lines of work: (1) the adversarial classifier framework from open-set DA (Saito et al., 2018), repurposed here to simultaneously detect unknown target classes and align feature distributions; (2) SVD-based regularization in domain adaptation (Chen et al., 2019; Cui et al., 2020), where prior work maximizes singular values to enhance class diversity, but this paper inverts the objective—maximizing large singular values while minimizing small ones ('polarization') to *reduce* diversity and prevent misassignment to source private classes; and (3) transferable semantic augmentation (Li et al., 2021b), which is modified by replacing the fixed augmentation weight γ_j with a data-driven ratio of target-to-source sample counts per class, so that common classes receive stronger augmentation than source-private ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The BSP module's plug-and-play nature (demonstrated by adding it to GLC with consistent gains) makes it a potentially reusable component for any DA framework where source private classes exist. However, the method's effectiveness is tightly coupled to the presence of source private classes—it actively degrades performance in the ODA setting (Table 6), limiting its generality. The authors acknowledge that BSP relies on the assumption that target samples are less likely to be assigned to source private classes, and that treating all unknown target classes as a single category ignores internal clustering structure. Open questions include whether the polarization strategy generalizes beyond the ResNet-50 backbone and whether the memory-bank smoothing is necessary at larger batch sizes.
