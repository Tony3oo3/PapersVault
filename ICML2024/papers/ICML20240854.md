## Problem

DP-SIGNSGD, which combines sign-based gradient compression with differential privacy, has universally adopted Gaussian noise inherited from DP-SGD without questioning whether this distribution is well-suited to the discrete sign-sampling operation. The paper asks whether a noise mechanism more naturally aligned with the sign function can provide tighter privacy accounting and higher gradient accuracy under the same privacy budget.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240854/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (MNIST at 32×32, CIFAR-10 at 32×32, CelebA at 128×128, medical images at 224×224) along with a chosen architecture (Dense, ResNet, VGG, or ViT) and a per-sample L2 gradient clipping threshold C. It outputs a trained classifier that produces class labels, together with an accumulated privacy cost (ε, δ) computed via a custom moments accountant tailored to the Logistic mechanism. In fine-tuning settings, pre-trained ImageNet weights serve as initialization, and the privacy budget is tracked over the fine-tuning epochs only.

## Method

The core contribution is DP-SIGNLOSGD, which replaces the additive Gaussian noise in DP-SIGNSGD with additive Logistic noise of scale s. The derivation proceeds by formulating the sign-sampling step as a nearest-point problem over {−α, α}^N, then constructing an exponential mechanism with score function s(g̃, v) = −vᵀg̃ that samples exactly from this discrete set. The paper proves that this exponential mechanism is mathematically equivalent to adding i.i.d. Logistic(0, s) noise before the sign function, yielding a simple additive-noise implementation. Theoretically, the Logistic mechanism achieves a tighter MGF bound of λ(λ+1)q²/(50s²) versus the Gaussian's λ(λ+1)q²/((1−q)σ²), enabling roughly 50× more trainable epochs under the same asymptotic bound (1.5× in practice via numeric integration). A custom closed-form MGF for the Logistic mechanism is plugged into the moments accountant to track accumulated privacy loss across training steps.

## Results

On MNIST (ε=6.4, δ=10⁻⁵), DP-SIGNLOSGD achieves 98.70% test accuracy with ResNet-18 vs. 96.49% for DP-SIGNSGD, and a striking 99.01% vs. 85.46% with ViT-B-16. On CIFAR-10 at ε=6.4, the proposed method reaches 58.85% test accuracy compared to 49.96% for DP-SIGNSGD (a ~9-point gap). Under the same privacy budget (ε=4.0, δ=10⁻⁵), the Logistic mechanism requires a smaller noise standard deviation (1.17) than Gaussian (1.48), enabling 1.5× more training epochs via the moments accountant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is DP-SIGNSGD (Jin et al., 2020; Lyu, 2021), which adds Gaussian noise before the sign function. DP-SIGNLOSGD consistently outperforms DP-SIGNSGD across all six datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, CelebA, Brain Tumor MRI, Blood Cell Images, Chest X-Ray) and all model architectures, with the gap widening as ε increases (e.g., on CIFAR-10, the test accuracy gap grows from 1.6 points at ε=0.4 to 14.4 points at ε=25.6). In fine-tuning experiments, two additional non-DP baselines are included: standard SIGNSGD (Bernstein et al., 2018) and P-SIGNSGD (Chen et al., 2020). The proposed method sometimes matches or even exceeds standard SIGNSGD (which adds no noise at all), particularly on larger models like ViT-B-16 on CelebA (91.00% vs. 87.60% test), suggesting the Logistic noise acts as a beneficial regularizer. The theoretical MGF bound shows the Logistic mechanism achieves a 50× tighter privacy accounting constant compared to Gaussian, translating to 1.5× more usable training epochs in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DP-SIGNSGD (Jin et al., 2020; Lyu, 2021), which naively transplants the Gaussian mechanism from DP-SGD into the sign-based setting. The key departure is a conceptual re-derivation: by formulating sign sampling as an optimization over {-α,α}^N and applying the exponential mechanism to that problem, the authors show the resulting distribution is exactly equivalent to additive Logistic noise before the sign function. This positions the work as a principled correction to an unexamined design choice in prior DP-SIGNSGD implementations, drawing on the foundational relationship between exponential and Laplace/Logistic mechanisms established in Dwork & Roth (2014).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides a theoretically grounded, drop-in replacement for Gaussian noise in sign-based DP training, yielding tighter privacy accounting (1.5× more trainable epochs in practice) and consistently higher accuracy across diverse architectures and datasets. The extension to majority-vote federated settings broadens its applicability to communication-efficient distributed learning. Open questions include whether analogous 'natural' noise distributions exist for other gradient compression schemes (n-bit quantization, sparsification), and whether the gap between the theoretical 50× bound and the practical 1.5× improvement can be closed with tighter MGF analysis.
