## Problem

Despite the empirical success of deep equilibrium models (DEQs) as implicit neural networks, it remains unclear whether DEQs offer genuine advantages over explicit networks or whether an equivalent explicit NN exists for a given DEQ. This paper addresses this gap by providing a high-dimensional spectral characterization of DEQ conjugate and neural tangent kernels, and by showing that shallow explicit networks can be constructed to replicate the same kernel behavior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241232/cropped_page6_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes high-dimensional data vectors (dimension p comparable to sample size n) drawn from a K-class Gaussian mixture model with √p-normalized means and covariances, along with the DEQ's activation function and variance parameters σ_a, σ_b. It produces a closed-form spectral approximation of the Implicit-CK and NTK matrices expressed through four scalar parameters (α_{*,1}, α_{*,2}, α_{*,3}, γ_*), and from these, the specific activation function parameters (e.g., slope and threshold for Hard-Tanh, or leak coefficients for Leaky-ReLU) of a 1- or 2-hidden-layer explicit NN that matches the DEQ's kernel. The equivalence holds in spectral norm with O(n^{-1/2}) error.

## Method

Using random matrix theory, the paper proves by induction on the recursive kernel definitions that the Implicit-CK and NTK of a DEQ, despite its effectively infinite depth, converge to random matrices whose eigenspectra depend on the activation φ and variance parameters only through a system of four nonlinear equations (Theorems 3.3 and 3.4). The proof combines Taylor expansions of the activation around the fixed-point scale τ_*, Gaussian integration by parts, and concentration of measure for GMM data. The key insight is that this four-equation system has the same structural form as the one governing explicit NN kernels (from Gu et al., 2022), enabling a direct matching procedure: one solves for the explicit NN's activation parameters so that its CK coefficients equal those of the DEQ. For Tanh-DEQs, a single-hidden-layer Hard-Tanh explicit NN suffices; for ReLU-DEQs, a two-hidden-layer Leaky-ReLU explicit NN is required, as shown by the constraint α_{*,2} ≠ ½α_{*,3} in the latter case.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: Theorems 3.3 and 3.4 show that the Implicit-CK and NTK of a DEQ, for high-dimensional GMM data, are well-approximated (in spectral norm, with error O(n^{-1/2})) by explicit random matrices whose coefficients depend on the DEQ's activation and variance parameters through only four nonlinear equations. Empirically, Figure 1 confirms the O(n^{-1/2}) spectral norm error decay for ReLU, Tanh, Swish, and Leaky-ReLU DEQs on two-class GMM data. On real-world classification (Figure 3), the carefully designed equivalent explicit NNs (H-Tanh-ENN for Tanh-DEQ, L-ReLU-ENN for ReLU-DEQ) closely track the accuracy of their implicit DEQ counterparts across MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR-10 as width m increases, while naive explicit NNs with the same activation (Tanh-ENN, ReLU-ENN) exhibit a noticeable accuracy gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three model families on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR-10: (i) the original implicit DEQ (Tanh-DEQ or ReLU-DEQ), (ii) the proposed equivalent shallow explicit NN with theoretically designed activations (H-Tanh-ENN or L-ReLU-ENN), and (iii) a naive explicit NN using the same activation as the DEQ (Tanh-ENN or ReLU-ENN). The key finding is that the equivalent explicit NNs closely match the DEQ classification accuracy across all three datasets as width m grows, confirming the theoretical CK/NTK equivalence. In contrast, the naive explicit NNs (Tanh-ENN, ReLU-ENN) show a clear and persistent accuracy gap relative to the DEQs, demonstrating that simply matching the activation function is insufficient—careful activation design via the four-equation system is necessary. Additionally, the equivalent explicit NNs offer a significant practical advantage: they avoid the iterative fixed-point solving required by DEQs, yielding substantially lower training and inference time costs (reported in Appendix E.5) while maintaining comparable accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of RMT-based kernel analysis: Feng & Kolter's (2020) recursive characterization of DEQ CKs/NTKs, and Gu et al.'s (2022) high-dimensional spectral approximation for explicit multi-layer NNs. The key conceptual step is showing that the fixed-point recursion defining DEQ kernels converges to a form structurally identical to that of a finite-depth explicit network, with all dependence on the activation and variance parameters funneled through just four scalar equations. This allows the authors to 'invert' the explicit-network formulas (from Gu et al.) to design activations that replicate a given DEQ's kernel, a connection that prior work on DEQs (Feng & Kolter, Agarwala & Schoenholz) explicitly left unexplored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: DEQs incur substantial computational overhead from iterative fixed-point solving during both training and inference, yet this work shows that a 1- or 2-layer explicit network with carefully chosen (non-standard) activations can match the DEQ's kernel spectral behavior, potentially eliminating that overhead. The main limitation is that the theory is proven for high-dimensional GMM data; the authors conjecture extension to concentrated random vectors but provide only empirical evidence on small-scale image datasets (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10). Open questions include whether the equivalence persists under finite-width training dynamics, for non-GMM data distributions, and whether the designed activations remain stable under SGD training at practical (non-infinite) widths.
