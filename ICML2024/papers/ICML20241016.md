## Problem

Standard Koopman Operator Regression (KOR) estimators are designed for one-step-ahead prediction and their forecasting error deteriorates as the horizon extends, because the leading eigenvalue of 1 causes the sum of operator powers to diverge and any small eigenvalue estimation error leads to either explosion or collapse. This paper addresses the gap by providing the first non-asymptotic forecasting error bounds that hold uniformly over infinite time horizons for ergodic dynamical systems, and by introducing a practical method to achieve such consistency.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dataset of consecutive state pairs (x_i, y_i) sampled from the invariant distribution, a symmetric positive-definite kernel defining an RKHS, and either a single initial state or samples from an initial distribution μ_0. Outputs include the forecasted conditional mean E[h(X_t)|X_0=x] for any observable h, the forecasted state distribution μ_t represented as a probability-weighted sum of Dirac measures on the training output points (preserving total mass by construction), and the conditional variance. The DLI correction adds only O(n²) centering cost on top of the standard estimator, and powering the estimator reduces to powering an r×r matrix for rank-r estimators.

## Method

The Deflate-Learn-Inflate (DLI) paradigm transforms any standard KOR estimator into a long-term consistent one through three steps: (i) Deflate — remove the known eigenpair (1, 𝟙_π) from the Koopman operator via the projector J_π, yielding an asymptotically stable operator with ρ < 1; (ii) Learn — estimate the deflated operator by minimizing the empirical risk on centered features, which is equivalent to replacing kernel Gram matrices K_x, K_y with their centered versions J_n K_x J_n, J_n K_y J_n; (iii) Inflate — recover the full forecast by adding back the invariant mean using empirical kernel mean embeddings k_{π̂_x}, k_{π̂_y}. The key theoretical insight is that the transient growth of estimator powers is controlled by the Kreiss constant and distance to instability from non-normal operator stability theory, enabling uniform-in-time bounds via Lemma 5.3. The approach is estimator-agnostic (works with KRR, PCR, RRR) and extends naturally to distribution forecasting via the Perron-Frobenius adjoint, with MMD error bounds in Theorem 6.1.

## Results

On the Cox-Ingersoll-Ross (CIR) model, DLI-RRR reduces the RMSE for conditional variance from 0.0470±0.0413 (RRR) to 0.0124±0.0051, a ~74% improvement, while also slightly improving the conditional mean RMSE (0.0673 vs. 0.0691). On the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, DLI-RRR achieves consistently lower relative MMD error than uncentered RRR across the entire forecasting trajectory. On the Alanine Dipeptide molecular dynamics dataset, DLI-RRR yields both lower MAE and significantly smaller variance across independent training samples for the backbone dihedral angles φ and ψ over a 0.1 ns horizon, and outperforms the standard kernel method in predicting the equilibrium mean squared deviation of atomic positions at long timescales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DLI-enhanced RRR against the standard (uncentered) RRR estimator from Kostic et al. (2022) across four experimental settings. DLI-RRR wins on every metric: on the CIR model it reduces conditional variance RMSE by roughly 74% (0.0124 vs. 0.0470) with a much tighter standard deviation (0.0051 vs. 0.0413), and marginally improves the conditional mean RMSE. On the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, DLI-RRR achieves lower relative MMD error than RRR at every time step, with the gap widening at longer horizons. On the Alanine Dipeptide dihedral angle forecasting task, DLI-RRR not only attains lower MAE but also a significantly smaller variance across 100 independent training subsamples, which the authors highlight as critical for risk management. For the atomic MSD prediction, DLI-RRR outperforms the standard kernel approach particularly at long timescales where the standard method's error grows without bound. The consistent advantage across all four benchmarks underscores that the DLI paradigm (deflation + centering + inflation) is a drop-in improvement over any existing KOR estimator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Koopman Operator Regression (KOR) framework of Kostic et al. (2022) and the sharp spectral learning rates of Kostic et al. (2023a), extending them from one-step-ahead prediction to infinite-horizon forecasting. The Deflate-Learn-Inflate (DLI) paradigm unifies two previously separate ideas—eigenvalue deflation from numerical linear algebra (Saad, 2011) and feature centering from statistics (Mardt et al., 2018; Seenivasaharagavan et al., 2023)—into a single agnostic wrapper around any KOR estimator. The theoretical analysis departs from prior KOR work by importing tools from non-normal operator stability theory (Kreiss constants, distance to instability; Trefethen & Embree, 2020; ElFallah & Ransford, 2002) to explain why one-step consistency is insufficient and to derive the first uniform-in-time forecasting bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DLI approach provides a drop-in modification (centering kernel Gram matrices) that provably prevents the well-known blow-up or collapse of standard KOR estimators at long horizons, with the first non-asymptotic error bounds that are independent of the forecasting time. This is practically important for applications like molecular dynamics and climate forecasting where reliable long-horizon predictions are essential. Key limitations include the requirement of geometric ergodicity (ruling out deterministic systems on L²), the α > 1 regularity condition, and the restriction to universal kernels; extending to non-stationary processes, continuous-time systems, and the α ≤ 1 regime remain open.
