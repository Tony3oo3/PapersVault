## Problem

Fourier Neural Operators (FNOs) and their variants rely on the FFT for spectral computations, which restricts them to equispaced rectangular grids. Real-world PDE applications frequently involve data on arbitrary non-equispaced point distributions (sensor networks, unstructured grids, complex geometries), and existing workarounds—interpolation to regular grids or learned diffeomorphisms—are computationally expensive and often inaccurate. The paper asks whether truncated spectral transforms can be evaluated directly and efficiently on arbitrary point distributions without any grid-mapping step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241233/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts PDE initial/boundary data, satellite observations, or airfoil geometry sampled at arbitrary non-equispaced points (1D, 2D, or spherical) and produces the corresponding solution field (velocity, pressure, humidity, stress) at those same point locations. A key practical feature is that the number and positions of sample points can vary between individual samples (e.g., different airfoil shapes yield different point clouds), and the method generalizes to test-time point clouds with substantially different point counts than those seen during training. The spectral transform operates on a fixed small number of modes (typically 12–32) regardless of the number of sample points N.

## Method

The core contribution is replacing the FFT-based spectral layer in neural operators with a direct matrix-vector product. A matrix V of size m×N is constructed whose entries are Fourier basis functions (or spherical harmonics) evaluated at the sample point positions, where m is the small number of retained modes (m << N). The forward spectral transform is simply X = Vx and the inverse is x = V*^T X, both computed as optimized batched matrix multiplications in PyTorch with O(mN) complexity. In D dimensions, the matrix is built via a flattened tensor-product structure over per-axis modes, and for spherical domains the basis functions are replaced by associated Legendre polynomials. This direct spectral evaluation (DSE) is a drop-in replacement for the FFT layer within FNO, UFNO, FFNO, and SFNO, eliminating the need for interpolation, NUFFT, or learned geometric deformations while enabling processing of data on arbitrary point distributions with significant training-speed gains.

## Results

Across six PDE benchmarks, DSE-based neural operators consistently outperform baselines on both training speed and L1 test error. On the 2D non-equispaced lattice (Shear Layer), FNO-DSE trains 4.4× faster (57s vs 251s per epoch) while achieving lower error (5.53% vs 6.76%). On the 2D point cloud (Airfoil), FNO-DSE is 2.2× faster (2.8s vs 6.2s) with a 5.4× lower L1 error (0.220% vs 1.20%) compared to Geo-FNO. On the spherical Shallow Water Equations, SFNO-DSE achieves 3.88% L1 error in 15s per epoch versus 7.29% in 86s for the linear-interpolation baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSE matches standard FNO-FFT on equispaced grids (0.0551% vs 0.0575% L1 error on 1D Burgers) while being slightly faster, confirming no accuracy loss from the direct evaluation. Against interpolation-based baselines (FFT + cubic interpolation, KB-NUFFT, Toeplitz-NUFFT), DSE is 9× faster than KB-NUFFT (0.11s vs 30.0s) and 7.7× faster than Toeplitz-NUFFT (0.11s vs 0.85s) on the contracting-expanding distribution, while also achieving lower error (0.184% vs 0.346% and 0.740%). Against Geo-FNO (learned diffeomorphism), DSE-based models are 2–3× faster and substantially more accurate on point-cloud problems: on Airfoil, FNO-DSE achieves 0.220% vs 1.20% L1 error, and on Elasticity, FFNO-DSE achieves 1.73% vs 2.20%. Against FFT-based UFNO and FFNO on equispaced grids, DSE variants are 4–4.4× faster per epoch on the Shear Layer (e.g., 57s vs 251s for FNO) with marginally lower error. On the spherical SWE benchmark, SFNO-DSE (3.88%) outperforms both the linear-interpolation SFNO (7.29%) and the Gaussian-interpolation SFNO (12.7%), with a 5.7× training speedup over the linear baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) family (FNO, UFNO, FFNO, SFNO) by replacing the FFT-based spectral layers with direct matrix-vector evaluations of truncated spectral transforms, leveraging the observation from Lanthaler et al. (2023a,b) that only a small fixed number of modes (12–32) are needed for FNO expressivity. It departs from prior approaches to handle non-equispaced data—Geo-FNO's learned diffeomorphisms, NUFFT's interpolation-plus-FFT pipelines, and NFS's vision-mixer interpolation—by avoiding any grid-mapping or interpolation step entirely. The method is also distinct from the non-uniform DFT literature, which typically targets full O(N²) transforms; here the truncation to m << N modes makes the O(mN) direct evaluation competitive with FFT's O(N log N).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DSE approach provides a drop-in replacement for FFT layers in any spectral neural operator, immediately enabling operator learning on arbitrary point clouds, non-equispaced lattices, and spherical geometries without interpolation or learned deformations. The order-of-magnitude training speedups observed on the Specific Humidity benchmark (1.5s vs 15s per epoch) and the consistent 4× speedups on Shear Layer make this practically significant for large-scale PDE surrogates. Limitations include the need to construct (or cache) the spectral matrix per point distribution, which becomes costly when every sample has a different geometry, and the current scope is limited to Fourier and spherical harmonic transforms—extension to wavelets or other spectral bases is left for future work. An open question is how well the approach scales when the number of modes must grow substantially for very high-resolution or highly oscillatory problems.
