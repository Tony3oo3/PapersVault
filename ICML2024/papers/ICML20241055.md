## Problem

Prior work assumed that maintaining a network's trainability (ability to minimize training loss) would automatically preserve its generalization ability, but this paper shows empirically that trainability methods fail to improve generalization after warm-starting on noisy, reduced datasets. Meanwhile, reinitialization methods that do recover generalization risk erasing all previously learned knowledge, which is costly for large models and problematic under data-access constraints. The paper asks how to restore plasticity while retaining generalizable prior knowledge.

## Input / Output

For supervised and continual learning experiments, the method consumes images at three resolutions—28×28 grayscale (MNIST), 32×32 RGB (CIFAR-10/100), and 64×64 RGB (Tiny ImageNet)—and outputs class labels, with the warm-starting protocol first training on subsets (10–100%) carrying 0–50% label noise before retraining on the full clean dataset. For reinforcement learning, the architecture operates on Atari game frames through either a 3-layer ConvNet (DrQ) or a 15-layer ResNet (BBF), producing discrete actions, and integrates as a single periodic reset of the online network to the momentum target network every 4000 gradient steps.

## Method

Hare & Tortoise pairs two networks: the Hare, which performs standard gradient-based updates each step to rapidly adapt to new data (analogous to the hippocampus), and the Tortoise, which accumulates knowledge slowly via an exponential moving average of the Hare's weights (θ_t ← μθ_t + (1−μ)θ_h, analogous to the neocortex). Every reset interval (10 epochs in supervised settings, 4000 steps in RL), the Hare's parameters are overwritten with the Tortoise's parameters (θ_h ← θ_t), which restores plasticity by escaping bad local minima while preserving the generalizable knowledge the Tortoise has slowly consolidated. Unlike random reinitialization or Shrink & Perturb, this soft reset retains prior knowledge because the Tortoise's weights are a smoothed, slowly-drifting version of the network's trajectory rather than a fresh draw from the initialization distribution. In RL, the method requires only one additional line of code—periodically copying the target network's weights into the online network—making it a drop-in enhancement for any algorithm that already employs momentum target networks.

## Results

On the Atari-100k benchmark, adding Hare & Tortoise to DrQ improves the median score from 0.193 to 0.260 and IQM from 0.243 to 0.287, while for BBF it raises IQM from 0.826 to 0.891 and median from 0.711 to 0.749 without extra computational cost. In warm-starting experiments (10% subset, 50% label noise), Hare & Tortoise with L2+Aug achieves 91.0% test accuracy on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18), approaching the 92.4% of a fresh network with augmentation, while trainability methods (Spectral, Regen, ReDo, CReLU) all remain near the 68–71% warm-started baseline. On Tiny ImageNet (VGG-16), where generalization loss is most severe, Shrink & Perturb + L2 + Aug (37.6%) still outperforms Hare & Tortoise, indicating that radical reinitialization remains preferable under extreme conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against trainability methods (Spectral decoupling, Regenerative regularization, ReDo, CReLU), Hare & Tortoise clearly wins on generalization: on CIFAR-10 with 10% subset and 50% noise, all four trainability methods score 67.8–70.5% test accuracy (nearly identical to the 70.4% warm-started baseline), while Hare & Tortoise with L2+Aug reaches 91.0%. Against EMA and Self-Distillation, Hare & Tortoise consistently outperforms in warm-starting, with the gap attributed to the periodic Hare reset enabling exploration of the optimization landscape. Against Shrink & Perturb, Hare & Tortoise wins on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 in continual learning (avoiding abrupt accuracy drops) but loses on Tiny ImageNet where severe generalization loss favors radical reinitialization. Against Head Reset, Hare & Tortoise achieves comparable performance in RL (DrQ+HR40k+H&T: IQM 0.328 vs. DrQ+HR20k: IQM 0.332) while avoiding the periodic performance collapse. In RL, Hare & Tortoise improves BBF's IQM by 6.5 points (0.826→0.891) at zero extra GPU cost, though it trails the self-supervised objective variant (IQM 0.940) which requires 2x the compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the warm-starting experiments of Ash & Adams (2020) to modern architectures (ResNet-18, ViT-Tiny, VGG-16) and systematically evaluates whether trainability-preserving methods (Spectral decoupling, Regenerative regularization, ReDo, CReLU) actually improve generalization. Architecturally, it draws on the complementary learning systems hypothesis (McClelland et al., 1995) and the EMA mechanism from Tarvainen & Valpola (2017), combining them with the periodic reinitialization idea from Shrink & Perturb (Ash & Adams, 2020) and Head Reset (Nikishin et al., 2022) into a unified soft-reset scheme. The key departure from prior work is the explicit decoupling of trainability from generalizability and the demonstration that the former does not rescue the latter in modern networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that trainability methods fail to improve generalization challenges a widely-held assumption in the continual learning and RL communities, redirecting attention toward reinitialization and regularization as the primary levers for maintaining plasticity. The Hare & Tortoise method is practically attractive because it requires only a single line of code to integrate into existing RL algorithms with momentum target networks (DrQ, BBF), and it avoids the knowledge loss of hard resets under limited data access. A key open question remains: why do warm-started models fail to generalize in the first place, and whether the Tortoise's slowly-updated weights identify specific regions of the optimization landscape that preserve generalizability without requiring periodic resets.
