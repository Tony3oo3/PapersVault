## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on text-to-image models focus solely on deceiving textual input filters, overlooking the visual checkers that API providers deploy at the output stage to block harmful images. Moreover, prior enumeration-based methods are inefficient and largely fail when both defenses are active, and gradient-based optimization is infeasible in the black-box setting where defenses deny all output (no feedback) during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241631/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a naive text prompt (e.g., 'a photo of a knife') and a paired target image as input, and produces a natural, human-readable adversarial text prompt that bypasses both the textual filter and the visual checker of a black-box T2I API (DALL-E, DALL-E 2, or Imagen). The attacker has no access to internal model parameters, filter logic, or checker thresholds—only the binary Pass/Deny response and, when Pass, the returned image. The LLM (LLaMA) with a rank-8 LoRA adapter generates the adversarial prompt, ensuring grammatical correctness and low perplexity.

## Method

UPAM consists of a frozen LLaMA backbone with a learnable LoRA adapter, trained in two stages via gradient-based optimization. In the pre-training stage, Sphere-Probing Learning (SPL) addresses the no-feedback scenario by sampling N points on a sphere around the current LoRA parameters, querying the black box for Pass/Deny at each point, and estimating a gradient direction from the 'Deny' points to push parameters toward the Pass/Deny boundary (slightly leaning toward Pass). In the fine-tuning stage, Semantic-Enhancing Learning (SEL) uses a CLIP-based image-text and image-image cosine similarity loss with an improved ZOO gradient estimator to align returned images with target semantics, while a gradient harmonization step projects out the boundary-directed component to prevent SEL from pulling parameters back into the Deny region. The LLM's language modeling capability ensures the adversarial prompts remain natural and stealthy.

## Results

Under Protocol A (train-then-test), UPAM achieves R-1 precision of 38.37% (DALL-E), 40.45% (DALL-E 2), and 36.16% (Imagen), outperforming the strongest baseline RIATIG by an average of ~35.31% (R-1) and 38.90% (R-3). Inference time drops dramatically to ~5 seconds per sample versus 300–600 seconds for enumeration-based baselines. Textual similarity stays below 0.2 across all models, and PPL (521–788) is substantially lower than all baselines (1000–5800), confirming both effectiveness and stealthiness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UPAM dominates all five baselines across every metric and every T2I model. Against the strongest baseline RIATIG (Liu et al., 2023), UPAM achieves roughly 4.4× higher R-1 precision (38.37% vs 8.65% on DALL-E) and reduces inference time from ~300 s to ~5 s. The enumeration-based methods (TextFooler, HomoSubs, EvoPromp, HiddVocab, MacPromp) all fail to pass the visual checker under Protocol B, scoring 0/10 or at most 1/10 successful image retrievals, whereas UPAM retrieves 7.3/10 (DALL-E) and 6.5/10 (DALL-E 2). In terms of stealthiness, UPAM's LLM+LoRA design yields PPL of 521–788, compared to 1000–5837 for the baselines, confirming that gradient-optimized adversarial prompts are far more natural than hand-crafted word substitutions or homoglyph replacements. The gap is most pronounced on Imagen, where UPAM's PPL (521.78) is roughly 2× lower than the next-best baseline (MacPromp at 3173.35) and R-1 precision is nearly 5× higher than RIATIG (36.16% vs 7.80%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of adversarial prompt attacks on T2I models (Daras & Dimakis 2022, Millière 2022, Liu et al. 2023) in two key ways: it targets both textual filters and visual checkers simultaneously rather than textual filters alone, and it replaces enumeration-based search with gradient-based optimization of a parameterized LLM+LoRA model. The gradient estimation machinery draws on Zeroth-Order Optimization (Spall 1992) and the improved ZOO gradient rule from BlackVIP (Oh et al. 2023), while the Sphere-Probing Learning scheme adapts boundary-probing ideas from model inversion (Kahla et al. 2022) to the no-feedback setting where the black box returns no output at all.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the commonly deployed dual-defense (textual filter + visual checker) architecture in T2I APIs is simultaneously bypassable with a single trained model, and that gradient-based optimization is 60–120× faster than enumeration at inference time. However, the practical stakes are tempered by the ethical constraint of using benign COCO classes (boat, bird, clock) as stand-ins for truly harmful content, so the real-world transferability of the attack remains untested. Open questions include whether adversarial training or model unlearning can robustly defend against gradient-based prompt attacks, and whether the SPL no-feedback gradient scheme generalizes to other black-box settings beyond T2I.
