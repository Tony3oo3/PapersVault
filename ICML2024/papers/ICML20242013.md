## Problem

Optimization over the generalized Stiefel manifold (X^T B X = I_p) is central to CCA, ICA, and the GEVP, but existing Riemannian methods require the full constraint matrix B and expensive retractions (eigenvalue or Cholesky decompositions), while infeasible methods like augmented Lagrangian are sensitive to hyperparameters. Crucially, no prior method handles the practically important case where B itself is unknown and only random estimates B_ζ (e.g., subsampled covariance matrices) are available, making the feasible set stochastic.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only stochastic gradients ∇f_ξ(X) and random estimates B_ζ of the constraint matrix (e.g., rank-r subsampled covariances), never forming B explicitly. Per-iteration cost is O(npr) for matrix multiplications with the rank-r batch, and space complexity is O(n(p+r)) versus O(n²) for methods that store the full B. The output is a sequence of iterates X^k that remain within a prescribed ε-safe region and converge to critical points on the generalized Stiefel manifold, yielding canonical correlation components, mixing matrices, or generalized eigenvectors depending on the application.

## Method

The proposed landing iteration updates X^{k+1} = X^k − η_k[Ψ_{ξ,ζ,ζ'}(X^k) + ω∇N_{ζ,ζ'}(X^k)], where Ψ is an unbiased estimator of a relative ascent direction (e.g., 2·skew(∇f_ξ(X)X^T B_ζ)B_{ζ'}X) and ∇N is the gradient of the infeasibility measure ½‖X^T B X − I_p‖²_F. The two components are orthogonal by construction, so the update simultaneously descends the objective and pulls the iterate toward the manifold without any retraction or matrix factorization. Convergence to ε-critical points is established via Fletcher's augmented Lagrangian as a smooth merit function, which—unlike the non-smooth merit function in Schechtman et al. (2023)—accommodates noise in the normal space arising from stochastic B_ζ. This yields O(1/K) sublinear convergence with fixed step-size (deterministic) and O(1/√K) in expectation with decaying step-size (stochastic), matching the rates of Riemannian gradient descent and Riemannian SGD on the same manifold.

## Results

On the deterministic top-p GEVP (n=1000, p=500, κ=100), the landing method with Ψ_B(X) achieves the fastest wall-clock convergence among all tested methods, benefiting from its O(npr) per-iteration cost with no matrix factorizations. On stochastic CCA over split MNIST (N=60000, n=392, p=5, r=512), the online landing method outperforms Riemannian gradient descent with rolling-averaged covariance after just one epoch (~2.5 s), while requiring only O(n(p+r)) memory versus O(n²) for the full covariance. The method achieves global sublinear convergence at rate O(1/√K) in the stochastic setting, matching the rate of Riemannian SGD, while handling stochastic constraints that Riemannian methods cannot address.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Riemannian steepest descent with Cholesky-QR retraction (Sato & Aihara, 2019), the landing method with Ψ_B(X) converges faster in wall-clock time on the deterministic GEVP (n=1000, p=500) because its per-iteration cost involves only matrix multiplications (O(npr)) rather than the Cholesky factorization and matrix inverse required by the retraction (O(n²p)). Against PLAM (Gao et al., 2022a), the landing method with Ψ_B(X) again converges fastest and is notably more robust to perturbations of the step-size η and penalty parameter ω, as shown by grid-search experiments where PLAM's performance degrades more sharply under parameter changes. In the stochastic CCA setting on split MNIST, the online landing method (which never forms the full covariance matrix) beats the averaged Riemannian gradient descent in objective value after the first epoch, though the rolling-average method catches up once it accumulates the exact covariance at the cost of O(n²) memory. The landing method is the only method in Table 2 that simultaneously supports stochastic constraints, requires no matrix factorizations, and achieves global sublinear convergence with O(n(p+r)) memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'landing' method—originally developed for the orthogonal group (Ablin & Peyré, 2022) and the standard Stiefel manifold (Gao et al., 2022b; Ablin et al., 2023)—to the generalized Stiefel manifold with a stochastic constraint matrix B. It departs from Schechtman et al. (2023), who extended landing to general smooth constraints but relied on a non-smooth merit function that precluded stochastic convergence analysis; here, Fletcher's augmented Lagrangian provides a smooth merit function that enables both deterministic and stochastic convergence guarantees. The method also contrasts with Riemannian optimization (which requires expensive retractions involving Cholesky/SVD/eigenvalue decompositions) and classical infeasible methods like augmented Lagrangian (which require solving subproblems and are sensitive to penalty parameters), by using only matrix multiplications and provably converging for any fixed ω > 0.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for large-scale CCA, ICA, and GEVP where forming the full covariance matrix B is prohibitive: the method reduces per-iteration cost from O(n²p) to O(npr) with batch size r, and memory from O(n²) to O(n(p+r)), while matching the O(1/K) and O(1/√K) convergence rates of Riemannian counterparts. A key limitation is the requirement that iterates remain in an ε-safe region around the manifold, which in the stochastic case must be assumed rather than guaranteed without additional distributional assumptions on the noise. Open questions include whether the safe-region constraint can be relaxed, whether the approach extends to inequality constraints or non-smooth objectives, and how the method performs in the regime where the batch size r is very small relative to n.
