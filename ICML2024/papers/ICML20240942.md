## Problem

GP-based few-shot classification requires variational inference over a non-conjugate softmax likelihood, and existing approaches either assume an unreasonable Gaussian likelihood or introduce auxiliary variables (e.g., Pólya-Gamma augmentation). More critically, the inner-loop VI in the bi-level meta-learning framework relies on first-order gradient descent, yielding slow convergence whose rate depends on the arbitrary parameterization of the variational distribution—a practical concern since the optimal parameterization is typically unknown.

## Input / Output

The model receives a support set of L labeled examples and a query set of M unlabeled examples per task, with features extracted by a shared Conv4 backbone (standard in Bayesian meta-learning). At test time, class label predictions are produced via Monte Carlo integration over the posterior of latent GP functions, and predictive uncertainty is quantified through the full predictive distribution. Training operates over batches of randomly sampled tasks (100 episodes per epoch), with the outer loop updating deep kernel hyperparameters and the inner loop performing 3 steps of mirror-descent VI per task.

## Method

The core contribution replaces first-order gradient descent in the inner-loop VI with mirror descent over the mean parameter, which the paper proves equivalent to natural gradient descent over the natural parameter (Theorem 3.1) and further reducible to a conjugate Bayesian inference step (Theorem 3.2). Concretely, each VI iteration approximates the softmax likelihood by a Gaussian whose natural parameter is updated via a first-order gradient of the expected log-likelihood, then the variational posterior is obtained by simply adding this to the GP prior's natural parameter—requiring no auxiliary variables and no second-order computation. This yields second-order convergence rate and parameterization invariance while maintaining first-order computational cost. The outer loop employs a deep kernel (base kernel + Conv4 feature extractor) whose hyperparameters are updated by maximizing the ELBO via Adam, forming a bi-level optimization analogous to MAML.

## Results

On 5-way few-shot classification, MD-BFSC achieves the best 1-shot accuracy on CUB (65.45%) and mini-ImageNet→CUB (40.75%), and competitive 5-shot accuracy across all three settings (78.38% CUB, 56.98% MI→CUB, 87.05% Omni→EMNIST). For uncertainty quantification, MD-BFSC attains the lowest ECE on CUB (0.005) and MI→CUB (0.005), and the lowest MCE on MI→CUB (0.014) and Omni→EMNIST (0.025). Convergence experiments show MD-BFSC's ELBO increases faster than vanilla gradient descent in both 1-shot and 5-shot inner-loop settings, and the full bi-level training converges more quickly in the 5-shot scenario.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MD-BFSC outperforms all non-GP baselines (Feature Transfer, Baseline++, MatchingNet, ProtoNet, RelationNet, MAML) by a wide margin across every setting, with the largest gaps on cross-domain tasks (e.g., 40.75% vs. 32.77% for Feature Transfer on MI→CUB 1-shot). Among GP-based methods, MD-BFSC beats DKT on 1-shot CUB (65.45% vs. 63.37%) and 1-shot MI→CUB (40.75% vs. 40.22%), but trails OVE PG GP(PL) on 5-shot CUB (78.38% vs. 79.07%), 5-shot MI→CUB (56.98% vs. 57.23%), and 1-shot Omni→EMNIST (74.02% vs. 77.00%). Against the closest GP competitor CDKT(ML)(τ<1), MD-BFSC edges ahead on 1-shot CUB (65.45% vs. 65.21%) and 1-shot MI→CUB (40.75% vs. 40.43%) but falls slightly behind on 5-shot CUB (78.38% vs. 79.10%). In uncertainty quantification, MD-BFSC achieves the best ECE on CUB and MI→CUB (both 0.005) and the best MCE on MI→CUB (0.014) and Omni→EMNIST (0.025), surpassing OVE PG GP(PL) and CDKT(ML) in those settings. Overall, MD-BFSC is most competitive in 1-shot regimes and cross-domain transfer, while the Pólya-Gamma-augmented baselines retain a small edge in 5-shot in-domain accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of GP-based few-shot classification (Patacchiola et al., 2020; Snell & Zemel, 2021; Ke et al., 2023) and information-geometric optimization. Prior GP-based FSC methods handle the non-conjugate softmax likelihood by introducing auxiliary variables (Pólya-Gamma augmentation, one-vs-each approximation) or by assuming a Gaussian likelihood, all of which add modeling complexity. The key departure here is the use of mirror descent (Nemirovskij & Yudin, 1985) to perform variational inference without any auxiliary variables, leveraging two established equivalences: mirror descent over mean parameters equals natural gradient descent over natural parameters (Raskutti & Mukherjee, 2015), and mirror descent reduces to a conjugate Bayesian update (Khan & Lin, 2017). The paper thus repurposes existing optimization theory to eliminate the need for data augmentation tricks while retaining the bi-level deep-kernel meta-learning framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameterization invariance property is practically significant because in bi-level meta-learning the inner-loop convergence rate directly affects outer-loop gradient quality, and practitioners typically lack guidance on which variational parameterization is optimal. The method achieves this with only first-order computation, matching the wall-clock cost of vanilla gradient descent (Table 4), making it a drop-in replacement. However, the outer loop still relies on first-order Adam updates due to computational constraints, and the accuracy gains over strong GP-based baselines (OVE, CDKT) are modest rather than transformative. An open question is whether extending second-order or mirror-descent-style updates to the outer loop (kernel hyperparameters, CNN weights) would yield further convergence benefits in the highly nonconvex landscape of deep kernels.
