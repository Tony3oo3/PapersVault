## Problem

Most autoencoder regularization techniques assume a Euclidean ambient data space, which is arbitrary and often fails to capture the true semantic or domain-specific geometry of the data. When the relevant structure is better encoded by a non-Euclidean similarity graph (e.g., geodesic distances along a manifold, or task-specific semantic distances), existing geometry-preserving autoencoders either require expensive ambient metric estimation or produce distorted latent representations that do not reflect the graph's geometry.

## Input / Output

The method takes high-dimensional data points (2D images, 3D point coordinates) together with a pre-constructed similarity graph whose edge weights encode semantic or geodesic distances between data pairs. It produces a low-dimensional latent embedding (typically 2D or 3D) that preserves the local geometric structure implied by the graph, without requiring explicit coordinates or a Riemannian metric on the data manifold. The graph can be sparse, and the method remains effective even when a large fraction of edges are missing.

## Method

GGAE augments the standard autoencoder reconstruction loss with a Riemannian geometric distortion regularizer that measures how far the encoder mapping deviates from an isometry between the data manifold (approximated by the graph) and the Euclidean latent space. The key technical contribution is a coordinate-free estimation of the distortion: it uses the graph Laplacian (constructed from shortest-path distances on the graph) to approximate the Laplace-Beltrami operator, which in turn yields an estimate of J_f G^{-1} J_f^T without ever needing local coordinates or a metric on the data manifold. For scalable mini-batch training, the paper proposes two batch Laplacian approximation strategies—Laplacian-Slicing (slicing a precomputed global Laplacian) and Batch-Kernel (recomputing the Laplacian from a batch submatrix of the kernel)—and shows empirically that Batch-Kernel, despite being biased, has far lower variance and converges to the true distortion much faster. The resulting local-metric-based regularization makes GGAE robust to errors in global geodesic distances and to missing edges in the graph, unlike global-distance-based methods such as SPAE.

## Results

Across four datasets (Swiss Roll, dSprites, Rotating MNIST, Robot Images), GGAE achieves the best average rank on all graph geometry preservation metrics: 1.5±0.6 on kNN, 1.2±0.5 on Spearman correlation, and 1.2±0.5 on KL_{0.01}, KL_{0.1}, and KL_{1}. On the latent dynamics learning task (robot images), GGAE achieves rank 1 with a dynamics prediction MSE of 5.927e-3, slightly outperforming SPAE-graph (5.985e-3) and substantially outperforming AE (8.306e-3). On Swiss Roll, GGAE achieves a Spearman correlation of 1.0000 and kNN of 0.9911, while on dSprites it reaches 0.9979 Spearman and 0.9683 kNN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GGAE outperforms all eleven baselines on every graph geometry preservation metric (kNN, Spear, KL_{0.01}, KL_{0.1}, KL_{1}) across all four datasets. The closest competitor is SPAE-graph, which also leverages graph geodesic distances but relies on global shortest-path distances; GGAE's local-metric-based regularization gives it a consistent edge (e.g., rank 1.2 vs. 1.8 on Spearman) and a decisive advantage under sparse graphs, where SPAE-graph's kNN drops from 0.9731 to 0.7772 (Swiss Roll, p=0.9) while GGAE's stays at 0.9853. TopoAE-graph and NRAE-graph, which preserve topological or neighborhood structure rather than metric geometry, rank 4th and 8th on kNN respectively, confirming that topology preservation alone is insufficient for geometry preservation. Standard autoencoders (AE, VAE) and Euclidean geometry-preserving methods (IRAE, GeomAE, GRAE) all rank 5th or worse on geometry metrics, demonstrating that the Euclidean ambient space assumption is inadequate when the data manifold carries non-Euclidean structure. On the latent dynamics task, GGAE and SPAE-graph produce smooth, evenly-spaced latent trajectories (MSE ≈ 5.9e-3), while methods with distorted latent spaces (AE, GeomAE) exhibit irregular jumps and higher prediction error (7.1e-3 to 8.3e-3).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the geometry-preserving autoencoder line (IRAE, GeomAE) by replacing the assumed Euclidean ambient metric with a Riemannian geometry derived from a user-supplied similarity graph, using a graph Laplacian to approximate the Laplace-Beltrami operator and compute a coordinate-free distortion regularizer. It also builds on graph-based autoencoders (SPAE, TopoAE, NRAE) but departs from them by relying on local metric information (via the graph Laplacian) rather than global geodesic distances, which is the key conceptual distinction from SPAE. The batch-based approximation methods (Batch-Kernel, Laplacian-Slicing) are novel engineering contributions that make the full-dataset distortion computation tractable for mini-batch training, drawing on convergence theory from Hein et al. (2007) for the Batch-Kernel variant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GGAE offers a principled, computationally efficient way to inject domain-specific geometric knowledge (encoded as a similarity graph) into autoencoder training without the cost of estimating high-dimensional ambient metrics, and its demonstrated robustness to 90% edge removal makes it practical for real-world settings where complete pairwise similarity is unavailable. The latent dynamics learning case study with robot images shows that graph geometry preservation directly translates to smoother, more accurate future-state prediction, suggesting broader utility in model-based control. Key open questions include the lack of a canonical bandwidth tuning strategy (requiring per-dataset manual tuning) and how to automatically construct appropriate similarity graphs when no ground-truth semantic distances are available, as illustrated by the MNIST experiment with label-modified distances.
