## Problem

Conventional score-based MCMC methods (MALA, HMC) rely on local gradient information and fail to cross low-density barriers separating distant modes in multimodal distributions, leading to prolonged trapping in a single mode. While Gaussian convolution can bridge disconnected modes by creating non-negligible density paths, the score of the resulting convolved marginal is intractable for non-Gaussian targets, making direct score-based sampling infeasible.

## Input / Output

The method requires only access to a differentiable, lower-bounded energy function E(x) and its gradient (no normalization constant, no training data, no learned score network), along with an initial sample x^(0) and Gaussian convolution hyperparameters α ∈ [0,1] and σ > 0. It produces a sequence of samples from the target p(x) ∝ exp(−E(x))/Z, with the number of Gibbs sweeps K and denoising steps L controlling the computational budget per sample. In the multi-level variant, a VP schedule with T noise levels replaces the single (α, σ) pair, eliminating manual hyperparameter tuning.

## Method

DiGS performs Gibbs sampling on the joint p(x, x̃) = p(x̃|x)p(x) by alternately drawing a noisy sample x̃ ~ N(αx, σ²I) and a clean sample x ~ p(x|x̃). The key insight is that while the convolved marginal p(x̃) has an intractable score, the denoising posterior p(x|x̃) ∝ exp(−E(x) − ‖αx − x̃‖²/2σ²) has a tractable score, enabling standard score-based samplers (MALA, HMC) in the denoising step. A Metropolis-within-Gibbs scheme proposes a new initialization x'_init ~ N(x̃/α, (σ/α)²I) accepted with a tractable MH ratio, preventing the denoising sampler from being trapped near the previous mode and ensuring correct density allocation across modes. A multi-level variance-preserving (VP) noise schedule (α_T < … < α_1 with σ_t² = 1 − α_t²) progressively reduces noise from near-isotropic to near-identity, allowing DiGS to produce valid samples at any timestep without requiring the asymptotic limit, and circumventing the need for precise single-level hyperparameter selection.

## Results

On the 2D Mixture of 40 Gaussians (MoG-40), DiGS achieves an MMD of 4.57×10⁻⁴ and MAE of 0.75%, dramatically outperforming MALA (MMD 1.73, MAE 93.3%), HMC (MMD 1.70, MAE 92.8%), and Parallel Tempering (MMD 1.89×10⁻², MAE 7.32%). On a 550-parameter Bayesian neural network, DiGS achieves a test NLL of 0.189±0.002, beating PT (0.241), HMC (0.315), and MALA (0.399). On the 60-dimensional alanine dipeptide molecular dynamics task, DiGS achieves a Ramachandran KL divergence of 4.3×10⁻³, outperforming the gold-standard 21-chain PT (5.3×10⁻³) while using 23× fewer energy evaluations (1.0×10⁹ vs. 2.3×10¹⁰).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiGS consistently outperforms all baselines across all three evaluation tasks. Against MALA and HMC, DiGS achieves orders-of-magnitude lower MMD on MoG-40 (4.57×10⁻⁴ vs. ~1.7) and lower KL divergence on alanine dipeptide (4.3×10⁻³ vs. 1.1×10⁻² and 7.1×10⁻³), demonstrating that local-gradient score-based samplers fail to cross mode barriers. Against Parallel Tempering, DiGS achieves a 41× lower MMD on MoG-40 (4.57×10⁻⁴ vs. 1.89×10⁻²) and a lower Ramachandran KL on molecular dynamics (4.3×10⁻³ vs. 5.3×10⁻³) while using 23× fewer energy evaluations, highlighting that tempering cannot bridge truly disconnected modes. Against Reverse Diffusion Monte Carlo (RDMC), DiGS achieves comparable accuracy with 10× fewer energy evaluations by avoiding the nested MCMC hierarchy. On the BNN task, DiGS achieves the lowest NLL (0.189) compared to PT (0.241), HMC (0.315), and MALA (0.399), suggesting broader mode coverage in the posterior.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiGS sits at the intersection of proximal samplers (Chen et al., 2022; Lee et al., 2021), score-based diffusion models (Ho et al., 2020; Song & Ermon, 2019), and classical score-based MCMC (MALA, HMC). It departs from proximal samplers by replacing rejection sampling around a single mode with full score-based sampling (MALA/HMC) in the denoising step, and from diffusion models by avoiding the need to learn or approximate the noisy marginal score—instead exploiting the tractable score of the denoising posterior p(x|x̃) via Gibbs sampling. The Metropolis-within-Gibbs initialization scheme and the multi-level VP noise schedule are novel contributions that address practical limitations of prior convolution-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiGS provides a general, training-free MCMC method that outperforms the gold-standard parallel tempering in molecular dynamics by 23× in energy evaluations, with strong gains also in Bayesian neural networks and high-dimensional MoGs. Key open questions include: the low acceptance rate (~0.15) of the MH initialization scheme limits efficiency, convergence guarantees for the multi-level noise schedule remain incomplete, and the method has only been tested with MALA in the denoising step (HMC or other samplers are unexplored). The approach could become a practical default for energy-based sampling in scientific computing where energy evaluations are expensive.
