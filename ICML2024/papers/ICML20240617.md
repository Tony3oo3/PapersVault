## Problem

Existing RL-based macro placement methods suffer from low sample efficiency, requiring thousands of online rollouts or large offline expert datasets to bootstrap, which is impractical for industrial chip design workflows that demand fast, high-quality placement across multiple design iterations. The paper addresses this by asking how to integrate the direct search capability of optimization with the adaptive learning of RL in a way that dramatically reduces the number of rollouts needed to reach competitive placement quality.

## Input / Output

The RL agent consumes four visual inputs at each placement step: the current canvas image showing already-placed macros, a binary position mask marking valid (non-overlapping) grid cells, a wiremask matrix quantifying the HPWL increment if the next macro were placed at each cell, and netlist/timestep embeddings. Macros are placed sequentially in descending area order on an N×N grid (N up to 512), and the output is the complete set of bottom-left corner coordinates for all macros, evaluated by the resulting HPWL. Actions are further restricted to grid cells with minimal wiremask values, effectively pruning the action space at each step.

## Method

EfficientPlace introduces a bi-level 'learning-inside-optimization' framework where a global MCTS tree search and a local RL agent operate synergistically. At the high level, a rolling frontier mechanism (Algorithm 2) maintains a bounded set of promising tree nodes using UCT-inspired scoring (Q-value plus exploration bonus), dynamically replacing underperforming frontiers with better candidates from their children. At the low level, a U-Net policy network trained with PPO executes rollouts from randomly selected frontier nodes, with the U-Net's skip connections and bilinear upsampling blocks enabling fine-grained spatial control on canvases up to 512×512. The wiremask restricts valid actions to minimal-HPWL-increment regions, and backpropagation of rollout results updates both tree node statistics and the RL policy within the same search episode. A theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) shows the expected frontier quality monotonically improves across iterations, and the approach outperforms both offline RL (ChiPFormer) and BBO (WireMask-EA) baselines within 1,000 steps.

## Results

On the ISPD2005 benchmark (8 circuits), EfficientPlace achieves the best HPWL (×10⁵) across all circuits within just 1,000 training steps, averaging 2.2 hours per chip. For example, on adaptec2 it reaches 46.79 vs. 64.38 for WireMask-EA (1k steps, 6.9h avg) and 67.10 for ChiPFormer (2k steps + pre-training). Even at 500 steps, EfficientPlace achieves second-best HPWL on most circuits. The method also outperforms SA (1M steps, ~15h avg) and all prior RL-based methods (GraphPlace, DeepPR, MaskPlace) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EfficientPlace (1k steps) achieves the lowest HPWL on all 8 ISPD2005 circuits, outperforming the closest competitor WireMask-EA (1k steps) by 1.5–27.5% depending on the circuit (e.g., 46.79 vs. 64.38 on adaptec2, 58.38 vs. 59.85 on bigblue3), while also running ~3× faster (2.2h avg vs. 6.9h avg). Against ChiPFormer (2k fine-tuning steps + pre-training), EfficientPlace wins on every circuit with margins up to 25% (e.g., 56.35 vs. 76.70 on adaptec3, 76.63 vs. 102.84 on bigblue4). Compared to MaskPlace (3k steps), the improvement is even more pronounced, with HPWL reductions of 22–44% across circuits. Against the optimization-based SA baseline (1M steps, ~15h avg), EfficientPlace achieves substantially lower HPWL (e.g., 5.94 vs. 19.24 on adaptec1) in a fraction of the time. The older RL methods GraphPlace (50k steps) and DeepPR (3k steps) are far behind, with HPWL values 2–7× higher than EfficientPlace on most circuits. In mixed-size placement (Table 5), EfficientPlace also achieves the best full-placement HPWL on all 8 circuits, confirming that superior macro placement translates to better end-to-end results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RL-based macro placement lineage (GraphPlace → MaskPlace → ChiPFormer) by retaining the visual-input RL agent (wiremask, position mask) and PPO training, but fundamentally repositions the role of tree search. Unlike AlphaGo/MuZero where MCTS serves as a local policy optimizer requiring many rollouts, EfficientPlace repurposes MCTS as a global strategic guide that manages a rolling set of 'frontier' nodes, directing the RL agent to exploit only the most promising regions of the search space. This 'learning-inside-optimization' bi-level framework also draws conceptual inspiration from optimization-based methods (SA, WireMask-EA) that maintain pools of best solutions, formalized here as the frontier update mechanism with UCT-inspired scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sample efficiency remains the critical bottleneck for deploying RL in industrial chip design, where engineers need fast placement across many design iterations. EfficientPlace's 3× speedup over the recent SOTA (WireMask-EA) while achieving better HPWL, and its ability to match ChiPFormer without any pre-training or expert data, makes RL-based placement more practically viable. However, the method currently optimizes only HPWL without explicitly incorporating congestion, dataflow, or regularity metrics, limiting its alignment with final PPA. Open questions include whether the frontier-based tree search generalizes to other EDA stages (routing, floorplanning) and whether the framework can handle multi-objective optimization without sacrificing its sample-efficiency advantage.
