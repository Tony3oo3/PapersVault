## Problem

In vertical-split (feature-split) distributed settings, two agents each observe different dimensions of the same samples and must communicate a limited number of bits to a central server that needs to estimate the full covariance matrix. Prior work characterized only special cases (scalar dimensions, infinite samples, or one-sided communication), leaving the fundamental trade-off between communication budget, sample size, dimensionality, and estimation accuracy open for the general multi-dimensional, finite-sample, two-sided setting.

## Input / Output

Agent 1 holds d₁-dimensional observations of m i.i.d. sub-Gaussian samples and Agent 2 holds the complementary d₂ dimensions of the same m samples; each agent is constrained to send at most B₁ and B₂ bits respectively to the central server. The server outputs a d×d positive semi-definite estimate Ĉ of the true covariance matrix C, with distortion measured in either operator norm or Frobenius norm. The problem is formulated as a min-max optimization over all σ-sub-Gaussian distributions, making the guarantees distribution-agnostic within that class.

## Method

The lower bound is established via Fano's inequality applied to two carefully constructed families of Gaussian distributions: one varying the cross-covariance (bounded using the Strong Data Processing Inequality for multivariate Gaussians) and one varying the self-covariance (bounded using the classical Data Processing Inequality), combined with packing-number arguments in matrix spaces under operator and Frobenius norms. The achievable scheme splits each agent's communication budget in half: one half encodes a quantized empirical self-covariance matrix via a covering net, and the other half encodes a quantized block of raw data samples so the server can reconstruct the cross-covariance. The resulting expected operator-norm error is O(σ²(√(d/m) + log(B_min/(d₁d₂))·√(max{d₁²/B₁, d₂²/B₂, d₁d₂/B_min}))), which matches the lower bound up to a logarithmic factor in the η-homogeneous regime where communication budgets scale proportionally to dimensions.

## Results

The paper establishes a minimax lower bound (Theorem 4.1) showing that any distributed covariance matrix estimation scheme with parameters (σ, m, d₁, d₂, B₁, B₂) must incur operator-norm error of at least Ω(σ² max{√(d₁d₂/B_min), √(d/m), 2^(−min{B₁/d₁², B₂/d₂²})}). The proposed achievable scheme (Theorem 4.4) attains error O(σ²(√(d/m) + log₂(B_min/(d₁d₂))·√(max{d₁²/B₁, d₂²/B₂, d₁d₂/B_min}))). In the η-homogeneous regime (where B₁/d₁ ≈ B₂/d₂), the upper and lower bounds match up to a logarithmic factor, establishing near-optimality. A key structural insight is that the cross-covariance estimation error scales as √(d₁d₂/B_min), meaning the communication budget of the 'poor' agent must scale proportionally to the dimension of the 'rich' agent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its lower-bound methodology against a naïve extension of the Bayesian Cramér-Rao (BCR) + Strong Data Processing Inequality (SDPI) approach from Hadar et al. (2019, STOC) and Hadar & Shayevitz (2019, IEEE Trans. IT). In the scalar case (d₁=d₂=1, m=∞, B₂=∞), both methods yield the same Ω(1/B₁) scaling. However, when extending to the general matrix case (d₁>1, d₂>1), the BCR+SDPI approach vectorizes the cross-covariance matrix and yields a Frobenius-norm lower bound of Ω(d₁d₂/B₁), which is weaker than the paper's bound by a factor of d_min = min{d₁, d₂}. The paper's Fano-based packing argument, combined with the SDPI tensorization property, avoids this loss and achieves the tighter √(d₁d₂/B_min) scaling. The paper also notes that Hadar & Shayevitz (2019) propose a scheme for d₁>1, d₂=1 without an optimality claim, and their approach outperforms per-coordinate estimation but does not address the general d₂>1 setting studied here.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on distributed correlation estimation under communication constraints initiated by Hadar et al. (STOC 2019) and Hadar & Shayevitz (IEEE TIT 2019), which studied only scalar correlations (d₁=d₂=1 or d₂=1) with infinite samples and one-sided communication budgets. Here, the authors generalize to full d₁×d₂ cross-covariance matrices, finite sample sizes, and two-sided finite communication budgets, while also handling general sub-Gaussian distributions rather than just Gaussian. Methodologically, the paper departs from the Bayesian Cramér-Rao + SDPI approach used in prior work, instead employing Fano's inequality with carefully constructed packing families and the strong data processing inequality, and explicitly demonstrates that naively extending the prior BCR-based method yields a weaker Frobenius-norm bound by a factor of d_min.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first fundamental information-theoretic trade-off curve for distributed covariance matrix estimation in the vertical-split setting, showing that in the practically relevant homogeneous regime the proposed scheme is near-optimal up to a logarithmic factor. A notable structural insight is that the 'poor' agent (with fewer dimensions) must allocate communication proportional to the rich agent's dimension, revealing an asymmetric communication requirement that is non-obvious. Open questions include closing the logarithmic gap between upper and lower bounds, extending the achievable scheme to more than two agents (only a lower bound is given for K>2), and understanding whether the log factor can be eliminated in specific parameter regimes. For federated learning and distributed statistics practitioners, these results provide a clear benchmark for how much communication is fundamentally necessary.
