## Problem

Existing Video-LLMs encode sampled frames independently without retaining precise temporal position information, and they are trained primarily on short trimmed clips for video-level captioning and QA. This leaves them unable to comprehend or localize specific segments within untrimmed, multi-minute videos, creating a gap between coarse-grained video-level understanding and fine-grained segment-level grounding and reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241680/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an untrimmed video (uniformly sampled into 300 frames) and a free-form text instruction or query. Outputs are generated as autoregressive text where temporal positions are expressed through a continuous temporal token space (300 learnable tokens with interpolation) rather than decimal text timestamps, enabling precise start/end time outputs. The model can produce segment-level captions with aligned timestamps, localized moment boundaries, per-segment action labels, or natural-language QA answers depending on the instruction.

## Method

Momentor introduces a Temporal Perception Module (TPM) that extends the LLM vocabulary with 300 learnable temporal tokens, constructs a continuous temporal feature space via linear interpolation between anchor points (avoiding the quantization error of discrete tokens), and applies a Neighboring Token Propagation mechanism that propagates parameter updates from each temporal token to its neighbors with exponentially decaying weights, enforcing embedding continuity. Temporal embeddings at each sampled frame's position are added directly to the projected frame features as fine-grained position encoding. Training proceeds in three stages: modality alignment on image/video-text pairs, Grounded Event-Sequence Modeling (an intermediate stage where the model decodes full event sequences with aligned timestamps to bridge coarse alignment and fine-grained grounding), and instruction fine-tuning on Moment-10M. Moment-10M is an automatically generated 10-million-instruction dataset built via an event boundary detection algorithm, an Instance-Event Matrix for structured spatio-temporal organization, and Vicuna-based instruction synthesis covering 8 task types including cross-segment reasoning. The architecture uses a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder and frozen LLaMA-7B, training only the linear projection layer and TPM.

## Results

On temporal grounding, Momentor achieves mIoU of 29.3 on ActivityNet-Captions and 28.5 on Charades-STA, substantially outperforming the best baseline (Valley at 21.9 and VideoChat at 25.9 respectively). On highlight moment retrieval (QVHighlights), Momentor reaches mAP 7.6 and R1@0.5 17.0, nearly doubling the best baseline (Valley: mAP 5.3, R1@0.5 8.7). On action segmentation, Momentor achieves MoF 24.4 on Breakfast and 17.8 on 50Salads, more than doubling the next-best method (Video-LLaMA: 11.6 and 14.3). On dense video captioning (ActivityNet-Captions), Momentor scores CIDEr 14.9 and METEOR 4.7, far exceeding all baselines (best CIDEr: 1.8 by Valley).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Momentor outperforms all four 7B baselines (Video-ChatGPT, VideoChat, Video-LLaMA, Valley) across every evaluated task. On temporal grounding (ActivityNet-Captions), Momentor's mIoU of 29.3 exceeds Valley (21.9) by 7.4 points and VideoChat (17.4) by 11.9 points; on Charades-STA, the mIoU gap is even larger (28.5 vs. 25.9 for VideoChat). For highlight moment retrieval on QVHighlights, Momentor more than doubles the R1@0.5 of the best baseline (17.0 vs. 8.7 for Video-ChatGPT and Valley). On action segmentation (Breakfast), Momentor's MoF of 24.4 is more than double Video-LLaMA's 11.6, the strongest prior method. On dense video captioning, Momentor's CIDEr of 14.9 represents an order-of-magnitude improvement over Valley (1.8) and Video-LLaMA (0.9), indicating that the Grounded Event-Sequence Modeling stage enables substantially richer and more accurate event descriptions. The closest competitor overall is Valley, which leads on ActivityNet R@0.3 (30.6) but is still 12.3 points behind Momentor (42.9) on the same metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

Momentor extends the Video-LLM line of work (VideoChat, Video-ChatGPT, Video-LLaMA, Valley) by addressing their fundamental limitation: the absence of fine-grained temporal representation and segment-level modeling. Architecturally, it departs from prior approaches that encode frames independently and project them without temporal information, introducing a Temporal Perception Module with a continuous temporal token space and neighboring token propagation to enable precise timestamp input/output. The training pipeline adds a Grounded Event-Sequence Modeling stage that bridges coarse-grained modality alignment and fine-grained segment-level instruction following, a training paradigm not present in prior Video-LLMs. On the data side, Moment-10M is the first large-scale video instruction dataset with segment-level annotations (actions, instance tracks, event boundaries), constructed via an automatic pipeline using Grounding DINO, PySceneDetect, and Vicuna, rather than relying on human annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Momentor demonstrates that a single unified Video-LLM can perform multiple fine-grained temporal tasks (temporal grounding, action segmentation, dense captioning, highlight moment retrieval) in a zero-shot setting, replacing the need for task-specific models that dominated prior literature. The Moment-10M dataset (10M instructions over 1.5M segments from 64.9k videos) is a substantial community resource for training and evaluating segment-level video reasoning. Limitations include the reliance on a frozen 7B LLM and frame encoder, the 300-frame uniform sampling that may be insufficient for very long videos, and the potential noise introduced by the fully automatic annotation pipeline. Open questions include whether the continuous temporal token space generalizes to videos of arbitrary length, whether the approach benefits from end-to-end fine-tuning of the LLM, and whether the automatic data generation quality is sufficient for more demanding downstream applications.
