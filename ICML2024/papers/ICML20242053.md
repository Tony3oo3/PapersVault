## Problem

Commonly used logic synthesis heuristics (Resub, Mfs2) apply node-level transformations sequentially to every node in a circuit DAG, but over 93% of these transformations are ineffective, making these heuristics 30–70× slower than alternatives and accounting for ~79% of total LS runtime. Learning a classifier to prune ineffective nodes is hindered by a severe out-of-distribution generalization problem: models trained on one set of circuits fail to generalize to unseen circuits due to distribution shift, degrading optimization quality when effective nodes are misclassified.

## Input / Output

For each node in a Boolean network DAG, the method constructs a local subgraph (root node plus a restricted set of neighboring nodes) and reformulates it as a fully-connected bipartite graph with the root as one partition and non-root neighbors as the other. The GCNN encoder produces a fixed-dimensional node embedding, which is fed into a multi-head classifier that outputs a binary effectiveness score per node. In the online phase, nodes whose scores fall below a top-k threshold (default 50%) are skipped, yielding an optimized circuit with reduced node count (size) and level (depth) while preserving functional equivalence.

## Method

The core contribution is the Circuit Domain Generalization (COG) framework, which formulates the OOD problem as a multi-domain learning task by partitioning circuits into domains (with an aggregation mechanism to balance sample sizes) rather than pooling all circuits into one dataset. A Knowledge-Driven Subgraph Representation (KDSR) module uses a single-layer GCNN on the bipartite subgraph to learn domain-invariant node embeddings, exploiting the insight that the transformation mechanism is invariant across circuits for a given heuristic. A Domain-Aware Distributional Classifier (DADC) then applies a shared backbone with M independent heads (one per training domain), averaging head outputs at test time to capture classification uncertainty. Theoretically, the paper derives a generalization error bound (Theorem 4.2) in an RKHS that depends on both the number of domains M and per-domain sample sizes n_k, yielding corollaries that equal sample sizes minimize the bound and that multi-domain training strictly outperforms a single mixed domain.

## Results

On the Mfs2 heuristic, PruneX-COG achieves a 37% average prediction recall improvement over the Random baseline and a 40.96% average runtime reduction compared to the Default Mfs2 heuristic under leave-one-out evaluation. On the Resub heuristic, PruneX-COG achieves a 33.7% recall improvement and 42.67% runtime reduction, reaching up to 3.1× faster runtime on the Hyp circuit. On very large-scale circuits (up to 20M nodes), PruneX-COG reduces runtime by up to 42% (saving up to 10.9 hours). When applied twice (2PruneX-COG with k=30%), the method simultaneously improves QoR—reducing size/depth by 6.88% on average—while achieving 25.98% runtime reduction over the Default Mfs2 heuristic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PruneX-COG against three baselines: (1) Random, which assigns uniform random scores to nodes; (2) EnsembleMLP, a simple multi-layer perceptron trained on all circuits as a single dataset; and (3) the Default heuristics (standard Resub and Mfs2 without any learned pruning). PruneX-COG substantially outperforms Random in prediction recall (37% average improvement for Mfs2, 33.7% for Resub), confirming that the learned classifier captures meaningful structure. Against EnsembleMLP, PruneX-COG consistently achieves higher recall, particularly on out-of-distribution circuits (e.g., on the Sin circuit for Mfs2, PruneX-COG reaches 0.79 recall vs. 0.65 for EnsembleMLP; for Resub, 0.81 vs. 0.28), demonstrating the critical role of the circuit domain generalization formulation. In the online phase, PruneX-COG reduces runtime by 40.96% (Mfs2) and 42.67% (Resub) on average while maintaining comparable QoR (size and depth) to the Default heuristics. The 2PruneX-COG variant (applying the heuristic twice with k=30%) further improves QoR—reducing depth by 30.23% on Hyp and size by up to 4.26% on Des perf—while still achieving 25.98% average runtime reduction over the Default Mfs2, showing that efficiency gains can be leveraged to improve optimization quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior ML-for-LS work (which tunes optimization flows, predicts post-design metrics, or improves decision-making within existing heuristics) by directly modifying the heuristics themselves—learning to skip ineffective node-level transformations. Conceptually, it repurposes domain generalization theory (Blanchard et al., 2011; Muandet et al., 2013) to a setting where domains (circuits) are not given a priori but must be constructed via an aggregation mechanism, and it draws on GCNNs for local subgraph encoding. Unlike prior GNN-for-EDA prediction work (CongestionNet, LHN) that pools all circuits into a single training set, PruneX explicitly formulates cross-circuit distribution shift as a circuit domain generalization problem, which the authors claim is the first such formulation in logic synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.1× runtime reduction on Resub/Mfs2 is practically significant for industrial chip design where these heuristics account for ~79% of total LS optimization time, directly impacting time-to-market. The circuit domain generalization formulation and its theoretical analysis (Theorem 4.2, Corollaries 4.3–4.4) provide a reusable framework for OOD prediction tasks across circuits in the broader EDA workflow. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other heuristics (Rewrite, Refactor), whether the ad-hoc circuit aggregation mechanism can be automated or learned, and whether the 'transformation-invariant domain knowledge' assumption holds for sequential (not just combinational) circuits. The method is currently validated only on two heuristics and combinational benchmarks, limiting confidence in broader applicability.
