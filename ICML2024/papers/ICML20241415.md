## Problem

Conservation laws for Euclidean gradient flows of linear and ReLU networks are well-characterized, but their existence, structure, and count under momentum-based dynamics (heavy ball, Nesterov) and non-Euclidean metrics (mirror descent, natural gradient) remain largely unknown. This paper asks: what conservation laws survive when one moves from first-order gradient flows to second-order momentum flows, and how does the geometry of the metric affect this?

## Input / Output

The framework takes a parametric model g(θ,x) (linear or ReLU network of any depth), a metric M (Euclidean identity, diagonal mirror for Shannon entropy, or data-dependent natural gradient Hessian proxy), a damping schedule τ(t) (∞ for gradient flow, constant for heavy ball, 3/t for Nesterov), and a dataset Z. It outputs the complete set of conserved functions h(t,θ,θ̇) (momentum case) or h(θ) (gradient case) that are invariant for all datasets, along with the exact count of independent laws computed via the dimension of a generated Lie algebra. The momentum-case outputs necessarily involve the factor exp(∫τ ds) multiplying velocity-dependent terms, distinguishing them structurally from gradient-flow invariants.

## Method

The paper formalizes conservation laws for momentum flows via a phase-space lifting to (t,θ,θ̇) ∈ ℝ^{2D+1}, proving that conserved functions in the momentum case exhibit non-trivial time dependence through exp(τt) (Theorem 2.1). The central structural result (Proposition 2.9) decomposes the momentum vector-field space W_α^mom into a time/velocity component plus the metric-scaled gradient-flow space W_θ, enabling transfer of known gradient-flow characterizations to momentum and non-Euclidean settings. Conservation laws are then identified as functions whose gradients are orthogonal to a finite set of vector fields χ_i spanning W_α^mom, and their count is certified via the dimension of the Lie algebra generated by these fields (Theorem 3.7). Applied to concrete cases, the framework reveals a systematic 'conservation loss': linear networks (PCA) retain r(r−1)/2 laws in the over-parameterized regime but lose r laws in the under-parameterized one, while ReLU networks (MLP), NMF, and ICNN lose all conservation laws entirely when momentum is introduced, regardless of metric.

## Results

The paper's central quantitative finding is a systematic 'conservation loss' when transitioning from gradient flow (GF) to momentum flow (MF). For two-layer linear networks (PCA) with r hidden neurons and r ≤ n+m, GF yields r(r+1)/2 independent conservation laws while MF yields only r(r−1)/2, a loss of exactly r laws. For ReLU networks (MLP), NMF (linear + mirror geometry), and ICNN (ReLU + mixed geometry), GF yields r conservation laws but MF yields zero — a complete loss of all conservation laws. In the over-parameterized regime r > n+m for linear networks, MF actually gains conservation laws relative to GF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; the core comparison is between gradient flow (GF) and momentum flow (MF) conservation law structures. The paper proves that momentum dynamics fundamentally reduce the number of conserved quantities: for linear networks in the under-parameterized regime (r ≤ n+m), MF loses exactly r conservation laws compared to GF (Proposition 4.4). For all ReLU-based architectures (MLP, ICNN) and for NMF, the loss is total — every conservation law present in GF disappears under MF. The only regime where MF gains conservation laws is the over-parameterized linear case (r > n+m), where the additional velocity-dependent terms in the conserved functions create new invariants. The paper also shows that conservation laws in MF necessarily depend on time (via exp(∫τ ds) factors) and velocity, in sharp contrast to the time-independent, parameter-only conservation laws of GF (Theorem 2.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conservation law framework of Marcotte et al. (2024), which characterized all conservation laws for Euclidean gradient flows of linear and ReLU networks via Lie algebra dimension, to two new settings: non-Euclidean metrics (mirror descent, natural gradient) and momentum-based dynamics (heavy ball, Nesterov). It leverages the Lagrangian formulation of Wibisono et al. (2016) to connect Noether's theorem to momentum flows, and builds on the reparameterization assumption (Assumption 2.13) from the prior work to reduce the problem to finite-dimensional vector field analysis. The key departure is showing that the gradient-flow-to-momentum limit is singular: conservation laws that are time-independent in the gradient flow case become time-dependent in the momentum case, and many (or all) are lost entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic loss of conservation laws when transitioning from gradient flow to momentum has direct implications for understanding what structural properties (balancedness, low-rank, sparsity) are preserved during standard deep learning training, since momentum is the default optimization technique in practice. The complete disappearance of all conservation laws for ReLU networks under momentum is a striking negative result that limits the applicability of conservation-law-based analyses to gradient-flow-only settings. Key limitations include the restriction to continuous-time flows (the discrete-time SGD-with-momentum case remains open), the requirement that the metric be dataset-independent (natural gradient is only partially addressed), and the fact that approximate conservation in the discrete setting is an important open problem the authors flag for future work.
