## Problem

In federated self-supervised learning, the global SSL objective generally does not equal the weighted sum of local objectives, so standard FedAvg cannot precisely minimize it—especially under non-i.i.d. data—because it only performs intra-client contrast and ignores inter-client contrast. Moreover, existing FedSSL methods either lack theoretical convergence guarantees or share components (predictors, raw representations) that introduce uncontrolled privacy leakage without formal DP analysis.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a disjoint subset of unlabeled RGB images (SVHN split into 5 clients of 10,000 images, CIFAR-10 into 10 clients of 5,000, CIFAR-100 into 20 clients of 2,500). Beyond standard model weights, clients upload H×H correlation matrices (512×512 in practice) of their local data representations, which are clipped to norm √μ and perturbed with Gaussian noise (scale σ²) before transmission. The final output is a trained ResNet encoder evaluated by a linear probe on downstream classification, with KNN accuracy also tracked during training.

## Method

FedSC decomposes the global spectral contrastive (SC) objective into three per-client terms— intra-client contraction, intra-client contrast, and inter-client contrast—and constructs a local objective that uses the server-aggregated correlation matrix of all *other* clients (R̄_{-j}) as a stop-gradient constant to enable inter-client contrast during local SGD. Clients periodically upload DP-noised correlation matrices (via a Gaussian mechanism with sensitivity μ/|D_j|) in addition to model weights; the server maintains a running aggregate and distributes it back so each client can compute its inter-client contrast term. This design yields gradient alignment between local and global objectives (Eq. 6), which FedAvg lacks, and the paper proves an O(1/√T) convergence rate for FedSC versus a constant error floor for FedAvg, along with a (ε,δ)-DP bound on the extra privacy leakage from sharing correlation matrices.

## Results

FedSC achieves the best or near-best linear-probe accuracy across all three datasets under both full and partial client participation. On CIFAR-100 (the hardest task), FedSC reaches 58.35% (full) and 56.64% (partial), outperforming the strongest baseline FedCA by 6.0 and 5.1 points respectively, and approaching the Centralized SC upper bound of 64.32%. On CIFAR-10, FedSC attains 80.06% (full) and 77.12% (partial), surpassing FedCA (78.22% / 77.22%) and FedAvg+SC (77.82% / 75.36%). On SVHN, FedSC (91.78% / 91.03%) is competitive with FedEMA (91.87% / 88.97%) and clearly ahead of FedCA (89.92% / 89.28%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedSC outperforms all five baselines on CIFAR-100 under both participation settings, with the largest margin over FedCA (52.35% full, 51.58% partial) by roughly 5–6 points. On CIFAR-10, FedSC leads FedCA by 1.8 points in full participation (80.06 vs. 78.22) and ties within noise in partial participation (77.12 vs. 77.22). On SVHN, FedSC is essentially tied with FedEMA in full participation (91.78 vs. 91.87) but clearly superior in partial participation (91.03 vs. 88.97). FedU, FedEMA, and FedAvg+BYOL all suffer severe degradation on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 (dropping 10–15 points below FedSC), which the authors attribute to their reliance on momentum-updated predictors that require many local updates to converge. FedX performs worst across all settings, lagging FedSC by 10–25 points, likely due to its cross-knowledge-distillation design being poorly suited to the spectral contrastive objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Spectral Contrastive (SC) objective of HaoChen et al. (NeurIPS 2021), deriving a novel decomposition of the global SC loss into intra-client contraction, intra-client contrast, and inter-client contrast terms. It departs from prior FedSSL methods (FedU, FedEMA, FedX, FedCA) by identifying that FedAvg fundamentally fails to minimize the global FedSSL objective under non-i.i.d. data because it only performs intra-client contrast, and by showing that sharing compact correlation matrices (H×H) rather than full representations or predictors is sufficient to recover the missing inter-client contrast. The paper is positioned as the first FedSSL method with both a convergence guarantee and differential privacy protection on the shared components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that FedAvg incurs a constant error floor (O(1)) for FedSSL, while FedSC achieves O(1/√T) convergence, provides a clear diagnostic for why prior federated SSL methods plateau on non-i.i.d. data. The demonstration that correlation matrices are a sufficient statistic for inter-client contrast—requiring far fewer parameters than predictors or raw representations—offers a practical communication-efficient design principle. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other SSL objectives (BYOL, SimCLR, VICReg), whether the convergence guarantees hold under more realistic non-convex training dynamics, and how the method scales to larger datasets and deeper architectures beyond CIFAR-scale experiments.
