## Problem

Existing pseudolabeling methods for fine-tuning VLMs assign a single hard pseudolabel per instance, which becomes heavily incorrect and class-imbalanced when the VLM's zero-shot accuracy is low on a downstream task. This paper asks whether generating a *set* of candidate pseudolabels per instance—rather than committing to one potentially wrong prediction—can improve true-label inclusion and class balance without requiring additional labeled data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242481/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled images from a downstream dataset together with class-name text prompts (e.g., "a photo of a [CLASS]") and, in SSL/TRZSL settings, a small labeled set of 2 samples per class. It outputs a prompt-tuned CLIP model (textual or visual prompts, prefix size 16, ViT-B/32 or ViT-L/14 backbone) that produces class predictions. The intermediate supervision signal is a per-instance candidate label set (average size close to 1) derived from a global confidence score matrix, rather than a single hard label, and is re-expressed as a binary indicator vector for partial-label loss computation.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage candidate pseudolabel generation strategy operating on a confidence score matrix over all unlabeled data. Intra-instance selection adaptively includes labels in descending confidence order until cumulative confidence exceeds a threshold τ (set as a quantile α of per-instance max confidences), while inter-instance selection retains only labels whose confidence for a given class exceeds a quantile β of that class's confidence distribution across all instances, enforcing class balance. The final candidate set is the intersection of both selections, and the classification problem is reformulated as partial-label learning, enabling direct use of existing loss functions (CC, RC, CAV, LW) without modification. The process is iterative: candidate pseudolabels are regenerated every T iterations with a curriculum-style increase in per-class sample count K_t, and model parameters are reinitialized between iterations. Setting α = 0% in the intra-instance threshold recovers hard pseudolabeling, showing the method subsumes prior approaches as a special case.

## Results

On the full unlabeled data setting with textual prompt tuning, CPL achieves top-1 accuracy of 89.66% (SSL) and 72.90% (UL) on Flowers102, 80.98% (SSL) and 77.39% (UL) on RESISC45, and 77.51% (SSL) and 67.26% (UL) on EuroSAT, consistently surpassing the strongest baseline GRIP by 5–19 percentage points. The largest gains appear on EuroSAT (+18.85% SSL, +10.05% UL over GRIP) and DTD (+5.14% SSL, +5.82% UL), where CLIP's zero-shot accuracy is weakest. Under visual prompt tuning, CPL similarly leads, e.g., 73.52% SSL and 86.67% TRZSL on RESISC45 versus GRIP's 71.22% and 82.19%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPL consistently outperforms all six baselines across all nine datasets and three learning paradigms. Against the two pseudolabeling baselines from Menghini et al. (2023), CPL surpasses FPL by 3–12 points and GRIP by 2–19 points (e.g., +18.85% on EuroSAT SSL, +5.14% on DTD SSL), with the largest margins on datasets where CLIP's zero-shot accuracy is lowest. In the versatility experiment (Table 3), CPL integrated into LaFTer improves top-1 accuracy by up to 10.2% over LaFTer alone on DTD, and outperforms UPL and CLIP-PR on all six UL benchmarks. On imbalanced CIFAR-100 (Table 5), CPL (77.32% balanced, 69.65% at δ=50) beats LaFTer (74.64%, 66.59%) by 2.7–3.1 points, confirming robustness under class imbalance. The only setting where CPL does not lead is CUB with visual prompts under SSL (49.50% vs. GRIP's 53.83%), suggesting that very fine-grained 200-class tasks with limited labeled data remain challenging even for candidate pseudolabels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the iterative pseudolabeling framework for CLIP prompt tuning established by Menghini et al. (2023, FPL/GRIP), which selects the most confident samples per class at each iteration. The key conceptual departure is replacing single hard pseudolabels with sets of candidate pseudolabels, drawing an analogy to multiple annotations in crowdsourcing and reframing the training objective as a partial-label learning problem. This allows the method to directly reuse existing partial-label loss functions (CC, RC, CAV, LW) without modification, while the intra- and inter-instance selection strategies address the class imbalance and low-confidence issues that plague hard pseudolabels when CLIP's zero-shot accuracy is weak.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical bottleneck: when CLIP's zero-shot performance is poor on a downstream task (e.g., EuroSAT at ~33%), hard pseudolabels become both inaccurate and class-imbalanced, severely limiting fine-tuning gains. The candidate pseudolabel approach is a lightweight, drop-in modification that consistently improves performance across nine datasets, three learning paradigms, and two prompt types, and can be plugged into other methods (e.g., LaFTer) for additional gains. The main open question is the inherent limitation of pseudolabeling — the true label may still be absent from the candidate set — and whether more sophisticated generation strategies or uncertainty-aware mechanisms could further close this gap. The finding that equal-weighting of candidate labels outperforms confidence-weighted soft targets also raises questions about when prior model confidence is informative versus misleading.
