## Problem

Transformers face two key inference bottlenecks: LayerNorm requires online mean/variance computation at test time, and softmax attention incurs quadratic complexity in sequence length. Directly replacing LayerNorm with BatchNorm in transformers causes training collapse and significant accuracy degradation, while existing linear attention methods (e.g., Flatten Transformer) still involve complex kernel designs that limit hardware efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240667/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For vision tasks, the method consumes RGB images at 224×224 (classification) or standard COCO resolutions (detection/segmentation) and produces class labels, bounding boxes with instance masks, or per-pixel segmentation maps. For language modeling, it processes text sequences of 512 tokens and predicts the next token. A key inference-time detail is that after progressive training, all normalization layers reduce to BatchNorm, which is algebraically fused into adjacent linear projections, eliminating any standalone normalization operation from the inference graph.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary components. First, Progressive Re-parameterized BatchNorm (PRepBN) blends LayerNorm and a new RepBN module via a linearly decaying coefficient γ (from 1 to 0 over training), where RepBN(X) = BN(X) + ηX with a learnable scalar η; Lemma 4.1 shows RepBN re-parameterizes exactly into standard BN with modified scale/shift (α+ησ, β+ημ), enabling fusion into linear layers at inference. Second, Simplified Linear Attention (SLA) replaces the softmax kernel with ReLU(Q)·ReLU(K)ᵀ for O(NC²) complexity and adds a depthwise convolution on V to recover local feature interactions, avoiding the more elaborate focused functions used by Flatten Transformer. Together these yield the SLAB transformer, which matches or slightly exceeds Flatten Transformer accuracy while reducing latency by 2–6 ms across DeiT, PVT, CSwin, and Swin backbones.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, SLAB-Swin-S achieves 83.6% top-1 accuracy with 16.2 ms latency, outperforming Flatten-Swin-S (83.5%, 18.6 ms) on both accuracy and speed. The PRepBN normalization alone improves top-1 accuracy over LayerNorm across all tested backbones (e.g., DeiT-T: 73.6% vs 72.2%; DeiT-S: 80.2% vs 79.8%; Swin-B: 83.6% vs 83.5%) while increasing throughput by 15–22%. On COCO object detection, PRepBN-equipped Mask R-CNN maintains comparable mAP (e.g., Swin-S: 45.9 vs 46.2 AP^b) while reducing latency from 63.1 ms to 57.3 ms. On Wikitext-103, PRepBN achieves slightly lower perplexity (19.2 val / 20.0 test vs 19.5 / 20.2) with 7% lower per-token inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LayerNorm-equipped DeiT, PVT, and Swin baselines, PRepBN consistently improves top-1 accuracy by 0.1–1.4% (e.g., DeiT-T: 73.6% vs 72.2%; PVT-T: 76.0% vs 75.1%) while boosting throughput by 15–22%, since BN can be fused into adjacent linear layers at inference. Compared to BN+FFNBN (Yao et al., 2021), PRepBN achieves clear wins on every backbone tested: +1.4% on DeiT-S (80.2% vs 78.8%), +0.5% on Swin-T/S/B, and +0.4% on Swin-S. Against Flatten Transformer (Han et al., 2023), SLAB wins decisively on latency (9.6 vs 15.2 ms for DeiT-T; 16.2 vs 18.6 ms for Swin-S) and matches or exceeds accuracy on DeiT-T (+0.2%) and Swin-S (+0.1%), though it trails by 0.3–1.3% on DeiT-S, PVT-T, CSwin-T, and Swin-T. On language modeling, PRepBN slightly improves perplexity over the LayerNorm Adaptive Inputs baseline (19.2 vs 19.5 val) while reducing per-token latency from 13.9 to 12.9 ms. On LLaMA-350M, PRepBN increases throughput from 44.0 to 50.4 tokens/s and raises average benchmark accuracy from 49.32 to 49.78, demonstrating that the normalization swap is effective even for modern LLM architectures using RMSNorm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two efficiency-focused lines of transformer research: (1) replacing LayerNorm with BatchNorm to enable inference-time fusion with linear layers, extending Yao et al.'s (2021) partial BN insertion in the MLP block to a full progressive replacement of all normalization layers, and (2) linear attention for sub-quadratic complexity, where it simplifies Flatten Transformer's (Han et al., 2023) focused kernel into a ReLU-based similarity plus depthwise convolution. The key conceptual departure from prior BN-in-transformer work is the progressive training schedule (linearly decaying γ) combined with a re-parameterized BN formulation (RepBN) that absorbs a learnable residual into the standard BN parameters, making the final model a pure BN-based architecture amenable to operator fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is clear for deployment: eliminating online normalization statistics and simplifying attention reduces inference latency by 2–6 ms across backbones while maintaining or slightly improving accuracy, and the method composes with quantization (demonstrated with RepQ-ViT). However, the accuracy gains are modest (0.1–1.4%), the training protocol requires careful tuning (reduced droppath, a 10-epoch statistics-only update at the end), and the evaluation is limited to models up to Swin-B and LLaMA-350M, leaving open whether the progressive strategy and SLA scale to much larger architectures or longer sequences. A broader open question is whether the ReLU-kernel linear attention can match softmax attention on tasks requiring fine-grained long-range dependencies beyond what depthwise convolution provides locally.
