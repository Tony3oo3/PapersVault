## Problem

Prior molecular conformer generation methods rely on domain-specific inductive biases—equivariant architectures, explicit torsional angle modeling, and rule-based cheminformatics to identify rotatable bonds—which limit scalability and can fail on complex or unusual molecules. This paper asks whether a simple, domain-agnostic diffusion model that directly predicts 3D atomic coordinates without any structural assumptions can match or surpass these specialized approaches, particularly as model scale increases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242063/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a molecular graph (atoms as vertices, bonds as edges) augmented with per-atom features (element type, degree, formal charge, valence, hybridization, aromaticity, ring count), encoded via the k leading eigenvectors of the normalized graph Laplacian as positional encoding. It outputs a full 3D conformer ensemble (2K conformers for K ground-truth conformers) by iteratively denoising random 3D coordinates. Notably, because the conformer is parameterized as a continuous field over the graph, the model can also be evaluated at interpolated points along bonds, producing plausible 3D positions for non-atomic locations never seen during training.

## Method

MCF formulates conformer generation as learning a diffusion model over conformer fields f: G → R³, extending Diffusion Probabilistic Fields (DPF) from Euclidean domains to graph domains. The score network is a PerceiverIO (Transformer encoder-decoder): a context set of (Laplacian eigenvector, 3D position) pairs is cross-attended into a latent array of learnable vectors, and a query set cross-attends to this latent array to predict the noise on 3D coordinates. Training uses the standard DDPM denoising objective on the query signal conditioned on the context, with no equivariance constraints, no torsional angle variables, and no rule-based substructure detection. The key empirical finding is that scaling the PerceiverIO from 13M to 242M parameters yields consistent gains across all benchmarks (GEOM-QM9, GEOM-DRUGS, GEOM-XL), outperforming equivariant and torsion-based baselines by large margins—suggesting that model scale can substitute for domain-specific architectural inductive biases.

## Results

On GEOM-QM9, MCF (base) achieves a recall COV of 95.0% and recall AMR of 0.103 Å, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art Torsional Diffusion (92.8% COV, 0.178 Å AMR) by a substantial margin. On the harder GEOM-DRUGS benchmark, MCF-L reaches 84.7% recall COV and 0.390 Å recall AMR, surpassing Torsional Diffusion (72.7% COV, 0.582 Å AMR) by over 15% in coverage. On GEOM-XL (molecules with >100 atoms), MCF-L achieves 1.64 Å precision AMR and 2.57 Å recall AMR, beating Torsional Diffusion's 1.93 Å and 2.84 Å respectively, demonstrating strong out-of-distribution generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCF outperforms all four learning-based baselines (CGCF, GeoDiff, GeoMol, Torsional Diffusion) on both GEOM-QM9 and GEOM-DRUGS across every reported metric (recall/precision COV and AMR). The largest gap is against CGCF and GeoDiff, which rely on two-stage generation or equivariant GNNs; MCF's recall AMR on GEOM-QM9 (0.103 Å) is roughly 3× lower than CGCF (0.425 Å) and 3× lower than GeoDiff (0.297 Å). Against the strongest baseline Torsional Diffusion, MCF improves recall AMR by ~42% (0.103 vs 0.178 on QM9) and precision AMR by ~46% (0.119 vs 0.221), while also achieving higher coverage. On GEOM-DRUGS, even the smallest MCF variant (MCF-S, 13M params) surpasses Torsional Diffusion by ~15% in both recall and precision COV, and MCF-L extends this lead to ~20%. On GEOM-XL, Torsional Diffusion fails to generate conformers for 25 of 102 molecules due to RDKit local-structure failures, whereas MCF handles all molecules; on the 77 molecules where both succeed, MCF-L achieves 1.64 Å precision AMR vs. Torsional Diffusion's 1.93 Å. OMEGA (a rule-based method) is only compared on ensemble chemical properties (Table 7), where MCF achieves the lowest E_min error (0.04 kcal/mol) but a higher mean energy error (1.00 vs 0.22 kcal/mol for Torsional Diffusion), indicating MCF occasionally generates higher-energy conformers.

## Relation to Prior Work

MCF extends Diffusion Probabilistic Fields (Zhuang et al., 2023), which learns distributions over functions in Euclidean space, to functions defined on molecular graphs, using the graph Laplacian eigenvectors as positional encodings. It departs sharply from the dominant conformer generation paradigm (GeoDiff, GeoMol, Torsional Diffusion) that bakes in domain-specific inductive biases—equivariant architectures, torsional angle parameterization, or interatomic distance modeling—by instead operating directly on 3D atomic coordinates with a domain-agnostic PerceiverIO score network. The paper positions itself explicitly in the 'bitter lesson' tradition (Sutton, 2019), arguing that general-purpose scalable architectures can outperform purpose-built equivariant models when given sufficient capacity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that rotational equivariance and explicit torsional angle modeling are unnecessary for state-of-the-art conformer generation challenges a core architectural assumption in molecular 3D ML and suggests that the field may be over-constraining models with inductive biases that limit scalability. The consistent S→B→L scaling gains across GEOM-QM9, GEOM-DRUGS, and GEOM-XL provide a concrete recipe for improving conformer generation through scale alone. Key open questions include whether this approach transfers to proteins and other macromolecules, how it performs in low-data regimes, and whether it can be adapted for conditional tasks like molecular docking or Boltzmann-distributed sampling. The computational cost of the Transformer-based score network and multi-step diffusion sampling remain practical limitations.
