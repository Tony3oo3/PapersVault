## Problem

Existing adaptive experiments for ATE estimation optimize only the propensity score (treatment-assignment probability) to minimize asymptotic variance, but leave the covariate density (which experimental units to sample) fixed. This paper shows that jointly optimizing both the covariate density and the propensity score further reduces the semiparametric efficiency bound, and provides a complete adaptive experimental design that achieves this minimized bound.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240938/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires a known evaluation density q(x) (e.g., from population statistics) and sequentially consumes past observations {(Y_s, A_s, X_s)}_{s=1}^{t-1} to construct nonparametric estimators of conditional means and variances. At each round it outputs an adaptive covariate density p̂_t(x) from which the next unit is drawn, an adaptive propensity score ŵ_t(a|X_t) for treatment assignment, and ultimately an ATE estimate θ̂_T via the AIPWIW estimator. The key constraint is that the experimenter can actively choose which covariate values to sample (not just which treatment to assign), and the evaluation density q(x) must be known a priori to compute importance weights.

## Method

The paper derives the semiparametric efficiency bound τ(w,p) = E_{X~q}[(σ₀²(1)(X)/w(1|X) + σ₀²(0)(X)/w(0|X))·q(X)/p(X)] as a joint functional of propensity score w and covariate density p, then solves the optimization to obtain the efficient propensity score (Neyman allocation: w*(a|x) = σ₀(a)(x)/(σ₀(1)(x)+σ₀(0)(x))) and the efficient covariate density (p*(x) ∝ (σ₀(1)(x)+σ₀(0)(x))·q(x)), which oversamples units with high conditional outcome variance. The AAS-AIPWIS experiment sequentially estimates these using Nadaraya-Watson kernel regression on past observations, draws each new unit from p̂_t(x), assigns treatment via ŵ_t, and at the end computes the ATE with an AIPW estimator augmented by importance weighting q(X_t)/p̂_t(X_t) to correct for the non-i.i.d. sampling. A martingale CLT argument (using the sample-splitting technique) proves that √T(θ̂_T − θ₀) → N(0, τ*) where τ* is the minimized bound, establishing semiparametric efficiency without requiring Donsker conditions.

## Results

The paper evaluates ATE estimation performance using empirical MSE over 200 experiments with T=2000 rounds, under two covariate distributions (Gaussian N(1,25) and Uniform [-10,10]). The proposed AAS-AIPWIW experiment achieves the lowest MSE among all compared designs (DRCT, RCT, AS-AIPW), confirming that jointly optimizing both the covariate density and propensity score reduces estimation error beyond optimizing the propensity score alone. Notably, the non-oracle AAS-AIPWIW (using estimated nuisance functions) performs almost identically to or even better than the oracle version that uses the true w* and p*, a phenomenon the authors attribute to a known paradox in semiparametric estimation. The theoretical guarantee is that the asymptotic variance of the AIPWIW estimator equals the minimized semiparametric efficiency bound τ* = (E_{X~q}[σ₀(1)(X)+σ₀(0)(X)])², which is strictly smaller than the bound under fixed covariate density q(x) whenever the sum of conditional standard deviations varies across x.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares AAS-AIPWIW against four baselines: (1) DRCT (deterministic alternating assignment), (2) RCT (uniform 50/50 randomization), (3) AS-AIPW (adaptive propensity score optimization only, no covariate density optimization — the approach of van der Laan 2008, Hahn et al. 2011, and Kato et al. 2020), and (4) oracle versions of AS-AIPW and AAS-AIPWIW that use the true w* and p* from the start. AAS-AIPWIW achieves the lowest empirical MSE in both the Gaussian and Uniform covariate settings, outperforming DRCT, RCT, and AS-AIPW. The efficiency gain over AS-AIPW (which only optimizes the propensity score) comes from the additional covariate density optimization, which is theoretically guaranteed by the Jensen inequality: τ* ≤ τ̃, with strict inequality when (σ₀(1)(x)+σ₀(0)(x))² varies across x. Surprisingly, the non-oracle AAS-AIPWIW matches or slightly beats the oracle AAS-AIPWIW, and the non-oracle AS-AIPW matches or beats the oracle AS-AIPW, consistent with the semiparametric paradox identified by Henmi & Eguchi (2004) and Hitomi et al. (2008).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: adaptive experimental design for ATE estimation (van der Laan 2008; Hahn et al. 2011; Kato et al. 2020), which optimizes the propensity score, and active learning under covariate shift (Sugiyama 2006), which optimizes the sampling density for regression. The key departure is jointly optimizing both the covariate density p(x) and the propensity score w(a|x) within a single adaptive experiment, rather than treating them independently. The AIPWIW estimator combines the A2IPW framework of Kato et al. (2020) with the importance-weighting correction for covariate shift from Uehara et al. (2020), and the asymptotic analysis relies on the sample-splitting/martingale CLT techniques from Chernozhukov et al. (2018) and van der Laan (2008).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that optimizing covariate density beyond propensity score optimization strictly reduces the semiparametric efficiency bound (via Jensen's inequality) provides a principled justification for non-uniform sampling in experiments, with direct implications for reducing sample sizes in costly clinical trials and policy evaluations. The assumption that q(x) is known is a practical limitation; the paper sketches extensions (rejection sampling, pilot experiments) but does not fully develop them. Open questions include how the method behaves in high-dimensional covariate spaces where nonparametric nuisance estimation becomes challenging, and whether the efficiency gains materialize in finite-sample regimes typical of real experiments.
