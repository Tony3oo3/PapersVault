## Problem

Prior work (Barsbey et al., 2021; Shin, 2021) showed that SGD-trained network weights can be compressible, but only under unverifiable assumptions that the weights are heavy-tailed and statistically independent. This paper asks whether compressibility can be guaranteed from the training procedure itself, without relying on such post-hoc assumptions about the learned parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242038/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-hidden-layer network with n hidden units, a supervised dataset of (x, y) pairs, and hyperparameters including step size η, noise scale σ, stability parameter α ∈ (1,2), and a choice of α-stable noise type (Type-I, II, or III). It produces a weight matrix Θ^n ∈ R^{p×n} that is provably structurally compressible: retaining only the ⌊κn⌋ columns with the largest Euclidean norms yields a pruned matrix whose relative Frobenius norm error is at most ε with probability 1−ε, provided n ≥ N and η ≤ n^{−α/2−1} for a threshold N depending on κ and ε.

## Method

The core modification is injecting additive α-stable noise (infinite variance) into each SGD iterate at every step, scaled as σ·η^{1/α} so that the discrete dynamics homogenize to a McKean-Vlasov SDE as η→0. Two technical contributions underpin the proof: (i) a propagation-of-chaos result for heavy-tailed SDEs showing that in the mean-field limit (n→∞), the weight matrix columns become i.i.d. with a common heavy-tailed distribution, and (ii) strong-error estimates for the Euler discretization of the n-particle SDE that control the finite-η approximation gap. Compressibility then follows from the classical result (Gribonval et al., 2012) that i.i.d. heavy-tailed sequences are dominated in magnitude by a small fraction of their elements, so pruning the remaining columns incurs vanishing relative error. The main theorem (Theorem 3.1) guarantees that for any compression ratio κ ∈ (0,1) and any ε > 0, there exists N such that for all n ≥ N and η ≤ n^{−α/2−1}, the relative compression error is at most ε with probability 1−ε.

## Results

The key metric is the pruning ratio (percentage of the weight matrix prunable while retaining 90% of its squared Frobenius norm). On CIFAR-10 with a single-hidden-layer FCN (n=5K, Type-I noise), injecting α-stable noise increases the pruning ratio from 11.60% (vanilla SGD) to 49.67% (α=1.75), 41.01% (α=1.8), and 30.25% (α=1.9), while post-pruning test accuracy drops by at most ~5 percentage points. On ECG5000 with n=10K, the pruning ratio reaches 54.72% (α=1.75) versus 11.44% for vanilla SGD. On MNIST (n=5K), the ratio goes from 10.58% to 40.63% (α=1.75) with only ~3% post-pruning accuracy loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole baseline is vanilla SGD (no injected noise), which serves as the 'none' row in every results table. The heavy-tailed noise injection consistently and dramatically outperforms vanilla SGD in compressibility: on CIFAR-10 the pruning ratio increases ~4.3× (11.60% → 49.67% at α=1.75), on ECG5000 (n=10K) it increases ~4.8× (11.44% → 54.72%), and on MNIST it increases ~3.8× (10.58% → 40.63%). The cost is a modest drop in post-pruning test accuracy (1–5 percentage points depending on α and dataset), with lighter tails (α=1.9) preserving accuracy better at the expense of lower compressibility. The paper also shows that noise-added networks are more robust to progressive pruning (maintaining higher relative accuracy at increasing pruning ratios) compared to vanilla SGD, across FCN and CNN architectures on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. No comparison against other compression methods (e.g., lottery ticket, sparsity regularizers) is provided; the contribution is framed as a theoretical guarantee of compressibility rather than a competitive benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the compressibility framework of Barsbey et al. (2021) and Shin (2021), which showed that heavy-tailed SGD weights are compressible but relied on an unverifiable independence assumption between weight columns. Here, the authors prove that this independence condition actually emerges in the mean-field limit when α-stable noise is injected into SGD, by establishing a propagation-of-chaos result for heavy-tailed McKean-Vlasov SDEs (building on De Bortoli et al., 2020, who treated the Brownian case) and deriving Euler discretization error bounds. The compressibility guarantee then follows from the classical ℓp-compressibility theory of Gribonval et al. (2012) and Amini et al. (2011), which characterizes when i.i.d. heavy-tailed sequences are compressible. The O(n^{-1/2}) convergence rate for the particle system improves upon the best-known rate for Lévy-driven McKean-Vlasov SDEs under classical Lipschitz assumptions (Cavallazzi, 2023), thanks to the specific one-hidden-layer network structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result providing a provable compressibility guarantee for neural network weights without requiring unverifiable statistical independence assumptions, reducing the problem to a simple, implementable modification of SGD (injecting α-stable noise). The practical implication is significant: a negligible computational overhead yields networks that are both more compressible and more robust to progressive pruning, as validated across ECG, MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100 with FCN and CNN architectures. Key limitations include the theoretical restriction to single-hidden-layer networks with a fixed second layer, the lack of explicit dependence between iteration count k and width n, and the exclusion of adaptive optimizers. Open questions include extending the theory to deeper architectures, understanding how data distribution and architecture interact with noise type to produce compressibility, and whether the approach transfers to modern training pipelines with Adam or similar optimizers.
