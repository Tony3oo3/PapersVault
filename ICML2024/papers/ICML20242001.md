## Problem

Existing reward-free RL results with kernel function approximation are limited to very smooth kernels with exponentially decaying eigenvalues, leaving important kernel families such as Matérn and Neural Tangent kernels (which exhibit polynomial eigendecay) without even finite sample complexity guarantees. This paper closes that gap by establishing order-optimal sample complexity for the broader class of kernels with polynomially decaying Mercer eigenvalues.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242001/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The exploration phase consumes N episodes of state-action trajectories collected without any reward signal, where the state-action space is a d-dimensional hypercube and transition probabilities are assumed to lie in the unit ball of the RKHS induced by kernel k. In the planning phase, a deterministic reward function r: Z→[0,1] is revealed, and the algorithm outputs a policy π that is ε-optimal (i.e., V*−V^π ≤ ε for all initial states). The sample complexity depends on the kernel's smoothness parameter α (e.g., α=2ν for Matérn with smoothness ν), yielding Õ((H³/ε)^(2+2d/α)) episodes.

## Method

The algorithm combines two key mechanisms: (i) a hypothetical exploration reward proportional to the kernel ridge regression posterior standard deviation (β(δ)σ^n/H), which drives the agent toward uncertain state-action regions rather than performing uniform exploration, and (ii) adaptive domain partitioning where the state-action hypercube is recursively split into smaller hypercubes whenever the observation count exceeds a threshold ρ^{-α}, and kernel ridge regression is computed independently within each subdomain. This partitioning yields tighter confidence intervals by exploiting the scaling of kernel eigenvalues with domain size (λ_m ≤ C_p m^{-p} ρ^α). In the planning phase, for each point z, the algorithm selects the exploration episode whose partition provides the tightest confidence interval and uses only observations within that subdomain for the kernel ridge predictor. The resulting sample complexity of Õ((H³/ε)^(2+2d/α)) is order-optimal in ε, matching the lower bound for kernel-based bandits with Matérn kernels in the degenerate H=1 case.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 4.5) establishes a sample complexity of Õ((H³/ε)^(2+2d/α)) exploration episodes to guarantee ε-optimal policies in the planning phase, where α captures the kernel's smoothness via polynomial eigendecay. For the Matérn kernel with smoothness parameter ν, this specializes to Õ((H³/ε)^(2+d/ν)), which matches the Ω((1/ε)^(2+d/ν)) lower bound for kernel-based bandits with Matérn kernels (Scarlett et al., 2017), confirming order-optimality in ε. The confidence interval multiplier scales as β(δ) = O(H√log(HN/δ)), and the algorithm uses adaptive domain partitioning to achieve tighter per-partition confidence intervals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The most direct comparison is with Qiu et al. (2021), which provides reward-free RL guarantees for kernel-based function approximation but only for kernels with exponentially decaying eigenvalues (e.g., Squared Exponential). For the Matérn family with smoothness ν, Qiu et al.'s sample complexity becomes unbounded whenever ν < d(d+1)/2, effectively excluding a large class of practically important kernels. This paper resolves that gap by proving finite, order-optimal sample complexity Õ((H³/ε)^(2+d/ν)) for all Matérn kernels, matching the known lower bound. The tabular result of Ménard et al. (2021) and the linear result of Wagenmaker et al. (2022) address fundamentally different (finite-dimensional) settings and are not directly comparable, but they confirm the general trend that sample complexity scales as 1/ε² in simpler settings, while the kernel-based polynomial eigendecay setting incurs an additional (1/ε)^(2d/α) factor reflecting the infinite-dimensional nature of the function class. The paper also notes that its H-dependency (H³) improves by a factor of H over the oracle-based discounted kernel RL result of Yeh et al. (2023), though the H-dependency remains an open question for further improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reward-free RL framework from tabular and linear settings to kernel-based function approximation, building directly on Qiu et al. (2021), who provided the first kernel-based reward-free RL results but only for kernels with exponential eigendecay (e.g., Squared Exponential). The key technical departure is the use of adaptive domain partitioning—adapted from Vakili & Olkhovskaya (2023) for standard episodic RL and Janz et al. (2020) for kernelized bandits—to handle the broader and practically important class of kernels with polynomial eigendecay, including Matérn and Neural Tangent kernels. The exploration algorithm follows the standard optimistic LSVI structure, with the novelty lying in how kernel ridge regression confidence intervals are localized to partition elements to achieve tighter bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result closes a significant gap: prior kernel-based reward-free RL methods yielded unbounded sample complexity for many Matérn kernel smoothness parameters, effectively excluding the most commonly used kernel families in practice. The order-optimal sample complexity in ε, matching the kernel-based bandit lower bound in the degenerate H=1 case, provides a clean theoretical guarantee that the exploration strategy is information-theoretically tight. Open questions remain regarding the dependence on episode length H (the paper notes it is unresolved whether the H³ factor can be improved) and whether the hypercube domain assumption can be relaxed to more general geometries without degrading the rates.
