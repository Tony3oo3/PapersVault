## Problem

Neural operators for PDE solving face a data-efficiency challenge: training targets from classical solvers are expensive to generate (e.g., ~1 CPU-hour per airfoil sample), and complex deep models overfit severely under limited data. Existing attention-based operators lack built-in regularization mechanisms that exploit the mathematical structure of kernel integral operators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242230/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes PDE input functions discretized on meshes of arbitrary geometry—structured grids (Darcy, NS2d, Pipe, Plasticity) or unstructured point clouds (Elasticity, Airfoil)—and produces solution values at the same or independently specified query locations. Because the orthogonal attention operates as a cross-attention between input and output point sets, the model is mesh-invariant and can be trained at low resolution (e.g., 43×43) and evaluated zero-shot at up to 421×421 without retraining.

## Method

ONO is a two-pathway neural operator: a bottom flow of transformer NN blocks extracts hierarchical features from the input function, and a top flow updates PDE solution hidden states via orthogonal attention. The key mechanism is that the NN features are projected to k-dimensional outputs and orthonormalized in function space by right-multiplying with the inverse Cholesky factor of the feature covariance matrix (maintained via exponential moving average over mini-batches), yielding neural eigenfunctions. The kernel integral operator is then parameterized as ψ′ diag(μ) ψᵀ h with learnable positive eigenvalues μ, which structurally mirrors attention without softmax. This orthonormalization constraint acts as an inherent regularizer that enforces linear independence among the learned eigenfunctions, reducing overfitting under limited data while keeping computational cost moderate since k ≪ M.

## Results

On six PDE operator learning benchmarks, ONO achieves state-of-the-art l₂ relative error on three: NS2d (0.1195), Airfoil (0.0056), and Pipe (0.0034), reducing prediction error by up to 31% over the best baseline (LSM). On Darcy, ONO achieves 0.0072 (second to LSM's 0.0069), and on Elasticity 0.0118 (second to GNOT's 0.0086). In zero-shot super-resolution on Darcy, ONO reduces error by 89% versus FNO at 421×421 resolution (0.0386 vs 0.3631), and in temporal generalization on NS2d it cuts unseen-timestep error by 12% (0.2143 vs 0.2446).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ONO outperforms FNO on every benchmark where both are reported (e.g., 0.1195 vs 0.1556 on NS2d, 0.0072 vs 0.0108 on Darcy) and dramatically surpasses it in zero-shot super-resolution (89% error reduction at 421×421). Against the Galerkin Transformer, ONO wins on both NS2d (0.1195 vs 0.1401) and Darcy (0.0072 vs 0.0084). Versus Geo-FNO, ONO is superior on Airfoil (0.0056 vs 0.0138), Pipe (0.0034 vs 0.0067), and Elasticity (0.0118 vs 0.0229), but trails on Darcy (0.0072 vs 0.0108) and Plasticity (0.0048 vs 0.0074). Against the latest SOTA baselines LSM and GNOT, ONO wins on NS2d, Airfoil, and Pipe, but is slightly behind LSM on Darcy (0.0072 vs 0.0069) and Plasticity (0.0048 vs 0.0035), and behind GNOT on Elasticity (0.0118 vs 0.0086). In the limited-data experiment on Elasticity, ONO shows the smallest performance degradation (58% error increase from 1200→400 samples) compared to ONO without orthogonalization (97%), Geo-FNO (92%), and DeepONet (68%), confirming the regularization benefit of the orthogonalization step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the neural eigenfunction framework (Deng et al., 2022b), which parameterizes kernel eigenfunctions with neural networks, and the kernel integral operator formulation from the neural operator literature (Kovachki et al., 2021). The key conceptual departure is recognizing that the Mercer's theorem expansion of a kernel integral operator into orthonormal eigenfunctions yields a structure isomorphic to attention without softmax, and that the orthonormality constraint naturally provides regularization. Unlike FNO-family methods that operate in the spectral domain or attention-based operators (GNOT, Galerkin Transformer) that lack built-in regularization, ONO introduces a dual-pathway architecture where a dedicated bottom pathway extracts eigenfunction features and a top pathway evolves the PDE solution via orthogonal attention, with orthogonalization implemented cheaply via EMA of the feature covariance matrix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The orthogonalization-as-regularization mechanism directly addresses the practical bottleneck of expensive PDE simulation data, showing that a theoretically grounded constraint can substitute for data volume. The 80% zero-shot super-resolution error reduction on Darcy (43×43 → 421×421) has immediate practical value for reducing computational cost of high-resolution PDE solving. A notable limitation is the higher memory footprint from the dual-pathway design, and the approach has not yet been validated on 3D problems or very large-scale PDE families. Open questions include whether the orthogonalization benefit persists at much larger model scales and whether the eigenfunction extraction pathway can be further compressed without sacrificing the regularization effect.
