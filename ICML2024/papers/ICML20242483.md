## Problem

Pre-trained Transformers exhibit sparse activation (over 90% zero hidden states), but this property has only been exploited for post-training inference acceleration. The paper asks whether sparse activation can be leveraged during pre-training itself to reduce computational costs, and how activation properties evolve across the training process to inform when sparse computation is safe to apply.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard text corpora (a ~27GB subset of The Pile) through conventional self-supervised objectives (CLM for GPT, MLM for BERT/T5) and produces pre-trained Transformer models of identical size to dense baselines. The key output distinction is that the final model retains an internal SMoE structure in its FFN layers, enabling it to be deployed either as a standard dense model or as a sparsely-activated MoE model (selecting top-K of N experts per token) for up to 2× faster inference without any additional fine-tuning.

## Method

SSD alternates between dense and sparse training phases during pre-training. It monitors activation pattern stability by computing the Adjusted Rand Index between neuron clusterings (via balanced k-means on FFN input weight rows) at consecutive checkpoints; when similarity exceeds a threshold τ, the dense FFN is converted to an SMoE layer by splitting weights into N experts and using cluster-center similarity as the gating score. Sparse training then computes only top-K experts (K=6, N=32), reducing FLOPs by up to 1.44×. The method reverts to dense training after a controlled fraction of steps (ratio r=0.5) to prevent representation collapse and allow activation patterns to continue evolving, repeating this cycle and ending with a final dense phase. The conversion uses the previous checkpoint's clustering as initialization to ensure smooth transitions, and the final dense model is already familiar with sparse computation, enabling direct sparse inference.

## Results

SSD achieves comparable or slightly better performance than dense pre-training with up to 1.44× FLOPs speedup (178T vs 237T FLOPs on GPT). On BERT-based NLU tasks, SSD reaches 83.6% average accuracy vs. 83.2% for Dense, 82.8% for PLD, 82.8% for MoE-Dropout, and 76.2% for SMoE. On T5-based NLU tasks, SSD achieves 86.6% average vs. 85.9% for Dense and 86.7% for PLD. For sparse inference, SSD models reduce FFN inference time by up to 2× (e.g., BERT: 2.3ms vs. 5.1ms) while maintaining performance on par with dense models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSD consistently outperforms SMoE (Fedus et al., 2022) across all settings: on BERT NLU tasks, SSD achieves 83.6% average vs. SMoE's 76.2% (a 7.4-point gap), and on T5 NLU tasks, 86.6% vs. 81.6%. Against PLD (Zhang & He, 2020), SSD matches or slightly exceeds performance (83.6% vs. 82.8% on BERT; 86.6% vs. 86.7% on T5) while also enabling efficient sparse inference, which PLD does not support. Against MoE-Dropout (Chen et al., 2023), SSD achieves comparable training FLOPs speedup (both up to 1.44×) but better downstream accuracy (83.6% vs. 82.8% on BERT; 86.6% vs. 86.0% on T5) and superior sparse inference trade-offs, as SSD's curve is consistently below MoE-Dropout's in the perplexity-vs-cost plots. Notably, SSD is the only acceleration method that matches or exceeds dense training performance on all evaluation settings, while also providing up to 2× faster sparse inference without additional training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MoEfication line of work (Zhang et al., 2022b), which demonstrated that trained Transformer FFNs can be converted to SMoE layers for efficient inference, by bringing sparse activation into the pre-training process itself. It departs from prior SMoE training methods (Switch Transformers, MoE-Dropout) that commit to a fixed sparse or gradually-densifying schedule, instead proposing an adaptive switching mechanism between sparse and dense training driven by activation pattern stability. The empirical observation that activation sparsity stabilizes early (~20K steps) while activation patterns evolve more slowly (~50K steps) is a new finding that motivates the design, extending earlier observations of sparsity in trained models (Mirzadeh et al., 2023) to the dynamic pre-training setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SSD offers a practical path to reducing pre-training costs (up to 1.44× FLOPs speedup) while producing models that are natively suited for sparse inference without additional training, addressing a gap where prior SMoE-trained models underperformed dense counterparts at equivalent parameter counts. Key limitations include the main experiments being conducted on ~100M-parameter models with scalability validated only on a single 8B model, the notable gap between theoretical FLOPs speedup and actual wall-clock speedup (1.10×–1.23×), and the method's current restriction to ReLU-based FFNs with open questions about applicability to GLU/SwiGLU architectures used in modern LLMs. The dynamic top-k expert selection result (truncating 75% of experts per token) opens an interesting but underexplored direction for token-adaptive inference.
