## Problem

In standard LoRA fine-tuning, both adapter matrices A and B are trained jointly, yet empirical observations suggest that A can be replaced by a random matrix with little performance loss while B is critical. This asymmetry had been noted in passing by prior work but lacked a formal theoretical explanation for why it occurs, whether it generalizes across architectures and tasks, and whether it could be exploited to improve parameter efficiency and generalization.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained foundation models (RoBERTa-large, BART-large, LLaMA-2-7B, ViT) and fine-tunes them on downstream tasks spanning text classification (GLUE), text generation (XSum, CNN/DailyMail), multi-task language understanding (MMLU), and image classification (DomainBed). The key structural detail is that the low-rank update ΔW = BA is decomposed asymmetrically: A ∈ R^{r×d_in} is drawn as a random orthogonal matrix and frozen, while B ∈ R^{d_out×r} is the only trainable component, reducing trainable parameters by roughly half compared to standard LoRA at the same rank.

## Method

The paper establishes that A's role is to extract r features from the input while B's role is to project those features toward the desired output, making B inherently more important for fitting. Theoretically, the authors prove via multivariate least-squares analysis and gradient analysis for general losses that freezing A to a random orthogonal matrix and training only B yields lower prediction loss than the reverse, with the gap growing as d/r → ∞. They further derive information-theoretic generalization bounds showing that training only B (fewer parameters) yields a √2 tighter bound when d_in = d_out, enabling the rank to be doubled at no additional parameter or generalization cost. Empirically, the method—initializing A as random orthogonal, freezing it, and training only B—matches or exceeds standard LoRA across RoBERTa, BART-Large, LLaMA-2, and ViT, while also improving out-of-domain generalization on DomainBed and reducing training time by eliminating gradient computation for A.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark with RoBERTa-large, freezing A as a random orthogonal matrix and training only B (B̂₀A_rand) achieves 87.4% average accuracy at 0.3% trainable parameters, matching full LoRA(r=8) at 87.2% with half the parameters. Doubling the rank to r=16 (0.8% params) yields 87.5%, slightly exceeding LoRA(r=8). On DomainBed with ViT, B̂A_rand(r=8) achieves 75.4% average OOD accuracy, substantially outperforming LoRA(r=8) at 68.8% and even full fine-tuning at 67.7%. On MMLU with LLaMA-2-7B, B̂₀A_rand(r=64) reaches 46.46% average accuracy, surpassing LoRA(r=32) at 44.76% with the same parameter budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard LoRA(r=8), the proposed B̂₀A_rand(r=16) matches or slightly exceeds performance (87.5% vs 87.2% avg on GLUE) at the same 0.8% parameter budget, while B̂₀A_rand(r=8) achieves 87.4% at only 0.3% parameters—half the cost. Compared to AdaLoRA (2.5% params, 87.9%), B̂₀A_rand(r=16) is within 0.4 points while using 3× fewer parameters. The method decisively outperforms (IA)³ (82.5%) and LoRA-FA (87.3%) on GLUE. On DomainBed OOD generalization, B̂A_rand(r=8) achieves 75.4% average OOD accuracy, beating LoRA(r=8) by 6.6 points, full fine-tuning by 7.7 points, and linear probing by 8.1 points. ## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) and its many parameter-efficient variants (VeRA, SVDiff, NOLA, BOFT, AdaLoRA), which have all implicitly or explicitly treated the A and B matrices asymmetrically in initialization or freezing schemes. Prior empirical work (Zhang et al., 2023a; Kopiczko et al., 2024) observed that freezing A preserves performance, but no prior work provided a formal theoretical account of why this asymmetry arises. The paper departs from Zeng & Lee (2023), who analyzed LoRA's expressive power only for linearized networks, by extending the analysis to nonlinear losses, multilayer architectures, and information-theoretic generalization bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway—that freezing A to a random orthogonal matrix and doubling the rank of B yields comparable or better performance at half the trainable parameters—offers an immediately adoptable efficiency gain for LoRA practitioners. The information-theoretic generalization bound (Lemma 4.5) provides a principled justification for why this should also improve out-of-distribution generalization, corroborated by DomainBed experiments. Open questions include whether the theoretical guarantees (derived for linear and single-layer settings) fully capture the behavior in deep transformer architectures, and whether the random-orthogonal-A assumption degrades in very low-rank or highly structured input regimes.
