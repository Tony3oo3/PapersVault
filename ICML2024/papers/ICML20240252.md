## Problem

Existing information-theoretic privacy (IT Privacy) data transformation methods fail to simultaneously satisfy all five desirable properties (SUIFT): suppressing sensitive attributes, preserving annotated useful attributes, maintaining sample-space invariance, managing unannotated generic features, and providing a rigorous theoretical basis. In particular, methods that handle unannotated attributes (e.g., GAP, MSDA) rely on heuristic ℓ₂ reconstruction losses without theoretical justification, while methods with information-theoretic guarantees (e.g., ALR, PPDAR) ignore unannotated features entirely.

## Input / Output

MaSS takes a raw data sample X (80×80 grayscale facial images, HuBERT-B feature embeddings of voice clips, 128-length normalized accelerometer/gyroscope clips, or normalized tabular rows) together with categorical labels for M sensitive attributes S and N annotated useful attributes U, and produces a transformed sample X′ in the same sample space as X. The framework only requires access to the observable joint distribution P(X,U,S); the unannotated generic features F need not be explicitly labeled or annotated. Because X′ remains in the original data space, it can be directly fed to pretrained off-the-shelf models (e.g., PIPNet for landmark detection) without retraining.

## Method

MaSS formulates IT Privacy as a constrained optimization problem—maximize I(X′;F) subject to I(X′;S_i)≤m_i and I(X′;U_j)≥n_j—and converts it into a fully differentiable training objective with three coupled modules. The data transformation module outputs X′=g_θ(X,a) with Gaussian noise a; sensitive suppression uses an adversarial cross-entropy loss relaxed via a penalty method; and annotated useful preservation uses a symmetric collaborative cross-entropy loss. The key architectural choice for unannotated feature preservation is an InfoNCE contrastive loss (rather than ℓ₂ reconstruction) that approximates I(X′;F) without assuming any distribution family for F or X′, parameterized by a shared network η that simultaneously serves as the feature extractor and the F extractor. The paper further provides rigorous operational bounds (Theorem 3.1) characterizing when the optimization is solvable and upper-bounding I(X′;F), and pre-trains all attribute inference networks on original data to accelerate convergence while deliberately keeping the useful-attribute inference model unfrozen to avoid unbounded KL estimation error.

## Results

On AudioMNIST (suppressing gender, accent, age; preserving digit as annotated and ID as unannotated), MaSS achieves NAG = 0.0000 on all three suppressed attributes while attaining the highest NAG on the unannotated preserved attribute ID (0.8514) and matching the best digit preservation (0.9983). On Adience (suppressing gender; preserving age and ID as unannotated), MaSS achieves NAG = 0.0000 for gender while reaching 0.7418 for age and 0.7662 for ID, outperforming all baselines that also achieve NAG = 0 on gender. On Motion Sense (suppressing gender and ID; preserving activity as unannotated), MaSS achieves NAG = 0.0000 for gender, 0.0026 for ID, and the highest activity NAG of 0.8977 among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaSS outperforms all five main baselines on the unannotated attribute preservation metric (NAG on ID): 0.8514 vs. MSDA (0.8428), GAP (0.7036), ALR (0.7032), PPDAR (0.7027), and BDQ (0.4038). On suppressed attributes, MaSS achieves NAG = 0.0000 across gender, accent, and age, matching GAP and PPDAR while clearly beating MSDA (0.0015/0.0013/0.0323), BDQ (0.0007/0.0013), and ALR (0.0056 on age). On the annotated preserved attribute (digit), MaSS ties PPDAR at 0.9983 and slightly trails ALR (0.9994) and MSDA (0.9981), but substantially outperforms GAP (0.9579). In the SPAct comparison (Table 12, annotated-only setting), MaSS achieves up to 5% lower NAG on sensitive attributes (e.g., ID: 0.0017 vs. 0.0560) at the cost of a marginal 0.17% drop in digit NAG (0.9953 vs. 0.9970). On Adience, among methods achieving NAG = 0 for gender, MaSS reaches 0.7418/0.7662 for age/ID, surpassing GAP (0.4907/0.5616) while MSDA fails to suppress gender (NAG = 0.3114).

## Relation to Prior Work

MaSS builds directly on the adversarial information-obfuscation framework of Bertran et al. (ALR, 2019), reusing its parameterization of the data transformation and penalty-method relaxation of mutual information constraints, and on the InfoNCE contrastive loss from Oord et al. (2018) with a SimCLR-style score function for estimating mutual information with unannotated features. It departs from prior IT-privacy methods (GAP, MSDA, PPDAR, BDQ) by replacing heuristic ℓ₂ reconstruction losses for unannotated attributes with a distribution-agnostic contrastive objective, and by providing rigorous operational bounds (Theorem 3.1) that no prior method in the SUIFT comparison table offers. The decision to keep the useful-attribute inference network unfrozen during training, justified by a KL-divergence error analysis, is a deliberate departure from ALR's frozen-network strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MaSS is the first framework to simultaneously satisfy all five SUIFT properties, which matters for privacy-critical deployments where unannotated features may later prove sensitive or useful and where formal safety bounds are required. The InfoNCE-based unannotated-attribute preservation is more general than ℓ₂ reconstruction (no Gaussian/factorization assumptions), making the framework applicable across modalities without architectural changes. Open questions include whether the mutual-information bounds translate into formal differential-privacy guarantees, how the method scales to very high-dimensional or streaming data, and whether the categorical-attribute assumption on S and U can be relaxed to continuous sensitive variables.
