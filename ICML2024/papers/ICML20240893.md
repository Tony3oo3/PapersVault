## Problem

Existing multi-concept learning methods for text-to-image diffusion models (Custom Diffusion, Cones, Break-A-Scene) require image crops or segmentation masks to learn multiple object-level concepts, making them unsuitable for scenarios where annotations are absent or concepts are unknown (e.g., medical logs, historical data mining). This paper asks whether multiple semantically disentangled object-level concepts can be learned simultaneously from a single sentence-image pair using only natural language descriptions, without any image annotations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single unmasked RGB image (256×256) and a natural language sentence containing one adjective and one noun per target concept (2–5 concepts), where the nouns are replaced with learnable pseudo-words. It outputs a set of learnable text embeddings (1–4 tokens per concept, total storage <0.1 MB), cross-attention-derived masks for each learned prompt, and supports mask-free local image editing by swapping learned prompts. Unlike competing methods, no crops, masks, or segmentation model outputs are required at training or inference time.

## Method

MCPL extends Textual Inversion to jointly optimize multiple learnable prompt embeddings within a single sentence while keeping the LDM denoiser and CLIP text encoder frozen, guided by the standard diffusion reconstruction loss. To prevent the multi-prompt optimization from collapsing into trivial image-level solutions, three regularization terms are introduced: (1) Attention Masking (AttnMask) computes a binary mask from the average cross-attention of each learnable prompt and applies it via Hadamard product to the diffusion loss, constraining learning to semantically relevant regions; (2) Prompts Contrastive Loss (PromptCL) applies an InfoNCE loss over augmented image views to push embeddings of different concepts apart in the latent space; (3) Bind Adjective associates each learnable noun with a descriptive adjective word, expanding the positive group in the contrastive loss to exploit the pretrained model's innate understanding of color/texture terms. The final objective is L = L_DM^AttnMask + γ·L_PromptCL^adj, and only the small set of text embeddings is stored, making the approach complementary to parameter-updating methods like Break-A-Scene.

## Results

On the user study (41 participants, 30 quizzes), the fully regularised MCPL-one+AttnMask+PromptCL+Bind adj. achieves 40.51% Text Alignment, 56.15% Prompt Following, 54.36% Semantic Correspondence on natural images, and 65.64% Semantic Correspondence on medical images, substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: MCPL-one+PromptCL at 15.38%/21.28%/26.67%/13.33% respectively). In embedding similarity evaluations (Figures 6 and 9), MCPL-one+CL+Mask achieves competitive DINOv1/CLIP/BERT scores versus the mask-based SoTA Break-A-Scene on natural images, though BAS leads on OOD medical DINOv1. Storage cost is <0.1 MB per concept versus 4.9 GB for BAS and 72 MB for Custom Diffusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Textual Inversion (TI-m, masked per-object), MCPL-one with all regularisations consistently outperforms on t-SNE disentanglement, embedding similarity in BERT/CLIP/DINOv1/DINOv2 spaces, and all user-study metrics (e.g., 56.15% vs. 3.08% Prompt Following). Against Break-A-Scene (BAS), the mask-based SoTA, MCPL achieves competitive or superior object-level DINOv1 similarity on natural images (Figure 9) but is outperformed on OOD medical DINOv1 due to less accurate attention-derived masks versus BAS's human-in-the-loop segmentation; however, MCPL requires <0.1 MB storage versus BAS's 4.9 GB and no external segmentation model. Against Custom Diffusion, DreamBooth, and ELITE (all compared qualitatively in Figure 41 for complex scene composition), MCPL delivers commendable results using only text tokens without updating diffusion model parameters, though it is not optimised for composition quality. Against Inspiration Tree (IT), MCPL excels at instruction-following discovery of visually and linguistically distinct concepts, while IT specialises in abstract, subtle concepts without human guidance; the authors position them as complementary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Textual Inversion (Gal et al., 2022), which learns a single text embedding for one concept, to simultaneously learn multiple object-level embeddings from a single image-sentence pair. It departs from prior multi-concept methods—Custom Diffusion and Cones (which require object crops) and Break-A-Scene (which requires segmentation masks)—by eliminating all image annotations and updating only textual tokens rather than diffusion model weights. The three proposed regularisation terms (Attention Masking, Prompts Contrastive Loss, and Bind Adjective) are the key methodological contribution, addressing the non-trivial optimisation challenge of mapping multiple prompts to distinct regions under a single image-level reconstruction objective. The work is also positioned as complementary to Inspiration Tree, which discovers abstract concepts without language guidance, whereas MCPL targets instruction-following discovery of visually and linguistically distinct concepts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The annotation-free, token-only formulation (<0.1 MB storage per concept vs. 4.9 GB for Break-A-Scene) opens a practical path for mining new visual concepts from historical or clinical data where segmentation masks are unavailable or prohibitively expensive to produce. The demonstrated capability for mask-free local editing supports hypothesis generation in scientific and biomedical workflows. However, the method's reliance on linguistically distinguishable adjectives, its degradation with more than 3–5 concepts per image, and its use of a relatively weak 256×256 LDM backbone limit its generality. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more powerful diffusion backbones, whether the adjective-binding requirement can be relaxed, and how to handle scenes where concepts are visually or linguistically near-identical.
