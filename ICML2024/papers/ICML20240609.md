## Problem

Deep learning architectures are specified either top-down via symmetry constraints (geometric deep learning) or bottom-up via tensor operations (automatic differentiation frameworks), but no existing theory coherently bridges both. GDL is fundamentally limited to invertible group actions and individual linear layers, while tensor programming cannot express abstract structural constraints. The paper argues that a unified algebraic framework is needed to simultaneously specify what constraints a model must satisfy and how it is implemented.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240609/cropped_page7_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes as input a symmetry group, an inductive/coinductive data structure (lists, trees, Mealy machines), or a parametric map (a pair (P, f) where P is a parameter space and f: P × A → B is the function), and produces a categorical algebraic specification: generalized equivariance constraints as (co)algebra homomorphism equations, formal criteria for when weight tying is correct (via 2-morphisms in Para), and a universal property characterizing the full architecture (not just individual layers). Unlike GDL which outputs a single linear layer, the output here is a complete parametric morphism in the 2-category Para, where parameters are first-class citizens drawn on a separate axis and reparameterizations are 2-morphisms.

## Method

The paper constructs a unified theory using monads and their algebras valued in the 2-category Para of parametric maps. At the 1-categorical level, group action monads (G × −) on Set recover GDL equivariance as monad algebra homomorphisms, while endofunctor (co)algebras generalize to non-invertible structures: lists yield fold equations (generalizing RNN recurrence), trees yield recursive NN constraints, and Mealy machine coalgebras yield unfold equations for stateful automata. The key advance is lifting to 2-categories: by working in Para, where 1-morphisms are parametric functions (P, f) and 2-morphisms are reparameterizations, the framework models nonlinear cells and weight tying (via the copy map Δ_P) as first-class 2-categorical data. Lax algebras for algebraically free parametric monads—constructed by iterating an endofunctor to a colimit—then derive recurrent, recursive, and similar neural networks from first principles, with the lax cells carrying comonoid structure that formally encodes when weight sharing is valid.

## Results

This is a theoretical position paper with no empirical experiments or quantitative benchmarks. The key theoretical contributions are: (1) showing that group-equivariant maps (the core of geometric deep learning) are precisely monad algebra homomorphisms for group action monads; (2) demonstrating that endofunctor (co)algebra homomorphisms recover folds over lists (RNNs), folds over trees (recursive networks), and unfolds over Mealy machines (full RNNs) as generalised equivariance constraints; and (3) establishing that the 2-category Para with lax algebras for free parametric monads provides a first-principles derivation of recurrent, recursive, and similar neural network architectures, including formal criteria for weight-tying correctness via comonoid structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper performs no empirical comparisons against baselines, as it is a theoretical position paper. Instead, it positions its framework relative to two existing paradigms: (1) Geometric Deep Learning (GDL), which the paper shows is a strict special case of its monad algebra homomorphism framework — GDL handles only invertible (group) symmetries and linear layers, while the categorical framework extends to non-invertible operations (monoids, endofunctors), non-linear parametric maps, and multi-layer architectural blocks; (2) bottom-up tensor programming frameworks (JAX, PyTorch), which the paper argues lack a principled way to specify and verify constraint satisfaction from syntax alone. The paper claims its framework subsumes both, recovering GDL's equivariance constraints as a special case while also encoding RNNs, recursive networks, weight tying, and automata-theoretic constructs (Mealy machines, streams, Moore machines) within a single algebraic language. No head-to-head quantitative comparison is provided or expected for a position paper of this type.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a unifying bridge between two established but disconnected lines of work: geometric deep learning (Bronstein et al., 2021), which specifies architectures via symmetry/equivariance constraints, and functional-programming-based tensor frameworks (JAX, PyTorch), which specify architectures via implementation. It recovers GDL equivariance as a special case of monad algebra homomorphisms (group action monads), then generalises to arbitrary endofunctor (co)algebras to capture inductive data structures (lists, trees) and automata (Mealy machines), and finally lifts everything into the 2-category Para (Cruttwell et al., 2022) to handle parametric morphisms and weight tying. The paper explicitly departs from prior categorical ML work (Gavranović, 2020; Sonoda et al., 2023) by claiming a single cohesive framework rather than fragmented applications, and from de Haan et al. (2020a) by going beyond functorial equivariance to full 2-categorical algebra with lax structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If the framework matures, it would provide the first single mathematical language in which both the 'why' (constraint satisfaction, invariance, equivariance) and the 'how' (sequential composition, parameter sharing, non-linear cells) of a neural architecture are expressed, potentially enabling verifiable correctness of weight tying and principled derivation of new architectures from first principles. However, as a position paper it offers no empirical validation, no implementation, and no demonstration that the 2-categorical machinery is tractable for practitioners beyond toy examples (two-pixel grids, single RNN cells). Key open questions include whether the lax algebra formalism scales to multi-layer non-linear architectures, whether the framework yields practical design guidance beyond re-deriving known architectures, and whether the categorical overhead is justified relative to simpler constraint-based or programmatic approaches.
