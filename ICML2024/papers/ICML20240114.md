## Problem

Formal verification of DNNs relies on over-approximation that accumulates and amplifies along the forward pass, causing verification tools to return inconclusive results for most samples. Existing pruning methods (e.g., magnitude-based pruning, sparse optimization pruning) introduce sparsity without guaranteeing that robustness or accuracy requirements are met. The paper asks whether one can generate networks that are inherently more amenable to over-approximation-based verification while provably preserving prediction performance.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a fully trained DNN (FNN or CNN) and a held-out validation set as input, and produces a VNN with the same architecture but sparser weights and biases. The optimization is performed layer-by-layer, fixing previously optimized layers and only re-parameterizing the current layer's weights and biases. The output VNN is guaranteed to classify every validation sample identically to the original DNN, with an optional robustness margin M on the output logits.

## Method

The key insight is that reducing the number of non-zero weights per layer directly reduces the over-approximation error that verification tools accumulate along the forward pass. The authors formulate a layer-wise optimization that minimizes the L1 norm (convex relaxation of L0) of the target layer's weights and biases, subject to constraints that (i) each ReLU neuron remains in the same linear segment as in the original network, (ii) downstream neuron values are recomputed consistently, (iii) the target layer output stays within an ε-neighborhood of the original, and (iv) the argmax classification is preserved for all validation samples. By fixing the ReLU activation states, all constraints become linear, yielding a solvable linear program (solved with Gurobi) per layer. The end-to-end process iterates from the first hidden layer to the last, progressively sparsifying the network while maintaining classification correctness on the validation set.

## Results

On MNIST, VNNs achieve verified robustness of 75.5% at δ=0.03 (ERAN) versus only 22.5% for the original 2×50 FNN, and up to 76× more samples are verified overall. On the safety-critical CHB-MIT dataset, VNNs verify up to 24× more samples (SafeDeep) while maintaining 82.5%±4.2% accuracy (vs. 85.7%±3.8% for original CNNs). On MIT-BIH, VNNs verify up to 34× more samples (ERAN) with 92.0%±3.0% accuracy, slightly exceeding the original DNNs' 91.5%±3.1%. Average verification time is reduced by up to 3× (e.g., 173 s vs. 335 s for the 8×200 FNN at δ=0.07 with SafeDeep).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Magnitude-Based Pruning (MBP, Guidotti et al., ECAI 2020), VNNs are up to 46× more verification-friendly on MNIST, 19× on CHB-MIT, and 27× on MIT-BIH, while maintaining comparable accuracy (e.g., 82.5% vs. 84.1% on CHB-MIT). Against Sparse Optimization Pruning (SOP, Manngård et al., Neurocomputing 2018), VNNs are up to 51× more verification-friendly on MNIST, with both methods achieving similar clean accuracy. Against the publicly available pruned FNNs from Ugare et al. (OOPSLA 2022, 7×200 architecture, 10–90% pruning rates), VNNs substantially outperform: despite the pruned models retaining high accuracy at δ=0, they do not demonstrate higher verification-friendliness than the original unpruned network, whereas VNNs consistently verify more samples across all perturbation levels. In all three comparisons, VNNs win decisively on verified robustness while matching or slightly exceeding baseline accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant research direction of improving verification tools (abstract interpretation, SMT solvers, incremental refinement) by instead modifying the network itself to be more amenable to existing over-approximation-based verifiers. It repurposes the sparsification idea from pruning literature (MBP, SOP) but fundamentally redefines the objective: rather than minimizing model size or preserving accuracy alone, it formulates a constrained linear program that minimizes L1-norm sparsity while hard-constraining classification correctness and optionally a robustness margin. The piecewise-linear ReLU handling and L0→L1 relaxation connect to convex optimization and abstract interpretation traditions, but the specific formulation—layer-wise LP with per-neuron activation-state preservation and downstream propagation constraints—is novel in the verification context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for safety-critical deployments (epileptic seizure detection, cardiac arrhythmia monitoring) where formal robustness guarantees are needed but current verifiers are intractable on dense networks. The finding that constrained sparsity simultaneously improves verification-friendliness and acts as a regularizer (making VNNs more robust than their dense counterparts) is a useful dual benefit. However, the evaluation is limited to small FNNs (up to 8×200) and compact CNNs on single-patient medical data, leaving open whether the approach scales to deeper/wider architectures, non-ReLU activations, or higher-dimensional inputs like ImageNet. The dependence on validation set size for maintaining accuracy (especially with ε > 0) is a practical constraint that may limit applicability to data-scarce safety-critical settings.
