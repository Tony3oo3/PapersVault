## Problem

Relative overgeneralization (RO) causes cooperative multi-agent learners to converge to suboptimal joint policies when agents overfit to the currently suboptimal behaviors of their teammates. While optimistic Q-learning variants (hysteretic Q-learning, lenient agents) address RO in tabular settings, no method has been proposed to incorporate optimism into state-of-the-art multi-agent policy gradient (MAPG) methods, and optimistic DQN approaches suffer from Q-value overestimation with function approximation.

## Input / Output

Each agent receives the full environment state in a fully observable Dec-MDP and outputs its own action from a continuous or discrete action space, with all agents sharing a common reward signal. The method is instantiated on MAPPO with a centralized state-value critic, and the only modification to the standard pipeline is a single-line clipping of negative advantage estimates to zero before the PPO policy update. The framework extends to a Leaky ReLU parameterization LR(A) = max(ηA, A) where η ∈ [0,1] controls the degree of optimism, with η=0 giving full optimism and η=1 recovering vanilla MAPPO.

## Method

OptiMAPPO clips negative advantage values to zero in the MAPPO objective, so that the policy gradient only increases the probability of actions with non-negative estimated advantage and never decreases any action's probability. The key insight is that in cooperative tasks, a negative advantage often reflects the current suboptimality of teammates rather than the action itself, so suppressing negative updates prevents premature convergence to local optima. The authors formalize this via the operator view of policy gradient, showing that the clipped advantage defines a new improvement operator I_V^clip whose composition with the projection operator P_V retains the optimal policy as a fixed point (Proposition 4.1). The approach is a drop-in modification to any advantage-based MAPG method and avoids the overestimation pathology that plagues optimistic Q-learning with function approximation because advantage estimation remains on-policy.

## Results

On the Climbing matrix game, OptiMAPPO achieves a return of 275 (global optimum) versus 175 for MAPPO/HAPPO and 150 for HATRPO. On Penalty games with k ≤ −25, OptiMAPPO reaches 250 while all baselines stall at 50. On MA-MuJoCo, OptiMAPPO dramatically outperforms FACMAC (e.g., Ant 2×4: 6103.97 vs. 307.58; HalfCheetah 6×1: 6982.65 vs. 3482.91). Across 19 tasks total, OptiMAPPO outperforms strong baselines on 13 and matches on the remaining 6.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OptiMAPPO decisively beats MAPPO, HAPPO, and HATRPO on all Penalty games with k < 0 (250 vs. 50) and on Climbing (275 vs. 175/150), while tying at 250 on Penalty k=0 where RO is absent. Against the optimistic Q-learning baseline FACMAC on MA-MuJoCo, OptiMAPPO achieves roughly 2–20× higher returns (e.g., Ant 2×4: 6104 vs. 308; Walker 2×3: 4571 vs. 713), demonstrating that advantage clipping in policy gradient is far more effective than optimistic Q-value updates. On Overcooked, OptiMAPPO outperforms or matches MAPPO, HAPPO, and HATRPO on all 6 tasks, and also surpasses Hysteretic DQN, which suffers from Q-value overestimation. General exploration methods MAVEN and NA-MAPPO fail to solve RO on matrix games (both stuck at 175 on Climbing, 50 on Penalty k<0), while CMAE ties OptiMAPPO on Penalized Push-Box (1.6) but relies on tabular Q and does not scale. In Appendix D, all popular deep MARL methods (IQL, IA2C, MADDPG, COMA, MAA2C, VDN, QMIX, FACMAC) fail on the matrix games, confirming that RO is a pervasive unsolved issue in existing approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected lines of work: classic optimistic Q-learning methods (hysteretic Q-learning, lenient agents) that solved relative overgeneralization in tabular matrix games, and modern multi-agent policy gradient methods (MAPPO, HAPPO, HATRPO) that achieve state-of-the-art performance but still converge to suboptimal joint policies. The key conceptual departure is transferring the optimism principle from value-space (modifying Q-value update rates) to policy-space (clipping negative advantages to zero), thereby avoiding the overestimation pathology that plagued optimistic DQN variants. The theoretical grounding draws on the operator view of policy gradient (Ghosh et al., 2020), showing the clipped-advantage update defines a valid improvement operator that preserves optimality at fixed points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result is practically significant because it offers a one-line modification to any advantage-based MAPG method that resolves a long-standing coordination failure mode, with demonstrated gains across 19 tasks spanning continuous control and discrete cooperative games. However, the approach inherits the on-policy constraint of PPO, and the paper acknowledges that optimism can be harmful under misleading stochastic rewards—a limitation only partially addressed by the Leaky ReLU extension (η parameter) left as future work. Open questions include whether adaptive optimism scheduling (à la lenient agents) can be integrated, and whether the advantage-clipping principle transfers to off-policy or model-based multi-agent settings.
