## Problem

Despite multiple contrastive learning (CL) loss variants (InfoNCE, SimCLR, DCL) achieving strong representation learning, their inner workings and optimal solutions remain largely opaque, with theoretical analyses relying on unrealistic assumptions like infinite batch sizes. A critical gap is that the non-asymptotic expected loss—the objective actually optimized in practice—has unknown minima for all InfoNCE variants, making it unclear why these losses exhibit different practical performance despite sharing the same asymptotic optima.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabelled RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for STL-10, 224×224 for ImageNet-100) as augmented positive pairs, with all other images in the mini-batch serving as negatives. It produces unit-norm representation vectors (128-dim) from ResNet-18 or ResNet-50 encoders, evaluated via a linear probe trained for 200 epochs. The key unusual aspect is that the loss operates on the geometry of representations on the unit sphere rather than raw feature distances, and the kernel variants replace the exponential similarity with Gaussian or logarithmic kernels parameterized by a bandwidth and a weighting coefficient γ.

## Method

The paper proves that InfoNCE, SimCLR, DCL, and the proposed DHEL all share the same unique mini-batch optimum (perfect alignment + regular simplex) when batch size M ≤ d+1, and the same asymptotic optimum (uniform distribution on the sphere), unifying them under hyperspherical energy minimization. DHEL decouples the uniformity term from positive samples by replacing the InfoNCE denominator with a sum over only negative representations, making alignment and uniformity independently optimizable. For kernel contrastive learning (KCL), the paper shows the expected loss is independent of batch size—unlike all InfoNCE variants—allowing non-asymptotic identification of the uniform distribution as the unique minimiser under mild kernel conditions (decreasing, convex, completely monotone). Empirically, DHEL and KCL (Gaussian and Logarithmic kernels) outperform SimCLR and DCL across batch sizes 32–256, show smaller hyperparameter sensitivity, and reduce dimensionality collapse.

## Results

The paper evaluates DHEL and two KCL variants (Gaussian and Logarithmic kernels) against SimCLR and DCL on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10, and ImageNet-100 using batch sizes of 32, 64, 128, and 256 with ResNet-18/50 encoders and linear-probe evaluation. Results are presented as error diagrams (median with 25th/75th quantiles across 11 temperatures or kernel hyperparameters) rather than single-point accuracy tables. DHEL and KCL achieve the highest median downstream accuracy across all four datasets and all batch sizes, with the advantage being most pronounced at small batch sizes (32–64). On CIFAR-100, DHEL utilises more than double the representation dimensions compared to SimCLR and DCL, directly addressing dimensionality collapse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SimCLR (Chen et al., 2020), DHEL significantly outperforms across all datasets and batch sizes, with SimCLR's upper 75th-percentile accuracy remaining below DHEL's median. Kernel methods (Gaussian and Logarithmic KCL) further outperform SimCLR and also exceed DHEL in several settings, while exhibiting the tightest hyperparameter spread, indicating superior robustness to temperature and kernel-weight choices. Against DCL (Yeh et al., 2022), DHEL's median accuracy is consistently higher, and on CIFAR-10 and STL-10 DHEL's upper quantile also surpasses DCL's; kernel methods again lead both. The key practical distinction is that KCL's expected loss is independent of batch size (zero gradient bias), whereas SimCLR and DCL suffer from both gradient variance and bias that change with batch size, explaining why KCL maintains stable performance across the 32–256 range while InfoNCE-based methods degrade more sharply at smaller batches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the mini-batch optimum analysis of Sreenivasan et al. (2023), which was specific to InfoNCE, to all InfoNCE variants (SimCLR, DCL) and a new decoupled variant (DHEL), showing they share the same regular-simplex optimum under M ≤ d+1. It extends the kernel contrastive learning framework of Li et al. (2021) by proving batch-size independence of the expected KCL loss and identifying its non-asymptotic minima, connecting both families to hyperspherical energy minimization. The conceptual unification—showing that InfoNCE variants, DHEL, and KCL all converge to the same optima in both the mini-batch and asymptotic regimes—provides a common theoretical lens that prior work had only partially established for individual loss families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that in the non-asymptotic regime (what is actually optimized during training), only kernel-based methods have known minima, meaning InfoNCE variants may be optimizing for subtly different objectives depending on batch size—a gap the paper identifies but does not fully resolve. DHEL's decoupling of alignment from uniformity and KCL's batch-size independence offer concrete design principles for more robust contrastive losses, with empirical gains at small batch sizes (32–256) where SimCLR typically degrades. However, validation is limited to CIFAR-10/100, STL-10, and ImageNet-100 with modest architectures (ResNet-18/50); the paper explicitly acknowledges that testing on full ImageNet with memory banks, momentum contrast, and larger batch sizes remains open, as does understanding why DHEL empirically favors uniformity over alignment.
