## Problem

Conventional diffusion models rely on a fixed, pre-specified linear Gaussian forward noising process that cannot adapt to the specific data distribution, limiting the flexibility of the latent space and creating a gap between the true log-likelihood and the variational bound. This paper asks whether allowing the forward process to learn a time-dependent, non-linear transformation of the data can close this gap while preserving the simulation-free training paradigm that makes diffusion models efficient.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240124/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes a standard Gaussian noise sample z_T ~ N(0,I) and iteratively applies the learned reverse process (parameterized by a U-Net) to produce an image; for density estimation, it takes an image x and integrates the corresponding ODE to compute a log-likelihood estimate. The transformation F_φ(x,t) is applied to the data at every timestep before noise injection, and the model is constrained so that F_φ(x,0) = x (identity at t=0). Experiments use 28×28 grayscale (MNIST), 32×32 and 64×64 RGB (CIFAR-10, ImageNet), and 256×256 RGB (CelebA-HQ) images, with uniform dequantization for discrete pixel values.

## Method

NDMs generalize diffusion models by inserting a learnable, time-dependent transformation F_φ(x,t) between the data and the Gaussian noise injection at each forward step, yielding marginal distributions q_φ(z_t|x) = N(z_t; α_t F_φ(x,t), σ_t² I). The forward process is constructed implicitly (non-Markovian, depending on x) via a posterior q_φ(z_s|z_t,x) that satisfies the marginals, and the reverse process is parameterized by predicting x (or equivalently the injected noise), making the KL divergence in the variational objective computable in closed form. Training is simulation-free: a single timestep t is sampled, one KL term is evaluated, and gradients update both the reverse-process parameters θ and the transformation parameters φ jointly. In continuous time, the reverse process becomes an SDE (or ODE in the deterministic limit) whose drift includes the time derivative of F_φ, computed efficiently via Jacobian-Vector Products. The key distinction from DDIM is that the model predicts the transformed point F_φ(x,t) rather than x itself, and the prior and reconstruction terms become parameter-dependent when F_φ is learnable, requiring the full ELBO rather than a simplified noise-prediction loss.

## Results

On ImageNet 32×32, NDM achieves 3.55 BPD NLL, outperforming all baselines (VDM: 3.72, Score Flow: 3.76). On ImageNet 64×64, NDM reaches 3.35 BPD, beating Improved DDPM (3.54) and VDM (3.40). On CIFAR-10, NDM achieves 2.70 BPD, competitive with VDM (2.65) and superior to all other baselines. On CelebA-HQ-256 in latent space, NDM achieves NLL ≤0.65 and FID 7.18, improving over LSGM (≤0.70, 7.22).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NDM consistently outperforms DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) on NLL across all datasets: 2.70 vs 3.69 on CIFAR-10, and 3.55 vs 3.89 on ImageNet-32 (Table 3, 1000 steps). Against Improved DDPM (Nichol & Dhariwal, 2021), NDM wins on ImageNet-64 (3.35 vs 3.54) and CIFAR-10 (2.70 vs 2.94). NDM surpasses VDM (Kingma et al., 2021) on ImageNet-32 (3.55 vs 3.72) and ImageNet-64 (3.35 vs 3.40), though VDM retains a slight edge on CIFAR-10 (2.65 vs 2.70). NDM also beats Score SDE (Song et al., 2021c) on CIFAR-10 (2.70 vs 2.99) and Score Flow (Song et al., 2021b) on both CIFAR-10 (2.70 vs 2.83) and ImageNet-32 (3.55 vs 3.76). On CelebA-HQ-256, Latent NDM improves over LSGM (Vahdat et al., 2021) in both NLL (≤0.65 vs ≤0.70) and FID (7.18 vs 7.22). In the direct DDPM-vs-NDM comparison with matched architectures (Table 3), NDM achieves lower NLL and NELBO at every step count, with the gap widening at fewer steps (e.g., 4.63 vs 5.02 BPD at 10 steps on CIFAR-10), though FID is slightly worse for NDM at 1000 steps (11.82 vs 11.44).

## Relation to Prior Work

NDMs extend the DDIM framework (Song et al., 2021a) by replacing the fixed linear data scaling in the forward process with a learnable, time-dependent, non-linear transformation F_φ(x,t), while retaining the simulation-free variational objective structure from DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) and the continuous-time SDE/ODE formulation from Score SDE (Song et al., 2021c). The paper demonstrates that DDPM, DDIM, VDM, Soft Diffusion, LSGM, Flow Matching OT, and blurring diffusion all arise as special cases of NDMs for specific choices of F_φ, positioning the framework as a unifying generalization rather than a single new model. A key conceptual departure from Schrödinger Bridge approaches is that NDMs maintain direct access to q(z_t|x), preserving the simulation-free training paradigm that those methods sacrifice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NDMs close the gap between the true log-likelihood and the variational bound by allowing the forward process to adapt to the data, yielding consistent NLL improvements and SOTA results on ImageNet 32/64 and CelebA-HQ. However, practical adoption is tempered by a 2.3× training cost from doubled parameters, reduced robustness to step reduction (notably at T=10), and incompatibility with classifier guidance for conditional generation. Open questions include whether better parameterizations of the reverse process (beyond the ε-prediction used here) could mitigate the step-reduction fragility, how to enable conditional generation, and whether the framework scales to higher-resolution or video generation tasks.
