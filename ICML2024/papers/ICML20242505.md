## Problem

Existing neural TPPs assume a specific parametric form for the intensity function (e.g., exponential decay in RMTPP, linear interpolation in THP), which limits expressiveness when the assumption deviates from reality. NDE-based TPPs that model hidden-state dynamics via jump SDEs lack theoretical guarantees for global existence and uniqueness of solutions, and they neglect the diffusion term associated with Brownian-motion-driven noise. This paper asks whether the intensity process itself can be governed by a flexible SDE with neural-network-parameterized coefficients, without pre-specifying any functional form.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-type event sequences S = {(t_i, m_i)} over a time interval [0, T], where each event carries a timestamp and a type mark from M categories. It produces a continuous-time log-intensity process η_t ∈ ℝ^M (one component per event type) that is positive by construction (λ_t = exp(η_t)). For prediction, it outputs the next event time via numerical integration of the conditional density and the next event type via argmax over normalized per-type intensities; for likelihood evaluation, it reports per-event NLL computed by trapezoidal-rule integration of exp(η_s) over the observation window.

## Method

The core contribution is the Neural Jump-Diffusion SDE (NJDSDE): dη_t = f_{θ_f}(η_t)dt + g_{θ_g}(η_t)dW_t + h_{θ_h}(η_t)dN_t, where three MLPs (drift net, diffusion net, jump net) with Tanh activations parameterize the coefficient functions. Between events the process evolves as a neural SDE solved by the Euler-Maruyama scheme with fixed step size; at each event time t_i the log-intensity jumps by h_{θ_h}(η_{t_i}), with the sign encoding excitatory or inhibitory influence. Training minimizes the negative log-likelihood via MLE, with the integral term approximated by trapezoidal rule over the Euler-Maruyama grid. A key theoretical result (Theorem 5) shows that uniform Lipschitz continuity of f and g plus continuity of h guarantees a unique strong solution, and classical TPPs (Poisson, Hawkes, self-correcting) emerge as special cases with fixed coefficient functions. The multivariate extension uses an M×K diffusion matrix and an M×M jump matrix to capture cross-type interactions.

## Results

NJDTPP achieves the best per-event negative log-likelihood (NLL) on all six real-world datasets, with particularly large margins on Earthquake (1.305 vs. 2.189 for NHP) and Taobao (−0.440 vs. 0.986 for THP). For next-event type prediction, NJDTPP attains the highest accuracy and weighted F1 on every dataset, e.g., 0.908 Acc / 0.891 F1 on Taxi and 0.861 Acc / 0.823 F1 on MIMIC-II. On synthetic intensity-recovery tasks, NJDTPP achieves MAPE of 0.1–0.2%, substantially outperforming the best classical baselines (e.g., 0.2% vs. 3.5% for Hawkes, 0.1% vs. 1.6% for Self-Correcting).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NJDTPP outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset and every metric reported. Against the RNN-based models RMTPP and NHP, NJDTPP reduces NLL by 0.05–0.98 (e.g., Earthquake: 1.305 vs. 2.189 for NHP) and improves event-type F1 by 0.03–0.13. Against the attention/Transformer-based models SAHP, THP, and AttNHP, the NLL advantage is even larger on Earthquake (1.305 vs. 3.941/3.387/2.376) and Taobao (−0.440 vs. 1.640/1.191/1.206), while also leading in prediction accuracy and F1 across all six datasets. Against the NDE-based NSTPP (Chen et al., 2020), NJDTPP wins on NLL for all datasets (e.g., MIMIC-II: 1.398 vs. 2.421) and on both prediction metrics. On the synthetic intensity-recovery task (Table 1), NJDTPP achieves MAPE of 0.1–0.2%, beating the RNN and NJSDE baselines of Jia & Benson (2019) by 5–100× (e.g., Hawkes: 0.2% vs. 5.9% for NJSDE, 22.0% for RNN), confirming that modeling the intensity process directly via a neural jump-diffusion SDE is more effective than modeling a hidden state.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: classical TPPs (Poisson, Hawkes, self-correcting processes), neural TPPs that parameterize intensity via RNNs or Transformers (RMTPP, NHP, THP, AttNHP), and neural differential equations for event sequences (Jia & Benson 2019, Chen et al. 2020). The key conceptual departure is that prior neural TPPs assume a fixed functional form for intensity (exponential decay, softplus interpolation) or model hidden-state dynamics via neural jump SDEs, whereas NJDTPP directly models the intensity process itself as the solution to a neural jump-diffusion SDE, with drift, diffusion, and jump coefficients learned by separate networks. The paper also extends the jump-diffusion SDE framework of Wang et al. (2018), which used fixed linear coefficients, by replacing them with neural networks and adding a diffusion term driven by Brownian motion that prior neural jump SDE approaches for event sequences neglected.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified SDE perspective provides a principled way to eliminate the functional-form assumption that limits existing neural TPPs, while the theoretical guarantees on existence and uniqueness of the positive solution address a gap that prior neural jump SDE approaches for event sequences left open. The consistent SOTA improvements across six diverse real-world datasets (social media, seismology, transportation, e-commerce, Q&A, healthcare) suggest the free-form intensity modeling captures dynamics that parametric assumptions miss. Open questions include the computational cost of Euler-Maruyama discretization for very long sequences, whether the added flexibility risks overfitting on small datasets (MIMIC-II has only 527 training sequences), and how the framework extends to spatial or network-structured event data.
