## Problem

Existing time-series representation learning methods (contrastive learning, mask-and-reconstruction) fail to capture temporal dynamics due to misalignment between high-level representation objectives and low-level forecasting tasks, or temporal disruption from random masking. While LLMs show promise for time series, prior adaptations rely on frozen models, prompt engineering, or limited fine-tuning with sequence-level decoders that cause overfitting and prevent proper patch-level representation learning. No prior work effectively syncs LLM causal pre-training with time-series forecasting while using an appropriate patch-level decoding mechanism.

## Input / Output

The method takes a univariate historical time series window of length L (channel independence is assumed, so multivariate series are processed as separate univariate sequences) and a prediction horizon H. The window is divided into patches of length P with sliding stride S, and the prediction horizon is similarly patchified. A key design property is that the patch-wise decoder is independent of input/output length: once Stage 2 is trained for one horizon (e.g., H=720), the model can forecast at any other horizon without retraining, since each patch is decoded independently through a shared linear layer W_p ∈ R^{D×P}.

## Method

aLLM4TS adapts GPT-2 (first 6 layers) for time series via a two-stage self-supervised strategy. Stage 1 performs causal next-patch continual pre-training on diverse datasets (Weather, Traffic, Electricity, ILI, ETT), where each univariate series is patchified and the LLM predicts the next patch using causal attention with MSE loss, aligning LLM sequential modeling with temporal dynamics. Stage 2 fine-tunes for multi-patch prediction: non-parametric anchors (recent history or FFT-based) are patchified, concatenated with look-back patches, and fed through the LLM with a position-aware attention mask (each future anchor sees only history patches and itself), then decoded independently per patch. The critical architectural innovation is the patch-wise decoder (W_p ∈ R^{D×P}), which decodes each patch into temporal values independently rather than using a sequence-level linear layer (W_s ∈ R^{(L·D)×H}), reducing decoder parameters by ~99.6% and preventing downstream overfitting. In Stage 2, only Position Embedding and Layer Normalization are unfrozen (<0.01% of parameters), preserving the time-series-aligned representations from Stage 1.

## Results

On long-term forecasting (Table 1), aLLM4TS achieves the best or near-best MSE across 6 datasets (ETTh1, ETTh2, ILI, Weather, Traffic, Electricity) at 4 prediction horizons each, with an average 9.71% MSE reduction over GPT4TS (the prior SOTA LLM-based method) and 2.03% over PatchTST. On short-term M4 forecasting, aLLM4TS achieves 11.950 average SMAPE, outperforming GPT4TS (12.422) by 0.472 points. For anomaly detection, aLLM4TS attains the best average F1-score of 87.51% across SMD, MSL, SMAP, SWaT, and PSM, surpassing GPT4TS (86.72%). In few-shot (5% data) forecasting, aLLM4TS achieves 0.425 average MSE, a 30.6% reduction over TimesNet and 28.5% over DLinear.

## Baselines & Comparisons

aLLM4TS consistently outperforms GPT4TS (Zhou et al., 2023), the prior SOTA LLM-based time series model, by an average 9.71% MSE reduction on long-term forecasting and 0.472 SMAPE on M4 short-term forecasting, attributed to the two-stage forecasting-based pre-training and patch-wise decoder. Against Time-LLM (Jin et al., 2024), which uses text-guided prompting with frozen LLMs, aLLM4TS achieves over 9.40% MSE improvement, demonstrating that explicit representation adaptation outperforms prompt-based reprogramming. Compared to the strong MLP baseline DLinear (Zeng et al., 2023) and CNN-based TimesNet (Wu et al., 2022), aLLM4TS exceeds them by over 19.3% average MSE, while still winning over the Transformer-based PatchTST (Nie et al., 2023) by 2.03%. On anomaly detection, aLLM4TS achieves the best average F1 of 87.51%, narrowly beating GPT4TS (86.72%) and clearly surpassing all Transformer baselines (TimesNet 85.24%, FEDformer 84.97%). In few-shot (5% data) settings, aLLM4TS achieves 0.425 average MSE, outperforming GPT4TS (0.465) by 8.6% and DLinear (0.594) by 28.5%, validating the transferability of the forecasting-aligned representation. The method also dominates contrastive learning baselines (BTSF, TS2Vec, TNC, TS-TCC) in representation learning by 13.5%+ MSE, confirming that forecasting-based self-supervision aligns better with downstream tasks than contrastive or masked objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the patch-based time series paradigm introduced by PatchTST (Nie et al., 2023) and the LLM-adaptation-for-time-series line initiated by GPT4TS (Zhou et al., 2023), but departs from both in two key ways. First, it replaces the sequence-level linear decoder (which maps the entire patch sequence to the prediction horizon) with a small shared patch-wise decoder, arguing this disentangles encoding from decoding and avoids overfitting. Second, it replaces contrastive learning or masked-reconstruction pre-training with causal next-patch prediction, explicitly aligning the self-supervised objective with the causal language modeling objective that LLMs are already trained on, thereby making the adaptation more natural.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The patch-wise decoder insight is practically significant: a D×P shared linear layer (0.34% of the sequence-level decoder's parameters) outperforms the conventional approach, and it enables arbitrary prediction horizons without retraining. The two-stage causal pre-training + multi-patch fine-tuning paradigm offers a cleaner recipe for adapting LLMs to time series than prompt engineering or limited fine-tuning. However, the evaluation is limited to GPT-2's first 6 layers and channel-independent univariate processing, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger LLMs, multivariate joint modeling, or non-stationary regimes. The modest gains over GPT4TS (~9-10% MSE) suggest the architectural contribution, while useful, may not be the full story for closing the gap with task-specific models.
