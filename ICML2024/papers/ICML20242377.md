## Problem

Training large-scale vision foundation models on internet-scale data poses significant privacy and legal risks, yet existing DP training methods are limited to supervised learning on small curated datasets and underperform even handcrafted features. Most self-supervised learning algorithms (e.g., contrastive methods like SimCLR) are incompatible with DP-SGD because their loss functions entangle multiple samples, and the many training epochs required by SSL sharply inflate the privacy budget ε, rendering guarantees meaningless.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images resized to 224×224, with 75% of patches randomly masked during training. The encoder (ViT) produces a visual representation used for downstream transfer, while the decoder reconstructs the masked pixel patches via MSE loss. The DP pre-training operates on ~233M samples from LAION400M, with the privacy guarantee (ε=8, δ=1/2n) applying specifically to this natural-image dataset; the synthetic Shaders21k initialization (~1M programmatically-generated texture images) carries no privacy budget.

## Method

The paper identifies masked autoencoders (MAE) as the SSL algorithm naturally compatible with DP-SGD: the reconstruction loss decomposes into per-sample terms with no batch normalization or cross-sample dependencies, enabling straightforward per-sample gradient clipping. The second key ingredient is warm-starting DP training with weights pre-trained on programmatically-generated synthetic textures (Shaders21k), which lets the model learn spatial structure without expending any privacy budget and dramatically reduces the number of DP-SGD iterations needed. The full recipe is: (1) train MAE non-privately on synthetic images for 1000 epochs, then (2) apply DP-AdamW (ε=8, δ=1/2n, C=0.1, σ=0.48, T=6100 iterations) on LAION400M using the synthetic weights as initialization. This yields ViP, a ViT-based MAE (12-block encoder, 4-block decoder) whose encoder representations achieve 55.7% linear probing accuracy on ImageNet-1K—matching end-to-end trained AlexNet—while providing a formal (8, 1/2n)-DP guarantee.

## Results

ViP (DP-trained with ε=8 on LAION233M) achieves 55.7% linear probing accuracy on ImageNet-1K, matching end-to-end trained AlexNet (56.5%) while providing a formal differential privacy guarantee. On transfer tasks, ViP outperforms all prior DP-trained models (TAN: 49.0%, DP-NFNet: 45.3% on ImageNet-1K) and even exceeds non-private SimCLR on iNat-2021 (38.1% vs 34.8%). For COCO object detection, ViP achieves 45.2 AP (box) and 40.4 AP (mask), surpassing both SimCLR (37.9/33.3) and Mask R-CNN (40.0/37.1). Under DP fine-tuning on ImageNet-1K, ViP reaches 50.3% top-1 accuracy, a 10%+ absolute improvement over the previous SOTA (TAN: 39.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two strongest DP baselines, ViP wins decisively: it outperforms TAN (Sander et al., 2022) by 6.7 points on ImageNet-1K (55.7% vs 49.0%) and by 6.4 points on iNat-2021 (38.1% vs 31.7%), and beats DP-NFNet (De et al., 2022) by 10.4 points on ImageNet-1K. Compared to non-private AlexNet (Krizhevsky et al., 2017), ViP essentially ties on ImageNet-1K (55.7% vs 56.5%) but substantially outperforms it on transfer tasks—Places-365 (46.1% vs 39.8%), Places-205 (48.5% vs 35.1%), and iNat-2021 (38.1% vs 23.7%). Against non-private SimCLR (Chen et al., 2020a), ViP falls short on ImageNet-1K (55.7% vs 67.5%) due to SimCLR's home-field advantage, but wins on iNat-2021 (38.1% vs 34.8%) and is within 0.7–0.8 points on Places-365/205. On COCO detection, ViP (45.2 AP box, 40.4 AP mask) outperforms both SimCLR (37.9/33.3) and Mask R-CNN (He et al., 2017; 40.0/37.1), though it trails non-private MAE (50.3/44.9). For DP fine-tuning on ImageNet-1K, ViP (50.3% top-1) surpasses TAN (39.2%) by over 10 points and DP-ResNet-18 (Kurakin et al., 2022; 6.2%) by a wide margin, while the non-private MAE baseline (60.5%) remains the upper bound that DP training has not yet closed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines three established threads—masked autoencoders (MAE) for self-supervised representation learning, DP-SGD for private optimization, and programmatically-generated synthetic textures (Shaders21k) for initialization—into a unified recipe for differentially private vision foundation model pre-training. It departs from prior DP vision work (De et al., Sander et al.), which was limited to supervised training on ImageNet-scale labeled data, by leveraging the instance-separable loss of MAE to unlock internet-scale unlabeled data (LAION400M). It also extends the NLP DP pre-training line (Anil et al. for BERT, Ponomareva et al. for T5) to the vision domain, where the challenge of per-sample gradient computation in SSL is more acute due to the prevalence of contrastive objectives incompatible with DP-SGD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that DP pre-training of vision foundation models at internet scale is practically feasible, with ViP matching end-to-end AlexNet on ImageNet linear probing under ε=8. However, a substantial gap remains to non-private SimCLR (67.5% vs. 55.7%) and non-private MAE (60.5%), indicating that private learning still lags significantly behind mainstream SSL. Open questions include whether other SSL paradigms (contrastive, multi-modal) can be adapted to DP-SGD, whether user-level DP guarantees are achievable, and whether the synthetic pre-training warm-start can be further improved to close the remaining utility gap. The publicly released code and models provide a concrete starting point for future work on private foundation models.
