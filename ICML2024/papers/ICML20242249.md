## Problem

Graph Transformers' global attention mechanism is assumed to benefit node classification by capturing information from all nodes, but this paper reveals an over-globalizing problem: attention scores are disproportionately allocated to distant higher-order neighbors while near neighbors containing most useful information are weakened. Both empirical evidence (attention score distributions across hop distances) and theoretical analysis (Theorem 3.1) show that an excessively expanded receptive field degrades embedding smoothness and classification performance, particularly on homophilic graphs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph with N nodes, each having a d-dimensional feature vector, along with the adjacency matrix. The graph is first partitioned into P non-overlapping clusters via METIS, so the BGA module operates on subgraphs of size N/P (intra-cluster) and P cluster tokens (inter-cluster), while the GCN module operates on the full graph. Both modules independently produce node class label predictions; the final output is the per-node class label from either module (reported separately as CoB-G and CoB-T).

## Method

CoBFormer introduces a Bi-Level Global Attention (BGA) module that decouples attention into an intra-cluster Transformer (capturing local information within each METIS partition) and an inter-cluster Transformer (capturing global information between mean-pooled cluster representations), reducing complexity from O(N²) to O(N²/P + P²). A GCN serves as a complementary local module to capture graph structural information that the BGA module neglects. Rather than fusing the two modules via linear combination (which can produce incorrect predictions even when either module alone is correct), CoBFormer employs collaborative training: the GCN and BGA modules mutually supervise each other on unlabeled nodes using temperature-scaled soft labels (knowledge distillation), with a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.2) that this mutual supervision improves generalization by decomposing the joint cross-entropy into optimizable labeled-node and unlabeled-node terms.

## Results

On homophilic graphs, CoBFormer's BGA module (CoB-T) achieves 85.28% Mi-F1 on Cora, 74.52% on CiteSeer, and 81.42% on PubMed, outperforming the best baseline SGFormer by 3.8, 2.6, and 3.4 points respectively. On heterophilic graphs, CoB-T matches or slightly exceeds SGFormer (37.41% vs 37.72% Mi-F1 on Actor; 66.96% vs 66.68% on Deezer). On large-scale graphs, CoB-G reaches 73.17% Mi-F1 on Ogbn-Arxiv and 78.09% on Ogbn-Products, surpassing all baselines including SGFormer (72.50% and 72.54% respectively). The BGA module also reduces GPU memory by up to 94% (PubMed: 8.42G → 0.50G) compared to vanilla global attention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoBFormer is compared against two classic GNNs (GCN, GAT) and three state-of-the-art Graph Transformers (NodeFormer, NAGphormer, SGFormer). On homophilic graphs, both CoB-G and CoB-T substantially outperform all five baselines: on Cora, CoB-T achieves 85.28% Mi-F1 versus SGFormer's 81.48% and GCN's 81.44%; on PubMed, CoB-T reaches 81.42% versus SGFormer's 78.04%. On heterophilic graphs, CoB-T is competitive with the best Transformer baseline SGFormer, achieving 37.41% vs 37.72% Mi-F1 on Actor (a 0.31-point gap) and 66.96% vs 66.68% on Deezer (a 0.28-point win). On large-scale graphs, CoB-G leads on Ogbn-Arxiv (73.17% vs 72.50% Mi-F1) and CoB-T leads on Ogbn-Products (78.15% vs 72.54% Mi-F1), with the gap on Products being particularly large at 5.6 points over SGFormer. NodeFormer and NAGphormer consistently underperform SGFormer across all datasets, while GCN and GAT are strongest on homophilic graphs but weakest on heterophilic ones, confirming the paper's analysis that local information dominates in homophilic settings while global information is critical in heterophilic settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Graph Transformer line of work (NodeFormer, NAGphormer, SGFormer, GOAT) that adapts global attention to node-level tasks, and on prior hybrid approaches (CoarFormer, SGFormer, Gophormer) that combine local GNN modules with global attention. It departs from these by identifying a fundamental flaw in the global attention mechanism itself—the over-globalizing problem—rather than merely addressing scalability. The proposed CoBFormer replaces full global attention with a bi-level (intra-cluster + inter-cluster) attention via METIS partitioning, and replaces the standard linear fusion of local and global features with a collaborative training scheme (mutual soft-label distillation between GCN and BGA modules) that has a theoretical generalization guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The over-globalizing diagnosis challenges a core assumption in the Graph Transformer literature—that wider receptive fields are always beneficial for node classification—and provides both theoretical (Theorems 3.1–3.2) and empirical grounding for why this is the case. The practical impact is notable: the BGA module reduces GPU memory by up to 94% while matching or exceeding full-attention performance, making Graph Transformers more accessible on large graphs. Open questions include whether the METIS-based partitioning is optimal (vs. learned or spectral partitioning), whether the collaborative training framework generalizes to other graph tasks (link prediction, graph classification), and whether the bi-level decomposition can be extended to more than two levels for very large-scale graphs.
