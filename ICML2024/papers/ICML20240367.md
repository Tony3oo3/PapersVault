## Problem

The known O(1)-approximation algorithm for balanced cut in semi-random graphs (Makarychev et al., 2012) requires solving polylogarithmically many semidefinite programs to optimality, yielding a running time of at least Ω(n³). This paper initiates the study of the fine-grained complexity of beyond-worst-case graph partitioning, asking how fast one can solve semi-random instances while retaining constant-factor approximation guarantees.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes as input a graph G on n vertices generated from the semi-random model (Model 1.1) with balance parameter a and edge probability η ≥ Ω((log n)²(log log n)²/n), and returns an Ω(a)-balanced cut of value O(n²·η) in near-linear time O(|V|^{1+o(1)} + |E|^{1+o(1)}). For the hierarchical clustering extension (Theorem 1.3), the input is a graph from the hierarchical stochastic block model with p_min = Ω(log n / n^{2/3}), and the output is a tree T whose Dasgupta cost is O(OPT), also in near-linear time. The near-linear time guarantee requires a slightly stronger density threshold than the original Makarychev et al. result, trading a polylogarithmic factor in η for the speedup.

## Method

The algorithm iteratively applies a matrix multiplicative weights (MMW) framework to the balanced cut SDP, but replaces the expensive exact SDP solver with a novel probabilistic oracle of small width. The oracle combines three components: (1) a flatness/balance check (Arora-Kale), (2) Sherman's sparsest cut subroutine for the final rounding, and (3) a fast approximate heavy vertex removal procedure that uses subsampling to identify dense balls in the embedding in near-linear time, tolerating constant-factor false positives. The key insight is that geometric expansion of the planted random cut is robust to monotone adversarial perturbations, so even infeasible MMW solutions that cut too many edges during heavy vertex removal reveal a small-width separating hyperplane (via violated triangle inequalities or flow-based feedback), allowing the MMW iteration to make progress. After O(log n) iterations reduce the optimal cut value by a 1/log n factor, Sherman's near-linear time sparsest cut algorithm produces the final O(α)-valued balanced cut.

## Results

The paper presents the first near-linear time algorithm for balanced cut in the semi-random model of Makarychev et al. (2012), running in O(|V(G)|^{1+o(1)} + |E(G)|^{1+o(1)}) time and returning an Ω(a)-balanced cut of value O(α) = O(n²·η) with probability 1−o(1), under the density condition η ≥ Ω((log n)²(log log n)²/n). This matches the O(1) approximation guarantee of the prior polynomial-time algorithm of Makarychev et al. (2012), which required Ω(n³) time for SDP rounding on top of solving polylogarithmically many semidefinite programs. As a corollary, the paper also yields a near-linear time O(1)-approximation to Dasgupta's hierarchical clustering objective for the semi-random hierarchical stochastic block model (HSM) with p_min = Ω(log n / n^{2/3}).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with the algorithm of Makarychev et al. (2012), which achieves the same O(1) approximation for balanced cut in the semi-random model but requires solving polylogarithmically many SDPs with Ω(n³) constraints, yielding a running time of at least Ω(n³). The new algorithm reduces this to near-linear time O(|V(G)|^{1+o(1)} + |E(G)|^{1+o(1)}) while preserving the same approximation guarantee, at the cost of a slightly stronger density requirement: η ≥ Ω((log n)²(log log n)²/n) versus η ≥ Ω(√log n·(log log n)²/n) for Makarychev et al. The paper also improves upon Cohen-Addad et al. (2019) for the HSM model, reducing the running time of the recursive sparsest-cut algorithm for Dasgupta's objective from polynomial to near-linear. The key technical contribution enabling this speedup is a probabilistic oracle of small width that leverages geometric expansion to work with infeasible SDP solutions within the matrix multiplicative weights framework, rather than requiring optimal SDP solutions as in the prior work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first near-linear time algorithm for balanced cut in the semi-random graph model of Makarychev et al. (2012), which previously required solving polylogarithmically many SDPs to optimality (implying Ω(n³) time). The algorithm reuses the geometric expansion property and heavy vertex removal framework from Makarychev et al., but replaces the exact SDP solver with the matrix multiplicative weights (MMW) framework of Arora-Kale and Sherman, augmented by a novel probabilistic oracle of small width that works with infeasible solutions. The approach also extends the near-linear time guarantee to Dasgupta's hierarchical clustering cost in the hierarchical stochastic block model of Cohen-Addad et al. (2019), improving their polynomial-time algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work initiates the fine-grained complexity study of beyond-worst-case graph partitioning, showing that the robustness properties of SDP-based approaches (which typically require expensive optimal solutions) can be preserved under a near-linear time budget. Key open questions remain: the minimum edge density η required for constant approximation (the paper needs Ω((log n)²(log log n)²/n) versus Ω(√log n · (log log n)²/n) for the slower algorithm), whether the result extends to the more general PIE model, and whether the algorithm can be made competitive with state-of-the-art bisection heuristics in practice. The techniques—particularly the probabilistic oracle leveraging geometric expansion on infeasible solutions—may generalize to other beyond-worst-case problems like multicut and min-uncut.
