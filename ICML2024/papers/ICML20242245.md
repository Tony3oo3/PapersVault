## Problem

In multi-label image classification, exploiting label co-occurrence relationships improves recognition but simultaneously causes overfitting to correlative features, leading to false-positive predictions when only co-occurring (not target) objects are present. Prior work has identified this double-edged nature of co-occurrence but lacks a principled mechanism to retain the beneficial co-occurrence signal while eliminating the harmful overfitting effect.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image (typically 448×448) and additionally crops it into four equally-sized patches that are resized back to the original image dimensions before being fed through the shared backbone. The output is a set of multi-label class probabilities obtained by applying a sigmoid to TDE logits, which combine full-image logits and spatially-weighted patch logits. The patching mechanism is plug-and-play: PAT-I requires no retraining and can be applied at inference to any pre-trained model, while PAT-T integrates patching into the training loop with separate classification heads for images and patches.

## Method

The paper formulates co-occurrence as a mediator in a causal graph (target object X and co-occurring object Z jointly produce correlative features O, which influence prediction Y), then uses Total Direct Effect (TDE) to isolate the direct causal path X→Y while removing the mediated path through O. Because the target object's spatial location is unknown, the method implements TDE via patching: the image is divided into four patches, softmax weights are computed over patch logits to identify the pivot patch containing the target object, and the final TDE logit is p + λ·q where p is the full-image logit and q is the spatially-weighted patch logit. For training (PAT-T), the model uses a shared backbone with three separate heads—one for image predictions, one for patch predictions, and one for weight estimation—trained with an asymmetric loss on both image and patch logits to mitigate the distribution shift between full images and patches. This causal decomposition approach differs from prior correlation-exploiting methods (GCNs, attention-based interventions) by operating at the feature-representation level rather than explicitly modeling label graphs, and by providing a counterfactual interpretation that guarantees removal of the overfitting bias.

## Results

On MS-COCO, PAT-T with TResNetL(OI) achieves 91.7% mAP, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art Q2L-CvT (91.3% mAP) while using a backbone with 54.7M parameters versus CvT-w24's 277M. Under the same TResNetL backbone, PAT-T improves over ASL by 2.1% mAP (88.7% vs 86.6%), over Q2L-TResL by 2.2% (89.5% vs 87.3%), and over ML-Decoder by 1.4% (91.4% vs 90.0%). On VOC 2007, PAT-T achieves 96.2% mAP, outperforming ADD-GCN (96.0%) and ASL (95.8%). On VG-256, PAT-T+Q2L-TResL reaches 59.5% mAP, a 1.8% gain over Q2L-TResL alone (57.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAT-T outperforms all 18 compared baselines across all three datasets. On MS-COCO, the most direct same-backbone comparisons show PAT-T beating ASL by 2.1% mAP (88.7 vs 86.6, TResNetL), Q2L-TResL by 2.2% (89.5 vs 87.3, TResNetL), and ML-Decoder by 1.4% (91.4 vs 90.0, TResNetL(OI)). Against the previous SOTA Q2L-CvT (91.3% mAP, CvT-w24 backbone with 277M parameters), PAT-T+Q2L-TResL achieves 91.7% mAP with only 54.7M parameters. On VOC 2007, PAT-T (96.2% mAP) surpasses ADD-GCN (96.0%), SSGRL (95.0%), and ASL (95.8%), with the gap over older methods (CNN-RNN 84.0%, VGG+SVM 89.7%, HCP 90.9%) being particularly large. On VG-256, PAT-T+Q2L-TResL (59.5% mAP) improves over Q2L-TResL (57.7%) by 1.8%, and PAT-I alone (no retraining) already gains 0.3% over the baseline, confirming the plug-and-play nature of the inference-time patching strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Pearl's (2001) total direct effect (TDE) framework from causal inference, repurposing it to address overfitting to label co-occurrence in multi-label image classification. Prior MLC methods (GCNs, RNNs, label masking) explicitly model label correlations, while recent work (Xu et al. 2022, Liu et al. 2023) identified the overfitting problem and proposed task decomposition or attention-based interventions. This paper departs by providing a unified causal graph where co-occurrence features act as a mediator with both beneficial and harmful effects, and implements TDE through a practical patching-based strategy that requires no architectural changes to the backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The causal framing gives a principled explanation for why shared-backbone MLC models both benefit from and suffer due to co-occurrence, and the patching-based implementation is a practical plug-and-play technique that can be applied to any existing MLC model at inference time without retraining. The method achieves SOTA mAP (91.7% on MS-COCO) with a significantly smaller backbone (54.7M vs. 277M parameters), suggesting the causal correction is more effective than brute-force scaling. Open questions include how the approach scales to larger patch counts or video MLC, whether the simplified two-object causal graph generalizes to scenes with many interacting objects, and whether the dataset-dependent λ parameter can be learned automatically.
