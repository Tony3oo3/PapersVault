## Problem

Winner-takes-all (WTA) learners produce multiple hypotheses that, after training, form input-dependent centroidal Voronoi tessellations of the conditional distribution. However, the best way to convert these geometrically optimal hypotheses into a full conditional density estimate remained unclear: prior Dirac or uniform mixture interpretations fail to capture intra-cell density variations, while naive kernel placement (Kernel-WTA) leaks probability mass across cell boundaries and destroys the geometric structure. This paper asks whether WTA predictions can yield accurate probabilistic estimates that simultaneously preserve the centroidal Voronoi geometry and model local density structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes an input feature vector x (tabular features for UCI regression, or audio spectrograms for sound event localization) and produces a continuous conditional density ρ̂_x(y) over the target space Y. The WTA backbone outputs K hypotheses f_θ^k(x) and K scores γ_θ^k(x); the density estimator then places a truncated isotropic kernel (Gaussian in Euclidean settings, von Mises-Fisher on the sphere for audio) at each hypothesis, normalized by the Voronoi cell volume and restricted to that cell. The scaling factor h is tuned post-hoc on a validation set via golden-section search, and likelihoods are computed via Monte Carlo approximation of the cell-volume integrals.

## Method

The core contribution is the Voronoi-WTA estimator: for each hypothesis k, a kernel K_h(f_θ^k(x), y) is truncated to its Voronoi cell Y_θ^k(x) and normalized by the cell volume V(f_θ^k(x), K_h) = ∫_{Y_θ^k} K_h(f_θ^k, ỹ) dỹ, yielding ρ̂_x(y) = Σ_k γ_θ^k(x) · K_h(f_θ^k(x), y) / V(f_θ^k(x), K_h) · 1(y ∈ Y_θ^k(x)). This truncation is the key design choice distinguishing it from Kernel-WTA: it guarantees the cell-scoring property (predicted cell mass equals true cell mass) and the centroidal property (cell-restricted expectation matches ground truth) for any h, whereas Kernel-WTA loses both as h grows. Training is unchanged from standard WTA with scoring heads (min-over-hypotheses loss plus BCE scoring loss); no architectural modification is needed. Theoretically, the paper proves (i) weak convergence to the true conditional distribution as K→∞, independent of h, via vanishing Voronoi cell diameters, and (ii) a strictly smaller asymptotic quantization constant than a fixed-grid Histogram baseline, following from Zador's theorem and Jensen's inequality.

## Results

On the UCI regression benchmark (5 hypotheses), Voronoi-WTA (V-WTA) achieves competitive NLL against Deep Ensembles and MDN, e.g., 0.37 on Wine (vs. MDN −1.53, Deep Ensembles 0.94) and 2.42 on Protein (vs. Deep Ensembles 2.83). On the ANSYN audio dataset, V-WTA with h=0.3 and K=25 hypotheses achieves NLL 1.15, outperforming Kernel-WTA (1.23) and Histogram (1.45). On synthetic datasets, V-WTA matches or exceeds MDN on EMD and NLL for non-Gaussian distributions, and always achieves strictly lower quantization error than Histogram regardless of the number of hypotheses, confirming the theoretical prediction from Proposition 5.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Mixture Density Networks (MDN), V-WTA is competitive on NLL for non-Gaussian datasets (e.g., Rotating Two Moons, Changing Damier) but MDN retains an advantage on the Single Gaussian dataset due to its native Gaussian parameterization and per-hypothesis scaling. V-WTA consistently achieves lower quantization error than MDN across all synthetic datasets, reflecting the optimal Voronoi tessellation property. Against the Histogram baseline, V-WTA always achieves strictly better quantization error regardless of the number of hypotheses (as predicted by Proposition 5.2), and requires fewer hypotheses to reach comparable NLL/EMD resolution. Against Deep Ensembles on UCI, V-WTA is competitive or superior on several datasets (Wine: 0.37 vs. 0.94; Yacht: 2.05 vs. 1.18; Protein: 2.42 vs. 2.83), notably without directly optimizing NLL during training. Against Kernel-WTA, V-WTA's advantage grows with the scaling factor h: at h=1.0 on audio data, V-WTA achieves NLL 1.22 (K=25) while K-WTA degrades to 2.09, confirming that truncated kernels preserve the geometric structure that untruncated kernels destroy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the probabilistic interpretation of Winner-takes-all (WTA) learners from Letzelter et al. (2023), who modeled WTA predictions as Dirac or uniform mixtures, by replacing these with truncated kernel density estimators inspired by the Voronoi Density Estimator of Polianskii et al. (2022). The key departure from Polianskii et al. is that the Voronoi generators are not random samples but learned WTA hypotheses that form an input-dependent centroidal Voronoi tessellation (Rupprecht et al., 2017), and the setting is conditional rather than unconditional. The WTA training scheme itself (Lee et al., 2016) is used unmodified; the contribution is entirely in the post-hoc density estimation layer and its theoretical analysis via Zador's theorem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, training-free way to extract calibrated conditional densities from existing WTA/MCL models, with a convergence guarantee that is independent of the kernel bandwidth—a practical advantage over standard Parzen estimators. The theoretical quantization analysis (Proposition 5.2) rigorously establishes why the adaptive WTA grid dominates a fixed histogram grid, giving practitioners a clear justification for choosing WTA-based estimators. Open questions remain around WTA initialization sensitivity, the lack of out-of-distribution detection, and whether the global-optimality assumption (B.5) holds in practice for deep networks; the authors also note that the bandwidth h must be tuned post-hoc, which could be problematic under distribution shift.
