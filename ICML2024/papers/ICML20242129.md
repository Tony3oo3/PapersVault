## Problem

Existing multi-objective Bayesian optimization acquisition functions either rely solely on the first moment of the hypervolume improvement (HVI) distribution (e.g., EHVI uses the mean) or ignore the distribution entirely (e.g., ε-PoI uses a distance-based criterion unrelated to HVI quantiles). When the GP posterior exhibits large prediction uncertainty, HVI's distribution becomes highly dispersed, making mean-based acquisition functions unreliable. No prior work provides the exact probability distribution of HVI, which is needed to quantify risk and access arbitrary quantiles of the improvement.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a candidate decision point x, the current non-dominated approximation set P (n points), a reference point r, and the independent GP posteriors for each objective (posterior mean μ_i and variance σ_i² at x), plus a user-specified threshold ε. It outputs a scalar probability — the probability that the realized HVI at x exceeds ε — which is then maximized (via CMA-ES) to select the next evaluation point. Unlike ε-PoI, the method requires a reference point and operates on the full HVI distribution rather than a geometric distance, at the cost of quadratic (vs. n log n) computational complexity in |P|.

## Method

The paper first generalizes HVI by defining a negative HVI for dominated points (the negative volume of the region they dominate within dom(P)), turning acquisition-function plateaus into valleys. It then partitions the bi-objective space into (n+1)² axis-aligned cells using the augmented Pareto set, within each of which HVI reduces to a product of two truncated Gaussian random variables plus a constant offset. The exact conditional PDF and CDF of HVI on each cell are derived in closed form via a single integral over the product-of-truncated-Gaussians density, and the unconditional distribution is obtained by summing over cells weighted by the probability mass of each cell. From this distribution, the paper proposes ε-PoHVI, an acquisition function defined as 1 − CDF_HVI(ε), which directly targets the probability of achieving at least ε hypervolume improvement. Two adaptive schedules for ε (exponential scaling and exponential smoothing of realized HV gains) are introduced to balance exploration and exploitation across iterations.

## Results

On 14 bi-objective test problems (ZDT1–4, ZDT6, WOSGZ1–8, and a four-bar truss design), ε-PoHVI-scaling achieves the best overall Wilcoxon Rank-Sum score (25 wins / 9 ties / 8 losses out of 14 problems). On the challenging WOSGZ problems, ε-PoHVI-scaling substantially outperforms EHVI in mean hypervolume (e.g., WOSGZ1: 0.546 vs. 0.100; WOSGZ8: 0.841 vs. 0.154), while EHVI leads on the easier ZDT problems (e.g., ZDT1: 224.54 vs. 211.77; ZDT3: 235.45 vs. 212.83). The exact HVI distribution computation is roughly one order of magnitude faster than Monte Carlo approximation at comparable numerical accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ε-PoI (Emmerich et al., 2020), ε-PoHVI-scaling is significantly better on 9/14 problems, tied on 3, and worse on 2, with the largest gaps on WOSGZ problems (e.g., WOSGZ8: 0.841 vs. 0.494 HV). Against EHVI (Emmerich et al., 2006), ε-PoHVI-scaling wins on 8/14 problems but loses on 5, with EHVI dominating on the regular ZDT fronts (ZDT1: 224.54 vs. 211.77) and the real-world RE problem (41.10 vs. 39.63), while ε-PoHVI-scaling dominates on all WOSGZ problems where GP posterior uncertainty is an order of magnitude higher (σ₁ ≈ 1.5–5.4 vs. 0.003–0.044). Against ParEGO (Knowles, 2006), ε-PoHVI outperforms across all 14 test problems. Against Joint Entropy Search (Tu et al., 2022), JES only outperforms ε-PoHVI on ZDT6; on all other problems ε-PoHVI is competitive or superior. The paper attributes ε-PoHVI's advantage on WOSGZ to its use of HVI distribution quantiles rather than the mean, making it robust to the large prediction variance that renders EHVI less informative.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-objective Bayesian optimization acquisition function lineage initiated by Emmerich et al. (2006, 2020), which introduced EHVI (first moment of HVI) and ε-PoI (distance-based probability). The key departure is deriving the exact full probability distribution of HVI for bi-objective problems via a cell-partition method over the objective space, rather than relying on Monte Carlo approximation or using only the mean. It also generalizes the HVI definition itself (from Emmerich's 2005 thesis) to assign nonzero values to dominated points, making the acquisition landscape more tractable. The resulting ε-PoHVI acquisition function directly uses HVI's CDF to compute quantile-based improvement probabilities, a capability unavailable to prior acquisition functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exact HVI distribution provides a principled way to quantify risk in multi-objective BO when GP prediction uncertainty is high, a regime where EHVI (mean-based) becomes unreliable. The substantial empirical gains on WOSGZ problems (where posterior std dev is orders of magnitude larger than on ZDTs) validate that quantile-based acquisition outperforms mean-based ones under high uncertainty. However, the derivation is restricted to bi-objective problems with uncorrelated GPs, and the quadratic computational complexity limits scalability. Open questions include extending the cell-partition approach to three or more objectives, incorporating correlated GP posteriors, and validating on more real-world industrial problems.
