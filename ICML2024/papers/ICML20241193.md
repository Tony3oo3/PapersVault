## Problem

Signed Message Passing (SMP), the dominant approach for handling heterophilic graphs, suffers from two fundamental limitations that prior work has not identified: (1) even when each one-hop propagation matrix is 'desirable' (positive for homophilic, negative for heterophilic pairs), the cumulative multi-hop matrix can become undesirable, pulling nodes toward heterophilic multi-hop neighbors; and (2) SMP remains vulnerable to oversmoothing in multi-class settings, with class-mean separability decaying exponentially with depth. Both issues stem from SMP's multiset-to-element aggregation, which linearly collapses all neighbor embeddings into a single vector, irreversibly mixing heterophilic representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph adjacency matrix and a node feature matrix (X ∈ R^{N×d}) and produces node embeddings of the same dimensionality d, which are fed into a classification head for node labels. Structurally, each node's message vector is a concatenation of C chunks (one per class), where each chunk is a (d/C)-dimensional vector aggregating only same-class neighbor embeddings weighted by learned soft attention scores. A residual connection to the initial MLP-transformed features is added at each layer, and a regularization loss on the soft-label distribution is jointly optimized with the task loss.

## Method

M2M-GNN replaces the standard multiset-to-element (m-2-e) aggregation with a multiset-to-multiset (m-2-m) scheme: at each layer, neighbor embeddings are projected to d/C dimensions, then partitioned into C chunks via learned soft labels (computed by a temperature-scaled attention over ego and neighbor representations), each chunk is independently pooled, and the C chunk vectors are concatenated to form the message. A regularization term (L2 norm of summed soft labels across edges) prevents attention collapse into a single chunk. Theoretically, the paper proves that stacking one-hop desirable m-2-m operations preserves the desirable property at arbitrary multi-hop distances (unlike SMP, where Theorem 2.2 shows failure), and that m-2-m can escape oversmoothing even when class means have converged (Theorem 3.7), because the concatenation structure preserves per-class information that linear summation destroys. The time complexity matches that of standard GCN.

## Results

M2M-GNN achieves the best average ranking (1.7) across 11 node classification benchmarks, securing at least the third-best performance on every dataset. On heterophilic graphs, it leads on Texas (89.19%), Wisconsin (89.01%), Squirrel (63.60%), Chameleon (75.20%), Penn94 (85.94%), and Amazon-rating (49.18%). On homophilic graphs, it achieves 88.12% on Cora, 77.20% on Citeseer, and 90.35% on Pubmed. In the oversmoothing analysis, M2M-GNN maintains stable performance up to 64 layers, while SMP-based baselines (FAGCN, Goal, GPR-GNN) show substantial accuracy degradation as depth increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M2M-GNN outperforms all six SMP-based GNNs (GPR-GNN, FAGCN, GGCN, ACM-GCN, Goal, AERO-GNN) on average ranking, with the largest margins on heterophilic graphs: e.g., on Squirrel it achieves 63.60% vs. 61.76% (AERO-GNN) and 60.53% (Goal), and on Chameleon 75.20% vs. 72.28% (Ord.GNN) and 71.65% (Goal). It also surpasses the attention-vector-based DMP by a wide margin (e.g., 89.19% vs. 66.08% on Texas, 90.35% vs. 73.12% on Pubmed), confirming the advantage of multiset-to-multiset over multiset-to-element aggregation. Against concatenation-based methods, M2M-GNN beats Ord.GNN (A.R. 1.7 vs. 2.7) and H2GCN (A.R. 1.7 vs. 7.8), with the most notable gap on Squirrel (63.60% vs. 62.44% and 36.48% respectively). On homophilic graphs, M2M-GNN is competitive but not always best: Ord.GNN edges it out on Cora (88.37% vs. 88.12%) and ACM-GCN on Pubmed (90.00% vs. 90.35% — actually M2M-GNN wins here). The paper also shows that M2M-GNN's performance remains stable at 32–64 layers, while SMP-based baselines degrade significantly, validating the theoretical oversmoothing resistance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant multiset-to-element (m-2-e) message passing paradigm used by all existing GNNs, including SMP-based methods (FAGCN, GPR-GNN, GGCN, ACM-GNN, AERO-GNN, Goal) and concatenation-based approaches (H2GCN, Ordered GNN). It builds on the theoretical framework of desirable propagation matrices from Yan et al. (2022) and Choi et al. (2023), but extends the analysis to reveal that these desirable properties do not compose across layers in multi-class settings. The proposed m-2-m scheme repurposes the attention mechanism from Brody et al. (2021) and the regularization idea from Tsitsulin et al. (2023) to implement class-aware partitioning of neighbor embeddings without requiring ground-truth labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of two fundamental SMP limitations—undesirable multi-hop propagation and persistent oversmoothing in multi-class settings—challenges a widely-adopted design principle in heterophilic GNNs and provides a clear theoretical motivation for the m-2-m alternative. However, the practical gains over strong SMP baselines are moderate (typically 1–5 percentage points), and the method's reliance on a fixed number of chunks (ideally matching the number of classes) limits its applicability to open-set or very high-cardinality classification. Open questions include whether the theoretical guarantees extend to fully nonlinear architectures, whether the chunk count can be learned adaptively, and how the approach scales to graphs with millions of nodes.
