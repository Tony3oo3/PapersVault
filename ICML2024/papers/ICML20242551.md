## Problem

Classical reinforcement learning for tabular and linear mixture MDPs is subject to an Ω(√T) worst-case regret lower bound, and it remained completely open whether quantum computing could break this barrier in the online exploration setting. Prior quantum RL work either required a generative model (bypassing exploration entirely) or was limited to bandit problems. This paper is the first to study online exploration in quantum RL and establish provable logarithmic worst-case regret.

## Input / Output

The agent interacts with the MDP through quantum oracles: a transition oracle P̄_h that maps |s,a⟩|0⟩ to a superposition over next states weighted by √P_h(s'|s,a), and a reward oracle R̄_h returning the binary-encoded reward. Critically, unlike generative-model settings, the agent cannot prepare arbitrary |s⟩ states and must discover states through exploration via the CSQA subroutine. The algorithm outputs a sequence of greedy policies (one per phase of H episodes) along with transition kernel estimates, value/Q functions, and achieves O(poly(S,A,H,log T)) regret for tabular MDPs or O(poly(d,H,log T)) for linear mixture MDPs with d-dimensional features.

## Method

The core mechanism combines quantum mean estimation (which achieves 1/n accuracy vs. 1/√n classically) with a novel lazy-updating scheme based on the doubling trick, since quantum samples collapse upon measurement and cannot be reused across episodes. For tabular MDPs, Quantum UCRL re-estimates the transition kernel via multi-dimensional amplitude estimation only when the visit count for each (s,a) pair doubles, then performs optimistic value iteration with a bonus term Õ(HS/n_h(s,a)). For linear mixture MDPs, Quantum UCRL-VTR performs weighted ridge regression on estimated features and regression targets, with lazy updating triggered by the determinant of the covariance matrix. A key technical novelty is the feature estimation subroutine (Algorithm 4), which uses binary search combined with quantum multivariate mean estimation to satisfy the self-referential accuracy requirement ‖φ̂−φ‖₂ ≤ max_h ‖φ̂_h‖_{(Λ_h)^{-1}}, a challenge with no counterpart in classical RL or quantum bandits.

## Results

For tabular MDPs with S states, A actions, and horizon H, Quantum UCRL (Algorithm 1) achieves a worst-case regret of O(S²AH³ log(T) log(S²AH log(T)/δ)) over T episodes (Theorem 4.1). For linear mixture MDPs with d-dimensional representation, Quantum UCRL-VTR (Algorithm 2) achieves O(d^{5/2} H^{9/2} log^{3/2}(1+T³/d) · ι) regret where ι = O(log(dH log(1+T³/d)/δ)) (Theorem 5.2). Both bounds are logarithmic in T, breaking the Ω(√T) regret lower bound that is unavoidable for any classical RL algorithm. The key improvement over classical model-based RL is replacing the √T dependence with poly(log T), enabled by quantum amplitude estimation and multivariate mean estimation subroutines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the classical Ω(√T) regret lower bound, which is information-theoretically unavoidable for any classical online RL algorithm. Quantum UCRL reduces the tabular MDP regret from Õ(S²AH·√T) (UCRL2) to O(S²AH³ log T), and Quantum UCRL-VTR reduces the linear mixture MDP regret from Õ(dH·√T) (UCRL-VTR) to O(d^{5/2} H^{9/2} poly(log T)). The authors note that their tabular regret bound is worse by a factor of H compared to the concurrent work of Ganguly et al. (2023), which assumes a different (classical-proceeding) MDP model with additional quantum samples per step; however, the authors' analysis immediately implies the same bound under Ganguly et al.'s setting. Crucially, the linear mixture MDP setting is entirely absent from Ganguly et al. (2023), and the feature estimation subroutine (Algorithm 4) with its binary-search-plus-quantum-mean-estimation design is novel and has no counterpart in prior quantum bandit or quantum RL-with-generative-model literature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical UCRL2 (Jaksch et al., 2010) and UCRL-VTR (Ayoub et al., 2020) optimistic planning frameworks to quantum-accessible environments, replacing classical sampling-based model estimation with quantum amplitude estimation and multivariate mean estimation subroutines. The key departure from prior quantum RL work (Wang et al., 2021a) is that those methods assumed a generative model allowing queries to arbitrary states, thereby sidestepping the exploration problem entirely; here the agent can only access states reachable through the transition oracle, making online exploration the central challenge. The doubling trick for lazy updating and the novel feature estimation procedure (Algorithm 4) are introduced to handle the irreversibility of quantum measurement, which prevents sample reuse in the way classical RL algorithms rely on it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical demonstration that quantum computing can break the Ω(√T) regret barrier in online RL, establishing that logarithmic worst-case regret is achievable in both tabular and linear mixture MDPs under quantum-accessible environments. The result opens a new research direction at the intersection of quantum computing and RL theory, but significant open questions remain: the regret bounds are far from tight (e.g., S²AH³ for tabular, d^{5/2}H^{9/2} for linear), the analysis does not extend to general function approximation or linear MDPs, and no experimental validation exists. The practical impact is currently limited by the idealized quantum oracle model and the absence of fault-tolerant quantum hardware, but the theoretical framework provides a clear roadmap for future quantum RL algorithms.
