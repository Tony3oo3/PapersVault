## Problem

While adversarial robustness tradeoffs in linear classification are well-characterized, the analogous question for linear regression—how small can adversarial risk be when standard accuracy is constrained—remains largely open beyond the narrow setting of Euclidean-norm attacks on isotropic features. This paper asks: given a model with small standard risk, what is the best achievable adversarial risk, and under what conditions is robustness attainable without sacrificing accuracy?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240446/cropped_page6_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a Gaussian linear regression setup (features x ~ N(0,Σ), labels y = xᵀw₀ + z) together with an arbitrary attack norm ‖·‖ and attack strength r, and produces closed-form (up to multiplicative constants) expressions for the optimal adversarial risk E_opt(r), the optimal regularized model w^{prox}(λ), and the free-lunch threshold ε_FL(r) that separates regimes of free robustness from unavoidable tradeoffs. The outputs are analytic formulas parameterized by spectral quantities of Σ and alignment coefficients of w₀, enabling complete phase-transition diagrams rather than point estimates.

## Method

The core technique introduces multiplicative proxy functionals (E̅ and Ē) that bracket the true adversarial risk within absolute constants, making the optimization tractable for arbitrary norms. The key insight is that the optimal robust model is the proximal operator w^{prox}(λ) = argmin_w ‖w−w₀‖²_Σ + λ‖w‖²_⋆ evaluated at λ = r² (unconstrained) or λ = λ_opt(r,ε) (constrained to ε-accuracy), yielding an explicit regularized version of the generative model. The analysis relies on Lagrangian duality rather than Gordon's comparison inequality, which is what enables extension beyond Euclidean norms and isotropic covariances. Concrete phase-transition diagrams are then computed for polynomially-decaying spectral conditions (Euclidean attacks) and ℓ_p attacks on isotropic features, revealing a critical source exponent δ=1 where accurate models switch from non-robust to robust.

## Results

The paper establishes precise analytic formulas for the optimal adversarial risk E_opt(r) and the constrained optimal risk E_opt(r,ε) in linear regression under general norms and covariance matrices. The headline result (Theorem 5.2) identifies a sharp phase-transition at source exponent δ=1 for polynomially-decaying spectral conditions: for δ<1, E_opt(r) = σ² + r^{2(1-θ)} with θ=(1-δ)/β, while for δ>1, free lunch holds and E_opt(r) = σ² + r². In the sparse ground-truth setting (Theorem 5.1), for ℓ_p attacks with p∈[1,∞) and r ∝ 1/s^{1/q}, the unconstrained optimum satisfies E_opt(r) = o_d(1) while any ε-accurate model incurs E_opt(r,ε) = Θ((1-ε)²), confirming an unavoidable accuracy-robustness tradeoff.

| Regime | ε_FL(r) | λ_opt(r,ε) | E_opt(r) | E_opt(r,ε) | Free lunch? |

| 0 ≤ δ < 1 | r^{2(1-θ)} | ε^{2/(1-θ)} | σ² + r^{2(1-θ)} | σ² + ε² + r²ε^{-2φ} | No |

Here θ = (1-δ)/β, φ = θ/(1-θ), and W is the Lambert W function. All entries are given up to multiplicative absolute constants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper with no direct method-vs-method benchmarking; instead, it extends and sharpens prior theoretical results. Compared to Javanmard et al. (COLT 2020), which computed exact Pareto curves only for Euclidean-norm attacks on isotropic features (Σ=I_d), the present work generalizes to arbitrary covariance matrices and arbitrary attack norms, recovering the isotropic Euclidean case as a corollary (Theorem C.1). Compared to Xing et al. (AISTATS 2021), which showed the optimal robust model is a ridge estimator with an implicitly-defined parameter requiring numerical fixed-point solving, this paper provides the explicit closed-form ridge parameter λ=r², yielding a cleaner characterization. Compared to Scetbon & Dohmatob (AISTATS 2023), which gave only sufficient conditions for the generative model to be robust, this paper delivers a complete quantitative tradeoff: it identifies the exact free-lunch threshold ε_FL(r) above which no accuracy sacrifice is needed, and below which the optimal regularized model w^{prox}(λ_opt) is characterized explicitly. The empirical experiments (Figures 1–4) confirm all theoretical predictions with OLS estimators on simulated data, showing perfect conformity between predicted and observed adversarial risk curves across sparsity levels, spectral exponents, and attack norms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial robustness analysis for linear regression from the restricted setting of Euclidean-norm attacks on isotropic features (Javanmard et al., 2020; Xing et al., 2021) to arbitrary attack norms and general covariance matrices. It builds directly on the analytic formula for adversarial risk and multiplicative proxy approximations established by Scetbon & Dohmatob (2023), but departs from the Gordon's Comparison Inequality technique used in prior work, instead relying on Lagrangian duality to obtain explicit (rather than implicit) regularization parameters. The key conceptual advance is showing that the optimal robust model is always a proximal/regularized version of the generative model with a closed-form parameter λ = r², and that a 'free lunch' threshold separates regimes where accuracy and robustness are compatible from those where a tradeoff is unavoidable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first complete phase-transition diagram for accuracy-robustness tradeoffs in linear regression under general norms, identifying δ=1 (the source exponent) as a critical boundary where the generative model transitions from non-robust to robust. The practical implication that regularization (e.g., early-stopped gradient descent) is always optimal for adversarial robustness gives practitioners a clear algorithmic prescription. However, the analysis is confined to linear models with Gaussian features; the authors note that extending to over-parameterized neural networks in random-feature or NTK regimes would require random matrix theory techniques and remains open. The back-of-envelope prediction that MNIST/FashionMNIST kernel regression sits in the unavoidable-tradeoff regime (δ ≈ 0.04–0.16) is an interesting but unverified empirical claim.
