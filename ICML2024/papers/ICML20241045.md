## Problem

Sobolev Transport (ST) on graphs provides a closed-form, scalable variant of optimal transport, but its definition is inherently coupled to the L^p geometric structure, making it difficult to incorporate other prior geometries. Orlicz-Wasserstein (OW) overcomes this limitation by leveraging Orlicz geometry, yet its computation requires solving a challenging two-level optimization (over both the transport plan and a positive scalar). The paper asks whether one can retain the Orlicz geometric flexibility of OW while preserving the computational tractability of ST on graphs.

## Input / Output

The method takes two probability measures supported on a connected, undirected graph with positive edge lengths, a chosen N-function Φ (a strictly increasing convex function growing faster than linear, e.g., exp(t)−t−1 or exp(t²)−1), and a nonnegative Borel measure ω on the graph. In the discrete setting where measures are supported on graph nodes, the computation reduces to a sum over edges weighted by edge lengths, requiring only a one-dimensional minimization over a positive scalar k. The output is a scalar distance between the two measures, which in experiments is plugged into an RBF kernel exp(−t·d(·,·)) for SVM-based classification of documents or persistence diagrams.

## Method

The core construction replaces the L^{p'} Sobolev constraint on the critic function in ST with a constraint in a newly defined graph-based Orlicz-Sobolev space WL_Ψ(G,ω), where Ψ is the complementary N-function of the chosen Φ and the generalized graph derivative is measured in the Luxemburg norm. By exploiting the Kantorovich-dual structure and the duality between Orlicz norms (via the complementary function), the paper proves (Theorem 3.3) that the resulting Generalized Sobolev Transport (GST) admits a closed-form reduction to a univariate optimization: inf_{k>0} (1/k)(1 + ∫Φ(k|h(x)|)ω(dx)), where h(x)=μ(Λ(x))−ν(Λ(x)) is the cumulative mass difference along shortest paths from a fixed root. In the discrete node-supported case this becomes a finite sum over edges, solvable by standard scalar optimizers. ST is recovered as the special case Φ(t)=((p−1)^{p−1}/p^p)·t^p, and GST coincides with OW when Φ(t)=t and the graph is a tree, establishing GST as a computationally efficient regularization of OW.

## Results

The headline computational result is that GST is 100×, 5000×, and 3000× faster than OW for N-functions Φ₀(t)=t, Φ₁(t)=exp(t)−t−1, and Φ₂(t)=exp(t²)−1 respectively. For Φ₁ and Φ₂, GST completes in under 25 seconds while OW requires 17–33 hours on 10⁴ measure pairs from the AMAZON dataset. On document classification (TWITTER, RECIPE, CLASSIC, AMAZON) and TDA (Orbit, MPEG7), GST with all three N-functions matches or improves upon OW-Φ₀ in SVM classification accuracy, while OW with Φ₁ and Φ₂ is infeasible to compute at scale (e.g., >29 million pairs on AMAZON).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Orlicz-Wasserstein distance (Guha et al., 2023), GST achieves identical or better classification accuracy on all six datasets while being 100×–5000× faster in computation; OW with Φ₁ and Φ₂ is entirely infeasible at the required scale (tens of millions of measure pairs), so only OW-Φ₀ serves as a practical baseline. Against the tree-sliced Wasserstein baseline (Le et al., 2019, denoted OW-Φ₀-Tree), the paper reports mixed results: OW-Φ₀-Tree underperforms OW-Φ₀ on TWITTER and RECIPE but outperforms it on CLASSIC and AMAZON, which the authors attribute to the positive-definiteness of the tree-based kernel versus the indefinite kernel of full-graph OW-Φ₀. In TDA tasks, positive definiteness of the tree kernel also helps OW-Φ₀-Tree improve over OW-Φ₀, consistent with observations in Le et al. (2022). Overall, GST-Φ₁ and GST-Φ₂ improve upon GST-Φ₀ in accuracy on both document classification and TDA, suggesting the Orlicz geometric structure beyond the Lᵖ case is beneficial, though at a modest computational cost relative to the closed-form GST-Φ₀.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: Sobolev Transport (ST) on graphs (Le et al., 2022), which exploits graph structure to yield closed-form OT computations, and Orlicz-Wasserstein (OW) (Sturm, 2011; Guha et al., 2023), which replaces the L^p ground cost with an Orlicz-norm-based metric to capture richer geometric structure. The key departure is that ST is inherently coupled to the L^p functional structure via its Sobolev-space constraint, making it difficult to incorporate alternative priors, while OW's two-level optimization (over transport plans and a scalar) is computationally prohibitive. GST resolves this tension by introducing a graph-based Orlicz-Sobolev space for the critic function constraint, reducing the computation to a single univariate optimization while preserving the Orlicz geometric flexibility of OW.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction from OW's two-level optimization to GST's univariate problem (yielding 100×–5000× speedups) makes Orlicz-structured OT practically usable for large-scale graph-based measure comparison, which was previously infeasible. The paper demonstrates that the Orlicz structure (Φ₁, Φ₂) can improve classification accuracy over the L^p-based Φ₀ case, suggesting the geometric flexibility is not merely theoretical. However, the empirical gains are modest and preliminary (single SVM classifier, limited datasets), and the paper explicitly leaves open the unbalanced setting (measures with different total mass), which is essential for noisy or outlier-containing data. The requirement of a unique shortest-path root node, while generically satisfied, restricts applicability to certain graph topologies.
