## Problem

Sequential importance sampling (SIS) is empirically the most efficient method for estimating the permanent of nonnegative matrices, but it lacks accuracy guarantees because bounding the variance of importance weights is generally intractable. The most efficient practical methods with provable (ε,δ)-accuracy guarantees rely on rejection sampling, which is slower. The paper asks whether the nesting upper bounds used in rejection sampling can be incorporated into SIS to bound importance weights and enable efficient stopping rules.

## Input / Output

The method takes a nonnegative n×n matrix as input, which is preprocessed by removing redundant entries and applying 100 iterations of Sinkhorn balancing. It produces a single (ε,δ)-approximation of the permanent with a guaranteed relative error at most ε with probability at least 1−δ. The practical instantiation (DEEPNIS) uses the depth-16 variant of the extended Huber bound as the nesting upper bound, with the bound's tightness directly controlling the sample complexity.

## Method

Nesting Importance Sampling (NIS) defines the SIS proposal distribution by sampling each entry proportionally to the nesting upper bound of the remaining submatrix, rather than to the true (unknown) permanent. The key theoretical result (Lemma 2.2) shows that when the upper bound satisfies the nesting property—monotonicity over the partition tree—the importance weight Y(x) = a(x)/q(x) is bounded above by U(Ω), yielding a critical ratio of at most U(Ω)/a(Ω). This bound on Y enables the use of asymptotically optimal stopping rules for [0,1]-valued random variables (Dagum et al., 2000; Huber & Jones, 2019), giving an expected sample complexity of O((E[Y²]/E[Y]² + U(Ω)·ε/a(Ω))·ε⁻²·log δ⁻¹), which improves over rejection sampling's O(U(Ω)/a(Ω)·ε⁻²·log δ⁻¹) by a factor of up to ε⁻¹ in the high-accuracy regime. The practical DEEPNIS scheme combines this with deep bounds (depth-16 extended Huber bound) and Sinkhorn balancing, achieving up to two orders-of-magnitude speedups over the state-of-the-art DEEPAR rejection sampler.

## Results

DEEPNIS (the proposed nesting importance sampling scheme) consistently outperforms the state-of-the-art rejection sampler DEEPAR across all 12 real-world instances, achieving speedups ranging from 4× to 115× in running time for computing (ε,δ)-approximations with δ=0.05. The speedup ratio grows as ε decreases: for example, on cage5 (n=37), the ratio increases from 36× at ε=0.02 to 72× at ε=0.01 and 115× at ε=0.005. For larger instances (n≥51), DEEPAR frequently exceeds the 12-hour time limit while DEEPNIS completes within seconds to tens of thousands of seconds. On synthetic instances (Figure 1), DEEPNIS also dominates across all matrix classes (Uniform, Block Diagonal, Bernoulli) and sizes tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is DEEPAR (Harviainen & Koivisto, AAAI 2023), the state-of-the-art self-reducible rejection sampler that uses the same depth-16 extended Huber bound and Sinkhorn balancing preprocessing. DEEPNIS wins on every instance where both methods complete, with no ties or losses observed. The speedup is most dramatic at high accuracy (small ε): on cage5, DEEPNIS is 115× faster at ε=0.005 versus only 36× at ε=0.02, confirming the theoretical prediction that rejection sampling's O(ε⁻²) sample complexity is worse than NIS's O(ε⁻¹) regime. For instances with n≥51 (ENZYMES-g575, CAG-mat72, GD95-c), DEEPAR exceeds the 12-hour time limit at all tested ε values while DEEPNIS completes in under 3×10⁴ seconds. The paper also notes that DEEPNIS has higher run-to-run variance (factor of 2.51 between fastest and slowest of 20 runs) compared to DEEPAR (factor of 1.21), attributed to the stochastic stopping rule of Huber & Jones (2019).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work on permanent estimation: sequential importance sampling (SIS), which converges fast empirically but lacks practical stopping rules, and self-reducible rejection sampling (Huber 2006; Harviainen & Koivisto 2023), which provides accuracy guarantees but is slower. The key conceptual move is repurposing the nesting upper bounds that drive rejection sampling's accept/reject mechanism as the proposal distribution for SIS, yielding bounded importance weights and access to optimal [0,1]-valued stopping rules (Dagum et al. 2000; Huber & Jones 2019). The scheme is instantiated with the extended Huber bound and deep bounds (Harviainen et al. 2021), and is positioned as a 'boosted variant' of Huber's rejection sampler that trades exact sampling for reduced sample complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NIS is the first SIS scheme for permanent estimation with provable (ε,δ)-accuracy guarantees and practical stopping rules, closing a gap where prior SIS methods either lacked guarantees or had bounds too loose to be useful. The up-to-two-orders-of-magnitude speedup over the state-of-the-art rejection sampler DEEPAR, with the gap widening as ε decreases, validates the theoretical ε^{-1} vs ε^{-2} scaling advantage. The framework is general—applicable to any self-reducible problem with nesting bounds—opening the door to guaranteed-accuracy integration for other #P-hard counting problems. Open questions include developing tighter analyses for 'naive' nesting bounds (which converge fast empirically but have loose theoretical guarantees) and extending the approach to problems like weighted counting of Bayesian network structures or k-matchings.
