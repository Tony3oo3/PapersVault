## Problem

Existing backdoor defenses specialize in either detecting or removing backdoors, and the few that address both rely on impractical assumptions such as access to task-specific clean/poisoned samples for detection or model retraining for removal. This paper tackles the challenge of achieving both backdoored model detection and backdoor removal simultaneously without either task-specific samples or model retraining, which is critical for real-world scenarios like cloud-based models where users only observe outputs.

## Input / Output

EBBA requires only task-agnostic images (e.g., from the Internet) and black-box access to the model's logits and softmax outputs (last two layers), with no knowledge of model architecture, loss function, or task-specific data. EBBA+ additionally applies J types of image corruptions (Gaussian noise, raindrop effects, division by positive integers) to each image and compares pre- and post-corruption logits. The outputs are: a binary backdoor verdict with the identified target label (EBBA), per-image poisoned/clean classification, and a corrected class label for each poisoned image (EBBA+), enabling simultaneous trigger detection and backdoor removal.

## Method

EBBA exploits the observation (proven via NTK theory in Lemma 1) that a backdoored model's target label consistently ranks among the top softmax outputs even for clean or OOD inputs. It collects a uniformly distributed task-agnostic dataset, zeroes the maximum logit per sample (MZ) to prevent dominant-class energy from masking anomalies, computes a normalized energy score per label (NP_k = (e^{s_k·T}−1)/T), and flags the model as backdoored if any label's aggregate energy exceeds μ + λσ. EBBA+ builds on Lemma 2, which proves that image corruption shifts probability mass from the target label back to the original clean label. It defines a Normalized Transferred Energy (NTE_k = e^{s_k^{dj}−s_k}−1) summed over J corruptions, uses argmax NTE to recover the true label of poisoned images, and applies t-SNE clustering on the target-class subset to separate poisoned from clean images, achieving backdoor removal without any retraining.

## Results

EBBA is the only method that successfully detects backdoored models across all 15 evaluation scenarios (5 attacks × 3 data availability settings), while all baselines fail in at least one setting (Table 2). For poisoned image detection, EBBA+ achieves PDR of 0.95–0.98 and F1 of 0.94–0.97 across CIFAR-10, GTSRB, and ImageNet under all five attacks (Table 3). For backdoor removal, EBBA+ attains the highest Benign Accuracy (BA) among all methods: 89.52–90.02% on CIFAR-10, 98.36–99.06% on GTSRB, and 87.91–88.36% on ImageNet, while keeping Attack Success Rate (ASR) at 1.32–7.88% (Table 4). Crucially, EBBA+ requires neither model retraining nor task-specific clean/poisoned samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For backdoored model detection, EBBA is the only method that works without any poisoned or clean task-specific samples; NC, SCALE, TeCo, and Unicorn all fail in this setting (Table 2). For poisoned image detection, EBBA+ substantially outperforms FTD on invisible attacks (e.g., PDR 0.95 vs. 0.52 for WaNet on CIFAR-10; 0.96 vs. 0.53 for DUBA), while matching or slightly exceeding TeCo and SCALE on visible attacks (PDR 0.95–0.98 vs. 0.92–0.98). For backdoor removal, EBBA+ achieves the highest BA across all datasets and attacks without retraining: on CIFAR-10 it reaches 89.92–90.02% BA vs. 89.72% (RNP, best retraining baseline) and 88.02% (ZIP); on GTSRB it reaches 98.36–99.06% vs. 97.82% (RNP); on ImageNet it reaches 87.91–88.36% vs. 87.15% (ZIP). Notably, retraining-based methods like Fine-Pruning and NAD suffer severe BA drops on advanced attacks (e.g., Fine-Pruning drops to 62.91% BA on ImageNet-WaNet), while EBBA+ maintains competitive BA with ASR below 8% in all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the energy-based OOD detection framework of Liu et al. (NeurIPS 2020) to the backdoor defense setting, extending it with a max-zeroing normalization and a 'transferred energy' formulation that exploits probability shifts under image corruption. Theoretically, it grounds its observations in Neural Tangent Kernel analysis (Jacot et al., 2018), using the NTK representation to prove that the target label's kernel sum dominates other labels even for clean inputs. Conceptually, it departs from prior backdoor defenses (NC, RNP, TeCo, SCALE, ZIP, MEDIC) by eliminating two key assumptions—task-specific samples for detection and model retraining for removal—replacing them with task-agnostic internet images and a corruption-based energy transfer mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling backdoor defense in cloud/API settings where users cannot retrain models or obtain poisoned/clean samples, a scenario the paper argues is the norm in safety-critical deployments (autonomous driving, healthcare). The all-in-one pipeline (model detection → target label identification → poisoned image detection → backdoor removal) without retraining is a meaningful systems-level contribution. Open questions include: the method requires access to full logits (not just final predictions), the NTK-based proofs assume specific kernel forms and may not hold for all architectures, the ASR reduction is only 'comparable' rather than superior to retraining-based methods, and the narrow adaptive-attack vulnerability window (soft-label max between 0.98–0.99) suggests the defense is not fully robust to informed adversaries.
