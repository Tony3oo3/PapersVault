## Problem

Existing change point detection (CPD) algorithms overwhelmingly assume data resides in Euclidean spaces, making them inapplicable to data on Riemannian manifolds where the lack of vector space structure and intrinsic non-linear geometry prevent direct adaptation. The few manifold-specific CPD methods that exist are either parametric, offline, or lack theoretical performance guarantees. This paper addresses the gap by providing a general non-parametric online CPD framework on Riemannian manifolds with rigorous bounds on false alarm and detection rates.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential stream of data points x_t belonging to a Riemannian manifold (concretely, p×p SPD matrices under the affine invariant metric, or k-dimensional subspaces of R^p on the Grassmann manifold) and produces a binary change point flag at each time step. The test statistic g_t is the Riemannian geodesic distance between two online Karcher mean estimates, compared against a threshold ξ that can be set adaptively via recursive quantile estimation. No prior knowledge of the data distribution P(x) is required, and the algorithm operates in a single forward pass over the stream with O(p³) per-step cost for SPD and O(p²k) for Grassmann manifolds.

## Method

The algorithm maintains two stochastic Riemannian gradient descent (R-SGD) estimates of the generalized Karcher mean, each updated with a different constant step size (λ < Λ), so that the smaller-step estimate retains longer memory of the pre-change distribution while the larger-step estimate adapts quickly to post-change data. The CPD statistic is the Riemannian distance d_M(m_{λ,t}, m_{Λ,t}) between these two estimates, flagged as a change point when it exceeds a threshold. The key theoretical contribution is a non-asymptotic convergence result for R-SGD with constant step size, yielding a curvature-dependent linear rate ε = min{1/ζ(κ,D), αμ} that depends on the manifold's sectional curvature lower bound κ and domain diameter D. From this, the paper derives an upper bound on the false alarm rate (Theorem 4.2) and a lower bound on the detection rate (Theorem 4.3) that explicitly trade off step sizes, Karcher variance, gradient noise, and the geodesic separation between pre- and post-change Karcher means. For practical implementation, exponential maps are replaced by second-order retractions, and the framework is instantiated for SPD matrices (using matrix logarithm gradients and a specific retraction) and the Grassmann manifold (using SVD-based gradients and a thin-SVD retraction).

## Results

The paper evaluates performance using Average Run Length (ARL), Mean Detection Delay (MDD), and ROC curves (detection rate vs. false alarm rate) across synthetic and real-world experiments. On synthetic data over both the SPD manifold (p=8) and Grassmann manifold (p=15, k=5), the proposed method achieves a significantly lower MDD for any fixed ARL compared to all four baselines, with ROC curves approaching the ideal (area under curve close to one). On real voice activity detection (TIMIT + QUTNOISE, −3 dB SNR) and skeleton-based action recognition (HDM05, p=93), the proposed method consistently outperforms Scan-B and NEWMA in detection rate at a given false alarm rate, with the advantage being most pronounced at larger ARL values. The method requires approximately 200 samples to converge after a change point on synthetic data and ~80 samples on the action recognition task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines across every experiment. Against Scan-B (Li et al., 2019), a kernel-based Euclidean method adapted by vectorizing SPD matrices or using full Grassmann matrices, the proposed method achieves substantially lower MDD at equivalent ARL on both synthetic manifolds and both real-data tasks; Scan-B only matches or slightly beats the proposed method at very small ARLs (<40) in the action recognition experiment due to sliding-window delay in the proposed method's descriptors. Against NEWMA (Keriven et al., 2020), a two-EWMA Euclidean method using random Fourier features, the proposed method dominates in ROC performance on all four experiments, with the gap widening at higher ARLs; NEWMA and the proposed method show similar MDDs only at very small ARLs in the HDM05 experiment. Against F-CPD (Dubey & Muller, 2020), a manifold-aware but offline single-change-point method adapted here via sliding windows of 64 samples, the proposed method achieves lower MDD on both synthetic manifolds, benefiting from its recursive online operation over long time series. Against NODE (Wang et al., 2023b), a neural-network-based Euclidean density-ratio estimator, the proposed method again achieves lower MDD on both synthetic manifolds, confirming that accounting for intrinsic manifold geometry via R-SGD on the Karcher mean is more effective than learning a density ratio in a flattened Euclidean representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NEWMA two-estimate framework (Keriven et al., 2020), which compares two exponentially weighted moving averages with different forgetting factors for Euclidean change point detection, to Riemannian manifolds by replacing EWMAs with two stochastic Riemannian gradient descent (R-SGD) estimates at different constant step sizes. It generalizes the authors' prior EUSIPCO 2023 work (Wang et al., 2023a), which applied a similar idea to specific manifolds without theoretical guarantees, into a unified framework built around the generalized Karcher mean. The theoretical analysis builds on Bonnabel's (2013) R-SGD formulation and Zhang & Sra's (2016) geodesic convexity results, but introduces a new non-asymptotic convergence bound for constant step sizes that is essential for the online detection setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first general non-parametric online CPD framework for Riemannian manifolds with formal performance guarantees (false alarm and detection rate bounds), filling a gap where prior manifold CPD methods were either parametric, offline, single-change-point, or lacked theory. The curvature-dependent linear convergence rate for R-SGD with constant step size (Theorem 4.1) is a reusable theoretical tool beyond CPD. Key limitations include the requirement for sufficient inter-change-point spacing for convergence, the Hadamard manifold assumption in the theory (though the method works empirically on non-Hadamard manifolds like Grassmann), and the sensitivity of detection delay to manifold curvature. Open questions include extending the analysis to non-convex cost functions on general manifolds and developing adaptive step-size schemes that maintain theoretical guarantees.
