## Problem

In incomplete multi-view data, missing views degrade clustering performance, yet existing generative approaches either integrate recovery and clustering into a single fragile framework or rely on GAN/autoencoder architectures prone to mode collapse and training instability. No prior work has applied diffusion models to missing-view generation, and existing methods like VIGAN and CRA are limited to dual-view settings or suffer from complex, unstable training.

## Input / Output

The model takes the noised target view x^(v)(t), the noise step t, and all available conditional views c^(v) (which may themselves be partially missing, filled with zero vectors) as input, and outputs the predicted noise ε_θ^(v) used to iteratively denoise from pure Gaussian noise back to the recovered view. For image datasets (CelebA-HQ, Carl), raw images are first compressed to 512-dimensional features via per-modality autoencoders. One separate UNet model is trained per target view, and at inference the generated view is plugged into the dataset before any downstream multi-view clustering method is applied.

## Method

DMVG formulates missing-view generation as a conditional denoising diffusion process: the target view is progressively corrupted to pure noise, and a UNet-based noise predictor (with condition views concatenated at the bottleneck and time-step embeddings injected throughout) learns to reverse this process. A key design choice is classifier-free guidance—during training, conditional views are randomly dropped with probability p to enable unconditional learning, and during sampling the final noise estimate combines conditional and unconditional predictions (ω=1), improving generation quality and robustness to partial conditions. For high missing rates, DA-DMVG augments training by synthesizing additional incomplete samples with higher missing rates from existing ones (2^(m−1)−2 new samples per sample with m available views), forcing the model to learn from sparser conditional information. The framework is fully modular: any missing-view generation model can be paired with any multi-view clustering method, decoupling the two sub-tasks.

## Results

On the Carl dataset with 50% missing views, DMVG achieves RMSE of 0.08–0.17 and PSNR of 15.53–18.95 dB across three views, substantially outperforming CRA (RMSE 0.14–0.20, PSNR 13.80–15.53) and DCP (RMSE 0.60–0.67, PSNR 3.42–4.49). For the downstream IMVC task on Caltech7 at 50% missing rate, DA-DMVG as a preprocessing step improves clustering accuracy in 96% of cases; the most dramatic gain is DAD+IMSR, which jumps from 24.60% to 69.20% ACC. Across all four IMVC datasets, DMVG/DA-DMVG improves clustering in approximately 76% of experiments, with the largest relative gains on Handwritten (70% of cases) and Caltech7 (96% of cases).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMVG consistently outperforms VIGAN on Multi-Modal CelebA-HQ (e.g., PSNR 12.34 vs 12.10 for RGB, 10.76 vs 9.57 for sketch at 10% missing), while VIGAN suffers from mode collapse producing unusable outputs. Against CRA on Carl and NH-face, DMVG achieves lower RMSE and higher PSNR across all missing rates (10%, 30%, 50%); for example, on Carl thermal view at 50% missing, DMVG reaches PSNR 18.95 vs CRA's 15.53. DCP, which couples generation with clustering, produces far worse generation quality (RMSE ~0.6 vs DMVG's ~0.1) because it optimizes for clustering accuracy rather than faithful view recovery. For the downstream IMVC task, DA-DMVG as a preprocessing step improves most baselines: DAD+IMSR on Caltech7 at 50% missing gains +44.60 ACC (24.60→69.20), DAD+OPIMC gains +12.24 (44.12→56.36), and DAD+BSV gains +10.75 (38.31→49.06). However, a few exceptions exist where DAD+ slightly degrades performance (e.g., DAD+DAIMC on Caltech7: 44.89→44.44; DAD+OPIMC on BBCSport: 44.34→33.10), which the authors attribute to noise introduced by imperfect generation outweighing the benefit of filled views, particularly on small datasets like BBCSport.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to apply conditional diffusion models (building on DDPM and classifier-free guidance) to the task of missing-view generation in multi-view data, departing from prior GAN-based (VIGAN) and autoencoder-based (CRA) approaches. Conceptually, it also departs from generative IMVC methods (AIMC, GP-MVC, COMPLETER, DCP) that jointly optimize missing-view recovery and clustering in a single framework, instead decomposing the problem into two independent sub-tasks: a dedicated missing-view generation model followed by any standard multi-view learning method. The classifier-free guidance mechanism is repurposed from text-to-image generation to handle the three practical condition scenarios (full, partial, no condition) arising from random view missingness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular decomposition means any future improvement in missing-view generation can be immediately plugged into any multi-view clustering pipeline, lowering the barrier for the community. The data augmentation strategy (DA-DMVG) addresses a practical gap—high missing rates where the model over-relies on a few strongly correlated views—and shows consistent gains over simply training longer. However, the paper acknowledges that generated noise can degrade clustering in some cases, and that small datasets (e.g., BBCSport) may not benefit, leaving open the question of how to minimize imputation noise while preserving discriminative structure. Future work on noise-aware clustering or uncertainty-aware generation could close this gap.
