## Problem

Current model-based generalist agents like LEXA guide their dreaming (imagination) with naive strategies such as random i.i.d. sampling from the replay buffer, which is inefficient in large or complex state spaces. The paper asks whether and how an agent can 'dream better' in a more structured, strategic way, drawing on cognitive science evidence that humans use spatial divide-and-conquer strategies when navigating complex environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240689/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives 64×64×3 pixel observations from partially observable egocentric views (limited top-down in 2D navigation, first-person in 3D mazes) and, at test time, an unseen pixel-level goal image. It outputs a sequence of discrete actions for navigation or manipulation. Environments are non-episodic, requiring continuous exploration without resets, and success is measured by reaching within a 0.1 Manhattan distance (plus 45° orientation tolerance in 3D) within a step budget.

## Method

Dr. Strategy introduces 'Strategic Dreaming,' a divide-and-conquer framework built on a DreamerV2 RSSM world model. A VQ-VAE quantizes model states into N discrete latent landmarks (cluster centers partitioning the state space), and three policies are trained entirely in imagination: a Highway policy (landmark-conditioned, navigating to landmarks), an Explorer (curiosity-driven via ensemble disagreement), and an Achiever (goal-conditioned, trained with Focused Sampling that pairs nearby states within T_S steps rather than random pairs). During exploration, the agent evaluates curiosity only at landmarks (not random states), selects a 'Curious Landmark,' uses the Highway policy to reach it, then resumes exploration from that high-potential location. At test time, it finds the landmark nearest the goal, drives there via the Highway policy, then switches to the Achiever for precise short-range navigation—realizing the divide-and-conquer strategy end-to-end.

## Results

On 2D Navigation, Dr. Strategy achieves near-perfect zero-shot success rates of 94.03% (9-room) and 96.50% (spiral 9-room), and 67.11% (25-room), substantially outperforming the best baseline GC-Director (28.08%, 30.45%, 27.11%). On 3D-Maze Navigation, it reaches 86.66% on Maze-7x7 and 44.44% on Maze-15x15, surpassing LEXA (51.11%, 41.20%). On RoboKitchen (robot manipulation), Dr. Strategy achieves 19.44%, slightly below LEXA's 24.30%, indicating the divide-and-conquer strategy is most beneficial for long-horizon navigation rather than short-horizon manipulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dr. Strategy dramatically outperforms LEXA on 2D Navigation (94.03% vs. 19.75% on 9-room; 67.11% vs. 9.62% on 25-room) and 3D-Maze 7x7 (86.66% vs. 51.11%), with the gap widening as environment complexity increases. Against LEXA-Explore, the advantage is even more pronounced on 25-room (67.11% vs. 0.14%), where the non-strategic achiever completely fails. GC-Director, a goal-conditioned hierarchical variant of Director, is the strongest baseline on 2D Navigation (28.08–30.45%) but still trails Dr. Strategy by over 60 percentage points; it collapses on RoboKitchen (1.45%) due to the stationary third-person view limiting landmark distinguishability. On RoboKitchen, Dr. Strategy (19.44%) is slightly below LEXA (24.30%) and LEXA-Explore (20.07%), suggesting the strategic divide-and-conquer approach offers diminishing returns for short-horizon manipulation tasks where the need for long-range navigation is minimal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LEXA framework (Mendonca et al., 2021), a model-based generalist agent that trains an Explorer and Achiever through imagination in a DreamerV2 world model, by introducing a divide-and-conquer strategy called 'strategic dreaming.' It departs from LEXA's naive random-sampling-based dreaming by learning discrete latent landmarks via VQ-VAE and training a dedicated Highway policy to navigate between them, drawing conceptual inspiration from human spatial navigation strategies in cognitive science. The approach also relates to PEG (Hu et al., 2023), which adds strategic exploration to LEXA, and to Director (Hafner et al., 2022), a hierarchical MBRL agent, but uniquely combines strategic exploration, strategic achievement, and Focused Sampling within a single unsupervised goal-conditioned framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that structuring the dreaming process with a divide-and-conquer strategy yields large gains in long-horizon navigation (nearly 100% vs. ~20% success in 2D rooms), and the ablation revealing that Focused Sampling is the critical component (without it, performance collapses to the no-strategic-achievement level) provides a clear mechanistic insight into why local precision matters more than global exploration strategy. However, the gains diminish in visually complex 3D mazes (where landmark confusion occurs) and vanish in short-horizon manipulation (RoboKitchen), suggesting the approach is most beneficial for spatially extended, partially observable tasks. Open questions include adaptive landmark counts, hierarchical highway policies, and whether the strategy transfers to more complex manipulation or open-ended skill discovery settings.
