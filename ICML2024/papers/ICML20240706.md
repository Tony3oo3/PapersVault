## Problem

Existing kernel methods (RKHS, vvRKHS) and neural networks are restricted to complex- or vector-valued outputs, making it difficult to model structured data such as functions, operators, and matrices, or to unify multiple models in ensemble, multitask, and meta-learning settings. The rich algebraic structure of C*-algebras—natural generalizations of complex numbers to functions and operators—had not been systematically applied to machine learning, leaving a gap between operator-algebra theory and practical ML frameworks.

## Input / Output

For kernel methods, the input is a C*-algebra-valued vector (e.g., d functions or d matrices) and the output is a C*-algebra-valued function learned via kernel ridge regression in an RKHM. For the C*-algebra net, the input is a C*-algebra-valued vector x ∈ A^{d₀}, and the output is a family of scalar-valued networks {f_z(x)}_{z∈Z} indexed by a compact space Z; a single scalar-valued prediction is obtained by integrating over Z with respect to a probability measure P. In practice, the infinite-dimensional C*-algebra is discretized to a finite-dimensional subspace spanned by a basis {v₁,…,v_m}, so each weight parameter is represented by m complex coefficients.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary C*-algebraic generalizations. First, it extends RKHS to Reproducing Kernel Hilbert C*-modules (RKHMs) by defining C*-algebra-valued positive definite kernels and the associated A-valued inner product, enabling learning of function- and operator-valued maps and kernel mean embeddings of A-valued measures (e.g., positive operator-valued measures). Second, it introduces the C*-algebra net, where all weight matrices and biases are elements of C(Z) for a compact Hausdorff space Z, so a single network simultaneously encodes infinitely many scalar-valued networks parameterized by z. Two new theoretical results are established: (i) even with linear activations, the C*-algebra net f_z(x) is a degree-L polynomial in the basis functions v_l(z), so expressiveness in z grows with depth; and (ii) by choosing Z = W^N and optimizing over probability measures P on Z, the nonconvex weight-optimization problem of a standard network is recast as a convex problem over P, filling the gap between convex and nonconvex neural network optimization. The noncommutative product structure of C*-algebras (e.g., B(L²(Z)) or group C*-algebras) further induces interactions among the constituent networks and enables group-equivariant architectures.

## Results

This is a position paper with no empirical benchmarks or quantitative performance tables. The key theoretical contributions are: (1) Proposition 5.2, which shows that a C*-algebra net with linear activation functions still produces a degree-L polynomial in the basis functions v_l(z), meaning expressiveness with respect to z grows with depth L even without nonlinear activations; and (2) Proposition A.2, which proves that optimizing the probability measure P over the C*-algebra net's parameter space yields a convex optimization problem, bridging the gap between convex (learning P) and nonconvex (learning individual weights) formulations of neural network training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This position paper does not include empirical comparisons against baseline methods. It is a conceptual and theoretical proposal rather than an experimental study. The paper positions C*-algebraic ML as a unifying framework that subsumes existing approaches (RKHS, vvRKHS, standard neural networks) rather than competing with them on a benchmark. The closest 'comparison' is the theoretical observation that the C*-algebra net with a singleton Z reduces to a standard scalar-valued neural network (Proposition A.1), establishing equivalence in the degenerate case, and that the operator norm in C*-algebras yields milder dimension dependence in generalization bounds compared to the Hilbert-Schmidt norm when treating matrices as vectors (Hasimoto et al., 2023b).

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper unifies and extends the authors' prior line of work on reproducing kernel Hilbert C*-modules (RKHMs) and C*-algebra nets into a single algebraic framework for machine learning. It builds directly on Heo (2008) for the RKHM construction, on the classical RKHS and kernel mean embedding literature (Schölkopf & Smola 2001; Muandet et al. 2017), and on the authors' own 2021–2023 papers that introduced C*-algebra-valued kernels, kernel mean embeddings of operator-valued measures, and the C*-algebra net architecture. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the elevation of C*-algebra from a specialized tool in quantum mechanics to a general-purpose algebraic substrate that simultaneously generalizes complex scalars, vectors, functions, and operators, thereby unifying kernel methods and neural networks under one roof.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete set of open challenges that will determine whether C*-algebraic ML moves beyond theory: computational cost scales quadratically with the C*-algebra dimension, the absence of inverses in C*-algebras breaks standard Hilbert-space arguments (e.g., only an approximate representer theorem is available), and no software ecosystem exists yet. The most practically promising directions flagged are using the operator norm to obtain dimension-independent generalization bounds for kernel ridge regression, and the convex-optimization bridge (Subsection 5.2.2) that reframes nonconvex neural network training as a convex problem over probability measures on a C*-algebra. For the broader ML community, the paper's main value is as a roadmap: it shows where C*-algebraic structure could address few-shot learning, federated learning, and structured-data problems that current architectures handle only ad hoc.
