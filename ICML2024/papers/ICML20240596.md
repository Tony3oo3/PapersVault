## Problem

LoRA reduces trainable parameters for fine-tuning large foundation models but still requires substantial storage (e.g., 33.5M parameters for LLaMA2-7B), creating high infrastructure costs for communities hosting many adapters and limiting individual user customization on mobile devices. The paper asks how to aggressively compress trainable parameters even further by moving beyond the low-rank structure that LoRA relies on.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained weight matrices W₀ ∈ R^{d₁×d₂} (e.g., 768×768 for RoBERTa Base, 4096×4096 for LLaMA2-7B) and produces a same-shaped weight change ΔW. The trainable parameters consist of a shared, frozen 2×n spectral entry matrix E and per-layer coefficient vectors c ∈ R^n, totaling n×(2+L_t) parameters. At inference, ΔW is merged into W₀ so the fine-tuned model processes standard text sequences or 224×224 RGB images with no additional latency, producing class labels or generated text.

## Method

FourierFT replaces LoRA's low-rank decomposition (ΔW = BA) with a sparse spectral parameterization. It randomly selects n spectral entries from the d₁×d₂ frequency grid (shared across all L_t layers via a frozen 2×n matrix E), then learns only n real-valued coefficients per layer at those positions. The forward pass constructs a dense spectral matrix F (zeros everywhere except at the selected entries), applies the inverse discrete Fourier transform to recover ΔW in the spatial domain, and scales it by a hyperparameter α before adding to the pre-trained weight. The key insight is that the orthogonal Fourier basis provides sufficient expressiveness to recover informative weight changes from extremely sparse spectral information, even for non-frequency-sparse matrices. This yields n×(2+L_t) trainable parameters versus LoRA's 2×d×L_t×r, achieving 10–50× parameter reduction while matching or exceeding LoRA performance across NLU, NLG, instruction tuning, and image classification tasks.

## Results

On instruction tuning with LLaMA2-7B, FourierFT achieves a Vicuna score of 7.49 and MT-Bench score of 5.18 using only 0.064M trainable parameters, compared to LoRA's 33.5M parameters (a 523× reduction), while matching or exceeding LoRA on Vicuna (7.49 vs. 7.35). On the GLUE benchmark with RoBERTa Base, FourierFT (0.024M params) achieves an average of 85.0, comparable to LoRA (0.3M params, 85.2) and outperforming all adapter variants. On the E2E NLG task with GPT-2 Medium, FourierFT (0.048M params) attains BLEU 69.1, NIST 8.82, and METEOR 47.0, surpassing LoRA (0.35M params) on all three metrics. For image classification with ViT Base, FourierFT (72K params) achieves 77.75% average accuracy, matching LoRA (581K params, 77.58%) with only 12.4% of the parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FourierFT most dramatically outperforms LoRA in parameter efficiency: on instruction tuning it uses only 0.2% of LoRA's parameters (0.064M vs. 33.5M for LLaMA2-7B) while matching or exceeding its Vicuna score (7.49 vs. 7.35). On the GLUE benchmark, FourierFT (85.0 avg, RoBERTa Base) ties LoRA (85.2) and AdaLoRA (85.0) while using only 8% of their parameters, and clearly beats DyLoRA (84.5) and all adapter variants (83.0–84.2). On E2E NLG, FourierFT surpasses LoRA on BLEU (69.1 vs. 68.9), NIST (8.82 vs. 8.76), and METEOR (47.0 vs. 46.6) for GPT-2 Medium with 13.7% of LoRA's parameters. For image classification, FourierFT matches LoRA's average accuracy (77.75% vs. 77.58% for ViT Base) with 12.4% of the parameters, and slightly outperforms Full Fine-tuning on OxfordPets and DTD with ViT Large. The one case where FourierFT trails is BitFit on RoBERTa Base GLUE (85.0 vs. 85.4 avg), though BitFit only tunes bias vectors and is not a weight-based method.

## Relation to Prior Work

FourierFT builds directly on the LoRA paradigm of learning only the weight change ΔW, but replaces LoRA's low-rank spatial-domain factorization (ΔW = BA) with a frequency-domain representation: it learns a small set of sparse spectral coefficients and recovers ΔW via inverse discrete Fourier transform. This departs from the entire low-rank PEFT lineage (LoRA, AdaLoRA, DyLoRA, Q-LoRA) by exploiting the orthogonal expressiveness of the Fourier basis rather than rank constraints. The approach is conceptually grounded in sparse Fourier transform and compressed sensing literature, where a small number of spectral entries can faithfully reconstruct signals even when the data is not inherently frequency-sparse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a LLaMA2-7B adapter shrinks from ~131 MB (LoRA r=64) to ~250 KB (FourierFT n=1000), making storage and distribution of fine-tuned checkpoints nearly trivial for community platforms and mobile deployment. The method is simple to implement (a single IDFT call per forward pass) and scales favorably with model width since its parameter count is independent of d. Open questions include whether making the favored central frequency f_c trainable (suggested by ablations) could further improve performance, whether the Fourier basis is truly optimal compared to learned or task-specific bases, and whether the approach extends naturally to non-square weight matrices or multi-modal architectures.
