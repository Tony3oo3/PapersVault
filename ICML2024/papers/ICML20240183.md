## Problem

Prior analyses of differentially private gradient descent for linear regression required Ω(p^{3/2}) samples for nontrivial error bounds, far exceeding the O(p) rate of the non-private OLS estimator. Additionally, existing confidence interval methods for DP-trained models (e.g., Shejwalkar et al., 2022; Rabanser et al., 2023) lacked formal coverage guarantees. This paper closes both gaps by showing that standard DP-GD with fixed clipping achieves O(p) sample complexity and by providing the first provable, non-asymptotic coverage guarantees for instance-specific confidence intervals.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a dataset (X, y) ∈ R^{n×p} × R^n together with hyperparameters (clipping threshold γ, noise scale λ, step size η, number of iterations T, and initial vector θ₀) and outputs the full sequence of iterates θ₁, …, θ_T. The final iterate serves as the regression parameter estimate, while per-coordinate confidence intervals are extracted from the iterates using three strategies: independent runs, time-separated checkpoints, or batched means. The intervals are instance-specific—their width adapts to the observed variability of the algorithm on the particular dataset rather than relying on a fixed theoretical bound.

## Method

The algorithm is standard full-batch DP-GD: at each step it computes the squared-loss gradient, clips individual per-sample gradients to threshold γ, adds Gaussian noise calibrated to achieve ρ-zCDP, and updates the iterate. The key technical contribution is a closed-form characterization of the iterate distribution: under a deterministic 'No-Clipping Condition' on the data, the t-th iterate is Gaussian centered at the OLS solution plus an exponentially decaying bias term, with covariance governed by the step size and empirical covariance matrix. They prove this condition holds with high probability for Gaussian random-design data, yielding the O(p) sample complexity. For confidence intervals, they exploit the resulting Gaussian-process structure of the iterates and prove non-asymptotic coverage by bounding the total variation distance between the actual (correlated) iterate distribution and the ideal independent-Gaussian case, using a coupling argument between the clipped and unclipped algorithms.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 2.7) shows that DP-GD with standard (non-adaptive) gradient clipping achieves error $\|\hat{\theta} - \theta_T\| \leq c'\ln^4(n\rho/\beta p) \cdot \sigma p/(\sqrt{\rho}\, n)$ with $n = \tilde{\Omega}(p)$ samples, matching the linear dimension-dependence of OLS. Empirically (Figure 1), DP-GD and OLS both require $n$ to grow linearly with $p$ to maintain fixed error, while AdaSSP requires $n = \Omega(p^{3/2})$. The paper further shows that at reasonable sample sizes, the sampling error ($\|\hat{\theta}-\theta^*\| \propto 1/\sqrt{n}$) dominates the privacy noise ($\|\theta_T - \hat{\theta}\| \propto 1/n$), yielding privacy 'for free' in high dimensions. Confidence interval constructions achieve valid empirical coverage (~95%) with widths comparable to standard non-private OLS intervals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary empirical comparison is against AdaSSP (Wang, 2018), a popular sufficient-statistics-perturbation algorithm. In the iso-accuracy experiments (Figure 1), DP-GD and OLS both require $n$ to scale linearly with dimension $p$ to achieve expected $\ell_2$-error of 1/2, while AdaSSP's required sample size grows as $p^{3/2}$, making it substantially worse in high dimensions. The paper's DP-GD also matches the $\tilde{O}(p)$ sample complexity of recent adaptive-clipping methods (Varshney et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2023) but with a simpler algorithm that uses a fixed clipping threshold $\gamma = \tilde{\Theta}(\sigma\sqrt{p})$ rather than private quantile-based adaptive clipping. Compared to the prior DP-GD analysis of Cai et al. (2021), which only applies when $n = \Omega(p^{3/2})$, this paper's analysis extends the valid regime down to $n = \tilde{\Omega}(p)$. The confidence interval constructions are compared against non-private OLS intervals: their widths are roughly comparable, and they achieve valid ~95% empirical coverage across coordinates, whereas prior DP-GD checkpoint methods (Shejwalkar et al., 2022; Rabanser et al., 2023) provide no formal coverage guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper re-analyzes the standard DP-GD algorithm (Abadi et al., 2016) for linear regression, showing that a clean Gaussian characterization of the iterates—available when no gradients are clipped—yields O(p) sample complexity, matching the best adaptive-clipping algorithms (Varshney et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2023) without their added complexity. It departs from prior DP-GD analyses (Cai et al., 2021) that were limited to Ω(p^{3/2}) samples by proving that fixed clipping at γ ≈ √p suffices under mild data conditions. For confidence intervals, it extends the heuristic checkpoint-based approaches of Shejwalkar et al. (2022) and Rabanser et al. (2023) by providing the first formal finite-sample coverage guarantees, leveraging the Gaussian-process structure of the iterates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that vanilla DP-GD with fixed clipping achieves the same O(p) sample complexity as sophisticated adaptive-clipping algorithms simplifies the practical landscape for private linear regression and suggests the adaptive machinery may be unnecessary in well-specified settings. The instance-specific confidence intervals with provable coverage fill a gap in private statistical inference, enabling practitioners to interpret model parameters with calibrated uncertainty. Key limitations include the requirement for well-conditioned, Gaussian-concentrated data (polynomial dependence on condition number remains), the restriction to synthetic experiments, and the focus on the empirical rather than population parameter—each pointing to natural extensions for future work.
