## Problem

Existing adversarial camouflage methods for vehicle detectors suffer from two key limitations: world-align-based approaches cannot guarantee consistent texture projection between generation and evaluation, while UV-map-based approaches cannot render environmental characteristics such as lighting and weather. Additionally, all prior methods neglect diverse weather conditions during camouflage generation, causing attacks to fail under foggy or low-light scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242568/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D vehicle mesh (Audi E-Tron), camera pose parameters, and a multi-weather dataset of 40,960 photo-realistic CARLA images spanning 16 weather conditions (4 sun altitudes × 4 fog densities) as input, and produces an adversarial UV texture map that wraps the vehicle surface. The texture is optimized via back-propagation through a white-box YOLOv3 detector and evaluated against five additional black-box detectors. For real-world deployment, the UV map is printed and physically applied to a 1:12 scale Audi E-Tron model.

## Method

RAUCA introduces Neural Renderer Plus (NRP), which combines a Neural Renderer (NR) for accurate UV-map-based texture projection with an Environment Feature Extractor (EFE), an encoder-decoder network that learns to transfer environmental characteristics (lighting, shadows, weather) from reference images onto the rendered vehicle. The EFE outputs two feature maps fused with NR's output via pixel-wise multiplication and addition, producing a photo-realistic rendered image that preserves both texture accuracy and environmental realism. A multi-weather CARLA dataset with varied sun altitude and fog density is used during texture optimization to enhance robustness. The attack loss combines IoU between the detection box and ground truth, class confidence, and objectiveness score, weighted to focus on boxes overlapping the target, while a smooth loss ensures human-perceptible texture consistency. NRP is pre-trained with a viewpoint-weighted BCE loss (weight W = h×w/s) to balance rendering accuracy across camera distances before the adversarial texture is optimized.

## Results

On the multi-weather CARLA dataset, RAUCA achieves the lowest car AP@0.5 across all six detectors, indicating the strongest attack. On the white-box YOLOv3, RAUCA reduces AP@0.5 to 0.304, a ~13% improvement over the best baseline ACTIVE (0.439). On black-box detectors, RAUCA achieves AP@0.5 of 0.611 (YOLOX), 0.285 (DDTR), 0.449 (DRCN), 0.384 (SRCN), and 0.406 (FRRCN), outperforming all baselines by roughly 6–10%. In the unseen-weather transferability test (Table 2), RAUCA further extends its lead, reaching 0.369 on YOLOv3 versus 0.594 for ACTIVE (a 23% gap).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAUCA outperforms all four baselines on every detector in both the multi-weather and unseen-weather evaluations. Against the strongest baseline ACTIVE (ICCV 2023), RAUCA reduces YOLOv3 AP@0.5 from 0.439 to 0.304 (−13.5%) and DDTR from 0.384 to 0.285 (−9.9%), with consistent 6–10% gains on the remaining detectors. DTA (CVPR 2022) is the second-best baseline on YOLOv3 (0.507) but is surpassed by RAUCA by 20.3 percentage points; on DDTR, DTA (0.341) is actually closer to RAUCA (0.285) than ACTIVE is, yet RAUCA still leads. FCA (AAAI 2022) and DAS (CVPR 2021) are substantially weaker: FCA's inability to render environmental characteristics limits it to only marginally better-than-random performance, while DAS's partial-coverage design leaves large portions of the vehicle uncamouflaged. In the real-world fog experiment (Table 3), DTA and ACTIVE slightly edge out RAUCA (0.158/0.163 vs. 0.200), which the authors attribute to difficulty maintaining consistent fog density; however, RAUCA dominates in all other real-world conditions (noon, afternoon, night) and achieves the best overall average (0.195 vs. 0.231 for ACTIVE).

## Relation to Prior Work

RAUCA sits at the intersection of two lineages in adversarial camouflage: UV-map-based methods (DAS, FCA) that optimize 3D textures directly but cannot render environmental effects, and world-align-based methods (DTA, ACTIVE) that use encoder-decoder networks to capture lighting and shadows but suffer from inconsistent texture projection between generation and evaluation. The paper's Neural Renderer Plus (NRP) merges these by using the Neural 3D Mesh Renderer (Kato et al., 2018) for accurate UV-map projection while borrowing the encoder-decoder environmental feature extraction from DTA/ACTIVE. The key conceptual departure is the co-design of the renderer with a multi-weather dataset, arguing that neither component alone suffices for robust physical attacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Physical adversarial camouflage against vehicle detectors is a direct safety concern for autonomous driving, and the consistent ~10% AP@0.5 improvement across six diverse detectors (single- and two-stage) in both simulation and real-world settings makes this a practically relevant advance. The finding that multi-weather data actually degrades performance when paired with a renderer that cannot model environmental effects (Table 5) highlights an important co-design principle for future differentiable-rendering-based attacks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the single Audi E-Tron model, how defenses (e.g., adversarial training) would fare against multi-weather camouflage, and whether the simulation-to-real gap can be further closed without manual fog simulation.
