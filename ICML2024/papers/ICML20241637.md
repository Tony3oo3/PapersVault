## Problem

Existing zero-shot diffusion-based solvers for noisy linear inverse problems (DPS, IIGDM, DDNM, DiffPIR) lack a unified theoretical interpretation and all implicitly rely on hand-crafted isotropic Gaussian covariance to approximate the intractable denoising posterior p_t(x_0|x_t). This suboptimal covariance choice limits reconstruction quality and forces practitioners to tune hyperparameters (e.g., guidance strength ζ in DPS, weight λ in DiffPIR) per task. The paper asks whether a principled, data-driven posterior covariance can replace these ad-hoc choices while remaining plug-and-play with pre-trained models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a noisy measurement y ∈ R^m, a known linear degradation operator A ∈ R^{m×d}, and a pre-trained unconditional diffusion model (e.g., DDPM on FFHQ or ImageNet at 256×256). It outputs a reconstructed clean image x_0 ∈ R^d. A key practical detail is that the optimized covariance is obtained without retraining the diffusion model: either by converting existing reverse variance predictions via a closed-form formula (Eq. 22), by offline Monte Carlo estimation of the expected reconstruction error, or by a lightweight DWT-domain variance head trained on 5% of the dataset. The DWT variant reduces covariance parameters from O(d²) to O(d), making it tractable for high-resolution images.

## Method

The paper first proves that DPS, IIGDM, DDNM, and DiffPIR are all equivalent to approximating the conditional posterior mean E[x_0|x_t,y] using an isotropic Gaussian N(x_0 | D_t(x_t), r_t²I) for the denoising posterior, differing only in their choice of r_t. It then replaces this hand-crafted isotropic covariance with an optimized one obtained by maximizing the log-likelihood of a variational Gaussian q_t(x_0|x_t) = N(x_0 | D_t(x_t), Σ_t(x_t)), yielding three plug-and-play strategies: (1) converting pre-trained DDPM reverse variances to posterior variances via a fixed-point relation (Theorem 4.2), (2) Monte Carlo estimation of a signal-independent isotropic variance, and (3) learning a diagonal variance in the DWT domain to capture pixel correlations at O(d) cost. The optimized covariance is then injected into both Type I guidance (likelihood score approximation) and Type II guidance (auto-weighted proximal problem), with efficient evaluation via closed-form solutions for isotropic cases or conjugate gradient for general covariances, eliminating the need for per-task hyperparameter tuning.

## Results

On FFHQ, the proposed Convert method achieves the best LPIPS across all four tasks (0.0794 inpainting, 0.1836 Gaussian deblur, 0.2156 motion deblur, 0.1962 4× SR) and best FID for inpainting (25.90) and Gaussian deblur (52.42). On ImageNet, Convert achieves the best FID for inpainting (29.14) and Gaussian deblur (95.23), while DPS slightly edges it on SR LPIPS (0.3231 vs. 0.3477) and FID (92.89 vs. 96.76). The DWT-Var variant (learned covariance in wavelet domain) achieves the best SR LPIPS on FFHQ (0.1856) and outperforms optimally-tuned DiffPIR by a significant margin on Type II guidance without any hyperparameter tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed Convert method (converting reverse variances to posterior variances) outperforms DPS by large margins on deblurring tasks: on FFHQ motion deblur, Convert achieves FID 62.88 vs. DPS's 212.48, and on ImageNet motion deblur, FID 109.61 vs. 282.21. DPS slightly wins on ImageNet inpainting SSIM (0.8623 vs. 0.8559) and SR LPIPS/FID (0.3231/92.89 vs. 0.3477/96.76), but the paper notes DPS performance is highly unstable across hyperparameters. IIGDM is competitive on deblurring (e.g., FFHQ Gaussian deblur SSIM 0.7890 vs. 0.7905) but lags significantly on inpainting (FFHQ FID 49.89 vs. 25.90) and is far worse on ImageNet inpainting (FID 64.96 vs. 29.14). TMPD (Tweedie moment projected diffusion) is consistently the weakest baseline, with FID values 2–10× worse than the proposed methods across all tasks. For Type II guidance, the DWT-Var method outperforms optimally-tuned DiffPIR by a significant margin on LPIPS without requiring any hyperparameter tuning, while Convert and Analytic achieve comparable performance to the best DiffPIR setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides a unified theoretical lens showing that four distinct zero-shot diffusion-based solvers—DPS, IIGDM, DDNM, and DiffPIR—are all instances of the same underlying operation: approximating the intractable denoising posterior p_t(x_0|x_t) with an isotropic Gaussian N(x_0 | D_t(x_t), r_t²I) and using its mean to approximate the conditional posterior mean E[x_0|x_t, y]. The paper departs from this line by replacing the hand-crafted isotropic covariance with a principled MLE-optimized covariance, and provides plug-and-play mechanisms (converting DDPM reverse variances via Theorem 4.2, Monte Carlo estimation, or DWT-domain learning) that require no retraining of the pre-trained diffusion model. This positions the work as a direct extension of the Nichol & Dhariwal (2021) reverse-variance framework and the Karras et al. (2022) ODE formulation, while conceptually subsuming the guidance strategies of Chung et al. (2023a), Song et al. (2023), Wang et al. (2023), and Zhu et al. (2023) under a single variational-inference objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified interpretation gives the community a common language for comparing and combining zero-shot inverse-problem solvers, and the MLE-based covariance eliminates the need for per-task hyperparameter tuning (e.g., DPS's ζ or DiffPIR's λ), which is a significant practical simplification. The DWT-domain parameterization addresses the O(d²) cost of full covariance prediction, making the approach scalable to high-resolution images. Key limitations remain: the diagonal (or DWT-diagonal) constraint prevents exact recovery of the optimal covariance, spatial variance is only reliable at low noise levels (σ_t < 0.2), and the method still relies on a pre-trained unconditional model. Open questions include whether full-rank or structured (non-diagonal) covariances can be learned efficiently at scale, and whether nonlinear transforms beyond DWT (e.g., learned wavelet or neural codecs) could further reduce residual correlations.
