## Problem

PnP-ADMM methods typically assume the deep denoiser prior is trained on the same distribution as the test data, yet in practice distribution shifts between source and target domains are common and have been largely overlooked in the PnP literature. No prior convergence analysis of PnP-ADMM accounts for a mismatched denoiser under nonconvex data-fidelity terms and without nonexpansiveness constraints on the denoiser.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241838/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a corrupted measurement (a downsampled image with blur kernel for super-resolution, or coded diffraction patterns for phase retrieval) and a pre-trained DRUNet denoiser operating on 256×256 patches as the implicit image prior. It iteratively alternates a proximal data-fidelity step with a denoiser update to produce a reconstructed clean image. Unusually, the denoiser need not be trained on the target distribution; it can be a mismatched prior optionally fine-tuned with as few as 4 target-domain images.

## Method

The paper derives a nonconvex convergence guarantee for PnP-ADMM where the denoiser is a mismatched MMSE estimator, bounding the per-iteration distance δ_k between the target and mismatched denoiser outputs. Using the augmented Lagrangian as a Lyapunov function, the analysis shows the minimum gradient norm over t iterations is bounded by O(1/t) plus an error term A₂·ε̄_t that depends on the denoiser mismatch, without requiring convex data-fidelity or nonexpansive denoisers. For domain adaptation, the mismatched DRUNet is fine-tuned on a small number of target-domain samples to reduce δ_k, and the paper demonstrates empirically that even 4 adapted images substantially close the reconstruction performance gap on both super-resolution and phase retrieval tasks.

## Results

On the MetFaces super-resolution test set (s=4, averaged over 8 kernels), the target denoiser (MetFaces) achieves 31.29 dB PSNR and 0.8071 SSIM, while the worst mismatched prior (BreCaHAD, cell images) drops to 28.70 dB PSNR and 0.7010 SSIM — a gap of ~2.6 dB. The best mismatched prior (CelebA, facial images) reaches 30.96 dB PSNR and 0.7988 SSIM, showing that distributional similarity to the target matters. Domain adaptation with just 4 target images recovers most of the gap (30.71 dB PSNR), and 64 images nearly close it (31.05 dB PSNR vs. 31.29 dB target).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against other PnP algorithms; instead, it compares PnP-ADMM with different denoiser priors (trained on MetFaces, CelebA, AFHQ, RxRx1, BreCaHAD) as the 'baselines.' The target MetFaces denoiser consistently outperforms all mismatched priors across both super-resolution and phase retrieval tasks. The performance ranking of mismatched priors correlates with visual similarity to the target: CelebA (faces) > AFHQ (faces) > RxRx1 (fluorescence microscopy) > BreCaHAD (histopathology), with BreCaHAD losing ~2.5 dB PSNR on average. Domain adaptation experiments show that fine-tuning the BreCaHAD denoiser on just 4 MetFaces images recovers ~1.5 dB of the 2.5 dB gap, and 64 images recover ~2.1 dB, nearly matching the target. For phase retrieval at 20 dB input SNR, the BreCaHAD prior achieves 25.86 dB PSNR vs. 29.88 dB for the target, and adaptation with 64 images closes most of this gap (29.60 dB).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PnP-ADMM framework (Sreehari et al., 2016; Venkatakrishnan et al., 2013) by providing the first convergence analysis that explicitly accounts for a mismatched MMSE denoiser, building on the MMSE-to-regularizer connection from Gribonval (2011) and the nonconvex PnP-PGM analysis of Xu et al. (2020). Unlike prior PnP-ADMM convergence results (Ryu et al., 2019; Sun et al., 2021; Chan, 2019) that require convex data-fidelity terms and/or nonexpansive or bounded denoisers, the analysis here accommodates nonconvex data-fidelity and expansive denoisers. The domain adaptation strategy (fine-tuning a pre-trained denoiser on a few target-domain samples) is a straightforward application of transfer learning to the PnP setting, distinct from inference-time adaptation methods previously explored in imaging (Tirer & Giryes, 2019a; Dou et al., 2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Distribution shift between training and deployment data is a practical reality in imaging (e.g., a denoiser trained on natural images applied to biomedical data), yet the PnP literature has largely assumed matched distributions. The explicit error bound connecting denoiser distance to reconstruction error gives practitioners a quantitative handle on how much mismatch is tolerable, and the empirical finding that 4–64 target-domain samples can close most of the performance gap offers a low-cost remedy. Limitations include the requirement that both denoisers share the same range, the boundedness assumption on z-sequences, and the somewhat loose constants in the bound. Open questions include whether the analysis extends to iterative denoisers or diffusion-based priors, and whether the few-shot adaptation strategy generalizes to more severe distribution shifts (e.g., cross-modality).
