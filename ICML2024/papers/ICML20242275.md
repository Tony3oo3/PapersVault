## Problem

Moderate-sized LLMs (7B/13B) still lag behind GPT-4 and WMT competition winners in machine translation, and supervised fine-tuning is fundamentally capped by the quality of human-written references, which the paper shows can be inferior to model-generated translations. Additionally, SFT provides no mechanism to teach the model to reject near-perfect but flawed translations, creating a performance ceiling that standard training cannot break.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242275/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a source sentence in one language along with a translation prompt and produces a single translated sentence in the target language, operating in a many-to-many multilingual setup across 10 directions (cs, de, is, zh, ru ↔ en). For training, the method consumes triplet preference data: for each source sentence, three candidate translations (from GPT-4, ALMA-13B-LoRA, and the gold reference) are scored by two 10B reference-free models (KIWI-XXL and XCOMET), with the highest-scoring translation designated as preferred and the lowest as dis-preferred. Only 22K parallel sentences across 10 directions and 12M LoRA parameters (0.1% of the 13B model) are used for fine-tuning.

## Method

CPO is derived from DPO by replacing the reference policy π_ref with a uniform prior U, which cancels the reference terms in the loss and eliminates the need to store and run a second model, halving both memory and compute costs. A theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves that the DPO loss with an ideal reference policy is upper-bounded by the uniform-prior version, justifying the approximation. A behavior-cloning regularizer (equivalent to an SFT negative-log-likelihood term on preferred data) is added to prevent the policy from drifting away from the preferred distribution, yielding the final CPO loss as a sum of a preference-learning term and an NLL term. The preference data uses naturally occurring high-quality but imperfect translations as dis-preferred examples rather than artificially noised ones, which ablations show is critical for performance. Training is performed via LoRA (rank 16) on ALMA-13B-LoRA for one epoch with batch size 128, and the resulting ALMA-13B-R matches or exceeds GPT-4 and WMT winners on WMT'21, WMT'22, and WMT'23.

## Results

On WMT'22 en→xx (averaged across de, cs, zh, ru), ALMA-13B-R achieves 85.74 KIWI-XXL and 94.05 XCOMET, surpassing GPT-4 (83.83 KIWI-XXL, 93.23 XCOMET) and WMT winners (84.81 KIWI-XXL, 93.78 XCOMET). On WMT'23 across 6 directions, ALMA-13B-R scores 78.97 KIWI-XXL and 89.74 XCOMET, exceeding WMT winners (77.72, 88.24) and TowerInstruct (77.18, 88.11). The improvement over the ALMA-13B-LoRA base is substantial: +2.68 KIWI-XXL and +1.29 XCOMET on en→xx, achieved with only 12M learnable LoRA parameters (0.1% of model size) and 22K preference sentences. Human evaluation on zh→en confirms the gains, with ALMA-13B-R achieving a 77.8% win rate versus 62.5% for the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALMA-13B-R (CPO) outperforms GPT-4 on KIWI-XXL for en→xx (85.74 vs. 83.83 averaged) and on XCOMET (94.05 vs. 93.23), while GPT-4 retains a slight edge on de→en XCOMET (97.56 vs. 97.48). Against WMT competition winners, ALMA-13B-R matches or exceeds on most directions, with notable gains on zh (84.32 vs. 81.13 KIWI-XXL) and ru (85.74 vs. 84.81 KIWI-XXL), though WMT winners lead on cs KIWI-XXL (87.27 vs. 87.06). Compared to the ALMA-13B-LoRA base, CPO yields consistent improvements of 2–4 points on KIWI-XXL across all directions, whereas SFT on preferred data provides only marginal gains (+0.2 to +1.4) and DPO actually degrades performance in most settings. On WMT'23, ALMA-13B-R surpasses TowerInstruct by 1.79 KIWI-XXL and 1.63 XCOMET, and exceeds WMT winners by 1.25 KIWI-XXL and 1.50 XCOMET. Against conventional models (NLLB-3.3B, MADLAD-10B) and other LLM-based translators (Bayling-13B, BigTranslate, LLaMA-1/2-13B), ALMA-13B-R leads by wide margins—e.g., 85.74 vs. 79.46 KIWI-XXL (MADLAD-10B) and 85.74 vs. 68.61 KIWI-XXL (LLaMA-2-13B) on en→xx.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper derives its core method, Contrastive Preference Optimization (CPO), directly from DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) by replacing the reference policy with a uniform prior, eliminating the memory and speed overhead of maintaining two models. It builds on ALMA (Xu et al., 2023) as the base translation model, extending ALMA's SFT paradigm with a preference-learning objective that explicitly teaches the model to reject near-perfect but flawed translations. The behavior-cloning regularizer is borrowed from Hejna et al. (2023), and the overall framing departs from the standard SFT assumption that gold references represent an upper bound on translation quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that human-written FLORES-200 references are frequently outperformed by model-generated translations (73–79% win rate for ALMA/GPT-4 in xx→en) challenges a foundational assumption in MT training and reference-based evaluation, with implications beyond this specific task. CPO's practical efficiency—requiring only 12M additional parameters (0.1% of model size) and 22K sentences to match GPT-4—makes preference-based MT fine-tuning accessible at moderate scale. Open questions include whether the reference-quality finding generalizes to other NLP tasks, whether the CPO advantage over DPO persists at larger scales or with more diverse preference data, and whether the reliance on reference-free evaluation models for both data construction and scoring introduces subtle circularity despite the authors' ablation checks.
