## Problem

In learning-augmented non-clairvoyant scheduling, prior work assumed predictions for all n jobs or a querying model with perfect information. This paper tackles the practically more relevant setting where predictions are available for only B out of n jobs (selected uniformly at random) and may be arbitrarily inaccurate, establishing near-optimal lower bounds for perfect predictions and a learning-augmented algorithm that satisfies robustness, consistency, and smoothness while revealing a novel consistency-smoothness tradeoff absent in full-information settings.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives n jobs with unknown processing times, predictions y_σ(i) for a uniformly random subset of B jobs, and two hyperparameters λ ∈ [0,1] (controlling the robustness-consistency tradeoff) and ρ ∈ (0,1] (controlling the consistency-smoothness tradeoff). It outputs a preemptive single-machine schedule and a competitive ratio bound of the form min(2/(1−λ), C_ρ/λ + S_ρ/λ · n·E[η^σ]/OPT), where E[η^σ] is the total expected prediction error over the B predicted jobs. The competitive ratio is defined as a supremum over all job-size instances, with expectation taken over the random permutation selecting which B jobs are predicted.

## Method

The paper introduces two algorithms for perfect predictions: CRRR, which uses only the relative order of the B known job sizes and achieves a (2−B/n)-competitive ratio, and Switch, which uses the actual predicted sizes as breakpoints to alternate between round-robin on unknown jobs and Shortest Predicted Job First on known jobs, yielding a strictly better (n,B)-competitive ratio. For imperfect predictions, Switch is adapted by randomizing breakpoints as z_σ(i) = ξ·y_σ(i) with ξ drawn from a shifted exponential 1+E(1/ρ), ensuring smoothness in the prediction error. Robustness is then guaranteed by running this consistent algorithm at rate λ concurrently with round-robin at rate 1−λ (the preferential algorithm). The key technical insight is that ρ creates a tradeoff: decreasing ρ improves consistency (approaching 2−B/n) but inflates the smoothness factor 4/ρ·(1−B/n), a tradeoff that vanishes as B→0 or B→n.

## Results

For B = wn + o(n) known job sizes, the paper establishes a lower bound of 2 − w − (3−2√2)w(1−w) ≈ 2 − w − 0.172w(1−w) on the competitive ratio of any algorithm, improving upon the previous bound of 2 − w² (valid only for B = o(n)) from Benomar & Perchet (2023). The proposed Switch algorithm achieves a (n,B)-competitive ratio of 2 − B/n − 2(1−B/n)/(n+1), and CRRR achieves 2 − B/n, both substantially improving over the prior (2 − B(B−1)/(n(n−1))) ≈ 2 − w² bound. For imperfect predictions, the Preferential Algorithm (PA) achieves a competitive ratio of min(2/(1−λ), C_{ρ,n,B}/λ + S_{ρ,n,B}/λ · nE[η^σ]/OPT), revealing a novel consistency–smoothness tradeoff governed by ρ that vanishes as B → 0 or B → n.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with Benomar & Perchet (2023), which provided a (2 − B(B−1)/(n(n−1))) ≈ 2 − w² competitive algorithm and a lower bound of 2 only for B = o(n). The new Switch algorithm achieves 2 − B/n − 2(1−B/n)/(n+1) ≈ 2 − w, a strict improvement over 2 − w² for all w ∈ (0,1), and CRRR achieves 2 − B/n ≈ 2 − w using only the relative order of the B job sizes rather than their values. The lower bound is also strengthened from 2 (for B = o(n)) to 2 − w − (3−2√2)w(1−w), which is non-trivial for all w ∈ (0,1) and interpolates correctly between 2 (w=0) and 1 (w=1). In experiments (Figure 2), Switch outperforms CRRR for small n (e.g., n=20) on both exponential and Pareto distributions, while their ratios converge for n=1000, confirming asymptotic equivalence. The Preferential Algorithm's consistency–smoothness tradeoff (Figure 4) shows that smaller ρ improves consistency (ratio at τ=0) but increases sensitivity to prediction error, a behavior absent in prior learning-augmented scheduling results (Purohit et al. 2018; Bampis et al. 2022).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the B-clairvoyant scheduling framework of Benomar & Perchet (2023), which studied perfect predictions for B out of n jobs, to the learning-augmented setting with imperfect predictions. It builds on the preferential algorithm paradigm from Purohit et al. (2018) (running a consistent algorithm concurrently with round-robin at rates λ and 1−λ) and the foundational non-clairvoyant scheduling analysis of Motwani et al. (1994). A key departure is the discovery that knowing only the relative order of B job sizes (not their values) already yields a (2−B/n)-competitive algorithm via CRRR, substantially improving upon the prior (2−B(B−1)/(n(n−1))) bound, and that the Switch algorithm with randomized breakpoints achieves a strictly better finite-n ratio while remaining asymptotically equivalent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals a novel consistency-smoothness tradeoff specific to the partial-predictions regime (controlled by parameter ρ) that vanishes at B=0 and B=n, suggesting intermediate information levels exhibit unique algorithmic behaviors absent in the zero- or full-information settings. Open questions include closing the gap between the lower bounds (Corollaries 2.2, 2.3) and the Switch/CRRR upper bounds, adapting CRRR to imperfect action predictions with appropriate error metrics, and determining whether a smooth algorithm achieving consistency 2−B/n exists. The practical relevance is that in real deployments, predictions are often costly or unavailable for all jobs, making the partial-predictions regime the more realistic setting.
