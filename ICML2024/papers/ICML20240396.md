## Problem

Existing manifold-based deep models for SPD matrices (e.g., SPDNet) learn unconstrained mapping functions that lack interpretability, demand large training cohorts, and are difficult to adapt to new datasets. More fundamentally, the system-level mechanism by which static brain structure shapes dynamic functional fluctuations remains elusive, with prevailing views confining neural activity to focal regions rather than brain-wide oscillatory waves.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240396/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 90×90 SPD functional connectivity matrix (Pearson correlation of BOLD time courses from fMRI) and a 90×90 structural connectivity matrix (normalized fiber counts from DWI tractography), both parcellated via the AAL atlas. The SC matrix is used to construct graph-harmonic scattering transforms (via Laplacian eigen-decomposition) that constrain the mapping, while the FC matrix is the data being transformed. Beyond the final class label (4-way task or binary disease), the model outputs node-wise and frequency-wise attention maps that reveal which brain regions and oscillation frequencies drive the prediction.

## Method

The core contribution replaces SPDNet's unconstrained positive mapping Ψ with a block matrix P of graph-harmonic scattering transforms ψ_i^k = U g_k(γΛ) U^T δ_i, where U and Λ are eigenvectors/eigenvalues of the SC graph Laplacian and γ is a learnable spectral scale. The mapping P^T X P produces an NK×NK 'Supra-FC' matrix, which is reduced back to N×N SPD via block-wise max-pooling over frequency indices. An MLP-Mixer architecture then applies separate MLPs (with LayerNorm and ReLU) to learn optimal γ for column-wise and row-wise scattering transforms independently, mirroring the token/channel MLP split of MLP-Mixer in vision. This physics-informed constraint means the mapping is governed by the topology of the structural connectome rather than arbitrary learned weights, yielding a model with far fewer parameters, built-in interpretability through attention over nodes and frequencies, and the ability to transfer from large healthy cohorts to small disease cohorts via fine-tuning.

## Results

On the HCP-Aging task recognition benchmark (4-class), DeepHoloBrain achieves 99.5% accuracy, 98.9% recall, and 99.3% F1-score, outperforming the best baseline SPDNet (98.4% accuracy) by ~1.1 points. For Alzheimer's disease risk prediction, the model reaches 88.5% accuracy on OASIS and 82.0% on ADNI, surpassing SPDNet (87.1% and 80.0% respectively). In the transfer-learning setting (pre-trained on HCP-A, fine-tuned on ADNI), DeepHoloBrain attains 74.0% accuracy and 78.9% precision, exceeding SPDNet+ (71.2% accuracy, 74.1% precision) by 2.8 and 4.8 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepHoloBrain consistently outperforms all seven SPD-matrix baselines (SPDNet, CDL, LEML, SPDML, AIM, RSR, DeepO2P) across both OASIS and ADNI disease diagnosis and HCP-A task recognition, with the largest margins over CDL and LEML (e.g., 88.5% vs. 82.7% accuracy on OASIS). Among graph/transformer methods in Table 3, the advantage is most pronounced: GCN (69.0%), GAT (71.9%), GIN (58.5%), and GraphT (72.1%) all fall well below DeepHoloBrain's 99.5% on HCP-A task recognition, while BrainNetCNN (72.5%) and BolT (86.3%) are closer but still 13 and 3.2 points behind respectively. On ADNI disease diagnosis, BolT (81.2%) and BrainNetCNN (80.0%) are the strongest graph-based competitors, yet DeepHoloBrain (82.0%) still leads by 0.8 and 2.0 points. The paper attributes the gap to BolT and BrainNetCNN using only FC information, whereas DeepHoloBrain integrates both SC and FC through scattering transforms. In the transfer-learning setting, DeepHoloBrain+ (74.0% accuracy, 78.9% precision) outperforms SPDNet+ (71.2%, 74.1%) and all other pre-trained baselines, suggesting the physics-informed architecture generalizes better with limited disease-specific data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard SPDNet paradigm (Huang & Van Gool, 2017), where mapping functions on the Riemannian manifold are learned as unconstrained positive matrices, by replacing them with graph-harmonic scattering transforms derived from the structural connectome's Laplacian spectrum. The architectural design borrows the row-wise/column-wise decomposition of MLP-Mixer (Tolstikhin et al., 2021) and adapts it to the SPD manifold via a novel 'Supra-FC' expansion-and-pooling mechanism. The scattering transform operators themselves follow the geometric wavelet framework of Perlmutter et al. (2020) and Hammond et al. (2011), but are here used not as feature extractors on graph signals per se, but as the structural constraint that defines the mapping functions themselves, tying the learned geometry directly to brain anatomy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a physically grounded, interpretable alternative to black-box SPD matrix deep learning, with the scattering transforms providing a principled link between structural topology and functional dynamics. The pre-trained transfer result (fine-tuning on limited ADNI data after HCP-A pre-training) is practically relevant for clinical settings where disease cohorts are small. However, the 'DeepHoloBrain' holography interpretation remains a proof-of-concept metaphor rather than a validated mechanistic model of neural dynamics, and the max-pooling operation that restores SPD property is somewhat ad hoc. Open questions include whether the scattering constraint genuinely captures SC-FC coupling or merely acts as an effective regularizer, and whether the frequency-location attention patterns generalize beyond the AAL 90-region parcellation.
