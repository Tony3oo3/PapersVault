## Problem

While asymptotic regret optimality for self-tuning minimum variance regulators was established by Lai and Wei (1987), no finite-time regret bounds existed for this singular control problem. Existing finite-time analyses for linear-quadratic (LQ) control yield only √T regret and rely on a positive definite penalty on control effort, which is absent in the minimum variance (regulation) problem. This paper closes the gap by establishing the first finite-time logarithmic regret bounds for self-tuning regulation.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes the scalar system output y_t at each time step along with a known compact parameter set Θ ⊂ ℝ^{p+q} containing the true ARX parameters. It produces a scalar control input u_t clipped to [−B_u, B_u] and maintains two parameter estimates: a least-squares estimate θ_t^(I) computed only on exploration samples, and a recursive gain estimate λ_t updated on all samples. Both estimates are updated in O(1) time per step, making the algorithm computationally efficient for real-time deployment.

## Method

PIECE modifies the standard certainty equivalence (CE) controller with two key mechanisms: (i) input clipping to a compact interval [−B_u, B_u] whose threshold is derived from the known parameter set Θ to prevent large transient errors when the gain estimate λ_t involves division by a small b_{1,t}, and (ii) a diagnostic fallback that switches to the exploration-based estimate λ̃_t^(I) whenever the two estimates diverge beyond a confidence threshold. Exploration is conducted during increasingly infrequent episodes (starting at n_i = exp(i²)) where bounded white noise (|u_t| ≤ B_w) is injected, while exploitation uses the CE rule on all other steps. The finite-time analysis hinges on a lower bound on the minimum eigenvalue of the exploration covariance matrix, which grows as √N_t^(I), enabling tight control of estimation error and yielding C log T regret under bounded noise and C log³T under sub-Gaussian noise.

## Results

The PIECE algorithm achieves the first finite-time logarithmic regret bounds for the self-tuning regulation problem: O(σ²(p+q−1) log T) for bounded noise and O(log³ T) for sub-Gaussian noise, matching the asymptotically optimal pre-constant of Lai & Wei (1987). In simulations at T=1000, PIECE achieves cumulative regret of 36, 66, and 46 on three ARX examples, compared to 313/16,224,624/9,888 for standard CE and 364/5,816/15,644 for the LW algorithm. The improvement is most dramatic in Example II (p=4, q=4), where PIECE reduces regret by over 5 orders of magnitude relative to CE.

| I (p=2, q=2) | 313 | 364 | **36** |

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIECE outperforms both baselines on all three ARX examples. Against the standard Certainty Equivalence (CE) controller, PIECE reduces cumulative regret by roughly 9× (Example I: 36 vs. 313), over 245,000× (Example II: 66 vs. 16,224,624), and about 215× (Example III: 46 vs. 9,888). Against the asymptotically regret-optimal LW algorithm of Lai & Wei (1987), PIECE achieves 10× lower regret in Example I (36 vs. 364), 88× lower in Example II (66 vs. 5,816), and 340× lower in Example III (46 vs. 15,644). The key design differences driving these gains are input clipping to [−B_u, B_u] and a more efficient exploration schedule with increasingly infrequent probing episodes, which together eliminate the large initial transients that plague both CE and LW.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Lai & Wei (1987) asymptotically regret-optimal self-tuning regulator, which introduced probing inputs for exploration in a certainty-equivalence framework. The key departure is providing the first finite-time regret analysis for the singular minimum variance (regulation) problem, where no positive definite penalty is placed on control effort. This contrasts sharply with the extensive finite-time literature on LQ control (Abbasi-Yadkori & Szepesvári 2011, Simchowitz & Foster 2020, Lale et al. 2022), which only achieves C√T regret due to the positive definiteness of the control cost matrix. The PIECE algorithm is a simplified and optimized variant of LW that adds input clipping and a more carefully designed exploration schedule to enable the finite-time analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central conceptual result—that the singular minimum variance problem admits C log T regret while the non-singular LQ problem is lower-bounded at C√T—is a genuine reversal of intuition and opens the door to logarithmic regret in other singular adaptive control settings. The practical impact is significant for industrial applications (paper machines, ship steering, ore crushers) where poor initial transients are costly. Open questions include whether the C log³T bound for sub-Gaussian noise can be tightened to C log T, and whether similar techniques extend to ARMAX systems or broader RL settings such as MDPs and LQ systems.
