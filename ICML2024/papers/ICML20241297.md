## Problem

Existing information-bottleneck-based time series explainers suffer from two critical flaws: a signaling issue where sub-instances encode the label value without providing intuitive explanations, and a distribution shift issue where explanation sub-instances are out-of-distribution for the classifier, making their predictions unreliable. Prior work (e.g., TIMEX) addresses the OOD problem by retraining a white-box copy of the classifier, which requires model access and still permits label leakage.

## Input / Output

The method takes a time series instance X ∈ R^{T×D} and a trained black-box classifier f(·) as input, treating f as a frozen oracle. It produces a binary saliency mask M ∈ {0,1}^{T×D} (via a stochastic Bernoulli process with parameters π from a transformer extractor) and an explanation-embedded instance X̃ ∈ R^{T×D} (via an MLP conditioner). The mask is hardened using a straight-through estimator, and the explanation-embedded instance is designed to remain in-distribution for f so that f(X̃) provides a reliable label-consistency signal without requiring a separate white-box model.

## Method

TIMEX++ consists of two components trained end-to-end: (1) an explanation extractor g_φ (transformer encoder-decoder) that outputs a stochastic mask π, regularized by a KL divergence against a Bernoulli(r) prior (replacing the intractable I(X;X′) term) plus a temporal connectivity loss to penalize fragmented masks; and (2) an explanation conditioner Ψ_θ (3-layer MLP) that maps [M, X] to an in-distribution instance X̃. The key design is a two-step generation: first a reference instance X̃^r is formed by Gaussian-padding the masked sub-instance, then Ψ_θ produces X̃ that is pulled toward X̃^r (reference distance loss) while being constrained to match the original data distribution (KL divergence loss). Label consistency is measured via Jensen-Shannon divergence between f(X) and f(X̃), avoiding the signaling problem of direct cross-entropy with ground-truth labels. The total objective is L̃ = L_LC + α·L_M + β(L_KL + L_dr), optimized jointly with STE for discrete mask sampling.

## Results

On four synthetic datasets with known ground-truth explanations, TIMEX++ outperforms all six baselines on 9 out of 12 metric-dataset combinations, achieving average improvements of +11.01% AUPRC, +10.87% AUP, and +1.25% AUR over the strongest baseline TIMEX. On the real-world ECG arrhythmia dataset, TIMEX++ achieves 0.6599 AUPRC, surpassing TIMEX (0.4721) by 39.78% and IG (0.4182) by 57.8%. Occlusion experiments on five additional real-world datasets (PAM, Epilepsy, Boiler, Wafer, Freezer) confirm TIMEX++ consistently ranks first or second across both mean-substitution and zero-substitution perturbations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIMEX++ dominates the strongest baseline TIMEX across all four synthetic datasets on AUPRC (e.g., 0.8905 vs. 0.8324 on FreqShapes, 0.8468 vs. 0.7124 on SeqComb-UV), with the largest gap on SeqComb-UV (+18.86%). TIMEX++ also wins on AUP for all four datasets but trails TIMEX on AUR for SeqComb-UV (0.9069 vs. 0.9411) and LowVar (0.8332 vs. 0.9004), suggesting TIMEX retains slightly more recall at the cost of precision. Against gradient-based methods (IG, SGT+GRAD), TIMEX++ leads by a wide margin on AUPRC (e.g., 0.8905 vs. 0.7516 for IG on FreqShapes), while perturbation-based methods (Dynamask, WinIT) and the contrastive method CoRTX are consistently outperformed on all three metrics. On the real-world ECG dataset, TIMEX++ achieves 0.6599 AUPRC versus 0.4721 for TIMEX (+39.78%) and 0.4182 for IG (+57.8%), confirming the advantage generalizes beyond synthetic settings. In occlusion experiments on PAM, Epilepsy, and Boiler, TIMEX++ maintains non-decreasing AUROC as non-salient features are removed, outperforming all baselines at every threshold k, and achieves the best average rank (2.0 for zero-substitution) across five real-world datasets in Table 4.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Information Bottleneck (IB) framework for time series explainability by addressing two identified pathologies: the signaling problem (where sub-instances encode the label rather than genuine explanatory content) and the out-of-distribution problem (where masked sub-instances fall outside the classifier's training distribution). It builds directly on TIMEX (Queen et al., 2023), which introduced self-supervised model behavior consistency for time series explanations, and on the preservation game of Fong & Vedaldi (2017) for label consistency. The key departure is replacing the intractable mutual information terms in IB with a tractable KL-divergence-based compactness regularizer and a learned conditioner Ψ_θ that generates in-distribution, explanation-embedded instances in two steps (Gaussian padding followed by learned refinement), eliminating TIMEX's need for a white-box copy of the classifier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded, black-box-compatible framework for time series explanation that consistently outperforms six baselines across 11 datasets, with a compelling real-world flood prediction case study demonstrating domain-relevant feature attribution (water levels, gate openings, rainfall). The ablation showing catastrophic failure without the reference-distance loss L_dr (AUPRC dropping from 0.66 to 0.15 on ECG) highlights that naive padding alone is insufficient for reliable explanation. Open questions include the sensitivity to hyperparameters (α, β, r, λ_con) across datasets, whether the two-step generation process can be made parameter-free, and whether the approach extends to sequence-to-sequence or forecasting tasks beyond classification.
