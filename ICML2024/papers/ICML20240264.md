## Problem

In-context learning (ICL) in large language models is inherently temporary: demonstration examples only affect the current inference and are discarded afterward. Existing context distillation methods that attempt to make ICL permanent rely on expensive gradient-based fine-tuning, are largely empirical, and lack theoretical guarantees. This paper asks whether ICL prompts can be exactly and cheaply converted into model weights without any gradient descent.

## Input / Output

The method takes an ICL demonstration prompt (a sequence of M token embeddings) and a pretrained linearized-attention transformer with weights W_Q, W_K, W_V and existing bias terms. It produces two new bias vectors per attention layer: b_KV (added to the Key-Value matrix) and b_D (added to the normalizing denominator), totaling roughly 1% of the model's parameters. For linearized attention the conversion is exact (relative logit error ~1e-6 even at 2B parameters); for standard softmax attention (e.g., GPT-2) the conversion is approximate, reducing logit relative error from 16.56% to 9.17% via kernel approximation of the softmax.

## Method

The core insight is that in linearized attention, prepending ICL tokens to the input only modifies the Key-Value matrix A = Σ φ(K_j)V_j^T while leaving the Query term unchanged, so all ICL information is captured in A. The paper introduces two bias terms—b_KV appended to A and b_D appended to the normalizing factor D_2—and proves (Theorem 4.5) that setting b_KV' = R_{Θ,-M} b_KV + Σ R_{Θ,j-M} φ(K_j')V_j'^T and b_D' = b_D + D_2*(X') makes the model without the ICL prompt produce identical outputs to the model with the prompt prepended, for all inputs. The ICLCA algorithm propagates the ICL tokens through each layer sequentially, updating biases at every linearized-attention sub-layer while passing tokens through non-attention layers unchanged. For standard softmax-attention transformers, an approximate variant (ICLAA) first replaces the softmax similarity with a kernel feature map (e.g., Performers' PRF/ORF), applies the same bias conversion, and injects the resulting bias into the original softmax attention, trading exactness for architectural generality.

## Results

On the induction head task with a 12-layer linear attention transformer (dim=128, RoPE), the converted model M_new,no ICL achieves 99.95% in-context accuracy, exactly matching M_old,ICL (99.95%) and vastly outperforming M_old,no ICL (2.23%). The exact conversion is numerically verified across model sizes from 205K to 1.98B parameters, with relative logit errors ranging from 2.9×10⁻⁷ to 4.3×10⁻⁶. On pretrained GPT-2 (approximate conversion via Performers kernel), the relative logit error between M_old,ICL and M_new,no ICL improves from 16.56% (no conversion) to 9.17%, and generated text qualitatively reflects the ICL context.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against other ICL distillation methods (e.g., Askell et al. 2021, Choi et al. 2022, Snell et al. 2022) in controlled experiments, but positions its approach as theoretically exact versus their empirical fine-tuning-based approximations. The primary internal comparison is three-way: M_old,ICL (model with ICL prompt in context), M_old,no ICL (model without ICL prompt), and M_new,no ICL (model after bias conversion, no ICL prompt). ICLCA achieves exact parity with M_old,ICL on the induction head task (99.95% vs 99.95%), while M_old,no ICL drops to 2.23%, confirming the bias terms fully capture the ICL signal. For the approximate GPT-2 setting, the conversion reduces relative logit error by roughly 45% (16.56% → 9.17%), and qualitative text generation shows the model retains factual context (e.g., correctly identifying Microsoft, California, Apple) that the unconverted model misses entirely. The added bias terms also slightly accelerate training convergence on the induction head task, suggesting the modified architecture is not merely a conversion vehicle but a mild inductive bias improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the linearized attention formulation of Katharopoulos et al. (2020), which reveals the Key-Value matrix structure, and on RoPE (Su et al., 2023) for handling positional encodings in the conversion. It departs from prior context distillation work (Askell et al., 2021; Choi et al., 2022; Snell et al., 2022) that relies on gradient-based fine-tuning to approximate ICL incorporation, by showing that for linearized attention architectures, the conversion to bias terms is mathematically exact and requires no parameter updates. The Performers kernel approximation (Choromanski et al., 2020) is repurposed to extend the method approximately to standard softmax-attention transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exact, closed-form conversion of ICL prompts into ~1% additional bias parameters offers a theoretically grounded, computationally cheap alternative to expensive fine-tuning for permanently incorporating context into LLMs. The identification of the Key-Value matrix (rather than the Query-Key attention scores) as the locus of in-context information opens a new research direction into how contextual knowledge is stored in transformers. Key limitations include the requirement of linearized attention for exactness, the dependence of approximate conversion on kernel quality for softmax models, and the untested scalability of the approximate method to very large models. The RNN reinterpretation (Section 7) suggests natural extension to state-space models like Mamba and H3, but whether these architectures exhibit ICL at all remains open.
