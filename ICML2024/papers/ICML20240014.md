## Problem

Transformer-based model-based RL methods like IRIS and TWM rely on continuous representations or reconstructed images for policy learning, which complicates modeling discrete properties of the real world (e.g., disjoint object classes where interpolation is implausible) and limits fine-grained decision-making. This paper asks whether discrete abstract representations can be used for both world modeling and policy learning within a unified transformer framework to improve sample efficiency and interpretability.

## Input / Output

The agent receives 64×64×3 RGB frames from Atari 100k (100k environment steps, frameskip 4) along with the previously taken discrete action. Observations are first tokenized into K=16 discrete codes via a VQ-VAE (codebook size 512, embedding dim 512), and the policy consumes these tokens augmented with a CLS token and a MEM token (18 input tokens total). Outputs include a discrete action sampled from the policy, autoregressively predicted next-state tokens, a scalar reward estimate, and a binary episode-termination prediction, all generated over H=20 imagination steps.

## Method

DART employs a three-stage pipeline: (1) a VQ-VAE discretizes each 64×64 frame into 16 latent tokens; (2) a 10-layer GPT-style causal transformer decoder performs autoregressive next-token prediction to model environment dynamics, reward, and termination; (3) a 6-layer ViT-style transformer encoder learns the policy by attending over the predicted observation tokens plus a CLS token (aggregating state information) and a MEM token (carrying memory from the previous step). The key architectural distinction from IRIS is that the policy operates directly on discrete tokens rather than reconstructed images, enabling the attention mechanism to focus on task-relevant fine-grained details. Memory is handled without any recurrent network: the CLS token's output at step t becomes the MEM token at step t+1, with self-attention acting as a learned gate that selectively passes task-relevant information forward. Learnable positional encodings are added to the token sequence to recover spatial locality that discrete tokens inherently lack.

## Results

On the Atari 100k benchmark (26 games, 100k environment steps), DART achieves a median human-normalized score of 0.790, a 61% improvement over DreamerV3 (0.466) and a 173% improvement over IRIS (0.289). DART also attains the best IQM (0.575) and lowest Optimality Gap (0.458) among all no-look-ahead-search methods, and beats human performance in 9 out of 26 games. On the Crafter long-horizon task, DART achieves a return of 12.2±1.67 at 1M steps, outperforming both DreamerV3 (11.7±1.9) and IRIS (9.23±0.56).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DART decisively outperforms DreamerV3 on median (0.790 vs 0.466, +61%), IQM (0.575 vs 0.490, +15%), and Optimality Gap (0.458 vs 0.508, +10%), though DreamerV3 retains a higher mean (1.120 vs 1.022) driven by a few high-scoring games like CrazyClimber and Gopher. Against IRIS, DART wins on median (0.790 vs 0.289), IQM (0.575 vs 0.501), and OG (0.458 vs 0.512), and beats IRIS in 18 of 26 individual games, while IRIS edges out DART on mean (1.046 vs 1.022). DART also surpasses TWM (median 0.790 vs 0.505) and SPR (median 0.790 vs 0.396) by wide margins on all aggregate metrics. Against STORM, results are mixed: DART wins on games with small fast-moving objects (Breakout 41.9 vs 16, Pong 17.2 vs 11, DemonAttack 2452 vs 165) but STORM dominates on several others (CrazyClimber 66776 vs 34071, Gopher 8240 vs 1981, MsPacman 2673 vs 1133). On Crafter, DART achieves the highest return (12.2±1.67) over DreamerV3 (11.7±1.9) and IRIS (9.23±0.56) at 1M steps, confirming its advantage on long-horizon tasks with multiple interacting components.

## Relation to Prior Work

DART extends the IRIS (Micheli et al., 2023) framework, which first demonstrated that discrete image tokens with a GPT-style transformer decoder can serve as an effective world model, by replacing IRIS's CNN+LSTM policy with a ViT-style transformer encoder that operates directly on the discrete token space. The policy training objective is inherited from DreamerV2 (Hafner et al., 2021), and the VQ-VAE tokenization follows the same recipe as IRIS. The key architectural departure is the introduction of a learnable memory token that aggregates temporal information via self-attention, replacing the recurrent mechanisms (LSTM in IRIS, GRU in DreamerV3) used for partial observability, and the use of a transformer encoder for policy learning rather than a separate CNN-based network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DART demonstrates that using a unified transformer architecture for both world modeling and policy learning, operating entirely in discrete token space, yields substantially more consistent performance (median 0.790 vs. 0.466 for DreamerV3) on the Atari 100k benchmark without look-ahead search, while also providing interpretability through attention maps that reveal task-specific memory requirements. The memory token mechanism offers a simple, recurrent-free alternative to GRU/LSTM for handling partial observability, which could generalize to other sequential decision-making settings. However, the method is currently limited to discrete action spaces and does not incorporate look-ahead search, leaving open questions about scalability to continuous control (e.g., robotics) and whether the discrete token approach can be combined with planning algorithms like MCTS to close the remaining gap to methods like Efficient Zero.
