## Problem

Existing neural spatio-temporal point process (STPP) methods either assume restricted parametric forms for event distributions (e.g., Gaussian mixtures) or rely on inaccurate numerical approximations of the intractable normalization integral in the log-likelihood, both of which compromise prediction accuracy. Additionally, prior work provides only point predictions without quantifying uncertainty, despite the high inherent randomness of event times and locations making point estimates unreliable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of marked spatio-temporal events (time, discrete mark, d-dimensional location) and encodes them via a Transformer with self-attention and future masking. Outputs are generated as Q samples per event through Langevin Dynamics, from which point predictions (mean time/location, mode mark), confidence intervals (quantiles for time), confidence regions (density-thresholded areas for location), and mark calibration scores are derived. Event time is log-normalized and location is standard-normalized during training, with rescaling before evaluation.

## Method

SMASH decomposes the joint intensity λ(t,k,x) into a marginal intensity λ(t,k) and a spatial conditional p(x|t,k), then further decomposes the time-mark component into p(t|k)·p(k|t) to handle the discrete mark. Score matching is applied to the continuous conditional distributions of time and location (yielding normalization-free objectives), while the discrete mark is learned via conditional log-likelihood, forming a pseudolikelihood training objective. A Transformer Hawkes Process parameterizes the intensity per mark, and a Co-attention Denoising Network (CDN) parameterizes the spatial score function. In practice, denoising score matching with Gaussian perturbations replaces direct score matching to improve stability and low-density coverage. Sampling uses multi-step Langevin Dynamics with Tweedie's formula denoising to produce event time, mark, and location samples.

## Results

On the three marked STPP datasets, SMASH achieves the best Calibration Score (CS) for event time across all datasets (3.53% on Earthquake, 6.93% on Crime, 6.38% on Football), dramatically outperforming the next-best baselines (14.50–25.30%). SMASH also attains the lowest MAE for event time on Earthquake (0.29%) and Crime (1.16%), and the best CS for event location on Crime (1.08%) and Football (2.52%). For event mark prediction, SMASH achieves the lowest Expected Calibration Error (ECE) on all three STPP datasets (4.85%, 11.36%, 3.23%) while matching or exceeding the best accuracy. On the four marked TPP datasets, SMASH similarly achieves the best CS and ECE across the board.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMASH decisively outperforms all seven STPP baselines on Calibration Score for event time, reducing CS from 14.50–25.30% (best baseline) to 3.53–6.93%, a 2–7× improvement. Against the strongest neural baselines, SMASH beats DSTPP (the closest competitor) on CS_time by ~11 points on Earthquake and ~8 points on Football, and on CS_space by ~0.8 points on Football; however, DSTPP slightly edges SMASH on MAE_space for Football (19.41% vs. 20.35%). For event mark calibration, SMASH achieves the lowest ECE on all three STPP datasets (4.85%, 11.36%, 3.23%), substantially outperforming DeepSTPP (21.20%, 15.14%, 12.10%) and DSTPP (7.30%, 14.65%, 4.64%). On the four marked TPP datasets, SMASH achieves the best CS (0.27–0.85%) and ECE (2.23–12.23%) across the board, outperforming SMURF-THP—the closest score-matching baseline—by 0.05–0.47 points in CS and 0.41–3.42 points in ECE. Classical baselines (Poisson, Hawkes) are consistently the weakest, confirming that flexible neural modeling is necessary for these complex event sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

SMASH extends the score matching framework (Hyvärinen, 2005; Vincent, 2011) to marked spatio-temporal point processes by decomposing the joint intensity into a continuous spatial component and a time/mark component, applying score matching to the former and conditional likelihood to the latter. It builds directly on SMURF-THP (Li et al., 2023), which introduced score matching for marked temporal point processes, by generalizing to the spatio-temporal setting and replacing the single shared intensity with per-mark intensities for better time-mark dependency modeling. Compared to the diffusion-based DSTPP (Yuan et al., 2023), SMASH simplifies the generative process from multi-scale denoising to a single noise perturbation while reusing DSTPP's Co-attention Denoising Network for the spatial score, and it departs from likelihood-based STPP methods (NSTPP, NSMPP, DeepSTPP) by eliminating the intractable normalization integral entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SMASH is the first STPP framework to provide calibrated confidence intervals for event time and confidence regions for event location, addressing a gap the authors identify as critical given the high variance inherent in spatio-temporal event data. The consistency theorem (Theorem 3.1) provides theoretical grounding that the score-matching-based pseudolikelihood objective recovers the true parameters, which prior score-matching TPP work lacked. Open questions include whether the cascaded sampling (time → mark → location) can be made fully parallel without quality loss, how the method scales to very high-dimensional spatial features, and whether the single-noise-scale assumption holds for more complex event distributions. The practical impact is moderate: the method is applicable to a well-studied task and the confidence interval prediction adds a useful evaluation dimension, but the core modeling task (STPP) remains unchanged.
