## Problem

Sampling from unnormalized density functions in high-dimensional continuous spaces remains challenging: MCMC and SMC methods require extensive tuning of stochastic transition kernels, while hybrid flow+MC approaches (AFTMC, CR-AFTMC) still depend on Monte Carlo steps whose contribution to learning is unclear. The paper asks whether a purely deterministic flow-based transport can replace these stochastic mechanisms entirely, while still providing unbiased estimates of the log-marginal likelihood.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241969/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unnormalized target density (or a prior and likelihood for Bayesian problems) and initial samples drawn from a simple distribution (standard Gaussian for type-1 problems, the prior for type-2 problems). It produces transported samples at the target time, normalized importance weights computed by accumulating the PDE residual along each trajectory, and an on-the-fly estimate of log Z. The velocity field is discretized over T time steps (typically 256) with a cosine annealing schedule, and each time step is modeled by a separate two-hidden-layer feed-forward network (64 nodes per layer) initialized from the previous step's weights.

## Method

LFIS learns a time-dependent velocity field v(x,t) by enforcing the Generalized Liouville Equation in a physics-informed manner: at each discrete time step, a neural network is trained to minimize the squared residual of Eq. (5), [∇ₓ + Sₓ(x,t)]·v(x,t) = −∂ₜ δ log ρ̃ₓ(x,t), over samples transported to that time by previously trained networks. This recursive, self-supervised training requires no labeled data or target velocity field—only the prescribed annealed density path. The key theoretical contribution is showing that even when the finite network does not perfectly solve Eq. (5), the accumulated PDE error along each deterministic trajectory yields valid importance sampling weights, enabling asymptotically unbiased estimation of expectations under the target and of log Z via a path-integral identity. This eliminates the need for resampling, stochastic kernels, or Girsanov-based change-of-measure corrections used by diffusion-based samplers (PIS, DDS), making LFIS a purely deterministic flow model.

## Results

On the 2D mode-separated Gaussian mixture, LFIS achieves log Ẑ = -0.0002±0.004 (ground truth = 0), W₂ = 0.054±0.014, and ESS = 0.97±0.001, all best among the five methods compared. On the 10D funnel distribution, LFIS attains log Ẑ = -0.07±0.003, W₂ = 5.57±1.52, and ESS = 0.97±0.063, again the best in each metric. For the four Bayesian type-2 problems (LGCP D=1600, Ionosphere D=35, Sonar D=61, VAE D=30), LFIS is the only method whose log Ẑ estimates are consistent with the gold-standard SMC(1024) on all problems simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LFIS outperforms all four baselines (SMC, DDS, PIS, AFTMC) on every metric for both type-1 problems. On the 2D Gaussian mixture, LFIS's log Ẑ error is ~0.0002 versus SMC's 1.28, PIS's 0.0035, DDS's 0.31, and AFT's 2.43; its W₂ (0.054) is the lowest, and its ESS (0.97) is competitive with SMC (0.99) while far exceeding DDS (0.012) and PIS (0.59). On the 10D funnel, LFIS achieves log Ẑ = -0.07±0.003 versus SMC (-0.12±0.06), PIS (-1.14±0.13), DDS (-0.31±0.12), and AFT (-0.11±0.68), with the tightest variance. For the Bayesian type-2 problems, LFIS is the only method whose log Ẑ estimates fall within the statistical margin of the gold-standard SMC(1024) on all four problems (LGCP, Ionosphere, Sonar, VAE), whereas AFTMC shows large errors on Ionosphere (-121.63 vs. -111.61) and Sonar (-104.79 vs. -108.38), and PIS deviates on Sonar (-109.35 vs. -108.38). Notably, LFIS achieves these results without resampling, whereas SMC and AFTMC rely on resampling to maintain ESS.

## Relation to Prior Work

LFIS sits at the intersection of several established lines: the Generalized Liouville Equation (Gerlich, 1973) provides the governing PDE; the tempered density path ρ*(t) is inherited from Annealed Importance Sampling (Neal, 2001) and SMC (Del Moral et al., 2006); and the equation-based neural network training follows the Physics-Informed Neural Network paradigm (Raissi et al., 2019). The key departure from prior work is that LFIS is a *pure* deterministic flow method—unlike AFTMC/CR-AFTMC which interleave flow steps with MC kernels, or PIS/DDS which are end-to-end and only constrain the terminal distribution. The novelty lies in (i) enforcing the PDE structure at every time step rather than matching a target velocity field (as in Flow Matching), and (ii) deriving importance weights from the accumulated PDE residual along deterministic trajectories, a construct with no direct analogue in the stochastic-process-based samplers (PIS, DDS) that use Girsanov change-of-measure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LFIS offers a principled, parameter-light alternative to hybrid MC+flow samplers by eliminating the need to tune MCMC kernels, while the importance-sampling correction for imperfect neural networks provides a theoretically grounded way to recover unbiased estimates even when the PDE is not solved exactly. The practical impact is significant for Bayesian model selection and high-dimensional posterior sampling, where log-marginal-likelihood estimation is critical. Open limitations include the requirement for differentiable densities (excluding bounded uniform priors), memory overhead from computing ∇S* and cross-derivative terms, and sensitivity to the number of time steps due to explicit Euler integration—addressing these (e.g., via higher-order integrators or implicit schemes) is a natural next step.
