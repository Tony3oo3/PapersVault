## Problem

Open-domain text generation evaluation remains an unresolved challenge: reference-based metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, BERTScore) fail when the output space is highly diverse, and existing reference-free approaches either require extensive human annotations for negative samples or struggle to assign reliable instance-level scores. The paper asks whether the well-known scaling behavior of language models (larger models produce better text) can be directly exploited to construct reference-free evaluation metrics without manual annotation or task-specific negative sample design.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a generated text sequence together with a pair of same-architecture language models of different sizes (an 'amateur' and an 'expert'), from which step-wise log-likelihood ratios are computed. Discriminative CDM outputs a single scalar quality score per sequence by pooling the step-wise contrastive momentum values; Generative CDM outputs synthetic negative samples (constructed via segment infilling from a degraded distribution) that are then used to train a discriminator producing per-instance quality scores. No human annotations are required at any stage of metric construction.

## Method

CDM approximates an oracle quality function E(p) via a first-order secant between two model distributions, yielding the contrastive momentum m(s) = log p_e(s) − log p_a(s) as a quality signal. Generative CDM descends along −m from the amateur model to construct a degraded distribution log p_n ∝ log p_a − γm, then uses segment-infilling (removing a span from a positive example and regenerating it under p_n) to produce deceptive negative samples for training a discriminator-based metric. Discriminative CDM skips generation entirely and directly pools the per-token contrastive momentum m(x_t | s_<t) over the sequence (via sum, average, max, min, or a small learned classifier) to yield a scalar score. The key design choice is that the amateur–expert gap controls metric sensitivity: larger gaps between model sizes generally yield stronger correlations with human judgment, and the approach is validated across T5, Pythia, and LLaMA 2 model families on dialogue coherence and commonsense evaluation tasks.

## Results

On dialogue evaluation (Spearman correlation with human judgments), Discriminative CDM (T5 small-11b) achieves 0.59 coherence and 0.62 overall on FED, and 0.28 coherence and 0.27 overall on DSTC9, outperforming all baselines. Generative CDM achieves 0.53/0.55 on FED and 0.22/0.24 on DSTC9. On the CommonGen-trinity commonsense evaluation task, Discriminative CDM reaches 0.73 (raw) and 0.61 (binarized) Spearman correlation, surpassing G-Eval with Flan-T5-11b (0.61/0.53) and Generative CDM (0.63/0.55).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Discriminative CDM outperforms all baselines on both dialogue evaluation datasets. On FED, it beats G-Eval with LLaMa2-7b-Vicuna (0.59 vs. 0.57 coherence) and DEAM (0.59 vs. 0.47 coherence) by a clear margin, and on DSTC9 it surpasses DEAM (0.28 vs. 0.19 coherence) and G-Eval with Flan-T5-11b (0.28 vs. 0.19 coherence). Generative CDM also outperforms DEAM on DSTC9 (0.22 vs. 0.19 coherence) and matches it on FED overall (0.55 vs. 0.55). On the CommonGen-trinity commonsense task, Discriminative CDM (0.73 raw) beats G-Eval with Flan-T5-11b (0.61 raw) by 0.12, and substantially outperforms BOOST Commonsense Oracle (0.10 raw) and ACCENT (-0.08 raw), which show near-zero or negative correlation. Notably, DynaEval and the older baselines (Mesgar, Vakulenko) show negligible or negative correlation, confirming that CDM's distribution-contrastive approach is a significant improvement over prior automatic evaluation methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the contrastive decoding framework (Li et al., 2022), originally designed for improving generation quality, as a general evaluation methodology by treating the log-probability gap between an expert and amateur model as a proxy for text quality. It unifies two previously separate evaluation paradigms—discriminator-based metrics (ADEM, DEAM) that require hand-crafted negative samples, and distribution/divergence-based metrics (MAUVE, UniEval) that struggle with instance-level scoring—under a single contrastive distribution framework. The connection to ExPO (Zheng et al., 2024) is noted, though CDM operates in log-probability space rather than parameter space, and the AMR-guided manipulation strategy in Generative CDM directly extends DEAM's approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CDM offers a principled, reference-free evaluation framework that eliminates the need for human-annotated negative samples or complex semantic manipulation pipelines, making it more scalable to new domains. However, the method is inherently limited to settings where the partial order assumption (larger model = better) holds, and the first-order secant approximation of the oracle E(p) may be too crude for fine-grained distinctions. Open questions include extending the contrastive momentum to more than two model scales, developing higher-order approximations, and validating the framework on inverse-scaling tasks where the core assumption breaks down.
