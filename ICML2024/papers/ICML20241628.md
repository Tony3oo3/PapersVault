## Problem

Existing Verilog generation methods focus on syntactic and functional correctness but neglect downstream EDA optimization tasks such as synthesis node reduction and verification runtime. Additionally, the scarcity of Verilog data relative to general programming languages makes LLM fine-tuning prone to overfitting, and no prior work applies discriminator-guided controllable generation to engineering optimization in the EDA flow.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241628/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language problem description paired with a Verilog module definition (header and I/O ports) for generation tasks, or a reference Verilog module for rewriting/optimization tasks. It produces a complete, syntactically correct Verilog module; for downstream tasks, the output is an optimized implementation with fewer AIG netlist nodes after Yosys synthesis or shorter SAT solving time. The discriminator guidance operates at the token level during autoregressive decoding, so the output is generated sequentially with each token's probability reweighted by the discriminator's conditional class probability.

## Method

BetterV fine-tunes 6.7B–7B LLMs via domain-specific instruct-tuning that leverages Verilog-to-C alignment (using the V2C tool) to transfer the LLM's existing C knowledge to Verilog, alongside Verilog autocompletion tasks. Data augmentation uses the fine-tuned LLM to generate synthetic modules from collected headers, filtered by EDA tools for syntactic correctness. The key contribution is a task-specific generative discriminator (a smaller 1.3B model) trained as a class-conditional language model with a hybrid loss combining next-token generative loss and sequence-level discriminative loss (Eq. 7). During inference, Bayes' rule reweights the LLM's next-token distribution with the discriminator's conditional probability (Eq. 8), and a two-stage token filter (cumulative probability threshold ρ and discriminator-confidence threshold τ) prunes the vocabulary before sampling. This plug-and-play discriminator can be retrained for any downstream EDA objective—synthesis node count, SAT verification time, or functional equivalence—without modifying the base LLM.

## Results

On the VerilogEval benchmark, BetterV-CodeQwen achieves the best pass@1 scores of 68.1% (machine) and 46.1% (human), outperforming GPT-4 by 8.1 and 2.6 points respectively. For pass@10, BetterV-CodeQwen reaches 84.5% (machine) and 58.2% (human). On downstream EDA tasks, the generative discriminator reduces synthesis netlist nodes by 46.52% on average compared to reference Verilog and cuts SAT verification runtime by 22.45% on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BetterV-CodeQwen (6.7B) outperforms GPT-4 on pass@1 by 8.1 points (68.1 vs. 60.0) on VerilogEval-machine and by 2.6 points (46.1 vs. 43.5) on VerilogEval-human, though GPT-4 retains a slight edge on human pass@5 (55.8 vs. 53.7) and pass@10 (58.9 vs. 58.2). Against the strongest open-source baseline RTLCoder-DeepSeek, BetterV-CodeQwen leads by 6.9 points on machine pass@1 (68.1 vs. 61.2) and 4.5 points on human pass@1 (46.1 vs. 41.6). The instruct-tuning alone yields large gains over base models: CodeLlama improves from 43.1 to 64.2 pass@1 (machine), DeepSeek from 52.2 to 67.8, and CodeQwen from 46.5 to 68.1. On downstream EDA tasks, the discriminator-guided BetterV reduces synthesis AIG nodes by 46.52% on average versus reference and 31.68% versus its own base model, and reduces SAT verification runtime by 22.45% versus reference and 13.99% versus base. All prior Verilog-generation baselines (ChipNeMo, Thakur et al., VerilogEval) trail BetterV by 15–25 points on machine pass@1, confirming that the combination of domain-specific instruct-tuning and discriminator guidance substantially narrows the gap with proprietary models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discriminator-guided controllable generation lineage (CTRL, GeDi, PPLM, DExperts) from natural language text control to engineering optimization in EDA. Prior Verilog generation work (Thakur et al., Dehaerne et al., VerilogEval, RTLCoder) focused on functional and syntactic correctness via fine-tuning or prompt engineering, but neglected downstream EDA metrics. BetterV departs by (1) using V2C alignment to transfer C-programming knowledge into Verilog understanding during instruct-tuning, avoiding the verbose and fallible LLM-generated descriptions used by prior works, and (2) training task-specific generative discriminators to guide generation toward optimized implementations (fewer synthesis nodes, shorter SAT verification time) rather than merely correct ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play discriminator framework offers a practical path for LLMs to participate in PPA optimization at the RTL stage, potentially reducing the iterative loops in industrial EDA flows. However, the approach is limited to open-source models requiring token-level probability access, and the computational overhead of running a second model at every decoding step is non-trivial. The downstream optimization results, while promising, are demonstrated on small problem sets (18 synthesis problems, 5 verification designs), leaving open questions about scalability to larger designs and more complex EDA stages. The paper also raises the broader question of whether discriminator-guided generation can generalize to other structured-language domains (SQL, LaTeX, genomics) with similar data-scarcity and precision constraints.
