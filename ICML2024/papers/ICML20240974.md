## Problem

Data augmentation is a key driver of representation quality in self-supervised learning, yet audio-visual contrastive learning has largely avoided it because augmentations easily distort the audio-visual correspondence that the contrastive objective relies on. Prior works resort to limited augmentation ranges or alternative supervision signals (masked modeling, temporal cues) rather than fully exploiting augmentation diversity. The paper asks how equivariance can be leveraged to capture augmentation-related information while preserving cross-modal alignment.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 10-second audio waveforms (converted to 1024×128 log-Mel spectrograms) and a single video frame from each 10-second clip, both tokenized into 16×16 patches for ViT-B/16 encoders. Augmentations are parameterized as 24-dimensional (audio) and 18-dimensional (visual) real vectors encoding crop coordinates, jitter factors, blur σ, flip flags, time-shift, and SpecAug masks. The framework outputs audio and visual embeddings in a shared inter-modal space for contrastive alignment; downstream, these support event classification (AudioSet, VGGSound), zero-shot retrieval, and action recognition.

## Method

EquiAV introduces a shared attention-based transformation predictor that maps a parameterized augmentation vector and the original representation to an equivariant embedding, using the augmentation encoding as query and the original features as key/value in multi-head attention followed by an FFN. In the intra-modal space, an NT-Xent loss (with the positive pair retained in the denominator to emphasize hard positives) aligns equivariant embeddings with augmented-input embeddings, teaching the predictor to capture augmentation-induced displacement. For inter-modal contrastive learning, instead of using a single augmented pair (which risks breaking correspondence), the method samples S augmentation vectors, generates S equivariant embeddings from the single original input via the shared predictor, and uses their centroid as the representative embedding for cross-modal contrastive loss. This centroid approximates the expectation over all augmented representations, providing diverse supervision while maintaining accurate audio-visual alignment, and incurs less than 1% additional compute since no augmented inputs pass through the encoder.

## Results

On the primary audio-visual event classification benchmark (Table 1), EquiAV achieves 46.6 mAP (A-V) on AudioSet-20K, 54.6 mAP (A-V) on AudioSet-2M, and 67.1% accuracy (A-V) on VGGSound, outperforming the strongest SSL baseline MAViL by +1.7, +1.3, and 0.0 points respectively. For zero-shot audio-visual retrieval on AudioSet, EquiAV achieves 30.1 R@1 (Video-to-Audio) and 29.6 R@1 (Audio-to-Video), substantially surpassing CAV-MAE (18.8 / 15.1 R@1). On single-modal audio classification, EquiAV reaches 62.6% on FSD-50K, exceeding XKD (58.5%) and CAV-MAE-Scale++ (45.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EquiAV outperforms all SSL-based audio-visual baselines on the main classification benchmark: it beats MAViL (the closest competitor) by +1.7 mAP on AudioSet-20K A-V and +1.3 mAP on AudioSet-2M A-V, and surpasses CAV-MAE by +4.6 mAP on AudioSet-20K A-V. On zero-shot retrieval, EquiAV achieves a dramatic margin over CAV-MAE (30.1 vs. 18.8 R@1 Video-to-Audio on AudioSet), and matches or exceeds models pre-trained on the much larger HowTo100M dataset (e.g., Boggust, Aranjelovic, AVLnet) on MSR-VTT. For single-modal audio classification, EquiAV wins on FSD-50K (62.6% vs. XKD's 58.5%) and SPC-v1 (97.8% vs. MAViL's 97.4%) but trails XKD slightly on ESC-50 (96.0% vs. 96.5%). On action recognition, EquiAV surpasses XKD on all three benchmarks (UCF101: 89.7 vs. 88.4, HMDB51: 64.4 vs. 62.2, Kinetics400: 57.3 vs. 56.5) and also outperforms CrissCross, AVID, and BraVe. Against supervised baselines (MBT, Attn AV), EquiAV achieves higher A-V mAP on both AudioSet splits while using no labels during pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends EquiMod's equivariant representation learning (a transformation predictor that maps augmentation parameters to latent-space displacements) from single-modal visual learning to the audio-visual contrastive setting. It departs from prior audio-visual contrastive methods (CAV-MAE, MAViL) that enriched representations via masked modeling or temporal supervision by instead leveraging data augmentation through equivariance, sidestepping the well-known problem that augmentations disrupt cross-modal correspondence. The key architectural departure is an attention-based transformation predictor (replacing EquiMod's linear layer or SIE's hypernetwork) whose weights are shared between intra-modal and inter-modal use, enabling equivariance learned within each modality to be transferred to the joint space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a fundamental tension in multi-modal contrastive learning—augmentation enriches representations but corrupts cross-modal correspondence—by showing that centroids of equivariant embeddings (computed cheaply from a single encoded input) outperform both original and augmented embeddings for inter-modal supervision. The <1% computational overhead for generating 16 equivariant representations makes this practically attractive. Open questions include whether the shared-predictor transfer mechanism generalizes to other multi-modal pairs (e.g., vision-language) and whether the equivariance principle can be applied at finer granularities (patch-level, temporal) rather than at the global embedding level.
