## Problem

Gradient regularization (GR) is widely used to improve generalization in over-parameterized networks, but its interaction with adaptive optimizers (Adam, RMSProp) and learning rate warmup has been poorly understood. This paper reveals that GR can cause serious performance degeneration in these adaptive optimization scenarios, with the effect worsening as model complexity increases, and provides both empirical and theoretical explanations for why this incompatibility arises.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242529/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The paper trains standard Vision Transformer (ViT-Ti/S/B), Swin, and CaiT architectures on RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR-10/100), 64×64 (Tiny-ImageNet), and 224×224 (ImageNet), producing class labels as output. The contribution is not an architectural change but a modification to the training procedure: the GR warmup strategies alter how the gradient regularization coefficient is scheduled during the initial epochs of training, while the model architecture and data pipeline remain unchanged.

## Method

The paper identifies that GR biases gradient norms downward during early training, which—when combined with adaptive optimizers—causes the variance of the adaptive learning rate to diverge (proven via a Taylor-series analysis showing Var(ψ) increases monotonically with the gradient-norm decay rate γ). To fix this, three GR warmup strategies are proposed: (1) r-warmup, which linearly ramps up both the perturbation radius r and regularization degree λ following the LR warmup schedule; (2) λ-warmup, which keeps r fixed and ramps λ from 0 to λ₀, interpolating between the vanilla gradient and the GR gradient; and (3) zero-warmup, which disables GR entirely during warmup (λ=0) and switches to full GR afterward. The zero-warmup strategy yields the largest gains (up to ~3% on CIFAR-10 for ViT-B with Adam) because it allows the optimizer to accumulate accurate gradient statistics before any norm-biasing is introduced, and it also reduces computation overhead during warmup by skipping the extra forward pass needed for the perturbed gradient.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with Adam optimizer, zero-warmup GR achieves 9.42% test error for ViT-B, 10.68% for ViT-S, and 13.61% for ViT-Ti, compared to 12.36%, 12.40%, and 13.92% for the standard GR baseline respectively. The improvement is most pronounced for larger models: ViT-B benefits by approximately 3 percentage points on CIFAR-10. On CIFAR-100, zero-warmup GR reduces ViT-B error from 36.65% to 35.75%. Across all model sizes and both Adam/RMSProp optimizers, all three proposed GR warmup strategies (r-warmup, λ-warmup, zero-warmup) consistently outperform the vanilla GR baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is standard GR (vanilla gradient regularization with LR warmup) from Zhao et al. (ICML 2022). All three proposed GR warmup strategies consistently outperform this baseline across every model size, optimizer, and dataset combination. The improvement scales with model complexity: on CIFAR-10 with Adam, zero-warmup GR improves ViT-B by 2.94 points (12.36% → 9.42%), ViT-S by 1.72 points (12.40% → 10.68%), and ViT-Ti by 0.31 points (13.92% → 13.61%). Among the three strategies, zero-warmup GR (using vanilla training during warmup, then switching to full GR) achieves the best results in most cases, while λ-warmup GR is a close second. Notably, the paper also shows in Table 1 that standard GR with LR warmup can actually underperform vanilla training with LR warmup (e.g., ViT-B Adam: 12.36% vs. 10.83%), a degeneration that the proposed warmup strategies fully resolve and then surpass.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient regularization (GR) line of work initiated by Zhao et al. (ICML 2022) and Karakida et al. (ICML 2023), which established GR as a generalization-improving technique and revealed its equivalence to SAM under a specific parameter setting (λ=r). It departs from prior work by identifying a previously unexamined failure mode: GR's biased gradient estimation destabilizes adaptive optimizer statistics (Adam, RMSProp) during the warmup phase, causing performance degradation that worsens with model scale. The theoretical analysis builds on Liu et al. (ICLR 2020)'s characterization of adaptive learning rate variance, extending it to the non-stationary case where gradient norms decay exponentially—a regime induced precisely by GR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This finding is directly actionable for practitioners using SAM or GR with adaptive optimizers and LR warmup—a very common modern training recipe—since the paper shows GR can be strictly worse than no regularization at all for larger models. The proposed zero-warmup GR is a trivially simple fix (use vanilla gradients during warmup, switch to GR afterward) that recovers and exceeds baseline performance. Limitations include testing primarily on ViT/Swin/CaiT architectures at CIFAR scale, and the theoretical analysis relies on Gaussian gradient assumptions and exponential decay that may not hold in all regimes. Open questions include whether the optimal warmup duration scales predictably with model size and how GR warmup interacts with other regularization techniques like weight decay or label smoothing.
