## Problem

The mainstream SatML approach has been to 'lift and shift' models designed for natural images or other modalities to satellite data with minimal tailoring, ignoring the unique spatial/temporal scales, spectral diversity, petabyte-scale volume, and deployment constraints of satellite data. This position paper argues that satellite data constitutes a distinct ML modality requiring a dedicated research agenda, and that continued adaptation of ill-suited approaches comes at a cost of performance, scalability, and missed research opportunities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241737/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Satellite data arrives as raster tensors (H×W×C) with up to 13+ spectral channels at 12-bit radiometric resolution (e.g., Sentinel-2), stacked along a temporal dimension for time series, covering the entire globe at fixed spatial resolutions ranging from 0.3 m/pixel (WorldView-3) to 500 m/pixel (MODIS). The deployment output is a dense prediction map covering every pixel in a geographic region—approximately 1.5 trillion pixels at 10 m resolution for global land area—rather than individual per-image predictions. Labels are often sparse, spatio-temporally clustered, and decoupled from specific observations, unlike standard supervised settings where each input has a definitive paired label.

## Method

As a position paper, the core contribution is a structured argument organized around four pillars: (1) cataloguing unique satellite data characteristics (logarithmic spatial/temporal scales, multi-spectral channels beyond 3-channel RGB, petabyte-scale archives, and sparse ground-referenced annotations), (2) identifying deployment and evaluation challenges (dense prediction at scale, spatially-aware cross-validation, and distinct ethical concerns), (3) presenting evidence that specialized SatML methods—rotation-equivariant CNNs, scale-aware masked autoencoders, meta-learning with variable spectral channels, and geolocation-aware self-supervised pre-training—outperform lifted-and-shifted ImageNet-based approaches, and (4) articulating how satellite data can enrich core ML research on distribution shift, self-supervised learning, multimodal fusion, and positional encodings. The paper closes with concrete action items for the community, including new impact-oriented benchmarks, spatially-aware evaluation protocols, CARE-principles-based ethical frameworks, and institutional recognition of SatML within core ML venues and funding structures.

## Results

This is a position paper with no empirical experiments or results tables. The authors cite supporting quantitative evidence from prior work, including: Bastani et al. (2023) showing satellite-specific pretraining improves performance by up to 18% over ImageNet pre-training; Brown et al. (2022) achieving state-of-the-art land cover segmentation with an architecture ~100× smaller than standard segmentation models; and Benson & Ecker (2020) reporting up to 30% accuracy drop for damage-detection models when evaluated on a new disaster site. The paper also highlights data-scale figures: 66 PB of public Copernicus data (Jan 2024), 140 PB expected from NISAR, and ~1.5 trillion pixels covering global land area at 10 m/pixel resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a position paper, this work does not conduct head-to-head empirical comparisons between methods. Instead, it contrasts two broad research strategies: the mainstream 'lift-and-shift' approach (adapting ImageNet-pretrained or natural-image architectures to satellite data with minimal modification) versus specialized SatML methods designed around the unique properties of satellite data (multispectral channels, rotation invariance, spatio-temporal structure, dense prediction at scale). The authors argue the latter consistently outperforms the former, citing examples such as rotation-equivariant CNNs outperforming larger standard CNNs (Marcos et al., 2018), satellite-specific SSL pretraining beating ImageNet weights (Wang et al., 2023b; Stewart et al., 2023), and structured masking strategies outperforming random masking in masked autoencoders (Tseng et al., 2023; Hackstein et al., 2024). No single benchmark or unified evaluation protocol is proposed; the paper calls for the community to develop such benchmarks as a future action item.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper synthesizes and reframes prior survey work on ML for remote sensing (Zhu et al., 2017; Tuia et al., 2021, 2023; Reichstein et al., 2019; Rolnick et al., 2022) by making a specific conceptual argument: satellite data should be recognized as a distinct ML modality, analogous to how natural images, text, and biomedical images each spawned their own architectures (CNN, Transformer, U-Net). It departs from the dominant 'lift and shift' paradigm in SatML by cataloguing evidence that specialized approaches—rotation-equivariant CNNs, pixel-set encoders, satellite-specific SSL (SatMAE, SSL4EO), and meta-learning—outperform generic image models, and by arguing that the community must shift from treating satellite data as an application area to treating it as a first-class modality with its own research agenda.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies concrete open questions that remain unresolved: how to design spatially-aware evaluation protocols that reflect real deployment, how to handle petabyte-scale data curation and efficient inference for dense global prediction, how to develop ethical frameworks (CARE principles, model cards for geospatial models) that address the dual-use nature of satellite monitoring, and how to align benchmark-driven research with on-the-ground information needs in low-resource settings. Its practical impact lies in potentially reshaping venue acceptance criteria, funding priorities, and community norms for SatML research. A key limitation is that, as a position paper, it provides no new empirical results; its persuasiveness rests entirely on the cited evidence from other works, and the proposed community-level changes (new benchmarks, impact challenges, cross-disciplinary guardrails) remain aspirational without concrete implementation plans.
