## Problem

Interior-point methods based on self-concordant barriers have well-established global complexity guarantees for convex optimization, but their theoretical analysis in the non-convex setting was largely restricted to special constraint structures (non-negativity, symmetric cones) or quadratic objectives. This paper closes that gap by providing the first complexity guarantees for first- and second-order barrier algorithms applied to general non-convex objectives with arbitrary closed convex set constraints and linear equality constraints.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume a non-convex objective f (possibly non-differentiable at the relative boundary of K̄), a ν-self-concordant barrier h for K̄, a full-rank linear operator A with right-hand side b, and an initial ν-analytic center x⁰. They produce a sequence of feasible iterates in the relative interior of X = K ∩ L that terminate at an ε-KKT point (FOABM) or an (ε₁,ε₂)-2KKT point (SOABM), with the second-order condition measured relative to the barrier Hessian H(x̄) on the null space L₀. No projection onto the feasible set is ever required, and the objective need only be continuously differentiable on the relative interior.

## Method

Both algorithms minimize a potential F_μ = f + μh and determine search directions by solving subproblems over the tangent space L₀ = ker(A), regularized by the local norm ‖·‖_x induced by the barrier Hessian H(x). The first-order method (FOABM) minimizes a quadratic model Q_μ^(1) = F_μ + ⟨∇F_μ, v⟩ + ½‖v‖_x², while the second-order method (SOABM) minimizes a cubic-regularized model Q_μ,L^(2) = F_μ + ⟨∇F_μ, v⟩ + ½⟨∇²f(x)v,v⟩ + (L/6)‖v‖_x³, where the cubic term uses the local norm rather than the Euclidean norm. Both employ an adaptive backtracking line search to estimate the local Lipschitz constant without requiring it a priori, and the Dikin ellipsoid property (W(x;1) ⊆ K) guarantees that all iterates remain feasible. The key theoretical insight is that the local smoothness assumptions (Assumptions 3.1, 4.1) are stated relative to the barrier-induced local norm, which on bounded sets subsumes the standard Lipschitz-gradient and Lipschitz-Hessian conditions, yielding O(ε⁻²) and O(ε⁻³/²) iteration complexities that match the optimal unconstrained non-convex bounds.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no numerical experiments. The headline results are iteration complexity bounds: FOABM (first-order) achieves O(ε⁻²) outer iterations to reach a 2ε-KKT point, and SOABM (second-order) achieves O(max{ε₁⁻³/², ε₂⁻³/²}) outer iterations to reach an (ε₁, ε₂)-2KKT point. These match the optimal worst-case complexity bounds known for unconstrained non-convex optimization under standard Lipschitz-gradient and Lipschitz-Hessian assumptions. The bounds hold under weaker local smoothness assumptions (relative to the barrier's local norm) and for general convex set constraints, without requiring projections onto the feasible set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not run numerical benchmarks but positions its contribution against existing algorithmic families. Compared to proximal/projection-based methods (Ghadimi & Lan 2016, Cartis et al. 2012b, Birgin & Martinez 2018), FOABM/SOABM avoid the costly projection onto X̄ = K̄ ∩ L while maintaining the same O(ε⁻²) and O(ε⁻³/²) complexity. Compared to augmented Lagrangian methods (Birgin & Martinez 2020, Grapiglia & Yuan 2020), the proposed algorithms generate strictly feasible iterates in the relative interior at every step. Compared to existing barrier methods for non-convex problems (Haeser et al. 2019, O'Neill & Wright 2020, He & Lu 2022, Dvurechensky & Staudigl 2024), the key advance is generality: those works require K̄ to be a cone (non-negative orthant or symmetric cone) and rely on logarithmically homogeneous barriers, whereas this paper handles arbitrary closed convex sets with general self-concordant barriers. The complexity bounds match the optimal unconstrained rates (Carmon et al. 2019a,b), and the authors conjecture optimality via a reduction argument from the unconstrained lower bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical interior-point framework of Nesterov & Nemirovskii (1994) from convex to non-convex optimization by leveraging self-concordant barrier (SCB) theory and the cubic-regularized Newton method of Nesterov & Polyak (2006). Prior barrier methods for non-convex problems were restricted to special constraint structures (non-negative orthant, symmetric cones) and often required logarithmically homogeneous barriers tied to conic geometry. The key departure here is that the authors work with arbitrary closed convex sets K and general SCBs (without logarithmic homogeneity), using a local smoothness assumption relative to the barrier's Hessian-induced norm rather than global Lipschitz conditions, thereby unifying first- and second-order methods under a single penalty-based framework that avoids costly projections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a notable gap in the theoretical analysis of interior-point methods by showing that barrier algorithms for general non-convex problems with general convex set constraints achieve the same worst-case complexity as unconstrained non-convex optimization (O(ε⁻²) first-order, O(ε⁻³/²) second-order), without requiring projections or differentiability at the boundary. This is practically relevant for ML applications like constrained nonlinear regression with sparsity penalties or training Input Convex Neural Networks, where the feasible set is a general convex set and the objective is non-differentiable at the boundary. Limitations include the absence of numerical experiments, the requirement of a known analytic center, and the local smoothness assumptions that, while subsuming standard Lipschitz conditions on bounded sets, are not yet validated empirically. Open questions include extensions to inexact oracles, Newton-CG variants, and nonlinear functional constraints.
