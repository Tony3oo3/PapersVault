## Problem

Pre-trained Vision Transformers are typically reused at full scale for downstream tasks, ignoring resource constraints like memory and latency on edge devices, and risking negative transfer. Prior Learngene methods addressed this by selecting a few integral layers and manually stacking them with randomly initialized layers, but this overlooks the knowledge distributed across unselected layers and cannot adaptively produce models at arbitrary target sizes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242130/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained ViT ancestry model (DeiT-Tiny/Small/Base or Swin-Base) and downstream task data (e.g., CIFAR-100, iNaturalist-2019, Oxford Flowers). It produces initialized descendant ViT models with user-specified elastic scales, parameterized by the number of attention heads H_c per layer (width) and the number of FFN layers L_c (depth), ranging from 1.3M to 86.6M parameters. The intermediate artifact is a compact 'learngene' — a small set of probabilistically mixed MSA heads and FFN layers — that is trained for 100 epochs before expansion.

## Method

PEG samples a compact learngene from the ancestry model via probabilistic Gaussian mixtures: each MSA learngene head is a convex combination of all H_a heads in a layer with weights drawn from a standard Gaussian (normalized to sum to 1), and each FFN learngene layer is a convex combination of all L_a FFN layers with analogous weights. Theorems 3.1 and 3.2 guarantee that a finite Gaussian mixture with O((log 1/ε)^D') components can approximate the ancestry model's parameter distribution to arbitrary accuracy ε, justifying that the compact learngene retains significant knowledge. During expansion, a non-linear learnable mapping (a ReLU neural network) projects the H_b sampled heads to H_c heads and the L_b sampled FFN layers to L_c layers, enabling elastic initialization at any target width and depth. The projection weights W^O are proportionally pruned or expanded, and layer normalization, patch embeddings, and position embeddings are directly inherited from the ancestry model.

## Results

On the downstream task comparison (Table 2), PEG achieves 87.85% top-1 accuracy on Oxford Flowers (Tiny) and 91.01% (Small), surpassing the best non-fine-tuning baseline (Auto-Learngene) by 7.84% and 3.99% respectively. On iNaturalist-2019 (Small), PEG reaches 67.73%, exceeding Auto-Learngene by 7.81%. On ImageNet-1K (Table 1), PEG initialized with only 50 epochs of fine-tuning matches or exceeds From-Scratch trained for 100 epochs across all Small and Base-scale configurations (e.g., 70.24% vs 68.56% for Base, H_c=12, L_c=12), while reducing training cost by ~1.7×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PEG consistently outperforms From-Scratch, Heur-Learngene, and Weight-Multi across all seven downstream datasets and both Tiny/Small scales, with margins ranging from ~1% (CUB-200, Tiny) to over 20% (iNat-2019, Tiny). Against Auto-Learngene (the closest Learngene-based competitor), PEG wins on 6 of 7 datasets for Tiny and all 7 for Small, with the largest gap being +7.81% on iNaturalist-2019 (Small: 67.73 vs 59.92). Against full Fine-tuning of the pre-trained DeiT, PEG wins on Flowers for Tiny (87.85 vs 84.79) but trails on most other tasks, particularly iNat-2019 (56.80 vs 58.12 for Tiny; 67.73 vs 68.48 for Small), suggesting that full-model fine-tuning still retains an edge on large, diverse datasets. On ImageNet-1K, PEG with 50 epochs of fine-tuning matches or exceeds From-Scratch trained for 100 epochs on all Small and Base configurations, while requiring ~1.7× less total training compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Learngene framework (Wang et al., 2022; 2023), which condenses knowledge from a large ancestry model into a compact representation for initializing downstream models. The key departure is replacing the prior work's integral-layer selection and manual random-layer integration with a probabilistic Gaussian mixture sampling of individual MSA heads and FFN layers, followed by non-linear expansion to elastic scales. The theoretical grounding draws on Gaussian mixture approximation theorems (Ghosal & Van Der Vaart, 2001), while the mixture-of-experts literature (Switch Transformers, probabilistic attention keys) informs the sampling mechanism but is repurposed here as an initialization strategy rather than a training-time routing technique.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PEG addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: foundation models are typically deployed at a fixed scale, but real-world resource constraints (edge devices, latency budgets) demand models of varying sizes. The ability to sample a single compact learngene and expand it to arbitrary widths and depths without retraining from scratch could streamline the model-deployment pipeline. However, the approach is currently validated only on DeiT and Swin architectures at modest scales (up to 86M parameters), and the theoretical guarantee is an existence result rather than a bound on the specific sampling procedure used in practice. Open questions include whether the probabilistic mixture sampling generalizes to larger foundation models, non-ViT architectures, or tasks beyond image classification.
