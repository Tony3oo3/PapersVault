## Problem

Curriculum learning has been shown theoretically to speed up learning and improve generalisation, yet large-scale empirical studies find its benefits to be marginal in standard deep learning pipelines. This paper identifies a potential cause for this theory-practice gap: the high degree of overparameterisation that is ubiquitous in deep learning can erode the margin of improvement that curricula provide, effectively making the 'lottery ticket' already present in the overparameterised network.

## Input / Output

Theoretically, the framework consumes i.i.d. samples from a 4-cluster XOR-like Gaussian mixture in R^d (with orthogonal centroid directions μ₁, μ₂ and noise level σ) and produces ODE trajectories of order parameters (neuron overlap matrix Q, centroid alignment M, and second-layer weights v) that determine generalisation error and cluster coverage. Empirically, FashionMNIST images are corrupted with i.i.d. Gaussian noise (σ=0.5) and CIFAR10 images are given distracting frames of variable width (1–6 pixels) to create tunable difficulty, with a fixed 10% easy-sample fraction used across all protocols.

## Method

The paper derives a closed-system of ODEs in the high-dimensional limit (d→∞) for a 2-layer ReLU network with K hidden units trained via online gradient descent on the XOR-like Gaussian Mixture, tracking order parameters that capture neuron-centroid alignment and cluster coverage. A 'fading' curriculum is introduced where a fraction α of early samples have their signal norm rescaled by a factor φ (up to 3), and this is compared against random ordering and no-fading baselines. The central result is that increasing K (overparameterisation) exponentially raises the probability of a good initialisation (lottery ticket), which shrinks the Goldilocks noise regime where curricula are beneficial and ultimately makes the curriculum–no-fading performance gap vanish. Empirical validation on MLPs (FashionMNIST) and CNNs (CIFAR10) confirms that both width-wise and depth-wise overparameterisation reduce the curriculum advantage, consistent with the theoretical prediction.

## Results

In the XOR-like Gaussian Mixture (XGM) model, the paper shows that the generalisation error gap between curriculum and no-fading training peaks in a 'Goldilocks' noise regime (intermediate σ) and shrinks monotonically as the number of hidden units K increases, eventually vanishing in the highly overparameterised limit. On Fashion-MNIST (corrupted with Gaussian noise σ=0.5), the curriculum-over-random-order accuracy gap for an MLP first increases with width, peaking around width=100, then decreases as width or depth grows further. On CIFAR-10 (corrupted with distracting frames), increasing the number of CNN filters from small to large values consistently narrows the accuracy gap between curriculum and random-order training, confirming the theoretical prediction that overparameterisation erodes curriculum benefits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three training protocols—curriculum (fading), random order, and no-fading—across varying degrees of overparameterisation (K=4, 16, 64, …) in the XGM model, and across varying network widths/depths on Fashion-MNIST and filter counts on CIFAR-10. At the minimal parameterisation (K=4), curriculum achieves the largest asymptotic advantage in cluster coverage and generalisation error over both random order and no-fading, particularly in the Goldilocks noise range (σ≈0.3–0.5). As K increases, the curriculum gap shrinks: by K=64, all three protocols achieve near-identical coverage and loss, indicating the curriculum benefit has been absorbed by the lottery-ticket effect of overparameterisation. On Fashion-MNIST, the curriculum gap peaks at width≈100 (the Goldilocks point) and then diminishes with further width or depth increases; on CIFAR-10, the gap between curriculum and random-order accuracy monotonically decreases as the number of filters grows. Random order and no-fading converge in performance for long training times, while curriculum retains a small residual advantage, consistent with the 'eureka effect' from cognitive science.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate analytical threads: the ODE-based high-dimensional analysis of 2-layer networks on the XOR-like Gaussian Mixture (Refinetti et al., 2021; Ben Arous et al., 2023), which characterised the lottery-ticket effect of overparameterisation, and the curriculum learning literature (Weinshall et al., 2018; Abbe et al., 2021; Saglietti et al., 2022), which had studied curricula primarily in convex or simplified settings. The key departure is introducing overparameterisation as a variable into the curriculum analysis, revealing that the two strategies compete rather than synergise. The paper also draws on cognitive science concepts of fading (Pashler & Mozer, 2013) to define sample difficulty in the synthetic XGM framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete theoretical mechanism for the long-standing empirical puzzle of why curriculum learning fails to deliver its predicted benefits in modern deep learning: high overparameterisation erodes the margin of improvement that curricula can provide. This reframes curriculum design as a problem that is most relevant for moderately parameterised models or tasks where the network is not yet in the lottery-ticket regime. Open questions include whether the 2-layer XGM analysis generalises to deeper architectures, whether the NLP/RL exceptions (where curricula still help at scale) reflect genuinely under-parameterised effective capacity, and how to design curricula that remain effective in the overparameterised regime.
