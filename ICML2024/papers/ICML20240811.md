## Problem

Decentralized federated learning eliminates the central server but loses the global model that provides generalizability, making it difficult for each client to learn representations that transfer across other clients' domains. Existing methods that handle data heterogeneity (non-i.i.d. distributions) or model heterogeneity (different architectures due to varying hardware) typically require a central server, access to public real data, or both. DESA is the first method to simultaneously address data heterogeneity, model heterogeneity, serverless operation, and the absence of public real data in a single framework.

## Input / Output

Each client consumes three inputs during training: its own private image dataset (e.g., 28×28 digit images or 32×32 CIFAR-10-C corrupted images), a globally shared synthetic anchor dataset generated offline via MMD-based distribution matching (class-balanced, typically 50–200 images per class), and averaged logits from neighboring clients computed on the synthetic data. The output is a set of client-specific CNN models (ConvNet or AlexNet) that produce K-class labels; each model is evaluated both on its own local test set (intra-client) and on every other client's test set (inter-client) to measure cross-domain generalization. Notably, during FL training only lightweight logits (K values per synthetic image) are exchanged between neighbors, not full model parameters.

## Method

DESA operates in three phases: (1) each client locally synthesizes a small class-balanced anchor dataset by minimizing MMD between raw and synthetic feature distributions using a randomly sampled encoder, then all clients share their synthetic data to form a global anchor set D^Syn; (2) during decentralized training, a REG loss (supervised contrastive loss) pulls the client's local feature embeddings toward the synthetic anchor embeddings in latent space, enforcing domain-invariant representations; (3) a KD loss (KL-divergence) aligns the client's logits on D^Syn with the averaged logits of its neighbors on the same synthetic data, enabling knowledge transfer between heterogeneous architectures without parameter averaging. The total loss is L_CE + λ_REG·L_REG + λ_KD·L_KD, where the cross-entropy is computed on both local and synthetic data. Theoretically, the paper derives a generalization bound via HΔH divergence showing that under mild conditions the bound is tighter than local-only training, with the REG loss controlling the domain divergence term and the KD loss controlling the labeling-function discrepancy term.

## Results

On the heterogeneous model setting (Table 2), DESA achieves the best averaged global accuracy on DIGITS (74.47%) and OFFICE (54.46%), outperforming the strongest baselines FedProto (69.70%) and FCCL-Cifar100 (46.07%) by 4.8 and 8.4 points respectively. On CIFAR10C with 20% client sampling, DESA reaches 52.80%, surpassing all baselines except FCCL-Cifar100 (50.78%) which uses a public dataset. In the homogeneous model setting (Table 3), DESA achieves 95.53% on DIGITS and 82.92% on OFFICE, beating the best server-based baseline Scaffold (94.95% and 77.52%) while operating entirely serverless.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DESA outperforms all heterogeneous-model baselines on DIGITS and OFFICE by substantial margins: FedProto (69.70% DIGITS avg) is the closest competitor but still trails by 4.8 points, while FedHe (64.81%) and FedDF (67.32%) lag further behind. On OFFICE, DESA (54.46%) beats FCCL-Cifar100 (46.07%) by 8.4 points, and notably FCCL and FedDF both fail (NaN losses) on several DIGITS/OFFICE splits when using FMNIST as public data, revealing their strong dependence on specific public datasets. On CIFAR10C (heterogeneous), DESA (48.19% at 10% sampling) is the only method that loses to FCCL-Cifar100 (51.70%), which benefits from genuine semantic overlap between CIFAR-100 and CIFAR-10C; at 20% sampling DESA (52.80%) surpasses FCCL (50.78%). In the homogeneous setting, DESA (95.53% DIGITS, 82.92% OFFICE) beats all server-based baselines including Scaffold (94.95%, 77.52%) and MOON (94.37%, 73.64%) despite having no central server for model aggregation. On CIFAR10C homogeneous, DESA (64.47% at 10%) slightly underperforms Scaffold (65.66%) due to the extreme decentralization at low sampling ratios, but recovers to the best result (68.13%) at 20% sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

DESA sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) anchor-based feature regularization from VHL (Tang et al., 2022) and domain adaptation, (2) dataset distillation via distribution matching (Zhao & Bilen, 2023) for lightweight synthetic data generation, and (3) knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) as a substitute for parameter averaging under model heterogeneity. The key departure from prior heterogeneous FL methods (FedHe, FedDF, FCCL, FedProto) is that DESA requires neither a central server nor public real data, and from prior decentralized FL methods it additionally handles model heterogeneity. The theoretical grounding borrows the HΔH divergence framework from Ben-David et al. (2010) to derive a generalization bound that explicitly accounts for the synthetic and KD data components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DESA addresses a practically important gap: serverless FL with both data and model heterogeneity is the realistic setting for IoT and mobile deployments where clients have varying hardware and no trusted central authority. The lightweight communication profile (sharing only logits on synthetic anchors rather than full model parameters) makes it attractive for bandwidth-constrained networks. However, the evaluation is limited to relatively small-scale datasets (digits, office-31, CIFAR-10C) and modest architectures (ConvNet, AlexNet), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger vision or language tasks. The paper also acknowledges that performance degrades under very low client sampling ratios in the serverless setting, and the synthetic data quality (which underpins the theoretical guarantee) is an approximate rather than exact solution.
