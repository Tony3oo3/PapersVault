## Problem

Statistical generalization theory, which studies whether a model reaching a global minimum of training loss also achieves low test loss (the interpolation regime), cannot explain why LLMs exhibit OOD rule extrapolation, in-context learning, and data-efficient fine-tuning despite already achieving near-optimal test loss. The paper identifies the root cause: autoregressive probabilistic models are inherently non-identifiable, so models with zero or near-zero KL divergence (equivalent test loss) can behave markedly differently on out-of-distribution inputs, making test loss an insufficient predictor of these practically important properties.

## Input / Output

For the empirical case study, a decoder-only Transformer is trained via maximum likelihood on sequences from a probabilistic context-free grammar generating a^n b^n strings, then evaluated on OOD prompts (e.g., 'abaa') that have exactly zero probability under the training distribution. The theoretical case studies operate on mixture-of-HMMs pre-training distributions (following Xie et al., 2022) to construct counterexamples. Outputs include completion distributions on OOD prompts, rule extrapolation accuracy (the proportion of completions respecting the matching rule R1), and formal proofs establishing non-identifiability of ICL and parameter equivalence.

## Method

The paper defines three notions of non-identifiability for AR probabilistic models: functional non-identifiability (models differing only on zero-probability prefixes are KL-indistinguishable), ε-non-identifiability (models within ε KL can lack a property like ICL even with infinite data), and parameter non-identifiability (functionally equivalent models with different parametrizations). Three case studies demonstrate practical relevance: (1) a Transformer trained on the a^n b^n PCFG achieves 43.7% zero-shot rule extrapolation on OOD prompts despite the loss being agnostic to this behavior, confirmed by adversarial and oracle training variants that reach identical test loss but vastly different extrapolation accuracy; (2) Proposition 3.1 proves that for any ε > 0, a distribution q_n exists within ε KL of a mixture-of-HMMs distribution p that exhibits ICL, yet q_n provably lacks ICL by swapping the top two conditional probabilities on prompt-structured sequences; (3) parameter non-identifiability is illustrated via Liu et al.'s embedded-Transformer experiment showing 10% downstream accuracy differences from different parametrizations at identical pre-training loss. The paper advocates shifting from the interpolation regime to the 'saturation regime,' where both train and test loss are near-optimal, and calls for studying inductive biases, LLM-relevant generalization measures (compositional, systematic, symbolic), and transferability as the primary theoretical targets.

## Results

The primary empirical result demonstrates that a decoder-only Transformer trained via maximum likelihood on the a^n b^n PCFG achieves 43.7% OOD rule extrapolation accuracy (averaged over 20 seeds), well above the 0% chance level at initialization. Critically, three models trained under standard, adversarial, and oracle conditions all reach the same minimal test loss yet exhibit widely varying rule extrapolation performance, demonstrating that test loss is insensitive to this behavior. Theoretically, Proposition 3.1 proves that for any ε > 0, there exists a distribution q_n within ε KL divergence of a mixture-of-HMMs pre-training distribution p that provably lacks in-context learning, establishing ε-non-identifiability of ICL even with infinite data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The internal empirical comparison in §3.1 contrasts three training conditions on the a^n b^n PCFG: standard MLE training (43.7% rule extrapolation), adversarial training with poisoned data, and oracle training with helpful data. All three achieve identical minimal test loss, but their OOD rule extrapolation accuracies differ substantially, directly demonstrating that the loss function cannot distinguish between models with and without meaningful extrapolation behavior. The theoretical contribution (Prop. 3.1) builds on Xie et al. (2022)'s mixture-of-HMMs ICL framework by showing that their ICL guarantee is fragile: an arbitrarily small KL perturbation can destroy ICL, meaning the emergence of ICL is not a consequence of minimizing negative log-likelihood alone. The paper positions these findings as motivation for studying inductive biases in the saturation regime rather than relying on statistical generalization theory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the interpolation regime paradigm (Zhang et al., 2016; Kawaguchi et al., 2022) by arguing that a second shift is needed: from asking whether training-loss minimization yields good test loss, to asking what additional properties hold at the (non-unique) global minimum of test loss. It adopts and generalizes the 'saturation regime' terminology from Liu et al. (2023a), who demonstrated parameter non-identifiability in transfer learning, and applies identifiability theory from statistical inference and causal discovery to AR probabilistic models. The ICL analysis builds directly on Xie et al. (2022)'s HMM mixture framework, showing that their ICL guarantee is fragile under ε-relaxation of the KL objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean conceptual framework (three notions of non-identifiability) and concrete mathematical counterexamples showing that statistical generalization is provably insufficient to explain rule extrapolation, ICL, and fine-tunability in AR LLMs. However, as a position paper, its empirical evidence is limited to small Transformers on toy PCFGs, and the practical implications for scaling to real LLMs remain speculative. Key open questions include: how to formalize OOD generalization measures beyond test loss, which inductive biases (architectural, optimization, data) select useful solutions among the equivalence class of global minima, and whether algorithmic information theory (Kolmogorov complexity, RASP) can provide tractable characterizations of LLM-relevant inductive biases.
