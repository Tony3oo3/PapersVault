## Problem

Designing optimal knowledge distillation strategies for object detection remains heavily reliant on manual expert design, as distillation behaviors vary significantly across detectors with different backbones, paradigms (one-stage vs. two-stage), and labeling schemes (anchor-based vs. anchor-free). Existing automated approaches like Auto-KD only search on classification tasks and transfer to detection with a small, generic search space, missing task-specific knowledge such as foreground-background features and localization responses. This paper asks: can we automatically discover the best distillation configuration for diverse detector pairs without expert tuning?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241093/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained teacher detector and a student detector (which may differ in backbone, paradigm, and labeling scheme) along with RGB images from COCO. During search, it operates on mini-COCO (25K training, 5K validation images) as a proxy; after discovering the optimal distiller, the student is trained on the full 120K-image COCO training set. The output is a concrete distillation policy specifying transforms, distance functions, and loss weights for each of five knowledge channels, which when applied yields bounding boxes, class labels, and (for Mask RCNN) segmentation masks from the student detector.

## Method

DetKDS constructs a detection-specific search space of over 30,000 candidates spanning five knowledge inputs (global features, foreground-background features, instance features, logits, and localization responses), each with cascaded transforms (channel/spatial attention → multi-scale pooling → normalization across N/C/HW dimensions), advanced distance functions (ℓ₁, ℓ₂, Pearson, SSIM, KL, IoU/C-IoU/E-IoU/G-IoU/D-IoU), and loss weights from {0.1, 0.5, 1, 2, 5, 8, 10}. A divide-and-conquer evolutionary algorithm first searches each individual distillation loss in parallel using genetic operators (selection, crossover, mutation) with a distiller filter and 20% proxy subset for acceleration, then optimizes the combined weights of the discovered losses. Theoretical analysis shows this decomposition reduces search complexity from O(n^m) to O(m·n) and provides tighter approximation guarantees than evolving the full space. Discovered distillers for global, logits, and localization losses generalize across detector types, enabling transfer without re-searching for new student-teacher pairs.

## Results

On COCO val, DetKDS achieves state-of-the-art AP across all four detector paradigms with ResNet-101 teachers and ResNet-50 students: 41.0 AP on Faster-RCNN (+2.6 over baseline), 40.2 AP on RetinaNet (+2.8), 43.7 AP on GFL (+3.5), and 42.5 AP on FCOS (+4.0). For heterogeneous backbones (Table 9), DetKDS gains 3.7–4.1 AP over student baselines, and for cross-paradigm teachers (Table 8), it achieves 2.7–4.0 AP improvements. On Deformable DETR (Table 7), DetKDS reaches 46.2 AP, surpassing FGD (44.1) and MGD (44.0) by over 2 AP. The full search process requires only ~5 hours on 8×V100 GPUs using proxy settings and divide-and-conquer evolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DetKDS outperforms all 15 compared baselines across every detector paradigm. On Faster-RCNN, DetKDS (41.0 AP) beats the strongest prior methods—DiffKD (40.6), PKD (40.5), and FGD (40.4)—by 0.4–0.6 AP, and surpasses Auto-KD (40.1) by 0.9 AP. On RetinaNet, DetKDS (40.2) edges out MasKD (39.8) and DiffKD (39.7) by 0.4–0.5 AP, while FGD (39.6) and Auto-KD (39.5) trail by 0.6–0.7 AP. On GFL, DetKDS (43.7) leads PKD (43.3) by 0.4 AP and LD (43.0) by 0.7 AP, with a notable 1.4 AP margin over SKD (42.3). On FCOS, DetKDS (42.5) narrowly beats DiffKD (42.4) by 0.1 AP and FGD (42.1) by 0.4 AP. For heterogeneous scenarios, DetKDS consistently outperforms FGD, CWD, KD-Zero, and MasKD by 0.3–1.0 AP across two-stage, one-stage, and anchor-free detectors with ResNeXt-101 teachers (Table 9), and achieves 2.7–4.0 AP gains with cross-paradigm teachers including Mask-RCNN-Swin-S (Table 8).

## Relation to Prior Work

DetKDS extends the AutoML-for-distillation paradigm established by Auto-KD (Li et al., 2023b) from classification to object detection, addressing three key gaps: task-specific knowledge inputs (foreground-background, instance, localization features), a much larger search space (30,000 vs. 2,000 candidates), and a divide-and-conquer evolutionary strategy replacing MCTS. It also builds on the localization distillation formulation from LD (Zheng et al., 2022) for capturing bounding-box regression outputs. Conceptually, the paper departs from the handcrafted detection distillation lineage (FGD, PKD, GID, Defeat) by treating distiller design as a search problem rather than an expert-design problem, while unifying these prior methods as special cases within its search space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DetKDS provides the first automated framework for detection distillation that removes the need for expert design and per-detector tuning, with the practical benefit that searched distillers (especially L_global with catt→scale2→normN) generalize across two-stage, one-stage, and anchor-free detectors. The derived design guidance—favoring global/instance feature distillation, channel attention, and simple L1 distances with weights in [0.1, 10]—offers actionable heuristics for future detection distillation work. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more diverse architectures (e.g., pure ViT detectors), whether self-KD scenarios can eliminate teacher preparation costs, and whether the search framework extends to other dense prediction tasks like semantic segmentation or depth estimation.
