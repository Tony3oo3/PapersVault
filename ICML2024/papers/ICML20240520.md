## Problem

Existing multivariate time series imputation methods suffer from three coupled limitations: they typically train on fixed-length patches (local horizon), missing global trends and periodic patterns; they assume regular sampling intervals, failing on irregularly timestamped data; and they are all offline, requiring repeated passes over the full dataset, which is infeasible for streaming applications. No prior method simultaneously handles continuous-time imputation, probabilistic uncertainty quantification, and efficient online updates.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multivariate time series with D channels observed at N irregularly spaced timestamps, along with a binary mask indicating which (channel, timestamp) entries are observed. It produces point estimates and full posterior distributions (mean and covariance) at any continuous timestamp—including timestamps never seen during training (the all-channel-missing case)—plus interpretable learned trend and seasonality factor functions. The online setting means each new observation is processed in a single forward pass with O(D_r + D_s) cost per timestamp, with no retraining or backpropagation.

## Method

BayOTIDE decomposes the multivariate series X(t) = U·V(t) into D_r trend factors and D_s seasonality factors, where each factor is a 1-D temporal function modeled by a Gaussian Process (Matérn kernel for trends, periodic kernel for seasonality) and U is a channel-wise weight matrix. The key computational trick converts each GP prior into an equivalent linear time-invariant stochastic differential equation (LTI-SDE) and then into a discrete state-space model, reducing GP inference to a Kalman filter with linear cost. Online posterior updates use conditional expectation propagation (CEP) with moment matching to approximate the likelihood of each new observation as closed-form Gaussian/Gamma message factors for the weights, noise precision, and state variables, which are merged sequentially. A Rauch-Tung-Striebel smoother then recovers the full posterior over all states, and probabilistic interpolation at arbitrary timestamps follows from the Gaussian state transition between neighboring observed points.

## Results

On the 50% observed-ratio setting, BayOTIDE achieves the best RMSE, MAE, and CRPS across all three real-world datasets: Traffic-Guangzhou (RMSE 3.820, MAE 2.687, CRPS 0.055), Solar-Power (RMSE 1.699, MAE 0.734, CRPS 0.122), and Uber-Move (RMSE 1.901, MAE 1.361, CRPS 0.062). These gains are achieved with a single online pass over the data, whereas all baselines require multiple offline training epochs. At 70% observed ratio, BayOTIDE maintains its lead with RMSE 3.724 (Traffic), 1.621 (Solar), and 1.832 (Uber), and CRPS 0.053, 0.116, and 0.061 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BayOTIDE outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset and metric at both 50% and 70% observed ratios. Against the strongest deterministic baseline TIDER (ICLR 2023), BayOTIDE reduces RMSE by ~19% on Traffic-Guangzhou (3.820 vs. 4.708) and ~3% on Solar-Power (1.699 vs. 1.679, where TIDER is slightly better on RMSE but BayOTIDE wins on MAE and CRPS). Among probabilistic offline methods, CSBI (Schrödinger Bridge) is the closest competitor, yet BayOTIDE still improves CRPS by 26% on Traffic (0.055 vs. 0.074), 20% on Solar (0.122 vs. 0.153), and 17% on Uber (0.062 vs. 0.075). Notably, BayOTIDE achieves these gains while processing data in a single online pass, whereas CSDI, CSBI, and all DNN-based methods (BRITS, NAOMI, SAITS) require multiple training epochs and sequence patching. The ablation variants confirm that both the learned channel weights (BayOTIDE-fix-weight degrades severely, e.g. RMSE 11.032 on Traffic) and the seasonality factors (BayOTIDE-trend-only is slightly worse) are essential components.

## Relation to Prior Work

BayOTIDE extends the disentangled temporal representation framework of TIDER (ICLR 2023) and the seasonal-trend decomposition lineage (STL, CoST) by casting the decomposition in a continuous-time, Bayesian setting. It leverages the well-established equivalence between stationary-kernel GPs and linear time-invariant SDEs (Hartikainen & Särkkä, 2010; Särkkä, 2013) to convert GP priors into state-space models, enabling Kalman-filter-based inference. The online inference algorithm draws on conditional expectation propagation (Wang & Zhe, 2019) and moment-matching techniques, while the probabilistic imputation at arbitrary timestamps follows the standard SSM interpolation framework. The key departure from prior work is the combination of all these elements into a single-pass, online, continuous-time imputation model, whereas existing methods (CSDI, CSBI, GP-VAE, TIDER) are all offline and operate on discretized, regularly-sampled sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BayOTIDE addresses a practical gap: streaming multivariate time series (e.g., electricity load, traffic) arrive continuously, yet all prior imputation methods require offline retraining, making them infeasible for real-time deployment. The continuous-time formulation also naturally handles irregular sampling and all-channel-missing timestamps, which discretized patch-based methods cannot. Limitations include the reliance on stationary kernels (non-stationary trends require extension), the mean-field approximation that may understate cross-factor correlations, and the need to manually select the number of trend/seasonality factors. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very high-dimensional settings (thousands of channels) without factor-count tuning, and whether non-stationary kernel variants can capture regime shifts in long-horizon forecasting.
