## Problem

Formal Lyapunov stability guarantees for neural network controllers with nonlinear dynamical systems are difficult to obtain, and existing approaches rely on expensive solvers (SOS, MIP, SMT) that limit scalability. Prior neural certificate methods also imposed Lyapunov derivative constraints over entire explicitly defined regions rather than the actual region-of-attraction, yielding unnecessarily small certified ROAs, and none handled output feedback control with formal guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242311/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For state feedback, the controller receives the full state vector x_t; for output feedback, it receives both a state estimate x̂_t and the raw sensor output y_t (e.g., truncated lidar ray distances), which is a nonlinear function of the true state. The method jointly synthesizes a neural network controller, a neural network observer (Luenberger-style with NN gain, for output feedback), a Lyapunov function (NN or quadratic), and a certified ROA defined as the intersection of a sublevel set {ξ : V(ξ) < ρ} with a box region-of-interest B. The controller output is clamped to input bounds, and by construction produces the equilibrium control u* at the goal state x*.

## Method

The key contribution is a novel verification formulation (Theorem 3.3) that replaces the requirement of verifying the Lyapunov derivative condition over the entire region B with a disjunctive condition: for each point in B, either the Lyapunov condition holds and the next state remains in B, or the point lies outside the certified ROA (V ≥ ρ). This eliminates unnecessary constraints over non-invariant regions and yields a strictly larger certifiable ROA. Training follows a CEGIS loop where cheap PGD adversarial attacks (not expensive SMT/MIP verifiers) generate counterexamples violating the reformulated condition, and the outer loop minimizes a surrogate loss combining Lyapunov violation, an ROA-expansion term over candidate states, L1 regularization to reduce Lipschitz constants, and an observer accuracy loss. Post-training, the full Lyapunov condition is rigorously verified using α,β-CROWN's GPU-accelerated bound propagation with branch-and-bound, and bisection finds the largest ρ_max satisfying the condition.

## Results

For verification of DITL-trained models, the proposed α,β-CROWN-based verifier is 3.5× faster on Cartpole (129s vs. 448s) and 8.9× faster on PVTOL (217s vs. 1935s) compared to DITL's MIP-based verification, while being competitive on lower-dimensional tasks (Pendulum: 11.3s vs. 7.8s; Path tracking: 11.7s vs. 13.3s). The novel verification formulation (Eq. 14) recovers a strictly larger certified ROA than DITL's two-step approach for the same trained models on both inverted pendulum and path tracking. For their own trained models, verification completes in 33s (pendulum state), 94s (pendulum output), 39s (path tracking), 1.1 hrs (quadrotor state), and 8.9 hrs (quadrotor output). The method achieves the first formal Lyapunov certificates for output-feedback neural control on an 8-dimensional quadrotor with lidar sensing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DITL (Wu et al., 2023), the primary baseline using MIP-based verification, the proposed method wins decisively on scalability: verification is 3.5× faster on the 4-D cartpole and 8.9× faster on the 6-D PVTOL, while being roughly comparable on the 2-D pendulum and path tracking. More importantly, the novel verification formulation (Eq. 14) recovers a strictly larger certified ROA than DITL's two-step sublevel-set approach for the *same* DITL-trained models (Fig. 4), demonstrating that the formulation itself—not just the training—yields larger guarantees. Against NLC (Chang et al., 2019), which relies on SMT solvers and was limited to 2-D systems, the proposed method finds a strictly superset ROA on both inverted pendulum and path tracking (Fig. 5) and extends to 6-D and 8-D systems. Against UNL (Zhou et al., 2022), which handled only 2-D systems, the proposed method again achieves a larger ROA and scales to output-feedback settings with 8 internal state dimensions. The paper also identifies and documents an implementation bug in DITL's PVTOL verification (incorrect ∞-norm constraint), further validating the robustness of the proposed approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CEGIS-based neural Lyapunov control line (Chang et al., 2019; Dai et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2023) in two key ways: (1) a novel verification formulation (Theorem 3.3) that restricts the Lyapunov derivative constraint to the certified ROA sublevel set rather than the entire region-of-interest, yielding larger certifiable ROAs; and (2) replacing expensive SMT/MIP solvers during training with cheap PGD adversarial attacks, deferring the full α,β-CROWN verification to a single post-training pass. The observer design generalizes the Luenberger observer by replacing the fixed gain with a neural network, and the unified internal-state formulation (Eq. 5–7) cleanly subsumes both state and output feedback under one closed-loop dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide formal Lyapunov stability certificates for neural-network output-feedback controllers (pendulum with angle observation, 2D quadrotor with lidar), a practically critical setting where the separation principle does not hold for nonlinear systems. The formulation insight—that enforcing the derivative condition only within the ROA rather than the entire bounding box is both necessary and sufficient—removes an unnecessary restriction that had been limiting certified ROA sizes in all prior neural Lyapunov works. However, the approach remains limited to moderate dimensions (up to 8D) and post-training verification can still take hours (8.9 hrs for quadrotor output feedback), leaving open the question of scalability to higher-dimensional systems with complex observation functions like images or point clouds.
