## Problem

Existing gradient compression methods in federated learning (Top-k sparsification, quantization, low-rank) operate exclusively in the original parameter space, which may fundamentally cap the achievable compression rate. This paper asks whether an alternative space exists in which gradients are inherently sparser, enabling more aggressive compression with minimal information loss, and whether such a space can be constructed in a way that preserves the known convergence guarantees of FedAvg.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240957/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client produces a local weight-update gradient (the difference between post-training and pre-training parameters after H local SGD iterations), while the server additionally holds a small set of unlabeled public data (1% of the full dataset) used solely to construct the mapping. The mapping is built layer-wise from SVD of activation and output-gradient covariance matrices, is periodically reconstructed (every 20 rounds early in training, then less frequently), and broadcast to all clients. After clients compress in the mapping space and upload, the server aggregates, applies the inverse orthogonal mapping, and updates the global model weights.

## Method

The core insight is that a layer's weight gradient equals the outer product of its input activation and output gradient (dW_l = dz_l · a_l^T). By computing SVDs of the covariance matrices of these two vectors and applying the resulting orthogonal matrices to both sides of the gradient (φ_l(dW_l) = Q_l^T dW_l P_l), the mapping concentrates most gradient energy into fewer elements, yielding a sparser representation with zero information loss due to the orthogonal structure. Standard sparsification-based compressors (Top-k, k-Sparse-Binary) are then applied in this mapping space, where the same sparsity level achieves a smaller compression error (1−ρ) than in the original space, enabling more aggressive compression. Error feedback corrects the bias of non-unbiased compressors, and the orthogonal transformation property ensures the O(1/√(NTH)) linear-speedup convergence rate of FedAvg is fully preserved.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the proposed mapping (LMap) reduces the uplink communication from 1.014 GiB (FedAvg) to 0.0068 GiB, a 149.1× reduction, while on CIFAR-100 it drops from 23.09 GiB to 0.0940 GiB, a 245.6× reduction. Even under high public-data heterogeneity (HMap), the efficiency remains strong at ×135.2 (CIFAR-10) and ×256.8 (CIFAR-100). Notably, kSB+Map achieves a final test accuracy higher than uncompressed FedAvg on CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet, an extra benefit unique to the mapping space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines: Top-k, k-Sparse-Binary (kSB), FedDQ, FedPara, and vanilla FedAvg (all in the original parameter space). kSB+Map surpasses all baselines with large accuracy gaps across all four datasets (SVHN, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet) at the same communication budget, whereas naive kSB does not. Top-k+Map is comparable to or outperforms FedDQ on SVHN and Tiny-ImageNet, though FedDQ slightly edges it on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. FedPara is consistently outperformed by both Top-k+Map and kSB+Map. Most strikingly, kSB+Map reaches a higher final accuracy than uncompressed FedAvg on CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet, while the same kSB compressor in the original space does not. The public-data baselines (PD1, PD2) that also exploit the 1% public data fail to improve Top-k or kSB, confirming that the mapping's structural sparsification is the key differentiator rather than access to public data alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient compression literature for federated learning by introducing an orthogonal mapping to an alternative space before applying standard sparsification compressors (Top-k, Sparse-Binary). While prior work (Stich et al., 2018; Sattler et al., 2019; Karimireddy et al., 2019) compresses gradients directly in the original parameter space, this paper leverages the outer-product structure of weight gradients (input activation × output gradient) and uses SVD of their covariance matrices to construct a space where gradients are inherently sparser. Conceptually, it is related to smoothness-matrix-based compression (Safaryan et al., 2021) and basis-selection for second-order methods (Qian et al., 2022), but differs in targeting first-order gradients in FL and constructing the mapping from both input and output structures rather than a single smoothness matrix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: the mapping enables 149×–257× communication reduction while preserving the O(1/√(NTH)) convergence rate, and the counterintuitive finding that Sparse-Binary in the mapped space can exceed vanilla FedAvg accuracy on non-IID data suggests that structured compression can mitigate data heterogeneity effects. Limitations include the need for unlabeled public data at the server (though only 1% suffices), periodic reconstruction of the mapping as training progresses, and evaluation limited to relatively small-scale vision tasks (CIFAR, SVHN, Tiny-ImageNet). Open questions include whether the approach transfers to language models or larger architectures, how sensitive performance is to the staleness of the mapping between reconstructions, and whether the 'compression beats no compression' effect generalizes beyond the specific non-IID regimes tested.
