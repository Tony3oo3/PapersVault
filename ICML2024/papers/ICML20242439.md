## Problem

The prevailing interpretation of LoRA and similar PEFT algorithms as low-rank approximations of weight changes leaves their underlying mechanism underexplored, and no controllability analysis exists for these lightweight modules. This paper reframes PEFT as a control problem and shows that existing linear controllers (e.g., AdaptFormer) provably fail to achieve full controllability when the dataset is large or when image patches share common tokens, motivating a redesign of the controller architecture.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100 and SVHN, 224×224 for Food-101), which are split into a sequence of patch tokens plus a class token by a frozen pre-trained ViT-Base backbone. The output is a single class label produced by a linear head applied to the final class token. The only trainable parameters are the low-rank control matrices (and optionally multi-head attention weights) inserted in parallel to the FFN layer of each ViT block.

## Method

The paper recasts PEFT as a continuous-time control problem where lightweight matrices act as controllers perturbing frozen ViT dynamics, then establishes a sufficient condition for local controllability: the controller's image set must span (ℝ^D)^N at every time step. It proves that linear controllers cannot satisfy this condition when N > D+1, and token-wise controllers fail when patches share tokens. To address both limitations, the proposed Attention-augmented Nonlinear Control (ANC) augments the standard LoRA output with a parameter-free Cross-Patch Attention (CPA) head that uses the LoRA result directly as Q, K, and V (no extra projections), enabling token intermingling without adding parameters. The final control is Control(x) = LoRA(x) + CPA(LoRA(x)), applied additively in parallel to the FFN layer. An optional multi-head variant shares the LoRA weights across heads to increase controller complexity at minimal parameter cost.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, SVHN, and Food-101, the proposed ANC algorithm consistently outperforms all PEFT baselines at every rank configuration. At 0.67M trainable parameters, ANC-32 achieves 86.69% (CIFAR-100), 96.94% (SVHN), and 88.06% (Food-101) top-1 accuracy, narrowing the gap to Full-Tuning (87.90%, 97.67%, 90.09%) to just 1.21%, 0.73%, and 2.03% respectively. At the highest tested rank (2.44M params), ANC-128 reaches 87.17%, 97.11%, and 88.50%, with only 0.73%, 0.56%, and 1.59% remaining below Full-Tuning. The multi-head variant ANC-128-2H further closes the gap to 0.38% on CIFAR-100 while tuning only 4.82% of total parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ANC outperforms LoRA at every matched parameter budget: at 0.67M params, ANC-32 beats LoRA-16 by 1.38% on CIFAR-100 (86.69 vs 85.31), 0.65% on SVHN, and 2.36% on Food-101 (88.06 vs 85.70). Against AdaptFormer at the same 0.67M budget, ANC-32 gains 1.27% on CIFAR-100 (86.69 vs 85.42) and 1.85% on Food-101 (88.06 vs 86.21). The advantage persists and widens at higher ranks: at 2.44M params, ANC-128 surpasses LoRA-64 by 1.29% on CIFAR-100 and 2.08% on Food-101, and beats AdaptFormer-128 by 1.05% and 0.72% respectively. VPT, while competitive on SVHN (94.02%), lags ANC by 4.25% on CIFAR-100 and 5.08% on Food-101, confirming that perturbing lower ViT layers is essential. Notably, ANC-32 at 0.67M params already exceeds LoRA-64 (2.44M params) on Food-101 (88.06 vs 86.42), demonstrating that the cross-patch attention mechanism provides a more effective use of parameters than simply increasing rank.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) through the lens of classical control theory, departing from the dominant 'low intrinsic rank of weight changes' interpretation of LoRA (Hu et al., 2021). It builds directly on AdaptFormer's (Chen et al., 2022) architectural choice of placing low-rank controllers in parallel to the FFN layer, which decouples the control function from the frozen dynamics and makes controllability analysis tractable. The key conceptual departure is treating the lightweight matrices not as weight deltas but as control modules that perturb a frozen system, then applying sufficient-condition controllability analysis from continuous-time ODE theory to derive design constraints that existing linear controllers provably violate for large datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The controllability framework provides the first principled theoretical justification for why certain PEFT architectures succeed or fail, moving the field beyond purely empirical design heuristics. The parameter-free cross-patch attention mechanism is a practical, zero-cost modification that consistently outperforms LoRA and AdaptFormer, and the finding that augmenting nonlinearity can be more effective than increasing rank offers a new axis for PEFT design. However, the analysis is restricted to ViT on three relatively small datasets, the continuous-time formulation is a simplification of discrete-layer dynamics, and the sufficient condition is not necessary—so the full landscape of effective controller designs remains open. Extending the control-theoretic perspective to NLP transformers, deeper architectures, and deriving necessary (not just sufficient) conditions would be natural next steps.
