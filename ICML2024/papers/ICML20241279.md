## Problem

Previous methods for equation discovery in hybrid dynamical systems follow a two-stage paradigm—first clustering time series into small fragments, then discovering equations independently within each fragment—which fails to leverage shared dynamics across all segments of the same mode and severs the interdependence between mode categorization and dynamics representation. Additionally, all prior approaches are restricted to single-object scenarios, leaving multi-object interacting hybrid systems unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241279/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw continuous time series observations (e.g., position-velocity pairs for a hopper, S/I/R counts for disease spread, or multi-object trajectories) without any pre-labeled mode information or known number of modes. It outputs a set of K sparse closed-form ODEs (one per discovered mode, expressed as linear combinations of polynomial basis functions with L1-regularized coefficients), frame-wise mode assignment labels, and autoregressive forecasts. In the multi-object variant, the input additionally includes N object trajectories, and the model infers a latent temporal interaction graph alongside per-object mode assignments.

## Method

AMORE reformulates hybrid equation discovery as a single end-to-end generative model with latent categorical mode variables z_t and count variables c_t (inspired by REDSDS), where each mode k indexes its own sparse coefficient vector w_k over a shared polynomial basis library Θ(y_t). The observation transition p(y_t|y_{t-1}, z_t) computes derivatives as Θ_k·w_k and integrates forward, amortizing equation learning across all time steps assigned to mode k. Discrete latent variables are inferred exactly via a modified forward-backward algorithm, while the mode transition matrix is parameterized by an MLP with tempered softmax. Training maximizes the observation likelihood (or ELBO in the multi-object case) plus an L1 sparsity penalty on all coefficient weights, jointly optimizing mode assignments, count durations, and equation coefficients. For multi-object systems (AMORE-MIO), latent edge variables in a temporal graph model pairwise interactions; edges are inferred approximately via a two-round GNN message-passing with Gumbel-softmax relaxation, while modes and counts remain exactly inferred conditioned on the approximate edges.

## Results

On the Mass-spring Hopper dataset, AMORE achieves 99.1% segmentation accuracy and F1 of 0.993, compared to 70.5% and 0.691 for Hybrid-SINDy, while discovering equations with a reconstruction error (RER) of 2.4×10⁻⁴ versus 7.5×10⁻³. For forecasting, AMORE attains NMAE of 0.008/0.039 (location/velocity) and NRMSE of 0.026/0.059, substantially outperforming SVI (0.068/0.075 NMAE) and LLMTime (0.113/0.305 NMAE). On multi-object systems, AMORE-MIO achieves the best segmentation (Accuracy 0.741 on ODE-driven Particle) and lowest forecasting error (NMAE 0.146/0.217 one-step) across all baselines. On non-hybrid systems, AMORE outperforms SVI in log₁₀(NRMSE) on 4 out of 6 physical systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Hybrid-SINDy (the primary two-stage baseline), AMORE wins decisively on all metrics: segmentation NMI improves from 0.426 to 0.928 on Mass-spring Hopper and from 0.296 to 0.475 on SIR disease, while equation discovery RER drops by roughly 30× (2.4×10⁻⁴ vs 7.5×10⁻³ on Hopper). Against SVI (designed for non-hybrid systems), AMORE achieves 8–9× lower NMAE on hybrid forecasting tasks (0.008 vs 0.068 on Hopper; 0.088 vs 0.257 on SIR), confirming that the joint mode-equation learning framework is essential for hybrid dynamics. Against LLMTime and TimeGPT (general-purpose forecasting models), AMORE consistently achieves the lowest errors, e.g. NMAE 0.008 vs 0.113 (LLMTime) and 0.092 (TimeGPT) on Hopper. On multi-object systems, AMORE-MIO narrowly outperforms GRASS on both segmentation (Accuracy 0.741 vs 0.732 on ODE-driven Particle) and forecasting (NMAE 0.146 vs 0.151 one-step), with the gap attributed to the equation priors on latent dynamics. On non-hybrid systems, AMORE (with K=1) beats SVI on 4/6 systems in log₁₀(NRMSE) but is slightly worse on Lorenz'63 (−1.23 vs −1.27) and Selkov glycolysis (−1.49 vs −1.55), indicating minor degradation when the hybrid structure is unnecessary.

## Relation to Prior Work

AMORE departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm for equation discovery in hybrid systems (Hybrid-SINDy, Novelli et al.) by reformulating the problem as a single end-to-end generative model that jointly infers latent mode variables, count variables, and sparse equation coefficients. The mode-switching machinery (categorical mode variables with geometric-distribution count variables) is directly inspired by REDSDS, while the sparse L1-regularized coefficient learning follows the SINDy lineage. The multi-object extension (AMORE-MIO) introduces latent edge variables in a temporal graph to model inter-object interactions, a component absent from all prior equation-discovery methods and borrowed conceptually from GRASS and neural relational inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint amortized formulation addresses a structural weakness of two-stage methods: by learning each mode's equation from all its segments simultaneously, AMORE achieves near-perfect segmentation (NMI 0.93 vs. 0.43) and dramatically lower equation reconstruction error, while remaining robust to misspecified polynomial order and maximum mode count. The multi-object setting is explicitly unexplored in prior equation-discovery literature, making AMORE-MIO the first method to discover closed-form equations for interacting objects with switching dynamics. Open questions include scaling to higher-dimensional state spaces, discovering equations from video observations, and incorporating partial prior knowledge of the system structure.
