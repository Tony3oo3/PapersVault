## Problem

In practice, stochastic policy gradient methods are trained with noise injected into actions or parameters, but the resulting deterministic policy is what gets deployed. Despite this being standard practice, there was no theoretical framework quantifying how the exploration noise level affects the gap between the learned stochastic objective and the deployed deterministic performance, no global convergence guarantees for parameter-based exploration (PGPE), and no principled way to tune the noise variance to balance sample complexity against deterministic policy quality.

## Input / Output

The method operates on continuous-state, continuous-action MDPs with a parametric deterministic policy μ_θ. During learning, white noise (e.g., Gaussian) is added either to actions at every timestep (AB exploration via GPOMDP) or to policy parameters at the start of each trajectory (PB exploration via PGPE). The final output is the noise-free deterministic policy parameter θ_K, and the key tunable is the noise standard deviation σ, which the paper shows should scale as O(ε(1−γ)²d^{-1/2}) to achieve ε-accuracy on the deterministic objective.

## Method

The paper introduces a unified white-noise exploration framework that formalizes both AB and PB exploration as adding zero-mean noise to actions or parameters, then proves that the performance gap between the stochastic objective (J_A or J_P) and the deterministic objective (J_D) is bounded by O(L·σ·√d), where d is the action or parameter dimension. Under weak gradient domination (WGD) and smoothness assumptions, it derives global last-iterate convergence guarantees of Õ(ε^{-3}) samples for fixed noise and Õ(ε^{-5}) (with smoothness) or Õ(ε^{-7}) (without) for ε-adaptive noise that vanishes as accuracy increases. The analysis reveals a formal trade-off: smaller σ reduces the deterministic deployment gap but increases sample complexity, and the optimal σ balances these two effects. The paper further shows that WGD for the deterministic objective is inherited by both J_A and J_P, and that Fisher-non-degeneracy of white-noise policies provides an alternative route to WGD for AB exploration, then empirically validates the trade-off on MuJoCo environments.

## Results

The paper establishes the first global convergence guarantee for PGPE (parameter-based exploration) to the optimal deterministic policy, with sample complexity Õ(ε⁻³) for a fixed noise variance and Õ(ε⁻⁵) (with smoothness) or Õ(ε⁻⁷) (without) when the noise is tuned as σ_P = ε/(6L_P√d_Θ). For GPOMDP (action-based exploration), analogous bounds hold with the parameter dimension d_Θ replaced by the action dimension d_A and an additional (1−γ)⁻¹ factor. Empirically on MuJoCo (Swimmer-v4, Hopper-v4, HalfCheetah-v4), PGPE achieves the best deterministic performance with σ_P²=10 (Swimmer) or σ_P²=1 (Hopper, HalfCheetah), while GPOMDP is best with σ_A²=1 (Swimmer) or σ_A²=10 (Hopper, HalfCheetah), confirming the theoretical trade-off between exploration level and deterministic deployment quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two exploration paradigms: PGPE (parameter-based, Sehnke et al. 2010) and GPOMDP (action-based, Sutton et al. 1999). Theoretically, GPOMDP incurs an additional (1−γ)⁻¹ factor in sample complexity relative to PGPE due to its higher estimator variance in long-horizon settings, and its bounds depend on the action dimension d_A rather than the parameter dimension d_Θ. Empirically, GPOMDP outperforms PGPE on Hopper-v4 and HalfCheetah-v4 (environments with higher d_Θ), while PGPE outperforms GPOMDP on Swimmer-v4 (higher horizon T, lower d_Θ), consistent with the theoretical trade-off. Both methods confirm the predicted U-shaped relationship between noise level and deterministic policy quality: too little noise slows learning, too much noise degrades the deployed deterministic policy. The paper also notes that GPOMDP's dependence on (1−γ)⁻¹ is worse than PGPE's, suggesting PGPE is preferable for long-horizon tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the policy gradient convergence literature (Yuan et al., 2022; Papini et al., 2018) by shifting the convergence target from the optimal stochastic policy to the optimal deterministic policy, formalizing the common practice of 'switching off the noise' after training. It introduces a unified white-noise framework that subsumes both action-based (GPOMDP) and parameter-based (PGPE) exploration, enabling the first global convergence guarantee for PGPE under weak gradient domination. The analysis builds on the Lipschitz MDP and smooth policy assumptions from the DPG literature (Silver et al., 2014; Xiong et al., 2022) but applies them to the stochastic-to-deterministic deployment gap rather than to fully deterministic learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous characterization of the fundamental trade-off between exploration level and deterministic policy quality, showing that an ε-adaptive noise schedule (σ = O(ε)) is necessary to achieve global convergence to the deterministic optimum at rate Õ(ε⁻⁵) or Õ(ε⁻⁷). This gives practitioners a theoretical basis for tuning exploration variance rather than relying on heuristics. Open questions include whether adaptive (time-varying) variance schedules can be analyzed without breaking smoothness assumptions, whether sufficient noise can eliminate local optima entirely, and whether the framework extends to variance-reduced or second-order PG methods for faster Õ(ε⁻²) convergence to deterministic policies.
