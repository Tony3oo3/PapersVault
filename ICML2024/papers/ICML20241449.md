## Problem

Standard Normalizing Flow models for waveform generation are not robust to small input variations, suffer from initial errors during inference that produce periodic artifacts and blurry harmonics, and generally underperform GAN-based vocoders in perceptual quality. The paper seeks a generative framework that retains NF advantages (exact likelihood, single-step sampling, controllable latent space) while overcoming these robustness and quality limitations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241449/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model conditions on 80-band mel-spectrograms (100-band for DFlow-XL) extracted with 1024 FFT size and 256 hop size, which are upsampled to four length scales (4×, 16×, 64×, 256×) via transposed 1-D convolutions and injected as conditions into every WaveNet and UNet layer. The output is a raw audio waveform at 22.05 kHz (24 kHz for DFlow-XL) produced in a single forward pass through the Primary Flow inverse and the Decoder, with no iterative refinement or multi-step sampling required.

## Method

DFlow decomposes generation into a noisy-distribution modeling step (NF) and a refinement step (DAE), implemented as three modules: an Auxiliary Flow f of invertible autoregressive WaveNet transformations that simultaneously forms part of the NF and acts as the DAE encoder, a Primary Flow g of volume-preserving additive coupling layers parameterized by UNets, and a feed-forward UNet Decoder m. Training optimizes an ELBO combining the negative log-likelihood of the noise-perturbed input (computed via the invertible f+g flow) and a 1/β-scaled MAE reconstruction loss, enabling stable end-to-end training in a single stage without the separate training or stop-gradient tricks required by Latent Flow Models. The VP constraint on g regularizes the latent variable z_l to maintain variance matching the Gaussian prior, while the AR structure of f avoids the Squeeze operation that introduces periodic biases. At inference, only g⁻¹ and m are used (f is excluded), yielding a single-step, fully parallel generation that is ~3× faster than WaveGlow and approaches GAN-level speed.

## Results

On the LJ-Speech test set, DFlow achieves a MOS of 4.42 (vs. 4.53 ground truth), the highest among all compared likelihood-based and GAN-based vocoders, with the best PESQ (3.43), MCD (0.71), and V/UV F1 (0.95). In terms of efficiency, DFlow requires only 39.2B FLOPs/second (vs. 368.3B for WaveGlow) and achieves a GPU RTF of 0.014, making it ~3× faster than WaveGlow and approaching HiFi-GAN's 0.008. At scale, DFlow-XL (183M params, trained on 585h LibriTTS) matches BigVGAN's IND MOS (4.30 vs. 4.30) and slightly exceeds it on OOD non-speech (4.06 vs. 4.04).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFlow outperforms WaveGlow on every metric (MOS 4.42 vs. 3.92, PESQ 3.43 vs. 3.01, MCD 0.71 vs. 2.41) and is ~3× faster in synthesis (GPU RTF 0.014 vs. 0.045). Against HiFi-GAN, DFlow wins on MOS (4.42 vs. 4.39), PESQ (3.43 vs. 3.11), MCD (0.71 vs. 0.92), and V/UV F1 (0.95 vs. 0.94), but loses on M-STFT (1.21 vs. 0.99), likely because HiFi-GAN is the only model trained with a spectrogram loss. DFlow also surpasses DiffWave on MOS (4.42 vs. 4.31) and all perceptual metrics while being a single-step model versus DiffWave's 50-step sampling. Against WaveNet (MoL), DFlow leads on MOS (4.42 vs. 4.08) and all objective metrics except M-STFT. At large scale, DFlow-XL matches BigVGAN on in-domain LibriTTS MOS (4.30 vs. 4.30) and edges ahead on OOD non-speech (4.06 vs. 4.04) and OOD speech (4.01 vs. 3.99), while using less training memory (7.3 GB vs. 10.6 GB) and achieving a faster GPU RTF (0.032 vs. 0.036).

## Relation to Prior Work

DFlow sits at the intersection of normalizing flow vocoders (WaveGlow, FloWaveNet, WaveFlow) and denoising autoencoders, proposing the first joint end-to-end training of an NF and a DAE for waveform generation. It departs from Latent Flow Models (which require separate training stages or stop-gradient tricks) by making the DAE encoder an invertible AR flow, enabling exact likelihood computation on a fixed-distribution variable. The Primary Flow's volume-preserving constraint (additive coupling à la NICE) and the UNet-based parameterization distinguish it from prior NF vocoders that rely on affine coupling with squeeze operations, which the authors identify as a source of periodic artifacts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DFlow demonstrates that likelihood-based waveform generation can simultaneously achieve GAN-competitive quality, 71× real-time synthesis on GPU, and strong out-of-distribution generalization (singing, music), addressing the generative learning trilemma that has kept NFs behind GANs and diffusion models. The scalability results (DFlow-XL matching BigVGAN) suggest NF+DAE is a viable large-scale universal vocoder architecture. Open questions include whether periodic activations (e.g., SnakeBeta) can be integrated, whether the approach extends beyond audio to other high-dimensional signal generation, and whether the noise-injection scale (β) can be learned adaptively rather than scheduled.
