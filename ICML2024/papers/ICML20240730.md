## Problem

Standard magnitude-based parameter pruning, widely used to compress deep networks, fails for dynamical systems reconstruction (DSR) because even small-magnitude weights can substantially influence attractor geometry. The role of network topology in RNN-based DSR had not been studied, leaving open the question of whether a sparse, structured connectivity pattern could replace the full all-to-all weight matrix without degrading reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240730/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes time series from autonomous dynamical systems (Lorenz-63, Lorenz-96, Rössler, a bursting neuron model, and human ECG data), with the first N latent states directly identified with observations via an identity mapping. It produces a binary pruning mask m ∈ {0,1}^{M×M} that defines the RNN's connectivity topology, applied via element-wise multiplication to the weight matrix W. The GeoHub algorithm outputs an adjacency matrix parameterized only by the number of nodes n, mean degree k, and observation dimension N, serving as a reusable topological prior for initializing new RNNs on unseen systems.

## Method

The paper introduces geometric pruning, which iteratively removes the weight whose deletion causes the smallest change in KL divergence between the true and model-generated state-space distributions, revealing that network topology—not weight magnitude—governs DSR performance. Analysis of the resulting pruned graphs shows they combine hub-like (scale-free) degree distributions with small-world properties (high clustering coefficient, short average path length), with hubs preferentially associated with readout nodes. From these empirical properties, the authors derive the GeoHub algorithm, which constructs a directed graph by first establishing incoming edges via a modified preferential-attachment rule that boosts readout-node connectivity, then adding outgoing edges with a balancing term to prevent excessive hub formation in the out-degree. RNNs initialized with the GeoHub mask alone (with randomly drawn weights) match or exceed fully connected networks on both attractor geometry (D_stsp) and temporal structure (D_H) benchmarks, while training significantly faster, and outperform Erdős-Rényi, Watts-Strogatz, and Barabási-Albert topologies.

## Results

Geometric pruning enables up to 95% parameter reduction in RNNs for dynamical systems reconstruction (DSR) without significantly degrading attractor geometry (D_stsp) or long-term temporal statistics (D_H), with the improvement over random pruning being statistically significant in all 10/10 benchmark comparisons (p < 0.03). In contrast, magnitude-based pruning performs essentially no better than random removal (insignificant in 5/10 comparisons). The GeoHub topology—combining hub-like and small-world characteristics distilled from geometrically pruned networks—outperforms Erdős-Rényi, Barabási-Albert, and Watts-Strogatz topologies on all benchmarks (Lorenz-63, Bursting Neuron, ECG) and reaches acceptable DSR quality (D_stsp < 1.0) in fewer training epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Erdős-Rényi random graph baseline, GeoHub achieves substantially lower D_stsp across all benchmarks, confirming that structured topology matters beyond mere sparsity. Against the Barabási-Albert (scale-free) model, GeoHub consistently outperforms it, indicating that hub structure alone is insufficient—small-world properties (short path length, high clustering) are also necessary. Surprisingly, the Watts-Strogatz small-world model performs *worse* than the Erdős-Rényi random graph, suggesting that small-worldness without hub structure is actually detrimental to DSR. For the pruning criterion comparison, geometric pruning significantly outperforms both magnitude-based and random pruning in all 10/10 cases (p < 0.03), while magnitude-based pruning is statistically indistinguishable from random in 5/10 cases and only marginally better (or worse) in the remaining 5/10. GeoHub also trains faster, reaching D_stsp < 1.0 in fewer epochs than any other topology at comparable network size (~300 parameters).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the PLRNN architecture (Durstewitz, 2017) and the broader DSR literature (LSTMs, reservoir computers, Neural ODEs) to investigate a previously unexplored question: the role of RNN topology in dynamical systems reconstruction. It departs from the standard magnitude-based pruning paradigm rooted in the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (Frankle & Carbin, 2019), demonstrating that weight magnitude is a poor proxy for parameter importance in DSR. The GeoHub algorithm synthesizes the preferential attachment mechanism of the Barabási-Albert model with small-world properties (Watts-Strogatz) and directed hub structure, creating a topology that empirically matches what geometric pruning produces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that network topology, rather than specific weight values, is the dominant factor for DSR performance challenges the LTH in this domain and provides a practical, interpretable initialization strategy (GeoHub) that reduces parameter count by up to 95% while preserving reconstruction quality and accelerating training. Open questions include whether the optimal topology is universal across systems or system-specific, whether results generalize beyond DSR to other sequence modeling tasks, and how to make geometric pruning computationally tractable for large-scale applications. The counterintuitive result that pure small-world (Watts-Strogatz) topology underperforms random Erdős-Rényi graphs suggests that the interaction between hub structure and small-worldness is non-trivial and warrants further study.
