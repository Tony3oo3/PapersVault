## Problem

Existing egocentric VideoQA methods rely on general-purpose architectures with fine-tuning, failing to address three domain-specific challenges: small object recognition (objects appear incomplete, deformed, and blurred in first-person view), noise suppression (peripheral regions contain redundant content that dilutes attention), and spatial-temporal reasoning (the constrained first-person perspective limits observation of complete behaviors). No prior work concurrently tackles all three issues with dedicated architectural components.

## Input / Output

The method takes an egocentric video (224×224 resolution, 16 frames for EgoTaskQA or 32 frames for QAEgo4D) and a natural-language question as input. The video is partitioned into 32×32 patches further subdivided into 16×16 sub-patches (N=196 per frame), while the question is encoded via RoBERTa. Output is either a selected answer from predefined options (discriminative, via MLP over the [CLS] token) or a free-form generated answer (generative, via an LSTM decoder initialized with the video [CLS] token), depending on the dataset.

## Method

MFAS extends TimeSformer with three coupled components. First, a patch partition and merging module creates dual-scale (patch and sub-patch) branches with twin spatial-temporal attention, fusing cross-scale information via learnable up/down-sampling coefficients to preserve both fine-grained and coarse-grained spatial semantics. Second, a prior-guided patch selection module combines a hand-crafted prior matrix (encoding the observation that egocentric focus concentrates in the middle-lower frame region) with multi-scale spatial attention scores and a temporal continuity constraint (first-order neighborhood from the previous frame) to produce a binary mask that selects top-k sub-patches per frame, suppressing peripheral noise. Third, a hierarchical aggregation network performs multi-layer spatial-temporal cross-attention between video and question representations, with progressive down-sampling in the final three layers (sub-patch → patch → frame → video level) while applying the selection mask to attention to enforce focused, noise-free aggregation from local to global granularities.

## Results

On EgoTaskQA, MFAS achieves 48.69% overall accuracy on the direct split and 45.40% on the indirect split, surpassing the strongest baseline EgoVLPv2 (46.26% and 42.28%) by 2.43% and 3.12% respectively. On QAEgo4D (discriminative setting), MFAS with LSE supervision reaches 12.7% accuracy, 9.3 BLEU, 18.3 METEOR, and 27.0 ROUGE, outperforming EgoVLPv2† (11.9% acc, 8.6 BLEU) and HCRN (10.3% acc, 7.6 BLEU). In the generative setting on QAEgo4D, MFAS† ties EgoVLPv2† on accuracy (11.9%) and BLEU (8.6) but leads on METEOR (18.9 vs 17.6) and ROUGE (28.2 vs 26.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MFAS outperforms all 14 baselines on every metric in both the direct and indirect splits of EgoTaskQA. Against the strongest egocentric baseline EgoVLPv2 (Pramanick et al., 2023), MFAS gains +2.43% on direct All and +3.12% on indirect All accuracy; against the strongest exocentric baseline CMCIR (Liu et al., 2023b), the margins are +2.65% and +3.40% respectively. On QAEgo4D discriminative, MFAS† (12.7% acc) beats EgoVLPv2† (11.9%) by 0.8% and HCRN (10.3%) by 2.4%; on the generative setting, MFAS† ties EgoVLPv2† on accuracy and BLEU but leads on METEOR (+1.3) and ROUGE (+2.0). The gap over older baselines is substantial: HCRN (Le et al., 2020) trails by 6.49% on EgoTaskQA direct All, and ClipBERT (Lei et al., 2021) trails by 8.82%. The only notable weakness is in the 'action' semantic category under the direct split, where MFAS (17.34%) underperforms CMCIR (30.08%), attributed to CMCIR's use of more comprehensive video information for straightforward questions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends TimeSformer with a multi-scale patch partition and merging mechanism, departing from prior egocentric VideoQA methods (EgoVLP, EgoVLPv2) that applied general dual-tower or fine-tuning frameworks without addressing domain-specific challenges. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a prior-guided patch selection module that encodes egocentric-specific observations (gaze concentration in lower-center regions, hand-object interaction zones) as a fixed spatial prior, combined with learned spatial-temporal attention scores to dynamically mask redundant sub-patches. The hierarchical aggregation network further distinguishes this work by progressively down-sampling from sub-patch to video level while maintaining question-guided cross-attention, rather than treating all granularities uniformly as in standard VideoQA pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses three concrete failure modes of prior egocentric VideoQA methods (small object detection, peripheral noise, partial-observation reasoning) that had been overlooked when general VideoQA architectures were simply fine-tuned on egocentric data. The prior-guided selection mechanism offers a lightweight, interpretable way to reduce spatial redundancy without additional training cost, which could transfer to other egocentric perception tasks. However, the prior matrix is hand-crafted and fixed to a lower-center region, limiting generalizability to scenarios where the focus area shifts (e.g., looking up at a screen). The authors note future directions toward shot-level semantics and unifying exocentric/egocentric perspectives, but the current method remains tied to the specific egocentric framing assumptions.
