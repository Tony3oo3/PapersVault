## Problem

Existing learning-based keypoint detectors model local maxima heuristically within fixed-size patches, which hard-codes keypoint density, does not guarantee detected points are true critical points, and makes detection inherently scale-dependent. No principled mathematical framework existed to locate local extrema with built-in scale independence, leaving a gap between the theoretical guarantees of Scale-Space theory and the flexibility of learned detectors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (H×W×C) and produces a set of sparse 2D pixel locations as keypoints. Internally, a fully convolutional backbone (modified L2Net) maps the image to a single-channel scalar 'height map' of size H×W; at inference, keypoints are extracted via non-maximum suppression with a value threshold γ=0.7. Training operates on image pairs at 208×208 resolution with homographic correspondence maps from the WASF dataset, and the loss is computed on the height maps rather than on explicit keypoint labels.

## Method

MorseDet models keypoints as local maxima of a CNN's height map using the bijection between H₁ persistent homology generators (loops) and local maxima, computed via discrete Morse theory on the cubical complex of the image. The core novelty is a differentiable detector loss L_det = −Σ Pers(e)[Pers(e) − α·Sim(e)], where persistence (death minus birth time of each loop) acts as a prominence weight and boundary similarity penalizes topological features that are not reproducible across corresponding image pairs. This loss is differentiable via subgradient results from Leygonie et al. and Carrière et al., enabling end-to-end training with standard backpropagation. The approach is fully unsupervised (no keypoint labels), requires no patch-size hyperparameter, and the persistence weighting naturally suppresses noisy low-intensity peaks while focusing optimization on prominent, repeatable features.

## Results

On the HPatches benchmark, MorseDet achieves the best or second-best repeatability across all keypoint budgets (250–4000) for both illumination and viewpoint splits. For viewpoint changes, MorseDet leads at every keypoint count, reaching 47.2% repeatability at 4000 keypoints (vs. 47.6% for DISK at illumination). In the scale-repeatability experiment (Table 2), MorseDet outperforms SIFT by 6.3 points at 75% image resize (82.2% vs. 75.9%) and achieves the best average among learnable methods (62.2%), though SIFT still leads at extreme 25% downscaling (50.2% vs. 41.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MorseDet clearly outperforms D2-Net and R2D2 across all settings, with gaps of 10–35 points in repeatability, confirming that the topological loss provides a substantially stronger signal than patch-wise local-maximum heuristics. Against SIFT, MorseDet is competitive overall but SIFT retains an edge at extreme scale changes (25% resize: 50.2 vs. 41.3) and at low keypoint budgets under viewpoint change; MorseDet surpasses SIFT at 75% resize by 6.3 points and leads at all keypoint counts ≥ 500 for viewpoint. SuperPoint is the closest learnable competitor, ranking second-best on average, but MorseDet dominates on the viewpoint split (e.g., 47.2 vs. 46.4 at 4000 keypoints) while SuperPoint edges ahead slightly on illumination at 500 keypoints (47.7 vs. 47.3). DISK achieves the highest illumination repeatability at 4000 keypoints (57.4 vs. 55.2) but trails MorseDet on every viewpoint column. ALIKED is consistently the weakest baseline, with MorseDet outperforming it by 20–35 points across the board.

## Relation to Prior Work

MorseDet builds directly on the R2D2 architecture (L2Net backbone with smaller final-layer kernels) and its WASF training protocol, but replaces the patch-wise local-maxima detection with a topological characterization via persistent homology. The discrete Morse theory algorithms from Robins et al. (2011) provide the computational machinery for extracting persistence pairs from the height map, while the differentiability of persistence-based functions established by Leygonie et al. (2021) and Carriere et al. (2021) enables backpropagation through the loss. Conceptually, the paper departs from all prior learnable detectors (D2-Net, R2D2, DISK, ALIKED) that model keypoints as local maxima within fixed-size patches, arguing this imposes a scale-dependent frequency prior; instead, MorseDet treats keypoints as the death points of H₁ generators, yielding a scale-free, density-free formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first keypoint detector trained entirely by a topological loss with no keypoint labels, matching losses, or hardcoded density hyperparameters, offering a principled alternative to the patch-based heuristics that dominate the field. The ablation revealing collapse to a trivial grid pattern without the boundary-similarity term (α=0) provides a clear mechanistic explanation for why the loss works. However, practical adoption is currently limited by the CPU-only discrete Morse computation, the absence of a descriptor component, and the fact that SIFT still outperforms all learnable methods on extreme scale changes, suggesting the topological formulation has not yet fully captured the scale-space invariance that classical methods achieve by design.
