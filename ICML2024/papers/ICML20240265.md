## Problem

Existing policy gradient methods for s-rectangular robust MDPs achieve at best O(ε^{-4}) iteration complexity, are restricted to deterministic settings with exact gradient access, and require enumeration over all states and actions—making them impractical for large state spaces. The paper asks whether entropy regularization can yield a smoother objective that accelerates convergence, and whether the resulting algorithm can be extended to stochastic sampling and function approximation.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes as input the MDP parameters (state/action spaces, cost function, discount factor, initial state distribution), an s-rectangular compact convex ambiguity set P, and stochastic environment samples (state-action-next-state trajectories). For the large-state-space variant, it additionally requires known linear transition features ψ(s,a,s') ∈ R^{d_p} and linear Q-function features φ(s,a) ∈ R^d. The output is a policy π (tabular or implicitly parameterized by u via softmax of φ(s,·)ᵀu) and a transition kernel parameter ξ, together forming an (ε,τ)-Nash equilibrium that is also an ε-optimal robust policy for the original non-regularized problem when τ = O(ε).

## Method

The core insight is that entropy regularization transforms the non-differentiable robust objective Φ_ρ(π) = max_p J_ρ(π,p) into the smooth dual objective F_{ρ,τ}(p) = min_π J_{ρ,τ}(π,p), which is differentiable by Danskin's theorem and Lipschitz-smooth with parameter O(τ^{-1}). The algorithm is a double loop: the inner loop runs natural policy gradient (NPG) steps to approximate the unique optimal policy π_p for a fixed transition kernel, while the outer loop performs projected gradient ascent on p (or its linear parameter ξ) using the gradient ∇_p J_{ρ,τ}(π_t, p_t). Two novel technical tools handle the entropy regularizer: (1) bounding |π ln π| differences via the Lipschitz property of x ln x to control the potentially unbounded log-policy, and (2) establishing a recursive Lipschitz bound on the Bellman fixed point Q_p to show ln π_p is Lipschitz in p. Extensions to stochastic settings use TD learning for Q-estimation and trajectory-based sample averages for the transition gradient, while the large-state-space variant replaces tabular p with linear kernel p_ξ = Ψξ and tabular Q with linear approximation φ(s,a)ᵀw, preserving both Lipschitz smoothness and gradient dominance without additional approximation error from the kernel parameterization.

## Results

In the deterministic setting with exact gradients, Algorithm 1 achieves O(ε^{-3} ln ε^{-1}) iteration complexity (total updates on both policy π and transition kernel p), improving over the state-of-the-art O(ε^{-4}) of Guha and Lee (2023). In the stochastic setting, Algorithm 2 achieves O(ε^{-7} ln ε^{-1}) sample complexity, and Algorithm 3 extends this to large state spaces with the same O(ε^{-7} ln ε^{-1}) sample complexity up to a linear Q-function approximation error ζ. All three algorithms also solve the entropy-regularized robust MDP, yielding an (ε,τ)-Nash equilibrium that is simultaneously an ε-optimal robust policy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's Algorithm 1 improves the deterministic iteration complexity from O(ε^{-4}) (Guha and Lee, 2023 — the prior state of the art) to O(ε^{-3} ln ε^{-1}), a polynomial improvement in the ε-dependence. Compared to Wang et al. (2023), whose complexity is O(ε^{-4} + ε^4 γ^{-O(ε^{-4})}) and grows exponentially as ε→0, the proposed method is strictly better in both the polynomial and exponential terms. Li et al. (2023b) has O(ε^{-6}) iteration complexity, which is worse than the proposed O(ε^{-3} ln ε^{-1}). Kumar et al. (2023c) reports only O(ε^{-1}) policy updates but requires oracle access to a Lipschitz-smooth sub-gradient of the robust value function and does not report total iteration complexity, making a direct comparison inapplicable. The proposed Algorithms 2 and 3 are the first to provide provable sample complexity (O(ε^{-7} ln ε^{-1})) for s-rectangular robust MDPs in stochastic and large state space settings, where all four baselines have no results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the natural policy gradient (NPG) framework with entropy regularization from Cen et al. (2022) and the perfect duality result for entropy-regularized robust MDPs from Mai and Jaillet (2021), combining them into a two-timescale algorithm where the inner loop solves the smooth policy subproblem and the outer loop performs projected gradient ascent on the now-differentiable dual objective. It departs from prior s-rectangular robust policy gradient methods (Wang et al. 2023, Li et al. 2023b, Guha and Lee 2023) that directly optimize the non-differentiable robust objective, instead exploiting entropy regularization to obtain Lipschitz smoothness and gradient dominance properties that yield faster convergence. The paper also identifies and corrects a flawed gradient-dominance proof in Guha and Lee (2023), showing their claimed non-rectangularity extension actually requires s-rectangularity. For the stochastic and large-state-space extensions, it draws on TD learning convergence results from Li et al. (2023a) and linear mixture MDP parameterization from Ayoub et al. (2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first policy gradient algorithms with provable global convergence for entropy-regularized robust MDPs, a framework that combines robustness guarantees with exploration benefits relevant to inverse reinforcement learning. The improvement from O(ε^{-4}) to O(ε^{-3} ln ε^{-1}) iteration complexity, along with the first stochastic and large-state-space extensions, makes robust policy optimization more practical. However, the authors note the complexity is not minimax optimal because τ = O(ε) forces a small stepsize β = O(ε), leaving room for further acceleration via Nesterov methods or variance reduction. The purely theoretical nature (no empirical validation) and the specialized s-rectangular setting limit immediate practical impact, but the geometric properties established (Lipschitz smoothness of the entropy-regularized dual, gradient dominance) are reusable building blocks.
