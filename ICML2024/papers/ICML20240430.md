## Problem

Prior sample-efficient and computationally-efficient algorithms for robust sparse mean estimation, PCA, and linear regression under Huber contamination all incur a suboptimal error of Ω(ε√log(1/ε)), while the information-theoretically optimal error is Θ(ε). This paper asks whether one can close this gap: can one achieve O(ε) error with both sample complexity poly(k, log d, 1/ε) and polynomial running time in the sparse regime (k ≪ d)?

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives n = (k²/ε²)polylog(d/ε) ε-corrupted samples from a Gaussian (identity covariance for mean estimation, spiked covariance I+ρvvᵀ for PCA, or joint (X,y) distribution for regression), along with the corruption rate ε, sparsity parameter k, and task-specific parameters (spike strength ρ or noise level σ). It outputs a d-dimensional estimate (μ̂, v̂, or β̂) with ℓ₂-error O(ε) (or O(ε/ρ) for PCA, O(σε) for regression) with high probability, running in poly(nd) time. The sample complexity depends on k² rather than d, which is the key sample-efficiency gain over dense algorithms.

## Method

The core contribution is a novel multidimensional filtering method that adapts the dense O(ε) algorithm of Diakonikolas et al. (2018) to the sparse regime. Instead of filtering along a single sparse direction (which is inherently limited to ε√log(1/ε) error), the algorithm iteratively finds r = log(1/ε) sparse matrices A₁,…,A_r (each with k non-zero rows of k non-zero entries, unit Frobenius norm) that are mutually orthogonal in support, by maximizing ⟨Σ_w − I, A⟩ over k²-sparse matrices. It then filters using the combined quadratic score from all r matrices simultaneously, leveraging the Hanson-Wright inequality to remove log(1/ε) times more outliers than inliers per iteration. After the loop terminates, the algorithm decomposes coordinates into H (the union of supports of the final A_i's, of size O(k² log(1/ε))) and its complement: the empirical mean is O(ε)-accurate on the complement by a certificate lemma, while a dense O(ε) estimator is applied on the small H subspace using fresh samples. For PCA, a novel reduction to sparse mean estimation is developed via conditional distributions of projections orthogonal to an initial spike approximation; for regression, a similar reduction from Diakonikolas et al. (2023b) is extended to the sparse setting.

## Results

The paper achieves the information-theoretically optimal O(ε) error for robust sparse mean estimation with sample complexity (k²/ε²)·polylog(d/ε) and poly(nd) running time, improving on the prior best of Ω(ε√log(1/ε)) error. For robust sparse PCA, the algorithm achieves O(ε/ρ) Frobenius-norm error on the spike estimate with the same sample complexity, and for robust sparse linear regression it achieves O(σε) ℓ₂-error. All three results are the first to simultaneously be sample-efficient (poly(k, log d, 1/ε) samples), computationally efficient (poly(nd) time), and achieve optimal error up to constants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The key comparison is against Balakrishnan et al. (2017), the first sample- and computationally-efficient algorithms for all three tasks, which inherently incur Ω(ε√log(1/ε)) error due to their single-direction SDP-based filtering. This paper removes the √log(1/ε) factor by extending the multi-direction filtering idea of Diakonikolas et al. (2018) to the sparse regime via a novel (F,k,k)-norm-based relaxation. For sparse mean estimation, Diakonikolas et al. (2018) already achieved O(ε) error but required poly(d/ε) samples (sample-inefficient); this work achieves the same error with only (k²/ε²)·polylog(d/ε) samples. For sparse PCA, even in the dense setting, the prior best polynomial-time algorithm (Diakonikolas et al. 2018) required quasipolynomial time; this paper gives the first polynomial-time O(ε) algorithm. For sparse linear regression, Liu et al. (2020) achieved O(σε log(1/ε)) error; this paper improves to O(σε) by reducing to the sparse mean estimator, using Liu et al. only as a warm-start to handle the norm constraint on β.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the dense O(ε)-error robust Gaussian estimation algorithm of Diakonikolas et al. (2018) to the sparse regime, where the key obstacle is that maximizing variance over sparse directions is computationally hard. It leverages the k²-sparse matrix relaxation from Diakonikolas et al. (2019) but extends it by iteratively finding r = log(1/ε) such matrices on disjoint coordinate sets, enabling a decomposition into a small dense subproblem (solved by the 2018 algorithm) and a large subproblem where the empirical mean is already accurate. For PCA and regression, the paper introduces a novel reduction to sparse mean estimation via conditional distributions, building on the template from Diakonikolas et al. (2023b) and using Liu et al. (2020) as a warm-start for regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first set of sample- and computationally-efficient algorithms achieving the information-theoretically optimal O(ε) error for robust sparse mean estimation, PCA, and linear regression under Huber contamination, closing a gap where all prior efficient algorithms incurred ε√log(1/ε) error. The multidimensional sparse filtering technique and the PCA-to-mean-estimation reduction are reusable tools for future robust estimation work. Key open problems include extending to unknown covariance matrices, removing the spike-strength restriction in PCA, and developing gap-free sparse PCA algorithms.
