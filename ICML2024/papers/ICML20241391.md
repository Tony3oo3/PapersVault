## Problem

Importance measures (explanations) for NLP models are often unfaithful, yet measuring faithfulness via the erasure-metric requires masking tokens, which introduces out-of-distribution issues. Existing solutions like ROAR solve this by retraining the model, which is computationally expensive, leaks gold labels, and evaluates a proxy model rather than the deployed one. No prior method simultaneously satisfies all six desirable properties: no known ground-truth assumption, model-specific measurement, original dataset only, in-distribution inputs, computational cheapness, and general applicability to any classification task.

## Input / Output

The model accepts text sequences or text pairs (for NLI, paraphrase, and QA tasks) and produces a class label. For faithfulness measurement, the pipeline additionally consumes token importance scores from nine different IMs (gradient, x⊙grad, IG, LOO, Beam, in signed and absolute variants) and outputs a faithfulness score (RACU or ACU) per explanation. The in-distribution validation step produces per-observation p-values via MaSF, aggregated with Simes and Fisher methods over L×H embedding dimensions. Masked fine-tuning consumes the same text inputs but with a uniformly sampled masking ratio (0–100%) applied to half the mini-batch, while the other half remains unmasked.

## Method

The core contribution is masked fine-tuning: during fine-tuning of a RoBERTa MLM, half of each mini-batch is uniformly masked at a random ratio between 0% and 100% while the other half stays unmasked, making token masking in-distribution by design without sacrificing unmasked performance. This eliminates the need for retraining (ROAR) when measuring faithfulness. A three-step evaluation follows: (1) create the faithfulness measurable model via masked fine-tuning, (2) validate that masked test inputs are in-distribution using MaSF (empirical CDFs of layer-normalized embeddings aggregated via Max-Simes-Fisher), and (3) apply a recursive erasure-metric that iteratively masks x% of the most important tokens (recalculating the IM at each step to handle dataset redundancies) and compares performance against a random-masking baseline. Because masking is in-distribution, occlusion-based IMs (LOO, Beam) become consistently more faithful, and the cheap faithfulness measurement enables optimizing explanations toward maximal faithfulness, making the model indirectly inherently explainable.

## Results

On SST2, occlusion-based importance measures achieve the highest faithfulness: Beam reaches 187.3% RACU and LOO (signed) reaches 171.3% RACU, while gradient-based methods range from −12.2% (x⊙grad signed, unfaithful) to 105.6% (IG signed). On bAbI-2, Beam achieves 139.2% RACU and IG (signed) reaches 136.3% RACU. Across all 16 datasets, masked fine-tuning preserves unmasked performance (e.g., 94% on SST2, 99% on bAbI-2) while enabling in-distribution masking validated via MaSF p-values. The method is evaluated with 9 importance measures across 16 classification datasets using RoBERTa-base and RoBERTa-large.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole direct baseline is Recursive ROAR (R-ROAR) by Madsen et al. (2022a), which measures faithfulness by recursively masking tokens and retraining the model. The proposed FMM approach consistently outperforms R-ROAR on RACU where both are reported: on SST2, Grad (L₂) improves from 26.1% to 40.4%, x⊙grad (abs) from 18.6% to 23.5%, and IG (abs) from 32.9% to 45.3%; on bAbI-2, Grad (L₂) improves from 57.8% to 96.3%, x⊙grad (abs) from 48.1% to 92.0%, and IG (abs) from 42.0% to 98.3%. The authors note this comparison is imperfect because R-ROAR leaks the gold label during retraining and uses a different (non-masked-fine-tuned) model. Additionally, R-ROAR's RACU scores exhibit higher variance (wider confidence intervals) due to gold-label leakage causing oscillation in the faithfulness curve. The FMM approach also enables importance measures not available under R-ROAR (signed variants, LOO, Beam), which achieve substantially higher faithfulness scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the model-agnostic tradition of faithfulness measurement (Jain & Wallace, 2019; Hooker et al., 2019; Madsen et al., 2022a) by instead modifying the model itself through a simple fine-tuning procedure (masked fine-tuning) so that token masking is in-distribution by design. It adapts the erasure-metric concept from Samek et al. (2017) and the recursive evaluation protocol from Madsen et al. (2022a) (R-ROAR), but eliminates the costly retraining step. The in-distribution validation step generalizes the MaSF statistical OOD detection framework from Matan et al. (2022) from computer vision to NLP, providing a meta-validation that prior erasure-metric work lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making faithfulness measurement computationally cheap and built into the model, this work enables optimization of explanations toward maximal faithfulness (e.g., via beam-search), bridging the gap between post-hoc and inherent interpretability. Key limitations include the requirement for masked language models (not causal LM), the non-post-hoc nature (faithfulness must be planned into training), and the impossibility of statistically proving in-distribution status. Open questions include extending the approach to causal language models, developing better optimizers than beam-search, and whether the simplicity of masked fine-tuning generalizes to non-classification tasks like sequence generation.
