## Problem

While differential privacy auditing techniques exist for DP training algorithms, no known methods can audit private prediction algorithms where models are trained non-privately and predictions are noised at inference. This gap is increasingly relevant as private prediction becomes a key mechanism for task adaptation of large language models. The paper proposes the first framework for auditing private prediction, establishing empirical lower bounds on privacy leakage for four well-known algorithms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240234/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a training dataset, a private prediction algorithm (PATE, CaPC, PromptPATE, or Private-kNN), a Gaussian noise scale σ, and a query sequence, and produces per-query Rényi DP lower bounds composed losslessly across queries into overall (ε,δ)-DP leakage estimates. Because each query requires roughly 10^8 Monte Carlo experiments for statistical validity, the method introduces parametric modeling assumptions (e.g., multinomial distributions over teacher predictions) to sample histogram distributions without retraining models. Results are reported for three adversary types (Nat-AdvQ, Pois-AdvQ, Pois-NatQ) and compared against both data-dependent theoretical bounds and exact Rényi divergence calculations.

## Method

The auditing framework comprises three components: a Crafter generating neighboring datasets S and S' (via natural point addition or adversarial poisoning), the private prediction algorithm, and a Distinguisher selecting queries (naturally or adversarially) to compare output distributions. The core technical novelty is auditing Rényi DP of the noisy argmax mechanism using the 2-cut of the Rényi divergence, which admits a hypothesis testing interpretation and remains valid at all sample sizes via Clopper-Pearson confidence intervals. The authors also derive a closed-form integral (Eq. 4) for the exact Rényi divergence between noisy argmax outputs on neighboring histograms, providing a tight upper bound that separates looseness in theoretical analysis from weakness of the adversary. Per-query audits are composed using lossless RDP composition theorems, circumventing the invalidity of composing (ε,δ)-DP lower bounds. Applied across four algorithms and three adversaries, the framework reveals that ease of poisoning and adversary query control are the dominant factors governing privacy leakage.

## Results

The paper's primary quantitative finding is that the exact Rényi divergence calculation (Eq. 4) is significantly tighter than the data-dependent theoretical upper bound from Papernot et al. (2018) across all four private prediction algorithms, revealing slack in existing privacy analysis. Privacy leakage follows a consistent ordering across algorithms: PATE < CaPC < PromptPATE, correlating with ease of poisoning (random partitioning in PATE makes vote-flipping hardest, while single-example prompts in PromptPATE make it easiest). For adversaries restricted to natural queries (Pois-NatQ), privacy leakage is dramatically lower than for those with adversarial query control (Nat-AdvQ, Pois-AdvQ); in PATE, Pois-NatQ leakage is negligible while PromptPATE still shows significant leakage. For Private-kNN, an adversary with access to the embedding space (vs. natural input space) doubles privacy leakage on text classification datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its auditing framework against two theoretical baselines. Against the data-dependent privacy analysis of Papernot et al. (2018) for PATE/CaPC/PromptPATE, the exact Rényi divergence computation consistently yields tighter (lower) privacy bounds, and the 2-cut audit closely tracks the exact calculation, confirming the gap is due to analytical looseness rather than adversary weakness. Against the data-independent privacy bound of Zhu et al. (2020) for Private-kNN, even the strongest adversaries (Pois-AdvQ with embedding access) leave a large gap to the theoretical bound, suggesting substantial room for improvement via data-dependent analysis incorporating subsampling. The 2-cut audit method is validated against the exact Rényi divergence on synthetic histograms (Fig. 2), where it closely approximates the true value, while the k-cut and bootstrap variants (Appendix B) offer marginal gains at smaller orders but lack the always-valid and attack-interpretable properties of the 2-cut approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DP auditing paradigm—previously applied exclusively to differentially private *training* algorithms (DP-SGD, etc.)—to the distinct setting of private *prediction*, where models are trained non-privately and predictions are noised at inference. It builds directly on the adversary instantiation framework of Nasr et al. (2021) and the 2-cut hypothesis-testing interpretation of Rényi divergence from Balle et al. (2020), but adapts both to the discrete, high-dimensional output space inherent to noisy-argmax mechanisms. A key methodological departure is the introduction of RDP-specific auditing techniques (2-cut, k-cut, bootstrap) and an exact Rényi divergence computation for noisy argmax, since classical (ε,δ)-DP composition theorems are lossy and invalid for composing lower bounds across queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first auditing framework for private prediction, filling a clear gap given the growing relevance of private inference for LLM task adaptation (PromptPATE) and collaborative learning (CaPC). The finding that the data-dependent theoretical analysis is consistently looser than the exact Rényi divergence computation across all four algorithms provides concrete evidence of slack that can be tightened. Open questions include designing stronger multi-query poisoning attacks, incorporating group privacy into audits, and exploiting the privacy amplification from PATE's random partitioning—each of which could further narrow the gap between theoretical guarantees and empirical leakage.
