## Problem

Over-parameterized DNNs trained end-to-end with cross-entropy loss produce overconfident, poorly calibrated predictions, which is critical in safety-sensitive applications like medical diagnostics. This paper identifies that jointly training feature extraction layers (convolutions or attention) together with classification layers (fully connected) is a key driver of this miscalibration, and asks whether decoupling these two training stages can fix calibration without sacrificing accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100 and SVHN, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and outputs class labels with calibrated probability distributions. For V-TST, the predicted probability is not a single softmax but a Monte Carlo estimate of E_{q(z|x)}[p(y|z)] using m samples (m=1 during training, m up to 100 at test time), making the output inherently stochastic. The latent dimension Z of the intermediate representation is a tunable hyperparameter searched over {2, 8, 32, 128, 256, 512}.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage training procedure (TST): Stage 1 trains the full network end-to-end with cross-entropy until convergence; Stage 2 freezes all feature extraction parameters (convolutional or attention layers), reinitializes the fully connected classification layers, and retrains only those layers on the same data. The variational extension (V-TST) further places a standard Gaussian prior N(0,I) on the last hidden layer output z and replaces the CE loss in Stage 2 with an ELBO objective, where the variational posterior q(z|x)=N(μ(x),σ(x)) is parameterized by two parallel MLPs (one for mean, one for log-variance) that sit on top of the frozen feature extractor. This forces the classifier to learn a structured, regularized feature space where multiple nearby z values map to the same class, acting as an implicit data augmentation that prevents the classifier from pushing points arbitrarily far from decision boundaries. The approach is architecture-agnostic, demonstrated on both WRN-28-10 and ViT, and requires only ~1–10 additional epochs in Stage 2 to converge.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, V-TST (m=10) achieves 92.58% accuracy with an ECE of 1.27%, compared to the end-to-end WRN's 92.53% accuracy and 5.88% ECE — a 78.4% relative ECE reduction at essentially no accuracy cost. On CIFAR-100, V-TST (m=10) reaches 71.93% accuracy with 5.83% ECE versus the WRN's 71.56% accuracy and 21.74% ECE (73.2% reduction). On SVHN, TST achieves the best ECE of 0.43% at 95.1% accuracy, while V-TST (m=10) achieves 0.47% ECE at 94.76% accuracy, both dramatically outperforming the WRN's 3.48% ECE. The methods also reduce NLL on both training and test sets, indicating improved model fit rather than mere regularization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the end-to-end WRN-28-10, both TST and V-TST substantially reduce ECE across all three datasets (e.g., 5.88%→1.27% on CIFAR-10, 21.74%→5.83% on CIFAR-100, 3.48%→0.43% on SVHN) while maintaining or slightly improving accuracy. Compared to Temperature Scaling (the standard post-hoc calibration baseline), TST and V-TST achieve lower ECE on all three datasets (e.g., 1.27% vs. 4.4% on CIFAR-10; 5.83% vs. 15.24% on CIFAR-100), and also outperform it on shifted-data ECE (Table 2). Against the end-to-end ViT on CIFAR-10, V-TST (m=10) reduces ECE from 13.71% to 1.23% while matching accuracy (82.84% vs. 82.56%). However, on OOD detection (AUROC/FPR95), TST and V-TST generally underperform the WRN and Temperature Scaling baselines, suggesting that improved in-distribution calibration does not transfer to out-of-distribution recognition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the DNN calibration literature, departing from post-hoc methods (temperature scaling), data-augmentation regularizers (Mix-Up, label smoothing), and full Bayesian approaches (deep ensembles, MC-Dropout) by identifying a structural cause of overconfidence: the joint training of feature extraction and classification layers. The V-TST variant repurposes the ELBO objective from the VAE framework (Kingma & Welling, 2014) to a discriminative classifier head, distinguishing itself from the Variational Classifier (Dhuliawala et al., 2024) by using a simple image-independent Gaussian prior and avoiding the auxiliary discriminator and mini-max optimization. The two-stage procedure is explicitly contrasted with linear probing / fine-tuning (Kumar et al., 2022), which targets pretrained models, whereas TST/V-TST is a general training strategy applicable from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a simple freeze-and-retrain procedure yields 78–87% ECE reductions across WRN and ViT architectures on multiple benchmarks is practically significant for safety-critical deployment (medical diagnostics, autonomous driving) where calibrated probabilities are essential. However, the method's benefits are conditional: it primarily helps over-parametrized models trained from scratch, does not transfer to pretrained fine-tuning settings (TST worsens ECE on Tiny ImageNet), and unexpectedly degrades OOD detection performance. Open questions include whether combining TST/V-TST with last-layer Laplace approximations or other BNN methods can recover OOD sensitivity, and whether the calibration gains persist at larger scale (ImageNet, ViT-B/16 from scratch).
