## Problem

Existing privileged information (PI) methods for learning with noisy labels do not consistently outperform no-PI baselines because they still learn the noisy distribution p(ỹ|x) by marginalizing over PI, rather than explicitly separating clean from wrong labels. These methods lack an explicit mechanism to identify or correct mislabeled samples, causing the prediction network to overfit to noise through the input features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242100/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes RGB images (x) paired with a vector of PI features (a) such as annotator ID, experience, annotation time, or model confidence, augmented with a random per-sample identifier of tunable length. At inference, only the RGB image is available and the model outputs a class label via the prediction network alone. Post-training, the same prediction network yields per-sample confidence scores on the observed noisy labels that serve as a noise-detection signal (high confidence → likely clean, low confidence → likely wrong).

## Method

Pi-DUAL decomposes the output logits into a weighted sum of a prediction term f_θ(x) (depending only on input features) and a noise-fitting term ε_φ(a) (depending only on PI), with a sigmoid gating network γ_ψ(a) controlling the mixture: h(x,a) = [1−γ_ψ(a)]f_θ(x) + γ_ψ(a)ε_φ(a). Because the noise network never sees x, it cannot memorize label noise through input features, protecting the prediction network from overfitting. The model is trained end-to-end with a single cross-entropy loss on softmax(h(x,a)) versus the noisy label, requiring only one additional hyperparameter (random PI length). A theoretical risk analysis in a linear Gaussian setting shows that Pi-DUAL's generalization error scales with the number of gate disagreements rather than the total number of wrong labels, unlike OLS which scales with all wrong labels. The gating operates in logit space (not probability space), and the architecture is analogous to a two-expert mixture-of-experts layer with heterogeneous inputs.

## Results

Pi-DUAL achieves state-of-the-art test accuracy on all three high-quality-PI datasets: 71.3% on CIFAR-10H (worst, 64.6% noise rate), 71.6% on ImageNet-PI (low-noise, 48.1% noise rate), and 62.1% on ImageNet-PI (high-noise, 83.8% noise rate). On the low-quality-PI datasets, Pi-DUAL reaches 84.9% on CIFAR-10N and 64.2% on CIFAR-100N, performing on par with the best no-PI methods. For noise detection, Pi-DUAL's confidence thresholding achieves AUC of 0.954 on CIFAR-10H and 0.986 on ImageNet-PI (high-noise), substantially outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pi-DUAL outperforms the strongest PI baseline (TRAM++) by 4.5 points on CIFAR-10H (71.3% vs 66.8%) and by 3.1 points on CIFAR-100N (64.2% vs 61.1%), and beats the best PI method (AFM) by 1.3 points on ImageNet-PI low-noise (71.6% vs 70.3%) and by 6.8 points on ImageNet-PI high-noise (62.1% vs 55.3%). Against the best no-PI methods, Pi-DUAL gains 20 points over SOP on CIFAR-10H and 10.2 points over HET on ImageNet-PI high-noise. On CIFAR-10N, where PI quality is low (aggregated batch-level features), Pi-DUAL (84.9%) is slightly below ELR (86.6%) but still exceeds all PI baselines. For noise detection AUC, Pi-DUAL's gate thresholding reaches 0.982 on CIFAR-10H and 0.986 on ImageNet-PI high-noise, compared to 0.834 and 0.959 for the best competing method (TRAM++). Overall, Pi-DUAL consistently wins when high-quality sample-level PI is available and remains competitive with no-PI methods when PI is weak or absent.

## Relation to Prior Work

Pi-DUAL extends the privileged-information (PI) framework for noisy label learning established by TRAM/AFM (Collier et al., 2022) and TRAM++ (Ortiz-Jimenez et al., 2023), but departs from them architecturally by introducing a dual-path design with a PI-driven gating mechanism that explicitly routes clean and noisy labels to separate sub-networks. This is conceptually analogous to a sparsely-gated mixture of experts (Shazeer et al., 2017; Riquelme et al., 2021), but with heterogeneous experts receiving different inputs (x vs. a) rather than the same input. The theoretical risk analysis builds on the fixed-design framework of Collier et al. (2022), showing that Pi-DUAL's bias scales with gate disagreements rather than total noise, in contrast to OLS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Pi-DUAL provides a practical, single-stage, scalable method for noisy label learning that achieves clear SOTA margins on PI benchmarks (up to +6.8% on ImageNet-PI high-noise) and a strong post-hoc noise detection tool, all without per-sample parameters. Its key limitation is a strong dependence on high-quality PI features: performance degrades substantially when PI is absent or low-resolution, restricting applicability to settings where annotation metadata is genuinely informative. Open questions include extending the dual-path gating idea to other PI problems beyond supervised classification, and understanding whether the gating mechanism alone (without the noise network) suffices at very large scale, as suggested by the ImageNet-PI ablation.
