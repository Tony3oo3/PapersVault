## Problem

Gibbs sampling from continuous non-convex energy potentials is a central bottleneck in training and inference for energy-based models, yet existing quantum sampling algorithms (e.g., Childs et al. 2022) are restricted to strongly convex potentials and require gradient (first-order) oracle access. Classical high-precision samplers similarly rely on convexity or isoperimetric assumptions that fail for the multimodal landscapes encountered in ML. This paper addresses the gap by providing a quantum Gibbs sampler for generic non-convex periodic potentials using only zeroth-order queries, with polylogarithmic dependence on the target precision.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a quantum oracle O_E implementing the energy potential E (zeroth-order, no gradient access), lattice parameters N and M controlling discretization and upsampling resolution, and a solution time T for the Fokker-Planck equation. It produces samples from the Gibbs distribution e^{-E}/Z within ε total variation distance, obtained by measuring the final quantum state in the computational basis and then drawing a uniform sample from a small box around the resulting lattice point. For mean estimation, an additional oracle for a bounded periodic function f is required, and the output is an additive-ε estimate of E[f(X)] under the Gibbs measure. The energy is internally halved (E/2) to correct for the Born-rule factor of 2 in the discretized state.

## Method

The core mechanism is to solve the Fokker-Planck equation (a second-order PDE whose steady state is the Gibbs distribution) as a linear ODE on a coarse discrete lattice using quantum algorithms for linear differential equations (Berry et al. 2017; Krovi 2023), then apply a Fourier-domain upsampling isometry F_M^{-1} ι F_N built from quantum Fourier transforms to recover a high-precision continuous distribution from the coarse state. The key enabling condition is semi-analyticity—a milder-than-analyticity bound on the growth of higher-order derivatives that guarantees sub-exponential decay of Fourier coefficients, ensuring that a coarse lattice of size N = O(ad) suffices for O(e^{-N/a}) interpolation error. The algorithm achieves O~(κ_{E/2} e^{Δ/2} d^7 polylog(1/ε)) oracle queries for generic non-convex periodic potentials, and for λ-strongly Morse functions with a unique global minimum the complexity improves to O~(λ^{-2} e^{Δ/2} d^7), yielding an exponential quantum advantage in sampling precision and a quartic advantage in mean estimation precision over the best known classical Riemannian Langevin algorithms.

## Results

For non-convex periodic potentials, the algorithm achieves Gibbs sampling with O~(κ_{E/2} e^{Δ/2} d^7) zeroeth-order oracle queries and only polylogarithmic dependence on the target precision ε in total variation distance. For Morse functions with unique global minima, the complexity improves to O~(λ^{-2} e^{Δ/2} d^7) queries, yielding an exponential quantum advantage in sampling precision (polylog(1/ε) vs. ε^{-2} classically) and a quartic advantage in mean estimation precision (ε^{-1} vs. ε^{-4}). The algorithm requires no gradient access (zeroeth-order queries) and no convexity assumptions on the potential.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the classical Riemannian Langevin algorithm of Li & Erdogdu (2020) for Morse periodic potentials, this paper achieves an exponential advantage in sampling precision (polylog(1/ε) vs. ε^{-2}) and a quartic advantage in mean estimation precision (ε^{-1} vs. ε^{-4}), while also using zeroeth-order queries instead of first-order gradient queries. Compared to the quantum ULD and MALA algorithms of Childs et al. (2022), this paper removes the strongly convexity requirement entirely, handling generic non-convex periodic potentials, and uses zeroeth-order queries (vs. first-order for MALA). Against classical MRW (Dwivedi et al., 2018) and MALA (Lee et al., 2020), the quantum algorithm again drops the convexity assumption and achieves polylogarithmic rather than polynomial dependence on precision. The main trade-off is a higher polynomial dependence on dimension (d^7 vs. d^3 or d) and an exponential factor e^{Δ/2} in the diameter of the potential, which is acceptable in the high-temperature regime typical of ML applications.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as one of the first (alongside concurrent Childs et al. 2022) to analyze quantum algorithms for Gibbs sampling from continuous real-valued functions, departing from prior quantum sampling work that was restricted to discrete spin systems or strongly convex potentials. It builds on quantum ODE solvers (Berry et al. 2017; Krovi 2023) to integrate the Fokker-Planck equation, and on quantum Fourier transforms for a novel upsampling/interpolation step. The key conceptual departure from prior quantum sampling methods is the use of zeroth-order oracle queries (no gradients) and the introduction of semi-analyticity as a functional-analytic condition that enables efficient Fourier-domain interpolation, replacing the convexity or Poincaré inequality assumptions required by all prior classical and quantum Gibbs samplers for continuous potentials.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exponential precision advantage (ε^{-1} vs. ε^{-4}) for Morse functions with unique global minima, achieved with zeroth-order queries, is a significant theoretical result that suggests quantum computers could outperform classical Langevin-based samplers in the high-precision regime relevant to energy-based model training. However, the periodic boundary condition requirement and the d^7 polynomial dependence on dimension substantially limit near-term practical applicability to ML workloads. Open questions include whether the periodicity constraint can be relaxed (the Chebyshev extension is shown to be inefficient for general hypercube data), whether first-order oracle access could improve the mixing-time bottleneck, and whether the semi-analyticity framework generalizes to other PDE-based learning algorithms beyond the FPE.
