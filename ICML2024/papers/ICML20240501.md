## Problem

PAC-Bayesian generalization bounds for dynamical systems in state-space form are largely absent from the literature, with existing results either restricted to autoregressive models, assuming bounded signals, or relying on KL divergence which yields non-vanishing bounds for LTI systems with unbounded (sub-Gaussian) signals and quadratic loss. This paper fills that gap by deriving a PAC-Bayesian error bound for stochastic LTI state-space models with hidden states and unbounded signals that converges to zero at rate O(1/√N).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240501/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single finite time series of length N consisting of input-output pairs {y(t), u(t)} (or augmented features w(t) = [y(t), u(t)]^T for closed-loop settings), along with a user-specified prior density π on the compact LTI parameter set Θ ⊂ ℝ^{n_θ}. It produces a high-probability upper bound r_N(ρ, π) on the generalization gap E_{f~ρ}|L(f) − L̂_N(f)| that holds simultaneously for all posteriors ρ absolutely continuous w.r.t. π, with the bound depending on the order-2 Rényi divergence D̄₂(ρ‖π), the noise covariance of the data generator, and ℓ₁-gains of the predictor class. The posterior ρ^D can then be used to select a concrete LTI predictor via mean-model, MAP, or sampling.

## Method

The core technique replaces KL divergence with order-2 Rényi divergence in the PAC-Bayesian change-of-measure argument (Bégin et al., 2016), which sidesteps the technical obstruction that prevents KL-based bounds from vanishing under unbounded signals with quadratic loss. The proof decomposes the generalization gap into two terms: (i) the gap between the true generalization loss L(f) and an infinite-past empirical loss V_N(f), and (ii) the gap between V_N(f) and the standard finite-past empirical loss L̂_N(f). Each term is bounded via the Rényi change of measure combined with fourth-moment bounds on the prediction error, where the moments are controlled by the ℓ₁-norm of the error system G_e(f) (distance between predictor and data generator) and the predictor's robustness gain G_p(f) (sensitivity to initial-state transients). The resulting bound r_N(ρ,π) = (K/√(δN)) · D̄₂(ρ‖π) · [G₁ + (4/√N)G₂] converges to zero as N→∞ for fixed posteriors, with K, G₁, G₂ determined by the prior, noise level, and system gains.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) establishes a PAC-Bayesian generalization gap bound of the form r_N(ρ,π) = K/(√(δN)) · D̄₂(ρ‖π) · [G₁ + (4/√N)G₂], which converges to zero at rate O(1/√N) for a fixed posterior. In the numerical example (Section 5, n_u = n_y = 1, 2-state predictors, prior N(0, 0.02I), Gibbs posterior, δ = 0.1), the bound r_N(ρ,π) + E_{f~ρ}L̂_N(f) converges to E_{f~ρ}L(f) as N grows, confirming the theoretical guarantee. The authors note the bound remains fairly conservative in practice, with tighter constants identified as future work.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with the KL-divergence-based PAC-Bayesian bound for LTI systems from Eringis et al. (2023a). On the same synthetic example (n_u = n_y = 1, 2-state predictors, identical prior and posterior choices), the Rényi-based bound in this paper converges to zero as N increases, whereas the KL-based bound from Eringis et al. (2023a) converges to a non-zero constant, rendering it vacuous in the large-sample limit. This is the central empirical distinction: the Rényi divergence formulation avoids the technical difficulties that cause KL-based bounds to fail for unbounded signals with quadratic loss. The paper also contrasts its generalization-gap guarantee with finite-sample parameter-estimation bounds from Lale et al. (2020), Simchowitz et al. (2019), and Sarkar et al. (2021), noting that those results apply to specific learning algorithms and bounded signals, while the present bound is algorithm-agnostic and handles unbounded sub-Gaussian noise. No direct numerical head-to-head table is provided; the comparison is qualitative (convergence vs. non-convergence) supported by the Figure 1 simulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PAC-Bayesian framework from autoregressive and bounded-signal RNN models to linear time-invariant (LTI) state-space systems with unbounded (sub-Gaussian) signals and hidden states. It builds directly on the Rényi-divergence-based PAC-Bayesian change-of-measure technique of Bégin et al. (2016) and Alquier & Guedj (2018), and on the authors' own earlier KL-based bounds for LTI systems (Eringis et al., 2023a), which failed to converge to zero as N→∞. The key departure is the switch from KL to Rényi divergence, which the authors show is technically necessary to handle the unbounded-signal, quadratic-loss setting that is standard in control and econometrics applications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first PAC-Bayesian generalization gap bound for LTI state-space systems with unbounded signals and hidden states, filling a gap between the rich literature on autoregressive PAC-Bayes bounds and the finite-sample parameter-estimation bounds for specific LTI learning algorithms. The O(1/√N) convergence rate provides a theoretical guarantee that was unavailable before for this class of models, which underpins structured state-space models (SSMs) and recurrent neural networks. Limitations include the bound's conservatism (acknowledged in the numerical example), its restriction to linear systems, and the dependence on prior-dependent constants that may be hard to compute in practice. Open questions include extending the analysis to nonlinear state-space models and deriving oracle inequalities.
