## Problem

Neural CDEs (NCDEs) offer robustness to irregular sampling and decoupled forward passes, yet they underperform state-of-the-art recurrent models like S5, LRU, and MAMBA on multivariate time series benchmarks. Neural RDEs (NRDEs) improved on NCDEs via the Log-ODE method but parameterised the resulting vector field as a black-box neural network, ignoring the underlying Lie bracket structure and inflating the output dimension. This paper asks whether explicitly computing the Lie brackets of the CDE vector field can close the performance gap while reducing model complexity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multivariate time series as a control path X ∈ R^v, where the first channel is time and remaining channels are observations; no differentiable interpolation is required, so irregularly sampled data is handled natively. The path is segmented into fixed-length intervals, and the depth-N truncated log-signature is computed over each interval. The model produces a continuous hidden state h_t ∈ R^u evolved by an autonomous ODE on each interval, which is then mapped to either a class label (via a linear readout) or a continuous regression value.

## Method

Log-NCDEs replace the black-box vector field of NRDEs with one constructed recursively from the iterated Lie brackets of the NCDE's neural network f_θ, using the Hall basis of the free Lie algebra. This reduces the output dimension from u×β(v,N) to u×v while preserving universal approximator expressivity, since f_θ itself is the learned component. The Lie brackets are evaluated at each ODE step via Jacobian-vector products (JVPs) of f_θ, batched with Jax's vmap for efficiency. A key theoretical contribution is a bound on the Lip(γ)-norm of fully connected networks with SiLU activations for 1<γ≤2, ensuring the required regularity for the Log-ODE approximation; in practice, spectral-norm-style weight regularisation keeps this norm controlled during training. The depth-2 Log-ODE approximation is used empirically, with the ODE solver (Heun) stepping through each interval independently.

## Results

On the UEA-MTSCA benchmark (6 datasets), Log-NCDEs achieve the best average test accuracy of 64.4% and best average rank of 2.7, outperforming S5 (63.1%), LRU (61.8%), S6 (62.0%), MAMBA (58.6%), NRDE (60.2%), and NCDE (55.5%). On the PPG-DaLiA regression task (50,000 observations), Log-NCDEs achieve the lowest mean squared error of 9.20×10⁻², beating MAMBA (10.65×10⁻²) and NCDE (13.77×10⁻²). Log-NCDEs also have a lower average run time (2200s) than both NCDEs (7721s) and NRDEs (8338s) on UEA-MTSCA, thanks to the Log-ODE method reducing the number of forward passes on long sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Log-NCDEs outperform all five baselines on average across the UEA-MTSCA and PPG-DaLiA benchmarks. Against NCDEs (the direct predecessor), Log-NCDEs improve average accuracy from 55.5% to 64.4% and reduce average run time from 7721s to 2200s. Against NRDEs (the closest architectural sibling), Log-NCDEs win on all six UEA-MTSCA datasets and on PPG-DaLiA (MSE 9.20 vs 18.79×10⁻²), with the key difference being that Log-NCDEs explicitly compute the iterated Lie brackets rather than treating f̄_θ as an unconstrained neural network. Against the stacked recurrent models, Log-NCDEs beat S5 (63.1%), LRU (61.8%), S6 (62.0%), and MAMBA (58.6%) on average UEA-MTSCA accuracy, though S5 narrowly wins on SelfRegulationSCP1 (87.1% vs 82.1%) and LRU wins on EigenWorms (85.0% vs 82.8%). On PPG-DaLiA, Log-NCDEs beat MAMBA (9.20 vs 10.65×10⁻² MSE) and all other baselines, while MAMBA is the best stacked recurrent model on this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NCDE framework (Kidger et al., 2020) and its NRDE variant (Morrill et al., 2021) by replacing the black-box neural network parameterisation of the Log-ODE vector field f̄_θ with a construction based on iterated Lie brackets of the original CDE vector field f_θ. The mathematical foundation draws on rough paths theory, specifically the Log-ODE method (Lyons, 2014; Boutaib et al., 2013) and the free Lie algebra structure of the log-signature (Chen, 1957). The key conceptual departure from NRDEs is preserving the algebraic structure of the vector field through Lie bracket computation rather than learning an unstructured mapping, which drastically reduces the output dimension of the neural network for depth N ≥ 2 while maintaining expressivity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Log-NCDEs achieve state-of-the-art performance on both classification (UEA-MTSCA) and regression (PPG-DaLiA) benchmarks, outperforming recent state-space models (MAMBA, S5, LRU) while retaining NCDEs' natural handling of irregular sampling — a practical advantage for real-world sensor data. The main limitations are the restriction to depth-1 or depth-2 Log-ODE approximations (no theoretical Lip(γ) bounds exist for γ > 2, and computational cost grows rapidly with depth) and the inherent sequential nature of ODE solving that prevents parallelisation. Open questions include extending to higher depths with structured networks admitting cheap Jacobian-vector products, incorporating the recently proposed adaptive Log-ODE algorithm, and understanding whether the Lie bracket structure provides benefits beyond the specific NCDE/NRDE setting.
