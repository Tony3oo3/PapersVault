## Problem

Neural-symbolic energy-based models (NeSy-EBMs) like NeuPSL formulate inference as a non-smooth, constrained optimization problem, making predictions non-differentiable with respect to model parameters and rendering standard backpropagation inapplicable. Existing learning approaches either require expensive Hessian computations, unroll inference into computational graphs, or rely on value-based losses that can produce degenerate solutions. The paper addresses the gap of providing a principled, smooth, first-order gradient-based learning framework for general NeSy-EBMs that also yields efficient inference.

## Input / Output

The method consumes neural inputs (MNIST images, text features, or graph node features) alongside symbolic rules and linear arithmetic constraints that define a deep hinge-loss Markov random field. Inference produces target variable assignments in [0,1] (class probabilities, link existence, or regression values) by solving a constrained MAP problem, while learning jointly updates both neural network weights (via Adam) and symbolic rule weights (via mirror descent on the unit simplex). A key structural detail is that the energy function is an extended-value function: infeasible assignments receive infinite energy, so the feasible set is implicitly encoded in the constraint structure rather than handled separately.

## Method

The paper formulates NeSy-EBM learning as a bilevel optimization problem and applies a value-function approach with a novel Moreau envelope smoothing step that replaces the non-differentiable energy function with a continuously differentiable surrogate, enabling first-order gradient-based optimization via an augmented Lagrangian solver. For NeuPSL specifically, inference is reformulated as a smooth linearly constrained quadratic program (LCQP) with a small regularization parameter ε ensuring strong convexity, and a dual block coordinate descent (BCD) algorithm is introduced that directly produces the optimal dual variables needed to compute value-function gradients without computing a pseudoinverse of the constraint matrix. Two parallelization strategies—connected-component parallel D-BCD for sparse factor graphs and lock-free parallel D-BCD for connected graphs—exploit the additive separability of the dual objective. The dual BCD algorithm naturally leverages warm-starts across learning iterations, yielding up to 100× speedup over ADMM-based inference during training.

## Results

On the deep HL-MRF node classification task, NeuPSL trained with the proposed bilevel framework (BCE loss) achieves 81.28% accuracy on Cora and 69.64% on Citeseer, outperforming GCN (80.32% and 67.42%) and DeepStochLog (71.28% and 62.68%). On MNIST-Addition, NeuPSL with BCE reaches 95.70% accuracy (3,000 additions) and 93.04% (1,500 additions), surpassing DeepProblog by 3.1 and 5.6 points respectively. The dual BCD inference algorithm achieves up to a 100× cumulative learning runtime speedup over ADMM (e.g., 19.65s vs 4,554s on DDI with SP loss), and ADMM times out (>6 hours) on MNIST-Add2 while D-BCD completes in under 1.2 hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ADMM (the prior state-of-the-art NeuPSL inference), the proposed dual BCD algorithm consistently reduces cumulative learning runtime, achieving roughly a 100× speedup on DDI (19.65s vs 4,554s for SP loss) and completing MNIST-Add2 in under 1.2 hours where ADMM times out beyond 6 hours. On the deep node classification task, NeuPSL with bilevel learning (MSE/BCE) outperforms both DeepStochLog by 7–10 points and GCN by 2–2.4 points on Citeseer and Cora. On MNIST-Addition, NeuPSL with BCE surpasses DeepProblog by 3.1 points (3,000 additions) and 5.6 points (1,500 additions), and outperforms LTN by 1.8 and 21.3 points respectively, while CNN baselines remain far behind (78.99% and 1.69%). Internally, the bilevel (minimizer-based) losses consistently match or exceed value-based losses (Energy, SP) across all 7 non-deep datasets, with the largest gap on Cora where BCE achieves 81.07% versus SP's 74.16% (a 6.9-point improvement).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the NeuPSL framework (Pryor et al., 2023) and the value-function approach to bilevel optimization (Liu et al., 2021; Sow et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2023), adapting the latter to the constrained, non-smooth setting of NeSy energy-based models. It departs from the dominant implicit-differentiation and unrolling strategies for bilevel optimization by introducing a Moreau-envelope smoothing of the energy function, which is novel to NeSy learning and enables first-order gradient-based methods without Hessian computation. The dual BCD inference algorithm is positioned as the first method for NeuPSL that directly produces optimal dual variables needed for principled learning gradients, avoiding the expensive primal-to-dual pseudo-inverse mapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first principled, general gradient-based learning framework for NeSy-EBMs, a class that includes DeepProbLog, SATNet, and logic tensor networks, where predictions were previously non-differentiable and standard deep learning techniques inapplicable. The 100× learning runtime speedup over ADMM on DDI and the ability to complete MNIST-Add2 training where ADMM times out are practically significant for the NeSy community. Key limitations include the requirement for differentiable value-functions and the inference algorithm being specialized to the LCQP structure of deep HL-MRFs; extending the framework to approximate inference solutions and broader NeSy architectures remains open.
