## Problem

Existing HISR methods often ignore the significant feature differences between LR-HSI and HR-MSI, and most directly learn the mapping in either LR or HR space without establishing matching relationships at intermediate scales. This leads to spectral distortion and blurring of spatial texture in the reconstructed HR-HSI, as the cross-modal feature correlations are not explicitly modeled.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240808/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution hyperspectral image (LR-HSI) and a high-resolution multispectral image (HR-MSI) of the same scene as input, with a 4× spatial resolution gap between them. To align spatial domains, the HR-MSI is degraded via 4× downsampling followed by 4× upsampling (HR-MSI↓↑) before feature extraction. The output is a single high-resolution hyperspectral image (HR-HSI) at the spatial resolution of the HR-MSI and the spectral resolution of the LR-HSI.

## Method

MFTN consists of three modules: a multi-scale feature extraction module (MFEM) that extracts features at 1×, 2×, and 4× scales via stacked 3×3 convolutions and max pooling; a multi-scale feature transfer module (MFTM) containing three IMatchFormers that use 8-head multi-head attention to compute cross-modal correlation matrices between degraded HR-MSI (key) and LR-HSI (query) features, then weight HR-MSI (value) features to produce scale-specific transfer features; and a multi-scale dynamic aggregation module (MDAM) with three spectral-aware aggregation modules (SAAMs) that progressively fuse transfer features with shallow LR-HSI features using deformable convolutions for misalignment correction and a spectral-aware modulation (SAM) sub-module that applies learned multiplicative and additive coefficients from LR-HSI features to suppress spectral distortion. Training uses a joint loss combining L1 reconstruction, VGG-19 perceptual loss on RGB bands, and a novel transfer perception loss that constrains the SR-HSI's multi-scale features to match the IMatchFormer transfer features.

## Results

On Pavia Center, MFTN achieves the best scores across all six metrics: SCC 0.985, SAM 4.20, ERGAS 2.32, UIQI 0.969, SSIM 0.965, and PSNR 40.98 dB. On Chikusei, it similarly leads on every metric (SCC 0.978, SAM 1.57, ERGAS 2.88, UIQI 0.963, SSIM 0.965, PSNR 42.00 dB). On Botswana, MFTN attains the best SAM (1.24), ERGAS (1.83), SSIM (0.978), and PSNR (41.97 dB), with UIQI (0.949) ranking second behind HSRNet (0.954). Inference time is 5.7 ms on Pavia Center, 4.1 ms on Botswana, and 12.2 ms on Chikusei, faster than HyperRefiner on all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MFTN outperforms all nine baselines on the majority of metrics across all three datasets. The two traditional methods (PCA, GFPCA) are substantially outperformed, with MFTN gaining over 0.1 in SCC and over 10 dB in PSNR on Pavia Center. Among deep learning baselines, HyperRefiner is the closest competitor, but MFTN surpasses it on PSNR by 1.37 dB (Pavia Center), 2.18 dB (Botswana), and 0.57 dB (Chikusei), while also achieving lower SAM and ERGAS. HSRNet is the only method that beats MFTN on a single metric (UIQI on Botswana: 0.954 vs. 0.949), though MFTN leads on all other five metrics for that dataset. DARN, HyperPNN, SSFCNN, SSRNet, and HyperDSNet are consistently outperformed by MFTN on at least four of the six metrics per dataset, with gaps in PSNR ranging from 1.5 to 5.5 dB. In terms of runtime, MFTN (4.1–12.2 ms) is faster than HyperRefiner (9.1–12.3 ms) and HSRNet (7.0–52.1 ms) on all datasets, though slower than DARN and HyperPNN.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Transformer-based HISR line initiated by FusFormer (Hu et al., 2022b) and SST (Chen et al., 2023), which established that self-attention can capture long-range spectral-spatial dependencies. However, those methods treat the two modalities largely symmetrically and do not explicitly model the cross-modal feature mismatch between LR-HSI and HR-MSI at intermediate scales. MFTN departs by introducing IMatchFormer, a cross-attention module that explicitly matches degraded HR-MSI features against LR-HSI features at three spatial scales, and by adding a transfer perception loss that constrains the output to align with intermediate-scale transfer features—neither of which appears in prior HISR architectures. The spectral-aware aggregation (SAAM/SAM) also builds on the detail-injection philosophy of traditional pansharpening (Alparone et al., 2007) but re-implements it through learned modulation rather than hand-crafted injection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-scale cross-modal matching formulation offers a principled way to address the persistent spectral-distortion-vs-spatial-sharpness trade-off in HISR, and the transfer perception loss provides a useful inductive bias that anchors the reconstruction to intermediate-scale features. However, the gains over the strongest baseline (HyperRefiner) are modest (≈0.6–2.2 dB PSNR depending on dataset), and evaluation is limited to three standard small-to-medium scenes, leaving open whether the multi-scale matching strategy scales to very large satellite scenes or to sensor pairs with more extreme spectral overlap differences. The deformable-convolution alignment in SAAM is a practical but incremental addition; its contribution is not isolated in a dedicated ablation.
