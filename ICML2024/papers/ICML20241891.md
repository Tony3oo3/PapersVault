## Problem

Flow matching has been a powerful generative framework for continuous data, but extending it to discrete categorical sequences (e.g., DNA) is non-trivial. Naive linear flow matching on the probability simplex suffers from pathological behavior: the conditional probability paths have shrinking support, causing the model posterior to lose vertices at fixed time thresholds and producing discontinuous marginal vector fields that degrade generation quality, especially at higher simplex dimensions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241891/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a multi-simplex representation where each DNA token (A, T, C, G) is a point on a 4-dimensional probability simplex, and a full sequence of length L is a sequence of L such simplices. At inference, the model starts from independent Dirichlet(1) noise at each position and integrates a learned vector field over N steps (or 1 step when distilled) to produce a deterministic DNA sequence. Conditioning is achieved by feeding a cell-type class label or a transcription profile vector as additional input to the denoiser, enabling class-specific or profile-matched sequence design.

## Method

Dirichlet flow matching defines conditional probability paths as a one-parameter family of Dirichlet distributions Dir(x; 1 + t·e_i) that concentrate toward the target vertex while retaining full simplex support at all times, avoiding the shrinking-support pathology of linear FM. The corresponding conditional vector field is derived via a probability-flux argument across hyperplanes of constant x_i, yielding a closed-form scaling factor C(x_i, t) involving the derivative of the regularized incomplete beta function. Training uses a cross-entropy loss on a denoising classifier (predicting the clean token given the noisy simplex point), which is shown to share the same minimizer as the standard L2 flow-matching loss while ensuring the vector field stays on the simplex tangent plane. A key theoretical contribution is a linear relationship between the marginal flow and the score of a mixture of Dirichlets (v = UD⁻¹s), which enables both classifier-free and classifier guidance by linearly combining conditional and unconditional flows. Finally, because inference is a deterministic ODE, the model can be distilled into a one-step generator with minimal quality loss, achieving 100×–1024× speedups over iterative or autoregressive baselines.

## Results

On promoter DNA sequence design, Dirichlet FM achieves the lowest MSE of 0.0269 (100 NFE), outperforming DDSM (0.0334), D3PM (0.0375), and Bit Diffusion (0.0395–0.0414). The distilled one-step variant retains nearly identical performance (MSE 0.0278) while requiring only 1 NFE—a 100× speedup over diffusion baselines and 1,024× over the autoregressive language model. On enhancer generation, classifier-free guided Dirichlet FM achieves FBD of 1.9 (Melanoma) and 1.0 (Fly Brain), dramatically outperforming the language model (36.0 and 25.2) and Linear FM (19.6 and 15.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dirichlet FM outperforms all three discrete diffusion baselines on the promoter design task (Table 1): it beats DDSM (Avdeyev et al., 2023) by 0.0065 MSE (0.0269 vs. 0.0334), D3PM (Austin et al., 2021) by 0.0106 MSE (0.0269 vs. 0.0375), and Bit Diffusion (Chen et al., 2023) by 0.0126–0.0145 MSE (0.0269 vs. 0.0395–0.0414). On enhancer generation (Table 2), Dirichlet FM with classifier-free guidance achieves FBD of 1.9 on Melanoma and 1.0 on Fly Brain, a ~19× and ~25× improvement over the autoregressive language model (36.0 and 25.2), and also surpasses Linear FM (19.6 and 15.0). The distilled one-step Dirichlet FM (MSE 0.0278, FBD 6.1/15.8) matches the full 100-step model within ~3% while providing orders-of-magnitude speedup, a capability unavailable to the discrete-noise diffusion baselines (DDSM, D3PM, Bit Diffusion) or autoregressive models. DDSM, the closest simplex-based competitor, also requires 100 NFE and cannot be distilled, making Dirichlet FM strictly superior in both quality and efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022) from continuous Euclidean spaces to the probability simplex for discrete categorical data, deliberately departing from the standard linear interpolant by showing it induces pathological shrinking-support behavior (Proposition 1). The closest prior work is DDSM (Avdeyev et al., 2023), which also relaxes discrete data onto the simplex but uses Jacobi diffusion processes rather than Dirichlet distributions as intermediate noising distributions; Dirichlet FM is simpler and avoids DDSM's complexity. The guidance framework adapts the score-flow relationship from Dao et al. (2023) and Zheng et al. (2023) (derived for Gaussian paths in R^n) to the Dirichlet setting, and the distillation procedure follows the standard progressive distillation paradigm of Salimans & Ho (2022), applied here for the first time to an iterative generative model of discrete data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Dirichlet FM provides the first flow-matching framework for discrete sequences that supports both one-step generation via distillation and classifier(-free) guidance, addressing two key limitations of autoregressive and discrete-diffusion baselines. The large FBD gaps with guidance (1.9 vs. 36.0 on Melanoma) suggest that the flow-score connection is particularly effective for biological sequence design, though the evaluation is confined to three DNA datasets and a shared architecture with DDSM, leaving open whether the Dirichlet path is optimal or whether the approach transfers to other discrete domains (text, protein sequences, graphs). The FBD metric itself is a useful but straightforward adaptation of FID/FCD to biological sequences. A key open question is whether the one-step distilled model's quality degrades at longer sequence lengths or more complex conditional specifications beyond the 500–1024 bp regime tested here.
