## Problem

Nesterov's Accelerated Gradient (NAG) is a widely used momentum-based acceleration method in optimization, yet its convergence rates have never been formally characterized in the context of reinforcement learning, primarily due to the non-concavity of RL objectives. While momentum-based policy optimization is empirically successful in practice (e.g., in A2C and PPO), the theoretical understanding of how Nesterov momentum interacts with the non-concave RL landscape remains largely open.

## Input / Output

The method operates on finite MDPs with tabular softmax policy parameterization, where the policy is parameterized by unconstrained vectors θ ∈ R^{|S||A|} and the objective is the expected return under a surrogate initial state distribution μ drawn uniformly at random from the probability simplex. Two step-size regimes are considered: nearly constant step sizes η^(t) = (t/(t+1))·(1−γ)³/16 and exponentially growing time-varying step sizes with element-wise clipping. For Atari 2600 experiments, the input is a 210×160×3 binary state representation processed through a neural network policy, and the output is the optimized policy evaluated by cumulative game score.

## Method

APG adapts the monotone version of Nesterov's accelerated gradient (with a restart mechanism from Beck & Teboulle, 2009a) to the RL objective V^{π_θ}(μ), computing the gradient at the momentum point ω^(t−1) rather than the current parameter θ^(t), and applying a restart that retains the update only if it improves the objective. The key theoretical insight is a local near-concavity property: despite the global non-concavity of the RL objective, it becomes C-nearly concave in the vicinity of the optimal policy, and this region is absorbing—once APG enters it, the cumulative momentum effect cannot push the policy out. To handle the unbounded optimal parameter under softmax parameterization, the analysis introduces surrogate optimal parameters with bounded norms that induce nearly-optimal policies. These properties yield a Õ(1/t²) convergence rate with nearly constant step sizes and a linear O(e^{−ct}) rate with time-varying step sizes, both established almost surely under random initialization of μ.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: APG achieves a Õ(1/t²) convergence rate under softmax parameterization with nearly constant step sizes (Theorem 2), improving upon the O(1/t) rate of standard PG. With time-varying (exponentially growing) step sizes and element-wise clipping, APG achieves linear convergence O(e^{-ct}) (Theorem 3). Empirically, on a 5-state/5-action MDP, APG exhibits a log-log slope of approximately 2 (matching Õ(1/t²)), while both PG and HBPG exhibit a slope of approximately 1 (matching O(1/t)). On four Atari 2600 games (Pong, Carnival, Riverraid, Seaquest), APG demonstrates faster or comparable convergence to both PG and HBPG across all environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APG is compared against two baselines: standard Policy Gradient (PG, Mei et al. 2020) and Heavy-Ball Policy Gradient (HBPG, applying Polyak's heavy-ball momentum to PG). On the 5-state/5-action MDP, APG clearly outperforms both PG and HBPG: the sub-optimality gap decays at rate Õ(1/t²) for APG versus O(1/t) for both baselines, confirmed by the log-log slopes of 2 vs. 1. Under hard initialization (optimal action has smallest initial probability), APG escapes sub-optimality substantially faster than both PG and HBPG. On the four Atari 2600 games (Pong, Carnival, Riverraid, Seaquest), APG exhibits faster or comparable convergence to both PG and HBPG across all environments, with the Nesterov momentum providing a consistent benefit. The paper also notes that APG's Õ(1/t²) rate is strictly better than PG's O(1/t) rate, and that APG's time-varying step size result (O(e^{-ct})) matches the linear convergence rates of NPG and GNPG while using a simpler first-order momentum mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Nesterov's Accelerated Gradient (NAG) from convex optimization to policy gradient methods in RL, building directly on the monotone/restart variant of NAG from Beck & Teboulle (2009a) and the O(1/t) convergence analysis of softmax PG from Mei et al. (2020). It departs from existing RL acceleration methods (Natural Policy Gradient, Geometry-aware Normalized PG) that achieve linear convergence through geometric or normalization-based mechanisms, instead showing that the simpler momentum-based NAG can achieve O(1/t²) with nearly constant step sizes. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a 'local near-concavity' property of RL objectives near the optimal policy, which is absent from the standard non-convex NAG literature and is what makes the accelerated rate achievable despite global non-concavity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical characterization of Nesterov momentum convergence in RL, filling a notable gap given that momentum is one of the most widely used acceleration techniques in practice (PyTorch, A2C, PPO). The local near-concavity and absorbing-regime insights provide a structural understanding of why momentum helps in RL that could inform future algorithm design. However, the results are confined to tabular softmax policies with exact gradients in finite MDPs; extending to stochastic gradients, function approximation, and continuous state/action spaces remains open, as acknowledged by the authors. The Atari experiments (4 games) provide preliminary but limited evidence of practical benefit.
