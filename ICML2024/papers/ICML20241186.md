## Problem

Existing multimodal representation methods for robot decision-making rely on separate, hand-balanced objectives for temporal consistency, local/global task progression, and trajectory-level language grounding, often sacrificing one goal at the expense of another through hard-to-tune hyperparameters. No prior framework unifies all three desiderata—temporal smoothness, multi-scale progression capture, and instruction alignment—into a single principled training signal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241186/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw RGB image sequences (randomly sampled start-goal frame pairs of variable length from in-the-wild human videos) paired with natural-language task instructions, and produces a shared K-dimensional vision-language embedding space. Unusually, the same embedding serves dual purposes: as a frozen visual feature for downstream policy learning (LCBC, RL) and as a parameterized language-conditioned reward function for zero-shot trajectory optimization via MPPI planning, without any additional reward head.

## Method

DecisionNCE transforms the Bradley-Terry preference model into a representation-learning objective by exploiting implicit preferences (a video segment is inherently more aligned with its own instruction than with mismatched ones) and reparameterizing BT rewards in embedding space. Two reward schemes yield two variants: DecisionNCE-P uses a potential-based reward equaling the difference in cosine similarity between the goal and start frame embeddings relative to the text embedding, while DecisionNCE-T uses a transition-direction reward aligning the embedding-space displacement vector with the text embedding. A random segment-sampling strategy over variable-length start-goal pairs simultaneously captures local and global task progressions and, as proven analytically, induces implicit time-contrastive learning that enforces temporal consistency without a separate objective. The resulting InfoNCE-style loss contrasts start-end transitions (not single images) across all mismatched pairs in a mini-batch, merging language grounding and temporal consistency into one hyperparameter-free training signal.

## Results

On FrankaKitchen MPPI planning with zero-shot implicit rewards, DecisionNCE-T achieves 0.122 average success rate and DecisionNCE-P achieves 0.101, substantially outperforming CLIP (0.016), R3M (0.036), and LIV (0.054). On the WidowX real robot across 9 manipulation tasks, DecisionNCE significantly outperforms all baselines (CLIP, R3M, LIV, VIP, RT1) in LCBC success rate. On FrankaKitchen simulation LCBC with 1/3/5 demonstrations, DecisionNCE achieves the highest success rate across all dataset quantities, demonstrating effective extraction of task-relevant information from out-of-domain pretraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DecisionNCE-T (0.122) and DecisionNCE-P (0.101) both more than double the performance of the strongest baseline LIV (0.054) on zero-shot MPPI planning, and are 7.6x and 6.3x better than CLIP (0.016) respectively. On real robot LCBC, DecisionNCE wins across almost all 9 WidowX tasks; CLIP performs poorly because aligning language with single static images misses sequential task progression signals, while VIP fails because its pretrained vision and language embeddings are not jointly aligned. RT1 also underperforms because the small downstream dataset (100 demos per task) is insufficient to train a large end-to-end model. On simulation LCBC, DecisionNCE consistently achieves the highest success rate at 1, 3, and 5 demonstrations, confirming that its unified trajectory-level representation learning extracts more transferable task progression features than methods relying on separate objectives for temporal consistency and language grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

DecisionNCE repurposes the Bradley-Terry model from preference-based reinforcement learning as a representation learning objective by reparameterizing rewards with vision-language encoders, yielding an InfoNCE-style loss tailored for trajectory-level decision making. It departs from prior methods (R3M, LIV, Voltron, VIP) that decompose the problem into separate objectives for temporal consistency, language grounding, and task progression, each requiring careful hyperparameter balancing. The random segment sampling strategy draws from action recognition literature, and the implicit time contrastive learning property connects to Sermanet et al.'s time-contrastive networks, but the key novelty is showing these disparate components emerge naturally from a single BT-derived objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified objective eliminates the ad-hoc multi-loss balancing that plagues prior representation learning for robotics, offering a principled and hyperparameter-free alternative that practitioners can adopt more easily. However, the framework is currently instantiated only at CLIP scale on a single pretraining dataset (EPIC-KITCHEN-100), and the implicit preference assumption (that a video is inherently better aligned with its own instruction than mismatched ones) may break down for ambiguous or multi-step tasks. Scaling to larger Vision-Language Models and more diverse pretraining corpora remains an open question, as does whether the start-end transition simplification generalizes to tasks with highly non-monotonic progressions.
