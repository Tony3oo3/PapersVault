## Problem

On-policy RL methods such as PPO were designed for small batch sizes (~100 parallel environments) and fail to benefit from the massively parallel GPU simulators (tens of thousands of environments) now available. When a single Gaussian policy is rolled out across all environments, most sampled actions cluster near the mean, producing duplicated data and causing performance to saturate regardless of how many environments are added. The paper asks how to structure on-policy training so that large-scale parallelism actually translates into higher asymptotic performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state vectors from dexterous hand-arm robots (23-DoF Allegro-Kuka or 16/24-DoF hands), comprising joint angles and velocities, object pose and linear/angular velocities, and a task-specific goal observation. It outputs continuous joint actions for the robot. Unusually, the 24,576 parallel environments are partitioned into M=6 blocks, each running a distinct policy; the final deployed policy is the 'leader' (block 1), which has been updated with importance-sampled data from all five follower blocks, while followers serve only as diverse data generators.

## Method

SAPG splits N environments into M blocks, each executing a separate policy parameterized by a shared backbone conditioned on a small local vector phi_j (16 or 32 dimensions). A designated leader policy is updated with both its own on-policy PPO loss and an off-policy PPO loss computed on data from all followers, where the off-policy ratio is corrected by an importance-sampling weight mu = pi_{i,old}/pi_j and clipped to [mu(1-eps), mu(1+eps)]. Followers are updated only with their own on-policy data, preserving behavioral diversity. To further encourage exploration, each follower receives an entropy regularization term scaled by a distinct coefficient sigma(i-1), producing a mix of exploratory and exploitative trajectories. Off-policy data is subsampled to match the on-policy batch size to prevent noisy off-policy gradients from dominating, and the critic uses n-step returns for on-policy targets but 1-step returns for off-policy targets.

## Results

After 2×10¹⁰ samples, SAPG achieves the highest performance across all five dexterous manipulation tasks. On the three hard AllegroKuka tasks (measured by successes per episode), SAPG with entropy coefficient 0 reaches 35.7 on Regrasping, 23.7 on Throw, and 33.2 on Reorientation, while SAPG with coefficient 0.005 further improves Reorientation to 38.6. On the easier in-hand reorientation tasks (measured by episode reward), SAPG (coef=0) achieves 1.23×10⁴ on AllegroHand and 1.17×10⁴ on ShadowHand, outperforming all baselines in asymptotic performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla PPO, SAPG dominates on all tasks: on the hard AllegroKuka tasks, PPO achieves only 1.25 successes on Regrasping and 2.85 on Reorientation, while SAPG reaches 35.7 and 33.2–38.6 respectively — a >25× improvement. Against DexPBT (the strongest baseline on hard tasks), SAPG achieves 12–66% higher success rates, with the largest gap on Reorientation (38.6 vs. 23.2, a 66% improvement). Against PQL (the off-policy baseline), SAPG wins on asymptotic performance: on AllegroHand, SAPG (coef=0) scores 1.23×10⁴ vs. PQL's 1.01×10⁴ (21% higher), and on ShadowHand both reach ~1.28×10⁴. However, PQL is more sample-efficient early in training on the easier tasks, and on the hard AllegroKuka tasks PQL fails almost entirely (≤2.73 successes), confirming that off-policy methods struggle with high-DoF manipulation. Overall, SAPG is the only method that achieves strong performance across both hard and easy tasks simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAPG builds directly on PPO's clipped surrogate objective and the off-policy PPO formulation from Meng et al. (2023) for importance-sampled data aggregation. It departs from DexPBT (Petrenko et al., 2023), which also partitions environments among multiple policies but discards data from 'worse' policies, by instead fusing all collected transitions into a single leader update. The method also contrasts with off-policy approaches like PQL (Li et al., 2023), which can reuse all data but sacrifice asymptotic performance, positioning SAPG as a way to retain on-policy quality while gaining the data-coverage benefits of off-policy collection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical finding that PPO's asymptotic performance saturates at large batch sizes (tens of thousands of parallel environments) challenges the common assumption that simply scaling simulation capacity yields proportional gains, and is directly relevant to the growing sim2real robotics community using IsaacGym and similar engines. The leader-follower design with entropy-regularized followers provides a practical recipe for exploiting GPU-scale parallelism, though the approach is currently validated only on dexterous manipulation tasks and the optimal aggregation scheme (leader-follower vs. symmetric, subsampling ratio, number of policies) remains an open design space. A key limitation is that the method still requires on-policy data collection each iteration, so it does not address sample efficiency in the traditional sense—its benefit is purely in asymptotic quality given abundant compute.
