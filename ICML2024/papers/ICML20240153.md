## Problem

Continuous sequence-to-sequence prediction from raw sensor data (e.g., predicting velocity from IMU or tactile signals) is critical for robotics and medical devices, yet lacks both a comprehensive benchmark and effective deep sequence models. Classical model-based approaches require intractable system dynamics, while flat deep sequence models (LSTMs, Transformers) struggle with the high-frequency, long-sequence nature of sensor streams. The paper identifies that sensor data contains temporal structure at multiple natural frequencies that flat architectures fail to exploit.

## Input / Output

The method consumes full-length raw sensor trajectories (tactile magnetometer readings at 100 Hz from ReSkin/XELA, or IMU acceleration/gyro at 60–200 Hz), resampled to 50 Hz, with resting-signal subtraction for drift removal and one-step differences appended to each timestep. Outputs are physical quantities (end-effector velocity, joystick state, user velocity, or joint velocities) resampled to 5 Hz, predicted as the mean of a Gaussian with constant variance. Sequence lengths range from 450 to 12,000 sensor timesteps depending on the dataset, and the entire trajectory is passed to the model to handle data-dependent drift.

## Method

HiSS introduces a two-level temporal hierarchy by stacking SSMs at different resolutions: a low-level SSM (typically S4) processes non-overlapping chunks of size k (default 10, matching the 50 Hz/5 Hz sensor-to-output frequency ratio) and emits a single chunk feature per chunk (the last-timestep hidden state), while a high-level SSM (typically Mamba) operates on this compressed sequence to produce the output. The key insight is that sensor data exhibits phenomena at distinct natural frequencies (high-frequency vibration noise vs. low-frequency drift), so the low-level model learns temporally local representations while the high-level model focuses on global predictions over a shorter sequence. The architecture maintains O(N) computational complexity for non-overlapping chunks and is trained end-to-end with MSE loss. Across six real-world sensor datasets, HiSS achieves a 23% median MSE improvement over the best flat SSMs and demonstrates superior data efficiency in low-data regimes.

## Results

On CSP-Bench (six real-world sensor datasets), the best HiSS model (Mamba high-level + S4 low-level) achieves MSE of 0.5663 (Marker Writing), 0.1316 (Intrinsic Slip), 0.9010 (Joystick Control), 0.0302 (RoNIN), 0.0298 (VECtor), and 0.2527 (TotalCapture). HiSS outperforms the best flat SSM by a median 23% MSE improvement across tasks, with gains of 35.9% on Marker Writing, 30.7% on RoNIN, and 21.3% on VECtor. SSMs (Mamba, S4) themselves outperform the best Transformer and LSTM by 10% and 14% median MSE respectively. The sole exception is TotalCapture, where the flat LSTM (0.1767) outperforms all SSM-based models due to the high dimensionality of the input/output spaces.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiSS (Mamba/S4) outperforms the Causal Transformer [Vaswani et al., 2017] on all four tasks where it is evaluated, with the largest gap on Marker Writing (0.5663 vs. 2.3750 MSE). Against the LSTM [Kuchaiev & Ginsburg, 2017], HiSS wins on five of six tasks (e.g., 0.5663 vs. 1.1685 on MW, 0.0302 vs. 0.0444 on RoNIN) but loses on TotalCapture (0.2527 vs. 0.1767), where the high input/output dimensionality (39-dim IMU → 60-dim joint velocities) appears to favor the LSTM's gating mechanism. HiSS beats flat S4 [Gu et al., 2021] on every task (e.g., 0.5663 vs. 1.3190 on MW, 0.0302 vs. 0.0382 on RoNIN) and flat Mamba [Gu & Dao, 2023] on every task (e.g., 0.5663 vs. 0.8830 on MW, 0.0298 vs. 0.0319 on VECtor). Against MEGA [Ma et al., 2022], HiSS achieves lower MSE on all six tasks, with the most notable margin on Marker Writing (0.5663 vs. 0.8960) and RoNIN (0.0302 vs. 0.0370). Overall, the hierarchical SSM architecture consistently dominates flat sequence models, with the exception of TotalCapture where no SSM-based variant matches the flat LSTM.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiSS builds directly on structured state-space models (S4, Mamba) as its core sequence-processing primitive, and extends the hierarchical modeling paradigm previously applied to recommender systems, human activity recognition, and reinforcement learning into the SSM setting for continuous sensor prediction. Conceptually, the key departure from prior work is the observation that flat SSMs, despite their linear scaling, still reason over every sample at a single temporal resolution; HiSS introduces an explicit two-level temporal hierarchy where a low-level SSM extracts local chunk features and a high-level SSM performs global prediction over the compressed sequence. The paper also positions itself against the broader sequence-modeling literature (LSTMs, causal Transformers, MEGA) by demonstrating that SSMs are a more natural fit for high-frequency, long-context sensor data, and introduces CSP-Bench to fill the absence of a standardized evaluation suite for this problem class.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CSP-Bench is the first publicly available multi-sensor benchmark for continuous sequence-to-sequence prediction, addressing a long-standing gap that has hindered general-purpose algorithm development in sensor-based prediction. The practical finding that HiSS is more data-efficient than flat models and requires minimal preprocessing (e.g., low-pass filtering) is valuable for real-world deployments where labeled sensor data is expensive to collect. However, the failure on TotalCapture—where a flat LSTM outperforms all SSM-based models—highlights an unresolved limitation of SSMs on high-dimensional, noisy inputs, and the additional chunk-size hyperparameter remains a practical tuning burden. Open questions include whether the hierarchical structure generalizes beyond two levels, how to automatically select chunk size, and whether SSMs can be made more robust to high-dimensional sensor modalities.
