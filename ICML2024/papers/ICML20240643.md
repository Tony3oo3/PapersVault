## Problem

Standard nearest neighbor (NN) condensing seeks a minimal consistent subset of a labeled sample, but in some instance families the optimal unweighted condensed set requires Θ(n) points. This paper asks whether assigning weights to condensed points—thereby modifying the global distance function—can yield dramatically smaller condensed sets without incurring additional generalization error, and whether a practical heuristic for this weighted problem can be shown to be statistically sound.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240643/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled sample S in an arbitrary metric space (X, d) and produces a condensed subset S̃ ⊂ S together with a positive weight w(x) for each x ∈ S̃ (with w(x)=1 for all x ∉ S̃). The resulting classifier labels a query point x by its weighted-distance nearest neighbor in S̃, where the weighted distance is d̃(x, x') = d(x, x')/(w(x)·w(x')). Unlike standard NN, unequal weights produce circular (ball) decision boundaries between pairs of condensed points rather than the linear Voronoi boundaries of the unweighted case.

## Method

The paper defines the weighted distance NN (WNN) rule and proves it strictly generalizes both standard NN and a ball cover (BC) rule, establishing that WNN can condense instances to 2 points where NN requires Θ(n) (Theorem 3.1). A 2|S̃|-sample compression scheme is constructed via an encoding function that orders condensed points by a multiplicative weight-increase procedure and a reconstruction function that recovers weights from witness points, yielding generalization bounds (Theorem 3.4) nearly identical to those for unweighted NN. The practical algorithm (Algorithm 1) is a greedy set-cover heuristic: at each step it selects the point whose nearest-enemy ball (radius = distance to closest oppositely labeled point) covers the most uncovered same-label points, assigning that radius as the point's weight. Bayes consistency of the heuristic is established under separable metric space, atomless distribution, and piecewise-continuous labeling assumptions, leveraging the permutation-invariant compression bound and a sublinear condensed-set size argument.

## Results

On seven standard classification datasets (Magic, SatImage, Spambase, Twonorm, Phoneme, Segment, Shuttle), the WNN greedy heuristic achieves the best or tied-best compression ratio (fraction of training samples retained) and test error across all datasets. The most dramatic gains appear on Twonorm (6% retained vs. 15% for MSS and 16% for RSS, with test error 0.03 vs. 0.06/0.11) and Shuttle (0.5% retained vs. 3.0% for MSS and 0.8% for RSS). On small datasets where an exact integer-programming solver is feasible, WNN retains 12/35/4 points on Circle/Banana/Iris respectively, approaching the optimal IP solutions of 7/32/2 while far outperforming MSS (52/74/11) and RSS (45/66/9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed WNN greedy heuristic against two established unweighted NN condensing heuristics: MSS (Barandela et al., 2005) and RSS (Flores-Velazco & Mount, 2021). WNN achieves strictly better compression than both MSS and RSS on 6 of 7 datasets, tying MSS on Spambase (both retain 27%), and achieves the lowest test error on 5 of 7 datasets (tying on Phoneme and Segment). The largest compression advantage is on Twonorm, where WNN retains only 6% of training points versus 15% (MSS) and 16% (RSS), while also halving the test error. In the second trial against an exact integer-programming solver on small datasets (≤200 points), WNN retains 12/35/4 points on Circle/Banana/Iris, approaching the IP-optimal 7/32/2, while MSS and RSS retain 52/74/11 and 45/66/9 respectively — a 4–7× gap. The WNN heuristic thus consistently dominates both baselines on compression and matches or improves test accuracy, with the gap to the exact optimum being modest (within a factor of ~2) on the small-scale validation sets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical nearest neighbor condensing problem of Hart (1968) by introducing a weighted distance function, where each condensed point carries a weight that scales its influence on classification. The Ball Cover rule, which generalizes the NET algorithm of Gottlieb et al. (2018) from uniform to variable-radius balls, serves as a conceptual bridge: WNN subsumes both standard NN condensing and BC condensing, and the greedy heuristic is a direct application of Chvátal's (1979) set-cover approximation. The generalization bounds build on the compression-scheme framework of Gottlieb et al. (2014) and Hanneke et al. (2021), with the key addition of a permutation-invariant reconstruction that yields tighter bounds for the specific weight function used in the heuristic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central theoretical result—that WNN can achieve Θ(n)-fold better condensing than unweighted NN while incurring essentially no additional generalization error—resolves a natural tension between model power and overfitting risk in prototype-based learning. The Bayes consistency proof for the greedy heuristic provides statistical justification for its use, though the log-factor gap between the greedy solution and the optimal condensed set (inherited from set-cover approximation) remains an open optimization question. Empirically, gains over strong baselines like MSS and RSS are modest and dataset-dependent, and the exact IP comparison is limited to under 200 points, leaving the true quality gap between the heuristic and the WNN optimum uncharacterized at scale.
