## Problem

Differential privacy mechanisms are routinely calibrated and compared using a single (ε,δ) pair, yet two mechanisms can share the same (ε,δ) guarantee while offering substantially different privacy protections at all other operating points. The Blackwell theorem provides a rigorous decision-theoretic comparison but is inapplicable when trade-off functions cross—a common occurrence in DP-SGD—leaving no principled tool to quantify the excess privacy vulnerability of choosing one mechanism over another.

## Input / Output

The method requires only oracle access to the trade-off functions (or equivalently the SGM parameters σ, p, N) of two DP mechanisms and computes the Δ-divergence via grid discretisation in O(N) time. The primary output is a scalar quantifying worst-case excess privacy vulnerability, with the symmetrised version yielding a metric distance on the space of all DP mechanisms. A secondary output is the minimum Bayes error function R_min(π), which encodes the same information as the trade-off function but in a form amenable to hierarchical Bayesian modelling of adversary priors.

## Method

The paper introduces the Δ-divergence, defined as the infimum shift κ ≥ 0 that moves one mechanism's trade-off function below and to the left of another, thereby restoring universal Blackwell dominance. This is shown equivalent to the maximum difference in minimum Bayes error functions, providing a natural Bayesian decision-theoretic interpretation as worst-case regret in adversary error. The symmetrised Δ-divergence is proven to be a metric on the space of DP mechanisms, exactly corresponding to the Lévy distance between the associated CDFs. For composition, a Berry-Esséen-type bound shows that the Δ-divergence between N-fold self-compositions vanishes at rate O(1/√N), with the sign of the eventual Blackwell ordering determined by the ratio of PLRV moment functionals η = v₁/√(v₂−v₁²). Empirically, the framework reveals that standard DP-SGD calibration practices (large sampling rates, many steps) correspond to the most vulnerable regime, with a Δ-divergence of 0.12 translating to a 15% increase in data reconstruction attack success.

## Results

The paper introduces the Δ-divergence as a worst-case measure of excess privacy vulnerability between DP mechanisms. Key quantitative findings include: Δ(Gaussian‖Laplace) = 0.005 vs. Δ(Laplace‖Gaussian) = 0.034 for unit-sensitivity mechanisms, showing the Gaussian offers only marginally stronger protection for a narrow range of adversary priors. On CIFAR-10 with (8,10⁻⁵)-DP calibration, the baseline SGM (σ=2.0) achieves 72.6% validation accuracy, while the Δ-divergence increases monotonically with noise multiplier up to a maximum of ~0.12. This maximum Δ-divergence of 0.12 corresponds to a 15% increase in vulnerability to data reconstruction attacks relative to the baseline, as validated against Hayes et al.'s attack bounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Hayes et al. (2023), the paper demonstrates that the Δ-divergence provides a formal, quantitative explanation for their empirical finding that SGMs calibrated to the same (ε,δ) but with different (σ, p) parameters exhibit disparate reconstruction attack vulnerability. Specifically, the probability of a successful data reconstruction attack increases almost exactly linearly with the Δ-divergence from the baseline mechanism, with the theoretical upper bounds matched almost exactly by actual attacks. Against De et al. (2022), the paper re-assesses their CIFAR-10 SGM parameter selection and shows that their claimed 'optimal' noise multiplier σ̃=3.0 is at best Pareto-efficient rather than truly optimal: mechanisms with σ̃>3.0 are Pareto-inefficient because one could simultaneously increase accuracy and decrease excess vulnerability by choosing σ̃=3.0. The paper further shows that current best practices of using large sampling rates and many steps (as in De et al. and Berrada et al.) correspond to the most vulnerable regime in terms of Δ-divergence, a risk completely undetected when reporting only a single (ε,δ) pair.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the Blackwell dominance framework for comparing DP mechanisms (Dong et al., 2022) to the previously intractable case where trade-off functions cross, which is the norm for DP-SGD. It builds on the f-DP and privacy profile formalisms (Balle et al., 2020; Dong et al., 2022), the tightly dominating pair characterization (Zhu et al., 2022), and CLT-like convergence results for composed mechanisms (Dong et al., 2022; Sommer et al., 2018) to introduce the Δ-divergence and an approximate Blackwell ordering. The Bayesian interpretation via minimum Bayes error extends the hypothesis-testing view of DP by incorporating adversary priors, connecting to recent work on adversarial modelling (Balle et al., 2022; Jayaraman et al., 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that current DP-SGD best practices (large sampling rates, many steps) correspond to the highest excess privacy vulnerability regime, meaning that calibrating to a single (ε,δ) point can mask substantial differences in protection against reconstruction and membership inference attacks. The Δ-divergence provides a principled, worst-case metric that is computationally tractable (O(N) with oracle access) and connects to the Lévy distance, giving the space of DP mechanisms a natural metric structure. Open questions include whether the composition bounds (Theorem 3) are tight in practice, how to incorporate training cost into the Pareto analysis, and whether the framework extends to non-self-composition settings or adaptive mechanisms.
