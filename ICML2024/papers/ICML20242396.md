## Problem

Integrated Gradients (IG) produces noisy feature visualizations for vision models and is vulnerable to targeted attributional attacks, both stemming from its use of a linear path of attribution that is agnostic to the intrinsic geometry of the data manifold. While prior work has addressed noise (e.g., SmoothGrad, BlurIG) or robustness (e.g., adversarial training) separately, no method simultaneously tackles both issues by respecting the curved structure of the underlying data distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242396/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a target RGB image (192×192) and a black or white baseline image, along with a pre-trained convolutional VAE and a classifier trained on VAE reconstructions, MIG produces a pixel-wise feature attribution map. The VAE must be trained on the target dataset augmented with black and white images to ensure clean baseline reconstructions, and the classifier is trained on VAE reconstructions rather than raw images, making the entire pipeline dataset-specific. The geodesic path is computed in the 64×12×12 latent space and mapped back to image space via the VAE decoder before gradient integration.

## Method

MIG replaces the linear path in IG with geodesics on a Riemannian manifold learned by a convolutional VAE. The VAE decoder induces a Riemannian metric G_g = J_g^T J_g over the latent space, and geodesic paths between the baseline and target latent codes are computed by minimizing the energy functional via gradient descent with vector-Jacobian products (a modification of Shao et al. 2018 that uses the exact decoder Jacobian rather than the encoder approximation). The classifier's gradients are then integrated along the mapped geodesic path g(γ*(t)) in image space, yielding attributions that conform to the curved data manifold. This approach simultaneously reduces perceptual noise (by avoiding saturation regions and capturing pixel interdependencies) and increases robustness to targeted attributional attacks (by keeping interpolants on-manifold where VAE denoising properties suppress adversarial perturbations), without requiring adversarial training of the classifier.

## Results

On Oxford-IIIT Pets, MIG achieves a maximum sensitivity (SENSmax) of 0.17 and explanation infidelity (INFD) of 1.86, substantially outperforming IG (0.87 / 7.65), BlurIG (0.75 / 6.41), and Smooth IG (0.42 / 4.30). On the 102 Flowers dataset, MIG records SENSmax of 0.21 and INFD of 3.46, again the best among all compared methods. Under targeted attributional attacks, MIG attains the highest Structural Similarity Index (SSI) across all three backbones (VGG-16: 0.87, ResNet-18: 0.91, InceptionV1: 0.86 on Pets), compared to IG's 0.36–0.48 and Smooth IG's 0.61–0.69.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIG dominates all quantitative baselines on both datasets. Against IG (Sundararajan et al., 2017), MIG reduces SENSmax by ~80% (0.17 vs 0.87 on Pets) and INFD by ~75% (1.86 vs 7.65), and achieves SSI of 0.87–0.91 versus IG's 0.36–0.48 under targeted attacks. BlurIG (Xu et al., 2020) and Smooth IG (Smilkov et al., 2017) are intermediate but still substantially worse than MIG on both sensitivity and infidelity metrics. In the qualitative comparison (Figure 4), MIG produces visibly cleaner and more perceptually aligned feature maps than Saliency (Simonyan et al., 2014), Gradients × Input (Shrikumar et al., 2017), and Guided Backpropagation (Springenberg et al., 2015), all of which exhibit significant noise. EIG (Jha et al., 2020), which uses linear paths in a flat latent space, is the closest competitor to MIG; in one example (last row of Figure 4) EIG and MIG produce similar maps because the geodesic passes through a low-curvature region, but MIG consistently outperforms EIG on the quantitative metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: path-based feature attribution (building directly on Integrated Gradients, Sundararajan et al. 2017) and the Riemannian geometry of deep generative models (building on Shao et al. 2018's geodesic computation for VAEs). Rather than patching IG's noise through path optimization (GIG), blurring (BlurIG), or averaging (SmoothGrad), MIG addresses the root geometric cause by replacing the linear path with geodesics on a VAE-learned data manifold. The approach is further motivated by the recent PAG/off-manifold robustness literature (Srinivas et al. 2023; Bordt et al. 2023), which established that gradient alignment with the data manifold underlies both robustness and perceptual quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MIG offers a principled, geometry-driven solution that simultaneously reduces perceptual noise and improves robustness to targeted attributional attacks without requiring adversarial training of the classifier—a computationally expensive and accuracy-detrimental process. However, the approach introduces non-trivial overhead (training a VAE, computing geodesics via iterative gradient descent) and has only been validated on two fine-grained datasets (Oxford-IIIT Pets, Oxford 102 Flowers) with three backbones, leaving open questions about scalability to larger, more diverse benchmarks like ImageNet. The paper itself acknowledges that new adversarial attacks could specifically target the geodesic path, and the VAE's variational approximation may not faithfully capture the true data manifold geometry in all regimes.
