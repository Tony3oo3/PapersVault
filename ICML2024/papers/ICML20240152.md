## Problem

Estimating the total variation distance between two Bayes net distributions was previously only tractable for product distributions (graphs with no edges), leaving open whether efficient relative approximation schemes exist for structured Bayes nets such as those over trees or bounded-treewidth graphs. The paper addresses this by establishing a structure-preserving reduction from TV distance estimation to probabilistic inference, showing that any class of Bayes nets admitting efficient inference automatically admits an FPRAS for TV distance estimation.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes two Bayes nets P and Q specified by CPTs over the same DAG G with n nodes and alphabet size ℓ, along with accuracy ε and confidence δ. It constructs a new Bayes net L over the identical DAG G but with alphabet size ℓ² (jointly representing pairs (X_i, Y_i)), then makes O(n³ε⁻²ℓ log δ⁻¹) probabilistic inference queries to L. The output is a (1+ε)-relative approximation of d_TV(P,Q) with probability at least 1−δ, with total running time T(G,ℓ²)·O(n³ε⁻²ℓ log δ⁻¹) where T is the inference oracle's running time.

## Method

The core mechanism is a new notion of partial coupling: a joint distribution L on (X,Y) over the same DAG G where X~P, Pr[X=Y=w] ≤ min(P(w),Q(w)), and the CPTs are defined so that Pr[(X_i,Y_i)=(b,b)|parents]=(min of the two conditional probabilities), with remaining mass allocated to ensure the X-marginal matches P. This partial coupling is expressible as a Bayes net over G with alphabet [ℓ]², unlike a full coupling which may not preserve the DAG structure. The algorithm then applies importance sampling with estimator f(w)=g*(w)/g(w) under distribution π(w)=g(w)/Z, where g(w)=P(w)−∏min(P_{i|Π(i)},Q_{i|Π(i)}) overestimates g*(w)=P(w)−min(P(w),Q(w)). The normalization constant Z equals Pr_L[X≠Y] and is computed via a single inference query to L, while sampling from π is done iteratively symbol-by-symbol using O(nℓ) inference queries. A key technical bound Z ≤ 2n·d_TV(P,Q) ensures the estimand is inverse-polynomially large, making Monte Carlo sampling efficient and yielding the FPRAS for Bayes nets of O(log n) treewidth via variable elimination.

## Results

The paper establishes a structure-preserving reduction from TV distance estimation to probabilistic inference over Bayes nets (Theorem 1.1), yielding an FPRAS with running time T(G,ℓ²)·O(n³ε⁻²ℓ log δ⁻¹) and the same number of inference queries. As a direct corollary (Theorem 1.2), this gives the first FPRAS for estimating TV distance between two Bayes nets of treewidth O(log n) over the same DAG, running in poly(n)·O(ε⁻² log δ⁻¹) time for constant alphabet size. The paper also proves #P-completeness of exact TV distance computation between a bounded in-degree Bayes net and the uniform distribution (Theorem 1.3), and provides an FPRAS for the Bayes-net-vs-uniform case with running time O(n³ℓ^(2d+2)ε⁻² log δ⁻¹) where d is the maximum in-degree (Theorem 1.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Prior to this work, FPRAS for TV distance estimation existed only for product distributions (Feng et al., 2023; Feng et al., 2024), which correspond to Bayes nets over an edgeless graph. This paper extends the FPRAS guarantee to the much broader class of Bayes nets with O(log n) treewidth, resolving the open question of whether an FPRAS exists even for Bayes nets over trees (treewidth 1). The key technical advance over the product-distribution setting is the introduction of partial couplings, which overcome the structural obstacles that prevent a standard local coupling from being a valid Bayes net over the same DAG. In terms of complexity, the paper complements Bhattacharyya et al. (2023), who showed #P-completeness of exact TV distance for product distributions, by proving #P-completeness for the Bayes-net-vs-uniform case (Theorem 1.3) while simultaneously providing an FPRAS (Theorem 1.4), demonstrating a sharp separation between exact and approximate computation. No empirical baselines are compared, as the contributions are entirely theoretical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the local coupling approach of Feng et al. (2023), which gave an FPRAS for TV distance between product distributions, to general Bayes nets by introducing the new notion of partial coupling—a relaxation of classical coupling where the second marginal need not match Q exactly. The key conceptual departure is that a full coupling may be infeasible for structured distributions (due to dependency conflicts along the DAG), yet the partial coupling suffices for TV distance estimation. The reduction is structure-preserving: inference queries are made to a Bayes net over the same DAG as the inputs, so treewidth is inherited, enabling the FPRAS for O(log n) treewidth via variable elimination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This resolves the open question of whether an FPRAS exists for TV distance between Bayes nets over trees (treewidth 1), and more broadly establishes the first FPRAS for any class of Bayes nets admitting efficient inference. The partial coupling notion is flagged as potentially of independent interest beyond this specific application. Open questions include extending the framework to undirected graphical models and other distance notions such as Wasserstein metrics. The practical setting—comparing two learned parameterizations of a fixed Bayes net structure from different data batches—motivates the same-DAG requirement.
