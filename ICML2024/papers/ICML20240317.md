## Problem

Existing neural operators for PDEs on deformable domains learn a direct geometry-to-solution (G-S) mapping, which requires large datasets to cover the vast and combinatorially complex geometry space. For industrial applications such as engineering design optimization, even a single simulation can take hours or days, making it prohibitive to collect sufficient geometry-solution pairs. The paper asks whether a neural operator can instead learn the *change* in solution caused by a geometric deformation relative to a known reference, a task that is inherently more local and data-efficient.

## Input / Output

RNO takes a reference solution u_r sampled on an irregular mesh of the reference domain Ω_r, the query mesh points of the deformed domain Ω_q, and a discretized deformation vector field δx_q = φ⁻¹(x_q) − x_q constructed from 1-to-1 boundary correspondence between Ω_r and Ω_q. Geometric parameters (centers, radii, etc.) of both reference and query objects are also fed in. The network outputs the solution difference δu = u_q − u_r∘φ⁻¹ on the query mesh; the full predicted solution is then û_q = u_r∘φ⁻¹ + δu, where the pushforward u_r∘φ⁻¹ is obtained by interpolating u_r at the deformed reference points. The method handles variable numbers of geometric objects (1–9 holes, 2–14 pin-fins) and works on both 2D and 3D irregular meshes.

## Method

RNO is a stack of kernel-integral layers following the neural operator framework of Li et al. (2020b), but with two key architectural innovations. First, a geometry encoder uses a shared MLP over each boundary component's parameters and sums the outputs to handle an arbitrary number of geometric objects. Second, each integral layer employs Distance-Aware Cross Attention (DACA), which multiplies standard cross-attention weights by a Gaussian distance kernel h_γ(d) = exp(−d²/γ²) to amplify the signal from local geometric deformations; the attention jointly processes three input functions (hidden state, pushforward reference solution, and deformation field) on irregular meshes. The training target is the material derivative δu rather than the full solution, which reduces target complexity since the pushforward u_r∘φ⁻¹ is computed analytically. The deformation φ is constructed by matching boundary points between reference and query, assigning each interior query point the shift of its nearest boundary point weighted by a Gaussian distance decay, and applying a smooth cutoff near domain walls to prevent out-of-bounds shifts. A linear-complexity variant of DACA is also provided, approximating the Gaussian distance kernel via random feature mapping to reduce the O(N²) attention cost to O(Nd²D).

## Results

On the four primary PDE benchmarks (NS2d-360, NS2d-sq-360, Inductor2d-320, Heatsink3d-80), RNO achieves the lowest l2 relative error across all solution components, with headline values of 3.9e-2 (u), 1.1e-1 (v), 4.2e-2 (p) on NS2d-360 and 4.3e-3 (Az), 1.7e-2 (Bx), 2.2e-2 (By) on Inductor2d-320. Compared to the strongest baseline R-GNOT, RNO reduces error by roughly 2–3× on most components (e.g., 3.9e-2 vs 1.0e-1 on NS2d u; 4.3e-3 vs 2.5e-2 on Inductor2d Az). Against the natural pushforward baseline u_r∘φ⁻¹, RNO achieves 50–80% error reduction (Table 7). On the additional Airfoil-Euler dataset, RNO attains l2 error of 3.4e-2, outperforming GNOT (4.0e-2), Geo-FNO (7.9e-2), CORAL (8.1e-2), and NU-FNO (1.5e-1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RNO outperforms all six baselines (GNOT, Geo-FNO, R-GNOT, R-FNO-i, R-MIONet, and the pushforward-only baseline) on every component of the four primary datasets. The closest competitor is R-GNOT (GNOT augmented with reference solution and deformation inputs), which RNO beats by a factor of ~2–6× (e.g., 3.9e-2 vs 1.0e-1 on NS2d u; 4.3e-3 vs 2.5e-2 on Inductor2d Az), confirming that the gain comes from learning the material derivative δu rather than merely receiving extra inputs. Geo-FNO and R-FNO-i are consistently the weakest baselines, with errors 3–10× higher than RNO, largely because their grid-based architectures struggle with irregular meshes and varying numbers of geometric objects. On the Airfoil-Euler dataset, RNO (3.4e-2 l2) beats GNOT (4.0e-2), Geo-FNO (7.9e-2), CORAL (8.1e-2), and NU-FNO (1.5e-1). On the more challenging Airfoil-RANS dataset, RNO is competitive but not uniformly best: it matches R-GNOT on pressure (0.10 vs 0.11) but trails on velocity (0.32 vs 0.30 for u, 0.32 vs 0.19 for v) and turbulent viscosity (0.22 vs 0.19), which the authors attribute to the dataset not being purpose-built for reference-query pairing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in deformable-domain neural operators (Geo-FNO, GINO, NUNO, GNOT) that learn a direct geometry-to-solution (G-S) mapping, instead formulating the target as the material derivative of the solution operator — i.e., the *change* in solution due to a small geometric deformation. Architecturally, it builds on the kernel-integral/attention formulation of GNOT and Cao (2021) for irregular meshes, adapts the shared-encoder-with-summation design from Molinaro et al. (2023) for multiple geometry components, and introduces a distance-aware cross-attention (DACA) layer inspired by DA-Transformer (Wu et al., 2020) but weighted by physical Euclidean distance. The linear-complexity variant of DACA, using random feature mapping to approximate the Gaussian distance kernel, is a novel design not previously applied in this context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is data efficiency: RNO achieves competitive accuracy with 80–360 training samples where G-S neural operators require substantially more data to cover the vast geometry space, directly addressing the bottleneck in engineering design optimization where each simulation can take hours or days. The method is limited to smooth (diffeomorphic) deformations without topological changes, and the Airfoil-RANS experiments reveal that choosing appropriate references when boundary conditions also vary remains an open practical challenge. Future work could explore adaptive reference selection, handling of topological transitions, and integration into closed-loop shape optimization pipelines where RNO serves as a differentiable surrogate.
