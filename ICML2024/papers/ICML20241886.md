## Problem

Traditional feature selection methods identify a single universal set of informative features for the entire population, but in many domains (healthcare, recommendation, neuroscience) feature relevance varies with contextual variables (e.g., age, gender, location, time) that do not themselves predict the outcome. Existing approaches either ignore context, concatenate it with explanatory features (obscuring interpretability), or train separate models per context value (computationally expensive and requiring arbitrary binning for continuous contexts).

## Input / Output

The method takes a D-dimensional explanatory feature vector and an L-dimensional contextual variable vector (which can be categorical, continuous, or multi-dimensional, e.g., age+gender or latitude+longitude) as input. It produces a prediction (class label or regression value), a context-dependent binary gate vector indicating which features are informative for the given context, and in the weighted variant, a context-dependent weight vector quantifying each selected feature's contribution. During training, gates are continuous relaxations in [0,1] via truncated Gaussians; at inference they collapse to binary selections.

## Method

c-STG introduces a hypernetwork that maps contextual variables z to the parameters μ(z) of conditional Bernoulli distributions governing per-feature selection gates. The discrete Bernoulli variables are relaxed using a truncated Gaussian (μ_d(z) + ε_d, clipped to [0,1]) with the reparameterization trick for differentiable training, and the l0 sparsity penalty is approximated by the Gaussian CDF term Φ(μ_d(z)/σ), which penalizes open gates in a context-conditioned manner. A separate prediction network f_θ consumes the gated (and optionally weighted) features to produce the output, and both networks are trained end-to-end via Monte Carlo gradient estimation. The weighted c-STG variant augments the hypernetwork to also output a context-dependent weight vector w(z), so the prediction network receives x ⊙ s̃(z) ⊙ w(z), combining selection and significance. Theoretical analysis proves c-STG achieves risk ≤ STG and that STG's optimal gate parameters equal the expectation of c-STG's optimal parameters over the context distribution, formalizing the advantage of context-adaptive selection.

## Results

On the synthetic XOR1 dataset, both c-STG and weighted c-STG achieve 100% accuracy, far surpassing STG (73.98%), AFS (78.95%), and Contextual LASSO (50.60%). On XOR2, weighted c-STG reaches R²=0.9956, nearly perfect, compared to c-STG (0.8739), AFS (0.7572), and Contextual LASSO (0.7316). On real-world datasets, weighted c-STG achieves 89.23% accuracy on Heart Disease (vs. 87.88% for STG with context) and R²=0.5308 on Housing (vs. 0.4616 for Contextual LASSO), while c-STG attains 98.66% on rotated MNIST (vs. 98.55% for STG). In terms of sparsity, c-STG selects the fewest features on XOR1 (3.00), XOR2 (2.00), and MNIST (247.25 pixels), confirming that context-conditioned selection yields both higher accuracy and greater interpretability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

c-STG and weighted c-STG outperform all six baselines on every dataset. Against the closest competitor, Contextual LASSO, weighted c-STG gains +0.264 R² on Housing (0.5308 vs. 0.4616) and +1.04% accuracy on Heart Disease (89.23% vs. 83.19%), while c-STG achieves 100% on XOR1 versus Contextual LASSO's 50.60%. Compared to STG (the direct predecessor), c-STG improves XOR1 accuracy by +26 percentage points (100% vs. 73.98%) and XOR2 R² by +0.52 (0.8739 vs. 0.3516), demonstrating that conditioning gates on context is far more effective than simply concatenating context features. Against AFS (active feature selection), weighted c-STG leads on XOR2 by +0.24 R² and on Housing by +0.13 R², while matching or exceeding AFS on MNIST (98.69% vs. 94.83%). INVASE, a sample-wise method, is outperformed on all datasets, with the largest gap on XOR1 (100% vs. 52.36%) and Housing (0.5308 vs. 0.3335 R²). CEN, which models context-dependent linear coefficients without sparsity, is dominated on all datasets, particularly on XOR1 (100% vs. 50.08%) and MNIST (98.69% vs. 87.66%), confirming that stochastic gate sparsity provides a meaningful advantage over unregularized contextual models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Stochastic Gates (STG; Yamada et al., 2020), which uses a truncated-Gaussian relaxation of Bernoulli variables for population-level feature selection, by conditioning the gate parameters on contextual variables through a hypernetwork. It departs from prior contextual feature selection methods (CEN, Contextual LASSO, Contextualized ML) in two key ways: it accommodates non-linear prediction models rather than restricting to linear functions, and it uses an l0-like stochastic gate regularization instead of l1 shrinkage, yielding sparser and more interpretable solutions. The hypernetwork-based parameterization also distinguishes it from sample-wise (INVASE) and active feature selection (AFS) approaches, as it generalizes to unseen context values without per-sample feature acquisition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a single unified model that handles categorical, continuous, and multi-dimensional contexts, avoiding the combinatorial explosion of training separate models per context value. The neuroscience application—replacing 7,866 separate SVM models with one c-STG model while recovering the same biological findings—demonstrates practical efficiency gains that could accelerate analysis in high-dimensional time-series settings. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is the lack of established theory for tuning the regularization parameter λ, unlike LASSO's well-developed path-following procedures, making hyperparameter selection a practical bottleneck. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very high-dimensional settings (e.g., genomics with millions of features) and whether the Monte Carlo sampling variance becomes prohibitive in such regimes.
