## Problem

Most off-policy deep RL methods for continuous control rely on the discounted reward criterion, which introduces an arbitrary temporal bias and requires careful tuning of the discount rate for continuing tasks. While the average reward criterion is more natural for such tasks, no existing off-policy DRL method with continuous action spaces combines it with maximum entropy RL, leaving a gap between the sample-efficient SAC framework and average reward optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes continuous state vectors from Gymnasium/MuJoCo environments (e.g., Ant, Hopper, Walker2d, Humanoid, Swimmer, HalfCheetah) and outputs continuous actions sampled from a stochastic Gaussian policy. A key distinction from standard SAC is that the Q-function is defined as the expected cumulative sum of (reward minus average reward) rather than a discounted sum, and the method handles episode termination by injecting a learned Reset Cost penalty and resetting the environment, treating the task as effectively continuing.

## Method

RVI-SAC extends SAC to the average reward criterion through three components: (1) a Critic update based on RVI Q-learning where the offset f(Q) is estimated from a mini-batch and smoothed via a Delayed f(Q) Update (a two-time-scale stochastic approximation that tracks f(Q) with a separate learning rate to reduce target variance without depending on a reference state); (2) an Actor update justified by a newly proved Average Reward Soft Policy Improvement Theorem showing that SAC-style KL-regularized policy updates monotonically improve the soft average reward; and (3) an automatic Reset Cost adjustment formulated as a constrained optimization problem that penalizes the frequency of reaching terminal states via a Lagrangian dual variable, eliminating the need to manually tune the reset penalty. The Q-Network uses Double Q-learning with target networks, and the overall algorithm is proven to converge almost surely in the tabular setting via ODE analysis of the two-time-scale SA.

## Results

RVI-SAC is evaluated on six Gymnasium Mujoco tasks (Ant, HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2d, Humanoid, Swimmer) using total return over 1,000 steps as the metric, with 10 random seeds. RVI-SAC achieves equal or better performance than SAC with its best discount rate in all environments except HalfCheetah, and outperforms ARO-DDPG in all six environments. A key finding is that SAC's performance is highly sensitive to the discount rate (γ=0.99 works best in most environments, but Swimmer requires γ=0.999, which destabilizes other tasks), whereas RVI-SAC avoids this hyperparameter sensitivity entirely by using the average reward criterion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018b) with discount rates γ ∈ {0.97, 0.99, 0.999}, RVI-SAC matches or exceeds the best-performing SAC variant in 5 out of 6 environments (all except HalfCheetah, where it is comparable). In Swimmer specifically, SAC requires γ=0.999 to reach its best performance, but this setting degrades SAC in all other environments; RVI-SAC achieves Swimmer performance on par with SAC(γ=0.999) while maintaining SAC(γ=0.99)-level performance elsewhere. Against ARO-DDPG (Saxena et al., 2023), the only prior off-policy average-reward DRL method with continuous actions, RVI-SAC outperforms it in all six environments. The paper attributes this advantage to the Maximum Entropy Reinforcement Learning framework (stochastic policies with entropy regularization) that RVI-SAC inherits from SAC, as opposed to ARO-DDPG's deterministic policy. When both methods are given the same reward structure (automatic Reset Cost adjustment), RVI-SAC still outperforms both SAC-with-Reset and ARO-DDPG-with-Reset, confirming that the average reward criterion itself contributes to the performance gain beyond just the reward shaping.

## Relation to Prior Work

RVI-SAC sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the maximum entropy off-policy DRL framework of SAC, which provides the actor update and entropy-augmented objective; (2) RVI Q-learning (Abounadi et al., 2001; Wan et al., 2020), a tabular average-reward Q-learning algorithm whose critic update is extended here to neural networks via the novel Delayed f(Q) Update technique; and (3) ATRPO's (Zhang & Ross, 2021) environment-reset mechanism for handling episode termination in average-reward settings, which is generalized here through automatic Reset Cost adjustment via constrained optimization. The key departure from prior work is that no existing off-policy DRL method with continuous actions combined the average reward criterion with maximum entropy policy optimization, and the Delayed f(Q) Update is a new technical contribution that resolves the variance amplification problem when naively applying RVI Q-learning to neural networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: for continuing tasks (robot locomotion, queuing systems), the average reward criterion is more natural than discounted reward, yet the most sample-efficient off-policy DRL methods (SAC, TD3) are built around the discounted criterion. RVI-SAC provides a drop-in alternative that eliminates the need to tune the discount rate—a known source of instability and suboptimality—while maintaining competitive performance. Limitations include convergence analysis only in the tabular setting (no finite-time or function-approximation guarantees), evaluation restricted to six Mujoco tasks, and no comparison with on-policy average-reward methods like APO. Open questions include whether the Delayed f(Q) Update generalizes to other function approximators and whether the automatic Reset Cost mechanism scales to tasks with more complex termination dynamics.
