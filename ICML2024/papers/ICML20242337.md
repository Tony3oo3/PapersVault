## Problem

In test-agnostic long-tail recognition, the test label distribution is unknown and can be arbitrarily imbalanced. Existing Mixture-of-Experts methods (e.g., SADE) assign each expert to a fixed label distribution, capturing only global variations across distributions while leaving local variations—milder changes concentrated around a particular distribution—unmodeled. This paper asks how to comprehensively exploit the hierarchical structure of both global and local label distribution variations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242337/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) and outputs a class label prediction via a weighted combination of K expert scoring functions. At test time, a self-supervised routing mechanism learns per-sample aggregation weights over the experts, so the final prediction is a convex combination of expert outputs rather than a single model's output. Training consumes only the long-tailed training set; no test-distribution information is available during training.

## Method

DirMixE models the unknown test label distribution as a sample from a Dirichlet mixture meta-distribution: K Dirichlet components (forward, uniform, backward) capture local variations around each skill region, while the mixture weights capture global variation across regions. Each expert f^(i) = g^(i) ∘ ψ shares a backbone ψ and has a distribution-specific head g^(i) assigned to one Dirichlet component. Training uses Monte Carlo sampling of M label distributions from the meta-distribution, minimizing the mean logit-adjustment loss plus a semi-variance penalty (penalizing only above-mean losses) to stabilize optimization. Theoretically, the semi-variance regularization yields a sharper generalization bound than fixed-distribution MoE, and the paper proves that V_+ ≍ V holds for common tail-shaped loss distributions (exponential, Gamma, Pareto), justifying the surrogate.

## Results

DirMixE achieves the best mean accuracy across all three main benchmarks under the authors' evaluation setting: 87.57% (±3.12) on CIFAR-10-LT, 53.34% (±10.22) on CIFAR-100-LT, and 61.50% (±6.43) on ImageNet-LT. The most significant gains appear in Backward-LT (inverse long-tail) test distributions, where DirMixE outperforms SADE by 2.4–4.1 points on CIFAR-100 and 2.0–2.4 points on ImageNet. The method also exhibits lower variance in performance across test distributions compared to SADE and BalPoE, reflecting the benefit of the semi-variance regularization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DirMixE consistently achieves the highest overall mean accuracy across all three datasets, beating the closest competitor SADE by 0.11 points on CIFAR-10, 1.57 points on CIFAR-100, and 0.79 points on ImageNet. The advantage is most pronounced in Backward-LT (inverse long-tail) settings: on CIFAR-100, DirMixE scores 45.26% vs. SADE's 41.65% and BalPoE's 29.10%, a gap of 3.6 and 16.2 points respectively. Against non-MoE baselines (LDAM, LA, VS, LADE, DDC), DirMixE leads by 7.6–13.7 points on CIFAR-100 mean accuracy. On Forward-LT and Uniform distributions, DirMixE is competitive with SADE and BalPoE (within ~0.5 points), confirming that the gains come primarily from better handling of tail-heavy test distributions. BalPoE, while strong on Forward-LT, suffers a dramatic drop on Backward-LT (29.10% on CIFAR-100), highlighting its inability to generalize to inverse long-tail shifts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-agnostic long-tail recognition framework introduced by SADE (Zhang et al., 2022), which assigns each MoE expert to a fixed label distribution (a Dirac delta). DirMixE replaces these fixed points with Dirichlet distributions, so that inter-component diversity captures global variations while intra-component randomness captures local variations around each target. The logit-adjustment loss (Menon et al., 2021) serves as the per-expert training objective, and the self-supervised test-time aggregation is inherited from SADE. The semi-variance regularization, inspired by variance-based regularization in distributionally robust optimization (Duchi & Namkoong, 2016), is a novel addition that yields a sharper generalization bound than the fixed-distribution approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hierarchical decomposition of label-distribution randomness into global and local components provides a principled way to design MoE ensembles for test-agnostic settings, and the semi-variance objective gives a concrete optimization target with a provably tighter generalization guarantee. However, the empirical gains over SADE are modest (≤1.6 points on mean accuracy), and the method's advantage is concentrated in Backward-LT distributions, suggesting the local-variation modeling is most beneficial when the test distribution deviates most from the training one. Open questions include whether the Dirichlet mixture parameters can be learned end-to-end rather than hand-designed, how the approach scales to more than three experts or to continuous (non-simplex) distribution spaces, and whether the Monte Carlo sampling overhead becomes prohibitive at larger scale.
