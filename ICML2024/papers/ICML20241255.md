## Problem

Pure data-driven proxy models for process systems require large labeled datasets and generalize poorly out-of-sample (e.g., time extrapolation), while existing physics-aware methods focus narrowly on spatial derivatives and neglect integral conservation laws and constitutive relations, limiting their universality across different process systems. This paper addresses the gap by fully incorporating the general form of conservation relations into the network architecture to improve both training efficiency and out-of-sample generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241255/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PAPM takes a set of process system conditions—initial state U^0, boundary conditions (Dirichlet, Neumann, Robin, or Periodic), external source input X_F, and coefficients λ—and autoregressively produces the time-dependent spatial field U^t at arbitrary time points. The model operates on 2D grids (64×64 or 128×32) and is specifically designed for out-of-sample scenarios where training labels cover only T' steps but inference must extrapolate to T steps (T' << T), with coefficient interpolation and extrapolation as additional evaluation axes.

## Method

PAPM decomposes the universal conservation equation ∂U/∂t = −∇·(J_C + J_D) + q + F into four explicit components—Diffusive Flows, Convective Flows, Internal Source Term, and External Source Term—each handled by a dedicated sub-module, with boundary conditions embedded via convolution padding before any computation. The Temporal-Spatial Stepping Module (TSSM) provides three spatial operator variants (localized with predefined/structure-preserved convolutional kernels, spectral with FFT-based E-Conv and S-Conv, and hybrid) matched to whether the system's equation structure is explicit, implicit, or mixed, while all variants share a common Neural ODE (Runge-Kutta) temporal stepper for stable continuous-time integration. The key design principle is that the general form of conservation relations dictates the data flow direction through the network, with fixed or partially trainable kernels encoding known physics (e.g., upwind for convection, symmetric for diffusion) and small neural networks (ResNet or S-Conv blocks) learning only the unknown mappings (v, h_O, h_F). This structured decomposition requires only 1–10% of the parameters of prior leading methods while achieving a 6.7% average performance improvement across nine generalization tasks.

## Results

PAPM achieves the best mean L₂ relative error (ε) on 8 out of 9 evaluation tasks across five 2D PDE benchmarks, with an average absolute improvement of 6.7% over the prior leading method PPNN. Notably, PAPM requires only 0.014M–0.035M trainable parameters (≈1–3% of PPNN's 1.19M–1.21M) and 1.23 FLOPs/M (vs. PPNN's 348.56 FLOPs/M). In coefficient-extrapolation tasks specifically, PAPM delivers a 9.6% average performance boost, demonstrating superior out-of-sample generalization. The only task where PAPM is not the top performer is NS2d at ν=1e-5, where CNO achieves 0.030 vs. PAPM's 0.034.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the prior leading physics-aware method PPNN, PAPM wins on 8 of 9 tasks with margins ranging from 0.003 (Lid2d C Ext.: 0.160 vs. 0.163) to 0.255 (NS2d ν=1e-3: 0.110 vs. 0.365), while using only ~1–3% of PPNN's parameters. Against pure data-driven baselines, PAPM dominates: ConvLSTM and PeRCNN are consistently the weakest (ε > 0.2 on most tasks), while neural operators (time-FNO2D, MIONet) and U-Net variants (U-FNet, CNO) are competitive on some tasks but degrade sharply in extrapolation. CNO is the strongest data-driven competitor, achieving the best result on NS2d ν=1e-5 (0.030 vs. PAPM's 0.034) and second-best on NSM2d C Int. (0.197 vs. 0.189), but PAPM outperforms it on the remaining 7 tasks. Dil-ResNet is competitive on RD2d (0.021 vs. PAPM's 0.018) but struggles on NS2d and NSM2d. Overall, physics-aware methods (PPNN, PAPM) substantially outperform all pure data-driven baselines in time extrapolation, confirming the value of structured physical inductive biases.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAPM sits at the intersection of physics-aware deep learning and process system modeling, departing from prior physics-aware methods (PeRCNN, PPNN, PiNDiff) that primarily encode spatial derivative structures while neglecting the integral conservation and constitutive relations that govern process dynamics. It repurposes the general form of conservation equations (diffusion, convection, internal/external sources) as an explicit data-flow architecture rather than a loss constraint, and introduces the Temporal-Spatial Stepping Module (TSSM) to unify localized (finite-difference convolution), spectral (FFT-based), and hybrid spatial operators under a common Neural ODE temporal stepping framework. Unlike learned-correction methods that assume precise known equation terms, PAPM only requires knowledge of the general conservation form, with unknown mappings (velocity, source functions) learned by small neural sub-networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that encoding the full conservation/constitutive structure (not just spatial derivatives) yields meaningful out-of-sample gains—6.7% average improvement and 9.6% in coefficient extrapolation—while using only 1% of the parameters of the prior leading method (PPNN), making it attractive for industrial settings where simulation data is expensive. The TSSM's modular design (localized/spectral/hybrid paths) offers a practical recipe for adapting to different PDE structures without redesigning the architecture from scratch. Key limitations include restriction to 2D regular grids, five benchmark PDEs, and the need for COMSOL-generated data for the hybrid datasets; the authors flag 3D extension, irregular-grid (GNN) adaptation, and multi-physics coupling as open directions.
