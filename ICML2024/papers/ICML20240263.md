## Problem

Recovering high-resolution images from undersampled coherent measurements (m < n) corrupted by multiplicative speckle noise lacks both theoretical guarantees and efficient algorithms. No prior work established an MSE bound for the maximum likelihood estimator under the deep image prior hypothesis in the multilook setting, and existing DIP-based projected gradient descent methods suffer from DIP overfitting to noise and prohibitively expensive per-iteration matrix inversions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes L complex-valued measurement vectors y_ℓ ∈ C^m (each generated as y_ℓ = AX_o w_ℓ + z_ℓ with independent speckle and additive noise), a known undersampled measurement matrix A ∈ C^{m×n} drawn from the Haar measure on orthogonal matrices, and noise variances σ_w² and σ_z². It outputs a real-valued estimate of |x_o| ∈ R^n (the magnitude of the complex signal, since phase is unrecoverable from fully-developed speckle). Experiments use 128×128 standard test images with undersampling ratios m/n ∈ {0.125, 0.25, 0.5} and L ∈ {25, 50, 100} looks.

## Method

The algorithm solves the constrained MLE problem via projected gradient descent where the projection step uses Bagged-DIP: the image is partitioned into non-overlapping patches at K different scales (e.g., 32×32, 64×64, 128×128), independent untrained CNNs reconstruct each patch, and the K resulting full-image estimates are averaged to produce a low-variance, weakly-dependent projection. To handle the expensive inversion of the 2m×2m covariance matrix B(x) at each PGD iteration, the authors warm-start a single Newton-Schulz step from the previous iteration's inverse, reducing per-iteration cost from O(m³) to O(m²) (e.g., ~52.8s → ~0.1ms for 128×128 images). Theoretically, they establish the first MSE upper bound for the MLE under the DIP hypothesis, showing (1/n)‖x̂−x_o‖² = O(√(k log n)/m + n√(k log n)/(m√(Lm))), where k is the DIP parameter count, revealing that m² = O(k log n) suffices for high-accuracy recovery as L → ∞.

## Results

On 8 standard 128×128 test images, Bagged-DIP-based PGD achieves average PSNR of 29.78 dB / SSIM 0.856 at the most favorable setting (m/n=50%, L=100), degrading to 19.24 dB / SSIM 0.406 at the most challenging setting (m/n=12.5%, L=25). The method outperforms the prior vanilla PGD baseline (Chen et al., 2023) by 1.09–1.47 dB across all look counts. Newton-Schulz approximation reduces per-iteration gradient computation from ~52.8 s (exact inverse, 128×128) to ~1×10⁻⁴ s, a ~500,000× speedup, while producing virtually identical reconstructions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the only prior method applicable in the undersampled (m<n) speckle regime—vanilla DIP-based PGD from Chen et al. (2023)—Bagged-DIP PGD wins by 1.09 dB (L=100), 1.47 dB (L=50), and 1.27 dB (L=25) in average PSNR across all images and undersampling rates. In the well-conditioned A=I setting, a trained DnCNN-UB denoiser (fed the sufficient statistic) outperforms Bagged-DIP PGD by 1–3 dB, but this comparison is not apples-to-apples: DnCNN-UB requires training data and a perfectly conditioned measurement matrix, whereas Bagged-DIP PGD is training-free and handles arbitrary undersampled A. The paper also validates its theoretical MSE bound empirically: doubling m yields a measured 3.99 dB PSNR gain (theory predicts 4.5 dB), and doubling L yields 1.42 dB (theory: 1.5 dB), confirming the predicted m^(3/2) and L^(1/2) scaling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-look compressed sensing framework of Zhou et al. (2022) to the multilook setting, deriving sharper MSE bounds via decoupling techniques and replacing compression-code codewords with the range of a deep image prior. Algorithmically, it builds on the DIP-based projected gradient descent of Chen et al. (2023) by introducing Bagged-DIP (adapting Breiman's bagging to ensemble multiple patch-level DIP estimates) and a one-step Newton-Schulz warm-start for the matrix inversion that dominates PGD's per-iteration cost. The DIP hypothesis itself follows Ulyanov et al. (2018) and Heckel & Hand (2018), but the paper is the first to establish a theoretical MSE upper bound for the MLE under this hypothesis in the multilook speckle-noise regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical bound is the first to characterize how reconstruction error scales with the number of looks L, the undersampling ratio m/n, and DIP complexity k, revealing that polynomial (rather than logarithmic) growth in signal dimension n is inherent to multiplicative noise models. Practically, the one-step Newton-Schulz result reduces per-iteration cost from O(m³) to O(m²), making DIP-based PGD feasible at 128×128 resolution where exact inversion takes ~53 seconds per step versus ~0.1 ms. Open questions include whether the n·√(k log n)/(m√(Lm)) rate is truly tight (the authors suspect it is but rely on a union bound), whether richer bagging strategies (more patch sizes, random crops) close the remaining 1–3 dB gap to supervised DnCNN-UB in the well-conditioned A=I case, and whether the framework extends to non-Gaussian or partially-developed speckle.
