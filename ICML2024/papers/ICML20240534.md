## Problem

In zero-sum game learning dynamics, small initial uncertainties in player states can be amplified over time, making accurate prediction of future strategies difficult. The prior work by Cheung et al. (2022) used differential entropy to quantify this uncertainty growth, but it fails to capture the uncertainty evolution under alternating update rules (e.g., AltMWU), where entropy remains constant despite significant covariance growth. This paper asks: how can one properly track and bound the accuracy of prediction in FTRL learning dynamics across different discretization schemes?

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a zero-sum payoff matrix A, an initial covariance matrix P₀ over the randomized canonical coordinates (cumulative strategy X and cumulative payoff y), a regularizer choice (Euclidean norm or negative entropy), a step size η, and a discretization scheme (Euler or Symplectic). Outputs are structured as asymptotic growth-rate characterizations: Θ((1+γη²)²ᵗ) for Euler, O(1) or Θ(t²) for Symplectic/continuous time, plus a Heisenberg-type lower bound ΔX_{i,α}·Δy_{i,α} ≥ (1/√2)·w_L(P(t₀))/π involving the linear Gromov width of the initial covariance ellipsoid. A corollary converts these into Chebyshev-style probability bounds on the risk of deviating from the expected trajectory.

## Method

The paper formulates continuous-time FTRL as a Hamiltonian system where cumulative strategy plays the role of position and cumulative payoff the role of momentum, then analyzes how the covariance matrix of these canonical coordinates evolves under three discretizations. A key structural result (Proposition 3.1) establishes that Euler discretization of the Hamiltonian system is exactly equivalent to simultaneous updates (GDA/MWU), while Symplectic discretization is equivalent to alternating updates (AltGDA/AltMWU), proven via a novel combination of Type I and Type II symplectic methods per agent. For Euclidean regularizers, the covariance evolution reduces to a linear system whose solution is characterized via real Jordan normal form, yielding exponential growth under Euler but only polynomial (or bounded) growth under Symplectic. For general regularizers, the non-squeezing theorem from symplectic geometry (McDuff & Salamon, 2017) combined with variance analysis yields the Heisenberg-type inequality, showing that the product of standard deviations in strategy and payoff spaces cannot both be simultaneously small. The paper further proves that differential entropy is invariant under Symplectic (alternating) updates due to volume preservation of symplectic maps, while it grows linearly under Euler (simultaneous) updates, explaining why entropy is an inadequate uncertainty measure for alternating dynamics.

## Results

The paper establishes that covariance growth in FTRL dynamics depends critically on the discretization scheme: Euler discretization (simultaneous updates) yields exponential covariance growth of Θ((1+γη²)^(2t)), while Symplectic discretization (alternating updates) and continuous-time FTRL yield only polynomial growth of Θ(t²) for cumulative strategies (when AAᵀ is singular) or bounded O(1) growth (when AAᵀ is non-singular), with cumulative payoffs always remaining O(1). A Heisenberg-type inequality is proven: ΔX_{i,α}(t)·Δy_{i,α}(t) ≥ (1/√2)·w_L(P(t₀))/π, where w_L is the linear Gromov width of the initial covariance ellipsoid, establishing a fundamental trade-off between prediction accuracy in strategy and payoff spaces. Differential entropy is shown to be constant under alternating updates (Proposition 4.1), making it an insufficient uncertainty measure, while it grows linearly under simultaneous updates (Proposition 4.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between three discretization schemes for FTRL dynamics: Euler (simultaneous updates), Symplectic (alternating updates), and continuous-time. Symplectic discretization clearly outperforms Euler in prediction accuracy: covariance grows only polynomially (Θ(t²) worst case) versus exponentially (Θ((1+γη²)^(2t))) under Euler, meaning an observer can maintain bounded uncertainty over time with alternating updates but not with simultaneous ones. Against the prior uncertainty measure of differential entropy (Cheung et al., 2022), the paper shows it is fundamentally inadequate for alternating updates since it remains constant (Proposition 4.1), failing to capture the growing spread of trajectories visible in Figure 1. The covariance-based measure proposed here captures both simultaneous and alternating update dynamics and yields the Heisenberg-type trade-off (Theorem 5.4) that has no analogue in the entropy framework. Numerical experiments (Figures 5–7) confirm the theoretical predictions: variance curves for Symplectic discretization match the continuous-time behavior, while Euler discretization shows clear exponential divergence with oscillations from trigonometric components.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Hamiltonian formulation of FTRL dynamics established by Bailey & Piliouras (2019) and Wibisono et al. (2022), which recast cumulative strategies and payoffs as position-momentum coordinates. It departs from Cheung et al. (2022), who used differential entropy as the uncertainty metric for MWU in zero-sum games, by showing that differential entropy is provably constant under alternating (symplectic) updates and thus cannot capture uncertainty evolution in that setting. The paper then introduces covariance/standard deviation as the appropriate metric, leveraging the non-squeezing theorem from symplectic geometry (McDuff & Salamon, 2017) to derive a Heisenberg-type inequality, and connects symplectic discretization to alternating play via a novel combined Type I/Type II discretization scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that differential entropy is invariant under alternating updates is a clean negative result that invalidates the primary uncertainty metric from the closest prior work, forcing the field to adopt covariance-based measures for tracking prediction accuracy in game dynamics. The Heisenberg-type inequality (Theorem 5.4) provides a fundamental lower bound on simultaneous predictability in strategy and payoff spaces, suggesting an inherent trade-off that no observer can overcome. Open questions include extending these results beyond two-player zero-sum games (e.g., potential games, multiplayer settings), to constrained strategy spaces with general regularizers where the non-squeezing argument may not directly apply, and to understanding whether the covariance-based uncertainty framework transfers to stochastic or noisy learning dynamics beyond the deterministic setting studied here.
