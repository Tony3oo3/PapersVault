## Problem

Existing approaches to the Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus (ARC) — whether neural or symbolic — fail to leverage experience for inter-task generalization. Self-improving language models that rely on reward-based filtering are sample-inefficient on ARC because correct programs are extremely rare, making positive rewards vanishingly sparse. The paper addresses how to enable scalable self-improvement in such sparse-reward program synthesis settings without suffering from catastrophic forgetting as the relabeled data distribution shifts.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes demonstration input-output grid pairs encoded as sparse object-centric text, where each color's cells are listed as [x,y] coordinates rather than a full 2D array, substantially reducing token count for large grids. The model generates a program in Hodel's domain-specific language (DSL), which is then executed on input grids to produce output grids. Test examples are never used during training or sampling; only demonstration examples condition the policy, and test performance is reserved for final evaluation under a pass@3 protocol.

## Method

CodeIt alternates between a sampling stage and a learning stage across 100 meta-iterations. In the sampling stage, a 220M-parameter CodeT5+ encoder-decoder policy generates programs conditioned on demonstration examples; each syntactically valid program is executed, and its realized output replaces the target output (hindsight relabeling) before the (program, input, realized-output) triplet is added to a replay buffer. In the learning stage, the policy is trained via negative log-likelihood on experiences drawn from the buffer using prioritized sampling, where each experience's priority is proportional to the fraction of demonstration outputs it correctly reproduces. This design ensures every valid program contributes a training signal (addressing extreme reward sparsity) while oversampling task-solving experiences prevents catastrophic forgetting. The policy is initialized with 400 ground-truth programs from Hodel's DSL augmented by 19,200 mutated programs, and 24 programs are sampled per task per meta-iteration at temperature 0.95.

## Results

CodeIt achieves state-of-the-art performance on the ARC evaluation set, solving 59/400 tasks (14.75%) under the standard pass@3 protocol. On the ARC Eval 412 set (pass@1, matching Gendron et al.'s protocol), CodeIt solves 59/412 tasks, outperforming GPT-4 (49/412) by 10 tasks. The method uses a 220M-parameter CodeT5+ model with the Hodel DSL, requiring only 120 hours on a single A100 GPU over 100 meta-iterations. Ablations confirm that hindsight relabeling contributes a 40% performance improvement (42/400 without relabeling vs. 59/400 with), and prioritized sampling is critical for policy retention (38/400 policy-only without priority vs. 49/400 with).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CodeIt outperforms all five literature baselines on the ARC evaluation set. Against the strongest neural baseline, Gendron et al. (GPT-4, 49/412 pass@1), CodeIt achieves 59/412 pass@1, a 10-task improvement using a 220M model versus GPT-4. Against the strongest symbolic baseline, Ainooson et al. (brute-force search, 26/400 pass@3), CodeIt more than doubles the score at 59/400. Ferré (2023) achieves 23/400 and Ferré (2021) only 6/400, both substantially below CodeIt. Mirchandani et al. (text-davinci-003, 27/400 pass@1) is also clearly outperformed. Among the paper's own custom baselines, the mutation d₁ baseline (42/400) is the closest competitor, but CodeIt still leads by 17 tasks, and CodeIt uniquely solves 23 tasks that the mutation baseline cannot, while the mutation baseline uniquely solves only 13.

## Relation to Prior Work

CodeIt sits at the intersection of Expert Iteration (ExIt), hindsight experience replay (HER), and self-improving language models. It most directly extends DreamCoder (Ellis et al., 2020), which also applies ExIt to programming-by-examples, but replaces DreamCoder's growing DSL and computationally intensive search with a fixed domain-specific language and a pretrained LLM policy that generates programs via sampling. The key methodological departure from recent self-improving LLM approaches (Singh et al., 2023; Gulcehre et al., 2023) is the use of hindsight relabeling to handle extreme reward sparsity in ARC, combined with prioritized experience replay to prevent catastrophic forgetting from the shifting relabeled data distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CodeIt demonstrates that a 220M-parameter model with experience-based learning can outperform GPT-4 on ARC, suggesting that domain-specific abstractions and iterative self-improvement can compensate for model scale in reasoning tasks. However, the approach still relies on hand-designed components (Hodel's DSL, an interpreter, and initial ground-truth programs), and fails on tasks requiring solutions longer than ~11 lines. Open questions include whether the method can bootstrap from random weights without prior knowledge, whether it can discover its own DSL primitives, and whether the hindsight-replay + prioritization recipe generalizes beyond ARC to other sparse-reward program synthesis domains.
