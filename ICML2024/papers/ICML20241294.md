## Problem

Existing gradient-based NAS methods cannot model network width and depth in a direct and fully differentiable manner: multiple-element selection and single-number selection model structural hyperparameters indirectly with many extra parameters, while gradient-estimate topk approaches model them directly but are non-differentiable and require unstable gradient estimation. This limits search efficiency and final performance, making automated model scaling impractical for large models where high search costs (hundreds to thousands of GPU days) are prohibitive.

## Input / Output

The method takes a supernet architecture (CNN or Transformer) and a user-specified resource target (MACs, parameter count, or latency) as input, and outputs an optimal width-and-depth configuration along with a retrained sub-model. The search space is fine-grained, covering each channel, feature, attention head, or residual block individually in the range [1, x_max] with step 1, rather than the coarse-grained ranges with large steps (e.g., 32) used by prior NAS methods. The resource constraint is enforced differentiably during search via a log-ratio penalty that activates only when current consumption exceeds the target.

## Method

The core contribution is a differentiable topk operator that uses a single learnable pruning-ratio parameter a ∈ [0,1] to directly model the number of selected elements k = ⌊(1−a)N⌋. To make the topk function smooth and optimizable despite unevenly distributed element importance, the method first applies rank-based importance normalization (mapping each element's importance to its relative rank divided by N), then generates a soft mask via sigmoid(λ(c′_i − a)) with λ = N (or 4N for small element counts), which approximates a hard mask with <0.05 error for all but ~6 boundary elements. Element importance is tracked using Taylor importance with a 0.99 moving-average decay, updated each training step without gradient descent. The full DMS algorithm jointly optimizes all structural parameters under a combined loss of task loss and a differentiable resource-constraint loss, requiring only ~1/10 of the epochs needed for full retraining to converge on the optimal structure.

## Results

On ImageNet, DMS improves EfficientNet-B0 top-1 accuracy by 1.4% (78.5% vs. 77.1%) and DeiT-Tiny by 0.6% (75.1% vs. 74.5%), while outperforming the zero-shot NAS method ZiCo by 1.3% at only 0.4 GPU days of search cost. On COCO object detection, DMS improves Yolo-v8-n mAP by 2.0% (39.4% vs. 37.4%). For Llama-7B pruning to 5.47B parameters, DMS achieves lower perplexity (17.13 vs. 17.39 on WikiText2) and higher zero-shot classification accuracy across BoolQ, WinoGrande, ARC-e, and ARC-c compared to LLMPruner.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMS outperforms all 23 compared baselines across every task and architecture tested. Against gradient-based NAS methods, DMS achieves 70.7% top-1 vs. 55.5% (MES), 58.6% (SNS), and 61.6% (GET) on ResNet-50 at 3G MACs, using fewer than 1/250 the learnable structural parameters of MES/SNS. Against zero-shot NAS, DMS beats ZiCo by 1.3% (79.4% vs. 78.1% at 0.45G MACs) and Zen-score by 1.4% at comparable or lower search cost. Against high-cost NAS methods, DMS improves over EfficientNet-B0/B1/B2 by 1.4%/0.9%/1.0% respectively while reducing search cost by over 100× (0.4 GPU days vs. 1714 TPU days), and outperforms ScaleNet by 0.5% on EN-B0 with less than 1/50 the cost. In pruning comparisons on ResNet-50 at 1.6G MACs, DMS achieves 75.53% top-1, surpassing LFPC (74.46%), GReg2 (74.93%), CC (74.54%), and TPP (75.12%). For LLM pruning, DMS-Llama-7B (5.47B params) achieves 17.13 WikiText2 perplexity vs. 17.39 for LLMPruner, and higher zero-shot accuracy on all four classification benchmarks (e.g., 75.23% vs. 66.79% on BoolQ, 71.46% vs. 64.06% on ARC-e).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground within gradient-based NAS by introducing a fully differentiable topk operator that directly models structural hyperparameters (width/depth) with a single learnable pruning ratio per dimension. It departs from three prior gradient-based strategies—multiple element selection, single number selection, and gradient-estimate topk—by showing that the key obstacle to direct differentiable modeling is uneven importance distribution, which is resolved via a rank-based normalization step. The operator is conceptually distinct from prior differentiable topk work (Xie et al. 2020; Petersen et al. 2022), which made element selection differentiable for a fixed k, whereas here k itself is the differentiable quantity. The broader DMS pipeline draws on standard pruning/NAS infrastructure (Taylor importance, resource-constraint losses, supernet-then-retrain) but eliminates the expensive pretraining stage by showing that a larger randomly-initialized supernet is more efficient than a smaller pretrained one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving comparable or slightly better accuracy than SOTA NAS methods (EfficientNet, ScaleNet, ModelAmplification) at 100–500× lower search cost (0.4 GPU days vs. 1714 TPU days) makes automated model scaling feasible for large models where prior methods' public costs become prohibitive. The method's generality across CNNs, Transformers, and tasks (classification, detection, language modeling) suggests broad applicability. Open questions include whether the approach scales to searching LLMs from scratch (the Llama experiment only prunes a pretrained model), whether the fine-grained search space remains tractable for very large supernets, and whether the single-pruning-ratio-per-dimension abstraction is sufficient for more complex structural decisions (e.g., kernel size, resolution).
