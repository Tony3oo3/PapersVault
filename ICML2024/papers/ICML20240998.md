## Problem

Full-rank covariance variational families in black-box variational inference (BBVI) suffer from an O(dκ²ε⁻¹) iteration complexity driven by gradient variance, which becomes O(N⁴) in sample complexity for hierarchical Bayesian models with local variables where dimensionality d scales linearly with dataset size N. Mean-field families are scalable but too crude to capture posterior correlations. The paper asks whether structured scale matrices can achieve a provable middle ground between these two extremes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240998/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a Bayesian model with a finite-sum joint likelihood ℓ(z) = Σₙ ℓₙ(z) over N datapoints, a location-scale variational family parameterized by a mean vector m ∈ ℝᵈ and a lower-triangular Cholesky scale matrix C ∈ ℝ^{d×d}, and M Monte Carlo samples per gradient step. It produces a posterior approximation q_λ(z) and, critically, a provable iteration complexity bound of O(N) (vs. O(N²) for full-rank) for hierarchical models. The scale matrix C is constrained to a bordered block-diagonal structure, reducing storage from Θ((d_y + d_z N)²) to Θ((d_z d_y + d_y²)N).

## Method

The key insight is that the gradient variance in BBVI depends not on the total dimension d but on an 'effective dimensionality' d* = max_n Σ_j δ_{n,j}, where δ_{n,j} indicates whether the j-th column of the sub-matrix C_n is non-zero. By designing C_n to have non-zero columns only for the global variable z and the local variable y_n (but not other y_m's), the paper achieves d* = d_z + d_y, independent of N. This bordered block-diagonal Cholesky structure implicitly defines a 'hierarchical branched distribution' q(z, y₁,…,y_N) = q(z) ∏ₙ q(y_n|z), where local variables are conditionally independent given the global variable. The paper further proves that the 'standardized' parameterization (where z is standardized before interacting with y_n) preserves ELBO convexity under log-concave posteriors, while the 'non-standardized' parameterization does not, making the former strictly preferable for optimization. The convergence analysis builds on proximal SGD with the reparameterization gradient, yielding an O(N) iteration complexity and O(N²) sample complexity for the structured family.

## Results

The paper's headline result is theoretical: the bordered block-diagonal structured scale matrix achieves an iteration complexity of O(N) (sample complexity O(N³)) for hierarchical models with local variables, compared to O(N²) (sample complexity O(N⁴)) for full-rank families. In synthetic experiments, full-rank exhibits quadratic scaling of required iterations with dataset size N, while structured and mean-field both exhibit linear scaling (Fig. 3). On realistic hierarchical Bayesian models (rpoisson, volatility, IRT) at dataset sizes up to ~33K datapoints, the structured family achieves ELBO values comparable to mean-field at fixed stepsize, while full-rank is significantly more sensitive to stepsize choice and degrades faster as N grows (Fig. 4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three variational families: full-rank (Ranganath et al., 2014), mean-field/diagonal (Hinton & van Camp, 1993), and the proposed structured (bordered block-diagonal) family. Against full-rank, the structured family clearly wins on scalability: in synthetic experiments, full-rank requires quadratically more iterations as N increases, while structured scales linearly (matching mean-field). Full-rank is also far more sensitive to stepsize, with a much narrower range of stepsizes achieving the target accuracy, and this range shrinks rapidly with N. Against mean-field, the structured family performs nearly identically in terms of iteration count and stepsize robustness, but is strictly more expressive since it models correlations between global variables z and local variables y_n (i.e., it approximates the posterior as q(z)∏q(y_n|z) rather than q(z)∏q(y_n)). The structured family thus achieves the statistical expressiveness of full-rank (capturing global-local correlations) at the computational cost of mean-field, which is the paper's central practical claim.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the computational complexity framework for BBVI established by Domke et al. (2023) and Kim et al. (2023a), which showed that full-rank variational families suffer from O(dκ²ε⁻¹) iteration complexity due to gradient variance. The bordered block-diagonal scale matrix structure analyzed here was previously proposed by Tan (2021) and Tan et al. (2020) for practical use, but without any complexity analysis; this paper provides the first rigorous proof that this structure reduces the dataset-size dependence from O(N³) to O(N²) for hierarchical models. The convergence analysis leverages the general proximal SGD theory of Gorbunov et al. (2020) and Garrigos & Gower (2023), combined with the proximal operator and ELBO convexity results of Domke (2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first theoretical justification for why structured variational families outperform full-rank ones in BBVI, resolving a long-standing empirical observation that lacked rigorous explanation. The key insight—that the sparsity pattern of the scale matrix (not the parameter count) governs gradient variance and hence complexity—gives practitioners a principled design criterion for choosing variational families in hierarchical models. Open questions remain: the O(N³) sample complexity bound is likely pessimistic (the authors conjecture O(N²)), the κ² dependence is believed to be loose, and the analysis is restricted to location-scale families with Cholesky parameterization. The non-standardized parameterization result (Theorem 5) has practical implications, showing that a seemingly more flexible parameterization actually breaks ELBO convexity and slows convergence.
