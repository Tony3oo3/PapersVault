## Problem

Existing retrosynthetic planning strategies generate routes via locally-normalized, step-by-step probability maximization that cannot incorporate route-level criteria such as starting material feasibility, cost, or step count. This greedy, myopic generation suffers from exposure bias and lacks the forward-looking evaluation needed to produce high-quality synthetic routes, and no principled mechanism exists to steer generation toward specific criteria in a model-agnostic way.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241247/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target molecule and a set of candidate synthetic routes sampled from any base strategy (e.g., Neuralsym + Retro*-0) as input. The energy function E_θ is a standard Transformer where the target molecule feeds the encoder and the starting material set (right-shifted) feeds the decoder, producing a scalar energy score per route. At inference, the framework re-ranks the sampled routes by minimizing −log P_Retro(T|m_tar) + E_θ(T|m_tar, c) and returns the single optimal route or a ranked list. The reward function φ used during training combines Tanimoto similarity between a forward-model-predicted product and the target molecule with Tanimoto similarity between predicted and reference starting material sets.

## Method

The paper reformulates retrosynthetic planning as a locally-normalized conditional generation task (analogous to autoregressive LLMs) and proposes a conditional residual EBM that layers an energy function E_θ(T|m_tar, c) on top of any frozen base strategy P_Retro, yielding P_θ(T|m_tar, c) ∝ P_Retro(T|m_tar)·exp(−E_θ(T|m_tar, c)). Because ground-truth routes are unavailable and the normalization constant is intractable, training uses a Bradley-Terry preference-based loss (inspired by RLHF alignment in LLMs) on pairs of routes ranked by a heuristic reward φ that measures forward-synthesis feasibility and starting-material similarity to a reference route. The energy function is a Transformer pretrained on the target-to-starting-material task, making it a lightweight post-training adapter that requires no modification to the base model. At inference, the trained E_θ re-scores all routes sampled by P_Retro in a plug-and-play fashion, consistently improving top-1 accuracy by 2.5% over the previous state-of-the-art across template-based, semi-template-based, and template-free models.

## Results

On RetroBench, CREBM consistently improves top-1 exact-match accuracy across all tested strategies when paired with Retro*-0 search. The headline result is Neuralsym+Retro*-0 achieving 44.5% top-1 accuracy (up from 42.0%, a +2.5% gain), which the authors report as state-of-the-art. Improvements range from +1.0% (GraphRetro: 15.3%→16.3%) to +3.7% (GLN: 39.5%→43.2%; Transformer: 31.2%→34.9%). Gains are more pronounced for longer synthetic routes, with GLN+CREBM showing a +14.5% improvement at depth 6.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CREBM is evaluated as a post-training adapter on five of the eight baselines (Neuralsym, GLN, GraphRetro, Transformer, FusionRetro) using Retro*-0 search. It wins on every tested baseline: Neuralsym improves from 42.0% to 44.5% top-1 (+2.5%), GLN from 39.5% to 43.2% (+3.7%), Transformer from 31.2% to 34.9% (+3.7%), FusionRetro from 37.5% to 39.6% (+2.1%), and GraphRetro from 15.3% to 16.3% (+1.0%). The three baselines without a CREBM variant (Retrosim at 35.0%, G2Gs at 4.2%, Megan at 19.5%) serve as unmodified references; notably, G2Gs and Megan perform substantially worse than all other strategies, highlighting the gap between semi-template and template-free approaches. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that the energy function contributes positively: removing it drops top-1 from 44.5% to 42.0%, while using it alone (without the base probability) drops to 34.1%, and subtracting it causes a catastrophic drop to 19.2%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the residual energy-based model framework from Deng et al. (2020) for text generation to the domain of retrosynthetic planning, reformulating route quality improvement as a conditional residual EBM problem. The preference-based training objective is borrowed from the Bradley-Terry model and the broader LLM alignment literature (RLHF, DPO), with the key conceptual bridge being the observation that retrosynthetic planning is a locally normalized conditional generation task analogous to autoregressive text generation. Unlike prior work that modifies search algorithms or retrosynthesis models directly, CREBM operates as a frozen post-training adapter, leaving the base strategy untouched.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of CREBM means a single trained energy function can improve any combination of retrosynthesis model and search algorithm without retraining, which is practically valuable given the diversity of existing strategies. However, the current implementation only addresses route feasibility as a criterion, leaving cost, yield, and environmental impact unexplored; the authors acknowledge that multi-criteria compositional CREBMs are future work. The modest 2.5% top-1 improvement, while consistent, raises the question of whether the framework's gains are bounded by the quality of the base strategy's candidate pool, and whether the approach transfers to domains where ground-truth routes are entirely unavailable.
