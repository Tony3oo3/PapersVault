## Problem

SDS-based text-to-3D generation produces high-fidelity textures but suffers from severe geometric inconsistencies (e.g., Janus artifacts, implausible shapes) due to the absence of 3D structural priors. Existing remedies—fine-tuning 2D diffusion models on 3D datasets like Objaverse—constrain the output to the limited style and quality of available 3D assets, producing cartoonish or clay-like textures. The paper asks whether 3D geometric information can be injected at test time without full fine-tuning, preserving the expressiveness of the pre-trained 2D prior.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt and retrieves N=3 semantically nearest 3D assets from Objaverse via CLIP text and image embeddings (using ScaNN, <3 s). Retrieved assets are rendered at 100 uniform camera poses and used for both particle initialization and a ~7-minute LoRA adaptation that runs in parallel with the main SDS optimization. The output is a photorealistic 3D scene represented as an Instant-NGP NeRF, optimized over 20,000 iterations on a single RTX A6000 (~6 hours total).

## Method

ReDream extends ProlificDreamer's Variational Score Distillation (VSD) particle framework with two retrieval-augmented mechanisms. First, retrieved 3D assets initialize the particle-based variational distribution: during a warm-up phase, an auxiliary velocity v_asset pulls each particle toward its assigned asset via a rendered-image L2 gradient, derived from a retrieval-integrated prior p_{φ,ξ}(θ|c) ∝ p_φ(θ|c)·p_ξ(θ|c) that acts as an implicit geometric filter. Second, a lightweight LoRA adapter is trained on dense renderings of the retrieved assets concatenated with learnable view-prefix tokens (e.g., 'front view', 'side view'), which rebalances the 2D prior's probability density across viewpoints and mitigates the chronic view-bias that causes Janus-like artifacts. A delta-denoising-score regularization further prevents particles from drifting away from their geometric initialization. The approach requires no full fine-tuning of the diffusion model and adds negligible overhead beyond the parallel LoRA adaptation.

## Results

ReDream achieves the best CLIP-Score of 0.274 (CLIP ViT-L/14) and 0.227 (OpenCLIP ViT-L/14) across 50 prompts rendered at 120 viewpoints, outperforming MVDream (0.263/0.217), DreamFusion (0.242/0.185), and ProlificDreamer (0.218/0.204). On view consistency (A-LPIPS, lower is better), ReDream scores 0.041 (VGG) and 0.054 (AlexNet), substantially better than MVDream (0.062/0.072), DreamFusion (0.075/0.076), and ProlificDreamer (0.227/0.135). In a user study with 92 participants, 75.3% preferred ReDream's outputs over the ProlificDreamer baseline in terms of geometry and textural fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReDream outperforms all four quantitative baselines on every reported metric. Against ProlificDreamer (its direct baseline and the method it builds upon), ReDream improves CLIP-Score by 0.056 (0.274 vs 0.218) and reduces A-LPIPS by over 5× (0.041 vs 0.227 on VGG), indicating dramatically fewer geometric artifacts. Against MVDream, the strongest multi-view diffusion baseline, ReDream leads by 0.011 in CLIP-Score and by 0.021 in A-LPIPS (VGG), showing that retrieval-augmented score distillation surpasses full fine-tuning on 3D data for both fidelity and consistency. DreamFusion, the original SDS method, is outperformed on all metrics, with the largest gap in A-LPIPS (0.041 vs 0.075). Qualitatively, ReDream also outperforms image-to-3D methods Zero123 and Magic123 (shown in Fig. 9), which produce plausible geometry but suffer from degraded, overly smoothed textures, whereas ReDream retains high-fidelity textures from the 2D prior while achieving superior geometric consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Variational Score Distillation (VSD) framework of ProlificDreamer by introducing a retrieval-augmented mechanism that leverages semantically relevant 3D assets from Objaverse as geometric priors, rather than relying solely on the 2D diffusion prior. It departs from the fine-tuning-based approaches (MVDream, Zero123) that sacrifice 2D fidelity to gain 3D awareness, and from prior retrieval-augmented generation work (ReImagen, KNN-Diffusion) by applying retrieval specifically within the score distillation optimization loop. The lightweight LoRA adaptation of the 2D prior using retrieved asset renderings, combined with the delta denoising score regularization, positions the method as a test-time augmentation strategy that avoids full model retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core tension in text-to-3D generation: 2D diffusion priors produce high-fidelity textures but geometrically inconsistent 3D, while 3D-data fine-tuning fixes geometry at the cost of 2D quality. The retrieval-augmented approach offers a practical middle ground with negligible training overhead, and the substantial A-LPIPS improvement (0.041 vs. 0.227 for ProlificDreamer) suggests view consistency can be largely resolved without domain-specific training. However, the method still requires ~6 hours per generation, depends on access to a large 3D database, and is bounded by the quality of the underlying 2D prior (Stable Diffusion v2). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more complex multi-object scenes, whether faster 3D representations (e.g., Gaussian splatting) can reduce inference time, and how retrieval quality degrades for highly creative or compositional prompts.
