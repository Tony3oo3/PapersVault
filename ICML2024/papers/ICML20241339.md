## Problem

In multivariate time series forecasting (MTSF), univariate models that ignore inter-series relationships consistently outperform complex multivariate architectures, suggesting that existing models lack the proper structure to capture inter-series dependencies without disrupting temporal dynamics. This paper addresses the gap by asking how to effectively incorporate inter-series information into a predictor without altering its architecture, even when the predictor is a structurally simple univariate model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241339/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series history X_I ∈ R^{L_I×C} (a fixed lookback window of L_I=720 steps across C channels) and produces a forecast X_P ∈ R^{L_P×C} for the forecasting horizon. A key design choice is that temporal sparsity dynamically truncates each channel's input to a data-dependent length, so the effective lookback varies per series rather than being a single global hyperparameter. The ATS are generated entirely from the OTS input and reside in the same temporal domain, serving as exogenous variables that are jointly predicted with the OTS before being projected back into the C-dimensional output space.

## Method

CATS constructs Auxiliary Time Series (ATS) from the Original Time Series (OTS) using a bank of diverse constructors (1D convolutions at multiple kernel sizes, non-overlapping convolutions, independent convolutions, linear projections, identity copies, and learnable embeddings), then feeds both ATS and OTS through a simple 2-layer MLP predictor that maps the combined N+C channels from the input domain to the forecasting horizon. A linear projection then maps the N+C output channels back to C dimensions, and the result is added as a residual to the OTS shortcut (the univariate prediction), yielding the final forecast. Three principles govern ATS construction: continuity (a low-pass filtering loss that smooths ATS to capture inter-series trends while leaving intra-series details to the OTS), sparsity (channel attention scores that suppress unimportant ATS channels, plus per-channel temporal cutoff via a straight-through estimator), and variability (diverse constructor types to capture different dependency patterns). This structure functions as an adaptive 2D temporal-contextual attention mechanism, and the OTS shortcut guarantees that CATS performs at least as well as the base univariate predictor.

## Results

On 9 standard MTSF benchmarks (ETTm1/m2, ETTh1/h2, Electricity, Weather, Traffic, Exchange, Multi) across 4 forecasting horizons (L_P ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720}), CATS with a simple 2-layer MLP predictor achieves an average MSE of 43.9% relative to the naive Repeat baseline, with an average rank of 1.25 and 28 out of 36 first-place finishes. On the Multi dataset (strong inter-series dependencies), CATS achieves 59.6% MSE vs. 64.9% for ARM and 114.7% for PatchTST. On the extended MultiX datasets (20–200 series), CATS maintains 55.7–62.9% MSE while Transformer-based baselines degrade to 200–409%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CATS(2L) outperforms all six learned baselines on 8 of 9 datasets in average MSE, winning 28 of 36 individual (dataset × horizon) sub-experiments. The closest competitor is ARM (the authors' prior work), which CATS beats on 7/9 datasets but loses to on Exchange (73.0% vs. 70.2% MSE relative to Repeat). Against univariate baselines, CATS shows its largest margins on Multi (59.6% vs. 114.7% for PatchTST, 84.1% for DLinear) and ETTh2 (58.5% vs. 61.7% for PatchTST, 80.4% for DLinear), confirming that the ATS mechanism specifically targets inter-series relationships that univariate models ignore. Transformer-based baselines (Autoformer, Informer, FedFormer) are consistently 1.5–5× worse in MSE, with Informer particularly poor on ETTh2 (615.9%) and ETTm2 (366.5%). Notably, CATS achieves these results with only 2.47M parameters and 382M FLOPs (L_P=96), compared to 7.89M/426M for ARM, 15.5M/10.9G for Autoformer, and 11.3M/9.41G for Informer, demonstrating a ~3–28× reduction in computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

CATS carves new ground by reframing the multivariate forecasting problem: rather than modifying the predictor architecture to handle inter-series dependencies (as Crossformer, ARM, or iTransformer attempt), it constructs auxiliary time series that act as exogenous variables, allowing even a 2-layer MLP to capture 2D temporal-contextual relationships. The channel sparsity mechanism borrows from SE-Net-style channel attention in computer vision, while the temporal sparsity module addresses the well-known sensitivity of MTSF models to lookback window length. The paper explicitly positions against the counter-intuitive finding that univariate models (DLinear, PatchTST) outperform complex multivariate transformers, arguing that prior architectures lack proper structure for inter-series modeling rather than that such relationships are absent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CATS offers a lightweight, transferable solution that significantly reduces computational cost (e.g., 382M FLOPs vs. 10.9G for Autoformer at L_P=96) while matching or exceeding SOTA on standard benchmarks, making multivariate forecasting more accessible. The introduction of MultiX datasets addresses a critical gap in benchmarking: most standard MTSF datasets have weak inter-series dependencies, masking the true capability of multivariate models. Key limitations include the fixed number of ATS (no automatic expansion during training) and the observation that gains on standard datasets like ETT are modest, with the most dramatic improvements appearing on the authors' own Multi/MultiX benchmarks. Future work on scaling ATS construction with Mixture of Experts and extending to NLP/audio suggests broader applicability beyond time series.
