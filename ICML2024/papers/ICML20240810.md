## Problem

Existing Byzantine-robust aggregation methods in federated learning rely on either homogeneous (IID) data distributions or a semantically related proxy dataset with label annotations, making them ineffective under realistic non-IID heterogeneity or impractical when labeled proxy data is unavailable. This paper asks whether a completely random, unlabeled public dataset—sharing no label space with the local data—can suffice for Byzantine-robust aggregation in heterogeneous federated learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240810/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes local CNN model updates from K clients (trained on private non-IID image data via FedProx) and a random public dataset of unlabeled images (e.g., Tiny-ImageNet or Market1501) that is semantically unrelated to the local task. It produces a Byzantine-robust aggregated global model and a set of learnable per-client aggregation weights that suppress malicious contributions. The public dataset requires no labels and no domain alignment with the federated task, which is the key practical distinction from proxy-dataset baselines like FLTrust or Sageflow.

## Method

SDEA introduces a learnable aggregation weight vector M (softmax-normalized) that is optimized to minimize a composite loss on the random public data: Instance Sharpness (IS) minimizes per-instance prediction entropy to exploit the observation that benign clients produce over-confident (low-entropy) logits while Byzantine attackers produce high-entropy outputs, and Class Diversity (CD) maximizes the entropy of the batch-averaged prediction distribution to prevent the weight from collapsing onto a few low-entropy clients under data heterogeneity. A Cooperative Cluster (CC) module then applies FINCH (parameter-free first-neighbor clustering) to partition the learned weights into two groups, identifies the group with the larger mean as benign, and equally redistributes the benign group's total weight among honest clients to counteract the Matthew Effect. The weight M is updated for 20 Adam steps (lr=0.005) per communication round on batches of 64 public images, after which the final global model is computed as the weighted sum of local updates.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (β=0.5, Φ=0.2), SDEA achieves top-1 accuracy of 67.68% (PairF), 65.82% (SymF), 69.21% (RanN), 68.32% (LIE), 67.47% (MiMa), and 67.80% (MiSu), outperforming the best baseline by up to 4.49% (RanN). Under the more severe Φ=0.4 setting, SDEA maintains 62–65% accuracy while most distance-based and statistics-based baselines (Multi Krum, Bulyan, RFA, FoolsGold) collapse to near-random (~10%). On MNIST, SDEA reaches 99.2–99.3% accuracy across all attacks and heterogeneity levels, matching or slightly exceeding proxy-dataset methods (FLTrust, Sageflow) that require semantically aligned labeled data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDEA outperforms all six distance/statistics baselines on CIFAR-10 across every attack and heterogeneity setting. DnC is the closest competitor, achieving 64.2–65.6% at Φ=0.2, β=0.5, but SDEA leads by 2–5 points (e.g., 69.21 vs. 64.72 on RanN). RFA is competitive at Φ=0.2 (67.12 on PairF) but collapses to 12–24% at Φ=0.4, while SDEA retains 61–65%. Multi Krum, Bulyan, and FoolsGold are highly unstable: Multi Krum and Bulyan drop to ~10% (random chance) under Φ=0.4 for most parameter-based attacks, and FoolsGold shows extreme variance (38–64% at Φ=0.2). On MNIST (Table 5), SDEA (99.2–99.3%) matches or slightly exceeds FLTrust and Sageflow, which require semantically aligned proxy datasets (USPS, SVHN, SYN) and degrade sharply under large domain shift (e.g., Sageflow drops from 96.70% on SVHN to 89.51% on SYN for SymF). On Fashion-MNIST (Table 8), SDEA achieves 87.7–88.5% at Φ=0.2 and 86.8–88.5% at Φ=0.4, consistently outperforming all baselines by 1–15 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDEA departs from the three established families of Byzantine-robust aggregation—distance-based (Multi Krum, FoolsGold, DnC), statistics-distribution (Trim Median, Bulyan, RFA), and proxy-dataset (FLTrust, Sageflow)—by eliminating their reliance on data homogeneity or a semantically consistent labeled proxy. Conceptually, it repurposes the entropy-minimization principle from test-time adaptation (TTA) not to adapt model parameters to a target domain, but to optimize a learnable aggregation weight that separates benign from malicious clients on unlabeled random public data. The Cooperative Cluster module builds on FINCH (Sarfraz et al., CVPR 2019) for parameter-free grouping of aggregation weights, and the overall framework is orthogonal to and composable with existing heterogeneity-mitigation methods (FedProx, SCAFFOLD, prototype-based approaches).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in removing the need for a labeled, domain-matched proxy dataset—a costly prerequisite in medical imaging, fraud detection, and other sensitive domains—while still achieving Byzantine-robust aggregation under non-IID data. However, the method is explicitly limited to untargeted Byzantine attacks (performance degradation) and cannot handle targeted backdoor attacks where malicious clients may produce equally confident logits. Open questions include whether the equal-weight allocation in Cooperative Cluster can be refined toward a truly optimal distribution, whether the approach extends to asynchronous or partial-participation settings, and how sensitive the method is to the quality and domain of the random public dataset beyond the tested cases.
