## Problem

Despite widespread empirical evidence that multimodal networks exhibit unimodal bias—over-relying on one modality while ignoring others during joint training—there was no theoretical framework explaining how network architecture, dataset statistics, and initialization jointly determine the duration and consequences of this bias. Existing theoretical work was restricted to two-layer late fusion networks with whitened inputs, leaving the influence of fusion depth, cross-modal correlations, and non-whitened data unexplored.

## Input / Output

The theoretical analysis takes two input modalities as vectors with arbitrary (non-whitened) correlation matrices, a network of total depth L with fusion at layer L_f, and a small initialization scale u_0, and produces a closed-form time ratio t_B/t_A quantifying the unimodal phase duration. For empirical validation, the method is applied to scalar and 50-dimensional regression tasks with synthetic data, as well as a noisy MNIST digit classification task where two digit images (one potentially corrupted by Gaussian noise) serve as the two modalities. In the classification setting the output is a 10-class label, and the key measured quantity is the ratio of times at which each modality's unimodal accuracy reaches 50%.

## Method

The paper performs a fixed-point and saddle-point analysis of gradient descent dynamics in multimodal deep linear networks with non-whitened input, revealing that late and intermediate fusion networks possess two additional saddle manifolds (corresponding to unimodal solutions) absent in early fusion networks, forcing learning trajectories to plateau near a unimodal solution. By exploiting balancing properties and a rank-one ansatz for the weight matrices, they reduce the high-dimensional dynamics to a low-dimensional system and derive a closed-form expression for the time ratio t_B/t_A as a function of input-output correlation norms, cross-modal correlation, fusion depth L_f, total depth L, and initialization scale. The key architectural insight is that deeper fusion layers prolong the unimodal phase because fewer shared post-fusion layers remain to help the weaker modality escape its zero fixed point. In the overparameterized regime, they show that the long unimodal phase creates a dilemma: early stopping yields permanent unimodal bias, while continued training overfits the first modality, both resulting in generalization deficits relative to early fusion or unimodal baselines.

## Results

The paper derives a closed-form expression for the time ratio t_B/t_A (Equation 9) that quantifies the duration of the unimodal phase in two-layer late fusion linear networks, showing it increases with input modality correlation and disparity in input-output correlations. For deep networks (Equation 12), the time ratio additionally grows with fusion layer depth L_f and initialization scale. On the noisy MNIST classification task, the time ratio for MLPs increases from 1.30±0.24 (early fusion, L_f=1) to 6.38±1.12 (late fusion, L_f=5), and for CNNs from 1.04±0.03 to 2.53±0.45, confirming that deeper fusion produces longer unimodal phases. In the overparameterized regime, late fusion networks exhibit a generalization deficit where the first-learned modality overfits during the unimodal plateau, yielding permanent unimodal bias even with optimal early stopping.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not compare against external methods; instead, it compares architectural choices and validates analytical predictions against simulations. Early fusion (L_f=1) networks learn both modalities nearly simultaneously with no unimodal phase, while late fusion (L_f=L) and intermediate fusion (1<L_f<L) networks exhibit a clear unimodal plateau whose duration grows with fusion depth. The theoretical time ratio predictions (Equations 9 and 12) closely match numerical simulations of both linear and ReLU networks, with ReLU networks showing qualitatively identical dynamics but ~2× slower learning. On the noisy MNIST task, the qualitative trend (deeper fusion → longer unimodal phase) holds for both MLPs and CNNs, though CNNs show a milder effect (max ratio 2.53 vs. 6.38 for MLPs). The paper also shows that in the overparameterized regime, late fusion networks suffer a generalization deficit relative to early fusion networks and even unimodal baselines, due to overfitting the first-learned modality during the unimodal plateau.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the exact-solution framework for deep linear networks (Saxe et al., 2014; Atanasov et al., 2022) to the multimodal setting, where networks are not fully connected and inputs are not whitened—two assumptions that prior closed-form solutions relied on. It also generalizes the modality competition analysis of Huang et al. (2021, 2022), which was restricted to two-layer late fusion, to arbitrary depth and three fusion schemes (early, intermediate, late). The balancing property (Du et al., 2018) is adapted to the multimodal architecture, where it holds separately within each pre-fusion branch and within the shared post-fusion layers, yielding a three-variable dynamical system rather than the single-variable system of standard linear networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical calculation of the unimodal phase duration as a function of fusion depth, dataset correlation structure, and initialization scale, providing a principled explanation for why late and intermediate fusion architectures suffer from unimodal bias while early fusion does not. The finding that overfitting during the unimodal phase can convert a transient learning artifact into permanent bias with a generalization deficit has direct implications for architecture design and training protocols in multimodal systems. Limitations include the restriction to two modalities, linear (or linear-target) tasks, and small initialization; extending the analysis to more modalities, nonlinear tasks, and larger initializations remains open. The paper also raises the practical question of how to optimally trade off unimodal bias alleviation (favors early fusion) against heterogeneous feature extraction (favors late fusion).
