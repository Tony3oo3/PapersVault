## Problem

LLM-based formal theorem proving (e.g., DSP) relies on informal proofs to guide autoformalization, but these proofs are poorly aligned with the discrete search space of automated provers, and the selection and ordering of in-context demonstration examples—critical given the limited context window—remains an unsolved NP-complete combinatorial problem. No prior work jointly addresses both the structural format of the guiding proof and the optimal organization of demonstrations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242517/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a formal theorem statement in Isabelle/HOL and a pool of N=61 demonstration examples (each comprising a statement, a subgoal-based proof, and a formal sketch) as input. Due to a 3072-token context limit, only ~4.79 examples can be included per prompt, making subset selection and ordering critical. The output is a formal sketch in Isabelle syntax verifiable by Sledgehammer, plus a demonstration organization (a specific subset and sequence of examples) predicted by the diffusion model conditioned on the theorem statement.

## Method

The framework has two components. First, subgoal-based proof construction iteratively refines informal proofs into sequences of subgoals (s₀, s₁, …, s_{Δ+1}) where each subgoal is verified as reachable from the initial state and able to reach the final state via recursive LLM + Isabelle calls (a self-play-style refinement over 15 iterations). Second, demonstration reorganization formulates the joint subset-selection and ordering problem as finding a (Sub)hamiltonian path on a graph whose nodes are demonstration examples, and solves it with a discrete diffusion model (following Austin et al.) parameterized by a 3-layer anisotropic GNN with edge gating. Training data consists of 137 successful proof organizations collected via randomized sampling; at inference, the diffusion model denoises from a random graph to predict the optimal organization, and 200 candidate solutions are scored to select the best one. This achieves 45.5% pass rate on miniF2F-test (vs. 38.9% for DSP) and a 5× reduction in LLM calls to match prior SOTA.

## Results

On the miniF2F-test set, the proposed subgoal-based demonstration learning framework achieves a pass rate of 45.5%, a 6.6% absolute (17.0% relative) improvement over the previous state-of-the-art DSP method (38.9%). On miniF2F-valid, the method reaches 48.0% cumulative pass rate. Additionally, the diffusion-based demonstration reorganization yields a 5× improvement in sampling efficiency, matching DSP's 38.5% pass rate with only 20 LLM calls instead of 100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On miniF2F-test, the proposed method (45.5%) ties with LEGO-Prover (45.5%) and outperforms Lyra (44.2%) and DSP (38.9%), the strongest prior LLM-based baseline. The gap over DSP is 6.6% absolute, attributed to both subgoal-based proofs and diffusion-based demonstration reorganization. On miniF2F-valid, the method (48.0% cumulative) trails the concurrent LEGO-Prover (52.4%) and Lyra (52.8%), which employ complementary strategies (incremental theorem libraries and error-feedback verification, respectively). Search-based methods (Thor at 29.9%, Thor+expert iteration at 35.2%) underperform all LLM-based approaches due to the enormous action space in theorem proving. The ablation confirms both components contribute: removing subgoal-based proof drops test performance to 40.6%, and removing diffusion drops it to 44.1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DSP (Draft, Sketch, Prove) framework by replacing informal proofs with subgoal-based proofs inspired by subgoal learning in reinforcement learning (Zhang et al., 2021; Eysenbach et al., 2019), and by introducing a discrete diffusion model on graphs (following Austin et al., 2021 and Sun & Yang, 2023) to simultaneously solve the subset selection and ordering of in-context demonstrations. Prior work on in-context learning treated example selection (Liu et al., 2021) and ordering (Lu et al., 2021) as separate problems; this paper unifies them into a single (Sub)hamiltonian path formulation addressed via diffusion. The iterative subgoal refinement process, which alternates between LLM generation and ITP verification, draws on self-play ideas from RL (Silver et al., 2016) but applies them to proof structure rather than policy improvement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5× sampling efficiency gain (achieving DSP-level 38.5% pass rate with only 20 LLM calls instead of 100) is practically significant for reducing inference costs in formal theorem proving pipelines. However, the evaluation is confined to miniF2F (488 problems) with GPT-3.5-turbo and Isabelle, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to harder theorem collections, other ITPs (Lean, Coq), or more capable LLMs. The paper notes that LEGO-Prover and Lyra address orthogonal aspects (reusable theorem libraries, error-feedback verification), suggesting that combining these complementary techniques could yield further gains. A key limitation is that the diffusion model is trained on only 137 successful proof organizations, raising questions about scalability to larger demonstration pools.
