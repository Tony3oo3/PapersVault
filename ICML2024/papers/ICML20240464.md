## Problem

Existing realistic long-tailed semi-supervised learning (ReaLTSSL) methods, such as ACR and CPE, rely on predefined anchor distributions for unlabeled data, which limits their applicability when the true unlabeled distribution is unknown or falls outside the assumed set. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a framework that requires no prior assumptions about the unlabeled class distribution while still handling the mismatch between labeled and unlabeled data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240464/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of labeled RGB images (e.g., 500 for CIFAR-10-LT, 50 for CIFAR-100-LT) following a long-tailed class distribution, alongside a much larger pool of unlabeled images (e.g., 4000 for CIFAR-10-LT, 100k for STL-10-LT) whose class distribution is entirely unknown and may differ from the labeled distribution. Images are at 32×32 (CIFAR/STL-10) or 64×64 (ImageNet-127) resolution. The output is a per-image class label, evaluated on a balanced test set to avoid rewarding biased classifiers.

## Method

SimPro reformulates pseudo-label-based semi-supervised learning as an extended EM algorithm that explicitly decouples the conditional distribution P(x|y; θ) from the marginal class distribution P(y; π). In the M-step, the marginal distribution π admits a closed-form solution (the mean of soft pseudo-labels over unlabeled data), while the network parameters θ are optimized via SGD using a logit-adjusted cross-entropy loss that incorporates an overall frequency vector φ. In the E-step, a Bayes classifier combining the estimated π with the network logits generates high-confidence pseudo-labels for unlabeled samples, which feed back into the next M-step. The entire framework builds on FixMatch with only a handful of code changes: one line to compute the closed-form π, four lines to construct the Bayes classifier, and a scaling factor α to balance labeled/unlabeled losses when M ≫ N.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-LT (γ_l=150, N_1=500, M_1=4000), SimPro achieves top-1 accuracy of 74.2% (consistent), 93.6% (uniform), 83.5% (reversed), 82.6% (middle), and 81.0% (head-tail), consistently outperforming all baselines across all five unlabeled class distributions. On ImageNet-1k (γ_t=1, 64×64), SimPro reaches 25.0% top-1 accuracy, surpassing ACR (23.3%) by 1.7 points. On STL-10-LT (γ_l=10, N_1=450, M=100k), SimPro achieves 84.5%, outperforming ACR (83.0%) and DASO (78.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimPro outperforms all seven baselines (FixMatch, CReST+, DASO, ACR†, ACR, CPE, CoSSL) across every distribution and dataset tested. The largest margins appear on the two newly introduced distributions: on the middle distribution, SimPro beats ACR† by 8.8 points (82.6 vs. 73.8) and DASO by 13.6 points; on head-tail, it leads ACR† by 3.4 points (81.0 vs. 77.6). On the standard consistent distribution, SimPro gains 3.3 points over ACR† (74.2 vs. 70.9) and 4.1 over DASO. On the uniform distribution, SimPro edges out ACR† by 1.7 points (93.6 vs. 91.9), while on reversed it leads by a narrower 0.3 points (83.5 vs. 83.2). Notably, CReST+ degrades below FixMatch on the middle distribution (58.5 vs. 64.3), highlighting its reliance on distribution-consistency assumptions that SimPro avoids entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

SimPro extends the FixMatch pseudo-labeling framework (Sohn et al., 2020) and the classical EM algorithm (Dempster et al., 1977) into the realistic long-tailed semi-supervised learning (ReaLTSSL) setting, incorporating logit adjustment (Menon et al., 2021) for long-tail handling. It departs from prior ReaLTSSL methods—ACR (Wei & Gan, 2023) and CPE (Ma et al., 2024)—which rely on predefined anchor distributions to handle unknown unlabeled class distributions, by instead deriving a closed-form solution for the marginal distribution within the EM M-step. This explicit decoupling of conditional and marginal distributions is the key conceptual shift: rather than assuming or approximating the unlabeled distribution through a finite set of anchors, SimPro estimates it directly from pseudo-labels, yielding a Bayes classifier that improves pseudo-label quality in the E-step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the anchor-distribution assumption, SimPro offers a more general and practically deployable solution for ReaLTSSL, where the true unlabeled distribution is genuinely unknown and may not match any pre-specified pattern. The theoretical guarantees (closed-form M-step solution, Bayes classifier derivation, regret bound) provide a principled foundation that prior heuristic or multi-branch approaches lack. The introduction of middle and head-tail distributions as evaluation scenarios exposes the limitations of anchor-based methods and broadens the field's testing protocol. Open questions include whether the shared conditional distribution assumption (Assumption 1) holds in more complex real-world settings (e.g., domain shift in P(x|y)), and whether the framework extends naturally to multi-modal or video data where the EM structure may be less straightforward.
