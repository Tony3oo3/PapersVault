## Problem

Existing debiased training algorithms have been evaluated only in single-bias settings, yet real-world data often exhibit multiple spurious correlations simultaneously. In multi-bias scenarios, mitigating one bias can exacerbate another, and the intersection of all bias-conflicting samples becomes vanishingly small, causing existing methods to underperform even simple baselines like upweighting. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a training method that handles multiple biases simultaneously and introducing a realistic natural-image benchmark for evaluation.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes RGB images (e.g., 112×112 facial crops from CelebA) along with annotated bias attribute labels (e.g., gender, age, mouth slightly open) used solely to assign each sample to one of 2^D groups based on whether each attribute is majority (guiding) or minority (conflicting) within its target class. At test time, only the image is required and the model outputs a single class label (e.g., high vs. low cheekbones). The grouping is class-agnostic—each group contains samples from all target classes—distinguishing it from prior group-robustness methods that partition by (target class, attribute) pairs.

## Method

The method has two core components: (1) a grouping strategy that assigns each training sample a binary vector g ∈ {0,1}^D indicating whether each bias attribute is the majority or minority value within its target class, yielding 2^D groups where all groups share the same classification task but differ in their spurious correlation profile; and (2) a multi-objective optimization training loop inspired by MGDA that minimizes a softmax-weighted combination of group-wise losses L_θ = σ(α)ᵀ L(θ) while simultaneously updating the group-scaling parameter α to minimize L_α = σ(α)ᵀ L(θ) + λ‖σ(α)ᵀ(∇_θ L(θ))_†‖²₂, with λ updated by gradient ascent. This dual objective drives the model toward the minimax Pareto solution—minimizing the worst group loss—by dynamically upweighting groups with low training loss (typically the tiny, easily-overfit minority groups like CC), thereby preventing the model from exploiting any single spurious correlation as a shortcut. The algorithm updates θ every iteration but updates α and λ only every U iterations, and uses group-balanced mini-batch sampling to compute each group's loss.

## Results

On MultiCelebA (two-bias setting), the proposed method achieves 82.9% UNBIASED accuracy and 82.6% CC accuracy, substantially outperforming ERM (63.8% UNBIASED, 19.0% CC) and the strongest baseline LISA (80.9% UNBIASED, 77.6% CC). On the three-bias setting, it reaches 78.4% UNBIASED and 70.0% CCC, again leading all baselines. The method also achieves the best WORST-group accuracy on Waterbirds (91.8%) and CelebA (89.8%), and the best UNBIASED on BFFHQ (85.7%), demonstrating effectiveness in both multi-bias and single-bias scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 10 baselines on the UNBIASED metric across all three multi-bias benchmarks (MultiCelebA, UrbanCars, Multi-Color MNIST), with the largest margin over ERM (+19.1% UNBIASED on MultiCelebA) and a consistent 2–7% lead over the strongest competitor LISA. Notably, several existing debiasing methods (JTT, DebiAN, DFR) actually perform worse than vanilla ERM on multi-bias settings, with DFR_tr dropping to 62.5% UNBIASED versus ERM's 63.8%, confirming the paper's hypothesis that mitigating one bias can exacerbate another. On single-bias datasets, the method achieves the best WORST-group accuracy on Waterbirds (91.8% vs. DFR's 90.2%) and CelebA (89.8%, tied with SSA), and the best UNBIASED on BFFHQ (85.7% vs. GroupDRO's 85.1%). The method also achieves the best CC accuracy on UrbanCars (87.6% vs. LISA's 80.8%), demonstrating a 6.8-point improvement in the hardest group. The only metric where the method does not lead is INDIST on MultiCelebA, where ERM's 97.0% reflects its exploitation of spurious correlations on the majority group.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Multi-Gradient Descent Algorithm (MGDA) from Désidéri (2012) to the debiased training setting, repurposing Pareto-stationary optimization to resolve conflicts between group-wise losses. It departs from the dominant single-bias debiasing literature (GroupDRO, LISA, DFR, SUBG) in two key ways: a novel grouping strategy that partitions data by binary guiding/conflicting labels per bias type (yielding 2^D groups rather than C×2^D), and a dynamic group-weight adjustment mechanism targeting the minimax Pareto solution rather than simply upweighting the worst group. The paper also extends the multi-bias debiasing line initiated by Li et al. (2022, 2023), which was limited to synthetic images, to natural-image benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that several sophisticated debiased training methods (JTT, DebiAN, DFR) perform at or below ERM in multi-bias settings, revealing that the 'whack-a-mole' problem of conflicting biases is a genuine failure mode not addressed by prior work. The introduction of MultiCelebA, a natural-image multi-bias benchmark, fills a critical gap left by synthetic-only alternatives. A key limitation is the requirement for bias attribute labels, and the authors acknowledge that unsupervised multi-bias debiasing remains open. Scalability beyond four-to-five bias types is also constrained by the combinatorial explosion of groups and the practical difficulty of identifying that many spuriously correlated attributes in natural datasets.
