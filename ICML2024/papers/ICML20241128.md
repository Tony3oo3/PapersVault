## Problem

Collider bias (T→S←Y) in observational data creates spurious correlations between treatment and outcome, making CATE unidentifiable without further assumptions. Existing methods address confounding bias or selection bias caused by T and X alone, but no prior work can resolve collider bias without additional assumptions about the observational data distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241128/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a large-scale collider-biased observational dataset D_obs (only S=1 samples, e.g., 10,000 units) and a small-scale representative dataset D_rep (randomly sampled from the target population with unknown S labels, e.g., 100–500 units). It outputs generated S=0 samples D_gen whose size is dynamically set to n_obs·n₀/n₁ (matching the S=0/S=1 ratio in D_rep), which when concatenated with D_obs recovers the target population distribution. The combined dataset is then fed into any downstream CATE estimator (BNN, CFR, DRCFR, etc.) in a plug-and-play fashion.

## Method

C²GAM reformulates collider bias as an out-of-distribution problem where S=1 (observed) is a seen environment and S=0 (unselected) is an unseen environment. It employs two coupled generators—G_d generates missing S=0 samples from Gaussian noise, and G_s predicts missing S labels for D_rep—and two discriminators: D_o aligns D_obs with D_rep samples labeled S=1 by G_s, while D_u aligns G_d's output with D_rep samples labeled S=0 by G_s. Training alternates between optimizing both discriminators (fixed generators) and both generators (fixed discriminators) via mini-batch gradient descent, with an additional Wasserstein distance constraint ensuring the combined D_obs∪D_gen distribution matches D_rep. The coupled design is critical: D_o anchors G_s to known S=1 data, and the ratio constraint on n_gen allows D_u to guide G_d toward the correct S=0 distribution without ground-truth S=0 samples.

## Results

On the three real-world benchmarks (IHDP, Twins, ACIC), C²GAM+DRCFR achieves the best √PEHE across all S=1 and S=0 splits: 1.064±0.120 / 0.979±0.282 on IHDP, 0.296±0.021 / 0.301±0.030 on Twins, and 1.675±0.299 / 1.876±0.410 on ACIC. On synthetic data (n_rep=500), C²GAM+CFR reaches 0.984±0.074 (S=1) and 1.083±0.086 (S=0), roughly halving the error of the best data-fusion baseline (CW: 1.867 / 2.581). The method also remains robust when the representative dataset shrinks to n_rep=100, where C²GAM+CFR still achieves 1.444±0.184 (S=1) versus 2.169±0.250 for the next-best scenario.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C²GAM consistently outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and S-split. Against the strongest data-fusion baselines, C²GAM+DRCFR reduces √PEHE by ~25% on IHDP (1.064 vs. CW's 1.427 on S=1) and ~21% on ACIC (1.675 vs. CW's 1.958 on S=1). On Twins, C²GAM+DRCFR (0.296/0.301) beats the best generative baseline GANITE (0.329/0.331) by ~10%, and surpasses the best representation-learning baseline DRCFR alone (0.388/0.396) by ~24%. Statistical methods (Doubly Robust, Causal Forest) and generative methods (CEVAE, TEDVAE) perform worst, with √PEHE values 2–5× higher than C²GAM, because they either accumulate collider bias through multiple models or amplify it by generating from the biased distribution. Representation-learning methods (BNN, TARNet, CFR, DRCFR, ES-CFR) without C²GAM show moderate performance since they address confounding but not collider bias, while data-fusion methods (IPSW, AIPSW, CW) improve over raw estimators but still fall short because they rely on correct model specifications for reweighting rather than directly recovering the target distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GAN framework (Goodfellow et al., 2014) but departs fundamentally from prior generative causal inference methods (CEVAE, GANITE, TEDVAE) that use generative models to produce counterfactual outcomes for confounding bias. It also extends the selection-bias literature (Bareinboim & Tian, 2015; Correa et al., 2018), which handles only T→S←X selection, by tackling the harder collider-bias case T→S←Y where no valid adjustment exists. The key conceptual departure is reframing collider bias as an out-of-distribution problem with the selection indicator S as an environmental label, enabling a coupled two-generator/two-discriminator GAN that simultaneously recovers missing S=0 samples and missing S labels in a small representative dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Collider bias in observational treatment effect estimation was previously unaddressed without strong parametric assumptions, and this paper provides the first generative, assumption-light solution that is plug-and-play with any CATE estimator. The OOD reframing offers a principled lens for understanding why standard estimators fail under collider bias (distribution shift + spurious T-Y correlation). Key limitations include the requirement for a small but truly representative dataset (collected via costly interventions), the challenge of training generative models on high-dimensional data, and the fact that real-world validation relies on manually introduced collider bias rather than naturally occurring cases. Open questions include whether the approach scales to continuous treatments, multi-valued selection, or settings where the representative data itself is partially biased.
