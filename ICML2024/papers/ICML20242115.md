## Problem

Existing model-based visual RL methods for distractor elimination have primarily targeted heterogeneous distractors (e.g., noisy video backgrounds) and fail on homogeneous distractors that are morphologically similar to the controllable agent, such as a shifted copy of the agent itself. Prior approaches either condition distractor dynamics on agent actions (which is semantically inappropriate) or assume action-independent distractor transitions (which is insufficient to capture homogeneous distractor semantics), and rely on heuristic auxiliary losses that are unstable and prone to component inversion.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB visual observations (64×64 frames from DMC environments) that may contain either natural video backgrounds (grayscale Kinetics clips) or a shifted agent with identical morphology to the controllable one. It produces two types of outputs: a control action for the agent (continuous, from the DMC action space) and an implicit distractor action represented as a vector of d categorical (one-hot) variables of size d×d, which is used internally to drive the task-irrelevant world model. The policy operates exclusively in the task-relevant latent state space, never seeing the distractor component.

## Method

AD3 introduces the Implicit Action Generator (IAG), which infers distractor actions via a cycle-consistency autoencoder: a Task-relevant Action-conditioned Inverse Dynamics (TAID) encoder takes (o_t, a_t, o_{t+1}) and outputs a categorical implicit action â⁻, while a Forward Implicit Action-conditioned Dynamics (FIAD) decoder reconstructs o_{t+1} from (o_t, a_t, â⁻), with losses for latent-state cycle consistency, pixel-level difference reconstruction, and one-step image reconstruction. The categorical bottleneck (d one-hot variables of size d, following DreamerV2) prevents shortcut solutions and enforces extreme sparsity. These inferred implicit actions condition a separate RSSM for task-irrelevant dynamics, while agent actions condition a second RSSM for task-relevant dynamics; both are trained jointly via a standard ELBO with cooperative masked image reconstruction, requiring no additional heuristic objectives. Policy learning is conducted solely within the task-relevant world model through imagined rollouts, and IAG is updated iteratively with its gradients decoupled from world-model training.

## Results

AD3 achieves the highest mean episodic return across all 8 task-distractor combinations (4 DMC tasks × 2 distractor types), with headline scores of 658±104 on Cheetah Run+NBV, 653±130 on Cheetah Run+AS, 435±85 on Walker Run+AS, and 99±31 on Hopper Hop+AS. AD3 is the only method that consistently performs well across both heterogeneous (Natural Video Backgrounds) and homogeneous (Agent Shifted) distractors, while all baselines fail on at least one setting. On the additional Car Racing+NBV and Jaco Arm+AS tasks, AD3 also outperforms Dreamer and TIA (505±174 vs. 304±177 and 251±129 on Car Racing+NBV; 58±72 vs. 35±51 and 7±20 on Jaco Arm+AS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AD3 outperforms all four baselines on every one of the 8 task-distractor combinations in the main evaluation. Against TIA (the closest competitor on NBV tasks), AD3 gains +226 on Cheetah Run+NBV (658 vs. 432) and +368 on Finger Spin+NBV (491 vs. 123), while TIA suffers high variance (±251 on Cheetah Run+AS) due to unstable adversarial loss optimization. Against Dreamer, which surprisingly performs well on AS tasks (540 on Cheetah Run+AS) by retaining all visual information in its latent state, AD3 still leads by +113 (653 vs. 540) and by a wider margin on NBV tasks (658 vs. 151 on Cheetah Run+NBV). Denoised MDP fails most severely on NBV settings (117 on Walker Run+NBV, 13 on Hopper Hop+NBV) because its factorization assumptions break down with complex video backgrounds. DBC, the only model-free baseline, is consistently the weakest, scoring near zero on Hopper Hop and Finger Spin+NBV, confirming that reconstruction-free approaches struggle with both distractor types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on distractor elimination in visual model-based RL (TIA, Denoised MDP, DBC) by introducing a fundamentally different assumption: task-irrelevant components are driven by their own 'implicit actions' rather than by agent actions or no action at all. The architecture builds directly on Dreamer's RSSM backbone and DreamerV2's categorical latent variables, while the Implicit Action Generator (IAG) borrows its cycle-consistency training protocol and multi-step rollout design from FICC. The key departure from TIA and related methods is the explicit inference of distractor-specific actions via an encoder-decoder with categorical bottlenecking, replacing TIA's heuristic adversarial reward dissociation and distractor-only reconstruction losses with a principled ELBO-derived objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap—homogeneous distractors (e.g., a shifted copy of the agent) that prior methods largely fail on—and provides a plug-and-play IAG module that can be integrated into any model-based visual RL pipeline. The implicit action concept has broader implications beyond RL, potentially unifying the semantics of actionless in-the-wild videos and informing world model learning. Limitations include the additional training overhead of IAG, evaluation primarily on DMC-scale tasks, and the need for iterative re-inference of implicit actions as the IAG updates. Open questions include scaling to real-world multi-distractor scenes and whether the IABMDP assumption holds when distractors become partially task-relevant.
