## Problem

Existing XAI methods for explaining decoder-only Transformer sequence classifiers (attention-based, gradient-based, propagation-based) are either computationally expensive or produce attributions that fail to faithfully reflect the model's actual decision process. This paper identifies that the causal attention mechanism in decoder-only models provides a free source of perturbed-input predictions that prior work has not exploited for attribution computation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240935/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text input sequence and the per-position logit vectors (intermediate predictions) produced during a forward pass of a decoder-only model. SP-PI requires only these intermediate predictions from a single pass with zero additional compute; MP-PI additionally requires B forward passes on randomly masked versions of the input. Attributions are produced at token, word, or sentence granularity (groups of tokens treated as single features), yielding a scalar score per feature indicating its contribution to the predicted class logit.

## Method

The key insight is that due to causal (masked) self-attention, the prediction at position i in a decoder-only model depends only on tokens 1 through i, so it can be interpreted as the model's prediction on a masked input where all tokens after position i are replaced by a mask token. SP-PI exploits this by computing each feature's attribution as the difference between consecutive intermediate predictions (p_i^c − p_{i−1}^c), requiring no extra forward passes. MP-PI performs B inference passes with randomly sampled binary masks, collects all intermediate predictions from each pass, and connects the resulting (coalition, prediction) pairs to Kernel SHAP's weighted regression formulation. A central technical contribution is formulating an optimization problem to find the mask sampling distribution P′ that makes the induced distribution of intermediate coalitions match the Shapley distribution, enabling MP-PI to produce SHAP-like attributions at the same 2n compute budget as Kernel SHAP but with higher sample efficiency.

## Results

In the activation study (higher AUC is better), MP-PI achieves the best AUC across all 7 datasets, with headline values of 0.970 (IMDB), 0.946 (SST-2), 0.867 (AG-News), and 0.929 (Twitter-Emotion), offering up to a 10.3% AUC improvement over the best prior method. SP-PI, at zero additional compute cost, also leads among low-cost methods (≤1×), e.g. 0.951 on IMDB vs. 0.916 for GAE and 0.903 for Input×Grad. In the inverse activation study (lower AUC is better), MP-PI achieves up to a 57.5% AUC reduction over the best prior work, with values as low as 0.214 (Twitter-Emotion) and 0.273 (Twitter-Sentiment). At the same 2n budget, MP-PI consistently outperforms Kernel SHAP, e.g. 0.946 vs. 0.899 on SST-2 (AS) and 0.431 vs. 0.533 on SST-2 (IAS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SP-PI (zero-cost) outperforms all 1×-cost baselines—GAE, Input×Grad, and LRP for Transformers—on 6 out of 7 datasets in the activation study, e.g. 0.951 vs. 0.916 (GAE) and 0.903 (Inp×Grad) on IMDB, while requiring no backpropagation. MP-PI (2n cost) dominates all baselines on every dataset in both the activation and inverse activation studies. Against Kernel SHAP at the same 2n budget, MP-PI improves AUC by 1.1–7.1 points in the activation study (e.g. 0.946 vs. 0.899 on SST-2) and reduces AUC by 10.9–36.5 points in the inverse activation study (e.g. 0.431 vs. 0.533 on SST-2), demonstrating higher sample efficiency from reusing intermediate predictions. Against Integrated Gradients (also 2n cost), MP-PI leads on all 7 datasets, with the largest gap on Twitter-Emotion (0.929 vs. 0.832 AS; 0.214 vs. 0.361 IAS). A-Last, while zero-cost like SP-PI, is consistently outperformed by SP-PI on all datasets (e.g. 0.951 vs. 0.873 on IMDB), confirming that exploiting causal intermediate predictions is more informative than reading last-layer attention weights.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Kernel SHAP framework (Lundberg & Lee, 2017), repurposing its weighted regression formulation to compute SHAP-like attributions from a novel source of perturbed predictions. It departs from the broader XAI-for-Transformers literature (attention-based methods like GAE and A-Last, gradient-based methods like Integrated Gradients, and propagation-based methods like LRP for Transformers) by exploiting a property unique to decoder-only architectures: causal attention ensures that intermediate predictions at position i depend only on tokens 1..i, effectively yielding n perturbed-input predictions from a single forward pass at negligible cost. The key conceptual move is reframing these intermediate predictions as approximate model outputs on masked inputs, which connects the autoregressive generation process to the perturbation-based attribution paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As decoder-only LLMs are increasingly deployed in high-stakes domains (healthcare, finance), the need for faithful, low-cost explanations is pressing. SP-PI's near-zero computational overhead makes it practically attractive for production settings where gradient-based or multi-pass methods are prohibitive. Key limitations include the assumption that masked inputs remain valid model inputs, the correlated (non-independent) nature of intermediate samples that prevents exact SHAP recovery, and restriction to decoder-only sequence classification. Open questions include whether the approach extends to encoder-decoder or encoder-only architectures, whether it generalizes to generation tasks, and how sensitive attributions are to the choice of mask token.
