## Problem

SAM's original formulation uses the same surrogate loss (e.g., cross-entropy) for both the minimizer and the maximizer in its zero-sum game. The paper identifies a fundamental flaw: maximizing a surrogate upper bound on misclassification error does not guarantee maximizing the actual 0-1 loss, leading to weaker perturbations that fail to push correctly-classified samples into the misclassified region. The paper asks whether SAM should instead be applied directly to the 0-1 loss, which is the true objective in supervised classification.

## Input / Output

BiSAM is a drop-in replacement for SAM that consumes the same training data (32×32 RGB images with class labels for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 images for ImageNet-1K, tokenized text for GLUE) and produces optimized model parameters. It requires no changes to the training pipeline: the same ρ=0.05 neighborhood radius, batch size, and optimizer settings used for SAM apply directly, and the only additional hyperparameters are μ (log-sum-exp temperature) and the choice of lower-bound function φ. Computational cost per iteration matches SAM exactly since the perturbation step still requires one forward-backward pass.

## Method

BiSAM reformulates SAM as a bilevel optimization problem where the minimizer (model weights) minimizes a differentiable upper bound (cross-entropy) while the maximizer (perturbation ε) maximizes a differentiable lower bound of the 0-1 loss. The lower bound is constructed as (1/μ)log(Σⱼ exp(μφ(Fⱼ))) − (1/μ)log(K), where Fⱼ = f(x)ⱼ − f(x)ᵧ is the logit difference and φ is a valid lower bound of the 0-1 step function (e.g., tanh(αx) or −log(1+e^(γ−x))+1). The perturbation is computed via a first-order Taylor expansion of this lower-bound loss, yielding ε = ρ∇Q/‖∇Q‖, which is structurally identical to SAM's update but driven by a loss that actually aligns with increasing misclassification. This bilevel formulation is compatible with existing SAM variants (ASAM, ESAM, mSAM) by simply swapping the perturbation loss while preserving their respective normalization or data-selection mechanisms.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, BiSAM(-log) achieves 95.88% average test accuracy across five architectures (DenseNet-121, ResNet-56, VGG19-BN, WRN-28-2, WRN-28-10), outperforming SAM (95.78%) and SGD (95.48%). On CIFAR-100, BiSAM(-log) reaches 78.78% average accuracy versus SAM's 78.52%. On ImageNet-1K, BiSAM(tanh) attains 76.02% Top-1 accuracy compared to SAM's 75.83%. The relative improvement of BiSAM over SAM (measured as a fraction of the SGD-to-SAM gap) reaches 33.3% on CIFAR-10 and 44.1% on CIFAR-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAM (Foret et al., 2021), BiSAM(-log) strictly outperforms on every model for both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, with the largest gap on ResNet-56 (94.28% vs 94.09% on CIFAR-10; 74.67% vs 74.38% on CIFAR-100). On ImageNet-1K, BiSAM(tanh) edges out SAM by 0.19 points in Top-1 accuracy (76.02% vs 75.83%). Against ASAM (Kwon et al., 2021), A-BiSAM(-log) wins on 4 of 5 CIFAR-10 models (e.g., ResNet-56: 95.09% vs 94.86%) and ties on DenseNet-121 (96.79%), yielding a 0.07-point average gain. Against ESAM (Du et al., 2022), E-BiSAM(-log) improves on all five CIFAR-10 models, with the most notable gain on ResNet-56 (94.60% vs 94.21%) and VGG19-BN (94.43% vs 94.16%), for a 0.15-point average improvement. All comparisons use the same ρ=0.05 hyperparameter originally tuned for SAM, confirming the gains stem from the reformulation rather than additional tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly reformulates SAM (Foret et al., 2021), which casts sharpness-aware minimization as a zero-sum min-max game where both the weight minimizer and perturbation maximizer optimize the same surrogate loss (typically cross-entropy). The key departure is the argument that maximizing an upper bound on misclassification error (cross-entropy) does not guarantee maximizing the actual 0-1 loss, demonstrated via a clean counterexample. The fix repurposes the bilevel optimization framework (previously applied to hyperparameter tuning and meta-learning) to assign each player an aligned surrogate: an upper bound for the minimizer and a novel log-sum-exp lower bound for the maximizer. The paper also shows the PAC-Bayes generalization bound from Foret et al. extends to the 0-1 loss, and demonstrates compatibility with existing SAM variants (ASAM, ESAM, mSAM).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a genuine conceptual misalignment in SAM's formulation that had gone unnoticed: the ascent step optimizes the wrong quantity relative to the actual classification goal. The fix is elegant and computationally free, and the consistent (if modest) improvement across five architectures, three image datasets, NLP fine-tuning, and noisy-label settings suggests the misalignment was a real bottleneck. The main open question is relating the bilevel relaxation (Eq. 12) back to the true 0-1 loss minimax problem (Eq. 5), which the authors explicitly leave for future work. Broader implications include whether the same upper/lower-bound decoupling principle applies to other min-max formulations in adversarial training or GANs.
