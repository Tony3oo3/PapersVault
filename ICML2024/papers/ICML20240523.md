## Problem

Value-based deep RL methods train value functions via mean-squared error regression, which has proven difficult to scale to large networks such as high-capacity transformers, in stark contrast to supervised learning where cross-entropy classification scales reliably. This paper asks whether simply reframing the value-function regression problem as a classification task can unlock the same level of scalability in RL.

## Input / Output

The method takes a state observation (RGB frames for Atari/robotics, token sequences for Wordle, board encodings for chess) and an action as input, and outputs m logits that are passed through softmax to yield a categorical distribution over m evenly-spaced value locations (e.g., 51 bins in [-10, 10] for Atari, 128 bins in [0, 1] for chess). The scalar Q-value is recovered as the expected value of this distribution. The training target is not a scalar but a full categorical distribution constructed from the TD target, making the loss a cross-entropy between two m-dimensional probability vectors rather than a squared error between two scalars.

## Method

The core contribution is a drop-in replacement of the MSE TD loss with a categorical cross-entropy loss: the Q-network outputs m logits (one per bin) instead of a single scalar, and the scalar TD target is projected onto a categorical distribution before computing cross-entropy. Three projection strategies are studied: Two-Hot (exact 2-point linear interpolation), HL-Gauss (Gaussian CDF-based smoothing that spreads probability mass to neighboring bins, parameterized by σ/ζ), and C51 (distributional Bellman operator with categorical projection). HL-Gauss, with a default smoothing ratio of σ/ζ = 0.75 distributing mass to approximately 6 neighboring locations, consistently outperforms all alternatives. The approach is algorithm-agnostic, plugging into DQN, CQL, IMPALA, and Q-Transformer without architectural changes, and the paper provides controlled ablations showing the gains stem from reduced overfitting to noisy targets, better representation learning, and improved robustness to target non-stationarity.

## Results

HL-Gauss (categorical cross-entropy with Gaussian-smoothed targets) consistently outperforms MSE regression across all evaluated domains: 1.8–2.1× IQM improvement in multi-task Atari with IMPALA+ResNets, ~45% improvement over C51 in multi-game offline RL with ResNet-101 (80M params), 40% higher success rate on Wordle, 70% improvement over one-hot targets on Lichess chess puzzles, and 67% higher peak performance on robotic manipulation. In single-task online Atari (60 games, 200M frames), HL-Gauss achieves the best aggregated human-normalized IQM score, surpassing both C51 and MSE. In offline Atari (17 games), HL-Gauss and C51 remain stable with prolonged training while MSE degrades, and HL-Gauss outperforms C51.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HL-Gauss outperforms the primary regression baseline (DQN+MSE, Mnih et al. 2015) across every domain tested, with the largest gains in scaling settings (multi-task and multi-game Atari) where MSE performance degrades with larger networks while HL-Gauss continues to improve. Against C51 (Bellemare et al. 2017), HL-Gauss wins in both online and offline single-task Atari and in multi-game offline RL (~45% over C51 at ResNet-101), suggesting the cross-entropy loss itself is more important than modeling the full return distribution. Two-Hot (Schrittwieser et al. 2020) surprisingly underperforms MSE in offline Atari and is consistently worse than HL-Gauss, attributed to its failure to distribute probability mass to neighboring bins. In chess, HL-Gauss beats the one-hot targets of Ruoss et al. 2024 by 70% and nearly matches AlphaZero with 400 MCTS simulations. For robotic manipulation, HL-Gauss achieves 67% higher peak performance than the Q-Transformer MSE baseline (Chebotar et al. 2023) while being more sample-efficient. On replay ratio scaling, HL-Gauss performs equivalently to ReDo (Sokar et al. 2023), a method specifically designed to combat overfitting, without requiring network resets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the HL-Gauss histogram loss from supervised regression (Imani & White, 2018) to value-based RL, positioning it alongside C51 (Bellemare et al., 2017) and Two-Hot (Schrittwieser et al., 2020) as alternatives to the standard MSE TD loss in DQN and its variants. A key departure from prior distributional RL work is the finding that the cross-entropy loss itself, rather than modeling the return distribution, is the critical ingredient for performance gains — HL-Gauss (which does not model return distributions) consistently outperforms C51. The paper also connects to the broader supervised learning observation that reframing regression as classification improves scalability, asking whether this principle transfers to the non-stationary, bootstrapped setting of RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides a simple, well-evidenced 'drop-in' replacement for MSE that addresses the long-standing inability of value-based RL to scale to large networks (especially transformers), a gap that has persisted despite supervised classification scaling reliably. The finding that the loss function matters more than distributional modeling reframes how the community should think about C51's success and suggests that future algorithm design should focus on loss design rather than distributional representations. Open questions include whether these benefits extend to pre-training, fine-tuning, and continual RL settings, and whether theoretical analysis of cross-entropy optimization dynamics can yield further improved losses.
