## Problem

The predictive normalized maximum likelihood (pNML) distribution provides a principled MDL-based approach to uncertainty estimation, but computing it exactly requires retraining a hindsight-optimal model for every possible label, which is intractable for deep networks. Overparameterized models compound this by fitting arbitrary labels, causing the pNML denominator to degenerate (the 'infinity problem'), and existing approximations like ACNML that take explicit optimization steps are both computationally expensive and unreliable when the base model is overconfident.

## Input / Output

Given a trained CNN (ResNet-18 or LeNet) and its training dataset, the method first computes an EKFAC approximation of the Fisher information matrix in a single pass over the training set. For each test image, it then requires only one Jacobian-vector product per label to compute the Boltzmann influence function, yielding either a calibrated class probability distribution (for labelled or unlabelled points) or a scalar stochastic complexity score (for unlabelled points where the error term is undefined). The output distribution is controlled by two hyperparameters: α weights the test point relative to the training set, and β sets the inverse temperature that softens the proximal constraint.

## Method

IF-COMP introduces the Boltzmann influence function (BIF), a temperature-scaled variant of the influence function derived by applying a temperature β to the proximal Bregman objective before linearizing. This temperature scaling softens the second-order curvature in function space, allowing the hindsight-optimal model to accommodate low-probability labels that an overconfident base model would otherwise reject. The BIF is computed efficiently via an EKFAC eigendecomposition of the Fisher information, reducing the influence calculation to a squared L2 norm in the inverse eigenspace. IF-COMP then approximates the hindsight-optimal output probability for each label using a first-order Taylor expansion in log-probability space, and assembles these into the pNML distribution and stochastic complexity (error + parametric complexity terms). Unlike ACNML, which explicitly optimizes parameters for each label, IF-COMP linearizes the model directly, enabling post-hoc tuning of the example weighting α without recomputation and achieving a 7–15× speedup.

## Results

On OOD detection (OpenOOD benchmark), IF-COMP achieves new state-of-the-art AUROC on MNIST (99.40 Near, 99.97 Far) and CIFAR-10 (92.23 Near, 95.63 Far), beating all 20 baselines. On mislabel detection, IF-COMP achieves the highest AUROC across all CIFAR-10 noise types (96.86 Human, 88.07 Data-dependent, 95.69 Asymmetric, 97.83 Symmetric), outperforming methods that use additional training checkpoints. For uncertainty calibration on CIFAR-10C, IF-COMP achieves lower ECE than all Bayesian and NML baselines at corruption severities 3–5. Computationally, IF-COMP is 7–15× faster than ACNML per inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IF-COMP consistently outperforms ACNML (Zhou & Levine, 2021), the closest NML-based competitor, on pNML validation (Pearson R 0.928 vs. lower), uncertainty calibration (lower ECE at severities 3–5), and computational speed (7–15× faster). Against Bayesian baselines—ensembling (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017), SWA (Izmailov et al., 2019), SWAG-D (Maddox et al., 2019), and MC-Dropout (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016)—IF-COMP achieves lower ECE at corruption severities 3–5 on CIFAR-10C, while the ensemble remains slightly better at severity 2. On mislabel detection, IF-COMP beats Self-IF (Koh & Liang, 2017), Trac-IN (Pruthi et al., 2020), EL2N, and GraNd (Paul et al., 2021) across all CIFAR-10 noise types, notably achieving 88.07 AUROC on data-dependent noise where all other methods fall near random (~50). On OOD detection, IF-COMP surpasses RMDS (Ren et al., 2021) on MNIST (99.40 vs. 98.00 Near) and CIFAR-10 (95.63 vs. 92.20 Far), though RMDS retains an edge on CIFAR-100 Far (82.92 vs. 79.41). IF-COMP does not require extra checkpoints or multiple training runs, unlike Trac-IN, EL2N, and GraNd, making it strictly more information-efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: the MDL/pNML framework for universal coding (Rissanen, Roos & Rissanen) and the influence function literature (Koh & Liang, Bae et al.). Bae et al. (2022) showed that influence functions in neural networks actually approximate a proximal Bregman objective rather than true retraining; IF-COMP builds directly on this by temperature-scaling that proximal objective to create a Boltzmann influence function that can accommodate low-probability labels. The key departure from the closest prior method, ACNML (Zhou & Levine, 2021), is that IF-COMP linearizes the model in log-probability space rather than taking explicit optimization steps in parameter space, enabling a 7–15× speedup and post-hoc control over example weighting via the α parameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IF-COMP provides a practical, single-model route to pNML-based uncertainty that avoids the computational cost of Bayesian ensembles or iterative retraining, making MDL-based calibration accessible for large-scale deployment. The temperature-scaling insight is non-trivial: without it (β=1), the parametric complexity term degenerates into a mirror of log error and provides no additional signal, so the method's success hinges on careful per-dataset tuning. Limitations include weaker performance on CIFAR-100 (where all methods cluster near random for some noise types), the need for a full EKFAC eigendecomposition over the training set, and the fact that the linearization approximation's validity at scale (e.g., billion-parameter models) remains untested. Open questions include whether the BIF framework extends naturally to generative models or continuous output spaces, and whether the temperature parameter can be learned end-to-end rather than tuned on a validation set.
