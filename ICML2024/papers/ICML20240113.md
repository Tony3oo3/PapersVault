## Problem

Bourgain's 1985 theorem guarantees that any n-point metric space embeds into Euclidean space with O(log n) distortion, but this result is purely static. No prior work maintained a low-distortion embedding into ℓp space under a sequence of edge weight updates, leaving a fundamental gap in the dynamic algorithms literature. This paper asks whether such an embedding can be explicitly maintained with polylogarithmic expected distortion and near-linear update time, and further shows that in the fully dynamic setting (allowing both increases and decreases), no algorithm can achieve sub-W distortion with high probability without Ω(n) update time per step.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a weighted undirected graph G with n vertices, m edges, and integer weights in [1, W], and processes a sequence of Q edge weight increases (the decremental setting). After each update, it explicitly outputs all vertex coordinates that changed in the embedding ρ: V → ℝ^{log(nW)}, and can answer pairwise distance queries in O(log(nW)) time. The embedding dimension is log(nW) and the expected stretch is O(log²n); the algorithm runs in total time O((m^{1+o(1)} log²W + Q) log(nW)) with high probability. Edge deletions are simulated by large weight increases since ℓp space cannot represent infinite distances.

## Method

The core mechanism is a novel 'characteristic embedding' built from (β,R,ε)-distance preserving cuts: for each scale R_i = 2^i, a random subset S_i is constructed so that vertices closer than ε are never separated, while vertices farther than R_i are separated with probability ≥ 1/2. Each vertex's i-th coordinate is simply 0 or R_i depending on which side of cut S_i it falls on, avoiding the Fréchet embedding form d_G(v, S_i) that would require exact distance maintenance and break under dynamic updates. The cuts are obtained by first computing a (β,R)-weak decomposition via Bartal's ball-growing procedure (contracting edges shorter than R/2n, then growing random-radius balls), then independently assigning each cluster to one side of the cut with probability 1/2. For the dynamic setting, the weak decomposition is maintained using an adaptation of Forster et al. (2021)'s decremental clustering under edge weight increases, where clusters only split (never merge), and new sub-clusters receive fresh independent Bernoulli assignments to preserve the cut's distance-preserving properties. The final embedding concatenates O(log(nW)) such cuts at geometrically increasing scales, yielding the O(log²n) expected stretch bound.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1.3) achieves expected distortion O(log²n) with total update time O((m^(1+o(1)) log²W + Q) log(nW)) and query time O(log(nW)) for a decremental dynamic embedding into ℓ_p space of dimension log(nW). A lower bound (Theorem 3.1) shows that in the fully dynamic setting, no algorithm can maintain an explicit ℓ_p embedding with distortion o(W) with high probability without Ω(n) update time per step. Empirically, on SNAP LastFM subgraphs of 150, 300, and 600 nodes with 10,000, 5,000, and 1,000 edge-weight-increase updates respectively, the achieved stretch remains within a constant factor of the O(log²n) theoretical bound, with the ratio of embedded-to-exact average pairwise distances staying close to 1 across all queries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include a head-to-head comparison table against competing dynamic embedding methods, as it is the first work to provide a dynamic ℓ_p embedding. It positions its contribution relative to three key prior results: (1) Bourgain's static O(log n) distortion embedding, which this work extends to the dynamic setting at the cost of an additional log n factor (O(log²n)); (2) Forster et al. (2021), who maintain dynamic probabilistic tree embeddings with n^(o(1)) expected distortion and n^(o(1)) update time, but into a distribution of trees rather than ℓ_p space; and (3) Chechik (2018) and Forster et al. (2023) for decremental APSP, whose update-time bounds the paper's O(log²n)-stretch distance oracle nearly matches. In the empirical validation, the dynamic embedding's average pairwise distances are compared against exact all-pairs shortest paths computed offline after each update; the ratio of embedded to exact distances stays within a small constant factor across all three graph sizes, confirming the theoretical O(log²n) stretch in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the static metric embedding lineage (Bourgain 1985, Johnson-Lindenstrauss 1986) into the dynamic regime by combining Bourgain's padded decomposition with Bartal's ball-growing weak decompositions and the decremental clustering framework of Forster et al. (2021). It deliberately departs from the standard Fréchet embedding approach (where coordinates are distances to random sets) because those cannot be maintained dynamically without exact distance oracles; instead, it introduces a 'characteristic embedding' built from distance-preserving cuts that only requires tracking cluster membership. The closest prior work—dynamic embeddings into tree metrics by Forster & Goranci (2019) and Forster et al. (2021)—targets a fundamentally different (and less general) host space, making this the first dynamic embedding into the full ℓp family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result to bring polylogarithmic-distortion ℓp embeddings into the dynamic setting, opening the door to dynamic algorithms for NP-hard problems (sparsest cut, bandwidth, buy-at-bulk) whose approximation guarantees rely on Euclidean geometry. The impossibility result for the fully dynamic regime (Ω(n) update time for o(W) distortion with high probability) is an important delineation: it shows the decremental restriction is not merely a technical convenience but a fundamental boundary imposed by the completeness of ℓp space. Key open questions include whether weaker guarantees (e.g., expected rather than high-probability distortion) can recover the fully dynamic setting, and whether the O(log² n) distortion is tight for the dynamic case. The empirical validation on small graphs (≤600 nodes) leaves practical scalability untested.
