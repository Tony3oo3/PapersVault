## Problem

Stochastic weakly convex optimization has traditionally relied on a global Lipschitz continuity assumption on the objective or its subgradients, which fails for functions whose subgradient norm grows unboundedly (e.g., high-order polynomials or exponentials). Without this assumption, standard SGD and proximal subgradient methods can diverge, and it remained open whether the standard O(1/√K) convergence rate could be preserved under arbitrary non-Lipschitz growth.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a stochastic weakly convex composite objective f(x,ξ)+ω(x) with i.i.d. samples ξ, and either an explicit growth function G(·) bounding the local Lipschitz constant or an independent sample ξ' used to estimate it. The output is an iterate x^k whose Moreau-envelope gradient norm satisfies min_k E[‖∇ψ_{1/ρ}(x^k)‖²] = O(1/√K) with constant (not vanishing) probability. A key structural requirement is that the Moreau envelope has bounded level-sets (Assumption A5), which is natural for coercive non-Lipschitz objectives but replaces the usual global Lipschitz bound.

## Method

Within the model-based optimization framework, the paper designs two robust adaptive stepsizes that replace the fixed γ_k = O(√K) used under global Lipschitzness. When a growth function G(·) is known, the stepsize scales as γ_k = O((G(‖x^k‖)+1)√K), adapting to the local Lipschitz constant at each iterate. When G is unknown, a 'reference Lipschitz continuity' property (bounded variance of Lip(x,ξ) across independent samples) enables estimation via an independent copy ξ', and the stepsize becomes γ_k = O(max{Lip(x^k,ξ'), α)·√K, which generalizes gradient clipping to the model-based setting. The central technical tool is a probabilistic stability argument: the Moreau envelope potential is bounded on expectation, the adaptive stepsize restricts per-iteration displacement to O(1/√K), and a tail-bound (via Markov's inequality and the bounded level-set condition) shows iterates remain in a bounded ball with high probability, recovering the O(1/√K) rate without any uniform Lipschitz bound.

## Results

The paper establishes that stochastic model-based methods (including SGD) achieve an O(1/√K) convergence rate with constant failure probability for weakly convex objectives without global Lipschitz continuity, under either a known growth function G(·) or an unknown Lipschitz constant estimated via reference Lipschitz continuity. In experiments on robust nonlinear regression (m=300, n=100, 400 epochs), vanilla SGD diverges for the high-order polynomial problem r₂ at θ~10⁸, while the proposed robust stepsizes (SGD-G and SGD-R) converge robustly across a wide range of θ. For the exponential-growth problem r₃, the reference-Lipschitz-based stepsize (SGD-R) never diverges in practice, whereas vanilla SGD is highly sensitive to θ. Compared to mirror descent, the paper's SGD-based robust stepsize methods exhibit superior convergence in iteration count while solving a simpler proximal subproblem.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is mirror descent with Bregman divergence kernels from Davis et al. (2018a), which is the standard approach for non-Lipschitz optimization. On both r₁ (quadratic) and r₂ (high-order polynomial) problems, mirror descent is more stable than vanilla SGD, confirming its utility for non-Lipschitz settings. However, the paper's robust stepsize SGD methods (SGD-G and SGD-R) achieve superior convergence performance in terms of iteration count compared to mirror descent. Additionally, the paper's method solves a much simpler proximal subproblem (a quadratic regularized model) versus mirror descent's nontrivial root-finding subproblem, giving it a practical efficiency advantage. For the high-order polynomial r₂, vanilla SGD diverges at large θ values (~10⁸) while both robust stepsize variants and mirror descent remain stable, with the robust SGD approaches converging faster. The reference-Lipschitz stepsize (SGD-R) is the most robust in practice, never diverging across all tested problems and θ ranges, though it can be conservative on mild-growth problems like r₁.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model-based stochastic optimization framework of Davis and Drusvyatskiy (2019) by removing the global Lipschitz continuity assumption that underpins their convergence analysis. The robust stepsize strategy generalizes the gradient clipping approach of Mai and Johansson (2021) and Zhang et al. (2019) from the subgradient setting to the broader model-based framework, while the 'reference Lipschitz continuity' concept provides a principled way to estimate local Lipschitzness from independent samples. The paper also positions itself against the mirror descent / Bregman divergence approach of Lu (2019) and Davis et al. (2018a), showing that a simpler proximal subproblem with adaptive regularization can match or exceed their performance without requiring kernel design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This resolves a concrete open question in stochastic optimization: whether the standard O(1/√K) rate can be preserved for weakly convex objectives with arbitrary non-Lipschitz growth (e.g., exponential or high-order polynomial), without imposing any explicit bound on the Lipschitz parameter. The practical implication is that practitioners dealing with non-Lipschitz problems (phase retrieval, robust regression with polynomial/exponential losses) can use a simple stepsize modification rather than switching to more expensive mirror descent with carefully chosen Bregman kernels. Open questions include extending the analysis to momentum-based or adaptive gradient methods, handling the interpolation regime where level-set boundedness may fail, and whether the constant failure probability can be amplified to high probability without multiple independent runs.
