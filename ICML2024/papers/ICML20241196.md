## Problem

The state-of-the-art precision and recall (P&R) metrics for evaluating generative models require O(n² log n) time and O(n²) space due to exhaustive pairwise distance computation and k-NN sorting, making them computationally infeasible on commodity hardware for the massive datasets (70k–1.5M images) used to train modern generative models. This computational bottleneck hampers the field's ability to assess SOTA models trained on large-scale data.

## Input / Output

The method takes VGG-16 feature vectors extracted from n generated images and n real images (e.g., 70k from FFHQ, 1.5M from LSUN-Horse) and produces a precision score and a recall score that approximate the original P&R with ~1% relative error. A key design choice is that the method operates on a reduced set of m hub samples (m < n, controlled by threshold t) rather than the full n samples, while still computing radii against the full set to preserve accuracy. The method is evaluated across StyleGAN2/3, Projected-GAN, VQ-VAE-2, and Latent Diffusion models.

## Method

The paper identifies two redundancies in the original P&R: (1) samples with similar hubness values share nearly identical binary-score ratios, so a small set of hubs suffices for ratio estimation; and (2) most samples classified as inside a manifold are covered by the k-NN hypersphere of at least one high-hubness sample, so only hubs need to serve as hypersphere centers. Hubness-aware sampling extracts m representative 'hub' samples per set (those whose hubness value exceeds threshold t), reducing the effective computation from O(n² log n) to O(mn log n) time and O(n²) to O(mn) space. Because hub identification only requires approximate rather than exact k-NN results, the method further leverages IVF-PQ approximate nearest-neighbor search for hub detection, adding negligible overhead. The resulting eP&R metrics achieve near-identical precision/recall values (≤2.3% relative error across all tested models and datasets) while cutting wall-clock time roughly in half and peak memory by ~28% on FFHQ, with even larger gains on 1.5M-image datasets.

## Results

The proposed eP&R metrics achieve relative errors of only 0.4%–2.3% compared to the original P&R (Kynkäänniemi et al., 2019) across five datasets (FFHQ, LSUN-Car, LSUN-Church, LSUN-Cat, LSUN-Horse) and four generative model families (StyleGAN2/3, Projected-GAN, VQ-VAE-2, Latent Diffusion). On FFHQ (70k images), eP&R reduces parallel computation time from 144s to 75s and peak memory from 19.90 GB to 14.21 GB. On the large LSUN-Horse dataset (1.5m images), eP&R cuts parallel time from 2h10min to 64min, making the metric feasible on commodity hardware.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the original P&R metric from Kynkäänniemi et al. (2019), which requires O(n² log n) time and O(n²) space. eP&R matches the baseline on all tested dataset–model combinations with relative errors consistently below 2.3% (most under 1%), confirming that hubness-aware sampling preserves the P&R signal. The efficiency gains are substantial: on FFHQ, eP&R runs in 75s vs. 144s (parallel) and uses 14.21 GB vs. 19.90 GB peak memory; on LSUN-Church (120k images), time drops from 238s to 113s and memory from 37.24 GB to 25.01 GB. On the 1.5m-image LSUN-Horse dataset, eP&R completes in 64 minutes versus 2h10min for the baseline, a 2.1× speedup that makes evaluation feasible on a single GPU node. The ablation study further shows that each component (ratio redundancy removal, manifold-identification redundancy removal, and approximate k-NN) contributes incrementally, with the full eP&R pipeline achieving the best time–accuracy trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper connects two previously separate threads: the k-NN-based precision and recall metrics of Kynkäänniemi et al. (2019), which model image manifolds via hyperspheres, and the hubness phenomenon of Radovanovic et al. (2010), where certain points in high-dimensional spaces are disproportionately frequent nearest neighbors. Prior work on hubness applied it to classification (hubness-weighted k-NN, naive hubness-Bayesian k-NN) or to identifying high-quality GAN latents (Liang et al., 2022), but this paper is the first to exploit hubness values as importance identifiers specifically for reducing the computational cost of generative model evaluation metrics. The further use of approximate k-NN (IVF-PQ) for hub identification is justified by the observation that hubness ranking is insensitive to exact neighbor identities, a property not previously leveraged in the P&R context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As generative models are trained on datasets ranging from 400M (LAION-400M) to 5B (LAION-5B) samples, the O(n²) cost of the SOTA P&R metric becomes infeasible on commodity hardware, creating a practical bottleneck for evaluating state-of-the-art models. The eP&R metric reduces this to O(mn log n) with ~1% relative error, making P&R feasible on datasets up to 1.5M images on a single GPU. However, the paper acknowledges that Stage 3 (pairwise distances between hubs and full sets) remains a bottleneck and could be further optimized via subspace-aware distance computation. An open question is whether the approach maintains its accuracy at the truly massive scales (tens to hundreds of millions of samples) that modern text-to-image models actually use, as experiments top out at 1.5M images.
