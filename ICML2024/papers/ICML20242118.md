## Problem

While information-theoretic thresholds for exact recovery on the Contextual Stochastic Block Model (CSBM) were established under the unsupervised regime, the semi-supervised setting—where a fraction of node labels are revealed—lacked a complete characterization of when all unlabeled nodes can be perfectly classified. This paper identifies the sharp IT threshold for exact recovery in semi-supervised CSBM and asks whether practical estimators (spectral methods, GCNs) can attain this fundamental limit.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242118/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a transductive graph G=(V,E) with N nodes split into n labeled and m unlabeled vertices (training ratio τ=n/N), where the full adjacency matrix A (including edges among unlabeled nodes) and d-dimensional Gaussian feature vectors X are available. The output is a binary class assignment for each of the m unlabeled nodes. A key structural detail is that the estimator exploits cross-block edges A_UL between labeled and unlabeled nodes, which are unavailable in inductive settings and are essential for shrinking the exact recovery boundary.

## Method

The paper derives a rate function I(a_τ, b_τ, c_τ) = [(√a_τ − √b_τ)² + c_τ]/2 whose value relative to 1 determines the exact recovery threshold, where a_τ, b_τ encode graph sparsity and c_τ encodes feature SNR. It then constructs an optimal spectral estimator that combines a PCA-inspired GMM component (using the hollowed Gram matrix) with an SBM component (using eigenvectors of the unlabeled subgraph and cross-block edges), achieving the IT bound in both the q_m ≳ log(m) and 1 ≪ q_m ≪ log(m) regimes. For GCNs, the key insight is that the self-loop weight ρ must be set to 2c_τ q_m / log(a_τ/b_τ) to match the IT threshold; with this optimal ρ, both linear ridge regression on GCN features and a two-step gradient-trained GCN (one-step GD for feature alignment plus spectral estimation for ρ) achieve exact recovery whenever I(a_τ, b_τ, c_τ) > 1.

## Results

The paper establishes the sharp information-theoretic threshold I(a_τ, b_τ, c_τ) = 1 for exact recovery of all test nodes in semi-supervised CSBM, where I(a_τ, b_τ, c_τ) = [(√a_τ − √b_τ)² + c_τ]/2. The proposed spectral estimator ŷ_PCA achieves exact recovery with probability at least 1 − m^{1−I_τ} when I_τ > 1, and matches the optimal expected mismatch ratio e^{−I_τ·q_m} when I_τ ≤ 1. For GCN, the optimal self-loop weight ρ = 2c_τ/log(a_τ/b_τ) · q_m enables both linear ridge regression and gradient-trained GCN to attain the same IT bound, while without self-loops (ρ = 0) exact recovery is only guaranteed under the stricter condition I(a_τ, b_τ, 0) > 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with the unsupervised exact recovery boundary I(a_0, b_0, c_0) = 1 from Abbe et al. (2022), which corresponds to τ = 0 (no labeled nodes). The paper shows that the semi-supervised boundary I(a_τ, b_τ, c_τ) = 1 strictly shrinks the impossibility region: the extra information from the labeled subgraph A_L, the cross-block A_UL, and the feature vectors X_U makes exact recovery achievable at lower SNR than in the unsupervised case. This is visualized in Figure 2 where the red (semi-supervised) curve lies inside the blue (unsupervised) curve. Among the proposed estimators, the spectral method ŷ_PCA and the linear ridge regression with optimal self-loop both provably match the IT lower bound, while the gradient-trained GCN matches the IT bound above the threshold but exhibits a gap below it (Figure 6), leaving its optimality in the sub-threshold regime open.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unsupervised exact recovery thresholds for CSBM established by Abbe et al. (2022) to the semi-supervised (transductive) regime, where a fraction of node labels is revealed. It also generalizes the semi-supervised GMM analysis of Lelarge & Miolane (2019) to the combined graph-plus-feature CSBM setting, and moves beyond the constant-average-degree regime studied by Shi et al. (2024) to growing-degree graphs. The spectral estimator is inspired by PCA on the hollowed Gram matrix, while the GCN analysis builds directly on the Kipf-Welling architecture, with the key departure being the treatment of the self-loop weight ρ as a trainable parameter rather than a fixed hyperparameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first information-theoretic characterization of when all unlabeled nodes can be perfectly classified in semi-supervised CSBM, giving a concrete rate function I(a_τ, b_τ, c_τ) that separates feasible from infeasible regimes. The finding that a standard GCN with a specific optimal self-loop weight ρ = 2c_τ·q_m / log(a_τ/b_τ) matches the IT bound offers a principled, model-dependent recipe for tuning GCN architectures. A notable open question is whether GCN remains optimal below the IT threshold (I < 1), where simulations reveal a gap between the GCN error and the theoretical lower bound. Future directions include extending the analysis to nonlinear GCNs, XOR-SBM, and geometric graph models, and understanding how feature learning interacts with over-smoothing.
