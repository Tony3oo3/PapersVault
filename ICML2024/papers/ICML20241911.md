## Problem

Existing smoothed DRL agents (CROP, Policy Smoothing) apply Randomized Smoothing only at test time without training with it, resulting in substantially lower clean rewards and negligible robustness gains over their non-smoothed counterparts. Additionally, standard attacks like PGD are ineffective at reducing the rewards of smoothed agents, creating a false impression of empirical robustness. This paper addresses both the utility-robustness tradeoff and the evaluation gap.

## Input / Output

For S-DQN (Atari), the input is an RGB frame observation to which Gaussian noise N(0, σ²I) is added before passing through a DnCNN denoiser and then a pretrained Q-network; the output is a discrete action selected via hard RS (Monte Carlo estimation of the probability that the argmax action is preserved). For S-PPO (Mujoco), the input is a continuous state vector with added Gaussian noise, processed through a policy network whose outputs (mean and std of a Gaussian action distribution) are aggregated via median smoothing across M noisy samples; the output is a continuous action vector. Both methods additionally produce a certified radius (S-DQN), an action bound (S-PPO), and a trajectory-level reward lower bound as formal robustness guarantees.

## Method

S-DQN introduces a denoiser D (DnCNN) before the Q-network, trained with a joint loss combining reconstruction MSE (denoised state vs. clean state) and a modified temporal-difference loss where the current Q-value uses the denoised state but the target Q-value remains clean. At test time, it employs hard RS—defining a binary indicator Q_h for whether a given action is the argmax—and estimates the smoothed Q-value via Monte Carlo, yielding a certified radius formula that is independent of the Q-network's output range (unlike CROP's mean-smoothing approach). S-PPO directly trains with Median Smoothing (Chiang et al., 2020) during trajectory collection, using the median of noisy policy outputs to obtain a robust smoothed policy, and can be combined with smoothed adversarial training (S-ATLA, S-PA-ATLA). The paper also introduces a Smoothed Attack that adds the same Gaussian smoothing noise to the perturbed state before computing the adversarial gradient, making it 2.62× more effective than classic PGD against S-DQN.

## Results

On Atari (Pong, Freeway, RoadRunner), S-DQN(Radial) achieves a clean reward of 0.929 and a robust reward of 0.735 under ℓ∞ PGD (ε=0.05), compared to 0.210 for RadialDQN and 0.240 for RadialDQN+RS (previous smoothed). On Mujoco (Walker, Hopper), S-PPO(SGLD) achieves a clean reward of 0.840 and worst-case reward of 0.604, versus 0.266 for SGLDPPO and 0.259 for SGLDPPO+RS. Overall, S-DQN and S-PPO outperform the best previous smoothed agents by 2.52× and 1.80× in reward under the strongest attack, and surpass the best non-smoothed robust agents by 2.70× and 1.58× respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against previous smoothed DQN agents (CROP: RadialDQN+RS, SADQN+RS, WocaRDQN+RS), S-DQN(Radial) achieves 0.735 vs. 0.240/0.227/0.218 under PGD ε=0.05 — a 2.5–3.4× improvement — while also maintaining a clean reward of 0.929 versus 0.25–0.35 for the smoothed baselines. Against the best non-smoothed robust DQN agents (RadialDQN, SADQN, WocaRDQN), S-DQN(Radial) achieves 0.735 vs. 0.210/0.185/0.216 under PGD ε=0.05, a 2.7–4.0× improvement. For PPO on Mujoco, S-PPO(SGLD) achieves a worst-case reward of 0.604 versus 0.266 for SGLDPPO and 0.259 for SGLDPPO+RS, a 2.3× improvement over both. S-PPO(S-ATLA) achieves the highest clean reward (0.989) but a lower worst-case reward (0.449) due to vulnerability to the Optimal Attack, while S-PPO(Vanilla) achieves 0.226 worst-case, still outperforming VanillaPPO (0.132) and VanillaPPO+RS (0.138). The paper also introduces a Smoothed Attack (S-PGD) that reduces S-DQN's reward by 61.8% on average, over 2.62× stronger than classic PGD (23.6%), revealing that prior evaluations using non-smoothed attacks significantly overestimated the robustness of smoothed agents.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the randomized smoothing framework from Cohen et al. (2019) and the denoised smoothing idea from Salman et al. (2020) into the DRL setting, where prior work (CROP, Wu et al. 2022; Policy Smoothing, Kumar et al. 2022) applied RS only at test time without any RS-aware training. The key departure is introducing RS directly into the training loop: S-DQN pairs a DnCNN denoiser with a reconstruction-plus-TD loss, while S-PPO uses median smoothing (Chiang et al., 2020) over the policy's mean and std outputs. The paper also departs from CROP's mean-smoothing certified radius by adopting a 'hard RS' formulation (binary Q-values) that eliminates the need to estimate the Q-network's output range, yielding a tighter and more stable guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that all previously published smoothed DRL agents suffer substantially lower clean rewards with no robustness gain over their non-smoothed counterparts—undermines the practical utility of the prior certifiable-robustness results in this area and motivates the need for RS-aware training. The introduction of the Smoothed Attack, which is ~1.9× more effective than standard PGD/PA-AD at degrading smoothed agents, also reveals that prior empirical robustness evaluations were overly optimistic. Limitations include evaluation on only three Atari games and two Mujoco tasks, and the approach inherits the computational overhead of Monte Carlo sampling at test time; open questions include whether the denoiser-plus-hard-RS recipe transfers to other RL algorithms (SAC, TD3) and to higher-dimensional or multi-agent settings.
