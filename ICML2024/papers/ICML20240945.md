## Problem

Deep transformers are plagued by gradient explosion/vanishing, rank collapse, and instability from high QK attention scores, yet prior signal-propagation analyses relied on simplifying assumptions (IID inputs, uncorrelated outputs, ignoring QK initialization, simplified non-linearities) that break down with real data. No existing framework provides a complete, end-to-end account of how moments propagate through every component of a transformer, making it impossible to design principled initialization schemes that stabilize arbitrarily deep models.

## Input / Output

The theory is modality-agnostic and operates on standard transformer inputs: text token sequences (with input correlation estimated from a Zipf distribution over the vocabulary), speech audio features (with empirically measured input variance and correlation), and image patches for ViT. Outputs are standard for each task—perplexity for MLM pre-training, accuracy for downstream QA (MNLI, QQP, RACE), BLEU for speech-to-text translation, and top-1 accuracy for ImageNet classification. The key unusual aspect is that the initialization scheme is applied at the weight level before any forward pass, requiring no profiling or extra normalization layers at inference time.

## Method

The paper derives closed-form expressions for the mean, variance, and cross-position correlation of both forward activations and backpropagated gradients through every transformer component (Embeddings, Linear, ReLU/GeLU, LayerNorm, Dropout, Softmax, Single-Head Attention), then composes them into block-level (Attention, FFN) and full-model recurrences for both Pre-LN and Post-LN architectures. Crucially, the derivations account for previously neglected effects: dropout-induced variance inflation, input correlation from repeated tokens, the non-zero mean of ReLU, and the exponential sensitivity of softmax backprop to QK variance. Building on these formulae, DeepScaleLM sets embedding variance to (1−p)/num_embd, initializes FFN weights to σ² = (1/d)·√((1−p)/2), iteratively computes attention block weight variances to enforce unit block output, and applies residual scaling with λ²+β²=1 and β²=k/N. This conserves unit output and gradient moments across all layers, enabling stable training of 768–1000 layer models that outperform shallower baselines with 5–15% fewer parameters across BERT, GPT, encoder-decoder speech, and ViT architectures.

## Results

On BERT MLM (Pile-CC), DeepScaleLM enables a 192-layer/256-dim model (160M params) to achieve a perplexity of 12.9, outperforming the vanilla 12-layer/1024-dim baseline (185M params, PPL 14.2) with fewer parameters, and a 96-layer/512-dim model (319M) to reach PPL 11.7 where the 24-layer baseline (336M) achieves only 13.2. On GPT (decoder-only), the 96-layer DSLM model achieves PPL 10.1 (Pre-LN) vs. 10.4 for the 24-layer baseline. Cross-modality gains are consistent: speech translation (MuST-C en→de) improves from BLEU 24.9 to 25.6, and ImageNet-1k top-1 accuracy rises from 76.5% to 77.2% (90-epoch) and 79.8% to 80.3% (300-epoch) with a 4× deeper ViT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepScaleLM outperforms all ten compared methods on both the 192/256 and 96/512 BERT configurations. The closest competitor is LayerScale (Touvron et al., 2021), which achieves PPL 13.2 at 192/256 and 14.4 at 96/512, still trailing DSLM by 0.3 and 2.7 points respectively. DeepNorm (Wang et al., 2024) and SkipInit (De & Smith, 2020) are next at 14.4/13.4 and 15.1/13.1, while DSInit (Zhang et al., 2019) diverges at 96/512 despite using a smaller asymptotic β. Four methods (ReZero, μP, Noci et al., and ADMIN at 192/256) fail to converge entirely at these depths. The paper attributes DSLM's advantage to its signal-propagation-derived initialization that sets the residual scaling constant exactly to 1 (β²=2/N), whereas prior methods use larger hidden constants in their O(N⁻²) or O(N⁻¹·⁵) scalings, reducing model sensitivity and expressivity at depth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the signal propagation analysis lineage from Xavier initialization (Glorot & Bengio, 2010) and ResNet depth-scaling theory (Balduzzi et al., 2017) to the full transformer architecture, providing closed-form moment expressions for every component (embeddings, linear layers, ReLU/GeLU, LayerNorm, dropout, softmax, attention) that prior transformer-specific analyses (Xu et al., 2019; Dong et al., 2021; Davis et al., 2021; Noci et al., 2022) had only partially covered under simplifying assumptions like IID inputs or ignored QK initialization. The key departure is a unified end-to-end framework that simultaneously accounts for input correlation, dropout, non-linearity, and QK variance, enabling the derivation of DeepScaleLM — a concrete initialization and residual-scaling scheme that conserves unit output and gradient moments across arbitrary depth. Unlike prior stabilization methods (DSInit, DeepNorm, LayerScale, SkipInit) that use empirically chosen or asymptotically smaller scaling constants, DeepScaleLM sets constants exactly via the derived moment equations, positioning it as a theoretically grounded alternative rather than another heuristic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the depth barrier in transformers is not fundamental but an initialization/scaling artifact, enabling 768-layer models to converge and outperform shallower baselines with fewer parameters across text, speech, and vision modalities. The theoretical framework is validated to within ~10% error on real data and even after 150k training steps, giving practitioners a principled tool for diagnosing and fixing instability rather than relying on trial-and-error. Open questions include whether the benefits persist at much larger parameter scales (experiments top out around 350M parameters), whether the theory fully captures dynamics after very long training (hundreds of millions of steps), and whether the moderate perplexity gains (1–3 points) translate to meaningful downstream improvements at frontier model scale. The finding that dropout alone prevents rank collapse, and that the scaling parameters can be folded into weights for inference, makes the approach practically attractive for adoption.
