## Problem

Existing first-order algorithms for comonotone min-max optimization and comonotone inclusion suffer from at least one of three limitations: sub-optimal O(1/√T) convergence rates, restriction to the unconstrained setting, or absence of point convergence guarantees. No prior single-loop algorithm simultaneously achieves the optimal O(1/T) last-iterate rate in the constrained setting and point convergence to the solution set for negatively comonotone problems (ρ < 0). This paper asks whether both properties can be attained together and provides an affirmative answer.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume a single-valued L-Lipschitz operator F: ℝⁿ → ℝⁿ and a maximally monotone set-valued operator A: ℝⁿ ⇉ ℝⁿ (e.g., the normal cone of a convex feasible set for constrained problems), together with an initial point z₀ and step size η. Convergence is measured via the tangent residual r_{F,A}^{tan}(z) = min_{c∈A(z)} ‖F(z)+c‖, which is strictly stronger than the gap function used in variational inequality literature and implies both natural residual bounds and approximate solution guarantees. The output is a last-iterate (not averaged) O(1/T) rate on the squared tangent residual, plus point convergence to the solution closest to z₀ in the monotone case.

## Method

The paper proposes two algorithms: composite-EAG (for ρ ≥ −1/(20L)) and composite-FEG (for ρ > −1/(2L)), both extending their unconstrained counterparts by introducing an auxiliary sequence c_k ∈ A(z_k) so that F(z_k)+c_k ∈ (F+A)(z_k), reducing the constrained update to the unconstrained EAG/FEG structure applied to the composite operator. Convergence is established via potential function arguments: a quadratic potential V_k (or U_k for FEG) is shown to be approximately non-increasing with increments bounded by O(1)·‖F(z_k)+c_k‖², yielding the O(1/T) last-iterate rate through a telescoping sum. Point convergence is proven by establishing an O(1/√k) merging path property between the explicit algorithm and the implicit Optimized Halpern's Method (OHM), whose convergence to the nearest solution is classical. The negative comonotonicity term is handled through a weighted combination of Young's inequality and carefully balanced coefficients, which is the key technical obstacle distinguishing this analysis from the monotone case.

## Results

The paper establishes that composite-EAG achieves an optimal O(1/T) last-iterate convergence rate (in terms of the tangent residual) for comonotone inclusion problems with ρ ≥ −1/(20L), and composite-FEG achieves the same O(1/T) rate for the wider range ρ > −1/(2L). Both rates match the known Ω(1/T) lower bound for first-order methods (Diakonikolas 2020; Yoon & Ryu 2021). Additionally, composite-EAG is shown to have point convergence to the solution set for ρ ≥ −1/(20L), and composite-FEG has point convergence in the monotone case (ρ = 0), making them the first single-loop first-order methods to simultaneously achieve optimal rates and point convergence in the constrained, negatively comonotone setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to EG+ and CEG+ (Diakonikolas et al. 2021; Pethick et al. 2022), which achieve only O(1/√T) rates, both composite-EAG and composite-FEG improve the convergence rate to the optimal O(1/T) while maintaining or extending the range of applicable ρ values. Against the double-loop Halpern iteration (Diakonikolas 2020; Kohlenbach 2022) and APG* (Yoon & Ryu 2022), which achieve O(log T / T), the proposed single-loop algorithms remove the logarithmic factor entirely. Relative to EAG (Yoon & Ryu 2021) and FEG (Lee & Kim 2021), which are limited to the unconstrained setting, the composite variants handle general maximally monotone operators A (capturing constraints and non-smooth regularizers) while preserving the O(1/T) rate. Compared to ARG (Cai & Zheng 2023b) and AOG (Cai & Zheng 2023a), which achieve O(1/T) but only for ρ ≥ −1/(60L) and ρ ≥ 0 respectively (with unknown point convergence), composite-EAG extends the range to ρ ≥ −1/(20L) and composite-FEG to ρ > −1/(2L), while both provide explicit point convergence guarantees. Against fOGDA (Sedlmayer et al. 2023), which handles only the monotone case (ρ ≥ 0), the proposed algorithms extend to the negatively comonotone regime, with composite-FEG covering the widest range of ρ among all single-loop algorithms studied in the literature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two key accelerated algorithms—EAG (Yoon & Ryu, 2021) and FEG (Lee & Kim, 2021)—from the unconstrained monotone setting to the constrained comonotone inclusion (CMI) framework, which unifies variational inequalities, min-max optimization, and multi-player games under a single operator-theoretic formulation. The point convergence proofs leverage the merging path (MP) property introduced by Yoon & Ryu (2022) and the convergence guarantees of the Optimized Halpern Method (Lieder, 2021), establishing that the explicit algorithms track the implicit OHM iterates. The technical novelty lies in handling the composite operator F+A via a carefully designed auxiliary sequence c_k ∈ A(z_k), which allows the unconstrained EAG/FEG analysis to transfer to the constrained setting with minimal structural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a notable gap in the comonotone optimization literature by being the first to simultaneously achieve the optimal O(1/T) last-iterate convergence rate and point convergence in the constrained, negatively comonotone setting—properties that prior methods could only provide in isolation or under stronger (monotone) assumptions. The composite-FEG algorithm covers the widest range of ρ > -1/2L among all single-loop methods, making it broadly applicable to structured nonconvex-nonconcave problems. Key open questions remain: the merging path rate degrades to O(1/√k) (vs. O(1/k²) in the unconstrained monotone case), and no single-loop algorithm yet handles ρ > -1/L without logarithmic factors, leaving room for further algorithmic improvements.
