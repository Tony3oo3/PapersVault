## Problem

SoftMax faces an inherent trade-off between sparsity and multi-modality: lowering temperature increases sparsity but collapses multi-modality (ignoring relevant secondary entries), while raising temperature preserves multi-modality but oversmooths by assigning non-negligible mass to irrelevant entries. Existing sparse SoftMax variants (SparseMax, EntMax, EvSoftMax) resolve sparsity at the cost of multi-modality and often require alternative loss functions, limiting their applicability. The paper formalizes this tension with quantitative metrics and proves it is fundamental to any single-temperature reweighting function.

## Input / Output

MultiMax takes the same K-dimensional input vector as standard SoftMax (attention logits from query-key dot products, or class scores from a classifier head) and outputs a K-dimensional probability distribution on the simplex. The key distinction is that the mapping is input-adaptive: different temperature parameters are applied to different value ranges of the input, so the same function produces sparser outputs for inputs with many small entries and more multi-modal outputs for inputs with several large entries. It serves as a direct drop-in replacement requiring no architectural changes or alternative losses.

## Method

MultiMax prepends a piecewise differentiable modulation function σ(x) to the standard SoftMax exponential, applying distinct learned temperatures to different input value ranges: a temperature t_b > 1 for entries below threshold b (amplifying differences among small entries to push them toward zero, increasing sparsity) and a temperature t_d < 1 for entries above threshold d (compressing differences among large entries to preserve multi-modality). The final formulation uses second-order polynomial terms for smoother transitions at the turning points, with continuity enforced by bias terms and subgradients defined at kinks. The paper proves that this piecewise modulation achieves strictly better Pareto efficiency than any single-temperature SoftMax on both sparsity and multi-modality simultaneously. All parameters (t_b, t_d, b, d per order) are learned end-to-end with negligible overhead: only 96 extra parameters and 0.37% extra FLOPs for a 12-layer DeiT-small, making it a universal drop-in replacement for SoftMax in attention layers, classifier heads, and generative model outputs.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, MultiMax applied to both attention and output layers of Deit-small improves top-1 accuracy from 80.4% (SoftMax) to 81.0%, a +0.6% gain. On WikiText-103 language modeling, MultiMax reduces test perplexity from 29.4 to 28.7 (−0.7). On IWSLT-2014 English-to-German machine translation, MultiMax achieves 34.7±0.07 BLEU, a +0.3 improvement over the SoftMax baseline of 34.4±0.07. The method adds only 96 parameters and ~0.37% extra FLOPs to a 12-layer Deit-small model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet-1K, MultiMax (81.0%) outperforms Top-k attention (Wang et al., 2022b; 80.6%) and Ev-SoftMax (Chen et al., 2021; 80.0%) under the same 300-epoch Deit-small protocol, and matches Patch Diversification (Gong et al., 2021b; 81.2%) and AttnScale (Wang et al., 2022c; 80.9%) when given the same extended training budgets (400/500 epochs). On WikiText-103, MultiMax (28.7 perplexity) beats Top-k attention (Gupta et al., 2021; 29.1) by 0.4. On IWSLT-2014 En-De translation, MultiMax (34.7 BLEU) substantially outperforms SparseMax (Martins & Astudillo, 2016; 28.7 BLEU) and slightly edges out EntMax-1.5 (Peters et al., 2019; 34.6 BLEU) and Ev-SoftMax (34.7 BLEU, tied). Notably, SparseMax and EntMax-1.5 were excluded from the ImageNet comparison entirely due to training instability and incompatibility with label smoothing, whereas MultiMax trains stably as a drop-in replacement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the line of sparse SoftMax variants (SparseMax, EntMax-α, Ev-SoftMax) that achieve sparsity by thresholding but sacrifice multi-modality and require alternative loss functions. It also contrasts with anti-over-smoothing remedies (Patch Diversification, AttnScale) that address symptoms without identifying the root cause in the attention distribution. Conceptually, MultiMax repurposes the piecewise-linear rectifier idea (generalizing ReLU/Leaky ReLU) as a learnable input-dependent temperature modulator for SoftMax, breaking the formally proven sparsity–multi-modality trade-off without introducing new hyperparameters or requiring non-standard losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MultiMax offers a theoretically grounded, drop-in replacement for SoftMax that simultaneously improves sparsity and multi-modality, addressing over-smoothing at its root cause rather than through auxiliary regularization. The modest but consistent gains across vision, language, and translation tasks (0.6% ImageNet, 0.7 perplexity, 0.3 BLEU) suggest broad applicability, and the learned per-layer modulator functions provide interpretable insight into where sparsity vs. multi-modality is needed. Limitations include a ~10–40% training-time overhead with current PyTorch implementations, and the theoretical proofs rely on conditions (e.g., ε bounds) that are not always guaranteed in practice. Open questions include whether the approach extends to reinforcement learning value/policy networks and Gumbel-SoftMax categorical sampling, as the authors suggest.
