## Problem

Classical Fourier Neural Networks (FNNs) used in implicit neural representations have signal representation capacity that grows only linearly with model size, requiring increasingly many parameters to capture high-frequency components of complex signals. Meanwhile, quantum machine learning lacks practical application scenarios where quantum circuits demonstrably outperform classical methods on real-world tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242521/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes low-dimensional coordinates (1D timestamps in [-1,1] for audio, 2D spatial coordinates for 32×32 images) as input and outputs continuous signal values (amplitude or grayscale/color). Because the output is a continuous function parameterized by the network, it can be evaluated on arbitrarily dense coordinate grids, enabling out-of-the-box superresolution (e.g., 64×64 or 128×128 from a 32×32 training grid). For image generation, a hypernetwork generates QIREN parameters from random noise vectors, and the resulting INR produces 32×32 face images.

## Method

QIREN is a hybrid quantum-classical architecture consisting of N hybrid layers (each with a Linear layer, BatchNorm, and a data re-uploading quantum circuit) followed by a final Linear layer. The core insight is that multi-layer multi-qubit data re-uploading circuits are equivalent to Fourier series whose frequency spectrum grows exponentially with circuit size (up to (3^d - 1)L + 1 per dimension), in contrast to the linear growth of classical FNNs. The Linear layers before each quantum circuit expand and adjust the frequency spectrum to cover important frequency bands, while BatchNorm stabilizes training by preventing vanishing gradients in the quantum non-linearity. The quantum circuit uses Rot gates and CNOT gates in parameter blocks and RZ gates in the encoding layer, with the data re-uploaded L times to increase circuit depth and spectral coverage.

## Results

On the image representation task, QIREN achieves MSE ×10⁻³ of 4.0 (Astronaut), 1.1 (Camera), and 1.5 (Coffee), reducing error by up to 34.8% compared to the SOTA model SIREN (9.0, 1.5, 2.3) while using fewer parameters (657 vs. 701). On image superresolution, QIREN achieves MSE ×10⁻³ of 24.3, 7.9, and 9.4, outperforming SIREN (77.0, 26.3, 15.7) by up to 69% and even surpassing interpolation methods. On image generation, QIREN achieves FID scores of 11.53 (FFHQ) and 11.78 (CelebA-HQ), outperforming SIREN (22.31, 20.97) and ReLU+RFF (15.01, 13.91) with the fewest parameters (1.13M). On sound representation, QIREN matches SIREN at MSE ×10⁻³ of 5.5 while saving 35.1% memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QIREN is compared against two SOTA implicit neural representation baselines: SIREN (Sitzmann et al., 2020) and ReLU-based MLP with Random Fourier Features (Mildenhall et al., 2021). On image representation, QIREN decisively outperforms SIREN, achieving 34.8% lower MSE on Astronaut (4.0 vs. 9.0) and 33.3% lower on Coffee (1.5 vs. 2.3), while also using fewer parameters (657 vs. 701). On image superresolution, QIREN's advantage over SIREN is even more dramatic, with up to 68.4% lower MSE on Astronaut (24.3 vs. 77.0) and 69.2% lower on Camera (7.9 vs. 26.3). Against ReLU+RFF, QIREN wins on all image representation images (e.g., 4.0 vs. 5.1 on Astronaut) and all superresolution images. On sound representation, QIREN ties SIREN at MSE 5.5 but achieves 35.1% memory savings versus SIREN's 30.9%. On image generation (Table 2), QIREN achieves the best FID of 11.53 on FFHQ and 11.78 on CelebA-HQ, beating SIREN (22.31, 20.97) by roughly 2× and ReLU+RFF (15.01, 13.91) by ~23%.

## Relation to Prior Work

QIREN unifies two previously separate research threads: Fourier Neural Networks (SIREN, ReLU+RFF) for implicit neural representations and data re-uploading quantum circuits for quantum machine learning. Building on Schuld et al.'s (2021) derivation that data re-uploading circuits are Fourier series, the paper generalizes this analysis from single-layer/single-qubit to multi-layer/multi-qubit circuits, showing that the quantum circuit's frequency spectrum grows exponentially with circuit size while classical FNNs grow only linearly. The architecture combines these quantum circuits with classical Linear and BatchNorm layers in a hybrid design, and the image generation experiments build on the StyleGAN2 framework with a hypernetwork-based INR generator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper provides one of the first concrete, well-motivated application scenarios for quantum machine learning in a practical task, addressing a long-standing gap in the QML community. However, all experiments are conducted on classical simulators (Pennylane, TorchQuantum) rather than real quantum hardware, and the claimed exponential quantum advantage is conditional on 'optimal conditions' and prior structure in the target function's Fourier coefficients. Open questions include whether the exponential spectrum advantage translates to meaningful gains on real quantum devices with noise, whether the approach scales to 3D scene representation or larger images, and whether the modest practical improvements (35% error reduction on small 32×32 images) persist at scale.
