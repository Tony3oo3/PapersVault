## Problem

The relationship between generalization and memorization in stochastic convex optimization (SCO) remains incompletely characterized. While conditional mutual information (CMI) was proposed as a refined information measure that avoids the dimension-dependence of input-output mutual information, it was an open question (posed by Livni) whether CMI can precisely capture the accuracy-information tradeoff for SCO learners. This paper provides a tight characterization, showing that achieving small excess error necessarily requires large CMI scaling with the optimal sample complexity.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes an SCO problem (Θ, Z, f) with a learning algorithm A and evaluates its CMI under the super-sample construction, where a binary vector U selects between two i.i.d. copies of each data point. The output is a distribution-dependent CMI lower bound—Ω(1/ε²) for Lipschitz-bounded SCO and Ω(1/ε) for strongly convex SCO—alongside a memorization game guarantee where an adversary, given only the model output, correctly identifies Ω(1/ε²) or Ω(1/ε) training samples with high confidence. The results also yield a negative implication: CMI-based generalization bounds become vacuous (order Ω(1)) for any algorithm with optimal sample complexity.

## Method

The proof constructs hard SCO instances with data on the hypercube {±1/√d}^d: for the Lipschitz-bounded case, the loss is f(θ,z) = −⟨θ,z⟩ over the unit ball; for the strongly convex case, it is f(θ,z) = −⟨θ,z⟩ + ½‖θ‖². A fingerprinting lemma (adapted from differential privacy) shows that any ε-learner's output must exhibit significant correlation with the training samples under a carefully chosen product distribution. The authors then define a set of 'fingerprinting' indices where the model output distinguishes between the two super-sample entries, and use Fano's inequality combined with sub-Gaussian concentration to convert the aggregate correlation into a CMI lower bound. The technique extends to improper (unconstrained) learners via orthogonal projection and data processing, and to individual-sample CMI, ruling out constant-size sample compression schemes for SCO.

## Results

The paper establishes tight CMI-accuracy tradeoffs for SCO: in the Lipschitz-bounded (CLB) setting, every ε-learner has CMI ≥ Ω(1/ε²), matching the optimal sample complexity O(1/ε²); under strong convexity (CSL), the bound improves to CMI ≥ Ω(1/ε), matching the O(1/ε) sample complexity. A memorization game shows an adversary can identify Ω(1/ε²) (CLB) or Ω(1/ε) (CSL) training samples with high confidence, meaning any sample-efficient learner must memorize a constant fraction of its training set. As a corollary, CMI-based generalization bounds are vacuous (order Ω(1)) for all optimal SCO algorithms, and no dimension-independent sample compression schemes exist for SCO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against empirical baselines, as it is a purely theoretical work. Its results extend and strengthen prior theoretical bounds in several ways. Compared to Livni [Liv23], who showed IOMI scales with dimension d for SCO, this paper shows CMI (a refined measure) is bounded below by Ω(1/ε²) or Ω(1/ε) independent of dimension, answering an open question from [Liv23]. Compared to Haghifam et al. [HRTSR+23], who showed CMI-based generalization bounds are vacuous specifically for gradient descent, this paper proves the same limitation holds for every ε-learner with optimal sample complexity, a strictly stronger statement. The fingerprinting-based proof technique builds on Bun, Ullman, and Vadhan [BUV14] and Steinke [Ste16] from the differential privacy literature, adapted here to the L² Lipschitz-bounded SCO setting where the harder unconstrained non-strongly convex case requires additional technical machinery beyond the ℓ∞ hypercube constructions used in prior privacy attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the conditional mutual information (CMI) framework of Steinke and Zakynthinou [SZ20] and the fingerprinting code techniques from differential privacy [BUV14; Ste16; KLSU19], applying them to the setting of stochastic convex optimization. It extends the IOMI lower bounds of Livni [Liv23] by replacing the coarser input-output mutual information with CMI, which more precisely captures per-sample memorization, and generalizes the CMI limitations shown for gradient descent by Haghifam et al. [HRTSR+23] to all ε-learners with dimension-independent sample complexity. The data distribution construction draws on the prior-over-parameters technique of Kamath et al. [KLSU19] from private learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central negative result—that CMI-based generalization bounds are vacuous for every optimal SCO learner—fundamentally limits the applicability of the CMI framework beyond binary classification and 0-1 loss, redirecting theoretical effort toward alternative information-theoretic measures. The memorization game result (an adversary can identify a constant fraction of training samples) provides a rigorous formalization of the empirical observation that overparameterized models memorize, and rules out constant-size sample compression schemes for SCO, a stark contrast with VC classes. Open questions include whether the dimension requirement d ≥ Ω(n⁴ log n) is tight, whether the results extend to non-convex settings relevant to deep learning, and whether alternative information measures (e.g., hypothesis-conditioned CMI) can close the gap.
