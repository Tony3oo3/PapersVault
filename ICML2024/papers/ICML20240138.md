## Problem

Controlling the output of pre-trained diffusion or flow-matching models for inverse problems, conditional generation, and sample editing without task-specific retraining remains challenging. Existing training-free guidance methods rely on strong assumptions (e.g., linearity of the observation operator) or lack a principled explanation for why they work. The paper asks: what happens when you simply differentiate an arbitrary loss through the ODE sampling process with respect to the initial noise, and can this be understood as an implicit data-manifold projection?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240138/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen flow-matching or diffusion model (velocity field u_t), a differentiable cost function L (encoding an inverse problem constraint like H(x)≈y, or a conditional target like F(x)≈c), and optionally a corrupted observation y for initialization. It outputs a single generated sample x(1) that minimizes L while remaining in-distribution under the flow model. For image tasks, inputs are 128×128 RGB images (ImageNet-128) or latent representations of T2I models (COCO); for audio, EnCodec latent codes at 32kHz; for molecules, 9-dimensional per-atom feature vectors. The optimization runs for 5–15 minutes per sample on a V100 GPU, with runtimes up to 30–40 minutes for large T2I and T2-audio models.

## Method

D-Flow formulates controlled generation as source-point optimization: min_{x_0} L(x(1)), where x(1) is obtained by solving the ODE ẋ(t)=u_t(x(t)) from initial condition x(0)=x_0. The key theoretical contribution (Theorem 4.2) shows that for models trained with Affine Gaussian Probability Paths, the Jacobian D_{x_0}x(1) equals σ_1 times a time-ordered exponential of the conditional data covariance Var_{1|t}(x(t)), meaning the backpropagated gradient ∇_{x_0}L is implicitly projected onto high-variance data directions at every time step. This data-manifold projection acts as a free implicit regularization that keeps intermediate samples in-distribution without any explicit prior term. Practically, the method uses LBFGS with line search for the outer optimization, a midpoint ODE solver with 6 function evaluations (via torchdiffeq with gradient checkpointing), and optionally a χ^d regularization on the source norm for noisy settings. The framework is entirely training-free and requires only a differentiable cost, making it applicable to both linear and non-linear inverse problems, conditional molecule generation, and audio/image inpainting.

## Results

On face-blurred ImageNet-128 linear inverse problems (noiseless), D-Flow achieves FID of 4.14 (inpainting), 2.50 (super-resolution ×2), and 2.37 (Gaussian deblur), with PSNR of 37.67, 34.88, and 39.47 respectively, outperforming all baselines on most metrics. In the noisy setting (σ_y=0.05), D-Flow maintains strong performance (FID 4.76/4.26/5.35) while RED-Diff degrades severely (FID 14.63/10.54/21.43). For conditional molecule generation on QM9, D-Flow achieves MAE of 1.39 (polarizability), 344 meV (Δε), 182 meV (ε_HOMO), and 0.300 D (dipole moment), substantially outperforming the best baseline (GeoLDM) by 40–50% across all six properties. On audio super-resolution (×2), D-Flow achieves FAD 0.22 and PSNR 44.51 in-domain, versus RED-Diff's FAD 0.93 and PSNR 35.27.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D-Flow outperforms all three image inverse-problem baselines (ΠGDM, OT-ODE, RED-Diff) on FID for inpainting and super-resolution, and on LPIPS/PSNR/SSIM for Gaussian deblur, though RED-Diff achieves a slightly lower FID (1.62 vs 2.37) on noiseless deblur. The most striking advantage is in the noisy setting: RED-Diff's FID degrades from 5.40 to 14.63 on inpainting, while D-Flow only goes from 4.14 to 4.76, indicating the implicit data-manifold regularization is far more robust to observation noise. For conditional molecule generation on QM9, D-Flow using a single unconditional flow-matching prior beats all three task-specific conditional baselines (EDM, EquiFM, GeoLDM) by 40–50% MAE on every property (e.g., 182 vs 337–356 meV for ε_HOMO; 0.300 vs 1.025–1.111 D for dipole moment), despite the baselines each using a separately trained conditional model. On audio tasks, D-Flow consistently achieves the lowest FAD across all inpainting and super-resolution settings (e.g., FAD 0.22 vs 0.93 for in-domain SR×2) and substantially higher PSNR in super-resolution (44.51 vs 35.27 for ×2), while RED-Diff produces visible artifacts. The one area where D-Flow trades off is molecule stability: D-Flow achieves ~59% molecule stability vs EDM's 82.1%, though the MAE gap is not attributable to unstable molecules (stable-only MAE remains SOTA).

## Relation to Prior Work

D-Flow generalizes the idea of differentiating through a generative model's sampling process—previously applied to GANs (Bora et al., 2017), normalizing flows (Asim et al., 2020), and discrete diffusion solvers (Wallace et al., 2023; Samuel et al., 2023b)—to the broader class of continuous flow models trained with Affine Gaussian Probability Paths. The key conceptual departure is the theoretical characterization (Theorem 4.2) showing that the Jacobian of the ODE solution with respect to the source point is a time-ordered product of data covariance matrices, which explains why the gradient implicitly projects onto the data manifold. This positions D-Flow alongside variational approaches like RED-Diff (Mardani et al., 2023) but with a cleaner formulation that requires no explicit score-matching regularization and a principled explanation for its inductive bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's simplicity (optimize a single source noise vector with LBFGS) and its consistent SOTA performance across images, audio, and molecules without task-specific tuning make it an attractive general-purpose tool for controlled generation. The theoretical result connecting the implicit regularization to the data covariance structure provides a principled understanding that could guide future work on cheaper approximations of the same bias. The primary limitation is computational cost (5–15 minutes per sample on ImageNet-128), which the authors acknowledge leaves significant room for speedup; an open question is whether the covariance-projection property can be exploited with fewer solver steps or surrogate gradients while preserving the manifold-preserving behavior.
