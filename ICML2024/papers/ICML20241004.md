## Problem

Variational logistic regression and GP classification require computing the intractable expectation E_X[log(1+exp(X))], and existing tractable approximations (Jaakkola-Jordan quadratic bound, Polya-Gamma latent variables) introduce additional variational parameters or extend the variational family, which is known to underestimate posterior variance. This paper asks whether a tighter bound can be derived that avoids these extra parameters entirely while remaining computationally tractable.

## Input / Output

For logistic regression the method consumes n binary labels and p-dimensional covariate vectors; for sparse GP classification it additionally uses M inducing points (M=50 in experiments) with an ARD kernel. The output is a Gaussian variational posterior q(β) or q(u) from which predictive probabilities and 95% credible intervals are derived. The bound is truncated at order l (l=12 in all experiments, yielding <1% relative error), and optimization uses reparameterized gradient descent with convergence monitored via relative ELBO change (threshold 10⁻⁶ to 10⁻⁸).

## Method

The core contribution is a new upper bound η_l(θ,τ) on E_X[log(1+exp(X))] for X~N(θ,τ²), derived by splitting the integral at the mean and applying the truncated Maclaurin series of log(1+x) to the residual terms. The bound is a sum of 2l−1 terms involving exponentials and normal CDFs, requires no additional variational parameters (unlike Jaakkola-Jordan or Polya-Gamma), and is exact in the limit l→∞ with terms decaying as O(1/k²). Applied to variational logistic regression, it replaces the intractable softplus expectation in the ELBO with η_l evaluated at θ_i = x_iᵀμ and τ_i² = x_iᵀΣx_i; for sparse GP classification it is applied to the marginal predictive distribution at each data point. The resulting surrogate ELBO is maximized via gradient descent with natural-gradient-friendly reparameterizations (Σ=LLᵀ for regression; θ=Σ⁻¹μ, Θ=−½Σ⁻¹ for GP), yielding a method (VI-PER) that matches Monte Carlo accuracy while being an order of magnitude faster and avoiding the variance underestimation of Polya-Gamma.

## Results

On the logistic regression simulation (n=1000, p=25, mean-field Q family), VI-PER achieves an ELBO of −264, KL_MC of 0.0059, coverage of 0.906, and CI width of 2.44, closely matching the Monte Carlo ground truth (VI-MC: ELBO −264, coverage 0.904, CI width 2.42) while running ~12× faster (12 s vs 2 m 24 s). On the larger n=10,000 setting, VI-PER maintains KL_MC of 0.034 and coverage of 0.918 versus VI-PG's KL_MC of 4.89 and coverage of 0.761. On the soil liquefaction dataset (1.8M observations), VI-PER achieves AUC 0.858 with CI width 0.0335, matching VI-MC, while VI-PG underestimates uncertainty by a factor of 5 (CI width 0.0065).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VI-PG (the Polya-Gamma / Jaakkola–Jordan bound), VI-PER substantially outperforms on all uncertainty metrics: KL_MC drops from 5.36 to 0.0059 (n=1k) and from 4.89 to 0.034 (n=10k), coverage improves from 0.724 to 0.906, and CI width increases from 1.67 to 2.44, indicating VI-PG severely underestimates posterior variance. Both methods achieve identical AUC (0.977), so the predictive accuracy is comparable, but VI-PER provides far more calibrated uncertainty at a modest runtime cost (12 s vs 0.12 s). Against MCMC (Hamiltonian Monte Carlo, 30k iterations), VI-PER is within 0.001 on MSE (0.493 vs 0.492) and within 0.04 on coverage (0.906 vs 0.948), while running roughly 50× faster (12 s vs 10 m 46 s). On the GP classification illustrative example, VI-PER's KL_MC of 2.78 is an order of magnitude lower than VI-PG's 37.5, and its CI width (4.31) closely tracks VI-MC (4.22) versus VI-PG's underestimated 3.13. On the 1.8M-observation soil liquefaction dataset, VI-PER matches VI-MC on AUC (0.858 vs 0.857) and CI width (0.0335 vs 0.0335), while VI-PG's CI width is only 0.0065—a 5× underestimation that would be dangerous for risk assessment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Jaakkola & Jordan (2000) quadratic bound for the softplus function and its probabilistic reinterpretation via the Polya-Gamma (PG) formulation (Polson et al., 2013; Durante & Rigon, 2019), which has become the standard tractable approximation in variational logistic regression and GP classification. The key departure is that the proposed bound, derived from the Maclaurin series of log(1+x), requires no additional variational parameters and no extension of the variational family, unlike PG which introduces n extra parameters (one per data point). The sparse GP classification framework follows Hensman et al. (2015) and Titsias (2009), with the new bound applied at the level of the expected log-likelihood term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the well-documented variance underestimation problem in mean-field variational Bayes, showing that the PG/Jaakkola-Jordan bound systematically produces overly narrow credible intervals while the proposed bound recovers uncertainty levels consistent with Monte Carlo and MCMC. The soil liquefaction application (1.8M observations) demonstrates practical relevance for risk assessment where calibrated uncertainty is critical. Limitations include the truncation error inherent in the finite series and the standard variational family constraint (Gaussian), which may fail for multimodal or heavy-tailed posteriors. Open directions include extension to Bayesian neural networks, contextual bandits, and Bayesian optimization where accurate posterior uncertainty drives decision-making.
