## Problem

Dynamic survival analysis requires modelling individual-specific event intensities from both static covariates and irregularly sampled longitudinal features, but existing approaches either rely on parametric joint models that fail to scale beyond a handful of time channels, or on deep architectures (RNN+attention, ODE-RNN) that lack theoretical learning guarantees. No prior framework provides a principled, theoretically grounded parameterization of the intensity that simultaneously handles irregular sampling, yields interpretable connections to Cox models, and admits explicit bias-variance decompositions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes static features W^i ∈ R^s, an irregularly sampled time series X^i = {(X^i(t_k), t_k)}_{k=1}^K on a non-uniform grid D ⊂ [0,τ], and an at-risk indicator Y^i. The time series is embedded via piecewise constant interpolation (to avoid future information leakage) and augmented with a time channel before computing signatures. The output is a continuous-time individual-specific intensity λ^i(t) = exp(z^i(t) + β^T W^i), from which the conditional survival probability r^i_θ(t, δt) = P(T^i > t+δt | T^i > t, X^i_{s≤t}, W^i) is derived for ranking and calibration evaluation at arbitrary prediction horizons.

## Method

The core model parameterizes the log-intensity as z^i_*(t) + β^T W^i, where the latent state z^i_*(t) solves a controlled differential equation dz = G_*(z)^T dx^i(t) driven by the individual's time series, with a shared vector field G_* across individuals. Two estimators are proposed: (1) an NCDE-based estimator that approximates G_* with a feed-forward neural vector field and updates the latent state via a controlled ResNet recurrence on the piecewise-constant embedding; and (2) CoxSig, which exploits the CDE-to-signature linearization to write z^i_*(t) ≈ α^T S_N(x^i_{[0,t]}), reducing the model to a linear regression in truncated signature features with an elastic-net penalty. The signature formulation reveals that the model is a generalized Cox model with time-varying covariates (the time-only signature terms recover the log baseline hazard), and the paper derives a full risk bound decomposing discretization bias (proportional to grid spacing |D|), approximation bias (depending on smoothness of G_*), and a classical n^{-1/2} variance term. A variant CoxSig+ augments the static features with the first observed time series value to compensate for the translation invariance of signatures.

## Results

On the partially observed SDE (hitting time) dataset, CoxSig achieves an averaged C-Index of 0.857 and IBS of 0.091, substantially outperforming Dynamic DeepHit (0.545 / 0.094) and SurvLatent ODE (0.621 / 0.253). On the NASA C-MAPSS predictive maintenance dataset, CoxSig+ reaches 0.867 C-Index and 0.154 IBS, again beating DDH (0.813 / 0.156) and SLODE (0.438 / 0.145). On the real-world Califrais churn dataset, CoxSig+ attains 0.751 C-Index and 0.129 IBS versus DDH's 0.570 / 0.139. The notable exception is the low-dimensional Tumor Growth dataset (d=2), where DDH achieves the best C-Index (0.941) and NCDE the best IBS (0.130), consistent with the known weakness of signatures on very low-dimensional paths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Dynamic DeepHit (DDH), CoxSig/CoxSig+ achieve substantially higher C-Index on three of four datasets: OU (0.857 vs 0.545), NASA (0.858/0.867 vs 0.813), and Califrais (0.741/0.751 vs 0.570), while DDH wins on the low-dimensional Tumor dataset (0.941 vs 0.696/0.797). On IBS, CoxSig matches or beats DDH on OU, NASA, and Califrais but is slightly worse on Tumor (0.138 vs 0.133). Against SurvLatent ODE (SLODE), CoxSig dominates on C-Index across all four datasets (e.g., 0.857 vs 0.621 on OU, 0.858 vs 0.438 on NASA, 0.741 vs 0.542 on Califrais) and on IBS for OU (0.091 vs 0.253) and Califrais (0.130 vs 0.193), though SLODE edges out CoxSig on IBS for Tumor (0.136 vs 0.138) and NASA (0.145 vs 0.154). The NCDE variant of the proposed model consistently underperforms both baselines, even falling below random (C-Index ≈ 0.5) on NASA, highlighting that the signature-based linearization is the key contribution rather than the CDE formulation itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads: neural controlled differential equations (Kidger et al., 2020) for embedding irregular time series, and the CDE-to-signature linearization from rough path theory (Fermanian et al., 2021; Bleistein et al., 2023). It departs from the dominant deep-learning approaches to dynamic survival analysis (Dynamic DeepHit's RNN+attention, SurvLatent ODE's ODE-RNN) by parameterizing the counting-process intensity as the solution to a CDE driven by the observed time series, then showing this CDE admits a linear representation in the signature space. The resulting CoxSig estimator is positioned as a generalized Cox model with time-varying covariates, connecting the classical survival-analysis literature to modern path-based feature extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides rare theoretical learning guarantees (a full bias-variance decomposition with explicit discretization and approximation terms) for a survival-analysis model with time-dependent covariates, a gap that deep-learning methods in this area have largely left unaddressed. Practically, CoxSig offers a parsimonious, interpretable alternative to deep architectures that consistently outperforms them across finance, healthcare, and supply-chain datasets. Key limitations remain: signatures scale exponentially with time-series dimension, performance degrades on low-dimensional data, and the framework is restricted to single-event survival rather than general counting processes or competing risks—extensions the authors flag as future work.
