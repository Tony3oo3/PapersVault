## Problem

Riemannian HMC (RHMC) suffers from severe numerical instability because position-dependent momentum distributions produce large momentum realizations in high-curvature regions, leading to divergent transitions that waste computation and degrade sampling efficiency. While prior work addressed this by substituting more robust metrics (e.g., SoftAbs for Fisher information), the fundamental issue of unbounded particle velocity in high-curvature regions remained unresolved. This paper tackles the problem from the dynamics side by designing Hamiltonian systems whose particle speeds are intrinsically upper-bounded in a position-dependent manner.

## Input / Output

The method requires access to the unnormalized log-density of the target and its Hessian (or diagonal approximation for high-dimensional targets), from which the SoftAbs metric G_q is computed at each position. Hyperparameters include rest mass m, speed of light c, and leapfrog step size ε. The output is a sequence of MCMC samples from the target distribution, with the Hamiltonian trajectory optionally used for either Metropolis-Hastings endpoint acceptance or multinomial sampling across all intermediate phase points. For high-dimensional problems, only the diagonal of the Hessian is computed, reducing complexity from O(d³) to O(d²).

## Method

GR-HMC replaces the Gaussian kinetic energy of RHMC with a general relativistic kinetic energy K̃_G(q,p) = mc²√(pᵀG_q⁻¹p/(m²c²)+1) + ½log det(G_q), which introduces a position-dependent 'general relativistic mass' M_G that scales the velocity inversely, yielding local speed upper bounds that shrink in high-curvature regions. This effectively acts as a physics-motivated adaptive step size: the larger the relativistic mass at a given position, the smaller the effective integration step. A key technical contribution is a Box-Muller-based sampler for the multivariate relativistic momentum distribution that avoids the dimension-wise independence assumption of prior work, reducing sampling to a single univariate rejection sample for the radial component plus uniform angular draws. Efficient derivative computation reuses cache-able intermediate terms from the SoftAbs metric, and a diagonal Hessian approximation extends the method to medium- and high-dimensional targets.

## Results

On the 26D hierarchical Bayesian logistic regression (HBLR) problem, GR-HMC achieves ESS of 147.5 at ε=0.06, outperforming RHMC (105.8) and SR-HMC (15.6). On the 64D log-Gaussian Cox point process (logGCPP), GR-HMC reaches ESS of 4764.1 at ε=0.3, matching RHMC (4756.8) while SR-HMC achieves 3794.3. GR-HMC is dramatically more stable than RHMC: at ε=0.1 on 2D Neal's funnel, RHMC has 44.6% divergent simulations versus only 1.8% for GR-HMC. GR-HMC maintains high ESS across a broad range of step sizes (e.g., 4446–4764 for ε=0.2–0.4 on logGCPP), whereas RHMC collapses to ESS of 4.7 at ε=0.4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GR-HMC outperforms RHMC (Girolami & Calderhead, 2011) on ESS for most step sizes on both real-world problems: on HBLR it wins at ε=0.04–0.08 (e.g., 147.5 vs. 105.8 at ε=0.06) but loses at ε=0.1 (217.8 vs. 258.7), where the posterior is simple enough that RHMC's numerical errors are minimal. On logGCPP, GR-HMC dominates at ε=0.2–0.4 (4446–4764 vs. 1491–4757 for RHMC), and critically remains stable at ε=0.4 where RHMC collapses to ESS 4.7. GR-HMC also dramatically outperforms RHMC in numerical stability: at ε=0.1 on Neal's funnel, RHMC has 44.6% divergent trajectories versus 1.8% for GR-HMC. Against SR-HMC (Lu et al., 2017), GR-HMC consistently achieves higher ESS across all tested step sizes on both problems (e.g., 147.5 vs. 15.6 on HBLR at ε=0.06; 4764 vs. 3794 on logGCPP at ε=0.3), confirming that the position-dependent relativistic momentum is superior to the position-independent one. Standard HMC (Neal, 2011) is not directly compared in Table 2 but is shown in Table 1 to have higher divergence rates than both SR-HMC and GR-HMC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate extensions of Hamiltonian Monte Carlo: Riemannian HMC (Girolami & Calderhead, 2011), which introduces position-dependent momentum via a Riemannian metric, and Relativistic Monte Carlo (Lu et al., 2017), which bounds particle velocity using a special-relativistic kinetic energy. The key conceptual move is replacing the Gaussian kinetic energy in RHMC with the relativistic one, yielding position-dependent velocity upper bounds that naturally slow particles in high-curvature regions. The paper also departs from Lu et al. (2017) by removing their dimension-wise independence assumption and providing a Box-Muller-based sampler for the full multivariate relativistic momentum distribution, and it builds on Betancourt's (2013) SoftAbs metric and its cache-able derivative computations for practical implementation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GR-HMC addresses the primary practical barrier to using Riemannian HMC—numerical instability from large momentum realizations in high-curvature regions—by construction rather than by ad-hoc step-size tuning, reducing divergences from ~45% to ~2% at comparable step sizes. The method is orthogonal to integration-time control strategies (NUTS, randomized integration time), so it can be combined with them for further gains. However, the cubic Hessian cost remains a bottleneck, and the diagonal approximation needed for high-dimensional targets sacrifices some of the geometric adaptivity that motivates the Riemannian approach; future work on iterative Hessian approximations (e.g., L-BFGS) is needed to make the method competitive at scale.
