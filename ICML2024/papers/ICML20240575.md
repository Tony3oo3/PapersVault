## Problem

Zero-shot transfer from foundation models like CLIP still requires human-provided class descriptions to solve a new task, and prior unsupervised approaches either rely on expensive task-specific representation learning (HUME) or simple clustering on frozen features that performs poorly. The paper asks whether the representations of foundation models alone can uncover the underlying human labeling of a dataset in a fully unsupervised manner, given only the number of classes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240575/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TURTLE takes a dataset of unlabeled samples (images or single video frames) along with the known number of classes C, and pre-computed frozen representations from one or more foundation models (e.g., CLIP ViT-L/14 and DINOv2 ViT-g/14). No data augmentations or task-specific fine-tuning of representations are used; all representations are fixed throughout training. The output is a soft assignment of each sample to one of C clusters, which is matched to ground-truth labels via the Hungarian algorithm for evaluation.

## Method

TURTLE's core insight is that optimizing a generalization-based bilevel objective implicitly maximizes the margin of linear classifiers in the representation space, so the method searches for the labeling that induces maximal-margin classifiers simultaneously across K foundation model representation spaces. The task encoder is an ensemble of linear models (one per representation space) with softmax activations, and the objective sums cross-entropy losses of M-step-trained linear classifiers in each space. A key efficiency trick discards the expensive Jacobian term in the hypergradient by using the same data for inner and outer levels, reducing the problem to a first-order alternating optimization: fix the task encoder, train each linear classifier for M steps, then update the task encoder. Entropy regularization on each component's empirical label distribution prevents degenerate single-cluster solutions, and the entire procedure requires no task-specific representation learning.

## Results

TURTLE 2-spaces (CLIP ViT-L/14 + DINOv2) achieves 72.9% accuracy on ImageNet, setting a new state-of-the-art for unsupervised transfer. Across 26 datasets, TURTLE 2-spaces outperforms CLIP zero-shot by 4–9% absolute accuracy depending on backbone size, while TURTLE 1-space matches CLIP zero-shot on average using the same representation space. TURTLE also achieves a 10× training speedup over HUME, completing ImageNet training in under 5 minutes. On the 12-dataset prompt tuning benchmark, TURTLE reaches 71.2% average accuracy, surpassing the best unsupervised prompt tuning baseline (GDA, 63.5%) by 7.7 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the prior state-of-the-art unsupervised method HUME, TURTLE wins on all 26 datasets, with particularly large gains of 23% absolute on MNIST (98.0% vs. 75.3%) and 11% on Birdsnap (67.8% vs. 57.3%), while also achieving a 10× training speedup (4.8 min vs. 285 min on ImageNet). Compared to CLIP zero-shot transfer, TURTLE 2-spaces surpasses it on all model sizes (by 9%, 7%, and 4% absolute for ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14), and TURTLE 1-space matches CLIP zero-shot on average across 26 datasets despite using no class descriptions. Against unsupervised prompt tuning baselines (UPL, POUF, GDA), TURTLE achieves 7.7–12.2% absolute improvement in average accuracy, with standout gains of 27% on Flowers102 and 41% on EuroSAT. Against supervised linear probe (the upper bound), TURTLE 1-space ViT-L/14 approaches the supervised performance on 5 out of 26 datasets (STL10, CIFAR10, Flowers102, Food101, HatefulMemes) within 3 points, with a strong Pearson correlation of ρ=0.87 between unsupervised and supervised accuracy. TURTLE also outperforms simple K-Means clustering on all datasets, confirming that the margin-maximization objective provides substantially better guidance than distance-based clustering.

## Relation to Prior Work

TURTLE directly extends HUME (Gadetsky & Brbić, 2023), which introduced a generalization-based objective for inferring human labelings from pre-trained representations. The key departure is replacing HUME's task-specific self-supervised representations with frozen foundation model representations and simultaneously maximizing margins across multiple representation spaces, eliminating the need for expensive per-dataset pre-training. The theoretical analysis draws on the implicit bias literature (Soudry et al., 2018; Ji & Telgarsky, 2019) to show that the generalization-based objective implicitly enforces the maximum margin principle, connecting unsupervised label inference to classical SVM theory. Conceptually, TURTLE also unifies and supersedes earlier maximum margin clustering approaches (Xu et al., 2004) by providing a single framework applicable to any number of classes and representation spaces via efficient first-order bilevel optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that a fully unsupervised method requiring only the number of classes can match or exceed zero-shot CLIP (which requires human-written class descriptions) across 26 diverse datasets—challenges the assumption that some form of human linguistic guidance is necessary for effective transfer. This opens the door to fully autonomous task discovery in domains where class descriptions are unavailable or impractical to obtain. The 10× speedup over HUME (under 5 minutes on ImageNet) makes the approach practically deployable. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-vision modalities, how it behaves with weaker or domain-specific foundation models, and whether the multi-space margin-maximization principle can be further exploited as the foundation model landscape continues to evolve.
