## Problem

Existing failure detection in deep regression models relies on epistemic uncertainty as a surrogate for expected risk, but the paper demonstrates that uncertainty alone is insufficient: low-uncertainty regions can still carry high risk due to feature heterogeneity, and out-of-support regions may exhibit low uncertainty despite moderate risk. The paper addresses the need for a principled framework that accurately organizes regression test samples into distinct risk regimes without requiring rigid failure definitions or additional calibration data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240847/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a test sample (d-dimensional vector or 3-channel image) together with a pre-trained anchored model and a small batch of training anchors (e.g., 100 samples). It produces two scalar scores per sample — a predictive uncertainty (standard deviation across K anchor-conditioned predictions) and a manifold non-conformity score (max L1 discrepancy in reverse-anchoring predictions) — which are then binned via conditional quantile ranges (25th/75th percentiles) to assign each sample to one of four risk regimes: ID, Low, Moderate, or High. No calibration set or auxiliary error predictor is required at inference time.

## Method

PAGER builds on anchored training (stochastic data centering), where each input x is reparameterized as a tuple [r, x−r] with a randomly sampled anchor r from the training set, forcing the model to produce consistent predictions regardless of anchor choice. Forward anchoring yields epistemic uncertainty by marginalizing predictions over K anchors. The key novelty is reverse anchoring: swapping the roles of query and anchor so that F([x_t, r−x_t]) predicts the known target y_r, and the maximum L1 discrepancy across training samples defines the manifold non-conformity score (Score_1). A second score (Score_2) optimizes a modified query x̄ to match the anchor's target while regularizing x̄ to stay on the data manifold via an anchored autoencoder with cyclical consistency, better separating moderate from high risk. The two scores are jointly binned into a 3×3 grid that maps to four risk regimes, enabling failure characterization without any post-hoc calibration step.

## Results

On the HD regression benchmarks (Gaps setting, Table 2), PAGER(Score1) achieves the lowest FN on 7 of 9 datasets (e.g., 0.0 on NO2, 0.75 on Airfoil, 0.91 on Ailerons) and the lowest FP on 7 of 9 datasets (e.g., 3.36 on Camel, 3.56 on Airfoil, 4.05 on Ailerons), compared to DEUP and DataSUITE. PAGER(Score2) further reduces confusion metrics, achieving C_low as low as 6.15 (NO2) and C_high as low as 1.65 (Ailerons), versus DEUP's 19.32 and 23.94 on the same datasets. On the 1D benchmarks (Table 1), PAGER(Score1) achieves FN of 0.0 on f2 and f4, and FP of 0.0 on f2, while PAGER(Score2) achieves the best overall FN (4.79 on f1) and FP (1.33 on f1). The paper reports >50% reduction in FN and FP over baselines on HD regression benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAGER is compared against two baselines: DEUP (Lahlou et al., 2023), which uses a post-hoc auxiliary error predictor trained on curated calibration data to estimate epistemic uncertainty as a risk surrogate, and DataSUITE (Seedat et al., 2022), a task-agnostic method that scores data quality via feature inconsistency with the training distribution. PAGER(Score1) decisively outperforms both baselines on FN across nearly all HD regression datasets (e.g., 0.0 vs. 2.27 on NO2, 0.75 vs. 8.81 on Airfoil vs. DEUP; 0.91 vs. 14.39 on Ailerons), and on FP (e.g., 3.36 vs. 17.48 on Camel vs. DEUP). DataSUITE is particularly weak on FN for low-dimensional datasets (21.74 on Camel, 19.69 on Levy) and on high-dimensional DTI (29.18), while DEUP struggles with C_low confusion (50.59 on Camel, 34.67 on Levy). PAGER(Score2) further improves C_low and C_high over Score1 at the cost of higher inference time (40.9s vs. 1.55s per 1000 samples), making Score1 preferable for online/real-time settings and Score2 for scenarios requiring precise moderate-vs-high risk separation or under test-time corruptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAGER builds directly on the anchored training / stochastic data centering methodology of Thiagarajan et al. (2022, Δ-UQ) and the transductive extrapolation framework of Netanyahu et al. (2023), repurposing the anchor-reparameterization trick in a novel 'reverse anchoring' direction to estimate manifold non-conformity without auxiliary models. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant paradigm of using epistemic uncertainty alone (DEUP) or input-space non-conformity alone (DataSUITE) for failure detection, arguing that neither is sufficient in regression and proposing their principled unification through a single anchored model. The risk-regime taxonomy (ID / Low / Moderate / High) also extends the binary failure/non-failure framing common in classification-based detectors to a graded, application-agnostic characterization suited to continuous-valued prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that epistemic uncertainty is necessary but insufficient for regression failure detection challenges a widely adopted assumption and motivates a more structured, multi-signal approach to safe deployment in high-stakes domains (healthcare, physical sciences, RL). The proposed evaluation metrics (FN, FP, C_low, C_high) offer a more holistic assessment than standard Spearman-correlation or top-k error metrics, which could influence how the community benchmarks failure detectors going forward. Open questions include the computational cost of Score_2 (≈26× slower than Score_1), the dependence on retraining with anchoring (though a regression-head-only variant mitigates this), and whether the fixed quantile-based binning generalizes to highly imbalanced or multi-modal risk distributions.
