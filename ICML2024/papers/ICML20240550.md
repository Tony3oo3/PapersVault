## Problem

Value-based RL methods such as Q-learning, DQN, and DDQN require evaluating the max or arg max over all n actions at every step, yielding O(n) per-step complexity that becomes prohibitive for large discrete action spaces. This paper asks whether this linear bottleneck can be reduced to sublinear (logarithmic) complexity while preserving convergence guarantees and competitive performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240550/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an environment state (discrete or continuous) and the current Q-function as input, and produces a discrete action via stoch arg max and an updated Q-value via stoch max. Rather than scoring all n actions, only a union set C = R ∪ M of at most 2⌈log(n)⌉ actions is evaluated: R is a uniformly random subset of actions, and M is a memory subset of previously exploited actions (the 2 most recent per state for discrete states, or a random ⌈log(n)⌉-sized subset from the replay buffer for continuous states). This makes the per-step cost logarithmic in the action-space size regardless of whether the state space is tabular or represented by a neural network.

## Method

The core contribution is a stochastic maximization operator (stoch max / stoch arg max) that replaces exact maximization over the full action set with maximization over a randomly sampled sublinear subset C ⊆ A, reducing per-step complexity from O(n) to O(log n). This operator is plugged into Q-learning, Double Q-learning, Sarsa, DQN, and DDQN to yield Stochastic Q-learning, Stochastic Double Q-learning, Stochastic Sarsa, StochDQN, and StochDDQN. The paper proves that Stochastic Q-learning converges with probability 1 to a modified optimal Q-function Q* (optimal under the sampling distribution) by showing the associated Bellman-like operator Φ is a γ-contraction in the sup-norm, and that the conditional variance of the update noise is bounded. A memory-based variant is shown to recover exact maximization after O(n/⌈log(n)⌉) iterations once the Q-function stabilizes, and under a uniform value-distribution assumption the expected maximization error is bounded by b_t(s)/(⌈log(n)⌉+1). Empirically, the stochastic variants achieve exponential wall-time speedups (e.g., 60× on InvertedPendulum-v4) while matching or exceeding the returns of their deterministic counterparts and of continuous-action baselines (DDPG, A2C, PPO) on MuJoCo control tasks.

## Results

On the InvertedPendulum-v4 task (512 actions), StochDQN achieves the optimal average return in fewer steps than DQN, while StochDDQN nearly reaches optimal return where DDQN struggles. Wall-time speedups are dramatic: on InvertedPendulum-v4 with 1000 actions, stochastic methods take ~0.003 s/step versus ~0.18 s/step for deterministic counterparts (≈60× faster), and on HalfCheetah-v4 with 4096 actions, StochDDQN completes 100 k steps in ~2 hours versus ~17 hours for DDQN (≈10× faster). On FrozenLake-v1, Stochastic Q-learning outperforms all deterministic variants in cumulative reward, while on CliffWalking-v0 and a 256-action MDP all methods converge to the optimal policy in comparable step counts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against deterministic Q-learning, Double Q-learning, and Sarsa, the stochastic variants match or exceed cumulative reward on FrozenLake-v1 (Stochastic Q-learning is best) and reach the optimal policy in similar step counts on CliffWalking-v0 and the 256-action MDP, while being ~60× faster per step. Against DQN and DDQN on InvertedPendulum-v4, StochDQN converges to the optimal return in fewer steps and StochDDQN nearly reaches optimal return where DDQN plateaus below optimal; on HalfCheetah-v4 (4096 actions) StochDDQN matches DDQN's return while running ~10× faster. Against the continuous-action baselines DDPG, A2C, and PPO on InvertedPendulum-v4, both StochDQN and StochDDQN achieve higher average returns in fewer steps, and on HalfCheetah-v4 all DQN-family methods (stochastic and non-stochastic) outperform PPO and A2C. The stochastic maximization ratio ω_t rapidly approaches 1 and the gap β_t remains small, confirming that the sub-linear search does not materially degrade the value estimates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical Q-learning, Double Q-learning, DQN, DDQN, and Sarsa algorithms by replacing their exact max/arg max operations with stochastic counterparts (stoch max, stoch arg max) that search only over O(log(n)) randomly sampled actions rather than all n actions. It departs from prior work on large action spaces (Dulac-Arnold et al., 2015; Tavakoli et al., 2018; Tessler et al., 2019; Akkerman et al., 2023) that typically exploits combinatorial or multi-dimensional structure in the action space, by making no structural assumptions and applying to any general, potentially unstructured, single- or multi-dimensional discrete action space. The convergence analysis builds on the stochastic iterative dynamic programming framework of Jaakkola et al. (1993), extended to handle the additional stochasticity introduced by random subset sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exponential wall-time speedup (O(log n) vs O(n) per step) makes value-based RL practical for action spaces with thousands or more discrete actions, a regime where standard Q-learning variants become infeasible. The finding that stochastic methods can outperform their deterministic counterparts—attributed to reduced overestimation bias and enhanced exploration—suggests that the max operation in Q-learning is not merely a computational necessity but a source of systematic error. Open questions include how the gap between the convergence target Q* (which depends on the sampling distribution) and the true optimal Q-function behaves as n grows, whether the approach extends naturally to continuous action spaces without discretization, and how it interacts with action-space structure when such structure is available.
