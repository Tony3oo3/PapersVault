## Problem

Federated learning models trained on non-IID data suffer from severe miscalibration, yet existing calibration techniques either require a global validation set (impractical under FL privacy constraints) or fail to address both local and global calibration simultaneously. The paper further proves theoretically that even under bounded label-distribution variance across clients, the global calibration error of FedAvg-trained models remains asymptotically lower-bounded, meaning standard aggregation alone cannot resolve the calibration gap.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the raw pre-sigmoid logits θ(x) from the trained FL model along with each client's local validation split (no global validation set is needed). It produces a calibrated probability vector via an order-preserving MLP scaler of architecture K-64-64-K, and a globally aggregated scaler parameter set that can be applied to any client's model output. The scaler operates post-hoc on the final-layer logits, so the underlying FL classifier weights remain unchanged.

## Method

FedCal trains a client-specific order-preserving MLP scaler (K-64-64-K) on each client's local validation data by minimizing NLL, where the order-preserving constraint (from Rahimi et al., 2020) guarantees that the scaler never reorders class logits, preserving top-k accuracy. For global calibration, local scalers are aligned via the Weight Matching (Git Re-Basin) algorithm to resolve MLP permutation symmetries, then aggregated by simple parameter averaging—exploiting linear mode connectivity so the averaged scaler approximates a weighted sum of individual losses. Rather than a purely post-hoc step, scaler updates are synchronized periodically alongside model updates during FedAvg rounds, improving generalization. The paper also provides a theoretical lower bound (Ω(√G / μR²)) on global calibration error under bounded KL-divergence of label distributions, establishing that aggregation alone is insufficient and motivating the explicit scaler-based correction.

## Results

FedCal achieves the lowest global ECE across all four datasets (MNIST, SVHN, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100) at every non-IIDness level tested (β = 1, 0.5, 0.3, 0.1). On average, FedCal reduces global calibration error by 47.66% compared to the best baseline without a global validation set (ENS) and by 63.06% compared to uncalibrated FL (UNCAL). Notably, FedCal remains robust as non-IIDness increases, e.g., on SVHN at β=0.1 it achieves 1.25% ECE while LR-TS degrades to 32.03%. The method also preserves top-3 accuracy (92.3% on MNIST) thanks to its order-preserving scaler design.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedCal consistently outperforms ENS (Deep Ensemble, Lakshminarayanan et al.) across all 16 dataset/non-IIDness combinations, with the gap widening as non-IIDness increases (e.g., CIFAR-100 at β=0.1: 10.72% vs. 20.48% ECE). Against LR-TS (MD-TS adapted for FL, Yu et al.), FedCal shows a dramatic advantage under high heterogeneity, e.g., on SVHN at β=0.1 FedCal achieves 1.25% ECE while LR-TS collapses to 32.03%. Compared to VAL.TS (temperature scaling with a global validation set, Guo et al.), FedCal is slightly worse in most settings (e.g., 1.35% vs. 0.74% on MNIST at β=0.1), but crucially does not require a global validation set, which is often unavailable in practice. FedCal also complements FedProx (Li et al.): on MNIST at β=0.1, FedProx alone yields 3.19% ECE, while FedProx+FedCal reduces this to 1.07%, demonstrating that FedCal is orthogonal to and synergistic with existing non-IID mitigation strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedCal sits at the intersection of post-hoc calibration (temperature scaling, Platt scaling) and federated aggregation (FedAvg, FedProx), but departs from prior FL calibration work (MD-TS, FedLC) by eliminating the need for a global validation set and by formalizing a dual local+global calibration objective. The method repurposes two ideas from unrelated subfields: order-preserving networks (Rahimi et al., 2020) to prevent accuracy loss during calibration, and linear mode connectivity / Git Re-Basin (Ainsworth et al., 2022) to enable simple parameter averaging of MLP scalers across clients. The theoretical analysis builds on SCAFFOLD-style gradient dissimilarity bounds (Karimireddy et al., 2020) and Pinsker's inequality to establish a lower bound on global calibration error under bounded label skew.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Calibration in FL is critical for high-stakes deployments (healthcare, finance, autonomous driving) yet has been largely neglected; FedCal provides the first principled, post-hoc method that simultaneously addresses local and global calibration without a global validation set, and its theoretical lower bound shows this is a fundamental limitation of FedAvg rather than a solvable engineering problem. Limitations include evaluation only on label skew (one form of non-IID), four relatively small datasets, and added communication/compute overhead from the MLP scaler and weight matching. Open questions include how the approach generalizes to other non-IID forms (feature shift, quantity skew), the interaction between order-preserving constraints and weight matching at scale, and whether calibration effectiveness depends on model architecture and size.
