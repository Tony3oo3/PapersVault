## Problem

When non-private, data-dependent pre-processing steps (deduplication, imputation, PCA, scaling, quantization) are applied before a DP mechanism, the resulting privacy cost is typically either ignored or bounded via group privacy, which inflates the privacy parameter to O(nε). The paper asks: what is the precise additional privacy cost of non-private pre-processing, and can it be bounded with only minimal degradation rather than the polynomial blowup from group privacy?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240782/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a dataset S of size n in a bounded Euclidean space, a deterministic data-dependent pre-processing algorithm π (which maps S to a per-point function π_S), and a DP mechanism A (Gaussian, Laplace, Exponential, DP-GD, or DP-SGD variants) with privacy parameters ε, δ. The analysis is conditional on a dataset collection L, and the output is an explicit RDP parameter ε̂ for the composed pipeline A∘π, expressed in terms of the pre-processing sensitivities Δ_∞ and Δ_2 and the mechanism's RDP/SRDP parameters. The PTR variant additionally outputs a refusal signal ⊥ when the dataset is deemed pathological (e.g., eigenvalue gap below a threshold β).

## Method

The core technical contribution is a two-pronged framework: (1) Smooth Rényi Differential Privacy (SRDP), which bounds Rényi divergence between algorithm outputs on datasets with bounded L12 distance (allowing all n points to differ slightly) rather than only Hamming distance 1, and (2) L_∞ and L_2 sensitivities of the pre-processing algorithm, quantifying how many points and by how much a single-point change in S alters the pre-processed dataset. Theorem 2 combines these by constructing a synthetic intermediate dataset S̃ that is Hamming-1 neighboring to one pre-processed dataset and L12-close (distance ≤ Δ_2·Δ_∞) to the other, then applying RDP for the Hamming-1 leg and SRDP for the L12 leg, combined via the weak triangle inequality of Rényi divergence. This yields overall RDP guarantees that scale as O(τ·ε) rather than O(n·ε) under group privacy, where τ = Δ_2·Δ_∞ is typically O(1) for common pre-processing steps. A Propose-Test-Release wrapper converts the conditional guarantees into unconditional ones by refusing to release output on pathological datasets.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 2) shows that non-private pre-processing incurs only a constant-order privacy cost: for example, quantization with the Gaussian mechanism yields an overall RDP parameter of $1.05\alpha\varepsilon^2(1+\eta^2p^2)$, and mean imputation yields $1.05\alpha\varepsilon^2(1+4p^2/(n-p)^2)$, compared to $O(n\varepsilon)$ under naive group privacy. In the synthetic experiment (Figure 3, 1000 points, dimension 6000, approximate rank 50), the non-private PCA + DP-SGD pipeline achieves the lowest excess empirical loss across all privacy levels $\varepsilon$, outperforming both DP-PCA (with half the privacy budget) and DP-SGD without pre-processing. The SRDP-based analysis provides significantly tighter guarantees than group privacy, especially for small quantization thresholds $\eta$, few missing values $p$, and well-separated PCA eigenvalue gaps $\delta_{\min}^k$.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the naive group privacy / Dependent DP (DDP) analysis, which inflates the privacy parameter to $O(n\varepsilon)$ for any pre-processing step. The proposed SRDP-based framework (Theorem 2) achieves $O(\tau\varepsilon)$ where $\tau = \Delta_2 \cdot \Delta_\infty$ is typically $O(1)$ for well-structured datasets, yielding a polynomial improvement over group privacy. In the empirical comparison (Figure 3), non-private PCA + DP-SGD consistently achieves the lowest excess empirical loss across all privacy budgets $\varepsilon$, outperforming both DP-PCA (Chaudhuri et al., 2012, with half the privacy budget allocated to DP-PCA) and DP-SGD without any pre-processing. DP-PCA exhibits the worst performance because its inherent $\Omega(d)$ error dominates in the high-dimensional regime ($d=6000 \ll n=1000$ is not satisfied; here $d \gg n$). The no-preprocessing baseline suffers from the curse of dimensionality in the optimization bound, while the non-private PCA pipeline exploits the data's approximate low-rank structure at only a constant-order privacy cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Rényi Differential Privacy (Mironov, 2017) by introducing Smooth RDP (SRDP), a norm-based generalization that bounds divergence over datasets with bounded L12 distance rather than single-point Hamming distance. It departs from the group privacy and Dependent DP approaches (Zhao et al., 2017; Liu et al., 2016), which yield polynomial-in-n bounds, by showing that SRDP combined with a sensitivity measure of pre-processing functions yields constant-factor privacy amplification for common pre-processing steps. The Propose-Test-Release framework (Dwork & Lei, 2009) is repurposed to convert conditional guarantees into unconditional ones, and the work complements Debenedetti et al. (2023), who demonstrated privacy attacks via deduplication but did not quantify the resulting privacy loss across mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a widely overlooked gap: non-private pre-processing (imputation, deduplication, PCA, scaling) is standard practice before DP training, yet its privacy cost was previously unaccounted for or only bounded via loose group-privacy arguments. The practical takeaway—that common pre-processing steps typically add only a constant-factor privacy cost rather than an O(n) blowup—gives DP practitioners a concrete, implementable tool to certify end-to-end pipeline privacy. Open questions include extending the framework to more complex pre-processing (e.g., pre-trained deep feature extractors, private data synthesis) and to settings where the dataset collection L is not known in advance without the PTR overhead.
