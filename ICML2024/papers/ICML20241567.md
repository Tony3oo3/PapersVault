## Problem

Existing automated neuron explanation methods (Network Dissection, MILAN, CLIP-Dissect) focus almost exclusively on the highest-activating inputs, yet the paper demonstrates via ablation that these top inputs account for only ~0.26% of a neuron's total causal effect on network outputs. This means current explanations miss the vast majority of what a neuron actually does, and existing evaluation metrics that only inspect top activations are correspondingly unreliable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a target neuron's activation vector q_k ∈ ℝ^N over a probing dataset (e.g., 50,000 ImageNet validation images split 70/10/20), a concept set S of size M (ranging from ~1,000 class labels to ~8,400 concepts including Broden labels and common nouns), and produces a sparse set of (weight, concept) pairs E = {(w*_k,1, c_i1), ..., (w*_k,u, c_iu)} with u ≤ 10 concepts. For CNN channels, a summary function g (mean) collapses 2D activation maps to scalars before fitting. Evaluation outputs a correlation score ρ(k,E) and an ablation score α(k,E) computed on the held-out test split.

## Method

Linear Explanations (LE) describes each neuron as a weighted sum of concepts by first constructing a concept activation matrix P ∈ ℝ^{M×N} where P_ij ≈ P(c_i|x_j), either from ground-truth labels (LE-Label) or from calibrated SigLIP-ViT-L-16-384 pseudo-labels with learned scaling/offset parameters a, b optimized via binary cross-entropy on ImageNet superclasses (LE-SigLIP). A sparse linear weight vector w_k is then fitted to predict neuron activations from P using GLM-Saga with elastic-net regularization (η=0.99, emphasizing L1 sparsity). A greedy search (Algorithm 1) refines this into a very sparse explanation by iteratively selecting from the top-r=10 candidate concepts the one that most improves validation correlation, stopping when improvement falls below tolerance ε=0.02 or a maximum of v=10 concepts is reached. For evaluation, the paper extends the simulation paradigm from language models to vision by using a separate SigLIP-SO400M-14-384 model as a simulator that predicts P(c_i|x_j) for each concept in the explanation, then scores explanations via Pearson correlation or by replacing actual neuron activations with simulated ones and measuring the resulting change in cross-entropy loss (ablation scoring).

## Results

On ResNet-50 (ImageNet), LE(SigLIP) achieves a mean simulation correlation of 0.3772, roughly 2× higher than the best baseline CLIP-Dissect (0.1871) and 3× higher than Network Dissection (0.1242) or MILAN (0.0920). Under ablation scoring on the same model, LE(SigLIP) reaches 0.0727, more than 3× better than all baselines (best baseline: CLIP-Dissect at 0.0215). The advantage is consistent across architectures: LE(SigLIP) scores 0.4970 on VGG-16 (CIFAR-100), 0.4372 on ResNet-18 (Places365), and 0.3489 on ViT-B/16 (ImageNet), outperforming CLIP-Dissect by 2–4× in every setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LE(SigLIP) outperforms all three baselines across every model and dataset tested. Against CLIP-Dissect (the strongest baseline), LE(SigLIP) achieves roughly 2× higher correlation on ResNet-50 (0.3772 vs. 0.1871), 2× on ResNet-18 (0.4372 vs. 0.2208), 2.2× on VGG-16 (0.4970 vs. 0.2298), and 2× on ViT-B/16 (0.3489 vs. 0.1722). Against Network Dissection and MILAN, the gap is even larger (3–4×), partly because those methods only explain the top 0.5% of activations or the 15 highest-activating inputs, respectively. Under ablation scoring, LE(SigLIP) scores 0.0727 versus at most 0.0215 for any baseline, a >3× improvement. LE(Label) also consistently beats all baselines but trails LE(SigLIP) by a moderate margin, likely because SigLIP's larger concept set and alignment with the simulator model yield richer explanations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the simulation-based evaluation framework from Bills et al. (2023) for LLM neurons to the vision domain, using SigLIP as a human-aligned simulator, and departs from prior neuron explanation methods (Network Dissection, MILAN, CLIP-Dissect) by arguing they inadequately capture a neuron's full activation range. Methodologically, it inverts the linear probing paradigm: rather than predicting concepts from neuron activations, it learns a sparse linear combination of concepts to predict neuron activations, using GLM-Saga regularization followed by greedy search for interpretability. The linear composition approach is positioned as a natural alternative to the logical compositions of Compositional Explanations (Mu & Andreas, 2020), avoiding the information loss from binarizing activations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that the top 0.2% of activating inputs account for only ~0.26% of a neuron's causal effect challenges the dominant practice of explaining neurons solely through their highest activations, with implications for how the field evaluates and designs explanation methods. The simulation evaluation pipeline (correlation + ablation scoring via SigLIP) provides a practical, scalable benchmark for vision neuron explanations that was previously unavailable. However, ablation scores remain very low even for the best method (0.0727), and the method does not handle location-specific activations in lower layers, leaving open whether neurons are fundamentally uninterpretable or whether better simulators and explanations are needed. The finding that ViT-L/16's final transformer block is entirely dead (100% dead neurons) also raises questions about model architecture efficiency.
