## Problem

Existing spectral GNNs face two fundamental limitations: (1) their spectral filters are restricted to integer-order polynomials, limiting expressiveness and preventing adaptive frequency response (e.g., band-pass or band-stop), and (2) they require eigen-decomposition of the full graph Laplacian, confining them to transductive settings on small-scale graphs and precluding inductive learning on large-scale graphs where new nodes emerge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242282/cropped_page3_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a graph with adjacency structure and node feature matrix, SLOG samples a K-hop ego-subgraph around a target node (with configurable maximum neighbor counts per hop), computes the symmetrically normalized Laplacian of that subgraph, and applies the real-valued-order filter to produce a node embedding vector. The output is a per-node embedding used for semi-supervised classification; because the filter operates on the sampled subgraph rather than the full graph, the method naturally supports inductive prediction on unseen nodes without retraining.

## Method

SLOG introduces a two-component spectral filter ω(L_sym) = (I − ½L_sym)^p · (I + (L_sym − I)²)^q with trainable real-valued exponents p and q, which provably decomposes into two spatial-domain linear GCN operations (S₁^p · S₂^q) and can adaptively realize low-pass, high-pass, band-pass, or band-stop responses. To overcome the transductive limitation, SLOG(B) applies this filter on a randomly sampled K-hop subgraph around each target node, combining spatial GNN sampling with spectral signal processing. SLOG(N) stacks L layers of this filter with non-linear activations and residual connections, while SLOG(L) partitions the graph via METIS and interpolates a global uniform filter with per-partition local filters (weighted by hyperparameter β) to handle spatially varying heterophily. The filter parameters p, q are optimized jointly with weight matrices via Adam, with optional Alternating Minimization or Gaussian Process-based bi-level optimization for the exponent parameters.

## Results

On 8 heterophilic datasets, SLOG(B) achieves the best accuracy across all of them, e.g., 0.581 on Chameleon (vs. 0.578 BernNet), 0.392 on Squirrel (vs. 0.387 GraphSAGE), and 0.427 on Squirrel-filtered (vs. 0.409 TeDGCN). On 5 homophilic datasets, SLOG variants achieve the best in 3 out of 5 (Citeseer 0.766, DBLP 0.850, Coauthor-Physics 0.966) and are within 1.4% of the best on the remaining two. On large-scale graphs, SLOG(N) achieves 0.962 on Reddit (115M edges) where H2GCN runs out of memory, and SLOG(B) achieves 0.723 on Ogbn-arxiv, outperforming GraphSAGE (0.717) and GATv2 (0.675).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLOG(B) outperforms all 13 baselines on every heterophilic dataset, with the largest margins over GPRGNN (+4.9% on Squirrel), H2GCN (+6.8% on Squirrel-filtered), and BernNet (+5.6% on Squirrel-filtered). The closest competitor is BernNet on Chameleon (0.578 vs. 0.581) and H2GCN on Minesweeper (0.824 vs. 0.822 for SLOG(B), though SLOG(N) reaches 0.844). On homophilic datasets, SLOG(B) matches or beats GPRGNN and H2GCN on Citeseer (0.766 vs. 0.757) and DBLP (0.850 vs. 0.848), while SLOG(N) leads on Coauthor-CS (0.944 vs. 0.942 GPRGNN) and Coauthor-Physics (0.966 vs. 0.966 GPRGNN/H2GCN). On the large-scale Reddit graph (115M edges), SLOG(N) achieves 0.962 accuracy, surpassing GATv2 (0.957) and GPRGNN (0.950), while H2GCN fails entirely due to out-of-memory. Against the most directly related prior work TeDGCN (which also uses real-valued order), SLOG(B) wins on Squirrel-filtered (0.427 vs. 0.409) and Chameleon-filtered (0.420 vs. 0.422, a near-tie), while TeDGCN edges ahead on Tolokers (0.796 vs. 0.796, tied) and Amazon-ratings (0.425 vs. 0.451, SLOG wins).

## Relation to Prior Work

SLOG extends the real-valued order filter concept introduced by TeDGCN (Yan et al., 2023), which redefined GNN depth as a continuous trainable parameter, by replacing its single-component filter (I − ½L_sym)^d with a two-component filter S1^p · S2^q that supports band-pass and band-stop responses in addition to low/high pass. Simultaneously, SLOG departs from the transductive paradigm of prior spectral GNNs (ChebNet, BernNet, GPRGNN, H2GCN) that require eigen-decomposition of the full graph Laplacian, by borrowing the K-hop subgraph sampling strategy from spatial GNNs like GraphSAGE (Hamilton et al., 2017) and applying the spectral filter only on the sampled neighborhood. This fusion of spatial sampling with spectral filtering is the paper's central architectural contribution, positioning SLOG as a bridge between the two traditionally separate GNN families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By resolving both the polynomial limitation and the transductive limitation simultaneously, SLOG provides a practical path to deploying spectral GNNs on large-scale inductive settings (demonstrated on Reddit with 115M edges where competitors OOM), which was previously infeasible for spectral methods. The real-valued order filter's geometric interpretability (Proposition 3.1) and adaptive frequency response offer a principled alternative to hand-tuned polynomial orders. However, the paper acknowledges it is limited to node classification, leaving extension to link prediction and other graph tasks as future work. The SLOG(L) local-adaptive variant only helps on graphs with high heterophily locality, suggesting the global uniform filter may suffice for many practical cases, and the optimization of exponent parameters p, q remains challenging (requiring auxiliary methods like alternating minimization or Gaussian Process in the appendix).
