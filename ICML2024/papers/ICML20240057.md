## Problem

Existing generalization error bounds for graph neural networks become vacuous in the over-parameterized regime where the number of hidden units exceeds the number of training samples, leaving no theoretical guarantee on out-of-sample performance. This paper addresses the gap by deriving the first non-trivial upper bounds on GNN generalization error in the mean-field (infinite-width) regime, achieving an O(1/n) convergence rate that prior VC-dimension, Rademacher, and PAC-Bayesian approaches could not match.

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. graph samples, each consisting of a node feature matrix F ∈ R^{N×k} (with bounded ℓ₂-norm rows) and a binary adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{N×N}, along with a binary label y ∈ {−1, 1}. The theoretical output is an upper bound on the expected generalization error expressed in terms of the inverse temperature α, graph structural parameters (d_max, d_min, R_max), and the number of graph samples n. The learned model is a Gibbs measure over the parameter space of a single hidden unit, rather than a fixed parameter vector, reflecting the mean-field limit where the empirical measure over h hidden units converges to a continuous distribution.

## Method

The paper frames over-parameterized one-hidden-layer GCNs and MPGNNs within the mean-field regime, where the learning algorithm is a map from the empirical data measure μ_n to a probability measure m(μ_n) on the parameter space of a single unit. The KL-regularized empirical risk minimizer takes the form of a Gibbs measure m^α(μ_n) ∝ π(w) exp{−α δR/δm}. Two complementary bounding strategies are developed: (1) a functional-derivative approach that bounds the generalization error via the symmetrized KL divergence between m(μ_n) and a leave-one-out measure m(μ_{n,(1)}), yielding an expected upper bound of O(1/n); and (2) a Rademacher-complexity approach combined with a parametric upper bound on KL(m^α(μ_n)‖π) that avoids unknown terms like KL(m_true‖π), yielding a high-probability O(1/√n) bound. The bounds explicitly depend on graph filter norms (‖G(A)‖_∞ or ‖G(A)‖_F), the readout function's Lipschitz constant, and graph degree parameters, revealing how architectural choices (e.g., mean vs. sum readout, symmetric-normalized vs. random-walk filters) affect generalization.

## Results

The paper's headline theoretical result is an upper bound on the expected generalization error of over-parameterized one-hidden-layer GCNs and MPGNNs with convergence rate O(1/n) (Theorem 4.9), where n is the number of graph samples — the first such rate for GNNs. A complementary high-probability bound via Rademacher complexity (Theorem 4.14) achieves O(1/√n). Both bounds are independent of the hidden layer width h, whereas all prior bounds grow polynomially in h (e.g., O(h⁴) for VC-dimension). Empirically, on synthetic SBM/ER graphs and the PROTEINS dataset, the absolute generalization error consistently decreases as h increases from 4 to 256, confirming that width-dependent bounds are vacuous in the over-parameterized regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's functional derivative bound (Theorem 4.9) strictly dominates all five prior baselines on two axes: it achieves O(1/n) convergence in sample size versus O(1/√n) for VC-dimension (Scarselli et al., 2018), Rademacher complexity (Garg et al., 2020), both PAC-Bayesian methods (Liao et al., 2020; Ju et al., 2023), and continuous MPGNN (Maskey et al., 2022). Additionally, the bound is completely independent of hidden layer width h, whereas all prior methods exhibit polynomial growth in h (ranging from O(√h) to O(h⁴)), rendering them vacuous as h→∞. The Rademacher complexity bound (Theorem 4.14) matches the O(1/√n) rate of prior methods but again removes the h-dependence, and introduces a degree-ratio dependence O((d̃_max/d̃_min)^{3/4}) that is tighter than the O(d̃_max) or O(d̃_max·log^{1/2}(d̃_max)) terms in Garg et al. and Liao et al. Empirically, the observed decrease in generalization error with increasing h directly contradicts the predictions of all width-dependent baselines, validating the mean-field framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mean-field framework for generalization error analysis from one-hidden-layer neural networks (Aminian et al., 2023; Mei et al., 2018) to graph neural networks, specifically GCNs and MPGNNs. It leverages the Gibbs measure formulation from Hu et al. (2019) and the functional derivative representations from Aminian et al. (2023), but departs from prior GNN generalization work (VC-dimension, PAC-Bayesian, Rademacher complexity) by working in the over-parameterized regime where the hidden layer width is infinite. The key conceptual departure is that all prior GNN generalization bounds scale adversely with width h and become vacuous as h→∞, whereas this paper's bounds are independent of h and achieve O(1/n) convergence via symmetrized KL divergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide a non-vacuous generalization error upper bound with O(1/n) convergence rate for GNNs in the over-parameterized regime, resolving a gap where all prior bounds were uninformative when the number of parameters exceeds the number of data points. The bound's independence from hidden layer width provides theoretical assurance that over-parameterized GNNs can generalize well, aligning with empirical observations. The main limitation is the restriction to one hidden layer; extending the mean-field analysis to deep GNNs (inspired by Sirignano & Spiliopoulos, 2019) and to hypergraph neural networks are identified as important open problems. The empirical results on synthetic SBM/ER graphs and the PROTEINS dataset confirm the decreasing generalization error trend with width, but the theoretical bounds remain loose in absolute value.
