## Problem

Classical dynamic programming for optimal paths relies on a non-differentiable max operator, preventing gradient-based integration into neural networks. Existing work either smooths the max (losing the sparsity of hard assignments) or splits training into multiple phases with external DP aligners, making model performance critically dependent on those external components. This paper asks whether one can obtain structured sparse optimal paths in a fully differentiable, end-to-end manner within a generative model.

## Input / Output

For TTS, the model takes phoneme token sequences (lyrics) as the observed condition and a Mel-spectrogram (80 bins, 1024 frame size, 25% overlap, 22.05 kHz) as the data to reconstruct; for SVS, the condition additionally includes fundamental frequency. The latent variable is a sparse binary matrix y of the same dimensions as the edge-weight matrix W (e.g., t×n for t spectrogram frames and n phonemes), where each entry indicates whether the corresponding edge lies on the sampled path. The DAG topology (monotonic alignment or DTW) is fixed a priori, and the output spectrogram is produced by extending the condition along the sampled path before passing through a transformer decoder.

## Method

The core contribution is a probabilistic softening of the optimal path problem: a Gibbs distribution over all paths on a DAG with energy equal to the path score, shown to be equivalent to a message-passing algorithm via the max- and shift-closure properties of the Gumbel distribution (Gumbel propagation). This yields linear-time closed-form expressions for path likelihood, sampling, and KL divergence within the distribution family, providing all ingredients for variational Bayesian inference. In the BDP-VAE, a neural network in the posterior encoder learns edge weights W from (x, c), while a Glow-based flow model in the prior encoder infers spectrogram features from c alone to form W⁽⁰⁾, enabling inference without access to x. Training uses the REINFORCE score-function estimator for the reconstruction term (with a 5-iteration moving-average baseline for variance reduction) and the exact closed-form KL divergence, achieving end-to-end optimization of the sparse latent path.

## Results

On the RyanSpeech TTS benchmark, BDPVAE-TTS achieves an MCD of 8.49±0.96 and RTF of 3.00×10⁻⁴, outperforming the non-end-to-end FastSpeech2 baseline (MCD 9.96, RTF 3.87×10⁻⁴) and Tacotron2 (MCD 11.39, RTF 6.07×10⁻⁴) on both metrics. It also edges out Glow-TTS on MCD (8.49 vs. 8.58) but trails VAENAR-TTS (MCD 8.18, RTF 1.10×10⁻⁴). On the TIMIT DTW alignment task, BDP-VAE achieves a phoneme-duration MAE of 3.93±0.37 at inference, substantially better than the random-weight baseline (5.69±0.31), confirming that the learned latent paths capture meaningful structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BDPVAE-TTS outperforms the FastSpeech2 baseline on both MCD (8.49 vs. 9.96) and RTF (3.00×10⁻⁴ vs. 3.87×10⁻⁴), demonstrating that converting a non-end-to-end model into an end-to-end BDP-VAE framework improves quality while removing the dependency on an external DP aligner. It also beats Tacotron2 by a wide margin on both metrics (MCD 8.49 vs. 11.39; RTF 3.00×10⁻⁴ vs. 6.07×10⁻⁴). Against Glow-TTS, BDPVAE-TTS achieves a slightly lower MCD (8.49 vs. 8.58) but a marginally higher RTF (3.00×10⁻⁴ vs. 2.87×10⁻⁴), with the key distinction being that BDPVAE-TTS uses discrete alignment at both training and inference, ensuring train-test consistency. The main loss is against VAENAR-TTS, which achieves the best MCD (8.18) and RTF (1.10×10⁻⁴), though VAENAR-TTS relies on soft (continuous) alignment via causality-masked self-attention rather than discrete structural paths. The paper argues that BDPVAE-TTS's discrete latent paths provide superior interpretability and structural guarantees that the continuous-attention baselines lack, as evidenced by closer F0 trajectory matches to ground truth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unified DP-on-DAG framework of Mensch & Blondel (2018), which made dynamic programming differentiable via convex regularization, by replacing the smoothed max operator with a probabilistic softening: a Gibbs distribution over paths whose message-passing is made tractable through the max and shift properties of the Gumbel distribution. The VAE framework of Kingma & Welling (2014) is repurposed so that the latent variable is a discrete sparse path on a DAG rather than a continuous Gaussian, and a flow-based conditional prior (inspired by FlowSeq) handles the inference-time absence of data. The TTS application directly re-architects FastSpeech2 (Ren et al., 2020), converting its reliance on an external DP aligner into an end-to-end BDP-VAE pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides all closed-form ingredients (sampling, likelihood, KL divergence) for variational Bayesian inference over latent paths in linear time, which is a practically important unification for structured generative models. However, the REINFORCE estimator introduces high gradient variance and slow convergence, and the empirical gains are modest: BDPVAE-TTS achieves MCD 8.49 versus VAENAR-TTS's 8.18, so the interpretability and end-to-end advantages come at a small quality cost. Open questions include whether variance-reduction or the Gumbel-softmax reparameterization (sketched in Appendix D) can close this gap, and whether the framework generalizes beyond monotonic alignment and DTW to more complex DAG structures.
