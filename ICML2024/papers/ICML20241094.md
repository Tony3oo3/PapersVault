## Problem

In vision, representation learning and image generation rely on fundamentally different techniques—masked prediction or contrastive learning for representations, and diffusion or GANs for generation—whereas in NLP a single autoregressive objective serves both purposes. This paper investigates whether a unified autoregressive model with a denoising diffusion decoder can learn strong visual representations while retaining genuine image generation capability, closing the gap between these two traditionally separate goals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241094/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 224×224 RGB images segmented into non-overlapping patches (sizes 16, 28, 32, or 56) arranged in raster scan order with a prepended start-of-sequence token. During pre-training, the model predicts each patch conditioned on all preceding patches; for generation, it conditions on the top half of an image to synthesize the bottom half at 64×64 resolution. For downstream evaluation, the last patch's Transformer output serves as the global image descriptor (no pooling or special tokens), and the model is fine-tuned without causal masking for classification, or adapted via ViTDet + Cascade Mask R-CNN for detection and segmentation.

## Method

DARL uses a decoder-only Vision Transformer with causal attention masking and decomposed 2D RoPE (applying rotary embeddings separately along x and y axes) to predict image patches autoregressively. The patch decoder is either a linear layer (MSE loss) or a single Transformer block that jointly processes the backbone output and the noise-corrupted patch as two tokens (diffusion loss). A key finding is that the optimal noise schedule for representation learning is heavily biased toward high noise levels (e.g., γ ~ Beta(0.03, 1)), differing substantially from schedules used in image generation models—reflecting a capacity competition between learning higher-level abstractions and reconstructing low-level details. The diffusion objective with this tailored schedule outperforms MSE when training is extended (800 epochs) and model capacity is larger, achieving ImageNet top-1 accuracy within 1% of MAE while also producing coherent image samples.

## Results

On ImageNet top-1 accuracy with fine-tuning, DARL (diffusion objective) achieves 84.9% (ViT-L16) and 85.9% (ViT-H14), within 1% of the state-of-the-art MAE (85.9% and 86.9% respectively). The MSE variant reaches 84.7% (ViT-L16) and 85.5% (ViT-H14). On the VTAB benchmark, DARL (DIFF) attains a mean accuracy of 88.7% (Natural), 92.2% (Specialized), and 64.7% (Structured), outperforming the supervised baseline on 10 of 11 Natural/Specialized datasets. On COCO object detection, DARL (DIFF) achieves AP 56.4 and mAP 48.0, close to MAE (AP 57.2, mAP 48.6) and well above supervised pre-training (AP 54.1, mAP 45.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DARL (DIFF) trails MAE by a consistent ~1% across ViT-L (84.9 vs 85.9) and ViT-H (85.9 vs 86.9), which the authors attribute to the inductive bias difference between causal and non-causal attention rather than the prediction task itself. Against BeiT, the gap narrows to 0.3% on ViT-L (84.9 vs 85.2). DARL (DIFF) outperforms both contrastive baselines—MoCo v3 by 0.8% (84.9 vs 84.1) and DINO by 2.1% (84.9 vs 82.8 on ViT-L equivalent)—demonstrating that generative pre-training can match or exceed contrastive methods under fine-tuning. The MSE variant is competitive with DINO on ViT-B (82.7 vs 82.8) but falls short of MAE and BeiT at that scale. Most strikingly, DARL surpasses the prior generative pre-training result (Image-GPT, 72.6%) by over 12 points, validating the combination of autoregressive prediction with a denoising patch decoder. On COCO detection, DARL (DIFF) achieves AP 56.4 vs MAE's 57.2, a 0.8-point gap, while clearly beating the supervised baseline (AP 54.1) by 2.3 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

DARL unifies two previously separate threads in vision: autoregressive image modelling (Image-GPT, PixelRNN) and denoising diffusion (DDPM), applying them jointly at the patch level within a decoder-only Transformer with causal attention. It builds directly on the ViT backbone and the DDPM formulation, but departs from standard image diffusion by corrupting patches independently and by sampling the noise level from a Beta distribution rather than a fixed schedule. The 2D decomposed RoPE extends rotary positional embeddings from 1D NLP sequences to 2D spatial coordinates, and the overall framing draws an explicit parallel to GPT-style generative pre-training in language, where next-token prediction simultaneously yields strong representations and a generative model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that a single generative model (autoregressive + patch-level denoising) can match state-of-the-art masked prediction (MAE) within ~1% on ImageNet fine-tuning, while also producing image samples — a unification that contrastive and masked-prediction methods cannot offer. The finding that the optimal noise schedule for representation learning is heavily biased toward high noise (opposite of generation-oriented schedules) reveals a fundamental capacity competition between learning high-level abstractions and reconstructing low-level details, an open question that may resolve differently at larger scale. Limitations include weak performance on structured VTAB tasks (KITTI-Dist, dSprites), the acknowledged 1% gap to MAE (hypothesized to stem from causal masking rather than the objective), and the absence of a bottleneck that makes linear-probe evaluation less informative.
