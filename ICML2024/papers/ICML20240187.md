## Problem

Neural active learning (NAL) relies on two widely used query criteria—uncertainty-based and diversity-based sampling—that achieve strong empirical performance, yet the internal mechanism explaining why both work and how they relate to each other remains unexplored. This paper asks: what is the shared theoretical rationale behind the success of both criteria, and can they be unified under a single principle?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240187/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates in a pool-based selective sampling setting: it takes an initial labeled set D_{n0} of size n0, a large unlabeled sampling pool P drawn from the same feature-noise distribution, and a 2-layer ReLU CNN as the base model. It outputs n* newly selected query samples (labeled by an oracle) and provable 0-1 test error bounds on a test distribution D* that may have a different weak-feature probability p* than the training distribution. The data model is fully synthetic, with each sample consisting of one feature patch (strong μ1 with probability 1−p, weak μ2 with probability p) and one Gaussian noise patch in R^{2d}.

## Method

The paper introduces a unified feature-learning analysis of two representative NAL algorithms (Uncertainty Sampling via lowest confidence and Diversity Sampling via largest feature-space distance) built on a 2-layer ReLU CNN trained by gradient descent with logistic loss. Using a signal-noise decomposition of the first-layer weights into γ (feature learning progress) and ρ (noise memorization) coefficients, the authors prove that both query criteria reduce to the same sufficient event: prioritizing samples whose feature patch has the lowest expected γ, i.e., yet-to-be-learned (weak & rare) features. This shared 'perplexing sample' principle ensures NAL achieves benign overfitting (exponentially small test error) while strategy-free passive learning suffers harmful overfitting (constant test error ≥ 0.12·p*). The analysis further yields a Θ(p^{-1}) label complexity reduction for NAL over passive learning, and the results extend to non-linearly separable XOR data.

## Results

The paper provides provable guarantees for three querying strategies (Random, Uncertainty, and Diversity Sampling) over a 2-layer ReLU CNN on a feature-noise data model. After querying, both Uncertainty and Diversity Sampling achieve exponentially small test error bounded by exp(Θ(-ñ‖μ_l‖⁴/(σ_p⁴d))), while Random Sampling (passive learning) remains stuck at a constant test error of at least 0.12·p*. In terms of label complexity, NAL algorithms require only Ω(σ_p²d‖μ₂‖⁻⁴) samples versus Ω(p⁻¹σ_p²d‖μ₂‖⁻⁴) for passive learning, yielding a Θ(p⁻¹) reduction in labeling effort. Synthetic experiments (d=2000, ‖μ₁‖=9, ‖μ₂‖=3, p=p*=0.2, n₀=10, n*=30) confirm that both NAL methods learn weak features well and match the fully-trained model's test accuracy, while Random Sampling shows persistently poor weak-feature learning and low test accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three querying strategies: Random Sampling (strategy-free passive learning), Uncertainty Sampling (uncertainty-based NAL), and Diversity Sampling (diversity-based NAL), all built on the same 2-layer ReLU CNN trained by gradient descent with logistic loss. Both NAL methods provably outperform Random Sampling after querying: they achieve exponentially decaying test error while Random Sampling is stuck at a constant error floor of 0.12·p*, which worsens in out-of-distribution settings where p* > 0.5 > p. The key mechanism is that both NAL criteria inherently prioritize 'perplexing samples' (those with yet-to-be-learned weak & rare features), enabling adequate learning of all feature types within a small labeled set. In terms of label complexity, NAL algorithms reduce the required sample size by a factor of Θ(p⁻¹) compared to passive learning (e.g., for p=0.2, a 5× reduction). The synthetic experiments in Figure 2 confirm these theoretical predictions: after querying with n*=30 samples, both NAL methods match the fully-trained model (n_CNN=200) in test accuracy, while Random Sampling shows significantly weaker feature learning (lower γ₂) and poor test accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research lines: feature learning dynamics in overparameterized neural networks (Cao et al. 2022, Kou et al. 2023, Zou et al. 2023, Allen-Zhu and Li 2023) and neural active learning (NAL). Prior NAL work has produced effective uncertainty-based and diversity-based query strategies and some theory-driven guarantees (VC bounds, NTK-based), but has not explained the internal mechanism connecting the two criteria. By adopting the signal-noise decomposition and coefficient scale analysis from the feature learning literature, the paper provides the first provable unification showing both criteria inherently prioritize 'perplexing samples'—those containing yet-to-be-learned (weak and rare) features—within a 2-layer ReLU CNN under a multi-view feature-noise data model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of uncertainty and diversity sampling under a single 'perplexing samples' principle offers a principled lens for designing and combining future NAL algorithms, and the Θ(p^{-1}) label complexity reduction over passive learning quantifies NAL's advantage in imbalanced settings. However, the analysis is confined to a single-round, pool-based setting with a 2-layer ReLU CNN and a specific orthogonal feature-noise data model (with an XOR extension), leaving open whether the unification principle extends to deeper architectures, multi-round iterative querying, or real-world non-orthogonal feature spaces. The paper also notes that when learning-progress disparity between features is small, the two criteria may diverge in behavior, suggesting the unification is conditional rather than universal.
