## Problem

Sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) is widely presented as improving generalization by finding flatter minima, yet the sharpness metric induced by SAM does not consistently correlate with generalization error in modern architectures, and practically necessary concepts like m-sharpness lack theoretical grounding. This paper asks whether SAM's benefits can be explained by an alternative mechanistic principle that does not require loss surface flatness, and whether a better perturbation can be designed by targeting the right kind of model bias.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242009/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet, 32×32 for CIFAR) and produces class labels via a softmax classification head. The key operational detail is that the OBF perturbation is computed per-sample using the model's own softmax outputs to determine a per-sample forgetting strength α, which modulates how strongly the perturbation pushes predictions toward a uniform distribution. The perturbation uses a separate batch of size m=64 (distinct from the global training batch of 4096) to compute the ascent direction, maintaining the same two-forward-pass cost as standard SAM.

## Method

The paper reframes SAM's perturbation step as 'perturbed forgetting': the gradient ascent perturbation transiently discards undesirable model biases so that the subsequent gradient computed at the perturbed parameters provides a less biased learning signal. The core technical contribution is the Output Bias Forgetting (OBF) perturbation, which replaces the standard steepest-ascent direction with a loss L^BF(y,ŷ) = (1−α)L^CE(y,ŷ) − L^CE(U,ŷ) that avoids sharpening non-target predictions (which steepest ascent does, potentially amplifying output-exposed biases) and optionally pushes predictions toward a uniform distribution. The forgetting strength α is determined dynamically per sample via α = γ·max((1−λ/ŷ_y)/(1−λ), 0), activating the uniformity push only when the model's confidence in the correct class exceeds a threshold λ (typically 1/C). OBF is plugged into SAM, GSAM, and ASAM as a drop-in replacement for the perturbation direction, and empirically outperforms steepest-ascent perturbations on ImageNet, robustness benchmarks (ImageNet-R, ImageNet-Sketch, ImageNet-V2, ImageNet-Real), and CIFAR transfer learning, while sometimes converging to sharper regions—supporting the claim that training dynamics matter more than flatness.

## Results

On ImageNet-V1, SAM with the proposed OBF perturbation achieves 74.49% top-1 accuracy with ViT-S/32, outperforming standard steepest-ascent SAM (72.77%) by 1.72 points, and 80.30% with ViT-S/16 vs. 78.73% for steepest ascent. Gains are even larger on robustness benchmarks: ImageNet-R improves from 21.63% to 25.31% and ImageNet-Sketch from 19.68% to 22.58% for ViT-S/32 SAM. Notably, OBF does not always produce the flattest minima (e.g., ViT-S/16 SAM OBF has sharpness 17.1 vs. 2.4 for steepest ascent), yet still generalizes better, supporting the paper's argument that perturbed forgetting—not flatness—drives SAM's benefits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OBF consistently outperforms standard SAM with steepest-ascent perturbation across all four model families on ImageNet-V1, with the largest gains on ViTs (e.g., +1.72% for ViT-S/32, +1.57% for ViT-S/16) and smaller but consistent gains on ResNets (+0.18% for ResNet-50, +0.18% for ResNet-101). Against GSAM with steepest ascent, OBF wins on most benchmarks for ViTs (e.g., 74.41% vs. 73.41% for ViT-S/32, 80.32% vs. 78.95% for ViT-S/16) and matches or slightly exceeds it for ResNets. In the ASAM framework (Table B.1), OBF outperforms steepest-ascent ASAM on all benchmarks for both ViT-S/32 (74.73% vs. 74.45% on ImageNet-V1) and ResNet-50 (78.17% vs. 77.30%), while exhibiting higher sharpness (30.3 vs. 6.5 for ViT-S/32), further decoupling generalization from flatness. The shrink-and-perturb baseline (Ash & Adams, 2020) performs comparably to vanilla training (e.g., 69.05% vs. 69.29% for ViT-S/32), confirming that transient perturbed forgetting within SAM dynamics is substantially more effective than in-place parameter modification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets SAM (Foret et al., 2020) and its variants (GSAM, ASAM) through a 'perturbed forgetting' lens, departing from the dominant sharpness-minimization narrative by connecting SAM's perturbations to the information bottleneck principle (Tishby et al., 2000; Kawaguchi et al., 2023). It builds on the extragradient framework and the observation that small perturbation batches (m-SAM) generalize better, but reframes these dynamics as transient forgetting of output-exposed biases rather than flatness pursuit. The proposed OBF perturbation is conceptually related to Tiwari & Shenoy (2023), who also use uniform-distribution gradients to target biases, but differs by exploiting SAM's transient perturbation mechanism rather than an auxiliary layer. The work is complementary to Chen et al. (2023) on SAM avoiding harmful overfitting and Baek et al. (2024) on label-noise robustness, both of which also identify non-sharpness mechanisms underlying SAM's benefits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that OBF outperforms standard SAM, GSAM, and ASAM on ImageNet and robustness benchmarks while sometimes converging to sharper regions, the paper provides concrete evidence that the sharpness-minimization explanation for SAM is incomplete or misleading. The correlation analysis showing that forgetting of output-exposed biases predicts generalization more strongly than loss-surface flatness offers a practical diagnostic for practitioners. Open questions include characterizing the exact nature of biases targeted by different perturbations, extending the framework beyond image classification, and developing a more formal theoretical treatment of perturbed forgetting. The architecture-dependent optimal hyperparameters (γ≈0 for ResNets vs. γ=1 for ViTs) suggest the mechanism interacts with model inductive biases in ways not yet understood.
