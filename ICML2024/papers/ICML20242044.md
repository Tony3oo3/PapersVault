## Problem

Existing graph contrastive learning methods predominantly rely on low-pass GNN encoders under homophily assumptions, failing to generalize to heterophilic graphs where connected nodes have dissimilar features. Additionally, GNN-based inference is bottlenecked by iterative message passing, and prior distillation approaches to MLPs require supervised labels to train a teacher, making them inapplicable in label-scarce self-supervised settings. The paper asks: how can one design spectral filters generalizable across homophily levels without labels, and how can MLP inference efficiency be combined with GNN graph-context awareness in a purely self-supervised framework?

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph G=(V,E) with node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×D_f} and adjacency matrix A, producing node representations Z ∈ R^{N×d} via a trained MLP encoder. During training, both a K-order Chebyshev polynomial spectral GNN and the MLP encoder process the input, but at inference only the MLP is deployed, reducing per-node cost from O(KE) message passing to O(ND_f D) matrix multiplication. The representations are evaluated with a linear probe for node classification, and the method is augmentation-free—no edge dropping, feature masking, or graph perturbation is applied.

## Method

S3GCL decomposes the graph into two biased-pass views using a cosine-parameterized Chebyshev polynomial, where each filter (low-pass or high-pass) is controlled by only two learnable scalars (β_a, β_b) that modulate the Chebyshev interpolation coefficients via a cosine curve, emphasizing the relevant frequency band while suppressing the other. A Cross-Pass contrastive objective (a modified InfoNCE) aligns the full-pass MLP representations with each biased-pass GNN representation, eliminating the need for manual graph augmentation. To compensate for the MLP's lack of structural awareness, spatial positive pairs are introduced: structurally neighboring nodes (one-hop) serve as positives in the full-low loss, while top-k feature-similar nodes serve as positives in the full-high loss, implicitly encoding two-hop monophily and long-range semantic similarity. Theoretically, the paper proves (Theorem 2.5) that in heterophilic graphs a high-pass filter yields lower intra-class/inter-class distance disparity, justifying the dual-filter design, and shows (Proposition 3.1) that optimizing the cross-pass loss maximizes mutual information between neighboring node representations while minimizing their KL divergence.

## Results

S3GCL achieves the best average rank (1.14) on homophilic datasets and (1.29) on heterophilic datasets, attaining state-of-the-art accuracy on 11 of 14 benchmarks. Headline numbers include 88.47% on Cora, 76.31% on CiteSeer, 87.89% on PubMed, 86.12% on Texas, and 66.27% on Roman-empire. In terms of inference efficiency, S3GCL requires only 2.23 ms on Ogbn-Arxiv versus 389.6 ms for BGRL (×174 speedup), while also eliminating augmentation time entirely (0 ms vs. 98–54,272 ms for baselines).

## Baselines & Comparisons

S3GCL outperforms all nine baselines (DGI, GMI, MVGRL, GRACE, GBT, BGRL, DSSL, GREET, SP-GCL) on 11 of 14 datasets, with the largest margins on heterophilic graphs: +5.94% over SP-GCL on Texas (86.12 vs. 80.21), +2.90% over GREET on Roman-empire (66.27 vs. 63.37), and +2.94% over SP-GCL on Cornell (81.27 vs. 78.33). On homophilic graphs, gains are more modest but consistent, e.g., +1.02% over SP-GCL on Cora (88.47 vs. 87.45) and +1.75% over GREET on CiteSeer (76.31 vs. 74.56). S3GCL is narrowly outperformed on three datasets: SP-GCL leads on Amz-Comp (89.45 vs. 88.45), MVGRL on Minesweeper (79.78 vs. 79.33), and GBT on Tolokers (79.43 vs. 79.39), all by less than 1.1%. The efficiency advantage is substantial: S3GCL eliminates augmentation entirely (0 ms) and achieves ×174 faster inference on Ogbn-Arxiv compared to BGRL, while also requiring less training time than GRACE and GREET on both Cora and Actor.

## Relation to Prior Work

S3GCL sits at the intersection of three research threads: spectral GNNs (ChebNet and its Chebyshev-interpolation reparameterization), graph contrastive learning (InfoNCE-based objectives from GRACE, BGRL, etc.), and GNN-to-MLP knowledge distillation (GLNN, FF-G2M). It departs from standard GCL by replacing manual graph augmentations with a cross-architecture design that contrasts a full-pass MLP against biased-pass spectral GNN views, and from prior distillation work by eliminating the need for supervised labels through a self-supervised cross-pass objective. The cosine-parameterized Chebyshev polynomial is a targeted modification of the linear interpolation scheme in He et al. (2022b), reducing learnable parameters from K+1 to 2 per filter to enable label-free spectral filtering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practically important gaps in GCL: generalization across homophily levels (where most methods implicitly assume homophily) and inference latency (where GNN message passing is a bottleneck for real-time deployment). The 174× inference speedup on Ogbn-Arxiv while maintaining competitive accuracy is a concrete, deployable win. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that training still requires the polynomial spectral filter, so the efficiency gain is only realized at inference; making the entire pipeline MLP-only remains an open direction. The theoretical link between homophily and spectral frequency (Theorem 2.5) provides a useful design principle but is somewhat expected from spectral graph theory, and the method's reliance on kNN for semantic pairs introduces a preprocessing cost that scales with graph size.
