## Problem

Minimum of N (MoN) metrics such as minADE and minFDE are the de facto standard for evaluating multimodal trajectory predictions, yet they are not strictly proper scoring rules: a suboptimal predictive distribution can receive a lower error than the Bayes-optimal one, and scores are not comparable across different numbers of sampled trajectories K. This paper formalizes these failures under the proper scoring rules framework and demonstrates them empirically, showing that MoN can mislead model selection in safety-critical applications like autonomous driving.

## Input / Output

The method consumes K predicted trajectories of shape T×S (T temporal steps, S spatial dimensions, typically S=2 for 2D positions) drawn from a predictive distribution F_X, together with a single ground truth trajectory y of the same shape. It outputs three scalar scores: ES (joint spatiotemporal evaluation), EST (temporal score that marginalizes over spatial dimensions), and ESS (spatial score that marginalizes over temporal dimensions). The three variants differ only in how the Lp-norm distance is computed over the T×S matrix—entry-wise for ES, column-wise for EST, and row-wise for ESS—allowing practitioners to diagnose whether discrepancies are temporal, spatial, or joint.

## Method

The paper first proves that ADE_(L) (the L-lowest of N generalization of minADE) is improper for L≤2 by constructing explicit counterexamples where a degenerate or overconfident distribution outperforms the true distribution, and shows that ADE_(L) decreases monotonically with K and converges to zero for any distribution whose support covers the ground truth's support. As a strictly proper alternative, it adopts the Energy Score ES = E‖X−y‖_p^β − ½E‖X−X̃‖_p^β, whose first term (ED) rewards individual prediction accuracy while the second (EI) rewards diversity, achieving strict propriety for 0<β<2. The key practical contribution is three instantiations—ES, EST, and ESS—that differ in the axis over which the norm is computed, enabling joint, temporal, or spatial diagnosis of forecast quality. Empirically, the authors validate propriety on synthetic autoregressive trajectories and evaluate nine pre-trained models (STGCNN, SGCN, PECNET with MC/QMC/NPSN sampling) on ETH/UCY, showing that ES ranks models differently from minADE/minFDE in 3 of 5 datasets, revealing aspects of prediction quality that MoN misses.

## Results

On the ETH/UCY benchmark, the paper evaluates nine trajectory prediction models (STGCNN, SGCN, PECNET × MC/QMC/NPSN sampling) using both MoN metrics (minADE, minFDE) and the proposed Energy Score (ES). The headline finding is that ES and minADE/minFDE disagree on the best model in 3 out of 5 datasets (UNIV, ZARA1, ZARA2), where ES favors SGCN-QMC while minADE/minFDE favor SGCN-NPSN. On the arithmetic average across all datasets, ES assigns SGCN-QMC and SGCN-NPSN a tied score of 0.76, whereas minADE gives SGCN-NPSN the best score (0.21) and minFDE gives SGCN-NPSN the best (0.36). The propriety showcase further confirms that FES is minimized at the true distribution parameters while all MoN variants are minimized at higher-variance (suboptimal) distributions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates all nine pre-trained models from Bae et al. (CVPR 2022) — STGCNN, SGCN, and PECNET each with Monte-Carlo, Quasi-Monte-Carlo, and Non-Probability Sampling Network (NPSN) sampling — as the comparison set. The central finding is that minADE/minFDE consistently rank SGCN-NPSN as the best model across all five ETH/UCY sub-datasets, which is expected since NPSN explicitly optimizes minADE as part of its training objective. In contrast, the Energy Score ranks SGCN-QMC as the best model on three of five datasets (UNIV, ZARA1, ZARA2) and ties with SGCN-NPSN on ETH and the overall average (both at 0.76 ES). This disagreement implies that SGCN-NPSN's superior minADE/minFDE scores do not reflect a uniformly better predictive distribution; rather, ES captures aspects of distributional quality (e.g., calibration of uncertainty, diversity) that MoN metrics miss. The authors conclude that the choice between SGCN-NPSN and SGCN-QMC should depend on the downstream task, and that relying solely on MoN metrics can be misleading for safety-critical applications.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical analysis of Thiede & Brahma (2019), who showed that the variety loss (MoN) is not strictly proper and proposed a corrective procedure, by formally studying MoN under the proper scoring rules framework of Gneiting & Raftery (2007) and proving multiple propositions establishing its impropriety across all L-lowest-of-N variants. It then repurposes the Energy Score from the probabilistic forecasting literature (Gneiting & Raftery, 2007; Székely & Rizzo, 2013) as a strictly proper alternative, introducing three spatiotemporal variants (ES, EST, ESS) tailored to the structure of trajectory data as multivariate time series. The paper departs from the dominant evaluation paradigm in trajectory prediction, where minADE/minFDE are used as primary metrics, by arguing that distribution-aware evaluation via proper scoring rules is necessary for reliable model comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the field's standard evaluation metrics (minADE, minFDE) are provably improper means that model rankings in trajectory prediction literature may be misleading, with practical consequences for safety-critical applications like autonomous driving. The real-data experiment on ETH/UCY demonstrates that Energy Score and MoN disagree on which model is best in 3 out of 5 datasets, suggesting that practitioners may be selecting suboptimal models. Open questions include the computational cost of Energy Score (O(K²)), whether the choice between ES/EST/ESS should be task-dependent, and how to holistically reconcile different metrics when they produce conflicting rankings for downstream decision-making.
