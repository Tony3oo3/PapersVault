## Problem

Obtaining no-regret guarantees for reinforcement learning in continuous state and/or action spaces remains a major open challenge, as the problem is non-learnable without structural assumptions. Existing results cover specific parametric families (LQRs, Linear MDPs) or the very general but pessimistic Lipschitz MDP setting, leaving a large theoretical gap between them. This paper introduces ν-smoothness as a unifying assumption that generalizes most prior settings and yields regret bounds that adapt to the degree of smoothness.

## Input / Output

The algorithms operate on a finite-horizon MDP with state space [-1,1]^{d_S} and action space [-1,1]^{d_A}, where the transition and reward functions (or the Bellman optimality operator) satisfy a ν-smoothness condition in the C^{ν,1} Banach space. Over K episodes of horizon H, the agent observes state-action pairs and stochastic rewards, and outputs a sequence of policies whose cumulative regret is bounded. The smoothness order ν must exceed d/2 − 1 for LEGENDRE-ELEANOR or d − 1 for LEGENDRE-LSVI, and the feature map degree N is set as a function of K to balance approximation error and estimation error.

## Method

The core idea is to represent the continuous state-action space using a multivariate Legendre polynomial feature map, which forms an orthogonal basis in L²([-1,1]^d). This transforms the smooth MDP into a (approximately) Linear MDP in a finite-dimensional feature space of dimension Ñ = binom(N+d, N), enabling the use of existing linear-RL algorithms. LEGENDRE-ELEANOR applies ELEANOR to the Weakly Smooth setting by showing the Legendre representation yields bounded inherent Bellman error via Jackson-type approximation theorems, while LEGENDRE-LSVI applies LSVI-UCB to the Strongly Smooth setting where the MDP becomes exactly linear in the feature space. The orthogonality of Legendre polynomials is critical: it ensures Parseval-based norm control of the feature vectors and enables tight approximation bounds, as confirmed empirically by comparing against standard polynomial bases that fail to learn. The resulting regret is Õ(K^{(3d/2+ν+1)/(d+2(ν+1))}) for LEGENDRE-ELEANOR and Õ(H^{3/2} K^{(2d+ν+1)/(d+2(ν+1))}) for LEGENDRE-LSVI, both approaching √K as ν→∞.

## Results

For Weakly Smooth MDPs with smoothness order ν > d/2 − 1, LEGENDRE-ELEANOR achieves regret Õ(C_ELE^H · K^((3d/2+ν+1)/(d+2(ν+1)))), which is monotonically decreasing in ν and approaches Õ(√K) as ν → ∞. For Strongly Smooth MDPs with d ≤ ν+1, LEGENDRE-LSVI achieves regret Õ(H^(3/2) · K^((2d+ν+1)/(d+2(ν+1)))) with polynomial time complexity O(K²H), avoiding the exponential-in-H dependence. In the special case of Lipschitz MDPs (ν=0), LEGENDRE-ELEANOR yields regret K^((3d/2+1)/(d+2)), improving upon the prior best of K^((d+1)/(d+2)) for general-purpose algorithms. Empirically, on a modified LQR environment with H=20, the Legendre-based algorithm Leg(3) achieves near-optimal episodic return within ~100 episodes, while the standard polynomial baseline Poly(3) fails to learn significantly over 500 episodes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEGENDRE-ELEANOR dominates GOLF (Jin et al., 2021) on every setting where both apply: for Weakly Smooth MDPs, the regret exponent (3d/2+ν+1)/(d+2(ν+1)) is strictly smaller than GOLF's (2ν+3d+2)/(4ν+4) for all ν, and LEGENDRE-ELEANOR only requires ν > d/2−1 versus GOLF's stricter d < (2/3)ν+2/3. Against Song & Sun (2019), which achieves K^((d+1)/(d+2)) for Lipschitz MDPs, LEGENDRE-ELEANOR improves to K^((3d/2+1)/(d+2)), a meaningful gap that widens with dimension d. For Kernelized MDPs, LEGENDRE-ELEANOR matches or improves upon Vakili & Olkhovskaya (2023): the exponent (3d/2+m)/(d+2m) is smaller than (d+m+1)/(d+2(m+1)) for typical parameter ranges. LEGENDRE-LSVI is the only algorithm in the table that is both polynomial-time and achieves no-regret for Strongly Smooth MDPs; Ren et al. (2022) achieves a comparable K-exponent but requires Gaussian noise and is computationally inefficient. For LQRs and Linear MDPs with infinitely smooth feature maps, both proposed algorithms recover the optimal √K rate, matching Dean et al. (2018) and Jin et al. (2020) respectively, while additionally handling the unknown-feature-map case for Linear MDPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies several previously separate continuous-space RL settings (Lipschitz MDPs, Kernelized MDPs, LQRs, Linear MDPs) under a single ν-smoothness assumption, showing they are all special cases of either Strongly Smooth or Weakly Smooth MDPs. The technical core builds on two existing algorithmic frameworks—ELEANOR for low inherent Bellman error (Zanette et al., 2020) and LSVI-UCB for Linear MDPs (Jin et al., 2020)—but the key departure is the use of orthogonal Legendre polynomial feature maps to reduce smooth MDPs to linear representations, drawing on classical approximation theory (Jackson theorems) rather than RKHS or parametric structure. This contrasts with Kernelized MDPs, which require a specific kernel (e.g., Matérn) and known norm bounds, and with general-purpose algorithms like GOLF (Jin et al., 2021) that rely on Bellman-Eluder dimension assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified smoothness framework provides a single regret bound that adapts continuously from Lipschitz-level (K^{(d+1)/(d+2)}) to parametric-level (√K) as ν increases, filling a theoretical gap between the two extremes. LEGENDRE-LSVI's polynomial-time complexity (O(K²H)) is a practical advantage over ELEANOR-based methods, and the result that √K regret is achievable for Linear MDPs even without knowing the feature map has independent interest. However, the regret bounds' optimality for the general smooth MDP setting remains open, the exponential H-dependence in LEGENDRE-ELEANOR (though proven necessary for Lipschitz MDPs) limits practical applicability to long horizons, and the algorithms have not been tested on large-scale real-world problems.
