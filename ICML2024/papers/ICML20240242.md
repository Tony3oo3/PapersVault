## Problem

Existing human pose and expression retargeting methods face a fundamental trade-off: GAN-based approaches struggle with occluded body parts and fail to generalize beyond their training domain, while recent diffusion-based methods (DreamPose, DisCo) still suffer from poor identity preservation, limited generalizability to unseen styles, or the need for domain-specific fine-tuning. No prior method achieves robust, zero-shot identity-preserving pose retargeting that works across diverse human appearances and complex motions without additional training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference image of a person (512×512 RGB) and a target pose condition consisting of an OpenPose skeleton map and facial landmark map, which can originate from entirely different sources. It produces a re-posed 512×512 image that follows the target pose and expression while strictly preserving the reference person's identity (skin tone, clothing, facial features) and background. The entire pipeline operates as a plug-in to Stable Diffusion v1.5 without modifying its frozen pre-trained UNet weights, and an optional AnimateDiff motion module can be appended for sequential video output (16-frame batches).

## Method

MagicPose decomposes pose retargeting into two disentangled sub-tasks via a two-stage training strategy. Stage 1 (Appearance Control Pre-training) trains a parallel UNet copy of the SD-UNet that injects appearance guidance through a Multi-Source Self-Attention Module, which concatenates key-value pairs from both the frozen SD-UNet and the appearance control branch at every self-attention layer (encoder, middle, decoder) and every denoising timestep. Stage 2 (Appearance-disentangled Pose Control) jointly fine-tunes the appearance model and an OpenPose-initialized ControlNet so that the ControlNet exclusively encodes pose/expression while the appearance model handles identity. This design differs from prior attention-based methods (MasaCtrl, Reference-Only ControlNet) by using concatenation rather than replacement of K/V, operating across all layers and timesteps rather than a subset, and requiring no text prompt for appearance guidance. Training uses randomly sampled frame pairs from the same person in TikTok dance videos (335 sequences), with data augmentation via random masking of facial landmarks and hand poses to handle OpenPose detection failures.

## Results

On the TikTok dataset, MagicPose achieves the best performance across all image and video metrics: FID of 25.50, SSIM of 0.752, PSNR of 29.53, LPIPS of 0.292, L1 of 0.81E-04, Face-Cos of 0.426, and FID-VID of 46.30. The most striking improvement is in identity preservation (Face-Cos), where MagicPose scores 0.426 compared to 0.166 for the strongest baseline DisCo†, a +0.260 absolute gain. In a 100-participant user study, MagicPose receives 73% of votes for best identity preservation, far exceeding DisCo (16%) and all GAN-based methods (3–4% each).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MagicPose outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest diffusion-based competitor DisCo† (which benefits from additional pre-training data), MagicPose reduces FID from 30.75 to 25.50, improves SSIM from 0.668 to 0.752, and more than doubles Face-Cos from 0.166 to 0.426, indicating substantially better identity preservation. Against the GAN-based methods (FOMM, MRAA, TPS), MagicPose achieves 2–3× lower FID and 1.3–5× higher Face-Cos, while also producing temporally more consistent video (FID-VID of 46.30 vs. 66–90). DreamPose, the other diffusion-based baseline, is the weakest performer overall (FID 72.62, Face-Cos 0.085), confirming that simply adding pose conditioning to Stable Diffusion without explicit appearance control is insufficient. The user study reinforces these quantitative findings: MagicPose receives 73% of votes versus 16% for DisCo and 3–4% for each GAN method, with a chi-square test (χ²=116.02, p=1.11×10⁻¹²) confirming the preference is statistically significant.

## Relation to Prior Work

MagicPose builds directly on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion architecture and ControlNet's conditional generation framework, but departs from prior diffusion-based pose transfer methods (DreamPose, DisCo) by introducing a dedicated Appearance Control Model with a Multi-Source Self-Attention Module that concatenates rather than replaces key-value pairs from the reference image. This contrasts with MasaCtrl and Reference Only ControlNet, which are inference-only and require text prompts, whereas MagicPose is trained end-to-end with a two-stage strategy that explicitly disentangles appearance from pose control. The approach also supersedes GAN-based motion transfer methods (FOMM, MRAA, TPS) that rely on image warping and struggle with occluded body parts and out-of-domain generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in design (frozen SD weights, no modification to the base model) makes MagicPose practically deployable as an extension to existing Stable Diffusion pipelines, and the zero-shot generalization to unseen identities, full-body motions, and out-of-domain styles (cartoons, paintings) without fine-tuning is a strong practical advantage over prior methods that required domain-specific adaptation. Key limitations include reliance on OpenPose for pose detection (which produces incomplete skeletons under occlusion or rapid motion) and evaluation primarily on upper-body dance videos. Open questions include whether the two-stage training is strictly necessary versus joint training, whether the approach extends to 3D-consistent multi-view generation, and how it scales to more complex scene compositions beyond single-person retargeting.
