## Problem

Reinforcement learning with observation delays is hindered by a fundamental trade-off: augmentation-based methods that recover the Markov property by appending action histories suffer exponential state-space growth with delay length, while belief-projection methods (e.g., BPQL) that approximate values on the delay-free space lose critical information in stochastic environments. No existing approach simultaneously achieves sample-efficient learning under long delays and robust performance when environment dynamics are stochastic.

## Input / Output

The agent receives only the delayed observation s_{t−Δ} at time t and must maintain a rolling buffer of the last Δ actions to construct the augmented state x_t = (s_{t−Δ}, a_{t−Δ}, …, a_{t−1}). Crucially, AD-RL simultaneously constructs a second, shorter augmented state x_t^τ = (s_{t−Δ^τ}, a_{t−Δ^τ}, …, a_{t−1}) from the same trajectory, so both state spaces are available without additional environment queries. The output is a policy mapping the full augmented state to a discrete action (AD-DQN) or continuous action (AD-SAC), along with two Q-function networks operating on the respective augmented spaces.

## Method

AD-RL introduces an auxiliary task with shorter delay Δ^τ < Δ, whose augmented state space X^τ is exponentially smaller than the original X. A delayed belief function b_Δ(x^τ|x) bridges the two spaces by marginalizing over the intermediate unobserved states, allowing the auxiliary Q-function Q^τ to bootstrap the full-delay Q-function through a modified Bellman operator (Eq. 3) for discrete control or to drive both policy evaluation and improvement in a soft actor-critic loop (Eqs. 4–6) for continuous control. In practice, the agent selects actions conservatively by evaluating candidates from both policies under Q^τ, and the two Q-networks are updated alternately from the same replay buffer. The auxiliary delay Δ^τ is a tunable hyperparameter: setting it to 0 recovers BPQL (maximum sample efficiency but high approximation error in stochastic settings), while larger values retain more information at the cost of a larger auxiliary state space. Theoretical analysis shows the sample complexity improves by O(|A|^{Δ−Δ^τ}) and the performance gap to the optimal augmented policy is bounded by the L1-Wasserstein distance between the two policies, vanishing entirely in deterministic MDPs.

## Results

On stochastic MuJoCo (9 tasks, 1M timesteps), AD-SAC achieves the best normalized score on all 9 tasks, with particularly large margins on Swimmer-v4 (2.36 vs. 1.53 for BPQL) and Walker2d-v4 (1.19 vs. 0.89 for DC/AC). On deterministic MuJoCo with 25 delays, AD-SAC leads on 6 of 9 tasks (e.g., Ant-v4: 0.66, Pusher-v4: 1.29, Swimmer-v4: 2.52), while BPQL edges ahead on HalfCheetah-v4 (0.87) and Hopper-v4 (1.21). On deterministic Acrobot, AD-DQN converges within 500k steps versus A-DQN's failure to learn any useful policy beyond 20 delays, while AD-DQN remains robust up to 50 delays.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AD-SAC outperforms all four continuous-control baselines (A-SAC, DC/AC, DIDA, BPQL) on every stochastic MuJoCo task, with the largest gaps on Swimmer-v4 (+0.83 over BPQL) and Walker2d-v4 (+0.30 over DC/AC). BPQL, which is a special case of AD-RL with fixed zero auxiliary delays (Δ^τ=0), is consistently beaten by AD-SAC in stochastic settings, confirming that retaining some auxiliary delay information is critical when the environment is non-deterministic. In the deterministic MuJoCo setting (25 delays), AD-SAC and BPQL are competitive (BPQL wins on HalfCheetah and Hopper), consistent with the theory that the performance gap vanishes for deterministic MDPs. For discrete control, AD-DQN learns substantially faster than A-DQN (augmented DQN) on Acrobot and remains effective up to 50 delays, whereas A-DQN completely fails beyond 20 delays due to the exponential growth of the augmented state space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the augmentation-based approach to delayed RL (Altman & Nain, 1992; Katsikopoulos & Engelbrecht, 2003), which retrieves the Markov property by appending action history to the observed state, but which suffers from exponential state-space growth. It generalizes BPQL (Kim et al., 2023), which projects the augmented value function onto the delay-free space (equivalent to setting auxiliary delay Δ^τ = 0), by introducing an intermediate auxiliary-delayed task with 0 < Δ^τ < Δ. The framework is instantiated on top of DQN and SAC, and the theoretical analysis builds on Lipschitz-continuity assumptions standard in the RL literature (Rachelson & Lagoudakis, 2010; Liotet et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled trade-off knob (auxiliary delay Δ^τ) between sample efficiency (favors Δ^τ → 0) and approximation accuracy in stochastic environments (favors Δ^τ → Δ), with theoretical bounds on the performance gap and convergence guarantees. A key open question is the selection of Δ^τ, which the authors show is irregular and task-specific rather than following a simple functional form of Δ, making automated selection a non-trivial challenge. The implicit belief representation also incurs additional memory cost, and explicit belief learning remains an open direction. For practitioners deploying RL in latency-sensitive domains (robotics, trading, building control), this offers a drop-in improvement over both full augmentation and BPQL.
