## Problem

Online coverage path planning in unknown environments requires simultaneously mapping, planning, and navigating without prior knowledge of the area geometry. Previous RL-based approaches either constrained the path space with discrete grid actions or embedded RL within multi-stage pipelines, and none addressed the scalability challenge of representing large environments efficiently. This paper asks whether a single end-to-end RL model can learn continuous control signals for CPP while scaling to arbitrarily large areas.

## Input / Output

The agent observes four egocentric multi-scale maps (coverage, obstacle, frontier) at 32×32 pixels per scale with a scale factor of 4 and 0.0375 m/pixel resolution in the finest scale, spanning up to 76.8 m, plus a vector of lidar distance readings normalized to [0,1]. The frontier maps are the key design choice: a cell at any coarser scale is marked as frontier if and only if it contains at least one frontier point at the finest scale, preserving the existence of non-covered free space through all resolutions. The model outputs continuous linear and angular velocities normalized to [-1,1], directly controlling a differential-drive agent.

## Method

The agent is trained with Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) using a proposed Scale-Grouped CNN (SGCNN) that convolves each scale of the multi-scale maps independently via grouped convolutions (a 2×2 stride-2 layer followed by three 3×3 stride-1 layers with 24 channels), since pixel positions across scales do not correspond to the same world coordinates. A novel incremental total variation (TV) reward penalizes increases in the boundary length of the coverage map, discouraging the agent from leaving small uncovered holes that are costly to revisit. Training uses curriculum learning with progressively harder maps and increasing goal coverage thresholds (90%→99%), combined with randomly generated floor plans and obstacles to improve generalization. The multi-scale frontier representation reduces memory from O(d²) to O(wh·log d) grid cells, making large environments computationally feasible.

## Results

On Explore-Bench (omnidirectional exploration), the proposed method achieves a total T90 of 556 s and T99 of 1061 s across six environments, roughly halving the time compared to the best frontier-based method (Potential field frontier: 967 s / 1559 s) and tripling the speed over Active Neural SLAM (1781 s / 2228 s). On the lawn mowing task, the online method reaches 90% coverage in 310 min and 99% in 409 min total, which is 35% and 51% slower than the offline BSA baseline (229 min / 270 min) but faster than both the online TSP baseline (448 min / 498 min) and the offline TSP baseline for T90 (325 min). The method also outperforms the frontier-based RL approach of Hu et al. (2020) in non-omnidirectional exploration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against seven baselines across three CPP variations. On Explore-Bench (omnidirectional exploration), the proposed end-to-end RL method outperforms all three frontier-based methods (distance, RRT, potential field) by roughly 45–55% in total T90 time (556 s vs. 967–1054 s), and substantially outperforms Active Neural SLAM (1781 s) by over 3×. In non-omnidirectional exploration (180° lidar), the method beats the frontier-based RL approach of Hu et al. (2020), which was specifically trained for that sensor configuration. For lawn mowing, the method is 35% slower than the offline BSA baseline (Gonzalez et al., 2005) for T90, which is expected since BSA has full environment knowledge; however, it outperforms the online TSP-based solution (Bormann et al., 2018) by 31% in T90 (310 vs. 448 min) and even surpasses the offline TSP for T90 (310 vs. 325 min). The method also demonstrates robustness to noise, maintaining performance under three levels of Gaussian noise in position, heading, and lidar measurements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant multi-stage paradigm in RL-based coverage path planning, where a model selects a frontier goal and a separate controller navigates to it (Hu et al., 2020; Niroui et al., 2019), by predicting continuous control signals end-to-end via Soft Actor-Critic. It builds on the egocentric map representation from Chen et al. (2019a) and extends it with a multi-scale frontier map structure inspired by Klamt & Behnke (2018), making large-environment coverage computationally feasible. The total variation reward term is a novel addition not present in prior RL-based CPP work, addressing the specific failure mode of leaving thin uncovered stripes that area-only rewards fail to penalize adequately.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a task- and robot-agnostic framework for online CPP that scales to large environments via O(log n) multi-scale maps, and demonstrates that end-to-end continuous RL can match or exceed highly specialized planning methods (BSA, TSP+A*) while simultaneously solving the mapping problem. Key limitations include the 2D setting, single-agent assumption, known-pose requirement, and 100–150 hour training cost per agent, which limits immediate practical deployment. Open questions include sim-to-real transfer under unmodeled dynamics, extension to 3D or multi-agent settings, and whether the TV reward generalizes to non-grid coverage metrics.
