## Problem

Accumulator-aware quantization (A2Q) constrains weight ℓ₁-norms to guarantee overflow avoidance at low accumulator bit widths, but relies on an overly restrictive bound derived via Hölder's inequality and a naive initialization that forces networks to recover from superfluous quantization error. This paper identifies and corrects both shortcomings to improve the accuracy-vs-accumulator-bit-width trade-off without compromising overflow guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (CIFAR-10 at 32×32, ImageNet at 224×224) for classification or low-resolution images (BSD300) for 3× super-resolution, and produces class labels or upscaled images respectively. During inference, all hidden layers use uniform M-bit weights and N-bit activations accumulated into a P-bit signed register, with the quantization mapping (zero-centering, round-to-zero, clipping) applied once offline before deployment, incurring zero inference overhead.

## Method

A2Q+ introduces two key improvements over A2Q: (1) an improved ℓ₁-norm bound (Eq. 7) derived by zero-centering the quantized weight vector so that Σqᵢ=0, which exploits the full accumulator range and is agnostic to input sign, yielding up to 4× more ℓ₁-norm budget for unsigned activations; and (2) a Euclidean projection-based initialization (EP-init) that projects pre-trained floating-point weights onto an ℓ₁-ball of radius T to minimize initial quantization error. The resulting quantizer reparameterizes weights as w = (v − μᵥ)/‖v − μᵥ‖₁ · min(g, T₊), where T₊ is the new bound scaled by the per-channel scaling factor s, uses round-to-zero rounding to preserve the zero-centering constraint through quantization, and is trained via QAT with the straight-through estimator and a regularization penalty on g exceeding T₊.

## Results

On ImageNet W4A4, A2Q+ achieves 72.0% top-1 accuracy on ResNet50 at 12-bit accumulation, a +17% improvement over A2Q (55.0%) while retaining 95% of the 76.13% floating-point baseline. At 14-bit accumulation, A2Q+ maintains 75.7% on ResNet50 (vs. 73.8% for A2Q) and 71.4% on ResNet34 (vs. 65.8%). On CIFAR-10, A2Q+ Pareto-dominates A2Q across all accumulator bit widths, e.g., achieving 89.9% top-1 on MobileNetV1 at P=11 (vs. 71.8% for A2Q) and 92.8% on ResNet18 at P=12 (vs. 89.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole direct baseline is A2Q (Colbert et al., ICCV 2023), the prior state-of-the-art for accumulator-aware quantization with guaranteed overflow avoidance. A2Q+ consistently outperforms A2Q across all three ImageNet ResNet variants and all tested accumulator bit widths (P=12, 14, 16), with the largest gap at P=12: +17% top-1 on ResNet50 (72.0% vs. 55.0%), +18.7% on ResNet34 (62.1% vs. 43.4%), and +20.9% on ResNet18 (56.4% vs. 35.5%). The improvement is also substantial at P=14 (+1.9% on ResNet50, +5.6% on ResNet34, +6.8% on ResNet18) while at P=16 both methods nearly match the floating-point baseline. Notably, A2Q+ also achieves lower sparsity at the same accuracy level (e.g., 84.4% vs. 90.7% sparsity on ResNet50 at P=12), indicating a more efficient use of the weight budget. The Euclidean projection initialization (EP-init) independently improves A2Q by up to +11.7% on ResNet50 at P=12, confirming that initialization quality is a major factor in low-precision accumulation performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends A2Q (Colbert et al., ICCV 2023), which first established accumulator-aware quantization with guaranteed overflow avoidance by constraining the ℓ₁-norm of weights during QAT. The authors show that A2Q's bound is overly restrictive (not fully utilizing the accumulator's representation range and depending on input sign) and that its naive initialization from floating-point checkpoints introduces superfluous quantization error. The improved bound leverages zero-centering of weights (connecting to weight normalization and centered weight normalization literature), while the initialization strategy draws on the classical Euclidean projection onto an ℓ₁-ball (Duchi et al., 2008). The work also positions itself against prior overflow-mitigation approaches (Xie et al., Ni et al., Blumenfeld et al.) that either tune scale factors or train for wraparound robustness, in contrast to A2Q's guarantee-based approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for hardware-constrained deployment: reducing accumulator width from 32 to 16 bits yields ~2× throughput on ARM and 1.6× resource savings on FPGAs, and A2Q+ makes even more aggressive reductions (12-bit) viable with only modest accuracy loss. The zero-centering bound providing up to 4× more ℓ₁-norm budget for unsigned activations is a clean theoretical result that could inform future quantizer design. Open questions include: balancing the activation bit width N vs. accumulator bit width P trade-off (identified but deferred), whether the benefits extend to post-training quantization (they do not, due to round-to-zero), controlling the unstructured sparsity patterns that emerge, and whether the implicit dimensionality reduction from zero-centering can be mitigated for depthwise convolutions.
