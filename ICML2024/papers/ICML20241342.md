## Problem

Existing subgraph-level GNN explainers face two fundamental limitations: they typically induce explanations by selecting nodes (which can produce disconnected, unintuitive subgraphs and miss important edge structures like angle-shaped motifs), and they impose a fixed sparsity or size uniformly across all instances rather than adapting to each graph's true explanatory substructure. Additionally, the most faithful search-based methods (e.g., SubgraphX with MCTS) are computationally prohibitive, while training-based explainers introduce non-deterministic behavior across runs.

## Input / Output

Given a pre-trained GNN (GCN or GIN) and a single graph instance represented by its adjacency matrix A and node feature matrix X, EiG-Search produces an edge-induced subgraph explanation whose size is automatically determined per instance. The method first computes a scalar importance score for each edge via a linear-gradient approximation (the slope of a latent line from a zero-weight baseline adjacency to the original), then outputs the top-k edge set that maximizes Fidelity⁺ − Fidelity⁻. No hyperparameters (sparsity ratio, number of nodes/edges) need to be specified by the user.

## Method

EiG-Search is a training-free, two-phase algorithm with O(|E|) time complexity. In Phase 1, each edge's importance is approximated by a 'Linear Gradient': the GNN's prediction difference between the original graph and a graph where that edge's weight is set to zero, divided by the L1 distance between the two adjacency matrices in latent space. This avoids the gradient saturation problem of standard backpropagation and differs from Integrated Gradients by requiring no path integration—only a single straight-line evaluation. In Phase 2, edges are sorted by importance, and the algorithm iterates k from 1 to |E|, inducing a subgraph from the top-k edges and scoring it by Fidelity⁺ − Fidelity⁻, selecting the k that maximizes this score. The paper also provides theoretical proofs (Theorems 3.6–3.7) that edge-induced subgraphs are strictly more intuitive and at least as exhaustive as node-induced or node-and-edge-induced subgraphs, justifying the edge-based inducing strategy.

## Results

On node classification, EiG-Search achieves AUC of 0.999 (BA-Shapes), 0.996 (BA-Community), and 0.947 (Tree-Grid), matching or exceeding the best baselines (RCExplainer: 0.998/0.995/0.995; DEGREE: 0.991/0.984/0.935). On graph classification, EiG-Search achieves the highest overall fidelity (Fidelity⁺ − Fidelity⁻) across all sparsity levels on BA-2Motifs, MUTAG, Mutagenicity, and NCI1 with both GCN and GNN. In terms of efficiency, EiG-Search explains a graph in 0.003–0.14 s (training-free), compared to 0.05–1.44 s for GNNExplainer/PGExplainer/DEGREE and 419.8 s for SubgraphX on Mutagenicity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EiG-Search outperforms all 10 baselines in overall fidelity (Fidelity⁺ − Fidelity⁻) across all six graph-classification settings (BA-2Motifs, MUTAG, Mutagenicity, NCI1 with GCN/GIN), as shown in Figure 3. On node-classification AUC, EiG-Search matches or slightly exceeds RCExplainer (0.999 vs 0.998 on BA-Shapes; 0.996 vs 0.995 on BA-Community) but trails RCExplainer on Tree-Grid (0.947 vs 0.995), while still beating DEGREE (0.935) and PGExplainer (0.907). Among training-free baselines, EiG-Search consistently surpasses SA, Grad-CAM, SubgraphX, and DEGREE in fidelity, and also outperforms Integrated Gradients (IG) despite IG's similar global-importance design. In efficiency, EiG-Search (0.14 s explain time, no training) is roughly 10× faster than GNNExplainer (1.44 s) and ~3000× faster than SubgraphX (419.8 s) on Mutagenicity, while PGExplainer, RG-Explainer, and RCExplainer additionally require 977 s, 6360 s, and 76229 s of explainer training respectively. The paper also shows that augmenting any baseline (SA, Grad-CAM, IG, GNNExplainer, PGExplainer) with EiG-Search's Linear-Complexity Search improves their fidelity, confirming the search component's independent value.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of gradient-based attribution and subgraph search for GNN explainability, without directly building on a single prior method. It repurposes the base-point concept from Integrated Gradients (Sundararajan et al., 2017) but replaces the path integral with a single linear gradient, avoiding both saturation and path-sensitivity issues. It departs from the dominant node-induced subgraph paradigm (SubgraphX, DEGREE, RG-Explainer) by formally proving that edge-induced subgraphs are always at least as intuitive and exhaustive (Theorems 3.6–3.7), and replaces exponential or MCTS-based search with a linear scan over edges sorted by importance. The training-free design also distinguishes it from the large family of auxiliary-model explainers (GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, PGM-Explainer) that the authors argue introduce non-deterministic behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, provably linear-time, training-free method for subgraph-level GNN explanations that practitioners can adopt without tuning hyperparameters or training secondary models, addressing a real efficiency bottleneck in current explainers. The formal theorems establishing edge-induced superiority over node-induced subgraphs give the community a principled justification for shifting away from the node-selection paradigm that dominates existing work. The finding that optimal explanation size varies per instance challenges the widespread one-size-fits-all sparsity assumption. Open questions remain: the authors acknowledge that a more accurate edge-ranking algorithm and an advanced search guided by edge importance could further improve fidelity at the cost of efficiency, and the method's performance on very large graphs or multi-hop structural explanations is not deeply explored.
