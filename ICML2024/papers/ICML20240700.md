## Problem

Large neural networks require linear memory for optimization states (momentum, gradient accumulation), creating a scaling bottleneck. LoRA reduces this by training fewer parameters but constrains all weight updates to a low-rank subspace, degrading model quality. The paper asks whether sublinear optimization-state memory can be achieved without sacrificing the ability to perform full-rank weight updates.

## Input / Output

FLORA operates on per-layer weight matrices W ∈ R^{n×m} and their corresponding gradient matrices G ∈ R^{n×m}. It compresses each gradient into an R^{n×r} accumulator via a random Gaussian down-projection A ∈ R^{r×m} (sampled from N(0, 1/r)), then decompresses back to R^{n×m} at update time. The projection matrix is regenerated from a stored random seed rather than kept in memory, and is resampled every κ steps (momentum) or every accumulation cycle (gradient accumulation), which is the key mechanism that breaks LoRA's fixed low-rank constraint.

## Method

The paper first proves (Theorem 2.1) that LoRA's SGD dynamics reduce to a fixed random projection of the gradient followed by an up-projection, with the B-matrix term dominating when the learning rate is small. FLORA then applies this compression-decompression directly to the full weight matrix's optimization states, but resamples the projection matrix periodically so the cumulative update is no longer confined to a rank-r subspace. For gradient accumulation, compressed gradients are summed in R^{n×r} space and decompressed once per cycle; for momentum, the compressed EMA is transferred between old and new projections via M' = M_{t-1} A_{t-1} A_t^⊤ at each resampling step. Theoretical guarantees come from the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma (norm preservation under compression) and a concentration bound (Theorem 2.4) showing r = O(log(m/δ)·ε^{-2}) suffices for element-wise reconstruction of A^⊤A ≈ I, making the memory overhead O(nr) with a smaller constant than LoRA's O(nr) for two matrices plus their optimizer states.

## Results

On gradient accumulation, FLORA(256) on GPT-2-1.5B achieves 33.6 BLEU on IWSLT-17 De-En, matching the naive full-accumulation baseline (33.2 BLEU) while using only 2.04 GiB extra memory versus 5.78 GiB for naive. On T5-3B summarization, FLORA(512) reaches 44.5/20.9/36.4 ROUGE, on par with naive accumulation (44.4/20.9/36.3), at 5.4 GiB overhead versus 9.9 GiB. For momentum compression, FLORA(256) on GPT-2-110M recovers the full-momentum BLEU score of 19.9 with only 0.25 GiB extra memory, while LoRA(256) collapses to 9.83 BLEU. The memory overhead of FLORA at r=256 is roughly 30% of naive accumulation for large models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLORA dramatically outperforms LoRA across all settings: on GPT-2-110M, FLORA(256) achieves 25.4 BLEU versus LoRA(256) at only 13.4 BLEU (a 12-point gap), and on T5-3B, FLORA(256) scores 44.3/20.7/36.2 ROUGE versus LoRA(256) at 42.6/18.9/34.4. Against the naive full-accumulation baseline, FLORA matches or slightly exceeds performance at higher ranks (e.g., FLORA(512) on GPT-2-1.5B: 33.6 vs. 33.2 BLEU) while reducing memory overhead by roughly 65% (2.04 vs. 5.78 GiB). Notably, LoRA actually increases memory usage over the no-accumulation baseline when paired with Adafactor (due to extra low-rank matrices and Jacobian paths), whereas FLORA consistently reduces memory. In the momentum setting, FLORA(256) on GPT-2-110M recovers the full-momentum BLEU of 19.9, while LoRA(256) drops to 9.83. The paper also reports that FLORA outperforms the concurrent GaLore method in both memory reduction and model quality on C4 language modeling (Appendix C.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) by providing a theoretical analysis (Theorem 2.1) showing that LoRA's SGD dynamics reduce to a fixed random projection of the gradient, effectively reframing LoRA from a parameter-efficient fine-tuning method to a gradient compressor. It departs from LoRA by resampling the projection matrix at each step, which breaks the low-rank constraint on total weight updates while preserving sublinear memory complexity. The approach also connects to the memory-efficient optimizer line (Adafactor, 8-bit quantization) and the classical random projection literature (Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma), positioning FLORA as orthogonal to and composable with existing sublinear-memory optimizers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FLORA offers a practical path to training large models with sublinear optimization-state memory while retaining full-rank updates, addressing a key bottleneck for scaling beyond current hardware limits. The conceptual reframing of LoRA as a gradient compressor provides a unifying lens that may inform future parameter-efficient methods. However, the largest model tested is 7B, and the method does not reduce backpropagation memory, so its full practical impact at GPT-3 scale (175B) remains unverified. Open questions include whether the resampling interval κ and rank r can be adapted dynamically per layer, and how FLORA interacts with quantization or sparsity-based compression.
