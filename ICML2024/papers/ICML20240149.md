## Problem

Existing stochastic Frank-Wolfe (projection-free) methods for finite-sum constrained optimization suffer from two practical limitations: they either require periodic computation of full deterministic gradients or rely on large, geometrically growing batch sizes. Moreover, their convergence rates in terms of dataset size n and accuracy ε are suboptimal compared to what is achievable in the unconstrained setting. This paper asks whether one can obtain the best-known stochastic oracle complexities for both convex and non-convex objectives while using only small, fixed-size batches and (ideally) no full gradients at all.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a finite-sum objective f(x) = (1/n)Σ f_i(x) over a convex, compact constraint set X of known diameter D that admits a fast linear minimization oracle (LMO), and accesses the objective only through stochastic gradient evaluations ∇f_i at individual points. It produces an approximate constrained minimizer x^K together with explicit oracle-complexity guarantees: O~(n + √n·L̃D²/ε) stochastic first-order oracle calls and O~(√n + L̃D²/ε) LMO calls in the convex case (with batch size b = √n), and O(n + √n·[L²D⁴ + (h⁰)²]/ε²) calls in the non-convex case. Both algorithms are parameter-free, requiring no knowledge of the smoothness constants L or L̃ at runtime.

## Method

The paper introduces two algorithms built on the Frank-Wolfe update x^{k+1} = x^k + η_k(s^k − x^k) where s^k = argmin_{s∈X} ⟨g^k, s⟩, but replaces the deterministic gradient with a variance-reduced stochastic estimator. Algorithm 1 (Sarah Frank-Wolfe) uses the loopless PAGE/SARAH recursive estimator: with probability p it resets g^{k+1} = ∇f(x^{k+1}), and with probability 1−p it updates g^{k+1} = g^k + (1/b)Σ_{i∈S_k}[∇f_i(x^{k+1}) − ∇f_i(x^k)], with the optimal choice p = 2b/(n+2b) balancing full-gradient cost against variance. Algorithm 2 (Saga Sarah Frank-Wolfe) eliminates full gradients entirely by combining the SARAH recursive term with a SAGA-style table {y_i} of per-sample gradients, using a momentum parameter λ = b/(2n) to blend the two estimators. The convergence proofs rely on a Lyapunov function that jointly tracks the suboptimality f(x^k) − f(x*) and the estimator error ‖g^k − ∇f(x^k)‖², yielding linear convergence in the convex case and O(1/√K) Frank-Wolfe-gap convergence in the non-convex case.

## Results

In the convex case, both Algorithm 1 (Sarah Frank-Wolfe) and Algorithm 2 (Saga Sarah Frank-Wolfe) achieve a stochastic first-order oracle (SFO) complexity of Õ(n + √n·L̃D²/ε) and a linear minimization oracle (LMO) complexity of Õ(√n + L̃D²/ε) with batch size b=√n, which are the best known rates for stochastic projection-free methods. In the non-convex case, both algorithms achieve O(n + √n·L̃(L²D⁴+(h⁰)²)/(Lε²)) SFO complexity, matching the state-of-the-art and optimal per lower bounds. Algorithm 2 additionally eliminates the need for full deterministic gradient computations entirely, using only 2b stochastic gradient evaluations per iteration, while maintaining identical convergence guarantees.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines that avoid large batches: Mokhtari et al. (2020), Négiar et al. (2020), Lu & Freund (2021), Akhtar & Rajawat (2021), and Weber & Sra (2022). In the convex case, the proposed methods strictly dominate all baselines in ε-dependence: achieving Õ(1/ε) SFO complexity versus O(1/ε²) for Akhtar & Rajawat, O(1/ε³) for Mokhtari et al. and Weber & Sra, and O(n/ε) for Négiar et al. and Lu & Freund. In the non-convex case, the proposed O(n+√n/ε²) rate matches Yurtsever et al. (2019) and Gao & Huang (2020) but is achieved with small batches (b=1), whereas those methods require large or growing batches. Experimentally, on five LibSVM datasets (a9a, breast cancer, madelon, mushrooms, w8a) for both convex logistic regression and non-convex non-linear least squares with ℓ₁-ball constraints, the proposed algorithms converge faster in terms of full gradient computations than all baselines, despite the baselines (Négiar et al., Lu & Freund) being specifically designed for linear models. Algorithm 2, which avoids full gradients entirely, matches Algorithm 1's convergence in practice while requiring only 2b stochastic gradient calls per iteration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Frank-Wolfe (Conditional Gradient) method and the rich family of variance-reduced stochastic optimizers (SARAH, SAGA, SVRG, SPIDER, PAGE) by combining them into two new projection-free algorithms for finite-sum constrained minimization. Algorithm 1 (Sarah Frank-Wolfe) replaces the SVRG-based gradient estimator used in prior stochastic FW methods (Reddi et al., 2016; Yurtsever et al., 2019; Gao & Huang, 2020; Weber & Sra, 2022) with the loopless SARAH/PAGE estimator, yielding strictly better convergence rates in both convex and non-convex settings. Algorithm 2 (Saga Sarah Frank-Wolfe) further eliminates the need for full deterministic gradient computations by incorporating SAGA's table of per-sample gradients, a combination not previously explored in the projection-free literature. The paper also identifies and corrects a proof error in Reddi et al. (2016) and notes that several prior results (Qu et al., 2018; Gao & Huang, 2020) violate known lower bounds due to inconsistent convergence criteria.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes state-of-the-art oracle complexities for stochastic projection-free methods, with Algorithm 2 achieving the best known rates while requiring neither large batches nor full gradient evaluations—a practical advantage for large-scale ML problems where computing the full gradient is prohibitive. The LMO complexity in the convex case and SFO complexity in the non-convex case match known lower bounds, closing optimality gaps. Open questions remain for the SFO rate in the convex setting and the LMO rate in the non-convex case, where the current results have an unclosed gap to lower bounds. The authors also suggest that combining their variance-reduced approach with the practical acceleration techniques of Jaggi (2013) and Lacoste-Julien & Jaggi (2015) could yield faster strongly-convex convergence, and that the Lyapunov-function analysis technique may generalize to distributed and compressed optimization settings.
