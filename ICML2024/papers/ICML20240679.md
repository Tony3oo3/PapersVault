## Problem

In-context learning performance is highly sensitive to which examples are placed in the prompt, yet the standard practice of using off-the-shelf retrievers (BM25, SentenceBERT) is suboptimal because these encoders are not trained for ICL example selection. More effective prior methods require task-specific training or LLM-in-the-loop feedback, sacrificing the training-free advantage of ICL, while BERTScore-Recall—though training-free—is quadratically expensive in input length and relies on general-purpose encoders that may miss task-specific salient information.

## Input / Output

The gist model consumes a sequence of [task_instruction, example_input, gist_tokens] (or just [example_input, gist_tokens] for task-specific finetuning) and produces l fixed-length gist token embeddings from the final encoder layer. GistScore then computes a BERTScore-Recall-style similarity between the test input's gist embeddings and each candidate's gist embeddings, yielding a scalar relevance score per candidate. The top-k candidates by GistScore are selected and their full text is passed to any Inference LLM for ICL; the gist model itself is agnostic to the choice of Inference LLM.

## Method

Example Gisting trains an encoder-decoder LM (Flan-T5) with an attention masking bottleneck: gist tokens can attend to the input, but the output can only attend to the gist tokens, forcing all task-relevant information to be compressed into l gist token activations. Training minimizes a KL-divergence distillation objective between the full LM's output distribution and the gisting model's output conditioned only on the gist tokens. Two variants are explored: task-specific finetuning (GS[F]) on each dataset, and multi-task training (GS[M]) on ~5M prompts from FLAN 2022 spanning 146 task categories, where the task instruction is also gisted to enable out-of-the-box generalization to new tasks. GistScore computes (1/l)·Σᵢ maxⱼ cos(xᵢ, zⱼ) over the l gist token pairs, reducing to cosine similarity for l=1 and admitting a submodular set-extension (SET-GS) for greedy set-level selection that is particularly effective for compositional tasks like semantic parsing.

## Results

Across 21 datasets, 9 task categories, and 8 LLMs, finetuned GistScore (GS[F,1]) achieves an average 8-shot ICL accuracy of ~71.4%, beating the strongest training-free baseline BSR (~63.7%) by ~7.7 points and off-the-shelf SBERT (~56.2%) by ~15 points on average. The multi-task variant (GS[M,1]) recovers most of this gain (~67.7% average) without any task-specific finetuning, while being three orders of magnitude faster than BSR. On Semantic Parsing, the set-extension with 15 gist tokens (SET-GS[F,15]) achieves 75.7% average, outperforming all prior methods including trained baselines like CEIL and EPR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GS[F,1] consistently outperforms all baselines across every LLM tested. Against off-the-shelf retrievers, it beats SBERT by up to 21 points (e.g., 68.1 vs 46.5 on GPT-Neo-2.7B) and BM25 by up to 22 points. Against the stronger training-free BSR, gains range from 6 to 11 points (e.g., 76.5 vs 70.9 on Mistral). Against trained methods that use LLM feedback, GS[F,1] beats CEIL by ~5 points and LLM-R by ~8 points on average. The multi-task GS[M,1] matches or exceeds all training-free baselines (BM25, SBERT, BSR) on every LLM without any task-specific training, and also matches trained methods like EPR and CEIL on held-out datasets. On Semantic Parsing specifically, SET-GS[F,15] (75.7% avg) surpasses SET-BSR (72.1%), CEIL, and EPR, while SET-GS[M,15] (71.3%) matches them despite never having been trained on Semantic Parsing data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the gist-token attention bottleneck from Mu et al. (2023), originally designed for prompt compression, as a supervised encoder for in-context example selection. The scoring metric (GistScore) directly inherits the functional form of BERTScore-Recall (Zhang et al., 2020) but operates on a small number of gist tokens rather than full token sequences, and the set-selection extension follows the submodular coverage framework of Gupta et al. (2023). The key departure from prior work is the multi-task training paradigm: by gisting task instructions alongside example inputs, a single model can select examples for unseen tasks without any task-specific finetuning, preserving the training-free nature of ICL that prior trained retrievers (EPR, CEIL, LLM-R) sacrifice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-task gist model offers a practical drop-in replacement for off-the-shelf retrievers (BM25, SBERT) in ICL pipelines, delivering 20%+ absolute gains while being three orders of magnitude faster than BERTScore-Recall and generalizing to new tasks, domains, and languages out-of-the-box. A notable open question raised by the analysis is whether classification-task gains are driven primarily by label-precision (selecting examples with the correct label) rather than genuine informativeness, which would limit the method's interpretability and suggest the 'salient aspects' framing may be partially an artifact of finite output spaces. The authors also note potential extension to retrieval-augmented generation as future work.
