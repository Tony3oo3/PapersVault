## Problem

Despite the empirical success of Thompson sampling in traditional contextual bandits, no posterior-sampling algorithm had been developed for linear contextual dueling bandits, where the learner compares two options and receives only a binary preference. All existing algorithms (MaxInP, CoLSTIM, VACDB) rely on UCB-based exploration, and it was unknown whether Thompson sampling could match their minimax-optimal regret bounds in this setting.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the agent receives a prompt x_t and a (possibly infinite) action space A_t, then selects a pair of arms (a_t^1, a_t^2) by maximizing the inner product of independently sampled posterior parameters with the d-dimensional feature mapping φ(x_t, a). The agent receives a single binary preference y_t ∈ {±1} generated under the Bradley-Terry-Luce model, where the probability of preferring a_t^1 over a_t^2 depends on the reward difference. Feature vectors are bounded in ℓ₂-norm by 1, and the true parameter θ* is bounded by B.

## Method

FGTS.CDB samples two model parameters θ_t^1 and θ_t^2 independently from separate posterior distributions, each constructed with a novel Feel-Good exploration term that includes an additional inner product ⟨θ, φ(x, a^{3-j})⟩ with the adversarial arm's feature vector. This design ensures the two selected arms are conditionally independent given the history, which is critical for eliminating cross terms in the regret decomposition. The regret is decomposed into a Bellman Error term (estimation error on historic data) and a Feel-Good Exploration term (gap between sampled and true maximum reward), both of which are bounded via a decoupling argument and a potential-based analysis. The potential Z_t^j tracks the log-partition function of the posterior, and its telescoping sum over T rounds yields the minimax-optimal regret bound of Õ(d√T). For implementation, posterior sampling is performed via Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics with a decaying step size.

## Results

Theoretically, FGTS.CDB achieves a minimax-optimal expected regret bound of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(d\sqrt{T})$ (Theorem 5.2), matching the lower bound of $\Omega(d\sqrt{T})$ (Theorem 5.3). In experiments with T=2500 rounds, d ∈ {5, 10, 15}, and |A_t|=32 arms, FGTS.CDB outperforms all UCB-based baselines (MaxInP, MaxPairUCB, CoLSTIM, VACDB) by a large margin in cumulative regret across all dimensions. FGTS.CDB also exhibits the smallest standard deviation across 30 independent runs, indicating greater stability. The algorithm's hyperparameters (η, μ) are independent of the feature dimension d, simplifying tuning compared to UCB-based methods that require careful confidence-radius calibration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FGTS.CDB is compared against four UCB-based baselines: MaxInP and MaxPairUCB (Saha, 2021), CoLSTIM (Bengs et al., 2022), and VACDB (Di et al., 2023). All baselines share the same theoretical regret bound of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(d\sqrt{T})$, but empirically FGTS.CDB achieves significantly lower cumulative regret across all tested dimensions (d=5, 10, 15) over 2500 rounds. The gap is consistent and large, with FGTS.CDB also showing the tightest variance across 30 runs. A key practical advantage is that FGTS.CDB is the first algorithm that naturally handles infinite action spaces without requiring arm elimination or per-arm perturbation, whereas CoLSTIM and SupCoLSTIM are infeasible in that setting and MaxInP incurs additional computational overhead. The ablation study further reveals that FGTS.CDB performs well even without the Feel-Good exploration term (α=0), suggesting the stochastic nature of Thompson sampling itself provides sufficient exploration in the dueling setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Zhang (2022)'s Feel-Good Thompson Sampling (FGTS) from standard linear contextual bandits to the contextual dueling bandits setting, where the learner selects pairs of arms and receives binary preference feedback under a Bradley-Terry-Luce model. The key methodological departure is a redesigned Feel-Good exploration term that incorporates the adversarial arm's feature vector, which eliminates cross terms that would otherwise arise from the pairwise comparison structure and enables the decoupling argument to go through. All prior algorithms for linear contextual dueling bandits (MaxInP, CoLSTIM, VACDB) rely on UCB-based exploration; the only earlier Thompson sampling work for dueling bandits (Wu & Liu, 2016) is restricted to finite multi-armed settings and cannot be adapted to the contextual case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FGTS.CDB is the first Thompson sampling algorithm for linear contextual dueling bandits to achieve the minimax-optimal O~(d√T) regret bound, and it is the first such algorithm that naturally handles infinite action spaces without arm elimination or per-arm perturbation. The structural property that the two sampled arms are independent conditioned on history (unlike UCB methods where the bonus term couples them) simplifies both analysis and implementation. Open questions include whether variance-aware sampling-based algorithms can be designed for this setting, and whether the approach extends to preference-based reinforcement learning. The ablation showing reasonable performance even without the Feel-Good term (α=0) on synthetic data hints that posterior sampling's inherent stochasticity may suffice for exploration in dueling bandits, a question the authors leave for future work.
