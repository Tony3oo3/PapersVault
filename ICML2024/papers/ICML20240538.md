## Problem

Existing watermarking methods for Stable Diffusion (post-diffusion, VAE-decoder-based, or sampling-based) are all vulnerable in white-box scenarios where an adversary has full access to model weights and can simply replace the VAE, alter the sampling strategy, or strip the watermarking module. No prior work achieves protection where removing the watermark necessarily degrades the model's core generation capability, which is critical for open-source platforms like Civitai where customized checkpoints are freely shared.

## Input / Output

The method takes a customized Stable Diffusion checkpoint (U-Net weights) and a 48-bit secret string as inputs, and produces a watermarked model whose generated images (512×512 up to 768×768) carry the embedded watermark. At inference time, a text prompt drives generation as usual, and a separate secret decoder (EfficientNet-B1) extracts the 48-bit watermark from any generated image. The scaling matrix mechanism allows the same trained LoRA to encode arbitrary secrets without retraining, supporting multi-user deployments.

## Method

AquaLoRA is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (latent watermark pre-training) jointly trains a secret encoder and decoder to produce a cover-agnostic watermark pattern in the VAE latent space, using a Peak Regional Variation Loss (PRVL) to suppress local artifacts. Stage 2 (Prior Preserving Fine-Tuning, PPFT) learns this pattern into the U-Net via a modified LoRA where a scaling matrix S (constructed from orthogonal embeddings of the secret bits) is inserted as A×S×B, enabling flexible secret updates without retraining. The key insight is that the PPFT loss targets the original model's own noise predictions (ε_ϑ) rather than ground-truth noise, derived from the DDPM ELBO, which prevents the data distribution from shifting during watermark learning. The resulting LoRA is merged directly into U-Net weights, making the watermark inseparable from the model's generative capability.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion v1.5, AquaLoRA achieves 95.79% bit accuracy (91.86% under adversarial distortions) and a TPR of 0.990 (0.906 adversarial) at FPR=10⁻⁶, while maintaining a DreamSim of 0.201 and FID of 24.88. Across 25 customized models from Civitai, the method retains 94.81% bit accuracy (90.27% adversarial) and 0.976 TPR (0.861 adversarial). AquaLoRA is the only method providing white-box protection, and it achieves the best average robustness (91.86% bit accuracy, 0.906 TPR) across all distortion types in Table 2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AquaLoRA outperforms all baselines on adversarial robustness: it achieves 91.86% adversarial bit accuracy and 0.906 adversarial TPR, surpassing Tree-ring (0.810 TPR adv.), RivaGAN (84.19% bit acc. adv., 0.630 TPR adv.), Stable Signature (77.01% bit acc. adv., 0.580 TPR adv.), and DWT-DCT-SVD (70.55% bit acc. adv., 0.356 TPR adv.). On clean bit accuracy, post-diffusion methods (DWT-DCT-SVD at 100%, RivaGAN at 98.78%) slightly edge out AquaLoRA (95.79%), but these methods are trivially removable in white-box settings. In terms of fidelity, AquaLoRA's DreamSim (0.201) is notably better than Tree-ring (0.301) but higher than post-diffusion methods (~0.017–0.023), which is expected since integrated watermarks perturb the generation process. The critical distinction is that AquaLoRA is the only method offering white-box protection: all four baselines can be bypassed by an adversary with full model access (e.g., replacing the VAE or changing the sampler), whereas removing AquaLoRA's watermark from the U-Net destroys the model's generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LoRA parameter-efficient fine-tuning framework (Hu et al., 2021) by inserting a scaling matrix S into the A×B decomposition to encode arbitrary secret messages, and derives its Prior Preserving Fine-Tuning (PPFT) loss directly from the DDPM ELBO (Ho et al., 2020). It departs from prior diffusion watermarking approaches—post-diffusion pixel methods (DWT-DCT-SVD, RivaGAN), VAE-decoder embedding (Stable Signature), and initial-noise placement (Tree-ring)—by embedding the watermark directly into the U-Net weights, the component whose removal would destroy generation quality. This architectural choice is what enables the white-box protection guarantee that all prior methods lack, since an adversary with full model access can trivially swap the VAE or change the sampler but cannot remove the U-Net without destroying the model itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AquaLoRA is the first practical white-box watermark for the thriving ecosystem of shared customized SD checkpoints (e.g., Civitai), where creators currently have no protection against unauthorized redistribution or commercial use. Key limitations remain: the watermark degrades under strong cropping/rotation, does not cover editing/inpainting/outpainting tasks, and suffers modest accuracy loss at larger output resolutions. The cover-agnostic design also leaves an inherent vulnerability to averaging attacks (estimating Δz_w from many samples), which the authors acknowledge but only partially mitigate. Open questions include scaling beyond 64 bits, integrating watermarking with inpainting pipelines, and whether dynamic watermark updates can close the averaging gap.
