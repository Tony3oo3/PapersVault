## Problem

Attention-based language models excel at recall (grounding generations in previously seen tokens) but are bottlenecked by quadratic training compute and a KV-cache that grows linearly with sequence length during inference. Sub-quadratic alternatives like Mamba, H3, and RWKV maintain fixed-size recurrent states but underperform attention on recall-intensive tasks by 30+ accuracy points. The paper asks whether a simpler architecture can navigate the Pareto frontier between recall quality and inference memory/throughput, rather than being stuck at one extreme.

## Input / Output

The model consumes text token sequences (tokenized with GPT-2 BPE) and produces next-token predictions autoregressively. During generation, the recurrent state size is the critical efficiency parameter: it is determined by the linear attention feature dimension d' (default 16, giving O(d'²·d) state) plus the sliding window KV-cache (w×d per head, with w ≤ 128). By tuning d' and w, the same architecture can be configured to trade state size against recall capacity, spanning from near-attention quality (large state) to near-Mamba efficiency (small state).

## Method

BASED combines two complementary sequence mixers in a hybrid layer stack (~20% linear attention, ~20% sliding window attention, ~60% gated convolution): (1) global linear attention using a 2nd-order Taylor series feature map φ(q)ᵀφ(k) = 1 + qᵀk + (qᵀk)²/2 to approximate softmax, enabling O(Nd²) training and a fixed-size recurrent KV-state during generation; and (2) local exact softmax attention in small sliding windows (w = 64–128 tokens, chosen to match GPU tensor-core tile sizes for high occupancy). The key insight is that linear attention alone lacks the precision for local token shifts/comparisons needed for associative recall, while SWA alone limits recall range to the window width; together they recover 90.8% of full attention's recall at 1e-5× its latency. IO-aware CUDA kernels fuse the Taylor feature map computation with the causal dot product, storing the large O(d²·D) KV-state in thread registers partitioned across warps, reducing HBM↔SRAM data movement by O(Nd²) bytes and SRAM↔register movement by O(Nd³) bytes relative to the baseline.

## Results

At 1.3B parameters trained on 50B tokens, BASED achieves 6.30 overall Pile perplexity, matching Mamba (6.28) and Transformer++ (6.28), while outperforming Mamba by 10.36 accuracy points on recall-intensive tasks (SWDE 64.45 vs 52.75, FDA 30.40 vs 18.51, SQUAD 41.62 vs 35.92). On the associative recall slice of the Pile, BASED achieves 1.71 AR perplexity vs. Mamba's 1.74 and Transformer++'s 1.65. In generation throughput, BASED delivers 24× higher tokens/second than FlashAttention-2 at batch size 128 (1.3B params, 1024 tokens) and 56% faster prefill than FlashAttention-2 at 4K sequence length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Transformer++ (full softmax attention), BASED matches overall perplexity (6.30 vs 6.28 at 1.3B/50B) but still trails on recall-intensive tasks: SWDE 64.45 vs 76.50, FDA 30.40 vs 80.47, and SQUAD 41.62 vs 43.47. Against Mamba (the strongest sub-quadratic baseline), BASED ties on perplexity (6.30 vs 6.28) and LM-Eval Harness (53.81 vs 53.50) but wins decisively on recall: +11.7 points on SWDE, +11.9 on FDA, and +5.7 on SQUAD, for an average 10.36-point advantage. Against gated-convolution architectures (H3, Hyena, RWKV v5), BASED dominates across all metrics — e.g., at 360M/10B, BASED achieves 8.65 overall Ppl vs. H3's 10.60 and RWKV v5's 9.79, with AR Ppl of 2.07 vs. H3's 4.88. Against GLA (gated linear attention), BASED achieves lower perplexity (8.65 vs 9.12 at 360M/10B). In generation throughput, BASED's IO-aware implementation delivers 24× higher tokens/sec than FlashAttention-2 and 95% of Mamba's recurrent throughput at batch size 128, while achieving 56% faster prefill than FlashAttention-2 at 4K sequence length.

## Relation to Prior Work

BASED combines two well-studied primitives — linear attention (Katharopoulos et al., 2020) with a 2nd-order Taylor feature map (Zhang et al., 2024) for global token interactions, and sliding window attention (Beltagy et al., 2020) for local precision — into a single architecture that navigates the recall-memory tradeoff. The IO-aware CUDA kernels adapt FlashAttention's (Dao et al., 2022) tiling and SRAM/HBM management strategy to the linear attention setting, while the theoretical analysis builds on Arora et al. (2023a) lower bounds for gated convolutions. The key departure from prior work is the hardware-guided choice of very small window sizes (64–128 tokens) motivated by tensor core occupancy, and the demonstration that neither primitive alone suffices to match the Pareto frontier established by Mamba.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BASED provides a simple, open-source architecture that practitioners can use to trade off recall quality against inference memory/throughput by tuning two hyperparameters (window size and feature dimension), with a 24× generation throughput advantage over FlashAttention-2 at 1.3B scale. The theoretical lower bounds (Ω(N) bits for any recurrent model to solve MQAR) establish that the observed tradeoff is fundamental, not an architectural artifact. However, BASED still lags full attention by 10–24 accuracy points on recall-intensive tasks (e.g., SWDE: 48 vs. 72 at 1.3B/10B), and the gap narrows with more training tokens, leaving open whether the architecture can fully close the recall gap at scale or whether the specific Taylor+SWA combination is optimal.
