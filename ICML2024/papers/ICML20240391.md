## Problem

Contrastive learning has been highly successful for pairwise (second-order) representation learning in vision and NLP, but extending it to high-dimensional tensors for sparse ordinal tensor completion remains unsolved. Three specific barriers exist: (1) self-attention with only as many queries as tensor modes (typically 3–4) cannot adequately capture high-order nonlinear mode interactions; (2) standard binary positive/negative contrast levels ignore the fine-grained ordinal structure of real-world tensor entries (e.g., ratings 1–5, traffic speeds 3–83); and (3) the combinatorial explosion of negative samples and extreme sparsity (down to 10⁻⁷% non-zero) make efficient multi-level contrastive sampling nontrivial.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3rd-order sparse ordinal tensor specified by observed triples (i, j, k) with their scalar ordinal values (e.g., traffic speed, user rating), where the tensor can be extremely sparse (sampling ratios as low as 2.35×10⁻⁷% for Gowalla). Each triple's three mode indices are embedded into d-dimensional vectors (d=20) that serve as the three input tokens. The output is a scalar predicted value for each missing tensor entry, produced by a predictive MLP branch, while the contrastive branch simultaneously shapes the learned mode embeddings to encode fine-grained ordinal relationships.

## Method

HOCTC introduces three coupled innovations. First, Self-Attention with Query Expansion (SAQE) overcomes the limited-query bottleneck by learning L additional queries as ℓ₂-normalized linear combinations of the original three mode queries (via a matrix M ∈ ℝ^{L×3}), yielding L+3 total queries that attend to the original three keys/values through cross-attention, effectively creating higher-resolution 'sensors' for profiling the attention landscape. Second, the contrastive loss extends binary InfoNCE to fine-grained levels by partitioning contrastive triples into weakly-positive (0 < T_{i'j'k'} < T_{ijk}) and negative (T_{i'j'k'} = 0) sets, requiring at least one shared mode index, so the model learns to distinguish not just interaction from non-interaction but also stronger from weaker interactions. Third, Multi-Level Contrastive Sampling (MLCS) uses a mode-index-sorting scheme that partitions observed triples into blocks sorted hierarchically by mode indices (with randomized mode order per block), making weakly-positive triples discoverable within local mini-batch neighborhoods at O(l+p) cost per positive triple. The overall training minimizes a composite loss combining MSE on the predictive branch with the fine-grained contrastive loss on a separate contrastive branch, both operating on the SAQE-enhanced triple representation.

## Results

HOCTC achieves the lowest error in 11 out of 12 metric comparisons across four spatio-temporal datasets (PEMS, GZspeed, METR, CityTemp) and 8 out of 12 across four recommendation tensors (SG, Beauty, Movie&TV, Gowalla). On PEMS, HOCTC attains MAE 3.4033, RMSE 5.7479, and MAPE 8.2663, improving 16.3% over the best baseline (LightNestle, MAE 4.0680). On the largest sparse tensor Gowalla, HOCTC reduces MAE by 26.7% (0.4652 vs. 0.6345) and MAPE by 57.9% (16.16 vs. 38.42) relative to the best baseline (NTC). Ablation studies confirm that SAQE alone reduces MAE by 3.14%–19.14% and MLCS alone by 2.43%–16.77% across all eight tensors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOCTC outperforms all 16 baselines on MAE across every dataset. Among spatio-temporal baselines, LightNestle (Li et al., 2023b) is the strongest competitor, but HOCTC still leads by 16.3% on PEMS (3.40 vs. 4.07 MAE) and 14.8% on METR (3.45 vs. 4.05 MAE); on GZspeed, HOCTC wins on MAE and RMSE but LightNestle edges it on MAPE (9.86 vs. 10.58). Among recommendation baselines, NTC (Xie et al., 2020) is the closest on Gowalla (MAE 0.6345 vs. 0.4652, a 26.7% gap), while CoSTCo (Liu et al., 2019) is nearest on Beauty (MAE 0.8237 vs. 0.7772). In the extended comparison (Table 5), HOCTC beats recent CTR/recommendation models (AOANet, EDCN, MaskNet, SAM, FinalMLP, FinalNet, GDCN) by 3–25% on MAE. Against GNN-based recommenders (Fi-GNN, DG-ENN, TGIN in Table 6), HOCTC achieves 3–27% lower MAE, with the largest margin on Gowalla (0.4652 vs. 0.5687 for TGIN). P-TUCKER (Oh et al., 2018) and NeuralCP (Liu et al., 2018), the older linear/early-neural baselines, are consistently the weakest, trailing HOCTC by 30–60% on MAE across all datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends self-attention (Vaswani et al., 2017) with a query-expansion mechanism (SAQE) to overcome the fundamental limitation that tensor data provides only as many tokens as tensor modes (typically 3–4), and simultaneously extends contrastive learning from its standard second-order, binary (positive/negative) formulation to high-order, fine-grained (positive/weakly-positive/negative) comparisons guided by ordinal tensor entries. It departs from prior tensor completion methods (NeuralCP, CoSTCo, NTC, NTM, LightNestle) that model mode interactions purely through predictive loss, by introducing a contrastive self-supervised branch that enforces relational structure among triples. The multi-level contrastive sampling (MLCS) scheme addresses a gap in the contrastive learning literature where negative sampling is straightforward but 'weakly-positive' sample identification in sparse tensors is nontrivial.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that contrastive learning, previously applied almost exclusively to pairwise (second-order) relations in vision, NLP, and graphs, can be meaningfully extended to high-order tensor interactions with ordinal entries, yielding consistent 16–27% MAE improvements over strong neural tensor completion baselines across 8 datasets. The SAQE mechanism is a general-purpose technique applicable whenever self-attention operates on very few tokens, potentially useful beyond tensor completion. Open questions include the lack of theoretical analysis for multi-level contrastive learning, the restriction to 3rd-order tensors in experiments, and whether the generic (domain-agnostic) formulation can be further improved by incorporating spatio-temporal or structural priors.
