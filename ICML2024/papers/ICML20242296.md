## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of deep reinforcement learning are confined to linear function approximation or the NTK/lazy training regime, where neural network features remain fixed at initialization and no representation learning occurs. This paper asks whether a neural actor-critic can provably learn features during training while still converging to a globally optimal policy, a question left open by prior work that either restricts to linear critics or assumes data-independent feature maps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242296/cropped_page7_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes bounded state-action pairs (s,a) ∈ R^D with ‖(s,a)‖ ≤ 1 from a discounted MDP and produces a softmax policy π(a|s) ∝ exp(−f(s,a)) and a Q-function Q(s,a) = f(s,a), each realized as the mean-field limit of an over-parameterized two-layer network (width m for the actor, width M for the critic) with d = D+2 dimensional parameters per neuron. Both networks use a bounded, Lipschitz, smooth activation σ: R→(−1,1) and bounded odd second-layer weights β: R→(−1,1) scaled by R > 0, ensuring all function values are uniformly bounded by R.

## Method

The core contribution reframes both actor and critic optimization as Wasserstein gradient flows over probability distributions of network parameters, exploiting the convexity of the objective in measure space to obtain global optimality. The actor (MFLPG) follows a McKean-Vlasov SDE that combines the policy gradient with Gaussian noise—equivalent to standard policy gradient plus injected noise—and a KL-divergence regularization whose L2 component induces a dimension-free log-Sobolev inequality, yielding linear convergence to the globally optimal policy for any regularization strength λ > 0. The critic (MFLTD) introduces a double-loop proximal gradient scheme: the inner loop minimizes a convex majorization of the Bellman error over the measure space to neutralize the non-monotonic semi-gradient, while the outer loop iteratively updates the Q-function, guaranteeing sublinear convergence to the true value function with a bias independent of network scale. The discretized particle version (Algorithm 1) achieves an O(m^{−2}) performance gap with T = Ω(m³e^m) iterations, substantially faster than the O(m^{−1/16}) rate available in the NTK regime.

## Results

The paper's primary contributions are theoretical convergence guarantees rather than numerical benchmarks. Theorem 1 establishes that MFLTD converges to the true value function at a time-averaged rate of O(1/T_TD) with regularization bias O(λ_TD). Theorem 2 proves that MFLPG achieves linear convergence exp(−αλt) toward the globally optimal policy with an O(√λ) regularization bias, significantly outpacing the O(t^{−1/2}) rate typical of NTK-regime methods. Theorem 3 shows the fully discretized particle algorithm achieves O(1/m²) expected performance error with m particles. The single numerical experiment on CartPole-v1 (256 neurons, 4000 episodes, γ=0.99) qualitatively confirms that mean-field training with feature learning outperforms NTK-fixed-feature training, and that the double-loop MFLTD ultimately approximates the true value function more accurately than single-loop TD(1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MFLTD against two baselines on CartPole-v1. Against NTK-TD (which fixes representations at initial values, mimicking lazy training), the mean-field MFLTD with 256 neurons achieves lower Mean Squared Bellman Error, attributed to the increased expressiveness from data-dependent feature learning in the first layer. Against single-loop TD(1) (the standard Bellman semi-gradient method from Sutton, 1988), the double-loop MFLTD is slower in the low-accuracy regime under the same computational budget but surpasses TD(1) in the high-accuracy regime, ultimately converging closer to the true value function. Theoretically, MFLPG's linear convergence rate exp(−αλt) with arbitrary λ>0 contrasts with the sublinear O(t^{−1/2}) rate of Wang et al. (2020) in the NTK regime, and with Leahy et al. (2022) which requires a sufficiently large regularization parameter λ>c>0 for strong convexity, whereas this paper's analysis holds for any λ>0.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mean-field Langevin dynamics (MFLD) framework from supervised learning (Nitanda et al. 2022; Chizat 2022) to the actor-critic RL setting, where both policy and value function are represented by over-parameterized two-layer networks. It departs from the dominant NTK/lazy-training analyses of neural RL (Cai et al. 2019; Wang et al. 2020; Zhang et al. 2020, 2021) by working in the mean-field regime where first-layer features are allowed to adapt, and from Leahy et al. (2022) by proving convergence under arbitrary KL-regularization strength rather than requiring a large regularization constant. The proximal double-loop TD update is a novel mechanism to handle the semi-gradient non-monotonicity that plagues standard TD learning in the Wasserstein gradient flow setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first proof of global optimality and linear convergence for a neural actor-critic algorithm that performs genuine feature learning, closing a gap left by NTK-based analyses that assume fixed random features. The O(m^{-2}) width-scaling rate (vs. O(m^{-1/16}) in NTK regime) and the elimination of the 1/α network-scaling bias in TD convergence are theoretically significant. However, the empirical validation is limited to CartPole-v1 with two-layer networks, and the double-loop structure introduces practical computational overhead. Open questions include whether these guarantees extend to deeper architectures, continuous action spaces, and more complex environments, and whether the mean-field advantage over NTK persists at practical (finite) widths.
