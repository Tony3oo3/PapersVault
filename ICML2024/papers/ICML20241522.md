## Problem

Many important optimal transport variants—standard W2 OT, Schrödinger Bridge, unbalanced OT, and physically-constrained OT—each have distinct variational formulations and are typically solved by separate, problem-specific algorithms. This paper identifies a unifying Lagrangian action functional on the space of probability densities that encompasses all these problems, and asks whether a single computational framework can solve them without requiring optimal couplings or trajectory simulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241522/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes discrete samples from M temporal marginal distributions (e.g., single-cell RNA-seq snapshots at different timepoints) and an optional spatial potential function V_t(x) encoding prior physical knowledge. It outputs a neural network parameterizing the cotangent vector s_t(x,θ), which defines the velocity field ∇s_t for transporting particles, along with a generative model for the interpolated density path ρ_t. The parameterization enforces endpoint (and intermediate) marginal constraints by construction via a linear interpolation plus neural network perturbation, so no explicit constraint enforcement is needed during training.

## Method

The core insight is that for a broad class of Lagrangians (W2, WFR, Schrödinger Bridge, physically-constrained OT), the Hamiltonian dual objective is linear in the density ρ_t, meaning only samples from ρ_t are needed rather than density evaluations. The paper derives a min-max dual (Theorem 1) where the inner sup over cotangent vectors s_t generalizes Action Matching, and the outer inf over density paths is parameterized by a generative model x_t = (1-t)x_0 + tx_1 + t(1-t)·NNET(t,x_0,x_1;η). Training alternates gradient ascent on θ (the velocity field network) and gradient descent on η (the generative model) via the reparameterization trick, optionally augmented with Wasserstein gradient steps that directly move samples along the negative gradient of the Hamiltonian. This avoids both simulating ODE/SDE trajectories and computing minibatch OT couplings, distinguishing it from Flow Matching, Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge, and related approaches.

## Results

On the leave-one-out marginal interpolation task (Table 1), WLF-(UOT+potential, λ=1) achieves the best W1 distance across all datasets: 0.634 on EB (5-dim PCA), 34.16 on CITE-seq, and 36.13 on Multiome, substantially outperforming the best baseline [SF]²M-Geo (44.80 on Multiome) and exact OT (47.08 on Multiome). On the EB train/test split (Table 2), WLF-SB and WLF-(OT+potential) achieve the best mean W1 of 0.73, outperforming OT-CFM (0.77) and NLSB (0.78). On the melanoma treatment dataset (Table 3), WLF-OT achieves MMD 5.04e-3 and W2 5.20, beating the unbalanced baseline uDSB (MMD 1.75e-2, W2 6.11) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WLF-OT is comparable to OT-CFM (Tong et al., 2023b) on EB (0.814 vs 0.790) but slightly worse on CITE-seq and Multiome at dim=5, confirming that both methods capture minimal-prior dynamics similarly. WLF-UOT (λ=1) consistently improves over WLF-OT across all datasets (e.g., 45.90 vs 47.74 on Multiome dim=5), showing the benefit of allowing mass teleportation. The key advantage emerges when adding a physical potential: WLF-(UOT+potential) achieves 36.13 on Multiome dim=5, beating the best baseline [SF]²M-Geo (44.80) by ~8.7 W1 units and exact OT (47.08) by ~11 units. On the EB train/test split (Table 2), WLF-SB (mean W1 0.73) and WLF-(OT+potential) (0.73) outperform OT-CFM (0.77), NLSB (0.78), and all SB baselines (0.82–0.93). On melanoma (Table 3), WLF-OT achieves MMD 5.04e-3 versus uDSB's 1.75e-2 (3.5× improvement), while WLF-UOT achieves the best W2 of 5.01 versus uDSB's 6.11.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the Benamou-Brenier dynamical formulation of W2 optimal transport, the Wasserstein Fisher-Rao (unbalanced OT) geometry of Chizat et al., and the Schrödinger Bridge problem under a single Lagrangian action-minimization framework on the space of densities. The inner optimization (evaluating the action of a given curve) is explicitly identified as Action Matching (Neklyudov et al., 2023), while the outer optimization over constrained distributional paths is the novel computational contribution. The key departure from Flow Matching and Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge methods is that the dual formulation via the Legendre transform eliminates the need for optimal couplings, ODE/SDE simulation, or minibatch OT solvers. The Hopf-Cole reparameterization for the SB case, which converts a nonlinear-in-density Hamiltonian into a linearizable one, is a clean technical bridge to the Fokker-Planck perspective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified Lagrangian framework provides a principled way to combine kinetic energy (geometry) and potential energy (prior knowledge) terms, enabling flexible inductive biases for trajectory inference without ad-hoc modifications to the solver. A key limitation is the restriction to dual-linearizable Hamiltonians, which excludes certain nonlinear potential energies without additional reparameterization, and all experiments are conducted on low-dimensional PCA projections (5–100 dims) of single-cell data, leaving open whether the approach scales to high-dimensional or image-based settings. The framework also assumes time-independent diffusion coefficients for the SB case and does not explore ground-space Lagrangian costs beyond the quadratic cost, despite the theory supporting them. Future work could extend to more complex potentials, higher-dimensional data, and other scientific domains (quantum mechanics, social dynamics) as the authors suggest.
