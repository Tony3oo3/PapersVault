## Problem

Existing methods for training diffusion models from corrupted data (Ambient Diffusion, SURE-based approaches) rely on approximations that degrade generation quality, and no prior method could provably sample from the uncorrupted target distribution. The critical unsolved sub-problem was learning optimal denoisers at noise levels below the observed data noise level, which is necessary for exact sampling from p_0.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240401/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only noisy samples corrupted by additive Gaussian noise at a known level σ_{t_n} and produces samples from the original uncorrupted distribution. In the SDXL fine-tuning experiments, clean 1024×1024 images are encoded to latents and noise is added at levels t_n ∈ {0, 100, 500, 800}; generation uses 25-step DDIM sampling in latent space. For memorization detection, the input is extremely noisy latents (t=900) or masked images paired with text prompts, and the output is reconstructed images whose DINOv2 similarity to training data quantifies replication.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-part training objective that provably recovers the optimal denoiser E[X_0|X_t] at all noise levels using only noisy data. For σ_t > σ_{t_n}, the paper applies Tweedie's formula twice to derive an Ambient Denoising Score Matching loss that connects E[X_0|X_t] to the directly learnable E[X_{t_n}|X_t] without expensive divergence terms. For σ_t ≤ σ_{t_n}, a consistency loss enforces the Consistency Property across all time pairs, which—combined with Fokker-Planck PDE uniqueness from Consistent Diffusion Models—guarantees the solution is the true conditional expectation. The combined objective is trained jointly, and all results are extended to the Variance Preserving formulation used by SDXL. Empirically, fine-tuning SDXL with this framework on corrupted FFHQ data maintains competitive FID up to t_n=500 while significantly reducing training data memorization compared to clean-data fine-tuning.

## Results

On FFHQ (1024×1024), all models trained at different noise levels (t_n ∈ {100, 500, 800}) achieve comparable denoising performance, with latent MSE at evaluation level t=100 ranging from 0.0405 to 0.0411 and pixel MSE from 0.0068 to 0.0070. For unconditional generation (FID on 50k samples), models trained with consistency maintain performance comparable to the clean-data baseline up to t_n=500, while models without consistency deteriorate significantly as corruption increases. For memorization detection, the proposed noising method identifies a substantially higher fraction of near-identical samples (DINOv2 similarity > 0.95) compared to the Somepalli et al. (2022) prompt-based method, and fine-tuning with corrupted data (t_n=500) shifts the similarity distribution leftward, indicating reduced training-set memorization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary external comparison is against the Somepalli et al. (2022) prompt-based memorization detection method. The proposed noising-based attack (denoising extremely corrupted latents at t=900) finds a significantly higher fraction of generated samples with DINOv2 similarity above 0.95 (near-identical to training images) compared to the Somepalli baseline, which the authors attribute to having access to more information (noisy latents vs. text prompts alone). Internally, the key comparison is between models trained with and without the consistency loss: without consistency, FID degrades sharply as training corruption increases (generations become blurry, sampling from E[X_0|X_{t_n}] rather than p_0), while with consistency, FID remains comparable to the clean-data baseline up to t_n=500. For memorization, the model fine-tuned on corrupted data (t_n=500) shows a leftward-shifted similarity distribution relative to the clean-data model (Figure 8), confirming reduced training-set replication while maintaining competitive generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two threads from the authors' own prior work: the Ambient Diffusion framework (Daras et al., 2023b), which showed how to learn denoisers from corrupted data but left exact sampling as an open problem, and Consistent Diffusion Models (Daras et al., 2023a), which introduced a consistency property to reduce sampling drift. The key technical departure from prior corrupted-data methods (SURE, Noisier2Noise, G-SURE) is the use of a double application of Tweedie's formula to obtain an efficiently computable, divergence-free objective for all noise levels above the data noise, combined with a consistency loss to extend the learned denoisers below the data noise level. Conceptually, the approach is closest to Noisier2Noise but generalizes it from a single fixed noise level to a continuum of scales trained jointly in a denoising-score-matching fashion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a concrete open problem (exact diffusion sampling from noisy data) and provides a practical recipe for fine-tuning foundation models like SDXL on corrupted data, which directly addresses copyright and privacy concerns by reducing memorization while maintaining competitive FID. The memorization detection method (denoising extremely corrupted latents) reveals that diffusion models replicate training data at a significantly higher rate than previously measured, raising the stakes for regulatory and industry responses. Key limitations include the inability to handle linearly corrupted data (e.g., MRI, black-hole imaging), the ~3× training overhead from the consistency loss, and failure on very small datasets (<100 samples), leaving important applications partially unsolved.
