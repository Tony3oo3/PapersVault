## Problem

The sparsity of sparse Johnson-Lindenstrauss transforms was believed to be near-optimal, with an upper bound of s = O(ε^{-1} lg n) and a matching lower bound up to a lg(1/ε) factor. However, the lower bound relies on the standard basis as a hard instance, which only exists when d ≥ n. In the common regime where d ≪ n (e.g., bag-of-words with a fixed dictionary size but many documents), this gap is unexploited: the upper bound still depends on lg n rather than lg d, and no tighter lower bound was known.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set X of n vectors in R^d and produces a random m×d matrix A with m = O(ε^{-2} lg n) rows and at most s non-zeros per column, such that ||Ax||² ∈ (1±ε)||x||² for all x ∈ X with high probability. The embedding of a single point x costs O(s||x||_0) operations, exploiting input sparsity. The new sparsity bound s = O(ε^{-1}(lg n / lg(1/ε) + lg^{2/3}n lg^{1/3}d)) improves over the prior O(ε^{-1} lg n) whenever d is subpolynomial in n, and a matching lower bound s = Ω(ε^{-1} lg n / lg(m/lg n)) is shown to hold even when d ≪ n.

## Method

The paper provides a refined analysis of the CountSketch-based sparse JL construction of Kane and Nelson by decomposing each unit vector x into a head x_head (top ℓ entries) and tail x_tail, then bounding the three terms ||Ax_head||², ||Ax_tail||², and the cross term ⟨Ax_head, Ax_tail⟩ separately. For the head, the key observation is that when d ≪ n there are only d^{O(ℓ)} distinct head configurations, allowing a union bound with failure probability d^{-O(ℓ)} and yielding s = Ω(ε^{-1}ℓ lg d). For the tail, the small ℓ∞/ℓ₂ ratio (at most 1/√ℓ) is exploited via Jagadeesan's concentration result. The technical crux is bounding the cross terms: new concentration inequalities show that for well-behaved head supports, most entries of Ax_head are at most √5/s, enabling a two-part decomposition of the inner product sum where the 'large' part has few terms and the 'small' part concentrates strongly. The lower bound is strengthened by replacing the standard basis with vectors v_S = Σ_{i∈S} e_i/√|S| for all subsets S of size lg n/lg d, combined with a signature-based averaging argument over columns of A.

## Results

The paper's main contribution is a tighter sparsity upper bound for sparse JL embeddings: s = O(ε⁻¹(lg n / lg(1/ε) + lg^{2/3} n · lg^{1/3} d)) with m = O(ε⁻² lg n), improving upon the previous O(ε⁻¹ lg n) bound of Kane & Nelson (2014) when d ≪ n. A matching lower bound of s = Ω(ε⁻¹ lg n / lg(m / lg n)) is shown to hold even when d ≪ n (previously only for d = n), yielding a tight characterization when lg n ≥ lg d · lg³(1/ε). For oblivious subspace embeddings, the paper achieves s = O(ε⁻¹(k / lg(1/ε) + k^{2/3} lg^{1/3} k)) while maintaining the optimal dimension m = O(ε⁻² k), the first result to beat the O(k/ε) sparsity of black-box SparseJL at optimal m.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper improves upon three prior theoretical results. Against Kane & Nelson (2014), the new upper bound replaces the O(ε⁻¹ lg n) sparsity with O(ε⁻¹(lg n / lg(1/ε) + lg^{2/3} n · lg^{1/3} d)), which is strictly smaller whenever d ≪ n (e.g., for the Sentiment140 dataset with n = 1.6M and d = 37K, the second term is negligible). Against Nelson & Nguyen (2013), the lower bound is strengthened from requiring d = n to holding for any d with m ≤ (εd / lg n)^{1-o(1)}, thereby matching the first term of the new upper bound and closing the gap to a tight characterization in the regime lg n ≥ lg d · lg³(1/ε). For subspace embeddings, the paper's Theorem 1.3 is the first to maintain the optimal dimension m = O(ε⁻² k) while achieving sparsity s = o(k/ε), strictly beating the O(k/ε) obtained by black-box application of SparseJL. The bound is incomparable to Cohen (2016), which achieves O(log k / ε) sparsity but at the cost of a larger dimension m = O(k log k / ε²), and to Chenakkod et al. (2023), whose O(lg⁴(k/δ)/ε⁶) bound can be worse than O(k/ε) for some parameter regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SparseJL construction of Kane and Nelson (2014), which achieved sparsity s = O(ε^{-1} lg n) via a CountSketch-based random matrix, and on the near-matching lower bound of Nelson and Nguyen (2013) that applied only when d = Ω(n). The key departure is exploiting the regime d ≪ n—common in bag-of-words and tf-idf settings—by decomposing each vector into a 'head' (top-ℓ entries) and a 'tail,' then applying Jagadeesan's (2019) result on embedding vectors with small ℓ∞/ℓ2 ratio to the tail while using a combinatorial net argument for the head. The lower bound is strengthened by replacing the standard basis hard instance (which requires d ≥ n) with a collection of normalized subset-sum vectors, and the subspace embedding result leverages a coordinate-concentration lemma to eliminate the dependency on d that would arise from naively plugging n = 2^{O(k)} into the main theorem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes the gap between upper and lower bounds on sparse JL sparsity for the practically important regime d ≪ n, providing a tight characterization of when the input dimension can be exploited to reduce embedding time. The subspace embedding result is the first to achieve sparsity strictly better than the black-box SparseJL bound (O(k/ε)) while maintaining the optimal target dimension m = O(k/ε²), though it remains far from the conjectured O(lg k / ε). The improvements are asymptotic and the authors themselves note they are 'arguably small,' so practical gains depend on the specific parameter regime; the broader open question of whether O(lg k / ε) sparsity is achievable at optimal m for oblivious subspace embeddings remains unresolved.
