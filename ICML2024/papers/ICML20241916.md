## Problem

Deep graph clustering methods universally require a predefined cluster number K, which is often impractical to determine in real applications. The paper bridges this gap by introducing structural information from graph information theory as a differentiable clustering objective that reveals cluster structure without K, while also addressing the fact that the classic discrete structural entropy formulation cannot support gradient-based training and ignores node features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241916/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a weighted graph G=(V,E,X) with adjacency matrix A and node feature matrix X, projecting features into the Lorentz model of hyperbolic space via the exponential map. It produces a hyperbolic partitioning tree T_net* (height H is a hyperparameter, not K), from which node cluster assignments are extracted via a divisive top-down splitting procedure using hyperbolic distance, along with node embeddings at every tree level. No cluster number K is required at any stage; the tree structure itself encodes the clustering.

## Method

LSEnet formulates a differentiable structural information (DSI) objective that relaxes the classic discrete structural entropy to continuous level-wise assignment matrices, proven equivalent under binary assignment and shown to lower-bound graph conductance (Theorem 4.6), making it a valid clustering objective without K. The architecture operates entirely in the Lorentz model of hyperbolic space: a Lorentz convolution layer with self-attention embeds leaf nodes, then a recursive bottom-up process uses a Lorentz assigner (attention-weighted MLP producing soft assignment matrices) and a closed-form geometric centroid (Theorem 5.2) to learn parent nodes level by level. Training minimizes DSI via Riemannian Adam, and the final partitioning tree is decoded via breadth-first search, with redundant empty nodes pruned per the flexibility theorem. The hyperbolic geometry is motivated by the fact that trees embed in hyperbolic space with bounded distortion (Sarkar 2011), a property unavailable in Euclidean space.

## Results

On the four main clustering benchmarks, LSEnet (without predefined cluster number K) achieves the best NMI and ARI on Cora (63.97/63.35), Citeseer (52.26/48.01), and Computer (55.03/42.15), while DinkNet leads on AMAP (74.36/68.40 vs. LSEnet's 71.72/65.08). LSEnet also outperforms the classic structural entropy (CSE) baseline by 1.75–2.44% NMI on Cora and Citeseer in the 2-dimensional setting (Table 2). For link prediction, LSEnet's hyperbolic embeddings achieve the best AUC on all four datasets (e.g., 95.51 on Cora, 97.06 on Computer), surpassing QGCN in ultra-hyperbolic space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LSEnet wins on 3 of 4 datasets (Cora, Citeseer, Computer) in both NMI and ARI, with the closest competitor being Congregate on Cora (63.16 vs. 63.97 NMI) and Citeseer (50.92 vs. 52.26 NMI). On AMAP, DinkNet outperforms LSEnet by ~2.6% NMI and ~3.3% ARI, while DCRN is a distant second (73.70 NMI) but fails on Computer due to OOM. Among methods that also avoid a predefined K, RGC (reinforcement-learning-based) trails LSEnet by 6.4% NMI on Cora and 8.8% on Computer. For the 2-dimensional structural entropy comparison (Table 2), LSEnet beats CSE by 1.75–2.44% NMI on Cora/Citeseer and by 0.27–1.14% on Football/Karate. On link prediction (Table 3), LSEnet's hyperbolic embeddings surpass all Euclidean GNNs (GCN, SAGE, GAT) and hyperbolic baselines (HGCN, LGCN), and even exceed QGCN in ultra-hyperbolic space on Cora (95.51 vs. 95.22 AUC) and Computer (97.06 vs. 95.10 AUC).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected lines of research: structural entropy from information theory (Li & Pan, 2016), which measures graph uncertainty via partitioning trees, and deep graph clustering, which has relied on predefined cluster numbers. The key conceptual departure is formulating a differentiable structural information (DSI) objective in the continuous realm, enabling gradient-based training of a neural network to learn the partitioning tree. The architectural choice of the Lorentz model of hyperbolic space is grounded in the classical result (Sarkar, 2011) that trees embed with bounded distortion in hyperbolic geometry, and the Lorentz convolution and geometric centroid operations extend prior hyperbolic GNN work (HGCN, LGCN) to a tree-construction setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in deep graph clustering: the need to pre-specify the number of clusters K, which is often infeasible in real applications. The DSI objective provides a principled, K-free clustering criterion with a clean theoretical link to graph conductance (Theorem 4.6), and the hyperbolic partitioning tree offers an interpretable hierarchical structure. Limitations include the need to set tree height H and per-level node counts N_h as hyperparameters, evaluation on relatively small graphs (up to ~13k nodes), and the fact that the >20% NMI gain over RGC on AMAP may partly reflect RGC's RL-based K-estimation being less stable. Open questions include scalability to very large graphs and whether the hyperbolic geometry advantage persists beyond tree-like structures.
