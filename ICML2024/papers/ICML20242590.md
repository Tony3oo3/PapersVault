## Problem

Fine-tuning multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) on new tasks causes catastrophic forgetting, where performance on original pre-trained tasks degrades sharply. Existing anti-forgetting methods are designed for small models and rely on full-model fine-tuning, making them impractical for MLLMs, while parameter-efficient approaches like LoRA retain redundant parameter modifications that erode pre-trained knowledge. The paper asks how to enhance MLLM performance on target tasks without diminishing their effectiveness on original tasks.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a post-training adjustment requiring no retraining: it consumes the pre-trained weights Θ_pre, the fully fine-tuned weights Θ_sft, and a small calibration set of N input samples from the target task to approximate layer-wise expectations. It produces a fused model in which only a sparse subset (≤10% controlled by sparsity ratio ρ) of fine-tuned parameters are retained and each is augmented with a per-parameter compensation value derived from the inverse Hessian, while all remaining parameters revert exactly to their pre-trained values. The salience/sensitivity weighting ω in the hybrid score is the key hyperparameter governing the trade-off between target-task gain and original-task preservation.

## Method

Model Tailor is a two-step post-training procedure. Step 1 identifies a sparse binary mask M per layer by fusing a salience-based score (absolute deviation |θ_sft − θ_pre|) with a sensitivity-based score (second-order loss impact (θ_sft − θ_pre)²/[H⁻¹]_mm from Optimal Brain Surgeon theory) via a weighted combination s = ω·s̃_Δ + (1−ω)·s̃_ε, retaining only the top-ρ parameters. Step 2 applies a 'patch decorator' that compensates each retained parameter using the OBS update rule ΔΘ*_m = −(θ_sft^m − θ_pre^m)/[H⁻¹]_mm · H⁻¹_{:,m}, offsetting the loss increase caused by reverting unselected parameters to their pre-trained values. The final fusion is Θ_fusion = M ⊙ (Θ_sft + C) + (I−M) ⊙ Θ_pre, computed layer-wise with SparseGPT to reduce Hessian inversion complexity from O(nd²) to O(d³). The approach extends to multi-task settings by taking the union of task-specific masks and averaging their compensation vectors, enabling the model to absorb knowledge from multiple tasks simultaneously.

## Results

On InstructBLIP (Vicuna-7B) fine-tuned on Flickr30k with 10% sparsity, Model Tailor achieves an H-score of 93.67 and average performance of 93.10, outperforming full fine-tuning (92.12 / 86.08) while modifying only 28.8M parameters (10% of the 288M fine-tuned set). On LLaVA-1.5 (Vicuna-7B) fine-tuned on Flickr30k, Model Tailor reaches an H-score of 66.94 versus 63.40 for full fine-tuning, with pre-trained task average of 61.87 vs. 56.42. The method maintains ~99% of pre-trained task performance while achieving ~97% of full fine-tuning performance on target tasks, using only ≤10% of the fine-tuned parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Model Tailor outperforms all three baselines on the H-score metric across both models. Against DARE (random selection + rescaling, Yu et al. 2023), the advantage is most dramatic on LLaVA-1.5: on OKVQA, DARE collapses to an H-score of 1.64 (target task score drops to 0.83, near zero-shot) while Model Tailor achieves 47.71; on Flickr30k, DARE scores 36.64 vs. Model Tailor's 66.94. Against Grafting (loss-optimized mask, Panigrahi et al. 2023), Model Tailor leads by 0.89 H-score points on InstructBLIP-Flickr30k (93.67 vs. 92.78) and by 6.91 points on LLaVA-Flickr30k (66.94 vs. 60.03), with Grafting suffering more severe forgetting on pre-trained tasks. Against LoRA (Hu et al. 2021), Model Tailor is complementary rather than competitive: when applied post-hoc to a LoRA-fine-tuned LLaVA, it boosts the H-score from 62.99 to 72.89 on Flickr30k (+9.9 points) and from 52.48 to 58.04 on OKVQA, indicating that LoRA retains redundant parameter modifications that Model Tailor identifies and removes.

## Relation to Prior Work

Model Tailor synthesizes ideas from the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (sparse subnetworks suffice for task performance) and the Optimal Brain Surgeon (Hessian-based weight removal with compensation) into a post-training parameter fusion framework for MLLMs. It departs from prior anti-forgetting methods (DARE, Model Grafting) that were designed for uni-modal LLMs and rely on random or loss-optimized masks, by introducing a hybrid salience-sensitivity scoring mechanism and a principled compensation step derived from second-order loss analysis. The paper also positions itself against LoRA-based fine-tuning, showing that even parameter-efficient training retains redundant modifications that erode pre-trained knowledge, and against continual-learning formulations (He et al., 2023) by targeting a single fine-tuning event rather than sequential task streams.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MLLMs are increasingly fine-tuned for domain-specific applications, catastrophic forgetting on pre-trained capabilities becomes a practical bottleneck; Model Tailor offers a lightweight post-training remedy (≤10% parameter modification, ~21 minutes on a single GPU) that is agnostic to the upstream fine-tuning method. However, validation is limited to two 7B-scale models (InstructBLIP, LLaVA-1.5) and two task families (captioning, VQA), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger models, additional modalities (video, audio), or more diverse task distributions. The reliance on Hessian approximation via SparseGPT also raises questions about accuracy in highly non-convex regions of the loss landscape, and the method's behavior under iterative multi-round fine-tuning remains unexplored.
