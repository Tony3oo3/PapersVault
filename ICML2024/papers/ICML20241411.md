## Problem

Neural operator-based PDE solvers are largely confined to fixed domain sizes, geometric layouts, and boundary conditions, making them unsuitable for general parametric PDE problems with arbitrary scales and freeform geometries. The paper addresses this by asking whether neural operators can be combined with domain decomposition methods to produce a general-purpose surrogate solver that scales to arbitrary domain sizes while maintaining near-unity accuracy on each subdomain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241411/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For 2D electromagnetics, each 64×64 subdomain model takes a stack of images encoding the grayscale dielectric distribution (ε from 1 to 16), source or PML layout, and Robin boundary values, and outputs real and imaginary H-field maps at the same resolution. For steady-state fluid flow, the subdomain model takes an obstacle geometry image and velocity (u,v) Robin boundary conditions and outputs the full velocity field. The global DDM framework iteratively updates Robin boundary values between overlapping subdomains until the physics residue falls below a threshold, with the final output being the stitched global field solution at arbitrary domain sizes (up to 2100×2100 grids demonstrated).

## Method

SNAP-DDM subdivides the global domain into overlapping 64×64 subdomains and solves each with one of three specialized SM-FNO models (PML, source, or heterogeneous material), then iteratively stitches solutions using the overlapping Schwarz method with Robin boundary conditions. The SM-FNO architecture augments the standard FNO with two synergistic modifications: (1) a modulation encoder that compresses the input into a latent tensor which element-wise modulates the linear transform R in every Fourier layer, enabling input-dependent spectral processing of heterogeneous data, and (2) explicit residual connections within each Fourier layer that make deep architectures trainable. Training uses a hybrid data-physics loss where the physics term (Maxwell's equations or Navier-Stokes residual plus boundary condition residual) is dynamically weighted against the data loss via a per-epoch rescaling of α, which is critical for reducing the PDE residue to levels that permit DDM convergence. Ablation studies confirm both the self-modulation and residual connections are individually necessary, and that the physics loss term dramatically reduces L_pde and L_bc compared to data-only training.

## Results

The SM-FNO-v2 subdomain solver (trained on 1M data) achieves 99.0% accuracy with L_data = 1.01%, L_pde = 0.30, and L_bc = 0.030, outperforming all baselines on every metric while using only 10.2M parameters and 1.43G FLOPs. When integrated into the full SNAP-DDM framework, the method produces global electromagnetic field solutions with absolute errors near 5% across diverse freeform devices (microlenses, metasurfaces, metamaterial scatterers, thin-film stacks) spanning domain sizes up to 40×40 subdomains. For time complexity, SNAP-DDM outperforms the conventional FDFD solver for low-index materials (SiO₂, n=1.5) but requires significantly more iterations for high-index materials (TiO₂, n=2.48).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SM-FNO dominates all four neural architecture baselines (FNO, F-FNO, UNet, Swin Transformer) on every metric at both 100K and 1M data scales. At 1M data, SM-FNO-v2 achieves 1.01% L_data versus 2.93% (UNet), 3.52% (F-FNO), and 5.34% (FNO), while also reducing L_pde from 0.44–1.43 to 0.30 and L_bc from 0.078–0.124 to 0.030. Notably, vanilla FNO requires 131.2M parameters (13× more than SM-FNO) yet still underperforms, and Swin Transformer demands 9.60G FLOPs (6.7× more) with worse physics loss. The physics-augmented training is critical: removing it from SM-FNO-v2 inflates L_pde from 0.30 to 2.76 (9×), which would prevent DDM convergence. Against the conventional FDFD solver, SNAP-DDM is faster for SiO₂ domains but slower for TiO₂ due to increased iteration counts at high refractive index, indicating the method's speed advantage is material-dependent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) architecture and the classical overlapping Schwarz domain decomposition method, combining them in a way not previously reported. The SM-FNO architecture extends FNO with self-modulation connections (inspired by transformer self-attention and PINN multiplicative interactions) and explicit residual connections within Fourier layers, while the hybrid data-physics loss function draws on the authors' prior WaveY-Net work. The paper departs from prior neural operator approaches (FNO, F-FNO, DeepONet) that were limited to fixed domain sizes, and from PINN-based DDM work (Jagtap et al.) by using pretrained surrogate operators rather than per-problem training, enabling inference-time solving of arbitrary-scale problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SNAP-DDM provides a concrete, demonstrated pathway to arbitrary domain size PDE solving with neural surrogates, circumventing the fundamental scaling bottlenecks (data size, model size, memory) that plague end-to-end neural operators. However, the practical speedup is material-dependent: for high-refractive-index media like TiO2, the increased DDM iteration count makes SNAP-DDM slower than conventional FDFD, limiting near-term applicability. Open questions include whether preconditioning, multi-level DDM, and larger subdomains can close this gap, whether the approach extends to 3D, and whether the sub-optimal Schwarz convergence for fluid problems can be addressed with optimized or higher-order boundary conditions.
