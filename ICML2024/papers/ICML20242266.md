## Problem

Most existing methods train soft-pseudo-label generators using standard classification loss, which is misaligned with the actual goal of generating informative soft labels that capture data knowledge rather than making definitive predictions. Manually designing a better objective is difficult because datasets lack ground-truth soft labels, making it hard to evaluate whether generated soft pseudo-labels are accurate.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet) paired with one-hot class labels as input. It produces two outputs: a class prediction from the predictive model f, and a c-dimensional probability vector (soft pseudo-label) from the generator g that sums to 1. The framework is task-flexible—the task loss ℓᵗ can be swapped to adapt to different supervised settings such as partial label learning, where the input becomes a candidate label set rather than a single ground-truth label.

## Method

SEAL alternately trains a predictive model f and a soft-pseudo-label generator g (same architecture as f) under a meta-learning loop. A small meta-network parameterizes the loss function h used to train g, and its parameters are updated by backpropagating the task loss through a chain: clone g's parameters → optimize the clone with h → generate soft pseudo-labels → clone f's parameters → optimize the clone via bidirectional KL divergence against those soft labels → evaluate with a top-k task loss → backpropagate to the meta-network. This creates a feedback loop where the meta-network learns which loss shape best aligns g's output with the predictive model's needs, without requiring ground-truth soft labels. A balancing factor λ is linearly ramped up over the first T′ epochs to prevent unreliable early soft labels from degrading the classifier. Theoretically, the paper proves that training with proper soft pseudo-labels yields a larger class margin and thus a tighter generalization error bound than training with hard labels alone.

## Results

SEAL achieves the best classification accuracy across all three benchmark datasets: 91.79% on CIFAR-10, 70.72% on CIFAR-100, and 56.76% on TinyImageNet. The advantage grows with dataset complexity, with SEAL exceeding the second-best method (CS-KD) by 1.09% on TinyImageNet. In the partial label learning setting, SEAL also dominates all seven PLL baselines across every flipping probability on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, reaching 92.93% on CIFAR-10 at 0.3 flipping probability and 71.27% on CIFAR-100 at 0.03.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEAL outperforms all seven supervised-learning baselines on every dataset. On CIFAR-10, it leads the closest competitor TF-KD$_{self}$ (91.66%) by 0.13% and LSR (91.62%) by 0.17%; on CIFAR-100 it beats TF-KD$_{self}$ (70.44%) by 0.28%; on TinyImageNet it surpasses CS-KD (55.67%) by a clear 1.09% margin. In the partial label learning experiments (Table 3), SEAL dominates all seven PLL baselines across all flipping probabilities on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. The largest PLL gaps appear at high flipping rates: on CIFAR-10 at 0.9 flipping, SEAL reaches 85.36% versus 84.02% (PRODEN) and 81.39% (PLCR), while on CIFAR-100 at 0.1 flipping it achieves 67.21% versus 65.27% (VALEN) and 62.46% (PLCR). Notably, CAVL and PICO degrade severely at high flipping probabilities (e.g., CAVL drops to 13.19% on CIFAR-10 at 0.9), whereas SEAL maintains robust performance, confirming the advantage of the meta-network-parameterized objective over fixed loss designs.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEAL sits at the intersection of meta-learning (specifically learned loss functions, following Bechtle et al. 2020) and the soft-pseudo-label generation paradigm rooted in label enhancement (Xu et al. 2019, 2020) and self-knowledge distillation. The key conceptual departure from prior work is that previous methods train the soft-pseudo-label generator with the same classification loss (cross-entropy) used for the predictive model, which the authors argue is misaligned with the generator's actual objective of capturing data knowledge rather than making definitive predictions. SEAL resolves this by introducing a meta-network that learns the generator's objective function through a feedback loop informed by the predictive model's downstream performance, rather than relying on a manually designed loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, task-agnostic mechanism for generating soft pseudo-labels that could reduce the need for hand-crafted distillation objectives across supervised learning settings. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.1) that soft pseudo-labels yield tighter generalization bounds via larger class margins provides a formal justification for the approach, though the proof relies on strong geometric assumptions about feature separability. Limitations include evaluation only on small-scale datasets (CIFAR-10/100, TinyImageNet) with relatively shallow ResNets, leaving open whether the meta-learning overhead and performance gains scale to ImageNet-level problems. The flexibility demonstrated through the partial label learning adaptation is promising but remains a single-task validation of the broader claim.
