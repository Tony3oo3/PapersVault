## Problem

Existing post-training pruning metrics for LLMs (e.g., SparseGPT, Wanda, GBLM-Pruner) are designed through human expertise and tedious trial-and-error, and are highly format-sensitive—minor structural changes can cause perplexity to swing from ~7 to over 9000. There is no automated framework to systematically discover optimal symbolic pruning metrics, leaving the field dependent on manual metric engineering.

## Input / Output

The search process consumes three types of LLM statistics—weight matrices (W), gradients (G), and activations (X)—collected from 128 calibration samples on WikiText2, with the Hessian excluded due to its O(d_hidden²) computational cost. The output is a symbolic pruning metric represented as an expression tree whose leaf nodes are these statistics and whose internal nodes are 17 primitive operations (13 unary, 4 binary); the tree's output must match the shape of the weight matrix to assign per-element saliency scores. The metric is then applied layer-wise to produce binary sparsity masks at target ratios (50% unstructured, 4:8, or 2:4 structured), yielding a pruned LLM ready for inference without any weight updates.

## Method

Pruner-Zero formulates pruning metric discovery as a Symbolic Regression problem and searches the space using Genetic Programming over expression trees. Each individual is a tree with LLM statistics at leaves and primitive operations at internal nodes; evolution proceeds via tournament selection from the top-k candidates, subtree crossover, and constrained node mutation (unary→unary, binary→binary) to preserve structural validity. A key innovation is the Opposing Operation Simplification (OOS) strategy, which detects and removes mathematically redundant pairs (e.g., exp/log, sub/neg) to maintain population diversity and reduce search-space redundancy. Fitness is evaluated as perplexity of LLaMA-2-7B pruned at 50% unstructured sparsity on WikiText2, with each evaluation completing in under 5 minutes. The discovered metric is ‖|W|×|W|‖×σ(|G|), combining squared normalized weights with min-max-scaled gradients, and it generalizes across LLaMA, LLaMA-2, OPT, and Tiny-LLaMA families without retraining.

## Results

On WikiText2 perplexity at 50% unstructured sparsity, Pruner-Zero achieves 6.95 on LLaMA-7B, 5.94 on LLaMA-13B, 5.01 on LLaMA-30B, 4.33 on LLaMA-65B, 6.26 on LLaMA-2-7B, 5.36 on LLaMA-2-13B, and 3.82 on LLaMA-2-70B — all without weight updates. Under 2:4 structured pruning, Pruner-Zero reaches 10.61 (LLaMA-7B), 8.11 (LLaMA-13B), 6.51 (LLaMA-30B), 5.67 (LLaMA-65B), 10.52 (LLaMA-2-7B), 7.41 (LLaMA-2-13B), and 4.81 (LLaMA-2-70B). On zero-shot tasks (mean of 7 benchmarks), Pruner-Zero achieves 59.56% on LLaMA-7B and 71.10% on LLaMA-2-70B at 50% sparsity, surpassing even the dense LLaMA-2-70B baseline (67.08%). On GSM8K in-context learning, Pruner-Zero scores 0.1403 vs. 0.1312 (Wanda) and 0.1152 (SparseGPT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pruner-Zero consistently outperforms Wanda (the strongest no-weight-update baseline) across all 7 model sizes at both 50% unstructured and 2:4 structured sparsity, with perplexity improvements of 0.14–0.70 (e.g., 6.95 vs. 7.26 on LLaMA-7B; 4.81 vs. 5.16 on LLaMA-2-70B at 2:4). Against SparseGPT (which requires weight updates), Pruner-Zero wins on 6 of 7 models at 50% unstructured sparsity and on 6 of 7 at 2:4 structured, with the sole exception being LLaMA-2-7B at 2:4 where SparseGPT achieves 10.17 vs. Pruner-Zero's 10.52. The magnitude baseline is decisively outperformed by a wide margin (e.g., 6.95 vs. 17.29 on LLaMA-7B at 50%). A critical practical advantage is that Pruner-Zero requires no weight updates or retraining, matching Wanda's deployment simplicity while delivering lower perplexity. On zero-shot tasks, Pruner-Zero achieves 71.10% mean accuracy on LLaMA-2-70B at 50% sparsity, exceeding the dense model's 67.08%, while SparseGPT reaches 67.28% and Wanda 67.03%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the post-training pruning lineage (SparseGPT → Wanda → GBLM-Pruner) by reframing the design of the pruning metric itself as a Symbolic Regression problem solvable via Genetic Programming. It builds directly on Wanda's implementation framework and calibration-data protocol, and draws methodological inspiration from program-synthesis works like AutoML-Zero and EMQ that evolve expressions from scratch. The key departure is the Opposing Operation Simplification (OOS) strategy, which addresses a redundancy problem specific to evolving mathematical expressions that prior GP-for-ML work has not encountered in this form.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By automating what was previously a manual, expert-driven trial-and-error process, Pruner-Zero lowers the barrier to designing effective pruning metrics and produces a simple, interpretable expression (|W|×|W|×σ(|G|)) that consistently outperforms hand-designed alternatives across LLaMA, LLaMA-2, and OPT families without retraining. The finding that pruned models can exceed dense-model zero-shot accuracy (e.g., LLaMA-2-70B: 71.10 vs. 67.08) suggests pruning acts as beneficial regularization, though this needs further validation. Open questions include whether the GP-discovered metric generalizes to non-Transformer architectures, whether deeper or wider search spaces yield substantially better metrics, and whether the approach scales to higher sparsity ratios (>60%) where all current methods degrade sharply.
