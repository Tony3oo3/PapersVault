## Problem

Existing CVaR policy gradient algorithms optimize the conditional value-at-risk objective by computing gradients only from the worst q-fraction of sampled trajectories, discarding all remaining samples. This leads to severely low sample efficiency and slow convergence, particularly in safety-critical applications where the tail risk must be estimated accurately. The paper asks whether all trajectories can be leveraged simultaneously while still faithfully optimizing the CVaR criterion.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a finite-horizon MDP where at each step the agent observes a state X_t, selects an action A_t, and incurs a random cost C_t drawn jointly with the next state. The predictive tail probability model f_t^φ takes as input the next state X_{t+1} and the running cost offset C_{1:t} − η (a scalar), and outputs a probability in [0,1] representing the likelihood that the total cost will exceed the VaR threshold. The final output is the optimized policy parameter θ, with the reweighted trajectory H_{T+1}^Q = (X_1, A_1, Q̂_1 C_1, …, X_T, A_T, Q̂_T C_T) serving as the intermediate object fed into standard risk-neutral gradient estimators.

## Method

PCVAR reformulates the CVaR objective via its variational representation and then applies a Rao-Blackwellization argument to obtain a time-separable, risk-neutral form J_q(π) = E[Σ_t Q_t^{π,η} C_t], where Q_t^{π,η} = P(C_{1:T} ≥ η | H_{t+1}) is the predictive tail probability. A key structural result (Proposition 3.3) shows that for Markov policies, Q_t depends only on (X_{t+1}, C_{1:t} − η), reducing the estimation task to a standard supervised regression that is fully decoupled from policy optimization. The algorithm then runs three parallel SGD updates: θ is updated using conventional risk-neutral policy gradient estimators (score function, direct differentiation, or randomized finite differences) applied to the reweighted costs Q̂_t C_t; η is updated to track the VaR_q value; and φ is updated to minimize the squared prediction error of the tail probability model. A Lagrangian relaxation handles the leader-follower coupling between the outer θ optimization and the inner η, φ optimizations, and the approach extends naturally to general spectral risk measures via a linear combination of CVaR objectives.

## Results

In the continuous Blackjack experiment (q=0.1), PCVaR reduces the gradient variance to 6.71 at the CVaR-optimal policy (θ≈14.2), compared to 24.01 for NCVaR and 24.67 for GCVaR — a roughly 3.5× variance reduction. At the risk-neutral operating point (θ≈16.83), PCVaR achieves 33.66 variance versus 97.59 (NCVaR) and 96.92 (GCVaR), a ~3× reduction. In the real-world pair trading experiment on the Tiingo dataset (990 days, T=39 periods), PCVaR outperforms all baselines in cumulative return and exhibits the most stable performance throughout the testing period, with the same learning rate and batch size used across all three algorithms in the Blackjack experiment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PCVaR against two baselines: GCVaR (Tamar et al., 2015b), the standard CVaR policy gradient that uses only the worst q-fraction of trajectories, and NCVaR, a naive variant of PCVaR that omits the predictive tail probability reweighting. PCVaR decisively outperforms both on gradient variance: at the CVaR-optimal point it achieves 6.71 vs. 24.01 (NCVaR) and 24.67 (GCVaR), and at the risk-neutral point 33.66 vs. 97.59 and 96.92 respectively. PCVaR also converges faster to the CVaR-optimal policy parameter (Figure 2), reaching θ≈14.2 in fewer episodes than either baseline under identical learning rates and batch sizes. In the pair trading experiment on Tiingo data, PCVaR achieves the highest cumulative return and the most stable performance across the evaluation period, outperforming GCVaR, NCVaR, and a risk-neutral policy gradient baseline. The variance reduction is attributed to the Rao-Blackwellized reweighting (Lemma 4.4), which replaces the binary indicator I{C₁:ₜ≥η} with the smoother predictive tail probability Q_t, reducing noise in each gradient term.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the variational representation of CVaR (Rockafellar et al., 2000) and the existing CVaR policy gradient framework (Tamar et al., 2015b), which computes gradients using only the worst q-fraction of sample trajectories. The key departure is the introduction of a predictive tail probability process that reweights individual cost terms, enabling all trajectories to contribute to the gradient. The authors explicitly contrast their approach with Huang et al. (2021), who proposed both fully path-dependent (multiplicative) and state-dependent reweighting schemes, arguing that their predictive tail probabilities achieve full path-dependence while admitting a parsimonious, stably estimable representation via the Markov property.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for risk-sensitive RL practitioners: the reweighting strategy is a drop-in modification to any existing risk-neutral policy gradient algorithm (score function, direct differentiation, or finite difference), and the predictive tail probability model is trained as a decoupled supervised learning task. However, the paper is limited to Markov policies, provides no convergence rate or global optimality guarantees, and the empirical validation is on relatively small-scale problems (continuous Blackjack, pair trading). Open questions include whether the predictive tail probability idea extends to non-Markov policies or value-based methods, and whether the variance reduction observed empirically can be formalized under general temporal dependencies.
