## Problem

Well-trained deep networks on balanced data exhibit Neural Collapse (NC), where last-layer features collapse to within-class means forming a simplex ETF structure, but this phenomenon is rarely achieved on long-tailed datasets, causing minority-class representations to entangle and performance to degrade. Existing NC-based long-tailed methods either fix the classifier as a random ETF or add explicit geometric regularization terms, but none address the imbalance from a distribution-matching perspective that can flexibly combine with both fixed and learnable classifiers.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-LT, 224×224 for ImageNet-LT and Places-LT) through a CNN backbone (ResNet-32/50 or ResNeXt-50) and outputs a class label via a linear classifier. The DisA loss operates on mini-batches of last-layer d-dimensional representations (d=64–4096 depending on backbone and dataset), computing an N×K transport plan between batch samples and K ETF prototype vectors. The loss is added as a scalar regularization term to any standard supervised classification loss.

## Method

DisA formulates the last-layer representations of N training samples as a discrete uniform distribution P and the K simplex ETF weight vectors as a balanced distribution Q, then minimizes the entropy-regularized optimal transport distance between them using the Sinkhorn algorithm. The cost matrix uses cosine distance (1 − cos(h_i, m_k)), and the resulting transport plan T* adaptively assigns larger weights to minority-class samples, effectively balancing the representation geometry without explicit re-weighting. The DisA loss is a plug-and-play regularization term (L_total = L_sup + λ·L_DisA) that can be combined with any supervised loss (CE, LDAM, Focal, DR, etc.) and works in two modes: with a fixed ETF classifier (only the encoder is trained) or with a learnable classifier (both encoder and classifier are optimized). This distribution-matching formulation is complementary to existing NC-based regularizers like INC, which enforce geometric constraints directly on class means rather than matching the full sample distribution to the ETF prototype distribution.

## Results

On CIFAR-LT-10, DisA consistently improves all baselines: CE+DisA reaches 74.7% top-1 accuracy at ρ=100 (vs. 72.8% for CE), CE-DRW+DisA achieves 82.1% (vs. 80.9%), and INC-DRW-cRT+DisA reaches 84.9% (vs. 83.3%). On CIFAR-LT-100, the gains are larger under extreme imbalance: CE+DisA improves from 58.5% to 63.2% at ρ=10, and CE-DRW+DisA from 61.5% to 65.7%. On ImageNet-LT (ResNet-50), CE-DRW+DisA jumps from 47.1% to 53.2% overall accuracy, with the Many-shot subset improving from 53.8% to 64.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DisA acts as a plug-and-play regularization that improves every baseline it is combined with. Against re-weighting methods (LDAM-DRW, CE-DRW), DisA adds 1.0–2.6% on CIFAR-LT-10 and 2.9–5.8% on CIFAR-LT-100, with the largest gains at higher imbalance ratios. Against NC-based methods, INC-DRW-cRT+DisA outperforms INC-DRW-cRT by 1.1–2.6% on CIFAR-LT-10 and 2.4–2.3% on CIFAR-LT-100; ETF-DR+DisA improves over ETF-DR by 1.8–2.0% at ρ=200/100. Against RBL (the strongest NC-based baseline), RBL+DisA gains 0.8–0.6% on CIFAR-LT-10 and 0.1–0.6% on CIFAR-LT-100. On ImageNet-LT, CE-DRW+DisA (ResNet-50) surpasses CE-DRW by 6.1% overall (53.2% vs. 47.1%), while INC-DRW-cRT+DisA (ResNeXt-50) edges out INC-DRW-cRT by 0.3% overall (54.5% vs. 54.2%) but loses on the Few-shot subset (33.0% vs. 35.4%). Against contrastive/two-stage baselines (KCL, TSC, BBN, HCL, MiSLAS, ARB), DisA-augmented methods (e.g., CE-DRW+DisA at 82.1% for ρ=100) remain competitive but do not uniformly surpass the strongest reported numbers from those papers, which use different training protocols.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Neural Collapse (NC) phenomenon observed in balanced learning (Papyan et al., 2020) and the subsequent line of work attempting to induce NC in imbalanced settings. Prior NC-based long-tailed methods either fix the classifier as a random simplex ETF (Yang et al., 2022; Peifeng et al., 2023) or add explicit geometric regularization terms to compact within-class and separate between-class features (Liu et al., 2023b; Xie et al., 2023). DisA departs from these by reframing the NC objective as a distribution-matching problem: it uses entropic Optimal Transport (Sinkhorn) to align the empirical distribution of last-layer representations with the ideal balanced ETF distribution, yielding a plug-and-play regularization that works with both fixed and learnable classifiers without imposing explicit geometric constraints on the classifier weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of DisA as a single regularization term that consistently improves a wide range of baselines (CE, LDAM, DRW, INC, RBL, ETF-DR) across CIFAR-LT-10/100, ImageNet-LT, and Places-LT makes it a practical tool for long-tailed classification practitioners. The OT formulation provides an interpretable adaptive balancing mechanism—minority samples receive larger transport weights—which offers a principled explanation for the gains beyond ad-hoc re-weighting. A key limitation is the ETF dimension constraint (d ≥ K), which prevents direct application to high-dimensional backbones or very large class counts (e.g., ImageNet-1000 with 2048-d features), leaving open how to generalize the distribution-alignment idea beyond the simplex ETF geometry. The added Sinkhorn computation, while modest, scales with batch size and class count, which could matter for very large-scale deployments.
