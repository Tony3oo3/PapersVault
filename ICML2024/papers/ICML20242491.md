## Problem

Generalization bounds for multi-label learning suffer from a strong dependency on the number of labels c (linear or square-root), which renders them vacuous in extreme multi-label settings where c exceeds n. Existing analyses also fail to explicitly distinguish between first-order label correlations (decoupled components) and high-order label correlations (coupled components induced by norm regularizers), leaving the theoretical impact of these inductive biases uncharacterized.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a vector-valued function class F: X ⊂ R^d → R^c parameterized by a weight matrix w ∈ R^{d×c} with a norm constraint α(w) ≤ Λ and a bounded nonlinear feature map β(φ(x)) ≤ A, along with a loss function that is Lipschitz continuous w.r.t. either the ℓ₂ or ℓ∞ norm. It produces high-probability generalization bounds of the form R(f) ≤ R̂_D(f) + O(1/√n) (up to logarithmic factors) that are independent of c in the decoupling case, and O(1/√n) in the coupling case for specific norm regularizers (general norm, Schatten-p norm). For Macro-Averaged AUC, the bound depends on r₀ = min_j min{|X_j⁺|, |X_j⁻|}, explicitly capturing the effect of class imbalance.

## Method

The paper develops novel vector-contraction inequalities that decouple the relationship among different components of the vector-valued function by introducing a projection function class P(F) = {(j,x) ↦ f_j(x) : f ∈ F}. For ℓ₂ Lipschitz loss, the Rademacher complexity of the loss space is bounded via a refined Dudley's entropy integral, where the ℓ₂ covering number of P(F) is controlled by Sudakov's minoration and the worst-case Rademacher complexity of P(F), yielding a bound independent of c (improving from O(c/√n) to Õ(1/√n)). For ℓ∞ Lipschitz loss, the analogous step uses the fat-shattering dimension to bound ℓ∞ covering numbers, again achieving Õ(1/√n) in the decoupling case (improving from Õ(√(c/n))). In the coupling case, the paper refines the vector-contraction inequality of Lei et al. (2019) to derive explicit bounds for general norm and Schatten-p norm regularizers, showing how the induced label correlations affect generalization. For Macro-Averaged AUC, a new label-based ranking multi-label Rademacher complexity is defined, and U-process techniques convert the non-i.i.d. pairwise structure into a sum-of-i.i.d. form to derive the bound Õ(√(c/r₀)).

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments. The headline results are improved generalization bounds for multi-label learning: for ℓ₂ Lipschitz loss, the bound is tightened from O(c/√n) (prior state of the art) to Õ(1/√n) in the decoupling case (Theorem 3.7); for ℓ∞ Lipschitz loss, the bound is tightened from Õ(√(c/n)) to Õ(1/√n) in the decoupling case (Theorem 3.17). For Macro-Averaged AUC, a Õ(√(c/r₀)) bound is derived (Theorem 4.2), where r₀ = minⱼ min{|Xⱼ⁺|, |Xⱼ⁻|} captures class-imbalance severity. These bounds are independent of the number of labels c (up to logarithmic terms) and hold for general function classes under mild assumptions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's comparisons are entirely theoretical, benchmarking its generalization bounds against prior state-of-the-art bounds. For ℓ₂ Lipschitz loss, the prior best result for general function classes in the decoupling case was O(c/√n) (Maurer 2016; Wu & Zhu 2020; Wu et al. 2021a), while kernel-specific results achieved O(√(c/n)) (Lei et al. 2015; Wu & Zhu 2020). The paper's Theorem 3.7 improves this to Õ(1/√n), removing the c-dependency entirely. For ℓ∞ Lipschitz loss, the prior general-class bound was O(√(c/n)) (Foster & Rakhlin 2019; Liu et al. 2018), and coupling-case results achieved Õ(1/√n) only for norm-regularized classes (Lei et al. 2019; Wu et al. 2021b). The paper's Theorem 3.17 achieves Õ(1/√n) for general function classes in the decoupling case, matching the best coupling-case rates without requiring coupling-preserving constraints. For Macro-Averaged AUC, the paper's Õ(√(c/r₀)) bound is simpler than Wu et al. (2023)'s approach (which required transforming to multi-task learning with graph-dependent examples) and can also yield c-independent bounds by combining with Lemma 3.16.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of Rademacher-complexity-based generalization analysis for multi-label learning initiated by Dembczynski et al. (2010) and advanced by Wu & Zhu (2020) and Wu et al. (2021a), who derived O(c/√n) bounds using the standard vector-contraction inequality of Maurer (2016). The key departure is the introduction of a projection function class P(F) that decouples label components, enabling novel vector-contraction inequalities that eliminate the dependency on the number of labels c entirely, improving from O(c/√n) or O(√(c/n)) to Õ(1/√n) for both ℓ₂ and ℓ∞ Lipschitz losses. For the coupling case (high-order label correlations via norm regularizers), the paper refines results from Lei et al. (2019) and Foster & Rakhlin (2019) to cover general function classes with Schatten-p norm constraints, and introduces a label-based ranking Rademacher complexity for Macro-Averaged AUC analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tighter bounds provide the first theoretical guarantees for first-order label correlation methods (e.g., Binary Relevance) that were previously unaddressed by existing analyses, which all assumed coupling among components. The paper identifies an open question about whether the Ω(1/√n) lower bound is tight, arguing that label correlations should yield Ω(1/√(nc)) based on multi-task learning analogies, suggesting current methodologies have fundamental limitations. The Macro-Averaged AUC bound's dependence on r₀ (the minimum class size) formalizes the intuition that class imbalance degrades generalization, though the practical impact is limited since these are capacity-based bounds that do not directly guide algorithm design.
