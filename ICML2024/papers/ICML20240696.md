## Problem

Existing group inference methods (e.g., ERM-based misclassification, EIIL) produce pseudo group labels that yield a significant worst-group accuracy gap compared to oracle group labels, yet they either struggle with complex spurious correlations or require additional information such as partial group annotations or human priors. The paper asks whether a more accurate group inference method can be developed that requires no such auxiliary information, thereby closing the performance gap between inferred and oracle group labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240696/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

GIC takes as input feature representations (from an ERM-trained or pretrained model such as ResNet-50, LeNet-5, or BERT) of both the training set and a comparison dataset whose group distribution differs slightly from the training set. The comparison data may be labeled (e.g., a validation set) or unlabeled (e.g., a subset of test data or non-uniformly resampled training data), and no group labels are required for either dataset. The output is a binary spurious attribute prediction per training sample, which combined with the true label forms a pseudo group label (y, ŷ_s) that is passed to downstream invariant learning algorithms.

## Method

GIC trains a lightweight single-layer spurious attribute classifier by jointly optimizing two objectives: a correlation term (cross-entropy between true labels and predicted spurious attribute labels on training data) that enforces high label–spurious-attribute correlation, and a spurious term (KL divergence between the conditional label distributions P(y|ŷ_s) on training vs. comparison data) that penalizes invariant attributes whose correlation is stable across datasets. The key insight is that spurious attributes exhibit high correlation with labels in the training set but this correlation shifts when the group distribution changes, whereas invariant attributes do not. When comparison labels are unavailable, a proven lower bound (Theorem 3.1) replaces them with feature representations, enabling fully unlabeled comparison data. The KL terms are estimated via the MINE algorithm, and the resulting pseudo group labels are plugged into downstream invariant learning methods (Mixup, GroupDRO, Subsample, Upsample) to train the final robust classifier.

## Results

GIC combined with Mixup (GIC_Cy-M) achieves worst-group accuracies of 72.2% (CMNIST), 86.3% (Waterbirds), 89.4% (CelebA), and 72.5% (CivilComments), consistently outperforming all group-label-free baselines (ERM, JTT, EIIL, CnC) by 3–25 points across datasets. On CelebA and CivilComments, GIC nearly matches or exceeds oracle group-label methods (e.g., 89.4% vs. LISA's 89.3% on CelebA; 72.5% vs. LISA's 72.6% on CivilComments), while still trailing on Waterbirds (86.3% vs. DFR's 92.9%). The unlabeled comparison variant (GIC_C-M) achieves nearly identical performance (e.g., 89.5% worst-group on CelebA), confirming that true labels on comparison data are not strictly necessary.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GIC_Cy-M (GIC + Mixup) outperforms all group-label-free baselines on worst-group accuracy across every dataset: it beats JTT by 4.9–7.9 points, EIIL by 9.1–12.5 points, and CnC by 0.6–3.6 points (where CnC is reported). Against oracle group-label methods, GIC nearly matches LISA on CelebA (89.4% vs. 89.3%) and CivilComments (72.5% vs. 72.6%), and surpasses GroupDRO on CMNIST (72.2% vs. 69.8%) and CivilComments (72.5% vs. 70.0%), but still trails DFR on Waterbirds (86.3% vs. 92.9%) and LISA on CMNIST (72.2% vs. 73.3%). GIC also outperforms DFR on CMNIST (72.2% vs. 70.6%), CelebA (89.4% vs. 88.3%), and CivilComments (72.5% vs. 70.1%), and beats SSA on CMNIST (72.2% vs. 71.1%) and CivilComments (72.5% vs. 69.9%). The unlabeled variant GIC_C-M achieves within 0.1–1.0 points of GIC_Cy-M on all datasets, confirming that comparison data labels are not required. Table 2 further shows GIC consistently outperforms ERM and EI (EIIL's inference component) when paired with GroupDRO, Subsample, Upsample, or Mixup as the downstream invariant learning algorithm.

## Relation to Prior Work

GIC builds on the group inference line of work (JTT, CnC, EIIL) that infers spurious attribute labels without oracle group annotations, but departs from these methods by introducing a comparison dataset and a contrastive KL-divergence objective that explicitly violates the invariant learning principle to isolate spurious features. While prior methods like EIIL maximize GroupDRO loss or JTT uses ERM misclassifications as heuristics, GIC's two-term objective (correlation + spurious discrepancy) is designed to specifically target the distributional shift in spurious-attribute-to-label correlation across datasets. The paper also positions itself as a modular group inference component that plugs into existing invariant learning algorithms (Mixup, GroupDRO, Subsample, Upsample) rather than proposing a new end-to-end robust training procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GIC narrows the significant performance gap between oracle group labels and inferred group labels, making group-robust training more practical in settings where group annotations are expensive or unavailable. The semantic consistency finding—that GIC's misclassifications are semantically coherent and actually improve Mixup-based invariant learning beyond oracle labels—suggests that imperfect group inference can be beneficial for certain downstream methods, opening questions about whether deliberately 'noisy' group assignments could be optimized for specific invariant learning strategies. Limitations include the need for a comparison dataset with a different group distribution (however subtle), sensitivity to hyperparameters γ and K, and evaluation limited to binary spurious attributes on four datasets.
