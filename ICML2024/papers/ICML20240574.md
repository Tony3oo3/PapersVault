## Problem

Existing deep equilibrium (DEQ) models lack formal guarantees of fixed-point existence and uniqueness, and the convergence of their iterative solvers is not theoretically established, making them potentially unstable in practice. Prior approaches that do provide guarantees (e.g., monDEQ via monotone operator theory) impose complex parameterization constraints that are difficult to implement and introduce computational overhead.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 28×28 grayscale images (MNIST) or 32×32 RGB images (SVHN, CIFAR-10) and outputs a 10-class label. Internally, the DEQ layer operates on nonnegative vectors in the positive orthant: the input to the implicit layer is made nonnegative (or strictly positive for pcDEQ-1) via a ReLU or softplus preprocessing step, and the fixed point is computed by standard fixed-point iteration with a relative-error stopping criterion (tolerance 1e-4).

## Method

The paper constrains DEQ layers to be positive concave (PC) mappings by enforcing nonnegative weight matrices and elementwise concave activation functions (ReLU6, tanh, softsign, or sigmoid) on the nonnegative orthant. This places the layer in the class of standard interference (SI) mappings, whose asymptotic mapping has nonlinear spectral radius zero, thereby guaranteeing a unique fixed point and geometric convergence of the plain fixed-point iteration from any nonnegative starting point. Two variants are defined: pcDEQ-1 (NC activation requiring a strictly positive input) and pcDEQ-2 (PC activation requiring only a nonnegative input). Training uses the standard Jacobian-based implicit differentiation from Bai et al. (2019) without any modification, and the nonnegativity constraint on weights is enforced by projecting negative values to zero after each backpropagation step.

## Results

On MNIST, the best pcDEQ configuration (pcDEQ-1-MC-ReLU6) achieves 99.3% test accuracy with only 41K parameters, outperforming the strongest baseline monDEQ-SC (99.1%, 84K params). On SVHN, pcDEQ-1-MC-ReLU6 reaches 93.0% accuracy (131K params), surpassing monDEQ-MT (92.4%, 170K params). On CIFAR-10 with data augmentation, pcDEQ-1-MC-ReLU6* achieves 89.2% accuracy with 661K parameters, slightly exceeding monDEQ-MT* (89.0%, 1M params) while using roughly 34% fewer parameters. Across all three datasets, pcDEQ models consistently use fewer parameters than NODE, ANODE, and monDEQ baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

pcDEQ outperforms all three baselines (NODE, ANODE, monDEQ) on every dataset. On MNIST, the best pcDEQ (99.3%) beats the best monDEQ (99.1%) by 0.2% while using half the parameters (41K vs 84K). On SVHN, pcDEQ-1-MC-ReLU6 (93.0%) exceeds monDEQ-MT (92.4%) by 0.6% with fewer parameters (131K vs 170K). On CIFAR-10 without augmentation, pcDEQ-1-MC-ReLU6 (78.2%) leads monDEQ-SC (74.0%) by 4.2%. With data augmentation on CIFAR-10, pcDEQ-1-MC-ReLU6* (89.2%, 661K) marginally surpasses monDEQ-MT* (89.0%, 1M) while using ~34% fewer parameters. Notably, pcDEQ achieves these results with a simpler implementation (nonnegative weights + concave activations) compared to monDEQ's complex strong-monotonicity parameterization and FFT-based convolutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep equilibrium (DEQ) model framework (Bai et al., 2019) by imposing structural constraints—nonnegative weights and concave activation functions—that place the fixed-point mapping within the class of positive concave (PC) mappings from nonlinear Perron-Frobenius theory. The theoretical backbone is drawn from standard interference (SI) mapping theory, well-established in wireless network optimization (Yates, 1995; Cavalcante et al., 2016, 2019), which provides existence/uniqueness and geometric convergence guarantees without the complex strong-monotonicity conditions required by the closest competitor, monDEQ (Winston & Kolter, 2020). The geometric convergence result for PC mappings (Piotrowski & Cavalcante, 2022) is the key theorem enabling the use of plain fixed-point iteration without acceleration methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first practical application of SI mapping theory to deep learning, offering a simpler and more transparent set of architectural constraints (nonnegative weights + concave activations) than the monotone operator machinery in monDEQ, while still guaranteeing a unique fixed point and geometric convergence of the standard iteration. The empirical demonstration that the Lipschitz constant can exceed 1 during training (Figure 4) while the fixed point remains unique and the iteration still converges highlights that NPF conditions are strictly weaker than Banach fixed-point conditions. However, the approach is restricted to a narrow class of activations (ReLU6, tanh, softsign, sigmoid) and nonnegative weights, and validation is limited to small benchmarks (MNIST, SVHN, CIFAR-10); whether these constraints scale to larger, more diverse architectures and datasets remains an open question, as does whether the convergence rate can be improved beyond geometric.
