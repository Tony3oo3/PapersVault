## Problem

Existing trajectory-matching graph condensation methods fail to achieve lossless condensation on large-scale graphs because the supervision signals from expert trajectories are biased toward difficult nodes, causing the condensed graph to overlook representative patterns from easy nodes. This paper identifies this bias as the root cause of the persistent performance gap between condensed and original graphs, and proposes the first approach toward truly lossless graph condensation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242499/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a large-scale graph dataset with node features, labels, and adjacency structure (e.g., Ogbn-arxiv with 169K nodes, Reddit with 233K nodes and 57M edges) and produces a structure-free condensed graph dataset at ratios as low as 0.9% (Citeseer) to 5% (Reddit). The condensed graph is evaluated by training a randomly initialized GNN on it and measuring test accuracy on the original graph's held-out test set, with the goal of matching the full-dataset performance across multiple GNN architectures.

## Method

GEOM operates in three phases: (1) a buffer phase where expert trajectories are trained using curriculum learning that orders nodes from easy to difficult based on a homophily-based difficulty score, producing trajectories with more diverse supervision signals; (2) a condensation phase using expanding window matching, where the matching range gradually grows from early to later checkpoints rather than using a fixed or sliding window, ensuring the condensed graph first learns representative patterns from easy nodes before enriching them with difficult-node information; and (3) a Knowledge Embedding Extractor (KEE) that adds a KL-divergence loss using soft labels from well-trained GNNs to transfer additional dataset knowledge. Theoretical analysis formalizes how CL reduces initialization error (via flatter loss landscapes) and expanding window matching reduces matching error (by involving ~10× more trajectory stages), jointly minimizing accumulated error during evaluation.

## Results

GEOM achieves lossless graph condensation (matching or exceeding the full-dataset performance) on all five datasets at very low condensation ratios: Citeseer at 0.9% (73.0% vs 71.7% full), Cora at 1.3% (82.5% vs 81.2% full), Ogbn-arxiv at 5% (71.4% vs 71.4% full), Flickr at 1% (47.3% vs 47.2% full), and Reddit at 5% (93.9% vs 93.9% full). GEOM attains state-of-the-art results in 18 out of 19 experimental settings, with improvements of up to 2.8% over the previous best method SFGC. In cross-architecture evaluation, GEOM's condensed graphs achieve lossless performance in 20 out of 35 dataset-architecture combinations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEOM outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and condensation ratio tested. The closest competitor, SFGC (trajectory matching, NeurIPS 2024), is consistently outperformed by 1–2.8% (e.g., 73.0% vs 71.4% on Citeseer at 0.9%, 82.5% vs 80.1% on Cora at 1.3%, 93.9% vs 91.9% on Reddit at 5%). GCond (gradient matching, ICLR 2021) trails GEOM by 2.5–5.4% and suffers out-of-memory failures on large-scale datasets (Ogbn-arxiv, Reddit at higher ratios). DC-Graph (gradient matching, ICLR 2020) is substantially weaker, lagging by 5–10% on most datasets. The coreset selection baselines—Herding (ICML 2009) and K-Center (ICLR 2018)—perform worst overall, with Herding reaching only 57.1% on Citeseer and 67.0% on Cora at the lossless ratios, far below GEOM's lossless results. Notably, GEOM is the first method to achieve lossless condensation on all five datasets simultaneously, whereas SFGC and GCond fail to close the gap to the full dataset on large-scale graphs.

## Relation to Prior Work

GEOM extends the trajectory matching graph condensation framework of SFGC (Zheng et al., 2024) by diagnosing a fundamental limitation: expert trajectories trained on the full graph produce supervision signals dominated by difficult (low-homophily) nodes, biasing the condensed graph away from representative patterns. To address this, the paper integrates curriculum learning (adapted from CLNode, Wei et al., 2023) to generate more diverse expert trajectories, and introduces expanding window matching to progressively incorporate both easy and difficult node information during condensation. The theoretical grounding draws on the accumulated error decomposition from Du et al. (2023), showing that curriculum learning flattens trajectories (reducing initialization error) while expanding window matching increases the number of optimized stages (reducing matching error). A knowledge embedding extractor, inspired by network distillation (Hinton et al., 2015), adds a KL-divergence-based loss to transfer soft-label knowledge from expert checkpoints into the condensed graph.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GEOM achieves the first reported lossless graph condensation results, matching original-dataset GNN performance at condensation ratios as low as 0.9% (Citeseer) and 5% (Ogbn-arxiv, Reddit), with 20 out of 35 cross-architecture combinations also lossless. This is practically significant for reducing the computational cost of training GNNs on large-scale graphs, a bottleneck in real-world deployment. The main limitation is that the method still requires pre-computing expert trajectories, incurring additional training cost that scales with the original graph size; the authors acknowledge this and flag efficiency improvements as future work. An open question is whether the curriculum-based trajectory generation and expanding window strategy can be made more compute-efficient, potentially through online or amortized trajectory generation.
