## Problem

Existing Universal Adversarial Perturbations (UAPs) are fragile under real-world transformations such as rotation, contrast changes, and scaling that perturbations inevitably undergo during transmission (e.g., a camera sticker affected by weather, or an audio signal degraded by channel noise). This non-robustness renders standard UAPs ineffective in practical attack scenarios where the perturbation cannot be combined with the input precisely, yet no prior method constructs UAPs that remain adversarial after such transformations are applied to the perturbation itself.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of training images sampled from a data distribution μ, a frozen target classifier f, and a transformation set T (compositions of bijective sub-differentiable functions such as rotation, translation, scaling, shearing, and brightness/contrast). It produces a single input-agnostic perturbation vector u_r constrained to l_p-norm < ε (e.g., ε=100 for ILSVRC 2012, ε=10 for CIFAR-10 in l_2-norm), which remains adversarial with high probability even after arbitrary transformations from T are applied to u_r before addition to the input. For unknown transformation sets, the method instead trains on PRIME primitive transformations (spectral, spatial, color) to yield a universally robust UAP that generalizes to unseen corruptions.

## Method

RobustUAP formulates robust UAP generation as an expectation maximization over both the data distribution and the transformation neighborhood N_T(u_r), which is computationally harder than either EOT (transformation-only) or standard UAP (data-only). The key technical contribution is a Chernoff-based probabilistic bound (Theorem 4.1) that, given n = ⌈(1/2ψ²)ln(2/φ)⌉ samples from the neighborhood, guarantees the estimated robust ASR is within ψ of the true value with probability ≥ 1−φ. The algorithm (Algorithm 1) uses this bound via an EstRobustness subroutine to guide optimization: for each batch, it samples n transformations, then runs an inner PGD loop (up to 40 iterations) computing mean loss over batch × transformations, updating the perturbation with sign-gradient steps and l_p-projection, and stopping the inner loop only when the estimated batch robustness exceeds a threshold ζ. At the end of each epoch, EstRobustness is evaluated over the full training set and the algorithm terminates when the global robust ASR meets the target. For unknown transformations, training on PRIME's three primitive types (spectral, spatial, color) yields UAPs that generalize to unseen corruptions like fog, JPEG compression, and motion blur without domain-specific modeling.

## Results

On ILSVRC 2012 with the most challenging transformation set R(10),T(2,2),Sh(2),Sc(2),B(2,0.001), RobustUAP achieves a Robust ASR of 86.4% (γ=0.6), compared to 63.1% for SGD, 2.9% for Standard UAP_RP, and 0.0% for Standard UAP. On CIFAR-10 with the same transformation set, RobustUAP reaches 79.6% Robust ASR versus 58.3% for SGD. In a real-world wireless attack on the FIRE system, RobustUAP produces a median SNR drop of 4.06 dB, more than double the 1.84 dB drop from Gaussian noise and the 1.91 dB from standard UAP. The method also generalizes to unseen transformations: UAPs trained on PRIME primitives maintain >58.3% Robust ASR across all tested corruption sets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RobustUAP consistently outperforms all three baselines across every dataset and transformation set. Against Standard UAP (Moosavi-Dezfooli et al., 2017), RobustUAP achieves at least 53.4% higher Robust ASR; Standard UAP drops to 0.0% on most non-trivial transformation sets (e.g., R(20), R(10),T(2,2),Sh(2),Sc(2),B(2,0.001)) while RobustUAP maintains 75–93%. Against the SGD baseline (the strongest competitor), RobustUAP gains 11.6–23.3 percentage points on harder transformation sets (e.g., 86.4% vs 63.1% on ILSVRC with all five transformations), though the gap narrows to ~1% on the easiest set T(2,2). Standard UAP_RP (the iterative UAP algorithm modified to compute robust perturbations per point) is consistently the weakest robust baseline, often below 10% on challenging sets. Even when RobustUAP is restricted to the same compute time as SGD, it still outperforms SGD by up to 9% on the hardest transformation set, and SGD's performance flatlines when allowed to run longer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Universal Adversarial Perturbation (UAP) framework of Moosavi-Dezfooli et al. (2017) by incorporating robustness to real-world transformations, drawing conceptually on the Expectation over Transformation (EOT) approach of Athalye et al. (2018) for robust adversarial examples. While EOT creates input-specific perturbations robust to transformations and UAPs create input-agnostic perturbations, no prior work had combined both properties into a single optimization. The paper further leverages PRIME (Modas et al., 2022) primitive transformations to handle unknown transformation sets, and introduces Chernoff-based probabilistic bounds to certify robustness across the full transformation neighborhood rather than relying on random sampling alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard UAPs drop to 0% robust ASR under even moderate transformations (e.g., ±20° rotation) exposes a critical gap between the UAP threat model and practical attack scenarios like camera stickers or wireless signal injection, where perturbations inevitably undergo modification. The PRIME-based approach to handling unknown transformations offers a practical path to domain-agnostic robust UAPs without requiring explicit modeling of every possible corruption. Key limitations include inability to handle non-differentiable transformations, reduced effectiveness against adversarially trained models, and the preliminary (single-dataset) nature of the adversarial training results. Open questions include whether the probabilistic bounds can be tightened for non-uniform transformation distributions and whether the approach scales to video or multi-modal settings.
