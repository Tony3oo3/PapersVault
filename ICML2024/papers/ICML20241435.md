## Problem

While the equivalence between regularization and robustness is well-established for single-agent MDPs, the multi-agent Markov game setting remains largely unexplored: even two-player zero-sum robust matrix games with reward uncertainty are PPAD-hard, and no efficient planning oracle existed for the sample-efficient learning algorithms of Blanchet et al. (2023). This paper addresses the gap by establishing a general regularization-robustness equivalence for robust Markov games (RMGs) with s-rectangular uncertainty, characterizing exactly when the equivalence yields polynomial-time solvability, and proving fundamental hardness limits.

## Input / Output

The method takes a nominal Markov game G* = (P*, r*) with N players, horizon H, and an s-rectangular uncertainty set U = P × (r* + R) that may be policy-dependent (i.e., R(π) varies with the joint policy). It produces an MPRNE policy π† and an equivalent regularized Markov game whose regularizer at each stage (s,h) is the support function σ_{R_{i,s,h}}(−μ_i μ_{−i}^T) evaluated at the negative outer product of the joint policy. For transition uncertainty, the regularizer is generalized to a policy-value function Ω_{s,h}(P_h, −v, μ) that couples the policy with the future robust value vector, making the output a richer object than a standard policy-only regularizer.

## Method

The core mechanism uses Fenchel-Rockafellar duality to rewrite each player's robust value function as the nominal value minus the support function of the uncertainty set, σ_{R_{i,s,h}}(−π_{i,h}(s)π_{−i,h}^T(s)), thereby establishing an exact equivalence between computing an MPRNE of the RMG and computing an MPNE of a regularized MG with that support function as the regularizer. For transition uncertainty, the paper introduces a generalized policy-value regularization where the regularizer Ω_{s,h}(P_h, −v, μ) = σ_{P_{s,h}}(−vμ^T) depends on both the policy and the future value vector, accommodating the non-linearity introduced by uncertain transitions. The paper then proves PPAD-hardness for two-player zero-sum RMGs even under (s,a)-rectangularity, identifying the product structure π_1^T R π_2 in the support function as the structural bottleneck that simulates general-sum behavior. To recover efficiency, they introduce the 'efficiently player-decomposable' condition where σ decomposes as Ω_{i,i}(π_i) + Ω_{i,−i}(π_{−i}), which cancels cross-player terms in the RNE gap and reduces the equivalent regularized game to a zero-sum game solvable in polynomial time via backward induction. This class includes L_1 and L_∞ ball uncertainty sets, as well as entropy and KL-divergence regularizers.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental benchmarks. The headline results are: (1) a general equivalence (Theorem 3.6) showing that computing a Markov-perfect robust Nash equilibrium (MPRNE) of an s-rectangular RMG is equivalent to computing a Markov-perfect NE of a regularized MG, where the regularizer is the support function of the uncertainty set; (2) a PPAD-hardness result (Theorem 4.1) showing that even two-player zero-sum RMGs with (s,a)-rectangular reward uncertainty and |S|=H=1 are computationally intractable; and (3) a positive polynomial-time result (Theorem 4.3) showing that under the 'efficiently player-decomposable' assumption (satisfied by L1 and L∞ ball uncertainty sets), the MPRNE of a two-player zero-sum RMG can be computed in polynomial time via backward induction on the equivalent regularized game.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not run empirical comparisons against baselines, as it is a theoretical contribution. However, it positions its results relative to three key prior works. Compared to Zhang et al. (2020b), which devised RNE-learning algorithms but only proved asymptotic convergence, this paper provides a full equivalence characterization and polynomial-time algorithms for a broad class of uncertainty sets. Compared to Blanchet et al. (2023), which proposed provably sample-efficient RNE learning under (s,a)-rectangularity but required an efficient planning oracle that did not exist in the literature, this paper supplies exactly that missing oracle via the regularization equivalence. Compared to Derman et al. (2021, 2023), which established the robustness-regularization duality for single-agent MDPs, this paper extends the equivalence to the multi-agent Markov game setting and further characterizes when the equivalence preserves zero-sum structure, enabling efficient solution. The paper also shows that the PPAD-hardness result (Theorem 4.1) stands in sharp contrast to classical game theory, where two-player zero-sum matrix games are solvable in polynomial time via linear programming, highlighting that reward uncertainty fundamentally changes the computational landscape.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-agent regularization-robustness equivalence of Derman et al. (2021, 2023) from MDPs to multi-agent Markov games, showing that the support function of the reward uncertainty set serves as the natural regularizer. It builds on the RMG framework and robust Nash equilibrium definition of Zhang et al. (2020b) and provides the missing planning oracle needed by the sample-efficient learning algorithms of Blanchet et al. (2023). The key departure from prior work is the computational complexity analysis: while the single-agent robust MDP literature has well-understood polynomial-time algorithms, this paper reveals that the multi-agent setting introduces fundamental hardness even in the simplest zero-sum case, and identifies the precise structural condition (player-decomposability of the support function) that restores tractability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PPAD-hardness result for two-player zero-sum RMGs with (s,a)-rectangular reward uncertainty is a significant negative result that fundamentally limits what can be achieved with standard zero-sum LP methods in the robust setting, redirecting algorithmic research toward the player-decomposable class. The regularization equivalence provides a practical recipe: any off-the-shelf regularized MG solver (entropy, KL, norm-based) can be repurposed to compute provably robust policies, confirming an empirical observation with formal guarantees. Open questions include whether the player-decomposability condition is necessary or merely sufficient, how these results extend to more than two players, and whether the transition-uncertainty hardness (PPAD-hard for H=2) admits analogous tractable subclasses beyond the ones identified here.
