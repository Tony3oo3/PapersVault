## Problem

Existing multimodal LLMs can process monaural audio but cannot perceive or reason about spatial sound in 3-D environments—identifying direction, distance, and relationships between multiple concurrent sound sources. Additionally, no large-scale dataset of in-the-wild spatial audio question-answering pairs existed to train or evaluate such capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242542/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 10-second binaural audio clip (2 channels, 32 kHz) and a natural-language question as input. The audio is converted to a (4, 1024, 128) feature tensor by concatenating left/right Mel-spectrograms with cosine and sine transforms of the Interaural Phase Difference, all projected through a 128-bin Mel filter bank. Outputs are free-form text answers spanning four granularities: sorted sound-event label lists, directional/distance tuples (e.g., 'left, front, above; 2.5m'), and binary Yes/No responses for spatial reasoning questions about relative positions and distances between two concurrent sources.

## Method

BAT couples a novel spatial audio encoder, SPATIAL-AST, with LLaMA-2 7B via a LLaMA-adapter v2 projection module. SPATIAL-AST processes the 4-channel front-end features through a 3×3 2D convolution, a 16×16 patch-embed CNN, and 12 transformer layers (initialized from AudioMAE) with three dedicated [CLS] tokens for category, distance, and direction prediction; it is pre-trained with a two-stage curriculum (detection-only, then joint detection + distance + DoA) using cross-entropy losses with tuned weight ratios (λ₁=1250, λ₂=1, λ₃=2). The full BAT model is fine-tuned on SPATIALSOUNDQA using a three-stage perception-to-reasoning curriculum: single-source perception (types A, B), multi-source separation (types C, D), and finally spatial reasoning (type E). Ablations show that the intermediate separation stage is critical—removing types C and D from training drops reasoning accuracy from 76.89% to near-random 54.68%, confirming that the model must learn implicit source separation before it can reason about inter-source spatial relationships.

## Results

SPATIAL-AST (binaural, two-stage training with IPD) achieves mAP 50.03% for sound event detection, MAE 17.94° for direction-of-arrival estimation, and DER 32.54% for distance estimation. The full BAT model (binaural audio + text prompt) reaches 76.89% average Binary Accuracy on spatial reasoning questions (Type E), with 69.77% on direction reasoning and 84.01% on distance reasoning. On perception tasks, BAT achieves 75.54% DoA accuracy and 29.16% DER for single-source scenarios (Types A/B), and 9.89% mAP and 37.65% DoA accuracy for multi-source scenarios (Types C/D).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SELDnet (Adavanne et al., 2018), SPATIAL-AST (binaural, two-stage, IPD) wins on all four metrics: mAP 50.03% vs. 42.66%, ER₂₀° 23.89% vs. 25.19%, MAE 17.94° vs. 19.21°, and DER 32.54% vs. 38.46%. Against AudioMAE (Huang et al., 2022), the comparison is less direct since AudioMAE is trained on anechoic monaural data (mAP 53.56%), but in the fair monaural setting SPATIAL-AST achieves 51.39% mAP (Stage-1) or 52.26% (Stage-2), surpassing AudioMAE's 47.18% monaural mAP. For the full BAT model, the monaural BAT baseline (analogous to LTU) achieves only 54.53% average Binary Accuracy on reasoning, far below the binaural BAT's 76.89%, confirming that spatial (binaural) input is essential for spatial reasoning. The one-stage BAT (trained only on Type E reasoning) scores 73.55% BA, while removing multi-source separation tasks (Types C/D) drops it to 54.68%, highlighting the curriculum's importance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) spatial audio perception, where prior work like SELDnet and AudioMAE handles sound event detection and localization but is limited to specific channel formats (first-order ambisonics or monaural) and single-task objectives, and (2) multimodal LLMs for audio (LTU, Pengi, SALMONN, Qwen-Audio), which process monaural audio but cannot perceive spatial cues. BAT departs from both by introducing SPATIAL-AST, a binaural encoder that jointly performs detection, DoA, and distance estimation, and by fusing it with LLaMA-2 via LLaMA-adapter v2 to enable natural-language spatial reasoning over multiple concurrent sound sources — a capability absent from all prior audio-LLM systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BAT and SPATIALSOUNDQA establish the first benchmark and model for spatial audio reasoning with LLMs, opening a direction that connects embodied AI, virtual reality, and assistive technologies. Key open questions include the sim-to-real gap (all data is synthesized via SoundSpaces 2.0), the restriction to at most two sound sources and binaural (not ambisonic) input, and the binary-answer format for reasoning that limits open-ended evaluation. The finding that removing intermediate sound-source-separation tasks (types C/D) from training collapses reasoning to near-random performance highlights a non-obvious dependency in the curriculum that may generalize to other multi-source perception settings.
