## Problem

Masked face recognition suffers from insufficient or inaccurate representations because masks occlude key facial features, and the ill-posed mapping between occluded observations and ground-truth faces makes identity prediction unreliable. Existing generative approaches (inpainting) recover missing pixels but lose identity-discriminative structure, while discriminative approaches (finetuning recognizers) improve masked accuracy at the cost of generalization on unmasked faces. The paper tackles the coordination between reconstructing general, mask-robust representations and enhancing their identity discrimination within a single unified framework.

## Input / Output

The network takes a 256×256 RGB masked face image and a corresponding 256×256 binary occlusion mask as input. The generative encoder produces a 64×64×256 category-aware descriptor, which the discriminative reformer converts into a 512-dimensional identity-aware vector; a final fully-connected + softmax head maps this to an identity class prediction. Training uses synthetic masked faces generated by pasting 45 diverse mask patterns onto Celeb-A images via MaskTheFace, so no real masked-face annotations are required for backbone pretraining.

## Method

The approach cascades three modules—generative encoder, discriminative reformer, and feature classifier—and trains them in a greedy module-wise pretraining strategy. The generative encoder is initialized from the ICT Transformer-based inpainting model (pretrained on FFHQ) and finetuned with pixel reconstruction plus WGAN-GP adversarial loss to produce occlusion-robust, mask-invariant descriptors. The discriminative reformer is a 22-layer ResNet-like CNN trained via relational knowledge distillation from a VGGFace2 teacher, matching first-order (linear), second-order (pairwise distance), and third-order (triplet cosine angle) structural relations between teacher features on unmasked faces and student features on masked faces. Finally, the backbone is frozen and a lightweight FC+softmax classifier is finetuned with cross-entropy on labeled masked faces. The key design choice is performing the generative-to-discriminative transformation in latent space rather than reconstructing pixels first, which avoids re-mapping loss and leverages the flatter landscape of high-level representations.

## Results

On synthetic masked LFW, G2D achieves 97.58% verification accuracy (MR-MP) and 97.75% (UMR-MP), with EER of 3.27% and 3.05% respectively, outperforming all 16 baselines. On the realistic RMFD dataset (UMR-MP), G2D reaches 79.18% accuracy and 21.64% EER, a 6.6-point improvement over the best general recognizer (ArcFace at 72.35%). On MLFW, G2D delivers the best verification accuracy among all compared methods. On the normal-face benchmark CPLFW, G2D achieves 92.23%, competitive with MaskInv (92.86%) and above ArcFace (92.08%), confirming that the approach does not sacrifice unmasked-face performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

G2D outperforms all general face recognizers (CenterLoss, VGGFace, VGGFace2, ArcFace) by 3–16 points in accuracy on synthetic masked LFW, confirming that standard models degrade severely under occlusion. Among inpainting+recognition composites, the strongest baseline ICT+AF reaches 96.01% (MR-MP), still 1.57 points below G2D's 97.58%; notably, VGG2+GFC performs worse than VGG2 alone, showing that unregularized inpainting can be counterproductive. G2D surpasses the finetuning-based SRT approach (ResNet100+SRT: 92.80%) by 4.78 points, and the paper observes that SRT actually degrades the original ResNet50 baseline (85.85% → 78.87%), indicating overfitting to masked scenarios. Against DoDGAN (95.44% MR-MP, 94.32% UMR-MP), G2D leads by 2.14 and 3.43 points respectively, with a particularly large FMR1000 gap (33.44% vs. 58.97% on MR-MP), reflecting better balance between false matches and false non-matches. On realistic RMFD, G2D's 79.18% accuracy exceeds the best general model (ArcFace, 72.35%) by 6.83 points and DoDGAN (72.55%) by 6.63 points. On the normal-face CPLFW benchmark, G2D (92.23%) is marginally below MaskInv (92.86%) but above ArcFace (92.08%), demonstrating that the generative-to-discriminative pipeline preserves unmasked-face recognition capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the ICT Transformer-based inpainting model (Wan et al., 2021) as its generative encoder and positions itself at the intersection of two established lines of masked face recognition research: generative approaches (inpaint-then-recognize) and discriminative approaches (finetune or design specialized recognizers). It departs from both by proposing a unified cascaded architecture where a generative encoder produces occlusion-robust category-aware descriptors that are then converted into identity-aware vectors by a discriminative reformer, trained via relational knowledge distillation from a pretrained face recognizer. The greedy module-wise pretraining strategy—finetuning the encoder with reconstruction, then distilling the reformer, then finetuning a classifier—is the key methodological departure from prior work that either trains end-to-end or treats inpainting and recognition as separate stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that neither pure generative nor pure discriminative representations suffice for masked face recognition, and that their coordinated learning in a progressive self-supervised pipeline yields consistent gains across synthetic and realistic benchmarks. However, the model's 178.5M parameters (vs. ~25M for standard ResNet50-based recognizers) and reliance on synthetic mask synthesis limit immediate practical deployment, and the realistic RMFD accuracy of 79.18% still leaves substantial room for improvement. The authors identify simultaneous training on synthetic and realistic data and extension to other occlusion tasks (e.g., occluded person ReID) as natural next steps, and the finding that inpainting can degrade recognition accuracy (VGG2+GFC < VGG2 alone) highlights an underexplored tension in the inpaint-then-recognize paradigm.
