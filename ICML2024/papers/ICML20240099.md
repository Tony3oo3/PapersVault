## Problem

Existing unsupervised disentanglement methods rely on statistical independence assumptions (factorizing latent distributions) or group-theoretic definitions, which are unrealistic when factors of variation are correlated. No prior work has proposed a differentiable topological loss for disentanglement learning, despite the fact that disentangled representations should induce topologically mild changes in pixel space when traversing a single latent axis. This paper bridges that gap by formulating disentanglement as preservation of multi-scale topological structure under symmetry group(oid) actions on the data manifold.

## Input / Output

The method takes a batch of RGB images (64×64 for dSprites, 64×64×3 for 3D Shapes/MPI 3D, 96×96×3 for 3D Faces, 180×180×3 for CelebA) and produces a 10-dimensional latent code per image, where each dimension ideally encodes a single factor of variation. No ground-truth factor labels are required during training. In the GAN application, the input is a pretrained StyleGAN generator plus a batch of style-space latent codes, and the output is a set of discovered disentangled directions (e.g., azimuth, smile, hair color) in the style space.

## Method

TopDis adds a differentiable topological loss to any VAE-type objective: for each training step, a random latent axis is selected, latent codes are shifted along that axis using a Gaussian-preserving transformation (z_shifted = F⁻¹(F(z) + C)), and the RTD (Representation Topology Divergence) between the original and shifted reconstructed image batches is computed as the loss. RTD measures multi-scale topological dissimilarity via R-Cross-Barcodes of Vietoris-Rips filtrations on the two point clouds, and its subgradient is propagated back through the decoder. To prevent the topological regularization from degrading reconstruction, gradient orthogonalization projects the TopDis gradient onto the subspace orthogonal to the reconstruction gradient whenever their inner product is negative. The theoretical justification rests on Lie group(oid) actions: a truly disentangled factor corresponds to a continuous, invertible symmetry on the data distribution, which must preserve topological features, so minimizing RTD along that axis enforces disentanglement without assuming statistical independence of factors.

## Results

Adding the TopDis loss to six VAE variants (VAE, β-VAE, FactorVAE, β-TCVAE, ControlVAE, DAVA) consistently improves all four disentanglement metrics (FactorVAE score, MIG, SAP, DCI disentanglement) across dSprites, 3D Shapes, 3D Faces, and MPI 3D. Gains range from +8% to +50% on FactorVAE score, +27% to +70% on MIG, +6% to +110% on SAP, and +2% to +53% on DCI disentanglement depending on dataset and base model. The TopDis-augmented variant is the best-performing method in 94% of dataset/metric combinations, while reconstruction quality is preserved (Table 4). On the hardest dataset (MPI 3D), FactorVAE+TopDis achieves 0.665 FactorVAE score, 0.377 MIG, 0.238 SAP, and 0.438 DCI, outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopDis is added as an auxiliary loss to five state-of-the-art VAE variants (β-VAE, FactorVAE, β-TCVAE, ControlVAE, DAVA) and the classical VAE, and in every case the TopDis-augmented model outperforms its base on all four disentanglement metrics across all four synthetic datasets. The largest relative gains appear on MPI 3D (the hardest dataset), where FactorVAE+TopDis improves MIG from 0.336 to 0.377 (+12%) and SAP from 0.179 to 0.238 (+33%), and DAVA+TopDis improves FactorVAE score from 0.404 to 0.606 (+50%). Against the external baselines, FactorVAE+TopDis outperforms TCWAE (Gaujac et al., 2021) on dSprites across all reported metrics (e.g., MIG 0.36 vs. 0.32, SAP 0.082 vs. 0.072), and β-VAE+TopDis / β-TCVAE+TopDis achieve higher DCI disentanglement scores than the corresponding HFS (Roth et al., 2023) variants on dSprites (0.556 vs. 0.506; 0.531 vs. 0.499) and 3D Shapes (0.998 vs. 0.912; 0.901 vs. 0.857), though on MPI 3D the gap narrows (0.337 vs. 0.328; 0.356 vs. 0.328). The only notable exception is 3D Faces, where β-VAE+TopDis slightly underperforms β-VAE on MIG (0.545 vs. 0.561) and DCI (0.854 vs. 0.873), likely because β-VAE already achieves near-perfect FactorVAE score (1.0) on this simpler dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant statistical-independence paradigm in disentanglement (β-VAE's KL weighting, β-TCVAE's total correlation penalty, FactorVAE's factorization discriminator) by introducing a topological criterion: minimizing the multi-scale topological dissimilarity (RTD) between a batch of reconstructions and those obtained via a symmetry group(oid) action shift in latent space. It builds directly on the Representation Topology Divergence measure (Barannikov et al., 2022) and its differentiability (Trofimov et al., 2023), repurposing a tool originally designed for comparing static neural representations into a trainable loss. The Lie group(oid) action framework formalizes the shift operation and connects the topological objective to the equivariance-based definition of disentanglement from Higgins et al. (2018), while gradient orthogonalization (borrowed from multi-task learning) preserves reconstruction quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The topological perspective offers a principled alternative to statistical independence assumptions, and the paper demonstrates that TopDis improves disentanglement scores even when factors of variation are correlated—a setting where most prior methods degrade. The plug-in nature of the loss (applicable to any VAE variant and even to pretrained GANs for direction discovery) makes it broadly useful. Limitations include the O(N²) pairwise-distance computation, restriction to the image domain, and the need to tune the shift magnitude C and loss weight γ per dataset. Open questions include extension to non-image modalities (time series, robotics), scalability to larger batch sizes, and whether the topological criterion captures all relevant aspects of disentanglement for high-dimensional real-world data.
