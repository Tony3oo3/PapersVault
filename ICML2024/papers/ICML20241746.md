## Problem

Most existing confidence sequence constructions target scalar-valued bounded processes, with the elegant coin-toss (betting) framework of Waudby-Smith & Ramdas (2024) being a notable example. Extending this framework to vector-valued observations is nontrivial because the bet range in the original formulation depends on the candidate parameter, and natural multivariate data (e.g., categorical or probability-vector observations) have not been addressed. This paper asks whether an analogous general gambling framework exists for bounded, vector-valued processes and, if so, what properties the resulting CSs inherit from universal gambling strategies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequences of K-dimensional observations in three forms: one-hot categorical vectors (vertices of the simplex), probability vectors (interior points of the simplex), or general bounded vectors in [0,1]^{K-1} (handled via a simple affine reduction to the simplex). It produces a sequence of convex confidence sets C(Y^t; δ) ⊂ Δ^{K-1} that contain the true mean vector μ at every time step simultaneously with probability at least 1−δ. The sets are defined implicitly through a wealth process inequality, and for the universal-portfolio variant the computation at round t requires maintaining O(t^{K-1}) statistics via dynamic programming over the composition set G_{K,t}.

## Method

The core construction generalizes the continuous two-horse race to a K-horse race (HR_K) and continuous K-horse race (CHR_K), where the odds vector at round t is defined as X_t = Y_t ⊘ m (element-wise division by the candidate mean m), making the gambling fair exactly when m = μ. For categorical observations, the paper applies the Krichevsky-Trofimov Dirichlet mixture (α = 1_K/2) over constant bettors, yielding a KT CS whose confidence set is characterized by a KL-divergence inequality with a (K−1)/(2t)·log t correction term. For probability-vector observations, it applies Cover's universal portfolio (Dirichlet mixture with α = 1_K/K), producing a UP CS that is always non-vacuous and contains the empirical mean with probability 1. A key theoretical result shows that when the framework is adapted to sampling without replacement via the standard WoR reduction, the resulting KT CS is provably pointwise tighter than the posterior-prior ratio martingale of Waudby-Smith & Ramdas (2020), with simulations confirming roughly 26% faster stopping times for rank-decision tasks.

## Results

The paper's headline theoretical result (Theorem 5) proves that the KT-based WoR confidence sequence is always at least as tight as the posterior-prior ratio (PPR) CS of Waudby-Smith & Ramdas (2020), with strict inequality holding for non-degenerate sequences. In the WoR simulation (1000 balls: 600 red, 250 green, 150 blue), the KT CS achieves a ~26% reduction in stopping time (the first time all color ranks are distinguishable) compared to PPR. For i.i.d. categorical observations (K∈{3,4,5}, T=100, δ=0.05), the multivariate KT(K) CS is substantially tighter than coordinate-wise baselines (KT(2) with mixture or Bonferroni aggregation), with the gap widening as dimension K increases. For probability-vector-valued (Dirichlet) observations, the UP(K) CS is markedly tighter than KT(K), especially when the Dirichlet distribution is concentrated.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the PPR CS (Waudby-Smith & Ramdas, 2020), the proposed KT WoR CS is provably tighter (Theorem 5) and empirically achieves ~26% faster stopping time in the urn simulation. Against the Sison-Glaz method (1995), the KT CS produces larger confidence sets but maintains the correct time-uniform coverage, whereas Sison-Glaz's coverage falls well below the nominal 1−δ level due to its reliance on asymptotic approximations. Against Clopper-Pearson intervals with Bonferroni correction (1934), CP(2)+Bonf yields consistently tighter sets with guaranteed pointwise coverage but lacks the time-uniform (anytime-valid) guarantee that the KT CS provides. Against the Mardia et al. (2020) improved non-time-uniform bound, the KT CS is almost always tighter in the small-sample regime and becomes comparable only after the Mardia bound's required sample size t=Ω(K³) is reached, at which point the Mardia bound removes the O((log t)/t) redundancy term that KT still carries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the coin-toss betting framework of Waudby-Smith & Ramdas (2024) and the two-horse race reformulation of Ryu & Bhatt (2022) from scalar-valued to vector-valued bounded processes by recognizing that the scalar case is a special case of a K-horse race on the probability simplex. It applies universal gambling strategies—specifically the Krichevsky-Trofimov mixture and Cover's universal portfolio (Cover, 1991; Orabona & Jun, 2024)—to the resulting multivariate horse race, yielding CSs for categorical and probability-vector-valued observations. The WoR extension builds directly on the general reduction of Waudby-Smith & Ramdas (2020), and the paper shows the resulting KT-based CS is provably tighter than the posterior-prior ratio martingale from that work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a clean, general recipe for constructing tight time-uniform confidence sets for bounded multivariate processes, with the provable tightness over the PPR CS in the WoR setting (Theorem 5) and the ~26% faster stopping time offering concrete practical gains for applications like election audits. The main limitation is the O(T^K) computational complexity of the UP-based CS, which is prohibitive for high-dimensional data; efficient approximation of universal portfolios remains a long-standing open problem in information theory. The paper also flags nontrivial technical barriers to extending the approach to kernel or linear bandit settings, leaving an important direction for future work.
