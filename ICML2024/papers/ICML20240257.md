## Problem

Existing diffusion-based adversarial defenses—purification (e.g., DiffPure) and adversarial training with diffusion-generated data—remain vulnerable to adaptive attacks or fail to generalize across unseen threat models because they ultimately rely on discriminative classifiers that cannot control predictions outside the training distribution. This paper asks whether a pre-trained diffusion model can itself serve as a robust generative classifier without any adversarial training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240257/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (e.g., 32×32 CIFAR-10) that may be adversarially perturbed. Before classification, the input is optimized for N steps under an ℓ∞ budget η to maximize its unconditional data likelihood, producing a purified image x̂. The diffusion classifier then computes per-class conditional diffusion losses (noise prediction errors averaged over all timesteps) to yield class probabilities via a softmax, from which the predicted label is extracted.

## Method

RDC converts a pre-trained class-conditional diffusion model into a generative classifier by applying Bayes' theorem: p(y|x) ∝ p_θ(x|y), where the conditional likelihood is approximated by the variational lower bound (i.e., the expected weighted noise-prediction loss over timesteps). Two key innovations reduce cost and improve robustness: (1) a multi-head diffusion UNet whose final convolutional layer outputs noise predictions for all K classes simultaneously, cutting NFEs from K×T to T; and (2) a Likelihood Maximization pre-step that minimizes the unconditional diffusion loss w.r.t. the input under an ℓ∞ constraint, moving adversarial examples into high-likelihood regions before classification. Theoretically, the paper proves the optimal diffusion classifier achieves 100% robust accuracy under ℓ∞ and ℓ2 threats, and empirically RDC reaches 75.67% robust accuracy on CIFAR-10 (ε∞=8/255), surpassing prior SOTA adversarial training by +4.77% and generalizing >30% better under unseen threats like StAdv.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (512 test images), RDC (LM+DC) achieves 75.67% robust accuracy under the ℓ∞ norm (ε∞=8/255) and 82.03% under the ℓ2 norm (ε2=0.5), with 89.45% under StAdv, yielding an average robust accuracy of 82.38%. This represents a +4.77% improvement over the previous state-of-the-art adversarial training method AT-EDM (70.90% under ℓ∞) and a +3.01% improvement over the best dynamic/randomized defenses. Under unseen threats (StAdv), RDC surpasses all baselines by more than 53.90 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDC outperforms the strongest adversarial training baseline AT-EDM-ℓ∞ (Wang et al., 2023b) by +4.77% under ℓ∞ (75.67% vs 70.90%) and by +12.30% under ℓ2 when compared to AT-EDM-ℓ∞ (82.03% vs 69.73%), though it trails AT-EDM-ℓ2 by 2.74% on the ℓ2 norm (82.03% vs 84.77%). Against diffusion-based purification (DiffPure, Nie et al., 2022), RDC achieves a massive +31.15% improvement under ℓ∞ (75.67% vs 44.53%) and +16.44% under StAdv (89.45% vs 12.89%). RDC dominates all generative classifier baselines: SBGC and HybViT achieve 0.00% robust accuracy under all threat models, while JEM (Grathwohl et al., 2019) reaches only 8.20% under ℓ∞. Against perceptual adversarial training (PAT-self, Laidlaw et al., 2021), RDC gains +28.60% under ℓ∞ and +49.80% under StAdv. The most striking result is under the unseen StAdv threat, where RDC (89.45%) surpasses the best baseline (PAT-self at 39.65%) by nearly 50 percentage points, demonstrating strong generalization across threat models without any threat-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of using diffusion models as auxiliary tools for adversarial robustness—either as purification preprocessors (DiffPure, Blau et al.) or as data generators for adversarial training (AT-DDPM, AT-EDM)—by instead converting a pre-trained conditional diffusion model directly into a generative classifier via Bayes' theorem. It builds on the DDPM/EDM variational lower bound framework (Ho et al., 2020; Karras et al., 2022) and the broader generative classifier literature (SBGC, JEM, HybViT), but is the first to use a diffusion model's conditional likelihood as the classification signal with a dedicated multi-head UNet architecture. The Likelihood Maximization pre-optimization step is positioned as a more principled alternative to DiffPure's stochastic denoising, directly minimizing the unconditional diffusion loss rather than relying on forward-reverse diffusion to remove perturbations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a defense that generalizes across unseen threat models without retraining, achieving >30% average robustness improvement over adversarial training baselines on CIFAR-10—a significant gap given that generalization across threat models has been a persistent weakness of AT-based methods. However, the large gap between the theoretically optimal classifier (100% robust accuracy) and the empirical one (35.94% for DC alone) highlights that the current bottleneck is the quality of the diffusion model's density estimation, not the classification framework itself. Open questions include scaling beyond CIFAR-10 (the N+T NFEs cost is prohibitive for ImageNet), closing the likelihood-estimation gap, and whether the approach extends to more complex tasks like object detection or segmentation. The multi-head diffusion's initial 60% accuracy and the need for distillation from a pre-trained model also suggest architectural limitations that may constrain adoption.
