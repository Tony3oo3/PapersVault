## Problem

Despite strong empirical performance of state-space models (SSMs) like S4 and LRU on long-range reasoning, the theoretical expressivity of architectures combining linear diagonal RNNs with position-wise MLPs remained largely unexplored. In particular, the role of complex eigenvalues and initialization near the unit circle—empirically critical for SSM success—lacked a principled explanation, and it was unclear whether linear recurrences alone (without non-linearities inside the recurrence) could achieve universality for non-linear causal sequence-to-sequence maps.

## Input / Output

The architecture takes a length-L sequence of M-dimensional real tokens and produces a length-L sequence of S-dimensional outputs via a causal sequence-to-sequence map. A key structural constraint is that the MLP must be position-independent: the same function g is applied at every timestamp, so it must implicitly interpolate between the L distinct maps T_1, …, T_L using timestamp information encoded in the hidden state. For reconstruction experiments, the input is a flattened MNIST image (784 tokens) or PathFinder data (1024 tokens), and the output is the reconstructed input sequence from the final hidden state.

## Method

The paper proves universality via a separation of concerns: the linear diagonal RNN acts as a lossless encoder of the input sequence into its hidden state (via Vandermonde matrix properties, requiring N ≥ dim(V) for unstructured inputs), while the MLP performs all non-linear processing on this encoding. Reconstruction from hidden state to input is exact when eigenvalues are distinct, but its numerical stability depends critically on the Vandermonde condition number—which grows exponentially for real eigenvalues but can be made exactly 1 with complex roots of unity, providing a precise justification for complex-valued recurrences. Using Barron's approximation theory, the paper derives finite-width guarantees for the MLP (D = O(L/ε²)) and shows the width bound is penalized by the condition number of the reconstruction map, linking initialization to approximation complexity. The time-independence of the MLP is handled via a novel combination-of-Barron-maps theorem that interpolates across timestamps using bump functions with controlled Fourier decay (Tlas, 2022).

## Results

The paper proves a universality theorem: a single diagonal linear RNN (width N ≥ dim(V)) followed by a position-wise 1HL-MLP (width D = O(L/ε²)) approximates any Barron-regular causal sequence-to-sequence map to error ε. Empirically, on flattened MNIST (784 tokens) and PathFinder (1024 tokens), reconstruction from the final RNN hidden state is nearly perfect at N=256 (the standard SSM width) and negligible at N=1024. Complex eigenvalues initialized near the unit circle (r_min=0.99) are critical: with r_min=0, only the most recent tokens are recoverable, while r_min=0.99 enables full-sequence reconstruction. The architecture also successfully approximates non-linear ODE flows (Protein Transduction, Lotka-Volterra, Lorenz) with a single RNN+MLP layer (N=128, D=256).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that validates its universality claims empirically rather than benchmarking against competing architectures. The key internal comparisons are: (1) real vs. complex eigenvalues — real diagonal RNNs suffer exponentially growing Vandermonde condition numbers, while complex eigenvalues (e.g., Nth roots of unity) achieve condition number exactly 1, providing a precise justification for complex recurrences in S4/LRU; (2) initialization radius — eigenvalues sampled in T[0.99, 1] enable full MNIST reconstruction at N=784, whereas T[0, 1] recovers only the last few hundred tokens; (3) linear vs. non-linear decoder — at large N (1024), a linear reconstruction head suffices, but at smaller N a 1HL-MLP decoder (2048 units) achieves surprisingly accurate reconstruction, suggesting the RNN hidden state retains information that linear maps cannot extract. The paper does not directly compare against S4, Mamba, or transformer baselines on LRA tasks, instead focusing on reconstruction and ODE-approximation experiments that validate the theoretical framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the universality analysis of sequence-to-sequence models (previously established for non-linear RNNs by Hanson & Raginsky, and for SSMs with product-of-RNNs architectures by Wang & Xue) to the specific and practically dominant architecture of a single linear diagonal RNN followed by a position-wise MLP. It departs from HiPPO theory by showing that structured matrices are unnecessary—random diagonal initialization on a complex ring suffices for lossless encoding—and from the Muntz-Szász/Wang-Xue line by replacing convolutional approximation with a Vandermonde-based perfect-reconstruction argument. The proof machinery draws on Barron's theorem for finite-width MLP guarantees and on Tlas (2022) for interpolating sequences of Barron functions into a single time-independent map.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical justification for two empirically critical design choices in S4/LRU: the use of complex eigenvalues and initialization near the unit circle. The Vandermonde conditioning analysis shows that real eigenvalues cause exponentially growing condition numbers (hence information loss), while complex eigenvalues can achieve condition number 1, directly linking the vanishing-gradient problem to reconstruction quality. Open questions include the finite-alphabet setting, finite-precision effects, moderate-width error bounds (the worst-case N ≥ L requirement is strong), and whether the framework extends to selective/gated SSMs like Mamba (partially addressed in a follow-up). The separation-of-concerns principle—RNN as lossless encoder, MLP as non-linear processor—offers a clean conceptual lens for future SSM architecture design.
