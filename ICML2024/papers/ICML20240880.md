## Problem

Existing federated optimization methods either depend on the smoothness constant L (offering no communication advantage over centralized gradient descent) or on the bounded Hessian dissimilarity δ_B, but none achieve provably good communication complexity with respect to the weaker averaged Hessian dissimilarity δ_A for both strongly-convex and general convex problems. The paper identifies that the historically foundational DANE algorithm already achieves δ_A-dependent communication reduction and builds on this insight to design methods that are simultaneously communication-efficient and computationally efficient.

## Input / Output

Each of n clients holds a local loss function f_i(x) and computes a control variate h_{i,r} = ∇f_i(x^r) − ∇f(x^r) at every communication round. The local subproblem for DANE+ is F_{i,r}(x) = f_i(x) − ⟨x, h_{i,r}⟩ + (λ/2)‖x − x^r‖², while FedRed adds a second proximal term (η/2)‖x − x_{i,k}‖² anchored to the client's own previous iterate. For FedRed-GD, the subproblem reduces to a single closed-form gradient step (a convex combination of the local and reference points plus a gradient update), so the per-iteration local cost is O(1) rather than requiring multiple gradient evaluations. The output is a sequence of global iterates x^r with deterministic or in-expectation convergence guarantees expressed in terms of δ_A or δ_B rather than L.

## Method

The paper's key insight is that DANE's combination of a linear drift-correction term and a proximal regularizer constitutes a 'regularized drift correction' mechanism that exploits Hessian similarity to reduce communication. DANE+ generalizes DANE by allowing inexact local solvers (with explicit accuracy conditions) and arbitrary averaging strategies (standard or randomized), yielding deterministic communication reduction of O(δ_A/μ) for strongly-convex and O(δ_A R₀²/ε) for convex problems. FedRed then introduces a second regularizer (η/2)‖x − x_{i,k}‖² centered at the client's own previous iterate, which makes the subproblem well-conditioned (condition number O(1)) so that a single gradient step suffices. Communication is triggered only with probability p ≈ δ_A/L (or δ_B/L for non-convex), so the algorithm skips most rounds while the reference point ẋ_k is updated only on communication events. This doubly regularized design achieves the same O(δ_A/μ) communication complexity as DANE+ but with O(1) local computation per iteration, matching the total gradient evaluations of vanilla GD while requiring L/δ_A times fewer communication rounds.

## Results

The paper establishes that DANE+ and FedRed achieve communication complexity depending on the Hessian dissimilarity constants δ_A (convex) or δ_B (non-convex) rather than the smoothness constant L. Specifically, FedRed-GD requires O(δ_A/μ · log(R_0²/ε)) communication rounds for strongly-convex problems and O(δ_B F_0/ε) for non-convex problems, while needing only O(L/δ_A) local steps per round — the same total gradient computations as vanilla GD. On synthetic quadratic data with L/δ_A ≈ 20, both DANE+-GD and FedRed-GD require roughly 20× fewer communication rounds than GD to reach the same suboptimality, with FedRed-GD matching GD's total computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Centralized GD, Scaffold, FedDyn, and InexactDane, DANE+ and FedRed replace the smoothness constant L in the communication complexity with the (typically much smaller) Hessian dissimilarity constants δ_A or δ_B, yielding a speedup factor of L/δ_A for convex problems. Against SVRP-GD, FedRed achieves O(δ_A/μ) communication rounds for strongly-convex problems versus SVRP's O(δ_A²/μ² + n), a significant improvement when δ_A/μ is small. For non-convex problems, FedRed-GD matches CE-LGD's O(δ_B F_0/ε) rate while requiring only L/δ_B local steps per round, outperforming MimeMVR which has a more complex rate involving additional variance terms. In the strongly-convex case, FedRed-GD strictly dominates DANE+-GD in local computation: FedRed-GD needs a fixed L/δ_A steps per round, while DANE+-GD requires O(L/(δ_A+μ) · log(L/(δ_A+μ)(r+1))) steps that grow with the round index r. Experimentally on four LIBSVM datasets with regularized logistic loss, both DANE+-GD and FedRed-GD consistently outperform Scaffold, Scaffnew, FedProx, and GD in convergence speed, confirming the theoretical communication reduction from implicit Hessian similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits DANE (Shamir et al., 2014), a foundational distributed optimization algorithm previously analyzed only for quadratic objectives, and shows it already achieves communication complexity depending on the averaged Hessian dissimilarity constant δ_A for general convex and non-convex functions. The authors identify 'regularized drift correction' as the unifying mechanism implicit in DANE and Scaffold's control variates, then extend it into DANE+ (supporting inexact solvers and arbitrary aggregation) and FedRed (adding a second proximal regularizer to reduce local computation to a single gradient step). Compared to SVRP (Khaled and Jin, 2023), which achieves O(δ_A²/μ² + n) for strongly-convex problems, FedRed improves the rate to O(δ_A/μ) while also covering general convex and non-convex settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that standard local gradient steps (one per round) suffice to achieve the same communication reduction as more expensive local solvers, provided the client functions share Hessian similarity. This validates the use of simple GD as a local solver in federated settings and gives practitioners a clear recipe: set the communication probability p ≈ δ_A/L and the stepsize η ≈ L. Open questions include extending the analysis to client sampling, inexact control variates, decentralized topologies, and accelerated variants, as well as validating the theory on deep learning workloads where Hessian similarity may or may not hold.
