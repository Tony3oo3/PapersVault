## Problem

Testing causal relationships between continuous variables in the presence of unobserved confounders or mediators remains an open problem. Existing proxy-variable-based testing procedures are either restricted to discrete variables or rely on strong parametric assumptions to establish asymptotic validity, leaving no non-parametric, continuous-variable solution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241288/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes continuous tabular observations of three variables: X (treatment/exposure), Y (outcome), and W (an observed proxy for the unobserved confounder/mediator U satisfying X ⊥ W | U). It outputs a binary test decision—reject or fail to reject the null hypothesis H₀: X ⊥ Y | U—based on a chi-squared test statistic with r = l_X − l_W degrees of freedom. The bin numbers for discretization are set heuristically to ⌊log(n)⌋, with the constraint l_X > l_W to ensure the design matrix has full row rank.

## Method

The procedure discretizes W uniformly into l_W bins, then constructs a non-uniform discretization of X into l_X bins (l_X > l_W) that guarantees P(W̃|X̃) has full row rank under a completeness assumption on the conditional distributions L_{U|X} and L_{W|X}. Under H₀, the discretized probability matrices satisfy a linear equation P(ỹ|X̃) = P(ỹ|Ũ)P(W̃|Ũ)⁻¹ P(W̃|X̃), and the discretization error is shown to vanish asymptotically via TV-smoothness and tightness conditions on the conditional distributions. The test statistic is the squared least-squares residue T = nξᵀξ from regressing the estimated response on the design matrix, which converges to χ²_r under H₀ (uniform asymptotic level α) and diverges under a local alternative where the integral equation p(y|x) = ∫h(y,w)p(w|x)dw has no solution (asymptotic power 1). No parametric family is assumed for any variable, making the procedure fully non-parametric.

## Results

On the MIMIC-III sepsis dataset (3,251 patients), the proposed method correctly identifies both causal relationships, matching the RCT golden standards: Vancomycin→WBC (causal, ✓) and Morphine→WBC (non-causal, ×). On synthetic data, the type I error rate approximates the significance level α=0.05, while KCI and ICM exhibit considerably higher false positive rates. The type II error rate approaches zero as sample size n increases, confirming the asymptotic power guarantee (Thm. 4.18).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: KCI (kernel-based CI test), ICM (integrated conditional moment test), and Miao's discrete proxy testing procedure. On the real-world sepsis task, the proposed method is the only one that matches both RCT golden standards, while KCI and ICM both produce a false positive for Morphine→WBC (failing to adjust for latent confounding bias), and Miao produces a false negative for Vancomycin→WBC (likely due to loss of proxy information under coarse discretization with l_X=3, l_W=2). On synthetic data, the proposed method achieves type I error closest to the nominal α=0.05 level, whereas KCI and ICM show substantially inflated type I error rates. Miao's procedure suffers from the largest type II error rate among all methods. The ICM baseline's elevated type I error is attributed to its parametric model limitations and estimation error, while KCI's failure stems from conditioning on the proxy W rather than the unobserved U.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the proxy-variable hypothesis-testing framework of Miao et al. (2018), which established a linear-regression-based test for discrete variables under a latent confounder, to the continuous-variable setting via a carefully designed discretization procedure. The key departure from Miao et al. (2022), which also targeted continuous variables, is the removal of parametric assumptions: here, completeness (Asm. 4.1) and TV-smoothness (Asm. 4.7) replace distributional family assumptions, and the discretization error is shown to vanish asymptotically. The proxy-variable framework and completeness condition are inherited directly from Kuroki & Pearl (2014), and the test statistic (squared least-squares residue) follows Miao et al. (2018), with the novelty lying in the discretization construction and its asymptotic analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the first non-parametric, continuous-variable proxy-based causal test with guaranteed asymptotic level and power, filling a gap between the discrete-only procedure (Miao 2018) and the parametric continuous procedure (Miao 2022). The sepsis application, where the method correctly identifies vancomycin→WBC as causal and morphine→WBC as spurious (matching RCTs) while KCI, ICM, and Miao's discrete procedure all fail, demonstrates practical utility in clinical settings. Key limitations include the bivariate scope (no multivariate extension yet), the curse-of-dimensionality concern for high-dimensional conditioning sets, and the reliance on a valid proxy variable satisfying completeness—conditions that may be difficult to verify in practice. Future work on plugging this test into multivariate algorithms like FCI is a natural next step.
