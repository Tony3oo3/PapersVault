## Problem

Existing offline RL methods explicitly delimit rigid boundaries for out-of-distribution (OOD) actions based on dataset support, which impedes data near these boundaries from acquiring realistic Q-value estimates. While prior work has shifted focus from suppressing OOD overestimation to avoiding overly conservative learning from suboptimal behavior policies, the rigid boundary demarcation remains a fundamental limitation that prevents the model from adaptively extracting generalization knowledge from empirical data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242082/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline dataset of (s, a, r, s') tuples collected from unknown behavior policies, with no online interaction required. Before training, state-action pairs are clustered into K strata via a Gaussian Mixture Model, and the dataset is split into train/validation subsets using stratified sampling to preserve distributional similarity. The output is a pair of neural networks (actor and critic) parameterized by meta-learned weights w, where the critic provides Q-value estimates and the actor produces the greedy policy, both trained to generalize across adversarially chosen distribution shifts rather than merely fitting the original dataset.

## Method

ADS formulates offline RL generalization as a bi-level min-max optimization: the outer problem adversarially selects the 'hardest' train/validation splitting (maximum distribution shift) while the inner problem meta-learns model parameters w that minimize the validation loss after a gradient step on the train-subset. The adversarial splitting is found by ranking all samples by a first-order Taylor approximation of the generalization loss, ℓ(w;D) − α⟨∇_w ℓ(w;D), A⟩, and selecting the top ζ|D| samples as the validation subset, with A updated as the mean gradient over the complementary train subset. This alternating procedure (update w via Equation 8, then re-split data via Problem 9) is embedded within standard actor-critic training, with the loss optionally blended with a CQL-style support penalty to handle truly OOD actions. The paper derives a generalization error bound showing ADS implicitly minimizes the non-constant terms of the bound, and proves convergence of the bi-level optimization under standard smoothness and bounded-gradient assumptions.

## Results

On the D4RL MuJoCo benchmarks (9 datasets), CQL+ADS achieves the best average normalized score of 92.3, followed by MCQ+ADS (90.5) and TD3+BC+ADS (85.6), all outperforming the strongest non-ADS baselines PRDC (90.1) and STR (87.7). The largest individual gains are on halfcheetah-medium (CQL+ADS: 73.9 vs. PRDC: 63.5) and walker2d-medium (MCQ+ADS: 94.5 vs. STR: 85.9). On AntMaze sparse-reward tasks, ADS-enhanced baselines show dramatic improvements over their unmodified versions (e.g., CQL+ADS: 95.0 vs. CQL: 82.6 on antmaze-umaze; TD3+BC+ADS: 90.0 vs. TD3+BC: 47.0 on antmaze-umaze-diverse), though they remain competitive rather than dominant against specialized methods like POR and PRDC on the harder large-scale AntMaze tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CQL+ADS (avg 92.3) outperforms all non-ADS baselines on average, beating PRDC (90.1) by 2.2 points and STR (87.7) by 4.6 points. MCQ+ADS (avg 90.5) is particularly strong on medium and medium-replay datasets, achieving the best scores on hopper-medium (103.0 vs. STR's 101.3) and walker2d-medium (94.5 vs. PRDC's 85.2). TD3+BC+ADS excels on expert datasets, reaching 114.0 on both hopper-medium-expert and walker2d-medium-expert, surpassing STR (111.9 and 110.2) and PRDC (109.2 and 111.2). Against IQL (79.2) and POR (80.1), all three ADS-enhanced methods show substantial margins of 5–13 points on average. The paper notes that the optimal ADS variant depends on data quality: MCQ+ADS is best for suboptimal behavior policies (medium/replay), while CQL+ADS and TD3+BC+ADS are preferable as expert data proportion increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of offline RL conservatism mitigation and meta-learning-based domain generalization. It departs from the dominant paradigm of explicitly delimiting OOD boundaries (support constraints in CQL, pseudo Q-values in MCQ, nearest-neighbor restrictions in PRDC) by instead using adversarial data splitting to implicitly train the model to generalize across distribution shifts. The bi-level min-max formulation directly borrows from MAML-style meta-learning (Li et al., 2018) and adversarial domain generalization (Volpi et al., 2018; Gu et al., 2024), but repurposes these tools for the specific challenge of offline RL's distribution shift between behavior and learned policies. The GMM-based stratified sampling for maintaining distributional similarity between train/validation subsets is a practical adaptation drawn from survey sampling literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ADS provides a plug-and-play framework that can be layered onto existing offline RL algorithms (CQL, TD3+BC, MCQ) without architectural changes, offering a principled alternative to hard OOD boundary enforcement. The theoretical generalization error bound connects the adversarial splitting objective to a minimizable upper bound, giving the method a clear optimization target. However, the framework struggles with sparse-reward tasks (AntMaze scores remain near zero for most baselines even with ADS), is limited to continuous control, and the stratified sampling/GMM preprocessing adds implementation complexity. Open questions include whether the approach extends to discrete action spaces, whether it composes with diffusion-based policy generation, and whether the adversarial splitting converges to a meaningful worst-case in high-dimensional state-action spaces.
