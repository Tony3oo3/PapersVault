## Problem

Current protein language models operate exclusively at the residue (amino acid) scale, which prevents them from capturing atom-level structural information and limits their applicability to tasks involving both proteins and small molecules. This forces practitioners to combine separate protein and molecule encoders, which the paper shows is suboptimal for protein-molecule interaction tasks. The paper asks whether a single model can be pretrained to operate natively at both residue and atom scales without sacrificing protein understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242541/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a code-switch sequence where a small fraction (~1%) of residues are randomly 'unzipped' into their constituent atoms with 3D coordinates, alongside the remaining intact residues; for pure molecules, the input is simply the ordered atom set with coordinates. The unzipped atoms retain their parent residue in the sequence, so the model sees both the residue token and its expanded atomic representation simultaneously. Outputs are unified representation embeddings that, after fine-tuning, yield binding affinity scores, binary affinity labels, secondary structure predictions, contact maps, or molecular property values depending on the downstream task.

## Method

ESM-AA introduces two key mechanisms for multi-scale unified molecular modeling. First, it constructs code-switch protein sequences by randomly unzipping partial residues into their constituent atoms (analogous to multilingual code-switching in NMT), then pretrains with two objectives: multi-scale masked language modeling (masking both residues and atoms) and pair-wise distance recovery (denoising corrupted atom coordinates within each residue). Second, it designs a multi-scale position encoding where the residue scale uses RoPE (reusing the parent residue's position for its unzipped atoms to avoid ambiguity) and the atom scale uses a Gaussian-kernel Euclidean distance matrix injected as a bias term in self-attention. The model is a 35M-parameter 12-layer Transformer initialized from an ESM-2 checkpoint, pretrained on a mixture of 8M AlphaFold DB proteins and 19M molecules, enabling it to process both proteins and small molecules within a single unified representation space.

## Results

On the Enzyme-Substrate Affinity Regression (ESAR) task, ESM-AA with ProSmith fine-tuning achieves MSE=0.607, R²=0.560, and Pearson=0.752, outperforming the ESM-2+Uni-Mol baseline (MSE=0.642, R²=0.536, Pearson=0.733). On Enzyme-Substrate Pair Classification (ESPC), ESM-AA reaches 92.3% accuracy, MCC=0.797, and ROC-AUC=0.957. On Drug-Target Affinity Regression (DTAR), ESM-AA achieves MSE=0.196 and r2m=0.752, surpassing all prior methods including those using the much larger ESM-1b 650M (MSE=0.208, r2m=0.743). On DUD-E zero-shot virtual screening, ESM-AA attains 80.02% AUROC, exceeding Vina (71.70%) and Glide-SP (76.70%) but slightly trailing DrugCLIP (81.72%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESM-AA with ProSmith consistently outperforms the ESM-2+Uni-Mol dual-model baseline across all protein-molecule tasks: on ESAR it reduces MSE from 0.642 to 0.607 and improves R² from 0.536 to 0.560; on ESPC it gains 1.5% accuracy (92.3% vs 90.8%) and 0.043 MCC. On DTAR, ESM-AA (MSE=0.196, r2m=0.752) beats all prior methods including Yuan et al. using ESM-1b 650M (MSE=0.208, r2m=0.743), though Yuan et al. retains a slight edge on CI (0.913 vs 0.903). On protein-only tasks (secondary structure prediction, contact prediction), ESM-AA performs on par with ESM-2 35M, confirming that unified molecular modeling does not sacrifice protein understanding. On molecular property tasks (MoleculeNet), ESM-AA is comparable to Uni-Mol 48M, e.g., BACE 83.5% vs 83.2% AUC, and outperforms GROVER, PretrainGNN, and other molecule-specific models. On DUD-E virtual screening, ESM-AA (80.02% AUROC) surpasses Vina, Glide-SP, and all scoring functions but is slightly below DrugCLIP (81.72%), which benefited from 3.2M additional pocket pre-training data. On protein function annotation, ESM-AA exceeds ESM-2 35M on all four GO/EC tasks and approaches GearNet, a structure-based model, despite using only sequence input.

## Relation to Prior Work

ESM-AA extends the ESM-2 protein language model by borrowing the code-switching concept from multilingual NLP (Yang et al., 2020; Li et al., 2022a) to create hybrid residue-atom sequences, and adapts Uni-Mol's 3D distance-based position encoding for the atom scale. Unlike Uni-Mol, which models proteins entirely at atom scale and is limited to local pockets, ESM-AA only partially unzips residues (1% by default), enabling efficient whole-protein processing. The multi-scale position encoding—combining RoPE (inherited from ESM-2) for residue-scale relationships with a Gaussian distance matrix for atom-scale relationships—is a novel architectural design that avoids the incompatible vocabularies problem at the two scales. The paper positions itself against ProSmith's separate-encoder paradigm, arguing that a single unified model produces more aligned representations than fusing two independently pre-trained models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that a single transformer can serve as both a protein and a molecule encoder, eliminating the need for separate pre-training pipelines and producing more cohesive joint representations for protein-molecule interaction tasks. A notable limitation is that scaling from 35M to 150M parameters actually degrades protein-molecule task performance, revealing that the current 3D molecular representation learning paradigm (shared with Uni-Mol) has a capacity ceiling that constrains the unified model's scaling behavior. Open questions include whether richer molecular pre-training objectives or larger molecular datasets could break this bottleneck, and whether the code-switching approach generalizes to other multi-scale biological systems (e.g., nucleic acids, glycoproteins).
