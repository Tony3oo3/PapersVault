## Problem

Polar codes achieve Shannon capacity asymptotically but suffer from suboptimal finite-length performance, and large-kernel polar codes that could improve this are difficult to design and decode. Naive neural network parameterization of channel codes fails to generalize to unseen messages, performing worse than even repetition codes. The paper asks how to automate the search for good non-linear channel codes by combining the structured redundancy of polarization-driven architectures with the expressive power of neural networks.

## Input / Output

The encoder takes k binary message bits (e.g., 37) and produces an n-length real-valued codeword (e.g., 256) under a power constraint ||x||² = n, jointly performing coding and BPSK modulation. The decoder receives the noisy real-valued channel output (corrupted by AWGN, Rayleigh fading, or bursty noise) and sequentially estimates the k binary message bits. A binary variant (DEEPPOLAR-binary) constrains codeword symbols to {−1, 1} via a straight-through estimator, trading some BER for practical symbol-level power constraints.

## Method

DEEPPOLAR generalizes the Polar code's Plotkin tree by replacing each XOR operation with a 2-input neural network g_{d,b} and expanding the kernel from 2×2 to ℓ×ℓ (with ℓ = √n empirically optimal), creating a neural Plotkin tree that explores a vastly larger function space while retaining the structured redundancy inductive bias. The matching DEEPPOLAR-SC decoder mirrors the encoder tree, with each kernel decoded by ℓ sequential sub-networks that process bits in successive-cancellation order, conditioned on previously decoded bits. Training uses a two-stage curriculum: first, kernels are pretrained progressively on DEEPPOLAR(ℓ, j, ℓ) subcodes for j = 1→k; second, the full encoder and decoder are initialized with these pretrained kernels and jointly trained via alternating optimization with BCE loss. Skip connections feed the linear polar-encoded features into each neural kernel, aiding learning of the non-linear residual. This architecture scales across rates and block lengths (unlike KO codes limited to RM-compatible rates) and generalizes to unseen messages due to the polarization structure.

## Results

DEEPPOLAR (n=256, k=37, ℓ=16) achieves lower BER than KO(8,2), Polar(256,37) with SC decoding, and Reed-Muller codes across a broad SNR range on AWGN channels, with the largest gains at moderate-to-high SNR. The optimal kernel size is found to be ℓ=√n (i.e., ℓ=16 for n=256), yielding a depth-2 encoding/decoding tree; further scaling to ℓ=64 degrades performance due to a bias-variance tradeoff. On non-AWGN channels, DEEPPOLAR trained solely on AWGN retains up to 0.3 dB BER gain over Polar(256,37) on Rayleigh fast-fading and 0.25 dB on bursty noise channels without retraining. However, DEEPPOLAR underperforms baselines on Block Error Rate (BLER), a consequence of the BCE training objective acting as a BER surrogate rather than a BLER surrogate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KO(8,2) (Makkuva et al., 2021), the state-of-the-art neural code at (256,37), DEEPPOLAR (ℓ=16) achieves consistently lower BER across the SNR range, and DEEPPOLAR-binary (binarized via STE) closely matches KO codes while surpassing Polar. Against conventional Polar(256,37) with SC decoding, DEEPPOLAR wins on BER at all tested rates (k=28, 37, 64), whereas KO codes do not exist for k=28 or k=64 due to their Reed-Muller-based construction. Against the optimized large-kernel Polar code of Trofimiuk & Trifonov (ℓ=16, SC decoding), DEEPPOLAR marginally falls short, indicating that the learned non-linear kernel has not yet fully exploited the large-kernel design space. On BLER, DEEPPOLAR underperforms both Polar and KO codes, with errors more uniformly distributed across bit positions rather than concentrated at position 0 as in SC decoding. The DEEPPOLAR-SC decoder, when applied to a fixed Polar encoder, matches the performance of the standard SC decoder, confirming that the BER gains originate from the non-linear encoder rather than the decoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEEPPOLAR extends the KO codes framework (Makkuva et al., 2021), which replaced selected Reed-Muller components with neural networks, by generalizing from the RM code family to the broader Polar code family and simultaneously expanding the kernel size beyond 2×2. It builds on the theoretical result of Korada et al. (2010) that large non-singular kernels also induce polarization, and adapts the curriculum-based training strategy from CRISP (Hebbar et al., 2023) to the kernel-level pretraining of the neural Plotkin tree. Unlike KO codes, which are architecturally constrained to RM structures and scale only to (64,7), DEEPPOLAR inherits the rate flexibility of Polar codes while introducing non-linearity at every kernel node.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that automating the search for large-kernel polar codes via neural networks is viable and yields codes that outperform both classical Polar/RM codes and prior neural codes (KO) across multiple rates and block lengths. Key open questions include: (1) finding a stable differentiable surrogate for BLER optimization (current BCE loss trades off BLER for BER), (2) reducing the 1.6M-parameter decoder complexity for practical deployment, and (3) jointly optimizing frozen-position selection with the learned encoder. The finding that DEEPPOLAR's pairwise distance spectrum closely matches the Gaussian codebook provides a useful interpretive lens for understanding why these structured non-linear codes work.
