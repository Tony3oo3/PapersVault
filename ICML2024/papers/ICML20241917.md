## Problem

Online anomaly detection requires calibrating a threshold to convert continuous anomaly scores into binary decisions, but existing approaches rely on fixed cold-start periods and static thresholds that degrade under distribution shifts. No prior method provides distribution-free statistical guarantees on false positive/negative rates while simultaneously adapting to non-stationarity and leveraging potentially useful offline data without incurring unbounded degradation when that data is irrelevant.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241917/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a streaming sequence of real-valued anomaly scores S_t, a known quantile level p ∈ (0,1), a confidence parameter α, and optionally K pre-partitioned offline datasets of unlabeled samples. At each time step it emits a ternary label: anomalous (score exceeds upper CS bound), benign (score below lower CS bound), or abstain (score falls within the confidence interval). The abstain option is a first-class output rather than a failure mode, and the algorithm does not require knowledge of the underlying distributions, change-point locations, or whether offline data is relevant.

## Method

The core mechanism constructs confidence sequences (CS) around the pth quantile of the anomaly score distribution using the empirical-quantile-based CS of Howard and Ramdas (2022). A sample is flagged anomalous if it exceeds the upper CS bound, benign if below the lower bound, and abstained otherwise. For distribution shifts, the algorithm runs a backward-CS intersection test (Shekhar and Ramdas, 2023) on the current segment; when the forward and backward CS become disjoint, a change-point is declared and the CS is restarted. For offline data, the algorithm checks whether the CS of each offline dataset intersects the online CS: if exactly one matches, the combined sample pool yields a tighter interval and fewer abstains; if none match, the algorithm falls back to online-only with bounded additive degradation. A key theoretical contribution is a lower-bound proof showing that any non-abstaining scheme incurs Ω(√T) mistakes even on i.i.d. streams, establishing abstention as necessary for constant-error guarantees.

## Results

On synthetic streams (2000 samples, p=0.99, α=0.001), the proposed method achieves 0 FP+FN with 12.1% abstains in the stationary no-offline setting, and 5.2±1.3 FP+FN with 32.7% abstains under distribution shift, compared to 195±71 and 225±95 FP+FN for τ^30% and DSpot respectively. On MNIST one-class anomaly detection (1000-sample streams, p=0.99, α=0.01), NN+A5 achieves 2.1±0.2 FP+FN with 89±6 abstains in the stationary setting and 8.8±2.3 FP+FN with 210±8 abstains under shift, versus 12.7±0.9 and 91±8.0 FP+FN for the static τ^30% baseline. Incorporating relevant offline data reduces abstains from 12.1% to 9.3% (stationary) and from 32.7% to 18.9% (with shift) without increasing mistakes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method dramatically outperforms DSpot (Hundman et al., 2018) on FP+FN across all settings: under distribution shift without offline data, the method achieves 5.2±1.3 mistakes versus 225±95 for DSpot (a ~43× reduction), and with offline data, 2.0±1.5 versus 173±64 (~87× reduction). On MNIST, NN+A5 achieves 8.8±2.3 FP+FN under shift compared to DSpot's 129.5±10.3 (~15× fewer mistakes), while incurring moderate abstains (210 vs. DSpot's fixed 150). The static τ^30% baseline performs comparably to DSpot on mistakes but with higher variance, and both are far worse than the proposed method. The EQ baseline achieves zero abstains but incurs 219±71 FP+FN under shift (no offline), roughly 42× more mistakes than the proposed method. The key trade-off is that the proposed method accepts a modest abstain rate (12–33%) in exchange for near-zero or very low mistake counts, whereas baselines either abstain at a fixed rate (τ^30%, DSpot) or not at all (EQ) but suffer large error rates under distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the confidence sequence framework for quantile estimation (Howard and Ramdas, 2022) and sequential change-point detection via backward confidence sequences (Shekhar and Ramdas, 2023), combining them into a unified online thresholding algorithm for anomaly detection. It departs from prior dynamic thresholding approaches (e.g., DSpot, static cold-start methods) by providing the first distribution-free statistical guarantees on false positives, false negatives, and abstains simultaneously, while also handling distribution shifts and optional offline warm-start data. The paper positions itself as the first algorithm satisfying all five desiderata (high accuracy, low abstention, adaptivity, offline data learning, distribution-freeness) in a single framework, whereas prior work addressed these properties in isolation or under parametric assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first systematic theoretical foundation for online adaptive anomaly thresholding, showing that abstaining is fundamentally necessary for high accuracy (a lower bound result) and that confidence sequences can yield distribution-free guarantees that adapt to problem complexity. The practical impact is significant for security and infrastructure monitoring systems where alert fatigue and missed detections carry real costs. Key limitations include the requirement for temporally independent samples, sufficiently separated change-points (Assumption 5.1), and the exclusion of gradual drifts—open problems the authors explicitly flag as requiring new techniques. The bounded-degradation guarantee for irrelevant offline data is a useful safety property for deployment, but the practical utility depends on the well-separated offline dataset assumption holding in real systems.
