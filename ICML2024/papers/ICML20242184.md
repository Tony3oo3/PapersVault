## Problem

Diffusion guidance (classifier-based or classifier-free) is widely used to steer sample generation toward desired properties, and empirical observations consistently show that stronger guidance improves classification confidence while reducing sample diversity. However, no rigorous theoretical guarantees existed for this trade-off. This paper provides the first theoretical analysis of how guidance strength affects both classification confidence and distribution diversity, in the tractable setting of Gaussian mixture models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242184/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a GMM data distribution with shared covariance, a target class label y, a guidance strength parameter η ≥ 0, and initial noise drawn from a standard Gaussian. It produces generated samples from the conditional distribution, accompanied by theoretical guarantees: explicit lower bounds on the posterior probability of the target class (classification confidence) and an upper bound on the differential entropy of the output relative to the unguided process. The analysis applies to both DDPM (SDE) and DDIM (ODE) samplers in continuous and discrete time, leveraging exact closed-form score functions and classifier probabilities that are available for GMMs.

## Method

The core technique combines ODE/SDE comparison theorems with the Fokker-Planck equation. For classification confidence, the authors project the guided and unguided dynamics onto mean-vector differences, derive differential inequalities showing the guided process maintains a strictly larger projection, and invoke comparison theorems to prove the posterior probability of the target class is always higher under guidance, with convergence to 1 at rate 1−O(η⁻¹ log η). For diversity reduction, they apply the Fokker-Planck equation to track entropy evolution, showing the guidance term introduces a non-positive Hessian contribution that monotonically decreases differential entropy along the entire reverse process. The analysis also identifies a negative example: for a three-component aligned GMM, strong guidance combined with discretization induces a phase transition where the center component vanishes and samples are pushed toward side centers.

## Results

The paper establishes that for Gaussian mixture models, diffusion guidance (both classifier and classifier-free) monotonically increases classification confidence along the entire reverse trajectory: P(x_t, y) ≥ P(z_t, y) for all t ∈ [0,T] (Theorems 3.3, 3.7). Quantitatively, the classification confidence converges to 1 as guidance strength η→∞, with a rate of at least 1 − O(η⁻¹ log η) for the ODE (DDIM) sampler and 1 − O(η^{−e^{−T}}(log η)^{2e^{−T}}) for the SDE (DDPM) sampler (Theorems 3.6, 3.8). For distribution diversity, the differential entropy of the guided output is always bounded above by that of the unguided output: H₀(t) ≥ H(t) for all t (Theorem 4.2), providing the first theoretical explanation for the observed diversity reduction. A negative result (Proposition 5.3) reveals a phase transition in a three-component aligned GMM where strong guidance causes the center component to vanish under discretization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between guided and unguided diffusion samplers (DDIM/ODE and DDPM/SDE) on Gaussian mixture models, rather than against competing generative methods. For the ODE reverse process, the guided process strictly dominates the unguided process in classification confidence at every time step under mild conditions on component center correlations (Assumption 3.1), and the entropy gap H₀(t) − H(t) ≥ 0 holds without any assumption on component centers (Theorem 4.2). For the SDE reverse process, the same confidence ordering holds almost surely, but the entropy comparison is not established due to technical limitations. In the two-component GMM special case, the center-correlation assumption can be dropped entirely (Theorems 3.9–3.10). The discretized DDIM sampler preserves both the confidence boost and entropy reduction guarantees, though the confidence gain is attenuated by large step sizes (Theorem 5.1). A notable negative result shows that for a three-component aligned GMM (violating Assumption 3.1), strong guidance combined with discretization causes a phase transition where the center component splits into two side clusters, indicating that guidance strength must be chosen carefully in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the diffusion model framework of DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) and DDIM (Song et al., 2020), and the two guidance mechanisms introduced by Dhariwal & Nichol (2021) for classifier guidance and Ho & Salimans (2022) for classifier-free guidance. While prior work established these guidance techniques empirically and demonstrated their effect on FID/IS trade-offs, no prior work provided a rigorous theoretical characterization of how guidance strength affects classification confidence or distribution diversity. The paper's analytical toolkit draws on ODE/SDE comparison theorems (McNabb, 1986; Zhu, 2010) and the Fokker-Planck equation, applied here in a novel combination to the score-based diffusion setting restricted to Gaussian mixture models where closed-form score functions are available.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical justification for the empirically observed trade-off between sample quality and diversity under diffusion guidance, providing practitioners with rigorous guarantees that guidance monotonically increases posterior confidence and decreases differential entropy. The negative result for aligned three-component GMMs—where strong guidance causes a phase transition that eliminates the center component—highlights a practical caution: guidance strength selection is not always straightforward and depends on the geometric configuration of the data. Key limitations include the restriction to GMMs with shared covariance, the absence of an entropy reduction guarantee for the SDE (DDPM) sampler, and the acknowledged looseness of the quantitative bounds, all of which are flagged as open problems for future work.
