## Problem

Out-of-distribution generalization for regression under covariate shift remains relatively unexplored compared to the classification setting. This paper identifies a concrete mechanism—Spectral Inflation, where eigenvectors with small training variance exhibit large test-time variance—that explains why the closed-form OLS solution fails dramatically under covariate shift, and proposes a principled adaptation method to mitigate this failure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240507/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

SpAR operates on the final-layer representations of a pre-trained neural regressor: it takes the labeled training representation matrix X with targets Y_X, the unlabeled target representation matrix Z from the shifted distribution, and a scalar rejection confidence α (fixed at 0.999 in all experiments). It outputs a single adapted weight vector w_proj that replaces the last linear layer, producing continuous predictions. The adaptation is a one-time post-processing step requiring only two SVDs (on X and Z) and a projection, making it far cheaper than retraining or per-batch regularizers used by in-processing baselines.

## Method

The paper first derives a closed-form decomposition of the OLS OOD risk as a double sum over the eigenspectra of X^T X and Z^T Z, revealing that loss is driven by eigenvectors where the target distribution inflates variance relative to the source (Spectral Inflation). For a projected regressor w_proj = w_hat − Σ⟨w_hat, e⟩e over a selected set S of target eigenvectors, the expected loss decomposes into per-eigenvector bias and variance terms. SpAR selects S by comparing an estimated bias (using ⟨w_hat, e_{z,j}⟩²λ_{z,j}²) against the analytically computable variance term, projecting out eigenvectors whose estimated bias falls below a chi-squared quantile threshold scaled by the variance. This yields a regressor that provably minimizes OOD loss among all projection-based estimators (Theorem 3.3), and in practice requires only a single forward pass to collect representations plus two polynomial-time SVDs, with no gradient updates or additional training epochs.

## Results

On PovertyMap-WILDS (the primary real-world benchmark), C-Mixup+SpAR achieves the best worst-group Pearson r of 0.52±0.08 and ties for best all-group r of 0.79±0.04, outperforming all in-processing baselines. On synthetic 2D data, SpAR reduces OLS squared error from 2.5e6 to 2.8e0 (a ~900,000× improvement) when the true labeling vector aligns with the non-inflated direction. On tabular datasets (SkillCraft, CommunitiesAndCrime), SpAR consistently improves worst-group RMSE over both ERM and C-Mixup baselines, e.g., reducing SkillCraft worst-group RMSE from 8.93 to 8.32 (ERM+SpAR) and from 8.71 to 8.34 (C-Mixup+SpAR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpAR (as a post-processing step) outperforms all in-processing baselines on worst-group Pearson r on PovertyMap: C-Mixup+SpAR (0.52) beats DANN (0.33), Deep CORAL (0.36), AFN (0.39), RSD (0.44), and DARE-GRAM (0.44) by 8–19 points. Against pre-processing baselines, SpAR also wins: C-Mixup+SpAR (0.52) surpasses Noisy Student (0.42) and SwAV (0.45) on worst-group while matching their all-group performance. On the synthetic 2D experiments, SpAR matches PCR when Spectral Inflation is present (Experiments 1–3) but dramatically outperforms PCR when no shift exists (Experiment 4: SpAR 1.7e0 vs. PCR 8.0e2), demonstrating its adaptive nature. On tabular data, SpAR consistently improves worst-group RMSE over both ERM and C-Mixup (e.g., CommunitiesAndCrime: 0.161 vs. 0.166 for ERM, 0.162 for C-Mixup), while the OLS pseudoinverse replacement (ERM+OLS) degrades performance, confirming that the spectral projection is essential. On image datasets (RCF-MNIST, ChairAngles-Tails), SpAR improves every baseline it is applied to, with the largest gain on ChairAngles-Tails where Deep CORAL+SpAR reduces RMSE from 5.978 to 5.839.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spectral analysis of OLS regression under covariate shift initiated by Tripuraneni et al. (2021) and Lei et al. (2021), who derived similar decompositions of OLS test loss, by introducing a per-eigenvector bias-variance decomposition and translating it into a practical post-processing algorithm (SpAR). It departs from the dominant in-processing domain adaptation literature (DANN, Deep CORAL, DARE-GRAM) by operating entirely in the spectral domain after training, requiring only a single SVD and projection rather than additional training iterations. The method also complements C-Mixup (Yao et al., 2022), a data-augmentation approach for regression under covariate shift, which the paper shows can be combined with SpAR for further gains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled, eigenspectrum-based explanation for why OLS (and by extension, the final linear layer of a DNN regressor) fails under covariate shift, naming the mechanism 'Spectral Inflation' and showing it empirically in DNN representations. SpAR's practical appeal lies in its extreme lightness—a one-time SVD and projection—making it a drop-in post-processing step that avoids the computational overhead of in-processing regularizers. Key limitations include the assumption of pure covariate shift (not concept shift), the need for unlabeled target data, and the open question of how to better exploit the target eigenvalues λ_{z,j}² or adaptively select the threshold α. Whether Spectral Inflation generalizes beyond covariate shift to other distribution shift types remains an important open question.
