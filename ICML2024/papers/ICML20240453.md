## Problem

Existing time series pre-training methods based on masked modeling or contrastive learning fail to adequately capture the intrinsic temporal correlations that are the most vital information in time series data. Random masking distorts temporal structure, while instance-level contrastive learning neglects fine-grained temporal variations, leaving a gap in how encoders are optimized to model time-dependent relationships across different timestamps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240453/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multivariate time series (T timestamps × C variables) and during pre-training samples pairs of subseries at different temporal distances from the same series, applying channel-wise masking (default 25%) to the current subseries. During fine-tuning, it produces either multi-horizon forecasts (96–720 steps ahead) or class labels. A distinctive feature is the fine-tuning interface: the same input series is processed through multiple lineage embeddings to generate diverse representations that are either averaged (fixed-length input) or concatenated (extended-length input), allowing the model to natively handle inputs longer than the pre-training window.

## Method

TimeSiam samples Siamese subseries pairs (past x^past and current x^curr) at random temporal distances from the same series, then applies masking augmentation to the current subseries to create a past-to-current reconstruction task. Learnable lineage embeddings (N embeddings of dimension D) are dynamically matched to the temporal distance d between the pair, enabling the encoder to learn diverse time-dependent representations for different correlation scales. Shared-weight Siamese encoders (PatchTST or iTransformer) process both subseries, and a decoder with cross-attention (current as query, past as key/value) followed by self-attention and FFN reconstructs the masked current subseries under an L2 loss. At fine-tuning, the Fixed-Input-Multiple-Lineages paradigm averages encoder outputs across all lineage embeddings for a single input, while Extended-Input-Multiple-Lineages assigns each segment of a longer input its corresponding lineage embedding and concatenates the results, preserving chronological order.

## Results

On in-domain forecasting (Table 2), TimeSiam achieves the best average MSE across all 8 benchmarks with both PatchTST (e.g., 0.429 on ETTh1, 0.189 on ECL, 0.453 on Traffic) and iTransformer (e.g., 0.440 on ETTh1, 0.175 on ECL, 0.420 on Traffic), yielding average MSE reductions of 5.7% and 2.5% over random initialization respectively. On in-domain classification (Table 4), TimeSiam reaches 89.93% (AD), 90.67% (TDBrain), and 91.32% (PTB) accuracy, an 11.5% average improvement over random initialization. In cross-domain settings (Tables 3 and 5), TimeSiam pre-trained on TSLD-1G still outperforms all baselines, with some transfer results (e.g., TSLD-1G→ETTh1: 0.425) even surpassing in-domain pre-training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimeSiam achieves the best MSE on all 8 forecasting benchmarks and best accuracy on all 3 classification benchmarks, winning every head-to-head comparison. The strongest competitor is SimMTM (masked modeling), which is closest on most datasets (e.g., 0.440 vs. 0.429 on ETTh1, 0.192 vs. 0.189 on ECL with PatchTST) but is consistently outperformed by TimeSiam by 1–3% MSE. Among contrastive baselines, TF-C and TS2Vec are the strongest (e.g., TF-C: 0.453 on ETTh1, 0.362 on Exchange), yet they trail TimeSiam by 2–5% MSE on most datasets; CPC and TNC perform worst among contrastive methods, sometimes degrading below random initialization (e.g., CPC: 0.504 vs. 0.490 random on Traffic). For classification, COMET (84.50% on AD) and SimMTM (86.19% on AD) are the closest baselines, but TimeSiam leads by 3.7–5.4% accuracy across AD, TDBrain, and PTB. In cross-domain forecasting (TSLD-1G pre-training), TimeSiam extends its lead over SimMTM (e.g., 0.425 vs. 0.429 on ETTh1, 0.188 vs. 0.213 on ECL), confirming that the Siamese past-to-current reconstruction generalizes better under distribution shift than single-series masked modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

TimeSiam departs from the two dominant time series pre-training paradigms—masked modeling (TST, Ti-MAE, SimMTM) and contrastive learning (TS2Vec, CoST, TF-C)—by reframing the pre-training objective as a past-to-current reconstruction between temporally distanced subseries using shared-weight Siamese encoders. It repurposes the Siamese network architecture (originally from face verification and contrastive vision) for temporal correlation modeling, and borrows the cross-attention decoder from the Transformer literature to fuse past context into masked current subseries. The lineage embeddings mechanism is a novel addition that has no direct analogue in prior time series pre-training, drawing instead on the positional/segment embedding ideas from NLP and vision transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a recognized gap—prior methods model individual series in isolation and neglect inter-temporal correlations—and provides a simple, backbone-agnostic framework that consistently improves forecasting and classification across 13 benchmarks. The introduction of TSLD-500M and TSLD-1G, large-scale multi-domain pre-training corpora, is practically significant for a field that has lacked such resources. Open questions include whether the cross-domain advantage observed in forecasting (where TSLD-1G pre-training sometimes beats in-domain) extends to classification, and whether the lineage embedding mechanism generalizes to non-Transformer backbones or streaming/online settings.
