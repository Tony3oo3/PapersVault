## Problem

Existing unified MLLMs face a fundamental conflict: visual comprehension requires abstract, task-invariant visual tokens (a many-to-one image-to-token map), while visual generation requires complete, detail-preserving tokens (a one-to-one token-to-image map). Because prior architectures enforce equality between pre- and post-MLLM visual tokens, increasing training data for one objective consistently degrades the other, preventing simultaneous SOTA on both comprehension and generation benchmarks.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images (encoded into patch-level visual tokens via a frozen CLIP-ViT) and text prompts or interleaved image-text instructions, and produces either text outputs (captions, VQA answers) or images (text-to-image generation, instruction-based editing). A key structural detail is that the pre-MLLM morph-token sequence is fixed at 32 discrete tokens drawn from an 8,192-entry codebook, while the post-MLLM morph-token sequence can differ in length and content; at inference, shorter sequences are padded with special <Emp> tokens and longer ones are trimmed to 32. The final image is reconstructed by a VQ-GAN decoder operating on lower-level visual tokens produced by an auxiliary decoder-only transformer.

## Method

The core idea is to break the equality constraint between pre- and post-MLLM visual tokens: the encoder (a deconfounded Q-former with causal self-attention and slot-attention) abstracts CLIP-ViT patch features into 32 discrete morph-tokens that serve as visual prompts for comprehension, while the MLLM autoregressively generates a different set of visually complete post-MLLM morph-tokens for generation. A deconfounder dictionary initialized from a pre-trained ViT-VQGAN codebook is injected via a single-layer Q-former to remove spatially spurious correlations, making morph-tokens behave more like causal natural language. A separate decoder-only transformer then autoregressively expands post-MLLM morph-tokens into lower-level visual tokens for VQ-GAN pixel reconstruction, offloading high-frequency detail recovery from the MLLM. Training follows a 3-stage strategy: (1) vocabulary expansion on ~30M image-text pairs with autoregressive likelihood over concatenated morph-text sequences, (2) auto-encoding where comprehension loss (captioning) and generation loss (image reconstruction) are detached so they no longer conflict, and (3) instruction tuning on diverse interleaved vision-language tasks. The detachment is the critical mechanism: because the reconstruction loss operates on post-MLLM tokens rather than forcing them to equal pre-MLLM tokens, the two objectives can be optimized simultaneously without the many-to-one vs. one-to-one tension.

## Results

The 8B Morph-Token MLLM achieves new SOTA on multimodal comprehension benchmarks while retaining image generation capability: 124.0 CIDEr on NoCaps, 87.5 on Flickr30K, 56.8 on GQA, and 1477.7/389.3 on MME Perception/Cognition, surpassing both comprehension-only models (InstructBLIP, LLaVA) and unified MLLMs (Emu2-Chat 37B, Seed-LLaMA 8B, LaVIT 7B). On the DEMON in-context comprehension benchmark, it outperforms the previous SOTA VPG-C by 5.6% on MMC and 7.8% on KGQA. For image editing, it achieves L1 scores of 15.3 (EVR), 14.6 (MA5K), and 7.6 (MagicBrush), substantially outperforming other unified MLLMs and approaching or matching dedicated editing specialists like MGIE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against comprehension-only MLLMs, the 8B Morph-Token model outperforms InstructBLIP (11B) on NoCaps (124.0 vs 120.0), Flickr30K (87.5 vs 83.5), GQA (56.8 vs 47.9), and MME Cognition (389.3 vs 291.8), while LLaVA (13B) edges ahead only on MME Perception (1531.3 vs 1477.7). Among unified comprehension+generation MLLMs, it decisively beats Seed-LLaMA (8B) and LaVIT (7B) on every comprehension metric (e.g., GQA 56.8 vs 34.8/46.8; MME Cognition 389.3 vs 235.5/240.7) and also surpasses the much larger Emu2-Chat (37B) on NoCaps, Flickr30K, GQA, VSR, and MME Cognition. On the DEMON in-context benchmark, it outperforms the previous SOTA VPG-C across most categories (e.g., KGQA 56.4 vs 48.6, MMR 54.9 vs 50.3). For text-to-image generation, it achieves the highest CLIP similarity on all three splits (COCO Karpathy 70.6, COCO val30k 72.2, Flickr test 68.8), beating GILL, Emu, Emu2-Gen, Seed-LLaMA, and LaVIT. In image editing, it significantly outperforms all unified MLLMs (e.g., EVR L1 15.3 vs Emu2-Gen 22.8, Seed-LLaMA 28.4) and matches or exceeds dedicated editing specialists MGIE (EVR L1 15.3 vs 16.3) and InsPix2Pix (MagicBrush L1 7.6 vs 10.1), with the largest margins on L1 fidelity metrics where prior MLLMs struggle to preserve unchanged regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the autoregressive unified MLLM framework (Chameleon/CM3Leon, Emu, Seed-LLaMA, LaVIT) where images are tokenized and fed into an LLM for joint comprehension and generation. It departs from all prior MLLMs by relaxing the requirement that pre- and post-MLLM visual tokens must be identical, introducing 'morph-tokens' where the encoder produces abstract semantic tokens (via a deconfounded Q-former inspired by BLIP-2) while the MLLM outputs visually complete tokens for generation. The deconfounding mechanism draws on causal intervention theory and initializes a dictionary from ViT-VQGAN, while the decoder-only transformer for lower-level visual token generation is a novel component absent from prior token-based MLLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and resolves a fundamental tension in unified MLLMs: comprehension requires many-to-one visual abstraction while generation requires one-to-one visual preservation, and prior architectures forced both through the same token representation. The morph-token formulation with detached losses is a clean architectural principle likely to influence future unified MLLM designs. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video generation, whether the deconfounding is essential or merely helpful at scale, and whether the VQ-GAN bottleneck limits fidelity for high-resolution or fine-grained editing tasks. The reliance on a frozen VQ-GAN decoder also constrains the maximum achievable image quality relative to diffusion-based alternatives.
