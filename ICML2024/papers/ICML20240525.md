## Problem

Causal inference on networked treatment units faces violations of standard identification assumptions (ignorability, SUTVA) due to peer effects, yet no existing method can assess the local reliability of causal estimates without ground-truth counterfactuals. Moreover, despite the known role of graph geometry in driving network-induced endogeneities, no theoretical link between network curvature and the difficulty of causal parameter identification had been established.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240525/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a graph of treatment units with node features, binary treatment labels, and peer exposure values (average neighbor treatment), and produces estimated individual treatment effects (ITEs) per node. Uniquely, it also outputs per-node Ricci curvature scores that serve as a ground-truth-free reliability indicator for the causal estimates, and a one-time adjusted weighted adjacency matrix (via discrete Ricci flow) that preprocesses the graph before GNN-based estimation. The Ricci flow adjustment modifies only edge weights without altering the unweighted connectivity, making it a cost-efficient preprocessing step applicable to any GNN-based causal estimator.

## Method

The paper establishes a theoretical chain connecting Ricci curvature to causal identification: the Lott–Villani entropy–curvature inequality (Equation 2) links Ricci curvature to Boltzmann entropy, the Fluctuation Theorem links entropy to system robustness, and Bühlmann's distributional robustness framework identifies causal parameters as worst-case risk minimizers, bridging the two notions of robustness. The key result (Theorem 6.1, Corollary 6.3) shows that non-negative Ricci curvature implies a higher probability that the causal parameter is point-identified over a larger class of unobserved variables, using an entropy inequality from entropic causal inference (Kocaoglu et al., 2017) as the critical link. Practically, the proposed f-NetEst method applies discrete Ricci flow as a one-time preprocessing step, iteratively updating edge weights via w_vu^(i+1) = (1 − κ_vu)·d(v,u)^i to flatten the network and increase curvature on the majority of edges in sparse graphs. This adjusted weighted adjacency matrix is then fed into the NetEst GNN framework (graph convolutional encoder + adversarial regularizers + MSE estimator), yielding up to 52% relative PEHE reduction across nine empirical networks.

## Results

The proposed f-NetEst (NetEst with Ricci flow preprocessing) achieves the lowest PEHE on all nine datasets, with relative gains of up to 52% over NetEst (e.g., 0.033 vs. 0.069 on BlogCatalog, 0.127 vs. 0.165 on Cornell). NetEst itself substantially outperforms all non-GNN baselines (TARNet, CFR, T/X-Learner+RF) and the GNN-based NetDeconf across every dataset. The Ricci flow adjustment also reduces PEHE for other GNN-based meta-learners (T-Learner+GNN, X-Learner+GNN, NetDeconf) in most cases, confirming the geometric benefit is method-agnostic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

f-NetEst dominates all baselines on every dataset by a wide margin. Against NetEst (the direct predecessor without Ricci flow), f-NetEst achieves up to 52% relative PEHE reduction (BC: 0.033 vs. 0.069) and consistent 5–25% gains on the remaining datasets. Against the strongest non-GNN baselines (CFR, TARNet), f-NetEst is 10–25× better in PEHE (e.g., 0.033 vs. 0.895 on BC). Against the GNN-based NetDeconf, f-NetEst is roughly 20–30× better (e.g., 0.033 vs. 1.092 on BC). The Ricci flow adjustment also improves other GNN meta-learners: f-T-Learner+GNN beats T-Learner+GNN on 7/9 datasets (e.g., 3.268 vs. 4.178 on BC, 2.762 vs. 9.630 on Flickr), and f-X-Learner+GNN beats X-Learner+GNN on 6/9 datasets, confirming the geometric benefit transfers across architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges three previously disconnected research threads: causal inference on networked data (NetEst, NetDeconf), discrete Ricci curvature in geometric deep learning (Ollivier-Ricci, Forman-Ricci), and distributional robustness of causal parameters (Bühlmann's invariance framework). The key conceptual move is chaining together the known entropy-curvature inequality from optimal transport (Lott & Villani), the entropy-robustness link from system theory (Pouryahya et al.), and the entropy-based identifiability results from entropic causal inference (Kocaoglu et al., Compton et al.) to produce, for the first time, a formal theorem connecting graph geometry to causal identification difficulty. Methodologically, it repurposes the discrete Ricci flow (Jin et al.) as a one-time graph preprocessing step for GNN-based causal estimators, rather than as a standalone geometric tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical upshot is a data-free diagnostic: Ricci curvature of a node's neighborhood predicts the reliability of its causal estimate without needing ground-truth counterfactuals, which is a significant gap in current GNN-based causal inference. The theoretical framework also opens a path for other geometric tools (curvature-based regularization, spectral methods) to enter causal inference on networks. However, the Ricci flow adjustment is static (computed once, not updated during training), cannot target specific neighborhoods, and the authors note that reweighting edges requires careful interpretation in context. The theoretical assumptions (particularly Aii, requiring entropies of two datasets to be 'sufficiently close') are somewhat informal, and the 52% relative PEHE gain, while consistent across nine datasets, operates on already-small absolute error values from a strong baseline (NetEst).
