## Problem

Learning effective spatio-temporal representations remains a fundamental challenge in trajectory prediction, where agents interact both temporally and spatially. Existing data augmentation strategies (flipping, masking, noise addition) have not yielded significant improvements in representation quality. This paper identifies that no prior work has explored predicting spatial distortion in observed trajectories as a self-supervised pretext task to enhance the underlying representation learned by trajectory prediction models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240333/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes past observed 2D trajectory waypoints for multiple agents over a fixed observation window (e.g., 5 timestamps/2.0s for NBA, 9 timestamps for TrajNet++, 8 timestamps for ETH-UCY) and produces future trajectory waypoints over a prediction horizon (e.g., 10 timestamps/4.0s for NBA, 12 timestamps for TrajNet++ and ETH-UCY). During training, the input is duplicated into a clean view and a distorted view (clean waypoints plus scaled Gaussian noise with noise factor ω), and the model simultaneously outputs future trajectory predictions from both views and predicted distortion values for each view. At inference, only the clean view is fed through the model, so test-time cost matches the baseline.

## Method

SSWDP introduces two complementary modules on top of an existing trajectory prediction backbone (CVAE, Transformer, or GNN). The spatial consistency module creates a distorted view by adding ω-scaled Gaussian noise to the clean past trajectory, passes both views through a shared feature extractor (Θ_fe) and prediction network (Θ_sup), and enforces that predictions from both views remain close to ground truth via a joint supervised loss. The distortion prediction module adds a small MLP head (Θ_ss, 128→64 hidden units) that predicts the spatial relocation values present in each view—zeros for the clean view and the actual noise vector for the distorted view—using an MSE-based self-supervised loss. The total training objective is L_sup + λ·L_ss, jointly optimizing Θ_fe, Θ_sup, and Θ_ss. The key insight is that forcing the model to explicitly recover the perturbation it was given acts as a powerful pretext task that sharpens the learned spatio-temporal features, yielding 22–39% relative ADE/FDE improvements over baselines and substantially greater gains (61–83%) when tested in distorted environments.

## Results

On the NBA SportVU dataset (4.0s horizon), SSWDP+GroupNet achieves ADE 0.90 and FDE 1.14, improving over the GroupNet baseline (1.13/1.69) by 22.7%/38.9% relative reduction. On TrajNet++, SSWDP+AutoBot reaches ADE 0.091 and FDE 0.162, a 33.8%/36.4% improvement over the AutoBot baseline (0.128/0.234). On ETH-UCY, SSWDP+SSAGCN achieves average ADE 0.11 and FDE 0.19, a 16.60%/23.20% improvement over the SSAGCN baseline (0.13/0.24), and SSWDP+Graph-TERN achieves 0.22/0.33 average ADE/FDE, an 8.60%/14.00% improvement over Graph-TERN (0.24/0.38).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSWDP+GroupNet outperforms all 10 compared methods on the NBA dataset at every time horizon (1.0s–4.0s), with the largest gap over SSTGCNN (1.59/2.37) and PECNet (1.83/3.41) at 4.0s, and a 22.7%/38.9% relative improvement over the GroupNet baseline itself. On TrajNet++, SSWDP+AutoBot surpasses Traj-MAE (0.093/0.181) and the AutoBot baseline (0.128/0.234), achieving the best ADE (0.091) and FDE (0.162) among all listed methods. On ETH-UCY, SSWDP+SSAGCN achieves the best average ADE/FDE (0.11/0.19), beating SSAGCN (0.13/0.24), RMB (0.21/0.36), EqMotion (0.21/0.35), and TUTR (0.21/0.36); SSWDP+Graph-TERN (0.22/0.33) also improves over the Graph-TERN baseline (0.24/0.38) and matches or beats RMB, EqMotion, TUTR, and EigenTrajectory. In distorted environments, the advantage widens dramatically: SSWDP+AutoBot shows 76.8%/82.5% relative improvement over the AutoBot baseline on TrajNet++, and SSWDP+GroupNet shows 61.0%/36.1% improvement over GroupNet on NBA, indicating substantially greater robustness to input perturbation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of self-supervised representation learning (drawing from vision pretext tasks like jigsaw puzzles and rotation prediction) and trajectory prediction, proposing a novel pretext task—predicting spatial distortion in observed waypoints—that is orthogonal to the underlying prediction architecture. It explicitly departs from prior data augmentation strategies (flipping, masking, noise injection) that the authors demonstrate yield only marginal gains, arguing that the key is not the augmentation itself but the self-supervised task of predicting the distortion. The method is architecture-agnostic, integrated as a plug-in module on top of four distinct baselines spanning CVAE (GroupNet), Transformer (AutoBot), graph CNN (SSAGCN), and goal-oriented GNN (Graph-TERN) families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of SSWDP makes it immediately applicable to any existing trajectory prediction pipeline with minimal computational overhead (no change in inference time, ~1.25× training time), which is practically valuable for the field. The robustness results in distorted environments (76.8%/82.5% ADE/FDE improvement on TrajNet++) suggest that self-supervised distortion prediction may be a general mechanism for building noise-resilient trajectory models, an open question for autonomous driving and robotics where sensor noise is pervasive. Limitations include the need for per-dataset noise factor tuning, modest gains on ETH-UCY (8.6%/14% for the stronger baseline), and validation on only four architectures, leaving open whether the approach transfers to diffusion-based or language-conditioned predictors.
