## Problem

Disparities in foundation models—performance gaps, privacy violations, hallucinations, robustness failures, and safety concerns—are typically studied in isolation, with standard benchmarks performing only surface-level evaluations that fail to probe deeper representational sources of bias. This paper argues that these disparities are not independent but form an interconnected 'cascading disparity' phenomenon rooted in embedding/representation disparity, where the flattening of low-dimensional manifolds for marginalized groups propagates harm across all other dimensions.

## Input / Output

As a position paper, the work does not introduce a new model with specific I/O. It analyzes foundation models (LLMs, VLMs, diffusion models, voice assistants) trained on massive unfiltered datasets spanning text, RGB image, and audio modalities, and produces a unified theoretical framework identifying eight interlinked disparity categories, tracing their sources across the data-collection, training, and deployment lifecycle, and proposing four technical calls to action targeting the root representational cause.

## Method

The core contribution is a theoretical framework establishing embedding disparity as the root cause from which all other disparities cascade: insufficient manifold creation for marginalized groups forces memorization over generalization, which in turn drives privacy leakage, robustness fragility, hallucination, model multiplicity, and safety failures. The paper traces disparity origins through specific training choices—loss function design (next-token prediction vs. supervised), aggregation weighting, data ordering, batch composition, and learning rate—showing how each suppresses marginalized group representations. Four interconnected calls to action are proposed: (1) hierarchical manifold learning under mixtures of heterogeneous distributions rather than assuming a single long-tailed distribution, (2) new geometric metrics (Lipschitz continuity, manifold richness) to assess representation quality in embedding space beyond downstream task accuracy, (3) Mixture-of-Experts architectures with learned gating to dynamically allocate model capacity across distinct distributions, and (4) model-guided active learning to strategically target sparse regions in marginalized data distributions for collection.

## Results

This is a position paper and does not present quantitative experimental results. Instead, it provides a conceptual framework identifying eight interconnected disparity types (embedding/representation, performance, privacy, robustness, hallucination, model understanding, model multiplicity, and safety) and argues they form a cascading phenomenon rooted in representational disparity. The paper's main contribution is a taxonomy of disparity sources across the foundation model lifecycle (data collection, training, deployment) and four technical calls to action: training under mixtures of heterogeneous distributions, developing manifold-embedding-based fairness metrics, guiding capacity via Mixture-of-Experts models, and model-guided data collection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a position paper, this work does not compare its method against baselines or report empirical performance numbers. The authors contrast foundation models with traditional ML models conceptually (e.g., arguing that foundation models' self-supervised sequence-prediction objectives, massive scale, and emergent behaviors create qualitatively different disparity risks than task-specific supervised models). They also critique existing evaluation approaches such as StereoSet and standard fairness metrics (demographic parity, equalized odds) as insufficient for probing representation-level disparities in foundation models, but do not run head-to-head comparisons. The paper's 'comparisons' are entirely argumentative: it positions its cascading-disparity framework as a more holistic alternative to the prevailing practice of addressing each disparity type in isolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper departs from the dominant paradigm in fairness research that treats performance gaps, privacy leaks, robustness failures, and hallucination as independent problems to be addressed in isolation. It synthesizes threads from manifold learning theory, federated learning under heterogeneous distributions, and Hooker's (2021) argument that algorithmic discrimination extends beyond data to training choices (loss aggregation, batch composition, learning rate), but repositions them under a single causal claim: embedding/representation disparity is the root from which all other disparities cascade. The paper explicitly contrasts its framework with surface-level evaluation approaches like StereoSet and with the common simplifying assumption that marginalized data merely forms a long-tail distribution, arguing instead for a mixture-of-heterogeneous-distributions view with distinct low-dimensional manifolds per group.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cascading disparity framework, if validated, would redirect fairness research from patching individual downstream metrics toward fixing representation quality at the embedding level, with concrete technical directions (manifold-aware metrics, MoE-based capacity allocation, model-guided active data collection). However, as a position paper it offers no empirical validation of the central causal claim, and the calls to action—particularly hierarchical manifold learning at foundation-model scale and dynamic capacity allocation across distributions—remain speculative and computationally untested. Open questions include how to operationally define 'marginalized' distributions in a model-agnostic way, how to distinguish genuinely low-quality data from underrepresented but valid distributions, and whether the proposed metrics can scale beyond toy examples. The paper also explicitly defers sociotechnical dimensions (community engagement, monopoly effects, long-term snowballing of internet-scraped data) to future work, leaving a significant gap between the technical prescriptions and the systemic problem described.
