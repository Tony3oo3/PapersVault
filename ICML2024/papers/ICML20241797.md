## Problem

Multi-query reasoning methods like Tree of Thoughts require hundreds of LLM queries per problem to perform external tree search, incurring substantial cost, latency, and computational overhead. Single-query methods such as Chain-of-Thought avoid this but follow rigid linear paths that cannot explore alternative branches or backtrack when a subproblem proves infeasible. The paper asks whether algorithmic search (DFS/BFS) can be internalized into a single LLM generation through carefully structured in-context examples, eliminating the need for external search machinery.

## Input / Output

The method takes a problem statement (e.g., four numbers for Game of 24, crossword clues) plus 5 in-context examples structured as [PROBLEM, SEARCH PROCESS, SOLUTION], where the search process explicitly shows dead-end branches, backtracking, and subtree evaluations rather than just the final solution path. The output is a single continuous generation at temperature 0 that performs the entire search trajectory inline—numbered 'first operations' mark subtree roots, candidate viability is assessed in-context, and the model backtracks to alternative branches before emitting the final answer. Unlike ToT, which requires external code to maintain tree structure and parse separate query responses, AoT needs no coding and completes in 1–2 queries.

## Method

AoT embeds algorithmic search (primarily DFS with pruning, also BFS) directly into in-context examples, exploiting the LLM's innate generative recurrence to execute the full tree search within one unified generation sweep. The key mechanism is that in-context examples capture the complete search process—including failed branches, backtracking, and heuristic subtree selection—teaching the LLM to perform algorithmic exploration rather than linear step-by-step reasoning. Four design components structure the approach: dividing search into subtree explorations, generating solutions continuously without pauses (avoiding the Markov-property limitation of isolated token sampling), evaluating subproblem promise through in-context patterns instead of separate evaluation prompts, and backtracking guided by the algorithmic structure. Theoretically, AoT extends transformer expressivity from P (CoT) to NP, since the exponential intermediate tokens generated during search can simulate any Turing machine running in O(a^n) time. Notably, the LLM often surpasses the base algorithm's efficiency by injecting its own heuristics, visiting fewer nodes than a uniform DFS would.

## Results

On the Game of 24 benchmark, AoT achieves a 71% success rate with a single LLM query (5,450 prompt tokens, 998 completion tokens), outperforming ToT (69% success, ~109 queries, 13,900 prompt tokens) by a factor of over 100 in query count. On 5×5 Mini Crosswords, AoT reaches 52% word success with 2 queries versus ToT's 46.5% with >200 queries, while using 25× fewer total tokens. On dynamic programming tasks (Coin Change, Edit Distance), AoT achieves 96% and 90% respectively, substantially exceeding CoT (76%, 64%). Across models, AoT boosts GPT-4 from 7% (I/O) to 71%, Claude 3 from 6% to 68%, and Gemini 1.5 Pro from 6% to 55% on Game of 24.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AoT decisively outperforms standard I/O prompting (71% vs. 7.3%) and CoT (71% vs. 4.0%) on Game of 24, and similarly dominates on Mini Crosswords (52% vs. 14% I/O, 15.6% CoT-SC). Against CoT-SC, AoT achieves 71% with 1 query versus 9.0% with 100 queries on Game of 24, a ~8× success-rate improvement at 100× fewer queries. The most direct comparison is with ToT: AoT edges out ToT on success rate (71% vs. 69% on Game of 24; 52% vs. 46.5% on Mini Crosswords) while requiring only 1–2 queries instead of ~109–200+, and using roughly 2.5× fewer prompt tokens and 5.5× fewer completion tokens on Game of 24. On question-answering benchmarks (GSM8K, StrategyQA), AoT is competitive but does not clearly surpass ToT (89% vs. 90% on GSM8K; 84% vs. 83% on StrategyQA), suggesting the advantage is most pronounced on tasks requiring extensive search-space exploration rather than linear reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper internalizes the external tree-search mechanism of Tree of Thoughts (Yao et al., 2023) into a single LLM generation by using algorithmic search examples (DFS/BFS) as in-context demonstrations, shifting the in-context learning paradigm from [PROBLEM, SOLUTION] or [PROBLEM, STEPS] to [PROBLEM, SEARCH PROCESS, SOLUTION]. It departs from Chain-of-Thought (Wei et al., 2022b) by reconceptualizing the intermediate reasoning sequence not as a fixed linear path but as a dynamic, mutable search trajectory with backtracking and pruning. The work also draws on the System 1/System 2 cognitive framing, positioning AoT as a hybrid that combines the LLM's intuitive heuristics with the systematic exploration discipline of classical algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: AoT matches or exceeds ToT's performance (71% vs 69% on Game of 24, 52% vs 46.5% on Mini Crosswords) while reducing queries by over 100×, making complex reasoning feasible in latency-sensitive and cost-constrained settings. The finding that fine-tuning with AoT examples yields a 60% improvement versus only 8% for CoT suggests that explicit exploration structure, not just domain knowledge, is what LLMs lack. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond GPT-4-class models, how to make the algorithmic examples more token-efficient, and what the theoretical limits of this 'internalized search' paradigm are beyond the NP-class result.
