## Problem

Existing convergence analyses for Bures-Wasserstein (BW) gradient descent are either restricted to specific objectives (e.g., Gaussian barycenter) or require geodesic convexity, which is difficult to verify in BW geometry. No general global convergence guarantee existed for BW gradient descent under the more natural assumption of Euclidean strong convexity and smoothness, and the constrained setting with Euclidean convex sets had not been studied at all.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the space of n×n symmetric positive-definite matrices S_+^n, taking an objective function f that is Euclidean α-strongly convex, β-smooth, and L-Lipschitz, along with a Euclidean convex constraint set C (in practice, a BW ball W(Σ̃, ρ)). The output is the optimal covariance matrix Σ* with a linear convergence rate of the form (1 − ημ̃/16)^k, where the rate constant μ̃ depends on the condition number κ = λ_max/λ_min of the iterate sequence and the constraint radius ρ. A key practical contribution is a closed-form projection onto BW balls that requires only a singular value decomposition (to compute the optimal transport map T_{ΣΣ₀}), avoiding the semidefinite program that a direct Euclidean formulation would entail.

## Method

The core technique bridges Euclidean and BW geometry by showing that Euclidean strong convexity implies a Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL) inequality in BW geometry with coefficient proportional to λ_min(Σ), and that Euclidean smoothness plus Lipschitz continuity implies geodesic smoothness under BW distance via a key lemma bounding the discrepancy between Euclidean and BW directional derivatives. For the constrained case, the authors define a projected gradient g_C(Σ) = (1/η) log_Σ(Σ⁺) and verify both a PL-type inequality and a descent condition for g_C, where the descent proof hinges on a novel second-order perturbation bound for compositions of optimal transport maps (derived via Lyapunov equation analysis). The projection onto BW balls is solved in closed form by reducing the problem to a two-point Wasserstein barycenter with an explicit parameter γ determined by the distance to the ball boundary. The resulting linear convergence rate under a bounded-eigenvalue assumption (Assumption 4.5) is verified to hold for both constrained Gaussian barycenter and Wasserstein distributionally robust optimization (WDRO-MMSE) problems.

## Results

The paper evaluates projected BWGD on two problems: WDRO-MMSE (n=200, Wasserstein radii ρ=√n) and constrained Gaussian barycenter (n=30, ρ=4√n, N=50), using surrogate duality gap as the convergence criterion. With a vanilla step size, projected BWGD converges substantially faster than Frank-Wolfe on both problems. Incorporating Armijo line search (Armijo BWGD) yields comparable performance to FW on WDRO-MMSE and significantly outperforms Fully Adaptive Frank-Wolfe (FAFW) on the constrained barycenter, where FAFW exhibits notable fluctuation. Euclidean gradient descent with the BW projection (EGD) fails to converge to the global minimum despite the objective being Euclidean strongly convex and smooth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares projected BWGD against three baselines: Frank-Wolfe (FW), Fully Adaptive Frank-Wolfe (FAFW), and Euclidean Gradient Descent with BW projection (EGD). Projected BWGD with a fixed step size clearly outperforms FW in convergence speed on both WDRO-MMSE and constrained barycenter, attributed to the closed-form BW ball projection eliminating the need for the expensive linear oracle subproblem. Armijo BWGD achieves comparable convergence to FW on WDRO-MMSE but significantly outperforms FAFW on the constrained barycenter, where FAFW's reliance on three hyper-parameters causes visible oscillation in the duality gap. EGD with the BW projection (12) fails to converge to the global optimum, demonstrating a fundamental inconsistency between the BW projection operator and Euclidean gradient updates — a key practical insight that motivates using the BW Riemannian gradient rather than the Euclidean gradient in the projected update.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of BW gradient descent convergence analysis from problem-specific results (barycenter in Chewi et al. 2020, Altschuler et al. 2021; variational inference in Lambert et al. 2022) to a general guarantee under Euclidean strong convexity and smoothness, using the PL inequality and descent condition framework. It further generalizes to the constrained setting with Euclidean convex sets, a regime where prior Riemannian convergence theory (Zhang & Sra 2016; Boumal et al. 2019) does not directly apply due to geodesic non-convexity. The closed-form BW ball projection is a new practical tool that contrasts with the Frank-Wolfe-based approaches in the DRO literature (Shafieezadeh Abadeh et al. 2018; Taşkesen et al. 2023), and the key technical bridge between Euclidean and BW geometry (Lemma 3.6, Proposition 3.7) is positioned as a special case of Boumal et al.'s (2019) condition A3 but requires new arguments specific to BW distance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first general convergence guarantee for projected BW gradient descent, filling a gap where prior results were either unconstrained or tied to specific objectives. The necessity of the λ_min(Σ) eigenvalue dependency (shown via a lower bound) is a meaningful caveat for practitioners: convergence can be slow when iterates approach the boundary of S_+^n, even with bounded condition number. The closed-form BW ball projection enables efficient implementation for constrained barycenter and WDRO problems, outperforming Frank-Wolfe in experiments. Open questions include extending to weaker convexity assumptions, stochastic variants, and proving convergence of the Armijo-adapted BWGD variant used in experiments.
