## Problem

Existing composed image retrieval methods are limited to visually similar image pairs characterized by a small set of pre-defined relations or template-based instructions, failing to capture the rich, open-ended search intents that real users express (e.g., 'find other attractions in this country' given a hotel photo). No prior approach can retrieve images connected by diverse, beyond-visual semantic relations while maintaining parameter efficiency suitable for real-world deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242454/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a query consisting of an RGB image (288×288 for CoCa-based, 224×224 for CLIP-based) concatenated with a free-form text instruction, and produces a single embedding vector for cosine-similarity matching against a target image index. The target image is encoded as (image, empty string) to unify it into the same multimodal input format as the query. At inference, a single checkpoint handles all retrieval tasks—composed image retrieval, domain transfer, conditional similarity, and even image-to-image retrieval by supplying a fixed instruction like 'find a natural image of it'.

## Method

The core insight is that image pairs naturally co-occurring on the same web page carry a wide range of implicit semantic relations (e.g., 'charger of a product', 'inside view of'), which can be made explicit by synthesizing open-ended instructions via a PaLI (LMM) + PaLM2 (LLM) pipeline, yielding 36.7M (query image, instruction, target image) triplets. The model is a parameter-shared dual-encoder initialized from CoCa or CLIP, with four randomly initialized self-attention layers for deep modality integration and a single multi-head attention pooler that compresses the fused representation into one embedding. Training uses a contrastive loss where the query image itself (paired with an empty string) serves as an additional hard negative, forcing the model to differentiate closely similar images based on instruction semantics. This design achieves state-of-the-or-better results on eight benchmarks with a 50× smaller model size than prior SOTA methods like CIReVL (14.6B vs. 267M parameters).

## Results

MagicLens-L (CoCa-L, 613M params) achieves 34.1 mAP@5 on CIRCO, 48.2 R@10 on DTIN, and 16.7 R@1 on GeneCIS, outperforming the prior SOTA CIReVL (12.3B–14.6B params) by large margins on all three benchmarks (18.6, 15.9, and 15.9 respectively). On CIRR, MagicLens-L (CoCa-L) reaches 33.3 R@1 and 70.9 R_s@1, surpassing all baselines including PLI (25.5 R@1) and CIReVL (24.6 R@1). The smaller MagicLens-B (CoCa-B, 267M) also outperforms CIReVL on CIRCO (30.8 vs. 18.6 mAP@5) and DTIN (46.8 vs. 15.9 R@1) with a 50× parameter reduction. On the 1.4M-image human evaluation, MagicLens-L wins 80.0% of one-on-one comparisons against LinCIR on beyond-visual instructions (vs. 4.7% for LinCIR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MagicLens outperforms all eight baselines on CIRCO, DTIN, and GeneCIS. On CIRCO (mAP@5), MagicLens-L (CoCa-L) achieves 34.1, nearly doubling CIReVL (18.6) and more than tripling CompoDiff (12.6) and LinCIR (12.6), despite being 50× smaller than CIReVL. On DTIN (R@10), MagicLens-L (CoCa-L) reaches 48.2 versus 15.9 for CIReVL and 12.9 for Context-I2W, a 3× improvement. On GeneCIS (R@1), MagicLens-B (CoCa-B) leads with 17.4, ahead of CIReVL (15.9) and CompoDiff (14.9). On CIRR, MagicLens-L (CoCa-L) achieves 33.3 R@1, beating PLI (25.5), LinCIR (25.0), and CIReVL (24.6) by 5–9 points. On FIQ, MagicLens-L (CoCa-L) (38.0 R@10) edges out CompoDiff (36.0) and PLI (35.4), while MagicLens-B (CoCa-B) (35.2) trails PLI (35.9) slightly. In the 1.4M-image human evaluation, MagicLens-L wins 80.0% of one-on-one comparisons against LinCIR on beyond-visual instructions (vs. 4.7% for LinCIR) and 61.3% on complex visual instructions (vs. 24.0%), demonstrating a decisive advantage on non-visual search intents.

## Relation to Prior Work

MagicLens departs from the composed image retrieval (CIR) line of work (Pic2Word, SEARLE, LinCIR, CompoDiff) in two fundamental ways: it replaces synthetic or template-constrained image pairs with naturally co-occurring pairs mined from web pages, and it replaces pre-defined or template-based instructions with open-ended instructions generated by LMMs (PaLI) and LLMs (PaLM2). Architecturally, it builds on the dual-encoder paradigm of CLIP/CoCa but introduces self-attention layers for deep modality integration and a contrastive loss with query-image hard negatives, distinguishing it from prior approaches that either fine-tune small adapters on frozen encoders (PLI, LinCIR) or invoke LLMs/LMMs at inference time (CIReVL). The data construction pipeline—web mining, metadata expansion, and LLM-based instruction synthesis—is the paper's core conceptual contribution, positioning it as a self-supervised training recipe rather than a task-specific architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50× parameter efficiency (267M vs. 12–14B) while outperforming prior SOTA on CIRCO, DTIN, and GeneCIS makes instruction-following image retrieval practically deployable, and the web-mining + LLM-instruction data pipeline offers a reusable recipe for constructing large-scale self-supervised multimodal training data beyond retrieval. The 1.4M-image human evaluation demonstrating success on 'beyond visual' intents (80% win rate vs. 4.7% for LinCIR) validates that open-ended instructions unlock retrieval capabilities invisible to prior benchmarks. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or multi-image retrieval, how sensitive performance is to the quality of the foundation models used in data construction, and whether the dual-encoder architecture can be replaced by more expressive (but heavier) designs without losing the efficiency advantage.
