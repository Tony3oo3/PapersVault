## Problem

Successor features (SF) with generalized policy improvement (GPI) demonstrate strong empirical transfer performance in multi-task RL, but no convergence or generalization guarantees existed for the deep neural network approximation setting. This paper provides the first provable analysis of SF-DQN with GPI, establishing when and why the SF decomposition yields faster convergence and better transfer than conventional DQN.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242431/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes state-action pairs (s,a), a shared transition feature φ(s,a,s') (bounded by φ_max), and task-specific reward functions r_i(s,a,s') = φᵀw_i*, with data collected online via a behavior policy and stored in a replay buffer of size N. It produces the optimal Q-function Q_i(s,a) = ψ_i(s,a)ᵀw_i*, the corresponding optimal policy π_i*(s), the DNN-parameterized successor feature ψ_i(Θ_i; s,a), and the linear reward mapping w_i*. The key structural constraint is that φ is identical across all tasks while w_i* varies, enabling the decomposition to isolate dynamics from task-specific rewards.

## Method

The core mechanism decomposes each task's Q-function as Q_i(s,a) = ψ_i(s,a)ᵀw_i*, where the successor feature ψ_i is approximated by an L-layer ReLU DNN trained to minimize the mean squared Bellman error (treating φ as the 'reward'), and w_i* is learned via linear regression on (φ, r_i) pairs. Both are updated alternately via mini-batch SGD within an outer loop over tasks. Generalized policy improvement (GPI) selects actions by taking the max over all previously learned successor features: a = argmax_a max_{c∈[i]} ψ(Θ_c; s,a)ᵀw_i. Theoretically, the paper proves convergence at rate 1/T with generalization guarantees, shows GPI accelerates convergence by a factor depending on task relevance q*, and demonstrates SF-DQN outperforms DQN in transfer by a factor of (1+γ)/2 in estimation error.

## Results

Theoretically, SF-DQN converges to the optimal Q-function at a rate of 1/T (Theorem 1), and GPI accelerates convergence by a factor of (1−c)/(1−c·q*) where q* decreases as source-target task similarity increases (Theorem 2). In transfer learning, SF-DQN achieves a lower estimation error than DQN by a factor of (1+γ)/2 (Theorems 3 vs. 4). Empirically, on a synthetic two-task MDP, SF-DQN with GPI achieves a normalized average reward of 0.986 when tasks are highly similar (‖w₁*−w₂*‖=0.01), compared to 0.942 without GPI, while both methods converge to similar performance (~0.71) when tasks are dissimilar (‖w₁*−w₂*‖=10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is conventional DQN (without successor feature decomposition), as originally introduced by Mnih et al. (2013) and its GPI-augmented variant. The paper demonstrates that SF-DQN consistently outperforms DQN in transfer reinforcement learning: in Figure 2, the SF-DQN agent achieves a higher average reward on Task 2 (the transfer task) than DQN with GPI throughout training. Theoretically, Theorem 3 vs. Theorem 4 shows that SF-DQN's transfer error is smaller than DQN's by a factor of (1+γ)/2, meaning the advantage grows as γ→1 (long-horizon tasks). Additionally, SF-DQN with GPI outperforms SF-DQN without GPI when source and target tasks are similar (e.g., 0.986 vs. 0.942 at ‖w₁*−w₂*‖=0.01), but the gap vanishes for dissimilar tasks (0.717 vs. 0.707 at ‖w₁*−w₂*‖=10), confirming that GPI's benefit is task-relevance-dependent as predicted by Theorem 2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the successor feature (SF) framework of Barreto et al. (2017) and the successor representation of Dayan (1993) from tabular settings to deep neural network function approximation, providing the first convergence and generalization guarantees for SF-DQN with generalized policy improvement (GPI). The analysis draws on techniques from NTK-based convergence proofs for one-hidden-layer networks (Zhong et al., 2017; Zhang et al., 2022) and recent DQN convergence frameworks (Zhang et al., 2023a), extending them to multi-layer networks and the non-i.i.d. RL setting. It departs from prior SF theory (Barreto et al., 2017; 2018), which assumed well-estimated source-task Q-functions in tabular MDPs, by providing end-to-end sample complexity for both source-task training and transfer stages under DNN approximation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a notable theoretical gap: no prior analysis provided convergence or generalization guarantees for successor features learned with deep networks, despite their strong empirical performance in transfer RL. The quantification that SF-DQN outperforms DQN by a factor of γ in transfer error, and that GPI improves convergence by a factor depending on task relevance q*, gives practitioners principled guidance on when transfer via SF is most beneficial. Limitations include the requirement for good initialization (Assumption 19), the assumption that the transition feature φ is fixed and known, and experimental validation primarily on synthetic environments plus a single Reacher benchmark. Open questions include learning φ itself with a DNN and combining SF with other DRL algorithms beyond DQN.
