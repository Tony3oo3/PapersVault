## Problem

Existing inverse folding methods design protein sequences conditioned only on geometric backbone constraints, ignoring the biochemical properties (charge, hydrophobicity) at the molecular surface that co-determine protein function. This limits their ability to design proteins with specific functional behaviors such as high-affinity binding or enzyme-substrate catalysis, where poorly placed surface charges or hydrophobic patches can prevent interaction even when shapes are complementary.

## Input / Output

The method takes a protein surface as a point cloud of up to 5,000 vertices (after MSMS extraction, Gaussian kernel smoothing, and octree-based compression), where each vertex carries 3D coordinates plus two biochemical features (hydrophobicity and charge) inherited from its nearest residue. Vertices are sorted by the residue index of their nearest atom to preserve local-to-global spatial ordering. The output is an amino acid sequence of up to 1,024 residues generated token-by-token via an autoregressive decoder, designed to fold into the given surface while respecting its biochemical constraints.

## Method

SurfPro uses a two-stage hierarchical encoder followed by a 3-layer autoregressive Transformer decoder. The local perspective module applies a variant of equivariant graph convolution (EGCL) over K=30 nearest-neighbor vertices, propagating messages that combine 3D coordinate differences with biochemical features through gated residual updates. The global landscape module (FAMHA) performs multi-head attention over the entire surface while guaranteeing SE(3) equivariance by averaging attention outputs over 8 frame transformations defined by PCA principal components of the point cloud. The decoder then generates the sequence autoregressively, trained with negative log-likelihood. The key architectural insight is that local graph convolutions capture short-range geometric-biochemical interactions while frame-averaged attention captures long-range surface complementarity, together enabling the model to produce sequences that satisfy both shape and functional property constraints simultaneously.

## Results

On the standard inverse folding benchmark CATH 4.2, SurfPro achieves a 57.78% sequence recovery rate and 3.13 perplexity, substantially outperforming ProteinMPNN (44.95%, 5.19), PiFold (52.61%, 4.88), and LM-DESIGN (54.16%, 4.47). For protein binder design across six target proteins, SurfPro-Pretrain achieves a 26.22% average success rate (proportion of designed binders with lower AF2 pAE_interaction than the positive binder), outperforming the best prior method (LM-DESIGN, 16.37%) by nearly 10 percentage points. For enzyme design across five substrate categories, SurfPro-Pretrain reaches a 43.63% average success rate on ESP score, surpassing the best prior method (PiFold, 40.65%) by ~3 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SurfPro consistently outperforms all three inverse folding baselines (ProteinMPNN, PiFold, LM-DESIGN) on CATH 4.2, achieving the highest recovery rate (57.78% vs. 54.16% for LM-DESIGN) and lowest perplexity (3.13 vs. 4.47). In binder design, SurfPro achieves the lowest average AF2 pAE_interaction (16.95) across all six target proteins, beating LM-DESIGN (20.77) and PiFold (20.58); after PDB pretraining, SurfPro-Pretrain lifts the average success rate from 22.29% to 26.22%, a 6.9% improvement over the best prior method. In enzyme design, SurfPro achieves a 42.23% average success rate versus LM-DESIGN's 37.58% and PiFold's 40.65%, with SurfPro-Pretrain further improving to 43.63%. Against MaSIF (a surface-based ranking approach), SurfPro wins on 4 of 6 target proteins in pAE_interaction but is dramatically faster at inference (0.45 s/sample vs. 210.42 s/sample, ~467× speedup), since SurfPro generates sequences end-to-end while MaSIF requires ranking 100 candidates.

## Relation to Prior Work

SurfPro extends the inverse folding paradigm (ProteinMPNN, PiFold, LM-DESIGN) by replacing rigid backbone conditioning with a continuous protein surface augmented with biochemical properties (hydrophobicity, charge). The hierarchical encoder combines a variant of equivariant graph convolution (Satorras et al., 2021) for local geometric-biochemical feature extraction with a frame-averaging multi-head attention module (Puny et al., 2021) for global SE(3)-equivariant message passing, a combination not previously applied to protein surface-to-sequence generation. Unlike MaSIF and dMaSIF, which model surfaces for interaction prediction or use handcrafted fingerprints for binder search, SurfPro is an end-to-end generative model that directly produces amino acid sequences from surface point clouds without precomputed features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that biochemical properties on protein surfaces are not merely supplementary but essential for functional design: removing charge alone drops recovery rate from 57.78% to 21.34%, and removing global modeling collapses it to 15.51%. The 0.45-second inference time versus MaSIF's 210 seconds makes surface-based functional design practically deployable at scale. However, the method remains a refinement tool rather than a de novo designer—it requires an existing surface (typically from a known binder or enzyme), limiting its applicability when no positive starting point exists. The authors identify de novo surface generation (e.g., via diffusion models) as the key open direction to unlock full generative capability.
