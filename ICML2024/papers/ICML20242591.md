## Problem

Static public benchmarks for LLMs are vulnerable to data contamination, making reported scores unreliable indicators of true model capability. Moreover, existing evaluation protocols yield only aggregate scores and cannot decompose performance into fine-grained cognitive abilities or reveal how those abilities interact. The paper asks whether a dynamic, psychometrics-inspired protocol can simultaneously mitigate contamination and enable multifaceted ability analysis across diverse task types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242591/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes original multiple-choice or open-ended evaluation samples from four benchmarks (MMLU, ARC-C, GSM8K, BBH) and produces semantically equivalent but structurally different probing questions via LLM agents. For non-multiple-choice datasets (GSM8K, BBH), only paraphrasing and extra-context principles are applied, while MMLU and ARC-C support all five principles. Outputs include per-model accuracy on transformed benchmarks, Pearson/Spearman/Kendall correlation coefficients across the three ability dimensions, and a confusion matrix comparing original vs. probing correctness to quantify contamination signals.

## Method

MPA employs two LLM agents in an adversarial loop: a probing agent (GPT-4-Turbo, temperature 0.7) rewrites a question according to one of five psychometric principles, and a judge agent (GPT-4-Turbo, temperature 0) returns a binary Yes/No verdict on whether the transformed question preserves the original's concept and answer. The five principles are mapped to three cognitive abilities—language understanding (paraphrasing questions, paraphrasing choices, permuting choices), problem solving (adding non-essential context), and domain knowledge (adding a plausible but incorrect choice)—and can be dynamically combined to construct benchmarks of varying complexity. This modular, agent-based design replaces the hand-crafted graph or algorithmic transformations used by prior dynamic benchmarks (DyVal, NPHardEval), making it extensible to any task format without task-specific engineering. Human verification on 1,800 samples confirmed 94–97% equivalence and correctness of the generated probing questions.

## Results

All six evaluated LLMs show significant performance degradation on MPA-generated probing benchmarks compared to vanilla benchmarks. GPT-4-Turbo drops most on MMLU (84.40 → 68.86, −15.54%) and ARC-C (96.16 → 84.67, −11.49%), while GSM8K and BBH show smaller drops (−6.72% and −0.75% respectively), suggesting knowledge-based benchmarks are more susceptible to memorization. The three basic abilities (language understanding, problem solving, domain knowledge) exhibit very high mutual correlations (Pearson r = 0.986–0.994), with LU and PS being the most tightly coupled. An implicit Matthew effect is observed: larger models (GPT-4-Turbo, Gemini-Pro) show stronger cross-ability correlations than smaller models (Llama2-7b/13b).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare MPA against other dynamic evaluation methods (e.g., DyVal, NPHardEval) head-to-head; instead, the primary comparison is each model's vanilla benchmark score versus its MPA probing score. GPT-4-Turbo, despite the largest absolute drop on MMLU (−15.54%), retains the highest MPA accuracy across all four datasets (68.86% MMLU, 88.50% GSM8K, 84.67% ARC-C, 87.78% BBH), confirming it remains the strongest model even under dynamic evaluation. Open-source models (Llama2-70b, Mixtral-8x7b, Yi-34b) show comparable or larger relative drops than proprietary models, and the confusion-matrix analysis reveals a higher OT/PF ratio for open-source models, suggesting greater susceptibility to data contamination. Among the five probing principles, paraphrasing the question (P1), paraphrasing choices (P2), and adding a new choice (P5) are the most effective at degrading performance, while permuting choices (P3) is least effective and can even slightly improve scores for GPT-4-Turbo and GPT-3.5-Turbo on ARC-C. Prompt engineering (CoT, ICL) yields only marginal gains (≤1%) on MPA benchmarks, indicating the degradation is not easily mitigated by inference-time strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

MPA departs from prior dynamic evaluation methods like DyVal (graph-structure-based generation) and NPHardEval (NP-hard problem generation) by replacing algorithmic sample construction with LLM-based agents (probing + judge), making the protocol task-agnostic and extensible to diverse benchmarks. It operationalizes the psychometric framework of Burnell et al. (2023), who identified three basic cognitive abilities in LLMs, into concrete, modular probing principles that can be dynamically combined. The adversarial judge-agent design draws on the LLM-as-judge paradigm (Dubois et al., 2023; Li et al., 2023b), but repurposes it for validating generated evaluation samples rather than scoring model outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MPA offers a practical, general-purpose protocol to mitigate data contamination while enabling fine-grained, multifaceted ability analysis that static benchmarks cannot provide. The observed 'Matthew effect'—that larger models exhibit stronger correlations between basic abilities—provides a psychometric lens on emergent abilities and suggests that scaling may strengthen the integration of cognitive skills. Key limitations include dependence on GPT-4 as the agent backbone, coverage of only four datasets, and residual inconsistencies in generated samples; open questions include whether weaker models can eventually serve as agents, how the protocol extends to non-English or multimodal settings, and whether the ability-correlation structure holds across architectures and training regimes.
