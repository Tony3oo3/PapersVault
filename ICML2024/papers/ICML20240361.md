## Problem

Thompson Sampling for contextual bandits requires sampling from the Bayesian posterior at every round, but these posteriors are generally intractable. Existing approximate inference methods face a trade-off: Laplace approximation yields poor posterior representations (especially in high dimensions), while MCMC-based approaches like Langevin Monte Carlo are computationally expensive and require many iterations to converge. No prior work combined efficient Gaussian Variational Inference with Thompson Sampling while providing matching theoretical regret guarantees.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the algorithm receives a d-dimensional contextual vector x_t and a finite action set A(x_t), and outputs an action a_t selected by maximizing the expected reward under a sampled parameter. The internal state is a variational posterior N(μ, B B^T) maintained via a square-root covariance matrix B ∈ R^{d×d}, which enables O(d²) sampling (θ = μ + Bε) without any Cholesky decomposition. The posterior is warm-initialized from the previous round and updated with K_t Riemannian gradient steps per round, where K_t scales as O(κ_t² log(dT log T)) under diverse-context assumptions.

## Method

VITS approximates the intractable posterior with a full (non-mean-field) Gaussian variational family, justified by the Bernstein-Von Mises theorem, and optimizes the KL objective via Riemannian gradient descent on the Wasserstein-2 geometry of the Gaussian manifold. The key computational innovation is a square-root covariance parameterization B (where BB^T = Σ) with the recursion B_{k+1} = (I − h∇²U)B_k + h(B_k^{-1})^T, which avoids the O(d³) Cholesky factorization required by prior Wasserstein VI methods. Two further variants reduce cost: VITS-II replaces B^{-1} with a first-order Taylor approximation (C_{k+1} = C_k(I − h(C_k^T C_k − ∇²U))), dropping complexity to O(d²), and VITS-II Hessian-free eliminates the Hessian entirely via a Gaussian integration-by-parts identity. The paper proves a cumulative regret bound of Õ(d√(dT)) for linear contextual bandits, matching the state-of-the-art LinTS bound, and demonstrates that VITS-II outperforms LMC-TS in ill-conditioned settings while being computationally cheaper.

## Results

Theoretically, VITS-I and VITS-II achieve a cumulative regret bound of Õ(d√(dT)) for linear contextual bandits, matching the state-of-the-art LinTS bound from Agrawal & Goyal (2013). The number of optimization steps per round scales as O(κ_t² log(dT log T)), which reduces to O(log(dT log T)) under diverse context assumptions. Experimentally, on synthetic linear and quadratic bandits with controlled conditioning (ζ=0.1 ill-conditioned, ζ=1 well-conditioned), VITS-II matches LinTS in the linear setting and outperforms LMC-TS (10 or 50 Langevin steps) in the ill-conditioned regime. On the MovieLens dataset (1M ratings, 5-dim low-rank features, logistic rewards), VITS-II slightly outperforms LMC-TS while being substantially more computationally efficient.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LinTS (Agrawal & Goyal, 2013), VITS-II achieves the same Õ(d√(dT)) regret bound in the linear setting, but unlike LinTS it generalizes to non-linear reward models (e.g., quadratic, logistic). In the linear bandit experiments, VITS-II matches LinTS performance in both well-conditioned (ζ=1) and ill-conditioned (ζ=0.1) regimes. In the quadratic bandit and MovieLens experiments, LinTS is ill-suited (it assumes linear rewards) and VITS-II clearly outperforms it. Against LMC-TS (Xu et al., 2022), VITS-II achieves a comparable regret bound but with lower per-step computational cost (no Cholesky decomposition, O(d²) vs O(d³) for VITS-I). Empirically, VITS-II outperforms LMC-TS (both 10-step and 50-step variants) in the ill-conditioned linear bandit (ζ=0.1) and in the quadratic bandit, where LMC requires many Langevin iterations to converge to the posterior. On MovieLens, VITS-II slightly outperforms LMC-TS in cumulative regret while being significantly faster, since VITS only requires sampling from a low-dimensional Gaussian per arm rather than running full Langevin diffusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

VITS builds directly on the Riemannian gradient descent framework for variational inference on the Gaussian family developed by Lambert et al. (2022b), adapting it to the sequential bandit setting with warm-started posteriors. It departs from prior approximate TS methods in two key ways: (1) it uses the full non-diagonal Gaussian family rather than mean-field mixtures (Urteaga & Wiggins, 2018) or Laplace approximations (Chapelle & Li, 2011), justified by the Bernstein-Von Mises theorem; and (2) it introduces a square-root covariance parameterization (VITS-II) and a Hessian-free variant that reduce per-step complexity from O(d³) to O(d²), addressing the computational bottleneck that makes MCMC-based TS (LMC-TS, Xu et al. 2022) expensive in high dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first regret bound established for variational inference in a sequential learning context, matching the O~(d^{3/2}√T) rate of LinTS while remaining applicable to general (non-linear) reward models where the posterior is intractable. The practical significance is that VITS-II offers a drop-in replacement for LMC-TS with lower per-step cost and faster convergence in ill-conditioned settings, as demonstrated on MovieLens. A key limitation is that the theoretical guarantee is restricted to the linear bandit case, while the algorithm's main appeal lies in nonlinear applications; extending the regret analysis to general exponential-family or neural bandits remains an open problem.
