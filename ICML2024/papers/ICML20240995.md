## Problem

Evaluating pretrained representation quality conventionally requires running linear probes on numerous real-world datasets, which introduces data bias, high computational cost, and privacy concerns, and can conflate true model deficiency with evaluation-data bias. No existing method provides a task-agnostic, real-data-free way to quantify both the accuracy and adversarial robustness of a pretrained representation against a theoretically grounded reference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240995/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pretrained image encoder (ViT or ResNet variants) and feeds it synthetic binary-classification samples drawn from class-conditional Gaussians in the input space (e.g., 224×224×3 tensors), with a separation parameter s sampled uniformly from [0.1, 5] to span accuracy from ~55% to ~100%, and optionally a structured (heptadiagonal, channel-block) covariance to better match downstream data statistics. It outputs a single SynBench-Score in [0, 1] (the ratio of the representation's AUC to the input's AUC on the expected-bound vs. threshold-accuracy plot) plus the full robustness-accuracy trade-off curve parameterized by adversarial budget ε ∈ [0, 0.8].

## Method

SynBench constructs a family of synthetic binary classification tasks from class-conditional Gaussian mixtures whose optimal linear classifier and its ε-robust variant admit closed-form solutions (Theorem 3.1), yielding an analytical robustness-accuracy trade-off that serves as a task-agnostic reference. For each synthetic task, the frozen pretrained model maps the Gaussian samples to a representation space, where a new class-conditional Gaussian is fitted and the same robust Bayes-optimal classifier is applied to compute per-sample decision margins. The expected scaled bound of correctly classified samples is then averaged over all synthetic tasks whose accuracy exceeds a threshold a_T, producing an accuracy-constrained expected bound E_{θ,ε}(a_T) for both the raw input and the representation. SynBench-Score is defined as the ratio of the area under the representation's curve to the area under the input's reference curve, so a score near 1 indicates the representation preserves the ideal robustness-accuracy trade-off. The framework also enables ε-robust linear probing hyperparameter selection by picking the ε that maximizes SynBench-Score, which empirically improves downstream robust accuracy.

## Results

SynBench-Score achieves a Pearson correlation of 0.79 with average accuracy on 27 downstream tasks using only 2k synthetic samples, compared to 0.46–0.55 for Val loss, MDL, LogME, and SFDA. At 8k samples, SynBench reaches 0.89 correlation (vs. 0.65–0.81 for baselines), and at 30k+ samples all methods exceed 0.9. SynBench also provides a 50× speedup over task-agnostic baselines (MDL, SDL, εSC) and consistently selects ViT-B/16 over ViT-B/16-in21k across all sample sizes, while other metrics flip their recommendation. For ε-robust linear probing, the SynBench-guided ε selection yields +0.7% standard and +2.5% robust accuracy on Tiny-ImageNet for ViT-B/16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SynBench outperforms all four task-agnostic baselines (Val loss, MDL, LogME, SFDA) in Pearson correlation with downstream accuracy at small sample sizes: at 2k synthetic samples, SynBench achieves 0.79 while the baselines range from 0.46 to 0.55; at 8k, SynBench reaches 0.89 versus 0.65–0.81 for the others. SDL (Whitney et al., 2020) fails entirely at small sample sizes and is excluded from the main comparison. In terms of model selection consistency, SynBench consistently recommends ViT-B/16 over ViT-B/16-in21k across all tested sample sizes (2k–32k), whereas Val loss, MDL, and SDL change their recommendation depending on n. LogME and SFDA show the weakest correlation and are excluded from the consistency analysis. The paper also reports a 50× runtime speedup over MDL/SDL/εSC baselines, making SynBench both more accurate and substantially faster for representation evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the theoretical characterization of optimal robust classifiers for Gaussian mixtures (Dan et al., 2020; Bhagoji et al., 2019; Dobriban et al., 2020) as a *reference benchmark* for evaluating pretrained representations, rather than as a classification algorithm. Prior task-agnostic evaluation metrics (MDL, SDL, LogME, SFDA) still require real-world labeled data to compute their scores, whereas SynBench derives its reference entirely from the analytically tractable robustness-accuracy trade-off of class-conditional Gaussians. The ε-robust linear probing framework of Fan et al. (2021) is adopted as the downstream fine-tuning procedure whose hyperparameters SynBench can guide, but the core contribution is the synthetic-data evaluation loop itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SynBench offers a practical, data-free, and 50× faster alternative to multi-dataset linear probing for ranking pretrained representations, with Pearson correlations above 0.9 to average downstream accuracy at 30k synthetic samples. Its ability to consistently select the better model (e.g., ViT-B/16 over ViT-B/16-in21k) where established metrics like MDL and SDL flip their recommendations across sample sizes is a concrete advantage for model auditing and governance. Limitations include restriction to binary classification and image modalities, and the robustness scores on the synthetic task remain low (~50–55%), suggesting the Gaussian proxy may not fully capture the adversarial geometry of natural images. Open questions include extension to multi-class settings, other trustworthiness dimensions (privacy, fairness), and non-vision domains.
