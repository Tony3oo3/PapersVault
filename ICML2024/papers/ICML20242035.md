## Problem

In 3-D environments, the magnitude of pixel changes between transitions is large but their saliency is low, making it difficult for count-based exploration methods to produce meaningful intrinsic rewards without appropriate state aggregation. Existing density estimation approaches either count states too granularly (prediction-error methods) or lack a principled mechanism for determining the right level of state aggregation in high-dimensional observations. The paper asks whether clustering representations—particularly pre-trained ones—can resolve this transition saliency problem and yield effective pseudo-counts for exploration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242035/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB observations from 3-D navigation environments (VizDoom MyWayHome at 98×98×3, Habitat Replica Apartment-0), which are projected into 384-dimensional embeddings via either a frozen DINOv2-small CLS token or a random convolutional target network. It produces a per-timestep intrinsic reward (inverse square root of the pseudo-count, scaled by 0.1) that is added to the extrinsic reward for PPO policy updates, and maintains a growing global cluster table of (center, count) pairs that approximates the state-space density over the entire training run.

## Method

The core mechanism is a two-stage clustering procedure with only two hyperparameters (cosine similarity threshold κ and number of GMM clusters M). At the end of each episode, a Gaussian mixture model is fit on the batch of embeddings to produce M episodic cluster centers. Each episodic center is then matched against the global cluster table via cosine similarity: if the maximum similarity to any stored center falls below κ, a new center is appended (analogous to a simplified Dirichlet-process stick-breaking step); otherwise, its count is aggregated into the nearest existing center. The pseudo-count for each observation is the sum of the matched global count and a running episodic count, and the intrinsic reward is 1/√(pseudo-count). The key insight is that pre-trained DINO representations encode scene-level invariances that make GMM clustering meaningful in visually complex 3-D settings, while even random features suffice in simpler 3-D environments like VizDoom—demonstrating that the clustering structure, not the specific feature quality, is what resolves the transition saliency problem.

## Results

On Habitat (Apartment-0), Just Cluster It with DINO features achieves 17K visitation counts, outperforming RECODE (~15K), its own random-feature variant (14K), E3B (~7K), and RND (~5K). On VizDoom (MyWayHome-Sparse), clustering random features converges at ~1.5M steps, beating RECODE with DINO (~5M steps) and prediction-error methods (≥5M steps). On the harder VerySparse setting, the method converges at ~1.8M steps while RECODE fails to converge entirely. The method is also robust to random noise in observations, with performance actually slightly increasing under noisy conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RECODE (the closest clustering-based competitor), Just Cluster It wins on both environments: on Habitat it achieves 17K vs. ~15K visits, and on VizDoom it converges 2–3× faster (1.5M vs. 3.8–5M steps) while RECODE fails entirely on VerySparse. Against E3B, the advantage is large (17K vs. ~7K on Habitat), which the authors attribute to E3B performing only episodic clustering without a global table, making it unsuitable for singleton environments. Against prediction-error baselines (RND, Curiosity/ICM, RIDE), the clustering method dominates: RND reaches only ~5K visits on Habitat and ≥5M steps on VizDoom, while Just Cluster It achieves 17K visits and ~1.5M steps respectively. Notably, on the simpler VizDoom environment random features slightly outperform DINO features (1.5M vs. 1.7M steps), whereas on the visually complex Habitat environment DINO features are clearly superior (17K vs. 14K visits), confirming that pre-trained inductive biases matter more as observation complexity increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the pseudo-count framework of Bellemare et al. (2016) and the clustering-based exploration line (Ma et al., 2019; E3B; RECODE), but introduces a two-stage episodic-then-global clustering scheme that departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it fits a full Gaussian Mixture Model per episode to produce representative cluster centers rather than updating a single embedding at each step (as RECODE does), and (2) it explicitly leverages pre-trained DINOv2 representations to exploit visual invariances for state aggregation. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against prediction-error methods (RND, Curiosity, RIDE, BYOL-Explore) by arguing that the 'transition saliency' problem in 3D environments—large pixel changes with low per-step significance—makes prediction-error signals poorly calibrated, whereas clustering naturally aggregates similar observations into meaningful pseudo-counts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, low-hyperparameter pathway for integrating pre-trained visual representations into exploration, showing that DINOv2 embeddings substantially improve state aggregation in visually complex 3D scenes (Habitat) while random features suffice in simpler settings (VizDoom). The transition saliency framing offers a useful diagnostic for understanding why count-based and prediction-error methods behave differently across 2D/3D environments. Limitations include evaluation only on singleton navigation MDPs with two environments, and the method's reliance on a fixed cosine similarity threshold κ that must be tuned per environment. Open questions include extension to contextual MDPs, whether clustered representations can serve as fixed targets for prediction-error methods, and whether multi-modal or dynamics-aware representations further improve exploration.
