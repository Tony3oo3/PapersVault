## Problem

Building language-conditioned agents (LCAs) that execute diverse text-specified tasks in an environment is hindered by two limitations: single-task RL requires training a new policy per task (too slow for real-time use), and multi-task RL demands a carefully curated corpus of training tasks and still generalizes unreliably to unseen tasks. The paper asks whether one can decouple the grounding problem (mapping text to a target state) from the control problem (reaching that state) to obtain a zero-shot LCA that never sees task descriptions during training.

## Input / Output

At test time the system receives a free-form text task description (e.g., 'sitting on top of a box') and the current 343-dimensional environment state of a Humanoid-plus-cube MuJoCo scene. The text is embedded by a VLM text encoder; the environment is rendered to 224×224×3 RGB images from three fixed camera viewpoints (front, left, right at 0°, ±45°). The output is a 31-dimensional goal configuration (torso position/orientation, 19 joint angles, cube position and quaternion), which is then fed to a pretrained goal-conditioned policy that emits 19-dimensional actuator-torque actions over a 100-step episode.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage decomposition: (1) text-to-goal generation, and (2) goal-reaching via a pretrained GCRL agent. For text-to-goal, the authors precompute multiview configuration embeddings (averaged VLM image-encoder outputs over three rendering functions) for a 2.5M-configuration embedding-diversity dataset, then at test time retrieve top-k configurations by cosine similarity with the text embedding and optionally finetune them via projected gradient ascent on a distilled MLP that approximates the rendering∘VLM-image-encoder composition (yielding a 40000× speedup and well-behaved gradients). The GCRL agent is trained with PPO on a Euclidean-distance-to-goal reward over goals sampled uniformly from the diversity dataset, so it never sees any text during training. Multiview evaluation (three viewpoints) is shown to resolve occlusion, distance-ambiguity, and stability failures inherent in single-view 2D scoring, and the distilled model's smoothness makes it suitable for both rapid MTRL/STRL baseline training and gradient-based configuration finetuning.

## Results

On 256 zero-shot tasks in the Humanoid-plus-cube environment, the proposed GCRL-S LCA (retrieval + finetuning + VLM selection) outperforms the MTRL (test) baseline on exact VLM return in 205/256 tasks and on exact best-in-trajectory score in 210/256 tasks. GCRL-F (without VLM selection) achieves 198/256 and 210/256 respectively. The distilled model reduces VLM evaluation time by 40,000× (from 21.1 s to 4.8×10⁻⁴ s for 256 three-view embeddings), and the embedding-diversity dataset yields the highest retrieval scores across all five VLMs (e.g., 0.6260 with EVA02-E-14+ vs. 0.6132 for random policy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its GCRL-based LCA against three baselines: MTRL (test) for zero-shot generalization, MTRL (train) as an upper bound, and STRL as a single-task reference. All GCRL variants with the embedding-diversity dataset (GCRL-D, -F, -S) consistently outperform MTRL (test) across every metric, with GCRL-S winning 205/256 tasks on exact VLM return. Against MTRL (train), GCRL-F and -S are competitive on best-in-trajectory scores (124–128/256 tasks) but trail on VLM return (91–94/256), reflecting that MTRL directly optimizes the VLM reward while GCRL uses configurations as a proxy. Against STRL, GCRL-S wins on exact VLM return for 83/256 tasks and on exact best-in-trajectory score for 102/256 tasks, though STRL retains an advantage on many tasks due to its per-task exploration budget. The random-policy retrieval variant (GCRL-R) underperforms MTRL (test) on most metrics, confirming that dataset diversity is critical for the approach to succeed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-to-goal + goal-reaching decomposition proposed by Colas et al. (2020), which relied on restricted algorithmic annotations for the text-to-goal component, by replacing that component with VLM-based configuration retrieval and gradient-based finetuning. It departs from prior VLM-as-reward approaches (Mahmoudieh et al., 2022; Fan et al., 2022; Rocamonde et al., 2023; Adeniji et al., 2023) that use VLM scores directly as RL rewards, instead using VLMs only offline to identify goal configurations and then delegating control to a separate goal-conditioned RL agent. The practical pipeline—retrieval from precomputed embeddings, distilled-model finetuning, and multiview scoring—combines knowledge distillation and retrieval-augmented generation ideas with RL in a way not previously explored for language-conditioned control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a practical zero-shot LCA that outperforms MTRL baselines on 205/256 tasks without requiring any textual task descriptions during training, addressing a key bottleneck in building language-conditioned agents. The 40000× speedup from distilling the VLM composition into a small MLP makes VLM-in-the-loop RL training feasible (reducing what would be ~125 days of reward computation to hours), a practically important efficiency gain. However, the approach is limited to static configurations with natural final states (excluding dynamic tasks like juggling or cycling), and current VLMs still struggle with compositional spatial relationships, leaving open the question of whether this decomposition scales to richer, multi-object, dynamic environments.
