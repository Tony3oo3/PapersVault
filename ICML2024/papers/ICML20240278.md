## Problem

Standard multivariate time series classification (MTSC) methods rely on fully supervised learning that treats all time points equally, failing to exploit the fact that discriminative patterns are typically sparse and localized within a series. While multiple instance learning (MIL) offers a natural weakly supervised framing, standard MIL pooling is permutation-invariant and assumes instance independence, making it unable to capture the temporal correlations and ordering dependencies inherent in time series data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240278/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series of T time points (each d-dimensional) as a single 'bag,' where each time point is an 'instance' whose individual label is unavailable. It outputs a bag-level class label via a one-vs-rest binary MIL scheme (argmax over per-class probabilities) and, as a byproduct, instance-level importance scores extracted from the class-token attention map, enabling localization of the specific time points driving the prediction. The feature extractor (InceptionTime backbone) projects each time point to a 128-d embedding before pooling.

## Method

TimeMIL replaces the permutation-invariant MIL pooling with a time-aware transformer-based pooling that combines multi-head self-attention (to model temporal correlation between instances) with a novel learnable wavelet positional encoding (WPE) that captures multi-scale time-frequency ordering. The WPE applies channel-wise convolution with learnable Mexican-hat wavelet kernels whose scaling and translation parameters are optimized end-to-end, providing a conditional, input-dependent positional signal unlike fixed sinusoidal encodings. A learnable class token is prepended to the instance embeddings and passed through two transformer blocks (using Nyström approximation for linear-complexity attention), after which only the class token is fed to a two-layer MLP classifier. The paper provides an information-theoretic justification: Proposition 2 shows shuffling time points increases entropy, and Theorem 3 proves that modeling inter-instance correlation strictly lowers class-conditioned entropy, formalizing why time-aware pooling is necessary. Training uses binary cross-entropy with one-hot labels per class (one-vs-rest), window-based random masking augmentation, and a warm-up strategy that blends mean-pooled features with the class token for the first 10 epochs.

## Results

On 26 equal-length UEA datasets (Group 1), TimeMIL achieves 77.4% average accuracy, surpassing the second-best method (TodyNet, 72.6%) by 4.8% and attaining the best result on 18/26 datasets with an average rank of 2.33/11. On 10 general time series analysis datasets (Group 2), TimeMIL reaches 77.7% average accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline ModernTCN (74.2%) by 3.5% and achieving an average rank of 2.85/17. Across all 30 UEA datasets (Appendix G), TimeMIL attains 79.1% accuracy, 78.2% F1, and 0.883 AUC-ROC, leading all 9 compared methods on every metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimeMIL achieves the best average accuracy in both experimental groups: 77.4% on 26 MTSC-specific datasets (vs. TodyNet 72.6%, MOS-CNN 69.2%, ShapeNet 68.4%) and 77.7% on 10 general time series datasets (vs. ModernTCN 74.2%, TimesNet 73.6%, Crossformer 73.2%). The gains are most pronounced on challenging datasets: AtrialFibrillation (73.3% vs. 46.7% TodyNet, +26.6%), StandWalkJump (73.3% vs. 46.7% TodyNet, +26.6%), and PEMS-SF (93.1% vs. 78.0% TodyNet, +15.1%). The sole notable loss in Group 1 is PenDigits (60.0% vs. 98.7% TodyNet), a very short sequence (length 8) where the MIL formulation provides little advantage. In Group 2, TimeMIL trails ModernTCN on SelfRegulationSCP1 (89.8% vs. 93.4%) but wins decisively on Handwriting (48.7% vs. 30.6% ModernTCN) and PEMS-SF (93.1% vs. 89.1%). Against the MIL-specific baselines in ablation, TimeMIL's time-aware pooling outperforms ABMIL by 3.5% and ConjunctiveMIL by 2.8% in average accuracy, confirming the benefit of modeling temporal correlation and ordering.

## Relation to Prior Work

TimeMIL extends the attention-based MIL (ABMIL) paradigm from histopathology to time series, explicitly addressing ABMIL's permutation-invariance assumption that is incompatible with temporal ordering. The wavelet positional encoding draws on the conditional positional encoding paradigm (Chu et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023) but instantiates it with learnable Mexican-hat wavelet kernels to capture multi-scale time-frequency structure. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant fully supervised MTSC literature (InceptionTime, OS-CNN, PatchTST, ModernTCN) by reframing classification as a weakly supervised bag-labeling problem, justified through an information-theoretic entropy argument showing that modeling temporal correlation and ordering reduces class-conditioned entropy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MIL reframing offers a principled alternative to fully supervised MTSC that naturally localizes discriminative time points, providing inherent interpretability without post-hoc attribution methods. The consistent outperformance across 26+ baselines on 28 UEA datasets (average rank ~2.3–2.9) validates the weakly supervised formulation, and the decision-boundary visualization (Fig. 6) concretely illustrates why instance-level discrimination yields clearer margins than bag-level supervision. Key open questions include extending the framework to cross-channel temporal attention (acknowledged limitation), whether the MIL formulation transfers to forecasting or anomaly detection, and whether the gains persist on longer or higher-dimensional series where the Nyström approximation may degrade.
