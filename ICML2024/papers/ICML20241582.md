## Problem

Posthoc interpretability methods for audio classifiers are limited: gradient-based approaches produce saliency maps on non-intuitive features like mel-spectrograms that are difficult for humans to interpret, and existing decoder-based methods (e.g., L2I) do not generate listenable waveform outputs. The key gap is producing interpretations that are both faithful to the classifier's decision and directly perceivable as audio, even when the classifier operates on non-invertible compressed features.

## Input / Output

The method takes an audio waveform and the latent representations (four deepest layers) from a frozen pretrained classifier (CNN14) as input. It produces a binary mask over the linear-frequency STFT spectrogram, which—combined with the original signal's phase and inverted via ISTFT—yields a listenable audio waveform highlighting the segments responsible for the prediction. The approach is feature-agnostic: the classifier may internally use FBANKs or any other representation, but the mask is always applied to the linear spectrogram to preserve invertibility.

## Method

L-MAC employs a U-Net-style decoder of transposed 2D convolutions that maps the classifier's latent representations to a binary mask over the linear spectrogram. The decoder is trained with a dual-objective masking loss: it minimizes the categorical cross-entropy of the classifier on the masked-in portion (using the network's own prediction as the label) while maximizing cross-entropy on the masked-out portion, plus an L1 sparsity regularizer. A key design choice is applying the mask to the linear spectrogram rather than the classifier's input features, enabling phase-preserving ISTFT inversion into a listenable waveform. An optional fine-tuning stage adds a guidance term (‖M⊙X − X‖) to improve audio quality, applied selectively only when the mask already closely matches the binarized target spectrogram (via a cosine-similarity threshold) to avoid degrading faithfulness.

## Results

On the ESC50 in-domain evaluation (STFT-Mel), L-MAC achieves an Average Increase (AI) of 36.25%, Average Drop (AD) of 1.15%, Average Gain (AG) of 23.50%, Faithfulness on Spectra (FF) of 0.20, and Input Fidelity (Fid-In) of 0.42, substantially outperforming all gradient-based baselines. Under out-of-domain conditions, L-MAC's advantage widens further, reaching AI=60.63%, AD=4.82%, AG=35.85%, FF=0.39, and Fid-In=0.81. A user study with 15 participants confirms that L-MAC interpretations are preferred over L2I (MOS 59.13 vs. 55.1 on L2I showcase recordings; 64.00 vs. 50.8 on new mixtures).

## Baselines & Comparisons

L-MAC decisively outperforms all seven gradient-based baselines (Saliency, SmoothGrad, IG, GradCAM, Guided GradCAM, Guided Backprop, SHAP) on every faithfulness metric (AI, AD, AG, FF, Fid-In) in both in-domain and out-of-domain settings. The closest competitor is L2I (Parekh et al., 2022), the only other decoder-based method evaluated; however, L-MAC still achieves roughly 22× higher AI (36.25 vs. 1.63) and 1.8× higher FF (0.20 vs. 0.11) in-domain, and the gap widens to ~12× on AI out-of-domain (60.63 vs. 5.00). GradCAM is the strongest gradient-based method, reaching AI=8.50 in-domain and AI=14.12 out-of-domain, yet remains far below L-MAC. On sparseness (SPS), L-MAC (0.47) slightly edges out the best gradient-based method (Guided GradCAM, 0.44), while on complexity (COMP) L-MAC (4.71) is comparable to gradient-based methods and L2I at RT=0.2. In the user study, L-MAC is preferred over L2I by a margin of 4.03 MOS points on showcase recordings and 13.2 points on new mixtures, with fine-tuning further improving user preference.

## Relation to Prior Work

L-MAC sits at the intersection of decoder-based masking interpretability (Dabkowski & Gal 2017; Zolna et al. 2020; Phang et al. 2020) and audio-specific posthoc methods like Listen-to-Interpret (L2I, Parekh et al. 2022), which also trains a decoder on classifier latent representations. The key departure is that L-MAC applies the learned binary mask to the linear STFT spectrogram rather than to mel-spectrogram features or NMF activations, enabling phase-preserving inversion into a listenable waveform. The masking loss is adapted from prior work but uses the classifier's own prediction as the supervision label rather than ground-truth targets, and a selective fine-tuning stage with a cross-correlation threshold is introduced to improve audio quality without sacrificing faithfulness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The listenable output format addresses a genuine gap in audio interpretability: saliency maps on mel-spectrograms are not directly interpretable to non-experts, whereas a masked audio clip can be heard and intuitively understood. However, the evaluation is confined to environmental sound classification (ESC50, UrbanSound8K) with a single classifier architecture (CNN14/PANNs), leaving open whether the approach transfers to speech recognition, music tagging, or longer-form audio. The fine-tuning stage introduces a faithfulness–quality tradeoff that may not generalize cleanly, and the requirement to train a separate decoder per target classifier adds practical overhead. Broader applicability to raw-waveform models and multi-label or sequence-level tasks remains unexplored.
