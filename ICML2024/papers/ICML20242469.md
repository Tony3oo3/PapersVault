## Problem

Existing context-based offline meta-RL methods struggle with non-stationary dynamics because the context encoder conflates environment dynamics changes with behavior policy shifts, and they require pre-collecting trajectories before evaluation—impossible when dynamics change unpredictably mid-episode. No prior method learns an adaptive policy from purely offline data that can identify and respond to frequent, unknown dynamics changes on-the-fly.

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes a sliding window of H=8 most recent state-action pairs (zero-padded if history is shorter) and outputs a 2D task representation z. During testing, this window is populated exclusively by the contextual policy's own recent interactions—no pre-collection phase is needed. The contextual policy (CQL-based) then takes the current state s concatenated with z to produce an action, enabling step-by-step adaptation as the encoder continuously updates z from the evolving trajectory.

## Method

DORA trains a GRU-based context encoder with a two-term objective derived from the Information Bottleneck principle: (1) a distortion loss that maximizes a contrastive lower bound on I(z; M) using a radius basis function S(z_i, z̄_i)=exp(-‖z_i−z̄_i‖²/r) against moving-average task targets z̄_i, and (2) a debias loss that minimizes an upper bound on I(z; a) via KL divergence between p_φ(·|τ_{1:t}) and p_φ(·|τ_{1:t−1}), exploiting the fact that the newly appended action a_{t−1} carries behavior-policy information while the resulting state s_t does not. The encoder is trained jointly with a CQL contextual policy on multi-task offline datasets (200K transitions per task, collected by per-task SAC policies). The restricted RNN history length (H=8) ensures the encoder discards stale dynamics information, enabling rapid re-identification when parameters shift every 10–50 timesteps.

## Results

On six MuJoCo tasks with non-stationary dynamics (parameters changing every 50 timesteps), DORA achieves the highest average normalized return in 5 out of 6 tasks, with particularly strong margins on Pendulum-gravity (97.11 vs. 85.92 for Offline ESCP) and Cheetah-dof (91.83 vs. 80.72). In stationary IID dynamics, DORA leads in 5 of 6 tasks (e.g., 100.08 on Pendulum-gravity, 97.85 on Cheetah-dof), and in OOD dynamics it maintains a smaller performance drop (4.2%) compared to Offline ESCP (8.6%) on Hopper-gravity. The method also adapts to faster dynamics changes (every 10 timesteps) with only modest performance degradation (e.g., 64.28 vs. 71.61 on Cheetah-gravity).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DORA is compared against four baselines: Offline ESCP (an offline adaptation of the online context-sensitive policy from Luo et al., 2022), FOCAL (Li et al., 2021, distance-metric contrastive encoder), CORRO (Yuan & Lu, 2022, contrastive + generative modeling encoder), and Prompt-DT (Xu et al., 2022, Decision Transformer with task prompts). DORA outperforms all four baselines in 5 of 6 non-stationary tasks, with the largest gaps over FOCAL (e.g., 97.11 vs. 8.66 on Pendulum-gravity) and Prompt-DT (e.g., 91.83 vs. 20.24 on Cheetah-dof). The only loss is on Hopper-gravity in non-stationary dynamics, where Offline ESCP edges out DORA by ~4.5 points (54.29 vs. 49.74), though both have high variance (±25.39 and ±23.74). In stationary IID and OOD settings (Table 1), DORA similarly wins 5 of 6 tasks, with the same Hopper-gravity exception. CORRO and FOCAL consistently underperform, confirming that without explicit debiasing from the behavior policy, contrastive encoders struggle to disentangle dynamics from policy-specific features.

## Relation to Prior Work

DORA extends the offline meta-RL (OMRL) framework by incorporating the Information Bottleneck principle to jointly maximize dynamics-relevant information and minimize behavior-policy leakage in the context encoder. The contrastive lower bound on I(z;M) directly follows the proof structure of CORRO (Yuan & Lu, 2022), while the RNN encoder with restricted history length is adapted from ESCP (Luo et al., 2022). The key departure is the debiasing upper bound on I(z;a), formulated as a KL divergence between representations computed with and without the latest action, which has no direct analogue in prior OMRL work. Unlike existing OMRL methods that require pre-collecting trajectories before evaluation, DORA infers representations on-the-fly from the contextual policy's own short history, enabling adaptation to within-episode dynamics changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DORA fills a concrete gap: no prior method could learn an adaptive policy for non-stationary dynamics (dynamics changing within an episode) from purely offline data without a pre-collection phase, a setting that is natural for safety-constrained robotics. The IB-based debiasing objective is a clean, general recipe that could transfer to other representation-learning problems where a nuisance variable (here, the behavior policy) confounds the target signal. Limitations include the model-free design (no data augmentation), the 2D latent space, and evaluation restricted to six MuJoCo tasks with a single changing parameter; the authors themselves identify extending to a model-based framework for data efficiency as future work. Whether the debiasing mechanism generalizes to higher-dimensional latent spaces, multi-parameter dynamics, or real-robot settings remains open.
