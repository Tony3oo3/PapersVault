## Problem

Conventional sample selection methods for noisy labels rely on i.i.d. assumptions and GNN predictions, which fail on graph data because (1) nodes near topological class boundaries are highly informative yet entangled with mislabeled nodes due to heterogeneous neighbor aggregation, and (2) no existing difficulty measure incorporates graph topology to guide which nodes to select. This paper addresses both gaps by proposing a topology-aware selection criterion that is independent of GNN predictions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an undirected graph (adjacency matrix, node feature vectors) with noisy node labels and produces a progressively growing subset of confident nodes used for GNN training, ultimately yielding node class predictions. Unusually, the difficulty scoring (CBC) is computed from the graph structure via Personalized PageRank rather than from model predictions or losses, making it available before any GNN training begins and immune to over-smoothing effects that degrade prediction-based selection in GNNs.

## Method

TSS introduces a Class-conditional Betweenness Centrality (CBC) measure that counts how often a node is traversed by an α-discounted random walk between pairs of nodes with different (noisy) labels, using the Personalized PageRank matrix for efficient computation. This CBC score serves as a topology-based difficulty indicator: low-CBC nodes sit far from class boundaries and are easier to extract as clean, while high-CBC nodes sit near boundaries and are harder to distinguish from mislabeled ones. The training follows an easy-to-hard curriculum where a pace parameter λ progressively shifts the selection weight from low-CBC to high-CBC confident nodes, leveraging the memorization effect to extract clean labels at each stage. The CBC is robust to label noise because it only requires node pairs to have differing noisy labels rather than accurate ones. A theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) proves that TSS consistently minimizes an upper bound of the expected risk under the target clean distribution, with the bound tightening as the pace parameter increases and the error distribution deviates less from the clean distribution.

## Results

TSS achieves the best classification accuracy across all 7 datasets (CORA, CiteSeer, PubMed, WikiCS, Facebook, Physics, DBLP) and all three noise types (symmetric, pairflip, instance-dependent). On CORA with 30% symmetric noise, TSS reaches 85.02% accuracy, outperforming the next-best Me-Momentum (83.76%) by 1.26 points. On PubMed with 30% symmetric noise, TSS achieves 86.69%, surpassing NRGNN (86.19%) and CP (86.12%). On large-scale datasets with instance-dependent noise (Table 2), TSS leads by 0.6–4.4 points over the strongest baselines, e.g., 92.64% on Physics (30%) vs. CLNode's 90.96% and 66.30% on DBLP (50%) vs. NRGNN's 65.42%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSS outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and noise configuration tested. Among graph-specific denoising methods, NRGNN and PI-GNN are the closest competitors on small datasets (e.g., NRGNN achieves 86.19% vs. TSS's 86.69% on PubMed 30% symmetric), but TSS pulls ahead more decisively at higher noise rates (e.g., 81.16% vs. 77.59% on CORA 50% symmetric). The i.i.d. sample-selection baselines (Co-teaching+, Me-Momentum, MentorNet) degrade substantially on graph data, with Co-teaching+ dropping to 59.03% on CORA pairflip 40% versus TSS's 65.15%, confirming that topology-aware selection is necessary. On large-scale datasets (Table 2), TSS leads CLNode by 2.37 points on WikiCS (30%) and by 3.57 points on Physics (30%), while RCL consistently underperforms all other methods by 5–15 points. The gap between TSS and the strongest baseline widens with increasing noise rate, indicating that the CBC-guided progressive extraction becomes more critical as label corruption intensifies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sample-selection paradigm for noisy labels (Co-teaching+, Me-Momentum, MentorNet) from i.i.d. data to graph-structured data, where noise propagates along topological edges and GNN over-smoothing undermines prediction-based selection. It draws on classical Betweenness Centrality from graph theory and reformulates it as a Class-conditional Betweenness Centrality (CBC) measure via Personalized PageRank, providing a topology-based difficulty indicator that is independent of GNN predictions. The curriculum learning framework (Bengio et al., 2009) is repurposed as an 'easy-to-hard' schedule guided by CBC, positioning TSS as the first curriculum learning method explicitly designed for noisily labeled graphs, distinct from prior graph curriculum learning (CLNode, RCL) that ignores label noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Label noise on graphs is a practical concern where noise influence propagates through edges, making standard i.i.d. denoising strategies insufficient. TSS offers a principled, topology-aware alternative with a theoretical guarantee (upper bound on expected risk under the clean distribution), and the CBC measure is reusable beyond this specific task (e.g., OOD detection, graph representation learning). Limitations include reliance on computing the full PPR matrix (potentially costly on very large graphs), evaluation primarily on citation networks and a few social/academic graphs, and the authors themselves flag imbalanced and out-of-distribution graph data as open directions for future work.
