## Problem

The latent space of diffusion models is entangled and geometrically distorted: geodesics in the latent space do not map to geodesics on the data manifold, causing unwanted attribute changes during interpolation, poor inversion quality, and imprecise linear editing. Unlike GANs and VAEs, the latent space of diffusion models has received little study, partly due to the difficulty of analyzing their iterative sampling process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240684/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on 256×256 RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10), taking Gaussian noise x_T ∈ R^{256×256×3} as input for generation or a real image for DDIM inversion. The latent space is approximated as a hypersphere S^{n-1}(r) with r = √((1-ᾱ_t)n), and the isometry loss is applied to the encoder of the U-Net score model, which maps from this spherical latent space to the intermediate semantic feature space H (e.g., 8×8×512). The method is applied as a fine-tuning regularizer on pre-trained DDPM or LDM models, with the isometry loss selectively active only at timesteps t > pT (default p=0.5).

## Method

Isometric Diffusion introduces a scaled-isometry regularizer on the encoder of the diffusion model's score network, encouraging the mapping from the spherical latent space X to the semantic space H to preserve geodesics and angles. The key geometric insight is that the latent space is approximated as a hypersphere using stereographic coordinates, where the Riemannian metric G_s(z) = 4r⁴/(|z|²+r²)² · I_{n-1} is proportional to the identity, enabling efficient computation. The isometry loss L_iso = E[Tr(R²)] / E[Tr(R)]² (where R = J_f^T H J_f G^{-1}) is evaluated via Hutchinson's stochastic trace estimator with Jacobian-vector products, avoiding the intractable full Jacobian of the iterative denoising chain. The overall training objective is L_dsm + λ_iso · L_iso, applied only at high-noise timesteps where the spherical approximation holds, and the method is scale-free (unlike the path length regularizer) so it does not require EMA-based optimization.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ with DDPM, Isometric Diffusion reduces PPL from 648 to 455 (−29.8%), MCN from 497 to 180 (−63.8%), and VoR from 1.42 to 0.85 (−40.1%), while FID increases only marginally from 15.89 to 16.18. On LSUN-Bedrooms, PPL drops from 4515 to 3809 (−15.6%) and MCN from 320 to 186 (−41.9%). On CelebA-HQ with LDM, PPL improves from 439 to 397 and VoR from 1.04 to 0.54. The consistent pattern across all four datasets is substantial improvement in disentanglement metrics (PPL, mRTL, MCN, VoR, LS) at a small FID cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline comparison is against the path length regularizer (L_pl) from Karras et al. (2020), which is the standard geometric regularizer for GAN latent spaces. On CelebA-HQ with DDPM, the path length regularizer worsens FID substantially (15.89 → 20.04, +26%) while only moderately improving PPL (648 → 552, −14.8%). In contrast, Isometric Diffusion with the correct spherical Riemannian metric G_s achieves a much larger PPL reduction (648 → 455, −29.8%) while keeping FID nearly unchanged (15.89 → 16.18, +1.8%). The isometric loss with a naive Euclidean metric (G=I) performs worse than the path length regularizer on PPL (619 vs 552), confirming that the spherical geometry assumption is critical. On inversion and reconstruction (Table 3), Isometric Diffusion also outperforms the path length regularizer on all metrics: MSE 0.00599 vs 0.00667, PSNR 0.674 vs 0.614, SSIM 22.2 vs 21.7, and LPIPS 0.436 vs 0.521. The paper attributes the path length regularizer's inferiority to its EMA-based optimization, which is not scale-free and can penalize equivalent mappings unequally.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the scaled-isometry regularizer from Lee et al. (2021), originally designed for autoencoders, to the iterative denoising framework of diffusion models. It leverages the semantic latent space H identified by Kwon et al. (2023) as the target of the isometric mapping, and models the noise space as a hypersphere with stereographic coordinates to define the correct Riemannian metric. The approach is positioned as a principled generalization of the path length regularizer from StyleGAN2 (Karras et al., 2020), which the authors show is sub-optimal due to its EMA-based scale sensitivity, while their isometric loss is scale-free and coordinate-invariant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic geometric regularizer for diffusion model latent spaces, enabling smoother interpolation, more accurate DDIM inversion, and more controllable linear editing without architectural changes. The acknowledged FID–disentanglement trade-off (e.g., 15.89→16.18 FID on CelebA-HQ) remains an open question, and the method has only been validated on unconditional models at 256×256 resolution. Scaling to conditional/text-to-image diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) and video generation are identified as promising but unexplored directions, and whether the spherical approximation holds at all timesteps for very deep denoising chains remains to be verified at scale.
