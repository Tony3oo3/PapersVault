## Problem

Deep learning classification is almost universally trained by minimizing cross-entropy (i.e., KL divergence), yet no systematic framework exists for designing alternative objective functions from the broader family of f-divergences, and existing f-divergence-based classifiers require dual optimization (min-max or max-max) rather than a single training procedure. This paper asks whether a single-maximization formulation grounded in the variational representation of f-divergences can both generalize beyond cross-entropy and yield a new divergence that consistently outperforms it.

## Input / Output

For image classification, the supervised discriminator takes only the observation y (e.g., a 28×28 MNIST digit or 32×32 CIFAR image) and produces one scalar per class, from which the posterior p(X|Y) is read directly. For signal decoding, the input is a received 4-PAM symbol y_t = sgn(x_t)√|x_t| + n_t over a nonlinear channel, and the output is the decoded symbol. For continuous posterior estimation, the unsupervised architecture takes concatenated (x, y) pairs and outputs a single scalar density-ratio estimate. The key structural difference from standard classifiers is that the discriminator's optimal output is an invertible transform of the posterior (e.g., 1/(1+p) for SL), not the posterior itself.

## Method

The paper formulates classification as MAP estimation and expresses the posterior p_{X|Y}(x|y) = p_{XY}(x,y)/p_Y(y) as a density ratio learned by a discriminator network. Two complementary theorem families generate objective functions: Theorem 3.1 (top-down) plugs any f-divergence's Fenchel conjugate into a single maximization, while Theorem 4.1 (bottom-up) starts from the desired optimal discriminator output D⋆ = k(p_{X|Y}) and derives the integrand whose maximization yields it, with two degrees of freedom (the transform k and a rearrangement term g₁). Exploiting the bottom-up route, the authors observe that all known f-divergence objectives share g₁ ∝ 1/D^α · 1/(1−D)^β, and by setting β=0 with the GAN transform k(x)=1/(1+x) they derive the shifted log (SL) divergence, whose generator is f_SL(u) = −log(u+1)+log(2). Corollary 5.4 proves the SL objective has a flatter concavity near the global optimum than the GAN objective, implying a larger basin of attraction and faster convergence. The supervised architecture (one output neuron per class, input y only) reduces the KL-based objective exactly to standard cross-entropy, confirming the framework subsumes the state-of-the-art as a special case.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet, the proposed SL divergence achieves 95.43% top-1 accuracy, outperforming cross-entropy (CE) at 95.39% and GAN at 95.24%. On CIFAR-100 with DLA, SL reaches 78.65% versus 76.29% for CE and 78.63% for HD. On MNIST (small network), CE and GAN tie at 99.08% while SL achieves 99.03%. The SL divergence attains the highest accuracy in 13 out of 14 dataset-architecture combinations, with CE consistently ranking second and Pearson χ² performing worst across nearly all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares six f-divergence-based objective functions (CE/KL, RKL, HD, GAN, Pearson χ², and the proposed SL) as training losses across multiple architectures and datasets. The SL divergence wins in 13 of 14 dataset-architecture combinations, with the sole exception being CIFAR-100/ResNet where CE (78.29%) narrowly edges out SL (78.03%). CE is the strongest baseline, consistently achieving second-best accuracy, while Pearson χ² is the weakest, sometimes trailing CE by over 30 points (e.g., 37.19% vs. 72.73% on CIFAR-100/VGG). The GAN divergence performs competitively on MNIST (99.08%, tying CE) but degrades significantly on CIFAR-100 (65.66–77.34% depending on architecture). The SL advantage is most pronounced on harder datasets: +2.36% over CE on CIFAR-100/DLA and +2.17% on CIFAR-100/MobileNet, suggesting the flatter loss landscape (Corollary 5.4) provides greater benefit when the optimization problem is more challenging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the variational representation of f-divergences (Nguyen et al., 2010) and the reparameterization trick from f-GAN (Nowozin et al., 2016) to design classification objective functions, but reframes the problem through a Bayesian MAP lens using discriminative density-ratio estimation (Song & Ermon, 2020). It departs from prior f-divergence-based classification approaches (Wei & Liu, 2021; Zhong & Tandon, 2023) that require dual min-max or max-max optimization by formulating a single maximization problem that directly learns the posterior. The key conceptual departure is the bottom-up methodology (Theorem 4.1), which inverts the standard top-down f-divergence design by first specifying the optimal discriminator output and then deriving the objective function, leading to the novel shifted-log (SL) divergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, unified framework showing that cross-entropy (KL divergence) is one point in a broader family of valid classification objectives, and that the SL divergence consistently yields small but reliable accuracy gains across diverse datasets and architectures. The bottom-up design methodology offers a systematic recipe for generating new objective functions beyond those tied to known f-divergences, which could accelerate future loss function design. Open questions include whether the SL advantage persists at ImageNet scale, whether the flatter-landscape property (Corollary 5.4) translates to improved generalization in low-data or domain-shift settings, and whether the framework extends naturally to multi-task or generative classification scenarios.
