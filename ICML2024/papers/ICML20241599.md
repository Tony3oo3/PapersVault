## Problem

Current AI systems, particularly LLMs, are static models trained on fixed datasets that cannot dynamically interact with, learn from, or adapt to their environment, lacking grounded understanding of truth and causal reasoning. The paper argues that scaling these models alone cannot achieve AGI because they miss the perception–action–memory–learning loop that characterizes all known intelligence. This position paper calls for Embodied AI (E-AI) as the necessary next paradigm shift toward truly general agents.

## Input / Output

Unlike traditional AI that consumes pre-collected, curated datasets, an E-AI agent's input is generated through its own continuous interaction with the environment, making data collection an integral part of the learning pipeline rather than external to it. The agent processes multimodal sensory streams (vision, force, signal strength, language) in real time from an egocentric, first-person perspective, modeling only its immediate surroundings rather than a global scene. Outputs span both immediate reactive actions (reflex-like, model-free control) and goal-directed planned actions (model-based), alongside continuous updates to an internal generative world model and accumulated long-term knowledge.

## Method

The paper proposes a theoretical framework for E-AI grounded in cognitive architectures, identifying four essential components—perception, action, memory, and learning—that form a closed loop aligned with Friston's active inference and free energy principle. The agent maintains a hierarchical probabilistic generative model that predicts sensory observations top-down, reconciles them with bottom-up data, and acts to minimize predictive surprise, with prediction errors escalated only when irreconcilable at lower levels. Learning is framed as continual, interaction-driven adaptation rather than static training on fixed distributions, with simulators replacing curated datasets as the primary training substrate. The framework positions LLMs as one component (reasoning/language) within a broader embodied agent, analogous to how neonates arrive with inherent priors that are refined through ongoing experience, and identifies a new learning theory, noise management, and hardware innovation as the key open challenges.

## Results

This is a position paper and does not report quantitative experimental results. The authors propose a theoretical framework for Embodied AI (E-AI) grounded in Friston's active inference principle, identifying four essential components (perception, action, memory, learning) and outlining key challenges (new learning theory, noise/uncertainty management, simulators, human interaction, generalization, hardware). No benchmarks, metrics, or numerical evaluations are presented.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a position paper, this work does not include empirical comparisons against baselines. The authors qualitatively contrast LLMs (e.g., GPT-4, Gemini) and Social Media AI Recommendation Systems (SMAIs) with their proposed E-AI paradigm, arguing that LLMs are static and lack grounded interaction with the world, while SMAIs exhibit closer alignment with embodiment principles (clear survival-linked objectives, individuation through interaction, exploratory behavior). They further contrast supervised learning theory with reinforcement learning as insufficient foundations for embodied agents, and position their cognitive-architecture-based framework (perception, action, memory, learning) as a unifying alternative to purely data-driven scaling approaches. No head-to-head quantitative comparisons are provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper synthesizes and repositions ideas from embodied cognition (Lakoff & Johnson, Varela, Clark), robotics (Brooks, Pfeifer & Bongard), and active inference (Friston) into a unified call for Embodied AI as the necessary next step toward AGI. It departs from the dominant LLM/foundation-model paradigm by arguing that static, dataset-trained networks fundamentally lack the sensorimotor coupling, causal understanding, and dynamic belief revision that characterize genuine intelligence. The paper also repurposes Levin's TAME framework and the 4E cognitive science tradition to broaden E-AI beyond robotics and vision, encompassing any agent with sensorimotor coupling (e.g., intelligent network routers, social media recommendation systems).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper lays out a concrete research agenda—new learning theory for interactive data collection, hardware innovation beyond GPU clusters, bridging the sim-to-real gap, and formalizing generalization—that could reshape how the AI community approaches AGI. Its provocative claim that social media recommendation systems are actually closer to embodiment than LLMs reframes the alignment problem as procedural and evolutionary rather than propositional. However, as a position paper it offers no empirical validation, and the central thesis (embodiment is necessary for AGI) remains a hypothesis rather than a demonstrated result; the field still lacks a formal learning theory and scalable hardware to test these ideas.
