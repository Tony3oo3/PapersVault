## Problem

Optimistic learning algorithms achieve last-iterate convergence to Nash equilibrium in monotone games only under noise-free gradient feedback, while existing payoff-perturbation methods (e.g., iterative Tikhonov regularization) either provide only asymptotic guarantees or require carefully decaying perturbation strengths coupled with specific learning-rate schedules, making practical convergence slow and hyperparameter tuning difficult. This paper seeks a perturbation scheme that yields non-asymptotic last-iterate convergence rates under both full and noisy feedback without decaying the perturbation strength.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240002/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each iteration, each player receives gradient feedback of her payoff function (either exact or corrupted by zero-mean bounded-variance noise) and maintains a slingshot strategy that is re-initialized to the current strategy every T_σ iterations. The algorithm outputs a strategy profile at every iteration whose gap function (a standard measure of Nash-equilibrium proximity) is bounded by O(ln T / √T) under full feedback and O(ln T / T^{1/10}) under noisy feedback. The perturbation strength μ remains constant throughout; only the slingshot anchor moves, which is the key distinction from prior shrinking-regularization approaches.

## Method

APMD extends Mirror Descent by subtracting μ·∇_{π_i} G(π_i^t, σ_i^k) from the gradient feedback before the proximal update, where G is a divergence function (e.g., squared ℓ²-distance, KL divergence) and σ_i^k is the slingshot strategy. Rather than decaying μ over time, the slingshot strategy is periodically overwritten by the current strategy every T_σ iterations, so the perturbation magnitude μ·G(π_i^t, σ_i^k) naturally shrinks as the iterate approaches the slingshot anchor. The proof decomposes convergence into (i) exponential contraction toward a stationary point π^{μ,σ^k} within each slingshot interval, (ii) a gap-function bound linking GAP(σ^{k+1}) to the distance between consecutive slingshot profiles, and (iii) a telescoping argument over K = ⌊T/T_σ⌋ slingshot updates. The framework generalizes to arbitrary Bregman divergences and relative smoothness/strong-convexity conditions on G, and subsumes Boltzmann Q-Learning, Reward-transformed FTRL, and Mutant FTRL as special instantiations.

## Results

The paper establishes non-asymptotic last-iterate convergence rates for the gap function: O(ln T / √T) under full feedback (Theorem 4.1) and O(ln T / T^{1/10}) under noisy feedback (Theorem 4.5), using a constant learning rate and T_σ = Θ(ln T) or T_σ = Θ(T^{4/5}) slingshot update intervals respectively. Empirically, across three zero-sum polymatrix games (3BRPS, 10-action, and 50-action random payoff games), APMD with squared ℓ²-distance as both G and D_ψ achieves the fastest gap-function convergence, significantly outperforming MWU and OMWU in both full and noisy feedback settings (Figures 2 and 3). The KL-divergence and reverse-KL-divergence variants of APMD also converge faster than both baselines, though slightly slower than the ℓ² variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares APMD against two baselines: standard MWU (Bailey & Piliouras, 2018) and optimistic MWU / OMWU (Mertikopoulos et al., 2019), across three zero-sum polymatrix games under both full and noisy feedback. APMD outperforms both MWU and OMWU in all three games under full feedback, with the squared ℓ²-distance variant showing the steepest gap-function decay. Under noisy feedback (Gaussian noise with σ=0.1), MWU and OMWU exhibit markedly degraded convergence—OMWU in particular orbits around the equilibrium rather than converging—while APMD maintains its fast convergence trajectory, consistent with its theoretical robustness guarantee. The advantage of APMD is most pronounced in the 50-action random payoff game, where the baselines converge substantially more slowly. Notably, APMD's advantage persists even when using KL or reverse-KL divergences (corresponding to Reward-transformed FTRL and Mutant FTRL), confirming that the slingshot update mechanism, rather than the specific divergence choice, drives the performance gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the slingshot payoff perturbation idea introduced by Perolat et al. (2021) and refined by Abe et al. (2023) for two-player zero-sum games, generalizing it to N-player monotone games with non-asymptotic convergence guarantees. It departs from prior perturbation-based methods (Tikhonov regularization, Liu et al. 2023) that require carefully decaying the perturbation strength μ, which complicates learning rate selection; instead, APMD keeps μ constant and adaptively re-initializes the slingshot strategy σ at fixed intervals. The framework also unifies several existing algorithms—Boltzmann Q-Learning, Reward transformed FTRL, and Mutant FTRL—as special instantiations, providing a common theoretical lens for their convergence properties.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key contribution is establishing the first non-asymptotic last-iterate convergence rates (O(ln T/√T) full feedback, O(ln T/T^{1/10}) noisy feedback) for payoff-perturbed learning in monotone games, where prior perturbation methods only offered asymptotic guarantees. This is practically important because noisy feedback is the realistic setting, and optimistic methods (which achieve O(1/√T) in noise-free settings) are known to fail under noise. Open questions include whether the O(ln T/√T) rate is tight, how to handle bandit feedback, and whether the slow noisy-feedback rate (T^{-1/10}) can be improved with better perturbation schedules or noise-adaptive learning rates.
