## Problem

In federated learning, a single global model often performs unevenly across clients due to statistical heterogeneity, violating client-level fairness. Existing fairness-aware aggregation methods (AFL, q-FedAvg, TERM, PropFair) assign adaptive weights to local updates but are stateless, use fixed step sizes, and lack principled sequential decision-making guarantees. The paper identifies that these methods can be unified under Online Convex Optimization (OCO) but suffer from suboptimal designs that prevent sublinear regret guarantees.

## Input / Output

The server receives one scalar local loss per participating client each round (at most K bits total), which is far less information than the full model update. These losses are transformed via a CDF-driven mapping into bounded response values in [C₁, C₂], then used to compute a probability vector of mixing coefficients on the (K−1)-simplex. In the cross-device setting, only a random subset of clients' losses is observed per round, so the method must impute unobserved entries using a doubly robust estimator before updating coefficients for all K clients.

## Method

AAggFF replaces the stateless OMD update used by prior fair FL methods with a stateful FTRL objective that accumulates all past decision losses, paired with a negative logarithmic growth loss (strictly convex and Lipschitz when responses are bounded). Two specialized variants are provided: AAggFF-S uses Online Newton Step for the cross-silo setting (K < T), achieving O(K log T) regret with O(K²+K³) per-round cost; AAggFF-D uses an FTRL-adapted Exponentiated Gradient for the cross-device setting (K > T), achieving O(√(T log K)) regret with O(K) per-round cost and a closed-form update. For partial client observations in cross-device FL, a doubly robust estimator combined with a linearly approximated gradient provides unbiased gradient estimates for unsampled clients. The CDF-driven response transformation centers local losses relative to the round average before bounding them, ensuring the Lipschitz constant remains finite without manual tuning.

## Results

In the cross-silo setting, AAggFF-S achieves the best worst-case performance across all three datasets: 52.08 AUROC on Berka (vs. 49.44 for q-FedAvg/PropFair), 41.79 AUROC on MQP, and 85.17 Acc.5 on ISIC (vs. 77.32 for TERM), while also attaining the lowest Gini coefficient (10.16, 8.38, 2.52 respectively). In the cross-device setting, AAggFF-D achieves the best average accuracy on CelebA (91.27%), Reddit (12.95%), and SpeechCommands (76.68%), with the lowest Gini coefficient on all three (7.54, 22.59, 21.42). The accuracy parity gap (ΔAG) is consistently the smallest for AAggFF in both settings, e.g., 13.05 on ISIC vs. 15.19 for TERM, and 85.46 on SpeechCommands vs. 88.81 for q-FedAvg.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AAggFF-S outperforms all six cross-silo baselines on worst-case performance and Gini coefficient across Berka, MQP, and ISIC. Notably, q-FedAvg and FedMGDA catastrophically fail on ISIC (avg drops to ~42 vs. ~88 for others), while AAggFF-S maintains 89.76 avg with 85.17 worst-case. In the cross-device setting, AAggFF-D achieves the best average accuracy on all three datasets (CelebA 91.27%, Reddit 12.95%, SpeechCommands 76.68%) and the lowest Gini coefficient, outperforming FedAvg, q-FedAvg, TERM, FedMGDA, and PropFair. On Reddit, FedMGDA achieves a slightly smaller ΔAG (16.74 vs. 18.07) but at the cost of a much lower average (10.58 vs. 12.95), illustrating the fairness-utility trade-off. In the plug-and-play experiments (Table 7), AAggFF consistently boosts worst-case performance for FedAvg, FedProx, FedAdam, FedYogi, and FedAdagrad on both Heart and TinyImageNet without degrading average accuracy, confirming its orthogonality to the underlying optimizer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies a diverse set of fairness-aware federated learning methods (FedAvg, AFL, q-FedAvg, TERM, PropFair) into a single Online Convex Optimization framework, revealing that they are all instances of the Exponentiated Gradient algorithm with different response transformations and step sizes. It departs from prior work by replacing the stateless OMD updates used implicitly in existing methods with stateful FTRL objectives, adopting the strictly convex negative logarithmic growth as decision loss, and specializing the resulting algorithm for cross-silo (Online Newton Step) and cross-device (EG with doubly-robust estimation for partially observed responses) settings. The CDF-driven response transformation to ensure bounded responses and Lipschitz continuity is a practical addition not present in prior fair FL methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first sublinear regret guarantees for adaptive aggregation in fair FL, giving practitioners theoretical confidence that the mixing coefficients converge to near-optimal values. The plug-and-play design (no extra communication, no added local computation) makes it immediately deployable alongside existing FL algorithms like FedAdam or FedProx. Open questions remain around convergence analysis from the parameter perspective (not just mixing coefficients), robustness to malicious clients who could inflate local signals, and whether the FTRL objective can be replaced by FTPL to yield differential privacy guarantees for cross-silo settings.
