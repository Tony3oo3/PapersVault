## Problem

Existing T2I benchmarks and safety filters operate exclusively on explicit prompts where targets are directly named, leaving implicit prompts—descriptions that hint at a target without mentioning it—entirely unexplored. This gap is critical because implicit prompts can bypass keyword-based safety filters while still inducing the generation of restricted content, including celebrity likenesses and NSFW imagery, exposing a blind spot in current T2I safety mechanisms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242320/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consists of over 2,000 implicit prompts organized into three aspects (General Symbols, Celebrity Privacy, NSFW Issues) spanning twenty-plus subcategories, where each prompt describes a target through key attributes (location, style, representative works) without stating its name. For each prompt, four images are generated per T2I model to account for stochastic variation, and evaluation is aspect-specific: GPT-4V with manual verification for symbol identification, ArcFace face verification against five reference photos per celebrity, and a dual safety-checker pipeline (Stable Diffusion's built-in checker plus a multi-class NSFW classifier) for NSFW detection.

## Method

The paper introduces ImplicitBench, a benchmark of 2,000+ prompts constructed via GPT-4-assisted description generation for symbols and celebrities, and Lexica-crawled prompts filtered through NSFW keyword lists for the NSFW aspect. Six T2I models (three open-source Stable Diffusion variants and three closed-source APIs) are evaluated using three tailored methods: GPT-4V-based symbol recognition, ArcFace cosine-similarity face verification, and dual NSFW safety classification. Beyond the benchmark, the paper analyzes how celebrity fame level, gender distribution, and specific high-risk vocabulary (stylistic terminologies, body-part descriptions, ambiguous words) modulate generation accuracy and NSFW risk. Finally, three practical mitigations are proposed: training a recaption LLM on ImplicitBench pairs to sanitize input prompts, augmenting safety checkers with 'edged-unsafe' images generated by implicit prompts, and deploying dynamic contextual safety filters with user-intent clarification.

## Results

On General Symbols (Table 2), DALL-E 3 achieves the highest overall implicit-prompt accuracy at 89.03%, followed by Midjourney (84.90%), DALL-E 2 (83.65%), SD XL (83.54%), SD v2.1 (83.33%), and SD v1.5 (81.56%); all models struggle most on the Logo subcategory (35.84%–61.11%). On NSFW safety (Table 3), every model generates unsafe images from implicit prompts: SD v1-5 is worst at 33.38% overall unsafe rate, followed by SD XL (30.53%), SD v2-1 (30.05%), Midjourney (27.37%), DALL-E 3 (17.75%), and DALL-E 2 (13.27%). For Celebrity Privacy (Table 5), Midjourney and SD XL show the highest success rates for high-fame celebrities (e.g., 0.80 and 0.78 for actors/politicians), while DALL-E 2 and DALL-E 3 are substantially lower (0.33–0.51) due to their privacy policies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates six T2I models (SD v1.5, SD v2.1, SD XL, Midjourney, DALL-E 2, DALL-E 3) on the newly proposed ImplicitBench rather than proposing a new generation method. On General Symbols, DALL-E 3 leads overall (89.03%) by a clear margin over Midjourney (84.90%) and the SD family (81.56–83.54%), with its advantage most pronounced on Logo (61.11% vs. 35.84–55.55%) and Clothing (98.69%). On NSFW safety, DALL-E 2 is the safest model at 13.27% overall unsafe rate, followed by DALL-E 3 (17.75%) and Midjourney (27.37%), while all three SD variants are worst (30.05–33.38%), indicating that open-source models are significantly more vulnerable to implicit NSFW bypass. On Celebrity Privacy, Midjourney and SD XL outperform DALL-E 2 and DALL-E 3 (e.g., 0.80 vs. 0.33 for actors), which the authors attribute to DALL-E's stricter privacy policies blocking celebrity generation. Across all three aspects, closed-source APIs (DALL-E 3, Midjourney) generally show stronger implicit-prompt comprehension, but no model is immune to safety bypass via implicit prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by identifying 'implicit prompts'—descriptions that reference a target without naming it—as a distinct evaluation axis entirely absent from prior T2I benchmarks (DrawBench, HE-T2I, T2I-CompBench), which exclusively assess explicit prompts. It repurposes existing evaluation tools (ArcFace for celebrity verification, GPT-4V for symbol recognition, Stable Diffusion's safety checker and Qu et al.'s NSFW classifier for content safety) into a unified framework for probing T2I models on indirect descriptions. The safety-filter paradigm it critiques (keyword-based rejection of explicit NSFW or celebrity names) is well established in deployed systems, but the paper demonstrates this paradigm is structurally blind to implicit formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a concrete and systematic vulnerability: keyword-based safety filters in all six evaluated T2I models are bypassed by implicit prompts, with NSFW generation rates of 13–33% even in the most heavily guarded closed-source systems. This raises urgent open questions about how to build safety mechanisms that understand semantic intent rather than surface keywords, and how to balance the creative benefits of implicit prompting (lowering the skill barrier for users) against privacy and content-safety risks. The finding that celebrity fame positively correlates with generation accuracy suggests that the most publicly visible individuals face the greatest risk of AI-generated misinformation, a concern current privacy policies do not address. Limitations include reliance on GPT-4V (with known hallucination issues) for symbol evaluation and the subjective boundary between 'implicit' and 'explicit' that the authors acknowledge but do not fully formalize.
