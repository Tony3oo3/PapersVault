## Problem

The ℓ0-constrained mean-CVaR portfolio optimization problem is NP-hard, and existing exact solutions via combinatorial branch-and-bound methods visit tens of thousands of nodes and require commercial optimizers like Gurobi, making them impractical for real-world portfolio management. No efficient approach existed that could approximate the ℓ0-constrained model to arbitrary accuracy while avoiding combinatorial search.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241225/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a T×N sample return matrix (monthly price relatives from Kenneth French Data Library, with T=60 as the rolling window), a sparsity level m (tested at 10, 15, 20), a confidence level c=0.99, and an expected return level ρ=0.02. It outputs a sparse portfolio weight vector w∈Δ (simplex-constrained, no short-selling) with at most m nonzero components. A distinctive property is 'autonomous sparsity': when m is adjusted, over 89% of previously selected assets remain in the new pool, reducing transaction costs and managerial burden.

## Method

The core idea converts the ℓ0 constraint into an indicator function ι_m and replaces it with a tailed approximation ι̃_{m,γ} that imposes a quadratic penalty (1/2γ)·Σw_j² only on components beyond the m largest in absolute value, making the approximation arbitrarily tight as γ→0. The model is then reformulated as a two-variable (v, y) optimization where y is thresholded to the m-largest components of v via a hard-thresholding operator S_m, enabling decomposition into two subproblems. A Proximal Alternating Linearized Minimization (PALM) algorithm alternates between a v-update (solved via a nested Fixed-Point Proximity Algorithm for the linear inequality constraints) and a y-update (closed-form hard thresholding), with convergence guaranteed by Lipschitz continuity of the partial gradients. Theoretical analysis (Theorem 3.3) proves the gap between the ASMCVaR optimal value and the true ℓ0-constrained optimum is bounded by √(2L̃³γ), and Theorem 3.6 establishes equivalence of solutions between the single-variable and two-variable formulations.

## Results

ASMCVaR (m=10) achieves the highest final cumulative wealth across all 6 benchmark datasets, reaching 973.23 (FF25), 122.82 (FF25EU), 1122.76 (FF32), 758.66 (FF49), 966.32 (FF100), and 783.47 (FF100MEOP) — roughly 10× the CW of Mean-CVaR on FF100 (966.32 vs. 70.31). It also attains the highest Sharpe ratio on every dataset (0.23–0.28) and the highest positive alpha factor (0.0020–0.0029) with all p-values below 1%, confirming statistically significant excess returns over the market. The autonomous sparsity property is validated: over 89% of selected assets overlap when adjusting pool size from m=10 to m=15 or m=15 to m=20.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASMCVaR dominates all 8 competing methods and the 1/N baseline on every dataset across all three primary metrics (CW, alpha, Sharpe ratio). The closest non-sparse competitors, Mean-CVaR and Mean-CVaR-ℓ₁, achieve CW of 70.31 and 102.11 on FF100 respectively, while ASMCVaR (m=10) reaches 966.32 — a roughly 9.5× and 9.4× improvement. Among sparse methods, SSMP (Brodie et al., 2009) is the strongest but still trails ASMCVaR by a wide margin (e.g., 10.09 vs. 966.32 on FF100); the ℓ₁-based methods SSPO, SPOLC, S1–S3, and MT-CVaR all collapse to near-zero or negative CW on larger datasets (FF100, FF100MEOP), indicating severe overfitting. ASMCVaR (m=10) also achieves the highest alpha (0.0022–0.0029) with p-values all below 0.01, whereas most baselines show negative or insignificant alpha. Under proportional transaction costs up to 0.5%, ASMCVaR maintains its lead over all competitors including the low-turnover 1/N strategy, confirming practical robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mean-CVaR portfolio optimization framework of Rockafellar & Uryasev (2000) by addressing the NP-hard ℓ₀ sparsity constraint through a fundamentally different lens than prior combinatorial approaches (Kobayashi et al., 2021; Bertsimas & Cory-Wright, 2022), which rely on branch-and-bound with commercial solvers. The key conceptual departure is the 'tailed approximation' of the ℓ₀ indicator function, which replaces the nonconvex combinatorial structure with a smooth quadratic penalty on the tail components, enabling solution via the PALM algorithm (Bolte et al., 2014) with a nested FPPA (Micchelli et al., 2011). Unlike ℓ₁ regularization methods (Brodie et al., 2009; Lai et al., 2018) that only approximately control sparsity, this approach provides a theoretically guaranteed approximation to arbitrary accuracy as the parameter γ→0.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, solver-free alternative to the computationally expensive branch-and-bound methods for exact ℓ₀-constrained portfolio optimization, with a provable approximation guarantee. The 'autonomous sparsity' property—where 89%+ of selected assets persist when adjusting pool size—directly reduces transaction costs and managerial burden in real-world fund management. Open questions include whether the tailed approximation technique generalizes cleanly to other nonconvex constraints, whether momentum-based acceleration can further reduce the iteration count, and whether the approach scales to intraday or high-frequency data where the NP-hardness becomes even more acute.
