## Problem

Existing automated jailbreaking attacks on aligned LMs are either slow gradient-based methods (GCG, AutoDAN-2) that take over an hour per prompt and produce gibberish tokens, or black-box methods (AutoDAN-1, PAIR) that require expensive GPT-4 API access. The paper asks whether a gradient-free attack can achieve high jailbreak success rates within a single GPU minute while keeping adversarial prompts readable, and whether such a fast attack can also be repurposed for hallucination elicitation and membership inference.

## Input / Output

The method takes a tokenized user prompt embedded in a chat template (system prompt ⊕ user prompt ⊕ system prompt) and a target autoregressive LM, then appends an adversarial suffix of length L (default 40 tokens) to the user prompt. The output is a complete adversarial prompt whose suffix tokens are drawn from the LM's own next-token distribution, ensuring natural readability. Tunable beam parameters k₁ and k₂ (set equal by default to k) control the speed–readability–success-rate tradeoff, with k=15 yielding ~98% ASR in 2.65 minutes and k=5 achieving 66% ASR in 10 seconds on Vicuna-7B.

## Method

BEAST performs a gradient-free beam search over adversarial suffix tokens: at each of L iterations it maintains k₁ beam candidates, expands each by multinomially sampling k₂ next tokens from the target LM's own probability distribution (implicitly preserving readability without perplexity regularization), scores all k₁×k₂ candidates with an application-specific adversarial objective ℒ, and retains the k₁ lowest-scoring candidates. The objective is the perplexity of a target harmful string for targeted jailbreaking, the negative perplexity of the model's autoregressively sampled output for untargeted hallucination elicitation, and the negative prompt perplexity for membership inference. For universal suffixes targeting multiple prompts simultaneously, BEAST replaces the per-beam sampling distribution with a softmax over the summed logits across all target prompts, and scores candidates by the sum of individual losses. This design makes BEAST 25–65× faster than gradient-based baselines while producing readable adversarial tokens that transfer to unseen prompts and unseen models (e.g., GPT-4-Turbo).

## Results

BEAST achieves 89% attack success rate (ASR) on Vicuna-7B-v1.5 within one GPU minute, compared to 58% for the best gradient-based baseline (GCG) and 20% for AutoDAN-2. In two GPU minutes, BEAST reaches 96% ASR on Vicuna-7B. For hallucination elicitation, BEAST causes Vicuna-7B to produce 14.67% more incorrect outputs than clean prompts, with 22% of responses being irrelevant to the original question. For membership inference, BEAST boosts AUROC by up to 4.1 percentage points (e.g., Min-k% on OPT-2.7B: 54.3 → 58.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BEAST consistently outperforms all four jailbreaking baselines in the one-GPU-minute setting. On Vicuna-7B, BEAST achieves 89% ASR versus 58% for GCG (Zou et al., 2023), 20% for AutoDAN-2 (Zhu et al., 2023), 46% for PAIR (Chao et al., 2023), and 10% for AutoDAN-1 (Liu et al., 2023b). The gap is most dramatic against the gradient-based methods: AutoDAN-2 requires over an hour per prompt to reach 70% ASR, while BEAST reaches 89% in under a minute. Against the MIA baselines, BEAST-enhanced PPL improves AUROC from 57.1 to 57.4 on OPT-2.7B (Yeom et al., 2018), and BEAST-enhanced Min-k% improves from 54.3 to 58.4 on the same model (Shi et al., 2023), with the largest gain being +9.5 points on Pythia-6.9B (50.8 → 60.3). BEAST also demonstrates transferability that none of the baselines report: adversarial suffixes optimized on Vicuna-7B/13B transfer to GPT-4-Turbo at 12% ASR and GPT-3.5-Turbo at 40% ASR.

## Relation to Prior Work

BEAST departs from the dominant gradient-based jailbreaking methods (GCG, AutoDAN-2) and the GPT-4-dependent black-box approaches (AutoDAN-1, PAIR) by repurposing beam search — a standard NLP decoding technique — as a gradient-free adversarial optimization loop. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of discrete combinatorial search and adversarial ML: rather than optimizing continuous token embeddings or querying an external LLM, it multinomially samples candidate suffix tokens from the target model's own distribution and prunes via an adversarial objective. The paper also extends the application scope beyond jailbreaking to untargeted hallucination elicitation and membership-inference enhancement, tasks for which no prior fast adversarial procedure existed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 25–65× speedup over gradient-based baselines makes systematic red-teaming and large-scale vulnerability auditing feasible on a single GPU, which is practically important for the LM security community. The hallucination-elicitation result (≈15% more incorrect outputs, 22% irrelevant responses) is the first demonstration that a scalable adversarial procedure can degrade LM *usefulness* rather than just safety, opening a new axis of attack. Limitations temper the impact: LLaMA-2 remains largely resistant (9% ASR), the MIA AUROC boost is modest (~4%), and the hallucination effect was validated on a single model (Vicuna-7B) with a small human study. Open questions include whether perplexity-filter defenses can be made robust to BEAST's readable suffixes, and whether the hallucination effect generalizes to larger or more recent models.
