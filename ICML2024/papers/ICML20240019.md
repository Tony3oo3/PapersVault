## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks like PGD were designed for image classification and, when applied to pixel-wise prediction tasks (semantic segmentation, optical flow, image restoration), only partially fool the network because they sum pixel-wise losses without accounting for which pixels are already mispredicted. SegPGD addressed this for segmentation but relies on a discrete argmax-based binary decision that is non-differentiable, limits applicability to classification-only tasks, and introduces optimization instability. The paper seeks a unified, smooth, and fully differentiable attack that balances perturbation across the entire image domain and generalizes to both classification and regression pixel-wise tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a clean RGB image, a ground-truth or target label (one-hot encoded for segmentation, continuous values for regression tasks like optical flow or disparity), and full white-box access to the target model's forward pass and gradients. It produces an adversarial example whose perturbation is constrained to an epsilon-ball under either l_infinity or l_2 norm, initialized with uniform random noise in [-epsilon, +epsilon]. The attack is task-agnostic: the same update rule applies whether the downstream task is segmentation (20 or 150 classes), optical flow (2D vector field), or image restoration (pixel-wise regression to a clean reference).

## Method

CosPGD modifies the standard PGD gradient update by scaling each pixel's loss with the cosine similarity between the softmax-normalized prediction and the target at that location. For untargeted attacks, pixels whose predictions are closer to the ground truth receive a higher loss weight (cosine similarity approaching 1), while already-mispredicted pixels are downweighted; for targeted attacks, the scaling is inverted (1 − cos) so that pixels further from the target are prioritized. This continuous, differentiable scaling replaces SegPGD's discrete argmax-based binary partition, yielding smooth gradient directions across iterations (formally guaranteed by a Lipschitz-continuity proposition on the alignment score). The algorithm follows the standard PGD loop—sign-gradient step, epsilon-ball projection, and valid-range clipping—with the only change being the per-pixel cosine-alignment factor in the loss, making it a drop-in replacement that requires no task-specific hyperparameters beyond the standard step size alpha and perturbation budget epsilon.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2012 (DeepLabV3, ℓ∞ ε=8/255, 100 iterations), CosPGD reduces mIoU to ~0%, compared to 2.69% for SegPGD and 6.79% for PGD. On ADE20K (SegFormer, ε=8/255), CosPGD reaches 0.00% mIoU at just 10 iterations, while SegPGD remains at 0.33% even after 100 iterations. For targeted optical flow attacks on RAFT (KITTI 2015, 40 iterations), CosPGD achieves an end-point error of 4.84 versus 7.32 for PGD. On image deblurring (NAFNet, GoPro), CosPGD produces significantly lower PSNR/SSIM than both PGD and SegPGD, indicating a stronger attack.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CosPGD decisively outperforms PGD (Kurakin et al., 2017) across all evaluated tasks: on PASCAL VOC 2012 (DeepLabV3), CosPGD drives mIoU to ~0% versus 6.79% for PGD at 100 iterations; on targeted optical flow (RAFT, KITTI 2015), CosPGD achieves epe 4.84 versus 7.32 for PGD at 40 iterations. Against SegPGD (Gu et al., 2022), CosPGD is substantially stronger on semantic segmentation—reaching 0.00% mIoU at 10 iterations on ADE20K (SegFormer) where SegPGD still reports 0.33% at 100 iterations—and also superior in transfer settings (Table 4: 1.21% vs 1.77% mIoU on PSPNet). Against PCFA (Schmalfuss et al., 2022b), a dedicated optical flow attack, CosPGD is compared in Appendix C.3 across multiple architectures (RAFT, PWCNet, GMA, SpyNet). Against the Segmentation Ensemble Attack (SEA) from Croce et al. (2023) on Robust UPerNet, CosPGD is competitive: at ε=4/255 with 300 iterations, SegPGD is marginally stronger (≤0.25% mAcc gap), but at lower iteration counts (10–50), CosPGD is slightly stronger, and at ε≥8/255 CosPGD is consistently the strongest single attack in the ensemble.

## Relation to Prior Work

CosPGD is a direct modification of the PGD update rule (Kurakin et al., 2017), replacing the uniform sum of pixel-wise losses with a cosine-alignment-scaled version. It extends SegPGD (Gu et al., 2022), which was the first to exploit pixel-wise information for adversarial attacks on semantic segmentation but relied on a discrete argmax-based binary decision to weight the loss. The key conceptual departure is substituting this non-differentiable, task-specific (classification-only) scaling with a continuous cosine similarity between the normalized prediction and target, yielding a smooth, fully differentiable objective that generalizes to regression tasks like optical flow and image restoration. The paper also positions itself against PCFA (Schmalfuss et al., 2022b), a dedicated optical-flow attack, showing CosPGD subsumes it within a unified framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CosPGD provides a single, principled attack algorithm that unifies adversarial robustness evaluation across diverse pixel-wise prediction tasks (segmentation, optical flow, image restoration), removing the need for task-specific attack formulations. The demonstrated 10× efficiency gain (e.g., mIoU → 0 in 10 iterations vs. 100 for SegPGD on ADE20K) makes white-box robustness auditing substantially cheaper, which is practically important given the computational cost of iterative attacks. A key limitation is that non-targeted attacks still require ground-truth labels, restricting evaluation on test sets without annotations; the authors note that targeted usage mitigates this but leaves the non-targeted test-set setting open. Future work could explore whether the cosine-alignment principle extends to other structured prediction tasks or to black-box settings.
