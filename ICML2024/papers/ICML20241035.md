## Problem

Existing certified robustness for GNNs against graph injection attacks (GIA) only provides sample-wise certificates that verify each target node independently, assuming the attacker can craft a different perturbed graph per node. This is overly pessimistic because a real attacker can only produce a single perturbed graph to simultaneously disrupt all target nodes, making sample-wise certificates yield near-zero certified ratios at moderate attack budgets. No collective certificate (certifying a set of nodes under a shared perturbation) existed for GIA, as prior collective schemes for graph modification attacks assume a fixed receptive field that GIA inherently expands.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained message-passing GNN (GCN or GAT), the original graph adjacency matrix A₀, smoothing parameters (p_e for edge deletion, p_n for node deletion), an attack budget specifying ρ injected nodes each with at most τ edges, and a target node set T. The smoothed classifier probabilities are estimated via Monte Carlo (N=100,000 samples) with Clopper-Pearson confidence intervals and Bonferroni correction at α=0.01. The output is the certified ratio (|T|−M*)/|T|, where M* is the LP-optimal upper bound on the number of non-robust target nodes, computed by solving the relaxed LP with MOSEK through CVXPY.

## Method

The paper formulates collective certification as a worst-case budget allocation problem: the attacker allocates ρ injected nodes (each with ≤τ edges) to maximize the number of target nodes whose message-interference probability p(E_v) exceeds half the classification gap. For k=2 GNNs, this yields a binary quadratic constrained linear program (BQCLP) over the injected-to-existing adjacency matrix A₁ and injected-to-injected matrix A₂. Two LP relaxations are proposed: Collective-LP1 uses standard bilinear linearization introducing O(ρ²|T|) auxiliary variables, while Collective-LP2 exploits the structure of the quadratic term A₁ᵀA₂ᵀ1_ρ by pre-computing z=A₂ᵀ1_ρ, reducing the auxiliary variables to O(ρ|T|) and yielding a tighter, faster formulation. The LP relaxation is sound because it enlarges the feasible region, overestimating the number of non-robust nodes and thus guaranteeing that all remaining certified nodes are truly robust. Experiments on Cora-ML, Citeseer, and PubMed show Collective-LP2 improves certified ratios from 0% to over 80% at ρ=5% of graph size while solving in under 1 minute.

## Results

On Citeseer (τ=4, p_e=0.9, p_n=0.8), Collective-LP2 achieves a certified ratio of 81.2% at ρ=140, compared to 0.0% for the sample-wise baseline. On Cora-ML (τ=6, p_e=0.9, p_n=0.8), Collective-LP2 reaches 72.6% at ρ=140 versus 0.0% for sample-wise. The LP relaxation introduces a small integrality gap (~5% certified ratio drop) relative to the exact BQCLP solution, and at very small ρ (e.g., ρ=20), sample-wise can slightly outperform collective. Computationally, Collective-LP2 solves the certification problem in approximately 1 minute on Citeseer even at ρ=140.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the sample-wise certificate from Lai et al. (2023), which verifies each target node independently under a worst-case per-node perturbation. At large attack budgets (ρ ≥ 50), both collective methods dramatically outperform sample-wise: on Citeseer at ρ=140, Collective-LP2 certifies 81.2% of nodes while sample-wise certifies 0.0%, and on Cora-ML at ρ=140, Collective-LP2 certifies 72.6% versus 0.0%. At small ρ (e.g., ρ=20), sample-wise achieves 100% certified ratio while collective methods achieve 95–97%, a gap attributed to the LP integrality gap. Between the two collective variants, Collective-LP2 consistently matches or exceeds Collective-LP1 (e.g., 72.6% vs. 60.0% on Cora-ML at ρ=140) while also running significantly faster, particularly as ρ grows. The authors note that in practice the two certificates can be trivially combined (sharing the same smoothed model) to achieve the best of both regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of certified-robustness research: (1) sample-wise certificates for graph injection attacks (GIA) via node-aware bi-smoothing (Lai et al., 2023), and (2) collective certificates for graph modification attacks (GMA) that exploit interdependence among target nodes (Schuchardt et al., 2020). The key conceptual gap it fills is that the existing collective scheme for GMA assumes a fixed receptive field, which breaks under GIA because injected nodes expand the receptive field. The paper bridges this by formulating the collective GIA certification as a binary quadratic constrained linear program (BQCLP) and introducing two LP relaxations — a standard one and a customized one that reduces the number of auxiliary variables from O(ρ²|T|) to O(ρ|T|).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result is practically significant: on Citeseer with ρ = 5% of graph size, the certified ratio jumps from 0.0% (sample-wise) to 81.2% (Collective-LP2) while remaining solvable in ~1 minute, making provable defense against GIA far more useful. However, the LP relaxation introduces an integrality gap that degrades performance for small attack budgets (ρ ≤ 20), where sample-wise certificates can still be competitive; the authors acknowledge this and suggest combining both certificates. Open questions include extending the formulation to deeper GNNs (k > 2, which would require polynomial rather than quadratic constraints) and exploring tighter relaxations such as SDP to close the integrality gap.
