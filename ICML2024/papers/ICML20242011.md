## Problem

Existing causal discovery methods for time series data assume a single structural causal model (SCM) governs the entire dataset, but real-world data is often heterogeneous, arising from multiple distinct causal mechanisms (e.g., different gene regulatory networks across cell types, or varying stock-market interactions over time). This paper addresses the underexplored problem of discovering a mixture of unknown SCMs from multivariate time series, where both the causal graphs and the sample-to-component assignments are entirely unknown.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242011/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N multivariate time series samples (D variables × T timesteps) and a hyperparameter K specifying the number of mixture components (often set to 2K* to test robustness to over-specification), along with a time lag L. It produces K temporal adjacency matrices of size (L+1)×D×D encoding both lagged and instantaneous causal edges, the functional parameters (structural equations and noise models) for each graph, and a per-sample categorical membership assignment indicating which SCM generated each time series. The DAGs are learned as discrete Bernoulli variables via Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization, while the structural equations and noise models are continuous parameters optimized jointly.

## Method

MCD formulates causal discovery as a variational inference problem over a mixture of K SCMs, deriving an ELBO that jointly optimizes the causal graphs, structural equations, and per-sample mixing probabilities in an end-to-end fashion. The DAG adjacency matrices are parameterized as products of independent Bernoulli distributions sampled via Gumbel-Softmax, while mixing probabilities are learnable per-sample categorical distributions. Two variants are provided: MCD-Linear uses a Hadamard-product linear model with independent Gaussian noise, and MCD-Nonlinear builds on Rhino's architecture, using shared MLPs with per-model embeddings for structural equations and conditional spline flows for history-dependent noise. Theoretically, the paper proves a necessary-and-sufficient identifiability condition for mixtures of linear Gaussian SVARs and a sufficient condition (existence of K 'representative points' with dominant likelihood under one component) for general identifiable SCMs, and establishes the exact gap between the ELBO and true log-likelihood as a sum of KL divergences.

## Results

On Netsim-mixture, MCD-Nonlinear achieves AUROC 0.94±0.03 and F1 0.69±0.08, outperforming all baselines (best baseline: DYNOTEARS-s at AUROC 0.85). On DREAM3, all methods perform poorly due to high dimensionality and limited samples, but MCD-Nonlinear still leads with AUROC 0.58±0.01 and F1 0.10±0.01. MCD-Nonlinear also achieves strong clustering accuracy of 86.8±26.3% on Netsim-mixture and 95.6±4.8% on DREAM3, correctly grouping samples by their underlying causal model. On synthetic nonlinear data, MCD-Nonlinear outperforms all baselines that do not use ground-truth membership information, and approaches the performance of Rhino-g (which is given the true cluster assignments).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCD-Nonlinear clearly outperforms Rhino (the strongest single-graph baseline) on Netsim-mixture, improving AUROC from 0.84 to 0.94 and F1 from 0.62 to 0.69, demonstrating the benefit of modeling heterogeneity even when it arises from simple node permutations. Against PCMCI+ variants, MCD-Nonlinear gains 0.10–0.23 in AUROC over the best PCMCI+ configuration (PCMCI+-s at 0.82), and against DYNOTEARS it gains 0.09 in AUROC while substantially improving F1 (0.69 vs. 0.28–0.46). On DREAM3, where all methods struggle due to 100 nodes and only 46 trajectories per graph, MCD-Nonlinear edges out Rhino by 0.01 AUROC and 0.02 F1, and achieves a remarkable 95.6% clustering accuracy. On synthetic nonlinear data, MCD-Nonlinear surpasses all baselines that lack ground-truth membership (Rhino, PCMCI+, DYNOTEARS, VARLiNGAM) and approaches Rhino-g, which is given the true cluster assignments as additional information. MCD-Linear underperforms MCD-Nonlinear on both real datasets, learning only a single mode, confirming that the nonlinear variant is necessary for these heterogeneous settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

MCD builds directly on Rhino (Gong et al., 2022) for its nonlinear SCM component, reusing Rhino's architecture of shared MLPs, per-model embeddings, and conditional spline flows for history-dependent noise, but wraps it in a variational inference framework that simultaneously infers K causal graphs and per-sample mixture membership. It departs from the dominant temporal causal discovery methods (PCMCI+, DYNOTEARS, VARLiNGAM, Rhino) which all assume a single causal model governs the entire dataset, and extends the mixture-of-DAGs literature (Thiesson et al., 1998; Saeed et al., 2020; Markham et al., 2022) from independent data to time series with lagged and instantaneous effects. The Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization for DAG sampling connects to the NOTEARS/DYNOTEARS line of continuous structure learning, while the ELBO formulation draws on standard variational inference but applied to a novel discrete-continuous hybrid latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Heterogeneous data is the norm in domains like genomics, finance, and climate science, yet prior temporal causal discovery methods implicitly assume a single causal mechanism, risking oversimplification. MCD provides the first end-to-end framework with theoretical identifiability guarantees for mixtures of SCMs from time series, and its robustness to over-specification of K (performing better with more components than ground truth) makes it practical when the true number of regimes is unknown. Key limitations include the causal sufficiency assumption (no latent confounders), the requirement that K be set as a hyperparameter, and the observation that when underlying graphs are highly similar (as in the Netsim non-mixture setup), single-graph baselines can outperform the mixture model due to sample complexity. Open questions include extending to latent confounders, non-stationarity within a component, and scaling to very large K or D.
