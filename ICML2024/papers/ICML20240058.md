## Problem

Exploration in reinforcement learning with function approximation remains computationally intractable outside simple tabular or linear settings. Prior theoretical exploration objectives are either too general to admit efficient planning and exploration algorithms, or too narrow to apply to practical nonlinear MDPs without losing theoretical fidelity. The paper asks whether a single exploration objective can simultaneously provide intrinsic complexity control, reduce to standard policy optimization for planning, and yield efficient online exploration algorithms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240058/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For planning, the method takes a known MDP M, a policy class Π, and (for the tighter relaxation) a covering distribution μ with bounded L∞-concentrability; for online exploration it additionally takes a model class M and an offline estimation oracle. The output is a per-layer policy cover p_h ∈ Δ(Π) — a distribution over randomized non-stationary policies — whose mixture occupancy d_h^{M,p} collectively covers the state space in an average-case (L1) sense relative to every policy in Π. The cover is structured as a uniform mixture of at most ε^{-1} individual policies, each obtained by solving a standard reward-driven policy optimization subproblem.

## Method

The paper introduces the L1-Coverage objective Ψ_{h,ε}^M(p), which measures the worst-case (over π ∈ Π) expected ratio of a policy's occupancy to the ensemble's occupancy, discounted by an ε-regularization term that removes reachability assumptions. The key algorithmic insight is that optimizing this objective reduces to iteratively solving standard reward-driven policy optimization problems with a specific reward function r_h^t(x,a) = μ(x,a)/(Σ_{i<t} d_h^{M,π^i}(x,a) + C_∞·μ(x,a)), making it compatible with off-the-shelf planners like PPO or DQN. Two relaxations are provided: an L∞-Coverability relaxation (tighter, O(C_∞ log(1/ε)) bound, requires μ) and a Pushforward Coverability relaxation (looser, O(|A|·C_push log(1/ε)) bound, no μ needed). For online exploration, the CODEX algorithm applies a plug-in paradigm: at each episode, estimate the model via an estimation oracle, compute a (C,ε)-approximate policy cover for the estimated model using the planning relaxations, and collect a trajectory from the resulting cover, yielding the first computationally and statistically efficient reward-free exploration guarantees under L1-Coverability.

## Results

On MountainCar, L1-Coverage (Algorithm 1) discovers approximately twice as many unique discretized states as both MaxEnt and uniform exploration over 20 epochs, and is the only method that explores both hills in the environment. On Pendulum, L1-Coverage and MaxEnt achieve comparable coverage, both visiting all discretized states. In terms of the L1-Coverability objective value, Algorithm 1 attains the smallest values across all environments, confirming a tighter policy cover than the baselines despite MaxEnt achieving slightly higher entropy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares L1-Coverage (Algorithm 1) against two baselines: MaxEnt (Hazan et al., 2019) and uniform random exploration. On MountainCar, L1-Coverage outperforms MaxEnt by a factor of two in the number of unique discretized states visited, and uniquely explores both hills of the environment while MaxEnt and uniform exploration fail to do so. On Pendulum, L1-Coverage and MaxEnt achieve similar performance, both covering the full discretized state space. Notably, MaxEnt achieves slightly higher entropy than L1-Coverage on MountainCar despite visiting fewer states, suggesting entropy is an imperfect proxy for exploration quality. The L1-Coverability objective value (the paper's own metric) is consistently lowest for L1-Coverage across both environments, indicating it produces the tightest policy cover. The authors also note that their method is robust to optimization errors (REINFORCE is not near-optimal), the choice of covering distribution μ, and hyperparameters, working out-of-the-box with the same settings as the MaxEnt implementation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces L1-Coverage as a new exploration objective that generalizes prior optimal-design-based objectives (Wagenmaker et al., 2022; Mhammedi et al., 2023a) from tabular and linear MDPs to general nonlinear function approximation settings. It builds directly on the Decision-Estimation Coefficient (DEC) framework of Foster et al. (2021; 2023) for the online exploration guarantees, and on L∞-Coverability from Xie et al. (2023) as a structural parameter that bounds the new L1-Coverability via a non-trivial minimax argument. The pushforward relaxation draws on pushforward concentrability from offline RL (Xie & Jiang, 2021). The key departure from prior work is that L1-Coverage is both general enough to subsume Block and Low-Rank MDPs and tractable enough to be optimized via standard reward-driven policy optimization, filling a gap where prior objectives were either too general (computationally intractable) or too narrow (restricted to tabular/linear settings).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide computationally efficient algorithms for reward-free exploration under coverability, bridging a critical gap between statistical efficiency (which prior coverability-based results achieved) and computational efficiency (which was previously unavailable outside simple settings). The plug-in CODEX algorithm is practical in that it reduces exploration to off-the-shelf policy optimization, making it straightforward to integrate with existing RL pipelines. However, the empirical validation is limited to two simple continuous-control environments (MountainCar, Pendulum) with small discretizations and a basic REINFORCE planner, leaving open whether the approach scales to high-dimensional, realistic domains. Open questions include the weakest representation conditions under which coverability suffices, and whether the objective can be further generalized while retaining computational tractability.
