## Problem

Causal dynamics models (CDMs) have shown promise in reinforcement learning but are confined to small-scale environments because causal discovery scales as O(n³) in the number of variables and requires large sample sizes to recover joint distributions. Large-scale object-oriented environments with many objects across multiple classes remain unaddressed, as the combinatorial explosion of potential causal edges makes standard CDMs computationally infeasible and sample-inefficient.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242376/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires a pre-specified OOMDP structure (objects partitioned into classes with named fields) as prior knowledge, along with offline transition data of (state, action, next-state) tuples. It produces an Object-Oriented Causal Graph (OOCG) encoding class-level local and global causalities, next-state probability estimates via shared field predictors, and—when combined with MPC—planning action sequences. A key design constraint is that the model must handle a varying number of object instances per class at test time, which is achieved through attention-based aggregation rather than fixed-size inputs.

## Method

OOCDM introduces the Object-Oriented Causal Graph (OOCG), a bipartite causal graph where edges are defined at the class level: a local causality C_k.U → V' applies to all instances of class C_k, and a global causality C_l.U → C_k.V' applies to all cross-class object pairs. Under a causation symmetry assumption (objects of the same class are interchangeable in their effects on others), the paper proves the ground-truth causal graph is exactly an OOCG, reducing the search space from O(n²) individual edges to O(m²) class-level causalities. Field predictors f_{C.V} are shared across all instances of a class and use key-value attention over other objects to enforce symmetry and adapt to variable instance counts. Causal discovery uses conditional mutual information estimated by comparing the predictor's log-likelihood with and without each candidate class-level causality, eliminating the need for separate CIT models. Training optimizes an average instance log-likelihood (AILL) objective that combines losses under a randomly sampled OOCG (for robustness), the full OOCG (for CMI estimation), and the discovered OOCG (for final prediction), reducing overall causal discovery complexity from O(n³) to O(Nmn).

## Results

On causal graph discovery, OOCDM achieves 100% structural accuracy in Block₅, Block₁₀, and Mouse environments, outperforming the best baseline CDL-A (99.5% on Block₁₀). Causal discovery time drops from 7,000+ seconds (GRADER on Block₁₀) to ~2 seconds for OOCDM. On out-of-distribution prediction (AILL), OOCDM scores 21.3 on Block₂ o.o.d. versus −65.4 for GRADER and −7.2×10⁴ for MLP. For planning, OOCDM achieves 80.1 average return on Mouse (vs. 6.6 for GNN, the best non-OO baseline) and 266.2 on DZB (vs. 233.8 for GNN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OOCDM outperforms all four causal baselines (GRADER, CDL, CDL-A, TICSA) on causal graph accuracy across every environment, achieving perfect 100% recovery in 3 of 4 settings where the best non-OO method (CDL-A) reaches only 99.5%. On computational efficiency, OOCDM is 1–3 orders of magnitude faster than GRADER (e.g., 2.2 s vs. 7,000 s on Block₁₀; 7.4 s vs. 55,000 s on CMS) and faster than CDL/CDL-A in all environments. On prediction accuracy (AILL), OOCDM dominates on out-of-distribution data where non-OO baselines collapse (e.g., −1.1×10¹⁰ for CDL-A on Mouse o.o.d. vs. 11.2 for OOCDM), while the dense OO variant OOFULL also fails on o.o.d. data (−1.5×10⁹ on Mouse), confirming that causality—not just object sharing—is critical for generalization. On planning returns, OOCDM beats all non-OO methods by large margins (80.1 vs. 6.6 on Mouse; 3.4 vs. −8.8 on CMS) and slightly edges out OOFULL in 3 of 4 environments, with the exception being DZB where OOFULL's denser graph is less penalized by the large joint action space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Causal Dynamics Models (CDMs) from small-scale factored MDPs to large-scale object-oriented environments by introducing the Object-Oriented Causal Graph (OOCG), which shares class-level causalities among objects of the same class. It builds directly on CDL (Wang et al., 2022) for CMI-based causal discovery and on OOMDP formulations from Guestrin et al. (2003a) and Diuk et al. (2008), but departs from relational causal discovery (Maier et al., 2010) by not requiring explicit inter-object relations. The key architectural departure is the use of key-value attention in shared field predictors to enforce causation symmetry and handle varying object counts, replacing the fixed-variable predictors in prior CDMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OOCDM addresses a fundamental scalability bottleneck: conventional CDMs require O(n³) causal discovery and fail to generalize in environments with many variables, while OOCDM reduces this to O(Nmn) and achieves near-perfect causal graph recovery even at n=44. The finding that both OO structure and causality are independently necessary for out-of-distribution generalization (OOFULL without causality and GNN without OO structure both fail catastrophically) provides a clear decomposition of what drives generalization in dynamics models. Key limitations include the requirement that the OOMDP representation is given as prior knowledge, the assumption of dynamic symmetries (which may not hold in all real environments), and evaluation limited to four relatively controlled environments. Open questions include learning OOMDP representations from raw observations, handling unobserved confounders, and modeling explicit relational interactions between objects.
