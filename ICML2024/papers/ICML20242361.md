## Problem

Evidential Deep Learning (EDL) suffers from two key limitations: its uncertainty estimates do not reflect the distance between a testing example and the training data, degrading OOD detection performance, and its conventional parameterization (α_c = 1 + e_c) introduces a balancing problem between the constant 1 and unbounded evidence magnitudes, producing counter-intuitive class probabilities that limit classification accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242361/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single image (28×28 grayscale for MNIST or 32×32 RGB for CIFAR-10) and produces Dirichlet concentration parameters in a single forward pass. During prediction, the feature-space density of the test example—estimated via a GDA model fitted on training features—is normalized to [0,1] and multiplied with the network logits before the exponential activation, so the output concentration parameters are modulated by how far the example lies from the training manifold. From these parameters, aleatoric uncertainty (max expected class probability) and epistemic uncertainty (Dirichlet precision α_0) are derived as OOD scores.

## Method

DAEDL introduces two concurrent modifications to EDL. First, it replaces the conventional parameterization α_c = 1 + ReLU(z_c) with α_c = exp(z_c), which aligns the expected class probability exactly with the softmax output and eliminates the need to balance a fixed offset against unbounded evidence. Second, during prediction it integrates a feature-space density estimate: a GDA model is fitted on the training feature representations (no additional training required), and the normalized log-density s(x*) is multiplied with the logits before the exponential activation, yielding α = exp(z(x*) × s(x*)). Spectral normalization is applied to the feature extractor to ensure a Lipschitz-regularized feature space that makes the GDA density meaningful. Theoretically, DAEDL is shown to be equivalent to an adaptive temperature-scaled softmax (with sample-dependent temperature T = 1/s(x*)), interpretable as a Dirichlet-Categorical model with an improper prior Dir(0), and distance-aware in both feature and input space.

## Results

On OOD detection (AUPR), DAEDL achieves near-perfect scores on MNIST (99.90–99.92) and strong gains on CIFAR-10 (85.50–88.19), outperforming the runner-up I-EDL by 1.56–3.35 points across all four ID→OOD pairs. On CIFAR-10 image classification, DAEDL reaches 91.11% test accuracy (vs. 89.20% for I-EDL) and a Brier score of 14.27 (vs. 22.84 for PostNet). For distribution shift detection on MNIST-C, DAEDL scores 92.43 AUPR, surpassing MSP (78.54) by 13.89 points and I-EDL (86.06) by 6.37 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAEDL outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluated task. Against the closest competitor I-EDL, DAEDL gains 1.56–1.59 AUPR on MNIST OOD detection, 2.24–3.35 on CIFAR-10 OOD detection, 1.91 points in CIFAR-10 test accuracy (91.11 vs. 89.20), and 6.37 AUPR on MNIST-C distribution shift detection. Against EDL (the direct predecessor), DAEDL improves OOD AUPR by 2.88–3.86 points and test accuracy by 7.56 points. Against PostNet, DAEDL leads by 4.15 AUPR on MNIST→KMNIST and by 8.57 in Brier score on CIFAR-10 calibration. Against KL-PN and RKL-PN, DAEDL wins by large margins (7–39 AUPR points), with RKL-PN performing particularly poorly. Against MSP (the standard softmax-based baseline for distribution shift), DAEDL achieves 92.43 vs. 78.54 AUPR on MNIST-C and 77.74 vs. 75.01 on CIFAR-10-C at severity 5. Dropout, while competitive on MNIST OOD detection (94.00–96.56), degrades sharply on CIFAR-10 (45.57–51.39), where DAEDL reaches 85.50–88.19.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAEDL is a direct modification of Evidential Deep Learning (EDL, Sensoy et al. 2018), the most prominent Dirichlet-based uncertainty (DBU) model, introducing two targeted changes: an alternative parameterization (replacing 1+ReLU with exp) that makes the expected class probability identical to softmax, and a prediction-time integration of feature-space density estimated via Gaussian Discriminant Analysis, inspired by PostNet (Charpentier et al. 2020) and Mukhoti et al. (2023). The method also adopts spectral normalization in the feature extractor, a technique previously used in distance-aware uncertainty models (Liu et al. 2020) but not in DBU models. Theoretically, DAEDL is positioned at the intersection of adaptive temperature scaling (Joy et al. 2023), distance-aware uncertainty (Liu et al. 2020), and Bayesian Dirichlet-Categorical modeling, with the novel observation that its parameterization corresponds to an improper prior Dir(0) rather than the standard Dir(1) used by all other DBU models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAEDL offers a practical, single-forward-pass uncertainty estimation method whose components (alternative parameterization, GDA density, spectral normalization) are detachable and easily integrated into existing CNN architectures, addressing two concrete EDL limitations—poor OOD detection due to lack of distance awareness and degraded classification from the +1 parameterization. The clean theoretical results (uniform prediction for OOD data, equivalence to adaptive temperature-scaled softmax, distance awareness) provide a principled justification for the design choices. However, experiments are limited to MNIST and CIFAR-10, and the authors acknowledge that in complex scenarios the GDA density estimate and EDL outputs may not jointly capture uncertainty well; extending to larger datasets, regression tasks, and more complex architectures remains open.
