## Problem

Visual prompt tuning (VPT) and its variants suffer from three persistent challenges: sensitivity to random prompt initialization, fragility to prompt length, and significantly degraded performance under self-supervised pretraining (e.g., MAE). These issues limit the practical utility of parameter-efficient fine-tuning for downstream visual tasks, particularly when only unlabeled data is available for pretraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242055/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images resized to 224×224 and outputs a class probability distribution via a task-specific head. A key procedural detail is that before fine-tuning begins, the frozen pre-trained ViT backbone is run in inference mode over the downstream training set to extract patch embeddings at each transformer layer, which are then used to construct the initial prompt tokens. During adaptation, only the prompt tokens and task head parameters are updated while the entire transformer encoder remains frozen.

## Method

The paper observes via Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) analysis that during VPT training, prompt tokens progressively converge toward the distribution of patch tokens. Motivated by this, Self-Prompt Tuning (SPT) initializes learnable prompt tokens with downstream token prototypes obtained by clustering (K-means) or sampling (mean/max pooling, random sampling) the patch embeddings from a forward pass of the pre-trained backbone on the target dataset, ensuring high NMI between prompts and patches at the start of training. SPT comes in Shallow (prompts inserted only in the first transformer block) and Deep (prompts inserted in every block) variants, using less than 0.4% of learnable parameters. The streamlined sampling strategies reduce prompt construction time from days (K-means) to under a minute while retaining most of the performance gain, and the approach is robust to prompt length and scales well with model capacity.

## Results

Under MAE pre-training with ViT-B, SPT-Deep achieves 83.26% mean accuracy on FGVC and 59.23% on VTAB-1K Structured, outperforming full fine-tuning (82.80% and 53.82%) while using less than 0.4% of learnable parameters. Compared to VPT-Deep, SPT-Deep improves FGVC mean accuracy by 11.24% (83.26% vs. 72.02%) and VTAB Structured by 32.66% (59.23% vs. 26.57%). Under supervised ImageNet-21K pre-training, SPT-Deep achieves 91.40% FGVC mean accuracy with only 0.36M trainable parameters, surpassing full fine-tuning (88.54%, 85.98M params) and all PEFT baselines including SSF (90.72%) and SNF (90.74%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPT-Deep decisively outperforms VPT-Deep (Jia et al., 2022) under MAE pre-training, improving FGVC mean accuracy by 11.24% (83.26% vs. 72.02%) and VTAB Structured by 32.66% (59.23% vs. 26.57%), while under MoCo-v3 the gap narrows to +2.88% on FGVC and +15.98% on Structured. Against GateVPT (Yoo et al., 2023), the most recent self-supervised-specific prompt method, SPT-Deep leads by 9.87% on FGVC and 22.43% on VTAB Structured under MAE. Under supervised ImageNet-21K pre-training (Table 3), SPT-Deep (91.40%) surpasses SSF (90.72%), SNF (90.74%), MP (89.38%), E2VPT (89.22%), VPT-Deep (89.11%), Adapter (85.66%), and Bias/SpotTune (88.41%) on FGVC mean accuracy, while using fewer or comparable trainable parameters (0.36M vs. 0.25M–1.20M). Notably, SPT-Deep outperforms full fine-tuning in 19 out of 24 individual task evaluations under MAE pre-training, a result no prior prompt-based method has achieved. The advantage is most pronounced on tasks where VPT struggles most (e.g., VTAB Structured and CUB-200-2011 under MAE), suggesting SPT's initialization strategy specifically addresses the distributional mismatch that plagues random-init prompts under self-supervised backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT, Jia et al., ECCV 2022) and its variants (GateVPT, E2VPT), which insert learnable prompt tokens into frozen ViT backbones for parameter-efficient downstream adaptation. The key departure is the prompt initialization strategy: rather than random initialization followed by gradient-based optimization, SPT initializes prompts with downstream token prototypes derived from the pre-trained model's own patch embeddings, motivated by the empirical observation that prompt-patch normalized mutual information increases monotonically during VPT training. The method is explicitly positioned as a complement to VPT rather than a replacement, preserving the same architectural interface (shallow/deep prompt insertion) while changing only how initial values are constructed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10–30% accuracy gains over VPT under MAE pre-training address a well-documented and practically important gap: prompt tuning methods have been notably weaker with self-supervised backbones, limiting their utility in the increasingly common unlabeled-data regime. The finding that even fully frozen prompts (only the task head learnable) yield substantial gains suggests the initialization itself encodes most of the task-relevant information, which has implications for understanding what prompt tokens actually learn. Limitations include the absence of a theoretical guarantee for why high-NMI initialization helps, the computational cost of the K-means variant (up to 27 days), and the open question of why MAE pre-trained models remain harder to adapt than supervised ones even with SPT.
