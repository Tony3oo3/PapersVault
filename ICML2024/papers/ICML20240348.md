## Problem

LLMs encode diverse personality traits, but there is no systematic method to selectively elicit a specific target persona from the model. A simple baseline that directly queries the LLM achieves only 65.5% action consistency on persona elicitation—barely above random—revealing that the task is non-trivial and that existing prompting strategies (instructive, descriptive) are insufficient or even counterproductive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240348/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a test statement (a sentence the model should agree or disagree with) and prepends K=3 selected ICL examples (each a statement paired with its yes/no label) along with a system prompt 'Answer with Yes or No only.' The output is a binary action (yes/no) mapped from the model's generated token, plus the full token-level probability distribution used to compute four evaluation metrics: action consistency, action confidence, action/token-level uncertainty, and degree of alteration (KL divergence from the base model).

## Method

PICLe is grounded in a Bayesian decomposition of the LLM distribution into a mixture of persona distributions, where eliciting a target persona φ̃ corresponds to maximizing the posterior p_θ(φ̃|x). The key mechanism is a likelihood-ratio-based ICL example selection criterion: δ = log p_φ̃(x) − log p_θ(x), where p_φ̃ is a persona-specific model obtained via LoRA fine-tuning (rank 8, α=32, 4 epochs) on the persona statement pool, and p_θ is the original LLM. This selects examples that are highly indicative of the target persona yet underrepresented by the base model—i.e., the most 'informative' examples for shifting the model's persona posterior. The top-K examples by δ score are prepended to the query in reverse-score order, and the original (unfine-tuned) LLM performs inference. Ablations confirm that the ratio (difference of two log-likelihoods) is essential: using either model's likelihood alone drops consistency from 88.1% to ~72%.

## Results

On Llama-2 (7B chat), PICLe achieves 88.1% Action Consistency, a 22.6-point improvement over the base model (65.5%) and 3.5 points above the strongest ICL baseline (Similarity, 84.6%). On Vicuna (7B, non-RLHF), PICLe reaches 78.6% consistency versus 50.1% for the base model, and on GPT-J (6B) it achieves 67.0% versus 58.4% for the best baseline (Random). PICLe also exhibits the lowest action-level uncertainty (0.0621) and highest confidence (97.2%) among all methods on Llama-2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PICLe consistently outperforms all 11 baselines (3 non-ICL + 8 ICL) on Action Consistency across all three LLMs. On Llama-2, it beats the strongest ICL baseline (Similarity, 84.6%) by 3.5 points and the base model by 22.6 points. On Vicuna, where the base model essentially outputs a fixed response (~50% consistency), PICLe improves to 78.6%, surpassing the best baseline (Uncertainty, 76.1%) by 2.5 points. On GPT-J, which completely fails to follow yes/no instructions without ICL, PICLe achieves 67.0% versus 61.4% for the best baseline (Certainty-token). Notably, no single baseline dominates across all three models—Similarity is best on Llama-2, Uncertainty is best on Vicuna, and Certainty-token is best on GPT-J—whereas PICLe is the only method that is best on all three, demonstrating model-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Bayesian inference interpretation of in-context learning (Xie et al., 2021) and the multi-persona decomposition of LLMs (Wolf et al., 2023) to propose a principled ICL example selection criterion for persona elicitation. It extends the evaluation framework introduced by Perez et al. (2022) for probing LLM behaviors with new formalization (mapping function, action space) and four additional metrics (confidence, uncertainty, degree of alteration). The method departs from prior ICL selection strategies—similarity, uncertainty, diversity, and raw likelihood—by introducing a likelihood-ratio criterion that compares a persona-conditioned SFT model against the base model, selecting examples that are most 'informative' about the target persona.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, model-agnostic recipe for eliciting specific personas from LLMs with minimal overhead (one SFT epoch, three ICL examples), which is relevant for understanding and controlling LLM behavioral diversity. The Degree of Alteration analysis reveals that PICLe preserves the model's existing alignment for 'favorable' personas while making targeted distributional shifts for 'less favorable' ones, offering a mechanistic understanding of how ICL modifies model behavior. Key limitations include the restriction to a binary action space (yes/no), a single dataset of 99 personas, and the finding that in a label-aware setting, simple random selection nearly matches PICLe, suggesting the selection criterion's value is most pronounced in label-agnostic scenarios. Extending to open-ended generation and more nuanced multi-dimensional personas remains open.
