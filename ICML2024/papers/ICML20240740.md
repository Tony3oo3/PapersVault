## Problem

Transformers frequently exhibit prolonged plateaus in training loss followed by sudden, unexpected improvements (Eureka-moments) when learning multi-step tasks that require solving an unsupervised intermediate sub-task before the supervised final task. The cause of this optimization difficulty has not been identified, and it is unclear whether it stems from model capacity, hyperparameters, or an architectural property of the transformer itself.

## Input / Output

For the synthetic vision tasks, input is a composite 4-quadrant image (28×28 sub-images) containing two MNIST digit indicators and two FashionMNIST targets; the model outputs a single class label for the correct target, determined by whether the two digits match. For the reasoning task, input is a short token sequence 'a b c d =' and output is the correct token. For real-data validation, RoBERTa processes Wikipedia text (predicting masked tokens) and GPT-2 processes image-label sequences from Omniglot (predicting the missing label in a 2-way 4-shot ICL setting).

## Method

The paper traces Eureka-moments to the Softmax in self-attention: when attention is locally uniform over indicator tokens, the Jacobian of Softmax produces vanishingly small gradients for W_Q and W_K (0.5–1 orders of magnitude smaller than W_V), preventing the model from learning to shift attention toward the indicators needed for the intermediate task. To validate this mechanism, the authors apply three targeted interventions: (1) reducing the attention temperature τ, (2) Heat Treatment (HT), which schedules τ from a low value up to the default √d_k over the first half of training, and (3) NormSoftmax, which replaces the fixed √d_k scaling with the empirical standard deviation σ(QK^T) per head and layer. NormSoftmax proves most effective, raising the Eureka-ratio from 7/10 to 10/10, cutting the average Eureka-epoch from ~207 to ~28, and improving robustness to suboptimal learning-rate schedules (36/36 vs. 4/36 across 36 seed-schedule combinations). The intervention transfers to real data, yielding earlier Eureka-moments for RoBERTa on Wikipedia and higher ICL accuracy for GPT-2 on Omniglot.

## Results

On the main two-step vision task, vanilla ViT achieves a 7/10 Eureka-ratio with an average Eureka-epoch of 207, while NormSoftmax+HT achieves 10/10 with an average Eureka-epoch of only 17.5 (an ~12× speedup). On the harder No Position task, vanilla ViT fails entirely (0/4 Eureka-ratio), whereas NormSoftmax+HT still achieves a Eureka-moment (1/4) at epoch 19. NormSoftmax also dramatically improves robustness to learning rate: 36/36 successful runs across 9 schedules × 4 seeds, compared to only 4/36 for vanilla ViT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between vanilla ViT and targeted Softmax interventions. NormSoftmax (Jiang et al., 2023), adopted as an intervention, is the most effective single change: it raises the Eureka-ratio from 7/10 to 10/10 and reduces the average Eureka-epoch from 207 to 28 on the main task. Combining NormSoftmax with Heat Treatment (HT, the paper's own schedule that ramps τ from 1/3 to √d_k) further reduces the Eureka-epoch to 17.5. On the harder No Position task, W_QKV gradient scaling and vanilla ViT both fail (0/4), while NormSoftmax alone achieves 3/4 and NormSoftmax+HT achieves 1/4 at epoch 19. Warmup (20 epochs) and Weight Decay (0.5) provide only marginal gains (8/10 and 5/10 Eureka-ratio respectively) and do not resolve the underlying gradient imbalance. Across 9 learning-rate schedules, NormSoftmax succeeds in all 36 runs versus 4/36 for ViT, confirming that the Softmax-induced gradient problem, not hyperparameter sensitivity, is the root cause.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by creating a controlled synthetic-task framework to systematically study multi-step learning in transformers, a setting where prior observations of sudden training improvements (in ICL, BERT pretraining, diffusion models) remained unexplained. It connects to the line of work on unstable gradients in transformers (attention entropy collapse, uniform attention causing vanishing gradients) and to concurrent work by Reddy (2023) on saddle-points in ICL, but departs by identifying the specific mechanism—Softmax-induced gradient imbalance between W_V and W_Q/W_K—as the root cause. The most effective intervention, NormSoftmax (Jiang et al., 2023), is repurposed here not as a general training stabilizer but as a targeted fix for the multi-step learning failure mode, with the paper providing the mechanistic justification for why it works in this context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean mechanistic explanation for a previously unexplained and widely observed phenomenon (sudden training improvements after long plateaus), giving practitioners a diagnostic tool: if a transformer is stuck on a multi-step task, the Softmax temperature is likely the bottleneck. NormSoftmax and Heat Treatment are simple, drop-in modifications that reduce training steps by 5–10× and increase convergence probability from ~30–70% to 100% on synthetic tasks, with validated transfer to RoBERTa language modeling and GPT-2 ICL. Open questions include whether the analysis extends to tasks with more than two steps, whether the effect persists at very large scale (the paper tests up to ViT-S on ImageNet-100), and whether the synthetic-to-real transfer assumption holds for more complex multi-step structures. The finding that ResNets are entirely unaffected highlights a fundamental architectural difference in how attention-based models handle conditional information routing.
