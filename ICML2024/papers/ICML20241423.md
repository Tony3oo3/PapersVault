## Problem

Standard prediction sets for classification guarantee valid coverage only when training and target data share the same distribution over both labels and nuisance parameters. Under generalized label shift (GLS), where the joint distribution of the label Y and continuous/discrete nuisance parameters ν differs between train and target data, existing methods produce biased predictions and invalid uncertainty estimates. This paper addresses the largely unexplored problem of constructing set-valued classifiers that maintain conditional validity for all values of both Y and ν under GLS, while retaining high constraining power.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained probabilistic classifier (e.g., CatBoost) that outputs P_train(Y=y|x), a calibration set of simulated samples with known nuisance parameter values, and a new target observation x whose true ν is unknown. It produces a set-valued prediction H(x;α) ∈ {∅, 0, 1, {0,1}} with a guaranteed (1−α) conditional coverage for all y and ν simultaneously. When γ>0, the cutoffs become data-dependent: a (1−γ) confidence set for ν is constructed per observation, making inference slightly more expensive (seconds per observation) but yielding smaller prediction sets. The entire calibration is amortized—both the base classifier and the rejection probability function are estimated once and then evaluated on arbitrarily many new observations.

## Method

The core mechanism casts binary classification as a composite-versus-composite hypothesis test H_{0,y}: θ∈{y}×N vs. H_{1,y}: θ∈{y}^c×N, using the Bayes factor τ_y(x) as the test statistic. The key insight (Lemma 1) is that the rejection probability W_λ(C; y, ν) = P(λ(X)≤C | y, ν) is invariant under GLS, so it can be reliably estimated from training simulations. The paper estimates the full ROC curve (TPR and FPR as functions of cutoff C) across the entire nuisance parameter space via monotone regression on an augmented calibration set, then constructs NAPS cutoffs as C*_{α,y}(x) = inf_{ν∈S_y(x;γ)} W^{-1}(β; y, ν), where S_y(x;γ) is a (1−γ) confidence set for ν and β=α−γ. This infimum over the nuisance space ensures the type-I error is controlled uniformly for all ν, yielding conditional validity (Theorem 2) without retraining. Setting γ>0 restricts the optimization to a data-dependent confidence set for ν, recovering power while preserving validity—a frequentist analogue of likelihood profiling that, unlike profiling, guarantees coverage.

## Results

The central result is that Nuisance-Aware Prediction Sets (NAPS) guarantee valid coverage (≥1−α) for all nuisance parameter values under Generalized Label Shift (GLS), while all baseline methods fail. In the RNA-Seq experiment with 4 batch protocols, NAPS (γ=0) maintains valid coverage across all protocols, whereas standard prediction sets, class-conditional prediction sets, and conformal APS all undercover for at least two protocols. In the cosmic-ray shower experiment (99,850 CORSIKA simulations), NAPS with γ>0 outperforms the standard Bayes classifier at all confidence levels in both precision and FDR, while NAPS with γ=0 achieves high precision and low false discovery rates, particularly at high confidence levels. The method also achieves marginal validity as a by-product of its conditional validity guarantee (Theorem 2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard prediction sets (Sadinle et al., 2019), NAPS wins decisively on validity: standard PS are marginally valid only when train and target distributions match, but undercover for all α levels under GLS (Figure 1, top right), while NAPS remain valid in both settings. Against class-conditional prediction sets (Sadinle et al., 2019), NAPS again wins on validity: class-conditional PS guarantee coverage conditional on Y but not on ν, causing them to undercover for at least 2 of 4 batch protocols in the RNA-Seq experiment. Against conformal adaptive prediction sets (Romano et al., 2020), NAPS wins on both validity and informativeness: conformal APS undercover for at least 2 protocols, and at high confidence levels (1−α ≈ 1) they output {0,1} for every observation, rendering them completely uninformative, whereas NAPS maintain high power (Figure 10, Appendix G). Relative to the standard Bayes classifier, NAPS with γ>0 outperforms at all confidence levels in the cosmic-ray experiment (Figure 5), while NAPS with γ=0 slightly underperforms at low confidence/low energy but achieves higher precision and lower FDR at high confidence levels. The paper also notes that under SLS-only (no nuisance shift), NAPS over-covers and has lower power than class-conditional PS, confirming the expected tradeoff for a method designed for the harder GLS setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the set-valued classifier framework of Sadinle et al. (2019) by replacing fixed cutoffs on class posteriors with nuisance-aware cutoffs derived from ROC curves estimated across the entire nuisance parameter space via monotone regression. It also builds on the WALDO confidence-set construction of Masserano et al. (2023) to obtain valid (1−γ) confidence sets for nuisance parameters that enable data-dependent cutoffs with higher power. Conceptually, the paper departs from standard label-shift correction methods (Saerens et al., 2002; Lipton et al., 2018) and conformal prediction (Romano et al., 2020), which assume exchangeability or known target priors, by formulating classification as a composite-versus-composite hypothesis test where the Bayes factor serves as a frequentist test statistic without requiring likelihood estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: most existing uncertainty-quantification methods for classification (conformal prediction, standard prediction sets) lose validity when both the label distribution and latent nuisance parameters shift between training and deployment, a situation common in scientific applications like RNA-seq batch effects and cosmic-ray shower parameterization. The conditional validity guarantee (Theorem 2) is stronger than the marginal coverage offered by prior methods, and the invariance of rejection probability under GLS (Lemma 1) provides a clean theoretical foundation that avoids retraining. Key limitations include reduced power relative to standard methods when no shift is present, the non-trivial extension to multiclass one-vs-rest settings, and the computational cost of per-observation cutoffs when γ > 0 and the nuisance space is high-dimensional.
