## Problem

NeRF training degrades when training images contain distractors—unexpected objects present only in specific views (e.g., pedestrians, balloons, moving hands)—because the photometric loss treats all pixels equally, corrupting 3D consistency. Existing robust-learning approaches either assume temporal continuity of moving objects, require pre-trained segmentation for known distractor classes, or rely on heuristic loss-based masking that conflates hard-to-learn regions (e.g., semi-transparent surfaces) with actual distractors. The paper tackles this by proposing a post-hoc dataset pruning framework that identifies and removes distractors without prior knowledge of their type, count, or location.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of multi-view RGB training images with COLMAP-estimated camera poses and a fully trained (distractor-contaminated) Mip-NeRF360 model. It produces binary distractor masks at the segment level (via SAM zero-shot segmentation), which are then used to prune the training dataset before retraining. Notably, the pipeline operates on an already-trained model rather than modifying the training loop, and the only tunable thresholds are a fixed depth-reprojection tolerance (θ=0.1) and a segment-level ratio threshold (ε=0.1), both held constant across all datasets.

## Method

PruNeRF is a three-stage post-training pruning pipeline. First, it computes per-pixel Self-Influence scores (an approximation of Leave-One-Out retraining via the Hessian inverse restricted to the last layer) to measure how much each pixel deviates from the dataset's majority signal, outperforming loss and gradient-norm at separating distractors from hard-to-learn regions. Second, it assesses 3D spatial consistency by estimating depth from the trained NeRF, projecting a query pixel to a 3D surface point, reprojecting that point into other views, and flagging pixels whose Self-Influence scores fall outside the mean ± 3σ distribution of their 3D-corresponding counterparts. Third, it refines pixel-level flags to the segment level using SAM's zero-shot partitioning: a segment is marked as distractor if the fraction of flagged pixels within it exceeds ε=0.1, eliminating per-pixel noise and enabling object-level pruning. The entire framework is plug-and-play, compatible with any NeRF architecture and any sample-wise distraction metric.

## Results

On natural scenes (Statue, Android, BabyYoda), PruNeRF achieves 21.31, 22.86, and 31.73 PSNR respectively, outperforming the state-of-the-art RobustNeRF (20.89, 21.72, 30.87) by an average of 0.8 PSNR. On synthetic Kubric scenes, PruNeRF improves over RobustNeRF by an average of 1.08 PSNR and 0.002 MS-SSIM, reaching 39.19–41.23 PSNR across the five scenes. The largest gains appear on the most challenging dataset BabyYoda (up to 150 unique distractors), where PruNeRF reaches 31.73 PSNR versus 25.22 for vanilla Mip-NeRF360 with Charbonnier loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PruNeRF consistently outperforms RobustNeRF (the primary SOTA baseline) across all six evaluated scenes: +0.42 PSNR on Statue, +1.14 on Android, and +0.86 on BabyYoda in natural scenes, and +1.02 to +1.38 PSNR across the five Kubric synthetic scenes. Against vanilla Mip-NeRF360 with Charbonnier loss, the gains are substantially larger (e.g., +6.51 PSNR on BabyYoda), confirming that the pruning framework is the source of improvement rather than the base architecture. On synthetic scenes, PruNeRF also surpasses temporal methods (NeRF-W, NSFF, NeuralDiff, D²NeRF) that assume ordered video input, whereas PruNeRF operates on unordered multi-view images. D²NeRF performs competitively on synthetic scenes (where temporal continuity holds) but degrades severely on natural scenes (17.32 PSNR on BabyYoda vs. 31.73 for PruNeRF), highlighting the advantage of not requiring temporal ordering. The ablation (Table 4) further shows that each component—Influence Functions, 3D spatial consistency, and pixel-to-segment refinement—contributes incrementally, with the full pipeline adding ~1.7 PSNR over the base Mip-NeRF360 on Statue.

## Relation to Prior Work

PruNeRF positions itself as a dataset-centric alternative to the model-centric robust training approaches that dominate the NeRF distractor literature (NeRF-W's per-image latent codes, RobustNeRF's loss-based masking, D2NeRF's temporal decoupling). It repurposes Influence Functions from the data-poisoning/detection literature (Koh & Liang, 2017) as a pixel-wise distraction metric, arguing they outperform loss and gradient-norm for distinguishing true distractors from merely hard-to-learn regions. The 3D spatial consistency step via depth-based reprojection and the pixel-to-segment refinement via zero-shot SAM segmentation are novel combinations not present in prior NeRF robustness work, and the method explicitly avoids the temporal-continuity assumption required by D2NeRF/NSFF/NeuralDiff.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in NeRF dataset construction: the inability to preemptively filter unknown distractors without prior knowledge. By framing the solution as a pre-training pruning step rather than a modified training objective, PruNeRF is plug-and-play compatible with arbitrary NeRF architectures, which is a meaningful practical advantage over methods like RobustNeRF whose mask-based reweighting is tightly coupled to a specific training loop. Open questions include whether the two-stage pipeline (train → prune → retrain) can be made end-to-end, how the method behaves under sparse-view conditions, and whether the fixed thresholds (θ=0.1 for depth, ε=0.1 for segment ratio, 3σ for outlier detection) generalize beyond the tested scenes.
