## Problem

Fine-tuned models can exhibit worse OOD generalization than both the pre-trained model and a trained-from-scratch model, because spurious features embedded in the pre-trained model and those introduced by fine-tuning data jointly compound in the fine-tuned model. Prior fine-tuning generalization methods implicitly assume pre-trained features are sufficient and problem-free, focusing only on preserving them or avoiding overfitting to fine-tuning data, while ignoring inherent limitations (e.g., spurious correlations, missing task-relevant representations) in the pre-trained model itself.

## Input / Output

For recommendation tasks, tabular attributes (user/movie/product IDs, titles, occupations, zipcodes) are concatenated into natural-language sentences for the T5x backbone and simultaneously fed as embedding vectors into a small MLP; for vision tasks, 224×224 RGB images pass through both a ViT backbone and a small 4-layer CNN. The output is a single prediction per input (rating in [1,5] or class label), produced by multiple adapting layers whose predictions are jointly optimized during training rather than averaged at inference.

## Method

LEVI runs a large pre-trained model (T5x-small or ViT-base, frozen or lightly tuned) in parallel with a small randomly-initialized task-specific model (2-layer MLP with embeddings for recommendation, 4-layer CNN for vision), then fuses them via small adapting layers (single-hidden-layer MLPs) that concatenate intermediate outputs from multiple layers of the large model with the final output of the small model. The training loss sums the per-adapting-layer losses over all samples and all layers, so each adapting layer learns to exploit a different combination of general (early-layer) and specific (late-layer) pre-trained features alongside task-specialized features from the small model. This layer-wise, cross-model ensemble implicitly suppresses spurious correlations originating from either the pre-trained weights or the fine-tuning data, because the two models are affected by different spurious features and their complementary views cancel each other's weaknesses. Only the small model and adapting layers require gradient updates (~2M parameters), keeping training cost close to a single large model while inference adds only ~4M FLOPs over the backbone.

## Results

On the MovieLens recommendation task (genre shift OOD), LEVI achieves 1.065 RMSE, substantially outperforming full fine-tuning (1.268) and the best ensemble baseline FT+RobustModel (1.127). On Amazon Review product shifts, LEVI reaches 1.298 RMSE versus 2.029 for full fine-tuning and 1.752 for FT+RobustModel. On ImageNet domain shifts (trained on ImageNet-Sketch, tested on ImageNet-A/R/V2), LEVI achieves 33.62% OOD accuracy versus 21.62% for fine-tuned ViT and 30.30% for FT+RobustModel, while maintaining competitive ID accuracy (83.33% vs. 83.06%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEVI outperforms all four baselines across every OOD scenario. Against LP→FT (Kumar et al., 2022b), LEVI reduces RMSE by 0.29 on MovieLens genre OOD (1.065 vs. 1.357) and by 0.37 on Amazon product OOD (1.298 vs. 1.669). Against FT+RobustModel (Kumar et al., 2022a), the closest ensemble competitor, LEVI still wins on all OOD metrics, e.g., 1.065 vs. 1.127 on MovieLens and 33.62% vs. 30.30% on ImageNet OOD accuracy. Against FT+ZeroShot (Wortsman et al., 2022b), LEVI shows the largest gap on Amazon product shifts (1.298 vs. 2.515 RMSE), where the zero-shot pre-trained features actively harm performance. Against LoRA-tuned ViT (Hu et al., 2022), LEVI with a LoRA-tuned backbone improves OOD accuracy by 0.51 points on Medical (73.57% vs. 73.06%) and 3.56 points on ImageNet (25.64% vs. 22.08%), confirming compatibility with parameter-efficient fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEVI departs from the dominant line of fine-tuning generalization work (Kumar et al. 2022a,b; Wortsman et al. 2022b; Tian et al. 2023) that assumes pre-trained features are sufficient and focuses on preserving them during fine-tuning. Instead, it challenges this assumption by showing that pre-trained features can themselves carry spurious correlations that compound with spurious features in fine-tuning data. Architecturally, LEVI extends the intermediate-layer ensemble idea from Head2toe (Evci et al., 2022) by attaching adapting heads to multiple layers of a frozen pre-trained model, but introduces the novel component of jointly ensembling with a small trained-from-scratch model — the first work to do so for fine-tuning OOD generalization. It also integrates with LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) for parameter-efficient training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that fine-tuned models can be strictly worse than both the pre-trained model and a trained-from-scratch model on OOD data challenges a core assumption in the fine-tuning paradigm and suggests that when pre-training and downstream domains diverge significantly, simply preserving pre-trained features is insufficient. LEVI offers a practical, efficient remedy (comparable inference cost to a single large model) that consistently improves OOD performance across language and vision modalities. Open questions include whether the approach extends to generative tasks, whether the small model's architecture can be further optimized per task, and whether the layer-wise ensemble can be made adaptive (e.g., learned per-layer weights) rather than using equal weights.
