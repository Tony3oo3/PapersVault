## Problem

Despite rapid advances in deep tabular learning, tree-based methods (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost) still consistently outperform deep learning models on standard benchmarks. The paper identifies two root causes: the non-rotationally invariant structure of tabular data, which undermines rotationally invariant deep learning procedures, and the high feature sparsity typical of tabular datasets, which makes it difficult for dense models to focus on informative features.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single tabular data point containing both numerical and categorical features as input. Numerical features are embedded via a piece-wise linear quantile-based scheme (G quantile bins per feature), while categorical features use a shared-plus-individual column embedding. These are concatenated and then reduced via a stride-based patch embedding (stride L) into a 3D tensor of shape N×P×D_model. The output is a flattened final decoder representation passed through an MLP head, producing either a class probability vector (classification) or a scalar prediction (regression).

## Method

BiSHop is a hierarchical encoder-decoder built from stacked BiSHopModules, each containing two interconnected generalized sparse modern Hopfield (GSH) blocks: a column-wise block that applies self-attention (via α-EntMax) within each feature's embedding dimension, and a row-wise block that uses learnable pooling vectors (C ≪ N) with GSHPooling followed by cross-attention GSH across all features. This bi-directional design encodes the tabular structure as an inductive bias, addressing the non-rotationally invariant challenge. The encoder progressively coarsens representations using learnable merging matrices that aggregate r adjacent patches at each of H levels, while the decoder reconstructs fine-grained predictions via positional embeddings and cross-attention GSH with each encoder level. The sparsity parameter α in every GSH layer is learnable, so each scale automatically adapts its sparsity level, addressing the feature-sparsity challenge. The α-EntMax activation (derived from Tsallis α-entropy) replaces softmax in the attention computation, providing provably faster convergence and linear (rather than exponential) noise sensitivity compared to dense Hopfield/attention layers.

## Results

On 9 common classification datasets (Table 1), BiSHop achieves the best AUC on 8 out of 9 datasets, with particularly large margins on SeismicBump (91.88% vs. 81.59% for CatBoost) and Qsar (96.14% vs. 94.86% for T2G-FORMER). On the broader 19-dataset tabular benchmark (Table 2), BiSHop attains a mean score of 81.21% (accuracy for classification, R² for regression) and a mean rank of 2.79, outperforming all 7 baselines (best baseline: XGBoost at 80.65%, rank 3.84). Notably, BiSHop achieves 11 optimal and 8 near-optimal results while using fewer than 10% of the HPO trials (on average) compared to the 400 HPOs used by benchmark baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiSHop outperforms all tree-based baselines (LightGBM, CatBoost, XGBoost) on 8 of 9 classification datasets, with the largest gap on SeismicBump (+10.29% over CatBoost) and Blastchar (+3.72% over CatBoost). Among deep learning baselines, BiSHop surpasses FT-Transformer on 8/9 datasets (e.g., +10.28% on SeismicBump, +4.10% on Qsar) and SAINT on 7/9 (e.g., +5.41% on SeismicBump). On the 19-dataset OpenML benchmark, BiSHop achieves the best mean score (81.21%) and best mean rank (2.79) across all 7 baselines, with 11 outright wins and 8 near-ties (within 1.3%), while requiring on average fewer than 10% of the 400 HPO trials used by the benchmark. The only dataset where BiSHop does not clearly lead is Bank, where T2G-FORMER scores 94.47% vs. BiSHop's 93.95%, and Spambase where multiple methods tie at 100% AUC.

## Relation to Prior Work

BiSHop extends the modern Hopfield model–attention equivalence (Ramsauer et al., 2021) through the generalized sparse modern Hopfield model (Wu et al., 2024b), which introduces learnable sparsity via Tsallis α-entropy in place of softmax. The key architectural departure is the bi-directional design: two interconnected GSH blocks process data column-wise (intra-feature) and row-wise (inter-feature), directly encoding the non-rotationally invariant structure of tabular data as an inductive bias. The hierarchical encoder-decoder stack with scale-specific sparsity draws on the brain's multi-level associative memory organization, while the tabular and patch embeddings are adapted from prior tabular deep learning work (Huang et al., 2020; Gorishniy et al., 2021) and time-series patching (Nie et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BiSHop targets the well-documented gap where tree-based methods (LightGBM, XGBoost, CatBoost) consistently outperform deep learning on tabular data, a limitation identified by Grinsztajn et al. (2022). The paper's claim of closing this gap with fewer HPO runs is practically significant for practitioners who find exhaustive hyperparameter search costly. However, the evaluation uses a randomly selected subset of the OpenML benchmark (one-third of each suite, truncated to 10K samples), and the authors acknowledge not leveraging the external memory capacity of Hopfield models—a potentially powerful capability left unexplored. Whether the bi-directional sparse Hopfield design generalizes to very large or highly heterogeneous tabular settings remains open.
