## Problem

While StreamLLM identified attention sinks at the initial token of LLM inputs, it remained unknown whether such sinks exist at other positions, whether they universally benefit model accuracy, and whether they could be exploited for training-free performance gains. This paper addresses all three gaps by revealing that attention sinks pervade intermediate tokens (punctuation, newlines, etc.) and that reducing their excessive attention in most heads actually improves downstream accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242378/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ACT operates on a frozen pretrained LLM (e.g., Llama2-7B/13B-chat, Mistral-7B, Llama-30B) processing a standard text prompt. During inference, it intercepts the attention maps at intermediate layers (3rd through second-to-last), identifies sink tokens whose attention score exceeds α/N (α=5), and modifies only those entries before the softmax output proceeds to the value projection. The output is the same generated text or classification label as vanilla inference, but with calibrated attention. A small offline held-out set (~1000 samples per dataset, <10% of validation size) is used solely for head filtering, not for any weight update.

## Method

ACT is a two-phase, training-free attention calibration technique. In the offline head-filtering phase, each attention head is individually calibrated on a small held-out set and retained only if it improves accuracy, yielding a subset H of heads to calibrate. During online inference, for each head in H at intermediate layers, ACT (1) identifies sink tokens with attention score > α/N, (2) multiplies their attention by a reduction factor β=0.4, and (3) redistributes the freed attention mass proportionally to non-sink tokens weighted by their original attention values, preserving each row's sum to one. This proportional redistribution preserves the relative attention structure among semantic tokens while suppressing the disproportionate focus on low-semantic tokens. The key insight is that most mid-sequence attention sinks (punctuation, newlines) divert attention away from meaningful content, and selectively reducing them in the right heads yields consistent accuracy gains of up to 7.30% without any parameter updates.

## Results

ACT achieves an average accuracy improvement of 0.30%–7.30% over vanilla inference across seven models and multiple shot settings on domain-specific multiple-choice tasks. The largest gains appear on Llama-30B (7.30% avg. improvement, 0-shot) and Mistral-7B (3.79% avg. improvement, 0-shot), with a peak single-dataset gain of 13.26% on Hellaswag. On text classification, ACT yields 1.29%–5.12% average improvements (0-shot), and on open-ended QA it improves MT-Bench scores by 0.088–0.134, SQuAD v1 exact match by up to 10.14, and F1 by up to 16.42. On MMLU, ACT consistently adds 0.32%–1.09% accuracy across Llama2-7B, GPT-J-6B, Vicuna-7B, and OPT-2.7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is vanilla (unmodified) inference; no external attention-manipulation or inference-time optimization methods are directly compared. ACT outperforms vanilla inference in the vast majority of individual dataset–model–shot combinations: on the 0-shot multiple-choice benchmark, it wins on 6 out of 7 datasets for Llama2-7B-chat, 5 out of 7 for Llama2-13B-chat, 7 out of 7 for Mistral-7B, and 7 out of 7 for Llama-30B. The only notable losses are small (e.g., −1.05% on ARCE for Llama2-13B-chat 0-shot, −0.35% on ARCE for Llama2-7B-chat 3-shot), while the largest single-dataset gain is +13.26% (Llama-30B on Hellaswag). On MMLU (Table 3), ACT improves every tested model (Llama2-7B, GPT-J-6B, Vicuna-7B, OPT-2.7B) by 0.32%–1.09% over vanilla zero-shot. The paper also notes that ACT is orthogonal to in-context learning and can be stacked on top of 1/3/5-shot prompts for additional gains, though no combined baseline is formally tabulated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends StreamLLM (Xiao et al., 2023), which first identified attention sinks as tokens receiving disproportionately high attention scores, by showing the phenomenon is far more widespread than previously thought. While StreamLLM localized attention sinks to the initial token and recommended always preserving them, this work discovers sinks in later tokens (often punctuation or low-semantic tokens) and demonstrates that reducing their attention can improve accuracy. The proposed ACT technique is positioned as orthogonal to parameter-efficient tuning (LoRA, prompt tuning) and in-context learning, offering a new inference-time design knob that operates directly on attention distributions without any weight modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that attention sinks pervade the input sequence and that not all are beneficial challenges a working assumption from StreamLLM and opens a new axis for LLM enhancement that requires no training data or gradient updates. Practically, ACT provides a lightweight, model-agnostic calibration step (requiring only a small held-out set for head filtering) that can be stacked with in-context learning for additional gains. Open questions include whether the head-filtering step can be eliminated or made fully automatic, whether the approach generalizes to non-Transformer architectures or very long contexts, and whether the modest per-dataset improvements (sometimes negative) can be made more consistent across tasks.
