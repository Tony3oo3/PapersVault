## Problem

Existing GPU implementations of Probabilistic Circuits (e.g., SPFlow, EiNet, Juice.jl) suffer from severe time and memory inefficiencies that prevent training large-scale PCs on complex datasets. The root cause is that PC computation graphs have irregular connection patterns and log-probabilities at vastly different scales, making them incompatible with the dense matrix operations that standard deep learning frameworks exploit. This bottleneck limits both the size of trainable PCs and the number of training epochs feasible within practical compute budgets.

## Input / Output

The system takes a parameterized PC DAG (with sum, product, and input nodes) plus a batch of data samples (e.g., 16×16 image patches with 768 categorical variables of 256 values each, or sequences of 32–64 word tokens) and produces log-probabilities, PC flows, and parameter gradients for EM updates. A one-time compilation step converts the PC into a compact block-sparse representation with precomputed index tensors (sum_ids, prod_ids, param_ids) that are reused across all training and inference passes. The compiled structure supports tied parameters (e.g., homogeneous HMMs sharing transition/emission matrices across time steps) and can handle both dense and sparse PC topologies.

## Method

PyJuice compiles each sum layer into groups of node blocks with similar fan-in, then parallelizes over blocks of K_M sum nodes × K_N product nodes × K_B samples, reformulating the weighted logsumexp as a matrix-matrix multiplication that exploits GPU Tensor Cores. A dynamic programming partitioning algorithm (Algorithm 2) assigns node blocks to groups to balance workload while keeping pseudo-node overhead below a tolerance threshold. For backpropagation, PyJuice backpropagates PC flows (Choi et al., 2021) rather than raw gradients, which avoids inter-thread-block synchronization barriers and enables a separate compilation pass that partitions child node blocks by parent count. A key memory optimization recomputes product-layer outputs on demand during the backward pass using a shared scratch buffer, reducing peak GPU memory by 2–5× compared to storing all intermediate values.

## Results

PyJuice achieves 1–2 orders of magnitude speedup over all baselines across four PC structures (PD, RAT-SPN, HCLT, HMM) at sizes ranging from 500K to 2B edges. For example, on a RAT-SPN with 930K nodes, PyJuice completes an epoch in 13.8s versus 2445.1s for EiNet (177× faster) and 375.1s for Juice.jl (27× faster). PyJuice also consumes 2–5× less GPU memory, enabling training of models that cause OOM in all baselines (e.g., a 2.06M-node PD with 2.03B edges). On downstream tasks, PyJuice improves HMM language model perplexity on CommonGen from 9.78 to 8.65 and achieves 4.33 bpd on ImageNet32 (vs. 4.82 previously reported for HCLT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PyJuice dominates all five baselines across every PC structure and model size tested. Against SPFlow, PyJuice is 1000×+ faster (e.g., 2.0s vs. >25000s on the smallest PD), as SPFlow cannot exploit topological parallelism. Against EiNet, PyJuice is 17× faster at small scale (2.0s vs. 34.2s on PD-172K) and up to 227× faster at large scale (24.9s vs. 5654.3s on HCLT-1.42M), with EiNet hitting OOM on the largest PD. Against Juice.jl, PyJuice is 6–27× faster and avoids OOM on models where Juice.jl fails (e.g., PD-1.38M, HMM-259K). Against Dynamax (HMM-only), PyJuice is 186× faster at small scale (0.6s vs. 111.3s) and 101× faster at large scale (39.9s vs. 4039.8s). On the CommonGen language modeling task, PyJuice improves upon Zhang et al. (2023) by reducing perplexity from 9.78 to 8.81 (same 4096 hidden states, more epochs) and further to 8.65 with 8192 hidden states, demonstrating that the speedup translates directly into better model quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the topological layering parallelization strategy introduced by EiNet (Peharz et al., 2020a) and Juice.jl (Dang et al., 2021), which chunked PCs into layers of independently computable nodes. It departs from these prior systems by introducing a compilation step that converts PCs into compact block-sparse representations, enabling block-based parallelization that reformulates sum-layer computation as matrix multiplications amenable to GPU Tensor Cores. The backward pass leverages PC flows (Choi et al., 2021), a quantity previously used for group fairness, repurposing it as the core mechanism for efficient gradient computation that avoids inter-thread-block synchronization barriers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PyJuice removes the primary practical barrier to scaling PCs—memory and compute inefficiency—enabling training of models with up to 2B edges that were previously infeasible on a single GPU. The established baselines on ImageNet, CelebA-HQ, CommonGen, and WikiText-103 provide reproducible reference points for future PC structure and learning algorithm research. Open questions include whether the block-sparse compilation approach can guide the design of inherently block-sparse PC structures, and whether the system extends naturally to other tractable model families beyond the sum-product DAG formalism.
