## Problem

Deterministic future frame prediction has been less successful for visual representation learning than other self-supervised approaches, because the under-determined nature of frame prediction—where multiple potential futures arise from a single current frame—makes it difficult for deterministic models to approximate the multi-modal distribution of future frames from complex real-world videos. This paper investigates whether training a stochastic frame prediction model that explicitly captures this uncertainty can induce stronger representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240856/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pairs of RGB video frames (current x_t and future x_{t+k}, with k randomly sampled from 4 to 48) at 224×224 resolution from Kinetics-400, applying the same random resized crop and horizontal flip to both frames plus a small Gaussian noise to the future frame. The output is a set of visual representations (the [CLS] token and patch embeddings from a shared ViT-S/16 encoder) that encode both temporal dynamics between frames and dense spatial structure within each frame, used downstream for robot learning and video label propagation tasks.

## Method

RSP reconfigures the stochastic video generation model (Denton & Fergus, 2018) for representation learning by using a shared ViT-S/16 encoder, a posterior network (2-layer MLP over [CLS] tokens of both frames) and a prior network (2-layer MLP over the current frame's [CLS] token) that parameterize 32 categorical latent variables (32 classes each, following Dreamer) with a straight-through estimator. The decoder uses a cross-self attention architecture where [MASK] tokens attend to concatenated [h_t, z_{t+k}] tokens, and the training objective combines reconstruction loss, a KL divergence between posterior and prior (scaled by β=0.01 with KL balancing ratio α=0.8), and an auxiliary MAE objective (75% masking) that shares the same decoder by making [MASK] tokens attend to different inputs via cross-attention. This shared decoder design is critical: it makes the two objectives synergistic and parameter-efficient, outperforming a parallel decoder by 2.0%p on DAVIS. The stochastic (categorical) latent variable is essential—deterministic prediction drops performance from 60.1% to 54.4% on DAVIS, and Gaussian latents underperform categorical ones by 6.0%p.

## Results

On vision-based robot learning, RSP achieves a 36.0% average success rate on RLBench (6 manipulation tasks), substantially outperforming MAE (13.5%) and SiamMAE (6.0%). On CortexBench (4 locomotion/manipulation domains), RSP achieves the best or near-best score across all tasks, e.g., 61.6 on DMC and 66.2 on Trifinger. On video label propagation, RSP (ViT-S/16) achieves 60.1 J&F_m on DAVIS, outperforming MAE by 6.6 percentage points and SiamMAE by 2.0 points, while also leading on VIP (33.8 mIoU) and JHMDB (73.4 PCK@0.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RSP outperforms all five baselines on both robot learning and video label propagation benchmarks. Against SimCLR and MoCo v3 (contrastive methods), RSP wins by wide margins on RLBench (36.0% vs ~17% and ~19% average success) and DAVIS (60.1 vs 53.9 and 57.7 J&F_m), indicating that contrastive objectives alone are insufficient for temporal understanding. Against Dino (self-distillation), RSP leads on RLBench (36.0% vs ~26%) and DAVIS (60.1 vs 59.5), with the gap widening on manipulation tasks like Saucepan (93.4 vs 57.9). Against MAE, the most relevant image-only baseline, RSP achieves a 6.6%p improvement on DAVIS (60.1 vs 53.5) and a 22.5%p improvement on RLBench (36.0% vs 13.5%), demonstrating that stochastic temporal prediction captures information that masked image modeling misses. Against SiamMAE, the closest video-based baseline that predicts masked patches from future frames conditioned on unmasked patches, RSP achieves a dramatic 35.6% vs 6.0% on RLBench and a 2.0%p gain on DAVIS (60.1 vs 58.1), highlighting the benefit of explicitly modeling uncertainty over the future rather than conditioning on partial future observations.

## Relation to Prior Work

RSP reconfigures the stochastic video generation framework of Denton & Fergus (2018) — originally designed for video synthesis — into a representation learning objective, incorporating modern ViT encoders, categorical latent variables from discrete world models (Hafner et al., 2021a), and a shared cross-attention decoder inspired by MAE (He et al., 2022). It departs from deterministic frame prediction, which has been shown to struggle with complex real-world videos, and from SiamMAE (Gupta et al., 2023), which resolves future-frame ambiguity by conditioning on unmasked future patches rather than modeling the inherent stochasticity. The auxiliary MAE objective with a shared decoder is a novel architectural choice that allows both temporal and dense spatial objectives to be trained synergistically with minimal overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that capturing uncertainty in future frame prediction yields substantially better representations for downstream robot learning than deterministic or patch-conditioned alternatives, with a particularly striking gap on RLBench manipulation tasks (35.6% vs. 6.0% for SiamMAE). Limitations include low-fidelity generated frames, evaluation on a single pre-training dataset (Kinetics-400) and primarily ViT-S/16, and no scaling to larger models or longer training budgets. Open directions include incorporating diffusion-based video generation, extending to multi-frame prediction, and scaling to larger datasets and architectures to assess whether the stochasticity advantage persists at scale.
