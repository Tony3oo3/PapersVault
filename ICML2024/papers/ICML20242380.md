## Problem

Existing theory-of-mind (ToM) benchmarks in NLP model characters with large amounts of behavioral data, ignoring the few-shot and meta-learning nature of how humans actually understand new characters. Humans rapidly comprehend unfamiliar fictional characters by drawing analogies to previously seen characters across movies, a cross-task transfer ability that no prior benchmark evaluates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242380/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each task instance is a movie scene with up to 5 anonymized character IDs (P0–P4) and a candidate set of top-5 characters by dialogue count. The model receives a stream of training scenes (first 3/5 of the script, ~20 instances per character on average) and must predict identities in test scenes (remaining 2/5). Character names are perturbed with random gender-matched English names to prevent LLM memorization. In the inductive setting, the output is structured mental state descriptions across five ToM dimensions (personality, emotions, beliefs, desires, intentions) that are then used for identity prediction.

## Method

The paper introduces TOM-IN-AMC, a benchmark of 1,007 parsed movie scripts where each movie forms a natural few-shot task (807 meta-training, 100 meta-dev, 100 meta-test), and proposes ToMPro, a two-stage GPT-4 prompting method for the inductive setting. Stage-1 iteratively processes training scenes to build character mental states: personality is updated via recurrent memory across scenes, while emotions, beliefs, desires, and intentions are generated per-scene as instant states. Stage-2 feeds these descriptions into a guessing prompt to identify anonymized characters in test scenes. The approach is benchmarked against prototypical networks, LEOPARD, multi-task learning, and GPT-4 in-context learning, with ablations confirming all five ToM dimensions contribute and that iterative generation over training scenes is critical for capturing short-term mental states.

## Results

On the TOM-IN-AMC benchmark, humans achieve 88.0% instance-level accuracy on the development set, outperforming all AI systems by over 20%. The best AI method, GPT-4-based ToMPro (inductive setting), reaches 68.2% dev / 66.9% test accuracy, while the best non-LLM method LEOPARD achieves 59.4% dev / 58.6% test. In the transductive setting, GPT-4 ICL (20-shot) scores 67.8% dev, and the prototypical network (Longformer-P) reaches 55.4% dev / 53.2% test. Random and majority baselines score only 22.1% and 34.9% respectively, confirming the task's difficulty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ToMPro (GPT-4, inductive) outperforms all baselines: it beats LEOPARD (59.4% dev) by ~9 points, the prototypical network (55.4% dev) by ~13 points, and MTL of classifiers (42.8% dev) by ~25 points. In the transductive setting, GPT-4 ICL (20-shot) at 67.8% dev slightly edges out ToMPro's 68.2% dev, but ToMPro is the only method evaluated on the full test set among the top performers. The prototypical network trained on the prior TVSG dataset (45.9% dev) underperforms the one trained on TOM-IN-AMC (55.4% dev), confirming that character diversity across movies necessitates the meta-learning formulation. Ablations show that removing the iterative update (61.7%) or using a non-iterative 128K-context variant (61.5%) each costs ~6–7 points, validating the recurrent mental-state design. Replacing GPT-4 with GPT-3.5 in ToMPro drops performance from 68.2% to 60.3%, highlighting the importance of the LLM's ToM reasoning quality. Despite these gains, all AI methods remain >20% below the 88.0% human performance, underscoring a substantial gap in machine ToM capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the character-guessing task format from Sang et al. (2022b, TVSG) but reframes it within a meta-learning formulation where each movie constitutes a few-shot task, drawing on the prototypical network and LEOPARD meta-learning literature. It departs from prior ToM benchmarks (which rely on synthetic settings or large per-character data) by introducing a natural few-shot, cross-movie transfer setting that mirrors how humans actually acquire character understanding. The ToMPro prompting method is novel, explicitly decomposing character mental states into five ToM dimensions (personality, emotions, beliefs, desires, intentions) in an iterative, inductive pipeline, building on psychological taxonomies (Baron-Cohen, 1991; Apperly & Butterfill, 2009) rather than prior NLP ToM formulations that focus primarily on belief tracking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~20% performance gap between humans (88%) and the best AI system (GPT-4 ToMPro at 68.2%) highlights a substantial and well-characterized limitation in current LLMs' ToM capabilities, particularly in global character understanding and distinguishing significant from trivial information. The finding that humans resolve 44.8% of instances via stereotype/trope transfer directly validates the meta-learning framing and suggests that future ToM systems should explicitly model cross-context character analogies. Open questions include whether the inductive setting (generating mental descriptions) can be improved to close the gap, whether the iterative personality-update mechanism can avoid error propagation, and whether multi-modal inputs (visual appearance, voice) would narrow the human-AI divide. The benchmark also exposes a memorization vulnerability in LLMs that the name-perturbation protocol mitigates but does not fully eliminate.
