## Problem

Stochastic saddle-point optimization methods (e.g., primal-dual SGD) can diverge because gradient norms grow unboundedly as iterates grow, and standard fixes—projecting to bounded sets—require prior knowledge of the solution norm that is often unavailable. This paper provides convergence guarantees for unconstrained saddle-point problems where gradient noise scales linearly with iterate magnitude, without any projection or a priori bound on the domain.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes noisy subgradient estimates from a stochastic first-order oracle at each iterate, with noise that may scale proportionally to the current iterate norm (e.g., multiplicative noise in bilinear games). It requires only the initial points (x₁, y₁) and step-size parameters—no projection radius or domain bound. The output is the time-average of iterates (x̄_T, ȳ_T) as an approximate saddle point, or in the MDP application, a near-optimal policy π̄_T derived from the averaged occupancy measure. Guarantees hold against arbitrary data-dependent (adaptive) comparator points, including merit-function choices.

## Method

The core technique augments the objective with a quadratic regularization term centered at the initialization (‖x−x₁‖² for the min-player, ‖y−y₁‖² for the max-player), yielding a Composite Objective Mirror Descent-Ascent (COMIDA/COGDA) update. The regularization parameter is tuned so that the negative regularization contribution in one player's regret bound exactly cancels the positive gradient-norm term in the opposite player's bound, eliminating the unbounded gradient norms from the final guarantee. The resulting duality gap scales as O((‖x*−x₁‖² + ‖y*−y₁‖² + 1)/√T), depending only on initialization distance and the gradient norm at the starting point. A 'ghost-iterate' trick (Nemirovski et al., 2009) handles the bias introduced by adaptive comparators. The framework extends from Euclidean bilinear games to sub-bilinear objectives with general Bregman divergences, and is applied to average-reward MDPs to obtain a policy without prior knowledge of the bias span.

## Results

For unconstrained bilinear games, COGDA achieves a duality gap of O((L_M² E[‖x*‖² + ‖y*‖²] + L_b² + L_c²) / (L_M √T)) against arbitrary adaptive comparators, without requiring prior knowledge of the solution norms or any projection. For the more general sub-bilinear setting with Bregman divergences, COMIDA achieves O(L(E[D_x(x*‖x₁) + D_y(y*‖y₁)] + 1) / (√(γ_x γ_y) T)). Applied to average-reward MDPs, COMIDA-MDP finds an ε-optimal policy in O(B⁴ SA log(SA) / ε²) iterations (i.e., O(B⁴ S²A² log(SA) / ε²) simulator queries) without prior knowledge of the bias span B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with standard primal-dual stochastic gradient descent-ascent, whose duality gap bound contains the unbounded terms (η_x/T) Σ E[‖M̂(t)y_t + b̂(t)‖²] + (η_y/T) Σ E[‖M̂(t)ᵀx_t − ĉ(t)‖²], rendering the guarantee vacuous without projection. COGDA/COMIDA replaces these with initialization-dependent terms (2η_y/T) Σ E[‖M̂(t)ᵀx₁ − ĉ(t)‖²] + (2η_x/T) Σ E[‖M̂(t)y₁ + b̂(t)‖²], which are always finite. Compared to projection-based methods, the proposed approach eliminates the need to commit to a projection radius D_x, D_y that must contain the optimum. Against Liu & Orabona (2022), who achieve a slightly stronger Õ((‖x*−x₁‖ + ‖y*−y₁‖)/√T) rate, the present work's guarantee holds under the much weaker assumption of sub-quadratic gradient growth rather than uniformly bounded gradients. For the AMDP application, prior LP-based planners (Wang 2017; Jin & Sidford 2020; Cheng et al. 2020) require prior knowledge of the bias span B to bound the value function domain, whereas COMIDA-MDP operates without this knowledge, at the cost of a B⁴ dependence in the iteration complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stabilization technique of Fang et al. (2022) from single-player unconstrained online convex optimization to the two-player saddle-point setting, and generalizes the nearly identical regularization idea of Jacobsen & Cutkosky (2023) from deterministic Euclidean gradients to stochastic gradients with data-dependent comparators and general Bregman divergences. The algorithmic backbone is Composite Objective Mirror Descent (COMID, Duchi et al., 2010), and the analysis leverages the ghost-iterate trick of Nemirovski et al. (2009) to handle adaptive comparators. The key departure from prior saddle-point methods (extragradient, optimistic mirror descent) is that no projection onto a bounded set is needed: a quadratic regularization term anchored at the initialization replaces the projection entirely, even when gradient noise scales linearly with iterate magnitude.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the first clean convergence guarantee for stochastic saddle-point optimization in truly unconstrained domains with unbounded gradient noise, removing the need for prior knowledge of solution norms that plagued all prior projection-based methods. The application to average-reward MDPs is practically significant: it yields a planning algorithm that finds near-optimal policies without knowing the bias span, a parameter whose estimation is conjectured to be as hard as solving the MDP itself. The main open question is whether the quadratic dependence on initialization error ‖x*−x₁‖²+‖y*−y₁‖² can be improved to linear, which Jacobsen & Cutkosky's lower bound suggests may be impossible in general. The connection to the Halpern iteration hints at broader applicability of the anchoring-regularization trick across optimization and game theory.
