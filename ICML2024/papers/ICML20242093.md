## Problem

Existing counterfactual estimation methods for longitudinal data face two key limitations: (1) balancing strategies (e.g., CRN's gradient reversal, CT's KL-divergence minimization) are designed for categorical treatments and cannot handle continuous or mixed treatment types common in real-world settings; (2) the Direct Predicting (DP) strategy used by CT and CRN only leverages current-time treatments and representations, failing to capture long-range temporal treatment interactions critical in medical applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242093/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a patient's full historical trajectory—time-varying covariates, static covariates (e.g., age, gender), and treatment assignments of arbitrary type (continuous, categorical, or mixed)—and produces counterfactual outcome predictions and predicted covariates for future time steps. Multi-step-ahead prediction uses an autoregressive scheme where predicted outcomes and covariates are recursively fed back as inputs, and the treatment sequence for the prediction horizon is specified by the user. Notably, the framework handles continuous treatments natively (validated on the CISD dataset with Beta-distributed interventions), unlike prior methods restricted to one-hot encoded categorical treatments.

## Method

ACTIN comprises two modules. The balancing module employs a distribution-based adversarial game: a generator (the representation function Φ) learns to produce representations that fool a discriminator distinguishing real samples from the joint distribution P(Φ_t, A_t) from fake samples from the product of marginals P(Φ_t)P(A_t); at equilibrium, representations become statistically independent of treatments, and this mechanism generalizes to any treatment type. The integrating module uses a Temporal Integration Predicting (TIP) strategy that feeds the entire sequence of historical balanced representations and treatments into the prediction head, rather than only the current time point as in DP, thereby capturing long-range dependencies and temporal treatment interactions. The framework is base-model-agnostic (instantiated with TCN or LSTM here), trained end-to-end with a scheduled adversarial weight, EMA parameter averaging, and MSE losses for outcome and covariate prediction, achieving state-of-the-art accuracy at roughly 10% of CT's running time.

## Results

On one-step-ahead prediction (τ=1), ACTIN (TCN-based) achieves the lowest RMSE across all datasets: 0.75±0.06 on TG (γ=0), 1.26±0.21 on TG (γ=4), 4.56±0.10 on RW (MIMIC-III), and 0.15±0.00 on SS (Semi-Synthetic MIMIC-III). On multi-step-ahead prediction over the SS dataset (τ=11), ACTIN reaches 0.54±0.01 RMSE versus 0.60±0.02 for CT. In terms of efficiency, ACTIN's total running time is approximately 10% of CT's on the MIMIC-III-derived datasets, while using fewer parameters and lower computational load (MFLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACTIN (TCN-based) outperforms all four baselines—RMSN, CRN, G-Net, and CT—on every dataset and confounding level tested. Against the strongest baseline CT (Causal Transformer), ACTIN achieves a 1.1 RMSE improvement on TG (γ=4: 1.26 vs. 1.37) and a 0.05 improvement on RW (4.56 vs. 4.60), while the gap widens substantially on SS (0.15 vs. 0.20, a 25% reduction). On multi-step prediction over SS (τ=11), ACTIN leads CT by 0.06 RMSE (0.54 vs. 0.60), and on RW (τ=6) by 0.04 (10.30 vs. 10.34). The LSTM-based variant of ACTIN also slightly surpasses CT on SS multi-step (0.56 vs. 0.60 at τ=11), confirming that the dual-module framework elevates even simpler base models. In terms of efficiency, ACTIN runs at roughly 10% of CT's total time on MIMIC-III datasets, with fewer parameters and lower MFLOPs, making it substantially more practical for real-world deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

ACTIN extends the adversarial balanced-representation paradigm from CRN (Bica et al., 2020) and CT (Melnychuk et al., 2022) by replacing their classification-based balancing (gradient reversal or KL-divergence minimization) with a distribution-based GAN-style discriminator that aligns joint and marginal distributions, thereby generalizing to continuous and mixed treatment types. The Temporal Integration Predicting (TIP) strategy departs from the Direct Predicting (DP) strategy used in CRN and CT by feeding the full history of balanced representations and treatments into the prediction head rather than only the current time step, drawing conceptual inspiration from the autoregressive G-computation framework of G-Net (Li et al., 2021). The dual-module architecture is designed to let comparatively simple base models (TCN, LSTM) close the gap with the Transformer-based CT, positioning ACTIN as an efficiency-oriented alternative rather than a fundamentally new modeling paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving CT-level accuracy at roughly 10% of its running time on MIMIC-III-derived datasets, making counterfactual estimation over time more tractable for large-scale clinical deployment. The distribution-based balancing mechanism removes the categorical-treatment assumption that constrained prior methods (CRN, CT), opening the door to continuous dosing regimens common in oncology and critical care. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that counterfactuals are fundamentally unobservable, so all validation relies on synthetic or semi-synthetic data; the CISD dataset for continuous interventions, while useful, is still a simplified autoregressive process. Open questions include whether the TIP strategy's gains persist under distributional shift (as addressed by CoSTAR) and whether the adversarial balancing objective remains stable at much larger scales or with high-dimensional continuous treatments.
