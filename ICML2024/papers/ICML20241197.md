## Problem

Existing multiple kernel clustering (MKC) algorithms are designed almost entirely from heuristic observations, lacking a statistical understanding of how empirical kernel matrices relate to their expectations. This gap means MKC methods require hyperparameter tuning (impossible without true labels in clustering) and cannot scale to large datasets because they operate on full n×n kernel matrices.

## Input / Output

The method takes V multi-view training sets (each with n samples in R^{d^(v)}), the number of clusters k, and s anchor points (s << n) sampled without replacement from each view. Rather than consuming full n×n kernel matrices, it uses n×s anchor-based kernel similarity matrices G_v where G_v(i,t) = K_v(x_i, a_t), reducing storage to O(Vsn) and computation to O(s²n). The output is a consensus rank-k matrix G* from which cluster assignments are obtained by running k-means on its first k left singular vectors.

## Method

The key theoretical insight is that under isotropic Gaussian data assumptions, the expectation of a Gaussian kernel matrix is close to a rank-k block matrix when cluster means are well-separated, and the empirical kernel concentrates around this expectation at rate Õ(1/√d). The algorithm minimizes an objective that simultaneously (1) finds the best rank-k approximation of each base kernel (denoising via KPCA) and (2) fuses all rank-k approximations into a single rank-k consensus matrix. Optimization alternates between updating each view's rank-k matrix via truncated SVD of (G_v + G*)/2 and updating the consensus via truncated SVD of the average of all views' rank-k matrices. The anchor-based formulation replaces full kernels with sampled columns, yielding O(s²n) complexity linear in n, and the paper provides non-asymptotic bounds showing the anchor-based solution approximates the full-kernel solution at rate O(1/√s).

## Results

On 6 middle-scale benchmark datasets, SMKC achieves the best or near-best clustering performance across all metrics. For example, on Handwritten it reaches 77.44% ACC and 65.78% NMI (vs. 72.11% ACC for the strongest baseline SMKKM), and on Mfeat it attains 94.95% ACC (vs. 94.16% for LKAM). On 5 large-scale datasets (30K–326K samples), SMKC exceeds the best single-view kernel k-means with Nyström by 1.54%–10.69% in NMI while completing in under 320 seconds. Execution time is dramatically lower than competing MKC methods: 49.44 s on Handwritten vs. 401.72 s (SMKKM) and 2045.23 s (LKAM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMKC outperforms the strongest hyper-parameter-free baseline SMKKM (Liu, 2022) on 5 of 6 datasets in ACC (e.g., +5.33% on Handwritten, +2.37% on Mfeat) and on all 6 in NMI, while running 8–17× faster (49.44 s vs. 401.72 s on Handwritten; 112.88 s vs. 1960.15 s on ALOI-100). Against the weight-optimized MKC methods (MKKM, ONKC, MKKM-MiR, LKAM, LMKKM), SMKC wins on ACC for 5/6 datasets and on NMI for all 6, with the largest margin over LKAM on Handwritten (+8.06% ACC) and over ONKC on Wiki (+5.63% ACC). On the 5 large-scale datasets (30K–326K samples), SMKC consistently surpasses the best single-view kernel k-means with Nyström (Kumar et al., 2012) by 1.54%–10.69% in NMI (e.g., 97.74% vs. 78.85% on CIFAR-10; 55.07% vs. 48.36% on Winnipeg), confirming that multi-view fusion adds substantial value. The only near-tie is 100Leaves ACC where SMKKM edges SMKC by 0.04%, though SMKC still leads in NMI (92.50% vs. 92.39%) and is 4× faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multiple kernel clustering (MKC) lineage from MKKM (Huang et al., 2012) through SMKKM (Liu, 2022) and the consistency/stability analyses of Liang et al. (2022, 2023), but departs from the dominant heuristic weight-optimization paradigm by grounding the fusion objective in the statistical properties of kernel matrix expectations. Specifically, it leverages the anchor-based sampling framework from Yu et al. (2023, 2024a) and Nyström-style kernel approximation (Wang et al., 2019) to achieve scalability, while the core algorithmic idea—replacing kernel weight learning with rank-k low-rank approximation of each base kernel—draws on KPCA and best-rank-k approximation theory (Cohen et al., 2015). The key conceptual departure is using the expectation kernel's near-rank-k structure (under Gaussian data with well-separated means) as the theoretical justification for the entire optimization objective, rather than treating kernel fusion as a purely empirical design choice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: prior MKC methods lack statistical justification for their fusion objectives, and the hyperparameter-free property is practically important since clustering tasks lack ground-truth labels for tuning. The theoretical concentration bound (O(1/√d)) and the non-asymptotic approximation guarantee for the anchor-based variant provide a principled error analysis that was absent from the MKC literature. However, the method's applicability is constrained by its isotropic Gaussian assumption, equal view weighting, and exclusive use of Frobenius norm—limitations the authors acknowledge. Open questions include whether the rank-k expectation structure holds for non-Gaussian or anisotropic data, whether view-specific weighting can be incorporated without losing the hyperparameter-free property, and whether the theoretical guarantees tighten with more sophisticated sampling strategies.
