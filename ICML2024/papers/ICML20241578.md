## Problem

The Mapper graph is a foundational tool in Topological Data Analysis for encoding geometric and topological structures of data, but its most critical parameter—the filter function—has never been automatically optimized. While heuristics exist for tuning resolution, gain, and clustering, the filter is a general continuous function over a high-dimensional parameter space, making it intractable for existing methods. This paper provides the first principled, differentiable optimization scheme for Mapper filter functions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a point cloud X_n in a metric space (or R^p for gene-expression data) and a parameterized family of scalar filter functions {f_θ: X_n → R, θ ∈ R^s} (e.g., linear projections, Gaussian kernel combinations, or small neural networks). It outputs the optimized parameter vector θ̄ and the corresponding regular Mapper graph. The point cloud is consumed through a stochastic cover assignment scheme where each point is independently and probabilistically assigned to overlapping intervals of the filter range, with assignment probabilities governed by smooth bump functions of width δ.

## Method

The core construction is Soft Mapper, a probability distribution over Mapper graphs obtained by replacing the hard 0/1 point-to-interval assignments of standard Mapper with independent Bernoulli variables whose success probabilities are smooth bump functions (exp(1−1/(1−t²)) type) of the filter values. This relaxation makes the distribution of Mapper complexes differentiable in the filter parameters, which the standard hard assignment is not (it is discontinuous at interval boundaries). The optimization minimizes a topological risk—the expected persistence-based loss (e.g., negative L₁ total persistence) over the Soft Mapper distribution—via stochastic gradient descent with Monte Carlo sampling of cover assignments. Convergence to critical points is guaranteed by proving the risk is definable in an o-minimal structure and locally Lipschitz, leveraging the smoothness of the bump functions and results from tame optimization theory (Davis et al., 2020).

## Results

On 3D shapes, the optimized linear filter directions recover the intuitively optimal directions with cosine distances of 0.9999 (human), −0.9984 (octopus), and 0.9993 (table), confirming near-perfect recovery. On the human preimplantation RNA-seq dataset, the Pearson correlation between the optimized filter and sampling time improves dramatically from 0.133 (initial) to −0.755 (optimized), with a p-value dropping from 1.76×10⁻⁷ to 4.05×10⁻²⁸². Clustering scores (Rand, MI, Completeness, FM) consistently improve after optimization across all four datasets, with the largest gains on the octopus shape (e.g., FM from 0.298 to 0.708) and the sctda dataset (e.g., Rand from 0.010 to 0.381).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the optimized Soft Mapper against four baselines: PCA, t-SNE, UMAP, and Mapper with an arbitrary (initial) filter. On the 3D shapes, the optimized Mapper substantially outperforms the initial Mapper on all clustering metrics (e.g., octopus FM: 0.708 vs. 0.298; human MI: 0.563 vs. 0.425), and also beats t-SNE and UMAP on most metrics, though t-SNE retains an edge on octopus Completeness (0.641 vs. 0.519). On the sctda RNA-seq dataset, the optimized Mapper achieves the best Rand (0.381) and FM (0.581) scores, surpassing UMAP (0.266, 0.552) and t-SNE (0.259, 0.553), while PCA dominates on Completeness (0.919) due to its linear structure. The MEF reprogramming experiment (Appendix I) further confirms the trend: Pearson correlation with time improves from −0.056 to −0.402 after optimization. Overall, the optimized Mapper consistently wins or ties on the majority of metrics, with the largest relative gains observed when the initial filter is poorly aligned with the data's topological structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mapper graph framework (Singh et al., 2007) by introducing Soft Mapper, a probabilistic relaxation that replaces deterministic interval assignments with Bernoulli random variables, making the construction amenable to gradient-based optimization. It builds directly on the persistent homology optimization framework of Carrière et al. (2021) and the o-minimal convergence theory of Davis et al. (2020) to provide the first filter optimization scheme for Mapper graphs with provable convergence guarantees. The work departs from prior parameter-tuning methods (Carrière et al., 2018) that addressed only resolution, gain, and clustering, and from ensemble approaches (Kang & Lim, 2021; Fitzpatrick et al., 2023) that aggregate multiple Mapper graphs rather than identifying a single optimal filter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a clear gap in the Mapper literature by making the filter function—the most critical and previously untreatable parameter—optimizable with theoretical guarantees, opening the door to fully automated Mapper construction. However, the empirical validation is limited to linear filter families in the main experiments, small Monte Carlo sample sizes (M=10), and relatively small datasets, leaving open questions about scalability to high-dimensional or non-linear filter families and whether total persistence as a loss aligns with downstream task performance. The contrast between PCA's maximum-variance criterion and the persistence-based criterion (e.g., the table shape) suggests the approach captures genuinely different geometric information, but broader validation across diverse data types and loss functions is needed.
