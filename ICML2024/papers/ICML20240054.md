## Problem

Graph matching between correlated Erdős-Rényi graphs is well understood in the absence of corruption, but real-world networks (social media, protein-protein interaction) suffer from node-level corruptions where a subset of nodes interact randomly or adversarially with their network. This paper is the first to study fundamental limits of graph matching under node corruptions, establishing what can and cannot be recovered when a positive fraction of nodes are compromised.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240054/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of corrupted graphs (G̃₁, G̃₂) on n nodes each, generated from correlated ER(n,p) graphs with edge-correlation parameter s, after a fraction γ of nodes (split by ratio λ between the two graphs) have their incident edges resampled (weak adversary) or arbitrarily rewired (strong adversary). The output is a matching μ (an injective function on a subset of [n]) attempting to recover the latent permutation π*; the k-core estimator additionally identifies corrupted nodes by excluding them from its domain, while the maximum overlap estimator always outputs a complete permutation.

## Method

The paper introduces two adversary models—weak (random node subset with edges resampled from Bern(q)) and strong (adversarial subset with edges set arbitrarily after observing the graphs)—and derives matching impossibility and achievability thresholds. For the weak adversary, an indistinguishability argument proves no estimator can correctly match any positive fraction of corrupted nodes, while the k-core estimator with k=√(log n) achieves optimal recovery of all uncorrupted nodes when p = C log(n)/n and C > 1/(s²α*), where α* = 1−γ+λ(1−λ)γ²; below this threshold it still recovers almost all uncorrupted nodes but cannot identify the corrupted set. For the strong adversary, detection is shown impossible via a relabeling argument, yet the maximum overlap estimator achieves α-recovery when only one graph is corrupted, with explicit bounds on the maximum tolerable corruption fraction γ in terms of s and α. The proofs combine Chernoff concentration, hypergeometric distribution analysis, orbit decomposition of permutations for bounding the maximum overlap, and k-core structural results from random graph theory.

## Results

The paper establishes fundamental limits for graph matching under node corruption. Under the weak adversary, no estimator can correctly match any positive fraction of corrupted nodes (Theorem 3.1), and the maximum achievable recovery fraction is α* = 1 − γ + λ(1−λ)γ². The k-core estimator with k = √(log n) achieves α*-recovery when the edge probability satisfies p = C log(n)/n with C > 1/(s²α*), and achieves almost α*-recovery (matching all but a vanishing fraction of uncorrupted nodes) when C < 1/(s²α*). Under the strong adversary, detection of corrupted nodes is impossible, but the maximum overlap estimator still achieves α-recovery when only one network is compromised and γ < s(1−α²)/4 (logarithmic degree) or γ < 1 − √(1 − s²p(1−p)(1−α²)/2) (constant degree).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the k-core estimator's asymptotic guarantee against three computationally feasible algorithms in simulations (Figure 1, n=1000, p=0.1, λ=1): GRAMPA (spectral matching, Fan et al. 2022), Degree Profiling (Ding et al. 2021), and Canonical Labeling (Dai et al. 2019). All three feasible algorithms show a large gap relative to the k-core estimator's theoretical guarantee, with their recovery fractions dropping sharply as the corruption fraction γ increases. The paper notes that these algorithms are not robust even to the baseline random noise in the no-adversary setting (they require s→1 for good performance), and consequently they are also not robust to the spatial noise induced by node corruption. The k-core estimator, while not polynomial-time, provides the optimal theoretical benchmark: it precisely identifies and matches all uncorrupted nodes while excluding all corrupted ones under the stated conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-studied problem of matching correlated Erdős-Rényi graphs (initiated by Pedarsani & Grossglauser, 2011, and refined by Cullina & Kiyavash, 2017) to a setting where a linear fraction of nodes are corrupted, which the authors identify as the first such study. The k-core estimator analysis directly adapts techniques from Cullina et al. (2019) for partial recovery and Gaudio et al. (2022) / Rácz & Sridhar (2023) for SBM and inhomogeneous random graphs, while the robustness framing draws on parallel work on adversarial node corruptions in parameter estimation (Acharya et al., 2022) and community detection (Liu & Moitra, 2022; Hua et al., 2023). The key departure is shifting the corruption model from edge-level noise to node-level corruption, where a single corrupted node can invalidate O(n) edges, creating spatially clustered noise that is fundamentally different from the independent edge noise assumed in all prior matching results.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes clean impossibility and achievability thresholds that delineate exactly what is and isn't possible under node corruption, providing a theoretical foundation for robust network analysis in social and biological domains. A critical open question is the performance of computationally feasible algorithms (GRAMPA, Degree Profiling, Canonical Labeling) under node corruption, since the optimal k-core and maximum overlap estimators are exponential-time; simulations reveal a large gap between theory and practice. The strong adversary result that detection is impossible yet partial matching remains feasible (when only one graph is corrupted) has direct practical implications for network sanitization and privacy. The case where both graphs are corrupted under the strong adversary remains open, as does the design of polynomial-time algorithms with provable guarantees in the node-corruption regime.
