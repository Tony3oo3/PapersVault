## Problem

Despite their success in NLP and vision, transformers remain inferior to simpler linear and MLP-based models in multivariate long-term time series forecasting. The paper identifies the root cause as training instability: the attention mechanism induces entropy collapse, leading to sharp loss landscapes and convergence to poor local minima, a problem that prior work on efficient attention or temporal decomposition had not addressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240841/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a D×L matrix (D features, L=512 lookback timesteps) as input, arranged with features as rows to enable channel-wise attention over a D×D matrix rather than the conventional L×L temporal attention. It produces a D×H prediction matrix for horizons H ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720}. Reversible Instance Normalization (RevIN) is applied to the input before the network and inverted on the output, making the architecture invariant to per-feature mean and scale shifts between training and test distributions.

## Method

SAMformer is a single-layer, single-head transformer with the forward pass f(X) = [X + A(X)XW_V W_O] W, where A(X) is a channel-wise attention matrix (D×D) computed via softmax of XW_Q W_K^T X^T / √d_m, followed by a residual connection and a linear projection to the prediction horizon. The key training innovation is Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM), which replaces the standard MSE objective with a max over a ρ-neighborhood, steering optimization toward flatter minima and preventing the attention entropy collapse that causes overfitting. The paper provides a theoretical analysis (Proposition 2.2) showing why the alternative σ-Reparam fix fails: it reduces the nuclear norm of the attention scores, inducing rank collapse that excludes features from the attention mechanism. With d_m=16 and no feed-forward block, SAMformer has ~4× fewer parameters than TSMixer while outperforming it by 14.33% on average across eight standard benchmarks.

## Results

On 8 standard multivariate long-term forecasting benchmarks (ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2, Electricity, Exchange, Traffic, Weather) with prediction horizons H ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720}, SAMformer achieves an overall MSE improvement of 5.25% over TSMixer+SAM (its closest competitor) and 14.33% over standalone TSMixer. SAMformer has ~4× fewer parameters than TSMixer (average ratio 3.73 across datasets and horizons) while consistently ranking first or second on every dataset-horizon combination except Exchange. Against the foundation model MOIRAI (up to 314M parameters), SAMformer improves overall MSE by 1.1% (vs. MOIRAI-Base) to 7.6% (vs. MOIRAI-Large) while using orders of magnitude fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAMformer outperforms TSMixer (the prior SOTA MLP-based model) by 14.33% overall MSE, and still leads TSMixer trained with SAM by 5.25%, while using ~4× fewer parameters. Against transformer-based baselines, SAMformer beats FEDformer by 12.36%, PatchTST by 11.13%, and iTransformer by 3.94% overall (8.38% excluding Exchange, where iTransformer is the clear winner at all horizons). Informer and Autoformer are substantially outperformed across all datasets (e.g., on ETTh1 H=96, Informer reports 0.941 vs. SAMformer's 0.381). Against the large foundation model MOIRAI (14M–314M parameters, pretrained on ~27B samples), SAMformer is competitive or superior on most datasets, improving overall MSE by 6.9% over MOIRAI-Small, 1.1% over MOIRAI-Base, and 7.6% over MOIRAI-Large. The only dataset where SAMformer is consistently outperformed is Exchange, where iTransformer and PatchTST achieve substantially lower MSE (0.086 vs. 0.161 at H=96), suggesting that channel-wise attention is less suited to that particular data structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant trend of making time-series transformers more architecturally complex (efficient attention, frequency decomposition, patching) and instead identifies training instability—specifically attention entropy collapse and sharp loss landscapes—as the root cause of transformers' underperformance relative to simple MLP baselines like TSMixer. It builds on the Transformer architecture (Vaswani et al., 2017), the SAM optimization framework (Foret et al., 2021), and RevIN normalization (Kim et al., 2021b), while explicitly analyzing and rejecting the σ-Reparam fix from Zhai et al. (2023) via a theoretical rank-collapse argument. The channel-wise attention design is borrowed from computer vision (Restormer, ResNeSt) rather than the temporal attention standard in time-series transformers like Informer, Autoformer, or iTransformer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single-layer, single-head transformer (~50K parameters) trained with SAM matches or beats a 314M-parameter foundation model (MOIRAI) and the MLP SOTA (TSMixer) by 14% challenges the prevailing narrative that transformers need architectural complexity or scale to be competitive in time-series forecasting. The counterintuitive result that channel-wise (D×D) attention outperforms temporal (L×L) attention opens a new design axis for the field. Limitations include the single-layer constraint, the clear loss on the Exchange dataset to iTransformer, and evaluation on only 8 relatively small benchmarks; whether the SAM + channel-wise attention recipe scales to deeper transformers or larger, more diverse datasets remains an open question.
