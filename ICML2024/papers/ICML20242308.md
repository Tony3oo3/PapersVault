## Problem

Multiagent reinforcement learning suffers from low sample efficiency, requiring enormous numbers of expensive environment interactions to learn good joint policies. While increasing the replay ratio (network updates per interaction) is a natural strategy to reduce this cost, prior MARL work has not successfully trained at high replay ratios in the practical parallel environment setting, where the resulting overfitting and network plasticity loss destabilize learning.

## Input / Output

MARR operates on standard off-policy MARL transition tuples (global state, per-agent local observations, joint actions, rewards, next state, next observations) drawn from a replay buffer populated by parallel environments. The random amplitude scale augmentation multiplies all observation and state vectors by a shared random scalar z ~ U(0.8, 1.2) per transition, preserving sign structure while randomizing magnitude. The method is plug-and-play: it wraps existing off-policy MARL algorithms (QMIX, QPLEX, ATM for discrete actions; MADDPG, FACMAC for continuous actions) without modifying their network architectures or loss functions.

## Method

MARR enables MARL training at replay ratios up to 50 (SMAC) and 25 (MPE) in parallel environments through two synergistic mechanisms. First, a Shrink & Perturb reset strategy periodically interpolates each agent's policy/Q-network and the centralized critic toward their initial parameters (θ_t ← αθ_t + (1−α)θ_0, with α=0.8 every 2000 steps), re-injecting the plasticity that high-replay-ratio training erodes. Second, a random amplitude scale augmentation multiplies sampled observations and states by a random scalar consistent across the current and next time step of each transition, forcing the network to learn amplitude-invariant representations across the many repeated updates. The key insight is that parallel environments populate the replay buffer with more diverse experiences, making high replay ratios beneficial for data utilization, but only the Shrink & Perturb reset prevents the catastrophic plasticity loss that otherwise limits replay ratio scaling to modest values.

## Results

On SMAC (discrete actions), MARR scales the replay ratio to 50 (vs. default 1) and dramatically improves test win rate across all 7 scenarios within 1M environment interactions, with the largest gains on super-hard maps (3s5z_vs_3s6z, corridor) where base ATM achieves near-0% win rate even at 5M steps while MARR-ATM reaches good performance at 1M steps. On MPE (continuous actions), MARR scales the replay ratio to 25 and improves both MADDPG and FACMAC on the 3-agent predator-prey task, with MARR variants at 2M steps outperforming base algorithms at 5M steps. The ablation confirms Shrink & Perturb is the critical component (removing it drops performance to near-base levels at N_RR=50), while random amplitude scale provides additional gains on top.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MARR-QMIX outperforms standard QMIX by a large margin on all 7 SMAC scenarios (2s_vs_1sc, 2s3z, 3s5z, 10m_vs_11m, 5m_vs_6m, 3s5z_vs_3s6z, corridor) within 1M steps, and at 1M steps MARR-QMIX exceeds QMIX at 5M steps. MARR-QPLEX similarly dominates QPLEX across all SMAC maps. MARR-ATM is particularly impactful on super-hard scenarios where ATM alone fails (near 0% win rate at 5M steps) but MARR-ATM achieves competitive performance at 1M steps. On MPE, MARR-FACMAC improves over FACMAC on all three predator-prey tasks (3a, 6a, 9a), while MARR-MADDPG improves on 3a but fails to boost MADDPG on 6a and 9a, likely because MADDPG itself struggles with those scenarios. In the plasticity-technique comparison (Figure 7), MARR outperforms L2 Init (Kumar et al.), weight decay, multiagent permutation (MAP, Ye et al.), and layer normalization on both 5m_vs_6m and 10m_vs_11m at N_RR=50.

## Relation to Prior Work

MARR extends the single-agent plasticity-loss literature (Nikishin et al., 2022; D'Oro et al., 2023; Lyle et al., 2023) into the multiagent setting, where no prior work had addressed the overfitting-to-early-experiences problem under high replay ratios. It adapts the Shrink & Perturb reset strategy (Ash & Adams, 2020) and the random amplitude scale augmentation (Laskin et al., 2020) to the multiagent case, combining them for the first time to enable high-replay-ratio training in parallel MARL environments. This departs from the dominant permutation-based sample-efficiency line (Ye et al., 2022; Yu et al., 2023; Hao et al., 2023) by targeting data utilization through repeated updates rather than structural symmetries in the multiagent system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MARR offers a drop-in, algorithm-agnostic technique that reduces expensive environment interactions by an order of magnitude, which is critical for real-world MARL deployments where simulation or physical interaction is costly. The finding that parallel environments naturally support higher replay ratios than series settings provides a practical design principle for MARL training pipelines. Key limitations include inapplicability to on-policy algorithms, sensitivity to noisy data under high replay ratios, and the open question of whether the optimal replay ratio is scenario-dependent. Extending MARR to offline or continual MARL settings, and understanding the underlying mechanism of plasticity loss in multiagent dynamics, remain important future directions.
