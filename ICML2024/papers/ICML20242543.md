## Problem

Standard Split Conformal Prediction guarantees valid coverage only under i.i.d. or exchangeable calibration data, yet many practical settings (time series, reinforcement learning, adaptive control) produce samples that are correlated and naturally modelled as a Markov chain. This paper quantifies the coverage gap and prediction-set-size inflation induced by such correlations, and proposes a simple thinning strategy to recover near-optimal guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242543/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained regression model, a sequential calibration dataset of n (X,Y) pairs drawn from a Markov chain, a new covariate X_{n+1}, and a significance level α. For K-split CP, only every K-th calibration sample is retained, reducing the effective calibration size to n/K. The output is a prediction set C_n(X_{n+1}) = {y : s(X_{n+1},y) ≤ q̂_{α,n}} (a prediction interval in the real-valued case), with a coverage guarantee of 1−α up to an explicit gap that depends on the chain's mixing time t_mix.

## Method

The paper first proves that applying classical Split CP to a geometrically ergodic Markov chain yields a coverage gap of O(√(t_mix · ln(n)/n)) via McDiarmid-type concentration inequalities for Markov chains. To reduce this gap, K-split CP thins the calibration set by retaining every K-th sample, and the authors apply a blocking technique (tracing back to Bernstein, 1927) to show the gap drops to O(t_mix/(n·ln(n))) when K is chosen optimally. The optimal thinning parameter K* = W_0(n²(ln ρ)²)/ln(1/ρ) (involving the Lambert W function) balances the coverage gap against the finite-sample quantile correction K/n. An adaptive variant estimates the chain's spectral gap γ from the training data to compute K̂_N online, with only a negligible additional O(ln(N)/√N) perturbation to the coverage guarantee. The size of the prediction interval is shown to be asymptotically optimal (converging to the oracle interval [μ(x)−q_α, μ(x)+q_α]) under mild regularity assumptions on the noise density.

## Results

The paper establishes that the coverage gap of standard Split CP under Markovian data scales as O(√(t_mix · ln(n)/n)), while the proposed K-split CP (thinning every K-th calibration sample) reduces this to O(t_mix/(n·ln(n))), a factor of √(n)·ln(n)^(3/2) improvement. The optimal thinning parameter is K* = W₀(n²(ln ρ)²)/ln(1/ρ), which can be estimated online from the training data. On synthetic data (lazy random walk with ρ=0.97, Gaussian AR(1) with θ=0.9) and real-world data (EUR/SEK with ρ=0.57, French electricity price with ρ=0.78), all methods achieve approximately 1−α coverage as n grows, with K-split CP showing tighter coverage at smaller n.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the original Split CP (Vovk et al., 2005), which assumes exchangeable calibration data. K-split CP strictly outperforms Split CP on the coverage gap: theoretically, the gap shrinks from O(√(t_mix · ln(n)/n)) to O(t_mix/(n·ln(n))), and empirically, K-split CP shows less deviation from the target 1−α level at moderate n (visible in Figures 1 and 3). The cost is a negligible increase in prediction interval size, growing only by a factor of √(ln(n)) in the high-probability quantile bound. The paper explicitly does not compare to Adaptive Conformal Inference (ACI) methods (Gibbs & Candes, 2021; Zaffran et al., 2022), which adjust the confidence level online, noting that ACI targets non-stationary dependent data rather than the Markovian setting studied here. On the real-world EUR/SEK and electricity price experiments, all three variants (Split CP, K-split CP, Corrected K-split CP) achieve comparable coverage close to 1−α, with differences most visible at smaller calibration set sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Split Conformal Prediction framework (Vovk et al., 2005) to Markovian data, building most directly on Oliveira et al. (2022), who analyzed Split CP under general β-mixing assumptions. The key departures are: (1) leveraging the Markovian structure to obtain sharper, more explicit coverage-gap bounds via Markov-chain concentration inequalities rather than generic β-mixing arguments; (2) dropping the stationarity assumption required by Oliveira et al.; and (3) introducing K-split CP, a thinning-based method analyzed via the blocking technique that achieves a fundamentally better coverage-gap rate. The paper also positions itself against the adaptive quantile-adjustment approach of Gibbs & Candes (2021) and Zaffran et al. (2022), which modifies α online rather than restructuring the calibration set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first explicit, mixing-time-parameterized bounds on the coverage gap and prediction-set size inflation for Split CP under Markovian data, giving practitioners a clear diagnostic: the gap is negligible when n ≫ t_mix. The K-split CP method is a simple, plug-and-play modification (keep every K-th calibration sample) that reduces the coverage gap by a factor of √n·ln(n)^{3/2} without meaningfully enlarging prediction intervals, and the adaptive variant removes the need to know the mixing rate a priori. Open questions include fundamental limits on the coverage-versus-size tradeoff for any conformal method under Markovian data, and what happens when the chain mixes very slowly (t_mix comparable to n), where the current bounds become vacuous.
