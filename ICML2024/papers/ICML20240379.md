## Problem

Standard Mean Field Control (MFC) for MARL assumes all agents are homogeneous and independent, summarized solely by their empirical distribution. In practice, systems often contain a few complex agents (e.g., traffic lights, a moving truck) whose actions have non-negligible effects that cannot be captured by the mean field alone. No discrete-time, cooperative MFC framework incorporating such major agents existed prior to this work.

## Input / Output

The policy network receives the major agent state, the empirical mean field distribution of minor agents (represented as a finite-dimensional vector for discrete state spaces or an M-bin histogram for continuous spaces, with M=49 by default), and optionally individual minor agent states for decentralized execution. It outputs a joint M3FC action: a matrix ξ ∈ [-1,1]^(X×U) mapped to conditional minor action probabilities π'(u|x) via a softmax-like transform, plus a major agent action (categorical or diagonal Gaussian). Individual minor agents then sample their own actions from π'(·|x_i) given their local state, enabling decentralized execution from a centrally trained policy.

## Method

The paper formulates the first discrete-time Major-Minor Mean Field Control (M3FC) model, where the limiting system is a single-agent MDP with state (x⁰, μ) and action (h, u⁰), with h being a joint state-action distribution over the minor agents' state-action space. The key algorithmic contribution (M3FMARL, instantiated as M3FPPO) applies standard single-agent PPO directly to the finite N-agent system rather than the intractable limiting MDP, leveraging the fact that propagation of chaos guarantees O(1/√N) convergence between the two. Theoretically, the paper establishes existence of optimal stationary deterministic policies via a Bellman equation over the M3FC MDP, proves uniform propagation of chaos convergence under Lipschitz assumptions, and shows that finite-agent policy gradients computed in Algorithm 1 uniformly approximate the true M3FC MDP policy gradient as N→∞. The hierarchical structure—where one M3FC action specifies behavior for all minor agents simultaneously—resolves the credit assignment problem inherent in cooperative MARL by assigning aggregated credit to the joint action rather than to individual agents.

## Results

On a system with N=20 agents, M3FPPO achieves the best mean episode return on 4 out of 5 benchmark problems. The largest gains are on Foraging (1479.4 vs. 803.9 for MAPPO and 735.3 for IPPO) and Beach (−303.5 vs. −342.9 for MAPPO). On 2G, M3FPPO reaches −22.2 compared to −26.0 (MAPPO) and −43.9 (IPPO). The only problem where M3FPPO trails is Potential (−31.3 vs. −26.7 for MAPPO and −27.1 for IPPO), though it still substantially outperforms M3FA2C (−50.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

M3FPPO is compared against IPPO (independent PPO with parameter sharing) and MAPPO (multi-agent PPO with centralized critics), both standard state-of-the-art cooperative MARL baselines. M3FPPO outperforms both on 4 of 5 problems: on Foraging it nearly doubles MAPPO's return (1479.4 vs. 803.9) and nearly doubles IPPO's (735.3); on Beach it improves over MAPPO by ~40 points (−303.5 vs. −342.9); on 2G it beats MAPPO by ~4 points (−22.2 vs. −26.0); and on Formation it beats MAPPO by ~37 points (−63.9 vs. −101.1). The sole exception is Potential, where MAPPO (−26.7) and IPPO (−27.1) marginally outperform M3FPPO (−31.3) by roughly 4–5 points. Notably, M3FPPO achieves these results while being trained on N=300 agents and transferred to N=20, with significantly less per-agent control (all minor agents in the same state bin share the same action distribution), whereas IPPO/MAPPO are trained directly on N=20 with full per-agent action control.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discrete-time mean field control (MFC) framework for cooperative MARL by introducing major agents—individual agents whose actions have non-negligible, non-aggregated effects on the system. Prior MFC-based MARL work (Carmona et al., 2023b; Gu et al., 2021; 2023) assumed all agents are minor and interchangeable, summarized solely by their empirical distribution, while major agents had only been studied in continuous-time, non-cooperative MFGs (Nourian and Caines, 2013; Sen and Caines, 2014). The paper also subsumes common noise and global environment state extensions (Carmona et al., 2016; Mondal et al., 2023) as special cases of the major agent formulation, and departs from prior MFC-based RL by analyzing and training directly on finite-agent systems rather than the infinite-agent limit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The M3FC framework provides a principled, tractable route to cooperative MARL in heterogeneous systems where a few agents (e.g., a truck, a traffic light) cannot be absorbed into the mean field of many others (e.g., drones, cars). The propagation-of-chaos and near-optimality guarantees (Theorem 2, Corollary 1) give theoretical backing to the practical approach of solving a fixed-complexity single-agent MDP and transferring the policy to finite systems of varying size. Open questions include extending convergence rate analysis to non-finite state spaces, handling local (non-global) interactions, moving beyond binning to kernel-based MF parametrizations, and validating the framework on larger, more realistic domains beyond the five synthetic benchmarks.
