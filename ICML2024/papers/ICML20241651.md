## Problem

Existing group distributional robustness methods like GroupDRO optimize only the worst-performing group, which leads to rapid overfitting and a growing performance gap between majority and minority groups over training. Moreover, directly applying multi-group balancing to full networks is computationally prohibitive, and no prior method offers a controllable trade-off between worst-group and average performance. This paper addresses both the optimization gap (balancing all groups simultaneously rather than focusing on one) and the scalability gap (making the balancing procedure feasible for large transformer backbones).

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (224×224 for ViT/ResNet, 336×336 for CLIP ViT-L/14) paired with group annotations during training, and for CLIP additionally uses text class-name embeddings processed through a frozen text encoder. Only a small set of learnable prompt tokens (e.g., 94k parameters for ViT-B/16, 8k–12k for CLIP variants) is updated, while the backbone remains frozen. The output is a standard class label prediction, evaluated via worst-group accuracy, average accuracy, and mean (unweighted) accuracy across groups.

## Method

CPT replaces the single worst-group gradient of GroupDRO with a K-dimensional optimization that finds a convex combination of all group gradients maximizing an entropy-based balancing objective, solved as a small linear program at each iteration. The entropy term H(softmax(L(θ))) encourages the model to equalize loss magnitudes across groups, while a user-defined controlling vector c scales individual group losses to adjust the worst-group vs. average-performance trade-off. To keep the method scalable, the optimization is decoupled from the backbone via prompt tuning: for ViT, learnable tokens are prepended to patch embeddings at each layer; for CLIP, a learnable context prompt replaces the hand-crafted text prompt. This design limits trainable parameters to ~0.4% of the full model, enabling multiple controlling vectors (each with its own prompt set) at negligible additional cost.

## Results

On Waterbirds, CPT achieves 93.5% worst-group accuracy and 96.3% average accuracy using only 94k trainable parameters (0.4% of the full model), surpassing the previous best DFR (92.9% worst, 94.2% avg, 23M params) and GroupDRO (86.7% worst, 93.2% avg). On CelebA, CPT reaches 92.0% worst-group and 93.2% average accuracy, outperforming PDE (91.0% worst) and GroupDRO (86.3% worst). On MetaShift and ISIC, CPT achieves 77.0% worst-group accuracy and 64.9% AUROC respectively, exceeding the prior state-of-the-art DISC by ~3.5% and ~10 AUROC points while using 47k parameters versus 23M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPT outperforms all 23 baselines on worst-group accuracy across all four datasets. On Waterbirds, CPT (93.5%) beats the closest competitor DFR (92.9%) by 0.6% and GroupDRO (86.7%) by 6.8%, while also achieving the best average accuracy (96.3%) among all robustness methods, nearly matching ERM's 97.1% without its catastrophic 70.0% worst-group failure. On CelebA, CPT (92.0% worst) surpasses PDE (91.0%) and CGD (90.0%), the two strongest baselines. On MetaShift, CPT (77.0% worst) exceeds DISC (73.5%) by 3.5% and GroupDRO (66.0%) by 11%; on ISIC, CPT (64.9% AUROC) leads DISC (55.1%) by ~10 points. A critical efficiency advantage: CPT uses only 94k parameters (0.4%) versus 23M for all full-fine-tuning baselines, and on CLIP it uses just 8,192–12,288 parameters while still outperforming full fine-tuning (14M+ params) on worst-group accuracy (e.g., 79.8% vs. 77.1% on CLIP-RN50). The controllable vector c further allows CPT to trade off worst-group vs. average performance, something none of the fixed-reweighting baselines (GroupDRO, UW, Subsample) can do.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GroupDRO's worst-group optimization by replacing its single-group focus with a multi-objective optimization framework inspired by MGDA, which finds a descent direction benefiting all groups simultaneously. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of an entropy-based balancing objective that equalizes loss magnitudes across groups, combined with a controllable vector c to adjust group priorities. To make this scalable when multiple c values are needed, the authors couple the optimization with prompt tuning (following Lester et al.), freezing the backbone and training only lightweight prompt tokens. This positions the work at the intersection of distributional robustness, multi-objective optimization, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method achieves state-of-the-art worst-group accuracy on four benchmarks while updating only 0.4% of parameters, making it practical for debiasing large pretrained models like CLIP and ViT where full fine-tuning is expensive. The controllable vector c offers a flexible trade-off between worst-group and average performance that prior DRO methods lack. A key limitation is that different c values still require separately trained prompt sets; the authors suggest hypernetworks as a path toward end-to-end controllability. Open questions include whether the entropy-based balancing generalizes to settings with many groups (beyond K=4) and whether the approach extends to non-visual modalities.
