## Problem

Offline RL performance degrades sharply when the dataset contains very few high-reward trajectories, because the agent cannot learn how to transition from low-reward states to high-reward regions. Existing data augmentation methods (SER, TATU) generate short, unconditioned sub-trajectories that do not explicitly connect low-reward trajectories to high-reward ones, leaving the critical gap between them unfilled.

## Input / Output

The method takes an offline dataset of pre-collected trajectories and selects a pair consisting of a low-reward trajectory and a high-reward trajectory. It uses the terminal state of the low-reward trajectory and the initial state of the high-reward trajectory as boundary conditions, with a diffusion horizon H (100 for locomotion, 56 for kitchen/Adroit-pen). The output is a stitching sub-trajectory of Δ intermediate states, actions, and rewards that bridges the two trajectories, which is appended to form an augmented trajectory; the final augmented dataset D* = D ∪ D_aug is then used to train any offline RL algorithm at a configurable original-to-augmented data ratio.

## Method

DiffStitch is a four-module pipeline: (1) a step estimation module uses a conditional U-Net diffusion model to imagine future states from the low-reward trajectory's terminal state, then selects the number of stitching steps Δ by finding the imagined state with maximum cosine similarity to the high-reward trajectory's initial state; (2) a state stitching module constructs a masked sequence containing both boundary states and Δ mask tokens, and denoises it with the diffusion model to generate intermediate states; (3) a trajectory wrap-up module uses a 2-layer MLP inverse dynamics model to predict actions between adjacent states and a 4-layer MLP reward model to predict rewards; (4) a qualification module uses a forward dynamics MLP to verify each generated transition stays within a threshold δ of the predicted next state, discarding trajectories that violate environmental dynamics. The key design choice is diffusing only over states (since actions and rewards are less smooth) and conditioning on both start and end states simultaneously, which produces targeted transitions rather than unconditioned rollouts.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark, DiffStitch achieves the best average normalized score across all three algorithm families: 83.8 (IQL) and 80.2 (TD3+BC) on MuJoCo locomotion tasks, 75.7 (IQL) and 20.6 (TD3+BC) on Adroit-pen, and 63.6 (IQL) and 70.4 (TD3+BC) on Maze2d. The most dramatic gains appear on challenging tasks where original scores are low, e.g., TD3+BC on Med-Replay Hopper improves from 64.8 to 79.6, and IQL on Maze2d-umaze jumps from 42.7 to 77.0. DiffStitch is the only augmentation method applicable to Decision Transformer (DT), where it improves the locomotion average from 73.1 to 76.1 and the pen average from 60.3 to 64.9.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffStitch is compared against two prior data-augmentation methods: SER (Lu et al., 2023), which uses a diffusion model to generate individual transition tuples without trajectory-level conditioning, and TATU (Zhang et al., 2023), which rolls out short forward trajectories via a learned dynamic model with uncertainty-based truncation. DiffStitch outperforms SER in 14 out of 22 evaluated settings, with the largest margins on Maze2d (63.6 vs. 37.3 for IQL) and Adroit-pen (75.7 vs. 70.7 for IQL), while SER occasionally wins on individual MuJoCo locomotion tasks (e.g., Hopper Med-Expert IQL: 110.4 vs. 110.9). Against TATU, DiffStitch is consistently superior across all task families, with particularly large gaps on Maze2d (63.6 vs. 37.6 for IQL) and Kitchen (63.3 vs. 10.8 for IQL), where TATU's short-horizon rollouts fail to produce useful long-range transitions. A key structural advantage is that only DiffStitch generates complete trajectories, making it the sole augmentation method applicable to Decision Transformer; SER and TATU produce only short sub-trajectories or individual tuples that are incompatible with DT's sequence-modeling paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffStitch builds on the state-first diffusion + inverse dynamics architecture established by Ajay et al. (2022) and the dynamics-model-based qualification approach from Liang et al. (2023) AdaptDiffuser. It departs from prior offline RL data augmentation methods (SER, TATU) that generate individual transitions or short unconditioned rollouts, by introducing a trajectory stitching paradigm that explicitly conditions generation on both a start state (end of a low-reward trajectory) and a target state (start of a high-reward trajectory). This makes DiffStitch the first augmentation method that produces sub-trajectories designed to bridge two specific trajectories in the dataset, rather than generating data in a direction-agnostic manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in offline RL: when datasets contain few high-reward trajectories, algorithms struggle to learn transitions from low-reward to high-reward regions. DiffStitch's algorithm-agnostic nature (benefiting one-step, imitation, and trajectory optimization methods alike) broadens the applicability of data augmentation beyond what prior methods achieved. However, the trajectory selection strategy (random pairing of low- and high-reward trajectories) is acknowledged as suboptimal, and the pipeline requires training four auxiliary models, adding practical overhead. Open questions include smarter trajectory pairing strategies, whether the approach generalizes to continuous control domains beyond D4RL, and the cost-benefit tradeoff of the multi-model pipeline.
