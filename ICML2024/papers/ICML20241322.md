## Problem

Second-order RNNs (2RNNs) are provably more expressive than first-order RNNs, but their third-order parameter tensor makes them intractable. While CP decomposition offers a natural compression (yielding CPRNNs), and Sutskever et al. (2011) deployed such a model empirically, no formal analysis existed of how the decomposition rank and hidden size jointly control expressivity, or how CPRNNs relate to RNNs, 2RNNs, and MIRNNs in a precise inclusion hierarchy.

## Input / Output

The models consume character-level text sequences (one-hot encoded, alphabet size d=101) and produce a hidden state vector at each time step via a recurrent update. For language modeling evaluation, the hidden state is passed through a linear output layer to predict the next character, with performance measured in bits-per-character (BPC) on the Penn Treebank test set. The key architectural parameters are the hidden size n, the CP rank R, and the input dimension d, which together determine the parameter count as O(Rn + nd) for CPRNNs versus O(n²d) for full 2RNNs.

## Method

The paper parameterizes the second-order tensor of a 2RNN using a CP decomposition of rank R, yielding the CPRNN, and provides the first rigorous expressivity analysis of this architecture. Theorem 1 establishes that CPRNN expressivity strictly increases with rank up to the maximal typical CP rank R_typ-max and saturates at R_max, making rank a fine-grained interpolation between RNNs (R=0) and 2RNNs (R≥R_max). Theorem 2 shows that for a fixed rank, increasing hidden size improves expressivity only up to n=R, after which the rank becomes the bottleneck. The paper further proves that MIRNNs are a strict special case of CPRNNs (rank n with a diagonal C factor), and that CPRNNs are strictly more expressive than MIRNNs whenever R>n. Empirically, on Penn Treebank character-level language modeling, the authors show that for any fixed parameter budget, an optimally tuned CPRNN (choosing rank and hidden size jointly) outperforms RNNs, 2RNNs, and MIRNNs, and identify two distinct underfitting regimes corresponding to rank being too small or hidden size being too small.

## Results

On Penn Treebank character-level language modeling (BPC metric), CPRNNs consistently outperform RNNs, 2RNNs, and MIRNNs at every fixed parameter budget tested. With a CPRNN of rank 4 and hidden size 1024, the BPC decreases by 59% of the relative gap between RNN and 2RNN, while increasing parameters by only 2%. 2RNNs show poor performance below ~1M parameters (hidden size ≤ 64) and only match other models at 1.7M parameters (hidden size 128). Training time for hidden size 512 is ~12 min for RNN, 25 min–1 h for CPRNNs (ranks 50–1500), and ~1.5 h for 2RNNs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPRNNs outperform all three baselines (RNN, 2RNN, MIRNN) at every parameter budget tested on Penn Treebank, as shown in Figure 7. Against vanilla RNNs, CPRNNs achieve substantially lower BPC even at very small ranks (e.g., rank 4 captures 59% of the RNN-to-2RNN gap), confirming that second-order interactions provide a strong inductive bias. Against 2RNNs, CPRNNs match or exceed performance while using far fewer parameters (O(Rn+nd) vs. O(n²d)); 2RNNs are particularly weak below 1M parameters due to their small effective hidden size. Against MIRNNs, CPRNNs with rank > hidden size are strictly more expressive (Corollary 4), and empirically achieve lower BPC at matched parameter counts. The paper also identifies two underfitting regimes for CPRNNs at fixed parameter budget: low rank (rank is the bottleneck) and high rank (hidden size becomes the bottleneck), with the optimal configuration lying in between.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first formal expressivity analysis of CPRNNs, a model that Sutskever et al. (2011) deployed empirically for text generation without identifying it as a CP-decomposed 2RNN or providing theoretical grounding. It unifies the MIRNN (Wu et al., 2016) as a special case of CPRNNs where rank equals hidden size and the factor matrix C is constrained to be diagonal, and positions the CP rank as a natural hyperparameter interpolating between first-order RNNs (R=0) and full second-order 2RNNs (R=R_max). Unlike prior tensor-decomposition expressivity analyses that compare different tensor network structures (Cohen & Shashua, Levine et al.), this work studies how the rank of a single fixed structure controls model capacity, a strategy previously applied only to deep polynomial networks (Kileel et al., 2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a clean theoretical hierarchy (RNN ⊂ CPRNN(R) ⊂ 2RNN, with MIRNN as a specific point) and shows that for any fixed parameter budget, a properly tuned CPRNN outperforms all other architectures, offering practitioners a principled way to trade off expressivity and size. The discovery of two distinct underfitting regimes at fixed parameter count (rank bottleneck at low rank, hidden-size bottleneck at high rank) provides practical guidance for architecture selection. Open questions include whether strict inclusion holds in the gap between the maximal typical rank and the maximal CP rank, whether results extend to other decompositions (Tensor-Train, tensor ring) and to state space models, and whether the theoretical guarantees hold under finite-precision training.
