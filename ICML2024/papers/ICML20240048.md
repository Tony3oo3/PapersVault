## Problem

The widely used Hill equation models dose-response curves as a single sigmoidal transition, which cannot capture multi-mechanism drug behavior such as biphasic (hormetic) responses, and its scalar IC50 aggregate can mislead therapeutic decisions. Moreover, all established parametric models require per-drug, per-cell-line parameters, making zero-shot predictions for unseen drugs or cell lines impossible. This paper asks whether a non-parametric neural model can estimate entire dose-response curves while generalizing to previously untested drug molecules and cell lines.

## Input / Output

The model takes a drug molecule encoded either as a 2,048-bit Morgan fingerprint or as an annotated molecular graph (nodes carry atom type, charge, hybridization, ring membership, radii; edges carry bond type, conjugation, ring membership), a cell-line gene-expression vector over 2,089 genes selected by network propagation in a protein-protein interaction graph, and a scalar drug concentration. It outputs the full inhibitory-effect curve E(x) over the concentration axis rather than a single scalar, enabling evaluation at arbitrary concentrations including those outside the observed range. Zero-shot predictions are produced for drug–cell-line combinations never seen during training.

## Method

ARCANet encodes gene expression via a dropout-regularized FC layer, encodes the drug via either a residually-connected graph-attention network (m_mol layers on the molecular graph plus m_full layers on its fully-connected version) or a single FC layer on the Morgan fingerprint, and fuses the two through multi-head cross-attention (GNN variant) or concatenation with FC layers (fingerprint variant). The treatment model maps the interaction embedding and concentration to vectors of slope and bias parameters of multiple Hill curves, applies a sigmoid to obtain a vector of latent Hill responses, and a residual FC regression head then produces the final curve—biasing the network toward superpositions of enzymatic inhibitions while retaining universal-approximator capacity. Training uses a composite loss combining KL divergence on a softmax-normalized (variational) representation of the entire curve, with a tunable temperature controlling sensitivity to flat versus steep sections, and point-wise MSE to preserve absolute effect magnitude; this addresses the local-minimum problem where MSE alone favors predicting flat curves for the majority of non-toxic drug–cell pairs.

## Results

For zero-shot precision oncology (unseen cell lines), ARCANet(GNN) achieves MSE of 0.03 on CTRPv2, 0.005 on GDSC2, and 0.425 on PRISM, roughly halving the error of the best baseline (FunFor: 0.063, 0.009, 1.185 respectively). For drug discovery (unseen drugs), ARCANet(GNN) reaches 0.12 MSE on NCI60 versus FunFor's 0.14. In the interpolation and extrapolation settings on known drug-cell pairs, ARCANet(fingerprint) achieves the lowest MSE across all four datasets, e.g., 0.012 on CTRPv2 interpolation and 0.053 on CTRPv2 extrapolation, with statistically significant improvements over all parametric baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the individual 2P/3P/4P logistic models (Fallahi-Sichani et al., 2013), ARCANet(fingerprint) achieves significantly lower MSE on smoothing (e.g., 0.011 vs. 0.022 on CTRPv2), interpolation (0.012 vs. 0.013), and extrapolation (0.053 vs. 0.116 on CTRPv2); these parametric models cannot be applied to zero-shot settings at all. The 2P mixed-effect model (Vis et al., 2016, as implemented in the GDSC pipeline) only converges on GDSC2 due to numerical instability, and ARCANet still outperforms it there (0.003 vs. 0.005 MSE for smoothing). Against FunFor (Fu et al., 2021), the only baseline applicable to zero-shot prediction, ARCANet(GNN) significantly outperforms on all three precision-oncology datasets (halving MSE on CTRPv2 and PRISM) and on NCI60 drug discovery (0.12 vs. 0.14 MSE). Notably, the graph-based ARCANet(GNN) variant is essential for zero-shot generalization, while the lower-capacity fingerprint variant is slightly better when no generalization across unseen drugs or cell lines is required.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant parametric tradition of dose-response modeling (Hill/logistic equations, mixed-effect and hierarchical Bayesian models) by proposing the first non-parametric neural architecture that predicts the entire inhibitory-effect curve rather than a scalar aggregate like IC50. It repurposes graph-attention and cross-attention mechanisms from molecular representation learning to encode drug molecules, cell-line transcriptomes, and their interaction, enabling zero-shot generalization to unseen drugs or cell lines—a capability absent from all prior parametric and functional-random-forest approaches. The variational loss (KL divergence on softmax-normalized curves) is a novel training objective that has no direct precedent in the dose-response literature, though it draws on the broader idea of comparing probability distributions over discrete points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By predicting full curves rather than IC50, ARCANet can capture biphasic and non-sigmoidal pharmacological behaviors that the Hill equation structurally cannot represent, potentially avoiding misleading therapeutic decisions. The zero-shot capability for unseen cell lines (precision oncology) and unseen compounds (drug discovery) addresses a practical gap that all prior parametric models leave open. Key limitations include the need for substantial training data (parametric models still win when samples shrink by ~2 orders of magnitude), the ablation showing the physics-informed Hill-mixture treatment module contributes little over a plain MLP, and the absence of clinical validation. Open questions include whether the fingerprint-vs-GNN tradeoff generalizes to other molecular tasks and whether the variational loss provides benefits at larger scale.
