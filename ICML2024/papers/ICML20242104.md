## Problem

Constrained reinforcement learning lacks intrinsic interpretability across three critical dimensions: guaranteed convergence to an optimal policy, transparency in the training convergence process, and quantitative explanation of why specific sequential decisions are made. Existing post-hoc explanation methods produce incomplete or inaccurate rationales, while intrinsic interpretability approaches (e.g., Bayesian RL) sacrifice convergence guarantees and training stability. This paper presents the first intrinsically interpretable constrained RL framework that simultaneously addresses all three challenges through a probabilistic inference lens.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state observations and constraint limits in simulation, and additionally depth camera data (Intel RealSense D435i) and external force measurements (via VID-Fusion) in the real quadrotor deployment. A distinguishing feature of the output is that the critic network produces the full distribution of the action-value function (parameterized via quantiles) rather than a single point estimate, which directly enables the probabilistic decision interpretation: the posterior probability p(τ|L_i) quantifies how each latent factor (e.g., external wind force n_f) contributes to the current trajectory. The convergence rate guarantee of Θ(1/√T) is provided as a formal output alongside the policy.

## Method

AWaVO reformulates constrained RL as a Wasserstein variational optimization problem over an augmented Probabilistic Graphical Model, replacing the standard KL-divergence variational objective with an Adaptive Generalized Sliced Wasserstein Distance (A-GSWD). The framework has two coupled submodules: (1) ORPO-DR performs policy optimization where the actor network outputs the hypersurface parameters (l, θ̃) that adaptively determine the slicing directions for A-GSWD, while the critic network provides the full state-action distribution via distributional TD learning with quantile approximation; (2) WVI minimizes A-GSWD between the variational distribution q(a) and the optimality likelihood p(O|τ) to achieve probabilistic decision interpretation. The key technical novelty is that the slicing directions are not fixed but are dynamically determined by the policy itself, making the Wasserstein distance computation adaptive to the current uncertainty structure. Theoretical results prove A-GSWD is a valid pseudo-metric (true metric under injectivity of the AGRT), establish global convergence under conditions on the reward operator family F, and derive a Θ(1/√T) convergence rate with explicit dependence on network width m and depth H.

## Results

AWaVO achieves a guaranteed global convergence rate of Θ(1/√T) < C_rate ≤ Θ(1/T^1.2) across simulated tasks, empirically verified over 10 seeds. In simulated benchmarks (Acrobot, Cartpole, Walker, Drone), CRPO and AWaVO outperform PaETS, while PCPO edges out AWaVO on Acrobot, Cartpole, and Walker; however, AWaVO slightly outperforms PCPO in real-world quadrotor tasks with varying uncertainties. The method provides a probabilistic decision interpretation (p(τ|L)) quantifying how latent factors like external forces influence sequential control decisions, a capability absent in all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PaETS (Okada & Taniguchi, 2020), AWaVO clearly outperforms across all four simulated tasks in both return rewards and constraint satisfaction, though PaETS offers a Bayesian-network-based probabilistic interpretation at the cost of unstable convergence. Against TRPO-IPO (Liu et al., 2020), TRPO-IPO outperforms AWaVO on the more complex simulated drone task, but AWaVO matches or exceeds it on simpler tasks while providing formal convergence guarantees. Against PCPO (Yang et al., 2020), PCPO achieves slightly better performance on Acrobot, Cartpole, and Walker in simulation, but AWaVO slightly outperforms PCPO in the real quadrotor experiments where varying uncertainties are present. Against CRPO (Xu et al., 2021), CRPO shows comparable or marginally better convergence in the simulated drone task (without disturbances), but AWaVO differentiates itself by providing both training transparency (distributional representation) and probabilistic decision interpretation, which CRPO lacks entirely. The paper positions AWaVO as offering a reasonable trade-off: near-competitive performance with the strongest baselines while uniquely delivering formal interpretability across all three key challenges (convergence guarantee, training transparency, and decision interpretation).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established research threads: RL-as-probabilistic-inference (Levine 2018; Okada & Taniguchi 2020), optimal-transport-based distributional metrics (GSWD from Kolouri et al. 2019; ASWD from Chen et al. 2021), and distributional reinforcement learning (Bellemare et al. 2017; Dabney et al. 2018). Its core architectural departure is replacing the standard KL divergence in variational inference with an adaptive sliced Wasserstein distance (A-GSWD) whose slicing directions are themselves learned by the policy network, and coupling this with a distributional representation of the action-value function (ORPO-DR). The constrained RL task itself (CMDP) is treated as well-established, with the paper positioning AWaVO against primal-dual (TRPO-IPO, PCPO) and primal (CRPO) baselines rather than introducing a new problem formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal, intrinsic interpretability framework for constrained RL that simultaneously addresses convergence guarantees (Θ(1/√T) rate), training transparency via distributional value representations, and probabilistic decision-interpretation through posterior factorization p(τ|L_i). This is particularly relevant for safety-critical deployments (aerospace, autonomous driving, finance) where regulatory and trust requirements demand explainable sequential decisions. Key open questions remain: the trustworthiness of the critic network's Bayesian posterior is acknowledged but not formally bounded, scalability to larger state spaces is deferred to the appendix, and no human-centered validation of the interpretability claims is provided. The empirical gains over CRPO are modest in simulation, and the real-quadrotor advantage over PCPO is slight, suggesting the interpretability benefits come at a small performance cost rather than a clear win.
