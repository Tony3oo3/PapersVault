## Problem

Node-level OOD detection on graphs remains underexplored, with existing methods either modeling uncertainty solely on predicted categorical distributions while ignoring feature embeddings, or over-relying on individual node features under the assumption that neighbors share the same distribution. When IDs and OODs intermingle in a node's neighborhood, these approaches fail to produce discriminative scores. Additionally, the experimental landscape is limited, lacking evaluation settings that capture structural/topological distribution shifts.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the logits (final-layer embeddings) produced by a trained GNN encoder along with the graph's adjacency matrix, operating on the k-hop ego-subgraph centered at each node rather than on the node in isolation. It outputs a scalar Generalized Dirichlet Energy (GDE) value per node—lower for in-distribution, higher for out-of-distribution—which is thresholded to yield a binary ID/OOD decision. The k-hop neighborhood size and propagation coefficient α are the key structural hyperparameters controlling how much topological context is incorporated.

## Method

TopoOOD adapts the classic graph-level Dirichlet energy to the node level by defining a raw k-hop Dirichlet energy (raw kHDE) over each node's k-hop neighborhood, which measures the embedding smoothness (weighted pairwise distance) within that subgraph. The paper proves that under standard assumptions (ID clustering, OOD dispersal, uniform logit scale), ID nodes have strictly lower expected raw kHDE than OOD nodes. However, raw kHDE suffers from energy convergence: as k grows, all nodes' energies converge to the global graph Dirichlet energy, erasing discriminative power. To resolve this, TopoOOD introduces a propagation-based weighting scheme that assigns higher weights to nodes structurally closer to the central node, yielding the Generalized Dirichlet Energy (GDE), which preserves the ID/OOD expectation gap while remaining sensitive to local neighborhood composition. Training incorporates an outlier-exposure regularization loss that constrains GDE values of ID nodes to a narrow range while pushing OOD nodes' GDE higher, and the detector is backbone-agnostic, plugging into any GNN encoder (GCN, GAT, JKNet, MixHop).

## Results

On the Twitch dataset (cross-domain shift), TopoOOD achieves 99.72% AUROC, 99.99% AUPR, and 0.00% FPR95, surpassing the strongest baseline GNNSafe++ (95.36% AUROC, 33.57% FPR95) by 4.36% AUROC and 33.57% FPR95. On ogbn-Arxiv (temporal shift), TopoOOD reaches 87.11% AUROC and 32.10% FPR95, improving over GNNSafe++ by 12.34% AUROC and 45.33% FPR95. On synthetic topological distribution shifts (Table 2), TopoOOD achieves the highest AUROC in all 9 settings (3 datasets × 3 OOD types), with peak values of 99.96% (Coauthor-CS-Cliqueness) and 100.0% (Coauthor-Physics-Cliqueness), outperforming GNNSafe++ by up to 40.9% AUROC on triangleness splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopoOOD outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and OOD type evaluated. The closest competitor is GNNSafe++ (Wu et al., 2023), which uses free-energy scores from individual node logits with neighbor propagation under a distribution-homogeneity assumption; TopoOOD beats it by 4.36% AUROC on Twitch and 12.34% AUROC on Arxiv, while reducing FPR95 by 33.57% and 45.33% respectively. Among image-based OOD methods adapted to graphs (MSP, ODIN, Mahalanobis, OE, KNN), TopoOOD achieves 30–65% higher AUROC, indicating that node-level OOD detection fundamentally requires neighborhood topology rather than individual feature scores. Graph-specific baselines GPN (Bayesian GNN) and GKDE (kernel Dirichlet) are also substantially outperformed, with GPN additionally failing on Arxiv due to out-of-memory issues. On the synthetic topological distribution shifts (triangleness, squareness, cliqueness), TopoOOD achieves the best AUROC in all 9 settings, with margins of 20–41% over GNNSafe++ on triangleness splits, confirming that neighborhood disorganization is a more discriminative signal than the energy-based scores used by prior graph OOD methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the classical graph-level Dirichlet energy (Zhou & Schölkopf, 2005) into a node-wise, k-hop neighborhood metric for OOD detection, departing from GNNSafe++ (Wu et al., 2023) which computes energy solely from individual node logits under the assumption that neighbors share the same distribution. The training paradigm borrows outlier exposure (Hendrycks et al., 2018) to regularize the gap between ID and OOD energy scores. Conceptually, the key departure is shifting the OOD signal from the node's own prediction to the *disorganization level of its neighborhood*, and the introduction of topology-based distribution shifts (triangleness, squareness, cliqueness) as a new evaluation axis absent from prior graph OOD benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The energy convergence theorem (Theorem 3.2) provides a clean theoretical explanation for why naive neighborhood expansion fails, motivating the weighted GDE propagation scheme; this gives practitioners a principled way to choose the propagation depth k. The topology-aware evaluation settings (triangleness, squareness, cliqueness, plus AND, core number, closeness centrality) fill a genuine gap in how graph OOD detection is benchmarked, moving beyond domain or temporal splits. Open questions include how the method behaves on dynamic or heterogeneous graphs, whether the fixed α=0.5 propagation coefficient generalizes across graph densities, and whether the topological split thresholds (chosen for ~1:1:1 ratios) introduce label leakage in practice.
