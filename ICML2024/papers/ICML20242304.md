## Problem

Universal cross-domain retrieval methods require multi-domain training data to learn domain-agnostic embeddings, but assembling and labeling such data across arbitrary domains is prohibitively expensive or infeasible. This paper introduces data-free adaptive cross-domain retrieval (DFACDR), asking whether a pre-trained vision-language model can be adapted for retrieval across any domains without access to a single real training image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242304/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, the method takes a 224×224 RGB image, passes it through CLIP's frozen visual encoder (ViT-B/32, ResNet-50, or ViT-L/14), and applies a learned non-Euclidean MLP projection to produce a feature embedding in a Poincaré ball for nearest-neighbor retrieval. During training, no images are used at all—only category text labels are fed into CLIP's frozen text encoder with learnable domain word vectors inserted into prompts, making the entire training pipeline text-only.

## Method

TKI employs a two-stage 'aggregation after expansion' strategy. In the expansion stage, P learnable domain word vectors are inserted into text prompts ('A [DOMAIN]_p style of [CLASS]_i') and optimized with a min-max objective: a diversity-maximizing loss pushes domain-specific text features apart in the shared latent space, while a prototype-based contrastive loss ensures each domain vector remains category-independent. In the aggregation stage, the resulting P×C text features serve as proxies for image features (exploiting CLIP's cross-modal transferability) to train a non-Euclidean MLP on a Poincaré ball, using a supervised contrastive loss that pulls same-class features from different domains together. At inference, the trained non-Euclidean MLP is simply appended to CLIP's frozen visual encoder to extract retrieval-ready image embeddings, requiring no images during the entire training process.

## Results

On PACS, TKI achieves an average mAP of 73.12% and Prec@200 of 73.70% across all domain pairs, compared to 61.09% and 63.55% for Vanilla CLIP, yielding a +12.03 mAP improvement. On Office-Home, TKI reaches 37.11% mAP and 20.22% Prec@200 versus 30.17% and 17.72% for the baseline. On the more challenging DomainNet (6 domains, 345 classes), TKI improves average mAP from 15.76% to 21.31% for Real queries and from 11.34% to 16.75% for Sketch queries, with the largest relative gain on Quickdraw queries (0.80% → 1.97% mAP). All improvements are achieved without any real training images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Vanilla CLIP (frozen ViT-B/32 visual encoder used directly for retrieval without any adaptation). TKI consistently outperforms Vanilla CLIP across every domain pair on all three benchmarks. On PACS, the average mAP gain ranges from +12.03 (A→C, P→S direction) to +13.19 (P→C direction), with the largest single-pair improvement being +12.62 mAP on P→C (80.73% → 93.35%). On Office-Home, gains are more modest but still consistent: +6.94 mAP on average for A→* queries and +6.66 mAP for R→* queries. On DomainNet, TKI improves mAP by 35–147% relative across query domains (e.g., Quickdraw queries go from 0.80% to 1.97% mAP, a 146% relative gain). The paper does not compare against other cross-domain retrieval methods (e.g., UCDR, PROS) since those require real training data, which is explicitly excluded in the data-free setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CLIP's shared cross-modal latent space, repurposing its text encoder as a data-free proxy for visual feature learning. It departs from the universal cross-domain retrieval line (Paul et al., 2021; Tian et al., 2022; Agarwal et al., 2023), which requires expensive multi-domain training data, by eliminating all real images from training and instead using learnable domain prompts to synthesize domain diversity in text space. The 'aggregation after expansion' strategy combines prompt-learning ideas (akin to CoOp/PromptStyler) with a non-Euclidean (hyperbolic) MLP for the projection, a geometric choice not previously explored in cross-domain retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The data-free formulation removes the primary practical barrier to deploying universal cross-domain retrieval—labeling multi-domain training data—making the approach immediately applicable to arbitrary new domains without retraining. However, the method still requires access to category text labels (though the ablation with random labels partially alleviates this), and evaluation is limited to three image classification benchmarks with relatively few domains (4–6). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to truly open-ended domain pairs (e.g., medical to satellite imagery), how it interacts with test-time adaptation, and whether the hyperbolic projection's advantage persists at larger scale or with more diverse domain distributions.
