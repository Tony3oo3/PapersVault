## Problem

Existing LLM agents (AutoGPT, LangChain, ResearchAgent) fail to achieve high task completion rates in automated data science even with GPT-4, primarily due to LLMs' inability to generate reasonable ML experiment plans and their hallucination issues. Fine-tuning LLMs for this domain is impractical because feedback requires full code execution and back-propagation over billions of parameters. The paper asks whether a classical AI paradigm—case-based reasoning—can bridge this gap by grounding LLM agents in expert knowledge without parameter updates.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a natural language task description (e.g., 'predict airline review ratings from text'), paired with train/validation/test splits and a named evaluation metric (RMSE, AUROC, MSE, etc.), and produces an executable Python training script, a trained ML model, and test-set predictions. In the development stage, the output also includes a structured experiment plan that is iteratively revised over up to 5 steps; in the deployment stage, the output is a single-pass code generation with no iteration loop. The task description is embedded via a pretrained embedding model (1lm-embedder) for cosine-similarity retrieval against the case bank, and the code output must conform to a provided train.py template with specific function signatures (train_model, predict, compute_metrics).

## Method

DS-Agent integrates the CBR retrieve-reuse-evaluate-revise-retain cycle into a multi-agent LLM pipeline operating in two stages. In the development stage, a Retriever fetches top-k human insight cases from a Kaggle-derived case bank, a novel ReviseRanker (an LLM prompted to re-rank cases by estimated utility given execution feedback) dynamically adjusts which case is reused, a Planner generates an experiment plan, a Programmer writes Python code, a Debugger (inspired by Reflexion) fixes runtime errors, and a Logger records feedback for the next iteration; successful solutions are retained into both the human insight and agent case banks. In the deployment stage, a simplified CBR paradigm retrieves a single past successful (task, code) pair and an Adapter agent performs minor modifications for direct code generation, reducing per-run cost by over 90% (from $1.60 to $0.135 with GPT-4). The key architectural insight is that CBR replaces resource-intensive back-propagation with a flexible case-retention learning mechanism, while the ReviseRank step—absent in standard RAG—enables iterative performance improvement by conditioning retrieval on execution feedback rather than only on the static task description.

## Results

In the development stage (12 tasks), DS-Agent with GPT-4 achieves a 100% success rate, a mean rank of 5.0, and a best rank of 1.5, substantially outperforming ResearchAgent with GPT-4 (mean rank 9.8, best rank 5.4). In the deployment stage (18 tasks), DS-Agent with GPT-4 attains a ~99% one-pass rate and a mean rank of 12.7, while DS-Agent with GPT-3.5 reaches an 85% one-pass rate and mean rank of 17.1. Notably, DS-Agent with the open-source Mixtral-8x7b-Instruct improves the one-pass rate from 6% (zero-shot) to 31%, and the per-run cost drops from $1.60 (development, GPT-4) to $0.135 (deployment, GPT-4), a 91.5% reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ResearchAgent (Huang et al., 2023), DS-Agent wins decisively on both mean rank and best rank across all LLM backbones: with GPT-4, DS-Agent achieves mean rank 5.0 vs. 9.8 and best rank 1.5 vs. 5.4; with GPT-3.5, DS-Agent achieves mean rank 7.5 vs. 12.6 and best rank 3.3 vs. 11.8. DS-Agent with GPT-3.5 even surpasses ResearchAgent with GPT-4 on most individual tasks, and achieves a 100% success rate where ResearchAgent with GPT-3.5 nearly fails on every task type. Against AutoGluon (Erickson et al., 2020) on four tabular tasks (Appendix C.1), DS-Agent with GPT-4 outperforms AutoGluon by a large margin on 2 of 4 tasks (e.g., wild-blueberry-yield MAE 182 vs. 305; enzyme-substrate AUROC 0.9761 vs. 0.8683) and delivers comparable performance on the remaining two (media-campaign-cost RMLSE 0.2858 vs. 0.2707; spaceship-titanic accuracy 0.8062 vs. 0.8044). In the deployment stage, DS-Agent with GPT-4 achieves a mean rank of 12.7, outperforming zero-shot GPT-4 (28.2) and one-shot GPT-4 (28.0), while DS-Agent with GPT-3.5 (17.1) beats both GPT-4 baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

DS-Agent repurposes the classical Case-Based Reasoning (CBR) paradigm (Kolodner, 1992; Watson & Marir, 1994) for LLM-based agents, extending it beyond the retrieve-reuse pattern shared with RAG by adding an iterative evaluate-revise-retain loop grounded in execution feedback. It positions itself as a direct alternative to ResearchAgent (Huang et al., 2023) for ML research automation, arguing that LLMs' inability to generate reasonable experiment plans is better addressed through external case banks than through finetuning. The framework also draws on Reflexion (Shinn et al., 2023) for its debugging sub-loop and on LLM-as-reranker work (Sun et al., 2023) for the ReviseRank step, while contrasting with AutoML systems (AutoGluon, H2O) by replacing predefined search spaces with open-ended, language-driven model construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-stage design (expensive iterative development followed by cheap single-pass deployment) offers a practical recipe for reducing LLM agent costs by over 90% while maintaining near-perfect success rates, making automated data science more accessible for real-world use. The finding that DS-Agent with GPT-3.5 outperforms ResearchAgent with GPT-4 suggests that structured external knowledge (case banks) can compensate for weaker base-model capabilities, an important signal for practitioners constrained to smaller or open-source LLMs. Open questions include generalization beyond the three tested modalities (text, time series, tabular), dependence on Kaggle-quality human insights as a knowledge source, and whether the CBR loop's benefits persist as base LLMs improve natively at data science reasoning.
