## Problem

Finding the lowest-energy adsorbate placement on a catalyst slab requires a non-convex global search over sites and orientations, and current methods (AdsorbML) rely on brute-force placement at 100 heuristic/random sites followed by MLFF optimization. No prior work has formulated adsorbate placement as a learned generative problem that directly predicts the optimal site, leaving the computationally expensive multi-site search as the standard approach.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241002/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an optimized adsorbate structure, a slab structure with its cell parameters, and a fixed interstitial gap (z-distance between adsorbate and slab surface). The adsorbate is initialized at a uniformly random point on the slab (in fractional coordinates), and the diffusion process operates exclusively on 2D translations in the slab plane (with periodic boundary conditions) and 3D rigid rotations about the adsorbate center of mass. The output is a single predicted adsorbate coordinate set that is then refined by L-BFGS MLFF optimization (300 steps, Fmax 0.01 eV/Å) and verified by a DFT single-point calculation; success is defined as the DFT energy falling within 0.1 eV of the OC20-Dense benchmark.

## Method

AdsorbDiff formulates adsorbate placement as a conditional denoising diffusion process on the product manifold T(2) × SO(3), where the forward SDE adds Gaussian noise to translations and IGSO(3) noise to rotations, and the reverse process is solved via a learned score function parameterized by a GNN (EquiformerV2, GemNet-OC, or PaiNN) that maps 3D atomic positions and numbers to per-atom score vectors for translation and rotation. The key innovation is conditioning the diffusion on relative energies (E_rel = E_min − E_i) across all 100 DFT-optimized configurations per adslab, which leverages 100× more training data than unconditional training on only the single lowest-energy site. The score is trained via denoising score matching with separate losses for the translational and rotational components, and the reverse diffusion iteratively refines a random initial placement over ~98 steps to the predicted optimal site. Pretraining on OC20's 460k local-optima trajectories before conditional fine-tuning on OC20-Dense yields a substantial zero-shot improvement, and the framework demonstrates that GNN architecture choice has minimal impact on diffusion success rate (unlike force-field prediction), suggesting the score function encodes a simpler harmonic-like potential.

## Results

AdsorbDiff achieves a 31.8% DFT Success Rate with a single site prediction, a 3.5× improvement over the AdsorbML random-placement baseline (9.1%). Conditional denoising training on relative energies yields a 31.8% DFT SR versus only 11.4% for unconditional training, demonstrating the critical role of leveraging all 100 densely-sampled DFT configurations per adslab. Pretraining on 460k OC20 local-optima trajectories provides a 29.6% zero-shot DFT SR on OC20-Dense, and fine-tuning conditionally recovers the full 31.8%. Out-of-distribution generalization shows only a 3.8% drop in DFT SR for unseen adsorbates/slabs, while the end-to-end ML pipeline remains O(10⁴)× faster than pure DFT workflows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is AdsorbML (Lan et al., 2023), which performs brute-force MLFF optimization over randomly/heuristically placed adsorbate sites followed by DFT single-point verification. With a single site (Nsite=1), AdsorbDiff achieves 31.8% DFT Success Rate versus 9.1% for AdsorbML — a 3.5× improvement. The paper notes that AdsorbML would need approximately 5× more placements (Nsite≈5) to reach comparable accuracy, at proportionally higher computational cost. At higher Nsite values, AdsorbML's brute-force randomness actually reduces structural anomalies (desorption, dissociation, slab reconstruction), causing it to converge toward or slightly exceed AdsorbDiff's success rate; however, AdsorbDiff's sampled sites have lower diversity (2.7 Å std vs. 8.1 Å for AdsorbML at 10 sites), which increases anomaly rates. The conditional diffusion formulation also substantially outperforms its unconditional counterpart (31.8% vs. 11.4% DFT SR), confirming that conditioning on relative energies across all 100 densely-sampled sites is essential rather than training only on the single lowest-energy configuration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Riemannian diffusion framework from DiffDock (Corso et al., 2022) and De Bortoli et al. (2022) to the specific geometry of adsorbate-on-slab placement, introducing 2D translation with periodic boundary conditions and SO(3) rotation as the action manifold. It departs from the brute-force site exploration of AdsorbML (Lan et al., 2023) by learning a conditional score function over relative energies, enabling direct prediction of the global optimum rather than exhaustive sampling. The authors explicitly position this as the first diffusion formulation for adsorbate placement, distinguishing it from molecular docking on proteins through PBC, 2D translation, and the requirement of DFT-level accuracy in evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.5× success-rate improvement over the AdsorbML single-site baseline with a single diffusion prediction offers a practical path toward reducing the computational cost of catalyst screening, where brute-force placement across 100 sites is currently standard. The finding that GNN architecture choice has negligible impact on diffusion performance (unlike force-field prediction) is a useful practical insight that could simplify model selection. Key open questions remain: anomalies (desorption, dissociation, slab reconstruction) still significantly limit success rates, torsion angles are not yet incorporated for larger adsorbates, and the approach has only been validated on small adsorbates from the OC20-Dense benchmark.
