## Problem

1-bit (binary) object detectors achieve performance comparable to real-valued counterparts on commonly sized objects but suffer a 4–5% AP degradation on tiny object detection (TOD) datasets. The root cause is that 1-bit convolutions produce high-level features with limited discriminative capacity, making it difficult to separate sparse foreground pixels of tiny targets from the overwhelmingly dominant cluttered background.

## Input / Output

The method takes aerial RGB images (800×800 on AI-TOD) as input and produces bounding boxes with class labels as output, mounted on standard one-stage (FCOS, RetinaNet) or two-stage (Faster-RCNN) detectors with a ResNet-18 backbone and FPN (P2–P6). During training, a lightweight decoder network reconstructs the full-resolution input image from intermediate FPN features (P2, P3, P4) and a binary foreground mask derived from ground-truth boxes; both the decoder and the mask are discarded at inference, so the deployed model incurs zero additional latency or memory beyond the base 1-bit detector.

## Method

DFR-Det formulates training under the Information Bottleneck principle, augmenting the standard detection loss with a discriminative feature refinement (DFR) term. A decoder φ(·) (deconvolution + residual blocks + pixel-shuffle) maps intermediate FPN features back to input-image resolution, and a binary mask M built from ground-truth boxes decouples the supervision into two parts: ‖φ(F_f) − X_f‖₁ forces the foreground feature to faithfully reconstruct the target region, while ‖φ(F_b)‖₁ drives the background feature toward zero, effectively denoising it. The total loss is L_detection + γ·L_R + λ(‖φ(F_f)−X_f‖₁ + ‖φ(F_b)‖₁), where L_R is the standard binarization reconstruction error. Ablations show that using P2–P4 features and the full foreground-plus-background formulation (Eq. 10) yields the best 12.8% AP on AI-TOD with FCOS, closing most of the gap to the real-valued counterpart while retaining 13.8× computation speedup and 4.9× storage savings.

## Results

On AI-TOD, DFR-Det with FCOS and ResNet-18 backbone achieves 12.8% AP, approaching the real-valued FCOS (13.5% AP) while using only 1-bit weights (15.47 MB, 23.08 G OPs vs. 76.44 MB, 318.25 G OPs). On DOTA-v2.0, DFR-Det with FCOS reaches 30.9% AP (vs. 31.6% real-valued) and with Faster-RCNN reaches 31.1% AP (vs. 33.7% real-valued). The method consistently narrows the 1-bit-to-real-valued gap from ~4–5% (previous SOTA) to under 1% on FCOS for both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFR-Det outperforms all five baselines across every framework and dataset. On AI-TOD with FCOS, DFR-Det (12.8% AP) surpasses the strongest 1-bit baseline IDa-Det (10.7%) by 2.1 points, LWS-Det (9.0%) by 3.8 points, BiDet (8.2%) by 4.6 points, and ReActNet (7.9%) by 4.9 points; it also edges out the 4-bit LSQ quantization (12.7%) by 0.1 point while using 4× fewer bits. On DOTA-v2.0, DFR-Det with FCOS (30.9% AP) beats IDa-Det (28.7%) by 2.2 points and LWS-Det (25.9%) by 5.0 points, narrowing the gap to the real-valued model (31.6%) to just 0.7 points. The gains are consistent across RetinaNet (+1.5 to +4.5 AP over 1-bit baselines) and Faster-RCNN (+0.8 to +3.8 AP), confirming that the discriminative feature refinement loss is framework-agnostic. Notably, DFR-Det achieves these improvements with zero additional inference cost—the decoder and foreground mask are used only during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 1-bit detector line (BiDet, LWS-Det, IDa-Det) by reframing the training objective through the Information Bottleneck principle and introducing a foreground-masked decoder loss specifically targeting the foreground discrimination failure that 1-bit convolutions exhibit on tiny objects. While prior TOD methods address scale via data augmentation, multi-scale pyramids, or GAN-based super-resolution, DFR-Det instead operates at the feature-representation level during training, using a lightweight decoder (inspired by NAFNet's residual blocks) to reconstruct the input and decouple foreground/background supervision via a binary mask. The key conceptual departure from prior 1-bit detector work is that the refinement is applied only during training with zero inference overhead, distinguishing it from distillation-based approaches like IDa-Det that modify the detection loss itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in closing the 4–5% AP gap between 1-bit and real-valued detectors on tiny objects (versus only 1–2% on standard objects) without adding any inference cost, making 1-bit detectors viable for real-time aerial surveillance on embedded hardware. Limitations include evaluation only on ResNet-18 and three aerial-image datasets, leaving open whether the foreground-mask decoder generalizes to larger backbones, non-aerial domains, or even 2-bit/4-bit quantization regimes. An open question is whether the IB-derived formulation could be extended to other quantization levels or combined with the multi-scale and augmentation strategies the paper explicitly positions itself against.
