## Problem

The coefficient of prescriptiveness, a unitless measure quantifying how well a contextual policy exploits side information relative to a static benchmark, has been used exclusively as a post-hoc evaluation metric in the prescriptive analytics literature. No prior work has directly optimized this metric, nor has it been robustified against distributional uncertainty. This paper asks whether one can formulate and solve a distributionally robust optimization problem that maximizes the coefficient of prescriptiveness, and whether the resulting policies exhibit superior out-of-sample robustness under distribution shift.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a training set of (ζ, ξ) pairs (e.g., 200-dimensional covariates and arc travel times), from which a random forest infers the conditional distribution F̂_{ξ|ζ}, and a validation set used to calibrate the ambiguity set size α via a grid search over 40 candidate values. The output is a contextual policy x(ζ) that, given a new covariate vector at decision time, solves a small LP (or MILP for the shortest path application) to produce a decision, along with the optimal prescriptiveness level γ* ∈ [0,1] that balances between the static SAA benchmark and the fully anticipative oracle.

## Method

The paper introduces the Distributionally Robust Prescriptive Competitive Ratio (DRPCR), which maximizes the worst-case coefficient of prescriptiveness over a nested CVaR ambiguity set. A key convex reformulation (Proposition 3.4) recasts the problem as maximizing γ subject to a single constraint Q(x(·),γ) ≤ 0, where Q is the supremum of a convex, non-decreasing function over the ambiguity set. Under the nested CVaR structure, Q decomposes into |Ω_ζ| independent subproblems (one per covariate scenario), each an LP when the decision space is polyhedral, enabling a bisection algorithm on γ that converges in ⌈log₂(1/ε)⌉ iterations. The critical insight is that DRPCR implicitly anchors the adaptable policy to the SAA solution: under large distribution shifts, the optimizer learns when it is better to stay near the static benchmark (where relative regret is zero) rather than aggressively exploiting covariates, yielding a form of regularization fundamentally different from standard DRO approaches.

## Results

On a 45-node, 97-arc shortest path problem with 200 covariates, all four methods (CSO, DRCSO, DRCRO, DRPCR) achieve a similar out-of-sample coefficient of prescriptiveness of ~0.45 when no distribution shift is present. Under increasing mean perturbations of travel times (up to 60%), DRPCR maintains a positive coefficient of prescriptiveness (0.05 relaxed / 0.07 binary at 50% shift; 0.01 / 0.02 at 60% shift), while CSO, DRCSO, and DRCRO all drop to negative values (−0.13 to −0.32). DRPCR training time is approximately 10× that of DRCSO/DRCRO (~33 min vs ~3 min per instance) due to the bisection loop over γ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DRPCR against three baselines: CSO (contextual stochastic optimization using random forests, from Kallus & Mao 2023), DRCSO (distributionally robust contextual stochastic optimization with nested CVaR ambiguity set), and DRCRO (distributionally robust contextual regret optimization, the Δ=1 regret model from Poursoltani et al. 2023). At zero distribution shift, all four methods perform essentially identically (~0.45 coefficient of prescriptiveness). The critical differentiation emerges under distribution shift: at 50% mean perturbation, DRPCR achieves +0.05 (relaxed) / +0.07 (binary) while CSO, DRCSO, and DRCRO all fall to −0.09 to −0.13. At 60% perturbation, the gap widens further—DRPCR retains +0.01/+0.02 while the baselines range from −0.25 to −0.32. The paper also notes that forcing binary (integral) policies slightly improves the out-of-sample performance of DRCSO and DRCRO relative to their relaxed counterparts, but DRPCR remains the most robust across all shift levels. The 1-norm distance analysis (Figure 1b) reveals that DRPCR implicitly uses the SAA solution as an anchor, learning when to stay close to the reference policy under large shifts—a form of regularization absent in the other DRO-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Bertsimas & Kallus (2020), who introduced the coefficient of prescriptiveness as a unitless performance metric for contextual optimization policies, and on the broader distributionally robust contextual optimization (DRCSO) literature (Bertsimas & Van Parys 2022; Kannan et al. 2020; Nguyen et al. 2022). The key departure is that prior work used the coefficient of prescriptiveness only as a post-hoc evaluation metric, whereas this paper formulates it as an optimization objective and introduces the first distributionally robust version (DRPCR), connecting it to risk-averse regret minimization (Poursoltani et al. 2023) through a convex reformulation. The nested CVaR ambiguity set structure, drawn from dynamic risk measure theory (Ruszczyński & Shapiro 2006), enables a tractable bisection algorithm that decomposes the problem into per-scenario linear programs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DRPCR framework offers a principled way to calibrate contextual optimization policies that explicitly balances exploitation of side information against robustness to distribution shift, with the reference policy acting as an implicit anchor—a form of regularization fundamentally different from prior DRO approaches. However, the 10× computational overhead relative to DRCSO/DRCRO limits immediate practical adoption, and the experimental validation is confined to a single shortest-path problem with mean-shift perturbations. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-nested ambiguity sets, whether affine policy restrictions can reduce the problem size, and whether the robustness advantage persists under more complex distributional shifts (e.g., covariance changes, multimodal shifts) and in higher-dimensional decision spaces.
