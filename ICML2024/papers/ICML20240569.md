## Problem

When generative models are trained in a self-consuming loop—where each new generation is trained on a mixture of real data and synthetic outputs from previous generations—empirical studies observe model collapse and diversity degradation, but rigorous theoretical guarantees on how the learned distribution drifts from the original are largely absent. Prior theoretical work either analyzes only simplistic Gaussian toy models or directly assumes upper bounds on statistical and optimization errors rather than deriving them from the training dynamics. This paper fills that gap by establishing TV distance bounds for realistic generative models (diffusion models with neural network score functions and kernel density estimators) under general mixed-training compositions.

## Input / Output

The framework takes as input a sequence of mixed training distributions p_i = Σ β_i^k p_{θ_k} + α_i p_0 (or their non-parametric KDE counterparts), where the mixture weights α_i and β_i^k control the proportion of real versus synthetic data at each generation, along with per-generation sample sizes n_i and model hyperparameters (network width m_i, training time τ_i for diffusion models; bandwidth h_i for KDE). The output is a high-probability upper bound on TV(p_{θ_{i+1}}, p_0) expressed as a weighted sum over generations of terms scaling as n_k^{-1/4}√(d log(di/δ)) for diffusion models or n_k^{-s/(2s+2d)} for KDE, plus KL divergence terms. A key structural output is the identification of a phase transition threshold λ' in the synthetic-to-real data ratio beyond which additional synthetic data improves rather than degrades fidelity.

## Method

The core technique decomposes TV(p_{θ_{i+1}}, p_0) via the triangle inequality into TV(p_{θ_{i+1}}, p_i) + TV(p_i, p_0), then bounds each term separately and solves the resulting recursion. For diffusion models with a one-hidden-layer random-feature score network, the first term is bounded via Pinsker's inequality linking TV to KL divergence, then by decomposing the score-matching loss into statistical error (controlled via Rademacher complexity of the product function class), finite-width approximation error (via RKHS norm bounds under gradient flow), and optimization error (via gradient flow convergence in the RKHS), with the critical choices m_i ≍ n_i and τ_{i+1} ≍ √n_i yielding the n_i^{-1/4} rate. For KDE, the bound uses McDiarmid's concentration inequality plus a bias-variance decomposition with optimal bandwidth h_i = n_i^{-1/(2s+2d)} under Sobolev smoothness assumptions. The recursive structure TV(p_{θ_{i+1}}, p_0) ≤ f(n_i) + Σ β_i^k TV(p_{θ_k}, p_0) is then unrolled to produce the final weighted-sum bound, from which the paper derives concrete requirements (quartic sample growth for full synthetic cycles, Ω((i-1)/i) real data proportion in the final generation, or a constant α per generation) and the phase transition phenomenon where the error function f(λ,i) = ((1+λ)^{i+1} - λ^{i+1})/(1+λ)^{i+1/4} initially increases then decreases in the synthetic data ratio λ.

## Results

The paper derives TV distance upper bounds for diffusion models with one-hidden-layer score networks under self-consuming training. For the general data cycle, TV(p_{θ_{i+1}}, p_0) ≲ Σ_k A_{i-k}(n_{i-k}^{-1/4}√(d log(di/δ)) + √(KL(p_{i-k,T}||π))), where A coefficients depend on the mixing proportions. In the extreme full-synthetic case, controlling error to O(ε) requires quartic sample growth n_k = Ω((i√d/ε)^4) across generations, or alternatively injecting α = Ω((i-1)/i) proportion of real data in the final generation. A phase transition is identified: with fixed real data n and synthetic data m = λn, the TV distance initially increases with λ but decreases beyond a threshold λ' that grows with the number of generations i.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines or empirical comparisons. The key theoretical comparison is with Bertrand et al. (2023), who bound parameter deviation ‖θ_{i+1} − θ*‖ under direct assumptions on statistical and optimization error upper bounds, and only for training on real data plus synthetic data from the most recent generation. In contrast, this paper (1) avoids assuming error bounds by performing fine-grained analyses of gradient flow dynamics and Rademacher complexity for diffusion models, (2) bounds the distributional discrepancy TV(p_{θ_{i+1}}, p_0) rather than parameter distance, and (3) handles more general training compositions including data from all previous generations. Compared to Shumailov et al. (2023) and Alemohammad et al. (2023), who provide theoretical intuition via simple Gaussian toy models, this work delivers rigorous high-probability bounds for general distributions under Sobolev smoothness assumptions. The KDE analysis (Theorem 5.4) extends the framework to non-parametric models, yielding bounds with rate n_i^{-s/(2s+2d)} that avoid the curse of dimensionality when s = d (e.g., Gaussian case).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the emerging line of work on self-consuming generative models from empirical observations (Shumailov et al., 2023; Briesch et al., 2023) and simple Gaussian toy-model analyses (Alemohammad et al., 2023) to a rigorous theoretical framework for general mixed training scenarios. It departs from Bertrand et al. (2023) in three key ways: it derives statistical and optimization error bounds from first principles rather than assuming them, it analyzes distributional discrepancy (TV distance) rather than parameter deviation, and it considers training on data from all previous generations rather than only the most recent. The technical machinery draws on the diffusion model generalization framework of Li et al. (2023), the KL-divergence-to-score-loss connection from Song et al. (2021), and the random-feature score network parameterization from Yang (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first paper to provide rigorous TV distance bounds for self-consuming loops across both parametric (diffusion) and non-parametric (KDE) models under flexible data-mixing schedules, giving practitioners concrete sample-size and real-data-proportion requirements to prevent model collapse. The phase transition result—that modest synthetic data augmentation can initially degrade performance before eventually helping—is a nuanced finding that complicates the simple 'more synthetic data is better' narrative. However, the analysis is restricted to one-hidden-layer score networks and unbiased sampling, and the paper explicitly flags extension to biased sampling and deeper architectures as future work, limiting immediate practical applicability to current large-scale diffusion models.
