## Problem

Strategic classification typically assumes the learner knows the exact cost function governing how users manipulate their features, but in one-shot deployment settings (e.g., financial regulation, epidemic vaccination) the learner must commit to a single classifier without knowing this cost. The paper proves that even a small mis-estimation of the true cost can reduce accuracy to trivial levels, and prior work on unknown responses is limited to online settings with repeated model deployments, leaving the one-shot case unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled training set of feature vectors (x, y) with y ∈ {±1}, a system-specified compact convex uncertainty set C of positive definite cost matrices Σ (parameterized by per-dimension eigenvalue bounds [σ²_{iℓ}, σ²_{iu}]), and a scalar utility bound u*. It outputs a single linear classifier β that minimizes worst-case strategic 0-1 risk over all costs in C, producing binary predictions sign(βᵀx). The uncertainty set C must be chosen to contain the true (unknown) cost, and the convergence rate scales inversely with the diameter of C, so it should be as tight as confidence allows.

## Method

The paper introduces a cost-dependent strategic hinge loss ℓ_{s-hinge}^c(β) = max{0, 1 − y(βᵀx + u*‖β‖_*)} that uses the Σ-transformed dual norm of β to capture dimension-wise sensitivity to strategic movement under general norms (not just ℓ2). To handle the non-convexity of this loss, the paper proves that dual norm regularization (with coefficient λ ≥ q(y=1)) ensures convexity, whereas ℓ2 regularization would require a cost-dependent coefficient scaling with ‖Σ⁻¹‖₂. The minimax objective min_β max_{c∈C} R_{s-hinge}^c(β) is solved via two algorithms: a full-batch subgradient method that invokes Danskin's theorem and a MAXLOSSCOST subroutine (running in O(nd + n ln n) per iteration), and a stochastic mirror descent-ascent method that discretizes the cost space along the diagonal of eigenvalue intervals to avoid exponential dimension dependence. Both converge to the minimax solution at rate Õ(T^{-1/2}), which is dimension-independent for p-norms with p ≤ 2.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or comparison tables. The key quantitative results are convergence and hardness guarantees: (1) Theorem 3.1 proves that any non-zero error in estimating the cost matrix can cause worst-case 0-1 error of up to 1/2 (i.e., random or worse), establishing the impossibility of single-cost robustness. (2) Theorem 5.2 shows the full-batch subgradient method converges to the minimax solution at rate Õ(T^{-1/2}), with a generalization bound of O(B(4X+u*)/√n + 3√(ln(1/δ))/√n). (3) Theorem 5.3 shows the stochastic mirror descent-ascent method achieves the same Õ(T^{-1/2}) rate (up to log factors) with dimension-independent scaling for p-norms with p≤2. The dual norm regularization (Proposition 4.3) ensures convexity with only λ≥q(y=1), in contrast to ℓ₂ regularization which requires λ≥‖Σ^{-1}‖₂ (Theorem 4.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is entirely theoretical and does not include empirical comparisons against baseline methods. There are no datasets, no benchmark evaluations, and no head-to-head algorithmic comparisons. The closest analogue to a 'comparison' is the theoretical contrast between dual norm regularization (requiring only λ≥q(y=1)) and ℓ₂ regularization (requiring λ≥‖Σ^{-1}‖₂, which can be arbitrarily large), showing that the proposed regularizer is strictly more efficient. The paper also contrasts its one-shot minimax framework with prior online/sequential approaches (Dong et al. 2018, Ahmadi et al. 2021, Shao et al. 2023, Lechner et al. 2023), arguing that those methods provide no worst-case guarantees for a single deployment, but no empirical validation of this claim is provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the strategic classification framework of Hardt et al. (2016) by removing the assumption of known user cost functions, and generalizes the strategic hinge loss of Levanon and Rosenfeld (2022) from the ℓ₂ norm to arbitrary norms via a dual-norm formulation. While prior work on unknown user responses (Dong et al., 2018; Ahmadi et al., 2021; Shao et al., 2023; Lechner et al., 2023) handles cost uncertainty exclusively through online exploration across repeated deployments, this paper is the first to formalize the one-shot setting where a single classifier must be committed to without any feedback. The optimization is framed as distributionally robust optimization over a cost-induced distribution shift, with the inner maximization solved via a custom piecewise-linear algorithm and the outer minimization handled by subgradient methods (full-batch) or Stochastic Mirror Descent-Ascent (Nemirovski et al., 2009) with a carefully constructed diagonal discretization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central hardness result—that even an arbitrarily small cost mis-estimation can yield trivial (or worse-than-random) accuracy in the worst case—fundamentally changes how practitioners should think about strategic learning: a single 'reasonable' cost guess is provably insufficient, and robustness requires explicitly modeling cost uncertainty. The dual norm regularization insight provides a structurally principled way to convexify the non-convex strategic hinge loss without the extreme ℓ₂ penalty that would otherwise be required. Key limitations include the restriction to linear classifiers, the rational-agent assumption, and the open question of what happens when the true cost lies outside the chosen uncertainty set (the authors conjecture graceful degradation but leave it unproven). The framework is purely theoretical with no empirical validation, limiting immediate practical impact.
