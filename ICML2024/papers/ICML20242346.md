## Problem

Existing graph invariance learning methods rely on strong, often implicit assumptions about the correlation strength between spurious features S and labels Y (e.g., DisC assumes strong S-Y correlation, while environment inference/augmentation methods assume weak correlation). In real-world scenarios, P(S,Y) varies arbitrarily, causing these methods to fail catastrophically when the assumed correlation regime is violated. The paper asks whether a robust inductive bias exists that enables graph invariance learning regardless of the S-Y correlation degree.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard graphs (adjacency matrix + node feature matrix) as input and produces a graph-level class label via a GIN encoder followed by a shared classifier. Unusually, the pipeline is multi-stage: the input graph is first processed by a self-supervised encoder (MVGRL) at multiple training epochs and architectures to generate a collection of spurious representations, which are then passed through lightweight classifiers to produce per-sample logits matrices. The final model is retrained from scratch, and the classifier ρ(·) is shared between the ground-truth loss and the decorrelation loss, so a single forward pass yields both the predicted label ŷ_i and the estimated logits ŝ_i used in the invariant loss.

## Method

EQuAD is a three-step framework built on the key insight (Theorem 4.1) that global-local mutual information maximization (infomax) provably extracts spurious features S when the spurious subgraph is larger than the invariant one, independent of the S-Y correlation. Step 1 (Encoding) trains multiple MVGRL encoders at different epochs and architectures to collect a set of spurious representations H. Step 2 (Quantifying) trains linear/MLP classifiers on H to produce logits matrices S, where each sample's logit value encodes the degree to which its spurious pattern correlates with its label. Step 3 (Decorrelation) retrains a GNN encoder from scratch with a novel loss (Eqn. 7) that treats the quantified logits as soft targets, applying sample-specific reweighting (upweighting minority spurious-correlation samples) and model-specific temperature-scaled softmax reweighting (preferring logits matrices with lower validation metrics). Theorem 5.1 proves that under symmetric bin conditions, this loss uniquely recovers the invariant representation ĥ_c = h_c and guarantees ĥ_c ⊥ h_s, eliminating any dependence on P(S,Y).

## Results

On synthetic SPMotif datasets (FIIF), EQuAD achieves 74.61% accuracy at b=0.33 and maintains 69.47% even at b=0.90, consistently outperforming the best baseline (CIGA at 72.91% at b=0.33). On Two-piece graph datasets (PIIF), EQuAD achieves 75.81% at (α=0.8, β=0.9) and 71.95% at (α=0.7, β=0.9), where most baselines collapse (e.g., GALA drops to 62.25% and 49.65% respectively). On real-world DrugOOD and OGB datasets, EQuAD achieves state-of-the-art AUC on 6 out of 8 splits, with the largest gain on EC50-Assay (79.36 vs. 77.48 for iMoLD). The paper reports an average improvement of 31.76% over baselines on synthetic datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EQuAD outperforms all 17 baselines on every SPMotif setting, with the largest margin over CIGA (the strongest FIIF baseline) being +1.7% at b=0.33 and +10.6% at b=0.90. On the challenging PIIF Two-piece graph settings where H(S|Y) < H(C|Y), EQuAD dominates: it beats GALA by +13.6% at (0.8,0.9) and +22.3% at (0.7,0.9), while environment inference methods (GIL, MoleOOD) and augmentation methods (GREA, DIR) all fail catastrophically, dropping below 52%. On real-world DrugOOD, EQuAD achieves the best AUC on 6 of 8 splits, with the most notable win on EC50-Assay (79.36 vs. iMoLD's 77.48, +1.88). EQuAD slightly underperforms iMoLD on EC50-Size (66.37 vs. 67.09) and GREA on MolBACE (81.83 vs. 82.37), but leads on MolBBBP (71.22 vs. 69.84 for MoleOOD). DisC, which shares the 'learn spurious first, then invariant' philosophy, is consistently outperformed (e.g., 50.51 vs. 69.47 on SPMotif b=0.90), confirming that infomax-based spurious extraction is more reliable than correlation-dependent biased graph identification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the infomax principle (originally from Deep Graph Infomax, Veličković et al. 2018) in a counterintuitive direction: rather than using global-local mutual information maximization to learn invariant representations, the authors prove it reliably isolates spurious features under mild conditions (spurious subgraph larger than invariant subgraph). This inverts the identifiability results in self-supervised learning (Kügelgen et al., 2021) that show SSL isolates content from style. The EQuAD framework (Encoding-Quantifying-Decorrelation) departs from prior graph invariance methods (CIGA, DisC, GIL, MoleOOD, GREA) by eliminating their reliance on assumptions about the joint distribution P(S,Y), instead using spurious features extracted via label-free SSL as a stepping stone to recover invariant features through a novel decorrelation loss with sample and model-specific reweighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that EQuAD's inductive bias remains robust across varying spurious correlation strengths, addressing a fundamental brittleness in prior graph invariance methods that fail catastrophically when their assumed P(S,Y) regime is violated (e.g., environment inference methods fail when H(S|Y) < H(C|Y)). The 31.76% average improvement over baselines across 15 datasets is substantial and well-supported. Open questions include whether the core assumption (spurious subgraph dominates in size) holds universally, whether the framework extends to other modalities (vision, NLP) as the authors suggest, and whether the multi-stage pipeline's computational overhead (though comparable to baselines) scales to very large graph datasets.
