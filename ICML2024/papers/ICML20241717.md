## Problem

VideoQA Transformer models achieve competitive benchmark scores, but it remains unclear whether they genuinely learn to jointly leverage video and text modalities or simply exploit dataset biases and spurious shortcuts. Existing benchmarks lack diagnostic mechanisms to distinguish true multimodal understanding from unimodal or shortcut-based performance, and no systematic method exists to probe the degree of modality coupling in a trained model's representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241717/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The QUAG probe operates at inference time on frozen VideoQA models, taking a video clip (3–640 frames depending on the model) and a text question as input, and producing diagnostic accuracy drops under four short-circuiting operations (unimodal, crossmodal, video, text) that reveal which modality interactions the model actually relies on. QUAG-attention replaces self-attention with pre-averaged key/value tokens, reducing multiplication operations by up to 68% while maintaining accuracy. CLAVI takes real-world video segments from Charades-STA and produces 114,342 yes/no questions with temporal complements, evaluated via a consistent accuracy metric (CAcc_V, CAcc_T) reported separately on low-coupling control and high-coupling complement subsets.

## Method

QUAG is a lightweight, non-parametric inference-time probe that impairs modality fusion by applying a row-wise average-and-replace operator to specific quadrants (VV, VT, TV, TT) of the attention matrices in the multimodal fusion module, effectively collapsing token-level interactions within or between modalities. Four short-circuiting configurations isolate unimodal, crossmodal, video-specific, and text-specific contributions, and a rank-bound lemma proves that video/text short-circuiting reduces the attention matrix rank to at most l_T + 2. QUAG-attention extends this by pre-averaging key and value tokens before the attention computation (with log-scaling inspired by proportional attention), pruning keys/values from (l_V + l_T) to as few as 2 without any finetuning. CLAVI is constructed by swapping action segments in Charades-STA videos to create temporal video complements, paired with template-generated yes/no questions whose temporal cues (before/after, beginning/end) are also complemented, ensuring high modality coupling that cannot be resolved by unimodal shortcuts.

## Results

On the new CLAVI stress-test dataset, all four models achieve >70% balanced accuracy, but consistent accuracy on the high-coupling complement subset (BE and BA questions) drops to near-trivial levels: JustAsk (3.6% CAcc_V, 2.4% CAcc_T), Singularity-T (1.7%, 0.5%), and All-In-One+ (1.2%, 0.8%), while only FrozenBiLM retains meaningful performance (54.1% CAcc_V, 57.2% CAcc_T). On the low-coupling control subset, all models exceed 90% consistent accuracy, confirming they can exploit shortcuts. QUAG-attention reduces multiplication operations by 13.6% (FrozenBiLM) and 68.0% (JustAsk) with less than 1% accuracy drop across ActivityNet-QA, MSRVTT-QA, NeXT-QA, and ATP-Hard, demonstrating that the models' high benchmark scores do not depend on fine-grained multimodal token interactions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates four VideoQA models (JustAsk, FrozenBiLM, Singularity-Temporal, All-In-One+) on the new CLAVI benchmark. FrozenBiLM is the only model that demonstrates meaningful coupled multimodal understanding, achieving 54.1% and 57.2% consistent accuracy on the high-coupling complement subset, while JustAsk (3.6%/2.4%), Singularity-T (1.7%/0.5%), and All-In-One+ (1.2%/0.8%) all collapse to near-chance levels. On the low-coupling control subset, all models perform well (>92%), confirming they can answer via unimodal shortcuts. QUAG-attention further reveals that FrozenBiLM's complement-subset performance drops to 23% (CAcc_T) and 3.6% (CAcc_V) under video-average attention, confirming its genuine reliance on multimodal fusion, whereas JustAsk's performance barely changes under any short-circuiting operation (<1% drop), indicating it never learned to fuse modalities. On standard benchmarks (ActivityNet-QA, MSRVTT-QA, NeXT-QA, ATP-Hard), QUAG-attention reduces multiplication ops by 13.6% (FrozenBiLM) and 68.0% (JustAsk) with <1% accuracy loss, showing that the high scores on these datasets are not driven by fine-grained cross-modal token interactions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of multimodal fusion interpretability (Hessel & Lee 2020; Chefer et al. 2021; Liang et al. 2023) and shortcut/bias analysis in VideoQA (Buch et al. 2022; Lei et al. 2023; Agrawal et al. 2018 VQA-CP). Unlike prior work that analyzes either dataset biases or model representations in isolation, QUAG performs a combined dataset-model analysis at inference time via block-averaging of attention quadrants, requiring no additional parameters or finetuning. The QUAG-attention mechanism repurposes the log-scaling trick from proportional attention (Bolya et al. 2023) to maintain softmax calibration when collapsing tokens, while CLAVI extends the complement-based stress-testing idea from VQA-CP to the video domain with temporal video-question hard negatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that JustAsk shows <1% performance drop under all short-circuiting operations, and that QUAG-attention (a simple averaging replacement for self-attention) matches full-attention performance with 68% fewer multiplications, raises fundamental questions about whether current VideoQA benchmarks actually measure multimodal understanding. CLAVI's consistent-accuracy metric and the near-trivial complement-subset scores (1–5% for most models) provide a concrete stress-test that current benchmarks lack. Open questions include whether the failure is architectural (transformer attention insufficient for coupling), training-procedural (models never receive gradient signal to couple modalities), or data-driven (benchmarks don't require coupling), and whether more complex temporal or compositional tasks would yield different conclusions than the deliberately simple yes/no temporal ordering tested here.
