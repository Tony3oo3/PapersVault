## Problem

State-of-the-art Neural Logic Machines (NLMs) and Differentiable Logic Machines (DLMs) frequently repeat similar erroneous subprograms during training, causing prolonged oscillation around sub-optimal local minima. The original training objectives provide no mechanism to detect or penalize this error repetition, leading to poor convergence, degraded generalization, and wasted training epochs—especially on complex relational reasoning tasks like IsMGUncle or 6-Connectivity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241096/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For relational reasoning tasks, the input is a set of grounded background predicates (e.g., IsMother, IsFather, IsSon, IsDaughter for family trees; HasEdge for graphs) encoded as truth value tensors in [0,1]^(n×n) for binary atoms or [0,1]^n for unary atoms, where n is the number of entities. The model must learn a logic program that deduces the target predicate's truth value tensor (e.g., HasSister(x), IsUncle(x,y), k-Connectivity(x,y)). For RL tasks (Sorting, Path, Blocks World), the input is a grounded state and the output is a sequence of actions; the model is trained via REINFORCE with curriculum learning over environments of increasing object count.

## Method

FRGR (Failure Reflection Guided Regularizer) introduces a two-stage mechanism applied dynamically during training. First, a Root Cause Mining module extracts a discrete program representation from the neural model by selecting the largest-weight input-to-output connection in each logic module, enqueues representations of erroneous predictions into a sliding history queue, and applies the Apriori frequent-itemset algorithm to identify neuron-index subsets that co-occur across multiple erroneous programs (the 'root cause subprogram'). Second, a Repetition Regularization (ReReg) loss computes the intersection between the current program representation and the identified root cause, then applies an L1 penalty on the corresponding weight entries, discouraging the model from reusing the same erroneous connections. The total training objective is the standard classification (or REINFORCE) loss plus β·L_ReReg, where β controls regularization strength. This causes the model to escape oscillation around local optima by explicitly penalizing the structural patterns responsible for repeated failures.

## Results

On the hardest Family Tree task (IsMGUncle), NLM w/ FRGR achieves 60.44% generalization (n=100) versus 10.04% for vanilla NLM — a 6× improvement — while DLM w/ FRGR doubles generalization from 20% to 40%. On 6-Connectivity (Graph Reasoning), NLM w/ FRGR reaches 100% on both n=10 and n=20 (vs. 74.40% and 69.20% for NLM) and converges in 41.60 epochs versus 278.00 (85% fewer). On IsUncle, NLM w/ FRGR reduces training epochs by 45.4% (78.40 vs. 143.70) while achieving 100% graduation ratio. Under the data-scarce setting (1/500 of training data), DLM w/ FRGR maintains 80% performance on IsUncle where vanilla DLM drops to 40%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares FRGR against two state-of-the-art NLM backbones: NLM (Dong et al., ICLR 2018) and DLM (Zimmer et al., TMLR 2023). Against NLM, FRGR delivers dramatic gains on the hardest tasks: on IsMGUncle, generalization jumps from 10.04% to 60.44% (6×), and on 6-Connectivity, both performance and generalization reach 100% (vs. 74.40%/69.20%) with 85% fewer training epochs. Against DLM, improvements are more moderate but consistent: IsUncle improves from 60% to 80% on all metrics, IsMGUncle generalization doubles from 20% to 40%, and 6-Connectivity generalization rises from 53.28% to 90.10%. On simpler tasks (HasFather, HasSister, 1-OutDegree, 4-Connectivity), both baselines already achieve 100%, so FRGR provides no performance gain and occasionally adds a few extra epochs. The RL tasks (Path, BlocksWorld) show modest improvements in graduation ratio and generalization for NLM w/ FRGR, while DLM was not evaluated on RL tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Neural Logic Machines (NLM, Dong et al. 2018) and Differentiable Logic Machines (DLM, Zimmer et al. 2023) architectures, which implement forward-chaining Horn-clause reasoning via neural modules. Rather than proposing a new architecture, FRGR introduces a training-time regularization that repurposes the Apriori frequent-itemset mining algorithm (Agrawal & Srikant, 1994) to detect repeated erroneous subprograms in the model's extracted deduction process. The key departure from prior NLM training is the dynamic, data-driven identification of error root causes during optimization, as opposed to the vanilla gradient descent that the authors show leads to prolonged oscillation around suboptimal local minima.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that NLMs systematically repeat structurally similar errors during training and that explicitly penalizing these repetitions via a mined root-cause subprogram yields substantial gains in performance, generalization, and convergence speed—particularly on harder compositional tasks like IsMGUncle (5× generalization improvement) and 6-Connectivity. However, the approach is tightly coupled to the NLM/DLM architecture (program extraction relies on argmax over weight matrices), and all experiments are on small-scale benchmarks (≤100 family members, ≤20-node graphs, ≤50-object RL). Whether the root-cause-mining + penalty paradigm transfers to other neural-symbolic or purely neural architectures remains an open question the authors flag for future work.
