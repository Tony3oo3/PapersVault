## Problem

Generative LLM inference is memory-bandwidth-bound rather than compute-bound, making weight precision the primary lever for both model size and latency. However, existing post-training quantization methods (GPTQ, AWQ) rely on uniform quantization, which is sub-optimal for the non-uniform weight distributions in LLMs and struggles severely at 3-bit precision, producing large perplexity gaps from the FP16 baseline. The paper addresses how to achieve near-lossless 3-bit quantization by combining sensitivity-aware non-uniform bin allocation with explicit outlier handling.

## Input / Output

The method consumes FP16 pre-trained LLM weights and a small calibration set (as few as 10 text samples) used to compute per-weight Fisher information via gradient backpropagation. It produces a compressed model consisting of (i) 3/4-bit indices per output channel with a per-channel lookup table of FP16 representative values, and (ii) a sparse FP16 matrix in CSR format holding ~0.45% of the original weights (0.4% outliers by percentile threshold plus 0.05% top-sensitive values by Fisher information). At inference, custom CUDA kernels dequantize the dense indices via LUT and perform balanced sparse matvec on the CSR matrix, with all arithmetic in FP16.

## Method

SqueezeLLM combines two techniques. First, sensitivity-based non-uniform quantization formulates bin placement as a weighted 1-D k-means problem where each weight's clustering cost is scaled by its diagonal Fisher information entry (a Hessian approximation), pulling centroids toward values that most affect the final loss. The optimization target minimizes end-to-end loss perturbation (OBD-style) rather than layer-wise activation perturbation, which the paper shows yields up to 0.3 perplexity improvement. Second, the Dense-and-Sparse decomposition splits W = D + S, where S stores outliers (values outside a percentile threshold) and the top 0.05% most sensitive values in full FP16 precision using CSR format, while D retains a ~10× narrower value range that is far easier to quantize accurately. At deployment, a LUT-based dense kernel and a balanced sparse kernel (10 nonzeros per thread to handle skewed per-channel sparsity) are fused into a single launch, achieving 2.1–2.4× speedup over FP16 on an A6000 GPU.

## Results

On LLaMA-7B 3-bit quantization (C4), SqueezeLLM achieves 7.75 perplexity (dense-only) and 7.56 with 0.45% sparsity, compared to 9.55 for GPTQ and 28.26 for RTN, against an FP16 baseline of 7.08. At 4-bit, SqueezeLLM reaches 7.21 PPL on C4 with only ~0.13 degradation from FP16, while compressing the model by 3.95×. On LLaMA-13B 3-bit, SqueezeLLM(0.45%) achieves 6.92 PPL on C4 vs. 7.12 for GPTQ(g128) and 7.08 for AWQ(g128). Deployed on an A6000 GPU, the quantized models achieve up to 2.3× speedup over FP16 with peak memory reduced from 12.7 GB to 2.9 GB (7B, 3-bit).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPTQ (non-grouped), SqueezeLLM achieves a 1.80-point perplexity improvement on C4 at 3-bit for LLaMA-7B (7.75 vs. 9.55) and a 0.22-point gain at 4-bit (7.21 vs. 7.43). Against GPTQ and AWQ with group size 128 (comparable model size ~3.24 bits), SqueezeLLM(0.45%) outperforms both by up to ~0.3 perplexity at 3-bit (7.56 vs. 7.89/7.90 on C4) while also achieving 1.9× speedup versus GPTQ(g128)'s 0.2× due to the latter's expensive permutation overhead. Against SpQR at 4-bit, SqueezeLLM matches or slightly beats perplexity (7.21 vs. 7.28 on C4) while delivering 1.8× speedup versus SpQR's 1.2×. Against QuIP on LLaMA2-70B, SqueezeLLM wins at 4-bit (3.21 vs. 3.65 WikiText2 PPL) and 3-bit (3.55 vs. 3.84), and at 2-bit with 0.45% sparsity achieves 4.71 vs. QuIP's 6.20. Against OmniQuant, SqueezeLLM(0.45%) consistently matches or beats OmniQuant(g128) across all LLaMA/LLaMA2 models at both 3-bit and 4-bit (e.g., 5.96 vs. 6.03 on LLaMA2-7B 3-bit WikiText2).

## Relation to Prior Work

SqueezeLLM extends the post-training quantization (PTQ) line for LLMs initiated by GPTQ and continued by AWQ and SpQR, but departs in two fundamental ways. First, it replaces uniform quantization with sensitivity-based non-uniform quantization (weighted k-means using Fisher information), arguing that uniform quantization's integer-arithmetic advantage is irrelevant for memory-bound inference where all arithmetic is done in FP16. Second, it introduces a Dense-and-Sparse decomposition of weight matrices that directly extracts outliers and sensitive values into a sparse FP16 component, contrasting with SpQR's indirect grouping-based approach and with prior dense-and-sparse work applied only to attention maps. The sensitivity metric (Hessian/Fisher-based, targeting final loss perturbation) also differs from AWQ's activation-based importance and GPTQ's layer-wise perturbation minimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SqueezeLLM provides a practical path to near-lossless 3-bit LLM deployment (e.g., LLaMA-7B at 2.9 GB with only 0.67 perplexity degradation), which is significant for single-GPU inference where the Memory Wall is the dominant bottleneck. The ablation showing that sensitivity weighting is critical (18.08 → 7.75 perplexity) and that uniform quantization catastrophically fails at 3-bit (28.26 perplexity) provides useful guidance for practitioners choosing quantization strategies. Limitations include evaluation primarily on decoder-only LLaMA/Vicuna architectures, reliance on a roofline model for hardware predictions, and the fact that 2-bit quantization still requires sparsity to be competitive. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to encoder-decoder models, multimodal LLMs, and whether the Fisher information computation cost becomes prohibitive for very large or frequently updated models.
