## Problem

While Bayes-consistency of surrogate losses in regression has been studied, no hypothesis-set-specific, non-asymptotic H-consistency bounds existed for common regression surrogates such as the Huber loss, ℓ_p losses, or the squared ε-insensitive loss. This paper provides the first in-depth study of H-consistency bounds for regression, establishing both positive and negative results that clarify when minimizing a surrogate loss provably controls the squared loss estimation error.

## Input / Output

The method operates on tabular feature vectors x with a bounded hypothesis set H (|h(x)| ≤ B for all h, x) and a symmetric conditional distribution. For adversarial regression, an additional ℓ_p perturbation budget γ ∈ {0.001, 0.005, 0.01} is specified. The output is a real-valued prediction h(x) together with an explicit H-consistency bound of the form E_{ℓ₂}(h) − E*_{ℓ₂}(H) ≤ Γ(E_L(h) − E*_L(H)), where Γ is a non-decreasing function whose form depends on the surrogate loss (linear, power, or scaled-linear).

## Method

The paper first generalizes the H-consistency theorems of Awasthi et al. (2022a,b) by allowing the bounding function α(h,x) to be non-constant, which is essential for regression surrogates where the relationship between conditional regrets varies with x. Using these generalized tools, it proves H-consistency bounds for the Huber loss (requiring a positive probability mass condition p_min(δ) > 0), all ℓ_p losses for p ≥ 1 (with distinct Γ forms for p=1, 1<p≤2, and p≥2), and the squared ε-insensitive loss, while showing the ε-insensitive loss is not H-consistent. For adversarial regression, the paper derives smooth adversarial regression losses of the form L(h,x,y) + τ·sup_{x':‖x'−x‖≤γ}|h(x')−h(x)|, where L is any of the above surrogates, and shows that minimizing this regularized objective yields provable trade-off bounds between adversarial and standard squared loss errors. The approach is validated on the Diabetes and Diverse MAGIC wheat datasets using CVXPY, where the Huber-loss variant occasionally outperforms the squared-loss variant in both clean and robust error.

## Results

On the Diabetes dataset, the smooth adversarial regression algorithm with squared loss (L=ℓ₂) achieves a clean MSE of 1.24 and robust MSE of 1.26 at γ=0.001, compared to 2.53/2.57 for the adversarial squared loss baseline (ADV-SQ) — a roughly 51% reduction in clean error. At γ=0.005, the Huber-loss variant (L=ℓ_δ) achieves 0.78/0.79 versus 1.12/1.18 for ADV-SQ. On the Diverse MAGIC wheat dataset at γ=0.01, the Huber-loss variant achieves 0.97 clean / 1.01 robust MSE, compared to 1.30/1.78 for ADV-SQ, representing a 43% reduction in robust error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is ADV-SQ, which directly minimizes the adversarial squared loss (the worst-case squared loss under bounded ℓ_∞ perturbation). The proposed smooth adversarial regression algorithms consistently outperform ADV-SQ on both clean and robust MSE across all perturbation sizes on both datasets. The most dramatic gap appears on the Diabetes dataset at γ=0.001, where Ours (L=ℓ₂) achieves 1.24 clean MSE versus 2.53 for ADV-SQ (a 51% reduction), and on the Diverse MAGIC wheat dataset at γ=0.01, where Ours (L=ℓ_δ) achieves 1.01 robust MSE versus 1.78 for ADV-SQ (a 43% reduction). Notably, the Huber-loss surrogate (L=ℓ_δ) occasionally outperforms the squared-loss variant (L=ℓ₂), as seen on Diverse MAGIC wheat at γ=0.005 (1.03 vs 1.26 clean) and γ=0.01 (0.97 vs 1.22 clean), highlighting the practical benefit of robust surrogate losses even within the adversarial training framework. The paper attributes these gains to the theoretical H-consistency guarantees that ensure minimizing the surrogate loss controls the squared loss estimation error, while the added smoothness regularization term improves adversarial robustness without the over-penalization of direct adversarial loss minimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the H-consistency framework established by Awasthi et al. (2022a;b) for binary and multi-class classification to the regression setting, which had no prior H-consistency or finite-sample surrogate loss guarantees. The key technical departure is generalizing their theorems to allow non-constant coefficient functions α(h,x), which is essential because regression surrogates like the Huber loss and squared ε-insensitive loss require x-dependent coefficients. The adversarial regression application directly parallels the smooth adversarial classification losses of Mao et al. (2023f) and the robustness-accuracy trade-off framework of Zhang et al. (2019), transplanting the surrogate-loss-plus-smoothness-regularization recipe from classification to regression. The symmetric distribution and bounded hypothesis set assumptions follow Steinwart (2007) for Bayes-consistency in regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This fills a notable gap in the surrogate loss literature by providing the first non-asymptotic, hypothesis-set-specific guarantees for regression surrogates, with practical implications for choosing between Huber, ℓ_p, and ε-insensitive losses. The negative result showing that the standard ε-insensitive loss used in SVR does not admit H-consistency bounds (even under additional distributional assumptions) is a meaningful caveat for practitioners relying on SVR. Limitations include the requirement of symmetric conditional distributions and bounded hypothesis sets, and the adversarial regression experiments are restricted to linear models on small tabular datasets. Open questions include extending the analysis to non-symmetric distributions, establishing H-consistency with respect to other target losses (e.g., ℓ₁), and connecting the smooth adversarial regression losses to Moreau envelope theory.
