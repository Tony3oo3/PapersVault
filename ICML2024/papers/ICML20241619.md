## Problem

Existing global-scale canopy height maps (e.g., Potapov et al. 2021, Lang et al. 2023) exhibit substantially lower accuracy than regional products, with MAE values around 6–7 m versus ~2–4 m for regional maps. This quality gap is compounded by systematic geolocation errors in GEDI LiDAR measurements that corrupt ground-truth labels, and by erroneous height estimates in mountainous terrain where slope-induced signal artifacts are misinterpreted as canopy height.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241619/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 14-channel 512×512 pixel patches (4 Sentinel-1 SAR channels: VV/VH in ascending and descending orbits, plus 10 Sentinel-2 multispectral bands) at 10 m resolution, constructed as temporal per-pixel median composites over the summer leaf-on season with cloud/shadow removal for Sentinel-2. Sparse GEDI RH100 height measurements (10–400 per patch) serve as supervision, and SRTM surface topography is used to filter out measurements on slopes exceeding 20°. The output is a per-pixel continuous canopy height estimate in meters, assembled into a global 10 m wall-to-wall map in Web Mercator projection.

## Method

The architecture is a U-Net with a ResNet-50 backbone (trained from scratch) producing a single-channel linear output for per-pixel height regression. The key novelty is a shift-resilient loss function: for each GEDI track, the loss is minimized over all integer pixel shifts δ within a radius r (≈√2 pixels, motivated by 80.8% of tracks having <10 m geolocation error), selecting the shift that yields the lowest pixel-wise Huber loss, thereby tolerating systematic geolocation offsets without requiring a high-resolution DTM. A second critical component is SRTM-based label filtering that excludes GEDI measurements where the local surface slope exceeds 20°, preventing slope-induced overestimation in mountainous regions. Training uses AdamW with a weighted sampler to counter the heavily skewed height distribution (90% of measurements below 14 m), and the model is deployed globally via distributed inference on 312×312 pixel tiles with 100-pixel context borders.

## Results

On the global test set, the proposed method achieves an MAE of 2.43 m and RMSE of 4.73 m overall, and 4.45 m / 6.72 m for trees taller than 5 m, substantially outperforming both existing global canopy height maps. The RRMSE drops to 0.53 (overall) and 0.49 (trees >5 m), compared to 1.39/1.02 for Lang et al. and 2.38/1.77 for Potapov et al. The model still underestimates very tall trees (>30 m), a limitation shared across all available height maps, but consistently shows the lowest error variance across all height bins up to 30 m.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Lang et al. (2023), the proposed method reduces MAE from 6.47 m to 2.43 m (no filter) and from 8.80 m to 4.45 m (trees >5 m), roughly halving the error on all metrics. Against Potapov et al. (2021), the improvement is even larger in relative terms: MAE drops from 6.92 m to 2.43 m overall and from 10.01 m to 4.45 m for tall trees, with RMSE improving from 9.25 m to 4.73 m. Visually, the proposed global map closely matches the quality of the regional France map by Schwartz et al. (2023), correctly identifying fine structures such as forest gaps, roads, and small patches that both global baselines miss. The main remaining weakness is underestimation of very tall trees (>30 m), where the error aligns more closely with Lang et al. rather than maintaining the large margin seen at lower heights.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pipeline established by Schwartz et al. (2024) for regional canopy height estimation (Sentinel-1/-2 composites + GEDI labels + U-Net) to a global scale, while introducing two key departures: a shift-resilient loss function that explicitly handles systematic GEDI geolocation errors (as opposed to Schleich et al.'s DTM-based correction, which is infeasible globally), and SRTM-based slope filtering to exclude erroneous labels in mountainous terrain. The architecture (U-Net with ResNet-50 backbone) and training setup follow prior work, and the cloud-masking strategy adapts Braaten's s2cloudless approach. The paper positions itself as closing the quality gap between high-accuracy regional maps (e.g., Schwartz et al. 2023 for France) and existing global products (Potapov et al. 2021; Lang et al. 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The resulting 10 m global canopy height map is a major practical resource for carbon stock estimation, forest monitoring, and climate policy, and is publicly accessible via Google Earth Engine. The shift-resilient loss is a lightweight, general-purpose technique that any regression task with spatially-shifted sparse labels could adopt. Key limitations remain: the model still underestimates heights above ~30 m, the SRTM slope filter is an imperfect proxy for true terrain (since SRTM captures surface return, not bare-earth elevation), and the 3 m GEDI ground-level offset is not fully resolved. Open questions include whether higher-resolution DTMs or multi-temporal GEDI data could further reduce errors in tall-canopy and steep-terrain regions.
