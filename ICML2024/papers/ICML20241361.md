## Problem

Existing diffusion-based re-ranking methods propagate similarity across the entire nearest-neighbor graph, allowing misleading information from outliers and nearby manifolds to contaminate the ranking. Because the affinity graph is constructed from pairwise instance connections without regard to local cluster structure, similarity diffusion is prone to spreading noise from other manifolds throughout the graph, leading to suboptimal retrieval performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241361/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is training-free and operates on pre-extracted deep feature descriptors (e.g., MAC, R-MAC, R-GeM for instance retrieval; BoT, MGN, TransReID, etc. for person ReID). Given a query image and a gallery set, it computes pairwise Euclidean distances, constructs a k-nearest-neighbor graph, and produces a re-ranked similarity matrix F′. The final retrieval distance is a weighted blend of the original Euclidean distance and a Jensen-Shannon divergence computed between rows of F′, yielding a re-ranked list without any learned parameters.

## Method

CAS confines similarity diffusion to local clusters built from k-reciprocal neighbors rather than the full graph, explicitly filtering out cross-manifold contamination. The Bidirectional Similarity Diffusion (BSD) module optimizes a smoothness objective that simultaneously constrains forward (F_ki vs F_kj) and reverse (F_ik vs F_jk) similarity pairs, guaranteeing a symmetric output; the resulting convex problem is reformulated as a Lyapunov equation and solved via a conjugate-gradient iterative scheme. The Neighbor-guided Similarity Smooth (NSS) module then refines the matrix by enforcing that the similarity from x_i to x_j is consistent with the average similarity from x_j to x_i's local neighbors, weighted by the intra-neighbor reliability r², and aggregates neighbor similarity rows to produce an enhanced representation. Finally, a global propagation step using the transition matrix P = F̃ᵀF̃ extends the locally refined similarity to the full graph for ranking.

## Results

Using R-GeM descriptors, CAS achieves mAP of 80.7% on ROxf (Medium) and 64.8% on ROxf (Hard), and 91.0%/80.7% on RPar (Medium/Hard), outperforming the strongest prior methods (GSS: 78.0%/60.9%, CSA: 78.2%/59.1%) by 2.7–5.7 points. On the large-scale ROxf+1M and RPar+1M benchmarks, CAS reaches 61.6%/39.1% and 75.5%/59.7% respectively. On person ReID (Market1501, 150-identity training), CAS improves mAP by 3.2–4.2 points over the best re-ranking baseline (k-reciprocal) across all eight backbone networks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAS outperforms all 18 compared baselines across every dataset and protocol. Against the strongest diffusion-based methods, CAS gains +7.8 mAP over DFS (72.9→80.7) and +5.8 over EIR (74.9→80.7) on ROxf Medium, with even larger margins on the Hard protocol (+14.7 over DFS, +12.7 over EIR). Against the strongest learning-based methods, CAS surpasses GSS (78.0) by +2.7 and CSA (78.2) by +2.5 on ROxf Medium, and by +3.9 and +5.7 respectively on ROxf Hard. On RPar, CAS leads DFS/FSR by +1.3 mAP (Medium) and RDP by +1.3 (Medium), while on the +1M variants it achieves the best scores (75.5 on RPar+1M Medium, 59.7 on RPar+1M Hard). For person ReID on Market1501, CAS improves mAP by +3.2 over k-reciprocal (74.9→78.1 with BoT) and +3.5 over ECN (74.6→78.1), with consistent gains of 3–4 points across all eight backbone networks (BoT, OSNet, AGW, MGN, AdaSP, ABD-Net, TransReID, CLIP-ReID).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based re-ranking lineage initiated by Zhou et al. (2003) and refined by DFS/FSR (Iscen et al., 2017/2018) and RDP (Bai et al., 2019), which propagate similarity across an entire k-NN graph. The key departure is constraining the diffusion to local clusters defined by k-reciprocal neighbors (borrowed from Zhong et al., 2017) rather than the full graph, explicitly preventing cross-manifold information leakage. The Bidirectional Similarity Diffusion generalizes RDP's bidirectional smoothness term with a cluster constraint and a Lyapunov-equation formulation, while the Neighbor-guided Similarity Smooth introduces a novel neighbor-consistency regularization that has no direct analogue in prior diffusion or query-expansion methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cluster-aware constraint addresses a well-motivated failure mode of diffusion-based re-ranking—outlier and cross-manifold contamination—with a simple, training-free mechanism that consistently improves mAP by 2–5 points over the strongest diffusion baselines across multiple descriptors and datasets. The method's practical appeal is reinforced by its O(n³) complexity being comparable to existing diffusion methods while running faster in practice (1,278 ms vs. 1,857 ms for DFS on ROxford), and by requiring no GNN training. Open questions include whether the heuristic local-cluster approximation (fixed k₁, k-reciprocal expansion threshold) can be made adaptive, and whether the approach extends naturally to video or 3D retrieval where manifold structure is less well-characterized.
