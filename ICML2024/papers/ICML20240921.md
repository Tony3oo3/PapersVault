## Problem

The literature oscillates between over-optimism (claiming LLMs can autonomously plan and self-verify) and over-pessimism (reducing LLMs to mere format translators for symbolic solvers). This paper argues that LLMs cannot plan or verify by themselves but can serve as powerful approximate knowledge sources and candidate generators when combined with external sound verifiers, and proposes a principled framework to achieve this tighter neuro-symbolic integration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240921/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes natural language problem specifications (often incomplete), PDDL domain models (acquired with LLM assistance and human expert curation), and iterative structured feedback from a bank of critics. It produces executable plans with formal soundness guarantees (vetted by hard model-based critics like VAL), refined domain models, and completed problem specifications. Unlike simple LLM-to-solver pipelines, the LLM plays multiple distinct roles within the loop—generating candidates, reformulating plans into critic-specific representations (timeline view, causal link view), and assisting with model acquisition—rather than serving solely as a one-shot translator.

## Method

The LLM-Modulo Framework is a Generate-Test-Critique loop where an LLM generates candidate plans, a composable bank of critics (hard model-based verifiers like VAL for causal/timeline/resource correctness, and soft LLM-based style/preference critics) evaluates them, and a Meta (Backprompt) Controller compiles critiques into the next iterative prompt. Key design decisions distinguish it from prior neuro-symbolic approaches: LLMs are active candidate generators rather than front-end translators, critics are modular and composable (allowing constructive feedback with subplan suggestions from partial planners), and humans are deliberately excluded from the inner planning loop (appearing only for 'once per domain' model acquisition and 'once per problem' specification refinement). The framework inherits soundness from the hard critics while avoiding the expressiveness and search-complexity limitations of traditional symbolic planners, since the LLM can propose candidates in any format without being constrained by a fixed planning language. Empirically, back-prompting from VAL improves GPT-4's Blocksworld accuracy from ~35% to 82% within 15 rounds, and the framework achieves 6× baseline performance on a travel planning benchmark using GPT-3.5-Turbo.

## Results

In autonomous mode, the best LLM (GPT-4) generates only ~12% executable plans on PlanBench, with zero-shot Blocksworld accuracy ranging from 0.5% (Gemini Pro) to 59.3% (Claude-3-Opus) across models. With the LLM-Modulo framework (back-prompting from VAL as external verifier), Blocksworld accuracy improves to 82% within 15 rounds and Logistics to 70%, while the obfuscated Mystery BW domain plateaus at ~10%. On the Travel Planning Benchmark, LLM-Modulo with automated critics achieves a 6× improvement over the best prompting baselines (CoT, ReAct) even with weaker models like GPT-3.5-Turbo and a limit of 10 back-prompting cycles.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the TravelPlanner benchmark (Xie et al., 2024), where the best prompting strategies (Chain-of-Thought, ReAct) achieve only 0.7% pass rate on GPT-3.5-Turbo, LLM-Modulo with automated critics in the loop achieves a 6× improvement, demonstrating that external verification is critical for constraint-satisfaction planning. Against the autonomous PlanBench baseline (Valmeekam et al., 2023), where the best zero-shot LLM (Claude-3-Opus) reaches 59.3% on Blocksworld and GPT-4 reaches 34.6%, LLM-Modulo with VAL back-prompting lifts Blocksworld to 82% and Logistics to 70% within 15 rounds. However, on the obfuscated Mystery BW domain, LLM-Modulo only reaches ~10%, confirming that when LLMs cannot retrieve plausible candidates from training data, the framework's completeness is limited. The paper also shows that LLM self-critiquing (iterative prompting without external verifiers) actually degrades performance on Graph Coloring and planning tasks, as the LLM cannot recognize correct solutions and discards them.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by proposing the LLM-Modulo Framework, a neuro-symbolic architecture that deliberately positions itself between two extremes in the literature: (1) the 'LLMs as planners' narrative (ReAct, Reflexion, Inner Monologue) and (2) the 'LLMs as mere format translators' narrative (LLM+P, LogicLM). Conceptually, it repurposes the generate-test-critique loop from SAT Modulo Theories (Nieuwenhuis & Oliveras, 2006) and the model-based verification tools of the automated planning community (VAL, PDDL), while drawing empirical support from the authors' own PlanBench evaluations and from systems like FunSearch and AlphaGeometry that already employ external verifiers. The key departure is the explicit reframing of LLMs as 'approximate knowledge sources' (a pseudo-System-1 memory) rather than as reasoning engines, which redefines their role from planner to candidate generator and knowledge provider within a sound verification loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper matters because it provides a principled, well-evidenced counter-narrative to the current 'agentic LLM' rush, arguing that autonomous LLM planning is unreliable (≈12–35% success on PlanBench) and that self-verification is fundamentally broken (iterative self-critiquing can make performance *worse*). The LLM-Modulo framework offers a concrete architectural alternative with formal soundness guarantees, and the 6× improvement on travel planning (from 0.7% to ~4%) demonstrates practical viability. Open questions include: whether the LLM's candidate-generation completeness is sufficient for complex domains, how the critic bank scales with problem expressiveness, and whether the framework generalizes beyond PDDL-style planning to open-ended real-world tasks. As a position paper, the empirical validation remains preliminary, and the framework's full potential awaits broader implementation.
