## Problem

Despite ResNet's dominant practical success since 2016, the fundamental theoretical question of how many tunable weights are needed for ResNet to approximate a function to error ε remained largely open. Prior results (Lin & Jegelka, 2018; Oono & Suzuki, 2019) established universal approximation with O(ε^{-d}) parameters but did not characterize the optimal rate, leaving unclear whether ResNet could outperform standard ReLU FNNs in parameter efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method takes a d-dimensional input vector x ∈ [0,1]^d (or [-M,M]^d for monomial approximation) and produces a scalar output R(x) approximating a target function f. The ResNet architecture uses constant width N=4 in activation layers with Q=d+O(1) neurons in linear layers, and the number of tunable (non-zero) weights scales as O(ε^{-d/2}) for Lipschitz functions — a factor-d reduction over FNNs. The framework extends to polynomials (O(p|E|log(p/ε)) weights), Sobolev-space functions (O_{d,r}(ε^{-d/r} log 1/ε) weights), and general Lebesgue-integrable functions characterized by the modulus of continuity ω_f.

## Method

The paper establishes both lower and upper bounds. For lower bounds, Proposition 1 shows any ResNet in RN(Q,N,L) is equivalent to a ReLU FNN of width N+Q and depth 2L, so FNN lower bounds (from metric entropy or VC dimension) transfer directly. For upper bounds, the key construction leverages the identity mapping in residual blocks: a constant-width ResNet uses linear-layer neurons for value storage (forwarding input values via identity) and activation-layer neurons for intermediate computation, with results refreshed block-by-block. This allows approximating fundamental functions (x² via hat/sawtooth compositions, xy via the identity xy=((x+y)/2)²−((x−y)/2)²) with only O(1) non-zero weights per block, then composing to build monomials, polynomials, and smooth functions. For Lebesgue-integrable functions, a space-partition construction generates step functions with O(L^{2/d}) steps using depth O(L), achieving the ε-order-optimal rate O(ω_f^{-1}(ε)^{-d/2}) with constant width — matching the lower bound from VC-dimension arguments and improving the prior O(ε^{-d}) rate by a factor of d.

## Results

The headline result (Theorem 7) shows that a ResNet with constant width (=4) can approximate any Lipschitz continuous function on [0,1]^d to error ε using O_d(ε^{-d/2}) tunable weights, which is ε-order optimal and matches a generalized lower bound. This achieves a factor-of-d reduction in parameter count compared to classical ReLU FNN results (which require O_d(ε^{-d}) for continuous approximators). For polynomials of degree p with |E| terms, the bound is O(p|E|log(p/ε)) tunable weights; for Sobolev W^{r,∞} functions, it is O_{d,r}(ε^{-d/r} log(1/ε)). In the empirical validation (Table 4), b-ResNet with width 10 achieves MSE 0.0102 at d=100, outperforming a standard FNN with width d/10 (MSE 0.0139) while using fewer parameters.

**Table 1: Approximation rate comparison for ResNet structures on Lipschitz continuous functions over [0,1]^d.**

| Paper | Network Architecture | Upper Bound (params) | Optimality |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lin & Jegelka (2018) | ResNet, 1 neuron/layer | O_d(ε^{-d}) | Suboptimal† |
| Oono & Suzuki (2019) | ResNet CNN, O(1) channels | O_d(ε^{-d}) | Suboptimal† |
| **Ours (Thm. 7)** | **ResNet, constant width (=4)** | **O_d(ε^{-d/2})** | **Optimal** |

† Suboptimal in terms of VC dimension (Shen et al., 2022b; Siegel, 2023), though optimal under entropy limitation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is against prior ResNet approximation results. Lin & Jegelka (2018) showed ResNet with one neuron per layer achieves O_d(ε^{-d}) for Lipschitz functions; Oono & Suzuki (2019) obtained the same O_d(ε^{-d}) rate for ResNet-type CNNs with O(1) channels. The present work improves both to O_d(ε^{-d/2}) using a constant-width (=4) ResNet, a strict improvement in the exponent of ε. Compared to ReLU FNNs, the paper achieves a factor-of-d reduction in tunable weights: where FNNs need O(d·p·log(p/ε)) weights for monomials (DeVore et al., 2021), the b-ResNet needs only O(p·log(p/ε)). The upper bound in Theorem 7 is shown to be ε-order optimal by matching the lower bound from VC dimension analysis (Siegel, 2023) applied via Proposition 1 (ResNet ⊂ sparse FNN). In the small-scale experiment (Table 4), b-ResNet with width 10–40 consistently outperforms the FNN baseline (width d/10) in MSE across d=100, 200, 300, e.g., 0.0102 vs 0.0139 at d=100.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on ResNet approximation theory initiated by Lin & Jegelka (2018), who proved universality of one-neuron-per-layer ResNets with a suboptimal O(ε^{-d}) rate, and Oono & Suzuki (2019), who studied ResNet-type CNNs. The key conceptual departure is establishing a formal equivalence between ResNet and sparse FNNs (Proposition 1) to derive lower bounds, then constructing constant-width (bottleneck) ResNets that achieve the O(ε^{-d/2}) rate previously only known for unconstrained deep FNNs (Yarotsky, 2018; Yarotsky & Zhevnerchuk, 2020). The polynomial and smooth-function constructions draw on DeVore et al. (2021) and Yarotsky (2017) but adapt them to exploit the identity-mapping structure of ResNet blocks for a factor-of-d parameter reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that constant-width ResNet achieves the ε-order optimal approximation rate for Lebesgue-integrable functions provides a clean theoretical justification for ResNet's practical dominance and suggests that the identity mapping is not merely a training aid but a fundamental expressive resource. Open questions remain: whether the optimal rate extends to uniform-norm approximation with constant width (the paper notes this is non-trivial), the trade-off between depth, width, and the number of linear-layer neurons Q, and lower bounds with respect to input dimension d. The empirical validation is minimal (a single synthetic function family), so the practical relevance of the specific constructions is untested at scale.
