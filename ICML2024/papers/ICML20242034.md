## Problem

Existing text-rich image benchmarks (TextVQA, STVQA, ESTVQA) primarily evaluate a model's OCR capability, where accurate text extraction alone is sufficient to answer questions. This leaves untested the ability of large multimodal models to perform context-sensitive joint reasoning over embedded text and visual elements in an image — a skill essential for real-world tasks like navigating public spaces or interpreting memes. No prior dataset systematically benchmarks this context-sensitive text-rich visual reasoning capability.

## Input / Output

Each dataset instance pairs a text-rich image (sourced from 8 visual scenarios: time, shopping, navigation, abstract, app usage, web usage, infographic, and miscellaneous natural scenes) with a human-written instruction (question or imperative task, averaging 65 words) that is explicitly designed to be unsolvable by OCR alone. The model produces a free-form text response (averaging 117 words), which is scored via human acceptance rating (0–100%, majority vote of 3 annotators) on a 280-sample subset and via GPT-4-based automatic evaluation on the full 506-sample set. The dataset also includes human-written reference responses for every instance, enabling reference-guided automatic metrics (BLEURT, Rouge-L, BERTScore).

## Method

CONTEXTUAL is constructed through a three-stage annotation pipeline: (1) image selection from six sources (LAION-5B via CLIP retrieval, Rico UI screenshots, Open WebText webpages, and re-annotated test sets from InfographicVQA/STVQA/ESTVQA) with heuristic OCR-based filtering; (2) instruction-response pair creation by two author groups following guidelines that mandate context-sensitive reasoning (i.e., the instruction cannot be answered by text-based reasoning over detected OCR); and (3) MTurk verification achieving 96% correctness. The evaluation framework combines human evaluation (280 samples, 3 independent annotators, majority vote) with automatic evaluation using GPT-4, GPT-4V, BLEURT, Rouge-L, and BERTScore, with a correlation analysis (ROC-AUC, Spearman) confirming GPT-4 evaluation as the most faithful proxy for human judgment (ROC-AUC 85.9, Spearman 0.71). Fourteen foundation models are benchmarked — three augmented LLMs (GPT-4 with OCR/layout-aware OCR/captions), two proprietary LMMs (GPT-4V, Gemini-Pro-Vision), and nine open LMMs — alongside a human baseline, revealing a 30.8% gap between the best model (GPT-4V at 49.3%) and humans (80.1%), with GPT-4V struggling most on time-reading and infographics but outperforming humans on abstract contexts like memes.

## Results

On the CONTEXTUAL dataset (506 samples), GPT-4V achieves the highest LMM acceptance rating of 49.3% under human evaluation, yet still trails the human baseline of 80.1% by 30.8 percentage points. The best open-source model, ShareGPT-4V-7B, reaches only 21.8%, while the augmented LLM approach (GPT-4 with layout-aware OCR + captions) scores just 17.2%, confirming that OCR alone is insufficient for context-sensitive reasoning. Under GPT-4 automatic evaluation on the full dataset, GPT-4V scores 47.4% average versus 69.6% for humans, with the largest per-category gaps in Time (18.0% vs. 64.0%) and Infographics (28.0% vs. 72.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4V (49.3% human eval) is the strongest LMM but loses to humans (80.1%) by a wide 30.8-point margin, and outperforms Gemini-Pro-Vision (27.8%) by 21.5 points. Among open-source models, LLaVA-Next-34B (36.8% GPT-4 eval) is the best, narrowing the gap to proprietary models, while ShareGPT-4V-7B (22.6%), Qwen-VL-7B (21.8%), and LLaVA-1.5-13B (20.8%) cluster in the low-20s range. Notably, models specifically designed for text-rich reasoning—LLaVAR-13B (14.9%) and BLIVA-Vicuna-7B (10.3%)—underperform general-purpose open LMMs, indicating that OCR-focused training does not transfer to context-sensitive joint reasoning. The augmented LLM baseline (GPT-4 with layout-aware OCR + captions, 22.2% GPT-4 eval) confirms that textual representations of the image are insufficient without direct visual perception. GPT-4V uniquely outperforms humans on the Abstract category (100% vs. 75.5%), likely due to extensive meme/quote data in its training, while all models struggle most on Time reading (GPT-4V: 18.0% vs. humans: 64.0%). Gemini-Pro-Vision edges out GPT-4V on Web Usage (43.8% vs. 42.0%) and Miscellaneous Natural Scenes (52.8% vs. 48.0%), but trails on all other categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by distinguishing context-sensitive text-rich visual reasoning from the OCR-centric evaluation that dominates prior text-rich VQA benchmarks (TextVQA, STVQA, ESTVQA, InfographicVQA). While those datasets test whether a model can read embedded text, CONTEXTUAL explicitly requires that the answer cannot be derived from OCR alone, forcing joint reasoning over textual and visual cues. The paper also departs from general VQA and commonsense reasoning benchmarks (VQA, VCR) by focusing specifically on the interaction between text and its visual context across eight real-world scenarios. It repurposes images from existing sources (LAION-5B, Rico, InfographicVQA, STVQA, ESTVQA) but creates novel human-written instructions that enforce the context-sensitivity constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30.8% gap between GPT-4V and human performance, along with the finding that models specifically designed for text-rich reasoning (LLaVAR, BLIVA) perform poorly, reveals that the field's current understanding of 'text-rich visual reasoning' has been too narrowly equated with OCR accuracy. The observation that GPT-4V outperforms humans on abstract/meme content (100% vs. 75.5%) while struggling with time reading (18% vs. 64%) highlights specific, actionable gaps in training data diversity. Open questions include whether synthetic data generation pipelines can scale context-sensitive instructions effectively, how to improve fine-grained visual perception in open models, and whether the dataset's modest size (506 samples) limits generalizability of the findings.
