## Problem

Neural Collapse (NC) was originally characterized for class-balanced datasets, where last-layer features converge to a simplex ETF. Under class-imbalanced training with cross-entropy loss, the geometry deviates from the ETF, but the exact convergence structure of features and classifier weights—particularly under the non-negativity constraint imposed by ReLU activations—remained uncharacterized. Prior concurrent work (Hong & Ling, 2023) proved within-class collapse but did not derive the magnitudes, angles, or the explicit relationship between classifier weights and class-means, leaving NC2 and NC3 properties unaddressed for this setting.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard image classification inputs (MNIST, FashionMNIST at 28×28 grayscale; CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 at 32×32 RGB) with one-hot class labels, where the training set is deliberately imbalanced with varying samples per class. The key theoretical output is not a new prediction but a closed-form geometric characterization: last-layer features form a General Orthogonal Frame (pairwise orthogonal vectors with class-dependent lengths), and classifier weights align to a scaled-and-centered version of the class-means with scaling factors proportional to √n_k. The non-negativity constraint on features (H ≥ 0) is the distinguishing structural assumption that makes the orthogonal frame geometry emerge rather than a simplex ETF.

## Method

The paper analyzes the global minimizers of a regularized cross-entropy training problem under the Unconstrained Features Model with non-negative features (UFM+), where last-layer features are treated as free variables constrained to the non-negative orthant. The proof constructs a lower bound on the loss by applying Jensen's inequality to the log-sum-exp term, then uses a Cauchy-Schwarz decomposition with a carefully chosen alignment constant to separate classifier weights from features in the logit expression. At optimality, the equality conditions yield: (i) within-class feature collapse, (ii) pairwise orthogonal class-means with closed-form norms depending on n_k and the regularization parameters, (iii) classifier weights proportional to K√n_k h_k − Σ√n_m h_m, and (iv) an exact threshold C(N,K,λ_W,λ_H) below which a class's feature collapses to zero (Minority Collapse). Empirical validation uses MLP, VGG11, and ResNet18 backbones trained on imbalanced subsets of four datasets, measuring convergence of five NC metrics toward the predicted geometry.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 4.1) provides a closed-form characterization of the optimal geometry: class-means form a General Orthogonal Frame (GOF) with pairwise orthogonal vectors whose norms scale as √((K−1)/K · λ_W/λ_H · 1/n_k) · M_k, and classifier weights align with scaled-and-centered class-means (w_k ∝ K√n_k h_k − Σ_m √n_m h_m). The minority collapse threshold is derived exactly: a class k collapses (h_k = 0) when n_k ≤ N²·K/(K−1)·λ_W·λ_H. Empirically, all five NC metrics (NC1, NC2-W, NC2-WW^T, NC2-H^TH, NC3) converge to small values across MLP, VGG11, and ResNet18 on imbalanced CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, MNIST, and FashionMNIST, confirming the theoretical predictions. The balanced case (Corollary 4.2) recovers the classical simplex ETF structure, validating the generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical characterization paper rather than a method-comparison study, so there are no direct accuracy baselines. However, the derived geometry is contrasted with several known structures: (1) the original NC/ETF geometry for balanced data is recovered as a special case (Corollary 4.2); (2) the SELI geometry from UFM-SVM (Thrampoulidis et al., 2022) is shown to be distinct from the CE geometry for both finite and vanishing regularization (Appendix C); (3) the GOF for MSE loss (Dang et al., 2023) has the same orthogonal structure but different norm scaling; (4) the OF for supervised contrastive loss (Kini et al., 2023) has equal-length vectors regardless of imbalance, unlike the CE case where lengths depend on n_k. Compared to the concurrent work by Hong & Ling (2023), this paper additionally derives the full magnitude of prediction vectors, the explicit geometry of features and classifier (NC2, NC3), norm ratios, angles, and the exact minority collapse threshold — none of which are covered in that work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neural Collapse (NC) characterization from the class-balanced setting (Papyan et al., 2020; Zhu et al., 2021) to the class-imbalanced regime for cross-entropy loss, filling a gap left by prior theoretical work that had only characterized NC geometry for MSE (Dang et al., 2023) and SVM (Thrampoulidis et al., 2022) under imbalance. It adopts the UFM+ (non-negative features) framework from Kini et al. (2023) to capture ReLU activation effects, differentiating itself from the concurrent work of Hong & Ling (2023) which studied CE with standard UFM but did not derive the full geometry of features, classifiers, or their mutual relationship. The paper also builds on the General Orthogonal Frame (GOF) concept from the MSE imbalanced analysis, showing that CE loss under non-negative features yields a similar but distinct orthogonal structure with class-dependent lengths.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first closed-form characterization of the convergence geometry for CE loss under class imbalance with ReLU features, yielding an exact threshold for Minority Collapse that practitioners can use to diagnose when minority classes become indistinguishable. A notable finding is that, unlike MSE and SVM losses, the feature norm under CE loss is not always negatively correlated with the number of training samples, challenging a natural expectation. The main limitation is the restriction to d ≥ K (feature dimension at least number of classes); the bottleneck regime d < K remains unaddressed, and the UFM+ model, while motivated by ReLU, still abstracts away the full depth and non-linearity of practical networks.
