## Problem

While value overestimation in off-policy actor-critic RL has been extensively studied, the paper identifies a long-neglected dual problem: Q-values are systematically underestimated in the later stages of training. This underestimation arises because the Bellman update uses actions sampled from the current (still suboptimal) policy as target-update actions, which are inferior to the more optimal actions already accumulated in the replay buffer, thereby depressing the Q-target and hindering further policy improvement.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard state observations and (s, a, r, s') transitions from a replay buffer, but critically re-purposes the buffer to extract a high-expectile state value V(s) that approximates the maximum Q over historically encountered actions. The V-network is trained with an asymmetric expectile loss (τ=0.7) so that it upweights larger Q-values, effectively encoding the best-performing actions without explicitly modeling the policy mixture. Outputs include Q-value estimates, a learned policy π_θ, and the auxiliary V-function used for the exploitation Bellman target.

## Method

The core contribution is the Blended Exploitation and Exploration (BEE) operator, a linear combination B = λ·T_exploit + (1−λ)·T_explore that fuses two Bellman updates: T_exploit bootstraps from the max Q over replay-buffer actions (computed via IQL-style expectile regression on a V-network), while T_explore uses the standard SAC-style entropy-regularized Bellman evaluation with actions from the current policy. This design lets the agent 'seize serendipity' by exploiting past high-performing actions to raise the Q-target, while retaining exploration optimism through the policy-sampled component. The paper proves that B is a γ-contraction, satisfies policy improvement, and converges to the optimal policy under finite MDPs. Two practical instantiations are provided: BAC (model-free, built on SAC with expectile regression) and MB-BAC (model-based, integrating BAC into an MBPO-style Dyna framework where T_exploit is applied to real data and T_explore to model rollouts). A single hyperparameter set (λ=0.5, τ=0.7) suffices across 50+ continuous control tasks.

## Results

BAC outperforms all model-free baselines (SAC, TD3, DAC, RRS, PPO) across over 50 continuous control tasks spanning MuJoCo, DMControl, Meta-World, ManiSkill2, Adroit, Shadow Dexterous Hand, and MyoSuite benchmarks with a single hyperparameter set (λ=0.5, τ=0.7). On the failure-prone Humanoid-Standup task, BAC achieves average returns of 280,000 at 2.5M steps and 360,000 at 5M steps, which is 1.5× and 2.1× higher than the strongest baseline. On the real-world D'Kitty quadruped robot, BAC achieves a success rate approximately 50% higher than SAC on DKittyWalk-Medium and outperforms both SAC and TD3 across four challenging terrains (smooth road, rough stone road, uphill stone road, grassland). MB-BAC learns faster than all model-based baselines (MBPO, CMLO, AutoMBPO, SLBO, PETS) while matching or exceeding the asymptotic performance of model-free methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BAC consistently outperforms SAC across all six model-free benchmarks (DMControl, Meta-World, ManiSkill2, Adroit, Shadow Dexterous Hand, MyoSuite) with better sample efficiency; on the failure-prone Humanoid-Standup task, BAC achieves 2.1× the return of SAC at 5M steps, while the SAC agent ends in a wobbling kneeling posture versus BAC's stable standing. BAC also surpasses TD3, with the ad-hoc BEE-TD3 variant outperforming original TD3 in 15 DMControl tasks, confirming the BEE operator's generality. DAC and RRS, which use alternative exploration mechanisms (sample-aware entropy and random reward shifting respectively), are outperformed by BAC, with the DAC agent sitting on the ground and the RRS agent rolling around on Humanoid-Standup. PPO, an on-policy method that discards historical policies, is the weakest baseline, highlighting the value of off-policy replay. In the model-based setting, MB-BAC learns faster than MBPO, CMLO, AutoMBPO, SLBO, and PETS while achieving comparable or superior asymptotic performance, demonstrating that the BEE operator transfers effectively to Dyna-style frameworks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the off-policy actor-critic lineage (SAC, TD3) by identifying a previously overlooked failure mode—value underestimation in later training stages—distinct from the well-studied overestimation problem. It bridges offline RL's in-sample learning techniques (IQL's expectile regression, SQL, EQL) into the online RL setting, using them to compute a Bellman Exploitation operator that extracts high-quality actions from the replay buffer, while retaining SAC's entropy-regularized Bellman Exploration operator for the current policy. The resulting BEE operator is a linear blend of these two, and the practical instantiations (BAC, MB-BAC) build directly on the SAC and MBPO frameworks respectively, making the contribution a targeted modification to the Bellman update rather than a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes the exploration-exploitation tradeoff in online RL as a Bellman-operator design problem, offering a simple, plug-and-play mechanism that requires only two additional hyperparameters (λ, τ) and a single V-network. Real-world quadruped robot validation (D'Kitty) and consistent gains across 50+ continuous control tasks lend practical credibility. Open questions include whether the approach extends to discrete action spaces, how it interacts with more advanced exploration terms (pink noise, sample-aware entropy), and whether the adaptive λ mechanisms can fully eliminate the need for manual tuning. The paper also raises the broader question of how much of offline RL's conservatism is actually beneficial versus harmful when transplanted into online settings.
