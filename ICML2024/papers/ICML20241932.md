## Problem

Existing sharpness measures for neural network optimization (e.g., trace or operator norm of the Hessian) are limited in non-convex settings where eigenvalues can be negative, and they fail to respect parameter invariances such as scale-invariance inherent in ReLU networks. No unified, compact parameterized framework existed that could represent all possible sharpness measures while simultaneously supporting meaningful invariance properties under reparametrization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a differentiable training loss L(x), a perturbation radius ρ, and a triplet of hyperparameters (φ, ψ, μ) that jointly specify the target sharpness measure. At each iteration it consumes n+1 gradient evaluations (one at x and n at perturbed points x+ρv_i) plus n loss evaluations, requiring no explicit Hessian computation. The output is trained model parameters; the sharpness regularizer R_ρ(x) is estimated via Monte Carlo sampling from μ, with the number of samples n kept small (n=1 or 2 in practice) to limit overhead.

## Method

The paper defines a parameterized sharpness measure S(x; φ, ψ, μ) = φ(∫ ψ(½vᵀ∇²L(x)v) dμ(v)) and proves it is universally expressive: by varying (φ, ψ, μ) it can represent any continuous function of the Hessian matrix (Theorems 1–2). A corresponding zeroth-order loss L_{(φ,ψ,μ)}(x) = L(x) + ρ²R_ρ(x) is shown to be explicitly biased toward minimizing S over the zero-loss manifold (Theorem 3), generalizing prior explicit-bias results for SAM and average-direction SAM. Choosing μ to be invariant under a group action G yields G-invariant sharpness measures (Theorems 4–5), enabling scale-invariant objectives like the determinant of the Hessian. Two concrete algorithms are instantiated: Frob-SAM (m=2, Gaussian samples, φ(t₁,t₂)=2(t₂−t₁²), ψ(t)=(t,t²)) which minimizes the Frobenius norm of the Hessian, and Det-SAM (m=1, Lebesgue measure approximated by hypercube sampling, φ(t)=(2π)^d/t², ψ(t)=exp(−t)) which minimizes the determinant and is provably scale-invariant.

## Results

On full CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and SVHN, Frob-SAM, Trace-SAM, and Det-SAM achieve test accuracies of 94.96%, 77.16%, and 96.26% respectively, competitive with SAM (95.54%, 78.62%, 96.45%) and ASAM (95.49%, 78.91%, 96.18%). The advantage becomes pronounced under data scarcity: on 10%-subsampled CIFAR-10-S, Frob-SAM reaches 74.49% versus 72.96% for SAM (+1.5%), and on CIFAR-100-S it achieves 38.30% versus 36.87% for SAM (+1.4%). Under 20% label noise (CIFAR-10-C), Frob-SAM attains 87.57% accuracy, outperforming SAM (85.42%) by over 2% and SGD (82.78%) by nearly 5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAM (Foret et al., 2021), the proposed methods are broadly competitive on full datasets but clearly superior under limited data: Frob-SAM exceeds SAM by 1.5% on CIFAR-10-S (74.49% vs. 72.96%) and 1.4% on CIFAR-100-S (38.30% vs. 36.87%), while on noisy CIFAR-10-C it leads by 2.15% (87.57% vs. 85.42%). Against ASAM (Kwon et al., 2021), the proposed methods are slightly behind on full CIFAR-100 (77.16% vs. 78.91%) but competitive elsewhere; notably, ASAM's scale-invariance advantage is matched by Det-SAM's inherent scale-invariance without requiring Fisher information. Against SSAM (Mi et al., 2022), the proposed methods trail on full CIFAR-10 (94.96% vs. 95.78%) but surpass it on CIFAR-100-S (38.30% vs. 35.33%) and CIFAR-10-C (87.57% vs. 86.24%). Across all settings, every proposed variant (Frob, Trace, Det) consistently outperforms plain SGD by 0.5–5%, confirming the value of the sharpness-aware regularization. The paper notes that the best-performing sharpness measure is task-dependent, with Frob-SAM excelling under noise and data scarcity while Det-SAM and Trace-SAM are competitive on full datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the explicit-bias analysis of Wen et al. (2023a), who proved that SAM minimizes λ_max(∇²L) and the Gaussian-averaging variant minimizes tr(∇²L)/d, to a universal (φ,ψ,μ)-parameterized family that provably spans all continuous functions of the Hessian. It departs from prior scale-invariance approaches (ASAM by Kwon et al., 2021; Fisher-SAM by Kim et al., 2022) by showing that invariance under any group action follows simply from choosing a group-invariant measure μ, rather than requiring task-specific constructions. The concrete algorithms Frob-SAM and Det-SAM are new instances not previously studied, with Frob-SAM's update rule having a clean cross-covariance interpretation that has no direct analogue in the SAM/ASAM/SSAM literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The universal framework gives practitioners a principled way to design sharpness-aware objectives for any desired Hessian functional, and the invariance theorems (Theorems 4–5) provide a systematic recipe for handling parameter symmetries that plagues ReLU and linear networks. However, the universality bound requires m = d (or d(d+1)/2 for full Hessian functions), so practical use relies on small-m approximations whose optimality is not guaranteed. The experimental gains are modest (1–2% over SAM in noisy-label and limited-data settings) and the best-performing bias is dataset-dependent, leaving open the question of whether the sharpness measure can be meta-learned per task. A key limitation is that the explicit-bias theorem (Theorem 3) requires third-order differentiability and specific decay conditions on the loss, which may not hold for all practical architectures.
