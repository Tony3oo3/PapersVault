## Problem

Existing policy learning methods optimize either short-term or long-term rewards in isolation, yet in practice these can diverge or even oppose each other (e.g., clickbait boosts short-term CTR but causes long-term churn). No principled causal-inference framework existed for learning a policy that simultaneously balances both, especially when long-term outcomes suffer from systematic missingness driven by the short-term outcome itself.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular observational data where each unit has pre-treatment covariates X, a binary treatment A, an always-observed short-term outcome S, a possibly-missing long-term outcome Y, and a missingness indicator R. The missingness mechanism is allowed to depend on (X, A, S) rather than just X, reflecting realistic dropout patterns. The output is a binary treatment policy π: X → {0,1} that maximizes a weighted combination of short- and long-term rewards, along with semiparametrically efficient estimates of both reward quantities for any candidate policy.

## Method

The paper formulates balanced policy learning as maximizing V(π;s) + λV(π;y) and derives the optimal policy in closed form via the weighted sum of conditional causal effects τ_s(x) + λτ_y(x). Under Strong Ignorability and a weaker missingness assumption (R ⊥⊥ Y | X, S, A), it proves identifiability of both rewards and derives their efficient influence functions, revealing that the short-term outcome S reduces the semiparametric efficiency bound of the long-term reward when S is associated with Y given X. The resulting estimators are doubly robust for the short-term reward and quadruply robust for the long-term reward, constructed via sample-splitting to satisfy DML rate conditions. The paper further establishes consistency, asymptotic normality, and O_P(n^{-1/2}) regret convergence for the learned policy under standard nuisance-parameter rate requirements.

## Results

On the JOBS dataset, the proposed method (PRO.) with balanced strategy (λ=0.5) achieves a balanced reward of 2895.4, welfare change (ΔW) of 417.6, and policy error of 0.473, outperforming IPW (2809.2, 353.5, 0.491), OR (2661.5, 251.9, 0.522), and DM (2069.6, 665.7, 0.404). On the PRODUCT dataset, PRO.(OURS) achieves a balanced reward of 5183.4, ΔW of 742.7, and error of 0.490, again leading all baselines. Across both datasets, balanced rewards consistently exceed either short-term-only or long-term-only rewards, confirming the value of the balancing formulation. The proposed estimator also achieves the lowest policy error among the semiparametric methods (PRO. vs. IPW/OR) in the balanced setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four estimator families—Direct Method (DM), Outcome Regression (OR), Inverse Probability Weighting (IPW), and the proposed semiparametric estimator (PRO.)—each applied under three policy strategies (NAIVE-S, NAIVE-Y, OURS). In the balanced setting (λ=0.5), PRO. achieves the highest balanced reward on both JOBS (2895.4 vs. IPW's 2809.2 and OR's 2661.5) and PRODUCT (5183.4 vs. IPW's 5116.4 and OR's 5104.1), with the largest margin over DM (2069.6 on JOBS, 4975.4 on PRODUCT). PRO. also attains the best ΔW among semiparametric methods in the balanced setting (417.6 on JOBS, 742.7 on PRODUCT). In terms of policy error, DM achieves the lowest error on JOBS (0.404) but at the cost of substantially lower balanced reward, while PRO. offers the best reward–error trade-off. The balanced strategy (OURS) consistently yields higher balanced rewards than either NAIVE-S or NAIVE-Y across all methods, validating the paper's core claim that explicitly balancing short- and long-term rewards outperforms optimizing either alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the surrogate index framework of Athey et al. (2019a) and the DML/sample-splitting machinery of Chernozhukov et al. (2018) to a new policy learning setting that simultaneously optimizes short-term and long-term rewards. Unlike prior long-term effect estimation work that treats short-term surrogates as mere proxies (risking the surrogate paradox) or focuses exclusively on long-term outcomes, this paper formulates a constrained/weighted optimization over both horizons and derives a quadruple-robust estimator for the long-term reward that exploits the short-term outcome as an auxiliary variable. The missing data mechanism (R ⊥ Y | X, S, A) is deliberately weaker and more realistic than the standard MAR-on-X assumption used in prior surrogate-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practically critical gap: decision-makers in marketing, healthcare, and IT routinely face the tension between immediate gains and sustained outcomes, yet prior causal policy learning methods optimized only one horizon. The theoretical result that short-term outcomes reduce the semiparametric efficiency bound for long-term reward estimation (Proposition 6.2) provides a principled justification for collecting both. Key limitations include the no-unmeasured-confounding requirement (Assumption 5.1) and the binary treatment setting; extending to unmeasured confounders, dynamic multi-period regimes, and continuous treatments are natural open directions the authors acknowledge.
