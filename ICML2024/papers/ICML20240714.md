## Problem

Estimating room impulse responses (RIRs) for arbitrary source-receiver positions in enclosed 3D spaces remains difficult: physical measurement is unscalable, wave/geometry-based simulation requires detailed room knowledge and heavy computation, and recent deep learning approaches assume access to massive RIR ground-truth datasets. No existing method can predict RIRs for unseen position pairs in a self-supervised manner without requiring direct RIR labels or prior room acoustic properties.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240714/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two 3D spatial positions (source and receiver agent locations) as input and produces a 1D neural RIR in the time domain. Internally, the RIR is predicted as real and imaginary 2D time-frequency maps at three resolutions (128×128, 256×256, 512×512) and converted to the time domain via differentiable ISTFT. The output RIR is sound-class agnostic and can be convolved with any source signal; during training, a sine sweep is used as the probe signal, and the RIR length adapts to the dataset (20,001 points for Matterport3D, 32,768 for MeshRIR at 48 kHz).

## Method

DeepNeRAP is a self-supervised implicit neural representation that encodes sound propagation as a continuous function of two positions. The architecture comprises: (1) a learnable 500×500×2 2D spatial grid capturing room acoustic properties, (2) multi-scale position-aware feature extraction (256 scales) with sine/cosine position encoding to satisfy the Globality principle, (3) permutation-invariant element-wise addition of source and receiver features to enforce Reciprocity, (4) a 6-layer MLP encoder (512 hidden units), and (5) a multi-resolution decoder that predicts real/imaginary frequency maps at three scales via 2D transposed convolutions, followed by ISTFT. Training minimizes L2 loss between the neural-RIR-convolved source sound and the actually received sound in both time and frequency domains, requiring no RIR ground truth—only the emitted and received waveforms plus agent positions. The design is grounded in four physical principles (Globality, Reciprocity, Superposition, Sound Independence), making the learned primitive acoustically interpretable.

## Results

On the synthetic Matterport3D dataset, DeepNeRAP achieves SDR of 6.62 dB, T60 error of 6.04 ms, PSNR of 18.95 dB, and PESQ of 1.53, outperforming all baselines across every metric. On the real-world MeshRIR dataset, DeepNeRAP reaches SDR of 7.31 dB, T60 error of 5.01 ms, PSNR of 20.15 dB, and PESQ of 1.77, again leading all comparisons. The method uses only 4.71M trainable parameters, fewer than NAF (12.77M) and S2IR-GAN (12.78M), while requiring no ground-truth RIR data for training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepNeRAP outperforms all three baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against the closest competitor NAF (Luo et al., 2022), DeepNeRAP improves SDR by 1.46 dB (6.62 vs. 5.16) and reduces T60 error by 1.80 ms (6.04 vs. 7.84) on Matterport3D, with even larger margins on MeshRIR (SDR 7.31 vs. 5.36, T60 error 5.01 vs. 7.44). IR-MLP (Richard et al., 2022) and S2IR-GAN (Ratnarajah et al., 2023) trail further behind, with S2IR-GAN achieving the lowest SDR (3.81 dB) and highest T60 error (9.19 ms) on Matterport3D. A critical distinction is that all three baselines require ground-truth RIR data for supervised training, whereas DeepNeRAP is trained self-supervisedly using only source and receiver sound pairs, making it applicable in settings where RIR measurement is infeasible. The PSNR gap is particularly large: DeepNeRAP achieves 18.95 dB versus 15.17 (NAF), 13.67 (IR-MLP), and 12.98 (S2IR-GAN) on Matterport3D, indicating substantially better frequency-domain RIR reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeepNeRAP repurposes the implicit neural representation paradigm (popularized by NeRF and DeepSDF for vision and 3D shape) to the acoustics domain, but departs sharply from prior RIR prediction methods (NAF, IR-MLP, S2IR-GAN) by eliminating the need for ground-truth RIR supervision. Instead of learning from massive pre-collected RIR datasets, it learns from raw source-receiver sound pairs collected via active two-agent probing, making it self-supervised. The architecture is further distinguished by explicit incorporation of room acoustics physical principles (reciprocity, globality, superposition, sound independence) into the network design, which prior deep RIR methods largely ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-supervised formulation removes the primary practical barrier to RIR prediction—expensive and unscalable RIR measurement—making spatially continuous RIR estimation feasible in real-world deployment for AR/VR and architectural acoustics. The physics-informed design (permutation invariance for reciprocity, multi-scale global feature extraction for globality) yields acoustically interpretable primitives rather than black-box predictions. Key limitations include the assumption that two agents can traverse all reachable areas within a limited step budget, and the LTI assumption that breaks down in time-varying or nonlinear acoustic environments. Open questions include scalability to very large or dynamically changing rooms, real-time inference, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the relatively controlled settings tested here.
