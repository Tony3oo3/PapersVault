## Problem

Previous theoretical analyses of softmax gating mixture-of-experts (MoE) models have relied on the impractical assumption that data are generated from a well-specified probabilistic (e.g., Gaussian) MoE, leaving the statistical properties of MoE under realistic deterministic regression settings largely unexplored. This paper addresses that gap by studying the convergence behavior of least squares estimation when the response is a noisy realization of a deterministic gated MoE regression function, a setting where maximum likelihood is inapplicable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n i.i.d. regression pairs (X_i, Y_i) with X_i ∈ R^d drawn from a fixed distribution μ and Y_i = f_{G_*}(X_i) + ε_i, where f_{G_*} is a softmax-gated weighted sum of k_* parametric expert functions and ε_i is Gaussian noise. The estimator is overspecified: it searches over mixing measures with up to k > k_* components, and the output is a discrete mixing measure Ĝ_n = Σ exp(β̂_0i) δ_{(β̂_1i, η̂_i)} from which both the fitted regression function f_{Ĝ_n} and individual expert parameters (gating vectors β_1, expert parameters η) are recovered. The analysis is conducted in fixed dimension, with the parameter space Θ assumed compact.

## Method

The paper establishes a parametric L²(μ) rate of O_P(√(log n)/n) for the regression function via bracketing entropy arguments, then derives parameter and expert estimation rates by constructing Voronoi-cell-based loss functions (D_1, D_2) that are lower-bounded by the L² distance between fitted and true regression functions. The key technical ingredient is a novel 'strong identifiability' condition requiring that the function F(x; β_1, η) = exp(β_1^T x) h(x, η) and its first- and second-order parameter derivatives remain linearly independent, which eliminates PDE-based interactions between gating and expert parameters and yields polynomial rates (O_P(n^{-1/2}) or O_P(n^{-1/4}) depending on Voronoi cell occupancy). For ridge experts σ(a^T x + b), a 'strong independence' condition on the activation σ (satisfied by sigmoid, tanh, GELU) recovers polynomial rates when all a_j* ≠ 0, but the paper proves minimax lower bounds showing that polynomial activations or vanishing expert parameters induce a PDE interaction that makes all parameter rates slower than any polynomial in n, potentially as slow as O_P(1/log n).

## Results

The paper establishes that the least squares estimator of the softmax gating MoE regression function converges at the parametric rate O_P(√(log n)/n) (Theorem 2.1). For strongly identifiable experts (e.g., sigmoid, tanh, GELU activations) and ridge experts with strongly independent activations under Regime 1 (all a_j* ≠ 0), expert estimation rates are O_P(n^{-1/2}) when a single component fits the expert and O_P(n^{-1/4}) when multiple components fit it. In stark contrast, polynomial experts and ridge experts under Regime 2 (at least one a_j* = 0) exhibit rates slower than any polynomial O_P(n^{-1/2r}) for all r ≥ 1, potentially as slow as O_P(1/log n).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; the 'comparisons' are between different expert function classes under the same deterministic regression MoE framework. Strongly identifiable experts (sigmoid, tanh, GELU feed-forward networks) and ridge experts with strongly independent activations (Regime 1) achieve polynomial rates of O_P(n^{-1/2}) or O_P(n^{-1/4}), matching or improving upon the probabilistic Gaussian MoE rates from Nguyen et al. (2023b). In contrast, polynomial experts (including linear experts) and input-independent ridge experts (Regime 2) suffer from a PDE-induced interaction between gating and expert parameters, yielding rates slower than any polynomial — a phenomenon absent in the probabilistic setting where Nguyen et al. (2023b) obtained polynomial rates O_P(n^{-1/r̄_j}) depending on the solvability of polynomial systems. Table 2 makes this gap explicit: under the probabilistic model, linear experts fitted by multiple components achieve O_P(n^{-1/4}) or O_P(n^{-1/6}), whereas under the deterministic model the same experts degrade to sub-polynomial rates. The paper also notes that increasing expert network depth does not rescue polynomial experts from the slow-rate phenomenon, as the PDE interaction is intrinsic to the polynomial form.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of MoE parameter estimation theory from Ho et al. (2022) and Nguyen et al. (2023b, 2024a), which studied convergence rates under well-specified probabilistic Gaussian MoE models using maximum likelihood estimation. The key departure is moving to a deterministic regression setting where responses are noisy realizations of a gated MoE function, analyzed via least squares estimation rather than MLE. The Voronoi cell loss framework from Manole & Ho (2022) is adapted to construct parameter-level loss functions, and the PDE-based interaction analysis from Nguyen et al. (2023b) is re-examined under the fundamentally different deterministic setting, revealing qualitatively different estimation behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first statistical analysis of least squares estimation under MoE models, providing the first theoretical guidance on expert function selection for practical MoE training. The finding that polynomial (including linear) experts suffer non-polynomial estimation rates in the deterministic setting—contrasting sharply with their polynomial rates in the probabilistic setting—has direct implications for MoE architecture design in LLMs and vision transformers. Key limitations include the fixed-dimensional analysis, the well-specified model assumption, and the lack of depth analysis; open questions include extending to misspecified settings, other gating mechanisms (Top-K, cosine), and understanding how network depth interacts with the PDE-based parameter interactions.
