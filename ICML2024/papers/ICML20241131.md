## Problem

In class-imbalanced learning, minority classes suffer from poor feature generalization due to insufficient training samples, and standard knowledge distillation from pre-trained models fails to address this because it only transfers the posterior p(y|x) rather than the class-conditional distribution p(x|y) that encodes the teacher's understanding of intrinsic data structure. This paper identifies that the student model cannot recover the teacher's p(x|y) through conventional distillation on imbalanced data, leaving a critical information gap for rare classes.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100-LT, 224×224 for ImageNet-LT and iNaturalist) as input and produces class labels via a softmax classifier. The teacher model is a CLIP ViT-B/16 image encoder (89.2M params) configured in three modes—zero-shot with text prompts, Nearest Class Mean, or AdaptFormer fine-tuning—while the student is a smaller ResNet (0.47M–25.8M params). Beyond original training images, the method also consumes synthetic mixed images generated via Mixup or CutMix from a class-balanced sampling strategy, and a linear projection layer aligns the student's feature dimension to match the teacher's for the feature imitation loss.

## Method

The core contribution is the Class-Conditional Knowledge Distillation (CCKD) loss, which distills the teacher's p(x|y) rather than p(y|x) by normalizing the teacher's softmax output by its estimated class prior p^t(y) and the student's output by the training class distribution p_tr^s(y), yielding a closed-form KL divergence over adjusted logits (Proposition 3.1). Augmented CCKD (ACCKD) extends this with three components: (1) a synthetic class-balanced dataset D_mix constructed by mixing one class-balanced-sampled image with one randomly-sampled image via Mixup or CutMix (with λ > 0.5 to preserve balance), on which a simplified CCKD loss and teacher-weighted supervised loss are applied; (2) a feature imitation loss that maximizes cosine similarity between teacher and student feature vectors on both original and mixed samples; and (3) a linear alignment layer at the student encoder output to match feature dimensions. The overall training objective combines logit-adjustment supervised loss, CCKD loss, and feature imitation loss on both D_tr and D_mix with balancing coefficients α₁=1 and α₂=2.

## Results

On ImageNet-LT, ACCKD with an AF+LA teacher achieves 55.4% overall top-1 accuracy (61.1% Many, 53.4% Medium, 46.5% Few), improving over the logit-adjustment baseline (44.6%) by 10.8 points. On CIFAR-100-LT, ACCKD reaches 49.1% overall accuracy (64.6% Many, 50.7% Medium, 30.2% Few) versus 45.5% for the LA baseline. On iNaturalist 2018, ACCKD with AF+LA attains 66.7% overall accuracy, a 9.1-point gain over the LA baseline (57.6%). The paper reports an average accuracy improvement of 7.4% across all datasets compared to the strongest baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACCKD consistently outperforms all three baselines across every dataset and teacher model configuration. Compared to standard KD (Hinton et al., 2015), ACCKD gains 5.5 points on ImageNet-LT (55.4% vs. 49.9%) and 2.8 points on CIFAR-100-LT (49.1% vs. 46.3%) with the AF+LA teacher, with the largest gains on few-shot classes (46.5% vs. 32.6% on ImageNet-LT). Against DiVE (He et al., 2021), which flattens the teacher distribution via an extra temperature, ACCKD improves by 7.2 points on ImageNet-LT (55.4% vs. 48.2%) and 3.9 points on CIFAR-100-LT (49.1% vs. 45.2%), largely because DiVE's approach still distills p(y|x) rather than p(x|y). Versus the logit-adjustment baseline (Menon et al., 2021) without any teacher, ACCKD achieves gains of 10.8 points on ImageNet-LT and 3.6 points on CIFAR-100-LT, confirming that the pre-trained teacher's class-conditional knowledge is the primary source of improvement. Notably, unlike DiVE which sacrifices few-shot accuracy (10.8% on CIFAR-100-LT), ACCKD improves performance across all frequency groups simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) to the class-imbalanced learning setting, departing from prior KD-for-long-tail work (DiVE, He et al. 2021; Zhang et al. 2023) that trained teacher models from scratch on imbalanced data. The key conceptual departure is reframing the distillation target: rather than transferring the posterior p(y|x) as standard KD does, the authors argue the student should learn the teacher's class-conditional distribution p(x|y), which they show is the component critical for minority-class generalization. The method also builds on logit adjustment (Menon et al., 2021) for the supervised loss and repurposes Mixup/CutMix to construct a class-balanced synthetic dataset for additional distillation signal, while leveraging CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) as a frozen or lightly fine-tuned teacher.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical explanation for why standard knowledge distillation fails on imbalanced data (the p(y) mismatch corrupts the p(x|y) signal) and offers a principled fix that practitioners can adopt with minimal architectural changes. The 7.4% average accuracy gain across CIFAR-100-LT, ImageNet-LT, and iNaturalist 2018, with consistent improvements across many/medium/few-shot splits, makes this a practically relevant technique for long-tailed recognition. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-visual modalities or open-set recognition, how sensitive it is to the quality of the pre-trained teacher on highly fine-grained or out-of-distribution data (as seen with zero-shot CLIP on iNaturalist), and whether the class-balanced synthetic data construction can be replaced by more principled generative approaches.
