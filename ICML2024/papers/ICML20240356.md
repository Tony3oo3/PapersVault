## Problem

Calibration of probabilistic forecasts in regression has been extensively studied for univariate targets, but multi-dimensional regression models—common in model-based control and physical sciences—lack principled methods for assessing and correcting calibration of the joint predictive distribution. Calibrating each output dimension independently ignores inter-dimensional dependence, producing misleading assessments and suboptimal recalibrated samples.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained probabilistic model that outputs a predictive PDF (e.g., a diagonal Gaussian parameterized by mean and log-variance), a held-out calibration dataset of (x, y) pairs, and a test input x. It produces a monotonic recalibration mapping R: [0,1]→[0,1] learned on the calibration set, and at test time generates M recalibrated samples {ŷ_k} from the predictive distribution at x. The predictive distribution is represented entirely via samples rather than a closed-form density, and the recalibration operates in a scalar projected space Z defined by evaluating the predictive PDF at each sample.

## Method

The paper defines HDR calibration: a predictive PDF f̂_{Y|X} is HDR calibrated if the p-highest density region contains the true observation Y with probability p for all p∈(0,1), which is equivalent to calibration in the projected space where Z = f̂_{Y|X}(Y). The recalibration algorithm learns a monotonic mapping R in this projected space by matching the empirical CDF of projected calibration observations to the uniform distribution, then at test time draws M samples from f̂_{Y|x}, computes their projections ẑ_j = f̂_{Y|x}(ŷ_j), and re-samples from these tuples using binning to match the recalibrated distribution R∘F̂_{Z|x}. A pre-conditioning step adjusts the predictive PDF for mean bias, per-dimension standard deviation, and output-dimension correlation (estimated from the calibration set) before recalibration, ensuring the HDR level sets reflect the true dependence structure. This joint recalibration in the PDF-projected space naturally preserves inter-dimensional correlations, unlike per-dimension isotonic recalibration which renders dimensions independent.

## Results

Across all 5 benchmark regression datasets (scpf, rf1, rf2, scm1d, scm20d) and 3 nuclear fusion dynamics tasks (Fusion1–3), HDR recalibration achieves the best energy score in every case, e.g. −0.79 on scpf vs. 3.97 (pre-hoc) and 4.00 (SD recalibration), and −1.85 on Fusion1 vs. 1.95 and 1.93. HDR recalibration reduces HDR-ECE on 4/5 benchmark datasets and all 3 fusion tasks (e.g. 0.07 on scpf vs. 0.30 pre-hoc), while SD recalibration reduces SD-ECE on 4/5 benchmarks but fails to improve the energy score. In the demand-forecasting decision-making task, HDR recalibration lowers the accumulated loss to 865.34, a 5.6% improvement over pre-hoc (916.81) and 5.3% over SD recalibration (910.82).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is SD recalibration (Kuleshov et al., 2018), which applies isotonic recalibration independently to each output dimension. HDR recalibration dominates SD recalibration on the energy score across all 8 modeling tasks (e.g. −0.79 vs. 4.00 on scpf; −5.01 vs. 5.03 on Fusion3), indicating that jointly recalibrating the full multivariate distribution produces substantially better predictive samples than recalibrating marginals separately. On HDR-ECE, HDR recalibration achieves the best value on 4/5 benchmark datasets and 2/3 fusion tasks, while SD recalibration achieves the best SD-ECE on 4/5 benchmarks and 2/3 fusion tasks—consistent with each method optimizing its own calibration metric. The paper argues that SD-ECE is an inadequate summary of multi-dimensional miscalibration (as illustrated in Figure 1, where per-dimension calibration can appear perfect while the joint distribution is severely miscalibrated). In the downstream demand-forecasting decision task, HDR recalibration reduces accumulated loss by 5.6% over pre-hoc and 5.3% over SD recalibration, demonstrating practical benefit of joint calibration in a risk-sensitive setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the univariate calibration and recalibration framework of Kuleshov et al. (2018) to the multi-dimensional regression setting by introducing HDR calibration, which uses the predictive PDF as a projection function to define a joint calibration criterion. It builds on Hyndman's (1996) highest density region concept and connects it to highest predictive density (HPD) values, showing that HDR calibration is equivalent to calibration in the projected space. The approach departs from prior multi-dimensional calibration work (e.g., copula calibration via CDF projections) by choosing PDF-based projections, and from conformal prediction methods (Izbicki et al., 2022) by producing full predictive distributions rather than fixed-coverage prediction sets. The sampling-based recalibration algorithm generalizes the isotonic mapping of Kuleshov et al. to operate in the projected space and map samples back to the target space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: multi-dimensional probabilistic regression is ubiquitous in model-based control and physical sciences, yet calibration has been assessed and corrected per-dimension, which the authors convincingly show can mask significant joint-distribution miscalibration. The HDR calibration framework provides a principled, distribution-agnostic criterion for multi-dimensional calibration, and the sampling-based recalibration is applicable to any model that can produce samples and evaluate its PDF. Limitations include the PDF adjustment step being instantiated only for (diagonal) Gaussians, the requirement that the predictive distribution be sampleable and PDF-evaluable, and the reliance on a held-out calibration set. Open questions include whether other projection functions (beyond the predictive PDF) yield complementary or superior calibration notions, and how the method scales to very high-dimensional target spaces where PDF evaluation becomes expensive.
