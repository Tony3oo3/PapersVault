## Problem

Max pooling captures dominant set features but discards subtle distributional information, while average pooling preserves overall structure but dilutes salient elements; no single lightweight, parameter-free pooling operation balances both. Additionally, the Stacked Deep Sets architecture proposed by Zaheer et al. (2017) was never fully investigated for its scaling and expressiveness properties.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unordered sets of variable cardinality (32 elements for synthetic tasks, 300 for MoG, 1024 for point clouds) with no explicit spatial or relational structure imposed. Outputs are task-dependent: scalar statistics (mean, max, std, second-largest), per-element sorted indices, a single class label, or a log-likelihood value. For element-wise tasks like sorting, the network produces one prediction per set element; for set-level tasks, element-wise outputs are averaged to yield a single vector.

## Method

The paper introduces Relaxed Quantile Pooling, a permutation-invariant aggregation defined as the integral of the quantile function Q_X(p) weighted by a uniform density over [q−ε, q+ε], with default q=0.95 and ε=0.05. This smoothly interpolates between max pooling (ε→0, q→1) and average pooling (ε→0.5), and is implemented by partitioning the feature dimension into k=16 segments and computing per-segment quantiles via Hyndman–Fan interpolation. The pooling is embedded in a Stacked Deep Sets architecture where each residual layer computes g = P(Θ(h)), then updates h ← h + W[(h−g) ⊕ Θ_g(g)], isolating residual information from the pooled global feature. A theoretical result (Lemma 3.4) proves that for any ε>0, quantile-pooling networks can approximate max-pooling networks arbitrarily well, establishing universal expressiveness. The architecture scales linearly in depth (tested to 256 layers) and outperforms Set Transformer's learned PMA pooling on most tasks while remaining parameter-free at the pooling stage.

## Results

On ModelNet40 point cloud classification, Stacked Deep Sets (DSS) with quantile pooling achieves 90.71% ± 0.0022 accuracy, outperforming DSS with max pooling (90.30%), DSS with average pooling (89.97%), Deep Sets with quantile pooling (89.56%), and Set Transformer with PMA (88.53%). On the Mixture of Gaussians task, DSS with quantile pooling reaches a log-likelihood of −1.4937, second only to Set Transformer (−1.4867) and close to the oracle (−1.4778). Across the sample statistic regression tasks, quantile pooling ranks first on standard deviation and second-largest prediction, while max pooling ranks first on maximum prediction and average pooling ranks first on mean prediction within the DSS framework.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Deep Sets (Zaheer et al., 2017), the proposed Stacked Deep Sets with quantile pooling improves ModelNet40 accuracy from 89.56% to 90.71%, a gain of over 1.1 points, while also dominating on the sample statistic regression tasks (rank 1 on std and 2nd-max vs. rank 6–7 for DS+quantile). Against Set Transformer (Lee et al., 2019), DSS+quantile pooling wins on ModelNet40 (90.71% vs. 88.53%) and on the sorting clusters and Russian-dollable envelopes tasks, but loses on the Mixture of Gaussians task (−1.4937 vs. −1.4867 log-likelihood) and on mean regression, where PMA's attention mechanism excels at capturing the mean statistic. Against PointMLP (Ma et al., 2022), replacing its max pooling layers with quantile pooling yields a significant accuracy boost on ModelNet40, demonstrating that quantile pooling is a drop-in improvement for existing point cloud architectures. Against parameterized pooling methods (Equilibrium Pooling and PSWE), quantile pooling is 30× and 13× faster respectively while maintaining ~86% of their accuracy on sorting clusters, and surpasses both when additional layers are added to the DSS architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Deep Sets framework (Zaheer et al., 2017) by introducing a residual stacking mechanism that was mentioned but not fully explored in the original work, and by replacing the standard max/average pooling with a new Quantile Pooling operation. The theoretical grounding draws on PointNet's universal approximation theorem (Qi et al., 2017a) and Bueno & Hylton's analysis of pooling limitations, positioning quantile pooling as a measure-theoretic unification of max and average pooling. The paper explicitly contrasts with attention-based Set Transformer (Lee et al., 2019) and parameterized pooling methods (Equilibrium Aggregation, PSWE), arguing that a simple, parameter-free statistical operation can match or exceed their performance at a fraction of the computational cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Quantile pooling offers a drop-in, parameter-free replacement for max or average pooling that consistently improves performance across diverse set tasks while remaining as fast as the simpler alternatives, making it practically attractive for point cloud and set-structured data pipelines. The Stacked Deep Sets architecture demonstrates linear scaling to 256 layers, outperforming Set Transformer on most tasks, though Set Transformer retains an edge on mean estimation and Mixture of Gaussians. Open questions include whether the fixed q=0.95, ε=0.05 configuration generalizes to fundamentally different data regimes, whether the lack of learned quantile adaptability limits ceiling performance, and whether the approach extends to higher-order set interactions (e.g., Janossy-style) without losing its efficiency advantage.
