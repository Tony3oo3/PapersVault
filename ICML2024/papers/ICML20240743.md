## Problem

Dual propagation (DP), a single-phase local learning algorithm using dyadic neurons with oppositely nudged compartments, bridges the performance gap to backpropagation but lacks a rigorous theoretical foundation and its numerical stability is guaranteed only under symmetric nudging (α=1/2). This symmetry requirement is restrictive for biological and analog neuromorphic hardware where exact symmetry is hard to realize, and the role of asymmetric nudging in shaping learned representations (e.g., Lipschitz constants) remains unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB or grayscale images (MNIST/FashionMNIST at 28×28, CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet32x32 at 32×32) and produces class labels via a standard classification head. The unusual aspect is internal to the learning procedure: each neuron maintains two simultaneous internal states (s⁺ and s⁻) that are inferred in a single phase, with their weighted mean propagating upstream and their difference propagating downstream as the error signal, eliminating the need for temporally distinct inference phases required by CHL or EP.

## Method

The paper provides two complementary derivations of dual propagation. First, it shows the DP objective arises from repeated applications of two novel relaxations of the optimal value reformulation (SPROVR and AROVR) on deeply nested bilevel programs, revealing that the parameter α controls an adversarial perturbation step and that lower α values yield smaller Lipschitz constants. Second, it derives an improved variant DP^⊤ from a modified LeCun Lagrangian by reparametrizing states as s_k = αs_k⁺ + (1−α)s_k⁻ and multipliers as δ_k = s_k⁺ − s_k⁻, yielding adjoint-state-based fixed-point updates that are stable for any α ∈ [0,1]. When α = 1/2, DP^⊤ recovers the original DP dynamics exactly; for asymmetric nudging (e.g., α ∈ {0,1}), the modified updates avoid the divergence observed in original DP. Experiments on MLPs and VGG16 confirm that DP^⊤ trains successfully across all α values and that the adversarial interpretation of α has measurable effects on Lipschitz continuity and robustness.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with VGG16, DP^T (α=0, β=0.01) achieves 92.26% top-1 accuracy, matching BP (92.36%). On CIFAR-100, DP^T (α=0, β=0.01) reaches 69.33% top-1, slightly exceeding BP (69.1%). On ImageNet32x32, DP^T (α=1/2, β=0.01) achieves 41.59% top-1 and 65.63% top-5, on par with or slightly above BP. On MNIST, DP with α=0 yields a Lipschitz estimate of 15.87 (vs. 55.1 for BP), demonstrating that asymmetric nudging significantly improves adversarial robustness of the learned network.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is standard backpropagation (BP). On CIFAR-10 with VGG16, DP^T at β=0.01 matches BP within 0.1% top-1 accuracy (92.26% vs. 92.36%), while at β=0.1 with α=0 it slightly exceeds BP (92.41% vs. 92.36%). On CIFAR-100, DP^T (α=0, β=0.01) achieves 69.33% top-1, marginally above BP's 69.1%. The paper also compares against FGSM adversarial training on MNIST, showing that DP with α=0 (which implicitly regularizes hidden activations) achieves a Lipschitz estimate of 15.87, substantially lower than BP's 55.1, while maintaining comparable accuracy (98.43% vs. 98.43%). A key finding is that the original DP method diverges under asymmetric nudging (α∈{0,1}) with repeated inference iterations, whereas DP^T remains stable in all tested configurations, making it the more practical variant for neuromorphic or analog hardware where exact symmetry is unattainable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Dual Propagation (Høier et al., 2023), providing the missing theoretical foundation via bilevel optimization reformulations (optimal value relaxation) and revealing that the DP objective is an instance of both a saddle-point and an adversarial relaxed optimal value reformulation. The improved variant DP^T is derived from a modified version of LeCun's classic Lagrangian-based backpropagation derivation, connecting it to the adjoint state method. Conceptually, the work positions DP within the broader NGRAD family (CHL, EP, LPOM, lifted networks) while distinguishing it through its single-phase, dyadic-neuron architecture and compatibility with strong feedback.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The stability fix for asymmetric nudging is practically important for neuromorphic and analog hardware where exact symmetry is infeasible, and the revealed connection to adversarial robustness of hidden activations offers a new theoretical lens on why non-infinitesimal feedback parameters matter beyond mere gradient approximation. However, the weight transport problem remains unaddressed, the dyadic neuron model still deviates from known pyramidal neuron biology, and the computational cost of stabilized fixed-point iterations for α≠1/2 limits the method's efficiency advantage over the original DP. Open questions include whether the adversarial-robustness interpretation can be leveraged for certified robustness and whether the approach scales to architectures beyond VGG16.
