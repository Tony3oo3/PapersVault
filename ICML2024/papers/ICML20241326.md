## Problem

Mean-field variational inference for Bayesian Neural Networks suffers from a Monte Carlo sampling problem: most sampled weights fall in low-probability edge regions, and the resulting high variance in gradient estimates causes slow convergence or training failure, especially in deeper networks. This paper addresses the gap by replacing standard Monte Carlo sampling during feed-forward propagation with a lower-variance importance sampling scheme that is compatible with the reparameterization trick.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241326/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard classification images (MNIST 28×28 grayscale, CIFAR-10/100 32×32 RGB) and produces class predictions along with decomposed uncertainty estimates (aleatoric and epistemic) via predictive entropy. During training, the forward pass propagates first-moment estimates layer-by-layer using importance-weighted samples rather than raw Monte Carlo averages; during inference, stochastic forward passes from the variational posterior yield the predictive distribution and its uncertainty decomposition.

## Method

The paper derives the optimal importance sampling proposal distribution r*(w) ∝ |f(w)|q(w) for estimating the first moment of each layer's activation, then approximates it as a Gaussian whose mean and variance are computed via a second-order Taylor expansion of the non-linearity around the variational mean. The key technical contribution is a reparameterization of the proposal distribution: by drawing ε from N((μ*−μ)/σ², σ̂²/σ²) and setting w = μ + σ²⊙ε, the sampled weights follow the proposal distribution while the gradient with respect to variational parameters simplifies back to ∂w/∂μ = I and ∂w/∂σ = ε, identical to the standard reparameterization trick. This eliminates the complex chain-rule derivatives that would arise from sampling directly from the proposal, enabling efficient backpropagation. The importance weight γ ≈ 1/|f(w)| corrects for the distribution mismatch, and the entire procedure is applied layer-by-layer during the forward pass, reducing gradient variance without sacrificing the ability to model posterior uncertainty.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-20, RIS achieves 87.37±0.26% top-1 accuracy, outperforming all MFVI-based baselines (SWAG 86.80%, VOGN 85.36%, GLM 84.35%, Adversarial Sampling 86.33%, tempered MFVI 83.56%) and trailing only HMC (90.02%). Across multiple datasets, RIS consistently improves over standard Monte Carlo MFVI by 3.8–4.1 points (e.g., CIFAR-10 ResNet-20: 87.37% vs 83.56%; CIFAR-100 ResNet-56: 58.75% vs 54.62%). The method is also insensitive to the number of MC samples, maintaining 87.07% at 10 samples versus 87.74% at 100 samples, while tempered MFVI gains 2.6 points over the same range.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RIS outperforms all MFVI-variant baselines on CIFAR-10 ResNet-20: it beats SWAG (86.80%) by 0.57 points, Adversarial Sampling (86.33%) by 1.04 points, VOGN (85.36%) by 2.01 points, GLM (84.35%) by 3.02 points, and tempered MFVI (83.56%) by 3.81 points. The only method that surpasses RIS is HMC (90.02%), which the authors note requires substantially more computational resources. Against vanilla MFVI (22.46%), the improvement is dramatic, reflecting the severe optimization failure of standard MC sampling in deep networks. The gains are consistent across architectures and datasets: on CIFAR-100 ResNet-56, RIS reaches 58.75% versus 54.62% for MC MFVI (+4.13 points), and on MNIST LeNet it improves from 99.24% to 99.56%. The paper also shows that increasing MC samples from 10 to 100 narrows but does not close the gap to RIS (85.38% vs 87.74% for tempered MFVI vs RIS at 100 samples), indicating the advantage is structural rather than purely a sample-size effect.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MFVI framework for Bayesian Neural Networks established by Blundell et al. (2015) and the reparameterization trick of Kingma et al. (2015) by replacing direct Monte Carlo sampling from the variational posterior with importance sampling from an approximated optimal proposal distribution. It departs from Deterministic Variational Inference (Wu et al., 2019), which eliminates all stochasticity but sacrifices uncertainty modeling, by retaining a stochastic component while drastically reducing variance through a Taylor-linearized proposal. The approach also relates to adversarial sampling (Zhang et al., 2022) and cold posterior strategies (Wilson & Izmailov, 2020), which the authors still employ as a regularizer to prevent collapse to the prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RIS method offers a practical, drop-in replacement for Monte Carlo sampling in MFVI that reduces gradient variance without the full determinism of DVI, making it attractive for practitioners who need both stable training and calibrated uncertainty estimates. However, the method still relies on the cold posterior trick and is restricted to Gaussian approximating families, limiting its generality. Open questions include whether the Taylor approximation of the optimal proposal remains accurate for very deep or wide networks, whether the approach extends to non-Gaussian variational families, and whether the remaining gap to HMC (90.02 vs. 87.37 on CIFAR-10) can be closed without incurring MCMC-level computational cost.
