## Problem

All existing positive complexity results for exact SHAP score computation rely on the feature independence assumption, which is often unrealistic in practice. This paper introduces Markovian distributions as an intermediate class between fully independent and latent-variable models, and proves that computing the SHAP score for weighted automata, disjoint DNFs, and decision trees is tractable under this relaxed assumption—offering the first positive result that transcends feature independence.

## Input / Output

The method takes a weighted automaton (or equivalently a d-DNF/decision tree), a sequence w over a finite alphabet Σ, a feature position index i ∈ [|w|], and a Markovian distribution P specified by an initial distribution P_init and a sequence of stochastic transition matrices {P_i}. The distribution must be polynomial-time computable (transition matrices queryable in poly(n,|Σ|) time) and all entries must be strictly positive. The output is the exact SHAP score for feature i, computed in O(poly(size(A), |Σ|, |w|)) time. For the d-DNF/decision tree case, the input is a boolean formula over N variables and a Markovian distribution over {0,1}^N, which is sequentially mapped to the WA formulation.

## Method

The proof is constructive and proceeds by reformulating the SHAP score using a pattern formalism where coalitions are represented as sequences over an extended alphabet Σ_# = Σ ∪ {#}. The SHAP score is decomposed into SHAP₁ and SHAP₂ terms, each expressible as a composition of three language operators (Hadamard product, partition constant, and projection) over languages computed by WAs and WTs. The key technical challenge is constructing a WT that computes the conditional probability P(w' | w' ∈ L_p) via Bayes' rule, which requires a WA implementing 1/P(L_p). Under the Markovian assumption, this is achievable with a DWA of size O(|w|²|Σ|) whose states track only the current position, the last non-# position, and the last non-# symbol—exploiting the Markov property to compress the full history. For d-DNFs and decision trees, the result follows by polynomial-time reduction to SHAP(WA, MARKOV) via sequentialization of the boolean formula into a WA and the Markovian distribution into its sequential counterpart.

## Results

The paper establishes three main theoretical results: (1) Theorem 3.1 proves that computing the SHAP score for Weighted Automata under Markovian distributions is in FP, i.e., solvable in O(poly(size(A), |Σ|, |w|)) time; (2) Theorem 4.2 proves that SHAP for disjoint DNFs under Markovian distributions is in FP via a polynomial-time reduction to the WA case; (3) Corollary 4.3 extends this to decision trees, showing SHAP(DT, MARKOV) is in FP. These are the first positive complexity results for exact SHAP score computation that go beyond the feature independence assumption, which underpinned all prior tractability results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or quantitative comparisons. In terms of theoretical positioning: prior work by den Broeck et al. (2021) and Arenas et al. (2023) proved SHAP tractability for decision trees and decomposable deterministic circuits, but only under the feature independence assumption. The present paper relaxes this to Markovian distributions (a strictly broader class that captures feature correlations) and proves tractability for WAs, d-DNFs, and decision trees. The paper also notes that TreeSHAP (Lundberg et al., 2020) was later shown to compute interventional SHAP rather than the original SHAP score, and that under the relaxed (non-independent) assumption, computing SHAP for decision trees under naive Bayes or empirical distributions is #P-Hard (den Broeck et al., 2021). The Markovian assumption thus occupies a sweet spot: more realistic than independence, yet still tractable for the model families considered.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tractability results for SHAP score computation established by den Broeck et al. (2021) and Arenas et al. (2023), which were all derived under the feature independence assumption. It introduces Markovian distributions as an intermediate class between fully independent and fully correlated feature distributions, and proves that SHAP computation remains in FP for weighted automata, disjoint DNFs, and decision trees under this relaxed assumption. The proof strategy is novel in its use of formal language theory operators (product, partition constant, projection) over WAs/WTs, reducing the SHAP formula to a sequence of efficiently computable algebraic operations. The paper also clarifies the relationship between TreeSHAP and interventional SHAP, noting that prior polynomial-time algorithms for trees actually compute a different variant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first positive complexity result for exact SHAP computation that transcends the feature independence assumption, providing a principled and practically relevant relaxation that captures feature correlation while maintaining tractability. The constructive proofs yield concrete polynomial-time algorithms that extend TreeSHAP to the Markovian setting, with potential applications to sequential models (HMMs, RNNs) and tree-based classifiers where features exhibit local dependencies. Open questions include whether the result extends to higher-order Markovian distributions (n-gram models) and to broader model families such as Deterministic Decomposable Circuits, and whether the Markovian assumption is sufficient for the more complex correlation structures found in real-world tabular data.
