## Problem

No existing audio language model simultaneously combines strong audio understanding across diverse tasks, in-context few-shot learning with retrieval-augmented generation, and systematic multi-turn dialogue ability. Prior models either prepend audio tokens (quadratic complexity) or compute a single fused embedding (losing temporal information), and none support ICL-based adaptation to unseen tasks without fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241006/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts variable-length mono-channel audio (up to 33.25 seconds, processed as 7-second sliding windows with 5.25-second overlap, max 16 windows), a text instruction, and optionally k retrieved audio-text pairs for ICL/RAG. All tasks—captioning, question-answering, classification, and multi-turn dialogue—produce free-form text output, with classification options enumerated in the prompt so the model selects by generating the label text. For interleaved ICL samples, up to 8 retrieved examples are prepended before the target query.

## Method

Audio Flamingo conditions a 1.3B-parameter OPT-IML-MAX decoder-only LM on audio via gated cross-attention-dense layers (inherited from Flamingo), achieving linear complexity in the number of audio tokens rather than the quadratic cost of token prepending. The audio encoder is ClapCap with sliding windows to preserve temporal information, followed by 3 trainable self-attention transformation layers. Training uses two stages (pre-training then SFT) with a weighted mixture of losses across ~5.9M heterogeneous audio-text pairs. For ICL, the paper introduces block upper-triangular cross-attention masks so each output is conditioned only on preceding audio inputs, and computes loss over all outputs in an interleaved sequence (not just the last), improving robustness to varying numbers of in-context examples. ICL training data is constructed by retrieving top-k similar samples via LAION-CLAP embeddings with Faiss, and two GPT-4-generated multi-turn dialogue datasets (AF-Dialogue-AudioSetSL, AF-Dialogue-MusicCaps) enable strong dialogue ability.

## Results

On in-distribution benchmarks, Audio Flamingo (2.2B params) achieves 86.9% accuracy on ClothoAQA-unanimous (vs. 74.9% previous SOTA), 0.465 CIDEr on Clotho-v2 captioning, and 66.7% F1 on NSynth quality classification (vs. 46.3% previous SOTA). On zero-shot benchmarks, it reaches 0.502 CIDEr on AudioCaps (vs. 0.281 previous SOTA) and 92.7% accuracy on Medley-solos-DB (vs. 61.3% previous SOTA). With ICL-based RAG (8-shot), it gains an average >10% absolute improvement over zero-shot on classification tasks (e.g., US8K: 75.0% → 94.7%). On multi-turn dialogue, the chat model achieves 1.622 CIDEr on AF-Dialogue-AudioSetSL, nearly 2× the best baseline (LTU† at 0.823).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qwen-Audio (Chu et al., 8.3B params), Audio Flamingo (2.2B) wins on ClothoAQA-unanimous by 12.0 points (86.9% vs. 74.9%), ClothoAQA-non-binary by 20.4 points, and Clotho-v2 captioning (0.465 vs. 0.441 CIDEr), but slightly trails on MusicAVQA (71.6% vs. 72.1%) and NSynth instrument (77.1% vs. 78.8%). Against Pengi (Deshmukh et al., 308M), Audio Flamingo dominates on open-ended tasks (ClothoAQA non-binary: 49.5% vs. 29.1%; NS quality: 66.7% vs. 46.3%) but loses on CochlScene (83.0% vs. 91.6%). On zero-shot generalization, Audio Flamingo substantially outperforms Salewski et al. on AudioCaps (0.502 vs. 0.281 CIDEr) and Deshmukh et al. on Medley-solos-DB (92.7% vs. 61.3%), though it trails Han et al. on GTZAN (67.9% vs. 71.0%) and Elizalde et al. on RAVDESS (20.9% vs. 21.7%). For retrieval-augmented captioning, Audio Flamingo (0.518 CIDEr at 4-shot) significantly outperforms RECAP (Ghosh et al., 0.359 at 4-shot). On multi-turn dialogue, the Audio Flamingo chat model nearly doubles LTU (Gong et al.) on AF-Dialogue-AudioSetSL (1.622 vs. 0.823 CIDEr) and nearly doubles MU-LLaMA (Liu et al.) on AF-Dialogue-MusicCaps (1.143 vs. 0.585 CIDEr), while also outperforming Qwen-Audio (0.507 CIDEr) on the AudioSetSL test set.

## Relation to Prior Work

Audio Flamingo directly ports the gated cross-attention conditioning mechanism from Flamingo (Alayrac et al., 2022) to the audio domain, replacing the quadratic-complexity token-prepending approach used by Pengi, LTU, Qwen-Audio, and SALMONN with a linear-complexity alternative. For audio feature extraction, it builds on ClapCap (Elizalde et al., 2023b) but introduces sliding windows to preserve temporal information that single-vector CLAP embeddings lose. The paper departs from prior audio LMs in three key ways: (1) it is the first to combine in-context learning with retrieval-augmented generation for audio, providing both retrieved audio and text (unlike RECAP which only prepends captions), (2) it uses a two-stage pre-training + SFT curriculum rather than the close-then-open-ended task ordering used by LTU and SALMONN, and (3) it introduces GPT-4-generated multi-turn dialogue datasets (AF-Dialogue-AudioSetSL, AF-Dialogue-MusicCaps) that no prior audio LM had.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Audio Flamingo demonstrates that a 2.2B-parameter model can match or exceed 7B–13B audio LMs across captioning, QA, and classification benchmarks, suggesting that architectural choices (cross-attention conditioning, sliding-window features, instruction-tuned LM backbone) matter more than raw parameter count in this regime. The ICL+RAG capability opens a practical path for deploying audio understanding on truly novel sound categories without task-specific fine-tuning, and the multi-turn dialogue results (CIDEr 1.6 vs. 0.5–0.8 for baselines) indicate that audio chat is now a viable product direction. Key limitations remain: the 1.3B OPT-IML backbone caps reasoning depth, the model handles no speech transcription or audio generation, and the 33-second maximum audio length constrains long-form understanding. Open questions include whether scaling to 7B+ LMs preserves the parameter-efficiency advantage and whether the cross-attention approach generalizes to interleaved audio-video-text inputs.
