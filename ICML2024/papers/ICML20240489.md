## Problem

MCMC methods have not benefited from deep learning advances, largely because defining a differentiable objective that simultaneously encourages high-quality samples and good exploration of the state space is difficult. This paper asks how one can learn a neural-network-parameterized Markov transition kernel that efficiently samples from a given unnormalized target density, without requiring the full density (only ratios) and without hand-tuned acceptance criteria.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240489/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a target unnormalized density p(x) (accessed only through log-density ratios) and a set of initial samples obtained via a bootstrap process from a possibly slow-mixing kernel. The state space is augmented with auxiliary Gaussian noise v, so the chain operates in R^{2n}. The output is a trained parametric involutive transition kernel M_θ (a composition of Hénon maps) and a C2-equivariant discriminator D_φ, which together define a Metropolis-Hastings sampler that can be run to produce samples from the target distribution.

## Method

The deterministic proposal is parameterized as an involution M_θ = R ∘ L_θ, where L_θ is an R-reversible diffeomorphism decomposed as R ∘ g_θ^{-1} ∘ R ∘ g_θ (with g_θ a composition of parametric Hénon maps), guaranteeing M_θ ∘ M_θ = id and hence detailed balance by construction. Training uses an adversarial objective derived as a Pinsker-inequality upper bound on the total variation distance between the target and the one-step chain distribution: the proposal M_θ is trained to fool a discriminator D_φ, while D_φ is trained to approximate the log-density ratio log[p(RL_θ(x))/p(x)] + log J. A key theoretical result shows the optimal discriminator is C2-equivariant under the involution, which is enforced by construction via equivariant network architectures (product or composition parameterizations). A bootstrap loop alternates between generating samples with the current kernel and updating both M_θ and D_φ via stochastic gradient descent, with the Barker test used during training and the standard min(1,x) acceptance during sampling.

## Results

On the four 2D synthetic densities, Ai-sampler achieves perfect ESS (1000/1000) on the multimodal mog2 and mog6 distributions, where HMC collapses to ESS of 0.8 and 2.4 respectively, and A-NICE-MC reaches 355.4 and 320.0. On the ring5 density, Ai-sampler (ESS 396.5) outperforms both HMC (256.6) and A-NICE-MC (155.6). For Bayesian logistic regression on real datasets, Ai-sampler matches HMC's perfect ESS of 5000 on Heart and German, and surpasses both baselines on Australian (1746.5 vs. 1113.4 for HMC and 1015.8 for A-NICE-MC). In terms of sampling speed (ESS/s), Ai-sampler achieves ~1050 ESS/s on mog2 and mog6 versus 0.4 and 0.98 for HMC, a roughly 1000× speedup on multimodal targets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HMC, Ai-sampler achieves a dramatic ~1250× improvement in ESS on mog2 (1000 vs. 0.8) and ~417× on mog6 (1000 vs. 2.4), where high-density barriers between modes prevent Hamiltonian dynamics from mixing. However, HMC retains an advantage on the smooth single-mode ring density (ESS 981.3 vs. 378.0), where gradient-based proposals are naturally well-suited. Against A-NICE-MC (Song et al., 2017), Ai-sampler wins on mog2 (1000 vs. 355.4), mog6 (1000 vs. 320.0), and ring5 (396.5 vs. 155.6), but loses on ring (378.0 vs. 1000.0). On the Bayesian logistic regression tasks, Ai-sampler matches HMC's perfect ESS on Heart and German (both 5000) and outperforms both baselines on Australian (1746.5 vs. 1113.4 HMC, 1015.8 A-NICE-MC). In wall-clock efficiency (ESS/s), Ai-sampler is roughly 1000× faster than HMC on multimodal 2D densities and 2–10× faster on the Bayesian posteriors, benefiting from not requiring repeated gradient evaluations of the target density.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: the involutive MCMC framework of Neklyudov et al. (2020), which provides the deterministic transition kernel structure; time-reversible neural networks from Valperga et al. (2022), which supply a parameterization that guarantees involutivity by construction (eliminating the auxiliary random variable used by Song et al.); and the adversarial training paradigm for MCMC introduced by A-NICE-MC (Song et al., 2017). The key departure from A-NICE-MC is twofold: the proposal is reversible by construction rather than via a random coin flip, and the adversarial objective is derived as a Pinsker-inequality upper bound on total variation distance rather than an informal GAN-like Jensen-Shannon divergence. Additionally, the paper introduces a C2-equivariance constraint on the discriminator that follows from the involutive structure, restricting the discriminator's hypothesis space in a way not present in prior adversarial MCMC work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a theoretically grounded adversarial objective for learning MCMC kernels, with the C2-equivariance result providing a principled architectural constraint that prior work lacked. Practically, the learned kernel achieves perfect effective sample size on multimodal 2D densities where HMC is essentially stuck, and matches or exceeds HMC on Bayesian logistic regression posteriors without requiring gradient evaluations of the target density. However, the method is not uniformly superior (it underperforms HMC on the ring distribution), the bootstrap process requires initial samples from a potentially slow-mixing chain, and experiments are limited to low-dimensional settings. Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-dimensional posteriors, whether the adversarial training is stable without careful learning-rate scheduling, and how the method compares to more recent flow-based or diffusion-based samplers.
