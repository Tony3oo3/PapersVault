## Problem

Existing prompt-based continual learning methods are locked into a fixed prompting strategy: universal prompting (one shared prompt) handles uniformly mild semantic shifts, while specific prompting (one prompt per task) handles uniformly abrupt shifts. Real-world CL streams, however, mix mild and abrupt shifts, causing universal prompting to under-allocate prompts and specific prompting to over-allocate them, both yielding sub-optimal trade-offs between knowledge transfer and forgetting prevention.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (e.g., 32×32 CIFAR-100, 224×224 VTAB/ImageNet-R) that are tokenized and prepended with a learned prompt vector (p tokens of dimension d) at the first five ViT transformer layers. At inference, the [CLS] token of the backbone is matched against stored prompt keys via a distance function to select the appropriate semantic-group prompt, and the classifier head outputs a class label. The number of active prompts is not fixed a priori but adapts dynamically from 1 to T depending on observed semantic shifts.

## Method

AdaPromptCL introduces an assign-and-refine semantic grouping mechanism that dynamically manages a variable number of prompts. In the assignment step, each incoming task's semantic representation (extracted by average-pooling and L2-normalizing a warm-up prompt) is placed into an existing group if within threshold R of the group centroid, or a new group is created; simultaneously, neighboring task sets (threshold γR) are clustered via k-means to identify prospective groups, and prompts for those groups are proactively trained and stored. In the refinement step, simulation-based clustering permutes task arrival orders within each neighboring set to find orderings that reduce the total group count, and when a reduction is found, the refined groups retrieve their pre-trained prompts from the prospective repository. Training jointly optimizes prompt-key pairs for all prospective groups containing the current task using cross-entropy plus a key-matching loss, enabling the system to flexibly produce as few as 1 prompt (mild shifts) or as many as T prompts (abrupt shifts) within a single model.

## Results

AdaPromptCL achieves the highest last accuracy across all 10 evaluation datasets spanning three semantic shift scenarios. In the varying shift scenarios (the paper's primary focus), it reaches 56.40% on VTAB-Rec2, 56.86% on VTAB-Rec5, and 54.22% on VTAB-Rec10, outperforming the best baseline by 3.1–6.5 percentage points. In uniformly abrupt scenarios, it achieves 32.39% on VTAB-19T and 38.67% on VTAB-5T, with average improvements of 14.00% and 10.02% over the five baselines. In uniformly mild scenarios, it matches or slightly exceeds the best universal-prompting baselines (69.45% on ImageNet-R, 85.31% on CIFAR-100), confirming its adaptability across the full spectrum of semantic shifts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaPromptCL outperforms all five baselines (L2P, VPT, LAE, DP, S-Prompts) on every dataset and every shift scenario, with no losses or ties. In the varying shift scenarios, it achieves an average improvement of 6.35% over LAE (the strongest universal-prompting method) and 6.91% over S-Prompts (the strongest specific-prompting method), with the largest gap on VTAB-Rec10 where S-Prompts degrades to 47.72% while AdaPromptCL maintains 54.22%. In uniformly abrupt scenarios, the advantage is most pronounced: AdaPromptCL surpasses the best baseline (S-Prompts at 30.83% on VTAB-19T) by 1.56 points and the average of all baselines by 14.00%. In uniformly mild scenarios, AdaPromptCL is competitive with the best universal methods (LAE at 70.11% on ImageNet-R vs. AdaPromptCL's 69.45%), showing it does not sacrifice performance where a single prompt suffices. The paper also demonstrates that AdaPromptCL's advantage grows over the CL stream, reaching a 13.62% gap over S-Prompts on VTAB-Rec10 while using only 12.40% of the prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself between the two established extremes of rehearsal-free prompt-based continual learning: universal prompting (L2P, VPT, LAE) which maintains a fixed prompt set for all tasks, and specific prompting (S-Prompts, DualPrompt) which trains a dedicated prompt per task. It draws on online clustering (BIRCH-style assignment) and k-means-based prospective group identification from the multi-task learning semantic grouping literature, but adapts these to the sequential, rehearsal-free CL setting where future tasks are unknown. The key departure from prior work is the assign-and-refine mechanism that dynamically adjusts the number of prompts based on observed task semantics, rather than committing to a fixed strategy a priori.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evaluation of prompt-based CL methods across a spectrum of semantic shifts (uniformly mild, uniformly abrupt, and varying), introducing new benchmarks (VTAB-SimS, VTAB-RecR) that better reflect real-world task sequences. The finding that AdaPromptCL naturally converges to 1 prompt for mild shifts and ~18 for abrupt shifts—matching the optimal strategies for each regime—validates the adaptive framing and suggests a unified framework can replace the need to manually select between universal and specific prompting. Open questions include scalability to very long task sequences (beyond 19 tasks), the sensitivity of the threshold R to domain-specific semantics, and whether the warm-up overhead becomes prohibitive in latency-critical deployment settings.
