## Problem

In unsupervised domain adaptation, distribution shifts cause standard calibration methods to produce over-confident predictions and invalidate cross-validation-based model selection. Existing importance-weighted approaches focus on accurately estimating the importance weight itself, but it remains unclear how IW estimation error propagates to the final calibration or selection error. This paper asks whether one can directly minimize the group accuracy estimation error during the IW estimation process, thereby guaranteeing accurate calibration and selection in the target domain.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained UDA classifier (MDD, CDAN, or MCD with ResNet-50/101 backbone), a labeled source validation set, and an unlabeled target validation set. It outputs per-sample calibrated confidences (the group accuracy estimate for the group each sample belongs to) and a scalar model selection score (the mean group accuracy estimate over the target set). Internally, the optimization produces 2B binned importance weight vectors (B=10 by default) constrained within Clopper-Pearson confidence intervals, which are then used to compute M=10 group accuracy estimates.

## Method

IW-GAE partitions predictions into M confidence groups (using temperature-adjusted softmax maxima) and defines two estimators of the source group accuracy: a Monte-Carlo estimator from labeled source data and an IW-based estimator that depends on the importance weights. The core optimization finds binned IWs that minimize the squared discrepancy between these two estimators, subject to CI box constraints, source-target IW agreement constraints, and probability-matching constraints derived from the true IW's moment conditions. A nested structure solves the inner convex problem (via sequential least squares) for each temperature in a discrete set, then selects the temperature minimizing total group accuracy estimation error. Proposition 3.1 provides a theoretical guarantee that the optimization error upper-bounds the source group accuracy estimation error, which in turn upper-bounds the target group accuracy estimation error via a factor involving the IW supremum and the group probability ratio.

## Results

On the model calibration task, IW-GAE achieves mean ECE of 8.93 on OfficeHome (MDD), 6.32 on DomainNet (CDAN), and 14.70 on VisDA-2017 (MDD), outperforming the best baselines by 53%, 31%, and 21% respectively. On the model selection task, IW-GAE achieves 68.48% mean test accuracy for checkpoint selection and 67.95% for hyperparameter selection on OfficeHome, improving over the best baselines by 9% and 18% on a relative scale between lower and upper bounds. Overall, IW-GAE improves state-of-the-art by 22% in model calibration and 14% in model selection across all benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IW-GAE consistently outperforms all nine baselines across both tasks. In model calibration, it beats the strongest IW-based baseline TransCal (Wang et al., 2020) by 11.91 ECE points on OfficeHome (8.93 vs. 20.84) and 19.07 on DomainNet (6.32 vs. 25.39), and surpasses CPCS (Park et al., 2020) by 10.86 on OfficeHome. Temperature scaling (Guo et al., 2017) and IW-TS are significantly worse, with mean ECEs of 24.01 and 23.26 on OfficeHome respectively. Post-hoc methods PTS (Tomani et al., 2022) and AvUTS (Krishnan & Tickoo, 2020) also underperform IW-GAE (21.91 and 28.17 vs. 8.93 on OfficeHome), confirming the advantage of explicitly modeling distribution shift. In model selection, IW-GAE edges out target-only methods InfoMax (Shi & Sha, 2012) and TransScore (Yang et al., 2024) by 0.10 points in checkpoint selection (68.48 vs. 68.38) and 0.08 in hyperparameter selection (67.95 vs. 67.87), while substantially outperforming IW-based baselines IWCV (Sugiyama et al., 2007) and DEV (You et al., 2019) by 1.00 and 1.09 points respectively in checkpoint selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the importance-weighted calibration framework of Wang et al. (2020, TransCal) and the IW-based model selection of Sugiyama et al. (2007, IWCV), but departs from their shared strategy of first accurately estimating the importance weight and then plugging it into a calibration or selection formula. Instead, IW-GAE reframes both tasks as estimating group-level accuracy and formulates a nested optimization that directly minimizes the group accuracy estimation error, using the CI-based binned IW estimator of Park et al. (2022) only as a constraint rather than as the final weight. The group accuracy perspective, motivated by a bias-variance argument (Proposition 2.1), is the key conceptual departure: it unifies calibration (per-group confidence) and model selection (mean group accuracy) under a single estimation target with bounded error guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 22% average ECE reduction and 14% model-selection accuracy gain are large and consistent across three datasets and three UDA base models, and the theoretical bound (Proposition 3.1) links the optimization objective to the actual target-domain estimation error. A notable limitation is that all IW-based methods, including IW-GAE, fail to improve over vanilla calibration in i.i.d. settings with pre-trained LLMs, raising the open question of whether IW-based approaches are only beneficial under substantial distribution shift. The paper also leaves open whether the optimization-based IW selection can be made to work without the CI estimator in the constraints, which would broaden applicability to settings where nonparametric CI construction is infeasible.
