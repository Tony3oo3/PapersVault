## Problem

Existing 2-WL-based universality results for point cloud networks are restricted to invariant functions, leaving the fully equivariant case (jointly permutation-, rotation-, and translation-equivariant) unaddressed. Additionally, prior proofs that PPGN simulates 2-WL only guarantee pairwise separation with exponentially growing parameters, and no complete isomorphism test existed for position-velocity pairs commonly encountered in physical simulation tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240754/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a position point cloud X ∈ R^{3×n} and a velocity point cloud V ∈ R^{3×n}, along with optional node features (e.g., atomic numbers, charges) and edge features (e.g., magnetic attraction), and produces updated position and velocity point clouds (X^out, V^out) that are equivariant to permutations, rotations, and translations. The input is consumed by encoding (X,V) into a complete weighted graph whose edge weights are 4×4 pairwise distance matrices of centralized positions and velocities, making the representation invariant to rotation and translation before any network processing. For N-body dynamics the output is predicted particle positions after 1000 timesteps; for conformation generation it is a 3D molecular geometry obtained by iteratively following predicted gradient fields.

## Method

WeLNet encodes (X,V) as a weighted graph, applies PPGN (a 2-WL-simulating architecture using matrix-multiplication-based aggregation with analytic non-polynomial activations like softplus), and then performs an equivariant pooling step that combines the resulting pairwise features with the input positions and velocities via six MLPs to produce equivariant outputs. The key theoretical contribution is proving that PPGN with analytic activations can uniformly separate all 2-WL-distinguishable graphs in a continuous family using only 2d+1 features per edge (where d is the intrinsic dimension), reducing complexity from exponential to O(n^ω) — and that for position-velocity pairs, three iterations of 2-WL on the 4×4 distance-matrix graph are complete. The equivariant pooling mechanism (Eqs. 8–9) is shown to yield a universal approximator for all continuous equivariant functions, with completeness guaranteed for Lebesgue-almost-every parameter choice at T=5 iterations and Δ=12n+1 feature dimension. In practice, WeLNet uses 2 PPGN iterations and 4 convolution layers with scaled softplus activations, combining the 2-WL color refinement with an EGNN-style message-passing layer.

## Results

On the N-Body dynamics prediction task, WeLNet achieves a test MSE of 0.0036±0.0002, setting a new state-of-the-art that surpasses the previous best (Transformer-PS, 0.0040±0.00001) by ~10%. On the GEOM-QM9 molecular conformation generation task, WeLNet achieves a MAT score of 0.1614 Å (mean), improving over the previous SOTA UniMol (0.1907 Å) by more than 15%, though it trails UniMol on the COV metric (92.66% vs. 97.00%). On the N-Body task with gravitational external forces, WeLNet achieves MSE 0.0054±0.0001, outperforming MC-EGNN (0.0073) and ClofNet (0.0082).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WeLNet outperforms all 12 baselines on the standard N-Body dynamics task, beating the previous SOTA Transformer-PS (Kim et al., 2024) by ~10% in MSE (0.0036 vs. 0.0040) and the next-best MC-EGNN-2 (Levy et al., 2023) by ~12%. With gravitational external forces, WeLNet (0.0054) significantly outperforms MC-EGNN (0.0073) and ClofNet (Du et al., 2022; 0.0082), while on the Lorentz-like force it marginally beats MC-EGNN (0.0238 vs. 0.0240). On GEOM-QM9 conformation generation, WeLNet achieves the best MAT score (0.1614 Å) among all 13 compared methods, improving over UniMol (Zhou et al., 2023; 0.1907 Å) by over 15%, but it underperforms UniMol on the COV metric (92.66% vs. 97.00%), indicating that while WeLNet generates more accurate conformations on average, it covers fewer of the reference conformations. The separation experiment on the EXP dataset further validates the theory: analytic activations (Softplus, LeakyELU) separate all 600 2-WL-distinguishable graph pairs with a single neuron per edge, while ReLU fails to separate all pairs at that width.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PPGN architecture (Maron et al., 2019a) and the 2-WL completeness results for point clouds (Delle Rose et al., 2023; Hordan et al., 2024) in three directions: proving uniform 2-WL simulation with exponentially lower complexity than prior pairwise guarantees, extending completeness from positions-only to position-velocity pairs, and establishing fully equivariant universality (permutation + rotation + translation) where prior results covered only invariant or partially equivariant functions. The WeLNet architecture combines PPGN's 2-WL color refinement with EGNN-style message passing, and the uniform separation proof leverages the finite witness theorem from Amir et al. (2023). The key conceptual departure from prior work is showing that fixed-parameter PPGN networks with analytic non-polynomial activations can uniformly separate all 2-WL-distinguishable graphs in a continuous family, rather than requiring different parameters per graph pair.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The equivariant universality result fills a genuine gap in the theoretical landscape: prior work characterized invariant or partially equivariant functions but left the fully equivariant case (jointly permutation-, rotation-, and translation-equivariant) unaddressed. The complexity reduction from exponential to O(n^ω) makes 2-WL-based architectures practically viable, and the position-velocity extension directly addresses the input format of physical simulation tasks. However, the theoretical guarantees require complete distance matrices (no sparsification) and specific hyperparameters (T=5, Δ=12n+1) that differ from the practical configuration used (T=2, 4 convolutions), leaving open whether sparsified variants can maintain both completeness and competitive performance. The finding that a single neuron per edge suffices for analytic activations is a useful practical guideline but has limited impact on architectures already using wider feature dimensions.
