## Problem

Existing knowledge distillation methods transfer knowledge through point-to-point feature matching or pairwise/triple-wise relational structures, which capture only fragmented local information about the latent space. No prior work has exploited the global topology of the entire embedding feature set—encompassing distribution shape, multiscale structure, and connectivity—as distillation knowledge, leaving a gap in how comprehensively the teacher's latent geometry can be conveyed to the student.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K) and outputs class labels, but the training signal is computed at the batch level: a minibatch of m embedding feature vectors (treated as a point cloud in R^{h×w×c} or R^c after pooling) is fed through a Rips complex to produce a persistence image. The batch size acts as a critical hyperparameter—topology distillation only helps when m ≥ 16, since fewer points yield noisy or uninformative persistence diagrams. The student's embedding features are projected to match the teacher's channel dimension via a 1×1 convolution before PI computation.

## Method

TopKD defines global topology knowledge as the persistence diagram (PD) of a batch of embedding features, capturing the birth and death of topological features (connected components, loops) across the Rips filtration. Since exact PD computation is intractable per training step, the authors train a RipsNet to approximate persistence images (PIs) from point clouds, then freeze it during student training. The topology distillation loss is the L2 distance between the approximated PIs of the teacher and student embedding features, added to the standard cross-entropy and vanilla KD (KL-divergence on soft logits) losses. The two-stage procedure first trains RipsNet on 200K point clouds from the teacher's stage-4 features (using Gudhi for exact PIs), then uses the frozen RipsNet to compute PIs on-the-fly for both networks during student optimization. Ablations show that 0-dimensional PDs (connected components) outperform 1-dimensional or combined PDs, and the method is robust to which network stage's features are matched.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (ResNet34→ResNet18), TopKD achieves 73.60% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the teacher (73.31%) and all 14 baselines, with the closest competitor NORM at 72.14% (a 1.46% gap). On CIFAR-100 (ResNet56→ResNet20), TopKD reaches 71.58% top-1, outperforming vanilla KD (70.66%) and CRD (71.16%), though trailing CRCD (72.33%). On the heterogeneous ImageNet-1K setting (ResNet50→MobileNetV2), TopKD attains 76.80% top-1, again exceeding the teacher (76.16%) and the next-best method NORM (74.26%) by 2.54%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopKD outperforms all 14 baselines on ImageNet-1K in both homogeneous (ResNet34→ResNet18) and heterogeneous (ResNet50→MobileNetV2) settings, achieving the best top-1 accuracy in both cases and even surpassing the teacher model. On CIFAR-100 with same-architecture pairs, TopKD (71.58%) beats logit-based KD (70.66%), feature-based OFD (70.98%), and relation-based CRD (71.16%), but is outperformed by the contrastive method CRCD (72.33%). The topology-based approach shows its strongest advantage over relation-based methods that only capture pairwise or triple-wise structure (RKD 71.34%, CC 69.63%, SP 69.67%), confirming that global topological knowledge provides a more comprehensive distillation signal. On the heterogeneous CIFAR-100 setting (Table 2), TopKD leads in 4 out of 6 teacher-student pairs, with the largest margin over KD being +1.48% (ResNet32×4→ShuffleNetV2: 76.33% vs 74.45%). The ablation in Table 6 confirms that the topology loss and KD loss are synergistic: using both together (71.58%) substantially outperforms either alone (70.66% for KD, 69.72% for Top-only).

## Relation to Prior Work

TopKD extends the knowledge distillation framework by introducing a topology-based loss that captures the global structure of all embedding features simultaneously, departing from prior methods that rely on point-to-point matching (FitNet), pairwise similarity (CC, SP, RKD), or triple-wise geometric relations. It builds directly on the persistence image vectorization of Adams et al. (2017) and the RipsNet architecture of de Surrel et al. (2022) to make persistent homology computationally tractable within a training loop. The key conceptual departure is that while prior work (Du et al. 2022, Jeon et al. 2024) used persistent homology only as a preprocessing step on raw input data, TopKD applies it directly to intermediate latent-space features, treating the persistence diagram as the distillation target itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first study to use topological data analysis on latent-space features as distillation knowledge, opening a new axis of KD design beyond pairwise or pointwise relations. The ablation showing that the topology loss and KD loss create a beneficial synergy (71.58 vs. 70.66 and 69.72 individually) suggests that global structural constraints complement local logit matching in a non-trivial way. However, the Wasserstein distance analysis reveals that TopKD does not consistently reduce the actual PD distance between teacher and student, and the approximation errors for student networks remain an order of magnitude larger than for the teacher, indicating the RipsNet approximation is a meaningful bottleneck. Future work on directly matching PDs, incorporating higher-dimensional homology, or using exact PIs could close this gap.
