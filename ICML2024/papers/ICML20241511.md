## Problem

Estimating individual treatment effects from observational data is fundamentally challenging because each individual's outcome is only observed under one treatment, yet the goal requires inferring the difference under two treatments. Existing approaches either train separate outcome models per treatment (factual loss) or impute pseudo-outcomes for missing treatments, but the factual loss is misaligned with ITE risk under confounding, and pseudo-outcome methods inherit errors from the imputation step.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a tabular dataset of (covariate vector x, treatment t, observed outcome y) triples and produces an ITE estimate τ̂(x, t, t′) = μ̂(x, t) − μ̂(x, t′) for any pair of treatments. A key operational detail is that during training, pairs of instances are dynamically constructed by finding neighbors in a learned embedding space ψ(x) that have contrasting treatments, with a distance threshold δ_pair and a per-instance pair count num_z′ controlling pair quality. The approach is model-agnostic and applies to both binary (T ∈ {0,1}) and continuous (T ∈ [0,1]) treatments.

## Method

PairNet replaces the standard factual loss with a Pair loss that minimizes the squared difference between observed outcome differences of paired instances and predicted outcome differences: ((y_i − y_i′) − (μ(φ(x_i), t_i) − μ(φ(x_i′), t_i′)))². The model uses a shared representation network φ and treatment-specific prediction heads μ_t, so the architecture is identical to TARNet/CFRNet-style models. Expanding the Pair loss reveals it decomposes into two factual losses plus a residual alignment term that enforces positive correlation among prediction errors for nearby covariates with contrasting treatments — an inductive bias directly aligned with ITE estimation. Theoretically, the ITE risk is upper-bounded by the Pair loss plus an IPM distance between the observed covariate distribution p_t and the induced neighbor distribution q_t, which is provably tighter than the factual model's bound involving IPM(p_0, p_1), and PairNet is shown to be a consistent estimator of ITE under strict overlap.

## Results

On binary treatment benchmarks, PairNet achieves PEHE out error of 0.69 on IHDP (vs. 0.83 for the best baseline TARNet/DragonNet) and 2.46 on ACIC (vs. 2.57 for IPW), with all improvements statistically significant (p < 0.05). On the Twins dataset, all methods converge to ~0.32 PEHE out due to the binary outcome structure. For continuous treatments, PairNet applied to DRNet reduces PEHE out from 2.45 to 2.27 on IHDP and from 0.34 to 0.25 on TCGA-0, while PairNet on VCNet improves IHDP from 1.73 to 1.57.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PairNet outperforms all 11 binary-treatment baselines on IHDP (PEHE out 0.69 vs. 0.83 for TARNet/DragonNet) and ACIC (2.46 vs. 2.57 for IPW), with statistically significant gains (p < 0.05) over every method. Meta-learners (T-Learner, R-Learner, DR-Learner, X-Learner) perform worst due to error propagation from their two-stage pseudo-outcome imputation, with R-Learner reaching 3.24 PEHE out on IHDP. Representation learners (TARNet, CFRNet, FlexTENet) are the strongest baselines but still trail PairNet by 0.14–0.21 PEHE out on IHDP, attributed to their lack of residual-alignment inductive bias. Matching methods (kNN, Perfect Match) and weighting methods (IPW, DragonNet) are competitive on IHDP but degrade on ACIC. For continuous treatments, PairNet applied to DRNet consistently beats the factual-loss DRNet across all five datasets (e.g., 2.27 vs. 2.45 on IHDP, 0.25 vs. 0.34 on TCGA-0), while PairNet on VCNet wins on IHDP (1.57 vs. 1.73) but ties or slightly trails on TCGA and News where VCNet's strong smoothness prior saturates performance with sufficient data.

## Relation to Prior Work

PairNet departs from both major lines of ITE estimation: factual-loss methods (TARNet, CFRNet, DragonNet) that train independent outcome heads per treatment, and pseudo-outcome methods (meta-learners, matching, GANs) that impute missing counterfactuals. It retains the shared-representation architecture of TARNet-style models but replaces the per-treatment factual loss with a pair-based loss on the *difference* of observed outcomes for nearby instances under contrasting treatments. The key conceptual departure is the residual alignment cross-term (Eq. 5), which promotes positive correlation of prediction errors across nearby covariates — an inductive bias absent from all prior approaches. For continuous treatments, it builds on the pair-formation strategy from Nagalapatti et al. (AAAI 2024) and applies the same loss to DRNet and VCNet architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PairNet provides a theoretically grounded, model-agnostic training strategy that avoids the reliability issues of pseudo-outcome imputation while offering tighter generalization bounds and a consistency guarantee under strict overlap. The finding that even random pairs outperform the factual model (due to the residual alignment term) suggests the proximity of pairs matters less than previously assumed, which could simplify practical implementations. Open questions include whether the binary-treatment theoretical analysis extends cleanly to continuous treatments, whether strict overlap is too strong an assumption for real-world data, and how the method scales when the embedding space for pair formation is high-dimensional. The approach is simple to implement and integrates with existing architectures, making it a practical drop-in replacement for the factual loss.
