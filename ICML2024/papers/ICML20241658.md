## Problem

Existing contrastive losses for multi-view or multi-modal representation learning (k≥3) are extensions of pairwise methods, either averaging all k(k−1)/2 pairwise losses or comparing each view to the average of the remaining k−1 views. These approaches never exploit the fact that k views of the same object should be coherent as a simultaneous k-tuple, rather than being reducible to pairwise relationships. The paper asks whether a loss can be designed that scores the joint coherence of all k views at once, without any reduction to pairs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a batch of n objects, each represented by k views (k augmented images, k domain-specific images, or k EEG channels), which are encoded into a k×n×d tensor of unit-norm embeddings. The output is a single scalar M3G loss per batch, computed by solving an entropy-regularized multi-marginal optimal transport problem over the n×k pooled embeddings. The O(n^k) cost tensor construction limits practical use to small batches (n=16–64) and moderate view counts (k≤6), with the MM-Sinkhorn solver run to a tolerance of 10⁻³.

## Method

M3G is defined as the optimality gap of the ground-truth identity polymatching tensor J_{n,k} in the entropy-regularized MM-OT problem: it equals the cost of matching each object's k views together (using a multiway cost, defaulting to circular variance on the unit sphere) minus the minimum cost achievable by any polystochastic tensor over the n×k pooled embeddings. The cost tensor is built by evaluating the multiway cost over all n^k possible k-tuples, and the MM-OT problem is solved via the multi-marginal Sinkhorn algorithm. Crucially, the gradient of M3G is obtained in closed form via Danskin's theorem (as a Fenchel-Young loss), requiring only a single forward pass of MM-Sinkhorn without backpropagating through its iterations. In practice, M3G is plugged into a student-teacher architecture where k−1 student encoders produce embeddings contrasted against one teacher encoder, with gradients aggregated across the k−1 student branches to save memory.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k multiview SSL (ViT-B/16, batch size 64, 300 epochs), M3G achieves 75.61% top-1 linear accuracy at k=3 views and 75.75% at k=4 views, outperforming the best pairwise baseline (InfoNCE_pwe) by 0.25% and 0.49% respectively. On DomainNet domain adaptation (5 seen → 1 unseen domain, batch size 16), M3G consistently ranks first across all six unseen domains, with a mean improvement of 3.1% over the second-best method (InfoNCE_ave); the largest gap is on clipart (32.4% vs. 24.1%). On the PhysioNet EEG sleep-stage task (k=6 channels), M3G reaches 64.6% accuracy with 1000 samples per class, compared to 56.3% for InfoNCE_pwe.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares M3G against two families of pairwise contrastive losses—BYOL (Grill et al., 2020) and InfoNCE/SimCLR (Chen et al., 2020)—each extended to k≥3 views via either pairwise aggregation (pwe) or one-vs-average-of-rest (ave). M3G wins across all three experimental settings. On ImageNet-1k, M3G outperforms the strongest baseline (InfoNCE_pwe) by 0.25% at k=3 and 0.49% at k=4, while BYOL variants trail by a wider margin (e.g., BYOL_ave at 75.04% vs. M3G's 75.75% at k=4). On DomainNet, the advantage is substantially larger: M3G beats InfoNCE_ave (the second-best method) by a mean of 3.1% across all six unseen domains, and the gap over BYOL_pwe is even more pronounced (e.g., 32.4% vs. 9.6% on clipart). On the EEG task, M3G surpasses InfoNCE_pwe by 8.3 points at s=1000 samples per class (64.6% vs. 56.3%), with the gap narrowing at lower sample counts. The pwe aggregation consistently underperforms the ave aggregation for the pairwise baselines, particularly on DomainNet, whereas M3G avoids this pairwise-reduction limitation entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two theoretical pillars: the entropic multi-marginal optimal transport (MM-OT) framework and its MM-Sinkhorn solver (Benamou et al., 2015), and the Fenchel-Young loss framework (Blondel et al., 2020) that enables gradient computation of optimality gaps via Danskin's theorem without backpropagating through the solver. It departs from the dominant paradigm in multi-view contrastive learning—where losses designed for k=2 (InfoNCE, BYOL) are extended to k≥3 by averaging over all pairwise comparisons or using a one-vs-average-of-rest strategy—by instead formulating the problem as a single polymatching over all k views simultaneously. The paper positions M3G as the first contrastive loss that treats the k-tuple of embeddings holistically rather than reducing to O(k²) pairwise sub-problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The M3G loss provides a principled, theoretically grounded alternative to ad-hoc pairwise extensions for multi-view and multi-modal representation learning, with the practical advantage that its gradient requires only a single forward pass of MM-Sinkhorn (no unrolling or implicit differentiation through iterations). However, the O(n^k) complexity of the cost tensor fundamentally limits batch sizes to n≤64–128 for k≤6, which constrains the loss's applicability to settings where large-batch training is critical. Open questions include whether domain-specific cost structures can reduce the effective complexity, whether ε should be tuned as a function of batch size, and whether the modest empirical gains (0.25–0.49% on ImageNet, ~3% on DomainNet) persist at scale or with stronger backbones.
