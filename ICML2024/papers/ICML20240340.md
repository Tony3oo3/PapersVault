## Problem

Conventional multi-task RL assigns equal priority to all tasks, but when task difficulties vary significantly, the large gradients from easy tasks dominate early training and prevent the agent from ever learning harder tasks—a phenomenon the authors term the simplicity bias. This paper asks how to dynamically schedule which tasks to train on so that limited interaction budget is allocated to the most challenging tasks first, thereby mitigating negative transfer and improving overall performance.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a state vector from a MuJoCo Sawyer robotic arm environment and a learned 8-dimensional task embedding produced by a GRU-based task embedding network that processes the trajectory history (states, actions, rewards). This learned embedding replaces the one-hot task ID used in standard multi-task baselines, which is critical because the scheduling mechanism means the policy may never encounter certain task IDs during training. The output is a continuous action for the robotic arm, produced by a shared SAC policy network conditioned on both the state and the task embedding.

## Method

SMT maintains four task pools (main, training, solved, unsolvable) and at each scheduling interval selects the K hardest tasks—ranked by a difficulty metric derived from the KL divergence between the policy's trajectory distribution and the optimal-trajectory posterior (Levine, 2018)—into the training pool. A reset mechanism periodically reinitializes the policy and Q-network parameters while preserving replay buffers, preventing the simplicity bias from locking the network into easy-task solutions. Training is split into two stages: Stage 1 allocates per-task budgets of κB (where B is remaining budget) and moves tasks to the solved or unsolvable pool based on return thresholds M and m; Stage 2 trains all previously unsolvable tasks jointly to exploit potential positive transfer. The backbone is SAC with a VariBAD-style architecture augmented by a task-prediction decoder, and the entire scheduling, reset, and budget logic is algorithm-agnostic.

## Results

On the Meta-World MT10 benchmark, SMT achieves the highest average success rate of 86.8% ± 8.6%, outperforming the strongest baseline PaCo (82.4% ± 14.2%) by 4.4 percentage points. The advantage is most pronounced on difficult tasks: SMT reaches 62% on 'push' (task 1) and 76% on 'peg-insert-side' (task 7), where SAC-MT and PCGrad score 0% and 0% respectively. On the larger MT50 benchmark, SMT leads in average bottom-k success ratios, achieving 8.0% on the bottom-20 hardest tasks versus 0–4.6% for all baselines, and 56.0% overall (k=50) compared to 51.9–55.6% for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMT outperforms all four baselines on both MT10 and MT50. Against PaCo (Sun et al., 2022), the closest competitor, SMT gains 4.4% on MT10 average (86.8% vs. 82.4%) and 0.4% on MT50 overall (56.0% vs. 55.6%), with a more substantial 3.4% advantage on the bottom-20 hardest tasks (8.0% vs. 4.6%). Against PCGrad (Yu et al., 2020), SMT improves by 12.0% on MT10 average, primarily because PCGrad completely fails on tasks 1 and 2 (0% success) while SMT achieves 62% and 34% respectively. Against Soft Modular (Yang et al., 2020), SMT gains 12.4% on MT10 average, with the largest gap on task 7 ('peg-insert-side') where SMT reaches 76% versus 12%. Against SAC-MT-MH (Yu et al., 2019), SMT improves by 9.6% on MT10 average, again driven by superior performance on the hardest tasks where the multi-head baseline scores only 28% and 46%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on three distinct threads: the probabilistic inference framework of Levine (2018) to derive a task-difficulty metric, the reset mechanism of Nikishin et al. (2022) to combat primacy bias in deep RL, and the VariBAD architecture (Zintgraf et al., 2020) for learned task embeddings. It departs from prior multi-task RL methods (SAC-MT, Soft Modular, PCGrad, PaCo) that address negative transfer through architectural changes or gradient manipulation by instead proposing a scheduling-based approach that dynamically prioritizes harder tasks during training. The key conceptual departure is reframing the negative-transfer problem as a resource-allocation problem solvable through task ordering rather than loss reweighting or parameter sharing strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The simplicity bias—where easy tasks dominate early gradient updates and starve harder tasks of learning signal—is a practical obstacle for any multi-task RL deployment with heterogeneous task difficulty, and this paper provides a concrete, well-ablated remedy. The finding that the approach integrates cleanly with existing architectures (demonstrated with Soft Modular) suggests broad applicability. However, evaluation is limited to Meta-World robotic manipulation with SAC as the sole backbone, leaving open whether the scheduling strategy transfers to vision-based tasks, non-stationary environments, or fundamentally different RL algorithms. The added complexity of four task pools, per-task budgets, and periodic resets also raises practical deployment questions.
