## Problem

Vanilla resonate-and-fire (RF) neurons suffer from intrinsic limitations—numerical divergence from Euler integration, excessive continuous spiking, and traditional reset mechanisms that disrupt the oscillation phase—that prevent them from being effectively trained within recurrent spiking neural networks. Additionally, RSNNs in general face a convergence dilemma, typically requiring hundreds of epochs to reach stable performance. This paper addresses both issues simultaneously by introducing a balanced RF formulation that is both learnable and fast-converging.

## Input / Output

The network consumes sequential inputs at a discrete time scale of δ=0.01: row-by-row pixel values for S-MNIST/PS-MNIST (784 steps), level-cross-encoded ECG spike trains (1300 ms segments), and 700-channel audio spike trains (250 steps with zero-padding). Outputs are either a single class label (digit or speech classification) or per-step waveform labels (ECG P, PQ, QR, RS, ST, TP). Notably, no biases are used anywhere in the network; a few BRF neurons spontaneously learn to fire consistently, effectively emulating a bias through their resonating dynamics.

## Method

The balanced RF (BRF) neuron augments the Izhikevich RF membrane dynamics with three mechanisms: (1) a refractory period q(t) that exponentially decays and raises the spiking threshold after each spike, (2) a smooth reset that temporarily increases the dampening factor b(t) rather than abruptly resetting the membrane potential, preserving oscillation phase, and (3) an analytically derived divergence boundary p(ω) that constrains the dampening parameter b_c to ensure |u(t)| ≤ |u(t−δ)|, preventing numerical blow-up from Euler integration. The network is a single fully recurrent hidden layer of BRF neurons (with trainable angular frequency ω and b-offset b′) feeding into leaky integrator output neurons, trained end-to-end with full BPTT and a double-Gaussian surrogate gradient. The key insight is that the divergence boundary keeps the spectral radius of the state transition at or below unity, yielding a smooth, convex-like error landscape that enables convergence to 95% of final accuracy within 3–10 epochs—orders of magnitude faster than ALIF baselines—while producing only 14–46% of the spikes.

## Results

On S-MNIST, BRF achieves 99.0±0.1% test accuracy (vs. 98.7% for ALIF) with only 68,874 parameters (vs. 156,126) and 0.22× the spike operations of ALIF. On PS-MNIST, BRF reaches 95.0±0.2% and BHRF 95.2%, both exceeding ALIF's 94.3%, while vanilla RF collapses to chance (9.9%). On SHD, BHRF achieves 92.7±0.7% and BRF 91.7±0.8%, outperforming ALIF (90.4%) but trailing DCLS-D (95.1%) and RadLIF (94.62%). Convergence is dramatically faster: BRF reaches 95% of final accuracy in 3 epochs on S-MNIST versus 105 for ALIF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary ALIF-RSNN baseline (Yin et al., 2021), BRF and BHRF outperform on S-MNIST (99.0/99.1% vs. 98.7%), PS-MNIST (95.0/95.2% vs. 94.3%), and SHD (91.7/92.7% vs. 90.4%), while on ECG BHRF wins (87.0% vs. 85.9%) and BRF is essentially tied (85.8% vs. 85.9%). All BRF/BHRF variants use roughly half the parameters of ALIF and produce 0.14×–0.46× the spike operations, with the largest gap on SHD where BRF spikes only 14% as much as ALIF. Against the SNN-SoTa baselines on SHD (DCLS-D at 95.1% and RadLIF at 94.62%), BHRF (92.7%) and BRF (91.7%) still trail by 2.4–3.4 percentage points, though they use 1.8×–36× fewer parameters. The vanilla RF neuron without reset collapses to chance on PS-MNIST (9.9%) due to divergence, highlighting that the balanced modifications (refractory period, smooth reset, divergence boundary) are essential for stable performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Izhikevich's original resonate-and-fire (RF) neuron (2001) and the harmonic RF variant (AlKhamissi et al., 2021) by identifying and fixing three intrinsic limitations—numerical divergence, excessive spiking, and oscillation-disrupting resets—that had prevented RF neurons from being effective in recurrent spiking networks. It positions itself against the ALIF-RSNN baseline (Yin et al., 2021), which was the state-of-the-art for recurrent SNN sequence classification, and against prior RF implementations on neuromorphic hardware (Loihi 2) that demonstrated signal-processing utility but not competitive learning performance. The key departure is the analytically derived divergence boundary and the refractory-period-based smooth reset, which together transform the RF neuron from a theoretically appealing but practically unstable model into one that trains stably under full BPTT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The BRF-RSNN addresses the well-known convergence dilemma of recurrent SNNs (hundreds of epochs) by achieving 95% of final accuracy in as few as 3 epochs, while simultaneously reducing spike operations to 14–46% of ALIF baselines with fewer parameters. This makes RF-based recurrent networks a credible candidate for large-scale, energy-efficient sequence processing on neuromorphic hardware. Open questions include the sensitivity to initial angular frequency parameterization (the authors acknowledge this as a drawback), the limited exploration to a single hidden layer, and whether the convergence advantage persists at larger scale (CIFAR, full speech commands). The finding that BRF networks tolerate heavy pruning of recurrent weights—suggesting the resonating dynamics provide implicit memory—opens an interesting line of inquiry into whether explicit recurrence is necessary in resonator-based SNNs.
