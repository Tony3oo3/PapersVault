## Problem

Deep learning-based Blind Image Quality Assessment (BIQA) methods suffer from poor generalization to unseen distortion types and image contents because standard empirical risk minimization learns correlations between distorted images and MOS labels that are confounded by distortion type and image content. This paper identifies these confounding effects through a structural causal graph and proposes a causality-based training paradigm that eliminates them to improve generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242554/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single distorted RGB image (no reference image required) as input, scaled to 256×256 and randomly cropped to 224×224, and outputs a scalar quality score normalized to [0,1]. During training, the model is trained on synthetically distorted datasets (TID2013, KADID-10K) where distortion type labels are available, and evaluated both zero-shot and after fine-tuning on authentically distorted datasets (KonIQ-10K, LIVE-C, CID2013) where no distortion labels exist.

## Method

Causal-IQA constructs a structural causal graph identifying distortion type (D) and image content (C) as confounders between the distorted image I_d and MOS label m, then applies Backdoor Adjustment to derive the causal objective P(m|do(I_d)) = Σ P(m|I_d, c_i, d_j) P(c_i, d_j). A 'Counterfactual Division' step partitions training data into subsets where both distortion type and content cluster (obtained via GMM on 10-dim t-SNE-reduced ResNet18 features) are invariant, blocking the confounding paths. Training follows a meta-learning-inspired inner-outer loop: the inner loop performs one-step gradient updates on each confounder subset (weighted by GMM cluster probabilities), and the outer loop updates meta-parameters via a Reptile-style first-order approximation. Two variants are proposed—CIS (serial subset updates) and CIP (parallel subset updates)—both using a ResNet18 backbone initialized from ImageNet pre-training.

## Results

On authentically distorted datasets (fine-tuned from TID2013+KADID-10K), CIS achieves SROCC of 0.895 on CID2013, 0.828 on LIVE-C, and 0.881 on KonIQ-10K, outperforming the strongest baseline MetaIQA (0.766, 0.802, 0.850) by 12.9, 2.6, and 3.1 SROCC points respectively. In leave-one-distortion-out cross-validation on KADID-10K, CIS achieves an average SROCC of 0.808, surpassing MetaIQA (0.767) by 4.1 points. On TID2013 leave-one-distortion-out, CIP reaches 0.884 average SROCC vs. MetaIQA's 0.854.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CIS (serial) consistently outperforms all 12 baselines across all three authentically distorted test sets. Against the strongest baseline MetaIQA, CIS gains +12.9 SROCC on CID2013 (0.895 vs. 0.766), +2.6 on LIVE-C (0.828 vs. 0.802), and +3.1 on KonIQ-10K (0.881 vs. 0.850). CIP (parallel) also beats MetaIQA on all three datasets but by smaller margins (+12.8, +2.1, +1.5 SROCC), and is slightly behind CIS on CID2013 and KonIQ-10K while trailing on LIVE-C. Against traditional hand-crafted methods (BLIINDS-II, BRISQUE, ILNIQE, CORNIA, HOSA), both CIS and CIP lead by 15–55 SROCC points, confirming the advantage of the causal meta-learning paradigm over feature-based approaches. In the leave-one-distortion-out setting on KADID-10K, CIS achieves 0.808 average SROCC vs. MetaIQA's 0.767, with per-distortion gains of 1–10 points over MetaIQA across all 25 distortion types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the meta-learning-based IQA training paradigm established by MetaIQA (Zhu et al., 2020) by replacing its generic task partitioning with a causally-motivated Counterfactual Division that explicitly controls for distortion type and image content as confounders. The optimization strategy is derived from the Backdoor Criterion in structural causal models (Pearl, 1995) and instantiated via a Reptile-style first-order meta-learning loop (Nichol et al., 2018), building on MAML's inner-outer loop structure (Finn et al., 2017). Conceptually, it departs from the standard ERM training paradigm that maximizes P(m|I_d) by instead targeting the interventional distribution P(m|do(I_d)), which the authors argue is the correct objective for generalization. The paper positions itself as the first to apply causal inference to the IQA task, drawing on broader causal learning work in computer vision (scene graph generation, image recognition) but adapting the backdoor adjustment specifically to the confounding structure of distortion and content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The causal framing provides an interpretable explanation for why standard BIQA models fail to generalize from synthetic to authentic distortions: they inadvertently learn P(m|I_d, C, D) rather than the desired P(m|do(I_d)). This opens a principled path for future IQA work to identify and control additional confounders (e.g., viewing conditions, display characteristics) without ad-hoc data augmentation. Key limitations include the reliance on synthetic distortion labels for training (restricting applicability to authentic-only settings), the coarse 2-cluster GMM for content partitioning, and the exclusion of distortion degree as a confounder (justified empirically but potentially limiting). Open questions include whether the causal approach scales to more complex, continuous confounders and whether the Counterfactual Division can be replaced by learned representations that approximate the backdoor adjustment without explicit clustering.
