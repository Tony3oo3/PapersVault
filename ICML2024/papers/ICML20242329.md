## Problem

Prior audio generation research is predominantly task-specific, with each task (TTS, speech enhancement, music generation, etc.) requiring bespoke architectures, domain knowledge, and training pipelines. While LLM-based models have shown strong performance on individual audio tasks, their potential to unify multiple audio generation tasks into a single model remains largely unexplored. This paper asks whether a single LLM-based model can handle a wide range of audio generation tasks in a unified manner while maintaining competitive performance on each.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242329/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

All input modalities are converted to discrete token sequences before being fed to the LLM: audio is encoded by a custom neural codec (50 FPS, 3 RVQ layers, 15M params) into 150 tokens/sec; text instructions are embedded via a pre-trained T5 model; phonemes come from pronunciation dictionaries or DNN-HMM forced alignment; MIDI is flattened to frame-level F0; and speaker identity is represented by a 3-second speech prompt. The model outputs audio token sequences that are detokenized back into waveforms, supporting speech, sound, music, and singing voice at 50 FPS. The joint vocabulary across all modalities has size 4212, and the maximum context length is 6000 tokens per batch.

## Method

UniAudio is a 1B-parameter decoder-only Transformer that processes all 11 audio generation tasks as unified sequence-to-sequence problems, with conditions and targets spliced into a single token sequence separated by special tokens. The key architectural contribution is CodecFormer, a hierarchical model that splits audio token prediction into a patch predictor (modeling inter-frame dependencies over T frames) and a token predictor (modeling intra-frame dependencies over N=3 RVQ layers), reducing the effective sequence length from T×N to T and cutting self-attention complexity from O((T·N)²) to O(T²). A custom neural codec with a mel-spectrogram-based discriminator and sub-band STFT reconstruction loss achieves near-lossless reconstruction across all four audio types with only 3 RVQ layers. Training is two-stage: joint supervised training on 7 tasks over ~100K hours of multi-source data, followed by fine-tuning on 4 additional tasks with data re-balancing (α=0.05) to preserve prior task performance.

## Results

UniAudio achieves competitive performance across all 7 training-stage tasks. On TTS, it attains SIM 0.71 and WER 2.0 (vs. NaturalSpeech 2's 0.62/2.3), and on VC it reaches SIM 0.87 and WER 4.8 (vs. LM-VC's 0.82/4.9). For speech enhancement and target speaker extraction, UniAudio obtains the best DNSMOS scores (3.66 and 3.96) and MOS scores (3.68 and 3.72), though it lags on signal-level metrics like PESQ. On text-to-sound, it achieves FAD 3.12 (vs. Tango's 3.61), and on text-to-music, FAD 3.65 (vs. MusicGen's 4.52), though MusicGen leads on subjective OVL/REL scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On TTS, UniAudio outperforms NaturalSpeech 2 on speaker similarity (SIM 0.71 vs. 0.62) and intelligibility (WER 2.0 vs. 2.3), and substantially on zero-shot speaker cloning (SMOS 3.56 vs. 3.11), while matching on MOS (3.81 vs. 3.83). On voice conversion, UniAudio beats LM-VC on all four metrics (SIM 0.87 vs. 0.82, WER 4.8 vs. 4.9, MOS 3.54 vs. 3.41, SMOS 3.56 vs. 3.17). For speech enhancement and target speaker extraction, UniAudio achieves the best DNSMOS (3.66, 3.96) and MOS (3.68, 3.72) but trails FullSubNet+ and SpeakerBeam on PESQ/VISQOL; the authors argue these signal-level metrics are less appropriate for generative models. On singing voice synthesis, UniAudio edges out DiffSinger on MOS (4.08 vs. 3.94) while matching on SMOS (4.04 vs. 4.05). For text-to-sound, UniAudio improves FAD over Tango (3.12 vs. 3.61) but falls short on subjective OVL/REL (61.9/66.1 vs. 66.2/68.6). On text-to-music, UniAudio achieves a lower FAD than MusicGen (3.65 vs. 4.52) but is outperformed on KL (1.9 vs. 1.4) and subjective scores (OVL 67.9 vs. 73.3, REL 70.0 vs. 71.3), which the authors attribute to MusicGen's larger 20k-hour training corpus.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-based audio generation paradigm established by VALL-E, SPEAR-TTS, AudioLM, and MusicGen, which each target a single or narrow set of tasks (TTS, music, or speech enhancement). It departs from these by unifying 11 diverse audio generation tasks under one 1B-parameter model, drawing on the neural codec tokenization framework from SoundStream and EnCodec. The CodecFormer architecture is specifically inspired by MEGABYTE's hierarchical sequence modeling to address the quadratic complexity problem that arises when flattening RVQ token sequences, positioning it as an improved implementation of the flattening prediction strategy that prior work (MusicGen) identified as quality-optimal but computationally prohibitive at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UniAudio demonstrates that a single LLM-based model can achieve competitive performance across 11 heterogeneous audio tasks (speech, sound, music, singing) without task-specific architectures, validating the universal audio generation paradigm at 1B scale and 100k hours of data. The CodecFormer's reduction of effective sequence length from T×N to T makes higher-RVQ-layer training feasible, which is a practical efficiency gain for the field. Key limitations include the exclusion of tasks requiring new modalities (speech-to-speech translation, noise removal), the reliance on deterministic-length formulations for some tasks, and the observation that signal-level metrics (PESQ, VISQOL) may not fairly assess generative methods. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly open-ended audio editing, whether new modalities can be introduced at fine-tuning time, and whether the cross-domain transfer benefits persist at larger scales.
