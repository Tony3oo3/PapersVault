## Problem

Offline multi-objective optimization (MOO) has lagged far behind offline single-objective optimization (SOO), primarily because no benchmark comparable to DesignBench exists for the multi-objective setting. Without a standardized evaluation suite, it is difficult to compare methods or drive progress in offline MOO, despite the fact that many real-world design problems (protein design, engineering, NAS) are inherently multi-objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242292/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline dataset of (solution, objective-vector) pairs collected by expert MOEAs (NSGA-II, MOEA/D, NSGA-III) with an amateur survival operator to ensure diverse data coverage, along with a search-space definition that can be continuous, categorical, permutation, or sequence. The algorithm outputs a set of candidate solutions (e.g., 256) that are evaluated in a single batch against the ground-truth oracle; performance is reported as hypervolume after applying NSGA-II non-dominated sorting to select the top P percentile of solutions, since the standard 'P percentile of top K' metric from offline SOO does not directly apply to multi-objective settings.

## Method

The paper proposes Off-MOO-Bench, the first benchmark for offline MOO spanning synthetic functions (DTLZ, ZDT, OmniTest, VLMOP), multi-objective NAS, MORL (MuJoCo), combinatorial optimization (TSP, CVRP, KP, Portfolio), scientific design (molecule, protein), and real-world engineering (RE suite). It analyzes offline MOO methods along four axes—data, model architecture, learning algorithm, and search algorithm—and instantiates two surrogate families: DNN-based (end-to-end, multi-head, and multiple independent models, each optionally augmented with MTL techniques like GradNorm/PcGrad or offline SOO techniques like COMs, RoMA, IOM, ICT, Tri-Mentoring) and GP-based (qNEHVI, qParEGO, JES). A key practical finding is that data pruning (retaining only top-ranked solutions via NSGA-II sorting) is essential to prevent model collapse, where the surrogate overestimates a single region and the search yields only a handful of solutions. After training, a MOEA (default NSGA-II, 50 generations) searches within the surrogate to produce the final solution set, with task-specific genetic operators (SBX/PM for continuous, order crossover/inversion for permutations, local mutation for sequences).

## Results

The primary metric is average rank (lower is better) across six task categories (Synthetic, MO-NAS, MORL, MOCO, Sci-Design, RE), with 256 solutions evaluated at the 100th percentile using hypervolume (HV). Multiple Models + IOM achieves the best overall average rank of 4.61, followed by Multiple Models (5.02) and Multi-Head (7.00), all substantially outperforming the best solution set in the training data D(best) (rank 10.03). GP-based methods underperform: MOBO (qNEHVI) ranks 8.64, while MOBO-qParEGO (11.68) and MOBO-JES (10.30) are worse than D(best). Notably, no method surpasses D(best) on MOCO (permutation space), where D(best) achieves rank 2.00.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Multiple Models + IOM (rank 4.61) is the overall best method, outperforming vanilla Multiple Models (5.02) and Multi-Head (7.00), and beating D(best) (10.03) by a wide margin on Synthetic, MO-NAS, MORL, Sci-Design, and RE tasks. Among the adapted offline SOO techniques, IOM (invariant representation learning) clearly outperforms COMs (8.09), RoMA (7.07), ICT (9.64), and Tri-Mentoring (8.77), suggesting that representation-level invariance is more beneficial than adversarial smoothing or co-teaching in the multi-objective setting. GP-based MOBO methods are consistently weaker: qNEHVI (8.64) is competitive on Sci-Design (5.44) but poor on MOCO (13.69), while qParEGO (11.68) and JES (10.30) are worse than D(best) overall, indicating that GP surrogates with a single batch of 100 initialization points are insufficient for offline MOO. On MOCO (permutation search space), D(best) (rank 2.00) is the best, with MOBO-JES (3.00) and Multiple Models+IOM (4.25) trailing, highlighting that discrete combinatorial spaces remain unsolved for offline MOO. No single method dominates across all task types, and the gap between the best (4.61) and D(best) (10.03) is meaningful but not overwhelming, leaving room for future improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions offline MOO as the natural multi-objective extension of offline SOO, which has been well-served by the DesignBench benchmark (Trabucco et al., 2022) and a suite of advanced surrogate-based methods (COMs, RoMA, IOM, ICT, Tri-Mentoring). The authors adapt these offline SOO techniques to the multi-objective setting by organizing them around three model architectures (end-to-end, multi-head, multiple independent models) and borrowing multi-task learning strategies (GradNorm, PcGrad) for joint objective training. On the GP side, they repurpose mainstream MOBO acquisition functions (qNEHVI, qParEGO, JES) by restricting them to a single batch of evaluations, effectively converting an iterative online procedure into a one-shot offline search. The evolutionary algorithms (NSGA-II, MOEA/D, NSGA-III) serve dual roles: as data-collection engines for the benchmark and as the search algorithm within the trained surrogate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark fills a critical gap: without a standardized evaluation suite, offline MOO research cannot progress comparably to offline SOO. The key empirical finding is that while all DNN-based methods can beat the best training-set solution (confirming feasibility), no single method dominates across task types, and GP-based MOBO methods actually underperform the training-set baseline in the offline setting—a notable departure from their online success. The model collapse phenomenon (where an inaccurate surrogate causes one solution to dominate all others, collapsing the output to 1–2 solutions) and the persistent failure on permutation spaces highlight that offline MOO remains an open problem. Future directions include mixed search spaces, high-dimensional scaling, constraint handling, and few-shot adaptation, all of which are identified but left unresolved.
