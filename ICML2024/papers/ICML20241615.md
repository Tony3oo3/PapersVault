## Problem

Amortized variational inference is widely used in simulation-based inference to rapidly compute posterior approximations, but it produces miscalibrated posteriors with no theoretical guarantees on coverage. Existing calibration remedies—ensembling, loss-function modifications with tunable regularization—either lack formal guarantees or require careful hyperparameter selection whose optimal value is unknown without access to the true posterior. The paper asks whether one can obtain guaranteed marginal coverage with minimal computational overhead while retaining informative (small) prediction regions.

## Input / Output

CANVI takes as input a collection of pre-trained amortized posterior approximators {q^(t)(Θ|X)} (e.g., Neural Spline Flows trained under FAVI, ELBO, or IWBO objectives), the prior P(Θ), and the forward model P(X|Θ) from which i.i.d. samples can be drawn. It outputs a single selected approximator q^(*) along with a conformal quantile threshold q̂_R^*(α) that defines prediction regions C(x) = {θ : 1/q^(*)(θ|x) ≤ q̂_R^*(α)} with guaranteed marginal coverage at level 1−α. Because calibration and recalibration datasets are sampled directly from the joint P(X,Θ), their size is unbounded by the training budget, and the entire conformalization step completes in under one second per task.

## Method

CANVI applies split conformal prediction with the inverse-density score s(x,θ) = 1/q(θ|x), which recovers highest-density regions when q equals the true posterior. When multiple candidate approximators are provided, CANVI selects the one maximizing predictive efficiency (expected inverse Lebesgue measure of the prediction region), estimated via importance-weighted Monte Carlo with mixed samplers that blend the variational posterior and the prior to reduce variance under underdispersion. A critical design choice is a separate recalibration step on an independent dataset D_R after selection, which restores the exchangeability needed for the coverage guarantee that would otherwise be broken by conditioning on D_C for model selection. The paper proves that, under smoothness (C³) and boundedness conditions on the variational family, the efficiency of the CANVI-selected predictor is within a vanishing bound of the best candidate with high probability, with the gap shrinking as the calibration set size N_C grows.

## Results

On the ARCH benchmark, CANVI corrects severe miscalibration: ELBO-trained posteriors achieve only 3.96% coverage at the 95% target (vs. 94.96% after conformalization), IWBO reaches 40.74% (vs. 95.48% after), and FAVI reaches 92.60% (vs. 94.87% after). In terms of predictive efficiency (expected volume of the 95% prediction region, lower is better), FAVI-trained CANVI achieves ℓ = 0.7451, substantially outperforming ELBO (1.3184) and IWBO (1.4211). On the 11-dimensional PROVABGS galaxy SED task, FAVI achieves ℓ = 5.37×10⁶ while IWBO reaches 1.38×10⁹ and ELBO yields an infinite volume estimate, confirming that CANVI selects the most efficient approximator. Calibration is achieved in under one second per task with 10% of the simulation budget reserved for the calibration set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CANVI against six likelihood-free inference baselines on standard SBI benchmarks: NPE (Greenberg et al., 2019), NRE and BNRE (Hermans et al., 2020), NRE C and BNRE C (Delaunoy et al., 2022), and Ratio BNPE (Delaunoy et al., 2023). On the calibration metric (Figure 2), all six baselines exhibit systematic miscalibration—NPE and NRE tend to be overconfident (below the y=x line) while the 'C' variants (NRE C, BNRE C, Ratio BNPE) are overly conservative (above the line)—whereas CANVI lies precisely on the y=x calibration curve across all tasks and simulation budgets. The authors note that a direct efficiency comparison is 'moot' because the baselines fail to achieve even marginal calibration, making their prediction regions statistically invalid. Among the variational objectives tested as CANVI candidates, FAVI consistently produces the most efficient (smallest-volume) calibrated regions, while ELBO and IWBO suffer severe underdispersion that CANVI corrects at the cost of larger regions. The mixed-sampling volume estimator (K=10) is critical: without it (K=1), efficiency estimates are off by 10–70× for underdispersed posteriors (e.g., ELBO: 50.66 vs. true 1.32).

## Relation to Prior Work

CANVI sits at the intersection of two well-developed threads: split conformal prediction (Angelopoulos & Bates, 2021; Shafer & Vovk, 2008), which provides distribution-free marginal coverage guarantees via score quantiles, and amortized variational inference in simulation-based inference, where neural spline flows trained under FAVI, ELBO, or IWBO objectives produce rapid posterior approximations. The efficiency analysis directly extends the VFCP framework of Yang & Kuchibhotla (2021), reducing CANVI's approximator selection to a special case of their 'validity-first' algorithm and importing their finite-sample efficiency bounds. The paper departs from the dominant calibration strategy in the SBI community—regularization-based conservatism (Delaunoy et al., 2022, 2023; Falkiewicz et al., 2023)—by requiring no modification to the variational training procedure and no tunable hyperparameter, instead treating conformalization as a post-hoc wrapper that only needs forward-model samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CANVI provides the first framework that gives amortized VI in SBI guaranteed marginal calibration with zero tuning parameters and negligible computational overhead (calibration completes in under one second), addressing a widely acknowledged crisis in the field (Hermans et al., 2021b). The efficiency bound (Theorem 3.8) is practically important because it tells practitioners that CANVI will select the best approximator among candidates with high probability, and the bound tightens with calibration set size—which is free in SBI since one can always simulate more. Open questions include extending guarantees to misspecified forward models (distribution shift), group-conditional coverage (sketched in Appendix A but not fully analyzed), and functional-space posteriors. The galaxy SED application demonstrates scalability to 11-dimensional parameter spaces relevant to real astrophysical inference, though the underdispersed ELBO/IWBO cases produce regions too large to be scientifically informative, highlighting that conformalization fixes validity but cannot rescue fundamentally poor approximations.
