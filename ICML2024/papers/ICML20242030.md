## Problem

Prior concept guidance work has focused almost exclusively on truthfulness, leaving it unclear whether linear probing and activation steering generalize to other interpretable concepts such as appropriateness, humor, creativity, and quality. Additionally, the relationship between detection accuracy and guidability—previously assumed to be positive for truthfulness—remains untested across a broader concept space, and no unified metric exists to compare guidance quality across concepts, models, and configurations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242030/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt and a learned concept vector (the weight vector of a linear probe trained on normalized residual representations from the first t=16 tokens of the last assistant response). At inference time, the concept vector is added to or subtracted from hidden states in the top-k layers (by probing accuracy) with a scalar strength α, while preserving the hidden-state norm. The output is a guided generation whose quality is assessed by a concept-elicitation probability (via LLM-judge classifiers) and a perplexity-normalized effect size (PNES) that penalizes fluency degradation.

## Method

The paper unifies three linear probing techniques—logistic regression, difference-in-means, and PCA—into a single framework for extracting concept directions from normalized residual stream representations at selected layers. At inference, guidance is applied via a norm-preserving update (rep_guided = ||rep||₂/||rep + αw||₂ · (rep + αw)) restricted to the k layers with highest probing accuracy. The key evaluation contribution is the perplexity-normalized effect size (PNES), which models the concept-elicitation curve as tanh(bα) and the perplexity degradation as exp(cα²), then fits both via non-linear least-squares to produce a single scalar summarizing the tradeoff between concept strength and fluency loss. Experiments across four models (Llama-2-chat, Llama-2 base, Mistral-instruct, Mistral base) and six concepts reveal that truthfulness is robustly guidable, compliance is surprisingly controllable via the appropriateness vector (concept confusion), humor requires extensive tuning, and detection accuracy does not reliably predict guidability for non-truthfulness concepts.

## Results

The paper introduces Perplexity-Normalized Effect Size (PNES) to jointly measure concept elicitation success and fluency degradation. Across all models and probes, Compliance achieves the highest mean PNES (0.427), followed by Quality (0.273) and Truthfulness (0.245), while Creativity is the hardest to guide (mean PNES 0.094). The single best guidance configuration reaches PNES = 1.000 (Compliance, Mistral base, PCA, k=16, |α|=128), while Appropriateness remains the most difficult concept to elicit (best PNES = 0.414, Mistral base, PCA). Logistic regression achieves the highest mean PNES on Llama-2-chat (0.262), DiM dominates on Mistral base (0.406), and no single probe is best across all concepts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper unifies three probing techniques from prior work — Logistic Regression (Li et al. 2023), Difference-in-Means (Marks & Tegmark 2023), and PCA (Zou et al. 2023) — and compares them across six concepts and four models. No single probe dominates: Logistic Regression achieves the best Compliance PNES on Llama-2-chat (0.518 vs. 0.136 for DiM and 0.401 for PCA) and the best Truthfulness on Mistral-instruct (0.386 vs. 0.335 for DiM), while DiM excels on base models (Compliance 0.692 on Llama-2 base, 0.904 on Mistral base) and PCA achieves the highest Appropriateness (0.414) and Compliance (1.000) on Mistral base. A key finding contradicts prior work: higher detection accuracy does not predict better guidability for any concept except truthfulness, as shown in the detection-vs-guidance scatter plot (Fig. 7). The paper also demonstrates concept confusion — guiding along the appropriateness direction actually controls compliance rather than appropriateness per se, echoing similar observations by Zou et al. (2023) with happiness/compliance. Overall, the comparison reveals that guidability is highly concept- and model-specific, with no universal best probe.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously studied-in-isolation linear probing techniques (logistic regression from Li et al. 2023 and Burns et al. 2022, difference-in-means from Marks & Tegmark 2023, and PCA from Zou et al. 2023) under a single experimental framework for concept detection and activation steering. While prior work has overwhelmingly focused on truthfulness as the target concept (largely because TruthfulQA provides a ready-made dataset), this paper deliberately extends the analysis to appropriateness, humor, creativity, and quality, drawing on OpenAssistant annotations and a newly constructed ToxicChat-based dataset. The guidance update rule (add/subtract the learned direction with norm preservation) and accuracy-based layer selection are inherited directly from Li et al. (2023), while the PNES metric is a novel contribution that has no direct predecessor in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that optimal detection accuracy does not predict optimal guidability (with truthfulness as a notable exception)—challenges a working assumption in the field and suggests that the relationship between detectability and guidability is concept-dependent in ways not yet understood. The paper also documents concept confusion (the appropriateness vector effectively controls compliance), reinforcing that linear directions in activation space are not as cleanly separable as the linear representation hypothesis might suggest. Open questions include what structural properties of a concept determine its guidability, whether more sophisticated interventions (varying strength per layer, non-linear probes) can close the gap, and how to scale beyond the exhaustive manual tuning that currently makes each concept require separate attention. The PNES metric and the multi-concept testbed provide a useful foundation for future systematic studies.
