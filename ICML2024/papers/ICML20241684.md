## Problem

Multi-agent reinforcement learning with adaptivity constraints—where the number of policy updates (batch complexity) must be minimized due to costly deployments—has only been studied in single-agent MDPs. This paper asks whether a self-play algorithm can simultaneously achieve near-optimal regret and near-optimal batch complexity in two-player zero-sum Markov games, a question that was previously open.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a two-player zero-sum episodic Markov game with known reward function r, unknown transition kernel P, state space S, action spaces A and B, horizon H, and a total episode budget K. Internally it runs general history-dependent (non-Markov) policies for exploration, but the final output is a single Markov policy for each player that is ε-approximate Nash. The key constraint is that policy updates occur only at O(H + log log K) pre-determined checkpoints rather than after every episode, and the regret guarantee is Õ(√(H²S²ABT)) where T = HK.

## Method

Algorithm 1 is a policy-elimination method that partitions K episodes into O(log log K) stages of increasing length T^(k) = K^(1−1/2^k). Each stage performs three steps: (1) Crude Exploration visits each (h,s,a,b) tuple layer-by-layer using greedy policies under an intermediate model to identify infrequent tuples and construct an absorbing Markov game that replaces hard-to-reach transitions with an absorbing state; (2) Fine Exploration runs a uniformly explorative mixture policy (solved via a min-max optimization over the version space) to obtain a multiplicatively accurate empirical transition kernel; (3) Policy Elimination removes max-player policies whose pessimistic value estimate falls below the current best by a confidence-bound-dependent gap. The absorbing MG construction is the key technical device that decouples the estimation problem from the exploration problem, ensuring uniform value-function accuracy across all remaining policies with only O(H + log log K) total policy switches.

## Results

For two-player zero-sum Markov games, Algorithm 1 achieves a regret of $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{H^2 S^2 ABT})$ (optimal in total steps $T=HK$) with batch complexity $O(H + \log\log K)$, which is near-optimal given the matching lower bound $\Omega(H/\log_A K + \log\log K)$. In the bandit game special case ($H=S=1$), the regret reduces to $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{ABK})$ with $O(\log\log K)$ batches. For reward-free exploration, the sample complexity is $\widetilde{O}(H^3 S^2 AB/\epsilon^2)$ with $O(H)$ batch complexity, improving the $H$-dependence by a factor of $H^2$ over prior low-adaptivity methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to fully-adaptive multi-agent algorithms (VI-ULCB, Nash VI), our method achieves the same or better regret order while reducing batch complexity from $K$ (one policy update per episode) to $O(H + \log\log K)$, a dramatic improvement in adaptivity. Against the single-agent batched RL result of Zhang et al. (2022), our algorithm matches the $O(H + \log\log K)$ batch complexity but extends to the multi-agent setting ($B > 1$) at the cost of an extra $\sqrt{S}$ factor in regret. For bandit games, our result strictly generalizes BaSE (Gao et al., 2019): plugging in $B=1$ recovers the same $\tilde{O}(\sqrt{AK})$ regret and $O(\log\log K)$ batch complexity, while for $B>1$ it provides the first multi-player guarantee. In the reward-free setting, our Algorithm 4 improves the sample complexity from $\tilde{O}(H^5 S^2 A/\epsilon^2)$ (LARFE, single-agent) to $\tilde{O}(H^3 S^2 AB/\epsilon^2)$ (multi-agent), an $H^2$ improvement in the horizon dependence, while maintaining the optimal $O(H)$ batch complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-agent batched RL framework (Qiao et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2022) to the two-player zero-sum Markov game setting, generalizing the absorbing Markov game construction and the arm-elimination batch schedule (Cesa-Bianchi et al., 2013) from single-agent MDPs to multi-agent self-play. The core algorithmic components—Crude Exploration, Fine Exploration, and policy elimination—are direct multi-agent generalizations of their single-agent counterparts, with the key technical challenge being the larger joint policy space Π_A × Π_B and the need to handle the min-player's best response. The bandit game result (Theorem 5.1) also generalizes the batched multi-armed bandit algorithm of Gao et al. (2019) from B=1 to the full two-player game setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provably-efficient algorithm for multi-agent RL under adaptivity constraints, closing a gap where all prior low-adaptivity results were restricted to single-agent MDPs. The near-optimal batch complexity (O(H + log log K)) with a matching lower bound up to logarithmic factors provides a clean theoretical characterization of the fundamental tradeoff between regret and policy update frequency in competitive settings. Key open questions remain: the √S gap in regret versus the information-theoretic lower bound, the lack of computational efficiency (the algorithm requires enumerating over policy spaces), and the extension to function approximation or non-zero-sum games. The natural extension to reward-free exploration with O(H) batch complexity also improves prior single-agent results by H² in sample complexity.
