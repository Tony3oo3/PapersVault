## Problem

Existing hierarchical time series reconciliation methods pre-specify the projection matrix (e.g., orthogonal projection with W=I), treating all base forecast errors equally and ignoring the hierarchy structure. Oblique projections that assign data-dependent weights to individual series offer greater flexibility, but prior implementations like MinT require estimating error covariances that are infeasible to obtain for multi-step horizons. This paper asks whether the optimal oblique projection can be learned directly from data within an end-to-end neural forecasting framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n-dimensional hierarchical time series vectors y_t (comprising m bottom-level and n−m aggregated-level series) along with the binary aggregation matrix S ∈ {0,1}^{n×m} that encodes the hierarchy. It outputs coherent forecasts—either point predictions or full predictive distributions (Gaussian with mean μ and diagonal covariance Σ)—that are guaranteed to satisfy the aggregation constraint y = Sb by construction, since the reconciled forecast is always of the form SP·f_θ(y_{1:t−1}). The projection layer operates on the raw n-dimensional base forecast vector and maps it through P ∈ R^{m×n} before re-expanding via S.

## Method

The reconciliation step is modeled as a learnable, structured, dense neural network layer P appended to a neural forecaster backbone (TimesNet or Autoformer), so that both the forecaster parameters θ and the projection P are optimized simultaneously via SGD. Two projection families are considered: (1) a generalized Euclidean projection where P = (S^T W S)^{−1}(S^T W) and W is enforced symmetric positive-definite via the decomposition W = (Q + Q^T)/2 with Q parameterized through the Lezcano-Casado eigenvalue-like factorization; and (2) a general oblique projection where P is an unconstrained m×n dense layer whose idempotence (PS = I) is enforced through a Lagrange-multiplier penalty λ‖PS − I‖_F² added to the forecasting loss. The framework extends to probabilistic forecasting by applying the projection to Monte Carlo samples drawn from the predicted distribution (via the reparameterization trick) before computing the negative log-likelihood. Theoretical guarantees include unbiasedness preservation for both projection types and the property that the generalized Euclidean projection never increases error under the W-induced norm.

## Results

On RMSE (the primary metric), the proposed GenProj method achieves the best performance across all five datasets for both backbone models. With TimesNet, GenProj reaches 10.12 on Labour, 2.14 on Traffic, 613.95 on Wiki, 414.33 on Tourism, and 110.82 on TourismLarge. With Autoformer, GenProj achieves 21.96 on Labour, 0.71 on Traffic, 641.63 on Wiki, 413.18 on Tourism, and 115.17 on TourismLarge. The generalized Euclidean projection (Eucl) also consistently outperforms all sequential reconciliation baselines (BU, TD, MinT variants, ERM) and the end-to-end Hier-E2E baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against seven categories of baselines: Naive/SeasonalNaive, Bottom-Up, Top-Down, MinT (ols/shr/var), ERM, and the end-to-end Hier-E2E (Rangapuram et al., 2021). The proposed GenProj method wins on all five datasets for both TimesNet and Autoformer backbones, outperforming the strongest sequential baseline (MinT-ols or MinT-var depending on dataset) by 1–10% in RMSE and the end-to-end Hier-E2E baseline by 3–12% (e.g., 10.12 vs. 10.51 on Labour with TimesNet; 21.96 vs. 24.94 on Labour with Autoformer). The generalized Euclidean projection (Eucl) also beats all sequential methods and Hier-E2E but is slightly outperformed by GenProj, confirming that the less-constrained general oblique projection offers the highest adaptability. Notably, the unconstrained 'Gen' variant (λ=0, no idempotence constraint) is competitive but consistently slightly worse than GenProj, empirically validating the benefit of enforcing the projection structure. The ERM baseline performs poorly on Labour and TourismLarge (e.g., 22.73 vs. 10.12 RMSE on Labour with TimesNet), while Top-Down reconciliation degrades performance on Wiki (730.95 vs. 613.95), highlighting the limitations of pre-specified reconciliation strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the geometric reconciliation framework of Panagiotelis et al. (2021), which unified OLS and MinT reconciliation as specific instances of projections, by making the projection matrix itself a learnable component of a neural forecaster. Where Rangapuram et al. (2021) introduced the first end-to-end hierarchical forecaster with a fixed orthogonal projection, and Wickramasuriya et al. (2019) required infeasible multi-step error covariance estimation for MinT, this work learns a structured oblique projection (generalized Euclidean or general) jointly with the backbone forecaster via SGD. The positive-definite matrix parameterization draws on Lezcano-Casado (2019) trivializations, and the idempotence constraint for the general projection is enforced through a Lagrange penalty on ||PS − I||_F.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The learnable oblique projection addresses a key practical bottleneck: MinT's reliance on estimated error covariances becomes infeasible for multi-step horizons, while orthogonal projection treats all base forecast errors equally regardless of hierarchy structure. By learning the projection end-to-end, the method adaptively weights harder-to-predict series (evidenced by higher spectral norms on noisier datasets) without requiring explicit covariance estimation. Open questions include scalability to very large hierarchies (the TourismLarge dataset with 76,304 bottom-level series is the largest tested), whether the Lagrange multiplier for the general projection can be made adaptive rather than fixed at 10^4, and whether the W-induced norm advantage of the generalized Euclidean projection translates to the actual loss of interest in all practical settings.
