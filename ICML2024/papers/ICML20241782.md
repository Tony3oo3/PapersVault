## Problem

Standard Q-learning introduces an uncontrollable overestimation bias in the max-expectation term, while Double Q-learning introduces an equally uncontrollable underestimation bias. Neither bias can be tuned to match the environment's preference, and existing remedies (Maxmin, REDQ) either require many estimates or lack principled control. The paper asks whether one can design a Q-learning variant where the estimation bias is explicitly and precisely adjustable via a hyperparameter while preserving convergence guarantees and computational cost comparable to vanilla Q-learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single asynchronous trajectory of state-action pairs (S_n, A_n) with transitions to S_{n+1} and rewards r(S_n, A_n), processed through a linear feature map φ(s,a) ∈ ℝ^d. It maintains N parallel parameter vectors θ_n^{(i)} and outputs the empirical average θ̂_{N,n} = (1/N)Σθ_n^{(i)}, from which the greedy policy π*(s) = argmax_a φ(s,a)ᵀθ̂ is extracted. In the tabular case (d = |S×A| with canonical basis), the output is an exact Q-function; in the function-approximation case it is a d-dimensional parameter vector.

## Method

2RA Q-learning replaces the standard max-expectation term max_a φ(s',a)ᵀθ_n with a distributionally robust estimator: max_{a'∈A} min_{θ'∈B_ρ(θ̂_{N,n})} φ(s',a')ᵀθ', where B_ρ is an ℓ₂-ball of radius √ρ centered at the empirical average of N parallel Q-estimates. By a closed-form solution (Lemma 3.1), this reduces to max_{a'} {φ(s',a')ᵀθ̂_{N,n} − √ρ‖φ(s',a')‖₂}, making each update computationally equivalent to one Watkins' Q-learning step. The parameter ρ directly bounds the underestimation bias (Theorem 3.2), while N controls the variance of the average; setting ρ→0 and N→∞ recovers an unbiased estimator. The learning rate is set to N times the standard Q-learning rate, which Theorem 3.3 shows yields the same asymptotic mean-squared error as Watkins' Q-learning, and Theorem 3.1 guarantees almost-sure convergence to the optimal Q-function for any N and any ρ_n→0.

## Results

On CartPole (1000 experiments), 2RA Q-learning achieves the best mean hit time of 386.19±133.47 episodes, outperforming Double Q-learning (401.89±144.43), Averaged Q-learning (404.09±124.19), Watkins' Q-learning (457.35±128.18), and Maxmin Q-learning (645.02±270.01). On Baird's Example, all methods converge to the same asymptotic mean-squared error (AMSE), consistent with Theorem 3.3, but 2RA achieves faster convergence in the early learning phase. In random MDP environments, 2RA consistently outperforms baselines in early-stage learning, with performance gains correlating with environments that favor underestimation bias.

## Baselines & Comparisons

2RA Q-learning outperforms all four baselines on CartPole, achieving a mean hit time of 386.19 episodes versus 401.89 for Double Q-learning, 404.09 for Averaged Q-learning, 457.35 for Watkins' Q-learning, and 645.02 for Maxmin Q-learning. Against Double Q-learning, 2RA's advantage is modest (~16 episodes) but consistent, reflecting that both methods benefit from underestimation bias in CartPole. The gap over Maxmin Q-learning is substantial (~259 episodes), suggesting Maxmin's fixed N=10 selection strategy is less effective than 2RA's tunable ρ and N parameters. On Baird's Example, 2RA matches the AMSE of Watkins' and Double Q-learning (as predicted by Theorem 3.3) but converges faster in the early phase; however, Variance Reduced Q-learning (Wainwright, 2019) outperforms 2RA in this specific instance, which the authors attribute to the particular structure of Baird's environment. In random MDP environments, 2RA consistently leads in early-stage learning, and its performance advantage correlates with Double Q-learning's advantage, indicating environments where underestimation bias is beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Q-learning lineage by combining two previously separate ideas—averaging multiple Q-function estimates (as in Maxmin Q-learning and Averaged-DQN) and distributionally robust optimization (as in Nguyen et al., 2021, and distributionally robust Q-learning of Liu et al., 2022)—into a single update rule. Unlike Double Q-learning, which fixes the bias direction to underestimation, or Maxmin Q-learning, which requires large N to remove overestimation, 2RA introduces a Wasserstein-ball regularizer whose radius ρ directly controls the magnitude and sign of the estimation bias. The method collapses to Watkins' Q-learning when ρ=0 and N=1, and its closed-form robust estimator (Lemma 3.1) keeps per-iteration cost comparable to the original algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The principal value is a principled, two-knob mechanism (ρ and N) for tuning estimation bias without sacrificing convergence guarantees or computational efficiency, which is useful given that the optimal bias direction is environment-dependent and no general theory yet predicts it. Limitations include theoretical results restricted to the tabular setting, the need for ρ_n → 0 for convergence (which limits the practical regularization window), and the observation that choosing N too large with the N-scaled learning rate can cause numerical instability. An open question the authors flag is whether the environment-dependent bias preference can be characterized a priori, which would remove the need for manual hyperparameter tuning.
