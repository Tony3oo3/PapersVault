## Problem

Existing best-of-both-worlds (BOBW) algorithms for partial monitoring achieve highly suboptimal stochastic regret bounds: O(k⁴m² log T log(kT)/Δ) that scale with log²T, depend on the minimum suboptimality gap Δ rather than action-wise gaps Δₐ, and carry a k⁴ dependence on the number of actions. No prior BOBW algorithm for PM achieves an O(log T) stochastic bound, and the exploration-by-optimization (ExO) framework, which is essential for handling the complex feedback structure of PM, had only been analyzed with the Shannon entropy regularizer. This paper closes the gap by introducing a hybrid regularizer into ExO and providing a new analysis that yields near-optimal stochastic bounds while preserving adversarial guarantees.

## Input / Output

At the start of the game, the learner is given the loss matrix L ∈ [0,1]^{k×d} and feedback matrix Φ ∈ Σ^{k×d}, but during each round t the learner only observes a feedback symbol σₜ = Φ_{Aₜxₜ} (not the outcome xₜ or the incurred loss L_{Aₜxₜ}). The learner outputs an action Aₜ ∈ [k] sampled from a probability vector pₜ that is computed by solving a convex optimization (the ExO subproblem) at each round, which depends on the FTRL output qₜ, the current learning rate βₜ, and the loss-difference estimator Gₜ. The key structural constraint is that pₜ must lie in the restricted feasible region R(qₜ) = {p ∈ P_k : pₐ ≥ qₜₐ/2 for all a}, ensuring the self-bounding technique applies while remaining independent of k.

## Method

The algorithm uses FTRL with a hybrid regularizer ψ(q) = Σₐ βₐ[(φ^LB(qₐ)+qₐ−1) + γ(φ^CNS(qₐ)+qₐ)] combining the log-barrier and complement negative Shannon entropy, with action-dependent learning rates βₜₐ that adapt to the FTRL output. The FTRL output qₜ is transformed into the action selection probability pₜ by solving the ExO optimization over the restricted feasible region R(q) = {p : pₐ ≥ qₐ/2}, which is independent of k (unlike the prior P'(q) = {p : pₐ ≥ qₐ/(2k)}). The key technical contribution is Lemma 4, which bounds the optimal value of this ExO problem by 2m²k² Σ_b (1/β_b) min{q_b, (1−q_b)/(γq_b)} using a feasible solution p^H = (q + W_ν(q))/2 constructed from the water transfer operator, avoiding the uniform-distribution mixing that previously forced k-dependent bounds. This q-dependent bound is essential for the self-bounding argument: combined with the lower bound Reg_T ≥ (1/2)Σ_{a≠a*} Δₐ Qₐ, it yields the O(Σ_{a≠a*} k²m² log T/Δₐ) stochastic regret for locally observable games and the first O(c_G²k log T/Δ²) stochastic bound for globally observable games.

## Results

For locally observable games, the proposed algorithm achieves a stochastic regret bound of O(Σ_{a≠a*} k²m² log T / Δₐ), which is roughly Θ(k² log T) times smaller than the existing BOBW bound of O(k⁴m² log T log(kT)/Δ). In adversarial environments, the bound is O(k^{3/2} m √(T log T)), improving the existing BOBW bound by a factor of O(√(log k)). For globally observable games, the paper provides the first BOBW algorithm with an O(log T) stochastic regret bound of O(c_G² k log T / Δ²), while maintaining the minimax-optimal O((c_G T)^{2/3}) adversarial rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against ExpPM-BOBW (Tsuchiya et al., 2023a), the existing BOBW algorithm for PM. For locally observable games, the proposed method improves the stochastic bound from O(k⁴m² log T log(kT)/Δ) to O(Σ k²m² log T/Δₐ), eliminating the log²T dependence, reducing the k-dependence from k⁴ to k², and replacing the worst-case gap Δ with action-wise gaps Δₐ. The adversarial bound improves from O(k^{3/2} m √(T log T log k)) to O(k^{3/2} m √(T log T)), removing the √(log k) factor. For globally observable games, the paper achieves the first O(log T) stochastic BOBW bound (previously O(log T log(kT)/Δ²)), at the cost of an additional factor of k in the dependence. Against ExpPM (Lattimore & Szepesvári, 2020b), the proposed method matches or improves the adversarial rate while adding stochastic guarantees. Against TSPM and PM-DMED (stochastic-only baselines), direct comparison is difficult since TSPM applies to a linearized variant and PM-DMED's constant D(ν*) has unknown dependence on k, m, and d.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the exploration-by-optimization (ExO) framework of Lattimore & Szepesvári (2020b) for adversarial partial monitoring by replacing the Shannon entropy regularizer with a hybrid regularizer (log-barrier + complement negative Shannon entropy) previously developed for simpler settings like multi-armed bandits and combinatorial semi-bandits (Ito, 2021a; Ito et al., 2022b). The key departure from the prior BOBW algorithm for PM (Tsuchiya et al., 2023a) is twofold: (1) the hybrid regularizer enables O(log T) rather than O(log² T) stochastic bounds and action-wise gap dependence, and (2) a new analysis of the ExO optimal value over a k-independent feasible region R(q) (rather than the k-dependent P'(q)) removes a factor of k from the regret bound. The water transfer operator and self-bounding technique are inherited from the existing PM and BOBW literature, but their combination with the hybrid regularizer required non-trivial new analysis to handle the complex feedback structure of PM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a significant gap in the PM literature by providing the first BOBW algorithms with O(log T) stochastic regret for both locally and globally observable games, matching known lower bounds in T. The Θ(k² log T) improvement over prior BOBW bounds is practically meaningful for problems with many actions. However, the dependence on k remains suboptimal (k² for locally observable, k for globally observable), and the paper explicitly acknowledges that the true minimax dependence on k, d, and m is still unknown. Open questions include whether the hybrid regularizer can be further refined to tighten k-dependence, whether the ExO analysis extends to degenerate or duplicate-action games, and whether the blackbox conversion framework of Dann et al. (2023) can be adapted to PM.
