## Problem

Existing perspective-invariant learning approaches either require costly multi-view image collection with consistent annotations or rely on rigid data augmentations (rotation, flipping) that fail to capture the diverse, dynamic viewpoint changes encountered in real-world scenarios like UAV flights, auto-driving, and daily photos. This paper addresses the gap by enabling multi-perspective fusion learning entirely within the latent feature space using only conventional single-view images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240871/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-view RGB image as input and produces task-specific outputs: pixel-wise segmentation masks or object detection bounding boxes. The DLPL framework is plug-and-play, inserting four DLPL blocks into the encoder of existing ViT-based segmentors (SegFormer, UperNet, PoolFormer, SETR) or detectors (DETR, Deformable DETR, Sparse DETR) with fewer than 3M additional parameters. A pretrained SuperPoint network provides auxiliary point-ness maps during the PDD stage, but the final inference pipeline only requires the single input image.

## Method

DLPL decomposes perspective learning into three stages operating in latent feature space. First, Perspective Discrete Decomposition (PDD) extracts a point-ness map via SuperPoint, applies point-ness attention to visual features, discretizes the result into M levels with a differentiable spike-shaped function, encodes spatial co-occurring pixel relationships, and aggregates them into a perspective distribution graph P via multi-head attention. Second, a Perspective Embedding Space P is maintained via EMA over training batches and modeled as a Gaussian Mixture Model; Perspective Homography Transformation (PHT) then generates a transformed perspective P' by applying a weighted sum of Homography matrices between P and each GMM component, and a Visual Reconstruction (VR) module decodes P' back into visual features I'_rec supervised by a reconstruction loss. Third, multi-level Perspective Invariant Attention (PIA) performs cross-attention between the original features I and the perspective-transformed features I'_rec to fuse perspective-invariant semantics. Training uses a combined loss L = L_task + 0.4·L_rec, with PHT activated only after the first 30% of epochs once the embedding space stabilizes.

## Results

On UAV segmentation, DLPL (SegFormer) achieves 76.88% mIoU on UDD6, 70.60% on iSAID, 65.30% on UAVid, 68.22% on Aeroscapes, and 59.10% on the newly proposed DroneSeg dataset, outperforming the plain SegFormer by 2.6–3.8% mIoU across all five benchmarks. On UAV detection, DLPL (Sparse DETR) reaches 28.75 AP on VisDrone-dev and 65.57 AP on UAVDT-test, surpassing the plain Sparse DETR by 1.77 and 2.36 AP respectively. On Cityscapes and ADE20K, DLPL (SegFormer MiT-B5) attains 85.1% and 53.7% mIoU, improving over the plain SegFormer by 1.1% and 1.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DLPL consistently outperforms all compared baselines when integrated with the same backbone. Against SegFormer (Xie et al., NeurIPS 2021), DLPL gains 2.6% mIoU on UDD6 (76.88 vs 74.28), 3.4% on iSAID (70.60 vs 67.19), and 3.8% on DroneSeg (59.10 vs 55.33). Against SETR (Zheng et al., CVPR 2021), DLPL (SETR) improves UDD6 mIoU from 68.00 to 72.62 (+4.62%), though it slightly underperforms plain SETR on iSAID (65.45 vs 62.77) and UAVid (60.23 vs 58.52) — actually it still wins on those. Against Deeplab (Chen et al., ECCV 2018) and OCRNet (Yuan et al., ECCV 2020), DLPL (SegFormer) leads by 5.0% and 3.5% mIoU on UDD6 respectively. In detection, DLPL (DETR) surpasses plain DETR (Carion et al., ECCV 2020) by 2.13 AP on VisDrone-dev (26.45 vs 24.32). Compared to AutoAugmentation (Cubuk et al., CVPR 2019) as a perspective-oriented learning strategy, DLPL achieves 58.67% mIoU on DroneSeg versus 55.80% for AutoAugmentation, a 2.87% gap, and remains compatible with data augmentation for further gains (59.10% with Random Combination).

## Relation to Prior Work

DLPL carves new ground by repurposing classical homography theory and statistical texture decomposition (from Zhu et al. 2021a, Ji et al. 2022) into a latent-space perspective representation, rather than operating on raw pixels or relying on multi-view data collection. It departs from the two dominant prior strategies—multi-view dataset construction (Yu et al., 2023) and rigid data augmentation (Cubuk et al., 2019)—by learning a discrete perspective embedding via SuperPoint-guided decomposition and transforming it through a GMM-weighted homography matrix. The framework is architecture-agnostic, plugging into existing ViT segmentors (SegFormer, UperNet, PoolFormer, SETR) and detectors (DETR, Deformable DETR, Sparse DETR) as additional transformer blocks, rather than proposing a new backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: multi-view annotation is prohibitively expensive, and standard augmentations fail to capture the dynamic viewpoint changes in UAV and autonomous-driving scenarios. The consistent 2–5% mIoU gains across five UAV segmentation datasets, two detection benchmarks, and two driving/photo segmentation benchmarks—using fewer than 3M extra parameters—make DLPL a practical plug-in for existing pipelines. However, the method's reliance on a frozen SuperPoint network, the somewhat informal mathematical treatment of homography in latent feature space, and the limited exploration of extreme viewpoint changes (e.g., near-ground vs. high-altitude) leave open questions about robustness and generalizability beyond the tested scenarios.
