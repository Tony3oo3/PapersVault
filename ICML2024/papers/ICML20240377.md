## Problem

Standard diffusion transformers (e.g., DiT) scale quadratically O(n²) with pixel count, making direct pixel-space synthesis at megapixel resolutions computationally infeasible. Existing workarounds—latent diffusion, cascaded super-resolution, multi-scale losses, and self-conditioning—introduce architectural complexity, additional models, or quality degradation (e.g., VAE detail loss). The paper asks whether a pure transformer backbone can match U-Net efficiency for high-resolution pixel-space diffusion without these modifications.

## Input / Output

The model consumes Gaussian noise (optionally conditioned on a class label via a mapping network) and directly outputs RGB pixel-space images at resolutions from 128×128 up to 1024×1024, with no intermediate VAE encoding/decoding step. The architecture adapts to target resolution by adding one neighborhood-attention level per doubling in spatial size, keeping the innermost level at 16×16 tokens. Sampling uses 50 steps of DPM++(3M) SDE, and the model is trained with a continuous-time diffusion formulation on raw RGB pixels in [-1, 1].

## Method

HDiT replaces the flat transformer stack of DiT with a hierarchical hourglass structure: an encoder path progressively merges 2×2 tokens via Pixel-UnShuffle while a decoder path splits them back via Pixel-Shuffle, with learnable linear-interpolation skip connections at each level. Global self-attention is applied only at the lowest-resolution levels (where long-range coherence matters), while all higher-resolution levels use neighborhood attention, yielding O(n) asymptotic complexity in pixel count. Each transformer block uses adaptive RMSNorm (conditioned on class and timestep via a mapping network), GEGLU feedforward, axial RoPE positional encoding applied to half the query/key vectors, and scaled cosine-similarity attention. Training employs a Soft-Min-SNR loss weighting (a smoothed variant of Min-SNR with γ=4) and a Karras-style preconditioner, requiring no self-conditioning, progressive growing, or multi-scale losses to reach 1024×1024 pixel-space generation.

## Results

On FFHQ-1024², HDiT-85M achieves a FID of 5.23 (50k samples) and sets a new state-of-the-art for diffusion models with FD_D2 of 149 and KD_D2 of 0.354, substantially outperforming NCSN++ (FID 53.52). On ImageNet-256², the 557M-parameter HDiT achieves FID 6.92 and IS 135.2 without classifier-free guidance, outperforming DiT-XL/2 (FID 9.62) and ADM (FID 10.94) while operating directly in pixel space. The architecture achieves O(n) computational complexity scaling with pixel count, requiring only 198 GFLOPs at 512² versus 6,341 GFLOPs for DiT-B/4 at the same resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet-256², HDiT (FID 6.92) substantially outperforms DiT-XL/2 (9.62), LDM-4 (10.56), and ADM (10.94) despite operating in pixel space rather than compressed latents, and uses fewer parameters (557M vs. 675M+VAE for DiT-XL/2). However, HDiT is outperformed by the much larger 2B-parameter models simple diffusion (2.77) and VDM++ (2.40), as well as RIN (4.51) which uses self-conditioning during sampling. Among latent diffusion models, HDiT is competitive with U-ViT-H/2 (6.58) and MDT-XL/2 (6.23) but trails MaskDiT/2 (5.69). On FFHQ-1024², HDiT-85M (FID 5.23) dramatically outperforms NCSN++ (53.52) and is competitive with transformer GANs HiT-B (6.37) and StyleSwin (5.07), though it does not match the best GANs like StyleGAN-XL (2.02) or StyleGAN2 (2.70). Notably, HDiT achieves the best DINOv2-based Fréchet and Kernel distances among all diffusion models on FFHQ-1024², metrics that correlate better with human preference than FID.

## Relation to Prior Work

HDiT restructures the flat Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone into a hierarchical hourglass architecture inspired by Nawrot et al.'s hierarchical transformers for language, combining global attention at low resolutions with neighborhood attention at high resolutions to achieve O(n) rather than O(n²) scaling. It further departs from DiT's block design by adopting LLaMA-style components (adaptive RMSNorm, GEGLU, zero-initialized output projections) and replacing additive positional encodings with 2D axial RoPE. The training setup builds on Karras et al.'s preconditioned continuous-time diffusion formulation and introduces a smoothed Min-SNR loss weighting, but the core contribution is architectural: making a pure transformer competitive with convolutional U-Nets in computational complexity for pixel-space synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the VAE bottleneck, HDiT removes a fundamental quality ceiling on pixel-space diffusion (high-frequency detail loss, poor reconstruction for editing) and demonstrates that megapixel-scale generation is feasible without self-conditioning, multi-scale losses, or progressive growing. The 100× FLOP reduction over DiT at 1024² makes high-resolution pixel-space transformer diffusion practically viable, opening the door to more faithful image editing and potentially text-to-image and video applications. Open questions include whether the architecture can close the remaining quality gap to top GANs (StyleGAN-XL), whether combining HDiT with latent diffusion or self-conditioning yields further gains, and whether the fixed learning rate/batch size suboptimality observed in scaling experiments can be resolved via μP parametrization.
