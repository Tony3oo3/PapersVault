## Problem

Private k-means clustering has been studied under multiple privacy models (centralized DP, local DP, shuffle DP, MPC, continual observation), each yielding a distinct algorithm with its own delicate analysis. The paper asks whether a single algorithm can perform well across all these models, and answers affirmatively by showing that a 20-year-old greedy algorithm (Mettu & Plaxton, 2000) can be minimally modified to work uniformly in every setting where private clustering is known to be possible.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a multiset of points in the unit ball B_d(0,1) ⊂ R^d, a target number of clusters k, and privacy parameters (ε, δ), and outputs a set of k centers with cost bounded by M·OPT_{k,z} + A. Notably, the algorithm simultaneously produces valid solutions for all k' ≤ k at no additional privacy cost, enabling the elbow method as a free post-processing step. The quality guarantee is bi-criteria: a multiplicative approximation factor M (either O(1) or w*(1+α), where w* is the best non-private approximation ratio) and an additive error A that depends on the privacy model and parameters.

## Method

The core mechanism modifies the Mettu–Plaxton greedy procedure with two changes: (1) replacing data-dependent balls with a fixed family of balls centered on a net decomposition of the space, and (2) selecting balls with approximately maximum value (within additive error θ) rather than the exact maximum. This reduces private (k,z)-clustering to a 'generalized bucketized vector summation' subproblem, which is solved differently per privacy model: the exponential mechanism for centralized DP, private histograms for local/shuffle DP, and a binary-tree mechanism for continual observation. The O(1) approximation is then boosted to w*(1+α) via Nguyen's reduction (computing a solution with k' = k^{O_α(1)} centers and reducing), and the success probability is amplified from 2/3 to 1−β using a new k-means-specific cost estimation lemma that avoids expensive private cost queries. A structural lemma decomposes each cluster into a union of O(k·α^{-O(d)}·log n) pre-determined sets, enabling private cluster-size estimation via the same generalized summation oracle.

## Results

The paper's main meta-theorem (Informal Theorem 1.1) shows that a modified Mettu-Plaxton greedy algorithm achieves either O(1) multiplicative approximation with additive error ≈ k·polylog(n/β)·θ, or w*(1+α) multiplicative approximation with additive error ≈ √d·poly(k, log(n/β))·θ, where θ is the error of a private max-summation subroutine. In centralized (ε,δ)-DP, this yields O(1) approximation with k√d/ε additive error (matching the known lower bound) or w*(1+α) approximation with k^{O_α}(1)√d/ε additive error. In the MPC model, the paper achieves O(1) approximation with k√d/ε additive error, improving the previous best of k^{2.5}+k^{1.01}√d. The paper also presents the first result under continual observation, achieving w*(1+α) approximation with (k^{O_α}(1)+k√d)·log^{1.5}(T)/ε additive error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Ghazi et al. (2020) and Chaturvedi et al. (2021) in centralized DP, the paper matches the known bounds: O(1) approximation with k√d additive error (optimal per the Chaturvedi et al. lower bound) and w*(1+α) approximation with k^{O_α}(1)+k√d error. Against Chang et al. (2021) in Local and Shuffle DP (1 round), the paper improves the dependency on the success probability β from (k/β)^{O_α}(1) to k^{O_α}(1) for k-means, and extends the results from k-means to general (k,z)-clustering with w*(2^z+α) approximation, partially answering an open question from Chang et al. Against Cohen-Addad et al. (2022a) in the MPC model, the paper improves the additive error for O(1) approximation from k^{2.5}+k^{1.01}√d to k√d, achieving an optimal bound. The paper also presents the first result in the continual observation model, where no prior private clustering algorithm existed, achieving w*(1+α) approximation with a log^{1.5}(T) overhead over the static setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits the 20-year-old greedy algorithm of Mettu and Plaxton (2000) for (k,z)-clustering and shows that, with two key modifications (data-independent ball sets via nets, and approximate max-value selection), it becomes a unified differentially private algorithm across centralized DP, local DP, shuffle DP, MPC, and continual observation. Prior work had developed specialized algorithms per privacy model—iterative dense-ball selection for centralized DP (Stemmer & Kaplan 2018; Ghazi et al. 2020; Chaturvedi et al. 2021), histogram-based coresets for local/shuffle DP (Chang et al. 2021), and quadtree-plus-sampling for MPC (Cohen-Addad et al. 2022a)—each with its own delicate analysis. The paper's conceptual departure is reducing all of these to a single black-box requirement: a private generalized summation algorithm, with the structural insight that clusters can be decomposed into unions of pre-determined sets to enable private size estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification provides a 'go-to algorithm' for the 225+ known variations of differential privacy, lowering the barrier to studying new privacy models for clustering. It delivers the first private clustering result under continual observation and improves the probability dependency exponentially for local/shuffle DP while achieving optimal k-dependency in MPC. Limitations include the theoretical nature of the bounds (no empirical validation), the exponential-in-dimension dependency in the structured-cluster decomposition (Lemma 4.2), and the fact that the near-optimal additive error via the exponential mechanism (Section 5) is only shown for centralized DP and MPC, not for local or shuffle settings. Open questions include whether the unified framework extends to other clustering objectives (e.g., k-median with different distance metrics) or to streaming settings with more complex update patterns.
