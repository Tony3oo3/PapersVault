## Problem

Existing distributionally robust RL (DRRL) algorithms are either model-based, requiring estimation of the full transition model before constructing the ambiguity set, or rely on a simulator to draw arbitrary batches from any state-action pair. No prior work achieves a fully model-free DRRL algorithm that learns the optimal robust policy from a single incrementally arriving trajectory. The core difficulty is that the DR Bellman operator introduces a bi-level optimization (jointly solving for the dual variable η and the Q fixed point) whose nonlinearity makes the standard one-sample plug-in estimator used in non-robust Q-learning fundamentally biased.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a single online trajectory of (s, a, r, s′) transitions from a possibly misspecified MDP, with exactly one new sample observed per time step and no access to a simulator or batch queries. It outputs a distributionally robust Q-function Q(s,a) and the corresponding ε-greedy policy, where robustness is parameterized by the Cressie-Read f-divergence family index k (recovering χ² at k=2 and KL as k→1) and an ambiguity set radius ρ. The practical deep variant (DDRQ) replaces tabular Q and η with two-layer neural networks (120 hidden units) and uses a replay buffer of size 10⁶ with mini-batch size 4096.

## Method

The paper introduces DRQ, a three-timescale stochastic approximation algorithm that solves the Cressie-Read DR Bellman equation in a fully online, model-free manner. The fastest timescale incrementally estimates Z₁ = E[(η − max Q)₊^{k*}] and Z₂ = E[(η − max Q)₊^{k*−1}] to correct the bias arising from the nonlinearity E[f(X)] ≠ f(E[X]) in the dual gradient. The medium timescale updates the per-(s,a) dual variable η via stochastic gradient descent using the estimated Z₁, Z₂, while the slowest timescale updates the Q-table using the empirical DR Bellman target r − γ(c_k(ρ)Z₁^{1/k*} − η). The convergence proof generalizes the classical two-timescale ODE method of Borkar to a three-timescale setting, establishing almost-sure convergence to the optimal DR policy under standard stepsize conditions ζ₁ = o(ζ₂) = o(ζ₃). The dual reformulation from Duchi & Namkoong (2021) converts the inf over the ambiguity set into a sup over a scalar η, enabling the entire procedure to operate on a single sample per step without importance sampling or multi-level Monte Carlo.

## Results

In the Cliffwalking environment, DRQ with ρ=1.0 achieves the best robust returns across perturbation levels p∈{0.6, 0.7, 0.8}, while ρ=1.5 is preferred at extreme perturbation (p=0.9), significantly outperforming non-robust Q-learning (ρ=0) and R-contamination baselines. In terms of sample complexity, DRQ converges to the true DR value at a rate comparable to the model-based algorithm of Panaganti & Kalathil (2022), while Liu et al.'s multi-level Monte Carlo DRQ exhibits substantial deviation and converges orders of magnitude slower. In the American Put Option problem, DRQ significantly outperforms non-robust Q-learning when the market shifts to riskier conditions (p∈{0.6, 0.7}). The deep variant (DDRQ) achieves robust performance comparable to RFQI and soft-robust RL on CartPole and LunarLander, while non-robust DQN and FQI deteriorate sharply even under slight action perturbations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRQ clearly outperforms the R-contamination robust RL of Wang & Zou (2021), which performs similarly to or worse than non-robust Q-learning in most perturbation settings (p=0.8, 0.9) due to excessive conservatism. Against Liu et al.'s (2022) simulator-based DRQ, DRQ achieves substantially faster convergence to the true DR value, as Liu's multi-level Monte Carlo approach requires large batches per state-action pair and exhibits significant deviation from the optimal value. DRQ matches the sample efficiency of the model-based algorithm of Panaganti & Kalathil (2022) in both Cliffwalking and American Put Option, while being fully model-free and single-trajectory. In the deep learning experiments (Figure 5), DDRQ achieves comparable robust returns to RFQI (Panaganti et al., 2022) and soft-robust RL (Derman et al., 2018) on both CartPole and LunarLander, though RFQI underperforms on LunarLander likely due to its restriction to TV distance. Non-robust DQN (Mnih et al., 2015) and FQI fail to maintain performance under even mild action or physical environment perturbations, confirming the necessity of distributional robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Q-learning template (Tsitsiklis, 1994) to the distributionally robust setting by introducing a three-timescale stochastic approximation framework that generalizes the classical two-timescale machinery of Borkar (2009). The DR Bellman operator is constructed via the Cressie-Read family of f-divergences (Cressie & Read, 1984), which unifies KL and chi-squared divergences, and relies on the dual reformulation from Duchi & Namkoong (2021) to make the worst-case expectation tractable. The key departure from prior DRRL work (Zhou et al., 2021; Panaganti & Kalathil, 2022; Liu et al., 2022) is that all existing algorithms are either model-based or require a simulator with batch access to state-action pairs, whereas this paper achieves fully model-free learning from a single incrementally arriving trajectory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fully model-free DRRL algorithm trainable on a single trajectory, removing the need for environment modeling or simulator access that has limited the practical deployment of robust RL in large-scale or partially observable settings. The three-timescale convergence proof is of independent interest for the stochastic approximation community. Limitations include the primarily tabular focus (the DDRQ extension uses a simplified two-timescale update that may introduce bias in the dual variable), and experiments are confined to relatively small environments (Cliffwalking, CartPole, LunarLander, American Put Option). Open questions include whether the framework extends to other divergence families beyond Cressie-Read, whether the three-timescale structure is necessary in the deep learning setting, and how the approach scales to continuous or high-dimensional state spaces.
