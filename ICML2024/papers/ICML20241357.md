## Problem

Off-policy RL methods underutilize the heterogeneous data in the replay buffer, neglecting inherent patterns and knowledge from historical policies. While offline RL can extract an optimal policy from collected data, naively blending offline values with online values can be harmful when the offline policy is inferior to the online one. The paper asks: when and how can an offline optimal policy be adaptively leveraged to guarantee improvement in online off-policy RL?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241357/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives continuous state observations (up to 375 dimensions) from six task suites (Mujoco, DMControl, MetaWorld, Adroit, Myosuite, Maniskill2) and outputs continuous actions (up to 39 dimensions) via a Gaussian policy. The replay buffer stores (s, a, s', r) tuples collected by the online policy, which is simultaneously used to train both the online actor-critic and the offline optimal policy components. The offline optimal policy is never explicitly parameterized; instead, its value functions are approximated via expectile regression over the buffer, keeping the architecture to just two critics, one value network, and one actor (each a 2-layer MLP with 512 hidden units).

## Method

OBAC concurrently trains an offline optimal policy μ* (via IQL-style expectile regression with τ=0.9) on the same replay buffer as the online SAC-based policy π. The key mechanism is an adaptive constraint in the policy improvement step: at each state, if V^μ*(s) ≥ V^π(s), the actor update includes a behavior-cloning term (weighted by λ=0.001) that pulls the Gaussian policy toward the empirical action distribution in the buffer, effectively blending in the offline optimal behavior; otherwise, the constraint is disabled and standard off-policy updates proceed. This is derived from a constrained optimization problem solved via KKT conditions, yielding a closed-form policy that provably achieves monotonic Q-value improvement and convergence to the optimal policy. The implicit formulation avoids explicitly learning μ*, keeping computational cost comparable to standard model-free RL while achieving performance rivaling model-based methods like TD-MPC2 with 50% fewer parameters and 20% less training time.

## Results

OBAC is evaluated on 53 continuous control tasks across 6 domains (Mujoco, DMControl, MetaWorld, Adroit, Myosuite, Maniskill2), with results presented as learning curves over 1M environment steps. OBAC consistently outperforms all model-free baselines (SAC, TD3, BAC) and achieves performance comparable to the model-based TD-MPC2, while using only 50% of TD-MPC2's parameters and requiring 20% less wall-clock training time per 1M steps. Notably, OBAC successfully solves the challenging Baoding-4th multi-body manipulation task without any prior demonstrations, and exhibits lower performance decline under action noise (e.g., 11.11% decline on Hammer at σ=0.1 vs. 16.67% for BAC and 12.5% for SAC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018) and TD3 (Fujimoto et al., 2018), OBAC achieves higher asymptotic returns and faster convergence across all six task suites, with particularly large gaps on high-dimensional locomotion (DogRun) and muscle control (myoHand) tasks. Compared to BAC (Ji et al., 2023), the closest model-free baseline that also leverages offline Q-values, OBAC outperforms by adaptively activating the offline policy constraint only when the offline optimal policy is superior, avoiding the performance degradation that BAC suffers when the offline policy is suboptimal (early training). Against TD-MPC2 (Hansen et al., 2024), the strongest model-based baseline, OBAC achieves comparable final performance on most suites (DMControl, MetaWorld, Adroit, Maniskill2, Myosuite) with a simpler architecture (two critics, one value, one actor vs. dynamics model + reward model + 5 ensemble critics), though TD-MPC2 struggles on Mujoco tasks due to early episode termination from the done signal. In noise-robustness experiments (σ=0.1), OBAC shows the lowest performance decline on the Pen task (25% vs. 66.67% for BAC and 70% for SAC), confirming the benefit of the adaptive constraint mechanism under stochasticity.

## Relation to Prior Work

OBAC sits at the intersection of off-policy RL (SAC, TD3) and offline RL (IQL), building on the observation that a replay buffer implicitly encodes an offline optimal policy. It departs from prior work that naively interpolates offline Q-values into online value estimation (BAC/Ji et al. 2023, Zhang et al. 2022a) by introducing an adaptive, value-comparison-gated constraint that only activates when the offline policy demonstrably outperforms the online one. The implicit offline policy learning via expectile regression (from IQL) avoids the computational cost of explicitly learning a separate offline actor, while the KKT-derived closed-form solution provides a principled policy improvement step that generalizes standard actor-critic updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: off-policy RL methods underutilize the heterogeneous data in their replay buffers, and prior attempts to incorporate offline RL signals can be harmful when the offline policy is inferior. OBAC's adaptive gating mechanism offers a clean solution, and the empirical demonstration across 53 tasks in 6 domains (matching TD-MPC2 with 50% fewer parameters and 20% less training time) makes it a practical alternative to model-based methods. Limitations include the reliance on a fixed behavior-clone weight λ, the implicit (not explicit) offline policy approximation, and the acknowledged brittleness of RL training for real-world deployment. Open questions include whether more sophisticated offline RL methods or enhanced online exploration could further close the gap to model-based approaches.
