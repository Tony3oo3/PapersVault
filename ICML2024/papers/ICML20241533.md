## Problem

Existing molecular property prediction methods treat 2D molecular graphs and 3D conformer structures in isolation, and when multiple conformers are used, the computational cost of generating and aggregating them scales poorly. There is no principled, E(3)-invariant mechanism to aggregate an ensemble of 3D conformers with 2D graph information while preserving geometric structure, and prior conformer-ensemble approaches require large numbers of conformers (e.g., 200) to achieve competitive accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 2D molecular graph (atom types, bond orders, stereochemistry from SMILES via RDKit) and K 3D conformers generated by distance-geometry sampling with force-field refinement (typically K=5–10, far fewer than the 200 used by baselines). Each conformer provides Cartesian coordinates and atomic numbers; the 2D graph provides node features (atomic number, chirality, degree, hybridization, etc.) and edge features (bond type, stereo, conjugation). The output is a scalar molecular property (regression: logP, solubility, binding affinity) or a binary classification score (SARS-CoV-2 inhibition), with the prediction being invariant to both E(3) transformations of individual conformers and permutations of the conformer set.

## Method

CONAN integrates three components: a 2D GAT encoder for molecular graph features, a SchNet-based E(3)-invariant MPNN for per-conformer 3D embeddings, and a novel Fused Gromov-Wasserstein (FGW) barycenter aggregation that computes a structure-preserving 'geometric mean' of the K conformer graphs by jointly minimizing FGW distances over both learned feature matrices and pairwise distance matrices. The barycenter is solved via an entropic relaxation with Sinkhorn projections, enabling vectorized GPU computation and multi-GPU distributed training. The final prediction linearly combines 2D, 3D, and barycenter features (with a tunable weight γ on the barycenter term) followed by mean aggregation over conformers. A key theoretical result establishes that the empirical FGW barycenter converges to the true barycenter at rate O(1/K), justifying the use of small K values (5–10) for accurate approximation.

## Results

CONAN-FGW achieves the best RMSE on all four MoleculeNet regression benchmarks: 0.487 on Lipo, 0.529 on ESOL, 1.068 on FreeSolv, and 0.549 on BACE, using only 3–5 conformers per molecule. On the CoV-2 3CL classification task, CONAN-FGW reaches 0.918 ROC with just 5 conformers, surpassing CP3D-NDU (0.916 ROC) which requires 200 conformers. On the BDE reaction-prediction benchmark, CONAN-FGW with a LEFTNet backbone achieves 1.4829 MAE using only 10 conformers, outperforming LEFTNet alone (1.5276) and SchNet (1.9737) which both use 20 conformers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CONAN-FGW outperforms all 17 baselines across every evaluated dataset. Against the strongest 2D-only methods, it reduces RMSE by 26% over ChemRL-GEM on Lipo (0.487 vs. 0.660) and by 42% over MolFormer on FreeSolv (1.068 vs. 2.342). Against the best 3D conformer-based methods, it improves over UniMol by 19% on Lipo (0.487 vs. 0.603) and over SchNet-emb by 33% on FreeSolv (1.068 vs. 1.587), while using far fewer conformers (3–5 vs. 11 for UniMol). On the CoV-2 3CL classification task, CONAN-FGW achieves the highest ROC (0.918) with only 5 conformers, beating CP3D-NDU (0.916) which requires 200 conformers, and substantially outperforming ConfNet (0.493 ROC). On the BDE benchmark, CONAN-FGW with LEFTNet backbone achieves 1.4829 MAE with 10 conformers, improving over LEFTNet alone (1.5276) and dramatically over SchNet (1.9737), both of which use 20 conformers. The ablation variant CONAN (without FGW barycenter) still beats most baselines, confirming that the barycenter aggregation provides the largest single gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

CONAN extends the 2D-3D molecular representation learning line (Axelrod & Gomez-Bombarelli 2023; Zhu et al. 2023) by replacing simple conformer aggregation (averaging, attention) with a Fused Gromov-Wasserstein barycenter, drawing on the FGW framework of Titouan et al. (2019) and entropic OT solvers from Rioux et al. (2023) and Cuturi (2013). The 3D conformer encoding uses SchNet (Schütt et al., 2017) and the 2D graph uses GAT (Veličković et al., 2018), both standard components. The key conceptual departure is treating conformer aggregation as a structure-preserving optimal transport barycenter problem rather than a feature-level pooling operation, and providing the first O(1/K) convergence guarantee for empirical FGW barycenters (previously known only for Wasserstein space, Le Gouic et al. 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is that structure-aware aggregation via FGW barycenters allows CONAN-FGW to match or exceed methods using 200 conformers while requiring only 5–10, significantly reducing the computational bottleneck of 3D conformer generation. The O(1/K) convergence rate provides a theoretical basis for choosing small K, and the entropic solver with multi-GPU parallelism makes the approach scalable to large datasets like BDE. Open questions include whether more accurate conformer relaxation (e.g., DFT) would further improve results, whether the FGW barycenter advantage persists at much larger conformer counts, and whether the approach extends to self-supervised or foundation-model settings for molecular representation learning.
