## Problem

The dominant EnCD framework (encoder-only model + coordinate denoising) for 3D molecular representation learning suffers from two inherent flaws: (1) an inconsistency between the local coordinate-reconstruction pre-training objective and the global molecular-understanding downstream objective, which worsens in deeper layers, and (2) a twisted optimization where atom coordinates simultaneously serve as both the Content to be reconstructed and the Identifier for atom identity, causing unstable training and unreliable noisy distributions.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3D molecule represented as atom types (n×1 integer vector) and 3D coordinates (n×3 real vector), with hydrogen atoms removed for computational efficiency. During pre-training, a random 15% of atoms are dropped from the encoder input, and the decoder reconstructs the full coordinate set via an SE(3)-equivariant head. During fine-tuning, the decoder is discarded entirely; only the encoder's [CLS] token representation (a single d-dimensional vector) is fed to a task-specific MLP head for classification or regression.

## Method

MOL-AE employs a Transformer-based auto-encoder (15 encoder layers, 5 decoder layers, 512-dim, 64 heads) with 3D-aware pair-wise features computed via Gaussian Basis Kernel on inter-atomic Euclidean distances, updated layer-by-layer as attention biases. The core novelty is the 3D Cloze Test objective: instead of adding Gaussian noise to coordinates (as in coordinate denoising), it randomly drops atoms, inserts [MASK] embeddings for the missing positions, and adds sinusoidal positional encoding (based on SMILES atom order) exclusively to the decoder input as a stable Identifier. This decouples the Content/Identifier roles, prevents the model from learning noisy distributions, and avoids injecting SMILES order bias into the encoder's latent representations. Pre-training jointly optimizes coordinate reconstruction, pairwise distance reconstruction, and atom-type classification; fine-tuning uses full-parameter training on the encoder alone with a simple MLP head.

## Results

On the MoleculeNet benchmark, MOL-AE achieves a mean ROC-AUC of 79.04 across 9 molecular classification datasets, outperforming the previous SOTA Uni-Mol (75.98) by over 3 points. On 6 molecular regression datasets, MOL-AE achieves the best performance on 5 out of 6 tasks, with notable gains on FreeSolv (1.448 RMSE vs. Uni-Mol's 1.879) and QM7 (53.8 MAE vs. 58.9). The largest improvements over Uni-Mol appear on SIDER (+9.3 ROC-AUC) and MUV (+9.0 ROC-AUC), where the 3D Cloze Test objective's focus on realistic substructures provides the most benefit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOL-AE outperforms the strongest baseline Uni-Mol on all 9 classification datasets, with the most substantial gains on SIDER (+9.3), MUV (+9.0), and ClinTox (+3.7) ROC-AUC. On regression, MOL-AE beats Uni-Mol on 5 of 6 datasets, with the largest margin on FreeSolv (1.448 vs. 1.879 RMSE, a 23% reduction); the only loss is a negligible 0.0001 MAE gap on QM8. Compared to 2D pre-training methods (PretrainGNN, GROVER, MolCLR, MoleBLEND), MOL-AE achieves a 3+ point mean ROC-AUC advantage, confirming the value of 3D structural information. Among supervised baselines, D-MPNN and Attentive FP trail MOL-AE by 3.2 and 5.7 points in mean ROC-AUC respectively. The consistent dominance across both classification and regression tasks, and across all dataset sizes (from 642 molecules in FreeSolv to 437K in PCBA), indicates the gains are not dataset-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant EnCD framework (encoder-only model + coordinate denoising) used by Uni-Mol and Transformer-M, identifying two structural flaws: objective inconsistency between pre-training and downstream tasks, and twisted optimization caused by the dual role of coordinates as both content and identifier. It draws conceptual parallels to MAE in vision and MLM in NLP, repurposing the mask-based paradigm for 3D molecules by introducing the 3D Cloze Test objective (atom dropping + positional encoding in the decoder). The auto-encoder architecture with a Transformer-based decoder replaces the simple SE(3) head decoder, and the Gaussian Basis Kernel pair-wise features follow the approach of Transformer-M (Luo et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic decomposition of coordinate denoising into content vs. identifier roles provides a general diagnostic lens applicable beyond molecular ML, and the finding that positional encoding should be confined to the decoder (to avoid SMILES order bias contaminating latent representations) is a practical design principle for future 3D molecular models. The robustness to high drop ratios (up to 60%) suggests potential computational acceleration for large-molecule modeling. Open questions include whether the auto-encoder structure generalizes to other 3D tasks (e.g., protein structure, crystal modeling), whether the 3D Cloze Test can be combined with contrastive or generative objectives, and whether the SMILES-order-based PE can be replaced by more structure-aware identifiers.
