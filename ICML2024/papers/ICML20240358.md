## Problem

Autoregressive text-to-music models like MusicLM are trained with next-token prediction that lacks musicality priors, and their temperature-sampled diversity is only desirable along certain axes while adherence and quality should remain consistently high. No prior work has applied reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) at scale to align music generation with listener preferences, leaving a gap between automatic metrics (text adherence, acoustic quality) and the subjective, multi-dimensional nature of musical appreciation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text caption as input, which is encoded via MuLan contrastive embeddings to condition generation. It produces 30-second music audio by autoregressively generating frame-interleaved semantic tokens (from w2v-BERT) and coarse acoustic tokens (from SoundStream RVQ), with fine acoustic tokens generated in parallel via SoundStorm. For RL finetuning, only captions are used as prompts (no ground-truth audio), and the user preference dataset consists of 300,000 pairwise comparisons where users listen to two 20-second clips per prompt and select their favorite via a trophy icon.

## Method

MusicRL finetunes MusicLM's autoregressive stage (interleaved semantic and coarse acoustic tokens) using KL-regularized REINFORCE with a learned value function baseline. Three reward signals are employed: a MuLan cosine-similarity score for text adherence, a Conformer-based reference-free quality estimator predicting human MOS, and a Bradley-Terry reward model trained on 300,000 user pairwise preferences (initialized from the MusicLM checkpoint). The key architectural choice is sequential RL finetuning: first optimizing MuLan and quality rewards for 10,000 steps (MusicRL-R), then finetuning on the user preference reward for 1,000 steps (MusicRL-RU), which outperforms simultaneous multi-reward optimization. Ablations reveal that user preferences capture musicality beyond what text adherence or audio quality metrics explain, as dropping text tokens barely affects reward model accuracy while shortening audio substantially degrades it.

## Results

Across 101 diverse prompts with 3 raters per comparison (303 ratings per model pair), MusicRL-RU achieves the highest average MOS of 3.82 and 460 total wins, compared to MusicLM's 3.07 MOS and 133 wins. In pairwise win-rate evaluations, MusicRL-R is preferred over MusicLM 83% of the time, MusicRL-U 74%, and MusicRL-RU 87%. On automatic reward signals, RL finetuning on MuLan and quality rewards (MusicRL-R) improves the quality score from 3.5 to 4.4 MOS and the MuLan text-adherence score from 0.58 to 0.71, while RLHF on user preferences (MusicRL-U) raises the user preference reward from −1.5 to over +1.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the pretrained MusicLM model. All three RL-finetuned variants significantly outperform it: MusicRL-R (finetuned on MuLan + quality rewards) wins 83% of pairwise comparisons against MusicLM, MusicRL-U (finetuned on the user-preference reward model) wins 74%, and MusicRL-RU (sequential finetuning on both) wins 87%. Among the RL variants, MusicRL-R and MusicRL-U perform comparably (both 3.54 MOS, 362 vs. 372 wins), while MusicRL-RU clearly surpasses both, achieving a 66% win rate over MusicRL-R and 62% over MusicRL-U. All differences are statistically significant per a Wilcoxon signed-rank test. The results confirm that automatic rewards (text adherence + quality) and human-preference feedback are complementary, with their combination yielding the strongest model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly builds on MusicLM (Agostinelli et al., 2023), an autoregressive text-to-music model operating over SoundStream discrete audio tokens, and applies the RLHF paradigm that has become standard for aligning language models (Ouyang et al., 2022; Stiennon et al., 2020) and more recently image generation (Wallace et al., 2023). It departs from prior RL-finetuning of music generation, which relied on hand-crafted music-theory rules (Jaques et al., 2017; Kotecha, 2018) or restricted symbolic domains, by learning reward signals from large-scale user interactions (300K pairwise preferences) and combining them with automatic proxies for text adherence (MuLan) and acoustic quality (Conformer MOS estimator). The sequential finetuning strategy—first on automatic rewards, then on the user preference reward model—differs from simultaneous multi-reward optimization and is motivated by differing convergence rates across reward signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that RLHF at scale (300K user preferences) can improve a music generation model, and the ablation revealing that text adherence is nearly irrelevant to user preference (51.6% match, close to random) challenges the assumption that prompt fidelity is the primary axis of musical satisfaction. The finding that user preferences encode a 'musicality' signal beyond what automatic metrics capture underscores the need for continuous human-in-the-loop finetuning rather than one-shot alignment. Key open questions include the population gap between feedback providers and evaluators, the off-policy nature of the collected data, and how to mitigate demographic biases in user feedback that could narrow the model's musical diversity.
