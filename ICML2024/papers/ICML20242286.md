## Problem

Traditional deep ensembles for OOD detection rely on diversity from different weight initializations or data holdout, but the paper demonstrates via Loss Barrier analysis and a novel Self-Coupling Index metric that such models remain mode-connected and their penultimate-layer features are nearly affine-equivalent, providing insufficient feature representation diversity to meaningfully improve OOD detection over a single model of comparable size.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242286/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K) and produces an OOD detection score by first averaging the penultimate-layer feature vectors from M individual models (each with the same backbone architecture, e.g., ResNet-18/50 or ViT-B) and then applying a pluggable post-hoc scoring metric (MSP, Mahalanobis distance, Energy, or KNN). The feature averaging preserves the original feature dimensionality, so no architectural modification is needed at inference time, and the individual models are model-level parallelizable via GPU multi-instance scheduling.

## Method

MC Ensemble replaces the traditional diversity axis (initialization, data partition) with a new axis: distinct supervision tasks. Three individual models with identical architecture are trained on the same dataset but under different objectives—cross-entropy (SupCE), SimCLR contrastive loss, and supervised contrastive loss (SupCon)—so each develops a different 'comprehension' of the data, landing in non-affine-equivalent loss basins with high loss barriers between them. The paper introduces the Self-Coupling Index, computed via Sinkhorn optimal transport over penultimate-layer features, to quantitatively measure feature representation divergence between model pairs, and shows that cross-task pairs yield SCI values near 0.1 while same-task pairs yield values above 0.8. At inference, the ensemble averages the penultimate-layer features of all M models before scoring, and model selection from a candidate pool is formulated as a minimization of average ID loss plus a weighted Self-Coupling Index term to balance accuracy and diversity.

## Results

On the CIFAR-10 benchmark (ResNet-18, KNN scoring), MC Ensemble achieves an average FPR95 of 5.58% and AUROC of 98.73%, substantially outperforming the next-best method KNN+ (11.07% FPR95, 97.93% AUROC) and all other baselines. On the ImageNet benchmark (ResNet-50, KNN scoring), MC Ensemble achieves 30.69% FPR95 and 93.06% AUROC average, beating the best naive ensemble (3×SupCon at 35.67% FPR95). In the near-OOD setting (CIFAR-10 vs CIFAR-100), MC Ensemble reduces FPR95 by 30.05% and improves AUROC by 5.29% over the 3×SupCE naive ensemble.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MC Ensemble with KNN scoring (5.58% avg FPR95, 98.73% avg AUROC) outperforms all 13 compared baselines on the CIFAR-10 benchmark average. The closest competitor is KNN+ (Sun et al., 2022) at 11.07% FPR95, which MC Ensemble beats by 5.49 points; SSD+ (Sehwag et al., 2021) follows at 16.52% FPR95. Among SOTA methods (Table 5), MC Ensemble is second only to DOE* (Wang et al., 2023) which achieves 5.15% FPR95 vs. MC Ensemble's 5.58%, while MC Ensemble surpasses CIDER (12.57%), ASH-S (17.89%), DICE (24.95%), and FeatureNorm (30.79%) by wide margins. Against naive ensembles (Table 4), MC Ensemble's 5.58% FPR95 beats 3×SupCon (10.34%), 3×SupCE (27.78%), and 3×SimCLR (39.04%), with the Self-Coupling Index dropping from ~0.84–0.87 to 0.134, confirming the multi-comprehension diversity advantage. On ImageNet (Table 7), MC Ensemble (30.69% FPR95) outperforms all naive ensembles, with the gap most pronounced against 3×SupCE (46.39%) and 3×SimCLR (51.14%). The one exception is MC Ensemble+Mahalanobis on iSUN, where it underperforms the standalone SupCon model, suggesting the scoring metric interaction matters for individual OOD datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Deep Ensemble framework (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017) by identifying a fundamental limitation: models trained with the same criterion but different initializations are mode-connected (per Git Re-basin and linear mode connectivity literature), meaning their penultimate-layer features can be aligned by affine transformations and thus provide negligible representation diversity. The authors depart from prior ensemble strategies (weight initialization, data leave-out, k-fold) by introducing a new diversity axis—varying the training task itself (cross-entropy, SimCLR, SupCon)—and formalize this with two new diagnostic tools: the Self-Coupling Index (based on Sinkhorn optimal transport) and loss barrier measurement. The theoretical grounding draws on the bias-variance decomposition of OOD detection and the rectified activation distribution analysis from REACT (Sun et al., 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a widely held assumption in the ensemble-based OOD detection community that simply training more models with different seeds meaningfully improves detection, showing instead that the diversity is illusory under mode connectivity. Practically, the MC Ensemble recipe (train on different tasks, average penultimate-layer features, apply any standard OOD scorer) is simple to implement and compatible with existing scoring methods, and the distillation result (EnD2) shows the approach can be compressed to single-model inference cost with minimal performance loss. Open questions include whether the specific task combination matters (the paper uses CE+SimCLR+SupCon but does not systematically explore alternatives), whether the approach extends to non-vision modalities or generative models, and whether the computational overhead of training M diverse models is justified in resource-constrained settings.
