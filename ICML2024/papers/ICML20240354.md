## Problem

It remained an open question whether stochastic bilevel optimization can achieve the same optimal sample complexity as single-level optimization when using the same stochastic gradient estimator. Prior work showed gaps: SGD-based bilevel methods (SOBA) achieved O(ε^{-2.5}) versus O(ε^{-2}) for single-level SGD, and SAGA-based bilevel methods (SABA) achieved O((n+m)^{2/3}ε^{-1}) versus O((n+m)^{1/2}ε^{-1}) for single-level SAGA, leaving it unclear whether these gaps were inherent to bilevel structure or artifacts of the chosen estimator.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume upper-level objective f(x,y) and lower-level objective g(x,y) in either finite-sum form (averaging over n and m samples) or expectation form, accessed through stochastic gradient and Hessian-vector product oracles. The lower-level objective must be strongly convex in y for any fixed x, while the upper-level objective may be nonconvex. The output is an ε-stationary point x satisfying E||∇H(x)||² ≤ ε, where H(x) = f(x, y*(x)) is the value function, with explicit sample complexity bounds in terms of ε, n, m, and the minibatch size b.

## Method

SPABA adapts the PAGE probabilistic gradient estimator to the bilevel setting within the DecBO decoupling framework, which splits the hypergradient into three linear search directions (for x, y, and z) that are each estimated using PAGE's mechanism: a vanilla minibatch SGD step with probability p and a momentum-corrected minibatch SGD step with probability 1−p. This yields optimal sample complexity O((n+m)^{1/2}ε^{-1}) in the finite-sum setting and O(ε^{-1.5}) under mean-squared smoothness in the expectation setting, matching single-level PAGE with no gap. The paper additionally proposes MA-SABA (SAGA with standard momentum on the UL variable, achieving O((n+m)^{2/3}ε^{-1})) and SRMBA (STORM adaptation, achieving O(ε^{-1.5} log(ε^{-1}))), all fully single-loop. The convergence analysis employs a unified four-step Lyapunov function framework that handles biased estimators by tracking the descent of the mean-squared error E||∇H(x_k) − v_k^x||² through momentum contraction, the approximation errors in y and z via strong convexity, and the residual variance through the specific variance-reduction properties of each estimator.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: SPABA achieves optimal sample complexity of O(ε^{-1.5}) in the general expectation setting (under bounded variance and mean-squared smoothness) and O((n+m)^{1/2}ε^{-1}) in the finite-sum setting, both with O(1) batch size and no logarithmic factors. MA-SABA achieves O((n+m)^{2/3}ε^{-1}) in the finite-sum setting, bridging the gap with single-level SAGA. Empirically, on data hyper-cleaning (MNIST) and hyperparameter selection (covtype, IJCNN1), SPABA and MA-SABA converge faster in both iterations and wall-clock time than all baselines, with MA-SABA reaching the lowest final suboptimality gap on IJCNN1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AmIGO (Arbel & Mairal, 2022), which uses multiple SGD iterations with O(ε^{-1}) batch sizes to achieve O(ε^{-2}) sample complexity, SPABA improves to O(ε^{-1.5}) with O(1) batch size by leveraging the PAGE estimator. Against SUSTAIN (Khanduri et al., 2021b) and MRBO (Yang et al., 2021), which achieve Õ(ε^{-1.5}) with a logarithmic factor, SPABA removes the log factor entirely, yielding a strict O(ε^{-1.5}) bound. In the finite-sum setting, SPABA improves upon SABA (Dagréou et al., 2022) from O((n+m)ε^{-1}) to O((n+m)^{1/2}ε^{-1}), matching the single-level PAGE lower bound and closing the gap that existed for SAGA-based methods. Compared to SRBA (Dagréou et al., 2024), which also achieves O((n+m)^{1/2}ε^{-1}) but requires high-order smoothness and a double-loop structure, SPABA attains the same rate under standard smoothness with a fully single-loop design. Empirically, on the covtype hyperparameter selection task, SRMBA achieves the shortest runtime while SPABA reaches the highest test accuracy most quickly; on IJCNN1, MA-SABA attains the lowest suboptimality gap, outperforming all baselines in both iteration count and wall-clock time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DecBO decoupling framework of Arbel & Mairal (2022) and Dagréou et al. (2022), which reformulates bilevel hypergradient computation into three linear search directions amenable to stochastic estimation. Its central technical contribution is adapting the PAGE loopless variance-reduction estimator (Li et al., 2021) to this bilevel framework, yielding SPABA, while also extending the SAGA-based SABA with momentum (MA-SABA) and the STORM-based estimator (SRMBA). The work is positioned as resolving an open question raised by the observed complexity gaps in prior bilevel methods: SOBA (SGD-based) achieved O(ε^{-2.5}) versus O(ε^{-2}) for single-level SGD, and SABA (SAGA-based) achieved O((n+m)ε^{-1}) versus O((n+m)^{1/2}ε^{-1}) for single-level SAGA. The paper demonstrates these gaps are artifacts of the estimator choice rather than inherent to bilevel structure, since PAGE eliminates them entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By proving that no complexity gap exists between stochastic bilevel and single-level optimization under PAGE, the paper provides a clean theoretical resolution to a question that had motivated several prior works (MA-SOBA, SRBA) to close partial gaps. The unified four-step Lyapunov analysis framework for biased estimators is reusable and clarifies why momentum is essential in the UL update to control the mean-squared error term. A key limitation is that all proposed methods still require Hessian-vector and Jacobian-vector products, restricting applicability to problems where second-order information is affordable; the authors explicitly flag Hessian-free single-loop bilevel algorithms with optimal complexity as a promising open direction. Empirically, the methods show consistent improvements over baselines on data cleaning and hyperparameter selection tasks, though the experiments are on moderate-scale datasets.
