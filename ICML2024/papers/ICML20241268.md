## Problem

Existing offline RL policy regularization methods (e.g., TD3+BC, BCQ, BEAR) constrain the learned policy to mimic all actions from the behavior policy indiscriminately, leading to unnecessary conservativeness that hampers policy improvement—especially when the dataset is dominated by low-return trajectories. The key gap is the lack of a mechanism to selectively guide the learned policy toward high-advantage actions while still maintaining protection against out-of-distribution actions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline dataset of (s, a, r, s') transitions collected by an unknown behavior policy, with no online environment interaction during training. At inference, a continuous state vector is fed to the learned policy network, which outputs a continuous action vector in [-A, A]. Internally, a VAE encoder takes (s, a) pairs to produce latent variables, and the decoder reconstructs actions conditioned on (s, z), generating candidate high-advantage actions that are compared against dataset actions via the advantage function to determine the regularization target.

## Method

A2PR introduces two coupled components built on a TD3+BC backbone. First, Elevating Positive Behavior Learning (EPBL) modifies the VAE's ELBO by gating the reconstruction loss with an indicator 𝟙(A(s,a) > ε_A), so the VAE preferentially reconstructs high-advantage actions, yielding an augmented behavior policy that assigns greater density to superior actions. Second, Adaptive Advantage Policy Constraint (AAPC) selects the higher-advantage action between the dataset action and the VAE-generated action, then pulls the learned policy toward it only when its advantage exceeds ε_A; otherwise the policy self-learns, creating an adaptive constraint that relaxes on poor data. The advantage function is computed IQL-style via expectile regression on the value network, avoiding OOD action queries. Theoretical analysis proves a behavior policy improvement guarantee, a bounded Q-value overestimation error of 2L_Q(ε_0 + ε_1) + δ_error, and a performance gap bound that tightens relative to standard policy regularization.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark (Gym MuJoCo + AntMaze, 18 tasks), A2PR achieves a total average normalized score of 1422.47, outperforming the strongest baseline PRDC (1324.7) by 7.4% and IQL (1106.5) by 28.6%. A2PR attains state-of-the-art performance in 16 out of 18 tasks, with particularly large gains on hopper-medium (100.79 vs. 100.3 for PRDC) and antmaze-medium-diverse (85.60 vs. 78.8 for PRDC). On the supplementary mixed random policy dataset (99% random + 1% expert data), A2PR substantially outperforms both TD3+BC and IQL, demonstrating robustness to severely suboptimal data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

A2PR outperforms all eight baselines on the D4RL total average (1422.47), with the closest competitor being PRDC (1324.7, +7.4% gap) and IQL (1106.5, +28.6% gap). Against the three most recent SOTA baselines specifically: A2PR beats PRDC on 16/18 tasks (losing only on antmaze-umaze-diverse: 84.80 vs. 90.0, and tying on walker2d-random: 5.0 vs. 5.0), beats OAP on 17/18 tasks (OAP scores 0 on antmaze-large-play), and beats AW on 17/18 tasks (AW is particularly weak on AntMaze, averaging only 133.3). Against older baselines, A2PR's advantage is more pronounced: it surpasses CQL by 39.2% (1422.47 vs. 1021.8) and TD3+BC by 68.6% (1422.47 vs. 844.4). On the 99%-random mixed policy dataset, A2PR achieves substantially higher normalized scores than both TD3+BC and IQL, confirming its advantage in mitigating unnecessary conservatism from low-quality data.

## Relation to Prior Work

A2PR sits at the intersection of two established threads in offline RL: policy regularization (TD3+BC, BCQ, BEAR, CQL) that constrains learned policies to the behavior policy's support, and VAE-based behavior modeling (BCQ, SPOT, BEAR) that learns a generative model of the dataset. The key departure is replacing the indiscriminate use of all dataset actions as constraints with an advantage-guided VAE that generates an 'augmented behavior policy' biased toward high-advantage actions, thereby selectively relaxing conservatism on suboptimal data while retaining OOD protection. The advantage function is borrowed from IQL's expectile regression, and the overall actor-critic framework follows TD3+BC, making the contribution primarily a novel regularization mechanism rather than a new algorithmic architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unnecessary conservatism of existing policy regularization methods is a recognized bottleneck in offline RL, particularly for datasets dominated by low-return trajectories; A2PR's adaptive constraint mechanism directly addresses this with theoretical guarantees of behavior policy improvement and a bounded performance gap. The strong results on 99%-random mixed datasets suggest practical relevance for real-world settings where data quality is heterogeneous. However, the authors acknowledge that single-sample VAE generation can be unstable and that the VAE's expressiveness limits how well high-advantage actions can be identified, pointing toward diffusion models or more expressive generative models as a natural next step. The method's reliance on a well-tuned advantage threshold (ε_A) and task-specific hyperparameters (w1, w2, α) also limits its plug-and-play applicability.
