## Problem

Existing mutual information estimators face a fundamental trade-off: non-parametric methods (KSG, KDE) are not differentiable and scale poorly, while neural approaches like MINE require test-time optimization of a discriminant network for every new distribution, making real-time estimation infeasible. The paper asks whether MI can be estimated by a single feed-forward neural network that generalizes across distributions without any per-distribution retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240777/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a pair of jointly sampled sequences {(x_t, y_t)}_{t=1}^T ∈ R^{T×2} as input, after applying an empirical CDF (copula) transformation to normalize both marginals to [0,1]. It outputs a 2D tensor θ_{x,y} ∈ R^{L×L} — a discretized look-up table of the optimal Donsker-Varadhan discriminant — from which the MI estimate is obtained by a single summation over joint and shuffled-marginal samples. For high-dimensional inputs, the slicing technique projects to 1D before feeding into the same network.

## Method

InfoNet reframes the Donsker-Varadhan dual estimation by treating the optimal scalar discriminant function θ as the *output* of a neural network φ rather than a parameter to be optimized at test time. The architecture adapts Perceiver IO with two parallel pathways: one encodes joint samples via learnable queries and self-attention, while the other processes individual X and Y marginals through MLPs and cross-attention, with the two pathways integrated by a final attention module to produce the L×L discriminant tensor, followed by a fixed Gaussian convolution for smoothness. Training uses the discretized dual objective (Eq. 2) as the loss, with gradient ascent over a large batch of randomly generated 2D Gaussian Mixture Models (up to 20 components, 2000 samples each), so the network learns to predict the optimal discriminant for any unseen distribution through generalization. This eliminates test-time optimization entirely, reducing inference to a single forward pass (~0.01 s) while preserving differentiability and achieving order-preserving accuracy comparable to or better than MINE with 500 gradient steps.

## Results

InfoNet achieves 99.0–99.8% correlation order accuracy across GMMs with 1–10 components, maintaining near-perfect performance as estimation difficulty increases, while MINE-500 degrades from 98.5% to 78.1% and MINE-100 from 94.6% to 66.4%. On Gaussian sanity checks, InfoNet matches KSG's mean error while running 30× faster, and outperforms MINE-500 by 30% in accuracy at 50× lower runtime (0.011 s vs. 0.991 s). In test-time efficiency, InfoNet-16 (batch of 16) completes MI estimation in 0.001–0.003 s across sequence lengths of 200–5000, compared to 3.35–4.16 s for MINE-2000 and 0.25–1.80 s for KDE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KSG (Kraskov et al., 2004), InfoNet matches or slightly exceeds correlation order accuracy (99.0–99.8% vs. 97.0–99.0%) while being approximately 30× faster in test-time inference, making it the clear winner on the efficiency-accuracy trade-off. Against KDE (Silverman, 2018), InfoNet dominates in accuracy on multi-component GMMs: at MI=0.5, KDE exhibits a mean error of −0.109 while InfoNet's is only −0.063, and KDE's large negative bias worsens at higher MI levels (−0.292 at MI=0.9). Against MINE (Belghazi et al., 2018a), InfoNet is the most decisive winner: MINE-500 degrades from 98.5% to 78.1% order accuracy as GMM complexity grows from K=1 to K=10, while InfoNet remains above 99.0% throughout; MINE-100 and MINE-10 degrade even more severely (to 66.4% and 52.5% respectively). In absolute MI estimation error (Table 2), InfoNet's mean error at MI=0.5 is −0.063 versus −0.294 for MINE-500 and −0.420 for MINE-100, with variance roughly 2–3× lower. On the out-of-domain Pointodyssey video dataset, InfoNet achieves higher precision-recall for MI-based object segmentation than all MINE variants, confirming generalization beyond the GMM training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

InfoNet builds directly on the Donsker-Varadhan dual formulation used by MINE (Belghazi et al., 2018) for MI estimation, but departs fundamentally by replacing MINE's test-time gradient optimization with a feed-forward Perceiver IO–style network trained on a large family of simulated GMM distributions. The architectural design adapts the cross-attention and learnable-query structure of Perceiver IO (Jaegle et al., 2021) to output a discretized discriminant look-up table rather than a scalar score. The training philosophy—learning from a broad distribution family to generalize to unseen ones—echoes simulation-based inference (Cranmer et al., 2020), and the high-dimensional extension reuses the sliced MI projection technique of Goldfeld & Greenewald (2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30–50× speedup over MINE while matching or exceeding its accuracy opens the door to real-time MI estimation in streaming, embodied, and IoT settings where per-distribution retraining is infeasible. The consistent ~99% correlation-order accuracy across GMM complexity levels (K=1 to 10), where MINE degrades to ~78%, suggests the learned predictor captures structural properties of the DV dual more robustly than finite-step optimization. Open questions include how far the GMM-trained model generalizes to truly arbitrary or adversarial distributions, whether direct high-dimensional MI estimation (beyond slicing) is achievable, and whether the approach extends to conditional MI or other information-theoretic quantities.
