## Problem

Multi-agent LLM interactions (e.g., RECONCILE) substantially improve reasoning but require numerous expensive inference calls across multiple models and rounds, and they do not yield a single deployable model. This paper asks how to distill the rich reasoning knowledge embedded in these multi-agent discussions—correct chains, incorrect chains, and the interaction structure itself—into a smaller, efficient standalone student model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240280/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes Multi-Agent Interaction Graphs (MAGs): directed acyclic graphs where each node is a full Chain-of-Thought reasoning chain from one of three teacher LLMs (GPT-4, Bard, Claude2) in a given round, annotated with a binary correctness label, and edges encode which prior-round outputs conditioned each subsequent generation. At test time, the distilled student model receives only the raw reasoning question and produces a single Chain-of-Thought reasoning chain and final answer in one inference call, with the GCN module removed entirely.

## Method

MAGDI augments a base student LM (Mistral-7B-Instruct, LLaMA-2-7B/13B-Chat) with a two-layer Graph Convolutional Network that processes the MAG adjacency matrix to produce structure-aware node representations. Training combines three objectives aligned to four progressive levels: (1) next-token prediction on all correct reasoning chains (Levels 1–2), (2) a margin-based contrastive loss that pushes scalar scores of correct chains above those of incorrect chains (Level 3), and (3) a cross-entropy node-classification loss on GCN-processed representations that forces the model to discriminate correct from incorrect nodes given the interaction topology (Level 4). The GCN is used exclusively during distillation; at inference the student operates as a standard LM without any graph module. Fine-tuning uses LoRA (rank 16, alpha 32) for 10 epochs, and the final loss is a weighted sum of the three objectives.

## Results

On five in-domain reasoning benchmarks (StrategyQA, CommonsenseQA, ARC-c, GSM8K, MATH), MAGDI achieves an average accuracy of 56.88% with Mistral-7B-Instruct as the student, outperforming the zero-shot base model (46.17%) by 10.71% and the best single-teacher distillation baseline S1T-GPT4 (52.27%) by 4.61%. The multi-teacher baseline DSS-MT reaches 54.76%, still 2.12% below MAGDI. In terms of efficiency, MAGDI reduces the number of output tokens by up to 9× (average 4.5×) compared to the RECONCILE multi-agent framework while maintaining competitive accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the multi-teacher distillation baseline DSS-MT (Hsieh et al., 2023), MAGDI wins on all five benchmarks, achieving 56.88% average accuracy versus DSS-MT's 54.76% (+2.12%). The gap is most pronounced on MATH (12.76% vs. 9.98%) and GSM8K (52.27% vs. 51.84%), suggesting that modeling interaction structure is particularly beneficial for multi-step reasoning. Against the RECONCILE multi-agent framework (Chen et al., 2023b), which serves as the multi-teacher upper bound, MAGDI trades some accuracy (56.88% vs. 79.16% average) for a 4.5× reduction in output tokens (216.9 vs. 970.4 average), making it far more practical for deployment. Notably, the relative improvement from single-teacher to multi-teacher distillation via MAGDI (12.70%) exceeds the relative improvement from GPT-4 to the full RECONCILE system (5.79%), indicating that structured distillation effectively transfers multi-agent capabilities into a single student model. On out-of-domain tasks (BoolQ, SVAMP), MAGDI-MT also outperforms the single-teacher multi-task baseline by up to 7% (64.30 vs. 57.52 on SVAMP).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge distillation literature for LLM reasoning (Magister et al., 2023; Li et al., 2023; Fu et al., 2023), which has focused almost exclusively on single-teacher settings, to a multi-teacher regime where the student learns from the *interactions* between teachers rather than just their individual outputs. It builds directly on RECONCILE (Chen et al., 2023b) as the multi-agent interaction framework and introduces a novel graph-based representation (Multi-Agent Interaction Graphs) to capture the structural dependencies between agents and rounds. The method departs from prior graph-based dialogue modeling work (Ghosal et al., 2019; Shen et al., 2021), which targeted human-human or human-model conversations for downstream NLP tasks, by applying GCN-based structure learning specifically to distill reasoning capabilities into a standalone student model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAGDI provides a practical and modular pathway to convert expensive multi-agent LLM reasoning (requiring up to 12 inference calls) into a single efficient model with up to 9× token reduction, which is directly relevant for deployment cost and latency. The four-level framework (multi-teacher → interaction → negative reasoning → structure) is adaptable to varying data availability, making it broadly applicable. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond CoT-based reasoning to other prompting paradigms, whether the GCN is truly necessary versus other graph encoders at scale, and whether the reliance on proprietary API-based teachers (GPT-4, Bard, Claude2) for generating training MAGs limits reproducibility and scalability of the training data pipeline.
