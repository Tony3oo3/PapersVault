## Problem

Online exp-concave optimization (OXO) with zeroth-order (bandit) feedback has remained an open problem: the best known classical algorithm (Liu et al., 2018) achieves only O(nT^{2/3} log T) regret, far from the O(n log T) bound available under full-information feedback. This paper asks whether quantum computing can close this gap, and shows that a quantum quasi-Newton method achieves the near-optimal O(n log T) regret with only O(1) oracle queries per round, improving the classical bound by a factor of T^{2/3}.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the algorithm receives a quantum zeroth-order oracle Q_{f_t} (a unitary mapping |x⟩|q⟩ → |x⟩|q+f(x)⟩) and a decision point x_t in a bounded convex set K ⊆ R^n of diameter D. The quantum circuit uses n b-qubit registers plus one c-qubit register, where b = O(log(Tn/δ)) and c = O(log(Tn/δ)), requiring O(n log(Tn/δ)) qubits total. The output is a decision sequence x_1,...,x_T with a regret bound of O((DG + 1/α) n log(T + log T)) holding with probability > 1 − Tρ, where α is the exp-concavity parameter and G is the Lipschitz constant.

## Method

The core algorithm (QONS) replaces the exact gradient in the online Newton step with a quantum-estimated inexact gradient obtained via phase kickback: a superposition over a fine grid is prepared, the loss function difference F_t(u) is encoded as a phase, and the inverse quantum Fourier transform extracts the gradient through measurement. The key technical improvement over prior quantum gradient estimation (which required O(log n) repetitions) is that O(1) queries suffice by trading per-coordinate failure probability for a finer grid, with the error bounded in ℓ_1 norm. The matrix inverse is updated in O(n²) time per round via the Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury formula. The paper further extends the framework to a quantum AdaGrad variant (using diagonal G_t = diag(A_t)^{1−p}) and introduces a novel online Newton method with full Hessian update (A_t = A_{t−1} + H_t), for which a quantum Hessian estimation circuit is designed using the first-order oracle, achieving O(DLn/α log(T+1)) regret.

## Results

The proposed Quantum Online Quasi-Newton Method (QONS) achieves a regret bound of O((DG + 1/α)·n·log(T + log T)) with only O(1) quantum oracle queries per round against a completely adaptive adversary. This improves upon the best known classical zeroth-order algorithm (Liu et al., 2018) by a factor of T^{2/3}, matching the logarithmic regret of the full-information online Newton step (Hazan et al., 2007) while operating under the much more restrictive zeroth-order feedback setting. The non-smooth extension (Theorem 3) preserves the same O((DG + 1/α)·n·log(T + log T)) regret with O(1) queries per round, and the Hessian-update variant (Theorem 2) achieves O(DLn/α · log(T+1)) regret.

| Paper | Feedback | Regret |
|---|---|---|
| Hazan et al. (2007) | Full information (first-order) | O(DGn log T) |
| Liu et al. (2018) | Zeroth-order (classical) | O(DGn T^{2/3} log T) |
| **This work** | Zeroth-order (quantum) | **O(DGn log(T + log T))** |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines for online exp-concave optimization. Against the full-information online Newton step of Hazan et al. (2007), which achieves O(DGn log T) regret but requires the complete loss function (and hence exact gradients) as feedback, the proposed quantum algorithm matches the logarithmic regret order while operating under the far more restrictive zeroth-order feedback setting where only function values are accessible. Against the best classical zeroth-order algorithm of Liu et al. (2018), which achieves O(DGn T^{2/3} log T) regret using an unbiased gradient estimator, the quantum algorithm improves the dependence on T from T^{2/3}·log T to log(T + log T), a factor of T^{2/3} improvement. This quantum advantage is achieved with O(1) oracle queries per round (4 queries to construct and uncompute the phase-kickback circuit), whereas the classical method requires O(n) queries per round for finite-difference gradient estimation. The quantum advantage stems from the ability to estimate all n gradient coordinates simultaneously via quantum superposition and phase estimation, rather than requiring separate queries per coordinate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of quantum gradient estimation and online exp-concave optimization. It builds directly on the online Newton step framework of Hazan et al. (2007) for full-information exp-concave optimization and the quantum gradient estimation technique of Jordan (2005) / Gilyen et al. (2019), while extending the O(1)-query quantum gradient analysis from the authors' prior work on general convex optimization (He et al., 2022). The key departure from Liu et al. (2018), the best prior classical zeroth-order method, is replacing the unbiased gradient estimator (which yields O(nT^{2/3} log T) regret) with a quantum-estimated inexact gradient whose ℓ₁ error is controllable, enabling logarithmic regret. The paper also introduces a quantum Hessian estimation circuit and a classical online Newton method with Hessian update, filling a gap the authors note has no prior 'real' online Newton method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result closes a significant theoretical gap: the classical zeroth-order exp-concave optimization problem had a T^{2/3} regret factor that quantum methods eliminate, matching the full-information O(n log T) bound with only O(1) oracle queries per round. This demonstrates a concrete quantum advantage for a well-studied online learning problem against a completely adaptive adversary. Open questions include whether rank-k Hessian updates (1 < k < n) can further improve regret, whether a quantum algorithm can estimate the full Hessian with O(1) queries to a first-order oracle (currently O(n) queries are needed), and whether the quantum first-order oracle can be exploited for additional speedups beyond row-by-row Hessian estimation. Practical impact is currently limited by the requirement for fault-tolerant quantum hardware and the smoothness assumption (though a mollification-based extension to non-smooth functions is provided).
