## Problem

Deterministic image restoration algorithms (e.g., CGANs) are widely used to simultaneously achieve high perceptual quality and measurement consistency, but it was unclear what fundamental cost this pursuit entails. Prior work (Ohayon et al., 2023) made intuitive claims that such estimators must be erratic, but only for deterministic degradations and without a formal proof. This paper provides a rigorous theorem showing that for any non-invertible degradation (including stochastic ones like noise), the Lipschitz constant of a deterministic estimator must diverge as its joint perceptual quality approaches perfection, making it inherently vulnerable to adversarial perturbations.

## Input / Output

The framework is general: a deterministic estimator f maps a degraded measurement Y ∈ ℝ^{n_y} to a single restored image X̂ ∈ ℝ^{n_x}, where the degradation p_{Y|X} can be deterministic (downsampling, JPEG) or stochastic (additive noise, random blur). In the super-resolution experiments, inputs are 4× downsampled or real-world degraded images from DIV2K (100 test images), and outputs are 4× upscaled reconstructions. In the face-restoration experiment, inputs are 32×32 noisy face crops from CelebA-HQ and outputs are 512×512 restored faces. The key evaluation metric pairs a Lipschitz lower bound K̄ (measured via I-FGSM with α=1/255) against a joint perceptual index (Wasserstein-1 distance or Joint-FID between p_{X̂,Y} and p_{X,Y}).

## Method

The core result (Theorem 4.1) proves that for any non-invertible degradation, Lip(X̂) ≥ m₁/√(W_p(p_{X,Y}, p_{X̂,Y})) − m₂ for any deterministic estimator with joint perceptual index ≤ γ. The proof introduces the notion of β-ill-posedness (any non-invertible degradation is β-ill-posed for some β>0), then uses Markov's inequality on the optimal transport coupling to show that with positive probability, two inputs y₂⁽¹⁾ and y₂⁽²⁾ within O(√ε) of each other must be mapped to outputs separated by at least β − O(√ε), forcing the Lipschitz constant to scale as 1/√ε. Empirically, the authors validate this on 12+ super-resolution methods (RealESRGAN, BSRGAN, SwinIR-GAN, ESRGAN, GFPGAN, etc.) across three degradations, showing a clear inverse relationship between K̄ and √JFID. They further demonstrate practical consequences: an I-FGSM attack on GFPGAN shifts gender classification from 63.3% to 72.2% 'female' with imperceptible input changes, while a low-perceptual-quality RRDB baseline remains robust. Finally, they show the erratic behavior can be repurposed via Farthest Point Sampling on the input to approximate posterior sampling.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) proves that for any non-invertible degradation, the Lipschitz constant of a deterministic estimator satisfies Lip(X̂) ≥ m₁/√(W_p(p_{X,Y}, p_{X̂,Y})) − m₂, meaning the Lipschitz constant must grow to infinity as joint perceptual quality approaches perfection. Empirically, across 14 super-resolution methods evaluated on DIV2K with three degradations (Track1, Track2, bicubic ×4), a clear inverse relationship is observed between the Lipschitz lower bound K̄ and √JFID. In the adversarial attack experiment on CelebA-HQ, GFPGAN (high joint perceptual quality) shifts gender classification from 63.3% to 72.2% 'female' under I-FGSM (α=16/255), while RRDB (low perceptual quality) actually decreases from 59.1% to 56.9%, confirming the vulnerability is tied to joint perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 14 deterministic SR methods (RealESRGAN, BSRGAN, BSRNet, FeMaSR, DAN, ESRGAN, RRDB, RealSR-JPEG, RealSR-DPED, FSSR-JPEG, FSSR-DPED, SR-ResCGAN, SwinIR-GAN, SwinIR-PSNR) to validate Theorem 4.1, showing that methods with lower √JFID (e.g., SwinIR-GAN, ESRGAN, RealESRGAN) consistently exhibit higher K̄ values, while methods with higher √JFID (e.g., RRDB, SwinIR-PSNR, BSRNet) are more robust. In the targeted adversarial attack experiment, GFPGAN is clearly more vulnerable than RRDB: a tiny perturbation (PSNR ≥ 48.13 dB between input and attacked input) causes GFPGAN to generate new semantic features (makeup, jewelry) that flip gender classification by +8.9 percentage points, whereas RRDB shows no such vulnerability and even slightly decreases the 'female' fraction. The paper also demonstrates that the tradeoff holds across alternative statistical discrepancy measures (KID, Precision, Recall), confirming it is not an artifact of the FID metric. No single method 'wins' here—the paper's contribution is establishing that high joint perceptual quality and robustness are fundamentally incompatible for deterministic estimators, so the comparison is between the theoretical prediction and empirical validation rather than between competing methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper rigorously proves and generalizes the intuitive claim from Ohayon et al. (2023) that deterministic estimators achieving high perceptual quality and consistency must be erratic. The key advances are: (1) extending the analysis from deterministic degradations only to any form of p_{Y|X} (including stochastic noise, blur, etc.), (2) replacing hand-wavy arguments with a formal proof via a β-ill-posedness definition and Wasserstein-space geometry, and (3) introducing the 'joint perceptual index' (Wasserstein distance between p_{X,Y} and p_{X̂,Y}) as a unified measure that subsumes both perceptual quality and consistency. The paper explicitly distinguishes its result from the accuracy-stability tradeoff of Gottschling et al. (2023) and the perception-distortion tradeoff of Blau & Michaeli (2018), positioning it as a fundamentally different constraint arising from the non-invertibility of the degradation rather than from overfitting or distortion minimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theorem establishes a fundamental impossibility: no deterministic restoration algorithm can simultaneously achieve high joint perceptual quality and robustness, regardless of architecture or degradation type. This has direct security implications for widely deployed methods (GFPGAN, ESRGAN, SwinIR-GAN, etc.), as demonstrated by the gender-classification manipulation experiment. On the positive side, the paper shows the instability can be repurposed to approximate posterior sampling via Farthest Point Sampling on the input, offering a practical path to uncertainty quantification from deterministic models. Key limitations include demonstration only on super-resolution (not MRI, video, audio), evaluation limited to GAN-based methods, and the posterior exploration remaining qualitative without a formal convergence guarantee or practical application pipeline.
