## Problem

PAC learning for RL from reachability specifications was proven impossible due to the inherent non-robustness of infinite-horizon logical specifications, where small environment perturbations can invalidate near-optimal policies. This paper resolves the impossibility by introducing the Expected Conditional Distance (ECD) as an external parameter, proving via a lower bound that ECD is necessary and via an algorithm that it is sufficient for efficient PAC guarantees without any assumptions on the underlying environment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241928/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a simulator of an unknown MDP (exposing reset and step operations), a target state set T, a user-specified ECD bound ℓ, and PAC parameters ε and p. The ECD is an external, user-defined parameter that requires no knowledge of transition probabilities or structural properties of the environment, distinguishing it from prior internal parameters like mixing time or minimum transition probability. The output is an ε-optimal policy maximizing reachability probability to T among all policies with ECD at most ℓ, guaranteed with probability at least 1−p within a polynomial number of simulator calls.

## Method

The algorithm operates in two phases: first, ApproximateMDP explores the MDP by sampling each state-action pair Θ(n⁴ℓ²/ε⁴ log(n²|A|/p)) times to build a high-probability approximation of transition probabilities for all states reachable within 6ℓ/ε steps; second, a dynamic programming algorithm computes a near-optimal policy in the known approximate MDP by tracking reachability probability as a function of remaining steps and a discretized ECD budget, exploiting concavity of the value functions to achieve polynomial-time computation. The key insight is that ECD treats trajectories that never reach the target with weight 0 (unlike Shortest Stochastic Path which assigns them ∞), making it a purely external parameter that effectively bounds the learning horizon without imposing assumptions on the environment. A matching lower bound shows any PAC algorithm requires Ω(ℓ) samples, establishing ECD as a tight characterization of the sample complexity for reachability learning.

## Results

The paper establishes that ECD can be exponential in the smallest transition probability (Lemma 4.1: ECD ≥ p^(−|S|+1)), proving that PAC learning without ECD information is impossible for efficient sample complexity. The main positive result (Theorem 5.4) provides a PAC-MDP algorithm with expected sample complexity O(n⁶|A|ℓ³ε⁻⁶ log(n²|A|/p) log(n|A|ℓ/(εp))), which is polynomial in all input parameters (|S|, |A|, |T|, 1/ε, 1/p, ℓ). This is the first PAC-possible result for reachability specifications that makes no assumptions about the underlying environment, as ECD is a user-supplied external parameter rather than an internal environment property.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not present empirical comparisons but contrasts its theoretical guarantees with prior PAC-possibility results for logical specifications. Fu & Topcu (2014) achieve PAC for LTL but require full knowledge of the environment topology (all non-zero transitions). Ashok et al. (2019) relax this to requiring only the minimum non-zero transition probability. Perez et al. (2023) require knowledge of the mixing time. Yang et al. (2021) prove PAC impossibility for all non-finitary specifications (including reachability) without additional parameters. Alur et al. (2023) obtain PAC for LTL under discounted-sum semantics, which changes the specification itself. In contrast, the ECD parameter introduced here is external to the environment: a user can supply a value ℓ without any knowledge of transition probabilities, topology, or mixing time, making it the first environment-agnostic PAC guarantee for reachability specifications.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the line of work on PAC guarantees for RL from temporal logical specifications, where prior impossibility results (Alur et al. 2022; Yang et al. 2021) showed that reachability specifications are not PAC-learnable. Previous PAC-possibility results for logical specifications required internal environment parameters—topology knowledge (Fu & Topcu 2014), minimum non-zero transition probability (Ashok et al. 2019), or mixing time (Perez et al. 2023)—which impose assumptions on the unknown environment. This paper introduces the Expected Conditional Distance (ECD) as an external, user-supplied parameter that requires no knowledge of the environment, and proves it is both necessary (lower bound) and sufficient (algorithm) for efficient PAC guarantees on reachability, carving new ground by being the first environment-agnostic PAC-possible result for reachability specifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tight characterization—ECD is necessary and sufficient for efficient PAC-MDP on reachability—resolves a fundamental open question about what information is minimally required to overcome the PAC impossibility barrier for infinite-horizon logical specifications. The result is purely theoretical with no empirical validation, and the ECD parameter, while external, still requires the user to supply a meaningful value, which may be non-trivial in practice. Open directions include extending ECD parameterization to richer specification classes (safety, LTL, ω-regular objectives), developing model-free PAC algorithms, and empirical evaluation of the sample complexity bounds in realistic settings.
