## Problem

Existing neural controller frameworks for stochastic differential equations (SDEs) train on finite samples and cannot rigorously guarantee that stability or safety conditions hold over the entire state space, while CBF-based safety methods either incur high computational costs (QP/SOS) or only ensure safety with probability less than one. This paper addresses the gap by providing a framework that yields both rigorous exponential stability and almost-sure safety guarantees for learned neural controllers on SDEs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242485/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes the SDE drift f and diffusion g, a user-specified zeroing barrier function h defining the bounded safe region C, and samples N training points from C. It outputs a neural controller u(x) (spectral-normalized, with u(0)=0), an ICNN potential function V(x) satisfying positive-definiteness, and a monotonic-NN class-K function α(x). After training, the controller is post-processed through two closed-form projection operators (π̂_sf then π̂_es) to produce the final guaranteed controller, which can be evaluated in O(d) time per state.

## Method

FESSNC is a two-stage framework: (1) heuristic supervised training minimizing a combined loss L_es + L_sf that enforces the stochastic Lyapunov condition L_u V ≤ cV (c<0) and the ZBF safety condition L_u h ≥ −α(h) on sampled data, using ICNN for V, a monotonic NN for α, and a spectral-normalized NN for u; (2) post-training projection via closed-form approximate operators π̂_sf (for safety) and π̂_es (for exponential stability) that subtract/add a gradient-scaled correction term proportional to the constraint violation. The key theoretical insight is that the safety projection first restricts the state space to the bounded safe region C, which then satisfies the boundedness condition required by the stability projection (Theorem 3.1), enabling the composition π̂_es ∘ π̂_sf to provide both guarantees simultaneously. Hutchinson's trace estimator reduces Hessian computation from O(d²) to O(d) during training, and a detach trick further accelerates the r=1 diffusion case, making the framework scalable to 100-dimensional systems.

## Results

On the kinematic bicycle task, FESSNC achieves 100% safety rate and 100% success rate with the lowest control energy (1.5) and fastest training time (14 s), while providing the only theoretical (as opposed to numerical) guarantee of both exponential stability (ES) and almost-sure safety. The computational complexity is O(d) versus O(k^d · d^2)–O(k^d · d^3) for grid-based baselines, avoiding the curse of dimensionality. On the double pendulum task, FESSNC completes a full control process in 10.35 s compared to 146.64 s for GP-MPC, while maintaining 100% safety and stable balancing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FESSNC outperforms all five baselines on the combined criteria of stability, safety, speed, and scalability. Against GP-MPC (Kamthe & Deisenroth, 2018), FESSNC is ~14× faster per control step (10.35 s vs. 146.64 s on the double pendulum) and maintains stable balancing where GP-MPC drifts. Against BALSA (Fan et al., 2020), FESSNC is slightly slower (10.35 s vs. 3.08 s) but achieves 100% success rate versus BALSA's poor performance due to disjoint predefined Lyapunov/barrier functions. Among learning-based methods, FESSNC dominates SYNC (Zhang et al., 2023) with 100% vs. 90% success rate, lower control energy (1.5 vs. 1.8), and 9× faster training (14 s vs. 125 s), while providing a theoretical rather than numerical guarantee. FESSNC decisively beats NNDMC (Mazouz et al.) which achieves only 60% success rate and no stability guarantee, and RSMC (Lechner et al., 2022) which fails entirely (0% success, 10% safety). The key differentiator is that FESSNC is the only method providing a rigorous theoretical guarantee (via closed-form projection operators) rather than relying on finite-grid numerical verification, and its O(d) complexity avoids the exponential-in-dimensionality cost of all grid-based baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural stochastic control framework of Zhang et al. (2022), which heuristically trained neural controllers for SDEs but lacked rigorous guarantees over the full state space, by introducing closed-form projection operators that map learned controllers into the space of exponentially stable and safe controllers. It departs from prior CBF-based safety methods for SDEs (which rely on reciprocal barrier functions with complex conditions and QP solvers) by establishing a simpler zeroing barrier function (ZBF) theorem for SDEs and showing that the resulting safety constraint also satisfies the bounded-state-space condition needed for the stability projection. The Hutchinson trace estimator is repurposed from generative modeling to accelerate Hessian computations in the derivative operator, reducing training cost from O(d²) to O(d).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to provide simultaneous theoretical (not merely numerical) guarantees of both exponential stability and almost-sure safety for learned neural controllers on SDEs, with O(d) computational complexity that avoids the curse of dimensionality inherent in grid-based verification methods. The projection operators are also applicable to nonparametric controllers (e.g., kernel machines), broadening their utility. Key limitations include the requirement for fully actuated systems, known dynamics, and deterministic (not stochastic) control; the authors identify online learning, model-free settings, underactuated systems, and stochastic control as important open directions. The bounded-state-space condition for the stability guarantee is a subtle constraint that is only satisfied when safety is also enforced, creating a tight coupling between the two guarantees.
