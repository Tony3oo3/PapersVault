## Problem

Deep Kernel Learning (DKL) suffers from a critical overfitting pathology: the neural network freely projects inputs to maximize the marginal likelihood's fit term, eventually correlating all data points and collapsing the model capacity penalty. Existing remedies, such as full Bayesian inference over network parameters via MCMC, scale poorly for high-dimensional posteriors, leaving no practical scalable solution that preserves DKL's representational power while preventing spurious correlations.

## Input / Output

The method accepts heterogeneous inputs—tabular feature vectors, graph node feature vectors (e.g., 1433-dim word counts for Cora), or 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10/100)—and produces class probabilities with calibrated predictive uncertainty (mean and variance) or continuous regression values. A key architectural detail is that the NN embedding g_i is consumed twice: once as the kernel argument and once (after a saturation and linear projection) to generate input-dependent inducing locations Z_i, variational mean m_i, and lower-triangular covariance L_i, making the variational distribution q(u|g_i) spatially adaptive.

## Method

AVDKL couples a deep NN feature extractor with an amortized sparse GP by sharing parameters between the kernel-learning component and the variational-parameter generation component. Specifically, the embedding g_i = g(x_i) is passed through a saturation module (Tanh or Sigmoid, optionally preceded by a linear compression) and a final linear layer to produce input-dependent inducing points Z_i and variational parameters (m_i, L_i), yielding q(u|g_i) = N(u | m_i, L_i L_i^T). The ELBO is then L = Σ_i E_{q(u|g_i) p(f_i|u)}[log p(y_i|f_i)] − (1/N) Σ_i KL[q(u|g_i) || p(u|g_i)], where the KL term's entropy component penalizes excessive correlation among inducing points. Because the variational parameters are generated from the same network that produces the kernel embeddings, the model fit and capacity terms of the ELBO are explicitly linked, preventing the network from 'hacking' the Gram matrix. The saturation function is critical: it bounds the variational parameters for out-of-distribution inputs, ensuring well-calibrated uncertainty estimates in extrapolation regions.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, AVDKL achieves 0.7755 MNLL, 80.13% accuracy, and 7.18% ECE, outperforming SVDKL (0.8510 MNLL, 79.92% acc, 9.22% ECE) and ResNet-18 (0.8121 MNLL, 79.50% acc, 8.00% ECE). On CIFAR-10, AVDKL attains 0.1068 MNLL and 1.41% ECE, marginally beating SVDKL (0.1107 MNLL, 1.44% ECE) and ResNet-18 (0.1122 MNLL, 1.50% ECE) while matching their ~96.8% accuracy. On tabular data, AVDKL achieves the best test MNLL on 5 out of 6 UCI datasets, with the largest gains in regression (e.g., −2.283 MNLL on KeggD vs. −1.916 for SVDKL). On semi-supervised node classification, AVDKL reaches 85.48% accuracy on Cora, matching DeepGCN (85.47%) while substantially outperforming SVDKL (79.83%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SVDKL, AVDKL consistently achieves lower MNLL and ECE across all three domains: on CIFAR-100 the MNLL gap is 0.0755 (0.7755 vs. 0.8510) and ECE drops from 9.22% to 7.18%; on tabular data AVDKL wins on 5 of 6 datasets (e.g., Letter: 0.084 vs. 0.190 MNLL); on Cora, AVDKL's accuracy (85.48%) exceeds SVDKL's (79.83%) by over 5 points, though SVDKL achieves slightly lower MNLL. Against SVGP, AVDKL dominates on tabular regression (KeggD: −2.283 vs. −1.889 MNLL) and is competitive on classification. Against GDKL, AVDKL matches or beats it on 5 of 6 tabular datasets (e.g., Letter: 0.084 vs. 0.101 MNLL), with GDKL only edging ahead on MagicGamma (0.284 vs. 0.286). Against DLVKL, AVDKL wins on all six tabular datasets, with the largest margin on Letter (0.084 vs. 0.205 MNLL) and KeggU (−3.004 vs. −2.772 MNLL). On graph classification, AVDKL matches DeepGCN in accuracy (85.48% vs. 85.47% on Cora) while providing better uncertainty calibration, and outperforms it on CiteSeer MNLL (0.9438 vs. 0.9670).

## Relation to Prior Work

AVDKL merges two previously separate lines of work: stochastic variational deep kernel learning (SVDKL, Wilson et al. 2016b), which learns complex kernels via a neural feature extractor feeding a sparse GP, and input-dependent sparse GPs (IDSGP, Jafrasteh et al. 2022), which amortize the variational posterior over inducing points conditioned on the input. The key departure from both is a parameter-sharing scheme: the same deep network that produces the kernel embedding g_i also generates the amortized inducing locations and variational parameters, coupling the ELBO's data-fit and KL-regularization terms through shared weights. This design directly targets the overcorrelation pathology identified by Ober et al. (2021), where DKL's free network parameters 'hack' the Gram matrix to suppress the capacity penalty, without resorting to the expensive full-Bayesian MCMC treatment those authors proposed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a lightweight, architecturally simple fix (parameter sharing plus a saturation module) for a well-documented DKL failure mode, validated consistently across tabular, graph, and image benchmarks. The observation that SVDKL and AVDKL can achieve equivalent ELBO values yet diverge substantially in test MNLL (Figure 8) highlights that the ELBO is a poor generalization proxy when the variational parameters are decoupled from the feature extractor. Open questions include how the method scales to very large datasets (the amortization still requires per-input computation of inducing locations), whether the ad-hoc saturation module can be replaced by a principled prior, and whether the approach extends naturally to multi-output or hierarchical GP settings.
