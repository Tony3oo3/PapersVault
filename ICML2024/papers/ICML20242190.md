## Problem

Existing LLM watermarking methods cannot simultaneously satisfy three critical properties: preserving the original token distribution (so text quality is unaffected), enabling detection without access to the language model API or original prompts, and providing provable resilience against text modifications with a guaranteed false positive rate. Kirchenbauer et al. (2023) alters the sampling distribution, Kuditipudi et al. (2023) requires thousands of resampling steps for detection, and Hu et al. (2023a) needs the LM API and prompt during detection—no prior method achieves all three guarantees at once.

## Input / Output

For generation, DiPmark takes a text prompt, a secret watermark key k, a reweight parameter α, and a language model, and produces watermarked text by sampling tokens from a reweighted distribution at each step. For detection, it takes only the watermarked text, the secret key k, the vocabulary size N, a green-list separator γ, and a threshold z—no LM API access or original prompt is needed—and outputs a binary watermarked/not-watermarked decision. The cipher at each step is derived via a hash function h(k, s) where s is a texture key (a sub-sequence of the immediately preceding context), making detection a single-pass O(n) operation over the text.

## Method

DiPmark's core mechanism is a permutation-based, distribution-preserving reweight strategy: the cipher space is the set of all vocabulary permutations, and for a given permutation θ, a CDF-threshold function F^α(i|θ) = (1/(1−α))·max{Σ_{j≤i} P_M(t_j|x) − α, 0} reshapes token probabilities by transferring mass from the low-probability tail [0,α] to the high-probability head [1−α,1]. The final DiP-reweight is a convex combination P_W = (1−α)·P_W^α + α·P_W^{1−α}, and by permutation symmetry the expectation over all ciphers exactly recovers P_M, yielding a provably distribution-preserving watermark (Theorem 4.3, Corollary 4.4). Detection uses a 'green token ratio' statistic Φ(γ,x) = L_G(γ)/n − (1−γ) with a KL-divergence concentration bound rather than the imprecise z-test, giving a guaranteed FPR. The paper further proves a certified radius ε_0 = (Φ−z)/(2+a−γ+z) under which the watermark remains detectable under arbitrary text modifications, establishing provable resilience.

## Results

DiPmark (α=0.45) preserves text quality nearly identically to the unwatermarked model (BLEU 21.9 vs. 21.8 on WMT'14 En-Ro; perplexity 5.024 vs. 5.021 on CNN-DM), while the Soft watermark degrades BLEU to 20.4 and perplexity to 5.660 at δ=1.5. For resilience, DiPmark achieves AUC 0.9515 at ε=0.2 and 0.8060 at ε=0.3 under random text modification, matching the Soft watermark (0.9521, 0.8033) while outperforming Kuditipudi (0.8979, 0.7815) and Hu (0.8391, 0.7574). Detection of 1,000 LLaMA-2 sequences takes 90 seconds without LM or prompt access, versus 12 hours for Kuditipudi and 412 seconds (with LM access) for Hu. At FPR≤0.01, DiPmark achieves TPR=0.9794 with perplexity 3.14, compared to Soft(δ=1.5) at TPR=0.9724 with perplexity 3.56.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Soft watermark (Kirchenbauer et al., 2023), DiPmark matches resilience (AUC 0.9515 vs. 0.9521 at ε=0.2) and even slightly exceeds it under paraphrasing (0.9503 vs. 0.9469 at ε=0.2), but critically preserves the original token distribution: DiPmark(α=0.45) yields BLEU 21.9 and perplexity 5.024, essentially identical to the unwatermarked model, whereas Soft(δ=1.5) drops BLEU to 20.4 and perplexity to 5.660. Against Kuditipudi et al. (2023), DiPmark is dramatically more accessible (90 s vs. 12 h for 1,000 sequences) and more resilient (AUC 0.9515 vs. 0.8979 at ε=0.2 random modification). Against Hu et al. (2023a), DiPmark eliminates the need for LM API and prompt access during detection (0.3 s vs. 3.4 s per sequence) and achieves substantially higher resilience (AUC 0.9515 vs. 0.8391 at ε=0.2). In detection quality at FPR≤0.01, DiPmark(α=0.45) reaches TPR=0.9794 with lower perplexity (3.14) than Soft(δ=1.5) at TPR=0.9724 (PPL 3.56), indicating better detectability without quality loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiPmark builds directly on the red-green list watermarking framework of Kirchenbauer et al. (2023), retaining the permutation-based cipher and hash-function architecture but replacing their logit-bias reweighting with a distribution-preserving reweight strategy based on permutation symmetry. It departs from prior distribution-preserving methods (Kuditipudi et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2023a) by eliminating the need for LM API access or thousands of resampling steps during detection, and from Kirchenbauer et al. by provably preserving the original token distribution rather than shifting it. The paper also introduces a KL-divergence-based concentration bound for the green token ratio statistic, replacing the imprecise z-test used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For industry deployment of LLMs (e.g., ChatGPT, Bard), a watermark that alters the output distribution is unacceptable, making distribution preservation a hard requirement rather than a nice-to-have. The provable resilience with a certified radius (Theorem 6.2) is a theoretical first for LLM watermarking, giving practitioners a concrete guarantee on how much text modification the watermark can withstand at a fixed false positive rate. Open questions include whether detectability can be improved without sacrificing distribution preservation (the paper notes this as future work), whether the top-5 token limitation in the GPT-4 case study generalizes, and whether the certified radius is practically large enough for real-world adversarial editing.
