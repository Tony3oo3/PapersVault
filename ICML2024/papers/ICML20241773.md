## Problem

Recovering Structural Causal Models from observational data is fundamentally difficult: the DAG search space is NP-hard, the functional relationships depend on the unknown graph making the problem ill-posed, and existing amortized or flow-based approaches either lack acyclicity guarantees, scale quadratically, or fail to exactly represent SCMs. No prior framework eliminates the DAG from the SCM parameterization while providing identifiability guarantees under weak assumptions.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a single observational dataset of n i.i.d. real-valued samples in d dimensions (no graph, no interventions). Stage 1 (TO inference) is zero-shot: a pre-trained amortized model sequentially predicts leaf nodes from the data, producing a permutation P ∈ Σ_d without any per-instance training. Stage 2 (SCM learning) then trains a transformer autoencoder conditioned on P, yielding a learned fixed-point function H whose Jacobian recovers the causal DAG, and whose iterated application generates counterfactual samples under arbitrary point interventions do(X_i = a).

## Method

The paper reformulates SCMs as fixed-point problems X = P^T H(PX, PN) on causally ordered variables, where H has a strictly lower-triangular Jacobian w.r.t. X and a diagonal Jacobian w.r.t. N (Condition 2.2), completely eliminating the DAG from the parameterization. For TO inference, an encoder-plus-linear-classifier model is amortized on synthetic SCMs using a differentiable topological ordering error (d-TOE) that sequentially predicts and removes leaf nodes, reducing the search from O(d!) permutations to d sequential binary predictions. For SCM learning, a transformer autoencoder with a novel causal attention mechanism—where the softmax normalization is relaxed via a value-dependent denominator so rows can sum to any value in [0,1], enabling root nodes with zero parents—parameterizes the function space F_d. In the additive-noise setting, minimizing MSE on the ordered observations provably recovers the generating function H uniquely, and the learned model supports exact counterfactual generation via iterated fixed-point application with estimated 1-D noise quantile functions.

## Results

On the out-of-distribution causal discovery benchmark, FiP achieves directed F1 scores of 0.76 (±0.20) on LIN OUT and 0.81 (±0.15) on RFF OUT, outperforming the strongest baseline AVICI (0.73/0.74) on both settings. For counterfactual prediction, FiP attains re-scaled ℓ₂ errors of 0.13 (±0.10) on LIN OUT and 0.13 (±0.096) on RFF OUT, roughly halving the error of DECI (0.39/0.18) and outperforming DoWhy-AVICI (0.20/0.16). The TO inference component generalizes to problems up to 2× the training dimension (d_test=200 vs d_train=100) with only a ~10% TOS drop on O.O.D data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FiP outperforms all eight causal discovery baselines on both O.O.D metadatasets. The closest competitor is AVICI (Lorch et al., 2022), which FiP edges out by 0.03 on LIN OUT (0.76 vs 0.73) and 0.07 on RFF OUT (0.81 vs 0.74); GOLEM is competitive on LIN OUT (0.73) but collapses on RFF OUT (0.31). DECI is the strongest on RFF OUT among non-amortized methods (0.74) but is far behind on LIN OUT (0.36). Score-based and constraint-based methods (PC, GES, DP-DAG) are consistently the weakest, with F1 scores below 0.57. For counterfactual prediction, FiP (0.13/0.13) halves the error of DECI (0.39/0.18) and beats DoWhy-AVICI (0.20/0.16) on both O.O.D settings, confirming that the fixed-point SCM parameterization yields more accurate interventional and counterfactual estimates than flow-based or graph-based alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the amortized causal inference framework of Lorch et al. (AVICI, NeurIPS 2022) by replacing full DAG prediction with sequential leaf prediction to infer topological orderings, reducing complexity from quadratic to linear in the number of variables. It extends the autoregressive flow and TMI-map identifiability theory of Khemakhem et al. (2021) and Javaloy et al. (2023) by formulating SCMs as fixed-point problems on causally ordered variables, which removes the monotonicity constraint and recovers the full structural equations rather than just the generative map. The key conceptual departure is eliminating DAGs entirely from the SCM parameterization via a fixed-point formulation with a lower-triangular Jacobian constraint, and introducing a 'causal attention' mechanism that relaxes the standard softmax row-sum-to-one constraint to permit modeling of root nodes (nodes with no parents).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The fixed-point formulation provides the weakest known conditions for unique SCM recovery given a topological ordering, and the two-stage pipeline (amortized TO inference + transformer-based SCM learning) offers a complete, DAG-free framework for causal discovery and counterfactual inference. However, the practical evaluation is restricted to the additive noise model setting, and the TO inference component is amortized on synthetic data, limiting true zero-shot applicability to unseen functional forms. Open questions include extending the framework to general (non-additive) noise, handling latent confounders, and whether the amortized TO predictor can be made fully distribution-agnostic. The causal attention mechanism, which allows attention rows to sum to values in [0,1], is a reusable architectural primitive that could benefit other structured sequence modeling tasks.
