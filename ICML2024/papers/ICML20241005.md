## Problem

While Large Language Models have become the dominant foundation-model paradigm across language, code, audio, and robotics, video generation has remained almost exclusively in the domain of diffusion-based methods that require architectural modifications and adapter modules for each new task. This paper asks whether the canonical LLM training protocol—large-scale multimodal pretraining followed by task-specific adaptation—can be applied to video generation to produce competitive, motion-rich videos while natively supporting diverse tasks (text-to-video, image-to-video, editing, stylization) within a single unified model.

## Input / Output

All modalities are converted into discrete integer tokens within a unified ~300K vocabulary: video and images are tokenized by MAGVIT-v2 into 1280 tokens (128×128, 17 frames at 8 fps) or 2240 tokens (128×224), audio by SoundStream into 106 frames × 4 RVQ levels (4096 codes), and text via frozen T5 XL embeddings projected into the transformer space. The base model autoregressively generates video and audio tokens at 128×128 or 128×224 resolution, which a separate super-resolution transformer then upscales in two 2× stages to 896×512. Because the first frame is tokenized independently with a consistent (1,16,16) shape, any still image can serve as a video seed, and the autoregressive structure allows iterative 1-second extensions to produce coherent clips of 10+ seconds.

## Method

VideoPoet is a decoder-only transformer that treats video, audio, and text as a single token sequence, trained with the standard LLM two-stage protocol: (1) pretraining on a mixture of multimodal generative objectives (text-to-video, text-to-image, unconditioned generation, frame prediction, inpainting/outpainting, audio-video continuation) using Alternating Gradient Descent to handle variable sequence lengths with near-zero padding, and (2) task-specific fine-tuning on high-quality subsets. A key training strategy is a two-phase data schedule—image-heavy (90/10) for the first 25% of iterations to build object understanding, then video-heavy (10/90) to capture motion dynamics. The super-resolution module is a non-autoregressive transformer operating in token space with MaxViT-style windowed local attention along vertical, horizontal, and temporal axes, using token factorization (k=2) to reduce the 262K-way classification into two 512-way problems, and trained with noise augmentation in the discrete latent space to bridge the distribution gap between real and generated low-resolution inputs.

## Results

On zero-shot text-to-video benchmarks, VideoPoet (Pretrain) achieves an FVD of 213 on MSR-VTT, substantially outperforming the next-best method Show-1 (538) by more than 2×, and an FVD of 355 on UCF-101, beating Make-A-Video (367) and Show-1 (394). On UCF-101, VideoPoet also achieves the highest Inception Score of 38.44 (vs. 35.42 for Show-1). After task-adaptation fine-tuning on a high-quality subset, VideoPoet reaches a CLIP similarity of 0.3123 on MSR-VTT, surpassing all prior methods including Show-1 (0.3072) and Make-A-Video (0.3049).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoPoet (Pretrain) achieves the best FVD on both MSR-VTT (213 vs. 538 for Show-1, 550 for ModelScopeT2V, 998 for MagicVideo, 1294 for CogVideo) and UCF-101 (355 vs. 367 for Make-A-Video, 394 for Show-1, 410 for VideoFactory, 551 for VideoLDM, 617 for InternVid, 655 for MagicVideo, 702 for CogVideo), demonstrating a clear advantage in temporal coherence. On UCF-101 Inception Score, VideoPoet leads all baselines (38.44 vs. 35.42 for Show-1, 33.45 for VideoLDM, 33.00 for Make-A-Video). After task-adaptation, VideoPoet achieves the highest CLIP similarity on MSR-VTT (0.3123 vs. 0.3072 for Show-1, 0.3049 for Make-A-Video). In the human evaluation against six commercial/research models (Show-1, VideoCrafter, Phenaki, Pika, Gen2, WALT, Lumiere), VideoPoet wins on motion interestingness and motion realism but is outperformed by Lumiere on video quality. For video stylization on DAVIS 2016, VideoPoet achieves a CLIP similarity of 0.3417, outperforming Control-A-Video (0.3246) by a notable margin, and human raters consistently prefer VideoPoet on both text fidelity and video quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoPoet departs from the dominant video-generation paradigm of fine-tuning pretrained image diffusion models for temporal consistency, instead following the canonical LLM training protocol (large-scale multimodal pretraining followed by task-specific adaptation) within a decoder-only autoregressive transformer. Its visual representation pipeline builds directly on MAGVIT-v2 for image/video tokenization and SoundStream for audio, while the super-resolution module extends the MAGVIT non-autoregressive transformer with windowed local attention. The multi-task pretraining strategy draws on GPT-3/PaLM-style task mixing and the Alternating Gradient Descent training method, and the overall design extends the LLM-for-image-generation line (DALL-E, Parti, CogVideo) from single-task text-to-image/video to a unified multi-modal, multi-task video generation framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that a token-based autoregressive LLM can match or exceed state-of-the-art diffusion video models on motion quality and enables capabilities rarely seen in diffusion systems—autoregressive long-video extension beyond 10 seconds and zero-shot task chaining (e.g., image-to-video followed by stylization) within a single model. Key limitations remain: the discrete token reconstruction imposes an upper bound on visual fidelity, small objects coupled with significant motion are still difficult, and the approach demands very large compute (8B parameters, ~2 trillion tokens). Open questions include whether the tokenization bottleneck can be overcome at higher resolutions, whether the multi-task LLM formulation scales favorably relative to diffusion at even larger scales, and how to address the observed fairness biases in generated content.
