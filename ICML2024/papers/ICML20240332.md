## Problem

Existing pedestrian trajectory prediction methods assume complete observed sequences, yet real-world data frequently contains missing values due to sensor malfunctions and occlusion. No prior approach simultaneously handles trajectory imputation and future trajectory prediction in a unified end-to-end framework, leaving a gap between imputation-only methods and prediction-only methods that cannot accommodate incomplete inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240332/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 8 observed frames (3.2 s) of 4-dimensional pedestrian features (x, y, vx, vy) with NaN entries marking missing coordinates, plus a single scene image per dataset processed once through a frozen VGG-19 backbone. It produces an imputed past trajectory, 20 sampled future endpoints (Ω=20) plus 20 guided endpoints, K=3 intermediate control points per sample, and a refined 12-frame (4.8 s) future trajectory per endpoint via a learned correction vector field added to linear interpolation.

## Method

MS-TIP comprises three coupled modules: (1) an imputation transformer with diagonal-masked self-attention (diagonal entries of the attention map set to −∞ to force cross-timestamp correlation) that reconstructs missing coordinates using a dual loss (Observed Reconstruction + Masked Imputation Task); (2) a multi-scale hypergraph convolutional network (HGCNN) that builds KNN-based hyperedges at scales {2,3,5,7,9} to capture pairwise and group interactions, with node embeddings initialized by concatenating coordinates with scenic attention features (VGG-19 scene features attended to per-agent positions); and (3) an endpoint-conditioned prediction head that samples K=3 control points from a GMM (via MDN), sums them to obtain endpoints, then refines the linearly interpolated trajectory with a GCN+TCN correction field conditioned on scenic embeddings. The imputation module is pre-trained on the full dataset and then fine-tuned jointly with the prediction pipeline, and the model is trained on both original and imputed sequences per batch to capture imputation's effect on prediction.

## Results

MS-TIP achieves an average ADE of 0.22 and FDE of 0.36 across the five pedestrian trajectory prediction benchmarks (ETH, HOTEL, UNIV, ZARA1, ZARA2), outperforming the strongest baseline G-Tern (0.24/0.38) by 8.3% in ADE and 5.3% in FDE. The best individual results are 0.39/0.57 on ETH and 0.13/0.22 on HOTEL. MS-TIP attains the top ADE/FDE on four of five datasets (ETH, HOTEL, UNIV, ZARA1) and ties for second-best on ZARA2 (0.17/0.29, matching G-Tern).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MS-TIP outperforms all ten baselines on average ADE/FDE (0.22/0.36 vs. the next-best G-Tern at 0.24/0.38). Against the closest competitor G-Tern (Bae & Jeon, 2023), MS-TIP wins on ETH (0.39 vs. 0.42 ADE), HOTEL (0.13 vs. 0.14), UNIV (0.24 vs. 0.26), and ZARA1 (0.20 vs. 0.21), while tying on ZARA2 (both 0.17/0.29). BCDiff (Li et al., 2023) achieves the best single-dataset result on ZARA2 (0.16/0.26) but trails MS-TIP on all other four datasets and in the overall average. GroupNet (Xu et al., 2022a), the multiscale hypergraph predecessor, is surpassed by MS-TIP on every dataset, with the largest gap on ETH (0.39 vs. 0.46 ADE). Older graph-based methods (STGAT, SGCN, CARPE) and memory-based approaches (MemoNet) show substantially larger errors, with MemoNet averaging 0.55/1.15 ADE/FDE, more than double MS-TIP's performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

MS-TIP sits at the intersection of three research threads: self-attention-based time-series imputation (SAITS), endpoint-conditioned trajectory prediction with intermediate control points (G-Tern), and multiscale hypergraph interaction modeling (GroupNet). The paper's key departure is unifying imputation and prediction into a single end-to-end pipeline, whereas prior imputation methods (NAOMI, GMAT) stop at filling missing values and prior prediction methods assume complete observations. The diagonal masked self-attention modification and the scenic attention module (VGG-19 scene features) are novel architectural choices not present in any of the individual building blocks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation—sensor occlusion and malfunction producing NaN values in real pedestrian tracking—is well-motivated, and the end-to-end formulation avoids the error compounding of two-stage pipelines. However, the evaluation is confined to five small-scale pedestrian datasets (750–786 pedestrians each) with fixed-camera scenes, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger, dynamic, or outdoor environments. The modest average improvement over G-Tern (0.22/0.36 vs. 0.24/0.38 ADE/FDE) and the finding that uniform hyperedge weights match or beat learned distance-based weights suggest the multiscale hypergraph's contribution may be less critical than the imputation and scenic attention components. Scalability to real-time deployment and to scenes with hundreds of agents remains unaddressed.
