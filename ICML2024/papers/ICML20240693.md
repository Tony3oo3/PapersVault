## Problem

Motion learning tasks (optical flow, depth estimation, object tracking, video stabilization) are typically addressed by task-specific models, leading to duplicated research and suboptimal hardware utilization. A fundamental challenge across these tasks is photometric and geometric inconsistency (shadows, occlusions, textureless regions) that introduces uncertainty in pixel-wise feature matching. The paper asks whether a single Transformer-based architecture incorporating prototype learning can serve as a unified motion learner across heterogeneous tasks at different dynamics granularities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240693/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For optical flow, the model takes a source-target image pair (cropped and tiled in a Perceiver IO–style scheme to handle resolution variations) and outputs a dense flow field; for depth, a single RGB image yields a depth map; for tracking and stabilization, video frames or full videos produce bounding boxes or stabilized frames, respectively. The architecture is built on the Twins backbone with two encoder stages (window sizes 4 and 8), and by default maintains 20 prototypes updated over 3 EM iterations per layer. The prototype assignment matrix M ∈ [0,1]^{K×HW} provides an interpretable pixel-to-prototype correspondence map as a byproduct.

## Method

ProtoFormer reformulates standard Transformer self-attention into an EM-clustering-based cross-attention mechanism called Cross-Attention Prototyping: K learnable prototypes act as queries while image feature patches serve as keys and values, with the softmax computed over the K dimension (not HW) to mimic soft cluster assignment. Each layer performs N iterations (default 3) of an E-step (computing soft pixel-prototype membership) and M-step (updating prototypes as weighted feature averages), guaranteeing monotonic improvement in the marginal likelihood. A second component, Latent Synchronization, applies a masked cross-attention where prototypes provide keys/values and image features provide queries, with the mask derived from prototype-feature similarity, followed by an FFN to refine representations. This design reduces complexity from O(H²W²D) to O(NKHW·D) (e.g., 60 vs. 25,920 in the first stage at 960×432), and the paper provides a formal convergence guarantee (Proposition 1) showing linear convergence of the EM iterates under a contractivity condition.

## Results

On optical flow (C+T+S+K+H training), ProtoFormer achieves 0.48 EPE on Sintel Clean and 0.69 on Sintel Final, matching FlowFormer on Clean and outperforming it by 0.05 on Final, while also achieving the best KITTI F1-epe of 0.50 and F1-all of 1.09. On scene depth, ProtoFormer attains 0.594 Abs Rel and 0.486 RMSE on Sintel (18.6% improvement over AdaBins in Abs Rel) and 0.062 Abs Rel / 2.716 RMSE on KITTI, outperforming P3Depth on Abs Rel. On object tracking (LaSOT), ProtoFormer-enhanced UTT reaches 64.8% Success and 67.4% Precision, and on video stabilization (DeepStab) it improves StabNet's Stability Score from 0.75 to 0.80.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On optical flow (C+T+S+K+H), ProtoFormer ties FlowFormer on Sintel Clean (0.48) but clearly outperforms it on Sintel Final (0.69 vs. 0.74) and KITTI F1-epe (0.50 vs. 0.53), while also achieving the best Sintel test Clean (1.06 vs. 1.20) and KITTI test F1-all (4.35 vs. 4.68). Against CRAFT, ProtoFormer improves Sintel Clean by 0.12 (0.48 vs. 0.60) and Sintel Final by 0.37 (0.69 vs. 1.06). On scene depth, ProtoFormer surpasses P3Depth on Sintel Abs Rel (0.594 vs. 0.653) and KITTI Abs Rel (0.062 vs. 0.071), though P3Depth retains a slight edge on Sintel RMSE (0.396 vs. 0.486) and KITTI δ1 (0.953 vs. 0.949). For object tracking, ProtoFormer-enhanced UTT marginally improves over UTT alone (64.8 vs. 64.6 Success on LaSOT) but remains below TransT (64.9 Success, 69.0 Precision). On video stabilization, ProtoFormer consistently boosts both StabNet and PWStableNet by 0.02–0.05 in Stability Score, with PWStableNet+Ours achieving the best overall Stability Score of 0.83.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProtoFormer builds on the Twins vision Transformer backbone and reformulates its self-attention mechanism as an EM clustering process over a small set of learned prototypes, drawing on the prototype learning literature from few-shot and zero-shot classification. Conceptually, it departs from prior optical flow Transformers (FlowFormer, CRAFT, GMFlow) that rely on dense pairwise attention or cost-volume matching by replacing them with a sparse, density-based prototyping layer that iteratively assigns pixels to K motion prototypes. The task-specific decoders follow FlowFormer and R-MsFM designs, so the architectural novelty is concentrated in the encoder's attention mechanism rather than the prediction head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a clean conceptual unification—treating optical flow, depth, tracking, and stabilization as different granularities of the same prototyping problem—which could reduce the proliferation of task-specific motion models. The O(NKHW·D) complexity (vs. O(H²W²·D) for self-attention) and the visual interpretability of learned prototypes are practical benefits for deployment. However, the 'unified' claim is partially aspirational: each task still requires a separate decoder and distinct training protocol, and the authors themselves flag out-of-distribution robustness as an open challenge. Whether the prototype-based attention generalizes beyond the tested motion tasks (e.g., to video understanding or 3D reconstruction) remains to be seen.
