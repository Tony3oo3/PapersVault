## Problem

Existing algorithms for non-smooth non-convex stochastic optimization (Zhang et al. 2020c; Tian et al. 2022; Cutkosky et al. 2023) either assume bounded gradient variance or provide only in-expectation guarantees, both of which are inadequate for the heavy-tailed gradient distributions observed in practice (e.g., BERT training). This paper establishes the first high-probability bound for finding (δ,ε)-stationary points when stochastic gradients have only finite p-th moments for p ∈ (1,2], bridging the gap between the bounded-variance theory and realistic heavy-tailed noise.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes the objective exclusively through a stochastic gradient oracle returning i.i.d. samples with bounded p-th central moments (no bounded variance or bounded norm required), along with an initial point x₀, a total query budget N, and a validation round length R. The output is a single validated (δ,ε)-stationary point, selected from K candidate centers each accompanied by a support set of T points within a δ-ball; for smooth objectives, setting δ = ε/H converts the output to a 2ε-stationary point with Õ(ε^{-(3p-2)/(p-1)}) queries.

## Method

The core contribution is integrating gradient clipping into the online-to-non-convex framework of Cutkosky et al. (2023): the OGD update uses clipped gradients ĝₙ = (gₙ/‖gₙ‖)min(τ, ‖gₙ‖) rather than raw stochastic gradients, which makes the shifting regret controllable with high probability. The proof decomposes the objective decrease into five terms bounded by distinct concentration inequalities—sub-exponential sums for clipped gradient norms, Hoeffding-Azuma for one martingale difference sequence, and Freedman's inequality for another—with the clipping parameter τ = T^{1/p}(σ^p + G^p)^{1/p} log(2K/q)^{-1/p} balancing bias and variance. A separate validation subroutine estimates the averaged gradient norm over each candidate's support set using clipped gradients in O(ε^{-p/(p-1)} log(1/q)) queries, eliminating the 1/q factor present in prior validation methods. The final algorithm iteratively samples candidates uniformly and accepts the first whose estimated norm falls below 5ε/6, yielding a valid (δ,ε)-stationary point with Õ(ε^{-(2p-1)/(p-1)} δ^{-1}) total gradient queries.

## Results

The paper is primarily theoretical, with the headline result being a high-probability gradient complexity of Õ(ε^{-(2p-1)/(p-1)} δ^{-1}) for finding a (δ, ε)-stationary point under heavy-tailed gradients (p ∈ (1,2]). In the bounded-variance special case (p = 2), this reduces to Õ(ε^{-3} δ^{-1}), improving upon the prior best high-probability bound of Õ(ε^{-4} δ^{-1}). For smooth objectives, the algorithm achieves Õ(ε^{-(3p-2)/(p-1)}) queries, nearly matching the known in-expectation lower bound. A preliminary experiment on ResNet-18 trained on CIFAR-10 with injected Pareto(α=1.5) heavy-tailed gradient noise shows CGA achieving the highest training and testing accuracy among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CGA (the proposed Candidate Generation Algorithm) against three baselines on CIFAR-10 with heavy-tailed noise: SGD with momentum, SINGD (Zhang et al., 2020c), and ONCCA (Cutkosky et al., 2023). SGD with momentum fails to converge under the heavy-tailed noise, while SINGD and ONCCA make steady progress but are outperformed by CGA on both training and testing accuracy. The theoretical comparison is more striking: when p = 2 (bounded variance), CGA's Õ(ε^{-3} δ^{-1}) high-probability bound strictly improves over the Õ(ε^{-4} δ^{-1}) achieved by both Zhang et al. (2020c) and Tian et al. (2022), and also reduces the dependence on the gradient bound G and noise level σ from (σ²+G²)^{3/2} to (σ²+G²). The paper notes that SINGD and ONCCA do not explicitly handle heavy-tailed gradients, and their normalization steps only partially mitigate the effects, whereas CGA's gradient clipping provides a principled mechanism that yields the improved theoretical guarantee.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online-to-non-convex framework of Cutkosky et al. (2023), which reduces non-smooth non-convex optimization to online learning with shifting regret, by replacing raw stochastic gradients with clipped gradients in the OGD update to handle heavy-tailed distributions. The clipping bias/variance bounds are inherited from Zhang et al. (2020b) and Zhang & Cutkosky (2022), who established them for smooth non-convex optimization. The key departure from prior non-smooth non-convex work (Zhang et al. 2020c, Tian et al. 2022, Cutkosky et al. 2023) is the removal of the bounded-variance assumption, and the key departure from prior heavy-tailed work (Zhang et al. 2020b, Cutkosky & Mehta 2021, Nguyen et al. 2023) is the extension to non-smooth objectives with an explicit validation step that selects a single stationary point rather than guaranteeing existence within a candidate set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical treatment of non-smooth non-convex stochastic optimization under heavy-tailed gradients, filling a gap where prior results either assumed bounded variance or restricted to smooth objectives. The improvement from Õ(ε^{-4}δ^{-1}) to Õ(ε^{-3}δ^{-1}) in the bounded-variance special case (p=2) is a genuine tightening of the high-probability bound, and the smooth-objective corollary matches the known lower bound up to logarithmic factors. However, the algorithm requires knowledge of problem parameters σ and G to set the clipping threshold, and the empirical validation is preliminary (single architecture, single dataset, manually injected Pareto noise), leaving open whether the theoretical gains translate to practical training scenarios. Future directions include parameter-free clipping and time-varying thresholds.
