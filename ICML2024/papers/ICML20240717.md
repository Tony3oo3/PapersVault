## Problem

Diffusion-based offline RL methods suffer from non-deterministic sampling that produces unstable decisions sensitive to random seeds and computing platforms, with no mechanism to quantify the reliability of sampled trajectories. Existing confidence estimation approaches (e.g., conformal prediction) require ground-truth labels that are unavailable in offline RL settings where collected data may be suboptimal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240717/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes the current state (and goal state for goal-conditioned tasks) as input to a pretrained Diffuser, which generates candidate action trajectories. A lightweight RND-based confidence network (1D conv + FC layers, much smaller than Diffuser) then scores each candidate trajectory, producing a confidence score in (-∞, 0] that is negatively correlated with the Euclidean distance between predictor and target network outputs. The final output is the selected action trajectory with the highest confidence-adjusted sampling probability, enabling real-time reliability evaluation during environment interaction.

## Method

ReDiffuser extends Random Network Distillation to confidence estimation for diffusion-based offline RL by training a target-predictor network pair on historical trajectories generated by a pretrained Diffuser, where the confidence function is defined as the negative Euclidean distance between the two networks' outputs. This confidence score is integrated into the sampling process via a confidence guidance function g(τ|c_φ) = exp(β·c_φ(τ)/α(h)) that reweights the task-guided distribution toward more reliable decisions. For goal-conditioned tasks, adaptive-horizon planning collects training trajectories at multiple horizon lengths (multiples of 32) and uses a confidence discount α(h) = (h/h_N)^p to prefer shorter horizons when the goal is nearby. For reward-maximization tasks, value-embedded planning trains RND exclusively on the highest-value trajectory among 64 candidates per state, embedding value information into the confidence signal so that overestimated values are penalized as out-of-distribution.

## Results

On Maze2D, ReDiffuser achieves an average score of 148.4 (Single) and 160.1 (Multi), representing an 18.2% improvement over Diffuser (127.5 and 133.4 respectively). On KUKA block stacking, ReDiffuser scores 67.0±1.5 vs. Diffuser's 60.5±1.1. On D4RL Locomotion (HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2d across Medium-Expert, Medium, and Medium-Replay), ReDiffuser achieves an average of 82.6, outperforming Diffuser (80.3) and all non-diffusion baselines (BC 51.9, CQL 77.6, IQL 77.0, DT 74.7, TT 78.9, MOPO 42.1, MOReL 72.9, MBOP 47.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReDiffuser consistently outperforms Diffuser across all three task suites: +6.5 on KUKA, +20.9 (Single) / +26.7 (Multi) on Maze2D, and +2.3 on Locomotion average. Against non-diffusion offline RL baselines (BCQ, CQL, IQL, MPPI, DT, TT, MOPO, MOReL, MBOP), ReDiffuser achieves the highest scores in every setting, with particularly large margins over model-free methods like MPPI (16.2 avg on Maze2D) and MOPO (42.1 avg on Locomotion). Among diffusion-based competitors in Maze2D (Table 6), ReDiffuser leads AdaptDiffuser (148.4 vs. 144.3 Single avg), DS (131.5), HDMI (123.5), and HD (139.9). In Locomotion (Table 7, appendix), ReDiffuser (79.1 avg) trails Diffusion-QL (88.0), HD (84.6), and AdaptDiffuser (83.4) in average score, though it remains competitive and beats Diffuser (76.3) and DS (81.1) on individual tasks like Medium Hopper (100.2 vs. 92.9 for Diffuser). The method's advantage is most pronounced in goal-conditioned navigation (Maze2D) where adaptive-horizon planning provides the largest gains, while in reward-maximization locomotion the value-embedded planning yields more modest but consistent improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Diffuser (Janner et al., 2022), which frames offline RL as trajectory generation via iterative denoising, and adds a reliability layer on top of its task-guided sampling distribution. The key conceptual repurposing is adapting Random Network Distillation (Burda et al., 2019)—originally an intrinsic exploration bonus in online RL—as a lightweight out-of-distribution detector for diffusion-sampled decisions, sidestepping the need for ground-truth labels that conformal prediction methods (Angelopoulos et al., 2022; Horwitz & Hoshen, 2022) require. The confidence guidance mechanism then modulates the sampling distribution, yielding two task-specific variants (adaptive-horizon and value-embedded planning) that go beyond the fixed-horizon, value-only guidance of the original Diffuser.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in diffusion-based offline RL: the non-determinism of iterative denoising makes sampled decisions sensitive to random seeds and hardware, which is problematic in safety-critical settings like robotic manipulation. The RND-based confidence function is computationally cheap (small 1D-CNN + FC layers) and requires no retraining of the diffusion model, making it an easy drop-in addition. However, validation is limited to relatively simple tasks (Maze2D navigation, MuJoCo locomotion, KUKA stacking), the method inherits Diffuser's lack of exploration, and the authors acknowledge that sequential action execution in locomotion fails due to cumulative state bias—leaving open whether the approach transfers to more complex, real-world decision-making.
