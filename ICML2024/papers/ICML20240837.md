## Problem

Existing causal dynamics models in RL capture only global causal structures, missing fine-grained relationships that manifest exclusively under specific contexts (e.g., a traffic signal becomes irrelevant when a pedestrian is present). Sample-specific approaches can detect these local dependencies but lack interpretability and fail to generalize to unseen states. The paper asks how to discover and exploit context-specific causal structures in a principled, generalizable way for robust model-based RL.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240837/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a factored state vector (s₁,…,s_N) and action vector (a₁,…,a_M) from a factored MDP with fully observable states. It produces a predicted next state, a discrete subgroup assignment (one of K codes from a learned codebook), and a binary adjacency matrix (local causal graph) specifying which state/action variables causally influence each next-state variable within that subgroup. The LCG is used to mask irrelevant features during prediction, so the dynamics model only attends to locally relevant variables per context.

## Method

The core mechanism is a discrete latent codebook C = {e₁,…,e_K} that partitions the state-action space into K subgroups, where each code e_z jointly represents a subgroup E_z and its corresponding local causal graph G_z. An encoder maps (s,a) to a latent embedding h, which is quantized to the nearest code via VQ-VAE; a decoder then maps the code to a Bernoulli adjacency matrix A (sampled via Gumbel-Softmax) encoding the LCG for that subgroup. The dynamics model uses A to mask out features of non-causal variables when predicting each next-state component, and training jointly optimizes a masked prediction loss with L₁ sparsity regularization on A plus a standard VQ quantization loss in an end-to-end fashion. Theoretically, the paper proves identifiability of true LCGs via score maximization (Thm 1), that the optimal decomposition minimizes expected graph size (Prop 1), and that with sufficient K the method recovers all meaningful contexts (Thm 2).

## Results

FCDL achieves the highest downstream episode reward across all environments and noise levels. In Chemical (full-fork, n=2 noisy nodes), FCDL scores 15.27±2.53, outperforming the next-best NCD (10.95±1.63) by ~4 points and CDL (9.37±1.33) by ~6 points. In the Magnetic environment, FCDL achieves 4.81±3.01 on the OOD test, a 3.7× improvement over NCD (1.31±0.77) and 4.4× over CDL (1.10±0.67). On prediction accuracy in Chemical (full-fork, n=2), FCDL reaches 66.44% versus 41.60% for NCD and 35.59% for CDL, while dense models (MLP, Modular, GNN) drop to ~25–36%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FCDL consistently outperforms all five baselines on every downstream (OOD) task. Against dense models (MLP, Modular, GNN, NPS), the gap is largest in Magnetic where FCDL scores 4.81 versus 0.86–0.92 for dense baselines, reflecting their inability to discard locally spurious dependencies. Against global causal models (CDL, GRADER), FCDL gains ~5–6 points in Chemical (e.g., 15.27 vs. 9.37 at n=2) and ~3.7 points in Magnetic (4.81 vs. 1.10), demonstrating that fine-grained subgroup-level causal reasoning is strictly more robust than a single global causal graph. Against the sample-specific approach NCD, FCDL wins by ~4 points in Chemical (15.27 vs. 10.95) and by a factor of ~3.7 in Magnetic (4.81 vs. 1.31); the paper further shows via structural hamming distance (Fig. 7) that NCD's inferred LCGs are inconsistent between ID and OOD states and include spurious edges, whereas FCDL's subgroup-quantized LCGs remain stable and accurate. Notably, FCDL also surpasses the Oracle (which uses the true global causal graph) on all OOD tasks, confirming that fine-grained context-specific structure is strictly more informative than the global graph for robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends causal dynamics learning (CDL, GRADER) from global causal graphs to context-specific local causal graphs (LCGs), and generalizes sample-specific local independence inference (NCD) to a subgroup level via vector quantization. The key conceptual departure is repurposing VQ-VAE's discrete codebook—not for observation reconstruction as in prior generative work—but as a proxy for decomposing the state-action space into subgroups, each paired with its own causal structure. Theoretically, the method subsumes both prior causal dynamics models (K=1) and sample-specific approaches (K→∞) as special cases, positioning itself as a principled middle ground that provides identifiability guarantees absent in either extreme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world causal dependencies are often context-specific (e.g., a traffic signal is irrelevant when a pedestrian is present), and global causal models cannot capture this. The identifiability theorems (Thm. 1, Thm. 2) provide a clean theoretical foundation, and the empirical results show large robustness gains on OOD states. However, evaluation is limited to two small, purpose-built environments with fully observable factored states; scalability to high-dimensional or partially observable settings, integration with conditional independence tests for calibration, and validation on larger-scale tasks remain open. The codebook collapsing issue at small K and the need for factored state representations are practical constraints that limit immediate applicability.
