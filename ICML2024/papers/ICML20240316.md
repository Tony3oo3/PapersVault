## Problem

While prior work has shown that DNNs encode a small number of salient AND-OR interactions at the final output, no method existed to extract, quantify, and compare these interactions at intermediate layers, where features are high-dimensional vectors rather than scalar scores. This gap prevented tracking how knowledge (newly emerged vs. forgotten interactions) changes layer by layer during forward propagation, and thus blocked a principled link between layer-wise representation changes and generalization capacity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240316/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained DNN (VGG-11, ResNet-20/32, MLP-7, DistilBERT, BERT_BASE, or XLNet), a single input sample, and a small set of annotated semantic parts (10 foreground 3×3 patches for MNIST, 12 center 4×4 patches for CIFAR-10, or 10 words for SST-2) that define the n input variables over which interactions are computed. For each intermediate layer, a linear classifier probe is trained on that layer's features to produce a scalar log-odds score v^(l)(x), which is then decomposed into AND-OR interaction effects. Outputs include per-layer interaction strengths decomposed into overlap/forget/new components, completeness and redundancy ratios, cross-model IoU scores for generalization, and noise-stability metrics (|mean|/√variance under Gaussian perturbations).

## Method

The key technical contribution is extending the AND-OR interaction decomposition from the scalar network output to intermediate layers by training a linear classifier probe p^(l)(y|x) on each layer's features and defining a scalar score v^(l)(x) = log-odds minus a learnable residual δ_T (constrained to |δ_T| < κ = 0.04·|v^(l)(x_N) − v^(l)(x_∅)|) that absorbs tiny output noise. The standard Möbius-inversion formulas for AND and OR interactions are then applied to v^(l)(x_T) over all 2^n masked subsets, yielding per-layer interaction effects I_and(S|x, v^(l)) and I_or(S|x, v^(l)). To track layer-wise change, the paper defines three decomposition metrics—overlap, forget, and new—that partition the total interaction strength of layer l relative to the final layer L, plus completeness (overlap/all_final) and redundancy (forget/all_layer) ratios. For generalization, IoU of salient interaction sets across two independently trained DNNs is computed per order m; for stability, the ratio |E_ε[I(S|x+ε)]|/√Var_ε[I(S|x+ε)] is measured under Gaussian noise. The critical empirical finding is that adjacent layers encode highly similar interaction sets, making this probe-based scalarization a faithful and comparable representation of layer-wise knowledge without requiring explicit feature-space alignment.

## Results

The paper verifies interaction sparsity, finding only ~21.8 salient AND/OR interactions per MNIST image and ~45.6 per CIFAR-10 image, with all other interactions exhibiting negligible effects. Tracking through layers reveals that early and middle layers already learn most target interactions (completeness rises early), while later layers primarily remove redundant high-order interactions (redundancy first rises then falls). Low-order interactions exhibit higher IoU values across different DNNs trained on the same task (e.g., ResNet-20 vs. ResNet-32 on CIFAR-10, DistilBERT vs. BERT_BASE on SST-2), confirming stronger generalization. Stability metrics show that low-order interactions maintain more consistent effects under Gaussian noise (ε~N(0, 0.02²I)) than high-order interactions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an interpretability/analysis paper rather than a method paper, so it does not propose a new model to benchmark against baselines. Instead, it applies its interaction-tracking framework across seven architectures (MLP-7, VGG-11, ResNet-20, ResNet-32, DistilBERT, BERT_BASE, XLNet) on four datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, SST-2, UCI Census) to reveal consistent layerwise patterns. The paper qualitatively compares its interaction-based explanation against attribution methods (Integrated Gradients, Shapley values), arguing that interactions provide a more precise decomposition of inference logic (Fig. 6) and that Shapley values can be rewritten as a re-allocation of AND-OR interaction effects. Across all tested architectures, the same qualitative findings hold: low-order interactions dominate in strength, generalize better across models, and are more stable to perturbation, while high-order interactions are redundant, non-generalizable, and unstable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AND-OR interaction framework established by Li & Zhang (2023) and Ren et al. (2023a, 2024) from the final network output to intermediate layers, using a linear probing classifier to define a scalar score per layer. It departs from information-bottleneck approaches (Shwartz-Ziv & Tishby, 2017) and attribution methods (Shapley values, Integrated Gradients) by providing a countable, symbolic decomposition of knowledge rather than a continuous information-theoretic or per-variable importance measure. The paper also builds on Zhou et al. (2023) for the OR-interaction extension and the learned {γ_T} decomposition, and positions itself against concept-based interpretability (Bau et al., 2017; Kim et al., 2018) by offering mathematically guaranteed boundaries for each interaction primitive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The layerwise interaction tracking provides the first countable, per-layer quantification of newly emerged and forgotten knowledge in DNNs, connecting interaction order to both generalization capacity (via cross-model IoU) and robustness (via noise stability). A key limitation is the reliance on linear probes, which may miss nonlinearly-encoded knowledge, and the computational cost of 2^n interaction extraction restricts analysis to small n (10–12 annotated patches/words). Open questions include whether the redundancy-removal pattern holds for very deep or large-scale models, whether the linear probe captures all task-relevant structure, and how the framework extends to generative or multi-task settings.
