## Problem

In linear non-Gaussian acyclic models (LiNGAM) with latent confounders, prior graphical identifiability criteria (Salehkaleybar et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2022) only characterize when the entire mixing matrix can be recovered simultaneously. In practice, one often needs only a specific causal effect between a pair of observed variables, and some effects may be identifiable even when the full matrix is not. This paper provides the first necessary and sufficient graphical conditions for the generic identifiability of individual direct or total causal effects, in both the known-graph and unknown-graph settings.

## Input / Output

The identifiability algorithms take a canonical DAG partitioned into observed and latent nodes, the children sets of all nodes, and a query pair (j, i) of observed variables, and return a boolean indicating whether the total or direct causal effect of j on i is generically identifiable; the check runs in O(p³) time. For estimation, the method consumes N observational samples X₁,…,X_N ∈ R^{p_o} drawn from the lvLiNGAM model along with the known DAG structure, and outputs the estimated mixing matrix B' (and hence the causal effect entries). The estimation is an unsupervised optimization over the constrained parameter space R^G defined by the DAG's sparsity pattern.

## Method

The theoretical core establishes that the total causal effect of j on i is generically identifiable (without graph knowledge) if and only if no latent variable l satisfies both de_o(l) = de_o(j) and i ∈ de_o^{G\j}(l) (i.e., i is reachable from l without passing through j); the direct effect criterion additionally requires i ∈ ch(l) and k ∈ ch(j) ∪ {j} for some observed k with de_o(k) = de_o(l). When the graph is known, Theorem 3.6 further restricts admissible column permutations of B', adding the condition ch(l) \ ch(k) = ∅ for all k ∈ pa(j) ∪ {j}, which recovers identifiability in cases like the instrumental variable graph. For estimation, the authors propose Graphical RICA (GRICA), which adapts the RICA overcomplete ICA algorithm by dropping the reconstruction loss (since orthonormality of B' rows is incompatible with nonzero causal effects) and constraining the optimization to the DAG's sparsity pattern, reducing the problem dimensionality from p² to |E| edges and improving both stability and accuracy over unconstrained RICA.

## Results

GRICA consistently recovers the correct causal effect for both G1 and G2 graphs even with a small number of samples, while the RICA baseline often gets stuck in bad local minima and is unstable. On larger Erdős-Rényi graphs (one latent + five observed, and two latent + ten observed variables), GRICA significantly outperforms RICA in normalized Frobenius loss of the estimated mixing matrix. The identification algorithm (Algorithm 1) handles graphs with 1,000 nodes in approximately one second, with a proven O(p³) complexity. For random graphs, all causal effects are identifiable with knowledge of the graph, while the probability of identifiability without the graph drops with increasing edge density and decreasing proportion of observed nodes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRICA is compared against three baselines: the RICA adaptation from Salehkaleybar et al. (2020), the Cross-Moment method from Kivva et al. (2023), and the proximal causal learning method from Tchetgen et al. (2020). Against RICA, GRICA wins decisively on larger Erdős-Rényi graphs (Figures 10a–b), where RICA's unconstrained optimization over p² parameters leads to poor mixing matrix recovery, while GRICA's graph-constrained optimization over |E| parameters yields substantially lower normalized Frobenius loss. Against Cross-Moment, GRICA is more robust at small sample sizes, while Cross-Moment (being a consistent estimator for G1) eventually surpasses GRICA when enough samples are available to accurately estimate high-order moments. In the G3 proxy setting (Figure 9b), GRICA outperforms both RICA and Tchetgen et al. (2020) because it leverages the noiseless observation of L₁ through proxy Z, whereas the other methods are degraded by noise in observations from W.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the identifiability framework of Salehkaleybar et al. (2020), which provided necessary and sufficient graphical criteria for recovering the *entire* mixing matrix in latent-variable LiNGAM, to the more granular problem of identifying *individual* direct or total causal effects between specific pairs of observed variables. It also builds on the RICA algorithm (Le et al., 2011) for overcomplete ICA, adapting it with graph-structure constraints to reduce the optimization dimensionality from p² to |E|. The paper departs from prior work (Kivva et al., 2023; Shuai et al., 2023) that provided identification formulas only for specific causal graph topologies, by giving complete graphical criteria that apply to any DAG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The complete graphical characterization (necessary and sufficient conditions) for individual causal effects, combined with polynomial-time certification algorithms, gives practitioners a clear and efficient way to determine which effects are recoverable before attempting estimation. The results show that standard assumptions in causal inference—such as requiring as many instruments as treatments or satisfying proxy variable conditions—can be relaxed under the LiNGAM non-Gaussianity assumption, broadening the class of identifiable models. Open questions include extending the framework to cyclic models, relaxing the non-Gaussianity requirement via sparse mixing structures, and establishing consistency guarantees for the Graphical RICA estimator, which currently lacks rigorous large-sample analysis.
