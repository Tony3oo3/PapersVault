## Problem

Multi-view graph clustering is widely used in practice but lacks theoretical understanding. The paper formalizes a multi-view stochastic block model where each of t views is a 2-community SBM with a random binary projection of the true k-community structure, so no single view contains enough information to recover the full clustering. The central question is how many views are needed for efficient recovery, and whether the natural early-fusion strategy (merging graphs then clustering) is optimal.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes t graphs G_1,...,G_t on n vertices (each sampled from a 2-community SBM with parameters d_ℓ, ε_ℓ under a random binary mapping f_ℓ of the true k-community labels) and the number of communities k, and outputs a community vector ẑ ∈ [k]^n. Crucially, each individual graph only reveals a random bipartition of the k communities, so the full structure is recoverable only by combining information across views. The algorithm requires each view to be above the relative Kesten-Stigum threshold (d_ℓ·ε_ℓ²/4 > 1).

## Method

The paper's late-fusion algorithm (Algorithm 1) first applies a specialized pairwise weak-recovery estimator (built on node-robust SBM algorithms from Ding et al.) to each graph independently, producing matrices X̂_ℓ that estimate f_ℓ(z)f_ℓ(z)^T with pairwise correlation C_ℓ. It then sums the pairwise scores across all t views and constructs a directed graph where each vertex connects to its top n/k neighbors by summed score. A second-moment rounding procedure (Algorithm 2) then recovers communities by iteratively picking a representative row and grouping all vertices whose rows are within distance n/k of it. The key insight is that ∑_ℓ f_ℓ(z)_i f_ℓ(z)_j strongly correlates with [z_i = z_j], and with t = O(log k / C_T²) views, concentration ensures the constructed graph approximates the true community matrix well enough for exact or weak recovery. This achieves an exponential improvement over early fusion (union graph), which requires t = Ω(k²) views.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1.2) shows that weak recovery in the multi-view SBM is achievable in polynomial time with only O(log k) observations, each above the Kesten-Stigum threshold (d_ℓ·ε_ℓ²/4 > 1), compared to Ω(k²) observations required by the naive union-graph (early fusion) approach. Exact recovery (Corollary 1.4) is achievable with O(log n) observations. An information-theoretic lower bound (Theorem 1.3) shows that t = Ω(log k / C_T) observations are necessary for any blackbox algorithm processing pairwise correlation estimates, confirming the near-optimality of the upper bound up to a factor of C_T. Experiments on synthetic (d,ε,k,t)-MV-SBM instances (n=1000, k=10, t=10) confirm that the late-fusion Algorithm 1 achieves higher agreement than Louvain on the union graph across varying ε ∈ [0.5, 1.5] and d ∈ [50, 150].

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its late-fusion Algorithm 1 against the natural early-fusion baseline of applying Louvain's algorithm (Blondel et al., 2008) directly on the union graph ∪G_ℓ. On synthetic (d,ε,k,t)-MV-SBM instances with n=1000, k=10, t=10, Algorithm 1 consistently achieves higher agreement (Equation 2) than the union-graph baseline across all tested parameter ranges. In Figure 1 (varying ε from 0.5 to 1.5 with d=50), the late-fusion method maintains substantially higher agreement, with the gap widening as ε increases. In Figure 2 (varying d from 50 to 150 with ε=0.5), Algorithm 1 again outperforms the union-graph approach, with both methods improving as d increases but the late-fusion method maintaining a clear advantage. The theoretical analysis explains this gap: the union graph behaves as a k-community SBM with bias O(ε) and degree dt, requiring t = Ω(k²) for weak recovery, while the late-fusion approach only needs O(log k) observations by exploiting the full pairwise correlation structure of each view.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-layer stochastic block model literature (Paul & Chen, 2016; Han et al., 2015; De Bacco et al., 2017), where each layer encodes the full k-community structure, to a more general and realistic setting where each view only reveals a random 2-community partition of the k underlying communities. The algorithmic core builds on the node-robust weak recovery algorithms of Ding et al. (2022, 2023), which achieve the Kesten-Stigum threshold for 2-community SBMs, and on the concurrent multi-view SBM formulation of De Santiago et al. (2023). The paper's key conceptual departure is the formal separation between early fusion (clustering the union graph) and late fusion (clustering each view independently then merging), with the latter shown to be exponentially superior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exponential gap between early and late fusion (Ω(k²) vs. O(log k) observations) provides a rigorous theoretical justification for processing multi-view data separately before combining, a principle widely used in practice but previously lacking formal grounding in this model. The matching information-theoretic lower bound for black-box estimators (via Fano's inequality) establishes that the O(log k) dependence is essentially tight for this class of algorithms. Key open questions include characterizing the exact phase transition threshold as a function of per-view signal-to-noise ratio and number of views, and generalizing the analysis to views with more than 2 communities, which would require robust weak recovery algorithms for k > 2 that do not currently exist.
