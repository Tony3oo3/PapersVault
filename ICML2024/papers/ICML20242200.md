## Problem

Applying Transformers to PDE solving on large-scale meshes faces a dual challenge: canonical attention is computationally prohibitive at quadratic complexity over tens of thousands of mesh points, and directly attending over individual points fails to capture the intricate, high-order physical correlations (e.g., fluid-structure interactions, multiphysics couplings) that PDEs exhibit, especially on unstructured or hybrid geometries. Prior linear-attention approaches still operate on raw mesh points, which overwhelms the model's ability to learn informative relations, while Fourier-based methods degrade on complex non-periodic boundaries.

## Input / Output

The model takes as input a set of N discretized mesh points with C_g-dimensional coordinates (and optionally C_u observed physical quantities per point), supporting point clouds, structured meshes, regular grids, and unstructured meshes in 2D and 3D, with mesh sizes ranging from 972 (Elasticity) to 32,186 (Shape-Net Car). It outputs a predicted physical field (pressure, velocity, or stress) at each mesh point, and for aerodynamic design tasks additionally yields drag and lift coefficients computed from the estimated surface pressure and surrounding velocity. The number of slices M (32 or 64) is a hyperparameter independent of N, keeping the token count fixed regardless of mesh resolution.

## Method

Transolver replaces standard self-attention with Physics-Attention, which first projects each mesh point's deep feature into M learnable slice weights via a point-wise linear layer followed by softmax, then aggregates all N mesh points into M physics-aware tokens by weighted averaging (Eq. 2). Standard multi-head attention is then computed among only these M tokens rather than N mesh points, and the result is desliced back to mesh points using the same weights (Eq. 4), yielding O(NMC + M²C) complexity that is linear in N. The key insight is that mesh points under similar physical states (e.g., front vs. back of a car, different extrusion zones in elasticity) are naturally grouped into the same slice, so attention operates on physically meaningful, state-homogeneous tokens rather than individual geometric points. The paper further proves (Theorem 3.4) that Physics-Attention is equivalent to a learnable integral operator on the continuous domain Ω, extending the Monte-Carlo integral interpretation of canonical attention to the slice domain via a diffeomorphism.

## Results

Transolver achieves consistent state-of-the-art relative L2 error across all six standard PDE benchmarks, with headline improvements of 25.6% on Elasticity (0.0064 vs. GNOT's 0.0086), 29.4% on Plasticity (0.0012 vs. OFormer's 0.0017), 29.7% on Pipe (0.0033 vs. GNOT's 0.0047), and 24.7% on Navier-Stokes (0.0900 vs. ONO's 0.1195). On the two large-scale industrial design tasks, Transolver reduces drag coefficient error to 0.0103 on Shape-Net Car (vs. 3D-GeoCA's 0.0159) and lift coefficient error to 0.1030 on AirfRANS (vs. GINO's 0.1821), while achieving Spearman rank correlations of 0.9935 and 0.9978 respectively. The method also demonstrates favorable efficiency, using 5× fewer parameters and 1.3× faster inference than GNOT on Elasticity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Transolver outperforms all 20+ baselines across all six standard benchmarks. Against the strongest Transformer-based competitors, it beats GNOT (Hao et al., 2023) on Elasticity (0.0064 vs. 0.0086), Pipe (0.0033 vs. 0.0047), Navier-Stokes (0.0900 vs. 0.1380), and Darcy (0.0057 vs. 0.0105), and surpasses ONO (Xiao et al., 2024) on every benchmark, most notably Navier-Stokes (0.0900 vs. 0.1195) and Darcy (0.0057 vs. 0.0076). Among neural operators, it dominates LSM (Wu et al., 2023) on all six tasks, with the largest gap on Elasticity (0.0064 vs. 0.0218) and Navier-Stokes (0.0900 vs. 0.1535). On the industrial design tasks, Transolver substantially outperforms geo-FNO (Li et al., 2022) on Shape-Net Car (volume L2: 0.0207 vs. 0.1670) and AirfRANS (volume L2: 0.0037 vs. 0.0361), and beats 3D-GeoCA (Deng et al., 2024) on Shape-Net Car (volume: 0.0207 vs. 0.0319, C_D: 0.0103 vs. 0.0159) and GINO (Li et al., 2023) on AirfRANS (C_L: 0.1030 vs. 0.1821). The paper attributes the gains to Physics-Attention's ability to capture physically coherent states rather than attending over individual mesh points, which is particularly beneficial for complex fluid-structure interactions in Darcy and multiphysics car/airfoil simulations.

## Relation to Prior Work

Transolver extends the Transformer-based PDE solver lineage (GNOT, OFormer, ONO, FactFormer) by replacing direct attention over mesh points with attention over learned physics-aware tokens. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant 'model relations between individual discretized points' paradigm and instead proposes a bottom-up decomposition into learnable slices that group points under similar physical states, drawing an analogy to ViT patchification but with adaptive, physics-driven shapes. Theoretically, it extends the integral-operator equivalence proof from Cao (2021) and Kovachki et al. (2023) to the slice domain via a diffeomorphism argument, showing Physics-Attention is also a learnable integral operator. The design is orthogonal to Fourier/spectral neural operators (FNO, geo-FNO, LSM) and addresses their known failure mode on unstructured or complex geometries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent 22% relative improvement across six diverse benchmarks (point clouds, structured/unstructured meshes, regular grids) plus strong results on industrial car and airfoil design tasks positions Transolver as a practical, scalable PDE solver with linear complexity in mesh size. The demonstrated OOD generalization (nearly 99% Spearman correlation on unseen Reynolds numbers and angles of attack) and scalability to 25× resolution suggest a path toward large-scale pre-trained PDE foundation models, which the authors explicitly identify as future work. Open questions include how to automatically select the optimal number of slices M (which depends on the physical problem), whether the slice-based decomposition generalizes to higher-dimensional or time-dependent PDEs beyond the tested settings, and whether the approach can be combined with physics-informed losses for partially observed scenarios.
