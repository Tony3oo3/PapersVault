## Problem

Grid-based NeRF models (e.g., TensoRF-VM) replace expensive MLP queries with explicit feature planes, dramatically accelerating training and rendering, but at the cost of large storage overhead (e.g., ~68 MB for feature planes alone). Existing compression approaches rely on voxel pruning and vector quantization without accounting for the spatially varying importance of different grid locations, leaving room for more efficient, quality-aware compression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241648/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained TensoRF-VM model's three 2D feature planes (each of size C_i × W_i × H_i, with C_i up to 192 channels) along with the scene's training view images, and produces a compressed NeRF model comprising an entropy-coded bitstring, a lightweight 2-layer transposed CNN decoder, and a masked entropy model. At rendering time, the bitstring is decompressed to latent codes, decoded back into feature planes, and used for trilinear-interpolation-based novel view synthesis with only ~2 seconds of decompression overhead. Compressed file sizes range from ~1.6 MB (high-compression config) to ~3.4 MB (low-compression config) versus ~68 MB uncompressed.

## Method

The core contribution is an encoder-free, per-scene neural compression pipeline that directly optimizes three latent codes (initialized to zero) jointly with a lightweight 2-layer transposed convolutional decoder (SELU activation) and a masked entropy model, avoiding the amortization gap inherent to encoder-based approaches. An importance-weighted rate-distortion loss re-weights the L2 feature-plane reconstruction term using rendering importance scores (T_k·α_k aggregated via bilinear interpolation), guiding the model to faithfully reconstruct only high-density grid regions while tolerating degradation in sparse background areas. The entropy model employs a spike-and-slab prior with learnable binary masks (sampled via straight-through Gumbel-Softmax) that designate uninformative grid locations as zero, enabling selective compression and exploiting the spatial sparsity of the latent codes. The hyperparameter λ controls the rate-distortion trade-off, allowing the user to select from multiple compression levels.

## Results

On Synthetic-NeRF, TC-TensoRF-L achieves 32.93 dB PSNR and 0.961 SSIM at 3.4 MB, compared to the uncompressed TensoRF-VM at 33.09 dB / 0.963 SSIM / 67.6 MB — a ~20× size reduction with <0.2 dB PSNR loss. The high-compression variant TC-TensoRF-H reaches 32.31 dB at just 1.6 MB. Across all four datasets, TC-TensoRF-L outperforms VQ-TensoRF in BD-PSNR by 0.28–0.34 dB while reducing bitrate by 17–29% (Table 2). On Tanks and Temples, the method compresses a 72.6 MB model to 2.9 MB (TC-TensoRF-L) or 1.6 MB (TC-TensoRF-H) with PSNR of 28.42 and 28.08 dB respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VQ-TensoRF (Li et al., 2023a), TC-TensoRF-L achieves higher PSNR on Synthetic-NeRF (32.93 vs 32.86 dB), Synthetic-NSVF (36.34 vs 36.16 dB), and Tanks and Temples (28.42 vs 28.20 dB) while also being smaller (3.4 vs 3.6 MB, 4.0 vs 4.1 MB, 2.9 vs 3.3 MB). On LLFF, TC-TensoRF-L is marginally behind VQ-TensoRF in PSNR (26.44 vs 26.46 dB) but surpasses it in SSIM (0.826 vs 0.824) and is nearly half the size (4.9 vs 8.8 MB). Against Re:TensoRF (Deng & Tartaglione, 2023), the proposed method wins on all three metrics across every dataset — e.g., on Synthetic-NeRF it achieves 32.93 dB / 0.961 SSIM at 3.4 MB versus 32.81 dB / 0.956 SSIM at 7.9 MB. The Bjontegaard Delta analysis (Table 2) confirms a consistent advantage over VQ-TensoRF: +0.28–0.34 dB BD-PSNR and 17–29% BD-rate savings across Synthetic-NeRF, Synthetic-NSVF, and Tanks and Temples. Compared to the uncompressed TensoRF-VM, the method achieves a 20–100× size reduction (e.g., 67.6→3.4 MB on Synthetic-NeRF, 179.8→4.9 MB on LLFF) with less than 0.3 dB PSNR degradation in the high-quality regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the nonlinear transform coding paradigm from neural image compression (Ballé et al., 2020) to the specific problem of compressing TensoRF-VM feature planes, departing from prior NeRF compression methods (VQ-TensoRF, Re:TensoRF) that rely on voxel pruning and vector quantization. Two key architectural departures distinguish it: (1) it removes the encoder entirely, directly optimizing latent codes per scene to avoid the amortization gap that degrades compression performance, and (2) it introduces a spike-and-slab masked entropy model that exploits the spatial sparsity of feature grids, building on the importance scores from Li et al. (2023a,b) for a weighted distortion loss. The lightweight two-layer transposed convolutional decoder follows Yang & Mandt (2023b), and the factorized prior structure follows Ballé et al. (2016, 2018).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Grid-based NeRFs have become the dominant paradigm for real-time 3D scene rendering, but their large feature grids (67–180 MB) are a practical bottleneck for VR/AR deployment and transmission. This work demonstrates that entropy-coded neural compression can reduce these to under 4 MB with minimal quality loss, offering a fundamentally different compression mechanism (learned probability model + arithmetic coding) compared to the pruning/quantization approaches that dominate the space. Key limitations include the 40-minute per-scene compression time (vs. 7 minutes for VQ-TensoRF) and the need to transmit the decoder alongside the bitstream. Open questions include whether the per-scene optimization strategy generalizes to multi-scene or streaming settings, and whether the decoder itself can be compressed to eliminate that overhead.
