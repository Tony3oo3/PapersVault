## Problem

Standard variational sequential Monte Carlo (VSMC) is an offline method that requires reprocessing the entire data batch between every parameter update, making it inapplicable to streaming data where observations arrive sequentially. Existing online alternatives for state-space models—particle-based recursive maximum likelihood, online EM, and online variational filtering—either rely on computationally expensive backward sampling to approximate smoothing distributions or are algorithmically complex with many hyperparameters. This paper asks whether one can perform simultaneous online parameter estimation and particle proposal adaptation in a single, memory-efficient pass over a data stream.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a sequential observation stream y_0, y_1, ... from a state-space model whose transition density m_θ and emission density g_θ are specified (and parameterized by θ). At each time step it outputs updated model parameters θ_{t+1}, an updated amortized proposal kernel r_λ (typically a neural network conditioned on the current state and next observation), and a cloud of N weighted particles approximating the filter distribution. A key practical detail is that only the current particle cloud is stored—no particle trajectories are retained—so memory and per-step compute remain constant regardless of stream length. Two distinct particle counts are used: a small L (typically 5) for the proposal gradient step and a larger N (e.g., 1000–10000) for the model-parameter gradient step.

## Method

The core insight is that the VSMC surrogate gradient G_T(λ,θ) = E[∇ log ∏_t Ω_t/N] decomposes as a sum of per-time-step terms ∇_θ log Ω_t, so each new observation y_{t+1} yields an immediate stochastic gradient signal without needing to revisit past data. OVSMC therefore runs a reparameterized particle filter (using the reparameterization trick on the proposal r_λ) and, at each step, performs two sequential stochastic-approximation updates: first a small-L-particle gradient ascent on the proposal parameters λ, then a large-N-particle gradient ascent on the model parameters θ. The proposal is an amortized neural network (e.g., Gaussian mean and variance as functions of x_t and y_{t+1}), learned globally rather than time-step-by-time-step, which avoids local over-adaptation. Theoretically, under strong mixing and boundedness assumptions, the authors show the scheme is a Robbins-Monro procedure targeting the stationary mean field of an extended state-dependent Markov chain, and prove a non-asymptotic convergence bound E‖h(θ_τ)‖² = O(log t / √t) for the step-size γ_t = c/√t.

## Results

On the 1D linear Gaussian SSM, OVSMC (L=5, N=10000) recovers parameters close to their true values (A=0.8, S_u=0.5) after approximately 10,000–20,000 iterations, with faster convergence for more informative observations (S_v=0.2 vs. S_v=1.2). In the 10D batch setting, OVSMC achieves ELBO values comparable to batch VSMC while exhibiting reduced gradient noise. Against the OVF baseline (Campbell et al., 2021), OVSMC matches parameter estimation accuracy (mean absolute error on A and B) while running in under 1 hour versus ~17 hours for OVF. On the stochastic volatility model, OVSMC converges faster than PaRIS-based RML for the β parameter and is three times faster computationally.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against batch VSMC (Naesseth et al., 2018), OVSMC is competitive in the 10D linear Gaussian SSM batch setting: Figure 3 shows that OVSMC's ELBO evolution tracks VSMC closely with visibly reduced stochastic noise, making it a viable alternative even when data is available in full. Against OVF (Campbell et al., 2021), OVSMC achieves comparable mean absolute errors on the A and B matrices (Figure 4) but with a dramatic computational advantage—under 1 hour per run versus approximately 17 hours for OVF, a gap the authors attribute to OVF's non-particle variational family and large number of hyperparameters. Against PaRIS-based RML (Olsson & Westerborn Alenlöv, 2020) on the stochastic volatility model, OVSMC converges faster for the β parameter but shows somewhat more fluctuation when learning α and σ (Figure 5), which the authors attribute to RML's reliance on particle smoothing (backward sampling) that OVSMC avoids entirely; computationally, OVSMC is three times faster, and this ratio grows with the number of particles N.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the variational sequential Monte Carlo (VSMC) framework of Le et al. (2018), Maddison et al. (2017), and Naesseth et al. (2018)—itself a particle-based generalization of the importance-weighted autoencoder—by converting the offline, batch-reprocessing procedure into an online stochastic approximation scheme. It departs from traditional online SSM parameter learning (particle-based RML, online EM) by avoiding costly backward sampling and particle smoothing, and from Campbell et al. (2021) by remaining particle-based and computationally lighter. Unlike Zhao et al. (2022)'s streaming VMC, which optimizes local time-varying parameters step-by-step, OVSMC learns global amortized parameters via a Robbins-Monro scheme, and the theoretical analysis explicitly connects the online procedure to the asymptotic (infinite-batch) VSMC objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OVSMC provides a principled, memory-bounded online algorithm for simultaneous parameter estimation and proposal adaptation in SSMs without the backward-sampling bottleneck that plagues RML and online EM, with rigorous convergence guarantees under standard strong-mixing assumptions. The practical speedups (3× over PaRIS-based RML, ~17× over OVF) make it attractive for real-time applications. Open questions include whether the biased surrogate gradient still yields consistent parameter estimates under correctly specified models, and how the method behaves under non-stationary data where parameters drift over time—both flagged by the authors as future work.
