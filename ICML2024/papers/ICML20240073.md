## Problem

Existing high-dimensional change point regression methods rely on sparsity-based constraints (e.g., LASSO combined with dynamic programming) and cannot be easily adapted to exploit arbitrary signal priors or prior information on change point locations. Moreover, they lack principled uncertainty quantification for the estimated change points. This paper addresses the gap by providing a flexible AMP framework that accommodates general priors and yields exact asymptotic characterizations of both estimation error and posterior distributions.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes an n×p covariate matrix X with i.i.d. Gaussian entries, an n-dimensional observation vector y, a signal prior P_B over R^p, a change point prior π_Ψ over piecewise-constant configuration vectors in [L]^n, and an upper bound L on the number of distinct signals. It outputs the estimated signal matrix B̂ (p×L), estimated change point locations η̂, and an approximate posterior distribution over all valid signal configurations Ψ (and hence over the number of change points). The covariates must be i.i.d. Gaussian, the number of change points is fixed (not scaling with n or p), and the high-dimensional regime requires n/p → δ > 0.

## Method

The paper proposes an AMP iteration with non-separable denoising functions g^t and f^t that handle the temporal dependence induced by change points—unlike mixed regression where separable denoisers suffice. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) provides an exact state evolution characterization of the AMP iterates in the n,p→∞ limit, leveraging a generalized graph-based AMP framework from Gerbelot & Berthier (2023). To make the denoisers computable without knowledge of the true configuration Ψ, the authors introduce an ensemble state evolution that averages over a prior π_Ψ on change point locations, yielding closed-form optimal denoisers (Proposition 3.2) via conditional expectations. Change points are then estimated by MAP over the approximate posterior p(ψ|Θ^t, y), and the Hausdorff distance and posterior calibration are characterized exactly (Propositions 3.3–3.4). The per-iteration complexity is O(npL³), and the framework flexibly incorporates any signal prior (Gaussian, Bernoulli-Gaussian, sparse-difference) and any change point prior.

## Results

The paper evaluates AMP using the normalized Hausdorff distance between true and estimated change point locations as the primary metric, across varying sample-to-dimension ratios δ = n/p. State evolution predictions closely match empirical AMP performance (Figures 1–3), validating the asymptotic theory in Propositions 3.3 and 3.4. In head-to-head comparisons (Figures 4–5), AMP consistently achieves the lowest Hausdorff distance among all tested algorithms while also outperforming most in runtime. In the high-dimensional image experiment (p = 7225), AMP completes in approximately 1 hour on an Apple M1 Max, whereas competing algorithms (DP, DPDU, DCDP) fail to return output within 2.5 hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares AMP against four state-of-the-art change point regression algorithms: DP (Rinaldo et al., 2021), DPDU (Xu et al., 2022), DCDP (Li et al., 2023b), and charcoal (Gao & Wang, 2022). AMP consistently achieves the lowest Hausdorff distance across all settings tested (sparse Bernoulli-Gaussian signal prior in Figure 4; sparse difference prior in Figure 5), outperforming all four baselines. In runtime, AMP outperforms DP and DPDU, is competitive with DCDP (the fastest baseline), and significantly outperforms charcoal. The first three baselines (DP, DPDU, DCDP) are designed for sparse signals and combine LASSO-type estimators with dynamic programming partitioning, while charcoal targets sparse signal differences; none incorporates a prior on change point locations, a flexibility AMP provides through its configurable priors P_B and π_Ψ. In the extreme high-dimensional image setting (p = 7225 vs. p = 200 in Figure 4), the LASSO-based baselines become computationally infeasible, whereas AMP scales favorably due to its O(npL³) per-iteration complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AMP framework (originally for linear regression and generalized linear models) to the change point regression setting by introducing non-separable denoising functions, building directly on the state evolution theory for non-separable AMP from Berthier et al. (2019) and Gerbelot & Berthier (2023). It departs from the dominant high-dimensional change point literature (Rinaldo et al., 2021; Xu et al., 2022; Li et al., 2023b; Gao & Wang, 2022), which combines LASSO-type estimators with dynamic programming partitioning and is inherently tied to sparsity assumptions. The closest prior work is mixed linear regression via AMP (Tan & Venkataramanan, 2023), but that setting assumes independent signal assignments per observation, whereas change points create temporal dependence that necessitates the non-separable denoisers used here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first exact asymptotic characterization (via state evolution) of change point estimation performance in the high-dimensional regime, and uniquely enables arbitrary signal priors and change point priors beyond sparsity, plus uncertainty quantification through an approximate posterior distribution—capabilities absent from LASSO+DP competitors. Key limitations include the Gaussian covariate assumption (though universality is expected) and the natural but restrictive requirement that change point separation be O(n). An important open direction is extending the theory to rotationally invariant designs, which would substantially broaden applicability beyond i.i.d. Gaussian covariates.
