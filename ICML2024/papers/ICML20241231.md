## Problem

Despite over a decade of AutoML research achieving substantial gains in predictive performance optimization, the field has largely neglected the interaction between diverse user groups (domain experts, ML practitioners, ML researchers) and AutoML systems. Current AutoML tools are designed as rigid, press-the-button-once systems that optimize a single objective without supporting iterative human-in-the-loop workflows, multi-objective tradeoffs, or the expression of domain knowledge and user preferences. This position paper argues that a human-centered paradigm is essential for AutoML to fulfill its original democratization goals and reach its full potential.

## Input / Output

The envisioned human-centered AutoML system accepts not just a data set and pipeline specification but also iterative user feedback, domain knowledge (custom kernels, constraints, prior beliefs on promising configurations), and relative preferences between candidate solutions via preferential optimization. Outputs extend beyond a single configured pipeline to include multi-level interpretability explanations spanning four levels (model, algorithm, AutoML system, and AutoML comparative performance), hyperparameter importance analyses, Pareto fronts for multi-objective tradeoffs, and interactive feedback supporting the iterative CRISP-ML(Q) workflow. The paper emphasizes that the interface language should move beyond mathematical/statistical specification toward natural language, potentially via LLMs, to accommodate non-technical domain experts.

## Method

As a position paper, the core contribution is an analytical framework that identifies five goals for AutoML tools (predictive performance, optimization speed, transparency/interpretability, customizability/flexibility, usability/interaction) and formulates five hypotheses about currently underexplored research directions. The authors introduce a four-level interpretability taxonomy extending Moosbauer's three levels with AutoML comparative performance interpretability, map distinct user groups to their specific interaction needs across the CRISP-ML(Q) workflow, and argue that human expertise and AutoML optimization are complementary rather than competing. They survey existing work on interpretable and interactive AutoML, identify the gap between algorithmic AutoML research and HCI-driven user studies, and propose concrete future directions including LLM-based interfaces, preferential Bayesian optimization, modular AutoML frameworks, and extending human-centered automation beyond modeling to the full data science lifecycle.

## Results

This is a position paper without quantitative experimental results. The authors propose five hypotheses for human-centered AutoML: (1) transparency and interpretability are key at multiple levels (model, algorithm, AutoML system, comparative performance); (2) customizability and flexibility are essential for different user groups; (3) AutoML tools must integrate iteratively with the data science workflow; (4) human experts are essential collaborators, not just operators; and (5) human-centered AutoML empowers users rather than creating dependency. The paper identifies four open challenges (rigid system design, narrow optimization objectives, lack of interactivity, and efficiency constraints) and maps them to the CRISP-ML(Q) workflow.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a position paper, this work does not experimentally compare methods or report benchmark results. Instead, it surveys and synthesizes existing work across AutoML, interpretable ML, and HCI to argue that the field has underexplored user interaction. The authors reference user studies (Xin et al., 2021; Hasebrook et al., 2023; Sun et al., 2023; Crisan & Fiore-Gartland, 2021) showing that current AutoML tools score lowest on customizability and that practitioners prefer grid/random search over Bayesian optimization due to workflow integration difficulties. They also cite evidence that domain experts choose manual development over AutoML for high-stakes projects due to interpretability gaps (Xin et al., 2021), and that users with little ML knowledge overestimate their understanding of AutoML outputs (Zöller et al., 2023). The paper positions itself against the dominant AutoML research paradigm focused on predictive performance optimization, arguing that human-centered design is the missing piece for broader adoption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper synthesizes and repositions existing work across three largely separate threads: (1) the human-centered ML movement (interpretable ML, fair ML, interactive human-in-the-loop approaches), (2) the dominant AutoML research paradigm focused on predictive performance optimization (SMAC, Auto-Sklearn, AutoGluon, NAS, multi-fidelity HPO), and (3) HCI user studies on AutoML usability and trust. Rather than proposing a new algorithm, it unifies these threads under five explicit hypotheses and argues that the AutoML community has underexplored user interaction as a first-class design concern. It builds conceptually on early calls for human-centered AutoML (Pfisterer et al. 2019, De Bie et al. 2022) and the CRISP-ML(Q) workflow model, while extending the interpretability taxonomy of Moosbauer (2023) from three to four levels to cover AutoML-system-level and comparative-performance-level explanations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that AutoML's limited adoption across user groups stems from ignoring human interaction rather than from insufficient algorithmic performance—reframes the field's open problems and could redirect research effort toward interfaces, iterative workflows, and multi-level explainability. Key open questions it raises include: how to design AutoML that supports genuinely iterative, non-linear data science workflows; how LLMs can serve as practical interfaces for domain experts who cannot formalize objectives; and how to mitigate automation bias without sacrificing the efficiency gains that motivate AutoML. As a position paper it offers no new methods or benchmarks, so its impact depends on whether the community adopts its framing; the modular AutoML toolkit vision and the LLM-as-interface direction are the most concrete actionable threads for future work.
