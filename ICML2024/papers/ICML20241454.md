## Problem

Generating stable crystal structures requires jointly modeling discrete atom types, periodic fractional coordinates, and continuous lattice parameters, each with different geometric constraints. Existing diffusion-based approaches must impose an unnatural Gaussian base distribution on all variables and use separate diffusion mechanisms per variable, while also requiring thousands of integration steps at inference. This paper asks whether a flow-matching framework can natively accommodate crystal symmetries (permutation, translation, rotation, periodicity) and produce stable materials with far fewer integration steps.

## Input / Output

For Crystal Structure Prediction, the model conditions on a fixed composition (atom types and counts) and generates fractional coordinates on the [0,1)³ torus plus a 6-tuple of lattice parameters (three side lengths in Å and three angles in [60°,120°] via Niggli reduction). For De Novo Generation, the model additionally outputs atomic types using a 7-bit binary representation (sign-discretized from a continuous flow) rather than a 100-dimensional one-hot vector, reducing per-atom dimensionality by ~14×. The number of atoms n is sampled from the empirical training distribution before generation, and lattice angles are mapped to unconstrained space via a logit transform to avoid boundary issues during flow integration.

## Method

FlowMM generalizes Riemannian Flow Matching to a product manifold C = A × F × L that encodes crystal geometry: F is a flat torus with periodic wrapping, L is Euclidean with a logit-transformed angle component, and A is Euclidean (binary for DNG). The key geometric contribution is a translation-invariant conditional vector field on the torus, constructed by subtracting the mean tangent-space displacement from the per-atom log-map, which relaxes the standard flow-matching requirement that the conditional path concentrate on a single point and instead allows it to concentrate on a translation equivalence class. The network is an EGNN-style graph neural network that enforces permutation equivariance via message passing and translation invariance by featurizing edges as inter-atom displacements, while rotation invariance is inherited from the lattice-parameter representation. A carefully chosen informative base distribution (LogNormal for lengths, Uniform for angles) drastically simplifies lattice learning compared to diffusion's forced Gaussian base, and an inference-time anti-annealing velocity scaling further reduces the number of ODE integration steps needed, achieving ~3× efficiency over diffusion baselines.

## Results

On Crystal Structure Prediction, FlowMM achieves 61.39% Match Rate and 0.0566 RMSE on MP-20, and 17.54% Match Rate and 0.1726 RMSE on MPTS-52, outperforming DiffCSP (51.49% / 0.0631 and 12.19% / 0.1786) and CDVAE (33.90% / 0.1045 and 5.34% / 0.2106). FlowMM reaches its maximum match rate in ~50 integration steps, an order of magnitude fewer than DiffCSP's 1000 steps. On De Novo Generation (MP-20), FlowMM at 250 steps achieves a 4.32% Stability Rate with a Cost of 0.58 steps/stable material, compared to DiffCSP's 5.06% Stability Rate at 1.98 steps/stable, making FlowMM ~3× more efficient at finding stable materials.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowMM is compared against two baselines: CDVAE (Xie et al., 2021), a diffusion-based variational autoencoder, and DiffCSP (Jiao et al., 2023), a joint equivariant diffusion model. On CSP, FlowMM decisively outperforms both on the realistic datasets: it beats DiffCSP by ~10 points in Match Rate on MP-20 (61.39% vs 51.49%) and by ~5 points on MPTS-52 (17.54% vs 12.19%), while CDVAE trails by 20+ points. On the simpler Perov-5 unit test, FlowMM edges out DiffCSP (53.15% vs 52.02%), but on Carbon-24, DiffCSP achieves a lower RMSE (0.2759 vs 0.4122) despite FlowMM's higher Match Rate (23.47% vs 17.54%). On DNG, FlowMM is competitive with DiffCSP on Stability Rate (4.32% vs 5.06% at 250 steps) and S.U.N. Rate (2.38% vs 3.34%), but dramatically outperforms it on Cost (0.58 vs 1.98 steps/stable) and S.U.N. Cost (1.05 vs 2.99 steps/S.U.N.), reflecting its ~3× fewer integration steps. Both baselines are substantially outperformed on the Wasserstein distance for N-ary distribution (FlowMM: 0.107 vs DiffCSP: 0.125 vs CDVAE: 0.278), indicating FlowMM better captures the distribution of unique elements per unit cell.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes Riemannian Flow Matching (Chen & Lipman, 2024) to the specific symmetries of crystals—permutation, translation on a torus, and rotation—enabling a single unified generative framework for fractional coordinates, lattice parameters, and atom types. It departs from the dominant diffusion-model approach (CDVAE, DiffCSP, MatterGen) that required separate diffusion mechanisms for each variable type (score matching, discrete diffusion, denoising diffusion) and was forced to use a normal base distribution. The key conceptual departure is that Flow Matching's flexibility in choosing base distributions allows an informative LogNormal/Uniform prior over lattice parameters, which the authors show drastically simplifies learning compared to diffusion's constrained normal prior. The GNN backbone is borrowed directly from DiffCSP (Jiao et al., 2023), and the binary atom-type representation follows Analog Bits (Chen et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3× reduction in integration steps to find stable materials is a practically significant efficiency gain for materials discovery pipelines where DFT relaxation is the dominant cost. The base-distribution flexibility is a structural advantage over diffusion that may generalize to other structured generative tasks with constrained geometry. Open questions include whether even fewer integration steps (e.g., 10–20) could maintain stability rates, whether the model can propose truly novel compositions outside the training manifold, and how the approach scales to larger unit cells beyond 52 atoms. Limitations include the reliance on StructureMatcher (which is not a true equivalence relation), the 0 K homogeneity assumption, and the fact that S.U.N. identification may overcount novelty.
