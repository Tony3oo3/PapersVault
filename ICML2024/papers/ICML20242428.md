## Problem

Existing KV cache compression methods for LLM inference reduce memory by evicting token caches, but this eviction inevitably introduces output perturbation that worsens with higher compression ratios, degrading generation quality. No prior work recovers information from evicted caches, leaving a fundamental gap between memory savings and inference fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242428/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

CaM is a plug-and-play module that consumes the cumulative attention scores across generation steps, the current V-cache, and eviction indices produced by an upstream compression strategy (e.g., StreamingLLM, H2O, Scissorhands). It outputs an updated V-cache in which the value vectors of evicted tokens have been distributed into a window of m local tokens near the current generation position. The method introduces no additional matrix multiplications or iterative loops, adding negligible overhead (~1.2 ms/token and 0.2 GB in their setup).

## Method

CaM merges the value cache of each evicted token evenly into m consecutive local tokens (V_k ← V_k + V_i/m), grounded in the observation that mean attention over short token spans remains stable across future generation steps. A Bernoulli sampling mask M = Bernoulli(clamp(Ā_i / avg(Ā_{j:j+m}), 0, 1)), computed from cumulative attention scores, stochastically decides whether to merge, ensuring that merging is only applied when it provably reduces perturbation relative to direct eviction (Theorem 3.3: |Θ| < |Δ| when avg(A_{j:j+m}) < 2A_i). Theoretically, when the merge ratio exactly matches the attention proportion, the output is lossless (Theorem 3.2); in practice, the even-distribution strategy combined with the adaptive mask keeps perturbations strictly below those of eviction. CaM is model-agnostic and requires no retraining, operating solely on the V-cache at each generation step.

## Results

Under a 20% KV cache budget, CaM consistently improves all three baseline methods across six QA tasks on LLaMA-7B and OPT-13B. The most notable gains are on COPA (e.g., H2O on LLaMA-7B improves from 47.0 to 56.0, a +9.0% absolute gain) and RTE (StreamingLLM on LLaMA-7B improves from 49.5 to 54.6, +5.1%). Figure 3 further shows that CaM sustains improvements across cache budgets from 10% to 90% and model sizes from 6.7B to 65B, with particularly large gains on text summarization (XSUM, CNN/DailyMail) and long-sequence language modeling (PG-19). Efficiency overhead is minimal: CaM adds only 1.2 ms/token and 0.2 GB GPU memory over StreamingLLM on an NVIDIA 3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CaM is a plug-and-play module that improves every baseline it is applied to, never degrading performance. Against StreamingLLM, CaM yields the largest absolute gains on RTE (+5.1% on LLaMA-7B, +5.0% on OPT-13B) and COPA (+4.0% on both models), while also improving OpenBookQA, PiQA, and Winogrande by 1–2 points. Against H2O, the improvements are even more dramatic on weaker baselines: COPA jumps from 47.0 to 56.0 (+9.0%) and OpenBookQA from 22.8 to 27.6 (+4.8%) on LLaMA-7B, suggesting CaM is most beneficial when the underlying eviction strategy is more aggressive. Against Scissorhands, gains are more modest (e.g., PiQA 50.3→51.0, COPA 58.0→60.0 on LLaMA-7B) but still consistently positive across all six tasks. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that removing the Bernoulli merge mask actually degrades performance below the no-CaM baseline, validating that selective merging is essential rather than merging all evicted caches indiscriminately.

## Relation to Prior Work

CaM builds directly on the KV cache eviction paradigm established by StreamingLLM (sink tokens + sliding window), H2O (accumulated attention-based heavy-hitter selection), and Scissorhands (persistence of importance), but departs from their shared assumption that evicted caches should be permanently discarded. Conceptually, it repurposes the token-merging idea from Vision Transformers (ToMe) to the LLM inference setting, where the value cache of a to-be-evicted token is evenly distributed into a local window of retained tokens rather than simply deleted. The theoretical contribution (Theorems 3.2–3.3) provides a principled condition under which merging provably reduces output perturbation relative to eviction, and the Bernoulli sampling mask based on cumulative attention scores operationalizes this condition adaptively at each generation step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KV cache memory is a first-order bottleneck for deploying large LLMs (e.g., ~365 GB for LLaMA-65B at batch 128, sequence 2048), and CaM offers a plug-and-play, training-free remedy that adds negligible overhead (~1.2 ms/token, 0.2 GB) while consistently improving accuracy across LLaMA, OPT, and GPT-NeoX on QA, summarization, and language-modeling tasks. The ablation confirming that indiscriminate merging can degrade performance below the eviction baseline validates the theoretical necessity of the adaptive mask, but the paper leaves open important questions: whether layer-wise or head-wise merge adaptability could yield further gains, whether the even-merge assumption generalizes to architectures with heterogeneous attention patterns, and how the method interacts with quantized or compressed KV caches. As a composable module that slots into any existing eviction policy, CaM is likely to become a standard component in memory-efficient inference pipelines.
