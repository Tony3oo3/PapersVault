## Problem

Existing world models require action-labeled data to achieve frame-level controllability, while video generation models are only controllable at the video level (e.g., via text prompts). No prior approach could generate an interactive, action-controllable environment from video-only data without ground-truth action annotations, leaving a gap between passive video generation and truly playable virtual worlds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240185/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the user provides a single RGB image (160×90) as the initial frame—this can be a text-to-image generation, hand-drawn sketch, real photograph, or dataset frame—and then selects a discrete latent action (an integer in [0, 8)) at each timestep. The model autoregressively produces the next frame at 10 FPS with a 16-frame context window, operating at roughly 1 FPS inference speed. The latent action vocabulary is learned entirely unsupervised, so the user must discover what each of the 8 actions does by trial, though the mapping remains consistent across different prompt images.

## Method

Genie comprises three ST-transformer-based components: a VQ-VAE video tokenizer (200M params, 1024 codes, patch size 4) that compresses 16-frame video clips into discrete tokens; a latent action model (300M params, 8 codes, patch size 16) that infers discrete actions between consecutive frames from raw pixels using a VQ-VAE objective with a lightweight decoder discarded at inference; and a MaskGIT-based dynamics model (up to 10.1B params) that autoregressively predicts next-frame tokens conditioned on past tokens and stop-gradient latent action embeddings. The key architectural choice is the ST-transformer, which interleaves spatial attention (over H×W tokens within a frame) and causal temporal attention (over T frames at the same spatial position), making the dominant compute cost scale linearly rather than quadratically with sequence length. Training proceeds in two phases: the tokenizer is trained first, then the LAM (from pixels) and dynamics model (from tokens) are co-trained, with the dynamics model using random masking at rates between 0.5 and 1.0 and treating latent actions as additive embeddings rather than concatenated tokens to improve controllability.

## Results

The 11B-parameter Genie model achieves an FVD of 40.1 and Δt PSNR of 1.91 on the Platformers dataset (2.5B dynamics model variant), demonstrating both high video fidelity and strong controllability from learned latent actions. On the Robotics dataset, a 1B model reaches FVD 136.4 and Δt PSNR 2.07, confirming generality across domains. For agent training, the LAM-based behavioral cloning policy on CoinRun matches the oracle BC agent (which has access to ground-truth actions) using as few as 200 expert samples, despite never having seen the environment during pre-training. Scaling experiments show graceful improvement from 40M to 2.7B parameters, with the final 10.1B dynamics model trained on 942B tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external comparison is against C-ViViT, the spatiotemporal tokenizer from Phenaki (Villegas et al., 2023). ST-ViViT substantially outperforms C-ViViT on both video fidelity (FVD 81.4 vs. 272.7) and controllability (Δt PSNR 1.66 vs. 1.37), while also using less memory (0.9 GB vs. 1.6 GB) at a similar parameter count (~205M vs. 225M). The paper attributes C-ViViT's poor performance to its full space-time attention mechanism, which causes quadratic scaling with frame count and a tendency toward overfitting that requires strong regularization. Against the spatial-only ViT baseline, ST-ViViT improves FVD by 29% (81.4 vs. 114.5) and Δt PSNR by 19% (1.66 vs. 1.39), confirming that incorporating temporal dynamics in the tokenizer is critical for both generation quality and action controllability.

## Relation to Prior Work

Genie sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: action-conditioned world models (GameGAN, DriveGAN, Recurrent Environment Simulators) and large-scale video generation models (Phenaki, MaskViT, TECO). It adopts the MaskGIT-based autoregressive generation and ST-transformer architecture from the video generation side, and the frame-level action controllability goal from the world model side, but departs critically by eliminating the need for ground-truth action labels through an unsupervised latent action model. This positions Genie as a new class of model—distinct from both world models (which require video+actions) and video models (which are only video-level controllable)—that achieves frame-level controllability from video-only data at foundation-model scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Genie demonstrates that a foundation world model can be trained entirely from unlabelled internet video, opening a path to training generalist agents from the vast corpus of publicly available gameplay and robotics footage without expensive action annotation. The learned latent action space is shown to be semantically meaningful and consistent across diverse prompts, enabling behavioral cloning from unseen videos with as few as 200 expert samples. Key limitations remain: only 16 frames of memory constrain long-horizon consistency, inference runs at roughly 1 FPS, and the model can hallucinate unrealistic futures. The broader open question is whether this approach scales to 3D, photorealistic, and multi-modal environments, and whether latent actions can serve as a universal interface for agent learning across domains.
