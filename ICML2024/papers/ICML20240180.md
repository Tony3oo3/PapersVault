## Problem

Existing dynamical systems reconstruction (DSR) methods almost universally assume continuous, single-modality time series with Gaussian noise, yet real scientific data frequently consists of discrete random variables (class labels, spike counts) and simultaneous measurements from multiple data channels with different statistical properties. Multimodal data integration has been largely unexplored in the DSR context, and the key challenge is reconciling efficient training techniques like sparse or generalized teacher forcing—which rely on inverting a decoder to produce a forcing signal—with the impossibility of inverting decoders for discrete or multi-modal observations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes simultaneously observed time series of arbitrary statistical types (multivariate Gaussian, ordinal with ordered categories, Poisson/negative-binomial counts, and multi-categorical labels), each potentially with different dimensions and with missing data in any modality. It outputs a fully generative dendPLRNN that, after training, produces trajectories preserving the ground-truth system's attractor geometry, power spectra, and Lyapunov spectrum, and additionally enables cross-modal prediction (e.g., decoding ordinal observations from a continuous-only expert) and reconstruction from purely symbolic/categorical codings of chaotic attractors.

## Method

MTF uses a multimodal VAE (MVAE) with a temporal CNN encoder to map all data modalities into a shared K-dimensional latent code, which then serves as the sparse teacher-forcing signal for training a dendPLRNN reconstruction model. The MVAE and DSR model share all modality-specific decoder parameters (linear-Gaussian, ordered-logit, Poisson/negative-binomial), and the MVAE's prior is instantiated directly by the DSR model's latent dynamics, producing a consistency loss that enforces alignment between the two latent spaces. During training, at strategically chosen intervals (determined by the system's Lyapunov exponent or via grid search), the first K forward-iterated latent states are replaced by the MVAE's encoded states, while the remaining states evolve freely. The total loss combines the MVAE's negative ELBO, the consistency loss, and the DSR model's negative log-likelihood through the shared decoders, ensuring the reconstruction model maintains direct contact with the data rather than relying solely on the MVAE's posterior approximation.

## Results

On the Lorenz-63 system, MTF achieves a state-space disagreement of D_stsp = 3.4 ± 0.35 and Hellinger distance D_H = 0.30 ± 0.06, outperforming the multimodal SVAE (D_stsp = 11.1, D_H = 0.82) by roughly 3× on geometry and 2.7× on temporal structure. On the Rössler system, MTF reaches D_stsp = 1.45 ± 0.71 versus 10.7 ± 1.5 for SVAE, and on the 6-d Lewis-Glass network, D_stsp = 0.27 ± 0.07 versus 2.6 ± 0.5. On real fMRI + behavioral data, MTF achieves D_stsp = 0.55 ± 0.04 and D_H = 0.301 ± 0.007, again substantially outperforming SVAE (1.9 ± 0.22, 0.441 ± 0.019) and BPTT (3.31 ± 0.8, 0.52 ± 0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MTF decisively outperforms the multimodal SVAE (Kramer et al., 2022) across all three synthetic systems and the fMRI dataset: on Lorenz, D_stsp improves from 11.1 to 3.4 (3.3×) and D_H from 0.82 to 0.30; on Rössler, D_stsp drops from 10.7 to 1.45 (7.4×); on Lewis-Glass, from 2.6 to 0.27 (9.6×); and on fMRI, from 1.9 to 0.55 (3.5×). Against multiple shooting (MS; Voss et al., 2004), MTF wins on D_stsp for Lorenz (3.4 vs 4.5) and Rössler (1.45 vs 3.99), while MS is competitive on Lewis-Glass (0.33 vs 0.27, within 1 SEM); however, MS cannot compute prediction errors (PE/OPE) since it lacks an encoder for state initialization. Compared to BPTT-TF (Brenner et al., 2022, applied to Gaussianized data), MTF achieves substantially lower D_stsp on all systems (e.g., 3.4 vs 8.8 on Lorenz, 1.45 vs 8.9 on Rössler, 0.27 vs 2.51 on Lewis-Glass) and lower D_H (0.30 vs 0.86 on Lorenz), confirming that the MVAE-generated multimodal TF signal is critical when data are not purely Gaussian. The GVAE-TF variant (VAE-TF without modality-specific decoders) is competitive on Lewis-Glass D_stsp (0.28 vs 0.27) but degrades sharply on Lorenz (4.3 vs 3.4) and Rössler (12.1 vs 1.45), highlighting the importance of proper distributional modeling for each modality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the teacher-forcing lineage for chaotic dynamical systems reconstruction (STF from Mikhaeil et al. 2022, GTF from Hess et al. 2023) to the multimodal and discrete-data setting, where the standard decoder-inversion mechanism for generating forcing signals breaks down. The key conceptual departure is repurposing a multimodal VAE not as a sequential generative model of the dynamics (as in SVAE approaches like Kramer et al. 2022) but as a signal generator that produces a sparse control input guiding a separate DSR model (the dendPLRNN from Brenner et al. 2022) during training. This inverts the conventional VAE role: the MVAE's latent code serves as a training-time scaffold rather than the model's own state representation, with shared decoder models and a consistency loss coupling the two latent spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practically critical gap: real scientific data (neuroscience, medicine, climate) is routinely multimodal and includes discrete variables, yet prior DSR methods assumed single-source continuous Gaussian observations. The demonstration that chaotic attractors can be reconstructed from purely symbolic/categorical codings—with matching Lyapunov exponents—is, to the authors' knowledge, the first such result and opens DSR to domains like behavioral sequences or consumer data. Open questions remain about how much state-space topology is recoverable from non-continuous observations, under what conditions different modalities complement versus interfere with each other, and whether control signals can be incorporated directly at the encoder level.
