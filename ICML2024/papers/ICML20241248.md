## Problem

Preference-based RL requires extensive human preference labels for each new task, and these labels cannot be recycled across tasks. Existing meta-learning approaches reduce per-task labeling but still demand millions of pre-collected preference labels plus further online queries. PEARL addresses this by enabling zero-shot cross-task preference transfer that requires only a small number of source-task labels and no human annotations for the target task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241248/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes M source-task trajectory segments (state-action sequences of fixed length, e.g., 50 steps for Meta-World, 100 for Robomimic) with all pairwise preference labels, plus N unlabeled target-task trajectory segments, under the constraint that both tasks share the same action space. It produces transferred binary preference labels for all target-task trajectory pairs, a trained distributional reward model (RPT), and a target-task policy learned via offline RL (IQL). Target trajectories are first grouped into 2 clusters via K-means, and 2 trajectories are sampled from each cluster to form the N segments used in the transport computation.

## Method

PEARL comprises two components. Cross-task Preference Alignment (CPA) solves a Gromov-Wasserstein optimal transport problem between source and target trajectory distributions (using the Sinkhorn algorithm), yielding a transport matrix T where each element T_ij encodes the correspondence probability between trajectories. Target-task preference labels are then computed as weighted sums of source-task labels via trajectory pair matching matrices A^{jj'} = T_{.j} T_{.j'}^T, followed by min-max normalization and binarization. Robust Reward Learning (RRL) replaces the scalar reward head of Preference Transformer with a two-branch architecture that outputs both reward mean and variance (Gaussian distribution). The training loss combines a cross-entropy term on reward means, a stochastic cross-entropy term on K reparameterized samples from the reward distribution (weighted by λ), and an entropy-based regularization loss that prevents variance collapse by enforcing a minimum uncertainty level η. The full pipeline is: (1) compute CPA labels, (2) train RPT on those labels, (3) relabel offline transitions with the learned reward and train IQL for the final policy.

## Results

On Meta-World tasks, RPT+CPA achieves 84.0% average success rate using only zero-shot transferred preference labels, reaching 93.2% of the oracle PT performance (91.7% with scripted labels). Across all tasks including Robomimic, RPT+CPA attains 78.1% versus PT's 83.8% oracle, while the simpler PT+CPA variant reaches 71.2%. The CPA label transfer itself achieves 74.9% accuracy on average across all task pairs. In few-shot settings (0–20 oracle labels), RPT+CPA significantly outperforms PT and even exceeds oracle PT (100 labels) on Window Open and Lift-MH tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PT (Kim et al., ICLR 2023) with scripted labels, RPT+CPA achieves 84.0% vs. 91.7% on Meta-World and 78.1% vs. 83.8% overall — a gap of roughly 6–8 points that narrows to ~5.7 points on Meta-World, representing 93.2% of oracle performance with zero target-task labels. Against PT+Semi (Park et al., ICLR 2022), RPT+CPA is competitive on Meta-World (84.0% vs. 91.6%) but the comparison is less meaningful since PT+Semi still uses scripted labels. Against IPL (Hejna & Sadigh, 2023), RPT+CPA outperforms IPL on Meta-World (84.0% vs. 82.9%) while IPL+CPA (the no-reward-function variant) trails at 71.7%. The naive similarity-based transfer baseline PT+Sim is dramatically outperformed by both PT+CPA and RPT+CPA (38.1% vs. 76.5%/84.0% on Meta-World), confirming that Gromov-Wasserstein alignment is essential. In the few-shot regime (≤20 oracle labels), RPT+CPA surpasses PT by 10–20+ points and even exceeds oracle PT on Window Open and Lift-MH, demonstrating that CPA labels provide a strong complement to scarce human annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Preference Transformer (PT) architecture for non-Markovian reward modeling and repurposes Gromov-Wasserstein distance—previously applied to graph matching, domain adaptation, and cross-task imitation learning (GWIL)—as a relational alignment tool for trajectory distributions across tasks. It departs from prior preference-based RL methods (PEBBLE, SURF, RUNE, Meta-Reward-Net) that each require task-specific human labels, and from meta-learning approaches (Hejna III & Sadigh, 2023) that demand millions of pre-collected labels, by enabling zero-shot preference transfer with only a handful of source-task annotations. The distributional reward modeling (Gaussian mean + variance) extends the scalar reward paradigm used in all prior preference-based RL, drawing on distributional feature modeling from vision tasks but applying it to reward distributions specifically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PEARL addresses a practical bottleneck in preference-based RL: the inability to reuse human preference labels across tasks, which creates a costly labeling cycle for every new task. The zero-shot transfer achieving ~93% of oracle performance with no target-task labels is practically significant for robotic deployment where human annotators are scarce. However, the requirement of identical action spaces and the sensitivity to task dissimilarity (CPA accuracy drops to ~63% for less similar task pairs) limit immediate applicability. Open questions include extending to heterogeneous action spaces via learned representations, scaling to high-dimensional observations (e.g., images), and understanding the fundamental limits of relational alignment for preference transfer.
