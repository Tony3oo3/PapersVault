## Problem

Existing invertible residual networks (i-ResNet, i-DenseNet) rely on spectral normalization for Lipschitz certification, yielding very conservative bounds that limit expressivity for a given bi-Lipschitz constraint. Separately, surrogate loss learning typically requires input convexity (ICNN) to guarantee a unique global minimum, but convexity is often too restrictive to fit non-convex target functions. The paper addresses both gaps by providing certified bi-Lipschitz networks with much tighter bounds and a non-convex surrogate loss class that still admits a unique, efficiently-computable global minimum.

## Input / Output

The BiLipNet takes a vector x ∈ R^n (or a 32×32 RGB image for CIFAR-10/100) and produces a transformed vector y ∈ R^n with certified (μ,ν)-Lipschitz bounds, where μ and ν are smoothly parameterized hyperparameters. The PLNet composes a BiLipNet with a quadratic potential to output a scalar loss f(x) ∈ R satisfying the Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition. Computing the global minimum x* = G⁻¹(0) requires solving an implicit fixed-point equation per layer, handled by a three-operator splitting iteration rather than a closed-form inverse.

## Method

The core building block is a feed-through network (FTN): an MLP backbone where every hidden layer receives direct connections from both the input and the output, preventing vanishing gradients while preserving long-path expressivity. The residual layer F(x) = μx + H(x) is certified μ-strongly monotone and ν-Lipschitz via integral quadratic constraints (IQC), and the resulting SDP conditions are converted into an unconstrained direct parameterization using the Cayley transform, enabling standard first-order training without any projection or penalty. BiLipNets are formed by composing FTN layers with orthogonal affine layers (distortion 1), so the overall (μ,ν) bounds multiply across layers. The PLNet f(x) = ½‖G(x)‖² + c inherits the PL condition with constant m = μ², guaranteeing a unique global minimum. The inverse G⁻¹(y) is computed layer-by-layer via a Davis-Yin three-operator splitting algorithm, where the strictly lower-triangular structure of the FTN weights makes each resolvent step a cheap forward substitution.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, BiLipNet achieves 86.7% clean accuracy and 72.8% corrupted accuracy (c=0.65), outperforming SNGP's best of 76.7%/58.7% (c=0.95) by ~10 percentage points. On CIFAR-100, BiLipNet reaches 55.2% clean accuracy versus SNGP's 36.9% (c=0.95). For the 1D step-function fitting task, BiLipNet achieves a loss of 0.0685 with certified (0.11, 9.97)-Lipschitz bounds, compared to 0.2090 for i-ResNet and 0.2091 for i-DenseNet under the same (0.1, 10) constraint. On the 20-dimensional Rosenbrock surrogate, BiLipNet drives training error to ~10⁻⁸ while i-ResNet and i-DenseNet cannot achieve small training error at any distortion value.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiLipNet uniformly and significantly outperforms SNGP (spectral-normalized Neural Gaussian Process) on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 across all distortion levels: at c=0.65, BiLipNet gains ~10 pp clean accuracy on CIFAR-10 (86.7% vs 76.7%) and ~18 pp on CIFAR-100 (55.2% vs 36.9%), while also achieving lower ECE and NLL. Notably, BiLipNet at low distortion (c=0.35, τ=18.6) matches or exceeds SNGP at high distortion (c=0.95, τ=2.3×10⁶), demonstrating that the IQC-based parameterization is far more expressive per unit of certified distortion than spectral normalization. For surrogate loss learning, BiLipNet captures non-convex sub-level sets of the Rosenbrock function while maintaining a unique global minimum, whereas ICNN (convex constraint) has large fitting error and unconstrained MLP produces spurious local minima. On the 20D Rosenbrock task, i-ResNet and i-DenseNet fail to achieve small training error at any distortion value, while BiLipNet reaches ~10⁻⁸ training error. For computing the global minimum of a PLNet, the proposed DYS solver converges significantly faster than both the forward-step method (FSM) and ADAM applied directly to the surrogate loss, with lower distortion (τ=5) yielding even faster convergence than τ=50.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the IQC-based Lipschitz certification framework (Megretski & Rantzer, 1997; Fazlyab et al., 2019; Wang & Manchester, 2023) from one-sided Lipschitzness to the joint certification of strong monotonicity and Lipschitzness, enabling tight bi-Lipschitz bounds via a direct parameterization using the Cayley transform. It departs from prior invertible residual networks (i-ResNet, i-DenseNet) that rely on spectral normalization—shown here to be extremely conservative—by introducing the feed-through network (FTN) architecture with certified (μ,ν)-Lipschitz bounds. For surrogate loss learning, the PLNet relaxes the input-convexity requirement of ICNN (Amos et al., 2017) to the weaker Polyak-Lojasiewicz condition, while the model inverse is computed via a novel three-operator splitting formulation (Davis & Yin, 2017) that exploits the strictly lower-triangular structure of the FTN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for applications requiring certified input-output behavior: the 7× tighter inverse-Lipschitz bounds (0.11 vs 0.80) translate directly into better uncertainty quantification (10–20% accuracy gains on CIFAR-10/100) and more faithful surrogate loss fitting. The PLNet opens surrogate optimization to non-convex problems while retaining a unique, efficiently-computable global minimum—a property ICNN provides only for convex targets. Open questions include whether the distortion parameter can be adapted automatically during training, how the approach scales to very high-dimensional or structured (e.g., graph) inputs, and whether the PL condition is the right relaxation for all downstream optimization tasks or whether stronger conditions (e.g., KL-regularity) would be preferable in some settings.
