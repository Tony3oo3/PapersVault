## Problem

Existing learning-augmented online algorithms treat ML predictions as untrusted point estimates with no information about prediction quality, forcing a fixed consistency-robustness tradeoff chosen by the user. Meanwhile, uncertainty quantification (UQ) methods such as conformal prediction can certify that a prediction falls within an interval with a given probability, yet no framework existed for optimally exploiting this additional quality information in competitive online algorithm design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241915/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a probabilistic interval prediction (PIP) of the form (ℓ, u; δ), certifying that the critical value (skiing horizon N or maximum price V) lies in [ℓ, u] with probability at least 1−δ, together with problem-specific parameters (buying cost B, or price bounds [m, M]). It outputs optimized algorithm parameters — a randomized buying-day distribution y for ski rental or a protection function G for online search — along with the achieved distributionally-robust competitive ratio (DRCR), which equals (1−δ)·η + δ·γ, a convex combination of consistency and robustness. For ski rental, the infinite-dimensional LP reduces to O(B) variables and constraints; for online search, a geometric discretization yields O(ln(M/m)/ln(1+ε)) variables.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary paradigms. First, an optimization-based approach constructs an ancillary linear program over a family of hard instances, minimizing (1−δ)η + δγ subject to per-instance cost-ratio constraints, where the solution directly yields the optimal parameterized algorithm (RSR for ski rental, PFA for online search) and a matching lower bound via Proposition 1. Second, for multi-instance settings where the true distribution may not be worst-case or the PIP may be imprecise, an ε-net-based online learning algorithm (Algorithm 5) covers the UQ space with local expert instances (exponentiated gradients), each updated only on similar UQ vectors, achieving sublinear policy regret Õ(T^{1−1/(d+2)}) under L-Lipschitzness of the cost upper bound in θ. The key structural insight is that the worst-case distribution in the ambiguity set is a two-point distribution, reducing DRCR to a weighted sum of consistency and robustness, and that DRCR itself is Lipschitz in the UQ parameters, enabling the online learning guarantees.

## Results

The paper establishes optimal distributionally-robust competitive ratios (DRCR) for two classic online problems with probabilistic interval predictions (PIP). For ski rental, the optimal randomized algorithm RSR(y*) achieves DRCR = CR*_sr, and the underlying optimization reduces to a finite linear program with O(B) variables and constraints (Lemma 1). For online search, the protection-function-based algorithm PFA(G*) achieves the optimal DRCR = CR*_os, solvable via a discrete approximation with O(ln(M/m)/ln(1+ε)) variables. In the multi-instance online learning setting, the proposed ε-net algorithm achieves sublinear policy regret of Õ(T^{2/3}) for ski rental (exploiting discrete ℓ, u parameters) and Õ(T^{4/5}) for online search, competing against the optimal DRCR policy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The empirical evaluation (Figure 1) compares five algorithms on a multi-instance ski rental problem: WOA (worst-case optimal randomized algorithm, e/(e−1)-competitive), FTP (follow-the-prediction, fully trusting the prediction), RSR-PIP (the optimization-based algorithm achieving optimal DRCR), OL-Static (online learning with static regret, no UQ), and OL-Dynamic (the proposed online learning with policy regret leveraging UQ). RSR-PIP initially outperforms all baselines since it is designed for the worst-case distribution in the ambiguity set, but OL-Dynamic gradually catches up and eventually surpasses it as the number of instances grows, because the actual data-generating distribution is not adversarial. WOA and FTP consistently perform worst, confirming that neither pure worst-case optimization nor blind trust in predictions is sufficient. OL-Static approaches the RSR-PIP level but fails to improve beyond it, validating the importance of the policy regret formulation (which exploits UQ) over classic static regret (which does not).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learning-augmented online algorithms framework (Purohit et al., 2018; Wei & Zhang, 2020), which treats predictions as untrusted point estimates with no quality information, by incorporating uncertainty-quantified predictions (specifically probabilistic interval predictions from conformal inference). The optimization-based design paradigm builds on the consistency-robustness tradeoff structure from prior ski rental and online search work, while the multi-instance online learning approach adapts the ε-net framework of Hazan & Megiddo (2007) to exploit local regularity in the UQ prediction space. The protection-function-based algorithm for online search inherits its structure from El-Yaniv et al. (2001), with the key departure being that the protection function is now optimized via a distributionally-robust objective rather than a worst-case one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DRCR metric provides a principled interpolation between worst-case and average-case analysis that is directly computable from conformal prediction outputs, making it a practical bridge between ML uncertainty quantification and online algorithm design. The two-paradigm framework (optimization-based for single instances, online learning for multi-instance settings) is general enough to extend to other online problems beyond ski rental and online search. Key open questions include: how to handle UQ forms that do not yield tractable ambiguity sets (addressed only heuristically via the online learning approach), whether the Lipschitzness conditions on cost upper bounds hold broadly in practice, and how to extend the framework to problems where the critical value is multi-dimensional or the prediction structure is more complex than a single interval.
