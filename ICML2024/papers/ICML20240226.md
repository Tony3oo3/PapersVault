## Problem

The Wasserstein over Wasserstein distance is a natural metric on laws of random probabilities, but the infinite dimensionality of the space of probability measures causes its sample complexity to be slower than any polynomial rate in the sample size. This makes numerical estimation infeasible for realistic sample sizes, and no existing alternative distance simultaneously metrizes weak convergence, admits efficient estimation, and provides analytical bounds.

## Input / Output

The method takes two collections of n discrete probability measures, each with m atoms described by positions X_{i,j}^k and weights ω_{i,j}^k (stored as n×m×2 arrays), and outputs a single scalar distance. For numerical computation in one dimension, atoms are projected onto a fixed grid of M ≈ min(√n, √m) points, and the distance is computed via gradient ascent over the grid-restricted Lipschitz functions. The hierarchical empirical estimator (2) handles the case where individual random probabilities are not themselves discrete by approximating each P̃_i with m i.i.d. draws from it.

## Method

The hierarchical IPM (HIPM) is defined as d_F(Q₁,Q₂) = sup_{f∈F} W(L(P̃₁(f)), L(P̃₂(f))), the supremum of one-dimensional Wasserstein distances between the laws of integrals of test functions under two random probabilities. This can be rewritten as an IPM on P(X) with function class F = {P ↦ g(P(f)) : f∈F, g∈Lip₁(R)}, which is strictly smaller than the Wasserstein-Lipschitz class used by the Wasserstein over Wasserstein distance. The smaller class yields a parametric 1/√n convergence rate (up to dimension-dependent log factors) via Rademacher complexity bounds, in contrast to the sub-polynomial rate of W_W. The paper proves the distance metrizes weak convergence on compact spaces, derives closed-form expressions for species sampling processes (recovering W_W = W(P₁,P₂) for Dirichlet processes), and provides a gradient ascent algorithm with Lipschitz constraints projected onto a 1D grid for numerical computation.

## Results

The paper's central quantitative result is a comparison of sample complexity rates: the Wasserstein over Wasserstein distance W_W converges at rate O(log(log n)/log n) (upper bound) and is lower-bounded by c_γ·n^{-γ} for every γ>0 (i.e., slower than any polynomial), while the proposed Lipschitz HIPM d_Lip achieves parametric rate O(n^{-1/2}) in dimension 1, O(n^{-1/2}log n) in dimension 2, and O(n^{-1/d}) for d>2 (Theorems 4.1, 4.3). For the hierarchical empirical estimator with m atoms per measure, the combined rate is O(n^{-1/2}+m^{-1/2}) in 1D (Theorem 4.4). Proposition 6.1 further shows that among three Dirichlet process approximations, the Dirichlet multinomial and hierarchical empirical measure both scale as O(N^{-1/2}) in the number of atoms N, while truncated stick-breaking scales exponentially as (α/(α+1))^N but degrades for large concentration α.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between the Wasserstein over Wasserstein distance W_W (the established metric) and the proposed Lipschitz HIPM d_Lip. The paper proves that W_W suffers from a fundamental curse of dimensionality: its sample complexity is slower than any polynomial in n (Theorem 4.1), while d_Lip achieves the parametric n^{-1/2} rate in 1D (Theorem 4.3). Numerically (Figure 1, right panel), d_Lip converges visibly faster than W_W when estimating the distance between two independent realizations of the same Dirichlet process. For the Dirichlet process approximation task (Section 6, Figure 2), the paper compares three finite-dimensional approximations: the Dirichlet multinomial and the hierarchical empirical measure yield similar O(N^{-1/2}) errors and are robust to large α, while truncated stick-breaking has exponential convergence in N but its rate (α/(α+1))^N becomes vacuous when α is large (e.g., α ≈ n for posteriors with n observations). The paper's practical recommendation is to prefer the Dirichlet multinomial or hierarchical empirical measure when α is large, and stick-breaking when N can be made much larger than α and the space is low-dimensional.

## Relation to Prior Work

The Wasserstein over Wasserstein (W_W) distance, also called Hierarchical Optimal Transport, has been independently introduced and applied in Bayesian mixture model analysis (Nguyen, 2016), topic modeling (Yurochkin et al., 2019), joint clustering (Ho et al., 2017), GAN training (Dukler et al., 2019), and dataset discrepancy measurement (Alvarez-Melis & Fusi, 2020). This paper departs from that line by proving W_W has fundamentally poor sample complexity (slower than any polynomial rate) due to the infinite dimensionality of the probability space, and proposes the Hierarchical IPM (HIPM) as a replacement that retains the key topological properties while achieving parametric convergence rates. The construction draws on integral probability metrics (Müller, 1997) and the duality between Wasserstein and Lipschitz function classes, while the closed-form results for species sampling processes extend Nguyen's (2016) identity for Dirichlet processes to a much broader class.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The negative result on W_W's sample complexity is a significant caution for practitioners who have been using this metric in applications ranging from topic models to GANs, as it implies that finite-sample estimates may be unreliable even at large sample sizes. The HIPM provides a theoretically grounded alternative with explicit convergence guarantees, and the practical comparison of Dirichlet process approximations (multinomial, stick-breaking, hierarchical empirical) yields actionable guidance for Bayesian nonparametric practitioners. Key limitations include the restriction of the numerical algorithm to one-dimensional spaces, the lack of convergence guarantees for the gradient ascent procedure, and the absence of a clear qualitative characterization of when W_W and HIPM diverge substantially.
