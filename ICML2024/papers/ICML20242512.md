## Problem

Sampling from target distributions defined by a terminal potential over full sequences in language models requires intractable marginalization over future tokens at each step, since the target is non-causal. Prior SMC work for language models used fixed, few-step-ahead target information rather than learning intermediate twist functions that summarize the expected future value of a terminal-only potential. Additionally, there was no principled way to evaluate whether an inference method actually recovers the target distribution, as only one-sided (lower) bounds on the log partition function were available.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained base language model p0, a variable-length prompt s0, and a terminal potential φ(s_{1:T}) (e.g., a reward model score, classifier probability, or indicator function) as input. It produces K generated sequences with importance weights from which a single approximate target sample can be drawn, along with bidirectional bounds on log Z_σ and KL divergence estimates in both directions. The twist functions ψ_t are parameterized as neural networks mapping partial sequences to scalars, and the twist-induced proposal q_t^π(s_t|s_{1:t-1}) ∝ p0(s_t|s_{1:t-1})ψ_t(s_{1:t}) is tractable to sample within standard transformer architectures by summing over the discrete vocabulary.

## Method

The core framework is twisted SMC, where learned twist functions ψ_t(s_{1:t}) approximate the expected future potential value, enabling resampling that clones promising partial sequences and kills unpromising ones at each timestep. The paper introduces Contrastive Twist Learning (CTL), which minimizes per-timestep KL divergences between target marginals σ(s_{1:t}) and twisted intermediate targets π_t^θ(s_{1:t}), yielding a gradient with positive sampling from the target (via BDMC or rejection sampling) and negative sampling from the twisted distribution, analogous to energy-based model training. A twist-induced proposal is derived as the variance-minimizing one-step proposal, making the incremental weights independent of the sampled token for t < T. For evaluation, the paper proves novel bidirectional SMC bounds on log Z_σ: the standard lower bound from q_SMC sampling and a new upper bound requiring a single exact target sample (obtained via the BDMC trick), whose gap upper-bounds the symmetrized KL divergence between the SMC sample distribution and the target. The framework also establishes connections to soft RL, where twists correspond to Q-values and the proposal to an actor.

## Results

On the toxicity task (TinyStories), CTL achieves D_KL(σ||q) = 1.07±0.02, the best mass-covering divergence among all twist-learning methods, while PPO achieves the lowest D_KL(q||σ) = 0.98±0.01 (consistent with its training objective). On the sentiment task (GPT-2-Medium), CTL achieves the best KL in both directions: D_KL(q||σ) = 0.55±0.03 and D_KL(σ||q) = 0.47±0.01. On the infilling task, DPG performs best (D_KL(q||σ) = 13.27±0.44, D_KL(σ||q) = 4.90±0.03) due to its access to exact positive samples, while CTL (23.93/8.87) remains competitive among twist-learning methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CTL outperforms all other twist-learning methods (soft Q-learning, SIXO, FUDGE) on both KL divergence directions for the toxicity and sentiment tasks, with the largest gap over FUDGE (e.g., 1.07 vs. 2.00 on D_KL(σ||q) for toxicity). PPO achieves the lowest D_KL(q||σ) on toxicity (0.98 vs. CTL's 1.11), which is expected since PPO directly optimizes this mode-seeking divergence during training; however, PPO is worse on D_KL(σ||q) (1.32 vs. 1.07), revealing mode-dropping behavior. DPG is competitive with CTL on toxicity (1.09/1.12) and sentiment (0.72/0.57) but clearly outperforms CTL on infilling (13.27/4.90 vs. 23.93/8.87), where exact positive samples are available via the BDMC trick. The paper concludes that CTL is preferable when only approximate positive samples are available (toxicity, sentiment), while DPG is preferable when exact target samples can be drawn (infilling). The twist-induced proposal q^π provides orders-of-magnitude efficiency gains over the base model proposal p_0 for log Z_σ estimation, as shown in Fig. 3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts twisted Sequential Monte Carlo from state-space models (Briers et al., 2010; Doucet et al., 2001) to language modeling, where prior SMC work (Lew et al., 2023) used fixed or few-step-ahead target information rather than learned twist functions for terminal-potential-only targets. It formalizes connections between twist functions and soft RL Q-values (Levine, 2018; Lawson et al., 2018; Lioutas et al., 2022), positioning FUDGE, SIXO, and soft Q-learning as special cases within a unified SMC framework. The bidirectional SMC bounds on the log partition function extend the IWAE lower/upper bound literature (Burda et al., 2015; Brekelmans et al., 2021) to the resampling setting, and the contrastive twist learning objective draws on energy-based model training (Gutmann & Hyvärinen, 2010).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bidirectional SMC bounds provide a principled way to quantify inference quality (via KL divergence in both directions) for any controlled generation or fine-tuning method, addressing a gap where practitioners previously lacked reliable diagnostics for mode-dropping in PPO-style training. The twist-induced proposal yielding orders-of-magnitude efficiency gains over base-model sampling is practically significant for rare-event sampling (e.g., red-teaming). However, experiments are limited to small models (TinyStories 33M, GPT-2 Medium) and short sequences, leaving open whether the framework's advantages persist at the scale of modern LLMs with long contexts and complex reward models. The added complexity of training separate twist networks at each timestep also raises practical adoption questions.
