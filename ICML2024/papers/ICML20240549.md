## Problem

Previous comparisons of pseudo-outcome regression (POR) methods for CATE estimation focused heavily on the choice of pseudo-outcome transformation, but the paper argues the dominant driver of performance is actually the choice of regression weights. Inverse-propensity-weighted pseudo-outcomes become unstable when propensity scores approach 0 or 1, and no systematic framework existed for understanding which weights are optimal or how they affect convergence rates without assuming knowledge of the covariate distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240549/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a binary treatment indicator A, covariates X, observed outcomes Y, and pre-estimated nuisance functions (propensity scores π̂, outcome models μ̂_a) obtained via cross-fitting, and produces a pointwise CATE estimate τ̂(x_new) at a target location using local polynomial regression. The theoretical output includes convergence rate bounds under three levels of cross-fitting (2-way, 3-way, 4-way), with oracle-efficiency conditions expressed in terms of nuisance smoothness parameters s_π and s_μ relative to the CATE smoothness s_τ and covariate dimension d.

## Method

The core insight is that R-Learning is equivalent to a weighted POR with inverse-variance weights {A−π̂(X)}², and the paper extends this to the DR-Learner by using stabilizing weights ν̂(X)=π̂(X)κ̂(X) when regressing the DR pseudo-outcome f_DR,θ̂(Z) on X via local polynomial regression. These weights downweight observations with extreme propensity scores (high pseudo-outcome variance) without reintroducing confounding, since the CATE is conditional on X and Y is unconfounded within X-strata. A key algebraic identity shows the weighted conditional bias reduces to the product of nuisance biases (B_μ · B_π), enabling the derivation of oracle-efficient convergence rates. The paper proves oracle efficiency (minimax rate n^{−1/(2+d/s_τ)}) whenever s_π + s_μ > c_oracle, which is, to the author's knowledge, the fastest rate achievable without assuming knowledge of the covariate distribution, and simulations across six settings confirm weighted POR consistently outperforms unweighted POR.

## Results

Across six simulation settings (A–F) with sample sizes 250–1000, weighted POR (using inverse-variance weights) matched or outperformed unweighted POR in every setting, with performance similar across the three weighted variants (DR, covariance-based, and U-Learner pseudo-outcomes). In the simple illustration (Setting F), the R-Learner achieves rMSE = 0.288 versus rMSE = 1.38 for the unweighted U-Learner. Theoretically, the IVW-weighted DR-Learner with 4-way cross-fitting achieves the oracle minimax rate n^{−1/(2+d/s_τ)} whenever s_π + s_μ > d/(2+d/s_τ), which is the fastest rate shown without assuming knowledge of the covariate distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three unweighted POR baselines against their IVW-weighted counterparts across six simulation settings. Weighted DR-Learning (with weights κ̂(X)π̂(X)) outperforms the unweighted DR-Learner (Kennedy, 2023) in all settings, with the gap most pronounced in Settings A and C where propensity scores are complex. Weighted U-Learning (i.e., the R-Learner, with weights {A−π̂(X)}²) outperforms the unweighted U-Learner (Kunzel et al., 2019) in all settings, confirming that the R-Learner's implicit IVW is the key to its stability. The T-Learner (Kunzel et al., 2019) is dramatically outperformed by all weighted POR methods in Settings A, B, and C (where confounding or complex prognostic functions are present), but performs comparably in Settings D, E, and F (where the CATE is constant or the prognosis is simple). Overall, the three weighted POR variants perform similarly to each other, suggesting that the choice of weights matters more than the choice of pseudo-outcome transformation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the pseudo-outcome regression (POR) framework from Kunzel et al. (2019) and the R-Learning moment condition from Robinson (1988) / Nie & Wager (2020), and extends the DR-Learner from Kennedy (2023). Its central conceptual departure is reframing the design question in POR: prior comparisons focused on the choice of pseudo-outcome transformation (IPW, DR, covariance-based), but this paper argues the dominant performance driver is actually the choice of regression weights. It shows that R-Learning implicitly performs a POR with inverse-variance weights (IVWs) — a connection the authors state has not been discussed in the literature — and then extends this insight by adding IVW weights κ̂(X)π̂(X) to the DR-Learner, deriving oracle-efficient convergence rates without assuming knowledge of the covariate distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is clear: when fitting a POR for CATE estimation, using inverse-variance weights stabilizes the regression against extreme propensity scores and improves performance across all simulation settings, providing a simple diagnostic for practitioners. The theoretical contribution — oracle-efficient rates under only s_π + s_μ > c_oracle without assuming the covariate distribution — is the fastest known under minimal assumptions, though the strongest bound requires 4-way cross-fitting, which is impractical in small-to-moderate samples. An important open question the paper highlights is the partial aggregation setting (V ⊂ X), where variance-reducing weights would change the estimand itself, making IVW-based methods harder to apply without additional modeling steps.
