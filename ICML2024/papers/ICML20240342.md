## Problem

Cross-sectional brain network classifiers treat neurodegenerative diagnostic groups as independent categories, ignoring the progressive temporal dynamics inherent to diseases like Alzheimer's and Parkinson's. Additionally, brain connectomes exhibit coexisting homophily and heterophily, yet standard GNNs aggregate neighbor information uniformly, failing to capture localized spectral characteristics at each node.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240342/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes brain graphs with node features (cortical thickness from MRI, FDG-PET SUVR, or BOLD fMRI signals) and adjacency matrices (tractography-based connectivities or inter-regional BOLD correlations), parcellated on the Destrieux atlas (160 ROIs) or AAL atlas (116 ROIs). It produces a diagnostic class label (5 progressive stages for ADNI, 3 for PPMI) along with C sets of N trainable node-wise scale parameters, where each scale encodes the optimal spectral bandwidth (positive for low-pass smoothing, negative for high-pass emphasis) at each brain region for each diagnostic class, enabling interpretable regional analysis.

## Method

AGT introduces a node-variant convolution built on an adaptive wavelet basis ψ_{s_i^c} with trainable node-wise scales s_i^c that can be positive (low-pass, aggregating from a wide neighborhood) or negative (high-pass, emphasizing local node-specific features), allowing each brain region to capture its optimal mix of homophily and heterophily. The scale-filtered node features X_s are fused with the original features and passed through a 2-layer GCN using the adjacency matrix, which captures spectral coherence between graph structure and node features via graph convolution (AX_s). A novel group-level temporal regularization R_temp enforces sequential alignment of class-mean embeddings in latent space by penalizing violations of the triangular distance constraint d_{c,c+1} + d_{c+1,c+2} = d_{c,c+2} across three adjacent diagnostic groups, ensuring the learned representation reflects the ordered progression of neurodegeneration. The full loss combines cross-entropy with L2 regularization and the temporal term weighted by α, optimized end-to-end via backpropagation.

## Results

AGT achieves 90.3% accuracy on ADNI-CT (5-class), 94.8% on ADNI-FDG (5-class), and 83.6% on PPMI (3-class), consistently outperforming all 11 baselines across accuracy, precision, recall, and specificity. The margins over the second-best method are +2.5% accuracy on ADNI-CT, +4.4% on ADNI-FDG, and +2.4% on PPMI, with particularly large gains in precision (+11.0%) and specificity (+5.0%) on PPMI. Ablation confirms the adaptive local filter contributes +15.8% accuracy over any fixed-scale variant, and the temporal regularization adds ~1% accuracy across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGT outperforms all 11 baselines on every metric across all three experiments. On ADNI-CT, AGT (90.3% acc) beats the closest competitors BrainNetTF (87.8%) and LSAP (87.0%) by 2.5 and 3.3 percentage points respectively, while also achieving the highest precision (91.3%) and recall (89.9%). On ADNI-FDG, the gap widens: AGT (94.8% acc) surpasses LSAP (90.4%) by 4.4 points, and ADC (88.6%) by 6.2 points. On PPMI, AGT (83.6% acc) leads GAT (81.2%) by 2.4 points in accuracy but by a much larger 11.0 points in precision (62.4% vs. 51.4%) and 5.0 points in specificity (92.3% vs. 87.3%), indicating substantially better discrimination of the most deteriorated class. Notably, standard GNNs (GCN, GAT) and brain-specific methods (BrainGNN, BrainNetTF) underperform significantly on the 5-class ADNI tasks, with BrainGNN showing particularly poor precision (~20%), while spectral/diffusion-based methods (Exact, LSAP, GraphHeat) are the strongest baselines but still trail AGT by 2–4 points in accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

AGT extends the line of spectral GNNs with learnable filters (GPR-GNN, AdaGNN, Wave-GD) and node-wise scale learning (Exact, LSAP) by introducing an adaptive wavelet basis that simultaneously captures both low-frequency (homophily) and high-frequency (heterophily) characteristics per node, whereas prior node-wise scale methods like Exact and LSAP only extract low-frequency features. The spectral coherence formulation from Cho et al. (2023) provides the theoretical bridge between wavelet-domain dot products and spatial-domain graph convolution, which AGT leverages to unify node and edge features. The group-level temporal regularization is a novel loss component not present in prior brain network classifiers, which treat diagnostic labels as nominal categories rather than sequential stages of degeneration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enforcing sequential distance constraints between diagnostic groups in latent space, AGT addresses a fundamental limitation of cross-sectional brain network classifiers that ignore the progressive, irreversible nature of neurodegenerative diseases. The node-wise scale parameters provide clinically interpretable outputs—identifying specific brain regions (e.g., medial occipitotemporal gyrus, parahippocampal gyrus, superior temporal gyrus) whose importance shifts across disease stages, consistent with known neuroanatomy. Limitations include the reliance on pseudo-labels from a pretrained Exact model for scale selection during inference, the requirement of full-batch training for the temporal regularization, and validation on only two datasets with relatively small PPMI cohorts (195 subjects). Open questions include whether the temporal regularization generalizes to longitudinal data with repeated measures per subject, and whether the approach extends to diseases without a clear linear progression.
