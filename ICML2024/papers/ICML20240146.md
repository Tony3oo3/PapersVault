## Problem

Existing constrained decoding methods for LLMs suffer from two coupled failures: they impose significant inference overhead, and they distort the model's token distribution by forcing sub-optimal tokenization at grammar boundaries (token misalignment), which can reduce downstream task accuracy by up to 11 percentage points. No prior approach simultaneously supports full context-free grammars, achieves minimal invasiveness (preserving unconstrained-model accuracy), and maintains low or even negative inference overhead.

## Input / Output

DOMINO takes a tokenized text prompt, a pre-compiled formal language constraint (regular expression or context-free grammar), and a frozen LLM as inputs, and produces a grammar-valid token sequence as output. The grammar must be known ahead of time in an offline setting, and the method operates at the sub-word (BPE) token level, where individual vocabulary tokens may span multiple grammar terminals or only partially cover one. The output is guaranteed to be in the constrained language while preserving the model's natural tokenization preferences.

## Method

DOMINO separates constraint enforcement into an offline pre-computation phase and a lightweight online decoding phase. Offline, it constructs a character-level NFA scanner from the grammar, then for each scanner state enumerates every vocabulary token's traversal through the NFA to build a prefix tree of subterminal sequences (full terminals, start/end/continuation subterminals), aligning the LLM's sub-word vocabulary with grammar terminals. Online, at each decoding step it traverses only the relevant prefix tree(s) up to a configurable lookahead depth k, pruning branches that the parser deems illegal, and collects the surviving leaf tokens as the valid mask—avoiding the need to check the full vocabulary. Two further optimizations reduce overhead: opportunistic masking (checking the LLM's top-proposed token against the parser before computing the full mask) and a count-based speculative decoder that predicts multiple tokens conditioned on the current (subterminal, parser-state) pair, achieving up to 2.7× throughput over unconstrained generation while matching or exceeding unconstrained accuracy.

## Results

On GSM8K (5-shot, JSON-constrained), DOMINO (k=∞) matches or slightly exceeds unconstrained accuracy (0.418 vs. 0.415 on Mistral-7B; 0.262 vs. 0.262 on Llama-2-13B), while standard GUIDANCE drops accuracy by up to 11 percentage points (0.345 on Mistral-7B, 0.152 on Llama-2-13B). On CoNLL-2003, DOMINO again matches unconstrained accuracy (0.21 on Mistral-7B, 0.09 on Llama-2-13B) while GUIDANCE halves accuracy (0.098 and 0.062 respectively). In terms of throughput, DOMINO achieves up to 2.71× speedup over unconstrained generation (CoNLL-2003, Llama-2-13B) via speculative decoding, compared to GUIDANCE's 2.18× at the cost of significant accuracy loss, and llama.cpp's consistent ~0.8× slowdown.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DOMINO is compared against two main baselines: GUIDANCE (template-based and whitespace-flexible CFG programs) and llama.cpp (online EBNF grammar parsing). Against GUIDANCE, DOMINO wins decisively on accuracy: on GSM8K with Llama-2-13B, DOMINO achieves 0.262 accuracy versus GUIDANCE's 0.152 (a 11-point gap), and on CoNLL-2003 with Mistral-7B, DOMINO scores 0.21 versus GUIDANCE's 0.098. While GUIDANCE's template-based approach can achieve higher raw throughput (up to 2.18×), this comes at the cost of severe accuracy degradation due to token misalignment; DOMINO matches or exceeds unconstrained accuracy while still achieving 1.66–2.71× throughput. Against llama.cpp, DOMINO also wins on both accuracy and throughput: llama.cpp consistently runs at ~0.8× unconstrained speed and shows modest accuracy drops (e.g., 0.375 vs. 0.415 on GSM8K/Mistral-7B), whereas DOMINO is faster than unconstrained and maintains full accuracy. The whitespace-flexible GUIDANCE-WS variant partially recovers accuracy (e.g., 0.403 on GSM8K/Mistral-7B) but at a significant throughput cost (~0.54–0.90×), making it a less attractive trade-off than DOMINO.

## Relation to Prior Work

DOMINO builds on the pre-computation paradigm from Willard & Louf (2023) for vocabulary-aligned regex checking, extends it from regular expressions to full context-free grammars via a novel subterminal tree construction, and integrates speculative decoding (Chen et al., 2023) and opportunistic masking (llama.cpp) into a unified constrained decoding pipeline. It departs from online parser-guided methods (PICARD, GCD, SYNCHROMESH) that check the entire vocabulary at each step, and from template-based approaches (GUIDANCE, LMQL) that introduce token misalignment by forcing external tokenization. The key conceptual contribution is formalizing 'minimally invasive' constraining and showing that existing methods, despite guaranteeing syntactic validity, systematically distort the model's token distribution through suboptimal bridge-token choices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that naive constrained decoding can reduce task accuracy by over 10 percentage points (41.5% → 30.8% on GSM8K) challenges the widespread assumption that syntactic constraints are quality-neutral, and provides practitioners with a concrete diagnostic (perplexity, token alignment) for identifying invasive constraining. DOMINO's ability to achieve ~2× throughput over unconstrained generation while matching or exceeding unconstrained accuracy makes constrained decoding practically viable in high-throughput production settings. Open limitations include incompatibility with dynamic or input-dependent grammars and precomputation cost for very large grammars (e.g., entity disambiguation), pointing toward incremental or just-in-time compilation as a natural next step.
