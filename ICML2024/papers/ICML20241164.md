## Problem

Semi-supervised learning (SSL) suffers from a fundamental model selection gap: splitting a validation set from the already-scarce labeled data further degrades training, and the resulting small validation set provides unreliable generalization estimates. Existing model selection methods from UDA or fully-supervised settings are either not designed for SSL, require a validation split, or lack theoretical guarantees, leaving practitioners without a principled way to choose among candidate SSL models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a finite hypothesis set of candidate models (e.g., checkpoints across training epochs or hyperparameter configurations) along with the labeled training set and the full unlabeled set. For each candidate, it computes the labeled classification margin, the product of layer-wise spectral norms, and KNN-based local-consistency weights from the unlabeled data. The output is the single model minimizing the SLAM objective, accompanied by a non-vacuous generalization error estimate that upper-bounds the excess error relative to the best model in the search space.

## Method

SLAM exploits a key SSL-specific observation: the classification margin on labeled data alone (as opposed to unlabeled data) shifts downward when the model overfits noisy pseudo-labels, making it a reliable overfitting indicator. The SLAM objective combines three terms — empirical error on labeled data, the ratio of empirical spectral complexity (product of per-layer spectral norms) to the locally-reweighted labeled margin, and a sample-complexity term sqrt(log k / n) — and selects the model minimizing this composite score. To handle distributional bias from the small labeled set, a local-consistency reweighting scheme up-weights labeled samples whose KNN neighbors in the unlabeled pool receive consistent pseudo-labels and down-weights borderline or marginal samples. The theoretical guarantee is established via structural risk minimization and McDiarmid's inequality, yielding an oracle inequality that bounds the excess error of the SLAM-selected model by a term governed by the spectral-normalized margin complexity of the optimal model, ensuring convergence to the best-in-set model as the SLAM score decreases.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 early-stopping, SLAM achieves 95.51% accuracy with FixMatch (4000 labels), within 0.17% of the test-set-optimal model (95.68%), while outperforming all baselines by 0.3–1.5 points. On CIFAR-100, SLAM reaches 71.27% with FixMatch (10000 labels) vs. 72.01% optimal, again beating the next-best baseline (DEV at 70.85%) by 0.42 points. Against validation-set splitting on CIFAR-10, SLAM gains 15.8 points over 10% validation split for MixMatch (250 labels: 84.47% vs. 68.64%) and 4.3 points for FixMatch (4000 labels: 95.51% vs. 93.68%). On the out-of-class Semi-Aves benchmark, SLAM achieves 67.80% with FixMatch, within 0.23% of optimal and above all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLAM consistently outperforms all four baselines across nearly every setting. EB-Criterion (Mahsereci et al., 2017) is the weakest baseline, lagging SLAM by 27 points on MixMatch(40) CIFAR-10 (32.86% vs. 59.99%) and by 15+ points on CIFAR-100, indicating it is poorly suited to the SSL regime. DEV (You et al., 2019) is the strongest competitor, reaching within 0.3–0.5 points of SLAM on high-label-budget settings (e.g., FixMatch 4000: 95.25% vs. 95.51%), but it requires a validation split and was modified to proxy labeled-loss minimization, making it less directly applicable. SND (Saito et al., 2021) and MixVal (Hu et al., 2023) are highly inconsistent: MixVal collapses to 36.08% on FixMatch(40) CIFAR-10 and 39.04% on MixMatch(400) CIFAR-100, while SND drops to 63.12% on FixMatch(40), revealing that heuristic neighborhood-density methods fail when the SSL model is poorly trained. Against validation-set splitting (Table 4), SLAM gains 15.8 points over 10% validation for MixMatch(250) and 4.3 points for FixMatch(4000), confirming that even with sufficient labels, a held-out validation set is inferior to SLAM's training-data-only criterion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the spectrally-normalized margin generalization bound of Bartlett et al. (2017) and the structural risk minimization framework of Koltchinskii (2001), repurposing them into a model selection objective tailored for SSL. It departs from UDA-based model selection methods (SND, MixVal, DEV) that rely on unlabeled data or validation splits, and from EB-criterion which ignores SSL-specific phenomena like noisy pseudo-labels. The key conceptual departure is the observation that in SSL, the *labeled* margin (rather than unlabeled margin or overall loss) serves as a reliable overfitting indicator, a property absent in fully-supervised settings where over-parameterized networks can maximize margin while memorizing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Model selection for SOTA SSL algorithms (FixMatch, MixMatch, ReMixMatch) was previously unaddressed, forcing practitioners to either waste scarce labeled data on validation splits or skip selection entirely. SLAM fills this gap with a theoretically grounded, validation-free criterion, and the oracle inequality provides a rare formal guarantee that the selected model is within a bounded distance of the best-in-set model. Open questions include whether the spectral complexity surrogate remains informative for very deep or transformer-based architectures, whether the local-consistency reweighting generalizes to non-i.i.d. or distribution-shifted unlabeled data, and whether the approach extends to SSL paradigms beyond vision (e.g., graph or text SSL).
