## Problem

In cooperative MARL, learning goal-reaching policies in complex tasks (e.g., super-hard SMAC maps, sparse-reward GRF) is slow and often fails to achieve the semantic goal. Goal-conditioned RL has been largely unexplored in MARL because goals are not explicitly known, partial observability prevents global path planning at execution time, and the desired trajectory is a coordinated sequence rather than a shortest path. The paper addresses how to generate and follow goal-reaching reference trajectories in a learned latent space to provide principled training incentives.

## Input / Output

LAGMA consumes the global state vector (dimension ranging from 26 to 1170 across tasks) as input to the VQ-VAE encoder, which projects it into a D-dimensional (typically D=8) continuous embedding and then quantizes it to one of n_c codebook vectors. Each agent's local action-observation history is encoded by a GRU for the individual Q-network. The method outputs a joint action via QMIX-style value factorization, a quantized state embedding (a single codebook index), and a latent goal-guided intrinsic reward r^I that is added to the TD-target during centralized training. The extended VQ codebook stores top-k goal-reaching index sequences per starting quantized vector, and the sequence buffer D_seq maintains these trajectories for reference sampling.

## Method

LAGMA builds a modified VQ-VAE with a novel timestep-dependent coverage loss L_cvr that uses an indexing function J(t) to pull different subsets of codebook vectors toward the current embedding at each timestep, ensuring uniform coverage of the feasible state manifold rather than collapsing all vectors to its center. An extended VQ codebook D_VQ maintains count-based value estimates C_{q,t} for each quantized vector via a moving average, and a sequence buffer D_seq stores top-k goal-reaching index sequences (by C_{q,t}) for each starting node. During training, a reference trajectory τ*_{χ_t} is sampled from D_seq, and the latent goal-guided intrinsic reward r^I(s') = γ(C_{q,t}(s') − max_{a'} Q_{θ−}(s',a')) is applied only when the next quantized state lies on τ*_{χ_t} and differs from the current one. Proposition 4.1 proves that this reward, when s' is on a true goal-reaching trajectory, transforms the TD-target into an unbiased Monte-Carlo estimate of the true value V*(s'), guaranteeing convergence to the optimal policy. The overall loss augments the standard QMIX TD-loss with r^I, and the VQ-VAE is trained jointly with the policy under the CTDE paradigm.

## Results

On the SMAC MMM2 super-hard map (nominal setting), LAGMA achieves a 97.5% ± 0.026 winrate after 1M training steps, substantially outperforming QMIX (38.1% ± 0.078) and LDSA (44.4% ± 0.107). In sparse-reward SMAC settings (3m, 8m, 2s3z, 2m_vs_1z), LAGMA consistently reaches the highest winrate among all baselines, with the latent goal-guided incentive enabling faster convergence when positive reward signals are rare. On Google Research Football (3_vs_1WK, CA_easy), LAGMA matches or exceeds EMC's performance while avoiding the additional episodic buffer memory overhead. The policy robustness test on unseen MMM2 map variants shows LAGMA retains 50–57% of its nominal winrate, compared to 36–51% for QMIX and 14–18% for LDSA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAGMA outperforms all five baselines across every evaluated benchmark. Against QMIX (the backbone value-factorization method), LAGMA achieves roughly 2.5× higher winrate on the super-hard MMM2 map (97.5% vs. 38.1%) and consistently leads on all dense- and sparse-reward SMAC maps. Compared to LDSA (dynamic subtask assignment), LAGMA wins on every map, with the gap widening on harder tasks (e.g., 97.5% vs. 44.4% on MMM2 nominal). Against RODE (role-conditioned policy) and MASER (subgoal generation from replay buffer), LAGMA achieves higher final winrates on both SMAC and GRF, particularly in sparse-reward settings where reward signals are scarce. On GRF, LAGMA matches or slightly exceeds EMC (episodic control + curiosity-driven exploration) while requiring no additional episodic buffer, reducing memory overhead. The robustness test further confirms that LAGMA's quantized-embedding-based value sharing generalizes better to unseen initial positions than both QMIX and LDSA.

## Relation to Prior Work

LAGMA sits at the intersection of three research threads: state space abstraction for RL (previously studied in single-agent settings via methods like NECSA and EMU), goal-conditioned reinforcement learning (GCRL, which has been applied to single-agent sparse-reward tasks but only limitedly to MARL), and the CTDE value-factorization paradigm (QMIX and successors). The paper departs from prior state abstraction work by using a modified VQ-VAE with a novel coverage loss to handle the constrained state distributions typical of MARL (where feasible states occupy a small subspace, causing codebook collapse in standard VQ-VAE). It also departs from GCRL by addressing the fact that in cooperative MARL, goals are semantic and only discoverable through reward signals, and by providing a theoretically grounded intrinsic reward (Proposition 4.1) that guarantees a true TD-target rather than a heuristic exploration bonus, distinguishing it from approaches like MASER that use Euclidean distance in actionable representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a principled way to improve sample efficiency in cooperative MARL, particularly in sparse-reward settings where replay buffers rarely contain positive reward signals. The theoretical guarantee that the latent goal-guided intrinsic reward yields an unbiased estimate of the true TD-target is a clean contribution that distinguishes it from ad-hoc intrinsic reward designs. However, the paper honestly acknowledges a key limitation: on multi-objective tasks like SMACv2, where multiple distinct goals exist within the same task, LAGMA's reference trajectory storage (which only considers return) may fail to capture diverse goal-reaching paths, limiting its effectiveness. Open questions include how to incorporate trajectory diversity into the sequence buffer, whether goal- or strategy-dependent agent-wise execution could improve coordination in multi-goal settings, and whether the approach extends to non-episodic or continuous-time MARL tasks.
