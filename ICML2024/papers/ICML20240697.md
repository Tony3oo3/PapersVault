## Problem

Open-set semi-supervised learning requires classifying inliers from seen categories while rejecting outliers from novel categories in unlabeled data. Existing detect-and-filter strategies are inherently error-prone because reliable outlier detection is impossible without labeled outliers, and detection errors propagate permanently into the classifier. This paper asks whether the problem can be restructured to avoid outlier detection altogether.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240697/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled image set (4–25 samples per seen category on CIFAR, 1–5% on ImageNet-30) and an unlabeled image set containing a mixture of inliers and unknown-category outliers. At inference, each unseen image is scored by K one-vs-all sigmoid classifiers; if the maximum probability falls below 0.5 the sample is rejected as novel (label K+1), otherwise it receives the argmax class. Backbones are Wide ResNet-28-2 (CIFAR-10/100, 32×32) and ResNet-18 (ImageNet-30, 224×224).

## Method

BDMatch decomposes the K-class open-set SSL problem into K independent binary SSL problems, each asking 'is this image from category c or not?', so that outliers naturally become negative samples without any detection step. To handle the resulting class-imbalance (ratio 1/(K−1) in labeled data, worse in unlabeled data), it applies an adaptive logit adjustment that tracks a running-average prediction probability on unlabeled data as a proxy imbalance ratio and shifts logits toward the minority class, combined with a re-margining supervised loss and a dual-branch architecture (balanced + standard classifiers on a shared backbone). To address representation-compromise across binary problems with different discriminative preferences, it replaces the shared pooled feature with label-specific features extracted via a dot-product attention module over the backbone's spatial feature map, using a learnable query vector per class. Training follows the FixMatch consistency-regularization framework with a high confidence threshold (ρ=0.99) for pseudo-label masking.

## Results

BDMatch achieves the best inlier accuracy across all 10 experimental settings, with headline results of 91.77% on CIFAR-10 (6/4 split, 4 labels/class), 57.40% on CIFAR-100 (20/80 split, 4 labels/class), and 82.88% on ImageNet-30 (20/10 split, 5% labels). In terms of overall accuracy (balanced accuracy including outlier rejection), BDMatch reaches 78.31% on CIFAR-10 (6/4, 4 labels) and 65.48% on ImageNet-30 (20/10, 1% labels), improving over the previous best by up to 13.46% absolute. The method maintains stable performance as labeled data decreases, while most competing open-set SSL approaches suffer severe degradation under extreme label scarcity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BDMatch outperforms all 16 baselines on inlier accuracy across nearly all settings. Among standard SSL methods, FixMatch (81.58%) and CoMatch (86.08%) are the strongest on CIFAR-10 (6/4, 4 labels), yet BDMatch achieves 91.77%, a 5.69–10.19% absolute gain. Among open-set SSL methods, IOMatch is the closest competitor, achieving 89.68% on CIFAR-10 (6/4, 4 labels) and 81.43% on ImageNet-30 (5% labels); BDMatch surpasses it by 2.09% and 1.45% respectively, with the gap widening to 6.00% on ImageNet-30 (1% labels: 75.18% vs. 69.18%). On overall accuracy (which penalizes failure to reject outliers), the advantage is even more pronounced: BDMatch improves over IOMatch by 13.46% on ImageNet-30 (1% labels) and 12.21% on CIFAR-100 (80/20, 25 labels). Older detect-and-filter methods (UASD, DS3L, MTCF, OpenMatch) are substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 30–50% on many settings, confirming the paper's argument that error-prone outlier detection is a fundamental bottleneck. The only near-tie is on CIFAR-10 (6/4, 25 labels) where BDMatch (93.86%) and IOMatch (93.87%) are essentially equivalent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant detect-and-filter paradigm in open-set SSL (OpenMatch, T2T, OSP, IOMatch) by reframing the problem as a set of standard binary SSL problems via binary decomposition, eliminating the need for an explicit outlier detector. It builds on FixMatch's consistency regularization and confidence-based pseudo-labeling as the training backbone, adapts logit adjustment (Menon et al., 2021) and re-margining losses (Ren et al., 2020) to handle the class-imbalance inherent in the binary decomposition, and borrows label-specific feature learning from the multi-label classification literature to address representation compromise across binary problems. The dual-branch architecture for class-imbalance is adapted from long-tailed recognition (Kang et al., 2020), and the paper explicitly positions itself at the intersection of open-set SSL and class-imbalanced SSL, a connection it claims is the first to be explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The binary decomposition strategy offers a conceptually simpler and more robust alternative to detect-and-filter methods, with the notable finding that outliers are actively beneficial as negative samples rather than needing to be filtered (oracle purification actually hurts performance). This reframing opens a path to leverage the rich class-imbalanced SSL literature for open-set problems. However, the approach requires K separate classifiers and label-specific feature extractors, raising scalability concerns for very large label spaces, and the label-specific feature learning is currently only implemented for image data via spatial attention on feature maps. Open questions include whether the strategy extends naturally to non-image modalities, whether the K-classifier overhead becomes prohibitive at scale, and whether the adaptive logit adjustment mechanism generalizes to more extreme distribution shifts between labeled and unlabeled sets.
