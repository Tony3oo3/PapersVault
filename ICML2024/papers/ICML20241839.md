## Problem

The interaction between the smoothing distribution and robustness certification in Randomized Smoothing remains largely unexplored beyond the known optimality of Gaussian for ℓ₂ certified radius. It is unclear whether other distribution families can match Gaussian's certification or whether the curse of dimensionality in Double Sampling RS (where certified radius degrades with input dimension) can be further alleviated beyond the General Gaussian solution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241839/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10 or 224×224 ImageNet) and a pre-trained base classifier, then applies smoothing distributions parameterized by exponent η (ESG: S(σ,η) or EGG: G(σ,η,k)) to compute ℓ₂ certified radii. Certification is performed by solving a functional optimization problem (Neyman-Pearson for single-distribution RS, or the two-constraint DSRS problem) using Monte Carlo sampling with N=100,000 samples and Clopper-Pearson confidence intervals at α=0.001. The output is certified accuracy at various radius thresholds (0.25 to 3.50) and the Average Certified Radius (ACR) as an aggregate metric.

## Method

The paper introduces two distribution families—Exponential Standard Gaussian (ESG, generalizing Gaussian by exponent η) and Exponential General Gaussian (EGG, generalizing General Gaussian by exponent η)—and analyzes their effect on RS and DSRS certification. For ESG, the key insight is reversing the traditional computation: instead of binary-searching for radius r given sampling probability A, they derive A as a function of r under two mild high-dimensional assumptions, yielding the closed-form A = Ψ_{(d-1)/2}(1/2 + ρ/(2σ√d)), which converges to Cohen et al.'s Gaussian formula as d→∞ and is provably independent of η. For EGG, they prove that under a (σ,p,2)-concentration property, EGG with η∈(0,2) tightens the Ω(√d) lower bound constant factor μ compared to General Gaussian (η=2), with μ increasing monotonically as η decreases. In practice, where real classifiers do not perfectly satisfy concentration, larger η (up to 8.0) provides better certification, improving certified accuracy by up to 6.4% on ImageNet over the DSRS baseline.

## Results

On ImageNet, EGG with η=8.0 under DSRS achieves 59.1% certified accuracy at r=0.25 and 10.1% at r=3.00, compared to 58.4% and 6.1% for the DSRS baseline (General Gaussian, η=2.0), yielding up to a 6.4% absolute improvement at r=3.25 (8.6% vs. 2.2%). On CIFAR-10, EGG η=8.0 reaches 57.6% at r=0.25 and 0.5% at r=3.00, versus 56.7% and 0.1% for the baseline. For ESG distributions, certified accuracy is nearly identical across all tested exponents (η ∈ {1.0, 2.0, 4.0, 8.0}), confirming the theoretical finding that the exponent has negligible impact on RS certification in high dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is DSRS with General Gaussian (EGG η=2.0) from Li et al. (2022), which was the first method to break the curse of dimensionality for ℓ∞ certification. EGG with η=8.0 consistently outperforms this baseline across all radii on both CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, with the gap widening at larger radii (e.g., on ImageNet at r=3.00: 10.1% vs. 6.1%, a 4.0% absolute gain; at r=3.25: 8.6% vs. 2.2%, a 6.4% gain). The secondary baseline is NP certification (Cohen et al., 2019) for single-distribution smoothing; Figure 5 shows that while DSRS's advantage over NP shrinks as η increases in EGG, the absolute certified accuracy from DSRS keeps growing, suggesting an upper bound near Gaussian's certification. For ESG distributions, all exponents (η=1.0, 2.0, 4.0, 8.0) produce nearly identical certified accuracy (within 0.1–0.3% at most radii), effectively tying the Gaussian (η=2.0) SOTA and broadening the set of optimal smoothing distributions for RS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the randomized smoothing (RS) framework by generalizing the smoothing distribution's exponent η beyond the Gaussian case (η=2). It builds directly on Cohen et al. (2019), who established Gaussian as the SOTA distribution for ℓ₂ certified radius via the Neyman-Pearson lemma, and on Li et al. (2022), who introduced Double Sampling RS (DSRS) with General Gaussian to break the curse of dimensionality. The paper's two distribution families—Exponential Standard Gaussian (ESG) and Exponential General Gaussian (EGG)—are natural generalizations that vary η while preserving the formal variance, allowing a systematic study of how the exponent interacts with certification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that the 'optimal' smoothing distribution for RS is not uniquely Gaussian but extends to the entire ESG family, broadening design space without sacrificing certification. For EGG, the paper provides a theoretical path to tighten the Ω(√d) lower bound constant in DSRS, though experiments reveal the mechanism flips depending on whether real classifiers satisfy a concentration property. An open question the paper raises is whether EGG's certification improvement is bounded by Gaussian's (ESG η=2) ceiling, suggesting diminishing returns for further exponent tuning. The 6.4% ImageNet improvement is meaningful but the paper itself notes the gain is finite, leaving the fundamental question of whether any distribution can surpass the Gaussian/ESG family's certification.
