## Problem

Existing NTK-based approaches for ReLU neural network bandits require strong effective-dimension assumptions that yield superlinear regret (e.g., Õ(T^{(2d-1)/(2d)})) for any domain dimension d > 1. This paper asks whether a minimax-optimal Õ(√T) regret bound is achievable for nonlinear bandits with ReLU structure by directly estimating the reward model rather than relying on kernel-space concentration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242258/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each round the algorithm receives a d-dimensional covariate x on the unit sphere S^{d-1} and observes a scalar stochastic reward y = f_{Θ*}(x) + ξ. The output is a selected action x_t and, internally, a 2kd-dimensional parameter vector θ‡ in a transformed feature space that encodes both the original neuron weights and sign-flip correction terms. The action space is restricted to a region X(Θ̃, ν*/2) where the ReLU indicators are consistent with the true ones, ensuring the linear bandit model is well-specified.

## Method

The key insight is that once one-layer ReLU neuron parameters are estimated to within ν*/2 of their true values, the piecewise-linear indicator functions can be frozen, converting the nonlinear reward into a linear function of a transformed feature x‡ ∈ R^{2kd}. The extra k dimensions in x‡ (beyond the naive kd) explicitly capture the sign-flip ambiguity inherent in ReLU parameter estimation, so the linear bandit remains valid even when θ̃_i ≈ -θ_i*. OFU-ReLU runs a random exploration phase of length t₀ to obtain parameter estimates, then applies OFUL on the transformed linear problem. OFU-ReLU+ removes the need to know the gap ν* by splitting the horizon into geometrically growing batches with a geometrically decreasing gap guess, accumulating all past samples (rather than discarding them) so that after a constant number of batches the estimate is accurate enough for the linear regime to activate.

## Results

The paper evaluates cumulative regret over a time horizon T=1,000 with 50 trials and 95% confidence intervals on a one-layer ReLU network with d=2, k=3 neurons. OFU-ReLU significantly outperforms all baselines, converging in approximately 20 time steps while NeuralUCB variants require substantially longer to converge. The theoretical regret bound is Õ(kd√T), and the empirical results confirm that the algorithm achieves near-optimal regret even in short horizons where the theoretically required exploration phase (t₀) would be much longer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares OFU-ReLU against four baselines: OFUL (linear bandit assuming a linear reward model), NeuralUCB-F (20 neurons, two layers, unknown structure), NeuralUCB-T (knows the true one-layer structure with m=k neurons), and NeuralUCB-TW (knows structure but expands to m=2k neurons). OFU-ReLU wins decisively against all four baselines in cumulative regret. OFUL suffers large regret due to model misspecification since the true reward is nonlinear. All NeuralUCB variants use gradient descent to learn the model and converge slowly; even NeuralUCB-T, which is given the exact network architecture, is significantly outperformed by OFU-ReLU. The key practical advantage is convergence speed: OFU-ReLU reaches low regret in ~20 steps within T=1,000, whereas NeuralUCB methods (as noted in Zhou et al., 2020) typically require horizons of T≈10,000 to converge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant NTK-based approach in ReLU bandit literature (Zhou et al., 2020; Kassraie & Krause, 2022; Gu et al., 2024), which relies on effective dimension assumptions to achieve sublinear regret. Instead, it directly exploits the piecewise linear structure of ReLU activations, building on parameter estimation results for one-layer ReLU networks (Du & Lee, 2018; Zhang et al., 2019) and the OFUL linear bandit algorithm (Abbasi-Yadkori et al., 2011). The two-phase explore-then-commit design is motivated by Rajaraman et al. (2023), who showed such phase transitions are inevitable for certain nonlinear bandit problems, but this paper's key departure is reducing the ReLU problem to a linear bandit in a transformed feature space rather than applying UCB directly to the nonlinear reward.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to achieve the minimax-optimal Õ(√T) regret for ReLU neural network bandits without effective dimension assumptions, resolving a gap where prior NTK-based methods gave superlinear bounds (e.g., Õ(T^{(2d-1)/(2d)})) for any dimension d > 1. The practical impact is currently limited by the restriction to one-layer ReLU networks and a theoretically long exploration phase, though experiments suggest fast convergence in practice. Key open questions include whether the piecewise-linear reduction strategy extends to multi-layer architectures or other activation functions, and whether the batching strategy in OFU-ReLU+ can be tightened to remove the polynomial T^{8log(b)/log(a)} overhead.
