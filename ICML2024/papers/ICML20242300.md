## Problem

Existing ML methods for crystal property prediction focus on scalar invariants or simple vector outputs, overlooking the tensorial nature of macroscopic properties like dielectric, piezoelectric, and elastic tensors. The central challenge is how to design a model that simultaneously satisfies O(3) tensor equivariance (the tensor must transform correctly under arbitrary rotations/reflections) and crystal space group invariance (the tensor must respect the specific zero and equality constraints imposed by the crystal's symmetry), across arbitrary tensor orders.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242300/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a crystal structure represented as atom feature vectors, 3D Cartesian positions, and a 3×3 lattice matrix, and produces tensor properties of varying orders: a 3×3 dielectric tensor, a 3×3×3 piezoelectric tensor, and a 3×3×3×3 elastic tensor. A key constraint is that the output tensors must exhibit exact zero elements and mutually dependent (equal) elements dictated by the crystal's space group, with the specific pattern varying across the seven crystal systems. The dataset is curated from JARVIS-DFT with tensor values and structures extracted from the same DFT calculation files to guarantee symmetry congruence.

## Method

GMTNet is a four-module equivariant GNN: (1) a symmetry-informed periodic crystal graph built with k-th nearest-neighbor radius and RBF-encoded edge features; (2) crystal-level equivariant feature extraction combining Comformer invariant attention layers with TFN-style equivariant message passing using Clebsch-Gordan tensor products up to rotation order ℓ_max=3 or 4; (3) an equivariant tensor prediction module that simulates the physical response (e.g., D = εE) via tensor products of crystal-level equivariant features with the external field, then derives the tensor as a gradient ∂D/∂E, ensuring O(3) equivariance by construction; and (4) a symmetry enforcement module that averages crystal-level features over all rotation-reflection operations in the space group using Wigner D matrices, guaranteeing exact space group invariance even under numerical errors and structural distortions. Training uses Huber loss with AdamW and polynomial LR decay on a curated JARVIS-DFT dataset split 8:1:1.

## Results

On the dielectric tensor test set, GMTNet achieves a Frobenius norm (Fnorm) error of 3.50, outperforming ETGNN (3.92) and MEGNET (4.16). For high-quality prediction rate (EwT), GMTNet reaches 84.5% within the 25% threshold and 57.1% within the stricter 10% threshold, compared to 81.3%/41.6% for ETGNN and 74.9%/38.9% for MEGNET. GMTNet achieves 100% success rate in capturing symmetry-constrained zero and equal-valued tensor elements across all crystal systems, while MEGNET achieves 0% and ETGNN achieves at most 13.5% for zero elements. Extended to piezoelectric (order 3) and elastic (order 4) tensors, GMTNet maintains 100% symmetry compliance with Fnorm errors of 0.37 C/m² and 67.38 GPa respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMTNet outperforms both baselines on every reported metric. Against MEGNET (an invariant graph network), GMTNet reduces Fnorm from 4.16 to 3.50 (16% improvement) and increases the 10% EwT rate from 38.9% to 57.1% (nearly 50% relative gain); MEGNET also completely fails to capture any symmetry-constrained zero or equal-valued tensor elements (0% success rate across all crystal systems). Against ETGNN (an equivariant tensor prediction framework), GMTNet achieves a 10.7% lower Fnorm (3.50 vs. 3.92) and a substantially higher 10% EwT rate (57.1% vs. 41.6%), while matching ETGNN's 100% success on cubic-system equality constraints and far exceeding it on tetragonal (100% vs. 55.3%) and hexagonal/trigonal (100% vs. 0%) systems. For zero-element symmetry, GMTNet achieves 100% across all crystal systems versus ETGNN's 0–13.5%. GMTNet is also more efficient, using 37% fewer parameters (0.7M vs. 1.1M) and running 43% faster per batch (0.069s vs. 0.121s) than ETGNN.

## Relation to Prior Work

GMTNet builds on Tensor Field Networks (Thomas et al., 2018) for equivariant message passing via Clebsch-Gordan tensor products, and on Comformer (Yan et al., 2024) for invariant node feature updating, combining them into a unified crystal graph pipeline. It departs conceptually from ETGNN (Zhong et al., 2023), which constructs tensors through outer products of edge vectors (yielding O(nk^(m-1)) complexity and no crystal symmetry enforcement), by instead simulating physical responses via tensor products of equivariant crystal features with external field inputs and recovering tensors through gradients. The symmetry enforcement module, which averages crystal-level features over space group Wigner D-transformations, is a novel architectural component not present in prior equivariant GNNs for materials.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to simultaneously guarantee O(3) equivariance and crystal space group invariance for general-order tensor predictions (dielectric, piezoelectric, elastic), which is a prerequisite for physically meaningful predictions in materials design. The curated JARVIS-DFT tensor dataset with tailored evaluation metrics (symmetry zero/equality success rates, EwT thresholds) provides a much-needed benchmark for the field. Limitations include the relatively small dataset sizes (<15K samples) and inability to handle amorphous materials; open questions include whether the symmetry enforcement module generalizes to lower-symmetry or disordered systems and whether the gradient-based tensor recovery approach scales to tensors of order 5 or higher.
