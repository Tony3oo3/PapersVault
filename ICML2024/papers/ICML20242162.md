## Problem

Cross-domain offline RL aims to leverage source-domain data to compensate for scarce target-domain data, but simply merging the two datasets causes severe performance degradation due to dynamics mismatch. Existing approaches measure the domain gap via dynamics ratios or domain classifiers, which become unbounded or unreliable when the two domains differ significantly (e.g., broken joints or morphology changes), leading to rapid performance collapse. The paper addresses how to obtain a robust, bounded measure of domain discrepancy and a practical mechanism to selectively share only useful source transitions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two offline datasets of (s, a, r, s') transition tuples collected in MuJoCo environments (HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2d) that share the same state/action/reward spaces but differ in transition dynamics due to modified XML parameters (body mass, joint noise, broken joints, or morphology changes). The target dataset is a reduced subset (e.g., 10%) of standard D4RL data, while the source dataset is 10× larger, collected in the perturbed environment. The output is a target-domain policy (via IQL) trained on the full target batch plus a top-ξ filtered subset of source transitions, where the filtering is governed by a learned contrastive score function.

## Method

The core insight is to replace the unbounded dynamics ratio with the mutual-information (MI) gap between source and target transition functions, which is provably bounded by the entropy of the state distribution. The MI gap is estimated via a single contrastive InfoNCE objective where target-domain transitions serve as positive samples and cross-domain constructed transitions (target (s,a) paired with source s') serve as negatives, trained with two encoder networks φ(s,a) and ψ(s') and a linear score h(s,a,s')=exp(φ(s,a)ᵀψ(s')). A theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) shows the negative contrastive loss lower-bounds the MI gap, and Theorem 3.2 decomposes the MI gap into a KL dynamics term regularized by a state-visitatio KL term, explaining its boundedness. At training time, source transitions are ranked by the score h and only the top-ξ quantile is mixed into the target batch for IQL's TD update, optionally weighted by h to modulate the TD-error contribution. The entire pipeline is an add-on module that can be plugged into any offline RL backbone without modifying its core optimization.

## Results

On 18 D4RL MuJoCo tasks (HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2d × Medium/Medium-Replay/Medium-Expert) with body mass and joint noise dynamics shifts, IGDF achieves a sum average of 639.05 (body mass) and 654.85 (joint noise) using only 10% target data, corresponding to just -11.89% and -10.81% degradation relative to 100% target data baselines. Under larger dynamics gaps (broken thighs and morphology shifts), IGDF further widens its lead, achieving 538.31 and 525.71 sum averages while DARA drops to 335.14 and 232.60. The paper reports that IGDF attains 89.2% of the performance of SOTA methods trained on 100% target data while using only 10% of the target dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IGDF outperforms DARA (Liu et al., 2022) by a wide margin on both moderate and large dynamics gaps: on body mass shift, IGDF's sum score of 639.05 exceeds DARA's 546.56 by ~93 points, and on the harder morphology shift task, IGDF scores 525.71 versus DARA's 232.60 (a 293-point gap), confirming that DARA's likelihood-based dynamics gap becomes unbounded under large domain shifts. Against SRPO (Xue et al., 2023), IGDF wins on 11/18 tasks for body mass and 17/18 for morphology, with the largest gaps on Hopper-Medium-Expert (103.97 vs. 72.59 for joint noise) and Hopper-Medium-Expert broken (95.95 vs. 50.71). Compared to BOSA (Liu et al., 2024), IGDF achieves a higher sum on body mass (639.05 vs. 564.3) but trails slightly on joint noise (654.85 vs. 660.6 for DARA* best backbone). Simply merging cross-domain data without filtering (w/o Aug) degrades performance by 45–52%, demonstrating that naive data sharing is harmful. IGDF is positioned as a plug-in module compatible with any offline RL backbone (IQL used here), and its advantage grows with the magnitude of the dynamics gap, where discriminator-based methods (DARA, SRPO) fail most severely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in cross-domain offline RL of measuring dynamics gap via domain classifiers (DARA, Eysenbach et al.) or explicit dynamics models (BOSA), which suffer from unbounded estimation when domains differ significantly. Instead, it reframes the domain gap as a mutual-information gap between transition functions, estimated through a contrastive (InfoNCE-style) objective that learns representations of state-action pairs and next states. The learned score function then serves as a data filter for selectively sharing source-domain transitions, rather than modifying rewards (DARA, SRPO) or imposing support constraints (BOSA). The theoretical connection showing that the MI gap recovers the expected dynamics gap while remaining bounded by state entropy provides a principled justification for this shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The boundedness of the MI gap (by state entropy) addresses a fundamental fragility in prior cross-domain offline RL methods that degrade sharply under large dynamics shifts, making IGDF a more robust add-on module for arbitrary offline RL backbones. The practical result of achieving ~89% of full-data performance with only 10% target data is significant for settings where target-domain data collection is expensive (robotics, healthcare). A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the filtering criterion considers only transition-probability similarity and ignores trajectory quality (e.g., return), which could be incorporated as a future extension. The approach is also limited to the assumption of shared state/action/reward spaces, restricting applicability to morphology-level shifts rather than fundamentally different observation spaces.
