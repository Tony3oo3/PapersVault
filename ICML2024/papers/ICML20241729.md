## Problem

Prior work on differentially private federated learning (Lowy & Razaviyayn, 2023) achieved optimal convergence rates but required super-linear computational complexity proportional to |S|^{3/2} and provided guarantees only for homogeneous data distributions. This paper closes both gaps by delivering the same optimal rates with linear computational complexity and simultaneously for homogeneous and heterogeneous settings.

## Input / Output

Each of M machines holds a local dataset S_i of T i.i.d. samples and a convex G-Lipschitz L-smooth loss; the server broadcasts query points and aggregates gradient estimates in a fully synchronous parallel-SGD loop. The output is a trained model x_T (a weighted average of iterates) accompanied by a (α, αρ²/2)-Rényi DP guarantee for every machine, where ρ controls the privacy-utility tradeoff. Experiments use logistic regression (7850 parameters) on MNIST with M·T = 60,000 total samples across M ∈ {1, 10, 100} machines.

## Method

The core mechanism is a federated adaptation of μ²-SGD, combining an AnytimeGD-style weighted-average iterate with a corrected momentum estimator (β_t = 1/t) that reduces gradient variance as O(σ̃²/t) rather than the constant O(σ²) of standard SGD. The key privacy insight is that this momentum structure makes each sample's contribution to the gradient estimate decay as O(S/t), so a single-sample change has bounded per-step sensitivity 2S rather than accumulating over all T steps. For the untrusted server, each machine adds independent Gaussian noise to its own estimate before sending; for the trusted server, noise is added once after the server aggregates all M estimates, yielding an additional 1/M sensitivity reduction. Both variants achieve linear computational complexity in total dataset size |S|, matching standard non-private FL, and attain the optimal rates O(1/√(nM) + √d/(εn√M)) and O(1/√(nM) + √d/(εnM)) respectively.

## Results

On MNIST with logistic regression (M·T=60,000), the untrusted-server DP-μ² achieves 70.4% accuracy at ρ=16, M=1, degrading to 65.4% at ρ=4, M=100. The trusted-server variant maintains 69.5–70.4% accuracy across all M and ρ settings, showing substantially less degradation with increasing M (e.g., 69.5% vs. 65.4% at M=100, ρ=4). Higher ρ (weaker privacy) consistently improves accuracy by 0.3–5.0 percentage points depending on M. The theoretical convergence rates are O(1/√(nM) + √d/(εn√M)) for untrusted and O(1/√(nM) + √d/(εnM)) for trusted server, both matching known lower bounds with linear computational complexity in |S|.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is primarily a theoretical contribution and does not benchmark against external empirical baselines; the experiments serve as validation of the proposed DP-μ²-FL algorithms. The key internal comparison is between the untrusted-server and trusted-server settings: at M=100, ρ=4, the untrusted server drops to 65.4% accuracy while the trusted server retains 69.5%, a 4.1-point gap attributable to per-machine noise injection versus server-side aggregation before noise. As M increases from 1 to 100, the untrusted-server accuracy degrades more sharply (up to 5 points) than the trusted-server case (up to 0.9 points), confirming the theoretical advantage of the 1/M factor in the trusted-server privacy budget. Increasing ρ from 4 to 16 narrows the gap between trusted and untrusted settings, as weaker privacy requirements reduce the impact of the noise mechanism. The paper's main empirical claim is that the method achieves competitive accuracy under DP while maintaining linear computational complexity, in contrast to prior DP-FL approaches (e.g., Lowy & Razaviyayn, 2023) that require O(|S|^{3/2}) complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends μ²-SGD (Levy, 2023), a double-momentum stochastic optimization method, to the differentially private federated learning setting, combining it with the standard minibatch-SGD parallelization template (Dekel et al., 2012) and the AnytimeGD weighted-average iterate mechanism (Cutkosky, 2019). It directly addresses a key limitation of the prior optimal DP-FL results by Lowy & Razaviyayn (2023), which required super-linear O(|S|^{3/2}) computational complexity and were proven only for homogeneous data. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that μ²-SGD's corrected momentum structure inherently reduces the sensitivity of gradient estimates to individual samples (from O(G) to O(S/t)), thereby requiring substantially less Gaussian noise to achieve the same Rényi DP guarantee while maintaining linear computational complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a significant gap between the theoretical optimality of DP-FL convergence rates and their practical computational cost, showing that optimal rates can be achieved with the same linear complexity as non-private federated learning. The extension to heterogeneous data distributions (where prior optimal bounds were homogeneous-only) broadens applicability to realistic multi-organization settings. However, the empirical validation is extremely limited—only MNIST with logistic regression—and the open question of whether the trusted-server guarantees can be recovered under the weaker 'trusted shuffler' assumption (as in Lowy & Razaviyayn, 2023) remains unresolved. The convex-only scope also limits immediate relevance to the dominant non-convex deep learning setting.
