## Problem

Existing data protection methods against latent diffusion models (e.g., FSGM, ASPL, AdvDM) assume the textual prompt used during protection matches the one used during malicious fine-tuning, an assumption that is unrealistic since protectors have no knowledge of exploiters' prompts. The paper empirically shows that breaking this assumption substantially degrades protection (e.g., FDS increases 35% for FSGM under prompt mismatch), while the visual encoder (KL-VAE) in LDMs—being inherently prompt-independent—has been underexploited for defense.

## Input / Output

The method takes clean RGB images (512×512 from CelebA-HQ or VGGFACE) and produces a pixel-space perturbation δ constrained by ‖δ‖∞ ≤ 0.05 (or 8/255 for tighter budgets), added to the original image to form the protected image. The perturbation is optimized solely through the KL-VAE visual encoder of Stable Diffusion (v1.5 or v2.1), requiring only ~5 GB GPU memory versus ~24 GB for UNet-based defenses. Protected images are saved in PNG by default, with robustness to JPEG compression, random cropping, smoothing, and denoising also evaluated.

## Method

PID's core loss L_add-log = ‖μ_E(x+δ) − μ_E(x)‖² + log(σ_E(x+δ)²/σ_E(x)²) jointly maximizes the ℓ₂ distance between latent means and the log-ratio of latent variances, simultaneously disrupting semantic content (via mean) and structural/texture quality (via variance) of the fine-tuning process. This is optimized via PGD_1000 under an ℓ∞ constraint, and the key insight is that the KL-VAE encoder is completely independent of textual prompts, making the resulting perturbation robust to any fine-tuning prompt chosen by the exploiter. The paper shows L_add-log is the only tested target that significantly shifts both latent statistics (mean ℓ₂ distance ~3.5, variance ~0.06), outperforming mean-only targets (L_mean, L_mean^T), sample-based targets (L_sample), and additive targets (L_add). PID can also be hybridized with existing prompt-dependent defenses via a weighted combination L_combo = T(θ,c,x) + λL(x) with λ=0.05, further improving robustness under prompt mismatch.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ with Stable Diffusion v1.5 under the realistic prompt-mismatch scenario (c_prot ≠ c_explo), PID achieves FDS of 0.254 (frozen text encoder) and 0.303 (unfrozen), substantially outperforming FSGM (0.374/0.387), ASPL (0.370/0.412), and AdvDM (0.378/0.407). PID consistently maintains FID above 300 across all settings, while prompt-dependent baselines drop to 185–254. Notably, PID requires only ~5 GB GPU memory versus ~24 GB for UNet-based defenses, representing roughly 20% of the computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PID dominates all three prompt-dependent baselines (AdvDM, FSGM, ASPL) across every metric in both the matched and mismatched prompt scenarios. Under the critical prompt-mismatch setting, PID's FDS of 0.254 (frozen TE) is 32% lower than FSGM (0.374) and 31% lower than ASPL (0.370), while its FID of 352.8 exceeds FSGM's 224.5 by 57% and ASPL's 185.1 by 90%. The degradation of prompt-dependent defenses under mismatch is stark: FSGM's FDS worsens from 0.342 to 0.374 and FID drops from 246.4 to 224.5, while ASPL's FID plummets from 295.4 to 185.1. PID, being prompt-independent, shows far less sensitivity (FDS shifts only from 0.205 to 0.254, FID from 412.0 to 352.8). When hybridized (ASPL+PID), the combined defense further improves FDS to 0.254 and FID to 352.8 under mismatch, confirming PID's complementary value. PID also demonstrates superior cross-model transferability (FDS 0.268 for v2.1→v1.5 vs. 0.311–0.371 for baselines) and resilience to adaptive attacks (FDS remains ≤0.257 even under zero-σ or fixed-σ attacks).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of adversarial-perturbation defenses for LDMs (AdvDM, FSGM, ASPL) by identifying a critical shared assumption: all prior methods optimize perturbations conditioned on a specific textual prompt, making them inherently prompt-dependent. The key departure is leveraging the KL-VAE visual encoder's prompt-independence to construct a defense (PID) whose loss L_add-log jointly maximizes mean displacement and log-variance ratio in latent space, requiring no text condition. Conceptually, it repurposes the visual encoder from a passive projection layer into an active defense component, going beyond the mean-only manipulation explored in MIST and the sample-disruption loss in AdvDM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a simple prompt switch degrades FSGM's FDS by 35% and FID by 30% exposes a practical vulnerability in all existing LDM defenses, since protectors cannot know the exploiter's prompt. PID's ~20% GPU memory footprint (5 GB vs. 24 GB) while matching or exceeding UNet-based defenses makes prompt-independent protection accessible to individual users. However, the significant performance drop under JPEG compression (FDS rising from 0.254 to 0.345) and the limited evaluation scope (10 celebrities, 3 alternative prompts, Stable Diffusion family only) leave open questions about real-world robustness. The paper also acknowledges that PID is insufficient for the image-inpainting/editing threat model, pointing to future work.
