## Problem

Computing pairwise Wasserstein distances across a cohort of N point clouds requires O(N²·n²·I) Sinkhorn iterations, making large-cohort OT analysis intractable. While embedding distributions into a space where Euclidean distances proxy OT distances (analogous to MDS) is appealing, Wasserstein is a non-Euclidean metric, and it was previously unclear how well such embeddings can approximate the true distances or what error bounds apply.

## Input / Output

The method takes unordered point clouds of variable size in arbitrary dimensions (from 2D thresholded images to 254-dimensional spatial transcriptomics niches) and produces a fixed-dimensional (128-d) embedding vector per cloud via the encoder, plus a reconstructed point cloud of fixed size (median cohort cloud size) via the decoder. Because the encoder is permutation-invariant and length-agnostic, it handles clouds with any number of points without padding or ordering. The decoder's output point cloud need not match the input's point count, as it is trained only to minimize Wasserstein distance to the original.

## Method

Wasserstein Wormhole is a transformer-based autoencoder where the encoder uses stacked multi-head attention blocks (without positional embeddings, ensuring permutation equivariance) followed by averaging for permutation invariance, and the decoder mirrors this architecture to reconstruct point clouds from embeddings. Training is self-supervised via mini-batches: the encoder loss minimizes the squared discrepancy between pairwise Euclidean distances of embeddings and pairwise Sinkhorn divergences of the batch, while the decoder loss minimizes the Wasserstein distance between input and reconstructed clouds. The key theoretical contribution derives novel upper and lower bounds on the embedding stress for non-Euclidean distance matrices (based on the negative eigenspectrum of the criterion matrix F = −JDJ) and presents a projected gradient descent algorithm with Dykstra projections that is guaranteed to converge to the global optimum, providing a benchmark against which the stochastic Wormhole training is validated.

## Results

Wasserstein Wormhole achieves Pearson OT correlation of 0.97–0.99 across all eight benchmark datasets, consistently outperforming DiffusionEMD, DWE, and the Fréchet baseline. On the three high-dimensional biological datasets (MERFISH 254-dim, SeqFISH 351-dim, scRNA-seq 2500-dim), Wormhole is the only method that completes without running out of memory, achieving correlations of 0.97–0.98 and label accuracies of 0.96–0.98. On the Gromov-Wasserstein Rotated ShapeNet task, Wormhole reaches 0.98 OT correlation and 0.82 label accuracy, while the Fréchet baseline drops to 0.68/0.16. In the theoretical bounds experiment (Table 2), Wormhole's stress falls within the derived lower and upper bounds and approaches the PGD optimum (e.g., 0.100 vs. PGD 0.058 on MNIST-256), whereas classical MDS exceeds the upper bound (0.657 vs. upper 0.616).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiffusionAMD, Wormhole wins decisively on OT correlation for 3D point clouds (0.99 vs. 0.85 on ModelNet40; 0.99 vs. 0.81 on ShapeNet) and on MNIST (0.98 vs. 0.845), while the two are nearly tied on Fashion-MNIST (0.99 vs. 0.97). Against DWE, Wormhole achieves higher OT correlation on MNIST (0.98 vs. 0.92) and ModelNet40 (0.99 vs. 0.97), and is the only method that handles the Gromov-Wasserstein rotated task and all three high-dimensional biological datasets without OOM. Against the Fréchet Gaussian baseline, Wormhole dominates on both metrics: OT correlation improves from 0.86 to 0.98 on MNIST and from 0.95 to 0.99 on ModelNet40, while label accuracy jumps from 0.49 to 0.98 on MNIST and from 0.61 to 0.86 on ModelNet40. In the theoretical stress comparison (Table 2), Wormhole's error is 5–7× lower than classical MDS (e.g., 1.420 vs. 7.134 on Simplex-35) and within a factor of 2.4 of the PGD global optimum, confirming that the stochastic transformer training recovers near-optimal embeddings.

## Relation to Prior Work

Wasserstein Wormhole positions itself as a stochastic, parametric generalization of classical MDS to non-Euclidean distance matrices, building directly on the theoretical framework of Sonthalia et al. (2021) who showed cMDS degrades with dimension on non-Euclidean inputs. It departs from prior OT-preserving embedding methods (DWE, DiffusionEMD) by targeting a Euclidean latent space rather than Wasserstein space, and by using a transformer backbone that handles arbitrary point clouds in any dimension rather than requiring voxelized grids or shared supports. The theoretical contribution—deriving tight lower/upper bounds on embedding error and a PGD algorithm guaranteed to reach the global optimum—carves new ground by making the non-Euclidean MDS problem tractable in principle, while the stochastic mini-batch training of Wormhole makes it tractable in practice for massive cohorts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Wormhole uniquely scales pairwise OT computation to cohorts of 256K+ samples in 254 dimensions where all prior acceleration methods (DWE, DiffusionEMD, Low-Rank Sinkhorn) fail with out-of-memory errors, opening OT-based analysis to atlas-level spatial transcriptomics and high-dimensional single-cell data. The decoder's ability to compute Wasserstein barycenters and interpolations in linear time adds interpretability that pure encoder approaches lack. Key limitations remain: the method currently handles only uniform (unweighted) point clouds, does not recover the optimal transport map itself, and the Euclidean approximation, while empirically tight, has no guarantee of preserving all geometric structure of Wasserstein space. Future work on weighted clouds and differentiable transport maps could extend the framework substantially.
