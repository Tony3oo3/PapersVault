## Problem

Conformal prediction methods for multi-dimensional outputs either produce suboptimal hyper-rectangular prediction sets via coordinate-wise intervals, or require exchangeability assumptions that are violated by time series. No existing method provides valid conditional coverage for multivariate time series while producing tight prediction regions that capture inter-dimensional correlations and temporal dependency among non-conformity scores.

## Input / Output

The method takes T training pairs (X_t, Y_t) with Y_t ∈ R^p, a black-box point predictor f̂, a user-specified significance level α, and a small threshold ρ for low-rank covariance approximation. It sequentially outputs ellipsoidal prediction regions Ĉ_{t-1}(X_t, α) ⊂ R^p at each test time t > T, where the ellipsoid is centered at f̂(X_t) + ε̄, shaped by the pseudo-inverse of the residual covariance matrix, and sized by an adaptively re-estimated quantile of the Mahalanobis non-conformity score. At each step, the residual set is updated by adding the new residual and removing the oldest, making the method fully sequential and online.

## Method

MultiDimSPCI constructs a scalar non-conformity score as the Mahalanobis distance of each prediction residual using a low-rank pseudo-inverse of the residual covariance matrix, reducing the multivariate problem to a univariate quantile calibration. The ellipsoid radius is then calibrated via SPCI (sequential predictive conformal inference), which fits a quantile regression model to the non-conformity scores and re-estimates the relevant quantile at every test time, explicitly accounting for temporal dependency. An additional optimization over β ∈ [0, α] selects the tightest asymmetric region (difference of two concentric ellipsoids) that minimizes volume. Theoretically, the paper proves a finite-sample high-probability bound on the conditional coverage gap under stationarity and strong mixing of the noise process, without requiring exchangeability. Unlike copula-based CP methods that require searching multi-dimensional quantile vectors and return hyper-rectangles, MultiDimSPCI uses only second-moment information and produces ellipsoids that are empirically orders of magnitude smaller in high dimensions.

## Results

On three real multivariate time-series datasets (wind, solar, traffic) with target 95% coverage, MultiDimSPCI achieves valid coverage (0.95–0.97) while producing the smallest prediction regions among all CP baselines. For example, on wind data at p=8, MultiDimSPCI yields an average prediction set size of 72.10, compared to 252.67 (CopulaCPTS), 1,090 (Local ellipsoid), and 1,600 (Copula). In simulation (Table 2), MultiDimSPCI reduces prediction region size by several orders of magnitude over coordinate-wise SPCI as dimension p grows (e.g., at p=20 on VAR(w): 9.15e+12 vs 8.67e+14). Probabilistic forecasting baselines (TFT, DeepAR) frequently under-cover (e.g., DeepAR drops to 0.74 coverage on solar at p=8) while also producing much larger regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MultiDimSPCI consistently outperforms all five baselines on prediction set size while maintaining valid coverage. Against CopulaCPTS (Sun & Yu, 2024), MultiDimSPCI achieves 3–22× smaller regions (e.g., 72.10 vs 252.67 on wind p=8; 4.97 vs 3,280 on solar p=8) with comparable coverage. Against Local ellipsoid (Messoudi et al., 2022), MultiDimSPCI is smaller in most settings (e.g., 7.02 vs 13.07 on wind p=4), though Local ellipsoid edges out at p=2 on solar (1.32 vs 1.68). Against Copula (Messoudi et al., 2021), MultiDimSPCI is 1.7–22× smaller across all datasets and dimensions. Against the probabilistic forecasting baselines TFT and DeepAR, MultiDimSPCI dominates on both metrics: it maintains ≥0.95 coverage where TFT/DeepAR drop to 0.74–0.88, and its regions are 10–1000× smaller. In simulation (Table 2), MultiDimSPCI also beats coordinate-wise SPCI by orders of magnitude in region size as p increases, confirming the benefit of joint ellipsoidal calibration over independent per-dimension intervals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Sequential Predictive Conformal Inference (SPCI, Xu & Xie 2023b) from univariate to multivariate time series by constructing ellipsoidal prediction regions calibrated via a scalar Mahalanobis-type non-conformity score. It departs from copula-based multivariate CP methods (Messoudi et al. 2021; Sun & Yu 2024), which produce hyper-rectangular sets and assume exchangeability or i.i.d. sampling, by instead relying on a low-rank covariance estimate of residuals and adaptive quantile re-estimation during test time. The theoretical analysis builds on concentration inequalities for sample covariance matrices (Vershynin 2012) and extends coverage guarantees from i.i.d. to stationary strong-mixing noise, filling the previously unoccupied 'multivariate Y + non-exchangeable' cell in the CP taxonomy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first finite-sample conditional coverage guarantee for multivariate conformal prediction under non-exchangeable (strong-mixing) data, which is the natural setting for time series. A key open question is whether the ellipsoidal shape, which captures only second-order dependence, is sufficient in high dimensions where residual distributions may be highly non-Gaussian; the authors note convex hulls could yield tighter fits but face combinatorial complexity. The theory currently requires the noise process to be stationary and strong mixing, leaving general non-exchangeable sequences (e.g., with structural breaks) unaddressed. Practically, the method is model-agnostic and requires only a point predictor plus a covariance estimate, making it broadly applicable to any multivariate forecasting pipeline.
