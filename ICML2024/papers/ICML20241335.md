## Problem

A fundamental open problem in differentially private optimization is determining the optimal sample complexity for finding stationary points of non-convex loss functions. Prior to this work, the best known rate for smooth non-convex empirical losses was Õ((√d ln(1/δ)/εn)^(2/3)), and it was unclear whether this barrier could be broken. Additionally, no DP algorithm achieved the optimal Õ(√d ln(1/δ)/εn) rate for important non-convex subclasses such as quasar-convex or Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz (KL) functions, and state-of-the-art rates for population losses and GLMs left gaps relative to the non-private lower bounds.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a fixed dataset X of n samples, a loss function f(w,x) that is L-Lipschitz and β-smooth, and privacy parameters (ε, δ), and operates in two sequential phases: a warm-start phase producing w0 with suboptimality gap ψ, followed by a stationary-point-finding phase. The output w_priv satisfies E‖∇F̂_X(w_priv)‖ ≤ α for first-order stationarity and, when the Hessian is ρ-Lipschitz, ∇²F̂_X(w_priv) ≥ −√(ρα) I_d for second-order stationarity. The specific rate α depends on the function class (general non-convex, quasar-convex, KL*, population, GLM) and the warm-start quality ψ, with the algorithm achieving the convex lower bound Õ(√d ln(1/δ)/εn) for quasar-convex and KL* losses.

## Method

The core contribution is a warm-start meta-algorithm (Algorithm 2) inspired by Nesterov's non-private technique of running accelerated gradient descent before gradient descent. The key insight is that DP-SPIDER's stationarity guarantee scales with the initial suboptimality gap Δ_{w0}, so by first running a private approximate risk minimizer A to obtain a warm start w0 with small gap ψ, and then running DP-SPIDER (or DP-SPIDER-SOSP for second-order points) initialized at w0, the overall stationarity rate improves beyond what either algorithm achieves alone. The framework is flexible: the warm-start A is instantiated as the exponential mechanism for general non-convex losses, DP-SGD for quasar-convex losses, or the algorithm of Menart et al. (2023) for KL* functions, while B remains DP-SPIDER or DP-SPIDER-SOSP. Privacy is preserved via basic composition, splitting (ε, δ) into (ε/2, δ/2) for each phase. For population losses and GLMs, the same two-phase structure extends via Algorithm 4 and the Johnson-Lindenstrauss dimensionality reduction of Arora et al. (2022), respectively, yielding improved rates in the regime d < nε.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: it breaks the long-standing (√d/εn)^(2/3) barrier for DP stationary points of smooth non-convempirical losses, achieving Õ((√d/εn)·d^(1/6)) which improves whenever d < nε. For quasar-convex and KL* empirical losses, the method achieves the optimal rate Õ(√d/εn), matching the convex lower bound for the first time without assuming convexity. In the small-scale empirical evaluation (n = d = 100, synthetic non-convex loss), the warm-start algorithm outperforms both DP-SGD and DP-SPIDER in gradient norm at ε ≤ 1, while all three methods perform comparably at ε ∈ {2, 4}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The empirical evaluation (Section 9) compares the proposed warm-start algorithm (DP-SGD warm-start + DP-SPIDER) against two baselines: DP-SGD (Abadi et al., 2016) and DP-SPIDER (Arora et al., 2023), each run for 100 iterations on a synthetic non-convex loss with n = d = 100. The warm-start algorithm achieves lower gradient norms (both training and test) than both DP-SGD and DP-SPIDER in the high-privacy regime ε ≤ 1, where the privacy budget is most constrained. At ε ∈ {2, 4}, all three algorithms produce comparable gradient norms, indicating no clear benefit from warm-starting when the privacy budget is generous. Theoretically, the warm-start framework strictly improves over DP-SPIDER's (√d/εn)^(2/3) rate for general non-convex empirical losses whenever d < nε, and achieves the optimal √d/εn rate for quasar-convex and KL* losses—rates that neither DP-SGD nor DP-SPIDER alone can match without convexity assumptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DP-SPIDER (Arora et al., 2023) as its core stationary-point finder and on DP-SPIDER-SOSP (Liu et al., 2023) for second-order guarantees, wrapping both in a novel two-stage 'warm-start' meta-algorithm inspired by Nesterov's non-private AGD-then-GD trick. For structured function classes, it leverages the KL* excess-risk algorithm of Menart et al. (2023) as the warm-start stage and the Johnson-Lindenstrauss dimensionality-reduction method of Arora et al. (2022) for GLMs. The key departure from prior work is showing that the 2/3-exponent stationarity barrier, which had resisted improvement since 2022, can be broken by simply initializing a stationary-point finder at a privately computed approximate minimizer rather than at a fixed point.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper achieves the first optimal (up to logs) DP stationarity rates for quasar-convex and KL* non-convex losses without assuming convexity, and breaks the long-standing 2/3-exponent barrier for general smooth non-convex empirical losses. However, a d^{1/6} gap remains for general non-convex functions, and the authors conjecture that a fundamentally different algorithm may be needed to close it. The empirical validation is preliminary (single synthetic loss, n=d=100), leaving open whether the theoretical improvements translate to practical gains at scale, and whether more computationally efficient instantiations of the warm-start framework exist.
