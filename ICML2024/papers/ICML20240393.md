## Problem

Multistep returns are standard in RL, but the variance properties of compound returns—weighted averages of multiple n-step returns—had never been formally characterized. It was unknown whether averaging n-step returns could provably reduce variance relative to a single n-step return with the same contraction modulus, and whether such variance reduction translates into faster learning in practice.

## Input / Output

The return estimator consumes a trajectory of TD errors (each computed from a state, action, reward, and bootstrapped value) and produces a single scalar target for the value-function backup. In the DQN experiments, states are 10×10 multi-channel images; in MuJoCo, they are continuous state vectors. The output is a value or action-value estimate used for ε-greedy or policy-gradient action selection, with the key design constraint that the compound return must match the contraction modulus of a reference n-step return to enable fair bias-variance comparison.

## Method

The paper derives a variance model for arbitrary compound returns under uniform-variance and uniform-correlation assumptions on TD errors, then proves (Theorem 3.7) that any compound return sharing the contraction modulus of an n-step return has strictly lower variance whenever TD errors are not perfectly correlated. A finite-time analysis (Theorem 3.9) shows this variance reduction improves the O(1/√T) convergence constant for linear TD learning. To make the theory practical for minibatch experience replay, the authors introduce Pilar (Piecewise λ-Return), a two-bootstrap return that averages just two n-step returns with lengths chosen to approximate the TD(λ) weight decay on TD errors while preserving the target contraction modulus. Experiments with DQN on MinAtar and PPO on MuJoCo confirm that compound returns improve sample efficiency over their n-step counterparts, with gains growing as n increases.

## Results

In the random-walk prediction task, λ-returns achieve strictly lower RMS value error than their COM-matched n-step counterparts across all tested (n,λ) pairs, with the gap widening sharply as step size α→1. On MinAtar with DQN, Pilars (two-bootstrap compound returns) outperform n-step returns in 3 of 5 games (60%) at n=5 and 1 of 5 games (20%) at n=3, with Seaquest being the only game showing no significant difference. On MuJoCo with PPO, λ-returns (GAE) significantly improve sample efficiency over n-step returns in 7 of 9 environment-return configurations, with the benefit growing as n increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between compound returns (Pilar or λ-returns) and standard n-step returns, paired to have identical contraction modulus (or COM in the undiscounted case). In the 19-state random walk, λ-returns beat their n-step counterparts in all four tested (n,λ) pairs, with the advantage becoming pronounced at large step sizes where variance dominates. In MinAtar DQN experiments, Pilar outperforms n-step returns in 3/5 games at n=5 (Asterix, Breakout, Space Invaders) but only 1/5 at n=3, suggesting the variance-reduction benefit scales with the length (and hence variance) of the n-step return. In MuJoCo PPO experiments, λ-returns (GAE) significantly outperform n-step returns in 7/9 cases across HalfCheetah, Hopper, and Walker2D at (n,λ) pairs of (5, 0.804), (10, 0.905), and (20, 0.955). The paper does not compare against other compound return families (e.g., γ-returns, Ω-returns) or against alternative variance-reduction techniques, focusing exclusively on the n-step vs. compound-return contrast.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the n-step variance model of Konidaris et al. (2011) by introducing a uniform correlation coefficient ρ between TD errors, enabling a general variance formula for arbitrary compound returns. It builds on the error-reduction property identified by Watkins (1989) and the finite-time TD analysis of Bhandari et al. (2018), generalizing both to compound returns. The key conceptual departure is from the long-standing view that n-step and λ-returns are equivalent interpolations between TD and Monte Carlo learning; this paper proves for the first time that compound returns strictly reduce variance relative to any n-step return with the same contraction modulus, establishing a genuine theoretical learning advantage for λ-returns beyond their implementation convenience.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The variance-reduction property provides the first theoretical justification for preferring λ-returns over n-step returns in the absence of function approximation, resolving a long-standing ambiguity in the RL literature. Practically, Pilar (averaging just two n-step returns) offers a near-free improvement for DQN and PPO agents, with negligible additional compute. However, the finite-time guarantees are limited to linear function approximation, and the empirical gains are moderate (Pilar wins in 3/5 MinAtar games at n=5; λ-returns win in 7/9 MuJoCo PPO cases). Open questions include deriving optimal compound-return weight distributions, extending the analysis to nonlinear approximators and off-policy importance-sampling corrections, and understanding whether the observed gains are purely from variance reduction or also from favorable fixed-point properties.
