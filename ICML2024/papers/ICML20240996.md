## Problem

Prior universal consistency results for neural network classifiers (Farago & Lugosi, 1993) only covered shallow architectures with computationally infeasible constructions, and the consistency of finitely wide and deep networks was left as an open problem by Radhakrishnan et al. (2023). Additionally, convergence rate results in the classification literature typically impose explicit smoothness assumptions on the regression function, excluding more general function classes. This paper asks whether wide and deep ReLU networks are universally consistent and characterizes, via Kolmogorov-Donoho optimal exponents, the distributional regimes in which they achieve minimax optimal rates without requiring smoothness.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n i.i.d. samples (X_i, Y_i) from [0,1]^d × {0,1} and produces a binary classifier as a plug-in rule: threshold the scalar output of a ReLU neural network at 1/2. The network is constrained to a growing class defined by architecture S_n (with width or depth at least n), connectivity at most c_d·n, and weight magnitudes bounded by π(n), ensuring the parameter space is compact. The output is characterized by its excess classification risk E[g(X)≠Y] − L*, with the paper establishing both almost-sure convergence to L* for all distributions and explicit polynomial rate bounds under Tsybakov noise and density regularity assumptions.

## Method

For universal consistency, the authors build a Stone-Cech compactification of the disjoint union of bounded ReLU network parameter spaces, making the realization map continuous and the empirical risk minimizer well-defined via a measurable selection argument. The key observation is that a network with width n (or depth n) can interpolate the training data, achieving zero empirical logistic loss, while the compactification guarantees the minimizer converges almost surely to the Bayes classifier. For convergence rates, the excess risk is decomposed into an estimation error controlled by VC-dimension bounds on the ReLU network function class (yielding a polynomial rate in n) and an approximation error controlled by the Kolmogorov-Donoho optimal exponent γ* of the regression function class, leveraging the transference principle showing many classical spaces are optimally representable by neural networks. The Tsybakov noise condition (Assumption 3.1) bridges L² approximation error to classification excess risk via the Audibert-Tsybakov comparison inequality, and the resulting rate is minimax optimal up to polylogarithmic factors for Hölder and Besov function classes.

## Results

The paper establishes two main theoretical results. Theorem 2.2 proves universal strong consistency of wide and deep ReLU neural network classifiers: for any distribution, the empirical risk minimizer of the logistic loss over architectures with width or depth growing at least linearly in n achieves excess risk converging to zero almost surely. Theorem 3.6 shows that under a Tsybakov noise condition (parameter α), a bounded density assumption, and a Kolmogorov-Donoho optimal exponent γ* > 0, the plug-in classifier achieves the minimax optimal rate n^{-m*} (up to polylogarithmic factors). For Hölder classes C^β, the rate is m* = β(1+α)/(2β+d); for Besov classes B_{2,q}^m, the rate is m* = m/(2m+d) with the condition (10) being vacuous.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. In terms of prior theoretical results, the paper improves upon Farago & Lugosi (1993), who established universal consistency only for 1-hidden-layer sigmoidal networks with width o(n/log n) and an O(n^{-1/4}) rate for Barron space, by extending to arbitrary deep and wide ReLU architectures with a computationally feasible (convex surrogate loss) training procedure. It resolves an open problem from Radhakrishnan et al. (2023), who proved weak consistency only for infinitely wide/deep networks interpreted as NTK machines, by showing strong consistency for finitely wide and deep networks. Compared to prior rate results (Kim et al. 2021, Bos & Schmidt-Hieber 2022) that require explicit Hölder or compositional smoothness assumptions on the regression function, the Kolmogorov-Donoho framework encompasses a strictly broader class of function spaces (including Besov, modulation, and cartoon functions) without requiring explicit smoothness, while still achieving minimax optimal rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical universal consistency result of Farago & Lugosi (1993) from shallow sigmoidal networks to wide and deep ReLU networks, answering an explicit open problem left by Radhakrishnan et al. (2023), who only established weak consistency for infinitely wide networks via NTK. For convergence rates, it departs from the dominant smoothness-based paradigm (Hölder, Sobolev) by adopting the Kolmogorov-Donoho optimal exponent framework from Elbrächter et al. (2021), which characterizes function classes through an information-theoretic lens rather than explicit regularity assumptions. The proof of universal consistency relies on a Stone-Cech compactification of the neural network parameter space, a topological tool not previously applied in this context, while the rate analysis combines the Tsybakov noise condition (Audibert & Tsybakov, 2007) with VC-dimension bounds for ReLU networks (Bartlett et al., 2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical guarantee that computationally feasible (convex surrogate loss minimizers) finite-width deep ReLU classifiers are universally strongly consistent, closing a gap that had persisted since the 1990s. By framing the rate analysis through Kolmogorov-Donoho exponents, it unifies convergence guarantees across a much broader family of function spaces (Besov, modulation, cartoon functions) without requiring the practitioner to verify specific smoothness conditions. However, the results are purely theoretical with no empirical validation, the rate in Theorem 3.6 carries a polylogarithmic factor, and the condition (10) linking the Tsybakov parameter, Kolmogorov-Donoho exponent, and minimax rate, while vacuous for Besov spaces, is restrictive for Hölder functions with small smoothness. Open questions include whether the Stone-Cech compactification approach can yield explicit sample complexity bounds, and whether the framework extends to multi-class or regression settings.
