## Problem

State entropy maximization (MSE) has been studied exclusively in fully observable MDPs, where maximizing entropy over observations is equivalent to maximizing it over states. In partially observable settings, the agent only receives noisy observations, creating a significant mismatch between observation entropy and true state entropy. This paper asks: how can an agent maximize entropy over the true (unobserved) states using a policy conditioned only on partial observations, while remaining computationally and memory tractable?

## Input / Output

The agent receives a sequence of partial observations o_t drawn from an emission function O(o|s) and maintains a belief state b_t ∈ Δ(S) (computed exactly via Bayes rule when the POMDP is known, or via an approximate oracle with bounded L1 error ε otherwise). The policy is a Belief-Averaged (BA) policy π_θ(a|b) = ⟨θ, b⟩, a linear map from the belief simplex to the action simplex, requiring only |A|·|S| parameters regardless of horizon length. The output is a stochastic action distribution over a discrete action set, and performance is ultimately evaluated on the true state entropy H(d(τ_S)), which is inaccessible during training in the unknown-POMDP setting.

## Method

The paper introduces a family of proxy objectives—Maximum Observation Entropy (MOE) and Maximum Believed Entropy (MBE)—that approximate the intractable true MSE objective using quantities available to the agent, and derives a REINFORCE-style policy gradient (Theorem 4.2) specialized to the POMDP trajectory distribution. A key insight is the hallucination problem: the agent can exploit belief uncertainty to inflate the entropy of believed states without actually visiting diverse true states. To counter this, the paper introduces Reg-MBE, which subtracts a regularization term ρ·Σ_t H(b_t) that penalizes high-entropy beliefs, creating a dueling objective that rewards diverse believed states while discouraging the agent from inflating its own uncertainty. Theoretical results (Theorem 5.4) bound the gap between the true MSE and each proxy in terms of the hallucination probability, showing that MBE is a tighter lower bound than MOE when this probability is low. Experiments on four gridworld POMDPs with varying observation noise and transition stochasticity confirm that Reg-MBE outperforms both MOE and unregularized MBE, approaching the performance of the oracle MSE objective when good belief approximators are available.

## Results

The paper evaluates four proxy objectives (MSE, MOE, MBE, Reg-MBE) across four gridworld POMDPs with varying observation noise and transition stochasticity. In easy settings (deterministic transitions, small observation variance σ²=0.1), all objectives achieve comparable true state entropy. Under harder settings (σ²=10, stochastic transitions, or extreme observation-state disentanglement), MOE degrades significantly while MBE remains robust; Reg-MBE consistently outperforms non-regularized MBE with faster convergence or higher final entropy. When belief approximation noise is introduced (variance s² up to 0.04), Reg-MBE is significantly more robust than MBE, maintaining performance close to the exact-belief setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four objectives: the ideal MSE (requiring a POMDP simulator), MOE (entropy over observations), MBE (entropy over believed states sampled from approximate beliefs), and Reg-MBE (MBE with belief-entropy regularization). MOE is the weakest baseline, showing clear performance degradation as observation noise increases (Figures 2b, 2c, 2d) and under stochastic transitions (Figure 2e). MBE is remarkably robust to diminishing observation quality but suffers from the hallucination problem, where the agent inflates belief entropy to game the objective. Reg-MBE consistently beats MBE across all environments, showing faster convergence and higher final true state entropy. MSE (with full simulator access) achieves the best performance in most settings, confirming that training on true state entropy is optimal when available. In extreme disentanglement cases (Figure 2g), Reg-MBE slightly outperforms MSE, suggesting the regularization can be beneficial even when the ideal objective is accessible. Under belief approximation noise (Figure 3), Reg-MBE maintains near-ideal performance while MBE degrades substantially, demonstrating that hallucination mitigation also alleviates approximation error.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the state entropy maximization (MSE) framework of Hazan et al. (2019) and its single-trajectory, policy-gradient formulations (Mutti et al., 2021; 2022a) from fully observable MDPs to partially observable POMDPs, where the agent only receives noisy observations. It builds directly on the concurrent work of Zamboni et al. (2024), which characterized when Maximum Observation Entropy (MOE) is a valid proxy for MSE and introduced regularization to control observation randomness; here, the analogous regularization is applied to belief entropy to address the newly identified 'hallucination problem.' The Belief-Averaged (BA) policy class is a novel parameterization that sidesteps the exponential memory cost of history-conditioned policies while retaining non-Markovian expressiveness, and the proxy-gap theorems (Theorem 5.4) provide a formal bridge between the inaccessible true-state entropy and the computable believed-state entropy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formal treatment of state entropy maximization under partial observability, a setting that is arguably the realistic one for robotics, autonomous navigation, and other embodied AI applications where sensors are noisy and incomplete. The hallucination problem—where an agent can inflate its perceived state entropy by making its own belief more uncertain—is a fundamental limitation of any belief-based proxy objective and has implications beyond this specific task. However, the empirical validation is confined to small discrete gridworlds (≤36 states), and the method requires an external belief approximator whose quality is assumed rather than learned end-to-end; scaling to continuous or high-dimensional POMDPs, integrating belief learning into the pipeline, and investigating richer policy classes remain open.
