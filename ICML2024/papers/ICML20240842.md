## Problem

The token generation process of self-attention is inherently non-Markovian (the next token depends on the entire input prompt) and its optimization landscape is nonconvex, making rigorous theoretical analysis of learnability, generalization, and failure modes difficult. This paper asks whether self-attention can be formally mapped to a well-understood probabilistic model, enabling the application of classical tools from Markov chain theory to study when and how attention layers can be learned from their own outputs.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a discrete token sequence X = (x_1, ..., x_L) from a vocabulary of size K, which is embedded into R^{L×d} via a learned embedding matrix E. The last token x_L serves as the query, and the output is a probability vector π^X ∈ R^K over the vocabulary, from which the next token is sampled. For learning, the method operates on (prompt, next-token) pairs drawn either IID from a prompt distribution or autoregressively from a single trajectory, and recovers the attention weight matrix W ∈ R^{d×d} (equivalently, the base transition matrix P ∈ R^{K×K}).

## Method

The paper establishes an exact bijection between a 1-layer self-attention head (under the assumption that embeddings are linearly independent and the classifier satisfies CE^T = I_K) and a Context-Conditioned Markov Chain (CCMC), where a base Markov transition matrix P is reweighted by the empirical token-frequency vector m(X) of the input prompt. This equivalence transforms the nonconvex attention optimization into a convex log-likelihood problem over a (K−1)×K subspace of the weight matrix. Consistency of estimation is then characterized by connectivity of per-query co-occurrence graphs over the prompt support, yielding a fast O(K²/n) sample complexity rate for IID data. For single-trajectory learning, the paper proves a distribution collapse phenomenon: the self-reinforcing frequency weighting causes the generated token distribution to concentrate on one or a few 'winner' tokens, with the weak token's frequency decaying as t^{−q} (q = 1−p/(1−p)), providing a formal explanation for repetitive text generation in LLMs.

## Results

The paper establishes a sample complexity guarantee of O(K² log(n/(Kδ))/n) for learning a 1-layer self-attention model from n IID (prompt, next-token) pairs (Corollary 4.3), where K is the vocabulary size. Consistency of estimation is characterized by a necessary and sufficient condition: the co-occurrence graph G^(k) must be connected for every query token k ∈ [K] (Theorem 3.4). For single-trajectory learning, the paper proves a distribution collapse phenomenon where the frequency of the weak token decays as t^(-q) with q = 1 - p/(1-p) (Lemma 5.3), and identifies a phase transition at p = 1/3: below this threshold, weak→weak transitions occur only finitely many times, making consistent estimation impossible.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper that does not compare against empirical baselines or benchmark other methods on standard tasks. The closest comparative context is the prior work by Li et al. (2024, AISTATS), which established the convexity of the log-likelihood loss for 1-layer self-attention; the present paper builds directly on this convexity to derive consistency conditions, finite-sample rates, and single-trajectory analysis. The paper also positions itself against Makkuva et al. (2024), who study learning a standard Markov chain via 1-layer self-attention, whereas this work shows self-attention is inherently non-Markovian and maps it to the more general CCMC framework. Empirically, the paper provides illustrative experiments (Figures 4–5) demonstrating distribution collapse and the p = 1/3 phase transition in weak→weak transition counts, but these are qualitative validations of the theory rather than quantitative comparisons against competing methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the convexity result for next-token prediction with self-attention from Li et al. (2024), which enables tractable optimization under maximum likelihood. It departs from prior attention-theory work (Yun et al. 2020, Edelman et al. 2022, Tarzanagh et al. 2023a) that studied expressive power or optimization for classification, by instead formalizing the generative dynamics of a 1-layer attention head as a Context-Conditioned Markov Chain (CCMC) — a non-Markovian process parameterized by a base transition matrix and context-dependent frequency weighting. A concurrent work (Makkuva et al. 2024) also connects attention to Markov chains but targets learning a standard (Markovian) chain, whereas this paper shows the equivalence requires the non-Markovian CCMC structure, making the connection strictly more general.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CCMC framework provides the first rigorous mathematical explanation for why self-attention models inevitably produce repetitive text (distribution collapse), with a precise threshold (p=1/3) separating finite from infinite weak-token transitions in the single-trajectory setting. However, the analysis relies on a strong Assumption 2.3 (orthogonal output embeddings, d≥K, linearly independent token embeddings) that is far from realistic for modern LLMs, and the results are limited to a single attention head without positional encoding in the main body. Key open questions include whether the CCMC connection and consistency conditions extend to multi-layer transformers, non-orthogonal classifiers, and the role of EOS tokens in terminating generation.
