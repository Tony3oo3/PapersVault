## Problem

Existing LoRA-finetuning quantization methods (e.g., QLoRA, QA-LoRA) suffer from significant information loss during low-bit quantization that LoRA cannot effectively recover, causing quantized LLMs to severely degrade or even fail to benefit from finetuning—particularly at ultra-low bit-widths (≤3-bit) and large model scales (≥30B). The paper identifies that the root cause is the information-theoretic degradation of quantized weights, which is typically attributed only to numerical quantization error rather than the loss of representational capacity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weights in FP16 (LLaMA/LLaMA2 families, 7B–65B) and a small calibration set to determine per-block quantization parameters, then finetunes on instruction datasets (Alpaca 52K or Flan v2 1,836 tasks). The output consists of low-bit (2–4 bit) NormalFloat or integer quantized weights with per-block scaling factors and a calibration constant τ (both double-quantized to FP8/FP16), plus LoRA adapter matrices (rank r=64) with two additional learnable scalars β₁ and β₂ per layer. The IEC parameters can be merged into the LoRA matrices at inference time, incurring zero additional latency.

## Method

IR-QLoRA introduces two complementary techniques under a unified information-retention perspective. First, Information Calibration Quantization (ICQ) adds a per-block calibration constant τ to the quantizer (w−τ)/s and optimizes it via search-based entropy maximization: τ is initialized at the block median, then searched over [τ₀−0.1σ, τ₀+0.1σ] with 200 candidates to maximize the Shannon entropy of the quantized weight distribution, thereby preserving more mutual information between quantized and original weights. Second, Information Elastic Connection (IEC) augments both LoRA sub-units with parameter-free elastic operations: U₁ groups the h-dimensional input into r segments via gcd(h,r), averages each group, and adds the result to xℓ₁; U₂ repeatedly concatenates the r-dimensional intermediate x' o/r times and adds it to x'ℓ₂. This allows each LoRA matrix to directly access the original input representation rather than only the transformed one, diversifying the information transformation while adding only two learnable scalars per layer.

## Results

On the MMLU benchmark (5-shot, Alpaca finetuning, 4-bit), IR-QLoRA achieves 40.8% average accuracy on LLaMA-7B, surpassing the SOTA QA-LoRA (39.4%) by 1.4% and the QLoRA baseline (38.4%) by 2.4%. Across the full LLaMA family (7B–65B), IR-QLoRA consistently outperforms all baselines, with gains of 1.3% (13B) and 0.5% (30B) over QLoRA. Under ultra-low 2-bit quantization with Flan v2 finetuning, IR-QLoRA retains 33.7% accuracy on LLaMA-7B (vs. 33.5% for QLoRA), narrowing the gap to the 16-bit model (34.6%) to just 0.9%. The accuracy gains require only 0.31% additional training time on LLaMA-13B and 2.04% extra storage on LLaMA-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IR-QLoRA outperforms QLoRA (NormalFloat) across all LLaMA sizes on MMLU: +2.4% on 7B (40.8 vs. 38.4), +1.3% on 13B (49.3 vs. 48.0), +0.5% on 30B (58.2 vs. 57.7), and matching 65B (63.1 vs. 63.1). Against the SOTA QA-LoRA (integer quantization), IR-QLoRA achieves a clear 1.4% gain on LLaMA-7B (40.8 vs. 39.4) and a 0.6% gain on LLaMA-65B (63.1 vs. 62.5), with near-ties on 13B and 30B. Compared to QLoRA w/GPTQ, IR-QLoRA leads by 4.8% on 7B and 0.1–1.1% on larger models. On the CommonsenseQA suite (0-shot, 4-bit LLaMA-7B), IR-QLoRA achieves 63.7% average, beating QLoRA (62.0%) and QA-LoRA (61.8%). Under 2-bit quantization (Flan v2), IR-QLoRA maintains 33.7% on LLaMA-7B versus 33.5% (QLoRA) and 33.2% (QA-LoRA), while the 16-bit reference is 34.6%, demonstrating the method's robustness at extreme compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the QLoRA paradigm (Dettmers et al., 2023), which established the pipeline of NormalFloat post-training quantization followed by LoRA fine-tuning, by reframing quantization through an information-theoretic lens rather than the conventional numerical-error-minimization perspective. ICQ introduces a per-block calibration constant τ optimized via entropy maximization of quantized weights, departing from the fixed absmax scaling used in QLoRA and GPTQ. IEC augments the LoRA architecture (Hu et al., 2021) with parameter-free elastic connections that allow both sub-matrices to access original input features, addressing what the authors identify as a representational bottleneck in standard low-rank adaptation. The method is positioned as a drop-in enhancement compatible with both NormalFloat and integer quantization frameworks, as demonstrated by integration with QA-LoRA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: quantized LLMs with LoRA still degrade substantially at ultra-low bit-widths (2–3 bit) and large scales (≥30B), where information loss compounds nonlinearly. The information-retention framing provides a principled justification for the calibration strategy, and the near-zero overhead (0.31% additional time, 2.04% storage) makes the technique immediately deployable. However, the absolute gains are modest (1–2% on MMLU over strong baselines like QA-LoRA), and the entropy-maximization objective is a proxy that may not always align with downstream task accuracy. Open questions include whether the information-theoretic perspective yields further gains at even lower bit-widths (1-bit), whether the approach transfers to newer architectures with different weight distributions, and whether the entropy objective can be replaced by task-aware signals for larger improvements.
