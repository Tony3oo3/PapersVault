## Problem

Sequential importance resampling (SIR) particle filters suffer from weight degeneracy in high-dimensional or degenerate-likelihood settings, where most particle weights collapse to zero and only a few modes of the posterior are captured. While coupling/transport-based alternatives exist, they either require solving PDEs for vector fields (particle flow), linear programs that scale poorly with particle count (ensemble transform), or target different rearrangements (Knothe-Rosenblatt). This paper formulates the Bayesian conditioning step as a Brenier optimal transport problem solvable via neural networks in a fully likelihood-free, simulation-based setting.

## Input / Output

At each time step, the method consumes a set of N prior state particles (drawn from the propagation step), a single noisy observation Y_t, and samples from the likelihood model (simulated as Y_t^i ~ h(·|X_{t|t-1}^i)); it does not require the analytical form of the likelihood. A random permutation of the prior particles serves as the independent copy X̄, making the procedure self-contained within the particle ensemble. The output is a set of N uniformly weighted posterior particles obtained by applying the learned block-triangular transport map T(·, Y_t) to the prior particles, eliminating the need for resampling.

## Method

The conditioning step is cast as a conditional Monge problem: find a block-triangular map T(x,y)→(T(x,y), y) that pushes the independent coupling P_X ⊗ P_Y to the joint P_XY, minimizing the quadratic transport cost. Via Kantorovich duality, this becomes a max-min stochastic optimization over a c-concave potential f and the transport map T, both parameterized as residual neural networks (with an optional EnKF linear layer in T that ensures graceful degradation to the ensemble Kalman filter when no training is performed). The objective is approximated empirically over particle samples and trained with gradient ascent-descent (Adam optimizer), where T is updated to minimize the transport cost minus the potential, and f is updated to maximize the dual gap. The key theoretical contribution is a consistency result (Prop. 2.3) guaranteeing the unique OT map recovers the exact posterior, and an error bound (Prop. 2.4) showing the approximation error scales as O(√(optimality gap/α)) rather than exponentially with dimension as in SIR.

## Results

The OT method captures bimodal and multimodal posteriors that SIR particle filters miss due to weight degeneracy, even in low-dimensional settings (n=2, λ_w=0.04). On the dynamic bimodal example, OT and EnKF scale better with dimension than SIR (Fig. 4c), while EnKF's error plateaus with increasing particle count due to its Gaussian bias, unlike OT and SIR which continue to improve (Fig. 4d). On the 100-dimensional MNIST in-painting task, OT produces a realistic multimodal posterior concentrated around digits {0,2,3,8}, whereas SIR is overconfident on digit 2 and EnKF on digit 0. The raw computational cost of OT is substantially higher (e.g., 34.5s vs. 0.01s for the bimodal dynamic example; 530s vs. 0.4s for static MNIST), though the authors note this can be reduced via offline warm-start training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SIR particle filters, OT clearly wins on the ability to capture multi-modal posteriors: in the bimodal static example with small noise (λ_w=0.04), SIR collapses all particles into a single mode while OT retains both modes; in the dynamic bimodal example, SIR misses a mode during t∈[1,2]∪[3,4.5]; and on MNIST in-painting, SIR is overconfident on a single digit. OT also scales better with dimension than SIR (Fig. 4c), consistent with the theoretical result that SIR's error grows as γ^n/√N. Against EnKF, OT wins on multi-modal and non-Gaussian problems (bimodal examples, MNIST) where EnKF's Gaussian approximation is fundamentally limited, and OT's error decreases with more particles while EnKF's remains constant. However, EnKF is faster and competitive on unimodal problems like Lorenz 96, where OT performs similarly. On Lorenz 63, both OT and EnKF converge faster to the true state than SIR, with EnKF admitting larger variance; adding an EnKF layer to OT's architecture improves initial-stage performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OT formulation of Bayes' law for nonlinear filtering introduced by Taghvaei & Hosseini (2022), which itself builds on the min-max OT map estimation framework of Makkuva et al. (2020) and the block-triangular conditional transport structure from Kovachki et al. (2023). The key departures are practical: replacing the ICNN architecture (which imposed convexity constraints that limited expressivity) with ResNets, adding an EnKF block so the method gracefully degrades to EnKF without training, and providing the first consistency, stability, and error-bound analysis for the conditional OT filtering formulation. It also supersedes the authors' own prior conference paper (Al-Jarrah et al., 2023) with substantially more theory and experiments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that OT-based filtering can capture multi-modal posteriors in settings where SIR suffers weight degeneracy and EnKF is structurally limited to Gaussian approximations, with the MNIST inpainting experiments showing realistic uncertainty quantification in 100-dimensional latent spaces. However, the raw computational cost is 300–3000× higher than EnKF/SIR, and the bias-variance decomposition of the optimality gap remains incomplete. Open questions include whether the convexity assumption in Prop. 2.4 can be relaxed to 'convexity on sets of large posterior measure' (motivated by the empirical near-monotonicity of the learned map), and whether offline pre-training can close the computational gap for online deployment.
