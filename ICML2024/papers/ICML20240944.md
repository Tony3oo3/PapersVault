## Problem

Existing directed graph embedding methods face three simultaneous gaps: dual-embedding approaches produce degenerate representations for nodes with low in- or out-degree, spectral GNN-based single-embedding methods lack inductive generalization to unseen nodes, and supervised training couples embeddings to specific downstream tasks. No prior method satisfies all three desiderata—robustness across degree distributions, inductive capability, and task agnosticism—within a spatial GNN framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a directed graph (unweighted, with optional node attributes as initial features) and produces a single d-dimensional complex-valued embedding per node, decomposed into a real amplitude vector (encoding connectivity) and a real phase vector (encoding directionality). Downstream, these complex embeddings support four link-prediction subtasks (existence, direction, three-type, four-type classification) via a parameter-free decoder, as well as transductive and inductive node classification via a simple MLP head on concatenated amplitude-phase vectors.

## Method

DUPLEX represents each node as a complex embedding x_u = a_u ⊙ exp(iπθ_u/2) whose source and target roles are complex conjugates rather than independent dual vectors, enabling joint optimization from both in- and out-neighborhoods. A dual GAT encoder processes amplitude and phase separately: the amplitude encoder uses a standard undirected attention aggregator, while the phase encoder uses a novel directed aggregator that subtracts out-neighbor contributions from in-neighbor contributions to encode edge orientation. A mid-fusion layer allows controlled information exchange between the two streams via attention-weighted aggregation. Training is self-supervised through two parameter-free decoders that reconstruct the Hermitian Adjacency Matrix: a direction-aware decoder assigns softmax probabilities over the four HAM values {i, −i, 1, 0} using L1 distance, and a connection-aware decoder predicts edge existence via sigmoid on amplitude inner products, with a decaying weight λ to balance the two losses.

## Results

On link prediction, DUPLEX achieves 98.6% AUC on Citeseer (Existence Prediction) and 99.7% AUC on Citeseer (Direction Prediction), outperforming the strongest baseline SigMaNet (91.4% and 98.3% AUC respectively). On the more challenging Four-type Classification subtask, DUPLEX reaches 91.1% ACC on Citeseer and 98.1% on Twitter, surpassing SigMaNet by 14.9% and 0.9% respectively. For inductive node classification, DUPLEX attains 71.7% macro F1 on Citeseer and 85.9% on Cora-ml, exceeding the best inductive directed-graph baseline dir-GAT (70.4% and 85.0%) and all transductive methods (MagNet 65.0%, SigMaNet 63.1% on Citeseer).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DUPLEX outperforms all seven baselines on the primary link prediction task. Against the dual-embedding methods (NERD, DGGAN, DiGAE, ODIN), DUPLEX gains 7.2–18.2 percentage points in AUC on Existence Prediction, with the largest margins on smaller datasets (e.g., +18.2% over NERD on Citeseer EP). Against the single-embedding spectral methods (MagNet, SigMaNet), DUPLEX leads by 7.2–10.3% AUC on EP and 1.4–3.5% on DP, with the gap widening on the harder Three-type and Four-type Classification subtasks (e.g., +14.9% ACC over SigMaNet on Citeseer FP). On transductive node classification, DUPLEX beats NERD by 4.0% macro F1 on Citeseer but trails by 0.9% on Cora-ml, while it substantially outperforms DGGAN (+33.9%) and ODIN (+35.3%). On inductive node classification, DUPLEX surpasses dir-GAT (the strongest inductive directed-graph baseline) by 1.3% macro F1 on Citeseer and 0.9% on Cora-ml, and exceeds the transductive MagNet/SigMaNet by 6.7% and 22.8% macro F1 on Citeseer respectively, confirming its inductive advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

DUPLEX extends the MagNet/SigMaNet line of work, which applies Hermitian (magnetic) matrix structures to directed graph embedding, by shifting from spectral GNNs to spatial GNNs and from supervised to self-supervised training. It departs from the dominant dual-embedding paradigm (NERD, DGGAN, DiGAE, ODIN) by representing source and target roles as complex conjugates of a single embedding, thereby enabling joint optimization over both in- and out-neighborhoods. The dual GAT encoder with separate amplitude (undirected aggregator) and phase (directed aggregator) components is a novel architectural contribution, while the parameter-free decoders for reconstructing the Hermitian adjacency matrix draw on the classification-style loss design from prior link prediction work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DUPLEX addresses a concrete and well-motivated failure mode of dual-embedding methods—degraded representations for nodes with low in- or out-degree—through a clean SVD-based analysis and a complex-conjugate embedding design that provably avoids the zero-embedding collapse. The inductive, task-agnostic nature of the framework makes it practically useful for dynamic directed networks where new nodes appear and multiple downstream tasks must be served without retraining. Open questions include scalability beyond ~500K nodes, extension to weighted or multi-relational directed graphs, and whether the HAM-based formulation generalizes to other non-symmetric structures (e.g., hypergraphs with direction).
