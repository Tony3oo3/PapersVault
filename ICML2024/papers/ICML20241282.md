## Problem

Existing statistical methods for high-dimensional functional time series (e.g., functional VAR, PCA-based approaches) assume linear and Markovian temporal dynamics, which fail to capture the nonlinear and non-Markovian dependence prevalent in real-world data. Meanwhile, applying deep neural networks directly to such data yields black-box models that sacrifice the explainability needed in domains like finance, healthcare, and climate science. The paper seeks a framework that is simultaneously expressive enough for complex temporal dynamics and interpretable enough to reveal cross-sectional and serial relationships.

## Input / Output

The model consumes p-dimensional functional time series Y_t(·) observed at discrete grid points u_1,…,u_L on a compact interval U, where p (number of curves) is comparable to or exceeds n (number of time periods, typically <50 in the experiments). It produces h-step-ahead predictions of the full functional curve at each grid point, along with interpretable latent functional factors and a temporal covariance matrix Σ_X that encodes the learned non-Markovian dependence structure. Prediction is performed via a rolling-window protocol: the model is refit after each one-period shift of the training window.

## Method

DF²M factorizes the observed curves through an IBP-prior sparse loading matrix (Z⊙A) into a potentially infinite set of latent functional factors, then models the temporal evolution of each factor via a multi-task Gaussian process whose covariance is the product of a spatial kernel κ_U and a deep temporal kernel κ_X. The temporal kernel is constructed by mapping historical factor curves to finite-dimensional vectors via a function F, passing them through a unidirectional sequential deep network H (LSTM, GRU, or self-attention), and applying a standard kernel (e.g., squared exponential) to the resulting representations. This separable kernel structure, combined with sparse variational inference using shared inducing points across factors, yields a closed-form ELBO. A key computational result (Theorem 3) shows that sampling factors from a simplified proxy distribution depending only on same-period inducing variables leaves the ELBO unchanged up to a constant, avoiding expensive nL×nL matrix decompositions during ADVI training.

## Results

DF2M consistently outperforms its standard deep learning counterparts on both MSPE and MAPE across all four datasets. The most dramatic gains appear in the attention-based variant: DF2M-ATTN achieves MSPE 3.608 on Japanese Mortality (vs. 13.44 for standalone ATTN, a 73% reduction) and MSPE 14.22 on Global Mortality (vs. 39.52 for ATTN, a 64% reduction). DF2M-LSTM excels on Energy Consumption (MSPE 8.928 vs. 13.51 for LSTM) and Global Mortality (MSPE 7.672 vs. 13.28 for LSTM). The sole exception is Stock Intraday, where DF2M-ATTN (MSPE 104.2) and ATTN (MSPE 103.4) are essentially tied, and DF2M-LIN (MSPE 99.58) outperforms the more complex DF2M-LSTM (107.5) and DF2M-GRU (102.5), suggesting financial data is better captured by Markovian dynamics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four DF2M variants (LIN, LSTM, GRU, ATTN) against their standalone deep learning counterparts under identical architecture settings (hidden size 15, single layer, one attention head). DF2M-ATTN dominates on Japanese Mortality, reducing MSPE from 13.44 to 3.608 (73% improvement) and MAPE from 3.166 to 1.119. DF2M-LSTM wins on Energy Consumption (MSPE 8.928 vs. 13.51) and Global Mortality (MSPE 7.672 vs. 13.28), while DF2M-GRU shows the largest relative gain on Stock Intraday (MSPE 102.5 vs. 414.0 for GRU, a 75% reduction). The only near-tie is Stock Intraday with the attention variant, where ATTN (103.4) marginally beats DF2M-ATTN (104.2) on MSPE, though DF2M-ATTN still wins on MAPE (6.695 vs. 6.579 is actually a loss for DF2M-ATTN here). Notably, on Stock Intraday the simpler DF2M-LIN outperforms both DF2M-LSTM and DF2M-GRU, indicating that the non-Markovian deep kernels add unnecessary complexity for short-term financial dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

DF2M sits at the intersection of three research threads: Bayesian nonparametric factor models using the Indian Buffet Process for column sparsity (Griffiths & Ghahramani, 2011; Guo et al., 2021), multi-task Gaussian process dynamical models for temporal dependence (Wang et al., 2005; Bonilla et al., 2007), and deep kernel learning (Wilson et al., 2016). The key departure from prior statistical methods for high-dimensional functional time series (Gao et al., 2019; Chang et al., 2023a; Guo & Qiao, 2023) is the explicit relaxation of the linear-Markovian assumption by embedding sequential deep networks (LSTM, GRU, attention) within the temporal kernel of a GP dynamical model. Unlike black-box deep learning approaches, DF2M retains the interpretable factor structure of a sparse loading matrix, positioning itself as an explainable bridge between classical functional time series methodology and modern sequential deep learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: prior statistical methods for high-dimensional functional time series are restricted to linear Markovian dynamics, while deep learning lacks the explainability needed in domains like finance, healthcare, and climate science. The consistent outperformance over single-layer deep baselines across four diverse datasets, combined with interpretable latent factors and temporal covariance visualizations, makes the framework practically useful for applied forecasting. A stated limitation is the reliance on simple spatial kernels that neglect intricate within-curve relationships, and the evaluation is confined to small time horizons (n < 50), which is standard in the functional time series literature but limits claims about long-horizon scalability. Open questions include whether the deep kernel component remains beneficial as the number of time steps grows and whether more expressive spatial kernels (e.g., graph-based or adaptive) can further improve performance.
