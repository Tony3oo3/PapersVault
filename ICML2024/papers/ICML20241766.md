## Problem

Modern Hopfield networks based on softmax (Ramsauer et al., 2021) cannot achieve exact pattern retrieval and may converge to metastable states that mix multiple patterns, while the sparse variant of Hu et al. (2023) still only guarantees approximate retrieval. No unified framework existed that simultaneously provided provable exact retrieval, exponential storage capacity, and the ability to retrieve structured pattern associations (e.g., k-subsets) rather than single patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241766/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a continuous query vector q ∈ R^D and a matrix of N memory pattern embeddings X ∈ R^{N×D}, optionally augmented with a factor graph structure (variable nodes for patterns, factor nodes for pairwise/higher-order interactions) and a budget parameter k. The update rule produces a sparse weight vector ŷ_Ω(βXq) over patterns (with exact zeros outside the support) and a new state q' = X^T ŷ, which in the sparse case converges to a single stored pattern and in the structured case converges to a convex combination X^T y representing a k-subset or sequential k-subset of patterns. In downstream tasks this yields a bag-level classification score (MIL) or a binary word mask plus a sentiment prediction (text rationalization).

## Method

The paper defines a Hopfield-Fenchel-Young (HFY) energy E(q) = -β^{-1} L_Ω(βXq; 1/N) + ½‖q - μ_X‖² + const, where L_Ω is a Fenchel-Young loss induced by a generalized negentropy Ω (Tsallis α-negentropy for α-entmax, norm α-negentropy for α-normmax). Minimizing this energy via the concave-convex procedure (CCCP) yields the update rule q^{(t+1)} = X^T ŷ_Ω(βXq^{(t)}), where ŷ_Ω is the Ω-regularized argmax transformation. The key theoretical insight is that the margin property of Fenchel-Young losses (m = (α-1)^{-1} for Tsallis, m = 1 for norm) directly translates into sufficient separation conditions Δ_i ≥ mβ^{-1} for exact one-step retrieval, preserving exponential storage capacity O((2/√3)^D). For structured retrieval, the framework extends to SparseMAP over a marginal polytope conv(Y) defined by a factor graph, where the structured margin (m ≤ 1) guarantees exact convergence to pattern associations X^T y_i in a single iteration under analogous separation conditions.

## Results

On MNIST K-MIL, the proposed SparseMAP k-subsets pooling achieves the best accuracy when k matches K (e.g., 96.5% for K=3, 95.1% for K=5), outperforming softmax (91.1%, 89.0%) and sparsemax (92.9%, 91.6%). On the MIL benchmarks, SparseMAP k=3 achieves the highest ROC AUC on Fox (67.4) and SparseMAP k=2 on Elephant (93.2). For text rationalization, the sequential k-subsets model achieves the best human rationale overlap (HRO F1=0.63 on BeerAdvocate) while matching or slightly exceeding SPECTRA on downstream F1 (e.g., 0.93 on AgNews vs. 0.92).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Ramsauer et al. (2021) (1-entmax/softmax), the sparse variants generally match or outperform, with the notable exception that softmax surprisingly wins on MNIST K=1 (98.4 vs. 97.9–98.2); for K≥2, SparseMAP k-subsets with k=K consistently leads (e.g., 96.5 vs. 91.1 for K=3). Against Hu et al. (2023) (2-entmax/sparsemax), the normmax and SparseMAP variants achieve higher accuracy for K≥3 (e.g., 95.8–96.5 vs. 92.9 for K=3) and higher ROC AUC on Fox (67.4 vs. 66.1). In text rationalization, the proposed SparseMAP k-subsets and sequential k-subsets models outperform HardKuma (Bastings et al., 2019) on all four datasets (e.g., 0.93 vs. 0.90 F1 on AgNews) and SFE (Lei et al., 2016) by large margins (e.g., 0.93 vs. 0.92 on AgNews, 0.90 vs. 0.84 on IMDB). Compared to SPECTRA (Guerreiro & Martins, 2021), the proposed methods match or slightly exceed downstream F1 (0.93 vs. 0.92 on AgNews) and the sequential variant achieves the best human rationale overlap (HRO 0.63 vs. 0.61), attributed to its contiguity bias. Against Sparse Attention (Peters et al., 2019), the proposed methods achieve equal or better F1 on SST (0.81 vs. 0.82 for sparsemax) and AgNews (0.93 vs. 0.93) while substantially improving HRO (0.63 vs. 0.48).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the line of modern Hopfield networks (Ramsauer et al., 2021; Hu et al., 2023) under a single energy framework derived from Fenchel-Young losses (Blondel et al., 2020), showing that the softmax-based energy of Ramsauer et al. and the sparsemax-based energy of Hu et al. are special cases of a broader family parametrized by generalized negentropies (Tsallis α-negentropy, norm α-negentropy). The key conceptual departure is leveraging the margin property of Fenchel-Young losses to prove exact (zero-error) retrieval, going beyond the approximate retrieval guarantees of both prior works. The structured extension via SparseMAP (Niculae et al., 2018) generalizes retrieval from single patterns to pattern associations (k-subsets, sequential k-subsets), building on the factor graph formalism for structured prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exact retrieval guarantee with exponential storage capacity is a clean theoretical advance that resolves a limitation of prior modern Hopfield networks, where softmax-based updates could only converge to metastable states mixing multiple patterns. The structured extension opens a path toward Hopfield layers that retrieve relational or sequential pattern associations, with practical applications demonstrated in MIL and text rationalization. However, the empirical gains over strong baselines are modest (1-3% in most settings), and the structured case requires a MAP oracle whose cost scales with the combinatorial structure, limiting scalability to very large or complex factor graphs. Open questions include whether the well-separation conditions for exact retrieval hold naturally in learned embedding spaces and whether the framework extends to continuous (non-binary) structured outputs.
