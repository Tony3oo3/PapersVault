## Problem

Neural-network-based PDE solvers for initial value problems struggle to maintain high accuracy over time due to cumulative error propagation. Existing sequential-in-time methods each have fundamental limitations: TDVP's single tangent-space projection becomes inaccurate for nonzero time steps and is not reparameterization invariant, while OBTI faces a non-convex optimization landscape in parameter space that requires hundreds of iterations per step. No prior method achieves machine-precision per-step optimization while remaining efficient.

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial condition u₀(x) on a spatial domain (e.g., [0,2π)² or [0,2π)³), a PDE operator L, boundary conditions (periodic, or Dirichlet enforced via an additional loss term), and a time step size Δt. It produces a sequence of neural network parameter vectors θ_t that define the solution u(x,t) at each time step, with the network using periodic embeddings (concatenated cosine features) to natively satisfy periodic boundary conditions. The per-step optimization loss (squared L² distance in u-space) is driven to machine precision (~10⁻¹⁴) within a handful of iterations, and the L² norm is evaluated on a grid of ~1,000,000 points.

## Method

TENG generalizes TDVP and OBTI by solving the OBTI target-matching problem (minimizing L² distance between the network output and a time-integrated target in u-space) via repeated tangent-space projections within each physical time step. Each iteration computes the u-space gradient of the loss, projects it into parameter space by solving a least-squares system with the Jacobian (equivalently, a Hilbert natural gradient or Gauss-Newton step), and updates θ. This repeated projection makes the optimal solution reparameterization invariant even at nonzero Δt, and the least-squares solve can be performed approximately (with random parameter sub-sampling) without compromising final accuracy. Higher-order integration (e.g., TENG-Heun) is incorporated by constructing intermediate targets using the PDE operator applied to intermediate network outputs, avoiding the high-order derivatives of L that plague prior OBTI variants.

## Results

TENG-Heun achieves the lowest global relative L²-error across all four PDE benchmarks: 1.588×10⁻⁶ on 2D heat, 1.139×10⁻⁵ on 3D heat, 6.187×10⁻⁶ on Allen-Cahn, and 2.643×10⁻⁶ on Burgers' equation. TENG-Euler alone (3.006×10⁻⁴ on 2D heat) already matches or exceeds TDVP-RK4 (3.279×10⁻⁴), while TENG-Heun improves by 2–3 additional orders of magnitude over all baselines. Per-step optimization loss reaches machine precision O(10⁻¹⁴) within ~7 iterations, versus O(10⁻⁷) for OBTI after 300 iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TDVP-RK4 (Berman & Peherstorfer, NeurIPS 2023), TENG-Euler is comparable on 2D heat (3.006×10⁻⁴ vs. 3.279×10⁻⁴) but TENG-Heun outperforms it by 200× on 2D heat, 340× on 3D heat, 200× on Allen-Cahn, and 920× on Burgers'. Against OBTI (Chen et al., ICML 2023), TENG-Euler already beats both OBTI-Adam and OBTI-LBFGS by 1–2 orders of magnitude on every PDE, and TENG-Heun extends the gap to 3–4 orders of magnitude (e.g., 2.643×10⁻⁶ vs. 1.696×10⁻¹ for OBTI-Adam on Burgers'). Against PINN-ENGD (Müller & Zeinhofer, ICML 2023), TENG-Heun is 9× better on 2D heat (1.588×10⁻⁶ vs. 1.403×10⁻⁵) and 250× better on 3D heat (1.139×10⁻⁵ vs. 2.846×10⁻³). Against PINN-BFGS (Raissi et al., 2019), TENG-Heun wins on all four PDEs, with the largest margin on Burgers' (2.643×10⁻⁶ vs. 6.538×10⁻², a factor of ~25,000). Runtime is comparable: TENG-Heun takes 4.1–20.9 hours versus 4.4–22.1 hours for TDVP-RK4 and OBTI-LBFGS on the same hardware (single V100 GPU).

## Relation to Prior Work

TENG unifies two previously separate lines of work on sequential-in-time PDE solving with neural networks: the Time-Dependent Variational Principle (TDVP, rooted in the Dirac-Frenkel variational principle) and Optimization-Based Time Integration (OBTI). The paper's key conceptual observation is that TDVP is equivalent to solving the OBTI loss function with a single tangent-space projection, and TENG generalizes both by performing repeated natural-gradient (tangent-space) projections within each time step. This places TENG in the lineage of Amari's natural gradient and the Hilbert Gram matrix formulation, while also connecting to the Gauss-Newton method through an equivalent reformulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TENG achieves per-step optimization losses at machine precision (O(10⁻¹⁴)) with only ~7 iterations, compared to O(10⁻⁷) for OBTI after hundreds of iterations, and yields global errors orders of magnitude below TDVP, OBTI, and PINN across multiple PDEs. The reparameterization-invariance theorem (Theorem 4.1) provides a theoretical guarantee absent in TDVP, and the straightforward incorporation of higher-order integrators (TENG-Heun) avoids the high-order differentiation that plagues prior OBTI variants. Limitations include restriction to scalar-valued PDEs with periodic boundary conditions in 2D/3D; open questions include extension to vector-valued systems, non-periodic boundaries, and broader PDE classes (nonlinear, multiscale).
