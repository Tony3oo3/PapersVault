## Problem

It remains unclear whether gradient descent training of a shallow (two-layer) autoencoder captures the structural properties of the data it compresses, or whether it behaves identically to compressing unstructured Gaussian data. For the prototypical 1-bit compressed sensing setting with sparse Gaussian inputs, no prior theory characterized how sparsity affects the learned representation or the achievable MSE, leaving open whether the architecture's expressivity (decoder nonlinearity, depth) provides a provable advantage for structured data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a d-dimensional data vector x with i.i.d. components drawn from a sparse distribution (sparse Gaussian SG_d(p), sparse Rademacher, sparse Beta, sparse Gaussian mixture, or masked/whitened CIFAR-10/MNIST image pixels) and produces a reconstructed estimate x̂ ∈ R^d. The encoder applies a sign activation (1-bit quantization) to Bx, yielding a latent code z ∈ R^n with compression rate r = n/d ≤ 1. The output is the mean-squared error R(Θ) = d^{-1} E‖x − x̂‖², and the paper characterizes both the MSE value and the structure of the optimal weight matrices (A, B) as functions of the sparsity level p.

## Method

The paper proves (Theorem 4.1) that a two-layer autoencoder with a linear decoder trained via Riemannian gradient descent on sparse Gaussian data follows the exact same trajectory as on pure Gaussian data, yielding the Gaussian MSE R_Gauss = 1 − 2r/π regardless of sparsity. It then identifies a phase transition: for general i.i.d. data, SGD converges to either a Haar-rotation minimizer (MSE = R_Gauss) or an identity/permutation minimizer (MSE = 1 − r·(E|x₁|)²), with the critical sparsity p_crit determined by comparing E|x₁| to √(2/π). To provably beat Gaussian performance, the paper adds a component-wise nonlinear denoising function f (of the form α₁x + α₂·tanh(α₃x)) to the decoder, whose optimal choice f*(y) = E[x₁ | μx₁ + σg = y] is characterized via the first iterate of the RI-GAMP algorithm and state evolution. Finally, a three-layer decoder architecture with residual connections, directly mapped from the RI-GAMP fixed-point iteration, further reduces MSE and approaches the Bayes-optimal bound for rotationally invariant encoders.

## Results

The paper's headline theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) shows that for sparse Gaussian data x ~ SG_d(p), the two-layer autoencoder with a linear decoder (equation 2) trained via gradient descent achieves MSE = R_Gauss = 1 − 2r/π, identical to the compression of purely Gaussian data — completely disregarding sparsity. Adding a component-wise nonlinearity f to the decoder (equation 4) provably reduces the MSE below R_Gauss for all p ∈ (0,1) (Proposition 5.1), with the optimal f being the posterior mean. A three-layer decoder inspired by RI-GAMP (equation 23) further reduces the MSE and approaches the Bayes-optimal bound (Figure 8). On CIFAR-10 (p=0.4 masking), the nonlinear decoder achieves lower MSE than the linear one, and both match their respective theoretical predictions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Gaussian-data baseline of Shevchenko et al. (2023), which gives R_Gauss = 1 − 2r/π, the paper proves that the linear decoder (2) is provably stuck at this value for sparse Gaussian data (Theorem 4.1) — it cannot exploit sparsity at all. The nonlinear decoder (4) with optimal f strictly improves upon R_Gauss for every sparsity level p ∈ (0,1), as shown in Figure 4 (blue curve below the orange dashed line) and confirmed on CIFAR-10 (Figure 7, green dots below blue dots). The multi-layer decoder (23) further outperforms both the linear and single-nonlinearity decoders, approaching the Bayes-optimal MSE from Ma et al. (2021) (orange curve in Figure 8), which serves as the information-theoretic lower bound for a fixed rotationally-invariant encoder. On CIFAR-10 with p=0.4 masking, the linear decoder's loss matches R_Gauss exactly (Figure 3), while the nonlinear decoder captures the sparsity structure and achieves a lower loss consistent with the theoretical prediction of Proposition 5.1 (Figure 7). The paper also notes that for unstructured Gaussian data (p=1), multiple decoding layers do not help (per Shevchenko et al. 2023), making the benefit of depth specific to structured/sparse data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Gaussian-data analysis of Shevchenko et al. (2023), who showed that gradient descent on a two-layer autoencoder with sign activation achieves the MSE minimizer for unstructured data, to the setting of sparse/structured data. The theoretical machinery for characterizing the MSE of nonlinear decoders is borrowed from the RI-GAMP algorithm of Venkataramanan et al. (2022), whose iterates map directly onto multi-layer autoencoder architectures. The specific denoising nonlinearity f(x) = α₁x + α₂·tanh(α₃x) is adapted from the ResNet-like denoising architecture of Cui & Zdeborova (2023). The key conceptual departure from prior work is showing that the linear decoder is provably blind to sparsity, while the RI-GAMP-inspired multi-layer decoder provably captures it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a linear decoder achieves exactly the same MSE as for Gaussian data—completely ignoring sparsity—is a fundamental limitation that clarifies why more expressive decoders are necessary for structured data compression. The phase transition phenomenon (random rotation below critical sparsity, identity above) and the associated staircase dynamics in SGD provide a concrete, testable prediction that has been validated on CIFAR-10 and MNIST. Open questions include characterizing the timescale for SGD to escape the plateau at the Gaussian-level loss, and whether the RI-GAMP-inspired architecture generalizes beyond the specific 1-bit sign-activation setting studied here. The contrast with the Gaussian case (where depth provably does not help) sharpens the understanding of when architectural expressivity matters.
