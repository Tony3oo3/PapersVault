## Problem

Finding optimal Lagrangian multipliers for MILPs requires iterative descent methods (subgradient, bundle) whose iteration count grows rapidly with the number of relaxed constraints, making the approach computationally prohibitive for large-scale problems. This paper asks whether a single forward pass of a neural network can directly predict high-quality multipliers, effectively amortizing the per-instance optimization that iterative solvers perform.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240412/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a MILP instance encoded as a bipartite factor graph (one node per variable, one per constraint, edges where a variable appears in a constraint) whose node features are augmented with the continuous relaxation's primal values, dual values, and reduced costs. It outputs one scalar Lagrangian multiplier per dualized constraint, computed as the CR dual value plus a predicted non-negative deviation. Because the encoder is probabilistic (Gaussian per constraint), inference samples five multiplier vectors and retains the one yielding the best Lagrangian bound.

## Method

A graph neural network encoder processes the bipartite instance graph through stacked blocks of graph convolution and MLP with residual connections, layer normalization, and dropout, producing a high-dimensional embedding per dualized constraint. The final GNN layer parameterizes a Gaussian distribution over each constraint's latent vector, sampled via the reparameterization trick. A small MLP decoder maps each latent vector to a scalar deviation from the CR dual value, and the predicted multipliers are passed through a soft-plus to enforce non-negativity. Training is fully unsupervised: the network is optimized end-to-end to maximize the Lagrangian bound LR(π), whose subgradient with respect to π is the analytical residual b − A x̄ from the relaxed subproblem, enabling backpropagation through the decoder and the reparameterized encoder. The key design choice is predicting a deviation from the CR dual solution rather than absolute multipliers, which the ablation study confirms is essential for competitive performance.

## Results

On the four Multi-Commodity Network Design datasets, the proposed GNN encoder-decoder reduces the Lagrangian dual GAP from 12.99–22.29% (CR) to 2.09–4.77%, closing 77–85% of the gap to the optimal Lagrangian bound. On the two Generalized Assignment datasets, GAP drops from 1.91%/0.44% (CR) to 0.55%/0.15%, a 30–44% gap reduction over the second-best method LR(CR). When used to warm-start the SMS++ proximal bundle solver on MC-BIG-VAR, predicted multipliers halve resolution time at the coarsest tolerance (16.09 s vs. 34.34 s for zero-init) and remain ~40% faster at the finest tolerance (63.25 s vs. 105.03 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed GNN encoder-decoder outperforms all five baselines (CR, LR(0), LR(CR), LR(k-NN), LR(MLP)) on every one of the six datasets. The margin is largest on the MC datasets where CR is far from optimal: on MC-SML-40 the method achieves 2.09% GAP versus 10.70% for LR(MLP) and 12.97% for LR(CR), a 77–84% gap reduction. On GA-20-400, where CR is already tight (0.44%), the method still improves to 0.15%, beating LR(CR) (0.27%) by 44% in relative gap reduction. The k-NN baseline is consistently the worst ML approach (38–55% GAP on MC), confirming that instance-level feature extraction via GNN is essential. In the architecture ablation (Appendix H), the proposed block structure matches or slightly beats the Nair et al. (2020) architecture at 5+ layers (e.g., 2.29% vs. 2.47% at 5 layers on MC-SML-40) and substantially outperforms the Gasse et al. (2019) architecture, which is unstable without per-block layer normalization (2.59–3.16% vs. 1.56–2.29% across 5–10 layers).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MILP-to-bipartite-graph encoding paradigm established by Gasse et al. (2019) and the GNN block architecture (residual connections, layer normalization, dropout) validated by Nair et al. (2020), but repurposes these tools to predict constraint-level Lagrangian multipliers rather than variable assignments. It departs from prior Lagrangian prediction work (Nair et al. 2018 for stochastic MILPs, Kraul et al. 2023 for cutting stock, Sugishita et al. 2024 for unit commitment) by proposing a generic encoder-decoder framework applicable to any compact MILP with a given set of dualized constraints, trained end-to-end by directly maximizing the Lagrangian bound as an unsupervised energy-based objective. The reparameterization trick and probabilistic encoder connect the approach to variational inference and structured prediction literature, while the amortization framing links it to learning-to-optimize and unrolled iterative methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for operations research practitioners: closing up to 85% of the CR-to-LD gap in a single forward pass, and halving bundle-method resolution time via warm-starting, could substantially reduce computational costs for large-scale network design and assignment problems. Key limitations include the restriction to compact MILPs, the dependence on CR solutions as input (the -cr ablation degrades performance sharply), and evaluation on only two problem families. Open questions raised by the authors include whether the model can be stacked into an unrolled iterative solver, adapted to diffusion-model denoising, or extended to primal heuristics and branch-and-bound guidance, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the two tested problems to other Lagrangian decompositions.
