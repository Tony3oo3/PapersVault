## Problem

Existing post-training quantization methods for LLMs suffer severe performance collapse at ultra-low bit-widths (≤3 bits), with the closest prior binary method PB-LLM requiring 1.7-bit average weights and still yielding perplexity around 800. No prior PTQ approach achieves high-accuracy inference at true 1-bit weight precision for large language models, leaving a significant gap between the theoretical compression of binarization and practical LLM deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240806/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes FP16 pre-trained LLM weights and a small calibration dataset (used solely to compute the per-layer Hessian matrix H = 2XXᵀ). The output is a set of binarized weight tensors at ~1.07–1.11 bits average, structured as: (1) two binary tensors (original + residual) with per-channel scaling factors for salient columns, (2) two binary tensors with separate scaling factors for the concentrated and sparse groups of non-salient weights, (3) a 1-bit flag per element marking group membership, and (4) a sparse column bitmap identifying salient columns. The total hardware overhead beyond the 1-bit parameters is approximately 0.008 bits (for block-size-128 OBC flags).

## Method

BiLLM decomposes each weight matrix into salient and non-salient populations using a Hessian-based sensitivity metric (s_i = w_i²/[H⁻¹]_ii²), then applies distinct binarization strategies to each. For salient weights, identified structurally by per-column aggregation to avoid unstructured bitmap overhead, BiLLM uses a binary residual approximation: it binarizes the weights to get B_o, then binarizes the residual (W − α_o·B_o) to get B_r, yielding W ≈ α_o·B_o + α_r·B_r at 2 bits per salient element. For non-salient weights, which follow a bell-shaped distribution, BiLLM searches for an optimal break-point p* that partitions them into a concentrated region [−p,p] and a sparse region [−m,−p]∪[p,m], binarizing each group independently with its own L1-norm scaling factor to minimize total quantization error. Block-wise OBC error compensation from GPTQ is retained to propagate quantization errors to unprocessed columns, and the entire pipeline completes in under 30 minutes for a 7B model on a single GPU.

## Results

BiLLM achieves an average weight bit-width of 1.08–1.11 bits across LLaMA, LLaMA2, and OPT families, reaching a perplexity of 8.41 on LLaMA2-70B and 8.49 on LLaMA-65B (WikiText2), surpassing the full-precision OPT-66B (9.34). On the OPT family, BiLLM at 1.11 bits achieves 12.06 perplexity on OPT-66B, outperforming GPTQ at 3 bits (10.51) and PB-LLM at 1.7 bits (29.09). On zero-shot tasks (e.g., PIQA, BoolQ, OBQA, HellaSwag), BiLLM at ~1.1 bits consistently outperforms both GPTQ at 2 bits and PB-LLM at 1.7 bits across LLaMA-7B, LLaMA2-7B, and OPT-6.7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiLLM is compared against two primary baselines: GPTQ (2-bit, block size 128) and PB-LLM (1.7-bit, 10% INT8 salient weights). Against PB-LLM, BiLLM achieves a 35% reduction in weight bit-width (1.08–1.11 vs. 1.7 bits) while improving perplexity by 49.4%–77.0% across the OPT family (e.g., 12.06 vs. 29.09 on OPT-66B) and by even larger margins on LLaMA2-70B (8.41 vs. 28.37). Against GPTQ at 2 bits, BiLLM at ~1.1 bits outperforms it on LLaMA-65B (8.49 vs. 8.78) and LLaMA2-70B (8.41 vs. 9.12), and on OPT-66B BiLLM (12.06) is competitive with GPTQ at 3 bits (10.51). On zero-shot accuracy (Table 8), BiLLM leads on most tasks: e.g., 61.2% PIQA and 36.8% HellaSwag on LLaMA-7B vs. 52.8%/26.3% (GPTQ-2bit) and 54.6%/28.7% (PB-LLM). The only task where BiLLM trails is ARC-c on LLaMA2-7B (24.4 vs. 25.0 for PB-LLM), a marginal difference.

## Relation to Prior Work

BiLLM builds directly on the Hessian-based second-order error compensation framework of GPTQ and the block-wise OBC compensation strategy, repurposing them as the scaffolding for a 1-bit binarization pipeline. It departs from PB-LLM, the most recent binary PTQ method for LLMs, which required retaining over 30% of weights at INT8 (yielding 1.7-bit average) to produce coherent outputs; BiLLM instead achieves ~1.08-bit by structurally selecting salient columns via Hessian sensitivity and applying a two-stage binary residual approximation to them, while handling the remaining bell-shaped non-salient weights through an optimal split-point search. The method also extends the broader binarization literature (BWN, XNOR-Net, DoReFa-Net), which has been almost exclusively QAT-based, into the PTQ regime for LLMs where retraining is infeasible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BiLLM demonstrates that LLMs can be compressed to approximately 1 bit per weight via a single PTQ pass (under 1 hour on one GPU for 7B models) while retaining usable language modeling quality, enabling roughly 10× memory reduction (e.g., 129 GB → 15.4 GB for LLaMA2-70B) that makes edge and resource-constrained deployment more feasible. A key open question is practical inference: the paper acknowledges that binarized GEMM is difficult to implement directly due to the fine-grained per-channel grouping and residual structure, so the actual speedup on hardware remains unproven. Additionally, the perplexity gap versus full precision (e.g., 8.41 vs. 3.32 on LLaMA2-70B) is still substantial, and whether the approach generalizes to non-Transformer architectures or to tasks beyond perplexity (e.g., complex reasoning, code generation) at 1-bit remains to be established.
