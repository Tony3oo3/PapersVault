## Problem

Existing text-to-3D face generation methods rely on multi-stage pipelines (separate mesh and texture synthesis, diffusion-based latent optimization, or test-time tuning) that are slow—taking minutes to over 300 seconds per five-view generation—and still produce artifacts due to the absence of multi-view 3D supervision. Direct cross-modal mapping from text to 3D faces remains intractable because single-view training data cannot fully constrain 3D geometry, and the larger 3D visual semantic space makes cross-modal and cross-view consistency significantly harder than in 2D.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language text description, a random Gaussian noise vector z, and a camera pose p as input, and produces a 3D NeRF representation that can be rendered into 512×512 RGB images from arbitrary viewpoints in a single forward pass (0.64 s for five views). For manipulation, it additionally accepts an editing instruction and produces a modified multi-view face by interpolating style code offsets, requiring only 0.89 s for five-view editing without any per-instance optimization.

## Method

E3-FaceNet builds on StyleNeRF (NeRF++ backbone) and performs direct cross-modal mapping by projecting CLIP text features into the latent space and adding them element-wise to the noise vector before the mapping network, so text modulates both the 3D NeRF and the 2D upsampling stages. Two key innovations address the limitations of direct mapping: (1) a Style Code Enhancer (SCE) that applies cross-modal attention at each 2D upsample block to predict per-layer style code offsets Δw_i, injecting fine-grained text semantics into rendering and enabling text-driven editing via linear interpolation of offsets; (2) a Geometric Regularization that computes per-pixel 3D locations from NeRF depth estimates and virtual normal vectors from neighboring points, then enforces L1 smoothness on both to suppress artifacts and improve multi-view consistency without explicit 3D supervision. Training combines a nonsaturating GAN objective with R1 regularization, a CLIP contrastive loss for fast semantic alignment, NeRF-path regularization for 3D consistency, and the geometric and style-offset regularization terms.

## Results

On CelebAText-HQ, E³-FaceNet achieves 0.8521 MVIC and 0.2623 SA, outperforming Latent3D (0.7835 MVIC) and Describe3D (0.7973 MVIC) while reducing five-view inference time from 301.92s to 0.64s (~472× speedup). On MMCelebA, E³-FaceNet attains 12.46 FID and 5.84 KID, surpassing all compared T2D methods (best prior: PixelFace at 16.63 FID). For 3D face manipulation, E³-FaceNet scores 69.92 IP, 52.64 SA, and 75.92 EQ in user studies, far exceeding Latent3D (21.92 IP) and ClipFace (8.16 IP, 12.48 EQ), with a five-view editing time of only 0.89s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two T3D baselines, E³-FaceNet wins decisively on multi-view identity consistency (MVIC +6.87% over Latent3D, +6.48% over Describe3D on CelebAText) and is 472× and 36× faster respectively; it slightly trails Latent3D on CelebAText SA (0.2623 vs 0.2688) but leads on FFHQ-Text SA (0.2778 vs 0.2758). In the manipulation comparison, E³-FaceNet dominates ClipFace on all three user-study metrics (IP 69.92 vs 8.16, SA 52.64 vs 44.56, EQ 75.92 vs 12.48) and is orders of magnitude faster (0.89s vs 17 min + 1.83s), while also far surpassing Latent3D (IP 69.92 vs 21.92, EQ 75.92 vs 11.60). On the T2D MMCelebA benchmark, E³-FaceNet achieves the best FID (12.46) and KID (5.84), beating PixelFace (16.63 FID), OpenFaceGAN (20.72 FID), TediGAN (47.61 FID), and StyleCLIP (53.38 FID); it is the only method to simultaneously lead in both image quality and CLIP-Score among end-to-end approaches, though TediGAN edges it slightly on CLIP-Score (0.2865 vs 0.2770) at the cost of per-example optimization (200 steps per text).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on StyleNeRF (a style-based 3D-aware GAN using NeRF++ as backbone) and departs from the dominant multi-stage paradigm in T3D face generation (e.g., Latent3D's iterative CLIP optimization, Describe3D's separate geometry/texture pipeline, TG-3DFace's compositional text injection). Instead, it adopts the direct cross-modal mapping strategy proven effective in T2D face GANs (StyleCLIP, TediGAN, OpenFace), where text features modulate the latent code in a single forward pass, and extends it to the 3D-aware setting. Two novel components—the Style Code Enhancer (cross-modal attention in 2D upsample blocks) and Geometric Regularization (3D location and virtual normal smoothness)—address the specific failures of naive direct mapping in the 3D regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 470× speedup over Latent3D (0.64s vs. 302s for five-view generation) removes the primary barrier to real-time T3D face applications, and the fact that quality matches or exceeds slower multi-stage methods validates that direct mapping is viable for 3D-aware generation. Limitations include reliance on single-view training data (no true multi-view supervision), restriction to the face domain and StyleNeRF backbone, and semantic alignment that still trails example-dependent optimization methods like TediGAN. Open questions include whether the geometric regularization and SCE generalize to non-face 3D generation or other implicit representations beyond NeRF.
