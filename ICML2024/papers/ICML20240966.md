## Problem

Existing MPNN-based scene graph generation methods fail to incorporate prior knowledge about object co-occurrence during message passing, and they address the long-tail class distribution only through sampling or loss reweighting without adjusting the feature representation itself. This leads to biased predictions toward dominant predicate and object classes and missed fine-grained relational structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240966/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, runs Faster R-CNN with ResNeXt-101-FPN to detect objects (bounding boxes, class labels, visual features), and produces a scene graph of <subject, predicate, object> triplets. A key design constraint is that the CooK matrix is pre-computed from the training set (or an extended set combining VG and OI), and the TF-l-IDF layer operates on batches of node features, so performance scales with batch size. The approach is plug-and-play: it modifies only the message-passing update and adds one feature-reweighting layer, leaving the backbone MPNN architecture unchanged.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-part recipe inserted into any MPNN-based SGG model. First, a Co-occurrence Knowledge (CooK) matrix—computed as the conditional probability P(class_j | class_i) over the training set—is multiplied into the attention-weighted neighbor aggregation (replacing the standard attention score α_uv with cook_{u→v}·α_uv), so that message passing is biased toward object pairs that frequently co-occur. Second, a Learnable TF-l-IDF layer is applied after each MPNN iteration: it computes a per-class term-frequency score (occurrences of class c in the current image divided by total objects) and a learnable inverse-document-frequency score log((B+ε)/(n_c+γ)) with trainable parameters ε and γ, then element-wise multiplies the 1-order node features by the product to suppress head-class features and amplify tail-class features. The two operations are repeated L times (matching the number of MPNN layers), and the final 2-order node features are projected through a linear classifier for relation prediction. An 'Advanced CooK' variant merges co-occurrence statistics from both Visual Genome and Open Images via a hand-crafted label mapping to enrich the prior.

## Results

On the Visual Genome dataset, CooK+TF-l-IDF achieves the best PredCls mR@50/100 of 35.4/37.2, SGCls mR@50/100 of 19.1/20.3, and SGGen mR@50/100 of 14.2/16.3, outperforming the previous best (SQUAT at 14.1 mR@50 on SGGen) and PE-Net (12.4 mR@50 on SGGen). On Open Images, the method achieves a weighted score of 45.1, surpassing PE-Net (44.9) and HetSGG (43.3). When applied as a plug-in to existing MPNN backbones, the method yields mean relative improvements of 17.6% on mR@50 and 22.6% on mR@100 for SGGen across G-RCNN, GPS-Net, and BGNN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CooK+TF-l-IDF achieves the highest PredCls mR@50 (35.4) among all compared methods, beating VETO+Rwt (33.1) by 2.3 points and PE-Net (31.5) by 3.9 points. On SGCls mR@50, it leads with 19.1, surpassing PE-Net (17.8) and HetSGG (17.2). On SGGen mR@50, it edges out SQUAT (14.1) by 0.1 and exceeds PE-Net (12.4) by 1.8 and HetSGG (12.2) by 2.0. On the Open Images dataset, the method achieves the best weighted score (45.1) and wmAPphr (37.6), narrowly beating PE-Net (44.9, 37.4) and clearly outperforming HetSGG (43.3). The plug-in experiments (Table 3) confirm that the method improves every tested MPNN backbone: G-RCNN gains +1.3 mR@50, GPS-Net gains +1.6, and BGNN gains +0.7 on SGGen, demonstrating broad generalization. Notably, the method does not dominate on all R@K metrics (e.g., PE-Net retains higher PredCls R@50 at 68.2 vs. 60.4), indicating a trade-off between mean recall and top-k recall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MPNN-based scene graph generation (Graph-R-CNN, GPS-Net, BGNN) by injecting a precomputed object co-occurrence matrix (CooK) directly into the message-passing aggregation step, a prior-knowledge mechanism previously explored in multi-label classification (SSGRL, SALGL) and predicate-level SGG (BA-SGG) but not applied to object-level co-occurrence during node feature updates. It further introduces a learnable TF-l-IDF layer inspired by NLP's TF-IDF scoring to rebalance object-class features, departing from prior long-tail mitigation strategies that rely on loss reweighting, resampling, or prototype embedding. The method is designed as a plug-and-play module that can be layered onto any existing MPNN backbone without architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design and consistent improvements across multiple MPNN backbones (G-RCNN, GPS-Net, BGNN) make this a practical drop-in for SGG practitioners, and the 'Advanced CooK' idea of pooling co-occurrence statistics across datasets (VG + OI) suggests a path toward more generalizable prior knowledge. However, the method is restricted to MPNN-based architectures and supervised settings, limiting applicability to transformer-based or self-supervised SGG approaches. The TF-l-IDF layer's strong dependence on batch size is a practical constraint that may limit deployment in memory-constrained settings, and the overall gains (≤3.8% mR@50 on SGGen) are modest relative to the added complexity.
