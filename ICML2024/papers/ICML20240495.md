## Problem

Existing loss landscape visualization methods suffer from critical limitations: linear methods like PCA only intersect the high-dimensional training trajectory at a single point, losing fidelity away from that intersection; non-linear methods like UMAP and t-SNE are designed for model clustering rather than landscape reconstruction; and methods like LLE and Laplacian Eigenmaps lack inverse transforms needed to compute loss values at arbitrary grid points. The paper asks whether a non-linear projection can both faithfully capture entire training trajectories and adaptively scale the visualization to highlight regions of interest.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of neural network weight vectors (the full parameter vector of each model) sampled at equal epoch intervals along a training trajectory, normalized before encoding. It produces a standardized 2D grid in [-1,+1]×[-1,+1] where every grid coordinate can be decoded back into the original n-dimensional parameter space to evaluate the loss function, yielding a full loss landscape. Unlike PCA's uniform linear scale, the output grid has a variable, non-uniform scaling factor that can be tuned to zoom into specific regions near trajectory models.

## Method

Neuro-Visualizer is a vanilla MLP autoencoder trained with a reconstruction loss (MSE between input model parameters and their decoded reconstruction) to learn a non-linear manifold L ⊂ R^n that contains all trajectory models, then reparametrizes that manifold onto a 2D grid via a tanh-constrained encoder. The key differentiator is a modular constraint system: location anchoring losses (polar, center, or circle pinning) orient the trajectory on the grid to highlight specific optimization phases, while a grid scaling loss enforces a logarithmic proportional relationship between parameter-space distances and grid-space distances, controlling local zoom density via a hyper-parameter l^max. Because the decoder provides an explicit inverse mapping from grid coordinates to parameter space, loss values can be computed at every grid point—something impossible with LLE or Laplacian Eigenmaps. The total training objective is a weighted sum of the reconstruction loss and any active constraints, making the method flexible and customizable per experiment.

## Results

On the CoPhy-PGNN quantum mechanics application, Neuro-Visualizer achieves an average relative physics loss error (e_relative) of 0.0095 and an average projection error (e_proj) of 0.0005, outperforming all baselines by orders of magnitude. Specifically, it reduces e_relative by ~177× over PCA (1.6782), ~497× over Kernel-PCA (4.7250), and ~45× over UMAP (0.4295), while reducing e_proj by ~566× over PCA (0.2832), ~173× over Kernel-PCA (0.0865), and ~461× over UMAP (0.2307). Qualitatively, Neuro-Visualizer reveals complex basin structures and local minima in the loss landscape that PCA, Kernel-PCA, and UMAP fail to capture, and its learned manifold maintains consistent loss values between trajectory models and their grid projections.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Neuro-Visualizer decisively outperforms all three baselines on both quantitative metrics. Against PCA (Li et al., 2018), the most commonly used linear method, Neuro-Visualizer reduces relative loss error by ~177× and projection error by ~566×; PCA also produces visually inconsistent landscapes where model colors contradict contour values, and it fails to capture critical points of the Black-box Neural Network trajectory. Against Kernel-PCA (Schölkopf et al., 1997), despite being non-linear, Neuro-Visualizer is ~497× better on e_relative and ~173× better on e_proj; Kernel-PCA's visualization appears too simplistic to provide insight beyond PCA. Against UMAP (McInnes et al., 2018), Neuro-Visualizer is ~45× better on e_relative and ~461× better on e_proj; UMAP concentrates almost the entire grid on the terminal local minima, failing to show the full training trajectory. Qualitatively, Neuro-Visualizer's learned non-linear manifold passes through all trajectory models (ensuring color-contour consistency), reveals complex basin structures with multiple local minima, and supports user-defined constraints (anchoring, grid scaling) that none of the baselines offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the loss landscape visualization framework established by Li et al. (2018), which used linear projections (PCA) to map high-dimensional model parameters onto 2D planes. It departs from both linear methods (PCA, Kernel-PCA) and existing non-linear dimensionality reduction techniques (UMAP, t-SNE, LLE, Laplacian Eigenmaps) by proposing an autoencoder that learns a non-linear manifold with an explicit inverse transform, which is essential for evaluating loss at arbitrary grid points. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of user-defined constraints (location anchoring, grid scaling) that allow the learned manifold to be shaped for specific analytical purposes, a flexibility absent in fixed-projection baselines. The paper also forges a new connection between loss landscape visualization and knowledge-guided machine learning (KGML), a cross-application not previously explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Neuro-Visualizer provides a more faithful and controllable tool for understanding neural network optimization, particularly in multi-loss settings (PINNs, CoPhy-PGNN) where linear methods produce misleading or inconsistent visualizations. The constraint-based design opens a design space for tailoring visualizations to specific research questions (e.g., zooming into convergence regions, comparing multiple trajectories on a shared grid). Key limitations include poor scalability beyond ~10K parameters (infeasible for modern vision models with tens of millions of parameters) and an unexplored theoretical understanding of interpolation properties within the learned autoencoder manifold. Open questions include how to adapt the method to very large parameter spaces and whether the autoencoder's implicit assumptions about manifold structure could introduce systematic biases in the visualized landscape.
