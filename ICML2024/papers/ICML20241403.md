## Problem

Existing space-filling design methods (Halton, Sobol, Latin Hypercube) rely on continuous-space assumptions and natural ordering, making them inapplicable to categorical data where only Hamming distance is available. Random sampling, while trivially applicable, is known to be suboptimal for diversity preservation. The paper addresses the lack of both theoretical guarantees and practical algorithms for constructing diverse subsets in discrete categorical spaces.

## Input / Output

The methods take the dimensionality d of the boolean hypercube {0,1}^d and a target design size n as input, and produce a design D_n of n points along with computed diversity metrics (packing radius, covering radius, average covering). The Hamming distance serves as the sole metric, and the space has no natural ordering—only the binary difference between coordinates is exploitable. The framework extends to non-binary categorical variables via one-hot encoding, and the exact LP solvers are limited to small n (<6) while the greedy methods scale to larger designs.

## Method

The paper introduces two greedy algorithms with approximation guarantees. GIPPR (Greedy Integer Programming Packing Radius) iteratively adds the point farthest from the current design by solving a small integer linear program with d+1 variables and t constraints at each step, yielding a 2-approximation for both packing and covering radii via the k-center covering argument of Gonzalez (1985). GAC (Greedy Average Covering) iteratively adds the point that minimizes the average covering, exploiting the newly proven supermodularity of the average covering function to invoke the Nemhauser et al. (1978) submodular maximization bound, giving a (1/2)-approximation guarantee. Both methods are complemented by exact integer LP formulations for all three diversity metrics (Proposition 3.4) and novel theoretical bounds showing the optimal diversity decays logarithmically in n (Proposition 3.2), in contrast to the polynomial decay in continuous spaces.

## Results

On data collection tasks (n=20, d=10), GAC achieves the best mean squared error on 4 of 6 metrics, most dramatically reducing MIS-MS from 63.4 (Random) to 47.2 (±7.8) and MIS-LL from 295 to 163 (±56). GRIPPR wins on LAB-MS (3.47 vs 3.63 Random) and LAB-LL (1.03 vs 1.12 Random). On hyperparameter tuning, GAC achieves the best improvement over default on Cora (78.2%), CiteSeer (84.0%), and Item (41.7%), while GRIPPR dominates on Load (25.0%) and Anon (63.3% vs 46.8% Random, a 35% relative gain). Both methods consistently outperform Random, d-Halton, and k-DPP across all downstream tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Both GIPPR and GAC consistently outperform all three baselines (Random, d-Halton, k-DPP) across every evaluated task. Against d-Halton (discretized Halton sequence), the proposed methods win on all 11 metrics across both data collection and hyperparameter tuning; d-Halton is particularly weak on categorical data, sometimes performing worse than Random (e.g., d-Halton MIS-MS 65.4 vs Random 63.4). Against k-DPP, the gap is more pronounced on combinatorial tasks: GAC reduces MIS-MS by 27% (47.2 vs 65.0) and MIS-LL by 48% (163 vs 314) relative to k-DPP. On hyperparameter tuning, GRIPPR achieves 63.3% improvement on Anon vs 47.7% for k-DPP and 42.2% for d-Halton, while GAC leads on Cora (78.2% vs 76.1% k-DPP) and Item (41.7% vs 37.4% d-Halton). The only task where a baseline is competitive is LAB, where GRIPPR's 3.47 MS is only marginally better than d-Halton's 3.58.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established space-filling design literature (Halton, Sobol, Latin Hypercube, minimax/maxmin designs) from continuous hypercubes to the boolean hypercube, where no natural ordering exists. Methodologically, GIPPR builds directly on the farthest-point greedy scheme from the k-center problem (Gonzalez, 1985), but replaces the intractable continuous farthest-point search with an exact integer linear program, while GAC leverages the submodular optimization framework of Nemhauser et al. (1978) applied to the newly shown supermodularity of average covering. The paper departs from Pronzato & Müller's continuous optimal design constructions (Delaunay triangulation, Voronoi tessellation) and from DPP-based selection, positioning itself as an optimality-guaranteed alternative rather than a probabilistic model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a genuine gap: many practical applications (hyperparameter tuning, molecular design, demographic sampling) involve categorical variables for which no prior theoretical framework existed for diversity-optimal design construction. The clean logarithmic decay bounds and 1/2-approximation guarantees provide the first theoretical understanding of how design quality scales with size in categorical spaces, contrasting sharply with the polynomial decay in continuous settings. Limitations include exact solvers being restricted to n < 6, practical dimensionality capped around 50, and the methods not yet handling mixed continuous/discrete inputs. Open questions include whether deep-learning-based LP solvers can push dimensionality further and whether mixed diversity objectives can be optimized jointly.
