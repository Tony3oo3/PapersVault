## Problem

Although VAEs are widely used for disentangled representation learning, the theoretical explanation for why they promote disentanglement has relied on local linearity assumptions (Rolinek et al., 2019; Zietlow et al., 2021) that are inadequate for real-world non-linear neural decoders. Moreover, the critical link between decoder orthogonality and disentanglement had only been supported by experimental evidence, lacking a formal theoretical proof.

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on standard RGB image datasets (dSprites, 3D Faces, 3D Shapes, MPI 3D Complex) and examines the local behavior of trained VAE decoders around each data point. Rather than producing a single global output, the method decomposes the local decoder into a linear matrix M_D and a non-linear function f_D, then measures orthogonality of M_D columns via a novel OD-Score metric and correlates it with disentanglement scores (MIG, MIG-Sup) across β-VAE, β-TCVAE, and standard VAE architectures.

## Method

The paper models the VAE decoder locally as a composition of a non-linear function and a linear matrix, Dec_θ(z) = g_D(M_D z), under a 'polarized regime' assumption where latent variables split into active (small variance) and passive (near-unit variance) groups. It decomposes the reconstruction loss into deterministic and stochastic components, then proves (Theorem 1) that minimizing the stochastic part while fixing the deterministic part and KL divergence forces the columns of M_D to be orthogonal, with latent variance inversely proportional to column norms. A second theorem (Theorem 2) establishes that this orthogonality promotes disentanglement by aligning latent space principal axes with the standard basis and ensuring that adjusting a single latent variable changes only one generative factor. Experiments validate the theory by showing that the non-linear local approximation outperforms prior linearization approaches in accuracy, and that OD-Score (measuring deviation from orthogonality) is inversely correlated with MIG and MIG-Sup disentanglement scores across all tested architectures and datasets.

## Results

The paper's primary empirical contribution is showing that modeling the local VAE decoder as a composition of non-linear and linear functions (rather than purely linear) reduces the approximation error of the stochastic reconstruction loss L_rec^stoch across all four datasets (dSprites, 3D Faces, 3D Shapes, MPI 3D Complex) and three VAE architectures (VAE, β-VAE, β-TCVAE). Against Rolinek et al.'s linearization, the non-linear approach achieves lower error in all 12 dataset-architecture combinations, with statistically significant improvements (p<.05 or p<.01) in 10 of 12 cases (e.g., 0.4532 vs 0.4866 on dSprites β-TCVAE; 0.5296 vs 0.5799 on 3D Faces β-VAE). Additionally, the introduced OD-Score (Orthogonality Deviation Score) shows a consistent inverse correlation with MIG and MIG-Sup disentanglement scores, empirically confirming that greater orthogonality in the local decoder matrix M_D promotes disentanglement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its non-linear local decoder approximation against two prior linearization-based analyses. Against Rolinek et al. (2019), which approximates the stochastic reconstruction loss using the Jacobian (a purely linear model), the proposed non-linear approach achieves lower approximation error in all 12 dataset-architecture combinations, with statistically significant gains (p<.05 or p<.01) in 10 of 12 cases; the largest improvement is on 3D Faces β-VAE (0.5296 vs 0.5799, ~8.7% relative reduction). Against Zietlow et al. (2021), which assumes the local decoder is entirely linear, the non-linear model also wins in 11 of 12 comparisons (e.g., dSprites β-TCVAE: 0.8502 vs 0.9209; 3D Shapes β-TCVAE: 0.8122 vs 0.8848), with the sole exception being MPI 3D β-VAE where the linear model happens to give a lower error (0.5240 vs 0.8043). These results collectively demonstrate that the local non-linearity assumption is more faithful to real-world VAE behavior than the linear approximations used in prior theoretical analyses, validating the paper's core modeling choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical analysis of VAE-based disentanglement initiated by Rolinek et al. (2019), who showed that the stochastic reconstruction loss promotes local orthogonality in the decoder, and Zietlow et al. (2021), who formalized the PCA-like behavior under a local linearity assumption. The key departure is replacing the local linearity assumption with a local non-linearity model (decoder as composition of a non-linear function and a linear matrix), which the authors argue is necessary to capture real-world VAE behavior. The paper then provides the missing theoretical link—previously only supported by experimental evidence—between decoder orthogonality and disentanglement, going beyond the diagonal posterior narrative that dominates prior explanations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work clarifies a long-standing mechanistic question in DRL: beyond the well-known role of the diagonal posterior, the stochastic reconstruction loss actively promotes disentanglement by enforcing orthogonality in the decoder's linear component. The practical implication is that practitioners tuning β-VAE or β-TCVAE architectures can better understand the tradeoff between reconstruction fidelity and disentanglement quality. However, the analysis relies on the polarized regime assumption (active variables with σ²≪1) and the local Taylor approximation, which may not hold in all practical settings; the paper acknowledges that precise alignment of embeddings in the latent space remains an open question, and the theoretical framework has not been extended to non-Gaussian priors or more complex decoder architectures.
