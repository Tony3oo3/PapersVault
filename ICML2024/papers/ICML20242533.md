## Problem

Existing vision-language-action models operate on 2D inputs and learn direct perception-to-action mappings, lacking both 3D spatial understanding and the ability to imagine future scene states as a world model would. Additionally, available embodied datasets predominantly contain 2D images without depth, point cloud, or 3D bounding box annotations, making it difficult to train models that reason about 3D spatial relationships and generate multimodal goal predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242533/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes interleaved sequences of RGB-D images, 3D point clouds, and text instructions, with 3D scene features extracted via multi-view projections through a Q-Former. Outputs include generated goal RGB images and depth maps (256×256 resolution), goal point clouds, discretized 7-DoF robot actions (256-bin location/rotation tokens plus binary gripper tokens separated by <ACT_SEP>), 3D axis-aligned bounding boxes (6 location tokens per object), and free-form text. Special modality tokens (<image></image>, <pcd></pcd>) specify which diffusion decoder to invoke for goal generation.

## Method

3D-VLA builds on a 3D-LLM backbone (BLIP2-FlanT5XL with Q-Former for multi-view 3D feature extraction) and introduces a vocabulary of interaction tokens—object tokens, 6-token AABB location tokens, scene tokens, and discrete action tokens—to enable interleaved 3D-text-action reasoning. For goal generation, the paper pretrains two embodied diffusion models (Stable Diffusion V1.4 conditioned on concatenated RGB+depth latents for RGBD-to-RGBD editing, and Point-E with an added point cloud condition for point-to-point generation) on curated robotics data, then aligns them to the LLM via a transformer-based projector that maps LLM hidden states into each diffusion model's latent space. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) fine-tune diffusion models on robotics data to bridge the domain gap, (2) train the multimodal LLM on language tasks (QA, captioning, localization, action prediction) using cross-entropy loss, and (3) jointly optimize LoRA parameters in the diffusion models, new special token embeddings, and the projector to minimize combined LLM token loss and diffusion denoising loss. The model first generates an imagined goal state (image or point cloud), which is then fed back as conditioning for action token prediction, implementing a generate-then-plan world model loop.

## Results

On held-in reasoning tasks, 3D-VLA achieves BLEU-1 scores of 48.34 (Embodied QA), 55.69 (Task Caption), 53.09 (What-if QA), and 51.90 (Dense Caption), substantially outperforming the strongest 2D baseline BLIP2-FlanT5XL (37.31, 22.05, 28.23, 36.17 respectively). For 3D localization, 3D-VLA reaches IoU 29.33 and Acc@25 of 42.26, surpassing CoVLM (IoU 19.81) and Kosmos-2 (IoU 10.92). On RLBench action planning, 3D-VLA achieves 68% (Put Knife), 80% (Take Umbrella), and 40% (Pick up Cup) success rates, compared to LanCon-Learn's 28.8%, 45.6%, and 23.2%. On CALVIN long-horizon tasks, 3D-VLA completes 44.7% of the first task and 16.3% of the second, versus MCIL's 28.2% and 2.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3D-VLA outperforms all 2D VLM baselines (BLIP2-FlanT5XL, OpenFlamingo, LLaVA) by large margins on every reasoning task; for example, on Task Caption it achieves BLEU-1 of 55.69 vs. 22.05 for the held-in BLIP2-FlanT5XL, a 2.5× improvement. The 3D-LLM baseline performs very poorly (BLEU-1 ≈ 1) because its pretraining data (objects and indoor scenes) does not align with the embodied robotics domain. On 3D localization, 3D-VLA (IoU 29.33) beats both 2D grounding models Kosmos-2 (IoU 10.92) and CoVLM (IoU 19.81) by 47% and 48% respectively, validating the benefit of native 3D annotations over 2D-to-3D projection. For goal image generation, 3D-VLA (PSNR 17.21, FID 0.177) surpasses Instruct-P2P (PSNR 14.41), SuSIE (PSNR 15.20), and NeXT-GPT (PSNR 8.86), and even outperforms Instruct-P2P trained on the same robotics data (PSNR 16.67). On point cloud generation, 3D-VLA achieves P-FID 4.796 and Chamfer-L1 0.139, improving over Point-E (P-FID 5.241, Chamfer-L1 0.159). For action planning, 3D-VLA dominates LanCon-Learn on RLBench (e.g., 80% vs. 45.6% on Take Umbrella) and MCIL on CALVIN (44.7% vs. 28.2% on the first sequential task), with the goal-generation module providing an additional 10–12% boost over the no-goal variant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D-LLM architecture (Hong et al., 2023) by adding interaction tokens for embodied interaction and coupling it with pretrained diffusion decoders (Stable Diffusion V1.4 for RGBD-to-RGBD, Point-E for point-to-point) via a learned projector, creating a unified generative world model. It departs from prior 2D VLA models (RT-2, PALM-E) that learn direct perception-to-action mappings by introducing explicit goal-state imagination as an intermediate reasoning step, and from existing 3D foundation models (Chat-3D, Point-LLM, 3D-LLM) that focus on understanding current scenes rather than predicting future multimodal states. The data pipeline repurposes existing robotics datasets (Open-X, RLBench, CALVIN) by augmenting them with estimated depth (ZoeDepth), 3D bounding boxes (Grounded-SAM), and ChatGPT-generated language annotations to construct a 2M-sample 3D embodied instruction tuning dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that integrating 3D spatial reasoning with generative world-model capabilities (imagining goal images and point clouds) consistently improves embodied reasoning, localization, and action planning over 2D baselines, suggesting that explicit future-state prediction is a valuable inductive bias for robot control. However, significant limitations remain: depth estimation in real-world settings lacks unified scale, the diffusion decoder occasionally hallucinates or removes objects, and discrete action tokens struggle with precise manipulation of small objects. Open questions include whether this paradigm scales to real-world deployment without simulator-to-real gap, how to handle the long-tail quality distribution across heterogeneous datasets, and whether the generative world model component can be made more reliable and controllable for safety-critical applications.
