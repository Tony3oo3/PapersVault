## Problem

Active Exploration (AE) in Convex RL is not scalable to the vast experiment spaces typical of scientific discovery, as it requires solving a sequence of MDPs and estimating visitation densities at each iteration. While these spaces often possess natural geometric structures (e.g., radial symmetry in pollutant diffusion, permutation invariance in molecular design), no prior work formally connects AE with MDP homomorphisms or quantifies how abstraction improves sample efficiency. This paper bridges the two areas and provides the first theoretical analysis capturing the benefit of geometric abstraction on sample complexity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite MDP with known transition dynamics P, a known MDP homomorphism h = (ψ, {φ_s}) encoding the geometric structure, and noisy scalar observations of an unknown function f at visited states. It produces aggregated estimates of f computed via weighted averaging across f-equivalence classes (rather than per-state estimates), along with an optimal sampling policy for the original MDP obtained by lifting the optimal abstract policy back through the homomorphism. The homomorphism is assumed known a priori, which is reasonable for applications where geometric priors (symmetry groups) are physically motivated.

## Method

GAE extends MDP homomorphisms to Convex RL by showing that the optimistic gradient of the geometric estimation objective is invariant across f-equivalence classes (Proposition 4), enabling the Frank-Wolfe linear subproblem at each iteration to be solved on the smaller abstract MDP rather than the original one. The algorithm computes the optimal policy on the abstract MDP via value iteration, lifts it to the original MDP through the homomorphism, deploys it for τ_k steps, and aggregates f-estimates and visitation counts across equivalence classes to update the abstract state-action frequency. The key theoretical result is a regret bound of Õ((Φ^{1/2} S^{1/2} A F_max √σ²_max / η^{5/2}) · n^{-1/3}) where Φ = S̄/S is the geometric compression term, and a corresponding sample complexity of Õ(Φ^{3/2} S^{3/2} A³ F_max³ (σ²_max)^{3/2} / (η^{15/2} ε³)), making explicit that greater symmetry (smaller Φ) yields faster convergence. Proposition 5 further links Φ to the group cardinality via the Orbit-Stabilizer Theorem, showing Φ = |Stab(s)|/|G|.

## Results

In the pollutant diffusion environment (S=240 states, 5 actions), GAE achieves lower geometric estimation error than classic AE across all tested compression levels Φ, with the gap widening as Φ decreases (i.e., more symmetry is exploited). In the chemical compound toxicity environment (S=363 states, S̄=55 abstract states, Φ≈0.15), even this moderate compression yields a significant statistical advantage in estimation error. Computationally, GAE's normalized runtime decreases proportionally to the compression ratio, demonstrating that solving smaller abstract MDPs via value iteration is substantially faster than solving the full MDP. The theoretical regret bound scales as Õ(Φ^{1/2} S^{1/2} A F_max √σ²_max / η^{5/2} · n^{-1/3}), confirming that higher symmetry (lower Φ) directly reduces sample complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is classic Active Exploration (AE) from Tarbouriech & Lazaric (2019), implemented as GAE with an identity homomorphism (Φ=1, S̄=S). GAE wins on both statistical and computational efficiency in all tested settings. In the diffusion environment (Figures 2a–2d), GAE with compression Φ < 1 consistently achieves lower estimation error at the same sample budget, and the gap grows as Φ decreases; runtime is also reduced in proportion to the compression. In the strings/toxicity environment (Figure 2e), the moderate compression Φ≈0.15 (363→55 states via permutation invariance) still produces a clear statistical advantage over AE. An ablation in Figure 2c shows that removing the geometric inductive bias in the inference step (i.e., not performing weighted averaging across equivalence classes) degrades performance, confirming that the benefit of abstraction operates in both the policy-learning and the estimation phases. No comparison is made against other Convex RL methods or against kernel-based AE (Mutny et al., 2023), which is noted as a future direction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected lines of work: Active Exploration (AE) in MDPs (Tarbouriech & Lazaric, 2019; Mutny et al., 2023), which frames optimal experimental design as Convex RL, and MDP homomorphisms (Ravindran & Barto, 2001; van der Pol et al., 2020b), which exploit known symmetries for state-space abstraction in standard RL. The key departure is extending the MDP homomorphism formalism to Convex RL, where the objective is a convex functional of the state-action distribution rather than a scalar reward, enabling the first principled use of geometric structure in AE. The Frank-Wolfe optimization scheme from Convex RL (Hazan et al., 2019) is retained, but each sub-problem is solved on a smaller abstract MDP via policy lifting, and a new geometric estimation error replaces the classic per-state error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The geometric compression term Φ = S̄/S provides the first formal quantification of how much known symmetry structure reduces sample complexity in AE, scaling the bound as Φ^(3/2). This makes AE more practical for scientific discovery problems where experiment spaces are vast but structured (molecular design, environmental sensing). Key limitations include the assumption of a known homomorphism, homogeneous equivalence classes, and ergodicity; extending to learned/approximate homomorphisms, continuous domains, and deep RL representations are identified as important open directions. The analysis is also specific to finite MDPs with known dynamics, leaving open the question of how these benefits interact with model uncertainty or partially observed settings.
