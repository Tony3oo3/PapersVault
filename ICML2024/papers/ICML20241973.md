## Problem

In many real-world optimization problems (e.g., mechanical design, materials science), the feasible region is unknown and infeasible designs return no objective value at all, making standard constrained Bayesian optimization inapplicable. The paper observes that in such settings the global optimum almost always lies on the boundary between feasible and infeasible regions, yet no prior method explicitly targets this boundary for exploration. Existing approaches rely on Gaussian Process classifiers that struggle with complex, non-smooth constraint boundaries and use fixed or multiplicative constraint couplings that either over-explore infeasible regions or under-explore the boundary.

## Input / Output

The method operates on continuous design vectors x ∈ ℝ^d and consumes only binary feasibility labels (feasible/infeasible) alongside objective values from feasible samples; infeasible evaluations yield no continuous signal. The constraint surrogate is a binary classifier C(x) → [0,1] rather than a continuous constraint function, and the acquisition optimizer must satisfy a dynamic band constraint l(x) ≤ C(x) ≤ u(x) at each iteration. The output is a single next evaluation point per BO iteration, with the final result being the best feasible design found within a fixed evaluation budget (200 in experiments).

## Method

BE-CBO replaces the standard GP constraint classifier with a Deep Ensemble of MLPs trained via variational inference (ELBO with Bernoulli likelihood), which captures complex non-smooth boundaries more accurately and provides calibrated epistemic uncertainty σ_E(x). The key mechanism is a boundary exploration strategy: the acquisition function (EI) is optimized subject to a dynamic band constraint l(x) = 0.5 − σ_E(x) ≤ C(x) ≤ 1, where the lower bound widens with classifier uncertainty in early iterations and tightens as the boundary is learned, encouraging sampling near the feasible/infeasible interface without wasting budget deep in the infeasible region. The objective function is modeled by a standard GP (Matérn 5/2 kernel), and the constrained acquisition optimization uses SLSQP with an Adam-based initialization that pushes candidate points toward the boundary (C(x) ≈ 0.5) to satisfy the constraint. The paper demonstrates that DE outperforms GP classifiers in balanced accuracy, that the dynamic bound outperforms both multiplicative (CEI-style) and fixed-cutoff (SCBO-style) constraint couplings—especially in high dimensions with low feasibility ratios—and that the method generalizes to interior optima when the EI landscape favors them.

## Results

BE-CBO is evaluated on 12 benchmark problems (3 synthetic 2D functions and 9 real-world engineering designs spanning 2–30 dimensions) with 10 initial samples and 200 total evaluations, averaged over 10 random seeds. BE-CBO consistently achieves the best or near-best current-best objective value across all problems with the lowest variance, and discovers the global optimum earlier (e.g., at ~100 evaluations on LSQ vs. later for baselines). The sole exception is the 30D cantilever beam design, where SCBO slightly outperforms BE-CBO due to its TURBO-based local surrogate optimizer. BE-CBO also achieves the highest feasibility ratio (fewest wasted infeasible evaluations) and maintains stable runtime (~5K seconds for 200 iterations) across all problem dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BE-CBO outperforms CEI (Gelbart et al., 2014) on nearly all 12 benchmarks; CEI's multiplicative coupling of feasibility probability with EI tends to over-exploit the safe interior and under-explore the boundary, leading to slower convergence and higher variance. Against SCBO (Eriksson & Poloczek, 2021), BE-CBO wins on 11 of 12 problems, with SCBO narrowly ahead only on the 30D cantilever beam design where its TURBO trust-region optimizer provides an advantage in high-dimensional acquisition optimization. BE-CBO also surpasses SVM-CBO (Antonio, 2021) across all problems; SVM-CBO's two-stage design (SVM boundary exploration followed by BO in the feasible region) is less sample-efficient, and its SVM fitting time scales poorly with dimension. In terms of feasibility ratio, BE-CBO samples a higher proportion of feasible points than both SCBO (which restricts search to the feasible side) and CEI (which penalizes but does not exclude infeasible samples), meaning fewer wasted evaluations that return no objective information. Ablation studies confirm that the boundary exploration strategy (dynamic bound l(x)=0.5−σ_E(x)) is the most robust coupling mechanism, outperforming both the multiplicative (CEI-style) and fixed 0.5-cutoff (SCBO-style) alternatives, especially in high-dimensional settings with low feasibility ratios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends constrained Bayesian Optimization (CBO) by introducing a boundary exploration strategy and replacing the standard Gaussian Process constraint surrogate with Deep Ensembles. It builds directly on the BO framework of Jones et al. (1998) and the Deep Ensembles uncertainty estimation of Lakshminarayanan et al. (2017), but departs from prior CBO methods (CEI, SCBO, SVM-CBO) that either multiply the acquisition by feasibility probability or impose a fixed feasibility threshold. The key conceptual departure is formulating the search as a constrained optimization over a dynamic uncertainty band around the feasibility boundary (l(x) = 0.5 − σ_E(x)), explicitly motivated by the observation that physical-constraint optima lie on the boundary rather than in the interior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: many engineering and materials-science problems involve unknown physical constraints where infeasible designs yield no measurable output, making standard CBO formulations inapplicable. The comprehensive validation across 12+ benchmark problems (2D–30D) and the demonstration that all nine real-world engineering problems have boundary optima strengthen the case for boundary-focused search. However, the method is limited to continuous single-objective settings, does not account for the cost of infeasible evaluations, lacks theoretical convergence guarantees, and is outperformed by SCBO's TURBO-based optimizer in the highest-dimensional (30D) problem—suggesting that integrating local-surrogate optimizers with boundary exploration is a natural next step.
