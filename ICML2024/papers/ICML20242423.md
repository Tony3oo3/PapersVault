## Problem

Analog in-memory computing (IMC) systems require ADCs to convert compute results back to the digital domain, but ADCs operate with fixed quantization steps and clipping values determined by hardware design, unlike conventional quantizers whose parameters can be optimized during training. This fixed, non-optimizable quantization causes substantial accuracy degradation in state-of-the-art models, and prior IMC QAT methods only demonstrated success on simple datasets like CIFAR-10 without transferring to complex models and tasks.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained neural network models (CNNs and transformers) and produces standard task outputs (class labels, bounding boxes, answer spans, next-token probabilities) after quantization-aware training. The IMC hardware configuration uses 512×512 memory arrays with 4-bit activations and weights, and ADCs with a hardware clipping parameter k=4; higher-precision layers are mapped via matrix tiling. The first and last layers are kept at 8-bit and excluded from IMC mapping, and depthwise convolutions and the second BMM in self-attention are also excluded to preserve fidelity.

## Method

RAOQ comprises two classes of mechanisms applied in a two-stage training procedure. First, SQNR enhancement: A-shift treats activations as unsigned during quantization then shifts them to signed (by subtracting 2^{b_x-1}), concentrating mass at large absolute values to maximize the 2nd moment of the ADC input (~15× higher than signed quantization); W-reshape applies a kurtosis penalty directly on quantized weights to flatten their distribution and increase variance (~4× higher). Second, SQNR adaptation: BitAug augments the loss with gradients from randomly sampled neighboring ADC bit precisions to help SGD escape the additional local minima introduced by fixed-step ADC quantization; ADC-LoRA initializes low-rank adaptation matrices via MSE regression against the ADC-quantized output (rather than SVD, which ignores ADC effects), achieving >45× reduction in trainable parameters. The training proceeds as: (1) QAT for activations/weights using LSQ+ with W-reshape and A-shift, then (2) RAOQ finetuning with ADC quantization, BitAug, and ADC-LoRA.

## Results

On ImageNet with 4-bit activations/weights and 8-bit ADC, RAOQ achieves 70.45% top-1 accuracy for ResNet18 (vs. 66.61% for vanilla QAT), 76.27% for ResNet50 (vs. 74.16%), and 70.46% for MobileNetV2 (vs. 68.55%), staying within <1% of full-precision baselines. For object detection on COCO 2017, YOLOv5s with RAOQ reaches 33.49 mAP at 8-bit ADC (vs. 24.03 for vanilla QAT), a <2% drop from the 37.20 full-precision baseline. On SQuAD 1.1, BERT-base achieves 87.67 F1 with 8-bit ADC (vs. 84.53 for vanilla QAT), and OPT-1.3B on WikiText-2 shows only 14.42 perplexity (vs. 30.14 for vanilla QAT), a <1.6 drop from the 14.02 full-precision baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAOQ consistently outperforms all three prior IMC QAT methods (Jin et al. 2022, Sun et al. 2021, Wei et al. 2020) on CIFAR-10: at 4-bit/4-bit/3-bit ADC, RAOQ achieves 89.34% vs. 81.70% (Jin), and at 4-bit/4-bit/4-bit with M=256, RAOQ reaches 90.48% vs. 86.55% (Sun). On more complex tasks (ImageNet, COCO, SQuAD, WikiText), the gap widens dramatically — e.g., on ImageNet ResNet18/50 with 8-bit ADC, RAOQ achieves 70.45/76.27% vs. 68.98/75.14% (Jin), 69.24/75.58% (Sun), and 66.64/74.32% (Wei); on BERT-base SQuAD, RAOQ reaches 87.67 F1 vs. 86.23 (Jin), 87.02 (Sun), and 22.12 (Wei). For LoRA initialization, ADC-LoRA (MSE-based) outperforms both standard LoRA (Hu et al. 2021) and SVD-based LoFTQ (Li et al. 2023): on BERT-large it achieves 89.28 F1 vs. 89.19 and 89.10, and on OPT-1.3B it reaches 14.42 perplexity vs. 15.02 and 14.88. Against vanilla QAT (the most direct baseline in the main tables), RAOQ recovers nearly all accuracy lost to ADC quantization, e.g., closing the gap from 3.86 points (ResNet50, 8-bit ADC) to 0.04 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of IMC-aware QAT work (Jin et al., 2022; Sun et al., 2021; Wei et al., 2020), which demonstrated ADC quantization handling only on simple datasets like CIFAR-10 and MNIST, to SOTA-scale models and challenging benchmarks. It builds on LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) by adapting it into ADC-LoRA with an MSE-based initialization, departing from the SVD-based initialization in LoFTQ (Li et al., 2023) which the authors show is inapplicable to ADC quantization due to the extra quantization on computed outputs. The core conceptual departure from prior IMC QAT is shifting the problem from hardware-level clipping design to algorithmic manipulation of activation/weight statistics (A-shift, W-reshape) combined with multi-precision augmentation (BitAug) to make SGD-based optimization tractable under fixed ADC quantization steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ADC quantization has been the primary blocker preventing IMC from being applied to SOTA AI models at practical efficiency design points; this work demonstrates that purely algorithmic interventions can recover near-full-precision accuracy across CNNs and transformers on ImageNet, COCO, SQuAD, and WikiText without any hardware modification. The practical impact is significant for the IMC hardware community, as it validates that aggressive row-parallelism (512×512) with low-precision ADCs (7–9 bits) is viable for production-grade models. Open questions include integration with analog noise from specific memory technologies (only simple additive noise is tested), generalization to other IMC architectures and number representations, and whether the approach scales to models beyond ~1.7B parameters without further overhead reduction.
