## Problem

Existing orthogonalization and metadata normalization methods for removing sensitive information from model predictions assume linearity in both the prediction model and the correction function. When models contain non-linear activations (sigmoid, softmax, ReLU), classical orthogonalization fails to fully eliminate the influence of protected features on final predictions, leaving residual bias that can lead to unfair or privacy-violating decisions in medical, financial, and other risk-averse applications.

## Input / Output

The method takes a feature matrix Z (n×q, e.g., pre-trained embeddings, intermediate layer activations, or raw features), a protected feature matrix X (n×p, full column rank, with categorical variables one-hot encoded minus the reference column), and a target y. It produces corrected predictions ŷ^c, corrected model coefficients γ^c, or corrected features Z^c = P_X^⊥ Z. The approach extends to tensor-valued predictions (n×d₁×…×d_R) for intermediate CNN layers, requiring n > p and q > p, and can be applied post-hoc to pre-trained models or online during training.

## Method

The key theoretical insight (Lemma 3) is that despite the non-linearity of the evaluation model M^e, linearly orthogonalizing the prediction model's output via ŷ^c = P_X^⊥ ŷ + h(0)·1_n is sufficient to yield zero coefficients in M^e, because the GLM's Newton iterates reduce to a weighted projection that collapses to the standard orthogonal projection at the null solution. For GLMs, this is operationalized as a constrained optimization (Corollary 4) solved via a modified differential multiplier method that combines Lagrangian multipliers with a penalty term. For ReLU activations, Theorem 5 proves that the standard feature correction Z^c = P_X^⊥ Z suffices, exploiting the non-negativity of ReLU to show all cross-terms in the MSE expansion vanish. For tensor-valued intermediate layer predictions, the correction generalizes to a 1-mode tensor product (Corollary 7), allowing the orthogonalization to be inserted as a layer between any two ReLU layers in a CNN or LSTM architecture.

## Results

On the Adult Income dataset, the proposed C^h orthogonalization reduces the sex (male) coefficient from 1.066 (p<2e-16) to -0.000 (p=0.998) and the White race coefficient from 0.522 (p=0.001) to 0.005 (p=0.969), effectively eliminating all significant protected-feature effects. On MIMIC-CXR embeddings, C^h brings all p-values above 0.68 (vs. C^l which leaves age at p<2e-16 and Black race at p<2e-4), while slightly improving AUC by 0.02. The performance cost of correction is minimal: at most a 2% accuracy drop (COMPAS: 0.739→0.724) and a 0.005 AUC reduction on UTKFace (0.831→0.826). On the colorized MNIST task, the corrected CNN achieves substantially better test accuracy than the uncorrected model, which is fooled by spurious color correlations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four fairness baselines (Komiyama1, Komiyama2, Berk, Zafar) on the fairness benchmark (Figure 3) and one orthogonalization baseline (Xu et al., 2022) on Adult Income. All four fairness methods retain significant protected-feature effects (non-zero coefficients with small p-values), while the proposed C^h achieves near-zero coefficients with p-values > 0.95 across all datasets. Notably, Xu et al. (2022) actually worsens the bias on Adult Income: the male sex coefficient increases from 1.066 to 1.255 and the White race coefficient from 0.522 to 0.928, whereas C^h eliminates both. The classical linear orthogonalization C^l, which the paper extends, fails for non-linear models: on MIMIC-CXR it leaves age (p<2e-16) and Black race (p<2e-4) still significant, while C^h renders all effects non-significant. The performance trade-off is small (≤2% accuracy loss), and runtimes are competitive (0.48–13.2s vs. 0.56–15.0s for baselines on the fairness benchmark).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the orthogonalization/normalization framework established by Lu et al. (2021) for metadata normalization in CNNs and Rügamer et al. (2023) for effect identifiability, both of which assume linearity in the model and correction function. The key departure is showing that classical feature-level orthogonalization (projecting Z onto span(X)⊥) is insufficient when a non-linear activation h is applied to the linear predictor, and deriving corrected routines for GLMs (via constrained optimization with Lagrangian multipliers), ReLU networks (via a structural property of ReLU), and tensor-valued intermediate layer predictions. The paper also positions itself against the fair machine learning literature (Komiyama, Berk, Zafar, Scutari), demonstrating that fairness-constrained optimization does not guarantee the stronger orthogonality property it enforces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for risk-averse domains like medical imaging, where models trained on chest X-rays have been shown to encode racial information that could drive treatment decisions. The paper demonstrates that the correction incurs minimal performance cost (≤2% accuracy drop) while fully eliminating linear detectability of protected attributes in predictions. Key limitations include the requirement that n > p and q > p, the need to know which features Z relate to protected information, and the fact that orthogonalization does not imply stochastic independence—residual non-linear dependence could persist. Open directions include extending to high-dimensional settings (n < p), non-linear evaluation models, and automatically discovering which features encode protected information.
