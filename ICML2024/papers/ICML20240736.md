## Problem

The performance disparity between stochastic (SDE) and deterministic (probability flow ODE) sampling in diffusion models is well-documented empirically but lacks a rigorous theoretical explanation, and no direct method exists to compute the negative log-likelihood (NLL) under stochastic sampling. This paper addresses both gaps by reformulating score-based diffusion models through Feynman's path integral formalism, providing a unified framework that connects the two sampling schemes and enables explicit NLL evaluation in the presence of noise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240736/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained score network s_θ(x_t, t), SDE scheduling functions (f, g), a prior noise sample x_T, and a data point x_0, and produces generated samples x_0 along with NLL values computed via a perturbative expansion in the interpolating parameter ℏ. A key unusual aspect is that the NLL is not obtained by direct integration over stochastic paths but through a WKB-style series expansion where the O(ℏ) correction requires solving a coupled ODE system (the probability flow ODE plus a linearized perturbation equation) and evaluating discrete finite-difference approximations of ∇log q_t^{ℏ=0}(x) and its Hessian at each time step. Experiments are restricted to 2-dimensional synthetic data (Swiss-roll, 25-Gaussian) because the finite-difference derivative computation does not scale to high-dimensional image data.

## Method

The paper reformulates the forward and reverse SDEs of score-based diffusion models as path integrals weighted by the Onsager-Machlup action, re-deriving the reverse-time SDE and the score-matching loss as consequences of this representation. Following Zhang & Chen (2023), it introduces an interpolating parameter ℏ ∈ [0,1] in the generating SDE (Eq. 9) such that ℏ=0 recovers the probability flow ODE and ℏ=1 recovers the standard SDE; in the path integral, ℏ plays the role of Planck's constant, with ℏ→0 corresponding to the classical limit where only the action-minimizing path survives. The central technical result (Theorem 4.1) gives an exact self-consistent expression for log q_0^ℏ(x_0) valid at any ℏ, and Theorem 4.2 provides the first-order WKB correction by expanding around the ℏ=0 probability flow trajectory, yielding a computable O(ℏ) NLL correction that depends on the score estimation error s_θ − ∇log q_t^{ℏ=0}. This framework quantifies how noise in the sampling process affects generation quality: when the score is imperfect, the NLL and 2-Wasserstein distance can decrease with increasing ℏ, explaining the empirical observation that stochastic sampling sometimes outperforms deterministic sampling.

## Results

On 2D synthetic data (Swiss-roll and 25-Gaussian), the O(ℏ) correction to the NLL is consistently negative across all SDE schedulings and tolerance settings, confirming that stochastic sampling (ℏ>0) reduces the NLL relative to the deterministic probability flow ODE (ℏ=0). For Swiss-roll, the 1ST-CORR ranges from −0.31 (SIMPLE, tol=1e-3) to −3.27 (COSINE, tol=1e-5); for 25-Gaussian, it ranges from −3.61 (SIMPLE, tol=1e-5) to −19.65 (COSINE, tol=1e-5). The COSINE SDE scheduling yields substantially larger NLL improvements from noise than the SIMPLE scheduling. In the analytically tractable 1D Gaussian toy model, the NLL is a monotonically decreasing function of ℏ for any nonzero score error ε, and the 2-Wasserstein distance exhibits the same qualitative trend.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing generative methods; its contribution is a theoretical reformulation enabling NLL computation for stochastic sampling, a quantity that was previously intractable. The primary comparison is between the deterministic probability flow ODE (ℏ=0) and the stochastic SDE (ℏ=1): the negative 1ST-CORR values demonstrate that the stochastic scheme achieves a lower NLL, i.e., better likelihood, than the ODE. Between the two SDE schedulings, COSINE consistently produces larger NLL improvements from noise (e.g., −3.27 vs. −0.44 on Swiss-roll at tol=1e-5; −19.65 vs. −3.61 on 25-Gaussian at tol=1e-5), suggesting that the noise benefit is scheduling-dependent. The 2-Wasserstein distance (Fig. 4) corroborates the NLL findings, showing W₂ typically decreases in the small-ℏ region, confirming improved sample quality with stochasticity. The authors note that the COSINE SDE's larger first-order derivative near ℏ=0 is consistent with the steeper initial slope visible in the W₂ curves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SDE formulation of diffusion models (Song et al., 2021b) and the interpolating SDE parameterized by ℏ from Zhang & Chen (2023), but departs significantly by recasting the entire framework in Feynman's path integral language, identifying ℏ as an analog of Planck's constant. The Onsager-Machlup function from statistical physics provides the Lagrangian, and the WKB expansion—a standard semiclassical technique in quantum mechanics—is applied for the first time to diffusion models to perturbatively compute the negative log-likelihood in stochastic sampling. The paper re-derives Anderson's (1982) reverse-time SDE and the score-matching loss via path integrals, positioning these as consequences of the action principle rather than independent results.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The path integral formulation provides a unified physical lens that connects stochastic and deterministic sampling through a single parameter, and the WKB expansion yields the first explicit NLL computation for stochastic diffusion sampling—a quantity for which only formal expressions (Feynman-Kac) or bounds existed previously. However, empirical validation is restricted to 2D synthetic distributions (Swiss-roll, 25-Gaussian), and the authors acknowledge that the discrete-differential approach to computing derivatives does not scale to high-dimensional image data. Open questions include extending the analysis to Schrödinger bridges with general priors and understanding regimes where noise degrades rather than improves sample quality, as observed empirically by Karras et al. (2022).
