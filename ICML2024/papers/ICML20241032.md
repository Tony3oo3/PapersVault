## Problem

Existing first-order methods for stochastic bilevel optimization tie their analyses to a 'genie' that knows the lower-level solution y*(x) exactly, and the fundamental complexity of finding ε-stationary points with only locally reliable gradient access (within a Θ(ε)-ball around y*(x)) plus an O(ε)-accurate estimate of y*(x) was unknown. This paper asks: what is the minimax oracle complexity under a y*-aware oracle model, and are first-order methods fundamentally worse than second-order methods under minimal assumptions?

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes two continuously-differentiable functions f(x,y) and g(x,y) (with g strongly convex in y) through a y*-aware first-order stochastic oracle that, at each query point (x,y), returns unbiased gradient estimators for f and g (valid only when ‖y − y*(x)‖ ≤ r) plus an estimate ŷ(x) satisfying ‖ŷ(x) − y*(x)‖ ≤ r/2. The output is an ε-stationary point x of the hyperobjective F(x) = f(x, y*(x)), with the total number of oracle calls bounded by O(ε⁻⁶) without stochastic smoothness and O(ε⁻⁴) with it, matching the proven lower bounds.

## Method

For the upper bound, the paper reformulates the bilevel problem via a penalty surrogate L*_λ(x) = min_y [f(x,y) + λ(g(x,y) − g(x,y*(x)))] and runs a double-loop SGD on it, where the inner loop simultaneously tracks y (approximating y*_λ) and z (approximating y*) with a coupling projection that keeps v = y − z within a radius r_λ = l_{f,0}/(μ_g λ). The key insight for the O(ε⁻⁴) rate with stochastic smoothness is that coupling y and z with the same random seed ξ makes the variance of the gradient difference scale as O(‖y−z‖²) = O(1/λ²) rather than O(σ²), reducing inner-loop iterations from O(ε⁻⁴) to O(ε⁻²). For the lower bound, the paper constructs a hard instance where y*(x) = F(x) (the single-level zero-chain hard instance) and g(x,y) = (y − F(x))², so that progress in x is only revealed indirectly through ∇_x g with a small probability p = O(ε⁴) (or O(ε²) with smoothness), yielding matching Ω(ε⁻⁶) and Ω(ε⁻⁴) lower bounds via probabilistic zero-chains and randomized coordinate embedding.

## Results

The paper establishes tight (matching upper and lower) oracle complexity bounds for finding ε-stationary points in stochastic bilevel optimization using first-order methods. Without stochastic smoothness, the complexity is Θ(ε⁻⁶); with stochastic smoothness (mean-squared Lipschitz gradient estimators), it improves to Θ(ε⁻⁴). The O(ε⁻⁶) upper bound improves upon the previous best first-order result of O(ε⁻⁷) (Kwon et al., 2023b) by an O(ε) factor, and the O(ε⁻⁴) bound matches the best-known second-order method complexity under the same smoothness assumption.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is against prior first-order methods for stochastic bilevel optimization. Kwon et al. (2023b) achieved O(ε⁻⁷) without stochastic smoothness and O(ε⁻⁵) with it; this work improves both by an O(ε) factor to O(ε⁻⁶) and O(ε⁻⁴), respectively, using a penalty-method reformulation with tighter bias control via the v = y − z coupling. Under stochastic smoothness, the O(ε⁻⁴) first-order bound now matches the best-known second-order method complexity (Ji et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2021), showing that first-order methods are not inherently worse when gradient estimators are mean-squared Lipschitz. The lower bounds (Ω(ε⁻⁶), Ω(ε⁻⁴)) are proven via probabilistic zero-chain constructions adapted from Arjevani et al. (2023) and Carmon et al. (2020), establishing that no black-box algorithm with y*-aware oracles can beat these rates. The results also subsume the standard oracle model (r = ∞), meaning the improvements hold even without the y*-aware oracle assumption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the penalty-method reformulation of bilevel optimization from Kwon et al. (2023b, F²SA), which established an O(ε⁻⁷) upper bound with first-order oracles, and on the probabilistic zero-chain framework of Arjevani et al. (2023) and Carmon et al. (2020) for lower bounds in single-level stochastic nonconvex optimization. The key departure is the introduction of a y*-aware oracle model that abstracts away the cost of tracking lower-level solutions, allowing the authors to isolate the fundamental complexity of estimating hypergradients. By adapting the zero-chain construction to the bilevel setting—where progress in x is mediated through g—the paper derives matching Ω(ε⁻⁶) and Ω(ε⁻⁴) lower bounds that were previously unavailable for first-order bilevel methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The matching upper and lower bounds establish the minimax complexity of first-order methods for bilevel optimization under minimal assumptions, resolving a gap that had persisted since the O(ε⁻⁷) upper bound of Kwon et al. (2023b). The result that stochastic smoothness in y alone (not jointly in (x,y)) suffices to close the gap between first-order and second-order methods (both achieving O(ε⁻⁴)) has practical implications for algorithm design in meta-learning and hyperparameter optimization. The main open question, stated as Conjecture 1, is whether the Ω(ε⁻⁶) lower bound extends to fully global oracles (r=∞), which would require constructing a hard instance where g is globally strongly convex while ∇ₓg remains uniformly bounded—a nontrivial challenge the authors acknowledge may require new techniques.
