## Problem

Existing multi-step prompting strategies for LLMs (e.g., chain-of-thought, stop-and-go inference) query the model sequentially, so each decoding step is unaware of future template parts, producing disconnected and suboptimal intermediate responses. The paper asks whether treating the entire template as a single joint decoding problem can improve reasoning quality, and what the general effects of template-guided inference are on model capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240147/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text question or task description and a prompt sketch—a template interleaving deterministic instruction phrases (e.g., 'On the one hand', 'In conclusion') with variable placeholders that the model must fill. The output is the fully completed template, where each variable is constrained by stopping phrases and optionally by hard constraints (e.g., regex, enumerated choices), yielding structured reasoning steps followed by a final answer. Unlike sequential prompting, the entire template is decoded as one segmented sequence, so deterministic chunks are scored for likelihood and variables are optimized jointly rather than independently.

## Method

Prompt sketching reframes template-guided LLM inference as a single segmented sequence decoding problem rather than multiple isolated model calls, theoretically anchoring stop-and-go inference as a special case (greedy ARGMAX over the segmented sequence). The key enabling mechanism is chunking the generated sequence into deterministic and variable portions using per-variable stopping phrases, then applying sketch-aware decoders that operate at the template level. Two novel decoders are introduced: VAR (Variable-Level Beam Search), which extends hypotheses by n sampled values per variable rather than per token, and BEAMVAR (Variable-Grid Beam Search), which partitions beam width into pools per decoded variable (adapting dynamic beam allocation from Post and Vilar, 2018) so that low-likelihood deterministic chunks do not crowd out variable hypotheses. This joint optimization allows the model to anticipate future template content during generation, preventing issues like word repetition and enabling forward-referencing tasks that greedy decoding cannot handle.

## Results

On text-davinci-003 (100 samples/task), prompt sketching with ARGMAX decoding outperforms zero-shot chain-of-thought on 7 out of 8 reasoning tasks, with gains up to 4 percentage points (e.g., Matrix Shapes: 0.81 vs. 0.77; AQuA: 0.40 vs. 0.37). Combining sketching with the proposed BEAMVAR decoder yields up to 10 percentage point improvements over sequential CoT (e.g., AQuA: 0.47 vs. 0.37; StrategyQA: 0.77 vs. 0.71). On Llama-2 Chat (13B, 1000 samples/task), sketching improves over CoT on 6/8 tasks, with BEAMVAR achieving up to 7 percentage point gains (e.g., GSM8K: 0.353 vs. 0.276; Date Understanding: 0.656 vs. 0.591).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against zero-shot chain-of-thought (Kojima et al., 2022), prompt sketching with ARGMAX decoding wins on 7/8 tasks for text-davinci-003 (e.g., AQuA 0.40 vs. 0.37, Matrix Shapes 0.81 vs. 0.77, Tracking Shuffled Objects 0.64 vs. 0.62), with the sole exception being GSM8K where CoT BEAMVAR (0.64) edges out sketching (0.57–0.59). Sketch-aware decoders amplify these gains: BEAMVAR achieves 0.47 on AQuA and 0.77 on StrategyQA, surpassing the best CoT variant by 10 and 6 percentage points respectively. Against self-consistency (Wang et al., 2022, n=2 and n=4), sketching with BEAMVAR/VAR outperforms SC on most tasks (e.g., AQuA: 0.47 vs. 0.33/0.32; StrategyQA: 0.77 vs. 0.64/0.70; Matrix Shapes: 0.85 vs. 0.66/0.66), while SC with n=4 narrowly beats BEAMVAR on GSM8K (0.66 vs. 0.64) at roughly 2× the computational cost. Overall, the paper demonstrates that template-guided inference with joint variable optimization consistently matches or exceeds both sequential prompting and self-consistency baselines across diverse reasoning tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LMQL (Beurer-Kellner et al., 2023), the query language for LLMs that already supported template-guided generation via stop-and-go inference, and on the dynamic beam allocation scheme of Post and Vilar (2018) for constrained decoding. The key conceptual departure is reframing the entire multi-variable template as a single segmented sequence decoding problem rather than a sequence of isolated model calls, which theoretically subsumes stop-and-go as a special case (greedy ARGMAX) and enables novel variable-level decoders (VAR, BEAMVAR) that jointly optimize over all template variables. This positions the work between prior prompt-programming systems (LMQL, Guidance, PromptChainer) that compose disconnected LLM calls, and language model cascades (Dohan et al., 2022) that implicitly assume a sketching setting but do not optimize overall template likelihood.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, implementable framework (with open-source dclib) for template-guided LLM inference that gives practitioners guaranteed output structure while enabling joint optimization over reasoning steps — a capability absent from both naive prompting and sequential stop-and-go. The finding that sketch-aware decoders outperform simple stop-and-go on 5/8 tasks demonstrates that the decoding strategy, not just the prompt template, is a critical design dimension. Limitations include dependence on now-deprecated OpenAI APIs (preventing full reproduction), the need for iterative sketch design per task, and evaluation primarily on 13B–175B models from 2023. Open questions include how sketch-aware decoding interacts with scaling to frontier models, whether it composes with self-consistency or RL-based fine-tuning, and whether the benefits persist as models become more instruction-following natively.
