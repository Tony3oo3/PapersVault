## Problem

Physics-informed Gaussian processes (PIGPs) leverage PDE structure for Bayesian inference but ignore the additional constraints imposed by known Lie symmetries of the underlying equation. While Lie symmetries have recently been incorporated into physics-informed neural networks (PINNs), their integration into GP-based PIML frameworks remained unexplored. This paper addresses that gap by showing how the invariant surface condition (ISC) induced by a Lie symmetry can be embedded into a GP inference framework to improve both forward and inverse PDE solutions, particularly in low-data regimes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes noisy spatio-temporal solution observations, a (potentially nonlinear) PDE operator, a known Lie symmetry generator (infinitesimal generator ξ, τ, η), Dirichlet initial/boundary conditions, and sets of collocation points for virtual PDE and ISC observations. It outputs a full posterior distribution over the PDE solution (mean and covariance at arbitrary query points), enabling point predictions with calibrated uncertainty intervals, as well as posterior distributions over unknown PDE parameters in inverse problems. A distinctive feature is that the ISC can be enforced either softly (as a virtual likelihood with a trainable nugget) or hard (by designing the GP kernel on canonical coordinates derived from the symmetry, which provably satisfies both the PDE and ISC with probability one).

## Method

The PSGP assigns a GP prior to the solution u, then exploits GP closure under linear operators to induce joint Gaussian distributions over the PDE residual f and the ISC residual g, forming a single latent vector h = [u; d₀; …; dₘ; g] with a consistent joint prior. A three-way likelihood combines real solution-space data, virtual PDE observations (via the algebraic form F_θ), and virtual ISC observations (targeting zero), with trainable nugget terms controlling constraint strength. For nonlinear PDEs, inference proceeds via variational inference maximizing an ELBO with a Gaussian variational posterior q(h) = N(h | a, AAᵀ), where the PDE likelihood term is evaluated by Monte Carlo sampling; for linear PDEs, exact marginalization is possible. Dirichlet boundary conditions are enforced exactly by a mean function interpolating boundary values and a distance function that collapses the kernel to zero at the boundary, a result proven to hold for the variational posterior (Theorem 3.2). When the ISC admits a canonical coordinate reduction (e.g., z = x − t for the wave equation), the kernel is defined directly on that coordinate, eliminating the need for virtual PDE and ISC observations entirely.

## Results

On the Fisher-like equation (Table 1), PSGP achieves the lowest test-set MAE across all collocation point counts, reaching 360×10⁵ with only 16 virtual observations versus 1120×10⁵ for PIGP and 37600×10⁵ for PINN. On the wave equation (Table 2), PSGP obtains 14.9×10² MAE with 16 noisy observations while PIGP collapses to the trivial solution (66.7×10² MAE). For the heat equation inverse problem, PSGP recovers the true solution almost perfectly with only 10 data points and achieves the worst parameter estimate within two decimal places at N_u=25, substantially outperforming PIGP in parameter accuracy, MAE, and CRPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSGP consistently outperforms PIGP (Raissi et al., 2017) across all three PDEs, with the largest gains in the low-data regime: on the Fisher equation, PSGP reduces MAE by 3× at 16 collocation points (360 vs. 1120, ×10⁵), and on the wave equation, PIGP fails entirely (trivial solution, 66.7×10² MAE) while PSGP achieves 14.9×10². Against PINN (Raissi et al., 2019), PSGP is dramatically superior in sparse-data settings (e.g., 360 vs. 37600, ×10⁵ on Fisher at 16 points), though the gap narrows as data increases and both converge at 128 points. Compared to PSNN (Zhang et al., 2023c), PSGP maintains a clear advantage at low data (360 vs. 25000, ×10⁵ on Fisher; 14.9 vs. 20.1, ×10² on wave at 16 points), while PSNN achieves the lowest wave-equation error at 128 observations (2.1 vs. 3.2, ×10²). The heat equation inverse problem shows PSGP's advantage over PIGP is most pronounced in calibration (CRPS) and robustness to noise, with PSGP results remaining stable while PIGP oscillates in the noisy, low-data regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the physics-informed Gaussian process (PIGP) framework of Raissi et al. (2017) and the variational inference approach for nonlinear PDEs of Long et al. (2022) by incorporating Lie symmetry information through the invariant surface condition (ISC). While recent work has begun embedding symmetries into neural-network-based PIML (equivariant architectures, data augmentation, loss-function penalties), this is the first to integrate classical Lie symmetries into a GP-based inference framework, either as soft constraints via virtual ISC observations or as hard constraints via kernel design on canonical coordinates. The theoretical treatment of boundary condition enforcement (Theorem 3.2) generalizes the standard GP/NN distance-function trick to the variational PSGP posterior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that Lie symmetry constraints yield substantial sample-efficiency gains in both forward and inverse PDE problems, particularly in the low-data regime where GP inductive biases are most valuable. The clear limitation is the cubic scaling of GP inference, restricting applicability to small-to-medium datasets; the authors note that sparse GP methods (e.g., Hensman et al., 2013) could address this. Open questions include whether symmetries can be learned automatically rather than assumed a-priori, whether non-classical symmetries can be incorporated, and whether deep GPs would further improve performance on complex PDE systems.
