## Problem

Ferri et al. (2024) conjectured that, among all k×n binary matrices with distinct rows, the matrix H_{n,k} (rows are binary representations of 0 through k−1) minimizes the sum of distinct-row counts over all q-column projections — a quantity directly governing the worst-case teaching dimension in the Greedy machine teaching model. This paper proves the conjecture, generalizing the classical edge isoperimetry theorem for hypercubes (Hart, 1976) from the special case q = n−1 to all values 1 ≤ q ≤ n.

## Input / Output

The problem takes a k×n binary matrix M with all distinct rows and an integer q ≤ n, and evaluates m_q(M) = Σ_{Q∈C([n],q)} dif(M(Q)), the sum of distinct-row counts over all C(n,q) column submatrices. The paper proves the minimum over all such matrices is achieved by H_{n,k} and characterizes the minimum value h_q(n,k) through the recurrence h_q(n,k) = h_q(n,⌈k/2⌉) + h_{q−1}(n−1,⌊k/2⌋) with base case h_q(n,1) = C(n,q). Matrices are considered up to row and column permutations, and the result holds for all 1 ≤ k ≤ 2^n.

## Method

The proof is structured in two stages. First, the authors reduce the main theorem to a stronger sufficient condition (Theorem 3.1): the minimum of h_q(n,x) + h_{q−1}(n−1,k−x) over x ∈ [⌈k/2⌉, k−1] is attained at x = ⌈k/2⌉. Second, they prove this by showing h_q(n,x) grows at least as fast as h_{q−1}(n−1,k−x) shrinks, which reduces to comparing sums of binomial coefficients of the form C(n−|i|, q−|i|) over consecutive integer intervals. The key technical tool is a generalization of Graham's (1970) lemma (Lemma 4.5), which constructs a bijection between two intervals of integers such that the Hamming weight of each image is at least the Hamming weight of its preimage minus a fixed constant, enabling term-by-term domination of the binomial sums. The overall argument closes via induction on k, using the recurrence relation for h_q(n,k) and the fact that the minimizing split of k rows into two groups is always the balanced split ⌈k/2⌉ / ⌊k/2⌋.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 2.1) proves that for all positive integers q ≤ n and k ≤ 2ⁿ, the binary matrix H_{n,k} (rows are binary representations of 0 through k−1) achieves the minimum value m_q(n,k) of the sum of unique rows over all q-column submatrices. As a concrete illustration, for H_{4,5} the paper reports m₁ = 7, m₂ = 16, m₃ = 15, and m₄ = 5. The proof proceeds by establishing a stronger sufficient condition (Theorem 3.1) via a generalization of Graham's (1970) lemma on Hamming-weight-preserving bijections, then applying induction on k. The result generalizes Hart's (1976) edge isoperimetry theorem for hypercubes, which corresponds to the special case q = n−1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The 'comparison' is an optimality proof: H_{n,k} is shown to minimize m_q(M) over the entire space of k×n binary matrices with distinct rows, meaning no alternative matrix construction can achieve a smaller value for any q. In the special case q = n−1, the result recovers and generalizes Hart's (1976) theorem on the edge isoperimetry problem for the n-dimensional hypercube, where H_{n,k} was already known to maximize induced edges. The proof technique builds on Graham's (1970) lemma (completed by Jones & Torrence, 1999) via a new generalization (Lemma 4.5) that handles shifted intervals with a Hamming-weight deficit bound. The paper also notes an open problem: whether computing m_q(M) for a given matrix is fixed-parameter tractable when parameterized by q, with a straightforward O(n^q · k·q·log k) algorithm as the current baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper proves a conjecture from Ferri et al. (2024) that the binary-representation matrix H_{n,k} minimizes the sum of unique projection rows m_q(M) over all k×n binary matrices, for every q. The q = n−1 special case recovers the classical edge isoperimetry problem for hypercubes solved by Hart (1976), so the main theorem is a genuine generalization of that result to all 1 ≤ q ≤ n. The proof technique extends a lemma of Graham (1970) on Hamming-weight-preserving bijections, completing a proof gap later filled by Jones & Torrence (1999), and applies it in a new combinatorial setting involving sums of binomial coefficients over intervals of integers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides a clean, universal characterization of the worst-case consistency matrix in the Greedy machine-teaching model, confirming that binary-representation orderings are extremal for all projection sizes simultaneously. This has direct implications for understanding the teaching dimension and the structure of consistency graphs in machine teaching. The authors leave open whether computing m_q(M) is fixed-parameter tractable when parameterized by q, which would have practical algorithmic consequences. The broader relevance to machine teaching is that the consistency graph's structure is now better understood, though the result is purely theoretical and does not yield new algorithms or empirical benchmarks.
