## Problem

Training Transformer models with differential privacy faces two unique challenges not present in vanilla neural nets: (1) an 'attention distraction' phenomenon where DP noise induces anisometric variance in embedding parameters, and the nonlinear softmax in self-attention amplifies this variance into inflated attention scores for infrequent tokens, degrading model accuracy; and (2) existing efficient per-sample gradient clipping methods (e.g., Ghost Clipping) are incompatible with standard Transformer architectures because embedding parameter sharing creates a non-plain feed-forward topology that breaks their assumptions.

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of L discrete tokens (e.g., a user's chronological interaction history) over a vocabulary of size M and produces a prediction score for each candidate next token via cross-entropy loss. The DP guarantee is user-level: removing all information pertaining to a specific user yields an indistinguishable model. The experiments use small Transformer encoders (dimension 64, 2 blocks, 1 head) suitable for local inference on resource-constrained end devices, with sequence lengths significantly smaller than vocabulary size (L = o(M)).

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary techniques. First, the Re-Attention Mechanism tracks the effective error σ_eff^θ = σ_dp / B_eff^θ for each parameter (where B_eff^θ is the expected number of times θ is activated per batch), propagates this variance through layers via analytic natural-parameter propagation from Bayesian deep learning, and corrects attention scores by dividing by exp(Cσ²/2) to remove the multiplicative bias f(Var[K_i]) that inflates scores for high-variance tokens. Second, Phantom Clipping computes the per-sample gradient norm for the shared embedding layer in O(BL²) memory without instantiating per-sample gradients, by exploiting the one-hot structure of token encodings to decompose the norm into three terms computable from the one-hot matrices and their gradient counterparts. Together, these reduce DP Transformer training to the better-understood problem of DP vanilla neural net training, enabling the use of off-the-shelf model-agnostic DP techniques.

## Results

On MovieLens (ε=5), the Re-Attention Mechanism achieves 5.88% NDCG@10 and 11.13% HIT@10, a 29% relative improvement over the vanilla Transformer (4.57% NDCG@10). On Amazon (ε=5), it reaches 1.64% NDCG@10 and 3.01% HIT@10, a 20% improvement over vanilla Transformer (1.37% NDCG@10). At the higher privacy budget ε=10, gains narrow to 5.4% on MovieLens (8.42% vs 7.99% NDCG@10) and 27% on Amazon (1.99% vs 1.57% NDCG@10). For Phantom Clipping, the method achieves up to 450× larger batch sizes and 100× training speedup over Ghost Clipping on small models, reaching 0.68× the speed of non-private training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four model baselines (GRU, LSTM, Transformer without parameter sharing, and vanilla Transformer with parameter sharing) under DP-SGD training. The vanilla Transformer consistently outperforms all RNN-based baselines (GRU and LSTM) by a wide margin, e.g., 4.57% vs 2.65% NDCG@10 on MovieLens at ε=5, confirming the dominance of attention-based architectures for sequential modeling. The Transformer without parameter sharing (2.33% NDCG@10) underperforms the vanilla Transformer (4.57%), demonstrating that embedding sharing provides critical inductive bias under noisy DP gradients. The proposed Re-Attention Mechanism beats the vanilla Transformer across all privacy levels and both datasets, with the largest gains (29% on MovieLens, 20% on Amazon) at the strictest privacy budget ε=5, consistent with the theory that attention distraction worsens with higher noise. For the efficiency comparison, Phantom Clipping is 10–400× more memory-efficient and 4–100× faster than Ghost Clipping (Li et al., 2022b) on small models, while matching its memory complexity on the embedding layer while additionally supporting the standard Transformer's parameter sharing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Ghost Clipping (Li et al., 2022b) to handle the parameter sharing of the embedding layer in standard Transformers, which prior efficient gradient clipping methods could not accommodate due to their assumption of plain feed-forward topology. For the Re-Attention Mechanism, it repurposes natural parameter propagation techniques from Bayesian deep learning (Wang et al., 2016) in a black-box manner to track per-layer output variance during forward pass. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as a 'modular reduction' that isolates the Transformer-specific hardness (attention distraction and embedding-sharing incompatibility) from the general DP neural net training problem, which is already well-studied with model-agnostic techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the attention distraction phenomenon reveals a previously unrecognized failure mode where DP noise's anisometric effect on embeddings, combined with the nonlinear softmax, systematically inflates attention scores for infrequent tokens—this could affect any DP-trained Transformer regardless of domain. The Phantom Clipping technique makes standard Transformers (with embedding sharing) compatible with efficient DP-SGD, which is practically important for local inference on resource-constrained devices. However, the reduction's completeness remains an open question, and empirical validation is limited to small models (64-dim, 2 layers) on two recommendation datasets, leaving open whether these findings scale to large language models or other architectures.
