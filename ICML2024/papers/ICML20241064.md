## Problem

Prior GNN theory literature overlooks the dependence between graph topology and node features (A-X dependence), leaving unexplained why shuffling feature vectors among same-class nodes consistently improves GCN performance while leaving MLP and label propagation unchanged. The paper asks two questions: how should A-X dependence be measured while controlling for node-class confounds, and how does it mechanistically affect graph convolution?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241064/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The CFH measure consumes a graph with node feature vectors and class labels, subtracts class-conditional feature means to produce class-controlled features, then computes each node's mean distance to its neighbors relative to its mean distance to all other nodes, yielding a normalized score in [-1,1]. The CSBM-X generator takes parameters (n, μ₀, μ₁, Σ₀, Σ₁, d⁺, d⁻, τ) and outputs random graphs where τ independently controls A-X dependence while holding feature distance and class-homophily fixed. The feature shuffle experiment takes each benchmark graph and produces synthetic variants by randomly permuting feature vectors within each class at ratios from 0 to 1, preserving train/val/test splits.

## Method

The paper proposes a three-part framework. First, a Class-controlled Feature Homophily (CFH) measure quantifies A-X dependence by comparing each node's neighbor distance to a homophily baseline (average distance to all other nodes) using class-controlled features, with normalization guaranteeing boundedness in [-1,1], scale-invariance, and monotonicity. Second, a CSBM-X random graph model extends contextual stochastic block models with a new parameter τ that precisely controls CFH while independently fixing feature distance and class-homophily (Lemma 4.1–4.2). Third, Theorem 4.3 proves that the Bayes error rate of graph convolution D⁻¹AX is minimized at τ=0 (zero A-X dependence) and strictly increases as |τ| grows, because smaller CFH increases the force pulling each node's convoluted feature toward its class mean, reducing within-class variance and thus classification error. Empirical validation on 24 real-world benchmark datasets using GCNII, GPR-GNN, and AERO-GNN confirms that feature shuffling (which reduces CFH) yields consistent performance gains (mean +4%p) in high class-homophily graphs, with the effect moderated by feature distance and class-homophily exactly as the theory predicts.

## Results

In high class-homophily graphs (12 datasets), GCNII performance improves consistently with increasing shuffled node ratio, with a mean gain of 4%p and a maximum of 10%p on Cora-Full. In low class-homophily graphs, the improvement is minimal (mean 0.5%p), confirming that class-homophily moderates the benefit of reduced A-X dependence. Theorem 4.3 establishes that the Bayes error rate of graph convolution is minimized when CFH = 0 (i.e., no A-X dependence) and strictly increases as |τ| grows in either direction. Across 24 benchmark datasets, graph-level CFH scores are small and positive (mean |h̃^(G)| = 0.06, 23/24 below 0.13), with only a weak, non-significant correlation to class-homophily (Pearson's r = 0.293, p = 0.164).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between GCN (Kipf & Welling, 2017), MLP, and Label Propagation under feature shuffle: only GCN shows a significant performance increase (up to 10%p), while MLP and LP remain unchanged, isolating A-X dependence as the mechanism. Among GNN architectures, GCNII (Chen et al., 2020), GPR-GNN (Chien et al., 2021), and AERO-GNN (Lee et al., 2023) all exhibit similar improvement trends under feature shuffle in high class-homophily graphs, confirming the effect is architecture-independent rather than specific to one convolution design. The paper does not propose a new GNN to outperform these baselines; instead, it provides a theoretical explanation (Theorem 4.3) for why all GNNs benefit from low A-X dependence, positioning CFH as a previously overlooked mediator of graph convolution's effect. The CSBM-X random graph model is compared conceptually to prior CSBMs (Deshpande et al., 2018; Palowitch et al., 2022; Luan et al., 2023), which control X-Y and A-Y dependence but cannot independently control A-X dependence (CFH), a gap CSBM-X fills via its τ parameter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contextual stochastic block model (CSBM) framework of Deshpande et al. (2018) by introducing a new parameter τ that independently controls the dependence between graph topology and node features (A-X dependence), while holding class-homophily and feature distance fixed. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant GNN theory literature that has focused on class-homophily (A-Y dependence) and feature informativeness (X-Y dependence) as the key conditions for effective graph convolution, arguing that the overlooked A-X dependence is actually the mediating factor. The paper also generalizes the homophily concept from the discrete class domain to the continuous feature domain via the proposed Class-controlled Feature Homophily (CFH) measure, positioning itself as an extension of the homophily-GNN connection to a previously unexplored axis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central implication is that the recent empirical success of GNNs on standard benchmarks may have implicitly relied on those datasets having small, positive CFH scores (mean |CFH| ≈ 0.06), meaning existing GNNs could significantly underperform on graphs with large or negative A-X dependence. Two practical gaps remain open: (i) no benchmark datasets with systematically varying CFH levels exist, and (ii) the feature shuffle technique that reduces CFH requires test labels, so an unsupervised method to estimate and adaptively lower CFH is needed. The theoretical analysis also assumes a linear feature-class relationship and symmetric, unimodal node-level CFH distributions, limiting generalization to more complex real-world patterns.
