## Problem

Existing incomplete multi-view clustering (IMVC) methods suffer from three intertwined drawbacks: they require at least O(n³) computing and O(n²) memory due to constructing full-size self-expression affinity matrices and spectral embeddings; they rely on multiple hyper-parameters to balance error and regularization terms, limiting extensibility; and they produce non-zero variance results because clustering is decoupled from representation learning via a separate spectral step. ToRES addresses all three simultaneously by replacing self-expression affinity with compact prototype-sample affinity, eliminating hyper-parameters through dual prototype design, and directly optimizing discrete labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242368/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes M incomplete view matrices D_m ∈ R^{d_m × n} along with binary index vectors w_m that specify which of the n samples are observed on each view, and produces a hard discrete cluster label matrix L ∈ {0,1}^{c×n} where each column contains exactly one 1. No imputation or completion of missing entries is performed; the indicator matrices W_m are used throughout to restrict operations to observed samples only. The output is a direct assignment rather than a continuous embedding, which is what guarantees zero variance across runs.

## Method

ToRES formulates a joint objective that minimizes reconstruction error of observed data under two prototype decompositions: a cross-view prototype matrix E (shared across views, capturing consensus features) and view-wise prototype matrices E_m (view-specific, capturing complementary features), both coupled through an orthogonal aggregation matrix O and a space transformation G_m. The key architectural choice is to replace the n×n self-expression affinity with a c×c prototype-sample structure, reducing memory to O(n) and eliminating the need for a separate fusion stage. To remove hyper-parameters, the two prototype types jointly explore multi-view features without any balancing weight. To eliminate variance, representation learning and clustering are unified: the discrete label matrix L is optimized directly via a per-sample argmin over diagonal elements, rather than applying spectral clustering to a fused affinity. The resulting orthogonal-constrained subproblem for O is solved by two provably equivalent schemes—Scheme 1 partitions the feasible region into per-column quadratic programs, while Scheme 2 transforms the objective and iterates via SVD of the gradient—both yielding O(n) overall complexity.

## Results

On Webkb (20% PID), ToRES achieves 86.20% ACC and 32.61 NMI, outperforming the best baseline IMVTSC-MVI (86.02% ACC, 34.26 NMI) on ACC and HCP-IMSC (85.63% ACC) by 0.57%. On Wikifea (20% PID), ToRES reaches 56.28% ACC and 47.90 NMI, surpassing HCLS-CGL (53.44% ACC) by 2.84% and IKMKC (53.26% ACC) by 3.02%. On the large-scale EMNIST dataset (280,000 samples), ToRES is the only method that can complete within reasonable time (635.10 s), achieving 47.18% ACC and 44.27 NMI at 20% PID, while all 20 baselines fail to run. A distinctive property is zero variance (±0.00) across all datasets and missing ratios, and zero hyper-parameters (NoHp = 0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ToRES outperforms all 20 baselines on Webkb, Wikifea, AWA10, and AwAfea at 20% and 40% PID in ACC, with the largest margins over subspace/NMF-based methods (e.g., +13.03% ACC over AWP and +36.06% over IMG on Webkb). The closest competitor is IMVTSC-MVI, which edges out ToRES on NMI for Webkb (34.26 vs. 32.61) and AWA10 (12.12 vs. 13.25 at 20%), but ToRES wins on ACC and Purity. On SUNRGB-D (20% PID), AWP narrowly beats ToRES by 0.35% in NMI (26.08 vs. 25.73), while ToRES leads in ACC (20.93 vs. 17.61) and Purity (37.16 vs. 36.22). At 60% PID, ToRES degrades more noticeably (e.g., Webkb ACC drops to 73.83%), and GSRIMC surpasses it by 1.77% NMI on SUNRGB-D. The most striking practical advantage is scalability: on EMNIST (280K samples), no baseline can complete, whereas ToRES finishes in 635 s with 26.34 GB memory, and it is the only method with zero hyper-parameters and zero result variance across all experiments.

## Relation to Prior Work

ToRES departs from the dominant IMVC paradigm of constructing self-expression affinity per view, fusing them into a full n×n matrix, and applying spectral clustering. Instead, it repurposes the prototype/anchor idea (seen in APMC, ProImp) but elevates it to the core representation mechanism, introducing two complementary prototype types (view-wise for complementary features, cross-view for consensus features) to replace the role of hyper-parameter-weighted regularization. The direct discrete label optimization, avoiding spectral embedding, is conceptually aligned with one-step clustering approaches but applied here specifically to the incomplete multi-view setting. The two equivalent optimization schemes (feasible region partitioning via QP and objective transformation via SVD-based fixed-point iteration) draw on classical orthogonal Procrustes and alternating minimization techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of zero hyper-parameters, zero variance, and O(n) complexity addresses three practical pain points that collectively limit the deployment of existing IMVC methods in real-world pipelines where tuning is costly and reproducibility matters. The paper acknowledges that under high missing ratios (60%), prototype misalignment and cluster imbalance degrade performance, and that equally weighting cross-view and view-wise prototypes is suboptimal—both pointing toward adaptive weighting and robustness improvements as natural next steps. The linear complexity claim, validated on EMNIST (280K samples in ~635s), makes IMVC tractable at scales where O(n³) methods are infeasible, though the SVD on d_m×d_m matrices for G_m remains a bottleneck for high-dimensional views.
