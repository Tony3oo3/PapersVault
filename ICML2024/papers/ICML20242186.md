## Problem

MPC-based secure Transformer inference suffers from high communication and computation overhead, and while plaintext quantization reduces bit-width and accelerates inference, it cannot be trivially transplanted into the MPC domain. The core gap is that quantization operations (dynamic scaling, clipping) and type conversions between different bit-widths (e.g., INT8↔INT32, FP32↔FP16) are either infeasible or prohibitively expensive over MPC rings, and no prior framework supports end-to-end quantization-aware secure inference.

## Input / Output

The client encrypts a text input (sequence length 128 for BERT, 32–50 for GPT2) into 2-out-of-3 replicated secret shares and distributes them to three computing parties; the model owner similarly encrypts the quantized model parameters. The three parties jointly perform secure inference and return output shares to the client, who reconstructs the plaintext result: classification scores (accuracy, Matthews correlation, F1) for BERT on GLUE tasks, or a single generated token with perplexity for GPT2 on Wikitext-103. All intermediate computations operate over fixed-point rings Z_{2^32} or Z_{2^64} rather than floating-point, with the ring size determined per-layer by the quantization precision.

## Method

Ditto co-designs MPC-friendly quantization with novel MPC primitives in a two-step pipeline. First, the model owner applies layer-wise static dyadic quantization (scale = 1/2^f) to all weights and activations, using FXP32^8 for linear layers and FXP64^18 for non-linear layers (Softmax, LayerNorm, GeLU), thereby avoiding MPC-expensive dynamic operations like clip and max. A quantization-aware distillation procedure (layer-wise MSE loss between teacher and student hidden states) recovers utility lost from the reduced precision and polynomial approximations. Second, the paper introduces upcast and downcast MPC primitives that convert RSS shares between rings of different sizes (e.g., Z_{2^32} → Z_{2^64}) using a mask-and-open trick with a positive heuristic to compute the wrap bit via a single AND gate, requiring only 3ℓ+ℓ′ bits over 3 rounds. A quantization-aware compiler built on SecretFlow-SPU automatically maps plaintext data types to ciphertext rings and inserts type conversions at operator boundaries, enabling seamless execution of the mixed-precision secure inference DAG.

## Results

On GLUE benchmarks (Bert-base, sequence length 128), Ditto with Quad GeLU approximation achieves RTE 67.82, CoLA 54.52, QQP 87.72, and QNLI 91.78, closely matching the unquantized baseline (68.59, 57.06, 87.96, 91.62). On Wikitext-103, GPT2-base perplexity is 13.78 vs. 12.25 baseline, and GPT2-medium is 11.35 vs. 10.60. In terms of efficiency, Ditto reduces communication by 2.37–3.43× vs. PUMA and 1.28–1.70× vs. MPCFormer (Quad), yielding 3.14–4.40× and 1.44–2.35× runtime speedups respectively on Bert-base and GPT2 models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MPCFormer (Li et al., ICLR 2023), Ditto achieves comparable utility (e.g., Bert-base QNLI 91.78 vs. 91.68) while being 3.14–4.40× faster in runtime and 1.28–1.70× lower in communication, thanks to quantization reducing bitwidth. Against PUMA (Dong et al., 2023), Ditto is 1.44–2.35× faster with 2.37–3.43× less communication, though PUMA retains slightly better utility (e.g., Bert-base CoLA 56.96 vs. 54.52) due to its more accurate piecewise polynomial GeLU approximation at the cost of higher communication. Compared to the vanilla SecretFlow-SPU baseline, Ditto with quantization alone yields 1.41–1.56× speedup, and adding the Quad GeLU approximation further improves to 1.74–2.09×. Against 2PC methods Iron (Hao et al., NeurIPS 2022) and Bumblebee (Lu et al., 2023), Ditto is orders of magnitude faster on Bert models, reflecting the advantage of the 3PC honest-majority setting over 2PC. The one case where Ditto underperforms is GPT2-base communication, where MPCFormer has lower overhead due to GPT2's larger vocabulary inflating Ditto's embedding layer cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the secure Transformer inference line (MPCFormer, PUMA, SecureQ8) by integrating model quantization into the MPC execution pipeline, a gap the authors identify as unresolved: prior secure inference works either used uniform 64-bit fixed-point throughout or attempted int8 quantization without extending it to ciphertexts (SecureQ8). The key departure is a co-design between ML quantization (static dyadic/fixed-point with layer-wise scales) and novel MPC type-conversion primitives (upcast/downcast between different rings), combined with quantization-aware distillation to recover utility. The implementation builds on the SecretFlow-SPU framework, extending its compiler to support dynamic ring sizes and automatic type conversions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3–4× speedup over MPCFormer and 1.4–2.4× over PUMA with negligible utility loss makes secure Transformer inference substantially more practical for deployment, particularly in communication-bound settings. The type-conversion primitives are reusable building blocks for any future MPC system that needs mixed-precision arithmetic. Limitations include the restriction to 3PC semi-honest/honest-majority setting, evaluation only on BERT and GPT2 (no LLaMA-scale models), and a noticeable utility drop on CoLA. The authors note future work on more aggressive (lower-bit) quantization, and the broader question of whether these techniques extend to malicious-security settings or larger models remains open.
