## Problem

State-of-the-art model-based RL agents like DreamerV3 rely on pixel-level reconstruction to learn world models, which causes them to overfit to irrelevant exogenous noise (background videos, textures, camera shifts) at the expense of task-relevant dynamics. Prior decoder-free approaches either fail to capture task-specific information, lack robustness across noise types, or are highly sensitive to hyperparameters. The paper asks: how can one learn sufficiently expressive, compact state representations for world-model planning without pixel reconstruction, while remaining robust to diverse exogenous visual noise?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241923/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives 64×64 RGB pixel observations from continuous control environments (DMC, ManiSkill2) that may contain exogenous distractors such as Kinetics video backgrounds, Matterport3D home scenes, color perturbations, or moving cameras. The input is split into cuboid patches of size 10×10×4 (spatial×temporal), and 50% of patches are randomly masked to form a second input stream. The output is a continuous control action produced by an actor-critic policy operating entirely in the learned latent space, with no pixel-level decoder in the final architecture.

## Method

The core contribution is the Hybrid RSSM (HRSSM), a dual-branch recurrent state-space model where a mask branch (online RSSM processing randomly masked observations) and a raw branch (EMA-updated RSSM processing original observations) share the same recurrent historical state to ensure temporal alignment and training stability. Two novel losses replace pixel reconstruction: (1) a latent reconstruction loss that minimizes MSE between L2-normalized latent states of the two branches, filtering spatio-temporal redundancy while preserving semantic content; and (2) a bisimulation-based similarity loss that aligns cosine distance between latent states with the sum of immediate reward difference and discounted dynamics distance, capturing task-relevant behavioral structure. The paper provides theoretical analysis showing that masking alone cannot guarantee optimality (it may damage endogenous state) and bisimulation alone cannot ensure compactness (approximate dynamics with exogenous noise violate support conditions), establishing that both components are necessary. Gradients flow only through the mask branch, and the raw branch parameters are updated via exponential moving average, keeping the total model size (17.54M) slightly smaller than DreamerV3 (18.22M) despite the dual-RSSM structure.

## Results

On the Realistic Maniskill benchmark (100K steps), HRSSM achieves 284±85 on Lift Cube and 278±97 on Turn Faucet, substantially outperforming DreamerV3 (167±45, 138±83), TIA (274±173, 47±23), Denoised MDP (155±71, 71±27), and RePo (83±22, 92±88). On DMC default and distraction settings across 6 tasks, HRSSM consistently surpasses all baselines at 100k, 250k, and 500k training steps, with notably the lowest variance across tasks in the distraction setting. The model also demonstrates strong generalization to unseen video categories (e.g., walker_stand: 963±11 on unseen videos vs. 946±12 on seen videos).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HRSSM outperforms DreamerV3 on all evaluated tasks, confirming that removing pixel-level reconstruction does not degrade performance. Against TIA, HRSSM wins on both Maniskill tasks (284 vs. 274 on Lift Cube; 278 vs. 47 on Turn Faucet) with significantly lower variance (±85 vs. ±173 on Lift Cube). Against Denoised MDP and RePo, HRSSM achieves substantially higher scores on Maniskill (e.g., 278 vs. 71 and 92 on Turn Faucet) and consistently better sample efficiency on DMC distraction tasks. On DMC default settings, HRSSM matches or slightly exceeds DreamerV3 while beating TD-MPC, DBC, and SimSR across all six tasks. The one notable exception is cartpole swingup in the distraction setting, where HRSSM slightly underperforms DeepMDP and RePo, likely due to random masking inadvertently hiding the small pole.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DreamerV3 RSSM framework and extends it with two components drawn from distinct literatures: spatio-temporal masking (from masked autoencoders in CV) and the π-bisimulation principle (from state-similarity metrics in MDP theory). It departs from prior Dreamer extensions for exogenous noise (TIA, Denoised MDP, RePo, DreamerPro) by eliminating pixel-level reconstruction entirely and instead performing latent-space reconstruction between masked and unmasked branches, while grounding the need for both masking and bisimulation through a theoretical analysis showing neither is sufficient alone. The hybrid RSSM architecture with shared historical representations and EMA-updated raw branch is a novel structural contribution not present in prior Dreamer variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck for MBRL deployment: agents that overfit to irrelevant visual details in noisy environments. The decoder-free design is both computationally efficient (slightly smaller than DreamerV3 despite dual RSSMs) and avoids the reconstruction-driven overfitting that plagues pixel-based world models. Key limitations include the reliance on random masking (which can damage small but critical objects, as seen in cartpole swingup) and the absence of a learned, task-aware masking strategy. Open questions include whether signal-to-noise-ratio-based gating or learned masks could resolve the endogenous-state damage, and whether the bisimulation objective's sensitivity to approximate dynamics limits its applicability to more stochastic environments.
