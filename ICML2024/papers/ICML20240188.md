## Problem

Unsupervised learning for combinatorial optimization (UL4CO) via the probabilistic method faces two underexplored challenges: (1) deriving differentiable probabilistic objectives that satisfy desirable properties (upper-bounding the penalized objective, entry-wise concavity, matching the original optimum) under prevalent conditions like cardinality constraints, minimum, and covering is nontrivial, and (2) derandomization to obtain discrete solutions is limited to random sampling or naive iterative rounding without strong quality guarantees or efficient computation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a weighted graph G=(V,E,W) along with problem-specific parameters (e.g., cardinality k for facility location/maximum coverage, number of colors c for robust coloring, edge existence probabilities for uncertain graphs) and produces a discrete decision vector X∈{0,1}^n (a chosen node subset) or a color assignment X∈{0,...,c-1}^n. Internally, a GNN encoder (optional, for inductive settings) maps the graph to continuous probabilities p∈[0,1]^n, which are refined by gradient descent on a differentiable probabilistic objective and then converted to discrete decisions via greedy derandomization. For non-binary problems like robust coloring, p is a matrix in [0,1]^{n×c} with each row summing to 1.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution is showing that any probabilistic objective expressible as an expectation E_{X~p} g(X) is automatically differentiable and entry-wise concave (Theorem 1), reducing objective construction to finding a tight upper bound (TUB) of the penalized objective. For each prevalent condition (cardinality via Poisson binomial PMF, minimum via ordered decomposition, covering via product of non-selection probabilities, cliques via pairwise non-edge counting), the paper derives both the TUB-based expectation and closed-form incremental differences (IDs) Δf(i,x,p) that capture only the change in objective after locally fixing one variable. Derandomization proceeds greedily: at each step, all 2n (or nc for non-binary) IDs are computed in parallel, the best local derandomization is selected, and the process repeats until a discrete solution is reached, with a guarantee that the final solution is no worse than the initial continuous one and is a local minimum (Theorem 2). The derivations are modular and compositional—each CO problem is decomposed into its involved conditions, and the corresponding TUB objectives and IDs are summed to form the final objective and derandomization procedure.

## Results

On facility location (FL), UCOM2-long achieves the best average objective (1.00) across all five datasets with an average rank sum (ARS) of 8.8, while UCOM2-short matches near-optimal quality (obj 1.04) at a fraction of the runtime (1.89s vs. 91.56s for the best baseline CardNN-HGS). On maximum coverage (MC), all three UCOM2 variants achieve the best objective (1.00), with UCOM2-short reaching ARS 8.5 at only 5.41s average time versus 166.56s for CardNN-GS. On robust coloring, UCOM2(CPU) achieves the best average rank (2.50) with the least running time, outperforming Gurobi (ARS 6.50) and all greedy baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UCOM2 dominates all baselines on both speed and quality across FL and MC. Compared to the strongest UL4CO baseline CardNN-HGS (obj 1.05, time 91.56 on FL), UCOM2-long matches or beats its objective (1.00 vs. 1.05) while being 2.6× faster; UCOM2-short is 48× faster (1.89s) with only a 0.01 objective gap. Against MIP solvers, UCOM2-long achieves a 34% better average objective on FL (1.00 vs. Gurobi's 1.51) and is 3× faster; on MC, UCOM2 matches Gurobi's objective (1.00) but is 3× faster (151.82s vs. 464.42s). The EGN-naive baseline (Karalias & Loukas 2020 with naive objectives) is consistently outperformed: UCOM2-long improves FL objective from 1.30 to 1.00 and MC from 0.73 to 1.00. RL-based methods (Kool et al. 2019) are the weakest, with FL objective 2.24 (vs. 1.00) and 8× slower than UCOM2-long. On robust coloring, UCOM2(CPU) beats Gurobi on 6 of 8 dataset-color combinations in objective while being 3–40× faster, and outperforms both greedy baselines (greedy-RD, greedy-GA) and deterministic coloring (DC) across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Erdős Goes Neural (EGN) pipeline (Karalias & Loukas, 2020) and the iterative rounding framework (Wang et al., 2022) by systematically addressing prevalent conditions—cardinality constraints, minimum/maximum over subsets, covering, and cliques—that prior UL4CO works either approximated incorrectly or claimed were intractable. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it formalizes tight upper bound (TUB) construction as the principled target for probabilistic objectives, showing that prior approximations (e.g., softmin for min, min{1, Σp_v} for covering) violate required properties like entry-wise concavity, and (2) it replaces naive rounding with greedy derandomization using incremental differences, yielding stronger quality guarantees and faster execution. The modular design—deriving TUBs and IDs per condition, then composing them per problem—contrasts with the ad-hoc objective constructions in earlier UL4CO papers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a reusable, theoretically grounded toolkit for incorporating common CO conditions into differentiable optimization, lowering the barrier for applying UL4CO to new problems. Empirically, UCOM2 matches or exceeds MIP solvers (Gurobi) in solution quality while being 1–2 orders of magnitude faster, and outperforms the prior SOTA UL4CO method (CardNN) on both quality and speed across facility location, maximum coverage, and robust coloring. Open questions remain: the framework does not yet cover all conditions (e.g., cycles, trees), performance on genuinely hard instances is untested, and the reliance on independent Bernoulli variables limits expressiveness. The modular composition principle suggests the approach could extend to many additional CO problems with modest additional derivations.
