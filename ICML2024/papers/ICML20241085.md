## Problem

Multi-stage quantizers (e.g., RVQ) used as tokenizers for music generation language models produce codes from multiple codebooks that are statistically dependent. Modelling the joint distribution over all codebooks multiplies autoregressive steps by the number of codebooks, while modelling the product of marginals is fast but inexact unless codebooks are mutually independent. Prior work addressed this with ad-hoc decoding strategies but did not directly reduce the statistical dependence between codebooks at the source.

## Input / Output

The system takes a text prompt augmented with metadata (key, tempo, instrumentation) and produces a 32 kHz music waveform. Audio is tokenized by EnCodec at 50 Hz frame rate using 4 RVQ codebooks of 2048 entries each (2.2 kbps effective bitrate). The language model (300M params, MusicGen-small architecture) decodes tokens using a 'delay' strategy where codebook k is offset by k−1 timesteps, preserving the original codec frame rate and avoiding the K-fold inference cost of joint-distribution modelling.

## Method

The paper introduces an independence-promoting auxiliary loss L_inde computed as the Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) in a reproducible kernel Hilbert space between samples from the joint codebook distribution and samples from an approximate factorized distribution (obtained by shuffling codes along the batch dimension per codebook). A multi-scale Gaussian kernel with radii {0.1, 1, 5, 10, 20, 50} is used, ensuring the RKHS is universal so that MMD_H = 0 iff the distributions coincide. The loss is applied during EnCodec training with a weight of 10³, computed over a macro-batch of 1280 samples per GPU using gradient checkpointing to fit on a single V100. Crucially, the loss is adapted to match the 'delay' decoding strategy by applying the same time offsets to codes before computing MMD, promoting independence between the specific time-delayed code tuples that the language model actually predicts. This reduces total correlation between codebooks (verified via histogram-based mutual information estimation) without degrading reconstruction quality, and yields better music generation quality than the baseline at identical parameter count and inference speed.

## Results

On the MusicCaps benchmark, MusicGen-MMD (300M params) achieves FAD_clap-laion of 0.14, FAD_MERT-4 of 1.45, FAD_vgg of 2.98, KL-divergence of 1.18, and a MUSHRA-style OVRL score of 74.75±3.68, outperforming the MusicGen baseline (OVRL 62.54±3.68) by over 12 points on subjective quality while using the same number of parameters and inference speed. It also surpasses AudioLDM2-Music (347M) on all objective metrics and by ~5 points on OVRL. The method preserves the original EnCodec frame rate (50 Hz), avoiding the K× slowdown of joint-distribution (flatten) decoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MusicGen-MMD outperforms its direct baseline MusicGen (same 300M architecture) on every objective metric—FAD_vgg drops from 3.60 to 2.98, FAD_MERT-4 from 1.57 to 1.45, and KL from 1.22 to 1.18—and by a large margin on subjective quality (OVRL 74.75 vs. 62.54, a ~12-point gain). Against the latent-diffusion baselines, MusicGen-MMD beats AudioLDM2-Music (347M) on all four objective metrics and by ~5 OVRL points, and dominates AudioLDM (416M) across the board. Mustango (1.4B) reports lower FAD scores, but it was trained on an augmented version of MusicCaps and scores far lower on OVRL (49.26), making it a non-comparable entry. Noise2Music (1.3B) achieves a slightly better FAD_vgg (2.1 vs. 2.98) but with 4.3× more parameters and no publicly available implementation for full metric comparison. MusicLM and UniAudio, for which only partial metrics are reported from their original papers, are also outperformed on the available FAD_vgg and KL scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) multi-codebook audio tokenization for language-model-based music generation (EnCodec, MusicGen, MusicLM, AudioGen), where prior work proposed various decoding strategies (flatten, delay, parallel) to handle multiple codebooks but did not address the root cause of modelling error under factorized decoding; and (2) independence measurement in machine learning, where prior proxies (MINE, adversarial ICA) rely on adversarial training. The key departure is replacing adversarial mutual-information estimation with a non-adversarial MMD lower-bound in a universal RKHS, and further adapting the independence objective to match the specific decoding strategy (e.g., delay pattern) rather than enforcing marginal independence across all codebooks simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that factorized decoding (same frame rate as the auto-encoder) becomes nearly as good as joint-distribution modelling (K× slower), removing a key efficiency-quality trade-off in multi-codebook language models. The loss is simple, requires no adversarial training, and generalizes to other multi-stream codecs (demonstrated on RVQGAN). Open questions include the trade-off between codebook hierarchy (inherent to RVQ) and independence (promoted by the loss), whether conditional independence (given past tokens) should be targeted instead of marginal independence, and whether optimizing kernel bandwidths during training could tighten the MMD lower-bound further.
