## Problem

Existing gradient compression schemes for distributed training assume noiseless communication links, while over-the-air aggregation methods for noisy wireless channels are restricted to shallow networks and synthetic datasets. No principled compression scheme has been shown to scale to practical models (e.g., GPT-2, ResNet-18) over noisy channels while maintaining SGD-like convergence guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241399/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each worker computes a stochastic gradient matrix (m×n) and, given a power budget P and compression rank r, decomposes it into left and right factors of rank r via PowerSGD. These factors are transmitted over a multiple-access channel with additive Gaussian noise, where the per-coordinate power constraint (P/mn = SNR) serves as the communication cost. The server reconstructs the gradient average from the superposed noisy factor signals and updates the model parameters; bias vectors are sent uncompressed while weight matrices and convolutional filters are compressed.

## Method

LASER exploits the inherent low-rank structure of gradient matrices: instead of transmitting the full gradient (as Z-SGD does), it sends only the rank-r left and right factors computed by a single step of PowerSGD subspace iteration with warm start, coupled with error feedback to correct for compression bias. Because fewer entries are transmitted, each entry receives proportionally more power, reducing the channel noise variance by a factor of O(m) relative to Z-SGD. An optimal power allocation (Lemma 7/8) splits the total power P between the left and right factors to minimize the channel influence factor λ_LASER. The theoretical analysis shows LASER achieves the same 1/T convergence rate as classical SGD for quasi-convex and non-convex objectives, with an additive constant λ_LASER that is O(m) times smaller than Z-SGD's, and empirically demonstrates 50–64% perplexity improvement on GPT-2/WIKITEXT-103 and 16× power reduction on CIFAR-10.

## Results

On GPT-2 language modeling (WIKITEXT-103), LASER achieves 64% perplexity improvement over Z-SGD (76 vs. 212) in the low-power regime (P=10K) and 50% (35 vs. 71) in the moderate-power regime (P=160K), requiring 16× less power to reach any target perplexity. On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18), LASER requires 16× less power than Z-SGD to reach target accuracies up to 91%. On MNIST, LASER achieves 89.9% test accuracy at moderate power (P=1) and 92.3% at high power (P=10), outperforming all baselines. LASER also achieves a 165× communication cost reduction (3MB vs. 496MB per iteration) compared to uncompressed Z-SGD on WIKITEXT-103.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LASER consistently outperforms Z-SGD (uncompressed gradient transmission) across all four benchmarks and power regimes, requiring 16× less power for equivalent performance on WIKITEXT-103 and CIFAR-10. Against SIGNUM (Bernstein et al., 2018), LASER achieves substantially higher accuracy — SIGNUM fails to converge on CIFAR-10 within 150 epochs and trails LASER by 2.6–5.9% on MNIST. Against RANDOM-K (Vogels et al., 2019), LASER wins at moderate and high power on MNIST (89.9% vs. 89.3% at P=1; 92.3% vs. 91.5% at P=10) and dominates on CIFAR-10, though RANDOM-K is marginally better in the very noisy regime (86.1% vs. 84.3% at P=0.1 on MNIST). Against SKETCHING (Rothchild et al., 2020), LASER leads by 1.7–1.1% on MNIST and clearly outperforms on CIFAR-10. Against A-DSGD (Amiri & Gündüz, 2020b), LASER achieves 2.7–5.0% higher accuracy on MNIST; A-DSGD underperforms even simple compression schemes like RANDOM-K and does not scale beyond MNIST with 1-layer networks.

## Relation to Prior Work

LASER sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: (i) gradient compression for noiseless links (PowerSGD, SignSGD, Random-K, Count-Mean Sketch) and (ii) over-the-air aggregation over noisy wireless channels (A-DSGD, analog gradient aggregation, MAC-aware quantization). Prior noisy-channel schemes were restricted to shallow networks and synthetic data, while compression schemes assumed error-free bit pipes. LASER departs from both by showing that the low-rank factors computed by PowerSGD can be transmitted directly over the noisy MAC, exploiting the superposition property for in-transit averaging while reducing the channel influence factor by O(m) relative to uncompressed Z-SGD. The error-feedback framework from Stich & Karimireddy (2020) provides the convergence backbone, extended here to account for the additional channel corruption term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration of gradient compression gains for GPT-scale language modeling over noisy wireless channels, with a clean 16× power reduction and 165× communication cost reduction backed by matching convergence theory. The practical relevance is significant for federated learning, edge AI, and wireless communication settings where error-correcting codes are too latency-expensive. Open questions include extending to downlink noise, fast fading without channel-state information, and whether the O(m) variance reduction persists for very high-rank gradients where the low-rank assumption weakens. The finding that a constant power policy outperforms dynamic schedules (due to the implicit SNR decay from shrinking gradients) is a useful practical simplification.
