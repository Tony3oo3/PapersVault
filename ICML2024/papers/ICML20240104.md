## Problem

STC-based approximation algorithms for CLUSTER DELETION had a 4-approximation guarantee (worse than the 2-approximation from the canonical LP), were randomized with guarantees only in expectation, and relied on expensive black-box LP solvers for the STC LP relaxation. The paper bridges this theory-practice gap by improving guarantees to 3, providing a simple deterministic derandomization, and giving a purely combinatorial solver for the STC LP.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240104/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithms take an unweighted undirected graph G=(V,E) and produce a set of edges E_D to delete so that G−E_D is a disjoint union of cliques, along with a lower bound on OPT_CD (either |W| from a maximal edge-disjoint open wedge set or the STC LP objective). The combinatorial STC LP solver constructs a directed graph G_st with 2m+2 nodes and 2m+2|W| edges (where |W| can be O(n³)), then solves a minimum s-t cut to recover the half-integral LP solution x_ij ∈ {0, 1/2, 1} for each edge.

## Method

The paper's core insight is that a simple degree-based pivoting strategy (selecting the maximum-degree node in the auxiliary graph at each step) provides the same 3-approximation guarantee as the more complex B_k/N_k ratio-based deterministic pivoting, while running in O(m) time and space instead of Ω(|W|+m). For the combinatorial STC LP solver, the half-integral LP is reformulated as a binary program with two variables per edge (y_ij, z_ij) and constraints z_ik ≤ y_jk for each open wedge, which is then reduced directly to a minimum s-t cut via infinite-weight edges encoding the constraints. A separate O(m^1.5)-time algorithm for finding maximal edge-disjoint open wedge sets improves on the previous O(mn) bound by exploiting the fact that each triangle is visited at most 3 times and each open wedge addition deletes two edges permanently.

## Results

The paper improves the approximation guarantee for both MatchFlipPivot (MFP) and STC LP rounding from 4 to 3, and proves the 3-approximation for MFP is tight (Theorem 3.3). In practice, the degree-based deterministic variant (DegMFP) achieves a posteriori approximation ratios of 1.7–2.0 across 34 large SNAP graphs, consistently outperforming the randomized baseline (RanMFP) which achieves ratios of 1.95–2.05. The combinatorial STC LP solver (Comb-LP) is roughly 2× faster than Gurobi where both succeed, and scales to graphs an order of magnitude larger (1.97M nodes / 2.77M edges vs. 34.5K nodes / 421K edges). DegMFP runs in O(m) time for the pivoting step versus Ω(|W|+m) for the previous deterministic scheme, and the overall MFP runtime improves from O(mn) to O(m^1.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the randomized MatchFlipPivot (RanMFP) from Veldt (2022), which uses uniform random pivots and was previously shown to be a 4-approximation. DegMFP (the paper's degree-based deterministic pivot) consistently achieves better approximation ratios than RanMFP-100 (best of 100 random trials) across all 34 graphs, e.g., 1.825 vs. 2.043 on COM-DBLP and 1.465 vs. 1.705 on CA-HEPPh, while running in comparable or less time (8.3s vs. 17.5s on ROADNET-CA). Against the previous deterministic scheme RatMFP (Pivot Strategy 2, minimizing |B_k|/|N_k|), DegMFP achieves nearly identical approximation ratios but is 10–100× faster (e.g., 8.3s vs. 188.8s on ROADNET-CA; 138.6s vs. 17,441s on COM-ORKUT). For the STC LP solver, the combinatorial min-cut approach (Comb-LP) is ~2× faster than Gurobi where both succeed (e.g., 41.9s vs. 133.2s on CA-ASTROPH) and solves 21 of 34 graphs versus Gurobi's 6 of 34, with the largest solvable graph being ~57× larger in edge count. However, Comb-LP rounding sometimes produces slightly worse CLUSTER DELETION solutions than MFP (e.g., ratio 1.924 vs. 1.855 on CA-ASTROPH), though it always provides a tighter lower bound, so combining MFP's solution with the STC LP lower bound yields the best a posteriori guarantee.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the STC-labeling-plus-Pivot framework introduced by Veldt (2022), which itself builds on the MINSTC problem (Sintos & Tsaparas, 2014) and the Pivot algorithm (Ailon et al., 2008). The key departure is showing that a simple degree-based pivoting strategy achieves the same deterministic 3-approximation as the more complex B_k/N_k ratio-based derandomization of van Zuylen & Williamson (2009), while also providing a purely combinatorial min-cut reduction for the STC LP that replaces black-box LP solvers. The paper positions itself as bridging the theory-practice gap: improving approximation guarantees from 4 to 3 while simultaneously making the algorithms dramatically faster and more scalable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3-approximation is tight for MatchFlipPivot (Theorem 3.3), closing a question about whether the prior 4-guarantee was an artifact of loose analysis. The combinatorial STC LP solver's ability to handle graphs an order of magnitude larger than Gurobi (2.7M edges vs. 420K) makes LP-based rounding practically viable for the first time on large real-world networks. Open questions include whether the 3-approximation for the STC LP rounding algorithm can be improved (the analysis is not tight there), whether the special min-cut structure can be exploited beyond off-the-shelf flow solvers, and whether these ideas extend to CLUSTER EDITING. The cluster merging heuristic, while not optimized, suggests that practical performance is often well below the worst-case guarantee.
