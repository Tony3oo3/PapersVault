## Problem

Existing inference-time alignment methods like FUDGE and COLD use prefix scorers to steer a frozen language model toward high-reward outputs, but they lack a principled learning framework that connects the scorer to a well-defined RL objective. The paper asks: can we formalize a tokenwise KL-regularized RL objective for language model decoding and design a modular, provably correct solver that keeps the base model frozen while offering inference-time configurability over the reward?

## Input / Output

At training time, the prefix scorer receives a prompt and a partially decoded response prefix [x, y^t] and outputs a scalar value estimate V_θ([x, y^t]) approximating the expected cumulative reward of completing the response under the base policy. At inference time, the frozen base LM produces next-token logits for all vocabulary items given [x, y^t], and the prefix scorer produces value estimates for each candidate next token; these are combined (tokenwise) or used to rerank K sampled blocks of M tokens (blockwise) to produce the aligned continuation. The reward signal is only assigned at the EOS token, making the value function a look-ahead estimate over the remaining decoding horizon.

## Method

The paper formulates a tokenwise KL-regularized RL objective J_λ = λA − D where A is the advantage of the next-token policy over the base policy and D is the tokenwise KL divergence, then proves (Theorem 2.1) that the unique optimal policy has the closed form π*(z|x,y^t) ∝ π_ref(z|x,y^t)·exp(λV*(x,y^t,z)), reducing alignment to learning the value function V*. Two training procedures are provided: CD-FUDGE, which regresses the prefix scorer onto the terminal reward over on-policy rollouts (proven to converge to the optimal value function under regularity conditions), and CD-Q, an off-policy Bellman-backup method inspired by DQN that uses a stop-gradient target v_t = Σ_z π_ref(z|x,y^t)·V_θ(x,y^t,z) and can be trained on pre-collected data without base-model rollouts. At inference, the prefix scorer is applied in two modes: tokenwise sampling, which linearly combines base-LM logits with log-value estimates to sample each token, and blockwise best-of-K, which samples K continuations of length M from the base policy and selects the one with the highest prefix score, bridging the gap between sequence-level best-of-K and tokenwise RL. The framework is modular: multiple prefix scorers for different rewards can be combined at inference time to solve multi-objective RL without retraining, and a scorer trained on one base model transfers to an unseen base model without further tuning.

## Results

On the response length task (DSTC8 Reddit), blockwise CD-Q with K=6 achieves nearly identical length and KL divergence as best-of-K with K=50, a 10× efficiency gain. On the helpfulness/harmlessness (HH) task (Anthropic HH), blockwise CD-Q substantially outperforms tokenwise CD and all training-time baselines (PPO, DPO, IPO) in win-rate vs. KL tradeoffs, though it does not fully match best-of-K. On summarization (TL;DR), blockwise CD-Q outperforms IPO but again falls short of best-of-K. The CD-Q prefix scorer also transfers to unseen base models (PaLM 2-S, PaLM 2-XS) without retraining, retaining performance on par with best-of-K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KL-regularized PPO, blockwise CD-Q achieves strictly better reward-KL tradeoffs on all three tasks (length, HH, summarization), consistent with prior findings that best-of-K dominates PPO. Against DPO, blockwise CD-Q outperforms DPO alone, and the combination 'DPO + CD-Q (blockwise)' with K=8 matches 'CD-Q (blockwise)' with K=32, indicating a 4× sample efficiency gain; under a fixed throughput budget, CD-Q (blockwise) also beats 'DPO + best-of-K' for all K values tested. Against IPO, blockwise CD-Q clearly outperforms on both HH and summarization win-rate at comparable KL budgets. Against best-of-K, blockwise CD-Q matches or nearly matches the reward-KL frontier while requiring up to 10× fewer samples (e.g., K=6 vs. K=50 on length), though best-of-K retains a slight edge on HH and summarization win-rates at matched KL. The paper also shows that CD's modularity enables multi-objective control (combining HH and length prefix scorers) at inference time without retraining, something PPO/DPO/IPO cannot do.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: controlled decoding via prefix scorers (FUDGE, Yang & Klein 2021) and KL-regularized reinforcement learning for language model alignment. The key theoretical contribution is proving that FUDGE's prefix scorer, when trained on base-model rollouts, converges to the value function of the tokenwise KL-regularized RL objective, and that a DQN-style Bellman backup (CD-Q) provides an off-policy alternative. This positions CD as a modular inference-time add-on that sits conceptually between sequence-level best-of-K sampling and tokenwise RL methods like PPO/DPO, bridging the gap between them through the proposed blockwise decoding strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in modularity: a single prefix scorer can be combined with multiple reward scorers at inference time to solve multi-objective alignment without retraining, and it transfers to unseen base models (PaLM 2-S, PaLM 2-XS) without further tuning—capabilities that training-time methods (PPO, DPO, IPO) fundamentally lack. The 10× reduction in K needed to match best-of-K performance has direct implications for inference cost in production systems. Open questions include improving prefix scorer accuracy (currently ~0.6 vs ~0.7 for dedicated reward models), understanding why blockwise CD underperforms best-of-K on some tasks (HH, summarization), and whether the tokenwise RL formulation's theoretical optimality translates to the sequence-level tradeoffs that practitioners actually care about.
