## Problem

Existing equivariant GNNs for 3D dynamics (e.g., EGNN) only predict the next state from the current state, failing to capture temporal correlations along the full trajectory. This limits their ability to model how systems evolve over time and leads to error accumulation when rolled out iteratively. The paper addresses the challenge of directly modeling the entire future trajectory as a temporal function while preserving SE(3)-equivariance in 3D Euclidean space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242265/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single current 3D geometric graph (node features such as atom types/charges, plus directional features like positions and velocities) and produces a full trajectory of future states over a configurable time window ΔT, decoded in parallel with one forward pass. The input features are repeated P times and concatenated with sinusoidal time embeddings before processing. A key property is discretization invariance: the model can be trained at P timesteps and zero-shot generalize to 2P timesteps by interpolating time embeddings, without retraining.

## Method

EGNO formulates dynamics as a neural operator mapping a current state to a temporal function, and learns it by stacking equivariant temporal convolution layers with standard EGNN layers. The temporal convolution operates in Fourier space: it applies FFT over the temporal dimension, multiplies by a block-diagonal complex kernel matrix (separate M^h for invariant node features and M^Z for directional features), then applies inverse FFT. SE(3)-equivariance is preserved because Fourier transforms commute with rotations, the M^Z kernel performs scalar multiplications on 3D vectors (which commute with rotation matrices), and the nonlinearity on directional features uses an equivariant activation σ(||·||)·(·). Translation equivariance is handled by subtracting the center of mass before the temporal convolution and adding it back afterward. EGNN layers operate on node and channel dimensions within each timestep, while temporal layers operate on temporal and channel dimensions, treating the temporal axis as a batch dimension.

## Results

EGNO achieves the best F-MSE across all eight MD17 molecules, with a 36% relative improvement over its EGNN backbone on Aspirin (9.18 vs. 14.41 ×10⁻²). On the N-body simulation, EGNO attains F-MSE of 0.0054 (S2S) and A-MSE of 0.0022 (S2T), an 18% improvement over EGNN. On CMU Motion Capture, EGNO reduces F-MSE by up to ~52% on average (e.g., 8.1 vs. 28.7 on Walk, 33.9 vs. 50.9 on Run). On the AdK protein benchmark, EGHNO (EGNO with EGHN backbone) achieves F-MSE of 1.801, outperforming the prior SOTA EGHN (2.034).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EGNO consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset. Against EGNN (the primary backbone), EGNO achieves 18% lower F-MSE on N-body (0.0054 vs. 0.0071), 36% lower on Aspirin (9.18 vs. 14.41), and ~52% lower on average for motion capture. The trajectory-based variants EGNN-R (iterative roll-out) and EGNN-S (sequential readout) are both clearly inferior: EGNN-R suffers severe error accumulation (e.g., F-MSE 14.51 on Aspirin vs. EGNO's 9.18; 816.7 on Mocap-Run vs. 33.9), while EGNN-S, though closer, still lags behind EGNO because it does not explicitly model temporal correlations in the frequency domain. Among the non-EGNN baselines (TFN, SE(3)-Tr, MPNN, RF, ClofNet), EGNO achieves the lowest F-MSE on N-body (0.0054 vs. best baseline ClofNet at 0.0065) and dominates on all MD17 molecules. On the protein benchmark, EGHNO (EGNO stacked into the EGHN hierarchy) improves over EGHN from 2.034 to 1.801 F-MSE, confirming that the equivariant temporal convolution is a plug-and-play module compatible with different equivariant backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research lines: equivariant graph neural networks (EGNN, TFN, SE(3)-Transformers) that model spatial interactions in 3D but only predict the next state, and Fourier Neural Operators (FNO) that learn maps between function spaces but operate on scalar or non-directional outputs. EGNO departs from both by formulating 3D dynamics as a function over time and learning an equivariant operator to approximate it, using a novel equivariant temporal convolution layer parameterized in Fourier space. The key conceptual departure is that prior equivariant GNNs treat time as a sequential prediction problem (roll-out or sequential readout), while prior FNO work does not handle directional 3D features requiring SE(3) symmetry; EGNO is the first to unify these, with a formal equivariance proof (Theorem 4.1) showing that Fourier-space kernel operations on 3D vectors preserve SO(3) equivariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EGNO provides a practical and principled framework for 3D dynamics modeling with two notable advantages: (1) a single forward pass decodes an entire trajectory in parallel rather than requiring iterative roll-out, and (2) the model is discretization-invariant, enabling zero-shot inference at different temporal resolutions without retraining. The equivariant temporal convolution layer is modular and composable with any EGNN backbone (demonstrated with both EGNN and EGHN), suggesting broad applicability. Open questions include scaling to more complex dynamics (fluids, deformable materials, astronomical systems) and whether the Fourier-based temporal modeling extends to non-uniform or event-driven time discretizations. The ~3× data efficiency gain and large margins (36–52%) over strong equivariant baselines across four diverse domains make this a practically relevant contribution for the geometric ML and scientific ML communities.
