## Problem

Optimal data collection for policy evaluation in tabular MDPs has been studied without safety constraints, achieving O(n^{-3/2}) regret, but practical deployments require the behavior policy to maintain a cumulative cost no worse than a constant fraction of a known baseline. No prior work provided finite-time regret guarantees for safe data collection in the MDP setting (only asymptotic results existed for bandits), and it was unclear whether the faster O(n^{-3/2}) rate was even achievable under such constraints.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target policy π, a risk parameter α ∈ (0,1], a tabular MDP with known transition dynamics P but unknown reward/cost distributions, and a deterministic baseline safe policy π₀ (action 0 in each state). It produces a dataset D of K episodes × L steps (n = KL total samples) collected adaptively, from which a certainty equivalence estimator Y_n^π(s₁) is computed. The safety constraint is enforced at every episode k: the cumulative constraint value of all behavior policies must exceed (1−α)·k·V_c^{π₀}(s₁), making the constraint path-dependent rather than only terminal.

## Method

SaVeR (Safe Variance Reduction) alternates between three policies based on a running safety budget: a plug-in optimal sampling proportion b̂* (derived from the unconstrained oracle formula using upper confidence bound variance estimates) when the budget is positive and past √K episodes, the baseline π₀ when the budget is negative, and an exploration policy πₓ for the first √K episodes to estimate constraint means. The key mechanism is a deterministic action selection rule (argmax of b̂*/T ratio) that tracks the optimal proportions without random sampling, preventing inadvertent safety violations. The paper first proves a tractability condition characterizing when the safe oracle can achieve O(n^{-3/2}) regret, establishes the first lower bound for this setting, and shows SaVeR's regret matches it. For DAG MDPs (where closed-form optimal proportions are unavailable due to multiple paths to a state), an iterative value-iteration-like procedure approximates the sampling proportions.

## Results

Across four experimental settings (bandit with A=11, MovieLens-1M with A=30, a 4-depth 2-action tree MDP with 15 states, and a 4×4 stochastic grid MDP), SaVeR consistently achieves lower MSE than both SEPEC and safe on-policy as the number of episodes increases. In the tree MDP setting, SaVeR eventually matches the oracle's MSE. All algorithms satisfy the safety constraint (cumulative constraint violation remains below zero), but SaVeR and the oracle avoid excessive conservatism by not oversampling the safe baseline action, unlike SEPEC and safe on-policy which allocate disproportionate samples to the safe action at the cost of higher MSE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SaVeR outperforms SEPEC (Wan et al., 2022) in both the bandit and MovieLens-1M settings, achieving lower MSE at every episode count while maintaining the safety constraint. Against safe on-policy (which follows the target policy when the safety budget is positive and falls back to the baseline policy otherwise), SaVeR achieves substantially lower MSE in all four settings, with the gap widening as more episodes are collected. In the tree MDP experiment, SaVeR converges to the same MSE as the oracle (which knows true reward variances), demonstrating that the plug-in variance estimation is effective. In the grid MDP (a DAG structure beyond the tree MDP theoretical guarantees), SaVeR still outperforms safe on-policy, validating the DAG extension via iterative value-iteration-style estimation. A key practical distinction is that SEPEC and safe on-policy oversample the safe action to maintain the constraint, whereas SaVeR allocates samples more efficiently toward variance reduction while still respecting the safety budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: optimal data collection for policy evaluation (Carpentier & Munos 2011/2012; Mukherjee et al. 2022a) and safe sequential decision-making (Zhu & Kveton 2021/2022; Wan et al. 2022). The unconstrained MDP data collection literature achieves Õ(n^{-3/2}) regret via variance-aware stratified sampling, while the safe bandit literature provides only asymptotic guarantees. This paper is the first to combine both: it introduces safety constraints into the MDP data collection problem and provides the first finite-time regret guarantees in this setting, departing from prior constrained MDP work (Efroni et al. 2020; Vaswani et al. 2022) which targets policy improvement rather than evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The intractability result (Proposition 1) is a notable conceptual contribution: it shows that even an oracle with full variance knowledge cannot achieve the desired Õ(n^{-3/2}) rate in certain MDPs, meaning the safety constraint fundamentally alters the landscape of achievable performance. The tractability condition and matching upper/lower bounds provide a clean characterization of when safe data collection is feasible. However, the scope is limited to tabular and tree-structured MDPs, and the DAG extension relies on an iterative approximation without formal guarantees. Extending these results to linear/contextual settings and general MDPs remains open, as does understanding how the safety constraint interacts with function approximation.
