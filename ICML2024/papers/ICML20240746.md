## Problem

When machine learning practitioners replace the standard expected-loss criterion with risk-sensitive alternatives (CVaR, DRO, tilted ERM), it is unclear whether the resulting optimizer actually differs from vanilla ERM. This paper formalizes 'criterion collapse'—the phenomenon where optimizing one criterion implies optimality in another—and shows that under Bernoulli losses the collapse extends far beyond the previously known CVaR and DRO cases, while under surrogate losses with expressive models, monotonic criteria like tilted ERM are provably unable to avoid collapse into error probability minimizers.

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework operates on arbitrary loss distributions over a data space, mapping random losses to scalar criteria via functions like OCE, quantiles, or variantile. The empirical validation uses standard RGB image classification: CIFAR-10/100 (32×32) with ResNet-34, SVHN with ResNet-18, and FashionMNIST with a single-hidden-layer feed-forward network, all producing 10 or 100 class labels. The base surrogate loss is multi-class cross-entropy, and the learning criterion is applied on top of this fixed loss to produce different training dynamics.

## Method

The paper introduces two loss-restraining criteria, C_inn(L;θ) = ρ̃(E[L]−θ) and C_out(L;θ) = E[ρ̃(L−θ)], where ρ̃ is a non-monotonic convex function (e.g., absolute value or √(u²+1)−1) that penalizes deviation from a threshold θ. The key theoretical result (Proposition 3.1) shows that for any classification-calibrated margin penalizer and strictly increasing OCE dispersion ρ, minimizing the OCE surrogate over all measurable scoring functions necessarily drives error probability to its infimum, making collapse unavoidable. In contrast, Proposition 3.4 proves that C_inn and C_out can avoid this collapse by construction, since their non-monotonicity allows solutions with non-minimal error probability to be optimal. Empirically, these criteria correspond to the Flooding and SoftAD ascent-descent algorithms, and experiments across four image classification benchmarks show that when hyperparameters are tuned for validation accuracy, the non-monotonic methods achieve substantially better average surrogate loss and smaller model norms than monotonic alternatives (CVaR, DRO, Tilted ERM) while matching test accuracy.

## Results

The paper is primarily theoretical, with empirical results serving as supplementary validation. Across all four datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, FashionMNIST, SVHN), all five methods achieve near-identical test accuracy, confirming that criterion choice does not affect classification performance. The key differentiator is in surrogate loss and model norm: the non-monotonic methods (Flooding and SoftAD) achieve substantially lower average test surrogate loss than the monotonic methods (CVaR, DRO, Tilted ERM), and SoftAD uniquely also realizes a small model L2 norm. Notably, Tilted ERM always selects γ=0 (vanilla ERM) when hyperparameters are tuned for validation accuracy, confirming the theoretical prediction that monotonic criteria collapse to error probability minimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares five learning criteria (CVaR, DRO, Flooding, SoftAD, Tilted ERM) under a common multi-class cross-entropy base loss on ResNet-34/18 and a feed-forward network. In terms of test accuracy, all methods are essentially tied, as predicted by the theory that all criteria collapse to error probability minimizers under Bernoulli losses. The critical distinction emerges in surrogate loss and model norm: Flooding and SoftAD (non-monotonic criteria) achieve substantially lower average test surrogate loss than CVaR, DRO, and Tilted ERM (monotonic criteria). SoftAD is the only method that simultaneously achieves low surrogate loss and a small model L2 norm, suggesting it provides the best tradeoff across all three metrics. Tilted ERM is the weakest performer, always collapsing to vanilla ERM (γ=0) and exhibiting the highest surrogate loss and model norm. DRO also collapses to vanilla ERM on CIFAR-100 and FashionMNIST (ε̃=0), further confirming the theoretical collapse results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and substantially extends prior observations that DRO (Hu et al., 2018) and CVaR (Zhai et al., 2021) criteria collapse into error probability minimizers under Bernoulli losses, showing the phenomenon holds for a much broader class of criteria including OCE risks, quantiles, Cressie-Read DRO, and Orlicz-regret-based criteria. It then departs from this line by analyzing the surrogate loss setting, where the situation is fundamentally different: using the classification calibration framework of Bartlett et al. (2006), it proves that monotonic OCE criteria (including tilted ERM) cannot avoid collapse under expressive models, while non-monotonic loss-restraining criteria (encompassing Flooding and SoftAD) can. The OCE framework of Ben-Tal & Teboulle (2007) provides the mathematical backbone for the criterion family analyzed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The negative result that tilted ERM and other monotonic risk-sensitive criteria are fundamentally unable to avoid error probability minimization under expressive models challenges the assumption that risk-sensitive learning provides meaningful differentiation from vanilla ERM in practice. This has direct implications for practitioners choosing training objectives when accuracy is not the sole goal (e.g., fairness, interpretability, model complexity). Key limitations include restriction to binary classification with margin-type surrogates, and the empirical validation is limited to small-scale image classification (CIFAR, FashionMNIST, SVHN) with modest architectures. Open questions include extending the theory to multi-class and asymmetric losses, and whether the non-monotonic criterion advantage generalizes to larger-scale or different-domain settings.
