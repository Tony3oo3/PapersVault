## Problem

Sampling diffusion and flow models requires tens to hundreds of sequential function evaluations, making interactive and large-scale deployment costly. While model distillation can reduce NFEs, it is expensive to train, requires access to original training data, and can shift the generated distribution. Previous solver distillation (Bespoke Scale-Time) is limited in expressiveness, and no unified solver family provably subsumes all existing dedicated and generic ODE solvers while remaining trainable with a tiny parameter budget.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained velocity field (diffusion or flow-matching model), an initial noise sample x_0, and task-specific conditioning (class label for ImageNet, text prompt for T2I at 512×512 in a 64×64×4 latent space, or frame-aligned transcript + audio prompt for speech). It outputs a generated sample in 4–20 NFEs versus 160–320+ for the ground-truth adaptive RK45 solver. The solver itself is a set of <200 scalar parameters (time discretization plus per-step coefficients) trained on only 520 (x_0, x(1)) pairs, making it orders of magnitude cheaper to optimize than the underlying model.

## Method

BNS solvers are drawn from the Non-Stationary (NS) family, where the i-th update rule takes an arbitrary linear combination of all previously visited trajectory points x_0,…,x_i and all previously evaluated velocities u_0,…,u_i. A key structural result (Proposition 3.1) shows this is equivalently and uniquely parameterized as x_{i+1} = x_0·a_i + U_i·b_i, reducing the per-step parameters to one scalar and one vector. The solver is trained by gradient descent on a PSNR loss against high-accuracy RK45 reference samples, initialized from a generic RK solver (Euler or Midpoint) with an optional scheduler-change preconditioning (scaling σ_0) to improve conditioning under high CFG. Theorem 3.2 proves the NS family strictly subsumes all prior solver classes—Runge-Kutta, Multistep, Exponential Integrators, and Scale-Time solvers—explaining the consistent PSNR gains of 5–10 dB over the best baselines at matched NFE.

## Results

On class-conditional ImageNet-64 (FM-OT), BNS achieves 45.64 PSNR and 1.78 FID at 16 NFE, improving at least 5–10 dB in PSNR over the runner-up baseline (DPM++) and reaching within 5% of the GT solver's FID (~1.68 at 160–320 NFE). On the 512×512 Text-to-Image FM-OT model, BNS achieves 29.13 PSNR at 16 NFE (guidance w=2.0) versus 16.28 for RK-Midpoint and 14.86 for RK-Euler, and 21.23 PSNR at 16 NFE (w=6.5) versus 9.98 for RK-Midpoint. On audio generation, BNS consistently improves SNR by 1–3 dB over the best baseline across all 8 evaluation datasets. BNS requires fewer than 200 trainable parameters and 0.2–1% of the GPU-days used to train the underlying diffusion/flow model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BNS outperforms BST (Shaul et al., 2023) in PSNR across all NFEs on ImageNet-64/128, consistent with the theoretical solver hierarchy (BNS > BST > DPM > RK) proven in Theorem 3.2. Against DPM++ (Zhang & Chen, 2023) and UniPC (Zhao et al., 2023), BNS achieves at least 5–10 dB higher PSNR on ImageNet-64 and substantially better FID at low NFE (e.g., 1.78 vs. ~2.5+ FID at 16 NFE). On T2I, BNS improves PSNR by over 10 dB compared to RK-Midpoint and RK-Euler at all NFEs, and also outperforms DDIM (Song et al., 2022) and DPM++ (Lu et al., 2022) by 6–12 dB in PSNR (Table 5). Against Progressive Distillation (Salimans & Ho, 2022), BNS achieves comparable FID in the 8–16 NFE regime (e.g., 2.62 vs. 2.97 FID at 16 NFE on ImageNet-64) while using only 0.5% of the forward passes and 168 parameters vs. >200M; however, BNS loses to PD at NFE < 8 (e.g., 31.83 vs. 4.79 FID at 4 NFE on ImageNet-64). On CIFAR-10, BNS outperforms DPM-solver-v3 (Zheng et al., 2023a) at NFE ≤ 10 (e.g., 2.31 vs. 3.50 FID at 8 NFE) and is on par at NFE ≥ 12.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the solver distillation line initiated by Shaul et al. (2023), which searched over Scale-Time (ST) transformations to find model-specific solvers (BST solvers). The key conceptual leap is the introduction of the Non-Stationary (NS) solver family, where each step can form an arbitrary linear combination of all previously computed trajectory points and velocity evaluations, rather than being restricted to a fixed base solver (RK, Multistep) applied after an ST transformation. The paper proves (Theorem 3.2) that NS solvers subsume all previously known solver families—generic RK/Multistep, dedicated solvers (EDM, Exponential Integrators/DPM++), and ST-based solvers—providing a complete taxonomy. In contrast to model distillation methods (Progressive Distillation, Consistency Models) that fine-tune the full network, BNS keeps the model frozen and optimizes fewer than 200 solver parameters, requiring no access to training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: BNS solvers achieve 45 PSNR / 1.76 FID at 16 NFE on ImageNet-64, nearly closing the gap with Progressive Distillation in the 8–16 NFE regime while using 0.5% of the training forwards and <200 parameters versus >200M. This makes fast-sampling solver optimization accessible without the compute cost or data requirements of model distillation. Key limitations remain: BNS does not reach the extremely low NFE regime (1–4), requires a separate solver per NFE count, and relies on CFG for T2I generation. Open questions include whether a single solver can handle variable NFE without degradation, and whether further increases in solver expressiveness can push into the 1–4 NFE range.
