## Problem

While selective SSMs like Mamba match or outperform Transformers at small-to-medium scale, their development has remained disjoint from the Transformer community's collective algorithmic and systems optimizations. SSMs cannot leverage matrix multiplication units on modern accelerators, making them harder to train efficiently and understand theoretically. The paper asks whether a rich theoretical bridge between SSMs and attention variants can be built to transfer these optimizations and enable larger, faster sequence models.

## Input / Output

The SSD layer consumes an input sequence X ∈ ℝ^(T,P) along with per-timestep scalar decay factors A_t ∈ [0,1], and input/output projection vectors B_t, C_t ∈ ℝ^N, producing an output Y ∈ ℝ^(T,P). The state expansion factor N (typically 64–128) is a free parameter that controls information capacity without the linear-in-N slowdown of Mamba's scan. In the dual attention interpretation, B, C, X are renamed K, Q, V respectively, and the scalar-identity SSM recurrence becomes a structured mask L on the attention logits, replacing both the softmax and positional embeddings with a single data-dependent 1-semiseparable mask.

## Method

The paper establishes that any SSM is exactly matrix multiplication by a semiseparable matrix (Theorem 3.4), revealing that the recurrent (linear) and quadratic (attention-like) forms are simply two different pairwise contraction orderings of the same 4-way tensor contraction. This duality is generalized to Structured Masked Attention (SMA), where any structured matrix L yields a model with both a linear and a quadratic mode. The key algorithmic contribution is a hybrid SSD algorithm that block-decomposes the semiseparable matrix: diagonal blocks are computed in the quadratic attention mode (exploiting tensor cores via matmul), while off-diagonal blocks are factored through the low-rank semiseparable structure and reduced to a smaller recurrence, achieving O(TN²) training FLOPs with work dominated by matrix multiplications. The Mamba-2 architecture further simplifies Mamba by moving all data-dependent projections (A, B, C) to the beginning of the block in parallel—mirroring the Q, K, V projection pattern in Transformers—enabling the SSD layer to be computed with standard matmul-heavy kernels rather than custom scan implementations.

## Results

On zero-shot downstream evaluations (Table 1), Mamba-2-2.7B achieves 60.2% average accuracy across 7 tasks, outperforming Mamba-2.8B (59.9%), Pythia-2.8B (55.7%), and RWKV4-3B (56.4%) at similar or larger parameter counts. On the Pile validation set, Mamba-2-2.7B achieves 6.09 perplexity, beating Mamba-2.8B (6.22) and Pythia-2.8B (6.73). In speed benchmarks, the SSD algorithm is 2–8× faster than Mamba's fused scan at large state expansion (N=64) and surpasses FlashAttention-2 at sequence lengths ≥2K. On the MQAR associative recall task, Mamba-2 significantly outperforms Mamba-1 and even vanilla softmax attention across all tested settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mamba-2 consistently outperforms Mamba at every model size: at ~2.8B parameters, Mamba-2-2.7B achieves 60.2% average accuracy vs. Mamba-2.8B's 59.9%, and 6.09 vs. 6.22 Pile perplexity. Against Pythia (same tokenizer, dataset, and 300B training tokens), Mamba-2-2.7B beats Pythia-2.8B by 4.5 points in average accuracy (60.2% vs. 55.7%) and 0.64 in Pile perplexity, and the paper notes it even outperforms Pythia-6.9B. Mamba-2 also surpasses RWKV4-3B (60.2% vs. 56.4% avg accuracy) and Hybrid H3-2.7B (60.2% vs. 54.5%). In the MQAR associative recall task, Mamba-2 significantly outperforms both Mamba-1 and vanilla softmax attention, while the Based architecture (linear attention + local attention) serves as a competitive but inferior baseline. In speed benchmarks, SSD is 2–8× faster than Mamba's optimized fused scan at N=64 and crosses over FlashAttention-2 at sequence length 2K, being 6× faster at 16K. In scaling law experiments (Figure 4), Mamba-2 Pareto-dominates both Mamba and a strong Transformer++ recipe on perplexity, theoretical FLOPs, and wall-clock time simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously parallel research threads—structured state space models (S4, Mamba) and efficient attention variants (linear attention, RetNet, GateLoop)—by showing they are both instances of semiseparable matrix multiplication. It builds directly on Mamba's selective SSM by restricting the state transition to scalar-identity structure and re-deriving the computation through the lens of structured matrix algorithms, and it generalizes linear attention (Katharopoulos et al., 2020) to structured masked attention (SMA) where the mask can be any structured matrix. The Mamba-2 architecture change (parallel projections of A, B, C, X) is explicitly motivated by the analogy to Q, K, V projections in standard attention, marking a conceptual departure from Mamba's sequential projection design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The semiseparable matrix framework provides a principled bridge that allows algorithmic and systems optimizations from the Transformer ecosystem (e.g., FlashAttention-style tiling, tensor-core exploitation) to be transferred to SSMs, and vice versa. Practically, the SSD algorithm achieves 2–8× speedup over Mamba's selective scan while supporting 8× larger state sizes, and Mamba-2 Pareto-dominates both Mamba and Transformer++ on perplexity and wall-clock time. Open questions include whether the framework extends to more general (diagonal) A structures, whether attention-sink phenomena and interpretability tools transfer to SSMs, and whether the efficiency gap at short sequence lengths (where Transformers' MLP layers are extremely hardware-efficient) can be closed. The paper also notes that SSD does not subsume softmax attention, so the recall-throughput tradeoff between constant-state SSMs and full-history attention remains an active area.
