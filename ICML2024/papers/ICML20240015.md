## Problem

Probabilistic generating circuits (PGCs) were introduced to unify probabilistic circuits (PCs) and determinantal point processes (DPPs), but it was unclear whether their greater expressiveness stemmed from the generating-polynomial representation or from the allowance of negative weights. Additionally, PGCs were defined only for binary variables, leaving open whether tractable marginalization extends to categorical variables with larger image sizes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240015/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The transformation takes a PGC (an arithmetic circuit computing a probability generating polynomial) over n binary random variables of size s and produces a nonmonotone PC (an arithmetic circuit with potentially negative edge weights) of size poly(s, n) that computes the corresponding probability mass function as a set-multilinear polynomial. The output PC supports O(s) time marginalization over arbitrary subsets of categorical variable values, not just full assignments. The set-multilinearity and probability-distribution properties are semantic (not syntactically checkable in polynomial time for the latter), which motivates the compositional operations that preserve these properties by construction.

## Method

The key insight is that PGCs are PCs in disguise: the power comes from negative weights, not the generating-polynomial representation. The transformation substitutes each generating variable z_i with x_i/x̄_i in the PGC circuit, multiplies by ∏x̄_i, and then applies Strassen's division-elimination theorem to remove the resulting division gates, yielding a circuit of size O(s·n·log n·log log n) that computes a set-multilinear polynomial. The paper further proves that PGCs over quaternary variables cannot support tractable marginalization unless NP=P (via a reduction from counting perfect matchings in 3-regular bipartite graphs), establishing that the binary restriction is fundamental. Finally, it shows that any arithmetic formula can be represented as an affine projection of a DPP, implying that separating nonmonotone PCs from DPPs would resolve the long-open formula-vs-circuit separation in algebraic complexity.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental benchmarks. The key quantitative result is that any PGC of size s over n binary variables can be converted into a nonmonotone PC of size poly(s, n) (specifically O(s·n·log n·log log n) using fast polynomial multiplication, or O(s·n) via interpolation) that computes the same probability distribution as a set-multilinear polynomial. The paper proves that PGCs over quaternary random variables do not support tractable marginalization unless NP = P (Theorem 7.1), and that nonmonotone PCs computing set-multilinear polynomials support O(s)-time marginalization for categorical variables of arbitrary image size (Theorem 9.3). Additionally, any arithmetic formula can be represented as an affine projection of a DPP of size linear in the formula size (Theorem 11.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper establishes a hierarchy of expressiveness and tractability among model classes rather than comparing empirical performance. PGCs over binary variables are shown to be equivalent to nonmonotone PCs computing set-multilinear polynomials (Theorem 8.1), meaning the apparent power of PGCs comes from allowing negative weights, not from the generating-polynomial representation. Nonmonotone PCs strictly subsume both monotone PCs (which require nonnegative weights) and DPPs (which can only model negative correlations). PGCs over quaternary variables are shown to be strictly less tractable than nonmonotone PCs: while PGCs cannot support efficient marginalization (Theorem 7.1), nonmonotone PCs computing set-multilinear polynomials handle arbitrary categorical variables in O(s) time (Theorem 9.3). The relationship between nonmonotone PCs and DPPs (with affine projections) remains open and is shown to be at least as hard as the long-standing formula-vs-circuit separation problem in algebraic complexity (Theorem 11.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets the probabilistic generating circuits (PGCs) introduced by Zhang et al. (ICML 2021) as nonmonotone probabilistic circuits (PCs) with negative weights, showing that the generating-polynomial representation is not the source of PGCs' expressiveness over standard PCs or DPPs. The transformation from PGCs to nonmonotone PCs relies on Strassen's (1973) division-elimination technique to remove the rational expressions introduced by the change of variables. The paper also extends the PC framework of Choi et al. (2020) by dropping the nonnegativity constraint on edge weights and replacing syntactic decomposability with the semantic property of computing a set-multilinear polynomial, thereby connecting tractable probabilistic inference to algebraic complexity theory (formulas vs. circuits, DPPs as affine projections).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reframing of PGCs as nonmonotone PCs simplifies the landscape of tractable probabilistic models and suggests that future work on PGCs can be conducted entirely within the PC framework, potentially unifying toolkits. The hardness result for marginalization over non-binary PGCs (implying NP=P) provides a clear boundary for where PGCs can be practically deployed. A major open question the paper highlights is whether nonmonotone PCs strictly subsume DPPs under simple compositional operations; resolving this would imply a separation between algebraic formulas and circuits, a decades-old open problem in algebraic complexity theory.
