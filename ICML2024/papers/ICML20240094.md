## Problem

Diffusion models have become the state-of-the-art for adversarial purification, but existing guidance strategies (classifier guidance, classifier-free guidance) rely on label or text conditions that are unavailable when purifying adversarial examples. The paper asks whether contrastive guidance can theoretically and empirically steer the adversarial distribution toward the clean data distribution during reverse diffusion, and derives the proper forward noise level from a feature-learning perspective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240094/cropped_page15_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes adversarial RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, variable-size for GTSRB, 224×224 for ImageNet) and outputs purified images that are fed to a frozen classifier. The purification is plug-and-play: no classifier retraining is required, and the forward diffusion stops at a dataset-specific time t* (e.g., 0.075–0.125) rather than running to full noise, preserving global label semantics while erasing local adversarial perturbations.

## Method

The forward process adds Gaussian noise to the adversarial example up to a theoretically derived time t*, balancing attack removal against semantic preservation. The reverse process uses an SDEINT solver whose drift coefficient augments the learned score function ε_θ with a contrastive-loss gradient: ε̃_θ = ε_θ + λ∇_x ℓ(x_a, x_p; τ). The contrastive loss is either InfoNCE (theoretically guaranteed to enhance signal-direction learning) or hard-negative-mining (empirically stronger), with positive pairs taken from the previous reverse-diffusion timestep and negative pairs from the same minibatch. A feature extractor F(·) is trained per minibatch to compute similarities, and the entire pipeline requires no modification to the original diffusion model training. Theoretically, the contrastive gradient decomposes into a positive-pair term aligned with the signal direction y_iμ and a negative-pair term that reinforces the same direction, jointly boosting recovery of the correct label during denoising.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 against AutoAttack ℓ∞ (ε=8/255), the proposed method achieves 82.81% robust accuracy with WideResNet-28-10 (91.41% standard accuracy), outperforming DiffPure (70.64% robust) by 12.17% and the best adversarial training baseline (67.73% robust) by 15.08%. With WideResNet-70-16, it reaches 82.81% robust accuracy (92.97% standard), surpassing DiffPure by 11.52%. On GTSRB against the same attack, the method improves robust accuracy from 42.19% (DiffPure) to 69.53% (+27.34%) while also gaining 1.56% in standard accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiffPure (Nie et al., 2022), the proposed method consistently wins on robust accuracy: +12.17% on CIFAR-10 ℓ∞ (WRN-28-10), +11.52% on CIFAR-10 ℓ∞ (WRN-70-16), +5.80% on CIFAR-10 ℓ₂ (WRN-28-10), and +27.34% on GTSRB ℓ∞, while maintaining comparable or slightly higher standard accuracy. Against the strongest adversarial training baselines (Wang et al. 2023, Cui et al. 2023), the method achieves substantially higher robust accuracy (82.81% vs. 70.69% and 67.73% on CIFAR-10 ℓ∞) without requiring any adversarial examples during training, though it trails by 1–3% in standard accuracy. Against GDMP (Wang et al., 2022) on BPDA+EOT (Table 4), the method achieves 81.94% robust accuracy vs. 81.40% for DiffPure at t*=0.1, with a 1.59% standard accuracy gain. Against older purification methods (EBM+LD, DSM+LD, PixelDefend, ME-Net in Table 4), the improvement is dramatic: robust accuracy jumps from 9–70% to 81.94%. On GTSRB against BPDA+EOT ℓ∞, the method ties DiffPure on standard accuracy (80.00%) with a negligible 0.18% drop in robust accuracy, suggesting the contrastive guidance benefit is most pronounced under stronger adaptive attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DiffPure adversarial purification pipeline (Nie et al., 2022) by injecting contrastive loss guidance into the reverse diffusion process, drawing on the theoretical guarantee from Lu et al. (2023) that contrastive energy learning converges to exact energy guidance, and the practical pair-selection strategies from Ouyang et al. (2023) who applied contrastive guidance to adversarial training rather than purification. Unlike classifier-guided or classifier-free diffusion guidance, which require unavailable label or text conditions, the contrastive guidance here is fully unsupervised and operates on the purified sample itself. The theoretical analysis, conducted under a Gaussian mixture model, derives the proper forward noise level t* from a feature-learning perspective and shows that the contrastive gradient decomposes into positive and negative pair terms that both push the reverse process along the signal direction μ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12+ percentage-point robust accuracy gains over DiffPure on CIFAR-10 and the 27-point gain on GTSRB against strong adaptive attacks (AutoAttack, BPDA+EOT) demonstrate that guidance is not merely a nice-to-have but a critical component for diffusion-based purification to advance. However, the gains are inconsistent: on ImageNet the method is marginally worse than DiffPure, and on CIFAR-100 both methods remain at very low robust accuracy, suggesting the approach may not scale to harder, higher-dimensional settings. The standard-accuracy vs. robust-accuracy trade-off remains unresolved, and the paper itself notes the method 'tends to prefer stronger attacks and weaker classifiers,' leaving open whether contrastive guidance can be made to improve both metrics simultaneously. Computational cost of the iterative SDE solver with contrastive gradient computation at each step is a practical barrier for deployment.
