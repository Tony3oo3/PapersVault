## Problem

Standard compute-optimal training assumes a fixed model shape throughout training, yet different shapes (patch sizes, context lengths, widths) are optimal at different compute levels. This paper asks whether adaptively changing the model shape during training to traverse between scaling laws can reduce the compute required to reach a target performance, and whether a principled scheduler can determine the optimal transition points.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240061/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For vision experiments, 120×120 RGB images from ImageNet-21k are patchified into variable-length token sequences (patch sizes from 60 down to 8), and the model outputs a 10-shot linear-probe classification error on ImageNet-1k. For language experiments, text sequences from English Wikipedia and BookCorpus are processed by a 12-layer, 768-wide LLaMA-style Transformer with RoPE positional encodings, producing next-token cross-entropy loss on a held-out validation set truncated to the trained context length. The method's own input is the set of fitted power-law scaling curves for each shape configuration, and its output is a monotonic schedule specifying at which performance level to switch to the next shape.

## Method

The core mechanism fits a power-law scaling curve E_P = a_P(C+d_P)^{-b_P}+c_P for each discrete shape value P (patch size, context length, width, batch size, or training objective), then computes the partial derivative of the inverse law q_P(E*) = ∂f_P^{-1}(E)/∂E at the current error level E*. The scheduler selects the shape P that maximizes q_P, i.e., the configuration yielding the steepest descent in compute per unit error reduction, producing a monotonic transition schedule (e.g., large patch → small patch). Smooth transitions between shapes are enabled by FlexiViT's pseudo-inverse resizing of patch embeddings and bilinear interpolation of positional encodings for ViTs, RoPE extrapolation for context-length changes, and random-norm-matched weight expansion for width growth. The approach is validated across ViT sizes (V256-6 through V768-12) and LLaMA models, achieving up to 60% FLOPs reduction for ViTs and 40% for LLMs relative to the best fixed-shape baseline, and outperforms both FlexiViT's uniform random scheduler and simple linear/logarithmic schedules.

## Results

The adaptive patch size scheduler for ViTs achieves up to 60% FLOPs reduction to reach the same ImageNet-1k 10-shot error compared to fixed patch size training. For the compute-optimal barrier across model sizes, scheduled models halve the required compute to optimally train a ViT-Base model. For language models, adaptive context size scheduling yields up to 40% FLOPs reduction. The scheduler also outperforms FlexiViT's random patch sampling and both linear and logarithmic scheduling strategies across all model classes tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary baseline is FlexiViT (Beyer et al., 2023), which uses uniformly random patch size sampling at every training step. The proposed scaling-law-based scheduler consistently outperforms FlexiViT in terms of compute efficiency, as FlexiViT targets inference-time robustness to arbitrary patch sizes rather than training compute optimality. The scheduler also beats two simpler monotonic baselines—linear and logarithmic patch size transitions—which distribute transition points evenly or on a log scale without leveraging the fitted scaling laws. Against fixed (static) patch size training, the adaptive approach achieves up to 60% FLOPs savings for ViTs and up to 40% for LLMs with adaptive context length. The width scheduling experiments confirm that the principled transition points derived from scaling law gradients outperform the momentary performance drop seen with naive expansion, with the model quickly recovering to the predicted scaling curve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the compute-optimal training framework established by Hoffmann et al. (2022, Chinchilla) and the scaling law formalism of Kaplan et al. (2020) by relaxing the assumption that model shape must remain fixed throughout training. The smooth traversal between different patch sizes relies directly on FlexiViT (Beyer et al., 2023), which provides the mechanism for resizing patch embeddings and interpolating positional encodings without performance degradation. Unlike prior model-expansion work that addresses how and where to add parameters, this paper focuses on the when question, deriving transition points from the partial derivatives of inverse scaling laws. The approach also subsumes and outperforms FlexiViT's uniform random patch-size sampling, which targets inference robustness rather than training compute efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated 40–60% FLOPs reduction for reaching equivalent performance is practically significant for anyone training large-scale ViTs or LLMs, and the methodology generalizes cleanly across modalities (vision, language) and shape parameters (patch size, context length, width, batch size, training objective). Key limitations include the need to pre-fit scaling laws for each shape parameter (a non-trivial compute cost), the use of greedy hyperparameter search at small budgets, and the absence of learning-rate scheduling, which may shift transition points at larger scales. Open questions include whether the gains persist at infinite compute, how to jointly optimize multiple shape parameters in a principled (rather than greedy) manner, and whether the approach extends to depth, sparsity, or architectural changes beyond the Transformer.
