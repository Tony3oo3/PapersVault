## Problem

Growable neural network architectures for continual RL (e.g., ProgressiveNet) avoid catastrophic forgetting by freezing and retaining per-task modules, but their parameter count grows quadratically with the number of tasks, severely limiting scalability. Compositional alternatives that reuse learned modules require a dedicated network to learn the composition strategy, introducing a non-stationary and unstable training problem. The paper asks whether a growable architecture can combine linear parameter growth, autonomous module composition without a separate router, and full plasticity for new tasks.

## Input / Output

Each new module receives the current environment state (a 39-dim real-valued vector for Meta-World or a 210×160 RGB frame for ALE games, encoded by a per-module CNN into a d_enc-dimensional vector) together with the output matrix Φ of all previously frozen modules, of shape (k−1)×|A|. The module produces a policy distribution over the action space: a categorical distribution for discrete ALE actions or the mean of a Gaussian for continuous Meta-World actions. The final output is formed by adding the internal policy's feed-forward output to the tentative vector from the output attention head, so the module can either pass through a previous policy's output, blend multiple policies, or fully override them.

## Method

CompoNet is a cascading modular architecture in which each new task instantiates a self-composing policy module whose parameters are constant regardless of the number of prior tasks, yielding linear O(n) parameter growth. Each module contains three blocks: an output attention head that, conditioned on the state representation, computes a weighted combination of all previous modules' outputs via scaled dot-product attention (with cosine positional encodings); an input attention head that retrieves relevant features from both the previous policies and the tentative output vector; and an internal policy (a small feed-forward MLP) that adjusts, overwrites, or retains the tentative output. The key design insight is that the attention mechanism lets each module autonomously learn to compose prior policies—assigning high attention to a directly useful previous policy, learning an arbitrary function over them, or suppressing them entirely when they are irrelevant—without any dedicated composition network. Training applies SAC or PPO to the actor network only, restarting the critic per task, and frozen modules are never updated, guaranteeing zero forgetting by construction.

## Results

CompoNet achieves the highest performance and forward transfer across all three task sequences. On Meta-World (20 robotic manipulation tasks), it reaches 0.42±0.49 performance and 0.01±0.14 forward transfer, the only method with positive forward transfer in this highly interfering sequence (RT = −0.06). On SpaceInvaders (10 modes), it attains 0.99±0.01 performance and 0.74±0.22 forward transfer, exceeding the reference transfer of 0.70. On Freeway (7 modes), it achieves 0.94±0.06 performance and 0.80±0.07 forward transfer, surpassing the RT of 0.67. The architecture grows linearly in parameters with the number of tasks (vs. quadratic for ProgressiveNet), and empirical inference cost does not exhibit quadratic growth up to 300 tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CompoNet outperforms ProgressiveNet (Rusu et al., 2016) on every metric across all three sequences: on Meta-World it achieves 0.42 vs. 0.41 performance and 0.01 vs. −0.04 forward transfer; on SpaceInvaders it reaches 0.99 vs. 0.71 performance and 0.74 vs. 0.10 forward transfer; on Freeway it attains 0.94 vs. 0.47 performance and 0.80 vs. 0.30 forward transfer. Against PackNet (Mallya & Lazebnik, 2018), CompoNet wins decisively on all sequences, with the largest gaps on Freeway (0.94 vs. 0.51 performance, 0.80 vs. 0.31 forward transfer) and SpaceInvaders (0.99 vs. 0.63 performance, 0.74 vs. 0.36 forward transfer). CompoNet also surpasses FT-N (fine-tune with saved models) on forward transfer in Meta-World (0.01 vs. −0.21) and Freeway (0.80 vs. 0.64), while matching its performance on SpaceInvaders (0.99). Notably, CompoNet is the only method achieving positive forward transfer in the Meta-World sequence, where the reference transfer itself is negative (−0.06), demonstrating robustness to high task interference.

## Relation to Prior Work

CompoNet sits at the intersection of two CRL research threads: growable neural networks (ProgressiveNet, Terekhov et al.) that add modules per task to avoid forgetting, and neural composition approaches (Rosenbaum et al., Khetarpal et al.) that combine previously learned networks to solve new tasks. The key departure from growable NNs is that knowledge transfer occurs through composition of module outputs via attention rather than through shared hidden-layer lateral connections, which is what causes ProgressiveNet's quadratic parameter growth. The key departure from compositional NNs is the elimination of a dedicated routing network: each module autonomously learns to compose previous policies through its internal attention heads, avoiding the non-stationary joint optimization problem identified by Rosenbaum et al. (2019). The architecture also sidesteps the stability-plasticity dilemma of parameter-freezing methods (EWC, PackNet, Supermasks) by never modifying frozen modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The linear parameter growth (O(n) vs. O(n²) for ProgressiveNet) is a practically significant scalability improvement that makes growable architectures viable for very long task sequences—over 10K modules fit in a single 24GB GPU. The self-composing mechanism, where attention heads naturally learn to route to relevant previous policies or ignore them entirely, provides an elegant and interpretable solution to the composition problem without auxiliary training objectives. However, the paper retains the standard CRL assumption of known task boundaries and identifiers, and the theoretical O(n²) inference cost (though empirically manageable up to 300 tasks) remains a barrier to truly never-ending learning. Open questions include automatic task-boundary detection, further inference-time efficiency (quantization, distillation), and whether the attention-based composition generalizes to settings with heterogeneous action spaces or state spaces across tasks.
