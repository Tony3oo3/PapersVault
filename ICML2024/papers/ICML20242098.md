## Problem

Language models solving programmatic tasks (math, table QA, visual reasoning) typically rely on primitive functions, producing verbose and error-prone programs that are difficult for humans to verify. Existing methods for auto-inducing higher-level tools either generate hundreds of non-reusable functions, require additional training/validation data, or depend on iterative self-verification and rectification loops. The paper asks whether a training-free, supervision-free method can induce a small, reusable, and verifiable function toolbox on the fly while simultaneously producing solutions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of natural-language queries, each grounded on a task-specific environment: pandas DataFrames for table QA (with tables serialized as markdown or loaded from CSV/JSON), PIL Image objects for visual reasoning (with VisProg-style primitives like locate_objects and crop_region), or bare Python for math problems. For each query it outputs a Python program solution and, over the stream, a shared toolbox of induced functions whose docstrings and signatures are fed back into subsequent prompts. The toolbox is periodically trimmed, keeping only functions used above a logarithmic frequency threshold, so its size stays in the single digits to low tens rather than growing unboundedly.

## Method

TROVE maintains a shared function library F over a streaming sequence of examples and interacts with it in three modes per example: IMPORT (use existing toolbox functions), CREATE (write a new function and add it to F), and SKIP (fall back to primitives only). For each mode it samples K candidate (function, solution) pairs, then selects one via execution-agreement self-consistency: all solutions are executed, those producing the most frequent output are retained, and ties are broken by preferring fewer AST operations. The selected function is added to F, and its solution is adopted. Periodically, functions whose usage count falls below λ = ½·log₁₀(n) (where n is the number of examples processed) are removed, keeping the toolbox compact. The entire pipeline is training-free and requires no validation set, external supervision, or iterative rectification—only the test stream itself drives both solution generation and toolbox evolution.

## Results

On 11 datasets spanning math (MATH), table QA (TabMWP, WTQ, HiTab), and visual reasoning (GQA), TROVE with CodeLlama-7B-Instruct consistently outperforms the PRIMITIVE baseline by 30–120% on MATH sub-datasets (e.g., 0.25 vs 0.15 on algebra, 0.29 vs 0.21 on prealgebra) and by 4–100% on TABLEQA/GQA (e.g., 0.47 vs 0.43 on TabMWP, 0.44 vs 0.37 on GQA), while reducing solution complexity (#ops) and maintaining toolboxes of only 1–11 functions. With GPT-4, TROVE reaches 0.72 accuracy on MATH-algebra, 0.92 on TabMWP, and 0.44 on GQA, using 16, 38, and 8 induced functions respectively—94–98% smaller than competing tool-making methods. A human verification study on WTQ shows TROVE solutions are verified 31% faster (17.5 s vs 25.5 s) and 13% more accurately (0.87 vs 0.77) than PRIMITIVE solutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the internal PRIMITIVE baseline (no tool induction), TROVE improves accuracy by 30–120% across all seven MATH sub-datasets and by 4–100% on TABLEQA/GQA, while also reducing average solution complexity. Against the INSTANCE baseline (per-example tool creation, following CREATOR's two-step approach), TROVE achieves 8.7–83.3% higher correctness on MATH using 60–90% fewer tools, and substantially outperforms INSTANCE on TABLEQA and GQA where INSTANCE actually degrades below PRIMITIVE (e.g., 0.16 vs 0.37 on GQA) due to hundreds of low-utility functions confusing the model. Against external tool-making methods using GPT-4 (Table 3), TROVE outperforms CRAFT on MATH-algebra (0.72 vs 0.68) and TabMWP (0.92 vs 0.88) with 94–98% smaller toolboxes (16–38 vs 181–525 functions), and ties on GQA (0.44 vs 0.45) with a 98% smaller toolbox (8 vs 525). TROVE also beats CREATOR on all three overlapping datasets (0.72 vs 0.65, 0.92 vs 0.81, 0.44 vs 0.34) with 98–99% fewer tools, and substantially outperforms LATM (0.72 vs 0.30, 0.92 vs 0.09, 0.44 vs 0.29) which requires additional training and validation data. Notably, TROVE requires no training, no self-verification, no iterative rectification, and no tool retrieval modules, unlike all three compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

TROVE sits at the intersection of program-aided language models (PAL, VisProg) and LM-based tool induction (CREATOR, LATM, CRAFT), but deliberately strips away the additional machinery those methods require: no training or validation data, no iterative rectification, no self-verification modules. Its core mechanism repurposes self-consistency (Wang et al., 2023b) from answer selection to tool-and-solution selection, combined with a simple logarithmic trimming schedule, to maintain a compact reusable toolbox in a streaming, training-free fashion. Conceptually, it reframes tool induction as an online, execution-agreement-driven process rather than an offline, supervision-dependent one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, streaming design makes TROVE immediately deployable without the data collection and validation pipelines that prior tool-making methods demand, lowering the barrier to adopting reusable-function approaches in new domains. The human verification study (31% faster, 13% more accurate) provides concrete evidence that smaller, well-curated toolboxes have practical value beyond raw accuracy. Open questions include whether execution agreement generalizes to domains where programs cannot be easily executed or where answers are not uniquely determined, and whether the logarithmic trimming heuristic holds at much larger scales or with more diverse function spaces.
