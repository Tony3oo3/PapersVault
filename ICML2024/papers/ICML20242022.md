## Problem

Imitation learning in infinite horizon linear MDPs previously required a persistent excitation assumption on the feature covariance that is easily violated by deterministic policies, and the sample complexity scaled as O(ε^{-5}). This paper removes that exploration assumption entirely and improves the accuracy dependence to O(ε^{-4}), while also providing the first no-regret algorithm for infinite horizon linear MDPs with adversarial costs.

## Input / Output

The learner receives a fixed dataset of expert demonstration trajectories (the expert cannot be queried interactively), a known feature matrix Φ with ‖Φ‖_{1,∞}≤1, and the discount factor γ. During training, the algorithm collects on-policy state-action-next-state tuples from the MDP. The output is a stationary policy obtained by uniform random sampling from the K policies generated during the online phase (an online-to-batch conversion), guaranteeing ε+ε_E-suboptimality with high probability.

## Method

The core insight is a regret decomposition that splits imitation learning into two coupled online learning problems: a cost-estimation player (updated via projected OGD to control Regret_w) and a policy player (updated via a no-regret algorithm for adversarial linear MDPs to control Regret_π). The policy player (Algorithm 2) is the first algorithm for infinite horizon linear MDPs with adversarial full-information losses, achieving O(K^{3/4}) pseudo-regret by collecting on-policy data in batches of size τ, computing optimistic Q-estimates via ridge regression, and updating the policy via mirror descent only every τ steps. For the finite horizon case, Algorithm 4 (BRIG) exploits the fact that the cost is revealed before the policy update, allowing LSVI-UCB to be used as a best response and achieving the stronger O(√K) regret, which translates to O(ε^{-2}) MDP interactions.

## Results

ILARL (Algorithm 3) achieves O(d³/((1-γ)⁸ε⁴)) MDP interactions for infinite-horizon linear MDPs, improving the ε-dependence from O(ε⁻⁵) to O(ε⁻⁴) and removing the persistent excitation assumption required by PPIL (Viano et al., 2022). BRIG (Algorithm 4) further improves the finite-horizon case to O(H⁴d³ε⁻²) MDP interactions, a quadratic improvement in ε over applying ILARL to finite horizons. In experiments on a continuous gridworld with a stochastic expert, ILARL achieves the highest normalized return (matching expert performance) and is the most sample-efficient in MDP trajectories across both τ_E=1 and τ_E=2 settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Theoretically, ILARL strictly dominates PPIL (Viano et al., 2022) in all relevant parameters: it removes the persistent excitation assumption (where β ≤ d⁻¹ makes PPIL scale as Õ(d⁸/((1-γ)⁹ε⁵))), improves the ε-dependence from ε⁻⁵ to ε⁻⁴, and reduces the dimension dependence from d⁸ to d³. Empirically, on a continuous gridworld with a stochastic expert, ILARL outperforms GAIL, AIRL, REIRL, and IQLearn in both expert-trajectory efficiency and MDP-trajectory efficiency. For τ_E=1, all imitation learning methods surpass behavioural cloning, but ILARL achieves the highest return matching expert performance. For τ_E=2, only REIRL and ILARL still outperform BC, with ILARL again achieving the best normalized return. BRIG's O(ε⁻²) finite-horizon bound is a quadratic improvement over the O(ε⁻⁴) that would result from applying ILARL's infinite-horizon machinery to episodic problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the imitation learning framework of Viano et al. (2022, PPIL) by removing the persistent excitation assumption and improving the accuracy dependence from O(ε⁻⁵) to O(ε⁻⁴) in infinite horizon linear MDPs. The core technical contribution is a novel connection between imitation learning and online learning in MDPs with adversarial losses, where the regret is decomposed into a cost-update term (controlled by OGD) and a policy-update term (controlled by a new no-regret algorithm for adversarial linear MDPs). The finite horizon result (BRIG) further leverages LSVI-UCB (Jin et al., 2019) in a best-response fashion, exploiting the fact that the cost is known one step in advance, achieving O(ε⁻²) sample complexity. The adversarial MDP submodule (Algorithms 1–2) builds on the policy iteration structure of Zhong & Zhang (2023) and the regularization technique of Moulin & Neu (2023) for the infinite horizon extension.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The removal of the persistent excitation assumption is a qualitative advance, as this condition is easily violated by deterministic policies with tabular features and was a common but restrictive requirement in the literature. The connection between IL and adversarial MDPs provides a reusable conceptual framework that could inform future work on other structured MDP classes (bilinear classes, linear mixture MDPs). Key open questions include whether the expert trajectory bound can be further reduced (currently O(d/(1-γ)²ε²)), whether the d and H dependence in the finite horizon case can be improved, and whether the best-response trick can be adapted to the infinite horizon setting where greedy policies make the shift term intractable. The empirical validation, while confirming theoretical predictions, is limited to a simple continuous gridworld.
