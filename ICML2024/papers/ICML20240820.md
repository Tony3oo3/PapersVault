## Problem

Existing generalist models built on LLMs and vision-language architectures operate almost exclusively in 2D image domains, lacking the capacity to perceive, ground, reason about, and act within 3D physical environments. This limits their ability to solve real-world embodied tasks such as 3D grounding, scene-level reasoning, navigation, and robotic manipulation, which are inherently defined in 3D space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240820/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LEO consumes three input modalities simultaneously: an egocentric 2D RGB image (for the agent's first-person view), object-centric 3D point clouds extracted via Mask3D instance proposals (for a global third-person scene view), and a natural-language instruction. Outputs are generated autoregressively as a unified token sequence: textual responses (captions, QA answers, multi-turn dialogue, step-by-step plans) or embodied action commands, where continuous manipulation actions are discretized into bins and mapped to reserved SentencePiece tokens alongside discrete navigation commands, all sharing a single vocabulary.

## Method

LEO encodes 3D objects with a frozen PointNet++ followed by a Spatial Transformer that biases attention with relative position and size to capture inter-object 3D relations, producing object-centric 3D token embeddings; egocentric 2D images are encoded by a frozen OpenCLIP ConvNext. These visual tokens are interleaved with text tokens and fed into a Vicuna-7B LLM, where only LoRA adapters (~142M of ~7B parameters) plus the 2D encoder and Spatial Transformer are trained under a prefix language modeling objective. Training follows a two-stage scheme: (1) 3D VL alignment on object/scene captioning data to bridge 3D representations with language, then (2) 3D VLA instruction tuning across QA, dialogue, planning, navigation, and manipulation tasks. A key data contribution is an LLM-assisted generation pipeline that prompts ChatGPT with 3D scene graphs (from 3DSSG) using an Object-centric Chain-of-Thought (O-CoT) strategy and applies regex-based refinement filters to correct counting errors, hallucinated objects, and leaked object IDs, yielding high-quality 3D VL data at scale.

## Results

On 3D dense captioning (Scan2Cap val), LEO achieves CIDEr 72.4, BLEU-4 38.2, and sentence similarity 55.3, surpassing the best task-specific model Vote2Cap-DETR (CIDEr 61.8) and the task-specific fine-tuned generalist 3D-VisTA (CIDEr 66.9). On 3D QA (ScanQA val), LEO reaches CIDEr 101.4, METEOR 20.0, and refined EM@1 47.6, outperforming 3D-VisTA (CIDEr 69.6, EM@1 22.4) and 3D-LLM (CIDEr 69.4, EM@1 20.5). On embodied reasoning (SQA3D test), LEO achieves EM@1 50.0 (52.4 refined), exceeding 3D-VisTA (48.5) and ScanQA (47.2). On embodied acting, LEO matches or exceeds CLIPort on manipulation (e.g., 79.8% unseen on packing-google-objects-seq vs. 71.9% for CLIPort single) and achieves 15.2 SPL on MP3D ObjNav, substantially outperforming ZSON zero-shot (4.8 SPL) and Habitat-web demo (10.2 SPL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEO outperforms all task-specific captioning baselines (Scan2Cap, 3DJCG, Vote2Cap-DETR) by a wide margin on Scan2Cap, with CIDEr gains of +10.6 over Vote2Cap-DETR and +24.7 over 3DJCG. Against the closest generalist competitors, LEO surpasses 3D-VisTA by +5.5 CIDEr on Scan2Cap and by +31.8 CIDEr / +5.2 METEOR on ScanQA, while also achieving higher SQA3D EM@1 (50.0 vs. 48.5). On ScanQA, LEO's CIDEr of 101.4 substantially exceeds 3D-LLM (69.4) and the original ScanQA model (64.9), with the gap driven by stronger open-ended generation quality. On embodied manipulation (CLIPort), LEO is competitive with CLIPort single on seen tasks (98.8 vs. 98.0 on separating-piles) and clearly wins on the unseen packing-google-objects-seq task (79.8 vs. 71.9), though it trails CLIPort multi on put-blocks-in-bowls unseen (35.2 vs. 45.8). On object navigation, LEO achieves 15.2 SPL on MP3D-val, far exceeding ZSON zero-shot (4.8) and Habitat-web demo (10.2), and generalizes zero-shot to HM3D with 19.1 SPL versus ZSON's 12.6, though its absolute success rate (23.1%) remains below Habitat-web demo (35.4%) which uses fully supervised human demonstrations.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEO extends the two-stage training paradigm of Visual Instruction Tuning (Liu et al., 2023b) from 2D images to 3D embodied settings, and adopts the object-centric 3D tokenization and Spatial Transformer from 3D-VisTA (Zhu et al., 2023c) and Chen et al. (2022) to bridge point clouds with an LLM. It departs from concurrent 3D VL models (3D-LLM, Chat3D) by unifying vision-language understanding and embodied action (navigation, manipulation) under a single autoregressive architecture with a shared task interface, rather than treating acting as a separate downstream task. The LLM-assisted data generation pipeline with scene-graph prompting and O-CoT is a novel contribution that addresses the 3D data scarcity problem differently from box-based or multi-view approaches used by prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LEO demonstrates that a single ~7B-parameter model can handle the full spectrum of 3D embodied tasks (captioning, QA, dialogue, planning, navigation, manipulation) without task-specific heads, providing a practical blueprint for future embodied generalist agents. The finding that incorporating embodied acting tasks degrades VL performance (contrary to the VL-benefits-VLA direction shown in RT-2) is an important negative result that highlights an unresolved tension in VLA co-training. Key open questions include how to bridge the VL-to-action gap without mutual degradation, whether the scaling law holds at larger scales, and how to ensure safety and alignment for agents that can physically act in the world.
