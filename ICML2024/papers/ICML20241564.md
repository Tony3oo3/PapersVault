## Problem

While asymmetric non-contrastive learning (ANCL) methods like SimSiam and BYOL outperform contrastive learning in self-supervised settings, the effect of incorporating class labels into ANCL remains largely unexplored and lacks theoretical understanding. In particular, it is unclear how supervision alters the geometry of learned representations and how to balance supervised and self-supervised signals to avoid class collapse while gaining semantic alignment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs of augmented RGB images (224×224 center-cropped for evaluation; random crops, flips, color jitter, grayscale, and Gaussian blur during training) along with their class labels. It produces a frozen encoder backbone (ResNet-50 or ViT-Small) whose final-layer features serve as the learned representation for downstream linear probing, few-shot logistic regression, or Faster R-CNN initialization. A target pool of size 8192 stores L2-normalized projector outputs with labels to supply same-class supervised targets independent of batch composition.

## Method

The proposed supervised ANCL framework augments the standard ANCL loss with a supervised term: the predictor output p₁ is regressed toward both the stop-gradient target z₂ (self-supervised) and an averaged same-class target z₂^sup drawn from a target pool (supervised), combined as α·‖p₁−sg(z₂)‖² + (1−α)·‖p₁−sg(z₂^sup)‖². The target pool ensures same-class positives exist regardless of batch size and provides slowly-updated targets that help prevent collapse. Theoretical analysis via Lagrangian optimization under linear encoder/projector/predictor assumptions proves that the optimal predictor reduces intra-class variance by a factor of α, and that the encoder W shares the predictor's eigenspace, so W itself learns to compress within-class spread. Empirically, α ∈ (0,1) consistently outperforms both extremes: α=1 (pure self-supervised ANCL) retains too much intra-class variance, while α=0 (pure supervised) causes fine-grained classes to collapse into single points, degrading generalization on downstream tasks.

## Results

On 11 downstream transfer learning datasets (linear probing), SUPBYOL achieves the best average rank of 1.36, with highlights including 90.85% on CIFAR-10, 72.04% on CIFAR-100, 43.24% on CUB-200, 65.02% on Stanford Dogs, and 82.68% on Oxford-IIIT Pets. SUPSIAM follows closely with an average rank of 2.27 (89.95% CIFAR-10, 43.48% CUB-200, 64.65% Dogs). On ImageNet-100 linear probing, SUPBYOL reaches 87.43% top-1 accuracy and SUPSIAM 87.31%, compared to 84.93% (BYOL) and 82.15% (SimSiam). On VOC object detection, SUPSIAM achieves the best AP of 53.89 and AP50 of 80.28.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SUPBYOL outperforms its self-supervised counterpart BYOL on all 11 downstream datasets, with the largest gains on fine-grained tasks: +6.14% on CUB-200 (43.24 vs 37.10), +7.22% on Stanford Dogs (65.02 vs 57.80), and +3.90% on Oxford-IIIT Pets (82.68 vs 78.78). Similarly, SUPSIAM beats SimSiam on all 11 datasets, with particularly large improvements on CUB-200 (+7.38%) and Dogs (+10.08%). Both supervised ANCL methods surpass the supervised CL baselines (SUPCON, SUPMoCo) on 9 out of 11 datasets, with SUPBYOL achieving the best average rank (1.36) compared to SUPMoCo (4.09) and SUPCON (4.73). On ImageNet-100 linear probing, SUPBYOL (87.43%) and SUPSIAM (87.31%) match or exceed SUPCON (87.40%) and SUPMoCo (86.33%), while on VOC object detection SUPSIAM achieves the best AP (53.89) and AP50 (80.28), outperforming all CL-based methods. The paper notes that the performance gain from supervision in ANCL is smaller than in CL (since self-supervised ANCL already performs strongly), but the gains are most pronounced on fine-grained datasets where retaining intra-class variance is critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-supervised ANCL framework (SimSiam, BYOL) to the supervised setting by introducing a convex combination of self-supervised and supervised losses, with a target pool mechanism borrowed from MoCo's memory bank to guarantee class-matched targets. It departs from SupCon's per-sample loss weighting by aggregating supervised and self-supervised losses separately before combining them, enabling a cleaner theoretical analysis. The theoretical analysis builds directly on the spectral-decomposition framework of Tian et al. (2021) and Zhuo et al. (2023) for self-supervised ANCL, extending it with class-conditional distribution assumptions to characterize how supervision modifies the optimal predictor. The paper positions itself as the first to provide a theoretical analysis of supervised ANCL, filling a gap left by prior supervised CL analyses (Graf et al., Chen et al.) that focused on contrastive methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical result—that supervision reduces intra-class variance by exactly a factor of α, and that the optimal predictor acts as a low-pass filter eliminating augmentation noise while scaling intra-class variance—gives practitioners a principled way to tune the supervision strength rather than relying on grid search. The practical finding that supervised ANCL outperforms supervised CL (SupCon) on transfer tasks, particularly fine-grained datasets, suggests that the non-contrastive architecture is a better vehicle for incorporating label information. Limitations include pretraining on ImageNet-100 rather than full ImageNet, reliance on linear encoder/projector assumptions in the theory, and the need to manually set α; open questions include whether α can be learned automatically, how the approach scales to very large class counts where target pool design becomes critical, and whether the intra-class variance reduction characterization holds for non-linear architectures.
