## Problem

ViT-based visual trackers achieve high accuracy but are too computationally expensive for real-time deployment on UAVs with limited battery and processing resources. Additionally, extreme viewing angle changes inherent to aerial tracking are not adequately addressed by existing feature learning strategies, and prior adaptive-computation approaches (e.g., Aba-VTrack) introduce unstructured token access that negates their speed gains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241140/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracker takes a 128×128 template image and a 256×256 search image, both split into P×P patches and processed through a single-stream ViT backbone. The prediction head outputs a local offset, a normalized bounding box size, and a per-patch classification score over the search region; the final box is determined by argmax on the classification map followed by offset/size regression. During training, the ground-truth target location in the search image is used to extract interpolated target-patch tokens for the view-invariance loss, but this incurs no additional inference cost.

## Method

AVTrack introduces two key mechanisms into a single-stream ViT tracker. First, an Activation Module (AM) is inserted into each transformer block (after the first n_f layers); it applies a linear layer followed by sigmoid to a slice of the token sequence, producing a scalar activation probability that gates whether the block processes tokens or passes them through unchanged, with a block-sparsity loss (L1-style penalty on mean activation) encouraging deactivation. Second, view-invariant representations are learned by maximizing mutual information (via the Deep InfoMax Jensen-Shannon estimator) between the ViT's final-layer tokens for the template and the interpolated tokens at the ground-truth target location in the search image, adding only a training-time loss. The full training objective combines weighted focal loss for classification, L1 + GIoU for box regression, the sparsity loss, and the VIR loss, trained end-to-end from ImageNet-pretrained ViT-tiny/DeiT-tiny/EVA-tiny weights.

## Results

On VisDrone2018, AVTrack-DeiT achieves 86.0% precision and 65.3% success rate at 220 FPS, setting a new precision record among real-time UAV trackers. Across five benchmarks (DTB70, UAVDT, VisDrone2018, UAV123, UAV123@10fps), AVTrack-DeiT attains an average precision of 84.1% and average success rate of 64.3% at 256.8 GPU FPS and 59.5 CPU FPS. AVTrack-EVA achieves the highest GPU speed of 283.7 FPS while maintaining 81.8% average precision. All three AVTrack variants clearly outperform CNN-based and DCF-based trackers, with ViT-based methods exceeding 81% average precision versus 79.8% (best CNN) and 74.6% (best DCF).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AVTrack-DeiT trails only Aba-ViTrack (ICCV 23) in average precision (84.1% vs 85.3%) and success rate (64.3% vs 64.7%), but runs 1.4× faster on GPU (256.8 vs 181.5 FPS) and 1.2× faster on CPU (59.5 vs 50.3 FPS). On VisDrone2018 specifically, AVTrack-DeiT achieves 86.0% precision at 220 FPS, narrowly behind ROMTrack (86.3% at 51.1 FPS) by just 0.3% in precision while being 4.3× faster, and outpacing TransT (85.9% at 51.7 FPS) and SLT-TransT (85.6% at 29.5 FPS) by 3–7× in speed. Among CNN-based trackers, AVTrack-DeiT surpasses the best (UDAT, 79.2% avg precision) by 4.9% in precision and 4.2% in success rate. All three AVTrack variants exceed 81% average precision, clearly separating them from DCF-based methods whose best (RACF) reaches only 74.6%. The generalizability experiments (Table 4) confirm that adding VIR and AM to ARTrack, DropTrack, and GRM yields 1.0–2.2% precision gains and 18–22% speed improvements with less than 0.6% precision loss from AM alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

AVTrack extends the single-stream ViT-based tracking paradigm established by OSTRack and Mixformer, but departs from prior adaptive-computation trackers (DynamicViT, A-ViT, Aba-VTrack) by operating at the transformer-block level rather than the token level, avoiding unstructured memory access. For view-invariant representation learning, the paper repurposes the Deep InfoMax Jensen-Shannon MI estimator (Belghazi et al., ICLR 2019) in a tracking context, where it had not previously been applied. The block sparsity loss draws on l1-regularization ideas from statistical learning theory, and the overall framework is designed as a plug-and-play module compatible with existing ViT-based trackers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving near-SOTA tracking accuracy at ~220 FPS on a single GPU and ~60 FPS on CPU, validated on an embedded Jetson AGX Xavier, making it directly deployable on resource-constrained UAVs. The view-invariant representation learning via MI is a simple additional loss that improves precision by 3–5% across architectures, suggesting it could benefit other view-sensitive tracking or recognition tasks. Open questions include whether the block-level activation strategy scales to larger ViTs (only tiny variants are tested), whether the MI-based view invariance generalizes to non-UAV tracking scenarios, and how the method performs under more extreme domain shifts (nighttime, heavy occlusion) beyond the standard benchmarks.
