## Problem

No computationally efficient algorithm for offline RL with linear MDPs achieves O(ε^{-2}) sample complexity under partial data coverage; the prior best result (Gabbianelli et al., 2024) requires O(ε^{-4}) samples due to a double-loop primal-dual structure. Additionally, no existing computationally efficient method handles both partial coverage and constraints on additional reward signals simultaneously in the infinite-horizon discounted setting.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a pre-collected offline dataset of (s, a, s') triplets drawn i.i.d. from a behavior distribution, a known feature map φ: S×A → R^d with ‖φ‖₂ ≤ 1, known reward parameters θ, and (for the constrained variant) threshold vector τ. The output is a mixture policy π̄ = Unif(π₁,…,π_T) that is ε-optimal and satisfies J_i(π̄) ≥ τ_i − ε for all constraints. Computationally, the algorithm only evaluates the softmax policy at states appearing in the dataset, avoiding any cost that scales with |S|.

## Method

The algorithm formulates offline RL as a linear program over occupancy measures, introduces a low-dimensional variable λ = Φ^T μ, and parameterizes the high-dimensional μ and v through a policy π to obtain a low-dimensional Lagrangian in (ζ, λ, π). The key technical insight is restricting λ to a confidence set C_n(B) of linear combinations of dataset feature vectors, then applying a 2-approximate barycentric spanner (of size d) to reduce the effective parameter space from n dimensions to d, enabling a uniform concentration bound via a covering argument. This sidesteps the double-loop structure of prior work: the ζ-player and w-player act greedily after the λ-player and π-player, yielding a single-loop algorithm with O(ε^{-2}) sample complexity. For the constrained variant, a w-player is added that minimizes ⟨w, τ − Θ^T λ_t⟩ over the probability simplex to enforce constraint satisfaction, with Slater's condition ensuring strong duality.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical guarantee is a sample complexity of O(ε⁻²) for finding an ε-optimal policy in offline linear MDPs under partial data coverage (concentrability assumption), improving over the prior best of O(ε⁻⁴) by Gabbianelli et al. (2024). The specific sample size bound is n = O((C*)² d³ log(dn(log|A|)/(δε(1-γ)))) / ((1-γ)² ε²), where C* is the concentrability constant, d is the feature dimension, and γ is the discount factor. For the constrained setting, the bound becomes n = O((C*)² d³ log(dn(log|A|)/(δεφ(1-γ)))) / ((1-γ)² φ² ε²), where φ is the Slater margin. The algorithm is computationally efficient, requiring only O(nd² log_C d) time to compute the barycentric spanner and operating on d-dimensional variables rather than |S×A|-dimensional ones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The closest prior work is Gabbianelli et al. (2024), which also targets offline linear MDPs with partial coverage and computational efficiency but requires O(ε⁻⁴) samples due to a double-loop primal-dual structure; this paper eliminates the double loop via a barycentric spanner-based confidence set, achieving O(ε⁻²) and additionally supporting constraints on multiple reward signals. Compared to PSPI (Xie et al., 2021), which achieves partial coverage and computational efficiency in the linear function approximation setting, this paper improves the sample complexity from ε⁻⁵ to ε⁻² and extends to the constrained setting. The general function approximation baselines (FQI, CBPL, Minimax, CPPO, Zanette's Minimax) either lack partial coverage (FQI, CBPL, Zanette), lack computational efficiency (Minimax, CPPO), or lack constraint support (FQI, Minimax, CPPO, Zanette); no single prior method simultaneously achieves all four properties (partial coverage, computational efficiency, constraint support, and ε⁻² sample complexity) that this paper provides. The improvement over Gabbianelli et al. is thus both in sample complexity (ε⁻⁴ → ε⁻²) and in scope (unconstrained → constrained RL).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the primal-dual framework of Gabbianelli et al. (2024), which introduced a computationally efficient algorithm for offline linear MDPs under partial coverage but required a double-loop structure yielding O(ε⁻⁴) sample complexity. The key departure is the use of a barycentric spanner to project the λ-player's action space into a d-dimensional subspace, enabling uniform concentration bounds that eliminate the inner loop and achieve the optimal O(ε⁻²) rate. The paper further extends the framework to the constrained MDP setting by introducing a w-player for Lagrange multipliers on constraint rewards, a component absent in all prior linear MDP offline RL work. In the broader landscape, it sits between general function approximation methods (which lack computational efficiency under partial coverage) and earlier linear MDP algorithms (which had worse sample complexity or no constraint support).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first computationally efficient algorithm achieving O(ε⁻²) sample complexity for offline RL with linear MDPs under partial data coverage, closing a gap that had persisted since the problem was formalized. The extension to constrained RL is practically significant for safety-critical applications (robotics, healthcare) where one must optimize reward while respecting hard constraints, and the O(ε⁻²) rate with Slater's condition is the natural target. A stated limitation is that the feature coverage assumption used here is stronger than the one in Gabbianelli et al. (2024), and the authors explicitly leave the design of an algorithm under the weaker coverage notion to future work. The paper is purely theoretical with no empirical validation, so practical applicability remains to be demonstrated.
