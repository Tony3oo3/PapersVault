## Problem

Existing graph kernels suffer from complementary blind spots: R-convolution kernels ignore structural correspondence between substructures and global topology, global-based kernels neglect local structure, and vertex-based matching kernels (e.g., DBMK) assign a uniform unit value to every aligned vertex pair, failing to discriminate structural differences between them and capturing only single-level correspondence. No prior kernel simultaneously encodes structural alignment, multi-level correspondence, and both global and local topology in a single similarity measure.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of un-attributed graphs (no node or edge labels) and produces a scalar kernel similarity score. The adjacency matrix of each graph serves as the Hamiltonian for the CTQW, and the kernel is parameterized by H (set to 10 in experiments), controlling the maximum depth of expansion subgraphs used for vertex alignment. The resulting kernel matrix is fed to a C-SVM classifier, which outputs a class label; the kernel itself is not differentiable and does not participate in end-to-end training.

## Method

For each graph, the method computes the Average Mixing Matrix (AMM) Q = Σ_j P_j ∘ P_j via spectral decomposition of the adjacency matrix, where each row of Q encodes the time-averaged probability distribution of a CTQW visiting all vertices when departing from a given vertex. A quantum Shannon entropy H_Q(v) = −Σ_u Q_vu log Q_vu is then assigned to every vertex, simultaneously reflecting local topology (through the departure vertex) and global structure (through the full visitation distribution). Vertex alignment is established via a family of h-level correspondence matrices (h = 1…H) built from Depth-based Representations, requiring both minimal Euclidean distance in the affinity matrix and non-empty expansion subgraphs at each level. The final kernel sums, over all levels and all aligned vertex pairs, the product of the correspondence indicator and a Basic Reproducing Kernel K_r(v_i, v_j) = ½·exp(−[H_Q(v_i) − H_Q(v_j)]), which weights each aligned pair by the entropic similarity of its vertices rather than a fixed unit value. This design yields a polynomial-time O(n³) kernel that is theoretically linked to an Aligned Subgraph Kernel while encoding multi-level correspondence and discriminating structural differences among aligned pairs.

## Results

On ten benchmark graph datasets, QBMK achieves the best classification accuracy on 7 out of 10 datasets when paired with C-SVM under 10-fold cross-validation. Headline results include 88.55% on MUTAG, 84.36% on CATH2, 73.66% on BAR31, and 62.63% on BSPHERE31, all outperforming the strongest competing kernel by 2–14 percentage points. On the three datasets where QBMK is not the best (D&D, PTC, PPIs), it remains within 1–4 points of the top method. Against graph deep learning baselines, QBMK wins on MUTAG (88.55% vs. 85.83% for DGCNN) and PTC (59.38% vs. 58.59%), while DGCNN edges ahead on D&D (79.37% vs. 77.60%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the nine graph-kernel baselines, QBMK wins on 7/10 datasets. The closest competitors are JTQK (wins on D&D 79.89 vs. 77.60 and PPIs 88.47 vs. 84.47) and ISK (wins on PTC 60.26 vs. 59.38); on the remaining datasets QBMK leads by 1–44 points (e.g., +42.9 over QJSK on BAR31, +14.4 over SPGK on BSPHERE31). Among matching-kernel baselines, EDBMK and DBMK are the strongest but still trail QBMK on every dataset (e.g., BAR31: 73.66 vs. 70.08; GatorBait: 15.00 vs. 14.40). Against five graph deep-learning methods (evaluated on MUTAG, D&D, PTC), QBMK outperforms all on MUTAG (88.55 vs. DGCNN 85.83) and PTC (59.38 vs. DGCNN 58.59), while DGCNN narrowly wins on D&D (79.37 vs. 77.60); the remaining deep-learning baselines (EigenPool, DEMO-Net, DGK, DCNN) are consistently outperformed by QBMK on all three datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines of graph kernel research: R-convolution kernels (which decompose graphs into substructures but ignore structural correspondence), global-based kernels (which capture whole-graph structure but miss local detail), and vertex-based matching kernels like DBMK (which capture correspondence but assign uniform weight to all aligned pairs). The key departure is replacing the binary correspondence matrix of DBMK with a family of multilevel entropic correspondence matrices, where each aligned pair's contribution is weighted by a Basic Reproducing Kernel over quantum Shannon entropies computed via the Average Mixing Matrix of the CTQW. This design inherits the alignment framework from Bai et al. (2014a) and the depth-based representations from Bai & Hancock (2014), while the quantum entropy component draws on the CTQW/AMM formalism of Emms et al. (2009) and Godsil (2013).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The QBMK kernel addresses a genuine gap: no prior kernel simultaneously captures structural correspondence, multilevel alignment, and both global and local structure in a single measure. However, the kernel is explicitly non-positive definite (the authors cite Ong et al. 2004 to justify this), which limits its theoretical guarantees and may affect generalization in practice. The evaluation is restricted to un-attributed graphs and the deep-learning comparison covers only three datasets, leaving open whether the quantum-entropy advantage persists on larger, attributed, or heterogeneous graph benchmarks. The O(n³) complexity from the AMM computation also raises scalability questions for graphs with thousands of vertices.
