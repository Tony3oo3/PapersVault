## Problem

Block gradient descent (BGD) is empirically faster than vanilla gradient descent (GD) and the heavy ball method (HB) for least-squares, yet existing convergence bounds for BGD carry worse constants than GD, leaving its empirical superiority theoretically unexplained. This paper asks whether carefully tuned stepsizes can close this gap, and shows that under a block-wise orthogonality assumption, BGD with optimal stepsizes achieves an asymptotic convergence rate twice as fast as HB—without any momentum.

## Input / Output

The method takes a block-partitioned data matrix A = [A₁, A₂] satisfying the orthogonality constraint AⱼᵀAⱼ = I (j = 1, 2), a target vector y, and an initial point x₀. It produces the least-squares minimizer x* along with closed-form optimal stepsizes γ₁* = γ₂* = 2/(1 + √(1 − λ₁(CCᵀ))) expressed in terms of the largest eigenvalue of C = A₂ᵀA₁, and the corresponding minimum spectral radius. As a corollary, the same stepsizes yield an improved convergence rate (sin θᵣ − sin θ₁)/(sin θᵣ + sin θ₁) for generalized alternating projection between two subspaces, removing the θᵣ = π/2 assumption of prior work.

## Method

The core technique is to minimize, over stepsize variables (γ₁, γ₂), the spectral radius of the matrix M(γ₁, γ₂) that governs BGD's iterate update. Under the block-wise orthogonality assumption, M(γ₁, γ₂) simplifies to a block matrix depending only on C = A₂ᵀA₁, whose full spectrum is characterized by splitting into three cases (γ₁ = 1, γ₂ = 1, and both ≠ 1) and solving a family of quadratic equations parameterized by the eigenvalues of CCᵀ. The optimal stepsizes γ₁* = γ₂* = 2/(1 + √(1 − λ₁(CCᵀ))) coincide with the optimal stepsize of HB, revealing that BGD's acceleration stems from taking two partial-gradient descent steps per iteration at a cost comparable to one full HB step. In the rank-deficient case the minimum spectral radius equals (ρ_HB*)², proving BGD is asymptotically twice as fast as HB. The same spectral-radius minimization framework is then transplanted to the generalized alternating projection setting, yielding the GAP++ rate that dominates all prior methods for every principal-angle configuration.

## Results

Under the block-wise orthogonality assumption (Assumption 1), BGD with optimal stepsizes achieves a minimum spectral radius ρ*_BGD ≤ (ρ*_HB)², meaning BGD converges asymptotically at least twice as fast as the Heavy Ball method (HB) without using any momentum. For the generalized alternating projection (GAP) application, the proposed GAP++ stepsizes yield an asymptotic linear rate of (sin(θ_r) − sin(θ₁))/(sin(θ_r) + sin(θ₁)), which is always strictly better than or equal to the rates of all prior methods (AP, DR, RAP, PRAP, GDR, GAP) for all valid choices of principal angles θ₁ and θ_r. Numerical experiments (Figure 1) confirm the spectral radius ordering ρ*_BGD < ρ*_HB < ρ*_GD across condition numbers κ = 10, 10³, and 10⁵.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's GAP++ method dominates all five prior alternating projection baselines in Table 1: its rate (sin(θ_r)−sin(θ₁))/(sin(θ_r)+sin(θ₁)) is always ≤ the rates of AP (cos²(θ₁)), DR (cos(θ₁)), RAP, PRAP, and GAP, with strict improvement in most configurations. Against the closest competitor, PRAP (Bauschke et al., 2016), GAP++ is strictly better whenever θ_r < π/2, since sin(θ_r)−sin(θ₁) < sin²(θ_r)−sin²(θ₁) in the relevant regime. Against GAP (Fält & Giselsson, 2017), GAP++ removes the restrictive assumption θ_r = π/2 that was required for their claimed optimality, and their rate (1−sin(θ₁))/(1+sin(θ₁)) is recovered as a special case when θ_r = π/2. In the BGD-vs-HB comparison (Theorem 1), BGD with optimal stepsizes achieves ρ*_BGD ≤ (ρ*_HB)², a factor-of-two asymptotic speedup over the Heavy Ball method (Polyak, 1964) without any momentum term. The numerical experiments in Figure 1 confirm this ordering across condition numbers up to κ = 10⁵, with BGD visibly converging faster than both GD and HB at every iteration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decades-long line of work on block coordinate descent (Hildreth 1957; Nesterov 2012; Beck & Tetruashvili 2013) by resolving a persistent theoretical gap: BGD was empirically faster than GD but had provably worse convergence constants in deterministic settings. The key conceptual departure is showing that under block-wise orthogonality, optimal BGD stepsizes yield a spectral radius bounded by (ρ_HB*)², meaning BGD without any momentum is asymptotically twice as fast as the Heavy Ball method. The paper also bridges BGD to generalized alternating projection (Fält & Giselsson 2017; Bauschke et al. 2016), improving their rates by removing the restrictive assumption θ_r = π/2 that was violated with probability 1 for random subspaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides a clean theoretical justification for why BGD is empirically superior to GD in practice, and shows that the block structure itself acts as a form of acceleration, eliminating the need for momentum. The GAP++ stepsizes for alternating projection are strictly better than all prior methods (AP, DR, RAP, PRAP, GDR, GAP) for all principal angle configurations. Key limitations include the two-block restriction, the orthogonality assumption (satisfiable via QR but at computational cost), and the least-squares setting; open questions include extending to multiple blocks, combining BGD with momentum, and removing Assumption 1 entirely.
