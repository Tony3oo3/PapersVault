## Problem

Existing image restoration methods operate in close-set scenarios where test degradations match those seen during training, or in zero-shot settings that still require prior knowledge of the degradation type. Open-set image restoration (OIR), where test degradations are both unknown at test time and entirely absent from pretraining, remains largely unexplored. This paper identifies OIR as an unidentified distribution shift problem and shows that test-time adaptation is an effective methodology to bridge the gap between pretraining and test distributions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image (hazy, low-light, or noisy) as input and produces a restored clean RGB image. The input is center-cropped along shorter edges and resized to match the pre-trained diffusion model's resolution. Notably, the method requires no paired clean-degraded training data at test time, no explicit degradation type label, and no degradation-specific operator — only the single degraded image drives both the adapter optimization and the restoration guidance throughout the 1000-step denoising process.

## Method

TAO builds on an unconditional ImageNet-pretrained diffusion model (T=1000 steps) as a degradation-agnostic foundation, and introduces two test-time components. The Test-time Degradation Adapter (TDA) is a 4-layer CNN optimized at each denoising step via adversarial, reconstruction, and perceptual losses to translate generated clean estimates into the degraded domain of the test image, enabling self-supervision without ground truth. The Adapter-guided Image Restoration (AIR) exploits the temporal dynamics of denoising by applying stage-specific guidance: MSE loss on global content in early steps (t 999–700), perceptual + adversarial + MSE losses on mid-level content (t 700–50), and total variation loss on fine details (t 50–0). Two parallel images (guided and auxiliary) are generated at each step to prevent the adapter from collapsing to an identity mapping, and the adapter's output provides the gradient signal that steers the guided image toward the clean version of the test input.

## Results

On image dehazing (HSTS/RESIDE), TAO achieves PSNR 22.29 and SSIM 0.877, outperforming all zero-shot methods and most supervised baselines, trailing only DehazeNet (23.96/0.902) by 1.67 dB. On low-light enhancement (LOL), TAO obtains PSNR 17.10 and SSIM 0.804, achieving the best SSIM among zero-shot methods and second-best PSNR, with only MBLLEN (18.03/0.809) surpassing it on both metrics. On image denoising (Kodak, σ=30), TAO reaches PSNR 28.49 and SSIM 0.830, outperforming BM3D, N2V, and DIP on at least one metric while remaining competitive with N2S (28.71/0.864).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On dehazing, TAO beats all 6 zero-shot baselines (DCP, BCCR, GRM, NLD, DDIP, YOLY) by 1.05–6.54 dB PSNR, and surpasses 3 of 4 supervised methods (MSN, AOD, CAP) by 0.60–3.89 dB, losing only to DehazeNet by 1.67 dB. On low-light enhancement, TAO outperforms 8 of 9 baselines on SSIM (best among zero-shot at 0.804) and 7 of 9 on PSNR, with only MBLLEN edging ahead by 0.93 dB PSNR and 0.005 SSIM; it beats ExCNet (the closest zero-shot competitor) by 0.084 SSIM despite a 0.05 dB PSNR gap. On denoising, TAO surpasses BM3D on SSIM (0.830 vs. 0.795), N2V on PSNR (28.49 vs. 27.17), and DIP on both metrics, while N2S retains a small lead (28.71/0.864). Notably, TAO is a single unified model requiring no task-specific training, whereas all 23 baselines are individually designed for their respective degradation type.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAO repurposes the test-time adaptation (TTA) paradigm—previously applied to classification and segmentation—into the generative image restoration setting, using an unconditional diffusion model (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021) as a degradation-agnostic backbone. It builds directly on the conditional guidance mechanism from GDP (Fei et al., 2023) for steering denoising toward a target, but departs from all prior restoration work (close-set, all-in-one, and zero-shot) by requiring no predefined degradation set and no degradation-specific priors at test time. The temporal-dynamic observation from Choi et al. (2022) motivates the staged guidance strategy, while the adversarial domain-alignment adapter draws on standard GAN-style transfer learning applied in a novel single-sample, test-time context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TAO provides the first explicit formulation of open-set image restoration and demonstrates that a single pre-trained diffusion model, augmented with a lightweight test-time adapter, can handle multiple unforeseen degradation types without task-specific retraining. However, practical limitations temper its impact: loss weights and guidance scale must still be tuned per degradation type, the method operates on a single test sample, and the 1000-step denoising process is computationally expensive. Open questions include whether the framework scales to compound or spatially-varying degradations, whether the adapter can be made more efficient (e.g., fewer steps or a learned schedule), and whether the approach extends to video restoration or higher-resolution settings.
