## Problem

Aligning open-source LLMs with human preferences is bottlenecked by the cost and labor of human annotation, resulting in preference datasets that are either small in scale or restricted to narrow tasks. The open-source community in particular lacks a large, diverse, and fine-grained preference dataset to train competitive reward models and aligned chat models. This paper asks whether scaled AI feedback from GPT-4 can substitute for human annotation while preserving alignment quality.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes 63,967 instructions drawn from six sources (TruthfulQA, FalseQA, Evol-Instruct, UltraChat, ShareGPT, FLAN) and generates four completions per instruction from a pool of 17 models spanning different architectures, sizes, and capability levels, yielding 255,864 completions. GPT-4 then scores all four completions simultaneously on four fine-grained aspects (instruction-following, truthfulness, honesty, helpfulness) on a 1–5 scale with a written rationale, and separately produces a free-form textual critique with an overall 1–10 score. The final output is the ULTRAFEEDBACK dataset (over 1 million feedback entries in both scalar and text form), plus three trained models: UltraRM (a 13B reward model), UltraCM (a 13B critique model), and UltraLM-13B-PPO (a PPO-aligned chat model).

## Method

The core contribution is a systematic data-engineering pipeline that identifies scale and diversity as the two critical axes for effective preference data. To ensure diversity, the authors sample instructions across four ability dimensions and use 17 heterogeneous models with randomly assigned 'principle' prompts to elicit varied response styles and quality levels, deliberately mixing model families and sizes to prevent spurious style-quality correlations. To improve annotation reliability, GPT-4 scores all four completions for a given instruction in a single prompt (reducing cross-run inconsistency), decomposes quality into four fine-grained aspects with explicit 1–5 rubrics, and generates a chain-of-thought rationale before each score. For model training, UltraRM is fine-tuned on ULTRAFEEDBACK mixed with three open-source preference datasets using a binary ranking loss with margin normalization, while UltraLM-13B-PPO is aligned via PPO (80 iterations, 512 samples/iteration, lr=1e-6) using UltraRM as the reward signal. A best-of-n sampling strategy (n up to 16) with UltraRM as the selector provides a training-free alternative that raises AlpacaEval win rate from 76.5% to 91.5%.

## Results

UltraLM-13B-PPO achieves a 69.7% average win rate across AlpacaEval, Evol-Instruct, and UltraChat, outperforming all open-source models including the 5.4× larger LLaMA2-70B-Chat (67.7%). The PPO alignment yields a 16.8 percentage-point improvement over the base UltraLM-13B (52.9% → 69.7%). The UltraRM reward model reaches 71.0% average preference-prediction accuracy, surpassing the best open-source baseline (Ziya, 60.1%) by over 10 points. Best-of-16 sampling with UltraRM pushes the AlpacaEval win rate from 76.5% (single sample) to 91.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UltraLM-13B-PPO (13B) outperforms the much larger LLaMA2-70B-Chat by 2.0 points in average win rate (69.7% vs 67.7%), and beats WizardLM-13B-v1.2 (68.1%) and OpenChat-13B-v3.2super (67.9%) by 1.6 and 1.8 points respectively. Against its own pre-PPO base model UltraLM-13B, the RLAIF alignment delivers a dramatic 16.8-point gain (52.9% → 69.7%), with the largest improvements on UltraChat (+26.7 pts) and Evol-Instruct (+17.9 pts). On the reward-modeling side, UltraRM (71.0% avg) surpasses all open-source baselines—Moss (58.3%), Ziya (60.1%), OASST, and SteamSHP (54.5%)—by 6.3–16.5 points, and approaches the closed LLaMA2-70B reward model (72.0–80.0% on individual benchmarks). Even trained solely on ULTRAFEEDBACK without mixing other datasets, UltraRM still exceeds all open-source baselines by 6.3+ points, confirming the dataset's standalone quality. On critique quality (Table 8), UltraCM-13B (5.92 avg) nearly matches gpt-3.5-turbo (6.17) and substantially outperforms dedicated critique models Shepherd-13B (3.23) and SelFee-13B (5.41).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLHF/RLAIF lineage (Ouyang et al. 2022; Bai et al. 2022b; Lee et al. 2023) by scaling AI feedback from domain-specific or form-limited settings to a general-purpose, multi-aspect preference dataset. It draws on data engineering principles from instruction tuning (Ding et al. 2023; Xu et al. 2023) and applies them to the feedback stage, which prior work had largely neglected. The key methodological departure from Constitutional AI and RLAIF is the decomposition of annotation into four fine-grained aspects with simultaneous multi-completion scoring, combined with a pool of 17 diverse models to generate completions, addressing both scalability and diversity simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ULTRAFEEDBACK (1M+ GPT-4 feedback over 250k conversations) is the largest open-source preference and critique dataset, enabling the open-source community to perform feedback learning without the cost and bottleneck of human annotation. The practical release of UltraRM and UltraCM provides immediately usable tools for reward modeling and critique generation. Key limitations include single-turn-only dialogues, absence of safety-oriented data, and GPT-4's demonstrated underestimation of reasoning performance, leaving multi-turn alignment, complex reasoning, and safety as important open directions.
