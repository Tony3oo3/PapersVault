## Problem

Expected Calibration Error (ECE), the dominant metric for measuring model calibration, is known to be discontinuous in the space of predictors—arbitrarily small perturbations to model outputs can cause large jumps in the ECE value. The paper asks how fundamental these pathologies are, whether they can be eliminated by a simple modification, and whether they actually affect estimation of real-world model calibration in practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240334/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes model logits h(X) (the inverse-sigmoid of predicted probabilities), binary or top-class labels, and a noise variance σ controlling the smoothing scale. It outputs a scalar LS-ECE value computed via Nadaraya-Watson kernel regression over sampled noisy logits, and under the condition σ_n → 0 with σ_n = ω(1/n), the estimator converges in probability to the true (unsmoothed) ECE. The noise ξ is drawn from a distribution with bounded density (e.g., Gaussian or uniform) and is added in logit space before the sigmoid is applied.

## Method

The paper first provides a complete characterization of ECE discontinuities on arbitrary Polish spaces, showing ECE is continuous at g if and only if ECE_π(g) = ‖g* − g‖_{L¹}, and is always lower semicontinuous. To eliminate discontinuities, it introduces Logit-Smoothed ECE (LS-ECE), which adds independent noise ξ to the logit h(X) before computing the conditional expectation, ensuring the smoothed predictor has no non-singleton level sets. Continuity is proven by showing that L^∞ convergence of logits implies total variation convergence of the joint distribution (Y, ρ(h(X)+ξ)), which in turn implies convergence of the ECE functional via a general lemma connecting TV convergence to ECE continuity. The practical estimator replaces the population conditional expectation with a Nadaraya-Watson kernel regression using the noise density as kernel, yielding an O(1/√(nσ)) estimation error bound and a 10-line PyTorch implementation.

## Results

On the synthetic 2-point distribution, binned ECE oscillates between ≈0 and ≈1/2 depending on bin parity, while LS-ECE remains effectively constant near zero across all variance scalings. On CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 (ResNet-32, VGG-16, MobileNetV2), ECE_BIN stays nearly unchanged as bins range from 1 to 100, and LS-ECE tracks it closely with only small visual variance. On ImageNet-1K-val across six architectures (ResNet-18/50, EfficientNet, MobileNetV3, ViT, RegNet), the mean absolute difference between ECE and LS-ECE is very small and stable across all bin numbers and σ values, and ECE, LS-ECE, and smECE take near-identical values for every model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against binned ECE (Naeini et al., 2014), LS-ECE demonstrates a clear advantage on the synthetic 2-point distribution where ECE_BIN exhibits pathological oscillation between ≈0 and ≈1/2 with bin parity, while LS-ECE remains stable near zero. However, on all practical image classification benchmarks (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet), LS-ECE closely tracks ECE_BIN across the full range of bin counts (1–100), indicating that the theoretical discontinuities of ECE do not manifest in real-world settings. Against smECE (Blasiok & Nakkiran, 2023), LS-ECE produces near-identical values on ImageNet across all six architectures tested, with the mean absolute difference between ECE and smECE being comparable to that between ECE and LS-ECE. The paper's key empirical conclusion is that both LS-ECE and smECE serve as reliable sanity checks confirming that reported ECE results in the literature are not particularly brittle to the choice of binning or smoothing parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the ECE framework of Naeini et al. (2014) and the known discontinuity pathologies identified by Kakade & Foster (2008), Foster & Hart (2018), and Blasiok et al. (2023). Its key theoretical departure is providing a complete necessary-and-sufficient characterization of ECE discontinuities in the general Polish space setting, going beyond the discrete finite-support case where discontinuities are simply a measure-zero set. The proposed LS-ECE metric was developed independently and in parallel to Blasiok & Nakkiran's (2023) smECE, sharing the kernel-smoothing philosophy but operating in logit space rather than probability space, which the authors argue avoids thresholding artifacts and yields cleaner theoretical guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central empirical finding—that binned ECE closely tracks the continuous LS-ECE across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet with diverse architectures—suggests the theoretical pathologies of ECE are largely avoidable in practice due to inherent data and training noise. This provides a principled sanity-check tool for the large body of calibration literature that relies on ECE, without requiring practitioners to switch metrics. The authors explicitly note this is not a claim that LS-ECE is superior to other alternatives, and they identify large-scale empirical validation of existing ECE results in the literature as a natural future direction. A limitation is that the empirical validation, while spanning multiple datasets and architectures, uses only pretrained models and does not test whether the ECE–LS-ECE agreement holds under adversarial or distribution-shift conditions.
