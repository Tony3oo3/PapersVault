## Problem

Existing score-based diffusion models for protein conformation generation cannot properly incorporate physical prior knowledge such as MD force fields to guide the sampling process, causing generated conformations to deviate significantly from the true Boltzmann equilibrium distribution. Traditional MD simulations, while physically grounded, suffer from rare-event sampling and femtosecond time-step constraints that make them impractical for exploring the full conformational landscape of general protein systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242345/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a protein amino acid sequence as input, which is encoded via ESMFold node representations and concatenated into the conditional score network's single embedding. It outputs an ensemble of protein backbone conformations parameterized as N rigid frames in SE(3)^N (translations in R^{3N} plus rotations in SO(3)^N), from which full-atom structures can be reconstructed via side-chain packing. Force guidance is applied exclusively to the translational (α-carbon) components during reverse sampling to maintain structural stability, while rotational components are guided only by the score model.

## Method

CONFDIFF builds on SE(3) diffusion with independent translation and rotation SDEs, using a classifier-free guidance scheme that linearly mixes a sequence-conditional score network with an unconditional one (controlled by γ) to balance conformation quality and diversity. The key novelty is an intermediate force-guided network h_ψ(x_t, t) that approximates the exact intermediate force ∇_{x_t} E_t(x_t) derived in Proposition 2, which bridges the ground-truth MD force at t=0 to zero at t=1. The network is parameterized as an interpolation h_ψ = (1−t)∇E_0(x_0) + t(1−t)g_ψ(x_t, t) to enforce boundary conditions and reduce training variance, and is trained with a contrastive loss using batch-estimated Boltzmann weights over self-generated conformations. During reverse sampling, the force guidance term is subtracted from the score at every SDE step (scaled by η), steering the sampler toward lower-energy conformations without requiring MD trajectory data during training. The architecture uses invariant point attention blocks with ESMFold-conditioned embeddings, and energy/force evaluation relies on OpenMM with Amber-ff14SB and implicit solvent after faspr side-chain packing.

## Results

On the 12 fast-folding proteins, CONFDIFF-FORCE achieves the best RMSE_contact (3.25/3.38 Å) and highest VAL-CA (0.97/0.98), while CONFDIFF-BASE attains the lowest JS distances on PwD (0.29/0.27) and RG (0.25/0.22). On BPTI metastable state prediction, CONFDIFF-FORCE achieves the best RMSD_AVG (1.41 Å) and RMSD_CLS3 (2.12 Å), approaching the reference MD sampling (1.10/2.19 Å) and substantially outperforming EIGENFOLD (1.47/2.45 Å) and STR2STR (2.06–2.26/2.46–2.80 Å). Force guidance improves validity from 0.89/0.91 (CONFDIFF-BASE) to 0.97/0.98 while maintaining comparable diversity (RMSF 6.2/5.7 vs. 6.1/5.9 Å).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CONFDIFF outperforms both EIGENFOLD and STR2STR across nearly all metrics. Against EIGENFOLD, CONFDIFF-FORCE reduces JS distance on PwD from 0.53 to 0.29, improves VAL-CA from 0.15 to 0.97, and lowers RMSE_contact from 6.18 to 3.25 Å; EIGENFOLD's fully-conditional design severely limits diversity (RMSF 1.6 vs. 6.2 Å). Against STR2STR-SDE, CONFDIFF-BASE achieves lower JS distances (0.29 vs. 0.34 on PwD, 0.25 vs. 0.30 on RG) and lower RMSE_contact (3.61 vs. 3.68), while CONFDIFF-FORCE further reduces RMSE_contact to 3.25. On BPTI, CONFDIFF-FORCE achieves RMSD_CLS3 of 2.12 Å versus 2.45 (EIGENFOLD) and 2.80 (STR2STR-SDE), and RMSD_AVG of 1.41 Å versus 1.47 (EIGENFOLD) and 2.26 (STR2STR-SDE), approaching the 1000-frame MD reference (2.19/1.10 Å). STR2STR generates more diverse samples (RMSF 7.8 Å) but at the cost of higher JS distances and worse contact prediction, suggesting its sequence-agnostic diffusion explores structure space less faithfully to the true Boltzmann distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

CONFDIFF builds on the SE(3) diffusion framework of FrameDiff (Yim et al., 2023b) for protein backbone generation and the classifier-free guidance mechanism of Ho & Salimans (2022) to balance conformation quality and diversity. Its physics-guided sampling strategy is directly inspired by the Contrastive Energy Prediction (CEP) framework of Lu et al. (2023), which the authors extend from scalar energy prediction to vector-valued intermediate force prediction. The key departure from prior diffusion-based conformation models (DiG, EIGENFOLD, Str2Str) is the introduction of a dedicated force-guided network that approximates the intermediate force field during reverse diffusion, avoiding the need for MD simulation data during training (unlike DiG) and the absence of sequence/energy conditioning (unlike Str2Str).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a fundamental gap in data-driven protein conformation sampling: generated structures often deviate from the Boltzmann equilibrium distribution because training data (PDB) captures only a few low-energy states per sequence. The force-guided approach offers a practical advantage over energy guidance by avoiding numerical instability from energy normalization, and the computational cost analysis shows force guidance adds negligible overhead compared to energy guidance. However, the full-atom MD energy evaluation via OpenMM remains computationally expensive (seconds per evaluation, multiplied by 1000 diffusion steps), and the limited conformation diversity in PDB constrains how well fully-conditional models can capture the full landscape. Open questions include developing more efficient surrogate force models, reducing training variance, and extending to larger, multi-domain proteins.
