## Problem

Existing diffusion-based graph generation models cannot enforce hard, non-differentiable user-specified constraints (e.g., maximum bond count, exact atom composition) without retraining the model or assuming differentiability of the constraint. Prior conditional and guidance-based approaches only provide soft, approximate control that is uninterpretable and insufficient for applications like drug discovery where precise structural constraints must be met exactly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241813/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes any pre-trained unconditional diffusion model (continuous or discrete) as a black box, along with user-specified constraint parameters such as a maximum edge count B, per-atom-type count bounds c, a molecular weight threshold W, or a dipole moment range [ξ₀, ξ₁]. It outputs graphs whose adjacency and attribute matrices provably satisfy the constraint (e.g., A1 ≤ δ_d·1) while remaining close in distribution to the original model's output. For 3D molecules, projection is applied directly in the latent space of node types and positions rather than in the extracted graph space, and a simple learned charge vector Q approximates the dipole moment for constraint evaluation.

## Method

PRODIGY augments the standard reverse diffusion step with a weighted projection step: after each denoising update, the intermediate sample is moved a fraction γ_t toward its closest point in the constraint feasible set C, i.e., G_{t-1} = (1-γ_t)·G̃_{t-1} + γ_t·Π_C(G̃_{t-1}). Two scheduling variants for γ_t are provided: a distance-based form exp(-β·d_C) that projects more aggressively when the sample is already near the feasible set, and a time-based polynomial form that increases projection weight at later timesteps. The projection operators Π_C are derived in closed form via KKT conditions for each constraint type (edge count, triangle count, degree, valency, atom count, molecular weight, dipole moment), reducing to matrix additions/subtractions followed by clamping, with the Lagrange multiplier μ found by bisection (or per-row bisection for vector-valued constraints like degree and atom count). For discrete diffusion models, a rounding step converts the continuous intermediate back to a discrete graph. The entire procedure requires no retraining, no access to training data or model parameters, and adds only O(n²)–O(n³) per-step overhead from the projection.

## Results

PRODIGY achieves up to 100% constraint satisfaction (VAL_C) across 5 non-attributed and 2 molecular graph datasets, using 4 different pre-trained diffusion models (EDP-GNN, GDSS, DruM, DiGress) without any retraining. On non-attributed graphs (Table 4), VAL_C reaches 1.00 in 8 out of 27 model-dataset-constraint combinations, with ΔMMD remaining near zero (|ΔMMD| < 0.05 in most cases), confirming distributional preservation. For 3D molecule generation (Table 8), PRODIGY on unconditional GeoLDM achieves a dipole moment MAE of 0.00 (perfect constraint satisfaction) compared to 1.10–1.11 for purpose-trained conditional models, while maintaining identical atomic (98.9%) and molecular (89.4%) stability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against purpose-trained conditional diffusion models for 3D molecule generation (Table 8): PRODIGY on unconditional GeoLDM achieves a dipole moment MAE of 0.00 (i.e., 100% constraint satisfaction), outperforming both EDM-conditional (MAE 1.11) and GeoLDM-conditional (MAE 1.10), while matching their atomic stability (98.9%) and molecular stability (89.4%). However, PRODIGY's proxy dipole estimator carries a bias of 1.15 versus 0.04 for the conditional models, meaning the absolute dipole values are less accurate even though the constraint is perfectly satisfied. For molecular property control (Table 12), GDSS+PRODIGY achieves a HOMO MAE of 0.29, beating DiGress+Guidance (0.56) by nearly 2×, but trails it on dipole moment MAE (1.09 vs. 0.81). The key differentiator is that PRODIGY requires no retraining, no condition labels, and no differentiability of the constraint, whereas all compared conditional/guidance methods require either retraining the model for each new constraint (EDM, GeoLDM-conditional) or training a separate classifier/guidance network (DiGress+Guidance).

## Relation to Prior Work

PRODIGY extends the theoretical framework of Projected Langevin Monte Carlo (Bubeck et al., 2018) and Mirrored Langevin Dynamics (Hsieh et al., 2018) to modern diffusion-based graph generation, where the underlying distribution is approximated by a neural network rather than known analytically. It departs from prior conditional generation approaches (Hoogeboom et al., 2022; Vignac et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2023) that require differentiable properties, model retraining, or labeled condition data, by instead solving closed-form projection operators via KKT conditions at each denoising step. The method is positioned as the first plug-and-play solution for hard, non-differentiable constraints on pre-trained graph diffusion models, bridging the gap between theoretical constrained sampling and practical deep generative models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PRODIGY provides a practical, model-agnostic mechanism for enforcing precise structural constraints (edge count, valency, molecular weight, dipole moment) during graph generation without retraining, which is critical for applications like drug discovery where exact property bounds are required. Key limitations include reduced constraint validity for molecular weight (due to negative-value artifacts in the projection), the inability to handle non-linear property approximators (e.g., GNN-based predictors), and unresolved questions about how to project in latent diffusion spaces where constraints are defined in graph space. The approach opens a path toward interpretable, constraint-satisfying generation across domains, but extending it to complex non-linear properties and latent-space diffusion remains an open challenge.
