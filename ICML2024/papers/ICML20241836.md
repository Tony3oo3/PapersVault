## Problem

Estimating counterfactual mean outcomes under dynamic treatment policies in high-dimensional longitudinal settings is computationally challenging: existing LTMLE methods perform sequential regressions backward from the last time point, cannot pool data across time, and propagate estimation errors from the hardest (last) regression. Deep learning alternatives like DeepACE either fail to handle survival outcomes, do not provide valid statistical inference (confidence intervals), or cannot reliably solve the efficient influence function for bias correction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241836/cropped_page7_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of heterogeneous tokens per patient — baseline covariates W, time-dependent covariates L_t, treatments A_t, and binary survival outcomes Y_t = 1{death by time t} — along with a user-specified dynamic treatment policy g that maps histories to treatment assignments. It produces the counterfactual mean outcome E_g[Y_τ] under policy g at the final time horizon, accompanied by a 95% confidence interval derived from the empirical efficient influence function. Censoring nodes C_t can be incorporated as additional tokens with a separate censoring mechanism in the clever covariate and loss.

## Method

The core architecture is a decoder-only transformer with heterogeneous type embeddings (separate linear layers and learnable type encodings for W, L, A, Y tokens) and positional encodings, processing the full longitudinal sequence in parallel rather than sequentially. Training uses temporal-difference learning: each Q_t is supervised to match V_{t+1} (the expectation of Q_{t+1} under the counterfactual policy g), pooling information across all time points simultaneously and avoiding the error propagation inherent in backward sequential regression. After obtaining initial fits, the method applies TMLE targeting by fluctuating all Q_t along a one-dimensional logit submodel with a single shared ε across time points (temporal-difference targeting), solving the empirical efficient influence function P_n D*(Q_ε, π) = 0 to correct plug-in bias while preserving the bounded [0,1] domain of survival probabilities. This single-ε targeting pools information across time during the correction step, contrasting with standard LTMLE's per-time-point sequential targeting where errors still propagate backward.

## Results

On complex synthetic survival data (5-D covariates, non-Markovian, n=1000), Deep LTMLE★ achieves the lowest RMSE among deep methods: 0.0218 (τ=10), 0.0344 (τ=20), 0.0541 (τ=30), matching LTMLE(SL) at τ=10 (0.0185) and outperforming it at longer horizons. DeepACE exhibits growing negative bias (−0.0211 at τ=20, −0.0714 at τ=30) and over-conservative coverage (1.00 with σ̂_n ≈ 0.73–0.76), while LTMLE(GLM) shows large bias (0.1344 at τ=30). On semi-synthetic CIRCS data with unmeasured confounding, Deep LTMLE achieves near-zero bias (−0.0002 to 0.0108) and nominal coverage (0.95–1.00) across all horizons, whereas LTMLE(SL) coverage drops to 0.25 at τ=30. Computationally, Deep LTMLE runs in 38–116 s on GPU versus 271–2122 s for LTMLE(SL) on CPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LTMLE (Lendle et al., 2017), Deep LTMLE★ matches or slightly exceeds LTMLE(SL) in RMSE at all horizons (0.0218 vs. 0.0185 at τ=10; 0.0344 vs. 0.0344 at τ=20; 0.0541 vs. 0.0545 at τ=30) while achieving far tighter standard error estimates (σ̂_n ≈ 0.07–0.17 vs. 0.31–0.45), yielding more honest coverage (0.95–0.99 vs. 1.00). LTMLE(GLM) degrades severely at longer horizons with bias reaching 0.1344 at τ=30. Against DeepACE (Frauen et al., 2023), Deep LTMLE★ wins decisively on bias (0.0307 vs. −0.0211 at τ=20; 0.0496 vs. −0.0714 at τ=30) and RMSE (0.0344 vs. 0.0672 at τ=20; 0.0541 vs. 0.0962 at τ=30), with the gap widening as τ increases. On semi-synthetic CIRCS data with unmeasured confounding, Deep LTMLE achieves near-zero bias (≤0.0108) and nominal coverage (≥0.95) at all horizons, while LTMLE(SL) coverage collapses to 0.25 at τ=30 and DeepACE bias reaches −0.0770. Computationally, Deep LTMLE is 7–18× faster than LTMLE(SL) (38 s vs. 271 s at τ=10; 116 s vs. 2122 s at τ=30) by leveraging GPU-parallel transformer training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LTMLE framework (van der Laan & Gruber, 2012) by replacing its sequential regression with a heterogeneous transformer trained via temporal-difference loss, enabling simultaneous fitting of all time-point nuisance parameters and pooling information across the time horizon. It departs from DeepACE (Frauen et al., 2023) in three key ways: separating the targeting step from the training loss (avoiding an unprincipled hyperparameter), handling survival outcomes with process degeneracy, and using a transformer instead of LSTM. The temporal-difference targeting procedure—a single fluctuation parameter ε shared across all time points—is a novel contribution that contrasts with both LTMLE's backward sequential targeting and DeepACE's integrated loss approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides valid asymptotic inference (95% CIs) for counterfactual mean outcomes under dynamic treatment policies in high-dimensional longitudinal settings where LTMLE suffers from error propagation and computational infeasibility, and where prior deep learning methods (DeepACE, Causal Transformer) lack inference tools. The application to the CIRCS cardiovascular cohort demonstrates practical utility for clinical evidence generation. Open questions include handling competing risks, continuous time horizons, and the mechanism behind the observed robustness to unmeasured confounding, which the authors note but do not formally characterize.
