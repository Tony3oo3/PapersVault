## Problem

Existing low-rank compression methods for DNNs (e.g., Pufferfish, Cuttlefish) rely on expensive iterative SVD decompositions that operate on learned weights without accounting for data distribution, require manual per-layer rank selection, and incur significant training overhead from full-rank warmup phases. The paper asks whether low-rank structure can be learned directly during training in a data-adaptive, progressive manner that automatically determines optimal per-layer ranks without iterative decompositions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard full-rank DNN (CNN or Transformer) and its training data (RGB images at 32×32 or 300×300, or text sequences for translation) and produces a progressively compressed low-rank model where each layer's weight matrix W is replaced by a product U·V^T with a data-determined rank r_i. Unlike post-hoc compression, the rank per layer is discovered during training and can be further reduced at deployment time via greedy rank removal without retraining, enabling a graceful accuracy-latency trade-off for constrained devices.

## Method

MAESTRO introduces LoD (Low-rank ordered Decomposition), which factorizes every linear layer's weight into U·V^T and trains by stochastically sampling a prefix of ranks b ≤ r_i per layer at each step, analogous to ordered dropout applied to the decomposed representation. A hierarchical group-lasso penalty on the factor matrices drives unimportant ranks toward zero, and a progressive shrinking rule removes ranks whose norm product falls below a threshold ε_ps at the end of each epoch, automatically reducing the model footprint. Theoretically, the formulation recovers truncated SVD under uniform data and PCA under identity mapping, while in practice it adapts to the data distribution and target function. At deployment, a greedy search over the learned ordered ranks enables further compression without fine-tuning, and the method requires only two hyperparameters (λ_gl and ε_ps) rather than per-layer rank specifications.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k with ResNet-50, MAESTRO achieves 76.04% top-1 accuracy at 14.0M parameters and 3.4 GMACs (partial decomposition), outperforming Pufferfish (75.99%, 15.2M) and Cuttlefish (76.00%, 14.9M). On Multi30k with a 6-layer Transformer, MAESTRO reaches 6.90±0.07 perplexity at 0.248 GMACs and 13.80M parameters, compared to Pufferfish's 7.34±0.12 at 0.996 GMACs and 26.70M parameters — a 6% perplexity improvement at 25% of the computational cost and 51.7% of the parameters. On CIFAR-10, MAESTRO achieves 94.19±0.07% with ResNet-18 at 4.08M parameters (vs. Pufferfish's 94.17% at 3.3M) and +0.41pp over Pufferfish with VGG-19 at 44.8% of the model size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAESTRO outperforms all six baselines across the evaluated settings. Against the low-rank baselines, MAESTRO achieves +0.05pp accuracy over Pufferfish and +0.04pp over Cuttlefish on ImageNet-1k (ResNet-50, partial decomposition) while using fewer parameters (14.0M vs. 15.2M/14.9M) and lower GMACs (3.4 vs. 3.6); under full decomposition it gains +0.51pp over Pufferfish (71.54% vs. 71.03%). On Multi30k, MAESTRO reduces perplexity by 6% (6.90 vs. 7.34) at only 25% of Pufferfish's MACs and 51.7% of its parameters. Against Spectral Initialization (Khodak et al.), MAESTRO achieves +5.26pp higher accuracy on VGG-19/CIFAR-10 at 87.5% of the parameter size. Against pruning baselines, MAESTRO gains +1.25pp over IMP on ResNet-18 and +6.82pp over RareGems on VGG-19 at 43.6% of the footprint. Against the quantization baseline XNOR-Net, MAESTRO achieves +1.08pp (ResNet-18) and +2.18pp (VGG-19) higher accuracy at the same model size, while also providing structured (ordered) sparsity compatible with modern hardware, unlike RareGems' unstructured sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAESTRO extends Ordered Dropout (Horvath et al., 2021) from operating on raw layer parameters to operating on the ranks of factorized weights, introducing a non-uniform search space where each layer can have a different rank. It departs from iterative SVD-based low-rank training methods (Pufferfish, Cuttlefish, Spectral Init) by embedding the decomposition directly into the training loop via stochastic rank sampling and a hierarchical group-lasso penalty, eliminating the need for repeated SVD calls, manual per-layer rank selection, and full-rank warmup phases. The theoretical analysis positions the formulation as a data-dependent generalization that recovers truncated SVD under uniform data and PCA under identity mapping, bridging classical linear algebra with learned compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAESTRO offers a practical path to progressive model compression where the rank structure is learned from data rather than imposed a priori, enabling a single training run to yield models at multiple compression levels via greedy rank selection at deployment. However, the paper acknowledges that there is no guarantee on the final model footprint (controlled only by two hyperparameters), the technique has not been evaluated on recurrent architectures or for actual energy savings on hardware, and the intriguing nested-rank phenomenon across layers remains unexplored. Open questions include whether alternative sampling strategies could accelerate convergence, how the method behaves under non-IID federated settings, and whether the nested structure can be exploited for more principled rank selection.
