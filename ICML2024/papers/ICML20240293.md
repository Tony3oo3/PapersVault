## Problem

Transformer-based operator learning relies on self-attention, which is content-based and requires distinct attention computations for every training instance, resulting in high memory usage and computational cost. The paper asks whether self-attention is truly indispensable, or whether the spatial interrelations of sampling positions alone—without dependence on input function values—can provide an equally effective and far more efficient attention mechanism for PDE operator learning.

## Input / Output

PiT accepts input function values sampled on an arbitrary mesh (structured grid or unstructured point cloud) together with the mesh point coordinates, and produces output function values at any queried set of points, potentially at a different resolution than the input. A key design choice is the use of a fixed, coarser latent mesh (e.g., 32×32 for Darcy2D) on which the Processor operates, decoupling computational cost from input/output mesh resolution. The pairwise-distance matrix between all relevant mesh points is precomputed from coordinates and serves as the sole structural input to the attention mechanism.

## Method

The core contribution is position-attention, which replaces the content-based Q·K^T scoring of self-attention with a fixed pairwise-distance matrix D weighted by a learnable decay parameter λ: Softmax(−λD)·U·W^V. This makes the attention linear in the input features, independent of function values, and provably convergent to a Gaussian-kernel integral operator as the mesh is refined (Theorem 2.1). Two variants extend the mechanism: cross position-attention performs discretization-convergent downsampling/upsampling between meshes of different resolutions (used in the Encoder and Decoder), and local position-attention restricts the receptive field to a quantile of nearest neighbors (used for capturing local features in hyperbolic PDEs). The full PiT architecture stacks four global position-attention modules in the Processor, each followed by a two-layer MLP and residual connection, with multi-head implementations (typically 2 heads) that learn different spatial scales of attention decay.

## Results

On six PDE operator learning benchmarks, PiT achieves the lowest relative error on 4 of 6 tasks: ShockTube (0.0122), Darcy2D (0.00485), Vorticity (0.1140), and NACA (0.00480). On InviscidBurgers, PiT (0.0136) is second to FNO++ (0.00995), and on Elasticity, PiT (0.00649) is second to ONO (0.0056). In the discretization convergence test on Darcy2D, PiT trained on a 43² mesh achieves only 4.50% error on the 421² test mesh, 48% lower than FNO++'s 8.67% error. PiT also uses substantially fewer parameters (e.g., 313K on Darcy2D vs. 2.2–2.5M for Transformer baselines) and trains faster (14.7 s/epoch vs. 22 s/epoch for Galerkin Transformer).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PiT decisively outperforms DeepONet and shift-DeepONet on both hyperbolic benchmarks (InviscidBurgers: 0.0136 vs. 0.285/0.0783; ShockTube: 0.0122 vs. 0.0422/0.0276), reducing errors by 95% and 84% respectively. Against FNO/FNO++, PiT wins on ShockTube (0.0122 vs. 0.0156/0.0194), Darcy2D (0.00485 vs. 0.0109/0.00509), Vorticity (0.1140 vs. 0.1559/0.1315), and NACA (0.00480 vs. 0.0421), but FNO++ edges out PiT on InviscidBurgers (0.00995 vs. 0.0136). Among Transformer-based baselines, PiT beats OFormer and Galerkin Transformer on every benchmark where both are reported (e.g., Darcy2D: 0.00485 vs. 0.0128/0.00844; NACA: 0.00480 vs. 0.0183/0.0161). PiT also surpasses GNOT on Vorticity (0.1140 vs. 0.138) and NACA (0.00480 vs. 0.00757), and beats ONO on Vorticity (0.1140 vs. 0.1195) and NACA (0.00480 vs. 0.0056), though ONO is slightly better on Elasticity (0.0056 vs. 0.00649). PiT dominates Geo-FNO on both irregular-geometry tasks (Elasticity: 0.00649 vs. 0.0229; NACA: 0.00480 vs. 0.0138). In the ablation (Table 2), replacing position-attention with vanilla self-attention consistently degrades accuracy and, in three of six benchmarks, causes out-of-memory failures on a 24 GB GPU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Transformer architecture (Vaswani et al., 2017) and the neural operator framework (Kovachki et al., 2023), but makes a fundamental departure by replacing content-based self-attention with a position-attention mechanism induced solely by pairwise distances between sampling points. Prior Transformer-based neural operators (Galerkin Transformer, OFormer, GNOT, ONO) all rely on self-attention that computes distinct weights per input instance, whereas PiT's position-attention uses a fixed, pre-computable kernel that draws direct inspiration from numerical PDE schemes (e.g., upwind methods and domain-of-dependence principles). The cross and local position-attention variants further connect to downsampling/upsampling operations in multi-resolution numerical solvers, and the theoretical convergence analysis parallels Monte Carlo integration of integral operators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that self-attention is not necessary for Transformer-based operator learning, offering a more efficient and interpretable alternative grounded in numerical PDE theory. The discretization convergence property (48% lower error than FNO on Darcy2D zero-shot superresolution) is practically significant for deploying surrogate models across mesh resolutions without retraining. Open questions include scalability to high-dimensional PDEs where the latent mesh may require many points, and whether sparse or low-rank approximations of the quadratic distance kernel can further reduce cost. The framework also opens the door to combining position-attention with other efficiency techniques (random features, Nyström approximation) for even larger-scale problems.
