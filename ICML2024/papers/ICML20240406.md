## Problem

Despite the widespread use of linkage methods in hierarchical clustering, theoretical guarantees on the quality of the clusters they produce remain limited. In particular, the best known upper bound on the maximum diameter of complete-linkage was expressed in terms of OPT_DM(k) (the optimal maximum diameter), which obscures the well-known practical advantage of complete-linkage over single-linkage for producing compact clusters. This paper tightens these bounds by re-expressing them relative to OPT_AV(k) (the optimal average diameter) and by improving the exponent for large k.

## Input / Output

The input is a set of n points in an arbitrary metric space (X, dist). The linkage algorithm produces a full hierarchical clustering (a sequence of n−1 merges from n singletons down to one cluster), and the paper's contribution is to bound the diameter (or average pairwise distance, or radius) of the k-clustering obtained by cutting the dendrogram at level k. The key distinction from prior work is that the bounds are stated relative to OPT_AV(k) rather than OPT_DM(k), and since OPT_AV(k) can be as small as OPT_DM(k)/k, this yields up to a factor-k improvement in the worst case.

## Method

The core technique maintains a dynamic partition of the clusters built by complete-linkage into 'families' organized in a directed forest, where each family's diameter is bounded in terms of the diameters of the target optimal clusters it touches. For the k^{1.59}·OPT_AV(k) bound, the analysis tracks two quantities per family—φ(F) (number of leaves in the forest subtree) and φ_Σ(F) (sum of leaf diameters)—and proves by induction that diam(F) ≤ φ_Σ(F)·φ(F)^{log₂3−1}. For the tighter k^{1.30}·OPT_DM(k) bound (k>4) and (2k−2)·OPT_DM(k) bound (k≤4), the authors introduce a more involved mechanism: a dynamic graph G tracking cross-family merges, a set of excluded clusters, and the notion of 'pure clusters,' then bound the diameter of each newly created family via a spanning tree whose edge weights are the diameters of the clusters that connected the families. Finally, the framework generalizes to any linkage method whose inter-cluster distance function aligns with a cohesion criterion, yielding the first cohesion guarantee for average-linkage (k^{1.59}·OPT_AV(k) on average pairwise distance) and a matching bound for minimax on radius.

## Results

The paper establishes that the maximum diameter of the k-clustering produced by complete-linkage is at most k^1.59 · OPT_AV(k) (Theorem 3.3), improving the previous best bound of O(k^1.59 · OPT_DM(k)) by up to a factor of k since OPT_AV(k) ≤ OPT_DM(k). A tighter analysis yields (2k−2)·OPT_DM(k) for k ≤ 4 and k^1.30·OPT_DM(k) for k > 4 (Theorem 4.5), narrowing the gap to the Ω(k·OPT_DM(k)) lower bound. For small k, the approximation factors are 2, 4, and 6 for k = 2, 3, 4 respectively, improving on the previous factors of 3, 5.71, and 9. The techniques extend to average-linkage (max average pairwise distance ≤ k^1.59·OPT_AV(k)) and minimax (max radius ≤ k^1.59·OPT_AV(k)), providing the first cohesion-criterion guarantees for average-linkage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against the bounds of Arutyunova et al. (2021, 2023), who established O(k²·OPT_DM(k)) and then O(k^1.59·OPT_DM(k)) as upper bounds for complete-linkage. This paper improves the exponent from 1.59 to 1.30 when measured against OPT_DM (for k>4), and introduces the stronger k^1.59·OPT_AV(k) bound which can be up to a factor of k tighter. A key qualitative contribution is the separation between complete-linkage and single-linkage: using OPT_AV as the reference, complete-linkage achieves O(k^1.59·OPT_AV(k)) while single-linkage has an Ω(k²·OPT_AV(k)) lower bound (Theorem 3.4), confirming the practical intuition that complete-linkage produces more compact clusters. For small k (the most practically relevant regime), the new (2k−2) factors of 2, 4, 6 for k=2,3,4 strictly improve on the previous 3, 5.71, 9 from Arutyunova et al. (2023), and approach the 2-approximation hardness barrier (Megiddo 1990). The extension to average-linkage and minimax (Theorems 5.3–5.4) provides the first cohesion-criterion guarantees for these methods, with no prior comparable bounds in the literature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the family-partitioning analysis framework introduced by Arutyunova et al. (2021, 2023) for bounding the diameter of complete-linkage clusters, but departs from it in two key ways. First, it replaces the natural OPT_DM (minimax diameter) benchmark with OPT_AV (average diameter), which is a strictly weaker target and enables a separation between complete-linkage and single-linkage that was impossible under OPT_DM. Second, for the tighter small-k bound, it introduces a more sophisticated graph-based tracking of inter-family merges (Algorithm 4) that avoids the over-counting inherent in the simpler family-creation rule of Algorithm 3.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OPT_AV-based separation between complete-linkage and single-linkage validates a long-held practitioner intuition with a clean theoretical argument, and the improved small-k bounds (2k−2 for k≤4) are practically relevant since most applications use few clusters. The extension to average-linkage and minimax provides the first cohesion-criterion guarantees for these widely used methods. Open questions include whether the gap between the k^1.30 upper bound and the Ω(k) lower bound for complete-linkage can be closed, and whether the graph-based technique of Section 4 can be adapted to other cohesion criteria or higher-dimensional settings.
