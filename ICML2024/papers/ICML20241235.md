## Problem

In causal bandits with post-action contexts, algorithms exploiting the conditionally benign structure (e.g., C-UCB) achieve regret scaling with |Z| rather than |A|, but fail catastrophically in non-benign environments. Bilodeau et al. (2022) showed strict adaptivity is impossible and proposed HAC-UCB with T^{3/4} worst-case regret, but left open whether this rate was the unavoidable price of adaptivity. This paper resolves that open question by characterizing the full Pareto optimal frontier of simultaneously achievable regret rates across benign and arbitrary environments.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes as input the action set A, context set Z, marginal distributions q over Z for each action (which may be approximate), horizon T, and confidence level δ, and produces an adaptive policy that simultaneously achieves near-minimax regret in both conditionally benign and arbitrary environments. A key structural constraint is that the product of the benign and arbitrary regret rates must satisfy R₁·R₂ ≥ |A|T, meaning the worst-case rate necessarily degrades by a factor of √(|A|/|Z|) relative to the non-adaptive minimax rate. Without any knowledge of the true marginals, no algorithm can beat the non-causal minimax rate even in benign environments.

## Method

The adaptive algorithm applies Dynamic Balancing (DB) as a meta-learner over two base learners: C-UCB (optimal in benign settings) and UCB (robust in arbitrary settings), with hyper-parameters tuned to realize any point on the Pareto frontier satisfying the product constraint. A novel reduction maps causal bandits to linear bandits by ignoring realized contexts and treating each action's marginal vector ν_a as a linear action vector with conditionally sub-Gaussian noise, enabling the use of phased elimination with G-optimal design. This yields the first instance-dependent regret bound O(d_ν log(|A|T)/Δ_min) for causal bandits, where d_ν = dim(span{ν_a}) is a new complexity measure that can be strictly smaller than |Z|. Matching lower bounds are proven via a two-instance construction using KL divergence and Bretagnolle-Huber inequalities, establishing that the √(|A|/|Z|) price of adaptivity is unavoidable. For approximate marginals, the phased elimination analysis shows ε = Õ(1/√T) closeness suffices to recover all guarantees, while zero marginal knowledge provably precludes any causal improvement.

## Results

The paper establishes that the Pareto optimal frontier of simultaneously achievable regret rates in conditionally benign and arbitrary environments is characterized by the product constraint R₁(T)·R₂(T) ≥ |A|T (up to constants). The Dynamic Balancing (DB) algorithm achieves this frontier, yielding Ō(√(|Z|T)) regret in benign environments and Ō(√(|A|/|Z|)·√(|A|T)) regret in arbitrary environments. Additionally, a novel reduction to linear bandits yields the first instance-dependent bound of O(d_ν log(|A|T)/Δ_min) for conditionally benign environments, where d_ν = dim(span{ν_a}) ≤ |Z| is an intrinsic complexity measure. A lower bound shows that without any knowledge of the marginal distributions, no algorithm can beat the Ō(√(|A|T)) minimax rate even in benign environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against HAC-UCB (Bilodeau et al., 2022), the previously best adaptive causal bandit algorithm. HAC-UCB achieves Ō(√(|Z|T)) regret in benign environments but only Ō(T^{3/4}) in the worst case. The proposed Dynamic Balancing (DB) algorithm matches the benign rate while improving the worst-case rate to Ō(√(|A|/|Z|)·√(|A|T)), which is strictly better than T^{3/4} whenever |A|/|Z| < T^{1/4}. The paper proves via a matching lower bound (Theorem 3.7) that no algorithm can simultaneously achieve better rates than DB on the Pareto frontier, establishing that the extra √(|A|/|Z|) factor is the irreducible 'price of adaptivity.' Additionally, the phased elimination algorithm with the causal-to-linear reduction provides a strictly tighter worst-case bound depending on d_ν (the span dimension of the marginal vectors) rather than |Z|, and yields the first instance-dependent logarithmic regret for causal bandits in benign settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Bilodeau et al. (2022), which introduced the conditionally benign framework, the HAC-UCB adaptive algorithm, and the open question of whether HAC-UCB is Pareto optimal. The adaptive upper bound leverages the dynamic balancing (DB) model selection method of Cutkosky et al. (2021), while the instance-dependent results rely on a novel reduction from causal bandits to linear bandits with conditional sub-Gaussian noise, enabling the use of phased elimination with G-optimal design from Lattimore et al. (2020). The key departure from prior work is showing that HAC-UCB is strictly dominated by an instance of DB, and that the Pareto frontier is fully characterized (up to log factors) by the product constraint R₁·R₂ ≥ |A|T.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper resolves a concrete open question in causal bandits by providing a complete Pareto frontier characterization, establishing that the 'price of adaptivity' is a multiplicative factor of √(|A|/|Z|) in worst-case regret. The causal-to-linear reduction is a methodological contribution that enables the first instance-dependent (logarithmic) regret bounds in conditionally benign environments and opens the door to applying the broader linear bandit algorithmic toolkit. Open questions remain around adaptive instance-dependent regret (whether O(log T) can be achieved in both benign and non-benign settings simultaneously), the design of simpler algorithms than running DB over base learners, and extending the analysis to 'semi-benign' intermediate settings.
