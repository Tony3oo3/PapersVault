## Problem

Existing ML-based mask optimization methods (GAN-OPC, Neural-ILT, AdaOPC) can only generate initial masks for numerical ILT solvers and lack lithography grounding during inference, causing training-inference distribution shift. This paper asks whether an ML model can directly produce high-quality optimized masks without any numerical ILT solver in the loop, by grounding the model with intermediate lithography estimates at each optimization step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242324/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 2048×2048 grayscale chip design pattern (1 nm² pixel pitch, 2 μm × 2 μm tile) and, at each unrolling step, also consumes the lithography-simulated wafer image of the current mask estimate, making it a multi-step iterative process rather than a single-shot design-to-mask mapping. The output is a 2048×2048 grayscale optimized mask that includes sub-resolution assist features (SRAFs). Training uses 100K+ design-mask pairs from LithoBench, where ground-truth masks are generated by a GPU-accelerated numerical ILT solver.

## Method

ILILT reformulates mask optimization as a weight-tied fixed-point (implicit layer) problem where the update rule M_{t+1} = g(M_t, Z_t, Z*, w) embeds a differentiable lithography estimator f_l so that the wafer image Z_t of the current mask is fed back into g at every step, grounding the network with physical lithography conditions. Instead of solving the full DEQ fixed point via Newton's method, ILILT unrolls the weight-tied sequence to a fixed depth T (4–8 steps) and trains with back-propagation through time using a progressive loss that exponentially up-weights later unrolling steps to encourage smooth mask growth toward the ground truth. The backbone g is either CFNO (patch-based Fourier Neural Operator) or Pix2PixHD (hierarchical CNN), and the weight-tied structure reduces parameter count while improving generalization over standalone backbones and over non-shared learning-to-optimize unrolling.

## Results

On the LithoBench benchmark (2048×2048 design clips, 1 nm² pixel size), ILILT-P2PHD-8-45M achieves the lowest EPE violation count of 0.08, outperforming the GPU-accelerated numerical ILT solver (0.21) by 62% while running 5× faster (0.8 s/tile vs. 4 s/tile). ILILT-CFNO-8-130K reaches 0.25 EPE with a 400× smaller model than GPU-ILT. Across all unrolling depths (T=2–8), ILILT consistently beats standalone backbone models (Pix2PixHD: 0.12 EPE; CFNO: 0.51 EPE) and learning-to-optimize variants (L2O-P2PHD: 0.87; L2O-CFNO: 1.59), with PVB areas remaining comparable across all methods (~4600–4700 nm²).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ILILT-P2PHD-8 decisively outperforms the numerical GPU-ILT solver on EPE (0.08 vs. 0.21, a 62% reduction) while achieving a 5× throughput improvement (0.8 s vs. 4 s per tile), with PVB areas essentially tied (~4695 vs. 4656 nm²). Against the ML-based baselines, ILILT-P2PHD-8 reduces EPE by 99% over GAN-OPC (0.08 vs. 8.3) and 99% over Neural-ILT (0.08 vs. 6.2), also improving PVB substantially (4695 vs. 6686 and 8537 nm²). Compared to standalone backbone models, the weight-tied ILILT training yields a 33% EPE reduction over Pix2PixHD (0.08 vs. 0.12) and a 51% reduction over CFNO (0.25 vs. 0.51). ILILT also outperforms the learning-to-optimize (L2O) variants with non-shared weights: EPE drops from 0.87 to 0.08 (P2PHD) and from 1.59 to 0.25 (CFNO), confirming that weight-tying prevents error propagation across unrolled steps. No method shows a significant PVB advantage, as none explicitly targets process-variation robustness in its optimization objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Deep Equilibrium Model (DEQ) framework for implicit layer learning, adapting its fixed-point formulation to the inverse lithography problem, and on the implicit function theorem as the mathematical foundation for defining joint input-output conditions. It departs from prior ML-for-lithography work (GAN-OPC, Neural-ILT, DAMO, AdaOPC) that uses learned models only to generate initialization for numerical ILT solvers, instead formulating the full optimization as a weight-tied implicit layer with lithography-conditioned inputs at each step. Unlike learning-to-optimize (L2O) approaches that unroll optimization with independent per-step parameters, ILILT shares weights across unrolling steps, reducing model size and improving generalization while embedding a forward lithography simulator as a physics-grounding signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstrated AI solution that directly produces optimized masks without a numerical ILT solver in the inference loop, achieving lower EPE violations than the GPU-accelerated numerical baseline at 10× throughput. The ability to take over from any point in an optimization trajectory (fixing partially-optimized masks) addresses a practical limitation of design-to-mask mappers. However, evaluation is confined to 2μm×2μm clips on a single benchmark (LithoBench), PVB area shows no meaningful improvement, and the authors explicitly note that production-level full-chip validation remains necessary to confirm manufacturing readiness.
