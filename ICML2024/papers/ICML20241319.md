## Problem

Federated learning is limited by system heterogeneity: resource-constrained clients cannot train the full model, and existing solutions (pruning, depth-scaling, distillation, heterogeneous low-rank) assign different sub-models to different clients, causing performance degradation during heterogeneous aggregation. Moreover, most of these methods are heuristic-based and lack convergence guarantees linking the trained sub-models back to the original full model.

## Input / Output

Each client receives its local non-IID dataset and a resource capacity level β that determines the low-rank ratio α of its model. All clients train the same pre-factorized low-rank architecture (hybrid: first ρ layers full-rank, remaining layers decomposed into U and V matrices), so the server aggregates homogeneous parameters without channel or depth mismatch. At the end of training, the full model is recovered via W = UV^T for each decomposed layer, yielding a standard full-size model with the same forward-pass output as the low-rank model. In pFedLMT, only the common (undecomposed) layers are uploaded for aggregation while custom (decomposed) layers with client-specific rank ratios remain local.

## Method

FedLMT trains a single homogeneous low-rank model across all clients using standard FedAvg, avoiding the heterogeneous aggregation problem entirely. The key theoretical contribution is proving that convergence of the low-rank model x in the low-rank weight space W_g implies convergence of the recovered full model w in the full weight space W_f, via the chain-rule inequality ||∇g^l(x)||² ≥ (σ_min²(U) + σ_min²(V))||∇f^l(w)||², under non-convex smooth assumptions and a bounded-smallest-singular-value condition (supported by Marchenko-Pastur theory). The hybrid model architecture (keeping the first ρ layers unfactorized) and regularization terms (Frobenius decay, L2 decay, Kronecker decay) are shown to tighten the convergence bound. pFedLMT extends this by splitting parameters into common layers (aggregated globally) and custom layers (client-specific rank ratios, kept local), simultaneously addressing both system and data heterogeneity with convergence inherited from the FedSim framework.

## Results

Under the β4-β3-β2 heterogeneous setting (where no client trains the full model), FedLMT achieves 91.03% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10 and 71.08% on CIFAR-100, within 1% of the full-resource FedAvg upper bound (91.91% and 72.20%), while using only the smallest model capacity β2. Communication cost drops from 223.5 GB (FedAvg) to 28.62 GB (~87% reduction) and FLOPs from 11.18×10¹² to 2.80×10¹² (~75% reduction) on CIFAR-10. On TinyImageNet, FedLMT (48.53%) surpasses all baselines including DepthFL (44.84%), and on WikiText-2 it achieves the best perplexity of 2.93 versus 3.06 (HeteroFL) and 3.52 (FedAvg).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedLMT outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset where they are comparable. On CIFAR-10, FedLMT (91.03%) beats the strongest baseline FedRolex (89.11%) by ~2% and HeteroFL (85.02%) by ~6%, while using only 21% of the communication cost. On TinyImageNet, FedLMT (48.53%) leads DepthFL (44.84%) by 3.7% and FedHM (36.30%) by 12.2%. On WikiText-2, FedLMT achieves the lowest perplexity (2.93) versus HeteroFL (3.06) and FedAvg (3.52), with FedDropout failing entirely (4157.1). The key advantage is that FedLMT trains a homogeneous low-rank model at the smallest capacity β2, avoiding the performance degradation from heterogeneous sub-model aggregation that plagues width-scale (FedDropout, HeteroFL, FedRolex) and depth-scale (DepthFL) methods. Among low-rank baselines, FedLMT surpasses FedHM (which requires frequent SVD operations) and FLANC (which uses all-in-one neural composition) on accuracy while consuming less than 50% of their computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedLMT builds on the FedAvg training loop, the hybrid model architecture from Pufferfish (Wang et al., 2021) that avoids factorizing initial layers, and the Frobenius decay / spectral initialization techniques from Khodak et al. (2021). It departs sharply from prior system-heterogeneity methods (FedHM, HeteroFL, DepthFL, FLANC) by replacing heterogeneous sub-model aggregation with homogeneous pre-factorized low-rank training, thereby eliminating the approximation errors from repeated SVD operations and the parameter-mismatch problems of pruning/depth-splitting. For personalization, pFedLMT adapts the common/custom layer split from FedRep (Collins et al., 2021) and reuses the FedSim convergence framework (Pillutla et al., 2022), but the key theoretical novelty is Theorem 4.5, which to the authors' knowledge is the first to prove that convergence of a low-rank model in its own weight space implies convergence of the recovered full model in the full weight space under non-convex smooth assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first convergence guarantee linking low-rank model training to full-model convergence in FL, giving practitioners a theoretically grounded alternative to heuristic heterogeneous methods. The dramatic efficiency gains (up to ~87% communication reduction and ~75% FLOPs reduction while matching or exceeding accuracy) make FL viable for genuinely resource-constrained edge devices. Open questions remain: the physical interpretation of why low-rank training in a lower-dimensional space can drive convergence in the full space is not fully explained, the role of the smallest singular value and low-rank ratio on convergence rate is only loosely captured by the theory, and whether these results transfer to the dominant pre-training + fine-tuning paradigm (e.g., LoRA-style adaptation of LLMs) is left for future work.
