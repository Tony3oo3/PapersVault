## Problem

Prevailing LLM pruning methods (SparseGPT, Wanda) uniformly prune all layers at the same sparsity ratio, a default inherited from BERT-era pruning. However, LLMs exhibit a highly non-uniform distribution of activation outliers across layers, and the paper shows that pruning performance at high sparsity (≥70%) is strongly correlated with how well outliers are preserved per layer. The gap is that no prior work has exploited this non-uniform outlier structure to assign differentiated layerwise sparsity ratios for LLMs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM, a small calibration set (128 sequences × 2048 tokens from C4), and a target global sparsity S. It produces a sparse LLM where each Transformer block receives a distinct sparsity ratio constrained to [S−λ, S+λ] (with λ typically 8–20%), averaging to S across all blocks. Sparsity is assigned per Transformer block rather than per individual linear layer, and the resulting sparse model is compatible with standard inference engines like DeepSparse for unstructured sparsity acceleration.

## Method

OWL first computes the Layerwise Outlier Distribution (LOD) by scoring each weight with the Wanda metric A_ij = ‖X_j‖₂ · |W_ij| across all calibration tokens, then identifying weights whose score exceeds M times the layer mean (M=5 or 7). The layerwise sparsity is set proportional to (1 − D_i), where D_i is the outlier ratio of block i, so blocks with denser outlier populations receive lower sparsity to preserve those critical weights. A hyperparameter λ constrains each block's sparsity to [S−λ, S+λ], preventing the overly aggressive inter-layer differences that cause collapse (as seen with global pruning). OWL is a drop-in layerwise sparsity strategy that plugs into existing pruning metrics (Wanda or SparseGPT) without modifying their weight-selection or update logic, incurring negligible additional computation (≤2 seconds).

## Results

At 70% sparsity on WikiText, OWL w. Wanda reduces LLaMA-7B perplexity from 85.77 (Wanda alone) to 24.55, a gain of 61.22 perplexity points. OWL w. SparseGPT further improves LLaMA-7B to 19.49 perplexity (vs. 26.30 for SparseGPT alone). On zero-shot tasks at 70% sparsity, OWL achieves an average accuracy gain of 4.72 points over Wanda and 2.19 points over SparseGPT across 7 benchmarks. In the DeepSparse inference engine, OWL delivers 2.6× end-to-end decode speedup at 70% sparsity and up to 3.9× at 90% sparsity on CPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OWL w. Wanda dramatically outperforms Wanda with uniform sparsity at 70% sparsity, reducing LLaMA-7B WikiText perplexity from 85.77 to 24.55 (−61.22) and LLaMA-13B from 55.90 to 17.17 (−38.73). OWL w. SparseGPT also consistently beats SparseGPT with uniform sparsity, achieving 19.49 vs. 26.30 perplexity on LLaMA-7B and 14.55 vs. 19.24 on LLaMA-13B, though the gap narrows for larger models (8.28 vs. 10.45 on LLaMA-65B). Magnitude pruning is catastrophically outperformed by both OWL variants at all sparsity levels (e.g., 48419 vs. 24.55 perplexity on LLaMA-7B at 70%). In the layerwise sparsity ablation (Table 8), OWL outperforms Uniform, ER, ER-plus, and Global sparsity strategies across all sparsity levels above 50%, with the largest gap at 70% (24.54 vs. 85.77 for Uniform, 112.03 for ER, 229.17 for ER-plus, and 5147 for Global). In structured pruning (Table 13), OWL reduces LLaMA-7B WikiText perplexity from 1228.17 to 321.64 at 80% sparsity compared to LLM Pruner with uniform sparsity. On vision models (ConvNeXt, DeiT), OWL provides only marginal or no improvement over uniform Wanda, consistent with the hypothesis that activation outliers are less prominent in vision architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Wanda's pruning metric (weight magnitude × input activation norm) to define a Layerwise Outlier Distribution (LOD), and on SparseGPT as a strong second-order baseline. Conceptually, it departs from the prevailing assumption in LLM pruning (inherited from BERT pruning work) that uniform layerwise sparsity is optimal, by importing the non-uniform sparsity principle well-established in vision model pruning and grounding it in the emergent outlier structure unique to LLMs. The LOD-based sparsity assignment is a novel combination: the outlier-preservation insight from Wanda/Dettmers et al. meets the scale-free topology ideas from Mocanu et al. and Liu et al., but adapted specifically to the U-shaped outlier distribution observed across Transformer layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OWL pushes one-shot, training-free LLM pruning to 70% sparsity with substantially lower perplexity than prior SOTA, and delivers 2.6×–3.9× end-to-end CPU inference speedup, making high-sparsity LLM deployment on commodity hardware more practical. The LOD concept generalizes beyond unstructured pruning to N:M sparsity, structured pruning, mixed-precision quantization, and SVD low-rank approximation, suggesting a broader utility as a layer-importance signal. Limitations include diminishing gains at larger model sizes (6.62 perplexity improvement at 65B vs. 61.22 at 7B), minimal benefit on vision models where outliers are less prominent, and the fact that at 80%+ sparsity all methods still degrade severely. Open questions include whether LOD-guided sparsity can be combined with other compression axes (quantization + pruning) in a principled way, and whether the U-shaped outlier distribution is an artifact of specific architectures or a universal property of scaled Transformers.
