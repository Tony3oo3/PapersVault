## Problem

Existing methods either discover novel classes under the assumption of a shared domain (open-world semi-supervised learning) or handle domain shift but reject all unseen samples as outliers without discovering their internal structure (universal domain adaptation). This paper introduces the Cross-Domain Open-World Discovery (CD-OWD) setting, which requires simultaneously recognizing seen classes and discovering novel classes under a domain shift—a combination neither prior setting addresses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242163/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled source set from one domain and an unlabeled target set from a different domain, with the total number of target classes |C_t| provided as a prior. Each target sample is assigned either a seen class label (from the source) or a discovered novel class label. The seen/unseen split is defined by sorting classes alphabetically and designating the last n as unseen; the source set is then filtered to exclude those unseen classes. Evaluation uses the H-score (harmonic mean of seen and unseen accuracy), with unseen accuracy computed via Hungarian matching between discovered clusters and ground-truth novel classes.

## Method

CROW employs a three-step cluster-then-match procedure built on the well-structured representation space of a foundation model (CLIP ViT-L). First, it trains L2-normalized seen prototypes via cross-entropy on source features and clusters all target features with K-means (k=|C_t|) to obtain target prototypes. Second, it computes a co-occurrence matrix between target prototypes and seen classes, applies a column-wise softmax, and thresholds at τ=0.3 to produce a matching matrix that allows many-to-many associations—multiple seen classes can match one cluster and one seen class can match multiple clusters—thereby handling over- and under-clustering. Unmatched target prototypes become unseen prototypes. Third, it concatenates seen and unseen prototypes into a linear classifier and fine-tunes the last two ViT-L blocks plus the classifier using a combined objective: cross-entropy loss on source samples plus entropy-maximization regularization on target samples (weighted by λ=0.1). This contrasts with the match-then-cluster strategy of UniDA methods, which first threshold predictions to separate seen/unseen and then cluster the residual, a process sensitive to threshold selection and noisy misclassifications.

## Results

CROW achieves a 71.1% average H-score across 75 experimental settings (4 datasets × 3 seen/unseen ratios × multiple domain pairs), outperforming the strongest baseline GLC (65.8%) by 8% on average. On the 50% seen/unseen split, CROW reaches 84.9% H-score on Office, 69.6% on OfficeHome, 69.2% on VisDA, and 59.0% on DomainNet. The method is robust to hyperparameters, using a fixed threshold τ=0.3 and λ=0.1 across all experiments, and benefits from stronger foundation models (SWAG ViT-H achieves 70.3% H-score on VisDA vs. 69.2% for CLIP ViT-L).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CROW consistently outperforms all 10 baselines across all 12 dataset/split configurations. Against the strongest UniDA baseline GLC (65.8% avg H-score), CROW achieves a 5.3-point absolute improvement (71.1%), with the gap widening to 9.4 points over DCC, which also uses cluster-then-match but enforces one-to-one matching. Compared to the prior cross-domain discovery method NCDDA (64.3%), CROW gains 6.8 points, attributed to its robust matching that handles over/under-clustering. OW-SSL methods (ORCA at 43.7%, GCD at 44.2%) lag by roughly 30 points, confirming they cannot handle domain shift. Among UniDA baselines, SAN (65.7%) and OVANet (64.7%) are the closest competitors, but CROW still leads by 5.4 and 6.4 points respectively. On the 50% split, CROW achieves 80.3% unseen accuracy on Office vs. 77.6% (UniOT) and 74.7% (NCDDA), while maintaining 90.0% seen accuracy, demonstrating balanced performance across both seen and novel classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

CROW occupies the intersection of open-world semi-supervised learning (OW-SSL), which handles categorical shift but assumes a single domain, and universal domain adaptation (UniDA), which handles domain shift but rejects rather than discovers unseen classes. Methodologically, it builds on DCC's cluster-then-match strategy but replaces the rigid one-to-one matching with a robust many-to-many prototype matching via a co-occurrence matrix, and it leverages the well-structured latent spaces of foundation models (CLIP, DINOv2, SWAG) to make initial clustering reliable without self-supervised pretraining on target data. The paper departs from the match-then-cluster pipeline used by most UniDA methods, arguing that threshold-sensitive seen/unseen separation before clustering introduces noise that degrades novel class discovery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-domain open-world discovery setting mirrors realistic deployment scenarios (e.g., medical imaging across hospitals where both domain shift and new disease categories arise), and CROW provides the first method to address it with a proper evaluation protocol using H-score and Hungarian-matched unseen accuracy. A key open question is the method's dependence on foundation model robustness: CROW degrades sharply on domains like DomainNet Quickdraw where CLIP's representations are weak, suggesting that the approach's ceiling is bounded by the quality of the underlying representation. The paper also highlights an underexplored evaluation challenge—discovered classes may be semantically meaningful yet disagree with ground-truth annotations, complicating automated benchmarking.
