## Problem

Existing automated analog circuit design methods rely on search-based or reinforcement-learning algorithms that require hundreds of simulation iterations to converge on a valid topology for a given specification, making them slow and difficult to adapt to new design requirements. No prior work has demonstrated that a generative model can directly produce a circuit topology from specifications in a single pass, and the core challenge is how to represent graph-structured circuits in a format compatible with the autoregressive training paradigm of language models.

## Input / Output

The model takes a target voltage conversion ratio and power conversion efficiency (optionally plus a fixed set of circuit vertices for the edge-generation task) and produces a full circuit topology as a sequence of connection tokens in adjacency-matrix order, a duty cycle from {0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9}, and—under the topology-generation task—the vertex set itself. Numerical specifications can be fed either as six-decimal text strings or as raw floats through a learned linear embedding layer, and the circuit connections are encoded with four special tokens (<no_edge>, <edge_1>, <edge_2>, <both_edges>) arranged row-by-row according to a canonical vertex ordering.

## Method

LaMAGIC fine-tunes a Flan-T5-base encoder-decoder transformer (248M parameters) via supervised learning on ~120k simulated 3–5-component power converter circuits, using T5-style span masking to predict the masked duty-cycle and connection spans autoregressively. The central contribution is a family of five circuit formulations that progressively improve canonicality and autoregressive compatibility: a naive text description, a canonical edge-sorted form, a one-hot duty-cycle variant, a pure-text adjacency-matrix form, and a float-input adjacency-matrix form that replaces the standard word embedding for numerical specs with a shared linear projection. The adjacency-matrix formulation flattens the hypergraph into a fixed-length token sequence ordered by vertex, so each next-token prediction corresponds to one connection decision conditioned on the preceding subgraph. Data augmentation via random vertex-order permutations is applied to counteract the transformer's lack of permutation equivariance, and training proceeds in two stages—edge generation first, then topology generation—to build compositional circuit understanding. The float-input adjacency-matrix variant achieves the best transferability to 6-component circuits, reaching 96% success rate at 0.01 tolerance on the 3–5-component benchmark.

## Results

LaMAGIC achieves a success rate of up to 96% under a strict tolerance of 0.01 for generating valid power converter circuits from specifications. In terms of MSE (Table 1), the float-input adjacency-matrix formulation (FM) achieves the lowest voltage MSE of 0.002 and efficiency MSE of 0.001 on the topology generation task, while the pure-text adjacency-matrix formulation (PM) achieves the best efficiency MSE of 0.002 on edge generation. All proposed formulations (CF, CFDC, PM, FM) substantially outperform the naive formulation (NF), which has voltage MSE of 0.054 and efficiency MSE of 0.015 on edge generation. On 6-component transferability tests, FM achieves the best MSE of 0.038 (voltage) and 0.053 (efficiency) with 2000 training samples.

**Table 1: MSE of voltage conversion ratio and efficiency for different circuit formulations on edge and topology generation (3, 4, 5-component circuits).**

| Formulation | Edge Gen: Voltage MSE | Edge Gen: Efficiency MSE | Topology Gen: Voltage MSE | Topology Gen: Efficiency MSE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NF (Naive) | 0.054 | 0.015 | 0.031 | 0.006 |
| CF (Canonical) | 0.016 | 0.004 | 0.008 | 0.003 |
| CFDC (Canonical + Duty Cycle) | 0.007 | 0.003 | 0.005 | 0.003 |
| PM (Adjacency Matrix, text) | 0.007 | **0.002** | 0.009 | 0.003 |
| FM (Adjacency Matrix, float) | **0.006** | 0.013 | **0.002** | **0.001** |

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is the first to apply a generative language model to analog circuit topology design, so no direct numerical baselines from prior generative methods exist. The authors qualitatively contrast with RL-based search methods (Fan et al., 2021; Zhao & Zhang, 2022; Lu et al., 2023), which require approximately 500 simulation queries per new specification, whereas LaMAGIC generates a circuit in a single forward pass. The main quantitative comparisons are between five internal formulations: the naive formulation (NF) is consistently worst, while the adjacency-matrix-based formulations (PM, FM) and canonical formulations (CF, CFDC) all achieve 5–9× lower MSE. FM excels on topology generation and complex 6-component circuits, while PM and CFDC perform better at tight tolerances on edge generation. The paper also shows that an encoder-only T5 variant fails to converge, confirming the necessity of the autoregressive decoder architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant RL/search-based paradigm for automated power converter design (Fan et al., 2021; Zhao & Zhang, 2022; Lu et al., 2023), which requires hundreds of simulation iterations per new specification, by reframing topology generation as a sequence-to-sequence problem solvable in a single LM pass. Methodologically, it builds on Flan-T5 (Chung et al., 2022) as the pretrained backbone and adopts T5-styled masked language modeling (Raffel et al., 2020) for the adjacency-matrix formulation, but the core contribution is a systematic study of circuit-specific input/output formulations (canonical ordering, one-hot duty-cycle encoding, adjacency-matrix tokens, float-input embedding) that bridge the gap between graph-structured circuits and autoregressive LM training. The paper positions itself as the first to apply generative LM methodology to this problem, contrasting with all prior work that relies on iterative search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 96% success rate under a strict 0.01 tolerance demonstrates that one-shot LM generation can match or exceed the quality of iterative RL search while eliminating the simulation bottleneck, which is practically significant for design automation pipelines. However, the scope remains narrow: only power converters with four component types (capacitors, inductors, two switch phases) up to 6 components are tested, and the approach has not been validated on other analog circuit classes (op-amps, filters, etc.). Open questions include whether the float-input adjacency-matrix formulation generalizes to circuits with 10+ components, whether the approach transfers to continuous parameter sizing (not just discrete duty cycles), and whether the formulation insights (canonical ordering, matrix tokens) transfer to other graph-generation domains beyond circuits.
