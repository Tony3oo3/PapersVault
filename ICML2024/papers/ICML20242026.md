## Problem

Structured learning pipelines that combine a deep prediction model with a black-box optimization layer lack interpretability, since both components are opaque. While counterfactual explanations have been studied for classifiers, obtaining *plausible* (not merely close) explanations for such pipelines in high-dimensional settings like images is unaddressed: naive counterfactual search in raw feature space produces adversarial perturbations that are visually implausible and uninformative.

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial context x₀ (e.g., a 96×96 RGB Warcraft map), a trained prediction model φ (a CNN), a linear optimization solver, and either an alternative decision y^alt or a regret threshold ε (for the novel ε-explanations that require no target). It produces a counterfactual context x^alt that is both close to x₀ in feature space and lies on the data manifold, along with the corresponding alternative path or decision. The VAE is trained on the same dataset as φ, and the explanation search operates entirely in the VAE's latent space (default dimension 64), with the final explanation recovered by decoding the optimal latent code.

## Method

CF-OPT reformulates the counterfactual explanation problem in the latent space of a Variational Autoencoder, where plausibility is enforced by a thickened hypersphere constraint at radius C_{n_z} ≈ √n_z (justified by concentration of measure for high-dimensional Gaussians and shown optimal for a trade-off between expected distance and volume). Two key modifications make the VAE suitable for this structured setting: (i) a cost-aware training loss that augments the standard ELBO with a penalty on the L2 distance between predicted costs φ(x) and φ(d_ψ(z)), ensuring the reconstructed context preserves the optimization-relevant signal; and (ii) a latent hypersphere regularization Ω(z) = β(‖z‖₂ − C_{n_z})² that replaces the naive log-likelihood penalty, avoiding the exponential loss increase seen with centered-ball regularizations. The counterfactual is then found via the Modified Differential Method of Multipliers (MDMM), performing alternating gradient descent/ascent on the augmented Lagrangian of the constrained problem, with gradients propagated through the decoder and prediction model via automatic differentiation while treating the discrete optimization layer as piecewise-constant.

## Results

On the Warcraft Maps shortest-path pipeline (96×96 RGB images, 64-dim latent space), cost-aware VAE training with α=2 significantly reduces the relative regret of optimal paths under reconstruction compared to the standard ELBO (α=0). The latent hypersphere plausibility regularization (β=10) lowers both the task-agnostic reconstruction error and the decision-focused reconstruction error of produced counterfactuals, confirming more plausible explanations. The decision-focused metric shows that CF-OPT explanations are far more stable under VAE reconstruction than naive adversarial explanations, which vanish through the decoder. The latent hypersphere regularization maintains near-constant explanation loss across β values, while the log-likelihood regularization causes an exponential increase in loss as β grows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external counterfactual explanation methods; instead, it validates its components through internal ablations. CF-OPT with latent hypersphere regularization produces explanations with substantially lower decision-focused reconstruction error than naive adversarial search in raw feature space (Figure 8), confirming that the VAE-based approach avoids adversarial artifacts. Cost-aware VAEs (α≥0.5) yield significantly lower explanation loss than the standard ELBO-trained VAE (α=0) on 50 absolute explanation tasks (Figure 6). The latent hypersphere regularization (Ω) is shown to be superior to the log-likelihood regularization (Ω̃ = β‖z‖²) in that it does not degrade explanation proximity as β increases, whereas the log-likelihood variant causes an exponential loss increase (Figure 10). On tabular data (grid shortest-path and knapsack), CF-OPT scales efficiently with feature count, decision count, and model depth, with relative and ε-explanations requiring fewer iterations than absolute explanations due to the combinatorial nature of the latter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the counterfactual explanation framework of Forel et al. (2023) from restricted pipeline types (random forests, k-nearest neighbors) to general deep prediction models coupled with black-box linear optimization layers. It adapts the VAE-based plausibility modeling of Pawelczyk et al. (2020) from the classification setting to structured prediction, replacing their random-sampling strategy with a principled latent hypersphere regularization derived from the concentration of the Gaussian prior. The optimization algorithm builds on the modified differential method of multipliers (Platt & Barr, 1987), and the cost-aware VAE loss (Eq. 6) is a novel modification of the standard ELBO tailored to the downstream optimization task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to provide plausible counterfactual explanations for the broad class of structured learning pipelines combining deep models with optimization layers, a rapidly growing area in decision-focused learning. The paper highlights a practical vulnerability: in high-dimensional settings, naive counterfactual search produces adversarial examples, motivating the VAE-based plausibility constraint. Key limitations include the requirement for continuous features and reliance on a single VAE architecture; extending to discrete/categorical inputs and exploring diffusion models are identified as open directions. The ε-explanation, which requires no target decision, offers a useful tool for local sensitivity analysis that has no direct analogue in prior work.
