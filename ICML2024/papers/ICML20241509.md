## Problem

Gaussian process factor analysis (GPFA) models for spike count data face intractable Bayesian inference due to the non-conjugacy of Poisson or negative binomial likelihoods. Existing workarounds—black-box variational inference, numerical integration, or polynomial approximations—are either unstable, computationally expensive, or not fully Bayesian (e.g., treating the dispersion parameter as a fixed hyperparameter rather than a random variable). No prior method achieved both analytically tractable closed-form updates and full Bayesian treatment of all model parameters including per-neuron dispersion.

## Input / Output

The method takes binned spike count matrices Y ∈ ℕ^{N×T} (N neurons, T time bins of e.g. 5–15 ms), optionally across M repeated trials, and produces D low-dimensional latent GP trajectories (D ≪ N), a loading matrix W ∈ ℝ^{N×D}, per-neuron negative binomial dispersion parameters {r_n}, base firing rates β, and full variational posterior distributions over all variables. ARD gamma priors on the loading precisions enable automatic selection of the effective latent dimensionality, and the model naturally extends to binomial observations by fixing the trial count.

## Method

The key insight is a three-part data augmentation that renders the negative binomial GPFA likelihood conditionally conjugate: (1) Pólya-gamma variables ω_{n,t} linearize the exponential terms in f_{n,t}, converting the likelihood to a Gaussian over a transformed variable z_{n,t}; (2) gamma variables τ_{n,t} and Pólya-inverse-gamma variables ξ_{n,t} (from He et al., 2019) handle the gamma functions involving the dispersion parameter r_n, yielding a Power-Truncated Normal (PTN) posterior for r_n. This conditional conjugacy enables a variational EM algorithm with closed-form coordinate-ascent updates for every variable—including the dispersion parameters—without requiring any variables to be fixed or learned as hyperparameters. For scalability, the model incorporates sparse GPs with M ≪ T inducing points (reducing matrix inversions from O(T³) to O(M³)) and stochastic natural gradients with mini-batching, achieving O(D(M³ + BM²)) per-iteration complexity.

## Results

On the Allen Brain Observatory Visual Coding dataset (176 neurons, 75 trials), ccGPFA with a negative binomial likelihood achieves the best test negative log-likelihood of 0.3333±0.0010, outperforming GPFA (0.3565), PAL (0.3407), and bGPFA (0.3504), while running in 7.67 seconds—roughly 8× faster than the next-best baseline (PAL at 62.95 s) and ~170× faster than bGPFA (1321.97 s). On the MC_Maze behavioral decoding task, ccGPFA (NegBinomial) achieves an R² of 0.755±0.028 for predicting hand velocity, substantially exceeding PAL (0.585), bGPFA (0.327), and GPFA (0.290). In the scalability experiment at T=1500, the sparse ccGPFA model with 200 inducing points completes inference in 55 s versus over 31 hours for PAL, with comparable test NLL (1.415 vs. 1.514).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ccGPFA outperforms all three baselines on both accuracy and speed across all experiments. Against GPFA (Yu et al., 2008), ccGPFA (NegBinomial) reduces test NLL from 0.3565 to 0.3333 and improves R² from 0.290 to 0.755, while being ~9× faster; the Gaussian likelihood of GPFA is a poor fit for count data. Against PAL (Keeley et al., 2020b), ccGPFA achieves a lower test NLL (0.3333 vs. 0.3407) and higher R² (0.755 vs. 0.585) while running ~8× faster on the visual coding task and ~2000× faster in the scalability experiment (55 s vs. 112,644 s at T=1500); PAL's second-order optimization and polynomial coefficient fitting over the full dataset are the main bottlenecks. Against bGPFA (Jensen et al., 2021), ccGPFA achieves a lower test NLL (0.3333 vs. 0.3504) and a substantially higher R² (0.755 vs. 0.327) while running ~170× faster; bGPFA's per-trial latent parameterization and whitened RBF-specific inference appear to limit both its convergence speed and its ability to eliminate irrelevant latent dimensions via ARD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GPFA (Yu et al., 2008) from Gaussian to negative binomial spike count observations, addressing the non-conjugacy that prior works handled via black-box variational inference (Keeley et al., 2020a), polynomial likelihood approximations (Keeley et al., 2020b), or numerical integration (Jensen et al., 2021). The key departure is a data augmentation strategy drawing on Pólya-inverse gamma integral representations (He et al., 2019) that renders the model conditionally conjugate for *all* variables—including the negative binomial dispersion parameter—which prior methods either fixed as a hyperparameter or approximated via moment matching. This yields closed-form coordinate-ascent updates within a variational EM framework, in contrast to the iterative, hyperparameter-sensitive optimization required by competing approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conditional conjugacy enables two practical advantages that prior non-conjugate GPFA variants lack: seamless integration with sparse Gaussian process inducing points (reducing complexity from O(T³) to O(TM²)) and stochastic natural-gradient acceleration for long recordings. A notable limitation is the non-identifiability between the dispersion parameter and the latent function, which the authors address with a heuristic clipping step rather than a principled solution. Open questions include whether the augmentation generalizes to other non-conjugate count models (e.g., zero-inflated or over-dispersed variants), how the method behaves with correlated latent processes, and whether the fully Bayesian treatment of dispersion provides meaningful uncertainty quantification beyond point estimates in downstream decoding tasks.
