## Problem

Prior online conformal prediction methods (e.g., Gibbs & Candes, 2021) use a fixed step size for threshold updates, which provides long-run coverage guarantees under adversarial data but fails to converge under I.I.D. data—coverage oscillates wildly and prediction sets degenerate to empty or full sets infinitely often. No existing method simultaneously achieves both adversarial robustness and I.I.D. convergence for online conformal prediction.

## Input / Output

At each time step t, the method receives a feature vector X_t, a bounded conformal score function s_t: X×Y → [0,B] (e.g., residual |y − f̂_t(x)| for a regression model), and a target miscoverage level α ∈ (0,1). It outputs a prediction set C_t(X_t) = {y : s_t(X_t, y) ≤ q_t} and an updated threshold q_{t+1}. The boundedness of the score in [0,B] is essential for the uniform boundedness of q_t that underpins the coverage guarantees.

## Method

The core update rule is q_{t+1} = q_t + η_t(1_{Y_t ∉ C_t(X_t)} − α), which is equivalent to online subgradient descent on the (1−α)-quantile loss, but with a decaying step size η_t ∝ t^{−1/2−ε} for small ε ∈ (0,1/2). This single modification yields a 'best-of-both-worlds' guarantee: in the adversarial setting, long-run coverage error is bounded by O(1/T^{1/2−ε}) (Theorem 1), while in the I.I.D. setting, q_t converges almost surely to the true (1−α)-quantile q* (Theorem 3), implying instantaneous coverage converges to 1−α. The paper further proves that with a fixed step size, I.I.D. coverage oscillates between 0 and 1 almost surely (Propositions 1 and 3), making the decaying schedule not merely preferable but necessary for meaningful prediction sets. A generalized theorem (Theorem 2) extends the adversarial guarantee to arbitrary step-size sequences, enabling adaptive resets upon distribution shift detection.

## Results

On the M4 benchmark (3000+ time series, α=0.1), the decaying step size method achieves 0.900 long-run coverage (target 0.90) with a normalized quantile variance of 1.32 and MSE of 2.37, compared to DTACI's 0.895 coverage, 4.11 variance, and 5.44 MSE. The 'decay+adapt' variant further reduces variance to 1.14 and MSE to 1.97 while maintaining 0.885 coverage. The fraction of time steps producing infinite (uninformative) prediction sets drops from 3.7% (DTACI) to 0.7% (decaying) and 0.5% (decay+adapt). On Elec2 and ImageNet, the decaying step size yields substantially more stable instantaneous coverage and threshold values over time, whereas the fixed step size oscillates without converging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is DTACI (Gibbs & Candès, 2022), a strongly adaptive online learning method for conformal prediction. The proposed decaying step size method substantially outperforms DTACI on all secondary metrics: variance is reduced by ~68% (1.32 vs 4.11), MSE by ~56% (2.37 vs 5.44), and the fraction of infinite prediction sets by ~81% (0.007 vs 0.037), while matching or slightly exceeding DTACI's coverage (0.900 vs 0.895). The 'decay+adapt' variant, which increases the step size upon detecting distribution shift, achieves the best variance (1.14) and MSE (1.97) of all methods but trades a small amount of coverage (0.885). Against the paper's own fixed-step-size baseline, the decaying step size wins on variance (1.32 vs 1.58), MSE (2.37 vs 2.92), and infinite sets (0.007 vs 0.011), with essentially identical coverage. The authors note that the fixed step size's tighter rolling coverage is an artifact of overcorrecting (producing overly wide intervals after miscoverage events), not a genuine advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online conformal prediction framework initiated by Gibbs & Candes (2021) and refined by Angelopoulos et al. (2023), Bhatnagar et al. (2023), and Feldman et al. (2023), all of which use a fixed step size in the quantile-threshold update. The key departure is the introduction of a decaying step size (η_t ∝ t^{-1/2-ε}), which the authors show is necessary to avoid a fundamental pathology: with any fixed step size, the instantaneous coverage oscillates between 0 and 1 infinitely often even under I.I.D. data with a fixed model. The paper also draws a conceptual parallel to 'best of both worlds' results in online learning (stochastic vs. adversarial regret), but applies the idea to predictive coverage rather than estimation or regret.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first online conformal method to simultaneously guarantee long-run adversarial coverage and I.I.D. convergence to the optimal quantile, resolving a gap where prior fixed-step-size methods could only provide the former. The negative result showing fixed step sizes produce empty or full prediction sets infinitely often is a significant correction to the prior literature. Open questions include extending the analysis to online risk control, stationary (non-I.I.D.) time series, and developing principled 'decay+adapt' step-size schedules for distribution shifts, which the authors explore only preliminarily on M4 datasets.
