## Problem

Diffusion models are vulnerable to pixel-level backdoor attacks that produce malicious outputs when a hidden trigger is embedded in the input noise, yet conventional backdoor defenses for classification models fail because diffusion models consume Gaussian noise rather than natural images and predict noise rather than class logits. The only existing diffusion-specific defense (Elijah) lacks a unified loss formulation across attacks and relies on assumptions about target image similarity that are contradicted by state-of-the-art attacks like TrojDiff.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained diffusion model (DDPM, EDM, LDM, score-based, or consistency models) operating on RGB images at resolutions from 32×32 (CIFAR-10) to 256×256 (CelebA-HQ) and produces three outputs: a reversed trigger tensor of the same spatial shape as the input, a binary input-detection decision per noise sample, and two scalar model-detection features (M_r and V_r) derived from the dimensional-wise KL divergence between the reversed and benign noise distributions. The trigger is optimized in the noise space rather than the image space, and detection operates entirely on the statistical properties of the noise distribution.

## Method

TERD first unifies all known pixel-level backdoor attacks (BadDiffusion, TrojDiff, VillanDiffusion) into a single formulation x_t = a(x_0,t)x_0 + b(t)ε + c(t)r, then applies the triangle inequality to derive an accessible reversed loss that eliminates the attacker-specific coefficient d(t). Trigger reversion proceeds in two stages: (1) Trigger Estimation, which substitutes the unknown target image with a surrogate sampled from the Gaussian prior at high timesteps (where Theorem 4.1 guarantees the distributions converge), and (2) Trigger Refinement, which uses a differential n-step DDIM sampler to obtain a more precise x_0 and optimizes the loss at both high and low timesteps. For input detection, each noise sample is scored under both the benign N(0,I) and the reversed backdoor N(r,γ²) distributions, and samples with higher backdoor probability are flagged. For model detection, the dimensional-wise KL divergence between the reversed and benign distributions is summarized into mean (M_r) and variance (V_r) features, which distinguish whole-image triggers (high M_r) from patch-based triggers (high V_r) and are thresholded via a 3σ criterion or a one-layer classifier.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, TERD achieves 100% TPR and 100% TNR for both input detection and model detection (including the benign-only setting) across all three attacks (BadDiffusion, TrojDiff, and VillanDiffusion). The trigger reversion quality, measured by ‖r − r_o‖₂, ranges from 4.26 (TrojDiff) to 30.03 (VillanDiffusion), consistently outperforming the Elijah baseline. On high-resolution datasets (CelebA, CelebA-HQ at 256×256), TERD maintains 100% TPR and TNR across all detection tasks, demonstrating strong transferability from small to large datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Elijah (An et al., 2023), the first and only prior defense specifically designed for diffusion model backdoors. TERD decisively outperforms Elijah on every metric and every attack. On trigger reversion quality (‖r−r_o‖₂), TERD reduces the error by 37% for BadDiffusion (20.69 vs. 32.90), 81% for TrojDiff (4.26 vs. 22.60), and 30% for VillanDiffusion (30.03 vs. 43.03). For model detection, Elijah completely fails on TrojDiff (0% TPR) and achieves only 3% TPR on VillanDiffusion, while TERD reaches 100% TPR and TNR in all cases. In the benign-only (BO) setting, Elijah's TNR drops to 21.55% (BadDiffusion) and 47.50% (TrojDiff), whereas TERD maintains 100% TNR. The paper attributes Elijah's failures to its heuristic assumption that d(t)=0.5 and its reliance on generated image similarity, which breaks down for multi-target attacks like TrojDiff.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-studied backdoor defense task from classification models to diffusion models, where the fundamental challenge is that inputs are Gaussian noise rather than natural images, rendering conventional input-level and model-level defenses inapplicable. It builds on the DDIM multi-step sampler (Song et al., 2020a) for trigger refinement and the unified SDE framework (Song et al., 2020b) to encompass both discrete and continuous diffusion attacks. The key departure from the sole prior diffusion-specific defense, Elijah (An et al., 2023), is the derivation of a unified reversed loss via the triangle inequality that eliminates the need to assume specific attack formulations (e.g., d(t)=0.5), and the shift from image-space to noise-space detection using KL divergence between reversed and benign distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TERD introduces the first feasible input detection mechanism for diffusion models by exploiting the statistical separation between benign and backdoor noise distributions, a capability previously considered impossible due to the noise-only input space. The framework's generality—extending naturally to score-based and consistency models via the SDE formulation—suggests a broader template for defending any SDE-based generative model. However, evaluation is limited to CIFAR-10, CelebA, and CelebA-HQ with pixel-level attacks; open questions include applicability to text-to-image models, more sophisticated adaptive attacks that preserve benign utility, and whether the 100% detection rates hold under distribution shift or multi-trigger scenarios. The paper itself cautions that backdoor defense is only one facet of diffusion model security.
