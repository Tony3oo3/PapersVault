## Problem

Two-stage protein design methods (generate structure, then design sequence) suffer from two fundamental issues: the sequence module overfits because it is trained on accurate crystal structures but applied to noisier generated backbones, and the lack of interaction between modules prevents sequence errors from providing feedback to refine structures. While co-design methods exist for specific families like antibodies, no unified framework had achieved state-of-the-art performance across general protein families.

## Input / Output

Given only a target protein length N, CarbonNovo jointly generates an SE(3)-parameterized backbone (N frames, each with a rotation in SO(3), a translation in R³, and a torsion angle for the Cα–C bond) and a corresponding amino acid sequence of N residues from 20 types. The structure and sequence are produced simultaneously at each diffusion step rather than sequentially, ensuring they remain mutually consistent throughout generation.

## Method

CarbonNovo formulates joint structure-sequence generation as a unified energy-based model: an SE(3) score-based diffusion model characterizes the structure energy, while a Markov Random Field (with conservation bias and pairwise coupling terms) characterizes the sequence energy conditioned on the predicted structure. At every diffusion step, the structure module (a revised Evoformer with Triangle Attention and IPA) and the sequence module (a 4-block Triangle Attention Network) exchange single and pair representations, enabling mutual refinement. A pre-trained ESM-3B language model is integrated via network recycling, injecting evolutionary constraints as additional features into both modules. Training follows a multi-stage strategy—separate pre-training of each module followed by end-to-end co-training with auxiliary losses (FAPE, distogram, side-chain prediction, clash penalty)—and inference uses Langevin dynamics for structure and a discrete Metropolis-Hastings Langevin algorithm for sequence sampling.

## Results

CarbonNovo achieves a designability Fraction of 81.38% (ESMFold) / 77.38% (OmegaFold), substantially outperforming the next-best method RFdiffusion (69.81% / 60.00%) and Chroma (45.70% / 40.10%). On novelty, CarbonNovo reaches 43.15% / 40.92%, compared to 36.50% / 32.65% for RFdiffusion and 25.62% / 23.92% for Chroma. CarbonNovo also attains the lowest Rosetta energy (−2.83) and highest sequence log-likelihood (−2.44) among all compared methods, while maintaining competitive diversity (0.217). The ablation with ProteinMPNN (CarbonNovo+MPNN, 73.16% Fraction) confirms that the joint co-design strategy contributes an additional ~8% designability over the two-stage pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CarbonNovo outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest two-stage competitor RFdiffusion, CarbonNovo improves the designability Fraction by ~12 points (81.38% vs. 69.81% with ESMFold) and novelty by ~7 points (43.15% vs. 36.50%), while reducing scRMSD from 3.49 to 1.94. Chroma, the closest structural competitor, is surpassed by CarbonNovo by 36 points in Fraction (81.38% vs. 45.70%) and 18 points in novelty. The SE(3) diffusion baselines (FrameDiff-ICML, FrameDiff-Improved, FrameFlow) and the flow-matching baseline (Genie) are all significantly worse, with FrameFlow showing the poorest designability (11.88% Fraction) and highest scRMSD (18.83). On Rosetta energy and sequence plausibility (Table 2), CarbonNovo achieves the best values (−2.83 energy, −2.44 log-likelihood), edging out Chroma (−2.79, −2.49) and RFdiffusion (−2.64, −2.51). The CarbonNovo+MPNN ablation (73.16% Fraction) demonstrates that the joint co-design strategy itself contributes ~8% additional designability over simply applying ProteinMPNN to CarbonNovo-generated backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

CarbonNovo unifies the SE(3) score-based diffusion framework from FrameDiff (for structure) and the MRF-based sequence design from CarbonDesign/Chroma (for sequence) into a single energy-based model where both modules are jointly trained and communicate at every diffusion step. It departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm (RFdiffusion + ProteinMPNN, Chroma) by allowing sequence-design errors to provide gradient feedback to the structure module during both training and inference. A key architectural departure is the integration of a frozen protein language model (ESM-3B) via network recycling, injecting evolutionary priors directly into the generative process—a component absent from all prior de novo design methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint energy-based formulation addresses a fundamental limitation of two-stage pipelines: sequence design models trained on crystal structures overfit when applied to noisier generated backbones, and sequence errors cannot propagate back to refine structures. The ablation showing that the PLM contributes nearly 30 percentage points to designability (81.4% → 51.8%) suggests evolutionary information is a major, previously underexploited signal for de novo design quality. However, the paper is limited to in silico metrics with no wet-lab validation, and the approach is restricted to monomer design; extending to complexes, conditional design (e.g., binders), and experimental validation remain open questions.
