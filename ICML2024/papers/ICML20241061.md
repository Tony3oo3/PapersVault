## Problem

SGD-based continual learning suffers from catastrophic forgetting on non-stationary data streams, while ideal sequential Bayesian updates are immune to forgetting but only tractable for simple statistical models that lack the representational power needed for complex data. The paper bridges this gap by asking: can we meta-learn neural networks to serve as bridges between raw data and simple exponential-family statistical models, so that continual learning happens entirely within the forgetting-proof statistical component?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241061/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential, non-stationary training stream (a concatenation of K task streams, each accessed exactly once in order) and a test set drawn from the same tasks. During CL, the neural networks perform only forward passes on each example to produce sufficient statistics for the exponential-family posterior; no gradient descent occurs. Outputs are task-dependent: class labels (classification), continuous values (sine regression, rotation angle), completed image halves (image completion), or generated images (VAE/DDPM). During meta-training, the entire training stream is available and processed in parallel via batch inference rather than sequential updates.

## Method

SB-MCL formulates continual learning as the exact sequential Bayesian update of an exponential-family variational posterior q_φ(z|D), justified by the Fisher-Darmois-Koopman-Pitman theorem, which guarantees fixed-size sufficient statistics and thus zero forgetting. A meta-learned 'learner' network produces q_φ(x_t, y_t|z) (e.g., a noisy Gaussian observation of z) for each training example, which is integrated into the posterior via closed-form updates (e.g., Λ_t = Λ_{t-1} + P_t, μ_t = Λ_t^{-1}(Λ_{t-1}μ_{t-1} + P_t ẑ_t)). A separate 'model' network produces predictions conditioned on z and the test input. During meta-training, both networks are updated via first-order SGD on a variational lower bound (ELBO-like objective with KL regularization), using the reparameterization trick for gradient flow and batch inference for parallelism—no second-order gradients are required. The framework is domain- and model-agnostic: z can be any auxiliary variable (e.g., a 512-dim factorized Gaussian injected into an encoder-decoder), and it subsumes GeMCL, Prototypical Networks, and ALPaCA as special cases.

## Results

On the primary 10-task 10-shot classification benchmark, SB-MCL achieves 0.8% error on Omniglot, 0.2% on CASIA, and 23.1% on Celeb, outperforming OML (4.6%, 1.5%, 33.1%) and matching or exceeding Transformer (1.4%, 0.4%, 22.8%). On regression tasks, SB-MCL achieves 0.0011 loss on Sine, 0.039 on CASIA rotation, and 0.096 on Celeb completion, consistently beating OML and Linear TF. For deep generative modeling, SB-MCL achieves the best loss across VAE (0.428) and DDPM (0.0345 CASIA, 0.0302 Celeb) benchmarks. Meta-training time is 40 minutes for classification (vs. 6.5 hr for OML and 1.2 hr for TF) on a single A40 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SB-MCL decisively outperforms OML (Javed & White, 2019) across all benchmarks: 5.75× lower error on Omniglot (0.8% vs 4.6%), 7.5× on CASIA (0.2% vs 1.5%), and 30% lower on Celeb (23.1% vs 33.1%), with similar margins on regression and generative tasks. Against the Transformer-based CL-Seq baseline (Lee et al., 2023), SB-MCL is comparable on the standard 10-task 10-shot setting (e.g., Celeb: 23.1% vs 22.8%) but dramatically superior on longer streams—on CASIA with 500 tasks, TF degrades to 98.0% error while SB-MCL maintains 3.6%. SB-MCL also outperforms Linear TF in most settings (e.g., Omniglot: 0.8% vs 12.5%; CASIA rotation: 0.039 vs 0.068 loss) while maintaining constant per-example computational cost. A key advantage is that SB-MCL's performance improves or stays flat with more shots per task (e.g., CASIA classification: 0.2% at 10 shots → 0.1% at 50 shots), whereas TF and OML degrade with more shots due to accumulated forgetting or length generalization failure. In terms of efficiency, SB-MCL meta-trains in 40 minutes for classification versus 6.5 hours for OML and 1.2 hours for TF on a single A40 GPU.

## Relation to Prior Work

SB-MCL unifies GeMCL, Prototypical Networks, and ALPaCA as special cases of a single meta-continual learning framework grounded in the Fisher-Darmois-Koopman-Pitman theorem, which identifies the exponential family as the only distribution class supporting lossless sequential Bayesian updates with fixed-size sufficient statistics. It departs from SGD-based MCL (OML, ANML) by eliminating gradient descent during the inner CL loop entirely—neural networks are meta-trained once and then frozen, while a simple statistical model (e.g., a factorized Gaussian) absorbs all continual learning via closed-form Bayesian updates. It also contrasts with CL-Seq (Transformer-based sequence modeling), which achieves strong CL performance but suffers from growing per-example computational cost and poor length generalization; SB-MCL maintains constant cost by design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reframing catastrophic forgetting as a representational-capacity problem rather than an optimization-dynamics problem, SB-MCL lets practitioners focus on architecture and data scale (as in offline deep learning) instead of designing anti-forgetting regularization. The framework is domain- and model-agnostic, demonstrated across classification, regression, image completion, rotation prediction, VAEs, and DDPMs—the first MCL evaluation with modern deep generative models. A key limitation is the exchangeability assumption: the exponential family posterior is order-agnostic, which is suboptimal for curriculum-style learning where data sequence carries information. Relaxing the exponential family constraint toward more flexible (e.g., non-parametric) posteriors while retaining fixed memory is identified as an open direction.
