## Problem

Standard MBR decoding approximates the model probability distribution over hypotheses using a Monte Carlo (empirical) estimate, which introduces large estimation error because the hypothesis space is astronomically larger than the number of sampled sequences. The paper identifies that this second approximation is unnecessary: since model probabilities are directly accessible at inference time, there is no need to estimate them from sample frequencies. The gap is that no prior work combined probabilistic sampling (which produces higher-quality hypothesis pools than beam search) with the actual model probability for the MBR objective.

## Input / Output

The method takes an input sequence (text for translation/summarization, an image for captioning) and a set of hypotheses sampled from a conditional generation model or LLM. Crucially, it requires the model's log-probability for each sampled sequence, which is obtained concurrently with the sampling procedure at no additional cost. The output is a single selected hypothesis from the candidate pool, chosen by maximizing the expected utility weighted by model probability rather than by sample frequency.

## Method

MBMBR replaces the Monte Carlo empirical distribution P̂(y) = count(y)/|H_ref| with a model-based distribution P̂_MB(y) = α·P(y) for y in the deduplicated reference set R, and 0 otherwise, where α is a normalization constant. The paper proves (Theorem 4.1) that this model-based distribution is the information projection of P onto the set of all distributions with support restricted to R, minimizing KL divergence among all such distributions. In practice, this means the MBR objective becomes a weighted sum of utility scores where each unique hypothesis is weighted by its model probability rather than its occurrence count. A length-normalized variant (MBMBR_L) multiplies P(y) by e^{ℓ(y)} to counteract the model's tendency to favor shorter sequences. The approach is trivially implementable—requiring only the model log-probs already computed during sampling—and reduces the KL divergence to the true distribution by roughly half compared to the Monte Carlo estimate, translating to about half the samples needed for equivalent output quality.

## Results

On WMT'19 De-En with epsilon sampling (ε=0.02) and 256 samples, MBMBR achieves 35.86 BLEU vs. 35.77 for MBR, and MBMBR_L achieves 35.84. Across all 56 settings (2 datasets × 4 sampling algorithms × 7 sample sizes), MBMBR_L wins over MBR in 47/56 cases and MBMBR wins in 43/56. On MS COCO image captioning (64 samples, epsilon sampling), MBMBR reaches 34.96 BLEU vs. 33.24 for MBR (+1.72). On SAMSum summarization (64 samples), MBMBR achieves 34.50 ROUGE-L vs. 33.03 for MBR (+1.47), while on XSum, MBMBR_L achieves 36.18 vs. 35.90 for MBR. The only notable exception is E2E NLG data-to-text, where MBMBR underperforms MBR (16.07 vs. 22.30 BLEU at 64 samples), attributed to low-quality generated sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is standard MBR decoding (Eikema & Aziz, 2022), which uses a Monte Carlo estimate of the model probability. MBMBR outperforms MBR in 43 out of 56 settings on WMT'19 De-En and Ru-En, with the gap being most pronounced at smaller sample sizes (e.g., +0.75 BLEU at 4 samples on De-En with epsilon sampling). The length-normalized variant MBMBR_L wins in 47/56 settings, with the advantage over plain MBMBR being most visible in summarization (XSum: 36.18 vs. 35.51 ROUGE-L at 64 samples) and image captioning (nocaps: 32.68 vs. 27.27 BLEU at 64 samples), where length bias is more severe. The second comparison point is the sampling strategy from Freitag et al. (2023); epsilon sampling (ε=0.02) is used as the shared sampling method for both MBR and MBMBR, and the paper confirms it outperforms top-k, nucleus, and ancestral sampling across all tasks. MBMBR consistently improves over MBR regardless of the sampling algorithm, except with ancestral sampling where both methods produce lower-quality outputs. The one clear failure case is E2E NLG data-to-text, where MBMBR drops to 16.07 BLEU vs. 22.30 for MBR, which the authors attribute to the LLM generating low-quality sequences that make the MBR objective unreliable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sampling-based MBR decoding framework (Eikema & Aziz, 2020; 2022; Fernandes et al., 2022; Freitag et al., 2023) by replacing the Monte Carlo probability estimate with the model probability itself, restricted to the sampled hypothesis set. The idea of using model probability in MBR is not new—it was implicit in the original beam-search-based MBR formulations (Goel & Byrne, 2000; Kumar & Byrne, 2004)—but the paper's contribution is showing that this substitution is provably superior (via KL divergence minimization) even when combined with modern probabilistic sampling algorithms like epsilon sampling. The length-normalized variant (MBMBR_L) draws on prior work correcting length bias in NMT (Murray & Chiang, 2018; Bertsch et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MBMBR offers a drop-in replacement for standard MBR that requires only the model probability of sampled sequences (already computed during sampling), making it trivially implementable. The clean theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.1: model-based distribution is the information projection of P onto the restricted support) provides a principled justification, and the empirical finding that roughly half as many samples are needed to achieve the same KL divergence has direct practical implications for inference cost. However, the method's benefit is contingent on the model generating reasonably high-quality sequences—it degrades with ancestral sampling and on low-quality generation tasks (E2E NLG), and the length-bias correction, while effective, is a heuristic that assumes a specific exponential form of the bias. Open questions include whether more sophisticated bias corrections are needed for other model pathologies and whether the approach extends to settings where model probabilities are not directly accessible (e.g., black-box LLM APIs).
