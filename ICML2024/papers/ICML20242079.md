## Problem

State-space models (SSMs) like S4 empirically excel at long-term memory, but it remained unclear whether this capability stems from the linear-dynamics-plus-nonlinear-activation architecture or from the specific reparameterization of recurrent weights. This paper proves that SSMs without reparameterization still suffer from the same 'curse of memory' as traditional RNNs—only stably approximating targets with exponentially decaying memory—and identifies stable reparameterization as the true mechanism enabling long-term memory learning.

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework operates on bounded, causal, continuous input sequences x ∈ C₀(ℝ, ℝᵈ) vanishing at infinity, producing scalar output sequences via nonlinear functionals. In practice, the method is instantiated on text sequences (WikiText-103 for language modeling with the Hyena/S5 architecture), RGB images (MNIST at 28×28, CIFAR-10 at 32×32 for classification), and multi-task sequence benchmarks (Long Range Arena). The recurrent weight matrix Λ is parameterized as a diagonal matrix with eigenvalues λᵢ = f(wᵢ), where f is the reparameterization function and wᵢ are the trainable parameters.

## Method

The paper introduces a class of 'stable reparameterization' functions f: ℝ→ℝ satisfying a uniform integrability condition (Definition 3.4) that guarantees stable approximation of any decaying-memory target, in contrast to vanilla SSMs which are limited to exponential decay. From an optimization perspective, the gradient norm of the recurrent weight w is upper-bounded by C·|f'(w)|/f(w)² (continuous) or C·|f'(w)|/(1−f(w))² (discrete), where C is parameterization-independent. By imposing the criterion that this gradient-to-weight ratio be Lipschitz in w, the paper solves the resulting differential equation to derive the 'best' reparameterization f(w) = −1/(aw²+b) (continuous) or f(w) = 1 − 1/(w²+0.5) (discrete), which yields a gradient-over-weight ratio of exactly 2a|w|—bounded and linear in the weight magnitude. This 'best' parameterization is verified to improve training stability at large learning rates on MNIST, CIFAR-10, WikiText-103, and Long Range Arena compared to exponential, softplus, and direct parameterizations.

## Results

On the Long Range Arena benchmark, the proposed 'Best' parameterization (f(w) = 1 - 1/(w²+0.5)) achieves an average score of 86.48, outperforming the S4 exponential parameterization (86.09), with gains on Listops (60.80 vs 59.60), Text (88.5 vs 86.82), and Retrieval (91.3 vs 90.90). On MNIST, the 'Best' parameterization achieves a test loss of 0.089073 at LR=5e-2, dramatically outperforming Softplus (0.802772) and Exp (0.868350), while Direct parameterization diverges (NaN). On CIFAR-10 at LR=5e-2, the 'Best' parameterization achieves 1.713892 test loss versus 1.942372 (Softplus) and 1.846173 (Exp), again with Direct diverging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the S4 exponential reparameterization (f(w) = -e^w), which is the standard parameterization used in S4, S5, and Mamba. On Long Range Arena, the 'Best' parameterization wins on 4 out of 6 subtasks (Listops, Text, Retrieval, Pathfinder) and ties or slightly loses on Image (87.39 vs 88.65) and Pathx (96.1 vs 96.35), yielding a 0.39-point average improvement. On MNIST and CIFAR-10, the comparison extends to Direct, Softplus, and Exp parameterizations across a range of learning rates (5e-6 to 5e+0). The 'Best' parameterization consistently achieves the lowest test loss at every learning rate where all methods converge, and is the only method that remains stable at LR=5e-2 on MNIST (0.089 vs 0.80 for Softplus). At higher learning rates (≥5e-3 on MNIST, ≥5e-6 on CIFAR-10), the Direct parameterization diverges entirely (NaN), while the 'Best' parameterization maintains stable training, demonstrating its key advantage in optimization robustness for large-scale training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the curse-of-memory analysis from Li et al. (2020) and Wang et al. (2023) — originally formulated for linear and nonlinear RNNs — to state-space models, proving that SSMs without reparameterization inherit the same exponential memory decay limitation. It departs from the prevailing assumption that the SSM architecture (linear dynamics with layer-wise nonlinear readout) inherently overcomes this limitation, instead identifying stable reparameterization of recurrent weights as the critical mechanism. The proposed 'best' reparameterization f(w) = -1/(aw²+b) is derived from a gradient-boundedness criterion, building on the S4 exponential reparameterization (Gu et al., 2022b) and S5 softplus variant (Smith et al., 2023) but optimizing for a principled Lipschitz gradient-over-weight property.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical explanation for why S4 and its variants succeed at long-sequence modeling: it is the reparameterization, not the linear-dynamics-plus-nonlinear-activation structure, that lifts the memory limitation. This reframing has practical implications for designing new SSM variants (Mamba, RetNet, RWKV) where reparameterization choices can be made with theoretical grounding rather than heuristic tuning. Open questions include whether the multi-layer gradient bound (only sketched in a remark) holds rigorously, whether the 'best' parameterization's stability advantage persists at the scale of billion-parameter models, and how the results extend to selective/state-dependent SSMs like Mamba where the recurrent matrix is input-dependent.
