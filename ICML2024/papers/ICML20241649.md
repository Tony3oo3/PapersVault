## Problem

Existing diffusion-based pose-guided human image generation methods (PIDM, PoCoLD) rely on U-Net/CNN denoisers, limiting scalability and efficiency: PIDM operates in pixel space with high memory cost and slow inference, while PoCoLD's latent U-Net underperforms on FID and SSIM. Although transformer-based diffusion (DiT, MDT) has proven effective for general image generation, no prior work has applied masked diffusion transformers to the PHIG task, and existing PHIG methods use complex multi-level condition injection (disentangled CFG, multi-scale features) rather than a unified conditioning mechanism.

## Input / Output

The model takes a source person image (256×256 or 512×512) and a target pose rendered as a simple 3-channel RGB visualization of 18 OpenPose keypoints (resized to 224×224, in contrast to PIDM's 20-channel heatmap representation). Both are mapped into a shared latent space via a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion VAE (32×32×4), and the pose and source image are additionally encoded by frozen DINOv2-B and DINOv2-G respectively. The output is a target person image at the same resolution as the input, generated via 50-step DDIM sampling in latent space.

## Method

X-MDPT comprises three modules: (1) TDNet, a DiT-style transformer denoiser operating on 256 latent patches (32×32 latent with patch size 2) that predicts noise via AdaLN-Zero modulation; (2) CANet, which aggregates three condition sources—local VAE latent tokens, DINOv2-B pose tokens, and DINOv2-G global source tokens—each reduced to a single vector via 1×1 convolutions, then combined (addition or MLP) into one unified condition vector that guides the entire diffusion process through a single AdaLN pathway; (3) MIPNet, a training-only mask inter-prediction module that masks 30% of target tokens and predicts them using a self-attention block followed by a cross-attention block attending to all source image tokens, enforcing cross-view semantic correspondence. Unlike PIDM/PoCoLD's disentangled CFG requiring 3 forwards per step, X-MDPT uses standard CFG with the unified vector (2 forwards per step), and MIPNet is discarded at inference. The architecture scales across S/B/L configurations (12/12/24 layers, 384/768/1024 dim) with consistent metric improvements.

## Results

On DeepFashion (256×176), X-MDPT-L (350k steps) achieves the best FID of 6.25, surpassing PIDM (6.37) and PoCoLD (8.07). The compact X-MDPT-S (33.5M params) reaches FID 7.43 with 11× fewer parameters than PoCoLD (396M), while X-MDPT-L (460M) matches PIDM's FID with only 2/3 of its parameters and 5.43× faster inference (3.12s vs 16.98s for 8 images). At 512×352, X-MDPT-L (1M steps) achieves the best SSIM (0.7522) and LPIPS (0.1645), though PIDM retains a slight FID edge (5.84 vs 7.16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

X-MDPT-L (350k) achieves the best FID (6.25) at 256×176, narrowly beating PIDM (6.37) while using only 460M vs 688M parameters and running 5.43× faster. The compact X-MDPT-S (33.5M) outperforms the latent Unet-based PoCoLD (396M) on FID (7.43 vs 8.07) with 11× fewer parameters and 2.7× faster inference. Against all GAN-based baselines (Def-GAN, ADGAN, GFLA, NTED, CASD, DPTN), X-MDPT variants dominate across all three metrics, with FID improvements of 1.4–12.2 points over the best GAN (NTED, FID 8.68). At 512×352, X-MDPT-L (500k) slightly trails PIDM on FID (5.93 vs 5.84) but the fully-trained X-MDPT-L (1M) surpasses PIDM on SSIM (0.7522 vs 0.7419) and LPIPS (0.1645 vs 0.1768). Inference speed is a consistent advantage: X-MDPT-S generates 8 images in 1.19s vs PIDM's 16.98s (14.25× speedup), attributable to latent-space operation and standard CFG (100 forwards) versus PIDM's disentangled CFG (150 forwards) in pixel space.

## Relation to Prior Work

X-MDPT extends the DiT (Peebles & Xie, 2023) and MDT (Gao et al., 2023) lineage of transformer-based diffusion models into the pose-guided human image generation (PHIG) domain, where prior state-of-the-art methods (PIDM, PoCoLD) relied on Unet-based denoisers. The key architectural departure is the Mask Inter-Prediction Network (MIPNet), which replaces MDT's self-attention-only mask filling with a cross-view attention mechanism that leverages the source image to predict masked target tokens. Additionally, the Conditional Aggregation Network (CANet) consolidates all conditions (pose, local VAE features, global DINOv2 features) into a single vector for AdaLN modulation, contrasting with the multi-level feature injection strategies used by CASD, PIDM, and PoCoLD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that a compact transformer-based diffusion model (33MB) can match or exceed much larger Unet-based and pixel-space diffusion approaches on PHIG, offering a practical efficiency path for pose-guided generation. The 99.99% view-invariance of the aggregated condition vector provides a useful diagnostic for identity preservation, and the scalability results (S→B→L) suggest further gains are possible. However, evaluation is limited to the DeepFashion dataset, FID at 512×512 still lags slightly behind PIDM, and failure cases (clothing changes, incorrect OpenPose detections) remain unresolved. Open questions include whether the single-vector condition paradigm generalizes to more complex multi-condition tasks and whether cross-view masking benefits extend beyond the fashion domain.
