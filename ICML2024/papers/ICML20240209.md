## Problem

Gradient-based explanation methods such as Integrated Gradients produce precise, fine-grained feature attributions but require full internal access to the target model, which is often unavailable due to safety and security constraints. Existing black-box explainers (LIME, RISE) only need query-level access but yield coarse, fuzzy hot-region explanations that miss low-level structural details. The paper asks whether gradient-quality explanations can be recovered under a purely black-box setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240209/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an image explicand (28×28 grayscale for MNIST/Fashion-MNIST, 299×299 RGB for ImageNet), a black-box classifier accessible only through input-output queries, and a baseline image (zero matrix for grayscale datasets; a blurred self-version for ImageNet). It produces a per-pixel attribution vector that is visualized as a saliency map overlaid on the original input. The explanation is computed with a fixed budget of n* = 5k queries, a Gaussian search distribution (σ = 1.0 for grayscale, 0.3 for ImageNet), and optional Gaussian mask smoothing (kernel 5, σ = 0.7) for high-dimensional inputs to encourage spatially coherent perturbations.

## Method

GEEX replaces the exact gradient in Integrated Gradients with a Monte Carlo gradient estimator built on the log-likelihood trick, integrating estimated gradients along a straight-line path from a baseline to the explicand. A key design choice is treating the explicand as the location parameter of the search distribution, which decouples mask sampling from the concrete input value and allows a single pre-generated mask set (with pre-computed log-derivatives) to be reused across all explicand–baseline pairs. The two nested sums (path-integral steps × gradient-estimation samples) are merged into a single sum over (ε, α) pairs, yielding a dense one-sample estimator per query that provides a smoother approximation of the path integral than sparse interpolation. Mirror sampling is used for variance reduction, and the paper proves that GEEX satisfies four fundamental axioms (Sensitivity, Insensitivity, Implementation Invariance, Linearity) plus Completeness in the limit of sufficient samples, with the approximation error arising from both the line-integral discretization and the gradient-estimator variance.

## Results

Using normalized AOPC (Area Over Perturbation Curve) from evaluation via deletion as the metric, GEEX achieves the best scores on MNIST (0.9466/0.9486) and Fashion-MNIST (0.9350/0.9362) under baseline/Gaussian replacement, surpassing even the white-box IG method (0.9434/0.9415 and 0.9275/0.9219 respectively). On the higher-dimensional ImageNet task with InceptionV3, GEEX scores 0.7952 (baseline) and 0.9058 (Gaussian), slightly trailing IG (0.8805/0.9155) but still outperforming all other black-box methods. The gap to IG on ImageNet narrows as the number of queries increases, with GEEX converging toward IG's performance at higher query budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEEX consistently outperforms all four baselines (SG, RISE, LIME, and raw GE) across every dataset and replacement setting. Against SmoothGrad (SG), GEEX gains roughly 6–11 AOPC points on ImageNet (e.g., 0.9058 vs. 0.8557 under Gaussian replacement) and 6–11 points on the smaller datasets, confirming that raw gradients suffer from saturation. Against RISE, GEEX improves by 6–14 points (e.g., 0.9486 vs. 0.8896 on MNIST-Gaussian), reflecting RISE's coarser superpixel-based attribution. Against LIME, the margin is even larger (up to ~14 points on ImageNet-Gaussian: 0.9058 vs. 0.8837), as LIME's linear surrogate cannot capture fine-grained pixel-level structure. The most interesting comparison is with IG: GEEX actually surpasses IG on MNIST and Fashion-MNIST (by ~0.3–0.7 AOPC points), attributed to its smoother dense approximation of the path integral, but falls slightly behind IG on ImageNet (0.7952 vs. 0.8805 baseline; 0.9058 vs. 0.9155 Gaussian) due to higher gradient-estimation variance in the 299×299 feature space. The paper shows this gap closes as query count increases, with GEEX converging to IG's score at higher budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

GEEX sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: the path-integral framework of Integrated Gradients (Sundararajan et al., 2017), which provides axiomatic guarantees for gradient-based explanations, and Monte Carlo gradient estimation (Mohamed et al., 2020), a technique from black-box optimization that approximates gradients via query-level observations. The paper's core conceptual move is to substitute the true gradient in IG with a log-likelihood-ratio gradient estimator, thereby inheriting IG's axiomatic structure while requiring only query access. This departs from prior black-box explainers (LIME, RISE) that rely on surrogate models or expected-impact heuristics rather than gradient-like quantities, and from prior gradient-estimation work (e.g., NEST) that targets optimization rather than explanation. Mirror sampling (Brockhoff et al., 2010) is adopted as a variance-reduction strategy for the estimator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first black-box attribution method with rigorous proofs of Completeness, Sensitivity, Insensitivity, Implementation Invariance, and Linearity, closing a gap where prior black-box methods lacked such theoretical grounding. The finding that the 'smoothed' dense-estimator variant can surpass white-box IG on lower-dimensional tasks (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST) challenges the assumption that internal model access is strictly necessary for optimal explanations. However, the quadratic growth of estimator variance with feature dimension means that on high-dimensional inputs like ImageNet, GEEX still trails IG and requires substantially more queries to converge, limiting its practical advantage in the most demanding settings. The authors identify feature-space decomposition guided by the Linearity property as a promising but unexplored direction to address this scalability bottleneck.
