## Problem

Prior definitions of temporal distance in stochastic Markov processes fail to satisfy the triangle inequality, which prevents combinatorial generalization (stitching) and shortest-path finding. The root cause is that in stochastic settings the 'distance' from a state to itself is non-zero, violating the identity axiom. This paper shows that a simple change of variables on contrastive successor features resolves both issues, yielding a temporal distance that is a valid quasimetric even under stochastic dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes offline trajectory data as state-action pairs paired with geometrically-discounted future states (goals) and produces an asymmetric quasimetric over the joint state-action space S×A, where the asymmetry encodes the arrow of time (e.g., climbing up is harder than sliding down). The learned distance serves as a value function from which a goal-conditioned policy is extracted via advantage-weighted regression or behavioral-cloning regularization, all without temporal-difference bootstrapping. In the AntMaze experiments the state vectors are 111-dimensional continuous observations.

## Method

The core insight is that the optimal infoNCE critic from temporal contrastive learning, when evaluated at the goal and subtracted from its evaluation at the current state, yields a function that provably satisfies the triangle inequality (Theorem 3.4). Two practical variants are proposed: CMD 1-step parametrizes the energy function directly as the difference of a quasimetric network d_φ(s,a,g) and a learned scalar c_ψ(g), so the contrastive objective simultaneously learns both; CMD 2-step first trains standard contrastive features via inner-product parametrization, then distills the change-of-variables expression into a Metric Residual Network (MRN) using a Lagrange-multiplier constraint that enforces the margin between the MRN distance and the contrastive score. The resulting successor distance d_SD(s,g) = min_π log(p_γ^π(s⁺=g|s₀=g) / p_γ^π(s⁺=g|s₀=s)) reduces to hitting time in deterministic settings but remains a valid quasimetric in stochastic ones, enabling combinatorial generalization without bootstrapping.

## Results

On the AntMaze offline RL benchmark suite (111-dimensional robotic locomotion), CMD 2-step achieves the highest success rate on umaze (97.0%) and umaze-diverse (90.5%), while CMD 1-step leads on the harder medium and large environments (e.g., 74.5% on large-diverse vs. 51.5% for QRL and 54.1% for CRL). In a controlled 2D navigation experiment testing combinatorial generalization, the proposed temporal distance reaches ~80% goal-reaching success, compared to ~50% for the best prior method (Quasimetric RL). The method also demonstrates stitching: it successfully plans paths between state pairs that never co-occur in the training data, a property not exhibited by contrastive RL without metric distillation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMD 1-step outperforms Quasimetric RL (QRL) on all four harder AntMaze environments (medium-play, medium-diverse, large-play, large-diverse) by 1.5–23.0 percentage points, with the largest gap on large-diverse (74.5% vs. 51.5%), suggesting that a temporally well-defined distance in stochastic settings matters more than the quasimetric architecture alone. Against Contrastive RL (CRL/CPC), which uses the same contrastive features but without metric distillation, CMD 1-step gains 5.4–20.4 points on the harder tasks, highlighting the value of enforcing the triangle inequality as an inductive bias. Both CMD variants substantially outperform GCBC (a goal-conditioned behavioral cloning baseline) across all six environments, with gaps of 25–42 points on the large environments. Compared to IQL, CMD 1-step leads on all environments except umaze (where IQL reports 87.5% without a standard error), with the advantage growing in harder settings (e.g., 74.5% vs. 47.5% on large-diverse). In the synthetic 2D navigation experiment, CMD achieves ~80% success versus ~50% for QRL and ~40% for CRL, confirming that the quasimetric distillation step is the key differentiator over raw contrastive features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of contrastive representation learning for goal-conditioned RL (Eysenbach et al., 2022) and quasimetric network architectures (Wang & Isola, 2022; Liu et al., 2023). The key departure from prior temporal distance work (hitting times, quasimetric RL) is showing that contrastive successor features, after a simple change of variables (subtracting the self-distance), provably form a quasimetric even in stochastic settings—something prior hitting-time-based distances cannot guarantee. Rather than learning a distance from scratch with a metric architecture, the paper repurposes the contrastive critic's implicit structure, requiring only a distillation step into a Metric Residual Network to enforce the triangle inequality architecturally.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the first (per the paper's framing) temporal distance with a provable triangle inequality in stochastic MDPs, filling a gap that has limited the applicability of metric-based planning and generalization in realistic, noisy environments. The practical implication is that combinatorial generalization ('stitching') can be achieved without bootstrapping or temporal-difference learning, simply by imposing metric structure on contrastive features. Limitations include the requirement of discrete states for the theoretical guarantees and potential infinite distances in non-ergodic settings; extending the theory to continuous state spaces and understanding when the distance remains finite are natural open questions.
