## Problem

Despite the empirical success of MLP-Mixer in image classification, the underlying mechanism explaining why it outperforms conventional MLPs remained largely unexplored. Specifically, it was unclear what inductive biases the Mixer possesses compared to a naive MLP and which architectural factors drive its superior performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240710/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 90×90 for STL-10, 224×224 for ImageNet-1k), divides them into non-overlapping patches, and rearranges them into an S×C feature matrix where S is the number of patches and C is the per-patch channel dimension. The output is a class label via global average pooling followed by a linear classifier. Unusually, the effective width of the network is m=SC (ranging from 10⁴ to 10⁶), making each mixing layer equivalent to an extremely wide fully connected layer with highly sparse structured weights.

## Method

The paper reveals that MLP-Mixer is equivalent to a wide MLP with Kronecker-product weights by vectorizing the feature matrix: the token-mixing and channel-mixing layers become multiplications by I_C⊗W and V^T⊗I_S (with commutation matrices), yielding an effective width m=SC with sparsity 1/S or 1/C. In the linear activation case, this Kronecker parameterization induces an implicit L1 regularization and connects the Mixer to Monarch matrices (with weight-sharing diagonal blocks). The authors further propose the PK family, which generalizes the Mixer by allowing arbitrary permutation matrices J₁, J₂ in each layer, and the RP-Mixer variant that uses random permutations to destroy the block-diagonal structure while preserving the spectral properties. Under a fixed number of connections Ω, the effective width m=SC is maximized (and sparsity maximized) at S*=C*=(Ω/γ)^(1/3), and experiments confirm that performance improves as width increases toward this optimum, matching the trends observed for unstructured sparse-weight MLPs.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the wider Mixer (Mixer-SS-W, max. width 1.2×10⁵) achieves 12.07% test error, outperforming both the original Mixer-SS/8 (15.91%) and the β-LASSO sparse-training baseline (14.81%) under the same ~256M connections. On CIFAR-100, Mixer-SS-W reaches 38.13% test error versus 40.44% for β-LASSO. On ImageNet-1k, the wider Mixer-B-W (S=256, C=588) achieves 23.26% top-1 error, improving over the original Mixer-B/16 (S=196, C=786) at 23.56% with the same Ω=2.6×10⁸ connections per layer. The key finding is that maximizing effective width (by setting S≈C) under fixed connectivity consistently improves test error across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against β-LASSO (Neyshabur, 2020), the wider Mixer-SS-W clearly wins on both CIFAR-10 (12.07% vs. 14.81%) and CIFAR-100 (38.13% vs. 40.44%) with essentially the same number of connections (~256M), demonstrating that structured sparsity via the Mixer architecture outperforms dynamic L1-based sparsification. Against the SW-MLP of Golubeva et al. (2021), the paper shows that both architectures follow the same width–sparsity performance trend (test error decreases as effective width increases under fixed Ω), but the Mixer avoids the trainability degradation that SW-MLP suffers at very high sparsity due to its bounded maximal singular value (c_γ = 1+√γ) versus the unbounded growth of SW-MLP's largest singular value. Against the Monarch matrix of Dao et al. (2022), the paper shows that the Kronecker-product weights in the Mixer (a weight-sharing variant of Monarch) achieve comparable test error on MNIST (Figure 2d), confirming that the Mixer's inherent structure captures the benefits of Monarch matrices without requiring the full parameter budget. On ImageNet-1k, the wider Mixer-B-W (S=256, C=588) improves over the original Mixer-B/16 (S=196, C=786) by 0.30% top-1 error, validating that the S≈C configuration maximizing width is beneficial even at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads—MLP-Mixer architectures (Tolstikhin et al., 2021), sparse-weight MLPs with fixed connectivity (Golubeva et al., 2021), and structured matrix parameterizations including Kronecker products and Monarch matrices (Dao et al., 2022)—by showing that the MLP-Mixer's mixing layers are mathematically equivalent to an extremely wide MLP with Kronecker-product (hence highly sparse) weights. The vectorization identity vec(WXV) = (V⊤⊗W)vec(X) provides the bridge, revealing that the Mixer's token and channel mixing are simply block-diagonal operations in a reparameterized space. The paper extends this by introducing the PK family (permuted Kronecker layers) and the RP-Mixer, which deliberately destroy the block-diagonal structure via random permutations to more closely approximate unstructured sparse-weight MLPs while retaining memory efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is clear: for a fixed number of connections Ω, setting S ≈ C = (Ω/γ)^(1/3) maximizes both effective width and sparsity, yielding better generalization—this gives MLP-Mixer practitioners a principled hyperparameter choice that the original paper did not provide. The RP-Mixer offers a memory-efficient alternative to explicit sparse-weight MLPs (O(C²+S²) vs O(SC)² memory) that can match or exceed normal Mixers at sufficient depth, opening a path to wider effective architectures without prohibitive compute. Open questions include the lack of a full theoretical treatment of the linear mixing layer (bilinear regression has no known closed-form solution), whether the S≈C optimality holds beyond the fixed-Ω setting, and whether other structured architectures (e.g., convolutions, attention) harbor similar hidden sparsity that could be exploited.
