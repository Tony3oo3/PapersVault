## Problem

Prior replicable algorithms for learning large-margin halfspaces (Impagliazzo et al., STOC 2022) suffered from dimension-dependent sample complexity, were improper (outputting majority-vote ensembles rather than halfspaces), and had suboptimal dependence on the accuracy, margin, and replicability parameters. This paper asks whether these bounds are tight and provides the first dimension-independent, proper, polynomial-time replicable algorithms with strictly improved sample complexity across all parameters.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes m i.i.d. labeled samples (x, y) ∈ R^d × {-1, 1} drawn from a distribution with linear margin τ (i.e., a unit vector w* exists such that y(w*ᵀx/‖x‖) ≥ τ for all support points), along with parameters ε (target accuracy), ρ (replicability), and δ (confidence). It outputs a single unit vector ŵ ∈ R^d defining a proper linear classifier. The key constraint is that two independent executions on i.i.d. datasets, sharing internal randomness (JL projection matrix and rounding offsets), must produce identical outputs with probability at least 1−ρ.

## Method

The core mechanism is a single-shot pipeline: run a non-replicable learner (SVM in Algorithm 1, boosted SGD on a convex surrogate in Algorithm 2) on B independent batches, average the resulting unit vectors into z, project z into a k = Õ(τ⁻²) dimensional space via a data-oblivious Johnson-Lindenstrauss matrix A (shared across executions since it depends only on internal randomness), and round Az using the Alon-Klartag scheme with shared offsets and thresholds. The JL projection eliminates dimension dependence because the same random matrix is used in both executions, while the Alon-Klartag rounding—first analyzed here for replicability—preserves inner products in expectation and has a stability guarantee (Lemma 2.1) ensuring the rounded vectors coincide with high probability when the pre-rounding vectors are close. Algorithm 3 instead projects the entire dataset, covers the low-dimensional unit ball with a (τ/20)-net to form a finite hypothesis class, and applies a replicable finite-class learner from Bun et al. (2023), trading polynomial time for better τ-dependence.

## Results

The paper provides four replicable algorithms for learning large-margin halfspaces, all dimension-independent in sample complexity (no dependence on d), unlike prior work. Algorithm 1 achieves sample complexity Õ(ε^{-1}τ^{-7}ρ^{-2}) with polynomial running time and is proper (outputs a linear classifier). Algorithm 2 improves the margin dependence to Õ(ε^{-2}τ^{-6}ρ^{-2}) at the cost of an extra 1/ε factor, also in polynomial time. Algorithm 3 (inefficient) achieves the best sample complexity Õ(ε^{-1}τ^{-4}ρ^{-2}) with running time poly(d)·poly(1/ε,1/τ,1/ρ)^{1/τ²}. All algorithms are proper, whereas both prior algorithms from Impagliazzo et al. (2022) are improper.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Impagliazzo et al. (STOC 2022), both prior algorithms have sample complexity depending on the dimension d (as d or d³) and are improper (outputting majority votes over halfspaces rather than a single linear classifier). Algorithm 1 eliminates the dimension dependence entirely and improves the ε-dependence from ε^{-3} (foams) or ε^{-4} (box) to ε^{-1}, the τ-dependence from τ^{-8} or τ^{-10} to τ^{-7}, while also being proper and running in polynomial time. Algorithm 2 further improves the τ-dependence to τ^{-6} at the cost of ε^{-2}. The DP-to-Replicability reduction (combining Lê Nguyen et al. 2020 with Bun et al. 2023) achieves the best τ-dependence of τ^{-4} but incurs a quadratic blow-up in sample complexity (ε^{-2} instead of ε^{-1}) and doubly-exponential running time. Algorithm 3 matches the τ^{-4} dependence of the DP reduction while improving the ε-dependence to ε^{-1} and reducing the running time from doubly-exponential to single-exponential in 1/τ².

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Impagliazzo et al. (STOC 2022), who introduced the first replicable algorithms for large-margin halfspaces using foams/box discretization and a replicable boosting pipeline. The key departure is a fundamentally different technical approach: instead of discretizing in the full d-dimensional space and boosting weak learners, the authors use a single-shot pipeline (SVM or SGD on batches → average → Johnson-Lindenstrauss projection → Alon-Klartag rounding) that eliminates both the dimension dependence and the impropriety of prior work. The Alon-Klartag rounding scheme, previously used by Grønlund et al. (2020) for SVM generalization bounds, is repurposed here as the first application of this scheme in the replicability literature, with a novel stability analysis under shared randomness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dimension-independence result is significant because prior replicable algorithms for halfspaces necessarily incurred a dimension factor due to their discretization step, while non-replicable algorithms are already dimension-independent; this paper closes that gap for the first time. The properness (outputting a single linear classifier rather than a boosted ensemble) makes the algorithms more practical and interpretable. Open questions remain: whether the τ^{-4} sample complexity of Algorithm 3 can be achieved in polynomial time, whether an inherent barrier prevents reaching the non-replicable Õ(ε^{-1}τ^{-2}) rate, and whether the pigeonhole argument in Algorithm 1's analysis can be tightened from τ^{-3}n to τ^{-2}n.
