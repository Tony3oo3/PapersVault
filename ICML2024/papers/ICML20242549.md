## Problem

Rehearsal-based class-incremental learning methods are prone to overfitting on the small replay memory, causing poor generalization on previously learned tasks. This paper identifies the root cause through the lens of all-layer margin: the margin on rehearsal samples systematically shrinks as the model is incrementally updated for new tasks, meaning these samples can no longer resist the same perturbations and provide less useful signal for maintaining the decision boundary.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet-100) and outputs class labels over an ever-growing set of classes. During training, each batch mixes samples from the current task with a fixed-size rehearsal memory (500, 1000, or 2000 samples); MRFA specifically intercepts the intermediate layer features of rehearsal samples within the forward pass, so the raw input format is unchanged but the effective training signal is modified at the feature level.

## Method

MRFA augments rehearsal sample features at a randomly selected layer by performing a single gradient ascent step on the cross-entropy loss with respect to that layer's input feature, pushing the feature away from the decision boundary. The perturbation magnitude is scaled by the L2 norm of the feature multiplied by a random factor drawn from U(0, β), which balances scale across layers and introduces difficulty diversity. The augmented feature is then forwarded through the remaining layers to compute the classification loss, which is backpropagated to update the model. This augmentation is applied only to rehearsal samples (not current-task samples) and is compatible with knowledge distillation by replicating the same augmentation in the teacher model. The approach requires one extra forward-backward pass per batch but can be amortized by applying it every p batches with negligible accuracy loss.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (2000 memory, 10-10 split), MRFA improves average accuracy by 0.6–0.7% across all four baselines: Replay 61.95→62.59, iCaRL 64.52→65.17, FOSTER 74.49→75.23, and DyTox+ 75.54→76.23. On ImageNet-100 (2000 memory, 10-10), the gains are slightly larger, e.g., Replay 66.00→67.22 (+1.22) and DyTox+ 76.35→77.31 (+0.96). The improvements are consistent across all memory sizes (500/1000/2000) and both split strategies (10-10 and 50-10), with typical gains of 1–2% in average incremental accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MRFA is not a standalone method but a plug-in augmentation applied on top of existing rehearsal-based CIL baselines, so the comparisons are always 'baseline vs. baseline + MRFA.' It consistently wins across all four baselines (Replay, iCaRL, FOSTER, DyTox+) on both CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-100, in every memory size and split configuration tested. The gains are modest but uniform: roughly 0.5–1.5% in average accuracy, with the largest relative improvements seen on the weaker Replay baseline (e.g., +1.22 Avg on ImageNet-100 10-10) and the smallest on the strongest DyTox+ (e.g., +0.69 Avg on CIFAR-100 10-10). Notably, MRFA works across both CNN backbones (ResNet-32/18) and transformer backbones (Convit via DyTox+), and is compatible with knowledge distillation used in iCaRL and FOSTER. The paper also shows that applying MRFA to both current-task and rehearsal samples (MRFA-a) actually hurts performance, confirming that the benefit comes specifically from enlarging the margin on rehearsal samples rather than from general data augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the all-layer margin framework of Wei & Ma (2020), which provides depth-independent generalization bounds for deep networks, and applies it as a diagnostic lens to rehearsal-based class-incremental learning. While prior CIL work (iCaRL, FOSTER, DyTox) focuses on how to select or utilize rehearsal samples, and related augmentation work (RAR, GMED) modifies rehearsal inputs at the pixel level, this paper is the first to analyze and manipulate the margin at intermediate layer features. The method itself draws a conceptual parallel to adversarial training (gradient ascent for perturbation) but repurposes it as a training-time feature-space augmentation rather than a robustness objective, and it is designed to be compatible with knowledge distillation used in many CIL baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded explanation for why rehearsal-based CIL overfits—margin shrinkage on stored samples—and offers a simple, plug-and-play remedy that improves four diverse baselines by 1–2% without additional stored samples. However, the gains are modest, the method requires an extra forward-backward pass per batch (mitigated but not eliminated by skipping batches), and the hyperparameter β is scenario-dependent. Open questions include whether the margin-shrinkage diagnosis generalizes to rehearsal-free or generative-replay methods, and whether the layer-wise gradient-ascent augmentation could be extended to other continual learning paradigms such as domain or task-incremental settings.
