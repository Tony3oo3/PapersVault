## Problem

Model-based RL agents that train in imagination face a fundamental trade-off: RNN-based world models (e.g., RSSM in DreamerV3) require sequential processing during training, while Transformer-based world models are computationally expensive at inference due to attention. Additionally, existing hierarchical RL approaches are limited to two levels and do not learn separate world models at each abstraction layer, restricting the agent's ability to plan across multiple time scales. HIEROS addresses both gaps by combining a multi-level hierarchical imagination framework with a sequence model architecture that is efficient in both training and inference.

## Input / Output

HIEROS consumes raw Atari pixel frames (84×84 RGB) and discrete environment actions, processing them through a 3-level hierarchy where each level operates at a 4× time abstraction of the one below. The lowest level outputs the actual environment action; higher levels output compressed subgoals (8 categorical vectors of size 8) that the next lower level must reach. Subgoals can be decoded back into image space via the subgoal autoencoder, providing visual explainability of the agent's planning. The world model also produces imagined latent trajectories of horizon 16 for actor-critic training at each level.

## Method

HIEROS stacks three subactors, each containing an S5-based world model (S5WM), a goal-conditioned actor-critic trained via REINFORCE, and a subgoal autoencoder that compresses the deterministic world state into a discrete 8×8 categorical space. The S5WM replaces the RSSM's GRU with a stack of 8 S5 blocks, exploiting S5's dual-mode capability: parallel convolutional processing for training on full trajectories and iterative autoregressive stepping for imagination, while the directly accessible internal state x_t serves as the deterministic world state h_t. A reset mechanism (from Lu et al., 2023) handles episode boundaries during parallel training. The reward at each level combines extrinsic reward, a cosine-similarity subgoal reward, and a novelty reward from the autoencoder's reconstruction error. An efficient time-balanced sampling (ETBS) method with O(1) complexity corrects the skewed replay distribution via a closed-form CDF transformation, replacing the O(n) approach of prior work.

## Results

On the Atari 100k benchmark (25 games), HIEROS achieves a new state-of-the-art mean normalized human score of 120 and median of 56, outperforming DreamerV3 (112/49), IRIS (105/29), TWM (96/50), Director (52/18), and SimPLe (34/11). HIEROS also achieves the best IQM (53) and lowest optimality gap (49) among all compared methods. Training one game for 100k steps takes ~14 hours on an A100 GPU, comparable to DreamerV3 (~0.5 days) and TWM (~0.8 days) but significantly faster than IRIS (~7 days).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HIEROS outperforms all five baselines on every reported aggregate metric. Against the closest competitor DreamerV3 (18M params), HIEROS gains +8 mean and +7 median normalized human score, with the largest per-game improvements in Frostbite (2901 vs 909), JamesBond (939 vs 445), and PrivateEye (1507 vs 882)—all multi-level games with distribution shifts. Against Transformer-based world models IRIS and TWM, HIEROS leads by 15 and 24 mean points respectively, while also being substantially faster at training (14h vs 7 days for IRIS). Against the hierarchical baseline Director, HIEROS achieves more than 2× the mean score (120 vs 52), attributed to its deeper 3-level hierarchy and S5-based world model. HIEROS underperforms on simple-dynamics games like Breakout (10 vs 31 for DreamerV3) and Pong (5 vs 18), where the hierarchical structure provides no benefit and the larger S5WM is harder to train within the 100k budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

HIEROS combines two established lines of work: the DreamerV3 world-model-based RL architecture (Hafner et al., 2023) and the Director hierarchical policy with subgoal autoencoders (Hafner et al., 2022a), while replacing the GRU-based RSSM with S5 structured state space layers (Smith et al., 2023). The key architectural departure is scaling hierarchical imagination beyond the two-level structure used in Director and most prior HRL+world-model work, and exploiting the dual parallel/iterative nature of S5 layers to get efficient training (parallel) and efficient imagination (iterative) simultaneously. The ETBS sampling method extends the time-balanced replay of Robine et al. (2023) from O(n) to O(1) via a closed-form CDF, and the use of the S5 internal state as the deterministic world state (rather than the sequence model output) is a design choice not made in comparable S4WM or Transformer-based world models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HIEROS provides a practical middle ground for world model backbones: more training-efficient than RNNs and more inference-efficient than Transformers, which is valuable for real-time or resource-constrained deployment. The finding that hierarchical structure systematically hurts performance on simple-dynamics games (Breakout, Pong) while helping on multi-level games (Frostbite, Krull) clarifies when HRL+world-models is beneficial and suggests automatic hierarchy depth adaptation as a useful future direction. Open questions include direct comparison with S4WM (code not public), evaluation beyond Atari 100k (BSuite, DMC, Crafter), and whether an S5-based actor network could enable multi-step imagination and look-ahead search within the hierarchical structure.
