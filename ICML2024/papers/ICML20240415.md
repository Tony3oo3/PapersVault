## Problem

In multi-group learning, existing algorithms face a trade-off: those achieving near-optimal group-wise excess error rates (e.g., O(√(log(|H||G|))/n_g)) output complex randomized ensembles requiring enumeration of the hypothesis class, while simpler deterministic predictors like Prepend suffer suboptimal rates with a cube-root dependence. No algorithm existed that was simultaneously simple, interpretable, deterministic, and near-optimal for the natural case where groups are hierarchically structured.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes n i.i.d. labeled examples, a fixed collection of hierarchically structured groups G (where any two groups are either disjoint or one contains the other, forming a rooted tree), a finite benchmark hypothesis class H, and per-group error thresholds ε_n(g). It outputs a single deterministic decision tree predictor f: X → Z whose tree structure mirrors the group hierarchy: each node g carries a working predictor f^g ∈ H, and prediction on input x follows the path from root to the deepest group containing x. Group membership g(x) must be evaluable at both training and test time, and the same predictor f must simultaneously achieve small excess risk on every group in G.

## Method

MGL-Tree (Algorithm 1) constructs the decision tree via breadth-first search over the hierarchical group tree. At each node g, it computes the group-conditional ERM predictor ĥ^g and compares the current tree's empirical risk on g against ĥ^g's risk; if ĥ^g is better by a margin ε_n(g), the node's predictor is updated to ĥ^g, otherwise it inherits its parent's predictor. The key technical insight is a monotonicity property: because of the hierarchical structure and BFS ordering, updating a child node's predictor never increases the empirical risk on any ancestor group, so the algorithm makes forward progress without violating previously satisfied error bounds. This yields a near-optimal group-wise excess error of O(√(log(|H||G|))/n_g) for finite H (and O(√(d log(|G|n)/n_g)) for VC dimension d), while producing a simple, interpretable, deterministic decision tree that avoids enumerating H.

## Results

The paper provides a theoretical guarantee that MGL-Tree achieves a group-wise excess error of O(√(log(|H||G|))/n_g) for finite H, matching the state-of-the-art rate of Tosh & Hsu (2022) while outputting a simple deterministic decision tree. Empirically, across all 12 US Census (Folktables) datasets, MGL-Tree consistently achieves test misclassification error on par with or better than the minimum of global ERM and group-specific ERM for every group, validating the multi-group learning property. On the CA Income dataset, MGL-Tree outperforms Prepend on many groups by preferring coarser-grained subgroup predictors (e.g., ALL or R1) over the finer-grained leaf predictors that Prepend tends to overfit to. In some cases, MGL-Tree with a simple logistic regression benchmark class even outperforms ERM random forest and XGBoost baselines on most groups.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MGL-Tree against the Prepend algorithm (Tosh & Hsu, 2022; Globus-Harris et al., 2022) and standard ERM/Group ERM baselines. MGL-Tree performs on par with or better than Prepend on all groups across all 12 datasets; the key empirical difference is that MGL-Tree's breadth-first search on the hierarchical tree causes it to retain coarser-grained parent predictors when the finer-grained child's predictor is not significantly better, whereas Prepend's arbitrary decision-list ordering tends to overfit to fine-grained leaf subgroups with insufficient samples. Against global ERM and group-specific ERM, MGL-Tree consistently matches or beats the minimum error of both, confirming the multi-group learning guarantee. Notably, MGL-Tree with a simple logistic regression benchmark sometimes outperforms more complex ERM random forest and XGBoost predictors on most groups (e.g., CA Employment), suggesting the tree structure provides a favorable inductive bias for hierarchically structured groups. The theoretical advantage over Prepend is that MGL-Tree achieves the near-optimal O(√(log(|H||G|))/n_g) rate, while Prepend's rate degrades to O(∛(log(|H||G|))/(γ·n_g)) where γ is the minimum group probability mass.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-group agnostic PAC learning framework of Rothblum & Yona (2021) and the near-optimal algorithms of Tosh & Hsu (2022) to the natural special case where the collection of groups has hierarchical structure. The key departure is that hierarchical structure allows a simple, deterministic decision tree predictor (MGL-Tree) built via breadth-first search to achieve the same O(√(log(|H||G|))/n_g) excess error rate that previously required either a randomized ensemble of n predictors or the suboptimal Prepend decision list. This directly resolves an open question posed by Tosh & Hsu (2022) about whether one can simultaneously achieve simplicity (interpretable, deterministic output) and near-optimal sample complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first algorithm that achieves near-optimal multi-group learning rates with a simple, interpretable, and deterministic predictor for hierarchically structured groups, removing the need to enumerate finite hypothesis classes or deploy randomized ensembles. Empirically, MGL-Tree consistently matches or beats both ERM and Prepend across twelve US Census datasets, and sometimes outperforms more complex tree-ensemble baselines, suggesting the tree inductive bias is well-aligned with hierarchical group structure. Limitations include the requirement that groups be known a priori and that they admit a hierarchical (laminar) structure, and the attribute ordering in the tree may affect performance. Open questions include extending the framework to non-hierarchical group collections, incorporating specific fairness criteria (e.g., equalized odds) as bounded loss functions, and understanding whether the tree structure provides benefits in slice-discovery settings where groups are found post-hoc.
