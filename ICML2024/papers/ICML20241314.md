## Problem

Existing open-source MLLMs are constrained by narrow training data (primarily natural-image vision-language pairs) and a limited range of LLM parameter sizes (typically 7B–13B dense models). This results in degraded performance on out-of-domain tasks such as OCR, tables, charts, and mathematics, while offering no practical options for mobile deployment or for unlocking stronger reasoning through sparsely-activated architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241314/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images at 448×448 (or 672×672 for SPHINX-Plus-2K) that are divided into a grid of sub-images plus one global downsampled image, alongside multi-turn text prompts in a unified conversational format. Fully-padded sub-images (arising from large aspect ratios) are replaced by a single learnable skip token rather than being encoded, shortening the LLM input sequence. Outputs include free-form text responses, bounding boxes for grounding/detection, executable code for visual programming, and multi-turn dialogues spanning bilingual (Chinese/English) conversations.

## Method

SPHINX-X modifies the SPHINX architecture in three ways: (1) it reduces four visual encoders to a Mixture of Visual Experts (MoV) retaining only DINOv2 and CLIP-ConvNeXt for complementary self-supervised and weakly-supervised features; (2) it introduces learnable skip tokens to bypass fully-padded sub-images, reducing sequence length while preserving relative positional information; and (3) it replaces SPHINX's two-stage weight-mixing pipeline with a single-stage all-in-one training that unfreezes all LLM and projection parameters simultaneously. Training data spans language instruction-following (multi-turn dialog, math, code), visual instruction-following (detection, pose, classification, grounding), vision-language instruction-following (VQA, captioning, fine-grained instructions), plus two newly curated datasets: a ~3M-page OCR-intensive PaperText corpus from PDFs and a multi-domain Set-of-Marks dataset with GPT-4V-generated dense captions. The family is instantiated on four base LLMs—TinyLlama-1.1B, InternLM2-7B, LLaMA2-13B, and Mixtral-8×7B—enabling a systematic study of how data diversity and parameter scale jointly drive multi-modal performance.

## Results

SPHINX-Plus (LLaMA2-13B) achieves 71.0 on MMBench, 74.8 on SeedBench, 47.9 on MM-Vet, and 36.8 on MathVista, surpassing the original SPHINX (67.1, 71.6, 36.6, 27.5) and LLaVA-1.5-13B (67.7, 61.6, 35.4, N/A) across most general MLLM benchmarks. SPHINX-MoE (Mixtral-8×7B) attains the highest MathVista score (42.7) and MME-Cognition (367.1) among all SPHINX-X variants, while also leading on TinyLVLM (335.3) and Q-Bench (66.2). On text-oriented VQA (Table 4), SPHINX-Plus-2K reaches 70.6 on TextVQA and 71.6 on DocVQA, and SPHINX-MoE achieves 184.4 on VisualMRC, both substantially outperforming the original SPHINX (58.8, 35.8, 95.3). On RefCOCO referring expression comprehension (Table 5), SPHINX-Plus scores 92.44/94.22/90.06 (val/test-A/test-B), exceeding the specialist G-DINO-L (90.56/93.19/88.24).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPHINX-Plus (13B) outperforms LLaVA-1.5-13B on MMBench (71.0 vs 67.7), SeedBench (74.8 vs 61.6), MM-Vet (47.9 vs 35.4), and Q-Bench (68.6 vs 62.1), but trails on MME-Perception (1457.7 vs 1531.3) and LLaVA-Bench In-the-Wild (71.7 vs 70.7, marginal). Compared to the original SPHINX, SPHINX-Plus improves MM-Vet by 11.3 points (47.9 vs 36.6) and MathVista by 9.3 points (36.8 vs 27.5), confirming the benefit of data scaling, though it slightly regresses on MME-Perception (1457.7 vs 1560.2) and POPE (89.1 vs 90.8). SPHINX-MoE (Mixtral-8×7B) achieves the best MathVista score (42.7) and MME-Cognition (367.1) in the family, surpassing both SPHINX and LLaVA-1.5-13B, but underperforms SPHINX on CC-Bench (15.4 vs 27.9) and MME-Perception (1485.3 vs 1560.2). On text-oriented VQA, SPHINX-Plus-2K leads all generalist models on TextVQA (70.6) and DocVQA (71.6), while SPHINX-MoE achieves the best VisualMRC score (184.4) among all compared methods. On RefCOCO, SPHINX-Plus (92.44 val) surpasses the specialist G-DINO-L (90.56) and all generalist baselines including Qwen-VL-7B (89.36) and Shikra-13B (87.83). SPHINX-Tiny (1.1B) remains competitive on TinyLVLM (301.5) and VisualMRC (147.5) despite its small size, but lags significantly on reasoning-heavy benchmarks like SeedBench (17.1) and MM-Vet (23.8).

## Relation to Prior Work

SPHINX-X is a direct architectural and training-pipeline refinement of SPHINX (Lin et al., 2023), which itself combined four visual encoders, multi-stage weight mixing, and high-resolution sub-image division. The key departures are: pruning the four-encoder mixture down to two complementary encoders (CLIP-ConvNeXt + DINOv2, termed MoV), replacing fully-padded sub-images with learnable skip tokens to shorten sequences, and collapsing the two-stage alignment-then-instruction-tuning pipeline into a single all-in-one stage. On the data side, it extends the MLLM training corpus beyond the natural-image and GPT-4-generated instruction data used by LLaVA, BLIP-2, and the original SPHINX by adding a 3M-page OCR-intensive PaperText dataset and a multi-domain Set-of-Marks dataset. On the model side, it systematically pairs the same training recipe with four LLM backbones spanning 1.1B to 8×7B parameters (including a sparse MoE backbone, Mixtral), connecting the MLLM literature to the broader LLM scaling and MoE literature (Mixtral, Switch Transformers).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides one of the first controlled, same-pipeline scaling studies for MLLMs, showing that both data diversity and LLM parameter count monotonically improve multi-modal performance from 1.1B to 8×7B, which gives practitioners a concrete guide for choosing model size versus deployment constraints. The one-stage all-in-one training and skip-token mechanism are practical simplifications that reduce engineering overhead without sacrificing performance, making large-scale MLLM training more accessible. However, SPHINX-MoE still lags GPT-4V on multi-discipline reasoning (MMMU, CMMMU), and the MoE expert analysis reveals no clear domain or modality specialization, leaving open whether richer multi-disciplinary data or longer training would induce such specialization. Video understanding remains a gap since the models are image-only, and the OCR/SoM gains, while meaningful, are achieved with relatively small curated subsets compared to the broader public data.
