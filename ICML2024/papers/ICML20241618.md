## Problem

Current image anonymization techniques focus on localized pseudonymization (masking, blurring, or generating faces/bodies) and evaluate success via binary detection or re-identification rates, overlooking privacy-compromising cues in the broader image context such as recognizable backgrounds, objects, or locations. No existing metric captures the human perception of anonymity across the entire image, which is critical for GDPR compliance where 'additional information' can lead to identification.

## Input / Output

PerceptAnon operates in two modes: HA1 takes a single anonymized RGB image (resized to 224×224) and predicts an anonymity score without any original reference, while HA2 takes an original–anonymized image pair and predicts a score by comparing both. The output is a continuous score in [0, 1] (regression) or an ordinal class label (10-class, 5-class, 3-class, or binary), derived from averaging six human annotators' 1–10 ratings. The HA1 mode is particularly notable because it mirrors real-world scenarios where an observer encounters an anonymized image without access to the original.

## Method

PerceptAnon is a learned metric built on ImageNet-pretrained CNN backbones (ResNet-50 for main results; ResNet-18/152, DenseNet-121, and AlexNet also evaluated). For HA1, a single CNN f_A1 processes the anonymized image; for HA2, a Siamese network f_A2 concatenates features from both the original and anonymized images before scoring. Training uses supervised regression (MSE loss on normalized mean human scores) or ordinal classification (cross-entropy on discretized scores), with the latter consistently yielding higher Kendall's correlation. The key differentiator is that the metric is trained on human annotations of entire-image anonymity (global scope retaining background context) rather than localized PII regions, enabling it to detect privacy-leaking cues in backgrounds, auxiliary objects, and residual details that traditional pixel-level or patch-based metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID) miss. The curated dataset comprises 4,000 anonymized images (500 each from COCO, VOC, LFW, CelebA-HQ × 4 techniques × 2 scopes) with 5,500 human annotations across both setups.

## Results

On the HA1 setup (anonymized image only, no reference), PerceptAnon achieves a Spearman's ρ of 0.8817 and Kendall's τ of 0.7119 on the full dataset, consistently outperforming all traditional image quality metrics across every train/test configuration. The best traditional metric, SSIM (inverted), reaches only ρ = −0.8358 (τ = −0.7601) on the full dataset, while LPIPS achieves ρ = 0.7675 (τ = 0.5544). Under Leave-One-Out validation, PerceptAnon maintains ρ between 0.8278 and 0.8643, demonstrating robustness to dataset distribution shifts, whereas traditional metrics drop more substantially (e.g., SSIM drops to −0.7321 on LOOV-CelebA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PerceptAnon is compared against five traditional image quality/similarity metrics: PSNR, MSE, LPIPS, SSIM, and FID. PerceptAnon outperforms all five baselines on every train/test configuration for both HA1 and HA2. On the full dataset (HA1), PerceptAnon achieves ρ = 0.8817 vs. the best baseline SSIM at ρ = −0.8358 (inverted), a gap of ~0.046 in Spearman's correlation; the gap widens to ~0.12 in Kendall's τ (0.7119 vs. 0.7601 inverted). LPIPS is the second-best traditional metric (ρ = 0.7675), trailing PerceptAnon by 0.114. Under LOOV-CelebA, where distribution shift is greatest, PerceptAnon retains ρ = 0.8478 while SSIM drops to −0.7321 and LPIPS to 0.7301, highlighting PerceptAnon's superior generalizability. In the HA2 setup (with original reference), the gap narrows slightly (PerceptAnon ρ = 0.8421 vs. SSIM −0.7655), but PerceptAnon still leads all baselines consistently.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in image anonymization research, which evaluates success via localized re-identification or detection rates on PII regions (faces, bodies). It draws conceptual inspiration from SemSim (Sun et al., 2023), which introduced human-judgment-based privacy assessment for reconstructed images, and from DreamSim (Fu et al., 2023) for learned perceptual metrics, but redirects these ideas toward a different privacy question: whether the entire anonymized image (including background, objects, and contextual cues) still leaks identity. The methodological backbone is deliberately simple—ResNet-50 CNNs and a Siamese variant—positioning the contribution as a new evaluation framework and dataset rather than an architectural advance, and it explicitly contrasts with the binary, region-scoped metrics (re-ID, detection) used in prior anonymization work (DeepPrivacy, DeepPrivacy2, CIGAN).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete GDPR compliance gap: pseudonymization of faces/bodies can leave the image identifiable through background locations, personal items, or scene context, yet no prior metric captures this. The finding that global (background-retaining) images consistently receive lower human anonymity scores than local (PII-only) images quantifies a concern that practitioners have voiced but lacked a measurement tool for. Key limitations include the small dataset (500 images per source, 6 annotators), score clustering toward the high end, and limited cultural diversity, all of which constrain generalizability. Open questions include whether PerceptAnon transfers to video, whether it can guide anonymization method design (not just evaluation), and how to handle the full spectrum of privacy-sensitive context in private settings (homes, workplaces) rather than the public scenes in COCO/VOC.
