## Problem

All existing safe RL algorithms (CPO, FOCOPS, CUP, etc.) are optimization-based, formulating the policy as a parametric function updated via gradient methods. It remains unclear whether sampling-based approaches—long considered less efficient than optimization—can produce safe policies with competitive performance. This paper investigates whether Langevin dynamics, a classical MCMC method for energy-based models, can directly infer safe actions in constrained RL.

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous environment state (dimensions ranging from 8 for Swimmer to 376 for Humanoid) and produces a continuous action (dimensions 2–17) through an iterative Langevin sampling process of T steps (typically 30–50), where each step combines a gradient of the energy function with Gaussian noise. Unlike standard actor-critic methods that output a single action from a parametric policy, LAC uses a Gaussian generator π_φ both as the practical policy for environment interaction and as the informative initialization for the short-run Langevin chain, which then refines the action toward the CPO optimal update policy.

## Method

The paper reformulates the FOCOPS optimal update policy as an energy-based model E(s,a) = (1/λ)(νQ_C(s,a) − Q_R(s,a)) − log π_k(a|s) and samples actions from it via Langevin dynamics. To make this practical, LAC addresses three challenges: (1) a parametric Gaussian generator π_φ approximates the Langevin output for fast environment interaction; (2) informative initialization (drawing the initial action from π_φ rather than a fixed distribution) with a short fixed T replaces the infeasible infinite-step MCMC; (3) clipped double Q-learning (TD3-style min for reward, max for cost) mitigates Q-value over/underestimation bias that would corrupt the energy function. The actor is trained by maximum likelihood on Langevin-generated actions (with optional KL-regularization to a target policy), while the Lagrange multiplier ν is updated via gradient descent on the dual objective, and twin critics are updated toward min/max targets computed at the Langevin-sampled next action.

## Results

On MuJoCo tasks, LAC achieves the highest reward among all constraint-satisfying methods: 1940.71 on Ant-v3 (limit 103.12, cost 103.04), 5023.27 on Humanoid-v3 (limit 20.14, cost 20.12), and 925.03 on HumanoidCircle-v0 (limit 50.00, cost 49.57). On Safety Gym tasks, LAC is the only method that consistently satisfies the cost limit of 25.00 across all four environments while achieving competitive reward (e.g., 24.01 on CarGoal, 14.20 on PointGoal). Notably, PPO-L and TRPO-L achieve higher raw reward on Swimmer-v3 (49.07, 47.07 vs. LAC's 34.56) but violate the cost limit by ~3x (69.65, 70.22 vs. limit 24.52), making them infeasible.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAC outperforms FOCOPS (the closest optimization-based competitor) on all four MuJoCo tasks while satisfying constraints: +7.6% reward on Ant-v3 (1940.71 vs. 1803.86), +57.2% on Humanoid-v3 (5023.27 vs. 3196.58), and +7.2% on HumanoidCircle-v0 (925.03 vs. 862.92). CPO and CUP are consistently dominated by LAC in reward while also being less reliable on constraint satisfaction (CPO violates the Ant-v3 limit). PPO-L and TRPO-L (Lagrangian baselines) achieve high reward on Swimmer-v3 but violate the cost limit by ~3x, and collapse to near-zero reward on HumanoidCircle-v0. On Safety Gym tasks, LAC is the only method that satisfies the cost limit of 25.00 on all four environments; CPO, PCPO, and TRPO-L all exceed the limit by 2–6x, while P3O and FOCOPS remain feasible but achieve substantially lower reward (e.g., 0.94 vs. LAC's 14.20 on PointGoal). RESPO is competitive on CarGoal (15.23 reward, cost 21.44) but underperforms LAC (24.01, cost 22.15) and is less consistent across tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on FOCOPS (Zhang et al., 2020), which showed that the optimal CPO update policy has an energy-based model (EBM) form, and on classical Langevin MCMC as a solver for EBMs. The key departure from the safe RL literature is replacing the standard optimization-based policy update (CPO, FOCOPS, CUP, P3O) with a sampling-based approach: instead of projecting the EBM-form optimal policy back into a parametric family via KL minimization, LAC uses Langevin dynamics to sample actions and a learned generator to approximate the resulting distribution. The clipped double Q-learning from TD3 is incorporated to address Q-value overestimation that would otherwise corrupt the energy function used by Langevin policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that sampling-based methods can be competitive with optimization-based methods for safe RL, challenging the prevailing assumption that Monte Carlo sampling is inherently inferior for policy learning. The exploitation-exploration trade-off is handled elegantly: Langevin noise provides exploration while the generator accumulates and reproduces high-quality transitions for exploitation. However, the Gaussian policy constraint limits expressiveness, the generator introduces approximation error, and experiments are confined to relatively low-dimensional MuJoCo and Safety Gym tasks. Open questions include whether more expressive generators (e.g., diffusion models) could replace the Gaussian policy, and whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional or more complex constrained environments.
