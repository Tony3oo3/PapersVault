## Problem

Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) trained with image captioning or image-text retrieval objectives perform only 'modality translation' between vision and language, lacking sufficient cross-modal interaction to handle complex scenarios like composed image retrieval where the model must synthesize information from both an image and a text condition. This deficiency results in subpar performance on benchmarks requiring deep multimodal context understanding, as the LLM cannot effectively compose and selectively extract information from joint visual-linguistic inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241109/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a reference image (encoded by frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 into visual features, then linearly mapped to 4 visual vectors in LLM embedding space) combined with a text condition, multiple image-text pairs, or a dialogue transcript as the multimodal context. For retrieval tasks, the output is a visual representation projected into CLIP feature space via 5 learnable [RET] tokens; for VQA, the output is a generated text answer. The LLM backbone (OPT-2.7B, OPT-6.7B, or Llama2-7B) and CLIP encoder remain fully frozen throughout training.

## Method

MCL introduces two composition-aware training objectives—Multimodal-Context Captioning (MC-Cap) and Multimodal-Context Retrieval (MC-Ret)—that require the LLM to synthesize visual and textual inputs rather than merely translate between modalities, trained on a 2.7M-tuple MMC dataset auto-generated by prompting Llama2 to produce text conditions and target captions from web image-caption pairs. A key architectural contribution is the stacking retrieval mechanism: multiple [RET] tokens are appended after the context in a stacking (independent) order rather than sequential order, with extra attention masks inserted between RET tokens so each token extracts diverse information from the multimodal context without being biased by preceding RET tokens. The combined loss jointly optimizes both naive captioning/retrieval objectives and the proposed composition objectives, with only the linear visual-to-LLM mapping layer and the LLM-to-CLIP projection layer being trained while all LLM and CLIP parameters stay frozen.

## Results

On zero-shot composed image retrieval, MCL with Llama2-7B achieves 17.67 mAP@5 on CIRCO, 26.22 R@1 on CIRR, and 16.3 R@1(avg) on GeneCIS, substantially outperforming the strongest prior LLM-based method FROMAGE (4.00 mAP@5 on CIRCO, 10.96 R@1 on CIRR). On VQAv2, MCL (Llama2-7B) reaches 42.6% zero-shot accuracy, surpassing FROMAGE (36.8%) and GILL (38.8%). On dense multimodal context tasks, MCL (Llama2-7B) achieves 23.1 R@1 on Visual Storytelling (5 captions + 4 images) and 29.8 R@1 on Visual Dialog, both significantly ahead of FROMAGE (18.2 and 20.8 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCL (Llama2-7B) decisively outperforms FROMAGE (Koh et al., 2023b) across all three CIR benchmarks, achieving 17.67 vs. 4.00 mAP@5 on CIRCO and 26.22 vs. 10.96 R@1 on CIRR, demonstrating that multimodal composition learning far exceeds modality-translation training for context understanding. Against CLIP-based zero-shot CIR methods, MCL surpasses SEARLE (11.68 mAP@5) and Pic2Word (8.72 mAP@5) on CIRCO by 5+ and 9+ points respectively, and outperforms CompoDiff (12.55 mAP@5) by 5.1 points. On CIRR, MCL (26.22 R@1) edges out SEARLE (24.22) and Pic2Word (23.90), while on GeneCIS it leads all baselines with 16.3 R@1(avg) vs. CompoDiff's 14.9 and FROMAGE's 14.3. The Combiner-MMC baseline (trained on the same MMC dataset but without an LLM) reaches 13.22 mAP@5, confirming that the LLM's compositional reasoning is critical beyond what a simple feature combiner can achieve. On VQAv2, MCL (Llama2-7B) achieves 42.6% accuracy, beating GILL (38.8%) and FROMAGE (36.8%), and on Visual Dialog retrieval it reaches 29.8 R@1 versus FROMAGE's 20.8, a 9-point margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on FROMAGE (Koh et al., 2023b), which established the architecture of a frozen LLM with a linear visual-to-text mapping layer and learnable [RET] tokens for visual output, trained via image captioning and image-text retrieval objectives. The key departure is the argument that these 'modality translation' objectives are insufficient for complex multimodal composition, and the introduction of two new training objectives (MC-Cap and MC-Ret) that require the LLM to synthesize information from both visual and textual inputs simultaneously. The paper also introduces the MMC dataset (2.7M tuples) generated by prompting Llama2 to augment web-collected image-caption pairs, and a stacking retrieval mechanism that decouples multiple [RET] tokens from sequential attention dependencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the training objective (composition learning vs. modality translation) matters more than LLM scale for multimodal context understanding, with a 2.7B model trained via MCL outperforming a 6.7B model trained via FROMAGE by a large margin on CIRCO. The MMC dataset and MCL training recipe provide a practical, low-parameter training pipeline (only a linear mapping layer and [RET] tokens are trained) that could be adopted by the broader MLLM community. Limitations include inheriting CLIP's known weaknesses (object counting, greyscale, shot angle) and the reliance on LLM-generated data whose quality ceiling is bounded by the generating model. Open questions include whether composition learning generalizes to additional modalities (video, audio) and whether the stacking retrieval mechanism scales to longer contexts.
