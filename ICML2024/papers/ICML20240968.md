## Problem

Existing clustered federated learning (CFL) algorithms struggle to achieve accurate client clustering under privacy-preserving protocols where raw local datasets are never shared, particularly when batch sizes are small, client counts are low, or distribution gaps are narrow. The paper asks what statistically well-founded features can reliably distinguish client groups during federated training and how clustering should be performed to guarantee both correct grouping and optimal model convergence without favorable initialization.

## Input / Output

The central unit receives per-client minibatch gradients (not raw data) computed on K models broadcast in round-robin fashion, accumulating them into per-client feature vectors in R^{Kd} via a cumulative moving average. The algorithm outputs K trained cluster-specific models and a final client-to-cluster assignment, with the clustering updated every P iterations up to a threshold T_cl after which only model updates continue. Gradients can be compressed by over 100× for scalability to models with >10^6 parameters without sacrificing clustering quality.

## Method

CFL-GP exploits the intuition that clients sharing a data distribution produce similar gradient updates, using accumulated gradient vectors across K models as clustering features. Every P iterations, one model is broadcast to all clients in round-robin; each client's gradient on that model is incorporated into its feature vector via a cumulative average, and spectral clustering (SVD-based dimensionality reduction to K dimensions followed by K-means) re-partitions clients. A cluster-model index matching step assigns each discovered group to a model by maximizing continuity with the previous assignment. Theoretically, CFL-GP is the first CFL algorithm to prove simultaneous convergence of both client clustering (monotonically decreasing misclustering rate O(1/δ²t)) and model parameters (contractive property with asymptotically optimal O(1/√(bC)) error rate) under only mild assumptions—Gaussian stochastic gradient noise, bounded model norms, and a minimum average gradient gap—without requiring good initialization or large inter-cluster gaps.

## Results

CFL-GP achieves perfect clustering (ARI = 1.00) across all three large-scale benchmarks (COST2100, CIFAR-10/ResNet-18, EMNIST/CNN), while baselines frequently fail to cluster correctly. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 (C=20), CFL-GP reaches 73.93% accuracy vs. 68.42% for MADMO and 70.75% for FedAvg; on EMNIST (C=10), it achieves 82.28% vs. 78.68% (IFCA) and 76.73% (MADMO). On the COST2100 CSI compression task, CFL-GP attains -19.38 dB NMSE (C=16), substantially outperforming IFCA (-15.83 dB) and FedAvg (-14.57 dB). The paper also reports that CFL-GP converges to optimal clustering up to 100× faster than existing methods, reducing both communication and computation costs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IFCA (Ghosh et al., 2020), CFL-GP matches or slightly exceeds accuracy on CIFAR-10 (73.93% vs. 73.97% at C=20, essentially tied) but decisively wins on EMNIST (82.28% vs. 78.68% at C=10) and COST2100 (-19.38 dB vs. -15.83 dB), while maintaining ARI=1.00 consistently where IFCA drops to 0.71 at C=10 on EMNIST and -0.01 on COST2100. Against MADMO (Sattler et al., 2020), CFL-GP dominates across all benchmarks: on CIFAR-10 at C=80, CFL-GP achieves 70.49% vs. MADMO's 52.53% (a 18-point gap), and on EMNIST at C=10, 82.28% vs. 76.73%. Against FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017), CFL-GP outperforms by 3.2–12.2 points in accuracy depending on the dataset and client count, while FedAvg always yields ARI=0.00 (no clustering). The paper further shows on synthetic linear regression that CFL-GP maintains ARI > 0.8 even at a very small distribution gap (Δφ=5°) where both IFCA and MADMO degrade to random clustering (ARI≈0), and on Rotated MNIST CFL-GP achieves ARI=1.0 across all batch sizes and client counts while baselines fluctuate significantly.

## Relation to Prior Work

CFL-GP extends the clustered federated learning framework established by IFCA (Ghosh et al., 2020), MADMO (Sattler et al., 2020), and PACFL (Vahidian et al., 2023), which cluster clients based on loss values, gradient differences on a converged model, or principal angles between data subspaces, respectively. The key conceptual departure is using accumulated gradient features across multiple models as clustering signals, combined with spectral clustering (building on Löffler et al., 2021's optimality results for Gaussian mixtures) to handle high-dimensional noisy gradients. Unlike prior CFL methods that require good initialization or large inter-cluster gaps, CFL-GP leverages the Gaussianity of stochastic gradient noise in high dimensions to provide convergence guarantees from arbitrary starting points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CFL-GP is the first CFL algorithm with provable guarantees for both optimal client clustering and model convergence without initialization assumptions, addressing a fundamental limitation of prior approaches that either require near-optimal starting models or partially correct initial clusterings. The up-to-100x faster clustering convergence and robustness to small distribution gaps (where baselines degrade to random clustering) have practical implications for communication-efficient deployment on resource-constrained devices. Open questions include the validity of the Gaussian SGN assumption in later training phases (where Panigrahi et al. show non-Gaussianity emerges), handling unknown cluster counts in practice, and whether the minimum gradient gap assumption holds for highly similar distributions in real-world settings.
