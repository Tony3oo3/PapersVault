## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods fail to scale to large high-resolution datasets (ImageNet-1K, ImageNet-21K) and large architectures due to high GPU memory demands, inability to generate informative synthetic sets at scale, and difficulty adapting to complex models. Prior approaches that use distributional similarity between distilled and full datasets lack theoretical justification for why such matching should work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241330/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a full training dataset (e.g., 1.28M images at 224×224 for ImageNet-1K), M=5 pre-trained ResNet-18 teacher models, and a target images-per-class (IPC) count. It produces distilled synthetic images plus soft labels with stored augmentation parameters (crop coordinates, flips) for E downstream training epochs, where the label storage cost scales as E×|S|×C and can rival the image storage itself for large class counts. Precomputed per-layer mean and covariance statistics of the full dataset are cached once via a single forward pass, eliminating the need to iterate over the full training set during synthesis.

## Method

D3S reframes dataset distillation as a domain shift problem (distilled set = source, full set = target), deriving a universal upper bound on distillation loss via KL divergence between joint image-label distributions (Theorem 3.1). The bound decomposes into an image-distribution term D_KL(p_T(x)||p_S(x)) and a conditional-label term D_KL(p_T(y|x)||p_S(y|x)). The image term is optimized by approximating intermediate network representations as multivariate Gaussians and minimizing the closed-form KL divergence between precomputed full-dataset statistics and running distilled-batch statistics, using Incremental Statistic Updating (ISU) to handle memory constraints. The label term is optimized via ensemble knowledge distillation (ENL) with M teacher models. Ensemble Synthesis (ENS) rotates which teacher model guides each batch's optimization, and a cross-entropy label alignment term (α=20) is added to the loss.

## Results

D3S achieves state-of-the-art distillation accuracy across all three large-scale benchmarks. On ImageNet-1K at 50 IPC, D3S reaches 60.2% top-1 accuracy with ResNet-18 (vs. 46.8% for SRe²L and 53.5% for CDA), and 65.8% with ResNet-50. At the most challenging 10 IPC setting on ImageNet-1K, D3S achieves 39.1% (ResNet-18), surpassing SRe²L by 17.8 percentage points. On ImageNet-21K at 20 IPC, D3S reaches 28.5% (ResNet-18) and 36.0% (ResNet-101), outperforming CDA by 2.1 and 0.1 points respectively, with the margin narrowing on this most complex dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D3S outperforms both baselines—SRe²L (Yin et al., 2023) and CDA (Yin & Shen, 2023)—on every benchmark and every IPC setting tested. The largest margin is on ImageNet-1K at 10 IPC, where D3S achieves 39.1% (ResNet-18) versus 21.3% for SRe²L, a 17.8-point gain. On ImageNet-1K at 50 IPC, D3S leads SRe²L by 13.4 points (60.2% vs. 46.8%) and CDA by 6.7 points (60.2% vs. 53.5%) on ResNet-18, with similar or larger gaps on ResNet-50 and ResNet-101. On Tiny-ImageNet at 50 IPC, D3S reaches 56.4%, exceeding CDA (48.7%) by 7.7 points and SRe²L (41.4%) by 15.0 points. On ImageNet-21K, the advantage narrows (e.g., 28.5% vs. 26.4% for CDA at 20 IPC on ResNet-18), which the authors attribute to the multivariate Gaussian approximation being less effective for this more complex 10,450-class dataset. Cross-architecture generalization (Table 2) confirms D3S's gains transfer to DenseNet-121, RegNet-Y-8GF, ConvNeXt-Tiny, and DeiT-Tiny, with the largest relative improvement on DeiT-Tiny (36.9% vs. 32.0% for CDA at ImageNet-1K 50 IPC).

## Relation to Prior Work

D3S reframes dataset distillation as a domain shift problem, drawing on the generalization bounds from domain adaptation theory (Ben-David et al., 2010; Nguyen et al., 2021a) to derive a universal upper bound on distillation loss in terms of KL-divergence between the distilled and full dataset distributions. This provides the theoretical justification that prior large-scale distillation methods (SRe2L, CDA) relied on heuristically through BatchNorm-statistic matching. The key methodological departure is replacing the diagonal-covariance BatchNorm loss with a full multivariate KL-divergence over intermediate network representations, combined with Incremental Statistic Updating (ISU) for memory efficiency and ensemble synthesis/labelling inspired by the multi-view theory of Allen-Zhu & Li (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the domain-shift bound is model-agnostic, which explains and enables strong cross-architecture generalization (ResNet, DenseNet, RegNet, ConvNeXt, DeiT) — a key desideratum for dataset distillation. A practically important finding is the extreme sensitivity of distillation performance to validation-time training duration (e.g., 39.1% → 50.1% on ImageNet-1K 10 IPC going from 300 to 900 epochs), motivating standardization in the field. The multivariate normal approximation appears to be a limiting factor on the most complex dataset (ImageNet-21K shows smaller margins), suggesting richer distributional approximations are needed. Open questions include whether the bound can be optimized without the Gaussian approximation and whether the approach extends to non-vision domains.
