## Problem

Decoder-only Transformers with causal masking and relative position encoding (RPE) suffer from two structural limitations: (1) the softmax constraint forces all attention scores to be non-zero and sum to 1, compelling the model to allocate disproportional attention to irrelevant tokens (especially initial tokens and punctuation), and (2) because softmax always produces a right stochastic matrix, RPE-based models cannot encode absolute positional information, limiting them on position-critical tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard token sequence as input and produces next-token predictions, identical in interface to any decoder-only Transformer. The key operational detail is that StableMask is a parameter-free modification to the causal mask itself: during training, the upper triangular portion of the attention score matrix is filled with positionally-decaying pseudo-attention values before softmax and zeroed out after; during inference, a single suffix token is appended to the sequence to preserve KV-caching compatibility without recomputing prior attention rows.

## Method

StableMask replaces the standard causal mask with a two-stage operation: before softmax, the upper triangular entries of the attention score matrix are set to linearly decaying pseudo-attention values p_ij = −(j−1)γ (where γ is a fixed decay-rate hyperparameter, default 0.5), and after softmax, those same entries are zeroed out to preserve causality. Because each row contains n−i pseudo-attention scores (more at the sequence start, fewer at the end), excess attention is absorbed positionally rather than dumped onto specific tokens, and the row sum of real attention probabilities becomes strictly less than 1, breaking the right-stochastic property and enabling absolute position encoding. The paper proves that the first layer of a StableMask Transformer can recover absolute positions [1,…,n] in its hidden state using only two embedding dimensions and one attention head. A FlashAttention-compatible tiling implementation is provided, adding only two fused on-chip element-wise operations (multiply by C, add P) per block, and an inference variant appends a suffix token with a fixed factor τ = ln(Σ e^{−iγ}) to maintain KV-cache reuse.

## Results

On WikiText-103, StableMask consistently reduces perplexity across all model sizes: e.g., OpenLLaMA 160M improves from 22.5 to 21.1 PPL, and BLOOM 160M from 27.6 to 26.1. On MiniPile (2 epochs), OpenLLaMA 430M drops from 15.7 to 15.1 PPL. At the 1.4B scale on The Pile (25B tokens), OpenLLaMA-SM achieves 10.4 PPL vs. 10.7 for the baseline, and gains on downstream tasks (e.g., Winogrande 54.1 vs. 53.5, OpenbookQA 32.6 vs. 31.4). In the AT-method ablation (Table 3), StableMask (PPL 21.1) outperforms both Learnable AT (21.6) and Fixed Value AT (22.4) on OpenLLaMA 160M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is StableMask vs. the standard causal-mask Transformer (no modification), where StableMask wins on every configuration tested (71M–1.4B, ALiBi/RoPE, WikiText-103/MiniPile/The Pile). Against AT-based methods inspired by StreamingLLM (Xiao et al., 2023) and ViT Register (Darcet et al., 2023), StableMask achieves 21.1 PPL on OpenLLaMA 160M/MiniPile, beating Learnable AT (21.6) by 0.5 PPL and Fixed Value AT (22.4) by 1.3 PPL. The paper attributes this gap to StableMask's position-adaptive pseudo-attention scores (more pseudo values at the start of the sequence where DA is worst), whereas AT methods add a fixed number of tokens regardless of position. On downstream tasks (Table 2), StableMask shows consistent but modest gains: +0.2 on LAMBADA, +0.3 on ARC-Easy, +1.2 on OpenbookQA, and +0.6 on Winogrande, while PIQA and ARC-Challenge remain tied. The method also enables stable window-attention extrapolation without the perplexity explosion seen in the baseline, a capability neither AT-based method provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

StableMask sits at the intersection of two lines of work: (1) methods addressing disproportional attention in decoder-only Transformers, including StreamingLLM's attention sinks (Xiao et al., 2023), ViT Register tokens (Darcet et al., 2023), and gated/trimmed softmax (Bondarenko et al., 2023), and (2) attempts to recover absolute positional information lost by RPE, notably URPE's learnable Toeplitz matrix (Luo et al., 2022). The key departure is that StableMask unifies both solutions into a single parameter-free modification of the causal mask itself—introducing positionally-decaying pseudo-attention scores in the upper triangular region—rather than adding learnable parameters, artificial tokens, or post-softmax corrections. It is also explicitly designed to be compatible with FlashAttention's tiling paradigm and KV-caching inference, which prior AT-based and URPE-style methods do not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter-free, mask-only nature of StableMask makes it trivially integrable into existing LLM training pipelines and hardware-optimized attention kernels, lowering the adoption barrier compared to methods requiring new tokens or learnable matrices. The demonstrated ability to perform stable windowed extrapolation without retaining initial tokens (eliminating the need for StreamingLLM-style tricks) is practically valuable for long-context inference. However, empirical gains are modest (roughly 0.5–1.5 PPL points across 71M–1.4B models), the largest tested scale is 1.4B parameters, and the interaction between StableMask's absolute-position encoding and very long sequences or other extrapolation techniques (e.g., YaRN, NTK-aware scaling) remains unexplored. The hyperparameter γ, while shown to be relatively insensitive in a narrow range, has not been studied across architectures or training regimes beyond the reported settings.
