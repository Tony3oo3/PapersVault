## Problem

Existing contextual bandit with knapsacks (CBwK) algorithms achieve sublinear regret only with polynomial dependence on the feature dimension d (e.g., O(d·√T) or O(√d·T)), rendering them infeasible when covariates are high-dimensional. The paper asks whether the inherent sparsity of the reward and cost weight vectors can be exploited to reduce this dependence to logarithmic in d while maintaining a fully online, single-pass estimation procedure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241384/cropped_page7_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At each period t the algorithm receives a d-dimensional feature vector x_t (with ‖x_t‖_∞ ≤ D) and must select one of K arms, observing the realized reward r_t and resource consumption b(a_t, x_t) ∈ R^m_≥0. The algorithm maintains only an O(d²) empirical covariance matrix and O(d) parameter vectors per arm (rather than the O(d·T) history required by LASSO-based methods), and outputs at each step a sparse s₀-sparse estimate μ_a^s for the reward weights, a sparse estimate Ŵ_a for the cost weights, and a dual variable η_t ∈ Δ^m that governs the arm-selection rule via a Lagrangian penalty on estimated resource cost.

## Method

The core contribution is the Online HT algorithm, which performs sparse recovery via stochastic gradient descent with hard thresholding at a slightly inflated sparsity level s (where ρ = s₀/s = 1/(9κ⁴)) and, crucially, gradient averaging over all past observations to counteract the information loss caused by the non-smooth ℓ₀ projection. This yields a statistically optimal estimation rate O(s₀σ² log d / (φ_min²(s)·t)) with only O(d²) per-iteration cost, compared to O(d³ + d²t) for LASSO. The estimator is embedded in a primal-dual framework: the dual variable η_t is updated by online mirror descent (Hedge) on the per-period resource surplus, while the primal arm is chosen by ε-greedy exploration over the estimated Lagrangian reward (μ_a^s)ᵀx_t − Z·x_tᵀŴ_a η_t. Under a diverse covariate condition the ε-greedy exploration can be dropped entirely, sharpening the regret from Õ((V^UB/C_min)^{1/3} T^{2/3}) to Õ((V^UB/C_min)√T + s₀√T), with only logarithmic dependence on d throughout.

## Results

The paper establishes a regret bound of $\widetilde{O}\left(\frac{V^{\mathrm{UB}}}{C_{\min}}\sqrt{T}+\left(\frac{V^{\mathrm{UB}}}{C_{\min}}\right)^{1/3}T^{2/3}\right)$ for the high-dimensional CBwK problem, with only logarithmic dependence on dimension $d$ (improving over the polynomial $O(d\sqrt{T})$ or $O(\sqrt{d}\cdot T)$ bounds in prior work). Under a diverse covariate condition, the regret improves to $\widetilde{O}\left(\frac{V^{\mathrm{UB}}}{C_{\min}}\sqrt{T}\right)$. For the sparse bandit problem, the algorithm achieves the optimal $\widetilde{O}(s_0^{2/3}T^{2/3})$ regret in the data-poor regime and $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{s_0 T})$ in the data-rich regime. Computationally, Online HT requires $O(d^2)$ per iteration and $O(d^2 T)$ total, versus $O(d^3 + d^2 t)$ per iteration and $O(d^3 T + d^2 T^2)$ total for LASSO-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LASSO-based sparse recovery (Kim & Paik, 2019; Hao et al., 2020), Online HT achieves comparable or better estimation error while reducing per-iteration complexity from $O(d^3 + d^2 t)$ to $O(d^2)$, and total complexity from $O(d^3 T + d^2 T^2)$ to $O(d^2 T)$; it also provides guaranteed exact $s_0$-sparse estimation without parameter tuning (Figure 1). Against the LASSO Explore-Then-Commit bandit algorithm (Hao et al., 2020; Li et al., 2022; Jang et al., 2022), the $\epsilon$-greedy Online HT method shows slower regret growth, and the greedy variant ($\epsilon_t = 0$) exhibits the fastest regret convergence (Figure 2). Against the UCB-based linCBwK algorithm (Agrawal & Devanur, 2016), Online HT outperforms in the data-poor regime (small $T$) where high dimensionality causes linCBwK to fail at controlling cumulative regret, while the two methods become comparable as $T$ grows large (Figure 3). The key theoretical advantage over all baselines is the logarithmic (rather than polynomial) dependence on dimension $d$ in the regret bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the primal-dual framework for bandits with knapsacks (Badanidiyuru et al., 2013; Agrawal & Devanur, 2016) to the high-dimensional sparse setting, where prior methods incur polynomial regret dependence on dimension d. The key methodological departure is replacing LASSO-based offline sparse estimation (Hao et al., 2020; Kim & Paik, 2019; Ren & Zhou, 2023) with a novel Online Hard Thresholding algorithm that uses gradient averaging to overcome the poor smoothness of the ℓ₀-projection, achieving optimal statistical rates in a fully online fashion. The paper also draws on iterative hard thresholding from compressed sensing (Blumensath & Davies, 2009) and the diverse covariate condition from recent sparse bandit work (Ren & Zhou, 2023) to further sharpen regret bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The logarithmic (rather than polynomial) dependence on feature dimension makes high-dimensional online allocation practically feasible in settings like personalized advertising and dynamic pricing where covariates can have thousands of dimensions. The Online HT algorithm also provides a general-purpose online sparse estimator with O(d²T) computational cost versus O(d³T + d²T²) for LASSO re-solving, which is a meaningful efficiency gain. Open questions include whether the diverse covariate condition (needed for the improved √T regret) is too restrictive in practice, whether the approach extends to non-linear reward models, and whether the gradient-averaging trick generalizes to other non-smooth projection operators in online learning.
