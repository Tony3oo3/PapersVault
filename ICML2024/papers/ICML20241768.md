## Problem

Modern deep IRL algorithms (e.g., GAIL) recover reward functions that fail to support effective retraining from scratch, even in the same environment where expert demonstrations were collected, and are often poorly shaped, requiring excessive environment interaction to optimize. Classical dual IRL methods guarantee retrainability by fully solving the RL problem at each iteration but are interaction-prohibitive at scale, while modern primal methods are efficient but lose this guarantee. The paper asks how to preserve the interaction efficiency of modern IRL while recovering rewards that are both effective and efficient to retrain on, including in novel target environments.

## Input / Output

The method takes 100 expert trajectories (generated by a PPO policy trained over 5×10⁷ timesteps) and access to the target environment's dynamics (MuJoCo continuous control: Hopper, Walker, Ant, Humanoid). The discriminator is trained on states only rather than state-action pairs, enabling imitation without action labels. The output is a shaped reward function — the IRL-recovered reward plus an evolved potential-based shaping term Φ: S → R (a 2-layer, 128-unit tanh network) — which can then be optimized by any downstream RL algorithm to produce a policy in the target environment.

## Method

EvIL is a two-stage procedure. Stage 1 (IRL++) modifies GAIL with three components: a replay buffer of all past learner trajectories for stable discriminator training, ensembles of 5 discriminators and 5 policies to broaden state-space coverage, and random policy resets with linearly decreasing probability to prevent premature convergence — together ensuring the recovered reward admits effective retraining. Stage 2 decouples shaping from discrimination by using OpenAI-ES (a zeroth-order population-based optimizer) to directly evolve the potential function Φ that maximizes the area under the RL training curve when retraining on the recovered reward, rather than attempting to approximate the optimal value function V*. This bypasses the fundamental limitation that the IRL game objective is invariant to potential-based shaping terms, so the discriminator cannot learn well-shaped rewards. The ES fitness is the AUC of performance vs. interactions over a fixed number of inner-loop PPO updates, and the shaping term is applied only during the early portion of retraining (first 20–50% of training) to reduce the effective planning horizon where it matters most.

## Results

On retraining with the recovered IRL reward, classic GAIL fails catastrophically: in Ant it yields negative reward, while in Hopper, Walker, and Humanoid the policy plateaus at sub-optimal performance. IRL++ (ensembles + buffer + resets) fixes this, enabling retraining that approaches expert performance. EvIL (IRL++ + ES-evolved shaping) further improves interaction efficiency, matching or exceeding expert performance on Hopper and Humanoid and achieving scores thousands higher than the IRL baseline on Ant. In the appendix Table 1, EvIL with 5 agents achieves a retraining score of 0.6696 (±0.0176) versus 0.1424 (±0.0153) for vanilla IRL — a ~4.7× improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Behavioural Cloning (BC, Pomerleau 1988), EvIL wins on both transfer tasks: on the trembling-hand environment (5% random action injection) and the more challenging randomised-dynamics environment (randomised link lengths and joint ranges), EvIL consistently outperforms BC in both final performance and interaction efficiency. BC shows a notable performance decline on the randomised-dynamics task across all three MuJoCo environments, highlighting its poor generalisation. Against classic GAIL (Ho & Ermon 2016), EvIL wins decisively: the GAIL-recovered reward fails to produce a viable policy when retrained from scratch (negative reward in Ant, sub-optimal plateaus elsewhere), whereas EvIL matches or exceeds expert performance on Hopper and Humanoid and achieves scores thousands higher on Ant. The V_E shaping baseline (using the expert's critic as a potential function) is inconsistent — it hinders training in Hopper, Walker, and Humanoid but helps in Ant — while EvIL's evolved shaping term provides uniform gains across all environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GAIL framework (Ho & Ermon, 2016) for primal IRL and the potential-based reward shaping theory of Ng et al. (1999), identifying a practical gap between the two: modern primal IRL is interaction-efficient but recovers rewards that fail to support effective retraining, while classical dual IRL guarantees retrainability at the cost of excessive interaction. The IRL++ component (ensembles, policy resets, replay buffer) draws on no-regret learning theory (FTRL vs. OGD) and pessimism techniques from offline RL, while the ES-based shaping term repurposes the zeroth-order optimisation machinery from the JAX-accelerated evolution strategies literature (Lu et al., 2022a; Salimans et al., 2017) to a new objective: directly maximising the AUC of the RL training curve rather than approximating V*. This departs from prior reward shaping work that attempts to learn a value function heuristic, instead treating shaping as a bi-level optimisation problem amenable to population-based search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in sim-to-real IRL pipelines: recovered rewards are often poorly shaped, making downstream retraining in the target environment prohibitively expensive. The finding that GAIL-style rewards fail to support effective retraining even in the source environment challenges the implicit assumption that solving the IRL game guarantees deployable reward functions. Limitations include reliance on an accurate simulator for the ES optimisation phase (300 generations × 64 population), evaluation restricted to MuJoCo continuous control with 100 expert trajectories, and an unaddressed sim2real gap for the learned shaping term itself. Open questions include whether ES-based shaping transfers to learned dynamics models, scales to higher-dimensional or discrete action spaces, and whether the 300-generation budget can be reduced without sacrificing shaping quality.
