## Problem

ImageNet accuracy has served as the de facto metric for model selection, but it fails to capture critical behavioral differences between models of comparable accuracy. Practitioners choosing between ConvNet and Transformer architectures, or between supervised and CLIP training paradigms, lack a systematic understanding of how these choices affect robustness, calibration, transferability, and other properties essential for real-world deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242025/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

All four models consume standard 224×224 RGB images and produce class labels over 1000 ImageNet categories, per-class confidence scores, and frozen feature representations for linear probing. The analysis additionally evaluates model behavior under controlled perturbations: scale factors (1×–3×), spatial shifts (0–100 px), resolution changes (112–640 px), cue-conflict composites, and synthetic photorealistic images with systematically varied factors (pose, size, texture, etc.).

## Method

The paper conducts a controlled empirical comparison of four pretrained models—ConvNeXt-Base and ViT-B/16 (DeiT3), each under supervised (ImageNet-21K pretrain + ImageNet-1K finetune) and CLIP (LAION-400M) training—matched in parameter count (~87–89M) and FLOPs (~15–18G) within each paradigm. Rather than proposing a new architecture or training procedure, the contribution is a multi-dimensional behavioral analysis spanning seven axes: factor-specific error ratios (ImageNet-X), shape/texture bias on cue-conflict images, calibration via ECE on in-distribution and out-of-distribution sets, robustness across seven ImageNet variants, transferability via VTAB linear probing, synthetic-data accuracy (PUG-ImageNet), and invariance to scale, shift, and resolution transformations. The key design choice is evaluating all properties without any additional finetuning, reflecting the practical scenario of using pretrained models directly. Appendix results extend the analysis to Tiny through Huge model sizes to disentangle the effects of scale from architecture and training paradigm.

## Results

All four models achieve similar ImageNet-1K validation accuracy within their training paradigm: 85.5% for both supervised ViT (DeiT3-Base/16) and supervised ConvNeXt-Base, and 67.0%/66.3% for CLIP ViT/ConvNeXt (zero-shot). On VTAB transferability (linear probing), CLIP models lead with 72.2% overall, supervised ConvNeXt follows at 71.0%, and supervised ViT trails at 67.8%. On ImageNet-X, CLIP models show substantially lower error ratios than supervised models across most factors, while supervised models achieve lower ECE (better calibration) on both ImageNet-1K and ImageNet-R. ConvNeXt outperforms ViT on PUG-ImageNet synthetic data (32.2% vs 29.4% for base supervised) and on transformation invariance, while ViT exhibits higher shape bias (39.8% vs 33.3% for base supervised).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four models of matched size (~87–89M params, ~15–17.5G FLOPs) across seven property dimensions. **Supervised ConvNeXt vs. supervised ViT (DeiT3):** ConvNeXt wins on VTAB transferability (71.0% vs 67.8%), calibration (lower ECE on both ImageNet-1K and ImageNet-R), transformation invariance (scale, shift, resolution), and PUG-ImageNet synthetic data (32.2% vs 29.4%); ViT wins on shape bias (39.8% vs 33.3%). **CLIP ViT vs. CLIP ConvNeXt:** The two are nearly tied on VTAB (both 72.2%) and most ImageNet-X factors, with ConvNeXt slightly ahead on synthetic data and calibration, and ViT slightly ahead on a few ImageNet-X factors (multiple objects, style). **Supervised vs. CLIP (cross-paradigm):** CLIP models achieve lower ImageNet-X error ratios (fewer relative mistakes), higher shape bias (+7–12%), and better VTAB transferability, while supervised models achieve lower ECE (better calibration) and higher accuracy on most ImageNet robustness variants (V2, A, C, Real, Hard), likely because those benchmarks share ImageNet-1K classes. The authors conclude that supervised ConvNeXt is the best default when the target distribution is close to ImageNet, while CLIP models are preferable under significant domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the architecture-comparison literature (Naseer et al. 2021, Minderer et al. 2021, Zhou et al. 2021) that typically pitted ViT against older ConvNets like ResNet, by substituting ConvNeXt for a more balanced architectural comparison and adding CLIP as a second training paradigm alongside supervised learning. It repurposes existing evaluation protocols—ImageNet-X error analysis, cue-conflict shape/texture bias, ECE calibration, VTAB linear probing, and ImageNet robustness variants—into a unified multi-property framework applied under matched accuracy and compute budgets. The work departs from prior single-property studies (e.g., Minderer et al. on calibration, Geirhos et al. on shape bias) by simultaneously profiling all properties for each model, and from CLIP-focused analyses (Walmer et al. 2023) by including a modern ConvNet backbone rather than only ViT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a concrete, multi-dimensional decision framework for model selection that goes beyond the saturated ImageNet-1K accuracy metric, with actionable recommendations (supervised ConvNeXt for in-distribution tasks, CLIP for domain shift). A key open question is whether the findings generalize to larger models and more diverse pretraining data (e.g., LAION-2B, DataComp), since the main analysis is limited to base-sized models on LAION-400M. The authors also highlight the need for new robustness and evaluation benchmarks decoupled from ImageNet's class set, as current robustness datasets share ImageNet's taxonomy and may overstate supervised models' advantage. Limitations include the inability to control for pretraining data volume and the exclusion of self-supervised models from the main comparison.
