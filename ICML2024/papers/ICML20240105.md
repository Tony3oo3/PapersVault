## Problem

In online advertising, budget pacing and ROS (return-on-spend) pacing are typically managed by separate teams and systems, yet the fully-coupled dual-optimal algorithm that jointly optimizes both constraints is impractical to deploy, while the natural sequential composition of the two services provably suffers Ω(T) constraint violations or regret. The paper asks whether a minimally-coupled architecture can retain the O(√T) guarantees of the fully-coupled system while preserving the operational independence of the two pacing services.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives per-auction values v_t (computed as tcpa × pconv), a total budget B = ρT, and auction feedback (allocation x_t(b_t), payment p_t(b_t)) drawn i.i.d. from an unknown distribution. It outputs a uniform bid multiplier k_t and bid b_t = k_t · v_t, with the min-pacing variant computing b_t^min = min{(1+λ_t)/λ_t · v_t, 1/μ_t · v_t, B_t} by taking the element-wise minimum of the two independently generated bids rather than a single unified formula. The theoretical outputs are O(√T) regret and O(√T log T) ROS constraint violation, with the budget always exhausted within O(√T) of the horizon end.

## Method

The min-pacing algorithm runs the budget and ROS dual variable updates (multiplicative weight updates with per-period constraint violations as gradients) in parallel, then takes the minimum of the two resulting bids at auction time. The key theoretical challenge is that, unlike the dual-optimal case, no global Lyapunov function is available for the min operation. The proof proceeds in three stages: (1) an ODE approximation of the dual dynamics shows the algorithm identifies which constraint binds and reaches an O(ε) orbit around the optimal dual solution in O(√T) steps, using a discrete Gronwall inequality to bound stochastic deviations from the expected path; (2) a local stochastic Lyapunov function based on KL divergence and classical stochastic stability results show the algorithm never leaves the orbit with high probability; (3) standard online convex optimization regret bounds control the accumulated loss while in orbit. The paper also proves a negative result: any sequential composition of the two pacing services admits instances with Ω(T) ROS violation or Ω(T) regret.

## Results

Theoretically, Min Pacing achieves O(√T) regret and O(√T log T) ROS constraint violation, matching the Dual-Optimal pacing algorithm, while Sequential pacing suffers Ω(T) regret and/or Ω(T) ROS violation. Empirically on 10⁵ campaigns from a large ad platform, Min Pacing captures 86% of the fluid-relaxation benchmark conversion value at ≤15% relative ROS error (vs. 81% for Dual-Optimal and 30% for Sequential), and 88% at ≤20% ROS error. At 0% ROS violation, 62% of campaigns satisfy the constraint under Dual-Optimal, 49% under Min Pacing, but only 11% under Sequential.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the Dual-Optimal pacing algorithm from Feng et al. (2022), which computes the bid as (1+λ)/(μ+λ)·v using a centralized update of both dual variables. Min Pacing matches this baseline's O(√T) regret and O(√T log T) ROS violation guarantees, and empirically performs comparably or slightly better: at 15% ROS violation, Min Pacing achieves 86% of benchmark value versus 81% for Dual-Optimal, and at 20% it reaches 88% versus 83%. The key practical advantage is that Min Pacing only requires taking the minimum of two independently computed bids (one from budget pacing, one from ROS pacing), avoiding the need to pass dual variables between systems. In stark contrast, Sequential pacing (where the ROS bid is fed into the budget pacing service) is shown to be fundamentally broken: it either violates the ROS constraint by Ω(T) or incurs Ω(T) regret, and empirically only 11% of campaigns satisfy the ROS constraint at all, with total value capped at 30% of benchmark even at 15% violation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the dual-optimal pacing framework of Feng et al. (2022), which established O(√T) regret for the fully-coupled Lagrangian dual algorithm under budget and ROS constraints, and on the uniform bidding optimality result of Aggarwal et al. (2019). The key departure is analyzing a minimally-coupled variant (min-pacing) that takes the minimum of the two constraint-specific bids rather than the single dual-optimal bid, and showing it retains the same asymptotic guarantees. The analysis departs from the Lyapunov-function technique used for the dual-optimal case, instead combining ODE approximation of the dual dynamics, stochastic stability via KL-divergence Lyapunov functions, and discrete Gronwall's inequality to handle the non-separable min structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides a practical architectural recommendation for ad platforms: the minimally-coupled min-pacing achieves near-optimal performance without requiring the expensive centralized coordination of the dual-optimal system, which is often infeasible due to organizational and infrastructure separation between budget-pacing and auto-bidding teams. The negative result on sequential pacing (Ω(T) regret or violation) is important because sequential architectures are the natural default in practice and the paper shows they are fundamentally inadequate. Open questions include extending the analysis to multi-bidder settings, non-i.i.d. (adversarial or non-stationary) environments, and the degenerate case where both constraints bind simultaneously (k^BUD = k^ROS), which the authors note requires additional techniques.
