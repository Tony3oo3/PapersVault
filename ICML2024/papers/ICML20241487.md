## Problem

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) fine-tunes LLMs on a fixed, randomly-collected preference dataset, wasting a limited oracle labelling budget on uninformative examples. The paper asks how to iteratively select the most useful prompt/completion pairs for preference labelling so that each oracle query yields maximum learning signal, improving both the rate of convergence and final win-rate under a fixed labelling budget.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a large pool of unlabeled prompts (24,895 for IMDB, 20,567 for TLDR), a pre-trained ~1B-parameter transformer (GPT-2 or Pythia), and a GPT-4 oracle that returns binary preference judgments with rationale. At each acquisition step, S prompts (4000/2048) are sampled, two completions are generated per prompt from the current model at temperature 0.7, and the top M=128 pairs are sent to the oracle. The output is an incrementally growing preference-labeled dataset and a re-fine-tuned model, evaluated via head-to-head GPT-4 win-rate against the initial model (IMDB) or human-written summaries (TLDR) on 1024 held-out prompts.

## Method

The paper wraps DPO in a pool-based active learning loop: at each of T steps, prompts are sampled, completions are generated from the current model, scored by an acquisition function, the top M pairs are labelled by the oracle, and the model is re-initialized to θ₀ and fine-tuned on all accumulated preference data. The key acquisition function is *preference certainty*, defined as the absolute difference in DPO's implicit rewards |r̂(x,y₁) − r̂(x,y₂)| between two completions for the same prompt; this surfaces examples where the model is confidently mis-ranked, which correspond to large gradient updates in the DPO objective (weighted by w = σ(r̂(x,y_l) − r̂(x,y_w))). A hybrid variant first ranks prompts by predictive entropy (Monte-Carlo estimate with N=8 samples) to select uncertain prompts, then applies preference certainty on the generated completions. Across IMDB and TLDR with 9 seeds, preference certainty improves win-rate by 1–6% over random DPO, with the hybrid giving a small additional gain at larger batch sizes on IMDB.

## Results

The primary metric is head-to-head win-rate (vs. the initial pre-trained model for IMDB, vs. human-provided summaries for TLDR), evaluated over 9 random seeds. The preference certainty acquisition function improves win-rate by 1–6% on average over the random (standard DPO) baseline. On IMDB at 768 acquired samples, preference certainty reaches 0.90 win-rate vs. 0.88 for random; on TLDR at 512 samples, it reaches 0.60 vs. 0.56 for random. The hybrid entropy + certainty approach shows a small additional gain at larger batch sizes on IMDB (0.92 at 768) but is not consistently superior across both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is random acquisition, which the authors explicitly equate to the standard offline DPO procedure (Rafailov et al., 2023). The preference certainty acquisition consistently outperforms random on both datasets once the acquired dataset exceeds 128 samples: on IMDB it gains +6 points at 256 samples (0.82 vs. 0.76) and +2 points at 768 (0.90 vs. 0.88); on TLDR it gains +4 points at 512 samples (0.60 vs. 0.56). At the smallest budget (128 samples) all strategies are statistically indistinguishable, which the authors attribute to the pre-trained model not yet encoding oracle preferences. The entropy-only acquisition does not reliably beat random (it actually underperforms at 256 on IMDB: 0.74 vs. 0.76), and the hybrid entropy + certainty approach offers only marginal gains over certainty alone, leading the authors to recommend preference certainty as the simplest effective strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) as its fine-tuning objective, extending the standard offline DPO pipeline—where all preference labels are collected upfront and the model is trained to convergence—into an iterative active learning loop. The key departure is the introduction of a pool-based active learning acquisition strategy that selects which prompt/completion pairs to label at each step, leveraging the implicit Bradley-Terry reward model inherent in DPO. It is positioned alongside but distinct from RaFT (Dong et al., 2023), which also performs online fine-tuning but consults the oracle on every generated completion rather than selectively acquiring labels, and from active learning for in-context learning (Margatina et al., 2023), which selects few-shot examples without updating model parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The preference certainty acquisition function offers a simple, drop-in strategy for making expensive preference labels (human or LLM oracle) more efficient during DPO fine-tuning, with a clean gradient-based justification for why confidently-wrong examples accelerate learning. However, the demonstrated gains (1–6% win-rate) are modest, experiments are limited to ~1B-parameter models, and the re-initialization-at-each-step protocol, while useful for isolating acquisition effects, limits direct practical applicability to large-scale fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the approach scales to frontier models, how it interacts with parameter-efficient fine-tuning (e.g., LoRA), and whether the online variant (skipping re-initialization) preserves the advantage—preliminary results in Appendix D are promising but underpowered (3 seeds, single dataset).
