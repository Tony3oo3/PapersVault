## Problem

Existing online optimization literature splits into two tracks: online metric problems (e.g., convex function chasing) that handle multidimensional decisions and switching costs but lack long-term constraints, and online search problems (e.g., one-way trading) that enforce long-term demand constraints but are restricted to unidimensional spaces without switching costs. No prior work simultaneously addresses multidimensional decision spaces, switching costs, and long-term demand constraints, and generalizing unidimensional threshold-based algorithms to the multidimensional case is non-trivial due to the shared constraint coupling dimensions and the multidimensional switching cost.

## Input / Output

At each time step t, the algorithm receives a convex hitting cost function f_t(x) with bounded gradients (L ≤ [∇f_t]^i/c^i ≤ U) and must choose a decision x_t in the l1 ball {x : c(x) ≤ 1} ⊂ R^d, paying hitting cost f_t(x_t) plus weighted l1 switching cost ‖x_t − x_{t−1}‖_{ℓ1(w)}. The cumulative constraint utilization z^(t) = Σ c(x_τ) must reach 1 by the horizon T, with the player starting and ending at the origin. For the learning-augmented variant CLIP, an additional untrusted advice sequence ADV = {a_t} is provided, and the algorithm outputs decisions satisfying (1+ε)-consistency against ADV and γ^ε-robustness against OPT.

## Method

The core algorithm ALG1 solves a pseudo-cost minimization at each step: minimize f_t(x) + ‖x − x_{t−1}‖_{ℓ1(w)} − ∫φ(u)du over the remaining constraint budget, where the threshold function φ(z) = U − β + (U/α − U + 2β)exp(z/α) encodes the best marginal cost seen so far and is parameterized by the competitive ratio α (closed-form via the Lambert W function). This generalizes threshold-based design from unidimensional online search to multidimensional spaces by jointly optimizing over all dimensions within a single convex problem, avoiding the decomposition failure that breaks independent per-dimension approaches. The learning-augmented algorithm CLIP augments this with a projected consistency constraint that compares CLIP's accumulated cost, switching trajectory, and worst-case projected completion cost against the advice's corresponding quantities, ensuring (1+ε)-consistency while the pseudo-cost minimization preserves robustness. Both algorithms achieve provably optimal bounds: ALG1 attains the tight competitive ratio α, and CLIP achieves the optimal consistency-robustness Pareto frontier γ^ε.

## Results

ALG1 achieves the provably optimal competitive ratio α (given by a Lambert W function of U/L and β), and empirically outperforms the closest heuristic ('simple threshold') by an average of 18.2%, 'agnostic' by 56.1%, and 'move to minimizer' by 71.5% in average empirical competitive ratio. The learning-augmented CLIP algorithm achieves the optimal consistency-robustness trade-off: for any ε ∈ [0, α−1], CLIP is (1+ε)-consistent and γ^ε-robust, matching the lower bound in Theorem 4.5. With correct advice (ξ=0), CLIP shows significant performance gains over ALG1, and as prediction quality degrades (ξ > 0.1), CLIP outperforms the fixed-ratio Baseline by an average of 60.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ALG1 and CLIP against three heuristics adapted from adjacent literature ('agnostic', 'move to minimizer', 'simple threshold') and a fixed-ratio learning-augmented Baseline derived from Lechowicz et al. (2024). ALG1 dominates all three heuristics across all parameter sweeps (varying U/L, β, d, σ), with the largest gap (71.5%) against 'move to minimizer' and the smallest (18.2%) against 'simple threshold'. The 'agnostic' and 'move to minimizer' heuristics improve relative to ALG1 as β grows (because OPT's switching cost also grows), but ALG1 still maintains a clear advantage. For the learning-augmented setting, CLIP significantly outperforms the Baseline as the adversarial factor ξ increases, showing an average 60.8% improvement when ξ > 0.1, demonstrating that CLIP's adaptive consistency constraint is far more robust to prediction errors than the simple convex-combination approach of the Baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established research tracks: online metric problems (convex function chasing, metrical task systems) which feature multidimensional decision spaces and switching costs but no long-term constraints, and online search problems (one-way trading, online knapsack) which feature long-term demand constraints but are restricted to unidimensional spaces without switching costs. The authors introduce CFL and MAL as the first problems combining both, and their core technical tool—pseudo-cost minimization—generalizes the threshold-based design paradigm from online search to multidimensional metric spaces, a non-trivial step since the shared constraint and multidimensional switching cost prevent decomposition over dimensions. On the learning-augmented side, CLIP builds on the consistency-robustness framework of Lykouris & Vassilvtiskii (2018) and prior work on CFC (Christianson et al., 2022) and OWT (Sun et al., 2021a), but introduces a projected consistency constraint that exactly matches the lower bound rather than achieving an asymptotically optimal trade-off.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide tight competitive bounds (matching upper and lower) for any online problem combining long-term demand constraints with multidimensional decision spaces and switching costs, and the first to achieve the exact optimal consistency-robustness trade-off in this setting. The dimension-free competitive ratio (independent of d) is notable and connects to recent dimension-free results in CFC. Key open questions include extending results beyond weighted l1 norms (e.g., Euclidean settings), generalizing MAL to tree or arbitrary metric spaces, and understanding whether the pseudo-cost minimization framework transfers to other online metric problems with constraints. The practical motivation from carbon-aware load shifting and EV charging gives the theory concrete grounding, though the structural assumptions (bounded gradients, l1 norms) limit immediate applicability to more general cost landscapes.
