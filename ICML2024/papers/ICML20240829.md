## Problem

Training large-scale 3D generative models is impeded by three compounding challenges: the extra spatial dimension makes U-Net feature maps memory-prohibitive, 3D data is irregular and sparse creating heavy I/O and storage costs, and existing compact representations (point clouds, latent vectors) sacrifice significant geometric detail. No prior method has combined a nearly lossless 3D encoding with an efficient diffusion training pipeline at the scale of ten million shapes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240829/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a variety of conditioning modalities—single-view or multi-view rendered images (from 55 predefined camera poses), point clouds (25,000 points during training, arbitrary at inference), low-resolution voxel grids (16³ or 32³), or an incomplete 3D shape for zero-shot completion—and outputs a 256³ truncated signed distance function (TSDF) that can be meshed via marching cubes. Internally, the diffusion target is a packed regular volume of shape 2^(2d) × 46³ (i.e., 64 channels at 46³ spatial resolution in 3D), formed by channel-wise concatenation of the coarse coefficient C₀, the filtered detail volume D̂₀, and the reshaped D̂₁. Generation completes in roughly 2 seconds (10 diffusion steps) for most conditions and ~8 seconds (100 steps) for single-view image conditioning.

## Method

The core representation is a wavelet-tree: a 256³ TSDF is decomposed via three levels of biorthogonal wavelet transform into coarse coefficients C₀ (46³) and detail coefficients D₀, D₁, D₂. A subband coefficient filtering step exploits empirical observations (magnitude–significance correlation, sibling correlation, D₂ insignificance) to discard D₂ entirely and retain only the top-K=16,384 most informative coordinates from D₀ along with their D₁ children, yielding a compact yet 99.56%-IoU representation at 1/15 the original size. To make this diffusable, a subband coefficient packing scheme reshapes D̂₁ into (2^d−1)(2^d) channels so that C₀, D̂₀, and D̂₁ all share the 46³ spatial grid, then concatenates them along the channel axis into a single regular volume—reducing GPU memory by ~64× compared to a naive spatial arrangement. Training uses a U-ViT denoiser with a subband adaptive loss that computes MSE separately on information-rich coordinates (magnitude > max/32) and a randomly sampled complement of equal size, preventing the high-magnitude detail coefficients from dominating the gradient. Conditioning is injected at three points in the U-ViT (input concatenation, group-norm modulation, and cross-attention at the bottleneck) using modality-specific encoders: CLIP L-14 for images, PointNet + PMA for point clouds, and 3D convolutions for voxels, all producing a unified sequence of latent vectors.

## Results

On the Image-to-3D task, the single-view model achieves IoU 0.5004 (GSO) and 0.4285 (Our Val), LFD 3406.61 and 4071.33, and CD 0.01748 and 0.01851, surpassing all prior baselines (Point-E, Shap-E, One-2-3-45) by a significant margin on all three metrics. The multi-view model further improves to IoU 0.7460 (GSO) and 0.6707 (Our Val) with LFD 1890.85 and 2217.25. Inference takes only ~2 seconds (10 iterations) for single-view and multi-view conditions, compared to 31 s for Point-E and 45 s for One-2-3-45, while the model uses only ~25M parameters versus 260M–500M for concurrent methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The single-view model clearly outperforms Point-E (IoU 0.1948→0.5004 on GSO), Shap-E (0.3488→0.5004), and One-2-3-45 (0.4159→0.5004) on IoU, while also achieving lower LFD and CD across all three baselines on both GSO and Our Val. Against the concurrent OpenLRM (open-source LRM), the single-view model is competitive: it achieves slightly lower GSO IoU (0.5004 vs. 0.5748) but better LFD (3406.61 vs. 3198.28) and CD (0.01748 vs. 0.01303) on GSO, and better LFD (4071.33 vs. 4348.20) and CD (0.01851 vs. 0.01499) on Our Val, all with only 25M parameters versus OpenLRM's 260M. The multi-view model dominates all baselines by a wide margin, reaching IoU 0.7460 on GSO (vs. 0.5748 for the best single-view baseline OpenLRM). In terms of efficiency, Make-A-Shape generates shapes in ~2 s (10 iterations) versus ~31 s for Point-E, ~6 s for Shap-E, and ~45 s for One-2-3-45, and trains ~290k shapes/day/GPU on A10G hardware, exceeding DMV3D (~110k on A100) and Instant3D (~98k on A100) despite using a less powerful GPU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Hui et al. (2022), which used wavelet-domain diffusion for 3D shape generation but relied solely on coarse coefficients C0 with a separate detail predictor. Make-A-Shape departs from that line by introducing a wavelet-tree representation that compactly encodes both coarse and detail coefficients via subband filtering and packing, making the full multi-scale signal directly diffusable without an auxiliary autoencoder. It also positions itself against two dominant strategies in prior work: lossy representations (Point-E's point clouds, Shap-E's latent vectors) that sacrifice detail fidelity, and multi-view image-based approaches (One-2-3-45, DMV3D, Instant3D, LRM) that depend on differentiable rendering and are slower to train. The generator architecture adopts U-ViT with CLIP-based conditioning, following the broader trend of applying 2D diffusion model design principles to 3D.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Training on 10 million shapes at 25M parameters with ~2-second inference represents a significant practical milestone for 3D shape generation, making large-scale 3D generative models more accessible than prior work requiring A100 clusters and 5–45 second inference. The wavelet-tree representation (99.56% IoU at 1/15 the size of raw SDF) offers a reusable, near-lossless encoding that avoids the information bottleneck of VQ-VAE-based pipelines. Key limitations remain: the unconditional model exhibits category imbalance (over-representation of CAD shapes), no category conditioning is used, and texture generation is not addressed. Open questions include whether data-driven plausibility metrics can filter implausible cross-dataset shape mixtures, and whether joint geometry-texture generation can be achieved without per-shape optimization.
