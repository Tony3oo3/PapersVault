## Problem

Low-precision (BF16) LLM training suffers from lost arithmetic when model parameters and optimizer updates differ greatly in magnitude, causing the effective update to deviate from the intended one and degrading model quality. The standard remedy—maintaining FP32 master weights and upcasting for optimizer steps—incurs significant memory overhead (16 bytes/parameter) and forfeits the speed benefits of low-precision FPU operations. The paper asks whether strict low-precision training can match FP32 master-weight quality without any high-precision copies.

## Input / Output

COLLAGE operates as a drop-in plugin for AdamW, consuming standard BF16 gradients and activations (computed via mixed-precision GEMM) and producing model parameters stored as length-2 MCF expansions—two BF16 components (θ, δθ) representing the unevaluated sum. In the COLLAGE-plus variant, the second-moment optimizer state v and the decay coefficient β₂ are likewise stored as BF16 expansions, so all optimizer variables remain in 16-bit storage. The total memory footprint is 10 bytes/parameter (light) or 12 bytes/parameter (plus), versus 16 for the FP32 master-weight baseline, with no runtime upcasting to FP32.

## Method

COLLAGE replaces critical single-float representations in the AdamW update loop with multi-component float (MCF) length-2 expansions, using the Grow algorithm (built on Fast2Sum) to add the optimizer update Δθ to the parameter expansion (θ, δθ) without lost arithmetic, and the Mul algorithm to compute the second-moment update as a product of two expansions. The key insight is that β₂ values like 0.999 round to 1.0 in BF16, making the second momentum monotonically increasing and shrinking updates; representing β₂ as the expansion (1, −0.001) restores exact arithmetic. Two variants are provided: COLLAGE-light applies MCF only to model parameters, while COLLAGE-plus additionally applies it to the second-moment state and β₂. The paper also introduces the Effective Descent Quality (EDQ) metric, which measures the projection of the actual parameter change onto the intended update direction, providing a fine-grained diagnostic for comparing precision strategies during training.

## Results

COLLAGE matches or exceeds the FP32 master-weights baseline (Option D) on pre-training perplexity across GPT models (125M–6.7B) and OpenLLaMA-7B, e.g., GPT-6.7B achieves 8.17/11.13 train/val perplexity identical to Option D. On OpenLLaMA-7B with β₂=0.99, COLLAGE-plus (6.11/4.62) substantially outperforms Option D (8.58/6.42). COLLAGE delivers up to 3.74× training throughput speedup (GPT-6.7B) and 15–24% peak memory reduction versus Option D. On GLUE finetuning, COLLAGE-plus exceeds Option D by +0.85% average on RoBERTa-base (7/8 tasks) and +0.1% on BERT-base (5/8 tasks).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard mixed-precision with FP32 master weights baseline (Micikevicius et al., 2018; Option D), COLLAGE-plus matches perplexity on GPT-1.3B/2.7B/6.7B and OpenLLaMA-7B (β₂=0.95), and clearly wins on OpenLLaMA-7B with β₂=0.99 (6.11 vs. 8.58 train perplexity), while using 12 vs. 16 bytes/parameter. Against the Kahan-summation BF16 approach (Zamirai et al., 2020), COLLAGE-plus achieves a higher effective descent quality (EDQ) and lower training perplexity, as Kahan only compensates at the parameter-update step but not at the exponential moving-average step where lost arithmetic also occurs. Against vanilla BF16 training (Kalamkar et al., 2019; Option A), COLLAGE-light already closes most of the gap (e.g., BERT-base phase-1 perplexity 5.99 vs. 8.67), and COLLAGE-plus further improves to match or exceed Option D. The paper also shows that a BF16+FP32-optimizer-states variant (D⁻ᴹᵂ) with the same bytes/parameter as COLLAGE-plus still underperforms, confirming that the MCF representation—not merely higher-precision storage—is what drives the quality gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

COLLAGE builds directly on the multi-component float (MCF) framework from the MCTensor library (Yu et al., 2022b), which itself formalizes Dekker's Fast2Sum (1971) and Priest's expansion definition (1991) into a PyTorch-integrated toolkit. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant mixed-precision-with-FP32-master-weights paradigm (Micikevicius et al., 2018) by arguing that the critical imprecision in the optimizer step can be compensated entirely within low-precision using length-2 MCF expansions, rather than upcasting to a higher-precision clone. The paper also positions Kahan summation (Zamirai et al., 2020) as a special case of COLLAGE-light under a magnitude assumption, unifying these approaches under a single MCF framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COLLAGE offers a practical, drop-in path to eliminate FP32 master weights from LLM training, yielding up to 3.7× throughput gains and 15–23% peak memory savings while matching or exceeding the quality of the standard (BF16, FP32) mixed-precision baseline across BERT, RoBERTa, GPT (125M–6.7B), and OpenLLaMA-7B. The proposed Effective Descent Quality (EDQ) metric provides a previously missing diagnostic for quantifying information loss at the optimizer step, enabling fine-grained comparison of precision strategies. Open questions include when COLLAGE is preferable to mixed-precision (the OpenLLaMA-7B β₂=0.99 case suggests context-dependence), whether the approach extends cleanly to FP8 hardware, and whether specialized fused kernels can close the remaining throughput gap between COLLAGE and vanilla BF16.
