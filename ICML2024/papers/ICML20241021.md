## Problem

Non-parametric density estimation methods that work in higher dimensions are essentially nonexistent: existing approaches are restricted to 1D or 2D, and even those often produce functions that are not non-negative or integrable. This paper introduces SOSREP, the first non-parametric estimator that simultaneously satisfies non-negativity, integrability, multi-dimensionality, and computational tractability, while providing explicit control over smoothness through a Sobolev norm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241021/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N i.i.d. tabular data points in R^d and a smoothness parameter a > 0 (set to 1/N for consistency). It produces an unnormalized pre-density f*²(x) that is non-negative and integrable over R^d but does not integrate to 1; for anomaly detection, the negative pre-density value at each point serves as the anomaly score. The SDO kernel has no closed analytic form for d > 1, so it is approximated via a finite set of random samples drawn from a spherically symmetric distribution, reducing kernel evaluation to a sum of cosines.

## Method

SOSREP formulates density estimation as minimizing a regularized log-likelihood objective over a Sobolev-type RKHS H^a whose norm penalizes only derivatives of a single order m = ⌈d/2⌉ (the minimal order for the space to be an RKHS). By the Representer Theorem, the solution is a linear combination of N kernel functions, reducing optimization to N coefficients α. The objective is non-convex, but the authors show that natural gradient descent (as opposed to standard gradient) preserves non-negativity of f on the data points, and a non-negative α initialization further stabilizes training. For hyperparameter selection of a, Fisher Divergence via score matching is used instead of log-likelihood, since the pre-density is unnormalized. The SDO kernel is approximated by sampling z from a spherically symmetric density w^a(z) = (1 + a(2π)^{2m}‖z‖^{2m})^{-1}, decomposed into a uniform sphere direction and a 1D radial component, following the Rahimi-Recht random features paradigm.

## Results

On the ADBench benchmark (47 tabular datasets, 18 algorithms), SOSREP with the SDO kernel ranks 2nd best overall in average AUC-ROC, behind only one other method. On the more challenging duplicate-anomaly setting (X5 duplicates), SOSREP rises to 1st place with an average AUC-ROC of 71.6, leading the closest competitor by 4%. The full evaluation across all 47 datasets takes approximately 186 minutes on a single RTX 3090 GPU (~4 min/dataset). SOSREP also outperforms standard KDE estimators (Gaussian and Laplace kernels) on most datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOSREP is compared against 15 state-of-the-art anomaly detection methods from ADBench (including Isolation Forest, LOF, and deep methods) as well as standard KDE with Gaussian and Laplace kernels. Overall, SOSREP achieves the 2nd-best average AUC-ROC among all 18 methods, and notably does so without any AD-specific pre- or post-processing, whereas many competitors include extensive task-specific tuning. On the duplicate-anomaly sub-task (X5 duplicates), SOSREP is the top performer with 71.6 AUC-ROC, a 4% margin over the nearest method, while former leaders like Isolation Forest suffer substantial performance drops. Against KDE with the same SDO kernel, SOSREP consistently produces sharper density contrasts between clusters (as shown analytically in Section F and empirically in Figure 4), yielding better anomaly separation. The paper also shows that SOSREP with SDO outperforms SOSREP with Gaussian or Laplace kernels, though the exact reason for this kernel preference remains an open question.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the 1D Sobolev-regularized density estimation objective of Good and Gaskins (1971) and Klonias (1984) to arbitrary dimensions, resolving the key obstacles that prevented prior work from scaling: the SDO kernel has no closed form for d>1 (addressed via a sampling scheme inspired by Rahimi and Recht, 2007), the non-convex optimization is handled with natural gradients that provably preserve positivity, and the lack of normalization is circumvented by adapting Fisher divergence / score matching (Hyvärinen, 2005; Song et al., 2020) for hyperparameter selection. It departs from KDE by proving the estimator is genuinely non-equivalent (clusters interact differently), and from Marteau-Ferey et al. (2020) by requiring only O(N²) per-step cost and N parameters rather than N² parameters and O(N³) cost, making it applicable to high-dimensional tabular data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SOSREP is the first non-parametric density estimator that is simultaneously non-negative, integrable, applicable in arbitrary dimensions, and computationally tractable, while carrying an explicit and interpretable smoothness prior (the Sobolev norm). Its 2nd-place ranking on ADBench (and 1st on the notoriously difficult duplicate-anomaly setting) demonstrates that a simple, interpretable kernel method can compete with or exceed purpose-built deep anomaly detectors. Key open questions include: whether the unnormalized pre-densities can serve as a basis for generative modeling via MCMC (the authors flag this as promising but unexplored), why the SDO kernel outperforms Gaussian and Laplacian kernels in practice, and how to compute the normalization constant efficiently for larger N.
