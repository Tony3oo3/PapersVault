## Problem

Existing clustering methods construct supervision signals exclusively from internal data (augmentations, momentum strategies), which are inherently upper-bounded by the information contained in the given images. Abundant external knowledge—such as textual semantics that naturally distinguish visually similar classes—has been largely overlooked as a source of supervision for clustering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241117/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 backbone) and a vocabulary of WordNet nouns (prompted as 'A photo of [CLASS]' and encoded by CLIP's text encoder) as input. It produces soft cluster assignment vectors of dimension K (the user-specified target number of clusters) from two parallel cluster heads—one over image features and one over constructed text counterparts—with final hard assignments obtained by argmax. No raw pixel-level processing or class-name priors are required; the only hyperparameter beyond K is the expected compact cluster size N̂ (default 300).

## Method

TAC operates in two stages. First, it constructs a text counterpart for each image: k-means on CLIP image embeddings yields semantic centers (k = N/300), all WordNet nouns are reverse-classified to these centers via cosine similarity, and the top-γ=5 most discriminative nouns per center form a curated text space; each image's text counterpart is then a softmax-weighted sum of these noun embeddings. Second, it trains two two-layer MLP cluster heads (one for images, one for text counterparts) with a cross-modal mutual distillation loss that encourages each image to share cluster assignments with the nearest neighbors of its text counterpart and vice versa, supplemented by a confidence loss (pushing assignments toward one-hot) and a balance loss (preventing cluster collapse). A training-free baseline that simply concatenates image and text-counterpart features before k-means already yields substantial gains, while the distillation stage further improves performance to state-of-the-art levels.

## Results

On five classic image clustering benchmarks, TAC achieves an average ACC of 83.2%, surpassing the previous SOTA SIC (79.9%) and zero-shot CLIP (78.7%). On the three more challenging datasets, TAC reaches 59.9% average ACC, outperforming CLIP(zero-shot) (56.7%) and SIC (54.6%). Notably, even the training-free variant TAC(no train) already achieves 79.5% average ACC on classic datasets, a 13.2-point improvement over CLIP(k-means) (66.3%), with a 10% ACC gain on ImageNet-1K alone. On ImageNet-Dogs, TAC achieves 83.0% ACC and 72.2% ARI, a 43.5-point ARI improvement over CLIP(k-means).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAC outperforms zero-shot CLIP on all five classic datasets, with the largest margin on ImageNet-Dogs (83.0% vs. 72.8% ACC, +10.2 points) and CIFAR-20 (60.7% vs. 58.3% ACC). On the challenging datasets, TAC beats CLIP(zero-shot) on DTD (50.1% vs. 43.1% ACC) and UCF-101 (68.7% vs. 63.4% ACC), but falls slightly short on ImageNet-1K (58.2% vs. 63.6% ACC), where CLIP benefits from its 1K class-name prior. TAC surpasses the previous SOTA SIC on all five classic datasets (e.g., 83.0% vs. 69.7% ACC on ImageNet-Dogs) and on DTD and UCF-101, though SIC edges ahead on ImageNet-1K (47.0% vs. 58.2% ACC — actually TAC wins here too at 58.2% vs. 47.0%). The training-free TAC(no train) already exceeds CLIP(k-means) by 13.2 points in average ACC on classic datasets, demonstrating that simply retrieving WordNet text counterparts provides substantial 'free' semantic information without any additional training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as introducing a fourth era of clustering—'externally guided clustering'—beyond the established progression from classic (data-distribution assumptions), deep (neural feature extraction), and self-supervised (augmentation/momentum-based) clustering. It builds directly on CLIP's zero-shot classification paradigm but inverts it: rather than classifying images into known class names, it classifies all WordNet nouns into image semantic centers to construct a discriminative text space. The cross-modal mutual distillation loss draws on contrastive learning principles (similar in spirit to SCAN, GCC, and CC) but applies them across modalities rather than within the image modality, and the entire pipeline operates on a frozen CLIP backbone with only lightweight MLP cluster heads trained.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that external textual knowledge, long overlooked in clustering, provides a powerful and readily available supervision signal that can push clustering accuracy beyond what internal data-derived signals achieve, even surpassing zero-shot CLIP in most settings without class name priors. The practical takeaway is that a training-free baseline (retrieve text counterparts, concatenate with image features, run k-means) already yields large gains, suggesting that much of CLIP's zero-shot capability is underexploited by the standard classification paradigm. Open questions include generalizing the external-knowledge paradigm to other modalities (object detection, segmentation), other data types (text, point clouds), and the non-trivial problem of selecting appropriate external knowledge for a given dataset. A limitation is the requirement to specify the target cluster number K, which is not always available in practice.
