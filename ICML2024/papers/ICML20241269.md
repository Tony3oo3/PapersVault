## Problem

Performative prediction requires minimizing a risk function whose data distribution shifts with the deployed model, but existing approaches either demand structural assumptions on the distribution (e.g., mixture dominance, linear dependence), rely on two-phase algorithms that separately estimate the distribution, or require gradient access. This paper addresses the gap of finding near-stationary solutions to the performative risk under a controlled Markov chain data setting using only zeroth-order (function evaluation) oracle access, without any additional assumptions on the distribution map.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes only scalar loss evaluations ℓ(θ; Z) at perturbed parameter points (no gradient or distribution access) and draws data samples sequentially from a controlled Markov chain whose transition kernel depends on the currently deployed model. The output is a uniformly random iterate θ_s from the trajectory, accompanied by a theoretical guarantee that the time-averaged expected squared gradient norm of the performative risk is bounded by O(d^(2/3)/(T+1)^(1/3)), requiring O(d²/ε³ log(1/ε)) total samples to reach ε-stationarity.

## Method

The DFO(λ) algorithm constructs a one-point zeroth-order gradient estimator g_δ(θ; u, Z) = (d/δ)ℓ(θ+δu; Z)u, which is unbiased for the smoothed performative risk gradient but whose variance scales as O(1/δ²), creating a fundamental bias-variance tradeoff. Two key mechanisms address this: (i) a two-timescale step size design where the query radius δ_k = d^(1/3)(1+k)^(-1/6) decays slower than the update step η_k = d^(-2/3)(1+k)^(-2/3), ensuring η_k/δ_k → 0 so the model adapts slowly to the high-variance estimator; and (ii) a sample accumulation mechanism with forgetting factor λ ∈ [0,1) that aggregates gradient estimates from all τ_k Markov chain samples within each epoch, weighting later (less biased) samples more heavily via λ^(τ_k−m). The inner loop length τ_k grows logarithmically to ensure the Markov chain mixes sufficiently, and the forgetting factor λ controls the tradeoff between bias reduction and variance, with optimal λ depending on the Lipschitz constant of the performative risk gradient.

## Results

The DFO(λ) algorithm achieves a convergence rate of O(1/T^(1/3)) to an ε-stationary point of the performative risk, requiring O(d²/ε³ log(1/ε)) total samples. In the 1D quartic experiment with AR(1) Markovian data (γ=0.5), DFO(λ) empirically converges at rate O(1/S^0.312), closely matching the theoretical O(1/S^(1/3)) prediction. In the 5D Markovian pricing problem, DFO(λ) converges to the highest expected revenue among all tested methods, while DFO-GD and SGD-GD fail to reach comparable performance. The forgetting factor λ=0.5 accelerates the transient phase but increases steady-state fluctuation compared to λ=0.25.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFO(λ) is compared against five baselines across three experimental settings. DFO-GD (no burn-in) and SGD-GD (stochastic gradient with greedy deployment) both fail to converge to any stationary point of the performative risk in all three experiments, confirming that naive approaches are insufficient under Markovian decision-dependent data. The two-point estimator g_2pt-I is empirically confirmed to be biased under decision-dependent samples and converges to a non-stationary point, validating the theoretical argument in §4.1. The two-point estimator g_2pt-II does converge to a stationary point but requires twice as many samples per update as the one-point DFO(λ) estimator. In the Markovian regression experiment, the two-phase algorithm from Miller et al. (2021) fails to find the optimal objective value even with n=10⁴ Markovian samples in its first phase, while DFO(λ) converges to a near-optimal solution. Overall, DFO(λ) is the only method that reliably reaches a stationary point across all settings without requiring structural assumptions on the distribution Π_θ or a separate distribution estimation phase.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stochastic zeroth-order optimization framework of Ghadimi & Lan (2013) and Nesterov & Spokoiny (2017) to the performative prediction setting with Markovian data, where prior DFO work (Ray et al., 2022) required a mixture dominance assumption and geometric decay environment. It departs from gradient-based performative prediction methods (Izzo et al., 2021; Li & Wai, 2022; Roy et al., 2022) by eliminating the need to estimate the data distribution Π_θ or impose structural assumptions on it, using instead a one-point gradient estimator specifically designed to avoid the bias that the classical two-point estimator incurs under decision-dependent sampling. The sample accumulation mechanism with forgetting factor λ is a new contribution that repurposes MCMC burn-in ideas into an online aggregation scheme for controlled Markov chains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first DFO guarantee for performative prediction under general Markovian data without distributional structure assumptions, offering a practical alternative when the data-generating process is complex or sensitive (e.g., strategic agents). The O(1/T^{1/3}) rate is notably slower than both gradient-based methods (O(1/T^{2/5})) and standard non-performative DFO (O(1/√T)), and the authors attribute this to a fundamental variance limitation of zeroth-order estimators under decision-dependent sampling—an open question whether this gap can be closed. The identified trade-off with the forgetting factor λ (larger λ helps when the performative risk gradient is smoother) provides actionable guidance but remains to be validated in more complex, high-dimensional settings.
