## Problem

Existing post-training quantization (PTQ) methods for LLMs either fail at extreme compression ratios (≤4 bits per weight) due to heuristic outlier suppression, or use unstructured codebooks too large for fast GPU inference. No prior PTQ method achieved both state-of-the-art quantization quality at 2–3 bits and hardware-efficient decoding, leaving a gap between quantization fidelity and practical deployment speed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241987/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weight matrices W ∈ R^{m×n} and proxy Hessian matrices H ∈ R^{n×n} (computed from 6144 calibration sequences of native context length). It produces quantized weights encoded as 16-bit E8P codewords (8 bits for a 256-entry source codebook index, 7 bits for sign flips, 1 bit for a ±1/4 shift) plus two sign vectors S_U and S_V of size m and n respectively. The total overhead beyond the quantized weights is less than 0.01 bits per weight, and the 1 KiB E8P codebook is shared across all linear layers, fitting in GPU L1 cache even after 32× bank-conflict duplication.

## Method

QuIP# combines three techniques. First, it performs incoherence processing via the Randomized Hadamard Transform (RHT), which conjugates W and H by structured random orthogonal matrices with O(n log n) cost and no floating-point multiplies, achieving a log(n) incoherence bound versus QuIP's log²(n) Kronecker approach. Second, it rounds the incoherent weights using BlockLDLQ, a novel g-block LDL decomposition that extends adaptive rounding to vector quantization, combined with the E8P codebook—a 2-bit 8-dimensional codebook built from the E8 lattice (optimal 8D sphere packing) that represents 2^16 codewords via a 256-entry lookup table plus sign/shift operations, enabling hardware-friendly decoding in fewer than 5 instructions per weight. Third, it applies inter-layer fine-tuning on a small development set, optimizing sign vectors as real-valued parameters to shape weights toward the codebook and recovering activation fidelity lost during quantization. For 3- and 4-bit quantization, residual vector quantization (RVQ) stacks multiple E8P stages.

## Results

On Llama 2 70B (context length 4096), QuIP# achieves WikiText2 perplexity of 3.91 at 2 bits, 3.35 at 3 bits, and 3.18 at 4 bits, compared to FP16's 3.12. A key finding is that QuIP# 3-bit (3.35) outperforms QuIP# 4-bit (3.18) on WikiText2, and 2-bit (3.91) scales comparably to higher bitrates — the first time a 3-bit PTQ method beats a 4-bit one. On zeroshot tasks (Llama 2 70B), QuIP# 2-bit achieves 34.6% ARC-C, 64.6% ARC-E, 75.1% PIQA, and 64.9% WinoGrande, approaching FP16's 40.0/69.3/78.5/67.3. QuIP# also achieves over 50% peak memory bandwidth on an RTX 4090 for 2-bit inference (56.84% on Llama 2 70B), enabling 3.2× speedup over FP16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AWQ, QuIP# is dramatically superior at extreme compression: at 2 bits on Llama 2 7B, AWQ produces a perplexity of 90.6 (effectively unusable) while QuIP# achieves 8.35; at 3 bits, AWQ reaches 23.9 vs. QuIP#'s 7.32. Against OmniQuant, QuIP# wins at all bitrates, with the gap widening at lower bits (e.g., 2-bit Llama 2 70B: OmniQuant 12.3 vs. QuIP# 6.12 on C4, ctx 2048). Against QuIP (the prior method), QuIP# improves substantially at 2 bits (Llama 2 70B WikiText2 ctx 4096: QuIP 5.90 vs. QuIP# 3.91) due to the RHT and E8P codebook, while at 4 bits the gap is smaller (3.58 vs. 3.18). Against AQLM, QuIP# outperforms at 2 and 3 bits (e.g., 2-bit Llama 2 70B: QuIP# 3.91 vs. AQLM 3.94 on WikiText2; 3-bit: 3.35 vs. 3.36) and ties at 4 bits, while also being significantly faster for inference (106.3 tok/s vs. 20.6 tok/s on Llama 2 7B, 2-bit) because E8P's 1 KiB codebook fits in L1 cache whereas AQLM's 1 MiB per-layer codebook does not.

## Relation to Prior Work

QuIP# is a direct successor to QuIP (Chee et al., 2023), replacing its Kronecker-product incoherence processing with the faster and theoretically stronger Randomized Hadamard Transform, and upgrading scalar LDLQ rounding to block vector quantization via the novel E8P lattice codebook. It also incorporates inter-layer fine-tuning in the spirit of AQLM (Egiazarian et al., 2024), though with a different parameterization (optimizing sign vectors as real-valued). Conceptually, QuIP# positions incoherence processing as a principled alternative to the heuristic outlier-suppression strategies used by AWQ and OmniQuant, and leverages the mathematical optimality of the E8 lattice (optimal 8D sphere packing) to achieve hardware-efficient vector quantization that avoids the exponential codebook blowup of unstructured VQ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 3-bit QuIP# outperforms 4-bit QuIP# (and even a theoretical lossless 4-bit model) directly refutes the widely-cited claim that 4-bit is the optimal compression point, suggesting the field should expect 2-bit models to eventually surpass 3-bit. Practically, QuIP# achieves over 50% peak memory bandwidth on consumer GPUs while AQLM's per-layer codebooks are too large for L1 cache and actually run slower than FP16, making QuIP# one of the first PTQ methods that is both high-quality and fast at extreme compression. Open questions include whether the fine-tuning step can be eliminated or made cheaper, whether the E8P construction generalizes to non-power-of-2 dimensions more gracefully, and whether more advanced multi-codebook quantization (beyond RVQ) could push 2-bit quality further.
