## Problem

Existing event-based motion deblurring methods assume that CIS images and EVS events share the same fixed spatial resolution and temporal exposure duration, which is rarely true in practice due to differences in shooting equipment and bandwidth limitations of EVS cameras. This paper is the first to investigate arbitrary-scale event-based motion deblurring, where the spatial and temporal scale ratios between images and events are unknown and continuous.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242373/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a high-resolution blurry image (e.g., 1920×1080 or 1280×720) and a low-resolution event stream whose spatial resolution and temporal exposure duration can differ from the image by arbitrary continuous factors (spatial scale R_s ∈ [1,4], temporal scale R_t ∈ (0,1]). The output is a high-resolution sharp image at the same spatial resolution as the input blurry image. A single trained model generalizes across all scale combinations without retraining, unlike prior methods that require separate models per scale.

## Method

SASNet implicitly aggregates cross-domain spatio-temporal correspondence features through two key modules. The Spatial Implicit Representation Module (SIRM) encodes event features, samples them at arbitrary spatial coordinates via a scale-aware encoding scheme, and decodes the result through an MLP-based implicit decoder to produce continuous spatial feature representations that focus on highly blurred local regions. The Temporal Implicit Representation Module (TIRM) performs time-aware event selection modulated by the temporal scale ratio, followed by grouped temporal shift operations (m=4 groups with shifts of ±3, ±7 pixels) to establish long-range bidirectional temporal correspondences with a large receptive field for global motion deblurring. The implicit (continuous) representation is the core differentiator: unlike explicit discrete upsampling or fixed-kernel convolutions, the MLP-based decoding and coordinate-based sampling allow the network to generalize smoothly to unseen scale values. Training uses random spatial and temporal scales sampled uniformly per batch, combined with stochastic weight averaging for broader generalization.

## Results

On the GoPro dataset (R_s=4, R_t=1), SASNet achieves 28.82 dB PSNR and 0.8811 SSIM, outperforming all frame-based and event-based baselines. On the real H2D dataset (R_s=2, R_t=1), SASNet reaches 35.72 dB PSNR and 0.9541 SSIM, a 3.11 dB gain over the best event-based competitor REFID. Notably, SASNet achieves these results with only 1.46M parameters and 43.35 GFLOPs, the lowest computational cost among all compared methods. Under severe blur (9 frames), SASNet degrades by only 0.29 dB versus 0.73 dB for EFNet, demonstrating robustness to increasing motion magnitude.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SASNet outperforms all eight baselines on both datasets. Against the strongest event-based method EFNet, SASNet gains +0.74 dB PSNR on GoPro (28.82 vs 28.08) and +1.13 dB on H2D (35.72 vs 34.59), while using roughly 6× fewer parameters (1.46M vs 8.47M). Against REFID, the gap widens dramatically on H2D: +3.11 dB PSNR (35.72 vs 32.61) and +0.0194 SSIM, with SASNet using 61× fewer parameters (1.46M vs 88.96M). Among frame-based methods, SASNet surpasses the best (Restormer) by +0.45 dB on GoPro and +2.33 dB on H2D, despite having 18× fewer parameters. The paper reports an average improvement of 1.96 dB over event-guided methods on GoPro and 2.5 dB on H2D, with the largest gains appearing in high-speed motion scenarios where event temporal information is most informative.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends event-based motion deblurring (EMD) from the fixed-scale assumption (R_s=1, R_t=1) used by all prior methods (EFNet, REFID, EVDI, UEVD) to an arbitrary-scale formulation where image and event spatial/temporal scales can differ continuously. Conceptually, it repurposes implicit representation ideas (MLP-based coordinate decoding from super-resolution) and simple temporal shift operations to replace the explicit multi-scale or attention-based fusion that prior EMD methods rely on. The H2D dataset, built on the OV60B hybrid EVS/CIS sensor, departs from prior dual-camera setups (DAVIS346, FLIR+Prophesee) by providing naturally aligned high-resolution events without manual calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The arbitrary-scale formulation addresses a genuine deployment gap: real hybrid sensors (like OV60B) inherently produce events at different spatial and temporal scales than CIS frames, yet all prior EMD methods require identical scales. The H2D dataset (1920×1080, 1836 frames, naturally aligned) is the first high-resolution real-world benchmark for this task and will likely become a standard evaluation set. However, the current degradation model only considers motion blur; the authors acknowledge that real-world scenes involve noise, atmospheric scattering, and other degradations that remain unaddressed. Extending the framework to multi-degradation scenarios and other event-based tasks (super-resolution, frame interpolation) is an open direction.
