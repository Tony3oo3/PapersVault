## Problem

Transformer-based robot control policies suffer from O(n) inference latency and poor data efficiency, making them unsuitable for real-time legged robot control that requires >50 Hz actuation. Existing architectures model robot trajectories in the time domain by analogy with language, ignoring the fact that physical robot motion energy is concentrated in a small number of low-frequency modes (m ≪ n), which leaves exploitable structure on the table.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sliding window of n=64 historical state vectors (projected gravity, joint velocities, joint positions, body angular velocity, last action, and remote-control commands — 79-dimensional for the quadruped) and, in the D4RL setting, concatenates return-to-go and action history into each token following the Decision Transformer convention. It outputs the next predicted action (12-dimensional expected joint positions for the quadruped motors). During inference, only the single next-step action is produced per call, with the frequency-domain cache updated via a Sliding DFT so that each step costs O(m) rather than O(n).

## Method

FCNet replaces the Transformer's attention with a Causal Spectral Convolution (CSC) block that applies a Short-Time Fourier Transform to the n-length state window, retains only the m lowest-frequency modes (m ≈ 10, n = 64), applies a learnable complex-valued linear transform W ∈ ℂ^{m×m} in the frequency domain, reconstructs the full spectrum via conjugate symmetry, and inverse-transforms back to the time domain. L such CSC+FFN layers are stacked between a position-wise encoder and decoder. For parallel training, the recursive Sliding-DFT update is reformulated as a linear convolution and computed via FFT in O(mn log n + m²n) time. For real-time inference, the frequency-domain coefficients are cached and updated incrementally with a Sliding DFT, reducing per-step cost to O(m) — roughly 3–5× faster than a KV-cached Transformer at equivalent model size, with latency staying below 20 ms even at 32 layers or hidden size 2048.

## Results

On D4RL-Mujoco, FCNet achieves an average score of 75.1, outperforming DT (74.7), DT-RetNet (63.6), CQL (63.9), and all MLP-based baselines (BC 46.4, BEAR 48.2, BRAC-v 36.9, AWR 34.3). On D4RL-Adroit manipulation tasks, FCNet scores 41.0 average versus DT's 27.8, winning 9 of 12 tasks. On the multi-environment legged robot locomotion dataset, FCNet consistently outperforms Transformer across all dataset sizes (1.9M to 120M steps), and achieves ~2ms inference latency on real-world hardware versus Transformer's ~3 Hz inference frequency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FCNet outperforms DT (Decision Transformer) on D4RL-Adroit manipulation (41.0 vs 27.8 average, winning 9/12 tasks) and on the multi-environment legged robot dataset across all data scales, though it roughly ties DT on D4RL-Mujoco (75.1 vs 74.7 average). Against DT-RetNet (RetNet-based SSM), FCNet achieves a substantial 11.5-point average advantage on D4RL-Mujoco (75.1 vs 63.6), indicating that Fourier-domain inductive bias is more effective than state-space modeling for robotic control. FCNet also clearly surpasses all MLP-based offline RL baselines (CQL 63.9, BEAR 48.2, BRAC-v 36.9, AWR 34.3, BC 46.4) on D4RL-Mujoco. On the legged robot locomotion dataset, FCNet outperforms Transformer at every dataset size tested (1.9M to 120M steps), with the gap most pronounced at smaller data scales where Transformer's performance drops sharply. In inference latency, FCNet is 3–5× faster than Transformer at 32 layers or hidden size 2048, maintaining sub-20ms latency while Transformer's grows linearly with context length.

## Relation to Prior Work

FCNet builds on the Decision Transformer (DT) paradigm of treating robot control as autoregressive sequence modeling, but departs fundamentally from the time-domain attention mechanism by introducing a Causal Spectral Convolution (CSC) block that operates in the frequency domain via STFT. It draws conceptual inspiration from Fourier Neural Operators and FITS (which exploit the simplification of differential dynamics in frequency space) and from physics-informed ML, but is the first to ground a full multi-layer controller architecture in the observation that robot trajectory energy concentrates in a small number of low-frequency modes. Compared to Transformer (O(n) inference) and State Space Models like RetNet (O(1) inference but weaker inductive bias for robotics), FCNet achieves O(m) inference with m ≪ n, positioning it as a frequency-domain alternative that is both more data-efficient and faster than attention-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial for real-time robotics: FCNet's ~2ms CPU inference latency enables deployment on legged robots requiring 50Hz+ control, whereas Transformer-based methods operate at ~3Hz and fail in real-world settings. The frequency-domain inductive bias also improves data efficiency, with FCNet outperforming Transformer even at 1.9M-step dataset sizes where Transformer underperforms. Key limitations include the lack of validation on very large pre-training datasets, no handling of scenarios where high-frequency information is critical (e.g., rapid impact dynamics), and no multimodal input support. Open questions include whether the frequency-domain approach scales to foundation-model-level robotics pre-training and whether it can be combined with online RL or attention mechanisms for long-horizon tasks.
