## Problem

Multi-task 3D robot manipulation policies built on voxel or multi-view representations still require large quantities of high-quality demonstrations, generalize poorly to unseen tasks, and execute inefficiently because they predict one action per timestep. The paper asks whether a pre-trained visual foundation model can supply task-relevant, object-oriented scene understanding that transfers to new instructions, and whether predicting a coherent action sequence in a single forward pass can replace step-by-step execution to reduce inference overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242417/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes four 128×128 RGB-D views (left shoulder, right shoulder, front, wrist) re-rendered as cube orthographic projections, concatenated with per-patch depth/coordinate features, plus a CLIP-encoded language instruction. It outputs a sequence of h=5 actions in one pass; each action is an 8-dim vector comprising a 6-DoF end-effector pose (3-D position from back-projected multi-view heatmaps, 3-D rotation discretized into 5° Euler-angle bins), a binary gripper state, and a binary collision-permission flag. During execution the sequence is consumed sequentially, so a single inference can drive multiple timesteps.

## Method

SAM-E uses the SAM ViT-B image encoder as a promptable perception backbone, adapted to embodied scenes via LoRA (rank 4) applied only to the Q and V projections of each self-attention block, keeping the bulk of SAM frozen. A two-stage multi-view transformer first processes each view independently (view-wise attention) and then fuses cross-view tokens with CLIP language tokens (cross-view attention) to produce view tokens enriched with 3D spatial and task-relevant semantic information. The key architectural novelty is a multi-channel heatmap policy head that predicts the full h-step action sequence in parallel: per-view position heatmaps are back-projected into a discretized 3D grid to yield target positions, while rotation, gripper, and collision channels are produced by a fully connected network weighted by the position heatmap. The training objective is standard imitation learning over keyframe-extracted sub-episodes, but the sequence head exploits the temporal-smoothness assumption (adjacent end-effector poses change by less than ε) so that predicting neighboring actions provides implicit priors that make each individual prediction more coherent than a standalone step-by-step policy.

## Results

On RLBench (18 tasks, 249 variations), SAM-E achieves a 70.6% average success rate, outperforming RVT (62.9%) by 7.7 percentage points and PerAct (49.4%) by 21.2 percentage points. SAM-E also reduces total inference steps from 6158 (RVT) to 1130, a ~5.5× efficiency gain, by predicting a 5-action sequence in a single pass (152 ms) rather than step-by-step (5×103 ms). In few-shot adaptation to 6 new tasks, SAM-E reaches 63.2% average success versus RVT's 37.1%, a 26.1-point (70.4% relative) improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RVT (the strongest multi-view baseline), SAM-E wins in 14 of 18 tasks, improving the average success rate from 62.9% to 70.6% (+7.7 pp, +12.2% relative) while reducing total inference steps by ~5.5× (1130 vs 6158). Against PerAct (voxel-based 3D representation), SAM-E achieves a much larger margin of +21.2 pp (49.4% → 70.6%). In the 10-task comparison with HiveFormer (Appendix E), SAM-E scores 88.6% vs HiveFormer's 83.3%, though SAM-E is trained on all variations making the comparison more challenging. In few-shot adaptation to 6 unseen tasks, SAM-E (63.2%) substantially outperforms RVT (37.1%) by 26.1 pp, and notably RVT's adaptation actually degrades below its from-scratch score (37.1% vs 39.3%), whereas SAM-E's adaptation improves over from-scratch (63.2% vs 42.4%), demonstrating the generalization benefit of the SAM foundation model.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAM-E builds on RVT's multi-view transformer architecture for 3D manipulation (cube orthographic projections, cross-view attention) and extends the action-chunking paradigm from 2D manipulation (ACT, RoboAgent) into 3D via a novel multi-channel heatmap head. Its key departure is replacing the from-scratch visual encoder with SAM's promptable ViT-B backbone, adapted through LoRA fine-tuning on embodied data, thereby inheriting SAM's web-scale pretraining for scene understanding. This positions SAM-E at the intersection of visual foundation models and imitation learning, distinct from voxel-based methods (PerAct) and point-cloud approaches that require expensive 3D representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a general-purpose segmentation foundation model can serve as an effective perception backbone for embodied manipulation with minimal parameter overhead (LoRA rank 4), and that predicting coherent action sequences in a single pass yields a practical 5× reduction in inference steps. The 70.4% relative improvement in few-shot adaptation over RVT suggests that foundation-model priors substantially reduce the data burden for new tasks. However, real-world validation is limited to 5 simple tasks with 10 demonstrations each, and all main results are in simulation (RLBench), leaving open whether these gains transfer to the long-tail of real-world manipulation. A key open question is whether SAM's advantages persist as embodied training data scales, or whether task-specific encoders eventually close the gap.
