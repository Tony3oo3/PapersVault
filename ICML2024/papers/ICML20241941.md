## Problem

While connections between contrastive and feature decorrelation-based self-supervised methods have been partially understood, the relationship between masked image modeling (MAE) and these Siamese-architecture methods remains largely unexplored. The paper asks whether a unified information-theoretic framework can simultaneously explain the implicit optimization objectives of contrastive, decorrelation, and masking-based losses, and whether such a framework can yield practical improvements to MAE.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241941/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images partitioned into n disjoint patches, applies a random binary mask (75% mask ratio by default) to produce a partially visible view, and encodes it into an l2-normalized representation vector. The output is a batch of representations Z ∈ R^{d×B} used for downstream linear probing or fine-tuning on ImageNet-1K (ViT-Base/ViT-Large) or CIFAR-100. The TCR regularizer operates on the batch covariance matrix ZZ^⊤, requiring only the second-order statistics of the representation batch rather than individual sample-level operations.

## Method

The paper introduces M-MAE (Matrix variational Masked Auto-Encoder), which augments the standard MAE reconstruction loss with a negative total coding rate (TCR) term: L_M-MAE = L_MAE − λ·TCR_μ(Z), where TCR_μ(Z) = log det(μI_d + ZZ^⊤) serves as a matrix-based entropy estimator over l2-normalized representations. Theoretically, the paper proves that Barlow Twins and spectral contrastive losses implicitly maximize matrix mutual information and matrix joint entropy (Theorems 4.4, 4.14), and that when the Siamese branches merge into a single branch (as in MAE), these quantities degenerate to matrix entropy, motivating the TCR regularizer. A key result (Theorem 5.2) shows that U-MAE's square uniformity loss is a second-order Taylor approximation of the M-MAE TCR term, establishing M-MAE as a principled generalization. The TCR coefficient μ is tuned per architecture (μ=1 for ViT-Base, μ=3 for ViT-Large), and the method achieves 62.4% linear probing accuracy on ImageNet with ViT-Base, a 3.9% improvement over MAE.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, M-MAE achieves 62.4% linear probing accuracy with ViT-Base, a 3.9% improvement over U-MAE (58.5%) and a 7.0% improvement over MAE (55.4%). For ViT-Large, M-MAE reaches 66.0% linear probing and 84.3% fine-tuning accuracy, surpassing U-MAE by 0.2% and 1.1% respectively. The fine-tuning gains are more modest overall (83.1% vs 83.0% for ViT-Base), with the most notable improvement being the 1.0% jump on ViT-Large fine-tuning (84.3% vs 83.3% MAE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

M-MAE outperforms both baselines across all four evaluation settings (linear probing and fine-tuning, ViT-Base and ViT-Large). Against MAE, the gains are substantial for linear probing: +7.0% on ViT-Base (62.4% vs 55.4%) and +3.8% on ViT-Large (66.0% vs 62.2%), while fine-tuning improvements are smaller (+0.2% ViT-Base, +1.0% ViT-Large). Against U-MAE, the most significant win is the 3.9% linear probing improvement on ViT-Base (62.4% vs 58.5%), confirming that the full TCR entropy regularizer captures higher-order structure beyond U-MAE's second-order uniformity approximation. On ViT-Large fine-tuning, M-MAE edges out U-MAE by 1.1% (84.3% vs 83.2%), which the authors note is a very significant margin at this scale. On CIFAR-100 (Table 3), M-MAE also leads on linear probing (60.9% vs 59.7% U-MAE, 59.2% MAE) but is slightly behind on fine-tuning (83.8% vs 84.5% MAE), suggesting the TCR regularizer primarily benefits linear transferability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the theoretical understanding of contrastive (spectral contrastive loss) and feature decorrelation (Barlow Twins) self-supervised methods by showing both implicitly maximize matrix mutual information and matrix joint entropy, building on the matrix information-theoretic framework of Skean et al. (2023) and Bach (2022). It then extends this lens to single-branch masked image modeling, where mutual information and joint entropy degenerate to entropy, motivating the M-MAE loss that adds a Total Coding Rate (TCR) regularizer to the MAE reconstruction objective. The paper proves U-MAE (Zhang et al., 2022) is a second-order Taylor approximation of M-MAE, positioning the new method as a principled generalization rather than an ad hoc modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified matrix information-theoretic framework provides a single language to reason about why contrastive, feature decorrelation, and masked image modeling methods converge to comparable performance, which was previously understood only through separate analyses. The 3.9% linear probing gain over MAE (and 3.9% over U-MAE) on ViT-Base is practically meaningful, though the fine-tuning improvement is more modest (1% on ViT-Large). Open questions include whether the TCR regularizer's benefits persist at larger scales or with different mask ratios, and whether the matrix information framework can be extended to analyze the differences (rather than shared information) between Siamese branches more rigorously — the paper's JS divergence analysis in the appendix is suggestive but preliminary.
