## Problem

The exact convex reformulation of two-layer ReLU networks with weight decay has exponentially many variables (one per hyperplane arrangement pattern), rendering it intractable. A randomized relaxation using a polynomial number of subsampled patterns was previously proposed, but the optimality gap between the relaxation and the original non-convex problem remained unknown. This paper closes that gap, proving the relative error is O(√log n) and thereby providing the first polynomial-time approximation guarantee for regularized ReLU networks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a data matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} with i.i.d. Gaussian entries (requiring n/d = c for a constant c and d sufficiently large), a label vector y ∈ R^n, a network width m = O(log n), and a weight decay parameter β. It produces network parameters (u_j, α_j) whose training loss is within a O(√log n) factor of the global optimum, with the relaxed convex program solved in O(d³m³) time via interior-point methods. The key practical distinction from the full reformulation is that only O(log n) randomly sampled hyperplane arrangement patterns are needed rather than the exponential (n/r)^r patterns of the exact convex program.

## Method

The proof proceeds in two steps. First, the unconstrained convex relaxation (variables free in R^d rather than constrained to cones) is shown to approximate the full problem within O(√log n) using matrix Chernoff bounds on the sample mean of random matrices D̃_i XX^T D̃_i, combined with a duality-based lower bound that selects a scalar multiple of y as the dual variable. Second, the cone decomposition step—recovering constrained variables u_i, v_i ∈ K_{D̃_i} from the unconstrained optimum w_i*—is shown to inflate the ℓ₂ norm by at most O(√log n), via a novel application of Gordon's comparison theorem to bound the 'cone sharpness' constant C(K, z) = min_{u,v∈K, u−v=z} ‖u‖+‖v‖. The sharpness bound exploits the fact that when n/d = c, the number of distinct cone constraints is only exponential in d (not n), so concentration arguments show each randomly sampled cone is 'not too sharp' with high probability. The resulting algorithm samples O(log n) Gaussian vectors to define hyperplane arrangement patterns, constructs the relaxed convex program, and solves it with standard interior-point solvers. Additionally, by combining the relaxation guarantee with the coupling lemma from Li & Liang (2018), the paper shows that local gradient methods with random initialization converge to stationary points whose loss is within O(√log n) of the global optimum, without requiring NTK or explicit iteration analysis.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical result (Theorem 2.1) shows that the relative optimality gap between the non-convex two-layer ReLU training problem and its randomized Gaussian convex relaxation is bounded by O(√log n), where n is the number of training samples. This yields a polynomial-time algorithm (Theorem 2.3) with O(d³m³) complexity that approximates the global optimum within a logarithmic factor, requiring only m = O(log n) neurons (hyperplane arrangement patterns) under the Gaussian data assumption (A1). Additionally, Theorem 2.5 proves that local gradient methods with random initialization converge to stationary points with O(√log n) relative training loss with high probability, provided assumption (A2) on pattern preservation holds. The constant κ in the width requirement is bounded by 20(√c+1)² for random data with n/d = c, making the width requirement practical.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no direct method-vs-method benchmark comparisons. The key comparison is against existing theoretical guarantees: prior exact algorithms for regularized two-layer ReLU networks (Pilanci & Ergen, 2020) required width m ≥ n+1 and had complexity O(d³(n/d)^(3d)), which is exponential in the data rank r. The present result achieves the same approximation with only m = O(log n) width and polynomial complexity in all dimensions, representing an exponential improvement in the required width. Compared to unregularized training hardness results (Boob et al., 2022; Goel et al., 2020), the paper shows that l₂ regularization fundamentally changes the landscape, enabling polynomial-time approximation. The only empirical verification (Figure 2) confirms assumption (A2) on MNIST and random data, showing that gradient descent preserves the majority of initial hyperplane arrangement patterns, but no quantitative accuracy or loss comparisons against other training methods are provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the exact convex reformulation of two-layer ReLU networks with weight decay from Pilanci & Ergen (2020), which requires exponentially many variables in the data rank. It adopts the cone-decomposition strategy from Mishkin et al. (2022b) to bridge the unconstrained and constrained relaxations, and leverages the stationary-point characterization of Wang et al. (2021) plus the coupling lemma of Li & Liang (2018) to transfer convex relaxation guarantees to local gradient methods. The key departure is closing the previously unknown optimality gap of the randomized (Gaussian) relaxation: prior work could solve the relaxed problem in polynomial time but had no guarantee on how close the solution was to the true non-convex optimum.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first polynomial-time approximation guarantee for regularized ReLU networks, showing that only O(log n) randomly sampled hyperplane arrangement patterns suffice to approximate the global optimum within an O(√log n) factor—an exponential improvement over the O(n) width previously needed. The result also provides a clean convex-optimization-based explanation for why local gradient methods (SGD, Adam) find good solutions, without relying on NTK or explicit iteration analysis. Key limitations include the Gaussian data assumption (A1), restriction to two-layer networks, and the residual logarithmic factor; extending to deeper architectures, CNNs, or transformers, and removing the log factor, are identified as important open problems.
