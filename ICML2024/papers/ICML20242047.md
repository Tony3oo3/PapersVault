## Problem

Pre-trained language models exhibit strong reasoning abilities without explicit fine-tuning, yet the mechanism by which next-token prediction pre-training gives rise to this capability remains poorly understood. This paper asks whether LMs can be viewed as aggregating indirect reasoning paths encountered during pre-training, and tests this hypothesis in two controlled settings: knowledge graph reasoning and chain-of-thought reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242047/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For KG reasoning, the LM is trained from scratch on random walk paths over a knowledge graph (each triple rendered as a three-token sentence with special entity/relation tokens), and at test time receives a partial triple prompt (entity, relation) to predict the missing entity as a probability distribution over all entities. For CoT reasoning, the method takes existing CoT training data (question, step-by-step solution, answer) and a pre-trained base LM, constructs a latent reasoning graph by clustering step-level hidden states, generates unlabeled random walk paths on this graph, and outputs a CoT solution with a final answer. The LM's output distribution is explicitly compared against weighted and unweighted random walk path aggregation distributions via KL divergence.

## Method

The paper formalizes reasoning paths as random walks on knowledge/reasoning graphs and hypothesizes that LMs reason by computing a weighted sum of relevant random walk path probabilities. For KG reasoning, they define a weighted aggregation distribution P_w inspired by the Path Ranking Algorithm, where each logical rule h receives a relation-dependent weight w_r(h), and prove via the log-sum inequality that KL divergence between P_w and the LM distribution is upper-bounded by the KL divergence over rule importance distributions—showing the LM learns a rule weighting scheme that is at least as good as explicit PRA. For CoT reasoning, they construct a latent reasoning graph by encoding each CoT step with the LM's hidden states, clustering similar states into nodes, and performing random walks that reorder and reconnect existing CoT steps into new unlabeled paths. Continued pre-training on these random walk paths (followed by standard SFT) consistently improves multi-step reasoning across math, multi-hop QA, and logical deduction tasks, with a characteristic optimal path length that correlates with the intrinsic reasoning length of each dataset.

## Results

The proposed random walk path augmentation method consistently improves over supervised fine-tuning (SFT) across four model sizes (Gemma 2B, Yi 6B, Llama 2 7B, Llama 2 13B) and five CoT reasoning datasets. Average accuracy gains range from +2.0 (Yi 6B: 52.6→54.6) to +2.9 (Llama 2 7B: 44.8→47.7). The largest single-dataset improvement is on StrategyQA with Llama 2 7B, where accuracy jumps from 58.4% to 63.7% (+5.3 points). On the KG reasoning side, the LM's distribution closely matches a weighted aggregation of random walk path probabilities (low KL divergence), and an optimal pre-training path length of 5–10 steps is observed across all five KG datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is standard supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with the same total number of training steps (N=2500), where the proposed method allocates the first M=500 steps to next-token prediction on unlabeled random walk paths before the remaining 2000 steps of SFT. The method wins on every dataset and every model size, with no losses observed. Gains are most pronounced on StrategyQA (multi-hop QA), where Llama 2 7B improves by +5.3 points (58.4→63.7) and Yi 6B by +4.2 points (65.8→70.0), likely because the short 3-step reasoning chains align well with the random walk structure. On GSM8K, Llama 2 13B shows the largest math gain (+4.1, 37.1→41.2). On LogicalDeduction, improvements are modest (+1.0 to +2.0), suggesting the method is most effective when the intrinsic reasoning length is short-to-moderate. The KG reasoning experiments further show that the LM's learned distribution is closer to a weighted path aggregation (P_w) than an unweighted one (P_s), and consistently outperforms both aggregation baselines in prediction accuracy, particularly on the more complex UMLS dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the classic Path Ranking Algorithm (PRA, Lao et al., 2011) for knowledge graph reasoning, reinterpreting its weighted rule aggregation as a lens through which to understand what language models learn during next-token pre-training. It positions itself within the growing line of work analyzing how pre-training data shapes LM reasoning (Bi et al., 2023; Xiao & Liu, 2023; Zhou et al., 2023; Ramesh et al., 2023), but departs from mechanistic interpretability approaches (Geiger et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2023) and in-context learning analyses by proposing a data-centric, Bayesian aggregation hypothesis. The hypothesis also echoes Prystawski et al. (2023)'s localized-structure account and Malach (2023)'s result that next-token predictors can learn from intermediate-step supervision, but extends both to more realistic reasoning tasks (KG link prediction, math word problems, multi-hop QA) with explicit random-walk path construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, testable mechanism—weighted aggregation of random walk paths—for why next-token prediction pre-training yields reasoning ability, and demonstrates that augmenting training with unlabeled random walk paths yields consistent (if modest, 1–5 point) gains across five CoT datasets and four model families. The discovery of an optimal path length, tied to the intrinsic reasoning length of each dataset, offers a practical principle for constructing pre-training sequences. However, the KG experiments rely on a small 124M GPT-2 trained from scratch, the CoT experiments use small datasets (GSM8K, AQUA, SVAMP) with LoRA fine-tuning, and the authors themselves note the method is effectively up-sampling the training set, which may amplify existing biases. A key open question is whether the random-walk augmentation strategy transfers to large-scale, diverse pre-training corpora where reasoning paths are not explicitly structured as graph walks.
