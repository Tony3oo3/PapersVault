## Problem

While the expressivity of E(n)-Equivariant Graph Neural Networks (EGNNs) has been characterized via geometric Weisfeiler-Leman tests, no theoretical generalization guarantee existed for this widely used architecture. This paper establishes the first high-probability generalization bound for EGNNs, revealing how spectral norms, network depth, and ε-normalization jointly control the generalization gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240932/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The scoring model consumes a geometric graph G=(A,C,Z) where A is the adjacency matrix, C∈R^{n×d_c} holds node features, and Z∈R^{n×d_z} holds 3D coordinates; all inputs are assumed bounded within a Euclidean ball of radius β. The model produces a scalar prediction via mean readout over node embeddings followed by a final MLP. The theoretical outputs are a generalization bound of the form O(d√L/√m · √Δ + √(log(2/δ))/√m) where Δ is a weighted sum of log-spectral-norms, and a practical regularizer R(W)=Σ w_{i,φ}·log(κ(W_i^φ)) that can be added to the training loss.

## Method

The paper derives the generalization bound through a three-step PAC-learning argument: (i) proving the EGNN scoring function is Lipschitz continuous with respect to its weight matrices (via telescoping induction over layers, leveraging ε-normalization to keep the Lipschitz constant polynomial rather than exponential in depth); (ii) showing that Cartesian products of spectral-norm-bounded weight matrix coverings yield a covering of the function class; and (iii) bounding the empirical Rademacher complexity via covering numbers and applying the standard Rademacher-to-generalization inequality. The resulting bound depends on the weighted sum of log(max{1,‖W_i‖₂}) across all weight matrices, with bottom EGNN layers receiving higher weights (L_egnn−ℓ+1) than top layers. This motivates a novel concave regularizer that explicitly minimizes the bound, which outperforms the standard average-spectral-norm regularizer (SPECAVG) on twelve QM9 regression tasks in both test MSE and generalization gap.

## Results

On QM9 molecular property prediction (4 tasks: ε_HOMO, Δε, μ, ε_LUMO), the proposed spectral norm regularizer (derived from the theoretical generalization bound) achieves the lowest test MSE and generalization gap in nearly all configurations. For example, at L_egnn=5, the method reduces test MSE from 25.42 (no regularization) to 17.39 on Δε, and cuts the generalization gap from 22.92 to 10.22. The theoretical bound correlates with the empirical generalization gap with Pearson coefficients >0.9 in 7 out of 8 settings (varying width and depth), and ε-normalization is shown to reduce generalization gaps as depth increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed log-spectral-norm regularizer (R(W) = Σ w_{i,φ} log(κ(W_i^φ))) against SPECAVG (average spectral norm across layers, following Yoshida & Miyato 2017) and an unregularized baseline. The proposed method wins on both test MSE and generalization gap in 11 out of 12 task/depth configurations; the sole exception is ε_HOMO at L=3 where SPECAVG edges out slightly on MSE (5.43 vs 5.76) and gap (3.33 vs 3.71). The advantage grows with depth: at L=7 on Δε, the proposed method reduces test MSE by 16% (23.93 vs 28.49) and the generalization gap by 34% (18.16 vs 27.46) relative to no regularization, while SPECAVG only achieves a 7% MSE reduction. SPECAVG itself provides modest gains over no regularization, confirming that spectral norm–based regularization helps, but the log-weighted variant derived from the theoretical bound is consistently superior. The computational overhead is modest: the regularized training time is approximately 1.5× the non-regularized time for the largest model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper derives the first generalization bound for E(n)-Equivariant Graph Neural Networks (EGNNs), filling a gap left by the expressivity analysis of Joshi et al. (2023) which characterized EGNNs via a geometric Weisfeiler-Leman test but left generalization open. Methodologically, it follows the Rademacher-complexity pathway used for MLPs (Bartlett et al., 2017; Neyshabur et al., 2018) and message-passing GNNs (Garg et al., 2020), but departs from the PAC-Bayesian equivariant bound of Behboodi et al. (2022) by obtaining a sum-of-logarithms dependence on spectral norms rather than a product, and by revealing that ε-normalization (present in the original EGNN code but never discussed theoretically) is essential for polynomial rather than exponential depth dependence. The proposed regularizer directly instantiates the derived bound, positioning it as a theory-driven alternative to the commonly used sum-of-spectral-norms penalty (Yoshida & Miyato, 2017).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EGNNs are among the most widely deployed architectures for 3D molecular and geometric graph learning, yet practitioners had no theoretical guidance on how model depth, width, or weight magnitudes affect generalization; this work provides that first principled account. The ε-normalization finding is practically actionable: it explains why the original authors' implementation (with ε=1) outperformed the unnormalized variant and suggests that deeper EGNNs should always use it. Key limitations include the bound's still-loose constants, the acknowledgment that spectral norms depend on training set size in a way that cannot be decoupled post-hoc, and experiments restricted to QM9 (small organic molecules). Open questions include whether the logarithmic spectral-norm dependence extends to other equivariant architectures (e.g., SE(3)-Transformers, Tensor Field Networks), whether the bound is tight, and how the equivariant coordinate update (Equation 2) quantitatively affects generalization relative to plain message-passing GNNs.
