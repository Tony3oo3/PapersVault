## Problem

Most open-source language models fail to precisely retrieve information at arbitrary positions within 128K-token contexts, and existing approaches to extend context length either require prohibitively expensive continual pretraining (400B+ tokens) or rely on suboptimal data strategies such as domain-specific upsampling. This paper asks: what is the minimal data recipe—both in quantity and composition—that enables a model pretrained on 4K context to pass the Needle-in-a-Haystack test at 128K through lightweight continual pretraining?

## Input / Output

The model receives a long document (up to 128K tokens) with a short 'needle' sentence inserted at an arbitrary position (1K–128K) and must verbatim recite that sentence, testing precise retrieval at every depth. For the BookQA evaluation, the input is a full book-length passage and the output is a plot-related answer. Training uses 80K context for the 7B model and 64K for the 13B model, so the model must generalize from its training length to the full 128K test length without additional fine-tuning.

## Method

The core contribution is a per-source length upsampling data recipe: starting from SlimPajama (which mirrors the LLaMA pretraining mixture), the authors keep each domain's proportion fixed while upsampling sequences longer than 4K from ~30% to ~70% within every domain, then pack all data into 80K/64K chunks. The only architectural modification is adjusting the RoPE base for longer contexts; the full model is then continually pretrained for 1–5B tokens at a constant learning rate of 2e-5. The key insight is that precise retrieval over long ranges is largely an intrinsic capability already acquired during large-scale pretraining, so only ~500M tokens are needed to 'unlock' it and ~5B tokens to saturate length generalization to 128K. Per-source upsampling outperforms global upsampling and domain-specific upsampling (e.g., books or code) because it avoids perplexity degradation in other domains, as evidenced by balanced per-domain loss differences in Table 5.

## Results

On the Needle-in-a-Haystack benchmark, the proposed per-source length-upsampled continual pretraining achieves 88.0% retrieval accuracy for LLaMA-2 7B (trained at 80K context) and 90.0% for LLaMA-2 13B (trained at 64K), surpassing GPT-4 Turbo 128K (87.1%) at the 7B scale and substantially outperforming all open-source baselines. Short-context performance is preserved, with MMLU scores of 43.3 (7B) and 52.4 (13B), comparable to the base LLaMA-2 models. On the InfiniBench BookQA task at 128K, the 13B model reaches 31.1, closing the gap to GPT-4 Turbo (37.4) while outperforming LongLoRA 13B (24.6) and YaRN Mistral 7B (26.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against YaRN Mistral 7B 128K, the proposed method wins decisively on Needle-in-a-Haystack (88.0 vs. 57.4 for 7B; 90.0 vs. 57.4 for 13B), attributed to YaRN's reliance on PG19 book data without length upsampling, though YaRN retains a higher MMLU score (59.4) due to its stronger Mistral base model. Against LongLoRA 100K, the method achieves a large margin on retrieval (88.0 vs. 70.0 at 7B; 90.0 vs. 54.1 at 13B) while also improving MMLU (43.3 vs. 37.9 at 7B; 52.4 vs. 50.1 at 13B), which the authors attribute to LongLoRA's use of sparse attention and absence of length upsampling. Against LongChat v1.5 32K, the gap is the widest (88.0 vs. 18.0), with the paper hypothesizing that LongChat's dialog-only data mixture and direct jump to finetuning without sufficient continual pretraining limit its long-context capability. On the downstream BookQA task, the 13B model (31.1) outperforms both LongLoRA 13B (24.6) and YaRN Mistral 7B (26.3), confirming that retrieval gains transfer to real-world long-document understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of context extension (Xiong et al. 2023, XVERSE) that treats long-context modeling as a new capability requiring hundreds of billions of tokens of continual pretraining. Instead, it builds on the RoPE base adjustment from Xiong et al. (2023) and the positional/attention innovations of YaRN and LongLoRA, but reframes the problem as a data-engineering question: the retrieval capability is already latent in 4K-pretrained models and can be unlocked with 1–5B tokens of appropriately mixed data. The key methodological departure from LongChat, Together, and LongLoRA is the per-source length upsampling strategy, which preserves the original domain mixture while increasing the proportion of long sequences within each domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that 128K context is achievable on 8×80G A100s in ~5 days (roughly 1% of prior compute budgets), this work makes long-context research accessible at academic scale and provides a concrete, reproducible data recipe. The finding that domain-balanced per-source upsampling outperforms the intuitive strategy of upsampling long-document domains (books, code) has practical implications for any future context-extension effort. Open questions remain: the recipe is validated only on LLaMA-2 7B/13B with SlimPajama, no instruction-tuned 100K+ models exist yet, and it is unclear whether RNN-like architectures (Mamba, RetNet) can generalize zero-shot to longer contexts without any continual pretraining.
