## Problem

Large-scale pre-training of neural operators for PDEs remains in its infancy because PDE datasets exhibit extreme heterogeneity in dimensions, temporal lengths, resolutions, and geometric configurations, and because existing pre-training strategies (contrastive learning, auto-regressive vision transformers) lack the flexibility and scalability to handle this diversity. No prior work has demonstrated a PDE foundation model that scales to billions of parameters while generalizing across more than ten distinct PDE systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240703/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes T=10 consecutive spatiotemporal frames (each H×W×C, unified to 128×128 resolution with channel padding and an optional geometry mask channel for irregular meshes) with Gaussian noise injected at a small scale (ε≈5e-5), and outputs the next timestep's PDE solution field. During fine-tuning, the same architecture adapts to different dimensionalities (e.g., 2D→3D by swapping 2D FFT for 3D FFT) and to steady-state single-step operator learning tasks.

## Method

DPOT combines a novel auto-regressive denoising pre-training objective with a scalable Fourier-attention transformer architecture. The training loss predicts the next frame from noise-corrupted previous T frames, which regularizes the model and closes the train-test distribution gap without the complexity of pushforward tricks. The architecture processes inputs through patchification, a temporal aggregation layer that compresses T frames via learnable Fourier features, and a stack of multi-head Fourier attention layers that apply a shared MLP in the frequency domain (FFT → MLP → IFFT) to approximate kernel integral transforms without the sparsity constraints of AFNO. A balanced sampling scheme equalizes gradient contributions across heterogeneous PDE datasets, and the authors prove a universal approximation theorem showing Fourier attention layers can approximate any continuous operator between Sobolev spaces on the torus.

## Results

On the main pre-training benchmarks (14 subsets across FNO, PDEBench, PDEArena, CFDBench), DPOT-S (30M) pre-trained achieves SOTA zero-shot L2RE on 9/12 datasets, with a 52% error reduction on FNO-1e-4 (0.0442 vs. 0.0922 for FNO-m). The largest model DPOT-H (1.03B) further reduces zero-shot L2RE to 0.0174 (FNO-1e-5), 0.0131 (FNO-1e-4), and 0.00229 (FNO-1e-3). After fine-tuning for 500 epochs, DPOT-L (500M) achieves SOTA on 9/12 tasks, reducing L2RE by over 50% on 8/12 tasks compared to all zero-shot baselines (e.g., 0.0104 on FNO-1e-4, 0.00323 on FNO-1e-3). On downstream tasks, DPOT-FT reduces 3D Navier-Stokes error from 41.0% to 22.6% and steady-state CNO L1-Median from 0.0357 to 0.0230.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPOT outperforms all non-pre-trained baselines (FNO, UNet, FFNO, GK-Transformer, GNOT, OFormer) on the majority of 14 subsets, with the largest margins on FNO-1e-4 (0.0442 vs. 0.0503 for FFNO, 0.0922 for FNO-m) and PDEArena-NS (0.0999 vs. 0.319–0.602). Against the closest pre-trained competitor MPP (McCabe et al., 2023), DPOT-S (30M) beats MPP-S on PDB-M1 (0.0285 vs. 0.0319) and PDB-SWE (0.00560 vs. 0.0024), while MPP retains an edge on PDB-DR (0.0112 vs. 0.0321) and PDB-SWE at the 30M scale; however, DPOT-H (1B) reverses this on PDB-DR (0.0191 vs. 0.0098 for MPP-L). The Fourier mixer in DPOT is shown to be superior to AFNO's sparse frequency-domain mixer (Table 8: test L2RE 0.0174 vs. 0.121) and to standard self-attention (Table 14: 0.0174 vs. 0.0238 with fewer parameters and FLOPs). On downstream transfer tasks (Table 2), DPOT-FT beats (Geo-)FNO on turbulence (0.135 vs. 0.193), 3D Navier-Stokes (0.226 vs. 0.410), and steady-state CNO (0.0230 vs. 0.0357), and also outperforms MPP-FT on turbulence (0.135 vs. 0.152). The only consistent loss is on PDB-DR where MPP-L (400M) achieves 0.0098 vs. DPOT-L's 0.0232, which the authors acknowledge as room for improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

DPOT builds directly on AFNO's Fourier-mixer architecture (FFT → MLP → IFFT) but removes the soft-thresholding sparsity constraint, adds a multi-head structure, and introduces a novel temporal aggregation layer that compresses adjacent frames via a learnable Fourier-weighted sum. On the training side, it extends MPP's auto-regressive PDE pre-training by injecting Gaussian noise into input frames (denoising), which regularizes the model and reduces the train-test distribution gap for long trajectories. The paper also departs from prior PDE pre-training efforts (Mialon et al.'s contrastive learning, Subramanian et al.'s unified steady-state PDE) by handling 10+ heterogeneous datasets with varying dimensions, resolutions, and geometries through a unified patchification + balanced-sampling pipeline, scaling to 1B parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic demonstration that PDE operator learning follows scaling laws, with a 1B-parameter foundation model trained on 100k+ trajectories across 12 datasets, establishing a concrete recipe for large-scale PDE pre-training. The cross-dimensional transfer (2D pre-training → 3D Navier-Stokes, 41%→22.6% error) and the Kolmogorov turbulence result (82%→33%) suggest the learned representations capture general fluid-dynamics structure, not just dataset-specific patterns. Open questions include why MPP-L still wins on PDB-DP, the slight CFDBench regression at 1B, and whether the denoising strategy generalizes to non-autonomous or stochastic PDEs. The paper also honestly notes interpretability and safety concerns for deployment in risk-sensitive physical systems.
