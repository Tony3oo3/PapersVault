## Problem

Existing few-shot semantic segmentation methods propagate category information unidirectionally from support to query, treating the support as an idealized category representation and ignoring intra-class diversity between support and query. Even methods that aggregate both into an intermediate representation still derive support information from the original, unmodified support feature, leaving the intra-class gap unresolved and the resulting category information sub-optimal for the query.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241253/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes K support image-mask pairs and one query image (all RGB, randomly cropped to 473×473) under a meta-learning episodic protocol, and produces a pixel-wise binary segmentation mask for the query. Evaluation is conducted in 1-shot and 5-shot settings across four folds on PASCAL-5i (20 classes) and COCO-20i (80 classes), with the backbone (VGG-16 or ResNet-50) frozen and only the FRL parameters trained via SGD for 200 or 50 epochs respectively.

## Method

IFRNet establishes a bidirectional reciprocative information flow (query → support → query → …) through a Feedback and Rectification Layer (FRL) stacked L times. Each FRL contains three components: (1) a Query Feedback Branch that uses a query prototype to activate the support feature and applies Query-Related Mask Pooling (merging the network's support prediction with the ground-truth support mask) to extract a query-adapted support prototype; (2) a Query Amplifier Branch that first self-activates the query with its own prototype (with deformable self-attention) and then re-activates it with the query-related support prototype, completing the bidirectional cycle; and (3) a Query Rectification Module that compares the self-activated and support-activated predictions to identify hard positive regions (supplement prototype) and hard negative regions (exclusion prototype), using them to re-activate and suppress ambiguous query areas. The total loss is a weighted sum of binary cross-entropy over the final prediction and all intermediate layer predictions.

## Results

On PASCAL-5i, IFRNet achieves 70.7% mean mIoU (1-shot) and 72.1% (5-shot) with ResNet-50, surpassing the previous best (MSI, 68.3%/71.1%) by 2.4% and 1.0% respectively. With the BAM ensemble module to remove base-class influence, IFRNet* reaches 72.1% (1-shot) and 74.5% (5-shot) with ResNet-50. On COCO-20i, IFRNet attains 47.0% (1-shot) and 52.3% (5-shot) with ResNet-50, while IFRNet* achieves 48.4% and 54.1%, outperforming the strongest baselines (MIANet*, InPNet*) by 0.8–1.7% in the 1-shot setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On PASCAL-5i with ResNet-50, IFRNet (70.7% 1-shot, 72.1% 5-shot) outperforms all non-ensemble baselines including MSI (68.3%/71.1%), SCCAN (67.0%/70.3%), IPMT (66.8%/68.2%), and DCAMA (64.6%/68.5%). Among ensemble-based methods, IFRNet* (72.1%/74.5%) surpasses HDMNet* (69.4%/71.8%), MIANet* (68.7%/71.6%), and InPNet* (68.7%/72.5%) by 2.7–3.4% in 1-shot and 2.0–2.7% in 5-shot. On COCO-20i with ResNet-50, IFRNet (47.0% 1-shot) slightly trails MIANet* (47.6%) and InPNet* (47.5%) without ensemble, but IFRNet* (48.4%) reverses this gap, leading by 0.8–0.9%. In the 5-shot COCO-20i setting, IFRNet (52.3%) and IFRNet* (54.1%) both exceed MSI (52.0%), MIANet* (51.7%), and InPNet* (52.4%). With VGG-16 on PASCAL-5i, IFRNet (67.8% 1-shot) leads MIANet* (67.1%) and HDMNet* (65.1%), while on COCO-20i VGG-16, IFRNet (43.3%) trails MIANet* (45.7%) but IFRNet* (46.1%) recovers the lead.

## Relation to Prior Work

IFRNet departs from the dominant one-way 'support → query' information flow in FSS (PFENet, DPCN, etc.) and from the simultaneous 'support + query → intermediate' aggregation used by IPMT and related methods. Drawing on cybernetics, it introduces an iterative bidirectional reciprocative flow (query → support → query → …) where the support prototype is dynamically reshaped by query feedback before being sent back, rather than being a static or one-shot fused representation. The Query Amplifier Branch borrows deformable self-attention from the cycle-consistent transformer (Zhang et al., 2021b), and the ensemble-based base-class removal follows BAM (Lang et al., 2022) for fair comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bidirectional feedback mechanism offers a principled way to reduce intra-class diversity without requiring additional labeled data or complex multi-prototype schemes, and the Query Rectification Module provides a lightweight self-correction signal from prediction disparity. However, the paper acknowledges that stacking beyond two FRL layers yields diminishing returns, suggesting the feedback loop has limited depth of refinement. Open questions include whether this iterative feedback paradigm generalizes to transformer-based backbones, video segmentation, or other few-shot tasks, and whether the reliance on a ground-truth support mask for Query-Related Mask Pooling limits applicability in fully unsupervised or open-vocabulary settings.
