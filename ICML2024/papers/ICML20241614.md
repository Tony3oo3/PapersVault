## Problem

Current state-space models face a fundamental trade-off: diagonal/modal parametrizations (S4, S5, LRU) limit expressivity for a fixed state dimension, while parallel scan-based inference incurs O(ℓn) memory that scales prohibitively with state size. Even S4's fast Cauchy/Vandermonde algorithms achieve only O((ℓ+n)log²(ℓ+n)) kernel computation, bottlenecking the O(ℓlogℓ) convolution. The paper asks whether a complete (non-diagonal) SSM representation can admit a parallel inference algorithm whose cost is entirely independent of state size.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a length-ℓ, d-channel input sequence (text tokens, image raster scans, or continuous signals) and produces a same-length output sequence via causal convolution, where each output at time t depends only on inputs up to t. The SSM operates in a multi-SISO fashion: each channel runs an independent single-input single-output rational transfer function, and channel mixing is applied afterwards via a linear projection. For language modeling the output is a next-token distribution; for LRA tasks it is a class label over the full sequence.

## Method

The core contribution is parametrizing SSMs by their rational transfer function (RTF) — a pair of numerator and denominator polynomials (a, b) plus a feedforward term h₀ — rather than by state-space matrices (A, B, C). This representation is complete (it spans all LTI SSMs including dense-matrix ones) yet requires only 2n+1 parameters, and is invariant under any invertible change of state variables. The key algorithmic insight is that evaluating the truncated transfer function at the ℓ-th roots of unity is equivalent to taking the FFT of the zero-padded polynomial coefficients, so the convolutional kernel's spectrum is obtained via a single FFT pair with O(ℓ) space and O(ℓlogℓ) time, entirely independent of state size n. For autoregressive inference, the RTF maps to a companion-form recurrence requiring only O(n) per step (two dot products and a shift). Stability is enforced through a zero-initialization scheme that minimizes the Montel constraint on denominator coefficients, avoiding the over-constraining that limits SpaceTime's expressivity.

## Results

On the Long Range Arena benchmark, RTF achieves an average accuracy of 87.71%, the best among attention-free models, outperforming S4 (86.69%), S4D (86.72%), S5 (87.46%), and SpaceTime (70.32%). On WikiText-103 language modeling, Hyena-RTF achieves a perplexity of 18.0, improving over Hyena-S5 (18.3), Hyena (18.5), and Transformer (18.6). On synthetic memorization tasks, RTF reaches 100% Copying accuracy at state sizes ≥256 (vs. ~33% for S4) and achieves the best Delay RMSE of 0.006 at n=1024 (vs. 0.029 for S4). RTF also delivers a 35% average training speed improvement over S4 layers across LRA tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RTF outperforms all attention-free SSM baselines on the LRA average: S4 (86.69%), S4D (86.72%), S5 (87.46%), Liquid-S4 (87.32%), H3 (84.8%), and SpaceTime (70.32%), while trailing only the O(ℓ²) Mega model (88.21%) and self-pretrained Transformers (81.81% — actually RTF wins here). On WikiText-103, Hyena-RTF (18.0 ppl) beats Hyena-S5 (18.3), Hyena (18.5), Hybrid H3 (18.5), and Transformer (18.6). On synthetic memorization, RTF dramatically surpasses S4 on Copying at state sizes ≥128 (93.3–100% vs. 33–35%) and matches or exceeds S4 on Delay at all state sizes. In the Laughing Hyena distillation setting, RTF achieves lower distillation MSE than the modal SSM (LH) at n=4 (0.018 vs. 0.032) but higher MSE at n=16 and n=64, yet still delivers comparable or better downstream evaluation scores (e.g., Winogrande 53.43 vs. 49.57 at n=64). RTF's key efficiency advantage is that its parallel inference latency remains constant regardless of state size, unlike S5 (scan-based) whose memory and latency scale linearly with state size, and unlike S4/S4D whose Cauchy/Vandermonde products add O((ℓ+n)log²(ℓ+n)) overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reparametrizes state-space models (SSMs) from the time domain (A, B, C matrices) to their dual frequency-domain representation, the rational transfer function (RTF), with numerator and denominator polynomial coefficients as learnable parameters. It builds directly on the S4/S4D lineage of structured SSMs and the Hyena/MultiHyena convolutional framework, while departing from diagonal or low-rank A-matrix restrictions by using the complete rational function that fully captures any LTI SSM's functional space. The companion-form recurrence and distillation framework are inherited from Laughing Hyena (Massaroli et al., 2023), and the discrete state-space setting extends SpaceTime (Zhang et al., 2023) by training the full numerator rather than a shared A with fixed B.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The state-free parallel inference algorithm (O(ℓ) space, O(ℓ log ℓ) time independent of state size n) is a practically significant simplification over both scan-based (O(ℓn) memory) and Cauchy/Vandermonde-based (O((ℓ+n)log²(ℓ+n))) approaches, relying solely on widely optimized FFT kernels. The complete representation theoretically subsumes diagonal SSMs, but the paper's own distillation experiments reveal that the modal form still has more favorable training dynamics at moderate state sizes, suggesting the expressivity advantage may not always translate to practical gains. Open questions include whether RTF's full expressivity advantage materializes at larger scales, how it compares to selective/input-dependent SSMs like Mamba, and whether the SISO-per-channel design limits performance relative to true MIMO formulations.
