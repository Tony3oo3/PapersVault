## Problem

Diffusion-based image compression methods achieve strong perceptual quality but fail to guarantee distortion fidelity, as the progressive denoising process inherently trades reconstruction accuracy for visual detail. The paper addresses how to correct the diffusion decoder's sampling process using privileged information available only at the encoder side, achieving a better distortion-perception trade-off without substantially increasing bit rate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241387/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a full-resolution RGB image (padded to multiples of 64×64 at inference) and produces a compressed bitstream consisting of the quantized latent representation ŷ (arithmetically coded via an entropy model) plus T=8 scalar linear factors γ*_t transmitted as 16-bit floats (128 bits total). At the decoder side, the bitstream is decoded and fed into an 8-step DDIM sampling loop with a corrected score function to reconstruct the image. Training uses 256×256 patches from DIV2K.

## Method

The key insight is that the original image x_0* is visible at the encoder side, allowing a theoretical decomposition of the conditioned score function into a standard score-network estimate plus a correction term. This correction is approximated by linearly combining the score network's pseudo noise-free estimate x̂_{0,t} with an end-to-end convolutional decoder's output x̂_{0,e}=D(ŷ), where the mixing coefficient γ*_t is found by minimizing a perceptual metric (LPIPS) between the combination and the original image. Only the scalar γ*_t values (128 bits total for 8 DDIM steps) are transmitted as privileged information, making the correction extremely cheap. The framework is built on ELIC's encoder, entropy model, and decoder, with an ADM-based score network trained in two phases: first the score network alone with MSE + DISTS loss, then all components jointly with additional rate and LPIPS losses.

## Results

CorrDiff is evaluated via R-D curves across 9 metrics (PSNR, VIF, GMSD, LPIPS-Alex, LPIPS-VGG, DISTS, PIEAPP, FID) on CLIC professional, Kodak, and DIV2K-test. The method uses 8 DDIM sampling steps and transmits only 128 bits (8 × 16-bit float16) for the correction factors γ_t*, achieving general superiority on diverse perceptual metrics while simultaneously improving distortion over other perceptual compression methods. The full model has 73.79M parameters and is trained on DIV2K (800 images, 256×256 crops) over 800K total iterations in two phases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BPG (classical) and ELIC (MSE-oriented DL), CorrDiff clearly dominates on all perceptual metrics (LPIPS, DISTS, PIEAPP) while remaining competitive on distortion. Against HiFiC (GAN-based perceptual compression), CorrDiff achieves better distortion (PSNR, GMSD) and comparable or better perceptual quality. Against ILLM (ICML 2023, SOTA perceptual compression), CorrDiff outperforms on all metrics except FID, where ILLM is competitive because FID is explicitly modeled during its training. Against CDC (NeurIPS 2023, the only other diffusion-based method), CorrDiff substantially outperforms on distortion metrics (PSNR, GMSD, VIF) and most perceptual metrics, while CDC retains an edge only on LPIPS-VGG because that specific metric is part of CDC's training objective. The ablation (Table 1) confirms that removing the end-to-end decoder correction degrades distortion by +28.8% BD rate, and removing the diffusion component degrades perception by +22.2% BD rate, validating that both components are necessary for the joint distortion-perception improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based image compression paradigm introduced by CDC (Yang & Mandt, NeurIPS 2023) by introducing a privileged end-to-end decoder that corrects the diffusion sampling process. It builds directly on ELIC (He et al., CVPR 2022) for the encoder, entropy model, and end-to-end decoder architecture, and on the ADM score network design. The key conceptual departure from CDC is the theoretical analysis of the score function approximation error when original images are visible at the encoder side, leading to a correction mechanism that transmits only per-timestep linear interpolation factors (128 bits total) rather than full-image correction signals. This positions the work at the intersection of the rate-distortion-perception tradeoff literature and the broader line of perceptual compression methods (HiFiC, ILLM, PO-ELIC).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental limitation of diffusion-based compression—excellent perceptual quality at the cost of poor distortion fidelity—by showing that a minimal correction (one scalar per timestep) can simultaneously improve both metrics. This opens a practical path for deploying diffusion models in bandwidth-constrained applications where both visual appeal and fidelity matter. Limitations include training only on 256×256 patches, reliance on a well-trained end-to-end decoder for the correction to work, and the linear (single-scalar) nature of the correction which may be insufficient for complex scenes. Open questions include scalability to higher resolutions, compatibility with latent diffusion models, and whether richer correction signals (e.g., per-channel or spatially-varying factors) could further close the distortion gap.
