## Problem

Classical sparse Bayesian learning (SBL) algorithms for maximizing the marginal likelihood function lack rigorous global convergence guarantees: MacKay updates and EM can get trapped in fixed points, variational inference relies on mean-field approximations, and iteratively reweighted ℓ₁ methods require the noise level to be known a priori. No existing SBL algorithm provides a globally convergent procedure that simultaneously estimates both the prior hyperparameters γ and the noise variance λ.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242084/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an overcomplete dictionary matrix Φ ∈ ℝ^{n×m} (with n < m, composed of basis functions such as polynomials, kernel functions, or identity columns), an output vector y ∈ ℝ^n, and an input vector x used to construct Φ. It outputs a sparse weight vector w (the posterior mean μ) with most components driven to zero, along with the estimated hyperparameter vector γ ∈ ℝ^m and scalar noise variance λ. The sparsity is enforced through a hierarchical Student's t prior (Gaussian prior with Inverse-Gamma hyperpriors), and the algorithm requires only initial values for γ and λ—no a priori knowledge of the noise level is needed.

## Method

The paper decomposes the negative log marginal likelihood L(γ,λ) into a strictly concave part L₁ and a strictly convex part L₂, then applies the concave-convex procedure (CCCP) to linearize L₁ via an auxiliary variable z. The critical innovation is introducing a second latent variable θ to re-express the data-dependent term y^T Π^{-1} y as min_θ (‖y−Φθ‖²/λ + θ^T Γ^{-1} θ), which decouples the highly coupled γ and λ. This yields an iterative Min-Min scheme: at each iteration, z and θ are updated analytically given (γ,λ), then γ and λ are updated analytically given (z,θ) via closed-form square-root expressions. The paper proves global convergence to a local minimum or saddle point of L(γ,λ) for any starting point using the Global Convergence Theorem, and shows the method is equivalent to iteratively reweighted ℓ₂ minimization with principled, automatically updated regularization and weighting parameters—addressing a gap where prior reweighting schemes lacked a principled update rule for the regularization parameter.

## Results

On the Gaussian-dictionary sparse signal recovery benchmark (n=60, m=100, k=4), the proposed Min-Min SBL achieves 100% recovery for ±1 spike signals at 35 dB SNR, versus 48% (MacKay), 7% (EM), 1% (IR), and 0% (VI). At lower SNRs (≤25 dB), only the proposed method yields non-zero recovery rates across all three signal distributions. On the Red Wine Quality kernel regression task, the proposed method achieves the lowest sparsity-error distance d (0.0951–0.0980) across four kernels, compared to 0.0952–0.4329 (MacKay), 0.9939–1.0048 (EM), 0.1004–0.1058 (IR), and 0.1270–0.1561 (VI).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed Min-Min SBL dominates all four classical SBL baselines (MacKay updates, EM, iteratively reweighted ℓ₁, and variational inference) across every experimental setting. In sparse signal recovery (Tables 1–2), the gap is most dramatic at low-to-mid SNR: at 10 dB the proposed method recovers 39–82% of signals while all baselines remain at 0%, and even at 35 dB it leads MacKay_SBL by 46–52 percentage points on spike and Gaussian signals. In the Lorenz system identification task (Figure 2), IR_SBL and VI_SBL never recover the correct equations at any noise level, while the proposed method maintains near-perfect discovery up to substantially higher noise than MacKay or EM. On the Red Wine Quality kernel regression (Table 3), the proposed method achieves the best sparsity-error distance d on three of four kernels (Exponential: 0.0951 vs. 0.1006 for IR; Matérn-3/2: 0.0957 vs. 0.1006; Gaussian: 0.0980 vs. 0.1004), with MacKay_SBL narrowly winning only on the Linear kernel (0.0952 vs. 0.0970). In the AMP comparison (Appendix B, Table 4), the proposed method slightly outperforms AMP on Gaussian matrices at high SNR but is dramatically superior on low-rank dictionaries (e.g., 59% vs. 2% at 35 dB), confirming that its advantage is not limited to i.i.d. Gaussian settings where AMP is theoretically strongest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CCCP-based optimization framework for sparse Bayesian learning (building on Wipf & Nagarajan's iteratively reweighted ℓ₁/ℓ₂ approaches) by introducing a second latent variable to decouple the highly coupled noise variance λ and prior hyperparameters γ in the marginal likelihood function. It departs from prior SBL optimization methods (MacKay updates, EM, variational inference, and iteratively reweighted ℓ₁) by providing the first globally convergent algorithm that simultaneously estimates both the prior and noise hyperparameters without fixing λ a priori. The paper also establishes a formal equivalence between its iterative Min-Min procedure and iteratively reweighted ℓ₂ minimization, supplying principled update rules for the regularization and weighting parameters that were previously heuristic in the reweighting literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The global convergence guarantee fills a notable theoretical gap in SBL, giving practitioners confidence that the algorithm will not get trapped in non-stationary fixed points as EM or MacKay updates can. The principled update rules for regularization and weighting parameters in the iteratively reweighted ℓ₂ interpretation offer a reusable design principle beyond SBL. However, the O(n³) per-iteration cost from matrix inversions limits applicability to large-scale problems, and the paper acknowledges this as the primary open challenge. Future work on reducing computational complexity (e.g., via Woodbury identities or stochastic approximations) would be needed to make the method competitive with AMP-style approaches on very large datasets.
