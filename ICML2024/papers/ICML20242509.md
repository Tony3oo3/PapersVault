## Problem

Iterative retrieval-augmented language models (RaLMs) interleave retrieval and generation at every decoding step, issuing sequential queries to an external knowledge base that dominate end-to-end latency. While one-shot RaLM avoids this overhead, it cannot adapt retrieved context as generation progresses, sacrificing quality. The paper asks whether the retrieval overhead of iterative RaLM can be reduced without altering the model's output distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242509/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

RaLMSpec takes a text prompt (up to 512 tokens), an external corpus (Wikipedia for QA tasks, WikiText-103 for KNN-LM), and a pre-trained language model (GPT-2, OPT, LLaMA-2, or a 247M-parameter KNN-LM base), and produces a generated response (up to 128 tokens) that is provably identical to the sequential baseline. The framework is retriever-agnostic, operating over exact dense (DPR/FAISS), approximate dense (DPR-HNSW), and sparse (BM25/Pyserini) retrievers, and supports both document-level iterative RaLM (where retrieved chunks are prepended to the prompt) and token-level KNN-LM (where k nearest-neighbor token distributions are interpolated with the LM output).

## Method

RaLMSpec replaces sequential knowledge-base retrievals with speculative retrievals from a per-request local cache, exploiting the temporal and spatial locality of retrieved documents (the same or consecutive entries recur across decoding steps). After s speculative steps, a single batched retrieval from the knowledge base verifies all s speculated documents at once; if a mismatch is detected, generation rolls back to the first mismatched position and re-decodes with the ground-truth document, guaranteeing output equivalence. Three additional optimizations further reduce latency: (1) cache prefetching populates the local cache with top-k documents per verification step to raise speculation accuracy, (2) the Optimal Speculation Stride Scheduler (OS³) adaptively adjusts s by maximizing expected verified documents per unit time using online estimates of speculation accuracy γ(X), speculation latency a, and verification latency b, and (3) asynchronous verification overlaps a verification step with an extra speculation step, hiding verification latency when it is shorter than the LM decoding latency.

## Results

For document-level iterative RaLM serving, RaLMSpec+PSA (with prefetching, optimal speculation stride scheduler, and asynchronous verification) reduces serving latency by 1.75–2.39× with an exact dense retriever (EDR), 1.04–1.39× with an approximate dense retriever (ADR), and 1.31–1.77× with a sparse retriever (SR), evaluated across GPT-2, OPT-1.3B, and LLaMA-2-7B on four QA datasets. For token-level iterative RaLM (KNN-LM) serving, RaLMSpec achieves up to 7.59× speedup with EDR and 2.45× with ADR, even reaching 3.88× and 2.37× respectively at k=1024. The speedup is most pronounced when retrieval dominates end-to-end latency (EDR), and diminishes when language model decoding is the bottleneck (ADR, SR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the RaLMSeq baseline (following Ram et al., 2023), RaLMSpec+PSA consistently wins across all model-retriever combinations, with the largest gains (up to 2.39×) when the exact dense retriever dominates latency. Against the KNN-LM baseline (Khandelwal et al., 2019), RaLMSpec achieves up to 7.59× speedup with EDR and 2.45× with ADR, demonstrating that the speculative retrieval framework generalizes to token-level retrieval workloads. On the iter-retgen workload (Shao et al., 2023), RaLMSpec+PSA achieves 1.68× (EDR), 1.08× (ADR), and 1.16× (SR) speedup on LLaMA-2-7B over Wiki-QA. On the FLARE workload (Jiang et al., 2023), RaLMSpec+PSA achieves 1.41× (EDR), 1.07× (ADR), and 1.07× (SR) speedup under the same setup. Notably, without the optimal speculation stride scheduler (OS³), RaLMSpec can actually be slower than the baseline for ADR (0.58–0.62×), highlighting that the adaptive stride is critical for fast retrievers where speculation overhead can outweigh retrieval savings.

## Relation to Prior Work

RaLMSpec adapts the speculate-verify-rollback pattern from computer architecture (Burton, 1985) and recent speculative decoding work for LLMs (Leviathan et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2023) to a new target: the retrieval step in iterative RaLM serving. The most directly relevant prior work is Alon et al. (2022), which reduces KNN-LM retrieval calls via pre-computed automaton states but does not guarantee identical model output. RaLMSpec positions itself as the first framework to achieve lossless (provably output-preserving) inference-time speed-up for generic iterative RaLM approaches, and is explicitly orthogonal to speculative decoding techniques that accelerate the language model itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As iterative RaLM systems (FLARE, Iter-RetGen, KNN-LM) become the standard for knowledge-intensive generation, retrieval overhead is a first-order serving cost; RaLMSpec provides a drop-in, model-agnostic acceleration layer that works across sparse, exact dense, and approximate dense retrievers without retraining. Key limitations include: speed-up is fundamentally bounded by the retrieval-to-total-latency ratio (hence modest gains for ADR/SR where LM decoding dominates), asynchronous verification is only simulated due to Python's GIL, and the KNN-LM adaptation relaxes the verification criterion (matching the decoded token rather than all k retrieved entries), which may not generalize to all token-level settings. Open questions include composition with speculative decoding, applicability to multi-turn or streaming RaLM, and whether the locality assumption holds for very diverse or adversarial query distributions.
