## Problem

Existing uncertainty estimation (UE) methods assume fully-labeled training data, yet many practical tasks such as Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) provide only weak bag-level supervision with no instance-level labels. This paper is the first to formally study Multi-Instance Uncertainty Estimation (MIUE), which requires quantifying predictive uncertainty at both bag and instance levels under this weakly-supervised setting, where standard Bayesian or evidential approaches cannot be directly applied.

## Input / Output

The method takes a variable-size bag of instance embeddings (e.g., 512-d feature vectors from 256×256 histopathology patches, or 28×28 grayscale digits) along with a single binary bag label as the only supervision signal. It produces two Dirichlet distributions — a bag-level posterior Dir(μ|α_bag) and an instance-level posterior Dir(ν|α_ins) — from which closed-form uncertainty measures (max α, α₀, expected entropy, mutual information) are computed. The instance-level estimator R(x) is a residual correction on top of the shared bag scoring function, so a single forward pass yields both bag and instance uncertainty estimates simultaneously.

## Method

The key insight, derived from the Fundamental Theorem of Symmetric Functions, is that a good instance estimator T = g∘f can be obtained directly from a good bag classifier S(X) = g(Σf(x_k)) by skipping the permutation-invariant pooling, eliminating reliance on attention scores as instance proxies. To compensate for the fact that g_φ is trained on pooled embeddings rather than raw ones, the paper introduces a residual network r_π(h) so that the instance estimator becomes R(x) = g_φ(f_ψ(x)) + r_π(f_ψ(x)), with independent parameters π. For training under weak supervision, positive bags use a weighted evidence aggregation strategy where instance evidences are multiplied by normalized probabilities predicted by T and summed into a single Dirichlet parameter, while negative bags use standard EDL loss; Proposition 4.3 proves this weighted aggregation yields a tighter upper bound on the ideal instance loss than naive uniform or weighted-loss alternatives. The complete objective jointly optimizes bag-level Fisher Information-based EDL loss, the weakly-supervised instance-level MIREL loss, and a RED regularization term, with the shared embedding parameters ψ optimized by both bag and instance objectives.

## Results

On MNIST-bags, MIREL (ABMIL+MIREL) achieves the best overall UE score of 86.47 at bag level and 78.63 at instance level, surpassing the next-best method (MC Dropout, 85.42/65.12) by 1.05% and 13.51% respectively. On the CAMELYON16 pathology dataset, MIREL obtains the best bag-level UE of 72.06 and instance-level UE of 72.85, exceeding the runner-up (Bayes-MIL, 57.84/68.33) by 14.22% and 4.52%. MIREL also improves the vanilla ABMIL baseline by 15.08% (bag) and 32.35% (instance) on CAMELYON16, and by an average of 16.49% in instance-level confidence evaluation across all tested MIL networks on MNIST-bags.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIREL outperforms all four general UE baselines (Deep Ensemble, MC Dropout, I-EDL, Bayes-MIL) on instance-level UE by margins of 7.62%–19.04% on MNIST-bags, with the largest gap over I-EDL (78.63 vs. 59.59). At bag level, MIREL leads MC Dropout (86.47 vs. 85.42) and Deep Ensemble (86.47 vs. 80.59), though it trails MC Dropout slightly in bag-level confidence (86.63 vs. 88.46). On CAMELYON16, MIREL dominates all baselines in OOD detection (82.51 AUROC vs. 72.43 for Z-EDL, 48.77 for Bayes-MIL), while non-MIL-specific methods (Deep Ensemble, MC Dropout, Z-EDL) drop below 0.5 AUROC on near-OOD (TCGA-PRAD) detection. MIREL also consistently improves every tested MIL backbone (Mean, Max, DSMIL, ABMIL) when added as a plug-in, with the largest instance-level UE gains for ABMIL (+19.43%) and DSMIL (+7.59%). Bayes-MIL, the only other MIL-specific UE method, achieves competitive instance accuracy (91.64%) but lags MIREL substantially in instance-level UE (61.69 vs. 78.63) and bag-level UE (74.57 vs. 86.47) on MNIST-bags.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of evidential deep learning (EDL, Sensoy et al. 2018) and multiple instance learning (MIL), drawing on the Fisher Information-based EDL loss (Deng et al. 2023) for bag-level training and the Fundamental Theorem of Symmetric Functions (Zaheer et al. 2017) as the theoretical backbone for deriving a new instance-level estimator. The key conceptual departure from prior MIL work (ABMIL, DSMIL) is the rejection of attention scores as the natural proxy for instance-level predictions: the authors prove that a good instance estimator T = g∘f can be directly deduced from a good bag classifier, and then augment it with a residual network trained under weak supervision. This positions MIREL as the first method to jointly model Dirichlet-based predictive uncertainty at both bag and instance levels in a weakly-supervised MIL setting, rather than treating instance scoring and uncertainty estimation as separate concerns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MIUE is a practically important but previously unstudied problem: histopathology diagnosis, video anomaly detection, and other labeling-intensive applications all require reliable uncertainty quantification at the instance level when only bag-level labels are available. The plug-and-play nature of MIREL (applicable to any permutation-invariant MIL network with a single forward pass) makes it immediately useful for practitioners. However, the evaluation is limited to small synthetic bag datasets (500–6000 bags) and one pathology dataset, binary MIL only, and AUROC as the sole UE metric; scaling to larger, multi-label, or multi-class MIL settings remains open. The authors also note that tighter bounds on the weakly-supervised posterior p(θ_w|D) and integration with other deterministic UE paradigms (distance-aware, feature-space regularization) are natural next steps.
