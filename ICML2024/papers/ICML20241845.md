## Problem

No-reference (NR) image and video quality metrics are widely used in video processing benchmarks, yet their robustness to adversarial attacks in the video domain remains underexplored. Existing attacks on NR metrics require many iterations to converge, making them too slow for real-time video processing (1–10 fps), and per-frame application of image-domain attacks to videos produces visible flickering that undermines temporal consistency. The paper addresses the need for a fast, imperceptible, and temporally stable adversarial attack that can be injected into video processing pipelines to inflate NR metric scores in public benchmarks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (299×299 on NIPS2017) or video frame (1280×720 on DERF) along with white-box access to a target NR quality metric model (PaQ-2-PiQ, Hyper-IQA, or TReS). It produces a perturbed image or video in one forward-backward pass per frame, achieving approximately 8 fps on an NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPU. The perturbation is constrained to high-frequency, high-variance regions of the image, and the attack is evaluated by relative gain in the NR metric score (averaged ~7.7% for images, ~15.3% for videos) while preserving perceptual quality measured by PSNR, SSIM, VIF, and LPIPS.

## Method

IOI combines a single FGSM gradient step with two post-processing modules that operate in the frequency and spatial domains. The frequency module applies FFT to both the original and FGSM-perturbed images, then uses a threshold parameter f to partition coefficients into low-frequency (LF) and high-frequency (HF) bands; the final adversarial image retains the original LF component while blending the perturbed and original HF components. The weighting module computes a spatial map from relative local variance (σ/m over a 3×3 window, normalized, zeroed below 1%, square-rooted) to suppress perturbations in smooth, low-texture regions where distortions are most visible. The final composition is I^a = LF(I) + w·HF(I^p) + (1−w)·HF(I), which guarantees an L∞ bound of (1−f)·MAE*(I^p, I) on the perturbation. A key empirical finding is that applying one iteration to every frame yields higher averaged relative gain than applying multiple iterations to a subset of frames, making the one-iteration design both faster and more effective for video quality metrics.

## Results

On the DERF video dataset (12 videos, PaQ-2-PiQ attacked), IOI achieves a subjective quality score of 2.97 (95% CI ±0.16), substantially outperforming the next-best method NVW (2.16) with non-overlapping confidence intervals. Objectively, IOI leads on SSIM (0.941), VIF (0.669), and LPIPS (0.098) while maintaining comparable PSNR (34.3 dB). On the NIPS2017 image dataset across three attacked models (PaQ-2-PiQ, Hyper-IQA, TReS), IOI consistently achieves the highest SSIM (0.945–0.952), VIF (0.695–0.756), and lowest LPIPS (0.058–0.059) at aligned relative gain (~7.7%), with PSNR slightly lower than some baselines (33.4–33.5 dB vs. up to 39.1 dB for AdvJND on TReS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IOI outperforms all eight baselines on SSIM, VIF, and LPIPS in both the image (Table 2) and video (Table 3) experiments, with the largest margins against UAP and FACPA (e.g., LPIPS 0.098 vs. 0.301 for UAP on video). On PSNR, IOI is competitive but not always the highest: AdvJND achieves 34.5 dB on video and up to 39.1 dB on TReS images, reflecting that IOI redistributes perturbation into textured regions rather than minimizing pixel-level error. In the subjective study (685 participants, Bradley-Terry scoring), IOI's score of 2.97 is the only one whose 95% CI does not overlap with any other method; NVW (2.16) and Korhonen et al. (2.06) are the closest competitors but still fall well short. FGSM, SSAH, and Zhang et al. are grouped together because at one iteration their fidelity-preserving components yield zero gradients, making them equivalent to plain FGSM. The paper also shows that at 20 iterations the gap narrows (all methods converge to similar quality), confirming that IOI's advantage is specifically in the one-iteration regime relevant to real-time video processing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on FGSM as its gradient-based backbone and extends the line of adversarial attacks on NR quality metrics (UAP, FACPA, AdvJND, NVW, Korhonen et al., Zhang et al.) by adding two post-processing modules—FFT-based frequency decomposition and variance-based spatial weighting—that make a single-iteration perturbation visually imperceptible. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it targets video quality metrics with an explicit temporal-consistency requirement, showing that per-frame one-iteration attacks outperform multi-iteration attacks applied to sparse subsets of frames; and (2) it replaces the standard L∞ constraint with a frequency-domain cosine-similarity constraint, providing a theoretical bound on the resulting L∞ norm via Theorem 1.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NR quality metrics are embedded in public benchmarks for super-resolution, video compression, and video generation, where a fast, imperceptible attack (8 fps) could be silently injected into competing pipelines to inflate scores. The paper's 685-participant subjective study and the non-overlapping confidence intervals (IOI: 2.97 vs. best prior: 2.16) provide unusually strong evidence that prior methods produce visibly degraded adversarial videos. Key limitations include the white-box requirement, low cross-model transferability, and vulnerability to resize-based defences (reducing gain from 14.6% to ~1%). Open questions include whether robust NR metrics can be developed via adversarial training without sacrificing correlation with subjective quality, and whether black-box or transfer-based one-iteration attacks can match white-box performance at practical speeds.
