## Problem

Standard causal language modeling during domain finetuning unselectively maximizes the likelihood of all tokens, causing the model to learn not only useful domain knowledge but also useless corpus statistics such as topic biases. This over-adaptation leads to significant forgetting of general knowledge and a poor stability-plasticity tradeoff, which is especially problematic for lifelong learning. The paper asks whether a simple modification to the language modeling objective can achieve selective learning of useful knowledge while avoiding corpus-level statistics.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLaMA-2 model (7B or 13B), a domain corpus (medical textbooks totaling ~25.7M tokens, or Wikipedia excerpts from SQuAD/MRQA datasets), and a context prepended to each document. The context can be a short domain-hint sentence, a random UUID string, or a learned soft prompt of 10 vectors. At inference time, the finetuned model is used without any context, operating as a standard autoregressive language model with no architectural changes.

## Method

The key insight is the probability decomposition p(x) = p(x|c)p(c): by prepending a context c to each document x and computing the negative log-likelihood loss only on the x tokens (masking out c), the model learns the conditional distribution p(x|c) rather than the marginal p(x). This 'explains away' corpus-level properties like topic distribution, so the model no longer needs to adapt its unconditional topic prior p(topic) to the domain. Instead, it learns a conditional topic prior p(topic|a) that activates domain topics only when the context is present, while factual knowledge encoded in conditional token probabilities p(x_[k..n] | x_[1..k]) is learned without compromise. The result is a smaller gradient norm, reduced KL-divergence to the pretrained model, and significantly less forgetting in both one-time transfer learning and multi-corpus continual learning settings.

## Results

On continual learning over 13 medical textbooks, conditional finetuning with a learned soft prompt reduces average forgetting from 2.5 (standard, LLaMA-2 7B) to 2.1 while improving cumulative accuracy from 60.3% to 60.7%. On the 6-corpus MRQA benchmark, learned-context conditional finetuning reduces average forgetting from 0.026 to 0.019 (7B) and improves cumulative F1 from 0.390 to 0.395. Mechanistically, the conditional objective has a 41% smaller gradient norm (1.13 vs. 1.93 for 7B) and produces a 56% smaller KL-divergence to the pretrained model on C4 (0.036 vs. 0.082), confirming less disruptive modification of the pretrained weights.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is standard causal-language-modeling finetuning (no context prepended). Conditional finetuning consistently outperforms standard finetuning on both forgetting and cumulative accuracy across all settings. On medical textbooks (7B), learned-context CF reduces average forgetting by 16% (2.1 vs. 2.5) and gains +0.4 points in cumulative accuracy (60.7 vs. 60.3); on MRQA (7B), it cuts forgetting by 27% (0.019 vs. 0.026) while matching or exceeding cumulative F1. Among the three context types, learned soft prompts are most effective in continual learning (most dissimilar across corpora, KL-divergence 0.033 vs. 0.006 for random UUIDs), while in single-domain transfer learning all context types perform similarly. The paper does not compare against other explicit anti-forgetting methods (e.g., EWC, A-GEM); the comparison is strictly standard vs. conditional finetuning under identical hyperparameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the well-studied concept of context (from inference-time in-context learning and prompt tuning) for a fundamentally different purpose: training-time selective learning. While instruction finetuning (Ouyang et al., 2022; Chung et al., 2022) uses context to learn the mapping between instructions and responses, and prompt tuning (Lester et al., 2021) learns soft prompts for inference, conditional finetuning uses a context to 'explain away' corpus-level statistics so the model avoids learning them. The probabilistic decomposition p(x) = p(x|c)p(c) provides a clean theoretical framing that connects to prior work on shortcut learning (Geirhos et al., 2020) and ensemble-based de-biasing (Clark et al., 2019; Sanh et al., 2021), but achieves selective learning through a much simpler mechanism—just masking loss on context tokens during standard causal LM training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The technique is remarkably simple (prepend context, mask its loss) and could be easily adopted as a drop-in modification to any domain finetuning pipeline, potentially reducing the stability-plasticity tradeoff problem in lifelong learning with LLMs. The finding that even uninformative contexts (random UUIDs) achieve similar selective learning reveals the mechanism is about the conditioning structure rather than semantic content, which opens questions about how to optimally design contexts for multi-corpus continual learning. Key limitations include evaluation on small corpora (tens of millions of tokens), basic QA-based evaluation that tests memorization rather than complex reasoning, and modest absolute improvements in forgetting metrics, leaving open whether the effect scales to billion-token training regimes.
