## Problem

Finding the optimal KL regularization strength in preference-based alignment (RLHF, DPO) requires retraining multiple models at different β values, which is prohibitively expensive for large language models. The paper asks whether the reward-regularization tradeoff can be explored and tuned at decoding time from a single trained aligned model, without any retraining.

## Input / Output

At each autoregressive decoding step, the method consumes the raw logits vectors (of dimension |V|) from both a reference (SFT) model and an aligned model conditioned on the same prefix, along with a scalar realignment parameter λ ∈ ℝ⁺. It outputs a single next-token probability distribution from which a token is sampled, iterated until an EOS token is produced. Notably, λ is not bounded above by 1: values λ > 1 correspond to extrapolation beyond the convex hull of the two logit vectors, effectively reducing the effective KL strength below the training-time β.

## Method

The core theoretical result shows that the optimal aligned policies at different KL strengths β/λ are all geometric mixtures of the reference distribution π^sft and a single aligned distribution π*(β), differing only in the mixing exponent λ. An autoregressive per-token approximation of this geometric mixture (Eq. 6) simplifies, via the log-scale equivalence of geometric and arithmetic means (Proposition 1), to a linear combination of logits: softmax[λ·h^aligned + (1−λ)·h^sft]. This yields Algorithm 1, a two-model decoding procedure that requires no gradient updates or retraining. The method is agnostic to the alignment algorithm used (PPO, DPO, etc.) and can be deployed either as a direct inference-time control knob for per-user/per-task alignment strength, or as a cheap proxy for hyperparameter search: one sweeps λ on a validation set, identifies promising β/λ values, and optionally retrains only at those points.

## Results

In the summarization task (Reddit TL;DR), DeRa models closely track retrained-from-scratch models across KL strengths β/λ ∈ {0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.15, 0.2}, with win rates against the SFT reference and the base-aligned model showing strong correlation (Figure 3). DeRa identifies that the default KL strength β=0.1 is over-regularized: reducing it to β/λ with λ=2 or λ=5 improves win rates, a finding confirmed by retrained models. On the Open LLM Leaderboard (Zephyr-7b), the original aligned model (β=0.1) scores 61.55 on GSM8K and 30.02 on IFEval, while the SFT reference scores 59.23 and 12.74 respectively, showing alignment improves instruction-following at the cost of some factual benchmarks. In the hallucination mitigation task, increasing λ from 0.011 to 2 progressively reduces hallucinations, while λ=10 causes reward hacking (verbatim copying).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between DeRa (decoding-time logit mixing) and models retrained from scratch at each target KL strength β/λ. In the summarization task, DeRa and retrained models show closely correlated win rates against both the SFT reference and the base-aligned model across all tested λ values, including extrapolation regimes (λ > 1) where the combined logits lie outside the convex hull. DeRa matches or slightly trails retrained models in absolute win rate but captures the same qualitative trends (e.g., identifying over-regularization at β=0.1). Against the SFT reference, both DeRa and retrained models show increasing win rates as λ increases (less regularization), confirming that the base model is over-regularized. In the hallucination task, DeRa with λ=2 effectively reduces hallucinations compared to λ=0.011 (near-reference behavior), while λ=10 degrades to incoherent verbatim copying. The paper also notes that weight interpolation (combining model parameters instead of logits) is feasible but incurs a performance penalty consistent with prior weight-merging literature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the KL-regularized alignment objective of Ziegler et al. (2019) and the closed-form optimal policy of Rafailov et al. (2023, DPO), proving that aligned models at different KL strengths form a geometric mixture of the reference and aligned distributions. The practical decoding procedure (linear logit interpolation) is conceptually related to logit-merging techniques in machine translation fusion and classifier-free guidance, but targets a distinct objective: varying the reward-regularization tradeoff rather than improving translation quality or prompt adherence. Unlike multi-reward alignment methods (Rame et al., 2023; Jang et al., 2023) that combine models trained on different reward functions, DeRa combines a single reference model with a single aligned model to sweep the regularization strength, and unlike proxy-tuning approaches (Mitchell et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024a) that decouple model scales, DeRa specifically addresses the hyperparameter search problem for KL strength.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DeRa offers a practically significant efficiency gain: practitioners can sweep over KL regularization strengths at decoding time (doubled inference cost) rather than retraining multiple models, which is especially valuable for large models where each alignment run is expensive. The autoregressive approximation's fidelity in the extrapolation regime (λ > 1) is notable but not guaranteed in theory, and the paper acknowledges that weight-combining alternatives trade performance for speed. Open questions include how well the approximation holds for very long sequences, for models with substantially different architectures between reference and aligned checkpoints, and whether the approach extends naturally to multi-objective alignment where multiple aligned models must be combined simultaneously.
