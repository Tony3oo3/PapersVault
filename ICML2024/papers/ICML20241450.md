## Problem

Knowledge Distillation has been widely adopted in offline continual learning but remains under-exploited in Online Continual Learning, where data is seen only once. The paper identifies three OCL-specific obstacles that make standard KD strategies infeasible: Teacher Quality (end-of-task snapshots are unreliable since the model may not be fully trained), Teacher Quantity (storing one model per task violates the strict memory constraint), and Unknown Task Boundaries (the exact moment of task transition in a continuous stream is not guaranteed). The paper argues that KD, like replay, should be a central component of OCL and demonstrates how to overcome these obstacles.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single-pass stream of RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-100) combined with a small replay buffer (M=1k–10k samples), where each image is also passed through a data augmentation pipeline to produce a second view. The output is a class logit vector over all classes seen so far; at inference, the final model is the element-wise average of the student and EMA-teacher weights rather than either alone, which the paper shows yields a consistent 0.5–2% accuracy gain over using either model in isolation.

## Method

The core mechanism is an evolving teacher whose weights are an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the student parameters, updated every training step with momentum α (typically 0.01). The training loss combines the baseline method's loss with a multiview KL-divergence distillation term: the student's logits on both the raw and augmented views are matched to the teacher's logits on the augmented view, weighted by a teacher-dependent coefficient λ_α that follows a linear relation with log₁₀(α) (λ_α = 4.5·log₁₀(α) + 14.5). This single hyperparameter α simultaneously controls the plasticity–stability tradeoff: low α yields a stable teacher retaining old-task knowledge, while high α yields a plastic teacher tracking the current task. The approach is architecture-independent, operates in logit space (unlike SDP's representation-space distillation), and can be plugged into any existing OCL baseline with only a few lines of code, as formalized in the paper's PyTorch pseudo-code.

## Results

The paper reports final average accuracy (mean over 5 runs) across four datasets. On ImageNet-100 (M=10k), GSA+ours achieves 52.41% vs. GSA's 40.18% (+12.2 pts), and PCR+ours reaches 49.66% vs. PCR's 36.99% (+12.7 pts). On CIFAR-100 (M=5k), GSA+ours attains 56.15% vs. GSA's 45.86% (+10.3 pts), and ER+ours jumps from 38.05% to 52.10% (+14.1 pts). Across all seven baselines and four datasets, MKD consistently yields 2–18 point improvements, with the largest gains on harder datasets (ImageNet-100, Tiny-ImageNet) and smaller memory budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MKD outperforms every baseline it is combined with across all four datasets and all memory sizes tested. On CIFAR-100 (M=5k), the gains range from +9.1 pts (ER-ACE: 39.61→48.73) to +16.3 pts (DER++: 25.71→41.97); on ImageNet-100 (M=10k), gains span +2.4 pts (OCM: 41.86→44.24) to +17.7 pts (DER++: 20.48→38.22). Against the closest competing distillation method SDP, MKD wins decisively: ER+ours (44.92%) surpasses ER+SDP (35.64%) by 9.3 pts on ImageNet-100 M=10k, and GSA+ours (52.41%) edges out GSA+SDP (51.39%) by 1.0 pt while being computationally cheaper (logit-space vs. representation-space distillation). Compared to Temporal Ensembles, ER+ours (44.92%) beats ER+Temp. Ens. (40.10%) by 4.8 pts on ImageNet-100 M=10k. Notably, MKD also reduces standard deviation substantially (e.g., GSA on CIFAR-100 M=5k: 1.89→0.60), indicating more stable training. The only case where the gain is modest is OCM on ImageNet-100 M=10k (+2.4 pts), likely because OCM's contrastive objective already provides some feature stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Exponential Moving Average (EMA) teacher from self-supervised learning (Momentum Contrast, BYOL/DINO) as a distillation mechanism for Online Continual Learning, departing from the frozen-snapshot teacher paradigm dominant in offline CL. It positions itself against prior OCL distillation attempts (DER, MMKDDA, SDP) by identifying three structural obstacles—Teacher Quality, Teacher Quantity, and Unknown Task Boundaries—that explain why KD has been underexploited in the online setting, and shows that a single evolving EMA teacher addresses all three simultaneously. The method is integrated as an additive loss on top of existing replay-based OCL baselines (ER, DER++, ER-ACE, DVC, OCM, GSA, PCR), making it orthogonal to and composable with their specific loss designs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple, architecture-independent, logit-space distillation loss with a single hyperparameter (α) can add >10 percentage points on ImageNet-100 and simultaneously mitigate multiple known OCL pathologies (task-recency bias, last-layer bias, feature drift, poor backward transfer), suggesting KD should be treated as a first-class component of OCL alongside replay. The finding that logit-space EMA distillation outperforms the more complex representation-space SDP teacher is practically significant for compute-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-replay or parameter-isolation methods, whether the λ_α = (9/2)·log₁₀(α) + 29/2 scaling law generalizes across architectures and task sequences, and whether the EMA teacher concept transfers to other online learning regimes (e.g., domain or task-incremental).
