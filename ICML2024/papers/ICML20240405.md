## Problem

ReLU linearization methods for private inference reduce cryptographic computation costs with only marginal global accuracy loss, but no prior work examined whether this loss is distributed equally across demographic subgroups. This paper identifies a 'Matthew effect' where underrepresented groups suffer disproportionately larger accuracy drops as more ReLUs are linearized, challenging the assumption that linearization uniformly affects model performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240405/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on encrypted RGB images (32×32 for SVHN and CIFAR-10, variable resolution for UTKFace) and produces class labels (age group, race, or digit). The CNN backbone (ResNet-18, ResNet-34, VGG-19, or WideResNet) is modified by replacing a fraction of ReLU activations with linear surrogates to reduce the number of expensive cryptographic operations during inference. The linearized model is then fine-tuned via knowledge distillation from the original ReLU model, with the proposed fairness-aware modification applied during this step.

## Method

The paper provides a theoretical bound (via second-order Taylor expansion) showing that the excess loss for a group after ReLU linearization is governed by that group's gradient norm and the maximum eigenvalue of its loss Hessian, both scaled by the parameter distance between the original and linearized models. Empirically, minority groups exhibit larger gradient norms and smaller distances to the decision boundary, explaining their disproportionate accuracy loss. The mitigation strategy (Algorithm 1) modifies the standard knowledge-distillation fine-tuning step by introducing Lagrangian multipliers that penalize the gap between each group's KD loss and the overall KD loss, effectively regularizing the model to equalize distances to the decision boundary across groups. This fairness-aware fine-tuning is plug-and-play, requiring only access to group labels during training and integrating seamlessly with existing SNL and DeepReDuce pipelines.

## Results

On UTKFace with race labels using ResNet-18 and SNL, the majority group (White, 42.5%) experiences almost no accuracy drop across all ReLU budgets, while the minority 'Other' group (7.1%) suffers an approximately 5-fold relative accuracy drop. With DeepReDuce (DR) on the same setup, the disparity is even more severe: the 'Other' group experiences a near 10-fold accuracy drop, reaching nearly 100% relative accuracy loss at the lowest ReLU budget. In the controlled 2D experiment (Figure 2), the original ReLU model achieves 99.3% global accuracy (100.0% majority, 93.0% minority), while the 50%-linearized model retains 99.0% global accuracy but drops minority accuracy to 88.0%. The proposed Lagrangian-based mitigation consistently improves minority-group accuracy across all tested linearization levels with minimal impact on majority groups.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two ReLU linearization methods—Selective Network Linearization (SNL) and DeepReDuce (DR)—against the original non-linearized model as the baseline. Both methods produce disparate accuracy degradation, but DR is substantially worse: on UTKFace race with ResNet-18, DR causes a ~10-fold relative accuracy drop for the 'Other' group versus ~5-fold for SNL, because DR linearizes contiguous blocks without data-driven placement control. SNL offers more predictable and consistent outcomes across ReLU budgets, making it the preferred substrate for the proposed fairness mitigation. The mitigation algorithm (Algorithm 1) is applied as a drop-in replacement for the standard KD finetuning step in both SNL and DR, and it reduces the relative accuracy drop for minority groups while preserving majority-group accuracy, with the effect being most pronounced at higher linearization levels (e.g., 2% ReLUs retained).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ReLU linearization literature for private inference (Mishra et al. 2020; Jha et al. 2021; Cho et al. 2022; Kundu et al. 2023) by introducing a fairness lens that prior work entirely overlooked. It draws on the approximation-theory results of Arora et al. (2018) and Liu & Liang (2021) to connect ReLU count to decision-boundary expressiveness, and on the model-compression-fairness line (Lukasik et al. 2022; Hooker et al. 2020) to position ReLU linearization as another compression mechanism with disparate impact. The proposed mitigation repurposes Lagrangian duality for constrained optimization (Fioretto et al. 2020) and plugs it into the existing knowledge-distillation finetuning step of SNL and DR, making it a drop-in modification rather than a new training pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a technique marketed as having 'negligible impacts on accuracy' actually produces up to 10-fold accuracy drops for minority groups is practically important for anyone deploying private inference on imbalanced data, and it broadens the model-compression-fairness literature to a new mechanism. Key limitations include the requirement for protected-group labels during finetuning and theoretical bounds that hold only under restricted (convex univariate) decision-boundary assumptions. Open questions include formalizing the relationship between group size, ReLU budget, and accuracy drop without such restrictions, and extending the mitigation to settings where demographic information is unavailable (e.g., via distributionally robust objectives or adversarial reweighting).
