## Problem

The state-of-the-art soft analytical side-channel attack (SASCA) relies on loopy belief propagation over a factor graph of AES, which lacks convergence guarantees and does not ensure accurate posterior estimates even when it converges. An unsuccessful SASCA therefore provides no certificate of security, and it is straightforward to construct easy inference problems where loopy BP fails catastrophically. This paper addresses the gap by developing an exact inference method for SASCA that avoids the unreliable loopy BP while remaining computationally feasible.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 20,000-dimensional power traces and a known plaintext, from which template attacks (multivariate Gaussians with LDA-based dimensionality reduction) yield local byte-valued beliefs p(v|ℓ) for 21 intermediate variables per MIXCOLUMNS call. These beliefs are either sparsified (clamping the k smallest probabilities to zero for a threshold ε) or used directly as weights in a weighted model counting query over a pre-compiled PSDD of ~20M nodes. The output is an exact joint key posterior p(k|ℓ) over 128 bits, from which both marginal posteriors per key byte and the most probable evidence (MPE) key assignment are extracted.

## Method

The core idea is to compile the loopy MIXCOLUMNS subgraph (168 binary variables, 648 CNF clauses) into a compact probabilistic sentential decision diagram (PSDD), which reduces the overall factor graph to a tree where standard message passing becomes exact. Two compilation strategies are developed: (i) a PSDD multiplication chain that exploits sparsity in the local beliefs to keep circuit products tractable, and (ii) a dynamic compilation pipeline that introduces 2560 auxiliary byte-indicator variables and reduces inference to a single weighted model counting (WMC) query with a backward pass for all marginals. A key efficiency trick conditions the SDD on a fixed value of g = x₁₂⊕x₃₄ and exploits the linearity of MIXCOLUMNS to handle all 256 values of g by permuting weight functions, reducing compilation cost by a factor of ~256. The merging trick further eliminates redundant variables connected by deterministic bijections (XTIME, XOR), shrinking the problem to 10 byte variables with indicators. This yields exact inference with 6× fewer operations than exhaustive enumeration for dense beliefs, and comparable runtime to SASCA for sparse beliefs, while improving top-1 success rate by over 31 percentage points.

## Results

On the ASCAD dataset (AES-128, first round), ExSASCA+MPE achieves a top-1 success rate of 67.61% (ε=0), outperforming the best SASCA configuration (100 iterations, ε=0) at 35.84% by over 31 percentage points absolute. Even with sparse approximations (ε=10⁻²), ExSASCA+MPE reaches 57.51%, still substantially above SASCA's 34.25%. Computationally, ExSASCA with ε≥10⁻⁸ runs in hundreds of milliseconds to seconds per trace (comparable to SASCA), while the dense (ε=0) variant requires only 6× fewer operations than exhaustive key enumeration for MPE queries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is SASCA (Veyrat-Charvillon et al., 2014), the state-of-the-art soft analytical side-channel attack using loopy belief propagation. ExSASCA wins decisively across all settings: at ε=0 (exact inference), ExSASCA+MPE achieves 67.61% top-1 success rate versus SASCA's best of 35.84% (100 iterations), a 31.77-point absolute improvement. Even when ExSASCA uses sparse approximations (ε=10⁻²) and SASCA does not, ExSASCA still leads by over 23 points (57.51% vs 34.25%). Under corrupted beliefs simulating protected implementations (Figure 4), SASCA degrades to near-zero success as α→1, while ExSASCA retains ~20% success rate. The paper also notes that exhaustive key enumeration (the only other exact method) is computationally infeasible at scale, requiring ~3 orders of magnitude more operations than ExSASCA with sparse messages, and 6× more than ExSASCA's dense variant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard SASCA framework (Veyrat-Charvillon et al., 2014), which relies on loopy belief propagation over the AES factor graph and provides no convergence or accuracy guarantees. The key conceptual shift is replacing the loopy MIXCOLUMNS subgraph with a single high-dimensional factor compiled into a probabilistic SDD (building on Darwiche's SDD formalism and Kisa et al.'s PSDDs), thereby reducing the inference problem to exact message passing on a tree. The compilation pipeline draws on dynamic vtree minimization (Choi & Darwiche, 2013) and the SCA-specific merging trick (Guo et al., 2020), while the dense-belief variant introduces a novel dynamic compilation strategy that reframes inference as weighted model counting over a single SDD shared across all 256 conditioning values via the linearity of MIXCOLUMNS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ExSASCA provides the first exact inference method for soft analytical side-channel attacks, removing the fundamental uncertainty of loopy BP: an unsuccessful attack now constitutes a certificate that the leakage cannot be further exploited under the model, rather than merely a failure of an approximate algorithm. The 31% absolute improvement in top-1 success rate and the 3-orders-of-magnitude reduction over exhaustive enumeration make exact inference practically viable for the first time. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-round attacks (limited by template availability), whether circuit-complexity lower bounds can be used to prove the intractability of certain cryptographic structures, and whether the dynamic compilation framework generalizes to other large-scale probabilistic-logical systems such as error-correcting codes and system verification.
