## Problem

Standard transfer learning only transfers pre-trained weights as an initialization, which limits the information passed to the downstream model, locks the downstream architecture to match the pre-trained one, and precludes combining multiple pre-trained models that learn complementary information. There is no efficient, architecture-agnostic method to selectively transfer only the task-relevant subset of knowledge from large foundation models into much smaller downstream models.

## Input / Output

AFT takes as input frozen, pre-computed last-layer features from any number of pre-trained models (e.g., DINOv2 ViT-G/14, OpenCLIP, LLaMA 13B, Flan-T5) concatenated into a single feature vector Ψ, alongside standard downstream training data (RGB images, text, or image-text pairs). The output is a small downstream model (ViT-S/16, ResNet-50, BERT-S, DistillBERT, or CLIP ResNet-50) that produces class labels, text classification labels, or entailment labels. The pre-trained features are computed once and cached, so training only requires retrieving them per batch, and the method works across architectures of arbitrary size difference (up to 50× parameter reduction).

## Method

AFT defines a prior p(θ) ∝ exp(−β·I(Φ; X | Ψ)) that penalizes the downstream model for encoding information about the input that is not already present in the pre-trained features Ψ. This mutual information bound reduces to a regularization R(θ) = min_μ E[‖Φ − μΨ‖²], where the learnable transformation μ acts on the pre-trained features rather than the downstream features (as in KD), so the downstream model only needs to lie in the span of Ψ rather than reconstructing all of it. To make optimization tractable and robust, AFT reformulates the objective in kernel space using a diagonal parameterization μ = diag(σ(s)), which reduces the variational parameters to a single vector and automatically handles orthogonal invariance. The kernel distance is estimated with a mini-batch Frobenius norm of the difference between downstream and weighted pre-trained kernel matrices, adding negligible overhead (~0.03 min per epoch) over standard training. The learned weights μ adaptively upweight informative pre-trained features and downweight irrelevant or noisy ones, enabling effective multi-source transfer and robustness to uninformative features.

## Results

On 6 vision datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Oxford Flowers-102, Oxford-IIIT Pets, DTD, Food-101) with ViT-S/16, MLP-Mixer-B, and ResNet-50 as downstream models, AFT reduces error by an average of over 15% relative to Standard Transfer Learning when transferring from DINOv2 ViT-G/14 (1.1B params). On CIFAR-100 with ViT-S/16, AFT achieves 89.23% accuracy when combining DINO+CLIP features, compared to 88.66% for B-Tuning and 87.96% for KD. On 8 NLP datasets with BERT-Small and DistillBERT, AFT achieves the lowest normalized error when transferring from Flan-T5 Large. AFT also achieves 74.39% accuracy on SNLI-VE (multi-modal), outperforming STL (73.69%) and KD (74.05%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AFT consistently outperforms Standard Transfer Learning (STL) by over 15% relative error reduction on average across all 6 vision datasets and 3 downstream models. Against Knowledge Distillation (KD, FitNets-style), AFT wins on ViT-S/16 and MLP-Mixer-B across all pre-trained model configurations, but KD slightly outperforms AFT on ResNet-50 (e.g., 87.62% vs 87.18% for DINO+CLIP). Against B-Tuning, AFT achieves higher accuracy in most settings, with the largest gap appearing when combining multiple pre-trained models (89.23% vs 88.66% for DINO+CLIP on ViT-S/16). AFT also outperforms the more sophisticated KD variants Relational Knowledge Distillation (RKD) and Factor Transfer (FT) in the aggregated comparison (Figure 1b). A key differentiator is that AFT uniquely benefits from combining multiple pre-trained models (e.g., DINO+CLIP or BiT+DINO+CLIP), while KD and B-Tuning show diminishing or no gains from multi-source transfer, and AFT achieves the highest correlation (0.97) between pre-trained linear probe accuracy and downstream accuracy, indicating superior scalability with pre-trained model quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

AFT is derived from feature-based knowledge distillation (FitNets, Romero et al. 2014) by a single conceptual flip: instead of requiring the student to predict teacher features (KD), it requires student features to lie in the span of teacher features, thereby permitting the student to ignore irrelevant pre-trained dimensions. This places AFT in the broader family of feature-space regularization for transfer learning alongside B-Tuning (You et al. 2022), which regularizes predictions rather than features and determines feature importance a priori, and Bayesian transfer learning (Shwartz-Ziv et al. 2022), which operates in parameter space. The kernel formulation and diagonal variational parameterization are novel technical contributions that simplify the inner optimization and prevent overfitting on small downstream datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AFT provides a practically important recipe for deploying small models (e.g., 22M-param ViT-S) that inherit knowledge from billion-parameter foundation models, achieving 100× inference speedup with negligible training overhead. The 0.97 correlation between pre-trained linear-probe accuracy and downstream accuracy demonstrates that AFT uniquely scales with pre-trained model quality, a property absent in KD or B-Tuning. Key limitations include restriction to last-layer features only and the finding that multi-model transfer helps in vision but not in NLP, suggesting the method's benefit is modality-dependent. Open questions include whether incorporating intermediate-layer features or task-specific feature selection could further close the gap to linear-probe performance.
