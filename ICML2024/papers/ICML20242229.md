## Problem

Existing active domain adaptation methods improve overall accuracy by preferentially querying easy-to-improve categories, while challenging categories that demand the most attention are systematically overlooked and can even suffer performance degradation. This paper introduces category-aware active DA, the first framework to explicitly target per-category performance enhancement and avoid the hidden cost of neglecting critical categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242229/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on latent representations extracted by a frozen base DA model (DANN with ResNet-50) rather than raw images. It takes labeled source representations and unlabeled target representations, binarizes all labels into 'One' (target category c) and 'Non-One' (all other categories), and produces a binary logistic regression classifier for category c along with a set of b target samples selected per iteration for annotation. The influence predictor f maps representations to scalar influence values, enabling label-free scoring of unlabeled target data.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces indirect importance criteria (uncertainty, diversity, domainness) with a direct influence-function-based estimation of each sample's impact on a category-specific binary surrogate classifier h'. For each iteration, the influence of every labeled source sample on the validation loss is computed via the Hessian-inverse formulation (Eq. 4), then a regression model f is trained to predict influence values from representations alone (Eq. 5), enabling label-free influence estimation for unlabeled target samples. Target samples with the highest predicted influence are selected (with diversity sampling from a 5×b candidate pool), annotated, and used to re-train h' with a weighted loss that emphasizes newly queried data. The convexity of logistic regression satisfies the influence function's theoretical requirements, and conjugate gradients with stochastic Hessian-vector products keep computation at O(nd).

## Results

On Office-Home (12 adaptation tasks, 65 categories), the proposed method achieves 79.32% average per-category accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline ISAL (73.21%) by 6.11% and the straightforward 'Always One' baseline by 3.51%. On DomainNet-126 (6 tasks), the method reaches 57.59% average per-category accuracy, surpassing the best baseline (Always One at 52.53%) by 5.06%. On VisDA-2017 (S→R), it achieves 66.68%, exceeding the best baseline (Always One at 63.25%) by 3.43%. The method also demonstrates that category-aware active learning for one category does not significantly harm other categories, with only 15 out of 65 non-targeted categories showing a drop, and the largest being just −0.33%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines across all three datasets. Against DANN (Ganin et al., 2016), the unsupervised DA baseline without active learning, the method gains approximately 30.7% in average per-category accuracy on Office-Home (79.32% vs. 48.63%), confirming the substantial benefit of active annotation. Against CLUE (Prabhu et al., 2021), which uses uncertainty-weighted clustering for sample selection, the method improves by roughly 14.6% on Office-Home, demonstrating that indirect uncertainty/diversity criteria are poorly suited for category-specific improvement. Against DUC (Xie et al., 2023), which employs Dirichlet-based evidential uncertainty calibration, the margin is about 12.3% on Office-Home. The closest competitor is ISAL (Liu et al., 2021), which also uses the influence function but relies on pseudo labels for target influence estimation; the proposed method surpasses it by 6.11% on Office-Home and by 5.06% and 3.43% on DomainNet-126 and VisDA-2017 respectively, attributed to the influence predictor trained on labeled source data rather than pseudo labels. The 'Always One' baseline, which simply selects samples most likely belonging to the target category, is the strongest non-influence-based method but still trails the proposed method by 3.51%, confirming that both 'One' and 'Non-One' samples contribute to category-specific recognition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the influence function framework (Cook & Weisberg, 1980; Koh & Liang, 2017) into the active domain adaptation setting, departing from prior active DA methods (CLUE, DUC, TQS, S3vaada) that rely on indirect criteria like uncertainty, diversity, or domainness to select target samples. The closest prior work is ISAL (Liu et al., 2021), which also applies influence functions to active learning but computes them via pseudo labels from a domain-specific model; here, the authors instead train a lightweight binary logistic regression surrogate and a separate regression predictor to estimate influence without ground-truth labels. The key conceptual departure is the category-aware objective: rather than optimizing overall accuracy, the method targets a single category by binarizing labels and measuring each sample's impact on that category-specific classifier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper highlights a practically important blind spot in active DA: methods that maximize average accuracy can silently degrade performance on critical categories (e.g., humans in autonomous driving), a risk the authors quantify with the CLUE example where the Eraser category drops to near 0%. The category-aware framing and the remedy for 'Non-One' category degradation offer a more robust deployment story. Open questions include whether the binary-surrogate approach scales to multi-category simultaneous adaptation, how the method behaves under severe class imbalance in the binarized setting (acknowledged but not addressed), and whether the influence-function-based selection generalizes beyond the relatively shallow logistic regression surrogate to deeper non-convex classifiers.
