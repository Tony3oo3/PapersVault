## Problem

Surrogate models in offline optimization often have narrow prediction margins outside the offline data regime, making their predictions brittle to small parameter perturbations and misleading the search toward sub-optimal candidates. Existing conservative approaches condition on search-specific or surrogate-specific properties, but lack a model-agnostic characterization of sensitivity that could coordinate conditioning between the search and surrogate in a unified way. The paper asks whether an explicit, optimizable sensitivity measure can serve as a plug-in regularizer that boosts a wide range of existing offline optimizers without modifying their core search logic.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input an offline dataset of design-performance pairs, an existing surrogate model (any parameterized architecture), and hyperparameters including a sensitivity threshold α (default 0.1) and bounded ranges for the adversarial perturbation distribution parameters ω_μ ∈ [-10⁻³, 10⁻³] and ω_σ ∈ [10⁻⁵, 10⁻²]. It outputs a regularized surrogate model whose parameters have been jointly optimized with the perturbation distribution, which then produces optimized design candidates via the original optimizer's search procedure. The regularizer is a scalar term added to any existing offline optimizer's loss, requiring only m=100 perturbation samples per iteration and a small neural network (one hidden layer, two units) for the re-parameterization.

## Method

BOSS defines a model-agnostic (α,ω)-sensitivity measure S_φ(α,ω) as the probability that the expected surrogate output shifts by more than threshold α under a Gaussian perturbation γ ~ N(ω_μ, ω_σ²I) of the model weights. This is embedded in a minimax objective min_φ max_ω [L(φ) + λ·S_φ(α,ω)], analogous to GAN training, where the surrogate is fit to data while the perturbation distribution is adversarially learned to expose the worst-case sensitivity. To make optimization tractable, two approximations are developed: (1) a neural re-parameterization that learns a logistic-regression network Φ(γ;w) to approximate the indicator of the sensitivity region, enabling gradient ascent on ω via the chain rule; and (2) a differentiable upper bound S_φ⁺(α,ω) = E_γ[min(1, (A(φ,γ)/α)²)] for gradient descent on φ, combined with a first-order Taylor expansion of the expected output difference to enable vectorized GPU computation. The resulting regularizer is loss-agnostic and can be seamlessly added to any offline optimizer that either uses surrogate derivatives or couples search and surrogate in a differentiable loss, as demonstrated across 11 baselines on 6 Design-Bench tasks.

## Results

BOSS is a sensitivity-informed regularizer applied on top of 11 existing offline optimizers across 6 Design-Bench tasks (3 continuous, 3 discrete). At the 100th percentile, BOSS improves baseline performance in 56/66 cases (84.85%), with an average gain of ~2.08% and a peak gain of 30% (REINFORCE on TF-Bind-10). Only 3/66 cases show a decrease, averaging at most 0.3%. BOSS also establishes new SOTA on Ant Morphology (CMA-ES: 191.5%→200.9%), TF-Bind-8 (ENS-MIN: 0.986→0.989), and ChEMBL (BO-qEI: 0.659→0.688).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BOSS is not a standalone optimizer but a plug-in regularizer that augments the training loss of existing offline optimizers, so comparisons are framed as 'baseline vs. baseline+BOSS.' The largest gains appear for weaker baselines: REINFORCE improves by +30% on TF-Bind-10 and +5.8% on D'Kitty Morphology, while CMA-ES gains +9.4% on Ant Morphology (setting a new SOTA of 200.9%). Stronger baselines like ICT, COMs, and MINs see more modest but consistent gains of 0.5–3.6%. BOSS degrades performance in only 3 out of 66 cases (CMA-ES on TF-Bind-8/10 by −0.3%/−0.4%, MINs on D'Kitty by −0.2%), all negligible. In a separate comparison (Table 3, Appendix D.4), BOSS-augmented ICT outperforms the recent DDOM and tri-mentoring methods on 5 of 6 tasks, with the largest margin on TF-Bind-8 (0.989 vs. 0.946/0.927) and ChEMBL (0.688 vs. 0.635/N/A).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing a model-agnostic sensitivity measure (probability of output change under small weight perturbations) as a regularizer for offline optimization, departing from prior approaches that address out-of-distribution prediction through surrogate-specific conservatism (COMs, RoMA) or search-specific conditioning (CbAS, MINs). Conceptually, it repurposes the adversarial perturbation paradigm from robust machine learning—where noise is applied to inputs post-training—by instead perturbing model weights during training and coupling it with the surrogate fitting objective in a minimax game. The sensitivity notion draws on model robustness literature (Stephenson et al., Tsai et al.) but applies it to the expected output over the training distribution rather than individual test points, making it a negotiating medium between search and surrogate that prior work lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BOSS provides a plug-and-play regularizer that consistently improves 11 diverse offline optimizers across 6 benchmark tasks (84.85% improvement rate, average ~2%), establishing that explicitly regulating surrogate sensitivity is a complementary axis to existing conservatism strategies. The approach's model-agnosticism means it can be layered onto future offline optimizers without architectural changes, and the authors note potential extension to safe Bayesian optimization and safe RL. Open questions include whether the Taylor approximation and neural re-parameterization remain tight for deeper surrogate architectures, whether the sensitivity concept transfers to online interactive settings, and whether the modest average gains (~2%) scale to higher-dimensional or noisier design spaces.
