## Problem

Foundation models exhibit significant performance variance under superficial prompt modifications such as option reordering in multiple-choice questions, a bias that persists even with in-context examples. Existing debiasing approaches either require labeled data (making them prone to inheriting data biases) or demand multiple forward passes per sample (scaling exponentially with option count). The paper asks whether bias can be mitigated directly in the model's internal representation space using no labeled data at all.

## Input / Output

For bias direction identification, the method consumes unlabeled question samples (100–1000 depending on dataset size) in multiple prompt orderings, collecting attention head activation vectors at the last token position. During inference, it takes standard image + text question inputs (e.g., a photo with a yes/no or multiple-choice prompt) and produces a text answer, with the intervention applied internally at selected attention heads before the output projection. The intervention strength α and number of intervened heads K are the only tunable hyperparameters, and the method requires no model retraining or fine-tuning.

## Method

STEERFAIR exploits the observation that model bias often follows simple, enumerable association rules (e.g., 'always choose the j-th option'). It constructs demonstrations from unlabeled samples that mimic each rule, collects the corresponding attention head activations at the last token position, and identifies a bias direction per rule via the first principal component (PCA) of the activation matrix. Multiple per-rule directions are combined into a single steering vector using QR decomposition to obtain an orthonormal basis followed by averaging, which removes inter-direction correlations. During inference, the model's attention head outputs are shifted away from this combined direction by subtracting α·ṽ before the output projection, with l₂-norm normalization to preserve original activation magnitudes. Only the top-K attention heads with the highest projected values on the first PC are intervened upon, keeping the modification minimally invasive while still reducing cross-ordering variance by ~10.86% accuracy points on average.

## Results

STEERFAIR reduces performance variance across prompt orderings by an average of 10.86% accuracy points and 12.95 score points across three instruction-tuned VLMs (LLaVA-13B, IDEFICS-9B, InstructBLIP-13B) on ScienceQA, MME, and VGR. On ScienceQA, STEERFAIR achieves 65.46% average accuracy with 0.017 std for LLaVA (vs. 64.28%/0.024 vanilla), and on VGR it reduces std from 0.242 to 0.006 for InstructBLIP. On MME, STEERFAIR drops LLaVA's std from 65.42 to 2.72 while maintaining average score (1333.56 vs. 1333.43). The method requires no labels, using only 1000 unlabeled samples to identify bias directions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Inference-Time Intervention (ITI; Li et al., 2023), a supervised attention-steering method evaluated with 100 and 500 labels. STEERFAIR (unsupervised, zero labels) surpasses ITI(100) on average across all three datasets and models, with the most dramatic gains in standard deviation reduction (e.g., LLaVA on MME: 2.72 vs. 47.42 std; InstructBLIP on VGR: 0.006 vs. 0.303 std). Against ITI(500), STEERFAIR matches or exceeds performance in most settings: it wins on average accuracy for LLaVA on ScienceQA (65.46% vs. 64.05%) and InstructBLIP on ScienceQA (56.92% vs. 55.38%), and achieves lower std for LLaVA on MME (2.72 vs. 10.43) and VGR (0.054 vs. 0.091). The one notable exception is InstructBLIP on MME, where ITI(500) achieves a much lower std (0.75 vs. 15.03), though STEERFAIR still reports the highest average score (1185.93 vs. 1180.37). Overall, the paper demonstrates that an unsupervised method can match or beat a supervised method requiring 5× more labels, while needing no ground-truth annotations at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

STEERFAIR builds directly on the Inference-Time Intervention (ITI) framework of Li et al. (2023), which identifies a direction in a transformer's attention head space and steers activations along it, and on the residual-stream interpretation of Elhage et al. (2021) that defines the precise intervention point within the MHA computation. The key departure is that ITI requires labeled data to identify the steering direction, whereas STEERFAIR exploits the structural simplicity of order bias (e.g., 'always pick the first option') to construct unlabeled demonstrations and extract bias directions via PCA, eliminating the label dependency. This positions the work as an unsupervised, inference-time alternative to both supervised attention steering and output-space calibration methods (Zheng et al., 2023a; Pezeshkpour & Hruschka, 2023) that require multiple forward passes per sample.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that STEERFAIR offers a drop-in, training-free debiasing step that requires only a few hundred unlabeled samples and no ground-truth labels, making it applicable to proprietary or closed-source models where fine-tuning is infeasible. The demonstrated generalization of bias directions across datasets with the same task suggests that a single calibration pass could transfer to new benchmarks, reducing deployment cost. However, the method's reliance on a simple averaging of bias directions limits its effectiveness as the number of options grows (e.g., 5-option MCQs), and the absence of theoretical guarantees on when bias is separable in activation space leaves open the question of how broadly the approach generalizes beyond position-based biases to more complex or non-enumerable forms of model bias.
