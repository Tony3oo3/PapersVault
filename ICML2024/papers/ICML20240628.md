## Problem

Standard evaluation metrics for conditional sequence models (log-likelihoods, sequence recovery) are only relative comparisons between models and cannot assess whether a single model actually fits its data or whether it is reliable (i.e., falls back to conservative guesses when uncertain). No principled, absolute, and testable criterion existed for evaluating the goodness-of-fit or calibration of conditional models with sequence-valued outputs, leaving practitioners without a way to detect statistically significant mismatches or to tune hyperparameters like sampling temperature.

## Input / Output

The method consumes held-out data pairs (X_i, Y_i) drawn from the true joint distribution, model samples Ỹ_i ~ Q_|X_i (requiring the model to be sampleable, not merely able to score sequences), and user-specified kernels k_X on the input space and k_Y on the sequence space. It outputs an ACMMD² discrepancy score quantifying absolute model accuracy, an ACMMD-Rel² score quantifying reliability, and a binary hypothesis test decision at a user-chosen significance level α. The reliability estimator additionally requires R auxiliary model samples per data point to approximate the MMD-based kernel on the space of probability distributions P(Y), introducing a controllable bias that vanishes as R → ∞.

## Method

The core contribution is the Augmented Conditional MMD (ACMMD), which transforms the conditional comparison between P_| and Q_| into a joint-distribution comparison by constructing two augmented joints (P_X ⊗ P_| and P_X ⊗ Q_|) and computing their MMD under a tensor product kernel k_X ⊗ k_Y. This yields a divergence that is zero if and only if the conditionals match P_X-almost everywhere (under C_0-universal kernels), and admits an unbiased U-statistic estimator computable purely from data and model samples. For hypothesis testing, a wild bootstrap procedure (equivalent to random swapping of Y_i and Ỹ_i) provides exact non-asymptotic type-I error control at level α. For reliability, the ACMMD-Rel variant compares the conditional distribution of Y given the model's prediction Q_|X against the identity mapping, requiring a universal kernel on P(Y) constructed as an exponentiated MMD kernel (Proposition 4.2), with an approximate U-statistic estimator whose bias is controlled by the number of auxiliary samples R.

## Results

On the CATH S60 dataset (17,540 structures), ProteinMPNN at its default temperature T=1.0 yields an ACMMD² of 0.0916 (p-value < 0.01), leading to rejection of the null hypothesis that the model fits the data. Sweeping the sampling temperature reveals that T=0.4 minimizes ACMMD² (best goodness-of-fit), while reliability (ACMMD-Rel²) continues to improve at even lower temperatures, indicating a trade-off between accuracy and calibration. Across 11 CATH superfamilies (≥500 proteins, length <100), the best-fitting family at T=1.0 is Homeodomain-like proteins (CATH 1.10.10.60), dominated by helical bundles, while the Immunoglobulin superfamily achieves the best fit at lower temperatures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is a methodology contribution rather than a model comparison study, so it does not benchmark ACMMD against competing evaluation metrics (e.g., log-likelihood, sequence recovery, or SKCE) in a head-to-head table. Instead, the internal comparisons focus on (1) different sampling temperatures of ProteinMPNN, showing that lowering T from 1.0 to 0.4 improves goodness-of-fit while T<0.4 further improves reliability at the cost of slightly reduced accuracy, and (2) different CATH superfamilies, revealing that model fit varies substantially across structural families and that the ranking is largely stable across temperatures. The synthetic toy experiment (Section 6.1) validates that ACMMD scales linearly with the perturbation |Δp| and that the associated test maintains the correct 0.05 type-I error rate while gaining power with more samples. The discriminative power study (Section 6.2.1) confirms that ACMMD² robustly increases with temperature shifts δT ∈ {0, 0.01, 0.05, 0.1}, and the ACMMD test correctly rejects H₀ at higher δT while maintaining the nominal false-positive rate at δT=0.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the kernel-based conditional goodness-of-fit framework of Jitkrittum et al. (2020) and the calibration testing framework of Widmann et al. (2021) and Glaser et al. (2023) from continuous output spaces to sequence-valued variables, a setting where prior methods are inapplicable. The core divergence (ACMMD) is built on the MMD of Gretton et al. (2012) applied to augmented joint distributions, with the conditional structure formalized via conditional mean embeddings (Park & Muandet, 2020). A key theoretical contribution is Proposition 4.2, which provides a general recipe for constructing universal kernels on the space of probability distributions over sequences, building on the discrete-mass kernel theory of Amin et al. (2023a). The wild-bootstrap hypothesis test follows the U-statistics framework of Serfling (2009) and the permutation-based error control of Domingo-Enrich et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first conditional goodness-of-fit test applicable to sequence models, filling a gap where prior kernel-based methods required continuous output spaces. For protein design practitioners, it provides absolute (not merely relative) evaluation metrics that can detect model misfit, guide hyperparameter tuning (e.g., sampling temperature), and assess reliability without requiring experimental validation. Open questions include whether the practical kernels used (ESM-2 and Gearnet embeddings) satisfy the universality conditions needed for the theoretical guarantees, whether statistical fit under ACMMD correlates with experimental design success rates, and how to perform fine-grained evaluation of specific sequence regions (e.g., CDR loops) rather than whole domains.
