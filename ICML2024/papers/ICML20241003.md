## Problem

Incremental gradient (IG) and single shuffle (SS) SGD are widely used in practice due to cache efficiency and strong empirical performance, yet their theoretical convergence guarantees for non-convex smooth functions have shown a linear dependence on dataset size n (O(n/ε)), making them appear n times slower than standard SGD. This contradicts empirical observations where shuffle-based methods consistently outperform SGD. The paper closes this gap by establishing convergence rates that remove the n-dependence from the optimization term.

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite sum of n L-smooth (possibly non-convex) functions, an arbitrary data ordering strategy (any deterministic or random sequence of indices, covering SGD, IG, SS, RR, and even degenerate cases like single-function sampling), and a step size γ. It produces a convergence rate bound on the time-averaged expected gradient norm ‖∇f(x_t)‖². Unusually, the analysis introduces an effective correlation time τ = ⌊1/(8√3·Lγ)⌋ that is not a hyperparameter of the algorithm but an internal parameter of the proof, adapting to the chosen step size rather than being fixed at the epoch length n.

## Method

The core contribution is a new proof technique based on perturbed iterate analysis with restart virtual sequences, where iterations are partitioned into chunks of size τ = Θ(1/Lγ) rather than the fixed epoch length n used in prior work. A new 'sequence correlation' quantity σ_τ² is introduced to measure how the data ordering affects convergence within each chunk, capturing the joint effect of ordering and individual gradient deviations from the full gradient. The key insight is that by adaptively choosing τ based on the step size (rather than fixing τ = n), the step size constraint relaxes from γ = O(1/Ln) to γ = O(1/L), enabling n-times larger steps per iteration. This yields an improved convergence rate of O(1/ε) in the optimization term for IG and SS (previously O(n/ε)), while also recovering the classical SGD rate as a special case and providing tight guarantees for degenerate orderings like single-function sampling.

## Results

The paper's headline result is a theoretical improvement in convergence rates for Incremental Gradient (IG) and Single Shuffle (SS) on non-convex smooth functions: the optimization term improves from O(n/ε) to O(1/ε), an n-fold speedup over prior best bounds. The full rate becomes LF₀·O(1/ε + min{nσ_SGD/ε^{3/2}, nσ_SGD²/ε²}), compared to the prior LF₀·O(n/ε + nσ_SGD/ε^{3/2}). Empirically, SS and RR consistently outperform standard SGD (sampling with replacement) across quadratic objectives, logistic regression (Australian, w1a, ionosphere, breast-cancer, a9a, rcv1 datasets), and neural network training (MNIST, CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18), with the gap widening at smaller learning rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Theoretically, this paper strictly improves upon all three prior convergence analyses for IG/SS: Mishchenko et al. (NeurIPS 2020), Lu et al. (ICLR 2022), and Mohtashami et al. (2022) all yield an O(n/ε) optimization term, while the new analysis achieves O(1/ε) by adaptively choosing the correlation period τ = Θ(1/Lγ) instead of fixing it to n. Experimentally, SS and RR consistently outperform standard SGD (Robbins & Monro, 1951) across all tested problems — quadratic objectives, logistic regression on six LibSVM datasets, and neural network training on MNIST and CIFAR-10 — with the advantage being most pronounced at smaller learning rates. Compared to Random Reshuffling (Nedić & Bertsekas, 2001), SS and RR show similar practical performance in most settings, though SS occasionally surpasses RR (e.g., CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 in Figure 4) while RR occasionally beats SS (e.g., quadratic objectives in Figure 1a). The authors note that SS is preferable in practice due to its fixed data order, which eliminates per-epoch reshuffling overhead and enables physical data distribution across devices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the perturbed iterate analysis framework (Mania et al., 2017; Stich & Karimireddy, 2022) and its recent adaptation to linearly correlated noise (Koloskova et al., 2023) to handle arbitrary, iterate-dependent noise perturbations arising from structured data orderings. The key departure from prior convergence analyses of incremental gradient and shuffle SGD (Lu et al., 2022b; Mohtashami et al., 2022; Mishchenko et al., 2020) is the introduction of an adaptive correlation period τ = Θ(1/Lγ) instead of the fixed epoch-length τ = n used previously, which removes the n-dependence from the optimization term. A new 'sequence correlation' quantity (Definition 4.2) unifies the classic SGD variance and the epoch-based variance from prior works into a single framework covering arbitrary data orderings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The n-fold improvement in the optimization term (from O(n/ε) to O(1/ε)) for incremental gradient and single shuffle provides the first theoretical justification matching the well-known empirical superiority of shuffling over uniform sampling in non-convex settings. The unified framework also yields tight guarantees for degenerate cases (e.g., single-function optimization) that prior epoch-based analyses could not handle. Open questions remain: whether the random reshuffling rate can be similarly improved (the current analysis gives √n worse stochastic term), and whether the new rates are tight (no matching lower bounds exist for non-convex IG/SS). For practitioners, the result reinforces the already-standard practice of using single shuffle in large-scale training, where its memory efficiency is a key advantage.
