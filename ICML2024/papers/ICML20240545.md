## Problem

Prior theoretical work on kernel-based distributional learning was almost exclusively limited to distributional regression analyzed via the Caponnetto–De Vito integral operator technique for kernel ridge regression, leaving distributional classification and other regularized risk minimization scenarios without oracle inequalities or generalization guarantees. No stability-based generalization bounds existed for the two-stage distributional sampling setting, and the general Hilbertian embedding framework (covering KMEs, sliced Wasserstein kernels, and future embeddings) lacked a unified theoretical treatment.

## Input / Output

Each training example consists of M_n i.i.d. samples S_1^(n),…,S_Mn^(n) drawn from an unknown distribution Q_n over a measurable space S, paired with a label y_n; the distribution Q_n itself is never observed. The method first maps each finite sample set to an empirical measure, embeds it into a Hilbert space H via a map Π (e.g., kernel mean embedding or sliced Wasserstein embedding), and then applies a kernel method on the embedded points. The learned hypothesis f_{D,λ} predicts by composing with Π: given a new distribution Q, the prediction is f_{D,λ}(ΠQ), supporting both real-valued regression and classification outputs.

## Method

The paper establishes two oracle inequalities for SVMs (regularized risk minimizers over an RKHS) in the distributional two-stage sampling setting, plus a stability-based generalization bound. The first inequality (Theorem 3.1) adapts the Lipschitz-continuity-of-SVMs argument from Steinwart & Christmann (2008, Thm 6.24), requiring strong differentiability and local Lipschitz conditions on the loss but only mild kernel assumptions; the second (Theorem 3.4) adapts the covering-number argument (Thm 6.25), needing only convexity and mild continuity of the loss but requiring a finite ε-net of the RKHS ball. Both bounds decompose the excess risk into an approximation error term, a term capturing the embedding estimation error (controlled by functions B_n that bound ‖Π̂(S)−Π(Q)‖ with high probability), and a standard concentration term. The stability-based result (Theorem 4.1–4.2) extends Bousquet & Elisseeff's β-stability framework to the distributional setting, yielding a generalization bound that separates the empirical risk gap from the expected embedding estimation error. All results are formulated for a general Hilbertian embedding Π and then specialized to kernel mean embeddings and sliced 2-Wasserstein distances, with concrete estimation bounds (e.g., MMD concentration for KMEs, log-concavity-based bounds for sliced Wasserstein) plugged in.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or quantitative benchmarks. The main contributions are two oracle inequalities (Theorem 3.1 and Theorem 3.4) bounding the excess regularized risk of SVM solutions in the distributional learning setting, and a stability-based generalization bound (Theorem 4.2) relating the true risk to the empirical risk of the learned hypothesis. The bounds are expressed in terms of comparison functions (K-class) and estimation error functions B_n(δ) that capture the two-stage sampling error, making them applicable to any Hilbertian embedding of probability distributions. Specializations to kernel mean embeddings (Corollaries 3.3, 3.6, 4.5) and sliced 2-Wasserstein embeddings (Corollaries B.2, B.3) yield concrete rates involving 1/√M_n for the embedding estimation error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper contains no empirical comparisons or baselines, as it is a theoretical contribution. In terms of theoretical scope, it extends prior work in several ways: (1) unlike Szabo et al. (2015) and Fang et al. (2020), which analyze only kernel ridge regression via the Caponnetto–De Vito integral operator technique, the oracle inequalities here cover general SVMs (including SVR with ε-insensitive loss and classification via SVMs); (2) unlike Meunier et al. (2022), which also relies on Caponnetto & De Vito (2007) and is restricted to distributional regression, the present results handle general loss functions and multiple learning scenarios; (3) the stability-based generalization bound (Theorem 4.2) is entirely new in the distributional learning literature, with no prior analogous result. The paper positions its oracle inequalities as distributional analogues of Theorems 6.24 and 6.25 from Steinwart & Christmann (2008), and the stability bound as a distributional analogue of Theorem 14.2 from Mohri et al. (2018).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical analysis of kernel-based distributional learning from the narrow setting of kernel ridge regression for distributional regression (Szabo et al., 2015; Fang et al., 2020; Meunier et al., 2022) to general regularized risk minimization over RKHSs (i.e., SVMs) covering classification, regression, and other scenarios. It adapts the oracle inequality techniques of Steinwart & Christmann (2008, Theorems 6.24–6.25) and the algorithmic stability framework of Bousquet & Elisseeff (2002) / Mohri et al. (2018) to the two-stage sampling setup, while formulating everything in the general Hilbertian embedding framework introduced by Meunier et al. (2022), thereby unifying KME-based and sliced Wasserstein-based approaches under a single theoretical umbrella.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

These are the first oracle inequalities and stability-based generalization bounds for distributional learning beyond KRR, filling a significant gap that limited the theoretical grounding of practically relevant scenarios like distributional classification (e.g., causal direction prediction, binary health status). The general Hilbertian embedding formulation means the results immediately apply to any future embedding method (e.g., kernel cumulant embeddings), making the framework future-proof. Key open questions remain: deriving learning rates requires bounding the approximation error function under distributional assumptions, removing boundedness assumptions on losses, and leveraging empirical process theory for sharper inequalities.
