## Problem

Existing crossmodal transfer methods (knowledge distillation, contrastive learning, parameter reuse) are designed for vision-language or audio-video pairs and fail to handle the structured, heterogeneous nature of tabular data with its mix of numerical and categorical attributes. Moreover, not all tabular attributes are visually relevant to a given image, so indiscriminate transfer can degrade image model performance. The paper asks: how can one selectively align specific tabular attributes with specific image channels to transfer only the relevant expert knowledge?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240883/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes paired RGB images (processed through a CNN backbone, default ResNet-50) and tabular records containing both numerical and categorical attributes (processed through an FT-Transformer). At inference, only the RGB image is required—the tabular modality is entirely absent. The output is a class label for classification tasks (e.g., 129 car models on DVM, binary attractiveness on CelebA) or a continuous value for regression tasks (e.g., pet popularity score, deal probability). The tabular data serves purely as auxiliary supervision during training to shape the image embedding space.

## Method

CHARMS establishes a channel-to-attribute alignment via optimal transport: it computes N×N sample-wise cosine similarity matrices for each image channel and each tabular attribute, constructs a cost matrix from their pairwise L2 distances, and solves an OT problem (Eq. 6) to obtain a transfer matrix T ∈ R^{D×C} that maps attributes to channels. K-Means clustering is applied to the CNN's feature channels to reduce redundancy before alignment. The training objective (Eq. 7) combines standard image and tabular classification losses with an auxiliary loss L_{i2t} that predicts each tabular attribute from its OT-weighted image channels—using MSE for numerical attributes and cross-entropy for categorical ones—thereby maximizing mutual information between the two modalities. The cost matrix is refreshed every 5 epochs as the feature extractors evolve, while the image backbone φ is updated continuously throughout training.

## Results

CHARMS achieves the best image-based performance across all six datasets: 91.75% accuracy on DVM (129-class), 86.61% on SUNAttribute, 82.20% on CelebA, 36.03% on PetFinder-adoption, 18.43 RMSE on Pawpularity, and 0.2495 RMSE on Avito. Notably, CHARMS surpasses the tabular-only baselines (LightGBM, FT-Transformer) on SUNAttribute, CelebA, and Pawpularity, demonstrating that transferred tabular knowledge can augment image predictions beyond what the table alone provides. Compared to the image-only ResNet50 baseline, CHARMS yields consistent gains of 2–4% accuracy on classification tasks and 0.18–0.21 RMSE reduction on regression tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHARMS outperforms all four crossmodal transfer baselines (KD, MFH, FMR, MMCL) on every dataset where they report results, with the largest margins on DVM (+4.3% over KD, +7.7% over MMCL) and Adoption (+0.7% over KD, +6.2% over MMCL). Against the image-only ResNet50, CHARMS improves accuracy by 4.3% on DVM, 3.0% on SUN, 0.7% on CelebA, and 1.3% on Adoption, while reducing RMSE by 0.18 on Pawpularity and 0.0017 on Avito. The tabular-only baselines (LightGBM, FT-Transformer) still dominate on DVM (97.5% vs. 91.8%), Adoption (41.0% vs. 36.0%), and Avito (0.229 vs. 0.250), but CHARMS surpasses them on SUN (86.6% vs. 85.6%), CelebA (82.2% vs. 79.6%), and Pawpularity (18.43 vs. 20.07), indicating that the transferred tabular knowledge genuinely enriches the image representation. MFH and FMR underperform even the image-only ResNet on several datasets (e.g., MFH drops to 75.1% on CelebA and 43.1 RMSE on Pawpularity), confirming that coarse or image-agnostic transfer strategies can be harmful. The paper also shows CHARMS outperforms CLIP (both linear-probe and fine-tuned variants) by 4–17% across datasets, attributing CLIP's weakness to information loss from text conversion and the modality gap in its embedding space.

## Relation to Prior Work

CHARMS extends the crossmodal transfer literature (FMR, MMCL, MFH, knowledge distillation) by specifically addressing the tabular-to-image setting, which prior methods designed for vision-language or audio-video pairs cannot handle due to tabular data's structured format and mixed numerical/categorical variables. The key methodological departure is the use of optimal transport to establish a sample-wise similarity-based alignment between image channels and tabular attributes, rather than relying on parameter projection, shared embedding subspaces, or output-level distillation. This OT-based channel-attribute matching, combined with mutual information maximization as the transfer objective, is the paper's central novelty over existing crossmodal transfer frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: expert knowledge recorded in tabular form (medical diagnoses, product specifications) can guide image model training when that knowledge is unavailable at inference time. The OT-based alignment produces interpretable channel-attribute correspondences (validated via GradCAM), offering a degree of explainability that black-box distillation methods lack. Limitations include reliance on sufficient mutual information between modalities, sensitivity to data imbalance from expensive annotation, and unaddressed test-time distribution shift. Open questions include extension to dense prediction tasks like segmentation and handling scenarios where tabular and image modalities have weak correlation.
