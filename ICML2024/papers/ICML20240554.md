## Problem

State-of-the-art active domain adaptation for semantic segmentation relies on prediction entropy as the data acquisition strategy, treating it as a proxy for overall model uncertainty. However, prediction uncertainty captures only prediction error and ignores data scarcity — the model's lack of exposure to certain classes in the target domain. This paper identifies that epistemic uncertainty, which encompasses both prediction error and data scarcity, is the more appropriate signal for selecting which pixels to label next, and shows that the hyperbolic radius in a hierarchy-free Poincaré ball setting naturally encodes data scarcity (ρ = −0.899 with class pixel frequency).

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from a labeled source domain (GTAV at 1280×720, SYNTHIA, or Cityscapes) and unlabeled target-domain images (Cityscapes or ACDC at 1280×640), producing per-pixel segmentation masks over 19 classes. Beyond the segmentation output, HALO generates a per-pixel acquisition score map computed as the element-wise product of the hyperbolic radius map and the prediction entropy map; pixels with the highest scores are selected for human annotation in successive active learning rounds under a fixed budget (2.2% or 5% of total target pixels).

## Method

HALO encodes pixel features into the Poincaré ball via a hyperbolic exponential map and classifies them with Hyperbolic Multinomial Logistic Regression (HyperMLR), without enforcing hierarchical labels. The key insight is that the Poincaré distance from the origin (hyperbolic radius) of each pixel embedding correlates strongly with the inverse frequency of its class in the target dataset, making it a natural estimator of data scarcity. The acquisition score is the element-wise product of the radius map and the prediction entropy map, which together approximate epistemic uncertainty (ρ = 0.824 with ensemble-based epistemic uncertainty) and outperform either signal alone by over 10 mIoU. A Hyperbolic Feature Reweighting (HFR) module, consisting of two linear layers with BatchNorm and ReLU, rescales normalized features before projection to prevent embeddings from approaching the ball boundary where gradients vanish, improving both training stability and final performance by ~1.6 mIoU.

## Results

HALO achieves state-of-the-art mIoU across all three active domain adaptation benchmarks: 74.5% on GTAV→Cityscapes (+3.3% over RIPU), 75.6% on SYNTHIA→Cityscapes (+4.2% over RIPU), and 66.4% on Cityscapes→ACDC (+2.9% over RIPU), all at a 5% labeling budget. With the SegFormer-B4 backbone, HALO further improves to 77.8% and 78.1% on the first two benchmarks. Notably, HALO is the first active learning method to surpass the fully supervised domain adaptation baseline (71.9% mIoU) using only 5% of target labels (74.5%, +2.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HALO consistently outperforms all compared active domain adaptation baselines. Against RIPU (Xie et al., 2022), the strongest prior method, HALO gains +1.2% mIoU at 2.2% budget and +3.3% at 5% on GTAV→CS, +2.4% and +4.2% on SYNTHIA→CS, and +0.9% and +2.9% on CS→ACDC. Against D²ADA (Wu et al., 2022), HALO improves by +3.2% (74.5 vs 71.3) on GTAV→CS and +2.9% (75.6 vs 72.7) on SYNTHIA→CS at 5% budget. Against LabOR (Shin et al., 2021), HALO leads by +4.2% mIoU at 2.2% budget on GTAV→CS. Against AADA (Su et al., 2020) and MADA (Ning et al., 2021), HALO achieves substantially higher mIoU (+15.2% and +9.6% respectively on GTAV→CS at 5%). In the hyperbolic training stability comparison (Table A2), HALO's HFR module outperforms Feature Clipping (Guo et al., 2022) by +1.2% mIoU (74.5 vs 73.3), while curriculum learning and initialization-based approaches are noted as incompatible with the ADA setup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the hyperbolic semantic segmentation framework of Atigh et al. (2022) and the active domain adaptation (ADA) pipeline of RIPU (Xie et al., 2022a), but departs from both in key ways. It reinterprets the hyperbolic radius—previously understood either as a hierarchical parent-to-child distance (Nickel & Kiela, 2017) or as an inverse uncertainty signal (Franco et al., 2023; Flaborea et al., 2023)—as a proxy for data scarcity, a third interpretation not previously articulated. For the acquisition strategy, it shifts from prediction uncertainty (entropy of pseudo-labels) used by all prior ADA methods to epistemic uncertainty, operationalized as the product of hyperbolic radius and prediction entropy, and introduces the Hyperbolic Feature Reweighting (HFR) module to address training instability specific to the Poincaré ball topology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HALO is the first active learning method to surpass the fully supervised domain adaptation baseline using only 5% of target labels, and it sets new SOTA across three synthetic-to-real and adverse-weather benchmarks. The radius-as-data-scarcity interpretation, while empirically well-supported (ρ = −0.899), lacks the rigorous mathematical formalization the authors themselves flag as a limitation, leaving open whether the relationship is a fundamental property of hyperbolic geometry or an artifact of the specific training setup. The reliance on large-scale synthetic pre-training (GTAV, SYNTHIA) and the acknowledged time cost of pixel-level annotation limit immediate practical adoption, and the method's generalization beyond semantic segmentation (e.g., detection, classification) remains untested.
