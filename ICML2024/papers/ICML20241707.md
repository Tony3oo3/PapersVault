## Problem

Existing freeze-thaw Bayesian optimization methods for hyperparameter optimization (e.g., DyHPO, DPL) require online retraining or fine-tuning of their neural network surrogates at every allocation step, introducing significant computational overhead, optimization instability, and additional hyper-hyperparameters. This paper eliminates the online refitting stage entirely by replacing it with in-context learning, enabling Bayesian learning curve extrapolation in a single forward pass without any model updates during the search.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241707/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a collection of partial learning curves—each pairing a hyperparameter setting (up to 10 dimensions, normalized to [0,1]) with observed performance at various training steps—along with a target extrapolation step, all normalized to [0,1]. It produces a discretized posterior predictive distribution over model performance at the target step (1000 equal-sized bins), generated in a single forward pass with the entire observation history H served as a permutation-invariant token sequence. The model is trained for context sizes up to 1,000 samples and HPO budgets up to 1,000 training steps.

## Method

FT-PFN is a 6-layer Transformer (14.69M parameters, 512-dim embeddings, 4 heads) trained as a Prior-Data Fitted Network on 2M synthetic datasets generated from a structured curve prior. The prior uses a randomly initialized neural network to map hyperparameters to parameters of a weighted combination of four basis functions (power law, exponential, inverse-log, Hill) with per-curve saturation/breaking points and Gaussian noise, capturing realistic learning curve behavior including divergence. At inference, the full observation history is fed as a contextual prompt and the PFN predicts the posterior predictive distribution in one forward pass—no gradient updates, fine-tuning, or hyper-hyperparameter tuning. The resulting ifBO method pairs FT-PFN with MFPI-random, a parameter-free acquisition function that randomizes both the extrapolation horizon (uniform in [1, b_max]) and the performance improvement threshold (log-uniform scaling of the gap to 1.0) at each step, effectively hedging across a portfolio of multi-fidelity probability-of-improvement criteria.

## Results

FT-PFN achieves substantially better log-likelihood (uncertainty calibration) than both DPL and DyHPO across all three benchmarks: e.g., at 400 context samples on LCBench, FT-PFN scores 1.876 vs. −14.577 (DPL) and −0.481 (DyHPO), while also achieving lower MSE (0.005 vs. 0.007 and 0.042). Inference is 10–100× faster: at 400 samples FT-PFN takes 0.225 s versus 17.686 s (DPL) and 16.860 s (DyHPO); at 1800 samples the gap widens to 1.715 s vs. 73.435 s and 166.491 s. When combined with the MFPI-random acquisition function, the resulting ifBO method achieves the best average rank across all benchmarks (LCBench, PD1, Taskset) and outperforms or closely competes with DPL and DyHPO in normalized regret, establishing new state-of-the-art in the low-budget HPO regime (1000 steps ≈ 20 full trainings).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPL (Kadra et al., 2023), FT-PFN dominates in both uncertainty calibration and speed: log-likelihood improves from −14.577 to 1.876 on LCBench (400 samples), a gap of over 16 nats, while runtime drops from 17.7 s to 0.23 s (~76×). DPL's very low log-likelihood reflects overly confident incorrect predictions due to its small ensemble size (5) and strong power-law assumption. Against DyHPO (Wistuba et al., 2022), FT-PFN again wins on log-likelihood (1.876 vs. −0.481 on LCBench) and MSE (0.005 vs. 0.042), with DyHPO's limitation being its inability to extrapolate beyond a single step effectively. In end-to-end HPO (Figure 3), ifBO outperforms both DPL and DyHPO on LCBench and Taskset in normalized regret, and competes closely on PD1, while consistently achieving the best average rank across all three benchmarks. Against the multi-fidelity baselines Hyperband (Li et al., 2017) and ASHA (Li et al., 2020b), all freeze-thaw methods (ifBO, DPL, DyHPO) clearly outperform in the low-budget regime, confirming the advantage of dynamic resource allocation over coarse-grained fidelity scheduling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: freeze-thaw Bayesian optimization (Swersky et al., 2014; Wistuba et al., 2022; Kadra et al., 2023), which allocates training resources one step at a time using a surrogate model and acquisition function, and prior-data fitted networks (Müller et al., 2022), which perform Bayesian inference in a single forward pass via in-context learning. The key departure from prior freeze-thaw implementations (DyHPO, DPL) is replacing their online surrogate refitting at every allocation step with a pre-trained PFN that takes the observed partial learning curves as context, eliminating iterative training overhead. The curve prior design draws on learning curve extrapolation literature (Domhan et al., 2015; Klein et al., 2017; Adriaensen et al., 2023) but extends it with a richer set of basis functions that can model divergence and saturation breakpoints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10–100× speedup in surrogate inference and the elimination of online refitting make freeze-thaw BO substantially more practical for real-world HPO pipelines, while the new SOTA on LCBench, PD1, and Taskset validates the approach in the low-budget regime (~20 full trainings) that is most relevant to modern deep learning. However, the method is currently limited to budgets ≤1000 steps, ≤10 hyperparameters, and normalized [0,1] inputs, leaving open the question of whether it can scale to the much larger budgets and higher-dimensional spaces encountered in LLM pretraining. The finding that standard EI acquisitions underperform a simple randomized PI when paired with a well-calibrated in-context surrogate also raises broader questions about how acquisition function design should adapt to the uncertainty characteristics of the underlying model.
