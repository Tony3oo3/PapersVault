## Problem

Standard multi-step time series forecasting minimizes average loss over the prediction window, which can produce highly disparate error distributions across individual time steps—particularly in transformer architectures where loss landscapes are non-monotonic and non-linear. This lack of per-step error control is problematic in applications like financial risk assessment or model predictive control where specific time-step errors matter. The paper addresses this gap by formulating forecasting as a constrained learning problem that explicitly bounds the expected loss at each prediction step while still optimizing average performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a context window of T_c multivariate time series features (e.g., 320 features for ECL, 20 for Weather, 7 for Exchange Rate) and produces T_p future output values with prediction windows of 96, 192, 336, or 720 steps (24–60 for ILI). Unlike standard ERM which only optimizes the window-averaged loss, the constrained formulation evaluates and constrains the expected loss at each individual time step i within the prediction window, with user-specified upper bounds ε_i that can be constant across all steps or monotonically increasing to reflect expected error growth.

## Method

The paper formulates multi-step forecasting as a constrained optimization problem (P-LS) that minimizes average loss subject to per-step loss upper bounds, then leverages Lagrangian duality to show a bounded duality gap despite non-convex transformer parametrizations. A primal-dual algorithm (Algorithm 1) alternates between gradient descent on model parameters with a time-weighted loss (weights determined by current dual multipliers), subgradient ascent on dual variables, and—optionally—gradient updates on slack variables ζ that relax infeasible constraints. The resilient variant (R-LS) introduces a differentiable convex cost h(ζ) on constraint relaxation, allowing the algorithm to automatically identify and loosen the most difficult constraints while preserving the overall loss-shaping objective. Approximation guarantees from Chamon & Ribeiro (2020) and Hounie et al. (2023) bound the duality gap in terms of the optimal dual variables, reflecting how challenging the constraints are to satisfy.

## Results

Across 288 experiment settings (8 architectures × 9 datasets × 4 prediction window lengths), the loss shaping constrained approach reduces Window STD (standard deviation of per-step MSE across the prediction window) by an average of 3.47% while simultaneously reducing average MSE by 4.47% compared to standard ERM training. The resilient variant further improves feasibility and, in several settings (e.g., iTransformer on ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2), achieves the lowest overall MSE among all three training paradigms. On the ECL dataset, exponential constraints achieve the lowest MSE for 2 out of 3 tested architectures across all window lengths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is standard Empirical Risk Minimization (ERM), i.e., unconstrained training that minimizes average MSE over the prediction window. The constrained (Ours) and resilient (Ours+R) variants are compared against ERM across all 288 settings. On average, constrained training reduces Window STD by 3.47% and MSE by 4.47% relative to ERM, indicating that the loss shaping constraint flattens the per-step error distribution without sacrificing (and often improving) aggregate accuracy. The resilient variant consistently outperforms hard constraints in feasibility: where hard constraints fail (infeasible at end of training), the resilient approach relaxes the most difficult constraints and recovers a feasible, often lower-MSE solution. However, the method does not uniformly dominate ERM: in some settings (e.g., Autoformer on ETTm2 at 720 steps, where ERM MSE is 0.271 vs. Ours+R at 0.336), the constrained approach trades off higher average MSE for a flatter error landscape. The paper does not perform head-to-head comparisons against other loss shaping or reweighting methods (e.g., CARD, Park et al. 2023), limiting direct competitive positioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the resilient constrained learning framework of Hounie et al. (2023) and the PAC constrained learning theory of Chamon & Ribeiro (2020) to the specific problem of multi-step time series forecasting, where per-timestep loss bounds replace the generic constraint structure. It departs from prior loss-shaping approaches (Park et al. 2023, Wang et al. 2024) that impose fixed or data-driven weightings on the loss, instead formulating the requirement as an explicit constraint on expected per-step loss, which is more interpretable and adapts to the model's actual difficulty at each step. The primal-dual algorithm leverages convergence results for non-convex parametrizations from Chamon et al. (2022), making it applicable to transformer architectures without convexity assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: state-of-the-art transformer forecasters can produce highly non-uniform error distributions across the prediction window, which is problematic for applications like financial risk assessment or model predictive control. The resilient formulation gracefully handles infeasible constraints by relaxing the hardest ones, and the bounded duality gap provides step-level approximation guarantees. Open questions include sensitivity to constraint specification (the paper uses a simple grid search over six values), the generalization gap that can render training-feasible constraints ineffective at test time, and whether the approach extends to other loss metrics or non-transformer architectures beyond the single FiLM model tested.
