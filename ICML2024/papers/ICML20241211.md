## Problem

Existing frame averaging methods for equivariant learning either sum over large frames whose size scales exponentially with dimension (e.g., 2^d for O(d)) or resort to sampling-based approximations that sacrifice exact equivariance. Moreover, prior frame averaging was restricted to a narrow set of groups (primarily O(d) and SO(d)), leaving important symmetry groups such as the Lorentz group O(1,d−1), the unitary group U(d), and the permutation group S_n without efficient exact-equivariance guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method is architecture-agnostic: it wraps any non-equivariant backbone Φ and produces a G-equivariant (or G-invariant) function by averaging over a provably minimal frame. Inputs are point clouds P ∈ R^{d×n}, graph adjacency matrices A ∈ Sym(n,R), or atomic structures, and the output is the equivariant/invariant transformation of Φ's output (particle positions, class labels, energy values, convex hull volumes, etc.). A key practical detail is that for full-column-rank inputs the minimal frame contains exactly one element, reducing the frame averaging operator to a single forward pass of the backbone with a fixed linear transformation Q₀ applied to the output.

## Method

MFA constructs the minimal frame F̂(x) = h·Stab_G(c(x)) by identifying a canonical form c(x) (a unique representative in the orbit of x) and computing its stabilizer. For the linear algebraic group G_η(d) (encompassing O(d), O(1,d−1), U(d), SO(d), SU(d), etc.), the paper develops a generalized QR decomposition that handles non-Euclidean pseudo-inner products, null vectors, and determinant constraints, yielding a unique canonical form R̂ and a frame of size 1 for full-rank inputs. Ambiguities from non-full-rank inputs are resolved via induced G-sets: a G-equivariant map φ (e.g., column selection or P^T η P) transforms the input to a space where canonicalization is well-defined, and the frame is pulled back via composition. For the permutation group S_n acting on graphs, the minimal frame is computed directly via the Nauty canonical labeling algorithm, which simultaneously yields the canonical form and the graph automorphism group (stabilizer). The resulting frame averaging operator reduces to a single backbone evaluation ⟨Φ⟩(P) = Q₀·Φ(Q̂⁻¹P), achieving exact equivariance at the cost of one forward pass.

## Results

On the E(3)-equivariant n-body problem, MFA-GNN achieves the best MSE of 0.0036 and fastest inference time of 0.0023 s, outperforming all dedicated equivariant architectures (SEGNN: 0.0043, CGENN: 0.0039) and the prior frame-averaging method FA-GNN (0.0054). On OC20 relaxed energy prediction, MFAENET reduces the invariance error from 6.199×10⁻² (FAENET) to 8.809×10⁻⁶ — a three-order-of-magnitude improvement — while maintaining competitive MAE (0.5437 vs 0.5446 eV in-domain). On the Sₙ-invariant GRAPH8C graph separation task, MFA-MLP achieves a perfect invariance error of 0.00 in 1.10 ms, whereas the sampling-based FA-MLP requires 256 samples (28.09 ms) to reach 0.09. On the Sₙ×O(5) convex hull task, MFA-MLP achieves MSE 1.5291, substantially outperforming PointNet (4.1475) and CGENN (3.5831).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MFA-GNN outperforms all dedicated equivariant GNN baselines on the n-body task: it beats SEGNN (0.0043) and CGENN (0.0039) on MSE, and is faster than all of them in inference time (0.0023 s vs 0.0025–0.0045 s). Against the prior frame-averaging method of Puny et al. (FA-GNN), MFA-GNN improves MSE from 0.0054 to 0.0036 while halving inference time (0.0023 vs 0.0041 s), attributable to the minimal frame having only 1 element versus 2^d for FA. On OC20, MFAENET dramatically outperforms FAENET (Duval et al., 2023b) in invariance error (8.8×10⁻⁶ vs 6.2×10⁻²) with comparable throughput (3919.8 vs 3863.3 samples/s), though FAENET achieves a slightly lower out-of-domain MAE. On graph separation (GRAPH8C/EXP), both FA-MLP/GIN and MFA-MLP/GIN achieve 0 failures, but MFA-MLP is 25× faster in inference (1.10 ms vs 28.09 ms for FA-MLP-256) and achieves zero invariance error versus 0.09×10⁻². On the convex hull task, MFA-MLP (MSE 1.5291) substantially outperforms both PointNet (4.1475) and CGENN (3.5831), while also achieving the lowest invariance error (2×10⁻⁷ vs 8×10⁻³ and 5×10⁻²). For top tagging under O(1,3), MFA-MINKGNN marginally improves accuracy over the non-invariant MINKGNN backbone (94.2178% vs 94.2136%), with the small gap attributed to the large training set (1M+ jets) allowing the backbone to learn symmetries implicitly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the frame averaging framework of Puny et al. (2021), showing that their eigendecomposition-based canonicalization is a special case of the more general induced G-set approach, and that their Theorem 3 is subsumed by the minimal frame construction via canonical form and stabilizer. The group averaging operator from Yarotsky (2022) provides the theoretical starting point, while the graph automorphism algorithms of McKay & Piperno (2014) supply the practical machinery for the permutation group case. The key departure is replacing the 2^d-sized eigendecomposition frame with a generalized QR decomposition that yields a single-element frame for full-rank inputs, and extending the framework from O(d)/SO(d) to the broader linear algebraic group G_η(d) encompassing the Lorentz group, unitary group, and their special subgroups.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: MFA achieves exact equivariance with a single backbone forward pass (frame size 1) where prior methods require 2^d evaluations or sampling-based approximations, and it is robust to degenerate eigenvalues that cause prior methods to produce infinite frames. The extension to the Lorentz group and unitary group opens frame averaging to particle physics and complex-valued domains previously inaccessible to this paradigm. Limitations include the focus on unstructured data (point clouds, graphs) rather than regular grids, the NP-intermediate complexity of graph automorphism for the S_n case, and the fact that on large datasets like OC20 (>500K samples), the invariance error advantage may be less critical for out-of-domain generalization. Open questions include whether the induced G-set approach can be extended to learned canonicalization functions and whether the framework applies to architectures on regular grids.
