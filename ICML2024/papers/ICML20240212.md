## Problem

Fully stochastic infinitely deep Bayesian neural networks (SDE-BNNs) provide principled uncertainty quantification in the continuous-depth limit but suffer from prohibitive training and inference costs due to simulating Brownian motion over the entire weight trajectory. Partial stochasticity—restricting randomness to a subset of parameters—has been shown to match or exceed fully stochastic performance in finite-depth networks, but no principled way existed to partition weights into stochastic and deterministic subsets in the infinite-depth regime, and it was unclear whether such models retain universal approximation guarantees.

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard grayscale (MNIST, 28×28) or RGB (CIFAR-10, 32×32) images, which are augmented to match the hidden-state dimension d_h before entering the continuous-time dynamics. The output is a class label accompanied by a full predictive distribution, from which calibrated uncertainty estimates (expected calibration error, predictive entropy) and OOD-detection scores are derived. The model is discriminative: it learns the conditional distribution p(y|x) through continuous latent variables rather than generating samples.

## Method

PSDE-BNNs partition the weight trajectory w_t over [0,1] into stochastic and deterministic segments via either a vertical cut (SDE dynamics on a sub-interval [t₁,t₂], ODE elsewhere) or a horizontal cut (SDE on a subset of weight dimensions, ODE on the rest), with the diffusion coefficient g_p fixed to match the prior to guarantee finite path-space KL divergence. The hidden state h_t evolves via an ODE driven by a convolutional network f_h whose parameters are w_t, while w_t itself is governed by a Neural SDE/ODE whose drift f_q is a hypernetwork parameterised by θ. Training maximises an ELBO that combines the data likelihood with a KL penalty on the path space, computed via stochastic variational inference with the adjoint method for backpropagation through the ODE segments. The paper proves that fully stochastic infinitely deep BNNs with linear output layers fail to be Universal Conditional Distribution Approximators, while PSDE-BNNs (with augmented input and sufficient dimensionality) do qualify, leveraging the Brownian motion as an infinite-dimensional noise source to satisfy the Noise Outsourcing Lemma.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, PSDE-BNN ODEFirst achieves 87.84% accuracy with 4.73×10⁻² ECE, outperforming the fully stochastic SDE-BNN baseline (86.04% accuracy, 9.13×10⁻² ECE) while training 74.1% faster overall. On MNIST, PSDE-BNN ODEFirst reaches 99.30% accuracy with 0.62×10⁻² ECE, matching or exceeding all baselines. The SDE-BNN, when given the same training time as PSDE-BNN, drops to 69.94% accuracy on CIFAR-10, highlighting the efficiency gap. Ablation studies show that a 10% stochasticity ratio suffices for competitive performance, with no meaningful trade-off between stochasticity ratio and downstream metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the fully stochastic SDE-BNN (Xu et al., 2022), PSDE-BNN ODEFirst wins on both accuracy (+1.80% on CIFAR-10, +0.75% on MNIST) and ECE (4.73 vs. 9.13 on CIFAR-10), while also training 74.1% faster overall (100 epochs vs. 300 epochs, with ~27% faster per-epoch time). Against the deterministic ResNet32 (He et al., 2016), PSDE-BNN ODEFirst is competitive on accuracy (87.84% vs. 87.35% on CIFAR-10) but substantially outperforms it on calibration (4.73 vs. 8.47 ECE), without requiring last-layer Laplace approximation. Against ODENet (Hu et al., 2020), PSDE-BNN ODEFirst achieves slightly lower CIFAR-10 accuracy (87.84% vs. 88.30%) but nearly halves the ECE (4.73 vs. 8.71). Against MFVI ResNet 32 (Blundell et al., 2015), PSDE-BNN ODEFirst edges out on CIFAR-10 accuracy (87.84% vs. 86.97%) but trails on ECE (4.73 vs. 3.04); however, PSDE-BNN SDEFirst narrows this gap to 3.56 ECE. On OOD detection (CIFAR-10-C), PSDE-BNN models achieve AUC 0.86–0.88 versus 0.84 for SDE-BNN, and show higher predictive entropy on corrupted inputs, indicating better uncertainty sensitivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SDE-BNN framework of Xu et al. (2022), which models infinitely deep Bayesian neural networks via Neural SDEs, by introducing partial stochasticity into the continuous-time weight evolution. The conceptual bridge comes from Sharma et al. (2023), who showed that finite-depth BNNs do not require full stochasticity for competitive uncertainty quantification, and from Dupont et al. (2019)'s Augmented Neural ODEs, which the expressivity proof relies on. The key departure is that prior partial-stochasticity work was confined to discrete-depth settings, whereas here the authors formalize vertical (temporal) and horizontal (dimensional) weight partitioning within the SDE/ODE hybrid dynamics of an infinite-depth network, and prove that this restricted stochasticity is actually necessary for universal conditional distribution approximation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a 74% reduction in total training time and ~26% faster inference relative to fully stochastic SDE-BNNs, making infinite-depth Bayesian networks more deployable. The theoretical result that fully stochastic infinite-depth BNNs fail as UCDAs while partially stochastic ones succeed is a non-trivial expressivity insight that reframes the role of stochasticity in continuous-depth models. However, empirical validation is limited to MNIST and CIFAR-10, no optimized solver exists yet for the horizontal-cut variant, and the paper leaves open whether combining both cut types, scaling to ImageNet, or incorporating variance reduction techniques (e.g., Sticking the Landing) would further improve performance and stability.
