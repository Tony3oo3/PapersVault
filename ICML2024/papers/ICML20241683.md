## Problem

Ensemble methods for out-of-distribution (OoD) generalization produce redundant models, and existing pruning techniques rely on supervised diversity metrics from training or validation data that do not transfer to shifted test distributions. The problem of selecting a complementary model subset that maximizes predictive diversity on unseen OoD data—without access to test annotations—remains unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pool of N pretrained ResNet-50 models (trained on ImageNet, operating on 224×224 RGB images), unlabeled test samples (used only for forward passes to compute Fisher Information Matrices), and a validation set from training domains (for the in-distribution accuracy Gram matrix). It outputs a binary selection vector z of length N with exactly K entries set to 1, identifying the pruned sub-ensemble; the final prediction is produced by averaging model weights or outputs over the selected subset.

## Method

The framework has two stages. First, Topology Learning constructs a weighted graph over the model pool where edge weights W_ij = exp(−D_ij²/2) are derived from diagonal Fisher Information Matrix distances computed on unlabeled test data, capturing parameter-output sensitivity without requiring labels. Second, Learning on Topology formulates a quadratic integer program that jointly minimizes an in-distribution accuracy term (from a normalized Gram matrix on validation data) and an OoD diversity term (from the topology adjacency matrix), weighted by a hyperparameter λ. The NP-hard binary problem is made tractable via semi-definite relaxation (introducing Z = zzᵀ with Z ⪰ 0) solved by standard SDP solvers, followed by a rounding step that selects the K models with the largest diagonal entries of Z. Theoretical guarantees show the pruned ensemble achieves higher average pairwise distance than the full pool (Lem. 4.1) and that the diversity term in the bias-variance-diversity decomposition reduces expected ensemble risk (Thm. 4.4).

## Results

On the multi-source OoD benchmark (DomainBed), DiWA + Ours achieves 79.9% average accuracy on VLCS and 52.1% on Terra Incognita, outperforming the uniform ensemble (79.3% / 51.3%) and greedy selection (79.2% / 50.4%) by 0.6–0.8% and 0.7–1.7% respectively. On single-source OoD generalization, DiWA + Ours reaches 73.6% on VLCS and 39.8% on Terra Incognita, surpassing SWAD (70.6% / 38.0%) and all other single-model baselines. The method also consistently outperforms traditional ensemble pruning methods (DREP, DivP, Spectral, SDP, COMEP) on both datasets, confirming that in-distribution diversity metrics do not transfer to OoD data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against single-model OoD baselines (ERM, SagNet, RSC, SWAD), DiWA + Ours achieves the highest average accuracy on both VLCS (79.9% vs. 79.1% for SWAD) and Terra Incognita (52.1% vs. 50.0% for SWAD), with the largest gap over ERM (46.1% on TI). Among ensemble selection strategies, the proposed topology-informed pruning consistently beats both uniform averaging and greedy selection across all three ensemble backbones (DENS, DiWA, Model Ratatouille), with the most pronounced gain on Terra Incognita where greedy selection actually underperforms uniform by 0.9%. Compared to traditional ensemble pruning methods (DREP, DivP, Spectral, SDP, COMEP), the proposed method achieves the best accuracy on both datasets (52.1% on TI, 79.9% on VLCS), while all traditional methods fail to substantially exceed the uniform ensemble baseline, confirming that in-distribution diversity metrics do not transfer to OoD data. On single-source OoD generalization, DiWA + Ours outperforms SWAD by 3.0% on VLCS (73.6% vs. 70.6%) and 1.8% on Terra Incognita (39.8% vs. 38.0%), and surpasses greedy DiWA selection by 2.1% on VLCS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SDP-based ensemble pruning formulation of Zhang et al. (2006) by augmenting the in-distribution accuracy objective with a topology term computed from Fisher Information Matrix distances on unlabeled test data, thereby addressing the gap that supervised diversity metrics do not transfer under distribution shift. The theoretical analysis adapts the bias-variance-diversity decomposition from Wood et al. (2023) to provide guarantees that the pruned ensemble promotes diversity and reduces generalization error on target distributions. Conceptually, the work bridges two previously separate threads—ensemble pruning (well-studied for in-distribution data) and OoD ensemble methods like DiWA, DENS, and Model Ratatouille—by proposing a post-hoc, label-free selection step that can be layered on top of any existing ensemble construction pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically confirms a practical gap: existing ensemble pruning methods (ranking, clustering, optimization-based) fail to outperform uniform ensembling under distribution shift, validating the need for OoD-aware selection. The model-agnostic, test-time-only design (no retraining, no labels) makes the approach broadly applicable to any ensemble pipeline, and the SDP relaxation matching brute-force performance offers a practical solution to an NP-hard selection problem. Key limitations include the empirical (rather than principled) choice of ensemble size K, and the modest absolute gains (0.6–1.7% over uniform ensembles) suggest the technique is a useful refinement rather than a transformative advance. An open question is whether the Fisher-based topology generalizes beyond the ResNet-50/ImageNet setting or to non-visual modalities.
