## Problem

The reverse-KL regularized contextual bandit is the standard mathematical formulation underlying practical RLHF pipelines (InstructGPT, LLaMA2, Claude), yet a rigorous theoretical analysis of this formulation has remained open. Existing theoretical frameworks assume deterministic reward-greedy optimal policies, which contradict the stochastic nature of generative models and fail to capture the KL constraint that prevents reward hacking and preserves output diversity. This paper provides the first comprehensive finite-sample theoretical analysis in offline, online, and hybrid settings for the KL-regularized formulation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM serving as the reference policy π₀, a prompt distribution d₀, and preference data consisting of (prompt, response₁, response₂, preference label) tuples following the Bradley-Terry model. It outputs an aligned LLM policy constrained to the Gibbs distribution family relative to π₀, which generates text responses. In the online/hybrid setting, the agent iteratively queries human feedback on response pairs generated by two policy variants (main agent and enhancer), with batch size m per iteration, and the offline dataset is retained in the data mixture throughout training.

## Method

The core contribution is the GSHF (Gibbs Sampling from Human Feedback) framework, which formulates RLHF as a reverse-KL regularized contextual bandit where the optimal policy is a closed-form Gibbs distribution π_r ∝ π₀ · exp(r/η). For offline learning, it introduces pessimistic reward estimation using elliptical-potential-based uncertainty bonuses (‖φ(x,a)−ν‖_{Σ⁻¹}) to provably handle distribution shift, yielding two algorithmic options: a modified objective with expected uncertainty (Option I) or a pointwise pessimistic reward fed into the policy oracle (Option II). For online/hybrid learning, it employs a non-symmetric two-agent structure: a main agent that exploits all historical data via MLE-based KL-regularized policy optimization, and an enhancer that strategically explores by maximizing relative uncertainty subject to a KL budget constraint. Practical implementations include an iterative DPO variant (Hybrid-GSHF-DPO) that adds adaptive margins proportional to uncertainty differences per preference pair, and a multi-step rejection sampling strategy where the enhancer uses best-of-n sampling. The theoretical guarantees show sample complexity scales with reward model dimension d rather than generative model complexity, and that online exploration provably improves the reward-KL trade-off frontier over purely offline training.

## Results

On the UltraFeedback evaluation, Hybrid-GSHF-DPO achieves a Gold Reward of 2.67 and a Gold Win Rate of 0.67, outperforming offline DPO (2.15 / 0.50) and RSO (2.25 / 0.54). In GPT-4 pairwise evaluation, Hybrid-GSHF-DPO scores 0.65 versus 0.50 for DPO and 0.53 for RSO. On out-of-distribution (OOD) prompts, the method attains an OOD Gold Reward of 2.46 with a smaller in-domain-to-OOD gap (Δ = 0.21) compared to DPO (Δ = 0.44) and RSO (Δ = 0.36), indicating reduced reward over-optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hybrid-GSHF-DPO consistently outperforms both DPO and RSO across all reported metrics. Against DPO, it improves Gold Reward by 0.52 (2.67 vs 2.15), Gold Win Rate by 0.17 (0.67 vs 0.50), and GPT-4 evaluation score by 0.15 (0.65 vs 0.50), while also halving the in-domain-to-OOD reward gap (0.21 vs 0.44). Against RSO, the gains are 0.42 in Gold Reward (2.67 vs 2.25), 0.13 in Gold WR (0.67 vs 0.54), and 0.12 in GPT-4 eval (0.65 vs 0.53). The method also surpasses the stronger Multi-Step RSO variant by 0.08 in Gold Reward and 0.04 in Gold WR, while maintaining a comparable OOD gap (0.21 vs 0.18). The OOD results confirm that the online iterative exploration reduces reward over-optimization relative to purely offline baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first rigorous finite-sample theoretical analysis of the reverse-KL regularized contextual bandit formulation that underlies virtually all modern LLM alignment pipelines (InstructGPT, Claude, LLaMA2). Prior theoretical RLHF work either assumed a deterministic reward-greedy optimal policy (Agarwal et al., 2019) or studied simpler dueling-bandit settings, leaving the stochastic-policy KL-regularized formulation unanalyzed despite its ubiquity in practice. The practical algorithms emerge naturally from the theory: the offline pessimistic variant connects to RSO (Liu et al., 2023a), while the online iterative variant generalizes DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) with an uncertainty-aware margin, and the policy improvement oracle is instantiated via PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) or DPO-style direct preference losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing that the standard RLHF objective admits provably efficient algorithms in offline, online, and hybrid settings, this work gives practitioners a principled justification for iterative alignment loops and clarifies when online exploration is genuinely necessary versus when diverse offline data suffices. The finding that sample complexity scales with reward-model complexity (not generative-model complexity) reinforces the value of the two-stage reward-modeling + policy-optimization pipeline over pure direct-preference approaches. Open questions include extending the analysis to general (nonlinear) function approximation, developing principled uncertainty estimators for neural-network reward models, and understanding multi-turn and multi-objective alignment within this framework.
