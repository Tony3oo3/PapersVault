## Problem

Retrieval-augmented language models (RALMs) face a fundamental tension: retrieving only a few relevant sentences loses complete contextual semantics, while retrieving entire documents causes LLMs to get 'lost in the long context' and be distracted by irrelevant text. This paper asks whether one can preserve full contextual semantics while remaining robust to lengthy, noisy reference contexts, without requiring fine-tuning or additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241365/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language query, a retrieved reference context (potentially thousands of tokens of document text), and task instructions as input. It produces a modified version of the reference context where key lexical units are wrapped in markdown bold syntax (**) at a user-chosen granularity (paragraph, sentence, or word level), which is then concatenated with the query and instructions as the final prompt to the LLM. The LLM's generated response is the ultimate output. No model weights are modified; the entire pipeline operates as a zero-shot, plug-and-play preprocessing step.

## Method

COFT is a three-stage framework. The Recaller performs named entity recognition on the query, expands candidates via one-hop neighbor lookup in the Wikidata knowledge graph, and retains only entities that also appear in the reference context. The Scorer computes a contextual weight for each retained entity as the product of a sentence-level TF-ISF score (adapting TF-IDF to the sentence granularity) and a self-information score derived from Llama-7B's token-level output probabilities over the query-context concatenation. The Selector applies a dynamic threshold τ = 0.5 × (τ_len + τ_info), where τ_len and τ_info are min-max normalized values of the reference context's length and informativeness, to filter the top-weighted entities and highlight their occurrences at the chosen granularity. This coarse-to-fine highlighting (paragraph → sentence → word) directs the LLM's attention to key texts while preserving the full document structure, achieving up to 32.1% average F1 improvement on the FELM hallucination benchmark across Vicuna-33B, ChatGPT, and GPT-4 backbones.

## Results

On the FELM knowledge hallucination benchmark, COFT achieves an average F1 improvement of 32.1% over the best existing method across three backbone LLMs (Vicuna-33B, ChatGPT, GPT-4) and three domains (World Knowledge, Sci/Tech, Wri/Rec). Specifically, COFT_w reaches 81.6 F1 on WK, 84.4 on Sci/Tech, and 81.1 on Wri/Rec with ChatGPT, and 87.3 F1 on WK with GPT-4. On reading comprehension (RACE-H/M), COFT improves precision by 7.8% and 3.7% over the best baseline with ChatGPT. On question answering under 80% noise, COFT achieves up to 10.5% F1 improvement over baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COFT consistently and substantially outperforms all four baselines (CoT, RALM, CoVe, CoN) across every backbone and domain. Against CoT, which often degrades performance below vanilla (e.g., ChatGPT WK drops from 9.1 to 2.6 F1), COFT_w achieves 81.6 F1 — a gap of over 79 points. Against RALM (BM25 retrieval), COFT_w improves ChatGPT WK F1 from 25.2 to 81.6 and GPT-4 WK from 53.6 to 87.3, demonstrating that simply retrieving more text without highlighting key entities is insufficient. Against CoVe (chain-of-verification), COFT_w leads by 61.6 F1 points on ChatGPT WK (81.6 vs 20.0) and 37.6 on GPT-4 WK (87.3 vs 49.7). Against CoN (chain-of-note), the closest competitor, COFT_w still leads by 63.4 F1 on ChatGPT WK (81.6 vs 18.2) and 18.5 on GPT-4 WK (87.3 vs 52.8). The ablation study further confirms that removing the recaller or scorer causes the largest performance drops, validating that entity extraction and contextual-weight scoring are the critical differentiators over prior retrieval-augmented and chain-of-X approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

COFT positions itself within the retrieval-augmented language model (RALM) lineage, addressing the well-documented 'lost in the middle' problem where LLMs degrade on long retrieved contexts. Rather than following the chain-of-X prompting tradition (CoT, CoVe, CoN) that adds reasoning steps, or simply retrieving more relevant passages, COFT introduces a preprocessing pipeline that highlights key entities within the full context using a knowledge graph (Wikidata) for candidate extraction, a TF-ISF + self-information scoring mechanism (adapting Sparck Jones's TF-IDF to sentence level), and a dynamic threshold selector. The method is explicitly designed as a plug-and-play complement to existing RALMs, operating at the input level rather than modifying the LLM's generation process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COFT offers a practical, training-free solution to a persistent deployment problem: LLMs fed lengthy retrieved documents lose focus on critical information. The plug-and-play nature (works with ChatGPT, GPT-4, Vicuna-33B without fine-tuning) and minimal inference overhead (~12%) make it immediately deployable in production RAG pipelines. However, the authors acknowledge a key limitation: COFT cannot update the LLM's internal knowledge, only redirect attention to relevant external text. Open questions include whether entity-based highlighting generalizes to tasks where key information is non-entity (e.g., numerical relationships, causal chains) and whether the Wikidata dependency limits applicability to domains with sparse KG coverage.
