## Problem

LLM-based AI assistants frequently produce factual errors and hallucinations in knowledge-intensive tasks, yet they lack the ability to recognize and communicate their own knowledge boundaries in natural language. While prior work explored calibration via classification heads or verbalized confidence, no systematic study had investigated whether an assistant can be aligned to genuinely say 'I don't know' to questions outside its parametric knowledge, nor how different alignment methods (prompting, SFT, preference optimization) affect this capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240323/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-turn text question (from open-domain QA datasets like TriviaQA, Natural Questions, or ALCUNA) and produces either a factual answer drawn from the model's own correct generations or a fixed refusal template: 'This question is beyond the scope of my knowledge, and I am not sure what the answer is.' The Idk dataset is model-specific: for each question, 10 responses are sampled (top_p=0.9, temperature=1.0), and the question is labeled 'known' only if the model answers correctly on all 10 attempts (Ik threshold=1.0), making the dataset's label distribution unique to each base model.

## Method

The paper constructs a model-specific Idk dataset by sampling 10 responses per TriviaQA question, classifying questions as known/unknown via an Ik threshold (accuracy rate across samples), and annotating unknowns with a refusal template while using the model's own correct responses for knowns. It then systematically compares six alignment strategies: Idk-Prompting, Idk-SFT, Idk-DPO (augmented with a small SFT loss term to preserve template generation), Idk-BoN (reward-model-guided best-of-10 selection), Idk-PPO, and Idk-HIR (hindsight instruction relabeling that encodes the Ik threshold as a controllable confidence-level instruction). The key finding is that SFT alone causes an 'alignment tax'—the model becomes overly conservative and refuses known questions (IDK-IK increases)—while preference-aware optimization (DPO, BoN, PPO) mitigates this by encouraging the model to answer questions it actually knows, achieving up to 78.96% TRUTHFUL rate (IK-IK + IK-IDK) on Llama-2-7b-chat. The knowledge quadrant framework (IK-IK, IK-IDK, IDK-IK, IDK-IDK) is used throughout to track how each method redistributes questions across the four categories.

## Results

On the TriviaQA test set, Idk-BoN (Best-of-10 sampling) achieves the highest TRUTHFUL rate of 78.96%, a 12.03-point improvement over Idk-Prompting (66.93%). Idk-DPO follows closely at 77.89% TRUTHFUL rate, while Idk-SFT reaches 74.75% but suffers an 'alignment tax' with IK-IK dropping from 37.36% (prompting) to 28.57%. On the out-of-distribution Natural Questions set, Idk-HIR achieves the best TRUTHFUL rate of 72.30% (↑10.83 over prompting), and on ALCUNA all aligned methods exceed 92% IK-IDK rate, confirming generalization to unanswerable questions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Idk-Prompting, which simply instructs the model to say 'I don't know' for unknown questions without any training. All alignment methods substantially outperform prompting: Idk-SFT improves TRUTHFUL by 7.82 points, Idk-DPO by 10.96, and Idk-BoN by 12.03 on TriviaQA. The key trade-off is that Idk-SFT reduces IK-IK from 37.36% to 28.57% (the 'alignment tax'), while preference-optimized methods (DPO, BoN, PPO) recover most of this loss—Idk-DPO restores IK-IK to 39.30% while maintaining a high IK-IDK of 38.59%. The paper also compares against logits-based calibration methods from Kadavath et al. (2022) (mc_logits, value_head, whole_llm) in Appendix C.6, finding that the Idk alignment approach outperforms these external classifier-based methods on TRUTHFUL rate. On the OOD Natural Questions set, Idk-HIR (72.30%) outperforms Idk-BoN (67.95%), attributed to the higher proportion of unknown questions in NQ favoring the more conservative HIR strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on teaching LLMs to express knowledge uncertainty (Kadavath et al., 2022; Yin et al., 2023b; Zhang et al., 2023a) by introducing a model-specific 'I don't know' dataset and systematically comparing alignment methods for knowledge-boundary detection. It builds on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) by incorporating an SFT loss term to stabilize template generation, and adapts Hindsight Instruction Relabeling (Zhang et al., 2023b) to provide a retraining-free knob for controlling the model's conservatism-aggressiveness trade-off. Unlike prior refusal-tuning work that uses a single fixed dataset, this paper emphasizes that the Idk dataset must be constructed per-model, connecting the model's internal knowledge to the training labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that SFT induces over-conservatism (an 'alignment tax' on IK-IK) while preference optimization recovers it provides actionable guidance for practitioners building truthful AI assistants. The model-specific dataset requirement is an important practical constraint: using another model's Idk labels degrades performance substantially, meaning the approach cannot be trivially transferred across architectures. Open questions include whether this framework generalizes beyond single-turn QA to multi-turn dialogue or retrieval-augmented settings, and whether the ~21% residual error (IDK-IK + IDK-IDK) can be further reduced. The HIR-based controllable confidence level is a promising direction for deployment scenarios with varying tolerance for factual errors.
