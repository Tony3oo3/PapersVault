## Problem

Learned dynamics models in offline RL suffer from large value gaps when evaluating target policies that differ from the data-collection policies, because the target policy visits state-action distributions unfamiliar to the model. Standard policy-agnostic models fit a single unified transition model to the entire mixed dataset, but this one-size-fits-all approach cannot allocate its limited capacity optimally for each target policy's specific distribution, compounding errors during long-horizon rollouts.

## Input / Output

The method consumes offline trajectories (state-action sequences) collected by multiple behavior policies. A GRU-based policy encoder processes the trajectory prefix up to timestep t to produce a 128-dimensional policy representation embedding, which is concatenated with the state-action pair and fed into the dynamics model for next-state prediction. A separate policy decoder reconstructs the action from the state and embedding, serving as a regularizer. For downstream OPE, the model produces policy value estimates via Monte-Carlo rollouts (N=10 episodes, γ=0.995) in the learned dynamics.

## Method

PCM introduces a policy-conditioned dynamics model that generates distinct transition predictions for different target policies via an RNN-based policy representation module. The architecture consists of a 3-layer GRU policy encoder (128 hidden units) that maps trajectory prefixes to policy embeddings, a 4-layer MLP dynamics model that takes state-action pairs plus the policy embedding to predict next states, and a 4-layer MLP policy decoder that reconstructs actions from state and embedding. Training jointly optimizes next-state prediction (negative log-likelihood) and a policy reconstruction regularizer (weighted by λ=0.01), with residual connections, layer normalization, and dropout for stable RNN training. The key insight is that conditioning on policy representations allows the model to adapt its predictions to each target policy's state-action occupancy distribution, yielding both smaller in-distribution training errors and a positive adaptation gain that partially compensates for distribution extrapolation to unseen policies, as formalized in Proposition 5.3.

## Results

On the DOPE benchmark (D4RL + RL Unplugged), PCM achieves a 0.21 average normalized absolute error, substantially outperforming PAM (0.51), FQE (0.43), IS (0.57), DICE (0.47), and VPM (0.56). On the most challenging task (hopper-medium-replay), PCM reaches 0.08 normalized error versus 0.34 for PAM and 0.43 for FQE. In offline policy selection for MOPO, PCM achieves a 71.7% average performance gain, compared to 59.4% for IS, 39.3% for DICE, and only 11.5% for PAM. PCM also attains the highest rank correlation (0.77 vs. 0.47 for PAM) and lowest regret (0.07 vs. 0.22 for PAM) across OPE tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCM outperforms all five OPE baselines (FQE, DR, IS, DICE, VPM) on nearly every task in the DOPE benchmark, with the largest margins on hopper-medium-replay (0.08 vs. 0.43 for FQE) and walker2d-medium-replay (0.25 vs. 0.56 for FQE). The closest competitor is PAM (the policy-agnostic ablation), which PCM beats by a factor of ~2.4 in average normalized error (0.21 vs. 0.51), confirming that the policy-conditioned mechanism—not merely increased model capacity—drives the improvement. In offline policy selection for MOPO, PCM achieves a 71.7% average performance gain, surpassing IS (59.4%), DICE (39.3%), FQE (20.6%), and PAM (11.5%); notably, FQE and PAM perform worse than simply selecting the last-epoch policy (30.2%). In model predictive control, PCM's cumulative reward closely matches the true dynamics and significantly exceeds PAM, with lower planning regret confirming more accurate action selection. The only task where PCM does not clearly dominate is ant-medium-expert (0.49 vs. 0.46 for PAM), suggesting the policy-conditioned mechanism is most beneficial when behavioral diversity is high.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model-based off-policy evaluation (OPE) line of work by introducing a policy-conditioned dynamics model that adapts its predictions to the target policy being evaluated. It draws on meta-learning and policy representation techniques (RL², Nagabandi et al., Chen et al. 2021) to encode policies via an RNN, but departs from prior policy-aware model learning (PAML, PDML, GALILEO) which target online adaptation or single biased behavior policies, by explicitly conditioning on diverse offline behavior policies to reduce value gaps for unseen target policies. The theoretical analysis builds on Janner et al.'s (2019) value gap bounds but refocuses the objective from minimizing training-distribution error to minimizing target-policy-distribution error, introducing an 'adaptation gain' term absent from prior bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-known practical bottleneck in model-based OPE: learned dynamics models produce unreliable value estimates when the target policy visits state-action regions underrepresented in the offline data. The finding that PCM's advantage grows with dataset diversity is practically important for settings where data is naturally collected from multiple behavior policies. Key limitations include the infinite-sample assumption in the theory, restriction to MuJoCo continuous-control tasks, and the reliance on an RNN-based policy encoder whose efficiency and scalability to high-dimensional or partially observable settings remain open. Future work on finite-sample analysis, more efficient policy representations, and extension to online or long-horizon settings would strengthen the contribution.
