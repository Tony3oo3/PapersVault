## Problem

Existing data subset selection methods each excel in only a narrow regime of selection ratios: score-based methods (Forgetting, EL2N) perform well at high ratios but degrade sharply at low ratios, while optimization-based methods (LCMat, AdaCore) do the opposite. No single method consistently achieves competitive performance across the full spectrum from 1% to 90% selection ratios, leaving practitioners without a universal approach.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240346/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled training dataset with pre-computed per-sample difficulty scores (e.g., Forgetting scores averaged over multiple training runs), a target subset size m, and a sliding step size t. It additionally requires training a lightweight feature extractor (the target architecture, e.g., ResNet18/50) on m random samples for ~20 epochs to obtain penultimate-layer features. The output is a single contiguous window defined by its starting index in the difficulty-ordered list, specifying exactly which m samples to retain for downstream training.

## Method

BWS sorts all samples by difficulty score and generates candidate window subsets of fixed size m by sliding a contiguous window across the ordered list with step size t. For each candidate window, it solves a kernel ridge regression (KRR) problem using features from a briefly-trained feature extractor, treating the KRR solution as a linear classifier evaluated on the full training set to score window quality. The window whose KRR classifier achieves the highest full-training-set accuracy is selected as the final subset. The theoretical motivation is that the optimal difficulty level shifts from easy (sample-deficient regime, m ≪ √(d/ln d)) to hard (sample-sufficient regime, m ≫ d² ln d), and the sliding window captures this transition. Using Conjugate Kernel features from the target architecture makes the KRR proxy architecturally aligned with the eventual training model, and the entire selection process runs in seconds rather than requiring full model training per candidate.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, BWS achieves up to 93.59% test accuracy with only a 40% data subset, and delivers a 15–30 percentage-point improvement over Forgetting score at low selection ratios (1–10%). On CIFAR-100, BWS reaches 79.69% at 90% selection ratio (matching full-dataset training of 78.81%), and on ImageNet it attains 75.53% at 90% ratio. BWS consistently outperforms all score-based (Forgetting, EL2N) and optimization-based (LCMat, AdaCore) baselines across the full 1%–90% selection ratio range, while requiring less than 11 seconds of selection time on CIFAR-10 versus over 500 seconds for LCMat.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BWS outperforms Forgetting score (Toneva et al., 2019) by 15–30 percentage points at low selection ratios (1–10%) on CIFAR-10 (e.g., 46.10% vs. 30.08% at 1%), while Forgetting only catches up at high ratios (≥75%). Against LCMat (Shin et al., 2023), BWS wins at high selection ratios (e.g., 93.59% vs. 92.45% at 40% on CIFAR-10) and matches or exceeds it at low ratios, while requiring <11 s of selection time versus >500 s for LCMat. BWS also surpasses AdaCore (Pooladzandi et al., 2022) across all ratios with substantially lower computational cost. Among universal methods, BWS outperforms Moderate DS (Xia et al., 2023) and CCS (Zheng et al., 2023) over the broad 1%–90% range without requiring hyperparameter tuning (e.g., CCS's β). On ImageNet, BWS also beats SSL Prototype (Sorscher et al., 2022) and Memorization score (Feldman & Zhang, 2020). In the label-noise experiment (20% noise on CIFAR-10), BWS achieves accuracy exceeding the full noisy dataset, while Forgetting, EL2N, LCMat, and AdaCore all degrade.

## Relation to Prior Work

BWS synthesizes two established lines of data subset selection—score-based methods (Forgetting, EL2N) that prioritize difficult samples and optimization-based methods (LCMat, AdaCore) that seek representative subsets—into a unified 'window' framework that selects contiguous difficulty-ordered intervals. The theoretical motivation draws on Sorscher et al. (2022), who showed regime-dependent optimal selection strategies, but BWS provides a closed-form linear regression analysis and, crucially, a practical algorithm. The kernel ridge regression proxy is grounded in the NTK/Conjugate Kernel literature (Arora et al., 2019; Jacot et al., 2018), repurposing these tools not for training but for evaluating subset quality without full model training. Unlike Moderate-DS and CCS, which also target universality across ratios, BWS avoids hyperparameter tuning by directly optimizing over window starting points via the KRR proxy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is significant: a single method that performs competitively from 1% to 90% selection ratios eliminates the need to choose between score-based and optimization-based methods depending on the target ratio, and the KRR proxy runs in seconds versus hours for LCMat/AdaCore. The robustness to label noise (outperforming full-dataset training with 20% noise) addresses a real deployment concern. Open questions include whether the window concept generalizes to web-scale multimodal settings (e.g., DataComp), whether difficulty scores other than Forgetting/EL2N work equally well, and whether the approach extends to tasks beyond supervised classification with clean labels. The method remains tied to a specific scoring step and a fixed feature extractor, limiting its applicability to settings where these are readily available.
