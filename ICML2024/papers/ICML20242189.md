## Problem

AlphaFold2-Multimer's primary structure loss, FAPE, implicitly optimizes the chordal distance between predicted and ground-truth group frames on SE(3). The chordal distance gradient vanishes as rotation error approaches π, causing the model to under-train high-rotation-error targets such as antibody-antigen complexes and leaving predictions 'stuck' at incorrect docking poses. No prior work had identified this fundamental limitation or proposed a loss that maintains stable gradients across the full rotation range.

## Input / Output

The method takes antibody (heavy and light chain) and antigen primary sequences as input, along with MSA information searched against cropped sequences, and produces a full 3D antibody-antigen complex structure with backbone and sidechain atom coordinates. No experimental structures are required for either component. Evaluation uses DockQ on the predicted complex, with DockQ > 0.23 as the accuracy threshold and DockQ > 0.8 as high accuracy, following the AF3 protocol. The fine-tuning dataset is drawn from SabDab with a September 2021 cutoff, and evaluation sets are constructed from post-cutoff entries with homology filtering via MMseqs2 clustering.

## Method

The paper first proves that inter-chain FAPE, when averaged quadratically over residues in one chain, reduces to the chordal distance between the relative group frames (G-FAPE), exposing the gradient vanishing problem at rotation angles > π/2. To fix this, they propose F2E (Frame Aligned Frame Error), which computes the geodesic distance between aligned residue frames rather than point-wise Euclidean distances, yielding L_F2E² = d_θ²(R_i→j) + L_FAPE²/α². They then derive a group-aware correction (G-F2E) that subtracts the chordal component—expressed as a function of the geodesic angle via d_c = 2√2·sin(d_θ/2)—to isolate a pure geodesic group-frame loss. The loss is applied by fine-tuning AF2-Multimer with LoRA (rank 16) on the 48-layer Evoformer while freezing upstream weights and fully training the structure module and prediction heads, using SabDab antibody-antigen complexes with interface-only spatial cropping.

## Results

On the full evaluation set (693 antibody-antigen complexes from SabDab), F2E achieves a 52.3% correct rate (DockQ > 0.23) and on the low-homology subset (novel antigens) achieves 43.8% correct rate, representing a 182% and 100% improvement over AF2-Multimer respectively. The ensemble of 5 fine-tuned models with iPTM-based selection reaches 43.8% DockQ > 0.23 on the low-homology set, matching the aggressive AF2sample protocol (37.5%) with a single model and surpassing it with ensemble. The best single predictions (oracle selection by ground-truth DockQ) reach 59% accuracy, indicating room for improved confidence scoring.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original AF2-Multimer (no fine-tuning), F2E with LoRA fine-tuning and ensemble improves the DockQ > 0.23 correct rate from 18.8% to 43.8% on the low-homology set (a 133% relative gain) and from ~18.5% to 52.3% on the full evaluation set (182% relative gain). Against AF2sample, an aggressive inference-time sampling protocol that generates 200+ predictions per target using multiple strategies (template removal, MSA re-sampling, increased recycling, dropout), F2E with a single model matches its 37.5% correct rate, and the 5-model ensemble surpasses it at 43.8%. The ablation further shows that the group-aware correction of FAPE alone (G-FAPE) lifts performance from 25.0% to 31.3%, and replacing the chordal rotational term with the geodesic F2E term adds another 6.2 points (31.3% → 37.5%), confirming that the gradient-stability fix is the key contributor beyond the group-aware reformulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on AlphaFold2 and AlphaFold2-Multimer, whose FAPE loss is the standard structure loss for protein complex prediction. The key conceptual departure is a theoretical re-interpretation: the authors show that inter-chain FAPE, under a near-rigid group assumption, reduces to optimizing chordal distance between group frames on SE(3), exposing a gradient vanishing problem at rotation angles near π. The proposed F2E loss replaces this with a geodesic (angular) distance, and the group-aware formulation is inspired by the superposition-based distance definition from ligand RMSD (Méndez et al., 2003). For training, the paper adopts LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning to avoid catastrophic forgetting of intra-chain structure knowledge, positioning itself within the broader PEFT literature rather than proposing a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnosis that FAPE's chordal-distance nature causes under-training of high-rotation-error samples (common in antibody-antigen docking) is a clean and actionable insight that could inform loss design in other SE(3)-parameterized structure models. The 182% relative improvement over AF2.3 on the full evaluation set and 100% on the low-homology subset is substantial, though absolute correct rates (52.3% and 43.8% at DockQ > 0.23) remain far from reliable for drug-discovery applications. Open questions include whether F2E helps during pre-training of next-generation protein foundation models (e.g., AF3-class systems), whether it generalizes to complexes with non-protein components, and whether the confidence score (iptm) can be improved to better select among the 5-model ensemble, since the best-of-ensemble DockQ reaches 0.59 while top-1 selection only achieves 0.438.
