## Problem

Referring Expression Segmentation (RES) requires expensive pixel-level annotations (~79.1 seconds per mask), making large-scale deployment in domains like medical imaging and autonomous driving impractical. While semi-supervised learning has been widely validated in other vision tasks, its application to RES has not been explored, and the core challenge of noisy pseudo-labels—particularly at object boundaries—remains unaddressed, trapping models in suboptimal performance.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image paired with a natural language referring expression as input and produces a binary pixel-level segmentation mask as output. During training, a small labeled set (0.5%–5% of the dataset) provides ground-truth masks, while a much larger unlabeled set supplies only image-expression pairs without masks. SAM is run offline on all unlabeled images to generate a multi-scale proposal library of hundreds to thousands of candidate masks per image, stored efficiently via Run Length Encoding.

## Method

SemiRES employs a teacher-student architecture with EMA-based teacher updates in a two-stage (burn-in + mutual learning) training procedure. The key innovation is SAM-based pseudo-label refinement: the teacher's noisy pseudo-labels are corrected by matching them against SAM's offline-generated multi-scale mask proposals. Two alternative strategies are proposed—IoU-based Optimal Matching (IOM), which selects the single SAM mask with the highest IoU to the pseudo-label, and Composite Parts Integration (CPI), which merges multiple SAM masks to handle under-segmentation (CPI-U) or over-segmentation (CPI-O) by selecting part-specific proposals based on overlap ratios. When no suitable SAM mask exceeds the matching threshold, a Pixel-Wise Adjustment (PWA) strategy reweights the per-pixel BCE loss using a Gaussian-shaped confidence mapping, down-weighting ambiguous pixels near 0.5 and up-weighting high-confidence pixels near 0 or 1.

## Results

On RefCOCO val with 1% labeled data, SemiRES achieves 50.90% oIoU, surpassing the fully supervised model (32.26%) by +18.64% and the plain semi-supervised baseline (42.62%) by +8.28%. At 0.5% labeled data, the gain over supervised is even larger at +17.94% (40.31% vs. 22.37%). The method consistently outperforms both baselines across all three datasets (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, G-Ref) and all labeled-data ratios (0.5%–5%), with the largest absolute gains observed at the lowest data budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemiRES is compared against two baselines: (1) a fully supervised model trained only on the small labeled subset, and (2) a plain semi-supervised baseline using the same teacher-student EMA framework with data augmentation but no pseudo-label refinement. SemiRES outperforms the supervised model by a large margin in every setting, with gains of +17.94% to +11.91% on RefCOCO val as labeled data increases from 0.5% to 5%. Against the semi-supervised baseline, SemiRES consistently improves by +8.28% to +10.79% on RefCOCO val, demonstrating that SAM-based pseudo-label refinement (IOM/CPI) plus PWA is the key differentiator. In the ablation (Table 4), a confidence-filtering approach that discards the lowest 5% of pseudo-labels yields only 42.99% on 1% RefCOCO val, far below SemiRES's 50.90%, confirming that rigid filtering under-utilizes unlabeled data. The method also generalizes to a different RES backbone (GRES/ReLA), where it improves over supervised by +12.03% on RefCOCO val (43.57% vs. 31.54%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FixMatch-style semi-supervised teacher-student paradigm (with EMA updates and burn-in) to the referring expression segmentation task, which had not previously been explored in a semi-supervised setting. Its key departure from prior pseudo-label refinement approaches—confidence-based filtering (FixMatch) and auxiliary correcting networks (Kwon & Kwak, 2022; Kim et al., 2023a)—is the use of SAM's offline multi-scale mask proposals as a geometric guide for correcting noisy pseudo-labels, rather than relying on the model's own confidence scores. The RES backbone (LAVT with Swin Transformer + BERT) and evaluation protocol follow established conventions, positioning the contribution squarely in the training methodology rather than the architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that 1% labeled data with SAM-guided pseudo-label refinement can outperform fully supervised training by ~19% on RefCOCO, this work offers a practical pathway to dramatically reduce annotation costs for RES in expensive domains (medical imaging, autonomous driving). The SAM-based refinement technique (IOM/CPI) is modular and potentially transferable to other pixel-level vision-language tasks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the LAVT/GRES backbones tested, whether the offline SAM mask library (hundreds-to-thousands of masks per image) remains feasible at scale, and whether the CPI-U noise-introduction issue can be further mitigated. The reliance on SAM's specific multi-scale segmentation quality (vs. SEEM) also raises questions about robustness to future foundation model changes.
