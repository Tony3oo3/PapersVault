## Problem

Existing ML-based MILP solvers like Neural Diving and Predict-and-Search train with binary cross-entropy loss, treating each variable prediction independently and ignoring the contrast between high-quality and low-quality solutions. This paper asks whether contrastive learning can produce more discriminative solution predictions by explicitly learning to separate optimal/near-optimal assignments from infeasible or low-quality ones, and how to effectively collect such negative samples for this new task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240795/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a MILP instance encoded as a bipartite graph (n variable nodes, m constraint nodes, edges where coefficients are non-zero) with node and edge features, and outputs a [0,1] probability vector over the q binary variables. At test time, these probabilities drive a Predict-and-Search procedure: the top-k₀ variables with lowest probability are fixed to 0, top-k₁ with highest to 1, and the remaining sub-MILP is solved with a proximity constraint allowing Δ flips, yielding a high-quality feasible solution for both binary and continuous variables.

## Method

ConPaS trains a GCN (2 rounds of message passing on the bipartite graph, followed by an MLP + sigmoid) with a quality-weighted InfoNCE contrastive loss that encourages predicted assignments to be similar to positive samples (optimal/near-optimal solutions) and dissimilar to negative samples. Two novel negative-sample collection strategies are introduced: (1) infeasible solutions obtained by randomly perturbing 10% of binary variables in positive samples and verifying infeasibility, and (2) low-quality feasible solutions found by solving a Local Branching MILP (or a maximin optimization for mixed binary-continuous problems) that maximizes the objective while staying within a Hamming-distance neighborhood of each positive sample. The loss uses a per-sample temperature τ(x|M) inversely proportional to the solution's energy, so better-quality positives receive stronger attraction in the contrastive objective. At inference, the predicted probabilities feed into the PaS search framework with tunable (k₀, k₁, Δ) hyperparameters to balance search-space reduction against solution quality.

## Results

At the 1,000-second runtime cutoff, ConPaS-LQ achieves the lowest primal integral across all four benchmarks: 2.8 (MVC), 5.4 (MIS), 19.7 (CA), and 192.0 (IP), compared to the best baseline PaS at 13.9, 34.5, 28.9, and 236.8 respectively. ConPaS-LQ achieves 9.82%–86.02% lower average primal gaps than the best baseline, while ConPaS-Inf achieves 3.54%–52.83% lower. On 50%-larger unseen instances, ConPaS-LQ's primal integral is 57.9%–70.3% lower than PaS, demonstrating strong generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConPaS-LQ outperforms all four baselines (SCIP, ND, PaS, Gurobi) on every benchmark problem in both primal gap and primal integral. Against SCIP, the strongest traditional solver, ConPaS-LQ reduces the primal integral by 93.7% on MVC (2.8 vs. 44.5), 88.3% on MIS (5.4 vs. 46.3), 85.9% on CA (19.7 vs. 138.9), and 45.1% on IP (192.0 vs. 349.3). Against the closest ML-based baseline PaS, ConPaS-LQ achieves 79.9% lower primal integral on MVC (2.8 vs. 13.9), 84.4% on MIS (5.4 vs. 34.5), 31.8% on CA (19.7 vs. 28.9), and 18.9% on IP (192.0 vs. 236.8). Against ND, ConPaS-LQ is 73.8% better on MVC, 76.4% on MIS, 72.3% on CA, and 21.3% on IP. In the Appendix comparison with Gurobi (used as the solver in the Predict-and-Search phase), ConPaS-LQ again outperforms both PaS and Gurobi on MVC and MIS, confirming that the contrastive learning gain is solver-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConPaS extends the Predict-and-Search (PaS) framework of Han et al. (2022), which itself generalizes Neural Diving (Nair et al., 2020) by replacing hard variable fixing with a trust-region-style proximity search. The key departure is replacing the binary cross-entropy training objective with a supervised contrastive loss (a quality-weighted InfoNCE variant) that explicitly contrasts optimal/near-optimal solutions against two novel types of negatives: infeasible solutions collected via random perturbation and low-quality feasible solutions collected via Local Branching or a maximin formulation. This builds on the authors' prior CL-LNS work (Huang et al., 2023), which applied contrastive learning to refine solutions in large neighborhood search, but here the contrastive signal is used to construct partial solutions from scratch rather than to guide iterative refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ConPaS demonstrates that contrastive learning can substantially improve the anytime performance of ML-guided MILP solving, with gains of up to 86% in primal gap over the best prior ML-based method, and it generalizes to instances 50% larger than training data. The framework is complementary to existing LNS-based contrastive approaches (it can warm-start them) and its predictions could be repurposed for branching priority or cut generation in BnB, broadening its utility beyond the PaS search. However, the approach does not guarantee completeness or optimality, and the paper leaves open the important question of integrating contrastive-learned predictions into optimal tree searches. The reliance on a MILP solver for both data collection (negative sample validation) and the downstream search limits end-to-end efficiency.
