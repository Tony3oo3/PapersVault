## Problem

Existing sparse co-training methods are confined to a single sparsity pattern per training run (e.g., only unstructured or only N:M), and only a handful can produce multiple sparsity ratios alongside the dense model. Real-world deployment demands simultaneous access to diverse sparsity patterns (unstructured, N:M, channel-wise) at multiple ratios to match heterogeneous hardware accelerators, yet no prior framework co-trains all of these at once.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241138/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained dense CNN (ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10 at 32×32, ResNet-50 on ImageNet at 224×224, or VGG-16/ResNet-50 on COCO) and produces a single shared dense network accompanied by 24 binary sparse masks (10 unstructured, 10 channel-wise, 3 N:M at 1:2, 2:4, 4:8). At inference, any mask can be applied to the shared weights to instantiate the desired subnetwork, yielding class labels, bounding boxes, or segmentation masks depending on the task. The key structural property is that all 24 subnetworks plus the dense model share one set of parameters, giving a 24× parameter-efficiency advantage over methods that store separate networks.

## Method

Sparse Cocktail alternates between three sparsity-pattern training phases (unstructured, channel-wise, N:M) while gradually increasing sparsity ratios across 10 Iterative Magnitude Pruning (IMP) iterations with weight rewinding to a fixed early epoch. Three key mechanisms ensure co-training stability: (1) Unified Mask Generation (UMG) derives all three mask types from individual weight magnitudes, where channel-wise masks are determined by a refilling criterion that scores channels using a weighted combination of unstructured and N:M mask magnitudes; (2) Dense Pivot Co-training inserts a one-step dense network update between each sparse subnetwork update, combined with dynamic distillation (stop-gradient loss toward the dense output) to regularize gradient directions across patterns; (3) Greedy Sparse Network Interpolation merges the dense networks from all IMP stages into a single shared parameter set by sequentially selecting interpolation factors that maximize average validation accuracy across all subnetworks. The result is a storage-efficient 'cocktail' where one dense weight tensor plus lightweight binary masks encodes the entire suite of sparse subnetworks.

## Results

On ResNet-18 + CIFAR-10, Sparse Cocktail achieves 92.48% dense accuracy, 92.09% average unstructured accuracy, 90.02% average channel-wise accuracy, and 91.83% average N:M accuracy across 24 co-trained subnetworks sharing a single parameter set (1× parameters). On ResNet-50 + ImageNet, the corresponding numbers are 76.32%, 73.23%, 72.22%, and 75.19%. The method produces 24 subnetworks (1 dense + 10 unstructured + 10 channel-wise + 3 N:M) with only 1× the parameter count, compared to 10× for AC/DC and 1.94× for AST, yielding an average subnetwork capacity of 24 per shared network versus 2 for AC/DC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AST (the SOTA sparse-to-sparse co-training method), Sparse Cocktail matches unstructured accuracy (92.09% vs 92.08% on CIFAR-10; 73.23% vs 73.15% on ImageNet) and is slightly behind on N:M (91.83% vs 92.11% on CIFAR-10; 75.19% vs 76.02% on ImageNet), but additionally produces channel-wise subnetworks that AST cannot generate. Against AC/DC, Sparse Cocktail is marginally behind on dense accuracy (92.48% vs 92.58% on CIFAR-10) and unstructured ImageNet accuracy (73.23% vs 75.80%), yet avoids AC/DC's requirement of 10 separate dense/sparse network pairs (10× parameters vs 1×). Against MutualNet (SOTA for channel-wise co-training), Sparse Cocktail is slightly behind on CIFAR-10 channel-wise average (90.02% vs 90.23%) but wins on ImageNet (72.22% vs 72.04%), with the paper reporting over 1% gains at higher sparsity ratios (e.g., 69.45% vs 68.57% at 61% channel sparsity on ImageNet). Overall, Sparse Cocktail trades small per-pattern accuracy losses for the ability to co-train all three sparsity patterns simultaneously in a single 1×-parameter network, effectively generalizing and encapsulating all three baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sparse Cocktail extends the sparse co-training paradigm (AST, AC/DC, MutualNet) from single-pattern to multi-pattern co-training by unifying iterative magnitude pruning with weight rewinding (from the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis), alternating sparse training with a dense pivot step (adapted from AST), refilling-based channel mask generation (from Chen et al., 2022), and greedy network interpolation (from Lottery Pools). The key departure is that prior methods each handle at most one sparsity pattern per training run, whereas Sparse Cocktail simultaneously produces unstructured, channel-wise, and N:M subnetworks at multiple ratios from a single shared dense backbone. The Unified Mask Generation mechanism is the conceptual bridge that makes multi-pattern co-training feasible by grounding all mask types in individual weight magnitudes rather than heterogeneous criteria.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: real-world deployment spans CPUs (favoring unstructured sparsity), GPUs (favoring N:M or structured sparsity), and mobile accelerators (favoring channel-wise sparsity), and a single shared-parameter network offering all patterns and ratios eliminates the need for separate pruning pipelines per hardware target. The paper demonstrates that multi-pattern co-training achieves comparable accuracy to single-pattern SOTA methods while delivering 24 subnetworks from 1× parameters (vs. 10× for AC/DC), a compelling parameter-efficiency story. Limitations include evaluation only on ResNet-18/50 and VGG-16, a modest 11–15% training overhead from the distillation step, and a small but consistent accuracy drop from the interpolation step needed to share parameters. Open questions include whether the approach scales to transformers, larger sparsity pattern families, or higher sparsity ratios (>90%) where all baselines degrade.
