## Problem

Frei et al. (2023) showed that when data consists of subclasses with small inter-subclass correlation, the implicit bias of gradient flow on shallow ReLU networks produces classifiers that generalize well on clean data but are vulnerable to adversarial attacks of radius O(1/√K), even though more robust classifiers provably exist. This paper asks whether the harmful implicit bias can be altered by modifying the network architecture, and shows that a simple change to the activation function resolves the non-robustness.

## Input / Output

The method takes a data point x ∈ R^D drawn from a mixture of K Gaussian subclasses (grouped into two superclasses) and outputs a real-valued score whose sign gives the binary class label. In experiments, inputs are centered and normalized MNIST images (784-dim) or feature vectors extracted from a pre-trained ResNet152 on Caltech256. Robustness is quantified as the largest l2 (or l∞) perturbation radius under which the classifier maintains correct predictions, evaluated via PGD/APGD attacks.

## Method

The paper introduces a polynomial ReLU (pReLU) activation defined as [σ(⟨x,w_j⟩)]^p / ‖w_j‖^(p−1), which for p > 1 applies a cosine penalty that shrinks the post-activation value when the angle between input x and neuron w_j is large. This penalty makes the network positively homogeneous of degree 2 and, under gradient flow with small initialization, shifts the neuron alignment dynamics: for p = 1 (vanilla ReLU), neurons converge to the average class centers μ̄_+ and μ̄_−, yielding a classifier vulnerable to O(1/√K) attacks; for p ≥ 3, neurons instead align with individual subclass centers μ_k, yielding a classifier that is O(1)-robust. The paper provides formal theorems bounding the adversarial robustness of the resulting classifiers, a preliminary alignment analysis (Theorem 2) explaining why p ≥ 3 breaks the preference for average class centers, and numerical experiments on synthetic data, MNIST parity/digit classification, and Caltech256 that validate the conjecture and demonstrate substantial robustness gains.

## Results

On MNIST digit classification, pReLU networks maintain near-identical clean accuracy (~0.98) across all p values while dramatically improving adversarial robustness. Under ℓ∞ attacks (radius 0.05), robust accuracy jumps from 51.2% (p=1, vanilla ReLU) to 91.3% (p=4), and under ℓ∞ radius 0.1, from 4.0% to 63.7%. The gains are consistent across ℓ2 and ℓ1 attacks as well: at ℓ2 radius 1, robust accuracy improves from 30.1% to 77.5%, and at ℓ1 radius 5, from 50.0% to 80.7%. Theoretically, the paper proves that ReLU networks (p=1) are vulnerable to O(1/√K) attacks while pReLU networks (p≥2) are O(1)-robust, with critical attack radii of 1/√K and √2/2 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between vanilla ReLU (p=1) and polynomial ReLU (p=2,3,4) activations in a two-layer shallow network trained with Adam on MNIST. pReLU networks consistently and substantially outperform the ReLU baseline across every attack type and radius tested: at ℓ∞ radius 0.05, p=4 achieves 91.3% robust accuracy versus 51.2% for ReLU (a 40-point gap), and at ℓ∞ radius 0.1 the gap widens to 59.7 points (63.7% vs. 4.0%). The improvement is monotonic in p, with p=4 always best and p=2 already providing a large gain over p=1. Clean accuracy is essentially unchanged (within 0.2%), confirming that the robustness gain does not come at a generalization cost. On the MNIST parity task and Caltech256 (with ResNet-152 features), the same trend holds: larger p yields higher robust accuracy under APGD ℓ∞ attacks while maintaining or slightly improving clean test accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Frei et al. (2023), who showed that gradient flow on shallow ReLU networks trained on data with latent subclasses produces non-robust classifiers despite the existence of robust ones, by proposing a concrete architectural fix: a polynomial ReLU (pReLU) activation that alters the implicit bias to favor learning individual subclass centers rather than average class directions. The neuron alignment analysis in Section 4 builds on the small-initialization gradient flow framework of Min et al. (2024) and Boursier et al. (2022), extending their ReLU-specific results to the pReLU setting. The paper also connects to the broader implicit bias literature (max-margin, min-norm, sparsity perspectives) but distinguishes itself by focusing on how activation function design can be leveraged to steer the bias toward robustness rather than merely characterizing it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that a simple activation function modification (raising ReLU output to power p) can fundamentally change what a network learns during training, turning a non-robust classifier into a robust one without any change to the training procedure or data. However, the main convergence result (Conjecture 1) remains unproven, the theoretical guarantees require restrictive assumptions (orthonormal subclass centers, small initialization, high dimension), and the real-dataset experiments use Kaiming initialization and non-orthogonal data, so the gap between theory and practice is significant. Open questions include whether pReLU benefits extend to deep networks, whether the conjecture can be formally established, and whether the robustness gains persist under more realistic data distributions without the orthogonality structure.
