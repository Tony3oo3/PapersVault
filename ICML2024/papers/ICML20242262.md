## Problem

Prior long-term fairness notions in sequential decision-making aggregate step-wise bias ratios (ratio-before-aggregation), which inadvertently assigns unequal importance to decisions made at different time steps within the same group—a form of temporal discrimination. While the ratio-after-aggregation alternative (comparing total supply over total demand across all time steps) avoids this issue, it had not been formally integrated into an MDP framework, and no principled bias mitigation strategy existed because the policy gradient of the resulting ratio-of-cumulatives objective was unknown.

## Input / Output

The method operates within a Supply-Demand MDP where, at each time step, the agent observes a state (e.g., credit score, network health states, incident rates) and group identifiers, and receives in addition to the standard immediate reward two extra fairness signals: group-specific supply S_g(s_t, a_t) and group-specific demand D_g(s_t, a_t). The output is a trained policy network π_θ(a|s) that maps states to actions (approve/reject loans, select whom to vaccinate, allocate attention units across sites), along with auxiliary value networks for reward, supply, and demand used to compute the fairness-aware advantage during training.

## Method

ELBERT defines the Long-term Benefit Rate of group g as the ratio of cumulative discounted supply η_g^S(π) to cumulative discounted demand η_g^D(π), and bias as the max-min difference of these rates across groups. The key theoretical contribution is proving that the policy gradient of this ratio-of-cumulatives bias term reduces to standard policy gradients: by applying the quotient rule, ∇_π(η_g^S/η_g^D) decomposes into two standard policy gradients weighted by the current cumulative values, yielding a closed-form fairness-aware advantage function A_t^fair = A_t − α Σ_g (∂h/∂z_g)[(1/η_g^D)A_{g,t}^S − (η_g^S/(η_g^D)²)A_{g,t}^D]. This makes standard PPO directly applicable as the optimizer (ELBERT-PO), requiring only additional value networks for supply and demand. For multi-group settings, the non-smooth max/min in the bias is replaced by a log-sum-exp soft bias with temperature β, whose approximation gap is bounded by 2 log M / β, preserving the same gradient structure.

## Results

ELBERT-PO achieves the lowest long-term bias across all three sequential decision-making environments while maintaining high utility. In the lending environment, ELBERT-PO reduces bias to 0.02, a 87.5% decrease over G-PPO and over 75% decrease over R-PPO and A-PPO. In the harder infectious disease control environment, ELBERT-PO achieves a bias of 0.01 (matching R-PPO) while obtaining the same reward as G-PPO, which R-PPO fails to do. In the harder attention allocation environment (5 groups), ELBERT-PO simultaneously achieves the lowest bias and the highest reward among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELBERT-PO outperforms all three baselines on bias in every environment. Against G-PPO (standard PPO without fairness), ELBERT-PO reduces lending bias by 87.5% (0.02 vs. ~0.16) while matching its reward level. Against A-PPO (Yu et al., 2022), which only regularizes the advantage to reduce next-step bias, ELBERT-PO achieves over 75% lower bias in lending and substantially lower bias in the infectious environment, demonstrating that considering the entire future horizon (rather than just the next step) is critical. Against R-PPO, which injects historical bias into the immediate reward, ELBERT-PO matches the 0.01 bias in the infectious environment but achieves significantly higher reward, showing that directly modifying the reward signal harms utility. In the multi-group attention allocation setting, ELBERT-PO is the only method that simultaneously achieves the lowest bias and the highest reward, highlighting the advantage of the principled fairness-aware advantage function over heuristic reward or advantage modifications.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MDP-based long-term fairness framework established by D'Amour et al. (2020), Chi et al. (2021/2022), Wen et al. (2021), and Yin et al. (2023), all of which formulate sequential fairness using ratio-before-aggregation (summing step-wise biases). It departs from this dominant approach by adopting a ratio-after-aggregation metric (cumulative supply over cumulative demand), which the authors show avoids temporal discrimination within groups. The optimization method builds directly on PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), with the key technical contribution being an analytical reduction of the policy gradient of the ratio-of-cumulatives bias to standard policy gradients via a fairness-aware advantage function. It also contrasts with Yu et al. (2022)'s A-PPO, which only regularizes next-step bias rather than the full future.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of temporal discrimination in the dominant ratio-before-aggregation approach is a genuine conceptual correction that affects how long-term fairness should be measured in any sequential setting. The reduction to standard policy gradients makes the ELBERT framework practically deployable with off-the-shelf PPO implementations, lowering the barrier for applying principled long-term fairness to real sequential decision systems (lending, healthcare, resource allocation). A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that ELBERT only constrains the supply-demand ratio, not the absolute demand, which could be problematic in settings where the decision-maker's actions affect how many individuals enter the 'demand' pool. Open questions include how the framework scales to very large group counts, whether the soft-bias temperature β can be adapted online, and how to handle non-stationary group definitions over time.
