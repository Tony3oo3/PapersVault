## Problem

While representation learning techniques from CV and NLP have been successfully transferred to continuous control for state abstractions, learning temporal action abstractions (multi-step primitive behaviors) has not benefited from similar methodology transfer. In particular, existing approaches either require ground-truth subtask boundaries, use fixed-duration primitives, or rely on online RL for finetuning, leaving open whether NLP-style sequence compression can induce variable-timespan skills directly from offline multitask demonstrations.

## Input / Output

PRISE consumes multitask offline demonstration trajectories consisting of pixel observations (84×84 third-person views for MetaWorld; 128×128 third-person and wrist views for LIBERO), proprioceptive state vectors (8-dim or 9-dim), and optional natural-language task instructions (LIBERO). It produces a vocabulary of variable-length skill tokens (each token is a sequence of discrete action codes with its own horizon L_ξ) and a high-level policy that maps latent states to skill tokens; at deployment, selected tokens are decoded into continuous actions via a state-conditioned decoder (GMM or deterministic MLP).

## Method

PRISE reframes temporal action abstraction as a sequence compression problem by applying Byte Pair Encoding (BPE) to discretized action sequences. In Pretraining Stage I, a VQ-VAE-style quantizer maps (latent state, continuous action) pairs to a codebook of C discrete codes, trained jointly with a BYOL-like latent forward-dynamics loss and a state-dependent action decoding loss (GMM for multimodal human demos, L1 for scripted policies). In Pretraining Stage II, BPE iteratively merges the most frequent pairs of action codes across all multitask trajectories to build a vocabulary of K variable-length skill tokens, each representing a common motion pattern with a distinct time span. For downstream few-shot adaptation, a high-level policy π is trained via cross-entropy to predict skill tokens from frozen latent states, while the action decoder ψ is simultaneously finetuned with a combined objective that backpropagates decoding error into π, ensuring the policy accounts for reconstruction quality of its chosen tokens.

## Results

On MetaWorld 5-shot IL across 5 unseen tasks, PRISE achieves a mean success rate of 66.8%±1.8, outperforming the best baseline (PRISE w/o BPE at 60.1%±1.5) by 6.7 points. On LIBERO 5-shot IL across 8 unseen tasks, PRISE reaches 52.7%±0.9, a 9.2-point improvement over the best baseline (PRISE w/o BPE at 43.5%±0.6). On the LIBERO-90 multitask benchmark, PRISE substantially outperforms all BC baselines (ResNet-RNN, ResNet-T, ViT-T) and ACT, with per-task success rates ranging from 0.05 to 1.0 across 90 tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRISE consistently outperforms ACT (Zhao et al., 2023) on both few-shot IL benchmarks: 66.8% vs. 59.3% on MetaWorld and 52.7% vs. 42.0% on LIBERO, with the gap widening on LIBERO where ACT's pretrained encoder variant (42.0%) still trails PRISE by over 10 points. Against the BC baselines from the LIBERO paper (Liu et al., 2023), PRISE achieves substantially higher multitask success rates on LIBERO-90, where BC (ResNet-T) and BC (ViT-T) struggle on many tasks (frequently scoring 0.0–0.2) while PRISE maintains competitive or superior performance across the full 90-task suite. The ablation comparing PRISE to PRISE w/o BPE (which removes temporal abstraction and reduces to discrete action codes only) confirms that BPE is the critical component: PRISE wins on every individual task in both MetaWorld and LIBERO, with the largest margins on harder tasks like Disassemble (74.1% vs. 69.6%) and LIBERO Task 5 (90.0% vs. 75.0%). Notably, on LIBERO the pretrained PRISE encoder does not help BC or ACT baselines (consistent with negative transfer reported by Liu et al., 2023), yet PRISE's skill tokens still deliver a 9.2-point gain, isolating the benefit to the temporal abstraction mechanism rather than the visual representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRISE draws a direct methodological bridge from NLP subword tokenization to continuous control by applying Byte Pair Encoding (Gage, 1994; Sennrich et al., 2016) to sequences of VQ-VAE-quantized actions (van den Oord et al., 2017), with a BYOL-style forward-dynamics objective (Schwarzer et al., 2021) to prevent code collapse. It positions itself against prior temporal-abstraction discovery methods (CompILE, RPL, OPAL) that either require subtask annotations or fixed-duration primitives, and against ACT (Zhao et al., 2023), which learns fixed-length action chunks via BC. The key departure is that BPE discovers variable-horizon skill tokens in a fully unsupervised, frequency-driven manner, eliminating the need for subtask labels or a manually chosen primitive duration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that a simple, unsupervised sequence-compression algorithm from NLP can serve as an effective skill-discovery mechanism for robot manipulation, offering a practical and interpretable alternative to RL-based primitive learning. Limitations include the open-loop execution assumption for skill tokens (which introduces approximation error for closed-loop demonstrators), the requirement that pretraining data contain meaningful behavioral patterns, and evaluation restricted to simulation benchmarks (MetaWorld, LIBERO) rather than real robots. Open directions flagged by the authors include scaling to large multi-embodiment datasets like Open X-Embodiment and leveraging pretrained skill tokens for instruction-finetuning of language models, which could connect this line of work to the broader foundation-model-for-robotics agenda.
