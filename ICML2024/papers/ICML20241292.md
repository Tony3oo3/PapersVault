## Problem

Existing ECG self-supervised learning methods suffer from two fundamental limitations: contrastive eSSL distorts semantic information through naive input-level augmentation (e.g., cutout, drop), while generative eSSL learns only low-level signal patterns without high-level clinical semantics. Moreover, all eSSL approaches are incapable of zero-shot classification because they operate solely on ECG signals without access to clinical knowledge, and no comprehensive benchmark exists to evaluate ECG representation learning across diverse cardiac conditions.

## Input / Output

For pre-training, MERL consumes 12-lead ECG signals (10 s at 500 Hz) paired with their associated clinical text reports from MIMIC-ECG (771,693 curated pairs). At test time, a single ECG signal is encoded and compared via cosine similarity against a dynamically generated text prompt; the prompt is produced by CKEPE, which queries GPT-4 against SNOMED-CT (web) and SCP-ECG (local) databases to extract and restructure clinically verified attributes and disease subtypes into a structured ECG-statement-style prompt. The output is a per-category classification score (macro AUC reported) or a transferable ECG embedding in a shared latent space with text.

## Method

MERL jointly trains a 1D-ResNet18 ECG encoder and a Med-CPT text encoder using two complementary losses: Cross-Modal Alignment (CMA), an InfoNCE contrastive loss aligning ECG and report embeddings in a shared projection space (τ=0.07), and Uni-Modal Alignment (UMA), a contrastive loss on the ECG embedding alone where positive pairs are constructed via independent Bernoulli dropout (p=0.1) in latent space rather than signal-level augmentation, thereby avoiding semantic distortion. At test time, CKEPE leverages GPT-4's web-browsing capability to query SNOMED-CT and a locally curated SCP-ECG database, discarding any terms not found in these expert-verified sources, then reformulating the surviving terms into a descriptive, condition-specific prompt that includes signal-pattern attributes and finer-grained disease subtypes. This design eliminates the need for any annotated downstream data for zero-shot classification while also improving linear-probing performance across all data ratios (1%–100%) on six public datasets.

## Results

MERL achieves an average zero-shot AUC of 75.24% ± 1.7 across six ECG classification datasets (PTBXL-Super/Sub/Form/Rhythm, CPSC2018, CSN), surpassing the best eSSL method linear-probed with 10% annotated data by 3.2 points. In linear probing, MERL with only 1% training data on PTBXL-Super (82.39 AUC) exceeds all eSSL methods using 100% data, and MERL with 10% data outperforms all eSSL methods at 100% data on the remaining five datasets. Under domain shift (cross-dataset transfer), zero-shot MERL outperforms all eSSL methods linear-probed with 100% source data on 5 of 6 target domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MERL consistently outperforms all 10 eSSL baselines (SimCLR, BYOL, BarlowTwins, MoCo-v3, SimSiam, TS-TCC, CLOCS, ASTCL, CRT, ST-MEM) on linear probing across all six datasets at every data ratio (1%, 10%, 100%). The margin is largest at low data ratios: MERL with 1% data on PTBXL-Super (82.39) exceeds the best eSSL at 100% (ASTCL, 81.02), and MERL with 10% data surpasses all eSSL at 100% on the five more granular datasets (e.g., 85.32 vs. CRT's 82.03 on CPSC2018). In zero-shot classification, MERL (75.24% avg AUC) beats the only prior multimodal ECG method METS (58.84%) by 16.4 points, and even without prompt engineering, zero-shot MERL exceeds the best eSSL linear-probed with 1% data. With CKEPE prompt enhancement, zero-shot MERL surpasses the best eSSL linear-probed with 10% data. ST-MEM (Na et al., 2023) is the strongest eSSL baseline overall, particularly under domain shift, likely because its reconstruction-based pretraining avoids the semantic distortion introduced by naive signal-level augmentation used in contrastive eSSL methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

MERL adapts CLIP's two-tower contrastive architecture (Radford et al., 2021) from image-text pairs to ECG-report pairs, introducing Cross-Modal Alignment (CMA) and Uni-Modal Alignment (UMA) with latent-space dropout augmentation to avoid the semantic distortion that plagues signal-level augmentation in contrastive eSSL methods (SimCLR, CLOCS, ASTCL). It departs from prior multimodal ECG work (METS, ETP) by adding a dedicated unimodal contrastive objective and, at test time, by repurposing LLMs not as knowledge sources but as extraction-and-reformatting agents over expert-verified clinical databases (SNOMED-CT, SCP statements), addressing the hallucination risk that makes naive LLM-generated prompts unsafe for medical use. The paper also positions itself against generative eSSL (CRT, ST-MEM) by arguing that reconstruction-based pre-training captures only low-level signal patterns rather than the high-level clinical semantics needed for classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first comprehensive ECG representation learning benchmark (10 eSSL methods + MERL, 6 datasets, 100+ cardiac conditions, zero-shot/linear-probing/domain-transfer protocols) gives the field a standardized evaluation that was previously absent, and the zero-shot capability removes the annotation bottleneck for downstream clinical deployment. The CKEPE pipeline offers a practical template for safe LLM use in medicine—grounding generation in verified external knowledge rather than parametric memory—but the authors acknowledge the generation process remains 'not fully controlled and transparent,' leaving open questions about auditability and regulatory acceptance. Future work on aligning ECG with additional modalities (EHR, cardiac imaging, cardiologist notes) and on making the prompt pipeline fully deterministic would strengthen clinical applicability.
