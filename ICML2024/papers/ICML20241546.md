## Problem

Score function estimation in diffusion generative models relies on either biased neural network approximators or high-variance single-sample Monte Carlo estimators, each with significant drawbacks for both training and sampling. No existing estimator simultaneously achieves low bias and low variance without requiring a pre-trained network, limiting the quality of consistency training and the accuracy of PF-ODE integration.

## Input / Output

The estimator takes a noisy data point z ∈ R^d, a noise level t, and a pre-built FAISS KNN index over the training dataset D of N samples. It first performs a k-nearest-neighbour search (k=2056 in experiments) to identify the most probable posterior elements, then draws n samples (n=256) from a KNN-based proposal to compute a weighted average. The output is a score vector (μ̂_KNN − z)/t² and the posterior mean estimate μ̂_KNN, which can be plugged directly into PF-ODE solvers or consistency training objectives.

## Method

The key insight is that because the forward likelihood p_t(z|x^(i)) is Gaussian, the k most probable elements of the posterior p_t(x^(i)|z) are exactly the k nearest neighbours of z in ℓ₂ distance, reducing a combinatorial posterior search to a fast FAISS KNN query. The paper constructs a self-normalized importance sampling (SNIS) estimator where the proposal q_t(x^(i)|z) assigns probability proportional to the forward likelihood for the k nearest neighbours and a constant (the k-th neighbour's likelihood) for all remaining N−k elements, making the normalizing constant Z_q computable in O(k) time. Two theoretical bounds are derived: the trace-of-covariance of the KNN estimator is upper-bounded by (Z_q/p_t(z)) times the multi-sample Monte Carlo trace-of-covariance, and by a convex combination of the Monte Carlo and uniform-SNIS trace-of-covariances. Empirically on CIFAR-10, the estimator achieves near-zero bias and variance, outperforming both STF (which uses a uniform proposal) and a pre-trained EDM network score estimator across most noise levels.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 single-step unconditional generation, iCT+KNN achieves FID 3.59 and IS 9.49, improving over the reimplemented iCT baseline (FID 3.67, IS 9.45) and the iCT+STF variant (FID 3.79, IS 9.50). In estimator quality experiments, the KNN score estimator reduces peak score MSE by approximately 100× compared to STF and outperforms a pre-trained near-SoTA EDM network score estimator for most noise levels t. For PF-ODE integration, the KNN estimator drives FID to 0.5, matching forward-process initialization and demonstrating it can replace a learned network for ODE traversal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the iCT baseline (Song & Dhariwal, 2024), the KNN estimator improves FID from 3.67 to 3.59 and IS from 9.45 to 9.49 in the authors' reimplementation, though the original reported iCT numbers (FID 2.83) could not be reproduced. Against STF (Xu et al., 2023), the KNN estimator wins decisively: in estimator quality, peak score MSE is ~100× lower, and in consistency training, iCT+KNN achieves FID 3.59 versus 3.79 for iCT+STF, with the authors attributing STF's degradation to its significant bias at intermediate noise levels. Against the EDM network score estimator (Karras et al., 2022), the KNN estimator achieves lower MSE for most t values, though EDM shows fewer artifacts at the highest noise levels. Against the original consistency distillation and consistency training baselines (Song et al., 2023), iCT+KNN substantially outperforms CT (FID 3.59 vs. 8.70) and is competitive with CD (FID 3.59 vs. 3.55), all without requiring a pre-trained diffusion teacher model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-normalized importance sampling (SNIS) framework introduced by Hesterberg (1995) and applied to diffusion score estimation by Xu et al. (2023, STF). The key departure is replacing STF's uniform proposal with a KNN-based proposal that matches the posterior shape, exploiting the fact that Gaussian forward likelihoods make the k most probable posterior elements equivalent to k nearest neighbours in Euclidean space. This enables fast retrieval via FAISS and yields a proposal that closely approximates the true posterior across noise levels. The method is then applied to two established settings: consistency training (Song et al., 2023; Song & Dhariwal, 2024) and PF-ODE integration (Karras et al., 2022), where it replaces either single-sample Monte Carlo estimators or learned neural network score approximators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The estimator achieves near-zero variance and bias on CIFAR-10, outperforming even a trained EDM network, and improves consistency training convergence and sample quality. However, a striking negative result shows that using this high-fidelity estimator for PF-ODE integration merely retrieves training data (FID 0.5 matching forward-process initialization), revealing that diffusion model generalization depends on network bias rather than the score-matching objective itself. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond CIFAR-10, whether latent-space KNN (à la LDM) could capture semantic similarity better than pixel-space l2, and whether the estimator can be integrated into distillation pipelines to enable from-scratch training without a pre-trained teacher.
