## Problem

Causal discovery under missing data faces a chicken-and-egg dilemma: good imputation requires knowledge of causal dependencies, yet structure learning requires complete data. Naively imputing and then applying a causal discovery algorithm is empirically sub-optimal, as reconstruction-quality imputation does not guarantee correct independence constraints. Existing score-based extensions like MissDAG rely on EM with closed-form posteriors, limiting scalability and robustness in non-Gaussian, non-linear, or misspecified settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes an incomplete data matrix X ∈ R^{n×d} (with missing entries stored as NaN) and a binary missing mask M ∈ {0,1}^{n×d}, and produces a weighted adjacency matrix W ∈ R^{d×d} encoding the causal DAG. Missing entries are zero-filled for computational convenience before being passed to the imputation network. The final DAG is extracted by applying a 0.3 magnitude threshold and iteratively removing the weakest edge until the graph is acyclic. The framework handles both continuous and binary tabular variables and supports MCAR, MAR, and MNAR missingness mechanisms.

## Method

OTM formulates structure learning as a density-fitting problem that minimizes the Wasserstein distance between the model distribution (induced by the SCM) and the empirical distribution over observed data. The key theoretical contribution (Theorem 3.3) introduces a stochastic correction map φ that transports pseudo-complete imputations to the model distribution, rendering the OT cost tractable for any initial imputer. The final objective jointly optimizes an imputation network Φ (a neural network outputting a Gaussian over complete values) and the SCM parameters θ, with four terms: element-wise reconstruction loss, an MMD divergence enforcing distributional consistency between imputed and model-generated data, a DAGMA log-determinant acyclicity regularizer, and L1 sparsity. This design lets φ and θ co-adapt—φ corrects imputations to obey the current causal graph while θ learns structure from the corrected data—avoiding the EM bottleneck and closed-form posterior assumptions of MissDAG. The framework is modular: any score-based causal discovery method (DAGMA, NOTEARS, etc.) can be swapped in by replacing the acyclicity regularizer.

## Results

On synthetic non-linear ANMs (50 nodes, 100 edges, MCAR at 10–50% missing rates), OTM achieves consistently the lowest SHD and highest or second-highest F1-score across all missing rates, outperforming all imputation-then-learn baselines and MissDAG. On real-world datasets (Neuropathic Pain: 222 binary variables; Sachs: 11 continuous variables; DREAM4: 100 continuous variables), OTM again attains the best or near-best F1 and SHD, while MissDAG fails to recover correct edge directions on the higher-dimensional Neuropathic Pain and DREAM4 datasets. OTM also demonstrates superior scalability: as the number of nodes increases, MissDAG's SHD degrades significantly and its training time grows substantially, whereas OTM maintains stable performance with faster run-times.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MissDAG (the primary competing method), OTM wins decisively in non-linear settings: MissDAG relies on rejection sampling for the intractable posterior in its EM E-step, which introduces approximation error and computational overhead that compounds with graph size; on real-world Neuropathic Pain and DREAM4, MissDAG's underlying NOTEARS solver fails to converge in many EM iterations, causing it to miss true edge directions entirely. Against the imputation-then-learn baselines (Mean, MissForest, Batch Sinkhorn, RoundRobin Sinkhorn, each followed by DAGMA), OTM consistently achieves lower SHD and higher F1, particularly at 30% and 50% missing rates where the gap widens; notably, MissForest produces the best reconstruction-quality imputation yet does not translate into the best causal discovery, confirming the paper's central claim that reconstruction-optimal imputation is sub-optimal for structure learning. Against the OT-imputation baselines (Sinkhorn-based), OTM's advantage grows with missing rate because it jointly optimizes the imputation and the causal graph rather than treating them sequentially. Overall, OTM is the top or second-best method across all 13 experimental configurations (3 missing mechanisms × 3 missing rates × synthetic/real), with the only settings where it is not strictly best being a few low-missing-rate synthetic cases where MissForest+DAGMA is marginally competitive.

## Relation to Prior Work

OTM departs from the dominant EM-based paradigm for causal discovery under missing data (exemplified by MissDAG) by reframing structure learning as a Wasserstein density-fitting problem, where the transport cost between incomplete data points is defined via pseudo-completions. It builds on DAGMA's log-determinant acyclicity characterization for non-linear settings and NOTEARS for linear ones, while drawing on the OT imputation literature (Muzellec et al., 2020) for the cost function design. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a stochastic correction map φ that jointly optimizes with the SCM parameters, replacing MissDAG's iterative E-step/M-step with a single differentiable objective that avoids rejection sampling entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, scalable alternative to EM-based methods for causal discovery under missing data, with the practical advantage of being a drop-in wrapper around any existing score-based causal discovery algorithm. The empirical demonstration that reconstruction-quality imputation does not guarantee accurate structure learning reinforces the need for joint optimization, though this observation has been noted in prior work. Open questions remain around MNAR identifiability (where the missingness mechanism is non-ignorable), convergence guarantees for the highly non-convex objective, and extension to cyclic graphs or latent confounders—settings where the current framework is inapplicable.
