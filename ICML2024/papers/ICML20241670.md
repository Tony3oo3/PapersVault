## Problem

Conditional neural processes (CNPs) learn to impute unobserved pixels given a context set, but prior work always selects context points randomly or via simple statistics like pixel variance. The paper asks the dual question: which pixels would a CNP actually like to observe, and do such learned context points carry meaningful, class-relevant information about the underlying image?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241670/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a full RGB or grayscale image (64×64 for most datasets, 256×256 for scalability experiments) and produces a sparse set of M pixel locations (x_M) with their corresponding colour values (y_M), constituting a 'partial pixel specification' that is only ~3.13% of the original pixels at M=128. The ConvCNP decoder then imputes all remaining pixels to reconstruct the full image. A distinguishing property is that M can be changed at inference time (e.g., 128→256) without retraining, and the context can be post-hoc augmented with 3×3 neighbourhood tiles to increase information density.

## Method

PPS-VAE casts the CNP context set as latent variables within an amortised variational framework, introducing an abstractive latent variable a that jointly controls both the spatial arrangement of M pixel locations and their associated values. The generative model samples locations via Gumbel-Softmax conditioned on a, looks up pixel values from the image, and imputes the remainder using a ConvCNP decoder. The inference model employs an autoregressive CNN encoder that sequentially selects pixel locations conditioned on the full image and previously chosen points, with the CNN inductive bias being critical—MLP-based variants degenerate into edge-biased lookup tables with no spatial meaning. The ELBO is optimised end-to-end, and the key design choice is that the abstractive latent a sits before the context set (unlike latent neural processes where the latent sits between context and target), allowing it to capture instance-specific associations between spatial layout and pixel values.

## Results

On in-distribution classification (F1-macro, M=128), PPS-VAE achieves 90.21 on CLEVR (object counting), 46.72 on FER2013 (emotion), 29.56 on Tiny ImageNet (200-class), and 69.00/94.86/62.13 on three CelebA attributes. With post-hoc 3×3 tile augmentation, PPS-VAE reaches 94.62 on CLEVR—surpassing the full-image baseline (91.90)—and 35.00 on Tiny ImageNet. On log marginal likelihood (Table 1), PPS-VAE outperforms both PPS-CAE and ConvCNP (random context) across all four datasets, e.g., 4951 vs. 4471 vs. 4472 on FER2013.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VQ-VAE, PPS-VAE achieves a large margin on CLEVR (90.21 vs. 75.91 F1-macro) but is slightly behind on FER2013 (46.72 vs. 50.98) and t-ImageNet (29.56 vs. 29.02); on CelebA attributes the two are essentially tied. Against FSQ-VAE, PPS-VAE again dominates on CLEVR (90.21 vs. 73.27) and is competitive elsewhere, with FSQ-VAE holding a small edge on t-ImageNet (31.03 vs. 29.56). PPS-VAE clearly outperforms PPS-CAE (the global-prior variant) across all datasets, most dramatically on CLEVR (90.21 vs. 48.50) and FER2013 (46.72 vs. 35.28), confirming that instance-specific context selection is critical. On log marginal likelihood, PPS-VAE beats both ConvCNP (random context) and PPS-CAE on every dataset, e.g., 16611 vs. 15981 vs. 16089 on CLEVR. Notably, with post-hoc tile augmentation, PPS-VAE surpasses the full-image baseline on CLEVR (94.62 vs. 91.90), suggesting the learned sparse representation acts as a denoised, class-relevant summary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Conditional Neural Process (CNP) framework for image completion and the VAE variational inference paradigm, but deliberately inverts the standard CNP setup: rather than treating the context set as a randomly or heuristically sampled input, it casts context selection as a learned latent variable (the Partial Pixel Specification) inferred jointly with the CNP. Conceptually, it bridges discrete representation learning (VQ-VAE, Concrete Autoencoder) with learning-to-learn stochastic processes, and is related to masked image modelling and hard attention in that it selects specific pixels, but differs in that the selection is amortised, generative, and tied to a CNP decoder. The key departure from prior CNP work (which focuses on inductive biases and output dependencies) is that the context set itself becomes the object of learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PPS-VAE provides a principled, generative framework for asking which pixels are most informative for a CNP, yielding spatially interpretable context sets (object boundaries, interiors, background) that capture class-relevant information as validated by classification probes. The ability to dynamically increase context-set size at inference without retraining is a practical differentiator over VQ-VAE/FSQ-VAE. However, the work is limited to small 64×64 images, a fixed M chosen a priori, and an autoregressive encoder that scales poorly; open questions include adaptive M per image, comparison with slot-attention-style region capture, and whether the framework scales to high-resolution images with more expressive CNP decoders.
