## Problem

Current evaluations of hazardous LLM capabilities (biosecurity, cybersecurity, chemical security) are private, manual, and narrowly scoped, preventing the broader research community from measuring or mitigating malicious-use risk. Additionally, there is no public benchmark for evaluating methods that remove hazardous knowledge from language models, bottlenecking progress on machine unlearning as a safety intervention.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241145/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

RMU takes a pretrained autoregressive language model (Zephyr-7B, YI-34B, or Mixtral-8x7B), an unlabeled forget corpus of hazardous text (PubMed papers for biosecurity, filtered GitHub passages for cybersecurity), and a retain corpus (Wikitext), and produces a fine-tuned model with reduced WMDP QA accuracy while preserving MMLU and MT-Bench performance. Unlike output-level unlearning, RMU operates on hidden-state activations at a single chosen layer ℓ, updating gradients only on layers ℓ−2 through ℓ and restricting updates to MLP parameters to save memory on larger models.

## Method

RMU (Representation Misdirection for Unlearning) fine-tunes model weights by optimizing a two-part loss on internal activations rather than output logits or individual neurons. The forget loss pushes activations on hazardous data toward a fixed random unit vector u scaled by a large coefficient c, effectively inflating activation norms in early layers so that later layers can no longer meaningfully process the representation. The retain loss is an ℓ² penalty that regularizes activations on benign data back to the frozen model's original activations, limiting collateral damage to general capabilities. The full loss is L = L_forget + α · L_retain, with α tuned per model (e.g., 1200 for Zephyr-7B, 350 for YI-34B). For multi-domain unlearning, gradient updates are interleaved across the biosecurity and cybersecurity forget distributions, and the method requires no access to the full WMDP question set—only approximate text corpora.

## Results

On ZEPHYR-7B, RMU reduces WMDP-Bio accuracy from 63.7% to 31.2% and WMDP-Cyber from 44.0% to 28.2% (near the 25% random baseline), while preserving MMLU accuracy at 57.1% (vs. 58.1% base) and MT-Bench at 7.10 (vs. 7.33). On YI-34B, RMU drops WMDP-Bio from 75.3% to 30.7% and WMDP-Cyber from 49.7% to 29.0%, with MMLU retained at 70.6% (vs. 72.6%). On MIXTRAL-8x7B, RMU reduces WMDP-Bio from 74.8% to 34.0% and WMDP-Cyber from 52.0% to 30.8%, with MMLU at 67.1% (vs. 68.2%). Linear probes on RMU-unlearned models achieve only slightly above random accuracy, and GCG adversarial attacks fail to recover hazardous knowledge after 2,500 optimization steps (vs. <50 steps on base models).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RMU dominates all three baselines on the ZEPHYR-7B comparison. LLMU barely reduces WMDP-Bio (59.5% vs. 63.7% base) while catastrophically degrading MMLU (44.7% vs. 58.1%) and MT-Bench (1.00 vs. 7.33), indicating it destroys general capabilities without effectively unlearning hazardous knowledge. SCRUB achieves a moderate WMDP-Bio reduction (43.8%) but still falls far short of random, and similarly cripples MT-Bench (1.43) and MMLU (51.2%). SSD reduces WMDP-Cyber to 35.0% but only WMDP-Bio to 50.2%, while dropping MMLU to 40.7%. RMU is the only method that pushes both WMDP categories to near-random (31.2% and 28.2%) while retaining MMLU within 1 point of base (57.1% vs. 58.1%) and MT-Bench within 0.23 points (7.10 vs. 7.33). The baselines were not evaluated on YI-34B or MIXTRAL-8x7B due to their poor performance on ZEPHYR-7B.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of representation engineering (Zou et al., 2023a) and machine unlearning, departing from both output-based losses (standard LM training) and neuron-level interventions (mechanistic interpretability) by intervening directly on activation norms. Prior unlearning work has focused on privacy (erasing specific samples/facts) or narrow behavioral concepts (gender, fictional universes), while hazardous-capability evaluations at major AI labs remain private and manual. WMDP extends the unlearning task from artificial deletion sets to a real-world, expert-written benchmark of domain-level scientific knowledge, and RMU builds on the representation-engineering insight that perturbing activations in early layers can redirect model behavior without retraining from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WMDP is the first public, automatic benchmark for hazardous knowledge in LLMs, filling a critical gap that has limited scientific inquiry into reducing malicious use. The key open question is unlearning precision: RMU over-removes closely related knowledge (virology drops to ~26%, computer security to ~45%), and the method does not prevent relearning via finetuning in open-source settings. The paper explicitly positions unlearning as one component of a broader sociotechnical solution (paired with structured API access and KYC), and the $200K expert-written benchmark will likely become a standard reference for the field as AI safety evaluations move toward public, reproducible metrics.
