## Problem

Instruction tuning (IT) has become the predominant method for aligning base LLMs into conversational agents, yet its limitations remain underexplored due to a lack of comprehensive evaluation metrics, unclear understanding of the exact transformation IT induces on model parameters, and the rapid adoption of semi-open-source chat models as black-box tools. This paper systematically investigates what IT actually does to a model's knowledge and response behavior, revealing that it fails to enhance knowledge, degrades factual accuracy through pattern-copying, and increases hallucination by borrowing tokens from conceptually similar training instances.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240624/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes base pre-trained LLMs (LLaMA-2 7B/13B/70B, Mistral 7B, Phi-1.5 1.3B) and fine-tunes them with instruction-response pairs from six open-source IT datasets (Alpaca, MedInstruct, LIMA, databricks-dolly, Tulu-V2-Mix, and a GPT-4-simplified LIMA variant) under two paradigms: LoRA fine-tuning and full-parameter fine-tuning. Evaluation is restricted to open-ended, free-form instruction following (just-eval-instruct_1k and MedInstruct-test_216) with greedy decoding, and responses are scored on five aspects—helpfulness, clarity, factuality, depth, and engagement—via GPT-4 Turbo and expert human evaluators rather than conventional NLP metrics.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a multi-faceted empirical analysis framework combining three complementary techniques: (1) token distribution analysis, computing KL divergence, base probability, and base rank between base and fine-tuned model outputs to quantify how much IT shifts a model away from pre-trained knowledge; (2) causal analysis of hallucinations, where expert human evaluators trace hallucinated spans back to specific IT dataset instances and identify the conceptual links driving erroneous token borrowing; and (3) controlled comparisons across training paradigms (LoRA vs. SFT), dataset scales (1K to 326K), and proposed IT improvements (NEFTune, AlpaGasus, WizardLM). A key mechanistic finding is that LoRA fine-tuning primarily learns response initiation tokens (high KL divergence in the first 5% of each sentence) while extracting most content from pre-trained knowledge, whereas full-parameter fine-tuning causes a uniform distribution shift across all tokens, leading to pattern-copying and hallucination sourced from conceptually similar IT instances. The paper further proposes response simplification in IT datasets as a practical mitigation for style-imitation-induced hallucinations, validated through human studies showing reduced hallucination rates.

## Results

The paper's central quantitative finding is that a LoRA fine-tuned (LFT) model trained on only 1k samples outperforms a full-parameter fine-tuned (SFT) model trained on 326× more data (Tulu-V2-Mix) on both factuality and usefulness across open-domain (just-eval-instruct) and knowledge-intensive (MedInstruct-test) evaluations. In hallucination analysis, 87% of hallucinated phrases in SFT models are causally linked to conceptually similar instances in the IT dataset, compared to only 13.9% for LFT models. Additionally, approximately 81.2% of words beginning with shifted tokens in SFT responses are directly borrowed from the IT dataset, and scaling the IT dataset by 52× or 326× yields negligible improvement for LFT across all five evaluation metrics (helpfulness, clarity, factuality, depth, engagement).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three IT improvement methods—AlpaGasus (dataset filtering), WizardLM (instruction complexity), and NEFTune (noisy embeddings)—against a simple LoRA fine-tuned baseline. All three methods outperform vanilla SFT on Alpaca (consistent with their original papers), but the LFT model outperforms all of them across every metric and task type on just-eval-instruct, indicating that pre-trained knowledge still dominates and these methods do not contribute meaningful knowledge advancement. In the hallucination analysis, SFT models (which these methods build upon) show an 87% causal hallucination rate tied to the IT dataset, while LFT models show only 13.9%, suggesting the performance gap is driven by reduced token borrowing from training data. The paper also contrasts LFT against SFT directly: LFT with just 1k samples beats SFT with 326× more data on factuality and usefulness, and even an open-domain LFT model outperforms a domain-specific SFT model (MedInstruct) on its own domain test set. Overall, the paper's method (LFT leveraging pre-trained knowledge) wins on factuality, usefulness, and hallucination rate against all compared baselines, while losing on depth and engagement metrics where longer, more detailed responses are preferred.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of empirical observations that instruction tuning primarily shapes response style rather than injecting new knowledge (Gudibande et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2023) by providing a mechanistic, token-level analysis that distinguishes between LoRA and full-parameter fine-tuning. It builds directly on Lin et al.'s (2023) token-distribution analysis framework and multi-aspect GPT-4 evaluation protocol, but departs from prior work by introducing a causal analysis of hallucinations that traces erroneous tokens back to specific conceptually-similar instances in the IT dataset. The paper also positions itself against popular IT improvement methods (NEFTune, AlpaGasus, WizardLM) by showing they do not surpass a simple LoRA baseline, challenging the incremental-improvement narrative in the IT literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that LoRA fine-tuning with 1K samples outperforms full-parameter fine-tuning with 326K samples on factuality fundamentally reframes how practitioners should approach IT for open-domain chat models, suggesting that the field's investment in scaling IT data and full fine-tuning may be misdirected. The causal analysis of hallucinations—showing 87% of SFT hallucinated phrases originate from conceptually similar IT instances—opens a new diagnostic avenue for understanding and mitigating hallucination. Key open questions include whether these findings generalize to domain-specific IT, multi-turn interactions, RLHF/DPO alignment, and retrieval-augmented settings, all of which the authors explicitly leave for future work.
