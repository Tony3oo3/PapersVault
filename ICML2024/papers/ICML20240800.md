## Problem

Existing speech editing systems are constrained to a predefined set of content operations (insert, delete, replace) and require user-drawn masks or per-example prompts, while acoustic attributes such as timbre, volume, speed, emotion, and style remain largely unaddressed. No prior model can follow a free-form natural language instruction to edit both the semantic content and acoustic properties of a speech sample in a unified framework.

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural language instruction (e.g., 'Make it sound happy', 'Add the word sunny between good and day') and an input speech clip as conditions, and autoregressively generates discrete speech tokens representing the edited output. Speech is represented by Hubert semantic tokens (codebook 1000, 320× downsampling) and SoundStream acoustic tokens (12 quantization levels, codebook 1024, 320× downsampling at 16 kHz), while the instruction is encoded by a frozen Flan-T5-XL text encoder. The generated tokens are converted to a waveform via a BigVGAN unit-based vocoder.

## Method

InstructSpeech is a decoder-only LLM built on differentiable multiscale transformers (a large global transformer operating on patches and a small local transformer operating within each patch) that casts conditional speech generation as a sequence-to-sequence task. To steer generation toward the correct task, a fine-tuned Flan-T5-XL predicts a task index whose learned embedding is prepended to the input, and the model is trained jointly on an extensive set of speech editing and processing tasks (TTS, VC, ASR, duration prediction, region-based and free-form content editing, emotion/style/speed/volume/gender editing). For free-form semantic editing without a predefined mask, a multi-step reasoning pipeline decomposes the task into ASR, frame-level duration prediction, and region-based editing. A hierarchical adapter enables few-shot adaptation to new tasks by updating only ~1% of parameters (learnable prompts in the global transformer plus bias/norm tuning in the local transformer) with a zero-initialized gating factor. Triplet training data (instruction, input speech, output speech) is synthesized by combining GPT-3.5-Turbo for diverse instruction generation with pretrained speech generative models for target speech synthesis.

## Results

On acoustic editing tasks, InstructSpeech (Large, 1.2B) achieves 84.9% speed accuracy, 91.3% volume accuracy, 57.3% emotion accuracy, and 92.3% style accuracy, with a CISP text-speech alignment score of 0.61 and a subjective MOS of 4.05. For region-based semantic content editing, the Large model attains WERs of 5.1 (Add), 4.9 (Remove), and 5.6 (Replace) with 0.98 speaker similarity, outperforming both EditSpeech and A3T. The multi-step reasoning pipeline for free-form editing achieves an overall WER of 12.6 (Add), 11.9 (Remove), and 14.1 (Replace) for the Large model, with a phone recognition PER of 7.3 and alignment MAE of 0.61.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against YourTTS (Casanova et al., 2022), InstructSpeech (Medium) substantially outperforms on emotion accuracy (55.2% vs. 38.7%) and style accuracy (90.6% vs. 30.3%), as well as MOS (4.01 vs. 3.91) and SMOS (3.95 vs. 3.86), demonstrating that instruction-guided editing far exceeds prompt-based TTS for attribute manipulation. For region-based content editing, InstructSpeech (Large) beats EditSpeech (Tan et al., 2021) on all three WER metrics (5.1/4.9/5.6 vs. 9.2/5.9/6.5) and SIM (0.98 vs. 0.97), and similarly outperforms A3T (Bai et al., 2022) (5.1/4.9/5.6 vs. 7.5/7.8/6.9 WER; 0.98 vs. 0.96 SIM). The paper also compares against VALL-E (Wang et al., 2023a) and Spear-TTS (Zhang et al., 2023b) on zero-shot TTS speaker transfer in Appendix F.3, though specific numbers for those comparisons are not tabulated in the main text. InstructSpeech remains below ground-truth on emotion accuracy (57.3% vs. 86.9%) and style accuracy (92.3% vs. 96.1%), but the gap narrows considerably compared to prior methods, and the CISP alignment scores (0.61 text, 0.69 speech) confirm faithful instruction following.

## Relation to Prior Work

InstructSpeech builds on the multiscale transformer architecture from Megabyte and UniAudio for efficient long-sequence speech token generation, and adopts BigVGAN for waveform reconstruction. It departs from prior speech editing systems (EditSpeech, A3T, VoiceBox) that required user-drawn masks and supported only a fixed set of content operations, by formulating editing as instruction-following over both semantic and acoustic attributes. The hierarchical adapter design draws on LLaMA-Adapter's zero-init gating mechanism, while the CISP evaluation metric extends CLAP's contrastive audio-text pretraining to the speech-instruction domain. Unlike instruction-guided TTS (Prompt-TTS, Instruct-TTS) that synthesizes new speech from scratch, InstructSpeech edits existing speech, and unlike instruction-guided music/sound editing (AUDIT, InstructME), it targets human speech specifically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unified framework to follow natural language instructions for both semantic content editing and acoustic attribute manipulation (emotion, style, speed, volume, gender) in a single model, removing the need for user-drawn masks and predefined operation sets. The CISP benchmark provides a principled way to measure instruction-speech alignment that existing metrics (WER, speaker similarity) cannot capture. Key limitations include strong dependency on audio tokenizer quality, restricted speed editing to a single speaker identity, and challenges in truly open-domain instruction following. Open questions include fine-grained (phoneme-level) speed control, scaling to multilingual and multi-speaker settings, and whether the multi-step reasoning approach generalizes beyond the three content operations tested.
