## Problem

Real-world LLM applications require specific capabilities (e.g., reasoning), but training on large, mixed instruction tuning datasets can hinder development of those targeted skills. The open question is how to select the most relevant subset from a large, diverse instruction dataset given only a handful of examples embodying the target capability. Existing influence-based data selection methods fail in this setting because they assume SGD (not Adam), cannot handle variable-length sequence gradients, and are computationally intractable for LLM-scale models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242221/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a large heterogeneous instruction tuning pool (~270K examples from FLAN V2, COT, DOLLY, and Open Assistant 1), a very small validation set of few-shot examples per subtask (as few as 9 total for TYDIQA, 285 for MMLU), and a pre-trained LLM. It produces a selected 5% training subset and a LoRA-fine-tuned model. Notably, the selection model and target model can differ (LESS-T), and the gradient datastore is built once and reused across arbitrary new target tasks without recomputation.

## Method

LESS adapts first-order influence estimation to the Adam optimizer by replacing the SGD gradient inner product with cosine similarity between the validation gradient and the Adam update Γ(z,θ), which also resolves the variable-length sequence gradient norm bias that would otherwise favor short completions. To make this tractable at LLM scale, LESS uses LoRA to reduce trainable parameters to ~2% of the model, then applies Johnson-Lindenstrauss random projections to compress gradients to 8192 dimensions, building a reusable gradient datastore. A short warmup training phase (4 epochs on 5% random data) initializes the Adam moment estimates needed for Γ. For multi-subtask validation sets, each candidate example is scored by its maximum Adam influence across all subtasks, and the top 5% are selected for final fine-tuning of the target model.

## Results

LESS selects 5% of the instruction tuning data that consistently outperforms random selection by 2–5 points across MMLU, TYDIQA, and BBH on LLAMA-2-7B, LLAMA-2-13B, and MISTRAL-7B. Notably, in most cases the 5% LESS-selected subset outperforms training on the full 100% dataset (e.g., TYDIQA: 56.2 vs. 54.0 on LLAMA-2-7B; BBH: 56.0 vs. 53.0 on MISTRAL-7B). In the transfer setting (LESS-T), data selected using LLAMA-2-7B's gradient features boosts LLAMA-2-13B and MISTRAL-7B performance, confirming cross-model generalizability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LESS outperforms all three baselines on every evaluated dataset. Against BM25 (word-frequency/TF-IDF ranking), LESS gains +2.6 on MMLU (50.2 vs. 47.6), +3.5 on TYDIQA (56.2 vs. 52.7), and +1.7 on BBH (41.5 vs. 39.8). Against DSIR (n-gram importance resampling), the margins are even larger: +4.1 on MMLU, +11.7 on TYDIQA, and +4.7 on BBH, with DSIR sometimes performing worse than random selection. Against RDS (representation-based selection using final-layer hidden states), LESS leads by +5.2 on MMLU, +9.4 on TYDIQA, and +4.8 on BBH. The paper concludes that LESS is the only consistently effective approach, while surface-form and representation-based methods show minimal improvement over random selection, suggesting that instruction data requires a refined notion of similarity (i.e., optimizer-aware gradient influence) rather than topical or semantic matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

LESS extends the first-order influence formulation of Pruthi et al. (2020) from SGD to the Adam optimizer and from fixed-length to variable-length instruction data, addressing two practical gaps that had prevented gradient-based influence estimation from being applied to LLM instruction tuning. It draws on the coreset selection and data attribution literature (Koh & Liang 2017; Killamsetty et al. 2021; Han et al. 2023) but departs from prior data selection methods (BM25, DSIR, RDS) by operating on optimizer-aware gradient features rather than surface-form or representation-based similarity. The computational feasibility is achieved through a combination of LoRA (Hu et al. 2021) for parameter reduction and Johnson-Lindenstrauss random projections (Johnson & Lindenstrauss 1984; Park et al. 2023) for dimensionality reduction, creating a reusable gradient datastore that prior influence-based methods did not provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a LESS-selected 5% subset often outperforms training on the full 100% dataset across multiple models and benchmarks challenges the common assumption that more instruction tuning data is always better, with implications for understanding catastrophic forgetting and data quality in LLM fine-tuning. The transferability result—that a 7B model's gradient features can effectively select data for 13B or architecturally different models—suggests that data selection costs can be amortized across model families, making the approach scalable. Key limitations include the mandatory warmup training phase, the linear additivity assumption in influence scoring, and the fact that minimizing cross-entropy loss does not monotonically improve task performance in instruction tuning. Open questions include token-level (rather than sequence-level) selection, test-time adaptation, and extending the framework to non-differentiable objectives like toxicity.
