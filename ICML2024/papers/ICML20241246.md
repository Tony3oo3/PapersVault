## Problem

Existing conformal prediction (CP) methods produce uninformative prediction sets (nearly the full class set) under standard l∞-norm adversarial attacks when the underlying model is not adversarially trained, and even adversarially trained models from advanced methods like TRADES and MART exhibit larger prediction set sizes than vanilla AT, undermining CP efficiency. This paper identifies the gap between adversarial robustness and CP efficiency and asks how to train models that are both adversarially robust and CP-efficient.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241246/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, higher resolution for Caltech-256 and CUB-200) perturbed by l∞-norm bounded adversarial examples (ε=8/255 via PGD or AutoAttack). Rather than outputting a single Top-1 class, the model produces a prediction set—a subset of class labels—guaranteed to contain the true label with 90% coverage via split conformal prediction. The backbone is an adversarially pre-trained ResNet-50 (ε_pt=4/255) fine-tuned on each dataset.

## Method

The proposed Uncertainty-Reducing Adversarial Training (AT-UR) augments standard AT with two components: (1) a Beta-weighting loss that assigns each training sample an importance weight from a Beta density function (a=1.1, b∈{3,4,5}) evaluated at its normalized True Class Probability Ranking (TCPR), shifting the training focus toward 'promising' samples whose TCPR is neither 1 (already correct) nor very large (likely hopeless); and (2) an entropy minimization regularizer (λ_EM=0.3) that reduces the entropy of the predictive distribution. The key theoretical result (Theorem 5.1) proves that the Beta-weighting loss is an upper bound on the expected prediction set size at the population level, establishing a direct optimization target for CP efficiency. AT-UR is composable with existing AT baselines (AT, FAT, TRADES), yielding variants like AT-Beta-EM and TRADES-EM, and is validated across four datasets under both PGD100 and AutoAttack.

## Results

Under AutoAttack (ε=8/255), the proposed AT-UR variants consistently reduce Prediction Set Size (PSS) while maintaining ~90% coverage across all four datasets. The most substantial gains appear on high-class-count datasets: on Caltech-256, AT-Beta reduces PSS from 23.73 (AT) to 18.54, and TRADES-EM reduces PSS from 22.82 to 15.03; on CUB-200, TRADES-EM reduces PSS from 22.29 to 17.80. On CIFAR-100, AT-Beta achieves the best PSS of 11.61 (vs. 14.29 for AT and 12.22 for TRADES). Coverage remains within the target 90% range (typically 91–94%) across all variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla AT (Madry et al., 2018), AT-UR variants reduce PSS by 3–22% depending on dataset and variant, with the largest gains on Caltech-256 (AT-Beta: 23.73→18.54, a 22% reduction) and CUB-200 (AT-Beta-EM: 17.75→16.67). Against TRADES (Zhang et al., 2019), the EM variant is most effective, cutting PSS on Caltech-256 from 22.82 to 15.03 (34% reduction) and on CUB-200 from 22.29 to 17.80 (20% reduction), while Beta weighting is more effective on CIFAR-100 (12.22→10.76). Against FAT (Xu et al., 2021), AT-Beta and FAT-Beta-EM achieve the best PSS on CIFAR-100 (11.10 and 11.09 vs. 13.60) and Caltech-256 (18.07 and 18.16 vs. 22.85). Against MART (Wang et al., 2019), the paper shows in Table 1 that MART actually increases PSS relative to AT (e.g., 15.26 vs. 14.29 on CIFAR-100), confirming that improved Top-1 robust accuracy does not translate to better CP efficiency. Against Conformal AT (Einbinder et al., 2022), AT-Beta achieves a lower PSS of 30.18 versus 35.32 on CIFAR-100 under the same adversarial setting, demonstrating that Beta-weighting during training is more effective than post-hoc conformal training for reducing prediction set size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of adversarial training (AT) and conformal prediction (CP), building directly on vanilla AT (Madry et al., 2018), TRADES (Zhang et al., 2019), and FAT (Xu et al., 2021) as training baselines, and on split conformal prediction methods (APS, RAPS) for inference. It departs from prior CP-under-attacks work (Gendler et al., 2021; Ghosh et al., 2023) by moving from small l2-norm budgets to the standard l-infinity attacks used in the adversarial defense community, and from post-hoc calibration to CP-aware training. The key conceptual departure is showing that improved AT methods (TRADES, MART) that boost Top-1 robust accuracy actually increase prediction set size, motivating a new training objective (Beta-weighting on TCPR plus entropy minimization) with a theoretical upper-bound connection to PSS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For safety-critical deployments (medical imaging, autonomous driving), this paper establishes that adversarial training is a prerequisite for conformal prediction to produce informative prediction sets, and that the common practice of choosing the most robust AT method may actively harm uncertainty quantification. The main limitation is the assumption that the calibration and test sets face the same known adversary, which restricts practical applicability; the authors identify adversary-agnostic CP and extension to LLMs as future directions. The theoretical bound linking Beta-weighting to expected PSS is one of the first to formally connect importance weighting in training to conformal prediction set size, providing a principled justification for the proposed loss.
