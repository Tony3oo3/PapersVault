## Problem

Most multi-view clustering algorithms require the true number of clusters k as a priori input, which is rarely available in real-world scenarios. Existing k-estimation techniques (ELBOW, gap statistic) are computationally expensive, designed primarily for convex k-means-style data, and do not address cross-view heterogeneity. This paper tackles the gap by formulating k inference as a lightweight reinforcement learning problem that operates within the clustering iteration itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240390/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes V views of n samples (each view X^(v) ∈ R^{d_v × n}) and constructs per-view mutual nearest neighbor graphs, which are combined into a single consensus graph G with m edges via view voting. It outputs a cluster indicator matrix Y ∈ R^{n×k} and the inferred k, both produced without any user-specified cluster count. The k-inference module is fully decoupled from the underlying MVC algorithm, requiring only the consensus graph and the current Y to compute its reward, making it pluggable into any existing spectral or subspace-based MVC pipeline.

## Method

The core mechanism defines a reward R(k) = −α · Σ G_ij · 1(y_i ≠ y_j) that penalizes inter-cluster connectivity on the consensus graph, so that an incorrect k (too few or too many clusters) inflates the number of graph edges crossing cluster boundaries. K candidate replicas, each running spectral clustering with a different k, are treated as arms in a multi-armed bandit; an ε-greedy policy with optimistic initial values selects which replica to update at each iteration, and the resulting Y is scored against G to produce the reward. Because the inter-cluster connectivity reward is non-sparse and stabilizes quickly as Y converges, the full MDP reduces to a single-state bandit, eliminating the need for experience replay or complex state representations. The key insight over prior WSS-based rewards (Liu et al., 2023) is that WSS computed directly from Y_k is insensitive to k* without a learned representation space, whereas inter-cluster connectivity on the pre-constructed graph directly reflects structural distortion caused by an erroneous k.

## Results

On BBC14, the proposed method achieves 87.72% NMI, 99.31% Purity, and 94.11% ARI, outperforming the best baseline (CoMSC) by 13.64%, 2.76%, and 4.60% respectively. On Handwritten Numerals, it reaches 94.38% NMI and 97.50% Purity, surpassing the next-best methods (MLAN at 90.40% NMI, 95.60% Purity). On MSRC-v1, it attains 79.76% NMI and 88.57% Purity, leading all baselines. However, ARI scores are notably lower than several baselines on MSRC-v1 (74.51 vs. 91.77 for CoMSC) and ORL (69.18 vs. 98.71 for CSMSC), suggesting the method's advantage is concentrated in NMI and Purity rather than pairwise agreement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is compared against 14 baselines spanning co-training (Co-train, Co-reg), adaptive-neighbor (MLAN, AWP), graph-based (GMC), subspace (SMVSC, FPMVS, COMVSC, CSMSC, CoMSC, EOMSC, LMVSC), and partition-aligned (mPAC) approaches. On BBC14, the proposed method dominates all baselines across all three metrics, with the largest margin over CoMSC (+13.64% NMI) and PMSC (+42.02% NMI). On Handwritten Numerals, it leads in NMI (94.38 vs. MLAN's 90.40) and Purity (97.50 vs. MLAN's 95.60) but trails MLAN on ARI (94.46 vs. 98.29). On MSRC-v1, it achieves the best NMI (79.76 vs. GMC/CoMSC at 73.90) and Purity (88.57 vs. CoMSC at 82.06), yet its ARI of 74.51 is substantially below CoMSC (91.77) and CSMSC (91.51). On ORL, it edges out AWP on NMI (90.39 vs. 89.45) but falls well short on Purity (82.25 vs. EOMSC's 92.50) and ARI (69.18 vs. CSMSC's 98.71). Overall, the method's advantage is strongest in NMI and Purity, while its ARI weakness on MSRC-v1 and ORL suggests the inferred k or the spectral clustering step may produce cluster boundaries that, while globally coherent, do not maximize pairwise agreement with ground truth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reinforcement-learning-based k-inference paradigm of Liu et al. (ACM MM 2023) from single-view graph clustering to the multi-view setting, replacing the WSS-based reward with a graph-consistency reward measured by inter-cluster connectivity on a consensus MNN graph. It also departs from that work by simplifying the full DQN formulation to a multi-armed bandit, arguing that the reward's stationarity across iterations eliminates the need for state transitions. The base clustering module relies on partition-level multi-view subspace clustering (Kang et al., 2020), and the k-inference module is deliberately decoupled so it can be grafted onto any existing MVC framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular separation of k-inference from the clustering algorithm is a practically useful design choice, enabling automatic cluster-number estimation without modifying the underlying MVC method. However, the ARI scores are notably lower than NMI and Purity on MSRC-v1 (74.5 vs 79.8/88.6) and ORL (69.2 vs 90.4/82.3), suggesting pairwise cluster agreement is weaker than the headline metrics imply. The sensitivity analysis shows correct k identification in only 37/50 parameter settings, and the theoretical guarantee (Eq. 4) depends on a condition involving the number of graph edges m that is not tightly characterized. Open questions include whether the bandit approach scales to deep MVC methods with non-stationary rewards and to datasets with hundreds of clusters.
