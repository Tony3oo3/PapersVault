## Problem

Prior work on steerable spherical neurons was limited to the 3D case (O(3)), leaving open how to generalize equivariant spherical decision surfaces to arbitrary dimension n and how to compose them across multiple layers. Additionally, most equivariant point-cloud methods require constructing an alternative basis (e.g., spherical harmonics) or operate on hand-crafted invariant descriptors, limiting their generality and expressiveness for the full orthogonal group O(n) including reflections.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an N-point set in R^n (e.g., a 20×3 3D skeleton or a 16-point 5D set) and produces an O(n)-invariant scalar output (class score, regression value, or volume). Each input point is first quadratically embedded into R^{n+2} via conformal geometric algebra, then processed point-wise through cascaded equivariant hypersphere layers that increment the feature dimensionality by one per layer. Invariance is achieved by computing the Gram matrix of the resulting equivariant features and applying permutation-invariant aggregation (sort + max/mean pooling) over the N points.

## Method

The core unit is an O(n)-equivariant hypersphere neuron: a single learnable sphere in R^{n+2} is rotated to the n+1 vertices of a regular n-simplex, producing an (n+1)×(n+2) filter bank B_n(S) whose output transforms under the representation V_n = M_n^T R_O R R_O^T M_n, where M_n is the orthogonal change-of-basis matrix built from the simplex vertices. Layers are cascaded by re-embedding the (n+1)-D output into R^{n+3} and applying the next hypersphere, enabling deep propagation with unit dimensionality increments. An equivariant scalar bias and a non-linear normalization (Y / (σ(a)(‖Y‖−1)+1)) are shown to preserve equivariance. For invariant readout, the paper introduces the operator Δ = YY^T — the Gram matrix of equivariant features — which naturally encodes the conformal-geometric-algebra relation between two points and a sphere, and requires no additional basis construction beyond the input coordinates.

## Results

On O(3) action recognition (UTKinect-Action3D), DEH achieves 87.36% accuracy with 8.1K parameters, outperforming all equivariant and invariant baselines. On O(5) regression, DEH reaches 0.0007 MSE with only 275 parameters, surpassing EMLP, CGENN, VN, GVP, SGM, and Scalars. On O(5) convex hull volume prediction, DEH achieves 1.3166 MSE with 49.8K parameters, outperforming all competing methods including the E(n)-equivariant approach of Satorras et al. (2021). Across all three tasks, DEH also exhibits a favorable speed/performance trade-off, being faster than most competitors at equal or better accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On O(3) action recognition, DEH (87.36%) outperforms the non-equivariant MLP trained with O(3)-augmentation, as well as the equivariant VN, GVP, and CGENN methods, and the invariant SGM descriptor, while also being faster at inference. On O(5) regression, DEH (0.0007 MSE) beats the O(5)- and SO(5)-equivariant EMLP (Finzi et al., 2021), CGENN (Ruhe et al., 2023), VN, GVP, the invariant SGM and Scalars (Villar et al., 2021) methods, and both the vanilla and augmented MLP baselines; it is also more stable across training set sizes than CGENN. On O(5) convex hull volume prediction, DEH (1.3166 MSE) outperforms all competing equivariant and invariant methods, including the MLP version of the E(n)-equivariant approach (Satorras et al., 2021) and the CGENN results from Ruhe et al. (2023), though a point-wise MLP slightly edges out DEH in the lowest-data regime. Across all tasks, DEH achieves the best speed/performance trade-off, being faster than most competitors while maintaining superior accuracy or lower error.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the steerable 3D spherical neuron (Melnyk et al., 2022) and its O(3)-invariant point cloud application TetraSphere (Melnyk et al., 2024) to arbitrary dimension n, replacing the regular tetrahedron with a regular n-simplex and proving that n+1 vertices are both necessary and sufficient for O(n)-equivariance. The non-linear embedding and spherical decision surface originate from conformal geometric algebra (Perwass et al., 2003; Li et al., 2001), while the invariant operator (a Gram matrix of equivariant features) is derived from the two-point-and-sphere relation in Li et al. (2001). Unlike methods requiring alternative bases (spherical harmonics, tensor fields) or integral-based constructions (EMLPs), DEH operates directly on input points, and unlike scalar/vector methods (VN, GVP, CGENN), it uses a geometrically grounded multi-sphere constellation rather than learned linear combinations of scalar and vector channels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, dimension-agnostic theoretical framework for O(n)-equivariant deep features based on spherical geometry, with rigorous proofs that the regular n-simplex is the correct vertex configuration and that cascading layers while incrementing dimensionality preserves equivariance. However, all experiments are on small-scale benchmarks (20-point 3D skeletons, 2-point O(5) regression, 16-point O(5) convex hulls) with point-wise architectures, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger point clouds or benefits from graph-based neighborhood interactions. The current formulation only produces invariant outputs; extending to equivariant outputs and full E(n) (translation-equivariant) settings remains future work, as does wrapping the hyperspheres in GNN frameworks for scalability.
