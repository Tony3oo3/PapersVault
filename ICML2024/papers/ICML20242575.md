## Problem

Graphon mean field games model heterogeneous agent interactions on networks, but the widely adopted continuum-player formulation suffers from a fundamental joint measurability issue that complicates both analysis and algorithm design. Moreover, prior learning methods for graphon games required access to oracles (state-process generators or optimal-policy calculators), and no oracle-free algorithm with non-asymptotic sample complexity guarantees existed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graphon W ∈ L₁[0,1]² encoding pairwise interaction intensities, a Markov transition kernel P, running/terminal reward functions f and g, and an initial label-state law λ ∈ P_unif([0,1]×R^d). For algorithmic implementation, the continuous label space [0,1] is discretized into D classes via a projection Π_D, and the state/action spaces are assumed finite. The output is the equilibrium population measure μ̂ (a measure on [0,1]×C with uniform first marginal), the equilibrium closed-loop policy π̂: T×[0,1]×R^d→P(A), and an ε-approximate Nash equilibrium for the corresponding finite n-player game on a dense graph sequence.

## Method

The paper introduces a representative-player graphon game where a single agent with random label U~Unif[0,1] optimizes her reward against a fixed population measure μ, interacting only through the graphon operator Wμ(U) that averages neighbor states weighted by edge intensities. This formulation avoids the measurability pathology of continuum-player games by encoding all types into a single label-state joint measure via disintegration. For learning, the algorithm performs approximate fixed-point iteration where SARSA (with a Lipschitz softmax policy operator) estimates the optimal Q-function and MCMC estimates the induced population measure, both updated concurrently from the same online samples—making it the first fully oracle-free algorithm for graphon games. The sample complexity is O(κ⁻⁵ε⁻⁴log²ε⁻¹) total samples, with K=O(κ⁻¹log ε⁻¹) outer iterations, D=O(κ⁻¹ε⁻¹) label classes, and H=O(κ⁻³ε⁻³log ε⁻¹) inner updates per iteration.

## Results

The paper establishes the first non-asymptotic sample complexity bound for learning graphon game equilibria: O(κ⁻⁵ ε⁻⁴ log² ε⁻¹) total samples, where κ is the contraction constant and ε is the target accuracy (Theorem 5.4). In numerical experiments on Flocking-Graphon across four graphon types (uniform attachment, ranked attachment, Erdős-Rényi, threshold), the oracle-free SARSA+MCMC algorithm converges to the benchmark equilibrium in approximately 50 epochs, with consistent performance across all graphons. The theoretical contributions include existence (Theorem 4.5), uniqueness under Lasry-Lions monotonicity (Theorem 4.7), and approximate equilibrium for finite-player network games (Theorem 4.9) under weaker assumptions than prior work (L₁-integrable graphon rather than jointly Lipschitz).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Cui & Koeppl (2022, ICLR), whose equivalent-class method provides the benchmark equilibrium solution used to measure W1-distances in the Flocking-Graphon experiments. The key differentiator is that Cui & Koeppl's algorithm requires access to an oracle (a state process generator) and only establishes asymptotic convergence, whereas this paper's method is fully oracle-free (using only online SARSA and MCMC updates) and provides a finite-sample complexity guarantee of O(κ⁻⁵ ε⁻⁴ log² ε⁻¹). In the numerical experiments, the proposed algorithm matches the Cui & Koeppl benchmark solution, converging in ~50 epochs with consistent behavior across all four tested graphons. Compared to Zhang et al. (2024b), which assumes both a population dynamics oracle and an action-value function oracle under entropic regularization, this paper requires neither oracle and handles the general (non-regularized) setting. The paper also improves on prior work's assumptions: the graphon need only be L₁-integrable (not jointly Lipschitz), and graphon convergence is required in the weaker strong operator topology rather than cut norm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the representative-player graphon game formulation introduced by Lacker & Soret (2023) from continuous time to discrete time with continuous state and action spaces, and adds the first oracle-free online learning algorithm with sample complexity guarantees. It deliberately departs from the widely-adopted continuum-player formulation (Caines & Huang, 2021; Cui & Koeppl, 2022; Zhang et al., 2024b), which the authors show suffers from a fundamental joint-measurability incompatibility under the standard product σ-algebra. The learning scheme builds on fixed-point iteration methods for MFGs (Guo et al., 2019) and stochastic approximation theory (Zou et al., 2019), but is the first to combine SARSA-based policy estimation with MCMC-based population estimation in a fully online, oracle-free manner for graphon games.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mathematically cleaner and algorithmically tractable foundation for graphon games, resolving a measurability gap that has been overlooked in the continuum-player literature. However, the sample complexity analysis requires a contraction mapping assumption (Assumption 5.1(3)) that is restrictive and hard to verify in practice, and the algorithm is limited to finite state/action spaces with a discretized label space, leaving the continuous-space algorithmic problem open. Future work could address sparse graph limits (where graphon convergence fails), remove the contraction assumption via alternative fixed-point theorems, and develop function-approximation-based algorithms for continuous label spaces.
