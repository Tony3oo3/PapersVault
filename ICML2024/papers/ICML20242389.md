## Problem

Standard LLM alignment methods (RLHF, DPO) rely on human preference data to train reward models, which caps both the size and quality of the training signal at human performance level. The paper asks whether a single model can simultaneously improve at instruction following and at providing its own reward signal, thereby removing the human-feedback bottleneck and enabling iterative self-improvement beyond what the original seed data permits.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242389/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For instruction following, the model takes a text prompt and generates a free-form response. For self-rewarding, it takes an instruction–response pair and produces a chain-of-thought justification followed by an additive 1–5 score based on five criteria (relevance, coverage, usefulness, clarity, expertise). During the self-instruction creation loop, the model generates N=4 candidate responses per prompt via sampling (T=0.7, p=0.9), evaluates each with the same additive scoring prompt (averaged over 3 sampled evaluations), and the highest- and lowest-scoring responses form a DPO preference pair (discarded if scores tie).

## Method

The core contribution is a unified model that serves as both the policy (instruction follower) and the reward model (LLM-as-a-Judge), trained via an Iterative DPO loop. Starting from a Llama 2 70B base, M1 is SFT-trained on seed IFT data (3,200 Open Assistant examples) plus EFT data (1,630 evaluation examples teaching the additive scoring format). In each subsequent iteration, the current model generates new prompts via Self-Instruct-style few-shot prompting, samples 4 candidate responses, scores them with its own LLM-as-a-Judge capability, and the resulting preference pairs (AIFT data) are used to train the next model via DPO (β=0.1, lr=1e-6). The key insight is that because reward modeling is formulated as an instruction-following task within the same model, improvements in general instruction following transfer to better reward quality, creating a virtuous circle where each iteration produces a higher-quality preference dataset than the last—without any additional human annotations. This contrasts with prior iterative methods (e.g., Pairwise Cringe Optimization) that use a fixed external reward model, and with RLAIF approaches that still require a separate frozen judge.

## Results

On AlpacaEval 2.0 (win rate over GPT-4 Turbo, judged by GPT-4), Self-Rewarding iterations yield progressively higher scores: M1=9.94%, M2=15.38%, M3=20.44%. The M3 model outperforms Claude 2 (17.19%), Gemini Pro (16.85%), and GPT-4 0613 (15.76%), while remaining below GPT-4 0314 (22.07%) and Mistral Medium (21.86%). On MT-Bench, overall scores improve from 6.85 (SFT) to 7.25 (M3), with larger gains in writing, roleplay, and STEM than in math/coding/reasoning. Reward modeling pairwise accuracy with human rankings improves from 65.1% (SFT) to 81.7% (M3) across iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On AlpacaEval 2.0, Self-Rewarding M3 (20.44%) outperforms Claude 2 (17.19%), Gemini Pro (16.85%), GPT-4 0613 (15.76%), GPT-3.5 Turbo (14.13%), LLaMA 2 Chat 70B (13.87%), and Vicuna 33B (12.71%), but falls short of GPT-4 0314 (22.07%) and Mistral Medium (21.86%). Internally, each iteration improves over the previous: M2 achieves 55.5% head-to-head wins over M1, and M3 achieves 47.7% wins over M2, with M3 winning 62.5% of head-to-head comparisons against the SFT baseline. On MT-Bench, M3 (7.25) improves over the SFT baseline (6.85) and M1 (6.78), with the largest relative gains in writing (8.83→9.58) and roleplay (8.15→8.73), while math and coding show only modest improvements (3.00→3.50 and 3.50→4.20). On NLP benchmarks (ARC, HellaSwag, GSM8K, MMLU, NQ), Self-Rewarding models roughly maintain or slightly degrade performance relative to the Llama 2 base and SFT baseline, consistent with the 'alignment tax' observed in prior work. The paper notes that competing models in Table 1 typically use proprietary alignment data (often >1M annotations) or targets distilled from stronger models, whereas Self-Rewarding starts from only 3,200 Open Assistant seed examples and generates all subsequent training data autonomously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Iterative DPO framework from Pairwise Cringe Optimization (Xu et al., 2023), which used a fixed external reward model, by replacing it with the language model itself acting as its own reward model via LLM-as-a-Judge prompting. It also builds on Self-Instruct (Wang et al., 2023) for prompt generation and DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) for preference optimization, but departs from RLAIF (Lee et al., 2023) and Constitutional AI (Bai et al., 2022b) by eliminating the separate fixed reward model entirely. The key conceptual departure is that the model simultaneously improves at instruction following and reward modeling across iterations, creating a self-reinforcing loop rather than a one-directional training pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates a concrete path toward models that can improve beyond the quality of their initial human-annotated seed data, addressing a fundamental bottleneck in LLM alignment where reward models are either frozen or require ever-more human preferences. The finding that reward modeling ability improves as a byproduct of instruction following improvement (without additional evaluation training data) suggests a virtuous circle that could enable continual self-improvement. However, the paper acknowledges that gains saturate (diminishing returns across iterations), that response length increases may confound quality improvements, and that safety is not explicitly constrained in the reward signal. Open questions include scaling laws across model sizes, whether reward hacking can occur within the self-rewarding loop, and whether the approach generalizes to reasoning-heavy tasks where gains were minimal.
