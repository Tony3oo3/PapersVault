## Problem

Existing label recovery attacks in federated learning rely on assumptions that fail in realistic FL deployments: they assume no repeated labels per batch, require non-negative activation functions, work only on randomly initialized (untrained) models, consider only single-epoch local training, and apply exclusively to FedAvg where the server receives raw gradients rather than model updates. This paper addresses all five limitations simultaneously, enabling label recovery under multiple local epochs, heterogeneous data, various optimizers, and non-FedAvg schemes.

## Input / Output

The server consumes the local model updates of the output layer (bias Δb and weights ΔW) from each client, along with a small auxiliary dataset (as few as 5–100 labeled samples per class) used to Monte Carlo estimate the output logit distribution. The output is the count of samples per class in the client's training batch (a label distribution vector summing to the batch size), from which instance-level labels can be assigned. The method operates on model updates rather than raw gradients, making it applicable to standard FL protocols where only parameter differences are transmitted.

## Method

RLU exploits the analytical correlation between the expected local update of the output layer bias and the number of samples of each label in the training batch. For a single epoch, this yields a constrained least-squares problem (Eq. 10) whose coefficient matrix is built from 'erroneous confidence' values S_{n,j}—the expected softmax probability assigned to the wrong class—estimated via Monte Carlo sampling from a fitted multivariate normal on the output logits. For multiple local epochs, the method recursively simulates the evolution of the logit distribution across epochs by updating the mean vector μ using the relationship between bias updates and logit changes (Eq. 12), with the average embedded signal approximated from the ratio ΔW/Δb. A posterior search over initial label-count guesses (T=5 iterations) refines the estimate by matching the simulated final-epoch confidence to the true value computed from the collected local model. The framework generalizes to non-FedAvg schemes (FedProx, FedDyn, Scaffold, FedDC) and non-SGD optimizers (SGDm, NAG) by incorporating scheme-specific decay coefficients ρ^(τ) and historical update terms h_j^(t) into the expectation formula.

## Results

On untrained models with a single local epoch (m=1), RLU achieves near-perfect label recovery (1.000 cAcc and iAcc) across all four datasets (SVHN, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet), all three architectures (LeNet-5, VGG-16, ResNet-50), and all activation functions tested. With multiple local epochs (m=10), RLU maintains at least 84% cAcc and 90% iAcc in every setting, while the closest competitor iRLG drops below 70% iAcc on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. RLU also demonstrates robustness to data heterogeneity, sustaining ≥93% iAcc across Dirichlet concentration parameters α from 0.05 to 5 on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLU outperforms all three label-recovery baselines (LLG+, ZLG+, iRLG) in every experimental setting tested. Against LLG+ (Wainakh et al., 2021), RLU is consistently superior; LLG+ requires non-negative activations and collapses with Tanh (iAcc drops to 0.063 on SVHN) and SELU (iAcc drops to 0.014 on CIFAR-100), while RLU remains at or near 1.000. Against ZLG+ (Geng et al., 2021), RLU wins by a wide margin, particularly on Tiny-ImageNet where ZLG+ achieves only 53–67% iAcc versus RLU's 100%. Against iRLG (Ma et al., 2023), the gap is smallest for m=1 (iRLG reaches 99–100% iAcc) but widens dramatically for m=10: iRLG drops to 65–72% iAcc on CIFAR-10 and 69–90% on CIFAR-100, while RLU sustains 90–98% iAcc. For gradient inversion quality (Fig. 3), using RLU-recovered labels to fix the label variable and optimize only images yields higher PSNR and lower LPIPS than the joint optimization approach of IG (Geiping et al., 2020), confirming that accurate label recovery directly improves data reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the analytical label-recovery line of work initiated by iDLG and GradInversion, which exploit the correlation between output-layer gradient magnitudes and ground-truth labels. The key departure is from iRLG (Ma et al., 2023), which handles repeated labels but only on randomly initialized models; RLU introduces a Monte Carlo simulation of the 'erroneous confidence' distribution evolving across multiple local epochs to recover labels from well-trained models. Additionally, the paper generalizes the analysis from FedAvg+SGD to a unified framework covering FedProx, FedDyn, Scaffold, FedDC, and momentum-based optimizers (SGDm, NAG), which prior label-recovery methods have not addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results demonstrate that label recovery remains highly effective (≥90% iAcc) in realistic FL deployments with heterogeneous data, multiple local epochs, and non-standard FL schemes, raising concrete privacy concerns for practitioners. The finding that baselines like iRLG collapse to ~11% iAcc under SGDm with γ=0.9 while RLU maintains 92% highlights that prior security analyses were overly narrow. Open questions include whether stronger defenses (beyond DP noise, which still leaves 40–62% iAcc at σ=0.5 in multi-epoch settings) can close the gap, and whether the approach extends to non-classification tasks or generative FL models.
