## Problem

NAS performance is fundamentally bounded by the quality and coverage of its search space, yet existing search spaces are hand-crafted, task-specific, and limited in scale. Previous automated search-space design methods still require carefully chosen initial spaces, iterative fitness evaluation of subspaces, or starting from known good architectures, all of which introduce bias and additional cost. This paper tackles the challenge of scaling the initial search space to an unprecedented ~10^390 configurations and navigating it efficiently without repeated feedback loops.

## Input / Output

The method takes a conditioning token y encoding architectural constraints (FLOPs budget and parameter count) and produces a complete neural network architecture as a sequence of up to 20 cells, where each cell is a directed graph with up to 6 operation nodes drawn from 28 operations (9 types × 3 kernel sizes + skip connection), plus a channel count from {8,…,1024} and a stride from {1,2}. The micro-level graph designs are abstracted into K cluster families via GMM, so the macro generator only selects a family per cell while the CCNF resolves the specific graph within that family. The entire pipeline is pre-trained in a task-agnostic manner using zero-cost proxy vectors, so deployment to a new task requires only a few hundred ZC evaluations.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-level generative hierarchy that decomposes architecture generation into micro-cell design and macro-structure composition. At the micro level, a Graph VAE regularized with triplet margin loss and an auxiliary ZC-vector predictor encodes cell graphs into a 256-d latent space where L1 distance approximates ZC-similarity (computed from 4 proxies: NASWOT, SNIP-SSNR, params, FLOPs); a GMM then partitions this space into K clusters of functionally similar cells. A Conditional Continuous Normalizing Flow (CCNF) trained with the adjoint method learns to sample 'synonymous' designs within a cluster conditioned on the cluster's ZC mean, outperforming naive neighbourhood sampling. At the macro level, a decoder-only transformer (fine-tuned GPT-Neo-125M) is trained on the history of a two-phase evolutionary search (HL-EVO) that explores cell-family assignments in the exploration phase and mutates channels/strides in the exploitation phase, enabling the SG to generate full architectures autoregressively from a conditioning token in under a minute of compute.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k, the proposed SG+CCNF method achieves 78.3% top-1 accuracy at 450M FLOPs (4.3M params), 79.5% at 600M FLOPs (7.0M params), and 80.6% at 1000M FLOPs (8.2M params), all with only 0.08 GPU hours of search cost after a one-time 30 GPU-hour pretraining. On CIFAR-10/100 with 1M parameter constraints, the method reaches 97.6% and 81.0% accuracy respectively, outperforming all zero-cost NAS baselines. On NAS-Bench-360, the method improves upon the WRN reference in 6/8 tasks and surpasses DASH in 4/8 tasks without any task-specific feedback or supernet training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest zero-cost evolutionary baselines, the method achieves marginal but consistent accuracy gains: 78.3% vs. ZiCo's 78.1% at 450M FLOPs, 79.5% vs. 79.4% at 600M, and 80.6% vs. 80.5% at 1000M, while reducing search cost from 9.6 to 0.08 GPU hours (120× cheaper). It also outperforms ZenNAS (78.0%), TE-NAS (75.5%), Zero-Cost-PT (76.4%), NSENet (77.3%), and AutoSpace (77.5%) by 0.8–2.8 percentage points. The only methods achieving higher accuracy are OFA (79.1%, requiring 1200 GPU hours of supernet pretraining) and GPT NAS (79.1%, with a model 33× larger in parameters that likely violates the FLOPs constraint). On NAS-Bench-360, the method beats the WRN reference in 6/8 tasks and DASH in 4/8 tasks, despite DASH performing supernet training and HPO per task while the proposed method uses no task-specific feedback. The controlled CIFAR comparison (Table 1) confirms that simply scaling the search space without the generative hierarchy degrades performance (Evo(T-CET) drops from 97.2% on ZenNet to 96.4% on GraphNet), validating the necessity of the G-VAE + CCNF + SG pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of zero-cost NAS (ZenNAS, ZiCo, T-CET), flow-based generative modeling (FFJORD/CCNF, neural ODEs), and graph representation learning (G-VAE). It departs from prior automated search-space design methods (AutoSpace, NSENet) that iteratively evaluate subspace fitness by instead using a task-agnostic hierarchical generative model—G-VAE for micro-cell encoding, CCNF for conditional synonym generation, and a fine-tuned GPT-Neo-125M for macro-architecture sequencing—to navigate a ~10^390 search space without repeated model evaluation. The key conceptual departure is treating ZC proxies not as scalar ranking scores but as a multi-dimensional metric space for clustering, enabling the generative hierarchy to operate in a structured latent geometry rather than a flat combinatorial space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic search-cost reduction (0.08 GPU hours vs. 12–19 for strong baselines) makes high-quality NAS practically accessible for rapid prototyping and multi-task deployment, since the generative models are trained once and amortized across tasks. However, the method's heavy reliance on ZC proxies for organizing the search space remains a vulnerability, as these proxies are known to be unfaithful in some regimes, and the approach does not yet handle transformer/attention-based architectures or device-specific latency constraints. Open questions include whether the clustering-based (rather than ranking-based) use of ZC metrics generalizes to tasks where proxy accuracy is poor, and whether the hierarchical generative paradigm can be extended to hybrid convolution-attention search spaces.
