## Problem

Large Language Models demonstrate that a simple autoregressive objective combined with Transformers yields predictable, monotonic scaling in both model capacity and data volume. Whether this scaling behavior transfers to visual feature learning—where prior autoregressive approaches (iGPT, PixelCNN) lacked the clean scaling laws and required architecture-specific adaptations—remained an open question. This paper asks whether a vanilla autoregressive pre-training recipe, without the stability-inducing tricks (LayerScale, stochastic depth, QK-Norm) that supervised and contrastive vision methods require, can scale to billions of parameters and images with consistent downstream gains.

## Input / Output

During pre-training, each RGB image is split into non-overlapping 14×14 patches arranged in raster (row-major) order, forming a fixed-length token sequence; the model predicts the next patch as a normalized pixel vector via MSE regression. For downstream classification, patch features are extracted from an intermediate layer (roughly two-thirds of model depth, where semantic content peaks) and aggregated into a single global descriptor via multi-head attention pooling before a linear classifier. No text or captions are required at any stage, and the frozen trunk produces per-patch features that can also be used directly for dense prediction tasks.

## Method

AIM uses a Vision Transformer backbone with two key architectural modifications: (1) prefix attention, where a randomly sampled prefix of patches uses bidirectional attention while the remaining patches use causal attention, enabling the causal mask to be dropped entirely during downstream adaptation; and (2) a heavily parameterized 12-block MLP prediction head that processes each patch independently, preventing the trunk from over-specializing in pixel-level reconstruction. The training objective is standard next-patch prediction with per-patch normalized pixel MSE loss, and the model is scaled from 0.6B to 7B parameters (prioritizing width over depth, following LLM practice) on 2B uncurated web images (DFN-2B+) using a single set of hyperparameters across all model sizes. The paper demonstrates that the pre-training loss on a validation set directly correlates with frozen-feature downstream accuracy, and that no saturation is observed even at 7B parameters and 5B images seen, establishing a compute-driven scaling law analogous to LLMs.

## Results

AIM-7B (6.5B parameters) pre-trained on DFN-2B+ achieves 84.0% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1k with a frozen trunk (using features from the 20th layer) and 79.6% average attentive probe accuracy across 15 diverse image recognition benchmarks. The model exhibits strong scaling behavior: AIM-0.6B reaches 75.6% average, AIM-1B reaches 76.9%, AIM-3B reaches 77.6%, and AIM-7B reaches 77.8% (last layer) / 79.6% (best layer). No sign of performance saturation is observed even at 7B parameters and 5B images of pre-training, suggesting further gains are possible with larger models and longer schedules.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AIM outperforms all generative baselines: AIM-0.6B (75.6% avg) beats MAE-H (75.3% avg) at equivalent capacity, and AIM-3B/7B (77.6%/77.8% avg) surpass MAE-2B (77.4% avg) which was trained on 3B Instagram images. Against BEiT (65.4% avg), AIM achieves a large margin of over 10 points on average. Among joint-embedding methods, AIM-7B† (79.6% avg) outperforms DINO (75.0% avg) and iBOT (77.6% avg) by 4.6 and 2.0 points respectively. However, AIM falls behind DINOv2 (81.9% avg, 86.4% IN-1k), which benefits from higher-resolution inputs (518px vs. 224px) and a more complex training recipe involving multi-crop augmentation, KoLeo regularization, and LayerScale. The paper notes that AIM's pre-training is significantly simpler and scales more gracefully in both parameters and data without requiring such stability-inducing techniques.

## Relation to Prior Work

AIM extends the autoregressive image modeling line from iGPT (Chen et al., 2020a) and PixelCNN (Van den Oord et al., 2016) by combining a Vision Transformer backbone with modern LLM training practices (width-first scaling, cosine LR schedules, bfloat16) and large-scale uncurated web data (DataComp/DFN-2B). Two key architectural departures distinguish it from prior autoregressive vision work: prefix attention (borrowed from T5) to enable bidirectional inference downstream, and a heavily parameterized MLP prediction head inspired by contrastive learning to prevent trunk over-specialization. Unlike concurrent generative approaches (MAE, BEiT) or joint-embedding methods (DINO, iBOT, DINOv2), AIM requires no stability-inducing mechanisms (LayerScale, stochastic depth, QK-Norm, teacher-student momentum) and no multi-crop augmentation, making its training pipeline essentially identical to LLM pre-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that autoregressive pre-training for vision exhibits clean, compute-optimal scaling laws analogous to LLMs, with no sign of saturation at 7B parameters and 5B images seen—suggesting a viable path to much larger vision models. The practical simplicity (vanilla ViT + causal/prefix attention + pixel regression, no tricks) lowers the barrier to scaling vision pre-training compared to contrastive methods that require intricate hyperparameter tuning. Open questions include whether the scaling continues indefinitely, the compute-optimal allocation between model size and training duration, and whether text supervision (which AIM does not use) could further improve features. A noted limitation is that contrastive methods like DINOv2 still produce stronger features at equivalent model capacity, and MAE remains more sample-efficient on small datasets.
