## Problem

Online resource allocation under non-stationary customer arrivals with unknown click-through rates has not been addressed in a unified framework. Prior CBwK methods either assume stationary (i.i.d.) arrivals or known CTRs, and adversarial BwK results provide only competitive-ratio guarantees without sublinear regret. This paper asks whether a single algorithm can simultaneously achieve sublinear regret under near-stationary arrivals and an optimal constant competitive ratio under fully adversarial arrivals, while learning CTRs online.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives n resource capacities c_i, per-resource revenues r_i, a time horizon T, per-customer-type total arrival rates λ_l (provided as advice, estimated from historical data with ~0.9% error), and a finite parameter space Θ initialized from 500 historical observations. At each period t it observes a customer feature vector x^t ∈ R^d and must irrevocably assign one resource or reject. The output is a per-period allocation decision and the resulting cumulative revenue; the algorithm does not require knowing whether the arrival sequence is stochastic or adversarial, adapting online via internal switch conditions.

## Method

ULwE is a unified online algorithm within the CBwK framework that dynamically switches between two sub-policies: ALG_LP, which solves a linear program at each step using optimistic (max-over-confidence-set) purchase probability estimates to maximize expected revenue subject to capacity constraints, and ALG_ADV, a greedy policy that selects the resource maximizing a discounted revenue r_i·(1−Ψ(N_i/c_i)) where Ψ(x)=(e^x−1)/(e−1) penalizes over-consumption. A UCB-style confidence set Ω_i^t ⊆ Θ_i is maintained per resource, with parameters removed when accumulated prediction errors exceed √(t·log(2t/β)) thresholds. Switching from LP to ADV is triggered when either the estimated regret growth or cumulative resource consumption violates prescribed bounds, ensuring the algorithm degrades gracefully from sublinear regret O~(√(n|Θ|T)) under near-i.i.d. arrivals to a constant competitive ratio (1+1/(1−1/e)) under adversarial arrivals without prior knowledge of the arrival regime.

## Results

The paper evaluates ULwE against ALG_LP and ALG_ADV on simulated online resource allocation with 2 resources, 2 customer types, T=500 periods, and 100 independent runs. Under near-i.i.d. arrivals, ULwE and ALG_LP achieve similar low cumulative regret at T=500, while ALG_ADV incurs the highest regret due to its greedy nature degrading as resources deplete. Under adversarial arrivals, ALG_ADV outperforms ALG_LP, and ULwE adaptively switches from LP-based to ADV-based policy around 1/3 T, combining the strengths of both. The theoretical guarantee is a regret bound of Õ(√(n|Θ|T)) under near-i.i.d. arrivals and a constant competitive ratio (approaching 1−1/(2−1/e) ≈ 0.538 as min c_i → ∞) under general non-stationary arrivals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ULwE against two internal baselines: ALG_LP (an LP-based UCB algorithm for stationary arrivals) and ALG_ADV (a greedy algorithm with discounted revenue for adversarial arrivals). Under i.i.d. arrivals, ULwE matches ALG_LP in regret (both significantly lower than ALG_ADV), confirming that the LP-based strategy is optimal in stable environments. Under adversarial arrivals, ALG_ADV outperforms ALG_LP, and ULwE correctly detects the non-stationarity and switches to the ADV protocol around 1/3 T, achieving regret comparable to ALG_ADV after the switch. Under general (mixed) arrival patterns, ULwE demonstrates the most robust performance across varying levels of non-stationarity, while ALG_LP degrades as arrival rates fluctuate and ALG_ADV, though consistent, does not exploit the structure present in near-stationary phases. The key advantage of ULwE is its ability to seamlessly transition between strategies without prior knowledge of the arrival pattern, achieving the 'best-of-both-worlds' guarantee.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate strands of online resource allocation: the stochastic CBwK literature (Badanidiyuru et al. 2014; Agrawal & Devanur 2015) that achieves sublinear regret under i.i.d. arrivals, and the adversarial BwK / inventory-balancing literature (Immorlica et al. 2019; Cheung et al. 2022) that provides constant competitive ratios under adversarial arrivals. The ULwE algorithm introduces a switching mechanism between an LP-based UCB policy (for near-stationary regimes) and a greedy inventory-balancing policy (for adversarial regimes), drawing on the best-of-both-worlds paradigm established for multi-armed bandits by Wei & Luo (2018) and Zimmert & Seldin (2019/2021), but extending it to the resource-constrained, contextual setting with unknown click-through rates. The inventory-balancing penalty function follows Golrezaei et al. (2014), and the CR analysis for the adversarial branch is inherited directly from Cheung et al. (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main contribution is a clean 'best-of-both-worlds' guarantee: sublinear regret Õ(√(n|Θ|T)) when arrivals are near-i.i.d., and a tight constant competitive ratio under fully adversarial arrivals, achieved by a single adaptive algorithm without knowing in advance which regime applies. This is practically relevant for online advertising and revenue management where arrival patterns can shift unpredictably (e.g., Black Friday). Key limitations include the requirement to know total arrival rates λ_l a priori, the dependence of the regret bound on the parameter-space cardinality |Θ|, and the per-step LP solve that limits computational scalability. The authors note future work on LP-free and more computationally efficient variants, and the analysis assumes a finite discrete parameter space, leaving the continuous-parameter case open.
