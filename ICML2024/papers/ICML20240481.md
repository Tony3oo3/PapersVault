## Problem

Prior generalization bounds for heavy-tailed SDEs either provided only expected (not high-probability) guarantees or relied on non-computable mutual information terms between trajectories and the dataset. Additionally, existing bounds (e.g., Raj et al., 2023b) had a strong dependence on the parameter dimension d, rendering them vacuous in overparameterized settings. This paper seeks high-probability, computable generalization bounds for heavy-tailed SDEs with improved dimension dependence.

## Input / Output

The method takes a training dataset S of n samples, a differentiable surrogate loss f, a tail-index α ∈ (1,2), noise amplitudes σ₁ (Lévy) and σ₂ (Brownian), and an ℓ₂-regularization coefficient η. It produces the SDE solution W_T^S at a fixed horizon T as the learned parameters, along with a high-probability upper bound on the generalization gap E_U[L(W_T^S) − L̂_S(W_T^S)]. The bound is expressed in terms of the time-integrated expected squared gradient norm I(T,S) = ∫₀ᵀ E_U‖∇F̂_S(W_t^S)‖² dt, making it computable from training trajectories. For experiments, the continuous SDE is discretized via Euler-Maruyama with step size γ over N iterations.

## Method

The core technique combines PAC-Bayesian analysis with entropy flow estimates for the fractional Fokker-Planck equation (FPE) governing the SDE's density. A prior π is defined as the stationary density of the Lévy-driven Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, and the KL divergence between the posterior (law of W_T^S) and π is bounded by integrating the time derivative of a Φ-entropy. The key decomposition (Lemma 4.1) splits this entropy flow into a Φ-information term (from Brownian noise), a Bregman integral (from Lévy noise), and a gradient coupling term. In the purely heavy-tailed case (σ₂=0), the Bregman integral is shown to admit an integral representation B_Φ^α(v) = C_{α,d} ∫₀^∞ J_{Φ,v}(r) dr/r^{α−1}, which approximates the Φ-information near r=0 under a uniform regularity assumption (Assumption 4.3), enabling the same compensation argument. The resulting constant K_{α,d} = (2−α)Γ(1−α/2)dΓ(d/2)/(α·2^α·Γ((d+α)/2)·R^{2−α}) reveals a phase transition: heavy tails improve generalization when σ₁√(d/R²) < 1 and degrade it when σ₁√(d/R²) > 1.

## Results

The paper's empirical validation (FCN2 on 10% subsampled MNIST, α ∈ [1.6, 2], 10 seeds) confirms the predicted phase transition: the Kendall's τ correlation between the tail-index α and the accuracy gap flips from positive (heavy regime, heavier tails help) to negative (light regime, heavier tails hurt) at a dimension d ≈ 8×10⁴. The computable bound from Theorem 4.4 (Eq. 18) shows strong correlation with the actual accuracy gap when the unknown constant R is set in the range [2.8, 7], and near-perfect correlation at R = 15. A regression-based estimation of α from the accuracy error's dimension dependence (log G_S vs. log d) recovers the ground-truth tail-index with strong monotonic correlation, though it slightly underestimates α by a constant offset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against competing optimization methods empirically; instead, it benchmarks its theoretical bounds against prior generalization results. Compared to Raj et al. (2023b), the proposed bounds are high-probability (vs. expected-only), do not require a Lipschitz assumption on the loss, and have a strictly better dimension dependence: K_{α,d} scales as d^{1−α/2} (→ 1 as α→2⁻) versus R_{α,d} = O(d^{(1+α)/2}) in Raj et al., which becomes vacuous in overparameterized regimes (d > n). In the limit α→2⁻, the constant K_{α,d} → 1/2, recovering the known dimension-free behavior of Langevin dynamics bounds (Mou et al., 2017; Pensia et al., 2018), whereas Raj et al.'s bound does not. The main acknowledged drawback is that the proposed bounds grow with the time horizon T (no exponential decay in I(T,S)), while Raj et al.'s bound is time-independent; the authors address this partially via Theorem 4.5 (time-uniform but less interpretable integral) and leave full resolution as future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PAC-Bayesian + entropy-flow framework of Mou et al. (2017) for Langevin dynamics to the heavy-tailed setting by replacing the classical Fokker-Planck equation with the fractional Fokker-Planck equation, leveraging entropy-flow decompositions from Gentil & Imbert (2008) and steady-state properties from Tristani (2013). It departs from Raj et al. (2023b), which provided only expected (not high-probability) bounds with a worse dimension dependence (d^((1+α)/2) vs. d^(1-α/2)), and from earlier heavy-tailed generalization work (Simsekli et al., 2021; Dupuis et al., 2023) that relied on non-computable mutual information terms. The key technical novelty is identifying a Bregman integral structure in the fractional FPE that plays the role of Fisher information in the Brownian case, enabling a unified proof technique across both noise regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide high-probability, computable generalization bounds for heavy-tailed SDEs, filling a gap between the expected bounds of Raj et al. (2023b) and the non-computable information-theoretic bounds of earlier work. The predicted phase transition—where heavy tails are beneficial when σ₁√d₀ < 1 and harmful when σ₁√d₀ > 1—offers a concrete, testable criterion for when heavy-tailed optimization should be preferred, with partial empirical validation on MNIST. Key limitations include the time-dependence of the main bound (I(T,S) grows with T), the unknown constant R in Assumption 4.3, and the restriction to full-batch settings; open questions include deriving time-uniform versions of Theorem 4.4 and understanding the interaction between small-batch SGD noise and explicit Lévy noise.
