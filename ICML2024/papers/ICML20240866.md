## Problem

On-policy actor-critic methods rely on state-agnostic exploration (entropy regularization or homogeneous action noise), which promotes equal exploration regardless of how much the agent has already learned about a given state. The paper asks what minimal modification to A3C can introduce adaptive, state-aware exploration via approximate Bayesian inference over policy parameters, while preserving the policy-gradient objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240866/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The actor network takes a state vector (continuous MuJoCo observations) or a preprocessed image observation (ProcGen) and outputs a mean vector for a Gaussian policy (continuous actions) or a categorical distribution (discrete actions). The critic takes the same state input and outputs a scalar value estimate. Unusually, at decision time the actor parameters are sampled from an approximate posterior via a random dropout mask (Thompson sampling), so the same nominal weights produce different policies across episodes, and the advantage estimates are clipped to non-negative values before scaling the log-likelihood gradient.

## Method

VSOP introduces three modifications to A3C: (1) passing GAE advantage estimates through a ReLU so only positive advantages drive policy updates, (2) spectral normalization of actor and critic weights to bound the Lipschitz constant of the value function, and (3) dropout as a Bayesian approximation over both actor and critic parameters, enabling Thompson sampling for exploration. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) proves that gradient ascent with clipped advantages maximizes a lower bound on the state value function plus an additive term C_π(s) that is bounded proportionally to the Lipschitz constant of the value function, grounding spectral normalization as a regularizer on that term. The ReLU clipping is justified by introducing a normal-gamma distribution over actions whose precision is modulated by the clipped advantage, recovering the standard A3C gradient form while making the log-likelihood objective amenable to dropout-based variational inference. The resulting exploration is adaptive: states with little evidence of good actions yield high policy variance (more exploration), while well-visited states with positive advantages concentrate the posterior (less exploration).

## Results

On the MuJoCo continuous control benchmark (10 random seeds, last 100 episodes), VSOP achieves significant improvements over A3C, PPO, SAC, and TD3 on both the robust normalized Median and Interquartile Mean (IQM) metrics, while trailing only SAC and TD3 on the Mean metric. On the ProcGen generalization benchmark (5 random seeds), VSOP shows broad significant improvement over PPO across both PPO-normalized and Min-Max-normalized scores, and improves over EDE (Jiang et al., 2023) on IQM, mean, and optimality gap. Ablation studies confirm that no single mechanism (positive advantages, single-action setting, spectral normalization, Thompson sampling) contributes more than the sum of all changes, with positive advantages and the single-action regime having the largest individual impact.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VSOP outperforms PPO on MuJoCo for both Median and IQM metrics, and on ProcGen across all reported metrics (PPO-normalized and Min-Max-normalized), demonstrating improved generalization under distribution shift. Against A3C, VSOP shows significant gains on MuJoCo in both single-threaded and massively parallel (2048-thread) settings, though it trails PPO in the extreme parallelization regime. On MuJoCo, VSOP beats SAC and TD3 on Median and IQM but falls behind on the Mean metric, suggesting VSOP is more robust (less sensitive to outlier seeds) while SAC/TD3 achieve higher peak performance. Compared to RMPG (adapted for continuous control), VSOP provides the missing theoretical grounding and extends from the all-actions to the single-action formulation, yielding better continuous control results. On ProcGen, VSOP improves over EDE (Jiang et al., 2023) on IQM, mean, and optimality gap, supporting the hypothesis that adaptive exploration contributes positively to generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

VSOP is positioned as a minimal modification of A3C that introduces approximate Bayesian inference into on-policy actor-critic RL through three changes: ReLU on advantages, spectral normalization, and dropout. It provides the missing theoretical grounding for RMPG (Srinivasan et al., 2018), which also clips advantages but operates in an all-actions formulation without a value lower-bound guarantee, and extends RMPG to the single-action (sampled) setting more suitable for continuous control. The Bayesian framing via a normal-gamma distribution over actions recovers the A3C gradient estimator while enabling Thompson sampling for exploration, explicitly distinguishing itself from the maximum entropy RL paradigm (Levine, 2018) which also connects to policy gradients but optimizes a fundamentally different objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a coherent theoretical story linking three practical tricks (advantage clipping, spectral normalization, dropout) to a unified Bayesian objective, which could guide future design of on-policy methods. However, the conservative vector field assumption is often violated by ReLU activations in practice, and spectral normalization is found to be detrimental in massively parallel settings (2048 threads), limiting the method's scalability. Open questions include establishing convergence rates, extending theory to asynchronous and discrete-action settings, and whether the observed gains translate to large-scale deployments like RLHF or robotics where PPO currently dominates.
