## Problem

O-INFORMATION is a scalable, interpretable measure of the synergy-redundancy balance in multivariate systems, but existing estimation methods require either discrete distributions or Gaussian assumptions, making them inapplicable to most real-world continuous data. No prior method could reliably estimate O-INFORMATION and its per-variable gradients for general continuous distributions without restrictive modeling assumptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240178/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw multivariate data samples (N continuous random variables drawn from an arbitrary joint distribution) with no binning, discretization, or parametric distributional assumption required. It outputs a single O-INFORMATION scalar (positive for redundancy-dominated, negative for synergy-dominated systems), the constituent Total Correlation and Dual Total Correlation scalars, and optionally per-variable O-INFORMATION gradients that attribute each variable's contribution to the global synergy-redundancy balance. In the neural application, input consists of spike-count time bins (e.g., 25-dimensional vectors per brain region per 50 ms window) across multiple cortical areas.

## Method

The key insight is that all information-theoretic quantities needed for O-INFORMATION (TC, DTC, S-INFORMATION) can be expressed as KL divergences, and Proposition 1 shows that a KL divergence between two distributions equals an integral of the squared norm of the difference of their time-varying score functions under a diffusion noising process. SΩI learns a single amortized denoising score network (an MLP with skip connections, or optionally a transformer) that simultaneously approximates three types of score functions—joint E[X|X_t], conditional E[X^i|X_t^i, X^\i], and marginal E[X^i|X_t^i]—by parameterizing which variables are noised versus kept clean via a per-variable time vector τ. Training uses denoising score matching under the VP-SDE framework with importance sampling, and inference estimates TC and DTC via Monte Carlo integration over the diffusion time t, yielding O-INFORMATION as their difference. This amortized single-network design avoids the cumulative bias and O(N²) model count that plague pairwise MI-based baselines.

## Results

On synthetic benchmarks with N=10 variables across dimensions 5–20, SΩI maintains stable O-INFORMATION estimation accuracy (matching ground truth) across all three interaction regimes (redundancy, synergy, mixed), while all pairwise-MI baselines degrade substantially as variable dimension increases. On the synergy-dominant benchmark, baselines fail to capture the high Dual Total Correlation values, whereas SΩI correctly reports negative O-INFORMATION. On the real-world Visual Behavior Neuropixels dataset (80 mice, 6 visual cortex regions), SΩI estimates higher O-INFORMATION for 'change' stimuli than 'no change' stimuli across all 50 ms time bins, confirming redundancy-dominant information flow during novel scene detection and matching conclusions from prior PID-based analyses (Venkatesh et al., 2023) without requiring an artificial source–target partition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SΩI is compared against four pairwise neural MI estimators (MINE, NWJ, INFONCE, CLUB) used within the linear-decomposition framework of Bai et al. (2023), which requires training 2(N−1) separate models to reconstruct TC and DTC. All four baselines produce reasonable O-INFORMATION estimates at low variable dimensions (Dim ≤ 5) but degrade sharply as dimension grows to 15–20, particularly on synergy-dominant systems where high-order interactions dominate. An additional score-based baseline, Line-MINDE (Franzese et al., 2024), which also uses denoising score functions but still decomposes O-INFORMATION into individual MI terms, outperforms the four pairwise baselines yet remains inferior to SΩI, confirming that the key advantage of SΩI lies in its amortized single-network design and direct estimation of TC/DTC rather than summing many MI estimates. On the mixed benchmark, SΩI correctly transitions from negative to positive O-INFORMATION as redundancy strength increases, while baselines produce biased or divergent estimates. On the neural application, SΩI replicates the qualitative findings of Venkatesh et al. (2023) (who relied on Gaussian PID) without the restrictive Gaussian assumption or the need to partition brain regions into sources and a target.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the score-based KL divergence estimation framework from MINDE (Franzese et al., 2024) and Kong et al. (2024) to the computation of O-INFORMATION, a multivariate information measure introduced by Rosas et al. (2019) that had previously been estimable only under discrete or Gaussian distributional assumptions. The multi-variate denoising score network that amortizes joint, conditional, and marginal score learning builds directly on Bounoua et al. (2024)'s multi-modal latent diffusion framework, while the VP-SDE noising process follows Song et al. (2021). Conceptually, the paper positions itself as the first method to lift the distributional restrictions on O-INFORMATION estimation, filling a gap between the theoretical elegance of O-INFORMATION (which scales gracefully with variable count, unlike PID) and its practical inaccessibility for general continuous data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the Gaussian/discrete restriction, SΩI makes O-INFORMATION applicable to real-world multivariate systems (demonstrated on mouse visual cortex data) where such assumptions are invalid, and it scales to more variables than PID-based approaches. The amortized single-network design avoids the cumulative bias and O(N²) training cost of decomposing into pairwise MI terms, which is a practical advantage for systems with many variables. Open questions include whether the residual bias in gradient estimation (noted even with the transformer variant in Appendix D) can be fully resolved, whether the method handles very high-dimensional or non-stationary data, and how the Monte Carlo integration variance behaves as system complexity grows beyond the N=10 regime tested here.
