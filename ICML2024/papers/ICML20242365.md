## Problem

Existing linear attention methods, primarily designed for encoder-based LLMs like BERT, suffer significant accuracy drops when applied to autoregressive decoder-based LLMs because they disrupt the temporal dependencies critical for next-token prediction. Additionally, direct integration of linear attention with speculative decoding fails due to temporal misalignment: the tree-based attention in speculative decoding introduces multiple candidate tokens per time step, which standard linear attention and convolutional augmentations cannot correctly handle.

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained LLM weights (LLaMA-2-7B/13B, GPT-2, T5, FLASH-110M) and text token sequences, replacing the softmax attention module with an augmented linear attention that operates on the same Q/K/V projections. During speculative decoding, the input additionally includes multiple candidate tokens per time step arranged in a tree structure, which the unfolded convolution kernels process while respecting the tree-based visibility mask. Outputs are generated text tokens, GLUE classification accuracy, and perplexity on PG-19/Wiki40B, with the method extending maximum supported sequence length from 8K to 32K tokens on a single A100-80G GPU.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-branch augmented linear attention: (1) parallel local softmax attention within non-overlapping groups of tokens, (2) grouped linear attention that computes cumulative KV sums only at group boundaries to bypass sequential dependencies, and (3) a causally-masked depthwise convolution (DWConv) in the value branch to capture local feature dependencies. The causal mask on DWConv is the critical fix—standard convolutions leak future token information during autoregressive training, causing the model to converge to zero loss but zero evaluation accuracy. For speculative decoding compatibility, the DWConv is unfolded into matrix multiplication (analogous to img2col), enabling tree-based attention masks to be integrated with convolution kernels so each token accesses only its verified predecessor rather than unchosen siblings at the same time step. This design also ensures identical prefixes across speculative candidates are computed only once, reducing QKV memory by 75% and total latency by 64% compared to direct FLASH+Medusa integration, while achieving up to 6.67 perplexity reduction and 2× generation speedup.

## Results

On PG-19 (sequence length 4K), the augmented linear attention achieves a perplexity of 13.47 on LLaMA-2-7B and 11.55 on LLaMA-2-13B, representing a 6.67/6.33 reduction over the local-only linear attention baseline (21.61/19.25). On Wiki40B, the FLASH-110M model with augmented LA reaches 15.16 perplexity, a 0.24 improvement over the original softmax attention (15.40). Integrating the augmented LA with speculative decoding (Medusa) yields up to 2× generation speedup (e.g., 50.7 vs 25.9 tokens/s for LLaMA-7B) and a 64% total latency reduction (49.7ms vs 137.2ms) compared to direct FLASH+Medusa integration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The augmented LA consistently outperforms all seven existing linear attention baselines (FLASH-Local, FLASH-Global, Linformer, Performer, TransNormer, YOSO, ReLU) across encoder-based (BERT), decoder-based (GPT-2), and encoder-decoder (T5) architectures on GLUE, with an average accuracy gain of 1.87 points over the strongest LA baseline (FLASH-Local). On LLaMA-2-7B downstream tasks (BBH, PIQA, MMLU, COPA, ARCC, AGNews), Aug. FLASH achieves an average 3.53% accuracy improvement over FLASH-Local and 1.53% over FLASH-Global at the same 0.5s latency budget. Against the original softmax attention, the augmented LA on FLASH-110M actually reduces perplexity by 0.24 (15.16 vs 15.40 on Wiki40B), while on LLaMA-2-7B it extends the maximum supported sequence length from 8K to 32K with 56% lower latency. In the speculative decoding setting, the proposed unfolded DWConv + grouped LA design reduces total latency by 64% (49.7ms vs 137.2ms) and QKV memory by 75% (0.1GB vs 0.4GB) compared to direct FLASH+Medusa integration, while achieving ~2× throughput over non-speculative linear attention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-studied but previously disconnected lines of work: kernel-based linear attention (FLASH, Performer, Linformer, YOSO, TransNormer) and speculative decoding (Medusa, SpecInfer). It departs from prior linear attention research, which was predominantly designed and validated for encoder-based models (BERT, ViT), by conducting the first systematic evaluation of these methods on autoregressive decoder-based LLMs and identifying a fundamental incompatibility: standard local augmentation via depthwise convolution causes information leakage in causal settings. The proposed masked DWConv and unfolded convolution for tree-based attention represent a targeted fix that bridges the gap between linear attention's efficiency and speculative decoding's parallelism, building on the grouped attention idea from FLASH and the DWConv augmentation from vision transformer acceleration work (ViTCoD, Castling-ViT).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: extending maximum supported sequence length from 8K to 32K on a single A100-80G GPU, reducing inference latency by up to 56%, and achieving ~2× generation speedup when combined with speculative decoding. However, the evidence for the largest models (LLaMA-2-7B/13B) is limited to 1K-step fine-tuning rather than full pre-training, leaving open whether the perplexity gains and downstream accuracy improvements hold under more extensive training. A key open question is whether the grouped LA + local softmax attention hybrid is the optimal decomposition, and whether the unfolded convolution approach generalizes to more complex speculative decoding trees beyond Medusa's fixed-width candidates.
