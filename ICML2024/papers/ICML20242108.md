## Problem

Existing methods for tracing the origin of AI-generated images (watermarking, fingerprinting, classification) all require extra operations during training or generation, limiting their applicability to images produced without such modifications. The only prior alteration-free approach (RONAN) uses random initialization for gradient-based inversion, which is both ineffective (≈50% accuracy) and inefficient on state-of-the-art latent generative models with similar architectures. This paper asks whether origin attribution can be achieved accurately and efficiently without any additional requirements on the model's training or generation pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242108/cropped_page14_idx7.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires white-box access to the inspected model's autoencoder (encoder and decoder) and a single examined RGB image; it does not need the full generation pipeline (diffusion/autoregressive components) or knowledge of the generation hyperparameters (sampler, steps, etc.). The output is a binary belonging/non-belonging decision determined by comparing the final reconstruction loss against a threshold pre-computed offline via Grubbs' hypothesis testing on 100 model-generated samples. The method operates on 512×512 or 1024×1024 images depending on the inspected model's native resolution.

## Method

LatentTracer exploits the invertibility of the autoencoder in latent generative models to initialize gradient-based latent inversion, rather than starting from a random latent. Specifically, it first encodes the examined image with the model's encoder to obtain a starting latent (reducing initial distance to ground-truth from ~1.9 to ~0.02), then performs 100 steps of gradient descent to minimize the reconstruction loss between the decoder output and the examined image. The key insight is that images produced by a model's decoder carry an implicit 'natural watermark'—they can be reconstructed with near-zero loss by that same decoder, while images from other models or real photos yield significantly higher reconstruction losses. This encoder-based initialization is critical: without it, the reconstruction losses of belonging and non-belonging images overlap heavily, but with it, the two distributions are nearly completely separated, enabling 93–98% detection accuracy across Stable Diffusion variants and Kandinsky.

## Results

On the primary task of distinguishing images generated by the inspected model from those generated by other models, LATENTTRACER achieves an average detection accuracy of 93.4% across 16 model-pair settings, compared to only ~50% for the prior alteration-free method RONAN. When distinguishing belonging images from real images (LAION), the method reaches 97.9% average accuracy. In terms of efficiency, encoder-based initialization reduces inference time from 87–121 s (random initialization) to 21–30 s per image, requiring only 100 optimization steps versus 400. The method generalizes to continuous autoencoders (VAE, 98.2%), quantized autoencoders (VQ-VAE, 97.5%), and autoregressive models (ViT-VQGAN, 98.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the only prior alteration-free method RONAN (Wang et al., 2023d), LATENTTRACER achieves a dramatic improvement of ~43 percentage points in average accuracy (93.4% vs. ~50%), with the gap being most pronounced when distinguishing between models sharing similar architectures (e.g., SD v2-base vs. SD v1-4: 98.2% vs. 55.6%). Against watermarking and fingerprinting baselines (Dct-Dwt, SSL Watermark, FNNS, HiDDeN, Stable Signature) that require extra operations during training or generation, LATENTTRACER achieves perfect image quality (SSIM = 1.00, FID = 0) since it introduces no modification to the generated image, whereas all baselines degrade quality (SSIM 0.86–0.97, FID 19.0–20.6). Against Laszkiewicz et al. (2023), which inverts only the final layer, LATENTTRACER outperforms by 5–6 percentage points across all four tested model pairs (e.g., 98.2% vs. 92.7% for SD v2-base vs. SD v1-1), attributed to leveraging the full autoencoder rather than a single layer. The method also demonstrates robustness to post-processing: detection accuracy remains above 90% when SSIM between original and perturbed images is ≥ 0.9, covering JPEG compression (quality 60–95), Gaussian noise (σ up to 0.03), and moderate saturation/contrast adjustments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the inversion-based origin attribution framework of Wang et al. (2023d, RONAN), which used random initialization followed by gradient-based optimization to reverse-engineer the input of a generative model. The key departure is identifying that the autoencoder's encoder provides a far superior starting point for the gradient optimization (reducing initial distance to ground-truth latent from ~1.9 to ~0.02), a connection to encoder-based inversion techniques already used in image editing (Mokady et al., 2023; Parmar et al., 2023) but not previously exploited for attribution. Conceptually, the paper reframes the problem by focusing solely on the deterministic decoder rather than the full stochastic generation pipeline, and positions the decoder as an 'implicit watermark' that naturally fingerprints its outputs without any artificial insertion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that model owners can trace their generated images without modifying training or generation pipelines, avoiding the quality degradation and vulnerability to watermark-stealing/forgery attacks that plague artificial watermarking methods. A key limitation is that the method requires models to have unique autoencoders; it cannot distinguish images from models sharing the same VAE (e.g., SD v1-4 vs. SD v1-5), which the authors acknowledge as future work. Robustness degrades under strong post-processing (accuracy drops below 80% when SSIM falls below ~0.85), and the method assumes white-box access to the inspected model. The 'implicit watermark' observation opens an interesting question about whether this property is fundamental to all latent-space decoders or specific to the architectures tested.
