## Problem

Energy barriers (EBs) of metallic glasses are critical intermediaries for understanding structure-property relationships, but precise computation via ARTn takes ~41 days for a 3,000-atom system. Existing GNNs for molecular graphs cannot simultaneously achieve E(3)-invariance, expressiveness (capturing higher-order geometric information beyond scalar distances), and scalability to graphs with thousands of nodes, leaving a gap in efficient and accurate EB prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241181/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes atomic graphs with thousands of nodes (3,000–8,000 atoms) and ~100K–260K edges, where node features are atom types and edge features are full 3D displacement vectors (not scalar distances), making invariance non-trivial. It outputs a scalar energy barrier value per node (regression) and, via an extended GNNExplainer, per-edge importance scores that identify which structural connections drive each prediction. The edge construction uses a 5 Å Euclidean distance threshold between atoms.

## Method

SymGNN achieves E(3)-invariance in expectation through a symmetrization module that samples k orthogonal transformations from O(3) (parameterized by a Bernoulli sign and three Euler angles drawn from learnable von Mises distributions), encodes each transformed graph with a shared encoder, and averages the resulting embeddings. This avoids the expressiveness loss of scalarization (e.g., SchNet's distance-only features) and the computational cost of equivariant tensor operations (e.g., Equiformer), yielding O(kn + nd²) complexity. The prediction head is an Edge Graph Attention Network (EGAT) that incorporates 3D edge features directly into attention scores with skip connections. For interpretability, the authors adapt GNNExplainer to node regression by replacing the entropy objective with MSE, producing edge-importance masks that reveal medium-range-order structures and correlate with persistent homology cycle counts.

## Results

On the Cu64Zr36 dataset, SymGNN achieves a Pearson correlation of 0.7859±0.0056 on the test set, outperforming the strongest baseline SchNet (0.7588±0.0088) and the MD local sampling method (0.3614). On the cross-composition Cu80Zr20 and Cu50Zr50 datasets, SymGNN scores 0.6084±0.0167 and 0.5862±0.0277 respectively, again leading all baselines. Inference time on a 3,000-atom MG system is 0.26 seconds for SymGNN versus 41 days for precise ARTn simulation and 150 minutes for MD local sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SymGNN outperforms all seven baselines across all three datasets. Against the closest competitor SchNet (0.7588), SymGNN gains +0.027 on Cu64Zr36 and a much larger +0.358 on Cu80Zr20 (0.6084 vs 0.2505), indicating stronger generalization to unseen compositions. The MD local sampling method (Sastry et al., 1998) scores only 0.3614 on Cu64Zr36, less than half of SymGNN's score, while also requiring 150 minutes of inference versus 0.26 seconds. GCN with edge features (Kipf & Welling, 2017) reaches 0.5123, and EGAT with 1D edge-length features (Kamiński et al., 2021) reaches 0.7264, both clearly below SymGNN. The equivariant EGNN (Satorras et al., 2022) performs poorly at 0.2588, suggesting that equivariance alone without proper invariance handling is insufficient. MGCN (Lu et al., 2019) and FAENet (Duval et al., 2023) score 0.7352 and 0.6603 respectively on Cu64Zr36. Notably, DimeNet, Equiformer, and Torch-MD Net were infeasible to train on the 8,000-node graphs (training exceeded 2 days), highlighting SymGNN's scalability advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of invariant/equivariant GNNs for molecular systems and materials science. It departs from the two dominant strategies for handling E(3)-invariance—scalarization (SchNet, MGCN, FAENet, which restrict features to distances/angles and lose higher-order geometric information) and equivariant tensor operations (DimeNet, Equiformer, Torch-MD Net, which are expressive but scale poorly to graphs with thousands of nodes)—by proposing a symmetrization module that aggregates encodings over sampled orthogonal transformations, achieving invariance in expectation. The message-passing backbone builds on EGAT (Kamiński et al., 2021), and the explanation framework extends GNNExplainer (Ying et al., 2019) from classification entropy to regression MSE. The EB prediction task itself extends Wang et al. (2020), who used XGBoost to classify atoms in the top 5% of activation energy, to full regression on graph-structured inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic speedup (41 days of ARTn simulation → <1 second inference) makes EB prediction practically accessible for large-scale materials screening, and the generated explanations provide a concrete bridge between ML predictions and the medium-range order hypothesis central to metallic glass physics. The TDA analysis revealing that high-importance edges participate in significantly more optimal cycles (4.13 vs. 0.874) offers a new topological lens on what governs local energy barriers. Limitations include evaluation on a single alloy family (Cu-Zr) with only 9 samples, the invariance being approximate (in expectation) rather than exact, and the learned von Mises distributions lacking a clear physical interpretation. Open questions include whether the symmetrization approach generalizes to other material classes and properties, and whether the topological signatures identified here are universal across MG compositions.
