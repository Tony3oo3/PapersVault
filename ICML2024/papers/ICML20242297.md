## Problem

Existing personalized federated learning methods (e.g., pFedGraph, CFL) assume that optimal cooperation is achieved by collaborating with the most similar clients, using model parameter similarity as the sole cooperation criterion. This paper challenges that assumption, arguing that maximal similarity can yield zero information gain (two identical clients learn nothing from each other), and that moderate covariate shift in feature distributions actually provides beneficial complementarity. The gap is a principled mechanism that simultaneously leverages similarity in the conditional distribution p(y|x) and complementarity in the marginal distribution p(x) to determine optimal client-to-client cooperation strength.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local dataset of 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10/100) or paired image-text samples (CUB200-2011), partitioned under homogeneous, Dirichlet, or pathological heterogeneity schemes across 8–10 clients. Rather than sharing raw data, clients upload only model parameters and a low-dimensional representative subspace (the first k=3 left singular vectors of the model's feature matrix) to the server, preserving privacy. The output is a personalized model per client (not a single global model), which maps inputs to class labels; the server additionally maintains a dynamic N×N cooperation adjacency matrix W that is re-optimized every communication round.

## Method

FedSaC constructs a cooperation network over N clients whose adjacency matrix W is optimized to balance two metrics: (1) similarity S, computed as the cosine distance between local model parameters (proxying consistency in p(y|x)), and (2) complementarity C, computed as the average principal angle between the k-dimensional SVD subspaces of each client's feature matrix (proxying diversity in p(x)). On the server side, W is obtained by solving a convex quadratic program that minimizes a weighted sum of (W_ij − p^j)², α·C, and −β·S subject to row-stochastic and non-negativity constraints; the aggregated model for client i is then θ̃^i = Σ_j W_ij θ^j. On the client side, each client initializes its local model from the aggregated model and performs local SGD with a cosine-similarity regularization term (λ·cos(θ^i, θ̃^i)) to prevent excessive deviation. The SVD-based subspace extraction (complexity O(md²)) and the convex optimization (solvable via interior-point methods in polynomial time) add negligible overhead relative to local training, and the framework extends to multimodal settings by applying the same within-modality cooperation while using similarity-only aggregation across modalities at the classification layer.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, FedSaC achieves 70.89% (Homo), 80.46% (Dirichlet low), 93.14% (Dirichlet high), and 92.89% (Pathological) average accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline pFedGraph by up to 8.9% under high Dirichlet heterogeneity. On CIFAR-100, FedSaC reaches 34.84%, 41.80%, 56.27%, and 57.48% across the same four partitions, surpassing pFedGraph by 3.1–4.6%. In the multimodal CUB200-2011 setting, FedSaC attains 35.36% (visual) and 33.44% (textual) under high Dirichlet partition, exceeding the multimodal-specific baseline FedIoT by over 15 points. With a larger ResNet-18 backbone on CIFAR-100, FedSaC maintains its lead, reaching 67.75% under high Dirichlet partition versus 64.91% for pFedGraph.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedSaC outperforms all 12 unimodal baselines across every heterogeneity setting on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. The most direct competitor, pFedGraph (which relies solely on model similarity for cooperation), is surpassed by FedSaC by 3.5–8.9% on CIFAR-10 and 3.1–4.6% on CIFAR-100, with the largest gaps appearing under Dirichlet partitions where feature complementarity is most pronounced. Standard FL methods (FedAvg, FedProx, kNN-Per) degrade severely under high heterogeneity (e.g., FedAvg drops to 26.23% on CIFAR-100 Pathological), while FedSaC maintains 57.48%, demonstrating robustness to distribution shift. Personalized methods like Ditto, FedRep, and pFedHN show moderate gains over FedAvg but still trail FedSaC by 3–10% in most settings. In the multimodal CUB200-2011 experiment, FedSaC exceeds the purpose-built multimodal FL method FedIoT by 10–15% in visual accuracy and 4–16% in textual accuracy, confirming that the similarity-complementarity balance generalizes beyond unimodal image classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the personalized federated learning line that uses cooperation networks to mitigate statistical heterogeneity, building directly on pFedGraph (Ye et al., 2023) which constructs client collaboration graphs from model similarity, and on FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017) whose global aggregation objective is restructured into a per-client weighted cooperation framework. The key conceptual departure is challenging the assumption that model similarity alone should govern cooperation: the authors decompose distributional heterogeneity into concept shift (p(y|x) differences, where similarity helps) and covariate shift (p(x) differences, where moderate complementarity helps), and introduce an SVD-based principal-angle metric to quantify feature complementarity under privacy constraints. This positions FedSaC as a balanced alternative to the purely similarity-driven approaches (IFCA, CFL, pFedGraph, FedAMP) that dominate the personalized FL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a useful reframing of when and why clients should cooperate in FL, with the practical insight that the most similar partner is not always the most beneficial one. However, the evidence is somewhat limited in scale: experiments use 10 clients on CIFAR-10/100 and a small multimodal CUB200-2011 setup, with a simple CNN or ResNet18, and the hyperparameters α and β require scenario-dependent tuning (e.g., reducing complementarity weight for pathological partitions). Open questions include whether the complementarity signal remains informative at larger client scales with more complex architectures, how the SVD-based subspace metric behaves with high-dimensional or non-visual features, and whether the convex optimization for the adjacency matrix remains tractable in truly large-scale deployments (the paper shows n² server-side overhead).
