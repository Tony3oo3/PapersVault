## Problem

State-of-the-art position-based re-weighting functions for linear transformers (e.g., cosFormer) require explicit knowledge of sequence lengths, making them inapplicable to autoregressive tasks where target lengths are unknown and impossible to apply to simultaneous tasks where even input lengths are unavailable at decode time. Additionally, static positional representations produce fixed attention concentration patterns (e.g., locality bias) that cannot adapt to tasks requiring diverse long-range dependencies.

## Input / Output

The LeaP module takes each token's query or key vector (of dimension d_head) from an attention head and outputs a single scalar proportion in [0,1], effectively reducing the head dimension to one value per token. For LRA tasks, inputs are text or image patch sequences producing classification labels; for Wikitext-103, 512-token text sequences produce next-token predictions; for SimulST, pre-trained ASR encoder outputs from speech audio produce translated text under a wait-k schedule. The key constraint is that no sequence length is required at inference time, enabling autoregressive and simultaneous decoding.

## Method

LeaPformer generalizes cosFormer's position-and-length-dependent re-weighting into a proportion-based formulation where tokens are characterized by proportions P_q and P_k in [0,1] rather than absolute positions divided by sequence length. A compact two-layer feed-forward network (LeaP module) with ReLU activation between layers and sigmoid output dynamically derives these proportions from each token's query/key vector, replacing static positional encodings. The module is shared across all attention heads to minimize parameter overhead (0.2%–1.5% increase), and is applied to cosFormer's cosine re-weighting function σ(P_q,i, P_k,j) = cos(π/2(P_q,i − P_k,j)). This removes all theoretical dependence on sequence length and enables the model to learn task-appropriate attention concentration patterns—e.g., deferring locality bias for SOV-language translation—while maintaining linear complexity in sequence length.

## Results

On the Long-Range Arena benchmark, LeaPformer-1.5% achieves the second-best average accuracy (58.69) among all tested mechanisms, beating every non-quadratic transformer and scoring 5.20 on the proposed Relative Composite Performance (RCP) metric — a minimum 0.81 RCP advantage over the next-best method. On Wikitext-103b autoregressive language modeling, LeaPformer achieves 24.04 test perplexity, outperforming cosFormer (24.17) and ReLU Attention (24.16) by 0.13 ppl. On simultaneous speech translation, replacing only the decoder self-attention with LeaPformer yields just a 0.26 BLEU-4 loss (en-de) and 0.23 BLEU-4 loss (fr-en) relative to full softmax attention, while maintaining linear complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LeaPformer-1.5% (58.69 avg) outperforms all non-quadratic baselines on LRA: it beats cosFormer (57.43) by 1.26 points, Linear Attention (57.38) by 1.31, Performer (57.70) by 0.99, Reformer (58.07) by 0.62, Skyformer (58.12) by 0.57, BigBird (58.64) by 0.05, Linformer (53.61) by 5.08, and RoPE w/ Linear Attn. (57.48) by 1.21. It trails only softmax attention (59.25) by 0.56 points while achieving 7.7× higher training throughput at 4k sequence length (11.76 vs 1.64 itr/sec). On Wikitext-103b, LeaPformer (24.04 test ppl) beats cosFormer (24.17) and ReLU Attention (24.16) but remains 2.37 ppl behind softmax (21.67). On SimulST, LeaPformer with only decoder self-attention linearized achieves 14.81 BLEU (en-de) and 14.28 BLEU (fr-en), losing just 0.26 and 0.23 BLEU to full softmax (15.07 and 14.51 respectively), while cosFormer cannot be applied at all to simultaneous tasks without sequence-length augmentation. The S4D state-space model (49.09 avg on LRA) is substantially outperformed, though the authors note this may reflect the small model sizes used in the LRA setup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the position-based re-weighting line of work initiated by cosFormer (Qin et al., 2022) and related schemes like RoPE (Su et al., 2022), which augment linear attention (Katharopoulos et al., 2020) with position-dependent concentration functions. The key departure is reframing the dependence on explicit token positions and sequence lengths into a dependence on learned sequence proportions, replacing static positional encodings with a compact two-layer module that dynamically derives per-token proportions. This generalization removes the theoretical requirement of knowing sequence length a priori, which prior re-weighting functions (cosFormer, RoPE) inherently demand, thereby unblocking their use in autoregressive and simultaneous settings where target or even input lengths are unknown at decode time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates for the first time that position-based re-weighting functions can be applied to simultaneous speech translation, a setting previously considered infeasible for such mechanisms, with only 0.23–0.26 BLEU loss versus softmax attention when linearizing decoder self-attention. However, the approach still trails classical softmax attention by a substantial margin (2.37 perplexity on Wikitext-103b), and linearizing all attention blocks in SimulST causes significant quality degradation (BLEU drops of 3–5 points), limiting practical adoption. Open questions include whether learned proportions generalize to much larger models and longer sequences, whether the compact module's expressivity suffices for highly structured long-range dependencies, and whether the proportion-based framing can be extended to other re-weighting families beyond the cosine kernel.
