## Problem

Score-based diffusion models trained on valid data still allocate probability mass to invalid regions (e.g., vehicle collisions, ground penetration), and rejection sampling to filter these outputs is computationally prohibitive in real-time settings. While oracle functions that check constraints are ubiquitous in practice, no principled method existed for incorporating them into diffusion model training to suppress invalid outputs without distorting the valid-region distribution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataset of valid samples and a binary oracle O: X → {0,1} that partitions the data space into allowed and disallowed regions. For traffic scene generation, input is a 256×256 RGB roadway map and the output is 12 vehicles each with 7 features (position, length, width, orientation, velocity) in R^{12×7}. For motion generation, input is a text description (via CLIP embeddings) and the output is a 263-dimensional per-frame motion sequence. The oracle is a fixed, pre-defined function (not learned), and all training data is guaranteed to satisfy it.

## Method

Gen-neG is an iterative classifier-guidance procedure for diffusion models. Starting from a baseline DM trained via standard score matching, each iteration samples from the current model, labels samples with the oracle, and trains a time-dependent binary classifier on a balanced synthetic dataset using importance sampling weights (α and 1−α) to recover the Bayes-optimal classifier for the model's own distribution. The classifier's gradient is added to the score function (s_θ,φ = s_θ + ∇log C_φ), and the resulting guided model becomes the new baseline for the next iteration. The key theoretical contribution is proving that a Bayes-optimal classifier trained on the model's synthetic data exactly recovers the conditional score ∇log p_θ(x_t|y=1), guaranteeing zero mass on the disallowed region and improved likelihood on valid data. Optionally, the stacked classifiers can be distilled into a single student model via score-matching loss to reduce inference cost.

## Results

On traffic scene generation, Gen-neG (iteration 2) reduces overall infraction rate from 29.3% (baseline DM) to 12.2%, and distillation of the stacked model further reduces it to 5.6% while maintaining a comparable reweighted ELBO (−27.0 vs. −27.5 ×10⁻²). On HumanML3D motion generation, one iteration of Gen-neG reduces infraction rate from 27.66% to 24.25%, improves FID from 0.445 to 0.414, KID from 8.27 to 6.99, and r-ELBO from −1.06 to −1.01 (×10⁻²). On the 2D checkerboard experiment, Gen-neG dramatically reduces infraction rates while matching the data distribution, outperforming models trained on up to 1000× larger datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the normalizing flow baseline (Zwartsenberg et al., 2023), Gen-neG dramatically outperforms: the NF achieves 91.9% infraction vs. 5.6% for distilled Gen-neG, attributed to DMs being far more expressive. Against the time-independent classifier guidance approach (Wu et al., 2023), Gen-neG achieves comparable infraction reduction (12.2% vs. 21.4%) but with a vastly superior r-ELBO (−28.0 vs. −244 ×10⁻²), indicating the time-independent classifier causes severe distributional misalignment. Against MDM (Tevet et al., 2023) on motion generation, Gen-neG improves all reported metrics: infraction drops from 27.66% to 24.25%, FID from 0.445 to 0.414, KID from 8.27 to 6.99, and r-ELBO from −1.06 to −1.01. Against diffusion bridges (Liu et al., 2023), Gen-neG does not match the zero-infraction guarantee, but diffusion bridges require analytical access to constraints and are inapplicable to complex oracle-defined constraints, so they serve as a theoretical upper bound rather than a practical competitor. Against GAN data redaction (Kong & Chaudhuri, 2023), Gen-neG achieves significantly better MMD scores and quickly surpasses the GAN baseline in infraction rate on the checkerboard experiment.

## Relation to Prior Work

Gen-neG extends the classifier-guidance paradigm for diffusion models (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021; Song et al., 2021) by replacing the standard class-conditional classifier with one trained on fully synthetic data labeled by an external oracle, following the discriminator-guidance insight of Kim et al. (2022). The key conceptual departure is identifying that the class prior probabilities (α, 1−α) must be explicitly corrected via importance sampling when the synthetic training set is balanced, a condition the authors argue has been overlooked in prior guidance formulations. The iterative 'stacking' of successive classifiers and subsequent distillation into a single model is a procedural novelty not present in standard classifier guidance, positioning the work between rejection sampling (too expensive) and constraint-specific methods like diffusion bridges or reflected diffusion (too restrictive).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for real-time constrained generation: the reported 57% average GPU cost reduction in traffic scene generation directly addresses the latency bottleneck of rejection sampling in autonomous driving and robotics. However, the method's iterative nature means computational cost scales linearly with the number of stacked classifiers, and the authors acknowledge that revisiting the true training data in later iterations has not yet been successful, limiting large-scale adoption. Open questions include whether the stacking can be replaced by a single learned artifact, whether the approach extends to mixed continuous/categorical (tabular) data, and how to close the gap with diffusion bridges that provably achieve zero infraction but require analytical constraint access.
