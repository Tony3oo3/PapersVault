## Problem

Existing machine learning-guided cutting plane methods for ILPs focus exclusively on cut *addition* policies, permanently retaining every cut once introduced. This paper identifies that early cuts can become obsolete as the constraint set grows, and introduces the first cut *removal* policy: at each iteration, all possible Gomory cuts are added to maximize LP bound improvement, then the least useful cuts are removed to prevent exponential constraint growth. The key question is whether a learned removal criterion can outperform both handcrafted heuristics and ML-guided addition-only policies.

## Input / Output

The method takes an ILP instance (H, c) and, at each iteration, operates on the current cutpool C_k (all Gomory cuts from the simplex tableau) plus previously retained cuts P_k. Each candidate cut (α, β) is encoded as a 14-dimensional feature vector comprising coefficient statistics, objective alignment (parallelism), geometric distance to the current LP solution (efficacy), sparsity measures (support, integral support), normalized violation, and a binary indicator for cutpool membership. The MLP outputs a scalar predicted LP bound improvement score per cut, from which the top k+1 cuts are retained and the rest removed; a special constraint c^T x ≥ ⌈c^T x_k*⌉ is appended to guarantee monotonic objective improvement despite removals.

## Method

Algorithm 2 departs from the standard cutting plane loop by adding the *entire* cutpool C_k at each iteration (yielding the strongest possible LP bound) and then selecting only k+1 cuts from P_k ∪ C_k to carry forward, ensuring the constraint count grows by just one per iteration. The ideal removal criterion scores each cut by the LP bound drop when that cut is removed (Eq. 4), but this requires solving |P_k ∪ C_k| LPs per iteration. To avoid this overhead, the paper trains a single-layer MLP with sigmoid activation via imitation learning: trajectories from the exact look-ahead removal policy on 2000 instances per family provide (feature vector, normalized LP improvement) pairs, and the network is trained with quadratic regression loss. The critical architectural difference from prior cut-addition work is that cuts are not permanent — a cut useful at iteration 5 can be evicted at iteration 20 in favor of a fresher cut, and the monotonicity constraint c^T x ≥ ⌈c^T x_k*⌉ (proven in Corollary 3.2 to preserve the integer optimum) prevents the objective from regressing when informative cuts are dropped.

## Results

The cut removal method is evaluated on five MILP families (Packing, Bin Packing, Max Cut, Production Planning, Set Cover) using Integral Gap Closure (IGC) as the primary metric, where higher IGC with fewer iterations indicates better performance. In-distribution (500 test instances, ~100 variables/constraints), cut removal outperforms all cut addition baselines in Packing, Bin Packing, Production Planning, and Set Cover, with the largest margins in Packing and Production Planning. Out-of-distribution (500 instances ~50% larger), the advantage grows for Packing, Bin Packing, and Set Cover, confirming stronger generalization. In the SCIP solver (26 Neural Network Verification instances), B&C-Cut_Removal finds on average a 35% better solution than B&C-Cut_Addition, improving on 88.46% of instances. Wall-clock speedups range from 1.14×–5.12× over Paulus et al., 24.30×–47.26× over the Look-ahead Expert, and 2.15×–5.41× over non-neural heuristics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Paulus et al. (2022) (Neural Cut via imitation learning and the Look-ahead Expert), the cut removal method achieves higher mean IGC in Packing, Bin Packing, Production Planning, and Set Cover across both in-distribution and out-of-distribution settings, with the gap widening on larger instances. The sole exception is Max Cut, where cut removal does not clearly outperform the expert or Neural Cut, though the differences are minimal (a random policy performs comparably in early iterations). Against the heuristic baselines from Wesselmann & Stuhl (2012) (Random, Max Violation, Max Normalized Violation, Lexicographic, Min Similar), cut removal consistently achieves superior IGC across all five families. In the SCIP solver integration, cut removal yields a 35% average solution improvement over the vanilla cut-addition policy and 24–47× wall-clock speedup over the Look-ahead Expert, which requires solving |C_k| LPs per iteration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Gomory's cutting plane method by introducing a cut removal step that has no precedent in the cutting plane literature. All prior ML-guided approaches (Tang et al. 2020, Paulus et al. 2022, Huang et al. 2021) focus exclusively on selecting which single cut to add at each iteration; this work instead adds the entire Gomory cutpool and then removes most cuts using a learned scoring model. The scoring criterion (LP bound improvement) and the 14-dimensional hand-crafted feature encoding are directly adapted from Paulus et al. (2022), but the imitation learning target shifts from predicting the best cut to add to predicting which cuts to discard, and the simple MLP architecture is retained rather than the more complex models considered in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple MLP-based cut removal policy outperforms both the computationally expensive look-ahead expert and prior ML-guided cut addition methods across four of five ILP families, with the performance gap widening on larger instances. This challenges the implicit assumption in the cutting plane literature that the constraint set should only grow monotonically, showing that periodically discarding stale cuts is more effective than carefully selecting a single new cut. Open questions include whether more expressive models (e.g., GNNs capturing joint cut structure) can further improve performance, whether the number of retained cuts should be adaptive rather than fixed at k+1, and whether the approach extends to non-Gomory cut families in production solvers.
