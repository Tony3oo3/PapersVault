## Problem

Existing KV cache compression methods (sparse/eviction policies like H2O) irrecoverably discard KV pairs deemed less important, creating gaps in attention maps that cause significant performance degradation on tasks requiring recollection of previously omitted tokens. The paper identifies that the residual between full and sparse attention outputs is substantially low-rank, suggesting a principled mechanism to recover discarded information without storing all KV pairs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240454/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each decoding step, LESS consumes the current query, key, and value vectors, the sparse policy's cached KV pairs (K_C,t, V_C,t), and a constant-sized low-rank state consisting of H ∈ R^{R×D} and z ∈ R^{1×R} (with R=8 in experiments, equivalent to roughly 4 extra tokens of storage). It produces a combined attention output that blends standard softmax attention over the sparse cache with a kernel-based contribution from the low-rank state, then updates both the sparse cache (via the eviction policy) and the low-rank state (by absorbing newly evicted KV pairs) before the next step. The low-rank state size is independent of sequence length, so the proportional memory saving is identical for small and large batch sizes.

## Method

LESS synthesizes any eviction-based sparse KV cache policy with a constant-sized low-rank recurrent state by learning two small MLP kernels (φ for queries, ψ for keys, each with GELU activations and hidden dimension 512) that approximate the low-rank residual between full and sparse attention outputs. At each decoding step, KV pairs evicted by the sparse policy are recursively embedded into H and z via ψ(k)ᵀv and ψ(k) updates before deletion, so the fixed-size state accumulates information from all previously discarded tokens. The attention output is computed as a weighted combination of the sparse cache contribution (exp(qK_C'ᵀ/√D)V_C') and the low-rank contribution (φ(q)H), normalized by their respective denominators. Training is performed per-layer independently by minimizing L2 distance to the original attention layer's output projection with all original model weights frozen, and kernel initialization is scaled by 10⁻⁴ so the sparse policy serves as a warm start. The approach adds fewer than 2% parameters to Llama 2 13B and requires only a single GPU for training.

## Results

On Llama 2 7B with H2O as the underlying sparse policy, LESS(2%) reduces WikiText word perplexity from 13.333 (Baseline) to 10.745 and PG-19 from 37.013 to 32.157, recovering over 20% of the degradation relative to the full cache (8.791 / 23.787). On summarization with Falcon 7B, LESS(5%) recovers 41.4% of the Rouge-1 degradation on CNN/DailyMail (23.00 vs. Baseline 21.26, Full 25.92). In system benchmarks, LESS achieves 1.1–1.3× lower latency and 1.7× higher generation throughput compared to the full KV cache on an A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LESS consistently outperforms all sparse-only baselines across every sparsity level and dataset. Against H2O (Zhang et al., 2023), LESS(2%) cuts WikiText perplexity by 19.4% (10.745 vs. 13.333) and PG-19 by 13.1% (32.157 vs. 37.013) at 2% sparsity, and at 10% sparsity LESS(10%) achieves 9.230 vs. 9.295 on WikiText. Against Lambda-masking (Han et al., 2023; Xiao et al., 2023), LESS(5%) improves WikiText PPL from 10.66 to 10.12, MuTual R@1 from 53.50 to 54.51, and BoolQ accuracy from 77.28% to 78.65%. Against TOVA (Oren et al., 2024), LESS(5%) reduces WikiText PPL from 9.97 to 9.72 and PG-19 from 27.88 to 27.34. Critically, H2O+Performer (Choromanski et al., 2020) with random Fourier feature kernels performs nearly identically to the raw H2O baseline (e.g., 13.332 vs. 13.333 on WikiText 2%), confirming that learned kernels are essential for meaningful improvement. LESS narrows but does not fully close the gap to the full cache, with the largest remaining gap at extreme sparsity (2% H2O on PG-19: 32.157 vs. 23.787).

## Relation to Prior Work

LESS synthesizes two established families of KV cache compression: eviction-based sparse policies (H2O, Lambda-masking, TOVA) that discard 'unimportant' tokens, and low-rank/linear attention mechanisms (Performer, linear transformers) that compress information into constant-sized recurrent states. The key conceptual bridge is the observation that the residual between full and sparse attention outputs is itself low-rank, drawing on the sparse-plus-low-rank decomposition tradition from Robust PCA and Scatterbrain. Unlike prior work that treats these as competing alternatives, LESS positions the low-rank state as a 'sidekick' that recovers exactly the information the sparse policy discards, requiring only tiny per-layer MLPs trained independently on a single GPU.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KV cache memory often exceeds model parameter memory at practical batch sizes, making this a critical deployment bottleneck; LESS addresses it with a constant-sized cache (equivalent to ~4 extra tokens) that is cheap to train and adds <2% parameters. The method's practical appeal is strong: per-layer independent training, transferability across sparsity levels, and 1.3× latency / 1.7× throughput gains over full caching. Open questions include whether the residual can be fully closed (LESS still lags full cache on many tasks), how it behaves at extreme sequence lengths beyond 10K tokens, and whether the low-rank residual structure holds universally across architectures and tasks.
