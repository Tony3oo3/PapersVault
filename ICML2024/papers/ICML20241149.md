## Problem

In adaptive contextual bandit experiments (e.g., clinical trials), minimizing regret (patient welfare loss) and accurately estimating conditional average treatment effects (CATE) are conflicting objectives, yet no prior work characterized their minimax tradeoff in the heterogeneous, context-dependent setting. Furthermore, it remained unknown whether differential privacy constraints on the allocation mechanism and CATE estimator would degrade this optimal tradeoff. This paper provides the first matched upper and lower bounds for the multi-objective problem and shows that privacy is 'almost free' in this context.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a stream of n patients, each with a discrete feature x_t from a finite set of M features drawn from a potentially non-stationary (seasonal) distribution, and observes binary treatment rewards in [0,1] after allocation. A tunable parameter α ∈ [0,1] controls the regret-estimation tradeoff (α=1 minimizes regret, α=0 maximizes estimation accuracy), and a privacy parameter ε governs the (ε, 1/n)-anticipating differential privacy guarantee. The output consists of per-feature CATE estimates Δ̂(X_j) for all j=1,...,M, the sequence of treatment allocations, and a characterization of the Pareto optimal curve e·R = O(M) that no algorithm can improve upon.

## Method

ConSE operates in three phases: (1) in the first half of the horizon, it runs a per-feature successive elimination procedure with exponentially growing batch sizes to identify the optimal arm while simultaneously estimating each feature's occurrence frequency f_j(n/2); (2) in the second half, it runs a randomized controlled trial for T_min = max{log n, min_j f̂_j^{1-α}} periods per feature to collect unbiased CATE estimates; (3) it plays the identified optimal arm for the remaining time. The DP-ConSE variant introduces a 'doubly private' mechanism: rewards are privatized via a tree mechanism with Laplace noise at batch boundaries, and batch lengths are randomized using a novel Lap^+ distribution to protect the context (feature) information of each patient — a technique not previously used in DP-contextual bandit literature. The key theoretical result is that the product of regret and worst-case CATE estimation error equals O(M), matching the minimax lower bound, and this bound is preserved under (ε, 1/n)-anticipating differential privacy when ε is a small constant. Additionally, the CATE estimator is proven asymptotically normal with the same limiting variance as the non-private case, enabling standard confidence intervals and hypothesis testing.

## Results

The main theoretical result is a tight minimax characterization: for any admissible policy-estimator pair, the product of regret and CATE estimation error satisfies e_ν(n,Δ̂_n)·R_ν(n,π) = Θ(M), and the proposed ConSE algorithm (and its DP variant DP-ConSE) achieves the upper bound O(M) for every α∈[0,1], matching the lower bound Ω(M). The DP-ConSE algorithm is (ε, 1/n)-private while preserving the same O(M) product bound, demonstrating that differential privacy is 'almost free' when ε is a small constant. Empirically, on a normal linear bandit (n=20,000), the Mean Difference (MD) estimator achieves errors of 0.31, 0.03, and 0.008 at regret budgets of 200, 400, and 800 respectively, consistently outperforming the Double Machine Learning (DML) estimator (0.53, 0.07, 0.02). When the linear regularity assumption is violated, MD maintains errors of 0.03 and 0.008 while DML degrades to 4.1 and 4.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary theoretical baseline is Simchi-Levi & Wang (2023), which studies the regret–ATE tradeoff in a non-contextual (no features) setting. This paper strictly improves on all five dimensions: it extends to contextual bandits with M features, tightens the lower bound from Ω(1) to Ω(M), improves the upper bound from O(log n) to O(M) (tight up to a constant rather than a polylog factor), adds differential privacy guarantees, and establishes asymptotic normality of the estimator. Empirically, the paper compares its simple Mean Difference (MD) estimator against the Double Machine Learning (DML) estimator from Kim et al. (2021). Under well-conditioned linear bandit settings, MD outperforms DML at every regret budget (e.g., 0.03 vs. 0.07 at Reg=400). Under ill-conditioned settings where the linear regularity assumption fails, the gap widens dramatically: MD achieves 0.03 error while DML produces 4.1 error at Reg=400, indicating DML's sensitivity to model misspecification. The paper also notes that MD is computationally far cheaper (~1 second vs. ~5 minutes per experiment), reinforcing its practical advantage for long-horizon experiments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adaptive experiment design framework of Simchi-Levi & Wang (2023), which characterized the regret–ATE estimation tradeoff in non-contextual bandits, to the contextual bandit setting with heterogeneous treatment effects (CATE) and non-stationary feature distributions. For the privacy component, it adapts the DP Successive Elimination mechanism of Sajed & Sheffet (2019) and the anticipating differential privacy (ADP) definition from Shariff & Sheffet (2018) and Chen et al. (2022), but introduces a novel 'doubly private' design that privatizes both reward estimates (via tree-mechanism batching) and batch lengths (to protect feature/context information). The paper also departs from prior adaptive experiment design work (Dai et al. 2023, Zhao 2023) by establishing asymptotic normality of the CATE estimator, enabling standard confidence intervals and hypothesis testing that were previously unavailable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central result—that differential privacy is 'almost free' in the sense that the product of regret and CATE estimation error remains O(M) even under (ε, δ)-DP—challenges the prevailing assumption that privacy necessarily degrades both decision-making efficiency and statistical power simultaneously. This opens the door to privacy-preserving adaptive clinical trials without sacrificing the fundamental accuracy–welfare tradeoff. However, the experimental validation is limited to simple linear bandit instances without features, and the DP-ConSE algorithm is deferred to an appendix, leaving practical performance under realistic heterogeneity and non-stationarity untested. Open questions include whether the 'privacy is free' result extends to multi-arm settings, structured CATE models, or stronger (non-anticipating) DP definitions, and whether the seasonal non-stationarity assumption is sufficient for real-world patient arrival patterns.
