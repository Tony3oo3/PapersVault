## Problem

Existing multi-fidelity neural process methods share information across fidelity levels solely through encoder latent representations, leaving decoder parameters unshared. At the highest fidelity, shared representations are decoded with different parameters, producing inherently inaccurate outputs that degrade severely in out-of-distribution settings where highest-fidelity training data has limited domain coverage. This paper addresses the gap by explicitly incorporating decoders into the cross-fidelity information-sharing mechanism.

## Input / Output

The method takes K fidelity levels of context data, where each fidelity provides input-output pairs at a different spatial resolution (e.g., 16×16 through 128×128 meshes for PDEs, or 80×96 through 721×1440 grids for climate). Inputs are low-dimensional numerical parameters (boundary conditions, thermal diffusivity, or 12 climate driver variables), while outputs are high-dimensional spatial fields. At inference, the model produces a high-fidelity spatial-field prediction decomposed into an aggregated lower-fidelity component plus a learned residual, along with prediction uncertainty from the latent variable posterior.

## Method

MFRNP trains a separate Neural Process surrogate at each lower fidelity level, then at the highest fidelity K it models the residual R(x) = f_K(x) − (1/(K−1))Σ f̂_k(x) between ground truth and the averaged lower-fidelity predictions. The key architectural insight is that cross-fidelity information is shared through decoded outputs rather than latent representations: during training, lower-fidelity surrogates are evaluated at the highest-fidelity input points via ancestral sampling, their predictions are linearly interpolated and averaged, and the residual is computed dynamically to form the highest-fidelity training set. A novel Residual-ELBO loss couples two terms—an in-fidelity ELBO for each lower level and a residual ELBO at level K—so that the highest-fidelity latent z_K depends on all lower-fidelity latents and decoder parameters simultaneously, optimizing lower-fidelity decoders for accurate residual modeling. Inference simply aggregates lower-fidelity predictions at the target input and adds the predicted residual, enabling effective extrapolation beyond the highest-fidelity training domain.

## Results

MFRNP achieves the lowest nRMSE across all 7 PDE/fluid tasks under both Full and OOD settings, outperforming the best baseline (D-MFD) by an average of ~90%. In the Full setting, MFRNP reaches nRMSE of 0.005 on Heat2, 0.0039 on Heat3, 0.0046 on Poisson5, and 0.129 on Fluid, compared to D-MFD's 0.015, 0.108, 0.279, and 0.207 respectively. Under the OOD setting, the gap widens further: MFRNP achieves 0.005 on Heat2 vs. D-MFD's 0.213, and 0.013 on Poisson5 vs. D-MFD's 0.375. On the climate modeling task, MFRNP outperforms all baselines in 3 of 4 emission scenarios (perfect model test) and maintains consistent accuracy from 2017–2021 on ERA5-reanalysis data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MFRNP dominates all six baselines across every task and setting. Against the strongest multi-fidelity NP baseline D-MFD, MFRNP reduces nRMSE by 67–98% (e.g., 0.005 vs. 0.015 on Heat2, 0.0046 vs. 0.279 on Poisson5), with the gap widening dramatically under OOD conditions (0.005 vs. 0.213 on Heat2-OOD). MFHNP, which uses hierarchical latent passing, is outperformed by 81–98% (e.g., 0.0039 vs. 0.111 on Heat3), confirming that decoder-inclusive aggregation is critical. DMF and NARGP, representing earlier deep-learning and GP-based multi-fidelity approaches, are beaten by 96–98% on most tasks. The single-fidelity NP (SF-NP) serves as a lower bound and is outperformed by 98%+ on all tasks, validating the multi-fidelity framework. On the climate modeling task, MFRNP outperforms NNGPR (deep kernel GP) and DeepESD (Gaussian CNN) in 3 of 4 emission scenarios, while NARGP and DMF fail to scale to the 9-fidelity global dataset due to memory constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

MFRNP builds on the Neural Process framework (Garnelo et al., 2018) and the multi-fidelity hierarchical NP lineage (MFHNP, Wu et al. 2022; D-MFD, Wu et al. 2023), which share encoded latent representations across fidelity levels. The key departure is architectural: rather than passing encoder outputs up a hierarchy, MFRNP aggregates decoded predictions from lower-fidelity surrogates and models the residual at the highest fidelity, thereby incorporating decoder parameters into cross-fidelity information sharing. The novel Residual-ELBO loss is designed to make the highest-fidelity latent variable depend on all lower-fidelity latent variables and decoder parameters simultaneously, addressing the 'varying decoder' problem that limits prior latent-sharing approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~90% average nRMSE improvement over state-of-the-art baselines, particularly in out-of-distribution settings where high-fidelity data has limited domain coverage, is practically significant for climate modeling and PDE simulation where expensive high-fidelity runs are scarce. The successful scaling to 9 fidelities on a global-scale climate task (721×1440 ERA5-reanalysis) demonstrates that the residual formulation avoids the error propagation and dimensionality issues of hierarchical latent-space methods. Open questions include whether the uniform averaging strategy can be replaced by learned or physics-informed aggregation, and whether the doubled inference cost from ancestral sampling becomes prohibitive for computationally expensive encoders/decoders such as neural ODEs.
