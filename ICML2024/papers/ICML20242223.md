## Problem

Existing clinical outcome prediction models assume identical data sources are available at both training and inference, but real-world deployments often have multiple sources (e.g., a dialysis provider's EHR and a national registry) where only a subset is accessible at inference due to security, privacy, or portability constraints. No prior framework addresses how to leverage the full set of sources during model development while restricting inference to a single source.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives longitudinal tabular covariates from a single data source (e.g., 89 vital-sign/lab features or 620 diagnosis/procedure codes sampled monthly, or 80 lab values and 12 procedure codes sampled hourly in MIMIC). The classifier additionally consumes frozen encoder embeddings as a skip connection alongside the raw covariates. The output is a binary mortality probability within a configurable future horizon M (ranging from 0.5 to 5 years in the Private dataset, or 168 hours in MIMIC).

## Method

CLOPPS uses a two-stage pipeline. In stage 1, two identical attention-based encoders (multi-head self-attention with causal masking and rotary position embeddings) are pretrained via contrastive learning with three complementary losses: L_M aligns same-time-point observations across the two sources in latent space; L_L captures local temporal similarity within a window of w steps, weighted by a leave-one-out Kaplan-Meier survival function to emphasize outcome-relevant information; and L_F (inspired by Contrastive Predictive Coding) encourages each embedding to encode future information via a log-bilinear scoring function over a fixed temporal distance d. In stage 2, the encoders are frozen and their outputs are concatenated with raw covariates and fed into a classifier (MLP for cross-sectional, or OPT/RWKV for longitudinal) trained with standard cross-entropy on the single-source data. The key insight is that contrastive alignment forces each encoder to implicitly encode information from the other source, so at inference the single-source embedding carries cross-source signal without requiring the absent source.

## Results

On the Private real-world dataset (40,752 ESRD patients), CLOPPS with an RWKV classifier consistently outperforms all five baselines across all prediction horizons (M = 0.5, 1, 3, 5 years) and both data sources (dialysis provider and national data system) during inference. In the full-data-source setting (Table 7), CLOPPS(RWKV) achieves AUC of 0.8220 (M=0.5), 0.7979 (M=1), 0.7873 (M=3), and 0.8028 (M=5), with AP values of 0.3960, 0.4930, 0.7291, and 0.8315 respectively. On MIMIC-III (20,784 ICU patients, 168-hour mortality prediction), CLOPPS again consistently outperforms all baselines regardless of which data source (labs or procedures) is available at inference. On the synthetic Moving MNIST dataset, CLOPPS(MLP) achieves AUC 0.8855/0.8839 (top/bottom half) at M=2 frames, outperforming the MLP baseline (0.8817/0.8783) and RoFormer (0.8001/0.7712).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLOPPS outperforms all five baselines (Elastic Net, MLP, RoFormer, OPT, RWKV) in the primary partial-data-source setting on both the Private and MIMIC-III datasets, across all horizons and both data sources. Elastic Net, the weakest baseline, is outperformed by the largest margins (e.g., AUC gap of ~0.03–0.05 on the Private dataset at M=1). MLP is the strongest cross-sectional baseline but is consistently surpassed by CLOPPS by 0.5–2% AUC and 1–5% AP. Among longitudinal baselines, RoFormer (which shares the same encoder architecture as CLOPPS) is outperformed by CLOPPS by ~1–2% AUC, confirming that the contrastive pretraining provides the key advantage rather than architecture. OPT and RWKV baselines are competitive with each other but are both outperformed by CLOPPS with the same classifier backbone, demonstrating that the cross-source contrastive embeddings add value beyond what a single-source longitudinal model can capture. Notably, even in the idealized full-data-source setting (Table 7), CLOPPS matches or exceeds all baselines, indicating the learned representations do not degrade performance when additional data is available.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLOPPS builds on the SimCLR-style contrastive loss (Chen et al., 2020) and Contrastive Predictive Coding (Oord et al., 2018), but repurposes them for a fundamentally different alignment objective: matching representations across distinct data sources rather than across augmentations of a single source. Prior contrastive learning for medical time series (Tonekaboni et al., 2021; Yèche et al., 2021) learns representations within a single data source using temporal windows or neighborhood coding, whereas CLOPPS explicitly aligns cross-source embeddings so that either source alone can carry information from both. The supervised prediction stage is agnostic to architecture, plugging into existing longitudinal models (RWKV, OPT, RoFormer) or simple MLPs, positioning the contribution as a pretraining layer rather than a new end-to-end architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underexplored deployment barrier in clinical ML: national or retrospective data systems (e.g., USRDS) are available for model development but inaccessible at point-of-care inference, limiting model portability. CLOPPS demonstrates that contrastive cross-source pretraining consistently improves single-source prediction across three datasets, multiple horizons, and several classifier backbones, and substantially outperforms the naive imputation alternative. Limitations include restriction to tabular data, exactly two sources, and a single outcome (no competing risks); the finding that fine-tuning the encoder jointly with the classifier yields no additional gains over the frozen-encoder two-stage approach is a useful practical simplification. Open questions include extension to more than two sources, non-tabular modalities (imaging, text), and whether the approach generalizes to settings where sources are only partially overlapping or temporally misaligned.
