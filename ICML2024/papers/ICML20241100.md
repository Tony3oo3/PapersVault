## Problem

Object completion—reconstructing a full object from its partially visible, occluded portion—remains challenging because existing conditional generation methods (ControlNet, Stable Diffusion) lack reliable shape guidance and produce artifacts or misaligned completions. The paper identifies that the quality of mask-conditioned generation is directly governed by the quality of the conditioned mask, yet in practice only an incomplete (partial) mask is available, creating a chicken-and-egg problem between shape guidance and generation fidelity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB image containing a partially occluded object (cropped so the object's longest side is 360 px), the corresponding binary partial mask, and a short text prompt (typically the object class label) as input. It outputs a complete object image and a refined complete object mask after 5 iterative mask denoising (IMD) steps, each sampling N=5 images. The partial object is placed on a black background to suppress irrelevant background generation, and SAM receives a mix of box and point prompts derived from the partial object's bounding box for segmentation.

## Method

MaskComp introduces an Iterative Mask Denoising (IMD) process that alternates a generation stage (CompNet) and a segmentation stage (SAM) to progressively refine a noisy partial mask into a complete mask, framed as Gibbs-sampling-like inference over the joint distribution p(I, M). CompNet is a modified ControlNet built on frozen Stable Diffusion that adds a time-variant gating mechanism to modulate the influence of partial-object features across diffusion timesteps, plus an auxiliary FPN-based mask decoder supervised by Dice+BCE loss to encourage shape-aware encoding. In each IMD step, CompNet generates N complete-object images conditioned on the current mask; SAM segments each generated image, and a voting function (threshold τ=0.5 on the averaged mask probability) aggregates the N masks into a single refined mask for the next iteration. The key architectural difference from ControlNet is that CompNet is trained to recover the full object rather than reproduce the condition, and the iterative loop treats the partial mask as a noisy observation of the complete mask, with the S∘G composition acting as a mask denoiser.

## Results

On the AHP dataset, MaskComp achieves a FID-G of 16.9 and FID-S of 21.3, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline (Stable Diffusion 2.1) which scores 30.8 and 39.9 respectively. On DYCE, MaskComp reaches FID-G 20.0 and FID-S 25.4 versus 30.0 and 41.1 for Stable Diffusion 2.1. In the user study, MaskComp attains an average rank of 2.1 (AHP) and 1.9 (DYCE) with 53% and 63% of samples rated best, compared to 3.1/3.0 rank and 14%/12% best-rate for the next-best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaskComp decisively outperforms all three baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against ControlNet (FID-G 40.2/42.4), MaskComp reduces FID-G by 58% on AHP and 53% on DYCE, while also improving the user-study best-rate from 10%/8% to 53%/63%. Compared to Kandinsky 2.1 (FID-G 43.9/44.3), the gap is even wider at 61%/55% FID-G reduction. The closest competitor is Stable Diffusion 2.1 (FID-G 30.8/30.0), which MaskComp still beats by 45% on AHP and 33% on DYCE in FID-G, and by a factor of ~4× in the best-rate metric (0.53 vs. 0.14 on AHP; 0.63 vs. 0.12 on DYCE). The paper attributes these gains to the mask-conditioned generation providing robust shape guidance and the iterative mask denoising process progressively refining the initial partial mask into a near-complete shape before final generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaskComp builds directly on ControlNet's architecture for mask-conditioned diffusion generation and Stable Diffusion as the base generative model, but fundamentally redeploys them for a different objective: rather than faithfully reproducing the conditioned shape (ControlNet's goal), CompNet is trained to *complete* objects beyond the visible region. The iterative mask denoising (IMD) process draws a deliberate analogy to diffusion denoising—treating the partial mask as a noisy signal and the S∘G composition as a denoiser—while the alternating generation/segmentation loop is framed as a Gibbs-sampling-like procedure over the joint p(I,M). For segmentation, the paper leverages SAM as an off-the-shelf tool, and positions itself against amodal segmentation (Tran et al., 2022) as a more direct baseline for the mask-completion sub-problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IMD framework offers a principled, iterative mechanism for progressively refining shape guidance in generative models, which could generalize beyond object completion to other tasks where the conditioning signal is initially incomplete or noisy. A key practical limitation is inference cost: each IMD step requires N diffusion generations plus N segmentation calls (~15.5 s per step on a V100), making the full 5-step pipeline slow for interactive use. The paper also shows that training without ground-truth complete objects (using OpenImage) only slightly degrades performance, suggesting the approach could be adapted to real-world settings where complete references are unavailable. Open questions include whether the Gibbs-sampling analogy can be made more theoretically rigorous, whether the unimodal mask-distribution property holds for non-rigid or multi-instance objects, and how the method scales to 3D or video completion.
