## Problem

In decentralized learning, protecting user data against curious peers requires local differential privacy (LDP), which incurs an n-fold penalty in the privacy-utility trade-off compared to central DP (CDP). Existing relaxations like Network DP only match CDP for well-connected graphs, leaving a gap for arbitrary topologies. This paper asks whether a weaker-than-LDP but stronger-than-CDP privacy notion can recover the optimal CDP trade-off on any connected graph while still protecting all communications.

## Input / Output

Each of n users holds a local dataset of m samples and participates as a node on an undirected communication graph; before training begins, every pair of connected users exchanges a shared randomness seed via one round of encrypted communication. The algorithm takes as hyperparameters a clipping threshold C, two noise variances (σ_cdp for uncorrelated noise and σ_cor for correlated noise), and a symmetric doubly-stochastic mixing matrix W. The output is the trained model minimizing the global empirical loss, accompanied by a (ε,δ)-SecLDP guarantee computed via a privacy accountant that inverts a modified graph Laplacian (σ_cdp²I + σ_cor²L).

## Method

DECOR is a variant of decentralized SGD that injects two types of Gaussian noise into each user's clipped stochastic gradient before the gossip-averaging step: (i) pairwise-canceling correlated noise terms v_ij = −v_ji ~ N(0, σ_cor²I) for each neighbor j, and (ii) independent uncorrelated noise v̄_i ~ N(0, σ_cdp²I). The correlated noise is generated from the shared randomness seeds so that each edge's noise cancels when the two endpoints average their models; on a fully connected graph all correlated noise vanishes after one gossip step, but on sparse graphs a residual remains. The key theoretical contribution is bounding the accumulation of this non-canceling correlated noise across T iterations, showing it contributes only a non-dominant O(1/T²) term (scaled by a graph heterogeneity quantity H_G(W)) to the convergence rate, while the dominant O(1/T) or O(1/√T) term depends only on σ_cdp². The privacy analysis, conducted in the Rényi DP framework, shows that the per-step privacy loss scales as 1/(nσ_cdp²) + 1/(σ_cdp² + a(G)σ_cor²), where a(G) is the algebraic connectivity, yielding the optimal CDP trade-off O(d/(n²ε²)) under the new SecLDP definition for arbitrary connected graphs.

## Results

DECOR achieves an order of magnitude lower training loss than the LDP baseline across all privacy levels and network topologies (ring, grid, fully-connected) on both strongly convex tasks (least-squares regression, logistic regression on a9a) and a non-convex task (MNIST neural network). On MNIST, the gap between CDP and LDP is nearly 10 accuracy points at the lowest privacy budget, while DECOR on the ring topology is within less than 1 accuracy point of CDP. Theoretically, DECOR matches the optimal CDP privacy-utility trade-off of O(C²d log(1/δ)/(n²ε²)) under SecLDP for arbitrary connected graphs, improving by a factor of n over the LDP lower bound Ω(d/(nε²)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DECOR against two baselines: CDP (central differential privacy, the weakest threat model protecting only the final model) and LDP (local differential privacy, the strongest threat model independently obfuscating all communications). DECOR closely matches CDP performance across all three tasks, all privacy budgets, and all three topologies, with the gap being less than 1 accuracy point on MNIST even on the sparsest (ring) topology. In contrast, LDP is an order of magnitude worse in training loss on the convex tasks and nearly 10 accuracy points worse on MNIST at low privacy budgets. The theoretical privacy-utility trade-off of DECOR, O(C²d log(1/δ)/(n²ε²)), matches the CDP lower bound Ω(d/(n²ε²)) (Bassily et al., 2014) and improves by a factor of n over the LDP lower bound Ω(d/(nε²)) (Duchi et al., 2018). This is notable because prior decentralized approaches (e.g., Network DP by Cyffers et al., 2022) only match CDP for well-connected graphs like expanders, degrading to O(1/(nε²)) on ring topologies, whereas DECOR matches CDP for arbitrary connected graphs.

## Relation to Prior Work

DECOR extends the correlated Gaussian noise technique from Sabater et al. (2022), which was limited to decentralized averaging, to the full decentralized learning setting where non-canceling correlated noise accumulates across iterations. It builds on D-SGD (Koloskova et al., 2020) as the base algorithm and introduces SecLDP, a new privacy relaxation that sits between LDP and CDP by assuming shared secrets between connected user pairs. Unlike Network DP (Cyffers & Bellet, 2022), which restricts each user's view to neighbor communications and only matches CDP for well-connected graphs, SecLDP hides secrets from an adversary observing all communications and achieves the CDP-optimal trade-off for arbitrary connected topologies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DECOR closes the fundamental n-factor performance gap between LDP and CDP in decentralized learning, which has been a major limitation preventing practical private decentralized training. The mechanism is lightweight—requiring only one round of encrypted seed exchange—and the theoretical guarantee holds for any connected graph, making it broadly applicable. Limitations include the need for 2-connectivity to protect against non-colluding curious users, a convergence slowdown from residual correlated noise on sparse graphs, and empirical validation limited to n=16 users on small-scale tasks. Open questions include practical deployment at scale, handling of full collusion, and whether the SecLDP assumption of shared secrets is realistic in adversarial network environments.
