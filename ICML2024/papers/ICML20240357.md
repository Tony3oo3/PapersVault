## Problem

Existing latent diffusion model (LDM) inverse problem solvers rely on null text prompts, completely disregarding the text conditioning mechanism that is central to models like Stable Diffusion. This leads to suboptimal performance, as the wrong or absent prompt can cause dramatic quality degradation (over 10 FID in some cases). Additionally, naive latent-space optimization introduces grid-like artifacts that existing VAE regularization strategies (e.g., PSLD) cannot fully suppress.

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded measurement y obtained via a general forward operator A (e.g., ×8 average-pooling for SR, Gaussian/motion blur kernels, or binary masks for inpainting) plus mild additive Gaussian noise, and a pre-trained Stable Diffusion v1.4 checkpoint. It produces a restored 512×512 RGB image, and scales to arbitrary higher resolutions (e.g., 768×768) without patch-based processing. The text embedding is not a user-specified input but is automatically optimized on-the-fly during inference, requiring no ground-truth access.

## Method

P2L performs alternating minimization over three variables—text embedding C, latent z, and pixel x—during the reverse DDIM sampling loop. At each timestep, the prompt embedding is updated via K Adam iterations (typically 3–5) that minimize the data-fidelity loss ‖y − AD(ẑ₀|t)‖² using the DDS approximation, yielding an optimized prompt C*_t. The latent is then updated with a standard LDPS gradient step conditioned on C*_t. Every γ steps (3–5), a projection step decodes the latent to pixel space, solves a proximal problem (data fidelity + latent feasibility regularization) via conjugate gradient, and re-encodes the result, guaranteeing the clean latent stays in the encoder's range space. This projection replaces the soft VAE regularization of PSLD with an explicit hard constraint, eliminating grid artifacts while preserving data consistency.

## Results

On ImageNet 512×512, P2L achieves FID of 51.81 (SR×8), 54.11 (motion deblur), 39.10 (Gaussian deblur), and 32.82 (inpainting), with LPIPS of 0.386, 0.360, 0.325, and 0.229 respectively, while maintaining competitive PSNR (23.38, 24.79, 25.11, 21.99). On FFHQ 512×512, P2L achieves FID of 31.23 (SR×8), 28.34 (motion deblur), 30.62 (Gaussian deblur), and 26.27 (inpainting), with LPIPS as low as 0.168 for inpainting. The method delivers approximately 10 FID improvement over the best LDIS baseline in deblurring and inpainting tasks on both datasets, and substantially outperforms all pixel-domain diffusion solvers (DPS, DiffPIR, DDS, IIGDM) which degrade severely at 512×512 resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

P2L outperforms all four LDIS baselines (LDPS, GML-DPS, PSLD, LDIR) on FID and LPIPS across every task on both ImageNet and FFHQ, with the largest gaps in deblurring and inpainting (e.g., 54.11 vs. 71.12 FID for motion deblur on ImageNet vs. LDPS). Among LDIS methods, LDIR achieves the lowest PSNR (22.23 on SR×8 ImageNet) due to its smoothed Adam-like updates causing deviations from measurements, while PSLD and GML-DPS produce grid-like artifacts. TReg (Kim et al.), which optimizes the null-text embedding in CFG rather than the conditional embedding, performs worst among LDIS methods (FID 104.3 on SR×8 ImageNet), confirming that the optimization target matters. All pixel-domain diffusion solvers (DPS, DiffPIR, DDS, IIGDM) degrade catastrophically at 512×512 resolution — DiffPIR reaches FID 488.3 on SR×8 ImageNet and DDS reaches FID 203.2 — highlighting that latent-space operation is essential for high-resolution general-domain restoration. On FFHQ, P2L achieves the best FID in all four tasks (31.23, 28.34, 30.62, 26.27), though LDPS marginally edges it on PSNR for SR×8 (28.78 vs. 28.55), reflecting the perception-distortion tradeoff inherent to the VAE decoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

P2L extends the diffusion posterior sampling (DPS) framework from pixel-domain to latent-domain diffusion models, building directly on the LDM architecture of Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022) and the latent DPS formulation of PSLD/GML-DPS (Rout et al., 2023). Two key departures distinguish it: (1) it introduces on-the-fly optimization of the CLIP text embedding during reverse diffusion, a step that prior LDIS methods entirely neglected by using null or fixed prompts; and (2) it replaces PSLD's gradient-based VAE regularization with an explicit ADMM-inspired projection onto the encoder's range space, borrowing the decomposed diffusion sampling (DDS, Chung et al., 2024) approximation for the inner fidelity step. The method thus unifies prompt-tuning (from the NLP/VLM literature) with latent-space inverse problem solving in a single alternating minimization loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that text conditioning—standard in content creation but previously 'completely disregarded' in inverse problem solving—yields a consistent ~10 FID improvement over the best LDIS baselines across SR, deblurring, and inpainting at 512×512, and that a simple encoder-range-space projection outperforms quadratic-scaling patch-based methods for higher resolutions. The main limitation is computational: prompt tuning adds K forward/backward passes per denoising step, making P2L roughly 6× slower than LDPS at K=5, which limits time-critical applications. Open questions include whether the optimized embedding can be decoded into interpretable text (CLIP lacks a decoder), whether the approach transfers to encoder-decoder text models like T5/Imagen, and whether the PSNR compromise stems from SD v1.4's imperfect VAE or is fundamental to latent-space optimization.
