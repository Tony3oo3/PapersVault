## Problem

Existing OCO algorithms with arbitrary delays only minimize static regret, which is meaningless in non-stationary environments where the optimal decision drifts over time. It was unclear whether the optimal dynamic regret bounds known for the non-delayed setting (O(√(T(P_T+1)))) could be generalized to handle arbitrary delays, and how delays interact with the comparator path-length to affect achievable regret.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the algorithm plays a decision x_t ∈ K (a convex set containing the origin with diameter D) and queries ∇f_t(x_t), but only receives a set of delayed gradients {∇f_k(x_k) | k+d_k−1 = t} at the end of the round. The key structural detail is that DOGD processes these gradients one-by-one in ascending index order rather than summing them, which is critical for exploiting the In-Order property. The output is a dynamic regret guarantee of O(√(d̄T)(P_T+1)) under the In-Order assumption or O(√(dT)(P_T+1)) in the worst case, where d̄ and d are the average and maximum delay respectively.

## Method

DOGD performs a separate projected gradient descent step for each arriving delayed gradient in ascending time-stamp order, rather than aggregating all gradients received in a round as in prior work. This per-gradient update enables a novel analysis that isolates a 'comparator drift' term ∑‖u_t − u_{c_t}‖, which vanishes under the In-Order property and is bounded by O(dP_T) otherwise. Mild-OGD then runs N = O(log T) DOGD instances with geometrically spaced learning rates as experts, combined by a meta-algorithm that uses delayed Hedge (Korotin et al., 2020) to track the best expert based on their delayed surrogate losses ℓ_k(x) = ⟨∇f_k(x_k), x − x_k⟩. The surrogate loss design ensures all experts reuse the same gradient stream from the meta-algorithm, avoiding inconsistent delay patterns. A matching Ω(√(dT(P_T+1))) lower bound is established, confirming worst-case optimality.

## Results

DOGD achieves a dynamic regret bound of O(√(d̄T)·(P_T+1)) under the In-Order property and O(√(dT)·(P_T+1)) in the worst case, where d̄ and d are the average and maximum delay, T is the time horizon, and P_T is the comparator path-length. Mild-OGD improves this to O(√(d̄T·(P_T+1))) and O(√(dT·(P_T+1))) respectively, reducing the linear dependence on P_T to sublinear. A matching lower bound of Ω(√(dT·(P_T+1))) is established, proving Mild-OGD is optimal in the worst case. In the no-delay special case, both bounds reduce to the known optimal O(√(T·(P_T+1))) of Zhang et al. (2018a).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is theoretical rather than empirical. Against OGD (Zinkevich 2003), which achieves O(√T·(P_T+1)) in the non-delayed setting, DOGD magnifies the bound by a √(d̄T/T) = √d̄ factor under the In-Order property, and √d in the worst case. Against the optimal non-delayed bound O(√(T·(P_T+1))) of Zhang et al. (2018a), Mild-OGD achieves the analogous O(√(d̄T·(P_T+1))) or O(√(dT·(P_T+1))) by running multiple DOGD experts with a delayed Hedge meta-algorithm. Against the static-regret-only delayed OGD of Quanrud & Khashabi (2015), which achieves O(√(d̄T)) static regret, DOGD matches this static regret as a special case (setting all comparators equal) while additionally providing dynamic regret guarantees. The paper also notes that the black-box technique of Joulani et al. (2013) cannot directly yield dynamic regret bounds, and that Wang et al. (2021; 2023) only handle fixed known delays with restricted comparators. The established lower bound of Ω(√(dT·(P_T+1))) confirms that Mild-OGD's worst-case guarantee is information-theoretically tight, making it the first provably optimal algorithm for dynamic regret in delayed OCO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-studied but previously separate threads: OCO with arbitrary delays (Joulani et al., 2013; Quanrud & Khashabi, 2015; McMahan & Streeter, 2014), which focused exclusively on static regret, and dynamic regret minimization (Zinkevich, 2003; Zhang et al., 2018a), which assumed no delays. The key methodological departure from Quanrud & Khashabi (2015) is that DOGD processes each delayed gradient individually in arrival order rather than summing all gradients received in a round, enabling exploitation of the In-Order property. The meta-algorithm in Mild-OGD replaces the vanilla Hedge of Zhang et al. (2018a) with delayed Hedge (Korotin et al., 2020) to handle the fact that expert performance signals are themselves delayed, and uses surrogate losses to avoid inconsistent delay patterns between the meta and expert algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish both upper and lower bounds on dynamic regret under arbitrary delays, confirming that the √d multiplicative penalty over the non-delayed optimal bound is unavoidable in the worst case. The identification of 'comparator drift' as a new source of regret unique to the delayed setting is a conceptual contribution that clarifies why dynamic regret is harder than static regret under delays. Open questions include whether the O(√(d̄T)(P_T+1)) bound can be achieved without the In-Order assumption, whether the doubling trick can be replaced by continuous learning-rate adaptation (à la Joulani et al., 2016), and how to handle anonymous delays where time-stamps are unavailable.
