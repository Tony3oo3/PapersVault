## Problem

Mainstream recommendation models exhibit poor scalability: increasing embedding size fails to improve performance and can even degrade it, despite embeddings being the dominant parameter component (>92% of parameters). The paper identifies the root cause as embedding collapse—learned embedding matrices occupy a low-dimensional subspace—and reveals a two-sided effect of feature interaction: it both drives collapse by constraining gradient updates along dominant singular directions, and simultaneously provides essential overfitting resistance that cannot be removed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240671/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Models consume N sparse categorical fields (e.g., 39 fields for Criteo, 22 for Avazu) with per-field cardinalities ranging from 4 to 1.61M, each mapped through an embedding table E_i ∈ R^{D_i×K} to a K-dimensional vector. The interaction module I combines all N field embeddings into a scalar or vector h, which a postprocessing module F (MLP/MoE) maps to a binary CTR probability. The scaling axis is the embedding dimension K (base 10, scaled to 2x–10x), and the key diagnostic is the singular value spectrum of each E_i, quantified by the proposed information abundance metric IA(E) = ‖σ‖₁/‖σ‖∞.

## Method

The paper proposes multi-embedding (ME), which scales the number of independent embedding sets M rather than the per-set embedding size K, and assigns each set its own dedicated feature interaction module I^(m). The combined feature is the average h = (1/M)Σ h^(m), requiring nonlinearity within I^(m) to avoid degenerating to a single-embedding model. Theoretically, the interaction-collapse theory shows that gradient updates decompose into field-specific terms θ_i = Σ_k α_{i,k} σ_{i,k} v_{i,k}, so low-information-abundance fields impose imbalanced spectral weighting that collapses all interacting embeddings; multi-embedding breaks this coupling by letting each set learn distinct interaction patterns, increasing embedding-set diversity (measured via principal angles) and thus overall information abundance. Empirically, ME yields consistent AUC gains up to 10x scaling across six architectures (DCNv2, DNN, IPNN, NFwFM, xDeepFM, FinalMLP) on Criteo and Avazu, and has been deployed in Tencent's WeChat Moments advertising system with a 3.9% GMV lift.

## Results

On Criteo, multi-embedding (ME) DCNv2 achieves 0.81385 test AUC at 10× size, outperforming its single-embedding (SE) counterpart (0.81357) and all other SE baselines. On Avazu, ME NFwFM reaches 0.78974 AUC at 10×, the highest among all configurations. Across all six models and both datasets, ME consistently improves with model size while SE models either plateau or degrade (e.g., SE DNN drops from 0.81228 to 0.81142 on Criteo at 10×). The paper reports an average AUC improvement of 0.00110 for the largest ME models over their SE baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares multi-embedding (ME) against single-embedding (SE) versions of six mainstream CTR prediction models: DNN, IPNN, NFwFM, xDeepFM, DCNv2, and FinalMLP, plus AutoInt in the appendix. ME wins over SE at 10× size across all models and both datasets: on Criteo, ME DCNv2 gains +0.00028 AUC over SE DCNv2 (0.81385 vs. 0.81357), ME xDeepFM gains +0.00183 (0.81299 vs. 0.81116), and ME DNN gains +0.00145 (0.81287 vs. 0.81142). On Avazu, the gaps are even larger, with ME NFwFM gaining +0.00175 (0.78974 vs. 0.78799) and ME IPNN gaining +0.00204 (0.78949 vs. 0.78745). The critical distinction is scalability: SE models for DNN, xDeepFM, and FinalMLP actually lose performance when scaled to 10× (e.g., SE DNN drops 0.00086 on Criteo), while ME models consistently improve monotonically with size. At base size, ME already matches or slightly exceeds SE for most models, confirming that the multi-embedding design does not sacrifice small-model performance. The paper also notes that ME DCNv2 was deployed in Tencent's WeChat Moments advertising system, yielding a 3.9% GMV lift in online A/B testing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the collapse/representation-degeneration literature (neural collapse, dimensional collapse in contrastive learning) to a setting not previously studied: field-level feature interaction in recommendation models. Rather than proposing new interaction modules (as the long line of FM/DCNv2/AutoInt work does), it takes an analytical stance on existing architectures, identifying a specific mechanism—interaction-collapse theory—whereby low-information-abundance embeddings in one field constrain the learning of others. The proposed multi-embedding design is a direct transplant of the multi-head/group-convolution decoupling pattern from vision and NLP into the recommendation setting, combined with embedding-set-specific interaction modules to preserve the overfitting resistance that feature interaction provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete, previously unrecognized bottleneck (embedding collapse) that explains why naively scaling recommendation models fails, and provides a simple, broadly applicable fix validated across six mainstream architectures and two benchmark datasets. The two-sided effect of feature interaction—simultaneously causing collapse and preventing overfitting—is a nuanced finding that cautions against the intuitive remedy of removing interaction. Open questions include whether the interaction-collapse theory and multi-embedding remedy extend to sequential recommendation, graph-based models, or generative recommendation paradigms, and whether the modest AUC gains (0.001–0.002) observed on public benchmarks translate to the larger improvements seen in production (3.9% GMV lift at Tencent).
