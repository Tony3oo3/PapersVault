## Problem

Diffusion models struggle to accurately follow complex text prompts that require composing multiple objects with distinct attributes and spatial relationships into a single coherent image. Existing layout-based and attention-guidance methods provide only rough spatial constraints and fail on overlapped objects, while feedback-based approaches incur additional training costs. The paper seeks a training-free framework that can decompose and precisely control compositional generation without modifying the diffusion backbone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242340/cropped_page1_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the input is a single complex text prompt (e.g., describing multiple entities with attributes and spatial relations); for editing, it is a source RGB image paired with a target text prompt. The MLLM (GPT-4 in experiments) internally decomposes the prompt into a base prompt and n subprompts, then plans a region grid specified as rows separated by semicolons and columns by comma-delimited integers (e.g., "1,1;2,1"). The output is a single compositional RGB image (or edited image) where each planned subregion is generated independently and fused with the base-prompt latent via a tunable weighting coefficient β. The framework is fully training-free and compatible with arbitrary diffusion backbones (SDXL, ConPreDiff, ControlNet) and MLLM architectures (GPT-4, Llama 2, Vicuna, MiniGPT-4).

## Method

RPG (Recaption, Plan and Generate) is a training-free pipeline with three stages. First, a multimodal LLM recaptions the complex prompt by extracting key-phrase subprompts and expanding each into a denser, more descriptive caption to improve diffusion fidelity. Second, the MLLM performs chain-of-thought planning to partition the image canvas into complementary non-overlapping rectangular subregions and assign each recaptioned subprompt to a specific region, using carefully designed in-context examples that enforce numeric accuracy and attribute-level decomposition. Third, complementary regional diffusion generates a latent for each subprompt in parallel via standard cross-attention at every denoising timestep, resizes each latent to its assigned region dimensions, concatenates them spatially, and fuses the result with the base-prompt latent through a weighted sum (β·z_base + (1−β)·z_cat) to preserve global coherence. Unlike layout-based methods that rigidly constrain objects to bounding boxes, RPG allows object positions and sizes to adapt freely during generation, and extends to editing via contour-based mask-and-inpainting with multi-round closed-loop MLLM feedback for progressive self-refinement.

## Results

On T2I-CompBench, RPG achieves the best score across all six compositional metrics: Color (0.8743), Shape (0.6927), Texture (0.8506), Spatial (0.4781), Non-Spatial (0.3697), and Complex (0.5775). The largest margin over the strongest baseline (ConPreDiff) is in Spatial relationship (0.4781 vs. 0.2362, +100%) and Complex composition (0.5775 vs. 0.4184, +38%). RPG also substantially outperforms general-purpose models like SDXL (Complex: 0.5775 vs. 0.4091) and DALL-E 2 (Complex: 0.5775 vs. 0.3696).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RPG outperforms all 10 quantitative baselines on every T2I-CompBench metric. Against the strongest compositional baseline ConPreDiff (NeurIPS 2023), RPG gains +0.1724 on Color, +0.1290 on Shape, +0.1485 on Texture, +0.2419 on Spatial, +0.0502 on Non-Spatial, and +0.1591 on Complex. Against general-purpose SOTA models SDXL and PixArt-α, RPG leads by +0.1684 and +0.1658 on Complex composition respectively, with the Spatial gap being even larger (+0.2749 over SDXL, +0.2699 over PixArt-α). Among attention/layout-based methods (Structured Diffusion, Attn-Exctv2, Composable Diffusion), RPG's Spatial score (0.4781) is 2–6× higher than theirs (0.0800–0.1455), confirming that complementary regional diffusion handles spatial relationships far more effectively than cross-attention manipulation. For text-guided image editing, RPG is compared qualitatively against Prompt2Prompt, InstructPix2Pix, and MasaCtrl, producing more precise edits while better preserving source-image structure. DALL-E 3 and LMD+ are compared qualitatively in Figure 7, where RPG shows superior handling of numeric accuracy and complex multi-entity relationships.

## Relation to Prior Work

RPG departs from two main lines of compositional text-to-image work: (1) layout/attention-based methods (GLIGEN, LMD+, Attend-and-Excite, Structured Diffusion) that impose fixed spatial constraints or manipulate cross-attention maps, and (2) feedback/reward-based methods (GORS, ImageReward) that require additional training. Rather than treating the MLLM as a simple layout generator (as in LMD or LayoutGPT), RPG repurposes MLLM chain-of-thought reasoning as a global spatial planner that partitions image space into complementary subregions, combined with a novel resize-and-concatenate regional diffusion that does not rigidly constrain objects to their assigned regions. The closed-loop unification of generation and editing via iterative MLLM feedback is a further departure from prior one-shot generation pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature and the substantial T2I-CompBench gains (especially ~2× on spatial relationships over the previous best) make RPG a practical and strong baseline for compositional generation, and its modularity across MLLMs and diffusion backbones increases its utility. However, the framework's dependence on GPT-4 for recaptioning and CoT planning introduces cost and access constraints, and the rectangular region decomposition limits expressiveness for highly overlapping or irregular compositions. Open questions include whether hierarchical regional diffusion scales to arbitrarily complex scenes, whether the approach transfers to video generation, and whether smaller open-source MLLMs can match GPT-4's planning quality without significant degradation.
