## Problem

Existing conceptual counterfactual explanation (CE) methods represent image semantics as flat sets of concepts, which under-represents edit distances (e.g., treating one cyclist identically to many) and discards relational information critical for interpretable explanations (e.g., the 'wearing' link between a rider and a helmet). Additionally, the natural similarity measure for structured semantics—Graph Edit Distance (GED)—is NP-hard, making exhaustive pairwise computation infeasible for CE retrieval over large candidate pools.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240433/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query image (or audio clip) with its scene graph (objects as nodes, relations as edges), a black-box classifier, and a pool of candidate instances with their own scene graphs. It outputs a counterfactual instance from a different class along with the full graph edit path expressed as triples (concept-edge-concept insertions, deletions, and substitutions), plus a ranked list of candidates by semantic-graph proximity. Notably, the method works with both human-annotated and automatically generated scene graphs, and a 'blind' evaluation variant shows that humans can learn to classify from graph edits alone without seeing any images.

## Method

The core contribution is a siamese GNN (GCN, GAT, or GIN variant) that maps scene graphs into a shared embedding space where cosine similarity approximates GED, enabling efficient counterfactual retrieval without solving the NP-hard graph matching problem for all N-1 candidate pairs. Training is transductive: a random subset of N/2 graph pairs is used, with ground-truth GED computed via an LSAP-based algorithm whose node/edge edit costs are derived from WordNet shortest-path distances; the loss is a Multi-Dimensional Scaling objective that preserves inter-graph distances in the embedding. Node features are initialized with GloVe vectors to encode semantic similarity. At inference, all candidate graphs are embedded once, and the counterfactual is retrieved as the highest-ranked embedding belonging to a different class, with GED computed only once per query to produce the final edit path. GCN consistently outperforms GAT and GIN, attributed to the small hop depth (3–4) of the scene graphs where local neighborhood aggregation is most informative.

## Results

On CUB, the proposed GCN-based method achieves P@1 of 0.19 and binary P@4 of 0.49, substantially outperforming CVE (P@1 = 0.02, binary P@4 = 0.11). The method also produces the fewest average edits (10.5 total vs. 13.13 for CVE and 11.73 for SC). On Visual Genome (VG-DENSE), GCN-70K reaches P@1 = 0.25 and binary P@4 = 0.49, surpassing the Pyramid Match kernel (P@1 = 0.13) and SC (P@1 = 0.25, tied). Human evaluation confirms superiority: annotators prefer the proposed CEs 48.86% of the time over SC and 48.42% over CVE, and machine-teaching test accuracy reaches 93.88% (visually-informed) and 89.28% (blind), both exceeding CVE's 82.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CVE (Vandenhende et al., 2022), a white-box pixel-level method, the proposed approach wins decisively on all quantitative metrics: P@1 improves from 0.02 to 0.19, binary P@4 from 0.11 to 0.49, and total edits drop from 13.13 to 10.50 on CUB. Human preference also strongly favors the proposed method (48.42% win vs. 26.27% lose), and machine-teaching accuracy is 93.88% vs. CVE's 82.1%. Against SC (Dervakos et al., 2023), the prior black-box conceptual method, the proposed approach achieves lower average GED on Visual Genome (122.41 vs. 128.67 on VG-DENSE) and fewer total edits on CUB (10.50 vs. 11.73), while human preference shows a 48.86% win rate versus only 19.32% loss (chi-square p = 0.003). Against the Pyramid Match graph kernel (Grauman & Darrell, 2007), GCN-70K dominates on VG-DENSE ranking metrics (P@1: 0.25 vs. 0.13; binary P@4: 0.49 vs. 0.26), confirming that learned GNN embeddings outperform deterministic kernel-based similarity for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the semantic counterfactual framework of Dervakos et al. (2023) (SC) by replacing its set-of-concepts representation with scene graphs, thereby preserving inter-concept relationships that set-based edit distances discard. For efficient graph similarity, it adapts the siamese GNN and Multi-Dimensional Scaling loss from Bai et al. (2019), originally designed for unsupervised graph-level representation learning, to the specific problem of approximating GED for counterfactual retrieval. The lineage traces back to Filandrianos et al. (2022), which first established the model-agnostic, ontology-guided edit-cost paradigm that this paper retains while upgrading the underlying data structure from sets to graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By structuring semantics as graphs and leveraging GNNs to bypass the NP-hard all-pairs GED computation, this work makes graph-based counterfactual explanations practically feasible for large candidate pools, while producing explanations that are more expressive, actionable, and human-interpretable than both pixel-level (CVE) and set-based (SC) alternatives. The 'blind' machine-teaching experiment—where humans classify images at 89% accuracy using only scene graphs and edits, without seeing any images—suggests that semantic structure alone is nearly sufficient for conveying discriminative logic, a finding with implications for how explanations should be designed. Open questions include robustness to noisy or automatically generated scene graphs, scalability to much larger graph pools, and whether unsupervised GNN variants can eliminate the need for GED-labeled training pairs entirely.
