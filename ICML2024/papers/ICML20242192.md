## Problem

Passive RL aims to leverage abundant action-free, reward-free data (e.g., videos) to accelerate online reinforcement learning, but it remains unclear when such data is statistically beneficial and how to practically exploit it for long-horizon tasks. Existing methods like HIQL and AF-Guide use only a single planning horizon, making them myopic or unable to handle the distributional shift between offline passive data and online exploration, which causes severe extrapolation errors in value estimation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state observations (8-DoF or 9-DoF continuous state vectors in AntMaze/Kitchen, or 64×64×3 RGB images in Procgen Maze) paired with a goal state, and produces a continuous or discrete action at each step. The passive pretraining dataset contains only state sequences with no action or reward labels. For visual-input environments, both the SCP planning and intrinsic reward computation operate in a learned 10-dimensional latent goal space rather than raw pixel space, and the goal representation is trained solely from value-function gradients without auxiliary reconstruction or contrastive losses.

## Method

MSCP pretrains a goal-conditioned value function via GCAF-IQL (expectile regression) and extracts two state-centric planners via advantage-weighted regression: a fast 1-SCP predicting the immediate next state and a slow h-SCP (h=25 or 3) predicting a subgoal at moderate distance. The fast SCP serves as a pessimistic regularizer: because AWR training constrains its predictions to the dataset support, the L2 distance between the actual next state and the fast SCP's prediction yields an intrinsic reward that penalizes the low-level policy for exploring out-of-distribution states. The slow SCP provides long-term navigation guidance by conditioning the low-level policy and its advantage on the predicted subgoal rather than the ultimate goal. The low-level policy is trained online with a combined AWR objective that weights the fast SCP's one-step advantage (pessimistic regularization + locomotion guidance) and the slow SCP's value-difference advantage (navigation guidance) via a balancing hyperparameter C^f, while the pretrained value function is jointly fine-tuned on mixed offline/online batches to prevent catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

MSCP achieves normalized scores of 92–97 on AntMaze umaze/medium tasks and 55–81 on large/ultra tasks, consistently outperforming all passive RL baselines. On the hardest AntMaze-Ultra-Diverse task, MSCP scores 80.8±10.3 versus HIQL's 26.9±17.4 and AF-Guide's 0.0. On Procgen-Maze-500 (limited coverage), MSCP reaches 77.7±8.3 (train) and 62.3±9.3 (test), surpassing HIQL's 60.4±12.1 and 48.1±8.7 respectively. Pure online RL (SAC) fails to solve any AntMaze task (score 0), underscoring the critical benefit of passive pretraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSCP decisively outperforms HIQL (Park et al., 2023) across all 11 tasks, with the largest gaps on harder long-horizon tasks: 94.2 vs. 49.6 on AntMaze-Large-Diverse and 80.8 vs. 26.9 on AntMaze-Ultra-Diverse. HIQL's performance degrades sharply as maze size increases, confirming its susceptibility to extrapolation error from distributional shift. ICVF (Ghosh et al., 2023) fails completely on all AntMaze and Kitchen tasks (score 0), and only marginally contributes on CALVIN (44.9 vs. MSCP's 65.0). AF-Guide (Zhu et al., 2023) solves only the two easiest umaze tasks and scores 0 on all others, demonstrating that a single one-step planner is insufficient for long-horizon planning. On Procgen-Maze-500, where dataset coverage is narrowest, MSCP's advantage over HIQL is most pronounced (77.7 vs. 60.4 train; 62.3 vs. 48.1 test), validating the effectiveness of pessimistic regularization under limited coverage. MSCP approaches the offline RL oracle (which has access to action labels) on most tasks, e.g., 96.9 vs. 87.8 on AntMaze-Umaze-Diverse, suggesting that passive pretraining can match or exceed what is achievable with annotated data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the passive RL line of work initiated by Seo et al. (2022) and developed by HIQL (Park et al., 2023) and ICVF (Ghosh et al., 2023), which pretrain on action-free data to aid downstream online RL. The key departure is the introduction of multiscale state-centric planners: a fast 1-SCP that provides pessimistic regularization (a novel use of a one-step planner as a distributional constraint) and a slow h-SCP for long-horizon subgoal planning, building on the hierarchical decomposition of HIQL and the value pretraining of GCAF-IQL (itself derived from IQL). The theoretical analysis under identifiable dynamics generalizes the deterministic-dynamics assumption used in prior passive RL work, providing a provable horizon-reduction guarantee that no previous method in this setting offered.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical justification for why passive data helps in online RL (reducing effective horizon by a factor of 1−γ) and a practical algorithm that consistently outperforms single-scale baselines on long-horizon navigation and manipulation tasks. The finding that the fast SCP naturally prioritizes locomotion while the slow SCP handles navigation offers useful intuition for hierarchical policy design. Limitations include the reliance on identifiable dynamics for theory, the goal-conditioned formulation that sidesteps reward learning, and the fact that the pessimistic regularization is somewhat environment-specific (L2 distance in state space). Open questions include whether the multiscale approach extends to non-identifiable or partially observable dynamics, and whether the fast SCP's role as a regularizer generalizes beyond the AWR-style extraction.
