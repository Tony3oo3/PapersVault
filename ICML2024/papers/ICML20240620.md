## Problem

Existing interpretability methods for LLMs—linear probing, vocabulary-space projections (logit lens, tuned lens), and causal interventions (causal tracing, attention knockout)—each suffer from practical limitations: probing requires supervised training and predefined class sets, vocabulary projections degrade sharply in early layers and yield hard-to-interpret single-token outputs, and all methods are less expressive than natural language. The paper asks whether the LLM's own text-generation capability can be leveraged to 'translate' its hidden representations into human-readable explanations, and whether a single modular framework can unify and extend these disparate techniques.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a hidden representation h_i^l extracted at a chosen layer ℓ and token position i from a source model M running on a source prompt S, along with a user-designed target prompt T, a target model M* (which may differ from M), a target layer ℓ*, and a mapping function f (identity, affine, or learned). The output is the free-form autoregressive text (up to 20 tokens in experiments) generated by M* after the patched representation is injected at position i* in T, which can take the form of a next-token probability distribution, an extracted factual attribute, a multi-sentence entity description, or a corrected multi-hop reasoning answer. Unlike probing or logit lens, the output is not restricted to a fixed vocabulary or class set, and the representation is deliberately detached from its original source context to isolate what information it carries independently.

## Method

A Patchscope is defined by a quintuple (T, i*, f, M*, ℓ*) that specifies how to intervene on a target model's inference: the source representation f(h_i^l) replaces the target representation at position i* and layer ℓ* during M*'s forward pass on T, and the model continues generating from that point onward. The key insight is that the target prompt T acts as a 'decoder template'—by designing T to elicit a specific type of information (e.g., a few-shot token-identity prompt 'tok₁→tok₁; tok₂→tok₂;…' for next-token prediction, a relation verbalization like 'The largest city in x' for attribute extraction, or a few-shot entity-description prompt for contextualization analysis), the model's own nonlinear computation after the patch point performs the decoding without any trained probe or projection matrix. The paper demonstrates that logit lens, tuned lens, future lens, causal tracing, attention knockout, and linear probing are all recoverable as special configurations of this framework, while new configurations—token identity Patchscope, zero-shot feature extraction Patchscope, entity description Patchscope, cross-model Patchscope (patching a smaller model's representation into a larger same-family model), and a Chain-of-Thought Patchscope for multi-hop reasoning correction—achieve substantially higher accuracy, operate in early layers where prior methods fail, and require no training data.

## Results

On next-token prediction estimation across LLaMA2-13B, Vicuna-13B, GPT-J-6B, and Pythia-12B, the Token Identity Patchscope consistently outperforms Logit Lens and Tuned Lens from layer 10 onward, achieving gains of up to 98% in Precision@1 in layers 18–22. On zero-shot feature extraction across 12 commonsense and factual tasks (GPT-J-6B), the Patchscope significantly outperforms a logistic regression probe in 6 of 12 tasks (p<1e-5) and matches or exceeds it in all but one of the remaining six. On a two-step multi-hop reasoning task (Vicuna-13B, 46 samples), the CoT Patchscope achieves 50% accuracy, outperforming chain-of-thought prompting (35.71%) and vanilla generation (19.57%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Logit Lens (nostalgebraist, 2020) and Tuned Lens (Belrose et al., 2023) on next-token prediction, the Token Identity Patchscope wins from layer 10 onward across all four models tested, with the largest gap (up to 98% Precision@1 gain) in mid-to-late layers (18–22); in the first 10 layers all methods degrade, with Tuned Lens slightly ahead due to its learned affine mapping. Against the logistic regression probe (Kohn, 2015; Gupta et al., 2015) on feature extraction, the Patchscope achieves significantly higher accuracy in 6 of 12 tasks (e.g., 91.9% vs. 73.8% on substance phase, 63.8% vs. 5.1% on food-from-country) while the probe wins only on object superclass (65.6% vs. 54.8%) and is comparable on the remaining tasks. On multi-hop reasoning, the CoT Patchscope (50%) outperforms both the chain-of-thought baseline from Wei et al. (2022) at 35.71% and vanilla autoregressive generation at 19.57%, though the authors note this is a proof-of-concept leveraging known query structure rather than a general-purpose CoT replacement.

## Relation to Prior Work

Patchscopes reframes activation patching (Geiger et al., 2021; Vig et al., 2020) from a localization tool into a general 'translation' mechanism: rather than asking whether a representation is causally important, the framework patches it into a purpose-designed target prompt that elicits a natural-language decoding of its content. This single formulation subsumes vocabulary-projection methods (logit lens, tuned lens, linear shortcuts), computation-intervention methods (causal tracing, attention knockout), and probing classifiers as special cases, while opening configurations those methods cannot express—most notably, using a different (potentially larger) target model or a target prompt that encourages multi-token verbalization rather than a single-token probability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework gives interpretability researchers a principled design space (source/target prompt, model, layer, mapping function) for constructing inspection tools, making it easier to identify gaps and generate new methods. Practically, the token-identity patchscope removes the training-data requirement of tuned lens while matching or exceeding its accuracy, and the cross-model patching capability (e.g., explaining a 7B model's internals via a 13B model) is a new avenue for auditing smaller or less capable models. Key open questions include mitigating placeholder contamination in multi-token generation, extending cross-model patching beyond same-family architectures, and automating the selection of optimal source/target positions and prompts.
