## Problem

The theoretical performance of classical first-order methods under deterministic relative-error (multiplicative) gradient oracles in constrained optimization remains largely unexplored. Existing results on biased gradients are restricted to unconstrained problems or require special structural assumptions such as Polyak-Lojasiewicz or Kurdyka-Lojasiewicz conditions. This paper fills this gap by establishing convergence guarantees for the Conditional Gradient and Projected Gradient methods under relative-error oracles for both convex and nonconvex constrained problems without any error-bound type assumptions.

## Input / Output

The methods take a smooth function with L-Lipschitz gradient, a closed convex bounded feasible set C (box, general, Euclidean ball, or sign-preserving), and a deterministic gradient oracle satisfying ‖ĝ − ∇f(x)‖ ≤ ε‖∇f(x)‖ (or its coordinate-wise variant). The oracle may return different values on repeated queries at the same point, capturing adversarial, biased, or stochastic error regimes without additional assumptions. The output is a sequence of iterates with explicit convergence rate bounds that are either fully invariant to ε (box + CWEO for CG) or contain additive error terms scaling with ε·MR (general sets), where M bounds the gradient norm and R is the set diameter.

## Method

The paper analyzes two algorithmic frameworks: ECG (replacing the gradient in Frank-Wolfe with the erroneous oracle output) and EPG (replacing the gradient in projected gradient with the erroneous oracle output, augmented with a sufficient-descent acceptance criterion). The central insight is that for box constraints with a coordinate-wise erroneous oracle, the CG method is completely invariant to the relative-error magnitude because the LMO direction is unchanged by positive per-coordinate scaling of the gradient, making even arbitrarily large ε irrelevant. For general convex sets, the analysis yields additive error terms: CG achieves f(w^t) − f_opt ≤ 2εMR + 4LR²/(t+2) for convex objectives, and PG achieves an O(1/T) rate to ε-stationarity for nonconvex objectives. Two specialized variants are developed: SCG (using only the sign of the gradient for box constraints, showing the magnitude is entirely irrelevant) and RECG (a rescaled CG for convex problems over sign-preserving sets or the Euclidean ball, achieving relative guarantees of the form f(w̄^T) − θf_opt ≤ 8L(R²/θ)/(T+1) independent of the gradient norm bound M).

## Results

The paper establishes that the Conditional Gradient (CG) method is fully invariant to relative-error gradients when optimizing over the box set with a coordinate-wise erroneous oracle (CWEO): all standard convergence guarantees are preserved for any ε ∈ [0,1). For the Projected Gradient (PG) on the box with CWEO, nonconvex convergence is preserved (neglecting constants), yielding min_t ||q^{t+1} + ∇f(w^{t+1})||² ≤ 2L(3+ε)²(f⁰−f_opt)/((1−ε)²T). For general convex sets with a general EO, the ECG achieves f(w^t) − f_opt ≤ 2εMR + 4LR²/(t+2) in the convex case, and the EPG achieves min_t ||q^{t+1} + ∇f(w^{t+1})||² ≤ 8L(f⁰−f_opt)/T + ε in the nonconvex case. The RECG method on the ℓ₂-ball or sign-preserving sets achieves the relative guarantee f(w̄^T) − θf_opt ≤ 8L(R²/θ)/(T+1) where θ = (1−ε)/(1+ε).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or dataset comparisons. The paper positions its contributions relative to prior theoretical work: unlike Ajalloeian & Stich (2020) and Richtárik et al. (2021), which handle multiplicative-error oracles only for unconstrained problems or under PL/KL conditions, this paper provides guarantees for constrained optimization without such structural assumptions. Compared to Condat et al. (2022), which studies relative-error oracles only for convex PL-satisfying objectives via proximal gradient, this paper covers both convex and nonconvex objectives over general compact convex sets using CG and PG. The key comparative insight is that CG is substantially more robust to relative-error gradients than PG: CG guarantees are fully preserved on separable sets (box) with CWEO, while PG only retains nonconvex guarantees there, and for general convex sets with a general EO, no convergence guarantee for PG is established. The paper also introduces the SCG method, showing that only the sign of gradient components (not their magnitude) is needed for CG over the box, and the RECG method, which achieves a relative-error guarantee independent of the gradient norm bound M.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical Frank-Wolfe (1956) and projected gradient frameworks to the setting of deterministic relative-error gradient oracles, a norm condition originally introduced by Polyak (1987) and Carter (1991) for trust-region methods. Prior work on biased/multiplicative-error oracles has been largely confined to unconstrained problems (Ajalloeian & Stich, 2020; Richtárik et al., 2021) or requires additional structural assumptions like the Polyak-Lojasiewicz condition (Condat et al., 2022), while derivative-free methods (Berahas et al., 2022) handle constraints through fundamentally different mechanisms. The paper departs from this lineage by providing convergence guarantees for both convex and nonconvex constrained problems under a general relative-error oracle without any error-bound or PL/KL assumptions, and by introducing the sign-preserving set structure to recover relative (rather than additive) guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central insight that the Conditional Gradient method is completely invariant to relative-error magnitude on box sets (only the sign of the gradient matters) is a clean theoretical result that suggests CG may be inherently better suited than PG for distributed or compressed-gradient settings where multiplicative error is intrinsic. However, the paper is purely theoretical with no empirical validation, and the PG guarantees for convex constrained problems without PL remain an open question. Future directions include extending to stochastic EOs, incorporating Nesterov-style acceleration, and studying weaker oracle conditions such as the inner-product test of Bollapragada et al. (2018).
