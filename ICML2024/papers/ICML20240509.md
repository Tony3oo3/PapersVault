## Problem

Diffusion-based inverse problem solvers suffer from the perception-distortion tradeoff: reconstructions that look perceptually appealing often have poor PSNR/SSIM, and enforcing data consistency typically requires additional projection or guidance steps that are not guaranteed to succeed. No existing method simultaneously maintains data consistency throughout the entire reverse sampling trajectory, allows continuous trade-off between perceptual and distortion quality via a single parameter, and does so without ad-hoc correction steps.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single noisy degraded measurement ỹ = A(x₀) + z (e.g., a blurred or inpainted 256×256 RGB image with additive Gaussian noise at σ₁=0.05), the parametric degradation operator A_t(·) indexed by severity t∈[0,1], and a noise schedule σ_t. It outputs a clean reconstructed image x̂₀ = Φ_θ(y_t, t) at a user-chosen early-stopping time t_stop, and every intermediate iterate y_t along the trajectory is itself a valid, data-consistent degradation of the same underlying image. This means the user can stop at any point to obtain a reconstruction that is guaranteed consistent with the measurement, trading off detail for fidelity and speed.

## Method

Dirac models the observation as a sample from a Stochastic Degradation Process (SDP): y_t = A_t(x₀) + z_t, where A_t is a monotonically degrading deterministic operator and z_t is Gaussian noise with schedule σ_t. The reverse SDE is discretized into steps that combine an incremental reconstruction term (A_{t−Δt}(x̂₀) − A_t(x̂₀), reversing one step of degradation) with a standard denoising term, where x̂₀ = Φ_θ(y_t, t) is predicted by an NCSN++-style score network parameterized to output the clean image directly. A novel incremental reconstruction loss L_IR trains the network to simultaneously denoise and predict less-severe degradations, with a theoretical upper bound (Theorem 3.4) showing the approximation error scales with degradation smoothness, step size, and score error. Data consistency is guaranteed by construction (Theorem 3.6) because each reverse step preserves the form y_τ = A_τ(x₀) + z_τ, eliminating the need for projection. A greedy scheduling algorithm selects intermediate degradation severities to minimize the maximum pairwise image-quality distance, and an optional DPS-style guidance term provides minor additional improvement.

## Results

On FFHQ deblurring, Dirac-PO achieves the best LPIPS (0.2716) among all methods, while Dirac-DO achieves PSNR 28.47, closely matching SwinIR (28.53) and outperforming all diffusion-based baselines. On FFHQ inpainting, Dirac-DO achieves the best PSNR (26.98) and SSIM (0.8435), and Dirac-PO achieves the best LPIPS (0.2611). On ImageNet inpainting, Dirac-DO achieves the best PSNR (28.92) and SSIM (0.8958), while Dirac-PO achieves the best LPIPS (0.2079). The method also requires only 20–100 reverse diffusion steps for optimal perceptual quality, with acceptable results even at a single step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dirac outperforms DPS on all four metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID) for both deblurring and inpainting across both datasets; the gap is most pronounced on inpainting where DPS PSNR drops to 21.06 (FFHQ) and 22.71 (ImageNet) versus Dirac-DO's 26.98 and 28.92. Against DDRM, Dirac-PO wins on LPIPS and FID for deblurring, while Dirac-DO wins on PSNR and SSIM; DDRM remains competitive on FID for ImageNet inpainting (33.71 vs. Dirac-PO's 34.33). Dirac-DO closely matches the non-diffusion SwinIR on deblurring PSNR (28.47 vs. 28.53 on FFHQ) but significantly outperforms it on LPIPS (0.2972 vs. 0.3048) and FID (69.15 vs. 72.93); for inpainting, Dirac-DO surpasses SwinIR by 2.5 dB PSNR on FFHQ and 2.05 dB on ImageNet. Traditional solvers PnP-ADMM and ADMM-TV are substantially outperformed, particularly on inpainting where their PSNR collapses to 11–18 dB compared to Dirac's 25–29 dB, reflecting their inability to handle the severe information loss in masked regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dirac builds on the SDE-based score matching framework (Song et al., 2020) and denoising score matching (Vincent, 2011), but departs fundamentally from prior diffusion-based inverse problem solvers (DPS, DDRM) that rely on a pre-trained Gaussian-diffusion score model plus external projection or guidance steps to enforce data consistency. Instead, Dirac trains a score network directly on a Stochastic Degradation Process (SDP) that mirrors the actual corruption, making data consistency an intrinsic property of the reverse process rather than an add-on. The incremental reconstruction loss (L_IR) and the severity-based degradation scheduling are novel contributions that distinguish Dirac from blending-based approaches (Delbracio & Milanfar, 2023; Heitz et al., 2023) and from general-purpose corruption frameworks like Soft Diffusion (Daras et al., 2022) that target unconditional generation rather than reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The built-in data consistency guarantee (Theorem 3.6) and the continuous perception-distortion trade-off via early-stopping offer a principled, single-trajectory alternative to the multi-step projection schemes common in the field, which is practically valuable for applications where faithfulness to measurements is critical. However, the requirement to train a dedicated model from scratch for each degradation operator is a significant practical limitation compared to methods that leverage pre-trained score networks, limiting immediate adoption. Open questions include whether the SDP framework can be adapted to leverage pre-trained models (e.g., via fine-tuning or adapter layers), whether it extends to non-linear or unknown forward operators, and whether the theoretical guarantees hold under the approximate score estimation that is unavoidable in practice.
