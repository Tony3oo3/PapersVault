## Problem

Adversarially robust linear classification in the non-parametric setting was largely unexplored: prior consistent estimators required strong parametric assumptions (e.g., Gaussian mixtures) yielding O(d/n) rates, while Bao et al. (2020) proved that no convex surrogate for the adversarial 0/1 loss exists, ruling out standard ERM-based approaches. The paper asks whether a computationally tractable, provably consistent estimator can be constructed under only mild regularity conditions on the conditional feature distribution, for arbitrary attack norms.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an iid labeled dataset of n points (x_i, y_i) in R^d × {±1} and an attack budget ε ≥ 0, with the attacker's perturbation measured under an arbitrary norm ‖·‖ (e.g., ℓ_p for any p ∈ [1,∞]). It outputs a weight vector w with dual-norm constraint ‖w‖_⋆ = 1, defining a linear classifier f_w(x) = x^⊤w that is robust to all perturbations δ with ‖δ‖ ≤ ε. The constraint ‖w‖_⋆ = 1 is without loss of generality since the induced classifier is scale-invariant.

## Method

The estimator replaces the discontinuous adversarial 0/1 loss θ_ε(t) = 1_{t≤ε} with a smoothed surrogate θ_{ε,h}(t) = Q((t−ε)/h), where Q is a survival function (e.g., Gaussian or sigmoid) and h = h_n is a bandwidth that vanishes with sample size at a rate depending on both n and d. The estimator is any minimizer of the resulting smoothed empirical risk over the linear function class, which for Euclidean-norm attacks reduces to smooth non-convex optimization on the unit sphere (solved via Riemannian trust-region methods). The key theoretical contribution is proving that with h_n chosen as η^{-1}(√(d log n / n))^{1+Ω(1)}, the estimator achieves minimax-optimal excess adversarial risk of Õ(√(d/n)) under only a Lefvy concentration function (small-ball) regularity assumption on the margins, which holds whenever the class-conditional feature distributions admit densities. The bandwidth h_n provides an explicit tradeoff: smaller h yields faster statistical convergence but a rougher (higher-condition-number) optimization landscape.

## Results

The paper establishes that the proposed smoothed-surrogate estimator achieves a minimax excess adversarial risk of Õ(√(d/n)) uniformly over all attack strengths ε ≥ 0 (Theorem 4.2), matching the non-parametric classification rate. Empirically, on synthetic Gaussian mixture data (d=20) and ReLU random-features data, the adversarial risk of the estimator converges to the analytically known Bayes-optimal value E_ε^opt = Φ(−(1−ε/‖μ‖₂)₊·SNR) as sample size n grows from 100 to 10,000, across Euclidean-norm attack strengths ε ∈ {0.1, …, 1.0}. The smoothing bandwidth is set to h_n = √((d/n) log n) in experiments, consistent with Corollary 4.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary empirical comparison is against the fixed-bandwidth (h=1) sigmoid surrogate of Bao et al. (2020, COLT). On a 2D Gaussian dataset (a modified version of their 'twonorm' setup with σ₁=1, σ₂=0.1), Bao et al.'s estimator fails to reach the Bayes-optimal adversarial risk as n increases — it is provably inconsistent — while the proposed adaptive-bandwidth estimator (h_n vanishing with n) converges to the optimal value, as predicted by Theorem 4.2. The paper also contrasts with Bhattacharjee & Chaudhuri (2020), whose two-stage pruned nearest-neighbor estimator is consistent but non-quantitative (no rates) and computationally impractical due to an NP-hard maximal-separated-subset step. In contrast, the proposed estimator provides explicit Õ(√(d/n)) rates and reduces to a smooth (though non-convex) optimization problem solvable via standard Riemannian trust-region methods (Manopt) in the Euclidean-norm case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Bao et al. (2020), who established that no convex surrogate exists for the adversarial 0/1 loss and proposed static smoothed surrogates (e.g., sigmoid) for robust linear classification. The key departure is replacing Bao et al.'s fixed smoothing bandwidth with an adaptive bandwidth h_n that vanishes at a precise rate with sample size, which is what makes the estimator provably consistent. The paper also generalizes beyond the parametric (Gaussian mixture) and well-separated settings studied by Schmidt et al. (2018), Dan et al. (2020), and Bhattacharjee et al. (2021), and extends from Euclidean-norm attacks to arbitrary norms, while avoiding the NP-hard first stage of Bhattacharjee & Chaudhuri's (2020) two-stage non-parametric estimator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first consistent estimator for adversarially robust linear classification in a fully non-parametric setting with arbitrary attack norms, achieving the minimax-optimal rate of Õ(√(d/n)) that matches classical non-robust classification. The practical implication is that the bandwidth parameter provides a principled tradeoff between optimization difficulty (small h → harder non-convex problem) and statistical convergence speed, something absent in prior adversarial training heuristics. Open questions include extending the analysis to non-linear models (neural networks in linearized/kernel regimes), proving convergence to global optima rather than merely stationary points, and understanding whether the adaptive-smoothing principle transfers to deep adversarial training.
