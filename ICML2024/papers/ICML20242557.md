## Problem

Existing neural VRP solvers are structured and trained independently for each specific problem variant, incurring prohibitive training overhead and offering no generalization to unseen VRPs. The paper asks whether a single unified neural solver can be trained on a subset of VRP variants and generalize zero-shot to the remaining combinations of constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242557/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a VRP instance defined by node coordinates, demands, time windows, and a vehicle capacity, with unused constraint features padded to zero so that one architecture handles all 16 variants (combinations of C, O, B, L, TW). It outputs a complete tour as an autoregressive sequence of node visits, where feasibility is enforced by masking invalid nodes at each decoding step based on the active constraint set.

## Method

MVMoE builds on the POMO attention-based encoder-decoder and replaces the FFN in each encoder layer and the final linear projection in the decoder's multi-head attention with 4-expert MoE layers using Top-2 input-choice gating. The key architectural contribution is a hierarchical gating mechanism in the decoder: a problem-level gating network G1 (via mean-pooled representation) first decides whether to route the entire batch to a sparse MoE sub-layer or a dense linear layer, and only if the sparse path is chosen does a node-level gating G2 distribute individual nodes to experts. This adaptively skips expensive expert routing in some decoding steps, reducing training time by ~23% while preserving solution quality. Training uses REINFORCE with multi-task sampling across 6 VRP variants and an auxiliary importance-and-load balancing loss (α=0.01) to prevent expert collapse.

## Results

On 10 unseen VRP variants (zero-shot generalization), MVMoE/4E achieves average gaps of 5.999%–9.516% (n=50) and 7.088%–10.009% (n=100) relative to the best traditional solver, consistently outperforming POMO-MTL by 0.2–0.4 percentage points across all variants. On the 6 trained VRPs, MVMoE/4E achieves gaps of 0.092%–3.410% (n=50) and 0.261%–4.903% (n=100), solving 1K instances in 3–12 seconds versus 10+ minutes for OR-Tools. The hierarchical gating variant MVMoE/4E-L trades ~0.1pp in-distribution performance for ~23% training time reduction and notably stronger out-of-distribution generalization on CVRPLIB benchmark instances.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against POMO-MTL (the dense multi-task baseline from Liu et al., 2024), MVMoE/4E wins on all 10 unseen VRP variants in zero-shot generalization, with gaps reduced by 0.1–0.4pp (e.g., 5.999% vs 6.430% on OVRPB n=50; 8.775% vs 9.035% on VRPBLTW n=50). On the 6 trained VRPs, MVMoE/4E also slightly outperforms POMO-MTL (e.g., 0.896% vs 0.987% on CVRP n=50; 1.653% vs 1.846% on CVRP n=100). Against single-task POMO, MVMoE/4E is worse on each individual trained VRP (since POMO is retrained per problem) but dramatically better in aggregate: POMO's average gap across all 6 trained VRPs is 16.815% (CVRP-trained) to 44.586% (VRPTW-trained), while MVMoE/4E achieves 1.925% average. Against traditional solvers (HGS, LKH3, OR-Tools), MVMoE/4E has larger solution gaps (typically 1–10% vs 0–2%) but solves 1K instances in 3–12 seconds versus 10 minutes to 3.5 hours, representing a 100–1000× speedup. The hierarchical gating variant MVMoE/4E-L is slightly worse in-distribution but outperforms MVMoE/4E on large-scale CVRPLIB OOD instances (n>500), where the base gating suffers from extreme expert load imbalance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the multi-task VRP solving framework of Liu et al. (2024), which treats VRP variants as combinations of underlying attributes and trains a shared POMO-based network for zero-shot generalization to unseen variants. Its key departure is the first introduction of Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) layers into the combinatorial optimization literature, repurposing the conditional-computation paradigm from LLMs (Shazeer et al., 2017; Zhou et al., 2022) to enhance model capacity in attention-based VRP solvers without proportional compute cost. The proposed hierarchical gating mechanism is a novel architectural contribution tailored to the autoregressive decoding process, where the number of gating steps scales with problem size, making naive per-step MoE routing prohibitively expensive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes that MoE-based sparse architectures are a viable and effective paradigm for neural combinatorial optimization, opening a path toward larger, more general-purpose 'foundation models' for COPs. A notable open question is why the lighter hierarchical-gating variant (MVMoE/4E-L) outperforms the full MoE on out-of-distribution real-world benchmarks, which the paper attributes to extreme expert load imbalance under distribution shift—a phenomenon also observed in the MoE literature but not yet resolved. The authors acknowledge that current parameter scales remain far below LLMs, and scaling laws, interpretability of learned gating policies, and integration with neighborhood-constrained search for large-scale instances all remain open directions.
