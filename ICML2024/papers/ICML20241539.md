## Problem

The paper addresses the fundamental question of what is a sufficient and necessary condition for learnability in offline RL with function approximation. Prior notions of data coverage—concentrability coefficients, relative condition numbers, and data diversity—were mutually incompatible, gave conflicting characterizations of the learnable problem landscape, and lacked lower bounds establishing their necessity. The paper seeks a single tight characterization that unifies and strictly subsumes all prior notions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline dataset of (context, action, reward) tuples collected by an unknown fixed behavior policy μ, a function class F for value approximation (characterized by pseudo-dimension d or L1 covering numbers), and in the hybrid setting an online interaction budget m. It produces a randomized policy π̂ whose sub-optimality is bounded in terms of the policy transfer coefficients (ρ_π, C_π) between μ and the comparator policy π, yielding rates of the form (C_π d / n)^(1/2ρ_π) for parametric classes. The bounds are minimax-optimal up to log factors and are adaptive: the algorithm need not know ρ_π or C_π a priori.

## Method

The core contribution is the policy transfer coefficient (ρ, C), which requires that the expected value gap under the target policy is controlled by the squared Bellman error under the behavior policy, strictly subsuming concentrability, data diversity, and relative condition numbers. The algorithm (OfDM-Hedge) follows an actor-critic framework, alternating between pessimistic value estimation (minimizing empirical Bellman residual subject to a confidence penalty β) and policy improvement via the Hedge algorithm. Two key technical innovations enable tight bounds: a uniform Bernstein's inequality for Bellman-like losses using empirical L1 covering numbers (rather than domain-wide L∞ covering numbers), and a trick that removes the cost blowup of Hedge iterations by recognizing each iterate is only used in the form f(·, π_t), making the bound agnostic to T. For the hybrid offline-online setting, EXP4 adaptively combines the offline and online learners without requiring prior knowledge of the transfer coefficient, achieving nearly minimax-optimal rates across regimes.

## Results

The paper establishes (near) minimax-optimal sub-optimality bounds of order $(Cd/n)^{1/(2\rho)}$ for offline contextual bandits with VC-type function classes (pseudo-dimension $d$), where $\rho$ is the policy transfer exponent and $C$ is the policy transfer factor. For MDPs with horizon $H$, the bound becomes $(H^2Cd/n)^{1/(2\rho)}$, with an $\mathcal{O}(H)$ gap between upper and lower bounds. In the hybrid offline-online setting, the adaptive algorithm achieves $\min\{(C^*Kd/n)^{1/(2\rho^*)}, K\sqrt{d/m}\}$, matching the lower bound in the regime $m \geq (n/(C^*Kd))^{1/\rho^*}$. For nonparametric classes with polynomial covering numbers, the rate is $C_\pi^{1/(2\rho_\pi)}(K/n)^{1/(2\rho_\pi(p+1))}$.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is against prior notions of data coverage: concentrability coefficients, relative condition numbers, data diversity (Nguyen-Tang & Arora, 2023), Bellman residual ratio (Xie et al., 2021a), and Bellman error transfer coefficient (Song et al., 2022). Policy transfer coefficients strictly subsume all of these: bounds on any prior notion imply bounds on transfer exponents, but not vice versa. Concretely, Example 3.3 (d-dimensional halfspaces with spherical policies) yields a true sub-optimality of zero, which is tightly captured by the policy transfer exponent ($\rho \to 0$, bound = 0) but gives vacuous bounds under the HK transfer exponent ($\sqrt{C/n}$) and concentrability coefficient ($\infty$). Example 3.4 shows data diversity (equivalent to $\rho=1$) can be infinite while the minimal transfer exponent remains finite, yielding a non-vacuous rate. For MDPs, the paper's $n^{-1/2}$ rate at $\rho=1$ improves upon the $n^{-1/4}$ worst-case rates of Xie et al. (2021a) and Nguyen-Tang & Arora (2023), and extends their guarantees from finite or $L_\infty$-bounded function classes to any class with finite $L_1$ covering numbers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the offline RL literature's various notions of data coverage—concentrability coefficients, relative condition numbers, Bellman residual ratios, and data diversity—by introducing policy transfer coefficients that strictly subsume all of them. The conceptual inspiration comes from transfer learning (Hanneke & Kpotufe, 2019), but the authors deliberately modify the HK transfer exponent to use the expected value gap rather than squared error under the target policy, yielding a tighter characterization. Algorithmically, it follows the actor-critic pessimism framework of Zanette et al. (2021) and Xie et al. (2021a), but removes the cost blowup of the Hedge algorithm by recognizing that each iterate is only used in a single functional form. The hybrid offline-online setting extends the policy fine-tuning work of Xie et al. (2021b) and Song et al. (2022) with minimax-optimal adaptive guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The policy transfer coefficient provides the first unified, tight characterization of offline learnability that subsumes all prior data coverage notions, resolving a key gap in understanding when offline RL is feasible. The finding that in the hybrid setting there is no worst-case benefit to mixing offline and online data (one should choose purely offline or purely online based on the transfer coefficient) is a clean structural result with practical implications for algorithm design. Open questions remain: lower bounds for non-parametric function classes, fully adaptive hybrid algorithms that match lower bounds in all regimes (especially small online budgets), and whether pseudo-dimension and policy transfer coefficients are truly necessary conditions for learnability.
