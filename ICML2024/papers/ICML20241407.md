## Problem

Zero-shot editing with pre-trained diffusion models has advanced rapidly in the image domain but remains largely unexplored for audio. Existing audio editing approaches either train specialized models for fixed instruction sets or rely on computationally expensive test-time optimization, leaving a gap for flexible, training-free editing that can handle arbitrary modifications (e.g., changing a specific instrument, improvising a melody) while preserving the source signal's structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real audio signal (processed as a mel-spectrogram in AudioLDM2's latent space, decoded to waveform via HiFi-GAN) and either a target text prompt (ZETA) or a PC index with strength parameter γ (ZEUS). Optionally, a source text prompt is supplied during inversion for finer control. The output is an edited audio signal of the same duration. In ZETA, the starting timestep T_start (out of 200 total steps) governs the fidelity-to-text-adherence trade-off; in ZEUS, the perturbation is applied over a user-chosen timestep range [T_start, T_end] with a correction factor c_t that matches the uncertainty level at each step.

## Method

Both methods are built on edit-friendly DDPM inversion (Huberman-Spiegelglas et al., 2024), which extracts a sequence of noise vectors {x_T, z_T, …, z_1} that, when fed through the reverse diffusion process, reconstruct the source signal. ZETA simply re-runs the reverse process with a different text prompt (target instead of source) under classifier-free guidance, so the inverted noise preserves coarse structure while the prompt change drives fine-grained edits. ZEUS is the novel contribution: at a chosen timestep t′, it computes the top principal components of the posterior covariance Cov[x_0|x_t] via subspace iteration (each iteration approximated by a single forward pass through the denoiser), then adds these PCs to the denoised signal x̂_{0|t} at every subsequent timestep with a scaling factor γ·c_t·√(λ_i|t), where c_t is a closed-form correction derived from the DDPM schedule. The key insight is that these posterior PCs encode semantically meaningful editing directions (e.g., instrument participation, melody variation, vibrato strength) that are impossible to specify via text, and they can be combined linearly to create compound edits.

## Results

In the user study (300 AMT workers, 277 passing vigilance), ZETA editing is preferred over DDIM inversion (70.05%), MusicGen (60.31%), and SDEdit (64.97%) for matching the target prompt while preserving the original signal's essence. In a representative text-based editing example (Fig. 6), all methods achieve a similar CLAP score of ~0.34, but our method attains the lowest LPAPS of 4.29 versus 4.87 (SDEdit), 5.74 (MusicGen), and 6.26 (DDIM inversion), indicating superior fidelity to the source structure. For unsupervised ZEUS editing, our method achieves a lower FAD to the FMA-pop quality reference at every level of edit strength (measured by FAD to the original MusicDelta distribution) compared to SDEdit, confirming semantically meaningful modifications with higher perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ZETA/ZEUS against three baselines: MusicGen (conditioned on chromagram), SDEdit, and DDIM inversion, all using the same AudioLDM2 checkpoint where applicable. In the CLAP-vs-LPAPS trade-off (Fig. 8), our method dominates all baselines across the entire range of T_start values (40–100), achieving lower LPAPS at every CLAP level, while MusicGen is notably inferior due to its reliance on chromagram conditioning which fails for polyphonic music. In the user study, our method is preferred over DDIM inversion by 70.05% of workers, over SDEdit by 64.97%, and over MusicGen by 60.31%. In the specific Fig. 6 example, our method's LPAPS of 4.29 is 12.5% lower than SDEdit (4.87), 25.4% lower than MusicGen (5.74), and 31.5% lower than DDIM inversion (6.26), at matched CLAP. For unsupervised editing (Fig. 10), our method consistently achieves lower FAD to FMA-pop (higher musical quality) than SDEdit at any given edit strength, whereas SDEdit's uncontrolled noise-based modifications degrade quality more rapidly as the starting timestep decreases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two recent contributions: the edit-friendly DDPM inversion method of Huberman-Spiegelglas et al. (2024), which extracts noise vectors encoding global signal structure, and the posterior-covariance PC extraction framework of Manor & Michaeli (2024), originally developed for uncertainty quantification in inverse problems. ZETA directly ports the image-domain text-based editing recipe (invert with source prompt, regenerate with target prompt) to the audio setting using AudioLDM2, while ZEUS repurposes the posterior PC machinery as a tool for discovering semantically meaningful editing directions in the diffusion noise space without any supervision. The paper departs from the dominant audio-editing paradigms—specialized trained models (MusicGen, AUDIT, InstructME) and test-time optimization (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion)—by requiring neither training nor per-signal optimization, and it contrasts with the simpler SDEdit and DDIM-inversion baselines that lack the structural fidelity of DDPM inversion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of zero-shot audio editing, demonstrating that image-domain inversion-based techniques transfer to audio and that posterior PCs in the noise space yield musically interpretable editing directions (instrument separation, melody improvisation, vibrato control) that are difficult or impossible to specify via text prompts. Key limitations include the quality ceiling imposed by the pre-trained AudioLDM2 model, the uncontrollability of which PC corresponds to which semantic concept (a user must search), and the fact that the unsupervised method has not been validated on tasks beyond music and speech. Open questions include whether the PC-based directions can be made controllable (e.g., via learned mappings), whether the approach generalizes to longer-form audio, and whether the noise-space editing directions transfer across different diffusion backbones.
