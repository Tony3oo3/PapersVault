## Problem

Despite the empirical success of in-context learning in large language models, the theoretical understanding of how to train Transformers to achieve ICL and what governs their generalization capacity remains largely elusive, particularly for architectures with both nonlinear softmax self-attention and nonlinear ReLU MLP. Prior theoretical works either simplify away the softmax attention, ignore the ReLU activation in the MLP, or fail to characterize training dynamics under distribution shift. This paper provides the first joint analysis of training dynamics and ICL generalization for fully nonlinear Transformers, together with the first theoretical characterization of how magnitude-based pruning affects ICL performance.

## Input / Output

The model receives a prompt matrix P of shape (d_X + d_Y) × (l+1), where the first l columns are context input-output pairs (x_i, Embd(f(x_i))) and the last column is the query x_query with a zero-padded label embedding. Inputs follow a structured data model where each sample x = μ_j + κν_k combines a relevant pattern μ_j (determining the binary label) with an irrelevant pattern ν_k (noise), and the model outputs a scalar prediction whose sign yields the binary class label. Training data consists of N prompt-label pairs drawn from a subset of binary classification tasks, each task defined by two decisive relevant patterns among M₁ total patterns.

## Method

The paper analyzes a single-head, one-layer Transformer with softmax self-attention and a two-layer ReLU MLP, trained via SGD with hinge loss on multi-task binary classification data. The key mechanistic insight is that training causes the attention layer to project query and context embeddings primarily along the direction of their shared relevant pattern (amplifying its magnitude from Θ(1) to Θ(√log M₁)), concentrating attention weights on contexts sharing the same relevant pattern as the query, while the MLP layer aligns its neurons with the label embedding direction to produce correct predictions. The paper proves in-domain generalization to unseen tasks and out-of-domain generalization when out-of-domain relevant patterns are linear combinations of in-domain patterns with coefficient sums ≥ 1, with sample complexity polynomial in α^{-1} (fraction of relevant contexts) and β^{-1} (pattern norm). Additionally, the paper proves that magnitude-based pruning of small-magnitude MLP neurons preserves ICL performance, while pruning large-magnitude neurons causes generalization error growing linearly with the pruning rate.

## Results

The paper provides the first theoretical analysis of training dynamics for Transformers with both nonlinear (softmax) self-attention and nonlinear (ReLU) MLP, proving that a one-layer Transformer trained via SGD on a subset of binary classification tasks achieves in-domain generalization error O(ε) (Theorem 3.3) and out-of-domain generalization error O(ε) (Theorem 3.4) when ODR patterns are linear combinations of IDR patterns with coefficient sum ≥ 1. The required number of iterations scales as Θ(η⁻¹M₁α⁻²/³β⁻²/³√log M₁), and the training context length scales as Ω(log M₁/α) and Ω(1/(β²α)). For pruning, Theorem 3.7 shows that removing a constant fraction of small-magnitude MLP neurons yields error O(ε + M₁⁻¹/²), while pruning R fraction of large-magnitude neurons causes error Ω(R + (α'M₁)⁻¹). Experiments on GPT-2 confirm out-of-domain error < 0.01 when the coefficient-sum condition holds, and magnitude-based pruning preserves ICL performance up to ~15% pruning rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is theoretical: prior works (Li et al. 2023c, Zhang et al. 2023a, Huang et al. 2023, Wu et al. 2023a) each cover only a subset of the dimensions (nonlinear attention, nonlinear MLP, training dynamics, distribution shift), and all are restricted to linear regression tasks. This paper is the first to simultaneously address all four dimensions for binary classification. Empirically, the paper compares ICL against logistic regression, SVM (Gaussian and linear kernels), 1-NN, and 3-NN using the same context examples as training data. When α'=0.8 (high fraction of relevant contexts), ICL's advantage over other methods is modest; however, when α'=0.6, ICL becomes the most sample-efficient method for achieving small generalization error, demonstrating its ability to filter irrelevant data and resist label noise. For pruning, magnitude-based pruning outperforms random pruning: OOD generalization error remains near zero up to ~15% pruning rate (matching the fraction of small-magnitude neurons), while random pruning degrades performance immediately.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ICL training and generalization analysis initiated by Garg et al. (2022) and Huang et al. (2023) by being the first to jointly handle both nonlinear softmax self-attention and nonlinear ReLU MLP in the theoretical analysis. Prior works either ignore nonlinear attention (Zhang et al. 2023a, Wu et al. 2023a) or nonlinear MLP (Huang et al. 2023), and none characterize out-of-domain generalization under distribution shift for classification tasks. The prompt construction framework follows Von Oswald et al. (2023), and the feature-learning data model with relevant/irrelevant patterns draws on recent work by Shi et al. (2021) and Karp et al. (2021). The paper additionally introduces the first theoretical analysis of how magnitude-based pruning affects ICL performance, a question entirely absent from prior ICL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a complete mechanistic picture of how a trained nonlinear Transformer implements ICL—attention selects same-pattern contexts while the MLP distinguishes label embeddings—giving practitioners a principled understanding of what drives ICL success. The pruning result (constant-fraction small-magnitude pruning preserves ICL while large-magnitude pruning degrades it linearly) has direct practical implications for reducing LLM inference costs without sacrificing in-context capability. Key limitations include the single-head, one-layer architecture, binary classification focus, and simplified orthogonal-pattern data model; extending to multi-head, deeper Transformers, and generation tasks remains open. The out-of-domain generalization condition (ODR patterns as linear combinations of IDR patterns with coefficient sum ≥ 1) is a clean but narrow characterization that may not capture all realistic distribution shifts.
