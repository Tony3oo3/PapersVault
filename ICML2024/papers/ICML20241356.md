## Problem

Online RL policies trained on data collected under varying policies or dynamics suffer from distribution discrepancies that cause biased policy evaluation and high training variance. Existing methods address specific shift types (policy-only, dynamics-only, or particular combinations) with specialized algorithms and task priors, lacking a unified framework that handles all combinations of policy and dynamics shifts simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241356/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes continuous-state environment transitions (s, a, s', r) from two buffers: a small local buffer D_L (size ~1000) holding fresh on-policy samples under current/target dynamics, and a large global buffer D_G (size 1e6) storing all historical off-policy data. It produces a Gaussian policy π(a|s) and a Q-value function Q(s,a), plus a discriminator h(s,a,s') that classifies whether a transition tuple originates from the local or global buffer. The discriminator's output is converted into a log-density ratio R(s,a,s') = -log[1/h*(s,a,s') - 1] that explicitly quantifies the transition occupancy discrepancy.

## Method

OMPO unifies all policy and dynamics shift scenarios by identifying that discrepancies reduce to transition occupancy distribution mismatches ρ_T^π(s,a,s') ≠ ρ_T̂^π̂(s,a,s'). It derives a surrogate objective using log r(s,a) minus a weighted KL/f-divergence penalty, then converts this into a tractable min-max problem via three steps: (1) a GAN-style discriminator trained on local vs. global buffer samples recovers the log-density ratio R(s,a,s'); (2) the Bellman flow constraint is handled through Lagrangian relaxation introducing Q(s,a) as multipliers; (3) Fenchel conjugate of the f-divergence eliminates the unknown on-policy distribution, yielding an objective computable from global buffer samples alone. The resulting algorithm uses a two-timescale actor-critic structure where the inner Q-update and outer policy gradient are solved at different rates, with the discriminator providing the shift-correction signal at each training step.

## Results

On stochastic robot manipulation tasks (Table 1), OMPO achieves the highest success rates across 7 of 8 tasks, with particularly large margins on harder tasks: Coffer-Push (68.5% vs. SAC 15.8%, TD3 3.3%), Hammer (84.2% vs. SAC 15.4%, TD3 12.2%), and Drawer-Open (93.4% vs. SAC 57.7%, TD3 64.3%). In domain adaptation, OMPO-DR reaches convergence on Walker2d with only ~60 trajectories (60,000 target-dynamics steps). Across all three shift settings (stationary, domain adaptation, non-stationary), OMPO consistently outperforms specialized baselines with lower variance and faster convergence, as shown in learning curves (Figures 2, 3, 6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAC and TD3 on manipulation tasks, OMPO wins decisively on the hardest tasks (Coffer-Push: 68.5% vs. 15.8%/3.3%; Hammer: 84.2% vs. 15.4%/12.2%) but is slightly behind SAC on Panda-Reach-Sparse (93.1% vs. 94.5%). In stationary environments, OMPO outperforms SAC, TD3, and AlgaeDICE in both sample efficiency and asymptotic performance (Figure 2). For domain adaptation, OMPO surpasses DARC, DR, and SAC, and OMPO-DR achieves the best overall performance, reaching Walker2d convergence in ~60k target steps (Figure 3). In non-stationary environments, OMPO outperforms CEMRL and CaDM with faster convergence and smaller variance, while maintaining the same hyperparameters across all shift types (Figure 6). Notably, OMPO's advantage is most pronounced under severe dynamics shifts (gravity varying 0.5×–3×), where CEMRL degrades significantly while OMPO remains robust (Appendix F.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

OMPO extends the DICE family of distribution-correction methods (AlgaeDICE, DualDICE) from state-action occupancy matching to transition occupancy matching (s,a,s'), thereby subsuming dynamics shifts alongside policy shifts under a single objective. The discriminator-based estimation of distribution discrepancies borrows directly from GAN training, while the tractable min-max formulation relies on Fenchel-Rockafellar duality (Nachum & Dai, 2020) and Bellman flow constraints, both well-established tools in the DICE literature. The key conceptual departure is the unification of three previously separate settings—stationary off-policy RL, domain adaptation, and non-stationary dynamics—into one framework where only the data collection scheme changes, rather than requiring task-specific algorithms (DARC, CEMRL, CaDM).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework offers a practical path for RL practitioners facing distribution shifts in robotics and sim-to-real transfer, particularly given the strong OMPO-DR results that suggest occupancy matching can amplify the benefits of domain randomization. However, several limitations remain: the method requires strictly positive rewards, the local buffer size is a sensitive hyperparameter with no principled selection rule, and the vision-input ablation reveals that OMPO fails when dynamics changes (wind, gravity) are not directly observable in the state. Open directions include extending to offline-to-online RL, hierarchical settings with non-stationary high-level policies, and relaxing the positive-reward assumption.
