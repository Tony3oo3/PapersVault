## Problem

Existing video synthesis methods lack a universal, visually-grounded mechanism for fine-grained motion control. Prior box-based approaches (TrailBlazer, FACTOR) require text descriptions for each controlled object and do not support flexible region constraints, making it difficult to select and control arbitrary objects from a reference image or to specify approximate motion paths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242142/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, up to 8 box constraints per frame (each encoded as 4 normalized coordinates, a 3D RGB object ID, and a hard/soft boolean flag), and optionally a first-frame image, producing a 16-frame video at 256×256 resolution and 4 FPS. During inference, users typically specify boxes only on the first and last frames; the system automatically interpolates and relaxes these into soft box constraints for intermediate frames, and boxes can be drawn as line segments along frame borders to control objects entering or exiting the scene.

## Method

Boximator is a plug-in control module that inserts a new self-attention layer between the spatial self-attention and spatial cross-attention layers in every spatial attention block of a frozen video diffusion U-Net. Box constraints are encoded via Fourier embeddings of coordinates, object ID, and hard/soft flag, processed through an MLP into fixed-length control token sequences that attend jointly with visual tokens. The key training innovation is self-tracking: the model is first trained to generate colored bounding boxes as part of the video output, decomposing the hard box-object correlation problem into two easier sub-tasks (producing correctly colored boxes and aligning them to constraints), then fine-tuned to suppress visible boxes while retaining the internal alignment representation. Training proceeds in three stages—hard boxes with self-tracking, 80% soft boxes with self-tracking, then soft boxes without self-tracking—on 1.1M dynamic clips from WebVid-10M annotated with 2.2M tracked objects via LLaVA + Grounding DINO + DEVA.

## Results

On MSR-VTT, Boximator improves FVD over its base models (PixelDance: 381→237, ModelScope: 550→239) and further reduces FVD when box constraints are added (PixelDance: 237→174, ModelScope: 239→216). For motion control, adding box constraints yields dramatic AP gains: mAP increases from 0.094 to 0.349 (PixelDance) and 0.096 to 0.312 (ModelScope) on MSR-VTT, and from 0.050 to 0.445 (PixelDance) and 0.054 to 0.361 (ModelScope) on ActivityNet (4.4–8.9× improvement). Human evaluation confirms a 76% preference for Boximator over the base model on motion control and a 35.2% preference on video quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Boximator outperforms all listed video generation baselines on FVD: PixelDance+Boximator achieves 237 vs. PixelDance's 381, ModelScope+Boximator achieves 239 vs. ModelScope's 550, and both beat FACTOR-traj (317) and Phenaki (384). The closest comparable method, FACTOR (Huang et al., 2023), also uses bounding boxes but requires a text description per box and does not support soft boxes or visual grounding; Boximator achieves a lower FVD (237 vs. 317) and higher mAP (0.349 vs. 0.290*) on MSR-VTT. On ActivityNet, the AP improvement from adding box constraints is 4.4–8.9×, substantially larger than the 1.9–3.7× on MSR-VTT, confirming stronger control on more dynamic scenes. Ablations show that removing self-tracking drops mAP from 0.349 to 0.118 (PixelDance, MSR-VTT), and removing soft boxes drops it to 0.235, validating both design choices. In human evaluation, Boximator is preferred over the base model in 76% of cases for motion control (vs. 2.2% for the base model) and 35.2% for video quality (vs. 16.8% for the base model).

## Relation to Prior Work

Boximator extends the video diffusion model lineage (Ho et al., 2022b; PixelDance; ModelScope) by adding a lightweight self-attention control module inspired by GLIGEN's box-and-text region control for images, but deliberately removing the textual grounding requirement so that box-object association is learned purely from visual inputs. It departs from prior motion control methods (trajectories, human poses, depth maps) by offering a single universal constraint type—bounding boxes with hard/soft semantics—that can control arbitrary objects, camera motion, and composite entities without requiring per-object text descriptions. The concurrent works TrailBlazer and FACTOR also use boxes but remain text-grounded and lack soft-box constraints or a study of the associated training challenges.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in design (freeze base weights, train only a new attention layer) means Boximator can be retrofitted onto any future video diffusion model without retraining, and the self-tracking training trick—first generating visible colored boxes, then suppressing them while retaining the internal alignment—offers a general recipe for teaching diffusion models to associate discrete control tokens with visual entities. Key limitations include the 256×256, 4-second, 1:1 output resolution, reliance solely on WebVid for training data, and the observation that certain motions (e.g., rotation) remain better controlled via text than boxes. Open questions include scaling to longer/higher-resolution videos, integrating text-based motion cues alongside box constraints, and whether self-tracking generalizes to other control modalities such as keypoints or human poses.
