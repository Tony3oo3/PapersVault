## Problem

Diffusion models require hundreds to thousands of iterative reverse-diffusion steps at inference time, making them far more expensive than single-step generators like GANs. Existing distillation methods that compress this into one step either depend on access to the original training data, require auxiliary GAN losses, or fail to achieve FID reductions that match or exceed the multi-step teacher. SiD addresses this by introducing a data-free distillation framework that achieves exponentially fast FID reduction and can surpass the teacher model's quality in a single generation step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242578/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the distilled generator takes a single sample of Gaussian noise z ~ N(0, I) (scaled by σ_init = 2.5) and optionally a class label for conditional generation, producing one 32×32 or 64×64 RGB image in a single forward pass through the U-Net. During training, the method is entirely data-free: it consumes only the generator's own synthesized images (x_g = G_θ(σ_init z)) and the pretrained teacher score network's outputs, never touching the original training dataset. The generator and its auxiliary score network are initialized by copying the pretrained EDM teacher's architecture and parameters.

## Method

SiD reformulates the forward diffusion process as a semi-implicit distribution and derives three score identities: Tweedie's formula for diffused real data, Tweedie's formula for diffused fake data, and a Score Projection Identity that converts intractable expectations over the fake distribution into tractable ones over the joint (x_t, x_g). These identities enable a model-based explicit score-matching (MESM) loss — a Fisher divergence between the teacher score S_φ and the generator's true score — which is approximated by a fused loss L_θ^(2) − αL_θ^(1) combining a projected score-matching term with an L2 score-difference term. Crucially, SiD backpropagates gradients through both the pretrained teacher score network f_φ and the generator score network f_ψ (computing score gradients ∂f/∂x_t), a step absent in Diff-Instruct and DMD. The training alternates between updating the generator score network f_ψ via a standard denoising objective and updating the generator G_θ via the fused loss, with a noise-weighting coefficient ω(t) and a log-spaced noise schedule inherited from EDM. This yields a data-free, single-step generator that reduces FID exponentially during distillation and, on CIFAR-10, FFHQ, and AFHQ, surpasses the multi-step VP-EDM teacher.

## Results

SiD achieves FID 1.923 (α=1.2) on CIFAR-10 32×32 unconditional and FID 1.710 (α=1.2) on CIFAR-10 conditional, both with a single NFE, surpassing the VP-EDM teacher (FID 1.97 and 1.79, respectively, at 35 NFE). On ImageNet 64×64, SiD reaches FID 1.524 (α=1.2, 1 NFE) with Precision 0.74 and Recall 0.63, outperforming all other one-step distillation methods. On FFHQ 64×64 and AFHQ-v2 64×64, SiD achieves FID 1.550 and 1.628 respectively, both exceeding the VP-EDM teacher (FID 2.39 and 1.96 at 79 NFE). FID decreases roughly exponentially with the number of processed synthetic images, with SiD surpassing Diff-Instruct and DMD within under 20M images on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SiD (α=1.2) outperforms the VP-EDM teacher on three of four datasets: CIFAR-10 unconditional (FID 1.923 vs. 1.97), CIFAR-10 conditional (1.710 vs. 1.79), FFHQ (1.550 vs. 2.39), and AFHQ-v2 (1.628 vs. 1.96), all with 1 NFE versus 35–79 NFE for the teacher. The sole exception is ImageNet 64×64, where SiD (FID 1.524) trails the teacher's 1.36 FID at 511 NFE, though it still beats the teacher's 79-NFE configuration (FID 2.64). Among one-step distillation baselines, SiD surpasses CTM (FID 1.98 on CIFAR-10 uncond, 1.92 on ImageNet) by a meaningful margin, and decisively outperforms DMD (3.77 on CIFAR-10 uncond, 2.62 on ImageNet) and Diff-Instruct (4.53 on CIFAR-10 uncond, 5.57 on ImageNet). SiD also exceeds iCT-deep (FID 2.51) and all GAN-based one-step methods (StyleGAN2+ADA+Tune at 2.92, CT-StyleGAN2 at 2.9). Notably, SiD is data-free, whereas CTM requires training data and an auxiliary GAN loss, and DMD requires pairwise regression data, making SiD's data-free advantage particularly significant in settings where original training data is unavailable.

## Relation to Prior Work

SiD operates within the framework established by Diffusion-GAN (Wang et al., 2023c) of aligning diffused real and fake distributions, a framework subsequently adopted by Diff-Instruct and DMD. However, it departs from these methods by replacing JSD/KL divergence with a model-based score-matching loss (a Fisher divergence variant), and by fusing three score-related identities—Tweedie's formula for real data, Tweedie's formula for fake data, and the Score Projection Identity from Vincent (2011)—to make the intractable loss computable. The semi-implicit distribution perspective from Yin & Zhou (2018) provides the theoretical lens, while the noise scheduling, weighting, and implementation details are largely inherited from EDM (Karras et al., 2022) and Diff-Instruct (Luo et al., 2023c). A key technical distinction from Diff-Instruct and DMD is that SiD backpropagates through both the teacher and student score networks (computing score gradients), which is not done in those prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SiD is notable as the first data-free distillation method to match or exceed the FID of its multi-step teacher (VP-EDM) on most benchmarks using only a single forward pass, challenging the assumption that a student is inherently bounded by the teacher's reverse-diffusion quality. The exponentially fast FID reduction (roughly log-log linear) means practitioners can obtain high-quality one-step generators with far fewer synthetic images than competing methods. Key limitations include the need to simultaneously maintain three networks (increasing memory by ~50–70% over Diff-Instruct) and the fact that on ImageNet 64×64, SiD still falls short of both the 511-NFE teacher and the 1000-NFE RIN model. Open questions include whether extended training can close the ImageNet gap, whether LoRA-based parameter reduction can alleviate the memory overhead, and whether the score-gradient mechanism generalizes to latent diffusion or text-to-image settings.
