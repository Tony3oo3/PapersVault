## Problem

Existing MILP instance generation methods, particularly the learning-based G2MILP, iteratively modify a single randomly selected constraint, which disrupts the intrinsic interrelations among constraints and degrades the computational hardness of generated instances. The paper addresses two questions: which constraints should be modified to best preserve inherent instance properties, and how to recognize and jointly process strongly related constraints whose collective modification is necessary to maintain the feasible region.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240670/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a MILP instance encoded as a weighted bipartite graph where constraint vertices carry a 2-dimensional feature (bias and positional encoding i/|C|), variable vertices carry a 9-dimensional feature from the Ecole environment (objective coefficient, type flags, bounds), and edges are weighted by constraint coefficients A_ij. The output is a new bipartite graph in which a fraction η of constraints (organized into communities) have been replaced by newly generated constraints while all variable vertices remain unmodified. Generation proceeds iteratively, replacing one community at a time in ascending order of Gaussian sampling probability until the replacement ratio η|C| is reached.

## Method

ACM-MILP introduces two key mechanisms on top of a masked-VAE graph generation paradigm. First, constraint grouping: the Louvain community detection algorithm (with dataset-specific resolution parameter γ) is applied to the unweighted bipartite graph to identify communities of strongly related constraints that jointly define the feasible region, enabling collective rather than single-constraint modification. Second, adaptive constraint selection: the VAE encoder maps constraint vertices into a latent space, and the Gaussian sampling probability of each constraint (and by extension its community) is computed relative to the instance-specific mean, distinguishing generic high-probability constraints from instance-specific low-probability ones; only the lowest-probability communities are selected for modification. The decoder reconstructs each selected community via five sequential modules (bias, degree, variable pool, edge selection, and edge weight prediction) conditioned on the latent sample and graph-embedding features of unmodified vertices. Training uses a two-stage procedure: random community selection for broad embedding learning, followed by fine-tuning on low-probability communities to match the inference-time selection distribution.

## Results

On computational hardness preservation (the paper's primary metric), ACM-MILP achieves solving-time relative errors of 17.1% (MIS), 0.4% (SC), and 11.2% (CA) at η=0.1, substantially outperforming G2MILP (26.7%, 6.0%, 94.3%) and Random (275.9%, 14.5%, 149.9%). For branching nodes at the same setting, ACM-MILP errors are 18.4%, 0.3%, and 24.3% versus G2MILP's 31.5%, 10.0%, and 103.1%. In the downstream hyperparameter tuning task, ACM-MILP-generated instances yield the best test-set solving time on SC (3.804s) and CA (0.871s), beating both the default Gurobi configuration and G2MILP-generated instances.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACM-MILP is compared against three baselines: Bowly (heuristic, from-scratch generation), G2MILP (masked VAE with random single-constraint modification), and Random (G2MILP architecture with random output). ACM-MILP decisively outperforms G2MILP on computational hardness across all three datasets, most dramatically on CA where G2MILP's solving-time error is 94.3% versus ACM-MILP's 11.2%, and on branching nodes where G2MILP reaches 103.1% error versus 24.3%. On statistical distributional similarity (Table 1), ACM-MILP is competitive with G2MILP (slightly lower on MIS, higher on SC), indicating that the hardness gains come from better structural learning rather than overfitting to surface statistics. Bowly performs poorly on all metrics, producing instances that are far too easy (solving times near zero), confirming that hand-crafted generation cannot capture real-world hardness. In the hyperparameter tuning downstream task, ACM-MILP-generated instances produce the best tuned Gurobi configuration on SC and CA, while on MIS the default configuration is marginally best but ACM-MILP still outperforms G2MILP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends G2MILP (Geng et al., 2023), the first learning-based MILP instance generator using a masked VAE on bipartite graphs, by introducing two key modifications: adaptive constraint selection via Gaussian sampling probabilities in the VAE latent space (replacing G2MILP's random single-constraint selection) and collective constraint modification via Louvain community detection (replacing G2MILP's one-at-a-time approach). It also contrasts with heuristic generators like Bowly (2019) that manually control a few graph statistics, positioning its data-driven approach as more generalizable. The bipartite graph representation and iterative reconstruction paradigm are inherited directly from G2MILP, while the community detection and probability-based selection are novel architectural additions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that preserving computational hardness (solving time, branching nodes) is more critical than matching statistical distributions for downstream utility, as ACM-MILP achieves lower statistical similarity than G2MILP on MIS yet substantially better hardness preservation. This reframes the evaluation criteria for MILP generators and suggests that structural features governing solver behavior are not captured by standard graph statistics. Limitations include evaluation on only three synthetic datasets with a single solver (Gurobi), and the hyperparameter tuning gains over G2MILP are modest. Open questions include scalability to real-world MILP instances with thousands of constraints, applicability to other solver families, and whether the community detection resolution parameter can be learned rather than manually tuned per dataset.
