## Problem

Imitation learning methods assume expert demonstrations are optimal, yet real-world demonstrations are frequently sub-optimal due to collection noise or imperfect operators. Existing approaches either embed confidence weighting into the IL training loop (creating unstable bi-level optimization) or require a guaranteed fraction of optimal demonstrations, limiting practical applicability. This paper asks whether sub-optimal demonstrations can be purified as a standalone preprocessing step before any IL algorithm is applied.

## Input / Output

The method takes two sets of state-action trajectory pairs: a small set of optimal demonstrations D_o (used to train the diffusion denoiser) and a large set of sub-optimal demonstrations D_s (to be purified). State and action vectors are concatenated into a single latent x for the diffusion process. The output is a set of purified state-action pairs that replace D_s in downstream imitation learning (BC or GAIL), ultimately yielding a learned agent policy. The key tunable is the intermediate reverse timestep i_r (out of N=1000 total steps), which controls the trade-off between noise smoothing and semantic preservation.

## Method

DP-IL performs a two-step diffusion-based purification: (1) a forward DDPM process adds Gaussian noise to sub-optimal demos up to an intermediate timestep i_r, which provably reduces the KL divergence between the diffused optimal and sub-optimal distributions (Theorem 1); (2) a reverse diffusion process, driven by a denoising network ε_φ trained on optimal demonstrations, reconstructs purified demos from the noisy intermediate state. The theoretical contribution establishes that the L2 distance between purified and true optimal demos is bounded (Theorem 2), and identifies an optimal i_r* near an inflection point of the lower bound, with a positive correlation between perturbation magnitude ‖δ‖ and i_r* (Theorem 4). Because purification is decoupled from policy training, the purified demos can be plugged into any existing IL framework (BC, GAIL, DICE variants) without modification, avoiding the bi-level optimization instability of confidence-based methods.

## Results

On MuJoCo (4 tasks × 6 sub-optimal demonstration settings), DP-BC achieves the highest average cumulative reward in 20 out of 24 cases, with particularly large margins under high-noise conditions (e.g., Ant-v2 D1-L1: 261 vs. −22 for BC-all and −156 for DWBC; HalfCheetah-v2 D1-L3: 4042 vs. 859 for BC-all and 694 for DWBC). On the RoboSuite Sawyer Nut Assembly task with real human demonstrations, DP-BC achieves a 0.86 success rate, outperforming BC (0.76), BCND (0.56), and DWBC (0.82). The optimal reverse diffusion step i_r* ranges from 1 to 100 depending on noise level and task difficulty, with a clear trend of smaller i_r* for higher-quality demonstrations, consistent with Theorem 4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DP-BC (offline) and DP-GAIL (online) are compared against five baselines addressing imperfect demonstrations. Against BCND (Sasaki & Yamashina, ICLR 2021), DP-BC wins in 21 of 24 MuJoCo settings, with the largest gaps under high noise (e.g., Ant-v2 D1-L1: 261 vs. 28; Walker2d D1-L1: 1697 vs. 102); BCND's self-weighting strategy is noted to be unstable and sometimes collapses. Against DWBC (Xu et al., ICML 2022), DP-BC wins in 20 of 24 cases; DWBC performs competitively only on D2-L3 (mild perturbation) but degrades sharply under strong noise (e.g., Ant-v2 D1-L1: −156 vs. 261). Against DemoDICE (Kim et al., ICLR 2021), DP-BC wins in 20 of 24 cases; DemoDICE struggles most with high-noise D1-L1 demonstrations (e.g., HalfCheetah: 22 vs. 1365). Against 2IWIL/IC-GAIL (Wu et al., ICML 2019) and WGAIL (Wang et al., ICML 2021) in the online GAIL setting, DP-GAIL consistently achieves higher cumulative reward throughout training (Figure 1). The one notable exception is Hopper-v2 D1-L3, where DP-BC (1825) underperforms BC-all (3090) and DemoDICE (3207), likely because the low noise level makes purification unnecessary and the small i_r*=1 provides minimal benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the adversarial purification framework of DiffPure (Nie et al., 2022), which showed that forward-then-reverse diffusion can remove adversarial perturbations, and repurposes it for denoising imperfect expert demonstrations in imitation learning. It departs from the dominant confidence-based and DICE-based approaches (WGAIL, BCND, DemoDICE, DWBC) that integrate demonstration weighting into the IL training loop, creating bi-level optimization; instead, DP-IL decouples purification as a preprocessing step, making it a plug-in compatible with any downstream IL method (BC, GAIL). The theoretical analysis extends DiffPure's KL-divergence monotonicity result to the IL setting and adds a novel characterization of the optimal reverse timestep via an inflection-point argument (Theorem 4).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling of noise purification from policy learning is a clean architectural contribution that avoids the instability of joint confidence-estimation and IL optimization, and the theoretical bounds on purification quality (Theorem 2) provide rare guarantees in this area. However, the method still requires a set of optimal demonstrations to train the diffusion model, which limits its applicability when no reliable expert data exists. Open questions include whether the perturbation-based sub-optimality assumption holds for structured errors (e.g., systematic bias in human demonstrations), how the approach scales to very high-dimensional continuous control, and whether the optimal i_r can be learned automatically rather than tuned per task.
