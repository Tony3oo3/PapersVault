## Problem

State-of-the-art NR-IQA methods built on pre-trained semantic backbones assume all extracted features are relevant to quality, but the paper empirically shows that semantic noise causes 'feature aliasing': image pairs with similar content but vastly different quality scores receive nearly identical feature representations, misleading the model into focusing on semantic details rather than true quality indicators. The paper addresses how to distinguish images with similar semantic information but different quality scores by reducing the model's sensitivity to quality-irrelevant semantic features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241113/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single distorted RGB image, randomly cropped into ten 224×224 patches, each further divided into 16×16 patches with 384-dimensional input tokens, and outputs a scalar quality score. During training, the model additionally consumes unlabeled images from auxiliary datasets (e.g., LIVEFB, KonIQ) whose pseudo-labels are generated by a pre-trained teacher for adversarial feature matching. At inference, only the encoder-decoder is used; the SNM, QCC, and DLE modules are discarded, incurring no extra computational overhead.

## Method

QFM-IQM builds on a ViT-S (DeiT III) encoder with a quality-aware MHCA decoder and introduces three training-time modules. The Semantic Noise Matching (SNM) module maintains a temporary memory of batch features and labels, then selects K samples that maximize feature dissimilarity (cosine distance) while minimizing quality-score difference (L2 norm), using their product as a matching score. The Quality Consistency Constraint (QCC) module blends these matched noise features into the original feature vector via weighted averaging (controlled by λ₁, λ₂) and forces the decoder to predict the same quality score for both the original and perturbed features, effectively performing adversarial training against semantic noise. The Distilled Label Extension (DLE) module uses a pre-trained teacher to generate pseudo-labels for unlabeled images, expanding the pool of adversarial noise samples and improving generalization. This adversarial perspective—treating semantically different but quality-similar samples as perturbations—differs from prior contrastive learning approaches that construct discriminative embedding spaces.

## Results

QFM-IQM achieves the best SRCC on 7 out of 8 standard NR-IQA benchmarks, with headline results of 0.981 (LIVE), 0.954 (CSIQ), 0.916 (TID2013), 0.906 (KADID), 0.891 (LIVEC), 0.922 (KonIQ), and 0.920 (SPAQ). The only dataset where it does not lead is LIVEFB, where Re-IQA achieves 0.645 SRCC versus QFM-IQM's 0.567. In cross-dataset validation (train on one dataset, test on another), QFM-IQM achieves the best SRCC across all six transfer settings, e.g., 0.820 on CSIQ (trained on LIVE) and 0.796 on LIVEC (trained on KonIQ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QFM-IQM outperforms all 15 compared baselines on 7 of 8 datasets in SRCC, with the closest overall competitor being DEIQT (Qin et al., 2023), which it edges out by 0.001–0.025 SRCC depending on the dataset (e.g., 0.954 vs 0.946 on CSIQ, 0.916 vs 0.892 on TID2013). The largest margin is on TID2013, where QFM-IQM leads the runner-up DACNN by 0.045 SRCC (0.916 vs 0.871), and on KADID where it beats MUSIQ by 0.031 (0.906 vs 0.875). The sole loss is on LIVEFB, where Re-IQA achieves 0.645 SRCC versus QFM-IQM's 0.567, likely because LIVEFB's extreme diversity (39,810 images) exceeds what the adversarial noise-matching strategy can fully capture. In cross-dataset transfer (Table 2), QFM-IQM wins all six settings, e.g., 0.820 SRCC on CSIQ (trained on LIVE) vs. 0.808 for Re-IQA, and 0.796 on LIVEC (trained on KonIQ) vs. 0.794 for DEIQT. Against the contrastive-learning baseline CONTRIQUE (Appendix Table 11), QFM-IQM leads on 7/8 datasets, with the largest gap on TID2013 (0.916 vs 0.843 SRCC) and KADID (0.906 vs 0.934 — here CONTRIQUE actually wins on KADID by 0.028 SRCC). Hand-crafted baselines (DIIVINE, BRISQUE, ILNIQE) are consistently outperformed by 0.1–0.5 SRCC across all datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the ViT-based IQA architecture of DEIQT (Qin et al., 2023), adopting its encoder-decoder design with a quality-aware cross-attention decoder, but departs fundamentally in how it handles the semantic features extracted by the pre-trained backbone. Where prior contrastive-learning IQA methods (QPT, Re-IQA, CONTRIQUE) aim to construct discriminative embedding spaces that separate quality from content, QFM-IQM reframes the problem through an adversarial lens: it treats semantically dissimilar but quality-similar images as adversarial perturbations and trains the model to be invariant to them. The distillation-based label expansion (DLE) module also extends the data-efficient training paradigm established by DEIQT by incorporating unlabeled authentic images to enrich the adversarial training pool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically validates a concrete failure mode in pre-trained-feature-based IQA—feature aliasing, where semantically similar images receive near-identical representations regardless of quality—providing a clear diagnostic that the field can build upon. The adversarial robustness framing offers a principled alternative to contrastive objectives for decoupling quality from content, and the cross-dataset generalization gains (e.g., +4–5 SRCC points on LIVEC→KonIQ) suggest the approach captures more transferable quality signals. Open questions include whether the method extends to video or generative-image quality assessment, whether the gains persist with larger backbones (ViT-L/BigG), and whether the training-time overhead of maintaining temporary memory and performing K-NN matching scales to very large unlabeled pools.
