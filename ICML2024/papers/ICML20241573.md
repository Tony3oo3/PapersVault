## Problem

Linearized Laplace Approximation (LLA) provides high-quality uncertainty estimates for pre-trained DNNs, but its cubic scaling in the number of training points or parameters makes it intractable for large-scale problems. Existing efficient variants (diagonal/KFAC Hessian approximations, Nyström-based ELLA) either degrade predictive distribution quality or still require linear passes over the full dataset. The paper asks whether LLA-quality uncertainty can be obtained at sub-linear cost in training set size while exactly preserving the pre-trained DNN's predictive mean.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained DNN at its MAP solution (obtained via standard back-propagation) along with training data points, and produces a full predictive distribution per test point: the mean is exactly the DNN's forward-pass output (no retraining of the network), while the variance is computed from a sparse GP with M_β inducing points. Training uses mini-batches of size 100, so per-iteration cost is O(M_β³) and independent of the total dataset size N. For classification, class probabilities are obtained via the softmax approximation of Daxberger et al. (2021b) applied to the GP predictive distribution.

## Method

VaLLA reinterprets LLA's predictive distribution as a GP posterior in function space (via the Neural Tangent Kernel as prior covariance), then approximates it with a decoupled sparse GP from Cheng & Boots (2017) where the mean and covariance are parameterized by separate sets of inducing points. By working in the dual RKHS representation, the mean function is anchored to the pre-trained DNN output g(·,θ̂), so only the covariance operator (governed by M_β inducing points Z_β and a matrix A) is optimized. To prevent the prior variance σ₀² from collapsing to zero under standard ELBO maximization (since the mean is fixed), the authors replace the ELBO with an α-divergence objective (α=1), which reduces to maximizing the log-likelihood of training data minus a KL term. Mini-batch stochastic optimization of this objective yields sub-linear training cost in N, and early-stopping on a validation set prevents overfitting of the predictive variance. The resulting inverse Hessian approximation has the form I_P/σ₀² + Φ_{Z_β}^T A Φ_{Z_β}, structurally analogous to the GGN matrix but with data-dependent terms replaced by learned inducing-point contributions.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-56, VaLLA achieves the best NLL (0.183) and competitive ECE (0.009), yielding the lowest mean rank (1.375) across all four ResNet architectures compared to ELLA (2.375) and Sampled LLA (2.000). On the large-scale Taxi regression dataset (3M instances), VaLLA with 200 inducing points reduces NLL from 3.753 (ELLA) to 3.280 and CQM from 0.227 to 0.188, while training on only 16.6% of the data due to early stopping. On FMNIST, VaLLA improves accuracy from 86.6% (MAP/ELLA/LLA*) to 87.6% and reduces NLL from 0.373 to 0.332, while also achieving the best OOD-AUC (0.933).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VaLLA outperforms ELLA (Nyström approximation) on CIFAR-10 across all ResNet sizes in NLL (e.g., 0.183 vs. 0.187 on ResNet-56) and achieves a lower mean rank (1.375 vs. 2.375), while also being faster due to mini-batch training. Against Sampled LLA (Antorán et al., 2023), VaLLA wins on NLL for all architectures (0.183 vs. 0.185 on ResNet-56) but loses slightly on ECE (0.009 vs. 0.015 on ResNet-56, though Sampled LLA is worse on ResNet-20 at 0.006). On regression tasks, VaLLA substantially outperforms ELLA on NLL and CQM (e.g., Taxi: NLL 3.280 vs. 3.753, CQM 0.188 vs. 0.227) but gives slightly worse CRPS. On FMNIST, VaLLA improves accuracy by 1.0% over MAP/ELLA/LLA* (87.6% vs. 86.6%) and reduces NLL by 11% (0.332 vs. 0.373), while also achieving the best OOD-AUC (0.933 vs. 0.874 for ELLA). VaLLA underperforms LLA KFAC and Sampled LLA on OOD-AUC for MNIST (0.921 vs. 0.949 and 0.954 respectively), and its ECE is slightly worse than ELLA on MNIST (0.010 vs. 0.008), though the gap is small. Overall, VaLLA is the best or second-best method in nearly all settings, with the clearest wins on large-scale regression and CIFAR-10 NLL.

## Relation to Prior Work

VaLLA sits at the intersection of three research threads: the Linearized Laplace Approximation (LLA) for Bayesian DNNs (Immer et al., 2021), the GP reinterpretation of LLA via the Neural Tangent Kernel (Khan et al., 2019), and sparse variational GPs with inducing points (Titsias, 2009). The key conceptual departure is the use of the decoupled RKHS parameterization (Cheng & Boots, 2016, 2017), where the mean and covariance of the sparse GP are optimized with separate inducing point sets. This allows VaLLA to anchor the predictive mean exactly to the pre-trained DNN output while only approximating the predictive variance, preserving LLA's post-hoc nature. Compared to ELLA (Deng et al., 2022), which uses a Nyström approximation with linear cost in N, VaLLA achieves sub-linear training cost via mini-batch stochastic optimization. Unlike Sampled LLA (Antorán et al., 2023), which generates posterior samples via an expensive EM procedure, VaLLA provides a closed-form predictive distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VaLLA makes LLA practically applicable to datasets with millions of training instances (e.g., the 3M-point Taxi dataset) where full LLA and even ELLA become prohibitive, while maintaining predictive distributions that closely match the full LLA. The sub-linear scaling in dataset size and the preservation of the DNN's predictive mean make it a practical tool for post-hoc uncertainty quantification in large-scale regression and classification. Key limitations include the cubic cost in the number of inducing points, the need for a validation set and early stopping to prevent overfitting of the prior variance, and the requirement for efficient Neural Tangent Kernel evaluation, which restricts applicability to very large or complex architectures like ImageNet-scale models. An open theoretical question is whether the MAP solution g(·,θ̂) always belongs to the RKHS induced by the NTK, which the paper assumes but cannot prove in general.
