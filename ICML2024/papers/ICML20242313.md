## Problem

Training-free conditional diffusion methods (e.g., DPS, Freedom, UGD) apply a loss-guidance correction step during denoising that causes intermediate samples to deviate from the data manifold, degrading sample quality. The paper proves this deviation is fundamentally driven by the Jensen gap in the Tweedie-based gradient estimation, which grows linearly with dimension, forcing prior methods to use small guidance step sizes and many sampling steps to avoid artifacts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242313/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a plug-in module on top of any pre-trained diffusion prior (DDPM, DDIM, Stable Diffusion/LDM) and accepts task-specific conditions: noisy measurements (masked pixels, downsampled images, blurred images) for linear inverse problems, a reference image for style or FaceID guidance, or a text prompt combined with a segmentation mask or reference image for composite guidance. Outputs are 256×256 RGB images (FFHQ, ImageNet, CelebA-HQ) or 512×512 images (Stable Diffusion tasks). The guidance is applied at configurable intervals during the reverse diffusion process, with a guidance rate hyperparameter controlling the trade-off between condition alignment and sample diversity.

## Method

DSG exploits the concentration phenomenon of high-dimensional isotropic Gaussians: the posterior distribution at each denoising step concentrates on a hypersphere of radius √n·σ_t around the DDIM mean μ_θ(x_t). Rather than applying the gradient correction step freely in ambient space (as DPS does), DSG formulates the guidance as an optimization problem that minimizes the loss gradient objective subject to the constraint that the next sample lies on this hypersphere. This yields a closed-form solution: the next sample is placed at the point on the hypersphere in the direction of negative gradient, effectively replacing the free gradient step with a normalized step of fixed magnitude √n·σ_t. To preserve diversity, the final direction is a weighted blend of this steepest-descent direction and the unconditional random sampling direction (analogous to classifier-free guidance), then rescaled to satisfy the spherical constraint. The entire modification requires only a few lines of code change relative to DPS and incurs negligible computational overhead.

## Results

On FFHQ 256×256 linear inverse problems, DPS+DSG achieves LPIPS of 0.115 (Inpainting), 0.211 (Super-Resolution), and 0.208 (Gaussian Deblurring), with FID scores of 15.77, 30.30, and 28.22 respectively — substantially outperforming DPS (LPIPS 0.212/0.214/0.257; FID 21.19/39.35/44.05) and LGD (LPIPS 0.159/0.231/0.229; FID 28.21/34.44/32.57). On CelebA-HQ FaceID guidance, Freedom+DSG reduces FaceID distance from 0.545 (Freedom) to 0.371 while improving FID from 39.61 to 34.29 and KID from 0.0165 to 0.0092. On ImageNet 256×256, DPS+DSG achieves SSIM 0.879 and LPIPS 0.116 for Inpainting, surpassing DDNM (SSIM 0.875, LPIPS 0.125) and DPS (SSIM 0.828, LPIPS 0.189).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSG consistently outperforms all nine baselines across every evaluated task and metric. Against the closest competitor DPS, DSG reduces Inpainting LPIPS by 46% (0.115 vs. 0.212) and FID by 26% (15.77 vs. 21.19) on FFHQ, while also improving Super-Resolution FID from 39.35 to 30.30 and Deblurring FID from 44.05 to 28.22. LGD, which uses Monte Carlo sampling to reduce estimation bias, is also surpassed by DSG on all three inverse problems (e.g., Inpainting LPIPS 0.115 vs. 0.159). Older methods — DDRM, MCG, PnP-ADMM, Score-SDE/ILVR — are significantly outperformed, with FID gaps exceeding 40 points in most cases. On FaceID guidance (CelebA-HQ), Freedom+DSG beats both Freedom and LGD on all three metrics (FaceID 0.371 vs. 0.545/0.533; FID 34.29 vs. 39.61/39.13; KID 0.0092 vs. 0.0165/0.0170). On ImageNet Inpainting, DPS+DSG edges out DDNM (SSIM 0.879 vs. 0.875; LPIPS 0.116 vs. 0.125) while substantially outperforming DPS (SSIM 0.828; LPIPS 0.189). Notably, DSG also demonstrates superior robustness to fewer denoising steps: at 20 DDIM steps, DPS degrades severely (Inpainting LPIPS 0.590) while DPS+DSG maintains LPIPS of 0.267.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free conditional diffusion line (MCG, DPS, Freedom, UGD, LGD) by identifying a shared failure mode—manifold deviation caused by the Jensen gap in loss-guidance estimation—and addressing it through a fundamentally different mechanism. Rather than correcting the gradient estimate (LGD's Monte Carlo approach) or projecting onto a linear tangent space (MPGD's autoencoder-based projection), DSG constrains the sampling direction to a hypersphere derived from the concentration of high-dimensional isotropic Gaussians, yielding a closed-form solution that avoids both the linear manifold assumption and the need for auxiliary networks. The method is explicitly positioned as a plug-and-play module that replaces the correction step in existing methods like DPS, Freedom, and UGD with a few lines of code.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DSG offers a practically significant efficiency gain: by enabling guidance step sizes up to 400× larger than DPS while staying on the intermediate manifold, it dramatically reduces the number of denoising steps needed for alignment (e.g., 20 steps where DPS fails entirely). The closed-form solution and task-agnostic hyperparameters (g_r ∈ [0.05, 0.2], interval i) make adoption nearly trivial for practitioners working with any training-free CDM. The acknowledged trade-off—reduced sample diversity due to the deterministic spherical constraint—remains an open problem, and the paper notes that the guidance rate g_r only partially mitigates this. Whether the spherical approximation holds well for anisotropic or non-Gaussian intermediate distributions in more complex generative settings (e.g., video, 3D) is unexplored.
