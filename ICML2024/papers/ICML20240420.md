## Problem

Conventional community detection methods assume every node belongs to a community, but real-world networks (e.g., protein-protein interaction networks, email networks) contain scattered nodes that lack any community affiliation. Forcing these scattered nodes into clusters introduces bias, obscures the true community structure, and can have practical consequences such as masking security threats or biological implications. The paper addresses this gap by proposing a method that explicitly identifies and excludes scattered nodes before recovering community structure among the remaining tight nodes.

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{N×N} along with four hyperparameters: the number of communities K̃ (determined via cross-validation), the number of sub-sampling replications L, the sub-sampling size Ñ (a fraction of N), and a tightness threshold α. Rather than assigning every node a community label, the output partitions nodes into a tight set T̂ (which receives community labels via spectral clustering) and a scattered set Ŝ (which receives no community assignment). The key intermediate representation is an N×N average co-membership matrix whose entries lie in [0,1] and encode the frequency with which node pairs are co-clustered across L resamples.

## Method

TCD exploits the observation that tight nodes within the same community are consistently co-clustered across random sub-samples (co-membership → 1), while scattered nodes are randomly assigned to communities in each resample (co-membership bounded away from 0 and 1). Concretely, it performs L rounds of network sub-sampling by randomly selecting Ñ rows from the adjacency matrix to form rectangular sub-matrices; for each, it applies SVD to extract the leading K̃ right singular vectors and runs k-means to obtain a full-network clustering. The L resulting binary co-membership matrices are averaged into a single matrix C̄. A depth-first search on the weighted graph defined by C̄ then extracts connected components where all pairwise co-membership values exceed 1−α, identifying these as tight components; all remaining nodes are labeled scattered. Finally, spectral clustering is applied only to the sub-adjacency matrix of tight nodes to recover community labels. The approach runs in O(N²) time and is trivially parallelizable across the L resampling iterations.

## Results

On four semi-real benchmark networks (Football, Polbooks, Polblogs, BlogCatalog) with scattered nodes added at 25% of original size, TCD achieves the highest F-score for scattered node identification across all datasets (83.0% on Football, 68.7% on Polbooks, 54.7% on Polblogs, 43.6% on BlogCatalog) and the lowest misclustering rate on three of four (11.4% Football, 13.3% Polblogs, 35.3% BlogCatalog). On the real COVID-19 PPI network (291 nodes), TCD achieves the best Ncut of 0.087 and the best OEPR of 0.000, indicating both well-separated tight communities and functionally distinct scattered proteins. In simulation studies, TCD's F-score approaches 1 and misclustering rate approaches 0 as network size grows, with stable performance across varying scattered node counts, connectivity strengths, and overall network densities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TCD dominates all 10 baselines on F-score for scattered node identification across every dataset, with the closest competitor being Extraction (39.7% on Football, 54.1% on Polbooks) and RADAR (36.8% on Football), while TCD achieves 83.0% and 68.7% respectively. On misclustering rate, TCD wins on Football (11.4% vs. next-best RADAR at 16.0%), Polblogs (13.3% vs. RCD at 26.5%), and BlogCatalog (35.3% vs. RADAR at 43.9%), but loses to RCD on Polbooks (31.0% vs. 21.1%), where RCD's robust spectral approach excels at community recovery despite being unable to identify scattered nodes. CBNs, the most directly comparable method for scattered node detection, is consistently outperformed by TCD by 30–70 percentage points on F-score. On the real COVID-19 PPI network, TCD achieves the lowest Ncut (0.087) and OEPR (0.000), outperforming RADAR (Ncut 0.104, OEPR 0.667) and PRN (Ncut 0.110, OEPR 0.000, though PRN's zero OEPR is attributed to an artifact of very few scattered proteins). HCD and RCD cannot report F-scores since they do not identify scattered nodes, limiting their applicability to the full TCD task.

## Relation to Prior Work

TCD builds directly on the network sub-sampling framework of Chen & Lei (2018) and the tight clustering idea for i.i.d. data from Tseng & Wong (2005), repurposing them for the network setting. It departs from Cai & Li's (2015) GSBM-based robust community detection, which minimizes the influence of scattered nodes without explicitly labeling them, and from Gaucher et al. (2021), which assumes scattered nodes are hubs. The key algorithmic departure is the use of an averaged co-membership matrix combined with depth-first search to explicitly partition nodes into tight and scattered sets before applying spectral clustering, rather than treating outlier removal as a preprocessing or regularization step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical limitation in community detection—forcing non-informative nodes into clusters introduces bias and obscures biological or social structure—with a method that has provable identification guarantees under GSBM and O(N²) scalability. The KEGG pathway enrichment on the COVID-19 PPI network demonstrates concrete biological interpretability: scattered proteins lack functional pathway association while tight communities map to coherent biological processes. Open questions include extending the theory to DCSBM, incorporating node attributes into the tightness criterion, and generalizing the core/scattered decomposition to nonparametric settings like graphons.
