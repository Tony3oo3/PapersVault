## Problem

The 'linear representation hypothesis' in LLMs has at least three informal interpretations—subspace, measurement (linear probing), and intervention (steering)—that have never been formally connected, and it is unclear which inner product makes geometric operations like cosine similarity and projection semantically meaningful. Because model representations are only identified up to invertible affine transformations, the Euclidean inner product has no principled justification, leaving the geometry of concept directions underdetermined.

## Input / Output

The method consumes counterfactual word pairs (e.g., 'king'/'queen' for male⇒female) sourced from BATS 3.0, the word2word bilingual lexicon, and ChatGPT-4-generated pairs, along with the LLM's unembedding matrix to compute Cov(γ). It produces unit-norm concept direction vectors in the unembedding space, a causal inner product matrix M = Cov(γ)⁻¹, and derived embedding representations λ̄_W = Cov(γ)⁻¹ γ̄_W used to construct steering vectors. Outputs include logit-level predictions for probing and modified context embeddings λ(x) + αλ̄_W for controlled intervention, validated on LLaMA-2-7B (4096-dim, 32K vocabulary).

## Method

The paper formalizes linear representation via counterfactual pairs: a concept W has an unembedding representation γ̄_W if all counterfactual differences γ(Y(1))−γ(Y(0)) lie in Cone(γ̄_W), and an embedding representation λ̄_W if context pairs that shift only W's distribution differ along Cone(λ̄_W). Theorem 2.2 proves the unembedding representation yields a logit-linear probe (measurement), while Theorem 2.5 proves the embedding representation yields a steering vector that changes only the target concept (intervention). The key geometric contribution is the causal inner product ⟨·,·⟩_C, defined so that causally separable concepts are orthogonal; Theorem 3.2 shows the Riesz isomorphism under this inner product maps each unembedding representation to its embedding representation, unifying the two spaces. An explicit closed form is derived (Theorem 3.4): under an independence assumption on uniformly sampled unembedding vectors, M = Cov(γ)⁻¹, making the inner product computable directly from the vocabulary's unembedding statistics. Experiments on LLaMA-2-7B confirm that 26 of 27 concepts exhibit linear subspace structure, that the causal inner product respects semantic orthogonality, and that constructed steering vectors selectively shift target-concept logits without affecting off-target concepts.

## Results

Across 27 concepts tested on LLaMA-2-7B, 26 exhibit a clear linear subspace representation in the unembedding space (only thing→part fails), with counterfactual pair differences substantially more aligned with the estimated concept direction than random word pairs. The estimated causal inner product (M = Cov(γ)⁻¹) renders causally separable concepts approximately orthogonal, and intervention vectors constructed via λ̄_W = Cov(γ)⁻¹ γ̄_W successfully increase target-concept logits while leaving off-target concept logits constant. For the Gemma-2B model, the Euclidean inner product fails to capture semantic orthogonality, while the causal inner product still works, highlighting the importance of the inner product choice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external baseline methods but instead contrasts the proposed causal inner product (M = Cov(γ)⁻¹) against the standard Euclidean inner product as the geometric structure for the representation space. On LLaMA-2-7B, both inner products yield approximate orthogonality for causally separable concepts, but the causal inner product is clearly superior: for example, frequent→infrequent (concept 23) has high Euclidean inner product with many separable concepts, while these values are much smaller under the causal inner product. Conversely, English→French has low Euclidean inner product with other language concepts but high causal inner product with French→German and French→Spanish (which share French), while remaining nearly orthogonal to German→Spanish. On the more recent Gemma-2B model, the Euclidean inner product fails to capture semantic structure at all, whereas the causal inner product still produces the expected block-diagonal orthogonality pattern, demonstrating that the causal inner product is not an artifact of LLaMA-2's initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Wang et al. (2023)'s causal/counterfactual framework for defining concepts in generative models, extending it from diffusion models to LLMs and adding a geometric layer. It unifies three previously separate threads in the linear representation literature—subspace regularities in word embeddings (Mikolov et al., 2013), logit-linear probing (Nanda et al., 2023; Gurnee & Tegmark, 2023), and activation steering (Wang et al., 2023; Turner et al., 2023)—by showing they are the same object under a specific non-Euclidean inner product. The causal inner product concept draws on causal representation learning (Schölkopf et al., 2021; Khemakhem et al., 2020) but applies it to the specific geometry of softmax LLMs, yielding a closed-form estimator via the unembedding covariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled answer to 'which inner product should you use when doing vector arithmetic in LLM representation space,' replacing the implicit Euclidean assumption with a computable causal structure (Cov(γ)^{-1}). This enables construction of both probes and steering vectors from counterfactual word pairs alone, without needing carefully curated prompt pairs. Key open questions include: whether the framework extends to intermediate layers and non-binary concepts, whether the free parameter D in the causal inner product family can be identified by additional principles, and whether the causal structure persists in models with tied embeddings (as Gemma-2B results suggest it may not hold under Euclidean geometry). The empirical scope is limited to a single 7B model and the final unembedding layer.
