## Problem

Naively combining offline IL pretraining (e.g., BC) with online GAIL finetuning fails because GAIL's randomly initialized discriminator acts discordantly against the pretrained policy, steering erroneous optimization and causing the policy to unlearn its pretraining knowledge. The paper asks whether one can design an offline IL method that simultaneously produces a near-expert policy and a discriminator already aligned with that policy, enabling seamless and fast online finetuning without the unlearning pathology.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two offline datasets: expert demonstrations D_e and imperfect/suboptimal demonstrations D_s (which may be highly suboptimal, e.g., random or medium-replay trajectories), each consisting of state-action-next_state transitions. During the online phase, it additionally receives state observations from environment rollouts. The output is a parameterized imitation policy π(a|s) plus an aligned discriminator D_0(s,a) constructed by stitching two components (d* and y*) already computed during offline training, requiring zero additional data collection or computation. The method operates on both low-dimensional continuous states and high-dimensional RGB image observations (via CNN backbones).

## Method

OLLIE reformulates the standard reverse-KL IL objective into a surrogate that incorporates imperfect data through an auxiliary reward R̃(s,a)=log(ρ̃^e/ρ̃^o), then applies convex conjugate to transform the dual problem into a convex-concave Stochastic Saddle Point (SSP) problem solvable with unbiased stochastic gradients entirely offline. The optimal policy is extracted via weighted behavior cloning using the SSP saddle-point variable y*, and the aligned discriminator is derived in closed form as D_0(s,a)=(1 + d*/(1−d*) · 1/y*)^{-1} by simply stitching the offline discriminator d* and y*—no Monte Carlo rollouts or extra computation needed. This discriminator is then directly initialized into GAIL for online finetuning, eliminating the random-discriminator misalignment that causes unlearning. The approach is model-free, avoids intermediate reward inference, and inherits O(1/ε) convergence and O(1/√n) generalization guarantees from the SSP formulation.

## Results

On 21 tasks across 5 domains (AntMaze, MuJoCo, Adroit, FrankaKitchen, vision-based Robomimic), OLLIE achieves consistent and significant improvements over all baselines in offline IL, often by 2–4× in normalized score, and surpasses baselines in 20 out of 24 evaluated settings in Table 1. In online finetuning, OLLIE avoids the unlearning phenomenon observed in all baselines and reaches expert-level performance within fewer than 10 environmental episodes (e.g., improving offline performance by 2× on vision-based tasks). Convergence is typically achieved within 0.2M offline steps, and OLLIE remains robust across varying quantities and qualities of expert/imperfect demonstrations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OLLIE outperforms all five baselines (BC, NBCU, CSIL, DWBC, MLIRL, ISWBC) in 20 out of 24 evaluated settings in Table 1. BC and NBCU are consistently the weakest, often scoring near zero or negative on Adroit tasks (e.g., 0.0 on door/relocate) and failing to leverage imperfect data. CSIL is the strongest competitor in MuJoCo with medium-expert data (e.g., 100.5 on ant, 101.6 on halfcheetah) but is outperformed by OLLIE by 15–23 points in those settings; on Adroit tasks, CSIL is competitive on door (24.8 vs. 28.9) but far behind on hammer (16.7 vs. 46.1) and relocate (7.7 vs. 31.2). DWBC and MLIRL show moderate performance on MuJoCo but collapse on Adroit (scores near 0–7), while ISWBC is competitive on hopper med.-rep. (90.3 vs. 101.0) and pen (49.5–51.3 vs. 67.4–68.0) yet still trails OLLIE. The largest margins appear on Adroit tasks where OLLIE achieves 3–5× the scores of the best baseline, highlighting its advantage in exploiting suboptimal demonstrations for dexterous manipulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separated streams—offline IL (BC, DICE family, DemoDICE, DWBC, ISWBC, MLIRL) and online IL (GAIL and its adversarial variants)—by identifying and solving the discriminator misalignment problem that causes naive offline-to-online combinations to fail. Technically, it departs from the DICE family (ValueDICE, DemoDICE, OptiDICE) by using convex conjugate to transform the dual into a convex-concave SSP with unbiased stochastic gradients, rather than the biased 'log-expectation-exponential-expectation' form those methods inherit. It also explicitly bypasses the intermediate IRL/reward-learning step that prior offline-to-online RL approaches (MILO, CLARE, CSIL) rely on, operating in a model-free manner instead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discriminator alignment insight is practically important for anyone attempting to warm-start online IL from offline pretraining, a common recipe in robotics and autonomous driving where interaction is costly. The method's model-free, unbiased formulation makes it scalable to high-dimensional and vision-based settings, and the byproduct for offline RL (Algorithm 2) suggests a broader connection between offline IL and offline RL that remains underexplored. Key limitations include reliance on GAIL's adversarial and on-policy nature for finetuning (sample inefficiency, training instability), and the open question of whether the approach extends to non-adversarial or off-policy IL methods.
