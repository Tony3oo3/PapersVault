## Problem

Spontaneous neural activity often manifests as repetitive spatiotemporal patterns (e.g., hippocampal replay), but analyzing these patterns in large-scale recordings (hundreds of neurons over days) remains challenging due to the lack of efficient, scalable detection methods. Existing approaches like convNMF and seqNMF perform well on modestly sized datasets but do not scale to the recording sizes needed for comprehensive study of structured spontaneous activity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241013/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a binary spike matrix X ∈ {0,1}^{N×T} (N neurons × T time bins) along with user-specified hyperparameters K (number of patterns) and M (filter width in time steps). It produces K optimized 2D spatiotemporal filters W^(k) ∈ R^{N×M}, their convolution responses x̂^(k) (1×T curves whose peaks mark pattern occurrences), a neuron reordering for visualizing the detected sequences, and a data-specific significance threshold α derived from a null distribution of 1000 random filters (set at 4σ above the mean).

## Method

convSeq optimizes K 2D spatiotemporal filters via backpropagation (unlike convNMF/seqNMF which use NMF-style optimization), enabling GPU acceleration and automatic differentiation. The loss function maximizes the variance of each filter's convolution response to the data (so that repeating patterns produce sharp peaks), regularized by a total variation term (suppressing background activity) and a cross-correlation penalty (encouraging filter diversity when K > 1). The convolution uses no padding along the neuron dimension and zero-padding along time, with softmax applied over the filter's time dimension to constrain total response. A second formulation parameterizes each filter row as a truncated Gaussian with fixed σ, reducing trainable parameters by a factor of N and incorporating an inductive bias toward Gaussian spike-timing jitter. Statistical significance of detected peaks is assessed by comparing against a null distribution constructed from 1000 random filters, providing uncertainty estimates without requiring multiple optimization runs.

## Results

On a grid of 81 synthetic datasets varying pattern sparsity, inter-sequence interval, sequence length, and jitter, convSeq achieves higher detection ROC AUC than both seqNMF and PP-Seq across all conditions. In speed benchmarks, convSeq is approximately 32× faster than PP-Seq on the largest dataset (500,000 timesteps) using CPU only, and a further 6× faster with GPU acceleration, yielding roughly 192× speedup overall. On real data, convSeq successfully disentangles forward and backward place-cell sequences in mouse CA1 (Rubin et al., 2019) and extracts precisely timed sequences from songbird HVC recordings, with filter response peaks exceeding the 4σ significance threshold.

## Baselines & Comparisons

convSeq is compared against two baselines: seqNMF (Mackevicius et al., 2019) and PP-Seq (Williams et al., 2020). In detection performance (Figure 12), convSeq outperforms both baselines on all 81 synthetic datasets across the full grid of difficulty parameters (sparsity, ISI, sequence length, jitter), with the gap widening under harder conditions (higher jitter, longer ISI, sparser sequences). In speed (Figure 13), convSeq is roughly 32× faster than PP-Seq and substantially faster than seqNMF on the largest dataset (500,000 timesteps, N=452) using CPU alone; enabling GPU provides an additional 6× speedup. The K-scaling experiment (Figure 14) confirms that convSeq maintains its speed advantage even as the number of assumed patterns K increases from 1 to 6, whereas both baselines degrade more steeply. The paper's headline claim of 'over 100× faster' is supported by the combined CPU+GPU speedup relative to PP-Seq on large datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

convSeq builds directly on the convolutional non-negative matrix factorization (convNMF) lineage, first applied to neural data by Peter et al. (2016) and extended by seqNMF (Mackevicius et al., 2019), which jointly estimate pattern templates and their temporal loadings via NMF-style optimization. The key departure is replacing the NMF optimization with standard backpropagation in PyTorch, optimizing only the spatiotemporal filters (not the loadings), and adding a total-variation regularizer plus a cross-correlation diversity penalty. A second formulation parameterizes each filter row as a truncated Gaussian, reducing trainable parameters by a factor of N and enabling inductive biases. Unlike prior methods, convSeq also provides per-detection uncertainty estimates via a null distribution of random filters, without requiring multiple optimization runs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The >100× speedup over seqNMF and PP-Seq is practically significant for analyzing large-scale spontaneous activity recordings (hundreds of neurons over days), a regime where prior methods become intractable. However, the method still struggles with unidirectional patterns sharing neurons (producing spurious sub-threshold peaks) and requires multiple runs with different filter widths for strongly time-warped sequences. Open questions include whether per-neuron jitter parameters, dilated/strided convolutions, or more sophisticated diversity penalties could handle the remaining failure cases, and whether the approach generalizes to non-binary or multi-area recordings.
