## Problem

Equitable online resource allocation lacks optimal or near-optimal policies when equity is measured under ex-ante metrics (expected fairness per agent) rather than ex-post (realized fairness), and when the benchmark is a clairvoyant optimal rather than a distribution-free bound. The paper identifies two distinct equity models—external (heterogeneous demands) and internal (homogeneous demands, heterogeneous demographics)—and shows that ex-ante and ex-post metrics yield fundamentally different optimal policies and competitive ratios, with no prior work providing optimal algorithms for the ex-ante setting.

## Input / Output

For external equity promotion (EEP), the algorithm receives the full discrete demand distributions {p_ij} for n agents in an offline phase, then at each round i observes the realized demand D_i and must irrevocably commit a continuous amount X_i ≤ min(D_i, R_i) before seeing future demands. For internal equity promotion (IEP), the algorithm receives the Poisson parameter λ and integer serving capacity b, then at each arrival makes an irrevocable binary serve/reject decision. The EEP output is a real-valued allocation per agent; the IEP output is a binary vector indicating which agents are served, with the constraint that at most b agents total are accepted.

## Method

For EEP under ex-ante with independent demands, the paper formulates a benchmark LP (1) whose optimal value upper-bounds the clairvoyant optimal, then designs an LP-based policy (ATT) that uses Monte-Carlo simulation to estimate expected remaining supply and applies attenuation factors β_ij to achieve exactly half the LP's target filling rate per agent, yielding an optimal 1/2 competitive ratio. A second policy (DTH) uses an equivalent integral-form LP (6) with a double-thresholding mechanism: a CDF-based threshold p_i gates whether an agent is served at all, and a cap β_i limits the maximum allocation. For IEP under ex-ante, the paper characterizes the optimal solution to a concave program (5) via a unimodality argument on the arrival distribution, showing the optimal acceptance probabilities are uniform over a prefix of agents, then implements this via dependent rounding (Algorithm 2) to produce an optimal randomized policy. The key technical insight across both models is that simulation-based attenuation converts an offline LP solution into a valid online policy by ensuring the expected resource consumed by each agent matches the LP's allocation exactly.

## Results

For External Equity Promotion (EEP) under ex-ante with independent demands, the LP-based policy ATT achieves an optimal competitive ratio of 1/2 relative to the benchmark LP (1), and the double-thresholding policy DTH also achieves the same 1/2 CR. For Internal Equity Promotion (IEP), the randomized-rounding policy RD is proven optimal among all online policies under ex-ante, while Greedy is 1-competitive (optimal) under ex-post. For correlated demands, no online policy can achieve a competitive ratio better than O(1/ln n) under either ex-post or ex-ante metrics. An unconditional upper bound of 1/(2−2/e) ≈ 0.791 is established for EEP under ex-ante relative to a clairvoyant optimal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper, so comparisons are against analytical benchmarks rather than empirical baselines. The primary benchmark is the clairvoyant optimal (OPT), which observes all demand realizations in advance. ATT achieves a 1/2 competitive ratio relative to the benchmark LP (1), and this is shown to be tight via Example 3 (IID demands of 1 or ∞). Relative to the clairvoyant optimal directly, an unconditional upper bound of 1/(2−2/e) ≈ 0.791 is established, though the gap between 1/2 and this bound remains open. For IEP under ex-ante, the RD policy is proven optimal among all online policies (unconditional), but its competitiveness relative to the clairvoyant optimal is left as future work. The closest prior work, Manshadi et al. (2021), addresses EEP under ex-post with correlated demands and a distribution-free benchmark, a fundamentally different setting from this paper's ex-ante/independent-demands focus.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fair online resource allocation literature by introducing a principled distinction between external equity (heterogeneous demands) and internal equity (heterogeneous demographics), and by analyzing both under ex-ante and ex-post metrics. For external equity, it departs from Manshadi et al. (2021), which studied ex-post equity with arbitrarily correlated demands against a distribution-free benchmark, by focusing on ex-ante equity with independent demands against a clairvoyant optimal. For internal equity, it simplifies prior group-level fairness work in ride-hailing (Ma et al., 2022; Nanda et al., 2020) to individual-level equity with a Poisson arrival model, and borrows the dependent rounding technique from Gandhi et al. (2006) and the attenuation framework from stochastic matching (Brubach et al., 2020; Ma, 2018).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes clean impossibility results (O(1/ln n) competitive ratio for correlated demands under both metrics) and optimal or near-optimal policies for tractable subproblems, providing a foundational taxonomy for equity in online allocation. Key open questions include whether a constant-competitive policy exists for EEP under ex-post with independent demands, whether the 1/2 ratio can be improved under natural bounded-demand assumptions, and how to extend the individual-level IEP results to group-level fairness. The practical relevance to vaccine distribution, refugee resettlement, and emergency aid allocation is significant, though the current models are deliberately simplified (single resource, individual-level groups).
