## Problem

Existing theoretical guarantees for kernel ridge regression with random features (KRR-RF) rely critically on the i.i.d. assumption, leaving it unclear whether learning accuracy is compromised when data exhibits dependence structures common in time series, climate, and speech applications. This paper asks: does, and to what extent does, the dependency structure within data affect the convergence rate of KRR-RF, and can minimax optimality be preserved under weaker mixing conditions?

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequentially collected dependent data pairs (X_i, Y_i) where X_i ∈ R^d and Y_i ∈ [-U, U], with the process {Z_i} assumed stationary and τ-mixing (either exponential or polynomial decay). The kernel is bounded (sup K ≤ κ²) and the random feature mapping ψ is bounded (|ψ| ≤ κ), with M random features drawn from a distribution π where M ≪ n. The output is a function estimate f̂_{M,λ}(x) evaluated in the L²(ρ_X) norm, with the regularization parameter λ chosen via cross-validation from a discrete grid.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a non-asymptotic error analysis of KRR-RF under τ-mixing dependence, built on a Bernstein-type concentration inequality for Banach-valued τ-mixing sums (Blanchard & Zadorozhnyi, 2019) combined with integral operator theory. A key technical challenge is that the random feature mapping φ_M(·) is itself random, so the paper introduces Assumption 4.1 (sub-exponential tail on the derivative of ψ) to establish Lipschitz continuity of φ_M with high probability, which in turn preserves the τ-mixing property of derived stochastic processes via a Lipschitz mapping proposition. The error is decomposed into sample variance, empirical error, and approximation error, each bounded using the τ-mixing concentration inequality with problem-specific ℓ₁ and ℓ₂ effective sample sizes that depend on the decay rate. The main results show minimax optimality O_P(n^{-2r/(2r+α)}) (up to log factors) under exponential τ-mixing decay, but a strictly slower rate under polynomial decay where the effective sample size is degraded. Tighter lower bounds on M are further derived using the maximum random feature effective dimension N_∞(λ).

## Results

Theoretically, for τ-mixing data with exponential decay coefficient, KRR-RF achieves the minimax optimal rate O_P(n^{-2r/(2r+α)}) (up to a logarithmic factor), matching the i.i.d. rate from Rudi & Rosasco (2017). For polynomial decay with rate γ₁, the rate degrades to O_P(n^{-4γ₁r/(4γ₁r+2r+2γ₁α+1)}), which is sub-optimal but recovers the i.i.d. rate as γ₁→∞. Numerically, on synthetic time-series and Markov chain examples, prediction error decreases with training size n and stabilizes; the optimal M/n ratio is approximately 0.01 (i.e., M ≈ 40 for n=4000), yielding near-optimal accuracy at minimal computational cost. On the Delhi climate dataset (1462 training, 114 test samples), KRR-RF with M=30 produces a predicted mean-temperature curve that closely tracks the observed curve.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not conduct head-to-head experimental comparisons against competing kernel approximation methods (e.g., Nyström subsampling or random sketching); instead, the primary comparison is theoretical. Against the i.i.d. baseline of Rudi & Rosasco (2017), KRR-RF under exponential τ-mixing preserves the same minimax rate and requires the same number of random features M ≥ Cn^{(1+α(2r−1))/(2r+α)} (up to log factors), showing no additional computational burden from dependence. Under polynomial τ-mixing, the rate is strictly slower than the i.i.d. rate, with the gap controlled by γ₁: larger γ₁ (weaker dependence) yields a rate closer to optimal. The required number of RFs under polynomial decay is n^{2γ₁(1+α(2r−1))/(4γ₁r+2r+2αγ₁+1)}, which decreases as γ₁ decreases (stronger dependence), reflecting that stronger dependence reduces the effective information in the kernel matrix. The numerical experiments confirm that a small M/n ratio (~0.01) suffices for accurate prediction on both synthetic and real dependent data, validating the theoretical lower bounds on M.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the minimax optimality theory of KRR with random features established by Rudi & Rosasco (2017) from the i.i.d. setting to τ-mixing dependent data, a strictly weaker dependence condition than the α-mixing or β-mixing assumptions used in prior dependent-data kernel methods (Modha & Masry, 1996; Yu, 1994). The technical backbone is a Bernstein-type concentration inequality for τ-mixing Banach-valued sums from Blanchard & Zadorozhnyi (2019), which prior work applied to full KRR and Nyström subsampling but not to the random-features variant. A key departure from prior dependent-data kernel analyses is the need to handle the randomness of the feature mapping itself, requiring a sub-exponential tail assumption on the derivative of the random feature function to preserve the mixing property through the Lipschitz transformation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a genuine gap: no prior theoretical analysis existed for KRR-RF under dependent data, despite the method's widespread use in time-series and sequential-data applications. The clean separation between exponential decay (minimax rate preserved, no extra RFs needed) and polynomial decay (sub-optimal rate, with the gap governed by the decay exponent γ₁) gives practitioners a concrete diagnostic for when random features remain effective under dependence. The authors explicitly flag as open whether the polynomial-decay sub-optimality is a technical artifact or an inherent limitation, noting that establishing a matching minimax lower bound would require new tools. Practical impact is moderate: the theory validates KRR-RF for common time-series models (AR processes, Markov chains) but the experimental validation is limited to small-scale synthetic examples and a single climate dataset.
