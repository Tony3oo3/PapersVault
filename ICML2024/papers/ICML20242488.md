## Problem

Previous proofs of minimax optimality for score-based diffusion models (e.g., Oko et al., 2023) required the true data density to be bounded below by a positive constant and supported on a compact set, assumptions that exclude natural multi-modal or mixture distributions. This paper removes both the density lower bound and compact support assumptions, establishing minimax optimality under only a sub-Gaussian tail condition (plus Sobolev smoothness for the nonparametric rate).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242488/cropped_page11_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes n i.i.d. samples from the true distribution p_0 and a single Gaussian noise draw z ~ N(0, T I_d) to initialize the reverse SDE. It outputs a sample from p_0 by integrating the reverse SDE with a truncated kernel score estimator, halting at an early stopping time t_0 = n^{-2/(2β+d)} rather than running to t=0. The score estimator itself is a plug-in ratio of a kernel density estimator and its gradient, with bandwidth h = C√(t/log n) and kernel order ℓ = log n, truncated at threshold ρ_n = 1/(nt^{d/2}).

## Method

The core technical contribution is a truncated kernel score estimator ŝ_t(x) = ∇p̂_t(x)/p̂_t(x) · 1{p̂_t(x) > ρ_n} that achieves a time-dependent MSE bound of Õ(n^{-1}t^{-(d+2)/2}(t^{d/2} ∨ 1)) under only sub-Gaussianity. The truncation at ρ_n = 1/(nt^{d/2}) is the key mechanism: in high-density regions the KDE's MSE is proportional to p_t(x) so the score error is controlled by standard bias-variance arguments, while in low-density regions the sub-Gaussian assumption (via Rényi entropy and moment bounds on the score) ensures the contribution stays at the minimax rate. The total variation error between the true distribution and the generated sample is then bounded by applying Girsanov's theorem to the path measures of the true and approximate reverse SDEs, combined with Pinsker's inequality to convert the KL divergence into TV distance. An early stopping strategy at t_0 = n^{-2/(2β+d)} balances the integrated score estimation error against the early stopping error TV(X_0, X_{t_0}), yielding the minimax rate Õ(n^{-β/(2β+d)}) for the Sobolev class with β ≤ 2. The kernel is explicitly constructed from Legendre polynomials with compact support on [-1,1]^d, ensuring the required moment-vanishing properties up to order ℓ = log n.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.8) establishes that the total variation sampling error of the score-based diffusion model is bounded by polylog(n) · n^{−β/(2β+d)} under only sub-Gaussianity and Sobolev smoothness (β ≤ 2), matching the classical minimax rate in nonparametric density estimation up to logarithmic factors. The intermediate score estimation error (Theorem 3.5) is shown to be \widetilde{O}(n^{−1}t^{−(d+2)/2}(t^{d/2} ∨ 1)), which decays in time due to the Gaussian smoothing effect. The single numerical experiment on 28×28 MNIST with a U-Net score estimator confirms the predicted −3/2 slope for the 1-dimensional MSE convergence rate versus time (Figure 1), consistent with the theoretical exponent for d=1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or comparison tables; the only numerical validation is an illustrative MNIST experiment confirming the predicted convergence slope. In terms of theoretical comparisons, the key advance over Oko et al. (2023) is the removal of the density lower bound and compact support assumptions: Oko et al. achieve the same n^{−2s/(d+2s)} rate but require p₀(x) > C on a bounded domain, which implies LSI via Holley-Stroock and excludes multi-modal or well-separated mixture distributions. Compared to Chen et al. (2023b) and Benton et al. (2023), who assume a uniform-in-time upper bound on score estimation error, this paper derives a time-dependent score error that decays as t increases, yielding a sharper (and minimax-optimal) bound. The sub-Gaussian assumption (A1) is strictly weaker than the LSI assumed by Lee et al. (2022) and Wibisono & Yang (2022), and weaker than the bounded-support-plus-density-lower-bound of Oko et al. (2023). A concurrent work by Wibisono et al. (2024) achieves a similar \tilde{O}(n^{−2/(d+4)}) rate for score estimation under sub-Gaussianity plus Lipschitz score, but does not address the full diffusion sampling error or the Sobolev-class minimax optimality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the minimax optimality analysis of diffusion models initiated by Oko et al. (2023), which required the true density to be bounded below on compact support. It also departs from the uniform-in-time score error assumption used by Chen et al. (2023b) and Benton et al. (2023), instead deriving a time-dependent error bound that captures the natural decay of score estimation error as noise increases. The technical core borrows the truncated kernel score estimator from Zhang (1997) in empirical Bayes and the bias-variance framework from Tsybakov (2009), but applies them with refined analysis to the low-density regime that previously necessitated the density lower bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the density lower bound assumption, this result establishes minimax optimality for diffusion model sampling over a substantially broader class of distributions (all sub-Gaussian, including multi-modal and well-separated mixtures), revealing that the smoothing effect of the forward diffusion process is sufficient to handle low-density regions without additional structural assumptions. Open questions include whether the result extends to Sobolev smoothness β > 2, whether neural network-based score estimators (the practical standard) achieve comparable bounds, and how discretization error interacts with the early stopping strategy in finite-precision implementations. The purely theoretical nature and reliance on kernel estimators limit immediate practical impact, but the result clarifies the fundamental statistical limits of diffusion-based sampling.
