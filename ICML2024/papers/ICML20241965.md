## Problem

Existing deep anomaly detection methods for tabular data capture either feature-feature dependencies (e.g., internal contrastive learning) or sample-sample dependencies (e.g., influence-based methods), but no prior work successfully combines both. The authors hypothesize that both dependency types are class-dependent and that their joint exploitation is crucial for accurately flagging anomalies in structured data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241965/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a validation sample and applies each mask from a deterministic bank of all feature-subset combinations up to size r, concatenating each masked sample with the entire unmasked training set to form a single matrix fed to the NPT. The output is a scalar anomaly score per validation sample, computed as the mean reconstruction loss (MSE for numerical features, cross-entropy for categorical features) averaged over all masks in the bank. The non-parametric use of the full training set at inference is the key structural difference from standard parametric anomaly detectors.

## Method

NPT-AD trains a Non-Parametric Transformer with alternating Attention Between Datapoints (ABD) and Attention Between Attributes (ABA) layers via stochastic masked-feature reconstruction on normal samples only. At inference, the entire unmasked training set is concatenated with the masked validation sample and passed through the NPT, so the model reconstructs masked features by attending both across features within a sample (ABA) and across samples in the training set (ABD). The anomaly score is the average reconstruction error over a deterministic mask bank of all combinations up to r masked features. The core insight is that since the model learned feature-feature and sample-sample interactions exclusively from normal samples, anomalies will exhibit higher reconstruction error because the learned class-dependent relations fail to generalize to anomalous feature configurations and anomalous samples cannot effectively attend to the normal training set.

## Results

Across 31 benchmark tabular datasets, NPT-AD achieves a mean F1-score of 68.8% and mean AUROC of 89.8%, outperforming the best competing method (Shenkar & Wolf's internal contrastive learning, 67.2% F1 / 88.8% AUROC) by 2.4% and 1.2% respectively. NPT-AD also attains the lowest mean rank (3.1 for F1, 2.5 for AUROC) and the smallest variance (std 2.0 for F1, 0.8 for AUROC) among all 18 tested approaches, indicating more consistent performance across datasets. The method achieves near-perfect scores (F1 ≥ 95%) on 8 of 31 datasets including Lymphography, Ionosphere, Satimage-2, Pendigits, Shuttle, and Mulcross.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NPT-AD outperforms all 10 baselines on mean F1-score across the 31-dataset benchmark. The closest competitor is Shenkar & Wolf's internal contrastive learning (67.2% F1, 88.8% AUROC), which NPT-AD edges out by 1.6% F1 and 1.0% AUROC; notably, NPT-AD also shows lower variance (std 2.0 vs 2.9). Among other deep methods, the vanilla Transformer (56.2% F1) and NeuTraL-AD (50.8% F1) trail by 12.6% and 18.0% F1 respectively, while GOAD (51.0%) and DROCC (32.7%) are further behind. Among non-deep baselines, KNN (65.8% F1) is the strongest, followed by IForest (52.6%), COPOD (44.2%), PIDForest (39.8%), and RRCF (37.0%). NPT-AD achieves the best mean rank (3.1) and lowest standard deviation (2.0) among all methods, suggesting the non-parametric use of the full training set at inference stabilizes the anomaly score across random seeds. The ablation (Table 1) further confirms that combining both feature-feature and sample-sample dependencies (NPT-AD: 68.8% F1) substantially outperforms using only feature-feature (Transformer: 56.2%) or only sample-sample (Mask-KNN: 57.5%) dependencies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Non-Parametric Transformers (NPTs; Kossen et al., 2021), originally designed for supervised tabular classification, into a reconstruction-based anomaly detection framework via masked feature prediction. It sits at the intersection of three research threads: reconstruction-based AD (autoencoders, PCA), self-supervised/contrastive AD for tabular data (GOAD, NeuTraL-AD, Shenkar & Wolf's internal contrastive learning), and deep architectures that model both feature-feature and sample-sample interactions (SAINT, NPT). The key departure from prior AD methods is that previous tabular AD approaches exploited either feature-feature dependencies (Shenkar & Wolf) or sample-sample dependencies (Thimonier et al., 2022) in isolation, whereas NPT-AD is the first to jointly leverage both through a single masked-reconstruction objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ablation study (NPT-AD vs. vanilla transformer vs. Mask-KNN) provides concrete evidence that neither dependency type alone suffices across all datasets, and that the optimal combination is dataset-dependent — a useful diagnostic for practitioners choosing AD methods. The contamination analysis reveals a 'double contamination effect' unique to non-parametric inference (anomalies in the training set both corrupt learned feature relations and serve as attention targets at inference), which is a practically important caveat for real-world deployment. Open questions include whether external retrieval or hybrid parametric/non-parametric schemes can retain the sample-sample benefit while reducing the O(n) inference cost and contamination sensitivity that currently limit scalability to high-dimensional or very large datasets.
