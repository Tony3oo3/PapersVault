## Problem

Existing methods for nonconvex-PL bilevel optimization either achieve suboptimal convergence complexity (e.g., O(ε^{-1.5}) or O(ε^{-2})) or rely on expensive Hessian/Jacobian matrices and their inverses. The paper addresses the open question of whether a Hessian/Jacobian-free method can simultaneously achieve the optimal O(ε^{-1}) gradient complexity for finding an ε-stationary solution under the Polyak-Lojasiewicz condition.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the upper-level objective f(x,y), lower-level objective g(x,y) satisfying the PL condition, a convex regularization φ(x), initial points (x₁, y₁, v₁), and a finite-difference parameter δ_ε, and produces an ε-stationary solution (x, y) with ‖∇F(x)‖² ≤ ε where F(x) = f(x, y*(x)). Each iteration requires only 7 first-order gradient evaluations with O(p+d) per-iteration cost, and the algorithm operates in a single loop rather than the double or triple loops used by competing methods.

## Method

HJFBiO replaces expensive Hessian/Jacobian matrix computations with central finite-difference estimators that approximate Hessian-vector and Jacobian-vector products using only first-order gradients of g. A key innovation is a new projection operator M_{r_h} that constrains the projected Hessian-vector product at the vector level, avoiding the costly matrix-level eigenvalue projection S_{[μ,L_g]} required by prior work. An auxiliary vector v is maintained and updated via a projected gradient step on a strongly convex subproblem to approximate the projected Hessian-inverse vector product, eliminating explicit matrix inversion. The algorithm performs three single-loop updates per iteration—lower-level y via gradient descent, upper-level x via a proximal step on the estimated hyper-gradient, and auxiliary v via a projected gradient step—yielding the optimal O(ε^{-1}) gradient complexity under both global and local PL conditions.

## Results

HJFBiO achieves the optimal gradient complexity of O(ε^{-1}) for finding an ε-stationary solution of nonconvex-PL bilevel problems, matching the theoretical lower bound for first-order nonconvex optimization. It is the only method in the comparison that simultaneously achieves O(ε^{-1}) complexity, uses a single-loop structure, and is fully Hessian/Jacobian-free (requiring only O(p+d) computation per iteration). In the bilevel PL game experiments (d=100, d=200), HJFBiO converges fastest in norm of gradient vs. iterations among all compared methods. On the hyper-representation learning task, HJFBiO achieves the best distance-to-solution across all baselines and is comparable to MGBiO on loss, while requiring lower per-iteration computation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HJFBiO is the only method that combines all three desirable properties: optimal O(ε^{-1}) gradient complexity, single-loop structure, and Hessian/Jacobian-free computation. Compared to MGBiO (Huang, 2023b), which also achieves O(ε^{-1}) in a single loop, HJFBiO eliminates the need for expensive projected Hessian/Jacobian matrices and their inverses, reducing per-iteration cost from O(p²+d·p) to O(p+d); experiments confirm HJFBiO converges faster in the PL game and achieves better distance on hyper-representation learning. Against F²BA (Chen et al., 2024), which also reaches O(ε^{-1}) but requires a double loop and Lipschitz Hessian of f(x,y), HJFBiO avoids the stricter smoothness assumption and the extra inner loop. Hessian/Jacobian-free competitors like BOME (O(ε^{-1.5})), V-PBGD (O(ε^{-1.5})), SLM (O(ε^{-3.5})), and Prox-F²BA (O(ε^{-1.5})) all have strictly worse complexity. GALET (Xiao et al., 2023) nominally reports O(ε^{-1}) but the paper argues its convergence constants become meaningless (L_w → ∞) when the Hessian is near-singular, and it uses a triple loop with Hessian computations. In the hyper-representation learning experiments, HJFBiO outperforms all baselines on the distance metric ‖UUᵀ−H*‖_F²/‖H*‖_F² and is comparable to MGBiO on loss, while requiring fewer gradient evaluations per iteration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work on nonconvex-PL bilevel optimization: the momentum-based methods of Huang (2023b) that achieved optimal O(ε^{-1}) complexity but required expensive projected Hessian/Jacobian inverses, and the Hessian/Jacobian-free surrogate approach of Yang et al. (2023b) that used auxiliary vectors to avoid second-order computation. The key departure is combining these ideas with a novel vector-level projection operator M_{r_h} that replaces the expensive matrix-level spectral projection S_{[μ,L_g]}, yielding a single-loop algorithm with O(p+d) per-iteration cost. The paper also critically re-examines GALET (Xiao et al., 2023), arguing that its convergence guarantees become vacuous when the Hessian's non-zero singular values approach zero, and distinguishes itself from F2BA (Chen et al., 2024) by not requiring Lipschitz Hessian of the upper-level function f(x,y).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result closes a practical gap in nonconvex-PL bilevel optimization: practitioners can now achieve the theoretically optimal O(ε^{-1}) gradient complexity without computing or inverting Hessian/Jacobian matrices, which is critical for large-scale applications like hyperparameter tuning of deep networks where second-order information is infeasible. The single-loop structure further reduces wall-clock time compared to double- or triple-loop alternatives. Open questions include whether the finite-difference parameter δ_ε can be adapted online (currently it must scale as O(1/√T)), whether the 7-gradient-per-iteration overhead can be reduced, and whether the analysis extends to stochastic or distributed settings. The paper's experiments are limited to synthetic PL games and small-scale matrix sensing, leaving open the question of performance on large-scale real-world bilevel problems.
