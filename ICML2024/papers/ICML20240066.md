## Problem

Deep neural networks lack a principled, distance-aware mechanism for uncertainty quantification: unlike Gaussian Processes, they cannot naturally measure how far a new input lies from the training data. Existing deterministic uncertainty methods rely on auxiliary constraints (e.g., spectral normalization) that may undermine calibration, while ensemble methods are computationally prohibitive for deployment. The paper seeks a single-model, deterministic approach that is inherently distance-aware without requiring such external regularizers.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard input (e.g., a 32×32 CIFAR-10 image or a tabular data point) and produces both a prediction (class label or regression value) and a deterministic uncertainty score. The uncertainty is the expected Kullback-Leibler divergence between the input's encoder distribution (a multivariate Gaussian) and the learned codebook of k centroid Gaussians, computed in a single forward pass without Monte Carlo sampling. The codebook size k is a hyperparameter (10 for CIFAR-10, 1000 for ImageNet) and the latent dimension is typically 8–80.

## Method

DAB replaces the VIB's single variational marginal with a codebook of k centroid distributions that compresses the training dataset's encoders, formulated as a Rate-Distortion Finite Cardinality (RDFC) problem over the parameter space of a distributional family rather than Euclidean feature space. Training uses an alternating minimization algorithm (inspired by Blahut-Arimoto) cycling through four steps: gradient descent on encoder/decoder parameters, analytical computation of soft assignment probabilities π_x via a Boltzmann-weighted formula, gradient descent on centroid parameters, and analytical update of the marginal π. The key insight is that the distortion function operates on distributions of embeddings (KL divergence between Gaussians) rather than on points, yielding a statistical distance that is more informative than Euclidean distance for OOD detection. For misclassification prediction, a max-margin loss term is added to push misclassified training points away from the codebook, improving calibration. The approach can be applied post-hoc to pre-trained backbones (e.g., ResNet-50) by freezing the backbone and training only the DAB head and codebook.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 OOD detection, DAB achieves 0.986 AUROC / 0.994 AUPRC on SVHN (far OOD) and 0.922 AUROC / 0.915 AUPRC on CIFAR-100 (near OOD), outperforming all baselines including the 5-model Deep Ensemble (0.970/0.984 and 0.916/0.902 respectively). For misclassification prediction on CIFAR-10, DAB reaches 0.930 Calibration AUROC, closing the gap to the Deep Ensemble (0.951) while surpassing all single-forward-pass DUMs. On ImageNet-1K, DAB with a pre-trained ResNet-50 backbone achieves 0.866 Calibration AUROC and 0.732 OOD AUROC (vs. ImageNet-O), beating the 5-model ensemble (0.861 and 0.642) with only 13M trainable parameters versus 118M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAB outperforms all seven baselines on both OOD detection tasks (SVHN and CIFAR-100) across both AUROC and AUPRC metrics. Against the strongest single-model DUM, SNGP, DAB gains +1.5 AUROC points on SVHN (0.986 vs 0.971) and +1.4 on CIFAR-100 (0.922 vs 0.908); against DDU, the gap widens to +2.8 AUROC on CIFAR-100 (0.922 vs 0.894). DAB also surpasses the 5-model Deep Ensemble on OOD detection (0.986 vs 0.970 on SVHN, 0.922 vs 0.916 on CIFAR-100) while using a single forward pass and only 36.5M parameters versus 182.4M. On calibration (misclassification prediction, Table 4), DAB (0.930) trails the Deep Ensemble (0.951) by 2.1 points but leads SNGP (0.897), DUQ (0.889), DUE (0.856), and DDU (0.632) substantially. On ImageNet-1K (Table 5), DAB with a frozen pre-trained ResNet-50 backbone beats the 5-model ensemble on both Calibration AUROC (0.866 vs 0.861) and OOD AUROC vs ImageNet-O (0.732 vs 0.642) with roughly 9x fewer trainable parameters (13.1M vs 117.7M). The vanilla VIB with a 10-component mixture marginal trained by gradient descent performs worst among IB-based methods (0.715 AUROC on SVHN), highlighting the importance of DAB's alternating minimization algorithm over plain gradient descent.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAB extends the Variational Information Bottleneck (Alemi et al., 2017) by replacing its single variational marginal q(z;φ) with a codebook of k distributional centroids, learned via an alternating minimization algorithm inspired by the Blahut-Arimoto procedure from rate-distortion theory. This departs from prior IB methods (Alemi et al., 2018) that use a mixture marginal trained purely by gradient descent, which the paper shows conflates features across classes. The approach is also conceptually related to DUQ (Van Amersfoort et al., 2020), which also uses centroids for distance-based uncertainty, but DAB computes distances in distribution space via KL divergence rather than Euclidean space via RBF kernels, and decouples the number of centroids from the number of classes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAB provides a theoretically grounded, single-forward-pass uncertainty method that outperforms expensive deep ensembles on calibration and OOD detection while adding minimal parameter overhead, and it can be applied post-hoc to pre-trained backbones. The rate-distortion framing offers a principled explanation for why distance-aware models work and unifies uncertainty estimation across classification and regression. Open questions include whether the accuracy gap versus ensembles can be closed by a distance-aware decoder, whether the framework extends to non-image modalities (text, NLP), and how alternative statistical distances (beyond KL) affect performance.
