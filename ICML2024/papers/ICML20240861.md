## Problem

Contextual bandits with low-rank reward matrices have received growing attention, yet it remained unclear how to optimally exploit the low-rank structure and how much performance gain it provides. Existing algorithms either assumed the learner observes multiple entries per round, achieved suboptimal minimax regret, or relied on Frobenius-norm subspace recovery guarantees that are too coarse to yield tight final bounds. The key missing ingredient was tight singular subspace recovery guarantees in the two-to-infinity norm, which had remained largely unexplored for bandit settings.

## Input / Output

In each round, the learner observes a context i ∈ [m] drawn i.i.d. from a known distribution ρ, selects an arm j ∈ [n], and receives a single noisy scalar reward M_{i,j} + ξ_t where M is an unknown rank-r matrix (r ≪ min(m,n)) and ξ_t is σ-subgaussian noise. The learner is assumed to know upper bounds on the condition number κ, incoherence μ, max-norm ‖M‖_max, and 1/(m·min_i ρ_i). Depending on the task, the algorithm outputs an arm selection (regret minimization), a policy value estimate v̂^π (policy evaluation), or an ε-optimal policy π̂ (best policy identification), with sample complexity or regret guarantees that scale as (m+n)/ε²·log(1/δ) or r^{5/4}(m+n)^{3/4}√T respectively.

## Method

The paper introduces a generic two-phase algorithm design. In Phase 1, spectral methods (truncated SVD of a noise-averaged matrix) estimate the left and right singular subspaces of M, and the authors prove tight two-to-infinity norm error bounds via a leave-one-out argument combined with a Poisson approximation technique, yielding a delocalization phenomenon where the error is smaller by a factor of √(m+n) than Frobenius-norm bounds. In Phase 2, the problem is reformulated as a misspecified contextual linear bandit of dimension d = r(m+n) − r², where the misspecification ε_max is controlled quadratically by the subspace recovery error. For regret minimization (RS-RMIN), the second phase runs a variant of SUPLIN-UCB; for policy evaluation (RS-PE), a regularized least-squares estimator is applied; for best policy identification (RS-BPI), the argmax is taken over the estimated linear features. This reduction yields minimax regret of Õ(r^{5/4}(m+n)^{3/4}√T), strictly improving over the unstructured Õ(√(mnT)) bound, and nearly minimax-optimal sample complexity for PE and BPI.

## Results

For regret minimization, RS-RMIN achieves a minimax regret of Õ(r^{5/4}(m+n)^{3/4}√T) over T rounds, which in the homogeneous case simplifies to Õ(L·r^{5/4}·n^{3/4}·√T) — strictly better than the Õ(√(mnT)) achievable by unstructured UCB. For policy evaluation and best policy identification, the proposed two-phase algorithms (RS-PE, RS-BPI) achieve sample complexity scaling as Õ((m+n)/ε² · log(1/δ)), which is nearly minimax optimal and improves over the Õ((m+n)/(ε²+r) · log(1/δ)) of prior work (Lee et al., 2023). The subspace recovery error in the two-to-infinity norm scales as Õ(1/√T) after Õ(m+n) observations, a factor of √(m+n) tighter than the corresponding Frobenius norm bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RS-RMIN strictly dominates unstructured UCB: in the homogeneous case (m = Θ(n)), the regret scales as Õ(n^{3/4}√T) versus Õ(n√T) for UCB, a polynomial improvement in the number of arms. Compared to Lee et al. (2023), who achieve Õ((m+n)^{1/(3r+2)} T^{(3r+1)/(3r+2)}) for general low-rank bandits, RS-RMIN's Õ(r^{5/4}(m+n)^{3/4}√T) is tighter for all T ≥ 1 when r is fixed. For BPI, this paper's Õ((m+n)/ε² · log(1/δ)) removes the additive +r in the denominator present in Lee et al.'s Õ((m+n)/(ε²+r) · log(1/δ)), yielding a strictly better dependence on ε. Pal & Jain (2022) achieve Õ(T^{3/4}) for general rank r but under the stronger assumption of observing m entries per round (versus one entry here), making a direct comparison inapplicable. The paper also notes that reducing to almost-low-dimensional linear bandits (as in Jun et al. 2019, Kang et al. 2022) yields suboptimal regret of Õ((m+n)√T) in the homogeneous case, whereas the misspecified linear bandit reduction used here achieves the tighter Õ(n^{3/4}√T).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-phase spectral approach for low-rank bandits initiated by Jun et al. (2019) and Kang et al. (2022), but makes a critical conceptual departure: instead of reducing the problem to an almost-low-dimensional linear bandit using Frobenius norm subspace recovery, it leverages tight two-to-infinity norm guarantees (adapted from Stojanovic et al., 2024) to reduce to a misspecified linear bandit with controlled misspecification. The second phase then applies SUPLIN-UCB (Takemura et al., 2021) to solve this misspecified problem. The key insight is that the two-to-infinity norm exploits a delocalization phenomenon—subspace recovery error is spread across m+n directions—yielding a √(m+n) improvement over Frobenius norm bounds that was previously unexploited in bandit settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first minimax regret guarantee for contextual low-rank bandits that is always strictly tighter than unstructured bandits (Õ(r^{5/4}(m+n)^{3/4}√T) vs. Õ(√(mnT))), and achieves nearly minimax-optimal sample complexity for policy evaluation and best policy identification. The two-phase design principle (spectral subspace recovery → misspecified linear bandit) is a general and reusable framework. A key open question is whether Õ(√(poly(r)(m+n)T)) regret is achievable for general low-rank matrices; the current gap to this target is a factor of r^{5/4}(m+n)^{1/4}. A limitation is the dependence on L = ‖M‖_max ∨ σ (rather than σ alone) in the subspace recovery error, stemming from the bias of the spectral truncation method, which the authors note could potentially be improved via nuclear norm regularization.
