## Problem

Existing convergence guarantees for variational regularisation in inverse problems are stated in terms of global minimisers, yet practical optimisation of non-convex objectives converges only to critical points. Convex learned regularisers (e.g., ACR) provide such guarantees but sacrifice the interpretability of the regulariser as a distance function to the data manifold, degrading reconstruction quality. No convergence results existed for primal-dual optimisation methods in the general weakly convex setting, leaving a gap between the non-convex regularisers that perform best in practice and the convex ones for which theory is available.

## Input / Output

The method operates in a weakly supervised setting: it is trained on unpaired ground-truth images and unpaired noisy measurements (no ground-truth-to-measurement pairs required), and at test time it takes a single noisy CT measurement (sparse-view or limited-angle projection) and produces a reconstructed CT image by solving a Tikhonov variational problem. The regulariser is a scalar-valued neural network evaluated on the image, and the reconstruction is obtained by minimising the sum of the learned regulariser and a data-fidelity term over the image space.

## Method

The paper introduces the Adversarial Weakly Convex Regulariser (AWCR), parameterised as R_θ(x) = R_θ^IWCNN(x) + (μ₀/2)||x||², where the IWCNN composes an input-convex neural network (ICNN) with a smooth network to guarantee weak convexity while retaining non-convex expressivity. The regulariser is trained by minimising a WGAN-style adversarial loss on unpaired data, pushing it to be small on ground-truth images and large on artefact-laden reconstructions. The reconstruction is then solved via either a subgradient method or the primal-dual hybrid gradient method (PDHGM) with θ=1. The key theoretical contributions are: (i) a generalised definition of convergent regularisation in terms of critical points, proven achievable by weakly convex regularisers of the form R_wc + R_sc with γ < 2μ; (ii) convergence and an O(log k/k) ergodic rate for PDHGM under weak convexity and a Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz condition; and (iii) a universal approximation theorem for IWCNNs, ensuring the AWCR can approximate any continuous function including the distance to a non-convex data manifold.

## Results

On sparse-view CT, AWCR-PD achieves the highest PSNR (36.9993 dB) and SSIM (0.9108) among all weakly supervised methods, approaching the supervised U-Net (37.1075 dB, 0.9265 SSIM) and LPD (37.4868 dB, 0.9217 SSIM). On limited-angle CT, AWCR achieves the best PSNR among weakly supervised methods (26.7233 dB), outperforming ACR (26.4459 dB) and ACNCR (26.5420 dB), though it falls short of supervised LPD (28.9480 dB) and U-Net (29.1103 dB). AWCR-PD underperforms AWCR in the limited-angle setting (24.3254 dB vs 26.7233 dB PSNR) due to the severely ill-posed forward and adjoint operators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the supervised baselines, AWCR-PD on sparse-view CT (36.9993 dB PSNR) comes within 0.11 dB of U-Net (37.1075 dB) and 0.49 dB of LPD (37.4868 dB), despite requiring only unpaired data. Against the unconstrained adversarial regulariser (AR), both AWCR variants outperform AR in PSNR on sparse-view CT (36.53 and 37.00 dB vs 36.41 dB) and limited-angle CT (26.72 and 24.33 dB vs 23.65 dB), with AWCR also improving SSIM substantially (0.811 vs 0.626 in limited view). Against the convex ACR, AWCR wins on PSNR in both settings (26.72 vs 26.45 dB limited; 36.53 vs 34.58 dB sparse) while AWCR-PD dominates on sparse-view SSIM (0.911 vs 0.877). Against ACNCR, AWCR achieves higher PSNR in limited-angle CT (26.72 vs 26.54 dB) and sparse-view CT (36.53 vs 35.65 dB), and AWCR-PD leads on sparse-view PSNR (37.00 vs 35.65 dB) and SSIM (0.911 vs 0.909). The main trade-off is that AWCR-PD degrades in the severely ill-posed limited-angle setting (24.33 dB), where the primal-dual solver struggles with the ill-conditioned forward/adjoint operators.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the IWCNN architecture introduced by Shumaylov et al. (2023) by proving a universal approximation theorem and incorporating it into the adversarial regularisation framework of Lunz et al. (2018), replacing the ICNN-based ACR of Mukherjee et al. (2021) with a directly weakly convex parameterisation (AWCR). On the optimisation side, it fills a gap left by prior work on PDHGM (Chambolle & Pock, 2011; Sun et al., 2018; Guo et al., 2023) by proving convergence and an ergodic rate for the method in the general weakly convex setting with θ=1. The paper also departs from the standard convergence-of-global-minimisers framework in inverse problems by formulating convergent regularisation in terms of critical points, which is more aligned with what optimisation algorithms actually achieve for non-convex objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The critical-point formulation is a more realistic and practically relevant notion of convergence for non-convex regularisation, and the weak convexity condition provides a principled middle ground between the restrictive convexity of ACRs (which lose distance-function interpretability) and unconstrained non-convexity (which lacks guarantees). Open questions include whether the boundedness assumption in the PDHGM analysis can be dropped, whether prox-linear schemes can be analysed in the primal-dual setting, how to extract proximal operators directly from learned networks, and how the effectiveness of different learned regularisation approaches depends on the degree of ill-posedness. The empirical gains over ACR/ACNCR are modest, so the primary value is theoretical.
