## Problem

Bayesian Optimization of composite functions f = g∘h can exploit intermediate outputs h(x) for sample efficiency, but existing composite BO methods become intractable when h maps to high-dimensional spaces (images, text, PDE fields). Conversely, standard high-dimensional BO methods ignore the composite structure entirely, discarding the rich intermediate information. This paper addresses the unexplored intersection: optimizing composite objectives over high-dimensional input spaces with high-dimensional intermediate outputs, a setting common in generative AI, molecular design, and robotics.

## Input / Output

JoCo takes as input a high-dimensional vector x (e.g., 3072-dim word-embedding prompts, 32-dim PDE parameters, or 20 B-spline trajectory points), and upon evaluation observes both the intermediate output y = h(x) (ranging from 16-dim pollutant concentrations to 451,584-dim RGB images at 224×224×3) and the scalar objective f(x) = g(y). The method produces an optimal input configuration x* maximizing f, along with posterior distributions over the compressed latent spaces. A key structural detail is that the intermediate output is observed for free during each evaluation (grey-box setting), and the input space may be structured (e.g., word-embedding space) rather than a simple hypercube.

## Method

JoCo jointly trains four components: two neural-network encoders (E_X compressing inputs to a low-dimensional latent space, E_Y compressing intermediate outputs) and two Gaussian Process models (ĥ mapping latent inputs to a distribution over latent outputs, ĝ mapping latent outputs to a distribution over the scalar reward). The critical design choice is that E_Y is supervised by the final objective f through the ĝ loss term, so the intermediate-output embedding retains only information relevant to predicting the reward rather than reconstructing the raw output. The joint loss is the sum of negative marginal log-likelihoods of both GPs, and all four components are updated simultaneously via backpropagation after each evaluation using the most recent N_b data points. Candidate selection uses Thompson sampling with a two-stage procedure (sample from ĥ posterior, then from ĝ posterior) within a TuRBO trust region, enabling efficient exploration in the original high-dimensional input space.

## Results

JoCo outperforms all baselines across all nine high-dimensional composite BO tasks, with particularly strong anytime performance in the early stages of optimization. On held-out modeling accuracy (RMSE), JoCo achieves the best predictive performance across all tasks, with the largest gains on image generation tasks: 3.468 RMSE on Aircraft (vs. 6.809 for GP+DKL), 1.844 on Dog (vs. 5.741), and 5.854 on Sportscar (vs. 8.334). On the environmental modeling task, JoCo reduces RMSE from 14.419 (standard GP) to 4.191, a 71% improvement. The intermediate output dimensions range from 16 (env model) to 451,584 (image generation), with input dimensions up to 3,072.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JoCo is compared against four baselines: Vanilla BO with Deep Kernel Learning (DKL), TuRBO (trust-region BO), CMA-ES, and random sampling. JoCo wins on all nine tasks against all four baselines, with the most dramatic margins on the generative AI tasks (image and text generation) where intermediate outputs have dimensions in the hundreds of thousands. TuRBO is the strongest baseline and occasionally catches up to JoCo on simpler tasks (Rosenbrock, Env Model, PDE) given sufficient evaluations, but JoCo maintains a clear anytime advantage, reaching high reward with far fewer function evaluations. CMA-ES and random sampling are consistently and substantially outperformed across all tasks. In a separate comparison (Appendix A.4), JoCo also outperforms Compositional BO (CBO) by Astudillo & Frazier (2019) even on moderate-output-dimension problems (18–1000), while being significantly faster and requiring less memory; CBO becomes intractable at the output dimensions considered in the main experiments.

## Relation to Prior Work

JoCo sits at the intersection of two established BO research threads: composite/grey-box BO (Astudillo & Frazier, 2019; Maddox et al., 2021), which exploits the f = g∘h structure but breaks down when intermediate outputs are high-dimensional, and latent-space high-dimensional BO (TuRBO, DKL, autoencoder-based methods), which handles high-dimensional inputs but ignores composite structure. The key departure is the joint, continuously-updated training of neural network encoders for both input and intermediate output spaces alongside GP surrogate models, using the final objective as supervisory signal—eliminating the need for large pre-training corpora that prior latent-space BO methods require. The trust-region search (TuRBO) and Thompson sampling are inherited directly from the high-dimensional BO literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

JoCo is the first composite BO method that scales to intermediate outputs with hundreds of thousands of dimensions (e.g., 224×224×3 images), opening the door to sample-efficient optimization of generative AI models, molecular design, and fluid dynamics problems that were previously computationally infeasible for grey-box BO. The joint training design means no separate pre-training phase is needed, making the method practical in settings where only a small number of expensive evaluations are available. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-objective or constrained composite settings, whether theoretical guarantees on sample efficiency can be established, and how performance scales as intermediate output dimensions grow beyond the ~450K tested here.
