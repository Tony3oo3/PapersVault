## Problem

Steerable CNNs require the equivariance group to be fixed in advance, but real data often exhibits varying or unknown symmetries across scales, making over-constrained models lose performance while under-constrained ones miss useful inductive bias. Existing relaxation methods (RPPs, RSteer, Partial GCNNs) either are incompatible with the SCNN framework, lack an interpretable parameterisation of the equivariance degree, or cannot handle arbitrary subgroups of continuous groups without an infeasible number of additional layers.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D grayscale images (DDMNIST double-digit pairs at standard MNIST resolution) and 3D volumetric CT data (MedMNIST subsets: Nodule, Synapse, Organ) for classification, as well as 2D simulation fields (Smoke, JetFlow) for regression. Beyond the task output (class label or scalar prediction), each layer additionally produces a learnt likelihood distribution λ(h) over the compact transformation group H, recoverable via inverse Fourier transform of the stored coefficients, which directly encodes the layer's degree of equivariance without requiring forward passes.

## Method

The key insight is that the SCNN kernel projection is an implicit uniform averaging over the group H; the authors replace this uniform measure with a learnable likelihood distribution λ(h) parameterised by its Fourier coefficients over the irreps of H. These coefficients are initialised to yield a uniform distribution (full equivariance) and updated via backpropagation, with the Clebsch-Gordan decomposition of the tensor product of input/output irreps determining which Fourier modes contribute to each kernel. To ensure interpretability, they apply softmax normalisation to the likelihood, an alignment loss anchoring the maximum at the identity element, and KL-divergence regularisation between consecutive layers to enforce monotonically decreasing equivariance. An optional bandlimit on the maximum irrep frequency controls parameter count and acts as a regulariser, and the entire construction is implemented on top of the escnn library by modifying the steerable basis generation algorithm.

## Results

On DDMNIST with O(2) symmetries, the proposed O(2) PSCNN achieves 81.9% test accuracy, outperforming the fully equivariant SCNN (39.9%), RPP (77.2%), and restricted SCNN (79.9%). On the Smoke simulation dataset, the method achieves 0.57 RMSE (domain) and 0.77 RMSE (future), outperforming RSteer (0.67/0.80) and all other baselines. On MedMNIST Synapse, PSCNNs reach 77.0% accuracy (SO(3)), surpassing regular SCNNs (73.8%) and CNNs (71.6%), while on the non-symmetric Organ dataset RPPs (94.0%) outperform PSCNNs (90.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed PSCNN consistently outperforms RPP (Finzi et al., 2021) across all DDMNIST symmetries and network groups, with the largest gap on O(2) symmetries (e.g., 82.3% vs. 77.9% for SO(2) network group). On MedMNIST, PSCNNs beat RPPs on Nodule (87.1% vs. 80.1%) and Synapse (77.0% vs. 69.5%), but lose on the highly non-symmetric Organ dataset (90.5% vs. 94.0%), where RPPs' non-equivariant initialisation provides an advantage with limited data. Compared to RSteer (Wang et al., 2022b) on Smoke, the method achieves lower RMSE (0.57 vs. 0.67 domain; 0.77 vs. 0.80 future) with fewer parameters (3.12M vs. 5.60M), while on JetFlow the two are roughly on par (0.15 vs. 0.17 future). Against the restricted SCNN baseline (Weiler & Cesa, 2019), the method is competitive but does not uniformly dominate: it wins on most DDMNIST settings (e.g., 82.3% vs. 79.6% on O(2) with SO(2) group) but the restricted SCNN shows smaller performance regression when generalising from discrete D₄ to continuous O(2) symmetries (Δ = −0.216 vs. −0.242), suggesting that fixed equivariance can be more robust in out-of-distribution settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the steerable CNN (SCNN) framework of Cesa et al. (2022) and Cohen & Welling (2016b) by replacing the uniform group-averaging in kernel projection with a learnable likelihood distribution parameterised via Fourier coefficients. It departs from prior partial-equivariance methods (Partial GCNNs, RPPs, RSteer) by working natively within the SCNN framework rather than GCNNs, providing an interpretable likelihood distribution over the group without requiring additional residual layers or explicit subgroup enumeration. The key conceptual departure is that the degree of equivariance is directly readable from the learnt distribution via inverse Fourier transform, whereas RPPs and RSteer encode equivariance relaxation implicitly through weight modulations that require expensive analysis to interpret.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first SCNN-compatible method that yields a directly interpretable, layer-wise degree of equivariance, which is valuable for practitioners who need to understand what symmetries a network has actually learned rather than merely achieving competitive accuracy. Key limitations include restriction to compact groups (no partial translation equivariance), significantly increased parameter counts over regular SCNNs, and the need for alignment and KL-divergence hyperparameters. Open questions include whether a theoretical guarantee exists that the learnt likelihood corresponds to the true data symmetry, whether non-uniform initialisation as a prior could improve data-efficiency on non-symmetric tasks, and whether the computational overhead of recomputing the steerable basis each forward pass can be eliminated.
