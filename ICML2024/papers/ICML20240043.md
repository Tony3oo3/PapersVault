## Problem

Prior coreset constructions for regularized classification (logistic regression, SVM, sigmoid) all produce coreset sizes with polynomial dependence on the ambient dimension d, and without regularization, coresets can be as large as the full dataset. This paper provides the first dimension-free coreset bounds for classification, showing that coreset size can depend only on the regularization parameter k and the target accuracy ε, not on d.

## Input / Output

The method takes data points from R^d (or a general probability space) drawn from a distribution P, a non-increasing loss function φ (logistic, sigmoid, SVM, or ReLU), and a regularization parameter k defining the regularized loss ℓ_{k,φ}(x,w) = φ(⟨x,w⟩) + ‖w‖²/k. It outputs a weighted coreset Y ⊆ X with weights u_i = S/(m·s(x_i)) that approximates the full loss for all model parameters w. A notable feature is that for bounded data (‖x‖₂ ≤ A), the upper sensitivity function becomes constant, so s-sensitivity sampling reduces to uniform iid sampling from P, eliminating the need to compute per-point sensitivity scores and yielding direct sample complexity bounds for the continuous setting.

## Method

The key technical tool is a Rademacher complexity version of the sensitivity sampling concentration bound (Theorem 1.1), which replaces the VC-dimension term in prior results with the Rademacher complexity of the s-augmented function family T_F. This is combined with a generalized Talagrand contraction lemma (Corollary 2.3) to bound the Rademacher complexity of the augmented loss family in terms of the data norm expectation E₁ and loss-specific constants, yielding a dimension-free bound of O(√(S/m)). The sensitivity function is constructed via a ratio bound on the regularized loss (Lemma 2.6), which for logistic loss gives S = O(A²k/√log(A²k)) under bounded data. The paper also provides a simpler Fubini-theorem-based proof of the VC-dimension-based coreset theorem (Theorem 1.2) that removes the finite-space assumption, and extends all results to the RKHS framework, recovering dimension-free KDE coreset bounds as a corollary.

## Results

The paper establishes the first dimension-free coreset bounds for regularized classification: for sigmoid, SVM, and ReLU losses with regularization parameter k, a uniform sample of size O(k³/ε²) suffices as an ε-coreset, with no dependence on dimension d or input size n. For logistic loss, the bound improves to O(k³/(ε²√(log k))), yielding a √(log k) factor improvement over prior sensitivity-sampling results. These bounds hold for iid samples from an unknown distribution P, providing true sample complexity guarantees in the continuous setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The central advantage over all four prior baselines is the elimination of dimension d from the coreset size. Munteanu et al. (2018) and Mai et al. (2021) both have polynomial dependence on d (d³ and d respectively) and additionally depend on a data complexity parameter μ(X) that is hard to compute. Tolochinksy et al. (2022) achieves O*(kd²·log n·log k/ε²) via sensitivity sampling, which is quadratic in d and also depends on n; this paper's O(k³/ε²) bound is dimension-free and input-size-free. Curtin et al. (2020) is the closest prior result with O(dk·log k/ε²) using uniform sampling, but still carries a linear d factor; this paper removes d entirely and for logistic loss additionally gains a √(log k) improvement. The trade-off is that the k³ dependence is larger than the k·log k of Curtin et al. when k is small, but for the regime k = Θ(n^{1−κ}) the dimension-free nature becomes the dominant advantage in high-dimensional settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sensitivity sampling framework for coresets (Feldman & Langberg, 2011; Braverman et al., 2021; Feldman et al., 2020) by replacing the VC-dimension-based analysis with a Rademacher complexity bound (Theorem 1.1), which is the key technical departure. Prior coreset results for regularized classification (Tolochinksy et al., 2022; Curtin et al., 2020) relied on VC-dimension of linked-range spaces, inherently coupling coreset size to the ambient dimension d. By bounding Rademacher complexity of the augmented function family instead, the authors obtain the first dimension-free coreset sizes (O(k³/ε²)) for logistic, sigmoid, SVM, and ReLU losses, and further extend the framework from finite sets to continuous distributions, yielding sample complexity bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dimension-free bound is a clean theoretical advance: it shows that for regularized linear classification, the intrinsic complexity of the problem is governed by the regularization parameter k rather than the data dimension, which is reassuring for high-dimensional settings. The Rademacher complexity sensitivity sampling theorem is stated to be of independent interest and could apply to other coreset or sampling problems where VC-dimension is too coarse. Limitations include the restriction to linear models with specific convex loss functions, the purely theoretical nature (no experiments), and the fact that the O(k³) dependence on the regularization parameter may be loose in practice. Open questions include whether the Rademacher complexity approach can yield dimension-free bounds for non-linear or kernelized classification beyond the RKHS extension sketched in the appendix.
