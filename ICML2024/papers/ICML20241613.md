## Problem

Multi-objective Bayesian optimization (MOBO) acquisition functions that integrate over the objective space, such as expected hypervolume improvement (EHVI), scale super-polynomially with the number of objectives M and are sensitive to non-informative monotonic rescalings of the objectives (e.g., log vs. linear units). This limits their practical utility in many-objective settings and in domains where objectives carry arbitrary unit conventions, such as biochemistry where K_D and pK_D are interchangeable representations of the same quantity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-objective black-box function f: R^d → R^M, a design space X ⊂ R^d, and a growing dataset of noisy observations D_t. A multi-task GP surrogate with a Matérn 5/2 ARD kernel is fit on the observations, and its predictive posterior (L Monte-Carlo samples per candidate) is used to estimate a multivariate CDF via vine copulas. The output at each iteration is the next candidate design point x* that maximizes the CDF-based acquisition score, optimized with the gradient-free CMA-ES algorithm; over T iterations this yields an approximate Pareto front. Two variants are provided: v1 fits the CDF on all pooled posterior samples, while v2 fits it on posterior means to avoid poor CDF fits under large uncertainty.

## Method

BOtied exploits the observation that non-dominated solutions correspond to the highest multivariate rank, which coincides with the extreme level line of the joint CDF F_Y. The acquisition function is u(x, f̂, D_t) = 1 − F̂(f̂(x); D_t), where F̂ is a vine-copula-based estimate of the joint CDF over the surrogate's predictive posterior. Vine copulas factorize the M-dimensional joint density into M(M−1)/2 bivariate (conditional) copula densities organized in a tree structure, making estimation tractable even for large M. The probability integral transform applied before copula fitting guarantees invariance to arbitrary monotonic transformations and relative rescalings of the objectives, a property proven via Sklar's theorem. The paper also introduces the CDF indicator I_CDF(A) = min_{y∈A} F_Y(y), a Pareto-compliant quality metric that complements hypervolume by providing a bounded [0,1] scale and interpretable scores for the degree of objective competition.

## Results

On the three main synthetic benchmarks (Penicillin M=3, DTLZ2 M=4, DTLZ2 M=6), BOtied v2 achieves the best hypervolume (HV) across all settings: 342,762 on Penicillin, 2.32 on DTLZ2 M=4, and 0.42 on DTLZ2 M=6, outperforming NEHVI (314,294 / 1.80 / 0.27) by 9%, 29%, and 56% respectively. On the proposed CDF indicator (I_CDF, lower is better), BOtied v2 achieves 0.20, 0.11, and 0.064, competitive with or best among all methods. Wall-clock time per acquisition call shows BOtied is significantly faster than NEHVI and JES for M≥3, matching the speed of NParEGO which scalarizes objectives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BOtied v2 achieves the best HV on all three primary synthetic benchmarks, with the largest margin over NEHVI appearing at M=6 (0.42 vs. 0.27, a 56% improvement), consistent with the paper's claim that HV-based methods degrade super-polynomially with the number of objectives. NParEGO is the closest competitor, matching BOtied v2 on I_CDF for DTLZ2 M=4 (0.10 vs. 0.11) and DTLZ2 M=6 (0.065 vs. 0.064), but trailing on HV in all settings. MES achieves the best I_CDF on DTLZ2 M=6 (0.059) but lags substantially on HV (0.27 vs. 0.42), suggesting it finds a few very good points but poor coverage. PES and JES are consistently outperformed by both BOtied variants on both metrics across all three datasets. In terms of computational cost, BOtied matches NParEGO's wall-clock time while being significantly faster than NEHVI and JES for M≥3, making it the most scalable non-scalarization method tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Binois et al. (2015), who first identified the conceptual link between the Pareto front and the extreme level line of the joint CDF, and on the vine copula framework of Nagler & Czado (2016) for scalable high-dimensional density estimation. It departs from the dominant hypervolume-based acquisition functions (EHVI/NEHVI) and random scalarization (NParEGO) by replacing the volume-of-polytope computation with a multivariate rank score derived from the CDF, which is both scale-invariant and invariant to monotonic transformations of objectives. Prior multivariate ranking work (Kukkonen & Lampinen, 2007) aggregated individual objective ranks post-hoc, whereas BOtied is the first to exploit the full joint CDF structure via copulas within a MOBO acquisition function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CDF indicator and BOtied acquisition function offer a practical alternative to hypervolume-based methods that become super-polynomially expensive for M > 4 objectives, with wall-clock times competitive with the much simpler NParEGO. The invariance to monotonic transformations addresses a real pain point in applications like biochemistry where objectives are naturally expressed in different units (e.g., K_D vs. pK_D). Key limitations include the non-differentiability of the current implementation (requiring gradient-free CMA-ES optimization) and the increasing modeling complexity of vine copulas as M grows. Open directions include differentiable copula-based acquisition via differentiable sorting, constrained/discrete extensions, and incorporating input noise through the copula–multivariate value-at-risk connection.
