## Problem

Existing LLM compression methods apply either sparsification or quantization in isolation, causing severe performance degradation (or outright collapse) at high compression ratios. The paper asks whether jointly combining both techniques can maintain LLM performance under extreme compression, and identifies two key obstacles: the inherent contradiction between sparsification (which preserves large-magnitude weights) and quantization (which prefers small value ranges), and the harmful effect of activation outliers on both processes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240673/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM (e.g., LLaMA-7B/13B/33B, LLaMA2-7B/13B, ChatGLM3-6B) and 128 calibration samples from C4 as input. It produces a layer-by-layer compressed model that is simultaneously sparse (unstructured or structured 2:4/4:8) and quantized to a target bit-width, achieving up to 7.96× computation reduction. The editing strength per layer is drawn from a small predefined set R = {0, 4e-5, 5e-5, 6e-5, 7e-5}, and the sparsity ratio and quantization bit-width are jointly tuned to hit a target #MACs.

## Method

JSQ introduces two key components. First, the Salience with Activation Range (SAR) metric extends Wanda's importance criterion (‖X_j‖₂·|W_ij|) with an auxiliary salience term A_ij that measures the output activation range after zeroing out weight W_ij, weighted by a trade-off factor λ (empirically set to 2); this penalizes preserving weights whose removal would widen the activation range, making the sparse model friendlier to subsequent quantization. Second, the Search-based Activation Editor (SAE) clamps per-layer activations to [r·max+(1−r)·min, (1−r)·max+r·min] to suppress relatively useless outliers before compression, and uses simulated annealing to search over per-layer editing strengths (encoded as a vector V) to find the configuration that maximizes post-compression perplexity. The full pipeline iterates: forward-pass to collect activations → edit activations per V → sparsify and quantize each layer via SAR → evaluate → update V via simulated annealing, repeating until convergence.

## Results

On LLaMA-7B compressed to 3.24T MACs (7.96× reduction), JSQ achieves a WikiText-2 perplexity of 11.04 and an average zero-shot accuracy of 62.56% across seven classification benchmarks, outperforming the best quantization-only baseline OmniQuant (12.49 / 61.38%) and the best combined baseline SparseGPT+SmoothQuant (11.98 / 61.51%). Sparsity-only methods (Wanda, LLM-Pruner, SparseGPT) completely collapse at this compression ratio, with WikiText perplexities exceeding 5,000. The advantage generalizes across model families: on LLaMA-33B (4.77T MACs), JSQ reaches 7.68 perplexity vs. OmniQuant's 9.41, and on ChatGLM3-6B (2.20T MACs), JSQ achieves 69.55% average accuracy vs. 68.72% for Wanda+SmoothQuant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JSQ is compared against three sparsity-only methods (Wanda, LLM-Pruner, SparseGPT), two quantization-only methods (SmoothQuant, OmniQuant), and two naive pipeline combinations (Wanda+SmoothQuant, SparseGPT+SmoothQuant). At the headline 7.96× compression on LLaMA-7B, all sparsity-only baselines collapse entirely (perplexity > 5,000), confirming that pruning alone is insufficient at extreme ratios. Among viable baselines, JSQ beats OmniQuant by 1.45 perplexity points (11.04 vs. 12.49) and by 1.18% average accuracy (62.56% vs. 61.38%). Against the strongest combined baseline SparseGPT+SmoothQuant, JSQ improves perplexity by 0.94 (11.04 vs. 11.98) and average accuracy by 1.05%. The gap widens at higher compression: at 1.84T MACs (Appendix Table 9), OmniQuant and SmoothQuant crash (perplexity > 100,000), while JSQ maintains 21.53 perplexity and 54.46% average accuracy, a 10+ point margin over the best surviving baseline (SparseGPT+SmoothQuant at 44.33%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Wanda's sparsity criterion (importance = activation norm × weight magnitude) by adding an auxiliary salience term that penalizes weights whose removal would widen the activation range, thereby bridging the inherent tension between sparsification (which preserves large-magnitude weights) and quantization (which prefers narrow value ranges). It departs from prior joint compression frameworks (OBQ, APQ, differentiable joint pruning) that were designed for smaller networks and do not address the outlier problem specific to LLMs. The search-based activation editor represents a conceptual departure from the migration (Outlier Suppression+) and clipping (OmniQuant) strategies that are tightly coupled to the quantization step, instead proposing a simpler pre-compression clamping of activations optimized via simulated annealing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that JSQ achieves ~7.96× computation reduction on LLaMA-7B without the catastrophic performance collapse that afflicts single-technique baselines (Wanda, SparseGPT, SmoothQuant, OmniQuant) at comparable compression ratios, making extreme LLM compression viable for deployment. A key open question is whether the predefined editing-strength set R can be learned or auto-designed, as the authors acknowledge this requires prior knowledge. The approach also raises the question of whether the activation-editing insight generalizes to other compression dimensions (e.g., low-rank factorization) or to non-Transformer architectures, and whether the simulated annealing search cost is acceptable for very large models.
