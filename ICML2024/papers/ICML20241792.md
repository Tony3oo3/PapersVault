## Problem

While one-parameter persistent homology has a canonical descriptor (the barcode) with well-understood differentiability and gradient descent convergence, multiparameter persistent homology admits no unique barcode-like invariant due to fundamental algebraic obstructions. Multiple incomplete descriptors have been proposed (signed barcodes, multiparameter landscapes, persistence images, kernels), but no general framework exists to establish differentiability and convergence guarantees across this diverse family, risking the parallel development of competing specialized optimization theories.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on n-filtrations: monotonic maps from a fixed simplicial complex K to R^n, constructed from point clouds via Vietoris-Rips or function-Rips bifiltrations, or from graphs with learned node/edge attributes. The topological descriptor (e.g., a Hilbert decomposition signed measure or evaluated multiparameter persistence landscape) is computed on a finite grid induced by the filtration's value ordering, then pushed forward along the aligned grid inclusion into R^n. In practice, the signed measure is represented in a finite-dimensional vector space via lexicographic sorting of its point masses, and the loss is an optimal transport distance or integral against a test function, yielding a scalar objective for gradient-based optimization.

## Method

The core technical contribution is the 'semilinearly determined on grids' condition: a descriptor α: Fil_n(K) → R^D is semilinearly determined on grids if, for every grid G and componentwise surjective G-filtration h, the push-forward map push_h^α: incl(G) → R^D (which computes the invariant on the discrete grid filtration and pushes it along any aligned grid inclusion) is semilinear. The space Fil_n(K) is partitioned into finitely many cells (affine regions where the grid and ordering are fixed), and on each cell the descriptor factors through a linear diffeomorphism to an open, convex, semilinear subset of a Euclidean space. This yields an explicit Clarke subdifferential and, when composed with o-minimal definable parametrization Φ and loss E, guarantees that stochastic subgradient descent converges to critical points almost surely. The paper verifies the condition for two structurally different descriptors—the sorted Hilbert decomposition signed measure (affine on each cell) and the evaluated multiparameter persistence landscape (a maximum of finitely many semilinear functions)—and demonstrates empirical gains over one-parameter baselines in point cloud optimization, topological autoencoders, and graph classification.

## Results

On four graph classification benchmarks (ENZYMES, IMDB-B, IMDB-M, MUTAG), multiparameter topological descriptors (MPTOP) consistently outperform one-parameter descriptors (OPTOP) across four GNN architectures. For example, GraphDenseNet-MPTOP achieves 92.6% on MUTAG versus 86.2% for GraphDenseNet-OPTOP, and GraphResNet-MPTOP reaches 50.1% on IMDB-M versus 40.7% for GraphResNet-OPTOP. In the point cloud optimization experiment, the two-parameter function-Rips bifiltration loss prevents point divergence and preserves density structure, whereas the one-parameter VR loss causes points to scatter to infinity. In dimension reduction, multiparameter topological regularization robustly preserves the topology of two interlaced circles under background noise, while one-parameter regularization fails.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the one-parameter topological descriptor framework of Horn et al. (2022, ICLR), which uses k independent one-parameter node attributes with k different filtering functions. Across 16 architecture-dataset combinations, MPTOP (a single k-parameter descriptor) wins or ties in 14 of 16 cases, with the largest margins on GraphResNet-IMDB-M (+9.4 pp) and GraphDenseNet-MUTAG (+6.4 pp); the two losses are GIN-IMDB-B (−3.7 pp) and GIN-MUTAG (−1.1 pp). The second baseline is the one-parameter VR optimization of Carrière et al. (2021, ICML), used in both the point cloud and dimension reduction experiments. In point cloud optimization, the one-parameter VR loss causes points to diverge to infinity and destroys density structure, while the two-parameter function-Rips bifiltration loss (the paper's method) prevents divergence and preserves the initial density distribution. In dimension reduction with noisy data, the one-parameter topological regularization (Eq. 3) is highly susceptible to noise and fails to close the circles, whereas the multiparameter regularization robustly preserves topology in both low-noise and high-noise settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-parameter persistent homology optimization framework of Carrière et al. (ICML 2021) to the multiparameter setting, where no canonical barcode exists due to fundamental algebraic obstructions. Rather than developing separate differentiability theories for each of the many proposed multiparameter descriptors (signed barcodes, persistence landscapes, persistence images, kernels), it introduces a unifying condition—'semilinearly determined on grids'—that subsumes all currently known descriptors under a single convergence guarantee. The convergence proof leverages the tame-function framework of Davis et al. (2020), and the approach is positioned as a deliberate alternative to the concurrent, descriptor-specific treatment of GRIL by Mukherjee et al. (2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework prevents the fragmentation of multiparameter PH optimization into competing, descriptor-specific theories, giving the field a common language for proving differentiability and convergence. Empirically, the paper demonstrates that even the simplest multiparameter descriptors (Hilbert decomposition signed measures) consistently outperform their one-parameter counterparts across point cloud optimization, dimension reduction, and graph classification, suggesting that the additional parameter carries genuine topological signal. Key limitations include the computational cost of multiparameter PH (mitigated only by subsampling) and the open question of whether future descriptors will satisfy the semilinear-on-grids condition; the authors note that a definable-on-grids generalization (Remark 3.18) may be needed.
