## Problem

Over-parameterized neural networks generalize well even when perfectly interpolating training data, but the mechanism behind this phenomenon remains theoretically unexplained. While empirical work (Chiang et al., 2023) showed that randomly sampling NN parameters from a uniform prior conditioned on zero training loss generalizes comparably to SGD-trained networks, no theoretical guarantee existed for why this 'flat' parameter prior should produce simple, generalizing functions. This paper provides the first such guarantee, showing that the parameterization's structural redundancy is the key mechanism.

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite labeled training set S of N points and a uniform prior over Q-quantized (or continuous) NN parameters, and produces a single interpolating predictor via the Guess & Check rejection procedure (draw parameters until the network classifies all training points correctly). The principal output is a generalization bound whose sample complexity scales with the teacher's parameter count times log Q, with the student's width entering only linearly (scaled-neuron case) or as a geometric mean (vanilla FC). For the continuous-weight variant, quantization is replaced by angular margin assumptions on the training set relative to the teacher's hyperplanes.

## Method

The core insight is that a uniform prior over NN parameters induces a highly non-uniform prior over NN functions: simpler functions (requiring fewer active neurons) have exponentially more parameter representations due to the redundancy of zero-weighted or zero-scaled neurons. The paper formalizes this through the Guess & Check algorithm and proves the sample complexity is O(−log p̃), where p̃ is the probability a random parameter draw yields a teacher-equivalent function. For quantized networks, they bound −log p̃ by counting student configurations that replicate the teacher (zeroing outgoing weights of extra neurons), yielding a bound proportional to teacher parameters × log Q plus a linear student-width term. The scaled-neuron architecture (per-neuron scaling parameters) sharpens this to essentially the teacher's complexity plus a linear penalty, since zero scales cleanly deactivate entire units. For continuous weights, they replace quantization with first- and second-layer angular margin assumptions and derive analogous bounds for two-layer LReLU networks.

## Results

The main theoretical result is that the sample complexity for generalization of a typical interpolating NN sampled from a uniform prior over parameters is O(−log p̃), where p̃ is the probability that a random NN is teacher-equivalent. For quantized scaled-neuron fully connected networks, the effective sample complexity is C̃^SFC = (Σ(d*_l · d*_{l−1} + 2d_l)) log Q, which is close to the teacher's own complexity C* with only a linear (not quadratic) dependence on student width. For vanilla FC networks, the bound is C̃^FC ≈ √(C·C*), still significantly better than the student's full complexity C. ## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are between different theoretical bounds. The paper's posterior-sampling bound (C̃^SFC ≈ C* + linear in student width) is strictly tighter than the standard log-cardinality PAC bound (which scales as C, the full student complexity) and the vanilla FC bound (C̃^FC ≈ √(C·C*)). Compared to an explicit sparsity prior (Occam rule minimizing ℓ₀ norm), the posterior-sampling bound avoids an extra log C* multiplicative factor, making it tighter when the student width is not excessively large. Compared to norm-based bounds (Neyshabur et al., 2015; Golowich et al., 2018), the paper's bound avoids exponential dependence on depth (Q^{O(L)}) but pays a linear price in student width. Compared to PAC-Bayes, the paper's Lemma 3.2 gives a high-probability bound for a single posterior sample rather than only an expectation over the posterior, yielding a tighter result without the 1/δ multiplicative factor that Markov's inequality would introduce.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first theoretical proof for the empirical observation by Chiang et al. (2023) that random neural networks sampled from a uniform prior over parameters, conditioned on interpolating the training data, generalize as well as SGD-trained networks. The analysis builds on the finite hypothesis class PAC bound from Shalev-Shwartz & Ben-David (2014) and draws conceptual parallels to PAC-Bayes and Minimum Description Length frameworks, but the key departure is showing that parameterization redundancy alone—without any explicit sparsity or norm regularization—induces an informative prior over functions. Unlike prior work on random interpolating networks (Theisen et al., 2021 for linear models; Valle-Perez et al., 2019 requiring infinite width), this paper handles finite-width deep non-linear networks under a narrow-teacher assumption, and unlike the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (sparse in weights), the relevant sparsity here is in neurons.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper gives a clean mechanistic explanation for a central puzzle in deep learning: why overparameterized networks generalize when trained to zero loss. The practical implication is that the sample complexity of posterior sampling depends on the teacher's parameter count (times log Q) with only linear dependence on student width (for scaled-neuron architectures), suggesting that the parameterization itself acts as an implicit Occam's razor. Key open questions include whether SGD's implicit bias aligns with this posterior distribution, whether the narrow-teacher assumption is realistic for complex tasks, and whether the quadratic sample complexity dependence on ε in the non-realizable case can be tightened. The results also raise the question of whether quantization-aware training methods could be leveraged to improve generalization bounds in practice.
