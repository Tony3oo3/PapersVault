## Problem

Sequential Bayesian experimental design for non-exchangeable dynamical systems is computationally intractable due to nested expectations over intractable posteriors, and existing amortized methods (e.g., iDAD) rely on contrastive estimators (sPCE) whose bias grows exponentially with the true EIG value, requiring billions of regularizing samples for high-information systems. This paper addresses the gap by formulating sequential BED as risk-sensitive policy optimization and developing a particle-based inference algorithm that estimates the EIG without contrastive bounds.

## Input / Output

The method takes a discretized SDE model (Euler-Maruyama), a parameter prior (Gaussian or log-normal), and the running outcome-design history z_{0:t} = {x_{0:t}, ξ_{0:t-1}} as input. It produces a GRU-parameterized stochastic policy π_φ(ξ_t | z_{0:t}) that maps the history to the next design, with outputs constrained to a bounded interval via a tanh scaling (e.g., torque ∈ [-1,1], force ∈ [-5,5]). At evaluation, the trained policy generates a full sequence of T=50 designs in real-time, and the EIG is estimated via a nested Monte Carlo procedure using N=16 trajectory samples and M=1024 parameter particles.

## Method

The key insight is that the expected information gain factorizes into a sum of stage rewards, making sequential BED equivalent to a risk-sensitive decision-making problem that is dual to inference in a non-Markovian state-space model. The proposed Inside-Out SMC² algorithm inverts the nesting of SMC²: an outer particle filter (N particles) propagates trajectories z_{0:T} with weights driven by the potential g_t = exp(η r_t), while an inner IBIS particle filter (M particles) maintains a running posterior over θ at each trajectory, enabling computation of the marginalized dynamics and stage rewards without contrastive sampling. Policy optimization is performed via Markovian Score Climbing with a Rao-Blackwellized CSMC kernel, yielding a guaranteed-convergent gradient ascent on the log marginal likelihood. The amortizing policy is a GRU network with a learnable variance, trained with a slew-rate penalty to encourage smooth design trajectories, and requires only O(MT²) time per iteration (parallelizable over N), in contrast to sPCE's exponential sample requirement.

## Results

IO-SMC² achieves the highest EIG estimates across three of four dynamical systems: 3.53 on the conditionally linear pendulum (vs. 2.58 for iDAD), 3.72 on the nonlinear pendulum (vs. 3.01), and 21.23 on the stochastic cart-pole (vs. 18.99). On the stochastic double-link, iDAD slightly edges out IO-SMC² (11.73 vs. 11.53). A critical structural advantage emerges on the cart-pole: the sPCE bound used by iDAD saturates at log(10⁶) ≈ 13.82 for all policies, while IO-SMC²'s particle-based EIG estimator reaches 21.23 with only 1024 inner particles, whereas sPCE would require ~1.3 billion contrastive samples to avoid saturation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is iDAD (Ivanova et al., 2021), which amortizes sequential BED via a GRU policy trained on the sPCE lower bound. IO-SMC² outperforms iDAD on the conditionally linear pendulum (3.53 vs. 2.58 EIG, +37%), the nonlinear pendulum (3.72 vs. 3.01, +24%), and the stochastic cart-pole (21.23 vs. 18.99, +12%). On the stochastic double-link, iDAD marginally wins (11.73 vs. 11.53 EIG), though both methods learn qualitatively similar policies with coordinated design switches. The most consequential comparison is sample efficiency: on the cart-pole, sPCE requires ~1.3 billion contrastive samples to estimate an EIG of 21 (implying ~660 GB of memory), while IO-SMC² achieves this with 1024 inner particles. Simple baselines (random, PRBS, myopic) are consistently outperformed by both amortized methods, confirming the value of learned sequential policies over greedy or unstructured designs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: (1) amortized sequential Bayesian experimental design (DAD, iDAD), which learns a policy to select designs in real time, and (2) the control-as-inference / risk-sensitive decision-making framework (Toussaint & Storkey, Rawlik), which casts optimal control as inference in a state-space model. The key departure from iDAD is the elimination of the sequential Prior Contrastive Estimation (sPCE) bound in favor of a direct nested particle filter (IBIS inside SMC) that estimates the EIG without contrastive samples. The algorithmic core, Inside-Out SMC², inverts the nesting of the SMC² algorithm (Chopin et al., 2013): where SMC² places a particle filter inside IBIS, here IBIS is embedded inside a particle filter, and the whole is wrapped in a Rao-Blackwellized CSMC kernel for Markovian score climbing–based policy optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is the sample-efficiency gap: for the cart-pole problem with EIG ≈ 21, the sPCE bound would require roughly 1.3 billion contrastive samples to avoid saturation at log(L+1), while IO-SMC² achieves a comparable estimate with 1024 inner particles. This makes sequential BED feasible for high-information-gain dynamical systems where iDAD is computationally intractable. Key limitations include the requirement for closed-form conditional transition densities (ruling out intractable models like Markov jump processes) and the unresolved choice of the tempering parameter η, which governs a bias-variance trade-off in the risk-sensitive objective. The control-as-inference framing also opens the door to applying other inference tools (e.g., variational methods, differentiable simulation) to experimental design problems.
