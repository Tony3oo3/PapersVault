## Problem

Pure transformers aligned with the k-WL hierarchy (Kim et al., 2022) require impractical architectural assumptions—203 attention heads for k=2 and O(n⁶) runtime—making them infeasible in practice. The paper asks whether a hierarchy of increasingly expressive pure transformers can be designed that is both theoretically grounded in the k-WL and practically deployable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241490/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes node-labeled graphs and Laplacian eigenvalues/eigenvectors as positional encodings. For order k, the transformer operates on n^k tokens (one per k-tuple of nodes), or fewer tokens under the (k,s) variant restricted to tuples inducing at most s connected components. The structural embeddings must satisfy two properties simultaneously: node-identifying (attention can detect shared nodes between tuples) and adjacency-identifying (attention can recover the row-normalized adjacency matrix), which the paper proves holds for both LPE and SPE encodings.

## Method

The core contribution is a tokenization scheme for standard transformers that provably simulates the k-WL (and the stronger δ-k-WL) by encoding atomic types and using structural embeddings whose attention patterns reconstruct the generalized adjacency matrix between k-tuples. For k=1, adjacency-identifying PEs let a single attention head recover the row-normalized adjacency matrix, which combined with degree embeddings reproduces the 1-WL GNN update. For k>1, node-identifying PEs enable counting node-to-node matches between tuples to determine j-neighborhood adjacency, requiring only 2k attention heads instead of bell(2k). The (k,s)-GT variant further reduces token count while Theorem 6 proves a strict expressivity hierarchy: (2,1)-GT > 1-WL, (k,1)-GT > (k-1,1)-GT. An order transfer strategy pre-trains on lower-order tokens (e.g., (2,1)-GT) and fine-tunes on higher-order tokens (e.g., (3,1)-GT), enabling practical use of higher-order expressivity on small downstream tasks.

## Results

On the large-scale PCQM4Mv2 pre-training task, the (2,1)-GT with LPE achieves a validation MAE of 0.0870, nearly matching Graphormer (0.0864) and substantially outperforming TokenGT (0.0910). On node classification, (2,1)-GT+LPE reaches 92.4% accuracy on CS and 93.3% on PHOTO, surpassing both Graphormer (79.1% / 89.4%) and TokenGT (90.3% / 94.9%). For fine-tuning on ALCHEMY (12K), the pre-trained (2,1)-GT+LPE achieves a MAE of 0.101, beating all GNN baselines including SPE (0.108) and SignNet (0.113). On the BREC expressivity benchmark, the (3,1)-GT+SPE distinguishes 228/400 graph pairs, outperforming GINE+SPE (217) and Graphormer (79), though still below the 3-WL upper bound (270).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Graphormer (Ying et al., 2021), the (2,1)-GT+LPE wins decisively on CS (92.4% vs 79.1%) and PHOTO (93.3% vs 89.4%), and nearly ties on PCQM4Mv2 (0.0870 vs 0.0864 MAE), closing the gap between pure transformers and graph transformers with modified attention. Against TokenGT (Kim et al., 2022), the (2,1)-GT+LPE improves PCQM4Mv2 MAE from 0.0910 to 0.0870 and CS accuracy from 90.3% to 92.4%, but TokenGT retains an edge on PHOTO (94.9% vs 93.3%). On ALCHEMY fine-tuning, the pre-trained (2,1)-GT+LPE (MAE 0.101) outperforms SignNet (0.113), BasisNet (0.110), and SPE (0.108) from Huang et al. (2024), with the gap widening from 0.124 (from scratch) to 0.101 after pre-training. On the five OGB molecular classification tasks, the pre-trained (2,1)-GT+LPE beats the carefully tuned GINE+LPE baseline on 4 of 5 datasets (e.g., CLINTOX 86.7% vs 82.4% AUROC), while the pre-trained (2,1)-GT+SPE beats GINE+SPE on all five. On BREC, both (2,1)-GT and (3,1)-GT with SPE surpass GINE+SPE and Graphormer across all categories, though neither reaches the 3-WL upper bound of 270/400.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work aligning transformers with the Weisfeiler-Leman hierarchy, departing from Kim et al. (2022) who aligned transformers with k-IGNs and required bell(2k) attention heads (203 for k=2) and O(n⁶) runtime. By aligning directly with the k-WL rather than k-IGNs, the authors reduce the head requirement to 2k and achieve strictly stronger provable expressivity at every order k, including a (2,1)-GT that surpasses 1-WL with the same O((n+m)²) complexity as TokenGT (which only matches 1-WL). The paper also introduces a theoretical framework proving that standard Laplacian-based PEs (LPE) and SPE are sufficient for the required node- and adjacency-identifying properties, and derives the (k,s)-GT directly from the (k,s)-WL of Morris et al. (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first hierarchy of pure transformers with strictly increasing provable expressivity at every order k while remaining feasible in practice, addressing a key gap between theoretical expressivity guarantees and practical deployability. The empirical results show the (2,1)-GT nearly matching Graphormer on PCQM4Mv2 (0.0870 vs 0.0864 MAE) and beating GNN baselines on small-scale molecular tasks after pre-training, validating the pre-training/fine-tuning paradigm for pure graph transformers. Open questions include whether the remaining gap to Graphormer closes with larger pre-training data, whether order transfer to higher orders will help as datasets grow, and whether the theoretical expressivity gains translate to consistent downstream improvements beyond the BREC benchmark.
