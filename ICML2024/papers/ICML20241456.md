## Problem

Retrieval-augmented adaptation of pre-trained vision-language models (e.g., CLIP) has shown strong empirical results in low-data regimes, yet the mechanisms by which retrieval actually improves adaptation remain poorly understood. Specifically, it is unclear whether uni-modal (image-to-image) or cross-modal (text-to-image) retrieval is more effective, and whether the logit ensemble between zero-shot and cache-based predictions is a critical component or merely a design choice.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a test image, a pre-built K-shot feature cache of retrieved image embeddings (one per class, retrieved from LAION-5B via either seed images or text queries), and class text descriptions encoded by the CLIP text encoder. The output is an ensemble logit vector computed as a weighted sum of the zero-shot CLIP logit (cosine similarity between the test image embedding and each class text embedding) and the cache-based logit (average scaled cosine similarity between the test image embedding and each class's K retrieved image embeddings), from which a class prediction is made by argmax.

## Method

The paper conducts a systematic empirical and theoretical study of retrieval-augmented adaptation using the Tip-Adapter framework with LAION-5B as the retrieval database. Empirically, it shows that I2I retrieval (querying with seed images from the target distribution) consistently outperforms T2I retrieval (querying with class text descriptions) across all datasets and shot counts, primarily because I2I avoids the semantic ambiguity inherent in text queries that retrieve diverse but off-distribution samples. It further demonstrates that logit ensemble—taking a weighted average of zero-shot CLIP logits and cache-based logits—is essential, as using either component alone significantly degrades performance. Theoretically, the authors formalize the retrieval distribution shift and prove that I2I retrieval yields a provably smaller shift (ξ_s ≤ 2κ²) than T2I (ξ_t ≥ ν − 2κ), explaining the empirical gap. They also prove that logit ensemble reduces the 0-1 risk by leveraging complementary knowledge encoded in the text and image modalities, with the bound depending on the modality gap, inner-class concentration, and inter-class separation.

## Results

Across 7 fine-grained datasets (Caltech101, Birds200, Food101, OxfordPets, Flowers102, Textures, UCF101), I2I retrieval with logit ensemble achieves an average accuracy of 72.91% at 8-shot, compared to 66.76% for zero-shot CLIP and 67.77% for T2I retrieval. The gap between I2I retrieval and the oracle (direct retrieval from the target training set) narrows to as little as 1% on average at 12 shots. Critically, removing the logit ensemble (using only the retrieval cache logit) causes significant performance degradation, confirming that combining zero-shot and retrieval logits is essential rather than optional.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary baseline is zero-shot CLIP (ZOCLIP), which serves as the reference for all retrieval-augmented methods. I2I retrieval with logit ensemble consistently outperforms zero-shot CLIP across all 7 datasets and all shot settings (1–16), with the largest gains on fine-grained categories (e.g., Flowers102 improves from 41.41% to 85.87% with BLIP-2). T2I retrieval, by contrast, yields only marginal improvements over zero-shot CLIP (e.g., 67.77% vs. 66.76% average at 8-shot with CLIP RN50), and in some cases (Food101 with BLIP-2) actually underperforms zero-shot. The key finding is that I2I retrieval closes the gap to the oracle (ID retrieval from the target training set) to within ~1% at 12 shots, while T2I retrieval remains substantially below. The logit ensemble is shown to be the critical component: using only the retrieval cache logit (RET) without ensembling with zero-shot predictions leads to significant degradation, particularly on classes where retrieval introduces semantic ambiguity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CLIP dual-encoder architecture (Radford et al., 2021) and the Tip-Adapter feature-cache adaptation framework (Zhang et al., 2022a), but departs from the dominant line of work that proposes new retrieval-augmented adaptation algorithms or integrates additional knowledge sources (e.g., synthetic samples from generative models, language-model-augmented prompts). Instead, it adopts a reflective, analytical perspective: rather than designing a new method, it systematically dissects the existing retrieval-augmented pipeline to identify which components (retrieval modality, logit ensemble) actually drive performance. The theoretical framework is novel in formalizing the modality gap and retrieval distribution shift as the key quantities governing adaptation quality, connecting empirical observations to provable bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with clear, theoretically grounded guidance on two critical design decisions: use image-to-image (not text-to-image) retrieval, and always ensemble the zero-shot logit with the retrieved-sample logit. These findings are practically important because prior work often attributed retrieval success to sample diversity or quality, potentially leading practitioners to over-invest in retrieval quality while neglecting the ensemble mechanism. Open questions include whether the theoretical assumptions (cluster structure, uniform sampling from closest retrieval cluster) hold for larger or more diverse retrieval databases, whether the findings extend to generative retrieval or multi-hop retrieval settings, and how the results scale to open-vocabulary or long-tail scenarios beyond the 7 fixed-class datasets tested.
