## Problem

LLM-based program synthesis generates each program independently from primitive operations, with no mechanism to develop reusable abstractions across examples. This leads to two coupled failures: redundant code generation that introduces errors when the same subroutine is re-derived, and an increased reasoning burden on the model, which must map queries to sequences of primitives from scratch rather than selecting from a library of interpretable, verified functions.

## Input / Output

At training time, REGAL consumes a small set of (query, primitive program) pairs (e.g., 66–200 examples) and an execution environment (Python). At test time, the agent receives a natural-language query, retrieves up to 20 relevant helper functions from the learned Code Bank by name/description similarity, and splits a fixed ICL budget (10 examples) between primitive training programs and refactored Demo Bank programs. The output is a single executable Python program that may call learned helper functions alongside domain primitives; correctness is determined by execution against the expected result.

## Method

REGAL is a gradient-free, iterative refactoring pipeline that builds a verified library of helper functions (Code Bank) and usage demonstrations (Demo Bank) from a small set of primitive programs. Training proceeds in three stages per batch: (1) a frozen LLM refactors a cluster of related programs to extract candidate helper functions; (2) refactored programs are verified by execution, with failures retried using environment error feedback; and (3) the Code Bank is periodically edited (to fix over-specific functions) and pruned (to remove functions that fail a majority of unit tests). Preprocessing clusters queries via Ada embeddings and Ward's hierarchical clustering, then sorts batches by query length as a curriculum. At test time, a ReAct-style prompt first asks the LLM to identify relevant helper functions before generating code, enabling the model to offload verified subroutines and focus reasoning on the remaining composition.

## Results

Across five datasets and five LLMs, REGAL consistently improves over a primitive-program baseline. The largest gains appear for CodeLlama-13B: +11.5% on LOGO (45.6→57.1), +26.1% on Date (42.8→68.9), and +8.1% on TextCraft (18.8→26.9). GPT-3.5 also benefits, with +12.4% on LOGO (36.9→49.3) and +12.2% on MATH (43.2→55.4). Notably, CodeLlama-13B with REGAL outperforms GPT-3.5 without REGAL on LOGO (57.1 vs. 36.9) and TextCraft (26.9 vs. 15.4), demonstrating that a smaller open-source model augmented with learned abstractions can surpass a larger proprietary model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REGAL outperforms all three external baselines from prior work. On LOGO, REGAL with CodeLlama-13B achieves 57.1% accuracy, surpassing LILO (Grand et al., 2023) at 41.1% by 16.0 points. On Date, REGAL with GPT-3.5-turbo reaches 90.2%, exceeding Faithful-CoT (Lyu et al., 2023) at 83.3% by 6.9 points. On TextCraft, REGAL with CodeLlama-34B scores 30.8%, beating the ReAct baseline (Yao et al., 2023) at 25.6% by 5.2 points. Against the internal primitive-program baseline (same ICL budget, no abstractions), REGAL wins on 4 of 5 domains for most models, with the exception of CodeLlama-7B on LOGO (tie at 34.5%) and CodeLlama-13B on TabMWP (slight drop from 29.1 to 27.9). The most dramatic internal comparison is CodeLlama-13B on Date, where REGAL's abstractions yield a 26.1-point absolute gain, effectively closing the gap to GPT-3.5's performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

REGAL sits at the intersection of program induction (DreamCoder, LILO) and LLM-based tool use (CRAFT, CREATOR, Trove), but departs from both in key ways. Unlike the Bayesian/symbolic-search lineage that assumes λ-calculus domains, REGAL uses a frozen LLM to iteratively refactor, verify, edit, and prune helper functions in general-purpose Python, requiring no domain-specific guidance or in-context examples of desired abstractions. Compared to prior tool-induction work (Yuan et al., Qian et al., Wang et al.), REGAL emphasizes iterative refactoring with execution-based verification and periodic code-bank editing/pruning rather than one-shot tool generation, and it learns a shared library usable across multiple LLMs rather than a single model's toolset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication that a 13B open-source model augmented with ~200 learned abstractions can match or exceed GPT-3.5 on multiple domains is significant for cost-sensitive deployments, converting a per-query variable cost into a one-time fixed cost of library learning. The method's generality across five heterogeneous domains (graphics, dates, Minecraft crafting, algebra, tabular math) without domain-specific prompting suggests broad applicability. Key limitations include the requirement for an execution environment, the fact that code-based abstractions are less flexible than neural parameterizations in dynamically changing environments, and the observation that not all domains (e.g., MATH, TabMWP) benefit uniformly. Open questions include scaling to larger codebases, handling distribution shifts more gracefully, and whether the approach transfers to domains where primitives are already highly abstract.
