## Problem

While the empirical risk in federated learning is known to decrease with more communication rounds R, the effect of R on the true generalization error (population risk) remained largely unexplored. Existing generalization bounds for FL either covered only the one-shot case R=1, relied on highly restrictive assumptions (linear Bregman divergence losses, minibatch size one, linear aggregation), or failed to explicitly capture the distributed multi-round structure. This paper provides the first PAC-Bayes and rate-distortion generalization bounds for FL that account explicitly for the number of rounds R, the number of devices K, and per-client dataset size n under arbitrary loss functions and algorithms.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a general (P_{W|S}, K, R, n)-FL model as input, where each of K clients holds n i.i.d. samples partitioned into R disjoint subsets, runs an arbitrary local algorithm (e.g., SGD), and sends local models to a parameter server that applies an arbitrary deterministic aggregation. The output is a generalization error upper bound decomposed into per-client, per-round contributions: PAC-Bayes bounds involve KL-divergence terms with priors that can be distinct across devices and rounds (conditioned on the previous aggregated model), while rate-distortion bounds involve mutual information terms I(S_k^{(r)}; Ŵ_k^{(r)} | W̄^{(r-1)}). For FSVM, the bound becomes an explicit O(√(B² log(nK√K) Σ_r L_r / (nK²θ²))) margin bound where L_r depends on the contractivity constant q_{e,b} raised to (R−r).

## Method

The core technique extends the variable-size compressibility approach of Sefidgaran & Zaidi (2023) from centralized to the multi-round FL setting by decomposing the overall algorithm into K×R inter-dependent 'centralized-like' virtual algorithms, each differing from the original in one (client, round) pair, and applying successive Donsker-Varadhan change-of-measure arguments to handle the statistical couplings induced by multi-round interactions. For the FSVM specialization, the proof uses Johnson-Lindenstrauss dimension reduction with round-dependent target dimensions m_r = O(B² log(nK√K) log(K/t) / (K²θ²t²)) for t ≥ q^{(R−r)}, then studies the propagation of quantization distortion through subsequent SGD rounds via induction, exploiting the contractivity property of SGD. The key finding is that the margin generalization bound increases with R for fixed (n,K), implying that more frequent communication with the PS diminishes generalization power, and that the population risk decreases less fast with R than the empirical risk—or may even increase beyond a threshold R*. Experiments with FSVM on MNIST and ResNet-56 on CIFAR-10 validate these predictions, showing generalization error growing with R while total training data and SGD iterations remain fixed.

## Results

The paper establishes the first PAC-Bayes and rate-distortion generalization bounds for FL that explicitly depend on the number of communication rounds R. For FSVM (Theorem 4.1), the margin generalization bound is O(√(B² log(nK√K) Σ_r L_r / (nK²θ²))), which increases with R and decreases with K by a factor of O(√log(K)/K) relative to centralized learning. Experiments on MNIST (digits 1 vs 6, n=100, K∈{10,20,50}) confirm that the estimated generalization error increases monotonically with R, while the population risk barely decreases beyond ~5 rounds despite continued empirical risk reduction. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-56, the population risk is minimized at R*≈100 and may even start to increase beyond that threshold, while the empirical risk keeps decreasing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without a traditional method-vs-method comparison table. The key 'comparison' is between FL and centralized learning: the FSVM generalization bound improves over centralized by a factor of O(√log(K)/K), generalizing the R=1 result of Sefidgaran et al. (2022a) to arbitrary R. The paper also contrasts with Chor et al. (2023), who showed generalization error can increase with R but under restrictive assumptions (linear Bregman divergence loss, minibatch size one, linear aggregation); the present work removes all three restrictions and applies to arbitrary loss functions and algorithms. Experimentally, the central finding is that the population risk decays less rapidly with R than the empirical risk (which is well-understood to decrease with R per Stich 2019, Haddadpour et al. 2019), and in the ResNet-56/CIFAR-10 setting the population risk can actually increase beyond R*≈100, suggesting that stopping training early (fewer communication rounds) yields better test performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of generalization analysis for federated learning from the one-shot (R=1) setting of Sefidgaran et al. (2022a) and Barnes et al. (2022a,b) to the full multi-round interactive setting, and generalizes the restrictive result of Chor et al. (2023) (which required Bregman divergence losses, minibatch size one, and linear aggregation) to arbitrary loss functions and algorithms. The PAC-Bayes bounds build on the variable-size compressibility technique of Sefidgaran & Zaidi (2023) and classic PAC-Bayes results (McAllester, Maurer, Catoni), while the rate-distortion bounds and the FSVM analysis leverage Johnson-Lindenstrauss dimension reduction in a novel multi-round propagation framework. The paper positions itself as the first to provide generalization bounds for FL that explicitly capture the effect of the number of communication rounds R alongside K and n.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical implication—that one may deliberately stop FL training before empirical convergence to improve population risk—has direct relevance to bandwidth-constrained deployments (wireless, edge devices) where each communication round has a cost. However, the theoretical guarantees are limited to FSVM under specific assumptions (contractive SGD, sufficiently large K), and the neural network evidence (ResNet-56 on CIFAR-10) is purely empirical without theoretical backing. Open questions include whether the population-risk-increase phenomenon (beyond R*) holds universally, how to exploit the round-dependent KL terms as practical regularizers during training, and whether tighter bounds can be obtained for non-convex losses and deep architectures.
