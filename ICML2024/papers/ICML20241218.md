## Problem

Existing offset transfer learning techniques for high-dimensional linear regression rely on ℓ¹/ℓ²-norm similarity measures that are incompatible with functional data, which is inherently infinite-dimensional and generated by smooth underlying processes. Truncation-based FLR methods that reduce the problem to finite-dimensional regression lose the smoothness structure and lack theoretical justification for transfer. This paper develops the first transfer learning algorithms for functional linear regression with minimax-optimal excess risk guarantees under an RKHS framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target functional dataset of n₀ pairs (X_i, Y_i) where X_i are square-integrable functions over a compact domain T, along with T source functional datasets of n_t samples each, and a reproducing kernel K that encodes the desired structural property (e.g., Sobolev smoothness or periodicity). The source index set S may be known (TL-FLR) or unknown (ATL-FLR). The output is an estimated coefficient function β̂ in the RKHS H_K, accompanied by a minimax-optimal excess risk bound of the form n_S^{-2r/(2r+1)} + n_0^{-2r/(2r+1)}·ξ(h,R), where r is the eigenvalue decay rate and ξ(h,R)=h²/R² captures the relative signal strength between offset and source models.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-step offset transfer learning procedure in the RKHS: a transfer step fuses all source datasets in S via regularized empirical risk minimization to produce a centered source estimator β̂_S, followed by a calibration step that learns the offset δ on target data alone, yielding β̂ = β̂_S + δ̂. Task similarity is measured by the RKHS norm ‖β⁽⁰⁾ − β⁽ᵗ⁾‖_K, tying the transferred information to the structural properties encoded by the kernel. When S is unknown, ATL-FLR constructs T+1 candidate source sets by ranking sources via a truncated RKHS norm, applies TL-FLR to each candidate, and then performs sparse aggregation (selecting at most two candidates) to achieve an oracle inequality with aggregation cost log(T)log(n₀)/n₀ that decays faster than the nonparametric transfer risk. A key theoretical insight is that the transfer benefit depends not only on the discrepancy h but also on the signal ratio ξ(h,R)=h²/R², meaning that even with identical h, sources with larger RKHS norm provide more effective transfer.

## Results

The paper evaluates its methods primarily on synthetic functional data (n₀=150 target samples, n_t=100 source samples, 100 replicates) with excess risk computed via 1000 Monte Carlo test points. TL-FLR achieves a consistent improvement over the OFLR baseline when the number of transferable sources is large and the RKHS distance h is small (bottom-right of the heatmap), but can underperform OFLR when few sources are available and h is large. In the unknown-S setting, ATL-FLR outperforms all competing methods (OFLR, TL-FLR with true S, DetectTL, and ATL-FLR-EW) across all tested proportions of positive sources (|S|/T ranging from 0 to 1). The theoretical excess risk rate is n_S^{-2r/(2r+1)} + n_0^{-2r/(2r+1)}·ξ(h,R), with the aggregation cost for ATL-FLR being log(T)·log(n₀)/n₀, which decays parametrically and is negligible relative to the nonparametric transfer risk.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OFLR (Cai & Yuan, 2012), the target-only RERM baseline, TL-FLR shows a clear advantage when sources are highly similar (small h, many positive sources), with the log relative excess risk dropping substantially in the bottom-right region of the heatmap; however, TL-FLR can be worse than OFLR when h is large and few sources are transferable. Against DetectTL (Tian & Feng, 2022), ATL-FLR dominates across all settings: DetectTL achieves a considerable excess risk reduction only at relatively small h but provides limited improvement as h grows, indicating it struggles when source-target similarity is low. Against ATL-FLR-EW (Li et al., 2022), which uses exponential-weighted aggregation, ATL-FLR (sparse aggregation) shows similar overall patterns but with noticeably smaller gaps when the proportion of positive sources |S|/T is small, demonstrating that sparse aggregation is more robust to negative transfer sources. Overall, ATL-FLR is the best-performing method in all tested scenarios of the unknown-S experiment, never underperforming the target-only OFLR baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the offset transfer learning (OTL) paradigm from Kuzborskij & Orabona (2013) and the aggregation-based multi-source framework of Li et al. (2022) from finite-dimensional (multivariate/high-dimensional) regression to the infinite-dimensional functional linear regression setting under an RKHS framework. The key conceptual departure is replacing ℓ¹/ℓ²-norm similarity measures with RKHS distance, tying the transferred information to structural properties (smoothness, periodicity) of the coefficient functions rather than sparsity patterns. The sparse aggregation mechanism from Gaiffas & Lecué (2011) is repurposed for robust source selection, and the entire theoretical analysis builds on the minimax rate n^{-2r/(2r+1)} established by Cai & Yuan (2012) for target-only FLR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide minimax-optimal transfer learning algorithms for functional linear regression, filling a gap where existing OTL techniques lack theoretical justification in the infinite-dimensional functional setting. The identification of the signal-ratio factor ξ(h,R) challenges the common assumption that source sample size and discrepancy magnitude alone determine transfer effectiveness, revealing that source selection becomes more critical as more data are available. Open questions include how to adaptively identify different smoothness levels for source versus offset functions (the paper notes this is non-trivial in FLR due to the composite kernel structure), and how to address the O(n_S³) computational bottleneck in the data-fusion transfer step for large-scale applications.
