## Problem

Computing optimal regression trees is NP-hard, and existing optimal methods based on MIP or constraint programming fail to scale beyond small datasets and depth limits of three. The only prior dynamic programming approach for regression (OSRT) handles only piecewise constant predictors and lacks the depth-two precomputation trick that dramatically accelerates optimal classification trees. No scalable optimal method existed for piecewise linear regression trees at all.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242004/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular data with continuous features and a numeric target label. Continuous features are binarized (via a per-feature regression tree with at most 10 splits) to form binary predicates for tree branching, while the original continuous values are retained for fitting linear models in leaf nodes. The output is a binary regression tree of bounded depth whose leaf nodes contain either a constant predictor, a simple linear model (one explanatory variable with ridge penalty), or a full multiple linear model (elastic net), selected by the user.

## Method

The paper develops three optimal dynamic programming solvers (SRT-C, SRT-SL, SRT-L) that exploit the recursive subtree structure with caching, branch-and-bound pruning, and lower bounds (k-means, equivalent-points, look-ahead, similarity). The key scalability innovation is a special depth-two algorithm that precomputes per-instance cost tuples—(y, y², 1) for constant predictors and (y, y², 1, x_j, x_j², x_jy) for each feature j in simple linear regression—so that all four leaf-node costs for any pair of splits can be derived in O(1) via inclusion-exclusion (Eq. 7), reducing depth-two search from O(|F_b|²|D|) to O(m²|D|) where m is the maximum number of satisfied binary features per instance. For multiple linear regression (SRT-L), leaf nodes are solved with GLMNet coordinate descent under elastic-net regularization, and no depth-two shortcut is provided because the per-instance cost decomposition is intractable. The depth-two algorithm yields an average 8–10× speedup for SRT-C and SRT-SL, and up to 131× over OSRT at depth three, while out-of-sample R² matches or exceeds all heuristics and prior optimal baselines.

## Results

On scalability, SRT-C (piecewise constant) is 18× faster than the previous optimal DP method OSRT on average (geometric mean), and 131× faster specifically for depth-3 trees, while MIP methods (ORT, DTIP) fail to scale beyond depth 2. The depth-two algorithm alone improves run time by a factor of 8 (SRT-C) and 10 (SRT-SL) on average. On out-of-sample R² (d=5, piecewise constant), SRT-C achieves the best score on 7 of 12 datasets (e.g., 0.67 on Airfoil, 0.81 on Auto MPG, 0.99 on Yacht), with Wilcoxon tests showing it is significantly better than GUIDE and on par with CART and IAI. For piecewise linear regression (d=4), SRT-SL achieves the best R² on 10 of 12 datasets (e.g., 0.72 on Airfoil, 0.96 on Optical Network), and SRT-L with elastic net regularization is best on 9 of 12.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the previous optimal DP method OSRT (Zhang et al., 2023), SRT-C achieves identical optimal solutions but is 18× faster on average and 131× faster for depth-3 trees, thanks to the depth-two precomputation algorithm; OSRT also exceeds the 100 GB memory limit for |D| ≥ 10⁶. Against the MIP methods ORT and DTIP (Dunn, 2018; Verwer & Zhang, 2017), SRT-C outperforms by orders of magnitude in scalability—ORT and DTIP do not finish beyond depth 2 on most datasets, while SRT-C handles depth 9. Against the greedy heuristics CART (Breiman et al., 1984) and GUIDE (Loh, 2002), SRT-C is best on 7/12 datasets versus CART's 5 and GUIDE's 2; Wilcoxon signed-rank tests confirm SRT-C is significantly better than GUIDE and statistically equal to CART. Against the local-search method IAI (Dunn, 2018), SRT-C is statistically equal in out-of-sample R² but faster for d=3 (26×) and d=4 (5×), while IAI is faster at d=6 (9×) as expected for a heuristic. For the novel piecewise linear setting, SRT-SL and SRT-L significantly outperform GUIDE-SL and GUIDE-L, and SRT-SL is best on 10/12 datasets, with no prior optimal DP baseline existing for comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dynamic programming framework for optimal decision trees—developed primarily for classification (DL8.5, MurTree, Lin et al.)—to the regression setting. It builds directly on OSRT (Zhang et al., 2023), the only prior DP method for piecewise constant regression, by adapting the depth-two precomputation trick from classification to regression via a per-instance cost decomposition (sum of y, sum of y², count). The paper also departs from the MIP-based optimal regression tree literature (Dunn, 2018; Bertsimas & Dunn, 2017; Verwer & Zhang, 2017), which handles both constant and linear predictors but suffers from poor scalability, by providing the first DP formulations for piecewise simple and multiple linear regression trees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work makes optimal piecewise linear regression trees practically computable for the first time, removing a key barrier to using provably optimal interpretable models in regression. The depth-two algorithm is the critical enabler, yielding 8–20× speedups at depth 2–3 and making the overall method 1–2 orders of magnitude faster than prior optimal approaches. Open questions remain around binarization strategy (how to discretize continuous features for splits), complexity-tuning (joint optimization of tree size and leaf model complexity), and extending to non-binary split predicates. The paper also notes that the gap between optimal and heuristic out-of-sample performance is small, suggesting that the main practical value of optimal methods lies in guaranteed training-set optimality rather than dramatic generalization gains.
