## Problem

Physics-aware deep learning methods struggle with unsteady, fast transient, and advection-dominated spatiotemporal dynamics: learning-bias approaches (PINNs, PhyCRNet, PIFNO) face difficult loss landscapes and spectral bias for sharp gradients, while the prior inductive-bias model PARC circumvented advection by normalizing the velocity field, limiting its applicability to complex transient phenomena. Additionally, when governing PDEs are unknown (e.g., mesoscale energetic materials), physics-informed loss terms cannot be defined at all.

## Input / Output

PARCv2 consumes a current state field snapshot (temperature, pressure, or velocity) together with the velocity field and, for energetic materials, a microstructure density field, all defined on a 2D spatial grid (64×64, 128×256, or 128×192 pixels depending on the problem). It outputs the predicted state and velocity fields at the next discrete time step (Δt = 0.02 s for Burgers, variable for Navier-Stokes, 0.17 ns for energetic materials). The architecture is applied recursively: each output becomes the input for the subsequent step, enabling long-horizon rollout from a single initial condition.

## Method

PARCv2 extends the PARC differentiator-integrator recurrent CNN by explicitly incorporating spatial gradient operators (∇ and Δ, computed via central finite differences) into the differentiator to model the full advection-diffusion-reaction PDE structure, rather than normalizing away advection as in the original PARC. The integrator uses a hybrid scheme: a conventional numerical method (Heun's second-order or RK4) handles low-order truncation errors, while a separate CNN learns higher-order correction terms S_u and S_x from data. Training is two-stage: the differentiator is first trained with only the numerical integral in the L1 loss, then its weights are frozen while the data-driven integrator corrections are trained against ground-truth field differences. This separation avoids the gradient conflicts between PDE-residual and data losses that plague learning-bias models, and the explicit gradient operators provide an inductive bias that narrows the search space for the learned dynamics.

## Results

On the Navier-Stokes cylindrical flow benchmark, PARCv2 achieves an RMSE of 0.0727 m/s, roughly 1/4 of the next-best baseline (PhyCRNet at 0.2094 m/s). On Burgers' equation, PARCv2 reaches 0.0129 cm/s RMSE, outperforming FNO (0.0289) and PhyCRNet (0.0588). For the energetic materials problem, PARCv2 achieves the lowest temperature RMSE (229.52 K) and competitive pressure RMSE (1.634 GPa), while also producing the best hotspot solution-quality metrics (e.g., ε_{T^hs} = 149.27 K vs. 409.58 K for PARC with numerical integration). However, PARCv2's divergence-free compliance (ε_div = 0.3738 1/s) lags behind physics-informed baselines (PhyCRNet: 0.0565, PIFNO: 0.0768) on Navier-Stokes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PARCv2 decisively outperforms all baselines on the Navier-Stokes benchmark, achieving 0.0727 m/s RMSE versus 0.2094–0.3089 m/s for PhyCRNet, PIFNO, FNO, and PARC variants—a roughly 3–4× improvement. On Burgers' equation, PARCv2 (0.0129 cm/s) beats FNO (0.0289), PhyCRNet (0.0588), and PIFNO (0.0338), though the original PARC with numerical integration is marginally better (0.0074). For the energetic materials problem, PARCv2 achieves the lowest temperature RMSE (229.52 K vs. 248–307 K for others) and the best hotspot quality metrics (e.g., ε_{T^hs} = 149.27 K vs. 409–972 K), while FNO and PARC-with-numerical-integration are competitive on pressure RMSE. The key trade-off is that PARCv2's solution quality (PDE residual and divergence-free compliance) on Navier-Stokes is notably worse than physics-informed baselines (PhyCRNet and PIFNO), which explicitly minimize PDE residuals during training, suggesting PARCv2 prioritizes prediction accuracy over strict constraint satisfaction.

## Relation to Prior Work

PARCv2 extends the PARC differentiator-integrator architecture (Nguyen et al., 2023a) by incorporating explicit spatial derivative operators (advection, diffusion) and a hybrid numerical-plus-data-driven time integrator, addressing the original PARC's inability to handle strong advection dominance without ad-hoc normalization. Conceptually, it positions itself within the inductive-bias branch of physics-aware deep learning (alongside FINN and equivariant networks), in contrast to the dominant learning-bias paradigm (PINN, PhyCRNet, PIFNO) that enforces physics through PDE-residual loss terms. The hybrid integration scheme draws on both classical numerical methods (Heun's, RK4) and the data-driven quadrature idea from Deep Euler (Shen et al., 2020), combining them to correct low- and high-order truncation errors respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PARCv2 demonstrates that inductive-bias architectures can match or exceed learning-bias methods on prediction accuracy for advection-dominated and fast-transient PDEs without requiring explicit governing equations, which is critical for domains like energetic materials where mesoscale thermodynamics lack closed-form PDEs. However, the Navier-Stokes results reveal a fundamental tension: PARCv2 achieves the best RMSE yet the worst divergence-free compliance, suggesting that inductive bias alone cannot fully enforce physical constraints and that hybrid training (inductive architecture + physics-informed loss) remains an open question. Computational cost is modestly higher than FNO/PhyCRNet, and the authors acknowledge that interpretability and the societal implications of rapidly characterizing sensitive materials warrant further attention.
