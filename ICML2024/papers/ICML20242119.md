## Problem

Existing convergence analyses for FedAvg and local SGD show that the convergence error grows dramatically with the number of local updates I, especially when the gradient divergence ζ is large, yet empirical studies consistently show that more local updates improve convergence even under highly non-IID data. This gap arises because prior analyses rely on the local Lipschitz constant L̃, which is inflated by data heterogeneity and thus overestimates the error caused by local updates. The paper asks: what is the correct theoretical metric for data heterogeneity that explains why local SGD can outperform mini-batch SGD?

## Input / Output

The analysis takes the standard FedAvg setup with N workers, I local updates per round, global learning rate η, local learning rate γ, and local data distributions D_i, and produces convergence upper bounds in which the local Lipschitz constant L̃ is replaced by two smaller quantities: the global Lipschitz constant L_g (characterizing smoothness of the global objective) and the heterogeneity-driven pseudo-Lipschitz constant L_h (characterizing the deviation of averaged local gradients from the global gradient at the averaged model). For quadratic objectives, L_h has the explicit form max_i ||A_i − A||_2 (spectral norm of Hessian deviation), while L̃ = max_i ||A_i||_2, making the reduction in the bound concrete and verifiable.

## Method

The paper introduces a new assumption (Assumption 4.2) stating that the squared norm of the difference between the averaged local gradients and the global gradient at the averaged model is bounded by L_h² times the average model divergence, where L_h is the heterogeneity-driven pseudo-Lipschitz constant. This, together with a global Lipschitz gradient assumption (Assumption 4.1) applied only to the global objective f rather than each local F_i, replaces the standard local Lipschitz gradient assumption and is provably weaker. The key insight is that L_h captures the difference between the 'virtual' averaged model and the centralized model during local updates—a quantity that can be near zero even when individual local models drift far apart (large ζ). Using this assumption, the authors derive tighter convergence bounds for FedAvg, FedAvg with momentum, FedAdam, and partial participation, where all I-dependent error terms scale with L_h rather than L̃. For quadratic objectives with a common Hessian (L_h = 0), they prove that local SGD achieves O(1/(RI)) convergence versus O(1/R) for mini-batch SGD, identifying a region where local SGD strictly outperforms mini-batch SGD regardless of how large ζ is.

## Results

The key empirical validation is that the heterogeneity-driven pseudo-Lipschitz constant L_h is orders of magnitude smaller than the local Lipschitz constant L̃ across all tested models and datasets. For example, with CNN on CIFAR-10 at 100% non-IID data, L̃ = 1662.24 while L_h = 2.15 (a ~770× reduction), and for MLP on MNIST at 100% non-IID, L̃ = 141.92 while L_h = 2.36 (~60× reduction). In the quadratic objective experiment (Table 2), local SGD with I=10 local updates requires only 95±2.4 communication rounds to reach f(x)=0.8, compared to 925±1.7 rounds for mini-batch SGD with batch size 10 (I=1, s=10), confirming that local SGD can be ~10× faster when L_h=0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is primarily theoretical and does not benchmark against competing FL algorithms on downstream accuracy. Instead, it compares its new convergence bound (using L_h and L_g) against existing bounds that rely on the local Lipschitz constant L̃. The central comparison is that L_h is empirically 60–770× smaller than L̃ (Table 1), meaning the error term γ²L_h²(I−1)²ζ² in the new bound is dramatically tighter than γ²L̃²(I−1)²ζ² in prior work (Yu et al., 2019a; Yang et al., 2020). In the quadratic objective experiment (Table 2), local SGD (I=10, s=1) requires 95 rounds versus 925 rounds for mini-batch SGD (I=1, s=10) to reach the same target loss, a ~10× speedup that existing theory (Woodworth et al., 2020b) could not explain without constraining ζ to be small. The paper also shows that for CNN with 50–75% non-IID data, the largest tested I (I=80) still achieves the smallest training loss at fixed R, whereas prior theory would predict degradation. The comparison with Wang et al. (2022) and Das et al. (2022) highlights that those works still assume local Lipschitz gradients for each worker, while this paper's assumptions are provably weaker (Proposition 5.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the convergence analysis framework for FedAvg and its extensions (FedAvg with momentum, FedAdam) established by McMahan et al. (2017), Yu et al. (2019a), and Reddi et al. (2020), but departs from the standard local Lipschitz gradient assumption (L̃) that has been the backbone of prior analyses. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a heterogeneity-driven pseudo-Lipschitz constant L_h that replaces L̃ in local-update-related error terms, revealing that L̃ conflates smoothness with data heterogeneity. The paper also positions itself against Woodworth et al. (2020a,b) on local SGD vs. mini-batch SGD comparisons, and against Wang et al. (2022) and Das et al. (2022), which proposed alternative heterogeneity metrics but still relied on the local Lipschitz assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work resolves a long-standing tension between pessimistic theoretical convergence bounds (where error scales as I²ζ²L̃²) and empirical evidence that more local updates improve convergence even under high non-IID data. The identification of L_h as a fundamentally different and often much smaller quantity than L̃ provides a principled explanation for this gap and identifies a region where local SGD provably outperforms mini-batch SGD even with arbitrarily large gradient divergence. Open questions include: the formal relationship between L_g + L_h and L̃ remains conjectural, the quadratic-case analysis relies on a non-observable 'virtual' averaged model, and the partial participation error term depends on L_g rather than L_h, limiting the benefit of small L_h in that setting.
