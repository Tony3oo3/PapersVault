## Problem

While value existence is established for zero-sum differential games with one-sided information (Cardaliaguet, 2007) and for state-constrained games without information asymmetry (Lee, 2022), the combined setting of state constraints plus one-sided information lacks a value existence proof and a characterization of equilibrium strategies. This paper fills that gap by proving value existence and deriving the primal-dual Hamilton-Jacobi equations that govern behavioral strategies when the informed player must simultaneously manipulate belief and respect state constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240622/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an 8D combined state (x/y position and velocity for each player), a 1D belief over Player 1's binary type, 2D acceleration actions, a state constraint (minimum separation distance r=0.05), and a finite time horizon T. It outputs a value function V(t,x,p) approximated by PICNNs at each time step, behavioral strategies as probability distributions over the continuous action space conditioned on (t,x,p) for Player 1 and (t,x,p̂) for Player 2, belief splitting weights λ and target beliefs p^j, and full player trajectories. The value is piecewise: +∞ on the backward reachable infeasible set Q̄(t) (approximated by a PINN) and finite on Q(t), with convexity in p enforced architecturally.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution is Theorem 1, which proves V⁺=V⁻ under Isaacs' condition plus Lipschitz/boundedness assumptions, via a comparison principle showing V⁻ is a dual supersolution and V⁺ is a dual subsolution of the same constrained HJ equation min{ρ(x)U(t,x,p)−w, w_t+H(x,Dw)}=0. Numerically, a discrete-time Bellman backup (Eq. 10) alternates between minimax over actions and convexification in belief (Vex_p), with the state constraint entering through the indicator ρ(x) that sets value to +∞ on Q̄(t). Value approximation uses PICNNs (5 hidden layers, 256 neurons) trained in a supervised fashion on data generated by scanning a belief lattice at each time step, while the backward reachable set is characterized by a separate PINN solver. Strategy synthesis for Player 1 involves solving for splitting weights λ and target beliefs p^j that decompose the convexified value (Eq. 12), yielding a behavioral strategy where type i plays action u^j with probability λ_j·p^j[i]/p_k[i]; Player 2's strategy is derived from a dual game with I+1 dimensional conjugate belief, making her problem one dimension harder than Player 1's.

## Results

The paper's primary contribution is theoretical: it proves value existence (Theorem 1) for zero-sum differential games with both state constraints and one-sided information, and establishes uniform convergence (Theorem 2) of the discrete-time Bellman backup to the true value. In the 8D football case study (Hexner's game variant with state constraint r=0.05), the authors demonstrate that Player 1 (attacker) actively delays information reveal when equidistant from the two possible targets, and that the advantage of playing a belief-manipulating (convexifying) strategy over a non-revealing strategy is quantified as [min_u max_v V(t+τ, x', p) − V(t, x, p)]. The value networks use PICNN with 5 hidden layers × 256 neurons, trained over 10 time steps (τ=0.1) with 5000 sampled states per epoch, and the backward reachable set is approximated via a PINN solver.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing numerical methods in the standard sense; its comparisons are structural rather than competitive. The case study contrasts four configurations (constrained/unconstrained × informed/uninformed, Fig. 2), showing that the state constraint fundamentally alters Player 1's strategy from the analytical Hexner solution (move to goal after critical time t_r=0.4) to an active evasion-while-pursuing behavior. Player 2's equilibrium strategy (Fig. 5) is shown to be more conservative than her best response to Player 1's known strategy, reflecting her information disadvantage and the higher-dimensional (I+1 vs. I) dual value approximation problem. The paper positions itself against no-regret learning approaches (ReBeL, DeepNash, SoG) by arguing that those methods do not enforce belief splitting and thus cannot explicitly explain when and why a random action delays information release, whereas the primal-dual subdynamic principles derived here provide that mechanistic explanation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theory of zero-sum differential games with one-sided information (Cardaliaguet 2007, 2009; Souquiere 2010) by incorporating state constraints, a setting for which value existence was previously unproven. The primal-dual decomposition and belief-splitting structure inherited from De Meyer (1996) and Cardaliaguet (2007) are preserved, but the subdynamic principles must be modified to account for the backward reachable set and the +∞ penalty for constraint violation. On the numerical side, the paper repurposes PICNN (Amos et al., 2017) to enforce convexity of the value in the belief variable and PINN (Bansal & Tomlin, 2021) to approximate the backward reachable set, rather than introducing new learning architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a theoretical gap by proving value existence and deriving the correct HJ equations for the combined setting of state constraints and one-sided information, which is necessary for safety-critical applications (e.g., autonomous driving with adversarial agents) where both physical constraints and information asymmetry are present. The belief-splitting mechanism provides an interpretable account of when and why an informed player should play deceptive random strategies, going beyond the black-box prescriptions of no-regret learning methods. Open questions include scalability to higher-dimensional belief spaces (I > 2), tighter error bounds for the backward induction with convexification, and whether the PINN-based reachable set approximation remains tractable in high dimensions.
