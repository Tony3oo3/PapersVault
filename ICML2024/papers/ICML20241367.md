## Problem

In cross-domain RL with dynamics mismatch, existing adaptation methods rely on domain classifiers (DARC), value-based data filtering (VGDF), or dynamics models, each with practical limitations such as over-pessimism or model inaccuracy. This paper asks whether representation mismatch between domains can serve as a principled, decoupled signal for dynamics discrepancy, and provides theoretical bounds showing that representation deviation upper-bounds the performance gap of any policy across the two domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241367/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes source domain transitions (s, a, r, s′) — either streamed online or drawn from a static offline dataset (e.g., D4RL medium/medium-replay/medium-expert) — together with a limited budget of target domain transitions (10⁵ steps). It outputs an adapted SAC policy for the target domain. Unusually, the learned state and state-action encoders are trained exclusively on target domain data and are never fed into the policy or value function; they serve solely to compute a scalar representation-deviation penalty that modifies source domain rewards before the SAC critics and actor are updated.

## Method

PAR trains a deterministic state encoder f_ψ(s) and a state-action encoder g_ξ(z, a) on target-domain transitions using a latent-dynamics-consistency loss (predicting the next-state representation from the current state-action representation with a stop-gradient). For each source-domain transition, the squared deviation ‖g_ξ(f_ψ(s_src), a_src) − f_ψ(s′_src)‖² is computed and subtracted from the source reward scaled by a hyperparameter β, so that transitions whose dynamics are inconsistent with the target domain receive larger penalties. The penalized source rewards and unmodified target rewards are then used to train standard SAC critics and actor (with an additional behavior-cloning term when the source domain is offline). The key theoretical result (Theorems 4.4–4.5) shows that the representation-mismatch term, expressed as a KL divergence between P(z|s′_src) and P(z|s′_tar), upper-bounds the return difference of any policy between the two domains, justifying its use as a reward penalty. Because the encoders are decoupled from policy/value learning, the approach avoids the over-pessimism of domain-classifier methods and does not require an explicit dynamics model.

## Results

In the offline source domain setting (Table 1), PAR achieves the best mean return on 17 out of 24 tasks across three D4RL dataset qualities (medium, medium-replay, medium-expert). The most striking result is on halfcheetah (no thighs) with medium-expert data, where PAR reaches 10517±476, approximately 4× the strongest baseline (VGDF+BC at 2740±297). In the online setting (Figure 2), PAR achieves the best final performance on 6 out of 8 tasks and demonstrates 2× sample efficiency over the best baseline on tasks like halfcheetah (no thighs) and ant (short feet). PAR also beats the fine-tuning baseline SAC-tune on 7 out of 8 online tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAR is compared against five baselines across online and offline settings. In the online setting, PAR outperforms SAC-tar (target-only training) on all 8 tasks, DARC (domain classifier reward correction) on all 8 tasks, and the fine-tuning baseline SAC-tune on 7 out of 8 tasks, while matching or slightly trailing VGDF (value-guided data filtering) on 2 tasks. In the offline setting, PAR surpasses CQL-0 (zero-shot offline-to-target transfer) by large margins on nearly all tasks, outperforms H2O (importance-weighted dynamics-aware offline RL) on 14 out of 24 tasks, and beats VGDF+BC on 17 out of 24 tasks. The most dramatic win is on halfcheetah (no thighs) with medium-expert data, where PAR achieves 10517 vs. VGDF+BC's 2740 (~4×). PAR's main weakness is on medium-replay datasets for walker tasks (e.g., walker no right thigh: 1249 vs. H2O's 4254), suggesting sensitivity to high dataset diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAR builds directly on the dynamics adaptation setting formalized by DARC (Eysenbach et al., 2021) and VGDF (Xu et al., 2023), which address the same problem of transferring policies across domains with transition dynamics mismatch. However, it departs from the domain-classifier and value-filtering paradigms of those methods by repurposing latent dynamics representation learning (from model-based RL and state-action representation work like Hansen et al., 2022; Fujimoto et al., 2023) for a novel role: the encoders are trained exclusively on target-domain data and used solely to compute a reward penalty on source-domain transitions, remaining entirely decoupled from policy and value function training. The theoretical grounding connects representation deviation to dynamics mismatch via KL-divergence identities and Pinsker's inequality, providing a principled justification for the penalty that prior heuristic approaches lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PAR offers a theoretically motivated, model-free approach to dynamics adaptation that avoids requirements for manipulable simulators, expert demonstrations, or carefully chosen randomization parameters, making it applicable in settings where only limited target-domain interaction is feasible. The decoupled design (representations never enter the actor or critic) makes the method modular and easy to plug into any off-policy algorithm. Key limitations include the need for manual tuning of the penalty coefficient β, degraded performance on high-diversity medium-replay datasets, and restriction to identical state/action spaces. Open questions include adaptive β scheduling, robustness to diverse offline datasets, and extension to domains with differing observation or action spaces.
