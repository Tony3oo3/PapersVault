## Problem

Non-Euclidean graphs resist modeling by pure transformers because their irregular structure forces explicit adjacency-matrix encoding into attention maps, which in turn blocks the autoregressive generation mechanism that made transformers dominant in NLP and vision. Prior graph self-supervised methods either reconstruct corrupted sub-structures (losing global topology) or rely on node-centric generation with an O(n²) link-prediction bottleneck, and no existing framework provides a fixed-length Euclidean representation that is information-equivalent to the original graph.

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes a molecular graph as a Flexible Token Sequence (FTSeq) built via DFS from the canonical SMILES, where each token carries an atom/bond type embedding from a token Codebook (118 atom types, 92 bond types) plus a decoupled position encoding from a learnable position Codebook of m vectors. The encoder outputs K ordered Graph Words of fixed dimension (512 hidden, 128-dim GPE), which serve as a Euclidean 'language' for the graph. The decoder takes these K Graph Words as prefix prompts and autoregressively emits the full FTSeq (edges with attached/placed nodes); for conditional generation, normalized property scalars (QED, SA, logP) and a scaffold FTSeq are prepended to the encoder input.

## Method

GraphsGPT couples a Graph2Seq pure-transformer encoder with a GraphGPT GPT-style decoder, pretrained end-to-end on ~100M ZINC molecules via a GPT-style cross-entropy objective. The encoder flattens the graph into an FTSeq and compresses it into K learnable Graph Words through a transformer that uses a randomly shuffled position Codebook to enforce permutation invariance without sacrificing autoregressive compatibility. The decoder employs an edge-centric generation strategy: at each step it predicts the next edge type (O(|D_e|) classification), then resolves the left endpoint by cosine-similarity matching against existing node position encodings, and resolves the right endpoint either as an existing node (above a similarity threshold ε) or as a new atom, reducing overall generation complexity from O(n+n²) to O(n+n′). Block-wise causal attention is used so that within each (edge, new-node) block full attention is applied, while across blocks a standard causal mask enforces autoregressive ordering. The entire 50M-parameter model (8 layers, 8 heads, Swish + RMSNorm) is trained for 100K steps with AdamW on 8 A100s, and the resulting Graph Words support downstream tasks including property prediction via a linear probe, few-shot/conditional molecular generation, and Euclidean-space graph mixup.

## Results

On MoleculeNet (standard scaffold splitting), pretrained Graph2Seq-1W achieves SOTA on 8/9 molecular property prediction tasks, with headline numbers of 76.9 ROC-AUC on Tox21, 79.4 on HIV, 83.4 on BACE, and 0.860 RMSD on ESOL. In few-shot generation on MOSES (100K shots), GraphGPT-1W reaches 99.5% validity and 100% uniqueness at s=2.0, outperforming MolGPT (99.4% validity, 100% uniqueness) on novelty (1.0 vs 0.797). On ZINC-250K, GraphGPT-1W achieves 99.67% validity and 0.0002 NSPDK, substantially surpassing the best diffusion baseline GDSS (97.01% validity, 0.019 NSPDK). For graph mixup, the simple Euclidean latent-space mixup of Graph Words outperforms the ICML'22 G-Mixup method on all six evaluated tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest pretraining baselines, Graph2Seq-1W outperforms Mole-BERT (Xia et al., 2023) on 7/9 tasks (e.g., Sider 68.2 vs 62.8, BACE 83.4 vs 80.8) and GraphMVP (Liu et al., 2021) on 8/9 tasks, while losing only on Lipo (0.716 vs 0.681 RMSD). Compared to the node-centric GPT-GNN (Hu et al., 2020), Graph2Seq-1W achieves substantially larger gains, particularly on Sider (+10.1 pts) and HIV (+21.1 pts), demonstrating that the edge-centric GPT-style pretraining with pure transformer is more effective than node-centric GNN-based generation. Against contrastive baselines (GraphCL, JOAO, SimGRACE, AD-GCL), Graph2Seq-1W leads on all six classification tasks, with the largest margin on Sider (68.2 vs 61.5 for AD-GCL). In generation, GraphGPT-1W surpasses MolGPT (Bagal et al., 2021) on validity (99.5% vs 99.4%), novelty (1.0 vs 0.797 at s=2.0), and IntDiv, while also outperforming all nine diffusion/flow-based baselines (GraphAF, GraphDF, MoFlow, EDP-GNN, GraphEBM, SPECTRE, GDSS, DiGress, GRAPHARM) on ZINC-250K by large margins in validity (99.67% vs best 97.01%) and NSPDK (0.0002 vs best 0.019). For graph mixup, the Euclidean latent-space mixup of Graph Words outperforms G-Mixup (Han et al., 2022, ICML'22) on all six MoleculeNet tasks, e.g., BACE 85.4 vs 77.8 and BBBP 73.4 vs 70.2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TokenGT (Kim et al., 2022), which demonstrated that pure transformers with decoupled position encodings can serve as strong graph learners without explicit adjacency-matrix modifications to attention. It departs from the broader graph transformer lineage (GT, GAT, Graphormer) that requires structural information in the attention computation, and from node-centric generative pretraining (GPT-GNN) by proposing an edge-centric auto-regressive formulation that jointly generates edges and their endpoint nodes. The framework also extends the GPT-style pretraining paradigm from NLP to graphs, but introduces a key architectural innovation: a fixed-length Euclidean 'Graph Words' bottleneck that ensures information equivalence between the Non-Euclidean graph and its Euclidean representation, enabling operations like mixup that were previously intractable in graph space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing a principled Non-Euclidean-to-Euclidean bridge with guaranteed information preservation, GraphsGPT opens the door to a class of graph operations (interpolation, hybridization, mixup) that were previously considered fundamentally challenging due to the irregular structure of graphs. The tension between permutation invariance and auto-regressive generation remains partially unresolved (96–98% self-consistency rather than 100%), and the framework is validated almost exclusively on molecular graphs, leaving open whether the edge-centric formulation generalizes to other graph domains (social networks, 3D meshes, knowledge graphs). The simplicity of the pretraining objective—single GPT-style graph reconstruction—suggests that the field may have over-engineered multi-pretext strategies for graph SSL.
