## Problem

High-probability generalization bounds via algorithmic stability have traditionally required the loss function to be bounded, limiting applicability to settings like regularized regression, domain adaptation, and importance-weighting where losses are naturally unbounded. Prior relaxations handled subgaussian (Kontorovich, 2014) and subexponential (Maurer & Pontil, 2021) diameters, but the paper addresses the broader and more practical case of subweibull tails, which encompass heavier-tailed losses. Additionally, existing bounds scale with √n in the dominant term, and the paper seeks to improve this to a log n rate.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a training sample S = (Z_1,...,Z_n) drawn i.i.d. from a metric probability space (Z, d, μ) with finite subweibull diameter Δ_α(Z), a symmetric learning algorithm A that is γ-totally Lipschitz stable, and an unbounded loss function ℓ: Y×Y → R_+. The output is a high-probability bound on R(A,S) − R_n(A,S) that depends on γ, Δ_α(Z), n, and the confidence parameter δ, with the tail behavior governed by the subweibull parameter α (where α=2 recovers subgaussian and α=1 recovers subexponential). The concentration inequalities also serve as standalone tools, producing moment and tail bounds for functions of independent subweibull variables under a Lipschitz-type sensitivity condition.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a pair of concentration inequalities (moment and probabilistic) for functions of independent subweibull random variables, proved via a symmetrization argument combined with Latała's moment estimates for Weibull-tailed variables and the Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality. Because subweibull distributions are heavy-tailed, the standard exponential-moment proof techniques from Kontorovich (2014) and Maurer & Pontil (2021) fail; instead, the authors work through L_p moment bounds and convert to tail bounds via Markov's inequality. For the sharper √n-improvement, they extend Bousquet et al.'s (2020) sample-splitting/dyadic partitioning argument to unbounded subweibull variables, establishing a moment inequality for sums of weakly-dependent unbounded random variables (Lemma 2.15) that replaces the bounded-loss assumption with a subweibull diameter condition. The resulting generalization bound improves the dominant term from γΔ_α(Z)√n to γΔ_α(Z) log n, and the framework is instantiated for regularized nearest-neighbor regression and Rademacher complexity bounds.

## Results

The paper establishes high-probability generalization bounds for algorithmic stability with unbounded subweibull losses. The main bound (Theorem 2.12) gives R(A,S) − R_n(A,S) ≲ γΔ_α(Z) + γΔ_α(Z)(√(n log(1/δ)) + log^(1/α)(1/δ)), extending prior subgaussian (α=2) and subexponential (α=1) results to the full subweibull family. The sharper bound (Theorem 2.17) improves the dominant term from O(γΔ_α(Z)√n) to O(γΔ_α(Z) log n · log(1/δ)), yielding a √n-fold improvement over Kontorovich (2014) and Maurer & Pontil (2021). For the application to regularized 1-NN regression, the rate improves from O(1/√n) to O(1/n).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's bounds are compared against three prior theoretical results. Against Bousquet & Elisseeff (2002), the new bounds remove the bounded-loss requirement entirely, replacing the constant M with the subweibull diameter Δ_α(Z), while also extending the tail from subgaussian to subweibull. Against Kontorovich (2014) (subgaussian, α=2) and Maurer & Pontil (2021) (subexponential, α=1), Theorem 2.12 recovers their rates as special cases but extends to the full subweibull family (0 < α ≤ 2). The sharper Theorem 2.17 provides a strict improvement over both: the dominant √n dependence is replaced by log n, giving a √n-fold faster convergence rate. For the 1-NN regression application, this translates to an O(1/n) rate versus the O(1/√n) rate in Kontorovich (2014). The paper also notes that Yuan & Li (2023) achieve similar sharper rates but only up to subexponential (α=1), whereas the present work covers the heavier-tailed regime 0 < α < 1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two threads in learning theory: the algorithmic stability line (Bousquet & Elisseeff 2002 → Kontorovich 2014 → Maurer & Pontil 2021) that progressively relaxed boundedness assumptions on losses, and the sharper-bounds line (Bousquet et al. 2020) that improved convergence rates via sample-splitting for bounded losses. The authors unify these threads by introducing subweibull concentration inequalities (building on the ψ_α-norm framework of Vladimirova et al. 2020) that simultaneously generalize the subgaussian and subexponential diameter results and enable the Bousquet et al. sample-splitting technique in the unbounded setting. The key technical departure from Kontorovich and Maurer & Pontil is that standard proof techniques (e.g., direct exponential moment arguments) fail under heavier tails, forcing a moment-inequality-based approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The subweibull framework captures a strictly broader class of heavy-tailed losses (e.g., in regularized regression, domain adaptation, and importance-weighting) than prior subgaussian/subexponential results, making the generalization guarantees applicable to more realistic settings. The √n improvement in the sharper bound (Theorem 2.17) is practically meaningful for small-sample regimes. However, the paper is purely theoretical with no empirical validation, and the constants in the bounds are loose; a key open question the authors flag is whether common algorithms like SGD satisfy the required totally Lipschitz stability condition. The concentration inequalities for subweibull variables may find independent use beyond stability analysis.
