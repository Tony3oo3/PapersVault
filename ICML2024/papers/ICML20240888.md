## Problem

LLMs trained with a fixed context window exhibit unpredictable behavior and exploding perplexity when inference sequences exceed that window, a failure the paper attributes to an out-of-distribution (O.O.D.) positional encoding issue rather than a fundamental lack of long-context understanding. Existing remedies (PI, CLEX, YaRN, LongLoRA, etc.) all require fine-tuning, which is resource-intensive, risks degrading short-context performance, and depends on scarce high-quality long-text data. The paper argues that LLMs possess inherent long-context capabilities that can be elicited at inference time without any parameter updates.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained LLM with RoPE positional encoding and an arbitrary-length text sequence as input, requiring only two scalar hyperparameters: a group size G_s and a neighbor window size w_n. It produces the same output format as the original model (generated tokens / attention scores) but with attention computed over an extended effective context window of (L − w_n)·G_s + w_n tokens, where L is the original pretraining window. Because the modification is purely at inference time and only activates when the sequence exceeds the original window, short-context behavior is automatically preserved with zero overhead.

## Method

SelfExtend replaces the standard self-attention with a bi-level attention computed before the softmax. For token pairs whose relative distance exceeds the neighbor window w_n, it applies a floor-division mapping (position // G_s) to both query and key positions, collapsing unseen large relative distances back into the range of positions encountered during pretraining (grouped attention). For token pairs within w_n of each other, it retains the original RoPE-based self-attention (neighbor attention) to preserve local positional precision critical for next-token generation. The two attention matrices are merged by overwriting the out-of-window entries of the neighbor-attention matrix with the grouped-attention values, then a single softmax is applied. A shift of w_n − w_n//G_s is added to the grouped query positions to ensure a smooth transition at the window boundary. The entire procedure requires no weight updates, no additional parameters, and only a minor code modification to the attention kernel, making it a plug-and-play inference-time extension.

## Results

On LongBench, SelfExtend extends Llama-2-7B-chat from 4k to 16k context, improving HotpotQA from 25.4 to 34.34 and NarrativeQA from 18.7 to 21.69, while SE-Mistral-7B-16k achieves 45.28 on HotpotQA and 23.56 on NarrativeQA. On L-Eval, SE-SOLAR-10.7B-16k reaches an average of 58.30 (vs. 55.34 for base SOLAR-4k), and SE-Mistral-7B-16k achieves 45.27 (vs. 43.70 for Mistral w/ SWA). On PG-19 perplexity, SelfExtend keeps PPL stable at ~9.0–9.4 at 16k tokens where the vanilla Llama-2-7B-chat PPL explodes above 10³. On passkey retrieval, SelfExtend achieves 100% accuracy across all context lengths (4k–24k) and all depths without any fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SelfExtend-Llama-2-16k substantially outperforms the base Llama-2-7B-chat-4k on most LongBench tasks (HotpotQA 34.34 vs. 25.4; NarrativeQA 21.69 vs. 18.7) and also beats the fine-tuned CLEX-7B-16k (HotpotQA 34.34 vs. 28.44) and LongChat1.5-7B-32k (HotpotQA 34.34 vs. 31.5, NarrativeQA 21.69 vs. 16.9). SE-Mistral-7B-16k surpasses Mistral w/ SWA on NarrativeQA (23.56 vs. 19.40), HotpotQA (45.28 vs. 42.29), and GovReport (30.71 vs. 27.38), though MistralLite-16k (fine-tuned on 2B+ tokens) still leads on most tasks—albeit with potential data contamination. SE-Phi-2-8k dramatically improves over the 2k base (NarrativeQA 12.04 vs. 4.46, TREC 61.00 vs. 50.50), and SE-SOLAR-10.7B-16k improves over SOLAR-4k on NarrativeQA (22.63 vs. 16.50) and HotpotQA (46.19 vs. 44.03). On short-context tasks (Table 4), SelfExtend preserves performance nearly identically (e.g., SE-Llama-2-chat-16k: MMLU 48.09 vs. 48.20; SE-Mistral: GSM8K 31.39 vs. 30.93). In the varying-length passkey retrieval (Figure 6), SelfExtend maintains high accuracy on 64- and 100-digit passkeys where fine-tuned methods like LongLora and Together-32k degrade sharply, with only Yarn remaining competitive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on RoPE (Su et al., 2022) and reframes the context-window extension problem as a positional out-of-distribution issue rather than a fundamental capability gap. It departs from the dominant fine-tuning-based extension methods (PI, CLEX, YaRN, LongLoRA, ABF) by arguing that LLMs already possess inherent long-context ability and that a simple floor-division remapping of unseen relative positions to seen ones suffices. Conceptually, it shares the multi-level positional encoding intuition with T5 and iRPE, and subsumes ReRoPE as a degenerate special case (group size → ∞), while adding a neighbor-attention window to preserve local precision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SelfExtend offers a genuinely practical, zero-training, plug-and-play solution for context extension that can be applied to any RoPE-based LLM at inference time, lowering the barrier to long-context deployment. However, performance degrades with large group sizes, preventing arbitrarily long contexts, and the paper itself acknowledges that PPL is an unreliable proxy for true long-context understanding. Open questions include whether more sophisticated position-mapping functions (beyond floor division) can push the effective window further, and how to properly evaluate long-context ability beyond perplexity and synthetic retrieval tasks.
