## Problem

Learning conditional distributions requires estimating a collection of distributions (one per covariate value) from often severely imbalanced and scarce data, where many covariates have only a single observed response. Existing generative approaches such as CWGAN suffer from mode collapse and fail to recover the true conditional distributions, while naive resampling methods do not scale to high-dimensional covariate spaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241534/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a multi-dimensional covariate vector x (e.g., 8 attributes in LDW-CPS, 5 in ECM) and a uniform random variable U ~ U(0,1) as input, producing a scalar response sample T_θ(x, U) that approximates the conditional distribution Y|X=x. Training data is organized as groups (x_i, {y_{i,k}}_{k=1..N_i}) where N_i varies widely (1 to 47 in LDW-CPS), and a KDE-estimated conditional CDF with bandwidth h is used in place of the true CDF during training. The response space is restricted to one dimension, and the generative network is a 7-layer MLP with ReLU activations.

## Method

GENTLE learns a generative map T_θ(x,U) and a conditional Kantorovich potential network v_φ(x,y) via a min-max objective combining a fitness term (measuring |U − F̂_{μ_X}(T_θ(X,U))|² with KDE-estimated CDFs) and a regularization term (sum of pairwise entropic OT distances between T_θ(x_i,·)_#U and T_θ(x_j,·)_#U over edges of a minimum spanning tree on the covariates, computed via the semi-dual EOT formulation). The regularization enforces a Lipschitz smoothness condition across nearby covariates to prevent overfitting under data scarcity. The resulting nonconvex-nonconcave min-max problem is solved with a doubly-smoothed gradient descent-ascent algorithm (Zheng et al., 2023) that introduces auxiliary variables p, q and quadratic smoothing terms to guarantee convergence to a stationary point under the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz condition.

## Results

On the LDW-CPS dataset, GENTLE achieves a Wasserstein distance of 3767.62 (±1337.56) and KS statistic of 0.48 (±0.05), outperforming all four baselines on both metrics. On the ECM dataset, GENTLE achieves a WD of 0.97 (±0.14) and KS of 0.21 (±0.02), again the best across all methods. GENTLE also exhibits the smallest standard deviations on both datasets, indicating more consistent generation quality across test covariates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GENTLE outperforms all four baselines on both WD and KS across both datasets. CWGAN (Athey et al., 2021) suffers severe mode collapse, producing distributions that collapse to the training mean, resulting in the worst WD (13372 on LDW-CPS, 4.42 on ECM) and high KS values. WGAN-GP (Gulrajani et al., 2017) also exhibits shrinkage toward the conditional mean, yielding WD of 7829 (LDW-CPS) and 1.59 (ECM). MGAN (Baptista et al., 2020) is the closest competitor on LDW-CPS WD (4097 vs. 3768) but still loses on KS (0.91 vs. 0.48) and on both metrics for ECM. CDSB (Shi et al., 2022) avoids the collapse tendency of the GAN-based methods and is the strongest baseline on KS for LDW-CPS (0.56 vs. 0.48), yet GENTLE still leads on both metrics for both datasets with notably lower variance.

## Relation to Prior Work

GENTLE is the first work to cast conditional distribution learning as a neural entropic optimal transport problem, repurposing the semi-dual Kantorovich potential formulation (Genevay et al., 2016) and the neural OT map parametrization (Korotin et al., 2022; Makkuva et al., 2020) in a new context. It departs from prior conditional generative approaches—CWGAN (Athey et al., 2021), QRGMM (Hong et al., 2023), and CDSB (Shi et al., 2022)—by replacing adversarial or diffusion-based training with a min-max objective where the dual network approximates a conditional Kantorovich potential, and by introducing an entropic-OT-based Lipschitz regularization over a minimum spanning tree of covariates. The optimization leverages the doubly-smoothed gradient descent-ascent algorithm of Zheng et al. (2023) to handle the nonconvex-nonconcave structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practically critical gap: learning a full collection of conditional distributions from severely imbalanced, small-sample data (e.g., 13,000+ covariates with a single response in LDW-CPS), a setting where GAN-based baselines collapse to the mean. The entropic-OT regularization via a minimum spanning tree is a clean, interpretable mechanism for enforcing covariate-to-distribution smoothness without requiring paired data. However, the framework is restricted to one-dimensional responses and validated on only two datasets, leaving open whether the approach extends to multi-dimensional or time-series responses, and whether the KDE bandwidth sensitivity can be resolved more principledly.
