## Problem

Existing deep exploration strategies in RL—action noise, exploration bonuses, and randomized value functions—fail to consistently outperform basic action noise across diverse tasks. The paper identifies that randomized value function methods (e.g., NoisyNet, bootstrapped DQN) do not produce sufficiently diverse value function distributions, while bonus-based methods require additional networks and still underperform in most settings. RLE bridges these two families by directly perturbing rewards with structured random signals rather than randomizing over value functions or estimating state novelty.

## Input / Output

The agent receives the standard environment observation (4 stacked 84×84 grayscale frames in ATARI, or a state vector in ISAACGYM) augmented with a latent vector z ∈ ℝ^d sampled from a fixed distribution (unit sphere S^{d-1}). The policy π(·|s,z) and value network V^π(s,z) both condition on z, and the received reward at each step is the sum of the task reward R(s,a) and a state-dependent random reward F(s_{t+1},z) computed as the dot product of a standardized feature φ(s_{t+1}) with z. The latent vector is resampled at the start of each trajectory (or every 1280 timesteps in ATARI), not per timestep, to preserve temporal consistency within a rollout.

## Method

RLE introduces a randomized reward function F(s,z) = φ(s)·z where φ is a feature extractor whose parameters are slowly updated (τ=0.005) from the value network via a moving average, and z is a latent vector drawn from a fixed distribution (unit sphere in d=8 dimensions for ATARI, d=32 for ISAACGYM). Both the policy and value network are conditioned on z following the UVFA paradigm, so the changing random reward does not appear as unobserved noise. The random reward is computed at the next state s_{t+1} to credit the action taken at s_t, and the agent is trained with standard PPO on the combined reward R(s,a)+F(s_{t+1},z). The key design choices ensuring trajectory diversity are: (1) the state-dependence of F (correlated with φ(s) rather than white noise), (2) the latent conditioning that lets the policy adapt its behavior to each z, and (3) per-episode resampling of z that maintains temporal consistency while generating diverse trajectories across episodes. The feature network is randomly initialized and frozen in its linear head, with only the backbone slowly tracking the value network.

## Results

On ATARI (57 games, 40M frames), RLE achieves the highest interquartile mean (IQM) of human-normalized scores among all methods, with a probability of improvement (POI) over PPO, RND, and NoisyNet all having lower 95% confidence bounds above 0.5. On ISAACGYM (9 continuous control tasks), RLE achieves a POI of 0.66 (95% CI: [0.60, 0.72]) over PPO and a higher IQM of PPO-normalized return, with particularly large gains in ALLEGROHAND and SHADOWHAND. In the FOURROOM sparse-reward setting, RLE and NoisyNet achieve an average score of 0.6 while RND and PPO score 0, confirming RLE's ability to perform deep exploration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLE consistently outperforms standard PPO (action noise) across both ATARI and ISAACGYM: on ATARI, RLE's POI over PPO has a lower 95% CI bound above 0.5, and on ISAACGYM the POI is 0.66 (95% CI: [0.60, 0.72]). Against RND (bonus-based exploration), RLE achieves a higher aggregate IQM on ATARI, while RND's POI over PPO is below 0.5, indicating RND does not consistently improve over PPO despite excelling in a few hard-exploration games (e.g., Montezuma's Revenge: 4406.79 vs. RLE's 79.48). Against NoisyNet (randomized value functions), RLE again achieves a higher IQM on ATARI, and NoisyNet's POI over PPO is also below 0.5. In the FOURROOM sparse-reward setting, RLE matches NoisyNet (both score 0.6) while both RND and PPO score 0, and RLE's state visitation is more evenly spread across all four rooms compared to the other methods. Notably, RLE underperforms PPO on a small number of ATARI games (e.g., Asteroids, Hero, KungFuMaster, UpNDown, YarsRevenge), but its aggregate and statistical advantages across the full suite are clear.

## Relation to Prior Work

RLE bridges two established families of deep exploration methods: reward-bonus approaches (RND, curiosity-driven) that estimate state novelty, and randomized value function methods (NoisyNet, bootstrapped DQN) grounded in Thompson sampling. Conceptually, it draws from UVFA (Schaul et al., 2015) for conditioning policies on latent variables and from skill discovery (Eysenbach et al., 2018) for using varied reward functions to induce diverse behaviors. The key departure is that RLE replaces the complex novelty-estimation machinery of bonus methods and the ensemble/parameter-noise machinery of randomized value functions with a simple dot-product random reward F(s,z) = φ(s)·z, conditioned on a per-episode latent vector z, making it a lightweight plug-in on top of any policy-gradient algorithm like PPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RLE's practical significance lies in its simplicity and generality: it requires only adding a random reward term and augmenting the policy/value input with a latent vector, with minimal hyperparameter changes, yet consistently improves over PPO, RND, and NoisyNet across both ATARI (discrete) and ISAACGYM (continuous) benchmarks. The POI analysis showing that RND and NoisyNet do not statistically outperform PPO overall, while RLE does, challenges the assumption that more complex exploration machinery is necessary. Open questions include extending RLE to off-policy algorithms (DQN, SAC), adapting the fixed latent distribution P_z during training, and validating the approach in real-world robotics beyond simulators.
