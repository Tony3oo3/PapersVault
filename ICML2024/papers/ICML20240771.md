## Problem

Existing parameter pruning methods for continual learning (e.g., WSN) partition the network into only two parts—a frozen pruned sub-network for old tasks and unpruned parameters for new tasks—thereby ignoring shared knowledge between tasks. This monopolization of the pruned sub-network by old tasks severely limits model plasticity and capacity, causing significant under-fitting in long-term continual learning where many tasks arrive sequentially.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240771/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates in the task-incremental learning (TIL) setting, where task identity is available at inference. Inputs are images from sequentially arriving tasks (28×28 grayscale for PMNIST, 32×32 RGB for CIFAR-100 variants, 64×64 RGB for TinyImageNet), and the output is a class label prediction per task. The network parameters are organized into three binary-masked subsets (frozen, shared, free) whose masks are updated task-by-task via Huffman encoding, and the shared subset is further selected per-task using a CWI importance score computed from the current training gradient.

## Method

OSN extends the two-part pruning scheme of WSN into a three-part partition: (1) a frozen pruned sub-network preserving old knowledge, (2) a set of shared parameters selected from the pruned sub-network via a task-aware CWI importance function, and (3) unpruned free parameters for new-task learning. The shared parameters are updated by projecting their gradients onto the orthogonal complement of old-task feature space (constructed via SVD of the feature matrix), ensuring zero interference with past tasks while enabling knowledge transfer. To improve this projection, OSN introduces a sharpness-aware objective that seeks flat minima by adding a worst-case perturbation term to the loss, combined with a data perturbation strategy that interpolates pairs of training samples to broaden the effective data distribution. The orthogonal projection is refined by selecting the singular vector corresponding to the minimum singular value under a Frobenius-norm threshold, yielding a more stable flat-loss update direction for the shared parameters.

## Results

OSN achieves state-of-the-art average accuracy (ACC) across all five evaluation benchmarks: 97.19% on PMNIST, 79.78% on Split CIFAR-100, 63.81% on CIFAR-100 Superclass, 93.32% on 5-Datasets, and 75.42% on Split TinyImageNet (40 tasks, long-term CL). Backward transfer (BWT) is near zero (−0.01% to 0.01%) on most datasets, confirming near-forget-free behavior. On the long-term Split TinyImageNet benchmark, OSN outperforms the second-best method WSN by 3.46% in ACC, demonstrating the advantage of shared-knowledge exploration under many sequential tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSN outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset where a comparison is available. The closest competitor is WSN (Kang et al., 2022), the parameter-pruning method that OSN directly extends; OSN surpasses WSN by 0.68% on PMNIST, 3.92% on Split CIFAR-100, 2.47% on CIFAR-100 Superclass, 1.02% on 5-Datasets, and 3.46% on Split TinyImageNet. Among gradient-projection methods, GPM (Saha & Roy, 2021) and TRGP (Lin et al., 2022b) trail OSN by 4.82% and 5.32% respectively on Split CIFAR-100, while DFGP (Yang et al., 2023) lags by 5.19% on the same benchmark. Parameter-expansion and rehearsal-based methods (PackNet, SupSup, Connector) achieve zero forgetting (BWT = 0) but sacrifice plasticity, yielding 4–24% lower ACC than OSN depending on the dataset. On the long-term Split TinyImageNet (40 tasks), OSN's 75.42% ACC exceeds the next-best WSN (71.96%) by 3.46%, a gap that widens relative to other methods (e.g., SupSup at 59.60%, GPM at 67.39%), confirming that shared-knowledge exploration becomes increasingly critical as the number of sequential tasks grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Winning Subnetworks (WSN) framework, which partitions a network into a frozen pruned sub-network (preserving old knowledge) and a free unpruned portion (learning new knowledge), by introducing a third partition: shared parameters selected from within the pruned sub-network that are important for both old and new tasks. These shared parameters are updated via a sharpness-aware orthogonal projection, building on the null-space projection ideas of Wang et al. (CVPR 2021) and Saha & Roy (ICLR 2021), while the parameter importance scoring reuses the CWI function from SparCL (Wang et al., NeurIPS 2022). The sharpness-aware loss modification draws on the flatness-aware gradient projection of Yang et al. (ICCV 2023), but is applied specifically to the orthogonal projection of shared parameters rather than to all free parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled reframing of the stability-plasticity dilemma in parameter-pruning-based CL: rather than treating the pruned sub-network as purely a memory store, it identifies a subset of those parameters that can be safely updated to capture inter-task shared knowledge, yielding meaningful gains (up to 3.46% ACC) in long-term CL with 40+ tasks where prior methods degrade. A key limitation is that all experiments are in the task-incremental learning (TIL) setting with known task identity at inference; the authors explicitly note extending to class-incremental learning as future work. Additionally, the orthogonal projection still relies on approximate SVD of old-task features, and the method requires storing feature matrices across tasks, which may limit scalability to very long task sequences.
