## Problem

Existing graph self-supervised learning (SSL) frameworks struggle to simultaneously capture high-level abstract semantics and fine-grained structural features, leading to sub-optimal generalization across diverse downstream tasks. Contrastive methods tend to over-emphasize fine-grained instance discrimination while neglecting coarse abstractions, and generative methods force encoders into fine-grained reconstruction, leaving high-level patterns underexploited. No prior graph SSL framework explicitly leverages multi-granularity to adapt representations to tasks with differing semantic requirements.

## Input / Output

MGSE takes as input a frozen pre-trained teacher model (any existing graph SSL method, e.g., GraphCL, GraphMAE, GraphLoG) and unlabeled graphs with node/edge feature vectors. It produces K student model graph embeddings (K=3 by default, with prototype counts {2, 21, 50} defining coarse-to-fine granularities) that are ensembled via simple averaging into a single classification prediction. The framework is plug-and-play: it requires no modification to the teacher architecture and only adds K identically-structured student models plus K trainable prototype matrices, with computation scaling linearly as O(K·M) in edges.

## Method

MGSE extracts multi-granular semantic knowledge through self-supervised prototypical distillation: a frozen teacher model maps each graph to a representation, and K randomly initialized student models (same GNN architecture) are trained to match the teacher's output in K distinct probability spaces defined by prototype sets of increasing size (D₁ < D₂ < … < D_K). Soft prototypical assignments (cosine similarity to L₂-normalized prototype centroids with temperature scaling) produce target and anchor distributions at each granularity, and the training objective minimizes negative cross-entropy between them plus a ME-MAX regularization term that enforces uniform prototype utilization to prevent representation collapse. A sharpening operation (τ_t = τ_s^{1/P}, P<1) on the teacher distribution further encourages confident, discriminative targets. At inference, the K independently fine-tuned student models are ensembled by averaging their predictions, and a variance-reduction theorem (Theorem 4.2) guarantees the ensemble error is no worse than any single student, while Proposition 4.1 proves the combined distillation+regularization objective has non-zero gradient under representation collapse, ensuring the prototypes remain informative.

## Results

MGSE is applied as a plug-and-play module on six state-of-the-art graph SSL teacher models (GraphCL, RGCL, AD-GCL, JOAO, GraphMAE, GraphLoG) and evaluated on nine graph-level datasets (8 chemistry + 1 biology) under scaffold-split transfer learning. The framework consistently improves all baselines, with average AUC gains ranging from +0.68 (0.9%) for GraphLoG to +3.09 (3.9%) for GraphMAE, and a maximum single-dataset improvement of +7.02 AUC (9.2%) for GraphLoG on ClinTox. On node-level tasks (Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed, ogbn-arxiv), MGSE similarly boosts GRACE, GraphMAE, and CCA-SSG by 0.1–2.2% accuracy, with larger gains on the more complex ogbn-arxiv dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MGSE is not a standalone method but a plug-and-play distillation module, so it is evaluated by augmenting each of the six graph-level SSL baselines and three node-level baselines. On graph-level tasks, MGSE improves every baseline on nearly every dataset: GraphCL gains +2.0 to +3.8 AUC (e.g., BACE 75.38→79.22), JOAO gains +1.0 to +2.5 AUC, and GraphMAE shows the largest average gain (+3.09 AUC, 3.9%), particularly on multi-label datasets like SIDER (+2.16) and ClinTox (+2.11). The single largest improvement is GraphLoG on ClinTox (+7.02 AUC, 9.2%), though GraphLoG's average gain is modest (+0.68) because it already incorporates prototypical clustering. Improvements are smaller for baselines with strong structural augmentation (AD-GCL, RGCL), which already push representations toward coarse features. On node-level tasks, MGSE consistently improves GRACE (e.g., Citeseer 71.20→73.40, +2.2%), GraphMAE (ogbn-arxiv 70.55→71.68, +1.13%), and CCA-SSG (ogbn-arxiv 71.24→72.17, +0.93%), with the largest relative gains on the most complex dataset (ogbn-arxiv). No baseline shows a consistent degradation after MGSE is applied, with only two minor exceptions (GraphMAE on BBBP −0.38, GraphLoG on BACE −0.32).

## Relation to Prior Work

MGSE repurposes the knowledge distillation paradigm—originally designed for model compression (Hinton et al., 2014) and later shown to improve single-model performance via self-distillation (Fang et al., 2020; Allen-Zhu & Li, 2023)—into a multi-granular semantic extraction tool for graph SSL. It draws on prototypical contrastive learning (Li et al., 2021; Xu et al., 2021) to define granularity through prototype sets of varying cardinality, and on ME-MAX regularization (Assran et al., 2021) to prevent prototype collapse. The key departure from prior graph SSL work (GraphCL, GraphMAE, GraphLoG, etc.) is that those methods optimize representations at a single implicit granularity, whereas MGSE explicitly decomposes the teacher's knowledge into non-overlapping coarse-to-fine semantic levels via multiple students, then ensembles them at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of MGSE (applicable to any existing graph SSL teacher with up to 9.2% improvement) makes it a practical drop-in enhancement for the growing ecosystem of graph pre-training methods, particularly under out-of-distribution evaluation (scaffold split) where single-granularity representations degrade. The finding that multi-granularity benefits are most pronounced on multi-label tasks (Tox21, ToxCast, SIDER) suggests a principled reason: different labels may be governed by substructures at different scales. Open questions include whether the framework extends to domains beyond molecules and proteins (social networks, brain networks), whether the linear scaling in student count K can be further reduced via lighter architectures, and whether the N-vs-K extension (multiple teachers) can build toward graph foundation models.
