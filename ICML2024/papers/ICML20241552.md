## Problem

Existing LLM benchmarks rely on summary statistics like Mean Win Rate that ignore tail behavior and failure modes, and lack principled statistical significance testing for model comparisons. The paper addresses three gaps: (1) no interpretable aggregate for multi-metric evaluation, (2) no risk-aware ranking that accounts for left-tail events (e.g., toxic outputs), and (3) underutilized statistical significance testing in foundation model evaluation.

## Input / Output

The method takes N real-valued metrics computed on LLM outputs over a test set (e.g., 8 metrics on 5K Mix-Instruct samples, or 6 Perspective API toxicity scores on ~200K completions per model). All metrics are CDF-normalized by pooling evaluations across all models being compared, then aggregated into a single portfolio score per sample via a weighted geometric mean of CDFs (independent copula) or an empirical copula. The output is a ranked ordering of models with a confidence level (1−α), produced by pairwise stochastic dominance tests aggregated via Borda count, alongside the full portfolio distribution (quantiles, TVAR curves) for each model.

## Method

The core contribution is a distributional framework that replaces point-estimate ranking with stochastic dominance testing on a metrics portfolio. Each model's portfolio is constructed as a copula-based aggregation (independent copula: weighted geometric mean of CDFs; empirical copula: average win rate on CDF-transformed scores), yielding a single random variable per model whose distribution encodes all metric information. Models are then compared via First-Order Stochastic Dominance (quantile comparison) and, more importantly, Second-Order Stochastic Dominance (integrated quantile / TVAR comparison), which the paper proves is equivalent to preference by all risk-averse agents and consistent with mean-risk models (TVAR, Gini tail) from mathematical finance. To handle finite samples, the paper introduces Relative Stochastic Dominance (R-FSD, R-SSD) as a threshold-free pairwise test based on one-versus-all violation ratios, derives central limit theorems for the test statistics under regularity conditions on the CDFs, and estimates variance via bootstrap. Pairwise test outcomes are aggregated into a global ranking using Borda count with Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons.

## Results

On the Mix-Instruct dataset (5K samples, 12 models, 8 metrics), R-SSD with Independent Copula portfolio (P(IC)) achieves a Kendall-Tau similarity of 0.7–0.75 with the ChatGPT-based R-SSD ranking, while Mean Win Rate (MWR) is inconsistent with ChatGPT rankings. On the RealToxicityPrompts toxicity benchmark (5 models, 6 Perspective API metrics, ~200K completions per model), R-SSD @P(IC) and rank aggregation of per-metric R-SSD produce identical rankings (e.g., MosaicML MPT 30b ranks 1st, Llama 2 70b ranks 5th for generation-only evaluation). Computationally, the proposed framework completes all pairwise stochastic dominance tests in 17.7 s (1000 bootstraps) versus 15 min 50 s for the Deep-Significance ε-FSD implementation (3 bootstraps), a ~50× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Mean Win Rate (MWR) baseline from HELM (Liang et al., 2022), the paper's R-SSD method clearly outperforms: on Mix-Instruct, Flan-T5 ranks near last under R-SSD but ranks 6th under MWR, demonstrating that MWR overestimates risky models by ignoring left-tail behavior. R-SSD @P(IC) and @P(EC) both achieve Kendall-Tau similarity of 0.7–0.75 with the ChatGPT reference ranking, while MWR shows no such consistency regardless of aggregation technique. Against the Deep-Significance ε-FSD (ASO) framework (Ulmer et al., 2022), the proposed method extends from first-order to second-order stochastic dominance (capturing tail risk via TVAR/integrated quantiles) and is approximately 50× faster (17.7 s vs. 15 min 50 s) due to vectorized caching and multi-threading. Against the ChatGPT evaluation proxy from Jiang et al. (2023), the metrics portfolio with dependent Empirical Copula (EC) captures the human-evaluation ranking slightly better than the Independent Copula (IC), hinting that metric dependencies matter, though EC is ~100× more expensive to compute (1.5 h vs. 0.87 s on 5K samples). Overall, R-SSD agrees with the rank aggregation of all SSD-consistent mean-risk models (Kendall-Tau ≈ 0.85), confirming the theoretical link between second-order dominance and risk-averse decision making.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the almost stochastic dominance (ASO) framework of Ulmer et al. (2022), which applied first-order ε-FSD to neural network comparison, by introducing second-order stochastic dominance (SSD) tests and a threshold-free relative stochastic dominance formulation inspired by relative similarity tests (Bounliphone et al., 2016). The theoretical backbone draws on the mean-risk model literature in mathematical finance (Ogryczak & Ruszczynski, 2002), linking SSD to Tail Value at Risk and risk-averse preference. For multi-metric aggregation, the paper adapts copula-based portfolio construction from software metrics (Ulan et al., 2021) and synthetic data auditing (Belgodere et al., 2023), applying it to LLM evaluation for the first time in a stochastic-dominance setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a well-documented gap: nearly 50% of NLP papers lack statistical significance testing, and LLM leaderboards rely on Mean Win Rate, which the paper shows ignores tail risk and can misrank models (e.g., Flan-T5 appears mid-pack under MWR but near-last under SSD). Open questions include the curse-of-dimensionality limitation of empirical copula aggregation, the lack of a bootstrap consistency proof for quantile-based (as opposed to CDF-based) statistics, and whether the framework scales to the hundreds of metrics and models that future leaderboards may require. The practical impact hinges on whether the LLM evaluation community adopts distributional, risk-aware ranking over the simpler MWR paradigm.
