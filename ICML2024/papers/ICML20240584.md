## Problem

Existing neural network architectures for learning tangent vector fields on surfaces treat vector channels as independent scalars, failing to preserve fundamental intrinsic properties such as invariance to the arbitrary choice of local tangent bases, rigid motion, and isometric deformation. This limits generalization to unseen triangulations and shapes, as the network cannot disentangle vector length from orientation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240584/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a triangle mesh with per-vertex tangent vector features (15 channels of Heat Kernel Signature gradients, each expressed as a complex number in an arbitrary tangent basis) and outputs a per-vertex 4-Rosy cross field. The output vectors are parallel-transported from vertex tangent planes to face tangent planes before being fed to an off-the-shelf quad mesh extraction algorithm (Bommes et al.) to produce the final quadrilateral mesh. The architecture imposes no restriction on mesh genus and scales to meshes with over 60k vertices.

## Method

The Vector Heat Network maintains complex-valued (vector-valued) features throughout all layers, using two core modules stacked in blocks: (1) a trainable vector heat diffusion layer that solves the vector heat equation via implicit Euler integration with a deterministic connection Laplacian derived from parallel transport, accelerated spectrally using 128 eigenvectors with learned time-step sizes; and (2) a vector MLP with per-vertex linear layers and magnitude-based ReLU non-linearity. Because the connection Laplacian encodes parallel transport and is intrinsic to the surface geometry, the architecture is provably invariant to rigid transformations, isometric deformations, and arbitrary choices of tangent bases without requiring data augmentation. Training uses a combined magnitude-and-direction MSE loss on the 4th power of the output field (to respect 4-fold rotational symmetry), with dropout and L2 regularization, on a custom dataset of 1100 artist-created head meshes with SMPL topology.

## Results

The paper's primary quantitative result is an input-feature ablation (Table 1) on the quadrilateral remeshing task, where the gradient of Heat Kernel Signature (∇HKS) achieves the best overall performance with a direction loss of 0.106±0.278 and magnitude loss of 0.077±0.148, outperforming ∇GC (0.139/0.096), ∇MC (0.105/0.077), and PCD (0.128/0.090). The headline contribution, however, is qualitative: the Vector Heat Network is empirically shown to be invariant to rigid motion, isometric deformation, and arbitrary choice of tangent bases, while the scalar-valued baseline (Dielen et al., 2021) fails on all three. The architecture uses 6 vector diffusion blocks with 256 hidden channels and k=128 spectral modes, trained for 3,000 epochs on a single GPU in ~20 hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is the re-implementation of Dielen et al. (2021), a scalar-valued SpiralNet++-based architecture that outputs multiple scalar channels naively interpreted as vector components. The comparison is entirely qualitative (Figures 5 and 6): under rigid rotation of the input mesh, the proposed method produces an identical tangent vector field (zero error), while Dielen et al. outputs a visibly different field with high error. Similarly, when the choice of local tangent bases is changed, the proposed method remains invariant (matching ground truth), whereas Dielen et al. produces a rotated, incorrect output. No numerical error metrics (e.g., mean angular error) are reported for this head-to-head comparison; the evidence is visual. The paper also notes that Dielen et al. is sensitive to triangulation quality, whereas the proposed spectral connection-Laplacian approach generalizes across different discretizations of the same surface (Figure 8).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes the vector heat equation with the connection Laplacian (Sharp et al., 2019), the learned spectral diffusion framework of DiffusionNet (Sharp et al., 2022), and the vector-valued neuron paradigm (Deng et al., 2021) into a unified architecture for tangent vector field processing on triangle meshes. The key conceptual departure from prior work (notably Dielen et al., 2021) is maintaining complex-valued vector features throughout the network rather than decomposing vectors into independent scalar channels, which is what enables invariance to tangent basis choice, rigid motion, and isometric deformation. Unlike Sheaf Neural Networks that learn a graph-specific connection Laplacian, this work uses a deterministic Laplacian derived from differential geometry, trading expressiveness for cross-mesh generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The architecture provides a principled, invariant building block for any task requiring tangent vector field prediction on surfaces, with the invariance properties (rigid motion, isometry, basis choice, discretization) being essential for generalization to unseen shapes and triangulations. The quad meshing application demonstrates practical utility, but the dataset is relatively small (1,100 training meshes) and limited to character heads and animals, leaving open whether the approach scales to arbitrary genus or highly non-rigid shapes. Future directions include extending to point clouds and graphs, exploring quaternion-based generalizations, and building larger, more diverse training datasets for a generic learning-based remesher.
