## Problem

Conventional RLHF for LLM alignment relies on dueling bandit feedback, where a user must actively label a preference between two model-generated responses. In many practical writing-assistance settings, users instead provide implicit feedback by editing the model's output, yielding only a weak pairwise preference (edited ≻ proposed) without gold-standard labels. This paper asks whether LLMs can be trained effectively from such implicit, per-user edit feedback, and provides the first theoretical and algorithmic framework for doing so.

## Input / Output

At each training step, the method receives a text prompt x and the LLM's proposed response y, then observes a user-edited response ȳ that is an improvement over y but not necessarily optimal. The only signal extracted is the implicit preference ȳ ≻ y; no cardinal reward values are ever observed. The output is an updated LLM policy π that generates higher-reward responses for future prompts. In experiments, prompts are truncated to 462 tokens (summarization) or 300 tokens (helpfulness), and responses to 50–100 tokens, with the policy implemented as LoRA adapters on Llama 2 (7B/13B) or GPT-2 (124M).

## Method

CoRLL (Coactive RL for LLMs) is built on three design choices grounded in a new regret bound (Theorem 3.1) that generalizes Shivaswamy & Joachims (2015) from linear to non-linear models. First, the Bradley-Terry model links the observed pairwise preference to the log-ratio of policy probabilities, making the preference label directly compatible with a softmax policy. Second, DPO serves as the pairwise preference learner A_pair, regularizing against a reference policy π₀ to prevent degeneration. Third, the intractable argmax over the exponential response space is approximated by sampling k responses from the current policy and selecting the one with highest implicit reward R_π. The key distinction from dueling bandits is that the user's edit ȳ provides guided exploration: the improved response is generated by the user rather than randomly sampled, yielding more informative preferences and tighter regret bounds that do not depend on the number of actions.

## Results

On the Reddit TL;DR Summarization task (7B model), CoRLL with minimally-informative coactive feedback achieves a GPT-4 winrate of 0.6735 and similarity score of 0.8530, compared to 0.5595 and 0.7505 for noise-free dueling DPO. On the Helpful and Harmless Assistant task (13B model), CoRLL achieves 0.5875 winrate and 0.9240 similarity versus 0.5440 and 0.8280 for dueling. CoRLL remains effective even with up to ~40% mislabeled preferences, and even editing only the last 5 tokens of a response provides sufficient signal for learning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is standard DPO trained with dueling bandit feedback (two responses sampled from the current policy, with the expert reward used to determine the preference label). CoRLL with coactive feedback consistently outperforms noise-free dueling DPO on both 7B+ tasks: on TL;DR Summarization, CoRLL MinInf achieves a 11.4-point higher winrate (0.6735 vs. 0.5595) and 10.3-point higher similarity (0.8530 vs. 0.7505); on Helpful Assistant, the gaps are 4.4 points in winrate (0.5875 vs. 0.5440) and 9.6 points in similarity (0.9240 vs. 0.8280). Notably, the coactive advantage persists even when the coactive feedback is noisy (up to ~40% mislabeled), whereas the dueling baseline is noise-free by construction. The paper also shows that CoRLL achieves a better reward-vs-KL-divergence tradeoff than dueling, meaning it improves quality while staying closer to the reference policy. In the IMDB ablation (124M model), CoRLL is competitive with dueling DPO even at the lowest feedback quality (α≈0.2), though the gap narrows at higher α values.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends coactive learning (Shivaswamy & Joachims, 2012, 2015), originally developed for linear models, to the non-linear LLM setting by deriving a general regret bound (Theorem 3.1) that reduces coactive learning to a pairwise preference classification problem. It instantiates the pairwise preference learner A_pair with Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), connecting the Bradley-Terry preference model to the softmax policy structure of LLMs. The key departure from standard RLHF is the feedback mechanism: rather than dueling bandits where the model generates both candidates and a labeler picks, coactive learning uses the user's implicit edit as the preferred response, requiring only that the edit is an improvement over the model's output rather than a gold-standard answer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoRLL provides a practical pathway for LLM personalization that harvests feedback already generated during normal user interaction (edits, corrections) without additional labeling effort, and demonstrates robustness to up to ~40% mislabeled preferences. However, all experiments use simulated coactive feedback from an expert policy rather than real human edits, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to the messier, less consistent feedback real users provide. Open directions include better argmax approximations, combining coactive and dueling feedback, and integrating frameworks like Constitutional AI where an LLM itself provides the 'edit.' The paper also raises important privacy and governance questions about passively collecting user edits for personalization.
