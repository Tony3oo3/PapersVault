## Problem

Reconstructing the dynamic 3D shape and underlying physical skeleton of articulated objects from a single monocular video remains challenging because existing methods either require predefined shape/skeleton templates, multiple videos from diverse viewpoints, accurate camera poses, or 8–30 hours of A100 GPU training. No prior approach simultaneously learns both the visible mesh and the invisible skeletal structure without any of these prerequisites, limiting generalizability to out-of-distribution objects.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single monocular video (~100 frames) along with automatically generated segmentation masks (via Grounding DINO + SAM) and optical flow (via RAFT); no camera poses, 3D annotations, or skeleton templates are required. It outputs a canonical 3D mesh, a physical skeleton represented as Gaussian ellipsoids (centers, precision matrices, lengths) connected by joints, per-vertex skinning weights, per-frame SE(3) bone transformations, and per-frame camera parameters. The introduced PlanetZoo dataset specifically stresses the method with 180-degree visual fields and significant long-range camera motion.

## Method

S3O adopts a dual-phase strategy that decouples learning to reduce interdependencies. In the Coarse Shape Phase, a canonical frame is selected by its horizontal-to-vertical skeleton distance ratio, its 2D medial-axis skeleton is lifted to 3D using a symmetry-plane heuristic, and a coarse rest mesh is learned via BACON-style neural surface deformation supervised by silhouette, part-silhouette, and DINO feature losses. In the Motion & Fine Shape Phase, an EM-like loop alternates between an E-step (updating shape, camera, and bone SE(3) transformations with the skeleton fixed) and an M-step (adapting the skeleton to the deformed mesh via bone shifting and skeleton growing, then applying physical constraints that merge bones with synchronized motion and create joints where bone lengths vary significantly). A key mechanism is the Dynamic Rigidity loss, which computes an entropy-based rigidity coefficient from skinning weights to allow greater deformation freedom near joints while preserving rigidity along bones, replacing the overly restrictive ARAP formulation. Bone motion is estimated by warping 2D optical flow onto the mesh via bilinear interpolation and blending with skinning weights, gated by a ray-cast visibility mask to exclude occluded vertices.

## Results

On DAVIS, S3O achieves 80.4% average 2D keypoint transfer accuracy (PCK-T), outperforming LASR (70.8%) and ViSER (72.3%), while requiring only 3.8 GPU hours versus 9.1 and 10.4 hours respectively. On the newly introduced PlanetZoo dataset (which features significant camera motion over 180°), S3O reaches 66.2% average accuracy vs. LASR (62.8%) and ViSER (60.3%), with 4.2 GPU hours of training. On AMA-Human, S3O achieves a Chamfer Distance of 12.9, F-score of 55.8, and mIoU of 62.5, surpassing BANMo (14.6, 53.9, 61.8) while using only 4.9 GPU hours compared to BANMo's 12.6 hours (~61% reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

S3O consistently outperforms both LASR and ViSER across all evaluated animals on both DAVIS and PlanetZoo, with the largest margin on PlanetZoo (66.2% vs. 62.8% and 60.3%) where camera motion is more challenging. Training time is reduced by approximately 58–63% relative to LASR and ViSER (3.8–4.2 GPU hours vs. 9.1–12.1). On AMA-Human, S3O beats BANMo on all three 3D metrics (Chamfer Distance 12.9 vs. 14.6, F-score 55.8 vs. 53.9, mIoU 62.5 vs. 61.8) while requiring only 4.9 GPU hours versus BANMo's 12.6. For skeleton reconstruction, S3O generalizes better to out-of-distribution objects (tiger, elephant) than RigNet, which relies on 3D supervision, and produces more physically plausible bone distributions than Skeletor, which over-allocates bones to low-motion regions like the torso. Qualitatively, S3O also surpasses MagicPony and Hi-LASSIE in shape fidelity, particularly from non-side views where NeRF-based methods (BANMo, MagicPony) produce severely distorted top-view geometry due to camera pose errors.

## Relation to Prior Work

S3O builds on the Neural Mesh Rendering (NMR) lineage (LASR, ViSER, BAN Mo) but departs from their use of virtual bones (k-means clustering on mesh vertices) by learning a physical skeleton with Gaussian-ellipsoid bones and explicit joints. The dual-phase architecture—coarse parametric model learning followed by EM-like alternating optimization of shape and skeleton—is the core methodological novelty, drawing on BACON for neural surface deformation and Dynamic Rigidity (from LIMR) for joint-aware deformation constraints. The paper also introduces the PlanetZoo dataset to fill a gap in benchmarks that test reconstruction under significant camera motion, complementing DAVIS and AMA-Human.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for shape/skeleton templates, camera poses, and key points, S3O makes articulated reconstruction applicable to truly open-world single-video scenarios where prior methods (BAN Mo, MagicPony, RAC) fail on out-of-distribution objects. The 60% training-time reduction and lower run-to-run variance (std) compared to LASR/ViSER have direct practical value. However, the method still requires ~4 GPU-hours per object on A100, and the PlanetZoo dataset is synthetic with only ~100 frames, leaving open whether the approach scales to longer, noisier real-world sequences.
