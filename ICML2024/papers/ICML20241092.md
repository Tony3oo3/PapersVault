## Problem

In many Riemannian optimization problems, computing exact Riemannian gradients and retractions is computationally infeasible, yet the convergence and complexity of inexact Riemannian gradient descent in the general nonconvex, nonsmooth, and constrained setting remained unaddressed. Prior analyses were restricted to smooth objectives, compact manifolds, or the exponential map as retraction, leaving a gap for practical inexact algorithms on general manifolds with additional constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241092/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes an initial point on a product of Riemannian manifolds, a composite objective (smooth part plus convex Lipschitz nonsmooth part), geodesically convex constraint sets within each manifold, and a fixed number of iterations. It produces a sequence of iterates on the manifold, with the guarantee that an ε-stationary point (measured via a projected Riemannian gradient norm) is reached within O(ε^{-2}) iterations. A key practical feature is that the tangential subproblem at each iteration need only be solved inexactly, provided the cumulative optimality gaps are summable.

## Method

The paper introduces tangential Block Majorization-Minimization (tBMM), which lifts the Riemannian objective to the tangent space via retraction (pullback) and applies classical MM on the tangent space for each block. For each block at each iteration, a tangential surrogate (a majorizing function satisfying strong convexity and restricted smoothness) is constructed, minimized over the lifted constraint set in the tangent space, and the resulting direction is retracted back onto the manifold. The descent lemma for constrained tBMM bounds the per-iteration objective decrease in terms of the squared norm of the exact minimizer plus the inexactness gap, and summing over iterations yields the O(ε^{-2}) complexity. The framework unifies inexact RGD, block projected Riemannian gradient descent, and nonsmooth proximal gradient on Stiefel manifolds as special cases, with the constrained setting handled through the lifted constraint set T*θ which maps geodesically convex subsets to convex sets in the tangent space.

## Results

The main theoretical result is that tBMM converges to an ε-stationary point within O(ε^{-2}) iterations (Theorem 3.1), with the guarantee holding even under inexact subproblem solutions as long as the optimality gaps are summable. Numerically, tBMM using prox-linear surrogates consistently achieves lower relative reconstruction error than block projected gradient descent (block PGD) on nonnegative tensor decomposition (with and without nonnegativity constraints) and regularized nonnegative matrix factorization. On low-rank matrix recovery, tBMM outperforms NIHT at undersampling ratios ρ = 0.5 and 0.75, and is more robust across different matrix dimensions. The inexact RGD variant also demonstrates compelling convergence when the gradient noise Δ_n = c/(n+1)² is summable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares tBMM against two baselines across three problem classes. Against block projected gradient descent (block PGD), tBMM achieves lower relative reconstruction error on both nonnegative tensor decomposition with ℓ₁ regularization (with and without nonnegativity constraints on loading matrices) and regularized nonnegative matrix factorization, as shown in Figures 2 and 3. Against NIHT (Tanner & Wei, 2013) on low-rank matrix recovery (Figure 4), tBMM achieves lower relative reconstruction error at both undersampling ratios ρ = 0.5 and 0.75, and is more robust to varying matrix dimensions. The paper attributes tBMM's advantage over NIHT to two factors: (1) tBMM only requires an SVD of a 2r × 2r matrix per iteration versus a full m × n SVD for NIHT, and (2) NIHT's recovery guarantee breaks down when the oversampling ratio μ = r(m+n−r)/p > 1/2, which occurs in the ρ = 0.5 setting. No comparison is made against the Riemannian block MM methods of Li et al. (2023) or Peng & Vidal (2023), which are limited to smooth (ψ=0) objectives and cannot handle the nonsmooth problems tested here.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical Euclidean Majorization-Minimization framework (Mairal, 2013) to the Riemannian setting by introducing tangential surrogates defined on tangent spaces via retraction pullbacks, rather than majorizers defined directly on the manifold as in prior Riemannian BMM work (Li et al., 2023). It deliberately generalizes several restricted settings: prior Riemannian block MM analyses (Gutman & Ho-Nguyen, 2023; Peng & Vidal, 2023; Li et al., 2023) were limited to smooth objectives (ψ=0), while the nonsmooth proximal gradient method on Stiefel manifolds (Chen et al., 2020) was single-block. The tangential formulation also subsumes inexact RGD and block projected Riemannian gradient descent as special cases, providing a unified convergence and complexity theory that prior analyses could not deliver simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tBMM framework gives practitioners a single theoretical lens under which to analyze a wide family of first-order Riemannian algorithms, including inexact RGD and proximal gradient on Stiefel manifolds, with the standard O(ε^{-2}) complexity guarantee and robustness to summable inexactness in subproblem solves. A key limitation is that the analysis requires the lifted constraint set T*θ to be convex and the surrogates to satisfy strong convexity and restricted smoothness, which may not hold for all practical manifolds or constraint geometries. Open questions include whether the O(ε^{-2}) rate can be improved under stronger curvature assumptions, whether the framework extends to stochastic or distributed Riemannian settings, and how the tangential formulation compares in practice to manifold-based majorizers on high-curvature spaces.
