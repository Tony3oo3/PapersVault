## Problem

Implicit 3D shape generative models (e.g., DeepSDF) produce topological artifacts such as broken legs, unrealistic thin structures, and small holes when trained on limited data, because standard distribution alignment losses do not constrain topology. Existing geometric regularizations address surface quality but leave topological generalization unaddressed, and the PD stability bound between SDFs is not tight enough to guarantee smooth topological transitions by merely smoothing the SDF.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240309/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a collection of consistently oriented 3D shapes (from ShapeNet chair, table, and sofa categories) as training data and, at inference, a latent code z ~ N(0, I_d) to produce an implicit SDF surface. A parallel PD generator (PointNet-based) outputs a fixed-size 2D point cloud of n_k points per homology dimension k ∈ {0,1,2}, where n_k equals the maximum PD size across training shapes; a rectification layer R(x,y) = (x, x+RELU(y)) enforces the constraint x ≤ y, with points on the diagonal representing trivial features that model varying PD cardinality.

## Method

The core idea is to learn a separate PD generator (PointNet backbone + rectification layer) that produces smooth interpolations in PD space, then enforce that the implicit shape generator's synthetic PDs match the PD generator's outputs. Training proceeds in two stages: first, the shape generator (DeepSDF + VAD) and PD generator are pre-trained independently; then they are jointly optimized by alternating updates of θ (shape generator), φ (PD generator), and {z_i} (latent codes). The total loss combines DeepSDF data loss, PD matching between the PD generator and training shape PDs, PD consistency between the PD generator and synthetic shape PDs, an eikonal + normal-Laplacian regularization on the SDF, KL divergence on latent codes, and a PD smoothness loss that penalizes d_PD(PD^k,φ(z), PD^k,φ(z+εv)) over random perturbation directions. A theoretical analysis (Proposition 4.1 and Appendix B) shows that Laplacian smoothing is the optimal regularization for PD matching, justifying the use of the normal Laplacian from StEik in the shape regularization term.

## Results

On ShapeNet chair and table categories, Ours-DeepSDF-SE achieves PD-LAP scores of 0.77 and 0.88 respectively, outperforming DeepSDF-VAD (1.86/2.26), DeepSDF-SE-VAD (1.75/2.16), and 3DShape2VecSet (1.34/1.12) by 58–73% in topological generalization. On geometric accuracy (CD-mean), Ours-DeepSDF-SE scores 0.82 (chair) and 1.33 (table), matching or exceeding 3DShape2VecSet (0.91/1.37) while using a simpler DeepSDF backbone. On the sofa category, Ours-DeepSDF-SE achieves the best PD-LAP (0.94) and competitive CD-mean (1.12).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DeepSDF-VAD, Ours-DeepSDF-SE reduces PD-LAP by 58.6% (chair) and 60.9% (table) while also improving CD-mean by 31.1% and 31.4%, showing that topological regularization benefits both geometric and topological quality. Against DeepSDF-SE-VAD (which adds StEik geometric regularization), the PD-LAP improvements are 56.0% (chair) and 59.3% (table), confirming that topological regularization is orthogonal to geometric regularization. Against the stronger 3DShape2VecSet baseline (latent diffusion with a more advanced shape decoder), Ours-DeepSDF-SE achieves lower PD-LAP on all three categories (0.77 vs 1.34 chair, 0.88 vs 1.12 table, 0.94 vs 1.64 sofa) while matching or beating CD-mean on chair and table, demonstrating that simply improving the neural shape representation and distribution alignment paradigm is insufficient for topological generalization. The ablation 'No PD Smoothness' (PD-LAP 0.98/0.92) shows that the PD smoothness loss on the PD generator is critical, while 'No PD Training' (PD-LAP 1.43/1.64) shows that training the PD generator on data is essential to prevent it from degenerating into a weak regularizer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PD optimization framework from Poulenard et al. (2018) and Gabrielsson et al. (2020a), which optimized single shapes to match prescribed persistent diagrams, into the generative model setting by introducing learned PD generators as topological priors for implicit shape generators. It departs from prior topological regularization work (e.g., topology-preserving segmentation losses) by not requiring fixed topological targets but instead learning a continuous PD distribution from training data. The approach is explicitly complementary to geometric regularizations like StEik (Yang et al., 2023b), which the paper incorporates as a baseline component, and repurposes PointNet as a PD generator with a rectification layer to enforce the x ≤ y constraint on persistent diagram points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Topological artifacts (broken legs, spurious holes, thin structures) remain a persistent failure mode in implicit 3D shape generation, and this paper demonstrates that they cannot be resolved by geometric regularization alone—the Lipschitz-regularized SDF interpolation still produces topological defects, showing the bottleneck distance bound is not tight in practice. The 70%+ reduction in PD-LAP over strong baselines suggests topological regularization is an orthogonal axis of improvement to geometric feasibility. Key limitations include the lack of location information in standard PDs (limiting where artifacts are corrected), the computational cost of PD computation, and evaluation restricted to a few ShapeNet categories. Open questions include extending to dynamic shapes, using localized homology for spatially-aware topological constraints, and understanding the theoretical properties of the PD generator relative to the implicit shape generator.
