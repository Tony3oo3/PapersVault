## Problem

Generating long-form, variable-length stereo audio at commercial quality (44.1kHz) from text prompts remains challenging: autoregressive models handle variable length but are slow at inference, while existing diffusion models are trained on fixed-size chunks and cannot control output duration. No prior work combined fast inference with variable-length, long-form stereo music and sound effect generation at 44.1kHz, and standard evaluation metrics were designed only for short-form mono signals at 16kHz.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240505/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt (optionally including structured metadata like BPM, genre, moods, and instruments) plus two scalar timing values—seconds_start (offset into the original audio) and seconds_total (total duration)—which are converted into per-second discrete learned embeddings. Output is a 44.1kHz stereo waveform up to 95 seconds; the timing conditioning controls how much of the 95-second window is filled with content versus silence, enabling variable-length generation from a single fixed-window model. Inference completes in 8 seconds on a single A100 GPU (40GB VRAM).

## Method

Stable Audio is a latent diffusion model with three components: (1) a fully-convolutional VAE (133M params) derived from the Descript Audio Codec architecture (without quantization) that compresses 44.1kHz stereo audio by 1024× into 64-channel latents (32:1 overall compression) using Snake activations for better high-compression reconstruction; (2) a CLAP text encoder (HTSAT audio + RoBERTa text, 141M params) trained from scratch on the same 19,500-hour dataset via contrastive language-audio pretraining, with features taken from the next-to-last layer and injected via cross-attention; and (3) a 907M-parameter U-Net with 4 symmetric levels, FiLM layers for diffusion timestep conditioning, cross-attention for text and timing conditioning, and FlashAttention for memory-efficient long-sequence processing. The key novelty is the timing conditioning mechanism: seconds_start and seconds_total are mapped to per-second discrete learned embeddings that are concatenated with text features along the sequence dimension before cross-attention, allowing the model to generate content only up to the specified duration and fill the remainder with silence. Training uses a v-objective with cosine noise schedule, 10% dropout on conditioning signals for classifier-free guidance (scale 6), and DPM-Solver++ with 100 inference steps.

## Results

On MusicCaps, Stable Audio achieves the best FD_openl3 (108.69), KL_passt (0.80), and CLAP_score (0.46) among all compared models, while generating 95-second 44.1kHz stereo audio in just 8 seconds on a single A100 GPU—roughly 30× faster than MusicGen-large (242 s) and AudioLDM2-48kHz (242 s). On AudioCaps, Stable Audio attains the best FD_openl3 (103.66) but trails AudioGen-medium on KL_passt (2.89 vs. 1.42) and CLAP_score (0.24 vs. 0.45), likely due to the small proportion of sound effects in its training set. Qualitatively, Stable Audio scores highest in audio quality (3.0/4), text alignment (2.9/4), and musicality (2.7/4) on MusicCaps, and achieves 94.7% stereo correctness and 92.1% intro structure detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MusicGen-large (mono, 32 kHz), Stable Audio wins on every metric: FD_openl3 108.69 vs. 197.12, KL_passt 0.80 vs. 0.85, CLAP_score 0.46 vs. 0.36, and inference time 8 s vs. 242 s. Against MusicGen-large-stereo (the only other stereo baseline), Stable Audio again leads on all quantitative metrics (FD 108.69 vs. 216.07, KL 0.80 vs. 1.04, CLAP 0.46 vs. 0.32) and on stereo correctness (94.7% vs. 86.8%), while matching its musicality MOS (2.7 vs. 2.7). Against AudioLDM2-48kHz (mono, 48 kHz), the gap is even wider: Stable Audio improves FD by 2.7× (108.69 vs. 299.47), KL by 3.5× (0.80 vs. 2.77), and CLAP by 2.1× (0.46 vs. 0.22), while being 30× faster (8 s vs. 242 s). On AudioCaps, the picture is more nuanced: Stable Audio achieves the best FD_openl3 (103.66 vs. 186.53 for AudioGen-medium) but underperforms on KL_passt (2.89 vs. 1.42) and CLAP_score (0.24 vs. 0.45), which the authors attribute to the limited sound-effect content in their training data (25% of audios).

## Relation to Prior Work

Stable Audio extends the latent diffusion paradigm (Rombach et al., 2022; Moûsai; AudioLDM) to audio by combining a fully-convolutional VAE (inspired by Descript Audio Codec) with a U-Net diffusion model, departing from spectrogram-based encoders and diffusion decoders used in prior audio latent diffusion work. Its key conceptual departure is the introduction of timing conditioning (seconds_start, seconds_total) into a latent diffusion model—a technique previously seen only in Jukebox's autoregressive setting—enabling variable-length generation up to 95 seconds. The model also unifies music and sound effect synthesis in a single architecture, whereas prior works (AudioLDM2, MusicGen, AudioGen) typically target one domain, and it operates at 44.1 kHz stereo, a specification no prior open-source model achieves for both music and sounds simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8-second inference time for 95 seconds of 44.1 kHz stereo audio (≈12× realtime) makes high-fidelity, variable-length audio generation practically deployable, and the timing conditioning mechanism offers a simple, reliable way to control output duration without architectural changes. The proposed evaluation metrics (FD_openl3, KL_passt, CLAP_score adapted for long-form full-band stereo) fill a gap in the literature where standard metrics were designed for 10-second mono 16 kHz clips. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to even longer compositions (beyond the 95-second training window), how well the timing conditioning handles structurally complex multi-section pieces, and whether the modest text-alignment gap on sound effects (AudioCaps) can be closed with more diverse training data.
