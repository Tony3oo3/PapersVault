## Problem

Neural scaling laws imply that LLM performance requires ever-larger training datasets, yet no prior work had quantitatively modeled when the finite stock of public human-generated text would be exhausted relative to this growing demand. This position paper asks: if current LLM development trends continue, how soon will models be trained on datasets as large as the total available public human text, and what strategies could circumvent that bottleneck?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242024/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes historical dataset sizes from the Epoch DB (~80 LLM data points, 2010–2024), Common Crawl web page statistics (average plain-text bytes per page, growth rates), UN population projections and internet penetration S-curves, and probabilistic compute budget projections from Besiroglu et al. (2022). Outputs are log-normal distributions with 95% confidence intervals for both the effective data stock (median ~510T tokens for the indexed web, ~3100T for the whole web) and projected dataset sizes, with the key scalar result being the median exhaustion year of 2028 (range 2026–2032). The final dataset-size projection is an equally-weighted mixture of a historical-trend extrapolation and a compute-constrained projection, rather than a single curve.

## Method

The paper builds a two-sided probabilistic forecasting model. On the supply side, it estimates the indexed web stock via a pivot-word methodology (replicating van den Bosch et al., 2016) yielding ~250B pages × ~7000 bytes/page × ~0.25 tokens/byte ≈ 510T tokens, then applies quality filtering (10–40% of deduplicated data is usable) and multi-epoch adjustments (up to 5× effective size per Muennighoff et al., 2023) to obtain the effective stock. A cross-check model scales 2024 text production (180–500T tokens) by projected internet-user growth (sigmoid penetration × UN population). On the demand side, it fits a log-linear growth rate of 0.38 OOM/year to historical dataset sizes and separately derives a compute-constrained ceiling using the Chinchilla scaling law (D = √(20/6 · C)) with Besiroglu et al.'s compute projections. The intersection of the effective stock curve and the mixed dataset-size projection yields the exhaustion year, with sensitivity analyses for overtraining (5×) and non-public data inclusion.

## Results

The paper forecasts that the stock of public human-generated text data will be fully utilized by a training run between 2026 and 2032, with a median exhaustion year of 2028. At that point, models will be trained on approximately 4×10¹⁴ tokens (corresponding to ~5×10²⁸ FLOP of training compute for compute-optimal models), or one year earlier (~6×10²⁷ FLOP) if frontier models are overtrained by 5×. The estimated effective stock of public human text is 510T tokens for the indexed web [95% CI: 130T–2100T] and 3100T tokens for the whole web [95% CI: 1900T–5200T], while historical dataset sizes have grown at 0.38 orders of magnitude per year (~2.4×/year).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a position/forecasting paper rather than a method-comparison study, so there are no traditional baselines. The paper's central comparison is between two independent estimation approaches for the data stock: the indexed-web model (510T tokens, based on Google's index size × average page text) and the internet-population model (3100T tokens, based on projected internet users × per-user data generation). The indexed-web estimate is narrower and more conservative, while the whole-web estimate serves as an upper bound that includes the deep web. For projecting dataset size growth, the paper compares a naive historical extrapolation (0.38 OOM/y, which would exceed 1 quadrillion tokens by 2030) against a compute-constrained projection derived from scaling laws and energy limits (which decelerates after ~2030); the final forecast is an equal-weighted mixture of both. The paper also contrasts compute-optimal scaling (Chinchilla ratio D/N ≈ 20) with overtrained scaling (up to 5×–100× overtraining), showing that overtraining pulls the data bottleneck forward by roughly one year.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes several established threads—Chinchilla scaling laws (Hoffmann et al., 2022), compute trend projections (Besiroglu et al., 2022), web index size estimation (van den Bosch et al., 2016), and data-constrained scaling analysis (Muennighoff et al., 2023)—into a unified probabilistic forecasting model. While prior work raised qualitative concerns about data scarcity (Nostalgebraist, 2022; Amodei's 10% stagnation estimate) and Muennighoff et al. studied the effects of data constraints on training, no prior work had quantitatively projected *when* the constraint would bind by jointly modeling data stock growth and dataset size growth. The paper's contribution is thus the integration and the specific forecasting framework rather than any single new component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the field with a concrete, if uncertain, timeline (median 2028, 95% CI 2026–2032) for when public human text data becomes a binding constraint on LLM scaling, reframing data availability from a distant theoretical concern to a near-term strategic challenge. It also systematically surveys potential circumvention strategies—synthetic data, multimodal transfer learning, non-public data, and data efficiency—while honestly noting that the evidence for each is still mixed. Key open questions include whether synthetic data can sustain open-ended progress without model collapse, whether data efficiency gains (currently ~0.4 OOM/y) will outpace data exhaustion, and how to quantify the effective information content of non-text modalities for language modeling.
