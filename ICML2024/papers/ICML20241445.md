## Problem

Transformer-based LLMs in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) face a dual challenge: inference cost scales quadratically with context length, making long-document RAG prohibitively expensive, and irrelevant retrieved documents degrade output quality through the 'distraction phenomenon.' Existing efficiency solutions (sparse attention, architectural modifications) typically require retraining or fine-tuning, while prior prompt-level methods (e.g., Prompt Cache) do not simultaneously address both the compute overhead and the accuracy loss from noisy context.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three text segments—a system preamble, a set of n_d retrieved documents, and a user query—but rather than concatenating them into a single token sequence, it organizes them into a ForkJoin directed acyclic graph where each document-query pair forms an independent attention path. The preamble and document KV caches are precomputed offline and stored, so at serving time only the query tokens and a postamble are fed through the LLM. The output is a standard autoregressively generated text response, produced after pruning irrelevant paths and concatenating the surviving KV caches.

## Method

Superposition prompting represents the RAG prompt as a DAG of token sequences with explicit attention dependencies, generalizing the classical linked-list prompt to a ForkJoin topology where all documents attend to the preamble and all query copies attend to their respective document. Token positions across parallel paths are assigned via a path equilibrium scheme that linearly spaces overlapping paths to the harmonic mean of their lengths, avoiding the discontinuities of left-alignment that unfairly penalize shorter documents under ALiBi. A Bayesian saliency score (derived from the LM head's cross-entropy via Bayes' rule) ranks each document-query path, and the top-k paths are retained while the rest are pruned by discarding their KV caches. Two lossless runtime optimizations exploit the DAG structure: path caching precomputes preamble and document KV embeddings offline, and path parallelism batches the independent query computations across the batch dimension or distributed GPUs. The entire pipeline is zero-shot—no fine-tuning or architectural modification is required—and achieves up to 93× theoretical speedup with 43% accuracy improvement over naive RAG on NaturalQuestions-Open.

## Results

On NaturalQuestions-Open, superposition prompting achieves the highest accuracy across all four model families while delivering 93–98× theoretical speedup over Naive LLM-RAG. The headline result is on MPT-7B-Instruct, where superposition prompting reaches 0.465 Best EM Subspan accuracy (a 43% improvement over Naive LLM-RAG's 0.026) at 93.7× lower compute cost. On the multi-hop MuSiQue dataset, superposition prompting also achieves the best F1 and EM for every model, with the strongest configuration (t=4, k=1) reaching 0.120 F1 and 0.040 EM on MPT-7B-Instruct versus 0.064 F1 and 0.008 EM for Naive LLM-RAG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Superposition prompting dominates all five baselines on both accuracy and speedup across every model on NaturalQuestions-Open. Against the retrieval-based baselines (BM-25, TF-IDF, Contriever), superposition prompting achieves 13–43% higher accuracy while matching or exceeding their speedup (e.g., 0.465 vs. 0.338 for Contriever on MPT-7B-Instruct). Against Attention Sort, superposition prompting is dramatically superior in both dimensions: Attention Sort actually slows inference (0.3× speedup) and yields near-zero accuracy on OpenELM-3B-Instruct, whereas superposition prompting achieves 93–98× speedup and the best accuracy. Against Prompt Cache—the closest prior method—superposition prompting matches or slightly exceeds the speedup (93.7× vs. 91.8× on MPT-7B-Instruct) while substantially improving accuracy (0.465 vs. 0.278), demonstrating that the path pruning and DAG structure provide meaningful gains over simple modular KV caching alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RAG prompting paradigm by replacing the linear concatenation of documents with a ForkJoin DAG topology where each document-query pair is processed as an independent path. The closest prior work is Prompt Cache (Gim et al., 2023), which also exploits RAG's modular structure for local attention and KV caching, but superposition prompting retains cross-segment attention dependencies via the DAG and adds path pruning and parallelization mechanisms not present there. The approach also generalizes structured-attention methods (Parallel Context Windows, FID-ICL) from tree topologies to arbitrary DAGs, and differs from architecture-level long-context solutions (Longformer, Reformer) by requiring no retraining or fine-tuning of pre-trained LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The simultaneous ~93× theoretical speedup and 43% accuracy improvement (on NQ-Open with MPT-7B) is practically significant for production RAG deployments, where both latency and answer quality are critical. The finding that pruning irrelevant documents via Bayesian saliency improves accuracy—rather than merely saving compute—validates the 'distraction phenomenon' and suggests that structured filtering is a viable alternative to simply feeding all retrieved context. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond RAG to other long-context tasks, whether fine-tuning could further close the gap between theoretical and measured CUDA speedups (currently ~6–8× vs. 93×), and how the method behaves with much larger document sets or longer individual documents.
