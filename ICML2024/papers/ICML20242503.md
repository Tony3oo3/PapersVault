## Problem

Spectral features (eigenvalues, eigenvectors, projection matrices, spectral distances) have been widely adopted to enhance GNNs and Graph Transformers, but the eigenvector ambiguity problem necessitates spectral invariant architectures whose expressive power remained largely uncharacterized. No prior work established how much expressiveness these invariant spectral features actually provide, how different spectral invariant designs relate to each other, or where they sit relative to other expressive GNN families such as Subgraph GNNs and higher-order WL-based models.

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected graph and a choice of symmetric graph matrix (adjacency A, Laplacian L, or normalized Laplacian L̂), from which it computes the eigenspace projection invariant P^M(u,v) = {(λ₁, P₁(u,v)), …, (λₘ, Pₘ(u,v))} for every ordered node pair. These projection-value sets serve as edge features on a fully connected graph, and the output is a graph-level representation obtained by global pooling over node representations after K message-passing layers. The framework also subsumes distance-based variants where a scalar distance d(u,v) replaces the full projection set as the edge feature.

## Method

The paper introduces Eigenspace Projection GNN (EPNN), which encodes all spectral information between a node pair (u,v) as the set of (eigenvalue, projection-matrix-element) tuples and performs standard message-passing aggregation over a fully connected graph using these as edge features. A companion color-refinement algorithm EPWL formalizes the expressiveness, and the key theoretical result (Theorem 4.3) proves EPWL is strictly bounded by PSWL, a Subgraph GNN algorithm, thereby placing all spectral invariant architectures strictly below 3-WL. The paper further shows that all distance-based spectral GNNs (SPD, resistance, PageRank, commute-time, diffusion, biharmonic) are bounded by EPWL with L̂, and introduces Spectral IGN via the DSS equivariant framework, proving it is as expressive as EPNN while establishing strict expressivity gaps between Siamese IGN, Weak Spectral IGN, and Spectral IGN. Finally, the paper shows that projection matrices provide no additional expressiveness when combined with k-IGN or k-FGNN for k≥2, and proposes higher-order spectral features via token graphs as a promising open direction.

## Results

On the BREC benchmark, EPNN achieves a total score of 53.8, matching PPGN (3-WL) on Basic (100.0), Reg (35.7), and Ext (100.0) but trailing on CFI (5.0 vs. 23.0). EPNN substantially outperforms Graphormer (SPD-WL, total 19.8) and NGNN (SWL, total 41.5), while falling slightly short of ESAN (GSWL, total 55.2) and PPGN (3-WL, total 58.2). These empirical results align with the paper's theoretical expressiveness hierarchy, confirming that EPWL sits strictly between SWL and PSWL/3-WL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EPNN clearly outperforms Graphormer (SPD-WL) by a wide margin (53.8 vs. 19.8 total), validating the theoretical result that distance-based GNNs are strictly less expressive than EPWL. EPNN also beats NGNN (SWL) by 12.3 points in total score, consistent with the paper's finding that EPWL is incomparable to but generally stronger than vanilla SWL. Against ESAN (GSWL), EPNN trails by 1.4 points overall, primarily due to a CFI gap (5.0 vs. 15.0), which aligns with the theoretical ordering where ESAN is more expressive than EPWL. Against PPGN (3-WL), EPNN matches on three of four sub-tasks but loses 18 points on CFI (5.0 vs. 23.0), reflecting the strict upper bound of EPWL by 3-WL established in Corollary 4.5. Overall, the empirical ordering Graphormer < NGNN < EPNN < ESAN < PPGN mirrors the theoretical expressiveness hierarchy exactly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the fragmented landscape of spectral invariant GNNs (BasisNet, SPE, distance-based models like Graphormer/GraphiT/GRIT/PEG) under a single framework called EPNN, which treats eigenspace projection matrices as edge features in a message-passing scheme. It builds on the DSS equivariant framework (Maron et al., 2020) and 2-IGN (Maron et al., 2019b) to derive a theoretically-motivated architecture (Spectral IGN), and critically relies on the graph-theoretic result of Rattan & Seppelt (2023) connecting WL colors to projection matrices. The paper departs from prior work by establishing the first complete expressiveness hierarchy across all spectral invariant architectures and, surprisingly, by proving a tight upper bound via PSWL from the subgraph GNN literature — bridging two previously disconnected research directions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a definitive answer to how expressive spectral invariant GNNs can be: all are strictly bounded by 3-WL, and the message-passing aggregation (not just the raw spectral features) is what drives expressiveness. The negative result that spectral projection features add no expressiveness when combined with already-powerful GNNs like PPGN or k-IGN is practically important, discouraging a common design choice. Open questions include whether the choice of graph matrix (Laplacian vs. adjacency) affects expressiveness, and whether higher-order spectral features via token graphs can break the 3-WL barrier — a direction the authors identify but leave unexplored.
