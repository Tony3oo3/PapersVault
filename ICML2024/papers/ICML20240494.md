## Problem

Transformer-based models for time series suffer from noise sensitivity, high computational cost, and overfitting on smaller datasets, while CNNs capture local patterns well but struggle with long-range dependencies. The paper asks how to build a single lightweight convolutional architecture that robustly handles both short-term and long-term temporal interactions across diverse time series tasks (classification, forecasting, anomaly detection) without the overhead of self-attention.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240494/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series S ∈ R^{C×L} (C channels, length L), splits it into M patches of size p, projects each patch to dimension p′, and adds learnable positional embeddings to preserve temporal ordering. The final output is task-specific via a customizable linear head: a class label for classification, predicted future values over a look-ahead window for forecasting, or a reconstruction-error-based anomaly score for detection. The stride is set to half the patch size to ensure overlapping windows.

## Method

TSLANet replaces Transformer self-attention with two stacked blocks per layer. The Adaptive Spectral Block (ASB) applies 1D FFT to the patch embeddings, computes a power spectrum, and uses a learnable threshold θ to create a binary mask that adaptively removes high-frequency noise; it then multiplies the original spectrum by a global filter and the filtered spectrum by a local filter (equivalent to circular convolutions with full-sequence receptive fields), sums them, and applies IFFT to return to the time domain. The Interactive Convolution Block (ICB) runs two parallel 1D convolutions with different kernel sizes, cross-modulates their outputs via GELU-activated element-wise multiplication, and fuses them through a final convolution. Self-supervised pretraining uses a masked-patch autoencoder objective (MSE reconstruction) before supervised fine-tuning. The overall per-layer complexity is O(N log N) due to FFT/IFFT, yielding 93% fewer FLOPs and 84% fewer parameters than PatchTST while achieving higher accuracy.

## Results

On the 116-dataset classification benchmark (UCR, UEA, biomedical, HAR), TSLANet achieves 85.90% average accuracy, outperforming the next-best method ROCKET (83.46%) and all Transformer-based baselines (PatchTST 79.93%, TimesNet 77.61%, GPT4TS 74.96%). For multivariate forecasting, TSLANet attains the second-lowest MSE in 7 of 8 datasets (behind only Time-LLM, which relies on a 7B-parameter LLM), with 3.0% and 3.8% MSE improvements over PatchTST on ETT(avg) and Weather respectively. On anomaly detection, TSLANet achieves 87.54% average F1-score, surpassing GPT4TS (86.72%) and TimesNet (85.24%). In terms of efficiency, TSLANet requires 93% fewer FLOPs and 84% fewer parameters than PatchTST while delivering 8% higher accuracy on UEA Heartbeat.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSLANet outperforms all 23 baselines on the overall classification average (85.90%), with the closest competitor being ROCKET (83.46%) and TS-TCC (83.55%), both convolution-based methods. Among Transformer-based models, PatchTST (79.93%) and TimesNet (77.61%) are the strongest but still trail TSLANet by 6 and 8 points respectively; GPT4TS (74.96%) and Crossformer (72.17%) lag further behind. On forecasting, TSLANet is second only to Time-LLM (which uses a 7B Llama backbone) across most datasets, but achieves near-equivalent MSE (0.413 vs 0.408 on ETTh1) at 100x lower FLOPs (6.9e+10 vs 7.3e+12). TSLANet beats PatchTST by 3.0% MSE on ETT(avg) and 3.8% on Weather, and outperforms DLinear/RLinear on ETT and Weather while DLinear wins on Exchange (0.297 vs 0.369 MSE). For anomaly detection, TSLANet leads with 87.54% F1, narrowly ahead of GPT4TS (86.72%) and TimesNet (85.24%), and substantially ahead of vanilla Transformer (76.88%) and LogTransformer (76.60%). The single notable loss is on the Exchange forecasting dataset where DLinear achieves 0.297 MSE vs TSLANet's 0.369, and on the SWaT anomaly detection dataset where GPT4TS scores 94.23% F1 vs TSLANet's 92.80%.

## Relation to Prior Work

TSLANet carves new ground by returning to convolutional operations as the primary mechanism for time series modeling, explicitly departing from the Transformer-centric paradigm that dominated 2021–2023. It synthesizes ideas from several threads: patch-based embedding (à la PatchTST), frequency-domain filtering inspired by Global Filter Networks (Rao et al., 2021), and masked autoencoder pretraining (He et al., 2022), but recombines them into a unified lightweight architecture where FFT-based circular convolution replaces self-attention and an adaptive thresholding mechanism handles noise. The paper positions itself against both the Transformer camp (citing Zeng et al. 2023 and Li et al. 2023 on permutation-invariance issues) and the simpler linear/MLP camp, arguing that a well-designed convolutional model can capture both local and global dependencies without the parameter overhead of attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TSLANet offers a practically important efficiency story: O(N log N) complexity via FFT, 93% fewer FLOPs than PatchTST, and competitive or superior accuracy across classification, forecasting, and anomaly detection, making it attractive for resource-constrained or edge deployment scenarios. The adaptive spectral thresholding provides a principled, learnable noise-attenuation mechanism that is particularly relevant for real-world sensor and biomedical signals. However, the model still requires per-dataset self-supervised pretraining rather than truly universal pretraining, and its forecasting results, while strong, trail behind the much heavier Time-LLM. Open questions include whether large-scale cross-dataset pretraining can unlock the 'foundation model' ambition the authors articulate, and whether the adaptive thresholding generalizes to non-Gaussian or structured noise patterns.
