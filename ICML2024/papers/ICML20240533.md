## Problem

No single molecular pre-training model effectively handles all categories of downstream tasks: 3D denoising favors quantum properties, 2D graph masking favors biological properties, and 2D-3D contrastive learning favors physicochemical properties, yet the theoretical relationship among these methods and the reason for their task-specific preferences remained unexplored. This paper addresses both the theoretical gap (why each method suits certain tasks) and the practical gap (how to build a universal model).

## Input / Output

UniCorn consumes paired 2D molecular graphs (atom types and bond edges) and 3D conformations (Cartesian coordinates) simultaneously during pre-training on 15 million molecules. The learned 3D encoder representation is then fine-tuned with task-specific heads: a 2-layer MLP for MoleculeNet classification/regression tasks and an equivariant head (TorchMD-NET style) for quantum tasks (QM9, MD17, MD22). The 2D encoder (Graphormer, 12 layers, 512-dim) and 3D encoder (TorchMD-NET, 8 layers, 256-dim) operate in parallel, with the 3D encoder serving as the primary backbone for downstream prediction.

## Method

UniCorn unifies three self-supervised objectives into a single pre-training loss: (1) Masked Fragment Modeling using BRICS decomposition to mask and reconstruct chemically meaningful substructures on the 2D graph, (2) Torsion Augmented Denoising that first perturbs rotatable torsion angles to sample diverse low-energy conformations, then adds small Gaussian coordinate noise and predicts it on the 3D structure, and (3) Cross-modal Distillation that aligns the noisy 3D conformation embedding to the 2D graph embedding via a symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss, distilling 2D knowledge into the 3D encoder. The theoretical contribution proves that all three objectives are instances of contrastive learning that cluster molecular representations at different granularities—fragment-level (biological), conformational-ensemble-level (physicochemical), and near-equilibrium-level (quantum)—and that minimizing the contrastive loss guarantees a bounded clustering loss (Theorem 2.2). This multi-grained clustering is what enables a single representation to serve all three property categories simultaneously.

## Results

UniCorn achieves state-of-the-art performance on 33 out of 38 molecular tasks spanning quantum, physicochemical, and biological domains. On QM9, it attains the best MAE in 10 of 12 tasks (e.g., μ(D) = 0.0085, ε_HOMO = 13.0 meV, Δε = 24.9 meV), outperforming the best denoising baseline Frad (15.3 meV on ε_HOMO). On MoleculeNet biological classification, UniCorn reaches an average ROC-AUC of 78.4%, surpassing the second-best MoleBLEND (76.2%) by a 2.2-point margin, and achieves best performance on all 3 physicochemical regression tasks (ESOL RMSE 0.817, FreeSolv 1.555, Lipo 0.591).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniCorn consistently outperforms all 15 baselines across the three task categories. Against the strongest multimodal baseline MoleBLEND, UniCorn gains +2.2 points average ROC-AUC on biological tasks (78.4 vs 76.2), with particularly large margins on ToxCast (+3.3) and ClinTox (+4.5), and dominates all physicochemical regression tasks (e.g., FreeSolv RMSE 1.555 vs 1.910). Against the best 3D denoising baseline Frad, UniCorn improves QM9 ε_HOMO from 15.3 to 13.0 meV and wins 6 of 8 MD17 force prediction molecules (e.g., Aspirin 0.168 vs 0.209 kcal/mol/Å). Against masking baselines, UniCorn surpasses the best (Mole-BERT, avg 74.0) by 4.4 points on biological tasks, confirming that fragment-level masking plus 3D information is superior to 2D-only masking. On MD22, UniCorn achieves the best force prediction MAE on all 7 molecules, with the largest gap on Buckyball (0.249 vs 0.430 for Frad). The only task where UniCorn does not achieve the absolute best is SIDER (64.0 vs MoleBLEND's 64.9), a narrow 0.9-point difference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate molecular pre-training paradigms—2D graph masking, 2D-3D contrastive learning, and 3D denoising—under a single contrastive learning framework, showing they all cluster molecular representations at different granularities. The theoretical foundation draws on SimSiam (Chen & He, 2021) to derive a formal clustering interpretation (Theorem 2.2) and establishes mutual upper/lower bounds between reconstructive and contrastive losses (Theorem 2.1). Architecturally, it combines the torsion-augmented denoising strategy from Frad (Feng et al., 2023) and the coordinate denoising from Coord (Zaidi et al., 2022) with BRICS-based fragment masking and a cross-modal distillation module that deliberately distills 2D knowledge into 3D (the reverse of prior multimodal methods like 3D InfoMax and GraphMVP).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hierarchical clustering interpretation (trajectory-level → molecule-level → scaffold-level) provides a principled explanation for why different pre-training methods favor different downstream task types, a question the authors note was 'still under study' in prior work. This framework could guide the design of future molecular foundation models by clarifying which augmentation strategies to combine for which task domains. Open questions include whether the UniCorn representation transfers to molecular generation tasks, whether the theoretical unification extends to other domains (proteins, materials), and whether the 2D-to-3D distillation direction is optimal or whether bidirectional distillation could further improve performance.
