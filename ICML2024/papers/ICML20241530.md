## Problem

Existing experimental design algorithms (active search, Bayesian optimization) are overly exploitative, converging to local optima and producing redundant, low-diversity data. Prior diversity-inducing approaches rely on hard distance constraints or local penalization that are difficult to specify and enforce in high-dimensional or structured spaces. This paper seeks a unified, interpretable metric that naturally balances quality and diversity without requiring manually tuned distance thresholds.

## Input / Output

The method operates on an unlabeled data pool (molecules with Morgan fingerprints, alloys with physical attributes, MOFs with pore/linker features, or continuous optimization variables) paired with a domain-specific kernel (Tanimoto, RBF, or a weighted MOF-specific kernel) and a task-dependent quality function (posterior probability for active search, GP Thompson samples or UCB scores for Bayesian optimization). At each iteration it consumes the current labeled observations, the predictive model's outputs, and a target batch size, then produces a batch of queries maximizing the quality-weighted Vendi Score over remaining candidates. The final output is a set of diverse, high-quality data points, evaluated by the Vendi Score of collected positives or good solutions (effective discovery count).

## Method

The core contribution is the quality-weighted Vendi Score (qVS), defined as the product of the average quality score across items and the Vendi Score (exponential of the Shannon entropy of normalized eigenvalues of the kernel similarity matrix). An order-q generalization, inspired by Rényi entropy, smoothly controls the quality-diversity trade-off: smaller q approaches a count function favoring quality, while larger q demands near-complete uniqueness for each additional item to contribute to the score. For continuous domains the qVS is maximized via multi-start gradient-based optimization; for discrete or structured spaces a sequential greedy heuristic adds the candidate yielding the largest marginal qVS increase at each step. In active search, the quality function is the posterior probability of a positive label, and the qVS is maximized over candidate batches to select diverse queries. In Bayesian optimization, TuRBO's trust regions generate high-quality candidates via Thompson sampling, which are then filtered through qVS maximization to select a diverse final batch, eliminating the distance-threshold hyperparameter required by ROBOT.

## Results

On the bulk metal glass active search task, qVS-AS (q=1) achieves a Vendi Score of 134.45 at q=1, outperforming SELECT* (69.57), ECI* (23.92), and random search (6.99) by 93%, 462%, and 1823% respectively. On the photoswitch molecular discovery task, qVS-AS (q=0) achieves the highest raw discovery count (167.50 vs. SELECT*'s 125.90), while qVS-AS (q=1) achieves the best balanced diversity score (34.73 at q=1 vs. ECI*'s 29.70). In Bayesian optimization, qVS-BayesOpt (q=1) matches or exceeds ROBOT on the rover path-finding and lunar lander tasks, and uniquely outperforms both TuRBO and random search on the discrete MOF search (best storage capacity 21.24 vs. 20.47 for TuRBO-equivalent and lower for random). The paper reports a 70%–170% increase in effective discovery count (EDC) over baselines across tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ECI (Malkomes et al., 2021), qVS-AS dominates on both active search tasks: on bulk metal glass, qVS-AS (q=1) scores 134.45 vs. ECI*'s 23.92 at q=1 (a 5.6× improvement), and on photoswitch, qVS-AS (q=1) scores 34.73 vs. ECI*'s 29.70. Against SELECT (Vanchinathan et al., 2015), qVS-AS wins on bulk metal glass at q=1 (134.45 vs. 69.57) but SELECT* edges ahead on photoswitch at q=1 (30.09 vs. 34.73 for qVS-AS q=1, though qVS-AS q=0.1 achieves 31.71). In Bayesian optimization, qVS-BayesOpt (q=1) is competitive with ROBOT (Maus et al., 2023) on the continuous rover path-finding and lunar lander tasks, while TuRBO (Eriksson et al., 2019) produces overlapping, near-identical paths (Fig. 3) versus the diverse solutions from qVS-BayesOpt. On the discrete MOF search, where neither TuRBO nor ROBOT can be applied due to the lack of a continuous embedding, qVS-BayesOpt (q=1) achieves a best storage capacity of 21.24, outperforming the UCB-based BayesOpt baseline (20.47 at q=0) and random search, and also yields a lower energy penalty for toxin release.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Vendi Score (Friedman & Dieng, 2023) and its order-q generalization (Pasarkar & Dieng, 2023) by incorporating quality scores, yielding the quality-weighted Vendi Score (qVS). It positions itself as an alternative to two existing strategies for diverse experimental design: DPP-based likelihoods (which naturally incorporate quality via kernel modification but lack a clean diversity interpretation) and hard distance-constraint methods like ROBOT (Maus et al., 2023) that require a user-specified separation threshold τ. The qVS is then plugged into TuRBO-style trust-region Bayesian optimization (Eriksson et al., 2019) and k-NN-based active search (Garnett et al., 2012), replacing their exploitation-only acquisition criteria with a unified quality-diversity objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The qVS offers a hyperparameter-free (beyond the interpretable order q) way to balance quality and diversity in experimental design, removing the need to manually set distance constraints that are difficult to specify in high-dimensional or structured-data spaces. A notable open question is the lack of theoretical guarantees for the greedy optimization of qVS, and the order q still requires empirical tuning (the paper suggests dynamic q-setting as future work). The finding that diversity-aware search can outperform pure optimization in settings with many local optima (lunar lander, MOF search) suggests broader applicability beyond the explicitly diversity-targeted use cases, though the evidence is limited to a handful of domains with 10 repeats.
