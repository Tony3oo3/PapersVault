## Problem

Most anomaly detection methods assume a large clean normal dataset, but in practice training data is often contaminated with anomalies and only small auxiliary datasets of normal and anomalous samples are available. Existing semi-supervised approaches either ignore the additional normal dataset entirely or fail to address the overfitting that arises when these auxiliary datasets contain only tens to hundreds of samples.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three datasets: a large contaminated dataset X (mix of normal and anomalous samples), a small clean normal dataset X⁺, and a small clean anomaly dataset X⁻ that may cover only a subset of all anomaly types. The output is a scalar anomaly score per sample, computed as a weighted combination of the reconstruction error ‖x − G(E(x))‖² and the latent norm ‖E(x)‖², both normalized by validation-set statistics. The framework handles image (28×28, 32×32), text (768-dim BERT embeddings), and tabular data (up to 561 features).

## Method

The paper constructs two composite distributions P(x) = (1−λ)p_data(x) + λp⁺(x) and Q(x) = (1−β)p_g(x) + βp⁻(x), then minimizes the Pearson χ² f-divergence between them via an LSGAN, proving that under disjoint-support and weight conditions the generator converges exactly to the normal-data distribution. To counter overfitting from the tiny auxiliary sets, a flipping mechanism with probability γ randomly reassigns samples between the normal and anomaly roles in the discriminator loss, and an adaptive rule adjusts γ based on the discriminator's memorization gap. The GAN is extended to a BiGAN so that detection uses reconstruction error plus latent-space norm rather than expensive density evaluation. Theoretical results (Theorems 3.1–3.3) extend the convergence guarantee to the flipped case and to the partially-observed anomaly scenario, where the generator converges to a mixture of p⁺ and the unobserved anomaly component, with the normal dataset weight λ controlling the residual contamination.

## Results

The method reports AUROC as its primary metric across 15 datasets. Under the standard contaminated setting (20% contamination, 3 normal types, 5% normal / 1% anomaly auxiliary samples), the proposed method achieves 90.6 AUROC on MNIST, 85.2 on Fashion-MNIST, 67.5 on CIFAR-10, and 74.0 on 20NEWS, outperforming the strongest baseline (AA-BiGAN) by 8.2, 4.9, 3.8, and 3.3 points respectively. On the nine classic tabular anomaly detection datasets, the method achieves the best AUROC on 7 of 9 (e.g., 99.4 on Cardio, 91.8 on Satellite, 92.6 on Yelp), with second-best on Shuttle (99.5 vs. SOEL's 99.7) and Amazon (89.7 vs. SOEL's 90.5). The method also degrades more gracefully as contamination increases from 5% to 30%, maintaining a consistent 5–10 AUROC advantage over the best baseline across all contamination levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 10 baselines on the majority of datasets. The closest competitor is AA-BiGAN (Tian et al., 2022), which also uses a GAN-based approach with collected anomalies; the proposed method beats it by 8.2 AUROC on MNIST (K=3), 4.5 on FMNIST (K=3), 3.8 on CIFAR-10 (K=3), and 3.3 on 20NEWS (K=3), with the gap widening as contamination increases. Among semi-supervised baselines that also use a small anomaly dataset, Deep SAD, FeaWAD, RoSAS, and PReNet are consistently outperformed by 5–20+ AUROC points, with PReNet being particularly weak (e.g., 56.1 on MNIST K=3 vs. 90.6 for the proposed method). Unsupervised and self-supervised baselines (Deep SVDD, DIF, SLAD, ICL) suffer the most under contamination, with DIF dropping to 52.9 on MNIST (K=5) while the proposed method maintains 83.5. On the nine classic tabular datasets, SOEL (Li et al., 2023) is the only baseline that edges out the proposed method on two datasets (Shuttle: 99.7 vs. 99.5; Amazon: 90.5 vs. 89.7), while the proposed method wins decisively on the remaining seven (e.g., Cardio 99.4 vs. 97.6 for AA-BiGAN, Satellite 91.8 vs. 89.6 for FeaWAD). The ablation confirms that both the auxiliary dataset exploitation (+4.2 AUROC on MNIST) and the flipping mechanism (+2.4 AUROC) contribute independently to the overall gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GAN-based anomaly detection line (AA-BiGAN, Tian et al. 2022) by incorporating both a small clean normal dataset and a small clean anomaly dataset, whereas prior semi-supervised methods (Deep SAD, FeaWAD, PReNet) only leverage a single auxiliary anomaly set. The theoretical framework builds on f-divergence minimization in LSGANs, with the key departure being a correctness-guaranteed label-flipping mechanism to prevent discriminator overfitting on the small auxiliary sets. The incomplete-anomaly-type analysis (Theorem 3.3) generalizes the full-coverage assumption made in prior GAN-based approaches, showing the generator converges to a mixture that still suppresses observed anomaly types.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world training data is often contaminated, and while collecting a small anomaly set is feasible, collecting a small normal set is equally feasible yet largely ignored by prior methods. The theoretical convergence guarantees (under disjoint support) provide rare formal backing for GAN-based anomaly detection. Key limitations include the strong disjoint-support assumption between normal and anomaly distributions, which may not hold in high-dimensional real data, and the fact that the method still cannot fully recover the normal distribution when anomaly types are incomplete. Open questions include how the method behaves when supports overlap and whether the flipping mechanism generalizes beyond the LSGAN objective.
