## Problem

Adaptive optimizers like AdaGrad and Adam require a learning rate that depends on the unknown distance D to the solution, making manual tuning expensive especially in multi-agent or federated settings. D-Adaptation (Defazio & Mishchenko, 2023) provided a parameter-free estimate of D but suffered from a pessimistic error-term bound that introduced a full log(D/d_0) factor in the convergence rate. This paper asks whether the adaptation rate can be improved by handling the adaptive error term more tightly rather than upper-bounding it with the final estimate d_n.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-iteration sub-gradients g_k, an initial parameter vector x_0, a small initial distance estimate d_0 (default 10^{-6}), and an optional gradient-norm bound G (set to 0 in practice). Beyond the standard Adam state (m_k, v_k), Prodigy must additionally store the initial vector x_0 and a running weighted gradient sum s_k to compute the scalar distance estimate d_k, increasing memory by two vectors. A notable property is that the effective step size can increase between iterations (unlike vanilla AdaGrad), and the Adam variant uses a more conservative sqrt(β_2) decay for the distance-estimate numerator rather than β_2.

## Method

Prodigy modifies the AdaGrad step-size denominator to weight each past gradient by d_i^2, yielding η_k = d_k²ω_k / √(d_k²G² + Σ d_i²ω_i²‖g_i‖²), which is provably larger than D-Adaptation's step size while still controlling the adaptive error term. The distance estimate d_{k+1} is updated as max(d_k, Σ η_i⟨g_i, x_0−x_i⟩ / ‖x_{k+1}−x_0‖), and optional non-decreasing weights ω_k (e.g., ω_k = β_2^{−k/2}) prevent the denominator from growing too large, recovering Adam's exponential moving average structure. This design improves the worst-case convergence rate from O(GD·log(D/d_0)/√n) to O(GD·√log(D/d_0)/√n), matching the coin-betting rate, and the paper proves a matching lower bound for the class of exponentially bounded algorithms. Three variants are provided: a Gradient Descent form (Algorithm 1), a Dual Averaging form (Algorithm 2) that removes a log(n) factor, and the practical Adam form (Algorithm 3) used in all deep-learning experiments.

## Results

On ViT training on ImageNet, Prodigy achieves 74.63% test accuracy, closing most of the gap to hand-tuned Adam (75.4%) and exceeding D-Adaptation by more than 2%. On 12 convex logistic-regression benchmarks, Prodigy consistently outperforms D-Adaptation and adapts faster than DoG on 10 of 12 problems. On CIFAR-10 (VGG11, ResNet-50), Prodigy closes the 'few percent' accuracy gap that D-Adapted Adam had relative to tuned Adam. The theoretical convergence rate improves from O(log(D/d₀)) for D-Adaptation to O(√log(D/d₀)) for Prodigy, matching the rate of coin-betting methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against D-Adaptation (Defazio & Mishchenko, 2023), Prodigy wins on every experimental setting: it achieves higher accuracy on all 12 convex benchmarks, closes the CIFAR-10 gap to Adam, and exceeds D-Adaptation by >2% on ViT/ImageNet. Against DoG (Ivgi et al., 2023), Prodigy adapts faster on 10 of 12 convex problems; on CIFAR-10, DoG produces larger step-size estimates but this is counterbalanced by a larger denominator, and a heuristic Adam-like modification of DoG diverged. Against hand-tuned Adam, Prodigy reaches 'test accuracy values close to that of hand-tuned Adam'—on ViT it is within 0.77% (74.63% vs 75.4%), and on nanoGPT it is on par with AdamW in training loss. On large-scale tasks (RoBERTa, GPT, DLRM, VarNet), Prodigy matches the D-Adapted Adam baseline with only negligible differences, confirming that the improvement is most visible in settings where fast learning-rate adaptation matters (full-batch convex, vision transformers, small-scale deep learning).

## Relation to Prior Work

Prodigy is a direct modification of D-Adaptation (Defazio & Mishchenko, 2023), which itself builds on the AdaGrad step-size structure (Duchi et al., 2011) to eliminate the need for a manually set learning rate. The key conceptual departure is in how the adaptive error term is handled: rather than pessimistically bounding d_k by the final d_n (as D-Adaptation does), Prodigy incorporates d_k²-weighted gradients into the step-size denominator, yielding a provably larger step size and a √log(D/d₀) improvement over D-Adaptation's log(D/d₀) dependence. This brings Prodigy's finite-time rate in line with coin-betting methods (Orabona & Tommasi, 2017; Cutkosky & Orabona, 2018), while the Adam-like variant (Algorithm 3) is derived by matching Adam's exponential moving average structure (Kingma & Ba, 2015) through exponential weights ω_k = β₂^{-k/2}.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Prodigy closes a meaningful theoretical gap between D-Adaptation and the best-known coin-betting rates, and the accompanying lower bound for exponentially bounded algorithms establishes that this √log dependence is essentially optimal within that class. Practically, the method is already widely adopted (recommended optimizer for Hugging Face Diffusers DreamBooth LoRA training) and consistently narrows the gap to hand-tuned Adam across diverse architectures and tasks. A notable limitation is the additional memory for storing x₀ and s_k vectors, and the lower bound only covers the regime n < log(D/d₀), leaving a more general lower bound for larger n as an open problem. The smooth-case analysis carries an extra log² term that the authors acknowledge is likely an artifact, suggesting room for tighter theory.
