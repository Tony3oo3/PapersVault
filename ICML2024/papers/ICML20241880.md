## Problem

LLMs excel at text-based reasoning but struggle to extract structured, interpretable knowledge from discrete event sequences (e.g., medical records, kitchen activities). The posterior over symbolic logic trees that explain such sequences is intractable due to the discrete combinatorial space, and standard approaches like MCMC or PPO-based fine-tuning fail to capture the multi-modal distribution of valid rules. The paper asks: how can we perform tractable, diverse Bayesian inference over logic trees using LLMs as priors, while simultaneously learning a probabilistic event model?

## Input / Output

The method takes a discrete event sequence X = {(t_i, e_i)} with timestamps and event type names/IDs, plus a target event predicate Y, and converts the event history into a textual prompt for the LLM. The logic tree is constrained to a predefined predicate space Z with bounded depth d and width w (typically d=3, w≤7), growing level-by-level via backward chaining from the root predicate. Output is a sampled symbolic logic tree (a set of ordered predicate paths with learned weights) that both explains the sequence and drives next-event prediction through a rule-informed temporal point process intensity function.

## Method

LaTee formulates logic tree inference as an amortized EM algorithm over a mixture latent variable model, where the E-step trains a GFlowNet (initialized from a QLoRA-fine-tuned local LLM like OPT-1.3B) to sample from the intractable posterior p(R|X,Y) ∝ p_LM(Y|X,R)·p_w(X|R)·p_LM(R) using a forward-looking SubTB loss for local credit assignment across tree levels. The M-step uses GFlowNet samples to approximate marginalization, jointly updating the TL-PP rule weights w (via negative log-likelihood of the rule-informed intensity) and tunable LLM prior parameters φ. A frozen larger LLM (Zephyr-3B or Mistral-7B) handles the generation step p_LM(Y|X,R) in the M-step, while the fine-tuned local model handles inference in the E-step. The key distinction from prior GFlowNet-CoT work is the extension from token-level chain-of-thought to sentence-level logic tree generation with a modular likelihood that separates event dynamics (TL-PP) from language modeling, and the use of adaptive E-M scheduling via loss thresholding to balance convergence of the non-stationary GFlowNet policy with likelihood updates.

## Results

On MIMIC-3, LaTee (GFN fine-tuning) achieves an Error Rate of 27.50% and Mean Rank of 1.14, outperforming the best attention-based TPP baseline AttNHP (36.66% ER, 1.25 MR) by a relative margin of ~25%. On EPIC-100, LaTee reaches 55.25% ER and 2.15 MR, surpassing AttNHP (67.53% ER, 2.45 MR) by ~18%. On StackOverflow (non-semantic), LaTee matches AttNHP (33.45% vs 33.33% ER). All LLM prompting and fine-tuning baselines (k-shot CoT, ToT, SFT, PPO) perform substantially worse, with ERs ranging from 71% to 100%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaTee decisively outperforms all LLM-based baselines: k-shot CoT (k=0,1,3) and ToT achieve near-random ERs (71–100%) across all three datasets, while SFT and PPO fine-tuning remain 20–60 percentage points behind LaTee in ER. Against the strongest non-LLM baseline AttNHP, LaTee wins on both semantic datasets (MIMIC-3: 27.50% vs 36.66% ER; EPIC-100: 55.25% vs 67.53% ER) and essentially ties on the non-semantic StackOverflow dataset (33.45% vs 33.33% ER). Pt-AttNHP, a prompt-augmented variant of AttNHP, consistently underperforms AttNHP on all three datasets, suggesting its retrieval-memory mechanism fails to capture individual-level characteristics. The paper attributes LaTee's advantage on semantic datasets to the LLM's ability to leverage meaningful event names for structure discovery, with an additional ~25% ER reduction when semantic names replace numerical IDs.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaTee extends GFlowNets-CoT (Hu et al., 2023a) from token-level chain-of-thought generation to sentence-level symbolic logic tree generation, and adapts the GFlowNet-EM scheduling (Hu et al., 2023b) for a modular likelihood that combines a Temporal Logic Point Process (Li et al., 2020) event model with an LLM language prior. The tree growth mechanism borrows backward chaining from differentiable ILP (Rocktäschel & Riedel, 2017), but replaces discrete search with a learnable GFlowNet policy over a combinatorial predicate space. Unlike prior TPP models (AttNHP, PromptTPP) that learn black-box intensity functions, LaTee grounds the intensity in interpretable logic rules extracted from a fine-tuned LLM, and unlike prompt-based reasoning (CoT, ToT), it performs amortized posterior inference rather than one-shot generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled route to interpretable, knowledge-driven event prediction in high-stakes domains (healthcare, robotics) where black-box TPPs are insufficient, and the finding that GFlowNet fine-tuning yields substantially more diverse rule distributions than SFT or PPO suggests a general advantage of diversity-seeking objectives for structured reasoning. However, empirical validation is constrained to very small datasets (142–477 sequences) and models up to 6.7B parameters, leaving open whether the approach scales to longer sequences, larger predicate spaces, or larger LLMs. The observation that larger frozen LMs in the M-step (generation) contribute more to prediction accuracy than larger fine-tuned LMs in the E-step (inference) points toward a practical hybrid of local lightweight inference with API-based generation that remains unexplored.
