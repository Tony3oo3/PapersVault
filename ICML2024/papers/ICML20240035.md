## Problem

First-order methods for structured nonconvex-nonconcave min-max problems under cohypomonotonicity or weak MVI conditions have been limited to a nonmonotonicity parameter ρ < 1/(2L), despite conjectures that the true limit is ρ < 1/L. This paper closes that gap, extending the admissible range of ρ to 1/L while preserving optimal or best-known oracle complexity guarantees for both deterministic and stochastic settings.

## Input / Output

The algorithms operate in a double-loop structure: the outer loop performs Halpern or KM iterations, while the inner loop approximates the resolvent J_{η(F+G)} via FBF using first-order oracle calls (gradient evaluations of F and resolvent evaluations of G). The inexactness tolerance for the resolvent approximation is relaxed to Õ(k^{-3/2}) at iteration k, looser than the Õ(k^{-3}) required by prior analyses, which is critical for the stochastic extension. The output is a point x satisfying dist(0,(F+G)(x)) ≤ ε, certified by the fixed-point residual η^{-1}‖(Id − J_{η(F+G)})(x)‖.

## Method

The key insight is to exploit conic nonexpansiveness of the resolvent J_{η(F+G)} rather than plain nonexpansiveness, which previously forced the tighter ρ < η/2 < 1/(2L) requirement. Under cohypomonotonicity, Halpern iteration is applied to the firmly nonexpansive operator T = (1−α)Id + αJ_{η(F+G)} with α = 1 − ρ/η, requiring only ρ < η < 1/L; under weak MVI, KM iteration is analyzed via conic quasi-nonexpansiveness of the resolvent with respect to a solution. The tighter inexactness analysis (relaxing from Õ(k^{-3}) to Õ(k^{-3/2})) is essential for the stochastic extension, reducing complexity from Õ(ε^{-7}) to Õ(ε^{-4}) for cohypomonotone problems. For the stochastic weak MVI case, a multilevel Monte Carlo estimator controls the bias-variance tradeoff in the resolvent approximation, achieving Õ(ε^{-4}) expected stochastic oracle complexity.

## Results

Under cohypomonotonicity (Assumption 2), the inexact Halpern iteration (Theorem 2.1) achieves the fixed-point residual η⁻¹‖(Id − J_{η(F+G)})(x_K)‖ ≤ ε with Õ(ε⁻¹) first-order oracle complexity for the extended range ρ < 1/L, doubling the previous best-known bound of ρ < 1/(2L). Under the weaker weak MVI condition (Assumption 3), the inexact KM iteration (Theorem 3.1) achieves the same residual bound with Õ(ε⁻²) complexity for ρ < 1/L. In the stochastic cohypomonotone setting (Corollary 4.1), the method achieves Õ(ε⁻⁴) SFO complexity, a dramatic improvement over the Õ(ε⁻¹⁶) of Pethick et al. (2023b). For stochastic weak MVI (Corollary 4.4), a multilevel Monte Carlo estimator yields Õ(ε⁻⁴) expected SFO complexity under standard unbiased oracle assumptions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central contribution is extending the admissible range of the nonmonotonicity parameter ρ from 1/(2L) to 1/L for both cohypomonotone and weak MVI settings, while preserving optimal or best-known oracle complexities. For cohypomonotone problems, this doubles the range beyond all prior constrained methods (Cai & Zheng 2022 at 1/(60L); Cai et al. 2022b, Pethick et al. 2023b, Lee & Kim 2021, Tran-Dinh 2023, Gorbunov et al. 2023 at 1/(2L)) and also surpasses the unconstrained result of Cai et al. (2023) at 0.7/L. For weak MVI, the 1/L bound improves upon Diakonikolas et al. (2021) at 1/(8L), Cai & Zheng (2022) at 1/(12√3·L), Lee & Kim (2024) at 1/(3L), and matches or exceeds the 1/(2L) of Böhm (2022) and Pethick et al. (2022), as well as the 0.63/L of Fan et al. (2024). In the stochastic cohypomonotone case, the Õ(ε⁻⁴) complexity of Corollary 4.1 dramatically improves upon the Õ(ε⁻¹⁶) of Pethick et al. (2023b), which also required increasing batch sizes and was limited to ρ < 1/(2L). For stochastic weak MVI, the Õ(ε⁻⁴) result of Corollary 4.4 matches the complexity of Pethick et al. (2023a) but under strictly weaker oracle assumptions (standard unbiased SFO vs. MS-Lipschitzness and same-seed queries), making it applicable to a broader class of problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the classical Halpern and Krasnosel'skii-Mann fixed-point iterations, the FBF algorithm of Tseng (2000) for inner-loop resolvent approximation, and the conic nonexpansiveness framework introduced by Bauschke et al. (2021). The key departure from prior work (Diakonikolas 2020; Cai et al. 2023; Pethick et al. 2023b) is the use of conic nonexpansiveness of the resolvent rather than plain nonexpansiveness, which allows the Halpern/KM analysis to tolerate ρ < 1/L instead of the previously required ρ < 1/(2L). The inexact Halpern analysis also tightens the required inexactness level from Õ(k⁻³) to Õ(k⁻³ᐟ²), a refinement that becomes critical in the stochastic setting where inner-loop convergence is sublinear.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Doubling the admissible range of the nonconvexity parameter ρ from 1/(2L) to 1/L resolves a long-standing barrier and potentially brings a wider class of nonconvex-nonconcave min-max problems (e.g., certain GAN training dynamics, adversarial ML, and RL policy gradient problems) within reach of first-order methods with optimal or near-optimal complexity. The paper is purely theoretical with no empirical validation, so the practical significance depends on whether real ML problems exhibit ρ in the newly accessible [1/(2L), 1/L) range. Open questions include developing single-loop algorithms without the logarithmic factor from the double-loop structure, removing spurious log terms, and identifying more ML problems satisfying the cohypomonotonicity or weak MVI assumptions.
