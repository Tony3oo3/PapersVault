## Problem

Learning reusable skills from expert demonstrations via temporal variational inference tends to collapse into two degenerate local optima: either every timestep becomes its own skill (no compression) or the entire trajectory is treated as one skill (no segmentation). The root cause is that the search space over trajectory segmentations grows exponentially with horizon, and prior methods either restrict skill representations to natural language or lack external semantic guidance to navigate this space effectively.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240565/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes goal-conditioned expert trajectories where each action carries a discrete semantic token (e.g., 'PickupObject Apple'); in ALFRED, visual observations are 300×300 egocentric images preprocessed by a frozen Faster R-CNN into 512×7×7 embeddings, while in BabyAI the state is a symbolic 7×7×3 grid vector. The LLM (GPT-4) receives only the goal string and the action sequence—no visual observations—to produce initial segments of 1–5 actions each with a short language annotation. The output is a discrete skill library (capped at 100 skills), a frozen low-level skill-conditioned action policy, and a trainable high-level policy that selects skills and termination conditions for online hierarchical RL.

## Method

LAST operates in three stages. First, GPT-4 segments each trajectory into short subsequences (1–5 actions) with language annotations, imposing the hard constraint that downstream skills may only merge these segments, never split them, which drastically shrinks the segmentation search space. Second, a temporal variational inference framework with three transformer encoders (a full-context variational encoder for the posterior q, a causal encoder for the generative high-level policy p, and a causal low-level policy π) jointly learns skill-conditioned action prediction and skill-switching inference, where q conditions on the full trajectory plus LLM language annotations while p is restricted to causal history for online use. Third, a novel MDL-based auxiliary loss—unlike LOVE's compression objective—explicitly accounts for the previously selected skill k_{t−1} in the code-length calculation, yielding more accurate compression pressure and more stable gradients. For downstream tasks, the low-level policy is frozen and the high-level policy is fine-tuned with SAC and Gumbel-Softmax, regularized by KL divergence to the pretrained skill and termination distributions.

## Results

On BabyAI, LAST achieves 43% average zero-shot success rate, outperforming LOVE (37%), LISA (36%), and BC (34%). On the more complex ALFRED household simulation, LAST reaches 52% average subtask success rate, surpassing LOVE (47%), LISA (46%), BC (45%), and (SL)³ (34%). In the downstream task learning setting (online RL fine-tuning on six long-horizon ALFRED tasks), LAST outperforms all baselines in 5 out of 6 tasks, and is the only method achieving positive success rates on the three longest-horizon tasks (Pick clean, Pick heat, Pick cool, ~200 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAST outperforms all four baselines on both BabyAI and ALFRED in zero-shot transfer. On BabyAI, LAST leads by 6 points over LOVE (43% vs. 37%) and 7 points over BC (43% vs. 34%), with the largest margin on BossLevel (36% vs. 25–27%). On ALFRED, LAST achieves 52% average success rate, beating LOVE (47%) by 5 points and LISA (46%) by 6 points; the most notable gain is on Heat tasks (82% vs. 41% for LOVE and 21% for LISA) and Toggle tasks (37% vs. 28% for LOVE). The language-based (SL)³ baseline (34%) and the seq2seq baselines (17–26%) are substantially weaker, confirming that learned skill abstractions outperform direct sequence modeling. In the downstream RL fine-tuning setting, LAST is the only method that achieves positive success rates on the three longest-horizon tasks (~200 steps), while LOVE and LISA only succeed on the shortest task (<50 steps), demonstrating that the LLM-guided initial segmentation and temporal variational inference are both critical for learning reusable skills on complex long-horizon tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the temporal variational inference (TVI) framework for skill discovery from demonstrations (Shankar & Gupta, 2020) and the MDL-based compression objective from LOVE (Jiang et al., 2022) by introducing LLM-generated initial trajectory segmentation as a structural prior that constrains the search space. Unlike prior language-based skill methods (LISA, (SL)³) that restrict skill representations to natural language, LAST uses language only as a conditioning signal during inference while keeping skills as learned latent variables. The key architectural departure is a three-stage pipeline—LLM segmentation, TVI-based merging, and online hierarchical RL—that progressively narrows the exponentially large segmentation space, combined with a modified MDL objective that correctly accounts for the previous skill's influence on code length.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in skill-based RL: the combinatorial explosion of possible trajectory segmentations that causes TVI-based methods to collapse into degenerate solutions (all-one-step or all-one-skill). The LLM-guided initialization is a pragmatic solution that leverages commonsense priors without requiring the skill representation itself to be linguistic. Limitations include the requirement for discrete, language-annotated action spaces and dependence on LLM segmentation quality; the authors note that video foundation models could extend the approach to continuous or purely visual domains. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much longer horizons, whether the five-skill structure discovered in ALFRED is truly task-agnostic or an artifact of the dataset, and how sensitive performance is to LLM segmentation errors.
