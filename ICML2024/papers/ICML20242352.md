## Problem

Existing corruption-robust RL algorithms with provable guarantees are limited to the model-free setting, where value functions are learned via regression and uncertainty weighting naturally applies. In model-based RL, where transition dynamics are learned via maximum likelihood estimation (MLE), the log-likelihood structure makes it non-trivial to decompose the corruption term into a product of uncertainty and corruption, leaving the robustness of MLE-based model learning against adversarial transition corruption entirely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite model class M of transition probability functions, known normalized rewards (summing to [0,1] over H steps), and state-action observations as input. In the online setting, it produces a sequence of greedy policies over T episodes; in the offline setting, it produces a single policy from a pre-collected trajectory dataset. The corruption is measured as the cumulative sum of per-episode TV-based ratio deviations between true and corrupted transition probabilities, and the adversary is constrained to preserve the support of the true transition (i.e., cannot introduce transitions to previously impossible states).

## Method

The core technical contribution is a TV-based information ratio (IR) that characterizes uncertainty in the transition probability space, replacing the reward/value-based uncertainty used in model-free corruption-robust RL. By applying the inequality log x ≤ x−1 and splitting the integration over regions where dP_s − dP_* is positive or negative, the paper shows the corruption term in the weighted log-likelihood decomposes into a product of this TV-based uncertainty and the per-step corruption level, enabling uncertainty weights σ_t^h = max{1, U_t/α} to cancel the uncertainty factor. Online, CR-OMLE maximizes the weighted log-likelihood to obtain an MLE, constructs a confidence set with β² relaxation, and selects the most optimistic model; offline, CR-PMLE uses an iterative weight-computation procedure (since weights cannot be updated round-by-round) and applies pessimism via a minimax optimization over the confidence set. The resulting regret bound is Õ(√T + C) online and suboptimality Õ(√(1/T) + C/T) offline under a uniform coverage condition, both matching corresponding lower bounds in the corruption-dependent term.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments. The headline results are: (1) CR-OMLE achieves an online regret bound of Õ(H log B √(T log|M|·ED) + CH·ED), where the corruption-dependent term CH·ED matches the new lower bound Ω((H−2)(d−1)C/64) for linear MDPs up to log factors; (2) CR-PMLE achieves an offline suboptimality bound of Õ(H log B √(log|M|/(T·Cov(M,D))) + CH/(T·Cov(M,D))) under a uniform coverage condition, with the corruption term CH/(T·Cov) nearly matching the lower bound Ω(C/(Cov·T)) up to a factor of H. The uncorrupted main terms match the existing OMLE bound (Liu et al., 2023a), confirming that robustness is achieved without sacrificing sample efficiency in the clean case.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper contains no empirical baselines or dataset comparisons, as it is a theoretical contribution. In terms of theoretical positioning: the uncorrupted regret term of CR-OMLE matches the OMLE bound of Liu et al. (2023a), showing no overhead for robustness in the absence of corruption. The corruption-dependent term CH·ED is shown to be near-optimal via a new lower bound for linear MDPs, matching the corruption scaling of model-free results by Ye et al. (2023a) and He et al. (2022) but in the previously unexplored model-based setting. For the offline case, CR-PMLE is noted as the first provable model-based offline RL algorithm even without corruption, and its corruption term CH/(T·Cov) nearly matches the constructed lower bound up to a factor of H. The paper does not compare against distributional robust RL methods (e.g., Roy et al., 2017; Clavier et al., 2023), noting these address a fundamentally different notion of robustness (distributional shift vs. adversarial corruption during training).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: corruption-robust RL (Lykouris et al. 2018; He et al. 2022; Ye et al. 2023a,b), which has been studied exclusively in the model-free setting using value-function-based uncertainty weighting, and model-based RL via optimistic/pessimistic MLE (Liu et al. 2023a), which handles general function approximation through TV-based eluder dimensions. The key technical departure is that the regression-based decomposition of corruption into uncertainty × corruption terms (central to prior model-free results) does not directly apply to log-likelihood objectives; the paper resolves this by introducing TV-based information ratios as uncertainty weights for MLE, a construction that is non-trivial and requires new analytical tools (Freedman's inequality, TV-norm decompositions). The offline algorithm additionally borrows the weight-iteration scheme from Ye et al. (2023b) to handle the non-iterative nature of offline data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work providing provable corruption-robustness guarantees for model-based RL in both online and offline settings, filling a clear gap identified in the literature. The matching lower bounds (additive C dependence online, C/(T·Cov) dependence offline) establish that the proposed algorithms are near-optimal with respect to corruption level, giving practitioners confidence that no fundamentally better corruption handling is possible. However, the results are purely theoretical: the algorithms require finite model classes, the computational complexity is acknowledged as prohibitive for large model spaces, and no practical implementation is demonstrated. Open questions include extending the uncertainty weighting to representation learning, developing efficient practical algorithms (e.g., via bootstrapped uncertainty), and adapting the model-selection approach of Wei et al. (2022) to the model-based setting for unknown corruption levels.
