## Problem

Information-theoretic generalization bounds based on the mutual information (MI) between training data and learned hypothesis are fundamentally impractical for modern neural networks because MI estimation scales exponentially with the dimension of the weight vector. This paper asks whether the well-known compressibility of neural networks via random lower-dimensional subspaces can be exploited to derive tighter, computationally tractable MI-based generalization bounds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241510/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a training dataset S_n, a neural network's weight vector W ∈ R^D, a random orthonormal projection matrix Θ ∈ St(d,D) (d ≪ D) sampled from the uniform distribution on the Stiefel manifold, and a loss function satisfying sub-Gaussianity or Lipschitz conditions. It produces a generalization error bound expressed in terms of disintegrated mutual information I^Θ(W'; Z_i) between the low-dimensional projected weights W' = Θ^⊤W ∈ R^d and individual training samples, plus (in the rate-distortion variant) a distortion term measuring ‖W − ΘΘ^⊤W‖. The bounds are evaluated empirically using MINE for MI estimation and entropy-based upper bounds when quantization is applied, yielding non-vacuous values for networks with up to ~1.8M parameters.

## Method

The core technique is a disintegration argument over the random subspace: rather than bounding gen(μ, A^(d)) via I(ΘW'; S_n) in the full D-dimensional space, the proof conditions on Θ and bounds the error for each fixed Θ, then averages over P_Θ, yielding bounds in terms of the lower-dimensional disintegrated MI I^Θ(W'; S_n) or I^Θ(W'; Z_i). This produces bounds that are provably tighter than Xu & Raginsky (2017) and Bu et al. (2019) by Jensen's inequality, and whose MI terms are far cheaper to estimate since W' lives in R^d rather than R^D. The paper further extends to approximately compressible weights via a rate-distortion decomposition: the generalization error is bounded by a distortion term (2L·E[ρ(W, ΘΘ^⊤W)]) plus a rate term involving I^Θ(Θ^⊤W; Z_i), and a quantization-based variant replaces the MI with a closed-form entropy bound. A practical regularization penalty λ·E[ρ(W, ΘΘ^⊤W)] is added to the training objective to encourage weights to lie near the random subspace, trading off training error for tighter generalization guarantees.

## Results

The paper's empirical validation focuses on computing non-vacuous information-theoretic generalization bounds for neural networks, a task previously considered intractable. On MNIST (D=199,210) and CIFAR-10 (D=656,810), combining random slicing with quantization yields finite, non-vacuous bounds while maintaining test performance for adequate d. For a 3-layer feedforward NN on MNIST (D=1,794,000 parameters, n=1000), the rate-distortion bound in Theorem 5.2 is evaluated across d ∈ {100, 500, 1000, 2000} and λ ∈ {0, 1, 10, 100}, showing that (λ,d)=(10,1000) achieves a tight bound while preserving satisfactory train/test accuracy. In the Gaussian mean estimation example, the true generalization error is gen(μ, A^(d)) = 2d/n, and the derived bound scales as O(1/√n) while accurately reflecting the decrease in error as d→1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the individual-sample MI bound of Bu et al. (2019), Theorem 3.2. On binary classification of 20-dimensional Gaussian data with logistic regression (Figure 2), the paper's disintegrated bound (Theorem 4.2) is consistently tighter than Bu et al. (2019), and the gap widens as the subspace dimension d decreases. Critically, in the Gaussian mean estimation example (Figure 1), Bu et al. (2019) can only be applied when d=D (full dimension), because the sub-Gaussianity assumption on the loss cannot be verified for d<D; the paper's disintegration-based approach overcomes this by conditioning on Θ, yielding a valid bound for all d≤D. The paper also notes that their bound (3) is tighter than the standard Xu & Raginsky (2017) bound (2) by Jensen's inequality, since E_{P_Θ}[√(I^Θ(W';S_n))] ≤ √(I(ΘW';S_n)). Overall, the slicing-based bounds dominate the standard MI bounds in both tightness and computational tractability, with the advantage growing as the intrinsic dimension d shrinks relative to the ambient dimension D.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads: (1) information-theoretic generalization bounds based on input-output mutual information (Xu & Raginsky 2017; Bu et al. 2019) and (2) random subspace training / slicing of neural network weights (Li et al. 2018; Lotfi et al. 2022). The key technical move is applying the disintegration technique (conditioning on the random projection matrix Θ) to the standard MI bound proofs, yielding bounds in terms of disintegrated mutual information that closely parallel Sliced Mutual Information (Goldfeld & Greenewald 2021; Goldfeld et al. 2022). The rate-distortion extension draws on Sefidgaran et al. (2022) and Bu et al. (2021), but departs from them by treating compressibility as a training-time property (via a regularization penalty) rather than a post-hoc compression step, and by showing the distortion term can be controlled through a simple λ-penalty on the training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper achieves the first non-vacuous information-theoretic generalization bounds for neural networks trained on MNIST and CIFAR-10, a task previously considered fundamentally intractable due to the exponential sample complexity of MI estimation in high dimensions. The explicit dependence on the subspace dimension d provides actionable architectural guidance, and the proposed λ-regularization gives practitioners a concrete knob to trade off compressibility (tighter bounds) against training error. However, the bounds remain substantially looser than PAC-Bayes or data-driven approaches, the rate-distortion extension requires Lipschitz losses (limiting applicability to standard cross-entropy without modification), and the empirical validation is limited to small feedforward networks on two datasets. Open questions include whether the slicing framework extends to conditional MI bounds, convolutional architectures, and whether the regularization can be made adaptive during training.
