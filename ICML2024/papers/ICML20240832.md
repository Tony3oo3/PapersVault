## Problem

Grokking (delayed generalization) was previously documented only in narrow controlled settings such as modular addition or large-weight initialization, leaving open whether it is a universal phenomenon and what geometric mechanism drives it. The paper asks whether grokking can manifest on samples other than the held-out test set, and seeks a task-agnostic explanation for both delayed generalization and the newly observed delayed robustness on adversarial examples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240832/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The local complexity measure consumes a trained DNN's layer weight matrices and bias vectors together with a set of probe points (train, test, or random), constructing P-dimensional cross-polytope neighborhoods of radius r around each point and propagating them layer-by-layer to count neuron hyperplane intersections. The primary output is a scalar LC trajectory per point category over optimization steps, revealing three training phases; secondary outputs include 2D input-space partition visualizations (via SplineCam) showing where nonlinearities concentrate, and layerwise LC maps for deep architectures. The neighborhood radius must be kept small (r ≤ 0.014 for CNNs, r = 10⁻⁴ for ResNet18) to limit geometric deformation across layers.

## Method

The paper introduces local complexity (LC) as a task-agnostic progress measure defined as the density of spline partition boundaries (neuron hyperplane intersections) within a local neighborhood of the DNN input space, computed layer-by-layer by tracking sign changes of pre-activations on cross-polytope vertices. The central finding is that during training the partition undergoes a phase transition called region migration: nonlinearities systematically migrate away from data points and accumulate around the decision boundary, producing a 'robust partition' with large linear regions around training samples that underlies both delayed generalization and delayed robustness. LC dynamics exhibit a characteristic double descent (initial descent, ascent, second descent/migration), and the onset of the second descent consistently precedes grokking across MLPs, CNNs, ResNets, Transformers, and GPTs on diverse datasets. The authors further show that batch normalization suppresses grokking by pinning partition boundaries to training data, and that width, depth, batch size, and training-set size all modulate the timing of region migration, with overparameterization and larger batches expediting it while weight decay and memorization load delay it.

## Results

The paper demonstrates that grokking (delayed generalization) and a newly identified phenomenon, delayed robustness, occur across a wide range of practical settings: a ResNet18 on CIFAR-10 groks PGD adversarial examples after ~10⁴ optimization steps and attains near-equal robustness and generalization after 2×10⁵ steps; a 4-layer ReLU MLP on MNIST shows the same pattern; and a GPT-12 architecture on Shakespeare text exhibits delayed robustness in token embedding space. The proposed local complexity (LC) measure reveals a consistent double-descent training dynamic: an initial descent, an ascent phase where nonlinearities accumulate around data points, and a second descent (region migration) where nonlinearities systematically migrate toward the decision boundary. Batch normalization is shown to eliminate grokking entirely, while increasing network width, depth, or batch size expedites region migration, and increasing memorization load (randomly labeled data) delays it.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is an empirical study rather than a method-vs-baseline comparison, so the key contrasts are architectural and training-configuration ablations. The most striking comparison is with/without batch normalization: adding BN after each convolutional layer in a CIFAR-10 CNN completely eliminates adversarial grokking and removes the first LC descent, with LC increasing monotonically (confirmed theoretically via BN's partition-adaptation property from Balestriero & Baraniuk, 2022). Across architectures (MLP, CNN, ResNet18, GPT) and datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10/100, Imagenette, Shakespeare), the same three-phase LC dynamic and region migration are observed, establishing grokking as a universal training phenomenon rather than an artifact of specific settings. Increasing overparameterization (width from 20 to 2000, depth from 2 to 6) consistently expedites region migration and thus earlier grokking, while increasing memorization demand (randomly labeled data from 1K to 60K) delays it. Weight decay shows a non-monotonic effect: moderate values delay the second descent, while strong weight decay reduces peak LC during ascent. The paper also validates its LC approximation against exact SplineCam partition computation, confirming both methods exhibit the same double-descent trajectory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the spline theory of deep networks (Balestriero & Baraniuk, 2018) to define a local complexity measure, validated against the exact partition computation of SplineCam (Humayun et al., 2023a), and framed as a progress measure in the sense of Barak et al. (2022). It extends the grokking literature initiated by Power et al. (2022) on modular addition and Liu et al. (2022) on algorithmic data by demonstrating that delayed generalization and a newly identified form—delayed robustness—occur in standard practical settings (CIFAR10 CNNs, ResNet on Imagenette, GPT on Shakespeare) without any special initialization. The geometric explanation via region migration also connects to the mechanistic interpretability 'circuit' literature (Olah et al., 2020; Nanda et al., 2023), reframing circuit cleanup as a spatial reorganization of the input-space partition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that grokking is a near-universal training dynamic rather than a rare artifact of controlled settings fundamentally shifts how the field should think about late-training behavior, with the practical implication that training longer (or removing batch normalization) can yield adversarial robustness for free. The local complexity measure provides a task-agnostic, dataset-independent progress indicator that could guide early stopping or training scheduling. Key open questions remain: there is no theoretical justification for why the double-descent in local complexity occurs or why region migration is delayed until after interpolation, and the connection to neural collapse and the role of SGD noise versus Adam remain unexplored.
