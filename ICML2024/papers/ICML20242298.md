## Problem

Existing SDE-based generative models for probabilistic time series (e.g., ScoreSDE) require manually determining drift and diffusion coefficients to match a desired probability path, making new path design cumbersome, and they need thousands of sampling steps for high-quality generation. Additionally, prior sequence modeling backbones either rely on quadratic-complexity self-attention (lacking inductive bias) or on SSMs alone (capturing only local dependencies), so no single module efficiently models both local and global temporal structure at linear cost.

## Input / Output

For imputation, the network Net_θ receives the concatenation [X_0⊙M, X_t, M] (masked observation, noisy intermediate state, binary mask) plus covariate embeddings (observed time and feature identities); for forecasting it receives GRU-extracted historical features as conditioning. The model outputs 100 stochastic samples per imputation/forecast query, from which median and prediction intervals are derived. Inference uses only 10 reverse steps (vs. 50–100 for CSDI/CSBI), and the target distribution is a general multivariate Gaussian N(μ, Σ) with full covariance, not restricted to diagonal form.

## Method

D3M constructs the forward probability path directly from the closed-form solution of a linear SDE, decomposing it into a deterministic signal-dissipation term H_t (parameterized by a small coefficient vector ψ) and a stochastic noise-injection term β_t L ε. A single neural network Net_θ with two output branches simultaneously predicts ψ_θ and ε_θ, trained with a weighted sum of signal-dissipation and noise-matching losses; Theorem 3.1 proves this framework subsumes both SDE-based diffusion models and ODE-based flow models under appropriate choices of H_t and β_t. The backbone is a WaveNet stack in which each block first applies a damped EMA (a special SSM computed via Vandermonde convolution) to capture local temporal dependencies, then applies time- and feature-oriented linear gated attention (chunked to O(kc) complexity) to capture global cross-channel structure. Modified samplers enforce the boundary constraints on h(t) at inference time by estimating X̃_0 from the current state and back-solving for the remaining coefficient, guaranteeing valid probability paths without extra training.

## Results

On probabilistic time series imputation, D3M (Constant-Sqrt) achieves the best RMSE of 0.438 (10% missing), 0.615 (50% missing), and 0.814 (90% missing) on PhysioNet, and 18.19 on Air Quality, reducing RMSE by up to 4.6% and CRPS by up to 4.3% over the strongest baselines (SSSD, SAITS). On probabilistic forecasting, D3M (Constant-Linear) achieves CRPS_sum of 0.208 on Solar and 0.034 on Traffic (13.6% and 18.9% improvements over prior best), while matching ScoreGrad on Electricity (0.0192 vs. 0.0192) and Taxi (0.097 vs. 0.102) using only 10 sampling steps versus hundreds for SDE-based methods. Inference time is reduced by up to 87.7% compared to CSDI and ~90% compared to ScoreGrad.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D3M outperforms all 17 baselines on the primary imputation task. Against the strongest probabilistic imputation baselines, D3M (Constant-Sqrt) beats SSSD by 4.6% RMSE (0.438 vs. 0.459) and 4.3% CRPS (0.223 vs. 0.233) at 10% missing on PhysioNet, and beats SAITS by 4.6% RMSE (0.438 vs. 0.461) at the same setting. Against CSDI (the most widely used diffusion-based imputation method), D3M reduces RMSE by 17.1% (0.438 vs. 0.531) at 10% missing while requiring only 10 sampling steps versus CSDI's 50–100 steps. Deterministic baselines (BRITS, RDIS, SSGAN, TIDER, SAITS) are consistently outperformed by 10–30% in RMSE, confirming the advantage of explicit probabilistic modeling. On forecasting, D3M (Constant-Linear) achieves the best CRPS_sum on Solar (0.208 vs. SSSD's 0.241) and Traffic (0.034 vs. SSSD's 0.037), and matches or beats ScoreGrad on Electricity (0.0192 vs. 0.0192), Taxi (0.097 vs. 0.102), and Exchange (0.006 vs. 0.006) with ~90% less inference time. Flow-based and VAE-based baselines (GP Scaling, GP Copula, Transformer-MAF, TimeGrad, TLAE, TimeLAR) are uniformly outperformed on both tasks, with D3M achieving 13.6–18.9% CRPS improvements on Solar and Traffic over the best non-diffusion methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

D3M repositions the design of SDE-based generative models by working directly from the explicit solution of linear SDEs rather than from drift/diffusion coefficients, decomposing the probability path into a 'signal dissipation' process (H_t) and a 'noise injection' process (β_t). Theorem 3.1 formally unifies this framework with both ScoreSDE-style diffusion models and Flow Matching-style ODE models, showing they are equivalent under the same probability path. For the sequence-modeling backbone, the paper departs from CSDI's quadratic self-attention by combining a damped EMA (viewed as a special SSM from MEGA) with linear gated attention (Hua et al., 2022) inside a WaveNet architecture, preserving inductive bias while reducing complexity to linear.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification theorem gives practitioners a single, more intuitive design space (choose H_t and β_t) that subsumes both SDE and ODE generative models, potentially simplifying the design of new probability paths. The practical 10-step inference (vs. 50–100+ for CSDI/CSBI) with comparable or slightly better CRPS is a meaningful efficiency win for deployment in monitoring and forecasting pipelines. Open questions include whether the framework extends naturally to non-Gaussian target distributions, whether the constraint-satisfying modified samplers generalize cleanly to more complex h(t) families, and whether the EMA + linear gated attention backbone scales to very long or highly irregular sequences beyond the 8 datasets tested.
