## Problem

Existing conditional moment restriction (CMR) estimators for instrumental variable regression approximate the population distribution either by reweighting empirical samples (φ-divergences) or by sampling additional points (MMD-based methods). Both paradigms are blind to the geometry of the data manifold, making them vulnerable to corrupted or adversarially perturbed data points. No prior IV estimator accounts for how the learning signal changes in the neighborhood of observed data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a finite sample of (treatment T, outcome Y, instrument Z) triples from the joint distribution P₀, where T, Y, Z take values in finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces. It outputs a causal function f(T→Y) parameterized either parametrically (e.g., a quadratic or neural network) or non-parametrically via an RKHS. A distinctive aspect is that the objective requires computing first and second derivatives (gradient and Laplacian) of the moment function with respect to the data variables, so the model must be differentiable in its inputs; in the kernel implementation, infinite transport cost is placed on Z to fix instruments at their empirical locations while allowing T and Y to vary.

## Method

SMM formulates IV regression as an empirical likelihood problem where the distance between the empirical distribution and the set of distributions satisfying the CMR is the entropic optimal transport (Sinkhorn) distance. A Lagrangian duality derivation (Theorem 3.1) yields a dual objective, and a leading-order Taylor expansion under a Gaussian reference measure (Theorem 3.2) produces a tractable expression involving the moment function, its data-gradient, and its data-Laplacian. A 2-stage GMM-style construction substitutes a first-stage estimate into the gradient term, shifting computational complexity to the instrument function h. The kernel implementation (Theorem 3.6) applies a representer theorem to eliminate the minimax over h, reducing the problem to a simple convex minimization over model parameters with a closed-form matrix expression. The geometry-awareness arises because maximizing over h promotes instrument functions that correspond to local minima of the moment violation, thereby down-weighting data points whose small perturbations cause large changes in the learning signal.

## Results

On the NetworkIV benchmark (n=1000, 20 random datasets), Kernel-SMM achieves prediction error of 0.15±0.01 (sin), 0.12±0.04 (abs), 0.04±0.00 (step), and 0.07±0.03 (linear), performing on par with SMD and VMM while substantially outperforming MMR and non-causal LSQ. In the corrupted-data experiment, SMM degrades most gracefully as the proportion of random covariates increases, maintaining lower MSE than all baselines at high corruption rates. Under FGSM adversarial attacks (ε up to 1.0), SMM demonstrates stronger robustness than SMD and VMM, though MMR (the λ→∞ limit) shows the least sensitivity due to its strong regularization. The Neural-SMM variant matches or slightly outperforms DeepGMM and NeuralFGEL across all four NetworkIV function variants (e.g., 0.07±0.01 vs. 0.08±0.01 on sin).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MMR (Zhang et al., 2023), SMM dominates on all four NetworkIV functions (e.g., 0.15 vs. 0.40 on sin; 0.04 vs. >100 on step), though MMR's strong regularization makes it the most adversarially robust in the FGSM experiment. Against SMD (Ai & Chen, 2003), SMM is competitive in the clean setting (SMD slightly better on sin at 0.12 vs. 0.15, SMM better on abs at 0.12 vs. 0.20) but clearly superior under data corruption, where SMM's MSE grows more slowly as the fraction of random covariates increases. Against VMM (Bennett & Kallus, 2023), the two methods are closely matched in the standard setting (VMM better on linear at 0.03 vs. 0.07, SMM better on step at 0.04 vs. 0.05), but SMM again shows an advantage in both the corrupted-data and adversarial-robustness experiments. In the neural-network comparison (Table 2), Neural-SMM ties or slightly beats DeepGMM (Bennett et al., 2019) on sin (0.07 vs. 0.08) and matches it on the remaining three functions, while also matching or slightly outperforming NeuralFGEL (Kremer et al., 2022) (0.07 vs. 0.10 on sin, 0.07 vs. 0.08 on step). Overall, SMM's geometry-awareness does not sacrifice standard-setting accuracy but provides a clear robustness advantage under data corruption and adversarial perturbations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the empirical likelihood framework for conditional moment restrictions (CMR) by replacing the φ-divergence-based distance (which restricts the population distribution to reweightings of the sample) with a regularized optimal transport (Sinkhorn) distance, allowing data points to be moved in the data space. The duality derivation (Theorem 3.1) is directly inspired by the Sinkhorn DRO method of Wang et al. (2023), while the 2-stage structure follows the classical GMM estimator of Hansen (1982). The key conceptual departure from prior CMR estimators (FGEL, VMM, MMR, DeepGMM) is the introduction of data-derivative information (gradient and Laplacian of the moment violation) through the Taylor expansion of the entropic OT objective, making SMM the first geometry-aware CMR estimator. The kernel-based closed-form solution (Theorem 3.6) avoids the minimax optimization that plagues neural-network-based CMR estimators, positioning it as a plug-and-play alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The geometry-aware learning signal provides a principled mechanism for robustness against data poisoning and adversarial perturbations in IV regression, a setting where such robustness has been largely unaddressed. The kernel-based implementation is practical and avoids the training instability of minimax estimators, though it limits scalability to large samples. The Neural-SMM variant is competitive with state-of-the-art deep CMR estimators (DeepGMM, Functional-GEL) on standard benchmarks, but its detailed theoretical analysis is deferred to future work. Open questions include whether the geometry-awareness extends to high-dimensional data manifolds (e.g., images) where the kernel method becomes intractable, and whether the robustness gains persist under more realistic corruption models beyond uniform random replacement.
