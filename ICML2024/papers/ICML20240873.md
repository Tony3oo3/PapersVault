## Problem

Exchange-based multimodal fusion methods (e.g., TokenFusion) that replace less informative tokens with inter-modal features suffer from irreversible information loss and consistently underperform cross-attention mechanisms. However, full cross-attention introduces quadratic O(N²) computational complexity that becomes prohibitive for the large token counts typical in vision transformer backbones (e.g., 16,384 patches per modality), creating a gap between fusion quality and computational feasibility.

## Input / Output

The method requires spatially aligned multimodal inputs (RGB, depth, event, LiDAR) where each modality is converted to an image-like format with pixel-wise correspondence, at resolutions ranging from 480×480 (SUN RGB-D) to 1024×1024 (DeLiVER) for segmentation and 512×512 for image-to-image translation. All modalities share encoder parameters (except LayerNorm), and the module operates on patch tokens at each spatial position. Outputs include per-pixel segmentation masks (40 classes on NYUDv2, 37 on SUN RGB-D, 25 on DeLiVER), translated RGB/depth/normal maps, or 3D vehicle bounding boxes on KITTI.

## Method

GeminiFusion restricts cross-modal interaction to spatially co-located token pairs, reducing attention complexity from O(N²·c) to O(N·c²) (a 99.2% FLOPs reduction from 17G to 0.14G per layer). Within each pixel position, it unifies intra-modal self-attention and inter-modal cross-attention into a single attention operation where keys and values are concatenated from both the same-modality and cross-modality projections. A lightweight relation discriminator (1×1 convolution + softmax) computes a per-pixel relation score that modulates the cross-modal key, preventing the model from disproportionately attending to self-similar patches. Layer-adaptive learnable noise is added to the self-attention keys and values to counteract the inherent self-referential bias and ensure the softmax produces meaningful differentiation across the two attention branches. The module is plug-and-play, insertable into SegFormer or Swin Transformer blocks, and leverages standard ImageNet pre-trained weights without degradation.

## Results

On multimodal semantic segmentation, GeminiFusion with MiT-B5 backbone achieves 57.7% mIoU on NYUDv2, 53.3% on SUN RGB-D, and 66.9% on DeLiVER (RGB+D+E+L), outperforming the closest fair-comparison baseline CMNeXt (56.9/50.4/66.3). With the larger Swin-Large-22k backbone, GeminiFusion reaches 60.2% mIoU on NYUDv2 and 54.6% on SUN RGB-D. On the Taskonomy image-to-image translation task, GeminiFusion reduces FID by 5.99–6.89 points over TokenFusion (e.g., 41.32 vs 47.31 for Shade+Texture→RGB). On KITTI 3D object detection, inserting GeminiFusion into MVX-Net improves 3D AP_R11 (Easy) from 87.49 to 88.49 with only 1M additional parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among methods without additional pre-training strategies, GeminiFusion (MiT-B5) outperforms TokenFusion by +2.6% mIoU on NYUDv2 and +1.5% on SUN RGB-D, and by +2.7% to +3.4% on DeLiVER across all modality combinations. It also surpasses CMNeXt by +0.8% on NYUDv2, +2.9% on SUN RGB-D, and +0.6% on DeLiVER, and beats CMX by +0.8% and +0.9% on NYUDv2 and SUN RGB-D respectively. Against DFformer (which uses an RGB-D pre-trained backbone), GeminiFusion leads by +0.5% on NYUDv2 and +0.8% on SUN RGB-D. Methods that employ additional strategies (PolyMaX, EMSANet, DPLNet, OmniVec) report higher NYUDv2 mIoU (58.1–60.8%), but these rely on ImageNet-22K pre-training, self-supervised pre-training, or multi-dataset fine-tuning; GeminiFusion with Swin-Large-22k (60.2%) narrows this gap to within 0.6% of the best without any such strategy. On 3D object detection, GeminiFusion improves MVX-Net's 3D AP_R11 (Easy) from 87.49 to 88.49 with only 1M extra parameters, confirming its plug-and-play utility beyond segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two dominant multimodal fusion paradigms for vision transformers: exchange-based methods (TokenFusion, CEN) that substitute low-information tokens across modalities, and interaction-based methods (cross-attention) that compute full pairwise interactions. It empirically critiques the exchange-based line, showing that selective token pruning-and-substitution underperforms even unconditional full exchange, and that cross-attention consistently outperforms both. GeminiFusion then carves a middle path by restricting cross-attention to spatially co-located token pairs, inheriting the information-preserving properties of attention while achieving linear complexity, and is designed as a plug-and-play module compatible with SegFormer, Swin Transformer, and MVX-Net backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 99.2% FLOPs reduction (17G → 0.14G per cross-attention instance) while matching or exceeding full cross-attention performance makes efficient multimodal fusion practical for long-sequence and real-time settings, and the plug-and-play design with shared parameters enables direct reuse of unimodal ImageNet pre-training. A key limitation is that the method requires spatially aligned, homogeneous modalities (RGB, depth, LiDAR, events) and does not extend to heterogeneous pairs like image-text or image-audio, which the authors explicitly flag as future work. The empirical finding that TokenFusion's selective exchange is inferior to blanket exchange challenges the design assumption behind a line of parameter-free fusion methods and may redirect attention toward attention-based alternatives.
