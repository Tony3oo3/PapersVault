## Problem

Existing robust learning methods for corrupted training data require either a known or upper-bounded estimate of the corruption level ε, which is impractical in online or streaming settings where noise varies dynamically across batches. This paper eliminates that dependency by formulating robust learning as a variational inference problem over latent Bernoulli variables, automatically inferring ε from the data without any user-specified hyperparameter.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240929/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts any training set (images at 32×32 for CIFAR, 28×28 for MNIST, or tabular feature vectors) with potentially corrupted labels, processed either as a full batch or as streaming mini-batches of size b. It produces robust model parameters θ, an automatically inferred corruption level ε = 1 − mean(π), and per-sample non-corruption probabilities π_i ∈ (0,1) that serve as adaptive loss weights. In the deep learning variant, samples with π_i below a dynamically computed threshold τ are hard-truncated (set to zero) to prevent overfitting to corrupted labels.

## Method

RLVI introduces a latent Bernoulli variable t_i per sample under the Huber contamination model, then maximizes the marginal likelihood p(Z|θ,ε) by optimizing a variational lower bound (ELBO) with a factorized Bernoulli approximation r(t|π). The ELBO decomposes into a weighted negative log-likelihood Σ π_i ℓ_θ(z_i) plus a KL divergence term, and the corruption level is recovered in closed form as ε = 1 − ⟨π⟩, removing the need for any noise-level hyperparameter. The optimization proceeds via an EM scheme: the E-step solves for π_i using O(b) fixed-point iterations (exploiting convexity in π), and the M-step performs standard parameter updates (weighted least squares, cross-entropy minimization, or SGD with π-weighted gradients). For overparameterized deep networks, a truncation regularization is added: after overfitting is detected via validation accuracy drop, samples with π_i < τ are excluded from gradient updates, where τ is the largest threshold satisfying a 5% type-II-error bound on misclassified corrupted samples.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 45% symmetric label noise, RLVI achieves 88.69% test accuracy, outperforming the next-best method JoCoR (86.33%) and BARE (69.90%). On CIFAR-100 under the same conditions, RLVI reaches 64.11% vs. JoCoR's 62.37% and BARE's 46.56%. On the out-of-distribution benchmark CIFAR80N-O (45% symmetric noise), RLVI attains 63.18% vs. JoCoR's 62.29%. On MNIST, RLVI is competitive with BARE (98.70% vs. 98.78% at 45% symmetric noise), while on standard learning benchmarks (linear/logistic regression, PCA) RLVI achieves the lowest average relative error with tighter confidence intervals than SEVER, RRM, and HUBER.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLVI clearly outperforms all six deep-learning baselines (Co-teaching, JoCoR, CDR, USDNL, BARE, and standard SGD) on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and CIFAR80N-O across all four noise types at both 20% and 45% corruption levels; the margin is largest under pairflip and instance noise, where BARE drops to 57.18% on CIFAR-10 while RLVI maintains 89.13%. On MNIST, RLVI is essentially tied with BARE (98.70% vs. 98.78% at 45% symmetric) and slightly ahead of JoCoR, but BARE retains a small edge under pairflip noise. In the standard learning benchmarks (Section 4.1), RLVI achieves lower average relative error and tighter confidence intervals than SEVER, RRM, and HUBER on linear regression, logistic regression, and PCA. In the online learning experiment (Section 4.2), RLVI consistently outperforms both standard SGD and RRM in recall as the corruption level varies per batch, without requiring a pre-specified ε upper bound. A key practical advantage is that RLVI requires no hyperparameter tuning for the corruption level, whereas Co-teaching, JoCoR, CDR, and USDNL all depend on a schedule derived from a known ε; on the real-world Food101 dataset, RLVI achieves the best test accuracy without any ε specification, while the other methods peak only when ε is manually set to ~3%.

## Relation to Prior Work

RLVI is a specific, computationally efficient instantiation of the latent-variable robust modeling framework introduced by Wang et al. (2017), who proposed raising likelihood terms to latent variables and inferring them jointly with model parameters via probabilistic programming. Here, the latent variables are restricted to Bernoulli indicators under the Huber contamination model, which yields a closed-form ELBO and an EM-style algorithm requiring no hyperparameter specification. This departs from SEVER and RRM, which still require an upper-bound estimate ε̃ ≥ ε, and from deep-learning robust training methods (Co-teaching, JoCoR, CDR, USDNL, BARE) that bake ε into a sample-selection schedule. The variational inference machinery (ELBO, KL regularization) is standard, but its application to the Huber mixture with Bernoulli latents produces the key property that ε is inferred automatically as 1 − mean(π).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter-free nature of RLVI removes a persistent practical bottleneck: most existing robust learning methods require the user to specify or upper-bound the corruption level, which is infeasible in streaming or real-world settings where noise is unknown and non-stationary. The method's minimal computational overhead (O(b) vectorized operations per SGD step) and its applicability across standard ML, online learning, and deep learning make it a broadly deployable drop-in replacement for standard likelihood maximization. Open questions include whether the 5% type-II-error threshold for truncation is optimal across architectures, whether the Huber mixture assumption is sufficient for structured or adversarial corruption, and how the method behaves when the corrupting distribution q(z) is highly concentrated (near-duplicate outliers). The unbounded-likelihood pathology (Appendix D) also suggests that for certain model classes, a minimum-sample constraint n₀ is still needed, partially reintroducing a hyperparameter.
