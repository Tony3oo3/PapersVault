## Problem

While alternating gradient descent-ascent (Alt-GDA) is empirically observed to converge faster than simultaneous GDA (Sim-GDA) in minimax optimization, prior theoretical results only provided local convergence guarantees that are valid after enough iterations. This paper addresses the fundamental question of whether alternating updates are strictly better than simultaneous updates in terms of global convergence rates, and proposes a new algorithm that further exploits alternating extrapolation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241056/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithms take a twice-differentiable SCSC objective f(x,y) with (L_x, L_y, L_{xy})-Lipschitz gradients, an initial point (x_0, y_0), and constant step sizes α, β (plus extrapolation hyperparameters γ, δ for Alex-GDA). They produce a Nash equilibrium (x*, y*) with explicit iteration complexity bounds expressed in terms of fine-grained condition numbers κ_x = L_x/μ_x, κ_y = L_y/μ_y, and κ_{xy} = L_{xy}/√(μ_x μ_y). Alex-GDA requires only two gradient evaluations per iteration (one for x, one for y), the same as Sim-GDA and Alt-GDA, and half the cost of Extra-gradient.

## Method

The paper provides tight global convergence analyses via Lyapunov functions, showing Sim-GDA requires Θ(κ_x + κ_y + κ_{xy}²) iterations while Alt-GDA requires only O(κ_x + κ_y + κ_{xy}(√κ_x + √κ_y)), proving Alt-GDA is strictly faster. The key new algorithm, Alex-GDA, generalizes both Sim-GDA (γ=0) and Alt-GDA (γ=1) by computing gradients at extrapolated points x̃_{k+1} = (1-γ)x_k + γx_{k+1} and ỹ_{k+1} = (1-δ)y_k + δy_{k+1} with γ, δ > 1, achieving a tighter Θ(κ_x + κ_y + κ_{xy}) rate that matches Extra-gradient while using half the gradient computations. The Lyapunov function for Alex-GDA incorporates cross-terms involving both current and previous iterates to capture the two-step alternating structure. Additionally, Alex-GDA provably converges linearly on bilinear problems (with rate O((L_{xy}/μ_{xy})² log(1/ε))) where both Sim-GDA and Alt-GDA diverge, and achieves state-of-the-art FID scores on WGAN-GP image generation tasks.

## Results

Theoretically, the paper proves tight iteration complexity bounds: Sim-GDA requires Θ((κ_x + κ_y + κ_xy²)·log(1/ε)), Alt-GDA achieves O((κ_x + κ_y + κ_xy(√κ_x + √κ_y))·log(1/ε)), and the proposed Alex-GDA achieves Θ((κ_x + κ_y + κ_xy)·log(1/ε)), matching the Extra-gradient rate with half the gradient calls per iteration. On (100+100)-dimensional SCSC quadratic games, Alex-GDA (with momentum) requires 44.7–83.9 gradient computations to reach ε-accuracy, versus 1974–416,822 for Sim-GDA and 105–777 for Alt-GDA. On WGAN-GP image generation, Alex-Adam achieves FID scores of 1.53 (MNIST), 23.8 (CIFAR-10), and 6.3 (LSUN Bedroom 64×64), outperforming Sim-Adam (3.97, 45.0, 131.2) and Alt-Adam (1.85, 24.2, 9.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Sim-GDA, Alex-GDA is provably faster: the dependence on the cross-condition number κ_xy is linear (κ_xy) rather than quadratic (κ_xy²), yielding a strict theoretical gap. Against Alt-GDA, Alex-GDA improves the κ_xy dependence from κ_xy(√κ_x + √κ_y) to simply κ_xy, and empirically requires roughly 2–5× fewer gradient computations across all tested problem configurations. Against Extra-gradient (EG), Alex-GDA achieves an identical iteration complexity rate Θ(κ_x + κ_y + κ_xy) but requires only 2 gradient evaluations per iteration versus EG's 4, making it strictly more efficient in gradient-computation complexity. Against Optimistic Gradient Descent (OGD), Alex-GDA matches the same theoretical rate and, on bilinear problems, achieves an optimal rate exponent √((L_xy²−μ_xy²)/(L_xy²+μ_xy²)) that is 6× faster than OGD's exponent. In the GAN experiments, Alex-Adam outperforms both Sim-Adam and Alt-Adam on all three datasets, with the largest gap on LSUN Bedroom (FID 6.3 vs. 131.2 and 9.0 respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GDA framework (Dem'yanov & Pevnyi, 1972) by providing the first global convergence analysis that rigorously separates the roles of individual condition numbers (κ_x, κ_y, κ_xy) for Sim-GDA and Alt-GDA, going beyond the local convergence guarantees of Zhang et al. (2022). It departs from prior work by proposing Alex-GDA, a new algorithm that unifies Sim-GDA and Alt-GDA through linear extrapolation of iterates (γ, δ > 1), achieving the same iteration complexity as Extra-gradient (Korpelevich, 1976) while requiring only half the gradient evaluations per step. The Lyapunov function technique from stability analysis is adapted to handle the two-step alternating structure, which is the key technical novelty enabling the global analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing theory-practice gap by proving that alternating updates are strictly superior to simultaneous updates in global convergence, not just locally. Alex-GDA's ability to converge on bilinear problems—where both Sim-GDA and Alt-GDA diverge—has direct practical implications for GAN training and other adversarial settings. Open questions remain: whether Alt-GDA can achieve the O(κ) rate suggested by local analysis (Conjecture 8.1 hints it cannot), and whether Alex-GDA extends to general convex-concave objectives (Conjecture 8.2). The PEP-based estimate of O(κ^1.4) for Alt-GDA suggests the proven upper bound may not be tight, leaving room for improved analyses.
