## Problem

Existing structured compression methods for LLMs apply the same compression strategy uniformly across all Transformer sub-layers, ignoring that MHA and FFN sub-layers exhibit fundamentally different structural properties. This paper identifies that MHA weight matrices possess a pronounced low-rank structure while FFN matrices do not, and asks whether a differentiated compression approach—matching the technique to each sub-layer's inherent properties—can outperform uniform strategies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241151/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weights (LLaMA-1/2, Vicuna at 7B and 13B scale) along with 128 calibration text samples of 128 tokens each from BookCorpus, used solely to compute input activation norms for importance scoring. The output is a structurally compressed model where MHA projection matrices are replaced by low-rank factorizations (L_r × R_r) and FFN intermediate channels are pruned at the group level, optionally followed by LoRA fine-tuning on ~50k Alpaca samples to recover performance. The embedding layer and LM head are left unmodified, so the layer-level compression ratio is slightly higher than the model-level target.

## Method

LoRAP applies two distinct compression techniques to different Transformer sub-layers based on an empirical observation that MHA matrices retain 80% SVD energy with far fewer singular values than FFN matrices (e.g., 16.6% vs. 53.4% for Wq vs. Wgate). For MHA, it performs Activation Weighted SVD (AWSVD), which right-multiplies the weight matrix by a diagonal matrix of input activation L2 norms before SVD, yielding a stronger low-rank structure; parameters are then allocated unevenly (75% to Wv/Wo, 25% to Wq/Wk) to match each matrix's low-rank degree. For FFN, it performs gradient-free channel pruning by computing per-weight importance as |W_ij|·‖X_j‖₂, aggregating to channel importance via L2 norm, and pruning at the group level (simultaneously removing the i-th row of Wup, Wgate and the i-th column of Wdown). A key finding is that retaining the least important 1% of FFN parameters—rather than pruning them—yields a significant perplexity reduction, explained by the Junk DNA Hypothesis. Knowledge recovery is achieved by LoRA fine-tuning (rank 8, 2 epochs) on the compressed weights.

## Results

On LLaMA-7B at 20% compression without fine-tuning, LoRAP achieves 15.69 WikiText2 perplexity and 60.53% average accuracy across seven commonsense reasoning tasks, outperforming LLM-Pruner (19.09 PPL, 56.69% avg) and LoRAPrune (20.67 PPL, 56.50% avg). At 50% compression without fine-tuning, the gap widens dramatically: LoRAP reaches 56.96 WikiText2 PPL versus 112.44 (LLM-Pruner) and 121.96 (LoRAPrune), while maintaining 47.25% average accuracy versus 42.13% and 41.87%. After LoRA fine-tuning at 50% compression, LoRAP achieves 30.90 PPL and 52.17% average accuracy, surpassing all baselines by 1.8–3.5% in accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRAP consistently outperforms all three structured compression baselines (LLM-Pruner, LoRAPrune, LoRAShear) across both 20% and 50% compression ratios, with or without LoRA fine-tuning. At 20% compression without fine-tuning, LoRAP reduces WikiText2 perplexity by 3.4–4.98 points over LLM-Pruner and LoRAPrune, and gains 3.8–4.03% in average reasoning accuracy. At 50% compression without fine-tuning, the advantage is most dramatic: LoRAP achieves 56.96 PPL versus 112.44 (LLM-Pruner) and 121.96 (LoRAPrune), nearly halving the perplexity while also leading in accuracy by 5.1–5.4%. After fine-tuning at 50%, LoRAP still leads by 3.5% over LLM-Pruner and 2.5% over LoRAPrune in average accuracy, and by 1.8% over LoRAShear. In the sub-layer ablation (Table 8), LoRAP's AWSVD method achieves 19.49 WikiText2 PPL on MHA versus 25.09 for AFM* and 76.54 for SVD*, while its channel pruning achieves 32.64 PPL on FFN versus 84.15 for LoSparse and 126.13 for AFM, confirming that the differentiated strategy (low-rank for MHA, pruning for FFN) is the key source of the overall advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior structured compression methods (LLM-Pruner, LoRAPrune, LoRAShear) that apply a uniform compression strategy across all Transformer sub-layers, instead proposing differentiated treatment based on the observation that MHA exhibits stronger low-rank structure than FFN. The MHA compression (AWSVD) builds on Wanda's activation-based importance scoring and FWSVD's weighted SVD framework, while the FFN compression adapts LLM-Pruner's dependency-group structure and LoSparse's channel importance aggregation into a gradient-free channel pruning scheme. The knowledge recovery phase reuses LoRA fine-tuning, consistent with the broader trend of combining compression with parameter-efficient adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The differentiated compression strategy offers a practical, gradient-free pipeline for LLM compression that outperforms uniform approaches at multiple compression ratios across LLaMA-1/2 and Vicuna models, with the 50% ratio showing particularly large gains (e.g., 56.96 vs. 112.44 WikiText2 perplexity without fine-tuning). The finding that retaining the least important 1% of FFN parameters yields disproportionate performance recovery connects to the Junk DNA hypothesis and suggests that 'unimportant' weights encode task-critical knowledge. However, the method applies a uniform compression ratio across all layers (acknowledged as a limitation), and performance still degrades substantially above 40% compression even after fine-tuning, leaving open the question of layer-adaptive or task-aware compression budgets.
