## Problem

Existing RL domain-generalization methods (e.g., LUSR, DARLA, CURL) lack explicit semantic constraints on the feature extractor, causing learned representations to retain domain bias and degrade sharply on unseen domains. Additionally, these approaches require large amounts of multi-domain data, which is often impractical. The paper asks whether semantic information from a pretrained vision-language model can serve as an explicit, data-efficient constraint to learn a unified cross-domain visual representation.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a 512×512 RGB image from a top-mounted camera in CARLA plus the current vehicle velocity, and outputs a 2D continuous action (acceleration ∈ (−3,3), steering ∈ (−0.3,0.3)). The visual aligner itself is trained without paired source-target images; instead, it is supervised by matching the CLIP visual embedding of the transferred image against the CLIP text embedding of a domain-specific prompt, requiring only 100 images per domain from two training domains. The aligned image is then fed through a pretrained autoencoder feature extractor before the PPO policy head.

## Method

PVA is a three-stage framework. First, a prompt learner decomposes semantic information into three learnable components—global (task-level), domain-specific (per-domain), and instance-conditional (per-image, generated by a ResNet18+MLP network)—and tunes them via CLIP contrastive losses (L_domain and L_ins) so that each prompt accurately describes its image. Second, a UNet visual aligner g_θ maps any input image to a pre-selected unified domain, optimized by a composite loss: global CLIP text-image cosine similarity, patch-level CLIP matching with threshold rejection, and VGG feature preservation loss. Third, PPO trains the policy on aligned images from a single domain. The key insight is that CLIP's pretrained text-image alignment provides an explicit semantic anchor that prevents the aligner from discarding task-relevant information while removing domain-specific appearance, enabling zero-shot transfer with only ~200 cross-domain images.

## Results

On the CARLA autonomous driving task, PVA achieves cumulative rewards of 2178.4 (ClearSunset), 1775.7 (WetCloudySunset), and 1789.2 (SoftRainSunset) on three unseen weather domains, substantially outperforming all baselines. On seen domains, PVA scores 2004.6 (ClearNoon) and 1825.9 (HardRainNoon), matching or exceeding the best baselines. With only 100 images per domain for prompt tuning and visual alignment, PVA still achieves 1825.9 on ClearSunset versus 199.5 (CURL) and 313.9 (AdaIN) under the same limited-data setting (Table 2). In Mujoco tasks (Appendix D), PVA outperforms CURL by 40–200% across cartpole-balance, cheetah-run, and point-mass.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PVA dominates all seven baselines across every seen and unseen domain in CARLA. Against representation-learning methods, PVA outperforms LUSR by 1350 points on HardRainNoon (1825.9 vs 775.6) and by 1255 points on SoftRainSunset (1789.2 vs 533.8); it also beats DARLA by up to 1613 points (WetCloudySunset: 1775.7 vs 162.5) and CURL by 1180 points (ClearSunset: 2178.4 vs 801.0). Against image-to-image translation baselines, the gap is even more pronounced: PVA exceeds UNIT4RL by 1880 points on ClearSunset (2178.4 vs 298.1), CycleGAN by 1901 points (2178.4 vs 277.0), pix2pix by 2021 points (2178.4 vs 157.5), and AdaIN by 1676 points (2178.4 vs 499.9). Under the limited-data protocol (100 images per domain, Table 2), PVA still achieves 1825.9 on ClearSunset while CURL drops to 199.5 and AdaIN to 313.9, confirming that the explicit semantic constraint from VLM prompts is critical when cross-domain data is scarce. In the Mujoco validation (Table 5), PVA consistently outperforms CURL by 80–576 points across three tasks, demonstrating the framework's generality beyond driving.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt tuning paradigm from CoOp/CoCoOp (learnable text tokens for VLMs) into the RL domain generalization setting, using CLIP as a semantic bridge to constrain an image-to-image aligner (UNet). It departs from representation-learning baselines (LUSR, DARLA, CURL) that rely on unsupervised VAE or contrastive objectives without explicit semantic constraints, and from GAN-based translation methods (CycleGAN, pix2pix, UNIT4RL) that require paired data or unstable adversarial training. The key conceptual move is treating a VLM's text-visual alignment as a differentiable supervision signal for training a domain-agnostic image mapper, rather than learning domain-invariant latent codes directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that zero-shot policy transfer in vision-based RL can be achieved with only ~100 images per domain by leveraging pretrained VLM semantic structure, substantially reducing the data burden compared to prior methods that require thousands of cross-domain samples. However, the evaluation is confined to a single CARLA driving task with one VLM (CLIP), and the approach inherits CLIP's limitations in fine-grained spatial understanding. Open questions include whether the method scales to more complex multi-object scenes, whether alternative VLMs or smaller models suffice, and whether the alignment stage can be made online or adaptive during policy training rather than fixed beforehand.
