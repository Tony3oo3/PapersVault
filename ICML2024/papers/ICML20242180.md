## Problem

While near-optimal coreset constructions exist for single-response ℓ_p regression, no prior work achieved coresets for multiple ℓ_p regression (m simultaneous response vectors) with size sublinear in m. The paper constructs the first dimension-free (independent of m) strong and weak coresets for this problem, with sample complexity matching the single-response case up to polylogarithmic factors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a design matrix A ∈ ℝ^{n×d}, a target matrix B ∈ ℝ^{n×m}, and optionally an embedding matrix G ∈ ℝ^{t×m} (for constrained regression), and produces a random diagonal sampling matrix S with nnz(S) = Õ(ε^{-2}d) for p<2 or Õ(ε^{-p}d^{p/2}) for p>2. The guarantee is that ||S(AX−B)||_{p,p}^p = (1±ε)||AX−B||_{p,p}^p simultaneously for every X ∈ ℝ^{d×m} (strong) or that the minimizer is preserved (weak). As applications, the framework yields spanning coresets of size Õ(ε^{-1}k) for ℓ_p subspace approximation and sublinear uniform sampling algorithms for Euclidean power means.

## Method

The core technique generalizes the partition-by-sensitivity argument from active ℓ_p regression to a multi-scale strong coreset setting: rather than preserving ||Ax−b||_p^p directly, the algorithm preserves the difference ||S(Ax−b)||_p^p − ||Sb||_p^p at each scale R, using ℓ_p Lewis weight sampling with probabilities q_i ≥ min{1, w_i/α + v_i/β}. To remove the m-dependence, the proof sets failure probability to poly(εδ) per column, handles the small fraction of 'bad' columns via Markov's inequality and the subspace embedding property of S, and combines with the (1±ε) guarantee on 'good' columns. For weak coresets with an embedding G, an iterative argument alternates between a closeness-to-optimum bound (via strong convexity for p<2 or Bregman divergence for p>2) and an approximation bound, improving the ε-dependence from ε^{-2} to ε^{-1} over O(log log 1/ε) iterations. Applications to power means and subspace approximation use Dvoretzky's theorem for ℓ_p norms to reduce (p,2)-norm problems to entrywise ℓ_p regression.

## Results

The paper constructs the first strong coresets for multiple ℓ_p regression with sample complexity independent of the number of responses m: Õ(ε^{-2} d) for p < 2 and Õ(ε^{-p} d^{p/2}) for p > 2. For weak coresets (preserving only the minimizer), the bounds improve by an ε factor to Õ(ε^{-1} d) for 1 < p < 2 and Õ(ε^{-(p-1)} d^{p/2}) for p > 2. Matching lower bounds of Ω(ε^{-p} d^{p/2}) (strong) and Ω(ε^{1-p} d^{p/2}) (weak) are shown for p > 2, establishing a tight ε-factor separation between the two guarantees. As applications, the paper settles the sample complexity of uniform sampling for Euclidean power means at Õ(ε^{-2}) for p=1, Õ(ε^{-1}) for 1<p<2, and Õ(ε^{1-p}) for p>2, and gives the first Õ(ε^{-1} k) spanning coreset for ℓ_p subspace approximation for 1<p<2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For strong coresets, the paper's main contribution is extending the single-response Lewis weight sampling bounds (Cohen & Peng 2015) to the multiple-response setting with no dependence on m, which was previously open. For p > 2, the new lower bound Ω(ε^{-p} d^{p/2}) (Theorem 5.1) matches the upper bound and reveals a separation from the single-response case where only Ω(ε^{-1} d^{p/2} + ε^{-2} d) was known. For weak coresets, the paper's Õ(ε^{-1} d) bound for 1 < p < 2 matches the best known active regression bounds (Musco et al. 2022; Woodruff & Yasuda 2023b) but now handles the embedding matrix G that couples columns, a setting where prior column-wise strategies fail. For spanning coresets, the Õ(ε^{-1} k) bound for 1 < p < 2 improves the prior best of O(ε^{-1} k² log(k/ε)) (Shyamalkumar & Varadarajan 2012) by a factor of k/ε, and the new Ω(ε^{-1} k) lower bound (Theorem 5.3) shows this is tight up to polylog factors. For Euclidean power means, the paper's uniform sampling bounds match all known lower bounds, closing the gap left by Cohen-Addad et al. (2021) whose upper bound of Õ(ε^{-(p+3)}) was off by ε^4 from the lower bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ℓ_p Lewis weight sampling framework (Cohen & Peng, 2015) and the partition-by-sensitivity technique from active ℓ_p regression (Musco et al., 2022) to the multiple-response setting, where m target vectors must be simultaneously approximated. The key departure is that for p≠2, the Pythagorean-theorem argument that makes the p=2 extension trivial breaks down, requiring a novel multi-scale generalization of the sensitivity partition combined with a 'good/bad columns' argument using Markov's inequality to eliminate the m-dependence. The weak coreset proof additionally builds on the iterative size-reduction argument of Woodruff & Yasuda (2023b), alternating between approximation and closeness-to-optimum bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper settles the sample complexity of Euclidean power means up to polylog factors, answering an open question of Cohen-Addad, Saulpic, and Schwiegelshohn, and provides the first nearly-optimal spanning coresets for ℓ_p subspace approximation. The conceptual finding that multiple ℓ_p regression is as easy as single-response for p<2 (dimension-free in m) but exhibits a separation for p>2 is a clean structural insight. Open questions remain: a gap persists for p>2 strong coresets (upper bound ε^{-2}d^{p/2} vs. lower bound ε^{-p}d^{p/2}), no nontrivial lower bounds exist for weak coresets of single-response ℓ_p regression, and spanning coresets for p>2 remain unresolved.
