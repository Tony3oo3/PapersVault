## Problem

Stochastic localization (SL) offers a principled framework for sampling from unnormalized target densities by progressively denoising a stochastic observation process, but prior work lacked a practical, general-purpose methodology for arbitrary targets. The central obstacle is estimating the SL denoiser without access to samples from the target, and no existing approach systematically addressed the trade-off between when the posterior becomes tractable for MCMC and when the marginal remains log-concave for initialization.

## Input / Output

The method requires only the unnormalized target density (and its gradient for MALA), the scalar variance R_π² of the target (or an estimate), and a small set of schedule hyperparameters (denoising schedule type, number of SDE steps K, number of MCMC samples M, and log-SNR threshold η). The sole hyperparameter requiring careful tuning is the initial integration time t_0, which must lie in a 'sweet spot' where both the posterior and the marginal are approximately log-concave. The output is a single approximate sample Y_T^α/α(T) from the target, and multiple samples are obtained by running independent parallel realizations.

## Method

SLIPS integrates a discretized SDE whose drift is the SL denoiser, estimated at each step by running MALA on the random posterior q_t^α(x|Y_t^α) and taking the empirical mean. The key insight is the 'duality of log-concavity': the posterior must be log-concave (requiring t > t_q) for MCMC-based denoiser estimation to mix well, while the marginal p_t^α must be log-concave (requiring t < t_p) for the Langevin-within-Langevin initialization to be reliable; SLIPS starts integration at a t_0 satisfying both conditions. The time discretization is SNR-adapted rather than uniform, allocating smaller steps where the log-SNR changes rapidly. The initialization uses a nested Langevin scheme where each outer ULA step on p_{t_0}^α requires an inner MALA loop on q_{t_0}^α to estimate the score via Tweedie's formula. The paper also introduces flexible geometric denoising schedules (Geom-∞ and Geom) that generalize the standard linear SL process and allow tuning of the SNR profile shape.

## Results

On toy distributions (8-Gaussians, Rings, Funnel), SLIPS achieves the best or near-best entropic regularized 2-Wasserstein / sliced KS distances, e.g. 0.74 on 8-Gaussians (vs. 0.91 for OAT, 1.01 for RDMC) and 0.024 on Funnel (vs. 0.035 for SMC, 0.082 for RDMC). On Bayesian logistic regression posteriors (Sonar d=34, Ionosphere d=61), SLIPS achieves the highest predictive log-likelihood (−109.14 and −86.32 respectively), outperforming SMC (−111.02, −87.82), AIS (−111.04, −87.92), RDMC (−129.88, −109.84), and OAT (−280.92, −205.49). In high-dimensional bimodal Gaussian mixtures (d up to 100), SLIPS recovers the relative mode weight at 1% accuracy while all other methods fail. On the 1D φ⁴ field system (d=100), only SLIPS produces correct mode-weight estimates matching Laplace approximations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLIPS clearly outperforms all four baselines on the 8-Gaussians distribution (0.74–0.76 vs. 0.91–1.10) and the Funnel distribution (0.024–0.040 vs. 0.035–0.105), with the largest margins against OAT and RDMC. On Rings, SLIPS is competitive with AIS and OAT (all around 0.18–0.22) but clearly beats SMC (0.28) and RDMC (0.30). On the Bayesian logistic regression posteriors, SLIPS achieves the best predictive log-likelihood on both Sonar and Ionosphere, with a particularly large gap over OAT (−109 vs. −281 on Sonar) and RDMC (−109 vs. −130 on Sonar), while AIS and SMC are only marginally worse (−111). In the high-dimensional Gaussian mixture experiments (d up to 100), SLIPS is the only method that recovers the relative mode weight at 1% accuracy; AIS, SMC, OAT, and RDMC all fail to give accurate estimates in most dimensions. On the φ⁴ field system (d=100), only SLIPS produces correct samples, with other methods' results omitted due to high estimation error.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Stochastic Localization (SL) framework from geometric measure theory (Eldan, 2013; 2020) and its recent density-based sampling applications (Alaoui et al., 2022; Montanari, 2023) by introducing flexible denoising schedules inspired by diffusion model noise scheduling (Nichol & Dhariwal, 2021; Kingma et al., 2021). It departs from neural-network-based variational inference approaches (Zhang & Chen, 2022; Vargas et al., 2023a) by adopting a learning-free MCMC estimation of the denoiser, similar in spirit to RDMC (Huang et al., 2024) but formalizing the critical 'duality of log-concavity' trade-off that determines the initialization time. The paper also positions itself against the Multi-Noise Measurements (OAT) approach (Saremi et al., 2024), noting that OAT faces but does not explicitly resolve the same log-concavity tension.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SLIPS provides a practical, learning-free alternative to neural-network-based VI methods for sampling from unnormalized densities, with the duality of log-concavity offering a clear conceptual framework for understanding why naive initialization fails and how to choose the starting point. The high-dimensional Gaussian mixture results (accurate mode-weight estimation up to d=1000) demonstrate that MCMC-based SL can outperform both RDMC and OAT in challenging multi-modal settings. However, theoretical guarantees require the restrictive assumption dR²/τ² < 2, the initialization time t₀ still requires manual tuning, and the method's scalability to very high dimensions or highly non-log-concave targets remains an open question. Future work on tighter duality guarantees and adaptive schedule design could broaden the method's applicability.
