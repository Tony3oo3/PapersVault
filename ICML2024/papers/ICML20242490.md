## Problem

Existing graph sparsification methods for GNNs fall into two disconnected camps: topology-guided sparsifiers preserve spectral properties but ignore GNN training dynamics, while semantic-guided sparsifiers track edge importance during training but suffer severe performance collapse at high sparsity due to disrupted graph connectivity. No prior method dynamically manipulates sparsity at the data level while simultaneously preserving both topological integrity and semantic alignment with the original graph.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242490/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

GST takes a graph G = {A, X} (adjacency matrix and node feature matrix), a GNN backbone, and a target sparsity percentage s_g as input. It produces a binary edge mask M_g ∈ {0,1}^{N×N} defining a sparse subgraph G^sub = {M_g ⊙ A, X} that achieves the target sparsity while maintaining GNN performance within a tolerance ε. The method also internally constructs an anchor graph (dense mask + model output from limited full-graph training) that serves as the supervisory reference throughout dynamic sparse training.

## Method

GST operates in three phases: (1) limited full-graph training co-optimizes a 2-layer MLP graph masker Ψ and GNN parameters to capture an anchor graph encoding both topological and semantic benchmarks; (2) one-shot magnitude pruning of the anchor mask to the target sparsity yields an initial sparse subgraph; (3) dynamic sparse training iteratively prunes and regrows an equal number of edges every ΔT epochs, guided by a combined criterion φ(e_ij) = β^s·φ^(sema) + β^t·φ^(topo). The topological criterion measures each edge's impact on the anchor graph's Laplacian eigenvalues (approximated via top-K and bottom-K eigenvectors for O(o(N²)) cost), while the semantic criterion computes the gradient of KL divergence between anchor and current model outputs with respect to each edge. This Equilibria Sparsification Principle ensures the sparse graph converges toward maximum joint topological and semantic fidelity, a paradigm not previously applied to graph data.

## Results

GST achieves the highest extreme graph sparsity across all three small-scale datasets and backbones: 40%/43%/35% (GCN), 31%/26.43%/53% (GIN), and 35%/41%/39% (GAT) on Cora/Citeseer/PubMed respectively, yielding 33.29%–37.22% MAC savings and 1.30×–1.45× average inference speedup. On the large-scale Ogbn-ArXiv at 40% sparsity, GST+GraphSAGE retains 70.57% accuracy versus 66.57% (UGS), 64.57% (DSpar), and 64.66% (LSim). On Ogbn-Proteins, GST achieves 3.14×–3.42× inference speedup with negligible (≤1%) performance loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GST outperforms all six baselines (SCAN, LSim, DSpar, UGS, WD-GLT, MGSpar) in extreme graph sparsity while maintaining performance, achieving 1.67%–15.85% higher sparsity than the next-best method. Among topology-based sparsifiers, DSpar is the strongest baseline but still trails GST by 10–25 percentage points in achievable sparsity (e.g., 12.20% vs. 39.33% avg. for GCN). Among semantic-based sparsifiers, UGS and MGSpar are the closest competitors, yet GST still leads by 9–32 percentage points in sparsity (e.g., 25.94% vs. 39.33% for GCN). On large-scale Ogbn-ArXiv at 40% sparsity, GST+GraphSAGE (70.57%) surpasses UGS (66.57%) by 4.00%, DSpar (64.57%) by 6.00%, and LSim (64.66%) by 5.91%. On Ogbn-Products+ClusterGCN at 60% sparsity, GST (73.05%) outperforms DSpar (69.56%) by 3.45% and LSim (65.10%) by 7.95%. GST also uniquely preserves ~15% more spectral properties (bottom-200 eigenvalues) compared to UGS at 20% sparsity, a dimension where topology-based methods like LSim are competitive but semantic-based methods like UGS degrade sharply.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research lines in graph sparsification—topology-guided methods (effective resistance, spectral radius, Jaccard similarity) and semantic-guided methods (gradient-based pruning, UGS, meta-gradient sparsifiers)—by introducing a dynamic sparse training framework that operates on graph edges rather than network parameters. It adapts the iterative prune-and-regrow paradigm from Dynamic Sparse Training (RigL, SNIP, SET) to the graph data level, which prior DST work had not explored. The Equilibria Sparsification Principle formalizes the simultaneous minimization of topological (eigenvalue variation) and semantic (KL divergence of model outputs) discrepancies, providing a principled combination criterion absent from either prior line.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GST offers a practical path to 1.27–3.42× GNN inference speedup with negligible accuracy loss, which is directly relevant for deploying GNNs on large-scale graphs in resource-constrained settings. The Equilibria Sparsification Principle provides a general design template for future sparsifiers, and the demonstrated versatility (adversarial defense, graph lottery tickets) suggests broad applicability. However, the method requires an initial full-graph training phase to construct the anchor, adding overhead that may be prohibitive for extremely large graphs; the paper also leaves open questions about heterophilic/heterogeneous graphs and real-time sparsification in streaming applications.
