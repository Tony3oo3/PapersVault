## Problem

Pixel-wise semantic segmentation datasets are labor-intensive to annotate and error-prone, yet foundation models that can generate pseudo labels remain considerably noisy. Prior active label correction (ALC) methods are limited to classification tasks, and standard classification queries that ask annotators to assign a class from scratch are costly when pseudo labels are already available. This paper addresses how to efficiently correct noisy pixel labels in segmentation datasets with minimal human intervention by combining foundation-model priors with an annotator-friendly query design.

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial noisy pixel-wise labeled dataset (generated by Grounded-SAM zero-shot segmentation or an existing dataset like PASCAL) along with SAM-derived superpixels as input. Each correction query presents a single representative pixel with its pseudo label and a set of class options, asking the annotator to respond only if the pseudo label is wrong. The output is a corrected pixel-wise segmentation dataset (e.g., PASCAL+ with 2.6 million pixels relabeled) and a DeepLab-v3+ model trained on it; corrections are propagated from individual pixels to their entire superpixels, so a single click fixes an entire region.

## Method

The framework has three key components. First, a correction query replaces the standard classification query: instead of asking 'what class is this pixel?', it asks 'is this pixel a [pseudo label]? If not, give the correct label,' which Theorem 3.1 proves is information-theoretically cheaper (cost ratio 1 − 1/log₂L × p) and a user study confirms is ~25% faster in practice. Second, a diversified pixel pool selects one representative pixel per SAM superpixel (by cosine similarity to the superpixel's pseudo-dominant label), eliminating redundancy and reducing the search space. Third, a look-ahead acquisition function (SIM) extends the Confidence-In-Label score by weighting each pixel's unreliability by its cosine similarity to the representative pixel, so the acquisition anticipates the downstream benefit of expanding the corrected label across the whole superpixel. The entire pipeline warm-starts from Grounded-SAM pseudo labels (avoiding the cold-start problem of conventional active learning) and iteratively refines the dataset over T rounds, retraining a ResNet-101 DeepLab-v3+ after each round.

## Results

On PASCAL, ALC achieves 95% of the fully supervised mIoU with only 6K clicks, requiring just 30% of the budget needed by the prior SOTA AL method (Spx). On Cityscapes, ALC reaches the same 95% target with 150K clicks, using 75% of the SOTA budget. On the medical Kvasir-SEG dataset, ALC reaches 93% of fully supervised performance with only 6K clicks. The SIM acquisition function achieves 83.04% Data mIoU and 68.72% Model mIoU after correcting 5K pixels on PASCAL, substantially outperforming LCIL (56.59%/56.82%), SoftMin (59.28%/58.66%), and AIoU (59.95%/59.04%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three SOTA superpixel-based AL methods (Spx, MerSpx, MulSpx), ALC requires only 30% of the click budget on PASCAL and 75% on Cityscapes to reach 95% of fully supervised mIoU, a substantial reduction. In the fair head-to-head (Table 4), ALC with warm-start and SAM superpixels achieves 65.30% Model mIoU versus 57.79% for Spx with the same advantages, a 7.5-point gap attributable to the SIM look-ahead acquisition. Among noisy-label detection baselines, SIM outperforms LCIL, SoftMin, and AIoU on both Data mIoU (83.04% vs. 56.59–59.95%) and Model mIoU (68.72% vs. 56.82–59.04%) after 5K corrections on PASCAL, primarily due to its higher recall in identifying mislabeled pixels. For diversified pixel pool design, ALC's superpixel-based pool (83.60% Data mIoU, 68.71% Model mIoU) outperforms PixelPick's uniform top-5% sampling (66.88%, 62.59%) under identical acquisition and expansion settings. The correction query also proves more cost-effective than the classification query in a 20-annotator user study, requiring 75% of the labeling time at equal 95% accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends active label correction (ALC) from the classification setting (Bernhardt et al., 2022; Kim, 2022) to semantic segmentation, where labels are pixel-wise and correction must account for spatial structure. It departs from conventional active learning for segmentation (Spx, MerSpx, MulSpx) by starting from noisy foundation-model pseudo labels rather than unlabeled images, thereby avoiding the cold-start problem. The acquisition function builds on Confidence in Label (CIL) from Lad & Mueller (2023) but introduces a look-ahead mechanism that weights pixels by cosine similarity to anticipate the benefit of expanding a corrected label across an entire superpixel, a design not present in prior noisy-label-detection or AL methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of PASCAL+ (2.6 million corrected pixels) provides the segmentation community with a more reliable benchmark, directly affecting reproducibility and noisy-label-detection research. The correction query, proven information-theoretically cheaper than classification queries, offers a practical annotation protocol that reduces human cost without sacrificing accuracy. Open questions include how the framework scales to very large or open-vocabulary datasets, how sensitive it is to foundation-model superpixel quality in domains where SAM underperforms, and whether the look-ahead acquisition generalizes to 3D or video segmentation where superpixel analogues are less natural.
