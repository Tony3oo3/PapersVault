## Problem

Existing contrastive representation learning methods for time series treat all channels as uniformly sampled, ignoring the multi-frequency structure common in real-world data (e.g., daily stock prices alongside monthly GDP and quarterly earnings). Standard augmentations like permutation break frequency structure, and temporal consistency-based positive pair selection generates false positives when low-frequency sequences contain change points, making these methods unsuitable for multi-frequency inputs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-dimensional time series x_i ∈ R^{T×D} whose D features are partitioned into m groups of subseries x_i^j ∈ R^{T×d_j}, each sampled at a distinct frequency ordered from highest to lowest. It produces a per-timestep latent representation r_{i,t} ∈ R^L learned hierarchically, which is then fed to a lightweight downstream model (SVM, ridge regressor) for classification, forecasting, anomaly detection, or imputation. The encoding length is capped at 128 for sequences exceeding 2048 timesteps.

## Method

MF-CLR processes frequencies hierarchically from highest to lowest using a sequence of cross-frequency contrastive blocks, each handling two consecutive frequency groups with a shared TCN encoder (4 dilated conv layers, dilation 2^i, 64 channels). Within each block, a novel Dual Twister augmentation generates a positive view by simultaneously twisting the sequence along the temporal axis (via a probabilistic alignment policy shared across frequency levels) and along the value axis (with a bounded total deviation proven to upper-bound the DTW distance), preserving the original frequency structure. Training combines two losses: an instance-wise InfoNCE loss contrasting the original and augmented views, and a cross-frequency quadratic contrastive loss that pulls high-frequency representations closer in latent space when their corresponding low-frequency raw signals are similar. The block output concatenates the encoded high-frequency vector with a projected low-frequency subseries (via a 2-layer MLP), which recursively serves as the next block's input, progressively folding lower-frequency invariance into the representation.

## Results

On 30 UEA classification datasets, MF-CLR achieves 64.87% accuracy, 79.89% ROC-AUC, and 66.72% PRC-AUC, outperforming all self-supervised baselines and matching or exceeding most supervised methods. On anomaly detection (SMD, SMAP, MSL, SWaT), MF-CLR reaches 96.89% accuracy and 67.98% F1, surpassing the second-best self-supervised method (TS2Vec, 65.76% F1) by 2.22%. For forecasting on traffic, ETTm1, ETTm2, and weather, MF-CLR achieves the lowest MSE in both short-term (e.g., 0.0479 on ETTm1) and long-term (e.g., 0.0495 on ETTm1) settings among all self-supervised methods. In real-world stock price and net income forecasting, MF-CLR achieves top-2 MAPE in 7 out of 8 subtasks, outperforming the second-best self-supervised method by up to 7.18% on net income (US market).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MF-CLR outperforms all seven self-supervised baselines (T-Loss, TS-TCC, TNC, CPC, TS2Vec, CoST, TF-C) across all five downstream tasks. The most dramatic gaps appear against TNC and TF-C, which collapse to near-random performance (25.79% and 25.77% accuracy on UEA classification) in the multi-frequency setting, confirming the paper's argument that existing methods are ill-suited for multi-frequency inputs. Among competitive self-supervised baselines, MF-CLR leads CoST (62.62% Acc) by 2.25% and TS2Vec (61.67% Acc) by 3.20% on classification, and beats TS-TCC (64.00% F1) by 3.98% on anomaly detection. Against supervised methods, MF-CLR is competitive: it trails PatchTST (65.40% Acc) by only 0.53% on classification but surpasses TimesNet, Autoformer, Informer, and FEDformer. In the real-world stock forecasting case study, MF-CLR achieves 11.71% MAPE on US stable-market stock prices, outperforming TS-TCC (13.25%) and CoST (12.63%), while on Chinese stable-market net income it reaches 21.64% MAPE, beating TS2Vec (22.74%) and CoST (25.50%). The transfer learning experiments further show MF-CLR maintains stable performance as target dataset scale drops to 25%, whereas TS2Vec and FEDformer degrade significantly below 37.5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

MF-CLR extends the contrastive learning line for time series representation (T-Loss, TS-TCC, TS2Vec, CoST, TF-C) by explicitly addressing multi-frequency inputs, which prior methods implicitly treat as uni-frequency multi-dimensional series. Its cross-frequency contrastive loss adapts the quadratic contrastive formulation from Nonnenmacher et al. (2022), while the Dual Twister augmentation inverts the DTW alignment process (Berndt & Clifford, 1994) into an O(N) generative procedure. The hierarchical architecture—deploying a small TCN encoder per frequency level rather than one deep shared encoder—departs from the single-encoder paradigm common in prior contrastive time series work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-frequency covariates are ubiquitous in finance, healthcare, and supply chain, yet prior self-supervised methods silently degrade when sampling rates differ across channels; MF-CLR provides a principled mechanism (cross-frequency consistency + hierarchical blocks) to handle this without ad-hoc resampling. The transfer learning experiments showing stable performance down to 25% target data scale are practically valuable for domains where labeled data is scarce. However, the multi-frequency setting is largely constructed by resampling uni-frequency public datasets, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to genuinely heterogeneous sources with different physical semantics. The authors note future work on large-scale pre-training (à la TimeGPT) as the natural next step.
