## Problem

Standard Test-Time Augmentation (TTA) relies on label-preserving transformations and fixed averaging, making it inapplicable to combinatorial problems where such transformations are scarce and where rich semantic relations between instances remain unexploited. This paper asks whether metamorphic relations—necessary properties linking outputs of related inputs—can replace label-preserving transformations to enable effective inference-time augmentation for combinatorial problem solvers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242159/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a single combinatorial instance (a CNF formula, a 2D Euclidean Decision TSP instance, or a graph pair), MAGG applies a family of single-input metamorphic transformations (e.g., substituting a variable, inserting a midpoint node, or performing a graph edit) to generate n mutants, optionally iterated to a second level. The base model's predictions on all instances serve as node features in a directed Ego Metamorphic Graph whose edges encode the binary metamorphic relations; the GNN's output at the central node is the final prediction (a satisfiability probability or a discretized GED estimate).

## Method

MAGG constructs an Ego Metamorphic Graph (EMG) by sampling single-input metamorphic transformations from a task-specific family and applying them (optionally iteratively) to the target instance, producing a directed graph where each edge encodes a binary metamorphic relation between source and follow-up instances. A simplified Message Passing Neural Network is then trained on a held-out portion of the training set to aggregate base-model predictions across the EMG, using the graph topology to propagate information along metamorphic-relation edges. The key distinction from standard TTA is threefold: transformations need not preserve labels (e.g., SAT(q′)→SAT(q) rather than SAT(q′)=SAT(q)), the aggregation is a trainable GNN rather than fixed averaging, and the graph structure explicitly encodes the semantic constraint so the model can detect and correct violations. The aggregation model is deliberately minimal (2-dimensional hidden states, shared weights) to demonstrate that the gains stem from the metamorphic relations themselves rather than from a complex aggregator.

## Results

MAGG achieves up to 15% relative accuracy improvement over NeuroSAT on SAT (e.g., 0.9757 vs. 0.8444 on SR(40) with MAgg(10,10)), up to 4% relative improvement over TSP-GNN on Decision TSP (0.8321 vs. 0.8101 on TSP(0.02)), and up to 42% relative RMSE reduction over GREED on GED (0.2409 vs. 0.4151 on LINUX). The multi-hop setting MAgg(10,10) consistently outperforms the single-hop MAgg(10), confirming the value of indirect metamorphic relations. Notably, even the essentially linear aggregation model in MAgg(10) yields large gains, indicating the primary benefit comes from incorporating metamorphic relations rather than complex aggregation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NeuroSAT (Selsam et al., 2019), MAGG achieves substantial gains across all three SAT scales: +11.0% absolute on SR(40), +6.7% on SR(80), and +1.5% on SR(120) with MAgg(10,10), with the gap narrowing as instances become harder. Against TSP-GNN (Prates et al., 2019), MAGG improves accuracy by 2.5 points on TSP(0.01) and 2.2 points on TSP(0.02). Against GREED (Ranjan et al., 2022), MAGG delivers a dramatic 42% relative RMSE reduction on LINUX (0.2409 vs. 0.4151) and a 6.3% reduction on IMDB, but performs marginally worse on AIDS (0.7994 vs. 0.7957), which the authors attribute to node labels making random edit operations less informative. Against standard TTA (Shanmugam et al., 2021), MAGG dominates decisively: on SR(40), MAgg(10) reaches 0.9548 while STTA(10) only achieves 0.8431, confirming that non-label-preserving metamorphic relations and a trainable GNN aggregator are both essential for combinatorial problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard Test-Time Augmentation framework (Shanmugam et al., 2021) by replacing its reliance on label-preserving transformations with general metamorphic relations drawn from the metamorphic testing literature (Chen et al., 2018). The key conceptual departure is that metamorphic relations need not preserve labels, which the authors argue is critical for combinatorial problems where effective label-preserving transformations are scarce or nonexistent. The Ego Metamorphic Graph representation and the use of a trainable GNN (simplified MPNN) for aggregation are novel architectural choices that go beyond the fixed-averaging or weighted-sum strategies in prior TTA work, and the paper positions itself as the first TTA technique applicable to learning-based combinatorial problem solving.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper opens a practical path for inference-time augmentation in combinatorial optimization, a domain where standard TTA has been inapplicable due to the absence of label-preserving transformations. The substantial improvements (up to 42% RMSE reduction on GED) from a lightweight, training-free-at-inference method suggest that domain-specific metamorphic relations carry significant predictive signal that base models fail to exploit. However, the approach requires hand-crafted metamorphic transformations per problem domain, and the lack of improvement on the AIDS dataset (due to labeled nodes) highlights that the method's effectiveness depends on the structure of the problem. Open questions include whether metamorphic relations can be discovered automatically, whether the approach scales to larger or more diverse combinatorial problems, and whether the GNN aggregation can be replaced by simpler learned rules.
