## Problem

Vision Transformers use self-attention weights as intrinsic explanations, but these attention maps are highly sensitive to small adversarial perturbations, causing the model to focus on irrelevant regions and produce misleading interpretations. Prior work on ViT robustness has focused exclusively on preserving prediction accuracy under attacks, leaving the stability and faithfulness of attention-based explanations unaddressed. This paper asks whether one can construct provably faithful ViTs where both the top-k attention indices and the prediction distribution remain stable under bounded perturbations.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a plug-in preprocessing step on standard RGB images (224×224 or 256×256) fed into pre-trained ViT, DeiT, or Swin backbones. The input image is first corrupted with Gaussian noise, then denoised by a diffusion model before entering the ViT's self-attention layers. The output is a class prediction or pixel-level segmentation mask, accompanied by a class-aware attention heat map generated via Transformer Attention (Trans. Att.) that highlights the most relevant patches for the predicted class.

## Method

The core contribution is Denoised Diffusion Smoothing (DDS), which transforms a vanilla ViT into a Faithful ViT (FViT) by combining randomized smoothing with a denoising diffusion probabilistic model. Specifically, Gaussian noise is added to the input, the noise level is mapped to a diffusion timestep t*, and a pre-trained diffusion denoiser is applied to recover a clean estimate that is then fed through the original ViT's self-attention and downstream layers. The paper proves via Rényi divergence that this pipeline certifies both top-k attention index stability (interpretability robustness) and prediction distribution stability (utility robustness) within a computable faithful radius R, and further shows Gaussian noise is near-optimal for both ℓ2 and ℓ∞ norm threat models. The approach is evaluated on ImageNet, Cityscape, and COCO across three architectures, demonstrating consistent improvements in both classification accuracy and explanation faithfulness under PGD adversarial attacks.

## Results

Under PGD adversarial attacks (ρ_u = 8/255, ℓ_∞-norm), the proposed DDS method achieves the highest classification accuracy across all three architectures: 85% (ViT), 86% (DeiT), and 87% (Swin) on ImageNet, outperforming the best baseline (VTA) by 3–4 percentage points. For segmentation, DDS attains the best mIoU of 0.65 (ViT), 0.66 (DeiT), and 0.67 (Swin) on ImageNet, and the best mAP of 0.93–0.95, consistently surpassing all five baselines. On Cityscape and COCO, the method similarly leads in pixel accuracy and mIoU (e.g., 0.85 mIoU on Cityscape with Swin, 0.83 mIoU on COCO with Swin). Ablation confirms both the randomized smoothing and diffusion denoising components are necessary: removing both drops classification accuracy from 99.5% to 92.1% under attack.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DDS against five attention/explanation baselines: Raw Attention (Vaswani et al., 2017), Rollout (Abnar & Zuidema, 2020), GradCAM (Selvaraju et al., 2017), LRP (Binder et al., 2016), and VTA (Chefer et al., 2021). DDS consistently outperforms all five baselines across every model architecture (ViT, DeiT, Swin) and every dataset (ImageNet, Cityscape, COCO) on all reported metrics. The strongest baseline is VTA, which uses LRP-based relevance scoring over the attention graph; DDS improves over VTA by 3–4 points in classification accuracy (e.g., 0.85 vs. 0.82 for ViT on ImageNet) and by 0.03–0.05 in mIoU. GradCAM and LRP occupy the middle tier, while Raw Attention and Rollout are the weakest, confirming that single-layer or simple multiplicative attention aggregation is most vulnerable to adversarial perturbations. Notably, DDS also improves clean (no-attack) utility at small noise levels (δ = 2/255), achieving 86.35% vs. 85.22% for vanilla ViT on ImageNet, indicating the smoothing-diffusion pipeline does not merely trade accuracy for robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes randomized smoothing (Cohen et al., 2019), originally designed for certified prediction robustness, to stabilize attention vectors in ViTs, and pairs it with a denoising diffusion probabilistic model (Ho et al., 2020; Nichol & Dhariwal, 2021) as a denoiser to form the DDS pipeline. It departs from prior ViT robustness work (Mahmood et al., 2021; Salman et al., 2022) that targets only classification accuracy, and from prior faithfulness/stability analyses (Yeh et al., 2019; Yin et al., 2022) that apply to post-hoc explanation methods rather than self-explaining attention. The key conceptual move is showing that the same smoothing mechanism that certifies prediction invariance also certifies top-k attention index stability, unifying two previously separate objectives under a single Rényi-divergence framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal definition and provable construction of 'faithful' ViTs, giving practitioners a certified radius within which both attention maps and predictions remain stable—a guarantee no prior method offered for self-explaining architectures. The DDS approach is architecture-agnostic (ViT, DeiT, Swin) and attention-method-agnostic (works as a plugin with any explanation tool), making it broadly applicable. Limitations include the ~0.32 s/image denoising cost, restriction to ℓ2/ℓ∞ white-box perturbations, and the need to tune the noise standard deviation δ carefully (too large degrades clean accuracy). Open questions include extension to free-form/physical attacks, scaling to large vision-language models, and whether the diffusion denoiser can be replaced by lighter alternatives without losing the faithfulness guarantee.
