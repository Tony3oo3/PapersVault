## Problem

State-of-the-art graph contrastive learning (GCL) methods rely on GNN encoders with layer-wise message passing for inference, incurring substantial computational overhead that makes them unsuitable for latency-constrained applications. Simply replacing the GNN with an MLP eliminates message passing but causes significant performance degradation because critical structural information is lost. The paper addresses the open question of how to design a GCL algorithm that matches or exceeds SOTA GCL performance while enabling fast, graph-structure-free inference.

## Input / Output

The method takes node feature vectors X ∈ R^{|V|×D} as input and produces L2-normalized node representations of dimension K via a lightweight MLP encoder. During training, both an MLP (for central nodes) and a GNN (for neighbors) are active, but during inference only the MLP is used—no adjacency matrix or neighbor features are required. This means the deployed model operates purely on node attributes, making it applicable to inductive settings where new nodes appear without access to the full graph topology.

## Method

GraphECL introduces a cross-model contrastive architecture with an asymmetric GNN-MLP design: the GNN encoder processes neighbors to capture structural information during training, while the MLP encoder processes central nodes and serves as the sole inference model. The key loss (L_ECL) generalizes InfoNCE from uni-model instance discrimination over augmentations to cross-model neighborhood contrast, where positive pairs are (f_M(v), f_G(u)) for node v and its neighbor u, and negative pairs include both intra-model (f_G(u), f_G(v⁻)) and inter-model (f_M(v), f_G(v⁻)) components balanced by a hyperparameter λ. This design avoids graph augmentations entirely, instead directly encoding the 1-hop neighborhood distribution into the MLP representation. Theoretical analysis shows the loss converges when the estimated affinity matrix matches the normalized adjacency matrix, and establishes a generalization bound on linear probing error in terms of the graph homophily ratio and the (K+1)-th eigenvalue of the normalized adjacency matrix.

## Results

On the 10 standard transductive node classification benchmarks, GraphECL achieves 84.25% on Cora, 82.21% on Pubmed, 94.22% on Photo (homophilic), and 48.49% on Flickr, 35.80% on Actor (heterophilic), outperforming all 12 baselines on most datasets. On large-scale graphs, GraphECL reaches 27.22% on snap-patents, 71.75% on OGBN-ARXIV, and 61.45% on OGBN-Papers100M. Critically, GraphECL uses only an MLP for inference (no message passing), achieving ~200× faster inference than CCA-SSG on large-scale graphs while maintaining or exceeding accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphECL outperforms the strongest baseline GraphACL on most datasets: Photo (94.22 vs. 93.31), Flickr (48.49 vs. 46.95), Actor (35.80 vs. 30.03, a 5.8-point gain), and Pubmed (82.21 vs. 82.02), while matching it closely on Cora (84.25 vs. 84.20) and Wisconsin (69.19 vs. 69.22). On large-scale graphs (Table 2), GraphECL leads on snap-patents (27.22 vs. 26.18 for GraphACL) and OGBN-Papers100M (61.45 vs. 59.35), and ties on OGBN-ARXIV (71.75 vs. 71.72). Compared to augmentation-based GCL methods like GCA and BGRL, GraphECL gains 1.5–3.5 points on homophilic graphs and 1.5–6 points on heterophilic graphs, while using only an MLP at inference. The key differentiator is that all GNN-based baselines (DGI, GCA, BGRL, CCA-SSG, GraphACL) require message passing at inference, making them ~200× slower than GraphECL's MLP-only inference on large-scale graphs. Graph-MLP, the only other MLP-inference baseline, trails GraphECL by 7–13 points across datasets, confirming that the cross-model contrastive architecture is essential for capturing structural information without the homophily assumption.

## Relation to Prior Work

GraphECL generalizes the InfoNCE loss (Chen et al., 2020) from uni-model instance discrimination over augmentations to cross-model contrast over graph neighbors, and draws on the spectral contrastive loss framework (HaoChen et al., 2021) for its generalization bound. It departs from augmentation-based GCL (DGI, GCA, BGRL) by eliminating graph augmentations and the invariant assumption, and from Graph-MLP methods (Yang et al., 2016; Hu et al., 2021) by avoiding the homophily assumption. The cross-model MLP-GNN architecture is conceptually related to knowledge distillation on graphs but operates without task-specific labels, filling a gap between label-free GCL and label-dependent distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~200x inference speedup on large-scale graphs while matching or exceeding SOTA GCL accuracy addresses a critical deployment bottleneck for latency-constrained applications, making graph contrastive representations practically usable in production. The theoretical connection to 1-hop neighborhood distribution prediction provides a clean inductive bias that explains why the method generalizes to heterophilic graphs without explicit homophily assumptions. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is the reliance on node attributes, leaving graphs without features as an open problem. The modest performance margins over strong baselines (typically 1–3%) suggest the method's primary value is efficiency rather than representational breakthrough.
