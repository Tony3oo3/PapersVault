## Problem

Lu (2023a) established a worst-case computational separation between bimodal and unimodal learning, but it remains unclear whether such separations hold for typical (average-case) instances and whether they arise naturally in practice. This paper constructs an average-case separation under a low-noise LPN assumption and then proves that any average-case super-polynomial computational separation necessarily implies a cryptographic key agreement protocol, suggesting that super-polynomial computational advantages of multimodal learning may be rare in naturally generated data.

## Input / Output

The construction operates over binary vector spaces: the first modality X = Z_2^{1×n} × [n] encodes a sparse secret vector (each coordinate 1 with probability n^{-0.5}) paired with an index, the second modality Y = Z_2^{n×n} × Z_2^{1×n} encodes a uniformly random matrix and a noisy LPN instance, and the label space Z = Z_2^{n×1} × Z_2 is generated by a sparse random vector w. The multimodal algorithm consumes n³ triplets and outputs a hypothesis h: Y → Z achieving population risk ≤ n^{-0.5} (optimal), while any polynomial-time unimodal algorithm given only (y,z) or (x,z) pairs cannot achieve risk below 1/2 − 1/t(n) for any polynomial t.

## Method

The separation exploits the low-noise LPN assumption (noise rate θ = n^{-0.5}) to create distributional one-wayness between modalities: the first modality hides a sparse secret vector behind an LPN instance, while the second modality reveals the random matrix A and the noisy parity xA + b + e^(i), making the second modality hard to compute from the first. The multimodal learning algorithm recovers the label-generating vector w by sorting samples into bins by index i and taking majority votes of x_j z_j + z_j within each bin, where the LPN terms cancel algebraically to leave a biased signal. For the key agreement reduction, Alice samples bimodal data and transmits only the unimodal portion to Bob, who either applies the true labeling or sends random labels; Alice then runs the multimodal learning algorithm to decode Bob's bit, with security proven via a hybrid argument showing that any adversary predicting the bit with non-negligible advantage yields a unimodal learner contradicting the assumed separation.

## Results

The paper proves two main theoretical results. First (Theorem 1.2), under the low-noise LPN assumption (noise rate θ = n^{-0.5}), it constructs an average-case bimodal learning task where the multimodal algorithm achieves population risk ≤ n^{-0.5} (optimal) in polynomial time, while any unimodal algorithm running in time t(n) cannot achieve risk below 1/2 − 1/t(n) with probability at least 1 − 1/t(n). Second (Theorem 1.3), it shows that any average-case super-polynomial computational separation between bimodal and unimodal learning implies the existence of a cryptographic key agreement protocol, establishing that such separations require inherently 'cryptographic' hardness. The construction uses a distributional one-way mapping between modalities based on LPN trapdoor properties, with the second modality encoding the secret parity underlying the LPN instance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The paper positions its contributions relative to prior theoretical work: Lu (2023b) established a statistical separation (more samples needed unimodally), and Lu (2023a) established a worst-case computational separation under the assumption that an NP-hard problem is not in P. This paper strengthens Lu's computational separation from worst-case to average-case, and further characterizes the minimal hardness required for any such separation by showing it must be sufficient to construct cryptographic key agreement. The paper also contrasts its computational separation with the statistical regime, noting that the LPN-based separation does not hold statistically since LPN can be solved in 2^{o(n)} time with poly(n) samples, suggesting practical multimodal advantages are typically statistical rather than computational.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Lu's (NeurIPS 2023, ALT 2024) formal framework for multimodal versus unimodal learning, which established a worst-case computational separation under the assumption that an NP-hard problem is not in P. Karchmer strengthens this to an average-case separation using the low-noise LPN assumption, drawing on the Covert Learning algorithm of Canetti and Karchmer (TCC 2021) for the construction and on Pietrzak and Sjödin's (ICALP 2008) key agreement techniques for the lower bound. The paper also explicitly connects the bimodal PAC-learning model to the LUPI paradigm of Vapnik and Vashist (2009), noting the two are formally identical for the bimodal case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central conceptual contribution is the argument that super-polynomial average-case computational separations between multimodal and unimodal learning must be inherently cryptographic (implying key agreement protocols), and therefore unlikely to arise from natural data distributions. This suggests the practical success of multimodal systems (GPT-4, Gemini, etc.) is driven by statistical advantages (less data needed) rather than computational ones, a distinction with implications for how we interpret and design multimodal architectures. Open questions include whether polynomial (rather than super-polynomial) separations can be more natural, whether standard LPN (rather than low-noise LPN) suffices, and how these results connect to fine-grained cryptography.
