## Problem

Existing decoding algorithms for autoregressive language models rely on fixed truncation strategies (top-k, top-p) that either produce repetitive text when the candidate set is too small or incoherent, hallucinated text when it is too large. The paper tackles the question of how to dynamically determine the rational size of the next-token candidate set at each decoding step so that both diversity and coherence are preserved simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242582/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each decoding step the method consumes the LM's full vocabulary probability distribution (sorted in descending order) together with a single scalar threshold ε, and outputs a dynamically sized candidate set from which the next token is drawn via multinomial sampling. The candidate set size k varies per step according to the local distribution shape—smaller for peaked distributions, larger for flattened ones—requiring no per-step hyperparameter tuning beyond the global ε.

## Method

The core mechanism is an entropy-based confidence metric Conf_k(X) that quantifies the certainty gained when the k highest-probability tokens are moved from an 'unknown' pool into a 'known' candidate set, with the remaining tokens assumed uniformly distributed. The paper proves that the per-token confidence increment ΔConf = Conf_k(X) − Conf_{k−1}(X) is always non-negative and exhibits a descending trend as k grows, providing a principled stopping criterion. Adaptive decoding iteratively transfers tokens from the ordered vocabulary into the candidate set until ΔConf falls below the preset threshold ε, at which point the set is finalized and the next token is sampled. This yields an O(|V| log |V|) per-step algorithm whose truncation size adapts to the local distribution shape, distinguishing it from fixed-k or fixed-p baselines that cannot respond to the varying entropy of the predicted distribution.

## Results

On GPT2-XL, adaptive decoding achieves the best MAUVE score (25.18 on WritingPrompts, 79.57 on WikiText-103) and highest Diversity (89.30 and 93.23 respectively), while also producing the lowest repetition rates (rep-2: 6.86/5.20). On Llama2-7B, adaptive decoding attains the best MAUVE (35.26 on WritingPrompts) and lowest rep-3/rep-4 (2.63/1.58), with coherence of 35.10 surpassing all baselines except Mirostat. On the policy fine-tuned Llama2-7B-chat, adaptive decoding improves MAUVE from 31.85 to 37.06, Diversity from 87.41 to 93.99, and Coherence from 49.85 to 54.39 over top-p sampling. Human evaluation confirms adaptive decoding is preferred over top-k, top-p, and η-sampling (64.5%, 64.0%, and 50.1% win rates respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Adaptive decoding outperforms top-k and top-p on MAUVE across both models and datasets (e.g., 25.18 vs. 22.28/21.20 for GPT2-XL on WritingPrompts; 35.26 vs. 23.57/23.60 for Llama2-7B), confirming that fixed-size or fixed-probability truncation is suboptimal. Against Mirostat, adaptive decoding achieves substantially higher MAUVE and Diversity (e.g., 25.18 vs. 5.61 MAUVE for GPT2-XL), though Mirostat retains an edge on Coherence for Llama2-7B (56.23 vs. 35.10), which the authors attribute to partial repetition inflating coherence scores. Compared to typical decoding, adaptive decoding wins on MAUVE (25.18 vs. 20.36 for GPT2-XL; 35.26 vs. 25.78 for Llama2-7B) while maintaining comparable Diversity. The closest competitor is η-sampling, which matches or slightly exceeds adaptive decoding on Diversity and repetition metrics for Llama2-7B on WikiText-103 (91.96 vs. 90.55 Diversity), but adaptive decoding clearly leads on MAUVE for WritingPrompts (35.26 vs. 24.43) and in human evaluation (50.1% preferred over η-sampling). On the policy fine-tuned Llama2-7B-chat, adaptive decoding improves all three metrics over top-p (MAUVE +5.21, Diversity +6.58, Coherence +4.54), demonstrating that the method generalizes beyond MLE-trained models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground within the established family of truncation-based decoding algorithms (top-k, top-p, Mirostat, typical, η-sampling) by reframing the candidate-set sizing problem as a confidence-increasing process grounded in entropy. Rather than relying on a fixed hyperparameter (k, p, τ) or a perplexity target (Mirostat), it introduces an entropy-based confidence metric Conf_k and uses the increment ΔConf as a data-driven stopping criterion for expanding the candidate set. Conceptually, it is closest to η-sampling (Hewitt et al., 2022), which also leverages entropy, but departs by using the marginal gain in confidence rather than an entropy-dependent probability threshold, and by providing a theoretical guarantee of monotonicity. The approach is positioned as a principled alternative to the ad-hoc truncation heuristics that have been the norm since Holtzman et al. (2020) identified the degeneration problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a model-agnostic, theoretically motivated mechanism for balancing diversity and coherence that works across model scales (GPT2-XL 1.5B through Llama2-70B) and across both MLE-trained and preference-aligned (chat) models. However, the empirical gains over strong baselines like η-sampling are modest, and the per-step O(|V| log |V|) overhead is a practical concern for latency-sensitive deployment. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multimodal generation, whether the computational cost can be further reduced, and whether the small improvements on reasoning tasks (GSM8K, MultiArith) generalize to more complex problem-solving. The authors themselves note the need for better evaluation metrics and broader validation beyond text-only language models.
