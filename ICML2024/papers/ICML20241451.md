## Problem

Transformer-based world models like IRIS encode each frame independently with a large number of discrete tokens, causing the autoregressive dynamics model's attention to scale quadratically with sequence length. This makes scaling to visually complex environments with long time horizons computationally prohibitive, as maintaining a favorable imagined-to-collected data ratio becomes infeasible. The paper asks how to drastically reduce the token budget per frame while preserving the world model's ability to simulate both deterministic and stochastic dynamics.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes 64×64 RGB frames and discrete actions from the environment. The autoencoder compresses each frame into only 4 discrete Δ-tokens (40 bits total) by conditioning on the immediately preceding frame and action, rather than encoding the full frame. The autoregressive transformer interleaves continuous I-tokens (64-dim embeddings from an auxiliary CNN) with these Δ-tokens and action tokens, predicting the next Δ-tokens autoregressively along with reward and episode termination. The decoder reconstructs full 64×64 frames from past frames, actions, and predicted Δ-tokens, and the policy outputs a discrete action conditioned on the reconstructed observation.

## Method

Δ-IRIS introduces two key architectural changes over IRIS. First, the discrete autoencoder (VQ-VAE with factorized codes, vocabulary size 1024) is conditioned on the previous frame and action, so its 4 Δ-tokens per frame only need to capture stochastic changes that cannot be inferred from actions, offloading deterministic dynamics to the decoder. Second, the autoregressive transformer (3 layers, 512-dim, 8 heads, 21 timesteps) receives a sequence where continuous I-tokens from an auxiliary CNN are interleaved with discrete Δ-tokens and action tokens, providing a 'soft Markov blanket' that summarizes the current world state and eliminates the need to integrate over all past Δ-tokens. The autoencoder is trained with L1, L2, max-pixel, and commitment losses; the transformer with cross-entropy for transitions/termination and two-hot symlog regression for rewards. The policy is trained via REINFORCE with value baseline over imagined trajectories (horizon 15) initialized from real observations, using an actor-critic with shared CNN+LSTM weights.

## Results

On the Crafter benchmark, Δ-IRIS achieves a return of 16.1 (±0.1) after 10M frames, solving 17 out of 22 tasks and setting a new state of the art. It outperforms DreamerV3XL (15.1) and DreamerV3M (13.7) at the 10M-frame budget while using only 25M parameters (vs. 200M and 37M respectively) and trains 10× faster than IRIS (20 FPS vs. 2 FPS). On Atari 100k, Δ-IRIS achieves a mean human-normalized score of 1.39 and 11 superhuman games, surpassing IRIS (1.05), DreamerV3 (1.10), and STORM (1.27).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DreamerV3XL, Δ-IRIS trails at the 1M-frame budget (7.7 vs. 9.2) but surpasses it at 5M (15.4 vs. 14.2) and 10M frames (16.1 vs. 15.1) while using 8× fewer parameters (25M vs. 200M). Against DreamerV3M, Δ-IRIS wins at every frame budget (7.7 vs. 6.2 at 1M; 15.4 vs. 12.6 at 5M; 16.1 vs. 13.7 at 10M). Compared to IRIS (64 tokens), Δ-IRIS achieves a higher 1M-frame return (7.7 vs. 5.5) and trains an order of magnitude faster (20 FPS vs. 2 FPS); IRIS experiments were stopped before 10M frames due to prohibitive runtime. On Atari 100k, Δ-IRIS achieves the best mean HNS (1.39) and most superhuman games (11), outperforming STORM (1.27, 10 games), IRIS (1.05, 10 games), DreamerV3 (1.10, 9 games), and SimPLe (0.33, 1 game), while training in 26 hours (a 5× speedup over IRIS).

## Relation to Prior Work

Δ-IRIS directly extends IRIS (Micheli et al., 2023), which first cast model-based RL as a sequence-modelling problem over discrete image tokens produced by a VQ-VAE and predicted by an autoregressive transformer. The key architectural departure is borrowing the I-frame/P-frame distinction from video compression: the autoencoder is conditioned on past frames and actions so that it only encodes the stochastic delta, and the transformer's input sequence interleaves continuous I-tokens (frame embeddings) with discrete Δ-tokens to provide a soft Markov blanket. This positions the work between the RSSM-based DreamerV3 line (which carries deterministic state through a recurrent network) and the purely discrete-token IRIS line, while also connecting to trajectory-level video autoencoders like TECO and Phenaki that condition on past frames but lack the continuous-token mechanism for long-horizon reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× training speedup over IRIS (20 FPS vs 2 FPS) while achieving higher Crafter returns makes transformer-based world models practically viable for visually complex, long-horizon environments where IRIS was computationally prohibitive. The clean disentanglement evidence—showing that random Δ-tokens still yield correct deterministic dynamics while stochastic elements degrade—validates the architectural separation and suggests the approach generalizes to other domains with mixed deterministic/stochastic transitions. Open questions include whether the fixed 4-token budget can be made adaptive to local uncertainty, whether leveraging the world model's internal representations (as DreamerV3 does) could close the gap in low-data regimes, and whether the exploration bottleneck at 10M frames (4 unsolved tasks) can be addressed with biased replay or curiosity-driven sampling.
