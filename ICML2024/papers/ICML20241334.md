## Problem

When training differentially private (DP) models with side access to public data (free of privacy concerns), it was an open question whether public data could yield asymptotic improvements over two naive baselines: discarding private data entirely, or treating all data as private. This paper settles the optimal minimax error for three fundamental problems—mean estimation, empirical risk minimization, and stochastic convex optimization—under both central and local, pure and approximate semi-DP, showing that worst-case asymptotic improvements over the naive baselines are impossible, while identifying where constant-factor gains are achievable.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume a dataset partitioned into n_priv private samples and n_pub public samples (both drawn from a bounded domain, e.g., the unit ℓ₂-ball), along with a loss function and a privacy budget (ε, δ). The output is either a population mean estimate, model parameters minimizing training or population loss, or an excess risk guarantee. A key structural detail is that the algorithm is only required to be DP with respect to the private subset (semi-DP), meaning the public data can be used without any privacy noise, and for ERM the public data may even be out-of-distribution relative to the private data.

## Method

The paper's core algorithmic contribution is a family of 'even more optimal' semi-DP estimators that achieve the same asymptotic rates as the naive baselines but with strictly smaller constants. For central DP mean estimation, the Weighted-Gaussian estimator assigns a tunable weight r to private samples and (1−n_priv·r)/n_pub to public samples, then adds Gaussian noise calibrated only to the private weight's sensitivity; optimizing r over [0, 1/n_priv] trades off reduced noise (smaller sensitivity) against increased variance on public data, yielding MSE smaller than both throw-away and the standard Gaussian mechanism. For local DP, the semi-LDP estimator applies the optimal LDP randomizer (e.g., PrivUnit or M_Duchi) only to private samples and averages the noisy private outputs with raw public data, reducing error by a factor of n_priv/n when the privacy-noise term dominates. For SCO, Algorithm 1 (Semi-DP-SGD) uses the weighted gradient estimator at each iteration: it clips and averages private batch gradients, adds Gaussian noise scaled to the private weight α, and combines this with un-noised public batch gradients, achieving smaller excess risk than DP-SGD and PDA-MD while requiring no Hessian information.

## Results

The paper establishes tight (up to log factors) minimax error bounds for semi-DP mean estimation, ERM, and SCO, showing that no asymptotic improvement over the two naive baselines (throw-away or full DP) is possible in the worst case. Empirically, Algorithm 1 (Semi-DP-SGD via Weighted-Gaussian Gradient Estimation) reduces CIFAR-10 test error by 8–9% for logistic regression and by up to 18.9% for Wide-ResNet compared to the naive approaches. In a linear regression setting with ε=0.01 and n_pub/n=0.1, DP-SGD diverges while Algorithm 1 converges to small error. The local semi-DP mean estimation algorithm is shown to be optimal including constants.

|---|---|---|---|---|

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Algorithm 1 against three baselines: (1) Throw-away (train only on public data), (2) DP-SGD (Abadi et al., 2016; De et al., 2022), and (3) PDA-MD (Amid et al., 2022), the state-of-the-art semi-DP algorithm. Algorithm 1 consistently achieves the smallest test loss across all tested privacy levels (ε=2, 4) and public data fractions (n_pub/n) on synthetic Gaussian linear regression (Figures 3–4). On CIFAR-10, Algorithm 1 reduces test error by 8–9% (logistic regression) and up to 18.9% (Wide-ResNet) relative to both throw-away and DP-SGD. In a challenging linear regression regime (d=50, n=1000, ε=0.01, n_pub/n=0.1), DP-SGD diverges entirely while Algorithm 1 converges to small error. PDA-MD is outperformed by Algorithm 1 in all settings; notably, without warm-start, PDA-MD can perform worse than throw-away, whereas Algorithm 1 remains robust to cold-start conditions. Theoretically, the paper proves that no semi-DP algorithm can beat the minimum of the two naive baselines asymptotically, but Algorithm 1 achieves strictly smaller constants than both.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semi-DP framework (Beimel et al. 2013; Alon et al. 2019) from PAC learning and query release to the fundamental optimization problems of mean estimation, ERM, and SCO, providing the first complete minimax characterizations for all three under both central and local, pure and approximate DP. It builds on lower-bound techniques from Dwork et al. (2015) tracing attacks, Bassily et al. (2020) fingerprinting, and Duchi & Rogers (2019) communication-complexity arguments, adapting them to the semi-DP setting via novel semi-DP Fano's inequality and packing arguments. On the algorithmic side, it departs from DP-SGD (Abadi et al. 2016) and PDA-MD (Amid et al. 2022) by introducing a Weighted-Gaussian estimator that differentially weights private and public samples, achieving smaller constants than the asymptotically optimal naive mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper settles a fundamental open question by proving that public data cannot yield asymptotic improvements over naive baselines (throw-away or full-DP) in the worst case for mean estimation, ERM, and SCO—calibrating expectations for the field. However, the 'even more optimal' algorithms with improved constants (and optimal constants for local mean estimation) provide practical gains, demonstrated by 8–19% test error reductions on CIFAR-10. Open questions remain about why certain problem structures (e.g., finite VC-dimension) do permit asymptotic gains while Lipschitz optimization does not, and about semi-DP learning with out-of-distribution public data.
