## Problem

While importance weighting (IW) is a standard remedy for covariate shift, recent work reveals that its benefit depends on model capacity and specification, with IW sometimes hurting performance in over-parameterized or well-specified settings. The interplay between regularization level, model capacity, and IW in terms of the bias-variance trade-off for high-dimensional kernel methods remained uncharacterized, leaving unclear when and how IW actually helps or hurts generalization.

## Input / Output

The method operates in the high-dimensional regime where both data dimension d and sample size n grow with n/d → ζ ∈ (0,∞). It takes n training pairs (x_i, y_i) drawn from distribution p, a test covariate x drawn from q, a regularization parameter λ > 0, and a general importance weighting function w̄(x) = d q̄(x)/dp(x) that need not equal the true density ratio w(x) = dq(x)/dp(x). The output is a closed-form function estimate f̄_{λ,Z}(x) = K(x,X)(K(X,X) + λn W̄(X)^{-1})^{-1} y, along with non-asymptotic upper bounds on bias and variance that are characterized by the spectral decay of a data-dependent regularized kernel matrix.

## Method

The paper derives an asymptotic expansion of high-dimensional inner-product and radial kernels under covariate shift, extending El Karoui's linearization to the setting where training data comes from p and test data from q, with the residual controlled by a similarity parameter c_{pq} between the two covariance matrices. The key insight is that the IW strategy acts as a data-dependent implicit regularization: the variance is governed by the effective dimension (capacity) of the regularized kernel K^{lin} + (λn/β_p) W̄(X)^{-1}, meaning IW modifies only the self-similarity of each data point (diagonal entries) without altering cross-point similarities, analogous to data subsampling. For bias, the analysis decomposes it into an intrinsic bias Tr(K^{lin}W)/n that is irreducible under any re-weighting (determined solely by the covariate shift), and a re-weighting bias that vanishes as λ → 0. Under a well-chosen regularization λ ∝ n^{-c_λ}, the IW strategy provably does not hurt bias, and the combined excess risk is controlled by the spectral decay of the data-dependent regularized kernel.

## Results

The paper provides theoretical upper bounds rather than empirical benchmarks. The key quantitative result is that the variance is dominated by V_x ≈ (1/d)·N(XX^T/d + (λn/β_p)·W̄(X)^{-1}; γ_p/β_p), showing that importance re-weighting acts as a data-dependent implicit regularization that can decrease variance. For bias, the decomposition yields B ≤ B_in + B_iw, where the intrinsic bias B_in = Tr(K^lin·W)/n is a constant-order term irreducible to the covariate shift itself, while the re-weighting bias B_iw vanishes as λ→0. Under a well-chosen regularization λ = C_λ·n^{-c_λ} with c_λ = (1−4c_{w̄,2})/(2r̄ + Ā), the total bias converges to zero at rate n^{-r̄·c_λ + c_H}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or method comparisons. The contribution is a refined bias-variance decomposition for kernel ridge regression under covariate shift in high dimensions, extending the kernel linearization framework of El Karoui (2010) to the shifted setting (Lemma 4.3) and building on the interpolation analysis of Liang & Rakhlin (2020) for the intrinsic bias term. The bounded-ratio assumption (Assumption 3.4) is inherited from Gogolashvili et al. (2023), and the well-chosen regularization analysis (Theorem 4.6) follows the classical kernel literature framework of Ma et al. (2023). The paper's novelty lies in showing that the IW strategy can be reinterpreted as data-dependent regularization on the kernel, unifying the variance reduction and bias behavior under a single spectral-decay characterization, rather than in outperforming specific competing methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the high-dimensional kernel regression analysis of Liang & Rakhlin (2020) and El Karoui (2010) to the covariate shift setting, where training and test covariates follow different distributions. It builds on the fixed-dimensional IW analysis of Gogolashvili et al. (2023) and Ma et al. (2023) by deriving asymptotic kernel expansions under distribution mismatch and showing that importance weighting acts as a data-dependent implicit regularization on the kernel matrix. The key departure from prior work is the unified bias-variance decomposition that separates an irreducible intrinsic bias (determined solely by the density ratio) from a re-weighting bias that vanishes near interpolation, a distinction not made in the fixed-dimensional literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical explanation for the empirically observed mixed effects of importance weighting in high-capacity models: IW reduces variance through an implicit regularization mechanism but introduces an irreducible bias floor from the distribution shift itself. This resolves the apparent contradiction between prior results showing IW is unnecessary in well-specified over-parameterized settings yet needed in misspecified ones. A key limitation is that the analysis yields only upper bounds rather than exact risk characterizations, since classical random matrix theory cannot directly handle the data-dependent IW transformation; the authors explicitly flag this as an open problem requiring advanced RMT techniques. The results are also restricted to inner-product and radial kernels with specific smoothness assumptions, and empirical validation is deferred to an appendix.
