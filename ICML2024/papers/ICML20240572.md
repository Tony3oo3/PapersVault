## Problem

While the Shapley Value is well-known to yield an optimal 1-additive approximation of a cooperative game via a weighted least squares (WLS) objective, an analogous WLS characterization for the Shapley Interaction Index (SII) has been a long-standing open problem. This gap motivated the proposal of the Faithful Shapley Interaction Index (FSI) as an alternative, but FSI differs from SII and its relationship to k-additive approximations remains unclear. The paper closes this gap by proving that SII is itself the solution to a WLS problem, enabling a direct extension of KernelSHAP to higher-order interactions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a black-box game function ν (e.g., a model's prediction given a subset of features), a specific data point to explain, a maximum interaction order k, and a Monte Carlo sampling budget b. Subsets of features are sampled once using KernelSHAP-style weights p(T) ∝ μ₁(t) with a border-trick, and the game is evaluated on all b sampled subsets. The output is a set of SII estimates φ̂₁,…,φ̂_k (one per order) and the aggregated k-SII values Φ̂_k, which form an efficient k-additive decomposition of the prediction where higher-order effects are absorbed into lower-order terms via Bernoulli-number weighting.

## Method

The core insight is that the k-additive approximation ν̂_k(T) = Σ_{S⊆T, |S|≤k} Φ_k(S) is constructed iteratively: ν̂_k(T) = ν̂_{k-1}(T) + Σ_{|S|=k} φ^SII(S)·λ(k, |S∩T|), where λ involves Bernoulli numbers. The paper proves (Theorem 3.7) that pairwise SII are the exact solution to a WLS problem with weights μ₂(t) = C(n-2k, t-k)⁻¹ for k≤t≤n-k and μ_∞ elsewhere, and conjectures (Conjecture 3.9) the analogous result for k>2. KernelSHAP-IQ samples subsets once, then iteratively for ℓ=1,…,k solves a WLS problem with coefficient matrix entries λ(ℓ, |T∩S|) and diagonal weights μ_ℓ(t)/p*(T), computing residuals y_{ℓ+1} = y_ℓ - X_ℓ·φ̂_ℓ at each step. For ℓ>2, the WLS is split by Conjecture 3.9 into a direct SII-weighted term for out-of-range subsets and a WLS solve for in-range subsets. The consistent variant converges to SII, while an inconsistent variant (single joint WLS over all orders) does not converge but still yields high-quality estimates in low-budget regimes.

## Results

The paper evaluates SII approximation quality using mean-squared error (MSE; lower is better) and precision at ten (Prec@10; higher is better) against brute-force ground truth, across seven benchmark scenarios (synthetic SOUM, DistilBERT sentiment LM, California Housing, Bike Rental, ResNet18, ViT, and Adult Census). KernelSHAP-IQ and inconsistent KernelSHAP-IQ clearly outperform all three sampling-based baselines on both regression benchmarks (Bike Rental, California Housing). For the language model task (n=14), KernelSHAP-IQ and SVARM-IQ achieve state-of-the-art results for second-order SII, and KernelSHAP-IQ further outperforms SVARM-IQ for third-order SII. Inconsistent KernelSHAP-IQ achieves high estimation quality in low-budget settings but does not converge to ground-truth SII values, whereas consistent KernelSHAP-IQ converges to SII for k=2 (proven) and k>2 (empirically validated).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares KernelSHAP-IQ against three sampling-based baselines: permutation sampling (Castro et al., 2009; Tsai et al., 2023), SHAP-IQ (Fumagalli et al., 2023), and SVARM-IQ (Kolpaczki et al., 2024b). On the regression benchmarks (Bike Rental with XGBoost, n=12; California Housing with a neural net, n=8), both KernelSHAP-IQ and inconsistent KernelSHAP-IQ clearly outperform all three baselines in both MSE and Prec@10 across all budgets. On the DistilBERT sentiment analysis task (n=14), KernelSHAP-IQ and SVARM-IQ rapidly outperform permutation sampling, SHAP-IQ, and inconsistent KernelSHAP-IQ, with both achieving SOTA for second-order SII. For third-order SII on the LM, KernelSHAP-IQ outperforms SVARM-IQ, which is the strongest existing baseline. On the high-player synthetic SOUM task (n=20), inconsistent KernelSHAP-IQ achieves competitive low-budget performance but fails to converge to ground truth, while KernelSHAP-IQ maintains convergence. Overall, KernelSHAP-IQ wins or ties on all tasks, with its advantage over SVARM-IQ being most pronounced at higher interaction orders (k≥3) and on regression tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-known WLS representation of the Shapley value (Charnes et al., 1988; Lundberg & Lee, 2017) to the Shapley Interaction Index, resolving a long-standing open problem that had previously motivated the proposal of the alternative Faithful Shapley Interaction Index (Tsai et al., 2023). The k-SII aggregation framework (Bordt & von Luxburg, 2023) provides the interpretable output, while the SII weight representations from SHAP-IQ (Fumagalli et al., 2023) inform the sampling and weighting structure. The key departure from prior work is showing that SII itself (not a new index) is the WLS solution, yielding a direct and principled extension of KernelSHAP to interactions via the iterative k-additive approximation structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Resolving the WLS representation of SII provides both theoretical clarity (linking the axiomatic and optimization perspectives for interactions) and a practical algorithm (KernelSHAP-IQ) that achieves SOTA performance for feature interaction estimation across diverse model classes. The iterative construction of k-additive approximations from SII values offers an elegant complexity-accuracy trade-off for interpretability. However, rigorous proofs are limited to k≤2, with higher orders resting on empirically validated conjectures, and the iterative computation means estimation errors propagate across orders. The exponential growth in the number of interactions remains a fundamental practical barrier requiring human-centered post-processing.
