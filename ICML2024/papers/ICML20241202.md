## Problem

Particle Gradient Descent (PGD) minimizes the free energy functional for MLE in latent variable models but, like standard gradient descent, lacks momentum acceleration. While momentum methods (Nesterov's Accelerated Gradient) and underdamped Langevin dynamics are known to accelerate optimization in Euclidean space and sampling in P(R^d) respectively, no prior work had incorporated momentum effects into the coupled gradient flow on the extended space R^{d_θ}×P(R^{d_x}) that underlies PGD. This paper asks whether PGD can be similarly accelerated and provides both the continuous-time theory and a practical discretization.

## Input / Output

The method takes fixed observed data y ∈ R^{d_y} and a parametric latent variable model p_θ(y,x) with parameters θ ∈ R^{d_θ} and latent variables x ∈ R^{d_x}. It produces the MLE estimate θ* along with a particle cloud {X_t^i}_{i=1}^M approximating the posterior p_{θ*}(·|y), where M is a user-chosen number of particles. Unlike standard MLE algorithms that only output parameters, MPD simultaneously tracks the evolving posterior approximation, making it directly applicable to generative tasks like VAE training where the posterior particles serve as latent codes for reconstruction.

## Method

MPD enriches both the parameter and latent spaces with momentum variables, replacing (θ, x) with (θ, m, x, u), and defines a Hamiltonian F on R^{2d_θ}×P(R^{2d_x}) that combines the free energy with kinetic energy terms. The continuous-time dynamics are a McKean-Vlasov SDE that blends Nesterov's Accelerated Gradient (for the θ,m component), underdamped Langevin diffusion (for the x,u component), and particle interactions (the θ drift depends on the particle law). The key discretization uses an Exponential Integrator that freezes nonlinear terms over each step and solves the resulting linear SDE analytically, with a gradient correction: the θ-gradient is evaluated at a partially-updated position (analogous to NAG's look-ahead) while the x-gradient uses the fully-updated θ. The paper proves existence/uniqueness of the flow, exponential convergence under a log-Sobolev inequality via a Lyapunov argument, and asymptotic propagation of chaos for the particle approximation.

## Results

On MNIST, MPD achieves a FID of 47.9±0.6, compared to PGD's 104.1±1.2, ABP's 45.9±0.8, SR's 109.97±12.5, and VI's 72.6±1.3. On CIFAR-10, MPD achieves the best FID of 93.2±1.5, outperforming PGD (106.2±4.8), ABP (97.0±8.0), SR (126.25±13.0), and VI (94.2±6.7). The momentum-enriched approach yields roughly a 2.2× reduction in FID over PGD on MNIST and a 1.14× reduction on CIFAR-10, with the largest gains observed when the particle cloud is poorly initialized.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PGD (Kuntz et al., 2023), MPD achieves a dramatic improvement: FID drops from 104.1 to 47.9 on MNIST (a 54% reduction) and from 106.2 to 93.2 on CIFAR-10, confirming that momentum in both the θ and X components is essential for acceleration. Against ABP (Han et al., 2017), the closest persistent competitor, MPD wins on CIFAR-10 (93.2 vs. 97.0) but is slightly behind on MNIST (47.9 vs. 45.9), where ABP's multiple ULA steps per iteration give it a marginal edge. Against Short Run (Nijkamp et al., 2020), MPD dominates by a wide margin on both datasets (47.9 vs. 109.97 on MNIST; 93.2 vs. 126.25 on CIFAR-10), which the authors attribute to SR's non-persistent particle restarts failing to locate the posterior mode. Against amortized VI (Kingma and Welling, 2014), MPD wins on MNIST (47.9 vs. 72.6) and is marginally better on CIFAR-10 (93.2 vs. 94.2), with the gap being small enough that the difference is within one standard deviation on CIFAR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Particle Gradient Descent (PGD) framework of Kuntz et al. (2023), which formulates MLE for latent variable models as a Wasserstein gradient flow on the product space R^{d_θ} × P(R^{d_x}), by incorporating momentum into both the parameter and particle components simultaneously. The momentum construction for the parameter component follows the Nesterov accelerated gradient / damped Hamiltonian flow lineage (Su et al., 2014; Wibisono et al., 2016), while the particle component follows the underdamped Langevin acceleration of Ma et al. (2021). The key departure is that prior acceleration results apply to either R^d or P(R^d) individually; this paper is the first to derive and prove convergence for a momentum-enriched flow on the full product space, requiring new Lyapunov analysis and propagation-of-chaos arguments for McKean-Vlasov SDEs with coupled momentum.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretically grounded accelerated particle method for MLE in latent variable models, with exponential convergence guarantees under a log-Sobolev condition. Practically, MPD outperforms PGD and is competitive with Alternating Backpropagation on VAE training (FID 47.9 vs 45.9 on MNIST), though the margin is modest. The main open limitation is the need to tune four momentum hyperparameters (η_θ, γ_θ, η_x, γ_x), for which no systematic strategy exists beyond cross-validation or heuristics. Future work on adaptive tuning and rigorous comparison of discretization schemes would strengthen the method's practical adoption.
