## Problem

While numerous pre-training objectives have been proposed for vision-based RL, their generalization capabilities remain unclear because prior evaluations are confined to in-distribution environments and use non-unified experimental setups (different datasets, architectures, and downstream tasks). This makes it impossible to isolate how the learning objective itself—rather than confounding architectural or data choices—affects performance under visual and task distribution shifts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240973/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 4 stacked grayscale Atari frames at 84×84 resolution, processed through a ResNet-50 backbone (with group normalization replacing batch normalization) that produces a 2048×6×6 spatial feature map. A game-specific neck applies spatial embedding, pooling, and a 2-layer MLP to yield a 512-dimensional latent vector, which a game-specific linear head maps to an 18-dimensional action distribution. During fine-tuning, the backbone is frozen while the neck and head are re-initialized; adaptation uses either 50,000 expert demonstration frames (offline BC) or 50,000 online Rainbow interactions, with performance measured via IQM-normalized scores across three seeds.

## Method

Atari-PB pre-trains a single ResNet-50 architecture on 10 million transitions from 50 Atari games (sourced from the DQN-Replay-Dataset, mixing suboptimal and expert checkpoints) under four categories of objectives: image-based (CURL, MAE), video-based (ATC, SiamMAE, R3M†), demonstration-based (BC, SPR, IDM, SPR+IDM), and trajectory-based (CQL-M, CQL-D, DT). The pre-trained backbone is then frozen and fine-tuned via offline BC or online RL across three distribution groups—In-Distribution (50 pre-training games), Near-OOD (10 same-genre games), and Far-OOD (5 novel-task games like Klax and Surround)—enabling controlled comparison of how each objective type transfers. The key empirical finding is that task-agnostic objectives (spatial features from images, temporal dynamics from videos) consistently improve generalization across all distribution shifts, whereas task-specific objectives (agent identification from demonstrations, reward fitting from trajectories) boost in-distribution performance but degrade under increasing task divergence. Ablations further show that mixed-optimality data outperforms expert-only data for OOD generalization, and that larger models help in-distribution but not Far-OOD settings.

## Results

The paper reports Inter Quantile Mean (IQM) of DQN-normalized scores across three seeds for 50 ID, 10 Near-OOD, and 5 Far-OOD Atari games, under both Offline BC and Online RL fine-tuning. Video-based pre-training (ATC, SiamMAE, R3M†) consistently outperforms image-based methods (CURL, MAE), which in turn surpass the frozen Random baseline across all three distribution groups. Trajectory-based methods (CQL-D, DT) achieve the highest ID scores but show the steepest performance decline in Far-OOD, while demonstration-based methods (BC, SPR, IDM) provide moderate Near-OOD gains but lose effectiveness in Far-OOD. The correlation between Offline BC and Online RL performance is strong (Pearson r = 0.85 in ID, 0.61 in Near-OOD, 0.63 in Far-OOD), indicating that pre-training quality transfers across adaptation scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark compares 10 pre-training algorithms across four data-type categories. Video-based methods (ATC, SiamMAE, R3M†) emerge as the strongest generalizers, slightly surpassing even end-to-end fine-tuning from scratch despite having a frozen backbone during adaptation. Image-based methods (CURL, MAE) consistently beat the Random frozen baseline but trail video methods, confirming that temporal dynamics add value beyond spatial features. Demonstration-based methods (BC, SPR, IDM) improve over video methods in ID and Near-OOD by capturing agent locations and task-relevant structure, but their advantage reverses in Far-OOD where task-specific knowledge becomes a liability. Trajectory-based methods (CQL-M, CQL-D, DT) achieve the best ID performance by encoding reward structure, yet show the largest generalization gap in Far-OOD, lagging behind both video and demonstration methods. The paper also notes that in Online RL, the smaller 3-layer CNN (CNN3) often outperforms the larger ResNet-50 (RN50) in end-to-end fine-tuning, consistent with known overfitting issues in non-stationary RL settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pretrain-then-finetune paradigm from CV/NLP into vision-based RL by introducing Atari-PB, a unified benchmark that isolates the effect of learning objectives on generalization. Prior generalization studies (Hansen & Wang 2021, Yuan et al. 2023, Taiga et al. 2022) examined only single, minor distribution shifts, while prior pre-training evaluations (Parisi et al. 2022, Majumdar et al. 2023, Hu et al. 2023) used heterogeneous datasets and architectures, making cross-method comparison impossible. By fixing the dataset (DQN-Replay-Dataset, 10M transitions), architecture (ResNet-50 with group normalization), and evaluating across three distribution levels (ID, Near-OOD, Far-OOD), this work provides the first controlled comparison of image-, video-, demonstration-, and trajectory-based pre-training objectives under identical conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides practitioners with clear, actionable guidance: task-agnostic objectives (spatial features from images, temporal dynamics from videos) consistently improve generalization across distribution shifts, while task-specific objectives (agent identification, reward fitting) help only in similar-task settings. The finding that mixed-optimality data outperforms expert-only data for generalization is practically important for dataset curation. Open questions include whether these findings transfer beyond Atari to more complex real-world domains, whether architectures can be designed to decouple task-agnostic and task-specific features for selective use, and how conclusions scale to larger models and datasets where the current ResNet-50 may be too small to fully exploit 100M+ transitions.
