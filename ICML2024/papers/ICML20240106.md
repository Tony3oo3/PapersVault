## Problem

Identifiability of sequential latent variable models with non-linear dynamics and regime-switching behavior has remained largely unexplored, despite its importance for interpretability and causal discovery in time series. Existing results for non-temporal deep LVMs (e.g., Kivva et al., 2022) do not extend to temporal settings, and prior sequential identifiability work (Hälvä et al., 2021; Yao et al., 2022) imposes restrictive assumptions such as stationarity, auxiliary variables, or injective decoders. This paper provides the first identifiability analysis for Markov Switching Models with non-linear Gaussian transitions and extends it to full Switching Dynamical Systems.

## Input / Output

The method consumes high-dimensional time series of length T (e.g., 32×32 video frames, 50-dimensional synthetic observations, monthly ENSO/AIR climate data, or 3D key-point sequences) and produces identifiable latent trajectories z_{1:T} up to affine transformations, discrete regime assignments s_{1:T}, and non-linear transition functions m(z_{t-1}, s_t) and Σ(z_{t-1}, s_t). For causal discovery, the Jacobian of the estimated transition function is thresholded to extract regime-dependent causal graphs; for segmentation, the posterior over discrete states yields per-frame regime labels. The emission model f: R^m → R^n is a piece-wise linear (e.g., Leaky ReLU) network, and identifiability holds up to affine transformations of the latent space rather than exact recovery.

## Method

The theoretical core is a two-step proof: (1) MSM identifiability is established by recasting the joint distribution p(z_{1:T}) as a finite mixture of K^T trajectory distributions and proving linear independence of the component family via a new lemma (Lemma B.3) that handles the overlapping-variable challenge in products of consecutive conditionals, requiring only unique indexing and analyticity of Gaussian moments; (2) SDS identifiability follows by extending Kivva et al. (2022)'s pushforward-measure argument to factored temporal transformations, showing the MSM prior is identifiable up to affine transformations when the emission f is weakly injective (or continuous and injective for full recovery of f). For estimation, MSMs are fitted via Generalised EM with gradient-based M-steps on neural-network-parameterised transitions, while SDSs use variational inference with a factored posterior q(z_{1:T}|x_{1:T}) that incorporates the exact posterior p(s_{1:T}|z_{1:T}), trained by ELBO maximisation with the prior computed exactly via the forward algorithm.

## Results

On the nonlinear ICA benchmark (50-dimensional observations from 2D latent MSMs), iSDS achieves MCC-strong of 0.936±0.041 and MCC-weak of 0.992±0.008, matching or exceeding both baselines. For video reconstruction, iSDS attains pixel MSE of 8.89×10⁻⁵ on synthetic 32×32 videos, 2.26×10⁻⁴ on rendered salsa dancing videos, and 7.85×10⁻³ on AIST Hip-Hop videos. In the regime-dependent causal discovery experiment on ENSO-AIR climate data, the identifiable MSM recovers a summer-only causal effect from ENSO to All India Rainfall, consistent with prior climate science findings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares iSDS against two nonlinear ICA baselines: iVAE* (Khemakhem et al., 2020), which uses ground-truth discrete states as auxiliary information, and Δ-SNICA (Hälvä et al., 2021), which uses structured nonlinear ICA with a mean-field variational design. Against iVAE*, iSDS wins decisively on both metrics: MCC-strong improves from 0.642 to 0.936 and MCC-weak from 0.770 to 0.992, showing that the temporal MSM prior provides substantially stronger identifiability than a static mixture prior even with auxiliary states. Against Δ-SNICA, iSDS achieves a near-tie on MCC-strong (0.936 vs. 0.940, within one standard error) but clearly wins on MCC-weak (0.992 vs. 0.979), indicating that iSDS's identifiability up to affine transformations yields tighter latent alignment. The authors note that Δ-SNICA's linear prior dynamics differ from the ground-truth cosine-network MSM structure, yet it still achieves competitive disentanglement due to its overparametrised linear SDS design, at the cost of losing transition identifiability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the identifiability framework of Kivva et al. (2022) for static deep generative models with finite mixture priors to the temporal setting, using the Yakowitz & Spragins (1968) linear independence criterion as the mathematical backbone. It departs fundamentally from prior sequential identifiability work (Hälvä et al. 2021, Yao et al. 2022, Lippe et al. 2023) by eliminating the need for auxiliary variables, stationarity assumptions, and injective decoders. The estimation algorithm builds on the variational inference design of Ansari et al. (2021) for switching dynamical systems, while the MSM identifiability proof introduces a new linear independence lemma (B.3) that cannot be derived from the Kruskal 3-way array techniques used for HMMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first identifiability theory for Markov Switching Models with non-linear transitions, opening a path toward interpretable and causally meaningful sequential latent variable models without the restrictive assumptions that have limited prior work. The regime-dependent causal discovery application to climate data (ENSO-AIR) demonstrates practical value, showing that identifiable MSMs can recover seasonally-varying causal structures. Key limitations remain: the first-order Markov assumption restricts expressivity, ELBO-based estimation lacks consistency guarantees, and state collapse in high-dimensional settings limits scalability. Future work should address higher-order MSMs, estimation consistency, and relaxing the (weak) injectivity requirement on the emission model that still persists.
