## Problem

Existing sequential planning neural solvers for min-max VRPs process merged agent and customer features through unified self-attention layers, producing ambiguous representations that conflate the partition task (assigning customers to routes) with the navigation task (ordering visits within each route). This ambiguity, combined with a fixed decoding order and depot-agnostic positional encodings, leads to imbalanced route lengths and suboptimal solutions. The paper asks whether explicitly decoupling these two coupled optimization tasks in the encoder architecture can yield significantly better min-max VRP solutions.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a graph defined by N customer coordinates (N×2), D depot coordinates (D×2), and a route count M, with Euclidean edge weights. It outputs an ordered set of M routes (each a sequence of customer indices starting and ending at a depot) that minimizes the maximum route length. For multi-depot variants, each route is additionally assigned to a specific start depot (and optionally a different end depot in FMDVRP), and the decoder applies feasibility masks to enforce depot-specific start/end constraints and, for mPDP, pickup-before-delivery and pairing constraints.

## Method

DPN introduces a bi-part P&N Encoder where each layer contains a navigation part (customer self-attention with Rezero skip connections) and a partition part (cascaded agent-to-customer MHA followed by customer-to-agent multi-head sharp attention that removes the Softmax temperature to enforce one-agent-per-customer assignment). Training uses a novel APS-Loss that samples K agent permutations per instance and averages the resulting min-max objectives as a REINFORCE baseline, exploiting the fact that the optimal solution is invariant to route ordering. A depot-aware Rotation-based PE replaces the standard sinusoidal PE, computing agent positional encodings as the real part of (x_d·W_a + b_a)·exp(i·m/1000^(⌊q/2⌋/d)) in complex space, thereby embedding depot coordinates into the agent distinction signal. The decoder is a single-layer glimpse attention that fuses a contextual embedding (aggregated encoder outputs, current agent/customer embeddings, progress ratios, and length statistics) with all agent and customer embeddings to produce action logits with distance-aware scoring.

## Results

On medium-scale min-max mTSP100 (M=5), DPN-×8aug-×16per achieves an objective of 2.2314 (1.92% gap to best), outperforming Equity-Transformer-×8aug (2.2563, 3.06% gap) and DAN (2.6995, 23.3% gap). On min-max mPDP100 (M=5), DPN-×8aug-×16per achieves 3.4960 (best overall), dramatically beating Equity-Transformer-×8aug (5.0735, 45.1% gap) and OR-Tools (14.315, 309.5% gap). Across all 12 medium-scale datasets, DPN reduces the average optimality gap by 0.72% on mTSP and 33.7% on mPDP compared to Equity-Transformer, while solving in <1s versus minutes-to-hours for heuristic baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPN-×8aug-×16per achieves the best learning-based result on all 12 medium-scale datasets (mTSP50/100, mPDP50/100) and all 13 larger-scale datasets (mTSP/mPDP at 200/500/1000), consistently outperforming Equity-Transformer (Son et al., 2024) by 0.72% average gap reduction on mTSP and 33.7% on mPDP. Against the parallel planning method DAN (Cao et al., 2021), DPN reduces the optimality gap from 10–23% to 0.3–2%, a dramatic improvement. DPN also substantially outperforms OR-Tools (7–10% gap) and matches or approaches the quality of strong heuristics HGA and LKH3 (which achieve 0–0.5% gaps) while running in <1s versus 4–20 minutes. On multi-depot problems (Table 4), DPN-F-×8aug-×16per beats CE, OR-Tools, NCE, and ScheduleNet on MDVRP50/100 and FMDVRP50, but CE and NCE slightly outperform DPN on FMDVRP100 (e.g., CE 1.09 vs. DPN 1.1012 at M=7). On the largest mPDP1000 instances, DPN-×8aug-×16per achieves 2.9114 (M=100) versus Equity-Transformer-F-sample's 3.38, a 13.8% improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sequential planning framework of Equity-Transformer (Son et al., 2024) for min-max VRPs by structurally decoupling the partition and navigation representations that prior methods process jointly through merged self-attention layers. The P&N Encoder draws on the observation that Softmax normalization in the unified attention conflates heterogeneous relations (agent-agent, agent-customer, customer-customer), while the APS-Loss adapts the symmetry-exploitation paradigm from POMO and Sym-NCO to the agent-permutation structure inherent in min-max objectives. The Rotation-based PE repurposes the rotary position embedding concept from RoFormer (Su et al., 2024) to incorporate depot coordinates into agent distinction, addressing a gap in the sinusoidal PE used by prior sequential planners.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DPN is the first sequential planning neural solver to handle multi-depot min-max VRPs (MDVRP, FMDVRP), a setting previously inaccessible to this method family, and achieves substantial gap reductions (up to 33.7% on mPDP) over the prior best sequential method. The decoupling principle—separating representations for coupled sub-tasks within a single encoder—offers a transferable architectural insight for other problems with partition-plus-optimization structure. However, the authors note DPN cannot be directly applied to general VRPs with capacity or time-window constraints, leaving open whether the decoupled representation idea generalizes beyond the min-max objective. The method's reliance on REINFORCE with a fixed number of permutations (K=60) also raises questions about sample efficiency at larger agent counts.
