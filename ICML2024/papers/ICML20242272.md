## Problem

Existing dataset distillation algorithms fail on image-text contrastive learning data because such data lacks the categorical clustering structure that unimodal methods rely on, leading to high batch variance that breaks short-term matching approaches. The first image-text distillation work (Wu et al., 2023) applies vanilla MTT without exploiting the unique structure of paired multimodal data, leaving the modality correspondence underutilized and the compression ratio limited.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242272/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a paired image-text dataset (e.g., Flickr30k or COCO with 5 captions per image) and produces a compact synthetic dataset consisting of learnable 224×224 images, 768-dimensional text embeddings (not raw captions), and a low-rank factorized similarity matrix S = ωI + (α/r)LRᵀ where L, R ∈ ℝ^{N×r} and ω ∈ ℝ^N. The number of synthetic pairs is slightly reduced (e.g., 499 instead of 500) to keep total parameter count fair with baselines, and the rank r is kept small (typically <50) so the similarity overhead is linear in data size.

## Method

LoRS extends the TESLA/MTT long-term parameter matching framework by jointly optimizing a ground-truth similarity matrix alongside the synthetic image-text pairs, replacing the fixed identity matrix assumed by standard InfoNCE. Three continuous contrastive losses (eNCE, BCE, wBCE) are derived to handle arbitrary similarity values, with wBCE providing the best balance between positive and negative pair penalties. The similarity matrix is factorized as S = ωI + (α/r)LRᵀ to reduce storage from O(N²) to O(Nr), following the LoRA-style decomposition. The approach is justified from two perspectives: (1) it automatically discovers and corrects false negatives in small synthetic batches, and (2) gradient analysis shows the learnable similarity weights act as flexible anchor coefficients in the contrastive training dynamics, enabling multiple anchors to be merged and thus achieving higher compression ratios.

## Results

On Flickr30k with 200 synthetic pairs (0.7% of the full dataset), LoRS_wBCE achieves IR@1=8.6, IR@10=36.6, TR@1=14.5, and TR@10=53.4, substantially outperforming the MTT-VL baseline (IR@1=4.6, TR@10=41.9) and TESLA_wBCE (IR@1=0.2, TR@10=17.4). Notably, LoRS with only 100 pairs (IR@1=8.3, IR@10=35.1) surpasses MTT-VL with 500 pairs (IR@1=6.6, IR@10=30.0), demonstrating a significantly higher compression ratio. On COCO (200 pairs), LoRS_wBCE reaches IR@1=2.4 and TR@10=22.6, again leading all baselines by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRS_wBCE decisively outperforms all baselines on both Flickr30k and COCO across every retrieval metric. Against the strongest dataset distillation baseline MTT-VL (Wu et al., 2023), LoRS achieves roughly 2× improvement in IR@1 (8.6 vs. 4.6) and 28% improvement in TR@10 (53.4 vs. 41.9) at 200 pairs on Flickr30k. TESLA_wBCE (Cui et al., 2023), an efficient MTT variant, performs poorly on image-text data (IR@1=0.2), suggesting that the base distillation algorithm alone is insufficient without the similarity mining component. Coreset selection methods (Random, Herd, K-Center, Forgetting) all remain below IR@1=2, confirming that simple data selection cannot capture the cross-modal correspondence. Gradient matching (DC, Zhao et al., 2020) and distribution matching (DM, Zhao & Bilen, 2023) fail entirely on image-text data (IR@1≈0.1–0.6 in Table 1), attributed to the high sample variance of uncategorized multimodal data. Data filtering via DataComp (Gadre et al., 2024) scores comparably to coreset selection (e.g., 6.52 vs. 6.52 IR@1 at 200 pairs on Flickr30k), while LoRS reaches 29.52, a 4.5× gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MTT/TESLA trajectory-matching framework for dataset distillation and adapts the LoRA-style low-rank factorization (Hu et al., 2021) to a new domain: the ground-truth similarity matrix in image-text contrastive learning. It departs from the first image-text distillation work (Wu et al., 2023), which applied vanilla MTT without exploiting the paired structure, by introducing the similarity matrix as a learnable component of the synthetic data. Conceptually, it extends the InfoNCE contrastive loss (which assumes an identity GT matrix) to a continuous, learnable similarity, drawing on the false-negative mining literature (Srinivasa et al., 2023) and the anchor-weighting interpretation of contrastive dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper advocates similarity mining as a foundational setup for image-text dataset distillation, and the ~50% relative improvement plus the 5× compression advantage (100 LoRS pairs beating 500 MTT pairs) make it a practical tool for multimodal model training at scale. Key limitations remain: text is distilled only as embeddings rather than tokens, the rank hyperparameter introduces a storage-performance trade-off, and the contrastive loss must be redesigned and empirically selected. Open questions include whether the approach extends to generative text distillation, larger-scale datasets beyond Flickr30k/COCO, and whether the learned similarity structure transfers across model families without re-distillation.
