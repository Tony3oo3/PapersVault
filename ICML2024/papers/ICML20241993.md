## Problem

While prior work demonstrated that zero-shot CLIP can be well-calibrated with temperature scaling, it remained unclear which factors govern VLM calibration robustness and under what conditions calibration transfers. This paper systematically investigates whether VLMs can be calibrated across distribution shifts, different label sets, different semantic hierarchy levels, with very few samples, and with simple prompts — questions that are critical for deploying VLMs in risk-sensitive domains where labeled calibration data may be scarce or mismatched to the target task.

## Input / Output

The calibration procedure consumes a small set of labeled images (as few as 40–50) paired with a single text prompt template (e.g., "a photo of a <class>") to estimate a temperature scalar or a per-class calibration function. The calibration set need not share the same label set, hierarchy granularity, or visual domain as the target test set, and in the calibration-by-synthesis application the images themselves are generated by Stable Diffusion from GPT-3-produced class descriptions. The output is a calibrated per-class confidence score whose value reflects the empirical frequency of correctness, enabling reliable model selection and accuracy estimation without access to the target domain's labeled data.

## Method

This is a comprehensive empirical study evaluating 35 VLMs (CLIP variants across ViT/ConvNeXt encoders, BLIP, FLAVA, SigLIP, etc.) under four post-hoc calibration methods — temperature scaling, spline fitting, histogram binning, and vector scaling — while varying five factors: distribution shift, label set, semantic hierarchy level, calibration set size, and feature-space distance between calibration and target sets. The central finding is that VLMs are remarkably robust to all these factors: temperature scaling drops average ECE to ~0.05 on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution test sets (versus ~0.15 for ImageNet-trained models), calibration transfers across label sets (e.g., CIFAR-10 → ImageNet) and hierarchy levels (e.g., "Spider" → "Black widow"), and only 40–50 randomly sampled images suffice to plateau calibration quality even for 1000-way tasks. The paper further introduces a calibration-by-synthesis pipeline in which GPT-3 generates class descriptions, Stable Diffusion produces synthetic images, and the resulting automatically-labeled set calibrates VLMs as effectively as real data, enabling deployment in domains where no labeled calibration data exists.

## Results

After temperature scaling, VLMs achieve an average ECE of 0.05 on both in-distribution and OOD test sets (ImageNet-S, ImageNet-R), while ImageNet-trained models see their ECE increase to 0.15, demonstrating that VLMs benefit far more from calibration. Calibration plateaus after merely 40–50 images, even for 1000-way (ImageNet) and 345-way (DomainNet) classification tasks. In the calibration-by-synthesis application (Table 1), generating a small synthetic calibration set with fine-grained labels via GPT-3 + Stable Diffusion reduces mean ECE from 0.17 to 0.07, MAE from 16.49 to 6.35, and improves Spearman rank correlation with model accuracy from 0.76 to 0.93.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between 35 VLMs (CLIP, BLIP, FLAVA, SigLIP, EVA-02, etc.) and 25 ImageNet-trained models (ResNet, ConvNeXt, ViT, Swin, DeiT, etc.) on ECE before and after temperature scaling. Before calibration, some ImageNet models (e.g., ResNet, ConvNeXt) actually show lower ECE than VLMs; after temperature scaling, VLMs drop to ~0.05 ECE while ImageNet models rise to ~0.15, a clear win for VLMs. Among calibration methods, temperature scaling and spline fitting both achieve strong calibration with <100 samples, while vector scaling requires ~10% of the full 50,000-sample validation set and histogram binning, though effective on average, may not benefit all outlier models. Cross-label-set calibration (e.g., calibrating on CIFAR-10 or DomainNet-Real and testing on ImageNet-R/S) reduces ECE substantially and yields linear/rank correlations >0.90 with model accuracy, outperforming no calibration. A single prompt ('a photo of a <class>') calibrated temperature transfers effectively to both other human-designed prompts and machine-generated CuPL prompts, matching multi-prompt calibration performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Tu et al. (2023), which demonstrated that zero-shot CLIP models can be well-calibrated via temperature scaling under distribution shifts, to a much broader empirical scope: 35 VLMs spanning diverse architectures (ViT, ConvNeXt), training objectives (contrastive, sigmoid, bootstrapped), and data scales (3M–2B pairs). It also builds on the calibration methodology of Guo et al. (2017) (temperature scaling, vector scaling) and Gupta et al. (2021) (spline fitting), applying them systematically to VLMs for the first time across multiple factors. The calibration-by-synthesis application repurposes CuPL-style prompt generation (Pratt et al., 2023) and Stable Diffusion to create automatically-labeled calibration sets, a practical technique not previously explored in the calibration literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The findings have direct practical impact for deploying VLMs in safety-critical settings: calibration requires only 40–50 labeled images, works across different label sets and semantic hierarchy levels, and can even be performed entirely with synthetic images generated by a text-to-image model. This substantially lowers the barrier to reliable VLM deployment in domains where labeled calibration data is scarce or expensive. Key limitations include the restriction to classification confidence (not regression or detection), the focus on backbone VLMs rather than LLM-based models like LLaVA, and the open question of whether these properties hold for compound architectures used in object detection or segmentation.
