## Problem

Existing Inverse Constrained Reinforcement Learning (ICRL) methods train and validate inferred constraints within a single environment, ignoring the discrepancy between training and deployment dynamics. The paper demonstrates that even a correctly recovered constraint can yield unsafe policies when the agent is deployed in a perturbed environment, as the optimal policy under that constraint may violate safety in the mismatched setting. This motivates the Robust Constraint Inference (RCI) problem: inferring constraints that guarantee safe control across an uncertainty set of transition dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes expert demonstration trajectories, the learning environment's CMDP (state space, action space, transition T^L, reward, and constraint threshold ε), and a scalar opponent strength parameter (1−α) that controls the radius of the (s,a)-rectangular uncertainty set centered at T^L. It produces a robust control policy π^pl and a robust constraint expressed as a feasibility function φ_∀(s,a) ∈ [0,1] (equivalently, cost c_∀ = −log φ_∀). The constraint is distilled post-hoc from the learned robust policy via standard ICRL, so the final deployable artifact is a single cost function usable without modeling the opponent.

## Method

AR-ICRL alternates between two sub-problems until the mixture policy reproduces expert demonstrations. First, Safety-Robust Policy Optimization formulates a two-player Markov game: a player policy π^pl maximizes reward while respecting the inferred constraint, and an opponent policy π^op adversarially maximizes cumulative cost; the mixture π^mix = απ^pl + (1−α)π^op models the worst-case transition within the uncertainty set (Proposition 4.1). In discrete environments this is solved via value-based Q-functions with robust state/action sets; in continuous environments it extends PPO with a state-dependent Lagrange multiplier network. Second, Adaptive Constraint Inference updates the feasibility function φ_ω by maximizing the discounted log-likelihood of expert trajectories under the mixture policy, with closed-form gradient expressions derived for both hard-optimal (deterministic cost-maximizing opponent) and soft-optimal (softmax opponent) settings. After convergence, the robust constraint is distilled from π^pl using classical ICRL, yielding a deployable cost function that certifies safety across the entire uncertainty set (Proposition 4.6).

## Results

Under large-scale fully random noise, AR-ICRL achieves the lowest constraint violation rate across all three continuous environments: 31% on Blocked Half-Cheetah (vs. 40% MEICRL, 60% BC2L), 53% on Blocked Ant (vs. 59% MEICRL, 68% VICRL), and 31% on Crippled Walker (vs. 38% MEICRL, 39% BC2L). It also attains the highest feasible reward in every environment (727.8, 2326.2, and 52.4 respectively) and the lowest episode cost in most settings. The advantage is consistent across all three noise types (fully random, partially random, and attack noise), with AR-ICRL maintaining the lowest violation rate in all 9 environment-noise combinations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AR-ICRL consistently outperforms all four baselines on the primary safety metric (constraint violation rate) across all three continuous environments and all three noise types. Against MEICRL (Malik et al., 2021), AR-ICRL reduces violation rate by 9 percentage points on Blocked Half-Cheetah (31% vs. 40%), 6 points on Blocked Ant (53% vs. 59%), and 7 points on Crippled Walker (31% vs. 38%) under fully random noise. Against VICRL (Liu et al., 2023), the gap is larger: 21 points on Half-Cheetah (31% vs. 52%) and 15 points on Ant (53% vs. 68%). Against IRCO (Yu et al., 2022), AR-ICRL leads by 12 points on Half-Cheetah (31% vs. 43%) and 7 points on Ant (53% vs. 60%), though the margin narrows on Crippled Walker (31% vs. 34%). Notably, BC2L and VICRL achieve higher feasible rewards on Blocked Ant (6218.7 and 5650.6 vs. 4382.9 for AR-ICRL under partially random noise) due to their step-wise cost prediction, but at the cost of higher violation rates (65% and 69% vs. 59%), which the authors note is unacceptable for safety-critical deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Inverse Constrained Reinforcement Learning (ICRL) and robust RL under model misspecification. It builds directly on the permissibility-indicator formulation of Malik et al. (2021) and the maximum causal entropy likelihood framework of Gleave & Toyer (2022) for constraint inference, while borrowing the game-theoretic opponent-policy abstraction from Viano et al. (2021) to handle transition dynamics mismatch. The key departure from prior ICRL is that all existing methods (Scobee & Sastry 2020, Malik et al. 2021, Liu et al. 2023) train and test constraints in an identical environment; this paper formalizes the Robust Constraint Inference (RCI) problem where the inferred constraint must remain valid under a bounded uncertainty set of mismatched dynamics, and introduces a two-player Markov game (player vs. safety opponent) as the mechanism to enforce this robustness during both policy optimization and constraint learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: deploying ICRL in safety-critical domains (autonomous driving, robot control) inevitably involves train-test dynamics mismatch, and the paper demonstrates that even a perfectly recovered constraint can lead to constraint violations under perturbed dynamics. The theoretical contribution (Proposition 3.3) formalizes why absolute robustness is infeasible and motivates the relative robustness framework. Key limitations include the requirement for online environment interaction (ruling out offline settings), the assumption that dynamics mismatch is bounded within a predefined uncertainty set, and the relatively modest empirical margins over baselines (e.g., 31% vs. 40% violation rate). Open questions include extending to offline ICRL, handling unbounded or adversarially chosen dynamics shifts, and scaling the two-player game to high-dimensional continuous control without the discrete-environment simplifications used in the forward pass.
