## Problem

Existing MARL algorithms excel in zero-sum or pure-cooperative settings but fail in mixed-motive environments where agent relationships are non-deterministic and best responses shift with co-players' behavior. While opponent modeling offers a path forward, prior approaches either suffer from intractable nested belief inference (I-POMDP) or lack efficient mechanisms to reason about and utilize inferred co-player information for rapid few-shot adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240805/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes grid-based environment states (agent positions, prey/snowdrift locations on an 8×8 grid), observed co-player action trajectories, and a maintained belief distribution over a discrete set of co-player goals (e.g., 'hunt stag' vs. 'hunt hare'). It produces the focal agent's next action, selected via Boltzmann rationality over MCTS-estimated action values averaged across N_s sampled goal combinations, along with updated belief distributions over co-player goals at both intra-episode and inter-episode timescales. The goal-conditioned policy network outputs a full action distribution per goal, which is used both for belief updates and for simulating co-player behavior during planning.

## Method

HOP hierarchically combines an opponent modeling module with an MCTS-based planning module. The opponent modeling module infers co-player goals via two complementary Bayesian updates: intra-OM refines beliefs within an episode using observed actions as likelihood evidence, while inter-OM updates inter-episode priors via a time-discounted Monte Carlo estimate (controlled by horizon weight α). A goal-conditioned policy network is trained by supervised learning (negative log-likelihood) to predict co-player actions given state and inferred goal. The planning module runs MCTS for N_s rounds, each sampling a goal combination from current beliefs and simulating co-player actions from the goal-conditioned policy, then averages the resulting Q-values across rounds. A policy-value network (following AlphaZero/MuZero) provides pUCT priors and initial state values for MCTS, and is updated with cross-entropy policy loss and MSE value loss. This goal-conditioned abstraction avoids the exponential nested belief inference of I-POMDP while enabling efficient adaptation through two-timescale belief correction.

## Results

In few-shot adaptation, HOP achieves the highest overall best adaptation percentage across all three environments: 100% in MSH-4h2s, 83.3% in both MSH-4h1s and MSG, compared to at most 50% for the next-best baseline (direct-OM in MSG). In self-play, HOP achieves the highest average reward in MSG (close to the theoretical optimum of 30.0) and learns the stag-hunting cooperative strategy in both MSH settings. Against three defectors in MSH-4h2s, HOP achieves a normalized reward of 0.36, substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: direct-OM at 0.31, LOLA at 0.28).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOP outperforms all five baselines in the majority of adaptation scenarios across all three environments. Against LOLA (opponent-learning awareness), HOP achieves 0.97 vs. 0.92 (vs. A3C co-players) and 0.97 vs. 0.77 (vs. SI co-players) in MSH-4h2s, with the gap widening in MSG where HOP scores 0.78 vs. LOLA's 0.31 against direct-OM co-players. Against A3C (self-interested RL), HOP leads in most settings (e.g., 0.99 vs. 0.91 in MSH-4h2s vs. LOLA), though A3C matches HOP against random co-players in MSH-4h1s (0.85 vs. 0.96). Against PS-A3C (prosocial RL), HOP shows the largest margins: 0.88 vs. 0.24 in MSH-4h2s vs. LOLA, and 0.65 vs. 0.33 in MSG vs. random, reflecting PS-A3C's inability to adapt away from its cooperative prior. Against SI (social influence), HOP dominates in MSH-4h2s (0.97 vs. 0.77 vs. LOLA) and MSG (0.78 vs. 0.74 vs. LOLA). Against PR2 (probabilistic recursive reasoning, tested only in MSG), HOP achieves 0.78 vs. 0.00 against LOLA co-players and 0.55 vs. 0.14 against defectors, indicating PR2's recursive reasoning fails in these mixed-motive settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

HOP synthesizes three research threads: (1) the AlphaZero/MuZero paradigm of combining learned policy-value networks with MCTS for planning, (2) computational Theory of Mind for inferring agents' goals from observed actions, and (3) opponent modeling in multi-agent RL (LOLA, I-POMDP, PR2). The key departure from I-POMDP is replacing intractable nested belief inference with explicit goal-level beliefs and goal-conditioned neural policies, making sequential multi-agent planning tractable. The dual belief-update mechanism (intra-OM within episodes, inter-OM across episodes) is a practical innovation not present in prior ToM or opponent-modeling work, and the application to mixed-motive environments (as opposed to the zero-sum or pure-cooperative settings dominating prior MARL) is the paper's primary positioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Mixed-motive environments are the most realistic multi-agent setting yet have been largely neglected in MARL, and HOP provides a principled, scalable framework for few-shot adaptation in this regime. The emergence of social intelligence—self-organized cooperation and an alliance of the disadvantaged that actively misleads a 'greedy' agent—suggests that goal-inference plus planning can produce sophisticated social behavior without explicit social reward shaping. Key limitations include the need for hand-defined goal sets (no autonomous goal abstraction), reliance on Level-0 ToM, and evaluation only in small 4-agent grid worlds; extending to higher-order ToM, larger-scale environments, and human-agent interaction remain open.
