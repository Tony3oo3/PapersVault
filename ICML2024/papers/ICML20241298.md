## Problem

Existing debiasing methods for language models impose constant constraints that remove all demographic or property information from every generated token, inevitably degrading fluency. No formal study had established how much fluency must be sacrificed to achieve a target level of fairness, nor whether these methods could achieve Pareto optimality in the fairness-fluency trade-off.

## Input / Output

The method operates at decoding time on a frozen language model (GPT-2 Large, OPT-7B, or Falcon-7B Instruct), taking a text prompt (possibly adversarial) and a small set of seed words identifying demographic groups. At each generation step, it applies bias-tuning (BitFit-style updates to the top 18 layers) to a copy of the model parameters, then mixes the debiased and original next-token distributions before sampling. The eLIDAO variant additionally prepends a zero-shot instruction to the prompt for a reference pass, ensuring demographic group mentions appear in the output so that prompt-context bias becomes visible to the debiasing mechanism.

## Method

The core theoretical result (Theorem 2.1) proves that to achieve I(g;a)=0, it suffices that at each token position the token is conditionally independent of at least one of the global property g or demographic group a, rather than both as prior constant-constraint methods require. LIDAO operationalizes this by adaptively minimizing either ℓ(g;t) or ℓ(a;t) at each step, using either a min-based strategy (with history-weighted rescaling to handle scale differences) or a product-based strategy (where the gradient naturally weights each loss by the magnitude of the other). For the adversarial-prompt setting, Proposition 3.1 bounds the joint bias I(g(x);a(c,x)) by the generation bias (solvable by LIDAO) plus a conditional entropy term measuring how inferable the demographic group is from the generation alone; eLIDAO minimizes this second term by using the LM's own instruction-following ability as a lightweight reference model to inject demographic group mentions into the output.

## Results

On the primary debiasing evaluation (Table 1, bias w.r.t. generated a(x)), LIDAO achieves the lowest bias in 7 out of 9 model-task scenarios. For example, on OPT, LIDAO reduces sentiment bias from 0.98 (base) to 0.03 and toxicity bias to 0.00, while maintaining PPL of 40.16 vs. UDDIA's 62.78. On GPT-2, LIDAO cuts sentiment bias from 4.41 to 0.32 and regard bias from 3.89 to 2.15 with PPL of 27.49. The extended eLIDAO further improves toxicity bias on GPT-2 from 9.35 to 5.91 while keeping PPL at 30.20.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LIDAO consistently outperforms UDDIA (its closest baseline, sharing the same MI estimators and bias-tuning mechanism) on both bias and perplexity across all three LMs: e.g., on OPT sentiment, LIDAO achieves bias 0.03 vs. UDDIA's 5.40 with PPL 40.16 vs. 62.78. PPLM degrades fluency substantially (PPL 34–72) while offering only moderate bias reduction, and on OPT it actually increases sentiment bias from 0.98 to 4.80. GeDi and DExperts (tested only on GPT-2 due to tokenizer incompatibility) show competitive PPL (16.00 for DExperts) but DExperts produces the most negative sentiment and highest toxicity among all methods, while GeDi inflates PPL to ~48. On the adversarial-prompt evaluation (Table 2, bias w.r.t. joint a(c,x)), eLIDAO achieves the best or second-best bias in 6 of 9 scenarios, consistently outperforming LIDAO which lacks prompt-awareness and suffers from the H(a(c,x)|a(x)) term identified in Prop 3.1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on UDDIA (Yang et al., 2023), which formulates debiasing as simultaneous minimization of mutual information between a global property and a demographic group at each decoding step. The key conceptual departure is a theoretical result (Theorem 2.1) showing that this simultaneous constraint is provably excessive: it suffices to ensure each token is independent of *at least one* of the two variables, not both. This reframes prior decoding-time methods (PPLM, GeDi, DExperts, UDDIA) as imposing 'constant constraints' that unnecessarily restrict generation, and motivates LIDAO's adaptive, limited-intervention strategy. The adversarial extension (eLIDAO) draws on Schick et al.'s (2021) self-debiasing idea but replaces a trained reference model with the LM's own instruction-following ability, making it lighter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formal information-theoretic characterization of the fairness-fluency trade-off in LM debiasing, providing a negative answer to whether existing constant-constraint methods can achieve Pareto optimality. The practical impact is a lightweight, training-free decoding-time method that works across model scales (0.7B–7B) without requiring external reference models or curated data. Key limitations include reliance on approximate MI proxies (so bias is reduced but not eliminated), focus on gender only, mono-lingual evaluation, and algorithmic (rather than human) fairness metrics. Open questions include developing tighter MI estimators, extending to race/age groups with adversarial prompts, and multi-lingual generalization.
