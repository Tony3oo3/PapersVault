## Problem

In-context learning (ICL) has been a hallmark capability of Transformer-based language models, but whether attention-free architectures such as state-space models (SSMs) can also perform ICL remains largely unexplored. This paper asks: can Mamba and other SSMs learn to learn from in-context demonstrations, and where do they succeed or fail relative to Transformers?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241612/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Models are trained from scratch on synthetic ICL tasks where the input is a prompt consisting of N input-output demonstration pairs (e.g., (x, f(x)) with x ∈ R^d) followed by a query input, and the model must predict the function output. For retrieval tasks (vector MQAR), the input is a sequence of key-value vector pairs plus query vectors, and the output is the corresponding value vectors. For formal language benchmarks (GINC, RegBench), the input is token sequences with 0–64 in-context examples and the output is next-token predictions. All regression tasks use d=20 dimensional inputs with N ranging from 41 to 512 points depending on the task.

## Method

The paper introduces a diverse suite of ICL tasks (linear/sparse regression, 2NN regression, decision trees, outlier-robust regression, sparse parity, chain-of-thought I/O, and vector MQAR) and trains Mamba, S4, S4-Mamba, and GPT-2-style Transformers from scratch on each task with matched parameter counts and FLOPs. The key architectural contribution is MambaFormer, a hybrid that interleaves multi-head attention blocks with Mamba blocks (replacing the MLP/FFN), with an initial Mamba block that encodes positional information and eliminates the need for explicit positional encoding. Ablations show that placing a Mamba block as the first layer is critical for learning sparse parity, while the attention component is essential for retrieval. MambaFormer achieves best-of-both-worlds performance: it matches Transformers on retrieval and decision trees while matching Mamba on sparse parity and heavy-outlier regression, outperforming either architecture alone on tasks where each individually struggles.

## Results

Across the ICL task suite, Mamba matches Transformer on standard regression tasks (linear, sparse linear, 2NN) and outperforms it on sparse parity (where Transformer performs at random-guess level) and many-outlier regression. However, Mamba fails on vector MQAR retrieval (MSE > 0.1 vs. Transformer's ~1e-6) and underperforms on decision trees. The proposed MambaFormer closes both gaps, achieving near-Transformer retrieval accuracy (MSE 3.37e-6 vs. 8.76e-7 at embed dim 128) while matching Mamba on sparse parity. On formal language benchmarks, hybrid models (Standard Hybrid, MambaFormer) achieve 99.8–99.9% accuracy on RegBench, far exceeding Mamba (69.4%) and Transformer (92.6%) at 15 epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017), Mamba matches on linear/sparse/2NN regression and CoT I/O, outperforms on sparse parity (Transformer cannot exceed random guessing even at 768 embed dim / 24 layers / 1M iterations) and many-outlier regression, but loses on decision trees, orthogonal-outlier regression, and vector MQAR (MSE 1.5e-1 vs. 2.5e-7). Against S4 (Gu et al., 2022), Mamba consistently outperforms across all tasks, with the input-dependent selection mechanism providing meaningful gains on complex tasks like 2NN and decision trees. Against LSTM (Hochreiter & Schmidhuber, 1997), the comparison is task-dependent: LSTM achieves 96.4% ICL accuracy on GINC (vs. Mamba's 87.1%), but on RegBench, LSTM lags far behind (51.0% at 15 epochs) while MambaFormer and Standard Hybrid reach 99.8–99.9%. MambaFormer (the proposed hybrid) wins or ties on every task in Table 1, simultaneously solving sparse parity and retrieval—tasks that are individually unsolvable by Mamba and Transformer respectively—while matching both on the remaining tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ICL evaluation framework established by Garg et al. (2022) for Transformers to state-space models, particularly Mamba (Gu & Dao, 2023), and its predecessor S4 (Gu et al., 2022). It builds on the retrieval evaluation paradigm from Arora et al. (2023) and Olsson et al. (2022), the sparse parity task from Bhattamishra et al. (2023), and the chain-of-thought ICL setup from Li et al. (2023b). The proposed MambaFormer architecture departs from the Standard Hybrid in Gu & Dao (2023) by prepending a Mamba block to eliminate positional encoding, drawing on the observation that recurrent layers inherently encode position. Conceptually, the paper challenges the prevailing assumption that attention is a necessary component for ICL by demonstrating that SSMs can match or exceed Transformers on several ICL tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evidence that SSMs are viable in-context learners, identifying complementary strengths: SSMs excel at sparse parity and heavy-outlier regression while Transformers dominate retrieval and decision-tree learning. MambaFormer offers a practical hybrid that closes both gaps, and its strong performance on RegBench (99.8–99.9% accuracy) suggests potential for real language ICL. Key limitations include the small model scale (≤90M parameters) and synthetic task settings, leaving open whether these findings transfer to the 7B+ regime where ICL typically emerges in practice. The paper also leaves unresolved which specific architectural components (convolution, gating, input-dependent selection) enable parity learning, a question with implications for future architecture design.
