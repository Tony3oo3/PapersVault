## Problem

Existing post-training quantization methods for Vision Transformers typically treat weight and activation quantization independently, overlooking their intricate interdependence. This neglect leads to considerable compounded quantization error, particularly severe at low bit-widths (e.g., W3A4), where many methods collapse to near-chance accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242550/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained ViT (ViT, DeiT, or Swin variant) and a tiny calibration set of 32 images (ImageNet) or 1 image (COCO), from which it estimates activation statistics (mean and covariance) for the quantization error proxies. It outputs channel-wise quantized weights and layer-wise quantized activations, using a uniform quantizer for most layers and a log√2 quantizer specifically for long-tail post-Softmax activations. The reparameterization technique from RepQ-ViT is applied to initialize activation quantizers, simplifying channel-wise to layer-wise quantization.

## Method

ERQ decomposes the joint weight-activation quantization error into two sequential sub-problems. First, Aqer formulates activation error mitigation as a Ridge Regression problem, yielding a closed-form weight adjustment δW* = −W·E[x̄δxᵀ](E[x̄x̄ᵀ]+λ₁I)⁻¹ that absorbs activation quantization error into full-precision weights. Second, Wqer iteratively quantizes the first half of remaining unquantized weights per output channel, mitigating the resulting error in two stages: (1) Rounding Refinement, which derives a Gaussian-based proxy (μμᵀ+Σ) for the output error to efficiently select top-k rounding-direction flips via gradient sign matching, reducing MIPQ cost from ~130 hours to ~4 minutes; and (2) a second Ridge Regression that distributes residual error into the remaining full-precision weights. This quantize-and-correct loop repeats until all weights are quantized, with the two stages collectively achieving 21–28% per-layer error reduction.

## Results

On ImageNet, ERQ achieves 45.68% top-1 accuracy for W3A4 ViT-S, surpassing GPTQ (23.32%) by 22.36 percentage points. For the standard W4A4 setting, ERQ attains 72.56% on DeiT-S, 68.91% on ViT-S, and 82.44% on Swin-B, consistently outperforming all baselines. At W5A5, ERQ reaches 84.50% on Swin-B and 78.83% on ViT-S. On COCO, ERQ achieves 36.8 box AP and 36.6 mask AP for W4A4 Mask R-CNN (Swin-T), and 50.0 box AP / 43.6 mask AP for W4A4 Cascade Mask R-CNN (Swin-S).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ERQ dominates all 14 baselines across every bit-width and model variant tested. The most striking margin is against GPTQ at W3A4, where ERQ gains 22.36 points on ViT-S (45.68 vs 23.32) and 9.25 points on ViT-B (53.88 vs 44.63); at W4A4 the gap narrows to 1.32–2.13 points but ERQ still leads on all seven models. Against RepQ-ViT, ERQ wins by 1.33–8.68 points at W4A4 and by 0.41–1.28 points at W5A5. AdaRound is the closest competitor at W5A5 (e.g., 84.09 vs 84.50 on Swin-B), but ERQ pulls ahead by 1.5–9.3 points at W4A4 and by 1.8–32.4 points at W3A4. BRECQ, QDrop, and PD-Quant show severe instability at W3A4 (often below 10% on ViT-S), while ERQ maintains 45.68%. On COCO detection/segmentation, ERQ leads GPTQ by 0.5 box AP / 0.3 mask AP (Mask R-CNN Swin-T, W4A4) and by 0.8 / 0.6 (Cascade Mask R-CNN Swin-T). FQ-ViT and PTQ4ViT collapse to near-random accuracy (≤0.64%) at W3A4, highlighting the fragility of prior ViT-specific quantizers without error-reduction mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

ERQ extends the iterative quantization-and-correction framework of GPTQ and Incremental Network Quantization by explicitly decomposing the entangled weight-activation quantization error into two sequential sub-problems. It borrows the reparameterization technique and log√2 quantizer from RepQ-ViT for activation quantization, and builds on AdaRound's floor/ceil rounding concept, but introduces a Gaussian-based proxy that replaces the prohibitively expensive MIPQ solver with a gradient-guided Rounding Refinement. The key conceptual departure from GPTQ is the addition of Aqer, which compensates activation quantization error into full-precision weights via Ridge Regression before any weight quantization occurs—something GPTQ does not address since it does not quantize activations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-step sequential error decomposition (activation first, then weight) provides a clean and principled framework that practitioners can adopt for ViT quantization, and the proxy-based Rounding Refinement offers a 150× speedup over MIPQ with negligible accuracy loss, making sub-4-bit ViT quantization practically feasible in minutes. However, the method currently only handles weight layers and does not account for quantization error in self-attention, and the authors acknowledge they cannot yet apply ERQ to LLMs due to computational constraints. Open questions include whether the Gaussian proxy assumption holds for deeper or more heterogeneous architectures, and whether more flexible rounding targets beyond binary floor/ceil could yield further gains.
