## Problem

Linear reduced-order models for parameterized PDEs are limited by the Kolmogorov barrier, achieving at best 1/√n error decay for transport-dominated problems like advection and wave dynamics. Existing neural network surrogates (e.g., operator learning) either require large training datasets or treat time as just another input coordinate, failing to exploit the special causal and continuous-in-time structure of PDE evolution. The paper asks: can one build a nonlinear reduced model that circumvents the Kolmogorov barrier while remaining data-efficient and respecting the distinct role of time?

## Input / Output

The CoLoRA network ũ takes a spatial coordinate x ∈ Ω as its direct input and produces a scalar (or vector) solution field value, functioning as an implicit neural representation evaluable at any resolution independent of the training grid. Time t and physics parameter μ do not enter as network inputs; instead, they modulate the network through a low-dimensional latent state φ(t,μ) ∈ ℝ^q (typically q=2–3) generated by a small hypernetwork h. The output is the full PDE solution field u(x,t;μ), and for systems of PDEs (e.g., Burgers', RDE) the output layer is widened to produce one field per variable.

## Method

CoLoRA modifies LoRA layers from LLM fine-tuning by introducing a continuous-in-time scalar (or vector) scaling α(t;μ) on the low-rank weight update AB, so each layer computes C(x) = Wx + α(t;μ)ABx + b. The matrices W, A, B, and biases are trained offline (pre-training) on a small set of high-fidelity trajectories, while only the few online parameters φ(t,μ) = [α₁,…,α_q] are adapted during the online phase. A hypernetwork h(t,μ;ψ) generates φ to enforce smooth temporal dynamics, and the offline loss jointly optimizes θ and ψ via mean relative error over training trajectories. In the equation-driven variant (CoLoRA-EQ), the latent state is advanced in time by solving a Dirac-Frenkel (Neural Galerkin) variational condition that makes the PDE residual orthogonal to the tangent space of the CoLoRA manifold, yielding Galerkin-optimal, causal, and conservation-preserving solutions. Composing multiple CoLoRA layers provides the nonlinearity needed to circumvent the Kolmogorov barrier, while the small q keeps the model data-efficient (≈10 trajectories suffice) and inference orders of magnitude faster than full-order solvers.

## Results

On the 3D spherical shallow water benchmark, CoLoRA-D achieves an MSE of 3.19e-06 with 335,744 parameters, outperforming CORAL (3.44e-06, 1,049,344 params) and DINo (4.48e-05, 2,022,912 params). On the 2D wave problem, CoLoRA-D reaches an MSE of 1.891e-07 with only 7,505 parameters, beating DINo (9.495e-06, 579,776 params) by nearly two orders of magnitude in both accuracy and parameter count. In the data-efficiency experiment (Table 2), with only 10 training trajectories, CoLoRA-D achieves relative errors of 2.37e-3 (Vlasov), 1.76e-3 (Burgers'), and 4.47e-4 (RDE), while F-FNO produces errors roughly one order of magnitude higher (7.48e-2, 2.40e-2, 5.69e-3). CoLoRA-D is more than four orders of magnitude faster than the full-order models, and CoLoRA-EQ still achieves about two orders of magnitude speedup while preserving causality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoLoRA-D outperforms all five baselines on every benchmark where they overlap. Against F-FNO (Tran et al., 2023), CoLoRA-D achieves roughly one order of magnitude lower relative error (e.g., 1.76e-3 vs. 2.40e-2 on Burgers') while using only 10 training trajectories versus the ~100 needed by F-FNO for comparable accuracy. Against DINo (Yin et al., 2023), CoLoRA-D wins on both the 2D wave problem (MSE 1.891e-7 vs. 9.495e-6) and 3D shallow water (3.19e-6 vs. 4.48e-5) while using 77× fewer parameters on the wave problem (7,505 vs. 579,776). Against CORAL (Serrano et al., 2023), CoLoRA-D achieves a slightly lower MSE on 3D shallow water (3.19e-6 vs. 3.44e-6) with approximately 3× fewer parameters (335,744 vs. 1,049,344). Against MP-PDE (Brandstetter et al., 2022) and DeepONet (Lu et al., 2021), CoLoRA-D wins by 1–4 orders of magnitude in MSE on both benchmark problems, with DeepONet being the weakest baseline (MSE 6.54e-3 on 3D shallow water vs. CoLoRA's 3.19e-6). The equation-driven variant CoLoRA-EQ is slightly less accurate than CoLoRA-D (e.g., 2.73e-3 vs. 2.37e-3 on Vlasov with 10 trajectories) but provides Galerkin-optimal solutions, causality, and conservation of physical quantities such as mass in the Vlasov problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoLoRA adapts the LoRA fine-tuning paradigm from large language models to PDE model reduction by introducing a continuous-in-time scaling α(t,μ) on low-rank weight matrices, so that the network is 'fine-tuned' as the solution evolves rather than at discrete downstream tasks. For the equation-driven variant (CoLoRA-EQ), it couples with Neural Galerkin schemes (Bruna et al., 2024) to obtain Galerkin-optimal latent trajectories via the Dirac-Frenkel variational principle. Conceptually, the key departure from prior implicit neural representation methods (DINo, CORAL, CROM) and operator learning (FNO, DeepONet) is treating time as a weight-adaptation variable rather than a spatial input, which imposes a sequential-in-time inductive bias and keeps the online parameter count extremely small (2–3 scalars). This connects to the long line of dynamic low-rank approximation in scientific computing (Koch & Lubich, Sapsis & Lermusiaux) but generalizes it to a multi-layer nonlinear setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoLoRA offers a practical path to breaking the Kolmogorov barrier for transport-dominated PDEs while remaining data-efficient (≈10 training trajectories suffice) and orders-of-magnitude faster than full-order solvers, making it attractive for data-scarce regimes where high-fidelity simulations are expensive. The equation-driven variant provides causality, conservation of physical quantities (e.g., mass in Vlasov), and a variational foundation that opens the door to residual-based error estimation—capabilities purely data-driven surrogates lack. Key open questions include: (i) theoretical guarantees beyond the contrived linear advection example, (ii) handling bifurcations or qualitative regime changes not represented in training data, and (iii) scaling to higher-dimensional parameter and spatial domains, where active data collection will likely be essential.
