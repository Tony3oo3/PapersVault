## Problem

Existing geometric molecular models are either invariant-only (relying on relative distances that lose higher-order structural signals) or equivariant-only (using costly tensor-product operations), and those handling both do so through heuristic, task-specific designs without a unifying principle. The paper identifies the need for a general, flexible architecture that simultaneously learns high-quality invariant and equivariant representations with strong performance on both task types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240275/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes atom-type embeddings (R^d per atom) and 3D Cartesian coordinates as input; the equivariant input is constructed from mean-centered positions decomposed into direction (unit vector) and scale (Gaussian Basis Kernel encoding of the norm), yielding R^{3×d} per atom. Outputs are task-dependent: scalar invariant predictions (energy, HOMO-LUMO gap) from the invariant stream, or vector/tensor equivariant predictions (relaxed positions, forces, particle trajectories) from the equivariant stream. The architecture operates at O(n²) complexity in the number of atoms, making it applicable to large adsorbate-catalyst complexes (460K+ training samples on OC20).

## Method

GeoMFormer is a two-stream Transformer where an invariant stream (R^{n×d}) and an equivariant stream (R^{n×3×d}) are updated in parallel via self-attention, cross-attention, and feed-forward layers. The key mechanism is bidirectional cross-attention: the invariant stream queries the equivariant stream using a dot-product operation (projecting R^{3×d} → R^d) to extract geometric signals, while the equivariant stream queries the invariant stream using a scalar-product operation (scaling R^{3×d} by R^d) to inject non-linear transformations. The equivariant self-attention modifies the standard attention score to a sum of 3D dot products over the feature dimension, and the equivariant FFN uses invariant representations as a gating function to avoid breaking equivariance. The paper proves that PaiNN and TorchMD-Net are special instantiations of this framework, demonstrating its generality.

## Results

On OC20 IS2RE (invariant energy prediction), GeoMFormer achieves an average Energy MAE of 0.4141 eV and 8.59% EwT, outperforming the best baseline Equiformer (0.4410 eV, 6.04% EwT) by 42.2% relative EwT improvement. On OC20 IS2RS (equivariant structure prediction), it achieves 10.67% average ADwT, a 27.6% relative improvement over GemNet-OC (8.36%). On PCQM4Mv2, GeoMFormer reaches 0.0734 MAE with O(n²) complexity, beating the best quadratic model GPS++ (0.0778) by 6.7% while remaining competitive with the O(n³) Uni-Mol+ (0.0708). On Molecule3D and N-Body Simulation, it achieves 16.3%/11.6% MAE reduction (random/scaffold splits) and 33.8% MSE reduction, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoMFormer outperforms all 32 compared baselines across every evaluated dataset. On OC20 IS2RE, it beats Equiformer (the strongest equivariant Transformer baseline) by 0.027 eV in average Energy MAE and 2.55 points in EwT, with particularly large gains on OOD splits (e.g., 6.70% vs 4.64% EwT on OOD-Ads). On OC20 IS2RS, it surpasses GemNet-OC (8.36% ADwT) by 2.31 points and TorchMD-Net (3.13%) by 7.54 points, demonstrating superior equivariant prediction. On PCQM4Mv2, GeoMFormer (0.0734 MAE, O(n²)) outperforms all quadratic-complexity baselines including GPS++ (0.0778) and GraphGPS (0.0858), while remaining close to the O(n³) Uni-Mol+ (0.0708). On Molecule3D, it achieves the best MAE on both random (0.0252 vs SphereNet's 0.0301) and scaffold (0.1045 vs SphereNet's 0.1182) splits. On N-Body Simulation, it reduces MSE from 0.0071 (EGNN) to 0.0047, a 33.8% improvement over the best equivariant baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoMFormer extends the Transformer architecture to geometric molecular modeling by introducing a two-stream design (invariant and equivariant) bridged by cross-attention modules, departing from prior work that either relies solely on invariant features (SchNet, DimeNet++, SphereNet) or equivariant operations (Tensor Field Networks, EGNN, PaiNN). The paper demonstrates that PaiNN and TorchMD-Net can be recovered as special instantiations of its general framework, positioning GeoMFormer as a unifying design philosophy rather than a single model. It builds on Graphormer-3D's structural encoding approach and the standard multi-head attention mechanism, but the key conceptual departure is the bidirectional cross-attention that lets each stream leverage the other's representational strengths.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, flexible template for geometric molecular modeling that practitioners can instantiate with various component choices, reducing the need for ad-hoc architecture design. Its O(n²) complexity makes it applicable to large molecular systems where O(n³) methods (e.g., Uni-Mol+) become prohibitive. Open questions include whether the architecture scales effectively to much larger model and dataset sizes, and how it extends to additional downstream tasks beyond energy, structure, and force prediction. The paper acknowledges these as future directions, and the practical impact will depend on whether the generality claim translates into consistent advantages across diverse real-world applications.
