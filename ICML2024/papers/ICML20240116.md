## Problem

Two known failure modes of self-attention—rank collapse (uniform attention degrades expressivity) and entropy collapse (localized attention destabilizes training)—appear to contradict each other because they implicate opposite attention patterns. The paper asks when self-attention localizes and whether a single parameter-level condition can simultaneously prevent both collapses.

## Input / Output

The theoretical analysis takes a sequence of T contextual tokens modeled as a Gaussian random walk and produces a signal propagation probability ρ(θ) over relative token positions, fully determined by two eigenspectrum parameters: the normalized mean ξ = tr(W)/√tr(W²) and the normalized scale η = √tr(W²)/λ. The practical LOCATER method takes standard text sequences for causal language modeling (64-token windows, batch size 64) and produces next-token predictions while regularizing the QK eigenspectrum to maintain a fixed trace while shrinking the Frobenius norm.

## Method

The paper introduces signal propagation probability to formalize how likely each token's signal reaches the QK-gradient, then approximates softmax with a piecewise linear function to enable tractable Gaussian moment computation. Under a Gaussian random walk assumption on token embeddings, they derive closed-form expressions for ρ(θ) showing that attention localizes precisely when the QK eigenspectrum concentrates around a non-zero mean (small tr(W²) with |tr(W)| bounded away from zero). They prove this single condition simultaneously slows rank collapse (via the ℓ₁-norm lower bound on |tr(W)|) and increases attention entropy (via the spectral norm upper bound on tr(W²)), reconciling the two apparently contradictory failure modes. The practical contribution is LOCATER, a regularization that minimizes tr(W_QK^T W_QK) while penalizing deviation of tr(W_QK) from a target value, which empirically shrinks the eigenspectrum scale, raises attention entropy, and improves perplexity on WikiText-2 across 1-, 3-, and 6-layer transformers.

## Results

On WikiText-2 with 1-layer transformers (d=128, T=64), applying LOCATER regularization with κ₁=100 and increasing κ₂ from 0 to 1 monotonically shrinks the eigenspectrum scale tr(W_QK²), increases attention entropy, and improves validation perplexity. The signal propagation probability ρ_i exhibits salient horizontal stripes (indicating localized attention on specific token positions) under LOCATER, whereas without LOCATER the probability remains uniform across all tokens at every training iteration. These trends are consistent across 1-, 3-, and 6-layer transformers and across embedding dimensions d=32 and d=128, confirming that eigenspectrum variance reduction drives both higher entropy and better language modeling performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is primarily theoretical and does not benchmark against competing methods in a standard sense; the empirical section serves as validation of the theoretical predictions rather than a method comparison. The implicit 'baseline' is the standard transformer trained without LOCATER (κ₂=0), which exhibits uniform signal propagation probability and lower attention entropy throughout training. The LOCATER-regularized model (κ₂>0) consistently outperforms this baseline on perplexity while simultaneously achieving higher attention entropy, empirically corroborating the paper's central claim that minimizing the W_QK-eigenspectrum variance reconciles the rank-collapse and entropy-collapse failure modes. The paper also contrasts its eigenspectrum-based localization characterization against the prior independent analyses of rank collapse (Dong et al., 2021) and entropy collapse (Zhai et al., 2023), showing that both phenomena are governed by the same eigenspectrum variance quantity (Eq. 8), thereby unifying two previously contradictory perspectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously independent lines of analysis on attention failure modes: the rank collapse of Dong et al. (2021), which shows pure attention layers lose expressivity, and the entropy collapse of Zhai et al. (2023), which links low attention entropy to training plateaus. The authors introduce a signal propagation probability framework grounded in a Gaussian random walk model (borrowed from Arora et al., 2016) to characterize when attention localizes, showing that both collapse modes are governed by the eigenspectrum variance of the query-key parameter matrix. The key departure from prior work is the reconciliation: rather than treating uniform and non-uniform attention as opposing failure modes, the paper shows that a concentrated eigenspectrum (non-zero mean, small variance) simultaneously mitigates both, providing a single geometric criterion for healthy attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The eigenspectrum-variance perspective offers a concrete, interpretable diagnostic for attention health and yields a practical regularization (LOCATER) that shrinks the QK spectral scale while preserving the trace, empirically improving perplexity on WikiText-2. However, the analysis is restricted to 1-layer transformers under a strong Gaussian random walk assumption, and the paper explicitly acknowledges that *why* localization improves performance remains unexplained. Open questions include extending the framework to multi-layer settings with layer normalization, relaxing the random walk assumption to capture real token correlations, and understanding the interaction between eigenspectrum dynamics and the multi-phase training behavior observed in the experiments.
