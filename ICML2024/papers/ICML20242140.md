## Problem

Existing pruning metrics (magnitude, gradient, PLATON) compare weights uniformly across the entire network, ignoring the hierarchical structure and the fundamentally different representational complexity of visual versus language modalities in vision-language models. This paper asks whether intrinsic dimension (ID) of layer representations can reveal modality-specific prunability and serve as a layer importance signal to guide asymmetric pruning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242140/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained vision-language model (BLIP, CLIP, FLAVA, LLaVA, or BUTD) and produces a sparsified version at target pruning ratios (40%–95%). ID is estimated per layer using TwoNN on 2,000 samples from MSCOCO, and the resulting per-layer ID values are multiplied into the importance score during iterative pruning over K epochs (typically 5) with a cubic schedule. The output is a pruned model evaluated on captioning, visual reasoning, retrieval, and classification tasks.

## Method

The paper estimates the intrinsic dimension of each layer's representations using the TwoNN algorithm and discovers a stark asymmetry: visual layers exhibit a hunchback-shaped ID profile ranging from 20 to 450, while language layers show a uniform, low ID (5–30). The core pruning mechanism multiplies the standard importance score S (magnitude, gradient, or PLATON) by the layer's ID, so that layers with higher representational complexity (visual) are pruned more conservatively and layers with lower complexity (language) are pruned more aggressively. This ID-weighted score is updated every 20 steps within each epoch of an iterative pruning schedule. The approach is model-agnostic and composable with any existing importance metric, requiring no architectural changes—only a per-layer scalar multiplier derived from the representation geometry.

## Results

On MSCOCO image captioning (BLIP model), the full model achieves CIDEr 133.3, BLEU@4 39.7, and SPICE 23.8. At 40% pruning, PLATON*ID retains CIDEr 131.4 (only a 1.9 drop), while at 80% and 90% pruning it achieves CIDEr 129.2 and 106.4 respectively, substantially outperforming all baselines. At extreme 95% pruning, PLATON*ID reaches CIDEr 39.0, compared to 39.5 for PLATON alone and 26.5 for Magnitude*ID. The ID-enhanced metrics consistently improve over their unenhanced counterparts, with the largest gains appearing at 80–90% sparsity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three weight-importance baselines—Magnitude (Zhu & Gupta, 2017), Gradient (Molchanov et al., 2019), and PLATON (Zhang et al., 2022)—each with and without the proposed ID multiplier. PLATON×ID dominates at moderate-to-high sparsity: at 80% pruning it achieves CIDEr 129.2 vs. 124.1 for PLATON alone (+5.1), and at 90% it reaches 106.4 vs. 93.1 (+13.3). Magnitude×ID improves over plain Magnitude by 17.8 CIDEr points at 80% (102.7 vs. 84.9) and 21.2 at 90% (56.5 vs. 35.3), while Gradient×ID gains 22.5 at 80% (69.7 vs. 47.2). At 40% pruning the gains are marginal (≤1 CIDEr point), and at 95% PLATON×ID (39.0) is essentially tied with PLATON (39.5). Across NLVR2 visual reasoning, ID integration rescues Magnitude and Gradient from near-random accuracy (51–53%) up to 75–80% at 40% pruning, while PLATON×ID maintains 81.5% vs. 81.4% for PLATON.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: intrinsic dimension (ID) analysis of neural network representations (Ansuini et al., 2019; Facco et al., 2017) and weight importance metrics for pruning (Zhu & Gupta, 2017; Molchanov et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2022). It adopts the TwoNN algorithm for ID estimation and the iterative pruning framework from Sanh et al. (2020), but its core contribution is an empirical study showing that vision and language modalities in pre-trained models occupy manifolds with fundamentally different ID profiles (hunchback vs. uniform), and that multiplying existing importance scores by layer-wise ID yields a simple yet effective layer-aware pruning signal. The work extends prior unimodal ID analyses (Ansuini et al., 2019 for vision; Aghajanyan et al., 2020 for language) to a joint multi-modal setting and connects ID to prunability following Ankner et al. (2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway — that language layers in vision-language models can be aggressively pruned (even 80%) with minimal performance loss while vision layers require conservative treatment — provides an actionable asymmetric pruning strategy for deploying multi-modal models under resource constraints. However, the method's impact is limited: the ID multiplication yields only modest gains over strong baselines like PLATON (e.g., 129.2 vs. 124.1 CIDEr at 80% pruning), the TwoNN estimation is computationally expensive at O(n²), and the paper does not explore how ID could inform architecture design or training strategies for maximally compressed models. Open questions include whether these ID-based asymmetries persist in much larger models (e.g., 7B+ parameter VLMs) and whether ID can guide structured (rather than unstructured) pruning or dimensionality reduction of individual layers.
