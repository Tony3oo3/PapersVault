## Problem

Certified unlearning provides strong theoretical guarantees for removing data from machine learning models, but existing results rely on convexity assumptions that are unrealistic for deep neural networks. Prior attempts to extend certified unlearning to DNNs either produced only empirical approximations without certification or depended on strong convexity assumptions that do not hold in practice. This paper bridges the gap by establishing certified unlearning guarantees for nonconvex (weakly convex) objectives typical of DNNs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained DNN model (MLP, AllCNN, or ResNet-18), a set of unlearned samples (typically 1,000 out of ~60,000 training points), and certification budgets (ε, δ). The original model must be trained with projected gradient descent enforcing a norm constraint ||w||₂ ≤ C to ensure the approximation error bound is tractable. The output is a certified unlearned model (original parameters plus a Newton-step correction plus Gaussian noise) together with a computable approximation error bound Δ that determines the required noise standard deviation σ = (Δ/ε)√(2 ln(1.25/δ)).

## Method

The core technique modifies the single-step Newton update in two ways to handle nonconvexity: (1) local convex approximation, which adds λI to the Hessian so that (H_{w*}+λI) is positive definite even when the Hessian has negative eigenvalues, and (2) a norm constraint on parameters that bounds ||w* − w̃*||₂ by 2C independent of the unlearned set size. For computational efficiency, the inverse Hessian is estimated via the LiSSA recursive algorithm (O(sp²) complexity, independent of dataset size n), and the gradient over the retained set is replaced by a scaled gradient over the unlearned set using the minimizer identity (reducing cost from O(np) to O(n_u·p)). The final unlearned model is the Newton-step approximation plus Gaussian noise calibrated to the derived error bound, yielding (ε,δ)-certified unlearning. The framework extends naturally to nonconvergence training (where the gradient norm is bounded by G rather than zero) and sequential unlearning (multiple successive requests), with the same error bound preserved in both settings.

## Results

On MNIST (MLP), certified unlearning achieves 97.60% F1 on the unlearned set (closest to the retrained model's 97.20%) and 98.28% F1 on the retained set (matching retrain's 98.27%). On CIFAR-10 (AllCNN), it reaches 90.68% F1 on the retained set and 83.04% on the test set, closely tracking the retrained model (90.10% and 82.39%). On SVHN (ResNet-18), it attains 93.73% F1 on the unlearned set and 92.94% on the test set. In terms of privacy, certified unlearning achieves the lowest membership inference attack accuracy (88.63% on SVHN, 78.00% on CIFAR-10) and AUC (92.18% on SVHN, 87.22% on CIFAR-10) among all approximate baselines, while providing a >10× speedup over retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Golatkar et al. (2020) baselines (Fine Tune, Negative Gradient, Fisher Forgetting), certified unlearning most closely tracks the retrained model on F1-scores: on CIFAR-10, Neg Grad drops retained-set F1 to 85.98% (vs. retrain's 90.10%) while certified stays at 90.68%; on SVHN, Neg Grad degrades test F1 to 92.10% vs. certified's 92.94%. L-CODEC (Mehta et al., 2022) preserves utility well but fails to reduce F1 on the unlearned set (95.00% on SVHN vs. retrain's 93.13%), indicating incomplete forgetting; certified unlearning achieves 93.73% on SVHN D_u, much closer to the retrained target. On membership inference, certified unlearning yields the lowest attack accuracy (88.63% on SVHN, 78.00% on CIFAR-10) and AUC (92.18%, 87.22%) among all approximate methods, outperforming L-CODEC (93.22%, 83.60%) and Fisher (90.47%, 79.70%). Against the newer NeurIPS 2023 baselines (SCRUB, NegGrad+, ℓ-1 Sparse), certified unlearning remains competitive on retained-set utility while providing the only formal (ε,δ) certification guarantee, and achieves a >10× speedup over retraining that none of the approximate baselines match in combination with certification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the certified unlearning framework established for convex models (Guo et al., 2020; Sekhari et al., 2021) to nonconvex deep neural networks by replacing the strong convexity assumption with a local convex approximation (adding λI to the Hessian) and a parameter norm constraint. The core estimation step builds on the single-step Newton update / influence function (Koh & Liang, 2017), and the certification guarantee is inherited from the Gaussian mechanism in differential privacy (Dwork et al., 2014). For computational efficiency, the paper adopts the LiSSA recursive estimator (Agarwal et al., 2017) to approximate the inverse Hessian, reducing complexity from O(np²+p³) to O(sp²+nᵤp). The key departure from prior work is that all theoretical guarantees hold without requiring the objective to be convex, and the framework is further extended to nonconvergence (early stopping) and sequential unlearning settings that prior certified unlearning work did not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first certified unlearning framework with theoretical guarantees for nonconvex DNNs, filling a significant gap between the well-developed convex certified unlearning literature and the practical need to unlearn from deep models under GDPR/CCPA. However, the certification budgets achieved in practice are relatively loose (ε ∈ [1e2, 5e3]), and key hyperparameters (Lipschitz constants L, M; Hessian norm bound H) must be set empirically rather than derived, limiting the precision of the certification. Open questions include tightening the approximation error bound for nonconvex objectives (the bound is orders of magnitude looser than the actual error, as shown in Table 3), scaling to larger architectures and datasets, and whether the sequential unlearning error bound degrades gracefully over many unlearning rounds. The practical impact is most relevant to organizations needing provable (rather than merely empirical) guarantees of data deletion from deployed DNNs.
