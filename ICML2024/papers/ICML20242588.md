## Problem

Diffusion-based generative models for antibody design are limited by the scarcity of labeled antibody-antigen complex data (SAbDab contains only thousands of non-redundant samples), leading to overfitting and restricted expressiveness. Prior methods such as DiffAb rely on discrete-time DDPMs and lack explicit physical or evolutionary priors to constrain the vast CDR sequence-structure space. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a continuous-time score-based diffusion model that integrates evolutionary, physical, and geometric constraints as unified priors.

## Input / Output

The model takes as input the full antigen structure (all chains) and the antibody framework regions (VH/VL constant and non-CDR residues) with their amino acid types, torsion angles, and 3D coordinates, encoded via an MLP conditional encoder into single and pair embeddings. It outputs the CDR amino acid sequences (20-residue discrete space) and backbone 3D structure as SE(3) frames (rotation + translation per residue); the full-atom variant additionally predicts side-chain torsion angles for all possible residues simultaneously. The diffusion process operates over continuous time t ∈ [0,1] with 100 reverse steps, and the final structure is refined with Rosetta FastRelax.

## Method

AbX is the first continuous-time score-based diffusion model that jointly models discrete sequence space (via a Conditional CTMC following τ-LDR) and SE(3) structure space (via SDEs following FrameDiff) for antibody CDR design. The score network uses AlphaFold2-inspired Invariant Point Attention and triangular multiplicative update layers, with ESM-2 (3B) embedded internally to encode the noised sequence as an evolutionary prior. Training combines denoising score matching losses (DSM for structure, ELBO + cross-entropy for sequence) with auxiliary physical constraints (van der Waals loss and bond-length/angle violation loss) and geometric constraints (FAPE, distogram, lDDT-Cα), the latter applied only when t < 1/4 to target fine-grained structure. A recycling mechanism (3 iterations) feeds the predicted sequence back through ESM-2 to refine conditional embeddings. Sampling uses tau-leaping for the discrete sequence reverse process and Euler-Maruyama discretization for the SE(3) structure reverse SDE.

## Results

On the RAbD test dataset (60 antibody-antigen complexes), AbX achieves 18.64% IMP, -1.01 Plausibility, 30.80% Loop AAR, and 3.24 Å Loop RMSD, outperforming both DiffAb and dyMEAN across all four metrics. On the DiffAb test dataset (19 antigens including SARS-CoV-2 and MERS), AbX reaches 42.93% IMP, -0.67 Plausibility, 30.25% Loop AAR, and 3.41 Å Loop RMSD, again leading all baselines. In the antibody optimization task, AbX consistently produces antibodies with lower binding energy than DiffAb across all noise scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AbX is compared against two baselines: DiffAb (a DDPM-based generative model) and dyMEAN (a GNN-based discriminative model). On the RAbD test set, AbX outperforms DiffAb by 6.57 percentage points in IMP (18.64% vs. 12.07%), 9.55 points in Loop AAR (30.80% vs. 21.25%), and 0.21 Å in Loop RMSD (3.24 vs. 3.45), while also achieving the best Plausibility (-1.01 vs. -1.38). Against dyMEAN, the margins are even larger: AbX achieves 18.64% IMP versus 0.00%, and 3.24 Å Loop RMSD versus 5.14 Å. On the DiffAb test set, AbX leads DiffAb by 5.80 points in IMP (42.93% vs. 37.13%) and 8.49 points in Loop AAR (30.25% vs. 21.76%), while beating dyMEAN by 37.67 points in IMP (42.93% vs. 5.26%). In the antibody optimization task, AbX consistently yields lower binding energy than DiffAb at every noise scale, confirming its advantage in enhancing existing antibody affinity.

## Relation to Prior Work

AbX extends the score-based diffusion framework of FrameDiff (Yim et al., 2023) for SE(3) structure generation and the continuous-time Markov chain formulation of τ-LDR (Campbell et al., 2022) for discrete sequence diffusion, unifying both into a single conditional model for antibody CDR design. It departs from DiffAb (Luo et al., 2022), which relied on discrete-time DDPMs, by introducing the first continuous-time score-based diffusion model that jointly models sequence and structure spaces. Compared to AbDiffuser, which estimates position-specific profiles as sequence priors from limited structural data, AbX leverages the large pre-trained protein language model ESM-2 as an evolutionary prior, and unlike DiffProperty, it integrates physical and geometric constraints directly into the training objective rather than using them as post-hoc screening criteria.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core contribution addresses a fundamental bottleneck in computational antibody design: the scarcity of labeled antibody-antigen complex data (SAbDab contains only thousands of non-redundant samples), which makes diffusion models prone to overfitting. By embedding evolutionary (ESM-2), physical (van der Waals, bond violations), and geometric (FAPE, distogram, lDDT) constraints into the generative process, AbX narrows the search space and produces more plausible, higher-affinity antibodies without requiring additional labeled data. The main limitation is the absence of wet-lab validation, and the authors note that incorporating full-atom force fields (e.g., AMBER) could further improve physical realism. For the antibody engineering community, this work provides a principled template for integrating domain-specific priors into generative models under data-scarce regimes.
