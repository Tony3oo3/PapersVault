## Problem

Flow-based generative models offer superior sampling flexibility over diffusion models, but no framework existed to define flows on discrete state spaces, preventing the construction of multimodal flow models for problems requiring both continuous and discrete data. In protein co-design specifically, prior methods either generated one modality (structure or sequence) and relied on a separate predictor for the other, or used diffusion models whose sampling parameters are rigidly fixed at training time.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a noised protein state—backbone Carbon-α translations in R³, local-frame rotations in SO(3), and amino acid assignments from 20 states plus a mask token—with independent noise levels for structure (t) and sequence (t̃) that can be set arbitrarily at inference. It outputs a complete protein of 60–384 residues with jointly generated structure and sequence, or conditionally generates one modality given the other by fixing its noise level to 1 (e.g., t=1 for inverse folding, t̃=1 for forward folding). The same trained model handles all generation tasks (co-design, inverse folding, forward folding) without architectural changes.

## Method

The core contribution is Discrete Flow Models (DFMs), which realize discrete flow matching via Continuous Time Markov Chains: the Kolmogorov equation serves as the discrete analogue of the Fokker-Planck equation, and a rate matrix R_t generates the probability flow p_t that interpolates from noise to data through a masking or uniform conditional interpolant. Training uses a simple cross-entropy denoising objective to predict clean amino acids from masked inputs, while the rate matrix is selected at inference time, decoupling training from sampling dynamics. A key flexibility is CTMC stochasticity (parameter η), which controls the number of state transitions in the trajectory and can be tuned at inference without retraining to adjust sample diversity or structural properties. For multimodal protein generation (Multiflow), a DFM for sequence is combined with FrameFlow for structure via factorized conditional flows over SE(3) and categorical spaces, with independent noise schedules enabling conditional inpainting. The unconditional generative process couples modalities through a shared transformer network that predicts denoised translations, rotations, and amino acid logits from the full noised protein state, trained with a joint loss of MSE on translations, log-map MSE on rotations, and cross-entropy on amino acids.

## Results

On protein co-design (Table 3), Multiflow achieves 0.86 Co-design 1 designability, substantially outperforming Protpardelle (0.63) and ProteinGenerator (0.37). For structure quality measured by PMPNN 8 designability, Multiflow reaches 0.99, surpassing RFdiffusion (0.87), Protpardelle (0.90), and ProteinGenerator (0.89). On the text8 benchmark, DFM with η=15 achieves a better NLL-vs-entropy Pareto frontier than D3PM and SEDD, demonstrating the benefit of inference-time stochasticity control. For inverse folding, Multiflow achieves scRMSD of 2.2±2.6, close to ProteinMPNN's 1.9±2.7, but forward folding performance (15.3±4.5 RMSD) lags far behind ESMFold (2.7±3.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Multiflow decisively outperforms both prior co-design methods: Protpardelle (0.63) and ProteinGenerator (0.37) on Co-design 1 designability, with Multiflow reaching 0.86. On structure quality (PMPNN 8 designability), Multiflow (0.99) surpasses RFdiffusion (0.87), Protpardelle (0.90), and ProteinGenerator (0.89), while maintaining comparable diversity (156 vs. 158 for RFdiffusion) and novelty (0.61 vs. 0.63). The gap between PMPNN 1 (0.88) and Co-design 1 (0.86) designability shows Multiflow's co-designed sequences are nearly as good as ProteinMPNN's dedicated inverse folding. On text8, DFM with η=15 dominates the NLL-vs-entropy Pareto frontier over D3PM and SEDD, which lack inference-time stochasticity control. For inverse folding, Multiflow (2.2±2.6 scRMSD) is close to ProteinMPNN (1.9±2.7), but forward folding is a clear weakness: Multiflow (15.3±4.5 RMSD) is far worse than ESMFold (2.7±3.9), indicating the joint model has not yet matched specialized folding predictors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper constructs the discrete analogue of continuous flow matching by replacing the ODE-driven probability path with a CTMC-driven probability flow, building directly on the flow matching framework of Lipman et al. (2023) and the continuous-time discrete diffusion formalism of Campbell et al. (2022). The key departure from prior discrete diffusion models (D3PM, SEDD) is that the rate matrix's stochasticity parameter η is decoupled from training and adjustable at inference time, whereas prior methods fix the noising process at training time. For protein co-design, the paper departs from Protpardelle and ProteinGenerator, which generate one modality and predict the other with a separate model, by learning a single joint generative process over both structure (via FrameFlow) and sequence (via DFM).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DFM framework provides the missing piece for flow-based multimodal generative modeling, enabling a single model to handle discrete and continuous data with the sampling flexibility that flows offer over diffusion. The CTMC stochasticity as an inference-time knob for controlling sample properties (e.g., secondary structure composition) is a practical capability absent from prior discrete generative models. However, the model's poor forward folding performance (15.3 RMSD vs. 2.7 for ESMFold) reveals that joint training does not automatically yield competitive performance on all sub-tasks, and side-chain modeling remains unaddressed. Open questions include whether the framework scales to more complex discrete-continuous combinations and whether the inference-time stochasticity control generalizes beyond protein design.
