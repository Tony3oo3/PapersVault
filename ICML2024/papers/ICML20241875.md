## Problem

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods like LoRA and Adapter have proven effective but their underlying principles remain unclear, and all require inserting additional plug-in modules into the model architecture. This paper asks why pre-trained models exhibit low intrinsic dimension—i.e., why a small subset of parameters suffices for adaptation—and provides both a theoretical explanation via PAC-Bayesian generalization bounds and a practical component-sparse fine-tuning method that requires no architectural modifications.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained language model (RoBERTa-base/large for GLUE, Llama-7B/13B/33B for instruction-tuning) and standard downstream task data, producing a fine-tuned model where only a sparse subset of parameter components (e.g., 0.8% for GLUE, 3–5% for Llama) are updated in-place. The sparsity rate and target modules (W_q, W_k, W_v, W_o in self-attention) are the primary hyperparameters, and the method operates entirely within the existing model structure without adding any new parameters or modules.

## Method

SIFT (Sparse Increment Fine-Tuning) exploits the quasi-sparse gradient property of pre-trained models—where ~1% of parameter components account for ~99% of the gradient norm—by updating only the top-x% components (by absolute gradient value) selected from the first training batch. The theoretical foundation shows that pre-training acts as a prior distribution shift in PAC-Bayesian terms, moving the model away from non-expressive parameter sub-manifolds and tightening the generalization error bound, which justifies why searching in a compressed subspace suffices. For memory efficiency, SIFT registers Sparse Parameters with Sparse Gradients and injects hook functions into backpropagation to capture and index partial gradients on-the-fly, reducing both gradient and optimizer-state memory to x% of full fine-tuning. The update direction is guaranteed to be sufficiently descending within the τ-steepest coordinates subspace when the gradient concentration condition holds, which the quasi-sparsity of pre-trained gradients ensures.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark with RoBERTa-Large, SIFT (0.8M trainable parameters) achieves an average score of 88.6, matching LoRA (0.8M) and within 0.3 of full fine-tuning (355M, avg 88.9). On instruction-tuning with Llama, SIFT matches LoRA on MMLU accuracy (e.g., 46.7 for Llama-13B) while achieving higher HumanEval pass@10 (26.2 vs 25.6 for Llama-13B; 37.2 vs 33.5 for Llama-33B). SIFT uses only 0.8% sparsity on attention projections, requiring no additional plug-in modules.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIFT matches LoRA exactly on the GLUE average (88.6) with the same 0.8M parameter budget, while slightly outperforming LoRA on MRPC (90.4 vs 90.2), CoLA (68.5 vs 68.2), and RTE (85.9 vs 85.2), but trailing on QNLI (94.1 vs 94.8) and QQP (91.0 vs 91.6). Against Adapter variants, SIFT clearly wins: it outperforms Adapter^P (0.8M) by 0.7 average points and Adapter^H (0.8M) by 2.2 points on GLUE. On instruction-tuning with Llama, SIFT ties LoRA on MMLU across all model sizes (7B: 40.7, 13B: 46.7, 33B: 55.7 vs 55.9) but achieves higher HumanEval pass@10 at 13B (26.2 vs 25.6) and 33B (37.2 vs 33.5). Compared to full fine-tuning, SIFT achieves 88.6 vs 88.9 on GLUE and 46.7 vs 48.8 MMLU on Llama-13B, using only 0.8M vs 355M (GLUE) or 0.5B vs 13B (instruction-tuning) trainable parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) lineage initiated by Adapter (Houlsby et al., 2019), Prefix-Tuning (Li & Liang, 2021), and LoRA (Hu et al., 2021), but departs fundamentally from all of them by introducing no additional plug-in modules. Instead, SIFT updates a sparse subset of components within the existing pre-trained parameters directly, drawing its mathematical framework from subspace methods in nonlinear optimization (Liu et al., 2021). The theoretical contribution is a novel application of PAC-Bayesian generalization bounds (McAllester, 2003) to explain pre-training as a prior distribution shift, connecting the observed quasi-sparse gradient structure and loss landscape changes to the compressed search space that underlies low intrinsic dimension (Li et al., 2018a).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quasi-sparse gradient property (1% of components dominating 99% of gradient norm) provides a concrete, empirically verifiable explanation for why PEFT works, and the PAC-Bayesian framing offers a principled lens that could guide future sparse training designs. However, the practical gains over LoRA are marginal (comparable scores on GLUE and MMLU), the module selection (q,k,v,o) remains heuristic, and the 'first batch' component selection strategy is somewhat ad hoc. Open questions include whether periodic re-selection of sparse components improves performance, whether the approach generalizes beyond attention projections to feed-forward layers, and whether the PAC-Bayesian theoretical guarantees translate more tightly into practical convergence bounds for sparse updates.
