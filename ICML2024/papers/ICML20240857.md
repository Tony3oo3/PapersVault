## Problem

Despite LoRA's widespread adoption as the standard parameter-efficient fine-tuning method, its theoretical understanding has been limited to expressive power (existence of good weight configurations) or kernel-regression approximations that ignore LoRA's quadratic parameterization. No prior work established whether gradient-based optimization can actually find good LoRA solutions or whether those solutions generalize, leaving the fundamental trainability and generalizability of LoRA unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained model (RoBERTa-base, ViT, or wav2vec2) with frozen weights and a small fine-tuning dataset of N examples with K-dimensional outputs (e.g., N=32, K=2 for NLP tasks; N=48, K=3 for image classification). It produces a low-rank weight update δ = BA with rank r satisfying r(r+1)/2 > KN, which is added to the pre-trained weights. The analysis operates under the NTK (linearized) regime, meaning the fine-tuned model is approximated by a first-order Taylor expansion around the pre-trained weights, and the output generalization bound scales as O(‖δ*‖_*/√N).

## Method

The paper provides a three-part theoretical analysis of LoRA fine-tuning in the NTK regime. First, it proves that full fine-tuning admits a low-rank solution of rank r with r(r+1)/2 ≤ KN by reducing the nuclear-norm-regularized problem to a semidefinite program and applying Pataki-style dimension-counting arguments. Second, it shows that LoRA with rank r(r+1)/2 > KN has no spurious local minima: by connecting LoRA's quadratic parameterization to the Burer-Monteiro factorization and leveraging Haeffele et al.'s characterization of second-order stationary points in matrix factorization, it proves via Sard's theorem that for almost all small perturbations P, every SOSP must be rank-deficient and hence a global minimizer. Third, it establishes a generalization guarantee using Rademacher complexity, showing the population risk of the LoRA solution exceeds the optimum by at most O(‖δ*_true‖_* · √K · R / √N). The key insight is that the rank threshold r ≳ √N emerges naturally from the dimension-counting condition that the manifold of full-rank factorizations has measure zero in the space of perturbations.

## Results

The paper's primary contributions are theoretical: (i) full fine-tuning in the NTK regime admits a low-rank solution of rank r satisfying r(r+1)/2 ≤ KN; (ii) LoRA with rank r satisfying r(r+1)/2 > KN eliminates all spurious local minima, so (stochastic) gradient descent converges to a global minimizer; (iii) the resulting low-rank solution generalizes with a bound scaling as O(‖δ*‖_F · √K / √N). Experimentally, with N=32 and K=2 (requiring r≥11), training curves for LoRA and full fine-tuning on a linearized RoBERTa-base converge to the same globally optimal loss across six NLP tasks (SST-2, QNLI, MR, CR, QQP, Subj), an image classification task (Bean Disease, N=48, 3 labels), and a speech classification task (SUPERB, N=64, 4 labels). The key empirical observation is that convergence rates differ: higher LoRA rank or full fine-tuning converges faster, suggesting lower-rank LoRA introduces unfavorable flat regions (plateaus/saddles) that slow gradient descent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without formal baseline comparisons; the 'compares_to' set is empty. The only informal comparison is between LoRA fine-tuning and full fine-tuning (without LoRA) on the same linearized models. Both methods converge to the same globally optimal training loss, validating the theory that LoRA with sufficient rank (r≥11 for N=32, K=2) does not introduce spurious local minima. However, full fine-tuning and higher-rank LoRA converge noticeably faster than lower-rank LoRA, indicating that while the landscape is free of spurious minima, it may still contain flat regions or saddle points that slow optimization. The paper explicitly notes that its theory guarantees the absence of spurious local minima but says nothing about the convexity or favorability of the landscape, leaving the rate-vs-rank tradeoff as an open question for future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first theoretical analysis of LoRA's optimization landscape, building on three distinct threads: (1) the Burer-Monteiro low-rank factorization framework and SOSP characterizations from Haeffele et al. (2014), (2) Pataki's (1998) dimension-counting technique for low-rank solutions in semidefinite programs, and (3) the NTK regime framework from Jacot et al. (2018) as adapted to fine-tuning by Malladi et al. (2023). It departs from Malladi et al.'s kernel-regression view (where the A matrix is effectively frozen and rank scales as Θ(1/ε²)) by treating LoRA's full quadratic parameterization, and from Zeng & Lee (2024) who established only expressive power (existence) without addressing trainability. The generalization analysis follows standard Rademacher complexity arguments applied to the nuclear-norm-regularized setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a significant gap: LoRA is the de facto standard for PEFT, yet its optimization behavior was previously unanalyzed. The rank requirement r ≳ √N provides a concrete theoretical guideline, and the no-spurious-local-minima result (under NTK + perturbation) explains why gradient-based methods succeed. Key limitations include the NTK regime assumption (restricting to small updates), the need for a nonzero perturbation P for the main theorem to apply directly, and the absence of convergence rate guarantees. Open questions include relaxing the NTK assumption via local analysis, establishing lower bounds on the minimum rank, and understanding the empirically observed tradeoff where lower LoRA rank creates flat landscapes that slow convergence without preventing it.
