## Problem

Single-pass gradient descent and gradient flow on population loss are fundamentally limited by the information exponent and leap exponent of the target function, requiring a diverging number of iterations for non-staircase multi-index targets. It was unclear whether the common practice of reusing training batches could overcome these barriers. This paper shows that multi-pass GD with finite stepsize dramatically expands the class of efficiently learnable functions, breaking both the information and leap exponent curses in just two gradient steps.

## Input / Output

The analysis operates in the high-dimensional limit (d→∞, n/d=α=Θ(1)) with i.i.d. Gaussian inputs z_ν∈R^d and multi-index targets f*(z)=g*(W* z/√d) where W*∈R^{k×d} has k orthogonal unit-norm rows. The two-layer student uses symmetric initialization (p even, paired neurons with opposite second-layer weights) and fixed second-layer weights, learning only first-layer weights via gradient descent with explicit regularization λ. The output is a characterization of which directions in the teacher subspace U* are learned in finite time T=2, expressed through the subspace P* of 'hard' directions where no polynomial transformation of the target output yields linear correlation, plus closed-form DMFT equations for the weight-projection dynamics.

## Method

The core mechanism is 'hidden progress': when reusing the same batch, the pre-activation h_ν^(1) after the first gradient step acquires a non-Gaussian component dependent on f*(z_ν) through the self-term ⟨z_ν,z_ν⟩/d≈1 in the gradient update, even when the weights remain orthogonal to the teacher subspace. This dependence allows the second gradient step to develop correlations with new directions in W* that were inaccessible in the first step, since σ'(h_ν^(1)) now carries information about h_ν^*. The rigorous analysis uses Dynamical Mean Field Theory (DMFT) with iterative conditioning to derive coupled stochastic processes θ^(t) and h^(t) characterizing the joint distributions of student/teacher weights and pre-activations, circumventing the need for pre-activations to remain Gaussian. The learnable directions are characterized by the subspace P* (Def. 3.1): directions v* where E_z[F(f*(z))⟨v*,z⟩]=0 for all polynomials F, with the complement P*⊥ learned in exactly T=2 steps under mild conditions on σ and g*. The DMFT framework yields closed-form integro-differential equations for the low-dimensional weight projections, providing rigorous guarantees in the correlated-samples regime without fresh-batch resampling at each iteration.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.2) shows that multi-pass gradient descent with batch size n = αd achieves weak recovery (non-vanishing overlap with the target subspace) in exactly T = 2 gradient steps for all directions in the orthogonal complement P⊥* of the hard subspace, with sample complexity O(d) independent of the information exponent (IE) or leap complexity (LC). This contrasts sharply with one-pass GD, which requires T = O(d^(IE−1)) steps for single-index targets with IE > 1, or T = O(d^(LC−1)) for multi-index targets. The paper further proves that directions in the hard subspace A* (e.g., even-symmetric directions) remain unlearnable in any finite number of steps even with batch reuse. Numerical simulations (d = 5000, n = 3d, σ = ReLU, η = 0.1) confirm the theory: for g* = He₃ (IE = 3), multi-pass GD achieves non-zero overlap at T = 2 while one-pass GD shows zero correlation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the information exponent framework of Ben Arous et al. (2021), multi-pass GD completely bypasses the IE barrier: targets with IE = 3 (e.g., He₃) that require O(d) iterations under one-pass SGD are learned in just 2 steps with batch reuse, reducing sample complexity from O(d^(IE−1)) to O(d). Against the leap complexity and staircase property of Abbe et al. (2023), the paper explicitly disproves their conjecture that batch reusing can reduce sample complexity only to O(d^(max(LC/2,1))): multi-pass GD achieves O(d) sample complexity for a broad class of multi-index targets with LC = 3, learning the full teacher subspace in T = 2 steps where one-pass GD never recovers the orthogonal direction in finite time. Compared to the large-batch one-pass SGD analysis of Dandi et al. (2023), multi-pass GD learns a strictly larger class of directions in finite time, as the 'hidden progress' mechanism (pre-activations developing non-Gaussian correlations with the target) is unavailable in the one-pass setting where pre-activations remain Gaussian. Against gradient flow on population loss (Bietti et al., 2023), multi-pass GD with finite stepsize overcomes the same information/leap exponent limitations, since batch reuse induces non-Gaussian pre-activation components that gradient flow on the population loss cannot develop.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the rigorous Dynamical Mean Field Theory (DMFT) framework established by Celentano et al. (2021) and Gerbelot et al. (2022) to analyze gradient descent in high dimensions, extending it to the correlated-sample regime where batches are reused. It departs sharply from the single-pass GD literature (Ben Arous et al., 2021; Abbe et al., 2021–2023; Dandi et al., 2023; Bietti et al., 2023), which characterized learnable functions via the information exponent and leap exponent under the assumption that pre-activations remain Gaussian. The key conceptual departure is that batch reuse introduces non-Gaussian correlations in pre-activations (the 'hidden progress' mechanism), fundamentally altering which directions become learnable and explicitly disproving a conjecture by Abbe et al. (2023) that batch reusing could only reduce sample complexity to the CSQ lower bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result reframes a central narrative in learning theory: the information and leap exponents, long treated as fundamental barriers to feature learning, are shown to be artifacts of the single-pass assumption rather than inherent properties of the target function. This has practical resonance since real training loops reuse batches, and the paper argues that correlated samples in high-dimensional datasets make the multi-pass regime more realistic than the idealized i.i.d. fresh-batch setting. Open questions include whether the results extend to non-Gaussian data, deeper architectures, and the limit of minibatch size 1 (observed empirically but not proven), as well as the persistent hardness of symmetric targets (phase retrieval, committee machines) where symmetry-breaking times diverge.
