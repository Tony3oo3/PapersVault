## Problem

State-of-the-art Safe Policy Improvement (SPI) methods such as SPIBB and MCTS-SPIBB cannot scale to multi-agent domains because the joint action space grows exponentially with the number of agents, making policy optimization intractable. No existing SPI algorithm can compute improved policies with safety guarantees for more than 3 agents in the tested benchmarks, leaving a critical gap for deploying offline RL in realistic multi-agent settings with state spaces up to 10^41 and action spaces up to 10^16.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240159/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a centralized behavior policy π₀, a dataset D of trajectories (quadruples of joint state, joint action, next joint state, reward) collected under π₀, the current joint state s̄, a coordination graph G encoding pairwise agent interactions, and the MLE factored transition model T^D. It outputs a single improved joint action ā* per decision step and, over the full horizon, a safe improved policy π_I that is guaranteed to be at least ζ-approximately as good as π₀ with probability 1−δ. Unusually, the dataset is not used to build a flat joint state-action count table; instead it is decomposed into per-factor local counts via dependency functions D_k, which dramatically increases effective sample sizes.

## Method

FV-MCTS-SPIBB integrates two novel components within an MCTS loop: (1) efficient local state-action counting that exploits the factored transition model to count observations at the agent level rather than the joint-action level, yielding larger counts and a smaller bootstrapped set; and (2) Constrained Max-Plus (or Constrained Var-El) action selection, where each agent's message-passing or variable-elimination step is restricted to its set of non-bootstrapped local actions B̄_m^{A_i}(s̄), ensuring the resulting joint action is non-bootstrapped without ever enumerating the full joint action space. During MCTS simulations, the algorithm first samples a joint action from π₀; if it is bootstrapped, it is returned as-is, otherwise a round of constrained message passing computes the optimal non-bootstrapped joint action using UCB-inspired exploration. The theoretical analysis shows that under component-wise UCB and convergence of Max-Plus/Var-El, the returned policy is Π₀-optimal in the MLE MDP and inherits SPIBB's safety guarantees asymptotically.

## Results

On Multi-agent SysAdmin (ring topology, 8 agents), FV-MCTS-SPIBB-Max-Plus achieves an average return of ~22.0, substantially outperforming MCTS-SPIBB (~13.0) and the behavior policy (~12.0). At 32 agents, FV-MCTS-SPIBB-Max-Plus is the only method that still produces a safe improvement, as MCTS-SPIBB breaks down due to the intractable joint action space. On Multi-UAV Delivery, FV-MCTS-SPIBB-Max-Plus improves the behavior policy from 457.7 to 655.6 (8 agents) and from 454.2 to 748.1 (16 agents). In safety tests with only 5 trajectories, FV-MCTS-SPIBB-Max-Plus maintains a return of 13.1 (above the behavior policy), while the unsafe FV-MCTS-Max-Plus degrades to 10.9 (~16% below baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MCTS-SPIBB (Castellini et al., 2023), FV-MCTS-SPIBB-Max-Plus achieves a large performance gap at 8 agents (~22.0 vs. ~13.0) and is the only method that scales to 24–32 agents where MCTS-SPIBB fails entirely due to the exponential joint action space. Against the unsafe baselines FV-MCTS-Max-Plus (Choudhury et al., 2021) and FV-MCTS-Var-El (Amato & Oliehoek, 2015), the SPI constraint is critical: with only 5 trajectories on SysAdmin, the unsafe variants drop below the behavior policy (10.9 vs. 13.1 for Max-Plus; similar for Var-El), while FV-MCTS-SPIBB consistently maintains or exceeds baseline performance across all dataset sizes. Against SPIBB (Laroche et al., 2019), FV-MCTS-SPIBB achieves comparable performance at 3 agents (the maximum SPIBB can handle), but SPIBB and all other policy-iteration-based SPI methods (DUIPI, Adv-Approx-Soft-SPIBB, etc.) cannot scale beyond 3–4 agents in these domains. The sample-efficiency advantage of FV-MCTS-SPIBB over MCTS-SPIBB is further confirmed in a dedicated experiment (Figure 7) where, at 8 agents with the same dataset, FV-MCTS-SPIBB-Max-Plus (mean 22.3, std 2.8) outperforms MCTS-SPIBB (mean 13.4, std 2.7) despite using 10× fewer simulations (100 vs. 1000).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads: (1) SPIBB's safety framework for offline policy improvement (Laroche et al., 2019), (2) MCTS-based scalability for SPI in single-agent settings (Castellini et al., 2023), and (3) coordination-graph-based multi-agent MCTS using Max-Plus or Variable Elimination (Choudhury et al., 2021; Amato & Oliehoek, 2015). The key methodological departure is the introduction of Constrained Max-Plus and Constrained Var-El, which restrict message-passing and maximization to non-bootstrapped local actions, thereby embedding SPIBB's safety constraints directly into the multi-agent action-selection mechanism. The efficient local state-action counting extends Simão & Spaan's (2019) factored SPIBB counting from single-agent to multi-agent settings by exploiting transition-model factorization at the agent level rather than the joint-action level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first multi-agent SPI algorithm with formal safety guarantees, and it scales to domains (10^41 states, 10^16 actions) where all prior SPI methods are intractable. The component-wise UCB assumption required for convergence remains an open theoretical problem, acknowledged by the authors and by prior work (Amato & Oliehoek, 2015; Choudhury et al., 2021). The method is limited to cooperative settings and two benchmark domains; extension to competitive or mixed-motive scenarios, and to settings with sequentially collected data, are identified as future work. The practical impact is significant for real-world multi-agent deployments (robotic fleets, drone swarms) where safety guarantees are essential but the combinatorial action space makes standard SPI infeasible.
