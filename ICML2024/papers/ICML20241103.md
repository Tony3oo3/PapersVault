## Problem

LLM training checkpoints consume enormous storage (e.g., Pythia-12B requires ~11 TB for 154 checkpoints), yet prior checkpoint compression work focuses almost exclusively on model weights while ignoring optimizer momentum states, which for Adam require double the storage of weights. Additionally, the temporal similarity between adjacent checkpoints and the correlation between weights and momentum remain underexploited, limiting achievable compression ratios.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three inputs at each save node: the current weight checkpoint W_t, the previously reconstructed checkpoint Ŵ_{t-1} (kept in memory for fast residual computation), and the full Adam optimizer state (first- and second-order moments m_t, v_t). It outputs a single 7zip-compressed file containing K-means cluster centers and indices for both the pruned residual weights and pruned momentum states, using 4-bit non-uniform quantization (two int4 values packed into one int8). Reconstruction requires only the random seed for initialization plus the sequence of compressed files, making the total storage footprint orders of magnitude smaller than saving full checkpoints.

## Method

ExCP applies a three-stage pipeline: (1) residual encoding computes ΔW_t = W_t − Ŵ_{t-1}, exploiting the sparsity of inter-checkpoint weight changes while leaving momentum unmodified since it is a moving average weakly correlated with prior steps; (2) joint weight-momentum pruning sets a per-layer weight threshold r_w = α/√m_t × median(W) using the second-order moment as a statistical indicator of weight change, and a momentum threshold r_o = β × mean(v_t) using the first-order moment, with the critical constraint that momentum at position i is retained only if the corresponding weight is also retained (W2M pruning); (3) non-uniform K-means quantization maps surviving values to 2^n − 1 cluster centers. The authors prove that Adam's regret bound under momentum pruning acquires only an additional term proportional to √(T·v̂_τ·(1−M_o)), which vanishes in the average-regret limit, preserving O(1/√T) convergence. This joint design achieves ~70× compression on Pythia-410M with nearly lossless downstream performance.

## Results

On ViT-L32 (ImageNet-1K), ExCP with full joint pruning achieves 35.21× compression on weights+momentum with 71.69% top-1 accuracy, compared to the 71.36% baseline. On Pythia-410M, ExCP achieves ~70× compression (4.53 GB → 0.06 GB) with a near-lossless average accuracy of 42.93 vs. 43.11 for the original model across six downstream tasks. On PanGu-π-1B and PanGu-π-7B, ExCP achieves ~25× storage reduction (14.64 GB → 0.59 GB and 98.59 GB → 4.10 GB) with average scores of 40.83 vs. 40.74 and 57.09 vs. 56.71, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExCP substantially outperforms all three checkpoint-compression baselines on effective (weights + momentum) compression ratio: 35.21× vs. 1.41× for CNR+ (Check-N-Run), 1.45× for QD+ (QD-Compressor), and 1.47× for DynaQuant. The large gap arises because prior methods compress only model weights while leaving optimizer momentum uncompressed; when momentum storage (2× weight size for Adam) is accounted for, their effective ratios drop to ~1.4×. In terms of accuracy, ExCP (71.69%) is competitive with DynaQuant (71.82%) and CNR+ (71.57%), and slightly above the uncompressed baseline (71.36%), while QD+ (71.24%) falls below baseline. On the LLM side, ExCP's ~70× compression on Pythia-410M and ~25× on PanGu-π models have no direct baseline comparison in the paper, but the near-lossless downstream scores (within 0.2–0.4 points) demonstrate that the aggressive compression does not degrade model quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the checkpoint compression line of work (LC-Checkpoint, Check-N-Run, Delta-DNN, QD-Compressor, DynaQuant) by addressing a gap those methods leave open: the optimizer momentum states, which for Adam require twice the storage of weights. It builds directly on the Adam convergence analysis of Kingma & Ba (2014), extending the regret bound to account for a pruning mask applied to the first- and second-order moments at a specific iteration. The compression pipeline itself combines three well-known techniques—differential (residual) encoding, magnitude-based pruning, and K-means non-uniform quantization—but the key conceptual departure is the joint weight-momentum pruning criterion, which couples the two masks so that momentum is only retained where the corresponding weight residual is also retained.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical stakes are high: the paper estimates that frontier LLM training checkpoints could cost tens of millions of dollars in cloud storage, and a 70× near-lossless compression ratio directly attacks that cost without modifying training code. The convergence proof for pruned momentum provides theoretical grounding that was absent from prior heuristic checkpoint-compression work. However, experiments are limited to models up to 7B parameters and a single optimizer (Adam); whether the compression ratio and near-lossless guarantee hold at the hundreds-of-billions scale, or for other optimizers (LAMB, Sophia, etc.), remains an open question. The authors also note future directions toward multi-modal and non-transformer architectures.
