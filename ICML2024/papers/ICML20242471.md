## Problem

Existing multicalibration-based fairness methods are restricted to one-dimensional predictors and require separate, task-specific algorithms for each risk measure (false positive rate, calibration error, prediction set coverage, etc.). This paper addresses the lack of a unified framework that can calibrate multi-dimensional output functions (e.g., token distributions, per-pixel scores) against multi-group fairness constraints in a single, general-purpose procedure.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-dimensional scoring function h(x) produced by a pretrained model (BERT token probabilities, U-Net pixel scores, or HiAGM document scores) along with demographic group indicators, and produces a post-processed function f(x) — an adjusted probability distribution over tokens, a threshold function selecting hierarchy nodes, or a per-pixel threshold for segmentation. The output is constrained to lie in a convex set F (e.g., the probability simplex for text generation, a bounded interval for thresholds), and the calibration is enforced via a threshold α on the expected inner product between a mapping functional s and subgroup indicator vectors g.

## Method

The paper introduces (s,G,α)-GMC, a generalized multicalibration definition where the mapping functional s can be multi-dimensional and can depend on the entire function f (not just f(x)), and the subgroup class G can encode multi-dimensional checking rules. The algorithm is a projected gradient descent: at each iteration, a violating subgroup g is identified, and f is updated by projecting f − η·g onto the feasible set F. Convergence is guaranteed under four assumptions — existence of a K_L-smooth potential functional L whose gradient equals s, monotonicity of L under projection, boundedness of g, and boundedness of L — yielding T = O(K_L(C_u − C_l)B / α²) iterations. The finite-sample variant uses alternating calibration/validation splits with a uniform convergence argument over G, requiring m = O(T·(d(G) + log(T/δ))/α²) samples. The framework instantiates three concrete applications: de-biased text generation (s = p(x) − E[p(x)]), prediction-set conditional coverage in hierarchical classification (s = σ − 1{covers}), and group-wise FNR control in image segmentation (s = 1 − |O∩O'|/|O| − σ).

## Results

In de-biased text generation, the GMC post-processing reduces gender disparity gaps to below α=0.002 across all sensitive attribute categories while preserving cross-entropy (9.9291 → 9.9285). In hierarchical classification (Web of Science, 46,985 documents), GMC achieves prediction-set conditional coverage within 95±2.5% for all output categories, whereas both the unprocessed leaf-node baseline and split conformal fail to meet the coverage guarantee for 'CS' and 'Medical'. In image segmentation (FASSEG, 118 facial images), GMC controls FNR deviations below the 0.005 tolerance across all gender and racial subgroups at 86% pixel accuracy, compared to 84% (U-Net) and 92% (split conformal) for the baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two main baselines: the unprocessed model output (raw BERT for text generation, raw U-Net for segmentation, raw HiAGM for classification) and the split conformal / conformal risk control method of Angelopoulos et al. (2023). In image segmentation, GMC achieves 86% pixel accuracy while controlling FNR disparities below the 0.005 tolerance for all subgroups; the U-Net baseline (84% accuracy) and split conformal baseline (92% accuracy) both fail to meet the FNR target on male and white subgroups. In hierarchical classification, GMC is the only method that satisfies the prediction-set conditional coverage guarantee (95±2.5%) across all output categories, while both the unprocessed leaf-node prediction and split conformal violate the guarantee for 'CS' and 'Medical'. In text generation, GMC reduces the disparity gap for male-stereotyped professions from 0.0031 to 0.0025 and for pleasant words from 0.0034 to 0.0027 (mean over 10 runs), with negligible cross-entropy degradation (9.9291 → 9.9285). Overall, GMC consistently meets its fairness guarantees where baselines fall short, at a modest accuracy cost in segmentation (86% vs. 92% for split conformal) but no measurable cost in text generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 1-dimensional multicalibration framework of Deng et al. (2023, Happymap) and the property-elicitation characterization of Noarov & Roth (2023) to multi-dimensional output functions, introducing (s,G,α)-GMC as a natural generalization. The algorithmic approach is a direct generalization of the projection-based update rules used in multicalibration (Ursula Hébert-Johnson et al., 2018) and outcome indistinguishability (Dwork et al., 2021), but the key departure is allowing both the mapping functional s and the checking functions g to be multi-dimensional, and permitting s to be a functional of f rather than merely a function of f(x). This multi-dimensional flexibility is what enables applications to language model calibration (where outputs are distributions over vocabulary) and pixel-level image segmentation, settings that prior 1-dimensional frameworks cannot address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a single theoretical lens under which diverse fairness guarantees—FNR equalization, prediction-set conditional coverage, and debiased text generation—can be derived with matching convergence and sample-complexity bounds, reducing the need for ad-hoc algorithm design per setting. A key limitation is that the approach is purely post-processing: it cannot correct biases baked into the base model's scoring function, and the Impact Statement explicitly acknowledges this. Open questions include whether the framework extends to online or sequential settings, whether the smoothness assumptions (bounded density of scores) hold in practice for deep network outputs, and how the method scales when the function class G grows combinatorially with the number of sensitive attributes.
