## Problem

When data points are interdependent (e.g., nodes in a graph or instances connected by implicit structures), the i.i.d. assumption breaks down and distribution shifts in the underlying geometry (the manifold defining proximity among samples) cause standard models to fail at out-of-distribution generalization. Existing approaches for graph generalization lack a principled mechanism to separate stable causal relations from geometry-specific confounding patterns that differ across domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242204/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes node feature vectors (D-dimensional raw features) and a graph adjacency matrix (N×N binary or weighted), where the graph may be fully observed, partially observed, or entirely unobserved (in which case kNN with varying k or angle-biased cosine similarity constructs the inter-instance structure). Output is a per-node prediction: class labels for classification tasks (Twitch, Arxiv, STL-10, CIFAR-10) or continuous gene expression values for regression (DPPIN). A small pseudo-dataset of size T ≈ 0.01N is additionally generated at training time to estimate the model-free prior over diffusivity hypotheses.

## Method

GLIND formulates message passing as a geometric diffusion process where the diffusivity at each step is not deterministic but sampled from a K-branch multinomial variational distribution q_φ(d|z), with branch selection approximated via Gumbel-Softmax for end-to-end differentiability. The key theoretical contribution is a causal deconfounding objective derived via do-calculus: by replacing the model-based prior p(d|z) with a model-free prior p₀(d) (estimated as a mixture of pseudo variational posteriors from a randomly generated sub-dataset), the KL regularization term becomes a re-weighting that down-weights frequent diffusivity components and up-weights rare ones, cutting off the confounding path from diffusivity to input. This yields three practical instantiations—GLIND-GCN (aggregated neighbor updates), GLIND-GAT (attention-weighted pairwise influence per branch), and GLIND-TRANS (linear-complexity all-pair attention via DIFFormer-style kernel)—each with K independent parameterized branches per layer that dynamically select the evolutionary direction of the diffusion trajectory.

## Results

On Arxiv (the most challenging OOD setting with 2018–2020 test data), GLIND-GCN achieves 57.06% accuracy and GLIND-GAT achieves 59.71%, representing 23.8% and 26.6% relative improvements over the best baselines (Mixup-GCN at 46.09% and Mixup-GAT at 47.17%). On DPPIN, GLIND-TRANS achieves the lowest RMSE across all 7 test domains with an average 21.6% reduction over the runner-up. On CIFAR-10 and STL-10 (unobserved geometries), GLIND-TRANS reaches 80.72/81.06/80.24% accuracy on the three test domains, outperforming the best baseline (EERM-Trans at 79.68/79.89/78.82%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLIND consistently outperforms all six baseline families (ERM, IRM, GroupDRO, DANN, Mixup, EERM) across all five datasets and all three model instantiations. The most dramatic gains appear on Arxiv 2018–2020, where GLIND-GCN (57.06%) and GLIND-GAT (59.71%) surpass the best baselines (Mixup-GCN 46.09%, Mixup-GAT 47.17%) by over 10 absolute points, indicating that standard OOD-robustness techniques fail to capture the geometry-induced distribution shift. On DPPIN, GLIND-TRANS achieves the lowest RMSE in all 7 test domains (e.g., 1.02 vs. 1.46 for Mixup-Trans on Hazbun), a 21.6% average reduction. On CIFAR-10, GLIND-TRANS (80.72/81.06/80.24%) edges out EERM-Trans (79.68/79.89/78.82%), the strongest Transformer-based baseline, while GLIND-GCN (79.24/80.26/79.39%) substantially outperforms all GNN-based baselines. On Twitch, GLIND-GAT achieves the best ROC-AUC on all three test subgraphs (67.82/54.50/64.32), with the largest margin on FR (+1.51 over GroupDRO-GAT). EERM, the strongest graph-specific OOD baseline, is notably absent on Arxiv due to out-of-memory issues, but where it is available it is consistently outperformed by GLIND.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads: geometric diffusion on manifolds (Weickert et al., 1998; Romeny, 2013), variational inference with stochastic latent variables, and causal deconfounded learning (Pearl et al., 2016). Architecturally, it generalizes GCN, GAT, and DIFFormer by replacing deterministic message-passing weights with a branching stochastic diffusivity sampled via Gumbel-Softmax, while the learning objective replaces the standard ELBO's model-based prior with a data-driven mixture-of-posteriors prior derived from a causal re-weighting argument (Theorem 3.1). The paper explicitly positions itself as the first to harness diffusion models for OOD generalization with interdependent data, departing from prior OOD methods (IRM, GroupDRO, DANN, EERM) that treat the encoder and the generalization objective as independent design choices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The causal interpretation of the latent diffusivity as a confounder between input and output provides a principled, theoretically grounded mechanism for learning shift-invariant representations on graphs, going beyond ad-hoc regularization. The consistent large improvements across five heterogeneous datasets (social networks, citation graphs, protein interactions, and image sets with synthetic kNN structures) and three architectural families suggest the framework is broadly applicable rather than dataset-specific. Open questions include whether the pseudo-dataset prior estimation (Eqn. 10) scales to very large graphs, how the method behaves under covariate shift versus concept shift, and whether the diffusion framework can be extended to OOD detection as the authors note. The requirement to tune K (number of diffusivity hypotheses) and the Gumbel temperature adds practical complexity that may limit adoption.
