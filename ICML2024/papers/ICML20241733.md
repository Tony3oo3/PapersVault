## Problem

Feedback Alignment (FA) methods replace backpropagation's weight-transport requirement with fixed random feedback weights, but the mechanism by which forward weights spontaneously align with these random feedback weights during training remains theoretically unexplained. This lack of understanding has limited FA's adoption, particularly for multi-class classification where performance lags behind standard backpropagation, and has prevented principled convergence guarantees for non-gradient-based training rules.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard image inputs (grayscale 28×28 MNIST, RGB 32×32 CIFAR-100, RGB 64×64 Tiny-ImageNet) and produces class labels via a scalar or vector-output network. The key structural difference from standard training is that the backward pass uses fixed random feedback matrices B_i (or their sign-matched counterparts in sign-FA) instead of transposed forward weights, meaning the network's forward and backward connectivity are decoupled. Experiments use two-layer fully-connected leaky-ReLU networks for theoretical validation and LeNet/ResNet-18 convolutional architectures for multi-class benchmarks.

## Method

The paper introduces a modular framework that factors the training loss decrease into three components: the cosine alignment angle between the true gradient and the FA update direction, the gradient norm, and the FA update norm. The central technical contribution is a conservation law (Theorem 5.2) proving that for (leaky) ReLU networks trained with (sign) FA, the change in the inner product between a neuron's outgoing weights and its feedback weights exactly equals half the change in the squared Frobenius norm of its incoming weights—an implicit regularization that inherently maintains alignment. Building on this, the authors define alignment dominance (analogous to the Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality) and prove exponential or polynomial convergence of the training objective when this condition holds, with explicit initialization requirements (e.g., w_L = b_L = 1) that guarantee the condition is satisfied. Empirically, sign-FA and adaFA (initialized with forward = feedback) substantially outperform plain FA on multi-class tasks, with sign-FA approaching backpropagation accuracy on CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet subsets.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (LeNet), sign-FA achieves 74.1% / 58.9% / 48.9% test accuracy on 10/25/50-class subsets, within 1.7–1.7 percentage points of full backpropagation (GD: 75.8% / 60.1% / 50.6%). On Tiny-ImageNet (ResNet-18), sign-FA reaches 64.9% / 53.0% / 47.7% versus GD's 67.5% / 55.9% / 53.4%, with the gap widening to ~5.7 pp at 50 classes. Plain FA is consistently the weakest method (e.g., 32.6% vs. 50.6% for GD on 50-class CIFAR-100), while adaFA sits in between. The conservation law (Theorem 5.2) is empirically validated with mean absolute deviations below 0.01 for sign-FA at width ≥ 100 on noisy MNIST.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four training methods: plain FA (Lillicrap et al., 2016), adaFA (Boopathy & Fiete, 2022), sign-FA (Liao et al., 2016; Moskovitz et al., 2018), and standard gradient descent (GD). Sign-FA is the strongest FA variant across all settings, narrowing the gap to GD to within ~1.7 pp on CIFAR-100 (e.g., 74.1% vs. 75.8% at 10 classes) and ~2.6 pp on Tiny-ImageNet at 10 classes, though the gap widens to ~5.7 pp at 50 classes on Tiny-ImageNet. Plain FA is consistently the worst method, trailing GD by 18–20 pp on 50-class tasks, confirming that random feedback weights without sign-matching are insufficient for complex multi-class problems. AdaFA (matching forward and feedback weights at initialization) outperforms plain FA but is outperformed by sign-FA, indicating that maintaining sign alignment throughout training is more important than initialization alone. The paper's key empirical takeaway is that sign-FA's relative advantage over GD is most pronounced on simpler tasks (10-class) and degrades gracefully as class complexity increases, supporting the theoretical claim that alignment with the gradient is the critical factor for FA performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Feedback Alignment (FA) line of work initiated by Lillicrap et al. (2016) and sign-FA by Liao et al. (2016)/Moskovitz et al. (2018), but departs from prior FA theory (Song et al. 2021, Boopathy & Fiete 2022) which was largely confined to kernel-regime or linear settings. The key conceptual departure is identifying the first implicit regularization (a conservation law) for a non-gradient training method, adapting the layer-balancing framework of Du et al. (2018) to the FA context. The alignment dominance criterion is a direct analog of the Polyak-Lojasiewicz / gradient dominance framework from Frei & Gu (2021), transplanted to non-gradient dynamics to yield convergence guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conservation law provides a mechanistic explanation for why FA auto-aligns and how Dale's principle can emerge naturally, filling a significant gap in the bio-plausible learning literature. However, convergence guarantees are currently limited to two-layer networks with leaky ReLU activations and linearly separable data, and the theory does not explain why alignment can increase non-monotonically over time. Open questions include extending convergence to deep networks, understanding FA's limitations on multi-class tasks (where sign-FA still lags gradient descent by ~2-6%), and whether the feedback weights act as a beneficial prior for generalization under distribution shift.
