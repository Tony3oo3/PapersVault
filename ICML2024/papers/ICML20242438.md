## Problem

Foundation models struggle with causal inference tasks like treatment effect estimation due to intricate reasoning and high numerical precision requirements, and existing approaches require re-optimization on each new dataset. A key open question is whether a theoretically justified self-supervised learning paradigm exists that enables a transformer-type architecture to perform zero-shot causal inference on unseen datasets without per-dataset re-fitting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242438/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multiple unlabeled observational datasets of tabular covariates X ∈ R^{N×D_X}, binary treatment indicators W ∈ {−1,+1}^N, and outcomes Y ∈ R^N, with datasets of varying sizes padded to a common length. It produces optimal covariate balancing weights α* ∈ [0,1]^N (normalized so treated and control groups each sum to 1) and the estimated ATE τ̂ = (α*W)ᵀY. Unusually, the balancing weights are read directly from the value tensor V of the final self-attention layer via α*_j = λv_j / h(X_j) W_j, where h(X_j) is the row-wise softmax denominator, rather than from a separate regression head.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution establishes a primal-dual equivalence between optimal covariate balancing (formulated as a dual SVM over a unit-ball RKHS) and the support vector expansion of self-attention with Q=K, showing that the attention output exactly recovers the SVM classifier at optimum. Training minimizes a penalized hinge loss L_θ(D) = (λ/2)‖Σ v_j/h(X_j) φ(X_j)‖² + [1 − W(softmax(KKᵀ/√D)V + β₀)]₊, which at its global minimum yields the optimal balancing weights. For zero-shot generalization, the value vector V is no longer a fixed parameter but a neural network transformation of the key matrix K and treatment vector W, trained end-to-end across M datasets by minimizing the sum of per-dataset losses. At inference on an unseen dataset, a single forward pass through the trained model directly outputs V and h(X), from which α* and τ̂ are computed in O(N) time, eliminating the need for per-dataset optimization.

## Results

On real-world datasets (Twins, IHDP, IHDP-resampled, ACIC, LaLonde CPS, LaLonde PSID), CInA achieves the best average ranking of 1.83 across all methods, with DML as the second-best at 3.0. On the ER-5000 synthetic benchmark (5000 random DAGs), CInA (ZS) matches DML performance while accelerating inference by ~10²×, and the supervised variant CInA (ZS-S) significantly outperforms all per-dataset baselines. In the single-dataset simulation setting, CInA consistently matches DML (which has the unfair advantage of using outcome variables during training) and outperforms all re-weighting baselines (IPW, SNIPW, Naive) except the ground-truth SVM reference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CInA matches DML (Chernozhukov et al., 2018) in the single-dataset setting despite DML having access to outcome variables during training, and achieves a better average ranking (1.83 vs. 3.0) across six real-world datasets. CInA outperforms IPW (Rosenbaum & Rubin, 1983) and self-normalized IPW (Busso et al., 2014) in most settings, with the notable exception of IPW on Twins. The SVM baseline (Tarr & Imai, 2021), which serves as the ground-truth reference in simulations, is outperformed by CInA (ZS) on real-world multi-dataset benchmarks (IHDP-resampled, ACIC) due to hyperparameter selection failures under distribution shift. On ER-5000, CInA (ZS) matches the best DML variant (selected from linear, kernel, and causal forest DML per Nie & Wager 2021 and Wager & Athey 2018) while being ~100× faster at inference; CInA (ZS-S) with supervised signals significantly surpasses all per-dataset baselines. The naive uniform-weight estimator is consistently the weakest baseline across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads: the optimal covariate balancing formulation via dual SVM (Tarr & Imai, 2021) and the primal-dual connection between self-attention and SVMs (Nguyen et al., 2022). By showing that self-attention with Q=K is exactly the support vector expansion of the optimal balancing classifier, the authors derive a gradient-based algorithm (CInA) that reads off optimal weights from a single forward pass. The key departure from prior causal inference work (IPW, DML, causal forests) is the multi-dataset amortized training paradigm, which enables zero-shot inference on unseen datasets without per-dataset re-optimization—a capability absent from all standard per-dataset estimators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is a theoretically grounded first step toward causal foundation models, demonstrating that transformer architectures can internalize the 'how to balance' knowledge across heterogeneous datasets and apply it zero-shot. The practical implication is substantial: a single trained model replaces per-dataset re-optimization, yielding ~100× faster inference while matching or beating DML and IPW. However, the scope remains limited to ATE estimation under SUTVA and unconfoundedness with binary treatments, and the empirical gains over strong baselines are competitive rather than dramatic. Open questions include scaling to larger and more heterogeneous dataset collections, extending to CATE and counterfactual queries, and whether the linear-independence regularity condition holds in high-dimensional real-world settings.
