## Problem

The standard Bayes optimality for Learning to Defer treats model confidence P(Y=r|x) and expert confidence P(Y=M|x) as independent quantities, requiring separate (and often unreliable) confidence estimation before making deferral decisions. This paper asks whether the dependence pattern between the model and expert observed in training data can be exploited directly to make deferral decisions, bypassing the confidence estimation step entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242151/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The scoring function g: X → R^{K+1} takes an image feature vector and an expert prediction m as input, producing K class scores plus one deferral score. The final decision is made by comparing the deferral score g_⊥(x) against the maximum class score g_q(x): if g_⊥(x) > g_q(x) the instance is deferred to the expert, otherwise the model's predicted class q is accepted. The loss is computed per training triplet (x, y, m) where y is the ground-truth label and m is the expert's prediction.

## Method

The paper introduces a 'dependent Bayes optimality' that reformulates the deferral condition as comparing joint probabilities P(Y≠r, Y=M|x) vs P(Y=r, M≠Y|x), capturing the human-model dependence pattern. From this, a deferral principle maps each training triplet to one of three actions: accept the model when the expert is wrong, defer when the model is wrong but the expert is right, and leave undecided when both are correct. The resulting Dependent Cross-Entropy (DCE) loss uses asymmetric softmax terms ψ_Y^y(g(x)) and ψ_{Y^⊥/q}^⊥(g(x)) to directly establish the ordering between g_q(x) and g_⊥(x) during training without any intermediate confidence estimation. The paper proves the DCE loss is a statistically consistent surrogate for the 0-1-deferral loss and induces a bounded expert confidence estimator in (0,1), validated on CIFAR-100, CIFAR-10N, CIFAR-100N, and ImageNet-16H with both synthetic and real-world experts.

## Results

On CIFAR-100N (real-world experts), DCE achieves 21.34% misclassification error, outperforming CE (25.61%), OvA (32.07%), and A-SM (28.50%), while also achieving the lowest ECE (21.94 vs. 27.29–32.23). On CIFAR-10N with 'Worse Label' experts, DCE reduces error to 10.69% compared to 17.83% (CE), 19.96% (OvA), and 21.20% (A-SM), with ECE dropping from 36–38% to 8.92%. On synthetic CIFAR-100 (p=94%, k=60), DCE achieves 15.81% error versus 16.22% (CE), 19.33% (OvA), and 18.40% (A-SM). DCE consistently achieves the highest coverage (i.e., defers fewer instances) while maintaining the lowest error across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCE consistently outperforms all three baselines across every dataset and metric. Against CE (Mozannar & Sontag, 2020), DCE reduces misclassification error by 4.27 points on CIFAR-100N (21.34% vs. 25.61%) and by 7.14 points on CIFAR-10N 'Worse' (10.69% vs. 17.83%), while also achieving substantially lower ECE (21.94 vs. 27.29 on CIFAR-100N). Against OvA (Verma & Nalisnick, 2022), the gap is even larger: DCE achieves 21.34% vs. 32.07% error on CIFAR-100N and 10.69% vs. 19.96% on CIFAR-10N 'Worse', with OvA suffering severe degradation under budget constraints (e.g., 54.32% budgeted error at 10% on CIFAR-100N). Against A-SM (Cao et al., 2023), DCE wins by 7.16 points on CIFAR-100N (21.34% vs. 28.50%) and 10.51 points on CIFAR-10N 'Worse' (10.69% vs. 21.20%). On synthetic CIFAR-100 (p=94%), DCE's advantage is smaller but still consistent (e.g., 15.81% vs. 18.40% for A-SM at k=60), and DCE achieves the best ECE at k=20 (1.68 vs. 4.17–5.45). The performance gap widens substantially on real-world experts, confirming that exploiting the human-model dependence pattern is most beneficial when the dependence structure is complex.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the L2D surrogate loss lineage initiated by Mozannar & Sontag (2020), extended by Verma & Nalisnick (2022) with OvA-based losses, and refined by Cao et al. (2023) with asymmetric softmax parameterization. All prior methods share a common architecture: independently estimate P(Y=r|x) and P(Y=M|x), then compare them to decide deferral. The key departure here is a reformulation of the Bayes optimality (mathematically equivalent to the original but expressed in terms of joint events) that reveals a dependence pattern between model and expert, enabling a deferral principle that bypasses confidence estimation entirely and yields a simpler two-term loss (DCE) rather than the multi-term constructions of prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is that L2D systems no longer need to accurately estimate two separate confidence functions—a known source of overconfidence and miscalibration in deep networks—making the approach more robust in data-limited or high-stakes settings. The notably lower ECE (e.g., 1.68 vs. 4–11 for baselines at k=20, p=94%) suggests the dependence-aware formulation produces better-calibrated deferral decisions. However, the ImageNet-16H results (Table 6) show DCE underperforming A-SM on noise type '125', indicating the advantage is not universal across all expert noise structures. Open questions include whether the deferral principle extends to multi-expert settings, whether other dependence patterns (beyond the binary right/wrong joint) can be exploited, and whether the approach transfers to sequential or interactive deferral scenarios.
