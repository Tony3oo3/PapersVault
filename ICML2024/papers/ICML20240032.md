## Problem

Sampling from unnormalized Boltzmann distributions (e.g., equilibrium states of many-body particle systems) is a foundational problem in science, but existing neural samplers like PIS and DDS require expensive forward/reverse SDE simulation and backpropagation through trajectories during training, preventing scaling to high-dimensional systems. Boltzmann generators and flow-based approaches depend on MCMC or molecular dynamics samples that are costly to obtain and often cover only a subset of modes. The paper asks whether a scalable, simulation-free neural sampler can learn directly from the energy function and its gradient alone while achieving full mode coverage of the target distribution.

## Input / Output

The method requires only the unnormalized energy function E(x) and its gradient ∇E(x) as input—no data samples from the target distribution are needed—along with a tractable prior (standard normal). For n-body particle systems (DW-4, LJ-13, LJ-55), the input is a 3n-dimensional point cloud with SE(3)×S_n symmetry, and the energy is invariant to rotations, translations, and particle permutations. The output is a trained equivariant diffusion sampler s_θ (an EGNN for particle systems, an MLP for the 2D GMM) that generates samples from μ_target(x) ∝ exp(−E(x)) by integrating the reverse SDE from the prior.

## Method

iDEM is a bi-level iterative algorithm whose inner loop trains a diffusion score network via Denoising Energy Matching (DEM), a novel simulation-free stochastic regression objective. The key insight is to express the score ∇log p_t(x_t) of the noised target as a ratio of expectations over N(x_t, σ_t²), placing the gradient on the energy term (∇exp(−E)) rather than the noise term, yielding a Monte Carlo estimator S_K(x_t, t) = −Σ w_i ∇E(x_{0|t}^{(i)}) with importance weights w_i ∝ exp(−E(x_{0|t}^{(i)})) that requires no SDE integration or backpropagation through trajectories. The outer loop uses the current sampler's reverse SDE to generate informative samples that populate a replay buffer, which feeds the inner loop—mimicking a well-mixed MCMC chain and enabling progressive exploration of the energy landscape. A theoretical guarantee (Proposition 1) shows the estimator bias decays as O(1/√K) under sub-Gaussian assumptions, and the framework naturally incorporates SE(3)×S_n equivariance by using a zero-center-of-mass Gaussian for the MC sampling distribution.

## Results

iDEM achieves the best NLL across all four benchmarks: 6.96 on GMM (d=2), 7.17 on DW-4 (d=8), 17.68 on LJ-13 (d=39), and 125.86 on LJ-55 (d=165), outperforming FAB (7.14, 7.16, 17.52, 200.32) and all neural sampler baselines. iDEM also attains the highest Effective Sample Size (ESS) on GMM (0.734), LJ-13 (0.231), and LJ-55 (0.106), and the lowest 2-Wasserstein distance on GMM (7.42) and LJ-55 (16.13). Critically, iDEM is the only method that successfully trains on the high-dimensional LJ-55 task, while PIS and DDS diverge. Training time is 2–5× faster than FAB (e.g., 7.75 h vs. 40.35 h on LJ-55).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FAB (the prior SOTA combining AIS with equivariant normalizing flows), iDEM achieves lower NLL on all four datasets (e.g., 125.86 vs. 200.32 on LJ-55) and higher ESS on GMM, LJ-13, and LJ-55, while training 2–5× faster (7.75 h vs. 40.35 h on LJ-55). Against PIS (path integral sampler), iDEM wins on every metric where PIS does not diverge; PIS fails entirely on LJ-55 and shows poor ESS on LJ-13 (0.004 vs. iDEM's 0.231). Against DDS (denoising diffusion sampler), iDEM dominates on all shared benchmarks; DDS diverges on both LJ-13 and LJ-55, and on DW-4 its NLL (11.27) is substantially worse than iDEM's (7.17). The ablation variant pDEM (prior-only, no outer-loop sampling) is competitive on lower-dimensional tasks but diverges on 2/3 seeds for LJ-55, confirming that the iterative outer loop is essential for stability in high dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

iDEM extends the denoising diffusion framework (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021) to the setting where the target is an unnormalized Boltzmann density with known energy but no data samples. It departs from prior neural samplers (PIS, DDS, DIS) that require expensive SDE simulation and backpropagation through trajectories during training, by introducing a simulation-free stochastic regression objective (DEM) that places the gradient on the energy function rather than the Gaussian kernel. The iterative bi-level scheme—where the diffusion sampler generates its own training data via a replay buffer—draws conceptual parallels to persistent contrastive divergence in energy-based models and to off-policy GFlowNet training, while the outer-loop amortized sampling distinguishes it from the purely off-policy pDEM variant. The paper also connects DEM to flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) and Reverse Diffusion Monte Carlo (Huang et al., 2024), positioning it within a broader family of simulation-free score estimation techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

iDEM is the first method to successfully train on the 55-particle Lennard-Jones system using energy-based objectives, a practical milestone for molecular and materials science applications where MCMC and MD are intractable. The simulation-free inner loop (O(1) gradient time, O(d) memory) yields 2–5× speedups over the prior SOTA (FAB), making high-dimensional Boltzmann sampling more accessible. Key open questions include reducing the variance of the biased DEM estimator (e.g., via adaptive importance sampling), accelerating the outer-loop SDE integration, and understanding whether the iterative self-sampling scheme can be replaced by more principled exploration strategies. The connection to GFlowNets and flow matching suggests the DEM objective may generalize beyond diffusion to other sequential generative structures.
