## Problem

Real-world longitudinal datasets (e.g., electronic health records) simultaneously exhibit high dimensionality, structured missingness patterns (MAR/MNAR), and stochastic observation times, yet existing methods address at most one of these challenges in isolation. GP-prior VAEs handle temporal correlation but assume MCAR missingness; missing-data VAEs handle structured missingness but ignore temporal structure; and point process models capture stochastic timing but do not jointly model high-dimensional marks with missingness. No unified framework existed that could model all three properties together.

## Input / Output

The model consumes per-patient sequences of high-dimensional observation vectors (e.g., 36×36 pixel images or 37 clinical attributes) with binary missingness masks, auxiliary covariates (instance id, time, gender, disease status, ICU type), and timestamps that may be regularly or irregularly sampled. Beyond standard imputation and future prediction outputs, the model produces an inferred intensity function of the temporal point process, which is fed back as an additional input to the GP kernel computations for both the observation and missingness latent variables, creating a closed-loop dependency. The Physionet dataset is extremely sparse (~85% missing), and the model handles variable-length patient sequences with up to 48 hours of ICU monitoring.

## Method

The model introduces three sets of latent variables with GP priors: z^y for observations, z^m for missingness masks, and z^λ for the temporal point process intensity. The key architectural insight is that the inferred point process intensity λ(t) = (z^λ(t) + β)² is injected as an additional input to the longitudinal additive kernels governing z^y and z^m, so that similarity between observations depends not just on elapsed time but on the underlying event rate. The point process uses a Hawkes-style self-exciting structure with a GP over the last D timestamps and a squared link function for analytical tractability. Training employs amortized variational inference with neural network encoders/decoders, scalable KL upper bounds from Ramchandran et al. (2021) that exploit the block-diagonal structure of longitudinal kernels, and mini-batching over patients. Two variants are presented: LLSM (without the point process) and LLPPSM (full model with the point process feedback loop).

## Results

On the irregularly sampled Health MNIST imputation task, LLPPSM achieves the lowest MSE of 0.128 at 50% missingness, 0.162 at 75%, and 0.207 at 90%, consistently outperforming the strongest baseline L-VAE (0.171, 0.264, 0.379) by 25–47%. For future data prediction on the same dataset, LLPPSM reaches MSE 0.025–0.030 across missingness levels, beating L-VAE (0.029–0.047) and LLSM (0.030–0.031). On the Physionet ICU dataset (37 attributes, ~85% missing), LLSM achieves the best future prediction MSE of 0.713, slightly ahead of L-VAE (0.720), while LLPPSM (0.745) underperforms due to the near-regular hourly sampling making the point process component redundant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three main baselines: GPPVAE (Casale et al., 2018), L-VAE (Ramchandran et al., 2021), and GRUI-GAN (Luo et al., 2018). LLSM and LLPPSM dominate all baselines on imputation across both regularly and irregularly sampled Health MNIST, with L-VAE being the closest competitor but still 25–47% worse in MSE at higher missingness levels. GRUI-GAN is competitive at moderate missingness (e.g., 0.165 vs. 0.130 for LLSM at 50%) but degrades sharply at 90% (0.277 vs. 0.191). On future data prediction, LLPPSM outperforms L-VAE by 10–36% in MSE on irregularly sampled Health MNIST, while LLSM matches or slightly beats L-VAE on the regularly sampled variant. On Physionet, LLSM achieves the best MSE (0.713) but LLPPSM (0.745) is outperformed by L-VAE (0.720), which the authors attribute to the near-regular hourly sampling making the point process intensity redundant and destabilizing training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads: GP-prior VAEs for longitudinal data (L-VAE, GP-VAE), VAEs with structured missingness (Collier et al., not-MIWAE), and GP-based temporal point processes (Liu & Hauskrecht, Lloyd et al.). No prior method simultaneously addresses high-dimensional observations, structured missingness (MAR/MNAR), and stochastic timestamps; each existing approach handles at most two of these challenges. The model inherits the longitudinal additive kernel structure from Ramchandran et al. (2021) and the squared-link GP point process formulation from Liu & Hauskrecht (2019), combining them through a shared variational framework with inducing points for scalability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is clear for electronic health records and similar longitudinal biomedical datasets where measurements are high-dimensional, irregularly sampled, and subject to non-random missingness. A notable limitation is that on the Physionet ICU dataset, where observations are taken at near-regular hourly intervals, the point process component (LLPPSM) actually degrades performance relative to the simpler LLSM, suggesting the method's benefit is contingent on genuinely stochastic sampling. Open questions include scalability to very large patient cohorts, robustness of the point process intensity to near-regular sampling patterns, and whether the separate latent variables for observations and masks could be further coupled to capture richer missingness dynamics.
