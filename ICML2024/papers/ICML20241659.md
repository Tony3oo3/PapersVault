## Problem

LM-based controllers for sequential decision-making must convert environment observations to text, and coupling these with interaction history produces long, verbose prompts that limit prior work to restricted domains with small observations and minimal history. This paper asks whether a simple, task-agnostic abstraction can make LM agents data-efficient in high-dimensional, long-horizon environments like NetHack, where prior neural agents required hundreds of billions of expert actions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241659/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a task instruction, a full-text anchor observation at the oldest timestep, and a sequence of Unix diff deltas (annotated with +/- symbols and @@ hunk headers) interleaved with past actions, all formatted with special <|action|> and <|observation|> delimiter tokens. It outputs a natural language action string generated via greedy decoding, terminated by the <|observation|> token. Context length is 1024 tokens for BabyAI-Text and extended to 4096 for NetHack; in NetHack, diff reduces mean tokens per observation by 460% (from 573 to 123), while in BabyAI-Text it increases them by 210% due to near-complete observation changes between timesteps.

## Method

The core mechanism is applying the Unix diff command between consecutive pairs of full-text observations in interaction histories, keeping only the first observation as a full-text anchor and replacing all subsequent observations with their symbolic deltas (Equation 4). This is entirely task-agnostic, requires no re-tokenization or new vocabulary, and provides two benefits: (1) relational abstraction that localizes salient per-timestep changes in object states and scene properties, yielding a denser learning signal, and (2) token-level compression that extends the effective memory horizon at fixed context length. Training uses GPT-2 (127M parameters) with standard cross-entropy loss on action tokens under teacher forcing, optionally augmented with an auxiliary world-model prediction loss on observation tokens. The approach achieves state-of-the-art NetHack performance (mean score 2766) with 1800× fewer training examples than prior neural agents, and a 25% reduction in optimizer steps for BabyAI-Text finetuning.

## Results

On NetHack, the best diff history language agent (GPT-2-127M, pretrained, action prediction only, LangHack-2x with 64M expert actions) achieves a mean game score of 2766±481 and median of 1802, matching the 115B-parameter XL-LSTM(CDGPT5) SOTA (2740) from Tuyls et al. (2023) while using 1800× fewer training examples. On the smaller LangHack dataset (32M actions), the diff history agent scores 1885±213, outperforming full-text history ablations by 98% (1885 vs. 31) and vision baselines (LSTM CDGPT5: 192, Transformer: 213) by 89%. On BabyAI-Text, diff history agents reach equivalent peak performance to full-text agents but require ≥25% fewer FLOPs (optimizer steps) in both 1K and 5K demonstration settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Piterbarg et al. (2023), whose 3.2B-parameter vision agents (LSTM CDGPT5 and Transformer-LSTM) achieve mean NetHack scores of 658 and 1318 respectively, the 127M-parameter diff history language agent trained on only 32M expert actions (100× fewer) scores 1885, outperforming both by 186% and 43%. Against Tuyls et al. (2023), whose 115B-parameter XL-LSTM(CDGPT5) achieves a mean score of 2740 on 115B expert actions, the diff history agent on LangHack-2x (64M actions) reaches 2766, essentially matching the SOTA with 1800× fewer labeled samples. Internally, ablating diff observations from the finetuning data causes a 98% drop in mean NetHack score (1885 → 31), confirming that the diff representation is the primary driver of performance. On BabyAI-Text, diff history agents match full-text agents in final performance but converge 25–33% faster in terms of optimizer steps, despite diff observations actually increasing average token count by 210% in that low-dimensional environment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Unix diff command—a tool designed for software version control—as a task-agnostic abstraction mechanism for LM-based sequential decision-making agents. It departs from prior abstraction methods for LMs (Scratchpad, Chain-of-Thought, Tree-of-Thought), which are all task-specific and require hand-engineered annotations or prompt schemas, by introducing a deterministic, zero-annotation transformation of interaction histories. In the LM-agent-for-decision-making line (Li et al. 2022, Carta et al. 2023), prior work was limited to low-dimensional environments with short histories; this paper extends the paradigm to the high-dimensional, infinite-horizon NetHack setting and introduces the LangHack dataset to support text-based training there.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a trivially implementable, off-the-shelf text-diff operation is responsible for a 98% performance gap in NetHack agent quality challenges the assumption that LM agents need learned or task-specific representations of environment state. The 1800× data-efficiency result suggests that representation quality, not data volume, may be the binding constraint for low-resource LM agent training. Key limitations include the requirement that observations be expressible in structured text (ruling out narrative or unstructured observations), the absence of explicit planning, and evaluation on a single model family (GPT-2 127M). Open questions include whether diff history generalizes to multimodal settings, whether it composes with System-2 planning, and whether the compression benefit in high-dimensional environments transfers to other domains like web navigation or code editing.
