## Problem

All regular stationary covariance functions used in GP regression are necessarily mean-reverting, causing the posterior to relax toward a fixed global mean far from data points. This produces pathological behavior on data that does not naturally revert to a constant (e.g., stock prices, trends), yet no stationary non-mean-reverting kernel exists within the proper (positive-definite) framework. The paper asks whether stationarity and non-mean-reversion can coexist, and shows they can only do so in the improper (infinite-variance) limit.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard input-output training pairs (x_k, y_k) with Gaussian observation noise and a set of query points, identical in format to ordinary GP regression. The output is a posterior mean and covariance at the query points, but computed via corrected formulas that include an additional rank-one correction term (involving the all-ones vector) absent from standard GP regression. Because the prior variance is infinite, the marginal likelihood is divergent, so hyperparameters (length scale, noise level) are optimized by minimizing the marginal likelihood conditional on a single randomly held-out observation.

## Method

The core mechanism is to write the covariance as k(t,t') = s(t,t') + c where s is a non-positive (conditionally positive definite) kernel and c → ∞, yielding an improper GP prior with infinite variance everywhere. Applying the Sherman-Morrison formula in this limit produces closed-form posterior mean and covariance formulas that differ from standard GP regression by a simple correction term involving the all-ones vector. The paper then constructs a family of smooth stationary non-reverting kernels: the Smooth Walk kernel s_sw(τ) = −τ tanh(τ/l) (infinitely differentiable, analogous to squared exponential), the Matérn Walk family obtained by convolving −|τ| with a Matérn kernel (Eq. 28), and the Gaussian Walk as the infinite-smoothness limit. These kernels are proven conditionally positive definite via tempered distribution theory, with spectral densities that are singular (∝ 1/ω²) at zero frequency, which is precisely what breaks positive-definiteness while preserving stationarity and eliminating mean reversion.

## Results

On the UCI regression benchmark (6 features, 100 training points), the improper kernels achieve median negative log-likelihood (NNL) of 2.41–9.37 across five datasets, compared to 37.55–1643.39 for the proper Squared Exponential GP. On the stock-price forecasting task, the Gaussian Walk (improper) kernel achieves a median NNL of 3.90 versus 76.02 for the proper SE GP. In the aggregated high-dimensional UCI results (full features), the Matérn Walk 1/2 (improper) kernel attains a mean error of 0.062, outperforming the best proper GP (Matérn 1/2, 0.430) by a factor of ~7.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the proper Squared Exponential GP (Williams & Rasmussen, 2006), the improper kernels deliver dramatic improvements: on the stock-price task, median NNL drops from 76.02 (SE) to 3.90 (GW), and on UCI datasets the gap ranges from ~10× (energy: 37.55 → 2.41) to over 100× (concreteslump: 1643.39 → 9.37). The proper Matérn class (Stein, 1999) is the strongest proper-GP baseline but remains far behind; for example, Matérn 1/2 achieves NNL 22.74 on autompg versus 1.79 for Matérn Walk 1/2 (improper). Against Intrinsic Kriging (Matheron, 1973), the improper GPs win on 4 of 5 UCI datasets shown in Table 2, with the sole exception being the bike dataset where IK(α=0.5) achieves 1.40 versus 4.85 for MW 1/2 (improper). The paper notes that intrinsic kriging with power-law kernels is a special case of the proposed framework, and the smoother improper kernels (GW, MW) generally outperform the less smooth IK baselines in the high-dimensional aggregated results (Table 4: MW 1/2 mean error 0.062 vs IK 0.499).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends several previously separate threads: improper priors in Bayesian statistics, conditionally positive definite kernels from spatial statistics (Matheron, 1973; Ong et al., 2004), and intrinsic kriging. Prior work on avoiding mean reversion in GPs either adopted explicitly non-stationary kernels (Zhang, 2015; Majumdar and Gelfand, 2007) or added explicit trend models (Journel and Rossi, 1989; Raich and Zhou, 2004), both of which sacrifice the location-invariance that makes stationary kernels attractive. The key departure here is showing that stationarity and non-mean-reversion can coexist if one works in the improper (infinite-variance) limit, yielding a family of smooth conditionally positive definite kernels (Smooth Walk, Matérn Walk, Gaussian Walk) that closely mirror standard SE and Matérn kernels but without the mean-reversion pathology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing tension in GP regression: the most commonly used stationary kernels (SE, Matérn) are provably mean-reverting, which produces pathological behavior on data with persistent trends. The practical impact is demonstrated on stock-price forecasting and UCI regression benchmarks where improper kernels substantially outperform proper ones. However, significant open questions remain: the isotropic extensions to d > 1 dimensions are only numerically verified (up to d=150) rather than analytically proven, and the broader kernel family is limited to a few constructions. The connection to intrinsic kriging and spline smoothing suggests a deeper unification that could yield further kernel families.
