## Problem

Prior theoretical analyses of DeepWalk and Node2Vec reframe the algorithm as matrix factorization, but this characterization only holds when the embedding dimension is at least as large as the number of nodes—far from the constant-dimensional embeddings used in practice. No existing work analyzes the actual gradient descent dynamics for low-dimensional embeddings on natural graph classes, leaving it unresolved whether the nonconvex optimization converges to a solution with meaningful cluster structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph G drawn from a symmetric Stochastic Block Model with K equally sized blocks and constructs a co-occurrence matrix C via r random walks of length L with a sliding window of size T. It then learns d-dimensional node embeddings x and context embeddings y (d can be as small as 1) by minimizing a softmax-based objective via gradient descent, initialized in a small ball of radius ε = 1/n^(2/3) and run until the embedding norm reaches εΔ = n^(1/6). The output is a set of node embeddings where vertices within the same community are tightly clustered and vertices in different communities are separated, provably recovering 1-o(1) fraction of each community.

## Method

The key technical insight is that when the embedding vectors have small norm (‖w‖ < ε), the softmax matrix Q in the gradient update is close to the uniform matrix J/n in Frobenius norm, so the nonlinear update decomposes into a linear update governed by M = D_C·(J/n) − C plus a small error term bounded by O(ε²). The linearized update matrix L has exactly (K−1) eigenvalues exceeding 1+ηγ (where γ scales with the SBM community gap p−q), while all remaining eigenvalues are bounded by 1+ηc/√(n^(ρ+2)). By tracking the projection of the iterate onto the top (K−1) eigenspace of L, the authors show this component grows geometrically while the orthogonal component stays controlled, yielding both within-cluster tightness and cross-cluster separation. The analysis works even for 1-dimensional embeddings, which is surprising since spectral methods typically require dimension ≥ K for K clusters, and the linear approximation is validated empirically to hold well beyond the theoretical parameter regime.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is a set of convergence guarantees for DeepWalk gradient descent on symmetric SBM graphs. Theorem 3.8 (clustering) shows that within-cluster embedding variation satisfies ‖x − μ‖ ≤ 5‖x‖/Δ where Δ = n^{1/6}, meaning intra-cluster spread shrinks as n grows. Theorem 3.10 (separation) guarantees inter-cluster center gaps of at least εΔ/(20K²√n) with probability ≥ 0.9 over initialization. Theorem 3.11 (weak recovery) then implies that 1 − o(1) fraction of each community is correctly recovered. Notably, these guarantees hold even for one-dimensional embeddings, which is surprising since classical spectral methods require embedding dimension ≥ K for K clusters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not benchmark DeepWalk against competing embedding methods (e.g., Node2Vec, LINE, spectral embeddings) on standard datasets. Instead, it positions its contribution relative to prior theoretical analyses: unlike Levy & Goldberg (2014), Qiu et al. (2018), Zhang & Tang (2021), and Barot et al. (2021), which require embedding dimension > n for their matrix-factorization characterization, this paper analyzes the actual gradient descent dynamics at constant (even 1) dimension. Compared to Harker & Bhaskara (2023), which proves the existence of a well-clustered global minimum but only for 2-block SBMs and without studying algorithmic convergence, this paper extends to K-block SBMs and proves gradient descent converges to the clustered solution. The experimental validation (Section 4.2) shows that the linear approximation to the gradient update remains close to the true nonlinear update for graphs up to n = 1000 nodes, suggesting the theoretical regime may be broader than the proven bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the theoretical analysis of DeepWalk beyond the matrix factorization framework used by prior works (Qiu et al. 2018, Zhang & Tang 2021, Barot et al. 2021), which only applies when the embedding dimension exceeds the number of nodes. It also builds directly on the authors' own prior work (Harker & Bhaskara 2023), which showed the DeepWalk objective has a clustered global minimum for 2-block SBMs but did not analyze whether gradient descent actually converges to it. The key technical departure is analyzing the nonlinear gradient descent dynamics directly via a linear approximation argument, inspired by analogous analyses of t-SNE (Linderman & Steinerberger 2019, Arora et al. 2018), rather than reframing the problem as spectral/matrix factorization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provable convergence guarantee for DeepWalk (or similar random-walk-based embedding methods) in the low-dimensional regime that is actually used in practice, filling a significant gap between the algorithm's empirical success and its theoretical understanding. A notable limitation is the reliance on Assumption 3.2 (spectral closeness of empirical and expected co-occurrence matrices) rather than tight recovery thresholds in terms of p, q, K, leaving the precise phase transition for recovery as an open question. The linear approximation technique—showing that small-norm initializations make the nonlinear update behave like a linear one—may generalize to other nonconvex graph embedding objectives, and the surprising result that 1D embeddings suffice for community separation (unlike spectral methods requiring dimension ≥ K) suggests the random-walk structure encodes community information more efficiently than previously appreciated.
