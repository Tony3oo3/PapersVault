## Problem

Dueling bandits with preferential feedback lack a natural notion of optimality when no coherent ranking (e.g., strong stochastic transitivity) exists among items. This paper tackles Borda regret minimization in the generalized linear dueling bandit model—where preference probabilities depend on pair-specific contextual features—without assuming any transitivity or consistency conditions. The key gaps addressed are the absence of a matching lower bound and the lack of algorithms achieving optimal regret for both stochastic and adversarial settings in this general linear model.

## Input / Output

The agent receives binary pairwise comparison feedback (item i preferred over j) and has access to known d-dimensional feature vectors φ_{i,j} associated with each item *pair* (not individual items), with the constraint φ_{i,j} = −φ_{j,i}. The regret definition is non-standard: each round's regret is 2B(i*) − B(i_t) − B(j_t), so both selected items contribute equally to the loss, making the problem fundamentally different from standard multi-armed bandits where only one arm is pulled. The output is a sequence of item-pair selections that minimizes cumulative Borda regret over horizon T.

## Method

Two algorithms are proposed. For the stochastic setting, BETC-GLM is an explore-then-commit algorithm: it first performs uniform random exploration to ensure the design matrix is well-conditioned, then executes a G-optimal design (minimizing max_{i,j} ‖φ_{i,j}‖²_{V(π)^{-1}}) to efficiently gather information, computes the MLE estimator θ̂, and commits to the empirical Borda winner for the remaining rounds, achieving Õ(d^{2/3} T^{2/3}) regret. For the adversarial setting, BEXP3 is an EXP3-type algorithm that maintains a distribution over items, computes one-sample unbiased estimates of the parameter and (shifted) Borda scores each round, and updates via exponential weights with uniform exploration, achieving Õ((d log K)^{1/3} T^{2/3}) regret. A novel lower bound of Ω(d^{2/3} T^{2/3}) is established via a hard-instance construction with 2^{d+1} items where 'good' items are distinguished only through comparisons with 'bad' items, revealing an inherent exploration-exploitation tension absent in standard linear bandits.

## Results

The paper establishes a worst-case regret lower bound of Ω(d^{2/3} T^{2/3}) for Borda regret minimization in generalized linear dueling bandits, and proposes algorithms (BETC-GLM for stochastic, BEXP3 for adversarial) that achieve matching upper bounds of Õ(d^{2/3} T^{2/3}) (or Õ((d log K)^{1/3} T^{2/3}) when K is not exponentially large in d). Empirically, on both a synthetic hard instance (d=6, K=128, T=10^6) and the EventTime dataset (K=100, T=10^6), BETC-GLM and BEXP3 achieve the lowest cumulative Borda regret among all tested methods. UCB-BORDA incurs linear regret due to its uniform second-arm sampling, while DEXP3 and ETC-BORDA show higher regret than the proposed methods because they do not exploit contextual information.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: UCB-BORDA (UCB with Borda reduction), DEXP3 (adversarial dueling EXP3 from Saha et al. 2021), and ETC-BORDA (a context-free explore-then-commit baseline). On both the synthetic hard instance and the EventTime dataset, BETC-GLM and BEXP3 clearly outperform all three baselines in cumulative Borda regret at T=10^6. UCB-BORDA performs worst, suffering linear regret because its second arm is always sampled uniformly at random, incurring a constant per-round penalty. DEXP3 and ETC-BORDA, which ignore the contextual feature vectors φ_{i,j}, require substantially more exploration and thus accumulate higher regret than the context-aware proposed methods. Notably, BEXP3 (designed for the linear model) performs comparably to BETC-GLM even when the true comparison probabilities follow a logistic model on EventTime, demonstrating robustness to mild model misspecification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generalized linear contextual bandit framework of Li et al. (2017) to the dueling bandit setting, adapting the explore-then-commit structure, MLE estimation, and G-optimal design to handle pairwise comparison feedback. For the adversarial setting, it adapts the DEXP3 algorithm from Saha et al. (2021b) to the contextual linear model. A key departure from prior contextual dueling bandit work (Saha 2021; Bengs et al. 2022) is the removal of the structural assumption φ_{i,j} = x_i − x_j, which in prior work implied the Bradley-Terry model and stochastic transitivity; here, only the antisymmetry constraint φ_{i,j} = −φ_{j,i} is required, making the setting strictly more general.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first nearly-optimal regret bounds (matching up to log factors) for Borda regret minimization in the most general linear dueling bandit setting without transitivity assumptions, closing a gap left by prior work that either assumed coherent rankings or restricted to structured contexts. The lower bound construction—where good items are mutually indistinguishable and must be separated via bad-item queries—reveals an intrinsic hardness that separates Borda regret minimization from standard linear bandit problems (d^{2/3}T^{2/3} vs. d√T). Open questions include whether the gap between the d^{2/3} and (d log K)^{1/3} bounds can be closed, whether the adversarial regret can be improved beyond (d log K)^{1/3}T^{2/3} for very large K, and whether the explore-then-commit structure remains optimal under more general (non-linear) link functions.
