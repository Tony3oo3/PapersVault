## Problem

Learning to defer predictions to multiple experts has been well-studied in classification, but the standard score-based formulation is inapplicable to regression due to the continuous, infinite label space. This paper addresses the gap by developing a principled predictor-rejector framework for regression with multi-expert deferral, providing surrogate losses with strong non-asymptotic H-consistency guarantees for both single-stage (joint learning) and two-stage (fixed predictor) scenarios.

## Input / Output

The method takes a tabular input instance x and produces a pair (h(x), r(x)): a regression prediction h(x) ∈ ℝ and a deferral decision r(x) ∈ {0, 1, …, n_e}, where 0 means the base predictor's output is used and j > 0 means the prediction is deferred to expert g_j. The deferral cost c_j(x, y) can be instance-dependent and label-dependent (e.g., L(g_j(x), y) + α_j), and the framework accommodates any bounded regression loss L. In the two-stage variant, h is a fixed pre-trained predictor and only r is learned.

## Method

The core contribution is a predictor-rejector formulation where the deferral loss L_def is decomposed via Lemma 3.1 into a combination of multi-class zero-one indicators, enabling the construction of a surrogate loss L_ℓ (single-stage, Eq. 2) that jointly optimizes h and r using a multi-class surrogate ℓ (e.g., logistic loss) weighted by cost terms. In the two-stage variant (Eq. 3), h is fixed and only r is optimized, dropping a constant term. The key theoretical result (Theorems 3.2, 4.1, 4.3) proves H-consistency bounds: the excess deferral loss is controlled by the excess surrogate loss via a calibration function Γ̄(t) that depends on the number of experts, the loss bound l̄, and cost bounds c̄_j. In the single-expert special case, the surrogate reduces to the binary margin-based form c(x,y)Φ(r(x)) + L(h(x),y)Φ(−r(x)), which subsumes Cheng et al. (2023) as a special case with squared loss and label-independent cost.

## Results

On the three UCI regression datasets (Airfoil, Housing, Concrete), the single-stage method with three experts and no base cost achieves System MSE of 8.53±1.57 (Airfoil), 12.43±2.03 (Housing), and 37.83±2.60 (Concrete). The two-stage method with three experts and no base cost achieves 8.81±1.56, 12.02±1.97, and 36.75±1.76 respectively. Performance improves consistently as the number of experts increases from one to three across all datasets and both methods. The base model (linear predictor) alone has substantially higher error, e.g., 23.35±1.90 on Airfoil and 120.20±8.09 on Concrete, confirming that deferral to experts yields significant gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper explicitly notes that it is the first to study regression with multi-expert deferral, so no external baselines exist for direct comparison. The main internal comparisons are between the single-stage and two-stage methods, and between configurations with and without base costs. The single-stage method generally outperforms the two-stage method (e.g., 8.53 vs. 8.81 on Airfoil, 37.83 vs. 36.75 on Concrete with three experts and no base cost), attributed to error accumulation in the two-stage process when the first-stage predictor has large errors. Adding base costs (α₁=4, α₂=8, α₃=12) slightly degrades performance in most settings, as expected since deferral becomes more expensive. Both methods substantially outperform the base linear model alone (e.g., 8.53 vs. 23.35 on Airfoil, 37.83 vs. 120.20 on Concrete), demonstrating the value of multi-expert deferral. The authors also include three simple baselines in Appendix F for completeness, further confirming the effectiveness of their approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the predictor-rejector formulation for learning to defer (Cortes et al., 2016b; Mao et al., 2023a, 2024a, 2024b) from classification to regression, where the score-based formulation commonly used in multi-expert classification deferral (Mozannar & Sontag, 2020; Verma et al., 2023) is inapplicable due to the continuous label space. It builds on the H-consistency framework (Awasthi et al., 2022a,b; Mao et al., 2023f) to derive non-asymptotic, hypothesis-set-specific consistency bounds for new surrogate losses tailored to multi-expert regression deferral. The single-stage formulation subsumes the regression-with-abstention framework of Cheng et al. (2023) as a special case (single expert, squared loss, label-independent cost), while the two-stage scenario extends the fixed-predictor deferral analysis of Mao et al. (2023a) to the regression setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide a principled theoretical framework for regression with multi-expert deferral, filling a gap between the well-studied classification deferral literature and the limited single-expert regression-with-abstention results. The H-consistency guarantees are stronger than Bayes consistency and directly yield algorithms, but the empirical validation is limited to three small UCI datasets with linear predictors and small neural-network experts, leaving open whether the approach scales to high-dimensional or large-scale regression problems. Key open questions include how to optimally select diverse experts, whether the two-stage error accumulation can be mitigated, and whether the framework extends to structured or multi-output regression.
