## Problem

Previous generative document retrieval (GDR) methods design document ID strings by clustering document embeddings, considering only the document distribution X while ignoring the query distribution Q. This paper shows via information bottleneck theory that the optimal indexing is dictated by the query distribution rather than the document distribution, since T serves as a bottleneck for transmitting information from X to Q.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240472/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For indexing, each document x is represented not by its own BERT embedding but by the mean BERT embedding of a set of associated queries Q_x (combining real training queries, docT5query-generated queries, and random document segments of ~60 words). Hierarchical k-means over these query-mean vectors produces ID strings of length m over alphabet V=[1,...,30], where m is the minimum value satisfying |V|^m ≥ |X|. At retrieval time, a query text is fed to a T5-based encoder with a PAWA decoder that autoregressively generates the document ID string via beam search.

## Method

The paper reformulates GDR indexing through the information bottleneck objective min I(X;T) − β·I(T;Q), showing under Gaussian assumptions that hierarchical k-means is the bottleneck-minimal clustering. The key practical difference from prior methods (DSI, NCI) is that k-means is applied to the mean BERT embeddings of queries associated with each document (μ_{Q|x}) rather than to the document's own BERT embedding (μ_x). The query set Q_x per document is constructed from three sources: real training queries, queries generated by docT5query, and random document segments, with the mean of their BERT embeddings serving as the maximum-likelihood estimator of μ_{Q|x}. The retrieval model follows the NCI architecture (T5 encoder + 4-layer PAWA decoder) trained with standard supervised sequence-to-sequence learning, requiring no architectural changes—only the indexing stage differs.

## Results

On NQ320K with T5-base, BMI achieves Rec@1 of 66.69, outperforming hierarchical k-means (65.43) by 1.26 points and random clustering (65.85) by 0.84 points. On MARCO Lite with T5-base, BMI reaches Rec@1 of 45.20, a 3.72-point improvement over hierarchical k-means (41.48). The gains are most dramatic with smaller models: with T5-mini, BMI improves Rec@1 by 7.06 points (17%) on NQ320K and 6.45 points (91%) on MARCO Lite over hierarchical k-means. In the SOTA comparison (Table 4), BMI with finetuned GenQ queries achieves Rec@1 of 67.8 on NQ320K, surpassing all static-index methods and closely competing with learned-index SOTA methods GENRET (68.1) and NOVO (69.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BMI consistently outperforms hierarchical k-means (NCI's default indexing, Wang et al. 2022) across all model sizes and both datasets, with the largest margins on MARCO Lite using T5-mini (+6.45 Rec@1, 91% relative improvement). Against locality-sensitive hashing (Datar et al. 2004), BMI wins on nearly all settings, though LSH occasionally matches or slightly exceeds BMI on NQ320K with T5-small. In the broader SOTA comparison (Table 4), BMI (Rec@1 67.8) surpasses all static-index methods including DSI (27.4), Ultron-PQ (25.6), Ultron-URL (33.8), SEAL (26.3), Tied-At. (65.3), and NCI (66.9). BMI also outperforms the learned-index method LMIndexer (Jin et al. 2023, Rec@1 66.3) by 1.5 points. However, BMI trails the two strongest learned-index SOTA methods: GENRET (Sun et al. 2023, Rec@1 68.1) by 0.3 points and NOVO (Wang et al. 2023, Rec@1 69.3) by 1.5 points, both of which use textual ID strings and learn indexes jointly with the retrieval model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative document retrieval (GDR) framework established by DSI (Tay et al., 2022) and NCI (Wang et al., 2022), which both design document ID strings by applying hierarchical k-means clustering to document BERT embeddings. The key conceptual departure is the application of Tishby et al.'s (2000) information bottleneck theory to GDR, which reveals that the optimal indexing should minimize a tradeoff between I(X;T) and I(T;Q) rather than merely minimizing distortion between X and T. This theoretical reframing leads to the practical insight that clustering query embeddings (μ_{Q|x}) rather than document embeddings (μ_x) yields bottleneck-minimal indexes, a distinction absent from all prior GDR indexing methods (DSI, NCI, SEAL, LSH-based approaches).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first information-theoretic formalization of GDR indexing quality, giving practitioners a principled criterion (the IB curve) for evaluating and comparing indexing methods beyond just end-to-end retrieval scores. The practical method is remarkably simple—replace document embeddings with mean query embeddings before running the same hierarchical k-means—yet yields substantial gains, particularly for smaller models where the bottleneck is most constraining. Limitations include the Gaussian assumption on p(Q|X) (which may not hold for multi-modal query distributions), reliance on BERT embeddings that are suboptimal for short query texts, and the static nature of the index (not co-learned with the retrieval model). Open questions include extending to non-Gaussian query distributions, learned indexing under the IB framework, and whether the query-clustering principle generalizes to cross-lingual or multimodal retrieval settings.
