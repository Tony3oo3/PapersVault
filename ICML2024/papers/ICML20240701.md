## Problem

Current Large Vision Models (LVMs) rely on 3B+ parameter architectures trained on 400B+ tokens to achieve multi-task visual understanding, making them impractical for resource-constrained settings. Additionally, when training on multiple vision tasks with naturally long-tail data distributions (e.g., 11M segmentation images vs. 0.2M pose images), the model catastrophically fails on underrepresented tasks, and standard re-sampling remedies actually worsen performance. This paper asks whether both the data volume and model size requirements can be substantially reduced without sacrificing multi-task capability.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a visual sentence constructed by concatenating VQGAN tokens from a task prompt (several input-output image pairs defining the task) followed by the tokenized test image (256 tokens per image, codebook size 8192, token dim 64). The autoregressive LLaMA transformer predicts the next 256 tokens, which are decoded by the VQGAN decoder into an output image (segmentation mask, pose map, derained image, etc.). For ImageNet classification, the VQGAN encoder is replaced by a patch embedding layer with an average-pooling and fully-connected head, producing a class label directly.

## Method

The architecture follows LVM: a VQGAN tokenizer converts each image into 256 discrete tokens, and a causal LLaMA transformer (300M or 80M parameters) performs next-token prediction with cross-entropy loss. The first key contribution is a data augmentation strategy for multi-task balancing: rather than re-sampling minority-task samples (which the paper shows degrades performance), they apply random crop and random flip to underrepresented datasets, tokenizing the augmented versions and mixing them into training, which outperforms both unbalanced training and re-sampling across all tasks. The second contribution is applying standard Hinton-style knowledge distillation to autoregressive LVMs for the first time: a LLaMA-1B teacher guides a LLaMA-300M or 80M student via soft-label KD loss on the token prediction objective, significantly narrowing the validation loss and perplexity gap (e.g., 80M student with KD achieves 147.78 vs. 156.19 perplexity on segmentation). The paper further demonstrates that data shuffling is essential to avoid catastrophic forgetting in multi-task training, and that the distilled 80M model achieves 83% top-1 ImageNet accuracy when the VQGAN encoder is swapped for a patch embedding layer.

## Results

On Pascal-5i foreground segmentation, the distilled LLaMA-300M model (with black-background finetuning) achieves mIOU of 18.58, 21.32, 19.90, and 21.08 across four splits, outperforming the non-distilled LLaMA-300M (12.46–16.92) and all VQGAN/BEiT baselines trained on ImageNet-1k. The compact LLaMA-80M model trained with KD reaches 83.04% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet classification. In multi-task training, data augmentation balances long-tail task distributions and yields lower validation loss than re-sampling (e.g., 4.94 vs. 16.11 on pose estimation), while KD narrows the gap between LLaMA-1B and LLaMA-300M from 0.14 to 0.09 in validation loss on segmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LVM-3B (Bai et al., 2023), the proposed distilled LLaMA-300M achieves substantially lower mIOU (18.58–21.08 vs. 48.94–50.82 on Pascal-5i), but the authors attribute this gap to using a Laion-trained VQGAN encoder rather than a custom one retrained on the Figures dataset. Compared to Visual Prompting baselines (Bar et al., 2022), the distilled LLaMA-300M outperforms VQGAN(Figures) (12.56–17.51) and BEiT(Figures) (3.20–5.38) but falls short of MAE-VQGAN(Figures) (27.83–30.44). Against BEiT (Bao et al., 2021) trained on ImageNet-21k, the proposed method achieves a large margin (18.58–21.08 vs. 0.38–0.95 mIOU). Versus VQGAN (Esser et al., 2021) trained on ImageNet-1k, the distilled model improves mIOU by roughly 12 points (e.g., 21.32 vs. 10.55 on Split 1). The paper's primary competitive advantage is not raw mIOU but data and parameter efficiency: the 300M model with KD approaches the 1B teacher's performance (loss 4.59 vs. 4.50 on segmentation) while using only ~30% of the parameters, and the 80M variant still achieves 83% ImageNet top-1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoregressive Large Vision Model (LVM) framework of Bai et al. (2023), which encodes images into discrete VQGAN tokens and trains a causal transformer for multi-task visual understanding via visual prompting. The key departures are twofold: (1) applying simple data augmentation (random crop and flip) to rebalance long-tail task distributions, replacing the more conventional re-sampling strategy, and (2) introducing knowledge distillation from a larger LLaMA teacher to a smaller LLaMA student within the autoregressive vision setting, a combination not previously explored for LVMs. The work positions itself against the prevailing 'scale up parameters and data' paradigm, arguing that 80M–300M parameter models with augmented data can approach the performance of 1B+ models trained on 400B tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that data augmentation and KD can substantially reduce both the data and parameter requirements for autoregressive vision models, this work lowers the practical barrier to building generalist vision models, making them accessible to groups without multi-node GPU clusters. However, significant limitations remain: the model can only handle tasks present in its training data, converting VQGAN-decoded images into quantifiable task outputs (e.g., keypoint coordinates) is still ad hoc, and catastrophic forgetting in sequential (non-shuffled) training remains unresolved. Open questions include whether these efficiency gains transfer to truly open-ended task generalization and whether the VQGAN tokenizer itself can be made more data-efficient.
