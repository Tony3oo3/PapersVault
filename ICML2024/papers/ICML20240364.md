## Problem

Token-based world models (TBWMs) like IRIS must generate next-observation tokens sequentially during imagination, creating a severe computational bottleneck that caps observation sequence length, degrades performance, and makes these methods impractical for complex visual environments. This sequential dependency means generating H observations requires KH sequential model calls, leading to poor GPU utilization and long training times.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240364/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent consumes 64×64×3 RGB frames and discrete actions, with the VQ-VAE tokenizer mapping each frame to a sequence of K=64 discrete tokens (an 8×8 spatial grid) from a vocabulary of 512. The world model outputs next-observation token distributions, scalar reward predictions, and binary termination signals, while the actor-critic controller outputs discrete action distributions. A key structural detail is that K dedicated prediction tokens (u) are used as inputs to generate the full next observation in a single parallel pass, but are never incorporated into the recurrent state that summarizes history.

## Method

REM introduces Parallel Observation Prediction (POP), a novel forward mode for RetNet that generates the entire next-observation token sequence in parallel by feeding K dedicated prediction tokens through the network conditioned on the current recurrent state, reducing sequential imagination calls from KH to 2H. Training requires a modified chunkwise forward (Algorithms 1–2) that first computes intermediate recurrent states S_{[i,j]} in parallel via a two-step procedure (parallel intermediate state computation followed by a lightweight sequential accumulation), then batch-computes Retention outputs for all (S_{[i,j]}, u) tuples with appropriate per-position encoding. The full agent follows a V-M-C structure: a VQ-VAE tokenizer (8×8 latent grid), a 5-layer RetNet world model with 4 retention heads and d=256, and an actor-critic controller operating on latent token embeddings with explicit action inputs, trained entirely in imagination using λ-returns and REINFORCE with entropy regularization. POP enables REM to use a 4× higher latent resolution (64 vs. 16 tokens) than IRIS while achieving 15.4× faster imagination, attaining superhuman performance on 12/26 Atari 100K games in under 12 hours.

## Results

On the Atari 100K benchmark (26 games), REM achieves an IQM human-normalized score of 0.673, the highest among all baselines, and a mean human-normalized score of 1.222 (tied with STORM). REM attains superhuman performance on 12 out of 26 games, more than any other method (IRIS: 10, STORM: 9, DreamerV3: 9). Critically, POP enables a 15.4× speed-up in the imagination phase compared to IRIS, allowing REM to use K=64 observation tokens (vs. K=16 in IRIS) while training in under 12 hours on a single GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary token-based baseline IRIS, REM wins on IQM (0.673 vs. 0.501), mean (1.222 vs. 1.046), and optimality gap (0.482 vs. 0.512), while being marginally lower on median (0.280 vs. 0.289); REM also achieves a 15.4× speed-up in the imagination phase. Against the strongest non-token-based baseline STORM, REM matches the mean (1.222) and wins on IQM (0.673 vs. 0.561) and optimality gap (0.482 vs. 0.472), but trails on median (0.280 vs. 0.425), indicating STORM has more consistent mid-range performance. REM clearly outperforms DreamerV3 (IQM 0.673 vs. 0.487, mean 1.222 vs. 1.124) and TWM (IQM 0.673 vs. 0.459) on all key metrics. Against SimPLe, REM dominates across every metric (IQM 0.673 vs. 0.130). Overall, REM is the only method to exceed 12 superhuman games and achieves the best IQM and optimality gap among all six baselines evaluated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the token-based world model (TBWM) paradigm introduced by IRIS (Micheli et al., 2023), which treated agent trajectories as language-like token sequences processed by a Transformer. The key departure is replacing the Transformer with a Retentive Network (RetNet, Sun et al., 2023) and introducing the Parallel Observation Prediction (POP) mechanism, which generates entire next-observation token sequences in a single forward pass rather than sequentially token-by-token. The V-M-C architecture and actor-critic training objectives follow the DreamerV2 lineage (Hafner et al., 2021), while the VQ-VAE tokenizer design is inherited from IRIS. POP requires a novel chunkwise forward mode for RetNet that supports batched prediction from multiple recurrent states simultaneously, a capability absent from the original RetNet formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

POP resolves the fundamental sequential-generation bottleneck that has limited TBWMs to short observation token sequences (K=16 in IRIS), enabling higher-resolution tokenizers (K=64) and making these methods practical for more complex visual environments. The 15.4× imagination speedup and sub-12-hour training on Atari 100K significantly lower the computational barrier for token-based world models. Open questions include whether the recurrent-state-based history summarization can be extended to full agent history, whether pretrained visual encoders can replace the learned tokenizer in data-rich settings, and whether POP generalizes to video generation tasks. The paper also provides the first empirical evidence of RetNet's viability in an RL setting, though evaluation is limited to Atari 100K.
