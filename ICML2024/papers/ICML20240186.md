## Problem

Existing neural PDE solvers—PINNs and Fourier-based neural operators—suffer from limited generalisability across geometries and resolutions, and lack a principled mechanism to exploit local spatial structure on irregular or non-uniform meshes. While graph neural networks and transformers have each been applied to PDEs separately, graph transformers had not been explored as a PDE solution operator, leaving open whether their attention-based message passing could capture the integral-kernel structure of neural operators while remaining discretisation-invariant and data-efficient.

## Input / Output

HAMLET consumes an L-point discretisation of the PDE domain as a graph whose node features are the concatenation of parameter values (e.g., diffusion coefficient, initial/boundary conditions) and spatial coordinates, with edges defined by a circular truncation radius (typically 0.08–0.1 for uniform grids, 0.001 for the 5,233-node Airfoil mesh). Query positions at arbitrary resolution (e.g., 32×32, 64×64, or 128×128) are embedded via Gaussian Fourier Projection and fused with the input graph features. For time-dependent problems the output is a full temporal sequence (91 or 22 steps) generated autoregressively by a recurrent MLP, while steady-state problems yield a single solution field.

## Method

HAMLET comprises three modules: (i) an input encoder that embeds graph node features through a fully-connected layer and processes them with stacked graph transformer blocks using multi-head self-attention with Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE); (ii) a query encoder that maps query positions via Gaussian Fourier Projection + MLP and fuses them with input features through a CrossFormer employing Galerkin-type cross-attention over learnable basis functions; and (iii) a decoder that either projects directly to the solution (steady-state) or runs a recurrent MLP propagator with residual connections to march through time (time-dependent). The key theoretical result (Proposition 3.1) shows the graph transformer residual block is a Monte-Carlo discretisation of the neural operator integral kernel, formally placing HAMLET within the neural operator class. Graph construction uses a single-radius circular neighbourhood rather than fixed-k KNN, which ablations show yields lower nRMSE and better captures spatial context. The architecture is trained end-to-end with MSE or relative L2 loss under a OneCycleLR schedule, and its graph-based inductive bias provides superior data efficiency compared to non-graph transformer baselines.

## Results

On the PDEBench datasets, HAMLET achieves the lowest nRMSE on 6 of 7 Darcy Flow/Shallow Water/Diffusion Reaction settings, with headline values of 1.40E-02 (Darcy Flow β=1.0), 2.04E-03 (Shallow Water), and 9.02E-02 (Diffusion Reaction). On the non-uniform Airfoil grid, HAMLET achieves a relative L2 norm of 3.03E-02, outperforming OFormer (3.49E-02), GNOT (4.31E-02), and EAGLE (1.19E-01). HAMLET also demonstrates superior data efficiency, maintaining lower nRMSE than OFormer across all reduced training set sizes (e.g., 2.78E-02 vs 3.32E-02 at 1K Darcy Flow samples; 4.75E-03 vs 2.91E-02 at 100 Shallow Water samples).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAMLET outperforms OFormer (the closest non-graph transformer baseline) on every Darcy Flow setting (e.g., 1.40E-02 vs 2.05E-02 at β=1.0), Shallow Water (2.04E-03 vs 2.90E-03), and Diffusion Reaction (9.02E-02 vs 3.28E+00), as well as on Airfoil (3.03E-02 vs 3.49E-02 relative L2). HAMLET also beats FNO, DeepONet, GeoFNO, and U-Net across all shared settings, with particularly large margins on Diffusion Reaction where OFormer and GeoFNO fail catastrophically (nRMSE > 3). The sole exception is Darcy Flow at β=0.01, where MAgNet (7.71E-02) outperforms HAMLET (3.11E-01), though HAMLET recovers the lead at β=0.1 and beyond. GNOT is competitive on Shallow Water (4.16E-03) but degrades severely on Diffusion Reaction (8.22E-01) and Airfoil (4.31E-02). EAGLE, the only other mesh-transformer baseline, is evaluated only on Airfoil and trails HAMLET by nearly 4× in relative L2 (1.19E-01 vs 3.03E-02). In data-scarce regimes (Table 3), HAMLET consistently maintains lower nRMSE than OFormer, with the gap widening as training data shrinks (e.g., 4.75E-03 vs 2.91E-02 on Shallow Water with only 100 samples).

## Relation to Prior Work

HAMLET positions itself as the first graph transformer architecture applied to PDE solution, combining the graph-based multi-head self-attention of Dwivedi & Bresson (2020) with the CrossFormer cross-attention mechanism from Li et al. (2023b) for query-position fusion. It departs from the dominant Fourier-based neural operator line (FNO, GeoFNO) and the non-graph transformer OFormer by replacing global spectral mixing with local graph message passing, while retaining the neural operator framework's discretisation-invariance guarantee. The recurrent MLP propagator for time-dependent decoding follows the design philosophy of OFormer but is embedded within a graph-structured latent space rather than a uniform grid, and the circular-radius graph construction replaces the KNN or mesh-based connectivity used by GNN solvers like MAgNet and GNOT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in HAMLET's demonstrated data efficiency (degrading less gracefully than OFormer when training data drops from 9K to 1K) and its ability to handle irregular, non-uniform meshes (Airfoil) without geometric reparameterisation, which is critical for real-world CFD and engineering applications. The theoretical proposition formally embedding the graph transformer residual block as a Monte-Carlo discretisation of a neural operator integral kernel provides a clean unification that may guide future architecture design. Key open questions include scalability to 3D PDEs (where graph construction cost grows sharply), whether the circular-radius heuristic generalises beyond the tested 2D domains, and whether Lie-symmetry augmentation (flagged as future work) can further close the gap on the hardest Darcy Flow setting (β=0.01) where MAgNet still outperforms HAMLET.
