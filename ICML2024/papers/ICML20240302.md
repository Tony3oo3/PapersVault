## Problem

Graph Information Bottleneck (GIB)-based GNN explainers rely on the assumption that the pre-trained GNN can accurately predict labels on explanation subgraphs. However, these subgraphs are typically far smaller and structurally simpler than the original training graphs, creating a severe Out-Of-Distribution (OOD) shift that makes the model's predictions on them unreliable and undermines the validity of the GIB objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240302/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph G (adjacency matrix and node feature matrix) and a pre-trained GNN model f(·) treated as a black box, and produces two outputs: an explanation subgraph G' with continuous edge importance scores (via Gumbel-Softmax relaxation of Bernoulli edge masks) and an in-distribution proxy graph G̃ whose adjacency matrix is the element-wise sum of a reconstructed explanation subgraph and a perturbed non-explanatory subgraph. The proxy graph, not the raw explanation subgraph, is what is fed to the GNN for label prediction, ensuring the prediction is made within the model's training distribution.

## Method

ProxyExplainer introduces a bilevel optimization framework where the outer loop optimizes the explanation subgraph via a GIB objective (KL divergence under an Erdős-Rényi edge model) and the inner loop trains a proxy graph generator to match the training data distribution. The proxy graph generator comprises two auto-encoders: a standard GAE that reconstructs the explanation subgraph G' and a Variational GAE that samples perturbations of the non-explanatory residual subgraph G−G', with the proxy graph formed by summing their adjacency matrices. The inner loss combines a weighted cross-entropy distribution loss (pulling the proxy graph toward the original graph's structure) and a KL regularization term on the VGAE latent space. Training uses an alternate schedule where the proxy generator is updated M times per explainer update, and the key theoretical contribution is reformulating P(Y|G') as an expectation over in-distribution proxy graphs, making the GNN's prediction reliable by construction.

## Results

ProxyExplainer achieves the highest AUC-ROC on all six datasets, with scores of 0.977 (MUTAG), 0.845 (Benzene), 0.934 (Alkane-Carbonyl), 0.758 (Fluoride-Carbonyl), 0.935 (BA-2motifs), and 0.960 (BA-3motifs). The paper reports an average improvement of 10.6% over the leading baselines on real-world datasets and 7.5% on synthetic datasets. On the AP metric (Table 2), ProxyExplainer also leads on MUTAG (0.756) and BA-2motifs (0.839), and on robust fidelity metrics (Table 3) it outperforms all baselines on both Fid_{α1,+} and Fid_{α2,-}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProxyExplainer outperforms all five baselines on every dataset. The strongest baseline is MixupExplainer, which leads on synthetic datasets (BA-2motifs: 0.906, BA-3motifs: 0.859) but drops sharply on real-world Benzene (0.611) due to its independence assumption between explanatory and non-explanatory subgraphs. PGExplainer is competitive on MUTAG (0.832) and Benzene (0.793) but is surpassed by ProxyExplainer by 14.5 and 5.2 points respectively. GNNExplainer and GradCAM are consistently weak, with GNNExplainer scoring as low as 0.485 on Benzene and 0.511 on BA-3motifs. ReFine shows the most inconsistency, peaking at 0.768 on Alkane-Carbonyl but falling to 0.571 on Fluoride-Carbonyl. The largest absolute gains for ProxyExplainer are on MUTAG (+11.4 over MixupExplainer) and BA-3motifs (+10.1), while the smallest is on Fluoride-Carbonyl (+5.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Graph Information Bottleneck (GIB) framework that underpins GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, and ReFine by identifying and addressing a fundamental flaw: explanation subgraphs are out-of-distribution relative to the training graphs on which the GNN was trained, undermining the reliability of P(Y|G') estimation. It departs from prior OOD-mitigation attempts—CGE's white-box student network and MixupExplainer's independence assumption between explanatory and non-explanatory subgraphs—by introducing a parametric proxy graph generator (GAE + VGAE) that operates under black-box access and does not require subgraph independence. The bilevel optimization formulation, where the inner loop enforces distributional alignment via KL divergence and the outer loop optimizes the explanation, is a novel architectural contribution to the GIB lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OOD problem in GNN explanations is a foundational reliability issue: if the explainer's subgraphs fall outside the model's training distribution, the entire GIB objective is optimizing a miscalibrated signal, potentially producing explanations that look plausible but are not actually predictive. The proxy graph approach offers a more general and assumption-light solution than prior work, making it applicable to black-box GNNs in real-world settings where independence constraints do not hold. However, the evaluation is limited to small molecular graphs and synthetic motif datasets; scalability to large-scale graphs (e.g., social networks, protein structures) and to model-level or counterfactual explanations remains open. The Erdős-Rényi edge-independence assumption and the bilevel training schedule also introduce practical complexity that may limit adoption.
