## Problem

Graph transformers rely on positional encodings to capture graph structure, but there is no systematic framework for comparing the distinguishing power of different encodings—especially across the two major types, absolute (APEs) and relative (RPEs). It is unclear whether APEs and RPEs are theoretically equivalent, whether converting one to the other is worthwhile, and how specific encodings (e.g., shortest-path distance vs. resistance distance vs. SPE) rank in expressive power.

## Input / Output

The framework takes unweighted graphs (directed or undirected), optionally with node features X ∈ R^{|V|×d}, and a positional encoding (an APE φ: V→R^l or an RPE ψ: V×V→R^k) as input. The output is a distinguishing-power comparison: which pairs of non-isomorphic (or non-feature-isomorphic) graphs can be separated by the corresponding graph transformer. A key intermediate object is the RPE-augWL test, a new WL variant that assigns colors using RPE values in the update step and serves as a computable proxy for RPE-GT distinguishing power.

## Method

The paper establishes that APE-GTs and RPE-GTs are equivalent in distinguishing power for unfeatured graphs by constructing explicit maps between the two: APEs are converted to RPEs via a DeepSet (ψ^f(u,v) = f(φ(u)) + f(φ(v))) and RPEs are converted to APEs via 2-equivariant graph networks (2-EGNs). A new RPE-augWL test is introduced and proven equivalent to RPE-2-WL for pseudo-symmetric RPEs, and equivalent to RPE-GT distinguishing power for diagonally-aware RPEs, thereby connecting the WL hierarchy to transformer expressivity. For featured graphs, the RPE-to-APE direction is shown to be strictly weaker (Example 1), giving RPEs an advantage. The framework is then applied to compare specific encodings: SPE-APE-GTs are proven at least as strong as RD-RPE-GTs (via equivalence of RD-WL and L†-WL), stacked matrix powers are shown to dominate spectral kernels, and combinatorially-aware RPEs are shown to yield GTs at least as strong as MPNNs.

## Results

This is primarily a theoretical paper establishing equivalence and hierarchy results among positional encodings for graph transformers. The headline theoretical results are: (1) APE-GTs and RPE-GTs are equivalent in distinguishing power for unfeatured graphs (Theorem 3.8, 3.10); (2) RPEs are strictly stronger than APEs derived from them for featured graphs (Example 1); (3) SPE-APE-GT is at least as strong as RD-RPE-GT for unfeatured graphs (Theorem 4.4); and (4) combinatorially-aware RPE-GTs are at least as strong as MPNNs (Corollary 4.17). Empirically, on the BREC dataset, SPD-RPE-GT was able to distinguish some graph pairs that RD-RPE-GT could not, suggesting RD-WL is not strictly stronger than SPD-WL in all respects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four families of positional encodings via their induced RPE-augWL tests and transformer distinguishing power. Against Zhang et al. (2023, RD-RPE-GT), the paper proves SPE-APE-GT (Huang et al., 2024) is at least as strong for unfeatured graphs (Theorem 4.4), but empirically observes that RD-RPE-GT can outperform SPE-APE-GT on graph regression tasks with node features (Appendix C.3), consistent with the theoretical strictness result of Example 1. Against Ying et al. (2021, SPD-RPE-GT), the paper proves RD-WL can distinguish cut edges (Theorem B.18), answering an open question, but BREC experiments suggest SPD may retain some advantage over RD. Against Xu et al. (2018, MPNNs/WL), the paper establishes that any combinatorially-aware RPE (e.g., SPD, adjacency matrix) yields RPE-GTs at least as strong as MPNNs (Corollary 4.17), and that A, Â, Ã, L, L̂, L̃ all induce WL-equivalent tests (Proposition 4.10). Against Huang et al. (2024, SPE), the paper shows SPE-APE-GT dominates RD-RPE-GT for unfeatured graphs but may lose when node features are present, providing guidance that converting RPEs to APEs via 2-EGNs is not always advisable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the WL-test framework for characterizing GNN expressive power (Weisfeiler & Leman, 1968; Xu et al., 2018) and the RPE-GT architecture of Zhang et al. (2023), which introduced the GD-WL test and resistance-distance-based attention. It also draws on equivariant graph networks (Maron et al., 2019b) as the mechanism for converting RPEs to APEs, a technique already used in BasisNet (Lim et al., 2022) and SPE (Huang et al., 2024). The key departure is establishing a unified theoretical framework that compares APEs and RPEs *across* categories rather than within them, introducing the RPE-augWL test as a bridge between the two paradigms and showing that the previously assumed interchangeability of APEs and RPEs breaks down for featured graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that RPEs are strictly stronger than their APE counterparts for featured graphs has direct practical implications: the common practice of converting RPEs into APEs (as in BasisNet and SPE) provably loses distinguishing power, suggesting RPE-GTs should be preferred when node features are involved. Open questions include the unresolved comparison between SPD and RD as RPEs, whether the DeepSet or 2-EGN is easier to learn in practice, and how to move from coarse distinguishing-power results to fine-grained expressivity measures (approximation rates, WL-inspired distances). The RPE-augWL test itself is a useful new tool for the broader GNN theory community.
