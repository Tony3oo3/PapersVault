## Problem

Masked Video Autoencoders (MVA) waste excessive computation and memory by reconstructing uninformative tokens and frames through random masking, requiring massive GPU clusters (e.g., 128 A100s for ST-MAE). The core issue is that video tokens are not equally informative—most contain temporally redundant information—yet existing methods treat all tokens and frames uniformly, making efficient video representation learning impractical on single-node hardware.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240839/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes short video clips of 2τ successive frames (e.g., 16 frames at 224×224) tokenized via a single 3D convolution into spatiotemporal embedding vectors. During pre-training, the decoder reconstructs RGB pixels only for the selected informative token regions; during fine-tuning, the encoder produces representations for downstream classification (action labels on UCF101/HMDB51/K400/SSv2, or object state change labels on Ego4D OSCC). Uniquely, the model processes only a fraction (ρ_pre) of tokens per frame and adaptively selects which frames to include, rather than consuming all frames at uniform intervals.

## Method

EVEREST introduces two parameter-free, online selection mechanisms into the MVA pipeline. First, redundancy-robust (ReRo) token selection computes the L2 distance between each token embedding and its same-position counterpart in the temporally adjacent frame pair, then retains only the top ρ_pre tokens by distance (indicating rich motion/change) before randomly subsampling ρ_post of them for the encoder; the decoder reconstructs pixels for all retained tokens. Second, information-intensive frame selection draws τ frames from a larger candidate pool with probability proportional to each frame's ReRo token count, biasing training toward information-dense frames without any pre-computation of optical flow or HOG. Both mechanisms operate entirely online during training and fine-tuning, reducing pre-training GFLOPs by 26–45% and memory by up to 4× (e.g., 164 GB vs. 634 GB for ViT-L at batch 256) while matching or exceeding VideoMAE accuracy across benchmarks.

## Results

On K400, EVEREST achieves 75.9%, 79.2%, and 82.1% Top-1 accuracy with ViT-S, ViT-B, and ViT-L respectively, matching or exceeding VideoMAE (73.5%, 78.4%, 82.0%) while reducing pre-training GFLOPs by 26–46% and fine-tuning GFLOPs by 45–49%. On UCF101 and HMDB51, EVEREST (ViT-B) reaches 93.4% and 68.1% Top-1, outperforming VideoMAE by +2.1% and +3.2% respectively. On Ego4D OSCC, EVEREST achieves 76.2% accuracy using only visual modality, surpassing the V+T Egocentric VLP baseline (73.9%) by 2.3 points. Memory usage drops from 634.1 GB (VideoMAE ViT-L, batch 256) to 164.1 GB (EVEREST ViT-L), a ~4× reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VideoMAE (the primary baseline), EVEREST wins on every accuracy metric: +2.4% on K400 ViT-S, +0.8% on K400 ViT-B, +0.1% on K400 ViT-L, +2.1% on UCF101, +3.2% on HMDB51, and +0.3% on SSv2, while cutting pre-training GFLOPs by 27–46% and memory by up to 81%. Against K-centered patch sampling (Park et al., 2022), EVEREST ViT-B (79.2%) surpasses the best K-centered variant TSformer (78.0%) by 1.2% on K400 with far lower GFLOPs (21.5 vs. 67.4). On Ego4D OSCC, EVEREST (76.2%, visual-only) outperforms Egocentric VLP (73.9%, visual+text) by 2.3 points and SViT (69.8%) by 6.4 points. Against MME and MVD, EVEREST achieves comparable or slightly lower fine-tuning accuracy (82.0% vs. 81.8% for MME; 82.8% vs. 83.4% for MVD on K400) but uses only 44% and 55% of their memory respectively, and requires no pre-computed optical flow. Compared to running-cell masking (VideoMAE-V2), EVEREST gains +2.4% on UCF101 and +6.7% on HMDB51, demonstrating that redundancy-robust selection captures semantic importance better than spatially structured random masking.

## Relation to Prior Work

EVEREST builds directly on the VideoMAE masked video autoencoder framework (same encoder-decoder architecture, 3D convolutional tokenization, and training protocol) but replaces its random masking strategy with a redundancy-robust token selection mechanism that computes L2 distances between temporally adjacent token embeddings to identify informative regions. This departs from both random masking (VideoMAE, ST-MAE) and motion-guided masking methods (MME, MGM, MGMAE) that require pre-computed optical flows or HOG features, offering a parameter-free, online alternative. The information-intensive frame selection further extends the standard uniform frame sampling used across VRL methods by probabilistically sampling frames proportional to their ReRo token occupancy, addressing temporal redundancy in uncurated videos like Ego4D.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: EVEREST enables single-node (8×A100) training of ViT-L backbones where VideoMAE suffers out-of-memory, and reduces pre-training time by ~55% while achieving comparable or better accuracy. This significantly lowers the barrier to video representation research, which has been gated behind multi-node GPU clusters. Open questions include whether the L2 distance metric generalizes to very long or highly dynamic videos, whether the approach extends to other MVA variants beyond the VideoMAE architecture, and whether the frame selection strategy remains effective when the redundancy pattern is non-stationary over time. The method's simplicity (no additional parameters, no pre-processing) makes it easy to adopt, but its gains are bounded by the assumption that temporal embedding distance is a reliable proxy for information density.
