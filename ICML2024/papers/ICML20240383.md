## Problem

Dataset condensation methods have been evaluated almost exclusively on standard unbiased benchmarks, leaving the impact of dataset bias on the distillation process entirely unexplored. This paper reveals that color and background biases are severely amplified during condensation (e.g., a 4% drop in full-data training becomes a 50%+ drop after distillation), while corruption bias is suppressed. Furthermore, state-of-the-art de-biasing training methods applied post-hoc to the synthetic set are insufficient to recover performance, motivating bias mitigation directly within the condensation objective.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a biased RGB training dataset (e.g., CMNIST, BG FMNIST, Corrupted CIFAR-10, BFFHQ) with a known bias-conflict ratio (0.5%–5%) and a pre-trained supervised contrastive ResNet18 model (128-dim projection head) that provides per-sample embeddings for density estimation. It outputs a condensed synthetic dataset (IPC 1/10/50) whose images better represent both bias-aligned and bias-conflicting samples, along with per-sample reweighting scores computed via KDE over the contrastive embedding space. Unlike standard DM/DSA which treats all real samples equally, the reweighted objective down-weights samples in dense bias-aligned clusters and up-weights sparse bias-conflicting outliers.

## Method

The core mechanism is a sample reweighting scheme built on Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) applied to supervised contrastive embeddings. A ResNet18 with a 128-dim MLP projection head is pre-trained with Generalized Supervised Contrastive (GSC) loss to produce embeddings where bias-aligned samples form tight clusters and bias-conflicting samples appear as outliers. For each sample, the KDE density is computed using a Gaussian kernel (variance σ²=0.1) over pairwise distances in this embedding space, and the normalized inverse of this density (via softmax with temperature 0.1) yields the reweighting vector W(T). This vector replaces the uniform mean in DM's distribution-matching objective (Eq. 1→2) or the uniform gradient average in DSA's gradient-matching objective (Eq. 4), so that the synthetic set is pulled toward intrinsic features rather than dominated by the spurious bias feature. The approach is plug-and-play: it adds ~18% runtime overhead for DSA and ~5% for the contrastive model training, and extends naturally to DREAM and IDM without architectural changes.

## Results

On CMNIST with 5% bias-conflicting samples and IPC 50, DM+Ours achieves 91.5% test accuracy versus 23.8% for vanilla DM (a 67.7% absolute gain), while DSA+Ours reaches 94.0% versus 68.5% for vanilla DSA. On BG FMNIST under the same settings, DM+Ours improves from 44.6% to 65.0% and DSA+Ours from 59.3% to 71.2%. On Corrupted CIFAR-10, where bias is naturally suppressed by distillation, gains are modest (e.g., DM+Ours: 38.7%→40.2% at IPC 50, 5% bias). On the real-world BFFHQ dataset (0.5% bias-conflict), the method still yields up to 6.2% improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's method (KDE-based reweighting) dramatically outperforms all baselines on bias-amplifying datasets. On CMNIST (5% bias, IPC 50), DM+Ours (91.5%) surpasses SelecMix applied post-hoc (53.7%), DFA (31.2%), MTT (33.9%), DREAM (50.4%), and IDM (26.5%) by wide margins. On BG FMNIST (5% bias, IPC 50), DSA+Ours (71.2%) beats MTT (62.3%), DREAM (53.2%), and IDM (42.0%), while DM+Ours (65.0%) exceeds all other DM-based variants. DFA actually degrades severely on BG FMNIST (17.6% at IPC 50 vs. 44.6% for vanilla DM), and MTT, while competitive on some settings, still suffers from residual bias. On Corrupted CIFAR-10, where distillation naturally suppresses corruption bias, all methods including the proposed one show only marginal differences (within ~2%), confirming that reweighting is most impactful when bias is amplified.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of dataset condensation (DM, DSA, MTT) and bias mitigation (SelectMix, DFA, LfF), but carves out a new problem: how bias propagates through the condensation process itself. It builds directly on DM's distribution-matching objective and DSA's gradient-matching objective, modifying them with a KDE-based reweighting scheme, and borrows the supervised contrastive embedding architecture from SelectMix to obtain bias-sensitive feature representations. The key departure from prior debiasing work is that reweighting is applied *during* the condensation optimization rather than during downstream model training, since the condensation process uses randomly initialized or partially trained surrogate models where standard debiasing losses cannot be applied.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that dataset condensation amplifies color and background biases (causing >50% performance drops) while suppressing corruption bias is a previously unknown failure mode that could undermine the reliability of condensed datasets in safety-critical applications. The practical KDE reweighting fix is lightweight (~18% overhead) and plugs into existing DM/DSA pipelines, making it immediately adoptable. Key limitations include inapplicability to trajectory-based methods like MTT, reliance on a pre-trained contrastive model for bias-sensitive embeddings, and evaluation limited to relatively small-scale datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, Fashion-MNIST) rather than ImageNet-scale condensation. Open questions include whether the amplification effect persists at larger scales and how to extend the reweighting to trajectory-matching paradigms.
