## Problem

Second-order optimizers like KFAC are memory-inefficient because their Kronecker factors are dense, and numerically unstable in low-precision (BFP-16) training because they require matrix inversion or decomposition at every preconditioner update. The prior inverse-free method INGD resolved the numerical instability but remained memory-inefficient, lacked a formal connection to KFAC, and had not been validated on transformer architectures or in half precision.

## Input / Output

The optimizer consumes the standard backpropagated gradient ∇_μℓ and the per-layer Kronecker curvature factors U (input activations) and G (output gradients) computed during the forward/backward pass. It produces updated weight matrices μ at each step, with the preconditioner (structured Kronecker factors K and C) refreshed every T iterations. A key practical detail is that all operations reduce to matrix multiplications and elementwise operations, enabling direct BFP-16 execution without casting to FP-32, and the structured factors (diagonal, block-diagonal, hierarchical, Toeplitz, rank-k triangular) store only O(kd) or O(d) parameters per factor instead of O(d²).

## Method

SINGD reparameterizes the precision matrix S as A^{-T}A^{-1} with A = K⊗C, then performs natural gradient updates in the matrix logarithm (tangent) space of K and C, where the Fisher block is locally orthonormalized to the identity. This transforms the KFAC matrix inversion into a simple subtraction followed by a truncated matrix exponential (K ← K(I − β₁/2 · m_K)), making the update purely matrix-multiplication-based and numerically stable in BFP-16. The paper proves (Theorem 1) that a specific INGD configuration—zero Riemannian momentum and non-adaptive damping—recovers KFAC to first order, yielding IKFAC. To reduce memory, SINGD imposes sparse structures on K and C by projecting the dense logarithm-space momentum onto a Lie-algebraic subspace (e.g., extracting the diagonal, lower-triangular entries with off-diagonal doubling, or Toeplitz averages) before the exponential map, preserving sparsity without requiring the curvature approximation U⊗G itself to be structured. The unified projection-based update rule supports multiple structures (diagonal, block-diagonal, hierarchical, rank-k triangular, Toeplitz) with a single algorithmic template, reducing INGD's 30% memory overhead to ~2% relative to SGD.

## Results

On VGG/CIFAR-100 (Table 1), SINGD-Diag in BFP-16 uses only 2.67 GiB peak memory (1.02× SGD) and 23.8 min training time (1.29× SGD), compared to INGD's 3.39 GiB (1.29×) and 34.1 min (1.84×), and KFAC's 4.00 GiB (1.52×, FP-32) and 83.2 min (4.49×). On GC-ViT/ImageWoof-10 (Table 6), SINGD-Diag achieves 15.8 GiB (1.02× SGD) and 200 min (1.06× SGD), while KFAC requires 359 min (1.89×). In test error, SINGD (including IKFAC and INGD as special cases) outperforms AdamW on most transformer, CNN, and GNN models in BFP-16, and the hierarchical structure often matches dense INGD performance at substantially lower memory cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SGD, SINGD-Diag adds only 2% memory and 29% time overhead in BFP-16, making it nearly as cheap as first-order methods while delivering second-order curvature information. Against AdamW, SINGD matches or beats its memory footprint (1.02× vs 1.02× SGD) and consistently achieves lower test error across transformer (Compact-ViT, Swin-ViT, GC-ViT, HDVT), CNN (VGG, ConvMixer, Rep-ViT), and GNN (Cora) models in BFP-16. Against KFAC, SINGD/IKFAC is numerically stable in BFP-16 whereas KFAC diverges or produces NaNs; KFAC also requires FP-32 casting and incurs 4.49× training time on VGG versus 1.29× for SINGD-Diag. Against INGD (the dense-structure special case of SINGD), the structured variants (diagonal, block-diagonal, hierarchical) reduce memory overhead from 29% to 2% relative to SGD while preserving or improving test error, with the hierarchical structure often matching dense INGD performance at a fraction of the cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected lines of second-order optimization: the Kronecker-factored approximate curvature (KFAC) lineage (Martens & Grosse, 2015) and the inverse-free natural gradient descent (INGD) lineage (Lin et al., 2023), which itself builds on the matrix-logarithm-space reparameterization of Lin et al. (2021). The key conceptual move is showing that KFAC is a special case of INGD (zero Riemannian momentum, non-adaptive damping/curvature), thereby making KFAC inverse-free and numerically stable in low precision. The paper then departs from both predecessors by imposing Lie-algebraic structures (diagonal, block-diagonal, hierarchical, Toeplitz, low-rank) on the Kronecker factors via subspace projection in the logarithm space, yielding SINGD as a unified, structure-preserving update rule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work makes second-order optimization practically viable for modern mixed-precision (BFP-16) training of transformer and CNN architectures, a setting where KFAC has been unusable due to numerical instability of matrix inversions. The finding that a simple diagonal structure reduces memory overhead from 30% (INGD) to 2% (SINGD-Diag) while matching or exceeding AdamW's test error is practically significant for practitioners seeking curvature-aware training without the memory cost. Open questions include whether these gains persist at billion-parameter scale, whether the structure choice (diagonal vs. hierarchical vs. dense) generalizes across architectures, and how SINGD interacts with other modern training techniques like gradient checkpointing or distributed training.
