## Problem

Kernel-based linear attention reduces ViT attention from O(N²D) to O(NDd), but making it truly mobile-friendly requires shrinking the per-head dimension d, which causes individual heads to learn trivial subspaces that overshadow valuable ones. This creates an efficiency-capability dilemma: small d is essential for mobile latency, yet it degrades model expressiveness. The paper asks how to support extremely small head dimensions without losing the representational capacity that multi-head attention provides.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242348/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is a drop-in replacement for the standard attention module in existing ViTs (DeiT, PVT-v2, EfficientFormerV2), consuming the same RGB image inputs (224×224 for classification, 512×512 for downstream tasks) and producing the same outputs: class labels, bounding boxes with instance masks, or pixel-wise segmentation masks. Inference is measured at batch size 1 on mobile hardware (iPhone 12 via CoreML, Pixel 6 via XNNPACK) and GPU (A100 via TensorRT), emphasizing single-image latency rather than throughput.

## Method

Mobile-Attention uses a sigmoid-cosine kernel Φ(x)=σ(x/‖x‖₂) with an extremely small per-head dimension d=4, yielding O(ND) complexity. Its key innovation is a head-competition mechanism built on information flow: incoming flow I^h = Φ(Q^h)ΣⱼΦ(K^j)ᵀ and outgoing flow O^h = Φ(K^h)ΣᵢΦ(Q^i)ᵀ are computed across all M heads, then normalized to produce competed flows (I̅^h and Ō^h) that quantify each head's relative importance. The competed outgoing flow Ō is softmax-normalized and element-wise multiplied with the value vectors (Heads Competing), while the competed incoming flow I̅ is passed through a sigmoid to gate each head's final output (Heads Gating). This competition suppresses trivial heads and amplifies informative ones, recovering the 3.6-point accuracy gap that vanilla small-d linear attention suffers, while keeping the overall complexity at O(2ND) for head competition plus O(NDd) for token-level linear attention.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k classification, Mobile-Attention consistently improves both accuracy and mobile latency across three ViT families: DeiT-S-MobiAtt reaches 80.0% top-1 at 7.2 ms CoreML (vs. 79.8% / 9.0 ms for DeiT-S), PVT-v2-b2-MobiAtt achieves 82.6% at 65.6 ms (vs. 82.1% / 101.0 ms), and EfficientFormerV2-L-MobiAtt attains 83.7% at 2.2 ms (vs. 83.3% / 2.7 ms). On COCO detection, EfficientFormerV2-L-MobiAtt scores 44.9 AP^box and 40.8 AP^mask at 7.7 ms CoreML, surpassing ResNet101 (40.4 / 36.4) by 4.5 and 4.4 points respectively. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, it reaches 46.0 mIoU, outperforming PoolFormer-S24 (40.3) by 5.7 points while being 3× faster on CoreML.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original ViT backbones (DeiT, PVT-v2, EfficientFormerV2), Mobile-Attention wins on both accuracy and CoreML latency in every configuration: e.g., DeiT-B-MobiAtt gains +0.8 pp top-1 while cutting latency from 18.2 to 13.3 ms (−27%), and PVT-v2-b2-MobiAtt gains +0.5 pp while reducing latency by 35%. Among linear-attention methods (Table 2), DeiT-S-MobiAtt (80.0%, 7.2 ms, O(ND)) outperforms Castling-DeiT-S (79.8%, 9.4 ms, O(ND²)) and Hydra-DeiT-S (73.5%, 7.0 ms, O(ND)), confirming that the head-competition mechanism is critical—removing it drops accuracy to 76.4%. For downstream tasks, EfficientFormerV2-L-MobiAtt beats ResNet101 by +4.5 AP^box / +4.4 AP^mask and PoolFormer-S24 by +4.8 AP^box / +3.8 AP^mask on COCO, while being 3× faster on CoreML than PoolFormer. In semantic segmentation on ADE20K, it surpasses the best linear-attention baseline EVA by +2.1 mIoU (43.1 vs. 41.0) at lower latency (4.7 vs. 6.8 ms). Against CNN baselines, EfficientFormerV2-L-MobiAtt (83.7%) exceeds ConvNeXt-T (82.1%) by 1.6 pp while being 38× faster on CoreML (2.2 ms vs. 83.7 ms).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends kernel-based linear attention (building on Flowformer's information-flow formulation and Performers' kernel decomposition) by repurposing the incoming/outgoing flow concept from the sequence dimension to the head dimension. While prior linear-attention work (Hydra, Castling, EVA) focused on reducing sequence-length complexity, Mobile-Attention targets the per-head dimension d, pushing it to a small constant (d=4) to achieve near-O(ND) complexity. The key departure is the head-competition mechanism, which contrasts with both standard multi-head attention (where heads operate independently) and naive inter-head attention (which reintroduces quadratic cost in the number of heads).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck for deploying ViTs on mobile devices: even linear attention with O(NDd) complexity becomes expensive when d is large in later transformer stages. By showing that d=4 with head-competition can match or exceed standard attention accuracy while cutting CoreML latency by 25–35%, the work provides a concrete, drop-in replacement for the attention module in existing ViT architectures. Open questions include whether the fixed d=4 choice generalizes across all architectures and input resolutions, whether the mechanism extends to autoregressive or video settings, and whether the competition signal remains informative at very large head counts.
