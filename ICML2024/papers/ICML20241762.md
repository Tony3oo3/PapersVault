## Problem

Under modest privacy budgets (ε=8), DP-trained models have historically learned representations no better than hand-crafted features, as shown by Tramer & Boneh (2020). The core question is how to achieve high-utility DP representation learning from scratch, with prior attempts limited by insufficient training data and objectives that waste the privacy budget on irrelevant image details.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images (processed at 224×224 resolution) and produces two outputs: an image embedding from the vision encoder (used for downstream linear probing, zero-shot classification, and compositional understanding) and an autoregressive text caption generated by the decoder. Training uses 233M deduplicated, NSFW-filtered, face-blurred image-caption pairs from LAION-2B, with captions truncated to 40 tokens. The encoder and decoder are both transformers connected via cross-attention, with the decoder using causal self-attention over the text tokens.

## Method

The key insight is that image captioning is an ideal objective for DP-SGD because it provides a per-sample additive loss (unlike contrastive learning) and text captions offer more condensed supervision than image-only reconstruction, enabling more efficient information extraction under the privacy constraint. The model is a transformer encoder-decoder (up to 407M parameters) trained with DP-SGD on Dedup-LAION-233M, initialized via MAE pre-training on synthetic Shaders21K images to avoid spending privacy budget on low-level features. A critical engineering contribution is scaling the batch size to 1.3M (achieving effective noise σ/B = 5.6×10⁻⁷, ~10× smaller than ViP), which is possible because vision-language pre-training tolerates extreme batch sizes without the performance degradation seen in supervised classification. Computational efficiency is improved ~5× over naive functorch-based per-sample gradients using ghost norm combined with automatic mixed precision, reducing training from 61 days to 13 days on 128 V100 GPUs.

## Results

Under a privacy budget of ε=8 on Dedup-LAION-233M, DP-Cap (Base) achieves 63.4% linear probing accuracy on ImageNet-1K, substantially surpassing the previous DP SOTA of 56.5% (ViP) and even exceeding non-private AlexNet (56.5%). The larger DP-Cap (Large) model reaches 65.8% on ImageNet-1K. On the ARO compositional understanding benchmark, DP-Cap (VGA 82.4%, COCO 86.6%, Flickr 87.2%) significantly outperforms non-private CLIP (VGA 62.9%, COCO 47.8%, Flickr 58.0%), while approaching non-private Cap (VGA 87.2%, COCO 87.0%, Flickr 87.4%). DP-Cap also achieves the first meaningful zero-shot classification performance among DP models (7.8% on ImageNet-1K, 54.4% on CIFAR-10), though it remains well below non-private Cap (25.2%, 90.0%) and CLIP (62.2%, 91.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DP-Cap decisively outperforms the previous DP SOTA ViP (Yu et al., 2023) across all vision benchmarks: 63.4% vs 56.5% on ImageNet-1K linear probing, and 31.8% vs 14.3% on 10-shot ImageNet (a 2.2× improvement). Against other DP models trained on ImageNet (TAN at 49.0%, DP-NFNet at 45.3%), DP-Cap leads by 14–18 points on ImageNet-1K. On the ARO compositional understanding benchmark, DP-Cap (VGA 82.4%, COCO 86.6%, Flickr 87.2%) dramatically surpasses non-private CLIP (VGA 62.9%, COCO 47.8%, Flickr 58.0%), demonstrating that DP training can capture complex semantic relationships. However, DP-Cap still trails its non-private counterpart Cap (77.5% vs 63.4% on ImageNet-1K LP; 25.2% vs 7.8% on zero-shot ImageNet), and also falls short of non-private SimCLR on ImageNet-1K (63.4% vs 67.5%), though it exceeds SimCLR on Places-365 (51.9% vs 46.8%) and iNat-2021 (44.5% vs 34.8%). Notably, DP-Cap (Base) slightly outperforms non-private MAE trained on the same dataset (63.4% vs 62.5% on ImageNet-1K LP), and the Large model (65.8%) approaches SimCLR's 67.5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Cap image captioning architecture (Tschannen et al., 2023) and the DP-SGD framework, but departs from the prior DP representation learning line (ViP, Yu et al. 2023) by replacing the MAE reconstruction objective with a vision-language captioning objective. The key conceptual shift is that image captioning provides an additive per-sample loss (natively compatible with DP-SGD) and, crucially, tolerates extreme batch sizes (up to 1.3M) without the performance degradation observed in supervised or MAE-based DP training. The synthetic pre-training initialization from Shaders21K is adapted from ViP, and the ghost norm efficiency technique is borrowed from Li et al. (2021), but the combination with extreme batch scaling and the captioning objective is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work challenges the prevailing view that high-utility DP representation learning from scratch is fundamentally infeasible under modest privacy budgets, showing that the right objective (image captioning) combined with internet-scale multimodal data (233M pairs) and extreme batch sizes can close much of the gap to non-private models. It is the first DP model to demonstrate zero-shot classification and strong compositional understanding (outperforming non-private CLIP on ARO), opening the door to privacy-preserving multimodal foundation models. Open questions remain: why image captioning uniquely supports extreme batch training, whether contrastive learning can be made DP-compatible, and whether model scaling benefits eventually reverse at very large sizes due to gradient SNR degradation.
