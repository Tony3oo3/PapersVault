## Problem

Existing conformal prediction methods for multi-site data assume conditional outcome invariance across environments, which is unrealistic in clinical networks where sites differ in patient populations and treatment practices. Moreover, privacy regulations (HIPAA, GDPR) prevent direct pooling of individual-level data across sites, and no federated conformal inference method existed for constructing valid prediction intervals on a target site under both covariate shift and heterogeneous conditional outcome distributions with missing outcomes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes data from K sites (one target, K−1 sources) where each subject contributes a covariate vector X, a site indicator T, a binary missingness indicator R, and the outcome Y only when R=1. Because individual-level data cannot be shared across sites, the procedure requires only the exchange of coarse summary statistics (e.g., for density-ratio estimation) between sites. The output is a distribution-free prediction interval Ĉ_α(x) = {y : S(x,y) ≤ r̂_fed} for the missing outcome at the target site, with marginal coverage guarantee P(Y ∈ Ĉ_α | T=0, R=0) ≥ 1−α conditional on the training data.

## Method

The paper derives the semiparametric efficient influence function (EIF) for the (1−α)-quantile of the conformal score in the target site under a common conditional outcome distribution (CCOD) assumption, showing that data from all sites with observed outcomes can be leveraged to achieve parametric-rate coverage. When CCOD is violated, a data-adaptive federated aggregation scheme is proposed: site-specific quantile estimates are computed via influence-function-based estimating equations (using density-ratio reweighting for source sites), and federated weights are obtained by solving a penalized loss that anchors at the nonparametric target-site estimate and applies an adaptive Lasso-type penalty proportional to the squared discrepancy |r̂_0 − r̂_k|², thereby upweighting informative sources and downweighting non-informative ones. Nuisance functions (conditional CDFs of conformal scores, missingness risk ratios, density ratios) are estimated via SuperLearner with cross-fitting, and density ratios use exponential tilt models. The resulting prediction interval inherits marginal coverage guarantees at O_P(n^{-1/2} + R_n) rates, where the second-order bias R_n is a product of nuisance estimation errors, permitting flexible machine-learning estimators.

## Results

Under the most challenging setting (n_k=3000 per site, strongly heterogeneous covariates, strong CCOD violation, homoscedastic errors), the proposed Federated II method achieves marginal coverage of 0.896–0.925 with prediction interval widths of 3.30–3.63, closely matching the oracle width of 3.29. In contrast, the pooled sample method fails catastrophically under CCOD violation, producing coverage of 1.000 and interval widths of 31.99 (ASR) or 4.06 (Local ASR). The target-only method (Yang et al., 2024) achieves similar coverage but with consistently wider intervals (e.g., 3.32 vs. 3.30 for ASR; 3.44 vs. 3.39 for Local ASR). In the STS-CHSD application (3,457 Norwood surgeries, 21.2% missing LOS), the federated method produces intervals over 30 days narrower than target-only for some patients (e.g., 80% PI for a South-region patient using Local ASR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the target-only method of Yang et al. (2024), which uses only the target site's data and ignores all source sites. The proposed federated method consistently produces narrower prediction intervals while maintaining comparable or slightly better coverage: for example, under strong CCOD violation with homoscedastic errors, the federated method achieves width 3.30 vs. 3.32 (ASR), 3.39 vs. 3.44 (Local ASR), and 3.63 vs. 3.66 (CQR), with coverage at or above nominal in all cases. The pooled sample method (which assumes full CCOD) is the most dramatic failure: under strong CCOD violation it produces interval widths 10–30× the oracle (e.g., 31.99 vs. 3.29 for ASR) with coverage pinned at 1.000, making it practically useless. The equal-weights baseline performs comparably to the federated method under homogeneous covariates but degrades under heterogeneous covariate distributions. Among the three federated weight variants (I, II, III), Federated II (the main proposal) performs best overall, while Federated III is recommended specifically for CQR under heteroscedasticity and heterogeneous covariates. In the STS-CHSD application, the federated method's intervals are tighter than target-only and comparable to pooled sample, suggesting near-oracle efficiency in this real-world setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-site conformal prediction framework of Yang et al. (2024), which derives a nonparametric influence function for prediction intervals under missing data, to a multi-source setting by deriving semiparametric efficient influence functions that leverage data from all sites under a common conditional outcome distribution (CCOD) assumption. When CCOD is violated, the paper adapts the federated adaptive weighting strategy from Han et al. (2021) (FACE) to conformal prediction, using a penalized optimization that upweights informative source sites and downweights non-informative ones. The key departure from prior conformal prediction work (Lei & Candès, 2021; Tibshirani et al., 2019) is the explicit handling of heterogeneous conditional outcome distributions across sites combined with missingness and privacy constraints, a combination the authors note has not been addressed in the federated conformal prediction literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practically important gap in multi-site clinical research networks where individual-level data cannot be shared due to privacy regulations (HIPAA/GDPR), yet valid prediction intervals are needed for a target population. The data-adaptive federated weighting provides a principled way to exploit heterogeneous source sites without requiring the strong CCOD assumption, and the product-rate bias in the coverage guarantee (R_n) is theoretically tighter than the min-rate bias of prior single-site methods. Open questions include developing covariate-adaptive ensemble weights for potentially more efficient aggregation, and formalizing sensitivity analysis when CCOD is violated. The method is restricted to marginal (not conditional) coverage guarantees, and the practical utility depends on the quality of nuisance function estimation via SuperLearner.
