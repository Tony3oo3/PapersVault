## Problem

In feature-based knowledge distillation for object detection, the spatial disparity between student and teacher feature maps is highly non-uniform, yet most methods apply a single constraint (e.g., MSE) uniformly across all regions. The paper identifies that high-disparity regions, which reflect genuine capability gaps between models, are harmed by overly strict point-by-point matching, while low-disparity regions benefit from it. The gap is a principled way to partition feature maps by disparity magnitude and apply region-specific constraints that balance leniency and strictness.

## Input / Output

The method consumes intermediate feature maps (post-neck) from both a pre-trained teacher detector and a student detector of the same architecture family but different backbone capacity. It computes channel-averaged spatial attention maps of shape H×W for each, derives their L1 disparity, and uses the running mean of that disparity as a dynamic binary threshold to split spatial locations into high- and low-disparity regions. The final output of the full pipeline remains standard detection outputs (bounding boxes and class labels), but the distillation loss is decomposed into two region-specific terms weighted by hyperparameters α and β.

## Method

DFD first computes spatial attention for teacher and student features by channel-averaging absolute values and applying spatial softmax, then takes the L1 distance between these attention maps to obtain a disparity map. The mean of this disparity map serves as an adaptive threshold that dynamically partitions the feature map into high-disparity (HD) and low-disparity (LD) regions during training. For LD regions, a strict point-by-point MSE loss enforces direct feature alignment. For HD regions, the student features are passed through a learnable Conv1-3-1(2C) transformation module before MSE is applied, providing a weaker, adaptive constraint that prevents the student from being misled by structural differences it cannot yet bridge. The total training loss is L_task + α·L_HD + β·L_LD, with only two hyperparameters, and the method is architecture-agnostic, validated on CNN and ViT-based detectors as well as pose estimation and segmentation tasks.

## Results

On COCO 2017 with 2x schedule, DFD improves RetinaNet-ResNet50 from 37.4 to 41.7 mAP (+4.0), FasterRCNN-ResNet50 from 38.4 to 42.4 mAP (+4.0), and RepPoints-ResNet50 from 38.6 to 42.7 mAP (+4.1), all using ResNeXt101-based teachers. The method also extends to ViT-based models (MaskRCNN Swin-Tiny: 42.7→44.4 mAP) and YOLOv6-Tiny (40.6→41.7 mAP). DFD outperforms the strongest prior baselines (FGD, MGD, PKD) by 0.5–1.0 mAP across all three detector architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFD is compared against three feature-distillation baselines: FGD (focal and global distillation with foreground/background masks), MGD (masked generative distillation), and PKD (Pearson correlation coefficient normalization). DFD wins on all three detector types: on RetinaNet it achieves 41.7 mAP vs. FGD 40.9, MGD 41.2, and PKD 41.2; on FasterRCNN it reaches 42.4 mAP vs. FGD 42.0, MGD 42.1, and PKD 41.4; on RepPoints it scores 42.7 mAP vs. FGD 42.0, MGD 42.3, and PKD 42.4. The largest margin is over PKD on FasterRCNN (+1.0 mAP), while the smallest is over MGD on RepPoints (+0.4 mAP). DFD also outperforms the more recent DiffKD (40.7/41.7) and CTFD (41.0/42.0) on RetinaNet and RepPoints respectively, achieving 41.2 and 42.5 mAP under 1x schedule. Notably, DFD requires no annotation-derived region masks (unlike FGD), reducing training time from ~29 h to ~18 h for RepPoints distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits within the feature-based knowledge distillation lineage for object detection, building on FitNet-style MSE distillation (Romero et al., 2014) and its detection-specific adaptations (Chen et al., 2017). It departs from annotation-driven region splitting (FGD, Defeat) by instead partitioning feature maps based on the actual spatial-attention disparity between teacher and student, and departs from uniform weak-constraint methods (PKD, MGD) by applying heterogeneous constraints: strict MSE on low-disparity regions and a learnable transformation module on high-disparity regions. The core conceptual move is treating distillation difficulty as spatially non-uniform and responding with region-specific constraint strength rather than a single global strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that high-disparity regions carry the most transferable signal (distilling only HD outperforms distilling only LD) provides a useful diagnostic for understanding where teacher-student knowledge gaps actually lie, independent of annotation-based foreground/background labels. The method's simplicity (two hyperparameters, a small conv transformation module) and generality across CNN/ViT backbones and one/two-stage detectors make it a practical drop-in for existing detection pipelines. Open questions include whether the mean-based adaptive threshold is truly optimal, whether the approach transfers to relational or logit-based distillation, and whether the learnable transformation module could be replaced by more expressive alignment mechanisms without overfitting.
