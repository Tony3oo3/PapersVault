## Problem

Prior work on LLMs for Bayesian optimization over molecules relied on heuristic, non-Bayesian uncertainty estimates (e.g., softmax token probabilities from point-estimated LLMs or in-context learning with GPT-4), leaving open whether LLMs' apparent chemistry understanding actually translates into principled, calibrated uncertainty for the exploration-exploitation tradeoff. This paper asks a sober question: are LLMs genuinely useful as surrogates for BO in molecular space, and if so, under what conditions (domain-specific pretraining, finetuning, prompting)?

## Input / Output

The method consumes SMILES (or IUPAC) strings of molecules from a discrete candidate pool D_cand (ranging from 392 to 10,000 molecules) along with observed molecule–property pairs D_t accumulated over BO iterations. It produces a Gaussian posterior predictive distribution p(g_t(x) | D_t) over the target property for every candidate molecule, from which an acquisition function (Thompson sampling or expected improvement) selects the next molecule to evaluate. For multi-objective settings, the output is a multivariate Gaussian over C objectives, and the acquisition function is a scalarized Thompson sampler.

## Method

The paper constructs two principled Bayesian surrogate architectures around LLMs. In the fixed-feature setting, a pretrained LLM's last-layer embeddings are extracted once and cached, then fed into a standard GP (Matérn kernel) or a Laplace-approximated 3-layer ReLU network. In the adaptive-feature setting, LoRA (rank 4) is applied to the LLM's attention weights while freezing the original parameters W*, and the key probabilistic contribution is a clear formulation that treats W* as a conditioning hyperparameter: the posterior p(θ | D; W = W*) is defined only over the PEFT weights ω and regression head weights w, with the Laplace approximation (Kronecker-factored Hessian) yielding a tractable linearized Laplace predictive distribution. This generalizes Yang et al.'s LoRA-LA to any PEFT method and provides the first explicit Bayesian interpretation of PEFT in the BO context. Experiments across 8 chemistry datasets and 8 LLMs (T5, GPT-2-M, Llama-2-7B, T5-Chem, MolFormer, fingerprints) show that domain-specific pretraining and finetuning are both necessary for LLMs to outperform simple fingerprint baselines, and that the resulting Bayesian surrogates are both more accurate and far cheaper than in-context-learning approaches.

## Results

Across 8 real-world chemistry problems, chemistry-specific LLM features (T5-Chem, 220M params) consistently outperform general-purpose LLMs (T5-Base, GPT-2-Medium, Llama-2-7B) and even 1024-bit Morgan fingerprints in terms of the GAP metric for Bayesian optimization. Finetuning via LoRA + linearized Laplace approximation (LLA) improves BO performance over fixed-feature surrogates in most problems, though one exception (Photovoltaics) shows degradation with default hyperparameters. The ICL-based BO-LIFT method with GPT-4 costs $75.81 for a subsampled Redoxmer run (|D_cand|=200, T=15, 5 seeds), while T5-Chem with a principled Bayesian surrogate runs on mid-range consumer GPUs at negligible cost and achieves better optimization performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Morgan fingerprints (1024-bit), general-purpose LLMs (T5, GPT-2-M, Llama-2-7B) underperform, while chemistry-specific features (T5-Chem, MolFormer) outperform them; T5-Chem edges out MolFormer slightly on average despite MolFormer being trained on more chemistry data (100M vs. 33M molecules). Against MolFormer (44M params), T5-Chem (220M params) is competitive overall but MolFormer is better at early optimization stages in multi-objective settings, likely due to MolFormer's smaller size enabling faster convergence. Against BO-LIFT (in-context learning with GPT-4), the paper's approach using T5-Chem as a fixed feature extractor with a Laplace-approximated surrogate is both more effective and dramatically cheaper—BO-LIFT with GPT-4 costs ~$20,000 for a full run (T=100, 5 seeds) extrapolated, while T5-Chem runs on a single consumer GPU. Finetuned LLM surrogates (LoRA + LLA) beat their fixed-feature counterparts in most of the 6 single-objective problems tested, confirming that domain-adaptive feature learning adds value beyond pretraining alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bayesian LoRA framework of Yang et al. (2023) by providing a clear probabilistic interpretation of PEFT as conditioning on frozen pretrained weights, and generalizing it to any PEFT method (not just LoRA). It departs sharply from prior LLM-for-BO work (Ramos et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2024), which relied on non-Bayesian, point-estimated LLMs with heuristic uncertainty from softmax probabilities, by instead applying the linearized Laplace approximation (Immer et al., 2021) to obtain principled posterior predictive distributions. The fixed-feature surrogate approach connects to the broader line of work using pretrained embeddings as implicit priors for BO (Chithrananda et al., 2020; Ranković & Schwaller, 2023), while the finetuned surrogate approach is novel in combining PEFT with Bayesian inference for molecular optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a sober, well-controlled empirical answer to a question with significant hype: general-purpose LLMs (Llama-2-7b, GPT-2) are actually worse than 1024-bit Morgan fingerprints for BO over molecules, and only domain-specific pretraining or finetuning makes LLMs useful. This has direct practical implications—practitioners can use a 200M-parameter chemistry-specific LLM (T5-Chem) with a Laplace-approximated surrogate on consumer hardware rather than paying for GPT-4 API calls, achieving better performance at a fraction of the cost. Open questions include whether the Bayesian PEFT formulation extends to continuous-space BO, whether optimizing the frozen weights W via marginal likelihood (analogous to deep kernel learning) would further improve performance, and what mechanisms underpin the exploration-exploitation tradeoff that domain-specific LLMs induce.
