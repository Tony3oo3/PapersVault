## Problem

Tight privacy characterizations for noisy gradient descent and its variants remain open even for convex losses. Existing f-DP analyses based on the composition theorem yield divergent bounds (scaling as √t), while recent convergent bounds were proved only in the inherently lossy Rényi DP framework. The central question is whether convergent privacy bounds can be achieved directly in the information-theoretically lossless f-DP framework, which would enable the tightest possible privacy accounting.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes as input a noisy gradient descent variant (NoisyGD, NoisyCGD, or NoisySGD) with specified loss properties (convex or strongly convex, M-smooth), gradient sensitivity L, constraint set diameter D, and algorithm parameters (learning rate η, noise rate σ, batch size b, number of steps t). It produces a closed-form f-DP bound expressed as a Gaussian DP (GDP) parameter μ that is convergent in t, which can be losslessly converted to (ε,δ)-DP or Rényi DP guarantees. The numerical validation uses regularized logistic regression on MNIST (28×28 grayscale digits) with NoisyCGD, comparing privacy ε at δ=10⁻⁵ across 50, 100, and 200 epochs.

## Method

The core technique constructs a shifted interpolated process {X̃_k}, an auxiliary stochastic process that starts at one trajectory (X̃_τ = X'_τ) and ends at the other (X̃_t = X_t) by taking convex combinations of the two processes' gradient updates with coupled Gaussian noise. This enables unrolling the composition argument only from the final time t back to an intermediate time τ (rather than all the way to initialization), replacing the divergent √t dependence with a quantity scaling in t−τ. A geometrically aware composition lemma exploits the (strong) contractivity of gradient descent updates to show that privacy leakage from older gradient queries decays exponentially, making the total bound convergent. The shifted interpolated process explicitly realizes the optimal shifts implicit in the shifted-divergence argument of Feldman et al. (2018), disentangling the Rényi divergence and Wasserstein distance components and enabling generalization from divergence-based relaxations to f-DP tradeoff functions.

## Results

On regularized logistic regression over MNIST using NoisyCGD, the paper's f-DP bounds yield a privacy budget of ε = 4.34 at 50 epochs, ε = 5.60 at 100 epochs, and ε = 7.58 at 200 epochs (all at δ = 10⁻⁵), substantially tighter than both the GDP Composition baseline (30.51, 49.88, 83.83) and the state-of-the-art RDP bounds (5.82, 7.61, 9.88). The tighter privacy accounting enables longer training under the same budget, improving test accuracy from 90.12% (50 epochs) to 91.37% (200 epochs). Theoretically, the paper establishes the first exact convergent f-DP bound for strongly convex losses, improving over prior RDP results by a factor of 2 in the GDP parameter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: the standard f-DP composition bound (GDP Composition, from Dong et al. 2022) and the state-of-the-art Rényi DP bounds (RDP, from Altschuler & Talwar 2022). The proposed shifted interpolation method dominates both baselines across all epoch counts. Against GDP Composition, the improvement is dramatic: at 50 epochs the privacy budget drops from 30.51 to 4.34 (a ~7x improvement), and at 200 epochs from 83.83 to 7.58 (~11x), because the composition bound diverges as √t while the new bound converges. Against the RDP baseline, the improvement is more modest but consistent: 4.34 vs 5.82 at 50 epochs (~25% tighter), 5.60 vs 7.61 at 100 epochs (~26% tighter), and 7.58 vs 9.88 at 200 epochs (~23% tighter). The paper notes that this factor-of-2 improvement in the GDP parameter translates to an even larger gain in (ε,δ)-DP due to the inherent lossiness of the RDP-to-(ε,δ)-DP conversion that the f-DP approach avoids.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the privacy analysis of noisy gradient descent from the inherently lossy Rényi DP framework to the information-theoretically lossless f-DP framework, building directly on the f-DP/GDP formalism of Dong et al. (2022) and the convergent RDP bounds of Altschuler & Talwar (2022). The key technical contribution—the shifted interpolated process—demystifies and generalizes the shifted divergence argument from Feldman et al. (2018), which had been an ad-hoc combination of Rényi divergence and Wasserstein distance, by explicitly constructing the auxiliary process that achieves the optimal shifts. As an immediate corollary, the paper recovers the f-DP characterization of the exponential mechanism from Gopi et al. (2022) via a fundamentally different proof connecting Langevin Monte Carlo to the exponential mechanism, rather than Gaussian isoperimetry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The factor-of-2 improvement in Rényi DP (and larger in (ε,δ)-DP) for strongly convex losses is practically significant: it enables training with half the noise under the same privacy budget, and the convergent nature of the bounds permits longer training without privacy degradation—addressing a key limitation of composition-based analyses. The shifted interpolation technique is potentially of independent interest beyond DP, as it enables coupling-style arguments for tradeoff functions (previously only available for Wasserstein distances). Open questions include extending convergent f-DP bounds to non-convex landscapes, correlated noise, adaptive and second-order algorithms, and developing computationally tractable approximations for the resulting f-DP bounds, which are expressed as compositions of many tradeoff functions.
