## Problem

Enforcing equivariance in neural networks typically requires purpose-built architectures (e.g., E2CNNs) tailored to specific symmetry groups, while data augmentation only yields approximate equivariance that emerges near convergence and on the data manifold. This paper asks whether equivariance can be achieved without any architectural constraints, and shows that deep ensembles trained with full data augmentation become exactly equivariant at all training times and for all inputs in the infinite-width limit — a property that is emergent, meaning individual ensemble members are not equivariant but their average is.

## Input / Output

The method accepts diverse inputs: spin configurations on a 5×5 lattice (Ising model), 28×28 grayscale images (FashionMNIST, MNIST), 32×32 grayscale images (CIFAR-10), and 224×224 RGB histological slices (NCT-CRC-HE-100K). During training, each input is augmented by all elements of the group orbit (e.g., all rotations by multiples of 360/k degrees for cyclic group C_k). The output is the ensemble-averaged prediction — a scalar energy value for the Ising model or a class label for image tasks — which is provably equivariant under the group action at any training step, including for out-of-distribution inputs.

## Method

The proof leverages the duality between infinitely wide neural networks and Gaussian processes described by the neural tangent kernel (NTK) and NNGP kernel. In three steps: (1) the NTK and NNGP kernels transform covariantly under the group representation (Theorem 5.1), (2) full data augmentation converts the group action on training samples into a permutation of training-set indices, and (3) this permutation commutes with any matrix-valued analytical function of the Gram matrices and their inverses (Lemma 5.2), which forces the ensemble mean to satisfy the equivariance relation at every training time t and for every input x. The result is 'emergent' because no individual ensemble member needs an equivariant architecture — equivariance arises purely from averaging over initializations. The paper further derives explicit bounds on equivariance breaking due to finite ensemble size (via a Gaussian tail bound on the MC estimator) and finite discretization of continuous groups, and validates the theory on Ising model, FashionMNIST, and histological image classification.

## Results

On the OOD MNIST validation set (orbit same prediction metric, higher = more equivariant), Deep Ensembles + Data Augmentation (size 100) achieves 3.85±0.12, 7.72±0.34, and 15.24±0.69 for group orders C4, C8, and C16 respectively, matching or exceeding manifestly equivariant baselines. On the Ising model, ensemble mean predictions show a relative standard deviation across orbits of ~0.8% out-of-distribution, compared to ~82% for individual ensemble members, confirming emergent invariance. On histological data (NCT-CRC-HE-100K), ensemble predictions are more invariant than individual members even at ensemble size 5, with the gap widening at size 20.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two manifestly equivariant baselines: E2CNN (Cesa et al., 2022) and learned canonicalization (Kaba et al., 2023). At C4, both baselines achieve a perfect score of 4 (the maximum for group order 4), slightly ahead of DeepEns+DA (3.85). At C8, DeepEns+DA (7.72) ties E2CNN (7.71) and outperforms Canon (7.45). At C16, DeepEns+DA (15.24) surpasses both E2CNN (15.08) and Canon (12.41), with the gap to Canon being substantial. The authors note that E2CNN's manifest equivariance is slightly broken for k>4 due to interpolation artifacts, explaining why the finite ensemble becomes competitive. Data augmentation alone without ensembling (3.41/6.73/12.77) is consistently worse than the full ensemble, confirming that the equivariance is genuinely emergent from averaging rather than from augmentation alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of NTK theory (Jacot et al., 2018; Lee et al., 2019) and deep ensembles (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017), using the closed-form Gaussian process description of infinite-width networks to prove a symmetry property of the ensemble mean. It departs from the equivariant-architecture literature (E2CNN, canonicalization, symmetrization) by showing that equivariance can be achieved without any architectural constraint, purely through ensemble averaging combined with full data augmentation. Compared to prior kernel + augmentation work (Dao et al., 2019; Li et al., 2019), which established invariance of augmented kernels at infinite training time, this paper proves equivariance (not just invariance) at all training times and for all inputs, including off-manifold points, under very mild assumptions on the group representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides a practical, architecture-agnostic route to equivariance: any practitioner already using deep ensembles with data augmentation gains approximate equivariance without redesigning their network, which is valuable in domains like medical imaging where equivariant architectures are hard to construct. Key limitations include the infinite-width assumption (finite-width corrections are beyond the paper's scope), the need for full group-orbit augmentation (continuous groups are only approximated), and the Monte-Carlo error from finite ensemble size, though the paper provides explicit bounds for both. Open questions include extending the proof to attention, pooling, and dropout layers, incorporating finite-width corrections, and developing a more faithful model of stochastic data augmentation that breaks the fixed-training-set assumption of NTK theory.
