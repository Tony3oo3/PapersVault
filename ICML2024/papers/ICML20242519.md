## Problem

Existing unified segmentation models (SAM, SegGPT) rely on single image-mask pair prompts designed for context-independent concepts and fail on context-dependent (CD) concepts where the target's identity is defined by its relationship to the surrounding environment. CD segmentation tasks (salient, camouflaged, shadow, transparent objects, and medical lesions) have been developed in isolation, each requiring a separately trained model, which limits cross-domain generalization and leads to repetitive architectural innovation. No prior framework shares 100% of its parameters across all CD tasks while maintaining state-of-the-art performance on each.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242519/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, Spider takes a 384×384 RGB image, a task indicator selecting one of eight concept filters, and a fixed set of 64 image-mask group prompts (selected via K-means clustering over high-level embeddings of the training set) for that task. The group prompts are processed by a frozen pre-trained encoder, so only the segmentation stream processes the query image. The output is a single-channel binary segmentation mask at the input resolution. During training, N=16 samples per task are drawn per iteration, with B=4 fed to the segmentation stream and the remaining 12 serving as prompts, and all eight tasks are concatenated into a single batch.

## Method

Spider shares 100% of its encoder-decoder parameters (vanilla UNet or FPN with ViT/Swin/ConvNeXt backbones) across all eight tasks, using a lightweight concept filter at the decoder tail to produce task-specific predictions. The concept filter is generated by a prompt stream: image-group prompts are encoded by a frozen pre-trained encoder into group prompt features F_mem that serve as key and value in a transformer decoder, while foreground and background mask-group prompts are condensed into descriptors via masked average pooling and refined through multi-head cross-attention against F_mem. The resulting foreground descriptor becomes the convolutional weight W_obj and the background descriptor becomes the bias b_ctx of the concept filter, which is applied to the final decoder feature map to yield a single-channel prediction. A 'Balance FP – Unify BP' training strategy concatenates all task samples into one batch (so batch normalization aligns cross-task distributions) and computes a joint PPA loss, ensuring unified gradient updates that prevent any single task from dominating. For continuous learning, only the last decoder layer and concept filter (<1% of parameters) are fine-tuned when adding new tasks, preserving old-task performance with <5% degradation.

## Results

Spider (ConvNeXt-L backbone) achieves the best performance across all 8 context-dependent segmentation tasks with a single set of parameters: Fβ=0.8821/Sm=0.9158 on DUTS (SOD), Fβ=0.7893/Sm=0.8674 on COD10K (COD), BER=0.0396/MAE=0.0265 on SBU (Shadow), BER=0.0636/MAE=0.0554 on Trans10K (TOS), mDice=0.8243/mIoU=0.8664 on Polyp, mDice=0.6956/mIoU=0.8127 on COVID-19 CT, mDice=0.8376/mIoU=0.8655 on BUSI (Breast), and mDice=0.8943/mIoU=0.8735 on ISIC2018 (Skin). The largest margins over the closest unified baseline SegGPT (ViT-L) exceed 30% on TOS (BER 0.0636 vs. 0.4631), COVID-19 (mDice 0.6956 vs. 0.1309), and Breast (mDice 0.8376 vs. 0.3455). Spider also outperforms every task-specific specialized model on its respective benchmark, e.g., 0.8821 Fβ vs. 0.8736 (PGNet) on DUTS and 0.8243 mDice vs. 0.7490 (WeakPolyp) on Polyp.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spider outperforms all 19 baselines across every task it is evaluated on. Against the closest unified competitor SegGPT (ViT-L, trained on 250K+ images), Spider achieves dramatic gains: BER drops from 0.4631 to 0.0636 on TOS (−86%), mDice rises from 0.1309 to 0.6956 on COVID-19 CT (+431%), and mDice rises from 0.3455 to 0.8376 on Breast (+142%), confirming that single-pair image-mask prompts are fundamentally insufficient for context-dependent concepts. Against EVP (which unifies only 3 tasks with separate adaptor parameters), Spider wins on all three shared tasks (e.g., Fβ 0.8821 vs. 0.8431 on DUTS; BER 0.0396 vs. 0.0481 on SBU). Against UniverSeg (medical-only unified model), Spider dominates all four medical benchmarks (e.g., mDice 0.8243 vs. 0.5525 on Polyp; mDice 0.8943 vs. 0.7605 on Skin). Against the strongest specialized models, Spider also wins on each task: 0.8821 Fβ vs. 0.8736 (PGNet) on SOD, 0.7893 Fβ vs. 0.7347 (FSPNet) on COD, 0.0396 BER vs. 0.0429 (SARA) on Shadow, 0.0636 BER vs. 0.1036 (RFENet) on TOS, 0.8243 mDice vs. 0.7490 (WeakPolyp) on Polyp, 0.8376 mDice vs. 0.5452 (CMUNet) on Breast, and 0.8943 mDice vs. 0.8588 (EGE-UNet) on Skin. The paper attributes Spider's advantage to the concept filter's high-level image-foreground/background matching, which avoids the pixel-level ambiguity inherent in single-prompt fusion used by SAM, SegGPT, and UniverSeg.

## Relation to Prior Work

Spider departs from prior unified segmentation models (SAM, SegGPT, UniverSeg) that rely on single image-mask pair prompts and pixel-level feature fusion, which the authors argue is fundamentally ill-suited for context-dependent concepts where targets lack fixed semantic classes. Instead, it introduces a group-prompt paradigm using multiple image-mask pairs (both foreground and background) processed through a transformer-based cross-attention mechanism to generate a concept filter (weight + bias) that acts on a shared encoder-decoder. This builds on the encoder-decoder backbone tradition (UNet, FPN) and the masked image modeling idea for prompt sampling, but repurposes them for high-level concept matching rather than direct feature fusion. The work extends EVP's partial unification of three CD tasks to eight diverse domains with truly 100% parameter sharing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper provides the first parameter-unified framework that competitively handles eight heterogeneous context-dependent segmentation tasks spanning natural scenes and multiple medical modalities, addressing a gap where prior generalist models (SAM, SegGPT) demonstrably fail. The continual learning capability—adding new tasks by fine-tuning <1% of parameters with <5% degradation on old tasks—offers a practical pathway for deploying unified models in evolving clinical or industrial settings without retraining from scratch. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video CD understanding, truly unseen concept categories, and whether the 64-prompt inference requirement is practical in latency-sensitive applications. The paper also raises the interesting question of whether natural scene data (e.g., camouflage) can transfer to improve medical lesion segmentation.
