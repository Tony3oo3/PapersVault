## Problem

Private nonparametric density estimation under central differential privacy had been studied only in one dimension, with smoothness restricted to integer values, and with the optimal estimator requiring prior knowledge of the true smoothness parameter β. This paper addresses all three limitations simultaneously: it extends the analysis to arbitrary dimension d, generalizes Sobolev smoothness to non-integer levels, and introduces a fully adaptive (data-driven) selection procedure that achieves near-minimax rates without knowing β.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n i.i.d. samples from the hypercube [0,1]^d along with a privacy budget ρ (in the zero-concentrated differential privacy sense) and the ambient dimension d. It outputs a private density estimate on [0,1]^d constructed as a truncated Fourier series, where the truncation rank is either fixed (oracle case) or selected adaptively from a logarithmic grid. The adaptive variant requires no knowledge of the smoothness β, and the entire pipeline—including model selection—satisfies ρ-zCDP via adaptive composition of the Gaussian mechanism across a polynomial-in-log(n) number of candidate models.

## Method

The estimator is a Fourier projection: it computes empirical Fourier coefficients on a hypercube basis {e^{i_C 2π⟨k,x⟩}} and adds complex Gaussian noise calibrated so that the released coefficients satisfy ρ-zCDP via the Gaussian mechanism. The key theoretical contribution is a minimax-optimal rate of max{n^{-2β/(2β+d)}, (n√ρ)^{-2β/(β+d)}} for any β>0 in dimension d, established via matching upper bounds (bias-variance-privacy decomposition) and lower bounds (Assouad's lemma with a privacy-aware coupling argument). For adaptivity, the paper adapts the Lepskii method by introducing a privacy-aware penalization term that simultaneously balances squared bias, sampling variance, and privacy-noise variance when selecting the truncation rank from a grid of O(log²n) candidate smoothness levels. The privacy budget is split across candidate models via the adaptive composition theorem, and the selection rule compares pairwise distances between estimators at different ranks against a penalized risk threshold, ensuring the final mechanism remains ρ-zCDP.

## Results

The paper establishes that the private projection estimator achieves the minimax-optimal rate Θ(n^{-2β/(2β+d)} + (n√ρ)^{-2β/(β+d)}) for Sobolev-smooth densities on [0,1]^d under ρ-zCDP, matching the lower bound up to constants depending on L, β, and d. The adaptive Lepskii-based selector achieves this same rate up to polylogarithmic factors (specifically a (log n)^{a} multiplier and a √(log₂ n) penalty on the privacy term) without requiring prior knowledge of the smoothness β. Two distinct adaptive procedures are provided: a risk-penalization variant (Theorem 6.1) and a bias-penalization variant (Theorem 6.3) that achieves near-optimality in squared error at the cost of requiring a bound on ‖f‖_∞.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper strictly generalizes all three prior works in Table 1. Compared to Wasserman & Zhou (2010), it moves from fixed to variable privacy (revealing the privacy-utility tradeoff), extends from d=1 to arbitrary d, and adds adaptivity. Compared to Barber & Duchi (2014), it generalizes from the single smoothness level β=1 to all β>0 (including non-integer values) and adds adaptivity, while retaining the same high-dimensional generality. Compared to the closest prior work, Lalanne et al. (2023b), it extends from one-dimensional to d-dimensional data, from integer-valued to arbitrary real smoothness β∈(0,+∞), and introduces the first privacy-aware adaptive selection procedure for central differential privacy. The estimation rate of this paper reduces to each prior rate as a special case (setting d=1 recovers Lalanne et al.; setting β=1 recovers Barber & Duchi; setting ρ→∞ recovers the non-private rate of Wasserman & Zhou), confirming that the privacy cost is precisely characterized by the (n√ρ)^{-2β/(β+d)} term.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of private nonparametric density estimation initiated by Wasserman & Zhou (2010) and Barber & Duchi (2014), and most directly builds on Lalanne et al. (2023b), which studied 1D densities with integer Sobolev smoothness under variable privacy. The key departures are threefold: generalization to arbitrary dimension d, extension to non-integer smoothness via Hölder remainders (circumventing a technical limitation of the Fourier-coefficient definition), and the introduction of a privacy-aware Lepskii method for adaptive model selection that balances bias, sampling variance, and privacy noise simultaneously. The privacy-aware penalization term in the Lepskii criterion is the main methodological novelty, as prior adaptive selection mechanisms (Exponential Mechanism, Report Noisy Max) do not naturally accommodate the three-way tradeoff.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide a minimax-optimal, smoothness-agnostic private density estimator under central zCDP in arbitrary dimension, giving practitioners a concrete recipe that does not require prior knowledge of the density's regularity. The two-regime characterization (privacy-negligible vs. privacy-dominant) clarifies when the privacy budget is a binding constraint, which is practically important for system design. Limitations include a polylogarithmic degradation in the adaptive rate and a ||f||_∞ requirement in one variant of the selection rule that the authors acknowledge may be an artifact of suboptimal analysis. Open questions include whether the polylog factor is fundamental, whether the Hölder-remainder approach extends to other function spaces (e.g., Besov), and how these rates compare under local rather than central privacy in the full-support setting.
