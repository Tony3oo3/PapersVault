## Problem

Zero-cost proxies (ZCPs) for NAS performance prediction exhibit a direct dependence on the number of convolutions rather than a genuine correlation with task performance, making them unable to distinguish structurally similar networks and inconsistent across diverse tasks. Existing model-based predictors, while more flexible, lack interpretability and often require minutes of training time, limiting their use in budget-constrained search settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the architecture graph (vertices, edges, and operation labels) from cell-based or macro NAS benchmarks, requiring no training data, mini-batches, or model compilation. For each network, GRAF computes features for all subsets of the operation set, yielding 47–1540 features depending on the search space, with computation well under one second per network. Outputs are continuous performance predictions (validation accuracy, hardware energy/latency, or adversarial robustness accuracy) produced by a random forest or XGBoost regressor trained on a small sample of evaluated architectures (32–1024).

## Method

GRAF defines a small set of interpretable graph properties—operation counts, minimum and maximum path lengths, input/output node degrees, and mean intermediate node degrees—each computed for every subset of the operation set, capturing depth, shortcut structure, and local connectivity patterns. The key insight motivating these features is that ZCPs like nwot and 12_norm directly encode convolution count rather than task-relevant properties, so explicitly representing operation counts and path structure removes this confound. These features are fed into simple tree-based predictors (random forest, XGBoost) in a supervised regression setup, optionally concatenated with ZCP scores or one-hot encodings. SHAP-based feature importance analysis reveals that different tasks favor different architectural properties (e.g., skip-connection path lengths for autoencoders, node degrees for scene classification), providing interpretability absent in prior predictors. The ZCP + GRAF combination consistently outperforms ZCP alone, one-hot encoding, path encoding, arch2vec, and Weisfeiler-Lehman features across all benchmarks and tasks evaluated.

## Results

On NB201 cifar-10 with 1024 training samples, ZCP+GRAF achieves a Kendall tau of 0.84, outperforming ZCP alone (0.81), GRAF alone (0.83), and one-hot encoding (0.74). On NB101 cifar-10, ZCP+GRAF reaches 0.73 versus 0.63 for ZCP alone, and on NB301 cifar-10 it achieves 0.64 versus 0.38 for ZCP alone. In the NASLib search setting on ImageNet16-120, ZCP+GRAF is more sample-efficient than all other predictors including BANANAS, GCN, and SemiNAS. GRAF computation takes well under one second per network (e.g., 27.3s for all 9445 NB201 networks).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against zero-cost proxies (ZCP) from NAS-Bench-Suite-Zero [Krishnakumar et al., 2022], GRAF alone outperforms ZCP on all benchmarks (e.g., 0.83 vs 0.81 on NB201 cifar-10), and the combination ZCP+GRAF achieves the best results across nearly all tasks, indicating the two encodings capture complementary properties. Against BRP-NAS [Dudziak et al., 2020], the RF-based ZCP+GRAF predictor typically matches or exceeds BRP-NAS on most tasks, though BRP-NAS with GRAF+one-hot feedback is competitive on NB301 and hardware tasks. Against TA-GATES [Ning et al., 2022], ZCP+GRAF (with XGBoost) performs better at small sample sizes and matches at larger sizes, while being faster and more interpretable. In the NASLib predictor survey comparison [White et al., 2021b], ZCP+GRAF outperforms all surveyed predictors including BANANAS [White et al., 2021a] and GCN [Wen et al., 2020] on NB101. In the Bayesian optimization search setting, ZCP+GRAF is more sample-efficient than SemiNAS [Luo et al., 2020] on ImageNet16-120, though SemiNAS catches up on cifar-10 in later stages. Against arch2vec [Dong & Yang, 2020a] and NASBOWL's WL features [Ru et al., 2021], adding these encodings to GRAF yields only marginal gains, suggesting GRAF already captures most of the structural information they encode.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing a diagnostic observation about zero-cost proxies (ZCPs) into a practical feature design: the authors show that many ZCPs have a direct (not merely correlational) dependence on the number of convolutions, and then propose hand-crafted graph features (GRAF) — operation counts, min/max path lengths, and node degrees — that explicitly encode these structural properties. Conceptually, GRAF sits between the data-dependent ZCP literature (Abdelfattah et al., Mellor et al.) and the learned-representation encodings (BRP-NAS GNNs, arch2vec, NASBOWL's WL kernels), arguing that simple interpretable graph statistics are sufficient to match or exceed these complex encodings when fed to a random forest. The work also connects to Lopes et al.'s finding that only a subset of operations matters for performance and to NASBOWL's motif-based interpretability, but departs by providing a unified, data-independent feature set that is computable in under a second per network without any training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The interpretability of GRAF enables practitioners to identify which architectural properties drive performance for a given task (e.g., skip-connection path lengths for autoencoders, node degrees for scene classification), which is valuable for search-space design and for deploying NAS in regulated domains. A key limitation is that the best results still require ZCPs as complementary input, suggesting that architecture-only features cannot capture all relevant information (possibly training dynamics or information-flow properties). Transferability across search spaces is limited due to different operation sets, and the paper acknowledges that new features must be designed for non-cell-based or transformer search spaces. An open question the authors raise is whether one could scale networks according to the most important features while maintaining performance, which could lead to more principled architecture design beyond search.
