## Problem

In low-data regimes (n<100), conventional tabular generative models (GANs, VAEs, diffusion models) are constrained by the limited training set and cannot produce sufficiently diverse or accurate synthetic data. While LLMs carry prior knowledge that could enrich generation, their outputs contain noisy or mislabeled samples that harm downstream performance — a curation problem that has been overlooked in the synthetic data literature. The paper addresses both the generation quality gap and the need for a principled mechanism to filter harmful synthetic samples.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small labeled tabular dataset (n<100), a frozen black-box LLM (GPT-4 or GPT-3.5 accessed via API at temperature 0.9), and natural-language contextual information describing the task, feature meanings, and domain background. The LLM is prompted with serialized in-context examples, feature descriptions, and generation instructions to produce a synthetic dataset D_syn. The output is a curated subset D_curated ⊂ D_syn, obtained by filtering samples via learning-dynamics metrics computed with an XGBoost model trained on D_train, which is then used to train any downstream classifier (XGBoost, Random Forest, Decision Tree, or Logistic Regression).

## Method

CLLM combines two components: (1) LLM-based generation via in-context learning, where a frozen LLM is prompted with background context, serialized in-context examples from D_train, and instructions to generate diverse synthetic samples, leveraging the LLM's pretraining prior to extrapolate beyond the limited training data; and (2) a learning-dynamics curation mechanism that trains an XGBoost classifier on D_train and tracks its predictions over E training checkpoints to compute, for each synthetic sample, an average confidence (marginal probability of correct prediction) and an aleatoric uncertainty (variance of the binary prediction across checkpoints). Samples with both low confidence (below τ_conf=0.2) and low aleatoric uncertainty (below an adaptive threshold) are discarded as likely mislabeled, yielding D_curated. The key insight is that learning dynamics provide a model-agnostic, principled signal for identifying noisy synthetic samples, and the curation mechanism improves downstream performance for all generative models tested, not just LLMs.

## Results

On 7 real-world tabular datasets, CLLM (GPT-4 + Curation) achieves the best downstream AUC in 20 out of 28 settings, with the largest gains in the low-data regime (n < 100). At n=20, CLLM GPT-4 (Curated) reaches 73.87 AUC on Covid-19, 84.82 on SEER, and 75.29 on Drug, outperforming the strongest conventional baseline (CTGAN Curated) by up to 10.2 AUC points (SEER). The curation mechanism consistently improves downstream performance for all generative models, with average rank of 2.71 at n=20 versus 13.86 for uncurated CTGAN. Performance approaches or exceeds the D_oracle upper bound (e.g., 73.87 vs. 74.41 on Covid-19 at n=20).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLLM (GPT-4 + Curation) outperforms all six baselines on 6 of 7 datasets at n=20, with the largest margins over GReaT (fine-tuned LLM, +7.4 AUC on SEER) and CTGAN (+10.2 AUC on SEER, +10.2 on Covid-19). The sole exception is the Adult dataset, where GReaT (77.25) and CTGAN (76.86) outperform CLLM GPT-4 (71.48), which the authors attribute to GPT-4 producing low-quality synthetic data for that particular dataset. Curation improves every baseline: for example, GReaT jumps from 47.57 to 74.43 AUC on SEER (n=20), and CTGAN from 59.00 to 63.67 on Covid-19. In the low-data regime (n=20), CLLM GPT-4 achieves an average rank of 2.71 versus 15.71 for uncurated GReaT and 13.86 for uncurated CTGAN. As n increases to 200, the gap narrows (CLLM rank 3.29 vs. CTGAN Curated 3.71), confirming that the advantage is most pronounced when training data is scarce. The paper also shows that ICL (in-context learning with a frozen LLM) outperforms fine-tuning (GReaT) in the low-data regime, where fine-tuning is prone to overfitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLLM builds on two distinct research threads: (1) tabular data generation via generative models (CTGAN, TVAE, Normalizing Flows, TabDDPM, SMOTE) and LLM-based generation (GReaT, which fine-tunes an LLM), and (2) data-centric AI and learning dynamics (Arpit et al., 2017; Seedat et al., 2023a,b). The key departure is twofold: it replaces LLM fine-tuning with in-context learning on a frozen black-box LLM (arguing fine-tuning overfits in the n<100 regime), and it introduces a principled post-generation curation step based on learning dynamics (average confidence and aleatoric uncertainty) that is model-agnostic and applicable to any synthetic data generator, not just LLMs. The paper explicitly contrasts its setting with transfer learning, meta-learning, and active learning, emphasizing that CLLM requires no external datasets, no specific downstream backbone, and uses aleatoric (not epistemic) uncertainty to discard rather than select samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The curation mechanism's broad applicability across all generative models (CTGAN, TVAE, SMOTE, GReaT, etc.) makes it a potentially reusable component for any synthetic data pipeline, and the hardness proxy (proportion of discarded samples predicting downstream AUC) offers a practical, low-cost signal for flagging poor-quality synthetic datasets before investing in model training. The finding that the largest gains occur for underrepresented demographic subgroups (e.g., age ≤40, ethnic minorities with 0–3 samples in D_train) has direct equity implications for healthcare and LMIC settings. Limitations include dependence on access to a capable LLM API (cost, availability in LMICs), the fact that curation does not directly address bias in generated data, and that performance gains, while consistent, are moderate (typically 1–5 AUC points over uncurated baselines). Open questions include whether better prompting or fine-tuning strategies could further close the gap to D_oracle, and whether the curation thresholds generalize across domains without per-dataset tuning.
