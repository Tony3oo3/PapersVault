## Problem

Existing analyses of minimum-norm interpolators in overparameterized regression are largely confined to Hilbert spaces (where closed-form solutions exist) or rely critically on Gaussian covariate assumptions, making them difficult to generalize. This paper asks whether the generalization properties of minimum-norm interpolators in arbitrary Banach spaces can be governed by well-known geometric invariants from the local theory of Banach spaces, such as uniform convexity, cotype, and K-convexity.

## Input / Output

The framework takes n i.i.d. samples (X_i, Y_i) from a well-specified model Y = f*(X) + ξ with ξ ~ N(0,1), a Banach space (B(X), ‖·‖) with a specified norm, and a symmetric convex function class F (the unit ball). It produces the minimum-norm interpolator f̂_n = argmin{‖f‖ : f interpolates all n observations) and two theoretical guarantees: an upper bound on the structural error T₁ (bias plus variance of the conditional expectation) expressed via the MM* estimate and Gaussian complexity, and a lower bound on the expected conditional variance T₂ expressed via the K-convexity constant and a spike-interpolation quantity Ψ_n. Notably, no closed-form solution for f̂_n is required, and the covariates need not be Gaussian.

## Method

The paper decomposes the MSE into a structural error T₁ (bias + Var[E[f̂_n|X]]) and a noise-effect error T₂ (E[Var[f̂_n|X]]), then bounds each using distinct geometric properties of the Banach space. For T₁, under 2-uniform convexity (UC(2)) with constant t, the bound is R_MM* · G_n(F)/t, where R_MM* is the MM* estimate of the random projection F_n and G_n(F) is the Gaussian complexity; the proof leverages the inflation factor controlled by uniform smoothness and the signal-retention property implied by UC(2). For T₂, under cotype 2 (CO(2)), a reverse Efron-Stein inequality is established by linearizing the interpolator map via first-degree Hermite polynomials and applying the K-convexity constant to relate the full variance to the marginal contribution of each coordinate, yielding a lower bound in terms of the L₂(P) norm needed to interpolate a single spike. The sharpness of the T₂ bound is confirmed for ℓ_p linear regression (p ∈ [1,2]) under sub-Gaussian covariates, extending prior Gaussian-only results.

## Results

The paper establishes three main theoretical bounds for minimum-norm interpolators in general Banach spaces. Under 2-uniform convexity (UC(2)), Theorem 3.1 shows the structural error (bias + variance of conditional expectation) is bounded by R_MM* · G_n(F) / t, where R_MM* is the MM* estimate, G_n(F) is the Gaussian complexity, and t is the UC(2) constant. Under cotype 2 (CO(2)), Theorem 3.2 provides a reverse Efron-Stein lower bound on the expected conditional variance of order n · Ψ_n(e_1, C·K_{‖·‖}·M_n(F)/(t√n))². For ℓ_p linear regression with sub-Gaussian covariates (p ∈ [1 + C·log log log(d)/log log(d), 2]), Theorem 3.3 shows the expected conditional variance is O(log²(d) · n/d), matching the lower bound and extending prior Gaussian-only results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines. The key comparisons are to prior theoretical results: (1) Theorem 3.1's upper bound aligns with the classical unlocalized ERM risk bound (van de Geer, 2000; Chatterjee, 2014), but extends it to interpolators in general Banach spaces without requiring a closed-form solution. (2) Theorem 3.3 extends Donhauser et al. (2022), which proved tight O(log²(d)·n/d) bounds only under Gaussian covariates, to the broader class of sub-Gaussian covariates — the first such extension in the benign overfitting literature. (3) The lower bound n/d from Theorem 3.2 in the ℓ_p setting matches the lower bound established by Muthukumar et al. (2020). (4) For the edge case p=1 with w*≡0, Wang et al. (2022) show the true rate is 1/log(d/n) rather than n/d, indicating Theorem 3.2's bound is not tight at p=1 and that 2-uniform convexity (or a similar property) is likely essential for sharpness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the benign overfitting literature (Bartlett et al. 2020; Tsigler & Bartlett 2023; Donhauser et al. 2022) that has primarily analyzed minimum-norm interpolators in Hilbert spaces or under Gaussian covariate assumptions, by importing the local theory of Banach spaces (Maurey & Pisier; Pisier 1981, 1999) as a general geometric framework. It departs from prior work in three key ways: (1) the function class need not be linear, (2) the norm need not induce an inner product (i.e., can be far from Hilbert), and (3) covariates need only be sub-Gaussian rather than Gaussian. The MM* estimate from asymptotic geometric analysis (Artstein-Avidan et al. 2015) and the K-convexity constant (Pisier 1981) serve as the central geometric quantities, replacing the eigenvalue-decay or Gaussian-width assumptions that prior tight analyses relied upon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This framework provides the first general geometric lens for understanding when minimum-norm interpolation generalizes well, unifying and extending prior results that were tailored to specific settings (Gaussian covariates, linear models, specific norms). The counterintuitive finding that higher uniform smoothness increases Gaussian complexity (and thus worsens the structural error bound) challenges the naive expectation that smoother norms are always better for interpolation. Key open questions include: whether the UC(2) assumption in the upper bound can be relaxed to CO(2), whether the reverse Efron-Stein bound is tight beyond ℓp-linear regression, and how to handle the p=1 regime where the current lower bound is suboptimal. The Gaussian noise assumption remains essential, limiting applicability to structured noise settings.
