## Problem

The dominant self-supervised objective for protein representation learning—wildtype (WT) mask prediction—does not align with the goal of protein engineering, which is to suggest mutations away from wildtype. The paper demonstrates that increasing WT accuracy beyond ~68% actually degrades downstream mutation effect prediction, and no existing SSL objective serves as a reliable proxy for mutation effect performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240637/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a protein microenvironment defined as all atoms within 20 Å of a masked residue's Cα (excluding the masked residue's own atoms), each encoded by 3D coordinates, atom type, partial charge, and solvent accessible surface area, plus a mutation pair represented as 'from' and 'to' amino acid type embeddings. The output is a continuous rank score (EvoScore) in [-0.5, 0.5] representing the relative evolutionary likelihood of the 'to' versus 'from' amino acid at that position; for zero-shot mutation effect prediction, this score is directly regressed against experimental ΔΔG or activity labels.

## Method

EvoRank reformulates self-supervised protein representation learning as a learning-to-rank problem over the empirical amino acid distribution derived from a protein's MSA. Training is two-stage: first, the graph-transformer backbone (from Diaz et al., 2023) is trained with an MSA soft-label loss that replaces one-hot wildtype targets with the position-specific amino acid distribution from UniRef90 MSAs; second, a Siamese regression head is fine-tuned with the EvoRank loss, which computes pairwise rank labels r = p_MSA(aa⁺)/(p_MSA(aa⁺)+p_MSA(aa⁻)) − 0.5 for all amino acid pairs and minimizes L2 distance between predicted and target rank scores. This forces the model to capture fine-grained evolutionary dynamics of the mutational landscape rather than memorizing wildtype residues, and MSAs are only needed at training time, not inference. The approach yields 42–83% Pearson correlation improvements over the best prior zero-shot structure-based model and narrows the gap to supervised fine-tuned frameworks to ~13% on average.

## Results

On zero-shot thermodynamic stability (ΔΔG) prediction across six datasets (T2837, S669, S-Sym, Myoglobin, FireProtDB, Gβ1), MutRank achieves Pearson correlations of 0.51, 0.40, 0.62, 0.68, 0.51, and 0.62, representing a 66% average improvement over its WT-mask predecessor MutComputeXGT and ~288% and ~72% improvements over ESM2 and ProteinMPNN, respectively. Critically, MutRank's zero-shot performance is within ~13% (Pearson) and ~3% (AUC) of the supervised Stability Oracle on average, making it the first self-supervised framework competitive with supervised fine-tuned models for ΔΔG prediction. On binding free energy and DMS phenotypes (Table 4), MutRank outperforms MutComputeXGT on all 9 datasets for all metrics, and surpasses or ties Stability Oracle on nearly all binding ΔΔG benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ESM2 (Lin et al., 2023), MutRank achieves dramatically higher Pearson correlations on all six ΔΔG datasets (e.g., 0.51 vs. 0.28 on T2837, 0.62 vs. 0.26 on S-Sym), with an average ~288% improvement, and also outperforms ESM2 on all 9 binding/solubility/activity datasets in Table 4. Against ProteinMPNN (Dauparas et al., 2022), MutRank wins on all six ΔΔG datasets with an average ~72% Pearson improvement (e.g., 0.68 vs. 0.35 on Myoglobin). Against its direct predecessor MutComputeXGT (Diaz et al., 2023, WT-mask), MutRank improves Pearson by 66% on average and AUC by 16%, and extends this advantage to all binding free energy and DMS phenotypes. Against the supervised Stability Oracle (Diaz et al., 2023), MutRank zero-shot is within ~13% Pearson and ~3% AUC on average, and on binding ΔΔG datasets (SKEMPIv2, AB-Bind, PlatinumDB) it actually surpasses or ties Stability Oracle on most metrics. Against RaSP (Blaabjerg et al., 2023) and ThermoMPNN (Dieckhaus et al., 2023), both supervised fine-tuned models, MutRank is competitive on AUC (e.g., 0.78 vs. 0.78 for ThermoMPNN on T2837) but trails on Pearson by 14–23% depending on dataset, confirming that EvoRank narrows but does not fully close the supervised gap for ΔΔG.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant WT-mask SSL paradigm (used by ESM2, ProteinMPNN, MutComputeXGT) where the training objective is to recover the wildtype amino acid at a masked position. Rather than using MSA information as a model input (as in MSA Transformer, Tranception) or for sequence reconstruction (as in EVmutation, DeepSequence), EvoRank incorporates MSA-derived amino acid distributions into the training loss itself via a learning-to-rank objective borrowed from the recommendation systems literature. The architecture builds directly on the MutComputeXGT graph-transformer microenvironment framework, with the key modification being a Siamese regression head that predicts pairwise amino-acid rank scores instead of a 20-class classification head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and demonstrates a fundamental misalignment: increasing WT accuracy beyond ~68% actually degrades downstream mutation effect prediction, undermining the field's primary proxy metric for representation quality. EvoRank addresses this by learning the evolutionary rank structure of amino acids at each position, yielding zero-shot performance competitive with supervised fine-tuned models (within ~13% Pearson on ΔΔG) and substantially outperforming ESM2 and ProteinMPNN across all evaluated phenotypes. Key limitations include validation on only one architecture (microenvironment graph transformer), inability to retrain ESM2 or ProteinMPNN with the new loss, and the model-size ablation showing no benefit from scaling beyond ~8M parameters, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to sequence-based or larger structure-based frameworks.
