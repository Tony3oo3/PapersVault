## Problem

Pre-trained diffusion models produce samples whose distribution deviates from the true data distribution due to mismatches between learned and true transition kernels at each timestep. Existing remedies either require expensive retraining of the diffusion model or simply increase the number of function evaluations (NFEs) uniformly across all samples. The paper asks whether one can correct this per-timestep kernel mismatch at inference time, using a well-trained model as a fixed proposal, without any retraining of the diffusion backbone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241505/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DiffRS takes a frozen pre-trained diffusion model (e.g., EDM, Stable Diffusion v1.5, DiT-XL/2) together with a noise sample from the prior and an optional conditioning signal (class label or text prompt), and outputs a single generated image. The NFE is variable per sample because rejections trigger additional forward/reverse steps, so the reported NFE is an average over the evaluation set. The method is resolution-agnostic and has been demonstrated at 32×32 (CIFAR-10), 64×64 (ImageNet), 256×256 (ImageNet), and 512×512 (COCO text-to-image).

## Method

DiffRS applies rejection sampling sequentially at every reverse-diffusion timestep, using the pre-trained Gaussian transition kernel p_{t|t+1}^θ as the proposal and the true (non-Gaussian) transition kernel q_{t|t+1} as the target. The acceptance probability reduces to a ratio of marginal density ratios L_t(x_t)/L_{t+1}(x_{t+1}), where L_t = q_t/p_t^θ is estimated by a time-dependent discriminator trained with a binary cross-entropy loss to distinguish real from generated samples at each noise level. When a sample is rejected, a re-initialization procedure applies one or more forward (noising) steps to the rejected sample and performs additional marginal rejection sampling to decide how far back to go, adapting the noise intensity per sample rather than restarting from the prior. A theoretical analysis proves that training the discriminator tightens the KL-divergence upper bound between the data and refined distributions, with the bound reaching zero under the optimal discriminator.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, DiffRS achieves a new SOTA FID of 1.59 (unconditional) and 1.52 (class-conditional) with an average of 64.06 and 88.22 NFEs respectively, outperforming the best prior methods (EDM+DG at 1.78/1.66) by a substantial margin. On class-conditional ImageNet 64×64, DiffRS reaches FID 1.26 with only 273.93 NFEs, approaching the SOTA of RIN (FID 1.23) which requires 1000 NFEs. On ImageNet 256×256 with DiT-XL/2, DiffRS achieves FID 1.76 with 306.88 NFEs, surpassing DG (FID 1.83 at 375 NFEs). On Stable Diffusion v1.5 (512×512), DiffRS reduces FID from 15.90 (DDIM, 100 NFEs) to 13.46 (166.95 NFEs) while maintaining comparable CLIP scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffRS consistently outperforms all same-checkpoint baselines on CIFAR-10: it improves FID from 2.01 (EDM Heun, 35 NFEs) to 1.59 (64 NFEs), beating EDM+DG (1.78) and EDM+Restart (1.95) by 0.19 and 0.36 FID respectively. On ImageNet 64×64, DiffRS (FID 1.26, 274 NFEs) approaches RIN (FID 1.23, 1000 NFEs) while using only ~27% of the NFEs, and clearly surpasses EDM+DG (1.38) and EDM+Restart (1.37) at comparable or lower NFEs. On ImageNet 256×256, DiffRS applied to DG achieves FID 1.76 at 307 NFEs, outperforming DG alone (1.83 at 375 NFEs) and DDPM (2.30 at 250 NFEs). For text-to-image generation with Stable Diffusion v1.5, DiffRS reduces FID from 15.90 (DDIM, 100 NFEs) to 13.46 (167 NFEs) at the same CLIP score, whereas simply doubling DDIM's NFEs to 200 only yields FID 15.29. DiffRS also improves upon DPM-Solver++ and Consistency Distillation base samplers at matched NFEs, confirming its generality across fast and distilled samplers.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffRS extends the line of work on refining the sampling process of pre-trained diffusion models without retraining the backbone. It builds directly on DG (Kim et al., 2023), which uses a time-dependent discriminator to adjust the transition kernel mean, and on Restart (Xu et al., 2023), which injects noise at fixed intervals to reduce accumulated error. The key conceptual departure is applying classical rejection sampling to the *transition kernels* at each timestep rather than modifying the sampling mean or using fixed restart schedules: the discriminator estimates a density ratio that yields an acceptance probability, and a novel re-initialization procedure (using the forward process plus marginal rejection) avoids the prohibitive cost of restarting from the prior. This transforms the discriminator's role from a gradient-based correction signal into a probabilistic accept/reject criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiffRS provides a plug-and-play sampling refinement that works across architectures (EDM, DiT, Stable Diffusion), samplers (Heun, SDE, DPM-Solver++, DDIM, Consistency Distillation), and scales (32×32 to 512×512), achieving SOTA on CIFAR-10 and near-SOTA on ImageNet 64×64 with fewer NFEs than prior methods. The theoretical guarantee—that discriminator training tightens the KL divergence bound—is clean and interpretable. Open questions include whether the heuristic estimation of the rejection constant M_t (via percentile) can be made principled, whether the method handles distribution shift between the pre-trained model's learned distribution and the true data distribution, and whether more sophisticated MCMC schemes (e.g., Metropolis-Hastings) could further reduce the NFE overhead from rejections.
