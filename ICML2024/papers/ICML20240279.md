## Problem

Existing privacy-preserving Byzantine-robust federated learning (PBFL) schemes built on Paillier or CKKS homomorphic encryption suffer from three critical limitations: low computational efficiency (requiring log₂N rotations for inner products and norms), exposure of the final model to the server, and rigid coupling to a single aggregation algorithm that makes them vulnerable to AGR-tailored poisoning attacks. No prior system simultaneously achieves high efficiency, client-only model ownership, and the flexibility to switch aggregation rules.

## Input / Output

Each client flattens its locally trained model into a vector (padded to a multiple of N=8192), encodes it via two complementary polynomial packing functions (pm1 and pm2), and uploads two CKKS ciphertexts to server S₁. The system outputs an encrypted global model under a client-side public key (pk_c) that only honest clients can decrypt, along with per-client trust scores computed from decrypted inner products, L₂ norms, and mean values. The model vector length is 72,710 parameters (HAR) or 101,770 parameters (MNIST), and the entire pipeline requires only 2 levels of ciphertext multiplication depth.

## Method

The key insight is that all state-of-the-art robust aggregation algorithms (FLAME, ShieldFL, FLTrust, FheFL) reduce to computing inner products, L₂ norms, and mean values of N-dimensional vectors. The paper designs a packing scheme where pm1(a) = ΣaᵢXⁱ and pm2(a) = -ΣaᵢX^(N-i) in the ring ℤ[X]/(X^N+1), so that a single polynomial multiplication pm1(a)·pm2(b) places the inner product ⟨a,b⟩ in the constant term, eliminating the log₂N rotations required by standard CKKS SIMD encoding. Two non-colluding servers (S₁ holding ciphertexts, S₂ holding the secret key) cooperate to compute these quantities via a compressed protocol that transmits only the constant term plus one polynomial component, reducing communication from 2N·log₂Q_L to (N+1)·log₂Q_L bits. A consistency check using a random vector r verifies correct packing before aggregation, and a secure key conversion step (adding a random polynomial r before re-encryption under pk_c) ensures the final model is inaccessible to servers and malicious clients. This architecture supports flexible AGR switching—M-FLAME, ShieldFL, and others—without modifying the encryption layer.

## Results

On MNIST (iid, 30 clients), M-FLAME under AegisFL's ciphertext maintains ~95% testing accuracy across all three attack types even with 12/30 malicious clients, while FedAvg drops to 43% under untargeted attack and suffers 97–99% backdoor success under scaling attack. Under scaling attack, M-FLAME reduces backdoor success rate from ~97% (FedAvg) to ~12–17%, and ShieldFL achieves ~14–52%. Ciphertext accuracy is on par with or marginally exceeds plaintext (e.g., M-FLAME ciphertext: 94.8–95.6% vs. plaintext: 94.5–95.3% on MNIST), confirming that approximate HE at Δ=2⁴⁰ does not degrade model quality. Computationally, the proposed packing scheme achieves ~24× speedup over original CKKS for inner products and L₂ norms, and ~30× for mean values.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017), M-FLAME under AegisFL dramatically outperforms on all attack types: on MNIST with 12 malicious clients, M-FLAME ciphertext retains 95.0% accuracy under untargeted attack versus FedAvg's 42.9%, and reduces scaling-attack backdoor success from 98.1% to 17.1%. Against ShieldFL (Ma et al., 2022), M-FLAME achieves higher accuracy under scaling attack on MNIST (95.0% vs. 89.1% testing accuracy; 17.1% vs. 37.5% backdoor success with 9 attackers), while ShieldFL is competitive on untargeted and label-flipping attacks. Against the original CKKS-based scheme of Miao et al. (2022), AegisFL achieves ~24× speedup in computing inner products and L₂ norms and ~30× for mean values by replacing log₂N rotations with a single polynomial multiplication, while communication volume is roughly equivalent (all N slots usable vs. N/2 in standard CKKS). Against Paillier-based ShieldFL in communication, AegisFL's traffic is significantly lower. Overall, M-FLAME is the strongest AGR across both datasets and all three attack types, and the ciphertext results confirm that the HE overhead introduces no measurable accuracy loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the line of privacy-preserving Byzantine-robust federated learning (PBFL) systems that use homomorphic encryption to simultaneously defend against privacy and poisoning attacks, including Paillier-based schemes (PEFL, ShieldFL, Lu et al.) and CKKS-based schemes (Miao et al., FheFL). It departs from these by introducing a custom polynomial packing scheme (inspired by Yasuda et al., 2014) that exploits the ring structure of RLWE-based HE to compute inner products, L2 norms, and mean values with a single ciphertext multiplication, eliminating the log₂(N) rotations required by standard CKKS SIMD packing. The paper also adapts FLAME's clipping formula to be compatible with its two-level multiplication budget, and positions itself as the first PBFL system where the aggregation rule (AGR) can be flexibly swapped without redesigning the cryptographic protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The flexibility to switch AGRs is practically important because no single robust aggregation algorithm dominates across all attack types and data distributions, and a fixed AGR is vulnerable to tailored poisoning attacks. The 24–30× speedup over standard CKKS for the core similarity computations makes HE-based PBFL substantially more deployable in cross-silo settings. However, the scheme remains incompatible with statistics-based AGRs (coordinate-wise median, trimmed mean) and feature-extraction-based AGRs (e.g., top singular vector), which the authors acknowledge as a significant open problem. The two-server, non-colluding assumption and the requirement for a trusted Key Center also limit practical deployment scenarios.
