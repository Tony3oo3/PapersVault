## Problem

Existing research on Limited Attribute Observation (LAO) in linear regression has focused almost exclusively on univariate outcomes with missing features, while in practice both outcomes and features are often simultaneously partially observed, especially in multivariate settings (e.g., longitudinal, panel, or image data). This paper addresses the gap by developing algorithms and theoretical guarantees for multivariate linear regression where the learner can observe only a limited subset of both feature and outcome coordinates at each time step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241920/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each time step t, the algorithm receives a p-dimensional feature vector x_t and a q-dimensional outcome vector y_t but is constrained to observe only p0 ≤ p feature coordinates and q0 ≤ q outcome coordinates, chosen adaptively by the algorithm. The output is a p×q weight matrix W (averaged over T iterations) that maps full feature vectors to multivariate outcome predictions. The observation budget is fixed in advance, and the sampling of which coordinates to observe is part of the algorithm's design rather than an external missingness mechanism.

## Method

The paper proposes four online algorithms: two for L2 loss (ridge and sparse-group-lasso regularized) and two for L1 loss (ridge and sparse-group-lasso regularized). The key technical contribution for L2 loss is an unbiased gradient estimator that samples p0−1 feature coordinates uniformly, q0 outcome coordinates uniformly, and one additional feature coordinate with probability proportional to the squared row norm of the current weight matrix, yielding a PAC guarantee with excess error O(√(pq/(T(p0−1)q0))). For L1 loss, the non-differentiability of the absolute value loss necessitates subgradient methods, which introduce an irreducible bias term 2√q·BB_x·√(1−p0/p) that prevents PAC guarantees when p0 < p. The Lasso variants use a FOBOS-style proximal update with a closed-form sparse-group-lasso proximal mapping (Proposition 3.2) that simultaneously performs element-wise and row-wise shrinkage.

## Results

On synthetic data (q=5, q₀=2, p=20, p₀=10), the proposed LSR method achieves mean prediction errors of 25.08 (σ²=5, T=10⁴) and 16.31 (σ²=5, T=5×10⁴), substantially outperforming the multivariate AERR baseline (AERR1: 37.41 and 22.19; AERR2: 36.77 and 21.52). Under outlier contamination (20% Cauchy noise), LADR (L₁ loss) reduces error to 29.96 vs. LSR's 79.66 at p₀=10, T=10⁴, demonstrating robustness. On the Yeast Cell dataset (p=106, q=18, T=542), LSR consistently achieves lower mean prediction error than multivariate AERR across all (p₀, q₀) settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the 'multivariate AERR,' which applies Hazan & Koren's (2012) univariate AERR algorithm independently to each outcome coordinate. LSR (Algorithm 1) outperforms AERR1 (same sample size T) by approximately 33% in mean prediction error at T=10⁴, σ²=5 (25.08 vs. 37.41), and by about 26% at T=5×10⁴ (16.31 vs. 22.19). Even against AERR2, which uses a larger q₀ to match total observation count, LSR maintains a clear advantage (16.31 vs. 21.52 at T=5×10⁴, σ²=5). On the Yeast Cell real dataset, LSR consistently produces lower mean prediction error than multivariate AERR across all tested (p₀, q₀) combinations. The batch imputation method Imp1 (mice) achieves the lowest error overall but requires full dataset access and is not directly comparable to the online algorithms. Under outlier contamination, LADR (L₁ loss, Algorithm 3) dramatically outperforms LSR (29.96 vs. 79.66 at p₀=10, T=10⁴), and even surpasses LSR0 (LSR on clean data) in some settings, highlighting the robustness benefit of the L₁ loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Limited Attribute Observation (LAO) framework from Hazan & Koren (2012) and Bullins et al. (2016), which addressed univariate outcomes with partially observed features, to the multivariate outcome setting where both features and outcomes are simultaneously partially observed. The L2-loss algorithm (Algorithm 1) generalizes the AERR procedure by constructing an unbiased gradient estimator that jointly handles sampling over both feature and outcome coordinates, while the Lasso-type variant (Algorithm 2) adapts the FOBOS proximal gradient framework of Duchi & Singer (2009) to the multivariate, partially-observed setting. The paper also subsumes the results of Cesa-Bianchi et al. (2011) and Bullins et al. (2016) as special cases (q=q₀=1), providing a unified theoretical treatment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean separation between PAC-learnability for L2 loss and the impossibility of PAC for L1 loss when features are partially observed (the persistent 2√q·BB_x·√(1−p₀/p) gap) provides a sharp theoretical boundary that practitioners should consider when choosing loss functions under observation constraints. The empirical demonstration that a joint multivariate algorithm substantially outperforms naive per-coordinate application of univariate methods validates the value of exploiting cross-outcome structure. Open questions include whether the L1 gap can be closed by observing a fraction of samples fully, whether the iid assumption can be relaxed for longitudinal or panel data, and how these ideas extend beyond linear models to deep or ensemble methods.
