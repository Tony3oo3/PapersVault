## Problem

When the true causal time lag is much shorter than the observational interval, temporal aggregation causes time-delayed causality to appear as instantaneous dependence, and practitioners routinely apply non-temporal causal discovery methods to such aggregated i.i.d. data. However, it remained unclear whether the instantaneous causal structure recovered from aggregated data is actually consistent with the true underlying time-delayed causal structure, particularly in general (nonlinear) settings where prior analyses were limited to linear models or imposed restrictive noise assumptions.

## Input / Output

The input is temporally aggregated data formed by summing (or averaging) k consecutive observations from an underlying VAR(1) process, treated as i.i.d. when k is large. The output is a characterization of when standard causal discovery methods—FCM-based (LiNGAM, ANM), constraint-based (PC, FCI), and score-based (GES)—can correctly recover the summary graph structure and causal direction from this aggregated data. The paper provides necessary and sufficient conditions (involving conditional density functions of the aggregated variables) rather than a single algorithm, and identifies partial linearity as a sufficient condition for recoverability.

## Method

The paper introduces two formal consistency notions: functional consistency (the aggregated model preserves the additive-noise functional form in the correct causal direction) and conditional independence consistency (the aggregated data satisfies the Markov condition with respect to the summary graph). For functional consistency, they derive the unique construction of the aggregated causal function as a conditional expectation (analogous to Rao-Blackwellization) and show that even in the linear non-Gaussian case, the central limit theorem causes the aggregated noise to become Gaussian, destroying identifiability. For conditional independence consistency, they prove that collider structures naturally preserve CI consistency under faithfulness, while chain and fork structures do not in general; they then establish a necessary and sufficient condition expressed as an integral equation over conditional densities, and show that partial linearity in either the X→Y or Y→Z mechanism is sufficient to restore consistency. Five simulation experiments validate these theoretical results and demonstrate that aggregation severely degrades all three method families in fully nonlinear settings, while a skeleton prior can rescue the PC algorithm's v-structure identification.

## Results

In the linear non-Gaussian case, Direct LiNGAM's correct rate drops from ~100% at k=1 to ~50% (random-guess baseline) as the aggregation factor k increases from 3 to 30, demonstrating that temporal aggregation destroys non-Gaussianity needed for identifiability. For conditional independence consistency (fork structure, k=2), the key test X̄⊥Z̄|Ȳ achieves the correct 5% rejection rate under linear+linear and partial-linearity settings, but jumps to 58% under fully nonlinear conditions, confirming the theoretical prediction that chain/fork structures lose CI consistency without linearity. On 4-variable nonlinear data, PC and FCI accuracy collapses on aggregated data while GES retains ~70% accuracy, though this advantage disappears when noise variances are perturbed (dropping below 30%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates six existing causal discovery methods (PC, FCI, GES, Direct LiNGAM, ANM, and kernel CI test) on temporally aggregated data to characterize their failure modes rather than proposing a new method. Direct LiNGAM and ANM both degrade to random-guess performance as k grows, with ANM deteriorating faster (k range 0–10 vs. 0–50 for LiNGAM) because aggregation disrupts the additive noise structure that ANM relies on. Among constraint/score-based methods on 4-variable nonlinear data, PC and FCI suffer the largest accuracy collapse on aggregated data, while GES retains ~70% accuracy—though this advantage is shown to be fragile, dropping below 30% when independent noise variances are perturbed. The kernel CI test confirms the theoretical predictions: partial linearity in either the X→Y or Y→Z mechanism is sufficient to restore the correct 5% rejection rate for the key conditional independence, while fully nonlinear settings yield 58% (fork) or 18% (chain) rejection rates. A trivial solution is demonstrated: providing the correct skeleton as prior knowledge to PC on aggregated data recovers the correct Markov equivalence class, since collider structures (v-structures) retain CI consistency even in the fully nonlinear case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work by Fisher (1970) and Gong et al. (2017) on the correspondence between simultaneous (instantaneous) models and true time-delayed causal processes. Fisher provided a correspondence principle under restrictive fixed-noise assumptions, and Gong et al. showed functional consistency is preserved under temporal aggregation in the linear VAR case. This paper generalizes both to the fully nonlinear setting and, crucially, introduces two formal consistency notions—functional consistency for FCM-based methods and conditional independence consistency for constraint-based methods—that had not been previously defined or analyzed in the general case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that a widely-invoked justification for instantaneous causal models (temporal aggregation) actually undermines the validity of standard causal discovery methods in general nonlinear settings, with results potentially 'seriously distorted.' The practical implication is significant: practitioners working with aggregated observational data (common in economics, neuroscience, and social sciences) should interpret causal discovery outputs with caution. The main open question is constructive—how to actually recover correct causal structure from aggregated data—since the paper diagnoses the problem but provides no general solution beyond the trivial prior-knowledge workaround. Future work on aggregation-aware skeleton discovery or aggregation-aware FCM formulations is clearly motivated.
