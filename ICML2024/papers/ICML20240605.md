## Problem

While both simplicity bias (low-rank features) and sharpness minimization (flat loss landscape) are recognized as implicit biases of SGD, the causal relationship between these two notions of 'simplicity' remains unexplored in non-linear settings. Moreover, the convergence of the Riemannian gradient flow of the trace of Hessian on the manifold of zero loss was previously established only for quadratically overparametrized linear networks, leaving open whether label noise SGD globally minimizes sharpness for general non-linear activations.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a two-layer network with fixed unit second-layer weights, consuming a training set of unit-norm vectors {(x_i, y_i)}_{i=1}^n, an activation φ satisfying strict positivity/convexity of φ' and φ'' (or the cube activation x³), and a data coherence condition X^T X ≥ μI. The output is a parameter vector θ* where every neuron's pre-activation on every data point equals φ^{-1}(y_i/m), yielding a rank-one feature matrix Θ*X, with an explicit convergence time of Õ(log(1/ε)·η^{-2}σ^{-2}) iterations for label noise SGD with sufficiently small step size η.

## Method

The paper proves that label noise SGD, in the small-step-size limit, follows a Riemannian gradient flow of the trace of Hessian on the manifold of zero loss, and that this flow converges globally and exponentially to the unique stationary point where all neurons collapse to a single vector. The key technical tool is a novel local geodesic convexity property: at approximate stationary points (where the manifold gradient norm is below √μ·β), the Hessian of Tr D²L on the manifold is positive semidefinite, established by bounding the covariant-derivative projection term using a β-normality condition on the activation (βφ'' ≤ φ'²φ'''). The convergence proof proceeds in two phases—constant-rate descent far from stationarity, then exponential decay via a Lyapunov argument on the squared gradient norm once the flow enters the g-convex region—yielding the first global linear convergence result for the gradient flow of trace of Hessian on a manifold of minimizers.

## Results

The paper establishes that label noise SGD with sufficiently small step size η converges to the global minimizer of the trace of Hessian on the zero-loss manifold in T = Õ(log(1/ε)·η⁻²σ⁻²) iterations (Theorem 1.1). The convergence is exponential: the Riemannian gradient flow reaches ε-stationarity after time t ≥ Tr(D²L(θ(0)))/(μβ²) + log(β²/(ϱ₁²ϱ₂²ε²)∨1)/(ϱ₁ϱ₂μ) (Theorem 3.5). At the global minimizer, all m neurons collapse to the same pre-activation vector θ_j*ᵀx_i = φ⁻¹(y_i/m), yielding a rank-one feature matrix (Theorem 3.3). Empirically, the second and third eigenvalues of the feature matrix converge to zero in the high-dimensional regime (d > n), and in the low-dimensional regime (d < n) neurons cluster into a small number of vectors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no direct numerical benchmarking against competing optimization methods. The key comparison is against prior theoretical results: Li et al. (2021) proved only local convergence of the sharpness gradient flow for quadratically overparametrized linear networks, while this paper establishes the first global linear convergence result for the gradient flow of the trace of Hessian on manifolds of minimizers for nonlinear two-layer networks. Blanc et al. (2020) characterized stationary points of the trace of Hessian only for 1-D two-layer ReLU/sigmoid networks trained on a single data point; this paper extends the characterization to arbitrary high-dimensional data (d ≥ n) with general activations satisfying φ′,φ″>0. The paper also subsumes the cube activation (x³) which does not satisfy the strict positivity assumption, by providing a separate proof (Lemma G.4). No empirical comparison against SAM, 1-SAM, or other sharpness-aware methods is provided beyond noting that the theoretical guarantees apply to them as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sharpness-minimization implicit bias framework of Li et al. (2021), who characterized the limiting dynamics of label noise SGD as a Riemannian gradient flow of the trace of Hessian on the zero-loss manifold but only proved convergence for quadratically overparametrized linear nets. It also builds on Blanc et al. (2020), who showed local divergence from non-stationary points but only for 1-D two-layer ReLU networks on a single data point. The key departure is providing the first global convergence proof for this flow in a genuinely non-linear setting, and—critically—showing that the resulting minimizers exhibit rank-one feature structure, thereby bridging the previously disconnected notions of parameter-space flatness and feature-space simplicity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a fundamental open question by proving that sharpness minimization provably implies simplicity bias (rank-one features) for two-layer networks with activations satisfying φ′, φ″ > 0 or the cube activation, in the high-dimensional regime d ≥ n. The novel geodesic convexity property of the trace of Hessian at approximate stationary points is a reusable geometric tool that may apply to other manifold-constrained optimization problems. Key limitations include restriction to two-layer networks, specific activation classes, and the high-dimensional setting; the low-dimensional regime (d < n) where neurons cluster rather than fully collapse remains theoretically uncharacterized, and extension to deeper networks is left as future work.
