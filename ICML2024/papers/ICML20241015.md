## Problem

Weight decay is ubiquitous in deep learning optimization, yet its mechanism for modern networks with normalization layers remains poorly understood, particularly for adaptive optimizers like AdamW and Lion. While prior work identified weight decay as an 'effective learning rate' scaler for scale-invariant weights under SGD, the equilibrium dynamics for other optimizers, the role of balanced rotation across neurons and layers, and the reason AdamW outperforms Adam+ℓ2 have not been explained.

## Input / Output

The analysis operates at the individual neuron level, treating each weight vector ω ∈ ℝ^C independently with its incoming activation vector and bias. The analytical framework assumes noise-dominated gradients (random walk setting) and produces closed-form predictions for the equilibrium weight norm, expected angular update size η_r, and RMS update size η_g that depend only on hyperparameters (learning rate, weight decay, momentum, dimension C) rather than on time-varying quantities like gradient magnitude. The Rotational Variant (RV) wrapper takes any inner optimizer's update components, projects them to be orthogonal to the weight vector and the all-ones vector, normalizes the angular update to a target equilibrium value, and rescales the weight to a fixed magnitude each step.

## Method

The paper derives a geometric equilibrium model where the Total Update Contribution (TUC) of gradients (u) and weight decay (d) balance in expectation, yielding a Pythagorean relationship that determines the steady-state weight norm. Applying this to AdamW, SGDM, Lion, and Adam+ℓ2 produces closed-form expressions showing that AdamW's equilibrium rotation depends only on η, λ, and β₁ (not gradient magnitude), while Adam+ℓ2's rotation depends on the gradient norm, causing imbalanced rotation across layers. The key empirical finding is that this balanced rotation is what makes AdamW superior to Adam+ℓ2 and explains the benefit of Weight Standardization. The paper further introduces Rotational Optimizer Variants (Algorithm 1) that directly enforce the equilibrium angular update without weight decay, eliminating the transient phase and substantially reducing the need for learning rate warmup while matching baseline performance.

## Results

The Rotational Variant (RV-AdamW) matches or slightly exceeds the AdamW baseline across most tasks without any weight decay: 92.4% vs 92.2% top-1 on CIFAR-10 ResNet-20, 72.5% vs 72.1% on ImageNet-1k DeiT-tiny, and 3.18 vs 3.18 loss on OpenWebText GPT2-124M. Removing weight decay from AdamW (λ=0) causes significant degradation (e.g., 72.1→69.3% on ImageNet, unstable GPT2 training), confirming that the benefits of weight decay come from controlling angular updates rather than explicit regularization. On IWSLT2014, the zero-shot RV underperforms (20.0 vs 34.6 BLEU) due to insufficient weight decay in the baseline, but light tuning (few-shot) recovers 34.7 BLEU. The paper also shows that AdamW outperforms Adam+ℓ2 by ~0.5% on CIFAR-10 ResNet-18, attributed to balanced vs imbalanced rotation across layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AdamW (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2019), the RV-AdamW matches or slightly exceeds baseline performance in zero-shot transfer (92.4% vs 92.2% on CIFAR-10, 72.5% vs 72.1% on ImageNet-1k, 3.18 vs 3.18 loss on OpenWebText), demonstrating that explicit angular update control is sufficient to replicate the benefits of weight decay. Against Adam+ℓ2 (Kingma & Ba, 2015), AdamW achieves ~0.5% higher validation accuracy on CIFAR-10 ResNet-18; the paper attributes this gap to balanced rotation in AdamW versus imbalanced rotation in Adam+ℓ2, where per-layer rotation varies by a factor of ~30× (corresponding to ~1000× in effective learning rate). Against Weight Standardization (Qiao et al., 2019), both WS and RV training significantly outperform standard Layer-Normalized training on CIFAR-100 ResNet-18, with WS balancing rotation at the neuron level while RV does so at the layer level. The paper further shows that the RVs reduce the need for learning rate warmup: GPT2-124M without warmup degrades significantly with AdamW but remains stable with RV-AdamW, and ResNet-50 on ImageNet shows similar (though smaller) warmup sensitivity differences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Spherical Motion Dynamics (SMD) framework of Wan et al. (2021), which derived rotational equilibrium for SGD with weight decay, to adaptive optimizers (AdamW, Lion, Adam+ℓ2) and introduces the concept of 'balanced rotation' as a unifying lens. It builds on the scale-invariance analysis of Van Laarhoven (2017) and Chiley et al. (2019) but departs by targeting neuron-level update dynamics rather than layer-level, and by connecting the equilibrium analysis to the LARS/LAMB family of relative optimizers. The Rotational Variants (RVs) proposed here are positioned as a controlled experimental tool that isolates the steady-state equilibrium behavior, distinguishing them from prior projection-based methods (AdamP, PathSGD) that operate on the union of scale-invariant weights rather than per-neuron.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean mechanistic explanation for why AdamW outperforms Adam+ℓ2 (balanced vs. imbalanced rotation) and why Weight Standardization aids training, two phenomena that were empirically well-known but poorly understood. The RVs offer a practical path to eliminating learning rate warmup and transient-phase instability, which could simplify training recipes for large-scale models. Open questions include whether balanced rotation is truly causal (vs. correlated) with performance, whether the random-walk analysis holds at very large scale or with complex architectures, and whether RVs can be tuned to outperform rather than merely match standard optimizers. The analysis is derived under a noise-dominated random-walk assumption, and while empirical validation is strong, the gap to a full theoretical treatment of real training dynamics remains.
