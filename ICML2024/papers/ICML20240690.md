## Problem

Legacy numerical PDE solvers are mathematically rigorous but cannot leverage big-data knowledge or adapt to specific PDE formulations, while existing neural PDE solvers treat networks as black boxes, rely on supervised losses that restrict the solution space, and lack guarantees of convergence or correctness on unseen geometries and boundary conditions. This paper bridges the gap by embedding the mathematical backbone of multigrid methods into a learnable neural architecture with provable convergence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240690/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The solver operates iteratively on 2D grids (tested from 257×257 up to 4097×4097), consuming the PDE source term f, Dirichlet boundary values b, a binary boundary mask M (encoding which grid points are boundary vs. interior), and problem-specific coefficient fields (e.g., spatially varying wavenumber k², velocity field v, or diffusion/reaction constants α, β). At each iteration it takes the current solution estimate u and produces a refined u, with boundary values enforced at every step via element-wise multiplication with M. Training is self-supervised: only (M, b) pairs with zero f-fields and simple 'donut-like' geometries are needed, yet the solver generalizes to arbitrary complex geometries, topologies, and non-zero source terms at test time.

## Method

UGrid integrates the U-Net encoder-decoder structure with the multigrid V-cycle: a fixed masked convolutional smoother (derived from the Jacobi iteration for each PDE type) handles high-frequency error, while a recursive UGrid submodule with learnable bias-free 2D convolutions (no activations or normalization, preserving linearity) handles low-frequency error across coarsened grids. The iteration follows pre-smooth → residual computation → UGrid correction → apply correction → post-smooth, with the UGrid submodule invoked recursively at 2× coarser resolutions. A novel residual loss L = E‖(1−M)(f − Ax)‖₂ replaces the legacy mean-relative-error loss, enabling self-supervised training, eliminating numerical oscillation at convergence, and guaranteeing input-independent final accuracy. Convergence is proven via spectral-radius analysis of the masked update matrix (Theorem 4.1), showing that if the prototype Jacobi iterator converges and P is full-rank diagonal, the masked iterator converges to the ground-truth solution.

## Results

On large-scale Poisson problems (1,050,625×1,050,625 systems), UGrid reaches relative residual error ≤10⁻⁴ in 10–20 ms, outperforming AMGCL (128–424 ms), NVIDIA AmgX (64–175 ms), and Hsieh et al. (20–58 ms, where it converges). UGrid's final relative residual errors range from 2.66×10⁻⁵ to 9.88×10⁻⁵, comparable to or better than the legacy AMG solvers. On XL-scale (4.2M×4.2M) and XXL-scale (16.8M×16.8M) Poisson problems, UGrid maintains its 5–20× speed advantage over AMGCL and AmgX without retraining, while AmgX diverges on several XXL-scale testcases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UGrid decisively outperforms all three baselines on large-scale Poisson problems. Against AMGCL, UGrid is 10–20× faster (e.g., 10.09 ms vs. 270.33 ms on Cat) with comparable or better final residual errors. Against NVIDIA AmgX, UGrid is 5–10× faster (e.g., 10.55 ms vs. 67.60 ms on Lock), and AmgX diverges entirely on several XL- and XXL-scale testcases. Against the prior neural solver of Hsieh et al. (2019), UGrid is 2–5× faster on the few cases where Hsieh converges, while Hsieh diverges on 7 out of 10 testcases (errors reaching 24,883×10⁻⁵ vs. UGrid's ≤9.88×10⁻⁵). On Helmholtz and diffusion-convection-reaction problems, UGrid similarly achieves 5–20× speedups over both AMG solvers, with AmgX diverging on the Sharp Feature Helmholtz case. The key differentiator is generalization: UGrid was trained only on 'Donut-like' geometries with zero f-fields, yet converges on all unseen complex geometries, topologies, and noisy inputs where Hsieh et al. fails.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Hsieh et al. (2019), which first integrated multigrid V-cycle structure with U-Net to obtain a neural PDE solver with convergence guarantees, by generalizing the convolutional operators to handle arbitrary boundary conditions and multiple PDE formulations (Helmholtz, convection-diffusion-reaction) without modifying the core iteration. It also departs from the broader neural PDE solver literature (PINNs, black-box CNNs) by embedding the mathematical backbone of multigrid directly into the network architecture, ensuring every layer has a corresponding multigrid counterpart. The proposed residual loss metric replaces the standard supervised loss that compares predictions to ground-truth solutions, enabling self-supervised training and provably input-independent convergence accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UGrid provides one of the first neural PDE solvers with both a convergence proof and a correctness proof, while simultaneously demonstrating strong generalization to unseen geometries, topologies, and problem scales (up to 4M×4M systems) without retraining. The residual loss is a practical contribution: it eliminates the need for ground-truth solutions during training and removes the numerical oscillation in relative residual error observed with legacy losses. Key limitations include restriction to linear PDEs (Theorem 4.1 does not extend to non-linear cases), 2D grids only, and no mathematical guarantee on convergence speed, which means small-scale problems may not benefit as much. Extending the framework to non-linear PDEs and 3D problems remains the primary open question.
