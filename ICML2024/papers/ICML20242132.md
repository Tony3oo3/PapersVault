## Problem

Classical gradient sketching methods (e.g., random coordinate descent) fail to achieve linear convergence when the regularization term is non-separable, and SEGA requires importance sampling—needing access to the diagonal entries of the smoothness matrix M—to attain its best rate when φ=0. This paper asks whether one can match SEGA's convergence rate without importance sampling while also handling non-separable regularization, and whether the choice of sketching distribution (Gaussian vs. coordinate) fundamentally affects the achievable rate.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes only the sketched directional derivative u^T∇f(x_k) (or its finite-difference approximation in the zero-order setting) and the proximal operator of φ, never requiring the full gradient or the diagonal of M. The step size scales as O(1/tr(M)) rather than O(1/(dL)), and the output is an ε-suboptimal solution x* with iteration complexity O((tr(M)/μ + d) log(1/ε)) for the smooth case, O((dL/μ) log(1/ε)) for general non-separable φ, and O((L·tr(W)/μ) log(1/ε)) when φ has the structured affine-subspace form.

## Method

GSGD replaces the coordinate basis vector e_i used in SEGA with a standard Gaussian vector u ~ N(0, I_d), and introduces a variance-reduction variate h_k updated as h_{k+1} = h_k + (uu^T/(d+2))(∇f(x_k) − h_k). The unbiased gradient estimator g_k = h_k + uu^T(∇f(x_k) − h_k) is then used in a proximal gradient step x_{k+1} = Prox_{αφ}(x_k − ηg_k). The specific choice of denominator d+2 (rather than d or d+O(1)) is critical: it ensures the Lyapunov analysis closes by making the coefficients of ‖h_k‖² and ‖∇f(x_k)‖² in the expected norm recursion both strictly less than 1. Because the Gaussian sketch's second moment involves tr(M) rather than d·λ_max(M), the convergence rate depends on tr(M)/μ—matching SEGA with importance sampling—without ever needing the diagonal of M. When φ satisfies the structured assumption involving an orthogonal projection W, the rate further improves to depend on tr(W) (the rank of W) instead of d, exploiting the low-dimensional affine constraint.

## Results

Theoretically, GSGD achieves an iteration complexity of O((tr(M)/μ) log(1/ε)) when φ=0, matching SEGA with importance sampling but without requiring access to diagonal entries of M. For inseparable regularization, GSGD achieves O((dL/μ) log(1/ε)), and for structured regularization (Assumption 2.3), it achieves O((L·tr(W)/μ) log(1/ε)), where the rate is governed by the rank of the projection matrix W. Empirically, on quadratic problems with d=500, GSGD converges significantly faster than Coordinate-SEGA in three of four Hessian settings (where tr(M)/μ ≪ dL/μ), and performs comparably in the fourth (where tr(M)/μ ≈ dL/μ). On the real-world Appliances Energy Prediction dataset (29 features, 19,735 records), GSGD also converges faster than SEGA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the Coordinate sketching version of SEGA (Hanzely et al., 2018) with uniform sampling. GSGD clearly outperforms SEGA in the first three experimental settings (diagonal matrices with concentrated eigenvalues), where tr(M)/μ is much smaller than dL/μ, resulting in visibly faster convergence in the objective value plots. In the fourth setting (uniformly distributed eigenvalues), where tr(M)/μ ≈ dL/μ, the two methods perform similarly. On the Appliances Energy Prediction linear regression task, GSGD again converges faster than SEGA, consistent with tr(M) ≪ dL for that problem. A key practical advantage is that GSGD does not require importance sampling (i.e., no need to compute coordinate-wise smoothness constants L_i), making it applicable in zero-order settings where only function evaluations are available, whereas SEGA's best theoretical rate depends on such sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient sketching framework initiated by SEGA (Hanzely et al., 2018) and SVRCD (Hanzely & Richtárik, 2019b), which introduced variance-reduced coordinate sketching for composite optimization with inseparable regularization. The key departure is replacing the coordinate sampling vector e_i with a Gaussian vector u ~ N(0, I_d) and a specific 1/(d+2) normalization, which the authors show naturally yields the tr(M)/μ convergence rate that SEGA requires importance sampling to achieve. The analysis also builds on the projection-matrix acceleration framework of Hanzely et al. (2020), extending it from coordinate sketching to Gaussian sketching to exploit low-rank structure in the regularization term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in removing the need to compute diagonal entries of the Hessian (or coordinate-wise smoothness constants), which is infeasible in zero-order settings or when only sketched gradients are accessible. This broadens the applicability of variance-reduced sketching methods to derivative-free optimization and federated learning scenarios. Open questions include whether the Gaussian sketching advantage persists in non-convex or non-strongly-convex regimes, how the method scales when d is extremely large (the d+2 denominator and O(d) per-iteration cost become relevant), and whether the acceleration from projection-matrix structure can be combined with Nesterov-type momentum for further gains.
