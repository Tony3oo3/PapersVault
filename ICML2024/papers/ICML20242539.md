## Problem

Classifier-free guidance in diffusion models produces over-saturated, color-cast, and anatomically irregular images at large guidance scales because its linear combination of conditional and unconditional denoising networks violates the score Fokker-Planck equation at t > 0. No systematic, training-free correction existed that addressed this non-linearity for general sampling tasks beyond RGB pixel-space image generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242539/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy image or latent vector, a condition (text prompt or class label), and a guidance scale ω, and produces a corrected denoising estimate ε_CH that plugs directly into any standard sampler (DDIM, DPM++2M, UniPC, SDE). The non-linear correction Δx is computed at each diffusion step via fixed-point iteration requiring 1–10 additional network evaluations. The projection operator P differs by domain: channel-wise mean for pixel-space RGB models, projection onto the residual vector (ε_uncond − ε_cond)·σ for latent-space models, and identity for low-dimensional non-image data such as the 8×8 magnetization field.

## Method

Under the Harmonic Ansatz (the Laplacian term in the score Fokker-Planck equation is negligible), the FP equation reduces to a quasi-linear PDE solvable by the method of characteristics, yielding an analytical solution where the guided denoising network is evaluated at shifted positions x₁ = x + ωΔx and x₂ = x + (1+ω)Δx rather than at x. The correction Δx is determined by the training-free fixed-point equation Δx = P∘(ε_θ(x₂,t) − ε_θ(x₁|c,t))·σ, solved via accelerated fixed-point iteration (RMSprop or Anderson acceleration). This guarantees zero mixing error (exact satisfaction of the FP equation) under the Harmonic Ansatz, eliminating the non-linear deviation that causes CFG's quality degradation at large ω. The approach is entirely training-free, requires no architectural changes, and is compatible with any existing continuous-data diffusion sampler.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (Table 1), characteristic guidance (CH) consistently reduces FID over classifier-free guidance (CF) at higher guidance scales while maintaining comparable Inception Score. At ω=6.0 with DDIM (50 steps), CH achieves FID 24.77 vs CF's 27.04, and with DPM++2M, FID 21.46 vs 23.47, with IS remaining within 0.05 of CF. The FID advantage grows monotonically with ω (e.g., at ω=4.0, DDIM FID drops from 19.85 to 18.15), while at low ω (≤0.6) the two methods are nearly identical. On ImageNet 256 latent diffusion, CH shifts the IS–FID trade-off curve rightward, meaning it attains a given IS at a lower effective guidance scale, indicating enhanced control strength.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against classifier-free guidance (Ho, 2022), characteristic guidance wins on FID at all guidance scales ω≥1.0 on CIFAR-10 (e.g., 24.77 vs 27.04 at ω=6.0, DDIM) while keeping IS essentially unchanged, breaking the typical FID–IS trade-off. On ImageNet 256 latent diffusion, CH improves IS at the cost of slightly higher FID, but the IS–FID curve shifts rightward, meaning CH reaches a target IS at a lower guidance scale (i.e., stronger effective control). On the conditional Gaussian experiment where the harmonic ansatz holds exactly, CH achieves lower KL divergence than CF for all four sampling methods (SDE, ODE, DDIM, DPM++2M), and corrects the severe diversity loss that CF suffers with ODE-based samplers. On the Landau-Ginzburg magnet simulation, CH better captures the two-peak magnetization distribution below the Curie temperature (lower NLL) where CF fails to produce distinct peaks. Against dynamic thresholding (Saharia et al., 2022), CH is strictly more general: it operates in any continuous data space (pixel, latent, scalar field) and corrects contextual/semantic inaccuracies, whereas dynamic thresholding only clips out-of-range RGB pixel values and does not address latent-space or non-image tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends classifier-free guidance (Ho, 2022) by identifying its fundamental failure mode as a violation of the score Fokker-Planck equation at t > 0 due to the non-linear term ∇‖ε‖². Rather than treating high-guidance-scale artifacts as a practical tuning problem (as dynamic thresholding does), the authors reframe it as a PDE-satisfaction problem and solve it via the method of characteristics under a harmonic ansatz (Laplacian ≈ 0), yielding a training-free correction that shifts the evaluation points of the conditional and unconditional networks. The approach builds directly on the FP-Diffusion framework (Lai et al., 2022) for the score PDE and the DDPM formulation (Ho et al., 2020), but departs from prior guidance work by insisting the guided denoiser must itself be a valid solution of the forward-process PDE, not merely a linear combination of two such solutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is, to the authors' knowledge, the first principled theoretical framework for correcting classifier-free guidance at large scales, and it is immediately deployable as a training-free Stable Diffusion extension compatible with all standard samplers. The magnet phase-transition experiment is a creative validation showing the non-linearity correction matters beyond image aesthetics, but the harmonic ansatz (∇²ε = 0) is a strong assumption that the paper acknowledges fails in overlap regions and for strongly non-Gaussian distributions. Open questions include whether the iterative fixed-point solve can be made cheaper (currently adding 1–10 extra network evaluations per step), whether the projection operator P can be made more principled for latent spaces, and whether FID is even the right metric when the target is p(x|c,ω) rather than p(x). The practical impact for text-to-image practitioners is real but the quantitative gains on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet are moderate rather than transformative.
