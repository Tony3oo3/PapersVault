## Problem

Existing text-to-music generation models rely on either autoregressive language models (requiring forward passes proportional to sequence length) or diffusion models (requiring hundreds of denoising steps), and most perform single-stage generation that struggles to capture the intricate dependency between detailed text descriptions and complex multi-instrument music. Additionally, prior work largely focuses on text-to-music synthesis alone, lacking the ability to handle practically useful tasks like music infilling and continuation within a unified framework.

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form natural language description (which can specify instruments, style, recording quality, and musical structure) as its primary input, and optionally an audio context segment for continuation (first 30% of the clip) or infilling (first and last 30%, with the middle 70% masked). Output is a 32 kHz mono music waveform, generated in 10-second chunks. The semantic and acoustic feature sequences are monotonically aligned, with the Encodec frame rate being a fixed multiple of the HuBERT frame rate, bridged by linear interpolation.

## Method

MusicFlow employs a two-stage cascaded flow matching architecture: a first-stage Transformer (84M params, 8 layers) predicts frame-level HuBERT semantic features from text, and a second-stage Transformer (246M params, 24 layers) predicts dense Encodec acoustic features conditioned on both text and the generated semantic features. A HuBERT model re-trained on music data serves as the semantic encoder, providing a pseudo-transcription that captures melody, rhythm, and harmony without requiring manual annotation. Both stages use a masked prediction objective with span masking (70–100% ratio), which enables zero-shot generalization to music continuation and infilling by simply adjusting the mask pattern. The acoustic stage models the continuous Encodec encoder output directly rather than discrete RVQ tokens, avoiding the length explosion and interleaving complexity of multi-codebook quantization. Classifier-free guidance is applied at both stages with condition-dropping probabilities of 0.3 to balance sample diversity against text coherence.

## Results

On MusicCaps, MusicFlow (bidirectional FM+FM, 330M params) achieves FAD 2.82, FD 14.2, KL-DIV 1.23, ISc 2.78, and CLAP-TEXT 0.56, outperforming AudioLDM-2 (746M, FAD 3.13) and MusicGen (1.5B, FAD 3.40) while using 50–80% fewer parameters. The LM+FM variant (546M) further improves FAD to 2.69 and FD to 13.2. In inference efficiency, MusicFlow requires only 50% of MusicGen's network forward passes and 5× fewer iterative steps than diffusion-based models to reach comparable or better quality. For music continuation and infilling, MusicFlow reduces FAD by ~50% over text-only generation (e.g., FAD 1.63 for continuation vs. 2.82 for TTM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MusicFlow (330M) clearly outperforms AudioLDM-2 (746M) on FAD (2.82 vs. 3.13), FD (14.2 vs. 18.8), and CLAP-TEXT (0.56 vs. 0.43), while using roughly half the parameters. Against MusicGen (1.5B), MusicFlow improves FAD by ~20% (2.82 vs. 3.40) and FD by ~41% (14.2 vs. 24.1), with only 22% of the parameters. MusicFlow also surpasses MusicLM (860M) by ~30% in FAD (2.82 vs. 4.00) with less than 40% of the parameters. However, MusicFlow trails the two strongest FAD baselines—Jen-1 (FAD 2.00) and Noise2Music (FAD 2.10)—though it matches or beats them on KL-DIV (1.23 vs. 1.29 for Jen-1) and CLAP-TEXT. In subjective pairwise evaluation, MusicFlow is preferred over both AudioLDM-2 and MusicGen, confirming the objective metric trends. For continuation and infilling, MusicFlow substantially outperforms AudioLDM on FAD (1.63/1.71 vs. 2.08/2.09), FD (6.50/6.5 vs. 25.08/45.93), and CLAP-SIM (0.88/0.89 vs. 0.61/0.59), demonstrating strong context utilization.

## Relation to Prior Work

MusicFlow sits at the intersection of three research threads: flow matching as a generative framework (Lipman et al., 2023), the masked-prediction / in-context-learning paradigm from Voicebox (Le et al., 2023), and the cascaded semantic-to-acoustic decomposition popularized by MusicLM (Agostinelli et al., 2023). Where MusicLM uses two autoregressive language models over discrete tokens, MusicFlow replaces both stages with non-autoregressive flow matching over continuous features (HuBERT for semantics, Encodec for acoustics), and where AudioLDM2 and Noise2Music apply diffusion directly to low-level audio representations, MusicFlow inserts a self-supervised semantic bottleneck to bridge the gap between free-form text and music. The masked-prediction training objective, borrowed from Voicebox, is repurposed here to unify text-to-music, continuation, and infilling in a single model without task-specific heads.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the efficiency envelope: a 330M-parameter model with ~50 NFE matches or beats 1.5B-parameter autoregressive and 746M-parameter diffusion baselines on MusicCaps, making high-quality text-to-music generation feasible on modest hardware. The unified masked-prediction formulation also eliminates the need for separate models for infilling and continuation, a practical simplification for production systems. Key limitations include reliance on 20K hours of proprietary (and predominantly stock-music) training data, a persistent gap to ground truth in subjective evaluation, and the observation that the first-stage model overfits beyond 8 layers—suggesting the semantic prediction task may be easier than the acoustic one. Open questions include whether more sophisticated ODE solvers can further reduce NFE, whether the approach scales to longer-form music, and how to address the genre diversity gap the authors acknowledge.
