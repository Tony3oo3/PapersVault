## Problem

Existing low-bit integer training methods are largely confined to uniform INT8 precision and lack principled mixed-precision allocation for weights, activations, and gradients simultaneously. Hessian-based sensitivity measures fail during training because non-zero gradients make the first-order term dominant, and prior mixed-precision approaches rely on fixed thresholds or expensive search procedures that do not adapt to the evolving quantization landscape across training stages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240435/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard neural network (CNN or Transformer) and a supervised training dataset, initializing all layer bitwidths to INT4. During training, it adaptively allocates per-layer bitwidths among INT4, INT6, and INT8 for weights, activations, and gradients independently, producing a mixed-precision model whose average weight bitwidth drops to ~4.2 bits. The output model supports both continued low-bit training and reduced-cost inference, with weight storage reduced by over 40% and inference BitOPs reduced by over 30% relative to full INT8.

## Method

AMPA introduces a magnitude-based sensitivity measurement that multiplies the L1 norm of layer gradients by the L1 norm of quantization loss (for weights/activations) or additionally by the L1 norm of activations (for gradients), and proves via Taylor expansion and Cauchy-Schwarz that this product upper-bounds the quantization-induced loss change. Starting from uniform INT4, every Itv epochs the framework ranks all layers by their sensitivity for each of the three tensor types and elevates the top α%, β%, and γ% most sensitive layers by 2 bits (INT4→INT6→INT8), with α<β<γ reflecting the empirically observed greater sensitivity to gradient quantization. A fair-allocation threshold (Thr) places layers into a taboo list after they have been selected Thr times post-INT8, preventing a few layers from monopolizing bitwidth increases and ensuring coverage across the network. The entire scheme requires only a few extra floating-point multiplications per layer per update, avoiding the computational overhead of Hessian estimation or solution-space search.

## Results

AMPA achieves 38–48% BitOPs reduction compared to full INT8 training with less than 2% accuracy loss across image classification, segmentation, and language modeling. On CIFAR-10, ResNet-20 reaches 92.26% top-1 accuracy (vs. 91.95% for UI8) at 41.59% BitOPs reduction, while MobileNetV2 reaches 94.62% (vs. 93.38% for UI8) at 45.79% reduction. On ImageNet, ResNet-50 achieves 74.94% top-1 accuracy with 39.08% BitOPs reduction, and ViT-S reaches 75.54% with 39.08% reduction. For language modeling, AMPA achieves perplexity of 121.34 on Wikitext-2 (vs. 120.36 for UI8) at 43.14% BitOPs reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UI8 (Zhu et al., 2020), AMPA trades 0.4–1.5% accuracy for 38–48% BitOPs reduction; on MobileNetV2 (CIFAR-10) and InceptionV3 (CIFAR-100), AMPA actually exceeds UI8 accuracy while halving compute. Against DAI8 (Zhao et al., 2021), DAI8 retains 0.5–2% higher accuracy but relies on channel-wise gradient quantization requiring two separate quantization passes, whereas AMPA uses a single uniform quantization per layer and achieves comparable accuracy at substantially lower BitOPs. Against WAGEUBN (Yang et al., 2020) on ImageNet, AMPA significantly outperforms (e.g., ResNet-50: 74.94% vs. 69.07% top-1). In the measurement comparison (Table 3), AMPA's magnitude-based sensitivity beats the distribution-based measurement of Zhang et al. (2020) by 0.3–0.7% accuracy at similar compression, and substantially outperforms the Hessian-trace measurement of Dong et al. (2020) which achieves 50%+ compression but suffers 1–3% accuracy degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on low-bit integer training quantization (Zhu et al. 2020, Zhao et al. 2021) by introducing adaptive mixed-precision allocation below INT8, a regime where prior methods have been limited. It departs from inference-time mixed-precision quantization (HAWQ, HAQ) by showing that Hessian-based sensitivity measurements are fundamentally inapplicable during training due to non-zero gradients, and replaces them with a magnitude-based measurement that provably upper-bounds quantization influence. The closest prior work is Zhang et al. (2020), which used a fixed distribution-difference threshold for gradient bitwidths; AMPA generalizes this to all three tensors (weights, activations, gradients) with an adaptive, sensitivity-driven update strategy and a fair-allocation mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical path to reducing training compute by ~38% BitOPs with under 2% accuracy loss across CNNs and Transformers, which is directly relevant to practitioners seeking cheaper training without architectural changes. The fair-allocation threshold (Thr) is a non-obvious but critical component—without it, training can crash on deeper networks—highlighting a practical pitfall in iterative bitwidth escalation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to sub-INT4 precisions, whether the sensitivity measurement generalizes to non-convex or generative training objectives, and whether the periodic recomputation overhead (every 5% of epochs) becomes prohibitive at very large scale. The FPGA simulation, while encouraging, leaves real-hardware latency gains unverified.
