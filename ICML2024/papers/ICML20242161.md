## Problem

Out-of-distribution generalization in image-based RL remains an open challenge; the standard remedy of data augmentation over-regularises to specific transforms and amplifies regression-target variance. Symmetries offer a complementary inductive bias, but they are hard to exploit because agent actions are not invariant under flips (which swap left/right), local and global symmetries interact in ways that break permutation-invariant attention, and enforcing them via augmentation is combinatorially expensive. The paper asks whether a scalable transformer architecture can learn to respect both local and global symmetries in a self-supervised, data-driven manner without relying on positional embeddings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB pixel observations from RL environments (14×14 for MiniGrid, 64×64 for Procgen and Atari) or natural images (32×32 or 128×128 for CIFAR-10), which are segmented into two patch hierarchies: a fine pixel-level grid for local GSA and a coarser patch grid for global GSA. The architecture emits both a symmetry-invariant token embedding (fed to the policy or classification head) and equivariant patch-dimension representations that transform predictably under the imposed group actions. For RL the final output is a discrete action; for the CIFAR-10 ablation it is a 10-way class label.

## Method

The core mechanism is Graph Symmetric Attention (GSA), which replaces ViT positional embeddings with a graph topology matrix G whose entries share trainable weights according to geometric relations (equal Euclidean distance for rotation invariance, equal horizontal distance for flip invariance), thereby breaking permutation invariance down to a chosen symmetry group at every attention layer. GSA is applied at two scales: a local window spanning several patches so that local symmetries do not alter the agent's broader neighbourhood, and a global window over the full image. A dedicated flip-breaking layer sums over directed triangle sub-graphs to preserve 90° rotation invariance while distinguishing clockwise from counter-clockwise orientation, which is essential for maintaining the agent's left/right and up/down semantics. Three architectural variants are provided—SiT (invariant), SeT (equivariant), and SieT (both)—where invariance is read from a token embedding and equivariance from the patch-dimension output. For scalability, the graph matrices are shown to be equivalent to depth-wise convolutions with graph-structured kernels, and a graph-symmetric dropout preserves the inductive bias during regularisation.

## Results

On the Procgen benchmark (4 environments, 25M steps, PPO+DrAC), the best SiT variant (SieT) achieves 51.7% average min-max normalized score, substantially outperforming ViT (5.9%), CNN (30.4%), and E2CNN (29.8%) with comparable parameter counts (~70k vs. 79.4k/70.7k). On the rotation-invariant CaveFlyer environment, SiT reaches 59.7% versus 4.0% for CNN and -1.8% for ViT, a ~15× improvement. On CIFAR-10, SiT achieves 80% test accuracy (vs. 76% for ViT with positional embedding and 56% without), reaching ViT-level performance with 2–5× fewer training epochs. On Atari 100k, SieT matches or slightly exceeds the ~3× larger CNN baseline (e.g., 366.3 vs. 344 on SpaceInvaders).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the ViT baseline (Dosovitskiy et al., 2020), all SiT variants achieve 7–9× higher average scores on Procgen (e.g., SieT 51.7% vs. ViT 5.9%), with the gap most dramatic on CaveFlyer (59.7% vs. -1.8%). Against the CNN baseline from Raileanu et al. (2020) with comparable parameter counts (~70k), SieT wins on average (51.7% vs. 30.4%) and by a large margin on CaveFlyer (34.5% vs. 4.0%) and Chaser (54.0% vs. 10.6%), though CNN edges ahead on Fruitbot (70.8% vs. 76.0% for SieT). Against E2CNN (Wang et al., 2022a) with similar sizes, SiT variants consistently outperform (e.g., SiT 59.7% vs. E2CNN 13.4% on CaveFlyer). Against the much larger UCB-DrAC ResNet (620k params, 9× SiT), SieT is competitive on average (51.7% vs. 48.2%) and substantially better on CaveFlyer (34.5% vs. 18.0%) and Chaser (54.0% vs. 44.6%), but trails on Fruitbot (76.0% vs. 85.8%). On MiniGrid LavaCrossing, SiT achieves 0.91 on rotated observations where CNN scores 0.00 and ViT scores 0.15, while matching CNN on in-distribution tasks (0.94 vs. 0.96 on easy-N1). On Atari 100k, SieT slightly outperforms the ~3× larger Rainbow CNN on SpaceInvaders (366.3 vs. 344) and BreakOut (4.97 vs. 4.3) but is marginally worse on Pong (-20.04 vs. -19.07).

## Relation to Prior Work

SiT extends the ViT architecture by replacing positional embeddings with a novel Graph Symmetric Attention (GSA) mechanism that imposes structural symmetry constraints at every attention layer, departing from prior ViT symmetry work (Fuchs et al., 2020; Romero & Cordonnier, 2021) that only modified positional embeddings while leaving subsequent layers permutation-invariant. The GSA mechanism is conceptually an evolution of the rotary embedding method (Su et al., 2021) and the permutation-invariant Set Transformer attention (Lee et al., 2019), adapted to preserve 2D grid symmetries (translations, rotations, flips) through shared-weight graph matrices. In the RL context, SiT positions itself as a transformer-based alternative to equivariant CNNs (E2CNN, Wang et al., 2022a) and as a complement to data augmentation approaches (DrAC, Raileanu et al., 2020), rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that symmetry-preserving attention can substantially improve OOD generalization in image-based RL (3× on MiniGrid, 7–9× over ViTs on Procgen) while matching much larger CNN baselines with ~9× fewer parameters, suggesting that inductive biases from symmetries can substitute for model capacity. However, the local GSA layer introduces a 2×–5× compute overhead that limits scalability to larger environments, and the benefits are most pronounced in symmetry-rich settings (e.g., CaveFlyer) while being more modest in environments without strong symmetries. Open questions include whether efficient custom CUDA implementations can close the compute gap, whether the adaptive symmetry-breaking property (symmetries approximately recovered during training) can be controlled more precisely, and how SiT interacts with emerging world-model and LLM-based RL architectures.
