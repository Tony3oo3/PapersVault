## Problem

LLM-based conversational agents struggle to adhere to predetermined response workflows in domain-specific settings like customer service and emotional support, often generating misaligned or non-compliant responses. While RAG can mitigate this by retrieving relevant examples, the critical challenge is designing an effective sample-selection strategy that identifies partial conversational flows matching the current dialogue context rather than treating dialogues as undivided wholes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241914/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the system receives a user utterance and the ongoing conversation context. The utterance is first converted into a set of short tags (≤3 words each) by an LLM, then used to deterministically navigate a pre-built DFA. The DFA state yields a set of dialogue IDs from training data; 5 dialogues are sampled and formatted as in-context learning examples. The output is a single system response generated by the LLM conditioned on those retrieved examples, with the constraint that the response ID must match the original input format.

## Method

DFA-RAG embeds a Deterministic Finite Automaton learned from training dialogues as a semantic router that directs the LLM's response generation along a deterministic pathway. The DFA is constructed in three stages: (1) an LLM extracts concise tag sequences from each utterance in the training dialogues, (2) a frequency-ordered tag tree is built where tags within each utterance are arranged by occurrence frequency and states are tracked with dialogue IDs, and (3) similar states are merged using a similarity score φ_sim that compares the weighted overlap of 1-hop child states. At inference, the user's tagged utterance drives deterministic state transitions through the DFA; upon reaching a state (or encountering a mismatch that halts navigation), the associated dialogue IDs provide retrieval candidates for in-context learning. This avoids gradient-based fine-tuning entirely, making the framework plug-and-play compatible with any pre-trained LLM while providing a human-readable, interpretable routing structure.

## Results

On dialogue generation quality (Win Rate via GPT-4 evaluation), DFA-RAG achieves an average of 74.3% with GPT-4 and 75.9% with GPT-3.5, outperforming the best baseline (RAG) by approximately 4 percentage points across six customer-service and information domains. On the task-oriented MultiWOZ benchmark, DFA-RAG achieves 93.3% Inform and 90.0% Success rates without using ground-truth dialogue states, surpassing all end-to-end baselines (best: TOATOD at 90.0/79.8) and approaching methods that rely on known dialogue states (best: MarCo at 94.5/87.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFA-RAG consistently outperforms all retrieval-based baselines (RandSamp, BM25, RAG) across every domain and both base LLMs, with the largest gains on AmazonHelp (+3.6 over RAG with GPT-4) and NikeSupport (+5.3 over RAG with GPT-3.5). Fine-tuning (FT-LLM) underperforms all retrieval methods, particularly on smaller datasets like NikeSupport (56.8% vs. 70.1% for DFA-RAG), confirming the data-efficiency advantage of the DFA-guided approach. On the MultiWOZ task-oriented benchmark, DFA-RAG (Inform 93.3, Success 90.0) surpasses all end-to-end baselines including TOATOD (90.0/79.8), RewardNet (87.6/81.5), and MTTOD (85.9/76.5), while remaining competitive with state-aware methods like MarCo (94.5/87.2) and KRLS (93.1/83.7) that exploit ground-truth dialogue states. The paper positions DFA-RAG as an evolution of the Semantic Router concept, replacing embedding-based cosine similarity with interpretable tag-based DFA navigation, and as a structured extension of standard RAG that encodes dialogue workflows into a learnable finite automaton.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RAG paradigm (Lewis et al., 2020) by replacing embedding-based retrieval with a structured, learned Definite Finite Automaton that encodes conversational workflows from training dialogues. It also positions itself as an evolution of the Semantic Router concept (Horsey, 2024), departing from cosine-similarity-based routing in favor of deterministic tag-based state transitions that are interpretable and data-driven. In the task-oriented dialogue literature, DFA-RAG occupies the end-to-end branch (no ground-truth dialogue states at inference) while borrowing the structured-pathway intuition of state-based policy methods, but without requiring gradient-based training or explicit state annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, interpretable mechanism for enforcing workflow compliance in LLM-based customer service and support agents, addressing a real deployment concern where unstructured generation risks policy violations. However, the approach is inherently limited to domains where a coherent multi-turn workflow is embedded in the training data, and its OOD handling (falling back to the last valid state) is heuristic rather than principled. Open questions include how the DFA scales to very large or highly branching dialogue spaces, whether the LLM-based tag extraction step introduces systematic biases, and whether the ~4% win-rate gain over standard RAG persists in more adversarial or open-domain settings.
