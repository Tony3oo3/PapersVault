## Problem

Training architectures with embedded optimization layers is difficult because the solution mapping often has degenerate derivatives (zero almost everywhere for discrete solutions), rendering standard gradient descent uninformative. While several ad-hoc methods exist for specific problem classes, no unified framework connects them to classical proximal optimization theory or handles general saddle-point problems with learnable constraint parameters. Additionally, it was unexplored whether finite-temperature updates could outperform true gradients even in differentiable settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the forward solver's optimal solution z* and the loss gradient ∇ℓ at that solution, along with a temperature τ and optional regularization ρ. The backward pass perturbs only the linear cost parameters of the Lagrangian (c → c + τ∇ℓ) and re-invokes the same solver oracle (warm-started with z*), producing a finite-difference gradient (1/τ)∇_w[L(z̃_τ, w) − L(z*, w)]. This requires no custom solver modifications beyond supporting the same problem class as the forward pass, and integrates directly into CVXPY's automatic differentiation pipeline.

## Method

LPGD defines a Lagrange-Moreau envelope of the loss w ↦ ℓ(x*(w)) by replacing the Euclidean proximity term in the classical Moreau envelope with a Lagrangian divergence D*_L(x|w) that measures how far x is from optimality given w. The gradient of this envelope is computed by linearizing the loss at x* and absorbing the linear term into the cost vector, so the L-proximal map reduces to a single forward solver call on perturbed parameters (c + τ∇ℓ, v). This yields a finite-difference update that carries higher-order sensitivity information beyond the first-order gradient. The framework unifies Direct Loss Minimization, Blackbox Backpropagation, Domke's perturbation, Identity with Projection, SPO+, and Fenchel-Young losses as special cases (τ→0 or τ→∞ limits), and introduces an augmented Lagrangian regularizer ρ to trade off smoothness against tightness of the envelope approximation.

## Results

On the Sudoku constraint-learning task, LPGD reaches a lower final training MSE than GD and converges faster in wall-clock time, with the speedup in the backward pass coming from warm-starting the solver. On the Markowitz portfolio optimization task, the best LPGD run achieves a 35% improvement in utility over the Markowitz initialization, compared to 23% for the best GD run, while also converging faster in iterations with similar total runtime. A temperature sweep (τ) shows that LPGD with τ=100 provides the best performance, capturing higher-order information beyond the true gradient, while τ=1 matches GD and τ=0.1 or τ=1000 degrade due to numerical instability or infeasibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares LPGD against standard gradient descent (GD) using the true conic program adjoint derivative from Agrawal et al. (2019b), natively implemented in CVXPY. LPGD wins on both experiments: on Sudoku it achieves a lower final loss and faster wall-clock convergence (the backward pass is faster due to warm-starting the solver with the forward solution), and on Markowitz portfolio optimization it achieves a 35% utility improvement versus 23% for GD while also requiring fewer iterations to converge. Notably, LPGD's advantage on Markowitz is most pronounced at finite τ=100, where it captures higher-order sensitivities beyond the true gradient; at τ=1 the two methods perform comparably, confirming that LPGD reduces to standard GD in the τ→0 limit. With higher learning rates, GD becomes noisier than LPGD, and both methods diverge at α=0.1 due to infeasible conic programs. The comparison demonstrates that even when non-degenerate derivatives of the solution mapping exist (as in both experiments), the finite-τ LPGD update can outperform the exact gradient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies a family of previously disparate methods for backpropagating through embedded optimization layers—Direct Loss Minimization, Blackbox Backpropagation, Implicit Differentiation by Perturbation, Identity with Projection, SPO+, and Fenchel-Young losses—into a single framework called Lagrangian Proximal Gradient Descent (LPGD). The key conceptual move is replacing the Euclidean distance in the classical Moreau envelope with a Lagrangian divergence, yielding a 'Lagrange-Moreau envelope' whose gradient descent recovers all the above methods as special cases (typically in the τ→0 or τ→∞ limits). The framework is rooted in classical proximal optimization (Moreau 1962, Rockafellar 1970, Nesterov 1983) but departs from prior work by treating finite τ as a useful operating point rather than merely a limiting device, and by extending the setting to saddle-point problems with learnable constraint parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification provides a common theoretical lens for understanding why these methods work and how they relate, which should help practitioners choose and tune them. The practical CVXPY/SCS implementation with warm-starting makes the backward pass often faster than the forward pass, lowering the barrier to using optimization layers in larger pipelines. The finding that finite-τ LPGD can outperform exact-gradient GD even when the solution mapping is differentiable opens a new direction for improving training of optimization-based models, though the evidence is currently limited to two small-scale experiments. Open questions include adaptive tuning of τ and ρ, extension to non-convex or mixed-integer settings, and whether the finite-τ advantage persists at larger scale.
