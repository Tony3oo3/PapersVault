## Problem

Existing protein backbone diffusion models trained from scratch (FrameDiff, Genie, Chroma) exhibit a significant designability gap compared to RFdiffusion, which relies on pre-training with RosettaFold2. This dependency on structure prediction pre-training hinders architectural refinement and limits practical applicability. The paper addresses whether a model trained without pre-training can match RFdiffusion-level designability while also improving generation efficiency.

## Input / Output

The model is fully unconditional: the only input is the desired protein length (number of residues, up to 1024) and optionally the number of chains for oligomer generation (dimers, trimers, tetramers). The output is a set of SE(3) residue frames—each comprising a 3×3 rotation matrix and a 3D translation vector for the Cα atom—representing the full protein backbone in 3D. For oligomers, a large positional index (200) is added as a chain breaker to distinguish inter-chain from intra-chain residues.

## Method

Proteus is a multi-track diffusion model operating on SE(3) residue frames, with each layer containing three components: an IPA-Transformer block (updating node/sequence representations), a backbone update layer (predicting translation and rotation deltas), and a novel graph triangle block (updating edge representations). The graph triangle block replaces AlphaFold2's O(N³) triangle attention with a local O(NK²) variant that selects K=32 nearest neighbors per residue based on current Cα distances, computes attention among those edges with a structure bias derived from RBF-encoded inter-atom distances of the third edge, and gates this bias by sequence representations of both endpoint residues. Two triangle multiplication layers then propagate information globally across all N² edges. The model is trained with denoising score matching on SO(3) and R³ plus auxiliary coordinate and distance-matrix losses at low timesteps, and uses self-conditioning (encoding the previous denoising step's prediction as additional features) to improve structural consistency. Data augmentation by including single chains extracted from oligomeric PDB structures (yielding 50,773 chains) further boosts designability.

## Results

On unconditional monomer backbone generation (averaged over lengths 100–600), Proteus achieves a designability (scTM) of 0.921, substantially outperforming RFdiffusion (0.705), FrameDiff (0.405), Genie (0.349), and Chroma (0.174). Proteus generates a backbone in 18.2 s on an A40 GPU with only 100 sampling steps, matching Chroma's speed (18.3 s) while being ~6.6× faster than RFdiffusion (120.2 s). The Time-for-Success Design (TSD) metric shows Proteus requires 196 s to produce a designable 800-residue backbone, compared to 30,500 s for RFdiffusion and failure for Genie, FrameDiff, and Chroma at that length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Proteus outperforms RFdiffusion on designability (0.921 vs 0.705 scTM) and sampling speed (18.2 s vs 120.2 s), while RFdiffusion retains an edge in diversity (0.328 vs 0.235). Against FrameDiff, Proteus more than doubles designability (0.921 vs 0.405) and is 2.2× faster (18.2 s vs 40.5 s). Compared to Chroma, Proteus achieves a 5.3× improvement in designability (0.921 vs 0.174) and a 6.2× improvement in diversity (0.235 vs 0.038) at essentially identical sampling speed (18.2 s vs 18.3 s). Against Genie, Proteus dominates on every metric: 2.6× higher designability, 10.3× faster sampling, and 1.4× higher diversity. On the TSD metric, Proteus is the only model that reliably produces designable backbones at 800 residues (196 s), whereas Genie, FrameDiff, and Chroma all fail at that length and RFdiffusion requires ~30,500 s.

## Relation to Prior Work

Proteus builds directly on the SE(3) diffusion formulation and training loss from FrameDiff (Yim et al., 2023), while adapting AlphaFold2's triangle attention and triangle multiplication mechanisms into a graph-based local attention block with O(NK²) complexity instead of the original O(N³). It also incorporates RFdiffusion's self-conditioning featurization and chain-breaker technique for oligomer generation. The key conceptual departure is that Proteus achieves RFdiffusion-level (or better) designability without any pretraining on a structure prediction network, instead relying on a multi-track interaction architecture where the graph triangle block integrates node, edge, and structural frame information holistically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the dependency on pre-trained structure prediction networks, Proteus removes a major practical barrier to model refinement and architectural experimentation in protein design, while simultaneously achieving 3× faster sampling (100 steps vs. 500+) and handling proteins up to 1024 residues. The experimental validation—16 expressed proteins with 9/12 and 3/4 showing monodisperse SEC peaks, thermostability to 95°C, and a 1.8 Å crystal structure—provides rare in-vitro confirmation for a from-scratch diffusion model. Open questions include whether the graph triangle approach generalizes to protein-ligand co-folding, apo-to-holo conformational transitions, and whether the performance gap over RFdiffusion on longer proteins (>400 residues) reflects a fundamental architectural advantage or dataset effects.
