## Problem

Existing local point cloud geometry encoders require O(n²) time and space to group neighborhoods and O(nKd) intermediate memory for MLP-based transformations, forcing them to downsample local neighborhoods and lose geometric detail. No prior encoder achieves linear time and space complexity while simultaneously using all neighboring points without downsampling, leaving a trade-off between descriptiveness and computational efficiency unresolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242387/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unordered 3D point cloud of n points in R³ and produces a dense per-point local geometry encoding as a complex vector in C^d (d=256 in practice), with one encoding per input point and no downsampling. The encoding is computed using all points within a soft receptive field controlled by parameter β, rather than a fixed number K of nearest neighbors. The complex-valued output is subsequently cast to real vectors via squared norm before being fed into downstream architectures such as PointNet++, Point Transformer, or simple MLP heads for normal estimation.

## Method

VecKM represents each point's local neighborhood as a Gaussian kernel mixture and vectorizes it via random Fourier features: the encoding is the mean of complex exponentials exp(i(x_k − x_0)A) over all neighbors, where A is a fixed random matrix with entries drawn from N(0, α²). Two theorems prove the encoding is reconstructive (the shape distribution can be recovered from the encoding) and isometric (inner products between encodings preserve shape similarity), grounded in Bochner's theorem and one-class SVM theory. The critical efficiency gain comes from a factorizability property: exp(i(x_k − x_0)A) = exp(ix_kA) / exp(ix_0A), which eliminates explicit neighborhood grouping, and the adjacency matrix is approximated by B × B^H using a second set of random features of width p ≪ n, reducing computation from O(n²d) to O(npd) and memory from O(n² + nKd) to O(nd + np). The method is parameterized by α (controlling detail level), β (controlling receptive field), d (encoding dimension), and p (approximation quality), and integrates into existing architectures by replacing or prepending the local geometry module.

## Results

On the PCPNet normal estimation task, VecKM achieves 17.34° average RMSE, outperforming the best baseline (PointNet with 300 neighbors at 20.17°) by over 16%, while running >100× faster. On ModelNet40 classification, VecKM-integrated architectures consistently improve accuracy (e.g., VecKM→PN: 92.9% vs. PointNet 90.8%; VecKM⇐PCT: 93.1% vs. PCT 92.9%) while reducing inference time by up to 5.98×. On S3DIS semantic segmentation, VecKM→PN++ improves instance mIoU from 64.05% to 67.48% (+3.43), and VecKM=PT achieves 69.53% mIoU (vs. 69.29%) while running 20% faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VecKM outperforms all seven compared baselines across every evaluation setting. In normal estimation (PCPNet), VecKM achieves 17.34° average RMSE, beating PointNet (best: 20.17°), DGCNN (best: 21.24°), and KPConv (best: 24.86°) by 14–30% respectively, with the largest margins under high-noise and gradient-density corruptions. In ModelNet40 classification, VecKM→PN improves instance accuracy from 90.8% (PointNet) to 92.9%, VecKM⇐PN++ from 92.7% to 93.0%, and VecKM⇐PCT from 92.9% to 93.1%, while also reducing inference time by 78% (vs. PN++) and 5.98× (vs. PCT). In ShapeNet part segmentation, VecKM→PN lifts instance mIoU from 83.1% (PointNet) to 84.9%, and VecKM⇒PN++ from 85.0% to 85.3%. On S3DIS, VecKM→PN++ improves instance mIoU by +3.43 over PointNet++ (67.48% vs. 64.05%), and VecKM=PT marginally improves over Point Transformer (69.53% vs. 69.29%) while running 20% faster. Compared to PointMLP, VecKM-based architectures achieve on-par or slightly better accuracy with significantly fewer parameters and faster inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

VecKM builds on the random-feature vectorization of continuous functions introduced by Frady et al. (2022) and Yuan et al. (2023), repurposing it to encode local point cloud geometry as a vectorized Gaussian kernel mixture. It departs fundamentally from the dominant neighborhood-grouping pipeline (PointNet, KPConv, DGCNN, PointMLP) that requires O(n²) memory and nK MLPs, by exploiting the factorizability of complex exponentials to eliminate explicit neighborhood grouping entirely. The factorization trick—replacing a sharp adjacency threshold with an exponential decay and then approximating the resulting kernel matrix via random features—borrows the same idea Peng et al. (2021) used to accelerate transformer attention, but applies it to the local geometry encoding problem. The paper positions VecKM as occupying a unique dual role: simultaneously a hand-crafted geometric descriptor and a learnable encoder, a categorization no prior method claims.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The linear time and space complexity (O(nd + np) vs. O(n² + nKd)) makes VecKM the first local geometry encoder that scales to very large point clouds (100k+ points) without memory overflow, which is practically critical for applications like autonomous driving and remote sensing. The theoretical guarantees (reconstruction and isometry via Bochner's theorem) provide a principled foundation that purely empirical encoders lack, and the conjecture that VecKM may be the only possible linear-complexity local geometry encoder, if proven, would close a design space. Open questions include whether the task-dependent tuning of α and β can be automated, whether the complex-vector interface to downstream architectures introduces unnecessary overhead, and whether the approximation quality degrades at extreme scales or with highly non-uniform point densities.
