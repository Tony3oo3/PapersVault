## Problem

Convergence analyses of Riemannian gradient descent (RGD) and Riemannian proximal point algorithm (RPPA) have relied on either assuming iterates stay in a compact set without quantifying its size, or stating smoothness/strong convexity constants without specifying the region where they hold. This leaves convergence rates incompletely quantified, since geometric deformations in Riemannian analysis scale with distances between iterates and optimizers. The paper asks whether these fundamental first-order methods naturally produce bounded iterates, and if so, what fully quantified rates follow.

## Input / Output

The algorithms operate on a uniquely geodesic Riemannian manifold with sectional curvature bounded in [κ_min, κ_max], a proper g-convex function f (optionally L-smooth or μ-strongly g-convex), an initial point x₀, and a proximal parameter η. The output is an ε-minimizer after a number of iterations explicitly bounded in terms of the initial distance R = d(x₀, x*), the curvature-dependent geometric constant ζ_R, and the smoothness/convexity constants. A key structural guarantee is that all iterates remain in a closed ball centered at the optimizer with radius O(Rζ_R) (for RGD with η=1/L) or O(R) (for RGD with η=1/(ζ_R L) and for RPPA/RIPPA), removing the need for a priori knowledge of a containing set.

## Method

The paper establishes that RGD with the standard step size η=1/L naturally confines iterates to a ball of radius O(ζ_R R) around any optimizer via an inductive argument combining L-smoothness descent with a cosine inequality that controls curvature effects, yielding rates O(ζ_R² LR²/ε) for g-convex and Õ(L/μ) for strongly g-convex functions. A second variant with η=1/(ζ_R L) is shown to be quasi-nonexpansive (iterates never move farther from the optimizer), trading a slightly worse rate for tighter iterate bounds. For RPPA, the paper proves the first non-asymptotic convergence in general manifolds (allowing positive curvature) by showing the proximal operator is quasi-nonexpansive, and introduces RIPPA—an inexact variant whose stopping criterion is implementable via a constant number of composite RGD or projected RGD iterations. The paper also quantifies the smoothness of the Riemannian Moreau envelope as (ζ_D/η)-smooth, establishing that RPPA is equivalent to RGD on this envelope even when positive curvature breaks g-convexity of the envelope.

## Results

The paper establishes fully quantified convergence rates for RGD and RPPA on general Riemannian manifolds without assuming bounded iterates. For g-convex L-smooth functions, RGD with η=1/L achieves O(ζ_R² · LR²/ε) iterations and Õ(L/μ) for the strongly g-convex case, with iterates staying in a ball of radius O(Rζ_R) around the optimizer. In the hyperbolic space specifically, RGD recovers Euclidean-matching rates of Õ(LR²/ε) and Õ(L/μ) with diameter D=O(R). RIPPA achieves O(R²/(ηε)) for g-convex and Õ(1+1/(μη)) for strongly g-convex functions, with iterates bounded by √2·R from the optimizer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so comparisons are between algorithm variants rather than external baselines. RGD with η=1/L is the only method that does not require knowing the initial distance R, at the cost of a larger iterate bound (O(Rζ_R) vs. O(R) for other methods). RGD with η=1/(ζ_R·L) is quasi-nonexpansive (iterates never move farther from the optimizer) but requires knowledge of R. RIPPA-CRGD achieves the best gradient complexity in Hadamard manifolds (where δ_r=1, giving Õ(LR²/ε) and Õ(L/μ)) but requires solving potentially expensive subproblems. Empirically on the Karcher mean problem, RIPPA outperforms both RGD variants on SPD matrices (S_+^{100}), while in hyperbolic space H^d, RGD with η=1/L converges significantly faster than RIPPA, and RGD with η=1/(Lζ_R) is the slowest. All methods exhibit monotonically decreasing distance to the optimizer in practice, going beyond what the theoretical bounds predict.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Riemannian optimization literature by removing a persistent gap: prior convergence analyses for RGD (Zhang & Sra, 2016; Martínez-Rubio & Pokutta, 2023) and RPPA (Bačák, 2013; Ferreira & Oliveira, 2002) either assumed iterates stay in a compact set or relied on projections to enforce boundedness, leaving rates partially unquantified. Here, the authors prove that RGD and RPPA iterates *naturally* remain in a ball around an optimizer whose radius is explicitly bounded in terms of the initial distance and curvature, eliminating the need for such assumptions. For RPPA specifically, this is the first non-asymptotic analysis in general manifolds (allowing positive sectional curvature), extending prior work that was restricted to Hadamard manifolds. The composite RGD and Moreau envelope smoothness results build on the proximal framework of Parikh & Boyd (2014) and Davis & Drusvyatskiy (2019) but provide the first curvature-aware quantification in the Riemannian setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The iterate-boundedness results are foundational: they allow convergence rates to be fully quantified without artificial compactness assumptions, and they reveal a structured trade-off space where one can exchange curvature dependence for tighter iterate bounds or simpler implementation. A key open question the authors highlight is whether a single algorithm can simultaneously achieve the best gradient complexity and the best iterate bounds without knowing the initial distance R. The empirical observation that RGD and RIPPA distances to the optimizer decrease monotonically in practice—beyond what the theory guarantees—suggests the bounds may be improvable. For practitioners working on manifold-constrained optimization (Karcher means, SPD matrix problems, operator scaling), the trade-off table provides concrete guidance on algorithm selection based on available problem information.
