## Problem

Dense linear layers dominate the compute cost of foundation models, and while structured matrices with fast matrix-vector multiplies could offer more efficient alternatives, there is no systematic understanding of how different structures compare to dense matrices in terms of scaling laws. A critical practical gap is that structured layers require drastically different initialization scales and learning rates than their dense counterparts, and naively reusing dense hyperparameters causes structured layers to significantly underperform. The paper asks whether structured matrices can achieve better scaling exponents than dense matrices and, if so, which structures and hyperparameter settings enable this.

## Input / Output

The structured layers are applied within MLPs and ViTs on CIFAR-10/100 (32×32 images with random crop, flip, MixUp, and label smoothing), ImageNet-1k (224×224 images with 8×8 or 32×32 patches), and GPT-2 on OpenWebText (text sequences of length 512). For the MLP scaling-law experiments, a fixed random permutation is applied to input pixels before feeding them to the network to avoid image-specific inductive bias and improve generalizability to non-image data. Outputs are class labels for image classification and next-token predictions for language modeling, with all linear layers (including the language modeling head in GPT-2) replaced by structured matrices.

## Method

The paper introduces Block Tensor-Train (BTT) matrices, a new structured matrix family that removes the parameter-sharing along block dimensions present in Tensor-Train, with Monarch matrices as a special case (BTT-rank 1, two cores). BTT has parameters equal to FLOPs, enabling it to learn more parameters per unit compute than parameter-sharing structures like Kronecker or TT. A key technical contribution is a structure-aware learning rate and initialization scaling derived from μP: the MVM of any structured matrix is decomposed into a sequence of dense batched MVMs, and μP is applied independently to each dense component, yielding per-component learning rate multipliers κ_i that can be orders of magnitude larger than the dense-layer rate. The paper further shows that weight normalization of BTT cores is necessary to prevent unbounded activation growth during transformer training. Empirically, BTT achieves the best scaling exponent among all tested structures on CIFAR-10/100, matches dense ViT-S/32 on ImageNet with 3.8× fewer FLOPs, and outperforms dense GPT-2 on OpenWebText when including language modeling head compute.

## Results

On CIFAR-10/100 with augmentation, BTT achieves the largest scaling exponent among all tested structures, yielding exponentially lower training loss than dense matrices for MLPs and ViTs at equal compute. On ImageNet-1k, BTT (rank 1–2) matches the performance of a dense ViT-S/32 with up to 3.8× fewer FLOPs. For GPT-2 on OpenWebText, BTT (rank 4) outperforms the dense GPT-2 baseline as a function of total compute, though the advantage narrows when excluding the language-modeling head. Monarch (b=4) achieves equal or lower train/test error than dense at the same compute on CIFAR but does not improve the scaling exponent, while Kronecker and TT consistently underperform dense due to parameter sharing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Monarch (Dao et al., 2022), BTT achieves a strictly better scaling exponent on CIFAR-10/100 for both MLPs and ViTs, meaning BTT's training loss decreases faster with compute. On ImageNet, both BTT and Monarch outperform dense ViTs, but BTT with rank 1–2 is more compute-efficient than Monarch with 4 blocks, consistent with the paper's finding that less compute per dimension is preferable. Against Tensor-Train (Oseledets, 2011), BTT is a direct generalization that removes parameter sharing along block dimensions; TT (with 2 cores, rank 16) underperforms dense matrices in the CIFAR scaling-law experiments, while BTT (2 cores, rank 1) significantly outperforms dense. The paper attributes this gap to the principle that structures with FLOPs equal to parameters (BTT, Monarch) scale better than those with parameter sharing (TT, Kronecker), where each parameter is reused √d times, reducing effective expressivity per unit of compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Maximal Update Parameterization (μP) framework from Yang et al. to structured matrices, providing the first systematic method for determining optimal initialization and learning rate scaling for non-dense linear layers. It builds directly on Monarch matrices (Dao et al., 2022) and Tensor-Train decomposition (Oseledets, 2011), proposing Block Tensor-Train (BTT) as a generalization that removes parameter sharing from TT, thereby interpolating between Monarch and dense matrices. The paper departs from the traditional framing of structured matrices as approximations of dense ones, instead positioning them as enabling fundamentally different compute-to-width trade-offs that can yield better scaling laws.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that structure-aware learning rates are essential for structured layers to outperform dense ones is practically critical, as prior work (including Monarch) largely reused dense learning rates and may have been suboptimal. The identification of 'parameters equal FLOPs' as a design principle provides actionable guidance for future structured layer design. However, the experiments are conducted at relatively small scales (CIFAR, small GPT-2), the language modeling gains are primarily attributable to the LM head rather than the transformer body, and the scaling law comparisons use fixed training iterations rather than compute-optimal schedules. Open questions include whether the scaling exponent improvements persist at foundation-model scale and whether a theoretical account exists for when structured matrices beat dense.
