## Problem

Recent SR methods achieve strong accuracy by stacking intricate operations (attention, feature mixing, global-local context modeling), but this stacking creates a substantial computational burden that limits practical deployment on resource-constrained devices. The paper addresses the gap between the expressiveness of Transformer-based SR and the efficiency of CNN-based SR by proposing a unified architecture that models both channel-wise and spatial-wise dependencies without the quadratic cost of self-attention or the disconnected nature of specialized blocks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242400/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-resolution RGB image (generated via bicubic downscaling) and reconstructs a high-resolution RGB image at ×2, ×3, or ×4 scale. Training uses 64×64 random crops from DIV2K and Flickr2K with L1 loss in both pixel and frequency (FFT) domains. The smallest variant (SeemoRe-T) operates with only 220K parameters and 45 GMACS, while the largest (SeemoRe-L) scales to 931K parameters and 197 GMACS, all evaluated on standard benchmarks (Set5, Set14, BSD100, Urban100, Manga109) and real-world SR settings.

## Method

SeemoRe stacks N residual groups, each containing two successive expert blocks: a Rank Modulating Expert (RME) and a Spatial Modulating Expert (SME). The RME employs a Mixture of Low-Rank Expertise (MoRE) that builds a feature pyramid via recursive strided depth-wise convolutions for global context, then decomposes features into low-rank subspaces (with exponentially growing dimensions 2, 4, 8) where spatial and contextual cues are modulated via element-wise multiplication; a learned router dynamically selects the top-1 expert per input, making inference cost independent of the number of experts. The SME replaces self-attention with a Spatial Enhancement Expertise (SEE) that uses striped depth-wise convolutions with a large 11×11 kernel to capture local spatial correlations at linear cost. Both blocks are followed by a GatedFFN for feature refinement, and a global residual connection plus pixel-shuffle upsampler completes the pipeline. The key design insight is that early network layers benefit from diverse rank choices (capturing fine details and noise) while deeper layers converge to lower ranks (focusing on structural features), enabling input- and depth-adaptive feature modulation.

## Results

SeemoRe-T (220K params, 45 GMACS at ×2) achieves state-of-the-art PSNR across all five benchmarks, reaching 32.22 dB on Urban100 and 39.01 dB on Manga109 for ×2 SR, while using 47% fewer parameters and 65% fewer GMACS than DDistill-SR at ×4. At the larger scale, SeemoRe-L (931K params, 197 GMACS at ×2) surpasses SwinIR-Light and SRFormer-Light on Manga109 ×4 by 0.57 dB and 0.31 dB respectively, while requiring fewer GMACS. Runtime is 5.66 ms for SeemoRe-T vs. 11.20 ms for DDistill-SR and 103.95 ms for SRFormer-Light on a 1280×720 input.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against all seven efficient CNN baselines, SeemoRe-T wins on every dataset and scale factor. The largest margins are over SAFMN (+0.38 dB on Urban100 ×2, +0.39 dB on Manga109 ×4) and ShuffleMixer (+0.33 dB on Urban100 ×2), while the gap over DDistill-SR is smaller (+0.04 dB Urban100 ×2) but comes with 47% fewer parameters and 65% fewer GMACS at ×4. Against lightweight Transformers, SeemoRe-L (931K, 197 GMACS) outperforms SwinIR-Light by 0.37 dB and SRFormer-Light by 0.21 dB on Manga109 ×2, and by 0.57 dB and 0.31 dB respectively on Manga109 ×4, all with fewer GMACS. SeemoRe-L ties DAT-Light on Manga109 ×2 (39.49 dB) but surpasses it on Manga109 ×4 (31.48 vs. 31.37 dB) while using fewer GMACS (50 vs. 50 at ×4). ESRT and SwinIR-NG are consistently outperformed by SeemoRe-L across all benchmarks. In the retrained fair-comparison setting (Table 3b), SeemoRe-T (220K, 45 GMACS) beats retrained SwinIR* (191K, 48 GMACS) by 0.66 dB on Urban100 ×2 and retrained DAT* (115K, 44 GMACS) by 0.31 dB, confirming the advantage is not merely a training-schedule artifact.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeemoRe repurposes the Mixture-of-Experts routing paradigm from Shazeer et al. (2017) in a novel way: rather than gating feed-forward capacity or routing to different network paths (as in Path-Restore), it dynamically selects the optimal low-rank dimension for channel-spatial feature modulation. At the macro level, it sits within the CNN-based efficient SR lineage (CARN, IMDN, DDistill-SR, ShuffleMixer, SAFMN), while at the micro level its striped depth-wise convolution for spatial enhancement draws from Conv2Former and Focal Modulation Networks but simplifies them into a single efficient operation. The key conceptual departure from prior efficient SR work is the explicit, input-dependent rank selection via a learned router, which no previous SR method has explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 220K-parameter CNN with 45 GMACS can match or exceed lightweight Transformer-based SR models (SwinIR-Light, SRFormer-Light) in reconstruction quality while being 3–5× faster at inference, which is practically significant for deployment on resource-constrained devices. The dynamic rank-selection mechanism opens an underexplored direction: using routing to adapt model capacity to input content and network depth in restoration tasks. Limitations include persistent blur artifacts, a small expert count (3) that constrains the search space, and the top-1 routing simplification that sacrifices some performance for efficiency. The authors note the approach could extend to other low-information restoration tasks (denoising, low-light enhancement), but scaling the expert ensemble remains an open challenge.
