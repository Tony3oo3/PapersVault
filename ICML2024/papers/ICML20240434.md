## Problem

Many clustering and regression tasks—k-means, mixed linear regression, generalized PCA—share a common 'sum-of-minimum' structure yet have been addressed with separate, ad-hoc algorithms. Prior work often replaces the non-smooth min with a product formulation for tractability, sacrificing modeling accuracy. This paper unifies these problems under a single optimization framework and provides the first tight theoretical guarantees (initialization quality and convergence rate) for the general case.

## Input / Output

The method takes N sub-functions f_i: R^d → R (each L-smooth and μ-strongly convex), a cluster count k, and optionally a regularization parameter λ. It outputs k parameter vectors x_1,…,x_k together with an implicit partition of [N] into k clusters, where each data point is assigned to the parameter minimizing its sub-function. In the GPCA setting, the parameters are orthonormal subspace-normal matrices A_j ∈ R^{d×r} with the constraint A_j^T A_j = I_r, and the output is the recovered subspace arrangement.

## Method

The algorithm has two phases. First, a generalized k-means++ initialization sequentially samples indices i_j with probability proportional to the optimality gap v_i^(j) = min_{j'<j}(f_i(x_{j'}^{(0)}) − f_i^*) (or the squared gradient norm as a computationally cheaper proxy), then sets x_j^{(0)} to the exact minimizer of f_{i_j}. Second, a generalized Lloyd's algorithm alternates between reclassification—assigning each i to the cluster j where f_i(x_j^{(t)}) is minimal—and group gradient descent on the dynamic objective F_j^{(t)}(z) = (1/|C_j^{(t)}|) Σ_{i∈C_j^{(t)}} f_i(z), with a momentum/ADAM variant available. The key theoretical results are a tight O(L²/μ² ln k) initialization guarantee (recovering the k-means++ bound when L=μ) and an O(1/T) gradient-decay convergence rate for the Lloyd phase, both under L-smoothness and μ-strong convexity of each f_i.

## Results

On generalized principal component analysis (GPCA) with N=1000 data points, the proposed sum-of-minimum formulation achieves 91–98% cluster accuracy across k∈{2,3,4} and d∈{4,5,6}, consistently outperforming the sum-of-product baseline by 15–35 percentage points (e.g., 98.24% vs. 81.88% at k=2, d=4). At 10 iterations, the proposed method maintains comparable or lower CPU time (0.08–0.32 s vs. 0.14–0.27 s) while retaining a 16–27 point accuracy advantage. For mixed linear regression (N=1000, k=4–6, d=4–8), the careful-seeding initialization reduces failure rates by up to 20% relative to normal initialization and cuts average iterations by 15–25%. On small-scale neural network training (k=5, 2-layer ReLU networks), careful seeding reduces epochs to reach ground-truth loss from 329.4 to 181.4 (for d_I=5, d_H=3) and lowers fixed-epoch training error from 4.26×10⁻³ to 3.44×10⁻³.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the sum-of-product GPCA formulation solved via block coordinate descent (BCD) from Peng & Vidal (2023). The proposed sum-of-minimum formulation with the generalized Lloyd's algorithm wins decisively on cluster accuracy across all tested configurations: at k=2 the gap is 16.4–24.9 points (e.g., 98.24% vs. 81.88% at d=4), and at k=4 the gap widens to 28.9–35.8 points (e.g., 93.73% vs. 57.89% at d=6). Computationally, the proposed method is competitive or faster: at 10 iterations, CPU times are 0.08–0.32 s for the proposed method versus 0.14–0.27 s for BCD, meaning the accuracy gain comes at no extra cost (and sometimes a slight speed advantage). The paper also compares three initialization strategies (normal Gaussian, uniform seeding, and the proposed careful seeding via Algorithm 1) on mixed linear and nonlinear regression; careful seeding consistently achieves the lowest failure rate and fewest iterations, though the margin over uniform seeding is modest (e.g., 0.050 vs. 0.057 failure rate at k=4, d=4). No scenario is reported where the baseline outperforms the proposed method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies k-means++ (Arthur & Vassilvitskii, 2007) and Lloyd's algorithm (Lloyd, 1982) under a novel 'sum-of-minimum' optimization framework that subsumes k-means clustering, generalized principal component analysis, and mixed linear regression as special cases. Where prior work on GPCA and mixed linear regression resorted to less-precise product reformulations to gain smoothness (Zhong et al., 2016; Peng & Vidal, 2023), this paper tackles the non-smooth min-structure directly and proves that the resulting algorithms retain the same O(ln k) initialization guarantee and O(1/T) convergence rate as their k-means counterparts, with an additional L²/μ² condition-number factor. The theoretical analysis follows the same inductive structure as the k-means++ proof but introduces new lemmas to handle the general L-smooth, μ-strongly convex sub-function setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework provides a single theoretical lens for a broad family of clustering and multi-model regression problems, and the tight O(L²/μ² ln k) bound clarifies exactly how ill-conditioning degrades initialization quality beyond the classical k-means setting. Empirically, the paper demonstrates that the more-precise sum-of-minimum formulation substantially outperforms the smoother sum-of-product reformulations in classification accuracy, suggesting that practitioners should not sacrifice model fidelity for optimization convenience. However, the experiments are limited to small-scale synthetic data (N=1000, d≤10), and the theory requires strong convexity of each sub-function, leaving the important non-convex case (e.g., deep neural networks without regularization) as an open question. Scaling the algorithms to large-scale or high-dimensional settings remains unaddressed.
