## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of Actor-Critic algorithms fail to simultaneously address five practically essential aspects: multi-layer neural network parametrization, Markovian (non-i.i.d.) sampling, continuous state-action spaces, last-iterate performance, and global optimality. No prior work covers all five criteria (the MMCLG criteria), leaving a significant gap between what theory guarantees and what practitioners actually deploy. This paper asks whether a unified convergence analysis encompassing all five dimensions is possible and answers affirmatively.

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on a discounted MDP with bounded but potentially infinite (continuous) state and action spaces, where samples are drawn from a geometrically mixing Markov chain rather than i.i.d. draws from the stationary distribution. The actor and critic are both parameterized by D-layer neural networks with m neurons per layer, and the algorithm uses a target-network scheme with experience replay over a buffer of n transitions. The output is a last-iterate global convergence guarantee: J(λ*) − J(λ_T) ≤ ε with sample complexity Õ(ε^{-3}), where the bound explicitly accounts for network width m, depth D, buffer size L, and the approximation/bias errors of the function class.

## Method

The core technique establishes last-iterate convergence by first bounding the optimality gap J(λ*) − J(λ_t) in terms of the accumulated critic estimation errors via the weak gradient domination (PL) property of MDPs, then decomposing the critic error into four components: approximation error (bounded by √ε_approx via Assumption 4), estimation error (shown to be exactly zero via a conditional-expectation identity), sampling error (bounded as Õ(1/√n) using Rademacher complexity for Markov chains per Bertail & Portier 2019), and optimization error (bounded as Õ(L^{-1/4}) + Õ(m^{-1/12}D^{7/2}) using NTK-type results from Fu et al. 2021). The algorithm itself is a simplified TD3 variant with a natural policy gradient actor update, a target-network critic update, and experience replay, where the critic is trained for J inner iterations with L gradient steps per iteration. Crucially, the analysis introduces no dependence on the cardinality of the state or action space, making it valid for continuous spaces, and the only restriction on the actor is smoothness of the log-policy gradient, accommodating arbitrary multi-layer neural network parametrizations.

## Results

The paper establishes the first last-iterate global convergence sample complexity bound of Õ(ε^{-3}) for Actor-Critic with multi-layer neural network parametrization of both actor and critic, under Markovian sampling in continuous state-action spaces. Theorem 1 decomposes the last-iterate optimality gap J*(λ*) − J(λ_t) into seven terms: Õ(1/t) + Õ(1/√n) + Õ(1/L^{1/4}) + Õ(m^{-1/12} D^{7/2}) + Õ(γ^J) + Õ(√ε_bias) + Õ(√ε_approx), where t is the number of outer iterations, n is the batch size, L is the number of gradient descent steps for the critic, m is the network width, D is the depth, J is the number of target-network updates, and ε_bias and ε_approx are approximation errors. Setting t = Õ(ε^{-1}), n = Õ(ε^{-2}), L = Õ(ε^{-4}), and J = Õ(log(1/ε)) yields the total sample complexity t·n = Õ(ε^{-3}).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper, so comparisons are between convergence guarantees rather than empirical performance. The paper is the first to simultaneously satisfy all five MMCLG criteria (multi-layer NN, Markovian sampling, continuous space, last iterate, global optimality), a combination no prior work achieves. Among works with multi-layer NN parametrization, Fu et al. 2021 achieves Õ(ε^{-6}) but only for finite action spaces with i.i.d. sampling and no last-iterate guarantee, while Tian et al. 2023 achieves Õ(ε^{-2}) but only for local convergence (not global) and finite state-action spaces. Among works with Markovian sampling and continuous spaces, Xu et al. 2020a achieves the best sample complexity Õ(ε^{-2}) but only for linear critics with average-iterate (regret) bounds, not last-iterate global convergence. The Õ(ε^{-3}) last-iterate global bound here is thus the tightest known for the full practical setting, improving on Fu et al. 2021's Õ(ε^{-6}) by a factor of ε^{-3} while also extending to continuous spaces and Markovian sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends several threads of Actor-Critic convergence analysis: the weak gradient domination (PL-like) condition for MDPs from Ding et al. (2022), the multi-layer neural network critic optimization error bounds from Fu et al. (2021), and the Rademacher complexity tools for Markov chains from Bertail & Portier (2019). Prior works each addressed a subset of the practical criteria—Tian et al. (2023) handled multi-layer NNs with Markovian sampling but only achieved local convergence on finite state spaces, while Fu et al. (2021) achieved global optimality for multi-layer NNs but required i.i.d. sampling and finite action spaces. This paper's key departure is a novel four-term error decomposition of the critic estimation error (approximation, estimation, sampling, optimization) that simultaneously accommodates Markovian dependence, multi-layer architecture, and continuous spaces, combined with the weak gradient domination property to convert average-iterate bounds into last-iterate global convergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical guarantee that a practically-relevant Actor-Critic algorithm (a simplified TD3) achieves global last-iterate convergence under all conditions encountered in real deployments: deep neural networks, on-policy Markovian data, continuous state-action spaces, and evaluation of the final iterate rather than an average. The O~(ε^{-3}) sample complexity is competitive with the best known rates for simpler settings (e.g., linear critics with i.i.d. sampling achieve O~(ε^{-2})), suggesting the practical overhead of neural parametrization and Markovian sampling is modest. Open questions include whether the geometric mixing assumption (Assumption 2) can be weakened, whether the approximation error ε_approx can be made explicit in terms of network width/depth rather than assumed, and whether the result extends to off-policy or multi-agent settings. The analysis is purely theoretical with no empirical validation, so the tightness of the bounds remains untested.
