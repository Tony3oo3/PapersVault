## Problem

While 1-dimensional real summation in the shuffle model of differential privacy is well-understood, the natural extension to d-dimensional vector mean estimation was open: it was unknown how many messages per user are needed to achieve central-DP-optimal error rates, and no optimal single-message protocol existed. The paper also identifies a critical robustness gap: accurate multi-message shuffle protocols are inherently vulnerable to malicious users who can induce error far exceeding the unit norm of their input.

## Input / Output

Each of n users holds a d-dimensional vector v^(i) with ‖v^(i)‖₂ ≤ 1, and the protocol must output an estimate of the sum (or mean) in R^d under (ε,δ)-shuffle DP. In the multi-message protocol, users send messages in R^{2d} (after Kashin transformation) to a single shuffler; in the single-message protocol, each user sends exactly one message from the discrete grid {0,1,…,r}^d. The shuffler permutes all messages before passing them to an untrusted curator, and the curator applies a fixed aggregation function to produce the final d-dimensional estimate.

## Method

The multi-message protocol applies Kashin's representation to map each unit vector to a 2d-dimensional vector with bounded infinity norm, then runs the optimal 1D GKMPS shuffle protocol independently on each coordinate with a per-coordinate privacy budget of ε/(2√(2d·log(2/δ))), yielding Õ(min(d, nε²)) messages per user and MSE Õ(d/ε²). The matching lower bound uses a reconstruction attack that enumerates all C(nk,k) subsets of k messages from the shuffled transcript, combined with a ρ-packing argument on the sphere: if k is too small, the reconstructed set is too small to contain all packing points, contradicting DP. For the single-message setting, the protocol generalizes Balle et al.'s randomized response to vectors by rounding each coordinate to a multiple of r and then, with probability γ, replacing the entire rounded vector with a uniform draw from {0,…,r}^d, achieving MSE O_δ(dn^{d/(d+2)}ε^{-4/(d+2)}), which is shown tight. The robustness analysis shows that in the single-shuffler setting, k malicious users can add Ω(kd/log²(nd)) MSE to any additive protocol, whereas a multi-shuffler variant limits the damage to O(k).

## Results

In the multi-message shuffle model, the paper achieves optimal MSE Õ(d/ε²) using Õ(min(d, nε²)) messages per user, matching central DP up to logarithmic factors. A matching lower bound shows that Ω(min(nε², d)/log(n)) messages are necessary for any unbiased or summation protocol to achieve optimal error. In the single-message setting, the protocol achieves MSE O_δ(dn^(d/(d+2)) ε^(-4/(d+2))), which is shown to be tight via a lower bound of Ω(dn^(d/(d+2))) for constant ε. For robustness, k malicious users can induce Ω(kd/log²(nd)) additive MSE in the single-shuffle setting, but only O(k) in the multi-shuffle setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The multi-message protocol matches the central DP rate of O(d/ε²) (Bassily et al., 2014) up to logarithmic factors, while requiring Õ(min(d, nε²)) messages per user. This improves upon the 1D shuffle protocols of Ghazi et al. (2021) and Balle et al. (2020), which achieve O(1/ε) additive error for scalar summation but whose natural d-dimensional extension would require exponentially many messages in d. For the single-message setting, the protocol generalizes Balle et al. (2019) from 1D to d-dimensional vectors, achieving MSE O_δ(dn^(d/(d+2)) ε^(-4/(d+2))), and is shown to be optimal for constant ε. Compared to Scott et al. (2021, 2022), who study single-message shuffle for sampled sub-vectors, this paper handles full d-dimensional vectors and provides matching lower bounds. The robustness analysis reveals that additive protocols in the single-shuffle model are inherently non-robust (each malicious user causes Ω(d/log²(nd)) error), whereas the multi-shuffle variant limits damage to O(k) for k malicious users.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 1-dimensional real summation results of Ghazi et al. (2021) to d-dimensional vector aggregation by applying the Kashin representation to decouple coordinates, and generalizes the single-message shuffle protocol of Balle et al. (2019) from scalars to vectors. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a reconstruction-attack-based lower bound technique (iterating over all k-subsets of shuffled messages combined with a packing argument) that establishes message complexity requirements, a tool not previously applied to high-dimensional mean estimation. The robustness analysis is entirely new for the shuffle model, building on poisoning-robustness definitions from Cheu et al. (2021) and Talwar (2022) in other privacy models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The message complexity lower bound (Ω(min(nε²,d)/log(n))) reveals a fundamental tension absent in the 1D case: achieving central-DP-level accuracy in high dimensions inherently requires many messages per user, ruling out near-single-message protocols for d-dimensional tasks. The robustness result is practically significant—it shows that the multi-message shuffle model, often chosen for its lower overhead compared to trusted aggregators, is inherently vulnerable to malicious users who can induce error Ω(d/log²(nd)) per attacker, far exceeding the unit norm of any legitimate input. This points to a concrete architectural trade-off: robustness in the shuffle model requires either multiple shufflers (with O(nB) storage overhead) or a trusted aggregator, a finding that directly informs system design for federated learning deployments.
