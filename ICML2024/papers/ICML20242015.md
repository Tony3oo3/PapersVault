## Problem

Analytical derivations of double descent curves have been limited to static least-squares estimators or pure gradient flow (no stochasticity), leaving the effect of SGD's inherent noise on the generalization error dynamics uncharacterized. This paper asks: how does the stochastic perturbation in SGD quantitatively modify the test risk trajectory compared to deterministic gradient flow, and can this correction be computed explicitly?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242015/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a data matrix X ∈ R^{n×d}, target vector y, a loss function with its Hessian, and a small learning rate γ as inputs. It produces the full time-dependent test risk under stochastic gradient flow, decomposed into the deterministic GF risk plus an additive correction proportional to γ·Tr{C(t)}, where C(t) is the covariance of fluctuations. In the large-size limit (n,d,p→∞ with p/n→α, d/n→ψ), the learning rate must be rescaled as γ=γ'/d for a well-defined limit, and the final expressions reduce to single and double integrals over the Marchenko-Pastur distribution.

## Method

The paper represents the transition probability of a general Itô SDE as a path integral over trajectories, then applies a Laplace (saddle-point) approximation in the small-learning-rate limit. The key decomposition is w(t) = w^ode(t) + √γ z(t), where w^ode is the deterministic gradient flow solution and z(t) is a zero-mean Gaussian process whose covariance C(t) = ∫₀ᵗ dτ U^GF(t,τ) Σ(τ) U^GF(t,τ)ᵀ involves a time-ordered exponential of the Hessian along the GF trajectory. This general formula is verified exactly against a solvable linear SDE, then specialized to a random weak features regression model where the diffusion matrix is proportional to the training loss times X_A^T X_A. In the large-size asymptotic regime, the stochasticity correction to test risk is expressed as double integrals F₁(α,t) and F₂(α,t) over the Marchenko-Pastur law, yielding an explicit closed-form for the t→∞ limit that vanishes above the interpolation threshold (α>1) but is a cubic polynomial in α below it.

## Results

The paper derives a general closed-form expression (Eq. 16) for the covariance C(t) of stochastic fluctuations around the deterministic gradient flow trajectory, valid in the small learning rate limit. Applied to a weak features model with i.i.d. Gaussian data in the large-size limit (n,d,p→∞ with p/n→α, d/n→ψ), the SGF test risk correction over GF is given explicitly by single and double integrals over the Marchenko-Pastur distribution (Eq. 30). At infinite time, the correction reduces to (γ′/4)(α/ψ)((1−α/ψ)+μ²)max(1−α, 0), which is non-zero only in the underparametrized regime α<1 and vanishes identically for α>1. Numerical simulations of discrete-time SGD (d=1000, n=400, ψ=2.5, γ=10⁻³) show good agreement with the analytical predictions for both small and large times, though the theory slightly underestimates the SGD–GD gap at intermediate times (1≲t≲10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between the analytical SGF test risk prediction and numerical simulations of discrete-time SGD, with pure gradient flow (GF) serving as the deterministic baseline. The key quantitative finding is that the stochastic correction to test risk, E_test^SGF(t) − E_test^GF(t), is proportional to the learning rate γ and is governed by the trace of the covariance matrix C(t). At infinite time, the correction is a cubic polynomial in α with roots at α=0, α=1 (the interpolation threshold), and α=(1+μ²)ψ (unphysical), meaning stochasticity increases test risk only below the interpolation threshold. The analytical curve (Eq. 32) matches the SGD simulation dots in Fig. 2 well across the full range of α∈[0,ψ]. For finite times (Figs. 3–4), the theory captures the qualitative behavior—including the migration of the maximum SGF–GF gap from above to below the interpolation threshold as time increases—but underestimates the magnitude at intermediate times (1≲t≲10), likely due to the Laplace approximation of the path integral. No direct comparison with other SGF formulations (e.g., DMFT or homogenized SGD) is performed, though the authors note expected agreement with Paquette et al. (2022) in the small learning rate limit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous-time SDE approximation of SGD introduced by Mandt & Blei (2015) and Li et al. (2015), for which Li et al. (2019) provided rigorous first-order convergence guarantees, by developing a path-integral formulation with a Laplace (saddle-point) approximation to compute the full covariance of fluctuations around the deterministic gradient flow trajectory. It departs from prior analytical treatments of double descent (Belkin et al., 2020; Bodin & Macris, 2021, 2022), which were restricted to least-squares estimators or pure gradient flow, by explicitly incorporating the stochastic term. The approach also contrasts with the Volterra integral equation methods of Paquette et al. (2022) and the DMFT formulations of Mignacco et al. (2020, 2021), circumventing the need to solve those integral equations through a saddle-point calculation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a general, model-agnostic formula for the SGF test risk correction in terms of the data matrix, loss Hessian, and GF trajectory, which could in principle be applied to more complex models (random features, diagonal networks, generalized linear models) where the weak features model is too simple. However, the current application is limited to the weak features model, and the Laplace approximation underestimates corrections at intermediate times. Key open questions include extending the framework to non-convex landscapes with multiple minima, making the path integral rigorous for state-dependent covariances (beyond Freidlin-Wentzell theory), and understanding whether the vanishing of stochasticity corrections in the overparametrized regime persists in more realistic models.
