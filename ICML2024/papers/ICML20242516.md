## Problem

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods like LoRA reduce training memory but leave the model size unchanged, offering no inference speedup, while structured pruning improves inference efficiency but incurs high training costs and performance degradation when naively combined with PEFT. The paper addresses how to simultaneously improve both training and inference efficiency of pretrained LMs while preserving task accuracy, a gap left by existing methods that treat pruning and tuning as separate, static processes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242516/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

APT takes a pretrained LM (RoBERTa, T5, or LLaMA) with its frozen weights, a task training dataset, and a target sparsity constraint γ_T as input. It produces a pruned LM where binary masks zero out selected MHA heads, FFN neurons, and hidden dimensions, while APT adapters with dynamically grown low-rank matrices (W_A, W_B) are merged into the frozen weights for deployment. The pruning masks are applied via Hadamard products on input/output dimensions, and the tuning rank r_apt grows linearly in salient layers during training, so the final model is a smaller, self-contained LM with no separate adapter modules at inference time.

## Method

APT introduces an adapter architecture that extends LoRA with binary pruning masks on input/output dimensions and a dynamically adjustable rank r_apt, enabling simultaneous structured pruning and parameter-efficient tuning within a single forward pass. Pruning decisions are guided by an outlier-aware salience score that combines the magnitude of activation-gradient products with the kurtosis of activations, computed in a memory-efficient batch-summed form; a binary search over salience-density-sorted blocks then selects which MHA heads, FFN neurons, and hidden dimensions to retain under the sparsity constraint. For tuning, APT computes per-adapter importance from the salience of W_B parameters and linearly increases the ranks of the top-half salient adapters, initializing new parameters with Gaussian noise in W_A and zeros in W_B to preserve output continuity. A self-knowledge distillation objective shares frozen parameters between teacher and student and duplicates tuning layers as teachers, eliminating the need for a separate fully-trained teacher model and reducing distillation memory overhead.

## Results

On RoBERTa-base at 60% sparsity, APT maintains 94.5 SST2 accuracy (vs. 94.8 full fine-tuning) and 86.4 MNLI (vs. 87.6), while achieving 2.4× inference speedup and 78.1% inference memory. On T5-base at 60% sparsity, APT preserves 95.0 SST2 and 87.0 MNLI with 38.6/17.0/35.8 ROUGE on CNN/DM. For LLaMA-2 7B at 30% sparsity, APT retains 86.4% of the dense model's average performance (50.0 vs. 53.4) with only 75.8% training memory compared to LoRA. APT speeds up RoBERTa and T5 fine-tuning by 8× and reduces LLaMA training memory to 30% compared to the LoRA+Prune baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA+Prune (Mask Tuning post-training pruning), APT achieves 2.5–9.9% higher task performance at the same 60% sparsity while training 8.4× faster on RoBERTa and 8.2× faster on T5, with comparable inference efficiency. Against Prune+Distill (CoFi), APT reaches near-identical accuracy (0.9-point drop on MNLI, same on SST2) but trains 2.5× faster and uses only 41.6% of the training memory, thanks to its self-distillation technique that avoids a separate teacher model. Against LoRA+Prune+Distill, APT outperforms on all tasks (e.g., 86.4 vs. 84.2 MNLI) while training 11× faster and using 54% less memory. On LLaMA-2 7B at 30% sparsity, APT outperforms LLMPruner by 16.5 points in average score (50.0 vs. 42.9) and LoRA+Prune by 9.9 points (50.0 vs. 45.5), while using 75.8% training memory versus LLMPruner's 253.6%. On BERT-base (Table 7), APT outperforms both PST and LRP at 50% density (83.2 vs. 81.0/82.2 GLUE avg.) and at 10% density (76.8 vs. 75.9/75.7), confirming the benefit of the outlier-aware salience scoring that considers both frozen and tuning parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

APT builds directly on LoRA's low-rank adapter architecture, extending it with binary pruning masks and dynamically adjustable ranks to simultaneously control frozen and tunable parameter counts. Its salience scoring derives from Movement Pruning's weight-gradient product, augmented with a kurtosis term to preserve outlier parameters, while its self-distillation objective is adapted from CoFi's layer-wise distillation. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the joint, adaptive scheduling of pruning (reducing model size early in training) and tuning (adding parameters to salient layers), whereas existing methods treat PEFT and structured pruning as separate, sequential, or static operations that individually sacrifice either training or inference efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8× training speedup and 70% memory reduction for large LM pruning address a practical bottleneck that limits LM deployment on consumer hardware, and the self-distillation trick (sharing frozen parameters between teacher and student) offers a reusable recipe for reducing distillation overhead. However, the performance gap widens for larger models (86.4% for LLaMA-7B vs. 98% for RoBERTa), training instability from dynamic parameter shape changes remains unresolved, and the approach has only been validated up to 13B parameters. Open questions include whether non-linear adapters (Prefix-tuning, HAdapters) could recover more performance, whether grouped-structure pruning could yield better hardware-aligned speedups, and whether the method scales to 70B+ models.
