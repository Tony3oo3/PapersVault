## Problem

Existing sample complexity analyses for matrix completion are largely restricted to the nuclear norm (p=1) case, leaving the broader family of Schatten p quasi-norm constraints (p<1)—which are equivalent to deep matrix factorization with Frobenius regularization—without tight generalization guarantees. Furthermore, no prior work provides a comprehensive PAC analysis for non-linear matrix completion models that incorporate learnable functions or neural encodings alongside norm-based rank constraints.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N i.i.d. samples of (entry index (i,j), observed value) pairs drawn from a joint distribution over [m]×[n]×R, where the sampling distribution may be uniform or arbitrary. It produces a completed matrix prediction g(i,j) for all entries, a learned componentwise rescaling function f: R→R (in the FRMC variant), and high-probability generalization or excess risk bounds. The latent matrix Z is constrained by a Schatten p quasi-norm bound (optionally weighted by smoothed row/column marginals) and an entry-wise bound B₀, while f is constrained to be L_f-Lipschitz and B_f-bounded.

## Method

The paper derives generalization bounds via a novel 'parametric interpolation' technique that decomposes a Schatten p-constrained matrix into a low-rank component (controlled by parameter counting) and a low-nuclear-norm residual (controlled by chaining), then optimizes the interpolation threshold. For the distribution-free unweighted setting, the rate is Õ(r^{1-p/2}(m+n)^{1+p/2}), which degrades relative to the uniform case; the authors introduce a weighted Schatten quasi-norm using smoothed empirical marginals to recover the Õ(r(m+n)) rate. The key architectural contribution is Functionally Rescaled Matrix Completion (FRMC), where a single trainable Lipschitz function f is applied entrywise to a Schatten-constrained latent matrix Z, and the authors prove via multi-class chaining lemmas that learning f adds only a negligible √((B²+B₀L_fℓB)/N) term to the generalization bound. Extensions cover neural encodings Ψ(i,j) added to Z and multiple latent matrices with different Schatten indices, yielding the first uniform convergence PAC analysis for neural network matrix completion.

## Results

On three real-world datasets (Douban, LastFM, MovieLens 25M), the proposed FRMC-FSW3 model (functionally rescaled matrix completion with weighted Schatten 2/3 norm) achieves the best test RMSE across all datasets: 0.7616 on Douban, 0.9904 on LastFM, and 0.7799 on MVL25. The most dramatic improvement is on LastFM, where FRMC-FSW3 (0.9904) reduces RMSE by over 60% compared to the standard nuclear norm baseline S2 (2.5885). The theoretical contribution provides sample complexity bounds of Õ(r(m+n)) in the uniform/weighted setting and Õ(r^{1-p/2}(m+n)^{1+p/2}) in the distribution-free unweighted setting, with the FRMC non-linearity adding only a negligible √((B²+B₀L_f·ℓ·B)/N) term.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares FRMC variants against four standard matrix completion baselines: S2 (nuclear norm, d=2), SW2 (weighted nuclear norm), S3 (Schatten 2/3, d=3), and SW3 (weighted Schatten 2/3). FRMC-FSW3 dominates all baselines on all three datasets. On LastFM, the improvement is most striking: FRMC-FSW3 (0.9904) vs. S2 (2.5885), a 62% RMSE reduction, and vs. SW3 (2.0417), a 51% reduction. On Douban, FRMC-FSW3 (0.7616) beats S2 (0.8042) by 5.3% and SW3 (0.8030) by 5.2%. On MVL25, gains are more modest (0.7799 vs. 0.8047 for S2, ~3% improvement), likely because the dataset is denser. Two consistent trends emerge: (1) adding the learnable rescaling function (FS) provides the largest single improvement, and (2) using p=2/3 (d=3) with weighted norms is consistently superior to p=1 (d=2), confirming the theoretical prediction that lower Schatten indices induce stronger rank sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generalization analysis of matrix completion from the nuclear norm (p=1) case studied by Shamir & Shalev-Shwartz (2011) and Foygel et al. (2011) to the full family of Schatten p quasi-norms (p < 1), which are equivalent to deep matrix factorization with Frobenius constraints. It introduces Functionally Rescaled Matrix Completion (FRMC), a simple non-linear model that applies a trainable scalar function to each entry of a latent matrix, and proves that this adds only a negligible term to the sample complexity. The proof technique relies on novel 'multi-class chaining' arguments that go beyond standard covering number approaches, and the weighted Schatten norm generalizes the weighted trace norm of Srebro & Salakhutdinov (2010) to arbitrary p.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first comprehensive PAC analysis of neural network matrix completion, bridging the gap between the deep matrix factorization literature (focused on optimization) and the statistical learning theory literature (focused on p=1). The result that a learnable non-linear rescaling function adds negligible sample complexity cost is practically important: it justifies adding simple non-linearities to matrix completion models without theoretical penalty, and experiments confirm significant real-data gains. Open questions include extending the analysis to non-flat (multi-output) matrices, which would illuminate the implicit rank-restriction phenomenon in deep network training, and whether the results extend to more complex non-linear architectures beyond the single scalar function.
