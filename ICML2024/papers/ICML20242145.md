## Problem

Privately fine-tuning pre-trained models achieves near no-privacy utility despite NoisySGD's worst-case √p dimensionality dependence, but the specific structural property of last-layer features that enables dimension-independence—and how fragile it is to perturbations—remains uncharacterized. This paper asks: what feature property makes private learning dimension-independent, and under what perturbations does this property break down?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242145/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes last-layer feature vectors (768-dimensional for ViT) extracted from ImageNet-pretrained models on CIFAR-10, paired with private training labels, and trains a linear classifier W ∈ R^{K×p} via NoisyGD under ρ-zCDP. The primary theoretical outputs are misclassification error bounds and sample complexity expressed as functions of the feature shift parameter β and dimension p, rather than empirical accuracy alone. Empirically, the paper also reports the per-class β distribution across ViT and ResNet-50 features to validate the theoretical framework.

## Method

The paper models pre-trained features through Neural Collapse theory, where ideal features form an equiangular tight frame (ETF), and introduces the feature shift parameter β = ‖x − M_k‖_∞ to quantify deviation from this ideal. The core result shows that when β ≤ p^{−1/2}, NoisyGD achieves dimension-independent sample complexity O(log(1/γ)) for misclassification error γ, in contrast to the typical 1/γ dependence of surrogate-loss analyses. The paper then analyzes robustness under stochastic, adversarial, and offset perturbations, finding that adversarial perturbations tighten the acceptable β threshold from p^{−1/2} to p^{−1}. To address non-robustness, the paper proposes PCA dimension reduction (relaxing the constraint from β²p ≤ 1 to ββ₀p ≤ 1) and feature normalization for offset perturbations, validated empirically on CIFAR-10 where projecting onto K−1 principal components restores dimension-independent NoisySGD performance.

## Results

The paper establishes that under Neural Collapse, NoisyGD achieves dimension-independent sample complexity O(log(1/γ)) when the feature shift parameter β ≤ p^{-1/2}. Empirically on CIFAR-10, the ImageNet-pretrained ViT yields β ≈ 0.10 while ResNet-50 yields β ≈ 0.20, confirming that stronger pre-training produces better feature representations. Under adversarial perturbations, the acceptable threshold tightens from p^{-1/2} to p^{-1}, making NoisySGD significantly more fragile. Applying PCA to project features onto K−1 = 9 principal components (k=10) before private fine-tuning restores dimension-independence and yields the best utility among tested projection sizes (k ∈ {10, 50, 100}).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing DP fine-tuning methods in a head-to-head accuracy comparison; instead, it compares pre-trained model choices and perturbation robustness. ViT outperforms ResNet-50 as a pre-trained backbone, achieving a feature shift parameter β ≈ 0.10 versus β ≈ 0.20 on CIFAR-10, indicating tighter Neural Collapse and thus better private fine-tuning potential. NoisySGD (private) degrades significantly as feature dimension increases under test-time Gaussian noise (variance 0.1), while non-private Linear SGD remains largely unaffected by dimension, confirming the theoretical dimension-dependence of the DP noise term. Among perturbation types, adversarial perturbations are the most damaging, tightening the β threshold from p^{-1/2} to p^{-1} and making the dimension-independence property the most fragile. PCA applied to the last-layer features before NoisySGD (projecting onto k=10 components) effectively restores dimension-independence, outperforming both no-PCA and larger projections (k=50, k=100), with k=100 actually degrading performance due to noise in additional principal directions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: Neural Collapse theory (Papyan et al., 2020), which characterizes the geometric structure of last-layer features in converged deep networks, and differential privacy in deep learning (Abadi et al., 2016; Song et al., 2013), where NoisyGD/NoisySGD suffers from a √p dimensionality curse. It builds on the DP fine-tuning paradigm (De et al., 2022; Li et al., 2021) and the initial dimension-independence analysis of Li et al. (2022), but provides a more structured explanation via the feature shift parameter β that quantifies deviation from ideal equiangular tight frame features. Unlike prior DP theory that analyzes convex surrogate losses, this work directly bounds 0-1 misclassification error, yielding logarithmic rather than polynomial sample complexity under near-perfect collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical explanation for why privately fine-tuning pre-trained models circumvents the dimensionality curse in DP learning, and identifies the precise condition (β ≤ p^{-1/2}) under which this holds. A key practical insight is that this dimension-independence is fragile: adversarial perturbations tighten the acceptable threshold to p^{-1}, and even minor stochastic noise degrades utility with increasing dimension. The proposed mitigations (PCA on last-layer features, feature normalization) are simple and theoretically grounded, but the analysis is restricted to last-layer fine-tuning and the NC setting; open questions include whether NC emerges when fine-tuning all layers under DP, how results extend to non-linear probes, and whether the fragility findings generalize beyond the controlled CIFAR-10 experiments.
