## Problem

Computerized Adaptive Testing (CAT) and Multistage Testing (MST) are the two dominant adaptive testing paradigms, but they operate at different granularities—CAT selects one question per step while MST selects pre-designed testlets—and no unified, data-driven framework exists to model both. This gap forces practitioners to rely on labor-intensive expert design of testlets and prevents automatic adaptation of the number of questions per step to individual students.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a student's sequential binary response history (correct/incorrect) and a question bank with calibrated IRT parameters (difficulty, discrimination) or NeuralCDM embeddings. At each step it outputs a variable-size question set (testlet) of up to M questions, where M is a hyperparameter that interpolates between CAT (M=1) and MST (M>1), and ultimately produces a scalar ability estimate θ. The structure parameters α are learned end-to-end from data, eliminating the need for manually predefined testlet pools.

## Method

UATS reformulates adaptive testing as a hierarchical structure search on a layered graph: each layer corresponds to a test step, and edges between a test node and question nodes encode which questions are selected. The discrete selection variables are relaxed to continuous weight parameters α, enabling differentiable bilevel optimization where the inner loop minimizes the IRT/NeuralCDM cross-entropy loss to estimate student ability θ, and the outer loop updates α to minimize the query-set loss. Because the global structure is intractable (future responses are unknown), the authors replace it with a greedy hierarchical search that alternates K inner gradient steps for θ and one outer gradient step for α at each test step. Theoretical analysis provides an exponential gradient-estimation error bound in K (Theorem 4.3) and a convergence guarantee for the outer gradient (Theorem 4.4), both relying on the strong convexity of IRT. The framework is model-agnostic, working with both IRT and NeuralCDM, and outperforms CAT and MST baselines while reducing test length by ~20%.

## Results

On the three real-world datasets (ASSISTments, NIPS-EDU, EXAM), UATS achieves the best ACC and AUC across all test lengths (5, 10, 20 steps) under both IRT and NeuralCDM cognitive diagnostic models. At 20 steps with IRT, UATS reaches 74.51% ACC / 78.63% AUC on EXAM, 68.17% / 74.85% on NIPS-EDU, and 67.07% / 68.00% on ASSISTments, yielding an average 1.31% relative AUC improvement and 0.71% ACC improvement over the strongest baseline (BOBCAT). In the ability estimation simulation, UATS achieves the same MSE with approximately 20% fewer questions. In the MST comparison, UATS outperforms all MST variants by an average of 2.7% in ACC and 3.1% in AUC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UATS outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and model configuration. The closest competitor is BOBCAT (bilevel optimization, IJCAI 2021), which UATS surpasses by 0.44–0.66 AUC points at 20 steps under IRT (e.g., 78.63 vs. 77.32 on EXAM) and by 0.84 AUC under NeuralCDM on NIPS-EDU (76.86 vs. 76.02). NCAT (RL-based DQN, AAAI 2022) is second-closest, trailing UATS by 0.74–1.97 AUC points depending on dataset and model. Information-based methods FSI (Lord, 2012) and KLI (Chang & Ying, 1996) are only applicable under IRT and are outperformed by 1.8–1.9 AUC points on EXAM. MAAT (Bi et al., 2020) and BECAT (Zhuang et al., 2023) show the largest gaps, with UATS exceeding them by 2.4–3.5 AUC points on NIPS-EDU under IRT. Notably, UATS also demonstrates more stable training convergence (Figure 4c) compared to the data-driven baselines BOBCAT and NCAT, with fewer performance fluctuations over 500 epochs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two long-standing but separate paradigms in adaptive testing—Computerized Adaptive Testing (CAT, one question per step) and Multistage Testing (MST, a fixed testlet per step)—under a single 'test structure search' formulation. Conceptually, it extends the bilevel optimization framework of BOBCAT (Ghosh & Lan, 2021) by allowing the inner-loop question set size to vary per step, and it draws on the greedy hierarchical search idea to make the global combinatorial problem tractable. The cognitive diagnosis layer (IRT or NeuralCDM) is treated as a plug-in, so the contribution is orthogonal to the student-modeling literature. The key departure from prior work is that testlet composition is learned end-to-end from data rather than hand-designed by psychometric experts, which was a hard prerequisite of MST.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the expert testlet-design bottleneck and unifying CAT/MST into one differentiable pipeline, UATS lowers the practical barrier to deploying adaptive testing in new domains (e.g., K-12 math, language proficiency) where psychometricians are scarce. The 20% reduction in test length at matched accuracy is a meaningful efficiency gain for high-stakes, high-volume settings. Open questions include fairness across student subgroups (acknowledged but deferred), scalability to question banks with millions of items, and whether the greedy hierarchical approximation loses significant optimality compared to true global search in very long tests. The paper also does not address test security or item exposure control, which are critical in real deployment.
