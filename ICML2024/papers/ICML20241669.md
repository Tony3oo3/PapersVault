## Problem

Conformal prediction provides distribution-free coverage guarantees but has traditionally required exchangeability or 'quasi-exchangeability' assumptions, excluding settings where AI/ML agents actively select data points and induce sequential feedback-loop distribution shifts (e.g., active learning, black-box optimization). This paper proves that conformal prediction can theoretically be extended to any joint data distribution, and provides a general procedure for deriving tractable weighted CP algorithms with valid coverage guarantees for specific non-exchangeable settings, particularly multistep feedback covariate shift (MFCS).

## Input / Output

The method takes a feature vector x, an IID-initialized calibration dataset of size n, a fitted ML predictor, and the agent's query probability functions p(x | Z_{1},...,Z_{j-1}) at each timestep (which are known in agent-induced MFCS). It produces a conformal prediction interval with a valid marginal coverage guarantee of at least 1-α. A key practical detail is the user-specified estimation depth d, which controls a tradeoff between computational cost (polynomial in n for small d) and approximation accuracy of the exact factorial-complexity weights; the interval width adapts automatically as the agent's shift accumulates over timesteps.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution reframes conformal prediction as an inverted permutation test, showing that the only role of exchangeability (or weighted exchangeability) is to simplify computation of the relative likelihood of each data ordering given the unordered set of observed values. Theorem 4.1 establishes that for any joint PDF f, a weighted CP set using weights P{Z_{n+1}=z_i | E_z} (computed via the joint density over all permutations) achieves valid coverage. For MFCS, the joint PDF factorizes into time-dependent agent query factors and time-invariant Y|X factors, the latter canceling in the weight ratio, leaving only the known agent query distributions. To make computation tractable, the paper introduces a d-step approximation that retains only the highest-order terms from the d most recent timesteps, reducing complexity from O((n+t)!) to O(∏_{j=t+1-d}^{t}(n+j)), and derives a recursive implementation for further efficiency when the ML model treats its training data symmetrically.

## Results

On protein design (Full CP, n=32, λ=8, 1,000 seeds), the proposed MFCS Full CP (d=2) maintains coverage at or above the 90% target across all 5 optimization steps on both blue and red fluorescent protein datasets, while both Standard Full CP and One-Step FCS Full CP fall below target at steps t=2,3,4. On protein design (Split CP with MLP, n_train=n_cal=32, λ=5, 500 seeds), MFCS Split CP with d∈{3,4} maintains target coverage across all 10 design steps where baselines break down. In active learning (GP regressor, 350 seeds, 70 steps), MFCS Split CP (d=3) sustains target coverage across all datasets and all 70 steps, with intervals becoming sharper as more labeled data accumulates, while Standard Split CP, One-Step FCS Split CP, and ACI all lose coverage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three baselines: Standard (Exchangeable) CP (Vovk et al., 2005), One-Step FCS CP (Fannjiang et al., 2022), and Adaptive Conformal Inference (ACI; Gibbs & Candès, 2021). The proposed MFCS CP method wins on the primary metric—maintaining valid 90% coverage—across all experimental settings where the baselines fail. Standard CP loses coverage immediately under sequential agent-induced shifts because its exchangeability assumption is violated from the first feedback step. One-Step FCS CP handles the first shift correctly (maintaining coverage at t=1) but degrades at subsequent steps (t≥2 in Full CP; later steps in Split CP and active learning), confirming that single-step correction is insufficient for accumulating multistep shifts. ACI, which retroactively adjusts thresholds, also fails to maintain coverage over the 70-step active learning horizon. The MFCS method's cost for maintaining validity is modestly wider prediction intervals relative to the invalid baselines at the steps where they lose coverage, but intervals are comparable in width where baselines still maintain coverage (e.g., t=1 and t=5 in Full CP protein design).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Tibshirani et al. (2019)'s weighted conformal prediction framework and Fannjiang et al. (2022)'s one-step feedback covariate shift (FCS) extension, but makes a key conceptual departure: it proves that the 'quasi-exchangeability' assumption (exchangeability, weighted exchangeability, pseudo-exchangeability) is not a theoretical necessity for conformal prediction validity, but merely a tractability condition. The authors reframe CP as an inverted permutation test where the only requirement is computing relative ordering probabilities, then provide a general three-step procedure (list assumptions → factorize the joint PDF → compute/estimate weights) to derive specific weighted CP algorithms for any distribution. They apply this procedure to multistep feedback covariate shift (MFCS), a deliberate generalization of one-step FCS to sequential agent-induced shifts that accumulate over time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) unifies prior CP validity guarantees as corollaries and removes a perceived theoretical boundary, potentially broadening the scope of settings where CP can be applied. Practically, the derived MFCS algorithms address a real gap: as AI agents autonomously collect data (active learning, Bayesian optimization, protein design), standard and one-step CP methods lose coverage, and this paper provides the first valid multi-step alternatives. Key limitations include the d-step weight approximation being heuristic (not guaranteed valid), the requirement that the agent's query distribution be known, and the computational cost of Full CP. Open questions include extending to conditional coverage, nonsymmetric score functions, and settings where the query distribution is unknown (requiring density-ratio estimation).
