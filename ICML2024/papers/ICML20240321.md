## Problem

Existing theoretical characterizations of overfitting in kernel ridgeless regression either rely on the Gaussian design assumption (which implicitly requires feature independence) or operate in asymptotic regimes where the input dimension diverges. This paper closes the gap by rigorously proving tempered and catastrophic overfitting under the weaker sub-Gaussian design assumption in a fixed input dimension, and identifies that feature independence—not just spectral decay rate—is a critical determinant of overfitting behavior.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a positive-definite kernel with a known eigen-spectrum (polynomial λ_k = Θ(k^{-1-a}) or exponential λ_k = Θ(e^{-ak})), a training set of size N with sub-Gaussian feature vectors of dimension M ≥ ηN, and additive sub-Gaussian noise. It produces matching non-asymptotic upper and lower bounds on the test error (excess risk), tight bounds on the condition number of the empirical kernel matrix K = ΨᵀΛΨ, and a classification of the overfitting regime. A key structural detail is that the bounds are expressed in terms of the ratio λ₁/λ_N and the sample size N, making the spectral decay rate the sole kernel-dependent quantity governing the regime.

## Method

The method proceeds in three steps. First, it derives non-asymptotic bounds on the condition number of the kernel matrix K = ΨᵀΛΨ using random matrix concentration: for polynomial eigen-decay, s_max/s_min = O(λ₁/λ_N) via Vershynin's concentration inequalities, while for exponential decay, s_max/s_min = Θ(λ₁/λ_N · N) via a combination of Tao's negative second moment identity and Gaussian anti-concentration. Second, it plugs these condition number bounds into the bias-variance decomposition framework of Tsigler & Bartlett (2023) to obtain matching upper and lower bounds on test error, yielding tempered overfitting (C ≤ R ≤ C⁻¹) for polynomial decay and catastrophic overfitting (R ≥ CN) for exponential decay. Third, it shows that when sub-Gaussian features are dependent (as in real kernel eigenfunctions), the smallest singular value lower bound acquires a random factor P that can vanish as N → ∞, breaking the tempered overfitting guarantee and allowing catastrophic overfitting even with polynomial decay—demonstrated empirically with the ReLU NTK.

## Results

The paper establishes that for kernels with polynomial eigen-decay (e.g., Laplacian), the condition number of the kernel matrix is O(λ₁/λ_N) and the test error is bounded by constants (tempered overfitting: C ≤ R ≤ C⁻¹). For kernels with exponential eigen-decay (e.g., Gaussian), the condition number scales as Θ(λ₁/λ_N · N) and the test error grows linearly with sample size (catastrophic overfitting: R ≥ CN). A key additional finding is that with dependent (non-independent) sub-Gaussian features, even polynomially decaying spectra can exhibit catastrophic overfitting, as the smallest singular value s_min(K) can vanish.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Mallinar et al. (2022), this paper relaxes the Gaussian design assumption to the weaker sub-Gaussian design assumption and provides non-asymptotic bounds (which Mallinar et al. lack), while also revealing that feature independence is essential for tempered overfitting with polynomial decay. Against Tsigler & Bartlett (2023), this paper avoids their stylized assumption of a bounded condition number by deriving tight non-asymptotic condition number bounds via random matrix concentration, and extends the analysis to exponential eigen-decay which Tsigler & Bartlett do not address. Compared to the concurrent work of Barzilai & Shamir (2023), this paper achieves tighter probability bounds (exponential decay vs. Markov-type), provides matching lower bounds on test error (not just upper bounds), and uniquely characterizes catastrophic overfitting under exponential eigendecay. The paper also identifies a qualitative gap: previous Gaussian-design-based analyses (Bordelon et al. 2020, Cui et al. 2021, Simon et al. 2021) cannot capture the possibility of catastrophic overfitting with polynomial eigen-decay when features are dependent, as demonstrated by the ReLU NTK counterexample.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the overfitting taxonomy of Mallinar et al. (2022) and the non-asymptotic test error bounds of Tsigler & Bartlett (2023) by relaxing the Gaussian design assumption to a weaker sub-Gaussian design assumption, and by providing the missing non-asymptotic condition number bounds for kernel matrices that Tsigler & Bartlett required as a stylized assumption. It also builds on the eigenlearning framework of Simon et al. (2021) and the replica-method analyses of Bordelon et al. (2020) and Cui et al. (2021), but departs from them by showing that the rotational invariance and smoothness properties of Gaussian vectors are not essential to the overfitting analysis. The key conceptual departure is identifying that feature independence—implicit in the Gaussian design but absent in the sub-Gaussian setting—plays a decisive role in whether polynomial eigendecay yields tempered or catastrophic overfitting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that dependent features can cause catastrophic overfitting even with polynomial eigendecay (as in the NTK counterexample) challenges the optimistic conclusion of Mallinar et al. (2022) that polynomial decay guarantees tempered overfitting, and raises concerns that prior literature relying on the Gaussian design assumption may have oversimplified the KRR setting. Open questions include: what additional properties of kernel features beyond the eigenspectrum determine overfitting behavior, whether realistic data distributions naturally produce independent eigenfunctions, and how to extend the analysis to truly infinite-rank kernels with dependent features. The practical limitation is that the sub-Gaussian design assumption still presumes finite rank and independent features, remaining distant from realistic kernel settings.
