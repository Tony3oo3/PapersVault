## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting relies on sparse SfM point clouds for initialization, which fail to produce points in textureless regions common in large-scale scenes (e.g., roads, walls). The unconstrained clone-and-split densification strategy then generates noisy, mispositioned Gaussians that overfit training views, causing significant rendering degradation in novel views. The paper addresses this by asking how to guide Gaussian densification using the scene's own geometric structure rather than relying solely on gradient-driven splitting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed RGB images (e.g., 9 scenes from Waymo with 1-in-8 held out for testing, or 9 scenes from MipNeRF360) along with a COLMAP SfM point cloud for Gaussian initialization. For outdoor scenes, sky regions are segmented with SegFormer and masked out during propagation. The output is a 3D Gaussian scene representation rendered at ~108 FPS via splatting-based rasterization, producing novel-view images with significantly improved PSNR (34.68 vs. 33.53 on Waymo) while using fewer Gaussians than the baseline.

## Method

GaussianPro introduces a progressive propagation strategy that renders depth and normal maps from existing Gaussians via alpha blending, then iteratively propagates these values from well-modeled neighboring pixels to under-modeled ones using patch matching with homography warping and NCC-based photometric consistency. Pixels whose propagated depth deviates significantly from the rendered depth (threshold σ=0.8) are backprojected into 3D to initialize new Gaussians with accurate positions and orientations, activated every 50 training iterations. A planar constraint loss (L1 + angular normal consistency plus a scale regularization that flattens Gaussians) further regularizes optimization to keep Gaussian shapes aligned with true surface geometry. This hybrid 3D/2D approach transfers geometric priors from textured regions to textureless ones, achieving better rendering with fewer, more compact Gaussians compared to 3DGS's unconstrained densification.

## Results

On the large-scale Waymo dataset, GaussianPro achieves 34.68 PSNR, 0.949 SSIM, and 0.191 LPIPS, surpassing 3DGS by 1.15 dB in PSNR and outperforming Zip-NeRF (34.22 PSNR) while maintaining real-time rendering at 108 FPS. On MipNeRF360, GaussianPro reaches 27.92 PSNR and 0.825 SSIM, slightly edging out retrained 3DGS (27.88 PSNR) but remaining below Zip-NeRF (28.54 PSNR) in quality, though at ~1200× higher rendering speed. The method also demonstrates superior depth accuracy on Waymo, reducing Abs Rel from 0.349 (3DGS) to 0.081 and improving δ₁ from 0.570 to 0.933.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS (the primary baseline), GaussianPro achieves a clear 1.15 dB PSNR gain on Waymo (34.68 vs. 33.53) with better SSIM and LPIPS, and a modest 0.71 dB improvement on MipNeRF360 (27.92 vs. 27.21 original; 0.04 dB over retrained 3DGS at 27.88). Against Zip-NeRF, GaussianPro wins on Waymo (34.68 vs. 34.22 PSNR) but trails on MipNeRF360 (27.92 vs. 28.54 PSNR); however, GaussianPro renders at 108 FPS versus Zip-NeRF's 0.09 FPS, a ~1200× speed advantage. Against Instant-NGP and Mip-NeRF360, GaussianPro dominates on both datasets in all metrics while being orders of magnitude faster. The paper also shows that simply feeding denser MVS point clouds into 3DGS (MVS+3DGS) yields comparable PSNR but at ~4× training time and significantly lower FPS (75 vs. 108), confirming that GaussianPro's progressive propagation is more efficient than brute-force denser initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023) by replacing its unconstrained clone-and-split densification with a progressive propagation strategy inspired by classic multi-view stereo patch matching (Bleyer et al., 2011; Xu & Tao, 2019). The key conceptual departure is working in both 3D world space and 2D image space: rendered depth and normal maps are propagated across neighboring pixels via homography-based patch matching, and the resulting geometric priors guide where new Gaussians are placed and how they are oriented. A planar constraint loss (building on NeuSG's scale regularization) further regularizes Gaussian shapes toward planar surfaces, addressing the geometric drift that 3DGS exhibits in textureless regions where SfM initialization is sparse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and well-motivated failure mode of 3DGS in large-scale outdoor scenes (e.g., autonomous driving) where textureless surfaces cause SfM to produce sparse point clouds, leading to poor Gaussian coverage and noisy geometry. The progressive propagation strategy achieves this without requiring a separate MVS preprocessing step, maintaining real-time rendering speed (108 FPS) while significantly improving depth accuracy (Abs Rel drops from 0.349 to 0.081 on Waymo). A notable finding is that fewer but better-placed Gaussians (1147k) outperform more but noisier ones (1629k), suggesting placement quality matters more than quantity. Limitations include no handling of dynamic objects and modest gains on small-scale, texture-rich scenes where SfM already provides good initialization.
