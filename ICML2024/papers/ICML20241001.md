## Problem

Existing continuous Wasserstein barycenter solvers either rely on difficult tri-level (min-max-min) adversarial objectives or are restricted to quadratic ground cost functions with entropic regularization. This paper addresses the gap by deriving a bi-level (max-min) formulation that accommodates general weak OT cost functions, enabling principled barycenter estimation in settings where prior methods are inapplicable or impractical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241001/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes empirical samples from K reference distributions P_k (e.g., 32×32 MNIST images, CelebA face crops, or 2D point clouds) and, for regularized variants, a prior distribution μ_0 (e.g., a Gaussian in latent space). It produces learned transport maps T_k that, given a sample x_k from P_k, generate samples from the barycenter Q*; the barycenter is implicitly defined as the common second marginal of all T_k. In manifold-constrained experiments, the maps are composed with a pre-trained StyleGAN generator so that outputs lie on the image manifold, and the cost functions become non-quadratic by construction.

## Method

The core contribution is a max-min dual formulation for the weak OT barycenter problem: sup over congruent potentials (Σλ_k f_k ≡ 0) of inf over transport maps of a weighted sum of weak cost terms minus potential evaluations. Transport plans are parameterized as stochastic maps T_k: X_k × S → Y (or deterministic maps for classical OT), and the congruence condition is enforced by parameterizing each potential as f_{k,θ} = g_{θ_k} − Σλ_{k'}g_{θ_{k'}}. Training proceeds via stochastic gradient ascent-descent, performing multiple inner minimization steps on the maps per outer maximization step on the potentials, with Monte Carlo estimation of the weak cost functionals. The approach supports classical, ε-KL, and γ-Energy cost functions, and Theorem 4.2 provides duality-gap-based error bounds on the recovered plans in Wasserstein-2, total variation, and energy-distance metrics respectively.

## Results

On the Ave, Celeba! barycenter benchmark in data space, the proposed method achieves FID scores of 39.0, 38.6, and 39.8 (for k=1,2,3), substantially outperforming SCWB (56.7, 53.2, 58.8) and WIN (49.3, 46.9, 61.5). In the manifold-constrained (StyleGAN latent space) setup, EgBary achieves lower FID (8.4–10.2) than the proposed method (30.7–31.7), though the latter avoids expensive MCMC sampling. On multi-dimensional Gaussian barycenter experiments, the proposed method achieves the best L2-UVP across all dimensions (0.01–0.08%), beating EgBary (0.02–0.84%) and WIN (0.03–0.75%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the data-space CelebA benchmark, the proposed method clearly outperforms both SCWB (Fan et al., 2021) and WIN (Korotin et al., 2022), achieving FID ~39 versus ~53–59 for SCWB and ~47–62 for WIN, a gap of roughly 10–20 FID points. In the manifold-constrained (StyleGAN latent space) setup, EgBary (Kolesov et al., 2023) substantially outperforms the proposed method (FID 8.4–10.2 vs. 30.7–31.7), which the authors attribute to EgBary's use of Langevin MCMC sampling that better suits latent-space optimization, albeit at significant computational cost. On the multi-dimensional Gaussian barycenter task (Table 3), the proposed method achieves the lowest L2-UVP at every dimension tested (2 through 64), with a particularly large margin at D=64 (0.08% vs. 0.84% for EgBary and 0.75% for WIN). Overall, the method wins in data-space and Gaussian settings but loses to EgBary in the most demanding manifold-constrained image generation task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines the Neural OT solver (Korotin et al., 2023b), which uses a bi-level adversarial max-min objective for weak optimal transport, with a congruence condition on the dual potentials (Σλ_k f_k = 0) to derive a practical barycenter algorithm. This departs from prior continuous barycenter methods that either require tri-level (min-max-min) adversarial objectives (Fan et al., 2021; Korotin et al., 2022) or are restricted to quadratic ground costs (Noble et al., 2023) or fixed priors (Li et al., 2020). The theoretical analysis generalizes error bounds from Kolesov et al. (2023) to cover classical, ε-KL, and γ-Energy weak cost functions, and the computational algorithm follows the standard stochastic gradient ascent-descent paradigm widely used in generative modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is flexibility: the method handles general (non-quadratic) cost functions without requiring exotic optimization procedures, enabling applications like the shape-color barycenter experiment where non-Euclidean costs separate shape and color information. However, the method underperforms EgBary (which uses Langevin MCMC) on the CelebA manifold benchmark (FID ~31 vs ~9), suggesting that adversarial bi-level training may not be optimal for latent-space settings. Open questions include whether the strong convexity assumptions needed for classical cost guarantees can be relaxed, whether the Gaussian parameterization bias can be mitigated, and whether the shape-color paradigm generalizes to more complex multi-attribute aggregation tasks.
