## Problem

Traditional RLHF alignment pipelines (PPO, DPO) require costly preference annotation, multi-model training, and are inflexible for adapting to diverse or personalized user preferences. The paper asks whether it is possible to directly estimate the decoding results of an aligned policy in a single inference step, obtaining RLHF benefits without any parameter tuning, data compilation, or external supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240587/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a dialog context, a natural-language preference principle prompt (e.g., 'be helpful and harmless'), and a frozen SFT model as input. At each decoding step it performs two forward passes (with and without the principle prompt) to compute a logit difference, applies a closed-form logit update, and then samples the next token. The output is a single preference-aligned response generated in one pass, consuming roughly 2× the inference cost of standard generation (two forward passes that can be merged into one batch).

## Method

The core contribution is a closed-form solution to policy optimization under a p-norm divergence constraint, derived by formulating the alignment objective as a Lagrangian dual and applying a first-order Taylor approximation of the Q-function around the original policy. The optimal logit vector μ* is computed as the original logits plus a scaled, normalized gradient direction, where the scaling factor depends only on the divergence budget and the constraint's partition function. The gradient direction is estimated via Self-Contrastive Decoding: the difference between logits produced with and without a preference principle prompt (p(μ|s,τ*) − p(μ|s)) serves as a proxy for the value-function gradient, eliminating the need for a trained reward model. A single hyperparameter (step size) controls the update magnitude, and the entire procedure operates entirely at inference time on a frozen SFT model, requiring no training data, annotation, or additional model parameters.

## Results

On the Anthropic-RLHF-HH general preference benchmark, Linear Alignment achieves a 40.9% win rate against the SFT baseline on Vicuna-7B (vs. 32.4% for PPO and 31.3% for DPO) and 35.2% on Mistral-7B (vs. 36.1% for PPO and 28.8% for DPO). On the novel Personal Preference Dataset (536 samples, 5 domains), Mistral-7B + Linear Alignment reaches 77.2% total accuracy, surpassing ChatGPT (76.6%) and significantly outperforming Llama2-13B-Chat (59.1%). The method requires only ~8% additional inference time (42.4 vs. 46 tokens/s) and ~9% more GPU memory compared to standard inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PPO, Linear Alignment achieves a higher win rate on Vicuna (40.9% vs. 32.4%) and a comparable rate on Mistral (35.2% vs. 36.1%), while also producing a more concentrated reward distribution (Figure 3) without requiring four collaboratively trained models. Against DPO, Linear Alignment clearly wins on both backbones (40.9% vs. 31.3% on Vicuna; 35.2% vs. 28.8% on Mistral), which the authors attribute to DPO's tendency to overfit training sequences. Against Best-of-N sampling, Linear Alignment wins in direct pairwise GPT-4 comparisons (Figure 2), though Best-of-50 shows a nominally higher win rate against SFT in Table 2 (48.6% vs. 40.9% on Vicuna) — a gap the authors explain as a GPT-4 evaluation bias toward homologous (same-distribution) response pairs. On the personalized preference benchmark, Mistral-7B + Linear Alignment (77.2%) exceeds ChatGPT (76.6%) and GPT-4 remains the strongest at 91.1%, while Llama2-13B-Chat (59.1%) shows the weakest personalization, suggesting over-alignment to general 2H principles impairs individual preference understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads: (1) the closed-form policy improvement operators from TRPO's constrained optimization (Schulman et al., 2015; Ciosek et al., 2019; Li et al., 2023a), (2) contrastive decoding (Li et al., 2022), repurposed here as Self-Contrastive Decoding to estimate value-function gradients from the same model's prompted vs. unprompted outputs, and (3) Constitutional AI's principle prompting (Bai et al., 2022b) as the mechanism for specifying preference directions. The key departure from prior alignment work (PPO, DPO, Best-of-N) is that instead of iteratively updating parameters or sampling-and-filtering, the method computes the optimal output distribution in a single closed-form step at inference time, treating alignment as a one-shot logit perturbation rather than a training procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that personalized preference alignment becomes a per-inference operation rather than a per-deployment training pipeline, enabling dynamic adaptation to individual users without retraining or annotation. However, the approach inherits the SFT model's capacity to interpret principle prompts, so its effectiveness is bounded by the base model's instruction-following quality, and the ~2× inference cost (two forward passes per token) limits its applicability in latency-sensitive settings. Open questions include whether the linear approximation remains valid for multi-dimensional or conflicting preferences, how the method behaves when the SFT model's prompted and unprompted distributions are nearly identical (as observed with larger models), and whether the personal preference gains generalize beyond the 536-sample evaluation set.
