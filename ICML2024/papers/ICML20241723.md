## Problem

No existing batched linear bandit algorithm simultaneously achieves minimax optimal regret, asymptotically optimal regret, and optimal batch complexity. Prior work either attained minimax optimality with suboptimal O(log T) batches, achieved asymptotic optimality only in fully sequential settings, or combined both optimality types but required far more batches (e.g., IDS needs O(log⁴ T)). This paper closes the gap by proving a lower bound of 3 batches for any asymptotically optimal algorithm and constructing an algorithm that matches it.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a fixed arm set X ⊂ R^d with K arms (each arm a d-dimensional feature vector), a time horizon T, and user-specified exploration rate sequences {T_ℓ} and elimination parameters {ε_ℓ}. It outputs a batched pull schedule where within each batch arms are pulled in parallel without policy switching, and the batch sizes are determined adaptively from data collected in prior batches. The algorithm terminates by committing to the estimated best arm for the remaining pulls, and the theoretical guarantees are expressed as regret bounds R_T and batch complexity in terms of d, K, T, and Δ_min.

## Method

E4 follows an Explore-Estimate-Eliminate-Exploit framework across batches. Batch 1 performs D-optimal design exploration (solved approximately via Frank-Wolfe) to obtain initial least-squares estimates, from which a novel allocation w is computed by solving a modified optimal allocation problem that uses under-estimated gaps (Δ̂_x − 4ε) and caps the allocation on the estimated best arm. Batch 2 combines D-optimal design pulls with the allocation w·α log T pulls, then applies a Chernoff stopping rule to test whether the estimated best arm is correct with probability ≥ 1−1/T; if it holds, all suboptimal arms are eliminated immediately. Batches ≥ 3 (entered only with probability O(1/(log T)²)) perform phased elimination with geometrically increasing D-optimal design rates. The key insight is that the modified allocation in Definition 4.3 ensures the Chernoff rule holds after batch 2 with high probability, yielding exactly 3 batches for asymptotic optimality while the phased elimination fallback preserves the Õ(√dT) minimax regret with O(log log T) batches.

## Results

E⁴ achieves Õ(√(dT)) worst-case regret with O(log log T) batches, matching the known lower bounds for linear contextual bandits. In the asymptotic setting, E⁴ achieves the optimal regret limsup R_T/log T ≤ c* with only 3 batches, which is proven to be the minimum possible (Theorem 5.2). On the End of Optimism instances (d=5, K=9, T=100000), E⁴ uses exactly 3.0±0.0 batches while PhaElimD uses 4.0, rs-OFUL uses ~102, and IDS requires >10⁵ batches. E⁴ is also the fastest algorithm, with runtimes of 0.04–0.33 seconds versus 6.42–246.53 seconds for IDS (a 5× to 1000× speedup).

## Baselines & Comparisons

E⁴ outperforms all four baselines on batch complexity: it uses exactly 3 batches on End of Optimism instances versus 4 for PhaElimD, 36–102 for rs-OFUL, and >10⁵ for IDS (sequential EndOA uses T batches). On regret, E⁴ matches IDS (both achieve asymptotically optimal regret) while significantly outperforming PhaElimD, rs-OFUL, and EndOA, whose regret grows substantially as ε decreases. In terms of runtime, E⁴ is consistently the fastest: 0.04–0.33 s versus 0.15–1.46 s (PhaElimD), 0.28–3.72 s (rs-OFUL), 2.23–10.19 s (EndOA), and 6.42–246.53 s (IDS), representing a 5× to 1000× speedup. The key differentiator is that E⁴ is the only algorithm simultaneously achieving minimax-optimal regret, asymptotic optimality, and optimal batch complexity in both finite-time and asymptotic settings, whereas IDS achieves minimax and asymptotic optimality but with a much higher batch complexity of O(log⁴T) and a suboptimal Õ(d√T) worst-case regret for fixed K.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: batched linear bandits (Gao et al. 2019; Esfandiari et al. 2021; Ruan et al. 2021; Hanna et al. 2023), which achieved minimax-optimal regret with O(log log T) batches but lacked asymptotic guarantees, and asymptotically optimal linear bandits (Lattimore & Szepesvári 2017; Combes et al. 2017; Kirschner et al. 2021), which achieved the optimal c* regret constant but were fully sequential. The E4 algorithm departs from prior batched methods by incorporating a modified optimal allocation (Definition 4.3, which uses estimated gaps minus a margin and adds a constraint on the best arm's allocation) combined with Chernoff's stopping rule from best-arm identification, enabling the algorithm to identify the optimal arm in just 3 batches with high probability. The D-optimal design exploration and phased elimination framework follows Lattimore & Szepesvári (2020), but the specific combination with the allocation-based second batch and the stopping rule is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

E4 is the first batched linear bandit algorithm to simultaneously achieve minimax-optimal regret (Õ(√dT)), asymptotically optimal regret (matching c*), and optimal batch complexity (O(log log T) for finite-time, 3 for asymptotic), closing a gap that no prior algorithm filled. The lower bound proving that 3 batches are necessary for asymptotic optimality (Theorem 5.2) is a clean, first-of-its-kind result that gives the field a precise target. The main open question, acknowledged by the authors, is whether optimal instance-dependent regret can be achieved while maintaining optimal batch complexity — the current instance-dependent bound requires O(log T) batches, which is near-optimal but not tight. Practically, the 5–1000× runtime speedup over IDS and the End of Optimism robustness make this relevant for applications where policy switching is expensive (clinical trials, e-commerce).
