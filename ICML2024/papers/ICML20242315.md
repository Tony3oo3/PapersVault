## Problem

The choice between deeper and wider neural network architectures remains unresolved, particularly under Sobolev training where loss functions penalize derivatives of the network output. Existing generalization error analyses for wider networks (WeNNs) are well-established, but for deeper networks (DeNNs), prior works either impose uniform parameter bounds that negate the super-convergence advantage of depth, or rely on suboptimal complexity measures like Rademacher complexity. This paper establishes near-optimal generalization error bounds for DeNNs without uniform parameter constraints and provides a principled criterion for selecting depth versus width.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a target function f ∈ W^{n,∞}([0,1]^d) with ‖f‖ ≤ 1, M i.i.d. uniform sample points, and a network with W = O(N²L) parameters (width N, depth L). The output is a generalization error bound of the form O((W/(log W)²)^{-4(n-k)/d} + W²/M · log M) for H^k losses (k=0,1,2), from which a phase diagram in the (W,M) plane determines whether DeNNs or WeNNs yield smaller error. For PDE applications (deep Ritz, PINN), the input additionally includes interior and boundary sample points, and the output is an energy or residual error bound for the approximate PDE solution.

## Method

The generalization error is decomposed into an approximation error (exploiting super-convergence rates of DeNNs/DSRNs, giving O(W^{-2(n-k)/d}) up to log factors) and a sampling error (O(W²/M · log M)). The key technical challenge is bounding the sampling error without uniform parameter bounds: since DeNNs use bit-extraction techniques that require unbounded weights, standard covering numbers cannot be directly estimated. Instead, the paper uses uniform covering numbers bounded via pseudo-dimension, which in turn requires a novel VC-dimension analysis of the derivative and Laplacian operator applied to deep ReLU/DSRN architectures, yielding Pdim = O(N²L² log L log N). The comparison with WeNNs (whose error is O(W^{-(n-k)/d} + W/M)) reveals that DeNNs dominate when M ≥ W^{(2n+2d-2k)/d}, while WeNNs dominate otherwise, and increasing the Sobolev order k shifts the boundary in favor of DeNNs.

## Results

The paper establishes that DeNNs achieve a super-convergence approximation error of O(W^{-4(n-k)/d}) (up to log factors) under H^k loss, compared to O(W^{-2(n-k)/d}) for WeNNs, but at the cost of a larger sampling error of O(W²/M log M) versus O(W/M) for WeNNs. The crossover threshold is M ≥ W^{(2n+2d-2k)/d}: above this, DeNNs yield strictly better generalization error; below it, WeNNs are preferable. In the small empirical validation (Table 2), a shallow network (Depth 1, Width 20) achieves mean test error 1.51e-3 in the small-data regime versus 4.96e-3 for a deep network (Depth 4, Width 10), while the deep network wins in the large-data regime (3.69e-4 vs 6.18e-4), consistent with the theoretical prediction.

**Table 1: Approximation and sampling errors for WeNNs and DeNNs under different Sobolev losses (up to logarithmic factors).**

| Loss | WeNNs Approx. | WeNNs Sampling | DeNNs Approx. | DeNNs Sampling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L² (k=0) | O(W^{-n/d}) | O(W/M) | O(W^{-2n/d}) | O(W²/M) |
| H¹ (k=1) | O(W^{-(n-1)/d}) | O(W/M) | O(W^{-2(n-1)/d}) | O(W²/M) |
| H² (k=2) | O(W^{-(n-2)/d}) | O(W/M) | O(W^{-2(n-2)/d}) | O(W²/M) |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the WeNNs generalization error bounds from Schmidt-Hieber (2020) and Suzuki (2018), which give O(W^{-2(n-k)/d} + W/M) for shallow networks with bounded parameters. The DeNNs result in this paper achieves a strictly better approximation rate (exponent doubled: -4(n-k)/d vs -2(n-k)/d) but a worse sampling rate (W²/M vs W/M). The net effect is that DeNNs outperform WeNNs when M ≥ W^{(2n+2d-2k)/d} (abundant samples, limited parameters), while WeNNs are superior in the opposite regime (large W, limited M). Additionally, as the Sobolev order k increases (e.g., from L² to H¹ to H²), the crossover threshold shifts leftward, expanding the region where DeNNs are preferable — meaning higher-regularity loss functions (as in PINN with H² loss) favor deeper architectures. The empirical results in Table 2 confirm this: the deep network (Depth 4, Width 10) underperforms the shallow network (Depth 1, Width 20) in the small-data regime but outperforms it in the large-data regime, matching the theoretical crossover prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generalization error analysis of Schmidt-Hieber (2020) and Suzuki (2018) for wider networks (WeNNs) to deeper networks (DeNNs) with flexible depth, while simultaneously extending the loss function from L² to Sobolev H^k norms (k=0,1,2). It departs from prior DeNNs generalization analyses (Duan et al. 2021, Berner et al. 2020, Advani et al. 2020) by removing the uniform parameter bound constraint that previously limited the super-convergence advantage of deep architectures, instead using uniform covering numbers bounded via pseudo-dimension (building on Bartlett et al. 2019 and Yang et al. 2023b). The approximation rates leverage the nearly optimal deep network results of Lu et al. (2021a), Siegel (2022), and Yang et al. (2023a,b), including the Deep Super ReLU Network architecture for second-order derivatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete phase diagram (Figure 1) that tells practitioners when to choose depth over width based on parameter count, sample size, and loss regularity — a decision that has been largely heuristic in practice. The extension to deep Ritz and PINN methods with flexible-depth networks is practically relevant for PDE solving, where prior error analyses only covered shallow architectures. Key limitations include the restriction to the underparameterized regime, the 1D Poisson equation in the PDE application, and the curse of dimensionality inherent in Sobolev spaces; the overparameterized regime, Korobov/Barron spaces, and CNN generalization are explicitly deferred to future work.
