## Problem

Deterministic CBP-based partial monitoring strategies offer regret guarantees on both easy and hard games but are consistently outperformed empirically by stochastic strategies whose guarantees are limited to easy games. In the contextual setting, CBPside was further restricted to easy games only. The paper asks whether randomizing confidence bounds—a technique proven effective for OFU-based bandit strategies—can also improve non-OFU successive-elimination strategies like CBP while preserving their theoretical guarantees.

## Input / Output

At each round the agent receives a context x_t (contextual setting) and a feedback symbol H[I_t, J_t] from a finite, non-numeric alphabet; the loss L[I_t, J_t] and the true outcome J_t are never observed. The agent outputs an action I_t selected from a candidate set S(t) that combines potentially optimal actions, neighbor actions, and underplayed informative actions. In the τ-detection use case, the per-class output reduces to a binary pass/verify decision that determines whether to spend a verification budget on a predicted class.

## Method

RandCBP replaces CBP's deterministic confidence width c_{ij}(t) = Σ‖v_{ija}‖∞√(α log t / n_a) with a randomized width c'_{ij}(t) = Σ‖v_{ija}‖∞ Z_{ijt}/√(n_a), where Z_{ijt} is drawn from a discretized, truncated Gaussian over K bins in [A, B] with tail probability ε and standard deviation σ. Because the expected randomized width is smaller than the deterministic one, the successive-elimination criterion (|δ̂| > c') is less restrictive, so more action pairs enter the confident set U(t) earlier, tightening the constrained simplex D(t) around p* and accelerating exploitation. For the contextual setting, CBPside* introduces pseudo-counts 1/‖x_t‖²_{G_{a,t}^{-1}} (derived from the inverse Gram matrix) in place of raw action counts, making exploration context-aware and enabling hard-game applicability; RandCBPside* then applies the same randomized-bound mechanism with a ridge-regression confidence width. The regret analysis shows the randomized bounds are always upper-bounded by their deterministic counterparts, so all existing CBP/CBPside regret guarantees carry over unchanged.

## Results

On non-contextual PM games (Apple Tasting and Label Efficient, T=20k, 96 runs), RandCBP achieves the lowest average cumulative regret in all four configurations (imbalanced/balanced × easy/hard), with statistically significant improvements (p<0.01) over all baselines in 3 of 4 settings. On contextual games (10-d contexts, T=20k), RandCBPside* achieves the best regret in both AT and LE, significantly outperforming CBPside*, STAP, and CESA (p<0.01). In the τ-detection use case, C-RandCBP reduces verification cost by 15–73% (case 1) and 18–62% (case 2) relative to the non-adaptive Explore-fully baseline while maintaining comparable f1-scores (e.g., 0.95±0.16 vs 0.96±0.15 at τ=0.025).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CBP (deterministic), RandCBP achieves significantly lower regret in imbalanced AT, imbalanced LE, and balanced LE (p<0.01), but is not statistically different in balanced AT (p=0.055), where CBP's high variance (std=138) masks the difference; RandCBP still wins 37/96 runs vs CBP's lower count. Against TSPM (Thompson Sampling for PM), RandCBP is significantly better in imbalanced AT and both LE settings, but ties in balanced AT (p=0.854), with RandCBP winning 37 runs vs TSPM's 13. Against PM-DMED and BPM-Least, RandCBP dominates across all four non-contextual configurations. In the contextual setting, RandCBPside* significantly outperforms CBPside* (its deterministic counterpart), STAP, and CESA (p<0.01 in both games); PG-IDS achieves lower regret on a truncated horizon (T=7.5k) in AT but scales cubically with the number of contexts, making it impractical for long horizons where RandCBPside* maintains lower complexity via Sherman-Morrison updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the randomization of confidence bounds—previously shown to improve empirical performance for OFU (optimistic-in-the-face-of-uncertainty) strategies in multi-armed bandits (Kveton et al., 2019; Vaswani et al., 2020)—to the fundamentally different class of successive elimination strategies in the broader partial monitoring framework. The core algorithmic building block is CBP (Bartók et al., 2012b), which uses both upper and lower confidence bounds to eliminate action pairs, rather than the single upper bound used in OFU. In the contextual setting, the paper corrects the confidence bound of CBPside (Bartók et al., 2012a; Lienert, 2013) by replacing a factor of d with σ_a, and introduces pseudo-counts based on the inverse Gram matrix to make exploration meaningful when contexts are drawn from a continuous space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first regret guarantees for hard contextual PM games without requiring the context set to be finite and known in advance, filling a notable gap in the PM literature. The τ-detection use case demonstrates that PM can be formulated for a concrete, practically relevant problem (cost-efficient verification of deployed classifiers), reducing verification costs by 62–73% relative to a non-adaptive baseline. Key limitations include the need to tune randomization hyperparameters (ε, σ, K) in an online setting where such tuning is difficult, and the empirical evaluation is restricted to small games (2–3 actions, 2 outcomes). Open questions include obtaining tighter regret bounds, establishing lower bounds in the contextual setting, and extending the approach to logistic and neural contextual PM settings.
