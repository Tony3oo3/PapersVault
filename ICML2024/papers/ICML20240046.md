## Problem

Prior work on Byzantine-robust federated learning has largely studied a simplified setting where all clients participate each round and perform only a single local step, ignoring two fundamental FL characteristics: client subsampling and multiple local updates. Client subsampling increases the effective fraction of Byzantine clients in each round, while local steps amplify drift among honest clients' updates, meaning a careless deployment of robust averaging (FedRo) can fail to converge. The paper asks: under what conditions on subsampling size and local steps does FedRo still achieve near-optimal convergence?

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes an initial model x₀, a pool of n clients each holding a local data distribution D⁽ⁱ⁾, and hyperparameters including the per-round sample size n̂, the tolerable Byzantine count b̂ < n̂/2, the number of local steps K, and two step-sizes (client γ_c, server γ_s). Each round, the server broadcasts x_t to n̂ randomly sampled clients; honest clients return K-step local update vectors u_t⁽ⁱ⁾ = x_{t,K}⁽ⁱ⁾ − x_t, while Byzantine clients may send arbitrary vectors. The output is a model x̂ (uniformly random from the T iterates) satisfying E[‖∇F(x̂)‖²] ≤ ε, where F is the average loss over honest clients, with the guarantee holding with probability at least p conditional on the subsampling condition being met.

## Method

FedRo replaces FedAvg's server-side averaging with an (n̂, b̂, κ)-robust aggregation rule A (e.g., coordinate-wise trimmed mean, Krum, geometric median) that bounds its deviation from the honest-client average by κ/|S| times the honest variance. The key theoretical contribution is a sufficient condition on n̂ involving the KL divergence D(b̂/n̂, b/n) that ensures the sampled Byzantine count stays below b̂ in all T rounds with probability ≥ p, plus a convergence rate whose non-vanishing Byzantine error term scales as (b̂/n̂)(σ²/K + ζ²) — notably decreasing with K local steps because each honest update approximates an average of K stochastic gradients. The paper further identifies a threshold n̂_th below which convergence is impossible and a second threshold n̂_opt beyond which the error order saturates (the 'diminishing return' of FedRo), and shows that for n̂ ≥ n̂_opt the asymptotic error is order-optimal at O((b/n)(σ²/K + ζ²)) without requiring all clients to participate.

## Results

The paper's empirical validation on FEMNIST (n=150, b=30) confirms that FedRo fails to converge when the number of subsampled clients drops below 30, validating the theoretical threshold in Lemma 3. With n=150 and b=15, the computed convergence threshold is n̂_th=26 (FEMNIST) and n̂_th=29 (CIFAR-10), with optimal Byzantine tolerances b̂_⋆=11 and b̂_⋆=13 respectively. Increasing the number of local steps K from 5 to 35 (with step-size scaled as Θ(1/K)) consistently improves model accuracy against stronger attacks (FOE, ALIE, mimic), confirming the theoretical prediction that the Byzantine-induced error term scales as O(σ²/K + ζ²). The diminishing return phenomenon is also observed: beyond n̂_opt, further increases in subsampling yield only marginal accuracy gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not perform a head-to-head comparison against other Byzantine-robust FL methods (e.g., Krum, MDA, or the scheme from Data & Diggavi 2021); instead, it validates its own theoretical predictions empirically. Within FedRo, the paper compares two aggregation rules — NNM (Allouah et al., 2023) and coordinate-wise Trimmed Mean (Yin et al., 2018a) — across four Byzantine attacks (sign flipping, fall of empires, ALIE, and mimic) on both FEMNIST and CIFAR-10. NNM generally provides stronger robustness against the more sophisticated attacks (FOE, ALIE, mimic), while Trimmed Mean performs comparably on simpler attacks. The paper also contrasts its convergence guarantee with that of Data & Diggavi (2021), noting that their bound has a residual asymptotic error of O(Kσ² + Kζ²) that grows with local steps, whereas FedRo's Byzantine error term O(σ²/K + ζ²) decreases with K. A concurrent work by Malinovsky et al. (2023) is discussed qualitatively, with the authors arguing its asymptotic error of O(ζ²/p) is sub-optimal by a factor of p when clients hold many local data points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Byzantine-robust federated learning line of work (Blanchard et al., 2017; Allouah et al., 2023) by rigorously analyzing how two standard FL features—client subsampling and multiple local steps—affect convergence of FedRo, which prior work largely ignored. The only prior study considering both features (Data & Diggavi, 2021) assumed Byzantine clients are always below one-third of the sample and yielded an asymptotic error proportional to K (local steps), whereas this paper shows the error is inversely proportional to K under careful step-size choices. The analysis builds on the (n,b,κ)-robustness framework of Allouah et al. (2023) and the FedAvg convergence analysis of Karimireddy et al. (2020), unifying them with a KL-divergence-based condition on the subsampling size.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first tight convergence guarantees for Byzantine-robust FL under realistic deployment conditions (partial participation, local SGD), giving practitioners concrete thresholds (n̂_th, n̂_opt) for choosing the number of clients to sample. The counterintuitive finding that more local steps reduce the Byzantine error term (σ²/K) challenges the standard wisdom that local steps are purely detrimental, and the 'diminishing return' phenomenon shows that sampling all clients is unnecessary for order-optimal error. Open questions include closing the O(κσ²) gap between upper and lower bounds in the federated setting, and the fundamental trade-off between robustness (filtering outliers) and inclusivity (preserving minority data distributions) highlighted in the Impact Statement.
