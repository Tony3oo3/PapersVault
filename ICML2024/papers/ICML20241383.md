## Problem

World models in model-based RL implicitly perform two tasks—observation modeling and reward modeling—on a shared representation, yet standard approaches (e.g., Dreamer) use equal loss weights that allow observation modeling to dominate due to its H×W×C dimensional scale versus the scalar reward. This domination causes the world model to learn spurious correlations and inaccurate reward predictions, severely limiting sample efficiency. The paper asks how to properly exploit the multi-task structure of world model learning without either task dominating.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241383/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates within the Dreamer framework, consuming RGB observations (64×64×3 for Meta-world/RLBench/DMCR, 128×128×3 for Minecraft) and producing discrete or continuous actions. The critical asymmetry is that the observation loss aggregates over all H×W×C pixel dimensions while the reward loss is a single scalar, creating a two-orders-of-magnitude scale gap that the harmonizers must resolve. No architectural changes are made to the encoder, decoder, or policy network; only the world model's training objective is modified.

## Method

HarmonyDream introduces lightweight 'harmonizers'—one learnable scalar σ_i per loss term (observation, reward, dynamics)—that dynamically rescale each loss to a common magnitude via the rectified harmonious loss L_i/σ_i + log(1+σ_i). The variational formulation guarantees that the optimal σ* equals E[L_i], normalizing each task's contribution to a unit scale regardless of its intrinsic dimensionality or training dynamics. The rectification (log(1+σ) instead of log(σ)) prevents the reward loss coefficient from exploding when the reward loss is very small, which would otherwise destabilize training. The approach adds zero hyperparameters and is orthogonal to the base MBRL method, demonstrated on DreamerV2, DreamerV3, and DreamerPro across robotic manipulation, locomotion, and video game benchmarks.

## Results

HarmonyDream (built on DreamerV2) achieves 10%–69% absolute success-rate or return improvements over DreamerV2 across visual robotic tasks, reaching >75% and >90% success on the hard Meta-world Push and Assembly tasks where DreamerV2 fails entirely. On RLBench, it gains 28% (Push Button) and 50% (Reach Target) absolute success rate. Applied to DreamerV3, HarmonyDream sets a new state-of-the-art on Atari 100K with 136.5% mean human-normalized performance (vs. 108% for DreamerV3 reproduced), and also improves on the MineDojo Hunt Cow task in Minecraft.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base DreamerV2, HarmonyDream consistently wins on all evaluated domains (Meta-world, RLBench, DMCR) with 10–69% absolute gains, most dramatically on hard tasks like Assembly (90% vs. near-0% success). On Atari 100K, Harmony DreamerV3 (136.5% human mean) surpasses DreamerV3 reproduced (108%), TWM (96%), IRIS (105%), and SimPLe (33%), matching or exceeding DreamerV3 in 23 of 26 games. Against implicit MBRL method TD-MPC, HarmonyDream clearly outperforms on Meta-world, as TD-MPC struggles without observation modeling to guide representation learning. Compared to Dreamer-based task-centric methods Denoised MDP and RePo, HarmonyDream achieves higher sample efficiency on Meta-world, DMCR, and Natural Background DMC. Among multi-task learning baselines (Uncertainty Weighting, DWA, NashMTL), HarmonyDream is the most effective and simplest; NashMTL in particular produces near-zero or extreme loss weights that hurt performance on RLBench and DMCR. Harmony DreamerPro also outperforms DreamerPro with its manually tuned reward weight w_r=1000, demonstrating that automatic harmonization beats costly manual tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Dreamer family of world-model-based RL methods (DreamerV2, DreamerV3, DreamerPro), reframing their world model learning objective as a multi-task learning problem with two tasks: observation modeling and reward modeling. It departs from the standard equal-weight assumption (w_o = w_r = w_d = 1) by introducing a variational harmonious loss that dynamically balances loss scales, drawing conceptual inspiration from multi-task learning literature (uncertainty weighting, NashMTL, DWA) while being simpler and more directly targeted at the dimensionality mismatch between high-dimensional observation losses and scalar reward losses. The paper positions itself between explicit MBRL (full observation reconstruction) and implicit MBRL (reward-only, e.g., TD-MPC, MuZero), arguing that neither extreme properly exploits the multi-task structure, and contrasts with task-centric architectural modifications like Denoised MDP and RePo that change the model structure rather than the loss weighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the observation loss dominates the reward loss by two orders of magnitude in scale, and that this imbalance causes spurious correlations and hinders agent learning, is practically significant for anyone using Dreamer-family methods. The proposed fix is remarkably simple (one learnable parameter per loss, no new hyperparameters) and demonstrates consistent 10–69% improvements across diverse visual domains and multiple base algorithms, including a new Atari 100K SOTA. However, the paper acknowledges its development is primarily empirical and intuitive, with no theoretical explanation for why the dynamic equilibrium is optimal or how to better measure task contributions beyond loss scales. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-Dreamer architectures, whether the rectified loss is the optimal regularization, and whether more principled measures of task interaction (beyond loss magnitude) could further improve balancing.
