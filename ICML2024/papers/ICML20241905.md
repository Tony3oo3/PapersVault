## Problem

Unsupervised compositional image decomposition requires discovering factors that capture both global scene properties (lighting, expression) and local objects, and that can be flexibly recombined across images and even across separately trained models. Prior energy-based approaches like COMET are unstable to train due to second-order gradients and produce blurry outputs, while vector-space and segmentation-mask methods cannot handle multiple instances of a single factor or cross-model composition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241905/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (64×64 for most datasets, 32×32 for Tetris) and a user-specified number of factors K. A ResNet encoder produces K low-dimensional latent vectors, each conditioning a shared U-Net denoising network. At inference, any subset or recombination of these latents (including latents from different images or different trained models) drives the diffusion sampling loop to produce a reconstructed or novel composite image.

## Method

The core insight is that a denoising network in a diffusion model estimates the gradient of an implicit energy function, so a set of K conditional denoising networks naturally parameterizes K composable energy functions. During training, a ResNet encoder extracts K latents from the input image, and a single U-Net is trained with a standard denoising MSE loss where the sum of its K conditional noise predictions (each conditioned on one latent concatenated with the timestep embedding) must match the true noise. This avoids COMET's expensive second-order gradient optimization entirely. At inference, factors are composed by summing their individual noise predictions and running the standard iterative denoising loop. The authors find that predicting x₀ rather than ε yields better reconstruction fidelity, and constraining latents to low dimensionality acts as an information bottleneck that encourages independent factor discovery.

## Results

Decomp Diffusion achieves the best reconstruction quality across all four evaluation datasets. On CelebA-HQ, it reaches FID 16.48, KID 0.013, and LPIPS 0.089, outperforming the next-best baseline (Hessian Penalty, FID 34.90) by more than 2× in FID. On CLEVR, it achieves FID 11.49 and LPIPS 0.012, substantially ahead of Slot Attention (FID 27.08, LPIPS 0.031). For disentanglement on Falcor3D, the method with 64-dim latents attains MIG 26.45 and MCC 80.42, surpassing COMET (MIG 19.63, MCC 76.55) and MONet (MIG 13.94, MCC 65.72).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Decomp Diffusion outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset and metric in the main reconstruction table. Against COMET (the closest prior method in spirit), it reduces FID from 62.64→16.48 on CelebA-HQ, 46.37→14.18 on Falcor3D, 124.57→21.59 on Virtual KITTI 2, and 103.84→11.49 on CLEVR. MONet and Hessian Penalty are the strongest baselines on CelebA-HQ (FID ~35) but still trail by roughly 2×; on CLEVR, Slot Attention is the best baseline (FID 27.08) yet Decomp Diffusion nearly halves that gap. For disentanglement (Table 2, Falcor3D), the 64-dim Decomp Diffusion model achieves MIG 26.45 and MCC 80.42, beating COMET (19.63 / 76.55) and MONet (13.94 / 65.72), while β-VAE variants and GENESIS-V2 score substantially lower on both metrics. In the cross-dataset recombination experiment (Table 5), Decomp Diffusion also outperforms COMET on both CLEVR (FID 75.16 vs. 98.27) and CLEVR Toy (FID 52.03 vs. 192.02), confirming that the advantage generalizes beyond single-dataset reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends COMET (Du et al., 2021a), which decomposes images into a set of energy functions whose sum's argmin recovers the input. The key conceptual move is recognizing that a denoising diffusion network parameterizes the gradient of an energy function, so a set of conditioned denoising models can replace COMET's explicit energy functions while inheriting the same composition-by-summation semantics. This departs from the broader unsupervised decomposition literature (β-VAE, MONet, Slot Attention, GENESIS-V2), which either uses fixed-dimensional vector spaces or disjoint segmentation masks, by allowing each factor to be a full generative model that captures both global and local scene structure. The composition mechanism also builds on the composable diffusion framework of Liu et al. (2022) and Du et al. (2023), but applies it to an unsupervised, per-image factor discovery setting rather than user-specified constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By replacing COMET's unstable second-order gradient training with a standard denoising objective, the method achieves substantially higher reconstruction fidelity (e.g., FID 16.48 vs. 62.64 on CelebA-HQ) while enabling a capability COMET lacked in practice: composing factors across independently trained models and datasets. The unified treatment of global factors (lighting, expression) and local factors (objects) in a single framework is a useful simplification over the field's tendency to separate these into distinct problem formulations. Key limitations remain: the number of factors K must be fixed a priori, factors are not guaranteed to be distinct or independent, and all experiments are at 64×64 resolution on relatively small datasets. Open questions include principled selection of K, scaling to high-resolution natural images, and whether the approach can leverage pretrained diffusion priors more effectively (the paper's attempt with Stable Diffusion without fine-tuning was unsuccessful).
