## Problem

Deployed LLMs remain static, making it difficult to inject new knowledge without retraining. Existing solutions—retrieval-based methods (ever-growing knowledge bases), model editing (limited to single facts), and long-context methods (context overload)—each fail to provide a fixed-size, self-updatable mechanism that integrates new knowledge efficiently while gradually forgetting stale information without degrading overall model integrity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242065/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During generation, the model takes a text prompt and attends to all 7,680 memory tokens per layer (32 layers total, ~1.066B memory parameters), producing generated text with linear complexity in memory size. During self-update, the model takes a new knowledge paragraph and only reads the last K=256 tokens from each layer's memory pool, producing an updated memory pool of the same fixed dimensions (32×7680×4096). The self-update cost is independent of total memory size N, depending only on K, which keeps inference on a single 48GB GPU regardless of input length.

## Method

MEMORYLLM augments Llama2-7B with a fixed-size memory pool of hidden vectors at every transformer layer, where each layer stores N=7,680 memory tokens of dimension 4,096. The self-update mechanism extracts the last K=256 tokens from each layer's pool, concatenates them with the hidden states of the new knowledge, passes them through the transformer layer, and designates the last K output tokens as new memory entries; K tokens are then randomly dropped from the existing pool to maintain fixed size, yielding exponential forgetting at rate K/N (approaching 1/e retention after N/K steps). Training combines three objectives: (1) predicting x₂ after self-updating with x₁ (with and without gradient flow through the update), (2) sequentially injecting n−1 segments and predicting the nth to build long-context understanding, and (3) injecting knowledge across multiple documents and predicting from the main document to force recall of older memories. Random dropping is chosen over aggregation because it preserves the integrity of existing hidden states while guaranteeing full recovery of the most recently injected knowledge.

## Results

On the model editing benchmarks, MemoryLLM-7B (with editing facts) achieves a harmonic-mean Score of 79.2 on ZsRE and 75.3 on CounterFactual, substantially outperforming all baselines. Its Efficacy reaches 99.8% (ZsRE) and 98.5% (CounterFactual), indicating near-perfect absorption of new facts, while maintaining a Specificity of 57.1 and 57.0 respectively. On LongBench long-context QA, MemoryLLM outperforms baselines on four of six datasets, with performance continuing to improve as context length grows from 4k to 16k tokens. The model retains knowledge after 20+ self-update steps and shows no accuracy degradation over 650,000 consecutive updates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MemoryLLM (w/EF) outperforms all five editing baselines on both ZsRE and CounterFactual. Against ROME (the strongest single-editing baseline), MemoryLLM gains +9.9 Score on ZsRE (79.2 vs 69.3) and +6.1 on CounterFactual (75.3 vs 69.2), with notably higher Efficacy (99.8 vs 88.7 on ZsRE) and Generalization (96.7 vs 70.2). Against IKE, which achieves 70.7 Score on CounterFactual with near-perfect Efficacy (99.8), MemoryLLM edges ahead at 75.3 while maintaining higher Specificity (57.0 vs 45.4). Fine-tuning baselines (FT, FT-L) show a clear efficacy–specificity trade-off: FT achieves 99.7% Efficacy on CounterFactual but collapses Specificity to 3.6, while FT-L recovers Specificity (83.3) at the cost of Efficacy (47.2); MemoryLLM avoids this trade-off, achieving 98.5% Efficacy with 57.0% Specificity. On long-context QA (LongBench), MemoryLLM surpasses Longllama-3B, Openllama V2, and both LongLoRA variants on four of six datasets, with the gap widening as context length increases beyond 4k tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

MEMORYLLM carves new ground at the intersection of memory-augmented transformers and model editing. Prior memory-based methods (Memory Transformer, RMT, EMMA) use small fixed-size pools (≤20 tokens or chunk-sized), while unbounded-memory approaches (KNN-LM, MemoryBank, RET-LLM) suffer from redundancy and uncontrolled growth. This paper departs from both by distributing a very large (~1B parameter) fixed-size memory pool across all transformer layers and using the transformer itself as the compression engine for a self-update mechanism, drawing conceptual inspiration from the Ebbinghaus forgetting curve for its exponential decay via random dropping. It builds on Llama2-7B as the static backbone (φ) while treating the memory pool (θ) as the dynamically updatable component, unifying model editing, long-context understanding, and knowledge retention under a single architectural framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: how to continuously inject new knowledge into a deployed LLM without retraining or unbounded context growth. The demonstration of model integrity after ~650K updates is a strong robustness signal, and the fixed-size design with theoretical forgetting guarantees offers a principled alternative to both retrieval-based and long-context approaches. Key limitations include the ~1B parameter overhead (14% of the base model), text-only modality, and the observation that actual knowledge retention falls short of the theoretical exponential decay bound. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models, whether the compression ratio (K) can be further reduced without quality loss, and how the architecture extends to multimodal settings.
