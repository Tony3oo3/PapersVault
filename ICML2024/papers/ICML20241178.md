## Problem

In privacy-preserving machine learning (PPML), nonlinear operators like ReLU are the dominant latency bottleneck due to expensive garbled-circuit computation, yet prior approaches that simply prune or replace ReLUs suffer unavoidable accuracy degradation. No existing method compensates for the lost representation capacity by leveraging additional linear computations or reusing the outputs of the remaining nonlinear operators, leaving a fundamental accuracy-latency tradeoff unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The architecture search takes a target latency budget (in seconds) and a dataset (ImageNet 224×224 or CIFAR-100 32×32 RGB images) as inputs, and produces an optimized CNN architecture with a specified number of ReLU operators and linear branches. At inference time under the DELPHI protocol, the user and model owner each hold secret shares of the image; the model outputs a class label after the online phase (SS for linear ops, GC for ReLU) completes, with offline FHE pre-processing generating the necessary shares and GC circuits beforehand.

## Method

Seesaw is a one-shot NAS method built on two novel building blocks: (1) sampling blocks that replace a single Conv-ReLU with multiple parallel linear branches (various kernel sizes, depth-wise separable conv, dilated conv, pooling, skip connections) combined via a weighted element-wise ADD before a single ReLU, and (2) aggregation blocks that reuse outputs from all preceding ReLU operators through residual connections, each passed through a resolution-reducing convolution before weighted ADD. The key search-space reduction is that only the ReLU/Identity choice per block (α_i) is searched, while branch weights (β_i,j) are treated as trainable parameters updated during training, dramatically shrinking the search space. A composite loss function penalizes both total latency deviation from target and online/offline imbalance, guiding pruning of ReLU operators and linear branches to balance accuracy against both online (GC-dominated) and offline (FHE-dominated) costs. After convergence, branches are pruned by weight threshold and, if offline latency still exceeds online, further pruned in ascending weight order across blocks.

## Results

On ImageNet, at 71% iso-accuracy Seesaw achieves 1.68× lower online latency and 1.55× lower total (online + offline) latency compared to the state-of-the-art SENet. At an iso-latency of 190 seconds, Seesaw reaches 74.81% top-1 accuracy, which is 3.65% higher than SENet and 9.38% higher than Sphynx. On CIFAR-100, Seesaw delivers 1.53× lower online latency at 70% iso-accuracy and 0.25% higher accuracy at an iso-latency of 8 seconds versus SENet. Compared to the unmodified ResNet baseline, Seesaw achieves approximately 4× end-to-end acceleration with comparable accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SENet (the primary state-of-the-art baseline), Seesaw consistently wins: on ImageNet it is 1.68× faster at 71% iso-accuracy and 3.65% more accurate at 190 s iso-latency; on CIFAR-100 it is 1.53× faster at 70% iso-accuracy and 0.25% more accurate at 8 s iso-latency. Against Sphynx, Seesaw achieves 9.38% higher accuracy at a comparable 180 s online latency on ImageNet, a substantial margin. Against the original ResNet-18/34 baselines, Seesaw delivers roughly 4× end-to-end speedup while maintaining similar accuracy, and when the ReLU budget is generous it can even exceed the insecure ResNet accuracy due to its additional linear operators. Against DELPHI (the underlying cryptographic protocol), Seesaw does not modify the protocol itself but reduces the number of expensive GC-based ReLU calls, thereby cutting the dominant online cost. Against SNL, Seesaw is complementary rather than directly competitive: applying SNL's pixel-level pruning on top of Seesaw-discovered architectures (Table 1) yields further 0.8–0.74% accuracy gains over SENet at 25K and 50K ReLU budgets, demonstrating that Seesaw's architecture search and fine-grained pruning are orthogonal and stackable.

## Relation to Prior Work

Seesaw extends the PPML NAS lineage (CryptoNAS, Sphynx, SNL, SENet) that has focused exclusively on reducing ReLU operators, by introducing a fundamentally different compensation strategy: adding more linear operations and reusing nonlinear outputs through residual aggregation to recover the representation capacity lost to ReLU pruning. The search mechanism is adapted from ProxylessNAS's one-shot supermodel training, but the search space is enlarged to jointly optimize both nonlinear (ReLU/Identity) and linear (branch selection) decisions. The execution framework follows DELPHI's secret-sharing + garbled-circuit protocol, and the ReLU sensitivity concept from SENet informs the architectural analysis, though Seesaw departs by treating linear operators as a first-class search dimension rather than a fixed substrate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Seesaw demonstrates that the accuracy-latency tradeoff in PPML is not fundamental but can be partially broken by exploiting the cost asymmetry between linear and nonlinear operations in cryptographic inference. The practical impact is significant: 1.68× online speedup at iso-accuracy on ImageNet makes private inference more viable for time-sensitive applications. Open questions include whether the linear-compensation strategy generalizes to other modalities (NLP, video), how it interacts with emerging PPML protocols beyond DELPHI, and whether the offline pre-processing cost can be further reduced as the linear operator budget grows. The paper acknowledges that pixel-level pruning (à la SNL) could be layered on top, suggesting the current architecture is a foundation rather than a final form.
