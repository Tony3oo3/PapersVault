## Problem

Existing pre-training-based methods for predicting ΔΔG (binding free energy change) upon protein-protein complex mutations face three key limitations: they fail to capture higher-order dependencies among multiple structural scales (1D sequence, 2D geometry, 3D conformation), they ignore how individual mutations alter the local backbone conformation of the surrounding microenvironment, and they require computationally expensive pre-training on massive unlabeled datasets (e.g., 143k PDB-REDO structures) for only ~7k labeled mutations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a wild-type protein complex 3D structure (no mutated structure required) represented as a heterogeneous attribute graph with E(3)-invariant node features (residue type embeddings, dihedral/torsion angle encodings, local-frame direction vectors) and edge features (relative sequential/spatial distances, cross-residue direction encodings), along with a set of amino acid mutations. The microenvironment around each mutation is extracted as a K-hop ego subgraph with sequence distance cutoff d_s=2 and spatial Cα distance cutoff d_r=15Å. The output is a scalar ΔΔG prediction per mutation, computed by subtracting pooled wild-type and mutant complex representations and passing the difference through an MLP.

## Method

Prompt-DDG constructs a hierarchical prompt codebook with three sub-codebooks (residue type, angular statistics, local conformation) that discretely encode common microenvironmental patterns at different structural scales via vector quantization. A novel pre-training task, Masked Microenvironment Modeling (MMM), jointly trains all three sub-codebooks by independently masking residue types (random flipping), geometric angles (zeroing), and conformation coordinates (Gaussian noising) in the microenvironment, then reconstructing each from the masked input using dedicated decoders, thereby modeling the joint distribution across scales. At inference, the pre-trained codebook tokenizes each mutation's microenvironment into three discrete prompt codes, which are combined via a lightweight weighted adaptation module (one-layer linear transforms producing per-scale weights) into a single prompt vector added to all residues in the microenvironment. This avoids expensive large-scale pre-training entirely, learning prompts directly on the small labeled dataset while capturing multi-scale microenvironmental differences between wild-type and mutated complexes.

## Results

On SKEMPI v2.0 (7,085 mutations, 3-fold cross-validation), Prompt-DDG achieves a per-structure Pearson of 0.4712 and Spearman of 0.4257, and overall Pearson of 0.6772, Spearman of 0.5910, RMSE of 1.5207, MAE of 1.0770, and AUROC of 0.7568. It outperforms all 15 baselines on 6 of 7 metrics, with the largest gains on per-structure Pearson (+5.94% over RDE-Network, +11.78% over DiffAffinity) and per-structure Spearman (+6.16% and +7.23% respectively). Notably, Prompt-DDG requires no additional pre-training data (trained only on the 7k SKEMPI v2.0 samples) yet surpasses all methods pre-trained on 143k PDB-REDO structures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Prompt-DDG decisively outperforms all pre-training-based competitors: it beats RDE-Network (the strongest pre-training baseline) by +5.94% per-structure Pearson and +6.16% per-structure Spearman, and DiffAffinity by +11.78% and +7.23% on the same metrics, while also leading on overall Pearson, Spearman, MAE, and AUROC. Against supervised methods, Prompt-DDG surpasses DDGPred on all metrics except RMSE (1.5207 vs. 1.4998), and exceeds End-to-End on every metric. Sequence-based methods (ESM-1v, PSSM, MSA Transformer, Tranception) and energy-based methods (Rosetta, FoldX) are far behind, with per-structure Pearson values below 0.38 versus Prompt-DDG's 0.4712. In the antibody optimization case study (Table 6), Prompt-DDG achieves the best average ranking of 10.69% for five known favorable mutations against SARS-CoV-2, compared to 18.26% for RDE-Network and 24.49% for DiffAffinity, and is the only method that ranks all five mutations in the top 40%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant pre-training paradigm for ΔΔG prediction (RDE, DiffAffinity, MIF), which requires massive unlabeled structural datasets (e.g., PDB-REDO with 143k structures) to learn general sequence-structure dependencies. Instead, it repurposes vector quantization (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) and E(3)-equivariant GNNs (Kong et al., 2022; Jumper et al., 2021) into a lightweight hierarchical prompt learning framework that encodes local microenvironments around each mutation into discrete multi-scale codes. The key conceptual departure is replacing global pre-trained representations with mutation-specific, microenvironment-aware prompts, and introducing a novel masked microenvironment modeling (MMM) pre-training task that jointly captures higher-order dependencies across 1D residue types, 2D angular statistics, and 3D conformational changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a prompt-based method trained on only 7k labeled mutations outperforms methods pre-trained on 143k unlabeled structures challenges the assumption that large-scale pre-training is necessary for effective mutation effect prediction, offering a more data- and compute-efficient alternative for protein complex design. The antibody optimization case study demonstrates practical utility in identifying favorable mutations for SARS-CoV-2 neutralization. However, the approach is limited to two-protein interactions, evaluated on a single dataset (SKEMPI v2.0 with ~7k mutations), and does not address multimeric proteins or enzyme-substrate systems, leaving open questions about generalizability to more complex biological scenarios.
