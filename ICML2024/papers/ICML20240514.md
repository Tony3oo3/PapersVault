## Problem

In knowledge distillation, a counter-intuitive 'capacity mismatch' phenomenon occurs where larger, more accurate teachers paradoxically produce worse students, yet prior explanations remain fragmented across architecture adjustments and teacher regularization. This paper identifies teacher calibration as the unifying root cause: larger models achieve higher accuracy but suffer from poorer calibration, and standard KL-divergence-based distillation is highly sensitive to this miscalibration, degrading student performance.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on standard RGB image classification (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64) where the teacher's temperature-scaled softmax logits serve as the distillation signal for a smaller student network (ResNet14, MobileNetV2, or WRN-22-1). The key input detail is that the teacher's calibration level—measured by Expected Calibration Error (ECE)—is the critical variable governing distillation quality, not the teacher's raw accuracy. The output is a distilled student model producing class labels, with the practical recommendation being to replace KL divergence with a ranking-based loss (DIST) to decouple student performance from teacher miscalibration.

## Method

The paper establishes a unifying calibration-based framework for capacity mismatch through three pillars: (1) empirical demonstration via temperature scaling that KD performance positively correlates with teacher ECE across homogeneous and heterogeneous architectures; (2) a unifying reinterpretation of existing remedies—TAKD's assistant models and ESKD's early-stopped teachers both implicitly improve teacher calibration rather than merely reducing capacity gap; and (3) a practical solution using DIST's Pearson-correlation-based inter-class and intra-class relation losses, which prioritize preserving the ranking order of class probabilities rather than matching absolute probability values. Ablation experiments with Spearman correlation analysis show DIST's KD performance has near-zero correlation with teacher ECE (|ρ| < 2%) versus KL-based KD's strong negative correlation (|ρ| > 76%), confirming that ranking-based loss effectively neutralizes the adverse effects of poor teacher calibration.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, ranking-based DIST achieves top-1 accuracy of 68.77% (ResNet-56→ResNet-14, τ=2.0) and 68.88% (ResNet-110→ResNet-14, τ=3.5), matching or slightly exceeding vanilla KD (68.56% and 68.42% respectively) while being nearly insensitive to teacher calibration. The Spearman correlation between ECE and KD performance is strongly negative for KL-based KD (−91.82%, −76.36%, −99.10% across three teacher-student pairs) but near zero for DIST (0.46%, 1.82%, −50.00%), confirming that ranking-based loss decouples distillation quality from teacher calibration. On the GMM synthetic experiment, training with the true posterior distribution (optimal calibration) yields 89.82% average accuracy vs. 89.13% with one-hot labels on 10-class, and 94.87% vs. 94.00% on 100-class, with dramatically lower variance (8% vs. 179%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla KD (Hinton et al., 2015), DIST matches or slightly exceeds top-1 accuracy at most temperatures (e.g., 68.77% vs. 68.56% at τ=2.0 for ResNet-56→ResNet-14) while reducing calibration sensitivity from Spearman −91.82% to +0.46%, meaning DIST's performance is essentially flat as teacher ECE varies from 10% to 62%. Against TAKD (Mirzadeh et al., 2020), the paper reinterprets the method's success: the assistant model's better calibration (e.g., ECE 9.24% for ResNet56 assistant vs. higher for larger assistants) drives the gain, not the intermediate size, as shown by Table 2 where smaller assistants with lower ECE yield higher KD performance. Against ESKD (Cho & Hariharan, 2019), the paper confirms that early-stopped teachers (100 epochs) maintain or improve calibration (ECE 3.48% vs. 5.16% for WRN-28-3 at τ=1.6) and achieve marginally better KD (71.95% vs. 71.71%), validating the calibration explanation. Against FitNet (Romero et al., 2015), the paper extends the calibration-KD correlation to feature-based distillation (Appendix Fig. 7), showing the phenomenon is not limited to response-based KL divergence. Overall, the paper does not propose a new architecture but validates DIST as a calibration-robust replacement for KL divergence, with the key quantitative win being the near-zero Spearman correlation versus the strongly negative correlation of KL-based KD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on vanilla KD (Hinton et al., 2015) and the ranking-based DIST method (Huang et al., 2022), but its primary contribution is a unifying analytical lens: it reframes the capacity mismatch problem and its various proposed solutions (TAKD, ESKD, residual KD, checkpoint selection) as fundamentally about teacher calibration rather than model size or architecture gap. It departs from prior fragmented explanations (Cho & Hariharan 2019, Mirzadeh et al. 2020, Li et al. 2021) by showing they all implicitly optimize the teacher's calibration, and it repurposes DIST's ranking-based loss as a calibration-insensitive substitute for KL divergence. The connection to Menon et al. (2021) on statistical properties of distillation is acknowledged, but this paper claims to identify calibration as the deeper mechanistic cause.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unifying calibration framework gives practitioners a single diagnostic (ECE) to understand why a large teacher underperforms in KD and a concrete remedy: switch from KL divergence to a ranking-based loss. However, all experiments are confined to CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny-ImageNet with standard CNN backbones, leaving open whether the calibration-KD correlation and the insensitivity of ranking-based losses hold for transformer architectures, larger-scale datasets (ImageNet, JFT), or non-vision modalities. The paper also does not explore whether post-hoc calibration of the teacher (e.g., temperature scaling at inference) could achieve similar gains without changing the loss function, which would be a simpler practical fix.
