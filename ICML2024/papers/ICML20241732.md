## Problem

Early time series classification aims to stop processing a data stream as soon as a pre-trained classifier is confident, but existing stopping rules either lack statistical guarantees or only control the average (marginal) accuracy gap. Marginal control is insufficient in practice because it permits the algorithm to perform poorly on sequences that halt early while compensating on late-halt sequences, undermining reliability precisely when early stopping is most valuable. No prior method provides distribution-free, finite-sample control of the accuracy gap conditional on the accumulated halt time.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained sequential classifier (e.g., an LSTM or an autoregressive LLM) that maps each prefix X^{≤t} to a label distribution, a confidence score function π̂(X^{≤t}) ∈ [0,1], and a labeled calibration set D_cal of i.i.d. (X, Y) pairs. It outputs a data-adaptive threshold vector λ̂ = (λ̂_1, …, λ̂_{t_max}) with one element per timestep, which defines the stopping rule τ̂(X) = min{t : π̂(X^{≤t}) ≥ λ̂_t or t = t_max}. The calibration set is split into two disjoint subsets D_cal-1 and D_cal-2 for the two-stage procedure, and the final stopping rule is applied to unseen test sequences to produce both a predicted label and a halt time.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage calibration framework that exploits the sequential structure of ETSC to handle the exponential (|Λ|+1)^{t_max} hyperparameter space that would make naive Learn-then-Test infeasible. Stage 1 (Candidate Screening) greedily determines each threshold η_t from t=1 to t_max, selecting the smallest value on a discretized grid that keeps the empirical conditional accuracy gap R̂_gap^{≤t} below α, yielding a candidate vector η̂ in O(t_max · |Λ| · |D_cal-1|) time. Stage 2 (Testing) applies fixed sequence testing on an independent holdout set, ordering hypotheses from the most conservative configuration λ^{t_max} = (∞,…,∞,η̂_{t_max}) down to the most aggressive λ^1 = (η̂_1,…,η̂_{t_max}), where each hypothesis H_{0,λ^t} is decomposed into finer nulls H_{0,λ^t}^{t'} for all t' ≥ t and rejected only if the maximum p-value across all finer nulls falls below δ. The p-values use the exact binomial tail bound (CDF_bin) since the gap loss is binary, and the FWER control follows directly from the fixed sequence testing protocol, yielding a distribution-free, finite-sample guarantee that the conditional accuracy gap R_gap^{≤t} ≤ α for all t ≥ t_0 with probability at least 1−δ.

## Results

On five structured time-series datasets (α=10%, δ=1%), the conditional method keeps the accuracy gap for the 20% earliest halts below α across all datasets (ranging from 0.023 to 0.064), while the marginal method exceeds α on ElectricDevices (0.117) and Crop (0.171). The conditional method achieves a lower average normalized halt time T_avg on 2 of 5 datasets (Crop: 0.450 vs. 0.580; WalkingSittingStanding: 0.061 vs. 0.125), demonstrating that controlling a stronger error notion does not necessarily sacrifice earliness. On the QUALITY NLP dataset, the conditional method maintains the conditional accuracy gap below 10% at all timesteps, whereas the marginal method's gap exceeds 10% for early halts, with T_avg of 0.831 vs. 0.483 respectively. Overall, the methods reduce up to 94% of timesteps while providing rigorous distribution-free, finite-sample accuracy gap control.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between the proposed conditional method and the marginal method (which closely resembles the calibration procedure of Schuster et al., 2022 for early exit in transformers). The conditional method decisively wins on the key safety metric: it keeps the accuracy gap for the 20% earliest halts below the nominal α=10% on all five structured datasets, while the marginal method violates this on ElectricDevices (0.117) and Crop (0.171). On earliness (T_avg), the conditional method is competitive or superior on Crop (0.450 vs. 0.580) and WalkingSittingStanding (0.061 vs. 0.125), but stops later on ElectricDevices (0.552 vs. 0.471), PenDigits (0.567 vs. 0.446), and Tiselac (0.215 vs. 0.209). On the QUALITY NLP dataset, the marginal method achieves a lower T_avg (0.483 vs. 0.831) but at the cost of exceeding the 10% accuracy gap threshold for early timesteps, whereas the conditional method maintains valid control across all timesteps. An ablation (Figure E.6) further confirms that the Stage 2 testing phase is essential: the Stage 1 candidate alone does not provide rigorous conditional risk control.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Learn-then-Test (LTT) calibration framework (Angelopoulos et al., 2021) from marginal to conditional risk control in the context of early time series classification, using the exact binomial tail bound of Bates et al. (2021) for p-value computation and fixed-sequence testing (Bauer, 1991) for FWER control. The key departure from prior LTT applications is a two-stage procedure—greedy candidate screening followed by fixed-sequence testing—that makes the method tractable when the hyperparameter space is a vector of length t_max, avoiding the exponential search that plagues vanilla LTT. The paper also distinguishes itself from early-exit transformer work (Schuster et al., 2021, 2022) by applying stopping over the input time horizon rather than network depth, and from prior ETSC methods by providing distribution-free, finite-sample guarantees rather than heuristic stopping criteria.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The transition from marginal to conditional accuracy gap control is practically significant: the marginal method can exhibit accuracy gaps well above the nominal level at early timesteps while still satisfying the average guarantee, undermining reliability precisely when early stopping is most valuable. The two-stage calibration makes LTT feasible for long sequences, and the 94% timestep reduction demonstrates substantial computational savings. Key limitations include the i.i.d. assumption (which may be violated in streaming or non-stationary settings), the requirement that the gap loss be binary (limiting extension to regression), and the fact that the conditional guarantee is still weaker than instance- or time-conditional control. Open directions include relaxing exchangeability, extending to regression losses, and jointly optimizing stopping over both time and network depth.
