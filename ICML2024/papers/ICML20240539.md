## Problem

Standard deep generative models (GANs, VAEs) cannot guarantee that generated objects satisfy hard combinatorial constraints such as playability, manufacturing feasibility, or path existence. Existing work either penalizes constraint violations without guarantees, post-processes solutions (losing diversity and not training end-to-end), or relies on ILP solvers that are slow and cannot capture data distributions. GenCO addresses this by guaranteeing constraint satisfaction throughout training while still modeling the data distribution and optimizing nonlinear objectives.

## Input / Output

The generator takes a random noise vector and produces a latent design description c, which is then passed through a differentiable combinatorial solver g to yield a provably feasible solution x. In the Warcraft setting, the generator outputs 96×96×3 RGB map images that a fixed ResNet converts to a 12×12 weighted grid for shortest-path solving; in the Zelda setting, soft grid solutions are projected to discrete 8-component game levels via MILP; in the photonic setting, VQVAE decoder outputs are projected to 0/1 grids via domain-specific solvers. The output is always a feasible combinatorial object (playable level, map with valid path, manufacturable device) rather than a continuous approximation.

## Method

GenCO learns a latent design space C such that every c ∈ C maps to a guaranteed feasible solution x = g(c) through a combinatorial solver, and trains a generative model G_θ (WGAN or VQVAE) to map noise to C end-to-end. The key architectural insight is that the generative loss (Wasserstein distance or ELBO) is computed on the solver's feasible output x rather than on intermediate soft solutions, so the generator only needs to capture the data distribution while the solver enforces feasibility. Backpropagation through the solver is achieved via identity-with-projection or blackbox differentiation techniques. An optional individual loss D (e.g., Maxwell's equations simulation) can be added to optimize nonlinear objectives on the feasible solutions. The framework is solver-agnostic, supporting MILP (Gurobi), LP, shortest-path, and domain-specific blackbox solvers across GAN and VAE generative backbones.

## Results

On Zelda game level generation, GenCO-Updated Adversary produces 99.5% unique levels (vs. 52% for GAN+MILP fix) with a GAN adversary loss of −10.10, indicating near-perfect indistinguishability from real levels. On Warcraft map generation, GenCO reduces the shortest-path cost to 23.99 (vs. 36.45 for Ordinary GAN and 35.61 for GAN+semantic loss) while maintaining comparable density (0.94) and coverage (0.93). On inverse photonic design, GenCO (reconstruction+objective) achieves 100% solution uniqueness and zero Maxwell's-equation loss, compared to 30.6% uniqueness and 1.244 average loss for VQVAE+postprocess.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Zhang et al. (2020) (GAN+MILP fix), GenCO-Updated Adversary wins decisively on uniqueness (99.5% vs. 52%) and fidelity (GAN loss −10.10 vs. 0.22), because integrating the MILP solver into the training loop prevents the generator from hiding infeasible continuous solutions that collapse to the same discrete output. In the photonic design setting, GenCO (reconstruction+objective) also outperforms the same postprocessing baseline on uniqueness (100% vs. 30.6%), coverage (0.738 vs. 0.006), and solution quality (0 vs. 1.244 Maxwell loss). Against Di Liello et al. (2020) (GAN+semantic loss) on Warcraft map generation, GenCO achieves a substantially lower shortest-path cost (23.99 vs. 35.61) while maintaining a lower GAN loss (0.636 vs. 0.899), showing that explicitly solving the path problem rather than applying a uniform cost penalty yields better trade-offs between distribution fidelity and objective quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenCO sits at the intersection of differentiable combinatorial optimization (Pogančić et al., 2020; Sahoo et al., 2022) and deep generative modeling (WGAN, VQVAE), building directly on the SurCo framework (Ferber et al., 2023) which optimized a single latent representation for a known problem. The key departure from prior constrained-generation work (Di Liello et al., 2020; Chao et al., 2021) is that GenCO does not penalize infeasibility or postprocess solutions; instead, it reparameterizes generation through a latent design space where every point maps to a provably feasible solution via a differentiable solver, so the generative loss is computed exclusively on feasible outputs. This contrasts with GAN+MILP-fix (Zhang et al., 2020) and rejection sampling, where the generator is never trained on the feasibility constraint and thus cannot learn to produce diverse feasible solutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GenCO provides the first end-to-end generative framework that simultaneously guarantees combinatorial feasibility, optimizes nonlinear objectives, and produces diverse solutions—three properties that prior methods could achieve at most two of. The practical impact is significant for domains like photonic device design, game level generation, and path planning where practitioners need multiple verifiably valid designs rather than a single optimum. Key limitations include dependence on the current state of differentiable solvers (scalability, problem coverage), the requirement that constraints be expressible in a mathematical program, and the inability to enforce non-analytical or soft constraints (fairness, aesthetics). Open questions include learning constraint representations from data and extending the framework to settings where the solver itself must be learned.
