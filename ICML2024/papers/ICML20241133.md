## Problem

Existing CLIP-based zero-shot semantic segmentation methods rely solely on the last-layer visual features, missing rich local object details present in intermediate layers. However, the authors show that naively fusing multi-level features degrades zero-shot performance because the large representational gap between layers disrupts CLIP's pre-trained vision-language alignment. The paper addresses how to exploit multi-level features to improve segmentation without weakening CLIP's zero-shot generalization to unseen classes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB image and a set of text class names (encoded via CLIP text templates such as 'A photo of a {class}.') as input. It produces a pixel-wise segmentation mask of shape C×H×W, where C is the number of classes (seen + unseen at inference). Internally, the ViT-B/16 encoder yields N patch tokens per stage, and the final mask is computed over these tokens before upsampling. The framework is plug-and-play and can be attached to existing one-stage methods like ZegCLIP or SPT-SEG without modifying their core pipelines.

## Method

Cascade-CLIP splits CLIP's 12-block ViT visual encoder into S stages (default: blocks {6-8}, {9-11}, {12}), each equipped with an independent text-image decoder and a non-sharing text embedding obtained via linear projection from the base CLIP text embedding. Within each stage, a Neighborhood Gaussian Aggregation (NGA) module fuses block features using trainable Gaussian weights that decay with relative block distance, preserving local feature structure while integrating complementary information. The per-stage segmentation masks are combined via element-wise summation before a final softmax, effectively ensembling the cascaded decoders. During training, visual prompt tuning (learnable tokens injected into the frozen encoder) allows gradients to back-propagate into middle layers, progressively increasing cross-layer feature similarity and strengthening vision-language alignment. The loss is a weighted combination of dice loss and focal loss (α=1, β=100), and the entire framework adds only ~28M trainable parameters over the baseline while maintaining comparable inference speed.

## Results

In the inductive zero-shot setting, Cascade-CLIP achieves mIoU^U of 43.4 on COCO-Stuff 164K and 83.1 on PASCAL VOC 2012, improving over the ZegCLIP baseline by 2.0 and 5.3 points respectively. In the transductive setting, Cascade-CLIP+ST reaches mIoU^U of 62.5 on COCO-Stuff and 93.4 on VOC, and on PASCAL Context it attains mIoU^U of 55.0 (transductive) and 47.2 (inductive). The method also demonstrates strong cross-dataset generalization, outperforming ZegCLIP when trained on COCO and evaluated on VOC (93.8 vs 93.6 mIoU^S) and Context (48.4 vs 47.5 mIoU^S).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cascade-CLIP consistently outperforms its primary baseline ZegCLIP across all three benchmarks and both evaluation settings: +2.0 mIoU^U on COCO-Stuff (43.4 vs 41.4), +5.3 mIoU^U on VOC (83.1 vs 77.8), and +1.7 mIoU^U on PASCAL Context (47.2 vs 45.5) in the inductive setting. Against two-encoder methods (ZegFormer, Zsseg, DeOP), Cascade-CLIP achieves substantially higher unseen-class mIoU while using a single CLIP backbone, e.g., 43.4 vs 38.4 (DeOP) on COCO-Stuff. In the transductive setting, Cascade-CLIP+ST surpasses TagCLIP+ST on COCO-Stuff (62.5 vs 60.0 mIoU^U) and matches or exceeds it on VOC (93.4 vs 92.7 mIoU^U). On PASCAL Context, the transductive Cascade-CLIP+ST achieves 55.0 mIoU^U, a 13.6-point improvement over ZegCLIP+ST (41.4). The method also generalizes well when plugged into other frameworks: adding Cascade-CLIP to SPT-SEG improves its COCO-Stuff mIoU^U from 40.7 to 43.6 (inductive) and from 57.5 to 62.1 (transductive), and to Frozen CLIP from 32.5 to 35.3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-stage CLIP-based zero-shot segmentation paradigm established by ZegCLIP, which matches text embeddings against last-layer visual features via a single text-image decoder. The key departure is the observation that CLIP's intermediate layers contain rich local object details (analogous to multi-level feature aggregation in closed-set segmentation like SegFormer), yet naively fusing these features with last-layer features disrupts the pre-trained vision-language alignment and degrades zero-shot performance. To resolve this, the authors propose a cascaded architecture with independent decoders per encoder stage and a Neighborhood Gaussian Aggregation module, positioning their work as a flexible plug-in that can be integrated into existing methods like ZegCLIP and SPT-SEG rather than a standalone replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The negative result—that standard multi-level feature fusion, a well-established technique in closed-set segmentation, actively harms zero-shot transferability—provides a useful caution for practitioners adapting CLIP to dense prediction tasks. The cascaded framework's flexibility (applicable to frozen CLIP, ZegCLIP, SPT-SEG, and transductive settings) makes it a practical building block, though the absolute gains over ZegCLIP are modest (~1–2 mIoU). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger CLIP variants (ViT-L, ViT-H), whether the specific 3-stage splitting transfers to different encoder depths, and whether the NGA's Gaussian weighting scheme is optimal compared to learned attention-based aggregation at scale.
