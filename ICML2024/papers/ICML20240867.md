## Problem

Dynamic Sparse Training (DST) inherently forms a bi-level structure where weight updates and mask topology changes are mutually dependent, yet all existing DST algorithms treat the mask update as a heuristic step (magnitude, gradient, or momentum-based) while only optimizing weights via SGD. This partial-heuristic approach yields suboptimal mask search, causes masks to stagnate early as weights converge, and incurs high system overhead from frequent topology changes that trigger computation graph reconstruction.

## Input / Output

BiDST takes standard RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 ImageNet-1K) and produces a sparse subnetwork at a target sparsity ratio (90%–99.9999%) consisting of both learned weights and a binary mask. During training, the mask is relaxed to continuous values and only periodically binarized via an argmax operation (every 8 iterations on CIFAR, every 5 on ImageNet), so the intermediate output is a soft mask that guides topology selection without requiring full network recompilation at every step.

## Method

BiDST reformulates DST as a bi-level optimization problem: the lower level trains sparse weights via SGD with an L2 regularization term, while the upper level optimizes the mask by minimizing the network loss with respect to the mask using implicit gradients that couple the topological space (mask) and parameter space (weights). The key computational trick is replacing the Hessian in the implicit gradient with zero (following MAML-style first-order approximation), reducing the mask gradient to a combination of two first-order partial derivatives that reuse existing activation gradients from the backward pass. Gumbel-Softmax provides a differentiable relaxation of the binary mask, and an extra search space S (2–8% of total mask elements) beyond the current non-zero weights gives the optimizer room to explore new topology. Because the mask is learned continuously and only binarized infrequently, BiDST requires far fewer topology switches (12–146 vs. 320–3840 for baselines), cutting system overhead by 16–25× on Snapdragon 888 hardware.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-32 at 98% ERK sparsity, BiDST achieves 92.21% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best baseline RigL-ITOP (90.40%) by 1.81%. On CIFAR-100 at the same sparsity, BiDST reaches 68.19% vs. RigL-ITOP's 66.35% (+1.84%). On ImageNet-1K with ResNet-50 at 90% uniform sparsity, BiDST achieves 75.3% top-1 accuracy with only 18 mask updates, surpassing MEST (75.0%, 60 updates) and RigL_2× (74.6%, 640 updates). The paper also reports up to 2.1× training speedup and 25× reduced system overhead on Snapdragon 888 hardware.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiDST consistently outperforms all 10+ baselines across every sparsity level and dataset. Against the strongest DST baselines, BiDST (ERK) beats RigL-ITOP by 0.42–1.81% on CIFAR-10 and 2.26–1.84% on CIFAR-100, with the gap widening at higher sparsity (e.g., +1.81% at 98% on CIFAR-10). Against uniform-sparsity DST methods, BiDST surpasses RigL by 0.61–2.99% and MEST by 1.12–2.68% on CIFAR-10/100. On ImageNet-1K (ResNet-50, 90% sparsity), BiDST achieves 75.3% top-1 with only 18 mask updates, beating MEST (75.0%, 60 updates) and RigL_2× (74.6%, 640 updates) while requiring far fewer topology changes. Non-uniform sparsity methods (LT, SNIP, GraSP, Deep-R, SET, DSR) are all outperformed by 1–4% at 98% sparsity, with DSR being the closest non-DST baseline. The key practical advantage is that BiDST's optimization-based mask update reduces topology changes from hundreds (RigL: 320–3840) to 12–146, yielding 16–25× lower system overhead on-device.

## Relation to Prior Work

BiDST reframes the established Dynamic Sparse Training (DST) paradigm—exemplified by SET, RigL, and MEST, which alternate heuristic magnitude-based pruning with gradient/momentum-based growing—into a formal bi-level optimization problem where mask and weight updates are jointly solved analytically. The method draws on bi-level optimization theory (Colson et al., 2005) for the problem structure, Gumbel-Softmax (Jang et al., 2017) for differentiable discrete mask optimization, and first-order implicit gradient approximations from MAML (Finn et al., 2017) and BOME (Liu et al., 2022a) to make the upper-level mask gradient computationally tractable. The key departure from prior DST is replacing the non-comprehensive heuristic MaskUpdate with a loss-driven gradient step that simultaneously considers topological and parameter space information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By grounding mask discovery in optimization rather than heuristics, BiDST reduces the number of topology changes needed (12–146 vs. 320–3840 in baselines), yielding a 16–25× reduction in system overhead from code recompilation on edge hardware—a practically significant win for on-device deployment. The consistent accuracy gains (up to 2.62%) across ResNet, VGG, and MobileNet architectures at multiple sparsity levels suggest the optimization-based mask search is more robust than magnitude/gradient heuristics, particularly at extreme sparsity. Open questions include whether the bi-level formulation extends to structured sparsity or transformer architectures, how the mask search space hyperparameter S should be set automatically, and whether the approach scales to billion-parameter models where even small per-step overheads compound.
