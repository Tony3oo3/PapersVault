## Problem

Existing noisy derivative-free optimization methods (ball smoothing, SPSA, Bayesian optimization) use either fixed isotropic sampling windows or struggle in high dimensions, failing to account for the heterogeneous curvature commonly observed in combinatorial optimization and neural network parameter spaces. When some parameters are far more sensitive than others, an isotropic window shrinks too rapidly in high-curvature directions while remaining too large in low-curvature directions, producing biased gradients and suboptimal convergence. This paper asks how to dynamically adapt the sampling kernel's shape to the local Hessian of the objective to reduce gradient estimation error under noise.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes only noisy scalar evaluations of the objective function through an oracle f̂(x,ζ) = f(x) + ζ(x) with bounded noise, requiring no gradient or Hessian access. At each iteration it draws a batch of B random vectors from a Gaussian distribution shaped by the current window matrix L, evaluates f̂ at the corresponding points (parallelizable, e.g., on GPU), and uses these to estimate both the position and window-shape gradients. The batch size B is scheduled as B₀/tr(LLᵀ)^(γ/2), increasing as the window shrinks to resolve finer structure. The output is a single optimized parameter vector x* in ℝᴰ, typically representing tuned hyperparameters for a heuristic solver.

## Method

DAS (Dynamic Anisotropic Smoothing) replaces the scalar window size of ball smoothing with a full matrix L ∈ ℝ^{D×D} that parameterizes the shape of a Gaussian smoothing kernel, defining h(L,x) = ∫ κ(v) f(Lv + x) dv. The algorithm performs stochastic gradient ascent on h with respect to both x and L, using Monte Carlo estimators: ∂h/∂x ≈ (L⁻¹)ᵀ E[v f̂(Lv+x,ζ)] and ∂h/∂L ≈ (L⁻¹)ᵀ E[(I + vvᵀ) f̂(Lv+x,ζ)]. The update dynamics include a preconditioning factor LLᵀ multiplying both gradients, which the paper proves ensures scale symmetry (invariance under linear reparameterization) and, at fixed points with a growth term λ, yields the condition λ(LLᵀ)⁻¹ ≈ ∇²f, meaning the window curvature converges to the Hessian of f. The paper further shows analytically that this Hessian alignment is precisely the choice of L that minimizes the variance of the gradient estimator, providing a theoretical justification for the anisotropic adaptation. Numerical stability is maintained via a two-step Euler integration with adaptive time-stepping and a CLAMP function that bounds the window norm between w_min and w_max.

## Results

On the 4-dimensional modified Rosenbrock function (β=0.5, n_s=10^5), DAS achieves a mean fitness of 0.981 (worst 0.962, best 0.994), substantially outperforming BOHB (mean 0.586), SPSA (mean 0.304), and fixed-window ball smoothing (mean 0.280). In the more challenging 8-dimensional setting (β=0.2), DAS is the only method that reaches the optimum at n_s=10^6 (best fitness 0.962), while all other methods remain near zero. On the practical SAT solver tuning task (N=150, α=4.0, T=148), DAS identifies parameter configurations with the highest average success probability, with the advantage attributed to its ability to adapt to heterogeneous parameter sensitivities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAS outperforms all three baselines on the 4D modified Rosenbrock benchmark, achieving a mean fitness of 0.981 versus 0.586 for BOHB, 0.304 for SPSA, and 0.280 for fixed-window ball smoothing (Gasnikov et al., 2022). However, in the lower-dimensional 2D case, BOHB actually achieves the best mean fitness (0.994 at n_s=10^5) and DAS's advantage diminishes, consistent with Bayesian optimization's known strength in low dimensions. In the 8D setting, DAS is the only method that successfully converges to the optimum given sufficient samples (n_s=10^6), while SPSA, Gasnikov, and BOHB all plateau near zero fitness. On the practical SAT solver tuning task, DAS finds the optimal parameter values (p_init≈−1, β≈2) that lie outside the [0,1]^4 search space used by BOHB, explaining BOHB's sub-optimality. The key differentiator is DAS's dynamic anisotropic window, which adapts to the heterogeneous curvature of the objective—critical when some parameters (e.g., dt) are far more sensitive than others—whereas fixed-shape methods (Gasnikov, SPSA) and Bayesian methods (BOHB) cannot capture this structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the smoothing-based derivative-free optimization framework of Nesterov & Spokoiny (2017) and Gasnikov et al. (2022b) by replacing the scalar window size with a full matrix L that dynamically adapts its shape to the local Hessian of the objective. The key conceptual departure from prior ball/Gaussian smoothing is the introduction of anisotropic kernel curvature, which the authors prove converges to the Hessian eigenbasis at fixed points, effectively acting as a preconditioner. The method is positioned against stochastic approximation (SPSA), Bayesian optimization (BOHB), and fixed-window smoothing, with the specific motivation of handling heterogeneous parameter sensitivity in combinatorial optimization solver tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical justification (via gradient estimation error minimization) for why anisotropic smoothing outperforms isotropic smoothing near optima with heavy-tailed Hessian spectra, and demonstrates practical gains on SAT and Ising solver tuning where parameter sensitivities differ by orders of magnitude. However, the evaluation is limited to 4-parameter spaces and specific problem sizes, and the method is restricted to continuous search spaces. Open questions include whether the Hessian-alignment property holds for non-Gaussian, highly non-convex objectives, how the method scales to the 100+ parameter spaces common in neural network training, and whether the O(D/n_s^{1/2}) convergence rate observed empirically can be proven rigorously.
