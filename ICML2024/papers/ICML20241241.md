## Problem

Graph Diffusion Convolution (GDC) architectures rely heavily on the graph Laplacian to derive their diffusion matrix, making them vulnerable to adversarial structural attacks, large noise in node features (especially on low-degree graphs), and inconsistent edges on heterophilic graphs. The paper asks whether a single versatile GDC variant can simultaneously address all three failure modes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241241/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an adjacency matrix A and a node feature matrix X as input. It first computes a modified transition matrix T (incorporating an additional feature-similarity term) and a graph diffusion matrix S via a truncated power series, then produces aggregated features F = SX. These aggregated features are fed into a lightweight downstream model (linear or 2-layer MLP) for node classification, effectively decoupling the one-time feature aggregation from iterative training. Four variants (I–IV) select different formulations of the additional term depending on the target scenario.

## Method

GADC is derived from a min-max reformulation of the Graph Signal Denoising problem (AGSD): the inner maximization introduces Laplacian-distance-bounded perturbations to the graph Laplacian, and the outer minimization solves for denoised features. Unlike adversarial training requiring PGD, the inner problem is quadratic and admits a closed-form solution, yielding an extra term εXXᵀ/‖XXᵀ‖_F in the transition matrix. The outer solution is computed via a truncated diffusion power series (avoiding O(n³) matrix inversion) with F approximated by X in the nonlinear term for scalability. This additional term is instantiated in four ways: (I) cosine-similarity reweighting of existing edges to penalize heterophilic connections, (II) full-pair feature similarity to add connectivity on low-degree noisy graphs, (III) edge-weight reassignment on high-degree noisy graphs, and (IV) complete adjacency reconstruction via cosine similarity to defend against structural attacks.

## Results

Under non-adaptive Meta-attack (Table 1), GADC(IV) achieves 76.00% / 70.29% / 66.06% test accuracy on Cora at 25%/50%/75% perturbation rates, 71.55% / 66.47% / 65.25% on Citeseer, and 86.74% / 85.92% / 85.29% on Pubmed, outperforming all baselines on Citeseer and Pubmed while remaining competitive on Cora. Under adaptive attacks (Table 2), GADC(IV) reaches 72.03% and 72.94% evasion accuracy on Cora and Citeseer respectively, surpassing the next-best baseline (Soft-Median-GDC) by 5.0 and 9.1 points. For Gaussian feature denoising (Tables 3–5), GADC(II) achieves 87.8% on Coauthor-CS and 93.6% on Coauthor-Phy at noise level 1, outperforming APPNP (81.7% and 89.2%) by 6.1 and 4.4 points; GADC(III) reaches 73.66% on ogbn-products at noise level 1, beating S²GC (63.17%) by over 10 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 15 baselines on non-adaptive Meta-attack, GADC(IV) achieves the best accuracy on Citeseer (71.55% at 25%, beating STABLE's 70.49% and EvenNet's 70.89%) and Pubmed (86.74% at 25%, beating GCN-GARNET's 85.14% and HANG-quad's 85.03%), while on Cora it trails STABLE (78.69%) by ~2.7 points at 25% perturbation but remains within 1 point of EvenNet (76.30%). Under adaptive attacks, GADC(IV) dominates all four baselines (GCN, SVD-GCN, GNNGuard, Soft-Median-GDC) by 5–22 points on both Cora and Citeseer. For feature denoising, GADC(II) outperforms APPNP by 6.1 points on Coauthor-CS (87.8% vs. 81.7%) and 4.4 points on Coauthor-Phy (93.6% vs. 89.2%) at noise level 1, and GADC(III) beats S²GC by 10.5 points on ogbn-products (73.66% vs. 63.17%) at the same noise level. Compared to other GDC variants (GLP, S²GC, AirGNN, IRLS), GADC(II) consistently leads at high noise levels (e.g., 69.5% vs. 67.6% for APPNP on Cora at noise 100), with the gap widening as noise increases. On heterophilic graphs (Cornell, Texas, Wisconsin), GADC(I) with ε=1.0 improves over ε=0 by 2–5 points (e.g., Texas: 77.8% vs. 74.9%), confirming the additional term's benefit for inconsistent edges.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Graph Diffusion Convolution (GDC) family (Gasteiger et al., 2019; APPNP, S²GC, GLP, AirGNN) by reformulating the underlying Graph Signal Denoising (GSD) problem as a min-max optimization, drawing directly on the adversarial training framework of Madry et al. (2018). The key departure is that instead of perturbing input features (as in standard adversarial training), the adversary perturbs the graph Laplacian matrix, and the inner maximization admits a closed-form solution due to its quadratic structure—eliminating the need for iterative PGD. The resulting GADC architecture introduces a single additional term in the transition matrix, which the paper then instantiates in four variants to address adversarial structural attacks, feature noise, and heterophilic graphs, conceptually overlapping with GNNGuard's edge-reweighting idea but implemented as a one-shot pre-computation rather than per-layer attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified AGSD framework provides a principled, single-mechanism explanation for why a feature-similarity-based correction term helps across three distinct failure modes (structural attacks, feature noise, heterophily), which prior GDC variants addressed with separate ad-hoc modifications. The decoupling of feature aggregation from downstream training (compute S once, then train a linear/MLP head) offers a practical efficiency advantage over iterative methods like APPNP. Limitations include the heuristic replacement of F with X in the transition matrix (validated only by a single error-norm check on Cora), the pre-computation nature that prevents layer-adaptive reweighting, and the fact that the XX^T term may still be costly for very large graphs despite the masked options. Open questions include whether the framework extends to dynamic or temporal graphs, whether the approximation error degrades at scale, and whether the closed-form inner solution generalizes to non-quadratic perturbation constraints.
