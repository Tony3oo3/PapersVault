## Problem

While Transformers are known to perform in-context learning, the precise mechanism by which they estimate a context-dependent parameter and use it for prediction remains theoretically uncharacterized. Prior works on in-context learning in linear Transformers relied on augmented (concatenated) token representations and an in-context loss distinct from the standard next-token prediction objective, limiting their applicability. This paper provides a full characterization of the in-context autoregressive learning mechanism for optimally-trained linear Transformers under the next-token prediction loss.

## Input / Output

The model receives a sequence e_{1:T} of tokens generated by a first-order autoregressive process s_{t+1} = Ws_t, where W is a randomly sampled orthogonal or unitary matrix (the 'context matrix'). In the augmented setting, each token is e_t = (0, s_t, s_{t-1}) ∈ R^{3d}; in the non-augmented setting, e_t = s_t ∈ R^d. The model outputs a prediction for s_{T+1} by selecting a section of the attention output at position T or T−1, and the training objective is the population mean-squared error averaged over sequence lengths T ∈ {2, …, T_max} and random draws of W.

## Method

The paper characterizes how a trained one-layer linear Transformer decomposes its prediction into an in-context mapping Γ (estimating W or a power of W) and a prediction mapping ψ (applying the estimate to a token). Under commuting-matrix and diagonal-parameter assumptions, with augmented tokens the optimal Γ is exactly one step of gradient descent on the inner objective L(W) = ½Σ‖s_{t+1} − Ws_t‖² starting from W₀ = 0, with step size asymptotically 3/(2T_max). With non-augmented tokens and multi-head attention, the optimal Γ captures a geometric relation: for unitary contexts it is the element-wise product ē_{T−1} ⊙ e_T, and for orthogonal (rotation) contexts it implements the trigonometric identity 2cos(θ)R_θ − I₂ = R_{2θ}, requiring only δ = d/2 heads. A key structural finding is orthogonality between attention heads at the global minimum, with positional encoding selecting the most recent token(s). For positional-encoding-only attention, ill-conditioning of the Hessian for slowly-varying contexts induces an implicit ℓ₂ regularization that produces smooth, cosine-like positional encodings.

## Results

The paper's main contributions are theoretical characterizations rather than benchmark scores. Key quantitative findings include: (1) the optimal in-context mapping for augmented tokens is one step of gradient descent with step size asymptotically 3/(2T_max); (2) for unitary context matrices, achieving zero loss requires H ≥ d heads with an orthogonality property (B*ᵀA*)ᵢⱼ = 0 for i ≠ j; (3) for orthogonal context matrices, only H ≥ d/2 heads suffice, and the in-context mapping implements the trigonometric identity 2cos(θ)R_θ − I₂ = R_{2θ}; (4) experimentally, the AR process fits Moby Dick text with 4× fewer sequences exceeding a 10⁻¹² fitting error threshold compared to shuffled data, validating the AR assumption for real text.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against competing in-context learning methods in a standard benchmark sense; instead, it validates its theoretical predictions against simpler baselines. In the augmented setting (Figure 5), L steps of gradient descent on the inner loss outperform L layers of the linear Transformer by orders of magnitude for L ≥ 3, confirming that Proposition 2 does not generalize beyond one layer. In the non-augmented setting (Figure 7), increasing the number of heads from 1 to 10 reduces MSE from ~1 to ~0.35, with a notable jump when data is duplicated along the dimension axis. A full Transformer with softmax and MLP (Figure 10) outperforms gradient descent across all depths, suggesting that the full architecture captures richer in-context mappings than the linear attention model studied theoretically. Finally, Figure 9 shows that adding a softmax attention layer makes learning the in-context mapping incompatible in the commuting-matrix setting, highlighting the necessity of linear attention for the theoretical results to hold.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the in-context learning analysis of linear Transformers from the linear regression setting (Mahankali et al., 2023; Ahn et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023) to autoregressive processes where the context-dependent parameter is a full orthogonal/unitary matrix rather than a single vector. It builds directly on Von Oswald et al. (2023b), who showed that augmented-token linear Transformers implement one step of gradient descent, by proving this is not merely a possible but the *optimal* in-context mapping under commutativity and block-structure assumptions. A key departure from prior work is the consideration of non-augmented tokens with learnable positional encoding, which prior analyses could not handle due to the concatenation requirement, and the first full characterization of global minima of the next-token prediction loss for AR processes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical characterization of how a trained Transformer decomposes its prediction into an in-context estimation of a context matrix followed by a simple prediction mapping, revealing that head orthogonality and trigonometric structure in positional encodings emerge naturally from optimization. However, the analysis is constrained by strong assumptions (commuting matrices, diagonal parameter blocks, linear attention, τ=T−1), and the non-commuting case is only addressed experimentally. Open questions include whether the gradient-descent interpretation extends to deeper or non-linear architectures, how the results change when τ=T in the non-augmented setting, and whether the observed head orthogonality and positional encoding smoothing persist in full Transformers trained on real language data.
