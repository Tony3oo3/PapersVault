## Problem

Emergency department crowding is driven in part by lengthy, often unnecessary laboratory testing (40–60% of ED lab tests are redundant), which delays diagnosis and treatment. Existing cost-effective diagnostic methods optimize test selection at the population level without personalizing recommendations to individual patient presentations, and none combine a biomedical language model with reinforcement learning to jointly minimize wait time and maximize prediction accuracy in a sequential, patient-specific manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241910/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a patient's 9 triage variables (demographics, vital signs, chief complaint) and sequentially ingests results from up to 12 laboratory groups (68 individual tests), linearizing each group's results into a pipe-delimited text string (e.g., 'testname: testvalue | testname: testvalue') appended to a running sequence. At each step it outputs either the next most informative laboratory group to order or a binary prediction for one of two targets: critical outcome (in-hospital death or ICU transfer within 12 h) and lengthened ED stay (LOS > 24 h). Because the benchmark is retrospective, the action space during training is restricted to groups the patient actually received.

## Method

ED-Copilot linearizes tabular triage and lab data into a text sequence and feeds it through BioGPT (a 345 M-parameter biomedical pre-trained transformer), whose [EOS] hidden states are passed to two MLP heads: one for next-group selection (autoregressive) and one for outcome prediction. Training is two-stage: first, supervised fine-tuning minimizes a combined cross-entropy loss over next-group and outcome labels; second, Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) fine-tunes the policy to maximize a reward of TN + α·TP − β·Cost, where Cost is the cumulative time-cost of ordered groups, thereby trading off F1-score against laboratory wait time. The PLM backbone is frozen during RL to enable large-batch experience replay, and the policy is personalized because it conditions on each patient's unique sequence of prior observations rather than applying a fixed, population-level test ordering. Ablations confirm that biomedical pre-training (BioGPT vs. GPT-2 at the same scale) and the RL stage each contribute independently to accuracy and time-cost reduction.

## Results

On MIMIC-ED-Assist, ED-Copilot achieves F1=0.413 and AUC=0.820 for critical outcome prediction, and F1=0.232 and AUC=0.707 for lengthened ED stay prediction, outperforming all baselines on both tasks. ED-Copilot halves average laboratory time-cost from ~265 minutes (4.4 hours) to 125 minutes (2.1 hours) for critical outcome and 154 minutes (2.6 hours) for lengthened ED stay. Compared to the next best tree-based baseline (LightGBM), ED-Copilot improves F1 by 1.9% and AUC by 0.7% on critical outcome, and by 3.5% and 0.2% on lengthened ED stay. Against the cost-effective RL baseline SM-DDPO, ED-Copilot achieves substantially higher F1 (0.413 vs. 0.353) and lower time-cost (125 vs. 182 min) on critical outcome. Ablation studies confirm that the BioGPT backbone, linearization technique, and RL training each contribute to performance, and that ED-Copilot provides personalized recommendations that particularly benefit high-severity patient cohorts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ED-Copilot is compared against four tree-based/dense baselines (Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM, 3-layer DNN) and one cost-effective RL baseline (SM-DDPO). On critical outcome, ED-Copilot outperforms the best tree baseline (LightGBM) by 1.9% F1 and 0.7% AUC while reducing time-cost by 53% (125 vs. 265 min). On lengthened ED stay, the F1 improvement over LightGBM is 3.5% with a 42% time-cost reduction. Against SM-DDPO, which also uses RL for cost minimization but is non-personalized, ED-Copilot achieves 6% higher F1 and 31% lower time-cost on critical outcome, demonstrating the benefit of the personalized PLM backbone. Ablation studies show that removing the RL stage increases time-cost back to 265 min while slightly reducing F1, confirming RL's role in cost reduction. The BioGPT backbone outperforms GPT2-Medium (same size) and Pythia (70M) on both accuracy and cost, validating the importance of biomedical pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cost-effective medical diagnosis framework of Yu et al. (2023, SM-DDPO), which used RL to sequentially select laboratory groups to minimize financial cost, by shifting the optimization target to time-cost and introducing a biomedical pre-trained language model (BioGPT) as the backbone for patient-specific, personalized recommendations. The linearization of tabular lab results into text sequences follows the approach of Hegselmann et al. (2023, TabLLM). Unlike prior ED prediction benchmarks (Xie et al., 2022) that focus on outcome prediction from fixed features, MIMIC-ED-Assist introduces the sequential test-recommendation task with time-cost constraints, and ED-Copilot departs from non-personalized baselines by conditioning its recommendations on each patient's triage and prior test results through the PLM's sequential encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ED crowding is a well-documented driver of increased mortality, medical errors, and staff burnout, and the demonstrated halving of average laboratory wait time (4h → 2h) while improving prediction accuracy is practically significant for ED throughput. The MIMIC-ED-Assist benchmark provides the community a standardized platform for evaluating time-cost-effective diagnostic assistants, though its retrospective nature, single-hospital origin (BIDMC), and restriction to hospitalized patients limit generalizability. Key open questions include whether the approach transfers to online clinical settings, whether larger PLM backbones (e.g., Llama 7B) combined with RL would yield further gains, and how the system performs across diverse healthcare systems with different test panels and workflows.
