## Problem

In infinite-horizon average-reward constrained RL, existing algorithms either restrict to ergodic CMDPs (which are self-exploratory), require impractical knowledge of problem-specific parameters like diameter or bias span, or are computationally intractable. No computationally tractable algorithm had achieved near-optimal Õ(√T) regret bounds for the broader class of communicating CMDPs, where a policy can get stuck in a subset of states and self-exploration fails.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241671/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm requires only the state space S, action space A, the cost function c, cost thresholds τ, and a prior distribution f₁ over transition probabilities (e.g., Dirichlet). It does not require knowledge of the horizon T, the CMDP diameter D, or the bias span sp(p), unlike prior methods. At each episode it produces a stationary policy π_k, and at each timestep it outputs an action a_t; the theoretical guarantee is a Bayesian regret bound of Õ(DS√(AT)) for both the main cost and each auxiliary cost component.

## Method

PSCONRL proceeds in episodes with two stopping criteria (doubling trick and episode-length cap). At each episode start, it samples transition probabilities p_k from the posterior. If the resulting CMDP is feasible, it solves a linear program over occupancy measures to obtain the optimal constrained policy. The key innovation is the infeasibility handling: when the sampled CMDP is infeasible, instead of dropping constraints (as in PSRL-CMDP for ergodic CMDPs), PSCONRL constructs a set of auxiliary shortest-path MDPs and follows the optimal policy toward the least-visited state, guaranteeing O(DS√(AT)) steps to make the sampled model feasible. This adaptive exploration mechanism is shown to be necessary for communicating CMDPs, where a reward-maximizing policy alone cannot guarantee sufficient state coverage. The resulting Bayesian regret bound of Õ(DS√(AT)) matches the known lower bound in order of T and is the first near-optimal bound achieved by a computationally tractable algorithm for communicating CMDPs.

## Results

PSCONRL achieves a Bayesian regret bound of Õ(DS√(AT)) for each cost component (main and auxiliary), matching the theoretical lower bound Ω(√(DSAT)) in order of horizon T. This is the best-known regret bound for communicating CMDPs achieved by a computationally tractable algorithm. Empirically, PSCONRL consistently achieves the lowest cumulative regret across all three gridworld benchmarks (Marsrover 4x4, Marsrover 8x8, Box) and satisfies constraint thresholds in the long run, while C-UCRL incurs near-linear regret in the Box environment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSCONRL outperforms all three baselines on cumulative regret across all three gridworld environments. Against C-UCRL (Zheng & Ratliff, 2020), PSCONRL achieves lower regret in Marsrover 4x4 and 8x8, and dramatically lower regret in the Box environment where C-UCRL gets stuck on a suboptimal safe policy and incurs near-linear regret; on constraint violation, PSCONRL is comparable to C-UCRL in Marsrover but significantly better in Box. Against UCRL-CMDP (Singh et al., 2023), PSCONRL achieves lower regret on Marsrover 4x4, and UCRL-CMDP is so computationally expensive (O((S²A)⁴) per episode due to a non-linear program over occupancy measures and candidate transitions) that it could only be evaluated on the smallest environment. Against FHA/Alg. 3 (Chen et al., 2022), PSCONRL again achieves lower regret on Marsrover 4x4, while FHA requires solving a linear program at every timestep with O(S²AT) decision variables, making it impractical for larger problems. The key theoretical advantage is that PSCONRL achieves Õ(DS√(AT)) regret for the broader class of communicating CMDPs without requiring knowledge of the horizon T or bias span sp(p), whereas all frequentist baselines either require such knowledge or are computationally intractable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the posterior sampling (Thompson sampling) framework for RL, previously applied to unconstrained MDPs (Ouyang et al., 2017) and ergodic CMDPs (Agarwal et al., 2022), to the broader class of communicating CMDPs in the infinite-horizon average reward setting. The key departure from CMDP-PSRL (Agarwal et al., 2022) is the introduction of an explicit exploration mechanism via shortest-path MDPs whenever the sampled CMDP is infeasible, which the authors show is necessary because the self-exploratory property of ergodic CMDPs does not generalize. It also contrasts with OFU-based approaches (C-UCRL, UCRL-CMDP, Chen et al.) that achieve suboptimal T^{2/3} regret or require intractable computation for near-optimal bounds, positioning posterior sampling as a computationally tractable alternative that matches the best-known regret order.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first computationally tractable algorithm achieving near-optimal O(√T) Bayesian regret for communicating CMDPs in the average reward setting, closing a gap where prior tractable methods only achieved O(T^{2/3}) regret. The O(√DS) gap between the upper and lower bounds remains open, and the authors conjecture (but do not prove) that the bounds extend to a frequentist setting. Practical impact is currently limited to small gridworld benchmarks, and the Bayesian prior assumption (support contained in communicating CMDPs) may be restrictive for real-world deployment. The work opens a promising direction for translating posterior sampling's advantages into frequentist guarantees for constrained RL.
