## Problem

While data augmentation methods have shown strong gains for in-distribution generalization, there is a notable absence of augmentation strategies specifically designed for graph out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization. Existing graph augmentation techniques (e.g., Graph Mixup, G-Mixup) exclusively interpolate within the training distribution, making them unable to reach the OOD regions where test data actually resides. This paper addresses the gap by proposing non-Euclidean-space linear extrapolation that generates causally-valid OOD graph samples tailored to specific structural and feature distribution shifts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241115/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes graphs represented as adjacency matrices, node feature matrices, and edge feature matrices, along with environment labels that indicate the type of distribution shift present in each training sample. It produces two types of outputs: (1) augmented OOD graph samples with extrapolated structures (larger/smaller graphs, novel scaffolds) and perturbed non-causal features, which are added to the training set with new environment labels; and (2) final graph-level or node-level classification predictions. The environment labels are critical because they guide which substructures are causal (preserved) versus environmental (extrapolated), and the augmented samples form additional environments used in a VREx-style invariant regularization objective.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-pronged extrapolation framework: G-Splice for structural shifts and FeatX for feature shifts. G-Splice defines structural linear extrapolation via graph 'addition' (splicing multiple component graphs with a cVAE-based bridge generator that conditionally produces inter-graph edges) and 'subtraction' (extracting causal or environmental subgraphs through label-environment-aware pair learning with GIN-based similarity matching). It generates OOD samples in three modes—single causal subgraphs (smaller/simpler), causal+environmental subgraphs spliced, and whole graphs spliced (larger/more complex)—covering both directions of size and base/scaffold shifts. FeatX learns an invariance mask via variance scoring to identify non-causal node features, then extrapolates those features beyond the training domain using a generalized modulo operation while preserving causal features and graph topology. Theoretical guarantees (Theorems 3.6–3.7, 4.2) establish that the generated samples are causally valid under a Causal Additivity assumption and provably span OOD regions in both distribution directions.

## Results

G-Splice achieves the best OOD performance across all 8 structure-shift datasets, with the most dramatic gains on GOOD-Motif-size (86.53% accuracy vs. 54.14% for the best baseline DropNode, a ~32-point improvement) and GOOD-Motif-base (79.86% vs. 75.58% for LISA). On the more challenging real-world datasets, G-Splice leads on NCI1-size (MCC 0.37 vs. 0.22 for VREx) and DD-size (MCC 0.45 vs. 0.42 for DIR). Adding VREx-style invariance regularization (G-Splice+R) yields further gains, e.g., 65.56% on HIV-size and 83.96% on Motif-base. On the combined structure+feature shift dataset FSMotif, G-Splice+FeatX nearly doubles accuracy over the best baseline (75.11% vs. 37.00% on FSMotif-S-C).

## Baselines & Comparisons

G-Splice outperforms all 17 baselines on every one of the 8 structure-shift datasets. The largest margins appear on the synthetic GOOD-Motif benchmark: G-Splice reaches 86.53% on Motif-size, surpassing the next-best DropNode (54.14%) by over 32 points, and 79.86% on Motif-base, beating LISA (75.58%) by ~4 points. Among invariant-learning baselines, CIGA is the strongest competitor on HIV-size (62.56%) and SST2-length (81.20%), but G-Splice still leads by ~2 and ~1 points respectively. Among graph data augmentation baselines, LISA is the closest on HIV-scaffold (71.94% vs. 72.82%) and Motif-base (75.58% vs. 79.86%), while interpolation-based methods like Graph Mixup and G-Mixup consistently underperform G-Splice by 3–10 points across datasets. On the combined structure+feature shift dataset FSMotif, G-Splice+FeatX achieves 75.11% accuracy on the size-color shift, nearly doubling the best baseline GIL (37.00%), and 54.11% on the base-color shift versus DropNode's 43.44%. Adding VREx-style invariance regularization (G-Splice+R) provides consistent additional gains of 0.5–4 points over G-Splice alone, confirming that augmented environment information synergizes with invariance regularization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of graph OOD generalization and data augmentation by introducing non-Euclidean-space linear extrapolation as a principled alternative to the interpolation-based augmentations (Mixup, G-Mixup, Graph Mixup) that dominate prior graph DA work. It departs from invariant-learning-based graph OOD methods (DIR, CIGA, GIL, EERM) by taking an explicit data-centric perspective: rather than regularizing the model to ignore spurious correlations, it generates causally-valid OOD graph samples via structural splicing (cVAE-based bridge generation) and causal/environmental subgraph extraction guided by environment labels. The theoretical framework formalizes 'linear extrapolation' in graph structure space (Def. 3.2) and feature space (Def. 3.1), extending the Mixup philosophy from interpolation to extrapolation with causal validity guarantees (Theorems 3.6–3.7, 4.2).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic data-centric solution for graph OOD that generates explicit, transferable OOD graphs rather than merely regularizing the model, opening a practical avenue for augmenting graph datasets with targeted distribution shifts. The Causal Additivity assumption (Assumption 3.3) and dependence on environment label quality are acknowledged limitations that constrain applicability to tasks where labels are determined by identifiable substructures. Open questions include extending to non-linear extrapolation, handling link prediction, and reducing reliance on pre-specified environment information. The introduced FSMotif dataset for compound structure+feature shifts is a useful addition to the OOD graph benchmarking landscape.
