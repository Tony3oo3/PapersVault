## Problem

Existing molecular docking methods either depend on holo-protein structures that leak ground-truth sidechain conformations (making them impractical for realistic scenarios) or ignore pocket sidechain flexibility entirely, producing physically unrealistic poses with steric clashes. This paper introduces 'flexible docking' as an under-explored task that simultaneously predicts ligand poses and pocket sidechain conformations, mimicking the induced-fit mechanism, and proposes a rigorous benchmark (including apo-dock and cross-dock) to evaluate it.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unbound (apo) protein structure—either a crystal structure or an ESMFold-predicted structure—with randomized pocket sidechain conformations, plus a small-molecule ligand, as input. Pockets are defined as residues within 8 Å of the ligand. The model outputs the ligand's rototranslation (position and orientation relative to the fixed backbone), the torsion angles (χ1–χ4) of pocket sidechains generated autoregressively, and a learned binding energy score usable as a confidence model for ranking sampled poses. The generative process operates on an (m+6)-dimensional manifold where m is the number of rotatable bonds.

## Method

Re-Dock extends diffusion bridge generative models to non-Euclidean geometric manifolds (SO(3) for ligand rotation, SO(2)^m for torsion angles, T(3) for translation), where the bridge process is constructed via Doob's h-transform with geometry-specific transition kernels (isotropic Gaussian on SO(3), wrapped normal on the torus). The key technical contribution is an Energy-to-Geometry mapping module inspired by the Newton-Euler equations: 3D interaction energy gradients (anti-clash potential plus Amber Lennard-Jones and Coulomb terms) are converted into total force and torque, which then drive the prior drift terms in the geometric bridge, injecting physical interaction priors directly into the generative trajectory. Sidechain torsion angles are generated autoregressively (θ1 → θ2|θ1 → θ3|θ1,θ2 → θ4|θ1,θ2,θ3) to avoid cumulative noise from sequential atom-group dependencies. The score network is a 6-layer SE(3)-equivariant graph neural network on a heterogeneous atom/residue-level graph, trained via denoising score matching with the learnable drift parameterized as a combination of direct score prediction and the Energy-to-Geometry mapped force, enabling simultaneous co-modeling of binding energy and conformations.

## Results

On the PDBBind flexible redocking benchmark (holo crystal proteins), Re-Dock(40) achieves 53.9% of predictions with RMSD < 2Å and a median RMSD of 1.8Å (Top-1), outperforming DiffDock(pocket,40) at 51.8% and 2.0Å. On the more challenging apo-docking task using ESMFold-predicted unbound structures, Re-Dock(40) reaches 42.9% < 2Å (Top-1) versus 37.8% for DiffDock(pocket,40), while running in 58s per complex compared to 61s for DiffDock and 1072s for GNINA-Flex. For sidechain pose generation (Table 2), Re-Dock(40) achieves 87.5% SC-RMSD < 1Å on holo structures, dramatically outperforming GNINA-Flex (3.3%) and SMINA-Flex (2.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Re-Dock(40) outperforms the closest generative baseline DiffDock(pocket,40) across all settings: +2.1% on holo Top-1 %<2Å (53.9 vs 51.8), +5.1% on holo Top-5 %<5Å (86.7 vs 79.2), and +5.1% on apo Top-1 %<2Å (42.9 vs 37.8), while also achieving lower median RMSD in every column. Against search-based methods (GNINA, SMINA, LeDock, SurfLex, QVina), Re-Dock leads by 19–48 percentage points on holo Top-1 %<2Å and by 33–42 points on apo Top-1 %<2Å. Among flexible docking baselines, Re-Dock(40) surpasses GNINA-Flex by 24.5 points (53.9 vs 29.4) and FlexPose by 11.9 points (53.9 vs 42.0) on holo Top-1 %<2Å, while being ~18× faster than GNINA-Flex (58s vs 1072s). Notably, Re-Dock operates under a harder protocol (randomized sidechain inputs, no holo-structure access) whereas most baselines receive the correct bound pocket structure, making the margin even more significant. On the PoseBuster physical-validity benchmark (Table 5), Re-Dock(40) achieves 32.8% PB-Valid Top-1 %<2Å, nearly 3× the next best deep-learning method (DiffDock at 12.0%), confirming superior generation of physically plausible poses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion bridge framework of Wu et al. (2022a) from Euclidean space to non-Euclidean geometric manifolds (SO(3), SO(2)^m, T(3)), and builds on DiffDock's (Corso et al., 2023) geometric modeling of ligand poses via rototranslations and torsions. The key departure is the introduction of an Energy-to-Geometry mapping module inspired by Newton-Euler rigid-body mechanics, which converts 3D-coordinate interaction energy priors (anti-clash potential, Amber force field terms) into geometric drift terms on the product manifold. Unlike prior generative docking methods that either assume holo-structures (introducing priori leakage) or ignore sidechain flexibility entirely, Re-Dock jointly generates ligand rototranslations and pocket sidechain torsion angles autoregressively, explicitly modeling the induced-fit process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in molecular docking: most deep learning methods either rely on inaccessible holo-structures or produce physically unrealistic poses with steric clashes. By co-modeling binding energy and conformations through the Newton-Euler mapping, Re-Dock provides a principled way to inject physical interaction priors into generative geometry, and the strong sidechain generation results (87% SC-RMSD < 1 vs. ~3% for baselines) demonstrate the value of explicit sidechain modeling. Limitations include the omission of torsion-force drift on SO(2)^m (left as future work), insufficient exploration of end-to-end pocket prediction, and the fact that main docking improvements over DiffDock(pocket) on holo-structures are modest (~2% absolute). The cross-dock and apo-docking results suggest real-world applicability, but generalization to truly blind docking remains open.
