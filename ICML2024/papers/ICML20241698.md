## Problem

Standard fine-tuning after self-supervised pretraining (contrastive learning or masked autoencoding) does not consistently improve out-of-distribution performance over supervised learning from scratch on labeled source data, as the paper demonstrates empirically on IWILDCAM-WILDS where pretraining yields negligible OOD gains. The paper identifies the root cause: generic pretraining augmentations can misalign the connectivity structure between source and target domains, causing pretrained representations to fail to transfer across domains. The key question is how to better leverage pretrained representations for domain adaptation when generic augmentations are insufficient.

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled source inputs (RGB camera-trap images at varying resolutions, histopathology slide patches, or multivariate time series of flux measurements across 6 photometric bands with associated uncertainties), unlabeled target inputs from the same modalities, and a domain-specific targeted augmentation operator. For the time-series tasks, inputs are irregularly sampled sequences of length up to 300 observations, zero-padded to uniform shape, with 2D positional encoding (time and wavelength). Outputs are class labels (182-class wildlife species, binary tumor presence, 14-class astronomical object type) or a continuous redshift value for regression.

## Method

Connect Later is a two-stage framework: first, self-supervised pretraining with generic augmentations (SwAV contrastive learning for images, masked autoencoding with 60% masking for time series) produces an encoder that learns good within-domain representations; second, supervised fine-tuning with targeted augmentations designed with knowledge of the distribution shift (e.g., Copy-Paste Same Y for habitat backgrounds, Stain Color Jitter for hospital staining, or redshifting via Gaussian process resampling for astronomical objects) connects the domains in representation space. The paper provides a general four-step methodology for constructing targeted augmentations: identify a feature space where domains differ, fit a transformed feature distribution to the target, define a transformation distribution using domain knowledge, and sample augmentations by composing these. A theoretical analysis using augmentation graph connectivity measures (α, β, γ) shows that when across-both connectivity exceeds across-class connectivity (γ > β), standard fine-tuning fails but Connect Later achieves zero OOD error, because pretraining uses unlabeled target data to make all target inputs reachable while targeted augmentations align the source-target structure.

## Results

Connect Later achieves state-of-the-art OOD performance across all four benchmarks. On IWILDCAM-WILDS (ResNet-50), it reaches 36.9% OOD Macro F1 (vs. 31.2% for standard fine-tuning and 30.4% for ERM), and on CAMELYON17-WILDS (DenseNet121) it achieves 94.9% OOD Avg Acc (vs. 91.4% standard fine-tuning, 65.2% ERM). On ASTROCLASSIFICATION, Connect Later attains 79.90% OOD accuracy, surpassing the prior SOTA Avocado model (77.40%) by 3%. On the newly contributed REDSHIFTS dataset, it achieves 0.247 OOD RMSE, an 11% relative improvement over the best baseline (standard fine-tuning at 0.277).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Connect Later outperforms all baselines on OOD metrics across every dataset. Against ERM+targeted augmentations (Gao et al., 2023), it gains 0.8% OOD on IWILDCAM (36.9 vs. 36.1), 4.4% on CAMELYON17 (94.9 vs. 90.5), 12.4% on ASTROCLASSIFICATION (79.90 vs. 67.54), and 0.039 RMSE on REDSHIFTS (0.247 vs. 0.286), demonstrating that pretraining representations and targeted augmentations provide complementary benefits. Against the Avocado SOTA (Boone, 2019) on ASTROCLASSIFICATION, Connect Later improves OOD accuracy by 2.5% (79.90 vs. 77.40). Domain-adversarial methods (DANN, CORAL) and Noisy Student are substantially outperformed: DANN achieves only 31.9% OOD on IWILDCAM and 64.5% on CAMELYON17, while CORAL scores 27.9% and 62.3% respectively. The self-training baseline (Sagawa et al., 2022) underperforms standard fine-tuning on both astronomy tasks (65.15% vs. 67.84% OOD on ASTROCLASSIFICATION; 0.289 vs. 0.277 RMSE on REDSHIFTS), and is far below Connect Later. Notably, on CAMELYON17-WILDS, standard fine-tuning already achieves strong OOD gains (91.4% vs. 65.2% ERM), yet Connect Later still adds 3.5% further improvement, confirming that targeted augmentations at fine-tuning time are beneficial even when pretraining is already effective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two threads: the theoretical analysis of contrastive pretraining for domain adaptation via augmentation graph connectivity (Shen et al., 2022), and targeted augmentations for out-of-domain robustness (Gao et al., 2023). It departs from the prevailing narrative that self-supervised pretraining with generic augmentations reliably improves OOD performance by showing this is dataset-dependent, using the connectivity framework to diagnose when and why pretraining fails. The key methodological contribution is repurposing targeted augmentations—previously studied only in the domain generalization setting where the target is unknown—into the domain adaptation setting where unlabeled target data enables distribution matching, and applying them at fine-tuning time rather than pretraining time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard fine-tuning after contrastive pretraining can underperform or match ERM from scratch is a practical caution for practitioners who default to pretrain-then-fine-tune pipelines for OOD tasks. The general four-step methodology for designing targeted augmentations (identify feature space, fit target distribution, define transformation, sample) provides a reusable template, though the paper acknowledges that selecting the feature space z and transformation T currently requires domain expertise and could potentially be learned from data. Open questions include whether the framework extends to more complex multi-domain shifts, whether the targeted augmentation design can be automated, and whether the benefits persist when pretraining and fine-tuning use different architectures.
