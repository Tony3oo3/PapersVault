## Problem

Existing algorithms for stochastic bilevel optimization with unbounded smooth upper-level functions (e.g., RNNs) rely on nested double-loop designs that require extensive hyperparameter tuning and are impractical. This paper asks whether a provably efficient single-loop algorithm can be designed for this setting, where the upper-level function satisfies a relaxed smoothness condition rather than standard Lipschitz smoothness, and the lower-level function is strongly convex.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes stochastic gradient and Hessian-vector product oracles for both the upper-level (nonconvex, unbounded-smooth) and lower-level (strongly convex) functions of a bilevel problem, operating with constant batch sizes. It produces an ε-stationary point of the bilevel objective within Õ(1/ε⁴) oracle calls, with convergence guarantees holding both in expectation and with high probability. The upper-level update uses normalized stochastic gradient descent with momentum (dividing the gradient by its norm), while the lower-level update uses standard SGD, both with constant learning rates.

## Method

SLIP is a single-loop algorithm that first warm-starts the lower-level variable with O(log(1/ε)) SGD steps, then simultaneously updates the upper-level variable via normalized SGD with momentum and the lower-level variable via SGD at each iteration. The key insight is that accurate lower-level estimates at every iteration (required by prior double-loop methods like BO-REP) are not necessary; instead, it suffices that the lower-level error is bounded by a constant at each step and its momentum-weighted average over all iterations is O(ε). The analysis establishes a novel connection between the lower-level tracking problem and stochastic optimization under distributional drift, where the drift arises from the slow change in the upper-level variable induced by gradient normalization. This refined characterization of the lower-level variable, combined with the distributional drift framework, yields Õ(1/ε⁴) oracle complexity that is nearly optimal up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

SLIP achieves a nearly optimal oracle complexity of O~(1/ε⁴) stochastic gradient or Hessian-vector product calls to find an ε-stationary point, both in expectation and with high probability, matching the best known rate for this problem setting. Empirically, on hyper-representation learning (SNLI, Amazon Review Dataset) and data hyper-cleaning (Sentiment140 with corruption rates p=0.2 and p=0.4), SLIP consistently achieves the highest training and test accuracy across all epochs compared to all baselines. In running time comparisons, SLIP is the fastest method overall, with a particularly significant speedup over BO-REP (the prior state-of-the-art for unbounded smoothness) due to its single-loop design that avoids redundant gradient computations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLIP is compared against 8 baselines: two meta-learning methods (MAML, ANIL) and six bilevel optimization algorithms (StocBio, TTSA, F²SA, SABA, MA-SOBA, BO-REP). On both hyper-representation learning (SNLI and ARD) and data hyper-cleaning (Sentiment140, p=0.2 and p=0.4), SLIP achieves the highest training and test accuracy at every epoch, consistently outperforming all baselines. The most direct comparison is with BO-REP (Hao et al., 2024), the only prior method handling unbounded smoothness: SLIP matches its O~(ε⁻⁴) theoretical complexity while being single-loop, and empirically runs significantly faster because the single-loop design computes the y-gradient as a byproduct of the x-gradient backpropagation, avoiding BO-REP's redundant periodic y-updates. Among the smoothness-based baselines (StocBio, TTSA, F²SA, SABA, MA-SOBA), SLIP also wins on accuracy, though these methods assume bounded smoothness and are not theoretically applicable to the RNN-based tasks tested. The running time comparison (Figure 3) confirms SLIP is the fastest method across all four experimental settings, with BO-REP as the second-fastest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bilevel optimization framework of Hao et al. (2024), which introduced the first algorithm (BO-REP) for the unbounded-smoothness upper-level / strongly-convex lower-level setting but required a complex double-loop structure with periodic lower-level refinement. SLIP departs from this by showing that per-iteration high-probability accuracy of the lower-level variable is sufficient but not necessary, and instead leverages a novel connection to stochastic optimization under distributional drift (Cutler et al., 2023) to control lower-level error with only constant-bounded per-step error plus small weighted-average error. This analytical perspective is entirely new to the bilevel optimization literature and enables the first provably convergent single-loop algorithm in this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-loop design is practically significant for meta-learning with recurrent architectures (RNNs, LSTMs, transformers) where the upper-level smoothness is inherently unbounded, as it eliminates the tuning burden of nested loops and achieves measurable speedups over BO-REP through shared backpropagation. The nearly optimal O(1/ε⁴) complexity (matching the nonconvex stochastic optimization lower bound) and the first high-probability convergence guarantee for stochastic bilevel optimization are important theoretical milestones. Open questions include whether the complexity can be improved to O(1/ε³) under individual relaxed smoothness of the stochastic oracle, and whether the distributional-drift connection generalizes to non-strongly-convex lower-level problems.
