## Problem

Existing counterfactual image editing methods manipulate latent vectors to change individual features but ignore causal relationships among generative factors, leading to spurious changes in unrelated features (e.g., changing a woman's age inadvertently changes her gender). The paper proves two fundamental impossibility results: counterfactual image distributions are not identifiable from i.i.d. samples and labels alone, and even with the causal diagram known, the target distribution remains non-identifiable, meaning no existing method can guarantee causal consistency of its outputs.

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial image, a counterfactual editing query specifying which generative factors to intervene on (e.g., do(Y=0) to make a person older), and a causal diagram encoding prior knowledge about how discrete generative factors (gender, age, hair color, etc.) causally relate to each other and to the image. It outputs a pair (i, i') where i is the initial image and i' is the counterfactual image sampled from P(I_{x'} | I=i), with the guarantee that the F-ctf query over a user-specified care set W falls within the analytically derived optimal counterfactual bounds. The generative factors V are assumed discrete and finite, while the image I is a high-dimensional pixel array.

## Method

The paper formalizes image counterfactual editing through Augmented Structural Causal Models (ASCMs), a two-stage generative process where exogenous variables interact with labeled generative factors V to produce unobserved factors, which are then mapped to image pixels via an invertible function f_I. Since exact identification is impossible, the authors introduce ctf-consistent estimators: a proxy model is valid if (1) it matches the observational distribution P(V,I) and (2) its F-ctf query over a user-specified care set W falls within the optimal counterfactual bounds derived from the causal diagram and P(V). The ANCM algorithm instantiates this by training a G-constrained Neural Causal Model (NCM) where a VAE decoder approximates P(I|Û) and a separate encoder Q_ω(Û|I) approximates the posterior, trained jointly with a cross-entropy loss on feature labels. Counterfactual sampling proceeds by drawing exogenous variables Û from the prior, decoding to obtain the initial image, then re-evaluating the decoder under the intervened submodel M_{x'} to produce the counterfactual image, ensuring causal effects propagate correctly through the generative factor graph.

## Results

On the Colored MNIST experiment, the key metric is whether the empirical F-ctf query P(C_{D=6}=1, B_{D=6}=0 | D=6, C=1, B=1) falls within the theoretical optimal bound [0.66, 1]. ANCM is the only method whose estimate (averaged over 4 runs) lies within this bound, while CVAE, CGN, and DEAR all produce out-of-bound estimates. On CelebA-HQ, ANCM preserves causal invariances: gender remains unchanged after an age intervention (matching the bound [1,1]) and gray hair probability increases as expected, whereas CVAE and DEAR may inadvertently alter gender. ANCM also produces higher-quality counterfactual images than DEAR when combined with DiffuseVAE refinement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ANCM outperforms all three baselines on the core criterion of ctf-consistency (F-ctf query within optimal bounds). CVAE (Sohn et al., 2015) fails most severely: it exploits the spurious D–C correlation to change digit color after editing, and it never removes the bar, missing the D→B causal effect entirely; on CelebA-HQ it also flips gender after an age intervention. CGN (Sauer & Geiger, 2021) preserves color correctly but never removes the bar, indicating it captures no D→B causal effect; it is not evaluated on CelebA-HQ due to its restricted variable set (Shape, Texture, Background). DEAR (Shen et al., 2022) fails on MNIST because it assumes Markovian generative factors and thus cannot handle the D–C confounding, leading to color changes; on CelebA-HQ it does preserve gender and smile correctly but produces visibly lower-quality images than ANCM. Overall, ANCM is the only method that simultaneously satisfies the theoretical optimal bounds, preserves non-intervened features, propagates causal effects to intervened features, and generates high-fidelity counterfactual images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Neural Causal Model (NCM) framework of Xia et al. (2021), which encodes causal constraints into deep generative models for counterfactual estimation, and extends it to the image generation setting through a new formalism called Augmented SCMs (ASCMs). It departs from prior counterfactual image editing methods (CVAE, CGN, DEAR) that manipulate latent spaces without accounting for causal relationships among generative factors, by proving that such approaches cannot guarantee causal consistency. The paper also extends the classical SCM framework by introducing the image variable as an invertible function of generative factors, creating a two-stage generative process that prior causal image editing work had not formally captured.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide formal guarantees (ctf-consistency) for counterfactual image generation, addressing a fundamental gap where prior methods could produce images that violate known causal invariances (e.g., changing gender when only age is intervened). The impossibility results clarify that no method can exactly recover the true counterfactual image distribution from observational data alone, setting realistic expectations for the field. Open questions include scaling ANCMs to higher-dimensional continuous image spaces, handling more complex causal structures with many unobserved confounders, and whether the care-set relaxation can be made adaptive rather than user-specified. The practical impact is currently limited by the small-scale experiments (MNIST, CelebA-HQ) and the discrete generative factor assumption.
