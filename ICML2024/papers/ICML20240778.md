## Problem

UDA models exhibit poorly calibrated predictive uncertainty on target data, a problem that is particularly challenging because no labeled target data exists and severe distribution shifts prevent source-domain calibration from transferring. Mainstream solutions treat UDA calibration as a covariate shift problem via importance weighting, but these are unreliable under severe or label shift, require additional model training for density estimation, and depend on access to labeled source data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240778/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

PseudoCal takes a batch of unlabeled target images and a fixed, already-trained UDA model (used purely as a black-box inference oracle) and produces a single calibrated temperature scalar T that rescales the model's logits to yield well-calibrated probability predictions. The only hyperparameter is the mixup ratio λ (fixed at 0.65 in all experiments), and no source data, labeled target data, or additional model training is required. For semantic segmentation, each pixel is treated as an individual sample, with 20,000 pixels randomly sampled per image for computational tractability.

## Method

The key insight is a factorization of the temperature-scaling NLL objective into separate contributions from correctly and wrongly predicted samples, revealing that any labeled set matching the real target's correct-wrong statistics will yield a temperature close to the oracle target temperature. PseudoCal exploits this by performing inference-stage input-level mixup (λ ∈ (0.5, 1.0)) between pairs of target samples from different clusters (i.e., with different pseudo-labels), generating a labeled pseudo-target set whose mixed labels naturally produce both correct and wrong predictions when evaluated by the model. Under the cluster assumption, samples far from the decision boundary remain correctly classified after mild cross-cluster perturbation while boundary samples remain misclassified, ensuring the pseudo-target set mirrors the real target's correct-wrong statistics. Standard temperature scaling is then applied to this labeled pseudo-target set, converting the unsupervised calibration problem into a trivially solvable supervised one. This design makes PseudoCal fully post-hoc, source-free, and applicable across closed-set, partial-set, and source-free UDA scenarios without any modification.

## Results

PseudoCal achieves an average ECE of 5.20% on Office-Home (closed-set), 5.16% on Office-31, 4.46% on VisDA, 2.81% on DomainNet, and 6.22% on Office-Home (partial-set), consistently approaching the Oracle upper bound (2.21%, 2.47%, 1.06%, 1.25%, 3.56% respectively). On source-free UDA, PseudoCal reduces ECE to 6.00% on DomainNet and 28.09% on ImageNet-Sketch, and on semantic segmentation (Cityscapes) it achieves 10.86% average ECE. Across all five UDA scenarios and ten UDA methods, PseudoCal outperforms the second-best calibration method by 1.88–15.05% in ECE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PseudoCal outperforms all four baselines across every UDA scenario. Against TempScal-src (Guo et al., 2017), which calibrates using only the labeled source validation set, PseudoCal reduces ECE from 9.53% to 5.20% on Office-Home and from 9.23% to 2.81% on DomainNet, demonstrating that source-domain calibration is insufficient under domain shift. Against CPCS (Park et al., 2020) and TransCal (Wang et al., 2020), both of which use importance weighting to address covariate shift, PseudoCal achieves substantially lower ECE (e.g., 5.20% vs. 17.09% for CPCS and 10.30% for TransCal on Office-Home), and unlike these methods it requires no additional model training or source data access. Against Ensemble (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017), the only other method that avoids source data, PseudoCal still wins by 4.24% on partial-set Office-Home, 8.87% on source-free DomainNet, and 15.05% on ImageNet-Sketch, while also being a single-model post-hoc method rather than requiring multiple trained models. The only scenario where PseudoCal does not clearly dominate is semantic segmentation (Table 7), where it achieves 10.86% vs. Ensemble's 11.75%—a modest 0.89% margin—though it still beats TempScal-src (11.93%) and No Calib. (15.48%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the mainstream UDA calibration paradigm (CPCS, TransCal) that frames calibration as a covariate shift problem requiring importance weighting and labeled source data. Instead, it reframes UDA calibration as an unsupervised target-domain problem, building on two repurposed ideas: mixup (Zhang et al., 2018), moved from a training-time regularizer to an inference-stage data synthesis tool, and temperature scaling (Guo et al., 2017), applied to the synthesized pseudo-target set rather than a source validation set. The theoretical grounding comes from a factorization of the NLL objective into correct and wrong prediction components, combined with the cluster assumption to justify sample-level correspondence between mixed and real target samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PseudoCal provides a unified, source-free calibration solution that works across closed-set, partial-set, and source-free UDA settings without requiring extra model training or access to source data — a practical advantage for privacy-preserving and black-box UDA scenarios. The ablation showing that naive pseudo-labeling (λ=1.0) dramatically worsens calibration (ECE nearly doubles) is a useful negative result that clarifies why the cross-cluster mixup mechanism is essential. Open questions include whether the approach extends to open-set UDA, how it behaves with extremely few target samples, and whether the ~60% harmonic correspondence rate holds under more severe distribution shifts or with weaker UDA models.
