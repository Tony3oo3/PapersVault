## Problem

CLIP-based vision encoders in large vision-language models (LVLMs) like LLaVA and OpenFlamingo are highly vulnerable to imperceptible adversarial perturbations on images, enabling stealthy targeted attacks that force arbitrary outputs. The only prior robust CLIP method, TeCoA, relies on supervised adversarial fine-tuning with cross-entropy loss, which distorts the original embeddings, degrades clean performance on non-ImageNet zero-shot tasks, and breaks downstream LVLMs without retraining. The paper asks whether one can make the CLIP vision encoder adversarially robust while preserving its original embedding so that all downstream tasks inherit robustness for free.

## Input / Output

The method takes unlabeled RGB images at 224×224 resolution and produces a fine-tuned ViT-L/14 vision encoder whose output is a robust image embedding. Training uses only the class-token embedding (not all patch tokens) and does not require the CLIP text encoder or any labels. The resulting encoder is a drop-in replacement: it can be plugged into any downstream LVLM (LLaVA, OpenFlamingo) or zero-shot classifier without modifying projection layers, language models, or prompt templates, and it produces the same clean embeddings as the original CLIP up to a small L2 deviation.

## Method

FARE (Fine-tuning for Adversarially Robust Embeddings) introduces an unsupervised adversarial fine-tuning loss that minimizes the squared L2 distance between the fine-tuned encoder's output on an adversarially perturbed image z and the original CLIP encoder's output on the clean image x, with the inner maximization over z solved via 10-step PGD at a chosen ℓ∞ radius. This design simultaneously enforces robustness (perturbed embeddings stay close to the clean original) and embedding preservation (as the loss approaches zero, the fine-tuned model reproduces the original CLIP output on clean inputs), unlike TeCoA's cosine-similarity-based cross-entropy loss which is invariant to radial scaling and thus allows arbitrary distortion of embedding magnitudes. A theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) bounds the change in cosine similarity between image and text embeddings by the L2 embedding deviation, guaranteeing that zero-shot classification performance is preserved. Training requires only 2 epochs on ImageNet with no labels, costing roughly 0.2% of the original CLIP training budget, and the method generalizes to any foundation model with an intermediate embedding layer linking modalities.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5 7B, FARE⁴ achieves 102.4 CIDEr on COCO (clean) and 40.9 CIDEr under ε=4/255 adversarial perturbation, compared to 115.5/3.1 for original CLIP and 88.3/35.3 for TeCoA⁴. FARE² maintains near-original clean performance (109.9 CIDEr on COCO) while achieving 53.6 CIDEr at ε=2/255. On zero-shot classification across 13 datasets, FARE⁴ attains 61.1% average clean accuracy (vs. 73.1% for CLIP and 54.2% for TeCoA⁴) and 32.4% robust accuracy at ε=4/255, slightly outperforming TeCoA⁴ (31.9%). Against stealthy targeted attacks (10,000 APGD iterations), FARE⁴ and TeCoA⁴ achieve 0% attack success rate at ε=4/255, while FARE² breaks only 2.0% of the time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to original CLIP, FARE⁴ preserves 88.7% of clean COCO CIDEr (102.4 vs. 115.5) while making the model robust (40.9 vs. 3.1 at ε=4/255); FARE² is even closer at 95.1% clean retention (109.9). Against TeCoA⁴ (the only prior robust CLIP method), FARE⁴ wins on nearly every metric: +14.1 CIDEr on COCO clean (102.4 vs. 88.3), +5.6 CIDEr on COCO robust at ε=4/255 (40.9 vs. 35.3), +6.9% average zero-shot clean accuracy (61.1% vs. 54.2%), and +0.5% robust accuracy at ε=4/255 (32.4% vs. 31.9%). FARE⁴ also outperforms TeCoA⁴ on hallucination (76.3% vs. 72.2% POPE F1) and chain-of-thought reasoning (62.3% vs. 59.9% SQA-I). The key advantage of FARE over TeCoA is that its unsupervised embedding-preservation loss avoids the clean-performance degradation that supervised adversarial fine-tuning causes on non-ImageNet tasks and LVLM integration, eliminating the need for any downstream retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from TeCoA (Mao et al., 2023), the only prior method for robust CLIP vision encoders, which performs supervised adversarial fine-tuning with cross-entropy loss on ImageNet. The key conceptual shift is replacing the supervised classification objective with an unsupervised embedding-preservation loss (FARE) that minimizes the L2 distance between the fine-tuned and original CLIP embeddings on adversarially perturbed inputs, thereby preserving the original feature geometry. This draws on the broader line of self-supervised adversarial training for contrastive models (SimCLR, BYOL) but adapts it to the CLIP setting with a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.1) that L2 embedding preservation implies cosine-similarity preservation for zero-shot classification. The paper also builds on the adversarial attack pipeline of Schlarmann & Hein (2023) for evaluating LVLM robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that FARE-CLIP serves as a drop-in replacement for the original CLIP encoder in any LVLM (LLaVA, OpenFlamingo, etc.) without retraining, simultaneously providing robustness against imperceptible targeted attacks, transfer attacks, and jailbreaking while preserving clean performance. This addresses a pressing deployment concern as LVLMs are increasingly used in real-world applications. Limitations include restriction to the vision modality (language-side attacks remain unaddressed), focus on CLIP-based LVLMs with frozen vision encoders, and the fact that robustness is bounded by the training radius. Open questions include whether the approach extends to LVLMs that fine-tune their vision encoder during training, and whether similar embedding-preservation strategies apply to other multi-modal foundation models.
