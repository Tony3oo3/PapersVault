## Problem

Existing communication-efficient DP mean estimation schemes (e.g., CSGM) are designed in L∞ geometry and require random rotation or Kashin's representation to handle L2-bounded data, introducing suboptimal leading constants in MSE. Moreover, these schemes do not extend to streaming DP settings where noise is correlated across rounds through matrix factorization, rendering them incompatible with DP-FTRL optimizers that introduce spatial and temporal coupling of compression randomness.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local gradient vector g_i ∈ R^d with ‖g_i‖₂ ≤ Δ₂, which is coordinate-wise sparsified via independent Bernoulli(γ) masks (γ = b/d controls the b-bit communication budget) before transmission to the server. In the non-streaming setting the server returns a single noisy mean estimate; in the streaming setting it produces T correlated releases of the form A·G̃ + B·Z, where G̃ is the sparsified data matrix and B·Z encodes the correlated Gaussian noise from the factorization A = BC. The L∞ clipping norm Δ∞ enters only as a higher-order correction, with primary sensitivity governed by Δ₂, so the privacy guarantee converges to that of the uncompressed Gaussian mechanism as d grows.

## Method

The core contribution is a novel Rényi DP accounting algorithm for the sparsified Gaussian mechanism that directly operates in L2 geometry by exploiting the convexity of the 1D subsampled Gaussian mechanism's Rényi DP profile and extending it to multi-dimensional settings via Karamata's inequality, yielding MSEs that converge to those of the uncompressed Gaussian mechanism as dimension d increases. For the streaming extension (Sparsified Gaussian Matrix Factorization), the paper addresses two challenges absent in prior work: temporal coupling (sparsification randomness at round t propagates to later rounds through the matrix mechanism) and spatial coupling (adaptive gradients couple compression seeds across coordinates). The key analytical technique is an LQ decomposition of B followed by a transcript decomposition O = P + Q(P) that transforms the adaptive releasing problem into a non-adaptive one, after which standard privacy amplification by subsampling applies. Empirically, the method achieves over 100× compression for DP-SGD on F-EMNIST (CNN) and Stack Overflow NWP (LSTM) with negligible accuracy loss.

## Results

The paper demonstrates at least a 100× improvement in compression rates for DP-SGD across Federated EMNIST (62-class image classification with a ~1M-parameter CNN) and Stack Overflow next-word prediction (4M-parameter LSTM), while maintaining accuracy comparable to the uncompressed Gaussian mechanism. The Δ∞/Δ₂ ratio achieved via random rotation is 6.4×10⁻³ for F-EMNIST and 3.3×10⁻³ for Stack Overflow, confirming that the L₂-CSGM noise multiplier converges rapidly to that of the uncompressed Gaussian mechanism as dimension grows. For the streaming DP-FTRL setting, the Sparsified Gaussian Matrix Factorization (SGMF) achieves meaningful compression over the uncompressed matrix mechanism, though compression rates are generally lower than in the DP-SGD (non-streaming) setting and performance is more sensitive to server learning rates and L₂ clip norms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the uncompressed Gaussian mechanism (Balle & Wang, 2018), L₂-CSGM achieves comparable accuracy at compression rates exceeding 100× on both F-EMNIST and Stack Overflow, with the gap narrowing as cohort size increases (e.g., cohort size 1000 vs. 100). The theoretical guarantee (Lemma 4.3) confirms that under the same MSE constraint, the Rényi DP of L₂-CSGM converges to that of the Gaussian mechanism as Δ∞²/Δ₂² → 0, which holds rapidly in high dimensions. Against the uncompressed matrix mechanism from Denisov et al. (2022), SGMF achieves comparable accuracy at higher compression rates in the DP-FTRL streaming setting, but the paper notes that compression gains are smaller than in the non-streaming DP-SGD case and that the matrix mechanism is more sensitive to hyperparameter choices (server learning rate, L₂ clip norm). The paper also contrasts L₂-CSGM with the prior L∞-CSGM (Chen et al., 2023), showing that the L∞-CSGM Rényi DP bound is inflated by a factor of dΔ∞²/Δ₂² > 1 (O(log d) with random rotation), making L₂-CSGM strictly superior in leading constants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the coordinate-subsampled Gaussian mechanism (CSGM) of Chen et al. (2023) from L∞ geometry to L2 geometry, eliminating the suboptimal leading constants that arise from random rotation or Kashin's representation. It further extends the sparsified Gaussian mechanism to the streaming DP setting by integrating it with the matrix factorization framework of Denisov et al. (2022) and the DP-FTRL optimizer of Kairouz et al. (2021b), a combination that prior work had not addressed due to spatial and temporal coupling challenges in adaptive releases. The key technical departure is a novel Rényi DP accounting that directly operates in L2 geometry and a transcript decomposition trick that reduces the adaptive streaming problem to a non-adaptive one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× compression improvement for DP-SGD and DP-FTRL is practically significant for production federated learning systems (e.g., Gboard), where communication bandwidth is a primary bottleneck. The theoretical result that L2-CSGM's privacy guarantee converges to the uncompressed Gaussian mechanism as dimension grows provides a clean justification for why L2 geometry is the correct setting, resolving a gap that made L∞-CSGM suboptimal. Open questions include whether the scheme composes with quantization or other lossless compression, whether the single-participation-per-epoch assumption can be relaxed, and whether the spatial/temporal coupling analysis extends to more general (non-Gaussian) noise or non-linear compression operators.
