## Problem

Existing LP/MILP instance generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: G2MILP preserves structural similarity but produces infeasible instances (as low as 16% feasibility on hard datasets), while DIG-MILP guarantees feasibility/boundedness but sacrifices structural fidelity by modifying all constraints, causing generated instances to fall out of the training distribution. This gap hinders the use of generated data for supervised ML tasks that require both distributional consistency and reliable labels.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an LP/MILP instance as a triplet (A, b, c) encoded into a weighted bipartite graph with 7-dimensional node features, and optionally downstream task prediction errors for task-oriented vector selection. It outputs a new instance (Â, b̂, ĉ) where only a fraction η of constraint or variable vectors in A are regenerated via diffusion, while b̂ and ĉ are sampled from uniform distributions bounded by collected primal/dual feasible solutions, guaranteeing 100% feasibility and boundedness by construction.

## Method

MILP-FBGen is a diffusion-based generative framework with three modules. The Structure-Preserving Generation Module (SPGM) reformulates each constraint vector as a 2×n matrix (binary connectivity row rescaled to {−1,1} plus a weight row), applies a continuous-space DDPM forward/reverse process, and uses a message-passing GNN on the bipartite graph to condition the denoising network, regenerating only η fraction of vectors to control the novelty–preservation trade-off. The Feasibility/Boundedness-Constrained Sampling Module (FBCSM) collects feasible solutions from the primal MILP and dual LP relaxations, then samples b̂ uniformly in [Âx̂, Âx̄] and ĉ uniformly in [Âᵀy̲, Âᵀŷ], where the upper/lower bounds incorporate variable bound propagation to maximize constraint tightness and avoid superfluous constraints. A theoretical proposition proves that any instance generated this way is both feasible and bounded. Two vector selection strategies (random and task-oriented, where selection probability is inversely proportional to downstream task confidence) guide which vectors to regenerate, with the task-oriented variant enabling iterative data augmentation focused on hard cases.

## Results

MILP-FBGen achieves 100% feasibility across all six datasets (SC, MIK, MIRP, PDS, WA, Anonymous) at every tested replacement ratio, while maintaining the lowest computational-hardness gap among all methods. On the medium-scale MIRP dataset (η=0.01), MILP-FBGen achieves a gap of only 2.89% versus 37.13% for G2MILP and 23.51% for DIG-MILP. On the hard WA dataset (η=0.001), MILP-FBGen reaches 100% feasibility with a 24.55% gap, whereas DIG-MILP drops to 16% feasibility with an 88.59% gap. MILP-FBGen is the only method capable of generating instances for the extremely large PDS dataset (n=317,105, m=188,399), achieving a 9.22% gap and 100% feasibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MILP-FBGen dominates all three baselines on both feasibility and structural preservation. Bowly (heuristic) is the weakest: it produces trivially easy instances (98.23% gap on SC) and cannot generate instances for any medium or hard dataset. G2MILP (VAE-based) matches MILP-FBGen on easy datasets (0.83% vs 0.38% gap on SC) but suffers severe feasibility collapse on harder problems—dropping to 57.1% on MIRP (η=0.1) and 16% on WA—making most generated instances unusable for supervised learning. DIG-MILP guarantees feasibility on easy datasets but at the cost of large structural deviation (32.98% gap on SC, 45.09% on Anonymous), and its feasibility also degrades on hard datasets (81.6% Anonymous, 16% WA). Critically, MILP-FBGen is the only method that can handle the PDS dataset (317K variables, 188K constraints), where both G2MILP and DIG-MILP fail entirely, and it achieves 100% feasibility with a 9.22% gap there. In downstream tasks, MILP-FBGen's augmented data yields the largest gains: 31.46% solving-time reduction on MIRP initial basis prediction and 19.41% on PDS hyperparameter tuning, versus 10.78% and negative gains for DIG-MILP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the destroy-and-repair paradigm for MILP instance generation introduced by G2MILP (Geng et al., 2023), replacing its VAE-based generative backbone with a denoising diffusion model (following Ho et al., 2020) and adapting the discrete-to-continuous mapping from Analog Bits (Chen et al., 2022) for the binary connectivity row. The key departure from both G2MILP and DIG-MILP is the addition of a feasibility/boundedness-constrained sampling module that constructs b and c from collected primal/dual feasible solutions, guaranteeing 100% feasibility while preserving structural similarity — a combination neither prior method achieves. The bipartite graph representation and GNN-based message passing follow the standard formulation from Gasse et al. (2019) and Fan et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Data scarcity remains the primary bottleneck for applying supervised ML to LP/MILP solving, and the 100% feasibility guarantee makes generated instances directly usable for label acquisition — a practical requirement that prior deep generative methods (G2MILP) fail to meet on non-trivial datasets. The paper acknowledges that the reverse diffusion process is time-consuming, which limits throughput for large-scale data augmentation, and the task-oriented selection strategy, while effective, is restricted to node-level regression/classification tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even larger instances beyond PDS, whether faster diffusion samplers (e.g., DDIM, score-based) can reduce generation cost, and whether the constrained sampling module generalizes to problems with equality constraints or mixed inequality directions.
