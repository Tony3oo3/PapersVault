## Problem

Diffusion-based denoisers require the noise level and covariance to be known a priori, which prevents blind denoising—the simultaneous recovery of both the signal and the noise characteristics. While diffusion models have been used for posterior sampling in non-blind settings, no existing work has fully addressed blind denoising using diffusion-based priors, leaving a gap for applications where characterizing the noise itself is as important as recovering the clean signal.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy observation y = x + ε (where ε is parametric Gaussian with covariance Σ_φ) and an analytical prior p(φ) on the noise parameters, and produces samples from the joint posterior p(x, φ|y). In the image application, inputs are 256×256 RGB images contaminated by colored noise with power spectrum S_φ(k) = |k|^φ, and outputs include individual denoised samples x̂, the posterior mean E[x|y] (which achieves higher PSNR), and the full posterior distribution over (σ, φ). In the CMB application, the 'noise' is the CMB signal itself, so the output posterior over φ directly constrains cosmological parameters (H₀, ω_b).

## Method

Gibbs Diffusion (GDiff) alternates two sampling steps: (1) a conditional diffusion model samples x | y, φ by solving a reverse SDE starting from a rescaled observation ỹ = a(t*)y, where the forward SDE is designed so that y corresponds to a stopped noising process at time t*; (2) Hamiltonian Monte Carlo samples φ | y, x (equivalently φ | ε = y − x) using the analytically tractable Gaussian log-likelihood. The key insight is that the forward SDE's noise covariance is set to the normalized Σ_φ, making the diffusion model naturally encode both the signal prior and the posterior sampling for any noise parameter value, with φ fed as a conditioning input to the score network. Theoretically, the paper establishes a compatibility condition for the existence of the Gibbs stationary distribution and derives a KL-divergence bound (Theorem 2.3) quantifying the error introduced by the diffusion model's approximation of the true conditional p(x|y,φ). The architecture is a U-Net (with attention for images, ResBlocks for CMB) trained via denoising score matching over 5,000 discrete time steps, conditioned on both time t and the noise parameters φ.

## Results

On ImageNet and CBSD68, GDiff's posterior mean E[x|y] systematically outperforms both BM3D (non-blind) and DnCNN (blind) across all noise levels (σ = 0.06, 0.1, 0.2) and noise types (pink φ=-1, white φ=0, blue φ=1). For example, at σ=0.2 with white noise on ImageNet, GDiff E[x|y] achieves 29.3 dB PSNR vs. 28.1 dB (BM3D) and 28.4 dB (DnCNN). However, individual posterior samples x̂ from GDiff score lower than both baselines on PSNR (e.g., 26.5 dB vs. 28.1/28.4 dB at σ=0.2, white noise), reflecting the trade-off between sample realism and point-estimate accuracy. The advantage of the posterior mean grows with noise level, reaching ~2.2 dB over BM3D at σ=0.2 for pink noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GDiff is compared against BM3D (a non-blind, classical collaborative filtering method that knows the noise level) and DnCNN (a blind CNN denoiser trained only on white Gaussian noise). The key finding is that GDiff's posterior mean E[x|y] outperforms both baselines across all 18 evaluation settings (2 datasets × 3 noise levels × 3 noise types), with margins of 0.5–2.2 dB in PSNR. For instance, at σ=0.2 with pink noise on ImageNet, GDiff E[x|y] achieves 25.7 dB vs. 23.5 dB (BM3D) and 21.7 dB (DnCNN). However, individual GDiff posterior samples x̂ consistently underperform both baselines on PSNR (by ~1–2 dB), illustrating the well-known trade-off between sample realism and point-estimate fidelity. The SSIM results (Table 2) confirm the same pattern: posterior means beat both baselines while individual samples lag behind. Notably, DnCNN's performance degrades for colored noise (φ≠0) since it was trained exclusively on white noise, making the comparison most fair at φ=0.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-model-as-denoiser paradigm (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021) and the earlier CMB dust-removal work of Heurtel-Depeiges et al. (2023) by wrapping the diffusion posterior sampler inside a Gibbs loop that jointly infers signal and noise parameters. It departs from prior blind denoisers (Noise2Self, El Helou & Süsstrunk) that were restricted to white Gaussian noise and point estimates, and from guidance-based diffusion inverse-problem methods (DPS, Π-GDM) that require known noise and suffer numerical instability under high condition numbers. The theoretical contribution—characterizing the stationary distribution of a Gibbs sampler where one conditional is an approximate diffusion model—fills a gap left by standard MCMC theory, which assumes exact conditionals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GDiff provides the first principled Bayesian framework for blind denoising with colored noise and full posterior uncertainty, demonstrated across two very different domains (natural images and CMB cosmology). Key limitations include the Gaussian noise assumption, the computational cost of iterative diffusion sampling, and a measurable (if modest) calibration bias introduced by the diffusion model's approximation of the true conditional, as revealed by SBC diagnostics. Open questions include extending the framework to non-Gaussian noise, improving sampling efficiency via consistency models or latent diffusion, and developing practical diagnostics for the conditional-compatibility condition that guarantees a well-defined stationary distribution.
