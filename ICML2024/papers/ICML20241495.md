## Problem

Performing ℓ_p leverage score sampling in the turnstile data stream model—where individual matrix entries can be added, deleted, or updated in arbitrary order—was previously intractable with (1±ε) guarantees and polynomial sketch size. Oblivious sketches for logistic regression achieved only O(1) approximations or required exp(1/ε) sketch dimensions, while subsampling-based coresets required offline or row-wise insertion access. The paper asks whether one can simultaneously obtain full turnstile flexibility, fully polynomial sketch/subsample size, (1±ε) factor approximation, and convexity of the reduced optimization problem.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a single pass over a turnstile stream of updates (i, j, v) to an n×d matrix, along with a target sample size k and an optional d×d conditioning matrix P (default identity). It outputs a set of Θ(k) tuples (i, ã_i, w_i) where ã_i is a slightly perturbed row satisfying ‖ã_i − a_i‖_p ≤ (ε/3)‖a_i‖_p, and w_i is a (1±ε)-approximation of the inverse sampling probability. The sketch uses r·s space with r = O(k log n · (α^p β^p / ε)^p) and s = O(log(n/δ)), and the entire pipeline runs in input sparsity time Õ(nnz(A)).

## Method

The core mechanism combines CountSketch with randomized row scaling: each row a_i is multiplied by t_i^{-1/p} where t_i ~ U(0,1), so that the probability of becoming a 'heavy hitter' in the sketch is proportional to ‖a_i‖_p^p / ‖A‖_p^p. Rather than using p-specific norm estimators (AMS for p=2, Cauchy for p=1), the algorithm extracts a threshold from the 0.65-percentile of sketched bucket norms across s independent CountSketch repetitions, and uses medians to estimate individual row norms and select the best representative approximation. A postprocessing step right-multiplies the sketch by a conditioning matrix P (from ℓ_p subspace embeddings) to convert row-norm sampling into ℓ_p leverage score sampling, and the returned weights are calibrated to (1±ε) approximate the inverse inclusion probabilities. Nisan's pseudorandom generator reduces the space from Ω(n) to polylog(n) overhead, and the framework extends to mixture distributions (e.g., leverage scores + uniform) needed for logistic and probit regression coresets.

## Results

The paper evaluates its turnstile ℓ_p sampler on three real-world datasets (Covertype, Webspam, KDDCup) for logistic regression and ℓ₁ regression, measuring the approximation ratio f(z̃)/f(z_opt) (lower is better) as a function of reduced size, reporting medians over 21 repetitions. The turnstile sampler consistently outperforms plain oblivious sketching by a large margin across all datasets and both problems. Its approximation ratio lies between the two baselines and is very close to off-line leverage score sampling, with occasional slight improvements over the off-line method for ℓ₁ regression (attributed to optimizer inaccuracies rather than the reduction algorithm itself).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its turnstile ℓ_p leverage score sampler against two baselines: (1) plain oblivious sketching from Munteanu et al. (ICLR 2023), and (2) plain off-line ℓ₁ leverage score sampling from Munteanu et al. (AISTATS 2022). The turnstile sampler decisively outperforms the oblivious sketching baseline by a large margin on all three datasets (Covertype, Webspam, KDDCup) for both logistic regression and ℓ₁ regression, confirming that the post-processing 'cherry-picking' step from the sketch is crucial for achieving near-optimal approximation ratios. Against the off-line leverage score sampling baseline, the turnstile sampler performs very closely, with approximation ratios nearly matching the off-line method across all tested reduced sizes. In a few ℓ₁ regression settings, the turnstile sampler even slightly outperforms the off-line method, though the authors attribute this to convergence limitations of the scipy optimizer rather than a genuine advantage of the reduction algorithm. Overall, the results confirm the paper's a priori hypothesis that a hybrid sketch-and-sample approach can approach off-line sampling quality while retaining the full flexibility of turnstile data stream updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the L_{2,2} turnstile sampler of Mahabadi et al. (2020) to all p ∈ [1,2] by replacing p-specific auxiliary sketches (AMS for p=2, Cauchy for p=1) with a unified statistical test based on percentiles of CountSketch repetitions. It then combines this with ℓ_p subspace embeddings (well-conditioned bases à la Dasgupta et al., 2009) to simulate offline ℓ_p leverage score sampling in the turnstile setting. The key conceptual departure from prior turnstile sketching work is that the oblivious sketch serves only as an intermediate data structure; a postprocessing stage extracts an approximate coreset, thereby violating the pure-obliviousness assumption under which exponential lower bounds (Li et al., 2021; Wang & Woodruff, 2022) were proved.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This yields the first polynomial-size turnstile streaming algorithm for logistic regression with a (1+ε) approximation, improving over the exp(1/ε) dependence of prior work and the O(1) factor of oblivious sketches. The framework is broadly applicable: any offline ℓ_p leverage score coreset construction can be simulated with poly(d/ε)·log(n) overhead, covering linear, ReLU, probit, and logistic regression. A key open question is whether ℓ_p spanning sets (which would reduce the d-dependence to near-optimal linear) can be constructed in turnstile streams, as current methods require reweighting that is incompatible with the post right-multiplication structure of turnstile sketches.
