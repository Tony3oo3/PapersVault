## Problem

Precipitation nowcasting must simultaneously capture mesoscale system evolution (highly deterministic, slow migration) and small-scale convective patterns (stochastic, minute-scale), yet deterministic models blur local details while probabilistic models degrade large-scale accuracy. Additionally, extreme precipitation events occur at kilometer scale with lifetimes of tens of minutes, demanding high-resolution forecasting that existing low-resolution diffusion-based methods cannot provide.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240633/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of historical radar echo frames (e.g., 13 frames at 384×384 px, 1 km resolution, 5-min interval for SEVIR) and produces a probabilistic ensemble of 10 future radar echo sequences (e.g., 12 frames covering 60 minutes). The deterministic stage outputs a single blurry prediction in pixel space, which is then encoded into a low-dimensional latent representation alongside the input context to condition the diffusion generator. All generation and denoising occur in the latent space of a frame-wise autoencoder before decoding back to pixel resolution.

## Method

CasCast decouples nowcasting into a cascaded pipeline: a deterministic backbone (EarthFormer or SimVP) predicts the mesoscale precipitation distribution via MSE loss, and a frame-wise guided diffusion transformer (CasFormer) then generates small-scale patterns conditioned on both the blurry prediction and the input context in a compressed latent space. CasFormer performs frame-wise encoding—splitting the latent noisy vector and the encoded blurry prediction into per-frame inputs, concatenating them along the channel dimension, and processing each through patch embedding plus L/2 diffusion attention blocks—followed by sequence-wise decoding via a sequence aggregator (MLP over concatenated frame features) with cross-attention to the encoded observations. This frame-wise guidance enforces a one-to-one correspondence between each blurry frame and its target latent vector, reducing denoising complexity and improving optimization for extreme events. The entire pipeline is trained at native 1 km resolution (unlike prior diffusion-based nowcasters limited to 128 px), using a linear noise schedule with 1000 training steps, DDIM 20-step sampling, and classifier-free guidance, with the autoencoder trained separately using pixel-wise and adversarial losses.

## Results

On the SEVIR dataset (1 km resolution, 384×384), CasCast achieves the lowest CRPS (0.0202, tied with PreDiff), the highest HSS (0.5602), and the best CSI-M-POOL16 (0.5225) among all compared models. Most notably, CasCast achieves a CSI-219-POOL16 of 0.2841 for regional extreme precipitation, surpassing the best deterministic baseline EarthFormer (0.1481) by +91.8% and the diffusion-based PreDiff (0.1213) by +134%. On HKO-7 and MeteoNet, CasCast similarly leads on CRPS (0.0205 and 0.0180) and CSI-POOL16 across all thresholds, confirming generalization across radar networks and resolutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CasCast outperforms all five deterministic baselines (ConvLSTM, PredRNN, PhyDNet, SimVP, EarthFormer) on every reported metric, with the largest gap in extreme-precipitation CSI-219-POOL16: +91.8% over EarthFormer (0.2841 vs 0.1481) and +48.8% over PredRNN (0.2841 vs 0.1909). Against the GAN-based NowcastNet, CasCast achieves a lower CRPS (0.0202 vs 0.0283) and higher HSS (0.5602 vs 0.5365), while matching NowcastNet's CSI-219-POOL16 (0.2841 vs 0.2700) without GAN training instability. Compared to the diffusion-based PreDiff (the closest competitor), CasCast ties on CRPS (0.0202) but substantially improves HSS (+14.1%), CSI-M-POOL16 (+25.7%), and CSI-219-POOL16 (+134%), which the authors attribute to generating at native 1 km resolution rather than upscaling from 128×128. The LDM baseline, also trained at reduced resolution, is clearly outperformed on all metrics. On HKO-7 and MeteoNet, CasCast (with SimVP as the deterministic backbone) again leads on CRPS and CSI-POOL16, confirming that the cascaded design generalizes across different radar networks, resolutions, and geographic regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

CasCast builds on two key lines of work: latent diffusion models (Rombach et al., 2022) for compressing high-dimensional radar data into a low-dimensional latent space, and the DiT transformer architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023) for the denoising backbone. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior precipitation nowcasting by introducing a cascaded decomposition—first predicting the deterministic mesoscale motion, then conditionally generating small-scale stochastic patterns via diffusion—rather than treating the entire forecast as either a single deterministic regression or a fully probabilistic generation. This positions CasCast between the deterministic models (EarthFormer, SimVP) and the fully probabilistic diffusion models (PreDiff, LDM), using the former's output as a conditioning signal for the latter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The +91.8% improvement in regional extreme-precipitation CSI (threshold 219, 16-km pooling) over the deterministic baseline is practically significant for disaster warning systems that depend on detecting convective-scale extreme events. A key limitation is that CasCast requires retraining on each new radar dataset, hindering cross-region deployment; the authors identify unified multi-dataset models (e.g., ClimaX) as a natural next step. The cascaded deterministic-then-generative paradigm is also a general architectural pattern that could transfer to other multi-scale spatiotemporal prediction problems beyond radar nowcasting.
