## Problem

Despite LLMs containing billions of parameters, it remains an open question whether they genuinely generalize beyond their training corpora or merely memorize them. Non-vacuous generalization bounds for LLM pretraining have been elusive because (i) autoregressive token predictions violate the independence assumption, (ii) the bits-per-dimension loss is unbounded, invalidating standard PAC-Bayes bounds, and (iii) LLMs have orders of magnitude more parameters than the image classifiers for which compression bounds already exist.

## Input / Output

The method takes a GPT-2-style transformer architecture (124M to 849M parameters) and pretrains it from scratch on the OpenWebText corpus (9B+ tokens) through a compressed parameterization defined by subspace dimension d, LoRA rank r, and prediction smoothing weight α. Rather than evaluating the empirical risk over the full training set, the bound is computed on a random subsample of n=10,000 documents, reducing computation from ~3 days on 8 GPUs to 45 minutes on a single GPU. The output is a non-vacuous upper bound on expected BPD and Top-k token error, along with a quantized, arithmetically-coded bitstream representing the compressed model.

## Method

The core technical contribution is SubLoRA, a nonlinear parameterization that composes LoRA low-rank updates (applied to self-attention and final linear layers) with a Kronecker-product random subspace projection, yielding θ = θ₀ + LoRA(Pw) where only the low-dimensional vector w must be specified. To handle the unbounded NLL objective, the paper introduces prediction smoothing: the model's token distribution is mixed with a uniform distribution over the vocabulary (weight α), which provably bounds BPD within an interval of size Δ = log₂(1+(1−α)V/α), enabling the standard finite-hypothesis compression bound. A further subsampling bound (Equation 3) accounts for evaluating empirical risk on only n ≪ m documents, splitting the confidence budget between the full-dataset and subsample Hoeffding terms. The compressed model is encoded via aggressive learned quantization and arithmetic coding, with the total bit count (weights + hyperparameters d, r, α, learning rate, quantization levels) serving as the Kolmogorov complexity upper bound in the generalization guarantee.

## Results

On GPT-2 small (124M parameters) pretrained on OpenWebText, SubLoRA achieves the first non-vacuous generalization bounds for LLMs: a BPD bound of 12.12 (vs. 15.62 for random guess), a Top-1 error bound of 96.41%, and a Top-100 error bound of 58.34%. Scaling to 849M parameters, the BPD bound improves to 12.01, demonstrating that larger models yield tighter generalization guarantees. The subsampling-based bound computation reduces evaluation time from 3 days on 8 GPUs to 45 minutes on a single GPU for the 9-billion-token OpenWebText dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SubLoRA (combining LoRA + subspace compression) strictly outperforms both LoRA-only and subspace-only baselines on the BPD bound (12.12 vs. 13.09 and 14.59 respectively) and the Top-100 error bound (58.34% vs. 67.26% and 75.95%). LoRA-only produces vacuous Top-1 error bounds (100%), while SubLoRA achieves a non-vacuous 96.41%; subspace-only is marginally better on Top-1 (96.52%) but far worse on BPD. The original uncompressed GPT-2 model achieves the best validation BPD (4.35) but yields completely vacuous bounds (70.76 BPD, 100% Top-1) due to its large parameter count. A key finding is that scaling from 124M to 849M parameters improves the BPD bound from 12.12 to 12.01, showing larger models are more compressible and generalize better, contrary to naive complexity-based expectations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PAC-Bayes compression bound framework (Catoni, 2007; Zhou et al., 2019; Lotfi et al., 2022) from image classification with bounded losses to the fundamentally different setting of LLM pretraining, where the NLL loss is unbounded, tokens are autoregressively dependent, and parameter counts are orders of magnitude larger. The key algorithmic contribution, SubLoRA, combines LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) with the random subspace projection from Lotfi et al. (2022) into a single nonlinear parameterization, whereas prior work used these techniques in isolation for different purposes (efficient fine-tuning vs. compression bounds). The paper also introduces prediction smoothing to convert the unbounded BPD loss into a bounded one, and a subsampling bound to make computation tractable on billion-token corpora—neither of which had a direct analogue in the image classification literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first mathematical proof that LLMs generalize beyond their training data rather than merely memorizing it, addressing a central open question in the field. The counterintuitive finding that larger models achieve tighter bounds (up to 849M parameters) challenges the naive expectation that growing hypothesis spaces should worsen generalization guarantees, and provides a theoretical explanation for the empirical scaling benefits of LLMs. Key limitations include the requirement to train from scratch through SubLoRA (bounds cannot be computed for arbitrary pretrained models), the poor text generation quality of compressed models, and the fact that the largest models tested are still far below the 7B+ scale of modern LLMs. Open questions include whether the non-IID token structure within documents can be further exploited, whether bounds can be retrofitted to already-trained models, and whether the scale trend persists at truly massive parameter counts.
