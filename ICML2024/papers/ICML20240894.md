## Problem

Existing semantic ID learning methods rely on a two-stage pipeline (off-the-shelf text encoder followed by rq-VAE or hierarchical clustering), which introduces information loss at each step and suffers from a distribution mismatch between the uniform encoder embedding space and the hierarchical structure required for effective semantic indexing. Learning semantic IDs end-to-end is non-trivial because IDs are discrete and sequentially structured, and no ground-truth (document, semantic ID) supervision is available.

## Input / Output

The semantic indexer takes a full document (or query, or concatenated user interaction history) as input and autoregressively generates a sequence of T=3 discrete semantic IDs, where each position is drawn from a position-specific codebook of size 512, 5120, or 51200 depending on corpus scale. The first ID position encodes coarse-grained semantics while later positions refine to finer details. For downstream tasks, the model is fine-tuned to generate the target item or document ID given a text input, with constrained beam search over a prefix tree ensuring only valid IDs are produced.

## Method

LMINDEXER uses a T5-base encoder-decoder as the semantic indexer, where the encoder processes the document and the decoder autoregressively produces continuous hidden states at each ID position, which are mapped to discrete IDs via dot-product lookup against position-specific codebook embedding matrices. A shallow 1-layer Transformer reconstructor reconstructs masked document tokens from the semantic ID representations, providing the self-supervised training signal that replaces missing (document, ID) supervision. Training proceeds progressively: at each position t, hints are gradually reduced to force new IDs to carry new information, a contrastive loss pushes documents sharing the same prefix apart in the current latent space to improve codebook utilization, and a commitment loss prevents catastrophic forgetting of previously learned IDs. A straight-through estimator (stop-gradient) enables gradients to flow through the discrete argmax codebook lookup back to the semantic encoder. Two warm-up phases—reconstructor warm-up (fixing the encoder) and encoder/codebook warm-up (training without the new codebook, then initializing it via KMeans)—prevent reconstructor collapse and posterior collapse respectively.

## Results

On sequential recommendation (Table 3), LMINDEXER achieves Recall@5 of 0.0415, 0.0222, and 0.0404 on Amazon-Beauty, Sports, and Toys respectively, outperforming the best non-generative baseline HGN (0.0325, 0.0189, 0.0321) by 28–41% relative. On document retrieval (Table 6), LMINDEXER reaches Recall@1 of 0.6631 and Recall@10 of 0.8589 on NQ320k, and NDCG@10 of 0.4695 on TREC-DL 1M, surpassing DPR (0.5360/0.8300 and 0.3941) and rq-VAE indexer (0.6480 and 0.4579). On product search (Table 4), LMINDEXER attains NDCG@5 of 0.3187, 0.2870, and 0.2865 across the three Amazon domains, beating DPR (0.2565, 0.2556, 0.2805) and all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LMINDEXER consistently outperforms both two-stage semantic ID baselines (rq-VAE indexer and HC indexer) across all three downstream tasks and all five datasets, with margins of 2–3× in recommendation Recall@5 (e.g., 0.0415 vs. 0.0136 on Beauty) and 5–15% absolute in document retrieval Recall@10. Against traditional sequential recommendation models (HGN, GRU4Rec, BERT4Rec, FDSA), LMINDEXER achieves the highest Recall@5 and NDCG@5 on every Amazon domain, beating the strongest non-generative baseline HGN by 28–41% relative. On document retrieval, LMINDEXER surpasses DPR on NQ320k (Recall@1: 0.6631 vs. 0.5360) and TREC-DL 1M (NDCG@10: 0.4695 vs. 0.3941), but DPR retains a slight edge on the larger MACRO 1M corpus (MRR@10: 0.5561 vs. 0.5485). LMINDEXER also outperforms SEAL on product search (NDCG@5: 0.3187 vs. 0.1271 on Beauty) and document retrieval (Recall@1: 0.6631 vs. 0.5990 on NQ), while being substantially faster at inference (12.21s vs. 21 min for SEAL on the full Beauty test set). BM25 is outperformed on all retrieval tasks, confirming that learned semantic IDs capture richer semantics than bag-of-words matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm for semantic ID learning (off-the-shelf text encoder → rq-VAE or hierarchical clustering) by formulating the problem as an end-to-end sequence-to-sequence task with a generative language model (T5-base). It builds on the VQ-VAE codebook mechanism (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) and the straight-through estimator (Jang et al., 2016) for discrete ID assignment, borrows the masked-token-prediction-with-hints reconstruction objective from RetroMAE (Xiao et al., 2022), and adopts the progressive training schedule from Sun et al. (2023). The key conceptual departure is learning semantic representations and hierarchical structure simultaneously within a single model, rather than introducing information loss at each pipeline stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical, self-supervised path to high-quality semantic IDs that transfer across recommendation, product search, and document retrieval without task-specific ID design, which is valuable for generative retrieval systems. A notable design insight is that a deliberately weak (1-layer) reconstructor yields better semantic IDs than stronger ones, since it forces the indexer to carry more semantic information rather than letting the reconstructor absorb it. Open questions include scalability to very large corpora (DPR still wins on MACRO-1M), whether the approach extends to multimodal documents, and how the method behaves with longer ID sequences or larger codebooks in production settings.
