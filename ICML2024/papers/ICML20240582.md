## Problem

Existing federated compositional optimization algorithms are limited to two-level problems and fail to achieve linear speedup with respect to the number of workers under heterogeneous (non-IID) data distributions. The paper identifies that locally computed Jacobian matrices are the primary obstacle: their heterogeneity introduces an irreducible additive term in the convergence bound, preventing convergence to the true optimum. No prior federated algorithm achieves both level-independent convergence and linear speedup for multi-level compositional problems under heterogeneity.

## Input / Output

Each worker n holds local stochastic functions F_n^{(k)}(·;ξ) for k=1,...,K, evaluated on mini-batches of size S. The algorithm communicates per-iteration the parameter vector x (dimension d_0), inner-level function estimators h^{(k)} (dimensions d_1,...,d_{K-1}), and Jacobian-vector product estimators v^{(k)} (dimensions d_0,...,d_{K-1}) every p iterations, rather than full Jacobian matrices of size d_k×d_{k-1}. In the experimental setting (risk-averse portfolio optimization), the input is tabular asset payoff data r_t ∈ R^d over T time steps, and the output is the optimal portfolio allocation vector x ∈ R^d.

## Method

Fed-DR-SCGD introduces a recursively defined Jacobian-vector product Ψ^{(k)}(x) = ∇F^{(k)}(h^{(k-1)})^T Ψ^{(k+1)}(x) computed from the last level K down to level 1, replacing the need to communicate full Jacobian matrices. Both the inner-level function estimators h^{(k)} and the Jacobian-vector product estimators v^{(k)} are updated via momentum-based variance reduction (the (1−αη²) recursive correction à la Cutkosky & Orabona), with h updated bottom-up and v updated top-down — hence 'doubly recursive.' At every p iterations, workers synchronize x, all h^{(k)}, and all v^{(k)} with the server, and the projection operator Π_{C_{Ψ^{(k)}}} keeps v^{(k)} bounded. The key theoretical challenge is that the Jacobian-vector product's estimation error at level k depends on the estimator at level k+1, creating recursive cross-level coupling in the potential function; the authors resolve this by explicitly tracking inter-level coefficient dependencies and constructing a multi-term Lyapunov potential that cancels these coupled terms, yielding the O(1/(N^{2/3} T^{2/3})) rate.

## Results

The paper's headline theoretical result is a convergence rate of O(1/(N^{2/3} T^{2/3})) for nonconvex multi-level compositional optimization, which is level-independent in K and achieves linear speedup in the number of workers N. On three financial datasets (Book-to-Market, Operating Profitability, Investment; each with T=13,781 time steps and d=100 assets), Fed-DR-SCGD is substantially more communication-efficient than all baselines, requiring far fewer megabytes to reach the same loss. The linear speedup is empirically confirmed: with 16 workers, the algorithm reaches a target loss in less wall-clock time than with 8 workers, using identical hyperparameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fed-DR-SCGD is compared against three single-machine multi-level compositional optimization baselines (NLASG, SCSC, SMVR) that are parallelized by communicating their inner-level function estimators, Jacobian matrices, and variables at the same frequency. Against NLASG and SCSC, Fed-DR-SCGD converges significantly faster in both epochs and communication cost, owing to its variance-reduced gradient estimator. Against SMVR (which also uses variance reduction), Fed-DR-SCGD achieves comparable convergence speed in epochs but is dramatically more communication-efficient, because it communicates only Jacobian-vector products (dimension d_{k-1}) rather than full Jacobian matrices (dimension d_k × d_{k-1}), which is critical when d is large (d=100 in the experiments). The linear speedup property (16 workers faster than 8 in wall-clock time) is unique to Fed-DR-SCGD among the compared methods, as the baselines do not achieve linear speedup under heterogeneous data distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of federated compositional optimization from two-level to multi-level problems under heterogeneous data distributions. It builds directly on the momentum-based variance reduction framework of Cutkosky & Orabona (2019) and the multi-level nested variance reduction structures of Zhang & Xiao (2021) and Jiang et al. (2022), but departs from them by applying variance reduction to the Jacobian-vector product rather than the full Jacobian matrix. The key conceptual departure from prior federated compositional methods (Gao et al. 2022, Tarzanagh et al. 2022, Huang 2022) is the identification of Jacobian heterogeneity—rather than merely function heterogeneity—as the root cause of the failure to achieve linear speedup, and the design of a doubly recursive estimator that communicates only Jacobian-vector products to mitigate this issue at reduced communication cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first federated algorithm to simultaneously achieve level-independent convergence and linear speedup for multi-level compositional optimization under heterogeneous settings, filling a clear gap between single-machine multi-level methods and federated two-level methods. The Jacobian-vector product estimator offers a practical communication efficiency gain over full Jacobian communication, which is important for real-world federated deployments. However, the experimental validation is limited to a three-level portfolio optimization problem with only 8–16 workers on tabular data, leaving open whether the approach scales to deeper compositions, larger worker counts, or more complex nonconvex landscapes. Open questions include whether the doubly recursive structure can be simplified, whether partial worker participation is compatible, and whether the theory extends to non-smooth or strongly convex objectives.
