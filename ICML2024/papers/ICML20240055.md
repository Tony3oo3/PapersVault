## Problem

Hypergraph models capture higher-order relationships (group membership, categorical features) that graph-based semi-supervised learning cannot, but their practical deployment has been blocked by the non-linearity of the hypergraph Laplacian. Existing algorithms either lack convergence guarantees (Hein et al., 2013), require super-quadratic time in the hypergraph size (Li et al., 2020), or rely on generic convex solvers with high polynomial dependencies. This paper provides the first fast, scalable first-order algorithms for hypergraph Laplacian systems and hypergraph PageRank with strong multiplicative-error guarantees over a general family of hypergraph potentials.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a weighted hypergraph G=(V,H,w), a seed vector s encoding partial labels, a regularization parameter λ, and a diagonal matrix M (typically the degree matrix D). It requires only a subgradient oracle for the hypergraph potential U and a prox step (solving a linear system in the preconditioner R) per iteration. The output is an ε-multiplicative approximation x̂ to the optimal solution of the variational problem, which corresponds to a hypergraph personalized PageRank vector; label predictions for unlabeled points are obtained by argmax over per-class solution vectors. The multiplicative error formulation (rather than additive) is essential because the problem is homogeneous: scaling s scales the optimal solution proportionally.

## Method

Algorithm 1 is a modified subgradient descent for minimizing F(x)−⟨s,x⟩ where F is convex and 2-positively homogeneous. The key technical insight is a novel analysis step (Corollary 4.3) that bounds the quadratic error term ‖z_t‖²_{R⁻¹} directly in terms of the suboptimality gap F(x̂_t), exploiting 2-positive homogeneity, rather than summing over iterations as in classical mirror descent—this yields a multiplicative error guarantee at rate O(u_R/(ℓ_R·ε²)) iterations. For hypergraph Laplacian systems with M=D, the update reduces to a Richardson-iteration-like step x̂_{t+1} = x̂_t − (ε/2)·D⁻¹(λDx_t + L(x_t) − s) with linear time per iteration, giving O((1+λ)/((λ_G+λ)ε²)) total iterations. For the U_∞ potential on bounded-rank hypergraphs, a clique-expansion graph Laplacian preconditioner eliminates dependence on the Poincaré constant λ_G, yielding O(max|h|/ε²) iterations and matching Spielman–Teng's near-linear-time regime for graphs. The framework applies uniformly to all convex 2-positively homogeneous hypergraph potentials, including directed models and submodular-cut-derived potentials, without algorithmic modification.

## Results

On the semi-supervised multi-class classification benchmark (Zoo, Mushroom, Covertype, 20 Newsgroups), the proposed algorithm achieves the same classification accuracy as prior methods while requiring only one-tenth of the running time — a tenfold speed-up. In the manifold learning experiments (concentric hyperspheres and hyperrectangles), hypergraph PPR outperforms graph PPR in AUC, with the gap widening as ambient dimension increases from 2 to 7+ dimensions. The weighted hypergraph potential U^(P) further improves over the unweighted U_∞ potential, particularly in higher dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior hypergraph Laplacian solvers: the primal-dual hybrid gradient (PDHG) method of Hein et al. (2013) and the Quadratic Decomposable Submodular Function Minimization (QDSFM) methods of Li et al. (2020). On the categorical clustering benchmark, the proposed algorithm matches the classification accuracy of both baselines while achieving a tenfold reduction in total running time, owing to the simplicity of each iteration (second-best average time per iteration). The Subgradient QDSFM variant from Li et al. (2020) has the fastest per-iteration cost but requires many more iterations and fails to converge to a minimizer in many instances. In the manifold learning setting, the hypergraph PPR (using the proposed algorithm) consistently outperforms the graph PPR baseline in AUC, with the advantage growing robustly with dimension — a regime where graph Laplacian methods are known to suffer from the curse of dimensionality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Richardson iteration / gradient descent framework for graph Laplacian systems (Spielman, 2018) to the non-linear setting of hypergraph Laplacian systems, where the operator is a set-valued subdifferential rather than a linear matrix. The proof technique draws on optimistic mirror descent (Rakhlin and Sridharan, 2013), while the class of hypergraph potentials is unified under the convex 2-positively homogeneous framework that encompasses submodular cut functions studied by Yoshida (2019) and Li and Milenkovic (2018). The key departure from prior work (Hein et al., 2013; Li et al., 2020) is providing a first-order method with explicit convergence guarantees for a general family of potentials, rather than being tailored to the U_∞ potential or requiring super-quadratic time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10x speedup and the generality of the algorithm (applicable to all convex 2-positively homogeneous potentials without modification) make hypergraph models practically deployable at scale for semi-supervised learning and ranking tasks. The empirical finding that hypergraph PPR outperforms graph PPR in high-dimensional manifold learning is notable given the known curse of dimensionality for graph Laplacians, though the evidence is preliminary. Open questions include whether nearly-linear-time solvers exist for general (unbounded-rank) hypergraphs, whether the O(1/ε²) convergence can be accelerated to O(1/ε), and whether the weighted hypergraph potentials converge to continuous manifold operators analogous to the graph Laplace-Beltrami operator.
