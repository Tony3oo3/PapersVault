## Problem

Existing clustered federated learning (CFL) methods for mutual transfer learning lack theoretical guarantees for learnability structure recovery and require tedious hyperparameter tuning, while centralized methods like DiffS rely on a fixed clustering threshold that cannot adapt to concept drifts in longitudinal or streaming data. No prior work simultaneously estimates the unknown domain subgroup structure and the model parameters within a federated framework while remaining robust to distributional shifts over time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242276/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of M clients holds a local dataset of n_i samples with global features X_i ∈ R^{n_i×p}, heterogeneous features Z_i ∈ R^{n_i×q}, and response y_i, modeled as a two-layer linear mixed-effects model with known noise variances σ_u² and σ_ε². The server outputs the recovered learnability structure S (a partition of domains into K subgroups), the shared global parameter β, and subgroup-specific heterogeneous parameters α_k, all updated over R communication rounds per time step τ. Only parameter updates (β_i^r, θ_i^r) are transmitted between clients and server; raw data never leaves the client.

## Method

AdaGrP combines an Adaptive Threshold Correction (Algorithm 2) with a Group Personalization aggregation scheme. At each communication round, clients perform local gradient updates on their mixed-effects loss, then the server computes standardized domain distances Δ_ij^r between updated heterogeneous parameters. The key novelty is a nonparametric k-nearest-neighbor density estimation procedure that searches for the threshold λ̃ where the estimated PDF of Δ crosses 2p_χ·χ²_q(λ̃), effectively locating the oracle threshold λ* without any hyperparameter tuning. This corrected threshold feeds into a complete-linkage clustering algorithm (Algorithm 1) to recover the learnability structure S^r. Aggregation then proceeds in a mixed manner: β is updated via standard FedAvg weighting, while α_k is aggregated only within each recovered subgroup. Theoretical analysis proves perfect learnability structure recovery in every round under a relaxed condition (Condition 3) that only requires sufficient local steps to bound the distance estimation error, and the method is shown to track drifting concepts across time steps.

## Results

On synthetic data (base case M=50, p=q=10, n=200, K=3), AdaGrP achieves 96.32% average learnability structure recovery accuracy (NMI) compared to 91.52% for the best CFL baseline, representing a 16.9% relative improvement. Under concept drift (changing learnability structure and varying noise levels), AdaGrP remains largely unaffected while baselines degrade significantly; FedDrift in particular fails to recover the true structure, estimating cluster counts ranging from single digits to nearly M. On the real-world NOAA nClimDiv dataset (125 years, 125 climate divisions, 5 time steps), AdaGrP recovers a learnability structure closely matching IECC climate zones, while AdaGrP (w/o) produces unbalanced, hard-to-interpret clusters and FedDrift merges all subgroups into one.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaGrP outperforms all three baselines (IFCA, FeSEM, FedDrift) on NMI, RMSE, and prediction error across both synthetic and real-world experiments. IFCA and FeSEM, despite benefiting from oracle knowledge of the true number of clusters K, still perform poorly in many settings due to their lack of theoretical guarantee for learnability structure recovery, and they degrade further under concept drift (τ=2,3). FedDrift, the SOTA method for concept drift in FL, is the weakest baseline: it requires extensive hyperparameter tuning (only δ=100 out of {0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100} yields reasonable results), estimates wildly varying cluster counts (from 1 to ~M), and ultimately merges all subgroups in the NOAA experiment. The ablation AdaGrP (w/o) with fixed threshold F_q^{-1}(0.99) is slightly worse than full AdaGrP, confirming that the adaptive threshold correction is the key differentiator, especially under varying noise levels where the fixed threshold becomes miscalibrated.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaGrP sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: centralized mutual transfer learning (specifically DiffS, which recovers learnability structure via complete-linkage clustering on standardized domain distances with a fixed threshold) and clustered federated learning (CFL methods like IFCA and FeSEM that partition clients into subgroups but are model-agnostic and lack recovery guarantees). The paper departs from DiffS by replacing its fixed threshold F_q^{-1}(0.99) with a nonparametric adaptive threshold correction, and from CFL by embedding the specific linear mixed-effects statistical structure of mutual transfer learning into the FL aggregation loop. The group personalization aggregation strategy is directly adapted from Liu et al. (2022b), where clients contribute updates only to their own subgroup.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: mutual transfer learning has been studied only in centralized settings, while CFL methods handle clustering in FL without the statistical structure or theoretical guarantees needed for learnability structure recovery. The tuning-free property (no hyperparameter search for the clustering threshold) is practically valuable for longitudinal applications where distributions shift over time. However, the method is tightly coupled to the linear mixed-effects model and assumes noise parameters σ_u, σ_ε are known a priori, limiting immediate applicability to non-linear or unknown-variance settings. Open questions include whether the adaptive threshold correction extends to nonparametric or deep model families, and whether the theoretical conditions (Condition 3) hold in high-dimensional or highly heterogeneous federated deployments.
