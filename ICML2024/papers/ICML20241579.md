## Problem

Frontier LLMs like GPT-4 fail on complex chemistry problems that require compositional reasoning over multiple interacting concepts, precise calculation, and multi-step derivations. The paper identifies that these failures stem not from missing domain knowledge but from the absence of a structured reasoning process that elicits relevant knowledge, grounds step-by-step derivations on it, and iteratively refines results. Prior work on LLMs for chemistry targeted simple single-hop retrieval tasks (molecule classification, property prediction), leaving complex multi-step scientific reasoning largely unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-response college-level chemistry problem (text) as input and produces a structured multi-part output: a list of relevant chemical formulae with variable explanations, a step-by-step reasoning process encoded as executable Python code (PoT), per-component confidence scores in [0,1], and a final numerical answer formatted to three decimal places. The reasoning is decomposed into an explicit formulae-retrieval phase followed by a calculation phase, and the iterative refinement loop can run up to n iterations, each producing revised formulae and reasoning with updated confidence scores before the final answer is generated.

## Method

STRUCTCHEM is a three-phase structured prompting strategy: (1) formulae generation, where the LLM is instructed to identify all explicitly and implicitly required chemical formulae with variable annotations, providing a grounded knowledge base; (2) step-by-step reasoning via Program-of-Thoughts (PoT), where the LLM translates the derivation into Python code with annotation lines for reasoning, ensuring precise calculation; (3) confidence-based review-and-refinement, an iterative loop where the same LLM re-examines the formulae and reasoning, assigns a confidence score in [0,1] to each revision, and only accepts a revision if its confidence exceeds the running maximum. This confidence-gated acceptance mechanism distinguishes STRUCTCHEM from prior self-verification approaches by preventing correct intermediate results from being overwritten by lower-confidence (potentially incorrect) revisions. The full pipeline is instantiated with GPT-4 or GPT-3.5 as the backbone, and the generated reasoning traces are also used to fine-tune smaller models (Llama-2-13B, Vicuna-13B) via LoRA.

## Results

On four college-level chemistry datasets (quan, chemmc, atkins, matter) from SciBench, STRUCTCHEM with GPT-4 achieves 47.64% average accuracy in the few-shot setting, surpassing the strongest baseline CoT-SC (39.93%) by 7.71 points and PoT (32.22%) by 15.42 points. In the zero-shot setting with GPT-4, STRUCTCHEM reaches 30.11% average accuracy versus 26.69% for CoT. With GPT-3.5 in few-shot, STRUCTCHEM achieves 31.66% average accuracy, a 13.52-point gain over CoT-SC (18.14%), demonstrating that the structured prompting strategy is effective across model scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STRUCTCHEM outperforms all five baselines across both zero-shot and few-shot settings with both GPT-3.5 and GPT-4. Against CoT-SC (the strongest baseline at 39.93% avg with GPT-4 few-shot), STRUCTCHEM gains 7.71 points, with the largest per-dataset margin on atkins (+9.34) and matter (+2.04). Against ToT (30.06%), the gap is 17.58 points, and the paper notes ToT underperforms because it excels at many-step problems but struggles with the moderate complexity (3–5 reasoning steps) of these chemistry tasks. Against PoT (32.22%), STRUCTCHEM leads by 15.42 points, suggesting that structured formulae generation plus confidence-based refinement adds substantial value beyond merely translating reasoning into Python code. Against the SciBench System Instruction baseline (19.13%), STRUCTCHEM improves by 28.51 points, and against plain CoT (27.85%), by 19.79 points. Notably, STRUCTCHEM's advantage is larger with GPT-3.5 (13.52 pts over CoT-SC) than with GPT-4 (7.71 pts), indicating the structured prompting compensates more for weaker models' instruction-following limitations.

## Relation to Prior Work

STRUCTCHEM builds on the chain-of-thought paradigm (Wei et al., 2022) and Program-of-Thoughts (Chen et al., 2023a) for its step-by-step reasoning phase, but departs from both by explicitly separating a domain-specific formulae-retrieval phase before any reasoning begins. It also diverges from self-consistency and self-verification approaches (Wang et al., 2023b; Weng et al., 2022) by introducing confidence-scored iterative refinement that prevents correct intermediate results from being overwritten by unfaithful revisions. Conceptually, it aligns with modular/decomposed prompting (Khot et al., 2023; Patel et al., 2022) but identifies a chemistry-specific macro decomposition—formulae then reasoning—rather than task-specific micro-decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the primary bottleneck for LLMs on complex science problems is not missing domain knowledge but the absence of a structured reasoning scaffold, a finding that generalizes beyond chemistry to any domain requiring precise grounded derivation. However, absolute accuracy remains modest (best ~60% on a single dataset), and reasoning errors still account for ~35% of failures, indicating that compositional multi-step reasoning remains a fundamental limitation. Open questions include whether incorporating external retrieval for formulae verification, or distilling the structured reasoning into smaller models, can close the remaining gap.
