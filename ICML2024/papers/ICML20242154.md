## Problem

Multitask-like multimodal frameworks that add unimodal losses alongside a multimodal joint loss suffer from a previously ignored gradient conflict in the shared unimodal encoders. The paper further shows that the conventional Pareto integration, which is the natural remedy for such conflicts, actually fails in multimodal settings because the easier-to-learn multimodal loss has smaller gradient magnitude and covariance, causing Pareto to overweight it, reduce SGD noise strength, and drive the model to sharper minima that generalize worse.

## Input / Output

The method operates at the gradient level during training: for each unimodal encoder (audio, video, image, or text), it receives the separately computed multimodal joint gradient and unimodal gradient, integrates them, and uses the result to update that encoder's parameters. Inputs span diverse modality pairs and triples (audio+video clips, colored+grayscale images, front+back 2D views of 3D objects, audio+video+text), and the model outputs a single class label per sample. The approach is backbone-agnostic, validated on both ResNet-18 CNNs with late fusion and MBT Transformers with dense cross-modal interaction layers.

## Method

MMPareto is a gradient integration algorithm that separately controls direction and magnitude to provide 'innocent unimodal assistance.' In the non-conflict case (cos β ≥ 0), it assigns uniform weights (2α^m = 2α^u = 1) to preserve the full SGD noise covariance. In the conflict case (cos β < 0), it first solves the standard Pareto optimization to obtain a common descent direction, then rescales the integrated gradient's magnitude to match the uniform-baseline norm (guaranteeing λ > 1 because Pareto overweights the smaller multimodal gradient), and finally applies a rescale factor γ > 1 to further boost noise strength. The theoretical analysis proves that conventional Pareto reduces the SGD noise variance relative to the uniform baseline (var(ζ_t) < var(ε_t)) whenever the covariance ratio k = C^u/C^m exceeds a threshold, explaining its generalization failure. The algorithm is guaranteed to converge to a Pareto stationarity and requires no architectural changes—only a per-encoder gradient modification at each SGD step.

## Results

On CREMA-D, MMPareto achieves 75.13% multimodal accuracy, 65.46% audio accuracy, and 55.24% video accuracy, outperforming the uniform baseline (71.10%) by over 4 points. On Kinetics Sounds, it reaches 70.13% multimodal accuracy, 56.40% audio, and 53.05% video, surpassing the best competing method G-Blending (68.90%) by 1.23 points. Notably, MMPareto's unimodal performance even exceeds individually trained unimodal models (e.g., 65.46% audio vs. 61.69% audio-only on CREMA-D), a result rarely achieved in prior work. On the Transformer-based MBT backbone, MMPareto maintains its lead, achieving 48.66% accuracy on CREMA-D from scratch versus 46.38% for the best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMPareto outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the closest imbalanced-multimodal competitor G-Blending (Wang et al., 2020), MMPareto gains +3.12 points on CREMA-D (75.13 vs. 72.01) and +1.23 on Kinetics Sounds (70.13 vs. 68.90). The gradient-modulation methods OGM (Peng et al., 2022), Greedy (Wu et al., 2022), PMR (Fan et al., 2023), and AGM (Li et al., 2023) all underperform the simple uniform baseline on at least one dataset, while MMPareto consistently exceeds it by 3–4 points. In the multi-task comparison (Table 3), conventional Pareto (Sener & Koltun, 2018) is notably inferior to the uniform baseline (e.g., 62.00 vs. 75.68 on CG-MNIST), confirming the paper's theoretical analysis; MMPareto recovers and surpasses the baseline (81.88 on CG-MNIST, 89.95 on ModelNet40, 70.13 on Kinetics Sounds). Among multi-task gradient-balancing methods, PCGrad (Yu et al., 2020) and MetaBalance (He et al., 2022) are the strongest competitors, but MMPareto still leads by 2.53 points on CG-MNIST (81.88 vs. 79.35) and 1.02 on Kinetics Sounds (70.13 vs. 69.11). GradNorm (Chen et al., 2018) underperforms the uniform baseline on ModelNet40 and Kinetics Sounds, further validating that generic multi-task strategies fail under the magnitude/covariance asymmetry specific to multimodal learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines: (1) imbalanced multimodal learning, where methods like OGM, Greedy, and PMR add targeted unimodal objectives to counteract modality dominance, and (2) Pareto-based gradient integration for multi-task learning (Sener & Koltun, 2018). The key departure is identifying that the natural Pareto solution for gradient conflicts actually *fails* in the multimodal setting—performing worse than a naive uniform sum—due to a systematic magnitude and covariance asymmetry between the easier multimodal loss and the harder unimodal loss. MMPareto corrects this by decoupling direction (via Pareto) from magnitude (via rescaling to the uniform-baseline norm), a distinction absent from prior multi-task gradient surgery methods like PCGrad or GradNorm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded explanation for why a widely adopted multi-task technique degrades in multimodal scenarios, which is practically important given how common the 'add unimodal loss' recipe is in the literature. The fix is lightweight (a few lines of gradient rescaling) and plug-and-play across CNN and Transformer backbones, making it easy to adopt. Open questions include whether the convergence guarantees hold under less ideal assumptions, whether the covariance-based analysis can be extended to estimate and exploit cross-loss correlations, and whether the approach transfers to large-scale multimodal pre-training (e.g., BLIP-2-style objectives) where the difficulty gap between objectives may differ.
