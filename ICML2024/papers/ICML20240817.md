## Problem

Existing diffusion-based structure-based drug design methods generate 3D ligand molecules entirely from scratch, without any reference or template ligands, making the optimization landscape difficult to navigate and yielding suboptimal binding affinities. This paper addresses the gap by introducing external, target-aware ligand references with high predicted binding affinity to steer the diffusion generation process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240817/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target protein 3D structure (atom positions in R³ and one-hot element/amino-acid type indicators) and a fixed external database of 128 ligand molecules sampled from the training set. From this database, PMINet retrieves the top-k ligands with highest predicted binding affinity to the target as references. The output is a 3D ligand molecule specified by atom positions and types; the number of atoms is sampled from an empirical distribution and bonds are assigned via post-processing. The retrieval database is fixed identically during both training and testing.

## Method

IRDIFF introduces a pre-trained protein-molecule interaction network (PMINet) built from SE(3)-equivariant EGNN encoders and cross-attention layers, trained on PDBBind v2016 to predict binding affinity. Two augmentation mechanisms exploit PMINet's interaction features during the reverse diffusion process: (1) self augmentation, where PMINet computes interaction features between the current predicted molecule estimate and the protein to refine the generative trajectory (using teacher forcing with ground truth during training), and (2) retrieval augmentation, where PMINet extracts binding-aware interaction features between the protein and top-k reference ligands, which are then merged into the molecular and protein representations via cross-attention. The augmented SE(3)-invariant hidden states feed into a 9-layer equivariant Transformer operating on a k-nn graph of the protein-ligand complex to predict atom positions and types. Because PMINet's cross-attention layer discards relative spatial coordinates, no shortcut signal leaks during training, and the augmentation preserves SE(3)-equivariance of the reverse kernel.

## Results

On CrossDocked2020, IRDIFF (n=128, k=3) achieves a state-of-the-art average Vina Score of -6.03 and median of -6.89, surpassing the ground-truth average of -6.36. It attains 67.4% average High Affinity (fraction of generated molecules binding better than ground truth) and a QED of 0.53, demonstrating a strong trade-off between binding affinity and drug-likeness. The model also achieves the best Vina Dock score of -8.42 (avg) and outperforms all baselines on bond distance JSD metrics (e.g., 0.214 for aromatic C:C bonds vs. 0.251 for DecompDiff).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IRDIFF outperforms all five baselines on binding-related metrics. Against the strongest diffusion-based competitor DecompDiff, IRDIFF improves average Vina Score by 6.3% (-6.03 vs. -5.67) and median Vina Score by 14.1% (-6.89 vs. -6.04), while also achieving higher High Affinity (67.4% vs. 64.4%). Against the autoregressive Pocket2Mol, IRDIFF gains 17.3% on average Vina Score and 46.6% on median Vina Score, though Pocket2Mol retains a slight edge in QED (0.56 vs. 0.53) and SA (0.74 vs. 0.59). Compared to TargetDiff, IRDIFF leads on every binding metric (Vina Score, Vina Min, Vina Dock, High Affinity) while also matching or exceeding its QED (0.53 vs. 0.48). The older VAE-based LiGAN and autoregressive AR methods are substantially outperformed on all binding and property metrics, with IRDIFF achieving roughly 2× the High Affinity of LiGAN (67.4% vs. 21.1%). Notably, IRDIFF is the only method that simultaneously achieves the best binding scores and a QED above 0.50, resolving the binding-vs-property trade-off observed in prior work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SE(3)-equivariant diffusion framework for SBDD established by TargetDiff (Guan et al., 2023a), adopting its DDPM formulation, EGNN-based message passing, and training objective. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of retrieval-augmented generation—a paradigm well-established in NLP and computer vision—into 3D molecular generation, where external reference ligands with high predicted binding affinity steer the denoising process. The PMINet component repurposes EGNN and cross-attention architectures (Satorras et al., 2021; Vaswani et al., 2017) as a pretrained binding-affinity model that serves dual roles: a retriever for reference selection and an interaction feature extractor for augmentation. Unlike prior SBDD methods (AR, Pocket2Mol, TargetDiff, DecompDiff) that generate molecules entirely from scratch conditioned only on the protein pocket, IRDIFF explicitly conditions on target-aware molecular references, bridging the forward binding-affinity prediction problem with its inverse generation problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IRDIFF demonstrates that incorporating external reference ligands via retrieval and self augmentation can significantly improve binding affinity (−6.03 Avg. Vina Score) while maintaining drug-likeness (0.53 QED), addressing a persistent trade-off in SBDD where diffusion methods outperform autoregressive ones on binding but lag on molecular properties. The finding that even a sequence-level PMINet (without 3D geometric information) can effectively guide 3D equivariant generation suggests the approach could scale to self-supervised pretraining on large protein-ligand sequence corpora without labeled affinities. Limitations include reliance on a small fixed retrieval database (128 molecules), evaluation on a single dataset (CrossDocked2020), and the fact that SA scores remain below ground-truth levels. Open questions include whether larger or dynamically updated retrieval pools yield further gains, how the method behaves on proteins without any known bound ligands in the database, and whether the augmentation strategy generalizes to multi-target or fragment-based design settings.
