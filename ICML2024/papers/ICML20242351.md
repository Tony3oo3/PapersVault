## Problem

Optimizing the global contrastive loss requires sampling negative examples from a softmax distribution whose partition function is intractable for large datasets. Existing approaches either demand prohibitively large batch sizes (SimCLR), incur high memory and computation costs (Negative Cache), or only converge to a neighborhood of stationary points with non-vanishing error (SogCLR). No prior method achieves global convergence to a stationary point while remaining efficient at small batch sizes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242351/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (96×96 for STL-10, 224×224 for ImageNet-100) and produces fixed-dimensional feature embeddings (512-d for ResNet-18, 128-d for ResNet-50). Positive pairs are formed by two random augmentations of the same image, while negative samples are drawn from the entire augmented dataset. A key implementation detail is that EMC² maintains one Markov chain state (an integer index) per image in the dataset, requiring only m integers of memory rather than the m_neg·d feature vectors needed by Negative Cache.

## Method

EMC² replaces the intractable softmax sampling in the contrastive gradient with an online Metropolis-Hastings (M-H) chain whose accept/reject ratio is simply the ratio of two unnormalized exponentials, avoiding any partition function computation. At each SGD iteration, the M-H chain is advanced by R steps (with P burn-in steps discarded) using the current model parameters, and the resulting samples yield a biased but controlled gradient estimate. The convergence proof shows the θ-dependent transition kernel is both geometrically ergodic (mixing rate 1 − BR/(2mm_neg·exp(2c²β))) and Lipschitz in θ, allowing the application of the biased stochastic approximation framework of Karimi et al. (2019) to establish an O(1/√T) rate to a stationary point independent of batch size B or burn-in parameters P, R.

## Results

On STL-10 with ResNet-18 (batch size b=32), EMC² consistently outperforms SimCLR, SogCLR, and Negative Cache across both linear probe (LP) and 1-NN accuracy metrics, while being ~2× faster than SimCLR/SogCLR and ~3× faster than Negative Cache in wall-clock time. On ImageNet-100 with ResNet-50 (b=256), EMC² shows performance gains over SimCLR and is on par with SogCLR. On a controlled STL-10 subset (500 images, b=4), EMC² achieves a squared gradient norm 2 orders of magnitude lower than all baselines, confirming its convergence to a more accurate stationary point.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMC² outperforms SimCLR (Chen et al., 2020) across all metrics on both STL-10 and ImageNet-100 regardless of batch size, as SimCLR is bottlenecked by the poor quality of its in-batch negative distribution at small batch sizes. Against Negative Cache (Lindgren et al., 2021), EMC² achieves better LP and 1-NN accuracy while requiring significantly less memory (m integers vs. m_neg·d real numbers) and lower computation per iteration; Negative Cache's stale-cache error prevents it from matching in-batch negative approaches. Compared to SogCLR (Yuan et al., 2022), EMC² matches or exceeds performance on ImageNet-100 and clearly outperforms on STL-10, where SogCLR's feature homogeneity assumption is violated. In the controlled convergence experiment (Figure 7, 500-image STL-10 subset, b=4), EMC² reaches a squared gradient norm approximately 2 orders of magnitude lower than all three baselines, validating its O(1/√T) global convergence guarantee. In wall-clock time (Figure 1, b=32), EMC² is ~2× faster than SimCLR and SogCLR and ~3× faster than Negative Cache over 100 epochs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the global contrastive loss formulation of Lindgren et al. (2021) and Yuan et al. (2022), and departs from prior negative sampling strategies (random batch in SimCLR, feature caching in Negative Cache, running-mean normalization in SogCLR) by directly tracking the softmax distribution via a Metropolis-Hastings Markov chain. The convergence analysis adapts the biased stochastic approximation framework of Karimi et al. (2019) to a state-dependent Markov kernel, requiring new proofs of geometric ergodicity and Lipschitz continuity of the θ-dependent transition kernel. The multi-state MCMC design (one chain per positive sample) is a key architectural choice that avoids the partition-function computation that would be needed in a single-state formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that EMC² achieves O(1/√T) convergence to a stationary point regardless of batch size challenges the prevailing assumption in contrastive learning that large batches are necessary for quality negative sampling, with direct practical implications for training on resource-constrained hardware. However, the empirical validation is limited to small-scale datasets (STL-10, ImageNet-100) and relatively small models (ResNet-18/50), leaving open whether the approach scales to the multi-million-sample regimes where large-batch training is currently standard (e.g., full ImageNet, web-scale corpora). The conservative 2^R dependence in the Lipschitz constant and the bounded-norm assumption (Assumption 3.1) also limit the generality of the theoretical guarantees. Future work on alternative MCMC samplers (Langevin dynamics) and tighter analysis could strengthen both the theory and practical applicability.
