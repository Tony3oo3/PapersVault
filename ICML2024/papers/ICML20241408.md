## Problem

Existing unbalanced optimal transport (UOT) methods produce dense transport plans and do not support explicit cardinality constraints on sparsity. While sparse OT variants exist for the balanced setting (e.g., SSOT, SCOT), no prior work learns (structured) sparse transport plans in the UOT setting with controllable sparsity levels. This paper addresses the gap by formulating sparsity-constrained UOT as a discrete combinatorial optimization problem with theoretical guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes two empirical distributions (source μ over m points, target ν over n points), a cost matrix C ∈ R^{m×n}, a universal kernel k (RBF or IMQ) for MMD regularization, and an explicit sparsity budget K (either a global cardinality K₁ on the entire plan or a per-column budget K₂). It outputs a non-negative transport plan γ with at most K₁ non-zero entries total (GenSparseUOT) or at most K₂ non-zero entries per column (ColSparseUOT), along with the discrete support set S that defines the sparsity pattern. The sparsity is enforced as a hard cardinality constraint rather than an implicit regularization effect, giving the user direct control over the number of non-zero transport entries.

## Method

The key insight is that the MMD-UOT objective, when parameterized by the support set S of the transport plan, defines a monotone, non-negative, α-weakly submodular set function F(S) = U(0) − min_{γ: supp(γ)⊆S} U(γ), where α ≥ u_{2K}/Ũ₁ is governed by restricted strong concavity and smoothness constants. The global sparsity constraint C₁ corresponds to a uniform matroid and the column-wise constraint C₂ to a partition matroid, so the problem becomes maximizing a weakly submodular function over a matroid. Two OMP-based greedy algorithms are proposed: Algorithm 1 (stochastic OMP for the uniform matroid) selects the element with the largest gradient value from a random candidate subset each iteration, and Algorithm 2 (OMP for the partition matroid) selects a uniformly random element from the best maximal independent set of the contracted matroid. Each iteration requires solving one convex MMD-UOT subproblem via accelerated projected gradient descent, yielding (1−e^{−α}−ε) and (1+1/α)^{−2} approximation guarantees respectively. A dual analysis further provides a computable duality gap for verifying solution quality.

## Results

On the SPFD network topology design task, the proposed GenSparseUOT achieves expected profits of 0.166–0.293 across network sizes l={100,175,250}, outperforming the GSOT baseline (0.014–0.044) by roughly 10×. On monolingual word alignment (Wiki dataset), the proposed method achieves 49.14% null-alignment accuracy and 94.79% total F1, matching the best baseline (BOT: 48.95% / 94.96%) while substantially outperforming MMD-UOT (41.35% / 93.14%) and SSOT (16.54% / 64.13%). On the CIFAR-10/CIFAR-10-rotate SMoE task, ColSparseUOT (λ₁=0.1) reaches 95.56% test accuracy with perfectly balanced expert allocation (~5000 inputs per expert), matching the best Top-1 MoE default (95.91%) while avoiding its heavily skewed allocation. In duality gap comparisons, the proposed solver achieves at least 3× lower duality gap than SCOT across all tested hyperparameter settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GSOT (Luo et al., 2023) on SPFD, the proposed GenSparseUOT wins decisively, achieving ~10× higher expected profit (e.g., 0.166 vs. 0.014 at l=100). On word alignment, the proposed method ties with BOT (the strongest baseline from Arase et al., 2023) at 49.14% vs. 48.95% null accuracy, while clearly beating POT (37.07%), KL-UOT (44.68%), MMD-UOT (41.35%), and SSOT (16.54%)—the latter two being the most directly comparable sparse/UOT baselines. On the SMoE CIFAR task, ColSparseUOT (λ₁=0.1) achieves 95.56% accuracy with balanced expert allocation, outperforming SCOT (Liu et al., 2023) which ranges from 56.48% to 90.56% depending on its regularization parameter and suffers from skewed load balancing. The Top-1 MoE baseline (Chen et al., 2022) achieves 95.91% by default but with two experts receiving zero inputs; its balanced variant drops to 93.74%, whereas the proposed method maintains ~95.56% with near-perfect balance. In the duality gap comparison (Table 4), the proposed Algorithm 2 consistently yields at least 3× lower duality gap than SCOT's dual solver, with some configurations reaching <10⁻¹⁰, indicating near-global optimality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MMD-UOT formulation (Manupriya et al., 2024b) by introducing explicit cardinality sparsity constraints on the transport plan, moving from the balanced OT setting (where sparsity-constrained OT was studied by Blondel et al., 2018 and Liu et al., 2023) to the unbalanced setting. The key conceptual departure is reframing the resulting non-convex, non-smooth optimization as weakly submodular maximization over a uniform or partition matroid, drawing on the OMP-based greedy algorithmic framework of Gurumoorthy et al. (2019) and the weakly submodular approximation theory of Das & Kempe (2018) and Chen et al. (2018). Unlike SCOT (Liu et al., 2023), which solves a dual relaxation in the continuous setting and requires ℓ₂-regularization, this work operates in the discrete support-selection space and works even without the ℓ₂ term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a novel conceptual bridge between optimal transport and submodular optimization, providing a principled discrete framework for learning sparse transport plans with theoretical approximation guarantees in the unbalanced setting. The duality gap analysis (Lemma 3.4, Proposition 3.5) offers a practical certificate of solution quality that is absent in prior sparsity-constrained OT work. Limitations include dependence on the submodularity ratio (which can be small, weakening guarantees), experiments on relatively small-scale problems (m=n=100 for SPFD, small sentence pairs for word alignment), and the fact that only two sparsity patterns (global and column-wise) are explored. Open questions include extending to other structured sparsity patterns (e.g., block-sparse, row-and-column joint), scaling to larger instances, and whether the submodular perspective enables other algorithmic innovations beyond greedy methods.
