## Problem

AI research faces exponentially growing computational demands that threaten economic, social, and environmental sustainability, yet no prior work has explicitly connected Tensor Networks (TNs)—with their inherent logarithmic compression capability—to the Green AI framework. This position paper is the first to establish that link, arguing that TNs are an underutilized asset for making AI more sustainable and inclusive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241439/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard data formats (vectors, matrices, images, text, video) that are first tensorized—reshaped into higher-order tensors via systematic index bookkeeping—before TN decomposition is applied. In kernel machines, the input vector x is never explicitly expanded into its Kronecker-product basis; instead, its TN representation is used throughout training and inference. In deep learning, weight matrices are tensorized and replaced by low-rank TN cores, producing compressed model parameters that serve as the output for both forward and backward passes.

## Method

The paper's core argument rests on the logarithmic compression property of low-rank TNs: CP and TT decompositions reduce storage and compute complexity from exponential in dimension D to linear (O(RID) and O(R²ID) respectively), while Tucker remains exponential (O(R^D)). Two application domains are analyzed in detail: (1) kernel machines, where TNs replace explicit Kronecker-product basis functions, reducing training complexity from O(NI^{2D}+I^{3D}) to O(DN(IR)²+D(IR)³); and (2) deep learning, where TT decomposition of fully connected layers reduces forward-pass complexity from O(I^D J^D) to O(DR²I·max{I^D,J^D}). The paper evaluates these gains using Green AI efficiency metrics (FLOPS, parameter count, runtime, electricity usage, CO2e) across seven representative models, showing speedups of 1.5×–21× and parameter reductions up to 99.9% with minimal or even positive accuracy changes.

## Results

Across kernel machines and deep learning, TN-based models achieve speed-ups ranging from 1.6× to 21.3× and compression ratios of 1.5× to 8.3×, while accuracy changes range from −30.83% (GPT-2 Small) to +3.25% (ResNet on CIFAR-100). In the most favorable cases, TNs improve both efficiency and accuracy simultaneously: Kim et al. (2016) report up to 86.4% memory reduction, 79.7% FLOPS reduction, 72.8% runtime reduction, and 76.6% electricity reduction on AlexNet/ImageNet with ≤2.2% top-5 accuracy loss, while Ye et al. (2018) cut LSTM parameters by over 80,000×, speed up convergence 14×, and increase accuracy by 15.6% on UCF11. Wesel & Batselier (2021) reduced kernel machine parameters by up to 99.9% while achieving superior accuracy 2.5× faster on a laptop than a competing method on a cluster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper positions TN-based models against two specific non-TN baselines. Against the tffm factorization machine implementation (Mikhail Trofimov, 2016), Novikov et al.'s exponential machine with TNs achieves a 21.3× speed-up at the cost of an 11.46% accuracy drop on synthetic classification data. Against Hensman et al.'s (2013) sparse Gaussian process for big data, Wesel & Batselier's (2021) Fourier-feature TN kernel machine runs 2.5× faster (7,141 s on a laptop vs. 18,360 s on a cluster) while delivering superior accuracy (+1.13%) on the Airline regression task. In both comparisons, the TN method wins on efficiency; the accuracy trade-off is modest in the kernel machine setting (≤11.5% drop) but the speed advantage is substantial. The broader deep learning comparisons (Kim et al., Ye et al., Yin et al.) show TNs matching or exceeding standard NN accuracy while reducing memory, FLOPS, and runtime by 70–86%, though the GPT-2 Small compression (Abronin et al., 2024) illustrates that very large language models still suffer significant accuracy loss (−30.83%) at only 1.5× compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed but previously unconnected literatures: the Green AI movement (Schwartz et al. 2020; Strubell et al. 2019/2020), which established efficiency metrics alongside accuracy as a research priority, and the tensor network / tensor decomposition community (Cichocki et al. 2016/2017; Oseledets 2011; Novikov et al. 2015), which has long demonstrated logarithmic compression properties for high-dimensional data. The paper's conceptual contribution is explicitly the first to frame TNs through a Green AI lens, arguing that their inherent O(RID) or O(R²ID) storage complexity (versus O(I^D) for full tensors) makes them a natural fit for sustainable AI. It does not introduce new TN architectures or new Green AI metrics but rather synthesizes existing evidence from kernel machines and deep learning to make the case for cross-community adoption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a genuine gap: TNs have been applied to AI for over a decade without being explicitly motivated or evaluated through sustainability metrics, while Green AI discussions have largely overlooked tensor-based compression as a first-class tool. Key open questions remain, including the absence of an Eckart-Young-type guarantee for optimal low-rank TN approximation, the NP-hardness of finding true tensor rank, and the lack of practitioner guidelines for rank selection. The authors also honestly note that TNs are limited to correlated data and have no impact on computing infrastructure or system-level sustainability, and that the rebound effect could partially offset efficiency gains. As a position paper, its practical impact depends on whether the TN and Green AI communities actually integrate, which remains to be seen.
