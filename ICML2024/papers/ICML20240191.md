## Problem

Existing identifiability results for nonlinear representation learning (e.g., local isometry ICA) require exact satisfaction of model assumptions, yet real-world data only approximately satisfies them. This paper initiates the study of robustness for representation learning, showing that some identifiability results are brittle to arbitrarily small misspecification while local isometry results are robust, and provides the first quantitative robustness guarantees for nonlinear ICA with approximately isometric mixing.

## Input / Output

The method takes observational data X = f(S) where S has independent non-Gaussian components with bounded connected support, and the mixing function f is measured by its isometry deviation Θ_p(f, Ω) — an integrated pointwise distance of Df and (Df)^{-1} to the orthogonal group (or to SO(d, T_{f(z)}M) in the undercomplete case d < D). The output is a quantitative MCC lower bound MCC(Ŝ, S) ≥ 1 − C·Θ_p²(f, Ω), the recovered linear mixing matrix A up to O(η) error, and the unmixing matrix W whose rows are local extrema of the contrast function H. Notably, the unmixing g is chosen as the maximally isometric representation of the observational distribution, depending only on f_*P and not on f directly.

## Method

The proof proceeds in two steps combined via composition. First, a rigidity theorem from nonlinear elasticity (Friesecke et al., 2002) is applied to show that when Θ_p(f, Ω) is small, the transition map g^{-1}∘f between the ground-truth mixing and the maximally isometric representation g is close to an affine map, with the nonlinear residual h bounded by ‖h‖_{P,q} ≤ C₁·Θ_p(f, Ω). Second, a perturbed linear ICA analysis (x = As + ηh(s)) shows that standard contrast-function-based ICA algorithms recover the linear mixing matrix A up to O(η) error and the sources with MCC ≥ 1 − C₂η², using Taylor expansion of the contrast function H(w) = E[G(w^T Σ_X^{-1/2} X)] around the ground-truth unmixing directions. Combining both steps yields the final bound MCC(Ŝ, S) ≥ 1 − C₃·Θ_p²(f, Ω), gracefully recovering the exact identifiability result of Horan et al. (2021) when Θ_p = 0. The paper also demonstrates non-robustness for other function classes (e.g., piecewise linear mixing) via a VAE experiment where the true mixing is identifiable in theory but the learning signal is insufficient to overcome the VAE's inductive bias toward simple orthogonal transformations.

## Results

The paper's main quantitative result (Theorem 5.11) establishes that for ICA with mixing close to a local isometry, the Mean Correlation Coefficient satisfies MCC(Ŝ, S) ≥ 1 − C₃·Θ_p²(f, Ω), where Θ_p measures the pointwise deviation of the mixing from being a local isometry. The intermediate rigidity result (Theorem 4.1) bounds the nonlinear residual as ||h||_{P,q} ≤ C₁·Θ_p(f, Ω), and the perturbed linear ICA result (Theorem 5.9) gives MCC(Ŝ, S) ≥ 1 − C₂·η² where η controls the perturbation magnitude. The unmixing matrix W is shown to satisfy |W − W̄| = O(η) (Theorem 5.7), recovering exact linear ICA identifiability as η → 0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical method comparisons or benchmark tables. The results are positioned as generalizations of prior identifiability results: Theorem 4.1 extends the exact identifiability of Horan et al. (2021) for local isometries to the approximate case, and Theorem 5.9 extends the classical linear ICA identifiability of Comon (1994) to the perturbed model x = As + ηh(s). The paper also demonstrates (via a VAE experiment in Figure 1 and Appendix H) that existing identifiability results for piecewise-linear mixing (Kivva et al., 2022) are not robust: a VAE initialized with the true mixing still converges to a spurious orthogonal transformation, illustrating brittleness that the local-isometry class avoids. No direct head-to-head algorithmic comparisons are provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the exact identifiability result for locally isometric mixing (Horan et al., 2021) to the misspecified setting where the mixing is only approximately isometric, using a geometric rigidity theorem from elasticity theory (Friesecke et al., 2002) as the key analytical tool. The perturbed linear ICA analysis (Section 5) generalizes the classical contrast-function framework of Hyvärinen & Oja (2000) and Comon (1994) to the model x = As + ηh(s), showing that standard ICA algorithms recover the linear mixing up to O(η) error. The paper explicitly positions itself as initiating the study of robustness for representation learning, noting that no prior robustness results exist for nonlinear mixing functions, and contrasts the robustness of local isometries with the brittleness of other identifiable function classes (polynomial, piecewise linear).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most impactful contribution is the negative result: it shows that several existing identifiability results (polynomial, piecewise linear mixing) are not robust to arbitrarily small misspecification, meaning the function class is dense in the space of all injective continuous functions and the identifiability guarantee is vacuous in practice. This clarifies a fundamental tradeoff between representation capacity and identifiability that the CRL community has not previously formalized. Open questions include finite-sample guarantees, robustness to approximate independence of latent variables, whether the rigidity-based approach extends to other function classes, and the practical algorithmic challenge of optimizing the non-convex isometry-minimization problem (8). The paper's scope is limited to ICA with local isometries and does not address the broader landscape of CRL assumptions.
