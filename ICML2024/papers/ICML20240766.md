## Problem

On-device federated learning for language models is limited by device memory (cannot train LLMs), high per-round communication and computation costs, and difficulty in debugging and deployment. Existing methods for generating differentially private synthetic text from federated clients produce data of insufficient quality to train or finetune language models. The paper asks whether a DP synthetic data pipeline can match or exceed the utility of direct DP-FL training at a fraction of the cost, while also enabling private finetuning of server-side LLMs.

## Input / Output

The method takes private text samples distributed across N=1250 clients (8 samples per client for JOBS/FORUMS/MICROBLOG, up to 128 for CODE), a public pretrained LLM (LLaMA-2-7B), and an initial population of public text samples as input. It produces a DP synthetic text dataset (2 million samples for on-device evaluation, 50K for on-server LLM finetuning) under (ε, δ)-DP with ε ∈ {1.29, 7.58} and δ=3×10⁻⁶. Clients only transmit 2048-dimensional histograms per round and download 2048 embedding vectors of dimension 384, rather than the full 82M-parameter model required by DP-FL. The synthetic data can be reused indefinitely for training multiple models without additional privacy cost due to DP's post-processing property.

## Method

PrE-Text adapts the Private Evolution (PE) framework from images to text through three key modifications. First, it replaces diffusion-based variation with iterative mask-filling using RoBERTa-large (masking 30% of tokens, filling with a masked LM, repeated W_steps=2 times). Second, it collects synthetic samples from all iterations (∪_{t=1}^{T} Set(S'_t)) rather than only the final round, and uses the pre-variation samples S'_t (which retain more private information) instead of the post-variation S_t. Third, it adds a post-processing expansion phase where LLaMA-2-7B is prompted with three randomly chosen DP seed samples to generate similar text, producing orders of magnitude more training data at zero additional privacy cost. The only privacy-sensitive step is the per-client nearest-neighbor histogram (computed via all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings), which is privatized with the Gaussian mechanism and aggregated via secure aggregation; thresholding at H removes noise-dominated histogram entries to improve signal-to-noise ratio.

## Results

On four private federated datasets (JOBS, FORUMS, MICROBLOG, CODE), PrE-Text outperforms all private baselines at both ε=1.29 and ε=7.58 for on-device DistilGPT2 models. At ε=1.29, PrE-Text achieves 0.718/0.672/0.680/0.644 next-token accuracy (vs. 0.703/0.665/0.667/0.643 for DP-FTRL, the strongest on-device baseline), while using 100× less communication per round, 6× less client computation, and 9× fewer rounds. For on-server LLaMA-2-7B finetuning, PrE-Text synthetic data improves accuracy over the non-finetuned model by 1.2–1.3 points across all four datasets at ε=1.29 (e.g., 0.522 vs. 0.510 on JOBS). PrE-Text also achieves 1.482/1.779/1.770/2.004 cross-entropy loss at ε=1.29, beating DP-FTRL (1.594/1.850/1.874/2.007) by 0.11–0.03 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PrE-Text wins against all three private baselines across all four datasets at both ε=1.29 and ε=7.58. Against DP-FTRL (the strongest on-device baseline), PrE-Text gains 1.5 points on JOBS (0.718 vs. 0.703), 0.7 points on FORUMS (0.672 vs. 0.665), 1.3 points on MICROBLOG (0.680 vs. 0.667), and 0.1 points on CODE (0.644 vs. 0.643) at ε=1.29; gaps widen slightly at ε=7.58 (e.g., 0.721 vs. 0.704 on JOBS). Against DP-FedAvg, the margins are similar or slightly larger (e.g., 1.7 points on JOBS at ε=1.29). DP-Prompt performs worst among private methods, trailing PrE-Text by 4.5 points on JOBS and 4.5 points on CODE at ε=1.29, largely because it cannot leverage secure aggregation and must add substantially more noise to individual text outputs. PrE-Text also beats the ε=0 public-data-only baselines (c4-only, Expand-only) at both privacy levels, confirming that the private synthetic data adds genuine signal. The gap to the ε=∞ upper bound (Expand-private) is 0.9–4.3 points, suggesting room for improvement as synthetic data generation techniques advance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Private Evolution (PE) framework from Lin et al. (2023), originally designed for DP synthetic image generation via diffusion models, to the text domain by substituting masked language models (RoBERTa) for the variation step and adding an LLM-based expansion phase (using LLaMA-2-7B) to amplify the DP seed set. It positions itself as a replacement for DP-FL (DP-FedAvg, DP-FTRL) rather than a complement, arguing that generating DP synthetic data centrally and finetuning on it is strictly more efficient than running private training on-device. The work also departs from concurrent DP synthetic text efforts (e.g., Xie et al., 2024) by explicitly benchmarking against on-device training baselines and demonstrating that the synthetic-data route is competitive in practice, not just in data quality metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates the first known method to privately finetune a large language model (LLaMA-2-7B) in a federated setting without requiring the model to reside on user devices, addressing a practical bottleneck as LLMs outgrow on-device hardware. The 100× communication and 6× client-computation reductions make private federated learning substantially more deployable on resource-constrained smartphones. Key limitations include reliance on public pretrained models (raising data-provenance and contamination concerns), evaluation on relatively small and homogeneous datasets (c4-en subsets), and modest absolute performance gains over DP-FL (e.g., ~1.5% accuracy improvement on JOBS at ε=1.29). Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly large LLMs (70B+), more diverse and heterogeneous client data, and whether the expand phase can be made more privacy-robust as seed quality degrades at very low ε.
