## Problem

Diffusion models require repeated forward passes through large score-estimation networks at every sampling step, making inference prohibitively slow. While early-exiting has been explored for LLMs, the time-dependent nature of score estimation in diffusion models—where difficulty varies across the denoising trajectory—has not been exploited to adaptively allocate computation. This paper asks whether a single pre-trained diffusion model can be fine-tuned to use fewer parameters at 'easy' time steps without degrading generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241474/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained diffusion models (DiT XL/2 at 256×256 on ImageNet, U-ViT-S/4 at 64×64 on CelebA, PixArt-α at 256×256 on SAM) and produces generated images. At inference, the input is standard Gaussian noise (plus a text prompt for text-to-image) and a time step t; the model adaptively routes through a subset of its transformer blocks depending on t. Fine-tuning requires only a small fraction of the original training data (e.g., 1% for PixArt, ~10–20% of original compute for DiT/U-ViT), making the adaptation cost negligible relative to pre-training.

## Method

Adaptive Score Estimation (ASE) introduces a time-dependent block-dropping schedule that progressively removes transformer blocks as the sampling time approaches the prior (t→1), based on the observation that score estimation near the noise distribution requires fewer parameters than near the data distribution. For DiT, entire blocks are dropped from the final block onward; for U-ViT, only decoder blocks are targeted and their linear layers are preserved to maintain skip-connection integrity. A lightweight fine-tuning algorithm then adjusts the model using two mechanisms: a high EMA rate to protect well-learned parameters near t→0, and a reweighted loss coefficient λ(t) that temporarily emphasizes learning near t→1 before reverting to uniform weighting. Unlike concurrent work (DeeDiff), ASE requires no additional uncertainty-estimation module and no extra memory, and it composes with fast solvers (DDIM, DPM-Solver) to compound speedups, achieving ~25–30% wall-clock acceleration while matching or improving FID.

## Results

On CelebA with U-ViT (DPM-50 solver), ASE achieves a FID of 1.92 with 23.39% sampling speed acceleration, outperforming the baseline FID of 2.87. On ImageNet with DiT (DDPM-250), the D3-DiT schedule reaches FID 8.647 with 30.46% acceleration versus the baseline FID of 9.078. On the large-scale PixArt-α text-to-image task, ASE attains FID 12.682 (vs. 12.483 baseline) with 14% wall-clock acceleration using only 1% of the original training data for fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASE outperforms all three baselines on generation quality (FID) while delivering competitive speedups. Against DeeDiff, which achieves the highest acceleration at 45.76%, ASE trades some speed (23.39%) for a substantially better FID of 1.92 versus DeeDiff's 3.90, meaning ASE actually improves quality over the unmodified baseline. ToMe (token merging) provides only 3.05% acceleration while degrading FID to 4.963, as the overhead of bipartite soft matching outweighs the token reduction benefit. Block Caching yields 9.06% acceleration with FID 3.955, also degrading quality relative to the baseline. Overall, ASE is the only method that simultaneously accelerates sampling and improves FID, making it the clear winner in the quality–speed trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the early-exiting paradigm from LLM inference (DynaBERT, CAT, CALM) to diffusion model sampling, introducing the key adaptation that the exit decision is governed by a time-dependent schedule rather than input-dependent confidence. It is explicitly orthogonal to the dominant line of fast-sampling work (DDIM, DPM-Solver, consistency distillation) that reduces the number of denoising steps, instead targeting the per-step compute cost τ. Among concurrent methods that also reduce per-step computation—ToMe (token merging) and Block Caching (feature reuse)—ASE differs by using a deterministic, time-indexed block-dropping schedule that requires no additional uncertainty module (unlike DeeDiff) and no feature-similarity matching overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 25–30% wall-clock speedup with no FID degradation is practically significant for deploying large diffusion models (DiT-XL, PixArt-α) in latency-sensitive settings, and the method's architecture- and solver-agnosticism broadens its applicability. The negative-transfer analysis (Table 4) adds a conceptual contribution: it shows that a single network trained across all timesteps suffers measurable task interference, and that explicitly partitioning parameters via early-exiting mitigates this—suggesting a rethinking of how diffusion models allocate capacity across the denoising trajectory. The main open question, acknowledged by the authors, is the manual design of dropping schedules; automating schedule discovery (e.g., via learned gating or search) would be a natural next step.
