## Problem

CLIP's contrastive InfoNCE loss and softmax-based zero-shot inference have not been understood through a principled geometric or transport-theoretic lens, leaving the underlying alignment mechanism opaque and limiting the ability to systematically generalize the loss or improve inference. This paper bridges optimal transport and contrastive vision-language pretraining by showing that CLIP training is an instance of inverse OT and that zero-shot inference is a half-constrained OT matching problem, thereby opening a family of OT-based losses and graph-matching inference procedures that were previously unexplored.

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes paired RGB images (processed through a modified ResNet-50) and text captions (processed through a 12-layer Transformer) from CC3M, producing aligned image-text embeddings in a shared space. During inference, a batch of images and a set of text label embeddings are jointly fed into a graph-matching OT solver; the output is a coupling matrix P whose rows give per-sample prediction confidence over labels, rather than a single softmax vector. For long-tailed inference, an additional known label distribution vector b is supplied to restore full marginal constraints, and for selective classification, a user-specified selective rate s determines how many samples the model commits to classifying versus rejecting.

## Method

The paper first proves that CLIP's dual InfoNCE loss is exactly a bilevel inverse-OT optimization where the inner problem is entropic OT with row-only (image) or column-only (text) constraints, and the temperature equals the entropic regularization coefficient. It then generalizes this by replacing the half-constraint set with variants: full Sinkhorn constraints (Entropic OT), double-bounded column constraints (DBOT), a Gromov-Wasserstein structural term that enforces edge-level consistency between image and text feature graphs (FGromov OT), soft KL marginal penalties (Unbalanced OT), and their combination (U-FG OT), yielding a loss family trained via the same bilevel IOT framework. For inference, the paper replaces pointwise softmax with a many-to-one graph-matching formulation using Fused Gromov OT, solved either by iterative softmax updates (FGromov OT-ISOftmax) or projected gradient descent with KL projection (FGromov OT-PGD), where the structural cost D₁PD₂ᵀ captures inter-sample similarity among both images and labels. The same OT machinery extends to long-tailed inference (restoring full U(a,b) constraints with a known label prior) and selective classification (using unbalanced OT to let low-confidence samples' probability mass shrink, or partial OT with an inequality constraint P1 ≤ 1 and total mass s to explicitly reject a fraction of samples).

## Results

On zero-shot classification with CC3M-pretrained models, FGromov OT achieves the best average top-1 accuracy of 20.6%, outperforming InfoNCE (19.2%), CyCLIP (19.2%), and Triplet (18.1%) across 19 downstream datasets. For inference with the OpenAI pretrained CLIP, FGromov OT-ISOftmax (graph matching) improves average top-1 accuracy from 44.3% (vanilla softmax) to 49.9%, a 5.6-point gain. In selective zero-shot classification at 50% selective rate, Unbalanced OT reaches 56.9% average accuracy versus 54.9% for softmax. For long-tailed inference, FGromov OT achieves 66.4% on CIFAR-100 (finetuned, LT) versus 52.7% for classifier normalization and 45.1% for vanilla softmax.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against InfoNCE (Oord et al., 2018), the proposed FGromov OT loss achieves 20.6% average zero-shot accuracy versus 19.2% for InfoNCE on CC3M-pretrained models (Table 1), with the largest gains on PCAM (+14.2 pts) and SUN397 (+2.2 pts). For inference with the OpenAI pretrained CLIP, FGromov OT-ISOftmax improves average accuracy from 44.3% (vanilla softmax) to 49.9% (Table 4), with notable gains on Food-101 (+6.8 pts), SUN397 (+9.6 pts), and Country211 (+23.1 pts). In long-tailed inference (Table 5), FGromov OT outperforms both Classifier Normalization (Kang et al., 2019) and Class-Aware Bias (Menon et al., 2020) under finetuned conditions: e.g., on CIFAR-100 LT, FGromov OT reaches 66.4% versus 52.7% (Classifier Normalize) and 41.5% (Class-Aware Bias); on ImageNet U, it achieves 59.9% versus 44.4% and 47.9% respectively. In selective zero-shot classification at 50% selective rate (Table 6), both Unbalanced OT (56.9%) and Partial OT (56.6%) surpass vanilla softmax (54.9%), with the largest improvement on PCAM (+16.5 pts for Unbal. OT). The FGromov OT loss also yields the best text encoder on STS tasks (58.20 average vs. 57.29 for InfoNCE, Table 3), confirming that structural matching benefits representation quality in both modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inverse optimal transport (IOT) framework of Shi et al. (ICML 2023), which showed that CLIP's InfoNCE loss is a special case of bilevel OT optimization with half-constraints, by (1) generalizing the training loss to a family of OT variants (full-constraint entropic OT, unbalanced OT, fused Gromov OT, DBOT) and (2) applying OT to the inference stage for the first time in the CLIP context. The inference procedure draws on fused Gromov-Wasserstein graph matching (Vayer et al., 2018; Xu & Cheng, 2023) and partial/unbalanced OT (Chapel et al., 2020) to handle long-tailed and selective classification. Conceptually, the paper bridges two previously disconnected communities—contrastive vision-language pretraining and optimal transport—by showing that both the training objective and the softmax-based inference are recoverable as constrained OT problems, and that relaxing or enriching those constraints yields practical improvements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OT-CLIP loss family gives practitioners a principled menu of alternatives to InfoNCE, with fused Gromov OT consistently capturing inter-sample relational structure that improves both zero-shot classification (avg 20.6 vs 19.2) and text encoder quality (STS avg 58.2 vs 57.3). More practically, the graph-matching inference (FGromov OT-ISOftmax) yields a ~5.6-point average improvement over vanilla softmax on OpenAI CLIP zero-shot classification without retraining, and extends naturally to long-tailed and selective settings where prior methods require ad-hoc confidence calibration. Limitations include training on the relatively small CC3M dataset with a ResNet-50 backbone, modest training-time gains, and batch-size sensitivity of the graph-matching inference (performance degrades or peaks non-monotonically with batch size). Open questions include whether these OT-based losses and inference procedures scale to ViT-Large or larger pretraining corpora, and whether the structural regularization benefit persists when the number of classes far exceeds the batch size.
