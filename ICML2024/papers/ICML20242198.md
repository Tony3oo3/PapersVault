## Problem

Conventional causal discovery methods for time series assume measurements at the system's causal timescale, but in practice data is often subsampled with numerous missing time-slices between consecutive observations, making full graph discovery unidentifiable. While intervention-based approaches can recover causal orderings, full interventions are expensive, unethical, or infeasible in domains like healthcare and social science. This paper addresses the specific challenge of learning causal relations from subsampled time series using only two available time-slices, without requiring interventions or prior knowledge of the subsampling rate.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only two cross-sectional snapshots X^{t_a} and X^{t_b} (each a d-variate vector) from a subsampled time series, where t_a < t_b and the gap t_b - t_a may span multiple unobserved time-slices. It first produces a unique descendant hierarchical topology as a d×d adjacency matrix in which every edge points strictly from an ancestor to a descendant (never to a non-descendant), then prunes spurious edges via CAM to yield the final DAG of the summary causal graph. Unlike standard time-series methods that require all intermediate slices, the algorithm operates on just these two snapshots, making it applicable to clinical records or periodic monitoring where only sparse observations exist.

## Method

DHT-CIT treats the earlier time-slice X^{t_a} as auxiliary instrumental variables that simulate interventions: perturbations in X_i^{t_a} propagate to X_i^{t_b} and its descendants but not to non-descendants under the Markov, acyclic-summary-graph, and stationarity assumptions. The key theoretical contribution is a descendant-oriented conditional independence criterion (Corollary 4.5): X_j^{t_b} is a descendant of X_i^{t_b} if and only if X_i^{t_a} is not conditionally independent of X_j^{t_b} given the conditional set X_{⊗i}^{t_a} (all variables at t_a except X_i^{t_a} and those independent of it). This single test per node pair, implemented via conditional HSIC with Gaussian kernel, constructs a unique descendant hierarchical topology with far fewer spurious edges than complete topological orderings produced by SCORE or CAM. A topological ordering adjustment step iteratively identifies leaf layers and resolves cycles by removing the weakest (highest-p-value) edge, ensuring acyclicity. Finally, CAM prunes remaining spurious edges within the constrained hierarchical ordering to recover the summary causal DAG.

## Results

On Sin-20-20 (20 nodes, 20 edges) with observational data D={X¹,X²}, DHT-CIT achieves SHD=1.00±1.32, SID=3.10±4.40, and F1-Score=0.98±0.03, substantially outperforming SCORE (SHD=7.40, SID=31.3) and CAM (SHD=10.3, SID=41.6). The key advantage is in #Prune: DHT-CIT requires pruning only 30.6 spurious edges on average versus 172.1 for SCORE and 360.0 for CAM, reducing the downstream search space by over 80%. On the smaller Sin-10-10 graph, DHT-CIT (SHD=1.00, F1=0.95) is competitive with sparse-graph-optimized methods like GOLEM (SHD=0.50) and ReScore (SHD=1.00), while still reducing #Prune from 35.8 (SCORE) to 13.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DHT-CIT clearly outperforms both topology-based baselines (SCORE and CAM) across all metrics, with the most dramatic gains in #Prune: 13.2 vs. 35.8 (SCORE) and 80.0 (CAM) on Sin-10-10, and 30.6 vs. 172.1 (SCORE) and 360.0 (CAM) on Sin-20-20. Against sparse-graph-optimized score-based methods (GOLEM, NOTEARS, ReScore), DHT-CIT is competitive on SHD and F1-Score but does not report #Prune for those methods, making direct comparison on pruning cost impossible; however, DHT-CIT's F1 of 0.98 on Sin-20-20 matches or exceeds GOLEM (0.97) and ReScore (0.95). Traditional time series methods (Granger, VarLiNGAM) and constraint-based methods (PC, FCI) perform substantially worse, with SHD values 10–170× higher than DHT-CIT, confirming that standard approaches fail under subsampling. CD-NOD shows moderate performance on Sin-10-10 (SHD=5.40) but was not evaluated on Sin-20-20 due to excessive runtime (>5 hours). On the real-world PM-CMR dataset, DHT-CIT is the only method that recovers the known causal structure T←X₁:₇→Y and T→Y, while GOLEM misses the T→Y edge and SCORE incorrectly identifies T as a parent of {X₁,X₅,X₆}.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends topology-based causal discovery (CAM, SCORE) to the subsampled time series setting where only two time-slices are available, a scenario where standard time series methods (Granger, VARLiNGAM) and prior subsampled-time-series approaches (Gong et al., Plis et al., Hyttinen et al.) either require all time-slices, linear models, or prior knowledge of the subsampling rate. The key conceptual departure is treating the earlier time-slice as auxiliary instrumental variables (simulated interventions) to identify a unique Descendant Hierarchical Topology (DHT) via conditional independence tests, rather than producing a non-unique complete topological ordering with many spurious edges as SCORE does. The pruning stage builds directly on CAM's generalized additive model framework, and the conditional HSIC test from Zhang et al. (2011) serves as the statistical tool within the new descendant-oriented criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: in healthcare and social science, researchers often have only two reliable time-slices (e.g., patient records from two visits) and cannot conduct expensive or unethical interventions. The DHT framework reduces the spurious-edge search space by 24–148 edges compared to SCORE, making downstream pruning more tractable. However, the method relies on strong assumptions (acyclic summary causal graph, stationarity, first-order Markov property) that may not hold in complex dynamical or biological systems, and performance degrades on very dense graphs or high-dimensional settings where HSIC test accuracy drops. Open questions include handling cyclic causal relationships, non-stationary data, and whether the DHT+SCORE hybrid can scale to truly large real-world systems without interventions.
