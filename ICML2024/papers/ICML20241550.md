## Problem

Concrete Autoencoders (CAEs), the state-of-the-art method for differentiable embedded feature selection, suffer from recurring training instability characterized by large spikes in reconstruction error. The authors identify that this instability strongly correlates with the CAE learning duplicate (redundant) feature selections, which degrades both convergence speed and generalization performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241550/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-dimensional feature vector x ∈ R^D (ranging from 77 to 1024 dimensions across datasets) and produces a subset of K selected features (typically K=50, or K=10 for Mice Protein) via K Gumbel-Softmax distributed selection nodes. The selected features x_S = Mx are then passed through an MLP decoder to yield either a reconstruction of the input (reconstruction task) or a class prediction (classification task). At test time, discrete features are selected via argmax of the learned logits, and the jointly trained decoder is evaluated directly without retraining.

## Method

IP-CAE replaces the direct parameterization of Gumbel-Softmax logits log α ∈ R^{K×D} with an indirect one: a learnable embedding Ψ ∈ R^{K×P} is transformed by a shared linear map W ∈ R^{D×P} to produce log α_i = Wψ_i + b. This changes the effective gradient update from a plain descent step to one involving a learned transformation matrix T_i = WW^T + ψ_i^T(ψ_i − ηW^T∇L)I, which both linearly transforms all gradients (via the shared WW^T term) and scales them by a step-dependent dot product between consecutive embedding vectors. The authors also propose Generalized Jensen-Shannon Divergence (GJSD) regularization as an explicit diversity-encouraging baseline, but show IP-CAE outperforms it across all datasets. The approach requires no additional hyperparameter tuning (P is simply set to D), does not require retraining the decoder after selection, and generalizes to non-linear decoders where vanilla CAE becomes particularly unstable.

## Results

IP-CAE achieves state-of-the-art top-1 classification accuracy across all six datasets, reaching 94.07% on MNIST, 91.85% on ISOLET, 97.92% on COIL-20, and 94.26% on Mice Protein, while also attaining the lowest normalized Frobenius reconstruction error (e.g., 2.03E-03 on Smartphone HAR, 6.90E-03 on Mice Protein). Compared to vanilla CAE, IP-CAE improves classification accuracy by 10.97–31.16 percentage points and reduces reconstruction error by 12–41% across datasets. IP-CAE also converges 2.5×–25.7× faster than CAE, reaching CAE's 200-epoch validation performance in a fraction of the training time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IP-CAE outperforms all three baselines (CAE, STG, LassoNet) on every dataset for both classification accuracy and reconstruction error. Against vanilla CAE, the gains are most dramatic on Mice Protein (+31.2 pp accuracy: 94.26 vs 63.10) and ISOLET (+16.0 pp: 91.85 vs 75.82), and IP-CAE also converges 2.5×–25.7× faster. Against STG, IP-CAE leads by 1.78 pp on MNIST, 6.90 pp on ISOLET, and 26.02 pp on Mice Protein; STG is competitive on COIL-20 (96.80 vs 97.92) but trails elsewhere. Against LassoNet, IP-CAE wins by 4.01 pp on MNIST, 7.52 pp on ISOLET, and 17.14 pp on Mice Protein; LassoNet is closest on Smartphone HAR (92.44 vs 93.71). The paper also notes that CAE and IP-CAE use the jointly learned decoder without retraining, whereas STG and LassoNet require a separate retraining step after feature selection, making the comparison favorable to the baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Concrete Autoencoders (CAE) by Balin et al. (2019), which perform differentiable feature selection via Gumbel-Softmax distributed nodes with temperature annealing. The key departure is replacing the direct parameterization of Gumbel-Softmax logits with an indirect one: a learnable embedding transformed by a shared linear layer, which the authors show alters the gradient update rule in a way that implicitly discourages duplicate selections. The paper also positions IP-CAE against other embedded feature selection methods—Stochastic Gaussian Gates (Yamada et al., 2020) and LassoNet (Lemhadri et al., 2021)—and introduces a Generalized Jensen-Shannon Divergence regularization (borrowed from mixture-model literature) as an explicit diversity-encouraging baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IP-CAE offers a drop-in, hyperparameter-free fix for a well-documented training instability in CAE, yielding up to 20× speedup and large accuracy gains across six diverse datasets without requiring decoder retraining. The finding that explicit diversity regularization (GJSD) helps but remains inferior to the implicit effect of indirect parameterization suggests the mechanism is richer than simply preventing duplicates, motivating a formal theoretical analysis that the authors identify as future work. The approach is also noted to be generalizable to Gumbel-Softmax distributions beyond feature selection, opening a broader applicability question.
