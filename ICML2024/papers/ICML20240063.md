## Problem

In variational quantum eigensolvers (VQEs) using sequential minimal optimization (SMO/NFT), each observation requires hundreds to thousands of measurement shots to suppress shot noise, making the total quantum computation budget the dominant cost. While adaptive shot strategies existed for gradient-based methods (e.g., SGLBO), no such technique was available for SMO-based optimizers, which use a fixed number of shots per observation regardless of optimization progress. This paper asks: how can one minimize the total number of quantum measurement shots while guaranteeing that each SMO iteration is performed with a required accuracy that adapts to the optimization stage?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a quantum Hamiltonian (e.g., Heisenberg/Ising), a parameterized ansatz circuit (L-layered Efficient SU(2)), and the growing set of previous GP observations (points, values, and their shot-noise variances). At each SMO iteration it outputs the number of measurement shots to allocate to each of the 1+2V_d equidistant points along the current axis (which can differ per point), the updated best parameter vector, and an adapted CoRe threshold κ_{t+1} for the next iteration. The total quantum cost is tracked as the cumulative number of shots per operator group, and the CoRe threshold is updated via linear regression over the last T_Ave best values, with a hard cap of 1024 shots per data point.

## Method

SubsCoRe augments NFT with a GP surrogate using the VQE kernel and the confident region (CoRe) concept, requiring that the entire 1D subspace being optimized lies within the CoRe (posterior variance ≤ κ_t²) after new observations. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) proves that observing 1+2V_d equidistant points with equal shots yields a uniform posterior variance along the subspace, independent of the test location, establishing min-max optimality and drastically simplifying the shot-allocation problem. SubsCoRe-Center further exploits the fact that the previous best point already has low prior uncertainty, so it solves a 2D search over the center-point variance and the shifted-point variance to minimize total shots. The CoRe threshold κ_t is adapted each iteration by linear regression on recent best values (κ^{t+1} = max(C_0, −C_1·Slope)), using fewer shots early when the energy drops rapidly and more shots near convergence. The minimum of the GP mean along the subspace is then found analytically via trigonometric polynomial regression on the GP predictions at the 1+2V_d equidistant points.

## Results

On the Ising Hamiltonian with Q=5 qubits and L=3 circuit layers, SubsCoRe-Center achieves the lowest energy difference (ΔEnergy) and fidelity difference (ΔFidelity) from ground truth as a function of cumulative measurement shots, outperforming all baselines with statistical significance (p < 0.05, Wilcoxon signed-rank test over 100 independent trials). The adaptive shot allocation allows SubsCoRe-Center to use fewer shots in early iterations (where the best value decreases rapidly) and more shots in the converging phase, reducing total quantum computation budget. The trial density after 2.5×10⁶ shots confirms that SubsCoRe-Center concentrates more trials near the true optimum compared to baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SubsCoRe-Center outperforms all three baselines on both ΔEnergy and ΔFidelity convergence curves (Figure 3). Against NFT (fixed 1024 shots per point), SubsCoRe-Center reaches lower energy and fidelity differences with fewer total shots, demonstrating that adaptive allocation is strictly better than a fixed budget. Against SGLBO (gradient-based with adaptive shots), SubsCoRe-Center achieves faster energy convergence; SGLBO does not report fidelity since its code does not store optimal parameters. Against EMICoRe (BO-enhanced NFT with fixed 1024 shots), SubsCoRe-Center again wins on both metrics, confirming that minimizing total shots (the true quantum bottleneck) is more effective than minimizing SMO steps. In the ablation (Figure 5), SubsCoRe-Center outperforms SubsCoRe-Bound, with the gap widening in the later optimization phase where the GP already has low uncertainty at the current best point from previous observations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NFT (Nakanishi et al., 2020) sequential minimal optimization framework for VQEs by introducing adaptive observation cost control, building directly on the VQE kernel and Confident Region (CoRe) concept from Nicoli et al. (2023a). While SGLBO (Tamiya & Yamasaki, 2022) already adapted shot counts for gradient-based methods, no prior work addressed adaptive shots for SMO-based optimizers where gradients are not estimated. The paper positions SubsCoRe as orthogonal to EMICoRe: both exploit CoRe, but EMICoRe uses it for point selection (minimizing SMO steps at fixed shots) while SubsCoRe uses it for cost control (minimizing total shots regardless of step count). The key theoretical departure is Theorem 3.1, proving that equidistant observations yield uniform posterior variance under the VQE kernel—a property that drastically simplifies the shot allocation problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reducing the total quantum computation budget (total shots) is the central practical bottleneck for VQEs, and SubsCoRe provides a principled, GP-based mechanism to do so while guaranteeing optimization accuracy. The theoretical result connecting equidistant sampling to min-max optimal uniform uncertainty under the VQE kernel is elegant and may inform future VQE algorithm design beyond this specific method. However, the evaluation is limited to a single 5-qubit Ising Hamiltonian with no hardware noise, and the authors explicitly defer real-device experiments and broader Hamiltonian testing (quantum chemistry, lattice field theory) to future work. The complementary relationship with EMICoRe (point selection vs. cost control) suggests a natural but unexplored combination that could further improve efficiency.
