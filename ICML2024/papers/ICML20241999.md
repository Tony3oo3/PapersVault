## Problem

Existing RL-based fine-tuning methods for diffusion models assume a static, fully known reward function and do not address the cost of acquiring ground-truth feedback in an online setting. In domains like drug discovery, protein design, and aesthetic image generation, reward queries are expensive (wet-lab experiments, human judgment), and the vast design space contains many infeasible samples (unnatural images, physically impossible molecules) that yield no meaningful reward. The paper asks: how can one fine-tune a diffusion model to maximize reward while minimizing the number of expensive reward queries and staying on the feasible manifold?

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained diffusion model specified by an SDE drift f^pre and initial distribution ν^pre (whose support defines the feasible space X_pre), plus an oracle that returns noisy scalar feedback y = r(x) + ε for any queried sample x. At each of K online iterations, the algorithm generates one or a batch of samples from the current model, queries the oracle, and updates both a reward model and the diffusion model without additional queries. The output is a sequence of fine-tuned drift terms f^(i) and initial distributions ν^(i) whose terminal-time marginals p^(i) concentrate on high-reward, feasible samples; the final model is used for generation. The reward r is defined as 0 outside X_pre, so infeasible samples are automatically penalized.

## Method

SEIKO is an iterative algorithm that interleaves three steps per epoch: (i) sample from the current diffusion model and query the reward oracle, (ii) retrain a reward model r̂ and an uncertainty oracle ĝ (via ridge regression/UCB or bootstrap) on the accumulated dataset, and (iii) update the diffusion model by solving a control problem that maximizes the expected optimistic reward (r̂ + ĝ) at the terminal time, minus α·KL to the pre-trained model and β·KL to the previous iteration's model. The key theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows this control problem admits a closed-form solution: the fine-tuned distribution p^(i) is proportional to exp((r̂+ĝ)/(α+β)) · {p^(i-1)}^{β/(α+β)} · {p^pre}^{α/(α+β)}, a geometric mean that provably keeps the support within the feasible manifold while the uncertainty bonus drives exploration toward under-covered regions. A regret guarantee (Theorem 2) bounds the performance gap to any target distribution by the expected uncertainty, yielding Õ(d/√K) for linear reward models. In practice, the control problem is solved via stochastic gradient descent on the SDE drift parameterized by a neural network, using Euler-Maruyama discretization and the Adam optimizer.

## Results

On the GFP protein sequence task (M=2000 feedback queries), SEIKO with bootstrap uncertainty achieves a value of 3.87±0.01, outperforming the best baseline (Non-adaptive at 3.66±0.03) by ~5.7%. On the image aesthetic quality task (Stable Diffusion v1.5, M=15360 total queries), SEIKO with UCB achieves a value of 8.17±0.33, surpassing the best baseline (Non-adaptive at 7.22±0.18) by ~13.2%. The method also maintains competitive diversity (Div=2.2–2.6) on the GFP task, confirming that exploration does not collapse the generated distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEIKO consistently outperforms all four baseline categories on both the GFP protein and image aesthetic tasks. Against non-adaptive methods (Clark et al. 2023; Prabhudesai et al. 2023), SEIKO-UCB gains +0.95 on images (8.17 vs 7.22) and +0.18 on GFP (3.84 vs 3.66), demonstrating the value of adaptive online data collection over a single static reward model. Against classifier guidance (Dhariwal & Nichol 2021), the advantage is even larger on images (+2.39, 8.17 vs 5.78), confirming that gradient-based steering without iterative reward learning is substantially less feedback-efficient. Against online RL baselines (Online PPO, which subsumes DPOK and DDPO), SEIKO-UCB leads by +2.04 on images (8.17 vs 6.13) and +0.21 on GFP (3.84 vs 3.63), attributing the gain to the interleaved reward-model updates and KL-regularized optimistic exploration. The Greedy ablation (removing both KL and optimism) trails SEIKO-UCB by +1.93 on images and +0.22 on GFP, isolating the contribution of the exploration mechanism. On the GFP task, the Bootstrap variant (3.87) slightly edges out UCB (3.84), while on images UCB (8.17) clearly dominates Bootstrap (7.77), suggesting the uncertainty estimator's effectiveness is domain-dependent.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEIKO builds directly on Uehara et al. (2024), which formulated diffusion model fine-tuning as an entropy-regularized control problem, and on the soft-entropy-regularized MDP framework of Neu et al. (2017) and Geist et al. (2019). The key departure from prior RL-based fine-tuning methods (DDPO, DPOK, reward backpropagation) is the introduction of an online, feedback-efficient setting where the reward model is learned iteratively alongside the diffusion model, rather than being fixed a priori. The method also draws on bandit literature (UCB, bootstrap) for the optimistic exploration component, but adapts it to the high-dimensional, manifold-constrained setting of diffusion models via KL regularization to the pre-trained model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to provide a provable regret guarantee for online fine-tuning of diffusion models, addressing a practical bottleneck in biology and chemistry where reward queries (wet-lab experiments) are extremely expensive. The bridge-preserving property (Theorem 1) elegantly shows that KL regularization to the pre-trained model inherently constrains exploration to the feasible manifold without explicit validity checks. Limitations include reliance on solving a trajectory-level control problem at each iteration (computationally expensive), experiments on relatively small-scale tasks (GFP, aesthetic scores, QED), and the assumption of a single scalar reward. Open questions include scaling to multi-objective rewards, larger foundation models, and settings where the feasible manifold itself is uncertain.
