## Problem

While single-agent jailbreaking of multimodal LLMs has been well studied, the safety implications of deploying MLLM agents in multi-agent environments with shared memory and pairwise communication remain unexplored. This paper identifies a previously unnoticed 'infectious jailbreak' paradigm in which an adversary needs to jailbreak only a single agent, after which the adversarial image propagates autonomously through agent-to-agent interactions, infecting (almost) all agents exponentially fast without any further adversary intervention.

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean RGB image (resized to 336×336, sampled from ArtBench) and M=512 multi-agent chat records (sampled from N=64 agents running 32 benign chat rounds) as optimization inputs, and produces a single adversarial image V^adv constrained either by an ℓ∞ pixel budget (ε/255) or a border-width budget h. The adversarial image is injected into one agent's FIFO image album (|B|=10) in a multi-agent system of up to 1 million LLaVA-1.5 agents, and the output is the exponential spread of exact-match harmful strings (drawn from AdvBench's non-violating set) or harmful JSON function-calling payloads across all agents within O(log N) chat rounds.

## Method

The paper formalizes the infectious dynamics of randomized pairwise chat as a logistic-type differential equation dc/dt = βc(1−c)/2 − γc, proving that when the transmission rate β exceeds twice the recovery rate γ, the virus-carrying ratio converges to 1−2γ/β and the number of rounds to infect N agents from a single seed scales as O(log N). To craft the adversarial image, the authors jointly optimize three losses via MI-FGSM (BIM with momentum): a CLIP retrieval loss L_R ensuring the image is the top-ranked result in the RAG module for any plan, a cross-entropy loss L_Q forcing the MLLM to generate a target harmful question regardless of chat history, and a cross-entropy loss L_A forcing a target harmful answer. The key mechanism is that the image acts as a self-propagating virus: when an infected agent chats, the RAG module retrieves V^adv, the image is transmitted to the answering agent and enqueued into their album, making them infected in turn—requiring no further adversary action. The paper also derives a provable defense principle: any mechanism that ensures β ≤ 2γ (e.g., faster recovery or lower transmission) will drive the infection ratio to zero asymptotically.

## Results

With N=256 agents, the infectious jailbreak achieves a cumulative infection ratio of 93.75% by chat round 16 (border attack, h=6, low diversity), reaching 90% cumulative infection at round 15 and 95% at round 17. Scaling to N=1,000,000 agents (with c₀=1/1024), nearly all agents are jailbroken before the 32nd chat round, confirming the O(log N) scaling predicted by theory. Under high textual chat diversity, performance degrades modestly (e.g., cumulative p₁₆ drops from 93.75% to 88.98% for border h=6), but the method still reaches ~99% cumulative infection by round 24.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three non-infectious baselines in the multi-agent environment (Figure 3, Left). Visual Prompt Injection (VP, based on Timbrell 2023) and Textual Prompt Injection (TP) both fail to jailbreak even a single agent, achieving 0% infection across all 32 chat rounds. Sequential jailbreak (using adversarial images from Zhao et al. 2023 or prompts from Zou et al. 2023, one agent per round) achieves only ~12.5% cumulative infection after 32 rounds, exhibiting a linear O(N) rate. In stark contrast, the infectious jailbreak reaches ~100% cumulative infection in just 15–17 rounds (O(log N)), representing an exponential speedup. The paper further shows that even with image corruptions (flip, resize, JPEG) and heterogeneous agent backbones (LLaVA-1.5 + InstructBLIP), the infectious method still achieves near-complete infection, while the baselines remain ineffective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes adversarial image optimization (MI-FGSM with border/pixel constraints) from the single-agent MLLM jailbreaking literature (Zhao et al. 2023; Qi et al. 2023a) and combines it with the retrieval-augmented memory mechanisms found in multi-agent LLM frameworks (Park et al. 2023; CAMEL; AutoGen) to introduce a fundamentally new attack paradigm: infectious jailbreak. Rather than building on a single prior method, it draws from three distinct threads—adversarial optimization, multi-agent interaction design, and epidemic dynamics modeling—to formalize how a single adversarial image propagates through agent-to-agent chat. The key conceptual departure from prior jailbreaking work is the shift from targeting one model to exploiting the interaction graph and shared memory banks of an entire agent population, with the propagation dynamics analyzed via a continuous-time SIR-like differential equation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that reckless large-scale deployment of MLLM agents with shared memory and retrieval creates a previously unrecognized vulnerability class where a single injected image can compromise an entire population in O(log N) rounds—meaning a billion-agent system needs only ~14 more rounds than a million-agent one. The derived provable defense principle (ensure β ≤ 2γ, i.e., recovery rate must exceed half the infection rate) gives a clear but currently unmet target for defense research; no practical defense satisfying this condition is provided. Key limitations include the proof-of-concept nature of the randomized pairwise chat simulation, the exact-match evaluation criterion that likely underestimates real-world effectiveness, and the requirement that the adversarial image be both stored in and retrieved from an agent's album. The open question of designing practical defenses that provably satisfy β ≤ 2γ while preserving agent utility is urgent as multi-agent MLLM systems scale toward billions of deployments.
