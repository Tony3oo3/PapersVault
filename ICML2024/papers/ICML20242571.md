## Problem

Applying policy gradient methods to SDE-based generative models is unstable because gradients estimated on a finite set of trajectories are ill-defined in data-scarce regions, and the policy exhibits uncontrolled behavior in those regions. This leads to poor sample complexity and training instability that has hindered the use of RL to optimize SDEs for reward-driven generation tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242571/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For structure-based drug design, the method takes a 3D protein pocket as input and generates 3D ligand molecules (atom coordinates and types) optimized for binding affinity, evaluated via AutoDock Vina scores. For text-to-image generation, it takes a text prompt and produces images fine-tuned to maximize ImageReward scores. In both cases, the SDE operates in continuous time from t=1 (Gaussian prior) to t=0 (generated sample), with the state space being molecule atom positions/types or image latent representations.

## Method

DiffAC enforces consistency between the backward SDE (the policy) and its associated forward perturbation process via score matching, ensuring that perturbing generated samples through the forward process yields valid training data covering the entire space. This allows the critic V_φ(x_t, t) to be trained on abundantly sampled perturbed states rather than sparse SDE trajectories, yielding well-defined policy gradients. The paper derives SDE-REINFORCE and SDE-DDPG estimators (Theorem 4.3) that compute gradients using one-step transitions from perturbed states, and introduces a KL-regularized actor update (Algorithm 2) that maintains approximate consistency during RL fine-tuning. A theoretical bound (Theorem 4.5) quantifies the policy gradient approximation error in terms of per-step KL divergences between the consistent reference SDE and the optimized policy.

## Results

On the CrossDocked2020 dataset, DiffAC achieves the best average Vina Score of -9.07±1.99 (Best Run) and -8.50±2.11 (Last Run), outperforming all baselines. The strongest baseline, Online EEGSDE-0.1, reaches -8.58±1.70 (Best Run) and -7.78±2.33 (Last Run), yielding a gap of 0.49 and 0.72 respectively. DiffAC also converges faster than all online optimization algorithms, demonstrating superior sample efficiency. On the text-to-image generation task (Appendix G), DiffAC outperforms DPOK by a large margin in ImageReward scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffAC outperforms all five baselines on the CrossDocked2020 structure-based drug design task. Against the base diffusion model TargetDiff (Guan et al., 2023), DiffAC improves the average Vina Score from -5.45 to -9.07, a gain of 3.62. Compared to the energy-guided EEGSDE variants (Bao et al., 2023), DiffAC surpasses the best offline variant (EEGSDE-0.1, -6.53) by 2.54 and the best online variant (Online EEGSDE-0.1, -8.58) by 0.49 in Best Run average. The autoregressive AR model (Luo et al., 2021) and Pocket2Mol (Peng et al., 2022) trail significantly at -5.75 and -5.14 respectively. On the text-to-image generation task, DiffAC (using REINFORCE with LoRA on Stable Diffusion v1.5) outperforms DPOK (Fan et al., 2023) by a large margin in ImageReward scores, confirming the generalizability of the consistency-based stabilization beyond molecular generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffAC sits at the intersection of SDE-based generative modeling (diffusion models) and policy gradient RL, building directly on the SDE-as-MDP formulation of Black et al. (2023) and Fan et al. (2023) (DDPO, DPOK), which first applied REINFORCE-style updates to pretrained diffusion models. It also extends the energy-guided sampling paradigm of EEGSDE (Bao et al., 2023) by replacing fixed-coefficient guidance with a fully online actor-critic loop. The key conceptual departure is the 'consistency' constraint (Definition 4.1): by requiring the backward SDE to remain consistent with its forward perturbation process, DiffAC can sample training data from the cheap forward process rather than the expensive backward simulation, resolving the ill-defined-gradient and data-scarce-region problems that prior RL-on-diffusion methods left unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework is genuinely general—it works with any SDE parameterization and any policy gradient variant (REINFORCE, DDPG, and by extension PPO), and the KL-regularization in Algorithm 2 provides a practical mechanism to maintain approximate consistency during online training, backed by a theoretical error bound (Theorem 4.5). The SOTA Vina score of -9.07 on CrossDocked2020 and the positive text-to-image transfer (Stable Diffusion + LoRA) suggest broad applicability to reward-optimized generation. Open questions include: how the method scales to higher-dimensional or longer-horizon SDEs, whether the consistency constraint is strictly necessary or merely helpful (the ablation in Appendix F shows sensitivity to the regularization coefficient η₂), and how it interacts with more sophisticated RL algorithms like PPO or offline RL with diffusion policies.
