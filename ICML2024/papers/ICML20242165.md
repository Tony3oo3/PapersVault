## Problem

Despite CNNs outperforming kernel methods and expertly designed features on image tasks, the specific low-dimensional structure they learn in their middle layers during feature learning remained uncharacterized. While bottleneck structures in fully-connected networks had been identified, the analogous question for CNNs—where translation invariance, weight-sharing, and pooling impose additional constraints on which representations are cheap to maintain—was open.

## Input / Output

The method operates on 2D images processed with full-size cyclic convolution filters and a user-specified pooling operator M_β = (1−β)I + βA₃. Inputs range from 28×28 MNIST digits (with global average pooling and a final FC layer for classification), 13×13 downscaled digit-0 images (autoencoding), synthetic composites of low-frequency shapes multiplied by a single high-frequency (5,5) pattern, to 4-frame ball trajectories under gravity. The key diagnostic output is the emergent frequency structure: singular values of MW_ℓ across all layers, colored by their DFT frequency index ω, revealing which few frequencies are retained in the bottleneck.

## Method

The paper defines the Convolution Bottleneck Rank (CBN rank) as the minimum pooling-weighted count of DFT frequencies needed in a factorization f = h∘g where g's channels are supported on finitely many frequencies, and proves the leading-order representation cost satisfies R(f;Ω,L) = L·Rank_CBN + R^(1) + o(1). The upper bound is constructive (identity layers on low-frequency representations cost Σ m̃_t^{-2} per kept frequency), while the lower bound follows from the Jacobian rank at constant-channel inputs, with the finite-depth correction R^(1) controlling regularity via weighted log-singular-values of the Jacobian. The paper then proves that any network with near-optimal parameter norm must have its weight matrices concentrated on a few DFT frequencies (Theorem 4.1), and under a large-learning-rate stability assumption, the activations are also supported on those same frequencies (Theorem 4.2). The analysis extends to downsampled CNNs, showing the CBN rank is invariant to downsampling when the target admits a low-frequency bottleneck, thereby justifying the common practice of strided convolutions. Numerical experiments on four tasks confirm the predicted structure: MNIST classification retains 6 constant frequencies, the digit-0 autoencoder keeps 3 constant + 4 degree-1 + 1 degree-2 frequencies, the synthetic task disentangles shape (‖ω‖₁≤2) from pattern (ω=(5,5)), and the Newtonian mechanics task encodes position in phase and velocity in phase difference of two degree-1 frequency pairs.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) establishes that the representation cost of a depth-L CNN satisfies R(f;Ω,L) ≤ L · Rank_CBN(f;Ω) + c, where the CBN rank counts the number and type of Fourier frequencies retained in the bottleneck, weighted by the inverse squared pooling response. Theorem 3.2 provides matching lower bounds via the Jacobian rank, and for linear one-layer CNNs the upper and lower bounds coincide exactly. Numerically, a depth-11 CNN on MNIST classification retains only 6 constant frequencies in its bottleneck (sufficient for 10-class linear separability), while a depth-12 autoencoder on digit-0 images retains 3 constant + 4 degree-1 + 1 degree-2 frequencies, each interpretable as encoding size, boldness, angle, or position.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without direct method-to-method comparisons; the `compares_to` list is empty. The numerical experiments serve as validation of the theory rather than benchmarking against competing architectures. The key empirical finding is that L2-regularized CNNs spontaneously develop the predicted bottleneck structure (few large singular values in middle-layer weights concentrated at low frequencies) without any explicit architectural constraint, confirming that the CBN rank governs the implicit bias. The paper also shows that adding explicit down-sampling/up-sampling layers does not change the R^(0) cost for functions with a natural low-frequency bottleneck (Theorem 5.5), justifying the common practice of strided convolutions. The Newtonian mechanics experiment demonstrates that the network autonomously discovers that position and velocity (encoded as phases of two degree-1 frequency pairs) are the minimal sufficient statistics for trajectory prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bottleneck structure analysis for fully-connected networks (Jacot, 2023a,b) to CNNs, introducing the Convolution Bottleneck (CBN) rank as the natural analogue of the FC-NN rank. A key departure from the FC-NN setting is that CNNs favor representations supported along a finite number of *frequencies* (with a preference for low frequencies when pooling is present), rather than arbitrary low-dimensional subspaces. The representation cost framework follows Gunasekar et al. (2018b) and Dai et al. (2021), while the activation boundedness argument draws on the large-learning-rate implicit bias results of Jacot (2023b). The paper also positions itself against kernel-method analyses of CNNs (NNGP/NTK), arguing that feature learning captures structure beyond what the architecture's inductive bias alone provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CBN framework provides a theoretical justification for the widespread practice of downsampling in CNNs: the L2-regularized network spontaneously concentrates its middle representations on a few low frequencies, and explicit down/up-sampling layers enforce this structure at reduced computational cost without increasing the asymptotic representation cost. The interpretability of the learned bottleneck features (e.g., position and velocity encoded in specific frequency phases for the Newtonian mechanics task) offers a concrete lens for understanding what CNNs actually learn. Key open questions include whether gradient descent provably converges to such low-norm solutions, whether the results extend to non-cyclic boundaries and local (non-full-size) filters, and whether the conjectured equality R^(0) = Rank_CBN holds in full generality.
