## Problem

Behavioral distance-based RL algorithms learn representations by enforcing an isometric (globally distance-preserving) mapping between the behavioral distance space and a low-dimensional embedding space. However, such an isometric embedding may not exist for arbitrary behavioral distances, making the learning objective ill-defined and potentially causing training instability when combined with bootstrapping. The paper asks whether a locality-preserving relaxation—allowing distortion in long-range distances while preserving local metric structure—can yield better representations that highlight natural value-based clusters in the state space.

## Input / Output

The method consumes stacked RGB frames (3 consecutive 88×88×3 frames, yielding a 9×88×88 observation tensor) and produces a 50-dimensional state embedding via a 4-layer CNN encoder (32 filters, 3×3 kernels) followed by L2 batch normalization. The embedding is used as a drop-in replacement for the representational distance in existing behavioral distance algorithms (DBC, R-DBC, KSME, RAP), which then feed it into a Soft Actor-Critic policy to produce action distributions. The kernel bandwidth η is set via the median-heuristic on the batch of target behavioral distances.

## Method

BeigeMaps replace the isometric (Isomap) embedding loss in behavioral distance algorithms with a locality-preserving objective derived from Laplacian eigenmaps. Concretely, given a batch of target behavioral distances d*, the method constructs a Gaussian kernel K = exp(−η·d*²), applies symmetric normalization K_sym = D^(−1/2) K D^(−1/2), and optimizes the NeuralEF loss (a differentiable eigenfunction approximation objective) over this normalized kernel using a neural network encoder. The orthonormality constraints of the eigenmap formulation—enforced via an orthogonality penalty and L2 batch normalization—naturally prevent the feature map explosion and collapse pathologies that plague prior behavioral distance methods. The approach is a drop-in modification: only the embedding update step (loss function) changes, while the target distance computation, policy learning (SAC), and auxiliary models (dynamics, reward) remain identical to the base algorithm.

## Results

On the DeepMind Control Suite (7 environments, normalized returns in [0,1]), BeigeMap variants achieve higher interquartile median (IQM) returns than their respective baselines across all four behavioral distance algorithms (DBC, R-DBC, KSME, RAP). The improvements are most pronounced for DBC+BeigeMap, R-DBC+BeigeMap, and KSME+BeigeMap, which stochastically dominate their baselines in performance profiles. RAP+BeigeMap also improves over RAP but with some confidence-interval overlap. Training cost is essentially unchanged (0.0823 vs. 0.0824 s/step for BeigeMap vs. Isomap variants), and the worst-performing BeigeMap model (R-DBC+BeigeMap) still outperforms the average baseline model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BeigeMaps are evaluated as a drop-in replacement for the isometric embedding objective in four behavioral distance algorithms: DBC (Zhang et al., 2020), R-DBC (Kemertas & Aumentado-Armstrong, 2021), KSME (Castro et al., 2023, used as a proxy for MICO), and RAP (Chen & Pan, 2022). DBC+BeigeMap, R-DBC+BeigeMap, and KSME+BeigeMap all stochastically dominate their baselines in performance profiles and show clear IQM improvements, with the gap being largest for DBC and KSME. RAP+BeigeMap improves over RAP in average returns but with overlapping confidence intervals, and its probability-of-improvement CI dips slightly below 0.5. Vanilla SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018) is included as a reference and is outperformed by all behavioral distance variants. Notably, R-DBC+BeigeMap outperforms R-DBC despite R-DBC's explicit anti-explosion/anti-collapse heuristics, because BeigeMap's orthonormality constraints inherently prevent both failure modes (embedding norms converge to √D ≈ 7.1 rather than growing unboundedly).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the behavioral distance representation learning line (DBC, R-DBC, MICO, KSME, RAP) by replacing their shared isometric embedding objective with a locality-preserving Laplacian eigenmap objective. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate threads: spectral dimensionality reduction (Belkin & Niyogi, 2001) and bisimulation-metric-based state aggregation (Ferns et al., 2004; Comanici & Precup, 2011), while using NeuralEF (Deng et al., 2022b) to enable out-of-sample generalization in continuous state spaces. The key departure is the explicit argument that global isometry is ill-defined for behavioral distances, motivating a relaxation that preserves local structure while permitting long-range distortion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BeigeMaps offer a principled, drop-in fix for a fundamental ill-posedness issue in behavioral distance algorithms, and the orthonormality constraints incidentally resolve known training instabilities (explosion/collapse) without ad-hoc heuristics. The paper's scope is limited to DMC continuous control with dense rewards; it does not test the distractor-robustness benefits that prior behavioral distance work emphasizes, nor does it explore the regularization-operator interpretation the authors mention as future work. Open questions include whether the locality-preserving objective generalizes to sparse-reward or multi-task settings, and whether the kernel bandwidth choice (median heuristic) is sensitive in high-dimensional observation spaces.
