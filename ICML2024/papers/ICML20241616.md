## Problem

Global convergence guarantees for average-reward policy gradient methods have required oracle knowledge of mixing time, making them impractical for complex environments where mixing time is expensive or infeasible to estimate. While PPGAE (Bai et al., 2024) first established global optimality for average-reward PG, its trajectory length scales as O(τ_hit · τ_mix · √T), rendering it computationally infeasible. The MAC algorithm avoids mixing time oracles but lacked a global convergence analysis, leaving an open gap in the theory of average-reward PG methods.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a finite-state MDP with a parameterized policy π_θ (no restriction on parameterization form) and a linearly parameterized critic V_ω(s) = ⟨φ(s), ω⟩ with ‖φ(s)‖ ≤ 1. Trajectory lengths at each step are drawn from a geometric distribution Geom(1/2) capped at T_max, so no mixing time or hitting time values are needed to configure the algorithm. The output is a sequence of policy parameters θ_t with a proven global convergence rate of Õ(T^{-1/4}) and the tightest known mixing time dependence of Õ(√τ_mix), plus a corrected reward-tracking error bound that removes an extraneous square root from prior analysis.

## Method

The paper extends the global convergence framework of Bai et al. (2024) to the MAC algorithm by deriving a modified general framework (Lemma 6) that accommodates the non-constant AdaGrad stepsizes inherent to MLMC gradient estimation. The key mechanism is that MAC draws trajectory lengths from a geometric distribution, which decouples trajectory length from mixing time entirely, while the MLMC estimator achieves the same bias as averaging T gradients using only Õ(1) samples. The proof combines the MLMC variance bound (Lemma 2), the local convergence rate of MAC (Lemma 4), and a telescoping KL-divergence argument to bound the gap J* − (1/T)ΣJ(θ_t). A secondary contribution corrects an error in Suttle et al. (2023) where the reward-tracking error term should be Õ(√(τ_mix log T_max / T_max)) rather than Õ(τ_mix log T_max / T_max), which propagates to tighten the final convergence bound.

## Results

The paper establishes the first global convergence guarantee for an average-reward policy gradient method without oracle knowledge of mixing time, achieving a convergence rate of Õ(T^{-1/4}) with the tightest known mixing time dependence of Õ(√τ_mix). In a 15×15 sparse gridworld goal-reaching task (200 samples per episode, 300 training episodes, 100 trials), MAC achieves a higher moving-average success rate than PPGAE, Vanilla AC, and REINFORCE, with PPGAE, Vanilla AC, and REINFORCE consistently converging to significantly less optimal solutions. The practical infeasibility of PPGAE is highlighted: even with τ_hit=10 and τ_mix=60, the minimum trajectory length H required for a single gradient update is approximately 10^14 samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAC outperforms PPGAE (Bai et al., 2024) on both theoretical and empirical grounds: MAC achieves a tighter mixing time dependence of Õ(√τ_mix) versus PPGAE's Õ(τ_mix²), requires no oracle knowledge of mixing time or hitting time, and achieves a higher success rate in the 15×15 gridworld experiment. PPGAE's trajectory length H = 16τ_hit·τ_mix·√T·(log T)² makes it practically infeasible for moderate mixing times (H ≈ 10^14 at τ_mix = 60), whereas MAC uses a geometrically-distributed trajectory length independent of mixing time. MAC also outperforms REINFORCE (Williams, 1992) and Vanilla AC in the gridworld experiment, with both baselines converging to significantly less optimal solutions. The key practical advantage is that MAC's trajectory length depends only on a geometric random variable (J_t ~ Geom(1/2)) capped at T_max, rather than on quantities that are expensive or infeasible to estimate in complex environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two complementary lines of work on average-reward policy gradient methods: the global convergence framework of Bai et al. (2024) (PPGAE), which requires oracle knowledge of mixing time and hitting time, and the Multi-level Actor-Critic (MAC) algorithm of Suttle et al. (2023), which uses MLMC gradient estimation to avoid such oracle knowledge but previously lacked a global convergence guarantee. The authors adapt the non-constant-stepsized general framework from Bai et al. (2024) to accommodate the AdaGrad stepsizes used in MAC, and additionally correct a technical error in the reward-tracking analysis of Suttle et al. (2023). The MLMC machinery itself draws on Dorfman & Levy (2022) for adapting to unknown mixing times in stochastic optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first global convergence guarantee for an average-reward PG method that does not require oracle knowledge of mixing time, closing a notable gap in the theory of average-reward RL. The tighter O(√τ_mix) dependence (versus O(τ_mix²) for PPGAE) is practically significant: PPGAE's trajectory length H scales as τ_hit·τ_mix·√T·(log T)², making it infeasible even at moderate mixing times (H ~ 10¹⁴ at τ_mix = 60), while MAC's geometrically-distributed trajectory lengths have no such dependence. Key open questions include whether the O(T^{-1/2}) rate achieved by Ganesh et al. (2024) with mixing time knowledge can be matched without it, and whether MAC's advantages persist in genuinely slow-mixing environments beyond the small gridworld tested here.
