## Problem

Proximal samplers achieve much faster convergence than Langevin-based algorithms in the deterministic setting, but their stochastic variants—where only mini-batch gradients are available—remain entirely unexplored. Prior attempts at stochastic proximal sampling (Altschuler & Chewi, 2023) require stationary points of the energy function f, which is infeasible for non-log-concave targets where finding ε-approximate stationary points costs O(ε^{-4}) oracle calls.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a finite-sum negative log-density f(x) = (1/n)Σf_i(x) accessed only through mini-batch stochastic gradients ∇f_b(x), an initial particle x_0 drawn from the standard Gaussian on R^d, and a mini-batch b ⊆ {1,...,n} drawn at each outer iteration. It outputs a single particle x_K after K outer iterations, each of which contains an inner sampling loop (SGLD or MALA) that approximately samples from a strongly log-concave conditional distribution. The convergence guarantee is stated in total variation distance TV(p̂_K, p_*) < ε, with gradient complexity measured as the total number of stochastic gradient evaluations across all inner and outer loops.

## Method

The Stochastic Proximal Sampler (SPS) framework replaces the deterministic joint distribution with a randomized one p_*(x,y|b) ∝ exp(−f_b(x) − ‖x−y‖²/(2η)), then performs two-stage alternating sampling: a Gaussian step y|x ~ N(x, ηI) followed by an inner sampler that approximately draws x|y from the strongly log-concave conditional. The key theoretical insight is that by initializing the inner sampler with a Gaussian centered at the previous particle (rather than at a stationary point of f), the per-step KL error δ_k can be bounded via the second moment of the particle trajectory, eliminating the need for stationary point information. Two concrete inner samplers are instantiated: SPS-SGLD (using a two-step SGLD update with trajectory averaging) achieving Õ(dε^{-2}) gradient complexity, and SPS-MALA (using warm-started MALA with an underdamped Langevin warm start) achieving Õ(d^{1/2}ε^{-2}), both improving over the state-of-the-art CC-SGLD bound of Õ(d^{4/3}ε^{-2}) by at least an Õ(d^{1/3}) factor under strictly weaker assumptions.

## Results

The paper establishes gradient complexity bounds for two stochastic proximal sampler variants: SPS-SGLD achieves $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(d\epsilon^{-2})$ and SPS-MALA achieves $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(d^{1/2}\epsilon^{-2})$ to reach $\epsilon$-sampling error in total variation distance, under LSI and component smoothness assumptions. These improve upon the state-of-the-art CC-SGLD bound of $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(d^{4/3}\epsilon^{-2})$ by at least an $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(d^{1/3})$ factor. Empirically, SPS-SGLD achieves at least 0.5 lower TV distance than vanilla SGLD across all tested dimensions (d=10 to 50) on a synthetic multi-modal target, with 12,000 stochastic gradient oracles and mini-batch size 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPS-SGLD ($\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(d\epsilon^{-2})$) outperforms CC-SGLD ($\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(d^{4/3}\epsilon^{-2})$) by an $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(d^{1/3})$ factor and AB-SGLD ($\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(d^{3/2}\epsilon^{-2})$) by an $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(d^{1/2})$ factor, while requiring strictly weaker assumptions (component smoothness vs. 6th moment or full smoothness). SPS-MALA ($\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(d^{1/2}\epsilon^{-2})$) further improves over SPS-SGLD by an $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(d^{1/2})$ factor when $\sigma^2=\Theta(1)$, though its advantage diminishes when $\sigma^2=\Theta(d)$ where it matches AB-SGLD's $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(d^{3/2}\epsilon^{-2})$. Compared to the earlier SGLD analyses of Raginsky et al. (2017) and Zou et al. (2021), SPS methods require only LSI (weaker than dissipative) and achieve polynomially better dimension dependence. Empirically, SPS-SGLD consistently achieves lower TV distance than vanilla SGLD across all dimensions tested, with the gap being at least 0.5 in TV units, and produces samples that better cover all modes of the multi-modal target distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deterministic proximal sampler of Lee et al. (2021) to the stochastic gradient setting, filling a gap that had been identified but unaddressed. It departs from Altschuler & Chewi (2023), which studied inexact proximal oracles but required access to stationary points of f, by replacing that requirement with second-moment control of the particle trajectory. The two concrete implementations (SPS-SGLD and SPS-MALA) draw on standard inner samplers—SGLD and warm-started MALA—but embed them within the proximal two-stage framework, yielding better dimension dependence than prior stochastic Langevin-based methods (Das et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the stationary-point requirement and providing expectation-based (rather than high-probability) convergence guarantees, the SPS framework makes proximal sampling practically implementable for non-log-concave targets where finding stationary points is prohibitively expensive. The d^{1/3} improvement in gradient complexity over the best-known SGLD variants is meaningful for high-dimensional Bayesian inference and posterior sampling. However, experiments are limited to synthetic multi-modal distributions, and the SPS-MALA complexity degrades to O~(d^{3/2}ε^{-2}) when variance scales with dimension, leaving open whether the theoretical gains persist on real-world problems. A natural next step is extending the framework to other non-Langevin samplers (HMC, diffusion-based MCMC) in the stochastic setting.
