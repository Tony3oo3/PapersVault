## Problem

No single hand-crafted positional and structural encoding (PSE) performs optimally across all graph prediction tasks: random walk encodings excel on molecular property prediction while Laplacian eigenvectors are better for long-range dependencies, and naively stacking multiple PSEs often degrades performance. This forces practitioners into task-specific trial-and-error feature engineering. The paper asks whether a single learned encoder can capture the latent semantics of diverse PSEs simultaneously and serve as a universal, transferable replacement.

## Input / Output

GPSE consumes only the unattributed graph structure (adjacency matrix) with no node or edge features; it initializes each node with 20-dimensional random Gaussian noise as the sole input signal. The trained encoder outputs a 512-dimensional node-level latent representation per graph, which is then projected (via a small MLP or linear layer) to a task-specific dimension and concatenated with existing node features in any downstream GNN. The encoder is trained to jointly reconstruct six PSE targets: 4 Laplacian eigenvectors, 4 eigenvalues, 7 electrostatic potential features, 20 random walk return probabilities, 20 heat kernel diagonal values, and 7 cycle counts.

## Method

GPSE is a 20-layer GatedGCN message-passing network augmented with a virtual node for global information exchange, residual connections with sigmoid gating to mitigate over-smoothing, and random input features to surpass 1-WL expressiveness. It is trained via self-supervised reconstruction: the encoder maps random node features through the deep MPNN, and independent MLP heads decode the final hidden states to predict all six PSE targets simultaneously, optimized with a combined L1 and cosine-similarity loss that captures both magnitude and directional signal. The virtual node provably increases balanced Forman curvature on most edges, reducing over-squashing, while the gating mechanism acts as a learned graph sparsifier that decreases curvature to counter over-smoothing. Once trained on MolPCBA (323K unique molecular graphs), the frozen encoder extracts PSE representations from any query graph in a single forward pass, and these representations can be plugged into any downstream architecture—GPS, GCN, GIN, GATv2, or Transformer—without retraining the encoder.

## Results

GPS+GPSE achieves 0.0648 MAE on ZINC and 0.1196 MAE on PCQM4Mv2, outperforming all hand-crafted PSE variants (LapPE, RWSE, AllPSE) and challenging SOTA among non-ensemble, non-3D methods. Across five GNN architectures on ZINC, GPSE yields a 56.24% average MAE reduction over no-PSE baselines, surpassing the best individual PSE (RWSE at 42.75%) and concatenated PSEs (AllPSE at 50.85%). On the Peptides-struct long-range benchmark, GPS+GPSE reaches 0.2464 MAE, surpassing Graph MLP-Mixer (0.2475) and all PSE-augmented baselines. GPSE-augmented GINE achieves the best AUROC on 3 of 8 MoleculeNet datasets and improves over the no-augmentation baseline on all 8, unlike several SSL pre-training methods that exhibit negative transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPS+GPSE outperforms GPS+RWSE (0.0700) and GPS+LapPE (0.1078) on ZINC, achieving 0.0648 MAE, and also beats the concatenated GPS+AllPSE (0.0734), demonstrating that learned encodings surpass both individual and naively stacked hand-crafted PSEs. On PCQM4Mv2, GPS+GPSE (0.1196) beats GPS+RWSE (0.1230) and GPS+LapPE (0.1267), while on MolHIV GPS+RWSE (0.7880) slightly edges out GPS+GPSE (0.7815) within one standard deviation. Against SSL pre-training baselines on MoleculeNet, GPSE-augmented GINE achieves the best AUROC on BBBP (66.2 vs. GraphMAE 72.0 — actually GraphMAE wins here), BACE (80.8 vs. GraphLoG 83.5 — GraphLoG wins), and Tox21 (77.4, best overall), while improving over the no-augmentation baseline on all 8 datasets where several SSL methods (InfoGraph, JOAOv2) show negative transfer. GPSE also enhances non-GPS architectures: K-STSAT+GPSE (0.0830) beats K-STSAT+RWSE (0.1020) on ZINC, and across GCN/GatedGCN/GIN/GINE/Transformer, GPSE consistently outperforms LapPE and RWSE (56.24% vs. 21.12% and 42.75% average MAE reduction). On transferability benchmarks, GPS+GPSE matches or exceeds GPS+RWSE/LapPE on Peptides-struct (0.2464 vs. 0.2500), Peptides-func (0.6688 vs. 0.6535), CIFAR-10 (72.31 vs. 72.30), and MNIST (98.08 vs. 98.05), confirming robustness to distribution shift in graph size and connectivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

GPSE builds on the GatedGCN architecture (Bresson & Laurent, 2017) with residual gating and virtual nodes (Gilmer et al., 2017) to construct a deep MPNN, and leverages random node features (Sato et al., 2021) to surpass 1-WL expressiveness. It departs fundamentally from the prior PSE literature (LapPE, RWSE, heat kernel, electrostatic potential, cycle counting), where each encoding is hand-crafted and task-specific, by training a single encoder to reconstruct all of them simultaneously as self-supervised targets. Unlike SSL pre-training methods (GraphCL, GraphMAE, GraphLoG) that rely on domain-specific node/edge features, GPSE extracts representations purely from graph topology, making it modality-agnostic. The paper positions itself as the first unified, transferable PSE extractor, contrasting with the prevailing 'select the right PSE per task' paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GPSE eliminates the trial-and-error burden of PSE selection and offers a drop-in replacement for hand-crafted encodings across any GNN or graph Transformer, with the practical advantage of constant inference-time complexity regardless of how many PSE types were used during training. The demonstrated transferability from ~25-node molecules to 169K-node citation graphs and superpixel graphs is striking and suggests a path toward foundational pre-trained graph encoders. Key limitations include the current inability to scale beyond ~1M training graphs and the lack of a theoretical explanation for why a single learned representation outperforms both individual PSEs and their naive concatenations. Open questions include whether more powerful GPSE variants can losslessly capture PSE semantics (the synthetic WL benchmarks show GPSE still trails LapPE/RWSE) and how to make the model efficient enough for billion-scale molecular datasets.
