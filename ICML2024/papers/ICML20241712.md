## Problem

Temporal credit assignment in RL is difficult when long delays and stochastic events separate critical actions from their consequences. TD(λ) offers a bias-variance tradeoff via a fixed λ, but no principled way to adapt λ to the local predictability of the environment-policy interaction. Existing model-based solutions (differentiable world models, imagination) are highly sensitive to model inaccuracies, leaving a gap for an approach that leverages learned models for credit assignment while remaining robust to arbitrary levels of model error.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state-action trajectories (percepts X_t = (R_t, S_t)) and a learned forward transition model that outputs P(X_{t+1}|X_t, A_t) — either a tabular count-based model or a neural network predicting state-deltas as categorical distributions. It produces standard tabular Q-values (or V-values) updated online via eligibility traces, where the trace decay at each step is set to the model's predicted probability of the next transition rather than a fixed scalar λ. In the factored-reward variant, each reward component maintains its own independent trace decay based on the predictability of that specific state component.

## Method

Chunked-TD uses the principle of history compression to adaptively set λ_{t+1} = P^π(x_{t+2}|x_{t+1}), the model-predicted probability of the next transition under the current policy. In near-deterministic regions where the model is confident, λ approaches 1 (Monte Carlo behavior), bridging long delays; in stochastic or poorly-modeled regions, λ approaches 0 (TD(0) bootstrapping), limiting variance. The sampling-based chunking procedure is reformulated as an exact expected λ-return update, and a Proposition proves that online eligibility-trace updates (Algorithm 1) exactly reproduce the offline λ-return at episode end in acyclic MDPs. Because the model is used only to decide when to bootstrap — not to generate targets — the approach degrades gracefully toward TD(0) as model errors grow, making it robust to arbitrary model inaccuracy. A C-factored variant extends this to decomposed rewards by assigning each reward component its own λ based on the predictability of its associated state component, enabling solutions to hard credit assignment problems like Key-to-Door.

## Results

On the Key-to-Door environment, C-factored (Chunked Expected-SARSA with per-component λ) collects the treasure in only 669±17 episodes without success, compared to 1881–2688 episodes for all fixed-λ Expected-SARSA baselines and 2285±2061 for C-default. On Chain-and-Split, Chunked SARSA rapidly converges to the true action-value gap (ΔQ=0.01), while SARSA(1)/MC fails entirely (negative ΔQ due to overestimation) and SARSA(0) converges more slowly. On Accumulated-Charge, Chunked SARSA achieves the lowest number of regretful choices across multiple learning rates, while SARSA(λ) exhibits high variance across seeds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SARSA(0) and SARSA(1)/MC (Rummery & Niranjan, 1994), Chunked SARSA wins decisively on Chain-and-Split: it converges to the correct ΔQ far faster than SARSA(0), while SARSA(1)/MC fails completely because it does not bootstrap from the shared parent state s_L, leading to overestimated action values and negative ΔQ. On Accumulated-Charge, Chunked SARSA outperforms SARSA(λ) across all tested λ values and learning rates, with notably lower variance across seeds. Against Expected-SARSA(λ) (Van Seijen et al., 2009) on Key-to-Door, C-factored achieves 669±17 failed episodes versus 1881–2688 for all fixed-λ variants, a roughly 3× improvement. The C-default variant (using a single shared λ from the full-state model) performs comparably to the worst fixed-λ baselines, confirming that the per-component factored λ is essential for handling incompressible distractor noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends TD(λ) with eligibility traces (Sutton, 1984) by replacing the fixed scalar λ with adaptive, model-predicted transition probabilities, drawing conceptual motivation from Schmidhuber's history compression principle (1991, 1992) rather than from bias-variance optimization. It is closely related to Sutton & Singh's (1994) variable-λ algorithms, which the paper reinterprets as using backward model predictions for trace decay; Chunked-TD instead uses forward model predictions, which are policy-independent and easier to learn. The C-factored variant combines this chunking mechanism with Q-decomposition (Russell & Zimdars, 2003) to handle environments with noisy, incompressible reward components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical advantage is robustness: since the model is used only to decide when to bootstrap (not for value estimation), Chunked-TD degrades gracefully toward TD(0) under model error, unlike imagination-based or gradient-through-model approaches that can fail catastrophically. However, the method requires a generative model that outputs transition probabilities, limiting applicability to continuous state/action spaces where such distributions are unavailable. Experiments are confined to small tabular MDPs, and the factored-reward variant requires a known reward decomposition. Extending Chunked-TD to deep function approximation, off-policy settings, and continuous domains remains open and is the clearest path to broader impact.
