## Problem

Existing LLM-based time series forecasting methods either rely on rigid text-prototype prompts or randomly initialized soft prompts, leaving the structured semantic space established during LLM pre-training largely unexplored. This paper asks whether the pre-trained word token embedding space can serve as a more distinctive and informative representation space to align with time series embeddings, thereby yielding better prompts for forecasting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241588/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series window of length τ (512 for long-term, 2×horizon for short-term) with N variables, applies reversible instance normalization, decomposes it into trend/seasonal/residual components, and patches each component independently before concatenating into meta-tokens. The output is a forecast of the next τ′ steps (96–720 for long-term, 6–48 for short-term), structured as the additive sum of separate trend, seasonal, and residual predictions projected from the LLM's output embedding.

## Method

S²IP-LLM introduces a two-stage prompting mechanism: (1) a decomposition-aware tokenization that patches trend, seasonal, and residual components separately and concatenates them into meta-tokens, providing more expressive local context than standard patching; and (2) semantic space informed prompting, where a small set of semantic anchors (V′ ≪ V) is derived from GPT-2's pre-trained word token embeddings via a learned mapping, and the top-K anchors most similar (by cosine) to the time series embedding are retrieved as prefix-prompts. The backbone is GPT-2-small (first 6 layers) with only positional embeddings and layer-norm parameters fine-tuned. Training minimizes MSE forecasting loss plus a cosine-similarity alignment term (weighted by λ) that pulls the time series embedding toward its selected semantic anchors, creating a joint space where temporal dynamics are grounded in the LLM's pre-trained semantic geometry.

## Results

On long-term forecasting (input length 512, horizons 96/192/336/720), S²IP-LLM achieves the best average MSE on 5 out of 7 datasets: Weather (0.222), Electricity (0.161), ETTh1 (0.406), ETTh2 (0.347), and ETTm1 (0.343), outperforming the strongest LLM-based baselines Time-LLM and OFA. On short-term M4 forecasting, S²IP-LLM attains the best weighted-average SMAPE (12.021), MASE (1.612), and OWA (0.857), surpassing all 13 baselines. In the few-shot setting (10% training data), S²IP-LLM ranks first or second on most datasets, e.g., ETTh2 MSE 0.419 vs. Time-LLM(G) 0.424 and OFA 0.437.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S²IP-LLM consistently outperforms the two LLM-based baselines (Time-LLM with LLaMA and GPT-2 backbones, and OFA) on most datasets, with the largest margins on ETTh2 (avg MSE 0.347 vs. 0.360/0.355/0.367) and ETTm1 (0.343 vs. 0.365/0.349/0.355). Against PatchTST, S²IP-LLM wins on Weather, ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, and ETTm2 but is slightly behind on Traffic (0.405 vs. 0.390) and ties on Electricity (both 0.161). Compared to iTransformer and DLinear, S²IP-LLM achieves substantially lower MSE across nearly all datasets (e.g., ETTh2: 0.347 vs. 0.382 for iTransformer and 0.502 for DLinear). On the M4 short-term benchmark, S²IP-LLM leads all 13 baselines on all three metrics (SMAPE 12.021, MASE 1.612, OWA 0.857), with the closest competitor being PatchTST (SMAPE 12.059, MASE 1.623, OWA 0.869). In the few-shot (10% data) setting, S²IP-LLM and the other LLM-based methods (Time-LLM, OFA) significantly outperform from-scratch models like TimesNet and Autoformer, confirming the benefit of pre-trained LLM knowledge transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-for-time-series line initiated by OFA (frozen GPT-2 with patched tokens) and Time-LLM (reprogramming via text prototypes and dataset descriptions) by introducing a fundamentally different prompting mechanism: instead of relying on dataset-specific textual context or randomly initialized soft prompts, S²IP-LLM derives 'semantic anchors' from the pre-trained word token embedding space and aligns them with time series embeddings via cosine similarity. The tokenization design builds on PatchTST's patching paradigm but departs by first decomposing the series into trend/seasonality/residual components and concatenating their patches into a single meta-token, combined with reversible instance normalization from Kim et al. (2021) to handle non-stationarity. The approach is positioned against TEMPO and TEST, which also use retrieval-based or prototype-aligned prompts, but distinguishes itself by grounding the prompt space in the LLM's own pre-trained semantic geometry rather than external text.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the pre-trained semantic space of an LLM can serve as a useful inductive bias for time series representation learning, offering a parameter-efficient alternative to full fine-tuning (only positional embeddings and layer norms are trained). The few-shot results (5% data) are particularly compelling, suggesting the semantic alignment provides a form of transferable prior that reduces data hunger. However, the gains over strong baselines like Time-LLM and PatchTST in the full-data setting are modest (often <2% MSE), and the semantic anchors—derived from word embeddings—lack an obvious interpretive link to temporal dynamics, raising open questions about whether the alignment is capturing meaningful structure or merely providing a useful regularization. Scaling to larger LLMs (beyond GPT-2-small with 6 layers) and validating on truly diverse, non-benchmark domains remain important next steps.
