## Problem

Quantization-conditioned backdoors (QCBs) remain dormant in full-precision models but activate upon quantization, evading existing detection and tuning-based defenses that operate on full-precision formats. Because QCB-backdoored models already fit benign samples well, standard fine-tuning and pruning defenses can only make minor weight changes to backdoor neurons, leaving the backdoor largely intact. This paper identifies a previously unexploited property of QCBs—activation distribution drift on backdoor neurons after quantization—and leverages it for purification.

## Input / Output

The method requires three inputs: the backdoor-exposed quantized model (e.g., 8-bit or 4-bit), its corresponding full-precision model (naturally available since QCBs are delivered as full-precision models that the user quantizes), and a small set of unlabeled images (5% of the training set, no labels needed). It outputs a purified quantized model with backdoor effects removed while preserving benign accuracy. The method operates layer-by-layer, processing each layer's weights independently, and PDA is omitted for architectures without BatchNorm layers (e.g., some ViT variants).

## Method

The core insight is that backdoor neurons exhibit a significant activation distribution drift after quantization on both benign and poisoned samples, while benign neurons show minimal drift. LAC (Layer-wise Activation Correction) minimizes a distance metric between the full-precision layer output W_o^l·I^l and the quantized layer output Q(W^l)·I^l, directly rectifying the aberrant activations in backdoor neurons without requiring labels or cross-entropy loss. PDA (Poisoned Distribution Approximation) further improves stability by perturbing benign inputs via PGD so their per-layer mean and variance match the running statistics stored in the full-precision model's BatchNorm layers, approximating the poisoned distribution that produces stronger activation deviations. A theoretical result (Theorem 4.1) shows that LAC+PDA approximately optimizes the CE loss on benign samples via second-order Taylor expansion, explaining why high benign accuracy is maintained without explicit label supervision.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, the proposed LAC+PDA method achieves ASR below 2% across all six attack/quantization settings (3 attacks × 2 bit-widths), with DER consistently above 97%. Against the hardest attack (PQBackdoor, 8-bit), it reaches 85.81% BA / 1.64% ASR / 98.74 DER, while the best baseline (FT-SAM) only achieves 81.93% BA / 6.52% ASR / 94.68 DER. On Tiny-ImageNet, the advantage is even more pronounced: against PQBackdoor (4-bit), the method achieves 55.53% BA / 0.39% ASR / 97.99 DER, whereas all six baselines fail with ASR above 32% and DER below 82%. The method also generalizes across architectures (AlexNet, VGG19, MobileNet-V2, ViT, EfficientViT) and trigger types (patch, input-aware dynamic, warping-based).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against six tuning-based backdoor defenses (FT, FP, MCR, NAD, I-BAU, FT-SAM) under three QCB attacks at 8-bit and 4-bit quantization. The most dramatic gap appears against PQBackdoor, the most sophisticated attack: on CIFAR-10 (8-bit), the proposed method achieves 1.64% ASR while FT and FP remain near 99% and 93% ASR respectively, and NAD collapses BA to 39.74%. On Tiny-ImageNet (PQBackdoor, 4-bit), the method reaches 0.39% ASR / 97.99 DER, whereas every baseline exceeds 32% ASR and stays below 82% DER. Against the easier CompArtifact and Qu-Anti-zation attacks, most baselines already perform reasonably (ASR < 5%), and the proposed method matches or slightly exceeds them in DER (e.g., 99.63 vs. 99.59 for NAD on CompArtifact 8-bit). FT-SAM is the strongest baseline overall, achieving competitive DER on PQBackdoor 8-bit (94.68) and Qu-Anti-zation 4-bit (99.36), but still trails the proposed method by 4+ DER points on PQBackdoor. The proposed method is the only defense that consistently keeps ASR below 2% across all six attack/quantization settings on both datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes layer-wise activation correction (LAC), a technique originally developed for mitigating quantization accuracy loss (Nagel et al., 2020; Li et al., 2021a), as a backdoor defense mechanism. It builds on the backdoor neuron identification framework of Zheng et al. (2022) but extends it to the quantization-conditioned setting, revealing that activation drift occurs even on benign samples after quantization—a property absent in full-precision backdoor analysis. Unlike concurrent work (Li et al., 2024) that manipulates the quantization process itself, this defense operates post-quantization and is composable with any quantization technique. The PDA module draws on data-free knowledge transfer ideas (Yin et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2023a) by leveraging BN running statistics to approximate poisoned distributions without access to poisoned data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QCBs represent a supply-chain threat that is invisible to all existing detection and purification defenses because the backdoor is dormant in full-precision format; this paper provides the first systematic post-quantization purification method that is label-free and does not rely on CE loss. Key limitations include the requirement for full-precision model access, the dependence on BN layers for PDA (though LAC alone suffices), and the need for a small set of unlabeled samples. Open questions include extending to data-free settings, defending when only quantized weights are available, and whether future QCB attacks can be designed to resist LAC-based correction. The finding that advanced quantization techniques already incorporating LAC may be inadvertently immune to existing QCBs has implications for both attackers and quantization practitioners.
