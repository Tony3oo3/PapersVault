## Problem

Hypothesis transfer learning (HTL) provides fast generalization rates when a learner is initialized with auxiliary hypotheses (e.g., pre-trained foundation models), but all prior HTL theory assumes the standard (non-adversarial) loss. Conversely, adversarial training generalization theory has been developed largely for models trained from scratch. This paper bridges the gap by studying HTL under ℓp-bounded adversarial attacks, asking whether a good auxiliary initialization can yield fast (O(1/n)) robust generalization rates for the adversarially trained model.

## Input / Output

The learner receives n i.i.d. samples from an unknown distribution D and a fixed set of k auxiliary hypotheses (e.g., deep neural networks or foundation models) that are kept frozen throughout training. The only trainable parameters are the weights β ∈ ℝ^k (fixed a priori, acting as a capacity control) and the hypothesis h_w from a simple class H (an RKHS or two-layer ReLU network). The output is the combined classifier h_{w,β}(x) = h_w(x) + Σβ_j f_j^aux(x) along with high-probability bounds on its robust generalization gap and excess robust risk, where the bounds explicitly depend on the robust population loss of the auxiliary model f_β^aux.

## Method

The paper analyzes two learning rules. First, A-RERM solves an adversarial regularized empirical risk minimization problem in an RKHS with a strongly convex regularizer; the robust generalization gap is bounded via Rademacher complexity of the adversarial loss class (leveraging H-smoothness of the loss to relate adversarial loss differences to hypothesis differences) combined with Kakade et al.'s strongly convex regularizer inequality. The resulting bound is optimistic: it achieves O(1/n) when the auxiliary model's robust loss L_adv^aux is small, degrading to O(1/√n) otherwise. Second, PSAT is a practical proximal stochastic gradient algorithm that initializes at the auxiliary model and performs min-max updates; its generalization is bounded via on-average stability (Kuzborskij 2018), yielding an O(1/n + α) rate without assuming convexity of the adversarial loss. Both results extend to two-layer ReLU networks with fixed random top-layer weights.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or results tables. The main quantitative contributions are generalization rate guarantees: (1) The A-RERM robust generalization gap (Theorem 3.1) achieves a fast rate of Õ(1/n) when the auxiliary model's robust loss L_adv^aux is small, and the standard Õ(1/√n) rate otherwise. (2) The excess robust risk (Theorem 3.2) achieves Õ(1/n) under near-robust-realizability but degrades to Õ(1/n^{1/4}) in general due to non-convexity of the adversarial loss. (3) The PSAT algorithm (Theorem 4.2) yields a generalization gap scaling as Õ(√Φ_adv(w₀) + E[ν_S²]log(T)(1/√(nT) + 1/n) + 1/n + α), with an additional dependence on the perturbation budget α not present in the A-RERM bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper presents no empirical baselines or head-to-head comparisons, as it is a theoretical contribution. In terms of theoretical positioning: the results recover the non-robust HTL fast-rate guarantees of Kuzborskij & Orabona (2017) exactly when the perturbation budget α=0, confirming the adversarial extension is a strict generalization. Unlike prior stability-based adversarial generalization analyses (Xing et al., 2021; Xiao et al., 2022b), the PSAT analysis does not assume convexity of the adversarial loss, which the authors note is unrealistic for practical loss functions. The A-RERM bound is data-dependent and optimistic (fast rate conditional on good initialization), whereas uniform convergence-based adversarial bounds (Yin et al., 2019; Khim & Loh, 2018) are distribution-free but typically yield worse rates. The PSAT bound introduces an additional α-dependent term absent from the A-RERM analysis, reflecting the practical cost of stochastic optimization under adversarial perturbations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hypothesis transfer learning (HTL) framework of Kuzborskij & Orabona (2013, 2017) to the adversarial setting, where the learner must produce a model robust to ℓp-bounded perturbations. It leverages the stability-based generalization machinery of Kuzborskij (2018) and the Rademacher complexity tools for smooth losses from Srebro et al. (2010), adapting them to the adversarial loss class. A key departure from prior adversarial stability analyses (Xing et al. 2021; Xiao et al. 2022b) is the explicit non-convexity of the adversarial loss, which the authors handle without the convexity assumption that prior work required. The connection to biased regularization in transfer learning (Tommasi & Caputo 2009; Denevi et al. 2019) is made explicit for the linear case, situating A-RERM within that broader family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical justification for the empirical observation (Hua et al. 2023) that initialization quality critically affects adversarial transfer learning, showing that a good auxiliary hypothesis yields a fast O(1/n) generalization rate versus the standard O(1/√n). This is directly relevant to the fine-tuning paradigm for foundation models, where pre-trained models serve as auxiliary hypotheses. Open questions include the unavoidable dependence of the PSAT bound on the perturbation budget α, the restriction to two-layer ReLU networks for the neural network extension, and the fixed-β assumption that limits the analysis to a single linear combination of auxiliaries. Developing algorithms that mitigate the α-dependence and principled regularization schedules are identified as promising future directions.
