## Problem

CLIP and similar contrastive vision-language models map each image to a single fixed vector, forcing all valid captions of the same image to converge to one representation. This invariance objective discards the fact that different captions highlight different visual aspects of the same image, limiting the expressiveness of the learned visual features. The paper asks whether explicitly modeling this one-to-many image-to-caption mapping can yield richer representations and better downstream performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RGB image and a text caption as input. The ViT visual encoder outputs K learnable mixture tokens (K=64 in most experiments) alongside standard patch tokens, while the text encoder produces a single vector. The final visual representation is contextualized: the same image yields a different feature vector depending on which caption is used, so at inference time for zero-shot classification the image must be encoded once per candidate label. The cross-attention fusion occurs only at the output layer, making the additional compute cost constant regardless of encoder size.

## Method

Llip augments a standard ViT visual encoder with K learnable mixture tokens that capture different visual aspects of the input. A multi-head cross-attention module then uses the text representation as the query and the mixture tokens as keys and values, producing softmax mixing weights that select and combine the tokens into a single contextualized visual vector. Training uses a modified SigLIP sigmoid contrastive loss where the visual representation for each image-text pair is computed by conditioning the mixture on the text, and the negative pairs in the batch prevent the model from ignoring the image and relying solely on the caption. The key architectural insight is that the same image can produce different visual representations for different captions, and the contrastive objective naturally enforces that the representation must be informative about the image content (since the same caption serves as a negative for other images in the batch).

## Results

With a ViT-G/14 encoder, Llip achieves 83.5% zero-shot top-1 accuracy on ImageNet, outperforming MetaCLIP (82.1%) by 1.4% and EVA-CLIP (82.0%, using a 2.5× larger ViT-E/14 backbone) by 1.5%. Across 22 zero-shot classification benchmarks, Llip attains a 79.7% average, beating the best prior baseline (MetaCLIP at 76.8%) by 2.9% and achieving the best score on 19 of 22 tasks. On zero-shot retrieval, Llip improves MS-COCO image-to-text R@1 by 6.0% (72.7 vs. 66.7) and text-to-image R@1 by 4.6% (54.2 vs. 49.6) over MetaCLIP with ViT-G/14.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Llip consistently outperforms MetaCLIP (the closest same-data baseline) across all model sizes: +4.7% average on ViT-B/32, +3.5% on ViT-B/16, +1.9% on ViT-L/14, +2.2% on ViT-H/14, and +2.9% on ViT-G/14. Against SigLIP, Llip gains +0.3% on ImageNet with ViT-G/14 while training on 4× fewer samples (12.8B vs. 40B seen). Compared to EVA-CLIP, which uses a ViT-E/14 backbone with 2.5× more parameters, Llip (ViT-G/14) still wins by 2.5% average (79.7 vs. 75.6). OpenCLIP (ViT-G/14) trails Llip by 6.2% average (73.5 vs. 79.7), and CLIPA-v2 is outperformed on ImageNet per Figure 4. The only baseline that surpasses Llip is DFN, which achieves higher ImageNet accuracy but is trained on 5B curated samples at 378×378 resolution versus Llip's 2.5B samples at 224×224, making the comparison non-apples-to-apples. On retrieval, Llip beats MetaCLIP by 6.0% on MS-COCO image-to-text R@1 and 4.6% on text-to-image R@1 with ViT-G/14.

## Relation to Prior Work

Llip extends the contrastive vision-language pretraining paradigm established by CLIP and refined by SigLIP, departing from the core invariance assumption that a single image representation must match all valid captions. Architecturally, it repurposes the register tokens from Darcet et al. (2023) as 'mixture tokens' and introduces a lightweight cross-attention module at the output layer to condition the visual representation on the text caption, drawing a conceptual parallel to JEPA-style predictive architectures where a context variable modulates the prediction. Unlike prior VLP methods that incorporate cross-attention within the text encoder (e.g., UniTER) at significant compute cost, Llip applies fusion only at the final representation level, keeping the vision and text encoders independent during the bulk of computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a fundamental limitation in all contrastive VLP methods—the forced one-to-one mapping between image and text representations—and shows that explicitly modeling caption diversity yields consistent gains across 22 classification benchmarks and retrieval tasks without changing the training data or recipe. The approach introduces a new scaling axis (number of mixture tokens) orthogonal to encoder size and patch resolution, and the clean ablation isolating text-conditioning as the critical ingredient provides clear guidance for future work. Open questions include whether the inference-time requirement of encoding each image with all candidate captions becomes prohibitive at scale, whether the shortcut-solution concern is fully mitigated in practice, and whether the approach transfers to generative or multi-modal reasoning tasks beyond contrastive evaluation.
