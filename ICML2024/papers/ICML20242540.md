## Problem

Building foundation models for sequential decision-making requires pretrained representations that generalize across diverse tasks, embodiments, and camera views with minimal downstream data. Existing vision-pretrained encoders (R3M, VC-1) suffer from a domain gap with control tasks, while control-centric pretraining methods like SMART require extensive task sets or fine-tuning. The temporal action contrastive objective TACO achieves state-of-the-art single-task results, but its strategy of treating all batch items as negatives breaks down in multitask offline pretraining where visual appearances differ drastically across tasks.

## Input / Output

During pretraining, the method consumes multitask offline trajectories of (state, action, next-state) tuples from pixel observations—84×84 RGB frames for DMC/MetaWorld and 128×128 dual-view images for LIBERO—along with action sequences of length K=3. The pretrained output is a visual state encoder (a ~4M-parameter shallow ConvNet for DMC/MetaWorld or a ResNet-18 for LIBERO) that maps pixel observations to latent state vectors. For downstream adaptation, the frozen or fine-tuned encoder feeds a behavior-cloning policy head trained on as few as 5 expert trajectories, producing a control policy that outputs continuous actions.

## Method

Premier-TACO modifies TACO's InfoNCE-based temporal contrastive objective by replacing its all-in-batch negative sampling with a single hard negative drawn from a temporal window of size W=5 centered at the positive future state within the same episode. This ensures negatives are visually near-identical to positives, forcing the latent representation to encode control-relevant temporal dynamics rather than trivial visual appearance differences. The loss maximizes mutual information between the embedding of [current state, action sequence] (projected via G_θ) and the embedding of the future state (projected via H_θ), while pushing apart the embedding of the sampled negative future state. Because only one negative per sample is needed, the method operates effectively at 1/8 of TACO's batch size (512 vs. 4096 on MetaWorld), saving ~87.5% computational time while achieving a 25% relative performance gain. The pretrained encoder is then adapted to unseen downstream tasks via behavior cloning with a small number of expert demonstrations, and the same contrastive objective can fine-tune large pretrained encoders like R3M to bridge the domain gap.

## Results

On Deepmind Control Suite (10 unseen tasks, 20 expert trajectories), Premier-TACO achieves a mean Agent/Expert Reward ratio of 79.0, outperforming the best baseline TD3+BC (63.3) by 25% and Learn-from-Scratch (38.2) by 101%. On MetaWorld (8 unseen tasks, 5 expert trajectories), Premier-TACO reaches 75.8% mean success rate versus 53.9% for the best baseline SMART, a 37% relative improvement. On LIBERO (8 long-horizon tasks, 5 expert trajectories), Premier-TACO achieves 58.0% mean success rate versus 44.4% for ATC, a 17% improvement, and is the first method to demonstrate any benefit from pretraining on this benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Premier-TACO achieves the best result on every single task across all three benchmarks (DMC, MetaWorld, LIBERO), with no losses to any baseline. Against the strongest policy-pretraining baseline TD3+BC (63.3 mean on DMC), Premier-TACO leads by 15.7 points; against the best pretrained visual representation (VC-1, 52.3 mean), the gap is 26.7 points. The closest competitor is SPR (62.4 on DMC, 55.3 on MetaWorld), which Premier-TACO outperforms by 16.6 and 20.5 points respectively. Notably, TACO (47.5 on DMC, 47.5 on MetaWorld) — the direct predecessor — is substantially outperformed, with the paper attributing the gap to Premier-TACO's hard-negative sampling strategy that enables 1/8 the batch size while gaining 25% relative performance on MetaWorld. On LIBERO, where all prior pretraining methods (including multitask TD3+BC and Inverse dynamics) actually degrade performance below Learn-from-Scratch, Premier-TACO is the first to show a clear pretraining benefit (58.0 vs. 40.3 for LfS). The paper also demonstrates that Premier-TACO's objective can fine-tune R3M, yielding ~50% average performance improvement over R3M with in-domain BC fine-tuning, which itself degrades R3M's performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

Premier-TACO extends TACO (Zheng et al., 2023a), a temporal action-driven contrastive objective that achieved state-of-the-art results in single-task visual control, by replacing TACO's use of all batch members as negative examples with a single hard negative sampled from a proximate temporal window within the same episode. This targeted modification addresses the core failure mode of TACO in multitask settings—where batch negatives from visually dissimilar tasks make the InfoNCE loss uninformative—while adding negligible computational overhead. The paper positions itself against vision-centric pretraining (R3M, VIP, VC-1) that suffers from domain gap, control-centric pretraining (SMART, DualMind) that requires extensive task sets or fine-tuning, and other contrastive objectives (CURL, ATC, SPR) that do not jointly model state-action temporal dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in making large-scale multitask offline pretraining for visual control computationally feasible (87.5% compute savings via smaller batch sizes) and in being the first method to demonstrate that pretraining actually helps on LIBERO, where prior multitask pretraining approaches degraded downstream performance. The compatibility with existing large pretrained encoders (R3M) and robustness to low-quality (random) pretraining data broaden applicability to real-world settings where expert data is scarce. Open questions include whether the approach scales to substantially more diverse embodiments and action spaces, whether combining pretrained state representations with temporal action abstractions (skills/options) yields further gains, and whether the window-based negative sampling strategy generalizes to non-visual or partially observable settings.
