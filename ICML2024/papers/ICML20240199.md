## Problem

While regression adjustment is well-established for variance reduction in ATE estimation, there has been limited research on applying it to distributional treatment effects (DTE, PTE, QTE) in randomized experiments, particularly with high-dimensional covariates and flexible machine learning methods. Existing approaches either target only mean effects, require continuous outcomes (as in quantile regression), or are restricted to low-dimensional covariates, leaving a gap for principled, ML-based regression adjustment of full distributional parameters.

## Input / Output

The method takes data from a K-arm randomized experiment with pre-treatment covariates X (potentially high-dimensional), treatment assignments W, and outcomes Y that can be discrete, continuous, or mixed discrete-continuous. It produces point estimates of the full distributional treatment effect curve F_{Y(w)}(y) − F_{Y(w')}(y) indexed over a continuum of outcome values y, along with uniform confidence bands via multiplier bootstrap. A key distinction from quantile regression approaches is that the framework naturally accommodates discrete and mixed outcomes without continuity assumptions, making it applicable to settings like the water consumption experiment where subtle discreteness in the outcome would invalidate QTE-based inference.

## Method

The core idea is to treat the conditional distribution function F_{Y(w)|X}(y|x) as a binary regression problem (predicting 1{Y(w)≤y} from X) and estimate these nuisance functions using flexible ML methods such as LASSO, random forests, gradient boosting, or neural networks. The regression-adjusted estimator subtracts the group-specific conditional distribution averaged over the treatment group and adds the overall conditional distribution averaged across all observations, yielding a Neyman-orthogonal moment condition that is first-order insensitive to nuisance estimation errors. Cross-fitting with L folds ensures independence between nuisance estimates and the observations they adjust, enabling valid asymptotic inference even with complex ML estimators. The paper establishes uniform asymptotic normality via a functional central limit theorem and provides a multiplier bootstrap procedure for valid uniform inference on any Hadamard-differentiable functional of the distribution, including DTE, PTE, and QTE.

## Results

In the simulation study (n ∈ {500, 1000, 5000}), all estimators show bias of at most 2% of the true DTE value. The ML-adjusted estimator (logistic LASSO with 5-fold cross-fitting) achieves approximately 40–50% RMSE reduction over the simple empirical distribution estimator at n=5000, while the linear-adjusted estimator achieves only 15–35% reduction. On the Ferraro & Price (2013) water consumption experiment (n=78,500), the regression-adjusted DTE (gradient boosting, 10-fold cross-fitting) yields substantially tighter 95% confidence intervals than the simple DTE, particularly in the range y ∈ [15, 110], and the PTE for the [70,80] range becomes significantly negative under regression adjustment where it was not significant under the simple estimator.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three estimators: the simple empirical distribution function, a linear-regression adjustment, and an ML (logistic LASSO) adjustment, all using 5-fold cross-fitting. The ML adjustment dominates both baselines on RMSE reduction across all sample sizes and all quantiles, with the gap widening as n increases (40–50% vs. 15–35% at n=5000). The linear adjustment still outperforms the simple estimator but by a smaller margin, which the authors attribute to the DGP containing irrelevant covariates that LASSO handles better than a linear model. On the real water-consumption data, the regression-adjusted DTE (gradient boosting) produces visibly narrower confidence intervals than the simple DTE, and the PTE reveals a statistically significant negative effect in the [70,80] gallon range that the simple estimator fails to detect. The paper does not compare against other distributional treatment effect methods (e.g., quantile regression adjustment of Jiang et al. 2023 or the finite-dimensional approach of Oka et al. 2024) in its empirical experiments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the regression adjustment framework for ATE estimation (List et al., 2022; Deng et al., 2013) to the broader class of distributional treatment effect parameters (DTE, PTE, QTE), using the Neyman-orthogonal moment condition and cross-fitting machinery from Chernozhukov et al. (2018). The distribution regression framework from Chernozhukov et al. (2013) provides the modeling foundation for conditional outcome distributions, while the asymptotic theory draws on Belloni et al. (2017) for uniform-in-P empirical process results. The key departure from prior work is the combination of high-dimensional ML nuisance estimation with distributional (rather than mean) parameters, accommodating discrete and mixed outcomes where quantile regression adjustment is inapplicable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, off-the-shelf recipe for variance-reduced inference on distributional treatment effects in randomized experiments, with the water consumption application demonstrating that regression adjustment can flip a non-significant result to significant (the [70,80] water usage range). Limitations include the requirement for experimental data with perfect compliance, no interference, and covariates that are genuinely predictive of the outcome; the authors note that substantial variance reduction may not materialize if pre-treatment information is weak. Open questions include extending the framework to observational settings, incorporating design-stage variance reduction, and handling network/peer effects.
