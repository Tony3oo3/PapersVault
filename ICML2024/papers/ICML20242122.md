## Problem

Existing goal-conditioned hierarchical RL methods rely on deterministic subgoal representation functions that map state space to a fixed latent subgoal space. These deterministic mappings cannot account for stochastic uncertainties from environmental noise or unexplored state regions, leading to unstable representations, unreachable subgoals, and poor generalization. This paper introduces the first probabilistic subgoal representation for HRL, replacing the single deterministic mapping with a posterior distribution over subgoal representation functions.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input the environment state (continuous coordinates or a 5×5×3 top-down RGB image) and produces a probabilistic subgoal representation as a Gaussian posterior (mean μ and covariance Σ) over the latent subgoal space, rather than a single point estimate. The high-level policy samples a subgoal g from this latent space every k=50 timesteps, and the low-level policy receives an intrinsic reward defined as the negative Euclidean distance between the current state's subgoal representation and the target subgoal. For online inference, the GP is reformulated as a state-space model (Kalman-filter-like recursion) that fuses subgoal information from an arbitrary number of previous planning steps with constant per-step computational and memory cost.

## Method

The representation function φ(s) consists of a neural network encoding layer producing an intermediate latent f, followed by a latent GP layer that models z as a noise-corrupted observation of f under a GP prior with a learnable Matérn kernel (hyperparameters γ², ℓ, σ²). A novel learning objective (Eq. 4) jointly trains the GP hyperparameters and hierarchical policies by combining a relative-distance ratio term (Δf¹/Δfᵏ) with a softplus-log term that minimizes low-level transition distances while maximizing high-level transition distances in the latent subgoal space, eliminating the need for a margin hyperparameter. Both high-level and low-level policies are trained with SAC using off-policy replay. For efficient online inference, the batch GP regression is relaxed to a Markov-chain (state-space) formulation where the Matérn kernel is realized as a linear SDE evolution operator, yielding recursive Kalman-filter-style posterior updates with O(1) complexity per state that encode long-term memory of high-level actions.

## Results

On deterministic MuJoCo continuous-control tasks (5M training steps, 10 seeds), HLPS achieves the highest success rate across all four Ant navigation/manipulation tasks. Headline numbers include 0.93 on Ant Maze (sparse), 0.93 on Ant Push (dense), 0.79 on Ant Fall (sparse), and 0.93 on Ant FourRooms (dense). The advantage is most pronounced on Ant Fall (sparse), where HLPS reaches 0.79 versus 0.29 for HESS and 0.54 for LESSON, and on image-observation variants where deterministic baselines degrade sharply (e.g., Ant Fall dense/w image: HLPS 0.66 vs. LESSON 0.19). Flat RL (TD3) fails to learn in all environments (0.00 success rate).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HLPS outperforms all five baselines on the majority of task/reward combinations. Against HESS (Li et al., 2022), the gap is largest on Ant Fall (sparse): 0.79 vs. 0.29, where HESS's novelty-based exploration misleads subgoal generation in the presence of random goals; on Ant FourRooms (dense) HLPS leads by 0.13 (0.93 vs. 0.80). Against LESSON (Li et al., 2021), HLPS wins on every task, with the most dramatic margin on image-observation variants (e.g., Ant Push dense/img: 0.84 vs. 0.24; Ant Fall dense/img: 0.66 vs. 0.19), indicating that deterministic local-constraint representations degrade severely in higher-dimensional state spaces. Against HRAC (Zhang et al., 2020), which uses a pre-defined subgoal space, HLPS dominates on all Ant tasks (e.g., Ant Push dense: 0.93 vs. 0.01) and is competitive on Ant Maze (sparse: 0.93 vs. 0.83). Flat RL (TD3) achieves 0.00 success rate in every environment, confirming the necessity of hierarchical decomposition. In the 7-DOF Reacher and Pusher robotic-arm tasks (Fig. 5), HLPS similarly surpasses HESS and LESSON, with LESSON struggling to reach goals at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the goal-conditioned HRL framework of Nachum et al. (2018), which defines a two-level policy structure with intrinsic rewards in a latent subgoal space, by replacing the deterministic subgoal representation functions used in LESSON (Li et al., 2021) and HESS (Li et al., 2022) with a non-parametric Gaussian Process prior. The key conceptual departure is treating the subgoal representation as a posterior distribution over functions rather than a single learned mapping, with a Matérn kernel encoding long-range state-space correlations. For efficient online inference, the paper adapts the state-space GP / Kalman filter formulation from Sarkka et al. (2013) to achieve constant-complexity recursive updates, drawing a direct analogy between GP regression and sequential filtering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By introducing the first probabilistic subgoal representation in HRL, this work addresses a fundamental limitation of deterministic mappings: their inability to represent uncertainty in unexplored state regions, which causes non-stationarity in both high-level transitions and low-level reward functions during off-policy training. The demonstrated transferability of learned subgoal representations and low-level policies across tasks (e.g., Ant Fall → Ant Push) suggests the GP-based latent space captures task-agnostic structure. Open questions include scalability to higher-dimensional subgoal spaces, sensitivity to kernel choice beyond Matérn, and whether the approach extends to discrete or partially observable settings. The method is currently validated only on MuJoCo continuous-control benchmarks with a 2D subgoal space, leaving its applicability to more complex or high-dimensional domains untested.
