## Problem

All prior gradient-based MCMC samplers for controlled text generation (COLD, MuCoLA, SVS) are provably unfaithful: their limiting distributions do not match the target energy-based distribution over text, because continuous-relaxation techniques (projection, Voronoi tessellation) introduce intractable or incorrect corrections. This paper identifies the failure modes theoretically and empirically, then asks what happens when one samples correctly from the target EBM.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained autoregressive language model (GPT-2-small or GPT-2-large) as the base energy, one or more differentiable soft-constraint functions (e.g., a topic classifier, sentiment classifier, or fixed positional tokens), and a fixed sequence length N, and produces a discrete word sequence of exactly N tokens. The energy is U(w) = -log p_LM(w) + Σ U_i(w), and the sampler operates over the finite discrete embedding space X = V^N ⊂ R^{Nd}, where each position is constrained to one of |Σ| word embeddings. Unlike autoregressive decoding, the sequence length is fixed in advance and the sampler can propose multi-position updates simultaneously.

## Method

The paper proposes two faithful gradient-based samplers, both corrected by Metropolis-Hastings so that the discrete transition probabilities are computable in closed form. The first, p-NCG (p-Norm Constrained Gradient), defines a discrete proposal q(x'|x) ∝ exp(-½∇U(x)ᵀ(x'−x) − (1/2α)‖x'−x‖_p^p) that mimics a Langevin step without the intractable projection integral; the p-norm (rather than ℓ₂) handles the unusual geometry of word embedding spaces. The second, GwL (Gibbs with Langevin), applies the same gradient-informed proposal to single-position updates within a Gibbs scan, removing self-transitions for efficiency. A hybrid scheme runs p-NCG during the initial mixing phase and switches to GwL once the chain nears convergence. Theoretically, the paper proves that p-NCG is locally balanced (its unadjusted stationary distribution converges to the target as α→0) for discrete log-quadratic distributions, while also showing the unadjusted mixing time grows exponentially as α→0, justifying the Metropolis-Hastings correction in practice.

## Results

On topic-controlled generation (E2E), p-NCG+GwL achieves 99% success rate with PPL 5.17, matching the unconditional GPT-2 fluency (PPL 5.10) while far exceeding all baselines in control adherence. On sentiment-controlled generation (SST-2), p-NCG+GwL reaches 96% success with PPL 23.61, compared to 84% and PPL 32.73 for SVS. On position-constrained generation (COLLIE), p-NCG+GwL achieves the lowest PPL (55.06) among all MCMC baselines, approaching the reference English distribution (PPL 57.42).

## Baselines & Comparisons

p-NCG+GwL dominates all baselines on both success rate and fluency. Against FUDGE, it improves topic success from 30% to 99% and sentiment success from 57% to 96%, while maintaining comparable PPL (5.17 vs 5.59 topic; 23.61 vs 24.27 sentiment). Against MuCoLA, the improvement is dramatic: PPL drops from 33.09 to 5.17 (topic) and from 85.74 to 23.61 (sentiment), while success increases from 58% to 99% and 66% to 96% respectively. Against SVS and SVS-Langevin, p-NCG+GwL achieves higher success (99% vs 92%/91% topic; 96% vs 84%/82% sentiment) and substantially lower PPL (5.17 vs 13.9/14.26 topic; 23.61 vs 32.73/26.76 sentiment), showing that the faithful sampler recovers fluency that the unfaithful Voronoi-based methods sacrifice. On the COLLIE position-constrained task, p-NCG+GwL achieves the lowest PPL (55.06) among all MCMC methods, approaching the reference English distribution (57.42), while MuCoLA and SVS variants have PPLs of 95.38, 77.16, and 79.13 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the line of gradient-based MCMC for discrete distributions (NCG by Rhodes and Gutmann 2022, D-MALA by Zhang et al. 2022, R-MALA by Grathwohl et al. 2021) and the Metropolis-within-Gibbs framework, extending them to the text generation setting with p-norm constraints. It departs from prior controlled text generation samplers (COLD, MuCoLA, SVS) by proving they are all 'unfaithful'—their limiting distributions differ from the intended energy-based target—and then constructing faithful alternatives where the Metropolis-Hastings acceptance probability is computable in closed form, avoiding the intractable high-dimensional Gaussian integrals that plagued the continuous-relaxation approaches. The Gibbs-based sampler (GwL) draws on the MALA-within-Gibbs literature and locally balanced proposal theory (Zanella 2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The proof that all prior gradient-based text samplers converge to the wrong distribution is a significant corrective for the field, as these methods had been producing good empirical results without theoretical justification. The proposed samplers are general (applicable to any fixed-dimensional discrete EBM, not just text) and the hybrid p-NCG+GwL approach achieves near-unconditional fluency while matching or exceeding control success rates. Key limitations include the fixed sequence length N (no insertions/deletions), manual step-size tuning, and the fact that the mixing time of the unadjusted proposal grows exponentially as step size decreases, making the Metropolis-Hastings correction essential in practice. Open directions include variable-length generation, automatic step-size adaptation preserving detailed balance, and proposal merging.
