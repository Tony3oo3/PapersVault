## Problem

Existing feature-space OOD detection methods (e.g., Mahalanobis distance, KNN) achieve strong effectiveness but rely on auxiliary models built from training features, incurring significant inference latency that is prohibitive for time-critical applications like autonomous driving. The paper tackles the unexplored question of where ID and OOD features reside relative to the classifier's decision boundaries, seeking a detection signal that captures training statistics inherently without requiring any auxiliary model or hyperparameter tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 224×224 for ImageNet) and produces one scalar OOD detection score (regDistDB) per sample, where lower values indicate higher OOD likelihood. Crucially, the score is computed solely from the penultimate-layer feature vector z_x and the linear classifier's weight/bias parameters—no training-set statistics beyond the single mean vector μ_train are needed, and no auxiliary model is constructed. The method is post-hoc and training-agnostic, applicable to any pre-trained classifier regardless of architecture or training objective.

## Method

fDBD formalizes the L2 distance from a penultimate-layer feature to each class's decision boundary and derives a closed-form lower bound (Theorem 3.2) computable in constant time from logit differences and pre-computed pairwise weight norms, avoiding expensive iterative optimization. The key empirical finding is that ID features reside further from decision boundaries than OOD features, and this separation is sharpened when features are compared at equal deviation levels from the training feature mean. The detection score regDistDB is the average of the closed-form distance estimates over all non-predicted classes, regularized (divided) by the L2 distance from z_x to μ_train, yielding a hyperparameter-free, auxiliary-model-free score with O(|C|+P) complexity. Theoretical analysis under a neural-collapse / ETF geometry model (Propositions 5.1–5.2) proves that boundary distance increases with deviation from the distribution mean and that ID features are on average further from boundaries than OOD features at equal deviation, providing a principled justification for the regularization.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18, cross-entropy loss), fDBD achieves an average FPR95 of 31.09% and AUROC of 94.20%, outperforming all baselines while maintaining the same 0.53 ms inference latency as the vanilla softmax-confidence detector. Under supervised contrastive loss, fDBD further reduces average FPR95 to 11.85% (AUROC 97.60%), matching or surpassing KNN+ (FPR95 12.18%, AUROC 97.86%) at less than one-third the latency. On ImageNet (ResNet-50), fDBD achieves average FPR95 of 51.19% and AUROC of 89.26% under cross-entropy loss, and 37.79% / 92.84% under SupCon, again with negligible latency overhead (~6.8 ms vs. 7.04 ms for baselines).

## Baselines & Comparisons

fDBD outperforms all output-space baselines (MSP, ODIN, Energy) by a wide margin: it reduces average FPR95 from 60.78% (MSP) and 44.88% (Energy) to 31.09% on CIFAR-10 cross-entropy, a ~49% relative error reduction over MSP. Against feature-space clustering methods, fDBD beats KNN (FPR95 31.09 vs. 33.66) and MDS (31.09 vs. 36.35) while being 3.7× and 5.3× faster in inference, respectively, since it requires no auxiliary model. Against ViM, which also incorporates class-specific information, fDBD achieves slightly better FPR95 (31.09 vs. 32.24) and AUROC (94.20 vs. 94.05) at lower latency (0.53 vs. 0.70 ms). Under supervised contrastive loss, fDBD nearly matches KNN+ (FPR95 11.85 vs. 12.18; AUROC 97.60 vs. 97.86) while running 3.5× faster (0.55 vs. 1.93 ms), and substantially outperforms SSD+ (FPR95 11.85 vs. 18.51) and CSI (11.85 vs. 28.73). On ImageNet, fDBD extends its lead, achieving the best average FPR95 (51.19%) and AUROC (89.26%) under cross-entropy, surpassing KNN(α=1%) by 3.13 FPR95 points and 4.67 AUROC points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in post-hoc OOD detection by repurposing the geometric concept of decision boundaries from classification theory into a feature-space OOD score, departing from the dominant auxiliary-model paradigm (Mahalanobis distance, KNN, clustering) that prior feature-space methods like MDS, KNN, and CSI rely on. The theoretical analysis draws on the neural collapse / ETF geometry established by Papyan et al. (2020) to formalize the relationship between feature deviation and decision-boundary distance. Unlike output-space methods (MSP, Energy, ODIN) that operate on logits, fDBD extracts class-specific geometric information from the penultimate layer without requiring any training-set statistics beyond a single mean vector, and it composes naturally with activation shaping techniques (ReAct, ASH, Scale) that modify the feature space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving competitive OOD detection with zero additional inference latency and no hyperparameter tuning, making it attractive for latency-critical deployments (e.g., autonomous driving) where auxiliary-model methods like KNN or MDS incur substantial overhead. The theoretical analysis (tight lower bound on decision-boundary distance, linear complexity, and propositions on ID/OOD separation at equal deviation levels) provides a principled foundation rather than a purely empirical recipe. Open questions include whether the ETF/neural-collapse assumption holds for all architectures and training regimes, whether the method extends to open-set or multi-modal settings, and how it interacts with adversarial robustness and domain generalization, which the authors flag as future directions.
