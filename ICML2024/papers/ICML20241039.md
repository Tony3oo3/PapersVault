## Problem

Solving the pressure Poisson equation in incompressible fluid simulations with mixed Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions is a major computational bottleneck, as traditional preconditioners (algebraic multigrid, incomplete Cholesky) require expensive per-step setup whenever domain geometry or boundary conditions change. Prior neural preconditioners (FluidNet, DCDM) handled only pure Neumann conditions and failed on mixed-boundary free-surface flows. This paper introduces a lightweight neural preconditioner that generalizes to arbitrary mixed boundary conditions and evolving domains without any runtime setup cost.

## Input / Output

The network takes a 3-channel indicator field I of shape (3, N, N) or (3, N, N, N) encoding fluid/air/solid regions, together with a full-grid residual vector r of size n_c (padded with zeros at non-fluid cells). It outputs a preconditioned search direction of the same full-grid size, from which non-fluid entries are stripped to yield the reduced-direction vector used by the NPSDO solver. The network is fully matrix-free, inferring all information about the discrete Laplacian A^I solely from the geometry image, and requires grid dimensions divisible by 2^(L-1) where L is the number of coarsening levels.

## Method

The core architecture is a multi-resolution network (L=6 levels) inspired by UNet and geometric multigrid, where each level applies a custom convolution block (CConv) whose 3×3 kernel at every spatial location is computed as an affine function of a 3×3 window in the geometry image I, yielding spatially varying weights that adapt to local boundary proximity and type. This design makes the network output a linear function of the input residual (unlike DCDM's nonlinear map), enabling its use as a true preconditioner within the NPSDO iterative solver, which handles the resulting mild asymmetry via A-orthogonalization against the last two search directions. The entire model has only ~25k parameters, enabling fast GPU evaluation (~80% of solver time) with no per-step setup. Training is unsupervised, minimizing the residual norm ‖b − A^I P^net(I, b)‖₂ over 107 matrices from 11 fluid simulation scenes, with transfer learning from a 5-level to a 6-level network.

## Results

Across 12 fluid-simulation scenes (2400 linear systems total), the proposed NPSDO solver achieves an average of 17.2 iterations and 0.267 s runtime, compared to 11.7 iter / 1.691 s for AMGCL, 43.3 iter / 0.819 s for AmgX, 203.2 iter / 2.667 s for incomplete Cholesky, and 623.1 iter / 3.232 s for unpreconditioned CG. The method is fastest on 95.6% of all systems, which account for 98.0% of total solve time. On mixed Dirichlet/Neumann boundary condition problems specifically, it outperforms all baselines on 93.27% of frames (96.36% of total solve time).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed NPSDO solver outperforms all six baselines on wall-clock time across every test case. AMGCL (algebraic multigrid) is the most effective preconditioner in terms of iteration count (11.7 vs. 17.2 for ours), but its 90% setup overhead makes it 6.3× slower overall; the paper's method converges before AMG's hierarchy rebuild even finishes. AmgX is 3× slower (0.819 s vs. 0.267 s average), and incomplete Cholesky is 10× slower (2.667 s) due to both setup cost and weaker preconditioning (203 iterations). Unpreconditioned CG is 12× slower (3.232 s, 623 iterations). Against prior neural preconditioners, DCDM requires 39 iterations and 12.35 s (46× slower than ours) on the FluidNet benchmark, while FluidNet as a standalone solver fails to reduce the residual below 2.035, and as a preconditioner stalls at 2.10×10⁻⁵ after 100 iterations without reaching the 10⁻⁶ tolerance. On mixed Dirichlet/Neumann problems where DCDM and FluidNet are inapplicable or fail to converge, the proposed method still achieves 93.27% frame-level speedups over all classical baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DCDM framework (Kaneda et al., 2023), which used a nonlinear neural network as a preconditioner for Poisson equations with pure Neumann boundary conditions, by generalizing to mixed Dirichlet/Neumann conditions and replacing the nonlinear map with a lightweight, linear multi-resolution architecture. The key architectural departure is the spatially varying convolution block, where geometry information from the domain indicator field directly modulates convolution kernel weights rather than being treated as an additional input channel (as in FluidNet or DCDM). The iterative solver (PSDO) is inherited from DCDM, but the preconditioner's linearity and the image-dependent kernel design are novel contributions that enable both faster inference and broader boundary-condition coverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For incompressible fluid simulation on structured grids, the pressure Poisson solve is the dominant per-frame cost, and this work demonstrates that a ~25k-parameter network can eliminate the expensive setup phases of AMG and IC while achieving faster convergence. The practical impact is significant for real-time and near-real-time applications (VFX, games) where per-frame solver rebuilds are prohibitive. Key limitations include restriction to structured MAC grids (no unstructured meshes), non-constant coefficients, and vector-valued unknowns; the network also processes the full domain regardless of fluid occupancy, which limits scaling for low-occupancy scenes. The spatially varying convolution remains the computational bottleneck and is not yet fully optimized, suggesting further speedups are possible.
