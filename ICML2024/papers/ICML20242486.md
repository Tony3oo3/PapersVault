## Problem

Entropy Minimization (EntMin), the standard mechanism for leveraging unlabeled samples, pushes predictions away from decision boundaries but is sample-specific and prone to being dominated by majority categories, reducing prediction diversity. The paper asks whether there exists more precise guidance information for unlabeled samples than the model's own soft-labels, and identifies that the one-hot label encodings used in ERM for labeled samples are the natural and accurate supervision signal that should also guide unlabeled sample learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242486/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of labeled samples with ground-truth one-hot labels and a (typically much larger) set of unlabeled samples, both passed through a shared feature extractor and classifier. Rather than producing per-sample pseudo-labels, LERM computes a single C-dimensional 'prediction mean' vector per category by taking a weighted average of all unlabeled samples' predicted distributions (weighted by each sample's predicted probability for that category). The output is a category-level loss: the L1 distance between each estimated label encoding and its corresponding ground-truth one-hot vector, summed uniformly across all C categories.

## Method

LERM reinterprets ERM's supervision signal: for labeled samples in category c, the guidance is the one-hot encoding e_c, and since labeled and unlabeled samples share the same category set, e_c is equally valid for unlabeled samples. Because per-sample label encodings are unavailable for unlabeled data, LERM estimates them via prediction means m_c^u = (Σ_i ỹ_{i,c}^u · ỹ_i^u) / (Σ_i ỹ_{i,c}^u), a weighted average of predicted distributions where the weight for sample i in category c is its predicted probability of belonging to c. The label-encoding risk R_ler = (1/C) Σ_c ||m_c^u − e_c||_1 is then minimized, ensuring both discriminability (predictions sharpen toward one-hot) and diversity (each category receives equal 1/C weight, preventing majority-category dominance). Theoretically, the paper proves that prediction means converge to true label encodings under perfect predictions, that LERM is upper-bounded by both ERM and EntMin under convexity conditions, and that minimizing LERM implicitly drives per-sample predictions toward one-hot vectors. LERM is designed as a plug-in: it adds a single λ-weighted term to the existing objective of any SSL, UDA, or SHDA method without modifying their architecture or training pipeline.

## Results

On the SSL task with 4 labeled samples per category, ERM+LERM achieves 75.57% Top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10 (vs. 57.04% for ERM, 59.62% for ERM+EntMin, and 52.46% for ERM+BNM) and 60.19% on CIFAR-100 (vs. 47.18% for ERM). Under the more challenging 1-label-per-category setting, ERM+LERM still improves over ERM by 5.98% on CIFAR-10 (38.22% vs. 32.24%), while EntMin and BNM both degrade below ERM. On UDA (Office-Home average), ERM+LERM reaches 69.18%, surpassing ERM (59.32%), ERM+EntMin (65.75%), and ERM+BNM (68.71%), and even outperforms standalone DANN (65.36%) and CDAN (68.70%). On SHDA (N→I), ERM+LERM achieves 79.04% accuracy, exceeding ERM by 11.62% and ERM+BNM by 1.46%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LERM consistently outperforms EntMin (Grandvalet & Bengio, 2004) across all settings: on CIFAR-10 with 4 labels/category, ERM+LERM (75.57%) beats ERM+EntMin (59.62%) by 15.95%, and with 1 label/category, ERM+LERM (38.22%) beats ERM+EntMin (28.17%) by 10.05% while EntMin actually degrades below ERM. LERM also surpasses BNM (Cui et al., 2020) in nearly all comparisons, e.g., ERM+LERM (75.57%) vs. ERM+BNM (52.46%) on CIFAR-10 (4 labels), and SDAT+LERM (72.66% avg) vs. SDAT+BNM (71.61% avg) on Office-Home. As a plugin, LERM improves FlexMatch (Zhang et al., 2021) by up to 12.83% Top-1 on CIFAR-10 (1 label: 53.69% vs. 40.86%) and DST (Chen et al., 2022) by up to 10.87% on CIFAR-100 (1 label: 43.78% vs. 32.92%). On UDA, ERM+LERM (69.18% avg on Office-Home) outperforms standalone DANN (Ganin et al., 2016) (65.36%), AFN (Xu et al., 2019) (67.97%), and CDAN (Long et al., 2018) (68.70%), while CDAN+LERM (70.13%) and SDAT (Rangwani et al., 2022)+LERM (72.66%) further improve over their respective baselines. On SHDA, KPG (Gu et al., 2022)+LERM achieves 80.17% on N→I, marginally exceeding KPG+BNM (79.96%) and substantially outperforming KPG+ERM (67.71%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper rethinks the fundamental question of what guidance information to use for unlabeled samples, departing from the standard EntMin approach (which uses model-assigned soft-labels) and BNM (which maximizes nuclear norm of predictions). The key conceptual move is recognizing that ERM's guidance for labeled samples is the one-hot label encoding, and that prediction means of unlabeled samples can serve as estimates of these encodings, yielding a new label-encoding risk. LERM is positioned as a generalization of ERM to unlabeled samples and a drop-in plugin for existing SSL, UDA, and SHDA methods, rather than a standalone framework. The theoretical analysis explicitly connects LERM to both ERM (upper-bounding relationship under convexity) and EntMin (upper-bounding relationship under cross-entropy), clarifying its position in the landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LERM addresses a well-known failure mode of EntMin—loss of prediction diversity under class imbalance—by being category-specific rather than sample-specific, and it does so with a single hyperparameter and minimal computational overhead. The consistent improvements across SSL, UDA, SHDA, and even source-free domain adaptation suggest broad applicability as a regularization plugin. Open questions include whether the approach extends to open-set or few-shot settings where the category set is not fully known, and whether the prediction-mean estimation remains reliable when the initial model is poorly calibrated. The paper's reliance on a fixed category set (shared between labeled and unlabeled data) is a limitation that constrains applicability to truly open-world scenarios.
