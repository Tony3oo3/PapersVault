## Problem

Self-attention's quadratic time and memory complexity in sequence length is the primary bottleneck for scaling Transformer language models to long contexts, and recent theoretical results (Alman & Song, 2023) show that sub-quadratic approximation of softmax attention is intractable under SETH. The paper asks whether an alternative similarity kernel can match softmax attention quality while admitting efficient low-dimensional approximation, and answers affirmatively with high-degree polynomial kernels.

## Input / Output

The model consumes text token sequences of up to 32k tokens (GPT-2 style decoder-only architecture) and produces next-token predictions via causal (lower-triangular) attention. Query and key vectors of head dimension h (64–256) are first passed through layer normalization before polynomial attention is computed; the attention output is a weighted sum of value vectors where weights are normalized p-th powers of inner products. The sketch size r (32 or 64) and block size b (1024) are the key hyperparameters controlling the approximation-accuracy vs. speed tradeoff.

## Method

PolySketchFormer replaces softmax attention with degree-p polynomial attention (p≥4), which empirically matches softmax quality on language modeling, selective copying, and induction head tasks. To compute polynomial attention in linear time, the paper applies polynomial sketching from numerical linear algebra (Ahle et al., 2020) to obtain a low-dimensional approximate feature mapping φ′: R^h → R^{r²} such that ⟨φ′(x), φ′(y)⟩ ≈ ⟨x,y⟩^p with provable ℓ₂ error bounds. A key technical contribution is a 'self-tensoring' trick that squares a degree-p/2 sketch to produce a degree-p sketch while guaranteeing non-negative attention weights, avoiding the training instability caused by negative weights. The random projection matrices in the sketch are optionally replaced by small learnable dense networks (learned sketches), yielding further quality gains. For causal masking, a block-based lower-triangular multiplication algorithm computes lt_△(A·Bᵀ)·C in O(n·b·(m+k)) time with only t = n/b sequential steps, and within each block the exact polynomial attention weight is used instead of the sketch approximation, improving model quality at negligible cost.

## Results

For GPT-2 small style models at 32k context length, Polysketch (learned + local, r=32) achieves a 2x training speedup over FlashAttention (fastest configuration) while matching or slightly beating softmax perplexity (11.47 vs 11.55 on PG-19, 14.32 vs 14.35 on Wiki-40B). On C4 downstream tasks, Polysketch (learned+local, r=64) achieves 17.98 C4 perplexity vs 17.81 for softmax (small model), 14.16 vs 13.98 (medium), and 12.70 vs 12.71 (large), with competitive or superior 0-shot/5-shot accuracies on HellaSwag, PIQA, and Physics. All kernel-based methods (Polysketch, Performer) maintain nearly constant training speed across context lengths from 512 to 32k, while softmax/FlashAttention speed degrades quadratically.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FlashAttention (Dao et al., 2022), Polysketch (learned+local, r=32) achieves a 2x training speedup at 32k context length (1.98 vs 1.03 steps/sec) while matching or slightly beating perplexity (11.47 vs 11.55 on PG-19). With r=64, the speedup narrows to ~10% (1.12 vs 1.03 steps/sec) but perplexity improves further (11.34 vs 11.55). Against Performer (Choromanski et al., 2020), Polysketch clearly wins on quality: at 32k context on PG-19, Polysketch (learned+local, r=64) achieves 11.34 perplexity versus Performer's 14.17, a gap of nearly 3 perplexity points. On Wiki-40B at 32k, the gap is even larger (14.29 vs 18.40). On downstream C4 tasks, Polysketch (learned+local, r=64) matches or exceeds softmax on most metrics (e.g., 30.6% vs 27.8% HellaSwag 5-shot, 32.3% vs 27.5% Physics 5-shot) while Performer is not directly compared in Table 1 but shows substantially worse perplexity in Tables 2–3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of work on approximating softmax attention (Performer, Random Feature Attention, sparsification methods) by instead replacing the exponential kernel with a high-degree polynomial kernel, which admits finite-dimensional feature maps amenable to efficient sketching. The sketching construction builds directly on the oblivious polynomial kernel sketches of Ahle et al. (2020), extending them with a 'self-tensoring' trick to guarantee non-negative attention weights—a property prior sketches lacked and which is critical for training stability. The block-based lower triangular multiplication for causal masking is a new algorithmic contribution that addresses the sequential-dependence bottleneck in RNN-style linear attention, complementing but distinct from FlashAttention's I/O-aware tiling approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2× training speedup over FlashAttention at 32k context with no quality degradation (and provable approximation guarantees) offers a practical path to longer-context training without the O(n²) bottleneck, and the key insight—that polynomial attention of degree ≥4 empirically matches softmax—opens a new design axis beyond approximating the exponential kernel. Limitations include evaluation only at GPT-2 scale (≤700M parameters), reliance on learned sketches whose optimal network architecture is unexplored, and untested applicability to encoder models or vision. Open questions include whether the speedup persists at much larger scales, how inference-time KV-cache benefits materialize, and whether combining polynomial sketching with sparsification (e.g., HyperAttention) yields further gains.
