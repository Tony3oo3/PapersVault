## Problem

GNNSAFE, the current state-of-the-art for node-level OOD detection on graphs, aggregates negative energy scores across neighboring nodes but is vulnerable to extreme score values that propagate through the graph, inflating variance in both ID and OOD score distributions and increasing their overlap. The paper identifies two root causes: the unboundedness of negative energy scores (fundamental cause) and logit shifts during supervised training (direct cause), neither of which GNNSAFE addresses.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a single graph (or multi-graph for Twitch) with node feature matrices and adjacency structure, processed by a GNN encoder (GCN, GAT, JKNet, MixHop, or MLP) to produce per-node logits. These logits are converted to negative energy scores, which are then iteratively aggregated over k hops using a label-propagation-style update. The final output is a per-node score used with a threshold γ (calibrated to 95% ID recall) to classify each node as ID or OOD; in the OOD-exposure setting, a squared hinge loss further separates ID and OOD energy intervals.

## Method

NODESAFE augments GNNSAFE's energy-based OOD loss with two regularization terms combined as L_UB. L_bound minimizes the coefficient of variation of logit 2-norms across all nodes, which by Proposition 3.3 constrains negative energy scores to the interval [log C − M_norm/√C, log C + M_norm/√C], eliminating the unboundedness that generates extreme scores. L_uniform minimizes the variance of per-node logit summations (Σ(z_v)) separately for ID and OOD samples, directly counteracting logit shifts that alter energy scores without affecting classification accuracy. The total loss is L_ALL = L_OOD + λ₂·L_UB, where L_UB = λ₁·L_uniform + (1−λ₁)·L_bound. The paper provides gradient analyses showing L_bound pulls individual 2-norms toward the mean with strength proportional to |z_i|, while L_uniform shifts the entire logit vector uniformly (preserving classification) to reduce summation variance.

## Results

NODESAFE achieves a 28.4% average FPR95 reduction over GNNSAFE (the prior SOTA) for Structure Manipulation OOD data without OOD exposure, and 22.7% with OOD exposure. On Cora-Structure (no OOD exposure), FPR95 drops from 73.15 (GNNSAFE) to 25.63 (NODESAFE), while AUROC rises from 87.52 to 94.07. On the multi-graph Twitch dataset, NODESAFE++ achieves an FPR95 of 3.43 versus GNNSAFE++'s 33.57 (a 90% reduction). The method also improves convergence speed, reaching best validation loss at epoch 61 on Cora versus GNNSAFE's epoch 148.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NODESAFE substantially outperforms the prior SOTA GNNSAFE across all datasets and OOD types: on Cora-Structure FPR95 drops from 73.15 to 25.63 (65% reduction), and on Pubmed-Structure from 44.64 to 23.80 (47% reduction). Against vision-domain baselines (MSP, ODIN, Mahalanobis, Energy), NODESAFE achieves 2–4× lower FPR95 in most settings; ODIN and Mahalanobis are particularly weak, often scoring near 100% FPR95. The graph-specific baselines GKDE and GPN are also clearly outperformed, with NODESAFE reducing FPR95 by 20–60 points on Cora-Structure. Compared to LogitNorm (GNNSAFE w/ L_LN), NODESAFE achieves consistently lower FPR95 (e.g., 25.63 vs. 61.04 on Cora-Structure), demonstrating that variance-based bounding is more effective than simple logit normalization. In the OOD-exposure setting, NODESAFE++ achieves FPR95 of 3.43 on Twitch versus GNNSAFE++'s 33.57, a 90% improvement, while maintaining competitive ID accuracy (71.85% vs. 70.18%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GNNSAFE (Wu et al., 2023), which introduced negative energy score aggregation inspired by label propagation for node-level OOD detection on graphs, by identifying that the aggregation process is vulnerable to extreme scores caused by two factors: the unboundedness of negative energy scores and logit shifts. The proposed NODESAFE adds two optimization terms—L_bound (constraining the variance of logit 2-norms) and L_uniform (constraining the variance of logit summations)—to the GNNSAFE training objective, building on the energy-based OOD framework of Liu et al. (2020). The paper also positions itself against LogitNorm (Wei et al., 2022), arguing that mathematical normalization of logits is insufficient compared to explicit variance-reduction regularization during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Node-level OOD detection on graphs remains an underexplored problem relative to graph-level or image-level OOD detection, and this paper provides the first method to simultaneously constrain both upper and lower bounds of logit 2-norms for this purpose. The substantial FPR95 reductions (up to 28.4%) on Structure Manipulation OOD are practically meaningful for security-sensitive graph applications, though the gains on temporal OOD (Arxiv) are more modest, suggesting the approach works best when ID and OOD score distributions are well-separated. Open questions include whether the two-term objective can be simplified, whether the method generalizes beyond energy-based scoring to other node-level detection frameworks, and how it behaves on much larger or heterogeneous graphs.
