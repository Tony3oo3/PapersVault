## Problem

Deeper GNNs suffer from oversmoothing (node representations converging to indistinguishable constants) and oversquashing (distant messages compressed into fixed-size vectors), but prior remedies—graph rewiring, regularization, residual connections—address symptoms rather than the root cause. This paper identifies that both issues originate from heterophily mixing in aggregation, where messages of different category semantics are indiscriminately combined, causing irreversible information loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241624/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph G=(V,E) with a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{|V|×m} and produces node representation vectors Z for classification. Unusually, the internal state is a 3rd-order tensor M ∈ R^{|T|×|V|×d} storing per-track, per-node messages, and a soft node-track affiliation matrix F ∈ R^{|T|×|V|} learned via attention. The number of tracks |T| must equal the number of node categories, and the multi-stage pipeline consumes pseudo-labels and representative nodes from prior stages as additional input.

## Method

MTGCN creates |T| isomorphic 'tracks' mirroring the graph topology, each dedicated to one category, so that messages of different semantics propagate independently and never mix. In each track, message passing uses GCN-style normalized adjacency aggregation but omits learnable weights and activations (following SGC), and incorporates initial residual connections to stabilize deep propagation. Node-track affiliations F are computed by dot-product attention between auxiliary node representations (from a simple 2-layer GCN) and track prototypes (averages of high-confidence representative nodes per category). A multi-stage self-evolutionary training pipeline iteratively refines pseudo-labels and representative nodes, progressively improving affiliation accuracy. The key architectural distinction from prior multi-channel models (e.g., DisenGCN) is that messages are decoupled from node representations, allowing them to pass through any node without being fused, which enables long-distance information flow without heterophilic mixing.

## Results

In the semi-supervised setting (Table 2), MTGCN-s3 achieves the best accuracy on Cora (86.40%) and Citeseer (74.60%), while MTGCN-s4 leads on Coauthor-CS (92.54%) and Coauthor-Physics (94.72%). In the fully-supervised setting (Table 3), MTGCN achieves the best accuracy on all three heterophilic graphs (Cornell 89.47%, Texas 92.10%, Wisconsin 90.20%) and on Cora (90.61%). For oversmoothing, MTGCN maintains stable accuracy (86.4% at 64 layers on Cora) while GCN drops to 28.7% and GAT to 14.9%. On the Tree-NeighborsMatch oversquashing benchmark, MTGCN achieves near-perfect training accuracy across all tree depths, while all other baselines degrade sharply beyond depth 5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MTGCN outperforms all 22 baselines on the majority of datasets. In the semi-supervised setting, MTGCN-s3 beats the strongest baselines GCNII (85.30%), PDE-GCN (84.30%), and ACMP (84.91%) on Cora by 1.1–2.1 points, and MTGCN-s4 dominates on Coauthor-CS (92.54% vs. GREAD's 88.52%) and Coauthor-Physics (94.72% vs. DisenGCN's 94.28%). The only semi-supervised loss is on Citeseer, where PDE-GCN (75.60%) edges out MTGCN-s3 (74.60%). In the fully-supervised setting, MTGCN achieves the best accuracy on all three heterophilic graphs (Cornell, Texas, Wisconsin), outperforming dedicated heterophily methods like GGCN, H2GCN, and ACM-GCN by 1–5 points, but trails GCNII (90.30%) on Pubmed and GGCN (77.14%) on Citeseer. For the oversmoothing depth experiment, MTGCN sustains 86.4% accuracy at 64 layers on Cora, far exceeding GCNII (85.3%) and PDE-GCN (84.3%), while GCN and GAT collapse to 28.7% and 14.9% respectively. On the Tree-NeighborsMatch oversquashing benchmark, MTGCN is the only method maintaining high accuracy beyond tree depth 5, where all other baselines (GCN, GAT, DropEdge, JKNet, GCNII, PDE-GCN, DisenGCN) degrade markedly.

## Relation to Prior Work

MTGCN builds directly on the GCN message passing framework (Kipf & Welling, 2017), reusing its normalized adjacency aggregation per track, while drawing on SGC (Wu et al., 2019) for omitting per-layer weights and GCNII (Chen et al., 2020b) for initial residual connections. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant line of oversmoothing/oversquashing remedies—graph rewiring, curvature-guided edge modification, spectral gap optimization, and regularization—by instead modifying the message passing architecture itself through category-separated tracks. The closest prior work is DisenGCN (Ma et al., 2019) and its multi-channel descendants, but MTGCN distinguishes itself by decoupling messages from node representations entirely, enabling non-local heterophily prevention rather than only local neighborhood routing. The theoretical motivation for the separation condition improvement is borrowed from Wei et al. (2020) on semi-supervised learning error bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a clean unifying diagnosis—heterophily mixing as the shared root cause of both oversmoothing and oversquashing—and a corresponding architectural fix that is easy to reason about. However, the space complexity of O(d|T||V|η) is a serious practical constraint: with many categories or large graphs, the multi-track tensor becomes intractable, as the authors themselves acknowledge with the Tree-NeighborsMatch degradation beyond depth 7. The node-track affiliation accuracy (65–85%) is far from perfect, and the multi-stage self-evolutionary training pipeline adds implementation complexity and sensitivity to the auxiliary model Ψ. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to graphs where the number of categories is unknown or very large, and whether a more powerful auxiliary model or learned soft affiliations could close the remaining gap to the idealized perfect-F scenario.
