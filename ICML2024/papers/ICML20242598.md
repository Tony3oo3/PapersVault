## Problem

Spiking Transformers achieve near-ANN accuracy with energy-efficient neuromorphic inference, but their GPU training is substantially slower than ANN counterparts due to the extra temporal dimension, and existing SNN pruning methods target inference efficiency rather than training speed. Conventional token sparsification metrics (random sampling, l1-norm, SSA-based ranking) cause noticeable accuracy drops because they fail to capture semantic foreground information and lack a standardized, consistent criterion for token importance across timesteps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242598/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet, 32×32 for CIFAR-10/100) or event streams (128×128 for CIFAR10-DVS and DVS128 Gesture), which are first processed by a spiking front-end (Conv2d → BN → LIF neuron → MaxPool) and then patchified into token sequences of shape T×N×D across T timesteps. A Timestep-wise Anchor Token is prepended to each timestep's sequence, and tokens are selectively pruned at designated transformer layers (every 3 blocks from block L/3 onward), reducing the token count for subsequent layers. The output is a standard class label predicted via Global Average Pooling and a classification head.

## Method

STATA introduces a Timestep-wise Anchor Token (TAT) — a learnable token prepended to each timestep's patch sequence — whose self-attention scores to all other tokens provide a standardized, timestep-specific importance ranking for token pruning at negligible extra cost (extracted directly from the existing SSA tensor). To address quality disparities in attention maps that undermine pruning accuracy, STATA adds two alignment losses during training: Intra-Alignment sorts attention maps by l2-norm within a layer and aligns the bottom half to the top half along both the timestep and head dimensions, while Inter-Alignment uses deep-layer attention maps (detached) to supervise shallow-layer maps via l2 distance after size-matching interpolation. The total training objective combines cross-entropy with weighted intra- and inter-alignment terms (α=β=1e-2), and the resulting token-pruned model achieves ~1.5× training speedup and ~48% energy reduction with comparable accuracy across Spikformer, Spike-driven Transformer, and Spikingformer architectures.

## Results

On ImageNet with Spikformer-8-768, STATA achieves 74.03% top-1 accuracy with ~1.53× training speedup, ~52% OPs reduction, and ~48% energy reduction compared to the original model (74.81%). On CIFAR-100 with Spikingformer, STATA reaches 79.9% top-1 accuracy (vs. 80.0% original) while reducing training memory by ~36% and inference energy by ~45%. Across neuromorphic datasets, STATA on Spikingformer matches the original on DVS128 Gesture (98.6%) and is within 0.2% on CIFAR10-DVS (81.2% vs. 81.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet (Spikformer-8-768), STATA (74.03%) substantially outperforms both Random Token pruning (70.13%) and SSA-based pruning (70.45%) by ~3.6–3.9% top-1 accuracy at the same ~1.53× speedup, while remaining within 0.78% of the unpruned Spikformer (74.81%). On CIFAR-100, STATA on Spikingformer (79.9%) nearly matches the original (80.0%) and exceeds the SSA-based baseline (78.47%) by 1.43%, confirming that the Timestep-wise Anchor Token and dual Alignments recover the accuracy lost by naive sparsification. Across the three Spiking Transformer architectures (Spikformer, Spike-driven T, Spikingformer), STATA consistently stays within 0.1–0.7% of each original model on all four datasets, demonstrating architecture-agnostic transferability. Compared to non-Transformer SNN baselines (e.g., STBP-tdBN at 70.9% on CIFAR-100, SEW-ResNet-152 at 69.26% on ImageNet), STATA-equipped Spiking Transformers maintain a clear accuracy advantage while simultaneously reducing training time and inference energy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Spikformer (Zhou et al., 2023b) Spiking Self Attention mechanism and extends the broader Spiking Transformer family (Spike-driven Transformer, Spikingformer) by introducing a token-level sparsification framework. It departs from prior SNN pruning work, which has focused almost exclusively on weight sparsity for inference energy savings, by targeting the underexplored problem of training-time acceleration through token reduction. The Timestep-wise Anchor Token repurposes the existing self-attention computation as a free importance scorer, while the dual Alignments losses draw on the knowledge-distillation intuition that deeper features should supervise shallower ones, adapted to the temporal and multi-head structure of Spiking Transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Spiking Transformers offer energy-efficient inference on neuromorphic hardware, but their training cost (thousands of GPU-hours on ImageNet) remains a major barrier to adoption; STATA addresses this with a plug-and-play sparsification module that yields ~1.5× training speedup and ~48% inference energy reduction while preserving accuracy. The finding that moderate token sparsification (γ=0.6–0.8) can actually improve accuracy over the unpruned model is a useful counterintuitive result suggesting that redundant tokens may introduce noise. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger Spiking Transformers (e.g., 24+ layers), whether the alignment losses transfer to other attention-based SNN architectures beyond the three tested, and whether the method remains effective at higher sparsity levels (γ < 0.2) where accuracy drops more sharply.
