## Problem

Despite co-training's information-theoretic guarantee of error-independent label complexity, very few hypothesis classes are known to be efficiently learnable via co-training under the realistic λ-weak dependence assumption, because the standard agreement-maximization step is computationally intractable. The paper asks whether a natural algorithmic template exists that achieves both label and computational efficiency for a broad range of concept classes.

## Input / Output

The learner receives a single-pass stream of unlabeled examples, each decomposed into two views (x₁, x₂), and makes irrevocable adaptive decisions on whether to query the true label for each arriving example. The output is a hypothesis pair (h₁, h₂) over the two views whose combined error under the marginal distribution D is at most ε with probability 1−δ. The streaming constraint (pool size one) is weaker than the pool-based active model used in prior empirical co-training work, and the label oracle is only accessible adaptively during the stream.

## Method

The core contribution is a black-box reduction (Algorithm 1) that transforms any mistake-bound online learner into a co-training algorithm: two online learners run simultaneously on the two views, and a label is queried only when the two hypotheses disagree on the current example (guaranteeing at least one makes a mistake) or when the current hypothesis pair is close to a constant. Under λ-weak dependence, Lemma 2.3 ensures that a hypothesis pair with low unlabeled disagreement is close to ±h* or ±1, so the algorithm can verify correctness using only O(log(1/δ)) additional labeled examples. This yields Õ(M/α) label queries for any class with mistake bound M, independent of ε. For classes with infinite Littlestone dimension (k-unions of intervals, homogeneous halfspaces), the paper designs specialized efficient algorithms: for intervals, a structural update rule that shrinks error regions by constant factors per mistake; for halfspaces, a recursive Forster-decomposition-based approach that creates margin structure on subspaces to enable perceptron-style learning without a global margin assumption.

## Results

The paper establishes a black-box reduction (Theorem 3.2) showing that any hypothesis class learnable in the mistake-bound online model with mistake bound M yields a co-training algorithm with label complexity $\tilde{O}(M/\alpha)$, independent of the accuracy parameter $\epsilon$. For $\gamma$-margin halfspaces, this gives $\tilde{O}(1/(\gamma^2\alpha))$ label queries in linear time per iteration (Corollary 3.3). For general VC classes of dimension $d$, the label complexity is $\tilde{O}(d\log(1/(\lambda\epsilon))/\alpha)$ (Theorem 4.1), and for k-unions of intervals it is $\tilde{O}(k^2\log(1/(\lambda\epsilon))/\alpha)$ (Theorem 4.2). For homogeneous halfspaces without a margin assumption, the label complexity is $\tilde{O}(d^2\log(1/\epsilon))$ under an approximate symmetry condition (Theorem 4.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary prior work to compare against is Blum & Mansour (2017), which gave the first efficient co-training algorithm for $\gamma$-margin halfspaces under weak dependence but required additional distributional assumptions (homogeneity and zero-mean marginal) and relied on solving polynomially many convex programs. The present work removes these extra distributional assumptions and achieves linear time per iteration via the perceptron-based reduction. Compared to standard passive supervised learning with label complexity $\tilde{O}(d/\epsilon)$, the co-training algorithms achieve an exponential improvement in label complexity for classes with finite Littlestone dimension (error-independent queries). For classes with infinite Littlestone dimension (e.g., k-unions of intervals, homogeneous halfspaces), the label complexity still improves over the $\Omega(1/\epsilon)$ lower bound of standard active learning, though the dependence on $\epsilon$ is only logarithmic. No prior work had established any efficient co-training algorithm under weak dependence for the Boolean classes in Table 1 (disjunctions, conjunctions, decision lists, DNF, CNF), making these the first such results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the weak-dependence co-training framework of Blum & Mansour (2017), whose key structural lemma (Lemma 2.3) is reused throughout, and extends it from a passive/pool-based setting to the stream-based active learning model where no provable co-training guarantees previously existed. The central methodological contribution is a black-box reduction from co-training to mistake-bound online learning, drawing on the Halving algorithm (Littlestone, 1988) and the perceptron, while the homogeneous halfspace result additionally leverages Forster decomposition (Diakonikolas et al., 2023b) to manufacture margin structure absent in the original distribution. The ε-cover construction from Hanneke & Yang (2015) is used to extend the reduction to general VC classes beyond those with finite Littlestone dimension.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing gap by providing the first provably efficient co-training algorithms under weak dependence for a broad range of concept classes (halfspaces, k-intervals, DNF/CNF, decision lists), where prior work either required stronger distributional assumptions or was computationally intractable. The error-independent label complexity (polylog in 1/ε rather than 1/ε) is a significant theoretical improvement over passive learning, and the clean reduction to online learning offers a reusable template for future co-training algorithm design. Open questions include whether the dependence on the label-balance parameter α is truly necessary for computational efficiency, and whether the framework extends to more complex hypothesis classes or to the pool-based active model with larger pools.
