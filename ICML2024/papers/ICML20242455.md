## Problem

Recommendation models lack the predictable scaling laws observed in large language models: their traditional upscaling mechanism (expanding embedding tables) does not improve the model's ability to capture complex feature interactions and misaligns with hardware compute advancements. This paper asks whether a dense-scaling architecture can establish a reliable scaling law for recommendation, where quality improves continuously with compute, parameters, and data size.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242455/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a blend of categorical sparse features (e.g., user click history) and continuous dense features (e.g., timestamps), each transformed into a standardized d-dimensional embedding via lookup tables or MLPs, yielding an input tensor X₀ ∈ ℝ^{n×d} where n is the total number of embeddings. Unlike DCN-style approaches that flatten to ℝ^{nd}, Wukong treats each embedding vector as an indivisible unit for interaction computation. The output is a scalar click or conversion probability score produced by a final MLP head on top of the interaction stack.

## Method

Wukong's core architecture is an Interaction Stack of l identical layers, each containing a Factorization Machine Block (FMB: pairwise dot-product FM followed by an MLP that reshapes the flattened interaction matrix back into n_F embeddings) and a Linear Compression Block (LCB: a learned linear recombination of input embeddings that preserves interaction order). Inspired by binary exponentiation, layer i captures feature interactions of order 1 through 2^i, so stacking l layers yields arbitrary-order interactions with only logarithmic depth. Residual connections and layer normalization stabilize training across the stack. An optimized FM exploits the low-rank structure of the pairwise dot-product matrix (valid when d ≤ n), multiplying by a learnable projection Y to reduce compute from O(n²d) to O(nkd). Scaling is achieved by first increasing layer count l, then enlarging n_F, n_L, k, and MLP width, with the total complexity approximately O(ndh log n + h²).

## Results

On six public datasets, Wukong achieves the best AUC across all of them: 0.9868 (Frappe), 0.7292 (MicroVideo), 0.9723 (MovieLens Latest), 0.7414 (KuaiVideo), 0.6488 (TaobaoAds), and 0.8106 (Criteo Terabyte). On the 146B-sample internal dataset with 720 features, Wukong outperforms all baselines by over 0.2% in Relative LogLoss and maintains a consistent scaling law across two orders of magnitude in model complexity (extending beyond 100 GFLOP/example), where prior architectures plateau or fail to train stably. Even the strongest baseline (DCNv2) requires a 40× increase in compute complexity to match Wukong's quality at comparable scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Wukong achieves the best AUC on all six public datasets, with the closest competitors being FinalMLP (tied at 0.9868 on Frappe and 0.9723 on MovieLens) and MaskNet (0.8100 vs. 0.8106 on Criteo TB). On the large-scale internal dataset (146B samples, 720 features), Wukong surpasses all baselines by >0.2% Relative LogLoss at every complexity level tested. Among baselines, AFN+, DLRM, and FinalMLP plateau in quality beyond ~31–36 GFLOP/example, while AutoInt+, DCNv2, and MaskNet fail to sustain quality gains at higher complexity (some even suffer training instability or loss explosion). DCNv2, the strongest baseline on the internal dataset, requires a 40× increase in compute to match Wukong's quality. xDeepFM was excluded from internal dataset experiments due to out-of-memory issues from its expensive outer-product operation at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

Wukong builds directly on Factorization Machines (Rendle 2010; Naumov et al. 2019) as its core interaction primitive, but introduces a fundamentally new architectural pattern: stacking FMs in a binary-exponentiation-inspired fashion so that each successive layer captures exponentially higher-order interactions (up to 2^l). It departs from the dominant sparse-scaling paradigm (expanding embedding tables à la DLRM, PERSIA) by focusing on dense scaling of the interaction component, and from attention-based or cross-network architectures (AutoInt+, DCNv2) by using bit-wise MLPs over flattened embeddings rather than embedding-wise FFNs, combined with a pyramid-shaped Linear Compression Block that contracts the number of embeddings per layer. The optimized FM leverages the low-rank dot-product compression observed in Wang et al. 2021 and Sharma 2023 to reduce quadratic complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration of a scaling law in the recommendation domain, showing that model quality improves predictably across two orders of magnitude in compute complexity (>100 GFLOP/example) where all prior architectures plateau or destabilize. The practical implication is significant: a single architecture can serve as a backbone from small vertical models to large foundational recommendation models, with quality scaling in lockstep with compute budget. Key open questions remain: the exact scalability ceiling is unknown (compute-limited), the theoretical underpinnings of why stacked FMs scale while attention/cross-networks do not are not yet formalized, and generalizability beyond recommendation to other heterogeneous-feature domains is untested. The proprietary 146B-sample internal dataset also limits independent verification of the scaling-law claim.
