## Problem

Existing monocular dynamic 3D reconstruction methods assume a single correct solution exists and impose additional constraints (depth priors, camera poses, shape templates) to recover one 'most plausible' scene. However, a monocular video inherently admits infinitely many valid 3D scene configurations due to the scale-motion ambiguity, and no prior work learns or represents all of them. The core difficulty is inferring accurate per-object scale ranges that are tightly coupled with object-camera joint motions and mutual visual occlusions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB video of N frames containing K rigid objects (no depth scans or camera poses required). Preprocessing uses SAM, TAM, RAFT, and per-object SfM to extract object masks, relative camera-to-object poses, and relative depths. At test time, the framework samples up to 1000 scale combinations from the learned valid ranges and renders novel views, depth maps, and object segmentations for each sampled 3D scene configuration, reporting the best-matching result against ground truth.

## Method

OSN comprises three components: (1) per-object scale-invariant TensoRF representations learned within a unit 3D volume, (2) a 4-layer MLP object scale network that takes a sampled K-object scale vector (one object fixed as anchor at scale 1, remaining K−1 sampled from [0,1)) and predicts a validity score in [0,1], and (3) a joint optimization module using two key rendering techniques. Scaled composite rendering transforms sampled points between object spaces via scale-aware coordinate transforms and composites colors/densities across all K objects for scene-level RGB, depth, and segmentation losses. Soft Z-buffer rendering provides a fast approximation for the scale network's supervision: it independently renders per-object depths once, then computes object segmentation for H scale combinations via a softmax over scaled depths, enabling binary cross-entropy training of the MLP without H full composite renders. Training follows a two-stage procedure: Stage 1 bootstraps per-object TensoRF models independently, then Stage 2 alternates R=5 rounds of optimizing the scale network (BCE on pseudo-ground-truth validity) and the object representations (scene-level RGB + depth + segmentation losses).

## Results

On the Dynamic Indoor Scene Dataset (per-object SfM depth setting), OSN achieves PSNR 25.98, SSIM 0.861, LPIPS 0.115, and SSIMAE 0.094, substantially outperforming all baselines. On the Oxford Multimotion Dataset, OSN reaches PSNR 19.67 and LPIPS 0.155, and on the NVIDIA Dynamic Scene Dataset it achieves PSNR 29.59 and LPIPS 0.053. The SSIMAE of 0.094 on the synthetic dataset is particularly notable, demonstrating superior 3D geometry recovery compared to the next-best method (Total-Recon at 0.137). In the multi-ground-truth evaluation (Table 2), OSN achieves PSNR 22.94±1.00 across 50 different ground truth scene configurations, while baselines show much higher variance (e.g., NSFF: 18.21±0.95).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSN outperforms all five baselines across all three datasets under the fairest comparison setting (per-object SfM depth). Against the strongest single-solution baseline Total-Recon (which additionally uses MiDaS depth for scale alignment), OSN gains +1.29 dB PSNR on the Dynamic Indoor Scene, +1.34 dB on Oxford Multimotion, and +1.77 dB on NVIDIA Dynamic Scene. Among the four monocular baselines (NSFF, DynNeRF, TiNeuVox, HexPlane), OSN leads by 3.8–5.8 dB PSNR on the synthetic dataset and 1.6–2.5 dB on real-world datasets. The depth estimation advantage is most pronounced: OSN's SSIMAE of 0.094 on the Dynamic Indoor Scene is 33% lower than Total-Recon's 0.137 and over 4× lower than the best monocular baseline (TiNeuVox at 0.368). In the multi-ground-truth evaluation (50 scene configurations), OSN's lower variance (±1.004 PSNR vs. ±1.535 for Total-Recon) confirms its ability to match diverse valid 3D explanations, while baselines produce a single fixed solution regardless of the ground truth configuration.

## Relation to Prior Work

OSN builds on the per-object rigid decomposition strategy established by Total-Recon and STaR, using TensoRF for scale-invariant per-object shape/appearance and SAM/SfM/RAFT for preprocessing. However, it fundamentally departs from the entire line of monocular dynamic 3D reconstruction work (NSFF, DynNeRF, TiNeuVox, HexPlane, Total-Recon) that formulates the problem as recovering a single most-plausible solution via additional constraints (depth priors, camera poses, shape templates). Instead, OSN introduces an Object Scale Network—a 4-layer MLP that predicts validity scores for sampled multi-object scale combinations—combined with scaled composite rendering and a soft Z-buffer rendering trick to jointly optimize all components, enabling infinite sampling of faithful 3D scene configurations from one video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to treat monocular dynamic 3D reconstruction as a multi-solution problem, which is arguably the correct formulation given the inherent scale ambiguity of monocular video. The practical impact is significant for scenarios without depth sensors or ground-contact priors (e.g., bouncing balls, flying objects), where prior single-solution methods are fundamentally limited. Key limitations include: rigid objects only (deformable scenes deferred to future work), scene-specific optimization (no generalization across scenes), and reliance on per-object SfM that can fail on challenging real-world sequences like KITTI. Open questions include whether the scale network generalizes to deformable objects, whether the approach scales to very large object counts, and whether the multi-solution representation can be made more compact or interpretable.
