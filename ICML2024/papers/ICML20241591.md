## Problem

In asymmetrical-domain text matching, text representations from different domains are initially separated in the common semantic space, yet existing models lack explicit constraints to align their distributions. While prior work (DDR-Match) showed that narrowing representation distributions improves generalization, the theoretical role of this alignment remained unclear, and the standard information bottleneck (IB) formulation does not constrain the interaction between the two text representations—a factor empirically critical for matching performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of text sentences sampled from different domains (e.g., a question and a candidate answer, or a premise and a hypothesis) and outputs a discrete semantic relationship label (entailment/contradiction/neutral or binary match). The RMIB objective operates on the intermediate representations Z₁ and Z₂ produced by the model's encoder layers before the fusion/classification head, requiring access to per-text representations rather than only the final fused output. For text-encoding models like SBERT where X₁ and X₂ do not interact before fusion, the minimality term is applied to the single fused representation Z instead of Z₁ and Z₂ separately.

## Method

RMIB extends the information bottleneck for text matching by adding two constraints beyond IB's standard sufficiency and minimality: (1) an Interaction term that maximizes I(Z₁;Z₂), and (2) an Inadequacy term that minimizes I(Y;Z₁)+I(Y;Z₂), ensuring neither single representation alone can determine the label. The key theoretical result (Theorem 2.2) shows these two constraints together are equivalent to maximizing the conditional mutual information I(Z₁;Z₂|Y), i.e., maximizing interaction given the task label. The minimality terms are implemented as KL divergences from p_θ(zᵢ|x₁,x₂) to a standard Gaussian prior p(Z) (chosen for maximum entropy / minimum prior knowledge), optimized via the reparameterization trick. The I(Z₁;Z₂) term is estimated using the MINE neural estimator (a multi-layer perceptron with sigmoid output), and the task terms reduce to standard cross-entropy and per-representation log-likelihood objectives, yielding a fully differentiable training loss with three hyperparameters α₁, α₂, α₃.

## Results

RMIB improves all four text matching models across five datasets, with the most dramatic gain on SBERT: +12.54% accuracy on SICK (72.05→84.59), +4.28% on SciTail, and +3.51% MAP on WikiQA. For interaction models, BERT(RMIB) reaches 87.40% on SICK, 95.16% F1 on SciTail, and 86.02 MRR on WikiQA. Improvements are largest on smaller datasets (SICK, SciTail, WikiQA) and smallest on SNLI and Quora, where BERT's SNLI accuracy actually drops by 0.03%. On three additional datasets (MRPC, WNLI, RTE), RMIB(BERT) gains +1.47%, +2.82%, and +6.49% accuracy respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDR-Match (Yu et al., 2022), RMIB consistently outperforms all three distance-metric variants (JS, MMD, WD) on every dataset where both are reported. The gap is most striking for SBERT: RMIB achieves 84.59% on SICK versus DDR-Match(WD)'s 64.39%, a 20-point margin, because RMIB explicitly enforces interaction between representations while DDR-Match only regularizes distributional distance. Against vanilla baselines, RMIB improves RE2 by +1.57% (SICK) and +1.68 MRR (WikiQA), BERT by +0.71% (SICK) and +1.01 MRR (WikiQA), and ESIM by +0.70% (SICK). The sole exception is BERT on SNLI, where RMIB yields a negligible −0.03% drop, which the authors attribute to SNLI's large dataset size (570k pairs) reducing the benefit of domain-alignment regularization. Overall, RMIB wins or ties on 24 out of 25 reported metric-dataset combinations across the four models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Information Bottleneck (IB) framework of Tishby et al. (2000) to the two-representation setting of asymmetrical-domain text matching, adding two new constraints—sufficient interaction between representations and inadequacy of any single representation—that standard IB does not impose. It departs from DDR-Match (Yu et al., 2022), which directly minimizes a distribution-distance metric between the two representation distributions, by instead aligning both representations to a shared prior (standard Gaussian) and proving this is equivalent to IB optimization. The theoretical bridge between domain alignment and information-theoretic minimality, and the equivalence between RMIB's constraints and conditional mutual information maximization given the label, are the paper's core conceptual contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, model-agnostic regularizer that consistently improves text matching across both interaction models (BERT, ESIM, RE2) and encoding models (SBERT), with the largest gains on SBERT where explicit interaction is absent. The theoretical equivalence results give practitioners a clear information-theoretic justification for why domain alignment helps, moving beyond heuristic regularizers. Key limitations include the need for manual hyperparameter tuning (α₁, α₂, α₃) and modest gains on large-data datasets (SNLI, Quora), suggesting the benefit diminishes when the model already generalizes well. An open question the authors flag is whether the hyperparameters can be set adaptively during training.
