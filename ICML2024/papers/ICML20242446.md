## Problem

Fine-tuning large language models faces a dual challenge: gradient-based optimizers require prohibitive memory for backpropagation, and the fine-tuning data may contain sensitive information requiring differential privacy. While zeroth-order methods eliminate the backward pass, naively combining them with standard DP-GD yields convergence rates that scale as O(d^{3/2}) in the model dimension d, making them impractical for billion-parameter models. The paper asks whether a nearly dimension-independent rate can be achieved under differential privacy with access only to a zeroth-order (forward-pass) oracle.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained language model (RoBERTa-355M or OPT up to 6.7B), a few-shot fine-tuning dataset (512 samples per class), and privacy parameters (ε, δ). It requires only forward passes to compute per-sample loss values at perturbed points x ± λu_t, with no gradient or activation storage. The output is the model weights at a uniformly random iteration τ ∈ {0, …, T−1}, which is (ε,δ)-differentially private. A key practical detail is that per-sample clipping operates on a scalar finite-difference value rather than a full gradient vector, eliminating the memory and runtime overhead that plagues DP first-order methods.

## Method

DPZERO (Algorithm 2) makes two key modifications to the naive DP-GD-with-zeroth-order-gradients approach. First, it decouples the two-point gradient estimator into a public direction u_t (sampled uniformly from the sphere √d·S^{d−1}) and a private scalar magnitude, so that only a single scalar Gaussian noise z_t is added rather than a d-dimensional noise vector, reducing the noise norm from O(dC²) to O(C²). Second, it replaces the worst-case clipping threshold C = Ld with a tighter C = Õ(L) that is nearly independent of d, justified by a tail bound showing the finite-difference |u_t^T ∇f(x;ξ_i)| exceeds O(L) only with exponentially small probability. Under a low effective rank assumption (Tr(H) ≤ r‖H‖₂ on the Hessian), the resulting convergence rate is Õ(√(r log(1/δ))/(nε)) on the squared gradient norm, matching the best known first-order DP-GD bound up to logarithmic factors in d. The total number of forward-pass evaluations is O(n²√r), and the per-iteration memory footprint is identical to the non-private zeroth-order baseline MeZO.

## Results

On RoBERTa (355M) fine-tuning, DPZERO (ε=6) achieves 92.2% accuracy on SST-2, 49.3% on SST-5, and 77.8% on SNLI, closely matching the non-private zeroth-order baseline MeZO (92.5%, 50.8%, 80.4%) while providing (ε,δ)-DP guarantees. On OPT-6.7B, DPZERO (ε=6) reaches 92.6% on SST-2 and 79.5 F1 on SQuAD, with only a 0.4–0.9% drop from MeZO. In terms of efficiency, DPZERO uses 2,668 MiB memory and 0.347 s/iteration—identical to MeZO—compared to 17,126 MiB and 2.12 s/iteration for DP-AdamW, a 6.4× memory reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the non-private zeroth-order baseline MeZO, DPZERO incurs a minimal privacy cost: at most 2.6% accuracy drop on RoBERTa (e.g., 92.2% vs 92.5% on SST-2 at ε=6) and at most 0.9% on OPT-6.7B (92.6% vs 93.0% on SST-2), confirming near-dimension-independent behavior. Compared to private first-order methods (DP-AdamW, DP-LoRA from Li et al. 2022), DPZERO achieves comparable or slightly better accuracy at ε=2 (e.g., 91.8% vs 90.5% on SST-2) while using 6.4× less memory (2,668 vs 17,126 MiB) and 6.1× less runtime per iteration (0.347 vs 2.12 s). Against the naive DP-GD with zeroth-order gradients (DPGD-0th, Algorithm 1), DPZERO eliminates the linear dimension dependence in the convergence rate, achieving O((log d)√r) vs O(d√r) under low effective rank. On OPT models up to 6.7B parameters, DPZERO matches MeZO's scalability (both fit in 24 GiB GPU memory) whereas first-order methods already run out of memory at OPT-1.3B. The synthetic quadratic experiments (Figure 1) confirm that DPZERO becomes nearly dimension-independent when the effective rank is O(log d), matching first-order DP-GD, while DPGD-0th degrades linearly with d regardless of rank.

## Relation to Prior Work

DPZERO sits at the intersection of two active research threads: zeroth-order fine-tuning of LLMs (MeZO, Malladi et al. 2023) and differentially private optimization (DP-GD, Abadi et al. 2016). The paper first shows that naively combining these (DPGD-0th) inherits an O(d^{3/2}) dimension dependence from both the zeroth-order gradient norm and the d-dimensional privacy noise, even under low effective rank. The key departure is a two-part fix: (1) recognizing that the random direction u_t is public, so only the scalar finite-difference magnitude needs clipping and noise, and (2) using a tighter, nearly dimension-independent clipping threshold justified by concentration of the directional derivative. This yields the first nearly dimension-independent convergence guarantee for DP zeroth-order optimization, matching the rate of first-order DP-GD under low-rank Hessian structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: DPZERO enables differentially private fine-tuning of 6.7B-parameter OPT models on a single 24 GiB consumer GPU with near-zero additional memory or runtime overhead compared to non-private MeZO, whereas first-order DP methods cannot even fit a 1.3B model. This democratizes private LLM fine-tuning for resource-constrained settings. Open questions include extending to mini-batch settings with population loss guarantees, incorporating momentum or variance reduction for improved rates, and validating at frontier model scales (100B+) where the low effective rank assumption may or may not hold. The paper also notes that the full-batch assumption in the theory limits direct applicability to very large datasets.
