## Problem

Existing two-phase kernel-based hypothesis transfer learning algorithms employ the same kernel regularization across both the source-model and offset-model phases, failing to adapt to the varying and unknown Sobolev smoothness of the target/source functions versus their (typically smoother) offset. This paper asks whether an HTL algorithm can provably and universally adapt to these unknown smoothness levels while achieving minimax optimality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241219/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired regression data: a large source dataset {(x_S,i, y_S,i)}_{i=1}^{n_S} and a smaller target dataset {(x_T,i, y_T,i)}_{i=1}^{n_T} with n_S ≫ n_T, where x ∈ X ⊂ R^d is a compact domain with Lipschitz boundary and y ∈ R. It produces a target function estimator f̂_T = f̂_S + f̂_δ, where f̂_S is the adaptive source model and f̂_δ is the adaptive offset model fitted on pseudo-labels y_T,i − f̂_S(x_T,i). The target/source functions are assumed in H^{m_0} and the offset in H^m with m ≥ m_0, both unknown to the algorithm.

## Method

SATL is a two-phase kernel ridge regression algorithm that uses fixed-bandwidth Gaussian kernels in both phases, with adaptive regularization selected via a training-and-validation procedure over a discrete candidate smoothness grid. The key theoretical insight is that a misspecified fixed-bandwidth Gaussian kernel in target-only KRR achieves minimax optimal rates for any Sobolev smoothness α_0 ≥ d/2, with the optimal regularization parameter decaying exponentially (log(1/λ) ∝ n^{2/(2α_0+d)}) rather than polynomially as in Matérn-kernel settings. This exponential decay avoids the saturation effect that plagues less-smooth kernels and enables universal adaptivity without knowing the true smoothness. The paper establishes a minimax lower bound in excess risk and shows SATL matches it up to logarithmic factors, with the rate decomposing into a source-estimation term n_S^{−2m_0/(2m_0+d)} and an offset-estimation term n_T^{−2m/(2m+d)}·ξ_U, where ξ_U quantifies the relative model strength between offset and source domains.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is that SATL achieves minimax optimality: its upper bound $\left(\frac{n_S}{\log n_S}\right)^{-2m_0/(2m_0+d)} + \left(\frac{n_T}{\log n_T}\right)^{-2m/(2m+d)}\xi_U$ matches the minimax lower bound up to logarithmic factors (Theorem 4.1). For target-only KRR with a fixed-bandwidth Gaussian kernel, the non-adaptive rate is $n^{-2\alpha_0/(2\alpha_0+d)}$ with the optimal regularization parameter following an exponential pattern $\log(1/\lambda)\asymp n^{2/(2\alpha_0+d)}$ (Theorem 3.1), and the adaptive training-and-validation procedure achieves $(n/\log n)^{-2\alpha_0/(2\alpha_0+d)}$ (Theorem 3.3). In experiments, the empirical regression coefficients of $\log\|\hat{f}-f\|_{L_2}$ on $\log n$ closely match the theoretical slopes $-2\alpha/(2\alpha+1)$ for $\alpha=2$ and $\alpha=3$, confirming the sharpness of the rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SATL against the finite basis expansion (FBE) transfer learning algorithm of Wang et al. (2016), using both Fourier and B-spline bases. In the fixed $n_T=50$ scenario (Figure 3a), SATL achieves overall smaller generalization error than FBE across all offset smoothness levels ($m=2,3,4$) and offset magnitudes $h$. In the varying $n_T$ scenario with $n_S=n_T^{3/2}$ (Figure 3b), SATL's empirical error is consistent with the theoretical upper bound $n_T^{-2m/(2m+1)}$ asymptotically in all settings, while FBE lags behind. SATL also outperforms the target-only KRR baseline in all transfer learning settings, confirming that the offset structure is being exploited. The key advantage of SATL over FBE is its adaptivity: SATL does not require knowing the true Sobolev smoothness $m_0$ or $m$ a priori, whereas FBE relies on a fixed basis that can be misaligned with the true function structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the misspecified-kernel KRR literature (Eberts & Steinwart 2013; Hamm & Steinwart 2021; Wang & Jing 2022) by showing that a fixed-bandwidth Gaussian kernel achieves minimax optimality in target-only KRR with an exponentially decaying regularization parameter, in contrast to the polynomial decay required for Matérn kernels. It departs from prior OTL methods (Wang & Schneider 2015; Du et al. 2017; Lin & Reimherr 2024) that impose the same RKHS regularization in both the source and offset phases, by demonstrating that the Gaussian kernel's universal smoothness coverage enables each phase to adapt independently to its own unknown Sobolev smoothness. The training-and-validation adaptive procedure builds on standard Lepski-style techniques but is applied in the novel context of exponential-rate regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first minimax-optimal adaptive HTL algorithm for nonparametric regression, resolving the open question of whether different smoothness levels in source and offset can be learned without prior knowledge. The identification of ξ_U as a relative-strength ratio (rather than just offset magnitude) offers a more nuanced understanding of when transfer actually helps, with practical implications for domain selection. Key limitations include the asymptotic nature of the bounds (requiring both n_T and n_S to be large), restriction to Sobolev-type spaces, and the logarithmic price paid for adaptivity; extending to few-shot regimes and more general RKHS structures remains open.
