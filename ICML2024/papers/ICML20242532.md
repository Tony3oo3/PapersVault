## Problem

Steerable CNNs require G-steerable convolution kernels to achieve equivariance, but no analytical or numerical solutions exist for the non-compact pseudo-orthogonal groups O(p,q) of mixed signature (e.g., the Lorentz group O(1,3)). This means no prior convolutional architecture could respect the full spacetime symmetries E(p,q) of pseudo-Euclidean spaces such as Minkowski spacetime, limiting PDE surrogates to spatial-only equivariance.

## Input / Output

The network consumes multi-channel multivector fields sampled on a (p+q)-dimensional grid, where each grid point carries a full Clifford algebra element (scalar, vector, bivector, etc. components). For the relativistic Maxwell task on R^{1,2}, time is a proper grid dimension (128×128×128) rather than a feature channel, and the electromagnetic field is encoded as a single bivector. The model predicts future states: one step ahead for Navier-Stokes (128×128) and Maxwell 3D (32×32×32), or 16 steps ahead for Maxwell 2D+1, given 4 or 16 previous time steps respectively.

## Method

CS-CNNs implement O(p,q)-steerable kernels implicitly as a composition K = H ∘ K̂, where K̂ is a Clifford group equivariant neural network (CGENN) mapping R^{p,q} → Cl(R^{p,q})^{c_out×c_in} and H is a kernel head performing a weighted geometric product to convert multivector outputs into the required Hom_Vec linear maps. The CGENN uses grade-preserving linear mixing, weighted geometric product layers, and gated activations, all of which are O(p,q)-equivariant by construction. A scalar orbital shell (exponentially decaying in the η^{p,q}-induced squared distance) provides radial or hyperbolic distance information to the kernel network. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3.4) proves that every such composition is automatically O(p,q)-steerable, making the resulting convolution E(p,q)-equivariant without requiring any analytical kernel basis or group sampling.

## Results

CS-ResNets outperform all baselines on all three forecasting tasks (Navier-Stokes 2D, Maxwell 3D, and relativistic Maxwell 2D+1), with the largest gains on the Maxwell tasks. On Navier-Stokes, CS-ResNets require only 64 training simulations to match or exceed the performance of a basic ResNet and FNOs trained on 5,120 simulations (80× more data), achieving a test MSE of 2.53×10⁻³. On the relativistic Maxwell task (R^{1,2}), CS-CNNs converge significantly faster than basic ResNets and are more sample-efficient. For E(2)-equivariance error, both the Steerable ResNet and CS-ResNet achieve equivariance up to numerical artifacts, while all non-steerable baselines (including G-FNO) fail to become equivariant even after training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Conventional ResNet (Wang et al., ICLR 2021), CS-ResNets achieve substantially lower MSE on all three tasks, requiring 80× fewer training samples on Navier-Stokes to match its performance. The Clifford ResNet (Brandstetter et al., ICLR 2023) uses the same kernel head but a non-equivariant kernel network, making it only translation-equivariant; CS-CNNs outperform it by enforcing full O(p,q)-steerability. Fourier Neural Operators (Li et al., ICLR 2021) are outperformed on all tasks, with CS-ResNets matching their accuracy at 1/80th the training data on Navier-Stokes. G-FNOs (Helwig et al., ICML 2023) fail entirely on multivector-valued data since they assume scalar or regular representations, and their discrete D₄ equivariance does not generalize to continuous O(2) or O(p,q) symmetries. The Steerable ResNet (Weiler & Cesa, NeurIPS 2019; Cesa et al., ICLR 2022) matches CS-CNNs on E(2)-equivariance error for the non-relativistic Navier-Stokes task, but cannot be extended to mixed-signature O(p,q) groups (e.g., O(1,2) for relativistic spacetime), which is the key advantage of CS-CNNs. Overall, CS-CNNs are the first models to respect the full Poincaré group E(1,2) on the relativistic Maxwell task, a symmetry no baseline can capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three prior threads into a unified architecture: the implicit kernel parametrization of Zhdanov et al. (2023), the O(p,q)-equivariant Clifford group equivariant neural networks (CGENNs) of Ruhe et al. (2023a), and the general steerable CNN theory of Weiler et al. (2023). The key gap it fills is that analytical or numerical solutions for O(p,q)-steerable kernels were unavailable because O(p,q) is non-compact for mixed signatures, ruling out the standard compact-group approaches. The paper departs from Brandstetter et al. (2023), which used a similar kernel head but a non-equivariant kernel network, making their architecture only translation-equivariant rather than fully E(p,q)-equivariant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CS-CNNs are the first convolutional networks respecting the full isometry group E(p,q) of pseudo-Euclidean spaces, enabling proper modeling of relativistic physics where time is a genuine spacetime dimension rather than a feature channel. The 80× sample-efficiency gain on Navier-Stokes (64 vs. 5120 training simulations) is consistent across tasks and dataset sizes, suggesting equivariance to spacetime symmetries is a powerful inductive bias for PDE surrogates. Open limitations include the non-compactness of O(p,q) orbits (requiring kernel truncation on finite grids), the incompleteness of single-convolution kernel spaces (recovered only by composition), and the assumption that multivector grades are irreducible representations, which may not always hold.
