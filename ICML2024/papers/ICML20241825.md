## Problem

Existing policy optimization algorithms for linear MDPs fail to achieve the optimal Õ(√K) regret bound in terms of the number of episodes K. Prior optimistic NPG methods (Liu et al., 2023b; Zhong & Zhang, 2023) suffer from suboptimal rates because Q-function truncation inflates the policy class capacity, breaking the uniform convergence arguments needed for tight concentration. This paper is the first to establish the optimal rate via a policy optimization approach in the stochastic bandit-feedback setting, and the first (by any method) in the adversarial full-feedback setting.

## Input / Output

The agent receives state observations s_h at each step of a finite-horizon (H-step) episodic MDP, a known feature mapping φ(s,a) ∈ R^d with ‖φ‖ ≤ 1, and either full loss vectors g_{k,h} ∈ R^d (adversarial) or scalar bandit losses ℓ_h^k(s_h^k, a_h^k) ∈ [-1,1] (stochastic). The output is a sequence of stochastic policies π^k over a finite action set A, with the guarantee that cumulative pseudo-regret against the best fixed policy in hindsight is O(d² H^{7/2} log⁴(dHK/δ) √(K log A)) with high probability. The linear structure assumption requires transitions P_h(s'|s,a) = φ(s,a)^T ψ_h(s') and losses ℓ_h(s,a) = φ(s,a)^T g_h.

## Method

The algorithm is an optimistic natural policy gradient (mirror descent) update π_h^{k+1}(a|s) ∝ π_h^k(a|s) exp(-η Q̃_h^k(s,a)), combined with two key innovations. First, a reward-free warmup phase (Algorithm 2, invoking CovTraj) collects data to ensure that for 'most' state-action directions (reachable with probability ≳ 1/√K), the least-squares regression error is small enough to forgo Q-function truncation. Second, restricted value functions (set to zero outside the known state set Z_h) replace truncated ones, so that the cumulative Q-function sum in the policy parameterization collapses into a single d-dimensional vector plus O(d log K) norm terms, keeping the policy class covering number at O(d³ log(βCK/ν)). A determinant-based epoch schedule limits bonus matrix updates to O(log K) times, and an inductive argument over (k,h) establishes that all estimated value functions remain in the low-capacity class, yielding the Õ(√K) regret via standard bias/OMD/optimism decomposition.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 1) establishes a regret bound of O(d² H^{7/2} log⁴(dHK/δ) √(K log A)) = Õ(√K) for both the adversarial setting with full-information feedback and the stochastic setting with bandit feedback. This is the first rate-optimal (in K) guarantee for adversarial linear MDPs with full feedback, and the first to achieve the optimal √K rate via a policy optimization approach in the stochastic bandit-feedback setting. The algorithm is computationally efficient, with per-episode cost dominated by O(d³) least-squares solves and O(d² log K) policy parameterization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments, so comparisons are between regret bounds. The paper's Õ(√K) bound for adversarial linear MDPs with full feedback improves upon the prior best of Õ(K^{3/4}) by Zhong & Zhang (2023), which used a similar optimistic policy optimization framework but required an additional batching mechanism. For the stochastic bandit-feedback setting, the Õ(√K) rate matches the information-theoretic lower bound and improves upon Liu et al. (2023b)'s Õ(1/ε³) sample complexity. In the harder adversarial bandit-feedback setting (not directly addressed by this paper), the current state-of-the-art is Õ(K^{3/4}) with an efficient algorithm (Liu et al., 2023a) and Õ(√K) only with a computationally inefficient one; this paper's techniques (reward-free warmup + restricted value functions) are the key ingredients that enable the rate improvement, and the authors note their approach is the first to achieve the optimal rate via policy optimization in the stochastic setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the optimistic natural policy gradient (NPG) framework of Kakade (2001) and its recent linear MDP instantiations (Liu et al., 2023b; Zhong & Zhang, 2023), which achieved suboptimal Õ(K^{3/4}) or Õ(1/ε³) rates. The key departure is the use of a reward-free warmup phase (via CovTraj from Wagenmaker et al., 2022b) to eliminate Q-function truncation, which in turn reduces the policy class capacity and enables the uniform convergence argument needed for the optimal √K rate. The linear MDP structure and least-squares concentration technique follow Jin et al. (2020), but the restricted (rather than truncated) value functions are the novel analytical device that makes the inductive bounding of estimation errors tractable without inflating the covering number.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm to achieve the minimax-optimal Õ(√K) regret in the adversarial linear MDP setting with full feedback, a problem for which no optimal-rate guarantee existed, and the first PO-based method to reach the optimal rate in the stochastic bandit-feedback setting. The conceptual insight—that a reward-free warmup can replace truncation and thereby shrink the policy class to a manageable capacity—is a reusable technique that may apply to other function-approximation RL problems. Open questions include whether the approach extends to the adversarial + bandit-feedback setting (where Liu et al., 2023a achieves √K only with a computationally inefficient algorithm), and whether the linear MDP assumption can be relaxed to more general function classes while preserving the optimal rate.
