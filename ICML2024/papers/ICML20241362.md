## Problem

Existing neuro-symbolic RL methods for visual tasks cannot refine their structured state representations with reward signals because the vision models they rely on (e.g., SPACE) are too computationally expensive to update during policy learning, causing significant performance degradation. Additionally, while symbolic policies are intrinsically interpretable, they require domain expertise (first-order logic, programming grammars) to understand, creating an accessibility barrier for non-expert users.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241362/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 4 stacked grayscale frames at 84×84 pixels (4 channels) as visual input, plus a pre-generated frame-symbol dataset of ~10,000 frames with object coordinates, existence labels, and bounding box dimensions distilled from FastSAM segmentation and DeAot tracking. Outputs include discrete game actions, normalized object coordinates in [0,1], concise symbolic policy expressions (EQL equations over coordinates), and GPT-4-generated natural-language explanations for both the overall policy and individual decisions (the latter conditioned on coordinate values and log-likelihood gradients).

## Method

INSIGHT comprises three jointly trained components: (1) a lightweight CNN perception module that predicts object existence (via distribution-balanced focal loss), coordinates (L1 loss), and shape, pre-trained on a frame-symbol dataset distilled from vision foundation models and then refined end-to-end with reward signals; (2) a policy learning module that pairs an EQL actor (with sparsity regularization for concise symbolic expressions) with a neural actor trained via PPO, where the EQL actor distills the neural actor through cross-entropy on action distributions to overcome the limited expressiveness of raw coordinates; (3) a GPT-4 explanation pipeline that performs concept grounding (mapping coordinate variables to task semantics) followed by chain-of-thought analysis of policy equations and gradient-based decision explanations. The key architectural insight is that distilling expensive vision foundation models into a small, trainable CNN enables reward-driven refinement of structured states without the computational overhead that froze prior methods' representations.

## Results

On nine Atari tasks, INSIGHT matches or exceeds the performance of a neural agent with the same architecture (e.g., 20.9±0.1 on Pong vs. 20.4±0.6 for Neural; 3828.1±261.1 on BeamRider vs. 3868.1±204.2) while outperforming all neuro-symbolic RL baselines by wide margins. On MetaDrive, INSIGHT achieves 63% success rate at 5M steps versus 49% for the neural baseline. Inference time is 2 ms per state, matching the neural baseline and being 25× faster than NUDGE (60 ms) and 25× faster than SPACE-Neural (50 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

INSIGHT outperforms all four NS-RL baselines on every task where a comparison is available. Against CGP (Wilson et al., 2018), the gap is dramatic: INSIGHT scores 3828 vs. 1342 on BeamRider, 844 vs. 2 on Enduro, and 410 vs. 13 on Breakout. Against DSP (Landajuela et al., 2021), INSIGHT achieves 20.9 vs. −1 on Pong and 2666 vs. 193 on Seaquest. Against NUDGE (Delfosse et al., 2024a), which relies on ground-truth object locations, INSIGHT still leads: 20.9 vs. −7.2 on Pong and 1233 vs. 80 on SpaceInvaders. Against DiffSES (Zheng et al., 2022), available only for Pong and SpaceInvaders, INSIGHT scores 20.9 vs. 20.2 and 1233 vs. 792 respectively. Against the CleanRL neural baseline (Huang et al., 2022), INSIGHT achieves higher returns on 6 of 9 tasks (e.g., 3828 vs. 2271 on BeamRider, 3043 vs. 2237 on MsPacman), confirming that the neuro-symbolic formulation does not sacrifice performance relative to standard deep RL.

## Relation to Prior Work

INSIGHT extends the neuro-symbolic RL (NS-RL) line of work (CGP, DiffSES, DSP, NUDGE) by addressing two key limitations: prior methods for visual tasks either froze structured state representations (DiffSES/SPACE) or required ground-truth object locations (NUDGE), preventing reward-driven refinement. The method builds on EQL for symbolic policy representation and neural guidance schemes (Nguyen et al., 2021; Landajuela et al., 2021), but departs by training the EQL actor and neural actor simultaneously rather than sequentially, and by distilling vision foundation models (FastSAM + DeAot) into a lightweight CNN perception module that is further refined end-to-end with reward signals. The LLM-based explanation pipeline draws on recent work using language models to interpret ML models but is the first to apply this to NS-RL policies and decisions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

INSIGHT closes the long-standing performance gap between interpretable NS-RL and opaque neural RL on visual tasks, matching neural baselines on all nine Atari games while retaining symbolic interpretability. The end-to-end refinement of structured states with rewards, and the finding that reward signals specifically improve coordinate prediction for policy-relevant objects (F-MAE), provide a mechanistic explanation for the performance gain. However, the EQL network cannot express logical operations needed for reasoning tasks, and the LLM-generated explanations, while accessible, contain factual errors and lack quantitative evaluation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more complex visual domains, whether the explanation quality can be systematically measured, and whether the distillation pipeline generalizes beyond Atari-style environments.
