## Problem

AI-assisted peptide drug design remains largely unexplored compared to small-molecule and protein generation, because peptides occupy a unique chemical space with protein-like topologies, larger molecular weights (500–5000 Da), and internal geometries that do not match small-molecule parameterizations. Additionally, no sufficiently large, high-quality protein–peptide complex dataset existed to train deep generative models for structure-based peptide design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241228/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a target protein's full-atom structure (residue types + 3D coordinates) as the conditional context and generates a peptide of a user-specified length (≤30 residues). The peptide is parameterized by backbone torsion angles (φ, ψ, ω) on a hypertorus T^(3N−3), a global centroid translation in R³, a global rotation in SO(3), and per-residue amino-acid type probabilities over 20 categories. For the optimization variant, an existing peptide is perturbed along the probability path at a chosen start time and then denoised; for docking, the peptide sequence is fixed and only the structural variables are generated. Side-chain rotamer angles (χ₁–χ₄) are predicted post-hoc by a fine-tuned Rotamer Density Estimator.

## Method

PPFLOW is a conditional flow-matching generative model that learns the joint distribution of peptide torsion angles, global pose, and amino-acid types given a target protein. The key architectural insight is parameterizing the backbone on the product manifold T^(3N−3) × SE(3) rather than full atom coordinates, which fixes inflexible bond lengths/angles as constants, reduces degrees of freedom from 6N to 3N+3, and guarantees chemical validity. The torus component uses Riemannian flow matching with a wrapped-normal prior and an independent-coupling path where the geodesic is approximated by per-coordinate linear interpolation with 2π-wrapping; the SE(3) component combines Gaussian CFM for translation and SO(3)-CFM (via Rodrigues' formula) for rotation; amino-acid types use a multinomial flow-matching objective with cross-entropy loss. The vector fields are approximated by a 6-layer Invariant Transformer Attention (ITA) encoder with Local Coordinate Systems (LOCS) for equivariant output heads, ensuring the generated distribution is SE(3)-equivariant. Training minimizes the sum of four flow-matching losses (torus, Euclidean, SO(3), multinomial) over the full binding complex at each time step, and sampling proceeds by integrating the learned ODE with Euler steps, reconstructing Cartesian coordinates via NERF at each step.

## Results

On the PPDBench test set (133 protein-peptide pairs), PPFLOW-FA achieves the best binding energy (ΔG = −351.27 kcal/mol) and the highest fraction of peptides with better binding than reference (IMP%-B = 35.43%), outperforming DIFFPP (−319.54, 28.75%) and DIFFBP-PP (−221.06, 19.34%). PPFLOW also achieves 100% chemical validity (vs. 94% for DIFFPP and 42% for DIFFBP-PP), higher novelty (0.84 vs. 0.79/0.76), and substantially greater diversity (0.76 vs. 0.28/0.41). In the side-chain packing task, the fine-tuned RDE-PP model reduces MAE on χ₁ from 38.58° (Rosetta) to 37.24° and NLL from 0.75 to 0.62.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPFLOW dominates both diffusion-based baselines on every generation metric. Against DIFFPP (a CDR-generation diffusion model adapted for peptides), PPFLOW-FA improves ΔG by ~32 kcal/mol, raises validity from 0.94 to 1.00, and more than doubles diversity (0.76 vs. 0.28). Against DIFFBP-PP (an atom-level diffusion model), the gap is even larger: PPFLOW achieves 100% validity versus 42%, and a ΔG advantage of over 130 kcal/mol, demonstrating that the high-degree-of-freedom atom-level parameterization is poorly suited to peptide design. In flexible re-docking (Table 2), PPFLOW is competitive with classical methods (HDOCK, Vina) on centroid-RMSD (2.60 Å at 10% vs. 7.29/6.27 Å) but trails DIFFPP on ligand-RMSD, while running 30× faster than Vina. For side-chain packing (Table 3), the fine-tuned RDE-PP outperforms Rosetta(fixbb) on three of four rotamer angles in both MAE and NLL, confirming that transfer from protein-protein to protein-peptide complexes is effective.

## Relation to Prior Work

PPFLOW extends the flow-matching generative framework (Lipman et al., 2022; Chen & Lipman, 2023) from Euclidean and SE(3) manifolds to hypertorus manifolds for torsion angles, building directly on Riemannian flow matching and the Independent Coupling formulation of Tong et al. (2023). The equivariant architecture draws from AlphaFold2's Invariant Transformer Attention (Jumper et al., 2021) and LOCS (Kofinas et al., 2022), while the side-chain packing component repurposes RDE (Luo et al., 2023) from protein-protein to protein-peptide complexes. The key conceptual departure from prior structure-based drug design methods (DiffBP, TargetDiff) is the shift from atom-level parameterization to torsion-angle parameterization on torus, motivated by the fact that peptides occupy a chemical space between small molecules and proteins where fixed bond lengths/angles make atom-level generation both over-parameterized and chemically invalid.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is positioned as the first deep-learning solution for target-aware peptide drug design, and the PPBench2024 dataset (15,593 pairs) addresses a genuine data bottleneck that has limited prior work in this area. The finding that torsion-angle parameterization yields 100% chemical validity versus 42% for atom-level diffusion is a practical insight: for peptide-scale molecules, the parameterization choice is more critical than the generative model for producing usable structures. However, the model's docking performance lags behind classical methods (HDOCK, Vina), and the side-chain packing improvement over Rosetta is modest, leaving the full-atom pipeline incomplete. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer peptides (>30 residues), whether the torus flow matching formulation generalizes to other cyclic molecular variables, and how to close the gap with energy-based docking for pose refinement.
