## Problem

Deep-learning approaches to reinforcement learning (e.g., NPE, MHDPA) fail to generalize to modified environments, produce uninterpretable models, and require millions of observations to learn even simple transition dynamics. Object-oriented methods that do generalize typically demand extensive domain-specific knowledge (e.g., hand-specified preconditions). QORA addresses the open problem of learning object-oriented transition models from scratch—without any domain knowledge—while simultaneously achieving generalization, interpretability, and sample efficiency (GIE).

## Input / Output

The method consumes variable-size sets of objects (each with a class, integer-valued attributes like 2D position or 1D color, and a unique ID) as states, along with a discrete action, and the resulting next state for online learning. Because object sets have no fixed order or cardinality, inputs cannot be represented as fixed-size tensors, precluding standard neural architectures. The output is a probability distribution over next-state attribute deltas, structured as piecewise first-order-logic rules (e.g., 'move right unless a wall or differently-colored door is adjacent'), which are directly human-readable.

## Method

QORA extracts two types of primitive relational predicates (absolute value checks and pairwise difference checks) from each observation, then iteratively combines them into increasingly informative conditions guided by a predictive-power score (S) and Occam's Razor. The key technical innovation is a bit-string encoding of first-order-logic formulas that simultaneously represents all possible quantifiers (∀, ∃) and connectives (∧, ∨, ¬) in a single evaluation, reducing the combinatorial search space exponentially. Learned conditions index sparse frequency tables that map (bit-string, outcome) pairs to probability estimates, allowing QORA to model both deterministic and stochastic transitions. A boosting step discards observations already explained by the current best hypothesis, implicitly conditioning all remaining candidates on it and accelerating convergence. A confidence-level hyperparameter α controls how conservatively new predicates are accepted, balancing learning speed against spurious correlations.

## Results

On the walls domain (8×8), QORA achieves zero EMD error with approximately 1,000 observations, while both neural-network baselines (NPE and MHDPA) saturate at ~0.5 EMD even after 2 million observations — a sample-efficiency gap of roughly 10,000×. QORA demonstrates zero-shot transfer from 8×8 to 128×128 environments (containing ~5,000 objects) with no additional training and zero post-transfer error. On the most challenging complex(4) domain (5 classes, 2 attributes, 12 actions), QORA reaches zero error within 500K steps. After convergence, QORA's per-step runtime is ~50× faster than NPE (with batching) and ~600× faster (without batching), yielding a combined potential learning speedup of ~6 million× over NPE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares QORA against two object-based neural-network architectures: the Neural Physics Engine (NPE, Chang et al., 2016) and a Multi-Head Dot Product Attention (MHDPA) model (Vaswani et al., 2017). On the walls domain, both baselines fail to reach perfect accuracy even after 2 million observations, saturating at approximately 0.5 EMD error, suggesting they learn wall behavior but not player-wall interaction. QORA, by contrast, achieves zero error with 3–4 orders of magnitude fewer observations (~1,000 vs. 2,000,000). On zero-shot transfer (8×8 → 16×16 for networks; 8×8 → 64×64 and 128×128 for QORA), the neural networks show a substantial immediate error spike, indicating reliance on level-specific features, while QORA transfers with zero error. In runtime comparisons (Figure 6), QORA is ~50× faster than NPE per step after convergence (with batching) and ~600× faster without batching, compounding the sample-efficiency advantage into an estimated 6-million-fold total learning speedup. The neural baselines were not evaluated on the more challenging doors, fish, lights, or complex domains due to their failure on the simpler walls domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

QORA departs from the dominant deep-learning paradigm for transition modeling (NPE, MHDPA) by replacing neural networks with first-order-logic conditions and frequency-table statistics, while also departing from prior object-oriented RL methods (DOORMAX, relational RL) that require extensive domain knowledge to specify preconditions. The closest prior work—MHDPA and NPE—satisfies the same problem constraints (object-oriented, no domain knowledge, transition modeling) but lacks interpretability and sample efficiency. The paper's core technical novelty is the bit-string encoding of quantified first-order-logic conditions, which implicitly enumerates all connectives and quantifiers in a single evaluation, combined with an Occam's-Razor-driven iterative condition-combining process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QORA demonstrates that interpretable, sample-efficient world models are achievable on structured domains, offering a concrete alternative to black-box neural approaches where trust and data efficiency matter (robotics, safety-critical systems). Key open questions include whether the approach scales to high-dimensional or continuous attribute spaces, whether the object-oriented representation assumption is a fundamental limitation for real-world perception, and the lack of a theoretical complexity bound for the search process. The benchmark suite is a useful contribution for evaluating generalization in object-oriented settings, though the domains remain relatively simple grid-worlds.
