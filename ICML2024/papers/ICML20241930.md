## Problem

Sampling-based sketches such as ℓ₀ samplers and ℓ₁ embeddings have been well-studied for vectors, but no efficient constructions existed for applying them to rank-one tensors. While a rank-one tensor in (R^d)^{⊗q} is specified by only O(qd) parameters, a naive sketch application would require expanding to d^q entries, negating the compact representation. The paper asks whether sampling-based sketches can be applied to rank-one tensors in time scaling with d rather than d^q.

## Input / Output

The method takes a rank-one tensor X = x₁ ⊗ … ⊗ x_q in (R^d)^{⊗q} (q = 2 or 3), specified by q vectors of length d, and produces either a sketch vector in R^m with m = O(log⁴n + log²(1/δ)·log n) for ℓ₁ embeddings, or a sampled support index i together with the value X_i for ℓ₀ sampling. All sketch matrix entries are restricted to {0, +1, −1}. The key constraint is application time: O(mn) for two-mode tensors and O(mn log²n) for three-mode tensors, versus the O(n²) or O(n³) cost of expanding the tensor first.

## Method

The core contribution is a 'p-sample' construction: a random subset of tensor indices where each entry is included with probability ≈ p in a nearly pairwise-independent manner, yet the sum over the sample can be computed in near-linear time. For 2-mode tensors, the sample set {(i,j) : i+j ∈ [T]} is summed via a sliding-window recurrence in O(n) time; for 3-mode tensors, denser samples use circular convolution (FFT) while sparser samples (probability between 1/n² and 1/n) are reduced to multiplications by triangular Toeplitz submatrices, each handled in O(T log²T) time via a recursive decomposition. These p-samples are then composed with random sign flips along each tensor mode (Kahtri-Rao structured measurements) to build ℓ₀ samplers across O(log n) sampling levels, and ℓ₁ embeddings following the Verbin–Zhang level-set approach, replacing CountSketch bucketing with T independent p-samples per level to preserve fast rank-one application.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical contributions are: (1) an ℓ₀ sampler with sketch dimension m = O(log(1/δ)·log²n·(log log n + log(1/δ))) that applies to rank-one tensors in O(mn) time (q=2) or O(mn log²n) time (q=3), and (2) an O(1)-distortion ℓ₁ embedding with sketch dimension m = O(log⁴n + log²(1/δ)·log n) with the same near-linear application times. Empirically, on 40×40×40 tensors over 1000 trials, the ℓ₀ sampler outputs entries within a few percent of the ideal uniform fraction across all tested support structures (disjoint rectangles and rectangle + random entries), with failure rates of 0–5 out of 1000 trials.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is primarily theoretical and does not run head-to-head experimental comparisons against prior sketching methods. The key implicit comparisons are: (1) A naive sketch applied to a rank-one tensor in (ℝⁿ)⊗q requires O(n²) or O(n³) time to expand and sketch, whereas the proposed method achieves O(mn) or O(mn log²n) time. (2) Kronecker-structured sketches (S₁⊗S₂) are trivially fast on rank-one tensors but, by a lower bound from Li et al. (2021), require poly(n) sketching dimension for O(1)-distortion ℓ₁ embeddings, while this paper achieves only polylog(n) space. (3) CountSketch-based constructions allow input-sparsity-time sketching for vectors but do not extend efficiently to rank-one tensors since the effective dimension becomes n² or n³. The empirical experiments validate correctness (near-uniform sampling) rather than comparing speed or quality against competing methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tensor sketching literature (Pham & Pagh 2013; Ahle et al. 2020), which has focused almost exclusively on ℓ₂/JL embeddings, to the fundamentally different setting of sampling-based sketches (ℓ₀ sampling and ℓ₁ embeddings). The overall sketch structure for ℓ₁ embeddings follows Verbin & Zhang (2012), and the ℓ₀ sampler follows the standard recipe surveyed by Cormode & Firmani (2014), but the key departure is the p-sample construction: instead of using CountSketch-style hashing (which is incompatible with fast rank-one tensor evaluation), the authors use fast convolution via FFT to sum over structured random subsets in near-linear time. They also explicitly rule out Kronecker-structured sketches (S₁⊗S₂) as insufficient, citing a poly(n) lower bound from Li et al. (2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens the door to fast sketching of rank-one tensors for ℓ₁ regression and ℓ₀ estimation, problems where prior fast tensor sketches were unavailable. The main open question is extending the p-sample construction to 4+ modes, where no known fast summation algorithm exists for codimension-2 subspaces. Additionally, the authors note that their sketches require O(n) time per entry (vs. O(1) for CountSketch), and composing their p-samples with CountSketch-style bucketing remains unresolved. The technique is broadly applicable to any sketch built from random subsampling plus random linear combinations, suggesting a wider family of problems could benefit.
