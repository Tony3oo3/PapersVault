## Problem

Training deep SNNs via BPTT requires storing intermediate states at every time-step, causing memory that grows linearly with simulation length and precluding online (forward-in-time) learning. Existing online methods like OTTT sidestep this by zeroing out the temporal derivative of the Heaviside spike function, which sacrifices gradient accuracy. The paper asks whether temporal dependencies can be captured accurately without unfolding the computational graph backward in time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240876/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For static datasets (CIFAR-10/100, 32×32 RGB), pixel values are fed directly as real-valued input currents at each discrete time-step; for the neuromorphic DVS-CIFAR10 dataset (128×128 ON/OFF event streams), spike events are aggregated into 2 time-steps and spatially downsampled to 48×48 before normalization. The network outputs a class label (10 or 100 classes) computed from an instantaneous loss at each time-step rather than the conventional average firing rate over all T steps, which is what enables the online update. The architecture is a VGG-11 with scaled weight standardization replacing batch normalization, since BN requires statistics across all time-steps and is incompatible with forward-in-time gradients.

## Method

NDOT replaces the discrete temporal sensitivity ε[t] used in BPTT with a continuous temporal dependency e[t] = (u[t] − V_th·s[t]) / (u[t−1] − V_th·s[t−1]) derived directly from the LIF membrane dynamics, which captures the combined effect of charging, leakage, and reset without requiring surrogate gradients on the spike function. The full weight gradient is decomposed into a spatial component g_u^l[t] (standard backpropagation across layers at a single time-step, computed via PyTorch autograd) and a temporal component â^{l−1}[t] (presynaptic activities tracked forward-in-time via the recursive update â[t] = e[t−1] ⊙ â[t−1] + s[t]). This decoupling means each time-step's gradient is computed independently in a single forward pass, yielding constant training memory regardless of T. The training loss is an instantaneous combination of cross-entropy and MSE (weighted by α), and parameters are updated either immediately at each step (NDOT_O) or after accumulating over T steps (NDOT_A). The paper further shows via the FTRL framework that NDOT's use of neuronal dynamics implicitly encodes historical information analogous to FTRL's explicit regularization term, explaining why it outperforms OTTT which discards temporal dependencies entirely.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, NDOT_A achieves 94.90% top-1 accuracy at T=6 time-steps, outperforming the best BPTT baseline SLTT (94.59% at T=6) and the forward-in-time baseline OTTT (93.73% at T=6). On CIFAR-100, NDOT_O reaches 76.61% at T=6, surpassing OTTT (71.11%) by 5.5 percentage points and TET (74.62% at T=4) by 2.0 points. On DVS-CIFAR10, NDOT_O achieves 77.50% at T=10, edging out SLTT (77.30%) and OTTT (77.10%). Notably, even at T=1, NDOT_O attains 94.28% on CIFAR-10, exceeding LTL (93.20%) which requires T=16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NDOT consistently outperforms the closest forward-in-time baseline OTTT across all three datasets: by 1.16% on CIFAR-10 (94.89% vs. 93.73% at T=6), by 5.50% on CIFAR-100 (76.61% vs. 71.11% at T=6), and by 0.40% on DVS-CIFAR10 (77.50% vs. 77.10% at T=10). Against the strongest BPTT baseline SLTT, NDOT_A surpasses it on CIFAR-10 (94.90% vs. 94.59% at T=6) and NDOT_O edges it out on DVS-CIFAR10 (77.50% vs. 77.30% at T=10). On CIFAR-100, NDOT_O (76.61% at T=6) beats all BPTT baselines including TET (74.62% at T=4) and TEBN (74.37% at T=4) by over 2 percentage points. The FTRL-OTTT ablation (75.89% at T=6 on CIFAR-100) confirms that explicitly incorporating historical information helps, and NDOT achieves comparable or better results (76.61%) by implicitly capturing temporal dependencies through neuronal dynamics. Even at the extreme low-latency setting of T=1, NDOT_O achieves 94.28% on CIFAR-10, exceeding LTL (93.20%) which requires 16 time-steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

NDOT is derived from the BPTT-with-surrogate-gradient framework for SNN training (Wu et al., 2018), but replaces the discrete temporal dependency ε[t] with a continuous neuronal-dynamics-based representation e[t] that captures the same information without requiring backpropagation through time. It also builds directly on OTTT (Xiao et al., 2022), which established the forward-in-time learning paradigm for SNNs but approximated temporal dependencies by setting them to zero; NDOT retains the forward-in-time structure while accurately tracking temporal gradients via the recursive update â[t] = e[t-1] ⊙ â[t-1] + s[t]. The FTRL algorithm is introduced as a conceptual lens to explain why capturing temporal dependencies matters, drawing a parallel between FTRL's explicit use of historical information and NDOT's implicit capture through neuronal dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NDOT provides a principled way to train deep SNNs with constant memory regardless of time-steps, making it compatible with online learning on neuromorphic hardware where storing full computational graphs is infeasible. The consistent outperformance over OTTT (up to ~5% on CIFAR-100) and competitive accuracy with BPTT methods at very low latency (T=1–6) suggests that accurate temporal gradient capture is more important than previously assumed. Limitations include evaluation only on CIFAR-scale datasets (no ImageNet), reliance on weight standardization instead of batch normalization (which may constrain architectural choices), and the use of instantaneous loss that is an upper bound of the offline loss. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger architectures and datasets, whether it generalizes to other neuron models beyond LIF, and whether the FRL connection can be made more formal to provide convergence guarantees.
