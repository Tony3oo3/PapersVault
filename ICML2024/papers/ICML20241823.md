## Problem

Existing bilevel optimization (BLO) algorithms rely on strong convexity or Polyak-Lojasiewicz conditions on the lower-level objective, which are violated by the non-convex discounted-return objective in reinforcement learning. This prevents direct application of implicit-gradient or penalty-based BLO methods to bilevel RL problems such as Stackelberg Markov games, reward learning, and RLHF, where the lower level is a policy optimization problem rather than a static convex program.

## Input / Output

The method takes a parameterized MDP M_τ(x) with parameterized reward r_x and transition kernel P_x, along with leader and follower policy parameters (x, y); in the RLHF application it additionally consumes human preference labels over pairs of state-action segments. The output is a jointly optimized pair (x*, y*) where the follower policy y* is the best response to the leader's choice x*, and in RLHF the leader variable x encodes the reward function parameters. The penalty reformulation converts the constrained bilevel problem into an unconstrained single-level minimization over x ∈ X and y ∈ Y, so the algorithm only requires first-order gradient estimates rather than second-order derivatives or exact lower-level solutions.

## Method

The paper introduces two tailored penalty functions for bilevel RL: a value penalty measuring the lower-level optimality gap (max V − V^π) and a Bellman penalty based on the Bellman-equation residual g(x,y) − v(x), which is τ-strongly convex in y. A key theoretical contribution is proving a gradient-dominance property for the regularized MDP (generalizing Agarwal et al. 2020) that ensures approximate solutions of the penalty reformulation are also approximate solutions of the original bilevel problem. The authors then establish differentiability of both penalties via a generalized Danskin's theorem and derive closed-form gradient expressions for each application (RLHF, Stackelberg game). The resulting PBRL algorithm is a first-order projected gradient method on F_λ(x,y) = f(x,y) + λp(x,y), where at each outer iteration an inner-loop policy mirror descent computes an approximate optimal follower policy π̂_k to estimate the penalty gradient, yielding a convergence rate of Õ(λ ε^{-1}) — the first provably convergent first-order algorithm for bilevel RL.

## Results

On 10 randomly generated Stackelberg Markov games (|S|=100, |A_l|=|A_f|=5), PBRL with both value and Bellman penalty achieves higher leader's return than independent policy gradient, with the value penalty variant reaching the highest value; the follower's optimality gap converges to zero for all methods. On Atari RLHF (Seaquest, BeamRider, MsPacman), PBRL achieves episode returns comparable to or exceeding DRLHF, approaching the A2C oracle (true-reward) upper bound on BeamRider and MsPacman. The paper presents results as learning curves (Figures 1–2) rather than tabulated final numbers, so no single scalar metric is reported.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against independent policy gradient (Daskalakis et al., 2020; Ding et al., 2022) in Stackelberg Markov games, PBRL with both value and Bellman penalty clearly outperforms the myopic baseline on leader's cumulative return, with the value penalty variant achieving the highest final value. Against DRLHF (Christiano et al., 2017) on Atari RLHF, PBRL wins on BeamRider and MsPacman episode return, achieving a higher best-episode return and more stable training; on Seaquest the two methods reach similar asymptotic performance, though DRLHF exhibits greater training instability. Against the A2C oracle (Mnih et al., 2016, trained with ground-truth reward), PBRL does not exceed the oracle but becomes comparable to it on BeamRider and MsPacman, indicating that the bilevel penalty formulation recovers most of the information available from the true reward signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends penalty-based bilevel optimization (Shen & Chen, 2023; Ye et al., 2022; Kwon et al., 2023) from the supervised/static setting to the dynamic RL setting, where the lower-level objective is the non-convex discounted return of an MDP. Existing BLO methods—both implicit gradient and penalty-based—rely on strong convexity or Polyak-Lojasiewicz conditions in the lower level that are violated by policy optimization; the paper introduces value and Bellman penalties specifically designed to capture RL optimality conditions without these assumptions. It also draws on the gradient dominance property from Agarwal et al. (2020) and policy mirror descent (Lan, 2023; Zhan et al., 2023) to establish convergence, and positions RLHF (Christiano et al., 2017) and DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) as special cases of the bilevel RL formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provably convergent first-order algorithm for bilevel RL, providing a theoretical foundation that was previously absent for applications like RLHF, Stackelberg Markov games, and reward shaping. The penalty reformulation avoids second-order derivatives (unlike PARL, Chakraborty et al., 2024), making it more practical for large-scale RL. Open questions include extending to multi-agent lower-level problems (e.g., incentive design), scaling to continuous action spaces and function approximation, and understanding whether the penalty-based approach can be combined with off-policy methods for greater sample efficiency. The empirical gains over DRLHF on Atari are modest, suggesting the theoretical framework's practical impact may require further algorithmic refinement.
