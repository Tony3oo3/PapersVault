## Problem

Designing protein binding pockets for small molecules without prior knowledge of the ligand's 3D structure has no general deep learning solution. Existing approaches either require the bound ligand structure as input (e.g., LigandMPNN, PocketOptimizer), rely on expert knowledge with manual steps, or are limited to single ligands. This paper addresses both the docking sub-problem (generating the ligand's 3D binding pose) and the full binding site design task (jointly predicting residue types and ligand structure), including the multi-ligand case critical for enzyme design.

## Input / Output

The method takes a protein pocket specified only by backbone atom coordinates (N, Ca, C, O per residue) and the 2D chemical graph of a (multi-)ligand, with no 3D ligand structure or side-chain information provided. It jointly outputs discrete amino acid identities for the pocket residues and continuous 3D Cartesian coordinates for all ligand heavy atoms in their binding pose. The joint discrete-continuous generation is performed via a single flow model that iteratively refines both variable types from t=0 to t=1, starting from a harmonic prior for positions and mask tokens for residue types.

## Method

FLOWSITE builds on HARMONICFLOW, a self-conditioned flow matching process that generates ligand 3D structures by learning a vector field that transports a harmonic prior (constructed from the ligand's covalent bond graph Laplacian) to the data distribution, using x₁-prediction parameterization and conditioning each step on the previous step's prediction. The architecture uses SE(3)-equivariant refinement TFN layers that update ligand coordinates at every layer (unlike standard TFNs that only produce a final update), followed by invariant graph attention layers that predict residue types from the predicted structure. FLOWSITE extends HARMONICFLOW by adding a discrete self-conditioned flow over residue types with a cross-entropy loss, enabling joint discrete-continuous generation without defining a discrete interpolation. A fake-ligand data augmentation strategy treats protein side chains as ligands to create additional training cases, and the complete loss combines the conditional flow matching loss, a refinement loss on intermediate TFN position predictions, the residue type cross-entropy, and an auxiliary torsion angle loss.

## Results

On the primary binding site design task, FLOWSITE recovers 47.0% of contact residues on Binding MOAD and 49.5% on PDBBind (sequence similarity split), substantially outperforming the best baseline (PiFold with 2D ligand at 40.4%/44.5%) and nearly closing the gap to the oracle method with ground-truth ligand positions (51.4%/51.2%). For the docking sub-task, HARMONICFLOW achieves 30.1% of predictions with RMSD < 2 Å on PDBBind's sequence-similarity split (Distance-Pocket), outperforming DiffDock's product-space diffusion (27.2%), and extends to multi-ligand docking on Binding MOAD with 44.4% < 2 Å, surpassing EigenFold Diffusion (39.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLOWSITE outperforms all baselines on both datasets. Against PiFold (no ligand), the best no-ligand inverse-folding method, FLOWSITE gains +7.6 points in recovery on Binding MOAD (47.0% vs. 39.4%) and +6.0 on PDBBind (49.5% vs. 43.5%). Adding a 2D ligand encoding to PiFold (PiFold 2D ligand) improves it slightly to 40.4%/44.5%, but FLOWSITE still leads by +6.6/+5.0 points. The DiffDock-Pocket Pos baseline, which uses a separate docking tool to fix ligand positions before sequence prediction, reaches 45.0% on PDBBind—still 4.5 points below FLOWSITE's joint generation. On the docking sub-task (Table 1), HARMONICFLOW beats DiffDock's product-space diffusion by 2.9 points in %<2 on the sequence-similarity split and by 22.0 points on the time split (42.8% vs. 20.8%). For multi-ligand docking (Table 2), HARMONICFLOW surpasses EigenFold Diffusion by 4.7 points in %<2 (44.4% vs. 39.7%) and achieves a lower median RMSD (2.2 vs. 2.4 Å). The oracle Ground Truth Pos method, which has access to the true 3D ligand structure, remains the upper bound at 51.4%/51.2%, meaning FLOWSITE recovers about 91% of the oracle's performance without any structural input.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the flow matching generative framework (Lipman et al., 2022; Tong et al., 2023b) and departs from the product-space diffusion approach of DiffDock (Corso et al., 2023) by operating directly on Cartesian atom coordinates with a harmonic prior rather than parameterizing ligand poses via center-of-mass, orientation, and torsion angles. It extends the self-conditioning strategy from AlphaFold2's recycling and discrete diffusion (Chen et al., 2023) into a flow matching context, and combines this with equivariant refinement TFN layers (inspired by EGNN-style coordinate updates) for structure prediction. The joint discrete-continuous generation framework draws on inverse folding methods (PiFold, ProteinMPNN) and antibody design work, but uniquely unifies docking and sequence design in a single self-conditioned flow without requiring the bound ligand structure as input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first deep learning solution for designing binding pockets for small molecules without prior knowledge of the ligand's 3D structure, and the first ML method for multi-ligand docking—both practically important for enzyme design, drug discovery, and antidote development. The joint discrete-continuous flow matching framework is general and could apply to other mixed-type generation tasks. Key limitations include the absence of experimental validation (no wet-lab binding assays), the modest gap to the oracle (47.0% vs 51.4% recovery), and the reliance on pocket-level rather than blind docking for the main results. Open questions include whether the generated pockets achieve meaningful binding affinity in practice, whether the approach scales to larger multi-ligand systems, and whether the flow matching formulation generalizes to other joint discrete-continuous biomolecular design problems.
