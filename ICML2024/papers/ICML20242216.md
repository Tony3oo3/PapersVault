## Problem

Existing visual scoring methods (IQA, IAA, VQA) suffer from poor out-of-distribution generalization and cannot be unified across tasks, while LMMs—despite strong visual understanding—perform poorly when directly trained to output numerical scores. The paper identifies that human raters are trained on and respond with discrete text-defined levels (e.g., 'good', 'poor') rather than precise scores, and that LMMs share this innate preference for qualitative adjectives, yet no prior work has systematically exploited this alignment for visual scoring.

## Input / Output

For images, input is a single RGB frame padded to square and resized to 448×448; for videos, frames are sampled at 1 fps and fed as a sequence (up to 61 frames under the 4096-token context, enabled by a visual abstractor that compresses each image from 1024 to 64 tokens). The output is a continuous score in [1, 5] computed as a weighted average of close-set softmax probabilities over five discrete rating-level tokens (bad/poor/fair/good/excellent), rather than a directly regressed number.

## Method

Q-ALIGN trains an LMM (mPLUG-Owl2: CLIP-ViT-Large encoder + visual abstractor + LLaMA2-7B) via instruction tuning where MOS scores are converted to five equidistant text-defined levels and formatted as instruction-response pairs, emulating how human raters are calibrated. At inference, the model's logits on the five level tokens are passed through a close-set softmax to yield per-level probabilities, which are then combined via weighted average (Eq. 4) to produce a continuous score—this is mathematically equivalent to the MOS aggregation from human ratings and generalizes Q-Bench's binary softmax as a two-level special case. The approach requires no architectural modification, uses standard cross-entropy loss, and converges in ~2 epochs. By simply concatenating IQA, IAA, and VQA training data, the unified ONEALIGN model achieves state-of-the-art on all three tasks simultaneously with improved OOD generalization, and the probability-based inference (vs. argmax level) adds ~6% accuracy by capturing finer-grained distinctions within each level.

## Results

On IQA (KonIQ test, trained on KonIQ), Q-ALIGN achieves SRCC 0.940 / PLCC 0.941, surpassing the best visual-only method MUSIQ (0.929/0.924) and CLIP-based LIQE (0.928/0.912). On IAA (AVA test), Q-ALIGN reaches SRCC 0.822 / PLCC 0.817, outperforming LIQE (0.776/0.763) and VILA (0.774/0.774). On VQA (LSVQ test), Q-ALIGN attains SRCC 0.883 / PLCC 0.882, exceeding FAST-VQA (0.876/0.877). Cross-dataset (OOD) improvements are especially pronounced: Q-ALIGN gains over 10% SRCC on unseen IQA datasets and 6% on VQA (MaxWell) compared to prior SOTA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On IQA, Q-ALIGN outperforms the strongest visual-only baseline MUSIQ by +1.1% SRCC on intra-dataset (KonIQ) and by a substantial margin on cross-dataset settings (e.g., +12.9% SRCC on KADID-10k, +10.5% on AGIQA-3K). Among CLIP-based methods, Q-ALIGN surpasses LIQE by +1.2% SRCC intra-dataset and by +4% to +8% on cross-dataset evaluations, while also exceeding CLIP-IQA+ by similar margins. On IAA, Q-ALIGN (SRCC 0.822) beats LIQE (0.776) by 7%, the LAION Aesthetic Predictor (0.721) by 10%, and VILA (0.774, which uses extra AVA-Captions pre-training) by 6%. On VQA, Q-ALIGN with only 1fps sparse frames outperforms FAST-VQA (0.876) by +0.7% SRCC on LSVQ test and by +6% on the cross-dataset MaxWell test (0.780 vs. 0.720). Notably, FEWSHOT-Q-ALIGN using only 20% of training data (IQA) or 10% (IAA) already matches or exceeds full-data SOTA baselines, demonstrating strong data efficiency. The ablation in Table 11 confirms that training with discrete levels (vs. direct scores) yields an average +10% SRCC improvement on cross-dataset evaluations, with up to +52.8% on the most challenging OOD transfer (SPAQ→KADID).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on mPLUG-Owl2 as its base LMM architecture and extends the Q-Bench line of work, which first demonstrated that LMMs can predict quality scores via a binary softmax over two tokens (good/poor). The key departure is replacing both direct score regression (used by NIMA, MUSIQ, LIQE, etc.) and the simplified binary strategy with a five-level discrete text-defined rating system (excellent/good/fair/poor/bad) that emulates how human annotators are actually trained and how MOS values are collected per ITU-R BT.500. Conceptually, the paper reframes visual scoring from a regression problem to a classification problem over ordinal text tokens, leveraging the LMM's innate tendency to respond with qualitative adjectives rather than numbers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that coarser discrete-level supervision outperforms finer continuous-score supervision by ~10% on OOD evaluations (and up to 50%+ in cross-domain settings) challenges the standard assumption that more precise targets yield better generalization, and suggests a broader principle for aligning LMMs on any ordinal scoring task. The ONEALIGN unification of IQA, IAA, and VQA under a single model with free dataset mixing (without the performance degradation typical of prior multi-dataset training) opens a practical path toward a single deployable visual scorer. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other LMM backbones, whether more than five levels or different level definitions help, and whether the observed cross-task transfer (e.g., IQA improving VQA) reflects genuine shared visual understanding or dataset overlap.
