## Problem

Contextual bandits with feedback graphs generalize classic bandits by allowing the learner to observe losses of multiple actions after each decision, but prior efficient algorithms (Zhang et al., 2023) require the feedback graph to be revealed before the learner acts. In many practical settings such as online pricing and first-price auctions, the graph is uninformed—only revealed after the decision or never fully revealed—leaving no prior contextual algorithm for this more challenging regime.

## Input / Output

At each round the algorithm receives a context vector x_t, a loss estimator f_t from a squared-loss online regression oracle, and a graph estimator g_t from a log-loss online regression oracle, and outputs a probability distribution p_t over K actions. The graph estimator is trained on observations that are only partially available (connections from the chosen action) or fully available (all K×K edge indicators) after the action is sampled, making the graph learning problem inherently delayed and asymmetric. The theoretical output is a regret bound of Õ(√(α(G)·T·max{Reg_Sq, Reg_Log})) for partially revealed graphs, where α(G) is the worst-case independence number over the graph class, with no polynomial dependence on K.

## Method

SquareCB.UG extends the SquareCB.G algorithm by replacing the true feedback graph G_t in the Decision Estimation Coefficient (DEC) with a predicted graph g_t(x_t,·,·) from a separate log-loss regression oracle, reducing the problem to simultaneous online regression over both losses and graphs. The critical technical insight is that the graph oracle must use log loss rather than squared loss: the log loss regret bounds the triangular discrimination between the predicted and true graphs, which is exactly the quantity needed to upper-bound the DEC with the predicted graph by the DEC with the true graph (via an AM-GM inequality). For partially revealed graphs the regret depends on the worst-case independence number α(G) over the entire graph class, while for fully revealed graphs the improved bound Õ(√(Σ_t α_t · max{Reg_Sq, Reg_Log})) adapts to per-round graph difficulty. The DEC minimization reduces to a simple convex program, and in the bidding application a closed-form two-point distribution over actions further simplifies implementation.

## Results

The paper evaluates SquareCB.UG on a first-price auction bidding task using two synthetic datasets (T=5000, 32-dim contexts) and a real eBay auction dataset (5000 samples, 78-dim features), measuring normalized regret (Reg_CB/T). SquareCB.UG achieves the lowest regret across all settings and, unlike SquareCB, its regret remains nearly flat as the number of discretized actions K increases (from 25 to 75 on synthetic data, from 50 to 150 on real data), matching the theoretical guarantee of no polynomial dependence on K. On the synthetic dataset with poor context diversity, the greedy algorithm degrades to the trivial 'always bid 0' baseline (suffering linear regret), while SquareCB.UG maintains sublinear regret. On the real eBay data, SquareCB.UG consistently outperforms all baselines with lower variance than the greedy algorithm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four algorithms: SquareCB.UG (proposed), SquareCB (Foster & Rakhlin, 2020, which ignores graph feedback), a greedy algorithm (picks the best action per loss predictor), and a trivial baseline (always bids 0). SquareCB.UG wins against all baselines on both synthetic and real data. Against SquareCB, the key advantage is robustness to the number of actions: SquareCB's regret grows with K while SquareCB.UG's stays flat, confirming the theoretical independence from K. Against the greedy algorithm, SquareCB.UG is particularly superior in low-diversity context settings where greedy suffers near-linear regret (matching the trivial baseline), and on real data where greedy shows high variance due to lack of exploration. The trivial baseline (always bid 0) is outperformed by both SquareCB.UG and SquareCB in all settings, serving as a lower bound on achievable regret.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SquareCB.G algorithm of Zhang et al. (2023) from the informed feedback graph setting (where the graph is revealed before the decision) to the uninformed setting, where the graph is only revealed after or never fully revealed. It builds on the DEC framework of Foster & Rakhlin (2020) for contextual bandits and the log-loss oracle / triangular discrimination machinery of Foster & Krishnamurthy (2021). The non-contextual uninformed setting was previously handled by Cohen et al. (2016), but no contextual algorithm existed; this paper is the first to bridge that gap, and it further generalizes from deterministic to stochastic feedback graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first efficient contextual algorithm for uninformed feedback graphs with strong regret guarantees that match the optimal informed-setting rate in the fully revealed case. The key open question is whether the worst-case independence number α(G) dependence in the partially revealed setting can be tightened to the per-round α_t, which the authors leave unresolved. The practical relevance is demonstrated through a first-price auction bidding application, though the broader impact is primarily for online learning researchers working on partial-information problems.
