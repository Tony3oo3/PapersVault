## Problem

Existing voice conversion training strategies rely on hand-engineered heuristic perturbations (formant-shifting, pitch-randomization, frequency shaping) to create speaker-mismatched training pairs, which represent only a limited subset of natural voice variations. This paper asks whether a model can generate its own diverse and increasingly challenging training transformations, replacing these fixed heuristics with self-synthesized voice-converted examples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241521/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a source speech utterance (for content extraction via Conformer-SSL) and 10 seconds of target speaker audio (for speaker embedding via TitaNet) as input, producing voice-converted speech at 22050 Hz. The content representation is a temporally downsampled sequence of 256-dim vectors (46 ms per frame) further grouped into duration-augmented vectors via cosine-similarity thresholding. The entire pipeline is text-free, requiring no transcriptions or phonetic posteriorgrams, and operates in either a guided mode (using ground-truth pitch/duration) or a predictive mode (using model-predicted pitch/duration).

## Method

SelfVC uses a FastPitch-style synthesizer with two 6-layer feed-forward transformers (encoder and decoder, hidden dim 1536) plus duration and pitch predictors, trained with a combined mel-reconstruction, pitch, and duration loss. The key training innovation is an iterative self-transformation procedure: after 100k mini-batch iterations of standard heuristic perturbation, the model's own voice-converted output (generated by feeding the source content embedding with a randomly sampled different speaker embedding) is re-encoded by the frozen Conformer-SSL to produce the perturbed content input for the next training step. This creates a curriculum where the reconstruction task becomes progressively harder as the model's conversion quality improves, forcing the synthesizer to rely more heavily on the speaker embedding rather than residual speaker information in the content features. The approach is trained entirely in a text-free manner on 360 hours of LibriTTS speech, with the content encoder and speaker encoder kept frozen during synthesizer training.

## Results

On zero-shot voice conversion (LibriTTS test-clean, 10s target speaker audio), SelfVC achieves SV-EER of 3.4%, SV-Sim of 0.58, Sim-MOS of 3.74, PER of 5.1%, CER of 1.6%, and MOS of 4.06, outperforming all prior methods on every metric. The closest prior method, ACE-VC, achieves SV-EER 6.6% and Sim-MOS 3.29, while YourTTS reaches SV-EER 6.6% and MOS 3.49. SelfVC is trained on only 360 hours of English speech (LibriTTS train-clean-360) yet surpasses YourTTS, which is trained on a substantially larger multilingual dataset. For cross-lingual voice conversion, fine-tuning SelfVC on CSS10 data reduces SV-EER from 12.7% to 4.4% (English→CSS10) and from 7.0% to 4.8% (CSS10→English).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SelfVC outperforms all eight prior voice conversion methods on every reported metric (SV-EER, SV-Sim, Sim-MOS, PER, CER, MOS). The largest gaps are against older methods: AdaIN-VC (SV-EER 28.7%), MediumVC (27.4%), and FragmentVC (23.3%) are far behind SelfVC's 3.4%. Among recent SSL-based methods, ACE-VC (SV-EER 6.6%, Sim-MOS 3.29) and YourTTS (SV-EER 6.6%, Sim-MOS 3.01) are the closest competitors, but SelfVC nearly halves their SV-EER and improves Sim-MOS by 0.45–0.73 points. Diff-HierVC and DDDM-VC achieve competitive intelligibility (PER 5.9% and 5.7%) but lag significantly on speaker similarity (SV-EER 10.8% and 13.7%). Internally, replacing heuristic transforms with self-transformations (SelfVC vs. Baseline-Heuristic) reduces SV-EER from 6.0% to 3.4% and raises Sim-MOS from 3.65 to 3.74, confirming the benefit of iterative self-synthesis. Notably, SelfVC trained on only 360 hours of English data outperforms YourTTS trained on a much larger multilingual corpus, highlighting the effectiveness of the self-transformation training strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

SelfVC extends the heuristic perturbation training strategy introduced by NANSY (Choi et al., 2021) and NANSY++ (Choi et al., 2023a), which used hand-engineered transforms (formant-shifting, pitch-randomization, frequency shaping) to create training perturbations for voice conversion. The key conceptual departure is replacing these fixed external augmentations with self-synthesized voice-converted audio generated by the model itself, drawing an analogy to ViewMaker networks (Tamkin et al., 2021) that used generative models to produce diverse views for representation learning. The synthesizer architecture follows FastPitch (Laencucki, 2021) but operates on grouped SSL content representations rather than text, and the paper explicitly avoids the information-bottleneck/quantization approaches (Polyak et al., 2021; Lakhotia et al., 2021) by training on imperfectly disentangled representations instead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-transformation training strategy is a general principle—using a model's own output as a progressively harder training signal—that could transfer to other controllable synthesis tasks beyond voice conversion. The text-free, phoneme-independent training pipeline makes it straightforward to scale to new languages, as demonstrated by the cross-lingual results where a model trained solely on 360 hours of English outperforms YourTTS (trained on a larger trilingual corpus) for English-to-CSS10 conversion. Limitations include the dependency on a separate HiFi-GAN vocoder, the need for 100k warmup iterations before self-transformations begin, and validation primarily on English with only light multilingual fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the iterative self-refinement converges to a fixed point, how the strategy scales to larger model families, and whether it generalizes to other audio domains (music, environmental sound).
