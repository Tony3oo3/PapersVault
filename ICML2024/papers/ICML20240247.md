## Problem

Offline RL methods face a fundamental trade-off: value function learning achieves Bellman optimality on short-horizon, dense-reward tasks but suffers from credit assignment errors over long horizons, while diffusion-based sequence planners excel at long-horizon reasoning but fail to stitch optimal policies from sub-optimal trajectories in short-horizon settings. No existing unified algorithm performs well across both regimes, and the paper further shows that Diffuser's guidance function converges to sub-optimal value estimates on noisy data, compounding the short-horizon weakness.

## Input / Output

At training time, PlanDQ consumes a fixed offline dataset of (state, action, next-state, reward) tuples. At planning time, given a current state, the D-Conductor samples a sub-goal sequence of length H·K (e.g., K=4 for Gym-MuJoCo, K=30 for AntMaze) via classifier-guided diffusion denoising, and the Q-Performer then outputs a single action conditioned on the current state and the first sub-goal. The high-level trajectory representation includes not only sampled states at every K-th step but also all intermediate actions between sub-goals, which aids value prediction in dense-reward settings.

## Method

PlanDQ is a two-level hierarchical planner: the high-level D-Conductor is a diffusion model over sub-goal sequences (states at fixed interval K plus intermediate actions), trained with a standard denoising objective and a separate return-predictor guidance function for classifier-guided sampling; the low-level Q-Performer is a goal-conditioned diffusion policy π(a|s, s_g) trained jointly with a diffusion reconstruction loss and a Q-learning objective (TD-error minimization on a goal-conditioned Q-function using combined external and intrinsic rewards). The key architectural insight is that sequence modeling at the high level provides long-horizon reasoning, while value learning at the low level restores Bellman optimality for the short-horizon, dense-reward sub-tasks that the low level actually solves. The paper demonstrates via a toy OpenMaze experiment that Diffuser's value predictor regresses to cumulative trajectory returns without Bellman updates, causing it to assign higher values to sub-optimal states on noisy data, whereas the Q-Performer's TD-based learning correctly identifies optimal regions. Ablations over four hierarchical configurations (PlanDD, PlanQD, PlanQQ, PlanDQ) confirm that the D-Conductor + Q-Performer pairing is the only combination that matches or exceeds flat baselines on short-horizon tasks while remaining competitive on long-horizon tasks.

## Results

On long-horizon tasks, PlanDQ achieves an AntMaze average of 83.0 (vs. 76.0 for HIQL and 75.2 for HD), a Kitchen average of 73.4 (vs. 68.9 for DTAMP), and 45.0 on Calvin (vs. 43.8 for HIQL). On short-horizon Gym-MuJoCo tasks, PlanDQ matches the best flat method DQL (105.7 vs. 106.0 on Med-Expert; 81.0 vs. 81.5 on Med-Replay) while outperforming all other hierarchical baselines by 8–12% (e.g., 81.0 vs. 72.3 for HD on Med-Replay). The paper reports an 8% improvement over the strongest hierarchical baseline (HIQL) on AntMaze and a 12% improvement over HD on the noisy Medium-Replay dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PlanDQ outperforms all hierarchical baselines (HIQL, HDMI, DTAMP, HD) on long-horizon tasks: it leads by 7 points over HIQL (83.0 vs. 76.0) and 7.8 over HD (83.0 vs. 75.2) on AntMaze, and by 4.5 over DTAMP (73.4 vs. 68.9) on Kitchen. On short-horizon Gym-MuJoCo, PlanDQ is the only hierarchical method that matches the best flat baseline DQL (105.7 vs. 106.0 Med-Expert; 81.0 vs. 81.5 Med-Replay), while other hierarchical methods (HD, DTAMP, HDMI) trail DQL by 1–9 points. Against flat methods IQL and CQL, PlanDQ dominates on long-horizon tasks (e.g., 83.0 vs. 46.4 on AntMaze) and is competitive on short-horizon tasks. The toy OpenMaze experiment further shows that the Q-Performer (DQL-based) outperforms the Diffuser-based low-level policy by 20% (90.4 vs. 74.9), explaining PlanDQ's advantage over HD on noisy datasets. PlanDQ ties HD on Kitchen Mixed (71.7) but leads on Kitchen Partial (75.0 vs. 73.3) and Calvin (45.0 vs. 31.7).

## Relation to Prior Work

PlanDQ sits at the intersection of two established lines of offline RL research: diffusion-based trajectory planning (Diffuser, HD, HDMI, DTAMP) and diffusion-based Q-learning policies (DQL). It departs from the hierarchical diffusion planners (notably HD, Chen et al. 2024) by replacing the low-level diffusion performer with a goal-conditioned Q-learning diffusion policy, motivated by the observation that sub-tasks at the low level are inherently short-horizon and dense-reward, where Bellman-based credit assignment is more effective than trajectory-level value regression. The paper also provides a theoretical argument (Appendix B) formalizing why Q-learning dominates Diffuser on short-horizon problems with noisy data, while Diffuser retains an advantage for long horizons where the path tree grows exponentially.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PlanDQ offers a practical unified architecture that avoids the well-known trade-off between flat value-based methods (strong on short-horizon dense-reward tasks) and sequence-modeling planners (strong on long-horizon sparse-reward tasks), achieving competitive or best performance across both regimes without task-specific tuning. The empirical demonstration that Diffuser's guidance function converges to sub-optimal value estimates on noisy short-horizon data provides a concrete diagnostic for practitioners choosing between planning paradigms. Key limitations include slower inference due to iterative diffusion sampling at the high level, sensitivity to offline data quality at the conductor level, and the use of a fixed sub-goal interval K; adaptive intervals and noise-robust high-level planners are identified as open directions.
