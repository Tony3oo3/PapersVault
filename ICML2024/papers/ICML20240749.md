## Problem

Existing SDR methods based on dependence measures (distance covariance, HSIC, ball covariance) require an additional independence assumption between the central subspace and its orthogonal complement (η₀ᵀX ⊥ η₁ᵀX) that is difficult to verify in practice, and their dependence measures do not properly reflect the strength of the relationship. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing the Hellinger correlation, which satisfies natural axioms for dependence measures and enables SDR without the independence restriction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sample of n paired observations {(X_i, Y_i)} where X_i ∈ ℝ^p and Y_i ∈ ℝ, with both assumed to have continuous distributions. It outputs a unit direction vector η̂_n ∈ S^{p-1} spanning the one-dimensional central subspace, which is then used as the reduced predictor in a downstream local polynomial regression. The optimization is parameterized in spherical coordinates (p−1 angles) and initialized using directions from existing SDR methods (SIR, SAVE, DR, MAVE), so the method functions as a refinement layer on top of any baseline SDR estimator.

## Method

The core mechanism is to maximize the Hellinger correlation H(αᵀX, Y) over α on the unit sphere, equivalently minimizing the Bhattacharyya affinity coefficient B computed from the copula density of (αᵀX, Y). The key theoretical contribution (Theorem 3.1) proves that this maximization recovers the central subspace without requiring η₀ᵀX ⊥ η₁ᵀX, using the generalized data processing inequality of the Hellinger correlation and a Jensen's inequality argument on the squared Hellinger distance. The sample-level estimator for B uses nearest-neighbor distances in the copula-transformed space (a Rényi information estimator from Leonenko et al., 2008), and the optimization proceeds in two stages: simulated annealing initialized from existing SDR directions, followed by Nelder–Mead downhill simplex refinement. Consistency of the resulting estimator η̂_n is established (Theorem 3.3) under only the single-index model assumption and continuity of the distributions.

## Results

On Model I (Y = (ηᵀX)² + ε) with normal predictors at n=100, the Hellinger-correlation-enhanced methods reduce the subspace distance Δ from 0.9546 (SIR) to 0.037 (SIR-HC), from 0.5154 (SAVE) to 0.0355 (SAVE-HC), and from 0.0654 (MAVE) to 0.0379 (MAVE-HC). At n=400, all -HC variants achieve Δ ≈ 0.018–0.020, confirming consistency. On the UCI Real Estate dataset, MAVE-HC achieves a test MSE of 0.243, matching the no-dimension-reduction GAM baseline (0.245) while reducing 5 predictors to 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed Hellinger-correlation refinement (HC) is applied as a post-processing step on top of seven existing SDR methods and consistently outperforms each one. Against the classical inverse-regression baselines, the gains are most dramatic: SIR drops from Δ=0.9546 to 0.037 (26× improvement) and SAVE from 0.5154 to 0.0355 (14×) on Model I at n=100, while DR improves from 0.2995 to 0.0371 (8×). Against the forward-regression gold standard MAVE, the improvement is more modest but still significant (0.0654→0.0379 at n=100, 0.0205→0.0195 at n=400). Against the modern dependence-measure baselines (distance covariance, HSIC, ball covariance), the HC refinement reduces Δ by roughly 3–4× (e.g., DCOV 0.1521→0.0373, HSIC 0.1578→0.0379, BCOV 0.1425→0.0339 on Model I, n=100) and also reduces standard deviations. On the real estate dataset, all seven baselines improve with HC, with MAVE-HC and SIR-HC achieving the lowest test MSE (0.243), matching the full GAM model without dimension reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Hellinger correlation introduced by Geenens & Lafaye de Micheaux (2022) and the Rényi information estimator of Leonenko et al. (2008) to construct a new SDR objective. It departs from prior dependence-measure-based SDR methods (distance covariance, HSIC, ball covariance) by removing the independence assumption η₀ᵀX ⊥ η₁ᵀX that those approaches require for identifiability, and by leveraging the Hellinger correlation's superior normalization and monotonic-transformation invariance. The method also repurposes classical SDR estimators (SIR, SAVE, DR, MAVE) as initialization points for a two-stage optimization (simulated annealing followed by Nelder-Mead simplex), positioning itself as a refinement layer atop existing techniques rather than a standalone alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key contribution is a consistency guarantee for the SDR direction estimator that does not require the unverifiable independence assumption between the central and orthogonal complement subspaces, which is a genuine theoretical advance over distance-covariance and HSIC-based SDR. However, the method is restricted to the single-index (d=1) case, and the non-sparse simulation results (Tables 11–13) show that improvements over MAVE are inconsistent at larger sample sizes, suggesting the optimization landscape becomes harder as the direction vector loses sparsity. Open questions include extending to multi-index models, classification settings, and establishing convergence rates, all of which the authors identify as future work.
