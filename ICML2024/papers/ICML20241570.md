## Problem

Local feature selection methods in interpretable ML can encode information about labels or non-selected features into their selection masks, producing misleading explanations that falsely indicate which features drove a prediction. No prior work had formally defined this problem or provided a method with a provable guarantee against it, leaving all existing selector-predictor architectures (L2X, INVASE, TabNet, REAL-X) vulnerable to what the authors term label and feature leakage.

## Input / Output

The method takes a d-dimensional feature vector x (or a 28×28 image treated as 784 pixel features, or 3×3 patches for fashion-MNIST) and produces a binary selection mask h ∈ {0,1}^d, a prediction f(x ⊙ h) computed only on selected features, and a step-by-step record of which features were added at each sequential decision round. Non-selected features are masked to ∅ (distinct from zero), and the sequential ordering of selections constitutes a narrative explanation of how the prediction was constructed.

## Method

SUWR (Sequential Unmasking without Reversion) structures feature selection as a T-step sequential decision process: at each step t, the selector ζ either stops (via a Bernoulli trial) or samples new feature indices to add to the growing mask, with every decision conditioned exclusively on the values of features selected in prior steps (x ⊙ h^t). Because features are only ever added and never removed, and because the selector never observes unselected feature values or labels, leakage is structurally impossible. The selector and predictor are jointly optimized via a REINFORCE policy-gradient approach that samples full T-step selection sequences and computes an unbiased loss estimate combining prediction error and sparsity penalty. The paper also derives necessary and sufficient conditions for leakage-free selection (Corollary 2.5) and proves via a linear programming formulation that all existing methods violate these conditions, while conjecturing that under full-support feature distributions, SUWR captures every possible leakage-free policy.

## Results

On the synthetic benchmark (11 features, 6 settings), SUWR achieves near-perfect TPR (>97%) across all settings and perfect CFSR (100%) in the control-flow settings (Syn4–6), while maintaining the lowest FDR among methods with comparable TPR. SUWR reaches oracle AUROC in Syn1–3 (0.700, 0.895, 0.903) and substantially outperforms all baselines in Syn4–6 (e.g., 0.810 vs. 0.781 for L2X on Syn4). On Fashion-MNIST, SUWR with only 6 selected 3×3 patches outperforms CAE with 10 patches, and on digits-MNIST it approximates the no-feature-selection baseline while selecting a fraction of pixels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SUWR outperforms all five baselines on the synthetic benchmark. Against L2X, SUWR achieves dramatically higher TPR (98–100% vs. 33–80%) and lower FDR in most settings, while L2X's low CFSR (34–56%) in Syn4–6 indicates it relies on leakage rather than selecting the control-flow feature. INVASE matches SUWR's TPR in Syn1–3 but has a very low CFSR (56–61%) in Syn4–6, strongly suggesting it benefits from leakage; SUWR's FDR is also lower in Syn1, Syn3, and Syn6. TabNet achieves competitive CFSR (~100%) but has substantially higher FDR (23–36% vs. SUWR's 0.4–20%) and lower AUROC in the control-flow settings. REAL-X is the closest baseline with comparable TPR and CFSR, but its FDR is consistently higher (8–52% vs. SUWR's 0.4–20%) and its AUROC is lower in Syn4–6 (e.g., 0.748 vs. 0.810 on Syn4). On Fashion-MNIST, SUWR surpasses both CAE (global selection) and REAL-X, with CAE requiring 10 patches to match what SUWR achieves with 6, and REAL-X underperforming even the global CAE baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established selector-predictor architecture (L2X, INVASE, TabNet, REAL-X) by identifying a fundamental flaw in the joint optimization paradigm: the selection mask can encode information about labels or non-selected features, rendering explanations unfaithful. Rather than building on a specific prior method, it carves new theoretical ground by formalizing label and feature leakage, deriving necessary and sufficient conditions for their absence, and proposing SUWR—a sequential unmasking process that structurally prevents leakage by ensuring each selection decision only conditions on previously selected features. The approach draws conceptual inspiration from dynamic feature acquisition (Li & Oliva, 2021; Covert et al., 2023) but repurposes the sequential decision structure for a fundamentally different goal: guaranteeing explanation faithfulness rather than minimizing acquisition cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first theoretical guarantee that a local feature selection method produces faithful explanations, which is a prerequisite for trustworthy interpretability in high-stakes domains like healthcare and finance. The proof that all existing state-of-the-art methods (including REAL-X, designed specifically to mitigate this issue) suffer from leakage fundamentally reframes the field's understanding of local feature selection. Key limitations include the computational overhead of sequential selection, the unintuitive 'blind first step,' and the fact that the conjecture that SUWR captures all leakage-free policies remains unproven. Open questions include scaling SUWR to high-dimensional data (e.g., natural images beyond 28×28) and whether the leakage formalism can inform new feature importance metrics.
