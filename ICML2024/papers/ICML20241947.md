## Problem

Existing image restoration methods face a fundamental trade-off: regression-based approaches (e.g., Restormer, PromptIR) minimize distortion but produce overly smooth results that lose structural detail, while generative methods (e.g., IR-SDE, diffusion models) yield visually appealing outputs but often leave residual distortions and inaccurate structures because they condition on the degraded image without encoding degradation-specific information. This paper addresses the gap by asking how to faithfully preserve image structures while minimizing distortion across multiple restoration tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image (noisy, rainy, hazy, or low-resolution) as input and produces a single restored high-quality RGB image as output. It operates in both unpaired and partially paired settings; in the unpaired case, paired training data is randomly shuffled to decouple input-target correspondence. The two-pass design means the degraded image is consumed twice: first unconditionally to produce an intermediate estimate and its residual, then again conditioned on the residual embedding to produce the final refined output.

## Method

RCOT models image restoration as a Kantorovich optimal transport problem with a Fourier residual-guided (FROT) cost that adds a penalty g(r) on the transport residual r = y − x, using ℓ₁ Fourier sparsity for rain/haze/SR and ℓ₂ for noise. The key architectural innovation is a two-pass transport map: a base generator G_{θ₁} first produces an intermediate result and computes the residual r̂₀ = y − G_{θ₁}(y), which is then encoded by a residual encoder E_{θ₂} into a degradation-specific embedding that conditions the second-pass restoration via cross-stage feature fusion (the TRC module). By duality, the FROT objective is reformulated as a minimax problem over a transport network T_θ and a potential network φ_ω, solved by adversarial training (analogous to GAN training). In the partially paired setting, a supervised ℓ₂ loss on available ground-truth pairs is added to the FROT objective, and the transport network backbone is MPRNet with RCAB-based residual encoder.

## Results

RCOT achieves the best performance across all four metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID) on all four restoration tasks. On 4× super-resolution (DIV2K), RCOT reaches 28.41 dB PSNR, 0.804 SSIM, 0.114 LPIPS, and a remarkably low 1.22 FID, substantially outperforming the next-best generative method LINF (FID 3.26) and the best regression method Restormer (FID 6.14). On denoising (Kodak24, σ=50), RCOT scores 29.53/0.828/0.121/33.13; on deraining (Rain100L), 37.27/0.980/0.015/7.97; and on dehazing (SOTS), 31.66/0.976/0.015/10.21, all leading their respective comparisons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RCOT outperforms all 15 baselines on every metric across all four tasks. Among OT-based methods, NOT (Korotin et al., 2023b) and OTUR (Wang et al., 2022a) are consistently the weakest, with OTUR achieving only 24.88 dB PSNR and 24.65 FID on DIV2K SR, far below RCOT's 28.41/1.22. Regression-based methods (MPRNet, Restormer, PromptIR) are competitive on PSNR/SSIM (e.g., Restormer reaches 27.90 dB on DIV2K) but lag significantly on perceptual metrics (FID 6.14 vs. RCOT's 1.22). Generative methods IR-SDE (Luo et al., 2023) and LINF (Yao et al., 2023) achieve the best LPIPS among non-RCOT methods (0.118 and 0.116 respectively) but still trail RCOT (0.114) and are far behind on FID (8.56 and 3.26 vs. 1.22). GAN-based methods (CycleGAN, DeCyGAN, ESRGAN, RankSRGAN) are clearly inferior, with FID values of 6.59–37.26 on SR and deraining tasks. The unpaired variant RCOT* also remains competitive, achieving third-best FID (4.51) on DIV2K SR without any paired supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OT-for-restoration paradigm established by Korotin et al. (Neural Optimal Transport, ICLR 2023) and Wang et al. (OTUR, TPAMI 2022), which parameterize the transport map and potential with neural networks and solve the minimax dual via adversarial training. The key departure is the introduction of a Fourier residual-guided OT (FROT) objective that injects degradation-specific knowledge into the transport cost, and a two-pass residual-conditioned transport map (RCOT) that uses the intermediate residual as a conditioning signal for refinement. This positions RCOT between the regression-based restoration methods (MPRNet, Restormer) that minimize distortion but lose structure, and generative methods (IR-SDE, IDM) that produce realistic but structurally inaccurate outputs, by explicitly encoding degradation information into both the cost and the map.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-pass TRC module is a lightweight plug-in that consistently improves PSNR/SSIM/FID across multiple backbones (MPRNet, NAFNet, Restormer) and four restoration tasks, suggesting broad applicability without architectural redesign. The substantial FID gains (e.g., 1.22 on DIV2K SR vs. 3.26 for the next best method) indicate that residual conditioning addresses a real gap in structure preservation that neither pure regression nor pure generation captures. However, the handcrafted choice of Fourier regularizer (ℓ₁ for sparse degradations, ℓ₂ for Gaussian noise) is task-specific and limits the path toward a unified all-in-one restoration model, which the authors acknowledge as future work. The relatively modest PSNR gains compared to the FID gains suggest the method's primary value is perceptual quality rather than pixel-level fidelity.
