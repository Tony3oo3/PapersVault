## Problem

Existing second-order convergence results for policy gradient algorithms assume unbiased gradient estimators, yet practical implementations under infinite-horizon discounted reward are inherently biased: vanilla policy gradient is biased by finite-horizon trajectory truncation, and actor-critic methods introduce additional bias through critic approximation of the value function. No prior work establishes that these biased variants can escape saddle points and reach second-order stationary points, leaving a gap between theory and the algorithms actually used in practice.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes initial policy parameters θ₀ and, at each iteration, a single sampled trajectory of length H = O(log(ε⁻²)) drawn under the current policy; for the actor-critic variant it additionally takes initial critic parameters w₀ and runs K = Õ(ε⁻⁸) inner-loop TD(0) steps with linear function approximation before computing the gradient estimate. The output is a policy parameter θ_T satisfying ‖∇J(θ_T)‖ ≤ ε and λ_max(∇²J(θ_T)) ≤ √χ·ε (an ε-second-order stationary point), achieved in Õ(ε⁻⁶·⁵) outer-loop iterations. The critic parameter converges to the solution of the projected Bellman equation, which under the linear realizability assumption approximates the true Q-function.

## Method

The paper establishes a general second-order convergence theorem (Theorem 3.6) for biased policy gradient by showing that if the gradient estimator is uniformly bounded and the bias satisfies E[‖d_{t+1}‖⁴|F_t] ≤ D⁴μ⁴, then the iterates escape strict-saddle regions and reach second-order stationary points in Õ(ε⁻⁶·⁵) steps, adapting the coupled-sequence technique of Vlaski & Sayed (2022) from unbiased SGD to the biased setting. For vanilla policy gradient, the truncation bias decays as γ^H, so choosing H = O(log(μ⁻¹)) makes the bias O(μ) and satisfies the theorem's conditions. For the double-loop actor-critic algorithm, the bias decomposes into a truncation component and a critic-approximation component; the latter is controlled by running K = Õ(ε⁻⁸) TD(0) steps in the inner loop, which requires a new finite-time convergence result for TD(0) on nonstationary Markov chains (Theorem 4.8) that removes the standard assumption that the chain starts in its stationary distribution—a critical relaxation since the underlying Markov chain changes after every policy update.

## Results

The paper establishes that biased vanilla policy gradient (GPOMDP estimator with finite-horizon truncation) reaches an ε-second-order stationary point in $\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-6.5})$ iterations with trajectory length $H = O(\log(\epsilon^{-2}))$. For the double-loop actor-critic algorithm (TD(0) critic + policy gradient actor), the same $\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-6.5})$ outer-loop complexity is achieved with $\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-8})$ inner-loop TD(0) steps. Separately, TD(0) on a nonstationary Markov chain converges at rate $O(1/\sqrt{K}) + O(1/K)$ with step size $\alpha = 1/\sqrt{K}$, compared to $O(1/\sqrt{K})$ when starting from the stationary distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's vanilla PG result ($\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-6.5})$) improves upon Zhang et al. 2020 ($\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-9})$) while matching the rate of Vlaski & Sayed 2022 for unbiased SGD, but is weaker than Yang et al. 2021 ($\tilde{O}(\epsilon^{-4.5})$). The key distinction is that all prior second-order PG results (Zhang et al. 2020, Yang et al. 2021) require artificially constructed unbiased estimators (e.g., Q-sampling or periodic step-size enlargement), whereas this paper handles the naturally biased estimators that arise in practice from finite-horizon truncation and critic approximation. For actor-critic, this is the first second-order convergence result; all prior actor-critic analyses (Yang et al. 2018, Qiu et al. 2021, Xu et al. 2020b) only establish first-order or global convergence. The TD(0) nonstationary result adds an $O(1/K)$ term over the stationary result of Bhandari et al. 2018, which is the price paid for not assuming the Markov chain starts in its stationary distribution — a realistic requirement for actor-critic where the policy changes each outer iteration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the second-order convergence framework of Vlaski & Sayed (2022) for unbiased stochastic gradient descent to the biased policy gradient setting, drawing on the federated learning analysis of Vlaski et al. (2020) as a template for handling bias. It departs from prior policy gradient second-order results (Zhang et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2021), which required artificially constructed unbiased estimators, by directly analyzing the biased GPOMDP and actor-critic estimators used in practice. The TD(0) convergence result generalizes Bhandari et al. (2018) by removing the assumption that the Markov chain starts in its stationary distribution, which is unrealistic in the actor-critic setting where the policy changes each outer-loop iteration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a notable gap: all prior second-order convergence guarantees for policy gradient assumed unbiased gradient estimators, yet every practical implementation (finite-horizon truncation, critic approximation) introduces bias. The result validates that standard biased PG methods can still escape saddle points, providing theoretical justification for their use. Limitations include the linear realizability assumption (Assumption 4.9) for the critic, the weaker sample complexity of Õ(ε^{-6.5}) compared to Õ(ε^{-4.5}) for unbiased methods, and the restriction to double-loop (not two-timescale or single-timescale) actor-critic. Open questions include extending the analysis to handle irremovable approximation error and to more flexible critic architectures.
