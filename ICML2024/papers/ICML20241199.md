## Problem

No existing theory guarantees the validity of bootstrap procedures for competitive equilibrium models such as the linear Fisher market (LFM) and first-price pacing equilibrium (FPPE). The equilibrium structure in the data—specifically the constrained Eisenberg-Gale convex program—causes standard multinomial bootstrap to fail for FPPE, and the nonsmoothness of the EG objective (from the max operation) complicates all bootstrap analyses. This paper fills this gap by developing statistically valid bootstrap inference for both models under mild regularity conditions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single observed finite market equilibrium: t i.i.d. items drawn from supply s, buyer budgets b, valuation functions v_i, and the solved equilibrium pacing multipliers β^γ (or inverse bang-per-bucks for LFM). It produces a bootstrap distribution estimate of √t(β^γ − β*) from that single equilibrium, which would otherwise require observing many independent market equilibria. For FPPE with scs, the output is a confidence interval for smooth functions of β* (e.g., revenue, utilities); for fully general FPPE, it is a confidence region for the pair (β*, δ*) constructed via test inversion. A key practical detail is that the EG program need only be solved to o_p(1/t) accuracy, and the Hessian is estimated via finite differences with stepsize η_t ∝ t^{-1/6}.

## Method

For LFM, the paper validates three bootstrap procedures: exchangeable bootstrap (replacing the empirical operator P_t with weighted P_t^{ex,b} in the EG program, proven via exchangeable bootstrap empirical process theory and Pollard's nonsmooth Taylor expansion), numerical bootstrap, and proximal bootstrap. For FPPE, the core difficulty is the box constraint (0,1]^n in the EG program. The paper first characterizes the full asymptotic landscape of FPPE as a random quadratic program (projecting −H^{-1}G onto a cone defined by the active constraint sets I_+ and I_0), then designs bootstrap procedures tailored to each regime: numerical/proximal bootstrap when I_+=∅, a new noise-and-project bootstrap (Eq. 17) under scs (I_0=∅) that adds ε_t-scaled Gaussian noise to β^γ and projects onto a reduced feasible set, and a Lagrangian-based confidence region via bootstrap test inversion for the fully general case. The proofs combine epi-convergence theory (to handle the constrained argmin) with empirical process theory (to handle the nonsmooth max in the EG objective), and the Hessian is estimated via a second-order finite difference scheme with an optimal stepsize derived in Theorem 5.

## Results

On the semi-real iPinYou dataset, the proposed bootstrap (Eq 17) achieves near-nominal 95% coverage for the sum of pacing multipliers when the stepsize exponent d is in [0.2, 0.3] and t ≥ 300 items (e.g., 95–100% coverage at d=0.3, t=500, n=10–50, α=0.1–0.5). Coverage degrades for d=0.4 at small t (as low as 73% at t=100, n=10) but recovers to ~95% as t grows. In synthetic experiments, the bootstrap histogram closely matches the true finite-sample distribution of √t(β^γ − β*) across uniform, exponential, and truncated-normal value distributions, confirming consistency. The paper also proves (Theorem 2) that the standard multinomial bootstrap is provably inconsistent for FPPE, motivating the new procedures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing bootstrap methods in a head-to-head empirical comparison; instead, it validates the proposed procedures against the ground-truth finite-sample distribution obtained by repeated independent FPPE sampling ("true resampling"), which is infeasible in practice. In synthetic experiments (8 buyers, 1000 items), the proposed bootstrap (Eq 17) produces histograms that closely match the true resampling distribution for all three value distributions (uniform, exponential, truncated normal) when d is chosen appropriately, while the standard multinomial bootstrap is shown to be provably inconsistent (Theorem 2) even in a one-buyer FPPE. For LFM, the paper validates three bootstrap variants (exchangeable, numerical, proximal) and shows the exchangeable bootstrap achieves the optimal 1/√t rate without tuning, whereas numerical and proximal bootstrap converge at a slower rate. For FPPE, the paper demonstrates that existing constrained-bootstrap methods (Hong & Li 2020; Li 2023) are inapplicable when strongly active constraints are present (i.e., when some buyers have leftover budgets, I₊ ≠ ∅), and the new epi-convergence-based bootstrap (Eq 17) fills this gap under the scs condition. The semi-real iPinYou experiments confirm that the proposed bootstrap achieves nominal 95% coverage for d ∈ [0.2, 0.3] across varying market sizes and proportions of unpaced buyers, while smaller d values (0.05, 0.10) lead to over-coverage and larger d (0.40) leads to under-coverage at small t.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the asymptotic analysis of Liao & Kroer (2023) and Liao et al. (2023), which characterized the limit distributions of FPPE and LFM under strict complementarity, by removing that assumption and showing the general FPPE limit is captured by a random quadratic program (a projection onto a cone), via Shapiro's (1989) theory of constrained programs. For the bootstrap procedures themselves, the paper builds on the numerical bootstrap (Hong & Li, 2020), proximal bootstrap (Li, 2023), and exchangeable bootstrap (Præstgaard & Wellner, 1993), but adapts them to the specific constraint structure of the Eisenberg-Gale program that characterizes both LFM and FPPE. The key departure from prior constrained M-estimation bootstrap work (Geyer, 1994; Hong & Li, 2020; Li, 2023) is that those methods assume no strongly active constraints, whereas FPPE naturally has them (buyers with leftover budgets), requiring the authors to develop new bootstrap procedures under scs and for the fully general case using epi-convergence theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first statistically valid bootstrap theory for competitive equilibrium models, filling a gap that the authors note has left practitioners without guidance when applying resampling methods to auction and market data. The demonstration that standard multinomial bootstrap fails for FPPE (Theorem 2) is practically important for advertising platforms that may have been using naive resampling. Open questions include extending the theory to nonstationary data, removing the smoothness (SMO) assumption, and developing bootstrap methods for fully general constrained convex programs without any regularity conditions on the constraint structure. The Hessian estimation stepsize (η_t ∝ t^{-1/6}) and bootstrap stepsize (d ∈ [0.2, 0.3]) findings provide concrete practical guidance but remain somewhat heuristic for real-world tuning.
