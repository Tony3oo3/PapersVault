## Problem

General reinforcement learning regret bounds scale as Ω(√DXAT), where D is the MDP diameter. For queuing systems, D is exponential in the buffer size S, making these bounds prohibitively large for practical admission control. This paper asks whether the specific birth-death structure of an M/M/c/S queue with multi-class jobs and class-dependent holding costs can be exploited to obtain a regret bound that is polynomial (or independent) in S rather than exponential.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes online observations of interarrival times and job class labels, along with known per-class rewards R^(i), holding cost functions C(·), service rate μ, and buffer size S. It outputs a deterministic stationary admission policy mapping each queue state s to a subset of job classes to accept, structured as a threshold policy over the priority ordering of classes by expected reward r^(i)(s). The arrival rates λ^(i) are the only unknown parameters and must be learned from data; the service distribution and reward structure are assumed known. The regret bound is expressed in terms of S, m, T, and the known bounds [Λ_min, Λ_max] on the global arrival rate.

## Method

UCRL-AC operates in episodes of doubling length and has three steps per episode: (1) construct confidence intervals for the global arrival rate Λ using a truncated empirical mean estimator and for the class-probability vector p via a total-variation bound; (2) identify the optimistic CTMDP in closed form (Theorem 4.3) — the model with the largest Λ and class distributions that maximize reward by priority order at each state — then compute its gain-optimal policy via Policy Iteration exploiting the birth-death structure; (3) apply that policy for the episode duration. The key structural insight is that because the optimistic CTMDP is known exactly, classical PI (not Extended-VI) suffices, and the bias can be computed in O(S) via closed-form expressions rather than O(S³) matrix inversion. This yields an expected regret of O(S log T + √(mT log T)) for finite servers, with the S-dependence vanishing entirely in the infinite-server case (c = S), and reduces memory from O(S²A) to O(mS).

## Results

UCRL-AC achieves an expected regret upper bound of O(S log T + √(mT log T)) for finite servers, which is an order of magnitude lower than the theoretical upper bounds of UCRL2 and UCRL3 (which depend on the MDP diameter D, exponential in S−c). In the infinite-server case (c = S), the dependence on S vanishes entirely, yielding O(log T + √(mT log T)). Empirically, UCRL-AC outperforms all baselines in regret, with the advantage growing as the buffer size S increases from 20 to 50. Policy Iteration converges in ~4.2 iterations (9.3 ms) versus ~1217 iterations (1.3 s) for Value Iteration, though both yield identical regret.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UCRL-AC outperforms UCRL2 and UCRL3 across all tested configurations (S ∈ {20, 50}, μ ∈ {0.3, 0.4, 0.5}), with the gap widening as S increases; the theoretical upper bound of UCRL-AC is an order of magnitude lower than those of UCRL2 and UCRL3 because it eliminates the dependence on the MDP diameter D (exponential in S−c). Against PSRL, UCRL-AC is initially worse for S=20, μ=0.3 due to overestimation of the global arrival rate leading to overly cautious policies, but surpasses PSRL as the horizon grows and for all larger S values. KL-UCRL is outperformed by UCRL-AC, and its theoretical guarantees do not even apply here since the transition matrix of an optimal policy is not necessarily contracting for the span (violating a key assumption). The key structural advantage is that UCRL-AC exploits the birth-and-death structure and Policy Iteration to avoid the diameter-dependent term entirely, whereas all four baselines inherit bounds that scale with D or S².

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the UCRL2 optimism principle (Jaksch et al., 2010) to the structured setting of multi-class admission control in an M/M/c/S queue, building on Feinberg and Yang's (2011) characterization of optimal threshold policies with class-dependent holding costs. The key departure is extending Feinberg and Yang's results to state-dependent arrival rates, which is necessary because the optimistic CTMDP may assign different class probabilities per state when holding costs reorder job priorities. The truncated empirical mean estimator from Bubeck et al. (2013) handles the heavy-tailed interarrival times, and the closed-form bias computation replaces the O(S³) matrix solve with an O(S) recursion, reducing memory from O(S²A) to O(mS).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

General RL regret bounds scale with the MDP diameter D, which is exponential in buffer size S for queuing systems, rendering them impractical. By exploiting the birth-death structure and threshold policy form, this paper achieves O(S log T + √(mT log T)) regret, and in the infinite-server case the S dependence vanishes entirely—matching the intuition that an M/M/∞ queue has no queueing delay. Open questions include extending to non-increasing state-dependent arrival rates (which would multiply the √(T log T) term by √S) and handling unknown service rates. The practical relevance spans QoS-differentiated network traffic, EV charging with priorities, and any system where buffer-limited admission decisions must balance multiple job classes.
