## Problem

Existing data-free neural compression methods (quantization, pruning) model neuronal interactions as weight-based linear transformations with limited nonlinearity, and Euclidean metrics used in prior dynamics-inspired approaches produce low-rank distance matrices that significantly diminish expressive power. This paper asks whether Riemannian geometry can provide a more expressive, parameter-efficient metric for modeling neuronal interactions, enabling data-free compression that matches or exceeds methods requiring real-data fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the weight matrices of a pre-trained model (e.g., ResNet, Swin-Transformer, DETR) as its sole training signal—no real dataset is accessed. Each m×n weight matrix is replaced by m+n neuron dynamical states of dimension D_q plus a small set of Riemannian metric parameters (projections U^(s) and scalars ρ_α), reducing parameters from mn to D_q·(m+n)+D_μ·(1+D_s·D_q). The output is a compressed model whose inference computes each weight on-the-fly via the RieM metric, with optional dyMerge further collapsing redundant neurons to reduce FLOPs.

## Method

RieM interprets each weight W_xy as a Riemannian metric g(q_x^(in), q_y^(out)) between two D_q-dimensional neuron states, where the metric explicitly captures interactions between all dimensional components via displacement vectors d^(s) and a non-linear summation with trainable projections (Option Two, Eq. 10). Training is entirely data-free: the reconstructed matrix Ŵ is backpropagated against the original W using L2 loss, updating only neuron states and metric parameters. Two complementary mechanisms enhance compression: Shared Correlation Counts (SCC) determines an optimal topological concatenation of weight matrices to exploit shared low-rank structure before compression, and dyMerge identifies output neurons with sufficiently close dynamical states and collapses them via a learned correlation coefficient to reduce inference FLOPs. The approach is architecture-agnostic and applies uniformly to CNNs, Vision Transformers, and DETR-family models.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k, RieM achieves 73.26% top-1 accuracy on ResNet-50 at 12.28 MB (8/16 W/A-bit), surpassing the best data-free baseline UDFC (72.09%) by 1.2% and the non-data-free SQuant (70.80%) by 2.5%. On Swin-Transformer, RieM reaches 80.85% top-1 on Swin-T and 82.96% on Swin-S at 8/8 W/A-bit, outperforming the ViT-specific PSAQ-ViT V2 by 0.64% and 0.83% respectively. On COCO object detection with a ResNet-50 DETR backbone, RieM-DETR achieves only −0.4 AP degradation at 43.6 MB, compared to −0.6 for T-DETR which requires real-data fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RieM consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across every architecture and dataset tested. Against the unified data-free compression framework UDFC (Bai et al., 2023), RieM gains +1.2% top-1 on ResNet-50 (73.26 vs 72.09), +3.06% on DenseNet-121 (73.15 vs 70.15), +2.36% on ResNet-34 (72.22 vs 69.86), and +1.34% on ResNet-101 (76.03 vs 74.69) at matched model sizes. Against ViT-specific methods, RieM beats PSAQ-ViT V2 (Li et al., 2023) by +0.64% on Swin-T (80.85 vs 80.21) and +0.83% on Swin-S (82.96 vs 82.13) at 8/8 W/A-bit, despite lacking any architecture-specific design. Against the tensorized T-DETR (Zhen et al., 2022) on COCO, RieM-DETR achieves −0.4 AP vs −0.6 AP at 43.6 MB, and −0.7 vs −2.2 AP at 33.4 MB, while T-DETR requires real-data fine-tuning. RieM also substantially outperforms older data-free quantization baselines: DFQ (Nagel et al., 2019) by +5.5% on ResNet-18, SQuant (Guo et al., 2022) by +2.5% on ResNet-50, and OSME (Choukroun et al., 2019) by +5.9% on ResNet-50. In the pruning setting, RieM surpasses Neuron Merging (Kim et al., 2020) by +5.1% on ResNet-34 and +3.6% on ResNet-101 at 10% prune ratio, and by +30.7% and +7.5% respectively at 30% prune ratio.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Pei & Wang (2023), which interpreted neural weights as path integrals between neuron states in a Euclidean space, by replacing the Euclidean metric with a Riemannian metric (RieM) that captures cross-dimensional interactions between neurons without additional trainable parameters. While prior work (Shao et al., 2018; Kaul & Lall, 2019) explored Riemannian geometry for data modeling, none applied it to neural compression or to modeling inter-neuron dynamics. The paper also positions itself against the data-free compression literature (UDFC, PSAQ-ViT, Neuron Merging), but departs fundamentally by reinterpreting the architecture itself—replacing weight matrices with neuron dynamical states and metric functions—rather than merely quantizing or pruning existing weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The data-free nature of RieM-based compression is practically important for privacy-sensitive settings where fine-tuning on real data is infeasible, and the consistent outperformance of data-dependent baselines across CNNs, Transformers, and DETR suggests the Riemannian formulation captures intrinsic structure that simpler metrics miss. However, the conversion time (hours to days per model) and the need to explore many hyperparameter configurations limit immediate deployment. Open questions include whether the Riemannian metric can be made differentiable end-to-end for joint training, whether the approach generalizes to non-vision modalities, and whether the theoretical upper bounds on distance-matrix approximation (Theorem F.1–F.2) can be tightened to predict compression quality a priori.
