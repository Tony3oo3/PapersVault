## Problem

Existing Data-Free Meta-Learning methods treat pre-trained models as interchangeable sources of knowledge, overlooking the heterogeneity arising from differences in training data, architecture, and optimization. This paper identifies a heterogeneity-homogeneity trade-off: homogeneous models reduce task conflicts but increase overfitting risk, while heterogeneous models provide diversity but introduce conflicting gradients that degrade generalization. Balancing this trade-off remains unaddressed in prior DFML work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242155/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pool of pre-trained models (e.g., 100 models with varying architectures like Conv4, ResNet-10, ResNet-18, trained on different 5-way subsets) without access to their original training data. At meta-test time, the resulting meta-model receives an N-way K-shot support set (e.g., 5-way 1-shot or 5-way 5-shot) and produces class label predictions on the corresponding query set. The pre-trained models can span multiple domains (CIFAR-FS, miniImageNet, CUB) and architectures simultaneously, with no constraint on their underlying scale or structure.

## Method

Task Groupings Regularization operates in two stages. First, it recovers pseudo-tasks from each pre-trained model via model inversion (cross-entropy + BN-statistics loss), computes Fisher Information Matrix diagonal entries as fixed-dimensional task embeddings using a shared frozen ResNet-34, then applies spectral clustering on a cosine-dissimilarity matrix to group the most dissimilar models together. Second, during meta-training, it samples heterogeneous models from the same group and applies Implicit Gradient Regularization (IGR): each task's gradient is evaluated after a small displacement v_i(θ) = β(∇L̄(θ) − ∇L_i(θ)), which provably (via Taylor expansion under Lipschitz Hessian) implicitly minimizes the gradient variance across tasks without explicit Hessian computation. This aligns conflicting optimization directions toward a shared representation, reducing the heterogeneity term in the generalization bound while preserving the diversity benefit of heterogeneous models.

## Results

On the standard DFML benchmarks, the proposed Task Groupings Regularization achieves 43.58% (1-shot) and 57.49% (5-shot) on CIFAR-FS, 36.20% and 49.00% on miniImageNet, and 33.87% and 47.37% on CUB (all 5-way). These represent improvements of 5.92%–6.33% over the strongest inversion-based baseline BiDf-MKD on CIFAR-FS, 5.54%–6.70% on miniImageNet, and 2.25%–3.05% on CUB. In the challenging multi-domain scenario (models from CIFAR-FS, miniImageNet, and CUB jointly), the method outperforms BiDf-MKD by 7.19% (1-shot) and 16.53% (5-shot), and in the multi-architecture scenario (Conv4/ResNet-10/ResNet-18) by 6.10% (1-shot) and 6.57% (5-shot).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two categories of DFML methods: non-inversion (OTA, DRO) and inversion-based (PURER, BiDf-MKD). The proposed method outperforms all baselines across every dataset and shot setting. Against the strongest inversion-based baseline BiDf-MKD, gains range from 2.25% (CUB 1-shot) to 6.70% (miniImageNet 5-shot), with the largest relative advantage on miniImageNet. Against PURER, the gap is even wider, reaching 16.44% on CUB 5-shot (47.37% vs. 40.93%). The non-inversion baselines OTA and DRO trail by 10–15+ points, confirming that data recovery via model inversion is essential for competitive DFML performance. In the multi-domain and multi-architecture stress tests, the advantage over BiDf-MKD widens to 7.19%/16.53% and 6.10%/6.57% respectively, demonstrating that the task-groupings regularization specifically addresses the heterogeneity challenge that prior methods overlook.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DFML line of work (PURER, BiDf-MKD, DRO) by identifying and exploiting model heterogeneity among pre-trained models, which prior methods largely ignore. It repurposes FIM-based task embeddings (Achille et al., 2019) and spectral clustering to group *dissimilar* models together — inverting the standard multi-task learning convention of grouping similar tasks — and then applies implicit gradient regularization (inspired by Dandi et al., 2022) to align conflicting optimization directions within each group. The theoretical PAC-Bayesian analysis formalizes a heterogeneity-homogeneity trade-off that connects the DFML setting to broader multi-task generalization theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world collections of pre-trained models are inherently heterogeneous in data, architecture, and training, yet existing DFML methods treat them as interchangeable. The heterogeneity-homogeneity trade-off provides a useful theoretical lens for understanding when joint training helps or hurts, and the FIM-based grouping + IGR pipeline is a low-overhead technique that could be adapted to other settings with heterogeneous model pools. Limitations include evaluation only on few-shot image classification with small backbones (Conv4, ResNet-10/18), reliance on a shared model for FIM computation, and the number of task groups being a manual hyperparameter. The authors note extension to NLP and automatic group selection as future directions.
