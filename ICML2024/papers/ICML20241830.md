## Problem

Vision-language Transformers (e.g., CLIP, BLIP2, LLaVA) incur escalating computational costs as token counts grow, yet existing token-reduction methods are either unimodal or restricted to modality-independent architectures. A critical open question remains: how to enable bidirectional cross-modal guidance for token reduction in modality-dependent models where the vision encoder must finish before the language branch can begin, creating a circular dependency that prevents the preceding modality from benefiting from the succeeding one.

## Input / Output

CrossGET is a drop-in acceleration framework that accepts the same image-text pairs as the original model (images at 336×336 to 480×480 depending on the backbone, paired with text sequences) and produces identical output types—retrieval rankings, captions, VQA answers, or class labels—while processing roughly half the tokens per layer. A distinctive property is that it operates both with and without fine-tuning: cross tokens are initialized from informative features (e.g., [CLS] for vision, [BOS]/[EOS] for language), so the framework can be applied zero-shot to frozen models, though trained cross tokens yield better performance recovery. Token reduction is applied selectively per modality and per layer, with the number of tokens eliminated per layer determined by a uniform split strategy (e.g., ⌊N/L⌋ per layer for L layers).

## Method

CrossGET introduces two coupled mechanisms. First, learnable cross tokens are injected into every layer of both modalities and trained via a JS-divergence loss that aligns their after-projection representations across modalities; at inference, these cross tokens act as proxies for the other modality, computing a cross-modal importance score (cosine similarity between the cross token's query and each other token's key) that guides both which tokens to merge and how to weight their ensemble (softmax-weighted summation rather than averaging). Second, the token-matching step is formulated as a discrete optimization over a complete graph and solved by a non-iterative, parallelizable algorithm: it computes the full N×N key-similarity matrix, sorts rows/columns by maximum similarity, applies a lower-triangle dependency mask to guarantee disjoint source and destination sets, then selects r source tokens (ranked by similarity minus cross-modal importance) and assigns each its best-matching destination. This complete-graph approach replaces ToMe's bipartite matching, yielding more reliable pairings while remaining fully parallelizable, and the cross-token design breaks the circular dependency in modality-dependent pipelines by decoupling guidance from the actual inference order.

## Results

On CLIP/Flickr30K image-text retrieval, CrossGET (full: CGSM+CGM+CGE) achieves 92.1 I2T R@1 and 79.6 T2I R@1 (avg 85.9), matching or slightly exceeding the original CLIP (85.7 avg) while reducing GFLOPs by 42% (12.0 vs 20.6) and improving throughput by 57% (401.8 vs 255.2). On BLIP/NLVR2 visual reasoning, CrossGET retains 83.2 test accuracy (vs 83.4 original) with 57% GFLOPs savings and 93% throughput gain. On LLaVA-1.5-7B, CrossGET nearly doubles forward throughput (60.4 vs 32.0) and improves generation throughput by 47% while maintaining >98% of original model capabilities across 10 evaluation benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CrossGET outperforms all token-reduction baselines at comparable or lower computational cost. Against ToMe (the closest unimodal baseline), CrossGET improves avg R@1 from 84.5 to 85.9 while using similar GFLOPs (12.0 vs 11.8); adding CrossGET's cross-guided matching and ensemble to ToMe itself also improves it (84.5→85.1), confirming the benefit comes from cross-modal guidance rather than extra parameters. Against the multimodal-specific TRIPS, CrossGET gains +2.3 avg R@1 (85.9 vs 83.6) at the same 12.0 GFLOPs, and TRIPS degrades severely (to 69.7 avg R@1) when forced to match CrossGET's 12.0 GFLOPs budget. Against UPop (multimodal pruning), CrossGET achieves +10.8 avg R@1 (85.9 vs 75.1) while using less than half the GFLOPs (12.0 vs 51.3). Against unimodal methods DynamicViT, EViT, and Hourglass, CrossGET leads by 2.3–3.3 avg R@1 points. Notably, CrossGET matches the original CLIP's I2T R@1 (92.1) and exceeds its T2I R@1 (79.6 vs 79.3) while halving computation, demonstrating that cross-modal guidance can preserve or even improve quality under aggressive token reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

CrossGET extends the unimodal token reduction/merging line of work (ToMe, DynamicViT, EViT, Hourglass) to the multimodal setting by introducing cross-modal guidance into the token-matching and ensemble decisions. It departs from TRIPS, which unidirectionally uses text features to guide vision token pruning and is limited to modality-independent architectures like CLIP, by using learnable cross tokens to break the circular dependency in modality-dependent pipelines (BLIP2, LLaVA) where the vision encoder must run before the language branch. The complete-graph soft matching algorithm also distinguishes itself from ToMe's bipartite matching by considering all token-pair similarities simultaneously, yielding more reliable matches at a small FLOPs cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CrossGET provides a drop-in, general-purpose acceleration framework that achieves 42–57% GFLOPs reduction with near-lossless performance across CLIP, BLIP, BLIP2, and LLaVA-1.5, and can be applied without any training (zero-shot) when cross tokens are initialized with informative features. This is practically significant for deploying large VLMs on resource-constrained hardware, and the orthogonality to quantization, pruning, and distillation means it can be stacked with other compression techniques. Open questions include extension to interleaved multi-image/text sequences (e.g., Flamingo), interaction with KV-cache compression in autoregressive generation, and whether the approach generalizes to video-language models where temporal redundancy adds another dimension.
