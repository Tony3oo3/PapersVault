## Problem

MR scan times are prohibitively long for population-level screening, and existing acceleration strategies either reconstruct images from undersampled k-space (losing pathology at low sampling rates) or use fixed, non-adaptive sampling patterns that cannot respond to the specific data already acquired. Prior learned sampling methods either optimize for reconstruction fidelity rather than diagnostic accuracy or produce a single static mask for all patients, failing to exploit the sequential nature of k-space acquisition.

## Input / Output

The RL agent observes the partially acquired complex-valued 2D k-space matrix (columns zero-filled where not yet sampled) and outputs a sequence of discrete column indices to acquire next, producing an adaptive binary mask over the k-space columns. The action space is Cartesian (one column per step) and dynamically shrinks as columns are selected. The final mask is fed to a frozen KSPACE-NET classifier that operates directly in the Fourier domain (no inverse Fourier transform to image space) to produce pathology class probabilities.

## Method

ASMR formulates sequential k-space column selection as a Markov Decision Process solved with Proximal Policy Optimization, where the state is the current partially acquired k-space, the action is the next column to sample, and the reward is the log-likelihood of the true pathology label from a frozen KSPACE-NET classifier trained across multiple sampling rates. The actor-critic architecture adapts KSPACE-NET (Fourier-domain convolution followed by a ResNet-18 backbone) to produce a categorical distribution over the remaining columns. Two key design choices distinguish ASMR from vanilla PPO: (1) label-balanced environment sampling to counteract class imbalance in medical datasets, and (2) explicit dynamic masking of already-selected actions rather than a penalty-based approach. The classifier is trained independently with random variable-density masks before being frozen as the reward model, decoupling classification learning from policy optimization.

## Results

On the FASTMRI dataset spanning Knee, Brain, and Prostate MR scans, ASMR achieves within 2% of a fully-sampled image-based classifier's AUROC while using only 8% of k-space (a 12.5× acceleration factor) on 6 out of 8 pathology classification tasks. At 8% sampling on the Knee dataset, ASMR reaches 87.7% AUROC for Abnormal, 93.8% for ACL, and 94.7% for Meniscal Tear, consistently outperforming all non-adaptive baselines. On the Brain dataset, ASMR achieves 7% average AUROC gains over reconstruction-optimized policies, and on Prostate it gains 9.82% over greedy sequential selection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASMR outperforms EMRT (Singhal et al., 2023) on 7 out of 8 tasks, with the largest gap on Brain Abnormal (74.9 vs. 75.0 at 8% — essentially tied) and consistent 3–6 point AUROC gains on Knee pathologies (e.g., 93.8 vs. 88.5 for ACL at 8%). Against learned non-adaptive probabilistic methods LOUPE (Bahadir et al., 2019) and DPS (Huijben et al., 2019), ASMR achieves at least 2.5% AUROC improvement on 7 out of 8 tasks, with the clearest wins on Knee (e.g., 94.7 vs. 92.1 for Meniscal Tear at 8%) and Brain Mass (87.7 vs. 83.5 at 8%). Compared to the VDS random policy (Lustig et al., 2007), ASMR gains 1.33–2.25% AUROC on average, with the gap widening at low sampling rates (≤8%). Against the non-adaptive greedy sequence (Macedo et al., 2019), ASMR achieves absolute gains of 1.87% (Knee), 7.01% (Brain), and 9.82% (Prostate) in AUROC. Finally, ASMR outperforms the reconstruction-optimized RL policy of Bakker et al. (2020) by 2.88% (Knee) and 7% (Brain) average AUROC, demonstrating that optimizing directly for classification rather than image reconstruction yields substantially better pathology detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of RL-based adaptive k-space sampling (Bakker et al. 2020, Jin et al. 2019, Pineda et al. 2020) by replacing the reconstruction-based reward with a classification log-likelihood reward, building on the KSPACE-NET architecture from Singhal et al. (2023) which demonstrated that direct pathology prediction from sparse k-space is feasible without reconstruction. It also departs from learned non-adaptive sampling methods (LOUPE, DPS) by making the sampling policy sequential and state-dependent, so that each new column selection can condition on previously acquired measurements. The key conceptual shift is treating k-space acquisition as a decision-making problem optimized end-to-end for diagnostic accuracy rather than image fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving within 2% of fully-sampled classification performance using only 8% of k-space (a 12.5× acceleration) is a concrete step toward making MR screening feasible at population scale, where current 40-minute scan times are prohibitive. The clear gap between reconstruction-optimized and classification-optimized policies (up to 7% AUC on brain) provides a useful principle: the sampling objective should match the downstream clinical task. Open questions include extending to multi-coil and volume-level classification, validating in prospective clinical settings, and whether the RL policy generalizes across scanner types and protocols without retraining.
