## Problem

Knowledge distillation for autoregressive language models lacks a standardized, theoretically grounded objective function: standard KLD causes mode averaging or collapse, while recent alternatives (RKLD, JSD) are task-dependent and lack analytical justification. Additionally, the use of student-generated outputs (SGO) to address training-inference mismatch is computationally expensive—consuming up to 80% of training time—and risks noisy feedback when the teacher is unfamiliar with the student's generations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source instruction prompt and a fixed training dataset (e.g., databricks-dolly-15k) as input, and produces a smaller student LM that generates text responses, summaries, or translations matching teacher quality. Distillation operates at the token level, decomposing sequence-level divergence into a sum of per-token KL terms over the vocabulary. The student model is trained with an additional language modeling loss on a pre-training corpus (OpenWebText) alongside the distillation loss, and for larger models (OpenLLaMA2-7B→3B) training uses LoRA with rank 16 on query and value weights.

## Method

DISTILLM combines two components: (1) a skew Kullback-Leibler divergence (SKL/SRKL) with mixing parameter α=0.1, which prevents gradient norm explosion by interpolating the denominator distribution and provably bounds the L2 approximation error of the empirical estimator (Theorem 1), yielding faster convergence and better generalization than KLD, RKLD, or JSD; and (2) an adaptive off-policy approach that uses a replay buffer to store SGOs at a decreasing probability λ_R = φ(1−t/T), while an adaptive SGO scheduler incrementally raises the probability φ of using SGOs only when validation loss increases, balancing noisy-feedback risk against training-inference mismatch. The off-policy strategy reuses previously generated SGOs rather than generating new ones every iteration, achieving 2.5–4.3× training speedup over on-policy baselines. The two components are synergistic: SKL's rapid early-stage convergence keeps the policy gap small, making off-policy reuse effective without the performance degradation that plagues KLD- or JSD-based methods under the same off-policy setup.

## Results

On instruction-following (databricks-dolly-15k), DISTILLM achieves the best ROUGE-L across all five benchmarks (Dolly, Self-Instruct, Vicuna, Super-Natural, Unnatural) for GPT-2, OPT, and OpenLLaMA2 model families, with the largest gains on Super-Natural Instructions (28.24 vs. 26.05 for GKD) and Unnatural Instruction (30.11 vs. 28.72 for GKD). On text summarization (SAMSum, T5-XL→T5-Base), DISTILLM reaches 49.11 ROUGE-L, outperforming ImitKD (48.57) and GKD (48.49). On machine translation (IWSLT 2017 En-De, T5-XL→T5-Base), DISTILLM achieves 30.32 BLEU vs. 30.24 (GKD) and 29.87 (ImitKD). DISTILLM achieves 2.5–4.3× training speedup over MiniLLM and GKD, requiring only 1.6× the time of naive KD for OpenLLaMA2-3B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DISTILLM consistently outperforms all five baselines (KD, SeqKD, ImitKD, MiniLLM, GKD) on both instruction-following and task-specific generation tasks. On instruction-following, GKD and MiniLLM—which incorporate student-generated outputs—actually underperform or even degrade relative to simpler methods on smaller student models (e.g., GPT-2), while DISTILLM maintains a clear lead (e.g., 28.24 vs. 26.05 ROUGE-L on Super-Natural Instructions). On SAMSum summarization, DISTILLM beats the strongest baseline ImitKD by 0.54 ROUGE-L (49.11 vs. 48.57) and GKD by 0.62 (49.11 vs. 48.49); on IWSLT translation, it edges out GKD by 0.08 BLEU (30.32 vs. 30.24). In terms of efficiency, DISTILLM's adaptive off-policy approach achieves 2.2–3.4× faster training than on-policy or mixed strategies, and for OpenLLaMA2-7B→3B it requires only 1.6× the training time of naive KD versus 3–7× for MiniLLM and GKD. Notably, DISTILLM is the only method that maintains performance when the student is initialized from pretrained (non-fine-tuned) weights, whereas MiniLLM and GKD suffer significant drops without prior fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoregressive KD lineage from Hinton et al. (2015) through SeqKD (Kim & Rush, 2016), ImitKD (Lin et al., 2020), MiniLLM (Gu et al., 2024), and GKD (Agarwal et al., 2024), which progressively addressed mode-averaging and exposure bias but lacked a unified theoretical objective and suffered from on-policy generation costs. The skew KL divergence is repurposed from Lee (2001)'s statistical language analysis into the KD setting, and the off-policy replay buffer is borrowed from RL (Mnih et al., 2015; Fedus et al., 2020). The key departure is providing the first comprehensive theoretical analysis (gradient stability + bounded approximation error via f-divergence concentration) justifying why a simple skew operation on KLD is preferable to the ad hoc divergences (RKLD, JSD) used in prior work, combined with an adaptive scheduler that eliminates the need for constant SGO generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.5–4.3× training speedup while matching or exceeding SOTA distillation quality makes KD of LLMs substantially more accessible, particularly for practitioners without large GPU clusters. The theoretical grounding (Theorem 1) gives practitioners a principled way to select the skew parameter α rather than relying on task-dependent hyperparameter search, which was a practical pain point in GKD. Key limitations remain: the method assumes shared tokenizers between teacher and student, is designed for supervised fine-tuning rather than preference optimization (DPO/PPO), and the KLD-based objective could potentially be further improved by interpolating with TVD or EMD-based losses as the authors note. Open questions include whether the adaptive off-policy strategy transfers to cross-tokenizer distillation and RLHF-style training pipelines.
