## Problem

Continual Test-Time Adaptation (CTTA) requires models to adapt to a stream of unseen domains while retaining previously acquired knowledge, but existing methods face a fundamental tension: updating shared parameters for new domains causes catastrophic forgetting of old ones, while restricting updates to a few modules yields suboptimal convergence. Additionally, prior CTTA benchmarks assume abrupt, disjoint domain switches, ignoring the gradual, overlapping domain transitions that characterize real-world deployment (e.g., cloudy→rainy, afternoon→night).

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB driving scene image at test time and produces a per-pixel semantic segmentation mask as its primary output. A lightweight Domain Discriminator (DD) head simultaneously assigns a pseudo-domain label to each input, which determines which domain-specific router and expert subset are activated. During the source-free CTTA phase, only the MoDE layers (low-rank down/up projections and router weights) are updated per minibatch, while the frozen ViT backbone and DD remain fixed, keeping the trainable parameter count as low as 0.09M (S variant) versus 54.72M for full-model baselines like CoTTA.

## Method

BECoTTA inserts Mixture-of-Domain Low-rank Experts (MoDE) layers atop each block of a pre-trained SegFormer ViT backbone, where each expert is a rank-r bottleneck (W_down ∈ R^{dim×r}, W_up ∈ R^{r×dim}) with a non-linear activation. Two mechanisms drive domain-specific adaptation: (i) Domain-Adaptive Routing, where D independent routers G_d each select top-K experts per layer based on the input, so that only experts relevant to the current domain are updated, isolating them from irrelevant domain knowledge; and (ii) a Domain-Expert Synergy Loss Θ(D;A) that maximizes the mutual information between domain labels and expert assignments, encouraging experts to specialize in or share knowledge across visually similar domains. During test-time adaptation, the frozen DD assigns a pseudo-domain label to each input, the corresponding router activates its expert subset, and an entropy-minimization loss with confidence-based filtering (threshold κ) drives the sparse update. An optional Source Domain Augmentation (SDA) step creates D proxy domains from the source data to initialize the routers and DD, yielding the BECoTTA+ variant that achieves up to +16.8%p over EcoTTA on CDS-Hard while using ~98% fewer trainable parameters.

## Results

On the CDS-Hard scenario (imbalanced weather & area shifts), BECoTTA(S) achieves 50.7% mean mIoU at round 10 using only 0.09M trainable parameters, outperforming EcoTTA (48.2%, 3.46M params) by +2.5%p while using 97% fewer parameters, and surpassing CoTTA (50.2%, 54.72M params) with 98% fewer parameters. On CDS-Easy (balanced weather shifts), BECoTTA(L)+SDA reaches 63.0% mean mIoU, exceeding CoTTA (58.5%) by +4.5%p. On the novel CGS benchmark (gradual domain shifts), BECoTTA(L)+SDA achieves 60.23% mean mIoU versus EcoTTA's 57.04%. For classification on CIFAR100-C, BECoTTA reduces average error to 35.5% versus CoTTA (38.1%) and EcoTTA (36.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CoTTA (54.72M params), BECoTTA(S) matches or exceeds performance (50.7% vs 50.2% on CDS-Hard w/o SDA; 63.0% vs 58.5% on CDS-Easy with SDA) while using 98% fewer trainable parameters. Against EcoTTA (3.46M params), BECoTTA(S) achieves +2.5%p on CDS-Hard (50.7% vs 48.2%) and +3.2%p on CGS (60.23% vs 57.04%) with 97% fewer parameters; BECoTTA+(L) shows a dramatic +7.8%p gap on CDS-Hard w/ SDA (54.2% vs 46.4%). Against TENT, BECoTTA avoids the severe catastrophic forgetting that drops TENT from 47.9% to 34.6% over 10 rounds (−13.3%p), maintaining stable performance (Δ ≈ 0). Against SAR, BECoTTA(S) leads by +3.1%p at round 10 on CDS-Hard (50.7% vs 47.6%). On CIFAR100-C classification, BECoTTA reduces average error by 2.6%p over CoTTA (35.5% vs 38.1%) and 0.9%p over EcoTTA (35.5% vs 36.4%), with particularly large gains on Contrast (−8.6%p vs EcoTTA) and Fog (−5.4%p vs EcoTTA). On zero-shot domain generalization, BECoTTA(M)+SDA achieves 49.12% average mIoU across BDD100k, Mapillary, GTAV, and Synthia, outperforming TTN (45.95%) and all other TTA baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

BECoTTA repurposes the Sparse Mixture-of-Experts architecture (Shazeer et al., 2017) for the CTTA setting, where prior methods (TENT, CoTTA, SAR, EcoTTA) update a shared set of parameters in a domain-agnostic manner, causing negative interference and catastrophic forgetting. The key architectural departure is the introduction of multiple domain-specific routers and low-rank experts (MoDE) that allow selective, input-dependent updates, combined with a novel domain-expert synergy loss that maximizes mutual information between domains and experts. The paper also extends the CTTA evaluation setting by proposing the Continual Gradual Shifts (CGS) benchmark, which generalizes the standard disjoint-domain assumption to realistic gradual domain transitions, drawing on the Blurry Continual Learning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~98% parameter reduction relative to CoTTA while matching or exceeding accuracy is practically significant for edge-deployed systems (autonomous driving, mobile devices) where memory and compute are constrained. The CGS benchmark fills a realistic gap in CTTA evaluation, as real-world domain shifts (e.g., cloudy→rainy) are gradual rather than abrupt. Limitations include the reliance on a Domain Discriminator trained during initialization (which may degrade on truly novel domains), validation primarily on driving-scene segmentation, and the modest absolute accuracy gains (+1.7–2.1%p) over the strongest baselines. Open questions include scalability to many more domains, robustness when SDA domains are entirely disjoint from test domains, and whether the synergy loss generalizes beyond the top-k routing policy.
