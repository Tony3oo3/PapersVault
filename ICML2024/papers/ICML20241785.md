## Problem

Learning the Boltzmann distribution of molecular systems with normalizing flows trained by energy suffers from mode collapse, failing to explore the full configurational space. Existing ML coarse-graining approaches (force-matching, flow-matching) require long all-atom MD trajectories to train the potential of mean force, and active learning on the CG side has previously relied on costly constrained MD simulations for each high-error configuration. This paper addresses both limitations by separating fine-grained and coarse-grained degrees of freedom and using a simulation-free conditional flow within an active learning loop.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a CG configuration (e.g., dihedral angles φ and ψ for alanine dipeptide), a differentiable potential energy function (AMBER ff96 with OBC GB/SA implicit solvation), and a short 50 ps all-atom MD trajectory as a starting dataset. It produces a conditional normalizing flow mapping latent variables to 58 fine-grained internal coordinates given the 2 CG coordinates, and an ensemble of 10 fully connected PMF networks (input: [cos φ, sin φ, cos ψ, sin ψ]) that output both the PMF value and its standard deviation. The PMF is extracted via a reweighting expectation (Equation 12) over flow samples rather than through force-matching, requiring only sufficient overlap between the flow and the target distribution.

## Method

The core architecture is a conditional normalizing flow of 12 monotonic rational-quadratic spline coupling layers (K=8 bins) with residual parameter networks, trained by energy (reverse KLD) to model p(x_FG | s) — the fine-grained distribution conditioned on CG coordinates. Because the main modes of the Boltzmann distribution are captured by the CG space, the conditional flow only needs to learn the 'soft' fine-grained degrees of freedom, which avoids the mode collapse that plagues unconditional energy-trained flows. The active learning workflow iterates three steps: (1) train the flow by energy on high-error CG configurations (with 30% random older points for stability), (2) train an ensemble of 10 PMF models using reweighting over stored flow samples, and (3) sample new high-error CG points via Metropolis Monte Carlo on the PMF ensemble, broadening them uniformly. The flow is initialized once and progressively updated across iterations, while the PMF ensemble is reinitialized each iteration. For low-dimensional CG spaces, a grid conditioning variant (uniform 100×100 grid) replaces the active learning loop and achieves comparable accuracy with fewer evaluations.

## Results

On alanine dipeptide, the proposed active learning (AL) workflow achieves a forward KLD of the PMF of (9.29±0.24)×10⁻⁴ using (3.35±0.10)×10⁷ potential energy evaluations, while the grid conditioning variant reaches (6.32±0.28)×10⁻⁴ with 2.40×10⁷ evaluations. This corresponds to speedups of 15.9× (AL) and 216.2× (grid conditioning) over all-atom MD simulations, compared to a 4.5× speedup for the state-of-the-art Flow AIS Bootstrap method. On the Müller-Brown potential, the AL workflow achieves a forward KLD of (2.04±0.23)×10⁻⁴ with ~1.13×10⁵ PE evaluations, outperforming a 10⁶-step MC simulation (KLD 1.02×10⁻²) by 10× in both accuracy and cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Flow AIS Bootstrap (Midgley et al., 2023b), the only prior method that learns the full Boltzmann distribution of alanine dipeptide without samples and without mode collapse. The proposed AL workflow uses approximately one order of magnitude fewer potential energy evaluations (3.35×10⁷ vs. 2×10⁸) while achieving a substantially lower forward KLD of the PMF (9.29×10⁻⁴ vs. 2.51×10⁻³), a ~2.7× improvement in PMF accuracy. The grid conditioning variant is even more efficient, reaching a KLD of 6.32×10⁻⁴ with only 2.40×10⁷ evaluations. Against all-atom MD, the method achieves higher PMF accuracy with two orders of magnitude fewer evaluations (e.g., 3.35×10⁷ vs. 1×10⁹ for MD-Long). The one metric where the proposed method trails Flow AIS Bootstrap is the reverse effective sample size (43.83% vs. 92.8% for AL; 83.92% for grid conditioning), reflecting that the conditional flow does not capture the full all-atom distribution as well as the unconditional flow, though the paper emphasizes that the primary goal is PMF accuracy for coarse-grained simulations rather than all-atom sampling quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-by-energy paradigm for normalizing flows (Noe et al., 2019) and the mode-collapse-mitigating α-divergence approach of Flow AIS Bootstrap (Midgley et al., 2023b) by conditioning the flow on coarse-grained coordinates, drawing on conditional normalizing flow architectures (Winkler et al., 2023; Ardizzone et al., 2019). The key conceptual departure is decomposing the Boltzmann distribution into a CG-level PMF (capturing main modes) and a conditional fine-grained flow (capturing only 'soft' degrees of freedom), which structurally avoids the mode collapse that plagues unconditional training-by-energy. For the active learning component, the paper replaces the constrained MD simulations used by Duschatko et al. (2024) for PMF evaluation in high-error CG regions with simulation-free sampling from the conditional flow, and contrasts with traditional force-matching and flow-matching approaches that require comprehensive all-atom training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that active learning on the CG side can proceed without costly constrained MD simulations, opening a path to efficient PMF construction for systems with large energy barriers where all-atom MD is infeasible. The 15.9–216.2× speedup over MD (vs. 4.5× for the prior SOTA ML method) is practically significant for molecular simulation workflows. However, the approach is validated only on two small systems (Müller-Brown, 22-atom alanine dipeptide) with low-dimensional (1D and 2D) CG spaces; scaling to higher-dimensional CG mappings (e.g., Cα backbone of proteins) raises open questions about whether the mode-seeking reverse KLD suffices for multimodal conditional side-chain distributions, and whether the grid-conditioning shortcut generalizes. The paper also notes that the KLD metric becomes unreliable at the achieved accuracy levels due to lack of ground truth in high-energy regions, leaving the true accuracy gain somewhat unquantified.
