## Problem

Conformal prediction provides distribution-free coverage guarantees but is vulnerable to adversarial evasion (perturbed test inputs) and poisoning (perturbed calibration data) that can break the coverage guarantee. The prior certified method RSCP only handles L2-bounded continuous data, certifies evasion but not poisoning, uses only the mean of smoothed scores (yielding loose bounds and oversized sets), and lacks finite-sample correction. This paper addresses all four gaps simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240681/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a black-box model's class probability estimates, an exchangeable calibration set, and a threat model specifying the perturbation ball (L2 radius for continuous images, or add/delete bit counts for binary/sparse graph features). It outputs a prediction set of labels (not a single class) with a certified worst-case coverage guarantee of at least 1−α. The method is score-function-agnostic (works with adaptive or threshold prediction sets) and supports both continuous and discrete/sparse data, with the calibration-time variant requiring only |D_cal| bound computations as preprocessing rather than per-test-point computation across all classes.

## Method

The key insight is to use the full CDF of randomized-smoothed conformity scores (rather than just the mean) to derive tighter upper bounds on worst-case score changes under perturbation. For continuous data, the CDF bound reuses Kumar et al.'s closed-form expression; for discrete/sparse data, the paper extends Bojchevski et al.'s region-partitioning LP algorithm to a CDF-constrained variant solvable in O(m·I) time. For evasion, conservative sets are formed by comparing upper-bounded test scores against the clean quantile (test-time) or by using a conservative lower quantile from calibration scores (calibration-time, faster). For poisoning, the paper formulates the worst-case quantile shift as a mixed-integer linear program over at most k perturbed calibration points, solved to obtain a conservative threshold. Finite-sample Monte-Carlo error is corrected via the Dvoretzky-Kiefer-Wolfowitz inequality for CDFs and Bernstein/Hoeffding bounds for means, with the error budget split across calibration and test estimates.

## Results

CAS consistently produces smaller prediction sets than RSCP across all datasets (CIFAR-10, ImageNet, Cora-ML), all perturbation radii, and all nominal coverage levels. The efficiency gain grows with the certified radius r, reaching 2–3× smaller sets at larger radii, particularly on ImageNet and Cora-ML. With finite-sample correction, RSCP's average set size explodes to nearly all 10 classes on CIFAR-10 at large radii, while CAS maintains a low average size with smaller variance. CAS also certifies a strictly larger maximum set-size-preserving radius (the largest radius at which robust sets match vanilla CP size), meaning more robustness is obtained 'for free.'

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is RSCP (Gendler et al., 2021), the prior state-of-the-art for evasion-robust conformal prediction. CAS outperforms RSCP on every reported metric: average set size is smaller at every radius and coverage level, empirical coverage is closer to the nominal 1−α (less needlessly conservative), and the maximum set-size-preserving radius is larger. The gap widens with perturbation radius, reaching 2–3× smaller sets on ImageNet and Cora-ML at large r. Under finite-sample correction, the advantage is most dramatic: RSCP's sets balloon to nearly all 10 CIFAR-10 classes at large radii, while CAS retains compact sets. For poisoning attacks (both feature and label), no prior baselines with provable guarantees exist, so CAS is the first method to provide certified robustness in this setting; the paper shows that the cost in set size is modest (e.g., only +0.17 average set size for k=1 label flips on CIFAR-10).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Gendler et al.'s (2021) Randomized Smoothed Conformal Prediction (RSCP) by replacing the mean-based score bound with a CDF-aware bound, yielding tighter (smaller) prediction sets under the same robustness guarantee. The CDF bound for continuous data is adapted from Kumar et al. (2020)'s confidence certification framework, while the discrete/sparse data bound extends Bojchevski et al. (2020)'s LP-based algorithm to handle the CDF variant. The paper also introduces an entirely new dimension—robustness to calibration-set poisoning (features and labels)—for which no prior CP method exists, and provides finite-sample correction via concentration inequalities (DKW, Bernstein, Hoeffding) that RSCP lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a critical gap by providing the first provably robust conformal prediction sets against poisoning attacks and extending robust CP to discrete/sparse data (e.g., graphs), broadening the applicability of distribution-free uncertainty quantification in adversarial settings. The CDF-aware bound is a general technique with potential independent interest beyond CP (e.g., certifying GNNs with regression tasks). Key limitations remain: the coverage guarantee is marginal rather than conditional, Monte-Carlo estimation of smooth scores is computationally expensive, and the certificate assumes the attacker's sole goal is to reduce coverage—other adversarial objectives (e.g., inflating set size or targeting specific label subsets) are unaddressed. The intersection of robustness and fairness (group-conditional coverage under attack) is identified as an open direction.
