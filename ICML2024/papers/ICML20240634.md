## Problem

Deep partial label learning (PLL) classifiers are heavily degraded by noisy false-positive candidate labels, especially under competitive noise where false labels are correlated with the true label, and DNNs become over-confident on any fed example. Existing deep PLL research lacks theoretical guarantees linking label noise (ambiguity degree) to classification performance, leaving the question of whether and when label smoothing can mitigate this degradation unexplored.

## Input / Output

Each training instance is an image (28×28 grayscale for Fashion-MNIST/Kuzushiji-MNIST, 32×32 RGB for CIFAR-10/100) paired with a candidate label set of size 3–11 (depending on dataset and noise level), of which only one is the true label. The model outputs a single predicted class label from L classes (10 or 100). The key distinction from standard classification is that the supervision signal is a set of ambiguous labels rather than a single ground-truth, and the method must internally identify the true label from the candidate set during training.

## Method

The paper introduces a smoothed partial label definition that distributes the smoothing mass r uniformly over the candidate label set (rather than all L classes), yielding a new loss that interpolates between the clean-label risk and the candidate-label risk. Theoretically, the authors prove lower and upper bounds showing the expected risk under smoothed partial labels approximates the risk under clean labels, and derive an optimal smoothing rate r_opt = (c+1)r*/Δ that is explicitly linked to a newly defined generalized ambiguity degree ε (the maximum probability of all false-positive labels co-occurring with the true label). Empirically, the LS-PLL algorithm alternates between (i) training a CNN (LeNet-5 or ResNet) by minimizing the smoothed partial-label cross-entropy loss and (ii) identifying the ground-truth label per instance via a moving-average of softmax outputs normalized over the candidate set, with the identified label fed back into the next smoothing step. Experiments on four benchmark datasets with competitive noise confirm that label smoothing consistently improves accuracy and tightens pre-logit cluster geometry, and that the empirically best smoothing rate closely matches the theoretically derived r_opt.

## Results

LS-PLL (with label smoothing) consistently outperforms the baseline (without label smoothing) across all four datasets and all noise levels. The gains are most dramatic on CIFAR-10, where LS-PLL improves accuracy by ~19.6% (Avg.#CL=3), ~26.2% (Avg.#CL=4), and ~28.3% (Avg.#CL=5) over the w/o LS baseline. On CIFAR-100, improvements range from ~23.8% (Avg.#CL=7) to ~14.5% (Avg.#CL=11). On Kuzushiji-MNIST, LS-PLL achieves up to 90.64% (Avg.#CL=3) vs. 85.28% without smoothing, and on Fashion-MNIST the gain is more modest at ~2.2% (Avg.#CL=3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external PLL baselines (e.g., PRODEN, NCPD, PICO); instead, the primary comparison is an ablation of label smoothing on/off within the same training pipeline. LS-PLL (w/ LS) wins on every dataset and every noise level, with the margin growing as the number of candidate labels increases. On CIFAR-10/ResNet-18, the gap widens from +19.6% at Avg.#CL=3 to +28.3% at Avg.#CL=5, confirming that label smoothing is most beneficial under higher ambiguity. On CIFAR-100/ResNet-56, the improvement is +23.8% at Avg.#CL=7 and +14.5% at Avg.#CL=11. On the simpler Fashion-MNIST and Kuzushiji-MNIST (LeNet-5), gains are smaller (+2.2% and +5.4% at Avg.#CL=3 respectively) but still consistent. The paper also validates that the empirically best smoothing rate (e.g., r=0.3 on CIFAR-10 at Avg.#CL=3) closely matches the theoretically derived optimal rate r_opt=0.24, linking the empirical gains to the generalized ambiguity degree.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of deep partial label learning (PLL) and label smoothing. It adapts the label smoothing formulation from Szegedy et al. (2016) to the PLL setting where the ground-truth label is unknown, defining a new smoothed partial label (Definition 3.1) that distributes the smoothing mass over the candidate label set rather than all L classes. It also extends the ambiguity degree from Cour et al. (2011), which considered only a single false-positive label, to a 'generalized ambiguity degree' that captures the joint probability of multiple competitive false-positive labels co-occurring with the true label. The theoretical contribution—proving lower/upper bounds on expected risk and deriving a closed-form optimal smoothing rate as a function of the ambiguity degree—is the first such guarantee in the deep PLL literature, which previously lacked any formal connection between label noise level and classification performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical bridge between label noise (quantified by the generalized ambiguity degree) and the optimal label smoothing rate in deep PLL, offering practitioners a principled formula (Eq. 8) for choosing the smoothing hyperparameter rather than relying on grid search. The empirical improvements are large (e.g., ~20–29% accuracy gains on CIFAR-10/100), though they are measured against a simple 'no label smoothing' baseline rather than against other deep PLL methods (PRODEN, NCPD, PICO, mutual learning), which limits the practical takeaway. Open questions include whether the optimal-rate formula generalizes to instance-dependent or non-i.i.d. noise, whether the approach extends to other weakly-supervised settings (multi-instance, multi-label), and whether the combinatorial terms in Δ can be estimated reliably from finite samples in real-world deployments.
