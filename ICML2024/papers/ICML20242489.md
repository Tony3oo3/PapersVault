## Problem

Existing IMTS forecasting methods rely on neural ODEs to handle intra-series irregularity but fail to explicitly model inter-time series correlations, and the canonical pre-alignment representation used to align asynchronous variables causes sequence length to grow explosively with the number of variables. No prior approach simultaneously addresses intra-series irregularity, inter-series asynchrony, and scalability for large variable counts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242489/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw, unaligned irregular multivariate time series where each variable has its own variable-length sequence of (timestamp, value) pairs, plus a set of arbitrary future-timestamp forecasting queries per variable. No pre-alignment or padding is required; the number of observations per variable can differ substantially. Output is a scalar predicted value for each query timestamp, produced by an MLP that concatenates the variable's flattened patch-level latent representation with a continuous time embedding of the query.

## Method

T-PATCHGNN first applies transformable patching: each univariate irregular series is independently segmented into patches spanning a fixed time horizon (e.g., 8 hours) but containing a variable number of observations, which aligns all variables at a consistent patch-level temporal resolution without pre-alignment. Each variable-length patch is encoded via a continuous time embedding concatenated with values, then processed by a Transformable Time-aware Convolution Network (TTCN) whose meta-filter generates length-adaptive convolution kernels, yielding a fixed-size patch embedding. A Transformer with positional encodings models intra-series sequential dependencies across patches. For inter-series correlation, time-adaptive GNNs operate on a series of time-varying adjacency matrices learned by gated-adding static variable embeddings with dynamic patch embeddings, so the graph structure evolves at each patch's time horizon. The final prediction is produced by an MLP over the flattened multi-patch representation concatenated with the query's time embedding, trained end-to-end with MSE loss.

## Results

T-PATCHGNN achieves the best MSE and MAE across all four datasets: PhysioNet (MSE 4.98×10⁻³, MAE 3.72×10⁻²), MIMIC (MSE 1.69×10⁻², MAE 7.22×10⁻²), Human Activity (MSE 2.66×10⁻³, MAE 3.15×10⁻²), and USHCN (MSE 5.00×10⁻¹, MAE 3.08×10⁻¹). The largest margin is on PhysioNet, where T-PATCHGNN outperforms the second-best baseline (GRU-D, MSE 5.59×10⁻³) by approximately 11%. On the remaining datasets, improvements over the second-best range from ~2% (MIMIC vs. TimesNet) to ~5% (Human Activity vs. Warpformer, USHCN vs. Crossformer). The model also achieves at least 65× faster training and 15× faster inference compared to ODE-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T-PATCHGNN outperforms all 17 baselines on every dataset for both MSE and MAE. Among MTS forecasting models, Crossformer (MSE 5.25×10⁻¹ on USHCN) and Graph Wavenet (MSE 5.29×10⁻¹ on USHCN) are the closest competitors, but they still trail T-PATCHGNN by ~5%; DLinear and TimesNet are far behind, particularly on PhysioNet where DLinear's MSE is 8.4× worse. Among IMTS-specific models, GRU-D is the strongest on PhysioNet (MSE 5.59×10⁻³) and MIMIC (MSE 1.76×10⁻²), yet T-PATCHGNN still leads by 11% and 4% respectively; Warpformer is the closest on Human Activity (MSE 2.79×10⁻³ vs. 2.66×10⁻³) and USHCN (MSE 5.25×10⁻¹ vs. 5.00×10⁻¹). ODE-based forecasting models (Latent-ODE, CRU, Neural Flow) are consistently outperformed, with CRU showing particularly poor results on Human Activity (MSE 6.97×10⁻³, more than 2.6× worse than T-PATCHGNN). The IMTS classification baselines SeFT and RainDrop degrade severely on Human Activity (MSE 12.20×10⁻³ and 14.92×10⁻³), confirming that their set-based representations are ill-suited for fine-grained forecasting. The interpolation model mTAND is competitive on PhysioNet and MIMIC but lags on the other two datasets, and all pre-alignment-based methods (GRU-D, Warpformer, mTAND) suffer from sequence length explosion, with Warpformer running out of memory beyond 500 variables while T-PATCHGNN scales to 2000 variables with only ~47% training time increase.

## Relation to Prior Work

T-PATCHGNN sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) neural ODE-based IMTS forecasting (Latent-ODE, GRU-ODE-Bayes, CRU, Neural Flows), which handles intra-series irregularity but largely ignores inter-series correlations; (2) time series patching (PatchTST), which the authors generalize from fixed-length patches on regular series to variable-length patches on irregular series with a unified time horizon; and (3) adaptive graph structure learning for MTS (GraphWavenet, MTGNN), which they extend to the asynchronous IMTS setting via time-varying graphs. The key conceptual departure is bypassing the canonical pre-alignment representation entirely by independently patching each univariate series, thereby achieving patch-level temporal alignment without the sequence length explosion that plagues timestamp-grid alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine scalability bottleneck in IMTS modeling: the canonical pre-alignment representation can inflate sequence length by up to 96× (MIMIC), making large-variable-count scenarios intractable for existing methods. The 17-baseline, 4-dataset benchmark is a valuable resource for an underexplored task, and the transformable patching idea is a clean, generalizable principle that could inform other irregular-signal processing tasks. Open questions include how the approach behaves with highly non-stationary sampling patterns, whether the fixed patch window size can be learned end-to-end, and whether the GNN-based inter-series modeling scales to hundreds or thousands of variables without architectural changes.
