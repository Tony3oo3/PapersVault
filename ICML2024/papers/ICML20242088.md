## Problem

Transferring pretrained diffusion models to target domains with only 10 images is difficult because (1) the iterative denoising process means no clean generated image is available during training for direct comparison with targets, and (2) the fully random Gaussian noise is non-targeted, causing uneven transfer pace across images and severe overfitting under limited data. Existing GAN-based few-shot transfer methods cannot be directly applied due to these fundamental differences in the DPM training paradigm.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained DPM (DDPM or LDM) trained on a large source dataset (e.g., FFHQ, LSUN Church, or ImageNet), a set of 10 target-domain images, and random Gaussian noise as input. It produces images in the target domain style at the same resolution as the source model. Only a lightweight LoRA-style adaptor module (1.3–1.6% of total parameters) is updated during fine-tuning, while the pretrained U-Net and autoencoder remain frozen. A binary classifier, trained on noised target images across all 1000 timesteps, provides the similarity guidance signal.

## Method

DPMs-ANT introduces two complementary strategies for few-shot DPM transfer. First, similarity-guided training replaces direct image comparison with a classifier-based gradient term ∇_{x_t} log p_φ(y=T|x_t) added to the standard DDPM noise-prediction loss, indirectly measuring the domain gap without requiring a clean generated image. Second, adversarial noise selection formulates a min-max objective where the inner maximization (solved via J=10 steps of gradient ascent with normalization) identifies the worst-case Gaussian noise the current model fails to denoise, and the outer minimization specifically targets that noise, concentrating learning on the most informative perturbations. The full procedure trains only the adaptor parameters for ~300 iterations (vs. ~5000 for baselines), achieving faster convergence and better FID/Intra-LPIPS on 10-shot tasks across both DDPM and LDM frameworks.

## Results

On 10-shot few-shot image generation, DPMs-ANT achieves the best Intra-LPIPS (diversity) across all five target domains, reaching 0.723 on LSUN Church→Landscape drawings and 0.648 on LSUN Church→Haunted Houses, outperforming the previous DPM-based method DDPM-PA (0.706 and 0.628 respectively) and all GAN-based baselines. On FID (quality), ANT achieves 20.06 on FFHQ→Sunglasses and 46.70 on FFHQ→Babies, substantially beating DDPM-PA (34.75 and 48.92) and the best GAN baseline DCL (38.01 and 52.56). The method requires only ~300 training iterations (vs. ~5,000 for baselines) and fine-tunes just 1.3% of parameters via an adaptor module, reducing GPU time from ~4.2 hours to ~3 hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPMs-ANT outperforms all five GAN-based baselines (TGAN, TGAN+ADA, EWC, CDC, DCL) and the prior DPM-based method DDPM-PA on Intra-LPIPS across every evaluated target domain, with the largest margins on LSUN Church→Landscape drawings (+0.019 over DDPM-PA, +0.049 over CDC) and FFHQ→Raphael's paintings (+0.040 over DDPM-PA). On FID, ANT achieves a dramatic improvement over DDPM-PA on FFHQ→Sunglasses (20.06 vs. 34.75, a 42% reduction) and a more modest gain on FFHQ→Babies (46.70 vs. 48.92), while also beating the strongest GAN baseline DCL by 17.95 FID points on Sunglasses. Notably, ANT achieves these results while fine-tuning only 1.3% of parameters (vs. 100% for all baselines) and requiring ~300 iterations instead of ~5,000, making it both more effective and substantially more efficient. The LDM variant (LDM-ANT) further extends these gains, achieving the highest Intra-LPIPS on Landscape drawings (0.738) and Haunted Houses (0.653) among all methods tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the few-shot image generation task from GAN-based methods (TGAN, CDC, DCL, EWC) to diffusion models, building directly on DDPM's training objective and the classifier-guidance mechanism of Dhariwal & Nichol (2021). It departs from the sole prior DPM-based few-shot method, DDPM-PA (Zhu et al., 2022), by identifying two challenges specific to diffusion transfer—iterative denoising prevents direct image-to-image comparison, and random Gaussian noise causes unbalanced transfer across samples—and addressing them with a similarity-guided classifier loss and a min-max adversarial noise selection scheme. The parameter-efficient adaptor (LoRA-style) follows Houlsby et al. (2019), and the overall pipeline reuses the conditional reverse-process formulation from classifier-guided diffusion for domain alignment rather than class conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The adversarial noise selection idea—finding the worst-case noise the current model fails to denoise and training specifically on it—offers a general principle for accelerating diffusion model adaptation that could transfer to other fine-tuning or domain-shift settings. The practical efficiency gain (≈300 iterations vs. ≈5,000 for baselines, with only 1.3–1.6% of parameters updated) makes few-shot diffusion transfer substantially more accessible. However, the evaluation is confined to 10-shot settings on a handful of small-scale datasets, and the authors themselves flag a privacy-leakage risk (e.g., reflections in generated sunglasses mirroring target images) that remains unresolved. Open questions include whether the adversarial noise strategy scales to larger domain gaps, multi-domain or sequential transfer, and higher-resolution generation.
