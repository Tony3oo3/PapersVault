## Problem

Pufferfish privacy, a flexible generalization of differential privacy for correlated data and arbitrary secrets, lacks two critical ingredients: (1) a unified, computationally tractable theory for designing additive noise mechanisms across diverse noise distributions, and (2) composition or amplification guarantees that would enable its use in iterative algorithms like private stochastic gradient descent. Existing mechanisms are tied to specific noise distributions (e.g., Laplace with ∞-Wasserstein distances) or rely on loose sufficient conditions, and no mechanism-agnostic sequential composition theorem exists.

## Input / Output

The mechanisms take a dataset X sampled from an adversary's prior θ, a numerical query f: D → R^d, a Pufferfish framework (S, Q, Θ) specifying secrets and indistinguishability pairs, and a noise distribution ζ. The key computational object is Δ_G, the maximum ∞-Wasserstein distance between conditional distributions P(f(X)|s_i, θ) and P(f(X)|s_j, θ) over all secret pairs and priors, which replaces the classical DP sensitivity. The output is the noised result f(X)+N with an explicit (α, ε)-RPP guarantee, or for the PABI application, the final model weights W_T after T contractive noisy iterations with a privacy bound that depends on per-step Wasserstein shifts rather than accumulating composition.

## Method

The paper introduces Rényi Pufferfish Privacy (RPP), bounding the Rényi divergence between output distributions conditioned on different secrets, and shows it subsumes standard Pufferfish privacy while enabling moment-based privacy accounting. The General Wasserstein Mechanism (GWM) applies the shift reduction lemma to decompose the Rényi divergence of noised distributions into a Wasserstein-constrained shift term and a noise-dependent term R_α(ζ, Δ_G), yielding closed-form guarantees for Gaussian, Laplacian, and generalized Cauchy noise. Two utility improvements relax the ∞-Wasserstein constraint: a δ-approximation (GAWM) that ignores low-probability tails via (z,δ)-proximity, and a distribution-aware variant (DAGWM) using p-Wasserstein shifts with Cauchy noise that captures correlation structure. For iterative algorithms, a dataset-dependent contraction lemma and a new PABI theorem bound the privacy loss of T noisy gradient steps by a sum of per-step R_α terms, recovering DP-SGD bounds as a special case while explicitly accounting for data correlations through conditional Wasserstein distances.

## Results

In attribute privacy experiments on three real-world datasets, the General Wasserstein Mechanism (GWM) achieves sensitivities of 8 (Student Scores), 8 (Heart), and 1 (Adult), compared to DP sensitivities of 20, ≥100, and 1 respectively. The Distribution-Aware GWM with Cauchy noise (DAGWM) further reduces sensitivities to 2.76, 7.80, and 0.42 on the same datasets. Lower sensitivity directly translates to less noise added and thus better utility at the same privacy budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its mechanisms against standard Differential Privacy (Dwork & Roth, 2014) as the baseline, measuring sensitivity (lower is better for utility). On Student Scores, GWM reduces sensitivity from 20 to 8 (2.5× improvement), while the Cauchy-based DAGWM achieves 2.76 (≈7.3× improvement). On the Heart dataset, the improvement is most dramatic: DP sensitivity is at least 100, while GWM achieves 8 (>12× reduction) and Cauchy achieves 7.80. On the Adult dataset, GWM matches DP (both at 1), but the Cauchy mechanism still improves to 0.42 (≈2.4× reduction). Overall, GWM and DAGWM consistently achieve equal or strictly lower sensitivities than DP across all three datasets, with the largest gains on datasets where attribute correlations are strong.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Pufferfish privacy framework (Kifer & Machanavajjhala, 2014) by introducing a Rényi divergence-based variant (RPP), analogous to how Rényi DP extends standard DP. The General Wasserstein Mechanism generalizes Song et al.'s (2017) Laplace-specific Wasserstein mechanism to arbitrary noise distributions, while the privacy amplification by iteration results adapt the shift reduction lemmas of Feldman et al. (2018) and Altschuler & Chewi (2023) to the Pufferfish setting. A notable conceptual contribution is the formal equivalence between Pufferfish privacy and distribution privacy (Chen & Ohrimenko, 2023), unifying two previously separate frameworks. The shift reduction lemma serves as the unifying technical thread connecting all the paper's mechanisms and amplification results.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Pufferfish privacy has been largely impractical due to the absence of general additive mechanisms and composition theorems; this paper addresses both gaps by providing closed-form mechanisms for arbitrary noise and a PABI-based analysis that bypasses composition entirely for iterative algorithms. The first application of Pufferfish to private convex optimization (DP-SGD) opens a path toward privacy-preserving ML on correlated data, where standard DP is either inapplicable or overly conservative. Limitations include the computational difficulty of Wasserstein distances in general settings, the still-loose PABI bounds, and the need for careful design of the Pufferfish instantiation to avoid weak guarantees. Open questions include tighter amplification via other shift reduction variants and numerical optimization of utility in complex practical scenarios.
