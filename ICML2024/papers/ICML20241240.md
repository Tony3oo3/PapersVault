## Problem

While it is known that ReLU networks with width d+1 can universally approximate continuous functions on [0,1]^d, the optimal approximation rate for these narrowest networks—i.e., the best achievable relationship between the number of tunable parameters and the approximation error—remained unresolved. Prior results either characterized rates only for wider networks (width 2d+10 or more) or achieved suboptimal rates (O(ω_f(L^{-1/d}))) for minimally wide architectures, leaving a gap between universality and quantitative optimality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous function f ∈ C([0,1]^d) (or a continuous mapping f: T → R^v over a compact set T ⊂ R^d) and constructs a ReLU FNN ρ: R^d → R (or R^d → R^v) with minimal width (max{d+1,5} for L^p scalar case, d+11 for L^∞ scalar case, max{d+1,v+6} for L^p vector case, d+v+10 for L^∞ vector case) and depth O(L). The approximation error is bounded in terms of the modulus of continuity ω_f evaluated at L^{-2/d} (or L^{-2/(d+1)} for the uniform norm), with the network width exceeding the theoretical minimum by only an absolute constant.

## Method

For the L^p norm, the construction partitions [0,1]^d into K^d small cubes (K = ⌊L^{2/d}⌋) plus a trifling region Ω of negligible Lebesgue measure. A width-d+1 network maps each point to its cube index β via per-coordinate step functions, encodes β as a scalar ψ(β), and a width-5, depth-O(L) point-fitting network (Proposition 3.4) approximates f at cube vertices, yielding rate O(ω_f(L^{-2/d})). For the uniform norm, the method follows Yarotsky's refined linear interpolation: a coarse grid at scale 1/n (n ~ L^{1/d}) captures the first-order approximation, then a bit-extraction technique encodes O((m/n)^d) information per coarse cube using only O(1) parameters, achieving rate O(ω_f(L^{-2/(d+1)})) at width d+11. Optimality is established via VC dimension lower bounds showing no ReLU FNN with O(L) parameters can exceed rate O(L^{-2/d}) on Lipschitz functions.

## Results

The paper proves that ReLU FNNs with width max{d+1, 5} and depth O(L) achieve the optimal approximation rate O(ω_f(L^{-2/d})) under L^p norm (p ∈ [1,∞)) for continuous functions on [0,1]^d. For the uniform norm, width d+11 suffices to achieve O(ω_f(L^{-2/(d+1)})). For vector-valued mappings to R^v, width max{d+1, v+6} achieves O(ω_f(L^{-2/d})) under L^p norm, and width d+v+10 achieves O(ω_f(L^{-2/(d+1)})) under the uniform norm. These rates are shown to be optimal via VC-dimension lower bounds for piecewise-polynomial activations (ReLU, LeakyReLU, ReLU², Softsign).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The central comparison is between this paper's minimal-width optimal-rate result and two prior paradigms. Hanin & Sellke (2017) and Park et al. (2020) achieve universality at the minimal width (d+v and max{d+1,v} respectively) but only obtain the suboptimal rate O(ω_f(L^{-1/d})), which is a factor of L^{1/d} worse. Yarotsky (2018) achieves the optimal rate O(ω_f(L^{-2/d})) but requires width 2d+10, roughly double the input dimension. This paper closes the gap by proving that width d+1 (or max{d+1,5}) is sufficient for the optimal rate, matching Yarotsky's rate at the minimum width. Kidger & Lyons (2020), Cai (2022), and Duan et al. (2023) establish minimal-width universality for various activations but do not characterize approximation rates (listed as N.A. in Table 1). For the uniform norm, the rate degrades slightly to O(ω_f(L^{-2/(d+1)})) at width d+v+10, which is nearly optimal and still improves upon the O(ω_f(L^{-1/d})) of Hanin & Sellke and Park et al.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed research threads: (1) determining the minimum width of ReLU FNNs for universal approximation (Hanin & Sellke 2017; Park et al. 2020; Kidger & Lyons 2020; Cai 2022; Kim et al. 2023), and (2) characterizing optimal approximation rates in terms of width and depth (Yarotsky 2018; Shen et al. 2019a, 2022b). Prior work established that width d+1 suffices for universality but left the rate question open, while rate-optimal constructions required width 2d+10 or more. The paper bridges this gap by combining the max-min string construction of Hanin & Sellke (2017) for space partitioning, the point-fitting technique of Shen et al. (2019a, 2022b) for value interpolation, and the bit-extraction method of Yarotsky (2018) for the uniform norm case, all within a register-model framework that keeps width at d+O(1). The extension to diverse activations (LeakyReLU, ELU, Sigmoid, etc.) follows directly from Zhang et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result completes the approximation-rate landscape for ReLU FNNs by showing that the width constraint does not bottleneck the rate: the minimally wide network achieves the same O(L^{-2/d}) rate as wider constructions, which is information-theoretically optimal via VC dimension lower bounds. This has practical implications in suggesting that practitioners can use the most parameter-efficient narrow architectures without sacrificing approximation quality. Open questions remain: whether the d+O(1) width bound is tight for the uniform norm (current result uses d+11 vs. the known lower bound of d+1), whether LeakyReLU networks at the true minimum width d can achieve the optimal rate, and whether the heavy dependence on input dimension d in the constants can be improved.
