## Problem

Existing synthetic tabular data generation methods are limited to narrow, fixed constraint types (e.g., differential privacy or fairness alone), preventing data owners from simultaneously imposing diverse, composable requirements such as logical constraints, statistical manipulations, and downstream model properties. CuTS addresses this gap by introducing the first framework for general, programmable customization of synthetic tabular data without significant utility loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242019/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CuTS takes a tabular dataset (with continuous features uniformly discretized into 32 bins and all features one-hot encoded) and a declarative CuTS program specifying constraints via commands like ENSURE (DP with ε, δ), ENFORCE (row constraints, implications, statistical equalities), and MINIMIZE (downstream bias or predictability). The generator is fed Gaussian noise of dimension 100 and produces synthetic rows in the same one-hot representation space; final samples are optionally filtered by rejection sampling to guarantee hard constraint satisfaction.

## Method

CuTS uses a two-phase training procedure: first, a 4-layer fully connected generator with residual connections and a per-feature gumbel-softmax output is pre-trained (non-privately or via an adapted iterative DP scheme) to match three-way marginals of the original data via total-variation loss. Second, it is fine-tuned by minimizing a composite loss that adds weighted differentiable regularizers automatically derived from the user's specifications. The key technical contribution is a set of novel differentiable relaxations: logical constraints are enforced via differentiable binary masks computed through matrix-vector products with constant indicator vectors; statistical specifications are evaluated by differentiable conditional marginals over filtered row subsets; and downstream specifications optimize a differentiable surrogate classifier trained in-loop on the generated sample. This allows arbitrary compositions of privacy, logical, statistical, and downstream constraints to be jointly satisfied while preserving high synthetic data quality.

## Results

On the Adult dataset, CuTS achieves 82.1% XGBoost accuracy with a demographic parity distance of 0.01 in the non-private setting, and 80.9% accuracy with 0.01 demographic parity under ε=1 differential privacy. This represents a 2.3% accuracy improvement over the state-of-the-art fair synthetic data method (TabFairGAN at 79.8%) while achieving 2× lower demographic parity distance. In the private setting, CuTS outperforms Prefair Greedy (80.2%, 0.04) and Prefair Optimal (75.7%, 0.03) on both accuracy and fairness. CuTS also demonstrates strong composability, maintaining 81.6% accuracy when simultaneously enforcing fairness, two statistical manipulations, and two logical implications.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CuTS outperforms all specialized fair synthetic data baselines on the Adult dataset: it beats TabFairGAN (79.8%, 0.02) by 2.3% accuracy and 2× lower demographic parity, and DECAF (66.8%, 0.08) by a wide margin on both metrics in the non-private setting. Under ε=1 DP, CuTS (80.9%, 0.01) surpasses Prefair Greedy (80.2%, 0.04) and Prefair Optimal (75.7%, 0.03) on both accuracy and fairness. For logical constraints, CuTS+FT+RS maintains ~85% accuracy at 100% constraint satisfaction, while AIM+SZ(ε=1) degrades severely on harder constraints (e.g., 67.6% on RC2 vs. CuTS's 83.4%). In unconstrained non-private generation, GReaT slightly edges CuTS on XGB accuracy (85.7% vs. 85.2% on Adult), but CuTS achieves ~7× lower TV distance on training marginals. In the private unconstrained setting, AIM is marginally stronger on some datasets (e.g., 84.1% vs. 83.5% on Adult), but CuTS is competitive and far more versatile. On the Health Heritage and Compas datasets, CuTS again achieves the best accuracy-fairness trade-off among all compared methods in both private and non-private settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

CuTS builds on the marginal-matching generative framework of GEM (Liu et al., 2021) and the iterative DP mechanism of AIM (McKenna et al., 2022), replacing their graphical model backbone with a neural generator trained via total-variation distance on marginals. It departs from prior work by introducing differentiable relaxations—binary masks computed via matrix-vector operations on one-hot encodings for logical constraints, and differentiable surrogate classifier training for downstream specifications—that convert declarative specifications into trainable regularizers. The DL2 primitives (Fischer et al., 2019) are repurposed to handle the arithmetic and logical composition of statistical expressions. Unlike prior methods that each address a single concern (DP, fairness, or logical constraints in isolation), CuTS unifies all of these into a composable program, positioning itself as a general framework rather than a specialized algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CuTS demonstrates that diverse customizations (privacy, fairness, logical structure, statistical manipulation, downstream behavior) can be stacked simultaneously with only a modest and stable quality cost (~3.4% accuracy for the first constraint, then near-zero marginal loss), which is a practically important result for data owners who face multiple regulatory and business requirements at once. Key limitations include the mandatory discretization of continuous features (32 uniform bins), the restriction to i.i.d. row-level constraints (no pairwise or relational constraints), and the use of only 3-way marginals without adaptive selection. Open questions include whether more sophisticated discretization, richer marginal workloads, or better generative backbones would further close the gap to unconstrained baselines, and whether the framework can extend to continuous features under DP without sacrificing differentiability.
