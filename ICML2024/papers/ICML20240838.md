## Problem

Pretrained Vision Transformers excel at large-scale pretraining due to their weak inductive bias, but this same property hinders their adaptation to visuo-motor control tasks, which require spatial locality and translation equivariance biases that convolutions naturally provide. Prior work has largely evaluated pretrained visual encoders in a frozen manner, leaving the question of how to effectively finetune ViTs for control—while preserving their pretrained knowledge and adding the missing control-centric biases—largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The system consumes 256×256 RGB images (center-cropped to 224×224) along with proprioceptive state for Adroit and MetaWorld tasks (proprioception is unavailable for DMC). The visual encoder's [CLS] token is concatenated with proprioception (where available) and fed into a 4-layer MLP policy that outputs a continuous robot action. Frame-stacking of 3 consecutive observations is used, with each frame encoded independently and fused via Flare before policy prediction.

## Method

CoIn is a lightweight add-on module (~3.1M parameters, 3.6% of ViT-B/16) that injects convolutional inductive biases into a pretrained ViT without modifying its architecture. A small CNN encoder (stem + three stride-2 3×3 convolutions) produces a multi-scale feature pyramid at H/8, H/16, and H/32 resolutions, each projected to the ViT's embedding dimension via 1×1 convolutions. These features are then integrated into the ViT via a deformable cross-attention layer where the ViT's patch embeddings act as queries and the CNN features serve as keys and values, with a residual connection back to the original patch tokens. The enriched tokens proceed through the unmodified ViT transformer blocks. The entire system is finetuned end-to-end via behavior cloning on 100 expert demonstrations (25 for MetaWorld) over 100 epochs, using standard CV finetuning strategies (weight decay, cosine LR, layer-wise LR decay) that the paper shows are critical to prevent the performance degradation typically observed when finetuning large ViTs on small control datasets.

## Results

Across 12 visuo-motor control tasks in three benchmarks (Adroit, MetaWorld, DMC), CoIn consistently improves mean success over finetuned ViT baselines: CLIP gains +11.3 points (59.6→70.9), MVP gains +1.7 (84.5→86.2), and VC-1 gains +3.9 (80.7→84.6). With CoIn, MVP reaches 86.2 mean success, surpassing the strongest ResNet-based baseline R3M (85.1). CoIn adds only 3.1M parameters (3.6% of ViT-B/16's 85.8M), and in a scaling study, ViT-B+CoIn (84.6) outperforms the larger ViT-L (83.4) with significantly fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoIn outperforms all three ViT finetuned baselines: CLIP+CoIn (70.9) beats CLIP Finetuned (59.6) by 11.3 points, MVP+CoIn (86.2) beats MVP Finetuned (84.5) by 1.7, and VC-1+CoIn (84.6) beats VC-1 Finetuned (80.7) by 3.9. Against ResNet-based methods, MVP+CoIn (86.2) surpasses R3M Finetuned (85.1) and VIP Finetuned (80.4), while VC-1+CoIn (84.6) is marginally below R3M (85.1). In adapter comparisons (Table 2), CoIn dominates: for CLIP, CoIn achieves 70.9 vs. AdaptFormer (59.4) and RoboAdapter (59.1–59.2), and for VC-1, CoIn reaches 84.6 vs. AdaptFormer (82.3) and RoboAdapter (82.5–83.0). Against ViT-Adapter (Appendix B, Table 7), CoIn achieves higher mean performance (84.6 vs. 80.8), lower MACs (19.06G vs. 26.07G), and faster inference (8.78ms vs. 14.50ms) with fewer trainable parameters (88.9M vs. 103.2M). The largest gains occur with CLIP, which lacks egocentric/control-centric pretraining, confirming that CoIn's injected convolutional biases are most impactful when the pretrained ViT lacks control-oriented features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) the growing use of large pretrained visual encoders (CLIP, MVP, VC-1, R3M, VIP) for robotic visuo-motor control, and (2) the integration of CNNs with pretrained ViTs in computer vision (ViT-Adapter, DPT, MIMDET). It departs from prior control work by specifically addressing the under-explored problem of *finetuning* ViTs for control rather than freezing them, and from ViT-Adapter by removing the Extractor module and repurposing only the Injector to inject spatial locality and translation equivariance biases into ViT patch embeddings for downstream control. The adapter-based comparisons (RoboAdapter, AdaptFormer) serve as ablations showing that the gains come from convolutional inductive bias rather than merely additional trainable parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, lightweight recipe (3.6% extra parameters) for making pretrained ViTs competitive with or superior to ResNet-based control encoders, which is timely as the field shifts toward ViT-based foundation models for robotics. The finding that standard CV finetuning strategies (weight decay, cosine LR, layer-wise LR decay) resolve the overfitting that plagues naive ViT finetuning for control is a useful practical contribution that prior work had not isolated. Key limitations include evaluation only in simulation with behavior cloning (no RL, no real-world robots), and the modest gains for already-egocentric models (MVP, VC-1) suggest the method's value is most pronounced for non-control-pretrained encoders like CLIP.
