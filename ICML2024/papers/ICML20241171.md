## Problem

The Robust Satisficing (RS) model was recently proposed as a practical alternative to Distributionally Robust Optimization (DRO), offering globalized distribution consideration and a more interpretable hyperparameter (tolerance rate ε vs. ambiguity radius r). However, no statistical guarantees—such as confidence intervals for the optimal loss or finite-sample generalization error bounds—had been established for the RS model, leaving its theoretical properties uncharacterized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241171/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N i.i.d. samples (u_i, y_i) drawn from a source distribution P* and a tolerance rate ε that sets the reference value τ_ε = (1+ε)·inf_x E_{P̂_N}[h(x,ξ)]. It produces a decision variable x̂_N, two-sided non-asymptotic confidence intervals for the optimal loss J*, and finite-sample generalization error bounds. A key structural property is that the confidence intervals are derived from the ERM problem rather than requiring explicit solution of a minimax optimization, and under distribution shift the bounds simply acquire an additional linear term in d_W(P*, P̃).

## Method

The paper establishes the statistical theory of the RS model by exploiting its globalized distribution structure and the type-1 Wasserstein distance. The core reformulation (Eq. 7) converts the intractable distributional constraint into an empirical expectation of a pointwise supremum, enabling a hierarchical solution: for each x, find the minimal fragility k_τ(x), then minimize over x. The key theoretical results follow from bounding d_W(P*, P̂_N) via exponential tail decay (Assumption 1) and Lipschitz continuity of the loss in ξ (Assumption 2), yielding two-sided confidence intervals with a remainder r_N that decays at rate N^{-min{1/m, 1/2}}. A fragility upper bound k_τ ≤ L (Lemma 1) further simplifies the intervals to depend only on L and τ_ε. All results extend to distribution shift by adding a linear multiple of d_W(P*, P̃), and under Lipschitz loss the paper shows an explicit hyperparameter correspondence r(ε) between RS and DRO, revealing that RS is less sensitive to tuning because r(ε) is concave and flattens near 1+ε ≈ 1.6.

## Results

In small-sample regimes (m_u=10), RS consistently outperforms the ERM baseline, with larger tolerance rates ε yielding better performance as the gap between empirical and sampling distributions widens. Under distribution shifts with DEGREE > 5, RS almost consistently outperforms ERM, while for minor shifts (DEGREE < 2) performance is comparable. In the hyperparameter sensitivity comparison, within a 15% relative error range around optimal hyperparameters, DRO's MSE spikes to 0.30 while RS maintains a stable MSE of 0.125, with optimal settings at r ≈ 0.215 for DRO and 1+ε ≈ 1.285 for RS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares RS against two baselines: Empirical Risk Minimization (ERM) and Distributionally Robust Optimization (DRO). Against ERM, RS wins in small-sample regimes where the empirical distribution poorly approximates the true distribution, and under substantial distribution shifts (DEGREE > 5), where RS's globalized distribution consideration provides stronger robustness. For minor distribution shifts (DEGREE < 2), RS is comparable to ERM, with slightly worse performance at larger ε values. Against DRO, RS achieves comparable minimum MSE but exhibits substantially lower sensitivity to hyperparameter tuning: within a 15% relative error range around the optimal hyperparameter, DRO's MSE surges to 0.30 while RS maintains 0.125. The paper also establishes that the RS tolerance rate ε maps to a DRO radius r via a concave function r(ε) that flattens near 1+ε ≈ 1.6, meaning RS tolerates larger variations in its hyperparameter for equivalent performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first statistical theory for the Robust Satisficing (RS) model introduced by Long et al. (2023), which was previously studied only through tractable reformulations and empirical comparisons. It positions RS as a natural alternative to Distributionally Robust Optimization (DRO), drawing on the Wasserstein-distance framework established by Esfahani & Kuhn (2015) and Blanchet et al. (2019), but exploits RS's globalized distribution constraint (as opposed to DRO's ambiguity set) to derive two-sided confidence intervals and generalization bounds without solving a minimax problem. The reformulation technique in Section 2.2 parallels the dual-representation approach used in DRO literature, but avoids the additional continuity and strong-duality assumptions that DRO requires.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a genuine gap by showing that RS's satisficing structure yields statistical guarantees that are strictly easier to derive than DRO's (two-sided vs. one-sided, no minimax solve), and that these guarantees extend naturally to distribution shifts with only a linear penalty in the shift distance. The practical finding that RS is substantially less sensitive to its tolerance-rate hyperparameter than DRO is to its radius (MSE spike to 0.30 vs. stable 0.125 within a 15% tuning range) has direct implications for deployment. Open questions include extending the analysis beyond Wasserstein distance (e.g., f-divergence), proving matching lower bounds, and addressing the curse of dimensionality inherent in the remainder rate N^{-1/max{m,2}}.
