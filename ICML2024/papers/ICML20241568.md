## Problem

Adaptive linesearch-free proximal gradient methods were previously established only under local Lipschitz continuity of the gradient, while the Hölder-smooth setting (ν < 1) typically required either ε-oracle approximations or backtracking linesearch procedures. This paper closes that gap by proving that a class of linesearch-free adaptive methods converges under mere local Hölder gradient continuity of unknown order, without any approximation or knowledge of the Hölder exponent.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a composite objective φ(x) = f(x) + g(x) where f is convex with locally Hölder continuous gradient of unknown order ν ∈ (0,1] and g is proper, lsc, convex with a computable proximal map. It requires only a starting point x^{-1}, two initial positive stepsizes γ_0 ≥ γ_{-1}, and a single parameter q ∈ [1,2]; no prior knowledge of ν or the Hölder modulus L_ν is needed. The output is a full sequence (x^k) converging to a minimizer with best-so-far cost rate O(1/K^ν), where the rate exponent adapts automatically to the problem's Hölder order.

## Method

The paper provides a unified convergence analysis of adaPG^{q,q/2}, a linesearch-free adaptive proximal gradient method whose stepsize update uses only local Barzilai-Borwein-type estimates ℓ_k and L_k computed from consecutive gradient differences. The key technical insight is factorizing the Euclidean stepsize as γ_k = λ_{k,ν}‖x^k − x^{k−1}‖^{1−ν}, which normalizes Hölder inequalities into Lipschitz-like bounds and reveals that the scaled stepsize λ_{k,ν} is lower-bounded precisely when the curvature-dependent term in the stepsize update is active. A Lyapunov function U_k^q(x⋆) combining distance-to-solution, step-length, and cost is shown to decrease monotonically, yielding full sequence convergence and the O(1/K^ν) rate. The analysis covers all q ∈ [1,2], thereby unifying and extending the convergence guarantees of several previously studied adaptive algorithms (Malitsky–Mishchenko, Latafat et al.) from the Lipschitz to the general Hölder regime.

## Results

The paper evaluates adaPG^{q,q/2} on four convex optimization tasks (p-norm Lasso, Hölder-smooth SVMs, p-norm regularized logistic regression, and mixture p-norm regression) using the number of calls to the design matrix A and A^T as the computational cost metric. Results are presented as convergence plots (Figures 1–4) rather than a single numerical table. Across all four tasks, adaPG^{q,q/2} consistently achieves lower objective values per matrix-vector product call than NUPG, and in most cases also outperforms the accelerated methods F-NUPG and AC-FGM. The theoretical sublinear rate established is O(1/K^ν), matching the standard convex Hölder-smooth rate of Bredies (2008).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares adaPG^{q,q/2} against three baselines: NUPG (Nesterov, 2015), its fast variant F-NUPG (Nesterov, 2015), and AC-FGM (Li & Lan, 2023). Against NUPG, adaPG^{q,q/2} wins consistently across all four experimental tasks (p-norm Lasso, Hölder-smooth SVMs, logistic regression, and mixture p-norm regression) in terms of calls to A and A^T, with the advantage being particularly pronounced in the locally Hölder setting (mixture p-norm regression). Against the accelerated baselines F-NUPG and AC-FGM, adaPG^{q,q/2} also performs consistently better in the p-norm Lasso and Hölder-smooth SVM experiments, and in many cases outperforms them in the logistic regression and mixture regression tasks as well. The authors attribute this advantage to adaPG's ability to exploit hidden Hölder growth structure that accelerated methods cannot leverage, and note that adaPG requires only 2 calls to A/A^T per iteration versus 1+#_LS for NUPG and 3+#_LS for F-NUPG, giving it a per-iteration cost advantage in addition to its convergence advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis of the adaPG^{q,r} family of linesearch-free adaptive proximal gradient methods (Malitsky & Mishchenko, 2020; Latafat et al., 2023a,b; Malitsky & Mishchenko, 2023) from the local Lipschitz regime (ν=1) to the general local Hölder continuity setting (ν∈(0,1]). It deliberately bridges two previously separate paradigms: the exact majorization-minimization approach of Bredies (2008) that requires knowledge of the Hölder exponent, and Nesterov's universal gradient methods (NUPG) that avoid this knowledge but introduce ε-approximation and linesearch. The key technical departure is factorizing the Euclidean stepsize as γ_k = λ_{k,ν}‖x^k − x^{k−1}‖^{1−ν}, which normalizes Hölder inequalities into Lipschitz-like ones and enables convergence proofs without a global stepsize lower bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the first convergence guarantee for linesearch-free adaptive proximal gradient methods under mere local Hölder gradient continuity of unknown order, covering all continuously differentiable semi-algebraic functions. This is practically significant because many real-world objectives (p-norm losses, mixture models, robust regression) are Hölder but not Lipschitz smooth, and practitioners previously had to resort to ε-oracles or backtracking. Open questions include whether the O(1/K^ν) rate can be tightened, whether the analysis extends to nonconvex or stochastic settings, and whether the method can handle the ν=0 (merely convex, non-differentiable) case suggested by some auxiliary results.
