## Problem

Existing Federated Continual Learning (FCL) methods focus on avoiding interference between tasks to prevent catastrophic forgetting, but overlook the potential for positive knowledge transfer across tasks learned by different clients at different time intervals (dual spatial-temporal transfer). Additionally, prompt-based FCL methods transmit entire task-specific prompt pools between server and clients, incurring growing communication overhead and raising privacy concerns as the number of tasks increases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241653/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system operates in an asynchronous federated setting where C clients sequentially learn tasks at their own pace on RGB images (ImageNet-R: 200 classes, 24k train/6k test; DomainNet: 345 classes across 6 domains). Each task contains a random subset of classes (20 for ImageNet-R, 35 for DomainNet) with only a fraction of samples per class. The frozen ViT-B/16 encoder processes images, and the method outputs class labels via a task-specific classification head, with prompts (M=10, L=8, D=768) as the only trainable parameters per task.

## Method

Powder introduces a two-step prompt aggregation mechanism for selective dual knowledge transfer: the first step multiplies the global prompt pool by a dual task correlation matrix (estimated from cosine similarity of prompt selection weights) to blend relevant knowledge across tasks, and the second step selects only top-k most relevant prompts for transmission to each client, reducing communication by 20–40%. A dual distillation loss modulates per-class distillation strength using a class correlation matrix and masks the true-label logits (following FedNTD), so that highly correlated classes retain transferred knowledge while weakly correlated classes remain free to learn from local data. The task and class correlation matrices are updated only when new tasks appear, keeping communication overhead minimal in steady-state operation.

## Results

On ImageNet-R (C=5, r=15), Powder achieves 84.08% Average Incremental Accuracy (AIA), the highest among all methods, and is the only method with positive Combined Transfer (CT = +6.04%), while the best baseline (GLFC-ViT) reaches 75.21% AIA with CT = −5.11%. On DomainNet, Powder attains 77.28% AIA and CT = +1.40%, again the sole method with positive transfer. Powder also reduces communication overhead to 493 MB (vs. 687–816 MB for prompt-based baselines) and storage to 2.64 MB (vs. 3.96–11.43 MB), while achieving the fastest training (262.65 s) and inference (3.73 s) per task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Powder outperforms all eight baselines on both AIA and CT across both datasets. On ImageNet-R, Powder's 84.08% AIA exceeds the strongest non-prompt baseline GLFC-ViT (75.21%) by ~9 points and the strongest prompt-based baseline Fed-CODAP (75.14%) by ~9 points. The most striking gap is in Combined Transfer: Powder achieves +6.04% CT while every baseline is negative (ranging from −1.18% for Fed-CODAP to −29.60% for CFeD-ViT), confirming that Powder is the first FCL method to realize positive dual knowledge transfer. On DomainNet, Powder leads by ~4.4 points in AIA over Fed-CODAP (72.84%) and ~6.9 points over GLFC-ViT (70.34%), with CT of +1.40% versus −0.15% for Fed-CODAP. In terms of efficiency, Powder reduces communication overhead by 20–40% (493 MB vs. 687–816 MB) and storage by up to 77% (2.64 MB vs. 11.43 MB for Fed-CODAP/Fed-CPrompt) while also being the fastest in both training (262.65 s vs. 294–459 s) and inference (3.73 s vs. 5.69–6.56 s). Non-prompt methods (FedWEIT, CFeD, GLFC, Fedspace) suffer substantially higher forgetting (FM up to 18%) and large negative backward transfer, whereas prompt-based baselines reduce forgetting but still fail to achieve positive forward or combined transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

Powder builds directly on CODAPrompt's decomposed attention-based prompt generation and the FedAvg federated aggregation paradigm, but departs from prior prompt-based FCL methods (FedCPrompt, HePCo) that focus exclusively on avoiding inter-task interference. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a dual task-correlation matrix that enables *selective* knowledge transfer across both spatial (cross-client) and temporal (forward/backward) dimensions, rather than treating all tasks as equally relevant or irrelevant. The dual distillation loss further extends standard knowledge distillation by modulating per-class distillation strength via a class-correlation matrix, addressing the tension between retaining transferred knowledge and preserving learning plasticity for new classes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Powder is the first FCL method to achieve consistently positive combined transfer, demonstrating that selective, correlation-guided prompt aggregation can turn knowledge transfer from a net liability into a net asset in federated settings. The 20–40% communication reduction alongside 9–12% accuracy gains makes it practically relevant for resource-constrained edge devices. Open questions include whether the correlation estimation (based on prompt selection weights) remains reliable as the number of tasks grows very large, whether the approach generalizes beyond ViT-based vision tasks to NLP or multimodal settings, and how robust the method is under highly non-iid client data distributions beyond the controlled overlap levels tested.
