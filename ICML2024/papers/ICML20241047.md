## Problem

While structural features of triples are known to be critical for knowledge graph link prediction, the exact k-hop neighborhood intersection among head, relation, and tail is prohibitively expensive to compute for KGs with millions of triples. The paper addresses how to efficiently estimate these intersection features at scale without sacrificing predictive accuracy, bridging the gap between structural informativeness and computational feasibility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241047/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method treats both entities and relations as vertices in a unified graph, so k-hop neighborhoods of a triple (h, r, t) include both entity and relation vertices reachable within k edges. For each candidate triple, the method produces a vector of k³+3k intersection features (estimated exact-hop intersection cardinalities plus per-vertex exclusive neighborhood sizes), which is fed into a single fully-connected layer to yield a scalar validity score used for ranking in standard filtered MRR/Hit@k link prediction evaluation.

## Method

The core insight is decomposing the triple intersection cardinality |N_k(h) ∩ N_k(r) ∩ N_k(t)| into a product of 3-way Jaccard similarity and 3-way union cardinality, then estimating each factor independently. For 3-way Jaccard, the paper introduces an asymmetric MinHash transform (φ concatenating two MinHash values, ψ duplicating one) that provably estimates the 3-way Jaccard without the overflow or collision issues of prior bucketing schemes. For 3-way union cardinality, HyperLogLog sketches of each vertex's k-hop neighborhood are merged via element-wise max. Multi-hop exact intersection features are then recovered inductively from cumulative estimates, and a single fully-connected network trained with a margin-based loss maps the resulting feature vector to a triple validity score, outperforming both embedding-based and GNN-based baselines while training in under 3 hours.

## Results

On the three large-scale heterogeneous KGs, Intersection Features achieves MRR of 0.617 (YAGO3-10), 0.789 (NELL-995), and 0.781 (WN18RR), consistently outperforming all 15 baselines. The margin is most pronounced on WN18RR, where the method leads the runner-up (NCRL, MRR 0.67) by 0.111. On the smaller, more homogeneous FB15K237, the method reaches MRR 0.625, surpassing the best GNN baseline KBGAT (0.526) by 0.099. Training time is under 3 hours on all five datasets, comparable to the fastest embedding method (TransE) and far below GNN baselines (e.g., KRACL at 136 hours on YAGO3-10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method decisively outperforms all embedding-based baselines (TransE, TransH, TransR, DistMult, ComplEx, ComplEx-N3, RotatE) across all three large-scale datasets, with the largest gap on NELL-995 where it leads ComplEx (MRR 0.694) by 0.095. Against GNN-based baselines, it surpasses KBGAT (0.550 on NELL-995), CompGCN (0.412), SE-GNN (0.214), and KRACL (0.445) by wide margins. On WN18RR, it beats the strongest recent method NCRL (MRR 0.67) by 0.111 and HousE (0.511) by 0.270. On the smaller FB15K237, it leads KBGAT (0.526) by 0.099 MRR. However, on FB15K the method (MRR 0.699) underperforms ComplEx-N3 (0.860), HousE (0.811), and KBGAT (0.800), which the authors attribute to FB15K's test set containing many inverse relations that those methods explicitly exploit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes two classic randomized sketching primitives—MinHash (Broder, 1997) and HyperLogLog (Flajolet et al., 2007; Heule et al., 2013)—for a new purpose in knowledge graph link prediction: estimating the 3-way intersection of k-hop neighborhoods of a triple's head, relation, and tail. The key theoretical contribution is an asymmetric transform (Definition 4.3, Theorem 4.4) that extends MinHash from pairwise to 3-way Jaccard similarity, combined with HyperLogLog's mergeability for 3-set union cardinality. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant paradigm of learning dense entity/relation embeddings (TransE family) or performing GNN message passing (KBGAT, CompGCN), instead arguing that explicit structural intersection features fed into a single fully-connected layer suffice for competitive or superior link prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for large-scale KG deployment: training completes in under 3 hours versus 54–136+ hours for GNN baselines, and the pre-computation of all MinHash/HyperLogLog sketches takes under 0.25 seconds, making the approach highly scalable. However, the method's advantage is not uniform—on FB15K it trails ComplEx-N3 and RotatE, and the semi-inductive setting reveals sensitivity to the test-triple-to-relation ratio. Open questions include whether the intersection features capture all necessary relational semantics (e.g., inverse relations, compositionality), whether the approach extends to inductive or temporal KGs, and whether the non-monotonic MinHash ablation (256 functions underperforming 128) signals a fundamental estimation-quality ceiling.
