## Problem

Single domain generalization aims to learn a model robust to unseen domains from only one training domain, but existing augmentation-based methods (adversarial perturbation, style enhancement) focus solely on generating diverse pseudo-domains while ignoring the underlying coherence between augmented and source domains. This neglect of inter-domain alignment leads to inferior generalization, particularly in in-the-wild scenarios where test styles are truly unseen.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image from one source domain (32×32 for Digits/CIFAR-10-C, 224×224 for PACS, 640×192 for KITTI depth estimation) and produces either a class label or a depth map. During inference, the input is first passed through the destylization module (AdaIN layer at a NAS-determined position) to project it into a style-invariant latent domain before the task head makes its prediction, making the framework label-format agnostic and applicable to both classification and regression.

## Method

StyDeSty couples a stylization module G (encoder-affine-decoder blocks with randomly sampled weights that generate diverse stylized versions of source images) with a destylization module F (AdaIN layer that unifies channel-wise statistics of all features into a single latent distribution), trained adversarially in a min-max game where G maximizes task loss while F minimizes it plus an alignment loss. The alignment loss combines L2 feature distance with a perceptual term measured in the task head's last hidden layer, and the task head is frozen during F's update to prevent it from weakening the alignment signal. A Gumbel-Softmax-based NAS strategy searches over candidate positions in the backbone to determine where to insert the AdaIN layer, balancing alignment ease against preservation of discriminative information. Training proceeds in two phases: a NAS stage co-optimizing the backbone and position logits, followed by a formal stage where F, H, and G are updated alternately with a semantic consistency constraint (MMD on a frozen VGG19 or the task head itself) preventing G from destroying image semantics.

## Results

On the PACS dataset (ResNet-18), StyDeSty achieves 71.35% average accuracy across all four training domains, outperforming the best baseline L2D (64.50%) by 6.85% overall and by 13.44% when trained on the Photo domain (63.25% vs. 49.06%). On Digits (LeNet-5), it reaches 78.82% average accuracy, surpassing L2D (74.46%) by 4.36%. On CIFAR-10-C at the severest corruption level (level 5), StyDeSty yields a 5.65% average improvement over the best competing method. For monocular depth estimation on KITTI, it achieves the best scores on all metrics (δ<1.25: 0.739, Abs Rel: 0.197, RMSE: 6.684).

## Baselines & Comparisons

StyDeSty consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and training domain tested. On PACS, the largest gap is against L2D (the strongest style-augmentation baseline): +14.19% when trained on Photo (63.25% vs. 49.06%) and +8.82% when trained on Sketch (62.22% vs. 53.40%), while against adversarial-augmentation methods like ADA and ME-ADA the gap is even wider (e.g., +18.13% on Photo→Cartoon). On Digits, StyDeSty beats L2D by 4.36% on average and M-ADA by 19.33%. On CIFAR-10-C at corruption level 5, it surpasses L2D by up to 8.67% (digital corruption). For depth estimation on KITTI, it improves over v-L2D by 0.014 on δ<1.25 and 0.011 on Abs Rel. The paper also demonstrates plug-and-play complementarity: adding the destylization layer to ProRandConv (Choi et al., 2023) lifts its PACS average from 68.88% to 73.44%, exceeding StyDeSty's own default 71.35%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on L2D's (Wang et al., 2021b) style complement module for the stylization component and on AdaIN (Huang & Belongie, 2017) for the destylization layer, but departs from the broader single DG literature by introducing an explicit adversarial min-max game between stylization and destylization rather than treating augmentation as a one-way data expansion step. Prior methods (ADA, M-ADA, MixStyle, L2D) focus on generating diverse pseudo-domains but largely ignore the coherence between augmented and source features; StyDeSty addresses this gap by projecting both into a shared latent domain via AdaIN and enforcing content consistency through a task-perceptual alignment loss. The NAS-based selection of the destylization position is a further departure, as no prior single DG method treats the insertion point of a normalization layer as a searchable architectural decision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 13.44% improvement on PACS (photo→sketch) and the plug-and-play results showing that the destylization layer can be grafted onto other single DG methods (Lv et al., Chen et al., Choi et al.) suggest a broadly applicable component rather than a monolithic framework. The versatility across classification and regression (depth estimation on KITTI) without requiring categorical labels broadens the practical scope. Open questions include whether the NAS strategy scales to backbones with more than ~10 candidate positions (the authors note convergence difficulties), whether multiple destylization layers could further improve performance, and whether the adversarial min-max training is necessary or whether a simpler joint objective could achieve similar alignment.
