## Problem

Tensor train representations, particularly Quantized Tensor Trains (QTTs), offer superior compression for visual data compared to CP, Tucker, and VM decompositions, but existing optimization strategies (TensoRF, TT-NF) get stuck in local minima and fail to reach the theoretical compression limit of the TT format. Additionally, these methods struggle with noisy inputs and incomplete data, limiting their practical utility for tasks like novel view synthesis and 3D reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sampled batches of spatial indices (not full tensors), making it applicable to settings where the complete signal is unavailable (e.g., NeRF ray sampling, missing pixels, noisy observations). For 2D/3D fitting, inputs are images up to 16k resolution or voxel grids up to 1024³; for novel view synthesis, multi-view images at 800×800 (Blender/NSVF) or 1920×1080 (TanksTemples) are used. Outputs are compressed QTT representations (two QTTs for NeRF: one for volume density σ and one for view-dependent color features), from which continuous values are retrieved via trilinear interpolation in QTT-index space.

## Method

PuTT learns QTT representations in a coarse-to-fine manner: training begins at a low resolution (e.g., 128² for a 16k image) and progressively upsamples the QTT using a fixed-bond-dimension-3 prolongation MPO that performs linear interpolation directly in the QTT format, analogous to the Haar wavelet transform. After each upsampling step, TT-SVD rank truncation keeps bond dimensions below a maximum threshold R_max, preventing exponential rank growth. At each resolution level, the QTT is refined via backpropagation on MSE loss over randomly sampled index batches, with a trapezoid rank structure and exponential learning-rate decay between upsampling steps. For novel view synthesis, two QTTs (T_σ and T_c) are jointly optimized through differentiable volume rendering, with the color QTT using a QTT-Block structure to handle the additional feature dimension. The key insight is that the multi-resolution QTT hierarchy—where each core corresponds to a spatial scale—naturally aligns with the coarse-to-fine upsampling, enabling stable optimization that avoids the local minima plaguing single-resolution QTT training.

## Results

On novel view synthesis, PuTT-280-M (12 MB) achieves 31.95 PSNR / 0.957 SSIM on Blender, 36.04 PSNR / 0.977 SSIM on NSVF, and 28.15 PSNR / 0.905 SSIM on TanksTemples, outperforming the same-size TensoRF VM-48-M (31.62 / 35.44 / 28.03 PSNR). At the smaller 7 MB budget, PuTT-200-S (6.7 MB) reaches 35.58 PSNR on NSVF, surpassing TensoRF CP-384-S (34.48 PSNR) by over 1 PSNR. For 2D image compression at 16k resolution, PuTT exceeds all tensor baselines by more than 2.5 PSNR and 0.1 SSIM; for 3D fitting at 1024³, PuTT leads CP and Tucker by over 1 PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PuTT consistently outperforms TensoRF (CP and VM decompositions) at matched model sizes: at 12 MB, PuTT-280-M beats TensoRF VM-48-M by 0.33 PSNR on Blender, 0.60 PSNR on NSVF, and 0.12 PSNR on TanksTemples; at ~7 MB, PuTT-200-S surpasses TensoRF CP-384-S by 1.10 PSNR on NSVF and 0.40 PSNR on TanksTemples. Against TT-NF (the prior tensor-train method), PuTT-200-S (6.7 MB) exceeds TT-NF-S (7.9 MB) by 0.57 PSNR on Blender while using fewer parameters. In 2D and 3D compression, PuTT significantly outperforms CP, Tucker, and VM decompositions at high resolutions (16k images, 1024³ volumes), with the gap growing as resolution increases, confirming the logarithmic scaling advantage of QTT over the linear/quadratic scaling of the other formats. PuTT also outperforms the deterministic TT-SVD baseline in PSNR across all 2D compression settings, demonstrating that the learned coarse-to-fine approach can match or exceed the analytical decomposition. At large model sizes (>60 MB), PuTT-600-L is competitive with TensoRF VM-192-L and Instant-NGP, though the advantage narrows, as the paper notes QTT benefits are most pronounced under high compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

PuTT sits at the intersection of tensor network compression (QTT from Oseledets 2009, TT-SVD from Oseledets 2011) and neural radiance field representations (TensoRF's CP/VM decomposition, TT-NF's tensor train fields). It departs from TensoRF by replacing CP/VM with the more expressive QTT format, and from TT-NF by introducing a coarse-to-fine optimization strategy inspired by multigrid PDE solvers (Lubasch et al., 2018) that uses a fixed-bond-dimension prolongation MPO to upsample QTTs. The key conceptual departure is treating the QTT hierarchy levels as a natural multi-resolution structure where each level is learned before being prolonged to the next, rather than optimizing the full-resolution tensor train directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PuTT addresses a practical bottleneck that has limited tensor train adoption in vision: QTT optimization gets stuck in local minima, making the theoretically superior format underperform CP/Tucker in practice. The coarse-to-fine MPO upsampling resolves this, enabling QTT's logarithmic scaling with resolution to actually materialize in learned representations. Limitations include converging performance with CP/Tucker at lower resolutions (<512), incompatibility with TensoRF's shrinkage mechanism, and modest (1-2 PSNR) gains in novel view synthesis at small model sizes. Open questions include whether the approach scales to large-scale or dynamic NeRFs, and whether combining QTT with hashing (as in Instant-NGP) could further improve efficiency.
