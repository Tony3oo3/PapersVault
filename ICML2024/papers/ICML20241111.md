## Problem

In multi-class unsupervised anomaly detection, reconstruction-based and conditional diffusion models suffer from the 'identical shortcut' problem: abnormal information in the input condition biases the generative output toward an anomalous distribution, causing anomalies to be faithfully reconstructed. Existing diffusion-based methods (DiAD, LafitE) use information-rich conditions that still retain anomaly details, so the shortcut persists. This paper asks whether the problem can be eliminated at its source by feeding only vague, anomaly-free information into the generative process.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RGB image, extracts a 272-channel feature map at 32×32 resolution via a frozen EfficientNet-b4 (stages 1–4 concatenated), and operates entirely in this feature space. It produces a pixel-level anomaly score map (L2 reconstruction difference, bilinearly upsampled to image size), an image-level anomaly score (max of average-pooled score map), and a reconstructed normal sample in feature space. A single unified model handles all classes simultaneously without per-class fine-tuning.

## Method

VPDM has two jointly trained components. First, the VPOT module applies a 4× downsampler (acting as a low-pass filter) to EfficientNet features to retain only fundamental shape/color information, then learns K=50 vague prototypes via entropic Optimal Transport (Sinkhorn) so that the transport plan T maps the downsampled feature distribution to the prototype distribution; the resulting vague condition ŷ is upsampled back to full feature resolution. Second, a modified DDPM incorporates ŷ into both the forward process (the mean becomes an interpolation between x^{t-1} and ŷ, and the endpoint shifts from N(0,I) to N(ŷ,I)) and the reverse process (a new conditional posterior q(x^{t-1}|x^0, x^t, ŷ) with three-term mean), so generation starts from the vague prototype and progressively adds details rather than denoising from pure noise. The unified loss L = L_ELBO + L_OT simultaneously trains the denoising network and the prototypes, with the OT term encouraging prototypes to capture diverse multi-class normal patterns.

## Results

On MVTec-AD (multi-class setting), VPDM achieves a mean AUROC of 98.4% for image-level detection and 97.8% for pixel-level localization, outperforming the strongest baselines HVQ-Trans (98.0/97.3) and DiAD (97.2/96.8). On VisA, VPDM reaches 94.2% detection and 98.9% localization AUROC, surpassing HVQ-Trans (93.2/98.7) and DiAD (86.8/96.0). On MPDD, VPDM attains 96.9% detection and 98.6% localization AUROC, exceeding HVQ-Trans (88.9/96.7) and UniAD (86.2/95.2) by substantial margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VPDM consistently outperforms all 13 baselines across all three datasets in the multi-class setting. On MVTec-AD, VPDM surpasses the strongest transformer-based baseline HVQ-Trans by 0.4% detection and 0.5% localization AUROC, and the diffusion-based DiAD by 1.2% detection and 1.0% localization. The gap widens substantially on VisA, where VPDM leads HVQ-Trans by 1.0% detection and DiAD by 7.4% detection (94.2 vs. 86.8), indicating that the vague-prototype strategy is particularly effective for complex multi-instance scenes. On MPDD, the advantage is most pronounced: VPDM exceeds HVQ-Trans by 8.0% detection (96.9 vs. 88.9) and UniAD by 10.7% detection (96.9 vs. 86.2), while also achieving the best localization (98.6 vs. 96.7 for HVQ-Trans). Notably, VPDM achieves near-perfect detection (100%) on several MVTec categories (Bottle, Carpet, Leather, Tile) and VisA's PCB4, while maintaining competitive localization scores, demonstrating robustness across both object and texture anomaly types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) by incorporating a vague condition into both the forward process mean and the terminal distribution, and combines it with optimal transport (Peyré et al., 2019) for prototype learning. It departs from prior diffusion-based anomaly detection methods (DiAD, LafitE) that inject information-rich conditions into the reverse process, arguing that such conditions still carry anomaly information and perpetuate the 'identical shortcut' problem. The key conceptual departure is filtering anomaly information at the input layer via low-pass filtering and prototype-based reconstruction (VPOT), rather than attempting to suppress it downstream in the generative network, drawing an analogy to human memory retrieval (Ratcliff, 1978).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and well-recognized failure mode in reconstruction-based anomaly detection (the 'identical shortcut') with a principled solution that removes anomaly information before it enters the generative model, which is more robust than post-hoc filtering strategies. The ablation showing a 22% performance drop when replacing the proposed conditional diffusion with standard DDPM highlights that vague conditions fundamentally change the generation task, requiring the forward process to be co-designed with the condition. Open questions include whether the specific low-pass filtering strategy is optimal, whether the approach generalizes beyond RGB industrial/medical images, and whether the 1000-step diffusion schedule can be reduced without sacrificing the quality gains from the vague-condition design.
