## Problem

Existing federated learning methods that apply Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) to local training find flatter minima for local objectives, but the aggregated global model does not lie on flat minima of the global objective—a gap the authors formalize as 'flatness discrepancy.' This discrepancy worsens as data heterogeneity across clients increases, causing significant performance degradation in non-IID settings that prior flatness-searching FL methods fail to address.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241078/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of 100 clients holds 500 CIFAR-10/100 images (32×32 RGB) with labels, distributed via Dirichlet(α) to control heterogeneity. Beyond the standard local data and current global model, FedGF additionally requires the previous round's global model to compute a pseudo-gradient direction (Δ^r = w^{r-1} − w^r) that approximates the global gradient for constructing the global perturbation. The server broadcasts this perturbation direction to clients each round, adding one extra download compared to FedAvg but no extra upload.

## Method

FedGF augments standard SAM-based local training by interpolating between a local perturbation (standard SAM direction) and a global perturbation (derived from the difference between consecutive global models, approximating ∇F(w^r)). The interpolated perturbed model w̃_{i,k,c} = c·w̃^r + (1−c)·w̃_{i,k} is where the local gradient is evaluated for the update. The interpolation coefficient c is adaptively set via a thresholded divergence metric D^r measuring how far local models deviate from the global model, averaged over recent W rounds—pushing c toward 1 under strong non-IID heterogeneity and toward 0 under near-IID conditions. Aggregation uses a global learning rate η_g (from Adaptive Federated Optimization) rather than simple averaging. Theoretically, larger c suppresses the σ_g² and σ_l² heterogeneity/variance terms in the convergence bound, and the flatness discrepancy Δ_F is shown to decrease as rounds progress, explaining both faster convergence and flatter global minima in non-IID regimes.

## Results

On the most challenging non-IID setting (Dirichlet α=0, 5 participating clients), FedGF achieves 78.41% test accuracy on CIFAR-10 and 45.37% on CIFAR-100, yielding gains of +5.09% and +10.02% over the strongest baselines (FedASAM and FedSMOO, respectively). In the IID setting (α=10), FedGF reaches 84.71% on CIFAR-10 and 54.16% on CIFAR-100, matching or slightly exceeding prior methods. FedGF also converges significantly faster, requiring only 75×10² model transmissions to reach FedAvg's final accuracy on CIFAR-10 (α=0) versus 180×10² for FedSMOO (2.40× fewer).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedGF substantially outperforms all baselines in the non-IID (α=0) regime: it beats FedASAM (73.32%) by +5.09% on CIFAR-10 and FedSMOO (35.35%) by +10.02% on CIFAR-100 with 5 clients. Against the flatness-searching baselines specifically, FedGF surpasses FedSAM by +10.30% (CIFAR-10) and +15.94% (CIFAR-100), MoFedSAM by +5.31% and +16.35%, and FedSMOO by +9.59% and +10.02%. Regularization-based methods (SCAFFOLD, FedDyn) fail entirely on CIFAR-100 α=0, while FedGAMMA is unstable across most non-IID settings. In the IID (α=10) regime, FedGF achieves the best CIFAR-10 accuracy (84.71%) but is marginally below FedASAM on CIFAR-100 (54.16% vs. 54.60%), confirming that the global-perturbation interpolation is most beneficial under strong heterogeneity. FedGF also demonstrates superior robustness to limited client participation, maintaining stable performance where MoFedSAM drops from 41.26% to 29.02% (CIFAR-100, α=0) as clients decrease from 20 to 5.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of SAM-based FL methods (FedSAM, MoFedSAM, FedSMOO, FedGAMMA) that apply sharpness-aware minimization to local training, but departs from them by identifying a fundamental gap: local flatness does not transfer to global flatness under heterogeneity. It builds on FedAvg's round-based architecture and SAM's min-max formulation, but introduces the novel concept of 'flatness discrepancy' and addresses it through interpolated perturbation between local and global views. Unlike regularization-based approaches (SCAFFOLD, FedProx, FedDyn) that constrain local model divergence, FedGF operates purely in the optimization landscape geometry, making it orthogonal to those methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The flatness discrepancy framework provides a useful diagnostic for understanding why flatness-based FL methods degrade under heterogeneity, and the adaptive interpolation coefficient offers a simple, communication-efficient mechanism (only 3 transmissions vs. 4 for FedSMOO) to close the gap. However, the experimental validation is limited to CIFAR-10/100 with LeNet/ResNet-18 architectures, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger models, other datasets, or non-vision tasks. The approximation error ε in the global perturbation direction and the reliance on a threshold-based heuristic for c also raise questions about robustness in more complex deployment scenarios.
