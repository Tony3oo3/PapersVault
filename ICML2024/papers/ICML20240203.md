## Problem

LLM autoregressive decoding is memory-bandwidth-bound: each step must transfer the full model from HBM to accelerator cache to produce a single token, severely underutilizing compute. Speculative decoding reduces the number of steps but requires a separate draft model that is costly to train, hard to align with the target model's distribution, and difficult to serve in distributed environments. MEDUSA eliminates the draft model entirely by attaching lightweight prediction heads directly to the backbone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240203/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MEDUSA takes a standard text prompt and produces generated text tokens, operating primarily at batch size 1 (the local-hosting use case). During inference, the backbone LLM processes the prompt once per decoding step, and K additional heads each emit a top-s_k set of token predictions for their designated future position. These predictions are assembled into a tree of candidate continuations (via Cartesian product), verified in a single forward pass using a custom attention mask, and the longest accepted prefix is appended to the output. The output distribution matches the original model under rejection sampling, or approximates it under the proposed typical acceptance scheme.

## Method

MEDUSA appends K single-layer feed-forward heads (SiLU activation + residual connection, initialized from the original LM head) to the backbone's last hidden states, where the k-th head predicts the token at position t+k+1. A tree-structured attention mask lets the model verify all candidate continuations in one forward pass without expanding the batch size, with positional indices adjusted to the tree topology. Two training levels are provided: MEDUSA-1 freezes the backbone and trains only the heads with weighted cross-entropy loss (λ_k = 0.8^k), while MEDUSA-2 jointly trains heads and backbone using a combined loss, differential learning rates, and a two-stage warmup to preserve next-token prediction quality. A typical acceptance scheme replaces rejection sampling, using an entropy-dependent probability threshold to accept plausible candidates and further boost speedup. When no aligned training data exists, a self-distillation pipeline generates data from the model itself, and LoRA-based distillation (KL divergence against the frozen adapter-off model) prevents quality degradation during MEDUSA-2 training.

## Results

MEDUSA-2 achieves a 2.83× wall-time speedup on Vicuna-7B and Vicuna-13B, and 2.35–2.66× on Vicuna-33B and Zephyr-7B (trained via self-distillation), while preserving generation quality within ±0.14 MT-Bench score of the original models. MEDUSA-1 (frozen backbone) achieves 2.18× on Vicuna-7B and 2.33× on Vicuna-13B with no quality degradation. The acceleration rate (tokens decoded per step) reaches 3.0–3.5, with a per-step overhead of only 1.18–1.27× relative to vanilla decoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MEDUSA-2 against speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2023) using open-source draft models (Llama-68M/160M, Tiny-Vicuna-1B). MEDUSA-2 consistently outperforms speculative decoding by a wide margin: 2.83× vs. 1.47× on Vicuna-7B, 2.83× vs. 1.56× on Vicuna-13B, and 2.35× vs. 1.60× on Vicuna-33B. The advantage is most pronounced on smaller models where the draft model's capacity is most limiting. In terms of quality, MEDUSA-2 matches or slightly exceeds the original model's MT-Bench score (within ±0.14), while speculative decoding was not evaluated on quality in this paper. The key practical advantage is that MEDUSA eliminates the need for a separate draft model, its pre-training, and the complexity of serving two models in a distributed system.

## Relation to Prior Work

MEDUSA repositions speculative decoding by replacing the separate draft model with lightweight single-layer FFN heads appended to the backbone's last hidden states, drawing on the parallel decoding idea from Stern et al. (2018) for machine translation. The tree-attention mechanism for concurrent candidate verification borrows graph-structure embedding concepts from GNNs (Ying et al., 2021) and is developed in parallel with SpecInfer (Miao et al., 2023) and staged speculative decoding (Spector & Re, 2023), but MEDUSA's top-down tree construction from head predictions avoids the bottom-up merging complexity. The typical acceptance scheme adapts the entropy-based truncation threshold from Hewitt et al. (2022), and the self-distillation pipeline repurposes LoRA adapters to eliminate the need for a separate teacher model during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MEDUSA provides a drop-in, parameter-efficient acceleration method that achieves 2.3–2.8× speedup without a draft model, making it far easier to integrate into existing serving stacks (now supported in TensorRT and Huggingface TGI) and trainable on a single consumer GPU via QLoRA. Key open questions include generalization beyond batch size 1 (where the compute-bound transition of linear layers limits gains), optimal tree construction for very long sequences where attention overhead dominates, and whether the joint-training recipe (MEDUSA-2) can be applied during pretraining rather than post-hoc fine-tuning. The paper's focus on single-request latency leaves the throughput-oriented serving regime largely unexplored.
