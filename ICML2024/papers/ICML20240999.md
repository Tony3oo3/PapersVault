## Problem

Semantic image synthesis models are trained on clean benchmark labels but must handle noisy user inputs at inference time (jagged edges, incomplete annotations, random pixel errors), creating a train-test distribution gap. In diffusion-based SIS, this gap is particularly harmful because the erroneous condition is fed at every one of T reverse timesteps, compounding the conditional score estimation error. No prior diffusion-model-based SIS method had addressed robustness to noisy semantic label inputs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 semantic label map (151 classes on ADE20K, 19 on CelebAMask-HQ, 172 on COCO-Stuff) that may be corrupted by downsampling, edge masking, or random noise, and produces a 256×256 photo-realistic RGB image. Unlike standard conditional diffusion models that feed a fixed label y_0 at every timestep, SCDM stochastically perturbs the label via a discrete forward process so that the condition y_t changes across timesteps, becoming fully masked (all pixels in an absorbing [mask] state) at t=T. The label is embedded into the U-Net decoder via SPADE normalization, with the [mask] state implemented as a zero vector to minimize architectural changes.

## Method

SCDM introduces Label Diffusion, a discrete diffusion process with an absorbing state that progressively masks semantic labels pixel-by-pixel, ensuring that noisy and clean label trajectories converge as t increases and become identical at t=T. A class-wise noise schedule parameterized by ψ_c (inverse average class area) and φ_c (log inverse class frequency) slows masking for small and rare classes, improving their generation quality. The generation process couples a discrete forward process for labels (no reverse needed since labels are given) with a continuous reverse process for images, trained with a hybrid loss that conditions on the perturbed label y_t rather than fixed y_0. The paper proves that the expected implicit classifier gradient under Label Diffusion equals a time-scaled version of the fixed-label gradient, and incorporates x_0-space extrapolation and classifier-free guidance to further improve few-step generation quality.

## Results

On the novel noisy SIS benchmark (ADE20K), SCDM achieves the best FID across all three noise settings: 32.4 (Downsampled), 31.2 (Edge), and 28.1 (Random), outperforming the best GAN baseline (OASIS) by 2.1, 6.4, and 8.2 FID points respectively. On the standard clean-label SIS setting, SCDM achieves FID of 17.4 on CelebAMask-HQ, 26.9 on ADE20K, and 15.3 on COCO-Stuff, setting new state-of-the-art among diffusion-based methods on all three datasets. Notably, SCDM with only 25 sampling steps (FID 27.7) outperforms the fixed-label baseline with 1000 steps (FID 28.1) on ADE20K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCDM dominates all diffusion-based baselines (SDM, LDM) on every noisy setting, with the most dramatic gap on the Random noise setup where SDM's FID degrades to 141.9 while SCDM maintains 28.1. Against the strongest GAN baselines, SCDM achieves lower FID than OASIS (32.4 vs 34.5 on DS) and SAFM (32.4 vs 36.7 on DS), though SAFM retains a slight mIoU edge (50.1 vs 44.7) by leveraging extra ground-truth instance maps at sampling time. On the standard clean-label setting (Table 2), SCDM surpasses SDM on all three datasets (FID 17.4 vs 18.8 on CelebAMask-HQ, 26.9 vs 27.5 on ADE20K, 15.3 vs 15.9 on COCO-Stuff) and also outperforms LDM on CelebAMask-HQ (17.4 vs 21.5) and ADE20K (26.9 vs 36.5). Among GAN methods on clean labels, SCDM's FID of 17.4 on CelebAMask-HQ beats all GAN baselines including SCGAN (20.8) and INADE (21.5), while on ADE20K it is competitive with ECGAN++ (24.7) and OASIS (28.3). The class-wise noise schedule further improves mIoU by +6.4 overall and +8.1 on rare classes compared to a uniform schedule, confirming the benefit of differential label diffusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SDM (Wang et al., 2022b), adopting its SPADE-embedded U-Net architecture and pre-trained weights, and on the absorbing-state discrete diffusion framework of D3PM (Austin et al., 2021) for the label corruption process. The key departure from prior conditional diffusion models for SIS (SDM, LDM) is the introduction of a stochastic conditioning trajectory: rather than fixing the semantic label y_0 throughout all T reverse steps, SCDM applies a discrete forward diffusion (Label Diffusion) to progressively mask labels, so that noisy and clean label trajectories converge as t→T. The class-wise noise schedule, which differentially controls masking speed based on class area and frequency, has no direct precedent in prior diffusion-based SIS work, and the theoretical connection between label diffusion and time-scaled implicit classifier gradients (Proposition 2) provides a new analytical lens on conditional guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap that prior SIS methods ignored: real-world users provide noisy, incomplete, or low-resolution semantic maps, yet all existing models are trained and evaluated on clean annotations. The noisy SIS benchmark (DS, Edge, Random) is a useful community resource for evaluating robustness, and the Label Diffusion mechanism is lightweight to implement (minimal overhead over standard conditional DMs). Open questions include whether the class-wise noise schedule can be learned end-to-end rather than hand-designed, whether the approach extends to other conditioning modalities (e.g., depth maps, edge maps), and how to preserve user intent when the noisy input is intentional rather than erroneous.
