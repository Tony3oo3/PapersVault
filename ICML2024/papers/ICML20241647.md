## Problem

Machine learning pipelines embed structured objects (sets, functions, distributions) into Euclidean latent spaces, but the resulting embeddings carry no algebraic structure from the source domain. This paper asks how to learn latent-space operations (e.g., set union) that provably satisfy the same axiomatic laws (associativity, commutativity, distributivity) as the original algebra, enabling efficient and self-consistent manipulation of embeddings without decoding back to the source objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241647/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pairs (or tuples) of 1024-dimensional latent vectors, each encoding a 2D set in [-1,1]² via a pretrained inr2vec encoder that compresses SIREN INR weights. It produces a single latent vector representing the result of a set operation (intersection or union) applied to the input sets. Operations are composed recursively over randomly generated terms of up to 10 symbols, and the final latent is decoded by a fixed decoder D(u, z) that predicts pointwise set membership.

## Method

The core mechanism is a learned bijection φ: L → M (parameterized as a 2-layer NICE invertible network) from the latent space to a 'mirrored algebra' M = ℝ^l endowed with closed-form operations (e.g., elementwise min/max for the Riesz algebra) that satisfy the target distributive lattice laws. Latent operations are induced by conjugation: f^L(z₁,…,zₙ) = φ⁻¹(f^M(φ(z₁),…,φ(zₙ))), which by construction makes φ an isomorphism L ≅ M and guarantees that any law satisfied by M is also satisfied by L (Theorem 3.2). Training minimizes binary cross-entropy between decoded predicted terms and ground-truth set realizations over randomly sampled symbolic terms. A key theoretical contribution proves that the full Boolean lattice (including complementation) is infeasible on ℝ^l with continuous operations—since complementation requires a fixed-point-free continuous involution, which cannot exist on contractible spaces—motivating the relaxation to a distributive lattice.

## Results

The Riesz mirrored algebra (elementwise min/max, satisfying all 8 distributive lattice laws) achieves the highest mean IoU across random set-operation terms and is perfectly self-consistent (IoU = 1.0) under any number of equivalent-term transformations, experimentally validating Proposition 3.1. Algebras satisfying fewer laws show a clear monotonic decrease in IoU, with operations satisfying 0 laws performing worst. The direct latent-space baselines (naive MLP and symmetric DeepSets-style) outperform transported algebras with a comparable number of satisfied laws, but remain inferior to the Riesz algebra across all experiments. In the self-consistency experiment, the bottom quartile of the learned baselines drops sharply after just two random symbolic manipulations, while the Riesz algebra remains at perfect consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the structural transport net against two direct latent-space parameterizations: a naive MLP (satisfying 0 distributive lattice laws) and a symmetric DeepSets-style parameterization f(z₁,z₂) = h(g(z₁)+g(z₂)) (satisfying 2 laws via commutativity, following Zaheer et al., 2017). On operation accuracy (mean IoU over random terms), the Riesz algebra (8/8 laws) decisively outperforms both baselines, though the baselines beat transported algebras that satisfy only 0–2 laws, suggesting their flexible parameterization partially compensates for the lack of structural guarantees. On self-consistency (IoU between predictions for equivalent terms), the Riesz algebra achieves a perfect score of 1.0 regardless of the number of law applications, while the symmetric baseline's median degrades moderately and its bottom quartile drops sharply after just two symbolic manipulations. The naive MLP baseline shows the weakest self-consistency overall. The authors conclude that while flexible parameterizations can learn some symmetries, the Riesz algebra is 'decidedly superior to both across all experiments.'

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of universal algebra and invertible neural networks, proposing the first general procedure to transport algebraic structures (operations + laws) from source data onto learned latent embeddings. It builds directly on NICE (Dinh et al., 2015) for the bijection parameterization and inr2vec (De Luigi et al., 2023) for the fixed encoder-decoder of shape INRs. Conceptually, it generalizes the equivariance/invariance principles underlying geometric deep learning (Cohen & Welling, 2016; Maron et al., 2018; Zaheer et al., 2017) from specific symmetries (permutation, translation, rotation) to arbitrary algebraic laws (associativity, commutativity, distributivity, absorption), while explicitly distinguishing its setting from DeepSets' finite-set regime by operating on positive-volume subsets of R^d.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, provably-consistent way to perform algebraic operations in latent space, which is essential for compositional ML workflows (e.g., shape editing, set-valued prediction, reachable set computation). The topological impossibility result—that no continuous fixed-point-free involution exists on R^n, ruling out Boolean lattice transport—is a clean and general constraint that any future work on latent-space complementation must confront. Key limitations include the requirement that the mirrored space match the latent dimension, the reliance on a pretrained encoder, and validation only on 2D synthetic shapes; open questions include generalizing to higher-dimensional or non-Euclidean mirrored spaces, joint training of φ with the encoder, and downstream applications beyond the controlled experiments.
