## Problem

Few-shot action recognition struggles when base and novel data exhibit significant distributional disparities, and existing methods either fine-tune all parameters (inefficient) or add new modules (complex). The key unresolved question is which components of a pre-trained model encode transferable, domain-invariant knowledge versus domain-variant knowledge that must be updated, and how to exploit this distinction for efficient adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241098/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 16 uniformly sampled frames per video clip, processed by a frozen backbone (ViT-B/16 or ResNet-50) to produce 1024-dim frame-level representations. During adaptation, only k labeled examples per novel action class are provided. Output is either a one-hot class prediction (CE variant) or a cosine-similarity match against text embeddings of action labels produced by a transformer-based text encoder with 8 attention heads (NCE variant). The latent space uses d=12 dynamic variables and S=35 latent domain slots.

## Method

CDTD employs a VAE-based temporal causal dynamics model (following the TDRL framework) where each frame's latent state z_t^d evolves via a non-stationary transition conditioned on an estimated domain variable z_t^u. The domain estimator uses learnable gating functions with 1×1 convolutions and a GRU to capture distributional shifts without domain labels, while the temporal dynamics transition is modeled with a flow network (normalizing flow) that computes the prior likelihood via the change-of-variables formula. The central architectural insight is that the temporal dynamics transition (φ) and image decoder (γ) are domain-invariant and remain frozen during few-shot adaptation, while only the image encoder (ω), domain estimator (θ), and classifier (ψ) are updated. Training uses ELBO loss (BCE reconstruction + β-scaled KL divergence) for the unsupervised representation stage, followed by supervised CE or NCE loss for the prediction stage, requiring an order of magnitude fewer updated parameters than competing methods.

## Results

On the Sth-Else dataset (all-way-k-shot, ViT-B/16 backbone), CDTD_NCE achieves 48.5% top-1 accuracy at 5-shot and 63.9% at 10-shot, surpassing the strongest baselines VL Prompting (44.9%/58.2%) and ViFi-CLIP (44.5%/54.0%) by 3.6 and 5.7 percentage points respectively. In the cross-dataset all-way-k-shot setting (Kinetics-400 base → SSv2, HMDB-51, UCF-101), CDTD_NCE attains the highest accuracy in 11 out of 12 experiments, e.g., 77.9% on HMDB-51 at 16-shot vs. 75.3% for VideoMAE. In the 5-way-k-shot setting (ResNet-50, CE loss), CDTD_CE achieves the best accuracy in 7 of 8 experiments, reaching 66.0% on UCF-101 (1-shot) and 54.4% on HMDB-51 (5-shot).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDTD_NCE outperforms all seven all-way-k-shot baselines (XCLIP, ActionCLIP, VicTR, VideoPrompt, ViFi-CLIP, VL Prompting, VideoMAE) in 11 of 12 settings, with the sole exception being UCF-101 2-shot where it trails VideoMAE and VL Prompting by 0.4 points (90.6 vs. 91.0). The largest margins appear on SSv2 (e.g., 9.5 vs. 8.2 for VideoMAE at 2-shot) and HMDB-51 (e.g., 77.9 vs. 75.3 at 16-shot), where distributional shift from Kinetics-400 is most severe. In the 5-way-k-shot setting, CDTD_CE beats SEEN, STARTUP, DYDIS, STRM, TRX, and OTAM in 7 of 8 experiments, with the only near-tie being UCF-101 5-shot (79.7 vs. SEEN's 79.8, a 0.1 gap). On Sth-Else specifically, CDTD_NCE improves over VL Prompting by 3.6 points (5-shot) and 5.7 points (10-shot), while CDTD_CE surpasses SViT by 3.2 points at 5-shot. The paper also demonstrates that CDTD requires an order of magnitude fewer parameters to update during adaptation compared to ViFi-CLIP, ORViT, SViT, and VL Prompting, highlighting its parameter efficiency advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

CDTD builds on the temporal causal representation learning framework of TDRL (Yao et al., 2022) and the latent domain learning approach of Deecke et al. (2022), combining them within a VAE-based generative model for video. It departs from prior few-shot action recognition methods that either fine-tune all parameters (ActionCLIP, ORViT, SViT) or freeze the backbone and add new prompt modules (VideoPrompt, VL Prompting), instead using causal structure to automatically identify which components (temporal dynamics transition and generation) are domain-invariant and should be frozen during adaptation. The represent-predict pipeline follows VideoMAE (Tong et al., 2022), and the minimal change principle (Kong et al., 2022) motivates the strategy of updating only domain-variant parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CDTD demonstrates that freezing the learned temporal dynamics during few-shot adaptation yields both higher accuracy and substantially fewer updated parameters than competing methods, offering a principled alternative to full fine-tuning or prompt-based approaches. The empirical validation that temporal dynamics remain consistent across severe distributional shifts (e.g., Kinetics-400 to Sth-Else) supports a broader hypothesis about transferability of physical-law-like relations in video. However, the paper lacks a theoretical generalization bound for the adaptation strategy, and the assumption that domain information is estimable without labels remains empirically validated but not formally justified. Extending the framework to instantaneous causal relations and providing formal guarantees are identified as key open directions.
