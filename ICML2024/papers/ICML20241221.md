## Problem

Existing antibody CDR co-design methods generate structures with unrealistic internal geometries (bond lengths, angles, and torsions deviating significantly from physicochemical constraints), and the standard affinity maturation pipeline (ITA with external ΔΔG predictors) is unreliable because those predictors are trained on Rosetta-generated structures that are out-of-domain relative to co-design model outputs, producing misleading mutation effect estimates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241221/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

For CDR co-design, the model takes the 3D structure of the antigen (48 nearest residues as epitope) and non-CDR antibody regions as context, and outputs CDR amino acid types plus four backbone atom coordinates (N, Cα, C, O) per residue. For affinity maturation, it takes a wild-type antibody-antigen complex (sequence + structure) and outputs mutant CDR sequences, predicted mutant CDR backbone structures, and a scalar ΔΔG value. The generative variant (GeoAB-Designer) initializes CDRs by sampling torsion angles from learned Von Mises mixtures and reconstructing via NeRF, while the refinement variant (GeoAB-Refiner) uses linear interpolation initialization.

## Method

GeoAB-Designer combines a Geo-Initializer (a 3-layer GAT that predicts parameters of K=4 Von Mises mixtures for the three redundant backbone torsion angles, trained by negative log-likelihood, followed by NeRF reconstruction) with a Geo-Refiner that performs heterogeneous residue-level encoding (9-layer GNN over sequence/intra/inter-structure edges) and atom-level position updates via binary, ternary, and quaternary interaction layers using generalized coordinates and forward kinematics to model bond lengths, angles, and torsions respectively. Geometric constraints inspired by AMBER force fields (quadratic bond-length/angle terms and cosine torsion terms) are added as auxiliary losses, with a dynamic EMA-decaying weight on the geometry loss to preserve initialized realism during training. For affinity maturation, GeoAB-Optimizer uses twin Geo-Refiners with shared parameters to simultaneously predict mutant CDR structures and ΔΔG from sequence input alone, trained jointly on paired data (with ΔΔG labels) and unpaired data (where ΔΔG target is zero), thereby eliminating the domain-mismatch problem of feeding externally predicted structures into a separate ΔΔG predictor.

## Results

On RAbD CDR-H3 co-design, GeoAB-Refiner achieves the best overall performance with AAR 0.4053, A-RMSD 1.3978, UA-RMSD 1.5852, lddt 0.9860, and TM-score 0.9858, improving A-RMSD and UA-RMSD by 10.71% and 10.02% over the best refinement baseline (C-DyMEAN). The generative variant GeoAB-Designer (AAR ~0.39, UA-RMSD ~1.5) outperforms DiffAB (AAR ~0.27, UA-RMSD ~9.5) by a large margin across all metrics. For ΔΔG prediction on SKEMPI2, GeoAB achieves the best per-structure PCC (0.3936), SRCC (0.3610), overall SRCC (0.4972), RMSE (1.4758), and MAE (1.0611), while DDG-Pred leads slightly on overall PCC (0.6580 vs. 0.6324).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among refinement baselines, GeoAB-R outperforms C-DyMEAN (the strongest competitor) on all five metrics, with the largest gains in A-RMSD (1.3978 vs. 1.5656) and UA-RMSD (1.5852 vs. 1.7641). MEAN, while competitive on A-RMSD (1.5764), lags behind GeoAB-R on AAR (0.3706 vs. 0.4053) and UA-RMSD (1.7866 vs. 1.5852). RefineGNN and its conditional variant C-RefineGNN are substantially weaker, with UA-RMSD values above 7. In the generative setting, GeoAB-D dominates DiffAB by a wide margin: UA-RMSD drops from ~9.5 to ~1.5, and lddt jumps from ~0.70 to ~0.985. For ΔΔG prediction, GeoAB achieves the best per-structure PCC (0.3936) and SRCC (0.3610), and the best overall SRCC (0.4972), RMSE (1.4758), and MAE (1.0611), but trails DDG-Pred slightly on overall PCC (0.6324 vs. 0.6580). GeoAB's key advantage over DDG-Pred and RDE-PPI is that it does not require mutant-type structures as input, avoiding the domain-shift problem that causes unreliable ΔΔG predictions in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoAB extends the equivariant graph neural network lineage (EGNN, graph mechanics networks) by generalizing atom-level position updates from binary to ternary and quaternary interactions using generalized coordinates, and by incorporating AMBER-inspired geometric constraints (bond lengths, angles, torsions) as explicit loss terms. For CDR co-design, it departs from prior methods (MEAN, DyMEAN, DiffAB) that treat residues as rigid bodies or ignore internal geometry, introducing a Von Mises mixture generative prior (Geo-Initializer) for redundant torsion angles followed by a deterministic refiner. For affinity maturation, it breaks from the ITA paradigm that relies on external ΔΔG predictors requiring mutant structures as input, instead jointly predicting both mutant CDR structures and ΔΔG within a single architecture to eliminate the domain-mismatch problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practically important gaps: generated CDR structures that violate basic physicochemical constraints (broken bonds, unrealistic angles), and affinity maturation pipelines where the ΔΔG predictor is trained on Rosetta-generated structures but applied to co-design model outputs, producing unreliable optimization signals. The joint structure+ΔΔG prediction approach is a clean architectural solution to the domain mismatch, and the geometric constraints provide a reusable recipe for any equivariant protein structure generator. Limitations include higher computational cost from the multi-order interaction layers, ΔΔG accuracy still trailing pretraining-based methods like RDE-PPI, and the approach being restricted to backbone atoms rather than full side-chain modeling.
