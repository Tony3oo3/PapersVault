## Problem

Existing deep learning methods for RNA secondary structure prediction rely on iterative constrained optimization (e.g., unrolled algorithms, Fenchel-Young losses) that cannot guarantee the output satisfies all biological validity constraints, suffer from poor generalization to unseen RNA families, and incur high inference cost. This paper asks whether the prediction can be reframed as a finite combinatorial matching problem that inherently enforces validity while remaining simple and fast.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241937/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RNA nucleotide sequence of length L (one-hot encoded as ℝ^{L×4}) and produces a binary L×L base-pairing matrix M where M_ij=1 indicates a bond between bases i and j. Unlike prior methods that may output structurally invalid matrices, RFold's output is guaranteed to satisfy all three hard constraints (valid Watson-Crick/GU base pairs, no loops shorter than 4 nt, and at most one partner per base) by construction, since the bi-dimensional argmax over row- and column-wise Softmax distributions enforces the one-pair-per-base constraint exactly.

## Method

RFold reformulates RNA secondary structure prediction as a symmetric K-Rook matching problem: finding the most probable non-attacking rook placement on an L×L board, which is equivalent to finding a valid base-pairing matrix. The key technical contribution is a bi-dimensional optimization that decomposes the joint matching into independent row-wise and column-wise Softmax distributions over a symmetric, constraint-masked hidden matrix Ĥ=(H⊙Hᵀ)⊙M̄, then combines them via element-wise product (equivalently, their arithmetic mean for gradient computation). The backbone is a U-Net that performs image-to-image segmentation on an L×L attention map produced by a novel Seq2map attention module, which computes pairwise interactions via per-dimension scaled queries/keys with ReLU² activation and a sigmoid gate. Training uses a simple MSE loss between the averaged row/column Softmax outputs and the ground-truth pairing matrix, requiring no iterative optimization, no Lagrange multipliers, and no additional constraint-enforcement parameters at inference time.

## Results

On the RNAStralign test set, RFold achieves an F1 score of 0.977 (Precision 0.981, Recall 0.973), outperforming the previous state-of-the-art UFold (F1 0.915) by 6.2 percentage points and E2Efold (F1 0.821) by 15.6 points. RFold also guarantees 100% structural validity (satisfying all three hard constraints), whereas E2Efold achieves only 50.31% validity. Inference speed is 0.02 s per sequence, approximately 8× faster than UFold (0.16 s) and 16× faster than MXfold2 (0.31 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RFold outperforms all 14 baselines on every dataset and metric evaluated. Against the strongest learning-based competitor UFold, RFold gains +6.2 F1 on RNAStralign (0.977 vs 0.915), +1.6 F1 on ArchiveII (0.921 vs 0.905), and a striking +11.7 F1 on long-range bpRNA-TS0 (0.701 vs 0.584), while also achieving 100% structural validity versus UFold's unguaranteed output. Against E2Efold, RFold improves F1 by 15.6 points on RNAStralign and 13.8 points on ArchiveII, and uniquely satisfies constraint (c) (at most one pair per base). Energy-based methods (Mfold, RNAfold, RNAstructure, CONTRAfold, LinearFold, CDPfold) all fall in the 0.42–0.63 F1 range on RNAStralign, far below RFold. On the large-scale bpRNA-TS0 benchmark, RFold (F1 0.644) surpasses SPOT-RNA (0.619) by 4.0 points and all energy-based methods by 9–11 points. On pseudoknot-containing structures, RFold achieves F1 0.982, edging out UFold (0.976) and E2Efold (0.911). The only trade-off is a slightly lower recall on ArchiveII (0.910 vs UFold's 0.928), attributed to RFold's strict constraint enforcement, but this is more than compensated by its substantially higher precision (0.938 vs 0.887).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant constrained-optimization paradigm in learning-based RNA folding (E2Efold's unrolled algorithm with Lagrange multipliers, RTfold's Fenchel-Young perturbation) by reframing the problem as a symmetric K-Rook matching task over a finite solution space. Rather than iteratively optimizing a continuous relaxation to approximate discrete constraints, RFold enforces the 'at most one pair per base' constraint exactly through a bi-dimensional decomposition into independent row-wise and column-wise softmax operations, eliminating the need for iterative solvers or convex relaxations. The Seq2map attention module draws on linear-attention ideas (Performers, Linear Transformers) to produce a pairwise interaction map from sequence, while the U-Net backbone treats the map-to-structure step as image segmentation, repurposing a standard architecture for a structured-prediction setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RFold is the first learning-based method to guarantee 100% validity of predicted structures (satisfying all three biological constraints simultaneously), which prior methods like E2Efold (~50% validity) and SPOT-RNA (0%) cannot achieve. The 8× speedup over UFold and 16× over MXfold2 makes it practical for large-scale screening. However, the strict constraint enforcement comes at a recall cost—RFold is more conservative in predicting base pairs, potentially missing true interactions, and the authors acknowledge that a better precision-recall trade-off strategy remains an open problem. The bi-dimensional softmax trick (row-argmax × column-argmax to enforce a permutation-like constraint) is a clean, generalizable idea that could transfer to other structured-prediction tasks requiring mutual exclusivity constraints.
