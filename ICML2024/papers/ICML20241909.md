## Problem

ML-based RNA secondary structure predictors (e.g., E2Efold, REDfold) output score matrices that frequently violate hard biological constraints such as allowed base-pair types, minimum loop size, and non-overlapping pairs. Existing post-processing methods—proximal gradient descent (E2Efold), row/column argmax (REDfold), and Blossom-based matching (CNNfold)—do not guarantee constraint satisfaction, as demonstrated by a toy counterexample and empirical violation rates up to 58% on real datasets.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RNA nucleotide sequence x of length L over {A, G, C, U} and a real-valued L×L symmetric score matrix Ŷ produced by a Stage-1 deep network (transformer or U-Net). It outputs a binary symmetric L×L base-pairing matrix Ỹ satisfying four hard constraints: (C1) binary and symmetric, (C2) only AU/GC/GU pairs, (C3) no pairs with |i−j|<4, and (C4) at most one partner per nucleotide. The only tunable hyperparameter is an offset s that thresholds which score entries are eligible for pairing.

## Method

The core contribution is proving that the constrained binary ILP for post-processing is equivalent to a Linear Sum Assignment Problem (LSAP). A mask matrix M encodes all structural constraints (allowed base pairs, minimum distance, score threshold), and the cost matrix is set to C = −2(Ŷ − s) ⊙ M. The equivalence theorem (Theorem 4.1) shows that any optimal solution of the original constrained problem maps bijectively to an optimal solution of the assignment problem and vice versa. The LSAP is then solved exactly in O(L³) time via the Jonker-Volgenant algorithm, guaranteeing zero constraint violations by construction. The framework is model-agnostic: it can be plugged behind any Stage-1 score network, and empirically it outperforms or matches existing post-processors in F1 while running 5–350× faster.

## Results

On RNAStrAlign, REDfold+Ours achieves the best F1 of 0.918 (Rec=0.907, Prc=0.944) with a post-processing time of only 0.014 s per sequence, outperforming REDfold+REDPP (F1=0.904) and REDfold+Blossom (F1=0.917). On ncRNA, REDfold+Ours reaches F1=0.847 with 0.005 s runtime, again the best among all methods. Critically, the proposed post-processing produces zero constraint violations (C2, C3, C4) across both datasets, whereas E2Efold+E2EPP violates (C4) in 56.8–58.3% of cases and SPOT-RNA violates (C2) in 57.7–91.1% of cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed post-processing (Ours) is evaluated as a drop-in replacement for existing post-processing steps within the E2Efold (transformer) and REDfold (U-Net) backbones, and compared against eight baselines. Against the strongest ML baselines, REDfold+Ours achieves F1=0.918 on RNAStrAlign, edging out REDfold+Blossom (0.917) and REDfold+REDPP (0.904), while being ~35× faster than Blossom (0.014 s vs. 0.496 s) and ~5.6× faster than REDPP (0.079 s). On ncRNA, REDfold+Ours (F1=0.847) similarly surpasses REDfold+REDPP (0.844) and REDfold+Blossom (0.840). Against traditional DP-based methods (RNAfold F1=0.602, RNAstructure F1=0.596) and earlier ML methods (CONTRAfold 0.664, SPOT-RNA 0.758, MXfold2 0.783 on RNAStrAlign), the proposed method leads by 13–32 F1 points. The most critical differentiator is constraint satisfaction: Ours yields 0% violations on all three constraints (C2–C4) for both backbones, while E2Efold+E2EPP violates the no-overlap constraint (C4) in 56.8–58.3% of predictions and SPOT-RNA violates the allowed-base-pair constraint (C2) in 57.7–91.1% of predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage ML-based RNA secondary structure prediction framework introduced by E2Efold (Chen et al., 2020) and REDfold (Chen & Chan, 2023), where a deep network outputs a score matrix that is then refined by a post-processing step. The key departure is replacing the existing post-processing algorithms—proximal gradient descent (E2Efold), row/column argmax (REDfold), and Blossom-based matching (CNNfold)—with a Linear Sum Assignment Problem (LSAP) formulation solved exactly by the Jonker-Volgenant algorithm. The paper demonstrates that prior post-processing methods do not actually guarantee constraint satisfaction, and provides a theoretical equivalence (Theorem 4.1) showing the constrained binary ILP reduces to a standard assignment problem, yielding a polynomial-time exact solution with zero constraint violations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for ML-based RNA structure prediction: the proposed post-processing eliminates all constraint violations (up to 58.3% in prior methods) while improving F1 scores and reducing runtime by 5–7×, making it a drop-in replacement for existing pipelines. Two limitations remain: the O(L³) assignment solver may be prohibitive for very long RNAs (tens of thousands of nucleotides), and the two-stage design disconnects the computational graph, preventing end-to-end training where the backbone could learn to anticipate the post-processing. Broader open questions the paper highlights include incorporating co-transcriptional folding dynamics and handling the possibility of multiple valid structural solutions for a single sequence.
