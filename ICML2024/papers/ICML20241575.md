## Problem

While online RL has demonstrated that learning multiple behaviors from a single task enables few-shot adaptation and behavior composition, the problem of discovering diverse solutions in offline RL remains largely unexplored. The central challenge is that offline datasets contain only state-action pairs without annotations of which behavior (latent skill) each pair belongs to, making it necessary to learn latent skill representations entirely in an unsupervised manner while simultaneously training a policy that exploits them.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed offline dataset of (s, a, r, s') tuples collected by an unknown behavior policy that may exhibit multiple distinct behaviors, with no latent variable labels present in the data. It produces a latent-conditioned policy π(a|s,z) where the continuous 2D latent variable z is sampled once at the start of each episode and held fixed, yielding qualitatively different locomotion styles (e.g., one-leg hopping vs. two-leg walking) for different z values. A learned posterior q(z|s,a) additionally maps any state-action pair back to its inferred skill, enabling the policy to be evaluated and adapted without retraining.

## Method

DiveOff alternates two updates in a coordinate-ascent scheme resembling EM. In the E-step, the latent-conditioned policy is updated via an advantage-weighted log-likelihood objective (an extension of MPO/AWAC) under a KL constraint to the behavior policy, where the weight exp(A^π(s,a,z)/α_π) up-weights high-advantage actions for each latent value. In the M-step, the posterior q(z|s,a) is updated under a trust-region KL constraint to its previous value, combined with a weighted VAE loss that enforces consistency between the posterior and the density induced by the optimal policy. A variational lower bound on the mutual information I(z;s,a) is jointly maximized to encourage the policy to produce distinct actions for distinct z values, while the encoder recovers z from the policy's own outputs. This joint, advantage-weighted training of policy and posterior distinguishes DiveOff from baselines that train a standalone VAE on the dataset or use DIAYN-style auxiliary rewards, and is the key mechanism that yields both high D4RL scores and high diversity scores simultaneously.

## Results

On the diverse-expert datasets, DiveOff achieves normalized D4RL scores of 93.3±9.9 (Walker2dVel), 81.2±33.8 (HopperVel), 96.8±0.3 (HalfcheetahVel), and 95.4±1.2 (AntVel), consistently outperforming or matching all baselines. On the diverse-medium-expert datasets, DiveOff reaches 98.4±4.7 on HopperVel and 96.1±0.6 on HalfcheetahVel. In terms of diversity (Table 2), DiveOff is the only method achieving both high diversity scores (e.g., 0.88±0.11 on HopperVel-diverse-expert, 0.96±0.04 on HalfcheetahVel-diverse-medium-expert) and high D4RL scores simultaneously. In few-shot adaptation (Table 3), DiveOff outperforms baselines on most test environments, e.g., 1941.8±62.2 on Hopper-LongHead with diverse-medium-replay training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TD3+BC (a single-solution offline RL method), DiveOff achieves comparable or superior D4RL scores on most tasks (e.g., 93.3 vs. 75.5 on Walker2dVel-diverse-expert; 101.0 vs. 67.9 on HopperVel-diverse-medium-replay), while also producing diverse behaviors that TD3+BC cannot. CLUE, designed for leveraging expert demonstrations rather than discovering multiple solutions, shows highly unstable performance (e.g., 17.5±13.0 on Walker2dVel-diverse-medium-replay) and lower diversity scores than DiveOff on most tasks. Against IQL and AWAC (single-solution baselines in Table 11), DiveOff (best) achieves comparable or better normalized scores (e.g., 99.8 vs. 90.8 for AWAC on Walker2dVel-diverse-expert; 99.7 vs. 66.5 for IQL on HopperVel-diverse-expert), demonstrating that learning multiple behaviors does not sacrifice peak performance. LAPO, designed for multimodal datasets, outperforms other single-solution baselines on diverse-expert data but DiveOff matches or exceeds it overall (e.g., 99.8 vs. 93.1 on Walker2dVel-diverse-expert). The key differentiator is that DiveOff is the only method achieving both high D4RL scores and high diversity scores simultaneously, whereas baselines like AWAC-L+VAE+DIAYN achieve moderate diversity at the cost of lower task performance, and CLUE achieves high diversity but with unstable task scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on discovering diverse behaviors via latent-conditioned policies (Kumar et al., 2020b; Osa et al., 2022; Sharma et al., 2020) from online RL to the offline setting, where the critical difference is that latent variable values are unknown in the dataset. The policy update in the E-step is an extension of AWAC/MPO with advantage-weighted log-likelihood, while the M-step introduces a VAE-style variational lower bound (Kingma & Welling, 2014) to jointly train the posterior and likelihood. The overall coordinate ascent structure is inspired by the EM algorithm, and the mutual information maximization strategy builds on Osa et al. (2022). The paper positions itself against the concurrent SORL (Mao et al., 2024), which restricts the latent variable to be discrete and formulates the problem as clustering, whereas DiveOff supports continuous latent variables and incorporates Q-value information into the latent representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of discovering multiple qualitatively distinct solutions from a single task in offline RL, filling a gap between the well-studied online setting and the practically important offline setting. The practical demonstration that diverse offline behaviors enable few-shot adaptation to modified morphologies (e.g., Hopper with altered knee or head length) shows a concrete path toward robust robot learning without online interaction. Key limitations include sensitivity to random seeds, failure to cover all possible solutions, and poor performance on medium-replay datasets where the diversity of successful states (rather than actions) is the bottleneck. Open questions include whether the framework can learn the full set of learnable behaviors, how to handle discrete latent variables more effectively, and whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional or more complex skill spaces.
