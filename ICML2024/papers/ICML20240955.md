## Problem

Existing depth compression methods reduce network depth by removing non-linear activations and merging consecutive convolution layers, but this merging causes the kernel size of the merged layer to grow, significantly undermining the latency reduction. The paper addresses this by extending the search space to jointly select which convolution layers and activation layers to remove, bypassing the kernel-size growth problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240955/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained CNN (ResNet-34, MobileNetV2, or DDPM) and a target latency budget T0 as input. It outputs a compressed network where selected activation layers are replaced with identity functions and selected convolution layers are replaced with 1×1 depthwise identity convolutions, after which the remaining consecutive convolution layers are merged into single layers at inference time. The method handles architectural specifics such as batch/group normalization fusion, strided convolution restrictions, depthwise convolution tracking, padding reordering, and skip-connection constraints.

## Method

LayerMerge jointly optimizes two index sets: A (activation layers to keep) and C (convolution layers to keep, with removed layers replaced by 1×1 identity convolutions that do not increase merged kernel size). The resulting NP-hard selection problem is approximated by a surrogate that maximizes the sum of per-merged-layer importance values under a sum-of-latencies constraint, where importance is measured as the performance drop after replacing a segment with its merged equivalent. The key efficiency insight is indexing lookup tables by (start, end, merged_kernel_size) rather than by the exponential set C, reducing table construction from O(L²·2^L) to O(L²·K₀) entries. The surrogate is then solved exactly via a dynamic programming recurrence over layers and discretized latency budgets in O(L²·P·K₀) time, with optimality proven by induction. After obtaining the optimal sets A* and C*, the pruned network is fine-tuned and consecutive convolution layers are merged only at inference time.

## Results

On MobileNetV2-1.0 (ImageNet), LayerMerge-55% achieves 72.99% top-1 accuracy with 1.63× PyTorch speed-up, matching the original network's accuracy while compressing latency to 55%. At higher compression, LayerMerge-33% reaches 70.99% accuracy at 2.49× speed-up, outperforming the depth compression baseline (Depth-53%: 70.65% at 2.47×). On DDPM (CIFAR-10), LayerMerge-73% achieves FID 4.16 at 1.13× speed-up, beating the depth compression baseline (Depth-89%: FID 4.21 at 1.04×). On ResNet-34, LayerMerge-71% reaches 74.26% accuracy at 1.36× speed-up versus Depth-78% at 73.49% and 1.24×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LayerMerge consistently outperforms the depth compression baseline (Kim et al., 2023) across all tested architectures: on MobileNetV2-1.0 it gains 0.16–0.34% accuracy at equal or greater speed-up, on MobileNetV2-1.4 it gains 0.23% at 1.99× vs 1.93×, and on DDPM it achieves lower FID (4.16 vs 4.21) at higher speed-up (1.13× vs 1.04×). Against channel pruning baselines (HALP on ResNet-34, AMC on MobileNetV2-1.0, MetaPruning on MobileNetV2-1.4), LayerMerge achieves higher accuracy at comparable or better speed-up, e.g., 74.26% vs 73.98% on ResNet-34 at ~1.36× vs 1.23×. The layer pruning variant (LayerOnly) is outperformed by LayerMerge on MobileNetV2 (72.99% vs 69.66% at similar speed-up) and DDPM (FID 4.16 vs 4.64), confirming the benefit of joint optimization. On DDPM, the channel pruning baseline Diff-Pruning (Fang et al., 2023) achieves better FID at higher compression (FID 4.85 at 1.40× vs LayerMerge-73% at FID 4.16 at 1.13×), but combining Diff-Pruning with LayerMerge yields the best result (FID 8.92 at 2.59×). Against knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2014), LayerMerge-55% on MobileNetV2-1.0 achieves 72.99% at 1.63× versus 69.69% at 1.17×, requiring only fine-tuning rather than training from scratch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the depth compression framework of Kim et al. (2023), which removes non-linear activation layers and merges consecutive convolution layers via a DP-based surrogate optimization. The key departure is introducing a second pruning modality—removing convolution layers themselves (replaced by identity layers)—so that the merged kernel size does not grow unboundedly, a fundamental limitation acknowledged but unaddressed by prior depth compression work (Dror et al. 2022; Fu et al. 2022; Kim et al. 2023). The joint selection over both activation and convolution layers is formulated as an NP-hard problem and solved exactly via a novel DP algorithm that exploits the combinatorial structure to keep lookup-table construction tractable. The method also subsumes pure layer pruning (Chen & Zhao 2018; Elkerdawy et al. 2020) as a special case, positioning it as a strict generalization of both depth compression and layer pruning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By addressing the kernel-size growth bottleneck, LayerMerge makes depth compression practically viable for architectures where prior methods saw diminishing returns, yielding consistent accuracy-preserving speedups on ResNet, MobileNetV2, and DDPM. The DP formulation is elegant and the lookup-table construction is embarrassingly parallel, making the method accessible to practitioners. However, the approach is restricted to CNNs (and U-Net-style diffusion backbones) and does not extend to transformer architectures, which now dominate many vision tasks. The paper also flags the known concern that pruning can amplify disparate impact across data sub-groups, an open problem that applies to this method as well.
