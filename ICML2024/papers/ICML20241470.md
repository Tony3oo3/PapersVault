## Problem

Training dual-encoder dense retrievers requires computing softmax over millions of targets, but exact computation is intractable. The standard workaround—caching target embeddings and periodically re-embedding them—incurs enormous computational cost (billions of re-encodings) and still leaves the cached representations stale between refreshes, degrading training quality. The paper asks whether a small learned model can correct stale embeddings at every training step, eliminating the need for expensive re-embedding entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241470/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query text (e.g., a question) and a corpus of target passages (e.g., 21M Wikipedia passages for NQ, 8.8M web passages for MSMARCO). Both are mapped to 768-dimensional vectors by separate encoder networks. At training time, the corrector network consumes the stale cached embedding of every target (a fixed buffer initialized once from the pre-trained model) and outputs a corrected 768-d vector used to select hard negatives via top-k inner product. At inference time, the corrector is discarded and the trained target encoder is used directly to produce ranked passages or, in the RLM setting, retrieved passages are fed to a T5 reader that generates a short answer.

## Method

The core contribution is a small two-layer MLP corrector network h(·;Ψ) with residual connections (8192 hidden units) that maps stale cached target embeddings g'(y) to corrected approximations h∘g'(y), closely matching the true target encoder output g(y). Because |Ψ| ≪ |Θ|, the corrector is cheap enough to run on all targets at every training step, replacing the need for periodic re-embedding. Training is joint: the dual-encoders are updated via a task loss (cross-entropy for retrieval, perplexity distillation + NLL for RLMs) computed on a truncated softmax using fresh g(y) values, while the corrector is updated via a cross-entropy loss aligning its corrected-embedding softmax to the fresh-embedding softmax on the same sampled subset. The paper further provides a generalization bound (Theorem 4.3) decomposing the true population risk into empirical risk, a Wasserstein-distance term capturing staleness (bounded by L‖u‖ for parameter drift u), and a Rademacher-complexity term capturing corrector capacity, showing the trade-off between network size, staleness, and sample count.

## Results

On Natural Questions with GTR-base initialization, the Target Corrector (0 re-embeddings) achieves R@1=35.78, R@10=75.06, R@100=91.37, nearly matching the Exhaustive re-embedding baseline (1.68B re-embeds) at R@1=36.29, R@10=75.55, R@100=91.73. With T5-base, the Corrector reaches R@1=23.64 vs. Exhaustive's 24.70. In retrieval-augmented language model training, the Corrector matches Exhaustive on NQ (52.3 vs. 52.4 exact match) and TriviaQA (66.4 vs. 66.5) while requiring zero re-embeddings versus 1.1B, representing a 4–80× reduction in re-embedding cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Dynnibal (Monath et al., 2023), the Corrector with 0 re-embeddings essentially ties Dynnibal+ requiring 42M re-embeddings on GTR/NQ (R@1: 35.78 vs. 35.86) and outperforms it on T5/NQ (R@1: 23.64 vs. 23.71 at 42M, and 24.76 at 80M), while Dynnibal is described as a more complex, harder-to-implement method. Against Stochastic Negative Mining (Reddi et al., 2019), the Corrector clearly wins: on GTR/NQ test, Corrector achieves R@1=35.78 vs. SNM's 32.03 (a 3.75-point gap) despite SNM performing 80M re-embeddings. In RLM training, the Corrector (GTR) outperforms Frozen Retrieval (Izacard & Grave, 2020) by 3.9 points on NQ (52.3 vs. 48.4) and 11.3 points on TriviaQA (66.4 vs. 55.1), and massively outperforms the No Retrieval baseline (Roberts et al., 2020) by over 26 points on NQ (52.3 vs. 25.4). Against the Two-Round training approach (Qu et al., 2021), the Corrector notably outperforms with T5 initialization (R@1: 36.65 vs. 29.50) and slightly edges it with GTR (49.61 vs. 49.06), suggesting the advantage is largest when the initial embedding drift is greater.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stale-buffer training paradigm for dual-encoder retrieval (Lindgren et al., 2021; Izacard et al., 2022) by introducing a small parametric corrector network that learns to transform stale cached target embeddings into approximately fresh ones, eliminating the need for periodic re-embedding. The corrector is conceptually inspired by auxiliary models in energy-based model training (Grathwohl et al., 2020, 2021) that handle intractable computations, but is applied here to the softmax approximation problem in large-output-space classification and retrieval. It departs from more complex alternatives like Dynnibal (Monath et al., 2023), which uses low-rank regression within tree index structures, by proposing a simpler jointly-trained MLP that requires no hand-tuned re-embedding schedule. The paper also extends the approach from supervised dense retrieval to latent-variable retrieval-augmented language models using perplexity distillation (Izacard et al., 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4–80× reduction in re-embedding cost while matching exhaustive re-embedding performance is practically significant for training dense retrievers over corpora of tens of millions of passages, where even a single full re-embedding pass is expensive. The method's simplicity (a 2-layer MLP with residual connections) and the fact that the initial buffer can be computed once and reused across training runs make it easy to adopt in existing pipelines. Open questions include how the approach scales to even larger target spaces where the buffer itself may not fit in accelerator memory, whether the corrector can be repurposed at inference time for approximate retrieval, and how much embedding drift it can effectively model before the approximation degrades. The theoretical analysis (Wasserstein distance bounds, Rademacher complexity) provides useful guidance on the trade-off between corrector capacity, staleness, and sample size, but the bounds are loose and the practical regime where they are tight remains unclear.
