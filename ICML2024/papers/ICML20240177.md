## Problem

Neural additive models (NAMs) provide interpretable per-feature contributions but lack principled uncertainty estimation, cannot identify and suppress uninformative features, and have no mechanism to detect feature interactions. This leads to overconfident predictions, noisy recovered functions, and poor performance when the underlying data-generating process involves interactions or redundant features.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a D-dimensional tabular feature vector, routing each scalar feature x_d through its own dedicated sub-network (a single hidden layer of 64 GELU units by default). Beyond the scalar prediction, the model outputs per-feature credible intervals (±2 std. dev.) that decompose additively across features, implicit feature relevance scores via ARD precision parameters λ_d, and a ranked list of top-k feature pairs for second-order interaction. The block-diagonal posterior structure ensures each feature's uncertainty reflects only its own shape uncertainty, not vertical shifts of other feature networks.

## Method

LA-NAM applies a linearized Laplace approximation around the MAP estimate, exploiting the additive structure to yield a block-diagonal Gauss-Newton posterior where each feature network has an independent covariance block Σ_d, further approximated via layer-wise Kronecker factorization for scalability. Feature selection emerges from optimizing the lower bound on the log-marginal likelihood with respect to per-feature prior precisions λ_d (automatic relevance determination), which drives uninformative feature networks toward zero. Predictive variance decomposes as a sum of per-feature Jacobian-covariance-Jacobian terms, giving closed-form credible intervals for both global and local explanations. For interaction detection, the method computes mutual information between last-layer output weights of feature pairs using a D×D dense Laplace approximation, selecting the top-k pairs to append as joint feature networks in a fine-tuning stage.

## Results

On 14 UCI regression and classification benchmarks, LA-NAM achieves lower negative log-likelihood than the original NAM on 12 of 14 datasets (e.g., 2.46 vs 2.69 on AutoMPG, 0.98 vs 1.02 on Wine), and is competitive with OAK-GP. With top-10 feature interactions (LA-NAM₁₀), performance improves further, reaching or exceeding LightGBM on several datasets (e.g., 0.76 vs 1.37 NLL on Yacht, 1.11 vs 0.81 on Energy). On the MIMIC-III ICU mortality task, LA-NAM achieves 79.6% AUROC and 34.8% AUPRC versus NAM's 77.6% and 32.3%, while LA-NAM₁₀ reaches 80.2% AUROC, approaching LightGBM's 80.6%. On HiRID, LA-NAM attains 90.1% AUROC and 0.174 NLL, outperforming NAM (89.6%, 0.228 NLL) and approaching LightGBM (90.7%, 0.172 NLL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LA-NAM consistently outperforms the original NAM (Agarwal et al., 2021) across nearly all UCI datasets, with the exception of Naval where NAM's −3.87 NLL beats LA-NAM's −7.24 (though OAK-GP's −8.93 is even lower, suggesting the dataset benefits from stronger regularization). Against OAK-GP (Lu et al., 2022), LA-NAM is competitive on most datasets but trails on Naval (−7.24 vs −8.93) and kin8nm (−0.20 vs 0.09), while winning on AutoMPG, Concrete, Wine, and Yacht. The fully-interacting LightGBM baseline remains the strongest on large-scale datasets (Protein, Power, kin8nm), but LA-NAM₁₀ closes the gap substantially, matching or exceeding LightGBM on Yacht (0.76 vs 1.37 NLL) and Energy (1.11 vs 0.81). On the MIMIC-III ICU mortality task, LA-NAM improves AUROC by 2.0 points over NAM (79.6 vs 77.6) and 0.3 points over OAK-GP (79.6 vs 79.9), while LA-NAM₁₀ reaches 80.2 AUROC, within 0.4 points of LightGBM's 80.6. The smoothing-spline GAM and EBM baselines (reported in Appendix B.6) are generally outperformed by LA-NAM on both UCI and ICU tasks, confirming that the Bayesian neural architecture with Laplace inference provides a meaningful advantage over classical additive and tree-based interpretable methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper takes the deterministic Neural Additive Model (NAM) of Agarwal et al. (2021), which relies on deep ensembles with ExU activations for diversity, and replaces it with a principled Bayesian posterior via a tailored linearized Laplace approximation. The block-diagonal structure of the posterior is the key architectural insight: it preserves the additive decomposition of predictive uncertainty across feature networks while enabling automatic relevance determination (ARD) for implicit feature selection and mutual-information-based interaction detection. The method draws on Kronecker-factored Gauss-Newton curvature (Martens & Grosse, 2015) and scalable marginal likelihood estimation (Immer et al., 2021a) to make the Laplace posterior tractable, positioning LA-NAM as a Bayesian counterpart to the OAK-GP (Lu et al., 2022) but within the neural network framework rather than the Gaussian process one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LA-NAM unifies interpretable prediction, calibrated per-feature uncertainty, implicit feature selection, and second-order interaction detection in a single framework, which is particularly valuable for safety-critical domains like ICU mortality prediction where clinicians need to trust and audit model decisions. The finding that adding just 10 selected interactions brings LA-NAM to competitive performance with the fully-interacting LightGBM gold standard on MIMIC-III suggests that most of the predictive power in tabular data can be captured with a sparse, interpretable model. Open questions include whether the block-diagonal independence assumption is too restrictive for highly correlated features (the paper notes OAK-GP struggles here too), how the method scales to higher-order interactions or very high-dimensional settings, and whether the Laplace approximation's calibration properties hold under distribution shift in clinical deployment.
