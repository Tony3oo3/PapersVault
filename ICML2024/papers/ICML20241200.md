## Problem

In offline RL, the discrete-time Bellman equation cannot accurately estimate values for unvisited states, causing the learned value function to misguide policies toward out-of-distribution regions (e.g., walls in maze environments). Existing offline RL methods regularize actions or penalize out-of-distribution state-action pairs but do not exploit the derivative structure of the value function to constrain its landscape in unexplored regions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard offline trajectory datasets with tuples (s, a, r, s', a') and produces a learned deterministic or stochastic policy π(s) along with a state-action value function Q(s,a). A key detail is that the method extracts first-order dynamics directly from the data via finite differences: state dynamics f(s,a) ≈ s'−s and action derivatives ȧ ≈ a'−a, assuming a constant time step Δt=1. The base algorithm (ReBrac for deterministic, EDAC for stochastic) and its network architecture are retained; only the critic loss is augmented with the first-order consistency terms.

## Method

The core contribution is a first-order consistency loss derived from the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation that constrains the learned Q-function's gradients to align with local state-action dynamics observed in the dataset, without requiring explicit knowledge of the reward function or system dynamics. For deterministic policies (built on ReBrac/TD3), the critic loss combines the standard Bellman error with three terms: (1) state consistency, penalizing misalignment between ∇_s Q(s,a) and the observed state change s'−s (normalized by magnitudes); (2) nearby-state consistency, applying the same constraint at an interpolated point Near(s) = s − ε(s'−s) to enforce structural coherence across neighboring states; and (3) action consistency, penalizing ‖∇_a Q(s,a)‖² only when it exceeds a threshold g_a (via an indicator function) to avoid overfitting to suboptimal dataset actions. A reward-dependent weighting W(r+w_c) modulates the consistency loss so that high-reward transitions receive stronger gradient alignment. For stochastic policies (built on SAC/EDAC), the first-order consistency is incorporated via reward shaping: the instantaneous reward is augmented with λ_r·exp(r + ∇_s Q·ṡ + ∇_a Q·ā) inside the Bellman target.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark, the proposed first-order consistency method achieves normalized scores of 82.6 (Mujoco), 85.7 (Antmaze), 79.6 (Adroit), and 63.3 (Franka Kitchen), outperforming the strongest baseline ReBrac by 4.0%, 11.4%, 6.1%, and 22.9% respectively. On the stochastic policy gradient variant (Table 3), the method achieves 95.1 on Walker2d-medium and 70.0 on Halfcheetah-medium, surpassing EDAC (92.5 and 65.9) and SAC (−0.3 and 55.2). Ablation studies confirm that both state consistency and action consistency components contribute meaningfully, with removing state consistency causing the largest drops (e.g., 94.0→80.4 on Umaze-diverse).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is built on ReBrac (Tarasov et al., 2023) and consistently outperforms it across all four D4RL domains: Mujoco 82.6 vs 79.4, Antmaze 85.7 vs 76.9, Adroit 79.6 vs 75.0, and Franka 63.3 (no ReBrac score reported). Against the strongest actor-critic baselines, the method beats ATAC on Mujoco (82.6 vs 78.6) and Adroit (79.6 vs 63.8), and dominates IQL on all four domains (e.g., Antmaze 85.7 vs 58.4, Adroit 79.6 vs 69.3). CQL and TD3+BC are substantially outperformed, particularly on Antmaze (85.7 vs 50.6 and 27.3) and Adroit (79.6 vs 54.0 and 64.6). The method also surpasses the sequence-modeling baseline DT by large margins on Antmaze (85.7 vs 19.8) and Adroit (79.6 vs no score). In the stochastic policy setting (Table 3), first-order consistency integrated with EDAC outperforms EDAC itself (95.1 vs 92.5 on Walker2d-medium) and dramatically outperforms vanilla SAC (95.1 vs −0.3), confirming the approach generalizes beyond deterministic policy gradients. The only notable exception is Hopper-medium-replay where ATAC edges out the method (102.5 vs 101.3), and Door-cloned where ReBrac slightly leads (1.1 vs 0.2), both within noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges discrete-time offline RL (building on TD3/ReBrac) and continuous-time RL (HJB equations from Doya, Munos, Kim et al.) by extracting first-order derivative information from the HJB equation and using it as a consistency constraint on the discrete-time value function. It departs from prior offline RL methods (CQL, IQL, TD3+BC, F-BRC) that rely solely on zeroth-order Bellman value matching or action-space regularization, and from direct HJB solvers that require explicit dynamics and suffer from the curse of dimensionality. The key conceptual departure is that the HJB equation is used not as a PDE to solve but as a source of local gradient constraints that can be approximated via finite differences from the dataset, eliminating the need for explicit reward or dynamics models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach offers a practical mechanism to improve value function generalization in offline RL without requiring model knowledge or solving PDEs, and it integrates cleanly into both deterministic (TD3/ReBrac) and stochastic (SAC/EDAC) actor-critic frameworks. However, the method requires action transitions (a′) in the dataset and assumes a constant time step, which limits applicability to certain data collection protocols. The moderate improvements (4–22.9% normalized score) suggest the first-order signal is helpful but not a panacea for the extrapolation problem; open questions include whether higher-order consistency (second derivatives) or adaptive weighting schemes could yield larger gains, and whether the approach transfers to model-based or multi-agent offline settings.
