## Problem

Most Byzantine-robust learning methods assume the identity of Byzantine workers remains fixed throughout training, but real-world distributed systems (federated learning, volunteer computing) exhibit dynamic Byzantine behaviors where workers intermittently switch between honest and malicious roles. It was previously unknown how a limited number of such identity changes affects convergence, and whether any method could maintain the state-of-the-art static convergence rate while tolerating a sublinear number of switches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240457/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each round, m workers compute stochastic gradients at three mini-batch sizes (1, 2^{J-1}, 2^J) where J is drawn from a geometric distribution, and send these to the server. The server applies a robust aggregation rule (e.g., coordinate-wise median or Median-Filtered Mean) independently at each mini-batch level, then constructs a single MLMC gradient estimate from the three aggregated vectors. A fail-safe filter checks whether the norm of the difference between the two highest-level aggregated gradients stays within a theoretically derived threshold; if it does not, the MLMC correction term is zeroed out and only the base-level aggregated gradient is used for the update.

## Method

DynaBRO applies a multi-level Monte Carlo (MLMC) gradient estimator to robustly aggregated worker updates, providing direct bias reduction rather than the indirect approach of reducing honest-worker variance (as in worker-momentum). The key innovation for the dynamic setting is a fail-safe filter: since MLMC relies on the expected decreasing distance between aggregated gradients at consecutive levels, a single Byzantine instance within a round could violate this structure, so the algorithm verifies that ‖ĝ^J − ĝ^{J-1}‖ remains within a high-probability bound and reverts to a single-sample update otherwise. This confines the history window to O(log T) in expectation (versus O(√T) for momentum), making the method resilient to identity switches at any round. Combined with a Median-Filtered Mean aggregator and an AdaGrad-Norm adaptive learning rate, DynaBRO achieves near-optimal convergence rates of Õ(V√((δ²+1/m)/T)) while tolerating Õ(√T) dynamic rounds, without requiring prior knowledge of the smoothness constant L or the Byzantine fraction δ.

## Results

On MNIST (17 workers, 8 Byzantine, sign-flip attack, CWTM aggregator), DynaBRO maintains stable final test accuracy across all Periodic(K) switching rates (K = 5, 10, 20, 100, ∞), while worker-momentum degrades progressively as K decreases; momentum with β=0.99 fails entirely at K=100, and β=0.9 degrades noticeably by K=20. On CIFAR-10 (25 workers, IPM attack, CWMed aggregator, δ_max=0.72), DynaBRO significantly outperforms both vanilla SGD and momentum (β=0.9) in the two higher-attack Bernoulli configurations (p=0.01,D=50 and p=0.05,D=10), where a non-negligible fraction of iterations exceed 50% Byzantine workers. In the mildest configuration (p=0.01, D=10), momentum slightly outperforms DynaBRO, consistent with the lower Byzantine pressure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is worker-momentum (Karimireddy et al., 2021), the state-of-the-art Byzantine-robust method in the static setting. On MNIST under Periodic(K) switching, DynaBRO clearly wins: its accuracy remains flat as the switching rate increases, while momentum's accuracy drops sharply—β=0.99 collapses at K=100 and β=0.9 degrades by K=20—because momentum's effective history window of 1/(1−β) iterations becomes contaminated by identity changes. On CIFAR-10 under Bernoulli switching, DynaBRO wins decisively in the two configurations with higher per-iteration Byzantine fractions (p=0.01,D=50 and p=0.05,D=10), where momentum and vanilla SGD both suffer significant accuracy drops. In the lowest-attack configuration (p=0.01, D=10), momentum with β=0.9 slightly outperforms DynaBRO, suggesting that when the dynamic attack is mild, the longer history window of momentum is less harmful and its variance-reduction benefit dominates. Overall, DynaBRO's shorter stochastic history window (O(log T) vs. O(√T) for momentum) is the key advantage in dynamic settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynaBRO builds on the MLMC gradient estimation technique (Dorfman & Levy, 2022; Beznosikov et al., 2023) and the (δ,κ_δ)-robust aggregation framework (Allouah et al., 2023), but departs from the dominant worker-momentum paradigm (Karimireddy et al., 2021) by applying bias reduction post-aggregation rather than relying on long history windows. The key conceptual shift is that MLMC confines the effective history to O(log T) in expectation, whereas prior methods like SafeguardSGD (O(T^{5/8})) and worker-momentum (O(√T)) use much longer windows that become unreliable under identity changes. The paper also introduces the MFM aggregator and AdaGrad-Norm adaptivity to achieve near-optimal rates without prior knowledge of the Byzantine fraction or smoothness parameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a genuine gap: most Byzantine-robust learning theory assumes fixed worker identities, yet real systems (federated learning with partial participation, volunteer computing) exhibit dynamic fault patterns. The clean O(√T) threshold for tolerable identity switches, combined with the demonstration that the dominant worker-momentum method fails under the same budget, provides a concrete and actionable characterization of when existing methods break down. Open questions include extending to heterogeneous data, achieving simultaneous adaptivity to noise level, smoothness, and Byzantine fraction, and whether adaptivity inherently trades off against robustness to identity changes.
