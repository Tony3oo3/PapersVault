## Problem

Existing diffusion-based image restoration methods either require task-specific prior knowledge (e.g., degradation matrices) to condition generation, or rely on bridge models that face difficulties in likelihood estimation (needing costly iterative approximation) and ignore the optimality of the underlying diffusion process. This paper addresses both limitations by identifying a diffusion process whose mean-reverting property naturally connects high-quality and low-quality images, and whose terminal variance can be analytically eliminated to form an exact point-to-point bridge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242395/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality image (masked, rainy, or downsampled) as the fixed terminal point x_T of the bridge and produces the corresponding high-quality image by running the reverse SDE or ODE from x_T back to x_0. Unlike standard diffusion restoration that adds noise to the LQ input before conditioning, GOUB uses the LQ image directly as the exact endpoint with zero variance. Training requires paired HQ-LQ data (e.g., CelebA-HQ with masks, Rain100H pairs, DIV2K 4×/8× downsampled pairs), and the network is a U-Net-like architecture without group normalization or self-attention, operating on 128×128 patches.

## Method

The core mechanism applies Doob's h-transform to the Generalized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (GOU) process, whose mean-reverting drift naturally drives a HQ image toward a Gaussian centered on the LQ image; the h-transform then eliminates the residual terminal variance, yielding a closed-form point-to-point bridge with an analytical forward transition (no multi-step SDE iteration needed). Training uses a maximum-likelihood ELBO objective (adapted from DDPM) rather than score matching, with the noise network parameterizing the score via ε_θ(x_t, x_T, t) and an L1 loss for better pixel-level detail. The paper further introduces a Mean-ODE variant that drops the Brownian drift term from the reverse process, exploiting the fact that each sampling step directly approximates the true mean during training, which proves superior for reconstruction fidelity. Theoretically, the paper shows VE and VP processes are special cases of GOU (via θ_t→0 and μ→0, λ→1 limits), and that prior bridge models (BBDM, DDBM-VE, I²SB) are all equivalent to Brownian bridges, establishing GOUB as a strictly more general and empirically superior framework.

## Results

On image deraining (Rain100H), Mean-ODE achieves the best PSNR of 34.56 dB and SSIM of 0.9414, while GOUB achieves the best LPIPS (0.046) and FID (18.14). On inpainting (CelebA-HQ thin mask), Mean-ODE leads in PSNR (31.39) and SSIM (0.9392), and GOUB leads in LPIPS (0.037) and FID (4.30). On 4× super-resolution (DIV2K), GOUB achieves the best PSNR (26.89), SSIM (0.7478), LPIPS (0.220), and FID (20.85) among all methods, while Mean-ODE achieves the highest PSNR (28.50) and SSIM (0.8070). The Mean-ODE consistently outperforms the corresponding Score-ODE by large margins (e.g., 31.39 vs. 18.23 PSNR on inpainting).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On deraining (Rain100H), Mean-ODE surpasses the strongest diffusion baseline IR-SDE by 2.91 dB in PSNR (34.56 vs. 31.65) and 0.037 in SSIM, while GOUB edges out IR-SDE on PSNR (31.96 vs. 31.65) and FID (18.14 vs. 18.64). On inpainting (CelebA-HQ thin mask), Mean-ODE outperforms PromptIR by 1.17 dB in PSNR (31.39 vs. 30.22) and IR-SDE by 3.02 dB, while GOUB achieves a dramatically lower FID of 4.30 compared to IR-SDE's 25.13 and PromptIR's 32.69. On 4× super-resolution (DIV2K), GOUB beats IR-SDE by 0.99 dB in PSNR (26.89 vs. 25.90) and DDRM by 2.54 dB, with a substantially lower FID (20.85 vs. 45.36 for IR-SDE). In the bridge-model ablation (Table 5), GOUB consistently outperforms both VEB and VPB across all three tasks, e.g., achieving 28.98 vs. 27.75 PSNR on inpainting and 26.89 vs. 24.21/25.40 PSNR on super-resolution, confirming the optimality of the GOU-based bridge over VE/VP-based alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the score-based diffusion framework (Song et al., 2021b) and the ELBO training formulation from DDPM (Ho et al., 2020), but departs from both standard diffusion restoration methods (DDRM, IR-SDE) that require task-specific degradation priors and from prior bridge models (BBDM, DDBM, I2SB) that rely on Brownian bridge or iterative Schrödinger bridge solvers. The key conceptual move is applying Doob's h-transform to the Generalized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process (Ahmad, 1988; Särkkä & Solin, 2019), whose mean-reverting property naturally encodes the HQ-to-LQ degradation, and whose h-transform eliminates terminal variance to yield a true point-to-point bridge. The paper further unifies prior bridge models by proving they are all special cases of the Brownian bridge, while showing VE and VP processes are themselves special cases of GOU, positioning GOUB as a strictly more general framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical unification showing that VEB, VPB, and I2SB are all Brownian bridges provides a clean taxonomy that clarifies why the choice of underlying diffusion process matters for restoration performance. The Mean-ODE finding—that omitting the Brownian noise term from the reverse SDE yields substantially better pixel-level reconstruction than the corresponding Score-ODE—suggests that for deterministic restoration tasks, the stochastic component may be counterproductive, though the very large gap (e.g., ~13 dB PSNR in inpainting) warrants further scrutiny across architectures and scales. Open questions include whether the GOU advantage over VE/VP persists at larger model scales, whether the approach extends to video or 3D restoration, and whether the L1 loss choice (rather than the bridge formulation itself) is the primary driver of the Mean-ODE's pixel-level gains.
