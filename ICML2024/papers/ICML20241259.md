## Problem

Estimating the full CDF under Local Differential Privacy (LDP) is fundamentally harder than under Central DP because the curator is untrusted and each user's contribution is heavily restricted by the privacy mechanism. Prior LDP approaches for distribution estimation relied on discretization (bucketing), which prevents asymptotic consistency and introduces sensitive hyperparameters. This paper asks whether a tuning-free, deterministic estimator for the entire CDF curve can be constructed under LDP with provable convergence rates.

## Input / Output

The method consumes binary responses Δ_i (answers to 'Is T_i ≥ X_i?' perturbed by a randomized-response mechanism with truthful rate r), the curator-generated thresholds T_i drawn from a chosen distribution G (either a continuous density or a finite preselected grid), and the privacy parameter r = tanh(ε/2). It produces a left-continuous staircase function F̂(x) on [0,1] with values in [0,1], plus, on finite grids, a diagonal asymptotic covariance matrix enabling confidence intervals and hypothesis tests. The estimator is order-invariant, meaning the output distribution is unchanged by permuting the input records, which enables additional shuffle-model privacy amplification.

## Method

The key insight is a reparameterization: the randomized-response LDP mechanism is statistically equivalent to observing truthful binary indicators 1{X_i* ≤ T_i} from an alternative variable X_i* (equal to X_i with probability r, Bernoulli(0.5) otherwise), reducing the problem to the classical current-status data estimation in survival analysis. The estimator is a constrained isotonic MLE that maximizes the log-likelihood over all non-decreasing functions from [0,1] to [0,1], computed via the Greatest Convex Minorant of a piecewise-linear path (H₁(x), H₂(x)), yielding a fully deterministic, hyperparameter-free algorithm. A final clipping step enforces the [0,1] range constraint while preserving optimality. Under density-based sampling, the estimator achieves L₂ and uniform consistency at rate O_p(r⁻¹n^{-1/3}) with pointwise Chernoff asymptotics; under preselected finite grids, it attains the parametric rate O_p(n^{-1/2}) with joint asymptotic normality and a diagonal covariance matrix, enabling direct statistical inference.

## Results

Under density-based sampling, the proposed constrained isotonic estimator achieves L2 error of O_p(r^{-1} n^{-1/3}) and uniform error of O_p(r^{-1} n^{-1/3} log n). Empirically, at n=10^7 with r=0.9 (ε≈2.94), the maximum absolute error drops to ~0.008 and L2 error to ~0.002 across all tested distributions (Uniform, Truncated Normal, Continuous Bernoulli). Under preselected sampling on finite grids (κ<∞), the estimator recovers the parametric rate O_p(n^{-1/2}) with asymptotic normality, and empirical 95% coverage rates reach 0.947–0.956 with RCE within 0.995–1.010 for n≥10^5. The algorithm runs in under 1 second for n≤10^7 on a single CPU core.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include a head-to-head comparison against competing LDP CDF estimation methods (e.g., Frequency Oracle, RAPPOR, or the square-wave MLE of Li et al. 2020). Instead, the empirical validation is structured around confirming the paper's own theoretical predictions: (1) the standardized maximum absolute error (SMAE = MAE × r·n^{1/3}/log n) remains bounded as n grows, confirming the O_p(r^{-1} n^{-1/3} log n) uniform rate; (2) the weighted MSE on finite grids converges to a χ²(κ) distribution, with 95% coverage rates reaching 0.947–0.956 and RCE within 0.995–1.010 for n≥10^5; (3) sampling with G=F (oracle knowledge of the true distribution) yields only marginal improvement over uniform sampling (e.g., at n=10^7, r=0.25: 0.019 vs. 0.019 MAE for U(0,1)), suggesting uniform sampling is practically sufficient. The key implicit comparison is against the non-private ECDF rate of O_p(n^{-1/2}): the LDP estimator under density-based sampling is slower (n^{-1/3}) but recovers the parametric rate on finite grids, and the r^{-1} factor quantifies the privacy cost (e.g., r=0.25 multiplies error by 4× compared to r=0.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws a conceptual bridge between Local Differential Privacy (via randomized response) and the current status problem in survival analysis, repurposing the nonparametric maximum likelihood estimation framework of Huang & Wellner (1997) and the grid-based isotonic inference procedure of Tang et al. (2012). It departs from prior LDP CDF estimation approaches (Frequency Oracle, RAPPOR, Li et al. 2020) that relied on discretization/bucketing, instead formulating the problem as constrained isotonic regression on continuous data. The key insight is that the randomized-response binary responses can be reinterpreted as truthful observations from an alternative variable X*, disentangling the privacy mechanism from the statistical estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a deterministic, hyperparameter-free algorithm for full CDF estimation under LDP with provable O(n^{-1/3}) consistency under density-based sampling and O(n^{-1/2}) asymptotic normality on finite grids, enabling direct construction of confidence intervals. This is practically significant because it eliminates the discretization artifacts and stopping-criteria hyperparameters that plagued prior bucketing-based methods. Open questions remain around density estimation (the estimator is non-differentiable), multivariate CDF extension, and bootstrap-based inference, which the authors identify as future directions.
