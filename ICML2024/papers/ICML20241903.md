## Problem

Prior work on the coupled dynamics of user choice and model retraining (Ginart et al., 2021; Dean et al., 2024) assumed full information where all services observe the entire data distribution, but realistic digital platforms operate in a streaming, decentralized setting where each service only sees data from the users who happen to choose it. This creates a highly non-stationary, partial-information learning problem with no existing convergence guarantees for decentralized gradient-based algorithms under bounded-rational user selection.

## Input / Output

At each time step, a single user data point x ~ P arrives and is assigned to one of k services via bounded rationality: with probability 1−ζ the user picks the lowest-loss model, and with probability ζ picks uniformly at random. Each service observes only the data points of users who selected it (not the full population), and the distribution of incoming data shifts as model parameters evolve. The output is the tuple of model parameters Θ^t = (θ_1^t, …, θ_k^t), with the overall loss f(Θ) = (1−ζ)·f_PR(Θ) + ζ·f_NP(Θ) serving as the learning objective that quantifies social welfare.

## Method

MSGD (Multi-learner Streaming Gradient Descent) is a simple decentralized algorithm: when a user arrives and selects service i, that service performs a single gradient descent step θ_i ← θ_i − η·∇ℓ(x, θ_i) on the one observed data point, while all other services remain unchanged. The key theoretical insight is connecting streaming data and user choice to induced sub-populations X_i(Θ), which allows the gradient of the overall loss to decompose as ∇_{θ_i} f(Θ) = (1−ζ)·a_i(Θ)·E_{x~D_i}[∇ℓ(x,θ_i)] + (ζ/k)·E_{x~P}[∇ℓ(x,θ_i)], meaning each service only needs its own local gradient. The convergence proof (to stationary points of f(Θ) almost surely) leverages a directional-derivative technique from online k-means (So et al., 2022) to handle the non-convexity introduced by the Θ-dependent partition boundaries, combined with martingale convergence arguments on the supermartingale formed by the objective minus accumulated smoothness terms.

## Results

The paper evaluates MSGD on two real-world tasks: movie recommendation (MovieLens-10M, squared loss, k=3 services) and employment prediction (ACSEmployment census data, logistic loss). Convergence of the overall loss f(Θ) is shown on a log-log scale, indicating polynomial decay in t. MSGD achieves lower overall loss than the Full Information baseline when users are highly rational (small ζ), but higher loss when ζ is large, since data sharing accelerates learning. On the ACSEmployment task, MSGD achieves higher accuracy on each model's induced subpopulation D_i(Θ), with accuracy increasing as the number of services k grows (k=2, 4, 6), while Full Information accuracy on the whole population P remains roughly constant regardless of k.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is a Full Information algorithm in which all k models perform a gradient step on every arriving data point regardless of user selection (i.e., data is shared across services). MSGD outperforms Full Information on the overall loss f(Θ) when user rationality is high (small ζ), because Full Information prevents models from specializing to their own induced subpopulations. However, when ζ is large (users choose nearly at random), Full Information achieves lower overall loss because each model receives comparably more data per unit time. On the ACSEmployment task, MSGD achieves higher accuracy on each model-specific subpopulation D_i(Θ) than Full Information, and this subpopulation accuracy increases with k, whereas Full Information's accuracy is nearly invariant to k since all models converge to the same minimizer of the population loss. Conversely, on the whole population P, Full Information consistently outperforms MSGD, and MSGD's whole-population accuracy can even decrease with more training steps as models over-specialize.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-learner retraining framework of Dean et al. (2024) and Ginart et al. (2021), which characterized emergent specialization under user choice and model updates, to the realistic streaming setting where each service observes only one data point per step from a non-stationary, choice-induced distribution. The learning objective generalizes performative prediction (Perdomo et al., 2020) to multiple competing services with bounded-rational users. The gradient computation for the induced-subpopulation objective borrows the directional-derivative decoupling technique from So et al. (2022) on online k-means convergence, and the convergence proof adapts martingale supermartingale arguments from stochastic nonconvex optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first convergence guarantee (to stationary points of the overall social-welfare loss) for a decentralized streaming algorithm in a multi-service user-choice setting, filling a gap left by prior full-information analyses. The empirical demonstration that MSGD achieves higher subpopulation accuracy than full-information training when users are highly rational—despite receiving far less data—quantifies a practically important specialization-vs.-global-accuracy trade-off relevant to deployment and regulation. Open questions include theoretical analysis under Boltzmann-rational user behavior (empirically convergent but unproven), alternative objectives like market share or population-level fairness, and whether coordination or explicit competition mechanisms could improve social welfare beyond the decentralized MSGD updates studied here.
