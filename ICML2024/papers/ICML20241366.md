## Problem

Applying spiking neural networks to time-series forecasting remains underexplored due to three key challenges: (1) existing SNN studies either neglect the temporal nature of spiking or oversimplify data encoding (e.g., repeating samples along the time axis), (2) aligning continuous time-series data with discrete spiking periods requires careful encoding to avoid information loss, and (3) there are no standardized guidelines for selecting SNN architectures tailored to time-series forecasting. This paper addresses all three gaps with a unified framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time-series sequence X ∈ R^{T×C} (T historical steps, C variates) and produces forecasted values Y ∈ R^{L×C} for L future steps. Critically, the input is first converted to binary spike trains of shape T_s × T × C via a spike encoder, where T_s sub-steps per time-series step provide temporal alignment (ΔT = T_s·Δt). The output is generated by a single linear projection from spiking hidden states, and the entire inference pipeline avoids floating-point multiply-and-accumulate operations to enable deployment on neuromorphic hardware.

## Method

The framework has three core components. First, temporal alignment divides each time-series step ΔT into T_s SNN sub-steps, bridging continuous data with discrete spiking. Second, two parametric spike encoders convert floating-point inputs to spike trains: a Delta encoder (capturing temporal changes via x_t − x_{t−1} through a linear layer) and a Convolutional encoder (capturing morphological shapes via learned conv kernels), both followed by batch normalization and LIF spiking neurons. Third, three ANN backbones are converted to SNN counterparts—Spike-TCN (membrane potentials reset per time step, SEW residual modules), Spike-RNN/Spike-GRU (membrane potentials persist across steps), and iSpikformer (iTransformer with Spikformer's spiking self-attention)—all trained end-to-end via surrogate gradients (arctangent approximation) with BPTT. The design strictly avoids floating-point MAC operations during inference, achieving ~70% energy reduction on 45nm neuromorphic hardware while matching or exceeding ANN baselines.

## Results

On four time-series forecasting benchmarks (Metr-la, Pems-bay, Solar, Electricity) with prediction horizons of 6–96 steps, iSpikformer achieves an average R² of 0.775 and average RSE of 0.476, nearly matching the state-of-the-art iTransformer (R²=0.776, RSE=0.480) while consuming ~66% less energy. Spike-RNN achieves an average R² of 0.768 and RSE of 0.503, significantly outperforming the GRU baseline (R²=0.733, RSE=0.573). Across all SNN variants, theoretical energy consumption on 45nm neuromorphic hardware is reduced by an average of 70.33% compared to their ANN counterparts, with Spike-GRU achieving the largest reduction at 75.05%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the statistical baselines ARIMA (R²=0.713) and GP (R²=0.705), all proposed SNNs substantially outperform, with iSpikformer achieving R²=0.775 and RSE=0.476. Compared to TCN (R²=0.770, RSE=0.483), Spike-TCN underperforms (R²=0.750, RSE=0.518), which the authors attribute to TCN's inability to persist membrane potentials across time steps, violating the SNN's event-driven nature. Against GRU (R²=0.733, RSE=0.573), both Spike-GRU (R²=0.759, RSE=0.517*) and Spike-RNN (R²=0.768*, RSE=0.503*) achieve statistically significant improvements, with Spike-RNN being the strongest RNN-based SNN. Versus Autoformer (R²=0.753, RSE=0.569), iSpikformer clearly wins on both metrics (R²=0.775, RSE=0.476). Most notably, iSpikformer nearly matches iTransformer (R²=0.776, RSE=0.480), with only a 0.001 R² gap and a 0.004 RSE advantage, while consuming 66.30% less energy per sample.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SNN literature from image/text classification and time-series classification into the underexplored domain of time-series forecasting, addressing three gaps left by prior work: (1) temporal alignment between continuous data and discrete spiking periods, (2) meaningful spike encoding beyond crude repetition or delta-only strategies, and (3) standardized model selection across architectures. It builds directly on Spikformer's spiking self-attention mechanism, the SEW residual module for gradient stability in SNNs, and the delta modulation concept for event-driven encoding, while adapting TCN, GRU, and iTransformer architectures to their spiking counterparts. The paper departs from prior SNN-for-forecasting efforts (Reid et al. 2014, Kulkarni et al. 2013) that used simple architectures or focused on hardware deployment, and from ANN time-series forecasting work by introducing a hardware-friendly, event-driven alternative with no floating-point MAC operations during inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~70% average energy reduction while maintaining near-SOTA accuracy (iSpikformer within 0.001 R² of iTransformer) makes SNNs a practically viable option for energy-constrained forecasting deployments on neuromorphic hardware. The finding that temporal state tracking is critical—Spike-RNN/GRU outperform their ANN baselines while Spike-TCN underperforms TCN—provides a useful design principle for future SNN architectures on sequential tasks. Key open questions remain: the spike decoder still relies on a floating-point linear layer (incompatible with pure spiking inference), the framework is acknowledged as engineering rather than theoretically grounded, and parallel training for TCN-like models without resetting membrane potentials is unresolved. The paper also leaves open whether spiking graph neural networks could capture spatial correlations in multivariate time series.
