## Problem

The deadly triad—off-policy data, function approximation, and bootstrapping—causes temporal difference learning to diverge, and existing remedies (L2 regularization, gradient TD, residual minimization) either require strong distributional assumptions that fail even for expected updates, converge slowly, or yield estimates worse than zero initialization. This paper asks whether two simple, individually insufficient augmentations—a target network and over-parameterized linear features—can together provide provable convergence without such restrictive conditions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline dataset of transitions (s, a, r, s') or complete trajectories under a behavior policy μ, a target policy π, and a full-rank linear feature matrix Φ whose dimension d exceeds the number k of distinct state-actions observed in the data. For trajectory data, terminal states are augmented with zero-reward self-loop transitions, and per-step normalized importance sampling ratios correct only the next-action distribution (not the state distribution). The output is a parameter vector θ* whose product with Φ yields value estimates, with high-probability error bounds decomposed into statistical, projection, and approximation components.

## Method

Over-parameterized Target TD (OTTD) updates a student parameter θ_t via semi-gradient steps on the empirical mean squared Bellman error, using a frozen target parameter θ_targ refreshed every m steps. The key theoretical result is that convergence requires only that the projection matrix NM† (where M = HΦ and N = H P̂_π Φ) have a sub-multiplicative norm ≤ 1, eliminating the spectral-radius condition on I − ηMᵀD_k(M − γN) that causes divergence in standard over-parameterized TD. For expected updates over the full state-action space this condition holds automatically since NM† = P_π is stochastic; for trajectory data, per-step normalized importance sampling makes the empirical transition estimator stochastic, satisfying the condition without any assumptions on the task or features. The resulting fixed point is independent of the data collection distribution, removing the need for state-distribution correction, and the result extends to Q-learning by restricting the argmax to actions observed in the dataset.

## Results

On Baird's counterexample, standard TD diverges (convergence metric 1.12 > 1), while OTTD converges with a rate faster than Residual Minimization (1−1.9e-5) and GTD2 (1−4.5e-6), as shown in Figure 1. The theoretical value prediction error bound for OTTD with expected updates is ‖Φθ*_TD − q_π‖_∞ ≤ (2/(1−γ)) inf_θ ‖Φθ − q_π‖_∞, matching the on-policy under-parameterized TD bound up to a constant. On the Four Room task with off-policy trajectory data, per-step NIS correction achieves value prediction errors comparable to direct target-policy action sampling, while unnormalized IS diverges due to high variance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OTTD (over-parameterized target TD) outperforms all compared baselines on Baird's counterexample: it avoids the divergence of standard OTD (Xiao et al. 2021, metric 1.12) and converges visibly faster than both RM (Baird 1995, metric 1−1.9e-5) and GTD2 (Maei 2011, metric 1−4.5e-6), as shown in Figure 1. Compared to under-parameterized Target TD (Zhang et al. 2021, metric 1−3.8e-3), OTTD achieves a weaker convergence condition (‖NM†‖ ≤ 1 vs. spectral radius of (ΦᵀDΦ)⁻¹ΦᵀDγP_πΦ < 1) that is naturally satisfied for expected updates over the full state-action space, whereas the under-parameterized condition fails on a two-state counterexample even with expected updates. On the Four Room task, OTTD with NIS correction matches the value prediction error of OTTD with direct target-policy action sampling, while unnormalized IS correction diverges. The paper also extends to Q-learning (OTQ), showing convergence under the condition ‖Φ_iᵀM†‖_∞ < 1/γ, which is automatically satisfied for episodic trajectory data with maximization restricted to seen actions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work—over-parameterized TD (Xiao et al. 2021, Thomas 2022) and target-network-augmented TD (Zhang et al. 2021, Asadi et al. 2023, Fellows et al. 2023)—to show that their combination yields a strictly weaker convergence condition than either alone. It departs from prior target-network analyses (which required spectral-radius conditions on feature-dependent matrices that fail for certain off-policy distributions) by proving that over-parameterization eliminates the data-distribution dependency entirely. The NIS correction (Hesterberg 1995) is repurposed from off-policy evaluation to stabilize bootstrapping on trajectory data, and the Q-learning extension (Watkins & Dayan 1992) follows the same target + over-parameterized template with a constrained argmax over seen actions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provably convergent practical algorithm for off-policy TD with function approximation that also achieves competitive empirical performance, resolving the deadly triad without requiring state distribution correction—a longstanding source of high variance and bias in offline RL. The key limitation is confinement to linear function approximation; extending the convergence guarantees to neural networks (where target networks are most impactful empirically) is identified as the crucial next step. The NIS-based trajectory formulation also opens a path to handling continuing tasks, though the error bounds there are weaker (MSE rather than infinity norm).
