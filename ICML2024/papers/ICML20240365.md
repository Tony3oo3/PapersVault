## Problem

Input perturbation for differential privacy—adding noise to the data and projecting back to a valid output space—was previously thought to be a generic but potentially suboptimal approach compared to task-specific mechanisms. This paper shows that, surprisingly, input perturbation achieves the best known (and conjecturally optimal) privacy-utility tradeoffs for structured outputs such as cosine similarity matrices and k-way marginal tensors, with the error governed by the geometric structure of the target set rather than the ambient dimension.

## Input / Output

For cosine similarity, the input is a matrix V ∈ R^{n×m} of unit vectors, with adjacency defined by the Frobenius norm ‖VV^T − V′V′^T‖_F (which captures O(√n) entry-wise changes for ±1/√n vectors), and the output is an n×n matrix approximating the Gram matrix VV^T. For k-way marginals, the input is a dataset of m binary vectors in {0,1}^n (or t-sparse variants) where adjacency means replacing one vector, and the output is a k-th order tensor whose entries encode all k-way marginal counts; the algorithm runs in O(m)·n^{O(k)} time.

## Method

The perturb-and-project framework adds Gaussian noise calibrated to the sensitivity Δ, then projects the noisy input onto a compact convex set S containing the true answer; the resulting error scales with the Gaussian complexity G(S) of the target set rather than the ambient dimension, so structured sets (e.g., PSD matrices with bounded diagonal) yield guarantees significantly better than the naive Gaussian mechanism. When exact projection onto S is computationally hard (e.g., onto rank-m tensors for odd k), the paper replaces S with a degree-O(k) sum-of-squares relaxation S′ whose Gaussian complexity is provably close to G(S), using tight SoS certificates for injective tensor norms of Gaussian tensors. For practical deployment, the single projection step is decomposed via von Neumann alternating projections onto simpler convex sets (e.g., the PSD cone and entry-wise clipping), with linear convergence requiring only O(log n) iterations. For sparse datasets, novel sum-of-squares certificates for sparse injective tensor norms yield improved guarantees whenever t ≤ n^{5/6}/log n, matching existing conditional lower bounds up to logarithmic factors.

## Results

For pair-wise cosine similarities (Theorem 2.1), the algorithm achieves expected Frobenius-norm squared error O(Δ·√log(2/δ)/ε)·n^(3/2), improving over the naive Gaussian mechanism by a √n factor. For k-way marginals (Theorem 2.2), the expected average query-wise squared error is O(m·n^(k/4)·√log(2/δ)/ε) for even k and O(m·(n^k·k·log n)^(1/4)·√log(2/δ)/ε) for odd k, matching the information-theoretic lower bound m·n^(1/4) (up to constants) for k=2. For t-sparse datasets (Theorem 2.3), the error improves to O(√log(2/δ)/ε)·m·(t^(3/2)/n)^k·√(k log n/t²) (even k) or O(√log(2/δ)/ε)·m·(t^(3/2)/n)^k·√(k log n) (odd k), yielding stronger guarantees whenever t ≤ n^(5/6)/log n.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the naive Gaussian mechanism, the perturb-and-project approach improves cosine similarity error by a √n factor (Theorem 2.1 vs. O(Δ/ε²)·n²) and improves k-way marginal error by a factor of roughly n^(k/2)·ε/√log(2/δ) in the regime m ≤ n^(3k/4). Against Talwar et al. (2014), this paper matches their even-k guarantees but strictly improves the odd-k case by a factor of n^(1/2)/(k log n), closing a gap that prior polynomial-time algorithms could not address. For k=2, the paper's bound O(m·n^(1/4)) matches the information-theoretic lower bound of Kasiviswanathan et al. (2010) up to constant factors, whereas prior polynomial-time algorithms (Talwar et al., 2014) achieved this only for even k. For sparse datasets, the paper's Theorem 2.3 yields strictly stronger guarantees than the dense Theorem 2.2 whenever t ≤ n^(5/6)/log n, a regime not addressed by prior work. The paper also notes that Kenthapadi et al. (2012) improves over the Gaussian mechanism only for vectors with o(n) non-zero entries, while Theorem 2.1 provides improvements for any set of unit vectors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads: the perturb-and-project framework from DP (Kifer et al., 2012; Dwork et al., 2009), sum-of-squares relaxations from robust statistics (d'Orsi et al., 2023; Hopkins et al., 2015), and alternating projections from convex analysis (Bauschke & Borwein, 1993). It departs from prior DP work by showing that the 'generic' input-perturbation approach—add Gaussian noise, then project back onto the admissible set—achieves the best known (and conjecturally optimal) guarantees for projection-based private release, rather than being a suboptimal fallback. For k-way marginals, it extends the even-k-only guarantees of Talwar et al. (2014) to odd k and further to t-sparse datasets via novel SoS certificates for sparse injective tensor norms (building on Choo & d'Orsi, 2021).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean geometric explanation for why simple practical DP subroutines (add noise, then iteratively project onto the PSD cone and clip entries) work well: the error is governed by the Gaussian complexity (metric entropy) of the target set, not by the dimension. This reframes the design of private algorithms as a problem of choosing the right projection set. Open questions include whether the SoS-based bounds are truly information-theoretically tight (the paper provides evidence but not proofs), whether the framework extends to non-convex or non-symmetric projection sets, and whether the information-computation gap for k-way marginals (linked to tensor norm hardness) can be closed. The practical algorithm requires only O(log n) alternating projection steps, making it immediately deployable.
