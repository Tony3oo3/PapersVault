## Problem

Prior fair GNN link prediction research has focused on dyadic fairness (parity between inter-group and intra-group links), neglecting within-group dynamics and the 'rich get richer' effect where high-degree nodes receive disproportionately higher link prediction scores. This paper asks how degree bias in networks specifically affects GCN link prediction within social groups, and how this preferential attachment bias can amplify existing degree and power disparities among subgroups (e.g., men vs. women within Computer Science).

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected graph with node features and two orthogonal social group partitions (e.g., academic field × gender), and produces inner-product link prediction scores for node pairs. The theoretical analysis characterizes these scores in terms of within-group degrees, while the fairness metric Δ^(b) outputs a scalar quantifying the disparity in mean LP scores between two subgroups (T^(1) vs. T^(2)) within each group S^(b). The fairness regularizer operates on sampled training edges during each epoch, computing Δ^(b) post-sigmoid over the cross-entropy loss's already-computed LP scores.

## Method

The paper proves via spectral graph theory that GCNs with a symmetric normalized filter (D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}) exhibit a within-group preferential attachment bias: the expected LP score between two nodes i, j in the same social group is approximately proportional to √(D̂_ii · D̂_jj), the geometric mean of their within-group degrees, when the group is well-separated and has high expansion. This is established by bounding entries of (D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2})^L via the spectral gap of the within-group normalized adjacency matrix, then combining with a first-order Taylor expansion of the GCN. The authors then define a within-group fairness metric Δ^(b) measuring the absolute difference in mean LP scores between two subgroups within S^(b), show it is driven by degree disparity between subgroups, and propose a training-time regularizer L_fair = (λ_fair/B)Σ_b Δ^(b) added to the standard cross-entropy loss. In contrast, GCNs with a random walk normalized filter (D^{-1}A) theoretically produce approximately constant LP scores within a group, though empirical results show moderate degree dependence remains.

## Results

Across 10 real-world network datasets, the theoretic LP score (proportional to √(D̂_ii·D̂_jj)) is a strong predictor of Φ_s GCN scores, with NRMSE as low as 0.025 (Twitch-DE) and PCC up to 0.917 (CS), while test AUC remains high (0.876–0.962). In contrast, the theoretic score is a much weaker predictor for Φ_r (random walk filter), with PCC dropping to 0.006–0.553, confirming the asymmetric PA bias. The proposed fairness regularizer reduces average within-group unfairness Δ^(b) substantially (e.g., NBA: 0.013→0.000; DBLP-Fairness: 0.145→0.072 for Φ_s at λ_fair=4.0) with only a modest test AUC drop (≤0.04).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper introduces a new form of unfairness (within-group preferential attachment bias) and explicitly states that no prior baselines exist for this metric. The primary empirical comparison is between the two GCN filter types: Φ_s (symmetric normalized) exhibits a strong PA bias (theoretic score predicts GCN scores with PCC 0.54–0.92), while Φ_r (random walk normalized) shows a much weaker association (PCC 0.01–0.55), consistent with the theoretical prediction that Φ_r's LP scores are approximately constant within groups. For the fairness regularizer, the paper compares λ_fair = 0 (no regularization) against λ_fair ∈ {1.0, 2.0, 4.0}: for Φ_s on DBLP-Fairness, Δ^(b) drops from 0.145 to 0.072 (λ_fair=4.0) at the cost of 0.037 AUC (0.778→0.741); on NBA, Δ^(b) drops from 0.013 to 0.000 with negligible AUC change. The authors note that existing LP fairness metrics (dyadic statistical parity, equal opportunity) are not directly applicable since they measure a different form of unfairness, and that even if inter-group and intra-group links are predicted at the same rate, high-degree nodes within a group can still be systematically favored.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GCN architecture of Kipf & Welling (2017) and the path-activation probability framework of Xu et al. (2018) to derive a spectral-graph-theoretic characterization of how degree bias propagates through GCN link prediction scores. It departs from prior fair GNN link prediction work (Li et al., 2021; Current et al., 2022), which focused on dyadic fairness (inter-group vs. intra-group parity), by introducing within-group fairness—disparities in LP scores among subgroups (e.g., men vs. women) within a single social group. The fairness regularizer adapts the regularization-based fairness approach of Kamishima et al. (2011) to the GNN LP setting, while the theoretical analysis draws on spectral properties of random walks within subgraphs, a tool not previously applied to GNN link prediction fairness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that a standard, widely-deployed GCN architecture inherently produces link prediction scores proportional to the geometric mean of within-group degrees, creating a 'rich get richer' dynamic that can amplify existing degree and power disparities in social, citation, and collaboration networks. The proposed within-group fairness metric and simple training-time regularizer offer a practical path toward mitigating this bias, with the regularizer reducing unfairness significantly without severe performance degradation. Key limitations include the theory's imperfect alignment with experiments for the random walk filter, the restriction to GCN with inner-product scoring in the theoretical analysis, and the inapplicability of the regularizer to frozen models. Open questions include extending the analysis to other GNN architectures, directed and heterophilic networks, and developing post-processing strategies.
