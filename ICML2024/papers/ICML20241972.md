## Problem

Spectral kernel networks (SKNs) rely on a spectral density function to capture data structure, but existing designs either reuse spectral densities of fixed kernels (amounting to kernel approximation rather than design) or assume Gaussian mixtures that ignore dependence structures between variables. This neglects a large portion of the hypothesis space and limits SKNs' ability to model complex inter-variable relationships in real-world data.

## Input / Output

CokeNet accepts feature vectors of varying dimensionality (80 to 2709 dimensions for UCI tabular/image datasets, 3×32×32 for CIFAR-10) and outputs class labels via a softmax head or continuous values for regression. The spectral kernel mapping internally operates on 2d-dimensional weight pairs (ω, ω′) sampled from a pseudo probability density, with the copula module producing a scalar density value per weight pair that modulates the cosine/sine feature activations. Network architectures follow n_feature × 512 × 256 × n_classes for real-world tasks.

## Method

CokeNet redefines the spectral density as p(ω,ω′) = c(ω,ω′)·p̂(ω,ω′) via Sklar's theorem, decomposing it into a learnable copula density c and a fixed pseudo probability density p̂ for initialization. The copula module is constructed from Archimedean copulas whose generator φ is expressed as a positive linear combination of exponentials (∑Aₖexp(−Mₖt)) per Bernstein's theorem, making it a one-layer neural network with exponential activations whose parameters are learned end-to-end. The copula density is then inserted into the spectral kernel mapping, yielding a cosine/sine-based feature map Φ(x) where each feature is weighted by c(ωₘ,ωₘ′), and L such layers are stacked to form the full network. Theoretical analysis demonstrates that the copula increases the entropy of spectral densities, tightens Rademacher complexity bounds (when ‖c²‖≤1 is enforced via regularization), and produces smoother loss landscapes with fewer local minima compared to plain SKNs.

## Results

On six UCI classification benchmarks, CokeNet achieves the best accuracy across all datasets, ranging from 0.6618 (DistalPhalanxTW) to 0.9135 (HandOutlines). The largest margin over the strongest baseline is +1.44% on DistalPhalanxTW (0.6618 vs. 0.6474 for DSKN/ASKL), while the smallest is +0.71% on Earthquakes (0.7553 vs. 0.7482 for DSKN/GRFF). On the supplementary CIFAR-10 image classification task, CokeNet reaches 81.72% accuracy, outperforming DSKN (81.48%), SRFF (73.59%), and CosNet (66.31%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CokeNet outperforms all five baselines on every one of the six UCI classification datasets. The closest competitor varies by dataset: SRFF is the strongest baseline on HandOutlines (0.9108 vs. CokeNet's 0.9135) and ProximalPhalanxOutlineCorrect (0.8866 vs. 0.9072), while DSKN and GRFF tie for best baseline on Earthquakes (0.7482 vs. 0.7553). GRFF consistently underperforms the other methods (e.g., 0.6131 on DistalPhalanxOutlineCorrect, 0.6459 on HandOutlines), which the authors attribute to its progressive layer-by-layer training strategy introducing unnecessary parameter disturbances. CosNet (complex-valued spectral kernel network) is competitive on some datasets (0.8049 on ProximalPhalanxTW) but lags significantly on others (0.6043 on Earthquakes and DistalPhalanxTW). On the supplementary CIFAR-10 task, CokeNet (81.72%) edges out DSKN (81.48%) by a narrow 0.24% margin, while substantially surpassing CosNet (66.31%) and SRFF (73.59%).

## Relation to Prior Work

CokeNet extends the Spectral Kernel Network (SKN) lineage initiated by DSKN (Xue et al., 2019) and grounded in Random Fourier Features (Rahimi & Recht, 2007), but departs from prior SKNs by redefining the spectral density through Sklar's theorem as a product of a copula density and a pseudo probability density. The copula module itself is adapted from Deep Archimedean Copulas (ACNet, Ling et al., 2020), where Bernstein's theorem parameterizes the Archimedean generator as a sum of exponentials, here repurposed as a learnable one-layer network. Unlike prior SKNs that either reuse fixed kernel spectral densities or assume Gaussian mixtures, CokeNet makes the dependence structure between spectral variables an explicit, end-to-end learnable component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine underexplored gap in SKN design—the choice and formulation of spectral density—by providing a principled, learnable mechanism (copulas) to capture inter-variable dependence that Gaussian mixture assumptions miss. The theoretical contributions (entropy decomposition, Wigner distribution analysis, Rademacher complexity bounds) give the approach a solid mathematical foundation. However, experimental validation is limited to small UCI tabular datasets and CIFAR-10, with only modest accuracy gains (typically 0.5–2 percentage points) over strong baselines like DSKN and SRFF. Open questions include scalability to high-dimensional or very large datasets, the behavior of the copula module in deep (multi-layer) settings, and whether the regularization constraint on copula density generalizes beyond the specific bound used here.
