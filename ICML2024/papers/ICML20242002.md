## Problem

Active feature acquisition (AFA) requires sequentially deciding which features to observe at inference time to balance prediction accuracy against acquisition cost. Existing approaches face a trilemma: deep RL methods struggle with the super-exponential state space and sparse rewards, generative surrogate models require learning complex conditional distributions, and greedy policies cannot capture jointly informative feature subsets. The paper introduces a nonparametric, non-greedy, and deployable oracle that sidesteps all three limitations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a partially observed feature vector x_o with known indices o, a training dataset D for kNN density estimation (k=5 by default), and a predictor ŷ capable of making predictions on arbitrary feature subsets (gradient boosted trees, either a dictionary of per-subset models or a single masked model for high dimensions). It outputs a sequential acquisition policy that selects one feature at a time until the expected loss reduction no longer justifies the cost α, then produces a final class prediction or, in the decision-making extension, an intervention a ∈ A. For high-dimensional settings (e.g., 784-d MNIST), the subset search is approximated by randomly sampling 10,000 candidate subsets rather than enumerating all 2^d possibilities.

## Method

The core contribution is the Acquisition Conditioned Oracle (ACO), defined by a novel objective that minimizes the expected prediction loss over all subsets v of remaining features, penalized by acquisition cost α·c(v;o), conditioned only on currently observed information (x_o, o). This makes the oracle non-greedy (it evaluates joint subsets rather than single features) and deployable (it never accesses unobserved features or the true label, unlike prior 'cheating' oracles). The practical Approximate ACO (AACO) replaces the unknown conditional distribution p(y, x_v | x_o) with a k-nearest-neighbors density estimate and approximates the subset search via random subsampling. A theoretical result shows the ACO policy's value lower-bounds the optimal AFA MDP value. The framework extends to decision-making by substituting the supervised loss with a learned Q-function and a partially-observed decision policy π_A(x_o), enabling cost-aware feature acquisition for treatment or recommendation tasks from observational data.

## Results

On the CUBE-0.3 synthetic dataset, AACO achieves near-optimal classification accuracy with under 5 features acquired on average, and the kNN+10k-subset approximation matches the full-search ACO-knn performance. On the 784-d MNIST, AACO reaches near 90% accuracy after approximately 10 pixel acquisitions, matching the SOTA of AMOPT. On the Open Bandit Dataset (1M+ instances, 65 features), the AACO decision policy agrees with the full-context policy over 90% of the time using only 20 features, with estimated value close to the full-context value function. On the Big Five Personality Test, AACO outperforms GSMRL and a Rescale baseline that uses ground-truth information to select questions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AACO outperforms JAFA (Shim et al., 2018) and GSMRL (Li & Oliva, 2021) across the real-world Gas, Grid, and MNIST-256 benchmarks, achieving higher accuracy at comparable or lower acquisition costs, despite using simple nonparametric kNN density estimates rather than deep RL or generative surrogate models. Against EDDI (Ma et al., 2018), a greedy VAE-based method, AACO also achieves superior accuracy on the real-world datasets, benefiting from its non-greedy subset optimization. On the high-dimensional 784-d MNIST, AACO matches the SOTA performance of AMOPT (Covert et al., 2023), a greedy mutual-information method, at near 90% accuracy with ~10 acquisitions, while most other baselines (JAFA, EDDI, GSM+Greedy) fail to scale to this dimensionality. Notably, GSMRL degrades in performance on 784-d MNIST relative to its 256-d variant, whereas AACO maintains or improves performance. On the Big Five Personality Test, AACO outperforms GSMRL by better leveraging feature dependencies without ground-truth annotations, and also beats a Rescale baseline that has access to ground-truth question relevance.

## Relation to Prior Work

The ACO is positioned as a third path between two dominant AFA paradigms: deep RL agents (JAFA, GSMRL) that struggle with the super-exponential state space and sparse rewards, and greedy policies (EDDI, GSM+Greedy, AMOPT) that cannot capture jointly informative feature subsets. It also departs from prior 'retrospective oracle' approaches (He et al., 2012; Madasu et al., 2022) by being deployable—operating only on information available to the agent rather than 'cheating' with the full feature vector and label. For the decision-making extension, the paper draws on the DTR/ITR causal inference framework (Murphy, 2003; Kosorok & Laber, 2019) to construct a partially-observed decision policy that plugs into the ACO acquisition loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a simple nonparametric pipeline (kNN density + random subset sampling + gradient boosted trees) matches or exceeds complex deep RL and generative-model baselines across prediction benchmarks and scales to 784-d MNIST where most prior AFA methods fail, offering a low-compute, interpretable alternative for cost-sensitive settings like medical triage or adaptive surveys. The extension to AFA for decision-making with observational data is, to the authors' knowledge, the first oracle-based approach in this setting, opening a new sub-problem. Open questions include characterizing when the ACO is strictly suboptimal relative to the full MDP, whether more sophisticated approximations (beyond kNN and random subsets) yield meaningful gains, and how the method behaves under non-uniform or instance-dependent acquisition costs.
