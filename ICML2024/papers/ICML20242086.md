## Problem

Graph-based policy learning (GPL) is the state-of-the-art approach for open ad hoc teamwork, where a single learner must collaborate with an unknown, dynamically changing set of teammates without prior coordination. However, GPL's joint Q-value representation over a coordination graph—decomposing value into individual and pairwise utilities—lacks a convincing theoretical explanation, limiting its applicability to safety-critical settings that demand trustworthy algorithms. This paper asks: what game-theoretic principle justifies this representation, and what additional constraints can be derived to improve learning?

## Input / Output

The learner receives a state observation s_t, per-agent observations extracted from the state, and the current team composition (a variable subset of agents that changes each timestep as teammates join or leave). Agent types are unobserved and must be inferred online via an LSTM-based type inference model. The output is a single discrete action for the learner, selected by maximizing its approximate action value, which marginalizes over teammates' predicted actions using a GNN-based agent model. The joint Q-value is structured as a sum over edges of the coordination graph (pairwise terms) plus a sum over nodes (individual terms), with the graph topology (star or complete) chosen per variant.

## Method

The paper formalizes open ad hoc teamwork as the Open Stochastic Bayesian Coalitional Affinity Game (OSB-CAG), extending the coalitional affinity game with Bayesian uncertainty over agent types and stochastic dynamics over a long horizon. It introduces the Dynamic Variational Strict Core (DVSC) as a solution concept: the learner's optimal policy is one that maximizes long-horizon social welfare (sum of all agents' preference rewards), which is proven equivalent to maintaining strict core stability of the temporary team. The key theoretical result (Theorem 2) shows that the joint Q-value decomposes exactly into pairwise terms Q_jk (discounted affinity weights, constrained to be non-negative and symmetric) plus individual terms Q_j, providing a game-theoretic justification for GPL's architecture. Building on this, CIAO adds two provable implementation tricks to GPL's fitted Q-learning loss: (1) a symmetry regularizer enforcing Q_jk = Q_kj ≥ 0, and (2) a graph-structure-dependent regularizer on individual utilities (for a star graph, the learner's individual Q equals the sum of teammates' individual Qs; for a complete graph, all individual Qs are equal). Two variants—CIAO-S (star graph, learner as hub) and CIAO-C (complete graph)—are trained with DQN and evaluated on Level-based Foraging and Wolfpack with up to 9 agents.

## Results

The paper evaluates CIAO-S (star graph) and CIAO-C (complete graph) against GPL on Wolfpack and LBF environments with maximum team sizes of 5 and 9 agents (trained with max 3 agents). CIAO-C outperforms GPL in the majority of scenarios across both environments and team sizes, validating the theoretical joint Q-value representation. The star graph (CIAO-S) is more effective with fewer agents, while the complete graph (CIAO-C) shows greater effectiveness with more agents, suggesting indirect influence through teammates becomes crucial as team size grows. Ablation studies confirm that enforcing non-negative pairwise utility (α ≥ 0) and non-zero individual utility are both critical for performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is GPL-Q (Rahman et al., ICML 2021), the state-of-the-art graph-based policy learning method for open ad hoc teamwork. CIAO-C outperforms GPL in the majority of scenarios across Wolfpack and LBF with both 5 and 9 maximum agents, demonstrating that the cooperative game theory-derived constraints (non-negative pairwise Q-values and symmetry regularizers) effectively narrow the joint Q-value hypothesis space and improve learning. CIAO-S also generally matches or exceeds GPL, with particular advantages in smaller-team settings. The ablation variants (CIAO-X-NP with negative pairwise utility, CIAO-X-ZI with zero individual utility, CIAO-X-FI without individual utility enforcement) all underperform their full counterparts, confirming that the theoretical constraints are not merely inductive biases but are necessary for the DVSC solution to exist within the learned representation. In agent-type generalization experiments (intersecting and mutually exclusive type sets between train and test), the star graph topology proves more generalizable than the complete graph, though both CIAO variants still outperform GPL overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the coalitional affinity game (CAG) framework from cooperative game theory by incorporating Bayesian games for uncertain agent types and stochastic games for long-horizon objectives, yielding the OSB-CAG model. It provides the first theoretical justification for GPL's (Rahman et al., 2021) heuristic joint Q-value decomposition over a coordination graph, showing it corresponds to the sum of agents' preference Q-values under a dynamic variational strict core (DVSC) solution concept. The algorithm CIAO is explicitly built on GPL's empirical framework, adding a provable regularizer and a non-negativity constraint on pairwise utilities derived from the theory, while also introducing a star-graph topology as an alternative to GPL's complete graph.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical grounding of GPL's joint Q-value representation addresses a key trustworthiness gap that limits deployment of ad hoc teamwork in safety-critical settings, and the DVSC concept offers a principled criterion for evaluating collaboration stability in open teams. However, the work is limited to fully observable environments with fixed teammate policies, and experiments cover only two relatively simple benchmarks (LBF and Wolfpack) with at most 9 agents. Open questions include whether the theory extends to time-varying agent types, partial observability, and whether the linear pairwise-plus-individual Q-value decomposition is the most expressive form for complex real-world coordination.
