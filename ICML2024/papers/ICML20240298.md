## Problem

Cooperative multi-agent RL problems in physical environments exhibit continuous E(3) Euclidean symmetries (translation, rotation, reflection), yet prior work has only exploited discrete rotation subgroups (C_n) or permutation invariance among homogeneous agents. No existing method provides a rigorous formalism for continuous E(3) multi-agent symmetries or an architecture that preserves equivariance under all E(3) transformations while operating under the CTDE paradigm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240298/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

States and observations are represented as 3D point clouds where each entity (agent, landmark, prey, robot joint) is a vertex with a 3D position and a feature vector (velocity, action, type label), connected by edges encoding relative positions. The centralized critic consumes the full state-action point cloud to produce a scalar value, while each local actor consumes its own observation point cloud (relative positions, own velocity, padded zeros for unobservable quantities) to output a local action vector. In SMAC, where actions are categorical, only the critic uses the SEGNN architecture while the actor remains an MLP.

## Method

The paper formalizes G-symmetric Markov games and proves (Theorem 4.4) that optimal value functions are group-invariant and that a group-invariant optimal policy exists, extending single-agent MDP homomorphism results to the multi-agent setting. To exploit these properties, it embeds steerable E(3)-equivariant GNNs (SEGNNs) as the actor-critic architecture: the centralized critic is E(3)-invariant (outputting a scalar value from the state-action point cloud) and the local actor is E(3)-equivariant (mapping observation point clouds to action vectors that transform consistently under E(3)). This inductive bias constrains the parameter search to the group-invariant subspace, reducing the effective hypothesis space and yielding symmetric policy gradients that make the optimization landscape more amenable to gradient-based search. The method is instantiated within MADDPG (MPE, MuJoCo) and MAPPO (SMAC) under CTDE, with point cloud representations naturally accommodating variable numbers of entities to enable zero-shot and transfer learning across different agent counts.

## Results

On MPE (Cooperative Navigation, Push, and Predator-Prey with N=3 and N=6 agents), the SEGNN-based critic alone ([SEGNN, MLP]) already outperforms both [MLP, MLP] and [GCN, MLP] baselines across all six scenarios, with further gains when the actor is also SEGNN-based ([SEGNN, SEGNN]) in Push_N3, Prey_N3, Navigation_N6, and Prey_N6. On MuJoCo tasks, [SEGNN, SEGNN] achieves faster convergence and better final performance in single/multi-agent reacher, swimmer, 3D hopper, and 3D walker, while matching [MLP, MLP] on cartpole (which has only rot_180° symmetry). On SMAC, the SEGNN-based critic improves performance in 6h_vs_8z (super hard) but not in 8m_vs_9m (hard), consistent with the imperfect E(3) symmetries in competitive scenarios. Zero-shot transfer from 3-agent to 6-agent MPE tasks shows the SEGNN architecture at step 0 already matches the final performance of [MLP, MLP] and [GCN, MLP] after 0.75M training steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against [MLP, MLP] (standard MADDPG), the proposed [SEGNN, SEGNN] and [SEGNN, MLP] methods achieve significantly higher returns across all MPE scenarios and most MuJoCo tasks (reacher, swimmer, hopper, walker), with the gap being largest in tasks with strong continuous E(3) symmetries; on cartpole (sparse symmetry) performance is comparable. Against [GCN, MLP] (PIC, Liu et al. 2020), the SEGNN-based methods consistently outperform on MPE and MuJoCo, and notably the GCN-based critic shows no improvement in rotation/translation invariancy during training, whereas the SEGNN-based critic maintains inherent invariancy by construction. On SMAC, against MAPPO-[MLP, MLP], the proposed MAPPO-[SEGNN, MLP] wins on 6h_vs_8z but does not improve on 8m_vs_9m, reflecting the limited E(3) symmetry in competitive settings with categorical actions. Against QMIX (Rashid et al. 2020a) on SMAC, results are mixed with no clear advantage, again attributed to the imperfect symmetry of the competitive environment and the non-Euclidean categorical action space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-agent symmetry exploitation line (Ravindran & Barto 2001; Van der Pol et al. 2020; Wang et al. 2022) to the multi-agent setting, and specifically departs from prior multi-agent Euclidean symmetry work (van der Pol et al. 2022; Yu et al. 2023) by handling continuous E(3) transformations rather than discrete C_n rotation subgroups. Architecturally, it repurposes steerable equivariant GNNs (SEGNNs, Brandstetter et al. 2022) — previously applied to molecular/physics prediction — as the actor-critic backbone within standard CTDE frameworks (MADDPG, MAPPO). The theoretical contribution (Theorem 4.4) generalizes MDP homomorphism properties to multi-agent games with distributed symmetries, providing the first rigorous justification for equivariant observation-based policies in the multi-agent setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, architecture-level solution to a common practical need in physical-world MARL (robotics, games, simulation): exploiting spatial symmetries without data augmentation. The zero-shot transfer from 3-agent to 6-agent scenarios is a practically compelling result that reduces training cost for variable-agent-count problems. Key limitations include the requirement to manually specify the symmetry group (no automatic discovery) and the restriction to non-recurrent architectures, which limits applicability to partially observable tasks requiring memory. Open questions include whether equivariant recurrent architectures can be constructed, and whether approximate or automatically discovered symmetries yield similar benefits.
