## Problem

Pure LLM-based agents exhibit intrinsic bias in their action distributions inherited from pretraining data, leading to exploitable and suboptimal play in competitive multi-agent games. Even when the LLM correctly reasons about the optimal strategy (e.g., uniform random play in Rock-Paper-Scissors), its actual action selection deviates systematically, and this bias becomes more damaging in complex social deduction games where opponents can predict and counteract it. The paper addresses this gap by asking how to build language agents that generate flexible, unbounded natural-language actions while possessing strong, bias-free decision-making ability.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural-language game observation (game history with announcements, discussion transcripts, and voting results) along with its own hidden role, player ID, and secret night actions. This raw observation is first structured into an information record (categorized as facts, potential truths, or potential deceptions) and a per-player deduction result (inferred role, reliability score 1–10, reasoning trace, and cited evidence indices). The output is a single language action sampled from N=3 diverse candidates generated by the LLM; the action type depends on the game phase and can be a secret action (kill/see/save a specific player), a free-form discussion statement, or a vote (for a player or abstain). Because the action space is unbounded natural language rather than a fixed finite set, the policy must operate over dynamically generated candidates.

## Method

The framework combines three components: (1) an LLM-based hidden role deduction module that structures observations and infers each player's role with confidence and evidence; (2) a diverse action generation module that prompts the LLM for N=3 strategically distinct action candidates (using single-shot prompting for secret/voting actions and iterative prompting for statement actions); and (3) a population-based RL training loop that learns a small self-attention policy to select among the candidates. The policy encodes the observation, action candidates, and a player-specific vector via text-embedding-ada-002, processes them through a residual self-attention block, and produces a selection distribution via scaled dot-product attention between the state embedding and each action embedding. Training uses MAPPO against a population of diverse agents (past RL checkpoints plus pure LLM agents with predefined play styles), initialized with behavior cloning, to achieve robustness against non-transitive opponent strategies without fine-tuning the LLM itself.

## Results

In the round-robin tournament, the proposed strategic language agent achieves the highest Villager win rates against all opponents (0.46 vs ReAct, 0.39 vs ReCon, 0.40 vs Concurrent, 0.36 vs Atomic) and the best Werewolf performance (lowest opponent Villager win rates: 0.16, 0.20, 0.22, 0.27). In human-agent experiments, the agent achieves an overall win rate of 0.50 when playing with six copies of itself (vs. 0.48 for humans) and 0.52 when playing with six human players (matching the human baseline of 0.52), demonstrating human-level strategic play.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three pure LLM-based agents (ReAct, ReCon, Concurrent) and one LLM+RL agent (Atomic, inspired by Cicero). As Villagers, the proposed method dominates all baselines: it achieves 0.46 win rate against ReAct Werewolves (vs. ReAct's own 0.22 self-play), 0.39 against ReCon Werewolves (vs. ReCon's 0.27 self-play), and 0.40 against Concurrent Werewolves (vs. Concurrent's 0.26 self-play). As Werewolves, the proposed method also achieves the lowest opponent win rates across all matchups (0.16–0.30), outperforming ReAct (0.19–0.46), ReCon (0.20–0.39), and Concurrent (0.19–0.40). The Atomic agent, which also combines RL with an LLM but uses a fixed set of 13 predefined atomic actions, is the closest competitor yet still trails the proposed method by 0.03–0.10 in Villager win rate, confirming that the unbounded language action space with diverse generation is critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Cicero (Meta et al., 2022) paradigm of combining LLMs with RL for strategic game play, but departs critically by replacing Cicero's fixed, finite set of predefined atomic actions with a dynamic, unbounded language action space generated on-the-fly by the LLM. It also builds on population-based training methods (PSRO, league training) from Go, poker, and StarCraft, adapting them to the setting where the action space is a set of natural language strings rather than vectorized indices. Compared to pure LLM-based agents (ReAct, ReCon, Concurrent), the key conceptual departure is recognizing that correct reasoning does not guarantee unbiased action selection, and addressing this with a separate RL policy trained over LLM-generated candidates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, well-evidenced demonstration that LLMs exhibit exploitable action-selection bias even when their reasoning is correct, and that a lightweight RL policy over LLM-generated candidates can effectively eliminate this bias in a complex social deduction game. The zero-shot transfer of the RL policy across different LLMs (GPT-4, LLaMA-7B, ChatGLM-6B) suggests the decision-making component is decoupled from the language model, which is practically useful. Open questions include whether the framework extends to multi-modal settings (tone, facial expressions), whether it generalizes to games with significantly different structures, and whether the agents' language style can be made indistinguishable from human players without human data.
