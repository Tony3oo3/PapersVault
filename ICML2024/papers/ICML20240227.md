## Problem

Backward error analysis has revealed that finite step sizes in gradient descent implicitly regularize solutions by penalizing the two-norm of the loss gradient, but no analogous characterization existed for adaptive optimizers like Adam and RMSProp. This paper asks: what implicit bias does Adam's finite step size introduce, and does it regularize or anti-regularize the training trajectory?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240227/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a loss function E(θ) (or mini-batch losses E_k(θ)) with bounded partial derivatives up to order 4, along with Adam's hyperparameters (step size h, gradient momentum β, squared-gradient momentum ρ, and stability parameter ε). It produces a continuous piecewise ODE whose solution θ̃(t) tracks the discrete Adam iterates to O(h²) global error, with the key structural output being an explicit correction term proportional to ∇‖∇E(θ)‖_{1,ε} — the gradient of the perturbed one-norm — whose sign (penalizing vs. anti-penalizing) depends on the relative magnitudes of β and ρ and the training stage.

## Method

The paper applies backward error analysis to the discrete Adam update by expanding the exponential moving averages (for both the first and second moments) as formal power series in h via Taylor expansion of the gradient along the trajectory. Matching the resulting O(h²) discrete update to a modified ODE discretization yields a piecewise ODE (one per mini-batch) with an explicit h-linear correction term. In the full-batch limit, this correction simplifies to a term proportional to ∇_j ‖∇E(θ)‖_{1,ε} weighted by {(1+β)/(1−β) − (1+ρ)/(1−ρ)}. The central finding is that for the typical hyperparameter regime ρ > β and small ε, this term anti-penalizes the perturbed one-norm of the gradient (the opposite of GD's two-norm penalty), providing a mechanistic explanation for Adam's tendency to find sharper minima and generalize worse. Numerical experiments on ResNet-50, CNNs, and Vision Transformers trained on CIFAR-10 confirm that stronger predicted anti-regularization correlates with lower test accuracy.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) provides a global second-order-in-h piecewise ODE approximation to mini-batch Adam, showing that the discrete trajectory stays within Ch² of the ODE solution over any fixed time horizon. In the full-batch, small-ε regime (typical during most of training), the implicit bias takes the form of anti-regularization: Adam penalizes the *negative* perturbed one-norm ‖∇E(θ)‖_{1,ε} when ρ > β (the standard hyperparameter setting), which is the opposite of GD's implicit two-norm regularization. Empirically on CIFAR-10 with ResNet-50, increasing ρ (strengthening anti-regularization) increases the perturbed one-norm and decreases test accuracy, while increasing β (strengthening regularization) decreases the norm and increases test accuracy, consistent with the theory across ResNets, CNNs, and Vision Transformers.

| | ε "small" | ε "large" |

| β ≥ ρ | ‖∇E(θ)‖₁ penalized (regularization) | ‖∇E(θ)‖₂² penalized |

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark Adam against competing optimizers in a standard accuracy-vs-baseline sense; instead, it contrasts Adam's implicit bias with that of GD and heavy-ball momentum GD. For GD, backward error analysis (Barrett & Dherin, 2021; Ghosh et al., 2023) yields an implicit penalty on the squared two-norm ‖∇E‖₂², which encourages flatter minima. Adam, by contrast, typically anti-penalizes the perturbed one-norm ‖∇E‖_{1,ε} (when ρ > β and ε is small), which the authors connect to ℓ∞-sharpness and thus to the well-documented tendency of adaptive methods to find sharper minima and generalize worse (Chen et al., 2018; Wilson et al., 2017; Zhou et al., 2020). The empirical validation on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-50, CNNs, ViTs) confirms the predicted direction: larger ρ/β ratio → higher perturbed one-norm → lower test accuracy, while the ODE approximation itself is verified to track the Adam trajectory to O(h²) on a 10⁵-parameter MLP trained on MNIST. The paper also notes that in the large-ε or very-late-training regime, Adam's bias reverts to the same two-norm penalty as GD, which may explain why Adam sometimes outperforms SGD for transformers where training spends long periods near interpolating solutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the backward error analysis framework—previously applied to full-batch GD (Barrett & Dherin, 2021), mini-batch GD (Smith et al., 2021), and heavy-ball momentum GD (Ghosh et al., 2023)—to the adaptive optimizers Adam and RMSProp. It builds directly on Ma et al. (2022), who showed the zeroth-order continuous-time limit of Adam/RMSProp is a perturbed signGD flow, by deriving a first-order (linear in h) correction term that reveals the implicit bias. The key conceptual departure from prior work is that the correction involves the perturbed one-norm of the gradient rather than the two-norm, and for typical hyperparameters (ρ > β) it acts as anti-regularization rather than regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides a concrete theoretical mechanism for the well-observed but poorly understood phenomenon that adaptive gradient methods generalize worse than SGD: Adam's implicit anti-regularization of the perturbed one-norm biases training toward sharper minima (higher ℓ∞-sharpness). Open questions include whether the bounded-Hessian assumption (standard in backward error analysis) holds near the edge of stability, how the theory behaves in the large-spike regime of Adam, and why Adam sometimes outperforms SGD (e.g., for transformers) despite the anti-regularization effect. The constant in the error bound diverges as ε→0, limiting the theory's applicability for very small stability parameters at later training stages.
