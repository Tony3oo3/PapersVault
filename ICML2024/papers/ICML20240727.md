## Problem

Deep RL agents struggle to learn effective control policies for high-dimensional, overactuated systems such as musculoskeletal models with hundreds of muscles, where the action space dimensionality causes sample efficiency to collapse. While the neuroscience concept of muscle synergies suggests that coordinated actuator groups simplify motor command generation, no prior method has derived such synergistic representations directly from the dynamical structure of the system itself, instead relying on pre-training trajectories or learned latent spaces that are costly and task-dependent.

## Input / Output

The method takes proprioceptive state (joint positions/velocities, muscle forces/lengths/velocities/activations) and task-specific observations (target positions, object states, gait phase) as input, and produces structured muscle activation signals: a unified action per synergistic group plus state-dependent correction weights per individual actuator, rather than independent activations for all m muscles. The action dimensionality is thus reduced from m to N_g + (m − N_g) with the group actions sharing a common value, and the correction weights are bounded by a schedule that relaxes over training timesteps to ensure early stability.

## Method

DynSyn first generates a synergistic representation by applying random joint-velocity perturbations to the musculoskeletal model, collecting muscle length trajectories, computing a pairwise cosine-similarity correlation matrix over segmented trajectories, and clustering muscles via K-Medoids into N_g functionally coherent groups. During RL training (built on SAC), the policy outputs a unified Gaussian action per group and state-dependent correction weights per actuator; the final muscle activation is the element-wise product of the group action and the clipped correction weight, with the correction boundary gradually relaxing from zero to full range over training. This structure prevents the agent from assigning contradictory actions to dynamically similar muscles while preserving per-actuator expressiveness for task-specific fine-tuning, yielding interpretable groupings that generalize across tasks and environmental conditions for the same model.

## Results

DynSyn achieves higher returns in fewer training steps across all six standard environments (FullBody-Gait with 700 muscles, Legs-Walk with 100, MyoLegs-Walk with 80, Arm-Locate with 81, MyoHand-Reorient100 with 39, and Ostrich-Run with 120). Critically, DynSyn is the only algorithm that achieves functional motor control in the 700-dimensional FullBody-Gait environment, where all baselines fail. In generalization tasks (rugged terrain, diagonal walking, fast walking), the same pre-generated synergistic representations transfer without retraining, and DynSyn maintains strong performance. Ablation studies confirm that the dynamical grouping (40 clusters for Legs) consistently outperforms random grouping, with lower variance across seeds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynSyn is compared against three baselines: SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018), SAR (Berg et al., 2023), and DEP-RL (Schumacher et al., 2023). DynSyn outperforms all three across every standard environment, with the gap widening as action dimensionality increases — in the 700-dim FullBody-Gait task, SAC, SAR, and DEP-RL all fail to learn functional gait while DynSyn succeeds. SAR requires a separate pre-training collection phase (M steps) before its training phase (N steps), and even with this overhead it underperforms DynSyn in both standard and generalization settings. DEP-RL, an exploration-based method, also trails DynSyn in convergence speed and final return. In the generalization experiments (Legs-Walk-Diagnal, Legs-Walk-Fast, MyoLegs-Walk-Hilly, MyoLegs-Walk-Rough), DynSyn reuses the same synergistic representation generated from the standard environment and still outperforms all baselines, demonstrating that the dynamical grouping captures task-agnostic structural features rather than task-specific patterns.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynSyn builds on SAC as its base RL algorithm and draws conceptual inspiration from the neuroscience literature on muscle synergies (d'Avella et al., 2003; Tresch & Kiehn, 2000), which posits that coordinated muscle groups simplify motor command generation. It departs from prior overactuated-control methods like SAR (Berg et al., 2023), which learns synergistic representations from pre-training trajectories, and DEP-RL (Schumacher et al., 2023), which improves exploration, by instead deriving synergistic groupings directly from the system's dynamical structure via random joint-velocity perturbations and K-Medoids clustering on muscle-length correlations. The state-dependent correction weights layered on top of grouped actions represent a novel adaptation mechanism not present in either baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DynSyn demonstrates that a 700-muscle full-body musculoskeletal model can be controlled by RL where prior methods fail, and that the same dynamically-derived representation generalizes across tasks (terrain changes, walking direction) without retraining, suggesting the representation captures intrinsic structural properties rather than task-specific features. Key limitations include reliance on proprioception alone (no vision or touch), the fact that only one solution among many valid control patterns is found, and the gap between simulation and real embodied systems. Open questions include whether the representation space can be expanded to capture the full solution manifold of overactuated systems and how to integrate additional sensory modalities for real-world deployment.
