## Problem

Existing SBDD generative models produce molecules with deceptively high binding affinities but physically infeasible 3D conformations (false positives). The paper identifies two root causes: autoregressive methods suffer mode collapse from unnatural atom ordering, and diffusion-based models struggle to denoise in a hybrid continuous-discrete space (continuous coordinates + discrete atom types), leading to distorted geometries and poor binding modes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241697/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a protein binding site represented as a set of atoms with 3D coordinates and per-atom features (one-hot element type, amino acid type, backbone/side-chain flag, arm/scaffold indicator), centered at the origin for SE(3) invariance. It outputs a 3D ligand molecule as a set of atoms with continuous 3D coordinates and discrete element types (C, N, O, F, P, S, Cl), where the number of atoms is fixed at inference time. The protein-ligand interaction is modeled via a k-nearest-neighbor graph (k=32) plus a fully-connected ligand graph for bond-type encoding.

## Method

MolCRAFT formulates molecule generation as a Bayesian Flow Network operating entirely in a continuous parameter space: atom coordinates are tracked via Gaussian parameters (mean μ, precision ρ) and atom types via continuous categorical parameters, with closed-form Bayesian updates replacing the need to invert a predefined forward process. The backbone Φ is an SE(3)-equivariant PosNet3D network (9 layers, 128-dim hidden, 16-head attention) that reconstructs the clean molecule from current parameters, pocket features, and timestep. The key sampling innovation is a noise-reduced strategy that bypasses the standard BFN step of sampling noisy data for the Bayesian update; instead, the network's continuous output (μ̂, v̂) directly updates the parameters via the flow distribution p_F, eliminating the high-variance discrete sampling at each intermediate step. This yields a 30× speedup (100 steps vs. 1000) while achieving reference-level Vina Score (−6.59 kcal/mol) and the best RMSD (41.8% below 2 Å) among all baselines.

## Results

On the CrossDocked test set (100 proteins, 100 molecules each), MolCRAFT achieves a reference-level Vina Score of −6.59 kcal/mol (vs. −6.36 for reference), outperforming the strongest baseline (AR, −5.75) by 0.84 kcal/mol. It also leads in conformational stability with a median Strain Energy of 195 kcal/mol (vs. 1,243 for TargetDiff and 983 for Decomp-O) and the best RMSD consistency (41.8% of poses within 2 Å of redocked poses, vs. 34.0% for reference). Overall Binding Feasibility reaches 33.8% and Success Rate 26.8%, both exceeding the reference set (29.0% and 25.0% respectively). Sampling is 30× faster than TargetDiff (141 s vs. 3,428 s for 100 molecules) while maintaining 96.7% generation success.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MolCRAFT outperforms all five baselines on direct pose quality (Vina Score): −6.59 vs. AR (−5.75), Pocket2Mol (−5.14), TargetDiff (−5.47), Decomp-R (−5.19), and Decomp-O (−5.67), a margin of 0.84 kcal/mol over the best baseline. On conformational stability, MolCRAFT's median Strain Energy (195) is an order of magnitude better than diffusion baselines (TargetDiff: 1,243; Decomp-O: 983) and competitive with Pocket2Mol (189), while achieving far superior binding affinity. For binding mode consistency (RMSD < 2 Å), MolCRAFT reaches 41.8%, surpassing reference (34.0%), AR (31.1%), Pocket2Mol (30.8%), TargetDiff (29.4%), and Decomp-O (23.9%); FLAG is an outlier at 8.2% due to severe steric clashes (40.83 avg). On overall feasibility (BF) and success rate (SR), MolCRAFT (33.8% / 26.8%) exceeds reference (29.0% / 25.0%) and all baselines, with the closest competitor being Pocket2Mol (24.6% / 24.4%). The paper also shows that at matched molecular sizes (Ours-small vs. AR/Pocket2Mol/FLAG at ~17–18 atoms; Ours-large vs. Decomp-O at ~29 atoms), MolCRAFT consistently achieves the best Vina Score, confirming that size-controlled comparisons are essential for fair evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MolCRAFT builds directly on Bayesian Flow Networks (BFN) by Graves et al. (2023), transplanting the sender-receiver parameter-space formulation into the SBDD setting for the first time. It departs from both autoregressive SBDD methods (AR, Pocket2Mol, FLAG), which the paper diagnoses as suffering from mode collapse due to unnatural atom ordering, and diffusion-based SBDD methods (TargetDiff, DecompDiff), which struggle with the hybrid continuous-discrete denoising space. The key conceptual move is unifying atom coordinates and atom types into a single continuous parameter space with smooth Bayesian updates, combined with a noise-reduced sampling strategy that bypasses discrete variable sampling at intermediate steps. The SE(3) equivariant backbone (PosNet3D) follows the standard equivariant GNN design used across prior SBDD work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical credibility gap in SBDD: generated molecules that score well after redocking but have unrealistic conformations (the 'false positive' problem), which undermines the practical utility of 3D generative models for drug design. Achieving reference-level Vina Score (-6.59 vs. -6.36 kcal/mol) with a 30× sampling speedup over diffusion baselines is a practically significant milestone, suggesting that parameter-space generation could become a competitive alternative to diffusion for molecular design. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the CrossDocked benchmark (100 test proteins), whether the noise-reduced sampling strategy transfers to other BFN applications, and whether the single-dataset evaluation is sufficient to validate the claimed superiority in capturing true interatomic interactions.
