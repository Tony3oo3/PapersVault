## Problem

GP-UCB achieves sub-linear Bayesian cumulative regret (BCR) bounds but requires manual tuning of a confidence parameter that can be prohibitively large, while Thompson sampling (TS) is hyperparameter-free yet suffers from over-exploration in practice. Although a randomized variant of GP-UCB (IRGP-UCB) was recently shown to achieve tighter BCR bounds by removing a √(log T) factor, it was unclear whether TS or other posterior-sampling-based methods could match this tighter guarantee, and no hyperparameter-free alternative existed that also avoided over-exploration.

## Input / Output

The method takes a black-box objective function f, an input domain X (finite or a compact convex subset of [0,r]^d), a GP prior specified by a stationary kernel (RBF, Matérn, or linear), and an initial dataset of a few evaluations. It sequentially produces evaluation points x_t by optimizing a randomized acquisition function over the original continuous space (no algorithmic discretization), with posterior sampling approximated via random Fourier features for continuous domains. The output is the sequence of evaluated points and, implicitly, the recommended optimum x̂_T = argmax μ_{T-1}(x).

## Method

The paper proves that TS achieves the tighter BCR bound O(√(Tγ_T log|X|)) for finite domains (and O(√(Tγ_T log T)) for continuous domains) by establishing a key lemma bounding E[η_t² 1{η_t ≥ 0}] ≤ 2 + 2 log(|X|/2), where η_t is the normalized deviation of the sampled path at the chosen point. It then introduces PIMS (Probability of Improvement from Maximum of Sample path), which selects x_t = argmin_x (g_t* − μ_{t-1}(x))/σ_{t-1}(x) where g_t* is the maximum of a posterior sample path. PIMS is shown to be equivalent to a randomized GP-UCB whose confidence parameter ξ_t is data-driven (determined by g_t*) rather than set by worst-case analysis, making it hyperparameter-free while achieving the same tighter BCR bound. For continuous domains, the regret proofs use discretization only as an analytical tool: TS leverages Lipschitz continuity of the posterior standard deviation, while PIMS uses a lower bound on posterior variance, avoiding any discretization in the actual algorithm.

## Results

Theoretically, PIMS achieves a BCR bound of O(√(Tγ_T log T)) for continuous domains and O(√(Tγ_T log|X|)) for finite domains, matching the tighter bound of IRGP-UCB and improving upon GP-UCB's O(√(Tγ_T log(|X|T))). Empirically, PIMS achieves the best or comparable simple regret across all three experimental settings (synthetic GP functions, Ackley/Shekel benchmarks, and alkox/Fullerenes emulators) without any manual hyperparameter tuning of the acquisition function. In the small length-scale setting (ℓ=0.1), PIMS evaluates points with mean posterior std 0.71±0.13 versus TS's 0.92±0.09, confirming that TS over-explores while PIMS maintains a tighter exploration-exploitation balance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIMS outperforms GP-UCB and IRGP-UCB in all experimental settings, with the gap widening as |X| grows (Fig. 1b, |X|=20⁴) because their theoretical confidence parameters scale with log|X| and become overly conservative. PIMS also outperforms TS, particularly on complex functions with small length scale (ℓ=0.1, Fig. 1c) and on the Ackley/Shekel benchmarks, where TS suffers from over-exploration (mean posterior std at evaluated points: 0.92 for TS vs. 0.71 for PIMS). Against heuristic baselines EI, MES, and JES, PIMS achieves comparable or superior simple regret in all settings, including the real-world alkox and Fullerenes emulators where EI, JES, and TS are all inferior. The usual PI (Kushner, 1964) is included only in the synthetic experiments to illustrate its over-exploitation tendency, which PIMS avoids by using the sample-path maximum g_t* instead of the observed best value as the improvement threshold.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the regret analysis lineage from GP-UCB (Srinivas et al., 2010) and Thompson Sampling (Russo & Van Roy, 2014) by building directly on the tighter BCR bounds of IRGP-UCB (Takeno et al., 2023), which introduced randomized confidence parameters to remove the √log T factor. The key technical departure is handling the additional randomness inherent in posterior-sampling-based methods (TS, PIMS), where the next evaluation point remains a random variable even after fixing the confidence parameter, unlike IRGP-UCB where it becomes deterministic. PIMS is shown to be equivalent to MES with a single Monte Carlo sample (Wang & Jegelka, 2017) and to a randomized GP-UCB, unifying three previously separate perspectives. For continuous domains, the paper provides regret proofs that avoid requiring discretization within the algorithm itself, preserving the hyperparameter-free property of posterior sampling methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PIMS offers a practically attractive alternative to both GP-UCB (which requires manual tuning of the confidence parameter) and TS (which empirically over-explores), while maintaining the same tight theoretical guarantees. The empirical demonstration that PIMS's implicit confidence parameter ξ_t adapts to problem complexity (e.g., increasing when the length scale is small) without any user input is a meaningful practical advantage. Open questions remain: GP hyperparameter tuning is still required, the RFF approximation error for continuous domains is not incorporated into the regret bound, and the analysis is restricted to the Bayesian setting (frequentist/RKHS analysis is left for future work). Extensions to multi-fidelity, constrained, and multi-objective settings are noted as practically important but unaddressed.
