## Problem

While total uncertainty quantification for LLMs has received growing attention, decomposing that uncertainty into aleatoric (data-driven) and epistemic (model-knowledge) components remains largely unaddressed for large language models. Standard decomposition tools such as Bayesian Neural Networks and Deep Ensembles are impractical for LLMs due to their black-box nature and the prohibitive cost of training or fine-tuning multiple model variants, leaving a gap in how practitioners can diagnose whether a prediction's uncertainty stems from an ambiguous query or from the model's own knowledge deficit.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query (a question or task instruction) and a frozen pre-trained LLM, and produces a three-way uncertainty decomposition: total uncertainty, an aleatoric component attributed to input ambiguity, and an epistemic component attributed to model knowledge gaps. Operationally, a separate clarification LLM (either prompted with in-context examples or fine-tuned on AmbigQA) generates a set of input clarifications; the prediction LLM is then sampled 10 times at temperature 0.5 for each clarified input, and semantically equivalent answers are clustered by the LLM itself before computing the output distribution. The aleatoric term is the mutual information between the output distribution and the clarification set, while the epistemic term is the expected entropy of the output distribution conditioned on each clarification.

## Method

The core contribution is input clarification ensembling, a framework that mirrors the Deep Ensembles uncertainty decomposition but swaps the ensembling axis: instead of ensembling different model parameterizations that minimize epistemic uncertainty, it ensembles predictions under different input clarifications that minimize aleatoric uncertainty. Given an input X, a clarification LLM produces a set of disambiguating texts C^(k); the predictive distribution is defined as q(Y|X) = E_{q(C|X)}[q(Y|X⊕C,θ)], and the total entropy decomposes into a mutual-information term I(Y;C|X) (aleatoric) plus an expected-entropy term E_{q(C|X)} H(q(Y|X⊕C)) (epistemic), structurally identical to the BNN/Deep Ensembles decomposition with the two uncertainty roles exchanged. The clarification LLM can be instantiated either by prompting GPT-3.5/GPT-4 with task instructions and retrieved in-context examples, or by supervised fine-tuning Llama-3-8B-Instruct on the AmbigQA training set (under 10 minutes on 4×H100 GPUs). The framework is component-aware: by clarifying only the instruction or only the question, it separately quantifies aleatoric uncertainty arising from each input component, enabling targeted user feedback and improved answer recall when users select from the generated clarification options.

## Results

On the ambiguity detection task (the core evaluation of uncertainty decomposition), the proposed method with GPT-4 clarifications achieves 71.7 AUROC on AmbigQA and 81.3 AUROC on AmbigInst, substantially outperforming all baselines (best baseline: 66.0 AUROC). With ground-truth clarifications (OURS*), performance reaches 89.8 AUROC on AmbigQA and 96.7 AUROC on AmbigInst. For total uncertainty quantification (mistake detection), the method achieves 72.3 AUROC on Natural Questions and 89.7 AUROC on GSM8K, matching or exceeding all baselines. A fine-tuned Llama-3-8B-Instruct clarification model also outperforms all baselines (67.1 AUROC on AmbigQA), requiring less than 10 minutes of training on 4×H100 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all five baseline families on ambiguity detection. Against Semantic Entropy (Kuhn et al., 2022), which uses total output entropy, the method gains +16.8 AUROC on AmbigQA (71.7 vs. 54.9) and +15.3 on AmbigInst (81.3 vs. 66.0), demonstrating that total uncertainty alone cannot disentangle aleatoric from epistemic sources. Against ASK4CONF-D (Tian et al., 2023), which prompts the LLM for ambiguity confidence, the method gains +16.7 AUROC on AmbigQA and +23.4 on AmbigInst. Against the Deep Ensembles variant (ensembling different in-context example sets), the method gains +18.1 AUROC over the aleatoric component (71.7 vs. 53.6) and +16.3 over the total component (71.7 vs. 55.4) on AmbigQA, confirming that varying in-context examples does not approximate the parameter posterior needed for valid decomposition. Against the confidence-based methods from Si et al. (2022) and Cole et al. (2023) (Self-Consistency, Sample Repetition, Sample Diversity), the method gains 13–16 AUROC points, showing that answer-frequency or diversity heuristics are insufficient for ambiguity detection. On the mistake detection task (Table 1), the method achieves comparable or slightly better AUROC than Semantic Entropy and Ensembles* on both NQ and GSM8K, while substantially outperforming ASK4CONF on GSM8K (89.7 vs. 58.1 AUROC), where confidence-prompting fails for complex reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the uncertainty decomposition formula from Deep Ensembles (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017) and Bayesian Neural Networks (Blundell et al., 2015) to the black-box LLM setting by replacing model-parameter ensembling with input-clarification ensembling. The key conceptual move is a symmetry argument: where Deep Ensembles vary model parameters to reduce epistemic uncertainty, this framework varies input clarifications to reduce aleatoric uncertainty, yielding an identical decomposition structure with the two uncertainty terms swapped. The paper explicitly demonstrates that the naive adaptation of Deep Ensembles to LLMs (ensembling different in-context example sets) fails to produce meaningful decomposition, motivating the input-clarification alternative. It also builds on the Semantic Uncertainty framework (Kuhn et al., 2022) for output distribution estimation and connects to the ambiguity-detection literature (AmbigQA, AmbigEnt) as a downstream application.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical, black-box-compatible method for practitioners to diagnose *why* an LLM is uncertain on a given input—distinguishing 'the model doesn't know' (epistemic) from 'the question is ambiguous' (aleatoric)—which directly informs whether to improve the model or rephrase the query. The introduction of AmbigInst, a synthetic dataset of ambiguous instructions, fills a gap in the ambiguity-detection literature. However, the approach assumes input ambiguity is the sole source of aleatoric uncertainty and relies on LLMs being well-calibrated, both of which may not hold for all downstream tasks. Open questions include whether the clarification module can be made more robust for subtle ambiguities (as seen in AmbigQA's lower performance) and whether the framework extends naturally to multi-turn or agentic settings.
