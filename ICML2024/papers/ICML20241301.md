## Problem

Existing combinatorial multi-armed bandit frameworks with probabilistically triggered arms (CMAB-T) assume each arm's outcome is a univariate random variable, which cannot properly model applications like episodic reinforcement learning (where transitions are multinoulli over state spaces) or indivisible goods distribution (where each good is sent to exactly one recipient). Prior work resorts to treating each dimension of a multivariant arm as separate univariate arms, discarding the joint statistical structure and yielding suboptimal regret bounds. This paper introduces CMAB-MT, a framework that natively handles d-dimensional multivariant arm outcomes with probabilistic triggering, and builds the first formal connection between episodic RL and the CMAB literature.

## Input / Output

Each round the agent selects a combinatorial action (super arm) from a set Π, which probabilistically triggers a subset of base arms via a triggering function D_trig; the agent observes the d-dimensional outcomes of triggered arms (semi-bandit feedback) and receives a reward. The key structural element is an action-dependent confidence region C_t(π) that maps each action to a set of possible mean vectors, coupled with a joint oracle that optimizes over the (action, parameter) space simultaneously rather than fixing parameters first. For episodic RL, the arms are transition kernels p(s,a,h) ∈ Δ_S with occupancy measures serving as triggering probabilities; for PMC-GD, arms are source nodes with multinoulli distributions over target nodes in a bipartite graph.

## Method

The CUCB-MT algorithm constructs action-dependent confidence regions around empirical means governed by a 1-norm MTPM smoothness condition that assigns per-dimension weights w_i and modulates by triggering probability q_i^{μ,π}, then uses a joint oracle to select the most optimistic (action, parameter) pair. The regret analysis combines a reverse amortization trick with a triggering probability equivalence (TPE) trick to handle probabilistic triggering, yielding both gap-dependent (O(log T)) and gap-independent (Õ(√T)) bounds. For episodic RL, the paper establishes two smoothness conditions: a simple constant-weight version (H·1) matching Jaksch et al. (2010), and a stronger value-function-weighted MTPM+ condition that achieves minimax-optimal Õ(√(H³SAT)) regret by leveraging variance-aware concentration of the optimal future value, improving prior bounds by a factor of O(log²T). For PMC-GD, a pseudo-reward function derived from the MTPM smoothness bound enables an efficient greedy joint oracle, improving regret by Õ(√(|V|/k)) over prior variance-adaptive methods.

## Results

For episodic RL, the paper achieves two regret bounds: Õ(√(H⁴S²AT)) (Theorem 2, matching Jaksch et al. 2010) and Õ(√(H³SAT log(SAHT))) (Theorem 3, matching the minimax lower bound Ω(√(H³SAT)) up to logarithmic factors). The second bound improves the leading term by a factor of O(log²(SAHT)) over Zanette & Brunskill (2019) and Zhang et al. (2023), which achieve Õ(√(H³SAT log⁵(SAHT))). For probabilistic maximum coverage with goods distribution (PMC-GD), the paper achieves Õ(√(k|U||V|T)) regret (Theorem 4), improving the best prior variance-adaptive bound Õ(√(|U||V|²T)) by a factor of Õ(√(|V|/k)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For episodic RL, Theorem 2 (using constant weights H·1) matches the seminal UCRL2 bound of Jaksch et al. (2010) at Õ(√(H⁴S²AT)). Theorem 3 (using value-function-dependent weights V_{h+1}^{p,π} and variance-aware confidence radii) achieves the minimax-optimal Õ(√(H³SAT log(SAHT))), improving the leading logarithmic factor from log⁵(SAHT) in Zanette & Brunskill (2019) and Zhang et al. (2023) to log(SAHT) — a factor of O(log²(SAHT)) gain — by leveraging sharp CMAB amortization techniques. As a by-product, the framework yields a gap-dependent regret scaling with O(log T) without the involved clipping techniques used by Simchowitz & Jamieson (2019). For PMC-GD, the multivariant treatment of each source node's multinoulli outcome (rather than decomposing into |V| separate Bernoulli arms) tightens the concentration inequality, yielding Õ(√(k|U||V|T)) versus Õ(√(k|U||V|²T)) for CUCB-T (Wang & Chen, 2017) and Õ(√(|U||V|²T)) for variance-adaptive methods (Merlis & Mannor, 2019; Liu et al., 2022), an improvement of Õ(√(|V|/k)) in the typical regime where |V| ≫ k.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CMAB-T framework (Chen et al., 2016; Wang & Chen, 2017) by allowing d-dimensional multivariant arm outcomes, introducing the 1-norm MTPM smoothness condition that generalizes the TPM condition to weighted per-dimension differences. It draws on episodic RL techniques—particularly the extended value iteration oracle from Jaksch et al. (2010) and the optimistic-pessimistic variance-aware bonuses from Zanette & Brunskill (2019)—to instantiate the abstract CUCB-MT algorithm for the RL application. The framework also subsumes the probabilistic maximum coverage (PMC) literature, where the multivariant structure (multinoulli outcomes per source node) enables tighter concentration than treating each target node as a separate Bernoulli arm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first formal connection between episodic RL and combinatorial bandits, showing that episodic RL is a CMAB-MT instance solvable with standard CMAB tools (optimism, smoothness, amortization), which may encourage cross-pollination between the two communities. The minimax-optimal regret for episodic RL with an improved O(log²T) factor over prior work, plus a gap-dependent bound 'for free' without clipping, are concrete theoretical gains. Open questions include extending the framework to function approximation (linear/nonlinear), handling unknown rewards, and identifying further applications where multivariant structure yields tighter bounds than univariate decompositions.
