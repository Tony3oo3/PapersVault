## Problem

Real-world networks are noisy and incomplete, but existing graph diffusion models (GDDMs) assume training on large collections of clean graphs and suffer from quadratic complexity that prevents scaling beyond a few hundred nodes. No prior framework supports conditional editing tasks—denoising extraneous edges, recovering missing edges, or performing structured style transfer—on a single, partially observable network. This paper formalizes these three editing tasks and proposes a scalable framework to address them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241986/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on subgraphs sampled from a single large observed graph (2-hop ego-networks, subsampled to ≤50 nodes), taking as input the subgraph's adjacency matrix, node features, and per-node global context vectors (top-two Laplacian positional encodings). For style transfer, an additional time-dependent attribute regressor and a guidance strength parameter λ are provided. The output is an edited subgraph (adjacency + features) that can be stitched back into the full network using the unique global-context vectors as node identifiers, enabling large-graph generation without regenerating the entire network.

## Method

SGDM converts single-graph diffusion training into learning over a collection of sampled subgraphs, eliminating the quadratic memory bottleneck and reducing overfitting to corruption artifacts. It augments any GDDM backbone (DiGRESS, EDGE, GDSS) with global context (Laplacian positional encodings added at each message-passing layer) and local context (structural features) to distinguish structurally similar subgraphs located in different parts of the network. For expansion, the reverse process is modified with an in-painting mask that preserves observed edges while filling in missing ones; for denoising, a complementary mask restricts generation to only the observed edges, letting the model decide which to keep; for style transfer, classifier guidance via a time-dependent regressor steers the reverse process toward a target attribute value. The framework also supports stitching generated subgraphs back into a full graph by coalescing edges identified through unique global-context vectors.

## Results

On the denoising task, SGDM+EDGE substantially improves over single-graph EDGE across all three datasets: on PolBlogs, Edge Overlap rises from 0.041 to 0.316 and Sparsity from 0.041 to 0.340, while Diversity reaches 1.000 (vs. 0.702 for EDGE). On CORA, Edge Overlap increases from 0.096 to 0.585 and Sparsity from 0.115 to 0.591. For expansion, both methods struggle (Consensus ≤ 0.055), though SGDM slightly improves Consensus on BA-Shapes (0.055 vs. 0.000). For style transfer, incorporating Global-Context reduces MAE on Sum-Degree from ~6.5 to ~3.5 and on Max-Degree from ~25–45 to ~9–11, though it degrades Num. Triangles (MAE 9.2→19.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is EDGE (Chen et al., 2023), the only existing single-graph diffusion method applicable to this setting. SGDM+EDGE wins decisively on Diversity (1.000 vs. 0.70–0.92), Sparsity (0.34–0.59 vs. 0.04–0.12), and Edge Overlap (0.32–0.59 vs. 0.04–0.10) for denoising, but loses on Consensus (0.59–0.75 vs. 0.92–0.99) because EDGE over-removes edges. For expansion, both methods fail (Consensus ≤ 0.055), though the paper attributes this to EDGE's degree-distribution conditioning constraining the model. When swapping backbones, DiGRESS and GDSS within SGDM achieve higher Consensus and Diversity than EDGE on expansion (e.g., DiGRESS reaches 0.309 Consensus on BA-Shapes vs. 0.055 for EDGE), though GDSS produces overly dense graphs (Sparsity up to 14.2). For large-graph generation (Table 12), SGDM matches or exceeds EDGE on clustering coefficient (0.308 vs. 0.217) and triangle count (1.301 vs. 0.977) while maintaining low Edge Overlap (9.44 vs. 16.5), outperforming OPB, HDOP, CELL, CO, TSVD, VGAE, and GRNN on most structural statistics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes graph diffusion models (DiGRESS, EDGE, GDSS) from their original setting—generating from a distribution of many clean graphs—to the fundamentally different problem of editing a single, partially observable network. The key conceptual departure is the subgraph sampling and stitching mechanism, which converts single-graph training into learning over a collection of smaller subgraphs, addressing the quadratic complexity that prevents existing GDDMs from scaling beyond a few hundred nodes. The editing tasks draw a direct analogy to image in-painting (Lugmayr et al., 2022) and classifier guidance (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021), but the authors note that no prior work has defined or supported analogous graph-based editing tasks. Global context via Laplacian positional encodings is introduced to differentiate structurally similar subgraphs located in different parts of the network, enabling both better generation and graph stitching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formalize a unified editing framework (denoising, expansion, style transfer) for refining a single partially observable network, filling a gap where prior methods like link prediction or graph GANs only supported individual sub-tasks. However, the experimental results are mixed: denoising shows meaningful improvements with SGDM, but expansion largely fails across all backbones (consensus near 0), and style transfer for triangle count actually degrades with global context. The evaluation is limited to three datasets with only 10 generated samples per metric, and no downstream task performance is reported. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly production-scale networks, how it handles dynamic graphs, and whether the poor expansion results reflect a fundamental limitation of diffusion-based approaches for this task.
