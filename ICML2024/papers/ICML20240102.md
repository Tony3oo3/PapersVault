## Problem

Despite LLMs' widespread use, their internal geometric structure remains poorly understood, leaving practitioners without principled, label-free features for downstream tasks like toxicity detection. Existing approaches either require expensive labeled data, are sensitive to prompt engineering, or demand knowledge of which layer embeddings to use. Additionally, the geometric limitations of RLHF-based safety guardrails have not been formally characterized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240102/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt (token sequence) fed through a pre-trained causal LLM (Llama2-7B/70B or Mistral-7B) and extracts 7 unsupervised geometric features per layer from the MLP's spline partition, yielding 224 features for 7B models and 560 for the 70B model. These features are then passed to lightweight classifiers (linear, random forest, or gradient-boosted trees) to produce toxicity labels, domain labels, or intrinsic dimension estimates. Notably, using only the first 3 layers' features with a random forest achieves 94.68% AUC at 0.005s latency, while all-layer features with a linear classifier reach 99.18% AUC at 0.061s.

## Method

The paper derives two closed-form geometric characterizations of LLM layers. First, the multi-head attention output for token i is shown to lie in the Minkowski sum of per-head convex hulls of preceding token embeddings, with intrinsic dimension upper-bounded by the count of nonzero attention values (Eq. 7); this enables an ID-based jailbreak where prepending semantically related sentences increases the embedding's intrinsic dimension, causing the model to visit regions of the space not covered by RLHF. Second, the MLP is expressed exactly as a continuous piecewise-affine (spline) operator whose partition Ω into polytopal regions is invariant to LayerNorm and skip connections (Proposition A.1), and whose expressivity grows exponentially with the MHA intrinsic dimension (Proposition 4.1). From this spline structure, seven unsupervised features are extracted per layer: four capturing the sign pattern (which polytope each token occupies) and three capturing the distance to the partition boundary, aggregated across tokens. These features require no fine-tuning, no labels, and no architectural modification, yet outperform all state-of-the-art toxicity classifiers by 12–17% AUC on the Omni-Toxic benchmark and surpass the Jigsaw Kaggle SOTA (99.88% vs. 98.85%).

## Results

On the Omni-Toxic toxicity detection benchmark, Spline-Llama2-7B with a linear classifier over all 224 features achieves 99.18% ROC-AUC, outperforming the best existing method (s-nlp, 82.08%) by +17.1%. Using only the first 3 layers with a random forest, the method still reaches 94.68% ROC-AUC at 0.005s inference latency. On the Jigsaw Kaggle challenge, Spline-Llama2-7B achieves 99.88% mean column-wise ROC-AUC, surpassing the Kaggle leaderboard SOTA of 98.85% without data augmentation or model ensembling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its unsupervised spline features against six existing toxicity detection models on the Omni-Toxic dataset. Spline-Llama2-7B (linear) achieves 99.18% ROC-AUC, beating the strongest baseline s-nlp (82.08%) by +17.1 points and ToxRoberta (79.87%) by +19.3 points. Even the most latency-efficient variant (3 layers + random forest at 0.005s) reaches 94.68%, still exceeding all baselines by over 12 points. On the Jigsaw Kaggle challenge, Spline-Llama2-7B (99.88%) and Spline-Llama2-70B (99.86%) both surpass the Kaggle SOTA (98.85%) using only gradient-boosted trees on the proposed features, without the complex ensembling, multi-embedding stacking, or translation-based data augmentation employed by top Kaggle teams. The Mistral-7B variant (not RLHF'd) confirms the results are not an artifact of RLHF, achieving 98.45% (linear) and 93.73% (3-layer RF).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spline (continuous piecewise-affine) theory of deep networks (Balestriero et al., 2018; Balestriero & Baraniuk, 2020) from generic feed-forward architectures to the specific geometry of LLM layers, deriving a closed-form characterization of multi-head attention output as a Minkowski sum of convex hulls whose intrinsic dimension is governed by the attention matrix. It generalizes prior observations on intrinsic dimensionality in LLMs (Aghajanyan et al., 2020b) by making the analysis context-dependent and sequence-aware rather than relying on fixed embedding dimensions. The toxicity detection application departs from the dominant paradigm of fine-tuning supervised classifiers (ToxRoberta, HateBERT, etc.) by instead extracting unsupervised geometric features from a frozen pre-trained LLM, positioning the work at the intersection of interpretability theory and practical safety tooling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The geometric characterization of MHA and MLP partitions provides a principled, architecture-level explanation for why longer and semantically richer prompts improve LLM performance (chain-of-thought, in-context learning), and reveals a concrete vulnerability: RLHF protection can be bypassed by increasing the embedding's intrinsic dimension through informed prompt manipulation, since the number of prompts needed to cover the space grows exponentially with dimension. The practical toxicity detector (99.18% AUC, 0.005s latency with 3 layers) is a significant step toward deployable, model-agnostic safety monitoring, though the authors acknowledge the first-order features may be insufficient for fine-grained generation tasks. Open questions include whether the geometric framework generalizes to non-causal or alternative attention architectures, and whether more robust RLHF can be designed with explicit awareness of the intrinsic-dimension geometry.
