## Problem

Multi-entity 3D physical environments suffer from exponential growth of the global state space as entity count increases, making policy learning intractable. While subequivariant RL has been applied to single-entity morphology control, extending it to inter-entity transformations is unexplored due to coupled local spaces, and existing benchmarks lack environments that probe multi-agent dynamics under Euclidean symmetry with randomized initial conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240289/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent receives a per-agent observation containing full 3D state (position, velocity, angular velocity) of all its own bodies plus root-body states of every other entity; positions are made translation-invariant by subtracting the mean root position across all entities, and scalar features include joint angles, ranges, and a 4-dim body-type one-hot. The policy outputs per-actuator torque in [-1,1] parameterized as loc/scale of a Tanh-Normal distribution, while the critic produces a single scalar value; both are provably SO_g(3)-invariant, meaning the same action is produced regardless of the global rotation around the gravity axis.

## Method

SHNN operates in a hierarchical pipeline: (1) a greedy bipartite graph matching assigns each agent to a specific object (or opponent), dynamically constructing a sparse local entity-level graph that decouples local transformations from the global structure; (2) subequivariant message passing over this local graph (using the E_g(3)-equivariant function from Han et al. 2022) fuses information from related entities while preserving the gravity-constrained symmetry; (3) a learned transform vector per entity is orthogonalized against the gravity axis to construct an SO_g(3)-equivariant local reference frame rotation matrix, which converts all geometric vectors into an invariant representation; (4) a body-level MLP (or Transformer/GNN variant) maps the invariant observation to the final policy. The entire framework is trained with MAPPO under the CTDE paradigm, and the key theoretical contribution is proving that the orthogonalization step yields SO_g(3)-equivariant rotation matrices, enabling lossless compression of the global state space without hand-crafted heading normalization.

## Results

On the MEBEN Team Reach benchmark, SHNN achieves Success Rates of 97.26% (1_ant), 47.82% (1_centipede), 77.93% (2_ants), 17.40% (2_ant_claw), 11.97% (2_unimals), and 8.70% (2_ant_claw_centipede). The most dramatic gains appear in multi-agent settings: SHNN reaches 77.93% on 2_ants versus 5.25% for MLP+HN, and 47.82% on 1_centipede versus 11.28% for MLP+HN. In Team Sumo environments, the advantage of SHNN over baselines progressively amplifies as the number and complexity of agent morphologies increase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SHNN against two primary baselines: MLP (from MxT-Bench, Furuta et al. 2023) and MLP+HN (from SGRL, Chen et al. 2023). MLP generally fails to achieve meaningful returns in most Team Reach cases due to vulnerability to local extremes in the expansive exploration space. MLP+HN shows competitive performance in single-agent or symmetric-morphology settings (93.39% vs. 97.26% on 1_ant; 10.86% vs. 11.97% on 2_unimals), but its effectiveness diminishes sharply in multi-agent or asymmetric scenarios: it drops to 5.25% on 2_ants (vs. SHNN's 77.93%) and 11.28% on 1_centipede (vs. SHNN's 47.82%). The paper attributes this gap to MLP+HN's non-learnable, non-adjustable heading normalization, which cannot adapt to task-specific inter-entity transformations. SHNN's subequivariant message passing and dynamic task assignment provide a learnable, task-aware alternative that consistently outperforms both baselines across all six Team Reach environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the subequivariant RL framework from Chen et al. (2023, SGRL) and the subequivariant GNN machinery of Han et al. (2022) from single-entity to multi-entity 3D physical environments, where inter-entity transformations introduce coupled local spaces that the prior single-entity formulation cannot handle. The key architectural departure is a hierarchical design: entity-level subequivariant message passing over a dynamically constructed task-assignment graph (via greedy bipartite matching) feeds into a learned local reference frame, which then drives body-level control. The MEBEN benchmark also bridges two previously disjoint lines of work—morphology-based RL (MxT-Bench, SGRL) and multi-agent RL (SMAC, JaxMARL)—by combining multi-morphology agents with cooperative/competitive dynamics under geometric symmetry constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exponential state-space growth in multi-entity 3D RL is a genuine bottleneck, and SHNN's plug-in subequivariant message passing offers a principled, learnable alternative to hand-crafted heading normalization that generalizes across morphologies and agent counts. MEBEN fills a notable gap as the first benchmark jointly addressing multi-agent dynamics, diverse morphologies, and E(3) subequivariance in hardware-accelerated 3D simulation. Key limitations include reliance on state-based inputs (no vision), restriction to Euclidean gravity-affected physics, and the finding that task assignment provides limited benefit in competitive (Team Sumo) settings, suggesting a co-learnable formulation of assignment and equivariance remains an open problem.
