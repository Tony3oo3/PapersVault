## Problem

Scaffolding multiple functional motifs into a single protein requires knowing their relative structural and sequential positions, which is not readily available and demands domain expertise. Existing inpainting methods (RFdiffusion, Chroma) fix motif positions manually, while conditional generation methods (SMCdiff) cannot guarantee motif presence. FADiff is the first approach to scaffold multiple motifs without any prior specification of their relative arrangement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241281/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes the internal 3D backbone structures and amino acid sequences of m motifs (each 10–80+ residues) as input, with no positional information. It outputs a full protein backbone structure where the scaffold residues are generated and the motifs are placed at automatically determined structural and sequential positions. The amino acid sequence order is constructed post-hoc via a TSP-like greedy algorithm using C–N atom distances, and the model trained on exactly two motifs generalizes to any number of motifs or even domains exceeding 100 residues.

## Method

FADiff treats each motif as a rigid anchor that floats independently during the SE(3) diffusion process. In the forward process, rotation and translation noise (following FrameDiff) are averaged over each motif's constituent residues using a virtual coordinate system at the motif's geometric center, preserving internal structure while allowing the whole motif to translate and rotate as a unit. In the reverse process, the VFN-Diff score network predicts per-residue updates that are similarly averaged over motif residues, and translations induced by rigid rotation are explicitly removed before score computation to maintain consistency with the forward process. Training uses denoising score matching with auxiliary C–N bond and backbone clash losses, and the key insight is that because all residues are treated equally in the diffusion dynamics (only the averaging enforces rigidity), a model trained on two motifs naturally generalizes to arbitrary numbers of motifs.

## Results

On the MS Benchmark for scaffolding two functional motifs, FADiff achieves an average in silico success rate (SR) of 73.05%, with per-length SRs ranging from 65.00% (310 residues) to 86.67% (360 residues). FADiff generalizes to scaffolding 3, 4, and 5 motifs (trained only on 2 motifs) with SRs of 62.38%, 58.40%, and 46.67% respectively, and reaches 80.00% SR for two huge domains (>100 residues each). On the VM dataset evaluation set, 67.5% of generated structures achieve TM-score > 0.5 relative to native structures, and 84.60% of generated structures are novel (pdbTM < 0.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FADiff outperforms both inpainting (RFdiffusion, Chroma) and conditional generation (SMCDiff) consistently across all protein lengths in Table 2. Against inpainting, FADiff gains 15–25 percentage points in SR (e.g., 76.67% vs. 51.58% at 160 residues; 86.67% vs. 79.47% at 360 residues), primarily because inpainting requires manually assigned relative motif positions that are often inappropriate. Against conditional generation (SMCDiff), FADiff's advantage is even more dramatic (76.67% vs. 23.75% at 160 residues), because SMCDiff cannot guarantee motif presence: while 87.25% of SMCDiff outputs are designable (scTM > 0.5), only 21.50% actually contain the desired motifs (motif RMSD < 0.1). FADiff, by contrast, guarantees motif presence by construction (motifs are rigid anchors in the diffusion process) while simultaneously automating their positional design, eliminating the need for domain expertise on relative motif placement.

## Relation to Prior Work

FADiff extends the SE(3) Riemannian diffusion framework of FrameDiff (Yim et al., 2023) and the Riemannian score-based generative modeling of De Bortoli et al. (2022) by introducing rigid anchor motifs that float independently during the diffusion process. It departs from prior motif-scaffolding approaches—RFdiffusion and Chroma (inpainting, which fix motif positions) and SMCdiff (conditional generation, which cannot guarantee motif presence)—by treating motifs as fundamental components of the generation output rather than fixed conditions or guidance signals. The score network is VFN-Diff (Mao et al., 2024), a geometric vector field network, making FADiff a targeted modification of the forward/reverse diffusion dynamics rather than a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FADiff addresses a practically important gap: designing multi-functional proteins (e.g., multi-epitope vaccines, multi-substrate enzymes) without requiring domain expertise to pre-specify relative motif positions, a constraint that has limited prior computational approaches. The demonstrated generalization from 2-motif training to 3–5 motifs and >100-residue domains suggests a scalable path toward arbitrary multi-motif design. Key open questions include wet-lab validation of the generated scaffolds, whether the rigid-motif assumption holds for flexible or conformationally dynamic motifs, and how the approach scales to very large proteins with many motifs where scaffold residues become sparse.
