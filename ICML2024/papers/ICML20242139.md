## Problem

Pre-trained unified multimodal representation spaces (e.g., ImageBind) are built on billion-level data and parameters, making further enhancement prohibitively expensive and prone to catastrophic forgetting. The paper asks: can we efficiently fuse knowledge from advanced expert spaces (e.g., state-of-the-art image-text or audio-text models) into a frozen unified space with minimal training cost, without degrading existing cross-modal alignments?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242139/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained encoders from a unified space (ImageBind) and one or more expert spaces (InternVL for image-text, CLAP for audio-text), along with unpaired data (2.3M texts, 1.3M images, 1.8M audios) used to construct pseudo multimodal pairs via softmax-based cross-modal retrieval. The output is an enhanced unified embedding where each modality's representation is a weighted linear combination of the original unified-space embedding and the projector-remapped expert-space embedding, enabling zero-shot cross-modal retrieval and classification across audio, image, and text with adjustable combining factors at inference time.

## Method

FreeBind introduces two complementary 'space bonds' for fusing expert knowledge into a unified space: a displacement bond that remaps the unified space into the expert space (inheriting full expert knowledge at the cost of some unified-space information), and a combination bond that remaps the expert space into the frozen unified space (preserving unified knowledge but only partially absorbing expert knowledge). Space alignment is learned by training simple MLP projectors with InfoNCE loss on pseudo multimodal pairs collected via softmax retrieval across shared and non-shared modalities, with a mixture-of-projectors strategy (7 projectors trained on different data subsets, ensembled by mean pooling) for robustness. For integrating multiple expert spaces, the method uses a sequential-then-parallel scheme: first applying displacement bond to the critical image-text space (followed by light fine-tuning of the audio encoder to repair lost knowledge), then applying combination bonds in parallel to additional audio-text spaces. A coarse-to-fine customized inference strategy allows flexible selection of which expert modules to include and adjustment of per-modality combining weights (σ_a, σ_t) to trade off between versatility and domain-specific expertise.

## Results

On zero-shot classification, the best enhanced space InternVL_IB†++(AT E.) achieves 93.00% on ESC-50 and 26.45 mAP on AudioSet, surpassing the audio-text expert CLAPg (90.95% / 23.36 mAP), while the versatile variant InternVL_IB++(Ver.) maintains 81.49% ImageNet accuracy (vs. 76.31% for ImageBind). On cross-modal retrieval, InternVL_IB†++(Ver.) reaches 29.42 R@1 on AudioCaps and 61.13 R@1 on COCO, outperforming ImageBind (9.24 / 57.28) by large margins. In the audio-text expertise setting, InternVL_IB†++(AT E.) achieves 43.10 R@1 on AudioCaps, exceeding the source expert CLAPg (40.25 R@1), and InternVL_IB's image-text retrieval (61.49 R@1 on COCO) surpasses the InternVL expert space (61.05 R@1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeBind comprehensively outperforms all pre-trained unified space baselines: ImageBind (67.25/13.96/76.31), WAV2CLIP (17.40/0.71/60.58), AudioCLIP (11.45/5.65/24.14), C-MCR (66.05/11.15/23.16), and Ex-MCR (68.35/6.67/60.58) on ESC-50/AudioSet/ImageNet classification, with the largest gains in audio classification (e.g., 88.55 vs. 67.25 ESC-50 for ImageBind++(Ver.)). Against the image-text expert InternVL (81.70 ImageNet), the displacement-bond product InternVL_IB achieves 81.54 ImageNet and 61.49 R@1 on COCO retrieval, slightly exceeding InternVL's 61.05 R@1. In the audio-text expertise setting, InternVL_IB†++(AT E.) surpasses both CLAPg (40.25 R@1 AudioCaps) and CLAPm (39.00 R@1) with 43.10 R@1, while also maintaining competitive image-text performance (54.75 R@1 COCO vs. ImageBind's 57.28). The versatile variants (Ver.) achieve a balanced trade-off, improving audio-text retrieval by ~20 R@1 over ImageBind while preserving image-text accuracy within 0.5 R@1. Notably, the combination bond alone (ImageBind++(Ver.)) already improves audio classification from 67.25 to 88.55 on ESC-50 without any fine-tuning, demonstrating the 'free lunch' property.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the space-alignment paradigm introduced by C-MCR and Ex-MCR, which connect two expert spaces sharing exactly one modality via a learned projector. FreeBind generalizes this to the more general setting of augmenting a pre-trained unified space (with multiple shared modalities) by integrating multiple expert spaces simultaneously, and introduces two complementary mechanisms—displacement bond (remapping the unified space into the expert space) and combination bond (projecting the expert space into the frozen unified space)—rather than a single alignment objective. The mixture-of-projectors strategy and the coarse-to-fine customized inference further differentiate the approach from prior single-projector designs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: billion-parameter unified spaces can be enhanced on a single 4090 GPU in hours, avoiding catastrophic forgetting and the enormous cost of retraining. The finding that absorbing audio-text knowledge (CLAP) can improve audio-image alignment reveals a cross-modal transfer effect that broadens the potential of knowledge fusion in multimodal representations. However, the current evaluation is limited to the 3-modality audio-image-text space of ImageBind; whether the space-bond framework scales to the 6–7 modality spaces of ImageBind or LanguageBind remains an open question, as does the behavior under more aggressive multi-space integration.
