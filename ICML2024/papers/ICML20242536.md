## Problem

In healthcare analytics, positive samples are defined by stringent medical criteria while negative samples form an open-ended, unbounded complement that has been largely underexplored. This asymmetry means that negative samples may contain medically valuable subgroups (e.g., patients with comorbidities or early-stage conditions) that prior cohort discovery methods fail to identify, leaving a critical gap in understanding disease-related risk factors and complications.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular EMR data consisting of 709 lab test values averaged over a 7-day input window (min-max standardized), along with binary labels where positives meet KDIGO AKI criteria and negatives are the unrestricted complement. It produces per-sample data Shapley values (computed via Monte Carlo permutation sampling with a logistic regression model and AUC metric), 64-dimensional manifold space representations, and a set of discovered cohorts defined as the joint spherical neighborhoods of DBSCAN core points among the top 40% high-Shapley-value negative samples.

## Method

The approach has three stages: (1) computing data Shapley values for each negative sample via Monte Carlo permutation sampling with early stopping, then constructing the Negative Sample Shapley Field as a scalar field over all negative samples; (2) transforming this field through a 3-layer stacked denoising autoencoder (709→256→128→64) trained with a combined loss that includes a reconstruction term for structure preservation and a novel isotropy constraint that penalizes non-uniform changes in Shapley values relative to pairwise Euclidean distances in the latent space, ensuring uniform 'temperature' gradients across all orientations; (3) applying DBSCAN on the top 40% high-Shapley-value samples in the resulting manifold space to identify dense, arbitrarily-shaped clusters as medically meaningful cohorts. The key differentiator is the isotropy constraint, which prevents the mis-discovery of hot zones that occurs in the anisotropic original field, and the Shapley-based exploration of interrelationships between samples rather than feature-level similarity.

## Results

On the NUH EMR dataset (20,732 admissions; 911 positive, 19,821 negative samples; 709 lab tests), the method discovers 7 distinct medically meaningful cohorts among the top 40% high-data-Shapley-value negative samples via DBSCAN in the SDAE-transformed manifold space. The data Shapley value distribution across all negative samples is well-fitted by a 3-component Gaussian mixture model, with the third component (high-value samples) further partitioned into 7 Gaussian sub-distributions corresponding to the discovered cohorts. All five classifiers (LR, GBDT, AdaBoost, RF, MLP) show AUC improvement when training on only negative samples with s_i > 0 versus all negative samples, and the SDAE manifold representation (709→64 dimensions) incurs only ~5% AUC degradation, confirming structure preservation. The discovered cohorts (inflammatory, hepatic/hematological, respiratory/metabolic) are validated by clinicians as consistent with known AKI pathophysiology.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against seven baselines across prediction performance and cohort discovery quality. On AKI prediction AUC (Figure 5), the proposed data Shapley filtering (s_i > 0) outperforms influence-function-based data valuation (IF > 0) across all five classifiers, as IF 'deletes informative negative samples' and fails to satisfy equitability conditions. For cohort discovery, contrastive PCA (cPCA, Abid et al. 2017/2018) and deep clustering methods (DCN, Yang et al. 2017; DEKM, Guo et al. 2021) all fail to identify medically meaningful cohorts, producing only one large cluster and a few smaller ones due to the anisotropic nature of the Negative Sample Shapley Field without the proposed isotropy constraint. PU learning methods (Elkan-Noto, Mordelet-Vert) are compared in Appendix G.3 but are framed as addressing a different problem (learning from positive/unlabeled data) rather than cohort discovery. Clustering all negative samples without Shapley-based filtering (Appendix G.6) similarly fails to isolate the high-value sub-populations. The proposed method's advantage is thus both quantitative (AUC gains from Shapley filtering) and qualitative (clinically validated, medically interpretable cohorts vs. uninformative single-cluster outputs from baselines).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes data Shapley values (Ghorbani & Zou, 2019) from their standard role in individual sample valuation (e.g., federated learning, feature explainability) toward exploring interrelationships among negative samples, constructing a 'Negative Sample Shapley Field' as a spatial object for clustering. The manifold learning component builds on stacked denoising autoencoders (Vincent et al., 2010) but introduces a novel isotropy constraint that penalizes non-uniform Shapley value gradients across orientations, addressing an anisotropy problem specific to the Shapley field. The clustering step uses standard DBSCAN, and the overall pipeline departs from prior cohort studies (which focus on positive samples) by targeting the open-ended, asymmetric negative sample space in healthcare binary classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine and previously unaddressed asymmetry in healthcare analytics: negative samples are open-ended and diverse, yet prior work treats them as a monolithic complement to well-defined positives. The clinically validated cohorts (inflammatory, hepatic/hematological, respiratory/metabolic) demonstrate that negative samples encode medically actionable substructure relevant to comorbidity and early warning. However, the approach is computationally expensive (Monte Carlo Shapley estimation with repeated model retraining), validated primarily on a single disease (AKI) at one hospital, and the isotropy constraint's theoretical justification remains heuristic. Open questions include scalability to larger EMR corpora, generalizability across disease types, and whether the Shapley field concept transfers to non-healthcare binary classification with asymmetric label definitions.
