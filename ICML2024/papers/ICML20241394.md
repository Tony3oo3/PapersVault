## Problem

AlphaZero-style planning-augmented self-play achieves superhuman performance in sequential games but fails in simultaneous games, where agents act concurrently and may play suboptimally or select different Nash equilibria. Existing zero-shot coordination methods train diverse agent populations but do not explicitly model the rationality of unseen opponents, limiting their ability to adapt behavior to agents of varying strength within a single episode.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241394/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The response model takes a game-state observation from the rational agent's perspective plus a vector of n−1 scalar temperature parameters (one per other agent, each in [τ_min, τ_max]) encoding their estimated bounded rationality. At test time, these temperatures are estimated online via maximum likelihood estimation over the log-likelihood of observed actions under a softmax model, requiring only 20–30 time steps to converge. The output is a joint action policy and value estimate for the rational agent, with the policy conditioned on the full temperature vector so that behavior shifts continuously from self-reliant (low τ) to cooperative/exploitative (high τ).

## Method

Albatross introduces the Smooth Best Response Logit Equilibrium (SBRLE), a novel equilibrium concept where one rational agent plays a smooth best response (softmax with fixed response temperature τ_R) to the Logit equilibrium policies of n−1 weak agents, each with its own temperature. Training is two-stage: first, a proxy model (actor-critic network conditioned on a single temperature τ) learns to approximate Logit equilibria across a range of rationalities via planning-augmented self-play with fixed-depth search and an LE-solver backup; second, a response model (conditioned on n−1 temperatures) learns the SBR to the frozen proxy policy using the same fixed-depth search but with an SBR backup that computes softmax over proxy-induced action utilities. Fixed-depth search with equilibrium backup replaces MCTS, which the authors show empirically outperforms MCTS variants (DUCT, EXP3, Regret Matching, SM-OOS) in simultaneous games. The continuous scalar temperature parameterization enables efficient online MLE (the log-likelihood is concave, solvable by line search) and allows the agent to adapt its strategy smoothly to any opponent strength without retraining.

## Results

In the cooperative Overcooked benchmark, Albatross outperforms the previous state-of-the-art PECAN by 37.6% on average when cooperating with a human behavior-cloning agent, and achieves the highest reward in 4 out of 5 kitchen layouts. Against scripted agents exhibiting out-of-distribution irrational behaviors (Table 1), Albatross matches or exceeds HSP in most settings. In the competitive Battlesnake game, Albatross consistently outperforms AlphaZero across all three game modes (Tron, Stochastic 2-Player, Stochastic 4-Player), with the largest reward gap against weaker opponents (fewer MCTS iterations), while the gap narrows against near-optimal opponents.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AlphaZero (Silver et al., 2018) in Battlesnake, Albatross wins in all three game modes, with the largest advantage against weak opponents (fewer search iterations) and convergence to similar performance against near-optimal opponents; in the 4-player stochastic tournament, Albatross achieves a higher TrueSkill score than AlphaZero. In Overcooked cooperation with a human behavior-cloning agent, Albatross outperforms all population-diversity baselines—Fictitious Co-Play (Strouse et al., 2021), Trajectory Diversity (Lupu et al., 2021), MEP (Zhao et al., 2023), and PECAN (Lou et al., 2023)—by 37.6% on average over PECAN, the strongest baseline. Against scripted irrational agents (Table 1), Albatross beats FCP, MEP, and TrajDiv in 5 out of 6 settings, and matches or slightly exceeds HSP (Yu et al., 2023) in 4 out of 6, losing only on the Asymmetric Advantage Onion Placement script where HSP's event-based training specifically targets such biased policies. PPO and PBT (Jaderberg et al., 2017) are substantially outperformed in all Overcooked layouts. The key differentiator is that Albatross uses explicit temperature-based opponent modeling rather than population diversity, enabling it to adapt its strategy within a single episode rather than relying on training-time exposure to diverse agents.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts AlphaZero's self-play-and-planning framework to simultaneous games by replacing MCTS with fixed-depth search and Logit equilibrium backup, and introduces the SBRLE equilibrium concept to model asymmetric bounded rationality in zero-shot settings. It departs from the dominant population-diversity paradigm for zero-shot coordination (PECAN, Fictitious Co-Play, Trajectory Diversity, MEP, HSP), which trains against diverse agent populations, in favor of explicit opponent modeling via a continuous temperature parameter estimated online through maximum likelihood. The SBRLE generalizes both the Logit equilibrium (symmetric rationality) and the Quantal Stackelberg Equilibrium (asymmetric but requiring knowledge of the rational agent's policy) to the zero-shot case where the weak agent does not know the rational agent's strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SBRLE provides a principled game-theoretic foundation for zero-shot interaction with agents of arbitrary strength, addressing a fundamental limitation of AlphaZero-style methods that assume perfect rationality and fail against subrational opponents. The 37.6% improvement over PECAN in Overcooked and the ability to exploit weak agents in Battlesnake demonstrate practical value, but the method's reliance on planning in the joint action space limits scalability to many agents or large action spaces, and the need for 20–30 observations to estimate temperature rules out very short episodes. Future work with MuZero-style learned environment models could address the planning limitation, and incorporating prior temperature distributions could extend applicability to single-shot interactions.
