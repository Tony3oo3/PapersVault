## Problem

Existing Neural Collapse analyses focus on feature or classification-head similarity in isolation, leaving the information-theoretic relationship between data representations and class heads unexplored. This paper asks whether matrix information theory can quantify and predict the dynamics of this interplay throughout training, and whether the resulting metrics can both explain known phenomena (grokking, linear mode connectivity, pruning) and serve as useful training objectives.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 96×96 for STL-10) in batches of 64, extracting L2-normalized feature vectors from a WideResNet backbone. It produces class labels as the primary output, but additionally computes two scalar diagnostics per batch: the MIR and HDR between the Gram matrix of in-batch features and the Gram matrix of the corresponding class-head weight vectors. In the grokking experiment, input switches to one-hot-encoded modular addition pairs (p=113) processed by a single-layer ReLU transformer, with output being the predicted residue.

## Method

The paper defines two normalized matrix-information metrics—MIR (matrix mutual information ratio) and HDR (matrix entropy difference ratio)—computed over Gram matrices of L2-normalized batch features and their corresponding classification-head weight vectors, then proves that at Neural Collapse HDR reaches its theoretical minimum of 0 and MIR approaches its maximum of ~1. These metrics are then repurposed as auxiliary loss terms: the training objective subtracts λ_mi·MI(G(f), G(V)) to maximize mutual information between feature and head similarity structures, and adds λ_id·|H(G(f)) − H(G(V))| to minimize their entropy gap. In semi-supervised learning, the same terms are applied to pseudo-labeled unlabeled data selected via FreeMatch's confidence threshold, extending the information constraint to the unlabeled portion of the pipeline. The approach is validated across standard supervised training, linear mode connectivity interpolation, label smoothing, 90% unstructured pruning, and modular-addition grokking, demonstrating that MIR and HDR track and predict phenomena that accuracy alone cannot distinguish.

## Results

In semi-supervised learning (Table 1), FreeMatch+Minimizing Entropy Difference achieves the best error rate on CIFAR-10 with 10 labels (4.69%) and 40 labels (4.63%), and FreeMatch+Maximizing Mutual Information achieves the best on CIFAR-100 with 400 labels (36.41%) and 2500 labels (25.77%), as well as STL-10 with 1000 labels (5.24%). In fully supervised learning (Table 2), adding the HDR loss improves CIFAR-10 accuracy from 95.35% to 95.57% and CIFAR-100 from 80.77% to 80.96%. The gains are most pronounced in low-label semi-supervised settings, where the information constraints help the model extract more structure from unlabeled data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's two variants (FreeMatch + MI loss and FreeMatch + HDR loss) are compared against 14 baselines spanning the full spectrum of semi-supervised methods. On CIFAR-10 with 10 and 40 labels, both variants clearly outperform FreeMatch (8.07/4.90), OTMatch (4.89/4.72), and SoftMatch (4.91/4.82), with the HDR variant achieving the best error rates (4.69/4.63). On CIFAR-100, the MI variant achieves the best error at both 400 labels (36.41 vs. 37.10 for SoftMatch and 37.98 for FreeMatch) and 2500 labels (25.77 vs. 26.04 for OTMatch). However, the method does not uniformly dominate: SoftMatch achieves a lower error on CIFAR-10 with 250 labels (4.04 vs. 4.56), and OTMatch is stronger on STL-10 with 40 labels (12.10 vs. 14.93). In fully supervised learning, the gains are modest (e.g., 95.57% vs. 95.35% on CIFAR-10), consistent with the authors' observation that abundant labeled data already provides sufficient information constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges Neural Collapse theory (Papyan et al., 2020) and matrix information theory (Skean et al., 2023) by introducing MIR and HDR as quantitative measures of the information interplay between data representations and classification heads. Prior Neural Collapse work focused on feature similarity or head alignment in isolation, while earlier matrix information applications (Tan et al., 2023b; Zhang et al., 2023a) targeted self-supervised learning; here the authors are the first to derive closed-form theoretical values for these metrics at the Neural Collapse point and to deploy them as both diagnostic tools and training loss terms. For the semi-supervised component, the method is built directly on FreeMatch (Wang et al., 2023), adding information-theoretic regularization to its pseudo-labeling pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MIR/HDR framework offers a unified lens through which multiple previously disconnected phenomena—standard training dynamics, grokking, linear mode connectivity, pruning, and label smoothing—can be described with a single pair of information-theoretic quantities, potentially guiding future mechanistic interpretability work. The grokking analysis revealing a two-phase reversal in MIR/HDR before the model generalizes is a genuinely new observation. However, practical gains are modest (sub-0.5% error reduction in semi-supervised settings on CIFAR-scale data), and the CIFAR-10 linear mode connectivity anomaly (MIR rising while accuracy collapses) remains unexplained, leaving open whether these metrics capture something fundamental or an artifact of small-scale experiments. Scaling to ImageNet or larger architectures, and understanding whether the information constraints help in truly label-scarce regimes beyond 40 labels, are important open questions.
