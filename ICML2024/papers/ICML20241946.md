## Problem

Tiny language models (~1B parameters) for edge deployment are typically trained by directly scaling down LLM recipes without careful analysis of how architecture, initialization, and optimization choices interact at small scale. This paper identifies that tiny models face unique challenges—disproportionate tokenizer parameter overhead, severe catastrophic forgetting, and heightened sensitivity to depth/width configuration—that are largely unaddressed in existing work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241946/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The models consume multilingual natural language text (Chinese and English in roughly 1:1 ratio) tokenized via a compact 48k BPE vocabulary built with SentencePiece, and produce autoregressive text completions. A key design constraint is edge-device deployment: the 48k tokenizer keeps embedding/head parameters below 20% of total model size (vs. 36.8% with the inherited 100k vocabulary), and inference speed (tokens/sec on a single V100) is explicitly tracked alongside accuracy. Training uses 1.6T tokens from diverse internet sources with AdamW optimizer and cosine LR decay.

## Method

The paper systematically ablates three design axes for a 1B-parameter decoder-only transformer: (1) architecture—replacing the 100k tokenizer with a 48k compact version and increasing depth from 12 to 21 layers (width 1792, expansion rate 2.77), where depth is the dominant performance factor; (2) parameter inheritance—initializing the tiny model from PanGu-π-7B by removing intermediate redundant layers (identified via layer-skipping ablation) and selecting intra-layer neurons via learnable binary masks, which outperforms L1/L2/Taylor pruning criteria; (3) multi-round training—retraining on a 50% loss-weighted subsample of the corpus to combat catastrophic forgetting, which yields a further ~3-point average gain over single-round training. The resulting PanGu-π-1B Pro and PanGu-π-1.5B Pro achieve an 8.87-point average improvement over the baseline PanGu-π-1B, with the 1.5B variant outperforming Qwen-1.8B despite 16.67% fewer parameters.

## Results

PanGu-π-1B Pro achieves an average score of 51.28 across 10 benchmarks, representing an 8.87-point improvement over the original PanGu-π-1B (42.41). The larger PanGu-π-1.5B Pro reaches 60.64 average, outperforming Qwen-1.8B (55.04) by 5.6 points while using 16.67% fewer parameters, and surpassing Phi2-2.7B (45.09) by 15.55 points despite being 1.8× smaller. On individual benchmarks, PanGu-π-1.5B Pro leads on MMLU (62.54), C3 (69.24), and AX-b (67.93), while Qwen-1.8B retains an edge on C-Eval (53.60 vs. 52.39) and EPRSTM (88.12 vs. 86.12).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PanGu-π-1.5B Pro (60.64 avg.) decisively outperforms Qwen-1.8B (55.04) by 5.6 points on average while using 16.67% fewer parameters, with particularly large margins on MMLU (+16.11), C3 (+10.94), and AX-b (+10.14), though Qwen retains a slight edge on C-Eval (+1.21) and EPRSTM (+2.00). Against Phi2-2.7B (45.09), which is 1.8× larger, PanGu-π-1.5B Pro leads by 15.55 points on average, and it surpasses Open-LLaMA-3B (43.66) by 16.98 points. In the 1B-parameter class, PanGu-π-1B Pro (51.28) dominates all competitors: it beats Phi-1.3B (41.99) by 9.29 points, RWKV-5-1.5B (39.94) by 11.34 points, and the LLaMA-derived 1.1–1.4B models (36.97–38.31) by 13–14 points. The ablation in Figure 1 attributes the 8.87-point gain from PanGu-π-1B to PanGu-π-1B Pro to tokenizer compression (+1.70), architecture tweak (+2.42), parameter inheritance (+5.68), and multiple-round training (remaining gain). Overall, PanGu-π-1.5B Pro achieves the best or second-best score on 9 out of 10 individual benchmarks in Table 8.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own PanGu-π architecture (Wang et al., 2023) by systematically rethinking every design choice for the tiny-model regime (1B–1.5B parameters), where the training recipes inherited from large LLMs no longer apply. It draws on structured pruning literature—LLM-Pruner (Ma et al., 2023) for L1/L2/Taylor criteria and Sheared LLaMA (Xia et al., 2023) for learnable binary masks—to formalize parameter inheritance from a 7B teacher into a 1B student. The GQA conversion (Ainslie et al., 2023) is adopted as a post-hoc efficiency step for edge deployment. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant 'scale up data and compute' narrative by arguing that for sub-2B models, architecture proportions, initialization, and multi-round training matter more than raw parameter count.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, ablation-backed recipe for building competitive tiny language models for edge deployment, with concrete recommendations (48k tokenizer, ~20 layers for 1B, depth over width, learnable-mask inheritance, two-round training at 50% sampling). The finding that catastrophic forgetting is significantly more severe in tiny models—and that a simple loss-weighted resampling strategy mitigates it—opens a direction for data-refinement methods tailored to small-capacity models. Open questions the authors flag include learning compact tokenizers end-to-end, hardware-aware architecture search, and new optimizers for multi-round tiny-model training. A limitation is that all experiments are conducted on a single 1.6T multilingual corpus and a single hardware platform (Ascend 910), so generalizability to other data distributions or architectures remains untested.
