## Problem

Adversarial robustness research has predominantly targeted continuous inputs, leaving categorical (tabular) data underexplored. Evaluating robustness on categorical data via attacks is computationally prohibitive because generating L0-norm bounded perturbations is NP-hard, and adversarial training on such data suffers from robust overfitting since heuristic search can only cover a small subset of the combinatorial adversarial space. The paper asks: how can adversarial robustness on categorical data be evaluated and enhanced without performing attacks?

## Input / Output

The method consumes one-hot encoded categorical features as a binary R^{p×m} matrix (where p is the number of features and m the number of category values per feature) along with a class label. For robustness evaluation, it outputs a scalar IGSG score combining the permutation-averaged Total Variation of feature-wise Integrated Gradient scores and the smoothed gradient magnitude, computed without generating any adversarial samples. For robustness enhancement, it outputs a trained classifier (MLP or Transformer) whose training objective includes the IGSG term as a regularizer. A key implementation detail is that IG computation introduces a dummy (m+1)-th category encoded as a zero vector to serve as a non-informative baseline for the Riemann integration.

## Method

The paper proposes IGSG = IG_avg + α·SG as an attack-free robustness metric, where IG_avg is the average Total Variation loss over feature-wise Integrated Gradient scores across all feature permutations (measuring attributional sensitivity imbalance), and SG is the mean q-norm of gradients computed over R randomly perturbed samples (measuring decision boundary curvature). The theoretical justification is an information-theoretic upper bound on adversarial risk (Theorem 3.2) derived via Pinsker's inequality, which decomposes into I(f;z_i) (suppressed by SG regularization) and feature-wise mutual information gap terms Ψ and Φ (suppressed by IG's TV regularization). For robustness enhancement, IGSG is incorporated as a regularization term in the training loss (min_θ E[ℓ] + β·IGSG), simultaneously smoothing the decision boundary and equalizing feature contributions. Ablation studies confirm that SG and IG regularization are complementary: SG alone leaves the model over-reliant on a few influential features, while IG alone lacks boundary smoothness, and their combination yields the strongest adversarial accuracy across FSGS, OMPGS, PCAA, FEAT, and GradAttack evaluations.

## Results

On the high-dimensional PEDec dataset (5,000 features), IGSG_reg achieves 86.5% adversarial accuracy under FSGS attack, surpassing the best baseline (AFD_L1 and JR at 74.3%) by 12.2 percentage points. On Splice, IGSG_reg reaches 89.6% under GradAttack (vs. 85.7% for Adv Train_L1) and 59.4% under FEAT (vs. 57.8% for JR). On Census, IGSG_reg achieves 67.2% under FSGS (vs. 63.4% for Fast-BAT_L1). Clean accuracy is maintained at 95.5–95.9% across all three datasets, confirming no accuracy-robustness trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IGSG_reg consistently outperforms all seven baselines on the high-dimensional PEDec dataset, where adversarial training methods suffer from robust overfitting due to the NP-hard combinatorial search space. Under FSGS on PEDec, IGSG_reg (86.5%) beats the strongest baselines AFD_L1 and JR (both 74.3%) by 12.2 points, and under GradAttack it leads JR (80.3%) by 5.3 points. On Splice, IGSG_reg wins on GradAttack (89.6% vs. 85.7% for Adv Train_L1) and FEAT (59.4% vs. 57.8% for JR), while IGR and JR collapse to near-zero accuracy (4.3% and 4.2% under FSGS), indicating their regularization is ineffective on this dataset. On Census, IGSG_reg achieves the best FSGS accuracy (67.2% vs. 63.4% for Fast-BAT_L1) but ties with Fast-BAT_L1 and JR on GradAttack (67.4%). The regularization baselines IGR and JR show highly inconsistent performance across datasets, whereas IGSG_reg maintains stable gains, confirming its domain-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) attribution-based interpretability (Integrated Gradients, Sundararajan et al. 2017; Smoothed Gradients, Smilkov et al. 2017), (2) information-theoretic generalization bounds (Xu & Raginsky 2017; Bu et al. 2019; Zhang et al. 2021), and (3) adversarial robustness for discrete/categorical inputs (FSGS, OMPGS, PAdvT). The key departure from prior work is the repurposing of IG and SG—originally interpretability and visualization tools—into a unified robustness metric (IGSG) that simultaneously serves as an attack-free evaluation criterion and a training regularizer, grounded in a novel information-theoretic adversarial risk bound (Theorem 3.2). Unlike adversarial training methods that require generating discrete adversarial samples (an NP-hard problem), IGSG_reg operates entirely through gradient-based optimization, avoiding the robust overfitting that the paper empirically demonstrates plagues search-based adversarial training on categorical data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for safety-critical tabular applications (malware detection, gene sequence classification, census analysis) where L0-norm categorical perturbations are the natural threat model and attack-based evaluation is prohibitively expensive. The 17–240× speedup over OMPGS for robustness evaluation and the avoidance of robust overfitting make this approach attractive for high-dimensional categorical settings (e.g., 5000 features in PEDec). Limitations include evaluation on only three datasets and two architectures (MLP, Transformer), a theoretical bound that is acknowledged as loose, and the Impact Statement's admission that the regularization may be counterproductive in degenerate cases (single-feature or perfectly balanced-feature datasets). The authors identify extension to text classification as future work, suggesting the method's applicability to NLP remains untested.
