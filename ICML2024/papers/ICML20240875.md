## Problem

Most 3D structure-based pre-trained protein models operate exclusively at the residue level (alpha carbon atoms), ignoring side chain atoms that are critical for tasks like molecular docking. Naively combining residue and atom information during pre-training fails because including full atom structure in the input causes information leakage that makes residue-level prediction tasks trivial, yielding insufficiently expressive residue representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240875/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a bilevel KNN graph (k=30) built from 3D protein coordinates, where alpha carbon nodes are shared between the residue-level and atom-level subgraphs, and a virtual origin node connects to all atoms and residues for global context. Node embeddings combine atom type, residue type, and ESM-650M sequence features; edges are encoded via Gaussian-kernel distances and Fourier-encoded direction angles in both global and residue-local coordinate systems. The model outputs both residue-level and atom-level node representations, which can be used independently or jointly for downstream tasks such as binding site prediction, function classification, and molecular docking.

## Method

Vabs-Net employs a two-track sparse attention architecture with an atom-atom track and a residue-residue track that interact exclusively through shared alpha carbon nodes, enabling information flow between levels without full cross-level connectivity. The key pre-training innovation is the Span Mask Protein Chain (SMPC) strategy: consecutive biologically meaningful residue spans (Poisson-distributed, mean length 6, covering 30% of the chain) are masked by retaining only their alpha carbon atoms while removing all side chain and other backbone atoms, preventing the model from trivially inferring residue types from surrounding atom geometry. At the atom level, a separate random noise strategy (Gaussian noise, scale 0.5 Å, on 30% of consecutive residues) provides atom-level pre-training signals. Pre-training objectives include residue type prediction (cross-entropy), seven torsion angle predictions (sin/cos with normalization loss), atom position and distance prediction via a movement head, and SASA prediction, all jointly optimized to learn complementary residue and atom representations.

## Results

On protein function prediction (EC/GO), Vabs-Net achieves F_max of 0.531 (BP), 0.695 (MF), 0.579 (CC), and 0.900 (EC), outperforming the strongest baseline GearNet-ESM-INR-MC (0.518/0.683/0.504/0.896) and SiamDiff-ESM (0.897 EC). On small molecule binding site prediction, Vabs-Net with pre-training reaches 60.1/52.0/58.8/56.3/64.1 IoU across five benchmarks, surpassing the best non-pre-trained baseline P2Rank (49.8/38.6/47.4/56.5/45.3) by 10+ points on average. For molecular docking via EquiBind, incorporating Vabs-Net features reduces Ligand RMSD from 8.91 (EquiBind alone) to 7.23 and Centroid RMSD from 6.02 to 4.11, outperforming GearNet (7.82/5.34) and SiamDiff (7.75/5.11).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vabs-Net outperforms all 13 baselines on EC/GO prediction, with the largest gains over residue-only models: +0.015 BP and +0.011 CC over GearNet-ESM-INR-MC, and +0.003 EC over SiamDiff-ESM (0.900 vs. 0.897). On small molecule binding site prediction, Vabs-Net (pre-trained) achieves 64.1 IoU on COACH420, beating P2Rank (45.3), GearNet (41.3), and SiamDiff (40.3) by 18.8, 22.8, and 23.8 points respectively, demonstrating the critical role of atom-level encoding. For DNA and RNA binding site prediction, Vabs-Net reaches 0.940 and 0.880 AUC, surpassing GraphBind (0.927/0.854), GearNet (0.849/0.778), and SiamDiff (0.823/0.735). In molecular docking, Vabs-Net features reduce EquiBind's Ligand RMSD by 1.68 Å (8.91→7.23) and Centroid RMSD by 1.91 Å (6.02→4.11), outperforming both GearNet (7.82/5.34) and SiamDiff (7.75/5.11) features. The ablation confirms that removing SMPC drops EC from 0.900 to 0.876 and COACH420 IoU from 64.1 to 63.0, while removing atom-level encoding drops COACH420 IoU to 56.3, validating both the bilevel architecture and the span masking strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the structure-based protein pre-training line initiated by GearNet (residue-level geometric pre-training) and SiamDiff (atom-level diffusion-based pre-training) by unifying both levels into a single bilevel graph architecture. It adapts the sparse attention mechanism from Graphormer/Uni-Mol to the all-atom protein setting and borrows the position movement prediction head from Graphormer-3D. The key conceptual departure is the identification of information leakage when atom structure is included in the input, which renders residue-level pre-training tasks trivial; the proposed SMPC strategy (span-masking side chain atoms) is the paper's central methodological contribution to resolve this conflict.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that naively combining atom and residue information degrades residue representations due to information leakage is a useful diagnostic for the protein pre-training community, explaining why prior bilevel attempts have not succeeded. The practical impact is significant for atom-sensitive downstream tasks like molecular docking, where side chain geometry is essential. However, the pre-training dataset (130K structures) is orders of magnitude smaller than sequence-based corpora (1.1B), and the model is limited to single chains, leaving open whether the approach scales to multi-chain complexes or benefits from much larger structural datasets.
