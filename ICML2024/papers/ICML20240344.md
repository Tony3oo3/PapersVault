## Problem

Existing Wasserstein Gradient Flow (WGF) generative models based on the JKO scheme suffer from quadratic training complexity O(K²) in the number of JKO steps, because sampling from each intermediate distribution μ_k requires sequential inference through all k preceding transport networks. This bottleneck restricts model size and training time, preventing WGF models from achieving competitive performance on high-dimensional image datasets like CIFAR-10 and CelebA-HQ.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240344/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a d-dimensional standard Gaussian noise vector as input and produces a single generated image in one forward pass (NFE = 1), unlike prior JKO models that require K sequential network evaluations. For image generation, the transport network T_θ uses a DCGAN backbone (~0.4M params, 'Small') or NCSN++ backbone (~48M params, 'Large'), operating on 32×32 CIFAR-10 or 256×256 CelebA-HQ images. An auxiliary random variable z is injected into T_θ during training to improve sample diversity.

## Method

The key insight is that each JKO step is equivalent to a Source-fixed Unbalanced Optimal Transport problem, which admits a semi-dual formulation. This yields an adversarial training objective where a transport network T_θ and a potential/discriminator network v_φ are trained alternately: v_φ is updated to maximize a divergence-based loss, while T_θ minimizes a cost-minus-potential loss c(ŷ_old, ŷ) − v_φ(ŷ) with c(x,y) = ‖x−y‖²/(2dh). The critical reparametrization trick replaces training of incremental maps ΔT_k (from μ_k to μ_{k+1}) with training of direct maps T_k (from μ to μ_{k+1}), so that sampling from μ_k requires only a single inference of T_{k−1} rather than composing k networks, reducing complexity from O(K²) to O(K). Each phase k initializes T_k from the previous T_{k−1}, and the functional F can be either KL or Jensen-Shannon divergence, with R1 regularization applied to the discriminator.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (32×32), S-JKO with the Large backbone achieves an FID of 2.62 (KLD) and 2.66 (JSD), outperforming the previous best WGF-based model NSGF (FID 5.55) by a large margin and approaching state-of-the-art diffusion models (Score SDE VE: 2.20, LSGM: 2.10). On CelebA-HQ (256×256), S-JKO (JSD) achieves FID 5.46, the first WGF-based model to reach competitive performance on high-resolution image generation. The Small backbone variant achieves FID 8.78 on CIFAR-10 in only 6 hours of training, compared to ≥30–50 hours for prior JKO-based methods (JKO-Flow: FID 23.1, JKO-iFlow: FID 29.1), demonstrating the O(K) vs O(K²) complexity reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S-JKO (Large) achieves FID 2.62 on CIFAR-10, outperforming the second-best WGF-based model NSGF (5.55) by nearly 3 FID points, and surpassing prior JKO-based methods JKO-Flow (23.1) and JKO-iFlow (29.1) by a wide margin. Against OT-based baselines, S-JKO (Large) beats UOTM (Large) (2.97) and Source-fixed UOTM (Large) (7.53), confirming that multi-phase JKO training mitigates the inherent distribution error of single-step UOTM. On CelebA-HQ 256×256, S-JKO (JSD) achieves FID 5.46, outperforming Source-fixed UOTM (7.36) and UOTM (6.36), and approaching diffusion baselines like Score SDE VP (7.23) and LSGM (7.22). The Small backbone variant (FID 8.78, 6h training) dramatically outperforms JKO-Flow (FID 23.1, ≥50h) and JKO-iFlow (FID 29.1, ≥30h) at comparable model size, validating the O(K) complexity reduction. On synthetic multi-modal datasets (Two Circles, 25-Gaussian Mixture), S-JKO successfully captures all modes while UOTM variants suffer mode collapse near the origin, demonstrating the advantage of iterative WGF approximation over single-step transport.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the JKO scheme (Jordan et al., 1998) for numerically approximating Wasserstein Gradient Flows, and on the semi-dual formulation of Unbalanced Optimal Transport (Chizat et al., 2018; Choi et al., 2023a). The key conceptual departure is establishing that each JKO step is equivalent to a Source-fixed UOT problem, which allows replacing the per-step transport map parametrization (used by Mokrov et al., 2021; Fan et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2023) with a single reparametrized transport network. This, combined with a reparametrization trick that avoids composing all prior step networks, reduces training complexity from O(K²) to O(K) while retaining the multi-step WGF structure that mitigates the inherent distribution error of single-step UOTM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

S-JKO is the first JKO-based generative model to achieve near-SOTA FID on high-resolution image datasets (5.46 on CelebA-HQ 256×256, 2.62 on CIFAR-10), demonstrating that WGF-based methods can be practically competitive with diffusion and GAN approaches. The O(K) complexity reduction is a meaningful efficiency win that makes WGF models viable for larger architectures and datasets. Open questions include the unexplained robustness difference between KLD and JSD to large step sizes h, whether the approach extends to even higher resolutions or video generation, and the accuracy-scalability tradeoff observed in low-dimensional numerical comparisons where ICNN JKO remains more faithful to the true WGF solution.
