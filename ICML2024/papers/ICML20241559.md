## Problem

Text-to-music diffusion models offer only high-level stylistic control via text prompts, while existing fine-grained control methods either require expensive supervised fine-tuning per control modality (e.g., Music ControlNet) or rely on approximate guidance during sampling that limits expressivity. The paper asks whether a single training-free, inference-time procedure can accept arbitrary differentiable control signals and achieve fine-grained, time-varying control over pre-trained TTM diffusion models without any model modification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241559/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained TTM diffusion model (512×160 Mel-scaled spectrogram, ~6 s at 22.05 kHz), class-conditional text tags (genre/mood/tempo), and a task-specific target control signal—such as a smoothed dB intensity curve, a 12-pitch-class chromagram melody, a self-similarity matrix for musical structure, or a reference spectrogram region for inpainting/outpainting/looping. It outputs an optimized initial noise latent x_T that, when passed through the standard DDIM sampler (20 steps) and a vocoder (BigVGAN/MusicHifi), produces a music spectrogram and decoded audio matching the target control while preserving text-conditioned style.

## Method

DITTO formulates control as an optimization over the initial noise latent x_T: it backpropagates an arbitrary differentiable feature-matching loss L(f(x_0), y) through the full diffusion sampling chain to update x_T, where f is a task-specific feature extractor (e.g., RMS energy through the vocoder for intensity, log-chromagram for melody, MFCC self-similarity for structure). The key technical enabler is gradient checkpointing applied at each diffusion step, which discards intermediate U-Net activations during the forward pass and recomputes them during backprop, reducing memory to one model call's worth of activations plus the T intermediate noisy tensors. This makes backprop through 20 DDIM steps (with CFG doubling forward passes) tractable on a single GPU (~5 GB) without modifying the sampler or model architecture. The procedure requires no fine-tuning—only x_T is optimized with Adam (lr=5e-3) for 70–150 steps—making it applicable to any differentiable control signal and any diffusion sampler, in contrast to DOODL which is restricted to the invertible EDICT sampler and incurs 2× the model calls and memory.

## Results

On intensity control, DITTO achieves an MSE of 4.758, dramatically outperforming Music ControlNet (38.411) and FreeDoM (23.292), while matching DOODL (4.785) with better FAD (0.682 vs 0.695) and CLAP (0.433 vs 0.342). On melody control, DITTO reaches 82.625% accuracy, beating Music ControlNet (81.353%) and substantially outperforming FreeDoM (31.544%). On musical structure control, DITTO achieves the best FAD (0.632) and CLAP (0.418), slightly trailing FreeDoM on MSE (0.024 vs 0.018). DITTO is also ~2x faster (1.86s vs 4.18s per step) and uses ~half the GPU memory (5.0 GB vs 8.3 GB) compared to DOODL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DITTO decisively beats Music ControlNet on intensity control (MSE 4.758 vs 38.411) with zero supervised training, and narrowly wins on melody accuracy (82.6% vs 81.4%), while subjective listening tests confirm DITTO is preferred 65% of the time over ControlNet on intensity but roughly tied on melody (48% wins). Against FreeDoM, DITTO wins on intensity (MSE 4.758 vs 23.292) and melody (82.6% vs 31.5%) but FreeDoM slightly edges out on structure MSE (0.018 vs 0.024); DITTO is strongly preferred in listening tests (71–80% wins). Against DOODL, DITTO matches or slightly beats on control metrics (intensity MSE 4.758 vs 4.785, melody 82.6% vs 81.6%) while being ~2x faster (1.86s vs 4.18s per optimization step) and using ~half the GPU memory (5.0 GB vs 8.3 GB), with DOODL exhibiting reward hacking (CLAP drops to 0.342 vs DITTO's 0.433). For outpainting and inpainting, DITTO achieves the lowest FAD across all overlap/gap sizes (e.g., 0.698 at o=3s outpainting, 0.686 at gap=2s inpainting), outperforming MultiDiffusion, Guidance Gradients, Naive Masking, and FreeDoM, with qualitative results showing DITTO avoids the audible 'seams' that plague all other baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

DITTO repurposes the concept of optimizing initial noise latents (introduced by DOODL for image generation) but decouples it from the EDICT invertible sampler, instead using standard gradient checkpointing on any diffusion sampler (DDIM, DPM-Solver++, etc.) to make backpropagation through the full sampling chain tractable. It departs from training-based control (Music ControlNet) by requiring zero fine-tuning, and from guidance-based methods (FreeDoM, Guidance Gradients, MultiDiffusion) by computing exact gradients on real model outputs rather than approximations at intermediate noise levels. The music-specific applications—particularly looping and musical structure control via self-similarity matrices—are framed as the first such formulations for TTM diffusion models, extending the controllable generation task beyond the intensity and melody controls already explored by Music ControlNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that diffusion initial noise latents encode substantial low-frequency musical structure (validated by the intra-batch variance experiment) challenges the prevailing assumption that x_T is semantically inert, and opens a general-purpose, training-free control interface applicable to any differentiable feature. However, the practical latency (~82s per sample vs. 0.6s for the base model) and the evaluation on a single 41M-parameter model with 6-second clips limit immediate deployment; scaling to larger architectures and longer contexts, achieving real-time interaction via faster samplers, and understanding how the latent-space expressivity insight generalizes beyond music remain key open questions.
