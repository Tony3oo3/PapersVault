## Problem

Finding a truly general, scalable unsupervised pre-training objective for generalist RL policies from offline data remains an open question. Existing approaches each have critical limitations: behavioral cloning requires expert demonstrations, goal-conditioned RL only yields goal-reaching behaviors with limited diversity, and unsupervised skill discovery struggles with scalability and offline learning. No prior method provides a single unified objective that captures diverse, optimal, long-horizon behaviors from unlabeled data while offering a clear zero-shot adaptation mechanism for arbitrary downstream tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled offline trajectory data D (state-action sequences with no quality assumptions — data can be optimal, suboptimal, or random) and produces a latent-conditioned policy π(a|s,z) where z is a unit vector in a learned Euclidean latent space R^D (D=32 for goal-conditioned tasks, D=50 for zero-shot RL). At test time, the policy is 'prompted' by a latent vector z computed either via closed-form linear regression against a new reward function (zero-shot RL) or by normalizing the latent displacement φ(g)−φ(s) toward a goal state (goal-conditioned RL). Test-time planning further refines z using only pre-computed Hilbert representations and elementary algebraic operations, requiring no additional neural network queries.

## Method

HILPs employ a two-stage offline pre-training procedure. First, a Hilbert representation φ:S→R^D is trained to preserve temporal distances (d*(s,g)≈||φ(s)−φ(g)||) by parameterizing a goal-conditioned value function as V(s,g)=−||φ(s)−φ(g)|| and minimizing an IQL-based temporal difference loss with expectile regression and hindsight relabeling. Second, a latent-conditioned policy π(a|s,z) is trained with offline RL (IQL) using an intrinsic directional reward r(s,z,s')=⟨φ(s')−φ(s), z⟩ where z is sampled uniformly from the unit sphere S^{D−1}, forcing the policy to learn optimal movements in every possible latent direction. The key insight is that the inner product structure of the Hilbert space makes the learned policy a successor-feature-like representation: arbitrary reward functions can be matched to a latent prompt z* via linear regression (zero-shot RL), and goal-reaching is achieved by setting z*=(φ(g)−φ(s))/||φ(g)−φ(s)||, which is provably optimal under bounded embedding errors (Theorem 5.1). An optional test-time midpoint planning procedure recursively finds equidistant subgoals in the latent space to mitigate embedding distortion on long-horizon tasks.

## Results

On the ExORL zero-shot RL benchmark (4 environments × 4 tasks × 4 datasets), HILP achieves the best overall interquartile mean (IQM) among 9 methods, outperforming the prior state-of-the-art forward-backward (FB) representations and all successor feature variants. On offline goal-conditioned RL (D4RL), HILP-Plan (with test-time midpoint planning) achieves 64.5±10.2 on antmaze-large-diverse and 59.2±12.7 on antmaze-ultra-diverse, surpassing the dedicated GC-IQL baseline (56.0±6.0 and 40.8±11.1 respectively). On hierarchical RL, HILP achieves a 51.3 average success rate across 6 D4RL datasets, outperforming OPAL (45.7), with the largest gap on antmaze-ultra-diverse (43.8 vs 21.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the forward-backward (FB) representations of Touati et al. (2023), HILP achieves a clear win on goal-conditioned RL: FB scores 0.0 on all four AntMaze datasets while HILP-Plan reaches 50.8–64.5, and on zero-shot RL HILP achieves the best overall IQM across all 256 evaluation settings. Successor feature baselines (FDM, Lap, AE, IDM, Rand, CL) are similarly dominated on goal-conditioned tasks (all near 0 on AntMaze) and trail HILP on zero-shot RL. Against dedicated goal-conditioned RL methods, HILP-Plan outperforms GC-IQL on all four AntMaze datasets (e.g., 59.2 vs 40.8 on antmaze-ultra-diverse) and GC-BC by large margins, while GC-IQL retains a slight edge on kitchen-mixed (59.5 vs 51.9). On zero-shot RL, HILP significantly outperforms GC-TD3, confirming that HILPs capture more diverse behaviors than goal-reaching policies alone. In the hierarchical RL setting, HILP outperforms OPAL (Ajay et al., 2021) by 5.6 points on average, with the largest gap on antmaze-ultra-diverse (43.8 vs 21.0) and antmaze-ultra-play (47.8 vs 22.8), suggesting HILPs capture more optimal long-horizon skills than VAE-based trajectory modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

HILPs build on the ℓ² temporal-distance representation parameterization from prior robotic representation learning (Sermanet et al., 2018; Nair et al., 2022; Ma et al., 2023) and the IQL-based goal-conditioned value learning of HiQL (Park et al., 2023) to train a distance-preserving Hilbert embedding. The zero-shot RL prompting scheme is directly derived from the successor features / forward-backward framework (Touati et al., 2023), while the directional spanning objective is closely related to METRA (Park et al., 2024). The key departure is decoupling representation learning from policy learning to enable fully offline unsupervised pre-training: unlike METRA (which requires online rollouts) or BC-based methods (which require expert data), HILPs train a state-spanning latent-conditioned policy purely from unlabeled offline trajectories using an inner-product intrinsic reward.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HILPs provide a single unified offline pre-training recipe that subsumes goal-conditioned RL as a special case while capturing far more diverse behaviors, enabling zero-shot adaptation to arbitrary reward functions without additional training. The test-time midpoint planning—requiring only elementary algebraic operations on pre-computed representations rather than neural network queries—is a practically attractive property for deployment. Key limitations include the assumption of deterministic MDPs, the potential inexpressiveness of symmetric Hilbert spaces for highly asymmetric environments (e.g., gravity-dominated dynamics), and the assumption that state space and dynamics remain unchanged at evaluation. Open questions the authors highlight include whether local isometries could handle partially observable settings, whether directional latent movements are sufficient for full expressivity, and whether fine-tuning or few-shot adaptation might outperform pure zero-shot prompting.
