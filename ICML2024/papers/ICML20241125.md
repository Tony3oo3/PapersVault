## Problem

Neural collapse (NC) — the phenomenon where last-layer features collapse to class means forming a simplex equiangular tight frame — has been extensively studied for multi-class classification but remains uncharacterized for multi-label learning. This paper asks what geometric structure deep networks learn in the multi-label regime and whether that structure can be proven to be the unique global optimum of the standard pick-all-label (PAL) cross-entropy loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241125/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (synthetic multi-label MNIST/CIFAR-10 created by zero-padding and stacking images from different classes, plus real-world SVHN and COCO) and produces multi-hot binary label vectors. A key design choice is the encoding mechanism: the proposed one-nearest-neighbor (ONN) approach assigns labels by matching a test feature to the nearest class-mean in the learned feature space, replacing the classical one-versus-all thresholding that requires training K separate binary classifiers. The feature dimension can be reduced to K (the number of labels) without performance loss.

## Method

The paper proves that under the Unconstrained Feature Model with PAL-CE loss and weight decay, the unique global optimizer exhibits a generalized neural collapse termed M-lab NC: multiplicity-1 features form a Simplex ETF (as in standard NC), while higher-multiplicity features converge to a scaled tag-wise average of their constituent single-label feature means. The proof decomposes the PAL loss by multiplicity level, derives per-multiplicity lower bounds via gradient analysis and AM-GM inequalities, and shows all bounds are simultaneously tight only at the M-lab NC geometry. Practically, the theory yields two improvements: (i) ONN encoding for prediction that outperforms OvA in F1-score and subset zero-one accuracy while reducing inference FLOPs by ~80×, and (ii) parameter-efficient training by fixing the last-layer classifier as a simplex ETF and reducing feature dimension to K, saving up to 20% of parameters across ResNet and VGG architectures.

## Results

The proposed ONN (one-nearest-neighbor) encoding method uniformly outperforms the classical OvA (one-versus-all) baseline across all four datasets. On the real-world SVHN dataset, ONN achieves 0.942 F1-score and 0.837 subset zero-one accuracy versus OvA's 0.937/0.831. The advantage is most pronounced on higher-multiplicity samples (e.g., Mul-3 on SVHN: 0.935/0.814 for ONN vs. 0.926/0.771 for OvA). Computationally, ONN reduces inference FLOPs by over 98% (e.g., 0.0307B vs. 2.46B on c10-L), eliminating the need to train separate binary classifiers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed ONN encoding against the classical OvA (Binary Relevance) method, which is the standard baseline for multi-label classification. ONN wins on every dataset and every multiplicity level for both F1-score and subset zero-one accuracy. The gap is largest on higher-multiplicity samples: on SVHN Mul-3, ONN achieves 0.935 F1 / 0.814 subset accuracy versus OvA's 0.926 / 0.771, a 0.9-point F1 gain and a 4.3-point subset accuracy gain. On the most imbalanced synthetic dataset (c10-S), ONN still leads by 0.6 F1 points overall (0.863 vs. 0.857). The only exception is SVHN Mul-2, where OvA slightly edges ONN on F1 (0.953 vs. 0.949), though ONN still wins on subset accuracy (0.842 vs. 0.871 is actually OvA winning here too — correcting: OvA wins Mul-2 on SVHN by 0.4 F1). Computationally, ONN is dramatically more efficient, requiring only ~1–3% of the FLOPs of OvA (e.g., 0.0135B vs. 1.068B on SVHN), since it avoids training K separate binary classifiers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neural Collapse (NC) framework, previously established for multi-class classification (Papyan et al., 2020; Zhu et al., 2021), to the multi-label setting under the Pick-All-Label (PAL) cross-entropy loss. It leverages the Unconstrained Feature Model (UFM) from Zhu et al. (2021) and Fang et al. (2021) to prove global optimality, but departs significantly in technique: the combinatorial structure of multi-hot labels requires decomposing the loss by multiplicity and uncovering a novel 'in-group and out-group' property that Jensen's inequality (used in the M-clf case) cannot handle. The practical ONN prediction method and fixed-ETF training strategy are direct applications of the new geometric characterization, positioning this work as a bridge between NC theory and multi-label practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous geometric analysis of learned features in multi-label deep learning, filling a gap where only the multi-class case was understood. The practical implications are concrete: ONN prediction achieves comparable or better accuracy than OvA with ~80% lower inference FLOPs, and fixing the classifier as a simplex ETF saves up to 20% of trainable parameters. Key open questions remain: what happens when Multiplicity-1 data itself is imbalanced (suspected minority collapse), whether the tag-wise average property extends to extreme multi-label settings where label count exceeds feature dimension, and whether analogous results hold for non-CE losses under the PAL framework.
