## Problem

Existing approaches to detecting LLM hallucinations—entropy-based uncertainty, verbalized confidence, and learned truthfulness directions—are either intractable for open-ended generative tasks, sensitive to decoding hyperparameters, or fail to generalize across tasks and prompts. The paper asks whether a geometric property of internal model representations, specifically the local intrinsic dimension (LID) of activations, can serve as a principled, task-agnostic signal for truthfulness without requiring task-specific training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt fed through a frozen LLM (Llama-2-7B/13B), extracting the D-dimensional hidden state of the last generated token at a selected intermediate layer, plus a reference set of 500 representations (from the same or a different QA dataset) used as nearest neighbors for the LID computation. It outputs a single scalar LID value per sample, where lower values indicate higher likelihood of truthfulness; this score is then thresholded or ranked to produce a binary correct/incorrect prediction evaluated via AUROC.

## Method

The core insight is that truthful continuations, being closer to the natural-language distribution, occupy lower-dimensional manifolds in representation space, while hallucinated outputs mix the human prompt distribution with the model's own generation distribution, inflating the local intrinsic dimension. The paper applies the MLE estimator of Levina & Bickel (2004), which models neighbor counts via a Poisson process parameterized by the intrinsic dimension, and augments it with the distance-aware GeoMLE correction of Gomtsyan et al. (2019) to account for non-uniform density in LLM activations. Layer selection is done empirically by choosing the layer whose summed LID values are maximal, shifted forward by one or two layers to align with detection performance. The entire pipeline is training-free: no classifier or probe is fitted, and the LID score directly serves as the truthfulness indicator, with bootstrapping and heteroskedastic weighted polynomial regression used to stabilize the GeoMLE estimate across different neighbor counts.

## Results

On four generative QA tasks (TriviaQA, CoQA, HotpotQA, TydiQA-GP), LID-GeoMLE achieves the best averaged AUROC of 0.746 (Llama-2-7B) and 0.764 (Llama-2-13B), outperforming the strongest entropy-based baseline (Semantic Entropy) by 5.2 points (7B) and 2.8 points (13B). On TriviaQA 0-shot specifically, LID methods surpass the best uncertainty method by 8% AUROC. The trained classifier SAPLMA (0.614/0.618) and verbalized uncertainty P(True) (0.540/0.576) are substantially outperformed, confirming that geometric representation features are more informative than learned probes or self-reported confidence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LID-GeoMLE consistently outperforms all five baselines across both model sizes and all four datasets. Against the strongest entropy-based method (Semantic Entropy, Kuhn et al. 2022), LID-GeoMLE gains 5.2 AUROC points on Llama-2-7B (0.746 vs 0.694) and 2.8 points on 13B (0.764 vs 0.736); the gap is largest on TriviaQA 0-shot where the improvement reaches 8%. The predictive and length-normalized entropy variants (Malinin & Gales 2020) are weaker still, averaging 0.663–0.664 (7B) and 0.702–0.716 (13B). The trained multi-layer classifier SAPLMA (Azaria & Mitchell 2023), which requires 3,000 labeled examples per dataset, is outperformed by a wide margin (0.614 vs 0.746 on 7B), indicating that LID captures geometric structure that supervised probes miss. Verbalized uncertainty P(True) (Kadavath et al. 2022) performs worst overall (0.540/0.576), sometimes falling below chance on TriviaQA 5-shot (0.471/0.511). SAR (Duan et al. 2023), evaluated only in the robustness appendix, also trails LID-GeoMLE by 3–6 AUROC points under both Rouge-L and NLI-based indicator functions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the maximum likelihood estimator for local intrinsic dimension (Levina & Bickel, 2004) and its geometry-aware correction (Gomtsyan et al., 2019) from the data-manifold analysis literature into the LLM hallucination-detection setting. It departs from the dominant uncertainty-based approaches (predictive entropy, semantic entropy, verbalized confidence) that operate at the output distribution level, and from representation-probing methods (SAPLMA, truthful-direction search) that require task-specific training, by instead exploiting a geometric property of intermediate activations that is task-agnostic and requires no additional training. The layer-selection heuristic and the bootstrapped distance-aware MLE are the paper's specific methodological contributions on top of the standard LID machinery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work offers a principled, training-free geometric signal for hallucination detection that generalizes across tasks (cross-dataset neighbors still work) and is stable to prompting format, addressing practical pain points of entropy-based methods. The observation that instruction tuning monotonically increases representation intrinsic dimension and that LID tracks generalization fluctuations during training opens a potentially useful diagnostic for model development beyond hallucination detection. Key limitations include reliance on white-box access to intermediate representations, evaluation restricted to English QA tasks, and unexplored applicability to faithfulness, adversarial robustness, or multi-modal settings.
