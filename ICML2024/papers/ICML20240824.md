## Problem

Deep Multi-View Clustering (DMVC) models have never been studied under adversarial threat: no well-defined attack objective exists for multi-view settings, and no defense mechanism has been developed. The paper addresses two questions: how to formulate and execute effective adversarial attacks that exploit the unique complementarity and consistency properties of multi-view data, and how to build a DMVC model that is robust to such attacks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240824/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attack takes V clean multi-view images per sample (e.g., visible + infrared for RegDB, original + Gaussian-noised for NoisyMNIST) and produces per-view perturbations δ constrained to an ℓ₂-ball of radius ε, which are added to the inputs to generate adversarial examples. The defense model (AR-DMVC-AM) consumes both clean and PGD-generated adversarial multi-view inputs through view-specific CNN encoders and outputs soft cluster assignments via a DDC module; the final hard label is argmax of the assignment vector. The GAN attack network uses a 4-layer convolutional generator with InstanceNorm and a 3-layer convolutional discriminator per view.

## Method

The attack is a GAN-based framework whose generator is trained with a saddle-point objective combining four losses: a complementarity-disruption term (L_a-com) that maximizes the squared distance between pre- and post-attack view-specific representations, a consistency-disruption term (L_a-con) that does the same for the multi-view consensus representation, an ℓ₂-norm constraint penalty, and the vanilla GAN adversarial loss. For defense, AR-DMVC integrates adversarial training into a contrastive-learning-based multi-view clustering model (CL-MVC) by adding a worst-case PGD-generated adversarial contrastive loss that enforces consistent attack embeddings across views. The key theoretical contribution is an Attack Mitigator (AM) regularizer derived from an information-theoretic analysis: Theorem 4.2 shows that the conditional mutual information I(x̃; ã | x) between adversarial inputs and their cluster assignments is upper-bounded by the KL divergence D_KL(p(ã|x̃) ‖ p(a|x)), so minimizing this KL term explicitly suppresses adversarial information leakage into the clustering output. The final objective (Eq. 14) sums the clean CL-MVC loss, the adversarial CL-MVC loss weighted by λ, and the AM KL regularizer weighted by γ.

## Results

On NoisyMNIST, the GAN-based attack reduces post-attack ACC from 0.99 (CoMVC, AECoDDC) to 0.11–0.20 for all baselines, while AR-DMVC-AM retains 0.85 ACC and 0.85 NMI post-attack. On RegDB, AR-DMVC-AM achieves 0.52 post-attack ACC versus 0.22–0.43 for baselines. On NoisyFashion, AR-DMVC-AM maintains 0.67 post-attack ACC compared to 0.25–0.47 for baselines. On PatchedMNIST, AR-DMVC-AM achieves 0.46 post-attack ACC versus 0.17–0.48 for baselines, with the largest margin over EAMC (0.17) and AECoDDC (0.21).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AR-DMVC-AM dominates all five baseline families on post-attack ACC across every dataset. Against EAMC (Zhou & Shen, CVPR'20), the margin is largest on NoisyMNIST (0.85 vs. 0.11 ACC) and PatchedMNIST (0.46 vs. 0.17). SiMVC/CoMVC (Trosten et al., CVPR'21) suffer severe degradation on NoisyMNIST (0.20 ACC) and RegDB (0.25–0.30), while AR-DMVC-AM retains 0.85 and 0.52 respectively. Multi-VAE (Xu et al., ICCV'21) is the most robust baseline on NoisyMNIST (0.39 post-attack ACC) but still trails AR-DMVC-AM by 0.46. AECoDDC/InfoDDC (Trosten et al., CVPR'23) collapse to 0.11 and 0.22 on NoisyMNIST, and SEM (Xu et al., NeurIPS'23) drops to 0.11, both far below AR-DMVC-AM's 0.85. The Attack Mitigator (AM) regularization consistently adds 0.03–0.10 ACC over AR-DMVC alone, confirming its role in improving robustness transferability (Table 2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-view adversarial clustering line (Chhabra et al., 2022, which used GANs to attack deep clustering models) to the multi-view setting by formulating attack objectives that simultaneously target view-specific complementarity and cross-view consistency. The defense builds on the CL-MVC architecture (contrastive learning from Chen et al., 2020 + DDC clustering from Trosten et al., 2021) and introduces adversarial training into the unsupervised multi-view regime, a setting where standard adversarial training techniques are difficult to apply directly. The information-theoretic Attack Mitigator draws on the multi-view information bottleneck framework (Federici et al., 2020) to provide a provable upper bound on adversarial information leakage into cluster assignments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic demonstration that state-of-the-art DMVC models are highly vulnerable to adversarial perturbations, with post-attack accuracy drops of 50–90% across seven baselines, which has direct implications for safety-critical multi-view applications. The proposed AR-DMVC-AM defense, backed by an information-theoretic guarantee, substantially narrows this gap (e.g., 0.99→0.85 ACC vs. 0.99→0.11 for AECoDDC on NoisyMNIST). Key limitations include the requirement for per-view representation access (inapplicable to unified-representation models) and the high computational cost of PGD-based adversarial training, leaving open the question of efficient, scalable defenses for large-scale multi-view deployments.
