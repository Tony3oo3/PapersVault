## Problem

Existing GFlowNet training strategies (Flow Matching, Detailed Balance, Trajectory Balance) are value-based in the RL sense, relying on flow-balance objectives that require careful design of trajectory samplers to balance exploration and exploitation in enormous combinatorial spaces with isolated modes. This paper identifies that no policy-based training framework existed for GFlowNets, despite the structural similarity between GFlowNet flow-balance and RL, and asks whether policy gradient methods can provide more robust gradient estimation for GFlowNet training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a non-negative reward function R(x) over a combinatorial object space (e.g., molecular graphs, nucleotide sequences, grid points), a graded DAG defining the generative process (states as partial objects, actions as incremental modifications), and a total flow estimator Z. It produces a trained forward policy P_F whose terminal distribution P_F^⊤(x) is proportional to R(x), along with an optionally co-trained backward policy P_B. A key structural constraint is that the DAG must be graded (topologically ordered), and the rewards are policy-dependent—defined as log-ratios of forward and backward transition probabilities—rather than fixed external signals.

## Method

The paper introduces policy-dependent rewards R_F(s,a;θ) = log(π_F(s,a;θ)/π_B(s',a;θ)) that reformulate the Trajectory Balance objective as minimizing the expected accumulated reward in an absorbing MDP over the DAG, enabling direct policy gradient computation. It derives a TRPO-style constrained objective (Eq. 7) for conservative forward policy updates, and formulates backward policy design as a second RL problem with guided rewards R_B^G(s',a;φ) = log(π_B(s',a;φ)/π_G(s',a)), where P_G is a non-Markovian conditional guided distribution. A coupled training strategy (Algorithm 1) jointly optimizes both the forward policy objective J_F and the backward policy design objective J_B^G in a single loop, avoiding the two-phase training of prior guided methods. Theoretical guarantees include a joint upper bound (Theorem 3.5) showing that minimizing J_F and J_B^G together decreases a composite objective, a monotonic performance improvement bound (Theorem 3.6) motivating the TRPO constraint, and an asymptotic convergence rate (Theorem 3.7) for the policy gradient updates.

## Results

Across all experimental domains (hyper-grid, sequence design, molecular design, and Bayesian Network structure learning), the proposed policy-based methods (RL-U, RL-B, RL-T, RL-G) achieve faster convergence and comparable or better final D_TV / Acc values than all value-based baselines (DB, TB, TB-Sub, TB-TS). On the 256×256 hyper-grid, RL-G achieves the smallest converged D_TV and converges substantially faster than all competing methods. On SIX6 and QM9, RL-T exhibits the fastest convergence rate in mode accuracy (Acc), while the converged Acc values of policy-based and TB-based methods are similar. The ablation study confirms that RL-U with λ < 1 (functional value baseline) outperforms TB-U across all γ settings in both convergence speed and final D_TV.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ten training strategies: DB-U, DB-B, TB-U, TB-B, TB-Sub, TB-TS (value-based) and RL-U, RL-B, RL-T, RL-G (policy-based). Policy-based methods consistently outperform all value-based methods in convergence speed across every task. On the 256×256 hyper-grid, RL-G achieves the lowest final D_TV, beating TB-U, TB-B, TB-Sub, and TB-TS; RL-T is more stable than RL-U during training, confirming the benefit of trust-region constraints. On 128×128, policy-based and TB-based methods reach similar converged D_TV, but policy-based methods converge significantly faster, while DB-based methods (DB-U, DB-B) are clearly inferior in both convergence rate and final metric. On SIX6 and QM9, RL-T has the fastest Acc convergence, and RL-G performs similarly to RL-B (parameterized P_B) but slightly better than RL-U (uniform P_B), indicating that guided backward policy design provides a modest but consistent gain over a fixed uniform backward policy. TB-TS (Thompson Sampling) does not close the gap to the policy-based methods, suggesting that exploration-based sampling improvements are less effective than the robust gradient estimation provided by the policy-gradient formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes GFlowNet training—previously treated exclusively as value-based RL (flow matching, detailed balance, trajectory balance, sub-trajectory balance)—as a policy-based RL problem by introducing policy-dependent rewards that express flow balance as expected accumulated reward. It extends the gradient equivalence between TB and KL objectives (Malkin et al., 2022b) to include all parameter gradients and remove infeasible dependencies on the target distribution P*(x). The TRPO-based objective (Eq. 7) and coupled forward/backward training strategy draw directly on Schulman et al. (2015) and Shen et al. (2023), respectively, but adapt them to the absorbing Markov chain structure inherent to graded DAGs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The policy-based framing opens the door to the entire policy gradient toolkit—GAE, natural gradients, PPO, mirror descent—for GFlowNet training, which prior value-based methods could not exploit. The coupled training strategy (RL-G) provides a theoretically grounded alternative to the ad-hoc mixing of P_B and P_G used in Shen et al. (2023), with convergence guarantees (Theorem 3.7) and monotonic improvement via TRPO (Theorem 3.6). Key limitations include the on-policy nature of the methods (restricting data efficiency), the restriction to graded DAGs (though noted as extendable to cyclic graphs), and the acknowledged risk of local optima in very large state spaces. Open questions include off-policy variants via importance sampling, extension to general graphs with cycles, and whether the policy-based advantage persists at much larger scales.
