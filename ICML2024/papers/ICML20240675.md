## Problem

Existing white-box LLM jailbreak methods (GCG, AutoDAN) either produce incoherent gibberish suffixes or fluent but uncontrollable attacks, with no mechanism to impose diverse constraints such as sentiment, semantic similarity, or positional coherence on the generated adversarial text. The paper identifies that the path to constraining automatic white-box attacks with controlled features remains an open problem, and formally connects controllable attack generation to the well-studied problem of controllable text generation in NLP.

## Input / Output

The method takes a malicious user query x and a white-box LLM (for gradient access) as input, and optionally a separate control prompt p (for position constraint) or a list of sentiment keywords. It operates in continuous logit space over a fixed-length sequence (20 tokens), running Langevin dynamics for 2000 iterations with a decreasing noise schedule. The output is a discrete text attack y — either a suffix continuation (x⊕y), a full paraphrase of x, or a bridging prompt inserted between x and p — decoded via an LLM-guided top-k filtering process that ensures fluency. Eight candidate samples are generated per query, and a GPT-4-based selector picks the final output.

## Method

COLD-Attack adapts the COLD energy-based constrained decoding framework to the adversarial attack setting by constructing a compositional energy function E(ỹ) = Σ λᵢ Eᵢ(ỹ) where each term encodes a specific constraint: attack success (negative log-likelihood of a target affirmative response), fluency (cross-entropy between softmax(logits) and the LLM's own predictions), semantic similarity (negative cosine of mean token embeddings), and lexical control (differentiable n-gram matching). Langevin dynamics with gradient steps and Gaussian noise is run in the continuous logit space for N iterations, avoiding the discrete token-level optimization of GCG and the autoregressive generation of AutoDAN-Zhu. A key decoding step then converts the final continuous logits to discrete text by restricting each position's selection to the top-k tokens from the underlying LLM's conditional distribution, which is critical for producing fluent output. The non-autoregressive, energy-based formulation enables complex constraints like paraphrasing and positional coherence that are intractable for token-by-token methods.

## Results

On the continuation-constraint setting (Table 2), COLD-Attack achieves 100% ASR on Vicuna, 96% on Guanaco, 92% on Mistral, and 92% on Llama2, while producing the lowest perplexity (PPL) across all four LLMs (24.83–32.96), substantially below GCG (406–821) and AutoDAN-Zhu (33–152). On the GPT-4-judged metric (ASR-G), COLD-Attack attains the highest scores on Guanaco (84%) and Mistral (90%), and is competitive on Vicuna (86%) and Llama2 (66%). COLD-Attack is also ~10× faster than GCG (≈20 min vs. ≈3.2 h per sample on a single V100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GCG, COLD-Attack matches or exceeds ASR on all four LLMs (100/96/92/92 vs. 100/100/100/90) while reducing PPL by 25–230× (e.g., 32.96 vs. 821.53 on Vicuna); on ASR-G, COLD-Attack wins decisively on Mistral (90 vs. 42) and Guanaco (84 vs. 84, tie) but trails slightly on Vicuna (86 vs. 92) and Llama2 (66 vs. 68). Against AutoDAN-Zhu, COLD-Attack achieves comparable ASR (within 0–4 pp) but consistently lower PPL (e.g., 26.24 vs. 79.53 on Mistral, 24.83 vs. 152.32 on Llama2) and higher ASR-G on Guanaco (84 vs. 80) and Mistral (90 vs. 84). Against the older gradient-based baselines (PEZ, GBDA, UAT), COLD-Attack dominates on every metric by a wide margin (e.g., 100% vs. 28% ASR on Vicuna for PEZ). Against AutoDAN-Liu (which uses manually crafted prompts), COLD-Attack achieves lower PPL on Llama2 (24.83 vs. 102.32) and comparable ASR, while being fully automated. In the paraphrasing-constraint setting (Table 5), COLD-Attack achieves 86–98% ASR versus 4–58% for PRISM, PAWS, and GPT-4-based paraphrasing, with BLEU/ROUGE/BERTScore confirming high semantic fidelity to the original query.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: adversarial LLM attacks (GCG, AutoDAN) and controllable text generation (COLD, PPLM, Fudge). It departs from GCG's discrete token-level greedy optimization and AutoDAN-Zhu's autoregressive generation by operating in continuous logit space via Langevin dynamics, inheriting COLD's energy-based framework. The key conceptual move is recognizing that the controllable attack generation problem differs from standard controllable text generation only by the addition of an attack-success energy term, making the two problems structurally identical and allowing direct algorithmic transfer. The paper positions itself as the first unified framework for imposing multiple simultaneous constraints (fluency, sentiment, position, paraphrasing) on white-box LLM attacks, whereas prior work addressed at most fluency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of the paraphrasing and position constraint settings expands the attack surface beyond the standard suffix-attack paradigm, making it harder for simple defenses (e.g., suffix removal) to neutralize threats. The model-dependent sentiment vulnerability finding (negative sentiment more effective on Mistral/Guanaco, positive on Llama2) suggests that safety evaluations must test across diverse stylistic and emotional dimensions rather than a single attack template. However, the method still requires white-box access, and Llama Guard remains the strongest defense (reducing ASR to ~32-42%), indicating that practical deployment of these attacks against well-guarded systems is still limited. Open questions include whether the energy-based framework scales to multi-turn attacks, whether defenses can be co-designed with the same controllability machinery, and how system prompts (acknowledged as a gap) affect attack transferability.
