## Problem

Existing matting networks rely on hand-crafted context aggregation modules (pooling or affinity-based) to exploit contextual information, but these modules are sensitive to context scale shifts between training and inference image sizes, causing performance degradation on larger images. The paper demonstrates that a basic encoder-decoder network without any explicit context aggregation module can actually learn more universal context aggregation, challenging the assumption that such modules are necessary.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241278/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

AEMatter takes an RGB image and a corresponding trimap (three-valued mask with definite foreground, definite background, and unknown regions generated via erosion/dilation with kernel sizes 1–30 px) as input, and outputs a continuous alpha matte clipped to [0, 1]. Training is performed on 1024×1024 random crops, substantially larger than the 256×256 patches used by most prior methods, which is central to the network's ability to learn scale-invariant context aggregation.

## Method

AEMatter replaces explicit context aggregation modules with a Hybrid-Transformer encoder (convolutional stem for low-level features + Swin-Tiny blocks for high-level context) followed by Appearance-Enhanced Axis-Wise Learning (AEAL) blocks that enlarge the receptive field via axis-wise attention—splitting features into axis-wise rectangular regions for multi-head self-attention at O(hw(h+w)) complexity instead of O((hw)²). The decoder uses Swin blocks with skip connections from the encoder, and the final alpha matte is predicted by convolutional blocks that omit normalization layers to preserve local detail. The key training insight is that using 1024×1024 image patches (rather than the typical 256×256) allows the basic encoder-decoder to learn universal context aggregation directly from data, eliminating the need for hand-designed aggregation modules that are brittle to scale changes.

## Results

On Adobe Composition-1K, AEMatter achieves SAD 17.53, MSE 2.26, Grad 4.76, and Conn 12.46, outperforming the closest competitor ViTMatte-B (SAD 20.33, MSE 3.00) by 2.8 SAD and 0.74 MSE. With test-time augmentation (TTA), AEMatter further improves to SAD 16.89, MSE 2.06, Grad 4.24, and Conn 11.72. On the four generalization datasets (Distinction-646, Transparent-460, Semantic Image Matting, Automatic Image Matting-500), AEMatter also achieves the best results across all four metrics, e.g., SAD 16.95 on Distinction-646 and SAD 122.27 on Transparent-460.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AEMatter outperforms all 14 compared baselines on every metric across all five evaluation datasets. The closest competitor is ViTMatte-B (Yao et al., 2024), which AEMatter beats by 2.8 SAD, 0.74 MSE, 1.98 Grad, and 2.32 Conn on Adobe Composition-1K; the gap widens further on generalization datasets (e.g., SAD 16.95 vs. no ViTMatte-B entry on Distinction-646). Among Transformer-based matting methods, AEMatter surpasses MatteFormer (SAD 23.80) and TransMatting (SAD 24.96) by 6.3 and 7.4 SAD respectively. Among pooling-based methods, AEMatter leads FBAMatting (SAD 26.40) and LFPNet (SAD 23.60) by 8.9 and 6.1 SAD. Among affinity-based methods, AEMatter outperforms GCAMatting (SAD 35.28) and TIMI-Net (SAD 29.08) by 17.8 and 11.6 SAD. On the challenging Transparent-460 dataset, AEMatter (SAD 122.27) nearly halves the error of the next-best method TransMatting (SAD 192.36), and on Automatic Image Matting-500 it achieves SAD 14.76 versus FBAMatting's 19.43.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the prevailing matting paradigm that explicitly designed context aggregation modules (pooling-based PPM/ASPP in FBAMatting, MGMatting; affinity-based GCAMatting, TIMI-Net) are essential for high-quality alpha matte estimation. Instead, it demonstrates that a basic encoder-decoder network—without any such modules—can learn universal context aggregation on its own, provided it has sufficient receptive field (Swin-Tiny backbone, axis-wise attention) and is trained on large image patches (1024×1024). The AEMatter architecture repurposes the Hybrid-Transformer design (convolutional stem for low-level features + Swin blocks for high-level context) from prior matting work (MatteFormer, TransMatting) but replaces their explicit context aggregation modules with a simpler axis-wise attention mechanism and a large-patch training strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that hand-crafted context aggregation modules are actually detrimental to universality (they degrade at larger inference sizes due to scale sensitivity) challenges a core design assumption in the matting literature and suggests a simpler, more robust path forward. The large performance margin over SOTA (SAD 17.53 vs. 20.33 for ViTMatte-B on Adobe Composition-1K) with a straightforward architecture makes this practically impactful for matting practitioners. However, the paper acknowledges limitations with degraded images (JPEG compression, lens blur) and coarse trimaps, and the large-patch training strategy (1024×1024) imposes significant memory requirements that may limit adoption. Open questions include whether the 'learn context aggregation implicitly via large receptive field + large patches' principle generalizes to other dense prediction tasks beyond matting.
