## Problem

Multi-plane representations (TensoRF, HexPlane, K-Planes) exhibit a bias toward fine-grained high-frequency details despite their multiresolution design, causing them to fail at capturing low-frequency global context such as object shapes and dynamic motion when training poses are sparse. Existing remedies—sinusoidal encoding underfits, and denoising regularization (Laplacian smoothing, TV loss) is hyperparameter-sensitive and can introduce artificial details or prevent convergence. The paper asks whether a principled architectural integration of two heterogeneous features with complementary spectral biases can resolve this without heavy reliance on regularization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse inward-facing multi-view images (8 views for static NeRF-Synthetic at 800×800, or 15/20/25 uniformly sampled poses for D-NeRF) and produces a 3D or 4D radiance field parameterized by 48-channel multi-plane grids (up to 200³ resolution) plus a small MLP. At inference, a ray is sampled at n points, each queried against the multi-plane features via bilinear interpolation and the coordinate MLP, then volume-rendered to yield per-pixel RGB and density. The dynamic case extends the temporal planes to 4D (x,y,z,t) with the same channel dimension.

## Method

The architecture replaces sinusoidal encoding with multi-plane encoding while retaining the original NeRF MLP backbone, but introduces a residual concatenation of raw coordinate values s_k and multi-plane features f_k across the first two hidden-layer blocks (Eq. 1), so the ReLU-based coordinate path preserves its inherent low-frequency spectral bias without being overwhelmed by high-frequency plane features. A channel-wise curriculum weighting (Eq. 2) progressively engages multi-plane channels via a cosine schedule over a training interval, ensuring the coordinate network establishes global context before fine-grained plane features are activated—this prevents all channels from collapsing into identical patterns. The loss combines photometric MSE, Laplacian smoothing on both spatial and temporal planes (with higher weight on temporal), and L1 sparsity on plane features. The key empirical finding is that this residual design makes the method far less sensitive to the denoising hyperparameter λ₁ than TensoRF or K-Planes, and that reducing multi-plane channels to 12 (1.0M params) still outperforms full-parameter baselines because the coordinate network substitutes for the low-resolution grid.

## Results

On the static NeRF-Synthetic dataset (8 training views), the proposed method achieves an average PSNR of 24.56 dB, SSIM of 0.896, and LPIPS of 0.092, outperforming K-Planes (24.24 dB) and FreeNeRF (23.40 dB). On the dynamic D-NeRF dataset with 25 training poses, it reaches 25.34 dB PSNR and 0.063 LPIPS, surpassing HexPlane (24.15 dB) and K-Planes (22.68 dB). Notably, with only 1.0M parameters (30% of K-Planes' 18.6M), the method still achieves 25.10 dB PSNR on D-NeRF, exceeding all full-parameter baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On static NeRF (8 views), the proposed method achieves the highest average PSNR (24.56 dB), edging out K-Planes (24.24 dB) by 0.32 dB and FreeNeRF (23.40 dB) by 1.16 dB; however, K-Planes retains a marginal advantage in LPIPS (0.085 vs. 0.092) and SSIM (0.897 vs. 0.896). On dynamic D-NeRF with 25 poses, the gap widens substantially: the method outperforms HexPlane by 1.19 dB (25.34 vs. 24.15) and K-Planes by 2.66 dB (25.34 vs. 22.68), with the advantage growing as pose count decreases (at 15 poses, K-Planes drops to 21.50 dB while the method holds at 22.30 dB). In terms of parameter efficiency, the 1.0M-parameter variant (Ours-12) achieves 25.10 dB on D-NeRF, surpassing K-Planes with 18.6M parameters (23.85 dB) and HexPlane with 9.7M parameters (24.00 dB). For training stability, the method's PSNR variance across test viewpoints (18.23) is comparable to FreeNeRF (17.31) and lower than K-Planes (19.61), iNGP (23.95), and TensoRF (23.22), indicating resistance to overfitting on sparse inputs. The method also demonstrates superior robustness to the denoising regularization weight λ₁, maintaining near-optimal PSNR (24.23–25.84 dB) across λ₁ ∈ [0.0001, 1.0], whereas TensoRF fails to converge entirely for λ₁ ≥ 0.01.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the multi-plane representation lineage (TensoRF for static scenes, HexPlane for dynamic 4D scenes) and the original NeRF volume rendering framework, but departs from both by replacing sinusoidal encoding with a residual concatenation of raw coordinate features and multi-plane features. It is conceptually related to CAM (Lee et al., 2024), which also combines coordinate networks with grid-based representations, but differs in explicitly leveraging the spectral bias of ReLU-based coordinate networks to handle low-frequency content while multi-plane features specialize in high-frequency details. The curriculum weighting strategy draws on progressive frequency activation ideas from FreeNeRF and BARF, but applies them channel-wise to multi-plane features rather than to sinusoidal encoding frequencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled explanation for why explicit multi-resolution representations (hash-grids, multi-planes) fail at low-frequency reconstruction despite their multi-scale design, and offers an architectural solution that reduces parameter count by ~90% while outperforming full-parameter baselines on sparse inputs. This has practical implications for deployment in memory-constrained settings and for scenes where heavy denoising regularization (Laplacian smoothing, TV loss) introduces artifacts or fails to converge. Limitations include per-scene hyperparameter tuning (λ1, curriculum schedule, initial resolution), higher rendering time than baselines (2.14 min vs 0.60 min for HexPlane), and evaluation primarily on bounded synthetic scenes with limited real-world validation (Tanks and Temples results are in appendix only).
