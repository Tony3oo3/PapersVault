## Problem

In offline-to-online RL, existing methods either extract only a pre-trained policy from the offline dataset (incomplete knowledge utilization) or directly replay offline samples during online fine-tuning, which introduces a distribution shift between the offline data and the current policy's induced distribution. The paper asks whether it is feasible to generate synthetic samples that conform to the online data distribution while still leveraging the full prior knowledge in the offline dataset, without suffering from the compounding errors of model-based rollouts.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline dataset of (s, a, r, s') tuples, a small positive buffer of recent on-policy trajectories, and the current policy π. It produces synthetic (s, a, s') samples whose joint distribution matches the online data distribution d^π(s)·π(a|s)·T(s'|s,a); the reward r is assumed accessible and is not part of the generated tuple. The generated samples are stored in a dedicated diffusion replay buffer that is mixed with the online buffer for policy and value updates, operating in continuous state-action spaces (e.g., 11-dim Hopper states, 3-dim actions).

## Method

EDIS trains a denoising diffusion model (residual MLP, 6 layers, 128 denoising steps) on the joint (s, a, s') distribution from offline data, then guides its reverse-time SDE with three energy functions: E₁(s) aligns the state marginal with the online state distribution, E₂(a) aligns the action conditional with the current policy, and E₃(s') aligns the next-state conditional with true dynamics. Each energy function is learned via an InfoNCE contrastive loss where positive samples come from the online buffer (for E₁) or offline+online data (for E₂, E₃) and negative samples are drawn from the denoiser itself. During sampling, the score function is augmented by subtracting the energy gradient at each denoising step, following the energy-guided diffusion framework of Lu et al. (2023b). Theoretically, this yields a suboptimality bound of O(1/(1−γ)²) without a concentratability coefficient, improving upon both direct offline replay (which incurs a C_d factor) and model-based rollouts (which suffer O(1/(1−γ)³) compounding error).

## Results

After 0.2M online fine-tuning steps (following 1M offline pre-training), EDIS achieves a 26.3% average improvement over Cal-QL (total score 1830.1 vs. 1448.5) and a 12.1% improvement over IQL (1395.1 vs. 1244.2) across 21 D4RL tasks. The most dramatic gains appear in random-policy datasets (e.g., hopper-random-v2: 17.6→98.1 with Cal-QL; walker2d-random-v2: 15.1→61.6) and Adroit manipulation (door-human-v1: 0.1→58.4 with Cal-QL), where the offline-to-online distribution gap is largest. The overall average improvement across both base algorithms is approximately 20%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDIS is evaluated as a plug-in module on top of two state-of-the-art offline-to-online baselines: Cal-QL (Nakamoto et al., 2023) and IQL (Kostrikov et al., 2022). Against Cal-QL, EDIS yields a 26.3% total-score improvement (1830.1 vs. 1448.5), with the largest relative gains in random-policy MuJoCo tasks (hopper-random: 17.6→98.1; walker2d-random: 15.1→61.6) and Adroit door-human (0.1→58.4), where the offline-to-online distribution gap is most severe. Against IQL, the improvement is more modest at 12.1% (1395.1 vs. 1244.2), partly because IQL's AWR-style policy learning retains conservatism that limits how much synthetic data can help. EDIS wins on the vast majority of individual tasks for both baselines; the few exceptions (e.g., halfcheetah-medium-expert with IQL: 85.4 vs. 87.9) are within one standard deviation. The paper also compares against model-based data augmentation (MLP transition and diffusion transition rollouts) in a controlled experiment, showing that EDIS's direct distribution-matching approach outperforms both, confirming that the gain comes from avoiding compounding rollout errors rather than merely from using a diffusion model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the offline-to-online RL paradigm (Cal-QL, IQL, balanced replay) by introducing a diffusion-based data generator that actively synthesizes online-distribution samples rather than passively replaying offline data. The core sampling mechanism builds directly on Lu et al. (2023b)'s contrastive energy prediction for energy-guided diffusion, adapting it to the RL setting with three task-specific energy functions (state, action, transition) learned via InfoNCE. The key conceptual departure from prior work is modeling the joint (s,a,s') data distribution directly rather than the transition function, which the authors argue eliminates compounding errors inherent in model-based rollouts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EDIS provides a theoretically grounded, plug-in solution to the long-standing tension between leveraging offline prior knowledge and avoiding distribution shift during online fine-tuning. The suboptimality bounds cleanly separate the three failure modes (initialization error, distribution shift via concentratability, compounding model error) and show EDIS avoids the latter two. Limitations include the computational overhead of the diffusion model (~10 min per 50k training epochs), reliance on the diffusion model's approximation quality for ε_d and ε_m, and evaluation restricted to D4RL benchmarks with relatively low-dimensional state spaces. Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-dimensional pixel-based observations (the paper reports preliminary DMC results) and whether the energy guidance can be made more sample-efficient.
