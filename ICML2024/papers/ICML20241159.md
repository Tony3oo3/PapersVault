## Problem

For pure private agnostic learning, a gap existed between the trivial lower bound (combining private realizable and non-private agnostic lower bounds) and the best known upper bounds in both the item-level and user-level settings. Previous item-level algorithms incurred a RepD(C)/α² term instead of the optimal VC(C)/α², and the user-level upper bound of Ghazi et al. (2023) used RepD(C) where the lower bound only requires VC(C) in the agnostic term. This paper closes both gaps, yielding near-tight characterizations.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a dataset of n users, each contributing m labeled examples (z_{i,j} ∈ X × {0,1}), along with a concept class C, privacy parameter ε, and target accuracy α. Privacy is defined at the user level: adding or removing an entire user's m examples constitutes a neighboring dataset. The output is a binary hypothesis h: X → {0,1} whose generalization error is within α of the best concept in C, with the guarantee holding with probability 1−β over both the data and the algorithm's internal randomness.

## Method

The key insight for item-level privacy is a surrogate score function q(z,h) = min_{c∈C} err_z(c) + dis_x(c,h) used with the exponential mechanism, which decomposes the generalization requirement into agnostic generalization over C (needing only VC(C)/α² samples) and realizable generalization over C×H (needing RepD(C)/α samples), rather than agnostic generalization over the full hypothesis class H. For user-level privacy, the algorithm is extended by replacing item-level error and disagreement with user-level analogues that count the fraction of users exceeding a threshold number of mistakes, exploiting the √m amplification of total variation distance between binomials to reduce the required number of users. For learning thresholds specifically, a binary search over the domain selects the distributional median at each step (using the stronger m-amplification when error rates are small) to reduce iterations from O(log|X|) to O(log(1/α)), achieving a nearly tight user complexity bound.

## Results

For item-level pure private agnostic learning, the paper achieves a sample complexity of Õ(RepD(C)/(αε) + VC(C)/α²), matching the trivial lower bound up to polylogarithmic factors (Theorem 3.4). For user-level privacy with m examples per user, the user complexity improves to Õ(RepD(C)/(√(mα)ε) + VC(C)/(mα²)), replacing the previous RepD(C)/(mα²) term with the tighter VC(C)/(mα²) (Theorem 4.6). For learning thresholds under user-level DP, the user complexity Õ(log|X|/(min(1,mα)ε) + 1/(√(mα)ε) + 1/(mα²)) is nearly tight, matching the lower bound (Theorem 5.2). In the high-accuracy regime (α→0), the dominant term VC(C)/(mα²) matches non-private agnostic learning, meaning the privacy constraint is essentially free.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Beimel et al. (2019) for item-level learning, the paper replaces the RepD(C)/α² term with VC(C)/α², which can be exponentially smaller (as shown by Feldman & Xiao 2014), and removes the extra 1/ε factor on the VC term present in Alon et al. (2020). Against Ghazi et al. (2023) for user-level learning, the key improvement is replacing RepD(C)/(mα²) with VC(C)/(mα²) in the third term, which matches the lower bound and is critical in the high-accuracy regime where this term dominates. For threshold learning under user-level DP, the paper achieves a nearly tight bound that matches the Ghazi et al. (2023) lower bound, whereas their upper bound had a larger log|X|/(√(mα)ε) term instead of the correct 1/(√(mα)ε) term. The practical significance is that in the high-accuracy regime (α→0), the new user complexity is dominated by VC(C)/(mα²), which equals the non-private agnostic learning cost, meaning privacy is free — the same number of users suffices as without privacy. The gap that remains is in the second user-level term (RepD(C)/(√(mα)ε) vs. the lower bound's VC(C)/(√(mα)ε)), which the authors conjecture can also be closed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pure private learning framework of Beimel et al. (2019), which characterized realizable learning via probabilistic representation dimension, to the agnostic setting by introducing a surrogate error score function that decouples the RepD(C) and VC(C) dependencies. It also builds on the user-level DP framework of Ghazi et al. (2023), improving their upper bound by replacing a RepD(C) factor with VC(C) in the agnostic term through a user-level error amplification argument based on binomial total variation distances. The key methodological departure from prior agnostic approaches (realizable-to-agnostic transformations by Beimel et al. 2015, Alon et al. 2020) is a direct algorithm design that avoids the 1/ε multiplicative penalty on the VC term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes the gap between upper and lower bounds for item-level pure private agnostic learning, establishing a near-tight characterization that was a long-standing open problem. The 'privacy is free' phenomenon in the high-accuracy regime (where the VC(C)/α² term dominates) is a clean theoretical insight with implications for understanding when privacy constraints are genuinely costly. The remaining open question is whether the gap in the user-level generic case (the RepD(C)/(√(mα)ε) term) can also be closed, and whether the threshold-specific improvement generalizes to broader concept classes. The paper also leaves open the heterogeneous-data setting where users hold different numbers of examples.
