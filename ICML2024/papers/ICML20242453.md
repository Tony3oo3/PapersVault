## Problem

Standard adversarial training under the ℓ∞-norm constraint assigns an identical perturbation budget ε to every pixel, implicitly assuming all pixels contribute equally to model outputs. The paper discovers that different pixel regions contribute unequally to both natural accuracy and adversarial robustness, and asks whether allocating distinct perturbation budgets to different pixel regions can simultaneously improve both metrics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242453/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10 and SVHN, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet-200) and outputs class labels via a CNN classifier. During training, each input image is perturbed with a pixel-reweighted adversarial perturbation: pixels identified as important by a class activation map retain the full budget ε (e.g., 8/255), while remaining pixels are constrained to a reduced budget ε^low (e.g., 7/255), producing a non-uniform perturbation pattern across the image.

## Method

PART (Pixel-reweighted AdveRsarial Training) modifies the adversarial example generation constraint by replacing the uniform ℓ∞ bound with two region-specific bounds: ‖v(Δ, I^high)‖∞ ≤ ε for important pixels and ‖v(Δ, I^low)‖∞ ≤ ε^low for the rest. Important regions are identified via GradCAM (or alternative CAM methods), which produces a class activation map that is resized, thresholded at ω>1, and converted into a binary mask m. This mask is element-wise multiplied into the PGD update step, effectively shrinking the perturbation budget for unimportant pixels during AE generation. The resulting pixel-reweighted AEs are then used to train the model with any existing AT objective (AT, TRADES, or MART), preceded by a 20-epoch burn-in period of standard AT to stabilize the classifier before CAM-based reweighting begins. A KKT-based theoretical analysis on a 2D linear model shows that unequal perturbation budgets lead to structurally different optimal perturbations, providing a formal justification for the pixel-reweighting strategy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18), PART-T (s=1) achieves 79.36% natural accuracy and 48.90% PGD-20 robustness, improving over TRADES by +1.20% accuracy and +0.62% robustness simultaneously. On SVHN, PART-T reaches 91.35% natural accuracy and 59.33% PGD-20 robustness (+2.44% and +0.59% over TRADES). On Tiny-ImageNet-200 (WideResNet-34-10), PART-M (s=1) achieves 43.75% natural accuracy and 14.93% PGD-20 robustness, outperforming MART by +1.07% accuracy and +0.16% robustness. Across all three datasets and all three AT baselines, PART consistently improves natural accuracy by 1.07–2.44% with at most a 0.15% drop in adversarial robustness, and in many cases improves both metrics simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AT (Madry et al., 2018), PART improves natural accuracy by +0.84% (CIFAR-10), +2.08% (SVHN), and +1.36% (Tiny-ImageNet-200) while maintaining or slightly improving robustness (e.g., +0.51% PGD-20 on SVHN, −0.04% on CIFAR-10). Against TRADES (Zhang et al., 2019), PART-T achieves the strongest gains: +1.20–2.44% natural accuracy improvement AND +0.22–0.92% robustness improvement across all three datasets, meaning it wins on both metrics simultaneously. Against MART (Wang et al., 2020), PART-M gains +1.07–1.85% natural accuracy with at most a 0.15% robustness drop (on CIFAR-10 PGD-20), and actually improves robustness on SVHN (+0.33%) and Tiny-ImageNet-200 (+0.16%). The consistent pattern across all baselines and datasets is that PART improves the accuracy–robustness trade-off: it recovers the accuracy loss typically incurred by adversarial training without sacrificing (and often improving) adversarial robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial training (AT) framework of Madry et al. (2018) by replacing the uniform ℓ∞ perturbation budget with pixel-reweighted budgets, guided by class activation maps (primarily GradCAM, Selvaraju et al. 2017). It departs from prior reweighted AT methods (Cai et al. 2018; Ding et al. 2020; Zhang et al. 2021; Wang et al. 2021), which reweight at the instance level, by pioneering a pixel-level reweighting strategy that treats different regions within a single image differently. It also distinguishes itself from attention-based defenses (Zhou et al. 2021; Wu et al. 2023) that use CAM for adversarial purification or regularization, since PART uses CAM solely to construct a mask that modulates the perturbation budget during AE generation within the standard AT loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PART addresses the well-known accuracy-robustness trade-off in adversarial training by showing that the uniform-ε assumption is suboptimal, offering a simple, plug-and-play modification that consistently improves natural accuracy (1–3%) with negligible robustness loss across CIFAR-10, SVHN, and TinyImageNet-200. Key limitations include the heuristic choice of ε^low (set to ε − 1/255 without search), the requirement of a 20-epoch burn-in period, and evaluation restricted to CNNs on relatively small datasets. Open questions the authors identify include designing an efficient search for ε^low, extending the framework to Vision Transformers, and providing a theoretical analysis of how pixel importance discrepancies affect AT generalization given the high correlation among generated AEs across epochs.
