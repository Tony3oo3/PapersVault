## Problem

Industrial Deep Learning Recommendation Models (DLRMs) rely on thousands of handcrafted heterogeneous features yet fail to scale with compute, plateauing in quality at a few hundred billion parameters. This paper asks whether recommendation can be reformulated as a pure sequential transduction task—treating user actions as a new generative modality—so that the scaling laws observed in language models (power-law quality vs. compute) also apply to recommendation systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242414/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a single unified time series of up to 8,192 tokens formed by interleaving content items (Φ) with user engagement actions (a: like, skip, video completion, share, etc.), with slowly-changing categorical features (demographics, followed creators) compressed and merged into the same sequence. Notably, all numerical/dense features (CTR counters, ratios, decayed aggregates) are removed entirely and must be recovered implicitly by the architecture. For ranking, the model outputs multi-task binary engagement predictions for a target candidate via target-aware cross-attention; for retrieval, it outputs the next content item from a billion-scale non-stationary vocabulary. Training is done in a streaming, single-pass fashion over tens of billions of daily user actions.

## Method

The paper proposes Generative Recommenders (GRs), which recast ranking and retrieval as causal autoregressive sequential transduction over an interleaved (Φ, a) sequence, enabling generative training that amortizes encoder cost across multiple targets (reducing complexity from O(N³d) to O(N²d)). The core encoder, HSTU, replaces softmax attention with pointwise aggregated attention (preserving engagement intensity and handling non-stationary vocabularies), eliminates feedforward layers in favor of element-wise SiLU gating (Norm(A(X)V(X)) ⊙ U(X)), and incorporates relative attention biases encoding both positional and temporal information. Two efficiency mechanisms are introduced: Stochastic Length (SL), which algorithmically subsamples long user histories to increase sparsity without quality loss, and M-FALCON, a micro-batched inference algorithm with KV caching that serves 285× more complex models at 1.5–3× throughput within the same inference budget. Deployed at 1.5 trillion parameters, GRs achieve 12.4% online metric improvements and demonstrate power-law scaling across three orders of magnitude of compute, up to GPT-3/LLaMA-2 scale.

## Results

On public datasets (MovieLens-1M, MovieLens-20M, Amazon Books), HSTU outperforms SASRec by 8.6%–38.4% in HR@10 and 7.3%–40.6% in NDCG@10, with HSTU-large reaching up to +65.8% NDCG@10 on Amazon Books. In industrial-scale streaming settings, HSTU achieves 3.978 log perplexity for retrieval and 0.4937/0.7805 NE for ranking (E-Task/C-Task), outperforming standard Transformers (which produce NaN in ranking) and Transformer++ (4.015/0.4945/0.7822). Deployed 1.5T-parameter GR models deliver +12.4% E-Task and +4.4% C-Task improvements in online A/B tests, while being 5.3×–15.2× faster than FlashAttention-2 Transformers on 8192-length sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HSTU consistently outperforms SASRec (Kang & McAuley, 2018) across all three public datasets, with the largest gains on Amazon Books (+38.4% HR@10, +40.6% NDCG@10 for HSTU; +60.6%/+65.8% for HSTU-large). Against standard Transformers (Vaswani et al., 2017) in industrial-scale streaming settings, HSTU achieves lower NE (0.4937 vs. NaN for ranking E-Task) and lower log perplexity (3.978 vs. 4.069 for retrieval), while also being 5.3×–15.2× faster in wall-clock time. HSTU also beats Transformer++ (Touvron et al., 2023a, using RoPE/SwiGLU) on both retrieval (3.978 vs. 4.015 log pplx) and ranking (0.4937 vs. 0.4945 NE). In the full industrial deployment, GRs outperform the production DLRM baseline (Mudigere et al., 2022) by +12.4% E-Task and +4.4% C-Task in online A/B tests, despite using a dramatically simplified feature set. On public benchmarks, HSTU also surpasses BERT4Rec (Sun et al., 2019) and GRU4Rec (Hidasi et al., 2016) by 8.6–38.4% in HR@10, confirming that the pointwise-attention HSTU design is superior to both bidirectional self-supervised and RNN-based sequential approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the DLRM paradigm (Mudigere et al., 2022) that has dominated industrial recommendation for a decade, replacing heterogeneous feature engineering with a unified sequential transduction framework. It builds on the Transformer architecture (Vaswani et al., 2017) but introduces pointwise aggregated attention (replacing softmax), elementwise gating (inspired by FLASH and SwiGLU), and relative attention biases incorporating temporal information. The work also extends the line of sequential recommenders (SASRec, GRU4Rec, BERT4Rec) and industrial target-aware modules (DIN, TWIN, TransAct) by unifying them into a single autoregressive generative formulation, and leverages efficient attention kernels (FlashAttention-2, ByteTransformer) as computational foundations for the HSTU encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that recommendation model quality follows a power-law scaling with compute across three orders of magnitude—up to GPT-3/LLaMa-2 scale—is a paradigm shift that breaks the known quality saturation of DLRMs and opens the door to foundation models for recommendations, search, and ads. The 12.4% online A/B improvement from a 1.5T-parameter model deployed at billion-user scale validates the approach practically. Open questions include whether scaling continues beyond the tested regime, how end-to-end generative recommendation (sampling full sequences via beam search) compares to current listwise/RL approaches, and whether the unified feature space generalizes across domains without task-specific engineering.
