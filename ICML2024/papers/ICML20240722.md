## Problem

In Abductive Learning, the abduction process yields a set of multiple valid candidate label sequences rather than a single ground-truth label, creating inherent ambiguity. Prior ABL methods resolve this by selecting one candidate (e.g., nearest by Hamming distance or highest model confidence), which is unreliable during cold-start training and can trap the perception model in a sub-optimal state through a vicious cycle of reinforced incorrect predictions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of m images (digits from MNIST/KMNIST/CIFAR-10/SVHN at 32×32 or formula symbols at 3×32×32), a logical knowledge base, and a target label (e.g., the sum of two numbers). During training, the model never sees per-instance ground-truth labels; instead, it receives a candidate set of valid label sequences abduced from the knowledge base given the target label. The output is a per-instance class probability distribution over L classes, from which the model learns via cross-entropy against these soft targets.

## Method

A³BL replaces the single-candidate selection of prior ABL with an EM-based optimization over all abduced candidates. In the E-step, the current perception model assigns a weight to each candidate in the abduced set (computed as the product of per-instance class probabilities, normalized via softmax with temperature 0.3), converting the ambiguous candidate set into instance-level class probability distributions. In the M-step, the model minimizes a cross-entropy loss between its predictions and these soft distributions, equivalently minimizing a weighted empirical risk over all candidates. The paper proves this procedure maximizes the joint log-likelihood of inputs and target labels via EM, guaranteeing convergence, and establishes a Rademacher-complexity-based generalization error bound. A top-k (k=32) candidate selection based on confidence is used to balance computational efficiency with risk consistency.

## Results

On the digit addition task, A³BL achieves 91.86% ± 0.86 accuracy on Addition-SVHN (n=1) and 72.82% ± 0.95 on Addition-CIFAR (n=1), maintaining stable performance across all digit sizes (n=1–4) with standard deviations below 1.2%. On the handwritten formula recognition task, A³BL reaches 78.92% ± 0.50 on HWF-CIFAR and 91.83% ± 0.55 on HWF-SVHN. A key advantage is stability: while ABL-conf suffers catastrophic variance (e.g., 64.66% ± 43.45 on Addition-SVHN n=2), A³BL's standard deviation never exceeds 1.22% across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

A³BL decisively outperforms both ABL variants: on Addition-SVHN n=2, A³BL reaches 91.81% while ABL-conf collapses to 64.66% (±43.45) and ABL-hamming to 19.76%; on Addition-CIFAR n=2, A³BL achieves 76.09% versus 62.94% (ABL-conf) and 20.67% (ABL-hamming). Against DeepStochLog, A³BL wins on all CIFAR settings (e.g., 76.09% vs 67.63% at n=2) and matches or slightly exceeds it on SVHN (91.81% vs 90.81% at n=2). NeurASP and DeepProbLog are effectively non-functional, scoring near random (6–10%) on SVHN and failing to complete training on most settings. On the HWF task, A³BL edges out ABL-conf (78.92% vs 78.47% on HWF-CIFAR; 91.83% vs 91.21% on HWF-SVHN) and substantially outperforms NGS variants (28.25% best) and DeepStochLog (75.41% on HWF-CIFAR), though DeepStochLog slightly leads on the original HWF dataset (99.65% vs 99.41%). The paper also reports that A³BL's total time complexity matches ABL's, with execution times within ~5% of ABL (e.g., 14m5s vs 14m4s on Addition-CIFAR n=1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Abductive Learning (ABL) framework of Dai et al. (2019) and Zhou (2019), which couples a perception model with a knowledge base via abductive reasoning to generate pseudo-labels. The key departure is from prior ABL variants that hard-select a single candidate (by Hamming distance or model confidence) as supervision; A³BL instead assigns soft weights to all abduced candidates via an EM procedure, drawing conceptual inspiration from weakly supervised learning and partial-label learning. It also positions itself against other neuro-symbolic integration approaches (DeepProbLog, NeurASP, DeepStochLog, NGS) that approximate or embed logic in differentiable computation, whereas A³BL retains a discrete logical engine and focuses specifically on the supervision signal derived from abduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cold-start problem—where an unreliable perception model leads to incorrect candidate selection and a vicious training cycle—is a practical bottleneck that has limited ABL's adoption. By providing convergence guarantees via the EM formulation and establishing the first error bound in the ABL framework, this work gives practitioners a principled, stable alternative that trains from scratch without requiring pretraining or semi-supervised data. A key open question is scalability: when the knowledge base is weak or the candidate set grows exponentially (e.g., from ~5 to ~50+ candidates as digit size increases), the method's efficiency degrades, and the authors suggest ILP-based knowledge refinement or parallel abduction as future directions.
