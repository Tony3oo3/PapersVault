## Problem

Instance attribution scores, which label training examples as helpful or harmful based on their sign, have been shown to be unreliable under dataset resampling — the sign can flip when the companion training set changes. LOO-based methods, which dominate the field, produce scores with small magnitude and high variance, making sign flips frequent. The Shapley value is theoretically more robust but requires an exponential number of fine-tuning runs, rendering it impractical for large pre-trained language models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled training set, a held-out test set, and a pre-trained LM (BERT, RoBERTa, or Llama2-7B with LoRA) under a prompt-based fine-tuning formulation. It outputs a scalar Shapley-value instance score per training example, whose sign encodes helpfulness; these scores are then consumed by downstream tasks such as test-prediction explanation, sequential data removal, data selection, and wrong-label detection. A key practical detail is that the eNTK matrix is precomputed once on the full training set, so all subsequent marginal-contribution evaluations are pure matrix operations on submatrices with no gradient-based training.

## Method

FreeShap replaces the fine-tuning step in Shapley value computation with kernel regression on the empirical neural tangent kernel (eNTK), exploiting the observation that eNTK matrices for any data subset are submatrices of the full-dataset eNTK matrix. The full eNTK matrix is precomputed once, and Monte-Carlo (or truncated MC) sampling over random permutations estimates the Shapley value by evaluating marginal contributions via submatrix kernel regression rather than retraining. Blockwise inversion further reduces the per-step kernel regression cost from O(n³) to O(n²), and an efficient kernel approximation (I_C ⊗ K₀) splits the regression into C independent tasks. Theoretically, the paper defines β-robustness as the probability of a sign flip under resampling and proves via Chebyshev's inequality that, under mild assumptions of diminishing influence and quadratic variance scaling, the Shapley value's β upper bound is no larger than LOO's, explaining its superior sign consistency.

## Results

FreeShap achieves Pearson correlations of 0.57–0.84 with the reference MC-Shapley across four NLP datasets (SST-2, MR, MRPC, RTE), consistently outperforming G-Shapley (0.28–0.68). On the robustness metric (percentage of non-robust sign occurrences), the Shapley value (via FreeShap) yields 18–66% non-robust occurrences versus 38–100% for LOO across dataset sizes of 500–2000. In data selection on MR, FreeShap achieves a 0.2223 performance gain at 10% subset size, compared to 0.0750 (Influence), 0.1970 (TracIn), and 0.1238 (Representer). FreeShap also identifies mislabeled data earliest in 7 out of 8 wrong-label-detection experiments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeShap consistently outperforms G-Shapley (Ghorbani & Zou, 2019) in approximating the reference MC-Shapley, with Pearson correlations 0.15–0.50 higher across all four datasets (e.g., 0.78 vs 0.28 on RTE). Against LOO-based methods including the Influence Function (Koh & Liang, 2017), the Shapley value (computed via FreeShap) is substantially more robust: non-robust sign occurrences drop from 86–100% (LOO) to 18–66% (Shapley) across dataset sizes. In data selection on MR, FreeShap (0.2223 at 10% subset) beats TracIn (Garima et al., 2020) at 0.1970, Representer Point (Yeh et al., 2018) at 0.1238, and Influence at 0.0750. For wrong label detection, FreeShap identifies poisoned data earliest in 7 of 8 experiments, outperforming all three LOO-based baselines. On Llama2-7B, FreeShap again provides more syntactically and semantically aligned explanations than Influence, TracIn, and Representer Point.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of data valuation (Data Shapley, Ghorbani & Zou 2019) and the NTK-based view of fine-tuning (Jacot et al. 2018; Malladi et al. 2023). It departs from the dominant LOO-based instance attribution literature (influence functions, TracIn, representer points) by arguing that these methods are fundamentally fragile in sign consistency under dataset resampling, and that the Shapley value—long considered too expensive to compute—offers a principled fix. The key technical departure is FreeShap, which replaces the fine-tuning step in Shapley computation with eNTK kernel regression, exploiting the submatrix structure of the precomputed kernel to amortize cost across all subset evaluations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The β-robustness notion exposes a practical vulnerability in widely-used data curation pipelines: if LOO-based scores flip signs under resampling, decisions to remove or retain training data become unreliable, with downstream consequences for model quality and data collection. FreeShap's eNTK substitution makes Shapley-based attribution tractable for LLMs (demonstrated on Llama2-7B with LoRA), opening a path toward more trustworthy data management at scale. Limitations include restriction to NLP classification tasks, white-box model access, and the eNTK approximation's fidelity to actual fine-tuning dynamics, which may degrade for very deep or non-NTK-regime models. Extending to generative/autoregressive tasks remains open.
