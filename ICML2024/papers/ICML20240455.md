## Problem

Existing marginal-based differentially private data synthesis methods (e.g., PGM) suffer from sensitivity to the number of selected marginals, limited scalability, inability to preserve geometric structure of ordinal features, and no straightforward mechanism for enforcing domain-specific constraints. PrivPGD addresses these gaps by replacing the distribution-learning paradigm with a particle-based optimization that directly minimizes a geometry-aware transport distance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240455/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a set of differentially private noisy signed measures (obtained via the Gaussian mechanism on selected marginals, e.g., all 2-Way marginals), a DP differentiable regularization loss encoding domain-specific constraints, and a target number of particles m (100k in experiments). The data domain is discretized into 32 equally-sized bins per continuous dimension and embedded into [0,1]^d. The output is a synthetic tabular dataset D_DP of m rows, produced by mapping final particle positions to the nearest grid points in the embedding.

## Method

PrivPGD constructs synthetic data by propagating m particles in a d-dimensional embedding space via mini-batch particle gradient descent, minimizing the sum of squared sliced Wasserstein distances (SW2²) between particle marginal distributions and privatized marginal distributions, plus a regularization penalty λR. The projection step first converts noisy signed measures into proper probability measures by solving an SW1 minimization (extending the 1-Wasserstein identity to signed measures), then quantizes them to m particles. The SW2² gradient is computed in closed form via sorting projected particle coordinates (O(m log m) per Monte Carlo projection), enabling efficient GPU-parallel computation. Domain-specific constraints are incorporated as additive differentiable penalty terms in the loss, and privacy is guaranteed by the data post-processing property since all randomness is injected before the optimization step.

## Results

At ε=2.5, δ=10⁻⁵, PrivPGD achieves the best or second-best performance across 9 datasets (50K–1.5M rows, 3–22 dimensions) on most of 6 evaluation metrics. On ACS Income, PrivPGD attains a covariance error of 0.001 versus 0.014 (PGM+AIM) and 0.051 (PGM+MST), and a counting-query error of 0.00078 versus 0.0017 (PGM+AIM). On the Medical Charges dataset, PrivPGD reaches a downstream regression error of 2.2 (matching the 2.1 oracle) with a covariance error of 0.00014, while GEM fails catastrophically (downstream error 75). PrivPGD consistently minimizes the average SW₁ distance (e.g., 0.00043 on ACS Income vs. 0.0017 for PGM+AIM), confirming its geometry-preserving advantage over TV-minimizing baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PGM+MST (McKenna et al., 2021), PrivPGD wins on every metric across all 9 datasets, with particularly large margins on covariance error (e.g., 0.001 vs. 0.051 on ACS Income) and SW₁ distance (0.00043 vs. 0.0054). Against PGM+AIM (McKenna et al., 2022), PrivPGD dominates on covariance, counting-query, thresholding-query, and SW₁ distance, ties on downstream error, but concedes TV distance on several datasets (e.g., 0.028 vs. 0.034 on ACS Income is a win, but 0.020 vs. 0.016 on Taxi is a loss). Against Private GSD (Liu et al., 2023), PrivPGD wins on most metrics; Private GSD retains an edge on TV distance and occasionally on covariance (e.g., 0.0083 vs. 0.020 on ACS Employment), but PrivPGD pulls ahead on SW₁ distance and query errors. Against GEM (Liu et al., 2021), PrivPGD wins overwhelmingly; GEM fails catastrophically on Medical Charges (downstream 75 vs. 2.2) and is 10–100× worse on query errors across all datasets. Against RAP (Aydore et al., 2021), PrivPGD wins on covariance, counting, and SW₁ distance in most settings, with RAP occasionally competitive on thresholding queries.

## Relation to Prior Work

PrivPGD sits within the well-established marginal-based DP data synthesis pipeline (marginal selection → privatization → generation) but replaces the generation step. Where prior methods like PGM (McKenna et al., 2021; 2022) learn a parametric graphical model via mirror descent to match noisy marginals, PrivPGD directly propagates particles in an embedding space using particle gradient descent (Chizat & Bach, 2018; Chizat, 2022), minimizing the squared sliced Wasserstein distance (Bonneel et al., 2015) between particle marginals and privatized marginals. The projection step, which converts noisy signed measures into valid probability measures, adapts the signed-measure extension of the 1-Wasserstein distance from Boedihardjo et al. (2022). Unlike query-based methods (GEM, RAP, Private GSD) that require predefining a query set, PrivPGD is optimized specifically for marginal queries while retaining the flexibility to add arbitrary differentiable penalty terms for domain-specific constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PrivPGD offers a practical and scalable generation step for marginal-based DP synthesis, handling 100k+ data points with all 2-Way marginals on GPU, and preserves geometric structure (e.g., ordering of age or discretized continuous features) that PGM-based methods discard. The ability to inject domain-specific constraints (e.g., hiding a specific population-level statistic) via a simple additive loss term is a unique practical advantage over both PGM and query-based baselines. A notable limitation is that at very low privacy budgets (ε=0.2), PGM's maximum-entropy inductive bias becomes advantageous, suggesting that incorporating similar regularization into PrivPGD is an important open direction. Broader questions remain about optimal embedding choices for non-ordinal categorical features and about extending the approach to continuous (non-discretized) data.
