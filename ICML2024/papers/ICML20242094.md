## Problem

Existing metric-constrained model-based clustering algorithms (TICC, STICC) ignore metric autocorrelation—the natural increase in feature variance with metric distance within a cluster—and suffer from computational instability and high complexity due to their EM-based optimization with soft metric penalties. This paper addresses both gaps by formulating clustering as a pairwise goodness-of-fit test problem that explicitly encodes autocorrelation structure.

## Input / Output

Each observation is a tuple (f_i, p_i) where f_i is a d_F-dimensional feature vector (e.g., PCA-reduced image embeddings, sensor readings, check-in patterns) and p_i is a position in a metric space (1D timestamps or 2D geo-coordinates with geodesic distance). The method requires a predefined metric distance d_c and a number of neighbors n for local model fitting; the number of clusters K is not preset but inferred by the downstream density-based clustering step. Output is a partition of observations into clusters, with no fixed cluster count.

## Method

MC-GTA fits a Gaussian Markov Random Field (GMRF) to each observation using its n nearest neighbors in metric space (via Graphical Lasso), then computes pairwise square Wasserstein-2 distances between the resulting model parameterizations as a model-based dissimilarity. The key novelty is a generalized model-based semivariogram: a multivariate extension of the classic geostatistical semivariogram that replaces univariate variance with W2², capturing how feature-model dissimilarity grows with metric distance. The clustering loss is a total hinge loss that penalizes intra-cluster pairs whose W2² exceeds the fitted theoretical semivariogram minus a margin δ, effectively implementing goodness-of-fit tests where pairs failing the test (i.e., statistically not from the same distribution) incur penalty. The penalty is zero beyond the semivariogram range ρ, making it local and computationally efficient. Final clustering is performed by applying DBSCAN to the resulting weighted distance matrix, avoiding EM iterations entirely and yielding a sequential, stable algorithm with O(n²d²) complexity.

## Results

MC-GTA-w (with metric constraints) achieves the best ARI and NMI on 8 out of 9 real-world datasets, outperforming the strongest baselines TICC and STICC by up to 14.3% in ARI (Gesture: 26.51 vs. 12.20) and 32.1% in NMI (Gesture: 55.34 vs. 23.20). On the iNaturalist-2018 spatial dataset, MC-GTA-w achieves ARI 42.70 / NMI 40.49, while STICC fails to converge entirely. MC-GTA is also 5–15× faster than TICC/STICC (e.g., 14 s vs. 508 s on Pavement; 594 s vs. 6325 s on iNaturalist-2018), and its hyperparameter tuning requires only re-running DBSCAN rather than full EM iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MC-GTA-w outperforms TICC on all three 1-D temporal datasets: Pavement (77.64 vs. 62.27 ARI), Vehicle (65.04 vs. 50.53), and Gesture (26.51 vs. 12.20, a 14.3-point ARI gap). On 2-D spatial datasets, MC-GTA-w beats STICC on Climate (20.08 vs. 17.62 ARI), iNaturalist-2018 (42.70 vs. NC), POI (39.81 vs. 11.04), and Land-use (36.54 vs. 15.35); STICC fails to converge on iNat2018 and POI entirely. The only dataset where MC-GTA-w is not the best is Climate, where SKATER achieves 23.44 ARI vs. MC-GTA-w's 20.08, though SKATER collapses on the other three spatial datasets (≤1.52 ARI). Compared to non-constrained model-based GMM, MC-GTA-w improves ARI by 49.6 points on Pavement (77.64 vs. 28.05) and 21.0 points on iNat2018 (42.70 vs. 21.72). Density-based baselines (DBSCAN, HDBSCAN) and constrained non-model-based methods (PCK-Means, MDST-DBSCAN) are consistently outperformed by large margins across all datasets. In terms of efficiency, MC-GTA runs in 14 s on Pavement vs. 508 s for TICC and 594 s on iNat2018 vs. 6325 s for STICC, a 5–15× speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the metric-constrained model-based clustering framework established by TICC (Hallac et al., 2017) and its spatial variant STICC (Kang et al., 2022), which combine Gaussian Markov Random Fields with soft metric penalties optimized via EM. The key departure is replacing the log-likelihood-based EM objective with pairwise Wasserstein-2 distances between per-observation GMRFs, and introducing a multivariate generalization of Matheron's classic semivariogram to explicitly capture metric autocorrelation — a factor all prior methods ignore. Clustering is then performed via density-based algorithms (DBSCAN) on a weighted distance matrix rather than iterative re-estimation, yielding a sequential, non-iterative procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a fundamental blind spot in metric-constrained clustering: the failure to account for metric autocorrelation, which can even reverse clustering decisions (a farther observation may be more similar than a nearer one once autocorrelation is considered). The 10× speedup over TICC/STICC while improving ARI by up to 14.3% makes this practically significant for large-scale temporal/spatial data mining. A noted limitation is performance degradation on spatially sparse data (e.g., Climate dataset), where the neighborhood needed for GMRF estimation becomes too large and noisy. Future work on extending to non-Gaussian MRFs and incorporating global priors for sparse settings are natural next steps.
