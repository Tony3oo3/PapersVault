## Problem

Fort & Scherlis (2019) identified the Goldilocks zone as a region of excess positive curvature of the loss Hessian associated with trainable initializations, but characterized it informally as a hollow spherical shell defined by initialization norm. This characterization is incomplete: the paper shows that adjusting softmax temperature can shift the zone to any norm, and the fundamental condition governing when positive curvature emerges was never derived. Additionally, the relationship between the Goldilocks zone and actual model trainability remained unexplained and, as this paper reveals, imperfect.

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on homogeneous ReLU networks (no biases) with a single initialization scale α and softmax temperature T, evaluated on standard image classification tasks (FashionMNIST 28×28, CIFAR-10 32×32). Because full Hessian computation is intractable, positive curvature is estimated by projecting the Hessian onto a d=50 random low-rank subspace via a sparse orthogonal matrix R, then computing Tr(H_d)/||H_d||_F explicitly. The key outputs are the G-term and H-term spectral norms from the Gauss-Newton decomposition, the positive curvature metric across α, and a taxonomy of training behaviors (convergence, divergence, lazy learning, zero-logit collapse) for each (α, η) configuration.

## Method

The paper derives that excess positive curvature of the loss Hessian is governed by a single condition: the spectral norm of the G-term must dominate that of the H-term in the Gauss-Newton decomposition (||G'||₂ ≳ ||H'||₂), a BBP-type phase transition. For homogeneous networks, the G-term scales as O(α^{2L-2}) while the H-term scales as O(α^{L-2}), so the G-term vanishes at high α due to saturated softmax (diag(p)−ppᵀ → 0) and at low α due to vanishing cross-class logit gradients. Using the random logit model of Fort & Ganguli (2019), the paper analytically shows that within the Goldilocks zone, maximum positive curvature corresponds to low model confidence (uniform softmax), which in turn implies low initial cross-entropy loss and a previously unknown vanishing-gradient regime where expected full-batch gradients cancel on balanced datasets. Empirically, the authors optimize LeNet-300-100 and LeNet-5 across a wide range of α and learning rates with full-batch gradient descent, revealing that the Goldilocks zone boundaries do not align with trainability boundaries—LeNet-5 fails to learn for α > 1.8 despite the zone extending to α ≈ 18, with failure characterized by an increasing fraction of zero logits.

## Results

The paper derives that the Goldilocks zone is governed by the condition ‖G'‖₂ ≳ ‖H'‖₂ (G-term dominance in the Gauss-Newton Hessian decomposition), not by initialization norm alone. Empirically, LeNet-5 on CIFAR-10 has a Goldilocks zone spanning α ∈ [1, 18], yet the model fails to train above random accuracy for any α > 1.8, demonstrating that positive curvature is an imperfect proxy for trainability. LeNet-300-100 on FashionMNIST exhibits lazy learning for α > 1, with test accuracy saturating at 75% (10% below the unscaled network). A previously unknown vanishing gradient phenomenon is confirmed: the expected cross-entropy gradient norm scales linearly with ‖Q̂ − Q‖ (the mismatch between model and dataset class priors), verified on LeNet-5 with 61,170 parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing methods in the traditional sense; instead, it contrasts optimization behaviors across initialization scales (α) and between two architectures. The key empirical contrast is between LeNet-300-100 (fully-connected, 2 hidden layers) and LeNet-5 (convolutional, 4 hidden layers): both exhibit similar divergence-then-recovery dynamics for α < 1, but diverge sharply for α > 1. LeNet-300-100 enters a lazy learning regime (zero training error, 75% test accuracy), while LeNet-5 becomes completely untrainable and develops an increasing fraction of zero logits even well within the Goldilocks zone boundaries. The paper also contrasts the original Fort & Scherlis (2019) characterization (Goldilocks zone as a norm-dependent spherical shell) with the refined condition (G-term dominance), showing the former is invalidated by the softmax temperature adjustment T = α^L which replicates the Goldilocks zone at any initialization norm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Fort & Scherlis (2019), who introduced the Goldilocks zone as a region of excess positive curvature associated with a narrow range of initialization norms, and on Fort & Ganguli (2019), whose random logit model and Gauss-Newton decomposition of the Hessian (originating from Sagun et al., 2016) provide the analytical backbone. The key departure is a conceptual reframing: by leveraging the homogeneity property from Dinh et al. (2017) and showing that softmax temperature T=α^L exactly replicates the Goldilocks zone at any initialization norm, the authors demonstrate that the zone is not a fixed geometric region in parameter space but is governed by the dominance of the G-term over the H-term in the Gauss-Newton decomposition. This shifts the explanation from a norm-based heuristic to a condition on the relative spectral norms of the two Hessian components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central practical implication—that excess positive curvature is an imperfect estimator of initialization trainability, with LeNet-5 failing to learn well within the Goldilocks zone boundaries—challenges a widely cited heuristic and calls for alternative initialization quality metrics. The previously unknown vanishing cross-entropy gradient phenomenon (caused by alignment between model average predictions and dataset priors) and the zero-logits failure mode open new directions for understanding training pathologies. However, the analysis is restricted to homogeneous networks (no biases, no residual connections) and vanilla gradient descent, leaving open how these findings extend to the inhomogeneous architectures and adaptive optimizers (Adam, AdaGrad) that dominate modern practice.
