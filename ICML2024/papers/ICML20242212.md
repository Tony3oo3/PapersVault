## Problem

Positive-Unlabeled (PU) learning on graph data is fundamentally challenged by heterophilic structures, where edges connect nodes of different classes. This heterophily violates the irreducibility assumption required for class-prior estimation (causing overestimation) and degrades latent label inference during classifier training by misleading message passing among same-class nodes. Existing graph PU methods implicitly rely on homophily, and no prior work addresses how to mitigate heterophilic edges when negative labels are unavailable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an undirected graph with node feature vectors, a subset of labeled positive nodes, and the remaining unlabeled nodes (containing both hidden positives and negatives). Beyond the standard binary classifier output, GPL jointly produces an estimated class prior and a learned adjacency matrix whose edge weights are optimized to suppress heterophilic connections. The adjacency matrix is parameterized as a learnable positive mask M ⊙ A, so the graph topology is fixed but edge weights are continuously adjusted during training.

## Method

GPL introduces a Label Propagation Loss (LPL) that uses k-iteration label propagation as a surrogate for heterophily reduction: minimizing the probability that observed positive nodes are misclassified as negative by LPA is proven (Theorem 4.2) to be equivalent to minimizing total heterophily influence on those nodes. The overall training is formulated as a bilevel optimization — the inner loop optimizes the adjacency matrix via LPL (strengthening homophilic edges, weakening heterophilic ones), while the outer loop trains a GCN classifier using the estimated class prior and the refined graph structure. A key feedback mechanism feeds the classifier's predicted positive/negative labels back into LPL (Equation 9), progressively enriching the supervision signal beyond the initially observed positives. Theoretical results further show that reducing heterophily tightens the class-prior estimation error bound (Theorem 4.4) and preserves P–N embedding separation that would otherwise collapse under aggregation (Theorem 4.5).

## Results

GPL achieves the highest F1 score across all 10 datasets, ranging from 74.1% on CiteSeer (h=0.26) to 82.3% on Wiki-CS (h=0.35) for homophilic graphs, and 36.2%–48.0% for heterophilic graphs (h=0.70–0.89). The most substantial gains appear on heterophilic datasets, where GPL improves over the best baseline by 10.2%–17.4% (e.g., +17.4% on Squirrel, +15.2% on Cornell). For class-prior estimation, GPL achieves the lowest absolute error on every dataset, with errors as low as 0.008 on PubMed and 0.01–0.04 on heterophilic graphs, compared to 0.13–0.39 for the best competing estimators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPL outperforms all 10 baselines on every dataset. Against the strongest graph-PU baselines GRAB and PU-GNN, GPL gains 2.1–5.8% F1 on homophilic graphs (e.g., 82.3 vs 80.3 on Wiki-CS) and 10.2–17.4% on heterophilic graphs (e.g., 37.9 vs 28.3 on Cornell, 48.0 vs 32.6 on Actor). The i.i.d.-based PU methods (TED, NNPU) degrade severely on heterophilic structures—GCN+NNPU drops to 11.7% on Texas while GPL maintains 46.3%—confirming that standard PU assumptions break down without explicit heterophily handling. For class-prior estimation (Table 2), GPL achieves the lowest absolute error on all 10 datasets (0.008–0.08), substantially outperforming TED (0.02–0.26), GRAB (0.07–0.37), and kernel-based estimators like KM (0.06–0.39), with the gap widening as heterophily increases. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that removing LPL causes the largest drop on heterophilic datasets (−63.3% on Cornell, −51.6% on Chameleon), while removing bilevel optimization or selected-node augmentation yields smaller but consistent losses (−2.7% to −20.2%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PU learning framework of Garg et al. (2021) from i.i.d. data to graph-structured data, adapting their mixture proportion estimation for class-prior estimation to operate on an optimized adjacency matrix. The label propagation mechanism underpinning the proposed LPL loss draws directly from the GCN–label propagation unification of Wang & Leskovec (2020), repurposing it as a differentiable surrogate for heterophily reduction without requiring negative labels. The paper departs from existing graph PU methods (GRAB, PU-GNN, LSDAN) that implicitly assume homophilic structure, and from heterophily-reduction methods (Geom-GCN, CPGNN) that require full label supervision, by identifying heterophily as the core obstacle and addressing it within a bilevel optimization that needs only positive labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a genuine gap: prior graph PU learning methods silently assumed homophily, yet real-world graphs (fraud detection, pandemic prediction) are often strongly heterophilic, causing those methods to degrade sharply. The clean theoretical decomposition—showing heterophily violates the irreducibility assumption for CPE and shrinks P–N embedding distance during GNN aggregation—gives practitioners a principled diagnostic for when and why their PU pipeline fails. Open questions include extension to multi-class PU learning, scalability of the iterative LPA inner loop to very large graphs, and whether the bilevel schedule can be replaced by a single-stage alternative. The authors also note future directions toward imbalanced and out-of-distribution graph settings.
