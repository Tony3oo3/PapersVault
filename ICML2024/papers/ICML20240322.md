## Problem

Black-box adversarial attacks that combine transfer-based and query-based methods rely on the surrogate model's local gradient as a prior, which is insufficiently informative and still requires hundreds of queries. The surrogate model's loss function encodes rich global information about the black-box model's behavior across the entire perturbation space, yet no prior work has exploited this as a function-level prior within a principled optimization framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240322/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural image (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 224×224 for ImageNet, or image-caption pairs for MS-COCO VLMs), a white-box surrogate model (e.g., WRN-34-10, ResNet-152, MiniGPT-4), and query access to the black-box target model. The search is constrained to an ℓ∞ ball of radius ε (8/255 for image classifiers, 16/255 for VLMs) around the natural input. The output is an adversarial example within the perturbation budget, with the number of queries consumed as the primary efficiency metric; for VLMs, success is measured by whether a target word/phrase is removed from the generated caption (verified via CLIPscore > 0.95).

## Method

P-BO models the black-box attack objective f with a Gaussian process whose mean function is initialized to the surrogate model's loss f′ (i.e., f ~ GP(f′, k)), so the posterior mean at each point is f′(x) plus a zero-mean GP correction fitted to observed query residuals. The next query is selected by maximizing a UCB acquisition function over the ℓ∞ ball. A key theoretical result shows the cumulative regret is bounded by ‖f − f′‖_k · √(8Tγ_T / log(1+σ⁻²)), meaning performance degrades when the prior is poorly aligned with the target. To handle this, an adaptive integration strategy introduces a coefficient λ on the prior (f ~ GP(λf′, k)) and estimates λ* by maximizing the log-likelihood of observed data under the GP model, effectively down-weighting the prior when it is uninformative (e.g., across dissimilar architectures on ImageNet) while fully leveraging it when the surrogate closely approximates the target.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, P-BO with adaptive λ* achieves 100% attack success rate (ASR) on all three target models (ResNet-50, DenseNet-121, SENet-18) with only 14–19 average queries (median 11), compared to 81–93 average queries for vanilla BO and 49–76 for P-RGF. On ImageNet, P-BO(λ*) reaches 91.4% ASR on Inception-v3 with 115 average queries, 98.7% on MobileNet-v2 with 95 queries, and 83.6% on ViT-B/16 with 189 queries. On MS-COCO VLMs, P-BO(λ*) achieves 95.8% ASR on InstructBLIP (13 avg queries), 90.8% on mPLUG-Owl (27 avg queries), and 96.7% on VPGTrans (24 avg queries), far exceeding the 45.8–79.2% ASR of BO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

P-BO(λ*) dominates all nine baselines on CIFAR-10, achieving 100% ASR with 14–19 average queries versus 81–93 for BO (Ru et al., 2020), 49–76 for P-RGF (Dong et al., 2022), and 86–151 for NPAttack (Bai et al., 2023). On ImageNet, the adaptive coefficient is critical: P-BO with fixed λ=1 actually underperforms vanilla BO on Inception-v3 (60.8% vs. 89.2% ASR) and ViT-B/16 (43.9% vs. 77.5%), confirming the theoretical prediction that a poorly aligned prior degrades performance; with adaptive λ*, P-BO recovers to 91.4% and 83.6% respectively, surpassing all baselines. On MS-COCO VLMs, P-BO(λ*) achieves 90.8–96.7% ASR with 13–27 average queries, dramatically outperforming BO (45.8–79.2% ASR, 58–96 queries) and P-RGF (34.2–54.2% ASR, 274–358 queries). The comparison with P-RGF (Dong et al., 2022) specifically validates the paper's core claim that a global function prior (surrogate loss) is more informative than a local gradient prior for query-based attacks. Against the closest competitor BO (Ru et al., 2020), P-BO reduces average queries by roughly 5–6× on CIFAR-10 and 1.5–2× on ImageNet while matching or exceeding ASR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Bayesian Optimization (BO) for black-box adversarial attacks (Ru et al., 2020; Zhao et al., 2019) by replacing the standard zero-mean Gaussian process with one whose mean is initialized by a surrogate model's loss function, shifting from local gradient priors (P-RGF, Dong et al., 2022) to a global function prior. Theoretically, it builds on the regret analysis of Srinivas et al. (2010) for GP-BO, extending it to the non-zero-mean case and showing the bound depends on the RKHS distance between the target and prior functions. The adaptive integration strategy, which treats the prior weight λ as a GP hyperparameter optimized via log-likelihood, departs from prior transfer-learning-in-BO work (Feurer et al., 2018; Golovin et al., 2017) that used heuristic or fixed fusion weights without theoretical grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic query reduction (from ~80–100 to ~15–20 on CIFAR-10) makes black-box attacks significantly more practical and raises the bar for model robustness, particularly as the method extends to large VLMs where safety is an active concern. The finding that a fixed prior (λ=1) can actively degrade performance below vanilla BO on ImageNet underscores that prior information is not universally beneficial and motivates adaptive mechanisms in other transfer-based settings. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even higher-dimensional inputs without dimensionality reduction, how it performs against stronger adversarial defenses (ASR drops to ~36%), and whether the GP-based framework can be made more scalable for very large query budgets.
