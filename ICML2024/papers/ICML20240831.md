## Problem

Theoretical guarantees for variational inference have been established primarily for the case where the variational family consists of single Gaussians, but mixture of Gaussians is a far more flexible and practically relevant family capable of capturing multimodal posteriors. No prior work had derived optimization or approximation guarantees for VI when the variational family is a mixture of Gaussians, leaving a significant gap in understanding how well and how fast such approximations can be computed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240831/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target potential V (defining μ* ∝ exp(−V) on ℝ^d), a number of mixture components n, a fixed variance parameter ε (all components share covariance ε²I_d with equal weights 1/n), and initial particle positions. It produces the optimal particle positions (Gaussian means), an approximate posterior as a convolution of the atomic measure with the Gaussian kernel, a descent lemma guaranteeing objective decrease per iteration, and a non-asymptotic approximation error bound of order (log n + 1)/n in reverse KL. The key structural constraint is that only the means are optimized while covariances and weights are fixed, reducing the infinite-dimensional VI problem to a finite-dimensional gradient descent on n particle locations.

## Method

The paper recasts VI over the fixed-variance, equal-weight Gaussian mixture family as minimization of a mollified relative entropy F_ε(μ) = G_{V_ε}(μ) + U_ε(μ), where the target is compared against the convolution of an atomic measure with a Gaussian kernel k_ε. This reformulation makes the objective well-defined for discrete measures and, crucially, smooth in the Wasserstein geometry (unlike the standard KL whose Wasserstein Hessian is unbounded due to the entropy term). For optimization, the paper derives a descent lemma (Proposition 4) showing that Wasserstein gradient descent decreases the objective at each step under L-smoothness of V and bounded second moments, yielding an O(1/L) rate on average squared gradient norms. For approximation, Theorem 7 establishes that the optimal n-component mixture achieves reverse KL at most C²_{μ*}(log n + 1)/n via an induction argument that adds one component at a time, adapting the Li & Barron (1999) technique from forward to reverse KL with new bounding lemmas involving the Hellinger distance. The particle dynamics take the form of an interacting particle system where each particle's update depends on all others through the Gaussian kernel, and the Wasserstein gradient of the mollified entropy involves integrals over ℝ^d that are approximated by Monte Carlo sampling.

## Results

The paper establishes two main theoretical guarantees. First, a descent lemma (Proposition 4) showing that Wasserstein gradient descent on the mollified KL decreases the objective at each iteration, with a smoothness constant M = L + K_{ε,n,h} where K_{ε,n,h} = 1/ε² + 2√(hn)/ε³ + √n/ε² + n√h/(2ε³). Second, an approximation bound (Theorem 7) showing that the optimal n-component equal-weight Gaussian mixture achieves KL(k_ε * μ_n | μ*) ≤ C²_{μ*}(log(n)+1)/n. Empirically, Figure 2 confirms the O(1/L) convergence rate of the average squared gradient norm, and Figure 3 confirms the O(log(n)/n) approximation rate across dimensions d ∈ {1, 2, 5, 10, 20}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not run head-to-head empirical comparisons against competing VI methods; its contributions are theoretical. The closest comparison is with Li & Barron (1999), who proved a forward KL rate of O(1/n) for mixture density estimation. The present paper's reverse KL rate of O(log(n)/n) is slightly slower, which the authors attribute to the fixed equal-weight constraint; they note that allowing non-fixed weights (setting α = 2/(n+1)) would recover the O(1/n) rate. For integral approximation error, the derived bound O(√(log(n)/n)) is comparable to the O(n^{-1/2}) rate known for MCMC with n particles (Latuszyński et al., 2013). The particle update scheme (Eq. 6) is shown to recover the fixed-covariance special case of Lambert et al. (2022), confirming consistency with the Bures-Wasserstein gradient flow framework. Unlike prior Gaussian VI results (Lambert et al., 2022; Diao et al., 2023) that require strong convexity of the target potential, the descent lemma here only requires L-smoothness, at the cost of losing displacement convexity of the objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Wasserstein gradient flow framework for variational inference (Lambert et al., 2022; Diao et al., 2023) from single Gaussians to mixtures of Gaussians with fixed covariance and equal weights, a setting the authors note has not been previously addressed theoretically. The optimization analysis builds on descent-lemma techniques from SVGD and MMD gradient flow literature (Arbel et al., 2019; Korba et al., 2020), while the approximation result adapts the inductive proof structure of Li & Barron (1999) from forward to reverse KL. A key conceptual departure from prior mollified-entropy work (Carrillo et al., 2019; Craig et al., 2023) is the use of a doubly-convolved negative entropy U_ε(μ) = ∫log(k_ε*μ)d(k_ε*μ), which is well-defined for discrete measures and directly corresponds to the KL of the mixture density, rather than the singly-convolved variant studied in PDE/kinetic theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide both optimization and approximation guarantees for VI with Gaussian mixtures, even in the simplified fixed-covariance/equal-weight setting, filling a notable gap since mixture models are dense in the space of probability distributions. The mollified KL framework elegantly resolves the non-smoothness of the standard KL in Wasserstein geometry (where the Hessian is unbounded due to the entropy term), enabling a clean descent lemma at the cost of losing displacement convexity. Major open questions remain: extending to variable covariances and dynamic weights (which shifts the geometry from Wasserstein to Fisher-Rao), obtaining non-asymptotic Hessian bounds in terms of ε to quantify the convexity-smoothness trade-off, and closing the gap between the O(log n/n) reverse-KL rate and the O(1/n) forward-KL rate of Li & Barron.
