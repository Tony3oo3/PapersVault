## Problem

Regret minimization in stochastic Contextual MDPs (CMDPs) under offline function approximation remained open: the prior best algorithm (Levy & Mansour, 2023) required a minimum reachability assumption p_min that can be arbitrarily small (even zero for deterministic transitions), yielding only Õ(T^{3/4}) regret in general. The key question was whether this structural assumption on the true CMDP could be replaced by a less restrictive assumption on the learner-chosen dynamics function class, while retaining rate-optimal Õ(√T) regret with only offline regression oracles.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives finite function classes F ⊆ C×S×A→[0,1] (rewards) and P ⊆ S×(S×A×C)→[0,1] (dynamics), along with offline least-squares and log-loss regression oracles that solve empirical risk minimization over these classes. At each episode it observes a context c ~ D and an MDP trajectory, and outputs a context-dependent policy π(c;·): S→A. The regret bound Õ(H³√(T|S||A|d_E(P)log(|F||P|/δ))) is independent of the context space size |C|, which may be arbitrarily large or even infinite (via covering number arguments).

## Method

E-UC³RL builds on the optimistic-in-expectation framework but introduces three key technical tools. First, it uses a log-loss regression oracle for dynamics (rather than LSR), which yields generalization guarantees in squared Hellinger distance, and extends the Eluder dimension from the ℓ₂ norm to general bounded metrics to control the stability of successive oracle estimates. Second, it constructs counterfactual confidence bonuses b_k^β(c,s,a,h) = min(1, β/2 / (1 + Σᵢ q_h(s,a|πᵢ(c;·), P̂ᵢᶜ))) where occupancy measures are computed using each past round's own dynamics estimate P̂ᵢ (stabilized via the Eluder dimension), rather than the current estimate. Third, a multiplicative value change of measure lemma (Lemma 4.1) bounds the value difference between true and estimated dynamics by the expected cumulative Hellinger distance, enabling confidence bounds that are both valid and computable. The algorithm then constructs an optimistic CMDP with these bonuses added to estimated rewards and plays its optimal policy via dynamic programming.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 3.1) establishes that E-UC³RL achieves a regret bound of Õ(H³√(T|S||A|d_P log(|F||P|/δ))) with probability at least 1−δ, where d_P is the Eluder dimension of the dynamics function class with respect to the Hellinger distance. This is the first rate-optimal Õ(√T) regret guarantee for stochastic CMDPs under the general offline function approximation setting, removing the minimum reachability assumption (p_min) required by prior work. The algorithm is computationally efficient, with runtime in poly(T, |S|, |A|, H) assuming efficient regression oracles. The authors note an apparent gap of H^2.5 and d_P factors relative to the known lower bound of Levy & Mansour (2023).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments, so comparisons are between theoretical guarantees. Compared to Levy & Mansour (2023), which achieves Õ(√T/p_min) regret under a minimum reachability assumption, E-UC³RL removes the p_min dependency entirely, replacing it with the Eluder dimension d_P of the dynamics function class — a property of the learner's chosen function class rather than the true MDP. This is strictly more general since p_min can be arbitrarily small (even zero for deterministic transitions), while the Eluder dimension can remain bounded. Compared to Foster et al. (2021), which achieves Õ(√T) regret for adversarial contexts, E-UC³RL requires only offline regression oracles (LSR and LLR) rather than the much stronger online estimation oracle, at the cost of restricting to stochastic (i.i.d.) contexts. Compared to Modi & Tewari (2020), which achieves Õ(√T) under a Generalized Linear Model assumption, the present work handles general finite function classes. The paper acknowledges a gap of H^2.5 and d_P factors between its upper bound and the known lower bound, leaving the question of whether the Eluder dimension dependence is necessary open.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Levy & Mansour (2023), which achieved Õ(√T/p_min) regret for stochastic CMDPs under a minimum reachability assumption, by replacing that structural assumption with the Eluder dimension of the dynamics function class. It extends the Eluder dimension from the ℓ₂ norm (Russo & Van Roy, 2013; Osband & Van Roy, 2014) to general bounded metrics, specifically the squared Hellinger distance, enabling its use with log-loss regression oracles for dynamics approximation (following Foster et al., 2021). The counterfactual confidence bonus structure is adapted from Xu & Zeevi (2020) for contextual bandits, and the multiplicative value change of measure lemma draws on Foster et al. (2021) to bridge the gap between oracle guarantees under true dynamics and confidence bounds under estimated dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first efficient, rate-optimal Õ(√T) regret algorithm for stochastic CMDPs using only offline regression oracles, removing the restrictive minimum reachability assumption that could be arbitrarily small (even zero for deterministic transitions) and was impossible to estimate online. The extension of the Eluder dimension to general bounded metrics is a clean theoretical contribution of independent interest. Key open questions include closing the H^2.5 and d_P gap between the upper bound and the known lower bound, removing the dependence on the Eluder dimension entirely, and extending the approach to rich-observation settings.
