## Problem

Discovering and modeling governing differential equations from data remains largely a manual process, and existing automated methods have critical limitations: SINDy is restricted to linear combinations of basis functions, Neural ODEs produce implicit equations that resist interpretation, and sequential ODE solvers like Runge-Kutta are too slow to train neural networks on batches of independent ODEs. No prior approach combines explicit, interpretable ODE representations with efficient differentiable solving at scale within a standard neural network training loop.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241643/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes time-series trajectories (e.g., positions/velocities of N bodies, spatial fields on a 256-point grid, or acceleration signals) and produces both explicit ODE coefficients (the governing equation itself) and numerical solutions over a specified number of time steps. Coefficients can be time-dependent or time-invariant, per-input or shared dataset-wide, and step sizes are learnable scalars rather than fixed hyperparameters. For PDE tasks, spatial dimensions are modeled as independent ODEs (1D) or via stacked MNN layers in a neural operator (2D), with the ODE solved for 30–100 steps per forward pass.

## Method

Mechanistic Neural Networks insert Mechanistic Blocks into standard architectures, each consisting of a mechanistic encoder (a neural network that outputs ODE coefficients α) followed by the NeuRLP solver. NeuRLP reformulates discretized linear ODEs as equality-constrained quadratic programs by combining Young's (1961) linear-programming formulation of ODEs with a diagonal quadratic relaxation and slack variables, then solves the resulting KKT system via Cholesky factorization or sparse conjugate gradients on GPU. This yields three key advantages over sequential solvers: step parallelism (all time steps solved simultaneously), batch parallelism (independent ODE systems solved in one forward pass), and differentiable, learnable step sizes. Nonlinear ODE terms are handled by introducing auxiliary variables into the QP and adding squared-loss consistency terms to the network's objective. Gradients flow through the solver via the KKT system, enabling end-to-end backpropagation without unrolling sequential steps.

## Results

On 2D Darcy Flow PDE solving, Mechanistic NN achieves RMSE 0.0065, outperforming FNO (0.0070) and all other neural operator baselines (GNO 0.0346, MGNO 0.0416, PCANN 0.0299). On the 2-body parameter discovery task, MNN reduces Force MSE from 879 (SONODE) to 345 and improves cosine similarity from -0.26 to 0.85 while recovering the mass ratio (2.02 vs. ground truth 2). On N-body prediction (JPL Horizons ephemerides), MNN improves over Neural ODEs by at least a factor of 10 in evaluation loss over 2000-step rollouts. The NeuRLP solver is approximately 200× faster than torchdiffeq's RK4+adjoint for fitting 1000-step noisy sinusoids while achieving lower MSE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SINDy (Brunton et al., 2016), MNN recovers exact governing equations for the Lorenz system (matching SINDy) but substantially outperforms it on nonlinear (tanh) and rational-function ODEs where SINDy overfits to the training trajectory and its learned vector fields diverge from ground truth. Against Neural ODEs (Chen et al., 2018) and second-order Neural ODEs (Norcliffe et al., 2020), MNN achieves at least 10× lower evaluation loss on N-body ephemerides prediction and correctly classifies nested spheres without augmentation (where unaugmented Neural ODEs fail due to diffeomorphism constraints). On the 2-body parameter discovery task, MNN reduces Force MSE from 879 to 345 and flips cosine similarity from -0.26 to 0.85 compared to SONODE, while both recover the mass ratio (2.02 vs. 2.11, GT=2). On 2D Darcy Flow, MNN (RMSE 0.0065) marginally beats FNO (0.0070) and substantially outperforms GNO (0.0346), MGNO (0.0416), and PCANN (0.0299). On 1D KdV, MNN is competitive with FNO and Lie-symmetry augmented models (Brandstetter et al., 2022) without requiring specialized rollout-stability tricks. On the F-16 aircraft vibration benchmark, MNN matches second-order Neural ODE accuracy while converging significantly faster and achieving 2× lower training error. The NeuRLP solver itself is validated as comparable in accuracy to scipy's RK4 on linear ODEs but ~200× faster than torchdiffeq's adjoint-based RK4 for long (1000-step) noisy fitting tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes a 1961 result by Young showing that linear ODEs can be solved via linear programs, modernizing it through differentiable quadratic programming relaxation (building on Amos & Kolter 2017, Wilder et al. 2019, and Pervez et al. 2023) to create a GPU-parallel, differentiable ODE solver (NeuRLP) that plugs natively into neural network training. Conceptually, it departs from Neural ODEs (Chen et al. 2018), which learn implicit ODEs as a continuous-time network, by instead generating explicit symbolic ODE representations as intermediate neural features and solving them in a single differentiable layer. For equation discovery, it extends beyond SINDy (Brunton et al. 2016) by supporting arbitrary differentiable nonlinear compositions of basis functions rather than restricted linear combinations, and for PDE solving it competes with specialized neural operators (FNO, Lie-symmetry augmented models) without task-specific architectural tricks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit ODE representations make MNNs inherently interpretable—researchers can inspect the discovered governing equations directly—while the NeuRLP solver's parallelism (step, batch, and learned step sizes) addresses a key bottleneck in training ODE-based models over long horizons. The paper demonstrates competitive or superior performance across five diverse scientific ML tasks (equation discovery, PDE solving, N-body prediction, parameter estimation, time-series forecasting), suggesting a unifying framework. However, the authors acknowledge no identifiability guarantees for the computed ODEs, limited exploration of the architectural design space, and the absence of active-learning or falsification-based validation, leaving open whether discovered equations are truly the governing mechanisms or merely good fits.
