## Problem

Stochastic compositional optimization (SCO) algorithms such as SCGD and SCSC have well-established convergence guarantees, yet their generalization behavior—how models trained on finite data perform on unseen test examples—remains entirely unexplored. No prior work has provided stability or generalization bounds for these algorithms, despite their widespread use in reinforcement learning, AUC maximization, meta-learning, and distributionally robust optimization.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes two independent i.i.d. training sets: S_ν = {ν_1,...,ν_n} for the outer function f and S_ω = {ω_1,...,ω_m} for the inner function g, along with constant step sizes η (for x-updates) and β (for the moving-average y-sequence) and a total iteration count T. The output is the model parameter x_T (or the averaged iterate (1/T)Σx_t in the convex case), accompanied by a compositional uniform stability bound (ε_ν, ε_ω) and a dimension-independent excess risk bound of the form O(1/√n + 1/√m) in the convex case and O(1/n + 1/√m) in the strongly convex case. A notable structural constraint is that the objective is a nested composition F(x) = E_ν[f_ν(E_ω[g_ω(x)])], so the generalization gap decomposes into an outer scalar term and an inner vector-valued term that cannot be handled by standard uniform stability arguments.

## Method

The paper introduces compositional uniform stability (Definition 2.1), which separately bounds the expected model change when a single outer sample ν_i or inner sample ω_j is replaced, and proves a quantitative link between this stability and the generalization gap (Theorem 2.3). The key technical obstacle is that the inner-function generalization term E[‖g(A(S)) − (1/m)Σg_ω_j(A(S))‖] is vector-valued and cannot be bounded by simply interchanging expectation and norm; the authors resolve this via a sample-splitting argument (Bousquet et al., 2020) that decomposes the term into a variance component and a stability component. For SCGD and SCSC in the convex and strongly convex settings, the stability bound contains two extra terms beyond standard SGD: the empirical gradient variance C_g of the inner function and the tracking error of the moving-average sequence y_{t+1} relative to g_S(x_t). By balancing these stability terms with the optimization error from prior convergence analyses (Wang et al., 2017; Chen et al., 2021a), the paper derives dimension-independent excess risk bounds, showing SCSC requires fewer iterations (T ≍ max(n^{2.5}, m^{2.5}) vs. T ≍ max(n^{3.5}, m^{3.5}) for SCGD in the convex case) to achieve the same O(1/√n + 1/√m) rate.

## Results

The paper provides the first stability and generalization analysis for stochastic compositional optimization (SCO) algorithms. In the convex setting, both SCGD and SCSC achieve a dimension-independent excess risk bound of O(1/√n + 1/√m), but SCGD requires T ≈ max(n^3.5, m^3.5) iterations while SCSC only needs T ≈ max(n^2.5, m^2.5). In the strongly convex setting, both achieve O(1/n + 1/√m), with SCGD requiring T ≈ max(n^(10/3), m^(10/3)) and SCSC requiring T ≈ max(n^(7/3), m^(7/3)). The key advantage of SCSC over SCGD is a reduction in iteration complexity by a factor of roughly n (or m) in both settings, attributable to the stochastically corrected moving-average update.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or dataset evaluations, so there are no direct baseline comparisons in the traditional sense. The paper positions its results relative to prior work in two ways: (1) compared to the non-compositional SGD stability results of Hardt et al. (2016), the compositional structure introduces two additional terms in the stability bound — an empirical variance term √C_g·η√T and a moving-average tracking term — which explain the higher iteration requirements; (2) compared to the uniform convergence approach of Hu et al. (2020) for the exact ERM minimizer of SCO, the paper's bounds are dimension-independent (not depending on the ambient dimension p of the optimization domain), whereas Hu et al.'s high-probability bound scales as O(√(p/(m+n))). The paper also notes that SCSC's stochastically corrected update reduces the iteration complexity relative to SCGD by approximately one power of n or m in both convex and strongly convex regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the algorithmic stability and generalization framework of Hardt et al. (2016) from non-compositional SGD to the stochastic compositional optimization (SCO) setting, where the objective involves a nested composition of expectations. It introduces 'compositional uniform stability' as the appropriate stability notion for this structure, and leverages the sample-splitting technique of Bousquet et al. (2020) to handle the vector-valued generalization term arising from the inner function. The optimization error analysis builds directly on the convergence results of Wang et al. (2017) for SCGD and Chen et al. (2021a) for SCSC, while the generalization gap estimation is the novel contribution that had been entirely absent from the SCO literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first stability and generalization analysis for stochastic compositional gradient descent algorithms, filling a notable gap given the growing use of SCO in AUC maximization, distributionally robust optimization, and meta-learning. The dimension-independent excess risk bounds (O(1/√n + 1/√m)) improve upon the dimension-dependent uniform convergence bounds of Hu et al. (2020), and the iteration complexity comparison (SCGD needing T≈max(n^3.5,m^3.5) vs. SCSC needing T≈max(n^2.5,m^2.5)) provides concrete guidance on algorithm choice. Key limitations include the restriction to convex/smooth settings, constant step sizes, and the absence of non-convex or non-smooth analysis; open questions include extending to multi-level compositions, achieving linear-time iteration complexity, and handling ReLU-activated neural network inner functions.
