## Problem

In offline black-box optimization, surrogate models learned from limited offline data are often inaccurate outside the data regime, yet no prior work provided a provable, model-agnostic bound connecting surrogate gradient errors to the resulting performance gap between surrogate-guided and target-guided optimization. This paper establishes the first such theoretical guarantee and asks how to train surrogates that directly minimize this gradient discrepancy rather than merely matching function values.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline dataset of n input-objective pairs (no further queries to the target function are permitted) and initial parameters for a feed-forward neural network surrogate (512→128→32→1, Leaky ReLU). It outputs a trained surrogate whose gradient field is then followed via m-step gradient ascent (with a fixed learning rate λ) from 128 diverse starting points to produce candidate optimized designs. The key constraint is that gradient information about the target function is never directly observed; it must be inferred entirely from value differences across the offline data.

## Method

MATCH-OPT replaces standard value-matching regression with a gradient matching loss derived from the fundamental theorem of line integrals: for each pair of inputs, the observed value difference Δz is matched against the line integral of the surrogate gradient along the segment connecting them, discretized into κ=5 intervals. To avoid O(n²) pair enumeration, training data is organized into monotonic synthetic trajectories (inputs binned by percentile, one sampled per bin), and the loss is computed along successive pairs within each trajectory. The final objective combines this gradient matching loss with a regression regularizer along the same trajectories, motivated by a tighter theoretical bound (Theorem 4.1) that depends on both gradient and value-matching quantities. The theoretical foundation (Theorem 3.2) shows the worst-case performance gap is upper-bounded by m·λ·ℓ·(1+λμ)^(m−1)·max‖∇g−∇g_φ‖, which becomes independent of m when λ≤1/m, directly justifying why minimizing gradient discrepancy is the right training objective for reliable gradient-based search.

## Results

On the DesignBench benchmark (6 tasks), MATCH-OPT achieves the lowest Mean Normalized Rank (MNR) of 0.283 at the 100th percentile and 0.350 at the 50th percentile, substantially outperforming the second-best method COMS (MNR 0.467 and 0.467 respectively). At the 100th percentile, MATCH-OPT is best on D'Kitty (0.957), second on Ant Morphology (0.931) and TF-Bind-8 (0.977), and third on Hopper (1.572) and Superconductor (0.732). At the 50th percentile, MATCH-OPT is best on Hopper (0.393) and second on TF-Bind-8 (0.594), while being sixth on Superconductor (0.439) and TF-Bind-10 (0.720).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MATCH-OPT wins outright on D'Kitty (0.957 vs. BONET 0.954 and COMS 0.953) and Hopper at the 50th percentile (0.393 vs. BONET 0.390), and is a close second on Ant Morphology (0.931 vs. CMA-ES 0.974) and TF-Bind-8 (0.977 vs. CMA-ES 0.978). Its most notable weakness is TF-Bind-10, where it ranks 6th (0.924) behind GA (0.966) and ENS-MEAN (0.968), and Superconductor at the 50th percentile where it ranks 6th (0.439) behind GA (0.641) and ENS-MEAN (0.644). The key differentiator is reliability: MATCH-OPT's MNR of 0.283 (100th) and 0.350 (50th) is markedly lower than the runner-up COMS (0.467) and BONET (0.417), meaning it consistently stays in the top-3 across tasks rather than excelling on some and failing on others. Against the strongest individual-task competitors (CMA-ES on ANT/TF8, BONET on DKITTY, COMS on Hopper), MATCH-OPT trades a small peak-performance gap for substantially more stable cross-task behavior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the offline black-box optimization framework established by Trabucco et al. (COMS, DesignBench) and the surrogate-guided gradient search paradigm, but departs from prior approaches that either condition the search model (density estimation, autoregressive modeling) or condition the surrogate via OOD regularization (RoMA, COMS, BONET). The key conceptual departure is providing the first provable upper bound on the optimization performance gap in terms of surrogate-target gradient discrepancy, and translating this into a practical gradient matching loss derived from the fundamental theorem of line integrals. The monotonic trajectory construction for efficient pair sampling is borrowed from Krishnamoorthy et al. (2023b) and Kumar & Levine (2020), but the gradient matching objective applied along these trajectories is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical bound (Theorem 3.2) gives practitioners a principled, model-agnostic criterion for surrogate quality in offline optimization—gradient matching rather than value matching—which shifts the design philosophy for surrogate training. The MNR-based reliability metric and consistent top-3 performance across diverse domains (robotics, materials, genomics) suggest gradient matching is a robust default strategy where no single method dominates. Open questions include: how to explicitly enforce the tighter bound condition (Theorem 4.1) during training, whether the quadratic pair complexity can be further reduced, and whether the approach extends to online settings (safe Bayesian optimization, safe RL) as the authors speculate. The higher running time (~1.3 hr vs. minutes for some baselines) is a practical limitation for time-sensitive applications.
