## Problem

While induction heads have been identified as critical circuit elements for in-context learning in transformers, relatively little is known about their diversity, interdependence, or the dynamics of their sudden emergence during training. Prior work relied on correlational progress measures at fixed checkpoints, leaving open what subcircuits must 'go right' to drive the sharp phase change in loss that coincides with induction head formation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241855/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes sequences of exemplar-label pairs followed by a query exemplar, where exemplars are Omniglot images encoded by a frozen ImageNet-pretrained ResNet18 and labels are randomly assigned one-hot vectors (default L=5, C=50 classes, E=1 exemplar per class). Each sequence constitutes a 2-way, 1-shot classification problem, and the model outputs the predicted label for the query exemplar. The analysis produces induction head strength trajectories, subcircuit learning curves under various clamping conditions, and data-dependent phase change timing as functions of class count and label count.

## Method

The paper introduces an 'artificial optogenetics' framework built on JAX/Equinox that exposes all intermediate activations via a call_with_all_aux method and supports causal clamping of activations throughout training (not just post-hoc), with proper gradient flow via JAX's functional autodiff. Using this framework, the authors first demonstrate that multiple induction heads contribute additively and redundantly to task performance, with many-to-many wiring to previous token heads. They then iteratively clamp computation steps (PT-attend, PT-copy, routing, matching, copying) to isolate three smoothly-evolving subcircuits—Layer 1 attend-and-copy, IH QK match, and IH copy—whose co-evolution produces the apparent discontinuous phase change. Finally, they show that increasing class count delays the match subcircuit while increasing label count delays the copy subcircuit, providing a mechanistic decomposition of data-dependent phase change timing.

## Results

Training 2-layer attention-only transformers on a 2-way 1-shot Omniglot FSL task (C=50 classes, L=5 labels) yields a sharp phase change in loss from a ~50% accuracy plateau to 99.99% accuracy, corresponding to induction head formation. The strongest induction head (Layer 2 Head 3) achieves 98% accuracy in isolation, while all 8 Layer 2 heads together contribute additively. Using the novel clamping framework, the authors identify three interacting subcircuits whose co-evolution produces the phase change: (A) Layer 1 previous-token attend+copy, (B) IH QK matching, and (C) label copying to output. The copy subcircuit (C) evolves smoothly and exponentially, while the match subcircuit (B) retains a phase change, and clamping Layer 1 shortens the phase change from ~2×10⁵ to ~7.5×10⁴ iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a mechanistic interpretability study without traditional task-level baselines; the 'compares_to' set is empty. The paper's primary methodological comparison is between the proposed clamping approach (causal interventions throughout training) and progress measures (correlational tracking of intermediate activations at checkpoints). The authors demonstrate that progress measures (e.g., attention pattern quality, loss with a component fixed at its final value) still exhibit phase changes when observed in isolation, whereas clamping reveals the underlying smooth exponential dynamics of individual subcircuits. Additionally, the paper contrasts single-head knockout ablations (which are misleading due to redundancy) with ablate-all-but-one-head analyses (which reveal each head's positive contribution), showing that knockout ablations can incorrectly dismiss network components as non-essential. The many-to-many wiring between previous token heads and induction heads is established via activation-level ablations, which the authors argue provide clearer signals than weight-based composition scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the induction circuit framework of Olsson et al. (2022) by moving from correlational progress measures (Reddy, 2023) to causal clamping interventions applied throughout training, inspired by the multi-component S-curve toy model of Jermyn & Shlegeris (2022). It departs from prior work's focus on the 'match' operation by identifying a previously overlooked 'copy' subcircuit as a critical interacting component in phase change dynamics. The additive redundancy of induction heads and many-to-many wiring to previous token heads build on head pruning findings (Michel et al., 2019) but are established through causal ablation rather than weight-based composition scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clamping framework provides a reusable JAX-based tool for causal analysis of training dynamics that prior PyTorch-based interpretability frameworks (TransformerLens, nnsight, pyvene) cannot easily support due to compilation incompatibilities. The decomposition of phase change timing into data-dependent subcircuit formation delays (classes → match difficulty, labels → copy difficulty) offers a mechanistic explanation for why data properties shift ICL emergence, with practical implications for vocabulary size selection in LLM training. Key limitations include the restriction to 2-layer attention-only transformers on a synthetic Omniglot task, leaving open whether the three-subcircuit structure and copy-subcircuit bottleneck generalize to deeper, larger models on natural language. The paper also leaves the question of why ICL fades with overtraining (Singh et al., 2023) as a direct future direction.
