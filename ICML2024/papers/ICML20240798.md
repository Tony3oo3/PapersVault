## Problem

Prior model-based RL results for Mean-Field Games (MFGs) rely on the Model-Based Eluder Dimension (MBED), which can be exponential in the number of states even in the tabular setting, raising the fundamental question of whether learning Nash Equilibrium in MFGs is inherently statistically harder than single-agent RL. Existing model-free approaches require strong structural assumptions (contractivity, monotonicity) that limit generality, leaving the sample complexity of MFGs under only basic realizability and Lipschitz continuity assumptions unresolved.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a model class M of transition functions (each mapping state, action, and state-density to a next-state distribution), a sampling oracle that returns trajectories by executing a deviated policy while the environment is fixed to a reference policy's induced density (a weaker model than a generative oracle over arbitrary densities), and hyperparameters controlling elimination thresholds and confidence. It outputs an ε-approximate Nash Equilibrium policy for the true model M*. The reward function is assumed known, with an extension to unknown rewards discussed in the appendix. The sample complexity scales polynomially with the Partial MBED and log|M|, the log-covering number of the model class.

## Method

The core contribution is the Partial Model-Based Eluder Dimension (P-MBED), which measures the complexity of the single-agent model class obtained by adversarially fixing the density input of each transition function, yielding an exponential improvement over MBED in tabular settings (bounded by |S||A| vs. exp(|S|)). The MEBP algorithm iteratively selects reference policies and runs a single-agent model elimination subroutine (ModelElim) that removes models inconsistent with observed trajectories, halving the candidate set each round. When models are clustered around their Central Model, a Bridge Policy is constructed via a Policy-Aware Model (PAM) abstraction that replaces density dependence with policy dependence, using weighted interpolation among Central Models to guarantee the bridge policy is an approximate NE of its own Central Model. This ensures that either an approximate NE is returned or the model class is halved, yielding a total of O(log|M|) single-agent elimination subproblems. The framework extends to Multi-Type MFGs by lifting heterogeneous agents into a single MFG with type-indexed states and constraining the policy space to type-consistent actions.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Thm. 4.1) establishes that the sample complexity of learning an ε-NE in MFGs is Õ(H⁷/ε² · (1+L_r H)² · dim_PE(M, ε') · log³(|M|/δ)) trajectories, with the algorithm terminating in at most log₂|M|+1 iterations. In the tabular setting (Prop. 3.4), P-MBED is bounded by |S||A|, yielding polynomial sample complexity in |S|, |A|, and H — an exponential improvement over the MBED-based bound of Huang et al. (2023), which can be Ω(exp(|S|)). For linear MFGs with decomposable transitions (Prop. 3.5), P-MBED reduces to Õ(d̃), matching the single-agent linear MDP complexity. The paper also proves an exponential separation from Mean-Field Control (Thm. F.9): even tabular MFC requires Ω(exp(|S|)) samples, while MFGs remain polynomially tractable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary theoretical comparison is with Huang et al. (2023), which introduced MBED and achieved sample complexity polynomial in MBED. This paper shows that P-MBED can be exponentially smaller than MBED: in the tabular setting, P-MBED ≤ |S||A| while MBED can reach Ω(exp(|S|)) (Prop. 3.4). Consequently, the sample complexity bound here avoids the exponential dependence on the number of states that plagued the prior MBED-based result. Additionally, the paper demonstrates that its bound does not suffer the exponential term (1+L_T)^H that appears in prior MFG/MFC results under similar Lipschitz assumptions (Remark F.4). The heuristic algorithm (Alg. 7) is evaluated on a synthetic linear MFG benchmark (Fig. 2, Appx. J), showing improved computational efficiency over the theoretically optimal Alg. 1 by replacing full policy-space optimization with NE-oracle calls, though no formal baseline comparison table is provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Huang et al. (2023), which introduced the Model-Based Eluder Dimension (MBED) and a model elimination framework for MFGs under realizability and Lipschitz assumptions. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of the Partial Model-Based Eluder Dimension (P-MBED), which fixes the density input adversarially and measures only the single-agent residual complexity, yielding an exponential improvement over MBED in tabular settings. The paper also generalizes the task to Multi-Type MFGs (heterogeneous agent populations), building on Ghosh & Aggarwal (2020) and Perolat et al. (2021) but removing their contractivity or monotonicity requirements. The algorithmic design draws on the Bridge Policy construction and Policy-Aware Model abstraction, which are novel technical tools not present in prior MFG learning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central result—that learning Nash Equilibrium in MFGs is no more statistically challenging than solving O(log|M|) single-agent RL problems—resolves a fundamental open question and removes the need for strong structural assumptions (contractivity, monotonicity) that have limited prior MFG learning results. The exponential separation between MFGs and Mean-Field Control (MFC) in the tabular setting provides a clean information-theoretic boundary for when mean-field approximation is genuinely beneficial. Open questions include extending the analysis to value-function approximation (rather than model-based), identifying conditions for computational efficiency beyond sample efficiency, and exploring whether the P-MBED framework transfers to other MARL settings beyond mean-field approximation.
