## Problem

Existing federated learning methods for heterogeneous medical models rely on public datasets to generate soft predictions for knowledge transfer, which raises severe privacy concerns and imposes additional computational and storage burdens on resource-limited healthcare institutions. This paper addresses how to enable effective information sharing among clients with different model architectures under non-IID data distributions without requiring any public dataset.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242244/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client processes its own private medical data—histopathological images at varying resolutions (384×384 downsampled to 48×48), OCT retinal images (256×256 grayscale), endoscopic images (256×256 RGB), or sleep time-series signals (1×3000)—using a client-specific model architecture (e.g., ResNet17 vs. ResNet5, or Unet++ vs. FCN). The output is a class label for classification tasks or a pixel-wise segmentation mask for segmentation. Critically, at inference time only the local model is used; the messenger model, receiver, and transmitter modules are discarded, so no additional inference cost is incurred.

## Method

MH-pFLID introduces an extremely lightweight messenger model (0.003M–0.196M parameters vs. 1M–40M for local models) that acts as a codebook for globally shared information, paired with a supervised head. Training alternates between two phases per round: (1) knowledge injection, where the frozen messenger guides the local model through an attention-based Information Receiver that computes QKV projections to align local features with global codebook features; and (2) knowledge distillation, where the frozen local model transfers knowledge to the messenger through an attention-based Information Transmitter that projects local features into the messenger's codebook space, supervised by both ground-truth labels and KL-divergence matching to local predictions. The messenger parameters are then aggregated via weighted averaging on the server. The QKV attention design in both receiver and transmitter disentangles client-specific biases from generalizable information, mitigating client drift, and the entire pipeline requires only local data—no public dataset is needed at any stage.

## Results

On the BreaKHis breast cancer classification task with different label distributions (8 heterogeneous architectures), MH-pFLID achieves an average ACC of 0.9006 and MF1 of 0.8465, outperforming the strongest baseline KT-pFL (0.8450 ACC, 0.7739 MF1) by 5.6% and 7.3% respectively. On the Polyp segmentation task with 4 heterogeneous models, MH-pFLID reaches a Dice of 0.7604, surpassing the best baseline FedSM (0.7146) by 4.6 points. On the Sleep-EDF time-series classification task, MH-pFLID achieves 0.8801 ACC and 0.8594 MF1, beating KT-pFL (0.8621 ACC, 0.8426 MF1). The messenger model adds negligible overhead: 0.032M parameters and 0.070 GFLOPS versus 1.1M–40M parameters and 0.7–80 GFLOPS for the local models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MH-pFLID outperforms all 14 baselines across every task and every client. The closest competitor is KT-pFL (Zhang et al., 2021), which achieves 0.8450 average ACC on BreaKHis vs. MH-pFLID's 0.9006 — a 5.6-point gap that widens to 7.3 points on MF1. Among homogeneous-model federated baselines, FedRep (Collins et al., 2021) and SCAFFOLD (Karimireddy et al., 2020) are the strongest, reaching 0.7578 and 0.7033 average ACC respectively on the resolution task, still trailing MH-pFLID (0.8583) by over 10 points. Heterogeneous-model distillation baselines FedMD (Li & Wang, 2019), FedDF (Lin et al., 2020), and DS-pFL (Itahara et al., 2023) all underperform KT-pFL, confirming that MH-pFLID's injection-distillation paradigm with a lightweight messenger is more effective than soft-prediction-based knowledge transfer. On the Polyp segmentation task, MH-pFLID (Dice 0.7604) beats the best segmentation-specific baselines FedSM (Xu et al., 2022; 0.7146) and LC-Fed (Wang et al., 2022; 0.6772) by 4.6 and 8.3 points respectively, while also surpassing all homogeneous-model methods (FedAvg, SCAFFOLD, FedProx, Ditto, APFL, LG-FedAvg, FedRep). On Sleep-EDF time-series, MH-pFLID leads KT-pFL by 1.8 ACC points and 1.7 MF1 points, with the gap most pronounced on the Transformer client (0.8725 vs. 0.8419 ACC).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge distillation paradigm for heterogeneous federated learning (FedMD, FedDF, KT-pFL, DS-pFL) by replacing the public-dataset-based soft prediction exchange with a lightweight messenger model that carries concentrated information via attention-based receiver and transmitter modules. The aggregation step directly reuses FedAvg's weighted parameter averaging, while the injection-distillation loop repurposes Hinton-style KD (KL divergence between messenger and local head outputs) without requiring any shared public data. The paper also positions itself against personalized FL methods for statistical heterogeneity (FedRep, LG-FedAvg, APFL, Ditto) by addressing the additional, orthogonal challenge of system heterogeneity—different model architectures across clients—which prior personalized FL work largely ignores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Eliminating the public dataset requirement removes a major practical barrier for medical federated learning, where collecting, labeling, and sharing public medical data is costly and raises serious privacy concerns. The messenger model's extreme lightness (0.035M parameters vs. 1.3–40M for local models) means the additional local training burden is negligible, making the approach feasible for resource-constrained healthcare institutions. However, the method has not been validated on tasks like medical object detection, 3D reconstruction, or image registration, and the baselines exclude the most recent heterogeneous-FL methods (pFedES, FedLoRA) that the authors acknowledge in related work. An open question is whether the codebook-style disentanglement generalizes to very large client populations or highly divergent architectures beyond the ResNet family and standard segmentation backbones tested here.
