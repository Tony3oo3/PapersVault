## Problem

Transformer-based video encoders are limited to short temporal contexts due to quadratic attention complexity, and existing extensions (masking, attention approximations, parametric memory modules) introduce substantial architectural and training complexity. This paper asks whether such modifications are truly necessary, or whether standard pretrained video transformers can be re-purposed for long-context understanding with minimal changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240101/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes long video clips (up to 256 frames at 256×256 resolution, sampled at 1–4 FPS depending on the benchmark) and a text caption or multiple-choice question. Videos are split into fixed 16-frame segments processed sequentially in a streaming fashion, with each segment producing a token representation that is concatenated into the final video embedding. At inference, this video embedding is compared via cosine similarity against text embeddings of candidate captions or answer choices to select the best match in a zero-shot CLIP-style paradigm.

## Method

MC-ViT repurposes a standard pretrained ViViT encoder by replacing full joint space-time attention with segment-level self-attention plus cross-attention over a non-parametrically consolidated memory bank. After processing each 16-frame segment, its activations are compressed into K representative tokens (default K=128 or 512) using one of three non-parametric consolidation schemes: random selection, greedy coreset selection, or k-means clustering (the default). These consolidated tokens are appended to a growing memory that subsequent segments attend to via multi-head cross-attention, bounding computational complexity to the segment length rather than total video length. The entire system requires no new learnable parameters beyond the original ViViT weights and interpolated positional embeddings, and is trained with a short contrastive fine-tuning schedule (5k–30k steps) on top of the pretrained encoder, optionally with LoRA adapters for smaller datasets.

## Results

On long video question answering, MC-ViT-L (424M params, 128+ frames) achieves 62.6% on EgoSchema (subset), 44.4% on EgoSchema (full), 48.1% on Perception Test, and 65.0% on Next-QA, outperforming all public baselines with up to 10× more parameters. On fine-grained action recognition, MC-ViT-L (313M) reaches 91.0% top-1 on Diving48 without spatial crops or bounding boxes, surpassing AIM ViT-L (90.6%) which requires 3× spatial crops. On EgoSchema's visual-only subset (questions not answerable by a blind language model), MC-ViT-L achieves 47.6%, exceeding GPT-4V's 32.5% despite using ~1000× fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MC-ViT decisively outperforms all public baselines on EgoSchema: MC-ViT-L (62.6% subset, 44.4% full) surpasses the next-best LongViTi (56.8%, 33.3%) by 5.8 and 11.1 points respectively, and the 5B-parameter SeViLA Localizer+ShortViTi (49.6%, 31.3%) by 13 and 13 points. On Perception Test, MC-ViT-L (48.1%) beats Flamingo (43.6%) and SeViLA (46.2%) despite using 7–20× fewer parameters. On Diving48, MC-ViT-L (91.0% top-1) exceeds AIM ViT-L (90.6%) without requiring spatial crops or bounding boxes, and MC-ViT-B (89.7%) surpasses ORViT (88.0%) which uses both spatial crops and bounding box information. The one clear loss is on Next-QA, where SeViLA (73.8%) substantially outperforms MC-ViT-L (65.0%), which the authors attribute to Next-QA relying heavily on language-only reasoning rather than long-context video understanding. Against proprietary models, MC-ViT-L is competitive with GPT-4V on EgoSchema (62.6% vs 63.5% raw subset) and surpasses it on the visual-only subset (47.6% vs 32.5%), while achieving 5% improvements over Bard+PALI on both EgoSchema and Perception Test.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ViViT (Arnab et al., 2021) as its base architecture and on Transformer-XL (Dai et al., 2019) for the streaming cross-attention mechanism that lets current segments attend to past activations. The key conceptual departure is from parametric memory-compression approaches (MeMViT, Compressive Transformers, ∞-former) that require additional trainable modules: here, the authors show that simple non-parametric consolidation (k-means, coreset, or even random selection) of past segment activations, combined with short fine-tuning of an off-the-shelf pretrained video transformer, is sufficient for strong long-context modeling. This positions MC-ViT as an orthogonal, drop-in extension of standard video ViTs rather than a new architecture, contrasting with modular systems that pair frame-level encoders with billion-scale LLM decoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that long-context video understanding does not require architectural innovation or orders-of-magnitude more parameters: a 200M-parameter model fine-tuned for a few thousand steps matches or beats 5B-parameter systems and is competitive with GPT-4V on EgoSchema. This opens a path to equipping existing video backbones with long-range temporal reasoning at minimal cost, and the consolidated representations could be plugged into LLMs to augment their temporal grounding. Open questions include whether richer consolidation strategies inspired by cognitive science (episodic vs. semantic memory, efficient coding) can further improve performance, and whether the approach transfers cleanly to other sequential modalities like audio or language.
