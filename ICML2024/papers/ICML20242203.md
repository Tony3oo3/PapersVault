## Problem

Existing zero-shot object navigation methods suffer from two critical limitations: map-based planners select waypoints at fixed intervals or map-growth thresholds, missing high-information decision points like intersections where a single additional step could reveal large unseen areas; and scene representations for LLM-based planning are restricted to either frontier-point neighborhoods or depth-camera range, failing to provide the holistic, human-like scene context (both along-path and egocentric) needed for effective commonsense reasoning.

## Input / Output

The agent receives sequential RGB-D frames (480×640, 79° horizontal FoV) and a pose sensor reading at each step, along with a target object category from a novel set (e.g., 'bed', 'chair', 'sofa'). It outputs discrete actions from {Stop, MoveForward, TurnLeft, TurnRight, LookUp, LookDown} with 0.25 m translation and 30° rotation increments. Internally, the system maintains a (K+2)×M×M 2D semantic map (K object categories + obstacle + explored channels) and selects mid-term goal waypoints exclusively at Reduced Voronoi Graph nodes, where the agent performs a 12-step full rotation to capture panoramic RGB views before each global decision.

## Method

VoroNav extracts a Reduced Voronoi Graph (RVG) from a real-time 2D semantic map by skeletonizing the unoccupied region, yielding nodes at intersections and endpoints that serve as informative decision waypoints. At each RVG node, the Global Decision Module generates two complementary scene descriptions per neighbor node: (1) path descriptions that textualize objects and their coordinates along the shortest GVD path to exploratory nodes (refined by GPT-3.5 into coherent prose), and (2) farsight descriptions produced by BLIP captioning of the panoramic RGB image whose line-of-sight best aligns with the ray toward that neighbor. These fused prompts feed GPT-3.5 to produce a per-node semantic probability, which is combined with a hierarchical reward (exploration weight 2 > efficiency weight 1 > semantic weight 0–1) to select the next mid-term goal. Local motion is planned via the Fast Marching Method on the obstacle map, and the entire pipeline is training-free, relying on Grounded-SAM for open-vocabulary segmentation and requiring no task-specific policy learning.

## Results

On HM3D, VoroNav achieves 42.0% Success and 26.0% SPL, outperforming the best competitor ESC (39.2% Success, 22.3% SPL) by +2.8% Success and +3.7% SPL. On HSSD, VoroNav reaches 41.0% Success and 23.2% SPL, surpassing the best competitor L3MVN (38.4% Success, 19.4% SPL) by +2.6% Success and +3.8% SPL. The method is training-free and uses GPT-3.5 for decision-making at sparse Voronoi graph nodes, with a global decision step averaging ~10.2 seconds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VoroNav outperforms all four compared baselines on every metric where a direct comparison is available. Against Frontier (Yamauchi, 1997), VoroNav gains +8.3% Success and +10.7% SPL on HM3D, and +5.0% Success and +5.5% SPL on HSSD, demonstrating that Voronoi-based waypoint selection at intersections is substantially more effective than nearest-frontier exploration. Against L3MVN (Yu et al., 2023b), VoroNav improves by +6.8% Success and +9.5% SPL on HM3D, and +2.6% Success and +3.8% SPL on HSSD, showing that the combined path-and-farsight prompting strategy yields better LLM reasoning than frontier-cluster-based descriptions. Against Pixel-Nav (Cai et al., 2023), which requires training and uses GPT-4, VoroNav still leads by +4.1% Success and +5.5% SPL on HM3D while remaining training-free. Against ESC (Zhou et al., 2023), the closest semantic competitor using the same GPT-3.5 backbone, VoroNav achieves +2.8% Success and +3.7% SPL on HM3D, attributable to its Voronoi-based decision points providing richer observations at each planning step.

## Relation to Prior Work

VoroNav builds on the classical Generalized Voronoi Diagram (GVD) framework for topological robot planning (Choset & Burdick, 1995) and repurposes it as a decision-making scaffold for zero-shot object navigation, departing from the standard frontier-based exploration paradigm (Yamauchi, 1997) that dominates prior ZSON work. In the LLM-guided navigation line (ESC, L3MVN, Pixel-Nav), prior methods feed LLMs observations only at frontier points or at fixed step intervals; VoroNav instead constructs a Reduced Voronoi Graph and generates fused path-and-farsight textual descriptions at Voronoi intersections, mimicking how humans describe scenes along traversable routes. The semantic mapping pipeline (Grounded-SAM + depth) and local planning (Fast Marching Method) are inherited from existing modular navigation stacks, while the hierarchical reward mechanism (exploration > efficiency > semantics) is a novel integration layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that making navigation decisions at topologically informative intersections (Voronoi nodes) rather than at arbitrary fixed intervals yields better LLM reasoning and lower collision rates—offers a practical design principle for modular navigation systems. However, the approach depends on cloud LLM APIs (GPT-3.5) with ~10-second global decision latency, limiting real-time deployment, and the 2D semantic map is bounded by depth-camera range, leaving a gap for environments with tall or occluded structures. Open questions include whether the Voronoi-intersection strategy generalizes to dynamic or multi-robot settings, whether the prompting strategy transfers to smaller local LLMs, and whether the modest SOTA gains (+2.6–3.8%) persist as base detectors and LLMs improve.
