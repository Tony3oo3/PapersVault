## Problem

Despite a decade of proposed knowledge graph representation learning (KGRL) methods, almost no theoretical generalization bounds have been derived for them. Existing PAC-Bayesian analyses for GNNs assume standard single-relation graphs and i.i.d. sampling, which do not apply to the multi-relational, finite, transductive setting of knowledge graph completion. This paper provides the first PAC-Bayesian generalization bounds specifically for KGRL methods.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a transductive knowledge graph where all entities and relations are observed during training, with entity and relation feature matrices (typically one-hot encoded) and a training triplet set sampled without replacement from the finite full triplet set. The model outputs a two-dimensional score vector per triplet (scores for labels 0 and 1) used for triplet classification, and the theoretical contribution produces a PAC-Bayesian upper bound on the gap between expected loss over the full triplet set and empirical margin loss over the training set, parameterized by the KL divergence between posterior and prior distributions.

## Method

The paper introduces the ReED framework, a generic Relation-aware Encoder-Decoder that unifies at least 15 existing KGRL models. The RAMP encoder performs relation-specific message passing using per-relation graph diffusion matrices and learnable projection matrices, while two decoder variants (translational distance and semantic matching) compute triplet scores via distance or similarity formulations. The key theoretical contribution is a transductive PAC-Bayesian bound for deterministic triplet classifiers, derived by adding Gaussian perturbations to fixed parameters and extending Bégin et al.'s transductive result. The proof requires two-step recursion unrolling to handle entity-relation interactions across multiple relations, yielding bounds that depend on the maximum infinity norm of graph diffusion matrices (k_r), Frobenius norms of weight matrices (s), and encoder depth (L). Empirically, the paper validates that mean aggregation (reducing k_r), weight normalization (reducing s), and fewer layers (reducing L) all decrease actual generalization errors on FB15K237, CoDEx-M, and UMLS-43.

## Results

The paper's experiments validate the theoretical predictions of Corollary 4.6 by measuring actual generalization errors (the gap between expected and empirical losses) on FB15K237, CoDEx-M, and UMLS-43. Across all three datasets and both decoder types (RAMP+TD and RAMP+SM), the mean aggregator consistently yields lower generalization errors than the sum aggregator, confirming that reducing k_r tightens the bound. Generalization errors increase monotonically with both the Frobenius norm of weight matrices (s ∈ {10, 15, 20}) and the number of encoder layers (L ∈ {1, 2, 3}), matching the O(L·τ^L·s^L) scaling predicted by the theory. On FB15K237 with BERT-extracted features, generalization errors also increase with the representation dimension d (64 → 96 → 128), further corroborating the d·ln(N_w·d) term in the bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not benchmark ReED against other KGRL methods (e.g., R-GCN, CompGCN, RotatE) in a standard link-prediction or triplet-classification accuracy comparison. Instead, the empirical section performs controlled ablations within the ReED framework itself, varying one factor at a time (aggregator type, weight-matrix norm s, number of layers L, and representation dimension d) while holding the others fixed. The 'baselines' are thus different configurations of ReED: sum-aggregator vs. mean-aggregator, s=10 vs. s=15 vs. s=20, and L=1 vs. L=2 vs. L=3. In every comparison, the configuration predicted by Corollary 4.6 to have a tighter bound (mean aggregator, smaller s, fewer layers, smaller d) also exhibits a lower measured generalization error, confirming that the theoretical factors are not merely loose upper bounds but genuinely predictive of actual generalization behavior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PAC-Bayesian generalization bound framework from Liao et al. (2021) and Ju et al. (2023) — which analyzed GNNs on standard single-relation graphs with i.i.d. sampling — to the multi-relational, transductive setting of knowledge graph triplet classification. It also builds on the transductive PAC-Bayesian theory of Begin et al. (2014) by extending it from stochastic to deterministic classifiers via random perturbation arguments (following Neyshabur et al., 2018). The ReED framework unifies over 15 existing KGRL methods (R-GCN, CompGCN, RotatE, TransE, DistMult, ANALOGY, etc.) under a single encoder-decoder abstraction, enabling a single theoretical analysis to cover both GNN-based and shallow-architecture models simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical generalization analysis for KGRL, providing formal justification for widely-used but previously unexplained design choices: parameter sharing, weight normalization, and mean-over-sum aggregation. The practical guidance is clear — reducing parameter count, normalizing weights, and using mean aggregation all tighten the bound — but the bounds themselves are loose (exponential in the number of encoder layers L), limiting their quantitative predictive power. Key open questions include extending ReED to attention-based KGRL models (which the authors acknowledge it cannot currently handle) and investigating the trade-off between generalization and expressivity in knowledge graphs.
