## Problem

Existing spherical sliced Wasserstein methods (e.g., SSW) project spherical measures onto great circles, yielding 1D marginals on S¹ that require circular optimal transport with an additional binary search step. No prior approach maps spherical distributions to 1D marginals on the real line where OT is solvable in O(N log N), and the stereographic projection—a natural tool for such a mapping—severely distorts distances near the North Pole, making naive composition with OT unsuitable.

## Input / Output

The method takes two empirical distributions on S^d (each a set of M points on the unit sphere in R^{d+1}) and outputs a scalar p-Wasserstein-type distance. Internally, each point is stereographically projected to R^d, passed through an injective map h into R^{d'}, and then sliced along L random directions θ ∈ S^{d'-1} to produce 1D marginals on R. For the rotationally invariant variant (RI-S3W/ARI-S3W), the distance is further averaged over N_R random rotation matrices sampled from SO(d+1), with ARI-S3W amortizing rotation generation via a pre-sampled pool.

## Method

The S3W distance composes the stereographic projection φ: S^d \ {s_n} → R^d with an injective map h: R^d → R^{d'} and then applies the classic Radon transform (via the H-variant of the GRT, g(x,θ) = ⟨h(x),θ⟩) to obtain 1D marginals on R, enabling O(N log N) per-slice OT. To counteract the severe distance distortion of stereographic projection, the paper introduces an analytic nearly-isometric embedding h_1(x) = arccos((||x||²−1)/(||x||²+1)) · x/||x|| that bounds the embedded distance by 2π and approximates spherical arclength, plus a learnable variant h_NN that concatenates h_1 with a neural network ρ trained to minimize the squared error between embedded Euclidean distance and spherical arclength. The rotationally invariant extension RI-S3W averages S3W over the Haar measure on SO(d+1), and the amortized variant ARI-S3W subsamples from a fixed rotation pool to reduce the O(N_R d³) cost of generating rotations. Theoretical analysis proves S3W_H is a true metric (not just a pseudo-metric) on P_p(S^d \ {s_n}) and that RI-S3W_H is a metric on all of P_p(S^d).

## Results

On CIFAR-10 self-supervised learning, ARI-S3W(5) achieves 79.92%/75.07% linear-probe accuracy (encoded/projected on S⁹), matching or exceeding Wang & Isola (79.76%/74.57%) and SimCLR (79.69%/72.78%) at comparable runtime (~21.5 s/epoch). In the gradient flow experiment on S², ARI-S3W(30) attains NLL −5025.37 and log W₂ −4.37, outperforming SSW (−4974.50, −3.27) while running ~20× faster (42.9 s vs. 95.0 s). On Earth density estimation, ARI-S3W(50) achieves the best NLL across all three datasets (Quake 0.78, Flood 1.24, Fire 0.10), surpassing SSW (0.84, 1.26, 0.24) and the Stereo baseline (2.04, 1.85, 1.34).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SSW (Bonet et al., 2023), the proposed S3W variants consistently win on both accuracy and speed: in gradient flow, ARI-S3W(30) improves NLL by ~51 points and log W₂ by ~1.1 while being ~20× faster; in SSL, ARI-S3W(5) gains ~9.5% encoded accuracy over SSW (79.92% vs. 70.46%) at lower runtime (21.5 s vs. 33.1 s per epoch); on Earth density estimation, ARI-S3W(50) beats SSW on all three datasets (e.g., Fire NLL 0.10 vs. 0.24). Compared to standard sliced Wasserstein (SW, Rabin et al., 2012), S3W variants achieve higher SSL accuracy (79.92% vs. 74.45% encoded) and better Earth NLL (0.78 vs. 1.12 on Quake). Against Wang & Isola (2020) and SimCLR (Chen et al., 2020), ARI-S3W(5) matches or slightly exceeds both in SSL accuracy (79.92%/75.07% vs. 79.76%/74.57% and 79.69%/72.78%) at comparable or lower per-epoch cost. The Stereo baseline (Gemici et al., 2016) is substantially outperformed on Earth density estimation, with ARI-S3W(50) achieving NLL 0.78/1.24/0.10 versus Stereo's 2.04/1.85/1.34. In the runtime comparison (Figure 3), S3W is orders of magnitude faster than exact Wasserstein and Sinkhorn distances on S¹⁰⁰, confirming the computational advantage of the sliced formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sliced Wasserstein framework (Rabin et al., 2012; Kolouri et al., 2019a) to spherical measures by composing stereographic projection with the generalized Radon transform, departing from prior spherical sliced OT methods (Bonet et al., 2023a; Quellmalz et al., 2023) that project onto great circles and require the more expensive circular OT solver. The key conceptual shift is mapping spherical distributions to 1D marginals on the real line (via stereographic projection + an injective correction function) rather than on the unit circle, enabling the O(N log N) linear OT solver. The injective correction function h, inspired by the augmented sliced Wasserstein framework of Chen et al. (2022), addresses the severe distance distortion inherent in stereographic projection, and the rotationally invariant extension draws on Haar measure over SO(d+1).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The S3W family offers a practical speed advantage (up to 20× over SSW) with competitive or superior accuracy across gradient flows, self-supervised learning, autoencoders, and density estimation, making it a viable drop-in replacement for existing spherical sliced OT methods in large-scale applications. The amortized rotationally invariant variant (ARI-S3W) provides a useful memory-speed trade-off for high-dimensional settings. Open questions include extension to unbalanced/partial OT on the sphere, integration with recent projection-efficiency improvements (Deshpande et al., 2019; Nguyen et al., 2024), and whether the stereographic singularity (handled via an ε-cap) introduces systematic bias in high-dimensional regimes. The method's reliance on a fixed projection point (north pole) means it is not intrinsically rotationally invariant, and the RI extension adds a multiplicative cost in the number of rotations.
