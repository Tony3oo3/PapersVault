## Problem

Conventional RL fine-tuning of language models using PPO with a KL-divergence penalty is vulnerable to text degeneration, where the model stops generating natural texts after optimization. The KL penalty coefficient β is highly sensitive, requiring expensive hyperparameter search that is especially problematic for large-scale models, and even with tuning, one objective (task reward or naturalness) tends to dominate, causing either reward hacking or insufficient task improvement.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (partial review, toxic sentence prefix, or concept set), a frozen reference policy (pre-trained LM such as GPT-2, GPT-J-6B, or T5), and a scalar reward function (sentiment classifier, toxicity classifier, or METEOR score) as inputs, and produces a fine-tuned policy that generates text continuations, detoxified completions, or commonsense sentences. The algorithm operates at the sentence level rather than word level: it generates full trajectories, computes per-token advantages via GAE, and then applies KL-masking and advantage truncation per token before the policy update. No additional hyperparameters beyond standard RL settings (learning rate, discount factor, GAE λ) are required.

## Method

DfPO reformulates policy gradient optimization as separate likelihood maximization and minimization, each gated by a KL-mask that filters out tokens whose update would push the policy away from the reference policy. Specifically, positive KL-masking (M_KL⁺ = 1 when log π₀ > log π_θ) selects tokens whose likelihood can be safely increased, while negative KL-masking (M_KL⁻ = 1 when log π₀ < log π_θ) selects tokens whose likelihood can be safely decreased. The truncated advantage function then applies only the positive part of the advantage to the maximization term and only the negative part to the minimization term, ensuring that each update simultaneously improves task score and preserves naturalness. Theoretically, DfPO is shown to be equivalent to PPO's clipped surrogate objective with ε=0, but using the reference policy π₀ in the log-ratio instead of the previous policy π_old, which eliminates the need for an explicit KL penalty term and its associated hyperparameter β. This design means the model selection is unambiguous: simply pick the checkpoint with the best validation task score, since naturalness is structurally preserved by the masking mechanism.

## Results

On IMDB text continuation (GPT-2), DfPO achieves a sentiment score of 0.620 with perplexity 32.56 (cold start) and 0.663 with perplexity 35.80 (warm start from supervised fine-tuning), outperforming PPO (0.602/33.82 cold, 0.626/35.05 warm) and NLPO (0.611/33.83 cold, 0.620/34.82 warm) without any hyperparameter search. On text detoxification (GPT-J-6B), DfPO reaches a non-toxicity score of 4.343 while preserving perplexity at 39.09, whereas PPO at a comparable non-toxicity level degrades perplexity to 52.61. On CommonGen (T5), DfPO+Sup achieves the best Rouge-1 (0.555), Rouge-2 (0.221), METEOR (0.336), and BLEU (0.080) among all methods while maintaining competitive BertScore (0.929) and SPICE (0.445).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PPO, DfPO consistently wins on both task score and naturalness: cold-start DfPO achieves 0.620 sentiment with 32.56 perplexity versus PPO's 0.602/33.82, and warm-start DfPO+Sup reaches 0.663/35.80 versus PPO+Sup's 0.626/35.05. Against NLPO, DfPO similarly dominates (0.620 vs 0.611 cold; 0.663 vs 0.620 warm) with lower perplexity in both settings. Against DPO (offline), DfPO+Sup matches the best DPO variant on sentiment (0.663 vs 0.661) while achieving substantially lower perplexity (35.80 vs 37.08), and decisively outperforms the perplexity-matched DPO variant on sentiment (0.663 vs 0.551). A critical practical advantage is that DfPO requires no hyperparameter search for the KL coefficient β, whereas PPO, NLPO, and DPO all exhibit extreme sensitivity to this hyperparameter, with small changes in β causing large swings in both sentiment and perplexity. On text detoxification, DfPO matches DPO(N) on non-toxicity (4.343 vs 4.343) while PPO(N) at a similar non-toxicity level suffers severe perplexity degradation (52.61 vs 39.09).

## Relation to Prior Work

DfPO builds directly on PPO's clipped surrogate objective, showing in Appendix A that DfPO is mathematically equivalent to PPO's clipping mechanism applied to the reference policy π₀ (rather than the old policy π_old) with ε=0, replacing the separate KL-divergence reward penalty with a binary mask on the log-ratio. This departs from the standard RLHF formulation (Ouyang et al., 2022; Ziegler et al., 2019) where KL regularization is added as a scalar penalty with a sensitive coefficient β, and from NLPO (Ramamurthy et al., 2023) which uses action masking to reduce the combinatorial token space. The conceptual shift is from *penalizing* deviation from the reference policy to *excluding* gradient contributions that would cause such deviation, framing policy optimization as stable likelihood max/minimization over a filtered subset of samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of the KL-penalty hyperparameter β is practically significant for RLHF pipelines, where tuning β is costly (especially for large models) and creates ambiguity in model selection. DfPO's simplicity (two indicator masks plus advantage truncation) makes it easy to drop into existing RL4LMs-style training loops. However, the evaluation is limited to relatively small-scale generative tasks (GPT-2 117M, T5 220M, GPT-J 6B) with sentence-level rewards, and the authors themselves note that applying DfPO to full RLHF/RLAIF with modern LLMs and sLLMs remains future work. An open question is whether the binary masking (ε=0) is optimal or whether a soft version with a small ε might capture more gradient signal without reintroducing sensitivity.
