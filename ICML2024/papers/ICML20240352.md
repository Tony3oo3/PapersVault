## Problem

While neural decoders have been successfully trained for fixed error correction codes, the joint design of the code itself and its decoder has remained intractable due to the extreme non-differentiability of binary linear operations over GF(2). This paper is the first to demonstrate that binary linear block codes can be directly optimized end-to-end alongside a neural decoder, producing codes that outperform their conventional counterparts under both neural and classical decoders.

## Input / Output

The decoder takes a noisy BPSK channel output y ∈ ℝⁿ and preprocesses it into a codeword-invariant representation h(y) = [|y|, H_Ω·bin(y)] ∈ ℝ^{2n−k} by concatenating magnitudes with the syndrome computed from the (trainable) parity-check matrix. The output is a soft denoising estimate of the multiplicative noise, from which the hard-decoded binary codeword is recovered via bin(sign(f_θ(h(y))·y)). The trainable code parameter Ω ∈ ℝ^{k×(n−k)} is itself an output of the optimization, defining the generator and parity-check matrices in standard form.

## Method

The code is parameterized in standard form G=[I_k, P] with P=bin(Ω), where Ω is a real-valued trainable matrix. Two key differentiability tricks enable gradient flow through GF(2) operations: (i) the binarization function uses a straight-through estimator with a thresholded gradient, and (ii) the XOR operation is replaced by a multilinear product in bipolar space via ξ(u⊕v)=ξ(u)ξ(v). The decoder is a Transformer (DC-ECCT) that is code-, length-, and rate-invariant by design, with a novel differentiable masking mechanism: the Tanner graph two-step connectivity matrix g(H_Ω) is passed through a learned scalar mapping ψ_γ to produce soft attention masks, allowing gradients to backpropagate from the decoder loss through the self-attention layers to the code parameters. Training uses BCE loss with the all-ones message m=𝟙_k (critical for non-vanishing gradients) and Adam with cosine decay, jointly optimizing Ω and decoder weights θ.

## Results

The paper reports -ln(BER) (higher is better) across six linear block codes at Eb/N0 = 4, 5, 6 dB. The proposed E2E DC-ECCT (end-to-end trained code + decoder) achieves the best -ln(BER) on most codes: e.g., 11.71 on POLAR(32,11) at 6 dB (vs. 10.19 for ECCT, 10.60 for SCL-L32, 5.70 for BP-L50), 12.84 on BCH(31,16) at 6 dB (vs. 10.63 for ECCT), and 12.33 on LDPC(49,24) at 6 dB (vs. 7.21 for ECCT). Even with a fixed (untrained) code, DC-ECCT already surpasses the SOTA ECCT on most codes, confirming the decoder architecture improvement is independent of code optimization. On the larger POLAR(64,32), SCL with list length 32 (13.98) slightly edges out E2E DC-ECCT (13.09), suggesting the code optimization converges to a suboptimal local minimum for this setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Belief Propagation (Pearl, 1988), E2E DC-ECCT achieves dramatic gains: on LDPC(49,24) at 6 dB it reaches 12.33 vs. BP's 5.34 (a 2.3× improvement in -ln(BER)), and on BCH(31,16) it scores 12.84 vs. 6.37. Against SCL (Tal & Vardy, 2015) with list length 32, E2E DC-ECCT wins on POLAR(32,11) (11.71 vs. 10.60) but loses on POLAR(64,32) (13.09 vs. 13.98), where SCL is near ML-optimal for the original polar construction. Against the SOTA ECCT neural decoder (Choukroun & Wolf, 2022), E2E DC-ECCT outperforms on all six codes at 6 dB, with the largest margin on LDPC(49,24) (+5.12) and BCH(31,16) (+2.21). Notably, even DC-ECCT with a fixed (untrained) code already beats ECCT on 5 of 6 codes, isolating the decoder architecture improvement from the code optimization gain. The paper further shows (Table 2) that the learned codes also improve BP decoding performance by large margins (e.g., 10.24 vs. 9.27 for BCH(31,16) at 6 dB), demonstrating the codes are universally better, not merely tailored to the neural decoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ECCT (Choukroun & Wolf, 2022) and FECCT (Choukroun & Wolf, 2024b) Transformer-based neural decoder architectures by making the parity-check matrix itself a learnable parameter, enabling end-to-end joint optimization of code and decoder. Prior neural decoding work (Nachmani et al., 2016; Bennatan et al., 2018; Choukroun & Wolf, 2022) treated the code as fixed and only trained the decoder, while earlier attempts at learned codes (O'Shea & Hoydis, 2017; Jiang et al., 2019b) operated in the continuous domain and were far from practical binary linear block codes. The key technical departure is a differentiable formulation over GF(2) using straight-through binarization, polarization-based XOR equivalence, and a learned soft mask derived from the Tanner graph connectivity, which together permit gradient flow from the decoder loss back to the code parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is that codes optimized jointly with a neural decoder also outperform their conventional counterparts under classical decoders (BP, ML), suggesting the learned codes are genuinely better rather than merely overfit to a specific decoder. This opens the possibility of using the framework to discover new code families that could inform future communication standards. However, the optimization is highly non-convex with no theoretical guarantees, results are limited to short codes (n ≤ 64), and the paper acknowledges that larger architectures and longer training could further improve performance. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer codes, whether the learned codes have interpretable structure, and whether the cross-decoder generalization holds at larger scales.
