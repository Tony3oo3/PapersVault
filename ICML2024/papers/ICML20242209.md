## Problem

Existing graph neural network approaches for fluid dynamics modeling assume training and test data share the same distribution, yet real-world fluid systems encounter varying environmental parameters (heat release rate, ventilation speed, viscosity) that induce distribution shifts and degrade OOD forecasting performance. Additionally, no large-scale dataset or systematic benchmark existed to evaluate OOD generalization in fluid dynamics, hindering algorithmic research in this underexplored area.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242209/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes historical spatio-temporal trajectories defined on geometric graphs with irregular grid structures (ranging from 2,048 to 22,225 nodes depending on the scenario) along with implicit environment parameters (e.g., HRR and ventilation speed for fire scenarios, viscosity for Navier-Stokes). Each node carries multiple physical variables (temperature, velocity components, flue gas concentration), and the model predicts future trajectories over the same graph topology. The OOD evaluation protocol trains on a subset of environments (e.g., 20 of 25 for Prometheus-P) and tests on held-out unseen environments, with prediction horizons up to 120 steps.

## Method

DGODE learns disentangled node and environment representations, then models their joint evolution via a coupled graph ODE. For feature extraction, a temporal GNN captures spatio-temporal relationships while an FFT-based frequency network extracts complementary periodic semantics; both are concatenated into node features, and a parallel backbone produces environment features. To mitigate distribution shift, environment features are discretized via vector quantization into a learned codebook, and adversarial mutual information minimization (using a classifier to predict the quantized environment from node features) enforces invariance of node representations across environments. The environment-aware graph ODE simultaneously evolves node states (driven by neighboring nodes and the environment state) and environment states (driven by all nodes), effectively adding a virtual environment node connected to the entire graph. A stability regularization term perturbs the quantized environment with alternative codebook entries to minimize variance in node trajectories, and a theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) proves that omitting environment states yields inconsistent least-squares estimators even in the infinite-sample limit.

## Results

DGODE achieves the lowest MSE across all five benchmarks in both in-distribution (w/o OOD) and out-of-distribution (w/ OOD) settings. On the newly introduced Prometheus-T, DGODE reaches 0.0344 (w/o OOD) and 0.0359 (w/ OOD), improving by 16.91% and 21.27% over the second-best baseline (LSM). On Navier-Stokes, the gains are most dramatic: DGODE achieves 0.0805/0.0925 MSE versus 0.1535/0.1731 for the next-best method (LSM), a 47.56%/46.56% relative improvement. The OOD performance gap between DGODE and baselines widens under distribution shift, e.g., on WeatherBench-W DGODE's MSE is 0.1087 versus 0.1301 for SGODE (17.65% improvement).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGODE outperforms all 13 baselines on every benchmark in both in-distribution and OOD settings. The largest margins appear on Navier-Stokes, where DGODE (0.0805/0.0925) nearly halves the MSE of the next-best LSM (0.1535/0.1731), and on Prometheus-T, where it beats LSM (0.0414/0.0456) by ~17–21%. Among graph-ODE baselines, SGODE (0.0643/0.0723 on Prometheus-T) and CG-ODE (0.0499/0.0565 on Prometheus-P) are the closest competitors but still trail DGODE by 46% and 20% respectively. Operator-learning methods FNO and LSM are competitive on in-distribution tasks (e.g., FNO at 0.0447 on Prometheus-T) but degrade more sharply under OOD shift than DGODE. Vision-based backbones (U-Net, SwinT, Earth) and GNN-based spatio-temporal models (CLCRN, MGNT, EAGLE, DGCRN) consistently underperform, with EAGLE showing the worst OOD results (e.g., 0.5238 MSE on Navier-Stokes w/ OOD). The consistent pattern—DGODE's advantage widening under OOD—validates the mutual-information disentanglement and environment-aware ODE design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the coupled graph ODE framework (Huang et al., 2021) by introducing environment states as a virtual node connected to all graph nodes, enabling simultaneous modeling of node and environment evolution. It departs from prior fluid dynamics modeling (Pfaff et al., 2021; Shao et al., 2022; Sanchez-Gonzalez et al., 2020), which assumes in-distribution training and testing, by explicitly addressing OOD generalization through vector-quantized environment discretization and adversarial mutual information minimization for representation disentanglement. The feature extraction pipeline combines temporal GNNs with FFT-based frequency networks, drawing on complementary spatio-temporal and spectral views, while the ODE backbone inherits from the neural ODE lineage (Chen et al., 2018).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Prometheus dataset (4.8TB raw, 340GB compressed, 55 environments across tunnel and pool fires) fills a critical gap in large-scale OOD fluid dynamics benchmarks, enabling systematic evaluation of generalization under varying HRR and ventilation conditions. The theoretical inconsistency result (Theorem 3.1) provides a clean formal justification for why environment states must be explicitly modeled rather than marginalized out. Open questions include whether the VQ-based environment discretization scales to truly continuous or high-dimensional parameter spaces, whether the disentanglement objective generalizes beyond the relatively structured HRR/velocity grid used here, and how the approach performs on 3D or multi-physics scenarios beyond the 2D fire and weather settings tested.
