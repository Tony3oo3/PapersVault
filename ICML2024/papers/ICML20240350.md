## Problem

Learning-augmented algorithms for online bipartite matching should ideally be 1-consistent (achieve perfect matching with perfect advice) while remaining at least as robust as the best advice-free algorithm when advice is poor. This paper shows that under the adversarial arrival model, no such algorithm can be both 1-consistent and strictly better than 1/2-robust, and then designs an algorithm that achieves this ideal under the weaker random arrival model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240350/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes as input an online bipartite graph with n offline and n online vertices (edges revealed sequentially upon arrival) and an advice histogram ĉ ∈ ℤ^{2^n} specifying predicted counts of each online vertex type (type = subset of offline neighbors), with at most n non-zero entries summing to n. It produces an irrevocable online matching and a competitive ratio guarantee that smoothly interpolates between the advice-free baseline β (≈0.696 via RANKING) and 1, depending on the L1 distance between the true and predicted type distributions. The testing phase consumes only o(n) arrivals, so the cost of potential mismatches during testing vanishes asymptotically.

## Method

TESTANDMATCH is a meta-algorithm that first computes a maximum matching M̂ on the advice graph, then uses a sublinear number of initial arrivals to estimate L1(p*, q) — the distance between the true and predicted type-count distributions — via the Poissonized minimax L1 estimator of Jiao et al. (2018). If the estimated distance falls below a threshold 2(n̂/n − β) − ε, the algorithm continues mimicking M̂ (MIMIC) for all remaining arrivals; otherwise it switches to an advice-free BASELINE. The key technical contribution is leveraging distribution testing to keep the testing phase sublinear in n (s_{r,ε,δ}·√log(r+1) arrivals), ensuring that any wrong matches made during testing cost only o(1) in the competitive ratio. The algorithm also handles the domain-size challenge by projecting the L1 distance onto the advice support plus a dummy type, reducing the effective domain from 2^n to r+1 ≤ n+1, and simulates IID sampling from the random-order arrivals via a re-observation trick.

## Results

Under the adversarial arrival model, the paper proves an impossibility result: no learning-augmented algorithm can be both 1-consistent and strictly better than 1/2-robust (Theorem 3.1). Under the random arrival model, TESTANDMATCH achieves 1-consistency and β·(1−o(1))-robustness, where β = 0.696 is the RANKING competitive ratio, with the competitive ratio smoothly interpolating between β and 1 as the L₁ distance between the true and advised type histograms decreases. The testing phase requires only o(n) arrivals (sublinear in n), and preliminary experiments on n=2000 instances show degradation below the RANKING baseline is less than 0.1 even under significant advice corruption.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the RANKING algorithm (Karp et al., 1990), which achieves a competitive ratio of β = 0.696 under the Random Order arrival model and serves as the advice-free fallback in TESTANDMATCH. TESTANDMATCH strictly dominates RANKING in the sense that it matches RANKING's competitive ratio (up to an o(1) factor) when advice is poor, while approaching a competitive ratio of 1 when advice is perfect. Compared to LOMAR (Li et al., 2023), TESTANDMATCH achieves full 1-consistency while simultaneously maintaining ≈β-robustness without knowing the advice quality in advance; LOMAR can only be 1-consistent when ρ=0 (blindly following its RL model), at which point it has zero robustness guarantees. Against Jin & Ma (2022), which operates in a two-stage setting, TESTANDMATCH achieves the full consistency-robustness Pareto frontier (1, ≈β) in the single-stage random arrival model. In the preliminary experiments (Appendix F, n=2000), TESTANDMATCH with all practical extensions enabled maintains competitive ratios within 0.1 of RANKING even under 50%+ advice corruption, while approaching 1.0 when advice is near-perfect.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of online bipartite matching (Karp et al., 1990) and learning-augmented algorithms, building directly on the Jiao et al. (2018) minimax L1 distance estimator as its core testing subroutine. It departs from prior learning-augmented matching work (Aamand et al., 2022; Feng et al., 2021; Jin & Ma, 2022; Li et al., 2023) in two key ways: it uses type-count histograms as advice rather than degrees, edge weights, or proposed matchings, and it is the first to leverage distribution testing / property testing techniques to verify advice quality online. The resulting TESTANDMATCH meta-algorithm achieves 1-consistency and β-robustness simultaneously, a guarantee that prior methods like LOMAR cannot attain without knowing the advice quality a priori.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The impossibility result under adversarial arrivals (no algorithm can be 1-consistent and >1/2-robust) is a clean and important negative result that delineates the fundamental limits of advice in online matching. The positive result under random arrivals provides the first principled framework for using property testing in learning-augmented algorithms, with a smooth interpolation between advice-free and perfect-advice performance. Open questions include whether the o(1) robustness loss can be eliminated, whether the approach extends to weighted matching (requiring a different distance metric like earthmover distance), and whether L1 over type histograms is the ideal advice quality metric given its sensitivity to random edge perturbations.
