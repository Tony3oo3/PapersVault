## Problem

Graph transformers such as EGT are limited to 2nd-order (pairwise) interactions, meaning neighboring pairs can only exchange information through their shared junction node, creating a bottleneck that prevents the model from capturing geometric concepts like angles. This limits expressivity to the 1-GWL test and hinders accurate molecular geometry prediction from 2D graphs alone, where prior approaches either require expensive DFT-computed 3D coordinates or rely on less accurate RDKit initial structures.

## Input / Output

The distance predictor takes a 2D molecular graph (atom types, bond types, shortest-path hop encodings) and outputs a binned, clipped interatomic distance matrix (256 or 512 bins over 8 Å for molecules, 16 Å for OC20), which fully defines molecular geometry up to reflection. The task predictor consumes these predicted distances (encoded via RBF or Fourier features into edge channels) and produces scalar molecular properties (HOMO-LUMO gap, adsorption energy) or binary labels (MOLPCBA, LIT-PCBA). For OC20 IS2RE, the input is an initial DFT crystal-adsorbate structure cropped to ≤64 atoms; for TSP, the input is a K-NN graph with 2D point coordinates and pairwise distances as edge features.

## Method

TGT extends EGT by inserting a triplet interaction module between the pairwise attention and edge FFN blocks, enabling direct communication between adjacent pairs (i,j) and (j,k) within a 3-tuple (i,j,k) while also incorporating the third pair (i,k) via bias and gating terms. Two variants are proposed: triplet attention (TGT-At), which computes full attention weights over neighboring pairs with O(N³) complexity, and triplet aggregation (TGT-Ag), which removes the query-key dot product to reduce complexity to ~O(N²·³⁷) via matrix multiplication of O(N²) weight and value matrices. Training follows a three-stage procedure: (1) train the distance predictor on 2D graphs with cross-entropy loss over binned distances, (2) pretrain the task predictor on ground-truth 3D structures corrupted by locally smooth noise (exponentially weighted neighbor displacements) with a secondary denoising objective, and (3) finetune the task predictor on predicted distances from the frozen distance predictor. Stochastic inference keeps dropout active during prediction, drawing ~10–50 samples per input for ensemble aggregation and uncertainty estimation, while novel regularization via triplet dropout (randomly zeroing triplet weights) and source dropout (masking entire attention columns to simulate node absence) improve robustness.

## Results

On PCQM4Mv2 (HOMO-LUMO gap), TGT-At achieves 67.1 meV validation MAE with RDKit coordinates and 68.6 meV without, outperforming the previous SOTA UniMol+ (69.3/70.5 meV) by 2.2 meV. On OC20 IS2RE (adsorption energy), TGT-At achieves 403.0 meV MAE and 8.82% EwT on validation, beating UniMol+ (408.8 meV, 8.61% EwT) while using significantly less compute (32 vs. 112 A100 GPU-days). Transfer learning from PCQM4Mv2 to QM9 yields SOTA on HOMO (9.9 meV), LUMO (9.7 meV), and HOMO-LUMO gap (17.4 meV), and the frozen distance predictor achieves SOTA on MOLPCBA (31.67% AP) and matches SOTA on LIT-PCBA (81.5% ROC-AUC). On TSP, TGT-Agx4 reaches 87.1% F1 at the 500K parameter budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TGT-At outperforms the previous SOTA UniMol+ by 2.2 meV on PCQM4Mv2 validation MAE (67.1 vs. 69.3), and notably achieves 68.6 meV without any RDKit input, still beating UniMol+ which requires RDKit coordinates. On OC20 IS2RE, TGT-At (403.0 meV, 8.82% EwT) surpasses UniMol+ (408.8 meV, 8.61% EwT) and all geometric GNN baselines (SchNet 666.0, DimeNet++ 621.7, SphereNet 602.4, EquiFormer 441.0 meV). On QM9 transfer learning, TGT-Ag achieves SOTA on HOMO (9.9 vs. 15.0 for Equiformer), LUMO (9.7 vs. 14.0), and HOMO-LUMO gap (17.4 vs. 27.4 for Transformer-M), outperforming all 18 compared methods on these three tasks. For molecular property prediction, the frozen TGT-At distance predictor enables TGT-Ag to reach 31.67% AP on MOLPCBA, beating the SOTA pretrained Graphormer (31.40%) and EGT+RDKit (31.09%), while on LIT-PCBA, EGT+TGT-At-DP matches the SOTA GEM-2 pretrained (81.5% ROC-AUC). On TSP, TGT-Agx4 (87.1% F1 at 500K params) outperforms EGT (85.3%), ARGNP (85.5%), and Graphormer (69.8%), demonstrating the generality of triplet interactions beyond molecular graphs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Edge-augmented Graph Transformer (EGT) by introducing direct pair-to-pair communication via triplet (3rd-order) interactions, addressing the bottleneck where neighboring pairs in EGT can only exchange information through their common node. It positions itself against prior 3rd-order mechanisms—axial attention (GEM-2) and triangular update (AlphaFold 2/UniMol+)—arguing that its triplet attention and aggregation are strictly more expressive while being more efficient than axial attention. Conceptually, it departs from geometric GNNs (GemNet, SphereNet) and iterative refinement approaches (UniMol+) by learning to predict interatomic distances directly from 2D graphs, eliminating the need for initial 3D coordinates or expensive DFT simulations at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TGT architecture achieves SOTA on multiple challenging benchmarks (PCQM4Mv2, OC20 IS2RE, QM9, MOLPCBA, LIT-PCBA) and demonstrates that 3rd-order interactions can replace explicit geometric input features, enabling a fully neural inference pipeline. The pretrained distance predictor serves as a transferable pairwise feature extractor for new molecular tasks, and stochastic inference provides practical uncertainty estimation for drug discovery and material design. Key limitations include the O(N^3) (or O(N^2.37) for aggregation) computational complexity of triplet interactions, which restricts scalability to very large molecules; the authors suggest sparse or low-rank interactions as a path forward. Open questions include whether the approach extends to molecule/conformation generation and whether the complexity overhead can be reduced without sacrificing the geometric expressivity gains.
