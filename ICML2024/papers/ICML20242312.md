## Problem

Equivariant neural networks require the symmetry group to be known a priori, and existing automatic symmetry discovery methods (e.g., LieGAN) are restricted to linear group actions in the observation space. Real-world data such as high-dimensional dynamical systems and 2D renderings of 3D objects exhibit symmetries that are inherently nonlinear in the observation space, making them inaccessible to these linear-only methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242312/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes consecutive state pairs (x_t, x_{t+1}) concatenated into a single vector v, where x can range from 10^4-dimensional grid observations (reaction-diffusion) to 2D images of 3D objects or 1D signals. It produces a Lie algebra basis {L_i ∈ R^{k×k}} defining the discovered symmetry group, latent representations z = φ(v) in a user-specified dimension k, and the full nonlinear group action π'(g,·) = ψ∘π(g)∘φ. Downstream, the fixed encoder/decoder pair supports equation discovery via SINDy and iterative long-term forecasting in the latent space.

## Method

LaLiGAN decomposes any nonlinear group action as π'(g,·) = ψ∘π(g)∘φ, where φ and ψ are neural-network encoder/decoder and π(g) is a linear representation in a latent space Z = R^k. A GAN-based symmetry generator learns the Lie algebra basis {L_i} and samples group elements via π(g) = exp(Σ w_i L_i), while a discriminator enforces invariance of the latent data distribution under these transformations. Training jointly optimizes a reconstruction loss (making φ the right-inverse of ψ) and an adversarial loss (making the latent manifold invariant under G), with orthogonal parametrization in the encoder to prevent dimensional collapse and zero-mean normalization to center the latent space. A universal approximation theorem guarantees that this decomposition can express any smooth, free, proper action of a compact Lie group with simply connected orbit space, using fixed-width one-hidden-layer networks.

## Results

On three dynamical systems (reaction-diffusion, Lotka-Volterra, nonlinear pendulum), LaLiGAN achieves lower equivariance error and discriminator logit invariance error than linear LieGAN across all settings. For the Lotka-Volterra system, LaLiGAN reduces equivariance error from 8.44e-2 to 3.00e-2 and logit invariance error from 4.05e-1 to 5.21e-3. In equation discovery on the 3D latent space of the reaction-diffusion system, LaLiGAN+SINDy recovers purely linear governing equations, while SINDyAE alone yields equations with multiple second-order polynomial terms. For long-term forecasting, the equation discovered in the LaLiGAN latent space outperforms both SINDyAE and Neural ODE in relative MSE over 600 timesteps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaLiGAN consistently outperforms linear LieGAN (Yang et al., 2023) on all three dynamical systems: on Lotka-Volterra it reduces equivariance error by ~2.8× (3.00e-2 vs 8.44e-2) and logit invariance error by ~78× (5.21e-3 vs 4.05e-1); on the pendulum it halves equivariance error (4.01e-3 vs 6.30e-3) and reduces logit invariance error by ~4× (5.33e-3 vs 2.11e-2). For equation discovery, LaLiGAN+SINDy produces purely linear governing equations in the 3D latent space of the reaction-diffusion system, whereas SINDyAE (Champion et al., 2019) alone yields equations with multiple second-order terms (e.g., z₁², z₁z₂, z₂z₃), and the SINDyAE latent space lacks the circular symmetry structure. In long-term forecasting, the LaLiGAN-discovered equation achieves lower relative MSE than both SINDyAE and Neural ODE (Chen et al., 2018) over 600 timesteps, with the Neural ODE baseline diverging to NaN on the Lotka-Volterra system after ~1000 steps. Direct SINDy in the observation space (Brunton et al., 2016) recovers near-exact equations for the low-dimensional pendulum and Lotka-Volterra systems, but LaLiGAN+SINDy still achieves better long-term prediction accuracy due to the symmetry-enforced linear latent dynamics reducing error accumulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaLiGAN directly extends LieGAN (Yang et al., 2023), which discovers linear symmetries of continuous Lie groups via adversarial training, by wrapping the symmetry generator in an autoencoder that maps observations to a latent space where group actions become linear. This positions the work at the intersection of two prior threads: automatic symmetry discovery (L-conv, LieGG, LieGAN) which was restricted to linear group actions in the observation space, and latent-space equation discovery (SINDy Autoencoder, Champion et al., 2019) which learned coordinate transformations without enforcing symmetry structure. The paper also subsumes equivariant representation learning (Shakerinava et al., 2022; Quessard et al., 2020) as a special case where the group is known, while removing the requirement for group-element labels on each data point.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is demonstrated through equation discovery: enforcing latent symmetry yields simpler (linear) governing equations and substantially better long-term forecasting compared to SINDy Autoencoder, which produces complex nonlinear equations that diverge quickly. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.1) is restricted to compact Lie groups with simply connected orbit spaces, leaving open whether the framework extends to noncompact groups (e.g., translations, boosts) in a principled way, though empirical results on Lorentz symmetry suggest it may. A key open question is how to automatically select the latent dimension and Lie algebra dimensionality, which currently require manual tuning, and whether the method can discover multiple interacting symmetries or discrete symmetries inherent to objects themselves.
