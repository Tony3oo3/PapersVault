## Problem

Quantum speedups for Gibbs sampling were previously established only for logconcave distributions (Childs et al., 2022), leaving the more general non-logconcave case open. The central technical obstacle is that the unadjusted Langevin algorithm (ULA), which avoids costly Metropolis-Hastings acceptance steps, produces a non-reversible Markov chain whose transition density does not satisfy detailed balance, breaking the spectral connection between the discriminant matrix and the transition matrix that underpins all standard quantum walk frameworks.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume access to classical oracles with superposition: a full gradient oracle O_{∇f}, an evaluation oracle O_f, and optionally a stochastic gradient oracle O_{∇̃f} that returns mini-batch gradients of size B from a finite-sum decomposition f(x) = (1/N)Σf_k(x). The output is a quantum state whose measurement yields a sample from π(x) ∝ exp(−βf(x)) with total variation distance at most ε from the target, or a multiplicative (1±ε) estimate of the partition function Z = ∫exp(−f(x))dx. All oracles are classical circuits simulated on a quantum computer with constant overhead, so oracle-call speedups are not suppressed by implementation cost.

## Method

The paper constructs a quantum simulated annealing schedule of length Õ(√d) that starts from an efficiently preparable Gaussian state and gradually increases the variance σ_i² so that successive states have constant overlap, ending at a state with constant overlap with the target Gibbs state. For the reversible MALA chain, the quantum walk operator's phase gap is characterized via conductance analysis (yielding Δ ≥ ρ√(2η/β)), and fixed-point amplitude amplification drives the state along the schedule. For the non-reversible ULA chain, the key novelty is a perturbation analysis: the quantum ULA walk operator U is shown to be within Õ(ηd) in spectral norm of the quantum MALA walk operator U*, so the MALA chain serves as a reversible 'bridge' whose well-understood mixing controls the ULA error. The stochastic variant further uses matrix concentration to show that noisy mini-batch gradients introduce only bounded perturbation to the walk operators. Partition function estimation combines the sampling routine with the quantum product estimator of Cornelissen & Hamoudi (2023) to evaluate a telescoping product of expectations along the annealing schedule.

## Results

The paper establishes query complexity bounds for three quantum sampling algorithms under dissipative gradient assumptions (Assumptions 1.1–1.2). Quantum MALA (Theorem 4.1) achieves Õ(βdρ⁻¹c_LSI⁻¹) queries to full gradient and evaluation oracles, Quantum ULA (Theorem 4.2) achieves Õ(βd^{3/2}ε⁻¹ρ⁻¹c_LSI⁻¹) queries to the full gradient oracle, and Stochastic Quantum ULA (Theorem 4.3) achieves Õ(β²d^{3/2}ε⁻²ρ⁻²c_LSI⁻¹)² queries to the stochastic gradient oracle. For partition function estimation (Theorem 5.1), the algorithm requires Õ(d^{5/4}ε⁻¹ρ⁻¹c_LSI⁻¹) queries to full oracles or Õ(d^{11/4}ε⁻³ρ⁻²c_LSI⁻¹) queries to the stochastic gradient oracle to achieve (1±ε) multiplicative accuracy with probability ≥ 3/4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to classical MALA (Ma et al., 2019b) with Õ(d²) query complexity, Quantum MALA achieves a polynomial speedup in dimension, reducing the d-dependence from d² to d. Against classical ULA (Ma et al., 2019b; Vempala & Wibisono, 2019) with Õ(d/ε²) complexity, Quantum ULA trades a worse d-dependence (d^{3/2} vs. d) for a better precision dependence (ε⁻¹ vs. ε⁻²), yielding a net speedup when ε is small. For the stochastic setting, Stochastic Quantum ULA (Õ(d^{5/2}/ε²)) improves upon classical SGLD (Zou et al., 2021) with Õ(d⁴/ε²) by reducing the dimension dependence from d⁴ to d^{5/2} while matching the ε⁻² precision scaling. The paper notes that no classical algorithm is known for partition function estimation under the same dissipative gradient assumptions, so no direct classical baseline comparison is possible for Theorem 5.1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the quantum simulated annealing framework of Childs et al. (2022) from logconcave to non-logconcave distributions, and introduces the first quantum algorithm for non-reversible Markov chains (quantum ULA) by developing a perturbation analysis that bridges the non-reversible ULA chain to the reversible MALA chain. The classical perturbation techniques of Zou et al. (2021) and Xu et al. (2018) for SGLD analysis inspire the approach but cannot be directly transferred to the quantum setting due to fundamental differences between iterative classical sampling and linear-algebraic quantum state preparation. The partition function estimation builds on the quantum product estimator of Cornelissen & Hamoudi (2023) combined with the paper's own sampling algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key open question from Childs et al. (2022) about whether quantum speedups extend to non-logconcave distributions and non-reversible chains is answered affirmatively, with polynomial speedups in dimension and precision over classical baselines. However, the results are purely theoretical and require fault-tolerant quantum computers with high-fidelity phase estimation circuits, making near-term practical impact uncertain. Open directions include analyzing underdamped Langevin variants, working directly with continuous SPDEs in the quantum domain to bypass reversibility issues, and extending fast-forwarding techniques to non-reversible chains. The perturbation analysis for non-reversible quantum walks is positioned as a reusable tool for future quantum Monte Carlo algorithms.
