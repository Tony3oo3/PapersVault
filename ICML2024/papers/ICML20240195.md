## Problem

Prior dense associative memory models (Krotov & Hopfield, Ramsauer et al.) studied auto-association with continuous-valued patterns, while hetero-association work was largely restricted to binary patterns or simple 1D sequence structures. No unified framework existed for arbitrary mixtures of auto- and hetero-association over continuous-valued patterns with general graph topologies, nor was there a principled mechanism to control the spatial range of hetero-association. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing CDAM, which embeds memories in an arbitrary graph and uses anti-Hebbian modulation to tune association range.

## Input / Output

The model takes p continuous-valued memory vectors of length n (e.g., 784-dim FashionMNIST images, 2000-dim downsampled 320×240 video frames, or GloVe text embeddings), an arbitrary (possibly directed, weighted, multi-)graph M with p vertices defining semantic relationships, and scalar parameters a and h controlling auto- and hetero-association strengths. The network is initialized with a noise-corrupted pattern σ(0) = ξ^μ + cζ and iterated until convergence, producing a final state whose Pearson correlations with all stored patterns reveal the retrieved structure — ranging from a single pattern (auto-association) to multi-scale community activations (hetero-association) or near-zero activity (neutral quiescence).

## Method

CDAM extends the Krotov-Hopfield dense associative memory by replacing the single projection matrix Ξ with a composite matrix Q = aΞ + hΞMᵀ, where M = D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2} is the normalized adjacency matrix of the memory graph, thereby encoding auto-associative (a) and graph-structured hetero-associative (h) interactions simultaneously within a single SOFTMAX-based update rule. The paper proves that Hebbian mixtures (a,h > 0) cannot achieve pure pattern retrieval for non-isolated graph vertices, and identifies four dynamical regimes — auto-association, narrow hetero-association, wide hetero-association, and neutral quiescence — governed by the sign and magnitude of a relative to the graph's degree. Anti-Hebbian auto-association (a < 0) is the key mechanism that widens the hetero-associative range, enabling multi-scale community extraction, stable temporal sequence recall, and finite automaton simulation, with the constraint a + h = 1 maintaining unbiased excitatory-inhibitory balance in the thermodynamic limit.

## Results

CDAM exhibits four distinct dynamical modes controlled by the auto-association strength a and hetero-association strength h: pure auto-association (a=1, h=0), narrow hetero-association (a=0.5, h=0.5), wide hetero-association (a=−0.5, h=1.5), and neutral quiescence (a=−2.5, h=1). The model replicates the classical Miyashita (1988) monkey temporal cortex experiment with R²=0.997 correlation using a=−2.45, h=3.45 on a C₃₀ cycle graph. On FashionMNIST (n=784 neurons), the best auto-association performance occurs when a is close to 0 and h close to 1 (maintaining a+h=1 for E-I balance), while pure auto-association (a=1, h=0) exhibits catastrophic forgetting as memory load p increases. The range of hetero-association is controllably tunable from 0 to ~6 graph hops (one-way ANOVA, F=5.41, p=0.001).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a model-introduction paper without a traditional set of competing baselines; the primary quantitative validation is the replication of Miyashita (1988), where CDAM with anti-Hebbian auto-association (a=−2.45) and Hebbian hetero-association (h=3.45) matches the experimentally measured auto-correlation decay over 6 temporal steps with R²=0.997. On FashionMNIST, the paper implicitly compares different (a, h) settings: pure auto-association (a=1, h=0) shows catastrophic forgetting (sudden performance drops) as p increases, while balanced settings near a≈0, h≈1 achieve a graceful capacity-precision trade-off reminiscent of the MESH scaffold approach (Sharma et al., 2022). For video sequence recall, the paper demonstrates that without sufficient anti-Hebbian auto-association (a<0), sequence recall becomes stuck or lags due to auto-correlations between statistically similar frames; only a=−2, h=3 achieves skip-free recall. The finite automaton simulation (a=0, h=1) precisely reproduces the full family-tree transition structure from all 16 starting states, confirming that the hetero-associative component alone suffices for directed state transitions.

## Relation to Prior Work

CDAM unifies the dense associative memory framework of Krotov & Hopfield (2016) and its SOFTMAX reformulation (Ramsauer et al., 2021) with the hetero-associative constructions of Millidge et al. (2022) and Chaudhry et al. (2023), but departs from all of these by simultaneously modelling auto- and hetero-association through an arbitrary graph structure rather than restricting to 1D cycles or binary patterns. The anti-Hebbian learning mechanism draws on inhibitory modulation literature in neuroscience (Haga & Fukai, 2019; Burns et al., 2022) to provide a control knob over the range of hetero-association, a feature absent from prior dense associative memory models. The theoretical analysis of four dynamical modes (auto-association, narrow/wide hetero-association, neutral quiescence) generalises the energy landscape analysis of Lucibello & Mezard (2023) to the mixed-regime setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled framework for understanding Transformer attention as a potential mixture of auto- and hetero-associative dynamics, opening concrete interpretability questions about whether attention heads implicitly learn graph-like memory structures. The replication of Miyashita (1988) with R²=0.997 and the demonstration that anti-Hebbian auto-association is necessary for stable temporal sequence recall (otherwise the network gets stuck on statistically similar frames) offer testable predictions for neuroscience. Key open questions include: how to define meaningful 'capacity' for mixed auto/hetero-association regimes, how the memory graph M might be learned biologically, and whether the speculative Transformer connections can be made rigorous. The theoretical analysis is currently limited to k-regular graphs, and no comparison to competing hetero-associative methods is provided.
