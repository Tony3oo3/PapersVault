## Problem

Unsupervised federated learning with mixture models has seen practical success through algorithms like FedEM and FedGMM, yet no finite-sample theoretical guarantees exist for these methods. Moreover, existing federated EM algorithms assume homogeneous tasks and are vulnerable to adversarial contamination or outlier tasks, with no mechanism to adapt to unknown levels of task similarity. This paper bridges the gap by providing a comprehensive non-asymptotic analysis that characterizes exactly when and how federated EM outperforms local single-task learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes K local tasks, each containing n unlabeled observations from an R-component mixture distribution (R≥2), where a subset S of tasks share similar d-dimensional component parameters (similarity controlled by h) while a fraction ε of tasks may be arbitrarily contaminated. The output consists of per-task mixture proportions {w_r^(k)} and per-task component parameters {θ_r^(k)} for all non-outlier tasks. The framework applies to general Radon-Nikodym mixture densities, with explicit rates derived for Gaussian Mixture Models and Mixture of Regressions, and is evaluated on tabular data (Pen-Based Recognition) and grayscale images (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST reduced to 10 dimensions via t-SNE).

## Method

FedGrEM alternates local E-steps (computing posterior cluster probabilities), local M-steps (closed-form update for mixture proportions plus one-step gradient ascent for component parameters, avoiding expensive full EM maximization), and a central penalized aggregation step that shrinks local parameter estimates toward a common center via an ℓ2 penalty with adaptively decaying coefficient λ^[t]. The penalty schedule λ^[t] = κ'·λ^[t-1] + C√n[R_w(n)+R_θ(n)] is the key mechanism enabling both adaptivity to unknown task similarity h and robustness to a small fraction ε of adversarially contaminated tasks. The theoretical analysis decomposes the estimation error into five interpretable terms (iterative, aggregation, heterogeneous proportions, task heterogeneity, outlier cost) and employs a novel localization trick that adaptively shrinks the uniform convergence ball radius across iterations, replacing a dimension-dependent 'slow rate' with a smaller 'fast rate' for the heterogeneous mixture proportion term. This yields the explicit bound Õ(κ₀^T + R²√(d/nK) + R²√(1/n) + min{h, R²√(d/n)} + εR²√(d/n)), demonstrating that FedGrEM improves over single-task EM when tasks are sufficiently similar and contamination is limited.

## Results

On the Pen-Based Recognition dataset, FedGrEM achieves a mis-clustering error of 9.62% (0% contamination) and 9.04% (13.6% contamination), substantially outperforming Local-EM (13.30%), FedEM (15.75%), and TGMM (25.14%). On Fashion-MNIST, FedGrEM reaches 33.27–34.44% mis-clustering error across contamination levels, compared to 36.06–37.93% for FedEM and 36.14–38.20% for TGMM. In simulations, FedGrEM's estimation error for θ parameters remains stable as task heterogeneity h increases, while FedEM and TGMM degrade and become inferior to single-task EM once h/SNR exceeds ~36%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedGrEM consistently outperforms FedEM (Marfoq et al., 2021) across all three real datasets and all contamination levels: on Pen-Based Recognition, FedGrEM achieves 9.04–9.62% vs. FedEM's 15.75–18.52%; on Fashion-MNIST, 33.27–34.44% vs. 36.06–37.93%. The gap widens with increasing contamination, as FedEM's error grows from 15.75% to 18.52% on Pen-Based while FedGrEM stays near 9%. Against TGMM (Wang et al., 2021), FedGrEM wins by a large margin (9.04% vs. 25.14% on Pen-Based; 33.27% vs. 36.14% on Fashion-MNIST), with TGMM showing the highest variance. In the GMM and MoR simulations, FedGrEM also surpasses FedGMM (Wu et al., 2023) in θ-estimation error, and unlike FedEM and TGMM, FedGrEM's performance degrades gracefully as task heterogeneity h increases, eventually matching single-task EM rather than falling below it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the federated EM line initiated by Marfoq et al. (2021, FedEM) and Wu et al. (2023, FedGMM), which assumed homogeneous tasks and no adversarial contamination, by introducing FedGrEM—a gradient EM variant with a penalized ℓ₂ aggregation step that adapts to task heterogeneity and remains robust to a small fraction of outlier/adversarial tasks. The gradient EM component builds on the convergence theory of Yan et al. (2017) and Balakrishnan et al. (2017) for single-task settings, while the penalized central update draws on multi-task learning regularization (Evgeniou & Pontil, 2004; Li et al., 2021). The key conceptual departure is the first non-asymptotic finite-sample analysis for any federated EM algorithm, achieved through a novel adaptive localization trick that decouples the estimation errors of heterogeneous 1-D mixture proportions from d-D component parameters during iterations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a significant theoretical gap: prior federated EM algorithms (FedEM, FedGMM, Dieuleveut et al.) had no finite-sample guarantees, making it impossible to determine when federated learning actually helps over local single-task EM. The five-component error decomposition (iterative, aggregation, heterogeneous proportions, task heterogeneity, outlier tasks) gives practitioners clear conditions under which federated EM is beneficial. Open questions include extending the theory beyond GMMs and MoRs to general exponential families, handling the label permutation problem more algorithmically (currently requiring correct initialization up to permutation), and whether the localization trick generalizes to other iterative algorithms with entangled parameters. The practical impact is bounded to unsupervised federated settings with mixture models, a relatively niche but growing area.
