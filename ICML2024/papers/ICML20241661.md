## Problem

Existing adaptive conformal inference methods rely on online gradient descent variants that require careful tuning of learning rates, with performance highly sensitive to this choice. This tuning burden becomes especially problematic for multi-horizon forecasting or large collections of data streams, where the scale of nonconformity scores varies and heuristic approximations risk degrading coverage. The paper asks whether parameter-free online convex optimization can replace gradient-based updates while preserving valid long-term coverage.

## Input / Output

At each time step t, the method consumes a feature vector X_t, the full history of past observations {(X_i, Y_i)}_{i≤t-1}, a pre-trained prediction model f_t, and a target miscoverage level α ∈ (0, 1/2). It outputs a symmetric prediction interval [Ŷ_t − s_t, Ŷ_t + s_t] where the conformal radius s_t is updated online. For multi-step forecasting (horizon H), H separate radii s^(1),…,s^(H) are maintained and updated independently, with each radius associated with a specific step-ahead position. No learning rate, grid of rates, or bound on nonconformity scores needs to be specified by the user.

## Method

The paper reframes adaptive conformal inference as a coin betting game: the gambler's wealth W_t tracks the cumulative pinball loss, and the betting fraction λ_t determines the conformal radius via s_t = λ_t · W_t. Two parameter-free betting strategies are deployed — the Krichevsky-Trofimov (KT) estimator extended to continuous coins, and the Online Newton Step (ONS) — replacing the OGD/SF-OGD updates that require a learning rate η. The KT-based predictor (Algorithm 1) updates λ_{t+1} = (t/(t+1))λ_t − (1/(t+1))g_t where g_t is the subgradient of the pinball loss, yielding a radius that adapts to coverage errors without any user-specified hyperparameter. The paper proves that under bounded nonconformity scores (S_t ∈ [0, D]), the KT-based predictor achieves the long-term miscoverage guarantee (lim_{T→∞} |T^{-1}Σ 1{Y_t ∉ Ĉ_t} − α| = 0) and enjoys sub-linear regret O(|s°|√(T ln(CT|s°|))) relative to any fixed benchmark radius, eliminating the need to know D or tune η.

## Results

On 5-step-ahead electricity demand forecasting, the KT-based conformal predictor achieves 88.9–89.1% empirical coverage (target 90%) with the narrowest prediction sets in 4 out of 5 horizons (e.g., width 7.58 vs. 8.36 for OGD at k=1). On 5-step-ahead stock price forecasting across five tickers, KT achieves 84.6–85.0% average coverage, closely trailing OGD (85.4–86.4%) while substantially outperforming SF-OGD (76.3–79.6%) and Prophet's native intervals (58.8–69.4%). The key advantage is that KT requires zero hyperparameter tuning, whereas OGD and SF-OGD performance is highly sensitive to the learning rate choice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OGD (Gibbs & Candès, 2021), the KT-based predictor achieves slightly lower coverage (84.6–85.0% vs. 85.4–86.4% on stocks; 89.0% vs. 89.9% on electricity) but produces narrower prediction sets in 4 of 5 horizons on electricity and requires no learning-rate tuning. Against SF-OGD (Bhatnagar et al., 2023), KT substantially wins on coverage (84.6–85.0% vs. 76.3–79.6% on stocks), as SF-OGD's decaying effective learning rate impairs its ability to adapt to distribution shifts, causing prolonged under- or over-coverage. Against Prophet's native prediction intervals (Taylor & Letham, 2018), KT dramatically outperforms (84.6–85.0% vs. 58.8–69.4% on stocks), with the gap widening at longer horizons as Prophet fails to adequately widen its intervals for multi-step forecasts. Overall, the betting-based method trades a small coverage deficit (~1–1.5%) relative to carefully tuned OGD for complete elimination of hyperparameter sensitivity, making it the most practical choice for deployment across many data streams or multi-horizon settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes parameter-free online convex optimization techniques—specifically the Krichevsky-Trofimov (KT) estimator and the Online Newton Step (ONS) betting scheme—from the coin-betting literature (Orabona & Pál, 2016; Cutkosky & Orabona, 2018) into the adaptive conformal inference setting. It departs from the dominant OGD/SF-OGD-based approaches (Gibbs & Candès, 2021; Bhatnagar et al., 2023) by eliminating the learning-rate hyperparameter entirely, replacing gradient-descent radius updates with wealth-based betting fractions that adapt automatically to the scale of nonconformity scores. The conceptual bridge is that the pinball-loss subgradients define adversarial coin outcomes, so any parameter-free OCO algorithm with sub-linear regret yields a valid adaptive conformal predictor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that practitioners deploying conformal prediction across many data streams or multi-horizon forecasts no longer need to tune (or grid-search) learning rates, a step that is error-prone and scales poorly. The provable long-term coverage guarantee under only a boundedness assumption on scores, combined with sub-linear regret, provides a clean theoretical foundation. A key limitation is that finite-sample coverage tends to sit slightly below the nominal level (e.g., ~88–89% vs. 90%), and the method is slightly inferior to carefully tuned OGD in terms of both coverage and set width. Open questions include whether the finite-sample undercoverage can be corrected without reintroducing parameters, and whether the approach extends naturally to asymmetric or multi-output prediction sets.
