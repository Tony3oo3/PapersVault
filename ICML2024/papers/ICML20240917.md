## Problem

Existing video super-resolution methods, whether RGB-based or event-based, primarily focus on motion learning and temporal alignment, leaving texture-rich regions blurry or jittery. While event cameras provide high-frequency dynamic details ideal for texture restoration, prior event-based VSR work has only exploited events for motion enhancement, leaving their potential for texture recovery unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240917/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution video frame sequence of T frames and the interframe event streams (T−1 intervals) as input. Events are converted into normalized voxel grids with B=5 temporal bins at the LR resolution (downsampled via bicubic interpolation to match frame downsampling). For synthetic datasets lacking real events, ESIM generates events from RIFE 8×-interpolated high-frame-rate video. The output is the corresponding super-resolved frame sequence, produced via pixel-shuffle upsampling followed by element-wise addition with the bicubic-upsampled input.

## Method

EvTexture builds on BasicVSR's bidirectional recurrent architecture and introduces a parallel two-branch design: a motion branch (SpyNet flow estimation + spatial warping) and a texture enhancement branch containing the Iterative Texture Enhancement (ITE) module. The ITE module uses a shared context extractor (8 residual blocks on the current LR frame) and a shared texture extractor (5-layer UNet on each voxel bin), feeding both into a ConvGRU-based iterative texture updater that processes the B=5 voxel bins sequentially with residual learning, progressively transferring high-frequency textural details into the propagation feature. After N iterations, the texture-enhanced feature is fused with the motion feature via a residual block, and the combined feature is upsampled through pixel-shuffle layers. Training uses Charbonnier loss with Adam and Cosine Annealing over 300K iterations on 8 RTX 3090 GPUs, with SpyNet frozen for the first 5K iterations.

## Results

On Vid4 (4× VSR), EvTexture achieves 29.51 dB PSNR / 0.8909 SSIM (average), surpassing the best RGB-based method VRT (27.88 dB) by +1.63 dB and the best prior event-based method EBVSR (28.46 dB) by +1.05 dB. On REDS4, EvTexture reaches 32.79 dB / 0.9174 SSIM, and on Vimeo-90K-T it achieves 38.23 dB / 0.9544 SSIM. The method also attains the best LPIPS scores (0.2185 on Vid4, 0.1684 on REDS4) with only 8.9M parameters and 136 ms runtime, and on the real-world CED dataset it achieves 40.52 dB (2×) and 33.68 dB (4×), outperforming EGVSR by +1.83 dB and +2.56 dB respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvTexture outperforms all 14 baselines across all four datasets. Against the strongest RGB-based competitor VRT, EvTexture gains +1.63 dB on Vid4, +0.60 dB on REDS4, and +0.03 dB on Vimeo-90K-T, while using fewer parameters (8.9M vs. 35.6M) and less runtime (136 ms vs. 243 ms). Against the closest event-based competitor EBVSR, EvTexture achieves +1.05 dB on Vid4, +1.32 dB on REDS4, and +0.67 dB on Vimeo-90K-T. The gap over EGVSR is particularly large: +4.67 dB on Vid4, +5.92 dB on REDS4, and +3.61 dB on Vimeo-90K-T, reflecting EGVSR's weaker texture handling. On the real-world CED dataset, EvTexture surpasses EBVSR by +0.38 dB (2×) and +0.26 dB (4×), and outperforms BasicVSR by +0.95 dB (2×). On the hardest texture subset of Vimeo-90K-T, EvTexture leads VRT by +0.47 dB, confirming its advantage is concentrated in texture-rich content.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the BasicVSR bidirectional recurrent architecture for video super-resolution but departs fundamentally from prior event-based VSR methods (EGVSR, EBVSR) that use event signals to enhance motion estimation and temporal alignment. Instead, it repurposes the high-frequency, high-temporal-resolution properties of events for a different sub-problem: texture restoration. The iterative refinement mechanism is adapted from RAFT-style recurrent flow estimation and applied here to progressively extract texture details from sequential voxel bins, while the two-branch (motion + texture) design is a novel architectural contribution not present in prior VSR work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to explicitly target texture restoration in VSR using event signals, addressing a gap the authors identify where prior work focused on motion or occlusion. The texture magnitude metric (Eq. 9) and the difficulty-stratified evaluation on Vimeo-90K-T provide a useful framework for future texture-focused VSR research. Limitations include reliance on ESIM-simulated events for three of four test datasets, evaluation only at 2× and 4× scales, and the open question of whether the approach generalizes to fast-moving or low-light scenarios where event signals may be sparse or noisy. The modular design (EvTexture+ can incorporate event-based flow) suggests the texture branch could be combined with other event-enhancement strategies in future work.
