## Problem

Existing unsupervised RL methods face a fundamental trade-off: empowerment-based skill discovery (e.g., DIAYN) learns distinguishable skills but produces static behaviors with poor state coverage, while entropy-based exploration (e.g., CIC) achieves broad state coverage but fails to generate discriminable skills. Moreover, MI-based objectives require variational estimators that are unstable in high-dimensional stochastic environments. The paper asks whether one can simultaneously maximize state coverage and skill discriminability without relying on MI estimation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes continuous state observations (dimension varies by domain: 2 for maze, 24 for Walker, 78 for Quadruped, 55 for Jaco Arm) and a 16-dimensional one-hot discrete skill vector. It produces a skill-conditional actor that outputs continuous actions, paired with an ensemble of 16 independent Q-networks. Unusually, each Q-network is trained exclusively on transitions assigned to its corresponding state cluster via a masking matrix, so the value function for skill i only sees states in cluster i, enforcing partitioned exploration at the data level rather than through a shared reward signal.

## Method

CeSD replaces the shared-parameter skill-conditional policy with an ensemble of independent Q-networks (one per skill), each trained via TD-learning on a disjoint subset of transitions determined by prototype-based clustering (Sinkhorn-Knopp with learned anchors). The intrinsic reward for each skill is a particle-based k-NN entropy estimate computed only within its assigned cluster, encouraging local exploration that expands the cluster boundary. A state-distribution constraint is then applied via a heuristic regularization reward that penalizes visiting states belonging to other clusters, pushing each skill's occupancy toward its non-overlapping cluster. Theoretically, the paper proves that partition exploration with uniform cluster sizes achieves the same maximum entropy as global exploration (up to a log n constant), and that the distribution constraint guarantees monotonically increasing state entropy per skill via a coupling-based entropy bound.

## Results

On the URLB benchmark (12 downstream tasks across Walker, Quadruped, and Jaco domains), CeSD achieves a 91.05% interquartile mean (IQM) and 15.47% optimality gap (OG), outperforming the previous state-of-the-art BeCL (25.44% OG) and the best entropy-based method CIC (75.18% IQM). CeSD achieves the best individual scores on 9 out of 12 tasks, dominating the Quadruped (all 4 tasks) and Jaco (all 4 tasks) domains, while CIC leads on 3 Walker tasks (Flip, Run, Walk). The method is evaluated over 10 random seeds per task (1,320 total runs) following the reliable evaluation protocol.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CeSD achieves the best aggregate performance (91.05% IQM, 15.47% OG) across all 12 URLB tasks, outperforming the previous SOTA BeCL (25.44% OG) by a large margin. Against the strongest entropy-based baseline CIC, CeSD wins on 9/12 tasks but loses on Walker Flip (541 vs 641), Walker Run (337 vs 450), and Walker Walk (834 vs 903), where CIC's global exploration advantage is more pronounced. In the Quadruped domain, CeSD dominates all four tasks by 28–146 points over BeCL (e.g., Walk: 889 vs 743; Stand: 919 vs 875). In the Jaco manipulation domain, CeSD leads all four reaching tasks by 37–90 points over CIC (e.g., Re. top left: 215 vs 149). Empowerment-based methods (DIAYN, DADS, SMM, APS) consistently underperform, with DIAYN scoring as low as 12±5 on Jaco Re. top left, confirming the paper's claim that MI-based skill discovery produces static skills with poor state coverage. Curiosity-driven methods (ICM, RND, Disagreement) are competitive on some Walker tasks but lag significantly on Quadruped and Jaco, where structured exploration is more critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

CeSD carves out a middle ground between two dominant paradigms in unsupervised skill discovery: empowerment-based methods (DIAYN, DADS, BeCL) that maximize mutual information between skills and states, and pure exploration methods (CIC, APT, ProtoRL) that maximize global state entropy. It builds on particle-based k-NN entropy estimation from APT and CIC for intrinsic rewards, and repurposes Sinkhorn-Knopp clustering (originally from DINO) to partition the state space into non-overlapping clusters. The key conceptual departure is bypassing MI estimation entirely—replacing it with an ensemble of independent Q-networks (one per skill) that perform local partition exploration, plus a state-distribution constraint that penalizes skills for visiting states assigned to other clusters. This contrasts with prior ensemble Q-networks (used for uncertainty estimation in online/offline RL) by giving each network a distinct objective tied to a specific cluster.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CeSD achieves state-of-the-art results on the URLB benchmark (91.05% IQM, 15.47% optimality gap) with a clean theoretical guarantee that partition exploration monotonically increases global state coverage, offering a principled alternative to the variational MI estimators that are known to be unstable in high-dimensional stochastic environments. The main limitation is the restriction to a discrete skill space (n=16 one-hot skills), which the authors acknowledge prevents generalization to continuous skill representations; they suggest randomized value functions or hyper Q-networks as future directions. An open question is whether the clustering-based partitioning remains effective as the number of skills or the state-space dimensionality grows substantially, and whether the heuristic distribution-constraint reward (Eq. 9) can be replaced by a more principled objective.
