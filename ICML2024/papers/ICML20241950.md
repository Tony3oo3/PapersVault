## Problem

Prior membership inference attacks on diffusion models either rely on training multiple shadow diffusion models (computationally prohibitive, requiring days per shadow model) or apply a single uniform threshold to reconstruction losses across all examples. This paper asks whether a lightweight, per-example conditional threshold can achieve comparable or better attack accuracy without the shadow-model overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241950/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attack takes a target RGB image z*, white-box access to the trained diffusion model parameters θ, and an auxiliary dataset D of public images from the same distribution (e.g., 32×32 CIFAR-10/100, STL-10, or Tiny-ImageNet). It computes the deterministic t-error at a fixed timestep t=50 for each auxiliary image, trains tiny quantile regression models on those scores, and outputs a binary IN/NO membership decision for z* with a tunable false-positive rate α controlled by the quantile level.

## Method

The core mechanism is a conditional quantile regression model q_α(z) that predicts the α-quantile of the t-error (a deterministic one-step forward-then-reverse reconstruction loss) conditioned on the input image z, yielding a unique per-example threshold rather than a uniform marginal one. Each weak attacker is a tiny ResNet (as small as 5,666 parameters, versus 710M in the target diffusion model) with multiple prediction heads for different α values, trained via pinball loss on auxiliary data. The paper introduces a 'bag of weak attackers' ensemble: m such models are trained on bootstrapped subsets of D, and the final prediction is a majority vote, which reduces variance and substantially improves TPR at low FPR. This avoids the need for shadow diffusion models, knowledge of training hyperparameters, or the target architecture, making the attack orders of magnitude cheaper to prepare than prior GSA/LiRA-style attacks.

## Results

The proposed Bag of Weak Attackers (7 weak quantile regression models, each with only 5,666 parameters) achieves near-perfect TPR across all four datasets: 99.94% TPR@1% FPR and 99.86% TPR@0.1% FPR on CIFAR-10, 99.89%/99.75% on CIFAR-100, 99.99%/99.98% on Tiny-ImageNet, and 99.98%/99.98% on STL-10. The attack requires no knowledge of the target diffusion model's training hyperparameters or architecture, and each weak attacker has ~125,000× fewer parameters than the target DDPM (7.1×10⁸). The marginal baseline, which uses a single uniform threshold, achieves only 5.78%–11.06% TPR@1% FPR, confirming that per-example conditional thresholds are essential.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two categories of baselines: the GSA shadow-model attack (Pang et al., 2023) and a marginal baseline using a single uniform threshold. On CIFAR-10, the proposed method achieves 99.86% TPR@0.1% FPR, substantially outperforming GSA1 (82.90%) and GSA2 (58.57%) at the same operating point, while also exceeding them at 1% FPR (99.94% vs. 99.70% and 97.88%). GSA results are only reported for CIFAR-10 due to the prohibitive cost of training shadow diffusion models on other datasets. The marginal baseline is dramatically weaker across all four datasets (5.78%–11.06% TPR@1% FPR), confirming that a single global threshold cannot capture the per-example variation in reconstruction loss. The proposed method also requires no knowledge of the target model's training procedure (hyperparameters, architecture), whereas GSA must replicate the full training pipeline for each shadow model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the quantile-regression-based membership inference framework of Bertran et al. (2023) from supervised classifiers to diffusion models, adopting the deterministic t-error loss from Duan et al. (2023) as the scoring function. It departs from the dominant shadow-model paradigm (LiRA, GSA) by reformulating the hypothesis test to condition on the observed model θ rather than on the training set Z, eliminating the need to train expensive shadow diffusion models. The key methodological addition is a 'bag of weak attackers'—bootstrap aggregation over tiny ResNet quantile regressors—that compensates for the difficulty of directly optimizing pinball loss and reduces variance without requiring hyperparameter tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-perfect MI accuracy (≈99.9% TPR at 1% FPR) across four datasets, achieved with models containing only ~5,700 parameters versus 710M-parameter diffusion targets, underscores that diffusion models are acutely vulnerable to membership inference and that efficient auditing is feasible. The finding that per-sample conditional thresholds succeed even when marginal error distributions are visually indistinguishable (32-channel models on Tiny-ImageNet) highlights a limitation of uniform-threshold baselines that practitioners should account for. A key open question, noted by the authors, is whether the approach extends to the black-box setting where model parameters are inaccessible, and whether the results hold for much larger, higher-resolution diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion-scale) where the generalization gap may differ.
