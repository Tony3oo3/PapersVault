## Problem

Deep networks empirically learn invariance to smooth spatial transformations (diffeomorphisms) during training, and this invariance strongly correlates with test performance, yet the mechanism linking hierarchical representation learning to the emergence of such invariance remains unexplained. Prior hierarchical generative models of data capture the compositional structure but do not account for the spatial sparsity that makes image labels stable under smooth deformations, leaving the two dominant explanations for deep learning's success—hierarchy and invariance—disconnected.

## Input / Output

Inputs are sparse one-hot encoded patches of dimension d×v where d = (s(s₀+1))^L, but only s^L positions carry informative features (the rest are empty columns), making the data extremely sparse. The network produces a class label among n_c classes, and the paper additionally computes two diagnostic sensitivities: the relative change in internal representations under synonym exchange (S_k) and under discrete diffeomorphisms that shift informative features within their patches (D_k). These sensitivities are measured at every hidden layer and at the output, normalized by the variance across random test pairs.

## Method

The paper introduces the Sparse Random Hierarchy Model (SRHM), a context-free grammar where each production rule embeds s informative features among s(s₀+1) positions, with uninformative features propagating empty patches down the hierarchy. This sparsity makes the classification task invariant to discrete diffeomorphisms (repositioning informative features within their local patches) and to synonym exchange, unifying the two invariances. Training CNNs and Locally Connected Networks on the SRHM reveals that invariance to both diffeomorphisms and synonyms is acquired at precisely the same training set size P* at which the task is solved, rationalizing the empirical correlation between diffeomorphism sensitivity and test error. The sample complexity scales as (s₀+1)^L · n_c · m^L for LCNs but only (s₀+1)² · n_c · m^L for CNNs, quantifying the benefit of weight sharing and showing that both remain polynomial in input dimension, beating the curse of dimensionality.

## Results

The paper derives sample complexity scaling laws for deep networks learning the SRHM: for Locally Connected Networks (LCNs), P* ~ C_0(s,L)(s_0+1)^L n_c m^L (Eq. 3), and for CNNs with weight sharing, P* ~ C_1(s_0+1)^2 n_c m^L (Eq. 4). Both are polynomial in the input dimension d = (s(s_0+1))^L, beating the curse of dimensionality. The central empirical finding is that the sample complexity for learning invariance to diffeomorphisms (P_D*) and to synonym exchange (P_S*) are nearly equal to the task sample complexity P* (Eq. 8), explaining the strong correlation between diffeomorphism sensitivity and test error observed on CIFAR-10 (Figure 1A).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between LCNs (no weight sharing) and CNNs (with weight sharing) on the SRHM. Weight sharing reduces the sparsity dependence from exponential in depth L (factor (s_0+1)^L for LCNs) to a constant quadratic factor (s_0+1)^2 for CNNs, making CNNs substantially more data-efficient as depth increases. The SRHM predictions are validated against CIFAR-10: the empirically observed power-law correlation between test error and relative diffeomorphism sensitivity (Petrini et al., 2021) is reproduced by the SRHM (Figure 1C), and the simultaneous emergence of invariance and performance holds across VGG, ResNet, and EfficientNet architectures (Appendix B, Figure 9). The paper does not benchmark against competing learning methods; its contribution is a theoretical framework unifying hierarchical structure and invariance, with the LCN-vs-CNN comparison serving as the main architectural ablation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Random Hierarchy Model (RHM) of Cagnetta et al. (2023b) by introducing spatial sparsity into the hierarchical generative process, creating the Sparse Random Hierarchy Model (SRHM). It draws on the production-rule framework of Mossel (2018) and the gradient-descent analysis of Malach & Shalev-Shwartz (2020), but departs from both by showing that sparsity naturally endows the task with invariance to discrete diffeomorphisms—a property absent from all prior hierarchical generative models. The paper thereby bridges two previously separate research threads: hierarchical/compositional data models (which ignored spatial invariance) and diffeomorphism-stability analysis (which ignored hierarchical structure).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic explanation for the empirically observed correlation between a network's sensitivity to diffeomorphisms and its test error on CIFAR-10 (Petrini et al., 2021), showing that both arise because the network learns a hierarchical representation at the same training-set size at which it acquires invariance. The quantified sample-complexity scalings (Eq. 3, Eq. 4) give concrete predictions for how sparsity and depth trade off, and the finding that weight sharing reduces the sparsity penalty from exponential to quadratic in depth has practical implications for architecture design. Key open questions include how to empirically measure synonym invariance on real image datasets (the authors suggest diffusion-based feature manipulation as a path forward) and whether the framework extends to natural language, where discrete synonym substitution is more directly testable.
