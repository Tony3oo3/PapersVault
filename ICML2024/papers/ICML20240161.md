## Problem

The XAI field is experiencing a crisis of identity caused by conflating fundamentally different goals: user-facing trust and justification versus model debugging and validation. The paper argues that the research narrative is dominated by human-values-oriented explanations (BLUE XAI), while model-validation-oriented explanations (RED XAI)—needed to question, debug, and extract knowledge from models—remain critically under-explored despite being essential for AI safety.

## Input / Output

As a position paper, the analytical input is the current body of XAI literature, survey papers, and methodological assumptions, while the output is a two-culture taxonomy (BLUE vs. RED XAI), a diagnosis of five prevalent fallacies, and a catalog of open research challenges. The paper does not propose a new algorithm; instead it reframes the research agenda by distinguishing who consumes explanations (lay users vs. model developers), when they are used (inference vs. training/debugging), and what properties matter (simplicity vs. faithfulness and actionability).

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a conceptual framework that disentangles XAI into two complementary cultures: BLUE XAI (human-values oriented, targeting end users with simple, trust-building explanations) and RED XAI (model-validation oriented, targeting developers with faithful, actionable, and potentially complex explanations). It diagnoses five fallacies that conflate these cultures (binary interpretability, silver-bullet methods, ground-truth explanations, mandatory user studies, and user-centric trust as the sole goal), then surveys recent review papers to demonstrate the empirical dominance of BLUE XAI in the literature. The paper closes by proposing five concrete RED XAI research challenges: constructing complementary multi-faceted explanations, building benchmarks and standardized tooling, adopting an explorer mindset for unknown-unknowns, leveraging Rashomon sets of equally good models for comparative analysis, and applying XAI techniques to scientific hypothesis generation and validation.

## Results

This is a position paper with no quantitative experiments or benchmark results. Its primary contribution is a conceptual framework dividing XAI into two cultures: BLUE XAI (human/values-oriented, targeting end-users) and RED XAI (model/validation-oriented, targeting developers). The paper identifies five fallacies in current XAI discourse (binary interpretability, silver-bullet methods, ground-truth explanations, user-study-as-ultimate-validation, and user-centric trust) and argues that RED XAI is significantly under-explored in the literature, with most 2023 survey papers focusing on BLUE XAI themes like trustworthiness and fairness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a position paper, this work does not propose a new method or compare against baselines. Instead, it surveys and categorizes existing XAI literature, noting that highly cited 2023 reviews (Chamola et al., Ali et al., Severes et al., Embarak, Farrow, Saeed & Omlin) predominantly address BLUE XAI concerns (trust, user rights, fairness, cognitive load), while RED XAI perspectives (debugging, model exploration, complementary explanations) appear in only a few works (Borys et al., Weber et al.). The paper positions itself against the 'single best XAI method' narrative and the 'ground truth explanation' evaluation paradigm, arguing for multi-faceted, interactive, and developer-oriented explainability approaches. It also contextualizes its RED XAI framing relative to Rudin (2019)'s critique of black-box explanations, clarifying that RED XAI's goal of model validation is distinct from the BLUE XAI justification that Rudin targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This position paper draws a direct conceptual parallel to Breiman's (2001) 'Two Cultures' in statistics, transplanting the data-understanding vs. algorithmic-performance divide into the XAI domain as BLUE (human-values) vs. RED (model-validation) cultures. It positions itself against the dominant BLUE XAI narrative in recent surveys (Chamola et al., Ali et al., Severes et al.) while acknowledging prior RED XAI work on model debugging (Anders et al., 2022; Weber et al., 2023) and the Rashomon set framework (Breiman, 2001; Fisher et al., 2019; Dong & Rudin, 2020). Rather than building on a specific method, the paper repurposes existing XAI tools (iNNvestigate, DALEX, Quantus, What-If Tool) as evidence that a model-exploration ecosystem already exists but lacks a unifying research agenda.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that the XAI field's 'crisis of identity' stems from conflating user-facing justification with developer-facing model interrogation—reframes priorities and could redirect research effort toward underexplored areas like systematic model debugging, complementary multi-method explanation, and XAI-driven scientific discovery. Key open questions it raises include: how to benchmark RED XAI methods without ground-truth explanations, how to integrate complementary explanations into a coherent exploration workflow, and how to extend model-validation explanations to foundation models where the application space is open-ended. As a position paper, its impact depends on whether the community adopts the RED/BLUE taxonomy as a productive organizing principle; the authors themselves acknowledge the two-culture framing is a deliberate simplification that will not capture all XAI use cases.
