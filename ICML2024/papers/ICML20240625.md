## Problem

EM and AM algorithms for mixed linear regression had only been analyzed under realizable generative models where data is produced by a known mixture of linear functions. In the agnostic setting—where no generative model is assumed and the goal is to minimize a population loss—prior work (Pal et al., 2022) required an initialization radius of O(1/√d), which is prohibitive in high dimensions. This paper closes that gap by proving that both gradient AM and gradient EM converge to population loss minimizers with only a dimension-independent Θ(1) initialization.

## Input / Output

The method takes n i.i.d. samples (x_i, y_i) with x_i ∈ R^d drawn from N(0, I_d) and y_i ∈ R bounded by |y_i| ≤ b, drawn from an arbitrary unknown distribution D. The output is a list of k linear parameter vectors (θ_1*, …, θ_k*) that jointly minimize either the min-loss (each sample assigned to its best-fit line) or the soft-min loss (soft assignment via softmax-weighted squared errors). The prediction protocol is list-decoding: for a new input x, the learner outputs k candidate labels {⟨x, θ_j⟩} and the loss is evaluated as the minimum (or soft-minimum) over the list.

## Method

The paper analyzes two iterative algorithms: gradient AM for the min-loss and gradient EM for the soft-min loss. At each iteration, gradient AM partitions samples by assigning each to its best-fit current iterate, then takes a gradient step on the quadratic loss over each partition; gradient EM instead computes soft-min probabilities (softmax over squared residuals) and takes a weighted gradient step. The key technical contribution is characterizing restricted Gaussians—covariates conditioned on falling in a problem-defined region S_j*—as sub-Gaussian with a shifted mean and controlled variance, enabling a lower bound on the minimum singular value of the restricted Gram matrix that drives the contraction rate. To handle inter-iteration dependence (since the partition sets depend on the current iterates, which depend on the data), the algorithms use fresh sample splits at each round. The resulting per-iteration guarantee is ‖θ_j⁺ − θ_j*‖ ≤ ρ‖θ_j − θ_j*‖ + ε with ρ = 1 − cγπ_min³ < 1, where the error floor ε scales linearly with the misspecification parameter λ and the step size γ, and the initialization only requires ‖θ_j⁽⁰⁾ − θ_j*‖ ≤ c_ini‖θ_j*‖ for a small constant c_ini.

## Results

The paper establishes exponential convergence guarantees for both gradient AM and gradient EM in the agnostic mixed linear regression setting. For gradient AM (Theorem 2.1), the contraction factor is ρ = 1 − cγπ_min³ with an error floor ε = O(γλ√(d log d log(1/π_min))) + O(γ(k−1)P_e), where P_e decays exponentially in (Δ−λ)². For gradient EM (Theorem 3.1), the contraction factor is ρ = 1 − 2γc(1−η)π_min³ with an error floor that additionally includes an exponentially decaying term in the separation gap. Both algorithms require only Θ(1) initialization (‖θ_j − θ_j*‖ ≤ c_ini‖θ_j*‖), a significant improvement over the O(1/√d) initialization required by Pal et al. (2022). The sample complexity is n ≥ C·d·log(1/π_min)/π_min³, which is optimal in d but sub-optimal in k. The Rademacher complexity of the soft-min loss class is bounded by 4kR(1+R)/√n, showing at most a k-fold increase over standard linear regression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or dataset evaluations, so there are no standard baseline comparisons. The primary theoretical comparison is with Pal et al. (2022), the direct predecessor that introduced the agnostic mixed linear regression framework. The key improvement is in the initialization requirement: Pal et al. (2022) require the initial parameters to be within O(1/√d) of the population minimizers (via an ε-net discretization argument), while this paper shows that a dimension-independent Θ(1) initialization suffices, achieved by using fresh samples each iteration instead of the ε-net argument. Additionally, the paper corrects an error in Pal et al. (2022, Theorem 4.2), showing that their singular value lower bound holds only for Gaussian covariates, not for general bounded covariates as originally claimed. The paper also extends the analysis beyond min-loss (covered by Pal et al.) to the soft-min loss with gradient EM, which was not previously analyzed in the agnostic setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the agnostic mixed linear regression framework introduced by Pal et al. (2022) in two key ways: it adds a gradient EM analysis (Pal et al. only considered gradient AM) and improves the initialization requirement from O(1/√d) to O(1) for gradient AM. The analysis builds on the generative-setting guarantees of Balakrishnan et al. (2017) for EM and Yi et al. (2014, 2016) for AM, but removes the realizable generative model assumption, requiring new tools for analyzing restricted Gaussians in the agnostic setting. The paper also corrects an error in Pal et al. (2022, Theorem 4.2) regarding the generality of covariate assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that EM and AM converge to population loss minimizers even without a generative model provides theoretical justification for using these classical iterative algorithms in practical agnostic settings where the data may not follow a clean mixture model. Key limitations include the requirement for Gaussian covariates, the need for sample splitting (fresh samples per iteration), and sub-optimal sample complexity in k (1/π_min³ vs. information-theoretic Ω(kd)). Open questions include whether the Gaussian covariate assumption can be relaxed to sub-Gaussians with small-ball conditions, whether resampling can be eliminated via leave-one-out analysis, and whether the agnostic framework extends to other mixture problems (classifiers, experts).
