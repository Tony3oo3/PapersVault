## Problem

Existing reward-free exploration (RFE) and unsupervised RL algorithms design intrinsic rewards that treat all collected samples equally, ignoring the heterogeneous uncertainty across different state-action pairs. This uniform treatment diminishes sample efficiency, and prior attempts to incorporate variance information (e.g., Zhang et al. 2023) rely on computationally expensive nested iterative optimization. The paper asks whether an intrinsic reward can be crafted that is both theoretically tighter and practically efficient.

## Input / Output

During the exploration phase, the algorithm consumes only state observations (state-vector modality) and a predefined value function class F (characterized by its covering number N_F(ε) and generalized eluder dimension dim(F)), with no reward signal available. In the planning phase, a reward function r = {r_h}_{h=1}^H is revealed and the algorithm must output a near-optimal policy π without any further environment interaction. The output policy is guaranteed ε-optimal with high probability after K = Õ(H² log N_F(ε) dim(F) / ε²) exploration episodes under the bounded total reward assumption.

## Method

GFA-RFE is a two-phase model-free algorithm whose core innovation is an uncertainty-aware intrinsic reward r_{k,h} proportional to the generalized eluder dimension D²_F, which quantifies how divergent functions in F are at the current state-action pair relative to past observations—high values signal under-explored regions. The second key component is variance-weighted Bellman regression: the standard Bellman residual loss is weighted by 1/σ̄², where σ̄ combines aleatoric uncertainty (transition variance) and epistemic uncertainty (D_F), so that low-variance, high-confidence samples receive greater influence in fitting the Q-function. In practice, the bonus oracle D̄²_F is instantiated as the variance across n-ensemble Q networks trained on different mini-batches, making the method directly implementable within standard Q-learning pipelines. Theoretically, this design yields a Õ(H² log N_F(ε) dim(F) / ε²) sample complexity, improving the H²d² factor over the prior best general-function-approximation result (Kong et al. 2021) by leveraging weighted regression, a truncated Bellman equation in the analysis, and the improved uncertainty metric.

## Results

On the URLB benchmark (DeepMind Control Suite), GFA-RFE achieves the best cumulative reward on 2 out of 8 tasks (Walker-Stand: 925±50, Quadruped-Walk: 726±146) and is second-best on 5 of the remaining 7 tasks. The headline metric is cumulative reward averaged over 8 runs, evaluated via online exploration followed by offline planning. GFA-RFE's strongest result is Walker-Stand (925±50), surpassing the next-best APT (891±62) by ~3.8%, while its weakest result is Quadruped-Stand (867±61) where Disagreement leads (953±16). Overall, GFA-RFE is competitive with or superior to the top-performing baselines (APT, Disagreement) across all 8 tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GFA-RFE is compared against six unsupervised RL baselines: ICM (Pathak et al., 2017), APT (Liu & Abbeel, 2021b), DIAYN (Eysenbach et al., 2018), APS (Liu & Abbeel, 2021a), Disagreement (Pathak et al., 2019), and SMM (Lee et al., 2019). GFA-RFE clearly outperforms ICM, DIAYN, APS, and SMM on all 8 tasks, with margins often exceeding 200–400 reward points (e.g., Walker-Stand: 925 vs. ICM's 466). Against the strongest baselines, GFA-RFE wins outright on Walker-Stand (925 vs. APT's 891) and Quadruped-Walk (726 vs. Disagreement's 720), and is within 1–15 reward points of the best on Walker-Run (339 vs. 340) and Walker-Flip (554 vs. 570). On Quadruped-Stand, GFA-RFE (867) trails Disagreement (953) and APT (910), representing its only clear loss to a top baseline. The paper notes that APT and Disagreement are the most consistently strong baselines, and GFA-RFE's uncertainty-aware intrinsic reward brings it to competitive or superior levels with these methods across the board.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reward-free exploration (RFE) framework of Jin et al. (2020a) to general function approximation, building on the generalized eluder dimension of Agarwal et al. (2022) and the weighted regression technique of Zhao et al. (2023). The key departure from Kong et al. (2021), the closest prior work in the same setting, is a variance-adaptive intrinsic reward based on the uncertainty metric D_F² combined with variance-weighted Bellman regression, replacing the 'sensitivity' score that treated all samples uniformly. Unlike Zhang et al. (2023) in linear mixture MDPs, which achieved similar variance-dependent weighting but required computationally expensive nested iterative optimization, GFA-RFE achieves its guarantees through a standard model-free Q-learning loop with ensemble-based uncertainty estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a significant gap in RFE theory by reducing the sample complexity from Õ(H⁶d⁴ε⁻²) to Õ(H²d²ε⁻²) in the linear case, matching the minimax lower bound of Hu et al. (2022) while remaining computationally efficient and model-free. Empirically, GFA-RFE is competitive with or slightly ahead of top unsupervised RL baselines (APT, Disagreement, RND) on DeepMind Control Suite, validating the theory-practice connection. Open questions include extending to time-homogeneous settings (which would remove an H factor), scaling to pixel-based observations, and whether the variance-weighted regression component provides gains beyond the intrinsic reward design in larger, more complex environments.
