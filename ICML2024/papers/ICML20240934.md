## Problem

Bayesian Causal Discovery (BCD) infers a posterior distribution over causal graphs and parameters, but the community has no consensus on which evaluation metrics reliably reflect the quality of this posterior approximation. This paper shows that in the low-sample, non-identifiable regimes where BCD is most needed, standard proxy metrics (E-SHD, AUPRC, NLL, etc.) fail to correlate with metrics that directly compare against the true posterior, and the true posterior itself can score worse than its approximations on these metrics.

## Input / Output

The study takes synthetic observational data from linear Gaussian additive noise models (ER and SF graphs with d=5 or 10 nodes, N∈{5,10,100,1000} samples) and evaluates five BCD methods (BCD-Nets, DIBS, VBG, BGIES, DIBS-Det) on eight metrics. Because the true posterior is tractable in closed form for d<6, the output includes not only standard metric scores but also Graph MMD (Hamming kernel) and Params MMD (RBF kernel) as gold-standard comparisons, Spearman rank correlation matrices between all metric pairs, and Kozachenko-Leonenko entropy estimates of both the true and approximate posteriors.

## Method

The paper performs a systematic empirical dissection of BCD evaluation by computing the exact posterior over all DAGs and parameters for linear Gaussian ANMs with d<6, then measuring how well each of eight proxy metrics (E-SHD, E-CPDAG SHD, AUPRC, AUROC, NLL, I-NLL, I-KL, I-MMD) correlates—via Spearman rank correlation—with two true-posterior-based metrics (Graph MMD and Params MMD). The key finding is that correlation between proxy metrics and true-posterior metrics is near zero in low-sample (n≈d) and non-identifiable settings, but improves substantially with more data and identifiability. The authors trace this failure to the entropy of the true posterior: when the posterior is highly entropic (many graphs with substantial probability mass), graph-based metrics that compare samples against a single ground-truth graph become uninformative. They further show that DIBS produces pathologically low-entropy solutions dominated by its prior in low-data regimes, and propose downstream-task evaluation (Bayesian experimental design or causal effect estimation) as alternatives when proxy metrics are unreliable.

## Results

The central finding is that at n=5, d=5 (non-identifiable), graph-based proxy metrics (E-SHD, E-CPDAG SHD, AUROC, AUPRC) exhibit near-zero or negative Spearman rank correlation with Graph MMD (the gold-standard metric comparing to the true posterior), with correlations ranging from −0.51 to 0.25. At n=100 in the identifiable setting, these same metrics correlate strongly with Graph MMD (0.62–0.71). Strikingly, the true posterior itself scores worse than several approximate methods (e.g., DIBS, BCD-Nets) on all proxy metrics in the n=5 regime, indicating the metrics are fundamentally misaligned with posterior quality when entropy is high. Entropy of the true posterior decreases monotonically with sample size and identifiability, confirming that the metrics' failure is tied to high-entropy posteriors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates six BCD methods—BCD-Nets, DIBS, VBG, BGIES (DAG Bootstrap + GIES), DIBS Det (deterministic), and DIBS BGe—against the analytically computable true posterior on linear Gaussian ANMs with d=5. The headline result is not that one method outperforms another, but that the true posterior itself is ranked worse than several approximations on all proxy metrics (E-SHD, AUROC, AUPRC, NLL, I-NLL) when n=5, in both identifiable and non-identifiable settings. DIBS consistently produces very low-entropy posteriors (often sampling a single edgeless graph in low-data regimes for SF graphs), making it appear 'better' on graph metrics despite poor posterior fidelity. VBG and BCD-Nets show the most responsive entropy reduction when interventional data is added incrementally (d=10), while DIBS and BGIES do not decrease entropy substantially. BGIES, though not strictly Bayesian, serves as a competitive baseline and is used extensively in prior work. The paper concludes that no single proxy metric reliably ranks methods by true posterior quality in the low-data, non-identifiable regime where BCD is most needed, and recommends downstream-task evaluation (causal effect estimation or Bayesian experimental design) as alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a meta-evaluation study that does not propose a new BCD algorithm but instead systematically interrogates the evaluation metrics the BCD community has inherited from classical causal discovery (SHD, AUPRC, AUROC, NLL, interventional divergences). It positions itself against the implicit assumption in prior BCD papers (Cundy et al., 2021; Lorch et al., 2021; Nishikawa-Toomey et al., 2022; Deleu et al., 2022/2023) that these proxy metrics faithfully rank posterior approximations. By computing the tractable true posterior for linear Gaussian ANMs with d=5, the authors create a gold-standard reference that prior work could not access, enabling a direct correlation analysis between proxy metrics and true-posterior comparison (Graph MMD, Params MMD).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard BCD evaluation metrics fail to correlate with true-posterior quality in low-sample, non-identifiable settings is consequential because that is precisely the regime where Bayesian causal discovery is most needed (e.g., biology with n≈d). If the field has been optimizing and comparing methods on metrics that do not reflect posterior fidelity, progress may be misdirected. The paper opens the question of what to evaluate instead, suggesting downstream tasks (Bayesian experimental design, causal effect estimation) as alternatives, but does not fully resolve the gap. Limitations include restriction to linear ANMs with d<6 and causally sufficient models, so whether the conclusions extend to nonlinear or high-dimensional settings remains open.
