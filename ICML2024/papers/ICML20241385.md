## Problem

CoCa's local interaction is unidirectional: its image captioning head generates text from images but provides no constraint on reconstructing visual content from text, limiting fine-grained cross-modal alignment. Additionally, image patches are often cluttered or semantically unrelated to their captions, so naive patch-level interaction introduces noise rather than meaningful alignment signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241385/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pretraining, the model consumes 224×224 RGB images (split into ViT patches) paired with BPE-tokenized text captions. The image encoder outputs a sequence of patch embeddings plus a [CLS] global token; the text encoder (causal-masked transformer) outputs token embeddings plus a [CLS] token. The three training heads produce: (i) normalized global embeddings for contrastive alignment, (ii) autoregressive text token logits for captioning, and (iii) pixel-value predictions for masked image patches guided by cross-attended text representations.

## Method

SyCoCa extends CoCa by adding a Text-Guided Masked Image Modeling (TG-MIM) head that reconstructs masked image patches using cross-attention to text embeddings, establishing bidirectional local interaction alongside the existing image-to-text captioning path. The key mechanism is an attentive masking strategy: for each image patch, the model computes the maximum inner-product similarity across all text token embeddings, then masks the top-scoring (most text-relevant) patches for TG-MIM reconstruction and the bottom-scoring (least relevant) patches for the IC captioning task, ensuring each objective focuses on complementary visual regions. The image decoder mirrors the text decoder architecture (cross-attention transformer) but replaces the token-prediction head with a pixel-prediction head following MAE's linear projection and reshape. Training jointly optimizes ITC (global contrastive), IC (autoregressive captioning), and TG-MIM (L1 pixel reconstruction) losses with weights λ_IC=2 and λ_TM=1, using 50% masking ratios for both high- and low-attention masks.

## Results

On zero-shot image-text retrieval (Flickr30K), SyCoCa achieves 46.6/76.3/83.6 R@1/5/10 for I→T and 35.9/64.6/75.6 for T→I, improving over CoCa by +5.4% and +14.7% on R@1 respectively. On MSCOCO retrieval, gains reach +14.7% (I→T R@1) and +12.4% (T→I R@1). For image captioning on MSCOCO, SyCoCa improves CIDEr from 71.1 to 75.6 (+6.3%), and on VQA it gains +9.1% on the validation split (43.0→46.9). Zero-shot classification average improves by +2.5% (41.9→42.2), though CIFAR-10 shows a −5.9% drop due to low input resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is CoCa (Yu et al., 2022), and SyCoCa outperforms it across all five evaluated tasks: retrieval (+5–15% R@1), captioning (+1–6% CIDEr), VQA (+8–9%), zero-shot classification (+2.5% avg, except CIFAR-10 at −5.9%), and fine-tuned classification (+2.7% avg). In the appendix comparison (Table 9, 930M params on LAION-2B+COYO-700M), SyCoCa achieves 89.2/99.1/99.6 R@1/5/10 on Flickr30K I→T, surpassing ALIGN (88.6/98.7/99.7) in 11 of 12 retrieval metrics despite using a smaller dataset, and matching CoCa (92.5/99.5/99.9) which uses 2.1B parameters and 3B+1.8B training pairs. On MSCOCO retrieval, SyCoCa (67.2/87.5/92.1 I→T) exceeds CoCa (66.3/86.2/91.8) and SigLIP (70.2 R@1 only reported). For captioning (Table 10), SyCoCa achieves 41.4 BLEU@4 and 143.7 CIDEr on MSCOCO, slightly trailing CoCa (40.9/143.6) on BLEU but leading on CIDEr and SPICE, and achieves the best NoCaps CIDEr (122.6) and SPICE (15.8). The CIFAR-10 regression (65.9→62.0) is attributed to the 32×32 input resolution limiting the benefit of pixel-level reconstruction, while higher-resolution STL-10 (96×96) shows a +1.2% gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

SyCoCa extends CoCa (Yu et al., 2022) by adding a text-guided masked image modeling (TG-MIM) head that reconstructs masked image patches conditioned on text, thereby symmetrizing the unidirectional image-to-text generation of CoCa's captioning objective. The reconstruction mechanism is directly inspired by MAE's asymmetric encoder-decoder paradigm, but repurposed for cross-modal guidance where text tokens serve as conditioning signals via cross-attention. The attentive masking strategy, which selects patches based on token-wise similarity scores, departs from the random masking used in prior MIM and multimodal modeling work (BEiT-v3, MaskVLM) by explicitly aligning which regions to reconstruct with the semantic content of the caption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine asymmetry in CoCa's design—image-to-text generation without a corresponding text-to-image reconstruction path—and shows that closing this gap with selective, text-guided reconstruction yields consistent improvements across retrieval, captioning, VQA, and classification. However, the practical cost is non-trivial: 30% more parameters and 67% longer training time for moderate gains, and the method actually degrades on low-resolution images (CIFAR-10), suggesting the reconstruction objective heightens sensitivity to lost detail. Open questions include whether the gains persist at the multi-billion-pair scale where CoCa was originally trained, whether lighter-weight decoder designs can reduce the training overhead, and whether the resolution sensitivity can be mitigated.
