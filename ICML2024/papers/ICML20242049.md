## Problem

For fixed-budget best-arm identification (A/B testing) with Bernoulli rewards, it was an open question whether an adaptive algorithm could match uniform sampling on all instances while strictly outperforming it on some. This paper proves the answer is negative: no algorithm can be universally better than uniform sampling, resolving two open problems posed by Qin (2022).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242049/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The learner faces K arms (K=2 for the main result, K≥3 for extensions) with unknown Bernoulli mean rewards μ_k ∈ (0,1) and a fixed budget T of sequential pulls. The output is a decision ĉ ∈ [K] identifying the best arm, with performance measured by the asymptotic error rate lim_{T→∞} T / log(1/p_{μ,T}). The analysis is instance-specific: the error rate depends on the particular μ through the KL-divergence-based function g(x,μ), and the results hold uniformly over the parameter set Λ of instances with a unique best arm.

## Method

The proof introduces the class of consistent and stable algorithms, where stability requires symmetric and continuous allocation behavior as arm means converge. The key argument proceeds in two steps: (i) any algorithm that is at least as good as uniform sampling on all instances must be consistent and stable (Theorem 3.3, proved by contradiction using a change-of-measure argument and a geometric lemma about the concavity of g(x,μ)); and (ii) every consistent and stable algorithm satisfies an instance-specific error rate lower bound that is exactly matched by uniform sampling (Theorem 4.1, proved via change-of-measure with a carefully chosen nearby instance π^{(n)} → (a,a) where a = λ(1/2,μ)). For K≥3 arms, the paper derives the exact asymptotic error rate of Successive Rejects by proving a matching lower bound to the existing upper bound, then exhibits three-armed instances where uniform sampling strictly outperforms SR.

## Results

The paper's central result (Theorem 2.2) is a negative/impossibility theorem: for two-arm bandits with Bernoulli rewards (A/B testing), no algorithm can be as good as uniform sampling on all instances while strictly outperforming it on any instance. Theorem 4.1 establishes that for the natural class of consistent and stable algorithms, the asymptotic error rate lower bound is exactly 1/g(1/2, μ), which is the performance of uniform sampling. For the K-armed case (K ≥ 3), Theorem 5.1 provides the first exact characterization of the Successive Rejects (SR) algorithm's asymptotic error rate as max_{j=2,...,K} j·loḡK / Γ_j(μ), and Theorem 5.2 shows that uniform sampling strictly outperforms SR on specific instances (e.g., μ = (0.5, 0.3, 0.3) where SR's error rate ≈ 71.1 vs. uniform's ≈ 70.36).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the uniform sampling algorithm against the Successive Rejects (SR) algorithm (Audibert et al., 2010) in the three-armed bandit setting. Using the exact error rate characterization of SR (Theorem 5.1), the authors show that uniform sampling strictly outperforms SR on certain instances. Specifically, for μ = (0.5, 0.3, 0.3), SR achieves an asymptotic error rate of approximately 71.1 while uniform sampling achieves approximately 70.36, meaning uniform sampling has a lower (better) error rate. Figure 3 in the paper maps out the full region of instances μ = (μ₁, μ₂, μ₂) with μ₁ > μ₂ where uniform sampling provably beats SR. This is surprising because SR is an adaptive algorithm that discards arms based on empirical performance, yet it cannot match the simple strategy of sampling all arms equally in these cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of the FB-BAI literature (Audibert et al., 2010; Karnin et al., 2013; Gabillon et al., 2012) and the static-algorithm analysis of Glynn & Juneja (2004), which characterized the asymptotic error rate of fixed-fraction sampling. It builds directly on Degenne (2023), who proved no algorithm matches the best static algorithm on all instances, and on Wang et al. (2023), whose large-deviation framework provided the upper bound on SR's error rate that this paper tightens to an exact characterization. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of the 'consistent and stable' algorithm class as the natural domain within which to prove a universal optimality result, shifting the question from 'can we find a better adaptive algorithm?' to 'what is the fundamental limit within any reasonable class?'.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves two open problems from Qin (2022) by proving that, for two-arm A/B testing with Bernoulli rewards, uniform sampling is universally optimal within the class of consistent and stable algorithms—meaning no adaptive algorithm can be at least as good everywhere and strictly better somewhere. This is a strong negative result that redirects research effort away from seeking universally superior adaptive algorithms in this setting. However, the results are limited to two arms and Bernoulli rewards; for K≥3 arms, only the SR algorithm is exactly characterized, and the general picture (including Gaussian rewards or unknown variances) remains open. The authors conjecture uniform sampling remains optimal for one-parameter exponential families but have not proved it.
