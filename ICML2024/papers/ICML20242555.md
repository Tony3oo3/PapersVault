## Problem

Existing neural Granger causality methods require building and training a separate predictive model for each target variable and impose sparsity constraints on first-layer weights, which limits the ability to capture complex inter-variable relationships and prevents recurrent architectures from learning full-time Granger causality. This results in computational inefficiency, scalability challenges for high-dimensional time series, and degraded estimation accuracy, particularly on nonlinear and sparse causal structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242555/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a D-dimensional multivariate time series, using past observations x_{1:D}^{t-τ:t-1} as input to a single shared multivariate forecasting model. It produces two types of causal graphs: a summary Granger causality graph (aggregated over lags) and a full-time Granger causality graph (per-lag), both derived from the learned D × Dτ input-output Jacobian matrix whose element J_{i,j,α} = ∂x_j/∂x_i^{(t-α)} quantifies the causal influence of variable i at lag α on variable j. A significance test (3-sigma rule on surrogate datasets) is applied to the Jacobian entries to threshold causal edges.

## Method

JRNGC replaces the per-variable model paradigm with a single multivariate residual MLP forecasting model that jointly predicts all D target variables from shared hidden layers. The key innovation is an input-output Jacobian matrix regularizer (either L1 norm or squared Frobenius norm) added to the MSE loss during training, which encodes the Granger causality null hypothesis without constraining first-layer weights. The Frobenius norm is computed efficiently via random projection onto a lower-dimensional space, reducing computational cost from O(g(D,τ)) to linear in D. In post-hoc analysis, the learned Jacobian matrix directly serves as the weighted causal matrix, enabling extraction of both summary and full-time Granger causality from a single trained model. The framework is architecture-agnostic and has been validated with both residual MLP and LSTM backbones.

## Results

On the CausalTime benchmark, JRNGC-F achieves the best AUROC on AQI (0.9279±0.0011) and Traffic (0.7294±0.0046), outperforming the next-best CUTS+ (0.8928, 0.6175). On VAR(100,5,10) summary causality, JRNGC-L1 reaches AUROC 1.000 and AUPRC 1.000, matching JGC but with 53× fewer parameters (110,300 vs 5,870,000). On Lorenz-96 with F=40 (highly nonlinear), JRNGC-L1 achieves AUROC 0.995 / AUPRC 0.994 for summary causality, substantially exceeding JGC (0.956 / 0.943). On fMRI (14.67% variable usage, highly sparse), JRNGC-F attains the best AUPRC (0.749) while maintaining competitive AUROC (0.898).

## Baselines & Comparisons

JRNGC-F achieves the highest AUROC on CausalTime AQI (0.9279) and Traffic (0.7294), beating CUTS+ by 3.5 and 11.2 points respectively, and LCCM by 7.1 and 17.5 points; however, LCCM edges out JRNGC-F on Medical AUROC (0.8013 vs 0.7540) and AUPRC (0.7554 vs 0.7261). On the VAR(100,5,10) summary graph, JRNGC-L1 matches JGC at AUROC 1.000 / AUPRC 1.000 but uses only 110,300 parameters versus JGC's 5,870,000 (53× fewer) and trains 71× faster per epoch (0.033 s vs 2.333 s). On Lorenz-96 with F=40, JRNGC-L1 (AUROC 0.995, AUPRC 0.994) substantially outperforms JGC (0.956, 0.943), cMLP (0.984, 0.968), and CR-VAE (0.654, 0.493), demonstrating the advantage of the Jacobian regularizer under strong nonlinearity. On fMRI, JRNGC-F attains the best AUPRC (0.749) among all methods, surpassing JGC (0.536) by 21.3 points, though JGC retains a slight AUROC edge (0.912 vs 0.898). On DREAM-3, JRNGC-F achieves the best AUROC across all five sub-datasets (e.g., 0.678 on E.coli-2 vs 0.666 for eSRU), confirming robustness on sparse, short gene-expression series.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant neural Granger causality paradigm established by Tank et al. (2021), which requires training a separate predictive model per target variable and enforcing sparsity on first-layer weights. It also extends Suryadi et al. (2023, JGC), which uses the input-output Jacobian matrix only in post-hoc analysis, by additionally incorporating the Jacobian as a training-time regularizer. The efficient computation of the squared Frobenius norm via random projection is adapted from Hoffman et al. (2019), and the residual MLP backbone draws on recent time-series forecasting architectures (Das et al., 2023; Zeng et al., 2023). The key conceptual shift is replacing per-variable first-layer sparsity with a global Jacobian constraint on a single shared multivariate model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling a single shared model to learn multivariate Granger causality, JRNGC reduces parameter count by roughly a factor of D (e.g., 110K vs. 5.87M for JGC on VAR-100) while matching or exceeding accuracy, and it unlocks full-time Granger causality estimation with RNNs/LSTMs that was previously infeasible under first-layer sparsity. The finding that the Jacobian regularizer simultaneously improves forecasting generalization (lower test MSE) suggests a useful coupling between causal structure learning and predictive performance. Open questions include scalability to very high-dimensional settings (D > 100) where L1-norm Jacobian computation becomes expensive, and whether the approach extends to non-stationary or irregularly sampled time series beyond the CausalTime benchmark.
