## Problem

Sample-based inference (SBI) for Bayesian neural networks is widely perceived as computationally infeasible, yet the specific barriers—posterior mode connectivity, the 'dying sampler' phenomenon with unbounded activations, and the failure of classical convergence diagnostics—have not been systematically characterized. The paper addresses this gap by uncovering how overparameterization, prior choice, and network depth jointly determine whether MCMC samplers can traverse the multimodal posterior landscape in finite time.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular regression data (UCI benchmarks with 20% held-out test splits) and an MLP architecture specification (up to 7 layers, 64 hidden units), along with a weight prior (Gaussian or Laplace). For the proposed DEI-MCMC variant, it additionally requires M pre-trained network weight sets obtained from standard non-Bayesian optimization as warm-start initializations. Outputs are posterior weight samples from up to 12 independent NUTS chains (8,000 samples each for smaller datasets), from which Bayesian model averaging yields point predictions (RMSE), calibrated predictive credibility intervals, and log-posterior-predictive-density (LPPD) scores for uncertainty assessment.

## Method

The paper's core empirical finding is that posterior mode connectivity increases with layer depth: first and last layers exhibit concentrated, disconnected modes, while intermediate layers become progressively more diffuse and traversable due to greater overparameterization. Building on this, the authors propose Deep Ensemble Initialized MCMC (DEI-MCMC), which trains M networks via standard optimization (e.g., Adam) and uses the resulting weight sets as MCMC starting points, thereby avoiding the 'dying sampler' problem where ReLU-activated chains get trapped in zero-probability regions. For convergence monitoring, they introduce a chain-wise rank-normalized split diagnostic (cR^(κ)) that avoids the false convergence signal produced by aggregating across layers with heterogeneous variances, and a cumulative LPPD criterion for function-space convergence that does not penalize legitimate between-chain differences arising from visiting distinct posterior modes. With DEI-MCMC, a 100-step warmup (100× shorter than cold-start) suffices, and as few as 10 samples per chain already outperform deep ensembles on LPPD, while 1,000 samples per chain achieve competitive RMSE across all benchmark datasets.

## Results

On six UCI regression benchmarks (Airfoil, Bike Sharing, Concrete, Energy, Protein, Yacht), DEI-MCMC with ReLU activation and two hidden layers of 16 neurons achieves the best RMSE across all datasets, e.g., 0.21 on Airfoil, 0.25 on Bike Sharing, and 0.03 on Yacht, consistently outperforming the Deep Ensemble (DE) baseline. For uncertainty quantification, DEI-MCMC attains the highest LPPD on 5 of 6 datasets (e.g., 0.58 on Airfoil, 3.08 on Yacht), surpassing DE even with as few as 10 posterior samples. Performance improves from 10 to 1,000 samples with diminishing returns beyond 1,000, and the method requires only 100 warmup steps (vs. 10,000 for cold-started BNNs) to reach competitive performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the Deep Ensemble (DE; Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017), which shares the same inductive biases (architecture, activation, number of members) as the BNN. DEI-MCMC outperforms DE on RMSE for all six datasets, with the largest gains on Airfoil (0.21 vs. 0.24), Bike Sharing (0.25 vs. 0.30), and Protein (0.70 vs. 0.77). On LPPD, DEI-MCMC surpasses DE on 5 of 6 datasets (Airfoil: 0.58 vs. 0.19; Yacht: 3.08 vs. 2.55; Bike Sharing: 0.63 vs. 0.42), with the sole exception being Energy where DE slightly edges out OURS(10) at 1.93 vs. 1.91, though OURS(1000) recovers the lead at 2.06. Notably, even with only 10 posterior samples, DEI-MCMC already matches or exceeds DE on most metrics, confirming that the ensemble initialization provides most of the benefit and additional sampling yields diminishing returns. The method also eliminates the 'dying sampler' problem that causes standard cold-started ReLU BNNs to fail, as no chain performs worse than the linear model baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) mode connectivity in loss landscapes (Garipov et al., 2018; Draxler et al., 2018), which the authors transpose to the Bayesian posterior setting; (2) BNN symmetry and overparameterization research (Wiese et al., 2023; Chen et al., 1993), previously studied almost exclusively from an ERM/optimization perspective; and (3) SBI methods for BNNs (Izmailov et al., 2021; Farquhar et al., 2020), which the authors argue have been under-analyzed regarding their interaction with posterior multimodality. The proposed DEI-MCMC repurposes deep ensembles (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017) as a warm-start mechanism for MCMC, and the convergence diagnostics extend the rank-normalized split-R̂ framework (Vehtari et al., 2021) to handle BNN-specific heterogeneity across layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic empirical characterization of how overparameterization, activation function choice, and network depth jointly affect SBI feasibility in BNNs, yielding actionable guidelines (multi-chain NUTS, layer-wise diagnostics, bounded activations) that practitioners can adopt immediately. DEI-MCMC is attractive as a modular, post-optimization tool that converts any trained ensemble into a BNN with minimal additional compute (100 warmup steps vs. 10,000). However, the scope is limited to UCI-scale regression with full-batch sampling, leaving open whether these findings and the DEI-MCMC approach transfer to large-scale classification, SG-MCMC settings, or out-of-distribution detection tasks. The layer-wise mode connectedness insight also opens a natural direction for layerwise inference methods that the authors flag but do not pursue.
