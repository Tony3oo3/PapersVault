## Problem

Existing LLM-based MLLMs either rely on pretrained vision encoders that cap visual understanding, or employ contiguous autoregressive image modeling that couples vision and language into a single system with an infinite label space. The paper asks how to build a vision system on a frozen LLM that simultaneously retains unique visual information and supports effective cross-modal interaction, without distorting the LLM's preexisting language knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242283/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 336×336 RGB images (processed by a frozen CLIP-ViT-L-336px encoder with 14×14 patches, yielding 578 image tokens) and text instructions as input. Each image is represented by a hybrid of contiguous CLIP features and discrete vision embeddings constructed from two concatenated LFQ codebooks (each of size 2^9, total vocabulary 2^18). The primary output is a text response (VQA answer, caption, or instruction-following reply); the model also supports text-to-image generation as a diagnostic of the learned image distribution.

## Method

Libra inserts a routed visual expert into each layer of a frozen LLaMA2-7B-Chat, adding vision-specific attention and FFN parameters (low-rank factorized) alongside the original language layers, forming a modality-routed MoE structure. A cross-modal bridge module applies learnable low-rank (rank-8) residual projections to keys and values only when queries and keys originate from different modalities, thereby decoupling inner-modal modeling from cross-modal interaction and preventing the visual expert from collapsing into language-aligned representations. Images are tokenized via a CLIP-based VQGAN with lookup-free quantization (the first highly reconstructive tokenizer built on a frozen CLIP encoder), and the model is trained with discrete next-token prediction on both vision and language tokens. Pretraining uses 50M image-text pairs with only the visual expert updated; instruction tuning on 665K LLaVA-Instruct samples unfreezes the full model.

## Results

Libra (11.3B params, 50M pretraining data) achieves 59.5 VQA Score on VizWiz, 73.5% on SQA, and 135.2 CIDEr on COCO captioning, surpassing Qwen-VL (9.6B, 1.4B data) by +20.6% on VizWiz despite using only 4% of the pretraining data. On MLLM-oriented benchmarks, Libra attains the best scores among open models on POPE (88.2), MMB (65.2), MMB^CN (58.8), SEED (62.7), MM-Vet (31.8), and MMVP (30.0), while remaining competitive with GPT-4V and Gemini-Pro. On general VQA, Libra scores 77.3 on VQAv2, 59.7 on OKVQA, and 63.8 on GQA, rivaling LLaVA1.5 (13.4B, 558K data) which uses the same instruction tuning data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qwen-VL (9.6B, 1.4B pretraining data), Libra achieves a striking +20.6% improvement on VizWiz (59.5 vs. 38.9) and +5.3% on SQA (73.5 vs. 68.2) while using only 4% of the pretraining data; Libra also leads on NoCaps (+3.6 CIDEr) and Flickr (+5.6 CIDEr). Against LLaVA1.5 (13.4B, 558K data, same instruction tuning data), Libra wins on VizWiz (+5.9), SQA (+1.9), and COCO captioning (+5.4 CIDEr), but trails on VQAv2 (77.3 vs. 80.0) and MME (1494.7 vs. 1510.7). Against the specialist CogVLM (17B, 1.5B data), Libra is consistently behind on VQA (e.g., 59.7 vs. 64.7 on OKVQA, 59.5 vs. 75.8 on VizWiz), reflecting the 30× data gap. On MLLM benchmarks (Table 2), Libra outperforms LLaVA1.5 on POPE (+2.3), MMB (+0.9), SEED (+4.1), MM-Vet (+1.3), and MMVP (+5.3), and surpasses Qwen-VL on MMB (+4.6), SEED (+4.5), and MMVP (30.0 vs. not reported), while Qwen-VL leads on MME^C (360.7 vs. 281.1). Libra remains behind GPT-4V and Gemini-Pro on MMB (65.2 vs. 77.0/73.6), SEED (62.7 vs. 71.6/70.7), and MMVP (30.0 vs. 38.7/40.7), but is competitive on POPE and MME.

## Relation to Prior Work

Libra extends the visual-expert-on-frozen-LLM paradigm introduced by CogVLM by adding a cross-modal bridge module that explicitly decouples inner-modal modeling from cross-modal interaction during attention computation. It also repurposes lookup-free quantization (LFQ) from the visual generation literature to construct the first highly reconstructive image tokenizer built on a frozen CLIP encoder, departing from both the VQGAN-based tokenizers of Emu and the simple projection layers of LLaVA/BLIP-2. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against two prior paradigms: unified from-scratch models (Unified-IO, OFA) that sacrifice language generality, and contiguous autoregressive image modeling (Emu, CogVLM) that the authors argue provides limited benefit for image-to-text comprehension.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled vision system offers a concrete design principle for MLLMs: vision and language should maintain separate parameter spaces with a controlled interaction channel, rather than being forced into a single coupled architecture. The finding that discrete autoregressive image modeling substantially outperforms contiguous modeling (which performs comparably to no image modeling at all) for downstream comprehension challenges the narrative established by Emu and CogVLM. However, Libra remains a prototype (11.3B params, 50M data) that does not achieve SOTA, the routed visual expert design lacks FlashAttention support limiting practical deployment, and the findings are validated on a single architecture, leaving open whether the decoupling principle generalizes across model scales and LLM backbones.
