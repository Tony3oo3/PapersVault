## Problem

Existing text-to-3D methods struggle to generate complex multi-object scenes with accurate spatial interactions, producing distorted geometry, multi-face artifacts, and content drift across views. Layout-based compositional approaches mitigate this but require manually designed layouts, while LLM-interpreted layouts remain imprecise and misaligned with real-world scene constraints. No prior framework provides an end-to-end pipeline that automatically derives scene structure from text, generates high-fidelity multi-object 3D content, and supports interactive editing.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt describing a scene (e.g., 'A kitchen with a stove, range hood, refrigerator, tile floor, and microwave on the table') and produces a 3D scene represented as a collection of layout-guided 3D Gaussians, where each object instance is a set of anisotropic Gaussians parameterized by center, color, opacity, and covariance, organized under a layout bounding box with position, scale, and rotation. The LLM (GPT-3.5) parses the prompt into discrete instances and their spatial relationships to initialize the layout. The framework also accepts conversational editing instructions (add/remove/move/rotate objects) that are translated into layout transformations for local re-optimization.

## Method

GALA3D introduces a Layout-guided Gaussian Representation where each scene object is a set of 3D Gaussians constrained within a layout bounding box, with an Adaptive Geometry Control module that enforces a folded-normal spatial distribution around the layout center and a shape regularization term to suppress elongated ellipsoids. For optimization, a two-stage compositional strategy is used: MVDream multi-view diffusion with SDS optimizes each instance Gaussian independently, while a fine-tuned ControlNet conditioned on rendered layout images performs scene-level SDS to enforce inter-object consistency and interactions. A Layout Refinement module backpropagates diffusion gradients through the layout parameters (position, scale, rotation, opacity) to iteratively correct the coarse LLM-generated layout during training. The total loss jointly optimizes instance SDS, a Manhattan-distance layout boundary loss, layout refinement, global scene SDS, and geometry regularization, with all components sharing a synchronized diffusion timestep.

## Results

GALA3D achieves an average CLIP Score of 34.573 across 22 test scenes (ranging from 1 to 10 objects), outperforming the best 3DGS-based baseline LucidDreamer (31.174) by 3.4 points and the best NeRF-based baseline MVDream (30.856) by 3.7 points. On the most challenging multi-object case (10 objects, Case 4), GALA3D scores 35.052 versus 26.895 for LucidDreamer and 27.417 for MVDream. In the user study, GALA3D receives the highest mean ratings across all four dimensions: Scene Quality (8.42), Geometric Fidelity (8.37), Text Alignment (8.55), and Scene Consistency (9.68), compared to the next-best method Set-the-scene (6.36, 5.03, 7.12, 6.12 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GALA3D outperforms all nine baselines on average CLIP Score, with the largest margins over compositional NeRF methods (Set-the-scene: +4.9) and NeRF-based methods (Latent-NeRF: +6.8, ProlificDreamer: +6.2). Among 3DGS-based competitors, GALA3D leads LucidDreamer by 3.4 points on average and by 8.2 points on the 10-object case, indicating that the advantage grows with scene complexity. Notably, GALA3D uses only a text prompt (T) as input, whereas Set-the-scene requires a manually specified layout (TL), yet GALA3D still surpasses it by nearly 5 points on average. In the user study with 125 participants, GALA3D dominates all four evaluation dimensions, with Scene Consistency (9.68) showing the largest gap over the runner-up Set-the-scene (6.12). The ablation in Table 3 confirms that removing Adaptive Geometry Control (−2.4), Compositional Optimization (−2.7), or Layout Refinement (−0.6) each degrades performance, validating the contribution of each module.

## Relation to Prior Work

GALA3D sits at the intersection of three research threads: 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) as the geometric representation, Score Distillation Sampling with multi-view diffusion priors (DreamFusion, MVDream) as the optimization backbone, and LLM-based layout interpretation (LI3D, SceneWiz3D) for scene structuring. It departs from prior compositional NeRF methods (Set-the-scene, CompoNeRF, Comp3D) by replacing manually designed layouts with LLM-generated coarse layouts that are iteratively refined during optimization, and by substituting the implicit NeRF representation with a layout-guided 3DGS that admits explicit geometric constraints. The key architectural departure from object-centric 3DGS text-to-3D methods (DreamGaussian, GaussianDreamer, LucidDreamer) is the introduction of per-instance Gaussian collections governed by layout boundaries, combined with a two-level compositional diffusion strategy (instance-level MVDream + scene-level ControlNet) that was previously absent from the 3DGS text-to-3D literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GALA3D demonstrates that LLM-interpreted layouts, when coupled with a refinement loop, can replace the manual layout design bottleneck in compositional 3D scene generation, making the pipeline genuinely end-to-end and accessible to non-expert users. The practical impact is significant for 3D content creation workflows (interior design, game asset prototyping), and the conversational editing capability opens a path toward interactive 3D scene authoring. However, several open questions remain: the evaluation relies solely on CLIP Score without ground-truth 3D supervision, the method requires 100K Gaussians per instance (memory-intensive for large scenes), and the layout refinement is tightly coupled to the diffusion optimization loop, raising questions about convergence and scalability to scenes with many interacting objects. Whether the layout-guided 3DGS formulation generalizes beyond the tested scene complexity (up to 10 objects) is unexplored.
