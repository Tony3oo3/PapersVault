## Problem

High-dimensional Bayesian Optimization remains bottlenecked by the cost of labeled function evaluations, and existing latent-space BO methods rely solely on queried data to train their GP surrogate, leaving the vast unlabeled sample space untapped. No prior work has integrated semi-supervised learning into BO to exploit cheap unlabeled data for improving surrogate generalization under tight evaluation budgets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242357/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a black-box objective function over a D-dimensional space, a small set of initial labeled points (100–200), and a pre-trained VAE (Grammar VAE with 25-dim latent space for expressions; Junction-Tree VAE with 56-dim latent space for molecules) that encodes inputs into a lower-dimensional latent space where all BO operations occur. It outputs the global maximum found through iterative acquisition-function-guided queries, with the GP surrogate model trained jointly on labeled data and teacher-predicted pseudo labels from optimized unlabeled samples.

## Method

TSBO embeds an uncertainty-aware teacher-student loop inside each BO iteration in the VAE latent space: an MLP teacher predicts mean-and-variance pseudo labels for unlabeled points drawn from an optimized sampler, a GP student is trained on those pseudo labels with the teacher's uncertainty injected into its covariance matrix, and the student's MSE on a subset of high-value labeled data provides selective regularization back to the teacher. Two optimized unlabeled data samplers are proposed—an Extreme Value Theory (GEV) sampler that models the tail distribution of top-K labeled values, and a parameterized Gaussian sampler whose mean and variance are updated via the reparameterization trick to minimize the student feedback loss. The refined teacher's pseudo labels then augment the GP surrogate used for acquisition-function maximization, improving both global and local generalization. Training uses an alternating one-step gradient scheme (student update, then teacher update, then sampler update) per BO iteration, built on the T-LBO baseline with EI acquisition and LBFGS optimization.

## Results

After 250 new data queries, TSBO-Gaussian achieves a best Penalized LogP of 25.02±4.794, Ranolazine MPO of 0.744±0.030, and Expression distance of 0.240±0.168, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline T-LBO (5.695, 0.620, 0.572 respectively) across all three high-dimensional tasks. In the broader comparison (Table 2), TSBO with only 200 initial labeled data and 500 queries reaches a Top-1 Penalized LogP of 32.92, surpassing T-LBO (29.06) and LOL-BO (N/A) while using up to 364.2× fewer initial labeled data. On the Expression task (Table 3), TSBO achieves 0.240±0.168 with just 100 initial data and 250 queries, beating PG-LBO (0.358±0.195) and W-LBO (0.386±0.016) under the same budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSBO-Gaussian dominates all seven baselines on every task. Against the direct baseline T-LBO (Grosnit et al., 2021), TSBO-Gaussian improves Penalized LogP by 4.4× (25.02 vs. 5.695), Expression distance by 2.4× (0.240 vs. 0.572), and Ranolazine MPO by 20% (0.744 vs. 0.620). W-LBO (Tripp et al., 2020) is the strongest non-TSBO method on Expression (0.475) and Ranolazine MPO (0.633) but is still outperformed by TSBO-Gaussian on all three tasks. LS-BO (Gómez-Bombarelli et al., 2018) and Sobol (Owen, 2003) are consistently the weakest, with Sobol achieving only 3.019 on Penalized LogP versus TSBO's 25.02. In the broader comparison (Table 2), TSBO with 200 initial data matches or exceeds methods using 250,000 initial data (e.g., T-LBO at 26.11, LOL-BO at 27.53) while achieving a higher Top-1 score of 32.92. PG-LBO (Chen et al., 2023b) is competitive on Expression (0.358) but trails TSBO-Gaussian (0.240) and is outperformed on Penalized LogP (23.23 vs. 28.04). MolDQN (Zhou et al., 2019), a reinforcement learning approach requiring ≥5,000 queries and 250,000 initial data, achieves a Top-1 of 11.84, far below TSBO's 32.92.

## Relation to Prior Work

TSBO builds directly on T-LBO (Grosnit et al., 2021), a VAE-based latent space BO method with deep metric learning, and on the foundational latent space BO framework of Gómez-Bombarelli et al. (2018). The teacher-student bi-level optimization mechanism is adapted from Meta Pseudo Labels (Pham et al., 2021) and the original pseudo-label idea (Lee et al., 2013), but repurposed here for the first time within a BO loop. The key conceptual departure from prior SSL work is the 'selective regularization' mechanism: rather than applying consistency or pseudo-label losses uniformly over all unlabeled data, the unlabeled data sampler is explicitly optimized (via EVT or a learned Gaussian) to place samples in high-value regions, aligning the SSL feedback with the BO objective of finding the global maximum.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core bottleneck in practical BO—expensive labeled evaluations—by showing that a well-regularized teacher can generate high-quality pseudo labels that substantially improve GP surrogate generalization, particularly in the local high-value region where acquisition functions operate. The 364× reduction in labeled data usage (Table 3) and the noise-robustness result (TSBO actually improves slightly under Gaussian noise) suggest the uncertainty-aware teacher-student interaction provides genuine robustness beyond mere data augmentation. Open questions include whether the heuristic diagonal-covariance uncertainty quantification scales to more complex objectives, whether the EVT-based sampler remains practical in very high-dimensional latent spaces, and whether the approach extends beyond VAE-based dimension reduction to other embedding strategies. The method is currently validated on only two datasets (ZINC250K, 40K expressions), limiting confidence in broader generalization.
