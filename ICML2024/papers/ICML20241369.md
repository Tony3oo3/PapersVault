## Problem

Orthogonal Fine-Tuning (OFT) preserves pretrained angular semantics via multiplicative orthogonal updates but suffers two critical limitations: (1) the full orthogonal matrix requires O(d²) parameters, and the block-diagonal workaround sacrifices expressiveness for parameter savings; (2) strict orthogonality freezes both vector norms and relative angles, preventing adaptation to subtle downstream semantic shifts. The paper asks whether one can achieve equivalent SO(d) expressiveness with only O(d) parameters while still allowing flexible norm and angular adjustments.

## Input / Output

The method operates on frozen linear weight matrices W ∈ R^{n×d} within pretrained transformer blocks (DeBERTaV3-base, LLaMA-2-7B, ViT-B/16). It produces updated weights W* = (∏_r P_r) W by left-multiplying with a product of sparse quasi-Givens rotation matrices, where each P_r is a block-diagonal composition of non-overlapping 2D rotations. At inference, the delta weights are merged into W, so the forward pass is identical to the original model with zero additional latency. Downstream tasks consume standard text sequences or 224×224 RGB images and produce class labels (GLUE, VTAB-1K), answer spans (SQuAD), or generated text (instruction tuning).

## Method

GOFT proves (Theorem 4.1) that any rotation in SO(d) can be decomposed into at most d−1 Givens rotations G(k−1, k; θ_k), each parameterized by a single learnable angle θ_k, reducing parameter complexity from O(d²) to O(d) with provably equivalent expressiveness. A parallel rotation strategy further reduces computation to O(log d) sparse matrix multiplications by grouping non-overlapping rotations into log d sequential stages P_r. qGOFT then replaces each strict Givens rotation with a quasi-Givens 2×2 matrix [α_i, β_i] (four learnable parameters per rotation), relaxing strict orthogonality to allow adjustable vector norms and slightly tunable relative angles. A soft orthogonality regularizer λ∑⟨α_i, β_i⟩² in the training loss keeps the quasi-rotations close to true rotations, balancing adaptation flexibility with preservation of pretrained angular structure. The total parameter budget remains O(d) (3× GOFT), and the method is a pure reparameterization tuning approach requiring no architectural changes.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark with DeBERTaV3-base, qGOFT achieves an average score of 89.35 with only 0.33M trainable parameters, outperforming all baselines at comparable budgets. GOFT matches OFT_{b=8} (88.78 avg) with an 8× parameter reduction (0.08M vs. 0.66M). On VTAB-1K with ViT-B/16, qGOFT reaches 79.4% average top-1 accuracy with 0.073M parameters, surpassing OFT_{b=8} (78.0%) and LoRA_{r=8} (74.5%). On LLaMA2-7B instruction tuning, GOFT achieves 46.36 MMLU 5-shot accuracy and 5.96 Vicuna-Eval score with only 0.007% tunable parameters, outperforming OFT_{b=16} (45.93 / 5.56) with a 16× parameter reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

qGOFT (0.33M) wins against all baselines at comparable parameter budgets on GLUE: it beats AdaLoRA (89.03) by +0.32, LoRA_{r=2} (88.33) by +1.02, and OFT_{b=4} (87.80) by +1.55 in average score. GOFT (0.08M) ties OFT_{b=8} (0.66M) at 88.78 average while using 8× fewer parameters, and outperforms FacT-TT_{r=16} (0.06M, 88.36) and FacT-TK_{r=32} (0.05M, 88.48) at similar ultra-low budgets. On VTAB-1K, qGOFT (79.4 avg) surpasses OFT_{b=8} (78.0) by +1.4 and LoRA_{r=8} (74.5) by +4.9, while GOFT (78.6) beats OFT_{b=8} by +0.6 with 8× fewer parameters. On SQuAD v1.1, qGOFT achieves 87.1 EM / 93.2 F1, outperforming LoRA_{r=2} (86.6 / 92.9) and OFT_{b=4} (85.9 / 92.6) at the same 0.33M budget. On LLaMA2-7B instruction tuning, GOFT (0.007% params) outperforms OFT_{b=16} (0.13%) by +0.43 MMLU 5-shot and +0.40 Vicuna-Eval, and beats AdaLoRA (0.25%) by +0.40 MMLU and +0.57 Vicuna-Eval; however, qGOFT slightly underperforms GOFT on this task (46.12 vs. 46.36 MMLU), which the authors attribute to the smaller pretraining-to-downstream gap in decoder-based LLMs. Full fine-tuning (184M) remains competitive on GLUE (88.27) but is outperformed by qGOFT (89.35) despite using 558× more parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Orthogonal Fine-Tuning (OFT) by Qiu et al. (2023), which applies Cayley-parameterized orthogonal matrices to pretrained linear weights to preserve angular distances in latent space. The key departure is replacing the O(d²) Cayley parameterization with a product of d−1 Givens rotations, a standard decomposition from numerical linear algebra (Press, 2007; Frerix & Bruna, 2019) that achieves equivalent expressiveness in SO(d) with only O(d) parameters. The quasi-orthogonal relaxation (qGOFT) further departs from strict OFT by allowing adjustable norms and slightly tunable angles under a soft orthogonality regularizer, addressing OFT's rigidity in adapting to downstream semantic shifts. Within the broader PEFT landscape, qGOFT sits in the reparameterization tuning family alongside LoRA and AdaLoRA but distinguishes itself through its geometric (rotation-based) rather than algebraic (low-rank additive) formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(d) parameter efficiency with provably equivalent expressiveness to full SO(d) rotations offers a practically attractive option for PEFT, especially when parameter budgets are tight and the method can be merged into weights at inference with zero overhead. However, the 3.3–3.5× training time overhead from O(log d) sparse matrix multiplications is a real cost that limits immediate adoption, and the authors acknowledge this as a key limitation. An interesting open question raised by the results is when strict orthogonality (GOFT) versus relaxed quasi-orthogonality (qGOFT) is preferable: GOFT wins on large decoder LLMs (LLaMA-2) while qGOFT wins on smaller encoder models (DeBERTa), suggesting the optimal level of flexibility depends on the pretraining-downstream gap and model scale. Future work on accelerating the sparse multiplications (e.g., via Taylor approximations or distributed CUDA kernels) could close the time gap and make the method more broadly competitive.
