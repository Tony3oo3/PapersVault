## Problem

Consistency models achieve dramatic sampling speedups over vanilla diffusion models by collapsing many backward ODE steps into one, yet no statistical theory existed to explain whether this acceleration preserves distribution estimation quality. The paper asks whether consistency models can match the Wasserstein estimation rates of standard diffusion models while enabling single-step or few-step generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240460/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the trained consistency model f_θ̂(·, T) takes a single standard Gaussian sample N(0, I) and maps it directly to a generated data point in R^d, bypassing the 500–1000 iterative denoising steps of vanilla diffusion. During training, the model consumes n i.i.d. samples from the target distribution p_data and is constrained to be R-Lipschitz in x at every time t ∈ [ε, T] with the boundary condition f(·, ε) = id, ensuring the output at the early-stopping time equals the input.

## Method

The paper formulates consistency model training as a Wasserstein-1 distance minimization over pushforward distributions: the objective sums W1(f_θ(·, τ_k)_# X_{τ_k}, f_θ(·, τ_{k-1})_# X_{τ_{k-1}}) across coarse time steps, enforcing that the network maps all points along a probability-flow ODE trajectory to the same endpoint. Two training variants are analyzed: distillation, which uses a pretrained score network s_φ to perform M-step ODE updates between coarse time steps, and isolation, which replaces s_φ with the explicit empirical score ∇log p̂_t derived from Tweedie's formula over the dataset. The key technical contribution is bounding the statistical error W1(f_θ̂(·,T)_# N(0,I), p_data) by decomposing it into forward-process convergence, ODE discretization, score estimation, concentration, and early-stopping terms, yielding Õ(n^{-1/(2(d+5))}) for distillation and Õ(n^{-1/d}) for isolation—matching vanilla diffusion model rates under Gaussian-tail and Lipschitz-score assumptions.

## Results

The paper establishes two main statistical estimation rate bounds for consistency models in terms of the Wasserstein-1 distance. For consistency distillation (Theorem 4.1), with proper hyperparameter choices (β̄,β̲ ∝ 1/(d log n), T = (log n)³, M = d²n^{1/(d+5)}, N' = log n), the bound is Õ(n^{-1/(2(d+5))}), matching the rate of vanilla diffusion models. For consistency isolation (Theorem 4.2), under a bounded support assumption, the bound is Õ(n^{-1/d}). The distillation rate is dominated by the score estimation error term Rβ̄√d·ε_score·√(TN'/ε), confirming that consistency models preserve the distribution estimation ability of standard diffusion models while enabling single-step sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments, datasets, or baseline comparisons. The 'comparison' is to the statistical estimation rates of vanilla diffusion models: the distillation rate Õ(n^{-1/(2(d+5))}) matches the rate established by Chen et al. (2023a) for standard diffusion models, demonstrating that consistency models do not sacrifice distribution estimation quality for speed. The isolation rate Õ(n^{-1/d}) is not directly comparable to the distillation rate due to the distinct training procedure (no pretrained score model). The paper also contrasts with Lyu et al. (2023), which assumes accurate score estimation and multi-step sampler, whereas this work provides sample complexity bounds for ensuring those estimation errors are small without such assumptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first statistical theory for consistency models (Song et al., 2023), building on the SDE/probability-flow-ODE framework of Song et al. (2020c) and the score approximation bounds of Chen et al. (2023a). It departs from the concurrent sampling theory of Lyu et al. (2023), which assumed an accurately estimated score function and multi-step sampler, by instead deriving sample complexity bounds that account for score estimation error. The key conceptual move is reformulating the originally heuristic iterative training procedure of Song et al. (2023) as a principled Wasserstein-1 distance minimization over distribution space, which enables the statistical analysis while encompassing the practical l2-based losses used in practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that consistency models preserve the same Wasserstein estimation rate as vanilla diffusion models (O~(n^{-1/(2(d+5))}) for distillation, O~(n^{-1/d}) for isolation) formally justifies the empirical observation that fast sampling does not sacrifice generation quality. This fills a notable theoretical gap for a method now widely deployed in practice (e.g., LCM-LoRA). Limitations include the nonparametric rate's exponential dependence on dimension d, the need for Gaussian tail or bounded support assumptions, and the fact that the analysis assumes global optimality of the training objective. Open questions include extending the theory to latent consistency models, conditional settings, and understanding whether the isolation rate can be improved under structural assumptions on the data.
