## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods are largely designed for vision tasks and struggle with the heterogeneous feature types (numeric, categorical, boolean) and complex inter-feature dependencies inherent in tabular data. No prior method explicitly exploits the structure of learned decision rules to separate what should remain invariant (logical rule structure) from what must adapt (numeric thresholds and rule weights) under distribution shift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241724/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TabLog takes a source-trained rule ensemble (a logical neural network with learned bin thresholds and rule weights) and a batch of unlabeled target-domain tabular samples as input. Numeric features are discretized into bins via learnable thresholds, while categorical and boolean features are one-hot encoded; all are treated as logical atoms. The model outputs a class label and per-class probabilities obtained by softmax over a weighted aggregation of K rule votes, where each rule's activation is a differentiable approximation of a logical formula over the atoms.

## Method

TabLog represents the classifier as an ensemble of propositional rules (Antecedent → Consequent) implemented via a logical neural network with differentiable Conj and Disj modules that approximate AND/OR connectives. Numeric features are mapped to atoms through learnable bin thresholds using sigmoid-based indicator functions, making the entire pipeline end-to-end differentiable. The key assumption is that the logical structure (which atoms appear in which rules, and which connectives join them) is invariant under distribution shift, while only the bin thresholds and rule voting weights are updated during test-time adaptation. A novel binning-informed contrastive loss (InfoNCE) generates positive pairs by perturbing features within the same quantile bin and negative pairs across different bins, encouraging the rule activations to be robust to minor input variations. During TTA, the model minimizes an entropy loss plus this contrastive loss on unlabeled target data, updating only thresholds and rule weights while freezing the logical structure.

## Results

On the four TableShift benchmark datasets with natural distribution shifts, TabLog achieves the best accuracy and Macro-F1 across all datasets: 62.64% accuracy / 60.96% Macro-F1 on ASSISTments, 98.78% / 49.70% on Sepsis, 62.92% / 62.81% on Hospital Readmission, and 89.54% / 48.03% on PhysioNet. The closest competitor, TAST with FT-Transformer, trails by 1.63 points on ASSISTments (61.01% vs 62.64%) and 1.15 points on Hospital Readmission (61.17% vs 62.92%). On simulated distribution-shift datasets (Airbnb, Channel, Jigsaw, Wine), TabLog also achieves the best or near-best performance across Gaussian noise, uniform noise, and random feature perturbation shifts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TabLog outperforms all eight baselines on all four TableShift datasets. Against the strongest supervised baseline XGBoost, TabLog gains +4.46 accuracy on ASSISTments (62.64 vs 58.18), +6.32 on Sepsis (98.78 vs 92.46), +0.87 on Hospital Readmission (62.92 vs 62.05), and +3.37 on PhysioNet (89.54 vs 86.17). Among target-domain-agnostic methods (VIME, TransTab, SCARF), TabLog leads by 15–55 accuracy points, indicating that pretraining for shift-invariance without seeing target data is far less effective than explicit test-time adaptation. Among TTA methods, the FT-Transformer variants consistently outperform MLP variants (e.g., TAST FT-trans 61.01 vs TAST MLP 43.72 on ASSISTments), and TabLog still surpasses the best TTA baseline TAST (FT-trans) by 1.63 accuracy on ASSISTments, 6.32 on Sepsis, 1.75 on Hospital Readmission, and 1.18 on PhysioNet. The largest margin is on Sepsis, where TabLog's 98.78% accuracy dwarfs the 92.46% of XGBoost/TAST and the 55.14% of TENT (FT-trans), suggesting the rule-ensemble structure is particularly effective when the target shift is moderate and the logical structure is well-preserved.

## Relation to Prior Work

TabLog builds on two distinct lines of work: the differentiable logical neural networks of Riegel et al. (2020), which provide a neural-network parameterization of propositional logic (conjunction and disjunction as learnable weighted sums), and the test-time training (TTT) paradigm of Liang et al. (2020), which adapts a source-trained model to an unlabeled target domain via entropy and self-supervised losses. The key departure from existing TTA methods (TENT, EATA, SHOT, TAST) is that those approaches are designed for vision and are ported to tabular data by swapping in MLP or FT-Transformer backbones, whereas TabLog instead preserves the logical structure of a learned rule ensemble and adapts only the discretization thresholds and rule weights. It also differs from tabular-specific representation-learning methods (VIME, TransTab, SCARF) that seek a shift-invariant embedding; TabLog's invariance assumption is structural (the rule topology is fixed) rather than representational.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Test-time adaptation for tabular data has been largely neglected despite tabular data's ubiquity in clinical, financial, and industrial settings, and this paper provides the first end-to-end, interpretable framework that consistently outperforms both vision-derived TTA methods and tabular-specific baselines across natural and simulated distribution shifts. The rule-based formulation offers a degree of interpretability (human-readable antecedents with explicit thresholds) that black-box TTA methods lack, which is valuable in high-stakes domains like clinical diagnosis. Open questions remain: the method assumes covariate shift only (no label shift), does not handle the emergence of new features, and has not been tested on tabular time-series or longitudinal data. Whether the logical-structure-invariance assumption holds under more severe or non-linear distribution shifts is an important direction for future work.
