## Problem

Existing model-based offline RL methods (e.g., MOPO, LOMPO, Offline DV2) estimate model uncertainty over the full latent state, conflating task-relevant dynamics with task-irrelevant distractors such as moving backgrounds in visual observations. This conflation inflates the uncertainty penalty, making learned policies overly conservative and degrading performance, especially when data is collected by sub-optimal or random policies. The paper asks whether separating endogenous (action-controllable) from exogenous (uncontrollable) dynamics can yield a tighter performance bound and a more accurate uncertainty signal for offline policy optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes fixed offline datasets of 64×64×3 RGB image observations (with moving background distractors from the Kinetics 'driving car' category), paired actions, and scalar rewards, collected by random, medium-replay, or medium-performance policies. It produces a policy π(a|s⁺) that maps endogenous latent states to actions, along with a value function V(s⁺) trained on imagined endogenous trajectories. The key structural detail is that the policy and value network operate exclusively on the endogenous state s⁺, never on the full latent state z or the exogenous state s⁻, which is used only for observation reconstruction during model training.

## Method

SeMOPO decomposes the latent state into endogenous s⁺ and exogenous s⁻ components under the EX-BMDP assumption, where transitions factor as T(s⁺'|s⁺,a)·T(s⁻'|s⁻). A conservative sampling strategy trains the separated model trajectory-by-trajectory (one behavior policy's data per epoch) rather than mixing all trajectories, which Theorem 3.4 shows yields higher log-likelihood and helps the model attribute action-dependent transitions to s⁺ and action-independent transitions to s⁻. Model uncertainty is estimated via ensemble disagreement over K endogenous dynamics models only, and this uncertainty penalizes the reward during policy optimization in the endogenous state space (following the MOPO/DreamerV2 pipeline). The paper proves that the resulting performance lower bound η_M(π̃) ≥ sup_π{η_M(π) − 2λ·ε_{ũ}(π)} is tighter than the analogous POMDP-based bound because the endogenous uncertainty is strictly smaller than the full-latent uncertainty when distractors carry non-trivial dynamics.

## Results

On the LQV-D4RL benchmark (9 tasks across 5 environments with moving background distractors), SeMOPO achieves the highest normalized test return in 7 out of 9 settings, with particularly large margins on random and medium_replay datasets (e.g., 0.77 vs. 0.27 for Offline DV2 on Walker Walk random; 1.24 vs. 0.81 for DrQ+BC on Hopper Hop medium). On medium datasets where the behavior policy is already competent, BC-based methods (BC, DrQ+BC) remain competitive, achieving 0.69 on Walker Walk medium vs. SeMOPO's 0.45. On the V-D4RL benchmark (no distractors), SeMOPO outperforms baselines on random datasets (e.g., 305 vs. 287 for Offline DV2 on Walker Walk random) but is comparable or slightly behind on medium datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeMOPO substantially outperforms both model-based baselines (Offline DV2 and LOMPO) across nearly all settings, with the largest gaps on random and medium_replay datasets where distribution shift is most severe (e.g., 0.77 vs. 0.27 on Walker Walk random; 1.24 vs. 0.02 on Hopper Hop medium). Against model-free baselines, SeMOPO dominates on random and medrep datasets but is outperformed by BC and DrQ+BC on medium datasets where the behavior policy is already competent (e.g., BC achieves 0.69 vs. SeMOPO's 0.45 on Walker Walk medium). InfoGating is the closest competitor, matching or exceeding SeMOPO on Cheetah Run medrep (0.66 vs. 0.64) and Hopper Hop random (0.79 vs. 0.68), but SeMOPO maintains a clear advantage on the majority of tasks. DrQ+CQL consistently fails (near-zero returns) across all tasks, indicating that conservative Q-learning does not transfer well to high-dimensional visual inputs with distractors. On the V-D4RL benchmark without distractors, SeMOPO's advantage narrows, confirming that its primary benefit comes from handling noisy observations rather than general offline RL improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeMOPO extends the MOPO framework (uncertainty-penalized reward for offline model-based RL) and the DreamerV2 RSSM architecture by introducing an explicit endogenous/exogenous state decomposition under the EX-BMDP assumption from Efroni et al. (2021). The key departure from prior offline visual RL methods (LOMPO, Offline DV2) is that they estimate model uncertainty over the full latent state, conflating task-relevant dynamics with task-irrelevant distractor dynamics; SeMOPO instead penalizes only endogenous-state uncertainty, yielding a tighter theoretical performance bound. The conservative sampling strategy—sequentially training on trajectories from individual behavior policies rather than random sampling from the mixture—repurposes the likelihood decomposition of the EX-BMDP to help the model separate the two state components, a mechanism absent from prior offline RL sampling strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LQV-D4RL benchmark fills a practical gap: prior offline visual RL benchmarks (V-D4RL) do not include moving distractors or explicitly model the non-expert policy collection setting, making it difficult to evaluate methods under realistic data-quality conditions. The finding that model-based methods (Offline DV2, LOMPO) can underperform simple behavioral cloning when uncertainty is biased by distractors is a cautionary result for the field, suggesting that the standard uncertainty-penalty recipe is not universally beneficial. The main open limitation is the independence assumption between endogenous and exogenous transitions, which may not hold in real-world tasks where distractors interact with the agent's state; the complete failure on Humanoid Walk (21-dim action space) also signals that the approach has not yet scaled to very high-dimensional control.
