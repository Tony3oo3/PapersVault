## Problem

While alignment algorithms like DPO effectively suppress undesirable behaviors such as toxicity, the internal mechanisms by which models become 'aligned' remain unexplained. This mechanistic gap makes it difficult to understand why aligned models can be surprisingly easily jailbroken or un-aligned, and limits our ability to design more robust alignment procedures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241052/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained language models (GPT2-medium, Llama2-7b) and a carefully crafted set of 24,576 pairwise toxic/non-toxic continuations generated via PPLM on Wikitext-2 prompts, and produces aligned models along with extracted toxic vectors (MLP value vectors and SVD-decomposed singular vectors) and their activation regions. Evaluation uses 1,199 challenge prompts from REALTOXICITYPROMPTS scored via the Perspective API for toxicity, Wikitext-2 for perplexity, and 2,000 Wikipedia sentences for F1, ensuring interventions preserve generation quality.

## Method

The paper first identifies toxic vectors by training a linear probe on the Jigsaw dataset, finding MLP value vectors with high cosine similarity to the probe, and applying SVD to decompose them into basis vectors spanning the toxicity representation space. After applying DPO, the key finding is that toxic vectors are not removed—every parameter shifts by less than 1e-5 in norm—yet toxicity is suppressed. For GPT2, DPO learns a distributed offset across many early-layer value vectors that shifts the residual stream out of toxic activation regions γ(MLP.k_Toxic), while for Llama2, the GLU gating mechanism σ(W₁x) is used to 'turn off' toxic vectors. This mechanistic understanding enables a simple un-alignment: scaling as few as 7 toxic key vectors by 10× in GPT2_DPO, or setting 8 gate values to 1 in Llama2_DPO, fully reverts the model to its pre-aligned toxic behavior.

## Results

On GPT2, DPO reduces toxicity from 0.453 to 0.208 (a 54% reduction) while maintaining comparable perplexity (23.34 vs. 21.7) and F1 (0.195 vs. 0.193) on Wikitext-2. On Llama2, DPO reduces toxicity from 0.359 to 0.138 (a 61% reduction) with PPL of 6.587. Critically, the paper shows that every parameter in the aligned model has cosine similarity >0.99 with its pre-aligned counterpart and norm difference <1e-5, meaning toxic vectors are not removed but merely bypassed. Scaling just 7 toxic key vectors by 10× in GPT2_DPO restores toxicity to 0.458 (near the original 0.453), and turning on 8 gate components in Llama2_DPO restores toxicity to 0.217.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a mechanistic interpretability study rather than a method comparison, so there are no external baselines. The paper's internal comparison is between direct vector interventions (subtracting W_Toxic, individual MLP value vectors, or SVD-decomposed toxic vectors from the residual stream) and DPO as the alignment procedure. DPO achieves the lowest toxicity (0.208 for GPT2, 0.138 for Llama2) compared to subtracting the full toxic probe (0.245/0.256) or individual value vectors (0.305/0.171), while preserving generation quality. The key mechanistic finding is that DPO's toxicity reduction is fragile: scaling 7 key vectors by 10× in GPT2_DPO or activating 8 gate components in Llama2_DPO fully restores pre-alignment toxicity levels, demonstrating that the alignment is a distributed offset rather than a removal of toxic capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of mechanistic interpretability and alignment research, building directly on Geva et al.'s (2022) key-value memory framework for interpreting MLP value vectors and Rafailov et al.'s (2023) DPO algorithm. It extends the line of work on how fine-tuning alters model mechanisms (Jain et al., 2023) from synthetic tasks to a natural-language RLHF setting, and provides the mechanistic underpinning that prior empirical jailbreak studies (Wei et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2023b) had only hypothesized. The use of PPLM (Dathathri et al., 2019) to construct preference data is a practical repurposing of controlled generation for alignment data construction rather than a methodological contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that DPO preserves toxic vectors nearly intact (cosine similarity > 0.99) while learning a distributed offset to bypass them provides a concrete mechanistic explanation for why aligned models are vulnerable to jailbreaks, shifting the design question from 'how do we suppress toxic outputs' to 'how do we eliminate the underlying regions rather than route around them.' The KL-divergence hypothesis—that the regularization term forces minimal distributed changes rather than localized edits—opens a promising direction for more robust alignment. Limitations include evaluation on only two relatively small models (GPT2-medium, Llama2-7b) and a single undesirable behavior (toxicity), leaving open whether the bypass mechanism generalizes to other alignment targets or scales to larger models.
