## Problem

Autoregressive models trained on chaotic dynamical systems suffer from trajectory blow-up or convergence to incorrect attractors over long rollouts, because standard trajectory-matching losses (1-step MSE, curriculum, pushforward) overfit short-term dynamics and fail to preserve the system's long-term statistical behavior. The paper asks how to incorporate knowledge of a system's invariant measure into the learning stage so that models remain point-wise accurate short-term and statistically accurate long-term.

## Input / Output

The method takes a state vector (3D for Lorenz 63, 512-point 1D spatial field for Kuramoto-Sivashinsky, 64×64 2D spatial field for Kolmogorov Flow) at time t as input and produces the next state at t+Δt via an autoregressive neural network (MLP or dilated convolutional network). Training data consists of trajectories whose initial conditions are sampled from the invariant measure supported on the attractor, with a warm-up period to ensure ergodic sampling. The regularization term additionally requires evolving both true and learned dynamics over ℓ steps to compare the resulting state distributions.

## Method

DySLIM augments any existing dynamical-systems learning objective (1-step, curriculum, or pushforward) with an MMD-based measure-matching regularization that penalizes the discrepancy between the true invariant measure μ* and the invariant measure μ*_θ of the learned dynamics S_θ. The invariant measure is approximated by time-unrolling samples ℓ steps under S_θ, and the MMD is computed using a mixture of rational quadratic kernels with system-specific bandwidths. Two equivalent regularization variants are introduced: an unconditional term comparing initial-condition samples to model-evolved samples, and a conditional term comparing true-dynamics-evolved to model-dynamics-evolved samples, with the conditional variant being more informative in high dimensions. The approach is system-agnostic, adds only O(n_b²d) computational overhead, and is demonstrated to stabilize training across Lorenz 63, Kuramoto-Sivashinsky, and Kolmogorov Flow up to 4,096-dimensional states.

## Results

On Kolmogorov Flow (state dimension 4,096), DySLIM regularization consistently improves long-term statistical accuracy across all base objectives. For the Curr objective, MELR drops from 5.35×10⁻² to 1.64×10⁻² (−69%) and Wass1 from 9.66×10⁻² to 4.76×10⁻² (−51%). For the 1-step objective, Wass1 improves from 16.2×10⁻² to 5.55×10⁻² (−66%) and TCM from 5.39×10⁻² to 2.45×10⁻² (−55%). The paper further shows that as batch size and learning rate increase, DySLIM-regularized models remain stable while unregularized baselines deteriorate rapidly, and wall-clock training time is essentially unchanged (Table 3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DySLIM against two training paradigms: Curriculum training (Curr; Keisler, 2022) and the Pushforward trick (Pfwd; Brandstetter et al., 2022), each used as a base objective with and without the DySLIM MMD regularization. DySLIM wins on nearly all metrics for both baselines: for Curr, it reduces MELR by 69% (5.35→1.64), Wass1 by 51% (9.66→4.76), and TCM by 19% (3.50→2.83); for Pfwd, it reduces MELR by 23% (3.19→2.46) and TCM by 80% (3.68→0.72). The one exception is MELRw for the 1-step objective, where DySLIM slightly worsens the metric (0.44→0.85), likely because the 1-step base is already very short-horizon. Qualitatively, on Lorenz 63 and KS, DySLIM-regularized models maintain correct attractor geometry over long rollouts while unregularized Curr models blow up and unregularized Pfwd models drift to a wrong attractor. The ablation over batch sizes and learning rates (Appendix E.3) shows DySLIM's advantage grows with scale: at larger batch sizes and learning rates, base objectives degrade sharply while DySLIM remains stable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself between two existing lines of work: stabilization techniques for learning chaotic dynamics (curriculum training, pushforward trick) and direct invariant measure learning (Botvinick-Greenhouse et al., 2023; Jiang et al., 2023). It repurposes the Maximum Mean Discrepancy (Gretton et al., 2012), a kernel-based two-sample test from statistical learning theory, as a system-agnostic regularization term that can be layered onto any existing dynamical systems learning objective. Notably, the paper shows in Appendix A that the pushforward trick (Brandstetter et al., 2022) is implicitly a weak Wasserstein-2 measure-matching loss, providing a unifying theoretical lens that connects the stabilization literature to the invariant measure literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DySLIM regularization addresses a fundamental and practically critical failure mode: learned chaotic models either blow up or converge to incorrect attractors, which is the central obstacle to using ML surrogates for long-term weather and climate prediction. The approach is system-agnostic, adds only quadratic-in-batch-size overhead, and scales to 4096-dimensional state spaces, making it a practical drop-in improvement for existing pipelines. Key open questions include whether the method extends to non-ergodic systems with multiple attractors, how it behaves when the invariant measure itself drifts (as in climate change scenarios), and whether the MMD bandwidth selection can be made fully automatic for high-dimensional systems.
