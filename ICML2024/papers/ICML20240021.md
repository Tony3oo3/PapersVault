## Problem

In safety-critical average reward RL, controlling the variance of the long-run average reward is essential, yet prior work has only addressed per-step variance as a surrogate, ignoring temporal correlations inherent in Markovian dynamics. No temporal-difference algorithm with provable finite-sample guarantees existed for estimating the true asymptotic variance (the variance of the cumulative reward random variable) in the average reward setting.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes two independently simulated trajectories under a fixed stationary policy μ, observing state-action-reward transitions (S_k, A_k, r(S_k,A_k), S_{k+1}, A_{k+1}) on the first trajectory and (S'_k, A'_k, r(S'_k,A'_k)) on the second. The dual-trajectory design is what enables a linear SA formulation: the identity 2Var[X] = E[(X−X')²] eliminates the need to explicitly estimate the mean inside the variance expression. The output is a scalar asymptotic variance estimate κ_μ, alongside intermediate estimates of the average reward J_μ and the Q-function Q_μ (tabular or linearly approximated).

## Method

The key reformulation expresses asymptotic variance κ_μ as 2E[(r−r')Q_μ] − ½E[(r−r')²], where Q_μ solves the Poisson equation r − J_μ = Q_μ − P_2 Q_μ, reducing the problem to simultaneously estimating J_μ, Q_μ, and κ_μ via a single linear stochastic approximation update. The Q_μ update is a standard TD step projected onto the subspace orthogonal to the all-ones vector (to handle the non-uniqueness of Poisson equation solutions), while the variance update is a scalar SA step driven by the two-trajectory residual. Convergence is established via a Lyapunov drift argument on the joint iterate Θ_k = [J_k, θ_k, κ_k], exploiting semi-norm contraction of the average matrix A (since average-reward operators are not contractive in any norm) and geometric mixing of the underlying Markov chain. This yields Õ(1/k) mean-squared error bounds for diminishing step sizes in both the tabular and linear function approximation settings, with the latter incurring a characterized approximation error bounded by 16r²_max/(1−λ²) · E².

## Results

The paper establishes a $\tilde{O}(1/k)$ convergence rate for the mean-squared estimation error of the asymptotic variance $\kappa_\mu$ in both tabular and linear function approximation settings (Theorems 4.3 and 5.2). The sample complexity to achieve mean estimation error $\leq \epsilon$ is $k = O(\log^2(1/\epsilon)/\epsilon^2) \cdot \tilde{O}(L\|Q_\mu^*\|_2^2/\Delta_1^4)$ (Corollary 4.5), which reduces to $\tilde{O}(|S|^2|A|^2)$ for a random walk on a complete graph and $\tilde{O}(|S|^{12}|A|^{12})$ for a cycle graph. In the linear function approximation setting, the approximation error is bounded by $(\kappa^* - \kappa_\mu)^2 \leq 16r_{\max}^2/(1-\lambda^2) \cdot \mathcal{E}^2$ (Proposition 5.3), where $\mathcal{E}$ is the minimum $D_\mu$-norm distance between the feature span and the set of valid Q-functions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental baselines or empirical comparisons. The paper positions its contribution as the first sequential (TD-type) estimator for asymptotic variance of a Markov chain with provable finite-sample guarantees, in contrast to prior Monte Carlo batched estimators (Wu 2009, Flegal & Jones 2010, Chien et al. 1997) that lack finite-sample bounds, and to Benton (2022) which provides only asymptotic convergence for a similar TD-type algorithm. The paper also distinguishes its problem from prior work on variance of TD iterates (Devraj & Meyn 2017, Yin & Wang 2020), noting that it estimates the asymptotic variance of the reward process rather than the variance of the learning algorithm's iterates. No head-to-head numerical comparisons are presented.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TD learning framework for average reward MDPs (Tsitsiklis & Van Roy, 1999) and the finite-sample Lyapunov drift analysis for average-reward TD (Zhang et al., 2021) to the problem of estimating asymptotic variance. The key algorithmic insight is to express the asymptotic variance via the Poisson equation solution (from Douc et al., 2018) and then use two independent trajectories to convert the resulting nonlinear expression into a linear SA update. This departs from prior variance-constrained RL work (Prashanth & Ghavamzadeh, 2016), which used per-period stationary variance as a surrogate, and from Benton (2022), which provided only asymptotic convergence for a similar TD-type estimator without finite-sample guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first sequential estimator for asymptotic variance of a Markov chain with provable finite-sample guarantees, filling a gap between the statistics literature (which has batched MC estimators without finite-sample bounds) and the RL literature (which lacked any finite-sample analysis for this risk measure in the average reward setting). The result provides a building block for actor-critic algorithms with variance constraints in safety-critical applications. Open questions include whether the two-trajectory requirement can be eliminated, whether the Ω(1/k) MSE rate is truly tight in the Markovian noise setting, and how to extend the approach to policy optimization (beyond evaluation) with comparable guarantees.
