## Problem

Standard Kalman filters and their nonlinear extensions (EKF, EnKF) assume Gaussian measurement models, making them highly sensitive to outliers and measurement model misspecification. Existing robust alternatives—variational Bayes with hierarchical priors, Huberised optimisation, or particle filters with generalised Bayes—require iterative inner loops or large sample counts per time step, inflating computational cost well beyond the O(m³) of the standard KF. The paper asks whether one can obtain provable outlier robustness while retaining the closed-form, single-pass update structure of the Kalman filter.

## Input / Output

At each time step the method consumes a single observation y_t ∈ ℝ^d, the known measurement function h_t (or its Jacobian for EKF), the measurement covariance R_t, and the prior predictive state (mean μ_{t|t-1}, covariance Σ_{t|t-1}). It outputs a Gaussian posterior (μ_t, Σ_t) in one pass with O(m³) cost, identical to the standard KF. The weighting function W(y_t, ŷ_t) is computed dynamically from the residual between the current observation and its prediction, so no additional hyperparameter tuning is needed per step—only a single scalar threshold c governs the degree of down-weighting. Because W ∈ [0,1], the posterior is always conservative (wider) relative to the unweighted KF when outliers are present.

## Method

WoLF replaces the negative log-likelihood in the Bayesian update with a weighted log-likelihood ℓ_t(θ_t) = −W²(y_t, ŷ_t) log q(y_t|θ_t), where W is a data-dependent weighting function of the observation residual. For the linear-Gaussian case, Proposition 3.1 shows this reduces to the standard KF update with R_t^{-1} simply replaced by W²(y_t, ŷ_t) R_t^{-1}, preserving the closed-form conjugate structure. Three weighting functions are instantiated: an Inverse Multi-Quadratic (IMQ) form for compensation-based robustness, a Mahalanobis-distance variant (MD) that accounts for anisotropic measurement noise, and a hard-threshold Mahalanobis (TMD) form for detect-and-reject behaviour. Theorem 3.2 proves that for any W satisfying sup W < ∞ and sup W²‖y_t‖₂ < ∞, the posterior influence function is bounded, establishing outlier robustness in the Huber sense—whereas the standard KF has an unbounded PIF. The same weighting mechanism extends directly to the EKF (by weighting the linearised log-likelihood) and the EnKF (by weighting per-particle or averaged-particle error terms), yielding WoLF-IMQ, WoLF-TMD, AP-EnKF, and PP-EnKF variants.

## Results

On the 2D tracking problem, WoLF-IMQ and WoLF-TMD achieve the lowest RMSE under both Student-t and Mixture observation models, while matching the standard KF's computational cost exactly (1.0× slowdown). In contrast, KF-B is 2.0×–3.7× slower and KF-IW is 1.2×–5.3× slower relative to KF. On corrupted UCI regression datasets, WoLF-IMQ and WoLF-TMD are the only methods that simultaneously achieve lower RMedSE and lower time-per-step than the OGD baseline (i.e., they fall in the third quadrant of the accuracy-vs-speed plot). On the Lorenz96 model (d=100, 1000 particles), the WoLF-EnKF variants (AP-EnKF, PP-EnKF) match or outperform the Hub-EnKF on in-state RMSE while being more robust to the threshold hyperparameter c.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard KF, WoLF-IMQ and WoLF-TMD match its O(m³) computational cost exactly (1.0× slowdown) while providing provably bounded posterior influence under contamination; the standard KF has an unbounded PIF and its posterior mean is visibly pulled by outliers in both the Student-t and Mixture tracking variants. Against KF-B (Wang et al., 2018, a detect-and-reject VB method), WoLF-IMQ and WoLF-TMD achieve comparable or lower RMSE on the Mixture variant while being 2.0×–3.7× faster, and on the Student-t variant WoLF methods clearly outperform KF-B which behaves similarly to the non-robust KF. Against KF-IW (Agamennoni et al., 2012, a compensation-based VB method), WoLF methods dominate on both accuracy and speed: KF-IW is 1.2×–5.3× slower and fails to be robust under the Mixture variant. Against OGD/Adam (Bencomo et al., 2023) on corrupted UCI datasets, WoLF-IMQ and WoLF-TMD are the only methods in the third quadrant of the RMedSE-vs-time-per-step plot, meaning they are both more accurate and faster than the OGD baseline. Against Hub-EnKF (Roh et al., 2013) on Lorenz96, AP-EnKF and PP-EnKF achieve nearly identical in-state RMSE trajectories but are substantially more robust to the threshold hyperparameter c, because WoLF methods fully disregard outlier measurements above c rather than clipping them. Against the standard EnKF (Roth et al., 2017), the weighted variants reduce the spike in L_t at outlier timesteps, confirming that the weighting mechanism transfers to the ensemble setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of generalised Bayesian inference (Bissiri et al., 2016) and classical Kalman filtering, showing that replacing the log-likelihood in the Bayesian update with a data-dependent weighted log-likelihood preserves the closed-form conjugate structure of the KF/EKF/EnKF. Prior robust filtering approaches—variational Bayes methods (KF-B, KF-IW), Huberised filters, and particle-filter-based generalised Bayes (Boustati et al., 2020)—all require iterative optimisation or large sample sizes per step. WoLF departs from these by using a simple, dynamically computed weight (IMQ, Mahalanobis, or thresholded) that scales the observation likelihood, yielding the same O(m³) cost as the standard KF while provably bounding the posterior influence function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for real-time applications (tracking, data assimilation, online neural network fitting) where robustness to sensor outliers is needed but iterative per-step optimisation is infeasible. The theoretical contribution—proving bounded PIF for specific weighting functions under the Huber framework—gives a clean, verifiable robustness guarantee that prior VB or Huberised methods lack. Key limitations acknowledged by the authors include the assumption of known measurement covariance R_t, known process noise Q_t, unimodal posteriors, and no robustness to outliers in the state process itself, all of which remain open for future work.
