## Problem

Privacy-preserving machine learning with fully homomorphic encryption (FHE) has been extensively studied for numerical ML operations, but the embedding layer for NLP tasks—where look-up table (LUT) operations over vocabularies of 400K–1.9M tokens are required—remains inadequately addressed. Existing approaches either require the client to encrypt a one-hot vector of vocabulary size (causing impractical ciphertext expansion) or offload embedding computation to the client, exposing the server's proprietary embedding model. This paper tackles the challenge of performing end-to-end encrypted embedding lookups that protect both client data and server model.

## Input / Output

The client decomposes each token index x ∈ ℤ_k into l coded inputs (x₀,…,x_{l-1}) ∈ ℤ_p^l (e.g., p=8, l=64 for a 400K vocabulary) and sends l small CKKS ciphertexts, each occupying only 1 slot per code rather than k slots for a one-hot vector. The server holds the compressed codebook embedding matrices {E^(i)}_{i=0}^{l-1} ∈ ℝ^{p×d} learned via compositional code compression. The output is d encrypted embedding vectors (one per token), each a CKKS ciphertext of dimension d (e.g., 50 for GloVe 6B50d, 768 for BERT/GPT-2), with guaranteed 16-bit precision without bootstrapping.

## Method

The core contribution is the CodedHELUT algorithm, built on two key components. First, an Encrypted Indicator Function (EIF) approximates the discrete indicator δ_a(x) using a Square Method (recursive squaring of (1−2(x−a)²/p²) to 2^r power) followed by a Cleanse function (the polynomial −2x³+3x² applied s times for noise-aware rounding), with theoretical noise bounds guaranteeing 2^{-u} approximation error. Second, coded compression (Shu & Nakayama, 2018) decomposes the large embedding T:ℤ_k→ℝ^d into l small codebooks T_i:ℤ_p→ℝ^d such that T(x)≈Σ_i T_i(x_i), reducing the LUT evaluation from O(k)=O(p^l) multiplications to O(p·l) multiplications and correspondingly reducing additive noise from p^l·B‖E‖_max to pl·B‖E‖_max. SIMD parallelization (packing l codes into one ciphertext for p1, batching multiple tokens for p2) further amortizes costs, achieving 0.018–3.283s amortized evaluation across GloVe, BERT, and GPT-2 embedding layers at 16-bit precision without bootstrapping.

## Results

On a synthetic 400K-entry table with 2^12 inputs, CodedHELUT with SIMD parallelization (p1) achieves an amortized evaluation time of 3.83 ms with 15-bit precision, compared to 81.16 ms for the naive HELUT-LT baseline (14 bits). On real-world word embeddings, CodedHELUT achieves amortized times of 0.018–0.242 s for GloVe 6B50d (400K vocab, d=50), 0.104–1.298 s for GloVe 42B300d (1.9M vocab, d=300), and 0.262–3.283 s for BERT/GPT-2 (d=768), all maintaining 16-bit precision without bootstrapping. On the IMDB sentiment analysis downstream task, the compressed embeddings used by CodedHELUT achieve 79.49% (GloVe 6B50d) and 85.27% (GloVe 42B300d) accuracy, within 0.5% of the original embeddings (79.64% and 85.74%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its proposed EIF against two external alternatives: the comparison-function-based EIF from Cheon et al. (2020) and the Sinc-approximation-based EIF from Lee et al. (2023). At log Δ = 35 and p = 8, the proposed EIF achieves 16-bit precision in 0.0762 ms with 11 Mult operations, outperforming Cheon et al. (14 bits, 0.1499 ms, 21 Mult) and Lee et al. (11 bits, 0.1462 ms, 22 Mult) on both precision and cost. At p = 64, the gap widens: the proposed EIF maintains 16-bit precision in 0.1087 ms while Cheon et al. drops to 12 bits and Lee et al. to 5 bits. In terms of ciphertext expansion for the full embedding pipeline, CodedHELUT requires only l slots per word (e.g., 32 slots for 16×32 coding on GloVe 42B300d), compared to 300 slots when the embedding layer is delegated to the client (Lee et al., 2022) and 1.9M slots for one-hot encoding (Badawi et al., 2020), yielding roughly a 9.3× reduction over the client-side approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CKKS approximate homomorphic encryption scheme (Cheon et al., 2017) and the coded compression technique for word embeddings (Shu & Nakayama, 2018), combining them into a novel pipeline for privacy-preserving embedding evaluation. It departs from prior PPML-with-FHE work (Badawi et al., 2020; Lee et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2022) that either required clients to encrypt full one-hot vectors (causing infeasible ciphertext expansion) or delegated the embedding lookup to the client (exposing the server's proprietary model). The key methodological departure is the Encrypted Indicator Function (EIF), which uses a recursive squaring method plus a noise-cleansing polynomial to approximate discrete equality tests under CKKS noise, outperforming both comparison-function-based (Cheon et al., 2020) and Sinc-approximation-based (Lee et al., 2023) alternatives in depth and multiplication count.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CodedHELUT is the first practical demonstration that an entire embedding layer (up to 1.9M vocabularies) can be evaluated under CKKS without bootstrapping, reducing per-token ciphertext from O(k) slots to O(l) slots and enabling end-to-end encryption that protects both client input and server model. A key limitation is the dependence on the specific Gumbel-Softmax compression method of Shu & Nakayama (2018), which constrains generalizability to arbitrary lookup tables and introduces a small but non-zero MSE in the compressed embeddings. Open questions include whether compression can be co-trained with the full model, whether the approach extends to attention-based LUTs in transformers, and whether the 16-bit precision ceiling (set by B*) can be raised with larger scaling factors or RNS variants.
