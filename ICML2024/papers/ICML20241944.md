## Problem

In personalized federated learning, personalized features and spurious (shortcut) features are closely entangled because both exhibit similar variability across heterogeneous clients, making personalization preservation and shortcut elimination conflicting objectives. Existing PFL methods either discard personalized features along with spurious ones (as in federated invariant learning) or fail to mitigate spurious correlations while retaining personalization, and no prior approach provides a provable mechanism to separate the two under non-linear feature spaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241944/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each federated client receives RGB images (28×28 for CMNIST/CFMNIST, natural photographs for WaterBird, multi-domain images for PACS) drawn from a non-IID local distribution and produces a per-client personalized invariant classifier that outputs a class label. The model architecture ranges from a single-hidden-layer DNN (CMNIST, CFMNIST) to ResNet-18 (WaterBird, PACS), with the final fully-connected layer serving as the personalized classifier. Test evaluation spans multiple out-of-distribution environments (e.g., varying shortcut strength p^e from 0 to 1, or leave-one-domain-out splits), reporting worst-case and average accuracy across all test distributions.

## Method

FedPIN formulates a heterogeneous structured causal model for federated clients and identifies a causal signature: spurious features Z_S are conditionally independent of global invariant features Z_C^g given Y, while personalized invariant features Z_C^p are not, enabling the anchor Z_C^g to distinguish the two. A global objective constructs the anchor by minimizing a cross-entropy loss plus a conditional mutual information penalty I(E,U;Y|Φ_g(X)), which is made tractable via the difference between a global classifier loss and an auxiliary classifier loss that also receives the client index. Locally, each client minimizes a loss combining cross-entropy, a contrastive loss (treating Φ_u(X) and Φ_g^*(X) as a positive pair and features from a separately trained extractor Ψ_u^* as negatives), and a variance term that upper-bounds the entropy H(Φ_u(X)), jointly maximizing I(Φ_u(X);Φ_g^*(X)|Y) − H(Φ_u(X)) to retain personalized invariant features while excluding spurious ones. The FedPIN algorithm alternates between server-side aggregation of global model updates and client-side local optimization of both the personalized model and the global sub-problem, achieving O(1/√T) convergence under Non-IID conditions.

## Results

FedPIN achieves the best worst-case test accuracy on three of four datasets: CFMNIST (59.8%), WaterBird (73.8%), and PACS (55.4%), outperforming the second-best method FedSDR by approximately 3%, 8.5%, and 3.3% respectively. On CMNIST, FedPIN (53.6% worst-case) is within 0.3% of FedSDR (53.9%). FedPIN also attains the highest average accuracy on CFMNIST (63.1%), WaterBird (75.8%), and PACS (58.6%), while all other PFL baselines (Ditto, FedRep, FedRoD, FedPAC, pFedMe) show worst-case accuracies below 50% on CMNIST and CFMNIST, indicating severe shortcut reliance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedPIN is compared against 11 baselines: four FL methods (FedAvg, DRFA, FedSR, FedIIR) and seven PFL methods (pFedMe, Ditto, FTFA, FedRep, FedRoD, FedPAC, FedSDR). All FL methods fail to achieve personalization and show poor worst-case accuracy (e.g., FedAvg drops to 3.4% on CMNIST-8 and 0.2% on CFMNIST-8), while most PFL methods (Ditto, FedRep, FedRoD, FedPAC, pFedMe) also struggle with shortcut mitigation, yielding worst-case accuracies below 25% on CMNIST and CFMNIST. The closest competitor is FedSDR, which also targets personalized invariant features but relies on linear separability assumptions; FedPIN outperforms FedSDR by 2.9% on CFMNIST-8 (59.8 vs 56.9), 8.5% on WaterBird-8 (73.8 vs 65.3), and 3.3% on PACS-6 (55.4 vs 52.1) in worst-case accuracy. On CMNIST-8, FedSDR narrowly leads FedPIN (53.9 vs 53.6 worst-case), but FedPIN recovers the lead on the 80-client setting (50.8 vs 50.4). The ablation in Table 2 further confirms that the information-theoretic constraint (conditional mutual information + entropy) is essential: removing either term (λ=0 or γ=0) degrades worst-case accuracy by 3–10% relative to the full FedPIN.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the invariant learning (IRM) framework into the federated setting by replacing IRM's linear-space invariance constraint with an information-theoretic regularization (conditional mutual information + entropy) that operates in non-linear feature spaces. It builds directly on FedIIR (Guo et al., 2023), which brought federated invariant learning but inherited IRM's linearity limitation, and on FedSDR (Tang et al., 2024), which first formulated personalized federated invariant learning but required the restrictive assumption that invariant and spurious features are linearly separable. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a causal signature—conditional independence of spurious features from global invariant features given the target, contrasted with the non-independence of personalized invariant features—that provides a principled mechanism to disentangle personalization from shortcuts without linearity assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine tension in personalized federated learning: preserving client-specific invariant information while eliminating spurious correlations, a conflict that existing PFL methods (fine-tuning, L2 regularization, feature alignment) fail to resolve, as demonstrated by the ablation showing local fine-tuning actively degrades OOD performance. The theoretical contributions—a tighter generalization bound that eliminates the OOD term under the SCM, and a convergence rate matching FedAvg—provide rigorous grounding. However, the approach relies on the structured causal model holding in practice, the contrastive loss is only an upper-bound proxy for the true conditional mutual information, and experiments are limited to relatively small-scale datasets (MNIST variants, WaterBird, PACS), leaving open questions about scalability to larger, more complex federated settings and the robustness of the causal assumptions under real-world data generation.
