## Problem

The vanilla stochastic proximal gradient method for non-convex composite optimization provably cannot converge to the stationary point beyond the variance of the gradient noise when using small or bounded batch sizes, necessitating mega-batches of size Ω(ε⁻¹) to achieve ε-error. This paper asks whether Polyak momentum can eliminate this mega-batch requirement while preserving the optimal O(ε⁻²) stochastic oracle complexity.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a stochastic first-order oracle for the smooth component f (with bounded noise variance σ²), a possibly non-differentiable proximal function ψ, and requires solving a proximal subproblem at each iteration that can be handled inexactly via a few inner SGD steps. The output is a randomly selected iterate x_t from the sequence {x₁,…,x_K} satisfying E[‖∇F(x_t)‖²] ≤ ε, with the critical property that batch size 1 suffices—no error-dependent batch scaling is needed. The method also handles the composite case where ψ is convex but non-differentiable by working with subgradients.

## Method

The paper augments the stochastic proximal gradient method with Polyak (heavy-ball) momentum, replacing the raw stochastic gradient g_k at each iteration with a momentum-aggregated estimate m_k = (1−γ_{k−1})m_{k−1} + γ_{k−1}g_k that is used in the proximal step. The key analytical insight is that the momentum causes the gradient-estimate variance Δ_k = E[‖m_k − ∇f(x_k)‖²] to decrease geometrically over iterations, breaking the noise floor that permanently traps the vanilla method at O(σ²) error. The convergence proof constructs a Lyapunov function Φ_k = F_k + aΔ_k and shows both the gradient norm and the momentum–full-gradient gap decrease at the same O(1/K) rate, yielding the optimal O(ε⁻²) total oracle complexity regardless of batch size. The analysis further extends to inexact proximal steps (solved via a constant number of SGD iterations) and to the non-differentiable convex ψ case, with numerical experiments on synthetic quadratics, ℓ_{∞,1}-regularized ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10, and statistical preconditioning confirming the theory.

## Results

The paper establishes that the vanilla stochastic proximal gradient method provably cannot converge below an error of σ²/4 (Proposition 4.1), regardless of stepsize or number of iterations. In contrast, Algorithm 1 (stochastic proximal gradient with Polyak momentum) achieves the optimal O(ε⁻²) convergence rate in terms of stochastic gradient oracle calls, with E[‖∇F(x_t)‖²] ≤ ε after K = O(LΦ₀σ²/ε² + LΦ₀/ε) iterations, without requiring any error-dependent mega-batches. On the synthetic quadratic problem, the vanilla method with batch size 1 oscillates around errors of ~22, ~672, and ~14381 for σ = 5, 25, 125 respectively, while Algorithm 1 converges to the target tolerance in all cases. On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 and ℓ_{∞,1} regularization (batch size 256, 300 epochs), Algorithm 1 achieves lower training loss and higher test accuracy than the vanilla method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole experimental baseline is the vanilla stochastic proximal gradient method (Ghadimi et al., 2016), which is the standard algorithm for non-convex composite optimization. On the synthetic quadratic problem, the vanilla method with batch size 1 completely fails to converge at any noise level (σ = 5, 25, 125), with error oscillating proportionally to σ² as predicted by the lower bound; even with batch sizes 16 and 64, it only converges at the lowest noise level (σ = 5). Algorithm 1 converges in all settings without any batch size requirement. On CIFAR-10 with ℓ_{∞,1} regularization and ResNet-18, Algorithm 1 outperforms the vanilla method in both training loss and test accuracy, with a noticeably smoother convergence curve. On the statistical preconditioning experiment (also CIFAR-10), Algorithm 1 again outperforms the vanilla method in both training loss and test accuracy. The paper also notes that the momentum parameter γ is less sensitive to batch size than the stepsize M, with γ = 0.1 being a robust practical choice across batch sizes 64, 128, and 256.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stochastic proximal gradient method of Ghadimi et al. (2016) by incorporating Polyak (heavy-ball) momentum, originally proposed by Polyak (1964) for deterministic convex problems. It departs from variance reduction approaches (Spider, Storm, Prox-Spiderboost) that achieve O(ε^{-3/2}) complexity but require stronger assumptions (average smoothness, two gradient evaluations per step). The inexact proximal step analysis builds on the inexactness criterion of Woodworth et al. (2023), and the momentum variance-reduction analysis parallels recent work on normalized SGD (Cutkosky & Mehta, 2020) and compressed optimization (Fatkhullin et al., 2023), but is the first to rigorously characterize this effect in the composite (non-smooth) setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Polyak momentum is the de-facto standard in deep learning practice, yet its theoretical justification in the composite optimization regime was absent; this paper closes that gap by proving optimal O(ε^{-2}) convergence without any batch-size requirement. This has direct practical implications for settings where large batches are infeasible (federated learning, medical imaging, multi-agent RL). An open question is whether the extra O(σ⁴/ε² + σ²/ε) term in the composite case (absent in the non-composite case) is truly inherent or an artifact of the analysis. The paper also leaves open the non-convex, non-differentiable ψ case, which it explicitly excludes as 'exotic.'
