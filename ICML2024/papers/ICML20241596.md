## Problem

Distribution matching methods for RLHF (GDC, DPG) have not received the same attention as contrastive methods like DPO, primarily due to high variance in their gradient estimates. This paper identifies that variance as the core bottleneck and proposes a self-normalized baseline to fix it, while also generalizing the target distribution via a Bayesian formulation that unifies PPO, DPO, and distribution matching under a single posterior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241596/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a text prompt x, the method generates n=32 candidate outputs from a proposal distribution (initialized as the SFT model and updated every k=250 gradient steps), scores each with a Bradley-Terry reward model, and computes self-normalized importance weights from the reward scores and log-probability ratios. The trained policy q_θ(y|x) is a standard autoregressive LM (Llama-7B with QLoRA, or a summarization LM with LoRA) that produces a single generated text response at inference time. The reward model is a separate GPT-J fine-tuned on preference triplets, and evaluation uses both the training reward model and an LLM-as-a-judge (Mixtral-8x7B or GPT-4).

## Method

BRAIN formulates the alignment target as the reward-conditioned posterior p(y|x, G=1) = p(y|x)·p(G=1|y,x)/p(G=1|x), where the SFT model is the prior and the reward model provides the likelihood. To train a policy q_θ to match this posterior without rejection sampling, it uses self-normalized importance sampling over n samples from a proposal q', but critically subtracts a self-normalized baseline estimate of E_{q_θ}[∇_θ log q_θ(y|x)] from the gradient. This baseline subtraction yields the BRAIN gradient estimate, which the paper proves is an unbiased estimator of the gradient of a novel self-normalized KL divergence whose unique minimum is achieved only when q_θ equals the posterior. Under Bradley-Terry assumptions, the posterior equals the PPO optimal policy, and DPO-sft emerges as the special case where n=2 and softmax is replaced by argmax. The proposal distribution is refreshed periodically (every k gradient steps) to reduce the gap between proposal and posterior.

## Results

On AnthropicHH, BRAIN achieves 95.40% win-rate against gold using the training reward model (TrainRM), outperforming the strongest baseline DPO-sft (87.37%) by 8 points, and 74.36% under independent LLM evaluation (Mixtral-8x7B), surpassing RSO (67.22%) by over 7 points. On Reddit TL;DR, BRAIN reaches 95.21% TrainRM win-rate (vs. 91.24% for RSO) and 64.74% LLM-eval win-rate (vs. 60.55% for DPO-sft). In GPT-4 head-to-head comparisons on 500 examples, BRAIN wins approximately twice as often as it loses against each baseline on AnthropicHH (e.g., 44.0% win vs. 21.4% loss vs. DPO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BRAIN outperforms all three baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against DPO, the gap is largest on AnthropicHH TrainRM (95.40% vs. 54.60%, a 41-point margin), while on LLM eval BRAIN leads by 7.3 points (74.36% vs. 67.02%). Against DPO-sft, BRAIN gains 8.0 points on AnthropicHH TrainRM and 7.7 points on LLM eval; notably, DPO-sft's TrainRM score (87.37%) far exceeds DPO's (54.60%) yet its LLM-eval score is nearly identical to DPO's, indicating reward hacking that BRAIN avoids. Against RSO, BRAIN leads by 10.8 points on AnthropicHH TrainRM and 7.1 points on LLM eval. On Reddit TL;DR, the margins are smaller but consistent: BRAIN beats RSO by 4.0 points (TrainRM) and 4.3 points (LLM eval). In GPT-4 head-to-head judgments, BRAIN wins ~44–45% of comparisons on AnthropicHH with only ~20% losses, while on TL;DR the win/loss ratio is closer (~45% vs. ~37%), suggesting the advantage is more pronounced in the dialogue setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

BRAIN extends the distribution-matching lineage (DPG → GDC → GDC++) by replacing the intractable EBM target with a Bayesian reward-conditioned posterior and, critically, by introducing a self-normalized baseline that addresses the high-variance gradient estimates that have kept these methods from competing with contrastive approaches. The paper's key conceptual move is showing that under Bradley-Terry assumptions, this posterior coincides with the PPO-optimal policy, thereby unifying the target distribution across PPO, DPO, and distribution matching. The self-normalized baseline then yields a formal proof that DPO-sft (n=2, argmax, fixed proposal) is a degenerate special case of the BRAIN objective, positioning BRAIN as a strict generalization that relaxes each DPO restriction incrementally.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of distribution matching and DPO under a single objective provides a principled roadmap for improving DPO: the ablation (argmax→softmax, n=2→n=32, fixed→updated proposal) shows each relaxation contributes measurable gains, suggesting further generalizations may yield additional improvements. The large ablation gap (95% → ~59% win-rate when self-normalization is removed) underscores that variance reduction, not the Bayesian reformulation per se, is the critical ingredient for making distribution matching viable in RLHF. Open questions include whether the self-normalized KL divergence has useful properties beyond the current existence/uniqueness proof, whether the method scales to larger models where generating 32 samples per prompt becomes prohibitive, and whether the advantage over DPO persists at the scale of frontier models where DPO has been most validated.
