## Problem

Multimodal image registration is challenging because sparse feature matching (SM) is efficient but fails in textureless scenes, while dense direct alignment (DA) is accurate but easily trapped in local minima without good initialization. No prior method combines both paradigms to exploit their complementary strengths, and the features required for SM (locally discriminative) and DA (locally smooth) are fundamentally incompatible, making a unified approach non-trivial.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242458/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multimodal image pair consisting of an input image I_I (192×192) and a template image I_T (128×128), which may differ in modality (RGB vs. infrared, near-infrared, or satellite). A single forward pass through SDME simultaneously produces 128-d per-pixel descriptors and a keypoint heatmap (sparse branch) and a single-channel feature map (dense branch). The S2D pipeline then estimates an initial 8-DoF homography via MNN matching + MAGSAC++ on the sparse features, and refines it through 15 iterations of ICLK optimization on the dense features, yielding the final homography.

## Method

SDME is a multi-task network with a shared 5-layer dilated-convolution encoder (following R2D2) that splits into a sparse branch (keypoint heatmap + 128-d descriptors) and a dense branch (128-d features converted to a single-channel map via local covariance). The sparse branch incorporates a Modality-Invariant Transformer Block (MITB) that uses M learnable elements as attention queries to enlarge descriptor receptive fields and capture cross-modality invariant structures. A mutual guidance mechanism couples the two tasks: the dense feature map guides sparse keypoint detection toward modality-invariant structures (corners, edges), while the sparse heatmap weights the DA objective to suppress ambiguous regions. Training uses MGDA-UB to dynamically balance the competing gradient objectives of the two branches, avoiding the negative transfer that arises from fixed weighted-sum losses.

## Results

On the four multimodal datasets, SDME achieves APE of 1.21 (VIS-NIR), 1.58 (VIS-NIR*), 1.82 (GoogleEarth), and 2.75 (VIS-IR-drone), with success rates of 100%, 99.20%, 100%, and 99.88% respectively. On the single-modal MSCOCO benchmark, it achieves APE 0.53 with 99.71% SR, competitive with the best deep homography estimator IHN (0.37). The S2D pipeline consistently outperforms both sparse-only (e.g., ReDFeat: 1.78 APE on VIS-NIR) and dense-only (e.g., PRISE: 6.25 APE on VIS-NIR) baselines, and the full model has only 1.07M parameters with a total runtime of 66.37 ms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the three multimodal datasets (VIS-NIR, GoogleEarth, VIS-IR-drone), SDME achieves the best APE and SR among all compared methods, outperforming the strongest sparse baseline ReDFeat by 0.57 APE on VIS-NIR (1.21 vs 1.78) and 0.27 on VIS-IR-drone (2.75 vs 3.02), and the strongest dense baseline PRISE by 5.04 APE on VIS-NIR (1.21 vs 6.25). Against the deep homography estimators DHN and MHN, SDME is dramatically superior on multimodal data (e.g., 1.21 vs 17.83 APE on VIS-NIR for DHN), while IHN is the only competitor that approaches SDME on single-modal MSCOCO (0.37 vs 0.53 APE) but degrades under large deformation. In the matcher comparison (Table 3), SDME with simple MNN matching outperforms SuperPoint+SuperGlue (0.53 vs 1.31 APE on MSCOCO) and LoFTR (2.75 vs 11.39 on VIS-IR-drone), demonstrating that the DA refinement step provides the dominant accuracy gain. On MSCOCO, SDME is slightly behind PRISE (0.53 vs 0.28 APE) and IHN (0.53 vs 0.37), indicating the S2D pipeline's advantage is most pronounced in the multimodal and large-deformation regimes where initialization quality matters most.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate paradigms in image registration—sparse feature matching (SM, following R2D2's lightweight detection/description architecture) and direct alignment (DA, following DeepLK's single-channel feature map and ICLK optimization)—into a sequential sparse-to-dense pipeline where SM provides initialization for DA. The multi-task network (SDME) that jointly learns both feature types is trained with MGDA-UB (Sener & Koltun, 2018) to resolve gradient conflicts between the competing objectives, and a mutual-guidance mechanism lets each branch inform the other. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior work by treating SM and DA not as alternatives but as complementary stages, and by introducing a modality-invariant transformer block (inspired by DINOv2-style attention) to handle cross-modal descriptor learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The S2D paradigm addresses a practical gap: sparse methods fail in textureless multimodal scenes while dense methods are initialization-sensitive, and the combined pipeline achieves both robustness and sub-pixel accuracy at ~66 ms runtime with only 1.07M parameters. The generalization finding—that a model pre-trained on single-modal MSCOCO can be fine-tuned on 10% of a small multimodal dataset to approach full-training performance—has direct practical value since multimodal data is far harder to collect. Open questions include whether the S2D strategy extends beyond 8-DoF homography to 6-DoF pose or non-planar deformations, whether the mutual-guidance losses generalize to more than two tasks, and how the approach scales to very large image pairs where the dense optimization cost becomes prohibitive.
