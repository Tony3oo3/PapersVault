## Problem

While the infinite-width limit of standard neural network training is well understood via the NTK and NNGP frameworks, the analogous theory for Jacobian-regularised (robust) training remained unexplored. Specifically, it was unknown whether the Jacobian of an MLP jointly converges to a Gaussian process with the network output at initialisation, and how the training dynamics under a Jacobian regulariser behave in the infinite-width limit. This gap left the theoretical justification for Jacobian regularisation as a robustness mechanism largely incomplete.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular training data with unit-norm input vectors and bounded outputs, along with a Jacobian regularisation coefficient λ ∈ (0,1] and a fixed output scale κ. The key output is a (d₀+1)×(d₀+1) matrix-valued kernel (the JNTK) whose entries capture covariances between the network output, individual Jacobian components, and their cross-terms, rather than a single scalar kernel. The final trained solution f_ntk is an analytic kernel regression function that interpolates both the target values and (implicitly) the Jacobian constraints at training points.

## Method

The paper extends the tensor-program framework to jointly track the MLP output and its input-output Jacobian through all layers, proving that both converge to a zero-mean GP at initialisation with an inductively defined Jacobian NNGP kernel. It then defines the Jacobian Neural Tangent Kernel (JNTK) as a (d₀+1)×(d₀+1) matrix whose entries are inner products of parameter gradients of the network output and each Jacobian component, and proves that this kernel becomes deterministic and constant during robust training in the infinite-width limit (under a full-rank assumption). The training dynamics reduce to a linear first-order ODE whose analytic solution at t=∞ is a standard kernel regression with a modified kernel Θ_λ = ΛΘΛ, where Λ is a diagonal matrix encoding the regularisation strength λ on the Jacobian entries. The proof of JNTK constancy requires tracking parameter movement in all of the 1, 2, and ∞ matrix norms simultaneously, a refinement beyond the standard NTK proof that is necessitated by the Jacobian regulariser.

## Results

The paper is primarily theoretical, with empirical validation on three datasets (Algerian Forest Fire, Banknote Authentication, Connectionist Bench). The key quantitative finding is that the max-norm distance between the finite-width JNTK and its deterministic limiting JNTK decreases monotonically with network width (from ~0.1 at width 64 to near-zero at width 8192), confirming Theorem 4.3. During robust training, the JNTK remains approximately constant for wide networks, with the distance from the initial JNTK growing slowly over 2048 training steps. In the eigenfeature analysis, Jacobian-regularised training (λ from 1 to 10⁻³) produces eigenfeatures where high test accuracy consistently implies high robustness to 0.01 and 0.1 perturbations, whereas standard training shows little or no such correlation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods in a traditional benchmark sense; instead, it contrasts standard training (no Jacobian regulariser) with robust training (Jacobian regulariser with coefficient λ). In the eigenfeature analysis (Figures 4 vs. 5), standard training yields eigenfeatures that are either highly accurate but fragile or highly robust but inaccurate, with no clear alignment between the two properties. In contrast, Jacobian-regularised training produces eigenfeatures where accuracy and robustness are positively correlated across all tested λ values (1, 10⁻¹, 10⁻², 10⁻³), meaning that achieving high test accuracy automatically satisfies robustness to perturbations. The paper also notes that the JNTK Gram matrix requires significantly deeper networks to satisfy the full-rank assumption (Assumption 4.4) compared to the standard NTK: 11 layers for GeLU and 6 for erf, versus no depth requirement for the standard NTK, reflecting the reduced flexibility imposed by Jacobian regularisation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the infinite-width analysis framework for neural networks—specifically the NNGP convergence (Lee et al., 2018; Matthews et al., 2018) and NTK training dynamics (Jacot et al., 2018; Lee et al., 2019)—to jointly include the input-output Jacobian of the network. The proof technique relies on the tensor-program framework (Yang, 2019), and the training objective builds on Jacobian regularisation introduced by Hoffman et al. (2019) and Jakubovitz & Giryes (2018). The key conceptual departure from prior NTK/NNGP theory is that all kernels (NNGP, NTK) are now matrix-valued of size (d₀+1)×(d₀+1) rather than scalar, capturing covariances between the network output and its partial derivatives, and the constancy-of-kernel proof requires tracking 1, 2, and ∞ matrix norms simultaneously rather than just the 2-norm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first rigorous infinite-width characterisation of Jacobian-regularised training, showing the trained network converges to an explicit kernel regression solution and giving practitioners a theoretical explanation for why Jacobian regularisation improves robustness. The empirical finding that Jacobian regularisation aligns accuracy and robustness across eigenfeatures (whereas standard NTK training exhibits a trade-off) is practically relevant for adversarial defence. However, the full-rank assumption on the JNTK (Assumption 4.4) is not known to hold theoretically and empirically requires non-trivial depth (e.g., 11 layers for GeLU), limiting the generality of the results. Open questions include whether the full-rank condition can be proven for standard activations, whether the analysis extends to non-MLP architectures (CNNs, transformers), and whether the kernel regression solution's robustness properties translate to finite-width networks at practical scales.
