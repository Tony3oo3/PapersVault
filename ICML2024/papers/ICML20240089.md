## Problem

Online matrix completion for collaborative filtering with rank-r reward matrices (r > 1) has only sub-optimal regret guarantees (Δ^{-2} dependence) from greedy algorithms, while optimal bounds (Δ^{-1}) are known only for rank-1 or under restrictive identical-preference cluster assumptions. This paper bridges the gap by introducing a weaker 'hott items' (separability) assumption—where each user's best item belongs to a small set of r special items—and deriving near-optimal regret bounds for rank > 1 without requiring users in the same cluster to share identical preference orderings.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates online over T rounds with M users and N items (M, N >> T), observing one noisy sub-Gaussian reward per recommended user-item pair per round. Two settings are considered: S=1 (one item per user, general regret) and S=r (r items per user, simplified regret where only the best of the r recommended items counts). The output is a per-user, per-round item recommendation policy with a poly-logarithmic dependence on T in the regret bound, where the leading term scales as Õ(NM^{-1}Δ^{-1}) for the general setting and Õ(σ²N/(M^{1/r}Δ_det)) for the simplified setting.

## Method

PHASEDCLUSTERELIM (S=1) works in two stages: first, it identifies r 'opinionated' users (one per hott-item cluster) by iteratively refining a graph where users are connected if their candidate item sets intersect, until an independent set of size r emerges; second, it uses these users as labels to form r overlapping user groups, then jointly eliminates sub-optimal items via phased low-rank matrix completion on relevant sub-matrices, with a critical expansion step that guarantees best items survive while exploiting user-user and item-item similarities. DETERMINANTELIM (S=r) exploits the fact that hott items uniquely maximize the squared determinant of the item embedding sub-matrix: it tracks all r-column subsets, estimates the average determinant across user sub-matrices using two independent reward estimates, and eliminates sub-optimal r-column sets in successive phases of exponentially increasing length, with the key technical contribution being a concentration lemma that characterizes sufficient rounds per phase given the redundancy of recommending overlapping item sets.

## Results

The paper provides two main theoretical regret guarantees: PHASEDCLUSTERELIM achieves \(\widetilde{O}(\max(1, NM^{-1})(\Delta_{\text{hott}}^{-2} + \Delta^{-1}) + 1)\) for general regret (S=1), improving upon the \(\widetilde{O}(NM^{-1}\Delta^{-2})\) bound of prior greedy algorithms, while DETERMINANTELIM achieves \(\widetilde{O}(\sigma^2 N / (c_{\text{avg}} c_{\text{max}} M^{1/r} \Delta_{\text{det}}) + 1)\) for simplified regret (S=r) under milder non-negativity assumptions. Synthetic experiments (M=200 users, N=200 items, rank r=4, T=300 rounds, σ²=0.25) confirm that the simplified phased algorithm (Alg. 4) incurs substantially lower cumulative and per-round regret than both baselines across all exploration settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its simplified phased algorithm (Alg. 4) against two baselines on a synthetic instance (M=200, N=200, r=4, T=300, σ²=0.25): (1) Alternating Minimization (AM) from Dadkhahi & Negahban (2018), and (2) Explore-Then-Commit (ETC) from Jain & Pal (2023) with exploration periods of 25, 50, and 75 rounds. The proposed algorithm achieves significantly lower cumulative regret than both AM and all ETC variants. In per-round regret, the proposed algorithm with 25 exploration rounds outperforms ETC with the same 25-round exploration period, while ETC with longer exploration (50, 75 rounds) reduces exploitation regret per round but pays a larger exploration cost. The AM algorithm exhibits similarly large regret throughout. The theoretical contribution is that PHASEDCLUSTERELIM improves the gap dependence from \(\Delta^{-2}\) (greedy) to \(\Delta_{\text{hott}}^{-2} + \Delta^{-1}\), where \(\Delta_{\text{hott}} \gg \Delta\) in practice, and DETERMINANTELIM recovers the optimal rank-1 guarantee of Jain & Pal (2023) while extending to rank r under weaker assumptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online low-rank matrix completion framework of Jain & Pal (2023) and Sen et al. (2017), which achieved regret bounds of Õ(NM^{-1}Δ^{-2}) via greedy algorithms, by introducing the 'hott items' (separability) assumption to obtain near-optimal Õ(NM^{-1}(Δ^{-1}+Δ_hott^{-2})) regret for rank-r > 1 reward matrices. The DETERMINANTELIM algorithm draws directly from Kveton et al. (2017)'s determinant-based approach for identifying extremal entries in low-rank matrices, adapting it to the multi-user recommendation setting where r items must be recommended to every user simultaneously. The paper departs from prior optimal algorithms (which were restricted to rank-1 or required restrictive latent cluster assumptions) by showing that the weaker separability condition—where only the best item per user must belong to a small set of 'hott' items—suffices for strong guarantees without requiring identical preference orderings within clusters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first near-optimal regret guarantees for online matrix completion at rank > 1 under a relatively benign assumption (hott items/separability), closing a gap that prior work could only address for rank-1 or under much stronger cluster-identity assumptions. The counter-example (Proposition 4) demonstrating why naive item elimination fails is a useful technical insight that motivates the phased multi-label clustering design. However, the empirical validation is limited to synthetic experiments, the algorithms have exponential dependence on rank r, and the incoherence/smoothness assumptions (A3) for PHASEDCLUSTERELIM remain somewhat restrictive for practical deployment. Open questions include whether the quadratic dependence on Δ_hott^{-1} is tight, whether the methods scale to non-stationary or adversarial settings, and whether the computational bottleneck of convex matrix completion sub-modules can be replaced by faster non-convex estimators without sacrificing guarantees.
