## Problem

Graph domain adaptation (GDA) methods that align marginal node representation distributions are fundamentally inadequate when graph structure shifts: conditional structure shift (CSS) changes how nodes of the same class connect to their neighbors, and label shift (LS) changes the class distribution between domains. The paper shows that even perfect marginal alignment leaves conditional representations mismatched under these shifts, and that a shared GNN encoder provably cannot correct CSS (lower-bounding target error at 0.25 in a CSBM example).

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled source graph (adjacency matrix, node features, node labels) and an unlabeled target graph of the same schema, and produces node class predictions for the target graph. Internally it learns two auxiliary objects: a |Y|×|Y| edge-weight matrix γ (density ratios of conditional neighbor-label distributions between target and source) applied to source-graph edges during GNN message passing, and a |Y|×1 label-weight vector β (target-to-source class-frequency ratios) used to reweight the cross-entropy loss. The largest curated dataset (MAG, ~380k nodes, 1.35M edges) splits citation networks by publication country to simulate regional shift.

## Method

Pair-Align decomposes structure shift into CSS and LS and addresses each with a dedicated reweighting mechanism. For CSS, it reweights source-graph edges by γ_{ij} = P_T(Y_v=j|Y_u=i, v∈N_u) / P_S(Y_v=j|Y_u=i, v∈N_u), which transforms the source neighborhood multiset distribution to match the target's, guaranteeing conditional alignment layer-by-layer under an SBM-style edge-independence assumption (Theorem 3.3). For LS, it weights the source cross-entropy loss by β_i = P_T(Y=i)/P_S(Y=i). Both γ and β are estimated iteratively via pseudo-label distribution alignment: γ is derived from a pairwise edge-type weight w solved by the constrained least-squares system Σw=ν (using soft classifier outputs on both graphs), and β from the node-level system Cβ=μ. The full pipeline alternates GNN training with periodic re-estimation of w, γ, and β every t epochs, with L2 regularization and a smoothing term δ to stabilize early-training estimates.

## Results

On the MAG dataset (377k nodes, 1.35M edges), PA-BOTH achieves 40.06% accuracy on US→CN and 45.16% on CN→US, outperforming the best baseline (IWDAN) by 16.7 and 13.4 percentage points respectively, and StruRW by 8.5 and 8.1 points. On the Pileup Mitigation task, PA-BOTH reaches 55.45% F1 (PU10→30) and 71.23% F1 (PU50→10), surpassing StruRW (52.41% and 70.93%) and SpecReg (52.61% and 67.95%). On synthetic CSBM with joint CSS+LS shift, PA-BOTH achieves 95.55% accuracy versus 87.01% for IWDAN (best baseline) and 75.96% for StruRW. On DBLP→ACM, PA-BOTH attains 70.97% accuracy, exceeding IWDAN (66.96%) and DANN (65.34%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DANN (Ganin et al., 2016) and IWDAN (Tachet des Combes et al., 2020) perform at or near ERM levels on MAG (e.g., 23.39% vs. 26.92% for US→CN), confirming that marginal or conditional feature alignment alone is insufficient when structure shift dominates. UDAGCN (Wu et al., 2020) runs out of memory on the full MAG graphs (377k nodes) and is only competitive on smaller datasets like Pileup. StruRW (Liu et al., 2023) is the closest competitor, achieving 31.58% on US→CN MAG and 52.41% F1 on PU10→30 Pileup, but PA-BOTH surpasses it by 8.5 and 3.0 points respectively, with the paper reporting over 25% relative improvement across MAG domains. SpecReg (You et al., 2023) is competitive on Pileup (52.61% F1 on PU10→30) but lags PA-BOTH by 2.8 points and is outperformed by 10+ points on MAG. IWDAN is the best baseline on the synthetic CSS+LS setting (87.01% vs. 95.55% for PA-BOTH) and on Arxiv time-shift, but remains 8.5 points behind PA-BOTH on the hardest synthetic scenario.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the non-graph domain adaptation literature on conditional distribution matching (Tachet des Combes et al., 2020) and label shift correction (Lipton et al., 2018) to the graph-structured setting, where the non-IID nature of interconnected nodes renders standard marginal alignment insufficient. It directly builds on StruRW (Liu et al., 2023), which first identified the need for edge reweighting to handle conditional structure shift, but departs from it by (1) using the correct γ weights (rather than w) as shown necessary by Theorem 3.3, (2) incorporating label shift correction via β-weighted loss, and (3) providing a more stable estimation procedure using soft pseudo-labels rather than hard labels. The paper positions itself as the first to rigorously formulate and simultaneously address both CSS and LS in a principled, theoretically grounded manner for node-level GDA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a useful conceptual decomposition of graph structure shift into CSS and LS, offering a clear diagnostic framework for practitioners to identify which type of shift dominates in their application. The curated MAG dataset (~380k nodes, 1.35M edges) is the largest real-world GDA benchmark to date and should enable more systematic study of region-based generalization in citation networks. Key limitations include the reliance on an SBM-like edge conditional independence assumption, the iterative estimation procedure that may be sensitive to early-training quality, and the method's specificity to node-level tasks with mean pooling. Open questions include whether the approach extends to graph-level classification, whether the SBM assumption can be relaxed for more heterogeneous graph structures, and how the method behaves under simultaneous feature and structure shift without a separate feature alignment stage.
