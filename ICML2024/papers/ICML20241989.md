## Problem

Optimizing over trained neural networks for data-driven decision-making (e.g., finding prices that maximize predicted revenue) often produces unrealistic prescriptions far from the data manifold — a phenomenon known as the Optimizer's Curse. Existing remedies either sacrifice predictive accuracy by using simpler models or rely on gradient-based methods that provide no guarantees on either optimality or realism. This paper addresses the gap by showing how to explicitly enforce realism as a tractable constraint while retaining the full predictive power of deep networks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained neural network (ReLU or general differentiable), its training dataset, a bounded polyhedral decision space (e.g., [0,1]^784 for MNIST, [0,1]^3072 for CIFAR-10), and a user-defined realism threshold t close to 1. It produces a single decision vector x* that maximizes the network's predicted output while being certified as an inlier under the LOF metric, along with the corresponding optimal objective value. For ReLU networks, the per-polytope subproblem is an LP (ℓ1/ℓ∞ distance) or SOCP (ℓ2 distance); for general differentiable models, the method uses projected gradient ascent with a single convex quadratic constraint.

## Method

The key insight is reformulating the highly non-linear Local Outlier Factor (LOF) metric with k=1 into a single tractable constraint: δ(x, x') ≤ t·max{δ(x', x''), d₁(x'')}, where x' is the highest-objective inlier in the current polytope and x'' is its nearest neighbor. This transforms the realism requirement into a linear or quadratic constraint depending on the chosen norm. For ReLU networks, the piecewise-linear geometry partitions the input space into polytopes; the paper introduces 'realistic polytopes' (those containing at least one inlier) and derives a tight upper bound on each polytope's achievable objective. An adaptive sampling algorithm then performs feed-forward passes on all inliers to identify realistic polytopes, prunes those whose upper bound falls below the best known inlier value (reducing the search space by 87.5–99.9%), and samples a small subset via softmax over remaining upper bounds, solving one LP/SOCP per sample. The approach extends to general differentiable models by replacing the per-polytope LP/SOCP with projected gradient ascent under the same LOF-derived convex constraint, and to tree ensembles and linear regression via their MIP formulations.

## Results

The paper evaluates 120 optimization problems (10 outputs × 12 networks) across MNIST and CIFAR-10 ReLU networks. The proposed AdSampling method finds the realistic solution with the highest objective in 60/80 MNIST experiments and 31/40 CIFAR-10 experiments (91/120 total), far exceeding the second-best method (PGA: 20/80 and 9/40). All other baselines (MIP, Simulated Annealing, SPSA, Genetic Algorithm) achieve 0 successes. The method also scales to networks with millions of parameters (ResNet50, ViT on ImageNet), converging in ~2–3 minutes per run on a single GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed AdSampling method dominates all five baselines across every network and dataset. The MIP formulation of Fischetti & Jo (2018) finds 0 optimal realistic solutions on the two smallest MNIST networks (3×50, 3×100) and times out (NA) on all larger networks within the 60-second window, with average optimality gaps of 307.78% and 1029.98% on the two solvable cases. Projected Gradient Ascent (PGA, Kingma & Ba 2014) is the only other method to find any optimal solutions, achieving 20/80 on MNIST and 9/40 on CIFAR-10, but is consistently outperformed by AdSampling on every individual network (e.g., 9 vs. 1 on MNIST 9×100, 9 vs. 1 on CIFAR 4×100). Simulated Annealing (Kirkpatrick 1983), SPSA (Spall 1992), and the Genetic Algorithm (Gad 2021) all achieve 0 successes across all 120 experiments, indicating that stochastic optimization without the polytope-restriction strategy cannot reliably locate the optimal realistic solution. A key scalability advantage is that AdSampling solves only LP/SOCP subproblems over selected realistic polytopes, enabling it to handle networks with millions of parameters (ResNet50, ViT) in minutes, whereas MIP-based approaches become intractable beyond 2–3 hidden layers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MIP-based optimization framework for trained ReLU networks (Fischetti & Jo, 2018; Anderson et al., 2020; Tsay et al., 2021) by introducing a realism constraint that addresses the Optimizer's Curse. The key conceptual move is repurposing the Local Outlier Factor metric—previously used as an evaluation tool in outlier detection or as a soft regularization in counterfactual explanation work (Kanamori et al., 2020; Tsiourvas et al., 2024)—as a single hard optimization constraint, exploiting its max-operator structure to yield a tractable linear or quadratic constraint. The adaptive sampling algorithm further leverages the piecewise-linear geometry of ReLU networks (Serra et al., 2018) to partition the decision space into realistic polytopes, converting one intractable MIP into many easy LP/SOCP subproblems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Optimizer's Curse is a fundamental barrier to deploying neural networks in high-stakes prescriptive analytics (pricing, treatment selection, resource allocation), where unrealistic prescriptions are not merely suboptimal but potentially harmful. This paper provides the first principled, scalable method that certifies realism while preserving optimality within each realistic region, and demonstrates practical scaling to ResNet-50 and ViT on ImageNet in minutes. Open questions include: how the approach behaves when the data manifold is highly non-convex or the LOF threshold t is poorly calibrated, whether the k=1 LOF constraint is sufficient for high-dimensional spaces, and how to integrate the realism constraint into end-to-end training rather than post-hoc optimization.
