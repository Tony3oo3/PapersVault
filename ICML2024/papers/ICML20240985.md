## Problem

Existing multi-view feature selection methods either concatenate views and apply single-view selectors or rely on pre-defined knowledge graphs, failing to capture synergistic interactions where features from different views provide more information about the target jointly than individually. This paper formally defines the problem of selecting minimal feature subsets with positive (synergistic) and zero/negative (non-synergistic) interaction information across multiple views, a task that has received surprisingly little attention despite the prevalence of multi-view data in domains like multi-omics.

## Input / Output

The method takes V views of tabular features (each view v has p_v dimensions) and a target label (regression or C-class classification), and produces two mutually exclusive sets of per-view binary gate vectors: synergistic gates g_S^v and non-synergistic gates g_N^v, each identifying which features in view v exhibit the respective interaction type. The gates are realized as clamped Gaussian perturbations of learned means (μ_S, μ_N), and a third union gate g_A = max(g_S, g_N) feeds a separate predictor. The selected subsets are constrained to be minimal (sparse) while satisfying the interaction information inequality, and the method also outputs target predictions from three dedicated predictors (synergistic, non-synergistic, and union).

## Method

SynFS formulates multi-view synergistic and non-synergistic feature selection as a constrained combinatorial optimization over binary gate vectors, where the constraint is expressed via interaction information: I(Y; X̄_S) > Σ_v I(Y; X_S^v) for synergistic features and the reverse inequality for non-synergistic features. To make this tractable, the paper applies a Gaussian-based continuous relaxation (following Yamada et al., 2020) to the Bernoulli gates, converting the problem into a differentiable Lagrangian objective that minimizes joint-view cross-entropy while maximizing marginal-view cross-entropy (for synergistic) or vice versa (for non-synergistic). The architecture comprises V view-specific synergistic selectors, V view-specific non-synergistic selectors, and three predictors (synergistic, non-synergistic, union), with a cosine-similarity loss enforcing mutual exclusivity between the two gate sets. Training proceeds iteratively—updating predictors, then the union predictor with both selectors, then each selector independently with the other components detached—to resolve the conflicting optimization directions that would otherwise drive synergistic selectors toward trivial solutions.

## Results

On synthetic datasets, SynFS achieves a perfect J-Index of 1.00 with 100% TPR and 0% FDR across all four scenarios (Syn1–Syn4), correctly identifying both synergistic and non-synergistic features. On real-world datasets, SynFS is the only method that produces positive Set Interaction Scores for synergistic features (SI(S) = 0.36 on METABRIC, 7.87 on TCGA, 0.38 on PBMC), while all baselines yield negative SI(S) values. In terms of predictive performance, SynFS achieves AUROC of 0.862 (METABRIC), 0.683 (TCGA), and 0.984 (PBMC), using only 6–7% of all features while remaining competitive with full-feature MLP baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SynFS decisively outperforms all three baselines on the key interaction-discovery metric: it is the only method producing positive SI(S) values across all three real-world datasets, whereas CompFS, STG, and RForest all yield negative SI(S) (ranging from −0.22 to −21.80), indicating they fail to identify genuinely synergistic cross-view features. On synthetic data with known ground truth, SynFS achieves a perfect J-Index of 1.00 on all four scenarios, while CompFS drops to 0.40–0.48 on the weaker-signal settings (Syn2, Syn3) and STG/RForest receive only normalized Group Similarity scores of 0.33–0.50. In terms of raw predictive AUROC, SynFS is competitive but not always the best: STG edges ahead on METABRIC (0.887 vs. 0.862) and CompFS on TCGA (0.686 vs. 0.683) and PBMC (0.987 vs. 0.984), though the gaps are within one standard deviation. The critical distinction is that SynFS achieves these competitive AUROC scores while simultaneously providing interpretable synergistic/non-synergistic feature partitions, a capability none of the baselines offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the embedded deep-learning feature selection paradigm (STG, CompFS) by introducing an information-theoretic objective based on interaction information to decompose selected features into synergistic and non-synergistic subsets across multiple views. It builds directly on the Gaussian-based continuous relaxation of binary gate vectors from STG (Yamada et al., 2020) and the composite feature grouping idea from CompFS (Imrie et al., 2022), but departs from both by formalizing a multi-view interaction-aware selection problem that neither addresses. The key conceptual departure is treating the multi-view setting not as a concatenation problem but as a structured interaction-discovery problem where the joint MI must exceed (or not exceed) the sum of marginal MIs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-omics and multi-modal biomedical data are increasingly central to precision medicine, yet the cross-view interactions that drive disease mechanisms remain largely unexplored by existing feature selection tools. SynFS provides the first principled method to categorize discovered features by interaction type, enabling interpretable biological hypotheses (validated here against medical literature for breast cancer and T-cell biology). Open questions include scalability beyond 2–3 views, handling of higher-order (non-pairwise) interaction structures, and whether the approximate MI estimation via cross-entropy remains reliable in high-dimensional, noisy real-world settings. The method also requires a priori definition of views, which can be non-trivial for single-modality data.
