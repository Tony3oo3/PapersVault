## Problem

Existing MARL methods for learning Stackelberg equilibrium strategies (AQL, BiRL, STEP) are restricted to environments with shared global states and require sequential policy updates for each agent, severely limiting scalability and applicability. No prior approach can simultaneously handle diverse environmental configurations (partial observation, individual global states, shared states) and mixed cooperative-competitive tasks while efficiently converging to SE solutions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242461/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes either a shared global state vector or per-agent local observation vectors (plus optional global state) as input tokens to the Inner Transformer Block, and the prioritized sequence of leader agents' actions as input to the Outer Transformer Block. Outputs are per-agent actions sampled autoregressively in priority order (supporting both continuous and discrete action spaces) and per-agent state value estimates. During training, joint action sequences are stored in a replay buffer enabling parallel gradient updates across all agents, whereas during execution actions are generated sequentially in priority order.

## Method

STEER's core insight is that the hierarchical leader-follower decision structure of Stackelberg games naturally maps onto autoregressive sequence modeling. The architecture comprises an Inner Transformer Block (ITB) that encodes n+1 tokens (a global state token plus n agent observation tokens) via multi-head self-attention to produce a global state embedding and agent-specific embeddings, and an Outer Transformer Block (OTB) that takes the global state embedding concatenated with leader actions as a causal sequence and uses masked multi-head self-attention to generate decision information autoregressively. Actor and Critic heads combine ITB and OTB outputs to form each agent's sub-game state embedding, from which actions and value estimates are predicted. Training uses PPO with parallel updates of all agents' policies (a key efficiency gain over sequential SG-based methods), and a knowledge distillation module with Log-RMSE and entropy loss enables decentralized execution via lightweight MLP student networks.

## Results

In matrix game scenarios, STEER achieves 100% convergence to the Stackelberg equilibrium in Penalty (k=0, k=-100) and Mixing, 95% in Coordination, 96% in Cooperation, and 72% in the extreme Penalty (k=-1000) setting, outperforming all baselines. In complex scenarios (MA-MuJoCo, GRF, HORM), STEER achieves the highest evaluation scores across all 9 sub-scenarios with faster convergence (higher sample efficiency). STEER also demonstrates significantly lower wall-clock training time compared to sequential-update baselines like STEP and HAPPO, particularly as the number of agents increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STEER outperforms all four baselines across every matrix game scenario. Against the closest SG-based competitor STEP, STEER leads by 28 percentage points in Penalty k=-1000 (72% vs. 44%), 1 point in Coordination (95% vs. 94%), and 6 points in Cooperation (96% vs. 90%), while matching STEP at 100% in Penalty k=0, k=-100, and Mixing. Against Transformer-based MAT, STEER is dramatically superior: MAT achieves 0% convergence in Penalty k=-100 and k=-1000, 0% in Mixing, and only 5% in Cooperation, whereas STEER maintains 72–100%. HAPPO and MAPPO, designed for fully cooperative or parameter-sharing settings, fail catastrophically in mixed-reward scenarios (0% in Penalty k≤-100, 6–28% in Mixing). In the three complex benchmarks (MA-MuJoCo, GRF, HORM), STEER achieves the highest final performance and fastest convergence in all 9 sub-scenarios, with the gap over baselines widening in mixed-reward and partial-observation settings where MAPPO and MAT degrade most severely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: SG-based MARL (AQL, BiRL, STEP) and autoregressive sequence models for RL (Decision Transformer, MAT, MADT). It departs from prior SG-based MARL by eliminating the shared-state assumption and replacing sequential policy updates with parallel Transformer-based training, while departing from prior Transformer-based MARL (MAT, MADT) by explicitly encoding the hierarchical leader-follower structure of SG into the architecture via the Outer Transformer Block. The central conceptual move is recognizing that SG's sequential decision structure is isomorphic to autoregressive generation, enabling a unified dual-Transformer design (ITB for state abstraction, OTB for sequential policy fitting) that prior work had not exploited.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STEER provides a practical, scalable architecture for learning Stackelberg equilibrium strategies across a wide range of environmental configurations (shared vs. partial observation, continuous vs. discrete control, cooperative vs. mixed tasks) that prior SG-based MARL methods could not handle simultaneously. The knowledge distillation scheme for decentralized execution is a useful bridge for real-world deployment where centralized access to all agents' actions is unavailable. Open questions include whether the fixed priority ordering can be made adaptive per time step (the authors note this as future work), how the method scales to very large agent counts, and whether the centralized training assumption is always justified in practice.
