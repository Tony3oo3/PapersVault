## Problem

Prior methods for learning from multiple noisy label sources (e.g., confusion-matrix estimation, MoE-based approaches) have overlooked the self-cognition ability of neural networks—their inherent capacity to autonomously distinguish noisy samples during training. This paper provides the first theoretical analysis showing that neural networks can simultaneously recognize instance-wise noise within each source and annotator-wise quality across sources, and builds a denoising method directly on these insights.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted feature vectors (768-dim BERT embeddings for text, 512-dim ResNet-18 features for SVHN, 100-dim LeNet features for MNIST, or 16 raw tabular features for Bank) paired with s=4 sets of binary noisy labels per sample. During training, the multi-tower model produces per-source predicted probabilities; at inference, a single averaged ensemble score σ((1/s)Σz_j(x)) is used. A distilled single-tower MLP with the same hidden dimension (128) serves as the deployment-friendly output model, requiring significantly fewer parameters than the multi-tower original.

## Method

SDM employs a multi-tower MLP (shared first layer + s tower heads) trained with binary cross-entropy on all noisy sources. A self-cognition module, active only during the first t₀=20 epochs, computes (a) an instance-wise filter matrix M^t via a small-loss percentile threshold per source (Theorem 2.1) and (b) an annotator-wise quality vector q^t via softmax-normalized cosine similarity between each tower's predictions and its source's noisy labels (Corollary 2.3). These are aggregated into a row-normalized reliability matrix W^t, which drives a mutual-denoising loss: each tower is pulled toward an EMA-weighted fusion of the other towers' logits, with the pull strength inversely proportional to W_{i,j} (i.e., less-reliable labels receive more external correction). A selective distillation module then trains a single-tower student using weighted random selection over noisy labels and teacher predictions, guided by W^t, yielding a lightweight model with comparable performance.

## Results

SDM achieves the highest AUC on 10 out of 12 dataset-noise-type combinations in the main comparison (Table 1). Under class-dependent noise, SDM reaches 85.43% AUC on Yelp, 75.93% on IMDb, and 81.39% on AgNews, outperforming the strongest baselines by 2.9–6.3 points. Under instance-dependent noise, SDM attains 98.17% AUC on MNIST and 89.45% on AgNews. On two real-world crowdsourced datasets (Music, Polarity), SDM also leads with 74.55% and 76.43% AUC respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDM outperforms all 11 baselines on 10 of 12 simulated dataset-noise combinations, with the largest margins on AgNews under class-dependent noise (+6.27 AUC over ADMoE) and IMDb under class-dependent noise (+3.93 over EBCC). The two exceptions are SVHN and MNIST under class-dependent noise, where CVL edges out SDM by 1.31 and 1.15 AUC points respectively, likely because these image datasets have lower inherent label quality that limits the self-cognition signal. Under instance-dependent noise, SDM leads on all six datasets, with the closest margin being +0.03 AUC on Bank (93.44 vs. CrowdLayer's 93.41). On the two real-world crowdsourced datasets (Music, Polarity), SDM also achieves the best AUC (74.55 and 76.43), surpassing the strongest baselines ADMoE and SLF by 1.81 and 0.30 points. The distilled single-tower model g_θ matches or exceeds the multi-tower G_Θ in AUC while using significantly fewer parameters, confirming the effectiveness of the selective distillation module.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the small-loss criterion (Gui et al., 2021) from single noisy label sources to the multi-source setting, providing theorems that formalize how neural networks self-recognize noise at both the instance level (within each source) and the annotator level (across sources). It departs from prior multi-source methods (CrowdLayer, DoctorNet, CVL, SLF, ADMoE) that focus on explicitly estimating confusion matrices or annotator weights, instead arguing that the network's inherent memorization dynamics already encode this information and can be directly exploited via loss-based filtering and cosine-similarity-based quality estimation. The multi-tower architecture draws on multi-task learning principles, and the selective distillation module repurposes weighted random selection to transfer knowledge from the multi-tower teacher to a lightweight student.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical results (Theorems 2.1–2.2, Corollary 2.3) provide a principled justification for why small-loss filtering and source-quality estimation work in multi-source settings, filling a gap where prior multi-source methods lacked such grounding. The practical impact is a method that consistently outperforms strong baselines across six datasets and two noise types, with the added benefit of a distilled single-tower model that matches multi-tower performance at lower inference cost. Key limitations include restriction to binary classification (the authors explicitly note multi-class extension requires new theoretical assumptions), reliance on the class-dependent noise assumption in theory (though empirically verified beyond it), and the observation that very low-quality sources can render the instance-wise filter ineffective, relying instead on the annotator-wise quality vector to down-weight them.
