## Problem

Prior theoretical works (Akyürek et al. 2022; von Oswald et al. 2023) demonstrate that Transformers can simulate gradient descent in their forward pass, but under restrictive assumptions—explicit ICL training objectives, hand-constructed sparse weight matrices, and narrow task families—that diverge sharply from how real LLMs are pretrained on natural data. This paper asks whether these theoretical equivalences between ICL and GD provide any evidence for the emergent ICL actually observed in pretrained models like LLaMA-7B, or whether the two mechanisms are fundamentally different.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a pretrained LLM (primarily LLaMA-7B, with GPT-J and Qwen-7B for ablations) and evaluates it under three conditions: ICL with N∈{1,2,4,8} demonstrations in the prompt, full-model GD fine-tuning on the same N demonstrations (200 epochs, four learning rates), and sub-model GD fine-tuning only W_V of a single deep or middle layer. Outputs are compared via three distributional metrics—accuracy, top-10 token overlap, and overlap cosine similarity (OCS)—plus an order-sensitivity metric (standard deviation of output probability distributions across 10 random demonstration orderings), all evaluated on four classification benchmarks (AGNews, SST-2, CommitmentBank, RTE).

## Method

The paper combines theoretical arguments with large-scale empirical evaluation. It first identifies three critical gaps in prior ICL≈GD theories: prior works train with an explicit ICL objective rather than the CLM objective used in real pretraining; hand-constructed weight matrices require >99.99% sparsity in W_K/W_Q (far exceeding real LLM sparsity); and ICL ability persists across evolving parameter checkpoints rather than requiring a single fixed weight configuration. It then proves Theorem 1, showing that algorithmic equivalence to ICL implies identical order sensitivity, and demonstrates that GD is order-stable (batch gradient) while ICL is order-sensitive (causal attention), yielding a contradiction. Finally, it conducts comprehensive empirical comparisons on LLaMA-7B across four datasets, four demonstration sizes, multiple learning rates, and three random seeds, finding consistent gaps between ICL and all GD variants on accuracy, token overlap, and OCS, with ICL outperforming GD at small demo counts and showing different scaling behavior.

## Results

On LLaMa-7B with 8 in-context demonstrations, ICL consistently outperforms full-model GD fine-tuning across all four datasets (e.g., AGNews: ICL > GD; SST-2: ICL > GD). The distributional metrics (Token Overlap and Overlap Cosine Similarity) reveal that ICL and GD produce substantially different output probability distributions — the overlap between ICL and GD outputs is consistently lower than the overlap between two ICL runs with different demonstration orderings. GD accuracy improves dramatically with more samples (e.g., AGNews: 0.42 with 8 demos → 0.69 with 512 demos), highlighting that ICL leverages prior knowledge rather than simply fitting to demonstrations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ICL against two GD-based baselines derived from von Oswald et al. (2023) and Akyürek et al. (2022): full-model GD fine-tuning and sub-model GD (fine-tuning only W_V of a single deep or middle layer). ICL outperforms all GD variants on accuracy when only 1–8 demonstrations are available, across AGNews, SST-2, CB, and RTE. On the distributional metrics (Token Overlap and OCS), ICL and GD outputs show a clear gap — the overlap between ICL and GD predictions is substantially lower than the overlap between two ICL runs with permuted demonstration order, indicating fundamentally different functional behavior. The sub-model GD variants (deep layer W_V and middle layer W_V) perform comparably to each other but both fall short of ICL, and neither reproduces ICL's high order-sensitivity. The paper concludes that ICL is not algorithmically equivalent to GD or its sub-model variants, as the two mechanisms modify the output distribution differently and respond inconsistently to demonstration ordering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a critical examination of the ICL≈GD equivalence hypothesis advanced by von Oswald et al. (2023), Akyürek et al. (2022), Garg et al. (2022), and Dai et al. (2023). It departs from these works by distinguishing between two different claims: hypothesis 1 (universal equivalence for any pretrained Transformer on any task) versus hypothesis 2 (existence of some weights that simulate GD), arguing that prior work only addresses the latter. The paper draws on empirical ICL studies (Lu et al., 2022; Hahn & Goyal, 2023) for order-sensitivity evidence and on distributional explanations (Xie et al., 2021; Pan et al., 2023) as alternative frameworks, while introducing new evaluation metrics (Token Overlap, Overlap Cosine Similarity) to compare ICL and GD output distributions at a finer granularity than accuracy alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ICL≈GD hypothesis has been influential in shaping how the community thinks about the mechanism of in-context learning, and this paper argues that the theoretical constructions (hand-constructed sparse weights, ICL-objective training) are too far from real pretrained LLMs to support the equivalence claim for naturally emergent ICL. The order-sensitivity theorem provides a clean theoretical argument, and the empirical gap between ICL and GD across multiple metrics, datasets, and model sizes suggests the two mechanisms operate differently. However, the paper explicitly frames the equivalence as an 'open hypothesis' rather than definitively refuted, and it does not offer a positive alternative explanation for how ICL works in pretrained models. Open questions include whether restricted conditions (specific task distributions, demonstration counts) could salvage a partial equivalence, and what the true functional mechanism of emergent ICL is.
