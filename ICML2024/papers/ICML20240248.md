## Problem

Existing interpretable GNNs (XGNNs) rely on attention-based weighted message passing to extract subgraphs and make predictions, but there is no theoretical guarantee that this paradigm faithfully approximates the true subgraph distribution. The paper shows that the prevalent approach of feeding a 'soft' expected subgraph into the classifier introduces a systematic approximation gap to the Subgraph Multilinear Extension (SubMT), leading to degenerated interpretability and poor out-of-distribution generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240248/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph G=(A,X) with binary adjacency matrix and node feature matrix, or a geometric graph (point cloud with 3D coordinates), as input. It produces a graph-level class label and an interpretable subgraph encoded as per-edge sampling probabilities α∈[0,1]^m that define a factorized Bernoulli subgraph distribution. The subgraph extractor and classifier share a graph encoder, and the attention scores serve dual roles: defining the sampling distribution for GMT-sam and providing the interpretable edge importance scores for visualization.

## Method

The paper formulates interpretable subgraph learning as the Subgraph Multilinear Extension (SubMT), F_c(α;G)=E_{G_c~g(G)}[f_c(G_c)], and proves that existing XGNNs (e.g., GSAT) cannot approximate SubMT because applying the nonlinear classifier to the expected soft subgraph violates the linearity required by the expectation (Proposition 3.3). To resolve this, GMT introduces two variants: GMT-lin reduces weighted message passing to a single round to preserve linearity, while GMT-sam performs t rounds of random subgraph sampling from the attention-elicited Bernoulli distribution and averages the classifier outputs over discrete samples, with Gumbel-softmax or straight-through estimators enabling gradient backpropagation through the discrete sampling. A final neural SubMT stage retrains a new classifier with the frozen subgraph extractor to distill the MCMC approximation into a single forward pass. The paper also introduces (δ,ε)-counterfactual fidelity as a new interpretability measure, proving that any XGNN that ε-approximates SubMT necessarily satisfies counterfactual fidelity (Proposition 4.2).

## Results

On regular graph benchmarks, GMT-SAM with GIN achieves 99.62% AUROC on BA-2Motifs, 99.87% on Mutag, 86.50% on MNIST-75SP, and 85.50% on Spurious-Motif (b=0.5), outperforming the strongest XGNN baseline GSAT (98.85%, 99.35%, 80.47%, 74.49%) by up to 11 percentage points. With PNA backbone, GMT-SAM reaches 88.27% on Spurious-Motif (b=0.5) versus GSAT's 79.84%. On geometric graphs, GMT-LIN achieves 96.17% ROC AUC on SynMol (vs. LRI-Bernoulli's 89.22%), and GMT-SAM attains the best prediction AUC across all four geometric benchmarks (e.g., 98.55% on ActStrack vs. ERM's 97.40%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMT-SAM consistently outperforms GSAT (the strongest XGNN baseline) across all regular graph benchmarks: on Spurious-Motif (b=0.5) with GIN, GMT-SAM achieves 85.50% AUROC vs. GSAT's 74.49% (+11 pp), and with PNA, 88.27% vs. 79.84% (+8.4 pp). On MNIST-75SP, GMT-SAM (GIN) improves over GSAT by 6 pp (86.50% vs. 80.47%). GMT-LIN, the simpler variant, also beats GSAT on most benchmarks (e.g., 80.57% vs. 65.25% on Spurious-Motif b=0.9 with GIN) but is slightly below GSAT on BA-2Motifs (98.36% vs. 98.85%). Against post-hoc methods, GMT-SAM (GIN) surpasses GNNExplainer (67.35%), PGExplainer (84.59%), and GraphMask (92.54%) on BA-2Motifs, and dramatically outperforms IB-Subgraph (86.06%) and DIR (82.78%) under distribution shift. On geometric graphs, GMT-LIN achieves 96.17% ROC AUC on SynMol vs. LRI-Bernoulli's 89.22% and GradGeo's 76.38%, while GMT-SAM attains the best prediction AUC on all four geometric datasets (e.g., 87.19% on PLBind vs. ERM's 85.31%). For prediction accuracy under OOD shift (Table 2), GMT-SAM (GIN) reaches 60.09% on Spurious-Motif b=0.5 vs. GSAT's 47.45% (+12.6 pp), and GMT-SAM (PNA) reaches 71.98% vs. GSAT's 68.41%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the two-step XGNN paradigm (subgraph extraction via attention + classification) established by IB-subgraph (Yu et al., 2021) and GSAT (Miao et al., 2022), but reframes the subgraph extraction problem through the lens of multilinear extension from submodular optimization (Calinescu et al., 2007). The key conceptual departure is identifying that the standard weighted message passing used by GSAT and other XGNNs fundamentally fails to approximate the Subgraph Multilinear Extension (SubMT) due to the non-linearity of the expectation over k>1 message passing rounds. GMT then replaces this biased approximation with either single-round linearized message passing (GMT-lin) or explicit random subgraph sampling via MCMC (GMT-sam), providing the first provable SubMT approximation guarantees for XGNNs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that the dominant weighted message passing paradigm in XGNNs has a fundamental approximation gap challenges a core working assumption in the interpretable GNN community and provides a principled explanation for why existing methods exhibit degraded interpretability under distribution shifts. The counterfactual fidelity metric offers the first faithfulness measure tailored specifically to intrinsic XGNNs rather than post-hoc explanations. Open questions include the computational overhead of GMT-sam at scale (particularly for node-level tasks with millions of nodes), the optimization tension between interpretation and generalization objectives, and whether more efficient SubMT approximations exist that avoid repeated sampling.
