## Problem

While transduction and rejection have been shown theoretically to yield adversarial robustness guarantees unattainable by either technique alone, no practical deep-learning defense has realized their promise—Goldwasser et al.'s (2020) transduction+rejection approach performed poorly under strong adaptive attacks like GMSA. This paper bridges that gap by providing both an improved theoretical sample-complexity bound and a practical algorithm that achieves state-of-the-art robust accuracy on standard benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241585/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes n labeled training images and m unlabeled test images (potentially adversarially perturbed within an ℓ∞ budget) and outputs, for each test image, either a class label or a rejection symbol ⊥. Because the model is retrained on each new batch of test inputs (transductive setting), it is suited to batch-processing scenarios rather than single-sample inference. Rejecting a perturbed input incurs no penalty; only rejecting a clean input counts as an error, which makes the rejection mechanism a free robustness gain on adversarial examples.

## Method

The proposed defense, TLDR, operates in two steps. Step 1 trains a CNN (ResNet-20 or WideResNet) via adversarial training on both the training set and the potentially perturbed test set, using a robust cross-entropy objective with private randomness (following TADV). Step 2 applies an empirical classifier-to-selective-classifier transformation: for each test point, PGD is run within a configurable rejection radius (ε/4 in practice) to check whether the prediction can be flipped; if a flip exists, the point is rejected (⊥), otherwise the original prediction is retained. Theoretically, this is grounded in a novel application of Tramer's classifier-to-detector reduction in the transductive setting, which weakens the required data condition from OPT_{U²}=0 (transduction alone) to OPT_{U^{2/3}}=0, yielding linear rather than exponential dependence on VC dimension. The paper also designs an adaptive attack combining GMSA with a rejection-aware loss L_REJ that simultaneously targets misclassification and incorrect rejection, providing a rigorous evaluation framework for the new transduction+rejection setting.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ℓ∞ budget 8/255, TLDR with WideResNet-28-10 achieves 81.6% transductive robust accuracy (20.8% rejection rate), surpassing the strongest inductive baseline (71.1% from Peng et al.) by over 10 points. On CIFAR-100 under the same budget, TLDR reaches 57.9% robust accuracy (17.1% rejection rate), exceeding the best existing baseline (42.7% from Wang et al.) by over 15 points. On MNIST (ℓ∞ budget 0.3), TLDR achieves 97.2% robust accuracy, outperforming the best transductive baseline TADV (94.3%) and standard adversarial training (89.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TLDR substantially outperforms all inductive baselines: on CIFAR-10 it beats Peng et al. (71.1%) by 10.5 points and Wang et al. (70.7%) by 10.9 points; on CIFAR-100 it exceeds Wang et al. (42.7%) by 15.2 points. Against transduction-only defenses, TLDR (73.9% on CIFAR-10 with ResNet-20) surpasses TADV (54.1%), RMC (39.6%), and DANN (5.5%) by large margins. The prior transduction+rejection method Rejectron (Goldwasser et al.) performs poorly under strong attacks (14.5% on CIFAR-10), while TLDR achieves 73.9% under the same GMSA-based evaluation. On MNIST, TLDR (97.2%) also outperforms the rejection-only baseline AT-with-rejection (96.8%) and all transduction-only methods. The paper's adaptive attack (GMSA with L_REJ) is shown to be significantly stronger than standard attacks (AutoAttack, PGD) against TLDR, confirming the evaluation is rigorous.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Tramer's (2022) classifier-to-detector reduction, which had previously been used only to demonstrate the vulnerability of defenses, as a constructive tool for building robust selective classifiers. Combined with the transductive robust learning framework of Montasser et al. (2021) and the GMSA attack of Chen et al. (2022), the paper derives the first practical algorithm (TLDR) for the transduction+rejection setting formalized by Goldwasser et al. (2020), whose theoretical guarantees were previously unrealizable in deep learning. The key conceptual departure is showing that bounded perturbations (critical for the construction to work) allow a novel application of the reduction that yields strictly weaker data assumptions (OPT_{U^{2/3}}=0 vs. OPT_{U^2}=0) than transduction alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first practical realization of transduction+rejection for adversarial robustness in deep learning, achieving 81.6% robust accuracy on CIFAR-10 (vs. 71.1% inductive SOTA) and 57.9% on CIFAR-100 (vs. 42.7%), with a clean theoretical explanation for why the combination is strictly stronger than either component alone. The empirical approximation of Tramer's computationally infeasible reduction via a simple PGD step is a practical contribution that could inform future efficient reductions. Key limitations include the transductive requirement (retraining per batch, limiting latency-sensitive deployment), the lack of a completeness guarantee under the weakest assumptions, and evaluation only on CIFAR-scale datasets. Open questions include scaling to ImageNet, developing a truly efficient reduction, and characterizing when the defense set Δ is nonempty under minimal assumptions.
