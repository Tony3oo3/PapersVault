## Problem

AlphaFold and ESMFold predict a single best structure per sequence, but proteins function as conformational ensembles with distinct states and collective motions. Existing diversification strategies (e.g., MSA subsampling) are limited to AlphaFold's MSA input, cannot generalize to PLM-based predictors like ESMFold, and cannot be trained on molecular dynamics ensembles beyond the PDB.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240896/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a protein sequence and MSA (for AlphaFLOW) or sequence alone (for ESMFLOW), plus an optional PDB template structure, and iteratively generates all-atom protein structures by sampling from the learned conformational distribution. Internally, the generative process operates on Cβ coordinates (α-carbon for glycine) as the primary structural representation, with all-atom positions and residue frames filled in as auxiliary outputs. A single sample requires up to 10 sequential forward passes of the base predictor, though a distilled variant reduces this to one pass.

## Method

The core insight is that AlphaFold/ESMFold can be repurposed as denoising models by prepending an input embedding module (analogous to the template embedding stack) that ingests a noisy structure and a time embedding, requiring minimal architectural modification. Flow matching is then applied with a harmonic (polymer-like) prior over Cβ coordinates, where the conditional probability path linearly interpolates between prior noise and data after RMSD alignment. Because the base networks are SE(3)-invariant, standard MSE loss is infeasible; instead, the authors define flow matching on the quotient space R^{3N}/SE(3) and train with a squared FAPE loss that learns the Fréchet mean of the conditional distribution. Training proceeds in two stages: fine-tuning on PDB structures, then continued fine-tuning on ATLAS MD ensembles, with an optional template-conditioned variant that accepts the initial PDB structure to diversify solved conformations.

## Results

On the ATLAS MD ensemble benchmark (82 test proteins), AlphaFLOW-MD achieves a per-target RMSF Pearson correlation of 0.85 with ground truth, a root mean Wasserstein distance (RMWD) of 2.61 Å, and a 44% success rate for capturing the dominant collective motion (PC cosine similarity > 0.5). On the PDB ensemble benchmark (100 test proteins), AlphaFLOW traces a substantially superior precision-diversity Pareto frontier compared to MSA subsampling, reaching diversity of 0.185 at precision 0.810, while MSA subsampling at comparable diversity (0.125) drops to precision 0.757. When provided with a PDB template (AlphaFLOW-MD+Templates), RMWD improves to 1.95 Å and per-target RMSF correlation reaches 0.90, and sampling converges to equilibrium properties faster than replicate MD simulations at equivalent GPU-hour budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlphaFLOW-MD substantially outperforms MSA subsampling (Del Alamo et al., 2022) across all three evaluation categories: it achieves a per-target RMSF correlation of 0.85 vs. 0.52 for MSA depth 48, a RMWD of 2.61 vs. 5.32, and doubles the dominant-motion capture rate (44% vs. 18% PC-sim > 0.5). Against unmodified AlphaFold (Jumper et al., 2021), AlphaFLOW-MD matches the ground-truth flexibility level (pairwise RMSD 2.89 vs. GT 2.90) while AlphaFold is essentially deterministic (RMSD 0.58); AlphaFLOW also achieves lower RMWD (2.61 vs. 3.58) and higher ensemble observable scores (e.g., weak contacts J=0.62 vs. 0.27). ESMFold (Lin et al., 2023) is completely deterministic (diversity 0.000 on PDB), while ESMFLOW injects substantial diversity (0.210) at only a small precision cost (0.777 vs. 0.806). Normal mode analysis (GNM/ANM; Bahar et al., 1997; Atilgan et al., 2001) underperforms AlphaFLOW-MD+Templates on nearly all metrics despite having access to the same PDB template structure (e.g., RMWD 2.47/2.54 vs. 1.95; PC-sim > 0.5: 13%/23% vs. 44%). The +Templates variant of AlphaFLOW-MD further improves over the no-template version on all metrics and, at equivalent GPU-hour budgets, converges to equilibrium properties faster than replicate MD simulations truncated to 160 ps–40 ns.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes highly accurate single-structure predictors (AlphaFold, ESMFold) as generative models by embedding them within a flow matching framework, drawing on the harmonic polymer prior from EigenFold (Jing et al., 2023) and the interpolant-based flow matching formulation of Lipman et al. (2022). It departs from the dominant MSA subsampling paradigm (Del Alamo et al., 2022), which is an inference-time intervention limited to MSA-based predictors, by instead modifying the training objective and architecture to support sequence-conditioned generative sampling. The key technical departure is formulating flow matching on the SE(3) quotient space with a Fréchet-mean training target (squared FAPE loss), resolving the incompatibility between SE(3)-invariant architectures and standard MSE denoising objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a principled, training-time alternative to MSA ablation that generalizes to PLM-based predictors and can be fine-tuned on arbitrary ensembles (MD, cryo-EM), opening a path toward learning Boltzmann distributions at scale. The finding that AlphaFLOW with templates converges to equilibrium properties faster than replicate MD simulations under equivalent compute budgets positions it as a practical proxy for expensive physics-based simulations. Key limitations include restriction to ATLAS simulation timescales (no slow conformational changes), the model technically generating only Cβ-level distributions with all-atom coordinates as a post-hoc fill-in, and the need for multiple forward passes at inference (mitigated but not eliminated by distillation).
