## Problem

DPO aligns LLMs using sentence-level reverse KL divergence, but text generation is inherently sequential and token-by-token. This mismatch causes the KL divergence of dispreferred responses to grow much faster than that of preferred responses during training, degrading generation diversity and linguistic capability. The paper identifies that DPO's mode-seeking reverse KL constraint, applied only at the full-sequence level, fails to regulate per-token divergence balance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a prompt x and a preference pair (y_w, y_l) of chosen and rejected token sequences, along with a frozen reference model π_ref (typically an SFT checkpoint). At each training step, the policy model π_θ computes per-token log-probabilities for both responses, and the loss is evaluated at every token position using the sequential forward KL divergence between π_ref and π_θ. The output is the updated policy parameters; no explicit reward model or on-policy sampling is required during training, preserving DPO's simplicity.

## Method

TDPO reformulates the RLHF objective as a token-level MDP (state = prompt + generated prefix, action = next token) and uses the Bellman equation to express the sentence-level reward as a sum of token-level advantages. It then converts the Bradley-Terry preference model into a token-level form, proving equivalence with the Regret Preference Model, and derives a closed-form optimal policy that naturally introduces a sequential forward KL divergence term δ alongside the standard DPO reward-difference term u. The final loss (TDPO₂) is −log σ(u − α·δ₂), where δ₂ = β·D_SeqKL(x,y_l;π_ref‖π_θ) − sg(β·D_SeqKL(x,y_w;π_ref‖π_θ)); the stop-gradient on the preferred response's KL prevents it from inflating during optimization, while the gradient on the dispreferred response's KL actively suppresses its unbalanced growth. The hyperparameter α (default 0.5) tunes the strength of this KL-balance regularization, and β (default 0.1) controls overall deviation from the reference model.

## Results

On the Anthropic HH dataset, TDPO2 achieves 67.33% alignment accuracy and 4.915 diversity entropy, outperforming DPO (59.43% / 3.196), f-DPO(FKL) (54.71% / 4.708), and TDPO1 (60.08% / 4.727) on both metrics simultaneously. On the IMDb sentiment generation task, TDPO2 achieves a superior reward-vs-KL-divergence frontier compared to DPO, attaining higher average reward while maintaining lower sequential KL divergence. On MT-Bench, TDPO2 achieves the highest win rate among all compared methods (SFT, PPO, DPO, TDPO1) in GPT-4-evaluated pairwise comparisons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TDPO2 dominates DPO on both alignment accuracy (+7.9 pp, 67.33% vs 59.43%) and diversity entropy (+1.72, 4.915 vs 3.196) on the Anthropic HH dataset, confirming that token-level forward KL constraints resolve DPO's mode-seeking diversity collapse. Against f-DPO(FKL), which already improves diversity over DPO by switching to forward KL at the sentence level, TDPO2 gains a further +12.6 pp in accuracy (67.33% vs 54.71%) while also matching or exceeding its entropy (4.915 vs 4.708), demonstrating that token-level control is strictly more effective than a simple divergence swap. On MT-Bench, TDPO2 achieves the highest GPT-4 win rate over SFT, PPO (via trlx), DPO, and TDPO1, indicating superior generation quality in multi-turn open-ended dialogue. On the IMDb frontier, TDPO2 achieves higher reward at every KL-divergence level compared to DPO, and its sequential KL divergence on dispreferred responses grows more slowly, directly addressing the imbalance identified in Figure 1.

## Relation to Prior Work

TDPO extends DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) by reformulating its sentence-level KL-constrained reward maximization into a token-level Markov decision process, drawing on TRPO-style advantage maximization and the Bellman equation to connect sentence-level rewards to per-token generation. It departs from f-DPO (Wang et al., 2023), which merely swaps reverse KL for forward KL at the sentence level, by embedding forward KL divergence constraints directly into the token-level objective through the advantage function, and by establishing a formal equivalence between the token-level Bradley-Terry model and the Regret Preference Model (Knox et al., 2022, 2023). The stop-gradient modification in TDPO2 is a practical refinement that prevents the forward KL term from inadvertently accelerating training on preferred responses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TDPO addresses a concrete and well-documented failure mode of DPO—uncontrolled KL divergence growth on dispreferred responses that degrades generation diversity—by providing a principled token-level mechanism to balance alignment and diversity simultaneously. However, the empirical validation is limited to small models (GPT-2 Large, Pythia-2.8B) and three datasets, leaving open whether the token-level KL control scales to 70B+ models or multi-turn conversational settings. The introduction of the α hyperparameter for tuning the KL balance adds a practical knob but also a tuning burden, and the paper does not explore how TDPO interacts with other recent DPO variants (IPO, KTO, etc.).
