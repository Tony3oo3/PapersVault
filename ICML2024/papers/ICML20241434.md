## Problem

Responsible AI licenses with behavioral use clauses (e.g., RAIL, OpenRAIL) have been rapidly adopted across ~40,000 model repositories since 2018, yet no single standard license exists. The proliferation of variant licenses with overlapping but non-identical clause sets risks confusing downstream users and diluting the deterrent effect of behavioral restrictions, while ad-hoc customization without structure makes it difficult for developers without legal expertise to select appropriate terms.

## Input / Output

The study consumes three input streams: (1) structured metadata from 174,163 HuggingFace model repositories (license type, application domain, creation date, download counts) queried via the Model Hub API; (2) full legal text of 77 behavioral use clauses extracted from 7 distinct responsible AI licenses (AI Pubs RAIL, BigScience OpenRAIL, CodeML OpenRAIL, LLaMA2, FALCON, ImpACT L/M/H, GRID, DBRX); and (3) semi-structured interview transcripts from 4 practitioners. Outputs include time-series adoption statistics, a 22-clause taxonomy clustered by domain (discrimination, disinformation, legal, privacy, health, military, other) and mapped to NIST AI RMF and CSET Harm Taxonomy, a working license generator prototype with a domain-organized clause selection interface, and a position statement advocating 'standardized customization.'

## Method

The paper employs a three-pronged mixed-methods design. First, a quantitative analysis tracks RAIL license adoption on HuggingFace from 1% (N=209) in September 2022 to 24% (N=41,700) in January 2024, cross-tabulated by application domain and download volume. Second, a qualitative clause analysis uses independent axial coding (Braun & Clarke, 2006) by two authors to extract 22 distinct behavioral restrictions from 7 licenses, then maps them onto NIST AI RMF and CSET Harm Taxonomy categories to identify consensus clauses (discrimination, disinformation, legal compliance appear in nearly all) versus domain-specific additions (medical oversight, military deployment, robotics physical harm). Third, semi-structured interviews with 4 AI developers (mean 18 years post-graduate experience) probe motivations for license selection, perceived gaps in existing OS licenses, and tooling needs. The key design contribution is a license generator prototype that enforces a mandatory core clause set while allowing domain-organized optional restrictions (privacy, health, military) to be toggled, producing a live-preview RAIL license tailored to the specified artifact type (model, code, data, application).

## Results

Analysis of 174,163 HuggingFace model repositories (Jan 2024) shows RAIL licenses grew from 1% (N=209) in Sep 2022 to 24.0% (N=41,700) in Jan 2024, making them the second most adopted license type after Apache (38.1%). The qualitative analysis identified 22 distinct behavioral use clauses across 7 responsible AI licenses, with near-universal coverage of discrimination, disinformation, and legal-compliance restrictions, while healthcare and military clauses appear in only a subset. Semi-structured interviews with 4 practitioners (mean 18 years post-graduate experience) confirmed that existing open-source licenses (e.g., Apache 2.0) were perceived as insufficient for ML-specific use restrictions, motivating adoption of RAIL-type licenses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a position paper without traditional method-vs-baseline comparisons; instead, it compares the restrictiveness and scope of 8 responsible AI licenses. FALCON is the most permissive, including only a subset of clauses (notably omitting discrimination-on-protected-characteristics, automated decision-making, and most military restrictions). OpenRAIL (BigScience) and ImpACT are among the most restrictive, covering the broadest range of categories including healthcare and military. LLaMA 2 adds unique privacy-specific clauses (e.g., inferring personal information without consent) and health-risk restrictions not found in other licenses. CodeML OpenRAIL is the only license to include an explicit malware-generation prohibition. All licenses share a core of anti-discrimination, anti-disinformation, and legal-compliance clauses, but diverge significantly on healthcare, military, and privacy restrictions, supporting the paper's argument that standardization with domain-specific customization is needed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the RAIL license framework proposed by Contractor et al. (2022b) and the OpenRAIL naming taxonomy from Contractor et al. (2022a), which introduced the concept of behavioral use clauses as a licensing mechanism for responsible AI release. It extends that conceptual proposal into the first large-scale empirical study of how these licenses have actually been adopted (170,000+ HuggingFace repositories), how their clauses cluster and diverge across 7+ license families, and what practitioners report as motivations and pain points. The paper also positions licensing alongside complementary governance tools (model cards, AI factsheets, ethical guidelines, the EU AI Act) but argues that contractual enforcement via licenses fills a distinct short-term gap that regulation and documentation cannot.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With RAIL-licensed repositories growing from 1% to 24% of HuggingFace in 18 months, the proliferation of non-standard license variants (FALCON's minimal clauses vs. ImpACT's tiered risk model vs. LLaMA2's derivative restrictions) creates real confusion for downstream users and risks diluting the deterrent effect of behavioral restrictions. The paper's call for 'standardized customization' with tooling support (license generator, dependency scanners) is timely but leaves open critical questions: whether the community can converge on a core clause set, how licenses interact with the EU AI Act's risk-based approach, and whether contractual restrictions are enforceable in practice or merely aspirational. The concern about concentration of power and the need for participatory design in standardization processes remain unresolved.
