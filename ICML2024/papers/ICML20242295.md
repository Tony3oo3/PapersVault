## Problem

ML models deployed in high-stakes societal applications must remain confidential due to legal and privacy constraints, yet consumers need assurance that these models are fair. Existing fairness auditing approaches rely on reference datasets, trusted third parties, and offer no guarantee of model uniformity (i.e., that the same model is used for every prediction). This paper addresses the gap by enabling public, cryptographic verification of a model's fairness without revealing its weights.

## Input / Output

The system takes a user query x* (a data point in R^n) and operates over a committed fully-connected ReLU network whose architecture is public but whose weights W are hidden behind a cryptographic commitment. It produces three outputs: the prediction label y, a personalized fairness certificate ε_LB (a lower bound on the local individual fairness parameter specific to x*), and a succinct ZK proof π (approximately 43.5 KB for the German Credit dataset). The certificate is a conservative estimate—the true fairness could only be higher—because the method uses projection distances rather than exact distances to keep the ZKP circuit tractable.

## Method

FairProof combines a ZKP-friendly fairness certification algorithm with a cryptographic verification protocol. The certification reduces local individual fairness to pointwise ℓ₂ robustness by fixing sensitive features to each value in their domain, then applies the GeoCert iterative polytope-traversal algorithm in the reduced (n−k)-dimensional space, reporting the minimum projection distance to a boundary facet as a lower bound ε_LB. The verification protocol avoids replicating the full certification by checking only five strategically chosen sub-functionalities (initial polytope membership, facet distances, neighboring polytope validity, boundary facet detection, and traversal order), each expressible as a low-complexity arithmetic circuit over the committed weights. Representative interior points of polytopes and facets are precomputed offline to enable efficient ZKP proofs, and certain computations (polytope construction, linear function derivation) are moved to an offline phase, reducing online proof generation time by up to 1.74×. Model uniformity is guaranteed via a standard cryptographic commitment to the weights, binding the organization to a single model for all predictions.

## Results

FairProof generates a verifiable fairness certificate per data point in 1.17–4.90 minutes (online proof generation, averaged over 100 test points) depending on the dataset and model architecture, with the German (2,4) model being the fastest at 1.17 ± 0.02 min. Verification time is orders of magnitude lower (in seconds), and the proof size is only ~43.5 KB, enabling quick real-time verification at the customer end. Moving certain computations to an offline pre-processing phase yields a 1.36×–1.74× reduction in online proof generation time. The certification mechanism successfully distinguishes fair from unfair models: fair models (trained with weight decay) consistently exhibit higher local individual fairness parameter ε than their unregularized counterparts across all three datasets and model sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FairProof is a novel system and does not compare against prior fairness-verification baselines, as no existing method provides publicly verifiable, confidentiality-preserving fairness certificates for neural networks. The paper's primary empirical comparison is between fair models (trained with weight decay regularization) and unfair models (no regularization), showing that the certification mechanism reliably assigns higher ε values to fair models across all three datasets (Adult, Default Credit, German Credit) and all tested architectures. Among the sub-functionalities of the ZKP protocol, VerifyBoundary is the most expensive (executed every iteration with non-linear comparisons), while VerifyMin is the cheapest (run once per query). The offline computation optimization (pre-computing representative-point proofs and linear-function proofs) provides the largest speedup for the German (4,2) model at 1.74×, and the smallest for Adult (8,2) at 1.36×, indicating diminishing returns as the number of traversed facets increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GeoCert (Jordan et al., 2019), an iterative polytope-traversal algorithm for certifying pointwise ℓ₂ robustness of ReLU networks, by reducing local individual fairness certification to a robustness problem in reduced dimensions. It departs from prior ZKP-based ML verification work (Zhang et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2021), which targets accuracy or inference integrity, by instead proving correctness of a fairness certificate. It also differs from prior fairness-with-confidentiality approaches (Pentyala et al., 2022; Kilbertus et al., 2018; Park et al., 2022) that rely on a trusted third-party auditor, and from the closest prior work (Shamsabadi et al., 2023), which handles decision trees and training-time fairness rather than post-training neural network inspection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FairProof is the first system to provide publicly verifiable, per-query fairness certificates for neural networks while preserving model confidentiality, addressing a real gap in high-stakes ML deployment where organizations cannot release weights. The practical feasibility (≈1.17 min/query, 43.5 KB proof for small networks) is encouraging but the scope is tightly limited to fully-connected ReLU networks with very few hidden units on tabular data; scalability to deeper or convolutional architectures remains open. The authors also flag potential gaming (fairwashing) and the fact that local individual fairness is not a complete fairness notion, leaving important questions about robustness of the protocol to adversarial model owners.
