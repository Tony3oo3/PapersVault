## Problem

DNA sequence modeling faces three challenges absent in NLP or protein modeling: the need for bi-directional context (upstream and downstream regulatory regions), reverse complementarity of the two DNA strands, and long-range interactions spanning up to 1 million base pairs. Prior long-range genomic models such as HyenaDNA are unidirectional and lack inherent RC equivariance, while Transformer-based models are limited by quadratic scaling to contexts of roughly 12,000 bps. No existing architecture simultaneously supports bi-directionality, RC equivariance, and efficient long-range sequence processing.

## Input / Output

The model consumes DNA sequences at character (base-pair) level tokenization, with a maximum context length of 131k bps for SSM-based models during downstream evaluation. For pre-training, the input is the human reference genome (GRCh37) processed with a BERT-style masking recipe (15% of tokens masked). For variant effect prediction, embeddings are extracted from a 1536 bp window centered at the SNP location for both reference and alternative sequences, concatenated along the channel dimension, and fed to a linear SVM probe. The RC equivariant variant (Caduceus-PS) produces outputs that transform predictably under reverse complementation, while the post-hoc variant (Caduceus-Ph) achieves RC invariance by averaging predictions on the original and RC sequences at inference.

## Method

The paper extends the Mamba selective state space block in two stages: BiMamba applies the Mamba module to both the original and reversed sequence with shared projection weights (avoiding a 2× parameter increase), then adds the flipped reverse output to the forward output; MambaDNA further splits the channel dimension in half, applies the RC operation to one half, processes both halves through a single shared Mamba/BiMamba module, and re-concatenates after a second RC, yielding a provably RC-equivariant operator. Caduceus-PS wraps BiMamba inside MambaDNA blocks and adds RC-equivariant token embeddings and an RC-equivariant LM head (using channel-flip symmetry), enabling RC-equivariant masked language modeling pre-training without data augmentation. Caduceus-Ph instead uses a stack of plain BiMamba blocks with RC data augmentation during pre-training and post-hoc conjoining (averaging predictions on original and RC sequences) at downstream inference. Both variants are pre-trained with MLM on the human reference genome and fine-tuned for downstream classification and variant effect prediction tasks.

## Results

On the Genomics Benchmark (8 regulatory classification tasks), Caduceus-Ph achieves the best overall performance, with top-1 accuracies of 0.915 on Coding vs. Intergenic and 0.973 on Human vs. Worm, while Caduceus-PS leads on Mouse Enhancers (0.793) and Human Enhancer Ensembl (0.900). On the 18-task Nucleotide Transformer Benchmark, Caduceus-Ph (1.9M params) outperforms Enformer (252M), DNABERT-2 (117M), and NT-v2 (500M) on 8 of 18 tasks, including H3 (0.815 vs. 0.719/0.785/0.784) and H3K79me3 (0.697 vs. 0.496/0.613/0.626). On the long-range variant effect prediction task, Caduceus-PS exceeds NT-v2 (500M) and even surpasses Enformer at distances >100k bps from the nearest TSS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HyenaDNA (436k, the closest SSM-based competitor), Caduceus models win on 7 of 8 Genomics Benchmark tasks and on nearly all histone marker and regulatory annotation tasks in the Nucleotide Transformer suite, though HyenaDNA retains an edge on splice site annotation (e.g., 0.949 vs. 0.948 on Donor). Against the much larger Transformer-based baselines, Caduceus-Ph (1.9M) surpasses Enformer (252M) on 8 of 18 Nucleotide Transformer tasks (e.g., H3: 0.815 vs. 0.719; H3K79me3: 0.697 vs. 0.496) and Caduceus-PS (1.9M) exceeds NT-v2 (500M) on the long-range VEP task, particularly at distances >100k bps from TSS where it also outperforms Enformer. Against the supervised CNN baseline (264k), Caduceus models achieve substantially higher accuracy across all Genomics Benchmark tasks (e.g., 0.900 vs. 0.744 on Human Enhancer Ensembl). The paper also shows that Caduceus w/o equivariance (470k) already outperforms the unidirectional Mamba baseline (468k) on most tasks, isolating the contribution of bi-directionality from equivariance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mamba selective state space model (Gu & Dao, 2023) along two axes: bidirectionality (BiMamba, via parameter-shared forward/reverse passes) and reverse-complement equivariance (MambaDNA, via channel-split RC processing). The RC equivariance mechanism adapts the parameter-sharing idea from Shrikumar et al. (2017) to the SSM setting, while the post-hoc conjoining strategy for Caduceus-Ph follows Zhou et al. (2021). The key departure from the closest prior work, HyenaDNA (Nguyen et al., 2023), is that HyenaDNA is unidirectional and lacks inherent RC robustness, whereas Caduceus integrates both inductive biases into a single architecture, enabling RC-equivariant masked language modeling for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By embedding bidirectionality and RC equivariance as architectural inductive biases rather than data-augmentation tricks, Caduceus demonstrates that a ~500k-parameter SSM model can match or exceed 500M-parameter Transformer models on long-range variant effect prediction, suggesting that domain-specific structure can substitute for raw scale in genomics. The MambaDNA block is presented as a drop-in component for any genomic architecture, which could accelerate adoption across the field. Open questions include whether these inductive biases generalize to non-human genomes or to tasks where RC symmetry is less relevant, whether the MLM pre-training objective is optimal for SSM-based DNA models, and how the approach scales to multi-megabase contexts where even linear-time SSMs face memory constraints.
