## Problem

Existing dynamic architecture-based continual learning methods adapt network width by growing new branches or activating neurons, but they overlook network depth—a critical factor for model performance. This fixed-depth limitation creates a performance bottleneck as tasks accumulate, since the model cannot increase its representational capacity in the depth dimension. The paper asks whether both depth and width can be jointly and principledly adapted in a continual learning setting.

## Input / Output

For supervised tasks, the method takes RGB images (28×28 for MNIST/Fashion MNIST, 32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and outputs class labels, with task identity available at test time for task-incremental settings. For unsupervised continual generation, it takes a 50-dimensional latent vector and produces 28×28 images via an adaptive VAE decoder. The network operates with a fixed maximum width M (e.g., 200 for fully-connected, 512 channels for CNN) and a truncation level K bounding the maximum number of layers, with skip connections routing the last activated layer's output to the classification head or generator.

## Method

The core contribution is a non-parametric Bayesian framework that models network depth growth via a beta process (using stick-breaking construction to produce per-layer activation probabilities π_l) and applies drop-connect regularization on width through a conjugate Bernoulli process that generates binary weight activation masks. Sequential Bayes is applied by treating the posterior from task t−1 as the prior for task t, with an ELBO that includes KL divergences on both structure variables (beta parameters v and Bernoulli masks Z) and weight distributions W to balance adaptation against forgetting. Discrete masks are relaxed with concrete Bernoulli during training, and the beta distribution uses implicit reparameterization for gradient computation. For task-incremental learning, a weight importance parameter γ is added to the variational mask distribution, and a fixed task-specific mask is sampled after each task's structure update. The framework is backbone-agnostic (fully-connected, AlexNet, fully convolutional) and extends to VAE decoders for unsupervised continual generation.

## Results

On the AlexNet-based CNN benchmarks, the proposed method achieves 79.93% on CIFAR100-20 and 50.59% on Tiny-ImageNet-10, outperforming all baselines on both. On CIFAR10-5 it reaches 91.44% (third behind HAT at 92.29% and IBPCL at 92.53%), and on CIFAR100-10 it scores 71.83% (second behind HAT at 72.42%). On fully-connected MNIST variants, the method achieves 99.33% on split Fashion MNIST (best overall) and 97.98% on permuted MNIST (second to IBPCL's 98.18%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all baselines on the two most challenging datasets: CIFAR100-20 (79.93% vs. IBPCL's 77.13% and HAT's 75.40%) and Tiny-ImageNet-10 (50.59% vs. SPG's 49.72% and IBPCL's 48.46%), with margins of ~2.8% and ~0.9% respectively. On CIFAR10-5, it trails IBPCL (92.53%) and HAT (92.29%) by roughly 1%, while on CIFAR100-10 it is second only to HAT (72.42%) by 0.6%. On fully-connected MNIST benchmarks, IBPCL edges out the proposed method on permuted MNIST (98.18% vs. 97.98%) and split MNIST (99.79% vs. 99.76%), but the proposed method wins on split Fashion MNIST (99.33% vs. 99.27%). Regularization-only baselines (EWC, VCL, UCL) are consistently outperformed by 5–10+ points across all CNN datasets, confirming the advantage of joint depth-and-width structural adaptation over fixed-architecture regularization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sequential Bayesian continual learning framework (VCL, Nguyen et al. 2018) and the Bayesian structural adaptation line (IBPCL, Kumar et al. 2021; HIBNN, Kessler et al. 2021) by adding depth adaptation alongside the existing width-only adaptation. The core mechanism—jointly inferring depth via a beta process and width via a conjugate Bernoulli drop-connect—derives from KC et al. (2021), which addressed batch learning, and is here embedded into a full sequential Bayes loop with task-specific masks. The paper also adapts the AdaVAE structural inference (Regmi & Li, 2023) to the unsupervised continual generation setting and builds on ER-ACE (Caccia et al., 2022) for class-incremental learning. A key conceptual departure from IBPCL/HIBNN is applying binary masks at the weight level rather than the neuron level, which the authors argue reduces information loss from deactivating entire neurons.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in dynamic-architecture continual learning: prior methods fix depth and only grow width, potentially capping model capacity as task complexity increases. The demonstrated robustness to truncation level (stable performance across K=3 to K=24) and the qualitative finding that inferred depth tracks dataset complexity (10→19 layers from CIFAR10-5 to Super-ImageNet-10) suggest the framework can self-calibrate model capacity without manual architecture search. Limitations include the need for truncation in practice, the added computational cost over IBPCL (~1.2× training time), and the difficulty of integrating pooling layers with stochastic depth. Open questions include whether the depth adaptation provides meaningful gains over simply using a sufficiently deep fixed network, and how the approach scales to very large backbones (ResNet-50+) where the O(M²) per-layer cost becomes prohibitive.
