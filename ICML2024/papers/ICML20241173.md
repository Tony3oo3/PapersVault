## Problem

Learning-to-optimize (L2O) methods for linear programming have primarily emulated interior-point or simplex methods, leaving the question of whether L2O can effectively emulate first-order methods (FOMs) like PDHG largely unexplored. This gap is significant because FOMs such as PDLP are the most scalable solvers for large-scale LP, yet they suffer from slow convergence when initialized from scratch (zero vectors), missing instance-specific structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes an LP in standard form (min cᵀx s.t. Gx ≥ h, l ≤ x ≤ u) and encodes it as a bipartite graph with 3-dimensional variable features (lower bound, upper bound, cost) and 4-dimensional constraint features (one-hot constraint type, RHS value), connected by a single scalar edge weight. The output is two-stage: PDHG-Net first produces an approximate primal-dual pair (X^K, Y^K) via a 4-layer unrolled network with channel expansion, which then warm-starts the PDLP solver to refine the solution to full optimality. The channel-expansion design (d_k columns per node) allows the same trained network to handle LP instances of varying sizes without retraining.

## Method

PDHG-Net unrolls K iterations of the PDHG (Chambolle-Pock) algorithm into a neural network, replacing each primal and dual update with a parameterized NN block. Two key design choices distinguish it from classical deep unrolling: (1) ReLU activations replace the projection operators, exploiting their piecewise-linear structure to enable exact recovery of PDHG iterations; (2) channel expansion (inspired by GNNs) replaces fixed-size trainable matrices with right-multiplied projection matrices, making the network generalizable across LP instances of different dimensions. The network follows a period-2 update cycle (primal block then dual block per layer), and the dual block performs twice as many aggregations as the primal block, mirroring PDHG's asymmetric structure. Training is supervised with L2 loss against near-optimal solutions generated by PDLP, and a theoretical result proves that a PDHG-Net with O(1/ε) neurons can achieve ε-accuracy on the primal-dual gap, matching the O(1/K) ergodic convergence rate of the underlying algorithm.

## Results

On PageRank instances, the PDHG-Net two-stage framework achieves up to a 3× speedup over vanilla PDLP, with solving times of 0.01s, 0.4s, 22.4s, and 4,508s versus PDLP's 0.04s, 1.1s, 71.3s, and 16,502s at 10³, 10⁴, 10⁵, and 10⁶ nodes respectively (improvements of 45.7%–72.7%). On harder LP relaxations (IP and WA datasets), the framework reduces solving time by 16.7%–25.0% and iteration count by 16.9%–27.8% compared to PDLP. Against a GNN-based L2O baseline, PDHG-Net achieves substantially lower ℓ₂ distance to the optimal solution (e.g., 0.95 vs. 30.35 at 10⁵ nodes) and maintains positive speedup where the GNN baseline actually degrades (−4.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla PDLP (the primary FOM baseline from OrTools), the PDHG-Net framework consistently wins on all three datasets and all problem sizes, with the gap widening as instances grow larger (up to 72.7% time reduction at 10⁶ PageRank nodes). Against Gurobi's LP solvers (Barrier, Primal Simplex, Dual Simplex), the proposed method is faster at every scale; Gurobi's solvers exceed the 10-hour time limit on 10⁵ and 10⁶ node PageRank instances, while PDHG-Net completes in 22.4s and 4,508s respectively. Against a GNN-based L2O architecture (trained under identical settings), PDHG-Net dominates on both metrics: it achieves 45.7% vs. 1.4% improvement at 10³ nodes, 67.6% vs. 19.3% at 10⁴, and 71.3% vs. −4.0% at 10⁵, while its ℓ₂ distance to the optimum remains 30× smaller at 10⁵ nodes (0.95 vs. 30.35), indicating the GNN lacks the robustness of the PDHG-unrolled design at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of deep unrolling (Gregor & LeCun, 2010) and first-order optimization, specifically unrolling the Chambolle-Pock PDHG algorithm into a neural network for LP. Prior L2O-for-LP work has focused on emulating interior-point methods or simplex methods, while GNN-based approaches (Chen et al., 2022b; Gasse et al., 2019) have been applied to LP without strong theoretical grounding. PDHG-Net departs from both lines by (i) targeting FOMs rather than IPM/simplex, (ii) using a period-2 primal-dual update cycle with channel expansion borrowed from GNNs rather than a period-1 message-passing scheme, and (iii) providing an exact alignment theorem between the network and the underlying algorithm, yielding an O(1/ε) neuron bound that prior GNN-based L2O-for-LP work does not offer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-stage framework (PDHG-Net prediction + PDLP warm-start) offers a practical recipe for accelerating large-scale LP solving by up to 3×, with the GPU inference cost becoming negligible (0.02%) at 10⁶ nodes. The clean theoretical guarantee that a finite-width PDHG-Net exactly replicates PDHG iterations provides a principled design template for unrolling other FOMs. Open questions include whether the approach extends to mixed-integer programming (the authors flag this as future work), whether it generalizes to other FOMs beyond PDHG, and how sensitive performance is to distribution shift between training and test LP instances. A limitation is that the method requires a corpus of similar LP instances for training, restricting its applicability to settings with abundant historical data.
