## Problem

Existing predictive coding (PC) inspired neural networks like PredNet fail to demonstrate improved prediction skills on real-world forecasting tasks and ignore the Precision Weighting mechanism central to PC theory. The hierarchical modeling in prior work is poorly implemented—targeting low signal-to-noise residuals directly and applying uniform weighting across regions—so redundant noise becomes a supervisory signal that hinders learning from important information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240261/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of past spatiotemporal vector fields (e.g., 4 context frames at 64×64 for synthetic data, 24 hours of 6-hourly ERA5 surface wind at 144×280, or 20-minute radar composites at 1 km resolution) and produces future field predictions over a target horizon (16 frames, 48 hours, or 90 minutes respectively). Uniquely, it also outputs spatially varying inverse precision maps that quantify local predictability at each spatiotemporal coordinate, and generates ensemble forecasts by sampling multiple noise priors. The context is consumed via a ViT encoder whose representations are injected into the U-Net through cross-attention at every denoising step.

## Method

CogDPM reinterprets the multi-step reverse diffusion process as the hierarchical inference of predictive coding: each denoising step from x_T to x_0 corresponds to a layer in the PC hierarchy, with early steps capturing coarse spatiotemporal structure and later steps refining details. At each step, a generative DPM G_θ (unconditional) and a perceptual DPM P_θ (conditioned on observations) produce predictions, and their difference serves as the prediction error signal. The key novelty is a precision weighting mechanism: the inverse precision is estimated as the variance of direct x_0 predictions across a sliding window of k consecutive denoising steps, and this spatially varying weight modulates the guidance strength in the state update x_{t-1} = G_θ(x_t) + f(U[x_t])·(P_θ(x_t,c) − G_θ(x_t)). This causes the model to allocate stronger conditional guidance to regions with lower predictability (higher variance), effectively filtering redundant noise from the supervisory signal. The backbone is a denoising U-Net with a ViT context encoder, trained with standard L1 noise-prediction loss and 10% classifier-free dropout; a cascade of low-resolution and super-resolution CogDPM models is used for the high-resolution UK precipitation task.

## Results

On MovingMNIST, CogDPM achieves the best CRPS (w8, max) of 0.0697, CSI of 0.7365, and FSS of 0.8588, outperforming the strongest baseline PredRNN-v2 (0.0774, 0.688, 0.8471) and the naive CFG DPM (0.082, 0.6959, 0.822). On Turbulence flow, CogDPM reaches CRPS (w8, max) of 0.0034, CSI of 0.7962, and FSS of 0.9722, again leading all baselines. On real-world ERA5 wind forecasting, CogDPM surpasses FourCastNet on CSI at 12 m/s and 16 m/s thresholds, RMSE, and CRPS across all 48-hour prediction times. On UK precipitation nowcasting, CogDPM outperforms the operational DGMR model on spatially pooled CRPS, economic value, and power spectral density.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CogDPM consistently outperforms all five baselines on the synthetic benchmarks. Against the closest competitor PredRNN-v2, CogDPM improves CSI by 4.9 points (0.7365 vs 0.688) and FSS by 1.2 points on MovingMNIST, and by 4.3 points on CSI (0.7962 vs 0.7529) and 2.2 points on FSS on Turbulence. The most direct comparison is with the DPM using naive classifier-free guidance (same U-Net architecture, constant guidance weight): CogDPM wins on CSI (0.7365 vs 0.6959 on MovingMNIST; 0.7962 vs 0.6725 on Turbulence) and FSS, while matching CRPS (w8,avg), confirming that precision-weighted guidance is the key differentiator. FourCastNet, a domain-specialized Fourier neural operator, is substantially outperformed on all metrics in the synthetic experiments and on ERA5 wind CSI at higher thresholds (12 and 16 m/s). On UK precipitation nowcasting, CogDPM beats the operational DGMR model on CRPS across all lead times, economic value (cost-loss ratio decision model), and radially averaged PSD, with the case study showing CogDPM correctly tracks a squall line for 90 minutes while DGMR dissipates it within 30 minutes.

## Relation to Prior Work

CogDPM builds directly on denoising diffusion probabilistic models (Ho et al., 2020) and classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2021), repurposing the multi-step denoising process as a hierarchical inference mechanism analogous to Predictive Coding in cognitive science. It departs from prior PC-inspired networks (PredNet, CPC, MemDPC, PCN) by addressing their shared omission of precision weighting, and from vanilla classifier-free guidance by replacing the constant guidance scalar with a spatiotemporally varying precision weight estimated from the variance of direct x₀ predictions across a window of denoising steps. The conceptual bridge—mapping diffusion timesteps to PC hierarchy levels and using inter-step prediction variance as an inverse-precision signal—is the paper's core architectural novelty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CogDPM provides a principled, training-free mechanism for estimating spatially resolved prediction confidence in spatiotemporal forecasting, which is directly actionable for weather-driven decision-making (e.g., identifying where a 48-hour wind forecast is unreliable). The outperformance of operational models (FourCastNet, DGMR) on real precipitation and wind tasks suggests that precision-weighted guidance can meaningfully improve probabilistic forecast skill beyond what constant-strength classifier-free guidance achieves. Open questions include whether the PC interpretation is mechanistically faithful or merely a useful analogy, whether incorporating active inference (explicitly deferred) would further improve predictions, and whether the precision estimation generalizes beyond diffusion-based architectures.
