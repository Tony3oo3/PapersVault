## Problem

Extending 3D Gaussian Splatting to dynamic scenes typically requires a deformation network per individual Gaussian, which drastically reduces rendering speed (e.g., D-3D-GS drops to ~45 FPS). The paper addresses how to model dynamic scene deformations while preserving the real-time rendering advantage of 3D-GS, by exploiting the observation that neighboring Gaussians often undergo similar rigid transformations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242043/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view video (monocular or multi-camera) with known camera poses and a sparse point cloud (COLMAP-derived for real scenes, random 10k points for synthetic) as input. It produces a canonical 3D Gaussian scene with M superpoints and a deformation MLP, then renders novel-view images at high resolution (800×800 synthetic, 536×960 real) at up to 227 FPS. At inference, superpoint deformations can be pre-computed across all timesteps and linearly interpolated, eliminating the need to run the deformation network per frame.

## Method

After a 3k-iteration warm-up that trains canonical 3D Gaussians without deformation, the method introduces a learnable association matrix (P×M) that softly assigns each Gaussian to its K nearest superpoints, initialized via farthest-point sampling. A compact 8-layer MLP (256 hidden) predicts per-superpoint rigid deformation (translation and rotation in se(3)) from positionally-encoded canonical coordinates and timestep, which is then broadcast to all member Gaussians. A property reconstruction loss enforces As-Rigid-As-Possible consistency by requiring that superpoint properties reconstructed from member Gaussians (and vice versa) match the originals. An optional 3-layer non-rigid MLP can be plugged in to add per-Gaussian residual deformation on top of the superpoint rigid motion, and the framework supports knowledge distillation from heavier teacher models like D-3D-GS.

## Results

On the D-NeRF synthetic benchmark (800×800), SP-GS achieves 37.98 PSNR, 0.9876 SSIM, and 0.0185 LPIPS at 227.25 FPS, the highest rendering speed among all compared methods. The SP-GS+NG variant (with non-rigid deformation) improves quality to 38.28 PSNR and 0.0152 LPIPS at 119.35 FPS. On the real-world HyperNeRF dataset (536×960), SP-GS reaches 25.61 PSNR at 117.86 FPS, and SP-GS+NG reaches 26.78 PSNR at 51.51 FPS. On NeRF-DS (480×270), SP-GS achieves 23.15 PSNR at 251.70 FPS, the fastest among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SP-GS achieves the highest rendering speed (227.25 FPS at 800×800) across all baselines, outperforming 4D-GS (143.69 FPS) by ~58% and D-3D-GS (45.05 FPS) by ~5×. In terms of visual quality, SP-GS (37.98 PSNR) surpasses 4D-GS (35.31 PSNR) by 2.67 dB and all NeRF-based methods (best: HexPlane-Slim at 32.97 PSNR) by over 5 dB, but trails D-3D-GS (40.23 PSNR) by 2.25 dB. The SP-GS+NG variant narrows the quality gap to D-3D-GS (38.28 vs. 40.23 PSNR) while still maintaining 2.6× the rendering speed. On the real-world HyperNeRF dataset, SP-GS (25.61 PSNR, 117.86 FPS) outperforms 4D-GS (25.02 PSNR, 66.21 FPS) in both quality and speed, and SP-GS+NG (26.78 PSNR) achieves the best quality among all methods. On NeRF-DS, SP-GS achieves the highest FPS (251.70) but slightly lower PSNR (23.15) than D-3D-GS (24.11), with SP-GS+NG closing the gap to 23.33 PSNR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) from static to dynamic scenes by introducing superpoints—clusters of 3D Gaussians that share a common rigid deformation, inspired by As-Rigid-As-Possible regularization and superpixel/superpoint over-segmentation. Unlike concurrent dynamic 3D-GS methods (D-3D-GS, 4D-GS, Dynamic 3D Gaussians) that apply a deformation network to every individual Gaussian, SP-GS predicts deformation at the superpoint level via a small MLP, drastically reducing per-frame computation. A novel property reconstruction loss enforces intra-superpoint consistency of positions, translations, and rotations, and an optional non-rigid MLP can be plugged in for scenes with complex deformations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SP-GS achieves a ~5× speedup over D-3D-GS (227 vs. 45 FPS on D-NeRF) while retaining competitive visual quality, making real-time dynamic scene rendering practical on consumer hardware (140 FPS on a GTX 1060). The superpoint representation unlocks downstream applications—model distillation, per-component pose estimation, and semantic scene editing—that are difficult with unstructured Gaussian clouds. Key limitations include the reliance on COLMAP for initialization (which degrades on dynamic scenes) and the rigid-motion assumption within each superpoint, which the optional non-rigid module only partially addresses; whether superpoint grouping generalizes to highly non-rigid or topologically changing scenes remains open.
