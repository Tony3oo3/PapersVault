## Problem

Existing feature attribution methods rely on post-hoc explanations of end-to-end classifiers (gradients, attention, SHAP), making them data-inefficient, computationally expensive for high-dimensional inputs, and often inaccurate in localizing class-specific regions. No scalable approach exists that directly learns feature attribution from coarse datum-level supervision across diverse data modalities without requiring encoder retraining or backpropagation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241389/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes token embeddings (of dimension d) from any pretrained encoder along with a user-defined graph connectivity structure (e.g., 2-hop for sentences, 8-way for image patches, distance-based for protein residues), forming a 'map graph.' It produces a prospect map — a scalar attribution score per token on the same graph topology — plus intermediate artifacts: a data sprite (categorical concept assignment per token) and a global kernel of concept monogram/skip-bigram weights. The entire pipeline operates in O(T) time with respect to token count, requiring no backward passes through the encoder.

## Method

Prospector heads are a two-layer, parameter-efficient module (at most 2K + C(K,2) parameters) that equips any encoder with feature attribution capability. Layer I quantizes each token embedding into one of K spatially resolved concepts via K-means clustering, producing a compressed 'data sprite.' Layer II (K2conv) performs a graph convolution over the sprite using a global kernel ω that scores concept monograms and skip-bigrams within a tunable receptive field r, yielding per-token attribution scores. The kernel is fit either via logistic regression with elastic net regularization or a parameter-free fold-change computation with Bonferroni-corrected Mann-Whitney U testing. The key design insight is to build inductive reasoning over quantized token associations rather than explaining a classifier, enabling linear-time inference, few-shot data efficiency, and modality-generalizable attribution across text, images, and graphs.

## Results

Prospector heads achieve mean localization AUPRC gains of 8.5 points on sequences (text), 26.3 points on images (pathology), and 22.0 points on graphs (protein structures) over the top modality-specific baselines. On the WikiSection text retrieval task, MiniLM equipped with a prospector head reaches 0.711 AUPRC, surpassing the best supervised MLP baseline (0.626) and DeBERTa's NLI entailment baseline (0.584) by 8.5 and 12.7 points respectively. On Camelyon16 and MetalPDB, prospectors also gain 8.8 points in average precision (AP) over the strongest baselines, with domain-specialized foundation model encoders (PLIP, COLLAPSE) yielding the largest improvements.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Prospectors outperform all six baseline categories across all three modalities. On sequences, MiniLM+Prospector (0.711 AUPRC) beats DeBERTa's zero-shot classification, NLI entailment (0.584), NLI attention, and pooled Shapley value baselines, as well as the semi-supervised MLP head (0.626) and one-class SVM novelty detection. On images, prospectors with PLIP or CLIP encoders surpass both concatenated mean attention (Chen et al., 2022) and concatenated prediction probability (Campanella et al., 2019) by 26.3 AUPRC points, with the gap widening for low-prevalence tumor regions. On graphs, COLLAPSE+Prospector outperforms GAT attention weights, SHAP values, and GNNExplainer (Ying et al., 2019) by 22.0 AUPRC points, with particularly strong performance on cysteine-dependent zinc-binding motifs. Notably, the fold-change (parameter-free) prospector variant outperforms the linear classifier variant in nearly all settings, and prospectors maintain robustness to region prevalence and dispersion where baselines degrade.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant explanation-based attribution paradigm (gradients, attention maps, SHAP, LIME, GNNExplainer) by reframing feature attribution as a direct learning problem over encoder representations rather than a post-hoc interrogation of a trained classifier. Conceptually, it synthesizes ideas from concept bottleneck models (spatially resolved concepts via quantization), transformer induction heads (skip-bigram chaining for implicit higher-order n-grams), and associative memory (the kernel as a scored dictionary of concept co-occurrences) into a unified two-layer module. The approach is positioned as encoder-agnostic and modality-general, building on the assumption that foundation models learn rich distributional semantics that can be adapted with only hundreds of parameters, rather than requiring the full classifier to be trained and then explained.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Prospector heads offer a practical, linear-time, parameter-efficient alternative to explanation-based attribution that generalizes across text, images, and graphs without encoder retraining or backpropagation, which is particularly valuable for data-scarce biomedical settings. The parameter-free fold-change variant outperforming the learned linear classifier variant suggests that explicit dataset-wide concept association learning (mirroring the multiple instance assumption) may be more effective than per-sample weight fitting, an open question the authors flag for further investigation. Key limitations include the absence of sensitivity analyses for token resolution and connectivity, reliance on open-source encoders only, and the fact that the strongest baselines (e.g., concatenated attention/prediction probability for pathology) are relatively simple, leaving open whether the large margins would hold against more sophisticated attribution methods. Future work on GPU acceleration, higher-order n-gram kernels, and deployment with frontier non-Transformer architectures could broaden applicability.
