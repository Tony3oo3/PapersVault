## Problem

Classical universal approximation theorems for neural networks rely on an infinite parameter space (learnable weights) to approximate target functions. This paper asks whether universal approximation can instead be achieved by composing a sequence of mappings drawn from a fixed, finite vocabulary — analogous to how a finite word set in language generates infinitely many sentences. The key gap is that prior results either require infinitely many distinct mappings or treat the network architecture as fixed with adjustable parameters, rather than treating the composition sequence itself as the variable.

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous mapping f: R^d → R^d (or an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism) defined on a compact domain Ω ⊂ R^d, along with an error tolerance ε > 0. It produces a finite sequence of mappings selected from a fixed vocabulary V of size O(d²), whose left-to-right composition approximates f on Ω within ε in the C-norm (or L^p-norm for d ≥ 2). The vocabulary elements are flow maps of autonomous ODEs with velocity fields restricted to unit coordinate vectors, elementary matrices E_ij, and single-coordinate leaky-ReLU functions, evaluated at time steps τ ∈ {1, √2}. No learnable parameters are involved; the only degree of freedom is the length and ordering of the composition sequence.

## Method

The construction proceeds in two parts. Part 1 shows that any affine flow map (H₁) or leaky-ReLU flow map (H₂) can be approximated by composing elements of the finite vocabulary V, using the Lie product formula to split a combined velocity field into axis-aligned components and Kronecker's approximation theorem to express arbitrary time parameters as integer combinations of time-1 and time-√2 flow maps (exploiting the irrationality of √2). Part 2 shows that any orientation-preserving diffeomorphism can be approximated by a deep composition of H₁ ∪ H₂ flow maps: first, the diffeomorphism is approximated by a neural ODE flow map (via Agrachev–Caponigro controllability results), then the ODE is discretized via a splitting scheme (following Duan et al.) into forward-Euler steps, and each step is decomposed into at most six flow maps from H₁ ∪ H₂ using a specific six-step composition involving affine and leaky-ReLU flow maps. The two parts are combined via a composition-approximation lemma to yield the final finite-vocabulary result.

## Results

The paper proves the existence of a finite vocabulary V of O(d²) flow maps (comprising affine flows φ_{±e_i}^τ, φ_{±E_ij x}^τ and leaky-ReLU flows φ_{±Σ_{e_i,0}(x)}^τ, φ_{±Σ_{0,e_i}(x)}^τ with τ ∈ {1, √2}) such that any orientation-preserving diffeomorphism on a compact domain Ω ⊂ ℝᵈ can be uniformly approximated to arbitrary precision ε by composing a sequence of maps from V (Theorem 2.2). As a corollary, for d ≥ 2, the same vocabulary achieves Lᵖ-universal approximation for all continuous functions (Corollary 2.3). Additionally, the paper proves that every regular language recognized by a DFA can be exactly captured by a compositional flow-space model (CFSM) with a fixed dimension d ≥ 2 independent of the number of DFA states (Theorem 5.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments, datasets, or baseline comparisons. The contributions are existence theorems and constructive proofs rather than performance benchmarks. The paper positions its results relative to prior theoretical work: unlike feedforward network universal approximation (Cybenko 1989, Hornik 1989) which requires adjustable weights (an infinite vocabulary), this work achieves universal approximation with a fixed finite vocabulary of O(d²) maps where only the composition sequence varies. Compared to the compositional matrix-space model (CMSM) of Rudolph & Giesbrecht (2010), the proposed CFSM generalizes matrix embeddings to continuous mapping embeddings, offering infinite-dimensional expressiveness and a fixed dimension d ≥ 2 that is independent of the number of states in the target regular language, whereas CMSM requires the matrix order to exceed the number of states.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the classical universal approximation framework (Cybenko, Hornik, Leshno) where a fixed architecture's weights are adjusted; instead, it fixes a finite vocabulary of O(d²) flow maps and varies only the composition sequence. The construction draws on neural ODE discretization (Chen et al., 2018), the splitting-method view of feedforward networks as dynamical system discretizations (Duan et al., 2022), and controllability results for OP diffeomorphisms (Agrachev & Caponigro, 2010; Caponigro, 2011), but the key novelty is eliminating all learnable parameters in favor of a fixed finite set. The compositional flow-space model (CFSM) for regular languages directly generalizes the compositional matrix-space model (CMSM) of Rudolph & Giesbrecht (2010) by replacing matrix embeddings with continuous mapping embeddings, moving from a finite-dimensional to an infinite-dimensional function space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result establishes a striking theoretical fact: universal approximation on compact domains requires only O(d²) fixed mappings with no learnable parameters, which reframes the expressiveness question from 'how many parameters' to 'how long a composition sequence.' However, the construction is purely existential—the required sequence length can be astronomically large, and no efficient algorithm for finding the composition is provided. Open questions include extending to unbounded domains, designing practical vocabularies for specific function families, and characterizing which languages beyond regular grammars CFSM can capture. The linguistic analogy, while motivating, remains loose and does not yet yield testable predictions for NLP architectures.
