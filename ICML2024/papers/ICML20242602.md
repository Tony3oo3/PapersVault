## Problem

Existing in-context RL methods face a severe data acquisition bottleneck: Algorithm Distillation (AD) requires training thousands of single-task RL agents to collect learning histories, while Decision Pretrained Transformer (DPT) requires the optimal policy, which is often intractable to obtain. The paper asks whether in-context RL can emerge from synthetic learning histories generated by simply injecting scheduled noise into a (potentially suboptimal) policy, thereby eliminating the need for large-scale RL training or oracle access.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-episodic sequences of (state, action, reward) triplets, with embeddings concatenated to inflate sequence length by 3×; positional encoding preserves transition order. For the pixel-based Watermaze environment, observations are 3×72×96 RGB frames, while Dark Room and Key-to-Door use discrete grid states. During evaluation, the context is cross-episodic and initially empty, so the agent must learn entirely in-context over a fixed number of episodes (250 for Dark Room, 1000 for Key-to-Door, 200 for Watermaze), outputting the next action at each step.

## Method

AD^ε generates synthetic learning histories by running a policy in the environment while scheduling a noise parameter ε from 1.0 (pure random actions) down to 0.0 (optimal) or a non-zero floor (suboptimal), decreasing ε after every single action rather than per episode. This produces trajectories whose reward monotonically improves, mimicking a genuine learning process without any RL training. A GPT-2 causal Transformer (from the CORL library) is then pre-trained via standard supervised next-token prediction on these noise-induced histories, distilling the implicit policy-improvement operator between successive transitions. The key insight is that the Transformer can learn to perform in-context policy improvement from this synthetic curriculum alone, and when ε_end > 0, the resulting in-context agent can outperform even the best (suboptimal) policy present in the data by 74–120%.

## Results

On suboptimal-policy data, AD^ε achieves a +120% improvement over the best policy in the data on Dark Room (6.36 → 14.08 cumulative reward), +74% on Key-to-Door (1.0 → 1.74), and +76% on Watermaze (0.52 → 0.92). With optimal generating policies, the in-context agent generalizes to unseen goals across all three environments, matching the performance of the original Algorithm Distillation (AD) method. The learning pace ablation shows an optimal ε decay rate of 7×10⁻⁶ (2000 episodes per goal), with both faster and slower schedules degrading in-context performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Algorithm Distillation (AD, Laskin et al. 2022), which requires training 1000+ single-task RL agents to collect learning histories. AD^ε matches AD's in-context generalization performance on Dark Room and Key-to-Door (Appendix A, Figure 7) while eliminating the need for large-scale RL training, using only a noise-injection curriculum on a single generating policy. AD^ε also significantly outperforms Expert Distillation (ED), which trains only on expert trajectories and fails to develop in-context learning ability. Against Behavior Cloning, AD^ε demonstrates clear generalization to unseen tasks, whereas BC cannot transfer. The paper does not directly benchmark against DPT (Lee et al. 2023) in experiments, but positions AD^ε as addressing DPT's requirement for an optimal policy by showing that suboptimal generating policies (40–50% of optimal) still yield in-context agents that outperform the best policy in the data by 74–120%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Algorithm Distillation (AD, Laskin et al., 2022), which distills a policy-improvement operator from actual RL learning histories, and on Decision Pretrained Transformer (DPT, Lee et al., 2023), which pretrains on optimal actions to enable posterior-sampling-like inference. The key departure is replacing both requirements—thousands of trained RL agents or an optimal policy—with a synthetic noise-injection curriculum (AD^ε) that schedules ε from 1 to 0, inspired by Brown et al. (2020) in imitation learning. The authors position AD^ε as a democratization of data acquisition for in-context RL, showing that the Transformer can perform in-context policy distillation without any explicit reward model or downstream RL training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is meaningful: it removes the computationally expensive and unstable process of training thousands of single-task RL agents, and eliminates the intractable requirement of obtaining an optimal policy for complex environments. However, the experimental scope is limited to relatively simple environments (9×9 grid worlds and a basic 3D watermaze), and the 'suboptimal' policies in the main experiments are still oracle-based with residual noise rather than genuinely learned suboptimal agents (partially addressed in Appendix B). Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-dimensional continuous control, whether the learning-pace sensitivity generalizes across architectures, and whether truly arbitrary (non-oracle) suboptimal data suffices for in-context RL emergence.
