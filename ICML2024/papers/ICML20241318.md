## Problem

Shuffling gradient methods (Random Reshuffle, Shuffle Once, Incremental Gradient) are empirically popular but their last-iterate convergence in terms of the function value gap F(x_{K+1}) − F_* has not been established. Prior theoretical results either analyze only the average iterate, assume no regularizer ψ(x)=0 (excluding constrained optimization and problems like Lasso), or measure convergence via squared distance ‖x_{K+1}−x_*‖² which cannot be converted to a function value bound when F is non-smooth or ∇f(x_*)≠0.

## Input / Output

The proximal shuffling gradient method takes an initial point x₁ ∈ dom(ψ), a stepsize schedule {η_k}_{k=1}^K, and a sequence of permutations {σ_k} of [n], and produces the last iterate x_{K+1} after K epochs of n gradient updates each followed by a proximal step on nψ. The convergence guarantee is stated as a bound on the function value gap F(x_{K+1}) − F_*, with the stepsize depending on problem parameters (smoothness constants L_i, shuffling variance σ_any² or σ_rand², initial distance D) and an extra 1+log K factor. For the Lipschitz case, a linear-decay stepsize η_k = η(K−k+1)/K^{3/2} removes the logarithmic factor entirely.

## Method

The proof adapts the auxiliary-sequence technique from Zamani & Glineur (2023) for GD last-iterate convergence, constructing z_k as a convex combination of x_*, x_1, …, x_k and bounding F(x_{k+1}) − F(z_k) instead of F(x_{k+1}) − F(x_*). The key difficulty unique to shuffling is an extra Bregman divergence term B_f(z_k, x_*) that does not telescope; the authors handle it by applying the recursive argument for every k ∈ [K] (constructing K distinct auxiliary sequences) and developing a new algebraic inequality (Lemma E.2) to recursively bound all B_f(x_k, x_*). The shuffling residual R_k is bounded uniformly via Lemma E.1, and the 1+log K factor in the stepsize is essential to control the geometric series arising from the recursive Bregman bound. For Lipschitz components, the linear-decay stepsize from Zamani & Glineur (2023) yields a clean O(ḠD/√K) rate without any logarithmic overhead, matching the sample complexity of full gradient descent.

## Results

The paper establishes the first last-iterate convergence rates for shuffling gradient methods measured by the function value gap F(x_{K+1}) − F_*. For L-smooth components without strong convexity (Theorem 4.4), the rate is Õ(L^{1/3}σ_any^{2/3}D^{4/3}/K^{2/3}) for any permutation and Õ(L^{1/3}σ_rand^{2/3}D^{4/3}/(n^{1/3}K^{2/3})) for random permutations, nearly matching the existing lower bound Ω(L^{1/3}σ_any^{2/3}D^{4/3}/(n^{1/3}K^{2/3})) from Cha et al. (2023). Under μ-strong convexity of f (Theorem 4.6), the rate improves to Õ(Lσ_any²/(μ²K²)) for any permutation and Õ(Lσ_rand²/(μ²nK²)) for random permutations, nearly matching the lower bound Ω(Lσ_any²/(μ²nK²)). For G-Lipschitz components (Theorem 4.7), the rate is Õ(G̅D/√K) for any permutation, matching the best known rate for the averaged output of IG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For the smooth convex case, the paper's last-iterate rate for any permutation Õ(L^{1/3}σ_any^{2/3}D^{4/3}/K^{2/3}) matches the fastest existing rate for the averaged output of IG (Mishchenko et al., 2020) up to a log factor, while extending to a general regularizer ψ(x) and any permutation type. For random permutations, the rate Õ(L^{1/3}σ_rand^{2/3}D^{4/3}/(n^{1/3}K^{2/3})) nearly matches the lower bound Ω(L^{1/3}σ_any^{2/3}D^{4/3}/(n^{1/3}K^{2/3})) from Cha et al. (2023) when ψ(x)=0 (since σ_rand²=σ_any² in that case). In the strongly convex setting, the paper's rate Õ(Lσ_rand²/(μ²nK²)) for random permutations nearly matches the lower bound Ω(Lσ_any²/(μ²nK²)) from Safran & Shamir (2021) and Cha et al. (2023), and is the first last-iterate result in function value gap that is nearly tight in both n and K. Compared to Mishchenko et al. (2022), which only provides rates in squared distance for ProxRR/ProxSO, the paper improves the dependence from L²σ_rand²/(μ³nK²) to Lσ_rand²/(μ²nK²) and measures the function value gap directly. For Lipschitz components, the paper's Õ(G̅D/√K) last-iterate rate for any permutation matches the O(GD/√K) averaged-output rate of IG from Bertsekas (2011) and the O(G²/(μK)) rate from Nedić & Bertsekas (2001) in the strongly convex ψ case, while extending to all permutation types and general regularizers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the auxiliary-sequence technique from Zamani & Glineur (2023) for GD last-iterate analysis and its stochastic generalization by Liu & Zhou (2024) to the shuffling gradient setting, where the biased per-epoch gradient introduces a Bregman-divergence residual that prior analyses could not handle. It also borrows the parameter-free stepsize idea from Ivgi et al. (2023) to remove the need for knowing the initial distance D. Conceptually, the paper departs from the entire prior shuffling literature (Mishchenko et al. 2020, Nguyen et al. 2021, Cha et al. 2023) by shifting the analysis target from average or tail-average iterates to the last iterate measured by the function value gap, and by allowing a general regularizer ψ(x) rather than restricting to ψ=0.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results close a long-standing gap between the empirical success of returning the last iterate in shuffling methods and the theoretical guarantees that only covered averaged outputs, providing the first provable last-iterate rates that nearly match known lower bounds for both smooth and Lipschitz components. Open questions include whether the extra log K factor in the stepsize is truly necessary, whether time-varying stepsizes can achieve optimal dependence on both n and K simultaneously, and whether random permutations (RR/SO) can match SGD's sample complexity in the non-smooth regime. For practitioners, the theory validates the standard practice of returning the last iterate in shuffling-based training loops, particularly in constrained or regularized settings like Lasso.
