## Problem

Single-step adversarial training suffers from catastrophic overfitting (CO), where robustness collapses to near zero within a few iterations, yet the precise mechanism behind the resulting decision boundary distortion remains unclear. Prior work treats the DNN as a monolithic unit, leaving unexplored how individual layers transform during CO and why affected models can perfectly defend against single-step attacks while being highly vulnerable to multi-step attacks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241224/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and outputs class labels via a standard classifier head. During training, the key distinction is that LAP simultaneously generates an L∞-norm input perturbation and a layer-wise L2-norm weight perturbation in a single forward-backward pass, with the weight perturbation magnitude λ_l decreasing logarithmically from the first to the last layer, so no additional backward propagation is required beyond the standard single-step AT step.

## Method

LAP (Layer-Aware Adversarial Weight Perturbation) prevents CO by hindering the formation of pseudo-robust shortcuts—large weights in former layers that allow the model to defend against single-step attacks while bypassing genuine robust learning. The method applies an adaptive weight perturbation λ_l = β·(1 − (ln(l)/ln(L+1))^γ) that is strongest in early layers and gradually diminishes toward later layers, directly targeting where shortcuts form. An efficient implementation jointly maximizes over both input δ and weight ν perturbations in one step (Eq. 6), avoiding the extra backward pass that standard AWP requires. The paper further provides a PAC-Bayes upper bound (Eq. 10) showing that the worst-case weight perturbation term is controlled by the layer-aware σ_l = λ_l·‖w_l‖₂, and empirically demonstrates that removing only 10% of large weights from former layers in a CO-affected model restores PGD robustness, confirming the shortcut-dependency mechanism.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with PreActResNet-18, LAP consistently prevents catastrophic overfitting (CO) where baselines collapse to 0% robust accuracy. For example, R-LAP achieves 15.69% Auto Attack accuracy at 16/255 noise (vs. 0% for R-FGSM), and N-LAP reaches 8.85% at 32/255 (vs. 0% for N-FGSM). When CO does not occur, LAP still improves robustness: N-LAP attains 44.97% Auto Attack accuracy at 8/255, surpassing N-FGSM (44.21%) and approaching PGD-10 (46.37%). Training cost is only 7% higher than vanilla FGSM (11.8s vs. 11.0s per epoch on RTX 4090).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAP is integrated into three single-step AT baselines (V-FGSM, R-FGSM, N-FGSM) and compared against CO-mitigation methods (AT Free, Grad Align, ZeroGrad, MultiGrad) and multi-step references (PGD-2, PGD-10). The most dramatic wins are against CO-affected baselines: R-LAP recovers 15.69% Auto Attack accuracy at 16/255 where R-FGSM drops to 0%, and N-LAP achieves 8.85% at 32/255 where N-FGSM is at 0%. Against non-CO baselines, LAP provides consistent but modest gains: N-LAP improves over N-FGSM by 0.76% at 8/255 (44.97% vs. 44.21%) and 0.97% at 16/255 (19.55% vs. 18.58%). R-LAP at 8/255 (43.14%) slightly exceeds PGD-2 (42.92%), demonstrating that single-step LAP can match multi-step AT. However, PGD-10 still leads at higher noise levels (e.g., 46.37% vs. 44.97% at 8/255, 21.98% vs. 19.55% at 16/255), indicating a remaining gap to the multi-step oracle. Grad Align avoids CO but is outperformed by LAP at 16/255 (19.07% vs. 15.69% for R-LAP is actually lower; however N-LAP at 19.55% exceeds Grad Align's 19.07%), and ZeroGrad/MultiGrad still suffer CO at 16/255 and 32/255 where LAP does not.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial weight perturbation (AWP) framework of Wu et al. (2020) and the sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) idea of Foret et al. (2020) by introducing layer-aware, adaptive weight perturbations that decrease in magnitude from former to latter layers. It departs from prior CO-mitigation work (Grad Align, ZeroGrad, MultiGrad, subspace extraction, local linearity regularization) which treated the network as a monolithic unit, by conducting a layer-by-layer analysis that reveals an asymmetry: former layers undergo earlier and more severe distortion. The 'pseudo-robust shortcut' concept provides a new mechanistic explanation for CO that unifies the observed performance paradox (perfect single-step defense but multi-step vulnerability) with the weight-level changes (increased singular value variance concentrated in early layers).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The layer-aware analysis provides a concrete mechanistic account of why CO occurs, moving beyond the prior 'distorted decision boundary' description to identify specific weights (large singular values in former layers) as the causal agents. Practically, LAP adds only ~7% training overhead over vanilla FGSM while reliably preventing CO across CNN and Transformer architectures, multiple datasets, and extreme noise magnitudes (up to 32/255), making it a drop-in improvement for single-step AT pipelines. Open questions include whether the pseudo-robust shortcut mechanism generalizes to multi-step AT settings or other training paradigms, and whether the logarithmic layer-decay schedule (Eq. 5) is optimal or whether architecture-specific tuning is needed for very deep or heterogeneous networks.
