## Problem

Despite the empirical dominance of Transformers, the mathematical understanding of self-attention's smoothness—specifically its Lipschitz properties—is incomplete. This gap matters because the Lipschitz constant governs adversarial robustness, generalization bounds, and expressivity, yet no tight, regime-aware bounds existed for self-attention or masked self-attention. The paper fills this gap by deriving sharp upper and lower bounds across multiple asymptotic regimes and introducing the first mean-field framework for masked attention.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a sequence of n d-dimensional token vectors X = (x_1, …, x_n) constrained to a compact set B_R^n, along with self-attention parameters (Q, K, V) defining the attention matrix A = K^T Q / √k. It produces upper and lower bounds on the global Lipschitz constant on B_R^n and on the local Lipschitz constant at individual inputs, expressed in terms of n, R, and the spectral norms of A and V. For masked self-attention, the mean-field framework additionally requires encoding each token's position as a coordinate s ∈ [0,1], making the input a probability measure on [0,1] × R^d rather than on R^d alone.

## Method

The paper derives the Jacobian of self-attention explicitly and uses it to obtain a general O(√n) upper bound (Theorem 3.3) that is shown tight for practical sequence lengths via a matching lower bound (Proposition 3.4). In the mean-field regime (n → ∞), self-attention is recast as a pushforward map on probability measures equipped with the 2-Wasserstein distance, yielding an O(R²e^{CR²}) bound that the paper proves cannot be improved (Proposition 3.6). In the large-radius regime (R → ∞ with fixed n), the attention matrix becomes 1-sparse row-wise, and the local Lipschitz constant is shown to be O(√n) independent of R (Theorem 3.7). For masked self-attention, the authors introduce a novel conditional optimal transport distance d_p on P_c([0,1] × R^d) that respects the sequential structure, and prove analogous bounds in all three regimes. Numerical experiments on pretrained BERT and GPT-2 confirm an n^{1/4} growth rate on real data and a √n rate on constructed adversarial inputs, validating the theoretical tightness.

## Results

The paper establishes that the Lipschitz constant of self-attention on B_R^n grows at most like √n (Theorem 3.3: Lip ≤ √3‖V‖₂(‖A‖₂²R⁴(4n+1)+n)^{1/2}), and proves this rate is tight for sequence lengths n ≤ 1+e^{2R²γ} (Proposition 3.4). In the mean-field regime (n→∞), the Lipschitz constant is bounded by ‖V‖₂(1+3‖A‖₂R²)e^{2‖A‖₂R²}, independent of n (Theorem 3.5). Empirically, on pretrained BERT and GPT-2 with real text data (Alice in Wonderland, AG_NEWS), the local Lipschitz constant grows approximately like n^{1/4}, while on constructed adversarial inputs it grows like √n, confirming the tightness of the theoretical bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without direct method-to-method comparisons; instead, it benchmarks its bounds against prior theoretical results and empirical observations. Compared to Kim et al. (2021), who showed self-attention is not globally Lipschitz and provided a quadratic-in-R lower bound, this paper gives a matching √n upper bound in n and identifies the precise regime where the bound is tight. Compared to Geshkovski et al. (2023a), who provided a mean-field upper bound of the form CR²e^{CR²}, this paper proves that bound cannot be significantly improved (Proposition 3.6) by constructing explicit two-Dirac measure configurations achieving Ω(R²e^{R²}) growth. The empirical experiments on BERT and GPT-2 show that real data exhibits a much milder n^{1/4} growth rate than the worst-case √n, while adversarial constructions recover the √n rate, validating the theoretical tightness. The paper also introduces the first mean-field framework for masked self-attention (using conditional optimal transport), filling a gap left by prior work that only handled permutation-equivariant (unmasked) attention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Lipschitz analysis of self-attention initiated by Kim et al. (2021) and Geshkovski et al. (2023a) in three key directions: from global to local Lipschitz constants, from unmasked to masked self-attention, and with tightness proofs across multiple regimes. The mean-field framework for unmasked attention builds directly on Sander et al. (2022) and Geshkovski et al. (2023a), while the novel mean-field framework for masked self-attention introduces a conditional optimal transport distance (from Hosseini et al., 2023) to handle the sequential, non-permutation-equivariant structure. The large radius regime analysis draws inspiration from Kim et al. (2021) but provides a cleaner characterization of the Jacobian's limiting form.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tight √n scaling of the Lipschitz constant establishes a fundamental obstruction to sequence-length-independent robustness guarantees for Transformers, with direct implications for adversarial robustness certification and Lipschitz-constrained architectures. The novel mean-field framework for masked self-attention paves the way for PDE-based analysis of Decoder dynamics, complementing existing work on Encoders. Open questions include how to design Lipschitz-constrained attention that avoids the dramatic expressivity loss from dividing by the mean-field bound, and why pretrained GPT-2 exhibits growth rates exceeding √n due to positional encoding correlations with sequence length.
