## Problem

Retrieval-augmented LLMs like Retro have been limited to ~7.5–11B parameters and have never been combined with instruction tuning, leaving a significant gap in zero-shot generalization compared to decoder-only LLMs scaled to hundreds of billions of parameters. This paper asks whether retrieval-augmented pretraining can be scaled to 48B parameters and whether subsequent instruction tuning can unlock the full potential of the retrieval-enhanced decoder.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242102/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pretraining, the model consumes 4096-token sequences split into 64-token chunks, retrieving top-2 nearest-neighbor chunks from a 1.2T-token Faiss-indexed database (19B chunks with BERT embeddings) per chunk. During instruction tuning and inference, the model takes multi-turn conversational prompts (system/user/assistant roles) with retrieved contexts (top-5 from DRAGON+ for QA tasks) concatenated into the prompt, and generates text answers of up to 256 tokens. A 0/1 gate on the cross-attention output allows the model to operate with or without the Retro encoder, effectively switching between a 48B retrieval-augmented model and a 43B pure GPT decoder.

## Method

The core technique is 'Retro-fitting': continuing pretraining of a 43B GPT (trained on 1.1T tokens) with retrieval augmentation on 100B additional tokens, retrieving from 1.2T tokens. Unlike the original Retro-fitting which freezes the decoder, this work unfreezes all decoder parameters and jointly trains the Retro encoder (a 2-layer bidirectional transformer), chunk-wise cross-attention layers, and the full GPT decoder, achieving better perplexity at only 2.58% additional GPU hours over the GPT counterpart. A manually-set 0/1 gate between the cross-attention output and the residual connection allows bypassing the Retro encoder during instruction tuning (where high-quality retrieved neighbors are unavailable) and at inference, making the model learn to operate both with and without retrieval. After instruction tuning on 128K conversational samples (loss masked to assistant responses only), the surprising finding is that ablating the encoder entirely (InstructRetro 43B) yields comparable downstream performance to the full 48B model, indicating that retrieval-augmented continued pretraining fundamentally improves the GPT decoder backbone itself.

## Results

On eight short-form QA and reading comprehension tasks, InstructRetro 43B (decoder-only, no encoder) achieves an average 7% relative improvement over InstructGPT_RAG 43B, with the largest gains on NarrativeQA (+11.3%) and NewsQA (+9.5%). On four long-form QA tasks, the average improvement is 10%, and on three summarization tasks it reaches 16% (e.g., GovReport ROUGE improves from 12.59 to 17.46). Notably, InstructRetro 43B outperforms Llama 2 RAG 70B on 7 of 8 short-form QA tasks and all 3 summarization tasks, while matching or exceeding FLAN-LaMDA 137B (3× larger) on most benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InstructRetro 43B consistently outperforms its direct counterpart InstructGPT_RAG 43B (same architecture, same data, no retrieval pretraining) across all 8 short-form QA tasks, with the largest margins on NarrativeQA (60.0 vs 53.9 F1) and NewsQA (57.4 vs 52.4 F1). Against Llama 2 RAG 70B, InstructRetro 43B wins on 7 of 8 tasks (losing only on DROP: 54.8 vs 57.2 F1) and on all 3 summarization tasks (e.g., GovReport 17.46 vs 16.98 ROUGE). Compared to FLAN-LaMDA 137B (3× larger), InstructRetro 43B achieves substantially higher scores on NQ (38.9 vs 20.7 EM), TriviaQA (78.3 vs 68.1 EM), and DROP (54.8 vs 22.7 F1), though FLAN-LaMDA edges ahead on SQuAD 1.1 F1 (80.1 vs 77.1). Against prior retrieval-augmented LLMs (Retro 7.5B, Retro++ 9B, Atlas 11B, Raven 11B), InstructRetro 43B achieves large gains, e.g., NQ EM of 38.9 vs 8.9–29.6. On RA-DIT 65B, InstructRetro 43B leads on NQ (38.9 vs 35.2 EM) and TriviaQA (78.3 vs 75.4 EM). The decoder-only InstructRetro 43B (encoder ablated) performs comparably to the full InstructRetro 48B, confirming that retrieval-augmented pretraining improves the GPT decoder backbone itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Retro (Borgeaud et al., 2022), which introduced retrieval-augmented pretraining for autoregressive LMs at 7.5B parameters, by scaling it to 48B via a 'Retro-fitting' procedure: continuing pretraining from a 43B GPT on 100B additional tokens with retrieval from a 1.2T-token database. A key methodological departure from the original Retro-fitting is unfreezing the decoder during continued pretraining rather than freezing it, which the authors show yields better perplexity. The paper then applies the standard instruction tuning recipe (FLAN-style multi-dataset blending) to the retrieval-augmented foundation model, positioning InstructRetro as a bridge between the retrieval-augmented pretraining line and the instruction-tuned LLM line (InstructGPT, FLAN).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that retrieval-augmented pretraining's perplexity advantage does not diminish with scale (the gap persists from 8B to 48B), and that only 2.58% additional GPU hours are needed relative to training GPT on the full 1.2T tokens. The most impactful open question is whether the encoder-free decoder (InstructRetro 43B) can be further improved with retrieval-augmented instruction tuning data, which the authors identify as a key future direction. Limitations include evaluation at a single model scale for the encoder-ablation finding, reliance on proprietary datasets for long-form QA, and the absence of RLHF, which limits comparison with the strongest instruction-tuned systems.
