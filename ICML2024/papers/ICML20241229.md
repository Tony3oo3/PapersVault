## Problem

Existing equivariant neural networks such as Vector Neurons are restricted to SO(3) acting on 3D Euclidean vectors, with no general framework for equivariant processing of data in arbitrary semisimple Lie algebras under adjoint (conjugation) actions. Additionally, Vector Neurons lack a mechanism to mix information across the geometric dimension, limiting their expressiveness for tasks where the group action involves left-multiplication on the geometric channels.

## Input / Output

The network consumes Lie algebra elements expressed as K-dimensional vectors (K = dim(g): 3 for so(3), 8 for sl(3), 10 for sp(4)) with a feature dimension C, i.e., inputs in R^{K×C}. For point cloud registration, two noisy point clouds are encoded via PointNet before feeding into the Lie Neurons backbone; for dynamics learning, the angular velocity vector is augmented with learnable equivariant weights m ∈ R^{3×C} that implicitly encode the inertia tensor. Outputs range from Lie algebra elements (equivariant regression, dynamics), scalars (invariant regression, classification), to SO(3) rotation matrices (registration).

## Method

Lie Neurons is an MLP-style architecture where linear layers multiply on the right (feature dimension) so they commute with the adjoint action applied as a left K×K matrix multiplication on the geometric dimension. Two equivariant nonlinearities are introduced: LN-ReLU, which uses the Killing form B(x,d) as an invariant scalar to project negative components back to zero (generalizing Vector Neurons' norm-based ReLU), and LN-Bracket, which computes the commutator [xU, xV] with a residual skip connection to capture noncommutativity. A geometric channel mixing layer constructs a K×K matrix M = x₁x₂ᵀ from two learned equivariant features and applies it to the input, enabling left-multiplication-style mixing in the geometric dimension (currently for so(n)). Pooling and invariant layers exploit the Killing form's adjoint invariance for max-pooling across elements and for producing scalar invariant outputs. The framework specializes to Vector Neurons on so(3) when only the ReLU nonlinearity is used, but the bracket layer and mixing module provide additional capacity that proves critical for tasks like BCH regression and rigid-body dynamics.

## Results

On the BCH formula regression task over so(3), Lie Neurons achieves a Frobenius error of 6.9×10⁻⁴ and log error of 4.9×10⁻⁴, outperforming EMLP (2.6×10⁻³ / 1.8×10⁻³) by over 3× and Vector Neurons (0.617 / 0.454) by nearly three orders of magnitude. On the sp(4) invariant function regression, Lie Neurons reaches an MSE of 2.70×10⁻⁴ with an invariance error of 2.00×10⁻⁴, compared to 0.107–0.126 MSE and 0.585–0.722 invariance error for MLP baselines. For ISS dynamics learning, Lie Neurons predicts trajectories with 0.005–0.018 rad/s error, while MLP and EMLP fail to generalize to unseen trajectories (errors of 0.43–1.03 rad/s). On point cloud registration (ModelNet40), Lie Neurons with mixing achieves 1.879° average error versus 2.227° for Vector Neurons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Vector Neurons (Deng et al., 2021), Lie Neurons achieves a ~900× lower Frobenius error on BCH regression (6.9×10⁻⁴ vs 0.617), demonstrating that the Lie bracket nonlinearity and geometric mixing layer are critical for capturing the full BCH structure; on point cloud registration, Lie Neurons with mixing (1.879°) also outperforms Vector Neurons (2.227°) by 0.35°. Against EMLP (Finzi et al., 2021), Lie Neurons is ~3.8× more accurate on BCH (6.9×10⁻⁴ vs 2.6×10⁻³) and dramatically superior on ISS dynamics (0.005–0.018 rad/s vs 0.429–1.027 rad/s), where EMLP fails to generalize to unseen trajectories. Against e3nn (Geiger & Smidt, 2022), Lie Neurons is ~930× more accurate on BCH (6.9×10⁻⁴ vs 0.641), as e3nn and Vector Neurons both fail to converge on this task. The MLP baseline, while competitive on the non-augmented BCH test set (0.017), degrades by 17× under adjoint augmentation (0.295), whereas Lie Neurons maintains identical performance, confirming its equivariance-by-construction property.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly generalizes Vector Neurons (Deng et al., 2021) from SO(3)-equivariant processing of 3D vectors to adjoint-equivariant processing of arbitrary finite-dimensional semisimple Lie algebras. The key conceptual shift is that the input domain is the Lie algebra itself (with the adjoint/conjugation action as the symmetry), rather than R^n with a left group action, and the invariant bilinear form is the Killing form rather than the standard inner product. Two novel architectural components—the Lie bracket nonlinearity and the geometric channel mixing layer—exploit Lie algebraic structure in ways that have no counterpart in Vector Neurons or in the broader equivariant MLP literature (EMLP, e3nn). The paper positions itself as an MLP-style generalization, contrasting with convolutional or transformer-based equivariant approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Lie Neurons provide a principled, architecture-level framework for equivariant learning on Lie algebras, which are the natural representation space for problems in robotics, control, and computer vision (rigid body dynamics, homography estimation, attitude estimation). The geometric mixing layer addresses a concrete limitation of Vector Neurons that becomes critical when the dynamics involve left-multiplication by a system parameter (e.g., the inertia tensor in Euler-Poincaré dynamics). Key limitations include: the mixing module currently works only for so(n), the Killing-form-based ReLU requires semisimplicity, and the paper assumes a known basis. An important open question the authors identify is finding equivariant lifts from standard sensor measurement spaces (R^n) into Lie algebraic spaces, which would broaden practical applicability.
