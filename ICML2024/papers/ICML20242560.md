## Problem

Existing text-to-video models for robotics are limited to synthesizing videos for language instructions similar to those seen during training, failing to generalize to novel combinations of objects and spatial relations. This is critical in decision-making, where a world model must synthesize plans for previously unseen task compositions in new environments. The paper addresses this by asking whether the natural compositionality of language can be exploited to factorize video generation and achieve compositional generalization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language instruction (e.g., 'move pepsi can near plastic bottle'), a starting observation frame, and optionally a goal image or goal sketch as multimodal conditioning. The language is parsed into a verb phrase (action) and a prepositional phrase (spatial relation), which serve as separate conditioning signals. The output is a video plan of 8 frames, generated at 64×64 and upscaled via a three-stage cascade to 256×256, with the starting frame concatenated to all noisy frames to enforce background consistency.

## Method

RoboDreamer factorizes the video generation distribution p(τ|L) into a product of conditional densities, each conditioned on a single parsed language component (verb phrase or prepositional phrase) or multimodal input, expressed as p(τ|L) ∝ ∏ p(τ|l_i)^(1/N). Training learns a shared score function ε(τ,t|l_i) per component using a hybrid denoising objective that randomly subsets the component set, encouraging each score to capture its relevant information while the average matches the full instruction. At inference, score functions from novel combinations of components are composed via classifier-free guidance, enabling zero-shot generalization to unseen language pairings. The architecture builds on AVDC/Imagen with spatial-temporal convolutions, temporal attention layers, and a three-stage cascaded diffusion pipeline; multimodal inputs (goal images, sketches) are encoded via a frozen VQVAE encoder and integrated through a Perceiver module with cross-attention into the U-Net.

## Results

On the RT-1 video generation benchmark, RoboDreamer achieves 90.1% human-evaluated task completion on seen instructions and 81.3% on unseen instructions, substantially outperforming AVDC (63.1/46.9) and HiP (70.3/50.1). With multimodal conditioning (goal image), RoboDreamer(t+i) further improves to 95.8% human evaluation and 444.3 FVD, compared to 46.9% and 517.1 FVD for AVDC. On RLBench robotic planning, RoboDreamer achieves a 49.3% average success rate across six tasks, surpassing Image-BC (31.6%), Hiveformer (44.2%), and UniPi (41.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoboDreamer outperforms all four baselines across both evaluation settings. Against AVDC (Ko et al., 2023), the primary video-generation baseline, RoboDreamer gains +27.0 points on seen tasks (90.1 vs 63.1) and +34.4 points on unseen tasks (81.3 vs 46.9), demonstrating that compositional factorization is critical for zero-shot generalization. Against HiP (Ajay et al., 2023), a latent video diffusion model, RoboDreamer leads by +19.8 (seen) and +31.2 (unseen) points, with the gap widening substantially on unseen combinations. On RLBench robotic planning, RoboDreamer (49.3% avg) beats Hiveformer (Guhur et al., 2023) by +5.1 points and UniPi (Du et al., 2023) by +8.3 points, with particularly large margins on long-horizon tasks like stack blocks (18.5% vs 10.6% and 7.1%) and take shoes (10.5% vs 1.0% and 3.8%). The ablation (RoboDreamer w/o text parsing) confirms that the compositional decomposition itself contributes +4.6 (seen) and +12.5 (unseen) points over the monolithic variant.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoboDreamer builds directly on the text-to-video generation framework for robot planning established by UniPi (Du et al., 2023b) and AVDC (Ko et al., 2023), which formulate planning as conditional video synthesis followed by inverse dynamics. Its key departure is the compositional factorization of the generative model: rather than conditioning a single monolithic diffusion model on the full instruction, it parses language into verb and prepositional phrases and trains a product-of-densities score function over these primitives, drawing on the EBM-based compositional generation literature (Du et al., 2023a; Liu et al., 2022) previously applied to images. The multimodal extension (goal images, sketches) parallels ControlNet-style conditioning but avoids the paired-data requirement by treating each modality as an independent factor in the product.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The compositional factorization provides a principled mechanism for zero-shot generalization to unseen task combinations in robotics, where exhaustive data coverage of all action-object-relation triples is infeasible. The large gap on unseen instructions (81.3 vs. 46.9 human score) is practically significant for deploying video-based planners in new environments. However, the approach remains limited to single-camera views, struggles with real-world image diversity, and the RLBench success-rate gains over strong baselines like Hiveformer are modest (49.3 vs. 44.2 average), suggesting the compositional advantage is most pronounced in the video-generation quality rather than end-to-end task completion. Open questions include integrating 3D/multi-camera inductive biases and co-training with web-scale video to improve real-world generalization.
