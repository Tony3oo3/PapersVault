## Problem

Existing demographic parity metrics have two critical blind spots: ΔDP only compares average predictions and misses distribution-level disparities, while ABCC integrates CDF differences over the entire prediction range and can conceal extreme local disparities at specific prediction values. Since real-world decision thresholds are set at particular prediction values, a classifier with a small overall ABCC can still exhibit severe group unfairness at the threshold where the maximal CDF gap occurs. No prior metric explicitly captures this worst-case local disparity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard supervised inputs—tabular feature vectors (Adult, Bank) or 180×180 face images (CelebA)—along with a binary sensitive group label (gender or age bracket) and a binary target label. It outputs a single prediction probability in [0,1] per instance. The fairness metric MCDP(ε) is then computed over the empirical CDFs of these probabilities partitioned by group, with the hyperparameter ε controlling the neighborhood width for local disparity measurement. For image datasets, a pretrained ResNet-18 backbone extracts features before the classification head.

## Method

The paper introduces MCDP(ε), defined as the maximum over all prediction points y₀ of the minimum CDF disparity |F₀(ŷ)−F₁(ŷ)| within the ε-neighborhood [y₀−ε, y₀+ε], which smooths sharp distributional spikes while still capturing the worst local gap. Two estimation algorithms are provided: an exact O(N²) traversal over sorted predictions and an approximate O(K²/ε) algorithm that samples equally-spaced points at step-size ε/K, provably over-estimating the true value with monotonically decreasing error as K grows. For training, DiffMCDP replaces the non-differentiable empirical CDF with a temperature-sigmoid approximation ΔF̃_τ(ŷ) and solves a bi-level optimization: the inner level finds ŷ* = argmax ΔF̃_τ(ŷ) (the prediction achieving maximal disparity), and the outer level minimizes the standard classification loss plus λ·ΔF̃_τ(ŷ*), directly regularizing the worst-case local disparity rather than an averaged quantity.

## Results

On the Adult dataset, DiffMCDP achieves the lowest MCDP(0) of 11.53 (vs. 21.18 for the best baseline DiffABCC) and the lowest ABCC of 6.09, while maintaining 73.90% AP. On Bank, it reaches MCDP(0)=11.02 and ABCC=1.83, both best among all methods. On the image datasets, DiffMCDP attains MCDP(0)=23.10 on CelebA-A (vs. 27.38 for DRAlign) and MCDP(0)=20.29 on CelebA-W (vs. 23.77 for DiffABCC), with the best ΔDP values (21.10 and 19.58 respectively). Across all four datasets, DiffMCDP consistently achieves the best maximal local disparity while remaining competitive on expectation-level (ΔDP) and distribution-level (ABCC) fairness metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffMCDP outperforms all baselines on the proposed MCDP(0) metric across every dataset: on Adult it reduces MCDP(0) from 21.18 (DiffABCC, best baseline) to 11.53, a 45% improvement; on Bank it improves from 13.22 (FairMixup) to 11.02. Against AdvDebias, DiffMCDP achieves substantially lower MCDP(0) (11.53 vs. 29.28 on Adult; 11.02 vs. 17.52 on Bank) and lower ABCC, though AdvDebias retains a slight AP advantage. Compared to FairMixup, DiffMCDP achieves lower MCDP(0) on all four datasets (e.g., 11.53 vs. 24.87 on Adult) and lower ABCC on tabular data, while FairMixup has a marginal ΔDP edge on Adult (3.87 vs. 6.63). Versus DRAlign, DiffMCDP wins on MCDP(0) across all datasets (e.g., 23.10 vs. 27.38 on CelebA-A) and achieves the best ΔDP on both image datasets (21.10 vs. 26.36 on CelebA-A; 19.58 vs. 22.20 on CelebA-W). The paper also notes that optimizing ΔDP (DiffDP) or ABCC (DiffABCC) alone does reduce MCDP(0) but cannot match the direct MCDP(0) regularization, confirming that expectation-level and distribution-level objectives are insufficient for minimizing maximal local disparity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the distribution-level fairness measurement line initiated by Han et al. (2023a), who introduced ABCC to capture full CDF disparity beyond the expectation-level ΔDP. The key departure is identifying that even ABCC's integral over all predictions can mask extreme local disparity at specific prediction values, motivating the MCDP(ε) metric that focuses on the worst-case local CDF gap. For training, the paper combines a temperature-sigmoid differentiable CDF approximation with the bilevel optimization framework of Ji et al. (2021), a combination not previously applied to fairness regularization. The paper positions itself against in-processing baselines (FairMixup, DRAlign, AdvDebias) that optimize different fairness objectives, showing that directly targeting maximal local disparity yields superior trade-offs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical concern: when a deployment threshold is set at the prediction value where local CDF disparity peaks, group unfairness can be dramatically worse than what aggregate metrics suggest. The theoretical upper bound linking MCDP(ε) to ABCC (Theorem 3.1, property ⑤) provides a principled justification for minimizing MCDP(0) as a conservative fairness objective. Open questions include whether the metric generalizes to multi-group or continuous sensitive attributes, whether the conservatism of MCDP(0) is desirable in all deployment settings, and whether the bilevel optimization approach scales to larger models. The approximate algorithm's 1000× speedup makes the metric practical for large-scale evaluation.
