## Problem

Federated Prompt Learning (FPL) with pre-trained Vision-Language models faces a fundamental tension: strong personalization to heterogeneous local data distributions can overwrite the category-agnostic knowledge in the global prompt, destroying CLIP's generalization to unseen categories and domains, while preserving generalization limits adaptation to local needs. No prior FPL method simultaneously addresses both personalization and generalization, and existing personalized federated learning techniques from traditional FL do not transfer directly to the prompt-tuning paradigm with frozen VLM backbones.

## Input / Output

Each client receives RGB images (standard resolutions per dataset, e.g., 224×224 for CLIP) paired with class labels, and the frozen CLIP image encoder extracts visual features while a learnable text prompt (16 tokens, dimension 512) is fed through the frozen text encoder to produce class predictions via cosine-similarity matching. The output is a per-client personalized prompt p_i = p_G + U_i V_i, where p_G is the aggregated global prompt and U_i V_i is a low-rank (bottleneck b=8) adaptation term retained locally; only p_G is communicated to the server each round.

## Method

FedPGP decomposes each client's prompt into a shared global prompt p_G (aggregated via weighted FedAvg) and a client-specific low-rank adaptation Δp_i = U_i V_i (U_i ∈ R^{d×b}, V_i ∈ R^{b×k}), ensuring personalization without overwriting the global prompt's generalizable content. A prompt-wise contrastive loss simultaneously enforces two objectives: it pulls the global prompt representation g(p_G) toward the handcrafted CLIP prompt representation g(p_C) (positive pair, preserving category-agnostic generalization) while pushing g(p_G) away from the personalized prompt representation g(p_i) (negative pair, encouraging the low-rank term to capture genuinely client-specific knowledge). The overall training objective is L = L_ce + μ·L_con with μ=1, optimized locally for E epochs per communication round before the global prompt is uploaded for aggregation. The low-rank bottleneck (b=8) acts as an intrinsic-dimensionality constraint, limiting how much of the global prompt's information can be rewritten by local adaptation.

## Results

On the average of five fine-grained datasets (OxfordPets, Flowers102, DTD, Caltech101, Food101), FedPGP achieves 95.67% local accuracy, 85.69% base-class accuracy, 81.75% novel-class accuracy, and a harmonic mean (HM) of 87.33%, outperforming all baselines on HM and local accuracy. For leave-one-domain-out generalization, FedPGP reaches 96.54% average accuracy on Office-Caltech10 and 86.76% on DomainNet, surpassing the next-best method (PromptFL at 96.04% and 86.04% respectively). Under pathological Non-IID personalization (10 clients, non-overlapping classes), FedPGP attains 98.96% on OxfordPets and 99.29% on Flowers102, exceeding pFedPrompt by 7.1 and 12.8 percentage points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedPGP outperforms all eight baselines on the harmonic mean (87.33% vs. 85.56% for FedMaPLe, 85.35% for FedCoCoOp, 84.46% for PromptFL, 83.78% for Prompt+Prox, 80.72% for zero-shot CLIP, and 77.55% for CoOp). Against CoOp, FedPGP gains +16.3 pp on base-class accuracy (85.69 vs. 69.40) and +8.6 pp on novel-class accuracy (81.75 vs. 73.16), confirming that federated aggregation with CLIP knowledge-guidance resolves CoOp's severe overfitting to base categories. Compared to PromptFL and Prompt+Prox, FedPGP improves local accuracy by +5.7 and +5.8 pp respectively while maintaining comparable base/novel performance, demonstrating that low-rank personalization recovers the client-specific adaptation sacrificed by uniform prompt averaging. Against pFedPrompt (the strongest personalization baseline), FedPGP leads by 7.1 pp on OxfordPets and 12.8 pp on Flowers102 under pathological Non-IID, while also preserving generalization that pFedPrompt does not explicitly address. On domain generalization, FedPGP edges out PromptFL by +0.5 pp on Office-Caltech10 and +0.7 pp on DomainNet, and dramatically surpasses CoOp (39.18% vs. 96.54% on Office-Caltech10), showing that federated prompt learning with CLIP guidance is essential for cross-domain transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedPGP extends the federated prompt learning paradigm established by PromptFL (federated aggregation of learnable text prompts via FedAvg) and the CoOp prompt-tuning framework for CLIP. It departs from prior FPL work by being the first to jointly optimize for both personalization (adapting to heterogeneous local distributions) and generalization (preserving CLIP's category-agnostic knowledge), whereas pFedPrompt focused solely on personalization and FedTPG addressed generalization without accounting for data heterogeneity. The method draws on low-rank adaptation (LoRA-style decomposition) from parameter-efficient fine-tuning and introduces a novel prompt-wise contrastive loss that simultaneously pulls the global prompt toward CLIP's handcrafted prompt (positive pair) and pushes it away from the personalized prompt (negative pair).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper identifies and formalizes a fundamental tension in federated prompt learning: aggressive personalization to local distributions erodes the generalization capacity that makes VLMs valuable in the first place. The low-rank adaptation mechanism provides a practical, interpretable knob (bottleneck dimension b) for controlling this trade-off, and the ablation showing full-rank adaptation collapses base-class accuracy from ~86% to ~48% while boosting local accuracy to ~99% makes the tension concrete. Limitations include evaluation on relatively small-scale benchmarks (10-class domain generalization, 5 fine-grained datasets), the absence of theoretical justification for why low-rank is the right constraint (left as future work), and the method's dependence on CLIP's handcrafted prompt as a fixed anchor, which may not transfer to other VLMs or open-vocabulary settings.
