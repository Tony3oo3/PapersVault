## Problem

Modern distribution compression methods (kernel thinning, Compress++, recombination) achieve better-than-i.i.d. approximation error but require unbiased input sequences, such as i.i.d. samples or fast-mixing MCMC targeting the correct distribution. In practice, MCMC outputs are routinely biased by burn-in, tempering, or approximate samplers, and the only existing debiased method (Stein Thinning) provides only O(1/√m) error—no better than i.i.d. sampling. This paper closes that gap by delivering provably better-than-i.i.d. compression guarantees even when the input targets the wrong distribution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n biased sample points from a Markov chain targeting some distribution Q ≠ P, a mean-zero Stein kernel k_P (with known zero expectation under P), and a target coreset size m. It outputs one of three coreset types: equal-weighted (m points each weighted 1/m), simplex-weighted (m points with non-negative weights summing to 1), or constant-preserving (m points with real-valued weights summing to 1). The kernel must satisfy a covering-number growth condition (POLYGROWTH or LOGGROWTH), and the input points must be slow-growing (radius O((log n)^γ) with bounded kernel diagonal), which holds with probability 1 for geometrically ergodic chains with sub-exponential targets.

## Method

The core strategy is a two-stage pipeline: first debias the input into an accurate approximation of P, then compress that approximation. For equal-weighted compression, Stein Kernel Thinning (SKT) runs Stein Thinning to produce a debiased equal-weighted sample, then applies Kernel Thinning to compress it to √n points in O(n²) time; Low-rank SKT replaces the greedy debiasing with an adaptive low-rank scheme (WeightedRPCholesky + accelerated entropic mirror descent over Q rounds) and the Compress++ divide-and-conquer framework to achieve the same error in Õ(n^{1.5}) time. For simplex-weighted and constant-preserving compression, Stein Recombination and Stein Cholesky replace the final KT stage with RecombinationThinning (WeightedRPCholesky + Carathéodory recombination + KT-Swap-LS + QP) or CholeskyThinning (WeightedRPCholesky on a constant-regularized kernel + KT-Swap-LS + closed-form reweighting), achieving the same Õ(n^{-1/2}) excess MMD with only poly-log(n) points. The theoretical foundation rests on new covering-number bounds for Stein kernels (which inherit growth properties from their base kernels even when unbounded), spectral decay rates for kernel matrices, and simplex-weighted coreset quality guarantees.

## Results

The paper's headline theoretical result is that Stein Kernel Thinning (SKT) achieves Õ(n^{-1/2}) excess MMD with only √n equal-weighted points in O(n²) time, compared to Stein Thinning's requirement of n points for the same error rate. Low-rank SKT (LSKT) matches this guarantee in Õ(n^{1.5}) sub-quadratic time. For weighted compression, Stein Recombination and Stein Cholesky achieve the same Õ(n^{-1/2}) MMD with as few as poly-log(n) points. In experiments across burn-in, approximate MCMC, and tempering biases, the proposed methods consistently outperform Stein thinning and standard thinning on both MMD and auxiliary energy distance metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Stein Thinning (ST, Riabiz et al. 2022), which greedily thins biased input to minimize MMD but is only guaranteed O(n^{-1/2}) error requiring n points. The paper's SKT achieves the same Õ(n^{-1/2}) error with only √n points, a √n reduction in coreset size, and the weighted variants (SR, SC) reduce this further to poly-log(n) points. In the burn-in experiment (Goodwin model, d=4, n=2×10⁶), the proposed methods consistently outperform both Stein thinning and standard thinning on MMD and auxiliary energy distance, while matching the quality of a 6-chain burn-in oracle (Vats and Knudson, 2021) using only a single chain. In the approximate MCMC experiment (Bayesian logistic regression on Forest Covtype, d=54, n=2²⁴), the proposals improve upon both standard thinning and Stein thinning across all three compression tasks in MMD, energy distance, and mean integration error. In the tempering experiment (cardiac calcium signaling model, d=38, n=3×10⁶), the methods perform on par with Stein thinning for equal-weighted compression but yield substantial gains over both Stein and standard thinning for the simplex-weighted and constant-preserving tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: Stein thinning (ST, Riabiz et al. 2022), which corrects bias in MCMC samples but only achieves i.i.d.-level O(1/√m) MMD error, and kernel thinning (KT, Dwivedi & Mackey 2021/2022) with Compress++ (Shetty et al. 2022), which achieves better-than-i.i.d. O(1/m) error but requires unbiased input. The key insight is a two-stage pipeline—first debias via greedy ST or a new low-rank debiasing scheme built on randomly pivoted Cholesky (Chen et al. 2022), then compress via KT, Compress++, recombination (Tchernychova 2016; Hayakawa et al. 2022/2023), or Cholesky-based thinning. The paper also contributes new theoretical infrastructure: the first covering number bounds and eigenvalue decay rates for (typically unbounded) Stein kernels, and new guarantees for simplex-weighted and constant-preserving coresets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for Bayesian inference: biases from burn-in, tempering, and approximate MCMC are ubiquitous, and the paper provides the first provably better-than-i.i.d. compression for such biased inputs, with weighted coresets as small as poly-log(n) points matching the guarantees of √n equal-weighted points. This is especially valuable for expensive downstream simulations (e.g., cardiac electrophysiology requiring 1000+ CPU hours per point). Open questions include extending guarantees to modified Stein discrepancies that better capture isolated modes (Liu et al. 2023; Bénard et al. 2024), combining with non-myopic or online compression, and whether the low-rank debiasing procedure can be made fully adaptive without the fixed-rank assumption.
