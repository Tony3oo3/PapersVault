## Problem

Deep Equilibrium (DEQ) models define network outputs as fixed points of nonlinear maps, but it is not guaranteed that such fixed points exist or are unique, raising stability and reproducibility concerns. The dominant prior approach, MonDEQ, ensures uniqueness by imposing operator-monotone parametrizations on weight matrices, which is a restrictive constraint that limits architectural flexibility. This paper asks whether weaker, more natural conditions on the activation functions alone can certify well-posedness without any restriction on the learnable weights.

## Input / Output

For image classification, the model consumes grayscale (MNIST, 28×28) or RGB (CIFAR-10/SVHN 32×32, TinyImageNet 64×64) images, injects them through a one-layer MLP with positive final activation, and iteratively solves the fixed-point equation to produce a hidden embedding that is fed to a softmax classifier. For graph node classification, the input is a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×f} together with the graph adjacency structure, and the output is a per-node class label. In both settings the fixed point is computed by iteration with Anderson acceleration, stopping at a relative residual of 10⁻³, and the implicit gradient is propagated via the implicit function theorem rather than storing intermediate layers.

## Method

The core contribution is a new existence-and-uniqueness theorem for DEQ fixed points based on subhomogeneous operators: if the DEQ map F is positive and μ-subhomogeneous (|Mz| ≤ μF(z) for all M in the Clarke Jacobian) with μ < 1, then F is a contraction in the Thomson projective metric and admits a unique fixed point with linear convergence rate μ^k. This translates to a practical design rule: the DEQ layer z = σ(Wz) + f_θ(x) is well-posed for any weight matrix W provided the activation σ is subhomogeneous, which holds for tanh(z)+α (μ<1 for α>1.2, μ<1/2 for α>1.603), sigmoid, SoftPlus, ReLU, and others. A 'power scaling trick' (raising the activation to a power 1−ε) further reduces the subhomogeneity degree when needed, and a normalization layer norm_φ is handled via an extended theorem requiring μ<1/2 (or μ<1 with entrywise-positive Jacobian). The paper instantiates these results as SubDEQ feedforward/convolutional classifiers and a nonlinear GNN variant of APPNP that replaces linear PageRank propagation with a subhomogeneous tanh-based diffusion, all trained end-to-end with Anderson-accelerated fixed-point solving and implicit backpropagation.

## Results

On MNIST (dense), SubDEQ(Tanh) achieves 1.92% misclassification error, outperforming MonDEQ(ReLU) (2.06%), nODE(ReLU) (2.36%), and Standard MLP (2.05%). On SVHN, SubDEQ(Normalized Tanh) achieves 9.36% error, substantially beating MonDEQ(ReLU) (11.04%), nODE(ReLU) (33.83%), and Standard CNN (12.72%). On CIFAR-10, SubDEQ(Tanh) reaches 27.95% error, surpassing nODE baselines (28.79–33.58%) but trailing MonDEQ(ReLU) (24.41%) and Standard CNN (27.16%). On TinyImageNet, SubDEQ(Normalized Tanh) achieves 70.18% error, beating MonDEQ(ReLU) (85.49%) and Standard CNN (73.76%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MonDEQ (Winston & Kolter, 2020), SubDEQ wins on MNIST dense (1.92% vs 2.06%), SVHN (9.36% vs 11.04%), and TinyImageNet (70.18% vs 85.49%), but loses on MNIST convolutional (0.71% vs 0.65%) and CIFAR-10 (27.95% vs 24.41%); additionally, SubDEQ requires fewer fixed-point iterations to converge than MonDEQ's Peaceman-Rachford method (Figure 1). Against nODE (Chen et al., 2018), SubDEQ dominates across all image datasets, with the largest margin on SVHN (9.36% vs 33.83% for nODE-ReLU) and CIFAR-10 (27.95% vs 33.58%). Against APPNP (Gasteiger et al., 2018) on graph node classification, the nonlinear SubDEQ GNN variant wins on Cora author (73.34% vs 70.83%), DBLP (89.40% vs 88.81%), and PubMed (78.58% vs 77.45%), but trails APPNP on Cora citation (73.39% vs 80.20%) and CiteSeer (62.66% vs 62.86%). Overall, SubDEQ's key advantage is that it requires no constraints on the weight matrix W (unlike MonDEQ's operator-monotone parametrization), while matching or exceeding baseline accuracy on most benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the monotone operator framework (MonDEQ, Winston & Kolter 2020) that has been the dominant approach to guaranteeing well-posedness of deep equilibrium models. Rather than constraining the weight matrix to a specific parametrization (W = AᵀA + B − Bᵀ), the authors introduce subhomogeneous operators and leverage the Thomson projective metric from nonlinear Perron-Frobenius theory (Lemmens & Nussbaum 2012) to prove fixed-point uniqueness under conditions on the activation function alone. The DEQ architecture itself (Bai et al. 2019) and the APPNP graph propagation scheme (Gasteiger et al. 2018) serve as the base models that are modified with subhomogeneous activations to obtain the SubDEQ variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that practitioners can use unconstrained weight matrices in DEQ models, removing a significant design restriction that MonDEQ imposes, at the cost of requiring subhomogeneous activations (often achievable with a simple constant shift or power-scaling perturbation). However, the empirical results are mixed: SubDEQ does not consistently outperform MonDEQ across datasets (e.g., MonDEQ(ReLU) achieves 24.4% on CIFAR-10 vs. 27.9% for SubDEQ(Tanh)), and the subhomogeneity degree μ < 1 (or μ < 1/2 with normalization) still imposes a non-trivial constraint that may not hold for all useful activations without modification. Open questions include whether the framework extends to multi-scale or hierarchical DEQ architectures, whether the power-scaling trick degrades expressivity in practice, and whether the convergence-rate advantage observed in Figure 1 persists at larger scales.
