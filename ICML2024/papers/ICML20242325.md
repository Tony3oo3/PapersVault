## Problem

Existing model merging methods for multi-task learning operate in weight space but produce merged models whose feature representations diverge significantly from those of the individual models — a phenomenon the paper identifies as 'representation bias.' This bias persists across tasks, architectures, and merging methods, and is shown to be a primary cause of the large performance gap between merged models and individually trained models or traditional MTL.

## Input / Output

The surgery module takes as input the k-dimensional feature representation (e.g., 512-d for ViT-B/32, 768-d for ViT-L/14) extracted by the merged model just before the task-specific head, and outputs a bias-correction vector of the same dimensionality that is subtracted from the input. Training requires only unlabeled test samples and access to the individual models (for computing target representations); no labeled training data is needed. The final system still produces per-task class labels, with the surgery module adding negligible overhead (~0.01% extra parameters).

## Method

Surgery is a task-specific, Adapter-like module (two learnable matrices W_down ∈ R^{r×k} and W_up ∈ R^{k×r} with a ReLU nonlinearity, default rank r=16) that estimates and removes the representation bias introduced by merging. It is trained with an unsupervised objective that minimizes the L1 distance between the corrected merged representation (Z_mtl − Φ_t(Z_mtl)) and the individual model's representation (Z_ind) on unlabeled test data, using the individual models as self-supervised targets. The approach is fully orthogonal to the merging method: it operates post-merging in representation space rather than weight space, so it can be plugged into any existing merging scheme (Weight Averaging, Task Arithmetic, Ties-Merging, AdaMerging, etc.) without modifying the merged weights. The paper demonstrates that this simple correction reduces representation bias by up to 56% and lifts average accuracy from 80.1% to 87.5% on AdaMerging (ViT-B/32), approaching the 88.9% of traditional MTL.

## Results

On ViT-B/32 (8 vision tasks), AdaMerging w/Surgery achieves 86.1% average accuracy (87.5% with rank=64), up from 80.1% for AdaMerging alone and approaching the 88.9% of Traditional MTL. On ViT-L/14, AdaMerging w/Surgery reaches 92.3% vs. 90.8% without surgery and 93.5% for Traditional MTL. In the NLP domain (5 BERT tasks), Weight Averaging w/Surgery jumps from 55.8% to 74.3% average accuracy, nearly matching Traditional MTL (75.0%). The surgery module adds only 0.014% extra parameters (131K vs. 907M for ViT-B/32).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Surgery module consistently improves every model merging baseline it is applied to. On ViT-B/32, Task Arithmetic w/Surgery (80.9%) outperforms Task Arithmetic (69.1%) by 11.8 points, and Ties-Merging w/Surgery (83.1%) beats Ties-Merging (72.9%) by 10.2 points. The largest relative gain is on Weight Averaging, where Surgery lifts accuracy from 65.8% to 80.0% (+14.2 points), surpassing even Fisher Merging (68.3%) and RegMean (71.8%). AdaMerging w/Surgery (86.1%) exceeds Concrete AM (84.0%) and approaches Traditional MTL (88.9%), closing most of the gap between model-merging-based MTL and data-based MTL. In the NLP domain, Weight Averaging w/Surgery (74.3%) nearly matches Traditional MTL (75.0%) and KD4MTL (75.5%), while Task Arithmetic w/Surgery (74.4%) surpasses both Weight Averaging (55.8%) and Task Arithmetic (64.3%) by large margins. The method is orthogonal to all baselines: it operates post-merging in representation space, so it can be stacked on top of any weight-space merging strategy without modification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as orthogonal to the existing model merging literature (Task Arithmetic, Ties-Merging, AdaMerging, Fisher Merging, RegMean), which operates in weight space to 'seek common' across tasks. Rather than improving the merging process itself, it introduces a post-merging, representation-space intervention: a task-specific adapter-like module (borrowing the low-rank bottleneck structure from Houlsby et al., 2019) trained with an unsupervised objective that uses individual models as self-supervised targets. Conceptually, it repurposes the adapter architecture for bias filtering rather than parameter-efficient fine-tuning, and it complements rather than competes with any existing merging scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear diagnostic (representation bias) for a previously under-explained performance gap between model-merging MTL and traditional MTL, and offers a lightweight (0.01% extra parameters), method-agnostic fix that closes most of that gap across ViT and BERT backbones. Limitations include the requirement to retain access to all individual models and unlabeled test data, the per-task surgery modules (scaling linearly with task count), and evaluation restricted to classification tasks with ViT/BERT architectures. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to generative or dense-prediction tasks, to merging models from different initializations or architectures, and whether the unsupervised signal remains effective when test data is highly domain-shifted.
