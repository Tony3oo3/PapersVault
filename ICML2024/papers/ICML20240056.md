## Problem

Determining whether two variables are adjacent in a causal graph requires searching over all subsets of remaining variables for a separating set — an exponential number of conditional independence tests that makes classical testing-based methods infeasible beyond ~100 variables. Gradient-based model search methods scale to larger systems but are unreliable and unstable in practice. The paper asks whether the adjacency test can be reformulated as a single differentiable optimization problem that is both scalable and provably equivalent to the original discrete search.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular data of N real-valued variables with K i.i.d. observations, optionally augmented with intervention data where the intervened targets are known (modeled as binary indicator variables added to the graph). It outputs a directed acyclic graph (skeleton plus oriented edges) along with the separating sets for non-adjacent pairs, which are used to orient edges via v-structure detection and Meek's rules. The method scales to N=1000 variables with 10,000 observations in hours on a single GPU, and the moral graph learning step can extend to N=10,000.

## Method

DAT replaces the discrete search over 2^M subsets with a continuous optimization over soft inclusion parameters ψ ∈ [0,1]^M, where each variable Z_m is mixed with independent thick-tailed noise: Z̃_{ψ,m} = ψ_m Z_m + (1−ψ_m) N_m. The objective is the variance of X explained by Y given Z̃_ψ, approximated by two neural networks (one predicting E[X|Z̃_ψ], another predicting the residual given Y and Z̃_ψ), optimized by alternating gradient steps. A key theorem proves that with thick-tailed noise distributions, the relaxed problem has the same answer as the discrete separating set selection problem, and the separating set is recovered as {Z_m : ψ_m = 1}. DAT-Graph first learns the moral graph via L1-regularized neural network variable selection, then performs two DAT tests per candidate edge (searching within each variable's Markov blanket) to improve robustness to near-violations of faithfulness, before orienting edges using the learned separating sets.

## Results

On synthetic Erdős-Renyi graphs with N=30 and s=3 parents, DAT-Graph achieves a skeleton SHD of 8±1, substantially outperforming SDCD (25±4), PC (44±2), GES (99±85), CAM (79±33), and NoGAM (79±24). At scale (N up to 1000, s=4), DAT-Graph matches SDCD on skeleton accuracy and surpasses it for large N; the hybrid method (DAT-Graph skeleton + SDCD orientation) achieves the best directed-graph SHD. On sparse graphs (s=2, N=200), DAT-Graph reduces skeleton SHD from 112 (SDCD) to 45, and the hybrid method further reduces it to 29. On real single-cell RNA sequencing data (N=1000 genes), the hybrid method achieves learned mutual information of 26.7/28.3/20.6 across three cell populations, compared to 5.2/12.7/4.3 for SDCD and substantially lower for DCDFG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SDCD (the state-of-the-art gradient-based method), DAT-Graph matches skeleton accuracy on dense large graphs but substantially outperforms it on sparse graphs (SHD 45 vs. 112 at s=2) and scales to N=1000 in ~4.5 hours. The hybrid method combining DAT-Graph's skeleton with SDCD's edge orientation beats both individually on directed-graph SHD at all scales. Against classical conditional independence tests (RCoT, GCIT, CCIT, CMIT), DAT achieves comparable AUC for adjacency detection but runs in minutes where RCoT would take weeks to years for large graphs due to exponential scaling. Against small-scale baselines (PC, GES, CAM, NoGAM), DAT-Graph achieves 3–12× lower skeleton SHD, with GES and CAM performing worse than trivial due to violated Gaussianity and additivity assumptions. On real RNA sequencing data, the hybrid method outperforms DCDFG (a method specifically designed for large-scale single-cell causal discovery) by a wide margin in intervention prediction accuracy (e.g., 26.7 vs. ~5 mutual information on the control dataset).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two historically separate paradigms in causal discovery: classical testing-based methods (PC, GES, CAM) that search for conditional independence relations, and modern gradient-based model search methods (NOTEARS, SDCD) that optimize over the space of all DAGs. The core novelty is the Differentiable Adjacency Test (DAT), which replaces the exponential discrete search over separating sets with a single differentiable optimization problem, drawing a conceptual connection to Invariant Risk Minimization (IRM) but extending it to continuous variables with a provable equivalence guarantee under thick-tailed noise. The moral graph learning step adapts the L1-regularized variable selection from SDCD (Nazaret et al., 2023), while the variance-explained objective follows Zhang & Janson (2020). The key departure from prior work is that DAT makes the separating set selection problem tractable at scale, enabling a testing-based approach to compete with gradient-based search on large systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAT-Graph addresses a fundamental scalability bottleneck in causal discovery: classical testing-based methods require an exponential number of conditional independence tests per variable pair, making them infeasible beyond ~100 variables, while gradient-based search is unstable and unreliable even in simple settings. By scaling to 1000 variables with linear compute growth and demonstrating 5× improvement in intervention prediction on real RNA sequencing data, the paper shows that testing-based and model-search approaches are complementary rather than competing. Key limitations include reduced accuracy on dense graphs, the assumption of no latent confounders or cycles, and multiple threshold hyperparameters. Open questions include extending DAT to cyclic or confounded settings, reducing hyperparameter sensitivity, and applying it to local subgraph learning for targeted causal queries.
