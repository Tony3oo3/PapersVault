## Problem

Standard collaborative PAC learning assumes a single classifier in the hypothesis class achieves zero error on all n data distributions, which is unrealistic when learning across diverse subgroups with different labeling functions. This paper asks: under what weaker realizability conditions can sample-efficient collaborative learning still be achieved when the n distributions are labeled by different functions, and what is the optimal sample complexity?

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives adaptive sample access to n data distributions over X×{0,1} and a hypothesis class F⊆{0,1}^X, and must output n binary classifiers f̂₁,…,f̂ₙ, one per distribution, minimizing the total number of labeled examples drawn. The sampling is adaptive: at each step the algorithm chooses which distribution to query, and the total sample budget is shared across all n tasks rather than allocated per-task. The algorithm is (ε,δ)-PAC, guaranteeing each f̂ᵢ has error ≤ε on Dᵢ with probability ≥1−δ.

## Method

The key insight is to reduce the multi-labeling-function problem to a single-labeling-function problem via a (G,k)-augmentation of the hypothesis class: F_{n,k} consists of functions g_{f,c} over X'=G×X that map (i,x) to f_{c_i}(x), where f=(f₁,…,f_k)∈F^k and c∈[k]^G assigns each distribution to one of k classifiers. Under the (k,ε)-realizability assumption (k classifiers in F suffice to ε-fit all n distributions), this augmentation yields a single classifier with ε-error on all augmented distributions, enabling ERM-based learning. The critical technical contribution is a tight VC dimension bound of O(kd + n log k) for F_{n,k}, improving over Crammer & Mansour's O(kd log(nkd) + n log k), which yields a sample complexity of O(kd log(n/k) + n log n). The paper further proves ERM on the augmented class is NP-hard for k≥3 (via reduction from graph coloring) and NP-hard for k=2 (via subset sum), then gives computationally efficient algorithms for two special cases: identical marginals (lifelong-learning-style clustering) and 2-refutable hypothesis classes (reducing ERM to approximate graph coloring).

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is a sample complexity upper bound of O(kd log(n/k) + n log n) for (k,ε)-realizable distributions, where d is the VC dimension of the hypothesis class. A matching lower bound of Ω(k)·m(⌊n/k⌋, d, ε, O(δ/k)) is established, showing the leading kd log(n/k) term is necessary under standard assumptions. The paper further proves that ERM over the augmented hypothesis class is NP-hard for k ≥ 3 (and for a specific class when k = 2), while providing computationally efficient algorithms for two special cases: identical marginals (achieving O(kd log(1/ε)/ε + n log(n/δ)/ε) samples) and 2-refutable hypothesis classes (achieving O((d log(1/ε) + n)/ε · log n + n log(1/δ)/ε) samples for k = 2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. In terms of theoretical improvements over prior work, the paper generalizes the collaborative PAC learning result of Blum et al. (2017), which achieves O((d+n) log n) sample complexity under the (1,0)-realizability assumption (a single classifier works for all distributions), to the more general (k,ε)-realizability setting with O(kd log(n/k) + n log n) samples. The VC dimension bound for the augmented class F_{n,k} improves upon Crammer & Mansour (2012), who gave O(kd log(nkd) + n log k), by removing a logarithmic factor to achieve O(kd + n log k). For the 2-refutable special case with k = 2, the proposed algorithm matches the information-theoretic sample complexity of the general ERM-based algorithm while being computationally efficient, whereas the general algorithm is NP-hard. The paper does not compare against multi-task learning or federated learning baselines empirically, but notes that its adaptive sampling model differs from the fixed-sample settings in those frameworks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Collaborative PAC Learning framework of Blum et al. (2017), which assumed a single labeling function simultaneously accurate on all n distributions, by relaxing this to a (k,ε)-realizability assumption borrowed from Crammer & Mansour (2012) in multi-task learning. The key technical bridge is a natural augmentation of the hypothesis class (mapping to a product space G×X), reducing the multi-labeling problem to a single-labeling collaborative learning instance, with the VC dimension of the augmented class bounded by O(kd + n log k). The paper also draws on approximate graph coloring theory (Karger et al., 1998; Kawarabayashi & Thorup, 2017) to design computationally efficient learners for 2-refutable hypothesis classes, and positions itself relative to multi-distribution learning, federated learning, and multi-source domain adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The NP-hardness of ERM on the augmented class (for k≥3, and even k=2 for a specific class) is a significant negative result: it shows that the information-theoretically optimal sample complexity is not achievable by the natural algorithmic approach, creating a gap between statistical and computational complexity in collaborative learning. The 2-refutability + conflict graph + approximate coloring pipeline is an elegant reduction that opens a path to efficient learners in structured settings. Key open problems include proving a matching Ω(d log n) lower bound for the (1,0)-realizable case, identifying natural distributional assumptions that yield bounded-degree conflict graphs, and closing the gap between the poly(n) overhead of the coloring-based algorithm and the polylog(n) information-theoretic bound for concrete hypothesis classes.
