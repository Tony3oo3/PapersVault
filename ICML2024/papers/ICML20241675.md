## Problem

The exact Rational Speech Acts (RSA) algorithm produces pragmatic program synthesizers that resolve ambiguity by modeling users as informative speakers, but its O(|W|²|U|ℓ) runtime makes real-time interaction infeasible for large program spaces. Prior scaling approaches rely on sampling and re-ranking that sacrifice accuracy. This paper asks whether the expensive example-dependent RSA ranking can be amortized into a single example-agnostic global ranking that is both fast and communicatively accurate.

## Input / Output

At inference time, the method takes a sequence of user-provided examples (strings in the regex domain, grid patterns in the animals domain) and a set of k consistent programs produced by a fast non-pragmatic synthesizer. The examples serve only to filter which programs are logically consistent; the precomputed global ranking σ then sorts those k programs, yielding a ranked list whose top-1 entry is the inferred program. The global ranking is computed entirely offline from the program space and is independent of any particular example sequence.

## Method

The method has two offline stages. First, a simulated communication dataset is generated by enumerating all programs, using the exact RSA speaker S₁ to sample the most informative example sequences for each program, and querying the exact RSA listener L₁ for partial rankings of consistent programs at each turn. Second, a single global ranking is distilled from these example-dependent rankings via either (a) an annealing procedure that iteratively swaps pairs in a global ordering to match sampled rankings until convergence, or (b) a small neural score function trained with a Bradley-Terry pairwise sigmoid loss over the dataset. At inference time, the fast synthesizer finds consistent programs and the global ranking sorts them in O(k log k) time. A key theoretical result proves that for single-example RSA (RSA_single), the column-normalization vector c_{1…i-1} constitutes an exact global ranking, making the approximation lossless in that special case.

## Results

In the live human study on the scaled-up regex domain (3500 programs), the annealing-based ranking synthesizer L_anneal achieves 93.75% communication success versus only 65.63% for the literal L_0, and also succeeds with fewer examples. In replay studies, ranking-based synthesizers retain most of L_1's communicative accuracy: on the animals domain H_0 replay, L_anneal reaches 71.65% vs L_1's 74.80%; on the regex domain H_0 replay, L_anneal reaches 81.67% vs L_1's 88.33%. Critically, both ranking-based methods run over 100× faster than exact RSA (L_1), requiring approximately the same wall-clock time as the literal L_0 synthesizer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its ranking-based synthesizers (L_anneal and L_neural) against the exact RSA synthesizer L_1 from Pu et al. (2020) on the animals domain and from Vaithilingam et al. (2023) on the regex domain, as well as the literal L_0 baseline. Against L_1, the ranking methods are close but not identical: on the animals H_0 replay, L_neural (78.34%) actually exceeds L_1 (74.80%), while on the regex H_0 replay, L_anneal (81.67%) trails L_1 (88.33%) by ~7 points. The gap widens on H_1 replays (where humans adapted to L_1's behavior), with L_1 retaining a 4–13 point advantage. However, the decisive advantage is speed: ranking-based synthesizers run over 100× faster than L_1, matching L_0's wall-clock time, making real-time interaction feasible in domains (e.g., 3500 regexes) where exact RSA is intractable. Against L_0, both ranking methods dominate by large margins (e.g., 81.67% vs 28.33% on regex H_0 replay), confirming that the distilled pragmatic ranking captures most of RSA's communicative benefit at negligible inference cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper amortizes the exact RSA-based pragmatic synthesizer (Pu et al., 2020; Vaithilingam et al., 2023) by distilling its example-dependent program rankings into a single example-agnostic global ranking, using either simulated-annealing or a Bradley-Terry neural score function. Prior scaling approaches to RSA (Monroe et al., 2017; Andreas & Klein, 2016) relied on sampling and re-ranking to restrict computation to subsets of programs and examples, whereas this work eliminates the example-dependent ranking entirely at inference time. The theoretical contribution generalizes Muggleton FREng (2023), who showed a global ranking suffices for single-example MAP estimation, to arbitrary-depth RSA chains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is enabling real-time pragmatic synthesis in domains too large for exact RSA (e.g., 3500 regexes), with orders-of-magnitude speedup while retaining most communicative accuracy. The exactness proof for single-example RSA is a clean structural result showing the example-dependent ranking collapses to a global one, suggesting humans may approximate pragmatics via a fixed prior rather than full recursive reasoning. Key open questions remain: whether an effective global ranking provably exists for the multi-example setting (cycles in example-dependent rankings may prevent it), and whether the distillation procedure can reliably find it at scale.
