## Problem

Permutation equivariant neural networks for graph data use the full symmetric group S_n as the symmetry group, ignoring the structural relationships between vertices that make the true symmetry group the (generally smaller) automorphism group Aut(G). While the combinatorics of set partitions had characterised S_n-equivariant layer functions, no analogous characterisation existed for Aut(G)-equivariant linear maps between tensor power spaces of R^n, leaving a gap in constructing neural networks that respect the actual symmetries of a given graph.

## Input / Output

The method takes a specific labelled graph G with n vertices (whose adjacency structure determines the embedding of Aut(G) in S_n) and a pair of tensor power orders (k, l) specifying the layer spaces (R^n)^{⊗k} and (R^n)^{⊗l}. It produces a spanning set of n^l × n^k weight matrices, each being a G-homomorphism matrix whose (I,J)-entry counts graph homomorphisms from a bilabelled graph's underlying graph to G with prescribed vertex mappings. Crucially, the spanning set depends on the specific vertex labelling and edge structure of G, not merely the abstract isomorphism class of Aut(G), so different embeddings of the same group in S_n yield different (though isomorphic) spanning sets.

## Method

The paper establishes a full, strict R-linear monoidal functor F^G from the category of bilabelled graphs to the category of G-homomorphism matrices, showing that the vector space Hom_{Aut(G)}((R^n)^{⊗k}, (R^n)^{⊗l}) is spanned by G-homomorphism matrices X_H^G indexed by isomorphism classes of (k,l)-bilabelled graphs (Theorem 4.27). The proof combines Chassaniol's generating set {M^{0,1}, M^{2,1}, A_G, S} for the equivariant category with Mančinska & Roberson's result that the category of bilabelled graphs is generated by the corresponding graph-theoretic objects under composition, tensor product, and involution. A concrete procedure generates the relevant (q,0)-bilabelled graph diagrams (where q = k+l) by starting from set partitions of [q], adding internal red edges bounded by 2m (twice the longest path in G) and external red edges bounded by m, then applying Frobenius duality to convert to (k,l)-form. This recovers the diagram basis for S_n-equivariant layers (via the edgeless graph K̄_n) and, by Frucht's theorem, extends the characterisation to any finite group by finding a graph whose automorphism group is isomorphic to that group.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental benchmarks. The main result (Theorem 4.27) provides a complete spanning set for the space of Aut(G)-equivariant linear maps between tensor power spaces of R^n, expressed as G-homomorphism matrices derived from isomorphism classes of (k,l)-bilabelled graphs. Corollary 4.28 gives the explicit form of any equivariant weight matrix as a linear combination of these spanning set elements. The paper demonstrates the framework by recovering the diagram basis for S_n-equivariant layers (matching Godfrey et al., 2023) and by computing explicit bases for Hom_{D_4}(R^4, R^4) under three different embeddings of D_4 in S_4, as well as spanning sets for Hom_{S_2}(R^3, R^3) and Hom_{S_2}((R^3)^{⊗2}, R^3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not perform experimental comparisons against other equivariant network architectures (e.g., GCNs, GATs, or permutation-equivariant networks). Instead, its theoretical comparisons are: (1) it recovers the diagram basis of Godfrey et al. (2023) for S_n-equivariant linear layers by applying its framework to the edgeless graph K̄_n, confirming consistency with prior work; (2) it provides explicit basis characterizations for D_4 (as Aut(2K_2) or Aut(C_4)) that the authors state are 'missing from the literature'; and (3) it subsumes Chassaniol's (2019) generating set {M^{0,1}, M^{2,1}, A_G, S} by showing that the bilabelled graph category G is generated by the same four elements under composition, tensor product, and involution (Proposition 4.26). The framework also generalizes Pearce-Crump's (2022; 2023a;b) set-partition-based characterizations by replacing set partitions with the richer combinatorics of bilabelled graphs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the bilabelled graph framework from Mančinska & Roberson (2020), originally developed to study quantum isomorphism, to characterize equivariant neural network layers for the automorphism group of a graph. It builds directly on Chassaniol's (2019) generating set for the category of Aut(G)-equivariant maps and extends the set-partition-based characterizations of Pearce-Crump (2022; 2023a;b) from the symmetric group S_n to the more restrictive Aut(G). The key conceptual departure is replacing the symmetric group (which ignores graph structure) with the actual automorphism group, yielding a stronger equivariance condition that respects the specific vertex relations in G.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first complete theoretical characterization of Aut(G)-equivariant linear layers, filling a gap between the well-studied S_n-equivariant case and the more structured Aut(G) setting. Combined with Frucht's theorem, it implies that equivariant layers for any finite group can in principle be constructed, which is a broad theoretical guarantee. However, the practical utility is limited by the exponential growth in the number of bilabelled graph isomorphism classes as n, k, and l increase, and the paper acknowledges that no implementation or empirical validation is provided. Open questions include finding ways to reduce the spanning set size and developing efficient algorithms for computing G-homomorphism matrices at scale.
