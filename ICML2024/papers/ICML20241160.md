## Problem

Existing graph OOD generalization methods rely heavily on task-dependent labels, which are often scarce or unavailable in practice (e.g., drug discovery). The self-supervised graph OOD generalization problem—learning GNNs that generalize under distribution shifts without labels—remains largely unexplored, with the critical challenge that invariant and variant information are highly entangled in holistic graph representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241160/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pretraining, the method consumes unlabeled graphs and their augmented views (via standard graph augmentation strategies) to learn disentangled representations without any labels. Each input graph is mapped to K factorized sub-representations (one per latent factor channel), and during downstream finetuning a linear predictor over these concatenated representations produces the graph class label. The number of disentangled channels K, representation dimensionality d, and invariance coefficient λ are tunable hyperparameters.

## Method

OOD-GCL comprises a multi-channel disentangled graph encoder (K independent message-passing GNN channels, each with its own readout and MLP, producing factor-specific graph representations) and a tailored disentangled invariant self-supervised learning module. For each channel k, the method infers an environment partition by k-means clustering (with adaptive cluster count via Silhouette Score) on that channel's representations, then applies a contrastive loss on the representations from all *other* channels (Z_n^{-k}) within each inferred environment, combined with V-REx invariance regularization. This forces the non-k channels to be invariant to the environment structure defined by the k-th factor, thereby excluding the k-th factor's information from them and promoting disentanglement. The iterative procedure alternates between updating the environment partitions and optimizing the encoder, and theoretical analysis proves both that the learned representations are disentangled (satisfying equivariant and decomposable properties) and that a linear predictor over them achieves OOD generalization by automatically zeroing out coefficients for non-invariant factors.

## Results

OOD-GCL achieves the best performance across all 7 evaluation settings (4 datasets × 2 shift types each). On Motif (base/size), it reaches 76.31% / 60.12% accuracy, outperforming the best baseline AIA (73.64% / 55.85%). On CMNIST (color shift), it achieves 41.89% accuracy vs. AIA's 36.37%. On molecular datasets, it attains 72.86% / 83.12% ROC-AUC on Molbbbp (scaffold/size) and 74.13% / 63.41% on Molhiv (scaffold/size), consistently surpassing all supervised and self-supervised baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OOD-GCL outperforms all 20 baselines across every dataset and shift setting. The strongest competitor is AIA (Sui et al., 2023), a supervised graph OOD method, which OOD-GCL surpasses by 2.7–5.5 points (e.g., 76.31 vs. 73.64 on Motif-base, 41.89 vs. 36.37 on CMNIST-color, 74.13 vs. 71.15 on Molhiv-scaffold). Among self-supervised baselines, InfoGraph and GraphCL are the closest, but OOD-GCL still leads by 2.6–6.5 points (e.g., 72.86 vs. 70.39 on Molbbbp-scaffold, 83.12 vs. 80.82 on Molbbbp-size). General invariant learning methods (ERM, IRM, V-REx, GroupDRO) consistently underperform, with V-REx being the best at 71.47 on Motif-base, still 4.8 points below OOD-GCL. Notably, OOD-GCL is the only method that does not require task-dependent labels during pretraining, yet it surpasses all supervised graph OOD methods (DIR, CAL, GSAT, OOD-GNN, StableGNN, CIGA, DisC, GREA, FLAG, M-Mixup, G-Mixup, AIA) by a clear margin, demonstrating the effectiveness of disentangled self-supervised learning for OOD generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: disentangled graph contrastive learning (Li et al., 2021a), which provides the multi-channel encoder architecture, and invariant learning for OOD generalization (IRM, V-REx, GroupDRO), which provides the invariance regularization. The key departure is that prior graph OOD generalization methods (DIR, CAL, GSAT, OOD-GNN, AIA, etc.) all require supervised labels, while prior graph self-supervised methods (InfoGraph, GraphCL, GMI, CNC) ignore distribution shifts entirely. OOD-GCL is the first to combine self-supervised pretraining with explicit disentanglement and invariance regularization for graph OOD generalization, extending the theoretical disentanglement framework of Wang et al. (2021a) to a multi-factor, environment-aware setting with a proof that the learned representations are provably disentangled.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical gap where graph labels are scarce or unavailable (e.g., drug discovery) but OOD generalization is still needed, showing that self-supervised pretraining with disentanglement can substitute for supervised invariant learning. The theoretical guarantee that a simple linear predictor suffices for OOD generalization given disentangled representations is a clean and useful result. Limitations include reliance on k-means clustering for environment inference (an approximation that may not capture true environment structure), the need to pre-specify the number of latent factors K, and evaluation on only four relatively small datasets. Open questions include whether the approach scales to large-scale graphs, whether it extends to node-level or link-level OOD generalization, and whether the clustering-based environment partition can be replaced by learned environment inference.
