## Problem

Existing partial identification (PI) methods that leverage marginal confounder information P(U) either restrict to extreme low-entropy cases without tightness guarantees or rely on surrogates like entropy rather than the full distribution. The paper asks: what is the closed-form tight PI region for any arbitrary P(U), and under what precise conditions does the marginal confounder information actually yield a non-vanilla (tighter-than-baseline) identification region?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242415/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the observed joint distribution P(X,Y) of binary treatment and outcome variables together with the marginal distribution P(U) of a discrete confounder with d_u levels (no additional restrictions on P(U) are imposed). It outputs closed-form piecewise-linear tight bounds for the interventional probability P(y|do(x)) and the average treatment effect, along with if-and-only-if criteria characterizing when these bounds degenerate to the vanilla Tian-Pearl bound. For multi-valued confounders (d_u ≥ 3), computing the exact tight bound reduces to the Subset-Sum Problem, so the bounds are expressed in terms of extremal subset-sum values that can be approximately extracted.

## Method

For binary confounders, the paper derives tight bounds by solving a non-convex fractional optimization over joint distributions P(X,Y,U) compatible with the observed marginals, exploiting the piecewise-monotone structure of the resulting min-max objective to obtain closed-form piecewise-linear functions of P(U=t). For multi-valued confounders, the key insight is that the tight bound equals the vanilla bound if and only if there exists a subset U of the confounder space whose probability mass falls within a specific interval determined by P(X,Y); this yields an if-and-only-if non-vanilla criterion that subsumes prior entropy-based sufficiency conditions. A new distance measure D(P(U), I) quantifies the identification improvement, with the gap between tight and vanilla bounds bounded between linear and quadratic rates in this distance. A fundamental negative result shows that under uniform sampling on the probability simplex, the probability of obtaining a non-vanilla PI region decreases exponentially with d_u, falling below 0.1 for d_u ≥ 10 regardless of P(X,Y).

## Results

The paper's primary results are theoretical: it establishes closed-form tight PI bounds for P(y|do(x)) and ATE given P(U), and proves that the probability of P(U) yielding a non-vanilla identification region decreases monotonically (and at most exponentially) with confounder cardinality d_u, falling consistently below 0.1 for d_u ≥ 10 regardless of P(X,Y). On the INSURANCE and ADULT datasets, the valid ATE bounds from Proposition 4.7 provide tighter intervals than the separate upper/lower bound estimation of Jiang et al. (2023), enabling more reliable decision-making. The paper also demonstrates that Li et al.'s (2023) entropy-based valid bounds are strictly looser than the proposed tight bounds for all ε ∈ (0, P(x)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Li et al. (2023), the paper proves its tight PI region is strictly tighter than Li's valid bounds for all ε ∈ (0, P(x)) when P(x) ≤ P(¬x), and when P(x) > P(¬x), Li's bound in the regime ε ∈ (P(¬x), P(x)) is actually looser than the vanilla bound (i.e., provides no improvement over having no P(U) information at all). Against Jiang et al. (2023), the paper's if-and-only-if criteria (Theorem 4.1) subsume Jiang's entropy-threshold sufficiency condition as a special case, meaning the paper identifies strictly more non-vanilla P(U) configurations. On the INSURANCE and ADULT real-world datasets, the ATE bounds derived from Proposition 4.7 (which are valid but not guaranteed tight) still produce narrower and more decision-relevant intervals than Jiang et al.'s approach of separately bounding P(Y=1|do(x')) for x'=0,1 and taking the difference. The paper does not report a single unified numerical metric (e.g., average bound width) across datasets in the main text; detailed experimental comparisons are deferred to Appendix N.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the partial identification (PI) framework of Tian & Pearl (2000) by incorporating the marginal confounder distribution P(U) as the sole additional information, without auxiliary variables or hyperparameters. It directly builds on and tightens the entropy-based valid bounds of Li et al. (2023) and Jiang et al. (2023), which restricted attention to small-entropy confounders and provided only valid (not tight) regions. The key departure is removing the entropy surrogate entirely: the paper derives closed-form tight bounds for *any* P(U) and establishes if-and-only-if criteria for when the bound is non-vanilla, revealing an intrinsic connection to the Subset-Sum Problem in theoretical computer science.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central negative result—that the probability of P(U) yielding a non-vanilla PI bound drops below 0.1 once confounder cardinality exceeds 10—provides practitioners with a clear, theoretically grounded criterion for whether investing in collecting P(U) is worthwhile. This challenges the implicit assumption in prior entropy-based work that marginal confounder information is broadly useful. Open questions include extending the results to multi-valued treatments/outcomes, continuous or high-dimensional confounders, and more complex causal graphs beyond the single-confounder diamond structure, as well as combining the tight PI framework with auxiliary-variable-based methods.
