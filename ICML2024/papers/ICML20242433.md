## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models suffer from concept bleeding, where cross-attention maps for distinct entities spill into each other's regions and the background, causing visual features to merge (entity leakage) or attributes to bind to wrong objects. Existing remedies either require training auxiliary detection networks, are designed for image editing rather than generation, or address only missing entities while ignoring the mutual overlap between entities.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multi-entity text prompt (e.g., 'a cat and a frog' or 'a blue bag and a yellow clock') and produces a single synthesized RGB image via Stable Diffusion V-1.4. It operates entirely at inference time by extracting 16×16 cross-attention maps from the U-Net's first upsampling block at selected denoising timesteps, requiring no additional training data or auxiliary networks. The entity set and their indices in the prompt must be specified as input to the algorithm.

## Method

ELA is a training-free inference-time procedure that localizes and anchors each entity's cross-attention map to a non-overlapping spatial region. Localization works by binarizing the difference between two entities' attention maps to separate them, then subtracting the binarized SoT (start-of-text) attention map to remove background, yielding a per-entity mask. Anchoring minimizes a combined loss over the latent z_t: a concentration term that maximizes the fraction of attention mass falling inside the mask, and an attenuation term that penalizes residual activation outside the mask, weighted by a hyperparameter λ. The latent is updated via a single gradient step (z_t ← z_t − ∇_{z_t} L) at a small set of early denoising timesteps where spatial layout is still malleable, avoiding disruption of later refinement stages. The same mechanism extends to attribute binding by including attribute tokens in the entity set and sharing their masks with the corresponding entity.

## Results

On the primary CLIP text-text similarity metric (BLIP caption faithfulness), ELA achieves 0.7964 (animal-animal), 0.8559 (animal-object), and 0.8135 (object-object), outperforming Attend-and-Excite (0.7638, 0.8312, 0.8000) and Stable Diffusion (0.7571, 0.7837, 0.7350) across all three entity-pair categories. On full-prompt CLIP text-image similarity, ELA scores 0.3307, the highest among all methods. In human evaluation, ELA achieves 34.02 on the ALL dataset (vs. 18.05 for Attend-and-Excite and 17.92 for Linguistic Binding), though it trails Linguistic Binding (31.76) on the attribute-binding-specific subset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELA outperforms all baselines on the three CLIP text-text similarity categories (animal-animal, animal-object, object-object), with the largest margin over Stable Diffusion (+3.93, +7.22, +7.85 points respectively) and a consistent ~3-point lead over Attend-and-Excite. StructureDiffusion performs nearly identically to Stable Diffusion across all metrics, confirming the paper's claim that it fails to address cross-attention leakage. On the Min. Entity CLIP text-image metric, Attend-and-Excite (0.2509) slightly edges out ELA (0.2429), reflecting its strength in preventing missing entities. In human evaluation (Table 3), ELA dominates the ALL category (34.02 vs. 18.05 for Attend-and-Excite and 17.92 for Linguistic Binding), but Linguistic Binding leads on the attribute-binding-specific subset (31.76 vs. 24.20), indicating ELA's primary advantage is entity leakage mitigation rather than attribute correspondence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the cross-attention analysis established by P2P (Hertz et al., 2022) and DPL (Wang et al., 2023a), which identified cross-attention map leakage as a mechanism for entity merging, but extends that insight from image editing to the generation setting. It departs from StructureDiffusion (Feng et al., 2023), which addresses attribute binding through linguistic structure without touching cross-attention maps, and from Attend-and-Excite (Chefer et al., 2023), which amplifies neglected entities but ignores spatial leakage. The key conceptual departure is formulating entity localization as a mask-derivation problem from attention map differences and then anchoring via a gradient step on the latent, making the approach entirely training-free unlike DPL or BoxNet-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of ELA makes it immediately deployable on any pre-trained Stable Diffusion checkpoint without fine-tuning, which is practically significant for practitioners who cannot afford retraining. However, the method's effectiveness degrades sharply beyond two entities (the authors acknowledge failure with 4+ entities), and the additional gradient steps during sampling introduce computational overhead that limits real-time use. Open questions include whether the localization-anchoring paradigm can be generalized to complex multi-entity scenes with spatial relationships, and whether the cross-attention leakage diagnosis holds for newer architectures (e.g., DiT-based models) that do not rely on U-Net cross-attention layers.
