## Problem

Speculative sampling accelerates LLM inference by drafting tokens with a lightweight model and verifying them in parallel, but existing draft strategies (Medusa's independent MLP heads, Lookahead's n-gram/Jacobi iteration) achieve only ~0.6 acceptance accuracy, limiting speedups. The paper identifies that autoregression at the second-to-top-layer feature level is inherently more regular than token-level prediction, yet the randomness introduced by sampling creates ambiguity in which feature to predict next, fundamentally capping draft accuracy and leaving a gap between what feature-level autoregression could achieve and what it actually does.

## Input / Output

The draft model receives two inputs per position: the target LLM's second-to-top-layer hidden state (a continuous vector of dimension hidden_dim) and the token sampled at the next time step (a discrete index), which are embedded and concatenated into a 2×hidden_dim vector. The output is a predicted next feature that is passed through the frozen LM head to yield a token distribution for drafting. The final generated text is verified via standard speculative sampling acceptance/rejection, guaranteeing the output distribution is provably identical to the original LLM's autoregressive output for both greedy and non-greedy settings.

## Method

EAGLE's core insight is twofold: feature-level autoregression is simpler than token-level autoregression due to greater sequence regularity, and the sampling uncertainty in feature prediction is resolved by feeding the draft model the token sequence advanced by one time step (the actual sampled outcomes), so it predicts f_{always} given f_I and t_{always} rather than guessing between branches. The draft model is a lightweight plug-in of one FC layer (reducing 2×hidden_dim to hidden_dim) plus one transformer decoder layer, trained with a combined Smooth L1 regression loss on features and a 0.1-weighted cross-entropy loss on token distributions, with uniform noise augmentation on features to mitigate error accumulation. During inference, tree attention generates a tree-structured draft in m forward passes (e.g., 10 tokens in 3 passes), and the target LLM verifies all drafted tokens in a single forward pass using recursive speculative sampling to guarantee exact distribution preservation. Training requires only ~70k ShareGPT dialogues and 1–2 days on 4×A100 GPUs for the 70B model, with fewer than 1B trainable parameters.

## Results

On MT-bench (greedy, T=0), EAGLE achieves speedup ratios of 2.79x–3.67x across Vicuna 7B/13B/33B and 2.78x–3.76x across LLaMA2-Chat 7B/13B/70B, with average acceptance lengths (τ) of 3.62–4.52 tokens per forward pass. At T=1, speedups range from 2.21x to 2.92x. Combined with gpt-fast (quantization + compilation), EAGLE accelerates LLaMA2-Chat 7B to 160.4 tokens/s on a single RTX 3090 (int4), a 6.5× improvement over vanilla HuggingFace inference (24.5 tokens/s). The draft model achieves an acceptance rate of ~0.75–0.85 (0-α), substantially higher than Medusa's ~0.6.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAGLE outperforms all three baselines across every model size and dataset tested. Against Medusa (Cai et al., 2023), which uses independent MLP heads to predict tokens from second-to-top-layer features, EAGLE achieves 1.5x–1.6x higher speedup ratios, driven by its ~0.8 acceptance rate versus Medusa's ~0.6. Against Lookahead (Fu et al., 2023), which relies on n-gram matching and Jacobi iteration, EAGLE is 1.7x–2.1x faster, as Lookahead's draft accuracy is even lower than Medusa's. Against vanilla speculative sampling (Leviathan et al., 2023) using a 7B draft model for 33B/70B targets, EAGLE achieves 2.97x–3.67x speedup versus only 1.12x and 1.88x respectively, because the 7B draft model's high overhead negates most of the parallel verification benefit. EAGLE also uniquely guarantees lossless output distribution preservation in both greedy and non-greedy settings, whereas Lookahead is confined to greedy decoding and Medusa does not guarantee distribution preservation in non-greedy mode.

## Relation to Prior Work

EAGLE builds directly on the speculative sampling verification framework of Leviathan et al. (2023) and Chen et al. (2023a), adopting their acceptance/rejection algorithm to guarantee distribution preservation. It extends Medusa's (Cai et al., 2023) use of second-to-top-layer features by replacing independent MLP heads with a single autoregressive decoder layer, and borrows tree-structured draft generation from SpecInfer (Miao et al., 2023). The key conceptual departure is identifying that feature-level autoregression is inherently simpler than token-level autoregression, and that the sampling uncertainty in feature prediction can be resolved by simply feeding the token sequence advanced by one time step into the draft model — a minimal architectural change that yields substantially higher draft accuracy (~0.8 vs. ~0.6 for Medusa).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EAGLE provides a practically deployable acceleration method (2.7x–3.5x for LLaMA2-Chat 70B) with theoretically guaranteed distribution preservation in both greedy and non-greedy settings, at a training cost of 1–2 days on 4×A100 — making it far more accessible than training a dedicated draft model. Its compatibility with orthogonal techniques like quantization and compilation (e.g., 160.4 tokens/s with gpt-fast on a single RTX 3090) positions it as a composable component in production LLM serving stacks. Open questions include the context-dependence of optimal tree structure, the limited speedup on MoE models (1.5x for Mixtral 8x7B), and the diminishing returns at larger batch sizes, all of which constrain its applicability in high-throughput serving scenarios.
