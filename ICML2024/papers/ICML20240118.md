## Problem

Masked Image Modeling (MIM) methods like MAE and I-JEPA predict masked tokens at exact deterministic positions, causing the model to overfit to specific location features rather than learning robust semantic representations. In practice, semantic content (e.g., a dog's tail) can plausibly appear in multiple locations, and forcing precise positional prediction is both unrealistic and suboptimal for downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20240118/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images, patchifies them into non-overlapping tokens (e.g., 16×16 patches for ViT-B/16), and masks a subset of patches. The predictor reconstructs the masked patches in feature space (I-JEPA's latent representation) rather than pixel space. The stochastic positional embeddings are applied exclusively to masked tokens, adding Gaussian noise with a learned low-rank covariance to their position embeddings, while context tokens retain deterministic positions. The final output is a set of image representations (average-pooled patch features or concatenated multi-layer features) used for linear probing or fine-tuning.

## Method

StoP replaces deterministic positional embeddings for masked tokens with samples from a Gaussian distribution N(ψ_j, Σ), where ψ_j is the fixed position embedding and Σ = σAA^T is a learned low-rank covariance controlled by a scalar hyperparameter σ. The reparameterization trick (ψ̂_j = An_j + ψ_j) enables gradient-based optimization of A. The critical design choice is tying A to the context projection matrix B (so A=B), which prevents collapse to A=0: setting A to zero destroys all context information, while making A large amplifies noise beyond the positional signal. This dual role forces a trade-off that provably prevents deterministic collapse (Proposition 3.1). The paper further shows the optimal predictor under stochastic positions performs Gaussian spatial smoothing (Proposition 3.2), explaining why StoP encourages more semantic, location-robust features. Implementation requires only three extra lines of code with no runtime or memory overhead.

## Results

StoP applied to I-JEPA achieves +1.7% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1k linear probing with ViT-B/16 (72.6% vs. 70.9%) and +2.5% with ViT-H/14 using only 1% of labeled data (65.4% vs. 62.9%). On downstream tasks, StoP yields +3.3% on iNaturalist 2018 (50.9% vs. 47.6% with ViT-H/14) and +1.3% on CLEVR counting (88.0% vs. 86.7%). For semi-supervised video object segmentation on DAVIS 2017, StoP improves J&F mean by +2.5% with ViT-L/16 (58.4% vs. 55.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

StoP consistently outperforms all MIM baselines: on ImageNet-1k linear probing (last layer, 100% data), +StoP with ViT-B/16 reaches 72.6% vs. MAE's 68.0% and data2vec's 77.3% (though data2vec uses 1600 epochs vs. 600). Against its direct baseline I-JEPA, StoP gains +1.7% (ViT-B), +1.0% (ViT-L), and +0.8% (ViT-H) on full-data linear probing, with the largest gain of +2.5% under the 1%-data protocol with ViT-H. On downstream tasks (Table 4), StoP beats I-JEPA on iNaturalist 2018 (+3.3% with ViT-H) and CLEVR counting (+1.3%), but slightly underperforms on CLEVR depth ordering (−1.0% with ViT-L). Compared to invariance-based methods, StoP (ViT-H/14) still trails iBOT (ViT-L/16) on iNaturalist (50.9% vs. 57.3%) and Places205 (58.4% vs. 60.4%), and DINO (ViT-B/16) on iNaturalist (50.1% vs. 50.9% is a narrow win for StoP), confirming that MIM-based approaches remain slightly behind augmentation-heavy invariance methods. On video segmentation (DAVIS 2017), StoP with ViT-H/14 achieves 60.1% J&F, surpassing I-JEPA (59.7%) and MAE (55.5%) but still below DINO (not directly reported at same scale).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Masked Image Modeling (MIM) paradigm, building directly on I-JEPA (Assran et al., 2023) by replacing deterministic positional embeddings with stochastic ones (StoP). Conceptually, it departs from the standard MIM assumption that masked tokens must be predicted at exact locations, instead modeling position as a Gaussian random variable with a learned low-rank covariance. The reparameterization trick from VAEs (Kingma & Welling, 2013) is repurposed to make the stochastic sampling differentiable, and a weight-tying constraint (A=B) prevents the model from collapsing back to deterministic positions — a design choice not present in prior MIM or positional-embedding work. The theoretical analysis connecting the optimal predictor to spatial smoothing (convolution with a Gaussian) provides a formal justification absent from earlier MIM formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

StoP offers a minimal (3-line) modification to MIM that yields consistent improvements across classification, dense prediction, and counting tasks without additional compute, making it an attractive drop-in enhancement for the MIM family. However, the paper acknowledges that StoP fails when applied to pixel-space reconstruction (MAE), that it does not address appearance uncertainty, and that invariance-based methods (DINO, iBOT) still outperform MIM+StoP on several benchmarks. Open questions include whether conditioning the noise on the input context could further improve performance, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the I-JEPA architecture to other MIM variants with different reconstruction targets.
