## Problem

Probabilistic forecasting of dynamical systems requires sampling from the conditional distribution of future states given the current state, but existing generative approaches (diffusion, flow matching) initialize from a Gaussian base distribution rather than the actual observed state. This paper identifies that the natural transport problem is mapping a point mass at the current state onto a full-support distribution, and shows that stochastic interpolants provide a principled SDE-based mechanism to perform this transport in finite time without bias.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single current-state observation (a point mass) as the SDE initial condition and produces an ensemble of future-state samples by integrating the learned SDE. For Navier-Stokes, input is a vorticity field snapshot at 128×128 (or 32×32 for the super-resolution variant) and output is full-resolution 128×128 vorticity fields at a fixed lag τ. For video, input is the previous latent frame (4×8×8 for KTH, 4×16×16 for CLEVRER, encoded by a VQGAN) plus a randomly sampled earlier frame for context, and output is the next latent frame decoded to pixel space. The SDE can be iterated autoregressively without retraining to produce multi-step trajectories.

## Method

The core construction is a stochastic interpolant I_s = α_s x_0 + β_s x_1 + σ_s W_s that bridges a point mass at x_0 to the conditional target ρ_c(·|x_0) as s goes from 0 to 1. The drift b_s(x, x_0) = E[R_s | I_s = x, x_0] is learned via simulation-free square-loss regression over data pairs, and the resulting SDE dX_s = b_s(X_s, x_0)ds + σ_s dW_s with X_0 = x_0 exactly reproduces the interpolant law. A key flexibility is that the diffusion coefficient g_s can be changed post-training by adjusting the drift via the score ∇log ρ_s (expressed in closed form from b_s), without any retraining. The specific choice g_s = g_s^F that minimizes the KL divergence between the exact and estimated path measures yields a Föllmer process, providing an entropy-regularized optimal transport interpretation. In practice, a UNet approximates the drift for Navier-Stokes and a transformer-based architecture is used for video, with β_s = s² chosen to ensure well-behaved gradients at s = 0.

## Results

On the KTH video prediction task, PFI achieves an FVD of 39.13 at 250k steps, outperforming the RIVER flow-matching baseline (41.88). On CLEVRER, PFI reaches 39.31 FVD at 250k steps versus RIVER's 48.96, a substantial margin. The auto-encoder bound (the FVD of the VQGAN encoder-decoder on real data) is 33.45 for KTH and 2.79 for CLEVRER, indicating PFI approaches the latent-space performance ceiling. On the Navier-Stokes forecasting task, the method accurately reproduces the enstrophy spectrum of the true conditional distribution from both full- and low-resolution (32×32) inputs, and the SDE-based forecast is reported to be 100× faster than direct stochastic PDE simulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is RIVER (Davtyan et al., 2023), which learns a deterministic flow via flow matching to map a Gaussian sample to the next video frame. PFI, which instead initializes an SDE at the previous frame (a point mass) and transports it to the conditional distribution of the next frame, wins on every reported metric. On KTH at 250k steps, PFI reduces FVD from 41.88 to 39.13 (≈6.6% relative improvement); on CLEVRER the gap is much larger, from 48.96 to 39.31 (≈19.7% relative improvement). The shifted FVD (subtracting the auto-encoder bound) confirms the same ordering: PFI achieves 5.68 vs 8.43 on KTH and 36.52 vs 46.17 on CLEVRER at 250k steps. The paper attributes PFI's advantage to initializing the generative process at the actual previous frame rather than at pure noise, making the transport problem easier and preserving more temporal structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stochastic interpolant framework (Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden, 2022; Albergo et al., 2023) to the specific case where the base distribution is a point mass at the current system state, rather than a Gaussian or other fixed distribution. This departure from the standard Gaussian-to-target paradigm is what makes the construction natural for forecasting: the SDE is initialized directly at the observed state. The theoretical connection to Föllmer processes (Föllmer, 1986) and the Schrödinger bridge problem is new in this context, providing a KL-optimality interpretation of the optimal diffusion schedule that goes beyond the classical entropy-regularized optimal transport framing. Compared to prior conditional generative models (conditional GANs, VAEs, flow matching as in RIVER/Davtyan et al. 2023), the key distinction is the principled incorporation of stochasticity through the SDE structure rather than ad-hoc noise injection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a clean, theoretically grounded recipe for probabilistic forecasting: train a drift via simple square-loss regression, then tune the diffusion coefficient post-hoc without retraining. The Föllmer process connection provides a principled criterion for this tuning that minimizes the impact of drift estimation error on the generated ensemble. Open questions highlighted by the authors include incorporating physical structure into the generative model, reducing SDE simulation cost, handling random time-lags, and scaling to empirical weather data. The 100× speedup over direct stochastic PDE simulation for Navier-Stokes and the consistent video prediction improvements over flow-matching baselines suggest practical utility, though the video gains are moderate (e.g., 44.38 vs 46.69 FVD on KTH at 100k steps).
