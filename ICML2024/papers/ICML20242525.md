## Problem

Existing visual explanation methods for Transformers (attention rollout, GAME, LRP) produce confusing heat maps on CLIP due to its extremely sparse softmax attention patterns, while similarity-based CLIP-specific methods (MaskCLIP, CLIPSurgery) only use forward processing and cannot guarantee that highlighted features actually influence the final prediction. There is no effective, result-specific gradient-based explanation method that properly accounts for CLIP's dual-encoder architecture and sparse attention structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242525/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given an RGB image and a text prompt, the method produces two complementary heat maps: an image-region importance map (text-specific, showing which spatial regions drive the matching score) and a word-importance map (image-specific, showing which words in the sentence matter for matching). The image explanation is computed from the last Transformer layer of the image encoder, while the text explanation aggregates the last eight layers of the text encoder. The explanation is result-specific: changing the text prompt changes the image heat map, and changing the image changes the word heat map.

## Method

Grad-ECLIP decomposes the last Transformer layer to express the class-token embedding as a weighted sum of spatial value features: o_cls = Σ_i softmax(q_cls·k_i^T/√C)·v_i. It then constructs the heat map by aggregating the value features v_i with two types of importance weights: (1) channel importance w_c, computed as the gradient of the image-text cosine similarity w.r.t. the attention layer's class-token output, and (2) spatial importance w_i, computed from a 'loosened' attention (the raw inner product q_cls·k_i^T normalized to [0,1]) rather than the strict softmax, which is too sparse to serve as a meaningful spatial weight. The final heat map is H_i = ReLU(Σ_c w_c · w_i · v_i). This top-down gradient-based approach differs from similarity-map methods (which are purely forward) and from standard Grad-CAM (which uses full layer outputs rather than attention values and suffers from highlighting unrelated regions in CLIP).

## Results

On ImageNet-S localization, Grad-ECLIP achieves a Point Game score of 0.890 and PG-energy of 0.600, substantially outperforming the next-best methods (CLIPSurgery: PG 0.576, GAME: PG-energy 0.444). It also attains the highest AP (0.566) among all baselines. On ImageNet faithfulness (Deletion/Insertion), Grad-ECLIP achieves the best Insertion AUC (0.384 top-1, ground-truth text) while its Deletion AUC (0.246) is competitive with class-specific methods but lower than class-agnostic Rollout (0.408), which is expected since Rollout is not text-specific. Processing time is 0.0165 s/image, comparable to Grad-CAM (0.0114 s) and far faster than CLIPSurgery (2.94 s) or RISE (6.24 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Grad-ECLIP decisively outperforms all baselines on Point Game (0.890 vs. CLIPSurgery's 0.576, GAME's 0.471) and AP (0.566 vs. CLIPSurgery's 0.461), confirming superior localization of the explained target. On PG-energy it leads all methods (0.600 vs. GAME's 0.444), while CLIPSurgery edges ahead on Pixel Accuracy (0.755 vs. 0.706) and maskIoU (0.347 vs. 0.287) because it spreads high values uniformly across the object region. On faithfulness (Table 1), Grad-ECLIP achieves the best Insertion AUC (0.384) among all methods, but its Deletion AUC (0.246) is lower than class-agnostic Rollout (0.408) and raw attention (0.383); the paper attributes this to the fact that class-agnostic methods do not need to be text-specific. Compared to Grad-CAM, Grad-ECLIP improves PG by ~4.8× (0.890 vs. 0.185) and AP by 40% (0.566 vs. 0.405). M2IB and MaskCLIP are consistently weaker across most metrics, and RISE (shown qualitatively only) performs worst due to its perturbation-based nature. Processing time is comparable to forward-pass methods (~0.017 s/img) and ~180× faster than CLIPSurgery (2.94 s/img).

## Relation to Prior Work

Grad-ECLIP extends the gradient-based CAM lineage (Grad-CAM, Layer-CAM, FullGrad) to the specific architectural challenge of CLIP, where only the [cls] token participates in the matching score and patch tokens in the final layer are unused. It departs from attention-map-based methods (Rollout, GAME) that the authors show produce confusing results due to CLIP's sparse softmax attention, and from similarity-map methods (MaskCLIP, CLIPSurgery, ECLIP) that are forward-only and do not reflect which features actually influence the prediction. The key conceptual departure is a top-down gradient formulation: channel importance is derived from the gradient of the matching score w.r.t. the attention layer output, while spatial importance uses a 'loosened attention' (normalized inner product) in place of the strict softmax, which the authors argue is too sparse to serve as a meaningful spatial weight.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Grad-ECLIP provides a fast (0.0165 s/image, comparable to forward-only methods) and architecturally grounded explanation tool for the most widely deployed vision-language model, making CLIP's decision process accessible without modifying the model or training additional parameters. The analysis section reveals that CLIP decomposes and additively combines concepts (e.g., color + noun, verb + noun) but is notably weak on physical attributes (shape, material) and comparative attributes (size, position), offering concrete diagnostic information for model developers. Open questions include adapting the method to VLMs with post-encoder attention pooling (CoCa) or cross-attention fusion (ALBEF), and establishing fine-grained word-to-region correspondence rather than sentence-level importance.
