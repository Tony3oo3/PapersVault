## Problem

The classical bounded difference inequality (McDiarmid's inequality) requires uniformly bounded conditional ranges, and existing unbounded extensions only cover sub-Gaussian and sub-exponential tails, which are still relatively light-tailed. Heavy-tailed distributions—where the moment generating function does not exist—are pervasive in high-dimensional statistics, stochastic optimization, and signal processing, yet lack bounded difference-type concentration inequalities. This paper provides such inequalities for all distributions with finite variance, closing the gap between light-tailed theory and heavy-tailed practice.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real-valued function f of n independent random variables, where each centered conditional version f_k(X)(x) has a right tail uniformly captured by a generic increasing function h(t) (e.g., ct for sub-exponential, c_θ t^{1/θ} for sub-Weibull, or c log t for polynomial decay). The output is a two-regime tail probability bound: a sub-Gaussian term exp(−t²/(2nΛ)) for small deviations below a threshold t(η), and a heavy-tail term matching exp(−h(t)) for large deviations above t(η). The key intermediate quantity Λ(τ,η) is a variance-like functional of the truncated conditional versions, and the threshold t(η) is defined as the supremum of t satisfying t ≤ (ηh(t)/t)·nΛ(t,η).

## Method

The proof combines the entropy method (Maurer, 2012) with a truncation technique (Bakhshizadeh et al., 2023). The entropy method provides subadditivity of entropy for functions of independent variables, yielding a Bernstein-type bound on the log-moment-generating function of the truncated variable; the truncation technique circumvents the non-existence of the MGF for heavy-tailed variables by working with f_k^τ(X)(x) = f_k(X)(x)·1(f_k(X)(x) ≤ τ). The total probability is decomposed via the union bound into the truncated event (controlled by the entropy bound with parameter β = ηh(τ)/τ) and the overflow event (controlled by the tail bound n·exp(−h(τ))). Optimizing over β and τ in two regimes—setting τ = t for large deviations and τ = t(η) for small deviations—produces the final two-regime inequality. The framework is then specialized by bounding Λ(τ,η) under sub-exponential, sub-Weibull, and polynomially decaying tail assumptions, with refined asymptotic results showing Λ(τ,η) → σ² as τ → ∞ via the Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem.

## Results

The paper establishes a general framework (Theorem 3.1) for bounded difference-type concentration inequalities for functions of independent heavy-tailed random variables with finite variance, yielding a two-regime bound: a sub-Gaussian tail exp(−t²/(2nΛ)) for small deviations and a heavy tail exp(−c_t·η·h(t)) + n·exp(−h(t)) for large deviations, with the transition at threshold t(η). For sub-exponential tails (Corollary 3.4), the simplified bound is 2exp(−ct/4) for t ≥ t(η) and 2exp(−t²/(2nα)) for t < t(η), where α = 16/c² + σ₋². For sub-Weibull tails (Corollary 3.6), the large-deviation bound becomes 2exp(−c_θ·t^{1/θ}/4) with threshold t(η) = (½c_θ·n·α)^{θ/(2θ−1)}. The refined results (Theorems 3.8–3.9) show that as the truncation parameter τ → ∞, the variance proxy Λ(τ,η) converges to the true variance σ², yielding near-sharp bounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper extends McDiarmid's bounded difference inequality (which requires uniformly bounded conditional ranges) and the sub-exponential extension of Maurer & Pontil (2021) to all heavy-tailed distributions with finite variance, including sub-Weibull (θ > 1) and polynomially decaying tails where the MGF does not exist. Compared to Maurer & Pontil (2021), the sub-exponential result (Theorem 3.3) achieves the same two-regime structure (sub-Gaussian for small t, sub-exponential for large t) with a similar n/c transition threshold, but uses a truncation-based proof rather than MGF bounding, enabling extension to heavier tails. For vector-valued concentration (Theorem 4.1), the bound matches the qualitative form of Latała (1997) — a mixture of exp(−t²/(cn)) and exp(−t/c) — but applies to general normed spaces without requiring symmetry or logarithmic convexity of tails. For sub-Weibull and polynomially decaying tails, the authors note no comparable bounded difference-type results exist in the literature; the closest prior work (Bakhshizadeh et al., 2023; Kuchibhotla & Chakrabortty, 2018) covers only sums of independent variables, not general functions. The algorithmic stability results (Theorems 4.5–4.6) extend Kontorovich (2014) from sub-Gaussian to sub-exponential and sub-Weibull tails on the metric d(X_i, X_i'), removing the boundedness requirement on loss functions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bounded difference inequality (McDiarmid, 1998) from its classical bounded-range setting through the sub-Gaussian (Kutin, 2002; Meir & Zhang, 2003; Kontorovich, 2014) and sub-exponential (Maurer & Pontil, 2021) regimes to all heavy-tailed distributions with finite variance. The key methodological departure is that prior unbounded extensions rely on bounding the moment generating function, which fundamentally fails for heavy-tailed distributions; instead, this paper combines the entropy method (Maurer, 2012) for tensorizing conditional entropies with a truncation technique (Bakhshizadeh et al., 2023) that allows MGF-based arguments to be applied only to truncated variables. The framework is general enough to recover sub-exponential, sub-Weibull, and polynomially decaying tail bounds as special cases, and the applications to Rademacher complexity and algorithmic stability directly follow the structure of Maurer & Pontil (2021) and Kontorovich (2014).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in extending generalization bounds (via Rademacher complexity and algorithmic stability) from bounded or light-tailed loss functions to heavy-tailed distributions with finite variance, which is increasingly relevant given the heavy-tail phenomenon observed in SGD and high-dimensional statistics. A key limitation is that the framework cannot handle infinite-variance distributions, and the authors suggest Φ-entropies and moment inequalities as potential tools for that setting. The polynomially decaying tail case requires c > 2 (i.e., more than two moments), leaving the c ≤ 2 regime as an open problem. Additionally, the bounds are dimension-dependent (inheriting the n-dependence of bounded difference inequalities) and do not achieve the dimension-free rates available for Lipschitz functions of Gaussian variables, though the trade-off is broader applicability without Lipschitz assumptions.
