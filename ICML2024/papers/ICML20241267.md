## Problem

Existing generalization bounds for SSMs and RNNs are norm-based, separating data and model complexity via input norms and parameter norms without capturing how temporal dependencies in the sequence data interact with the model's memory structure. Additionally, the standard HiPPO-based initialization for SSMs assumes the input sequence is constant over time, making output value scales fragile across different temporal patterns. This paper asks: can a generalization bound that explicitly encodes the interplay between SSM memory and data temporal structure guide better initialization and regularization?

## Input / Output

The SSM consumes a continuous-time input sequence x(t) modeled as a stochastic process with mean μ(t) and covariance K(s,t), discretized via Zero-Order Hold into a finite-length sequence of length L. The model produces a scalar output y(t) = ∫₀ᵀ ρ_θ(T−s)x(s)ds at each time step, where ρ_θ(s) = Ce^{As}B is the SSM kernel. In practice, inputs are flattened image pixels (e.g., 32×32 or 128×128 for Pathfinder/PathX), byte-level text tokens, or numerical sequence values, and outputs are class labels or per-position predictions. The generalization measure τ(θ) is computed per-layer via FFT convolution of the SSM kernel with batch-estimated sequence statistics (mean and variance along the batch dimension).

## Method

The paper derives a data-dependent generalization bound for SSMs via Rademacher complexity, showing the bound scales with ψ_Θ = sup_θ ∫|ρ_θ(T−s)|√K(s,s)ds + |∫ρ_θ(T−s)μ(s)ds|, which captures the interplay between the SSM's exponentially-decaying memory kernel and the data's temporal statistics (mean and variance). From this bound, two optimization designs follow: (1) an initialization scaling rule that rescales the output matrix C to C̃ = C/√τ(θ) after HiPPO initialization, guaranteeing bounded output scales O(√log T) regardless of temporal pattern; (2) a regularization term λ·τ(θ) added to the empirical loss, which penalizes the kernel-to-data-statistics interaction rather than raw parameter norms, and is computed efficiently via FFT convolution at O(bdL log L) per layer. Unlike weight decay on A (which would disrupt oscillatory frequencies), this regularizer acts on the hidden state space through the data-dependent measure and is applied layer-by-layer in multi-layer SSMs.

## Results

On the LRA benchmark (6 tasks), the proposed regularization (8) improves average test accuracy from 86.89% to 87.18% for S4-Legs and from 86.38% to 86.82% for S4D-Legs. On the synthetic Gaussian white noise task, combining the initialization scheme (7) and regularization (8) reduces test MSE from 4.70 to 0.60 for the most challenging temporal structure (b=0.01), a ~7.8× improvement over the baseline. The regularization adds only ~10% extra per-epoch training time (e.g., 16min34s → 18min6s average for S4-Legs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: S4-Legs (Goel et al., 2022, full-matrix A) and S4D-Legs (Gu et al., 2022b, diagonal A), both trained with the standard HiPPO/Legs initialization and no generalization-based regularization. The proposed regularization (8) wins on 5 out of 6 LRA tasks for S4-Legs (e.g., Image: 88.27% vs. 87.41%; Pathfinder: 96.02% vs. 95.89%) and 5 out of 6 for S4D-Legs (e.g., Image: 87.98% vs. 86.69%; Pathfinder: 95.04% vs. 94.87%), with the only exception being Retrieval where performance is essentially tied. The initialization rescaling (7) alone is less effective on LRA (average drops slightly) because the standard S4 training pipeline includes batch/layer normalization that already mitigates temporal-scale issues; however, on the synthetic task without normalization, (7) dramatically improves robustness (test MSE drops from 4.70 to 1.06 at b=0.01). Combining both (7,8) achieves the best test accuracy on 3 out of 6 tasks for each model and yields the best average on small-model variants (Table 9: 81.61% for S4-Legs, 82.25% for S4D-Legs vs. 81.01%/81.03% baselines). The regularization also outperforms alternative schemes (weight decay on A, filter norm regularization) on both synthetic and LRA benchmarks (Table 12–13).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the norm-based generalization bounds for RNNs (Zhang et al., 2018; Chen et al., 2019; Tu et al., 2019) by replacing the separation of data norm and parameter norm with a data-dependent measure that captures the interplay between the SSM's memory kernel ρ_θ and the temporal statistics (mean and covariance) of the input sequence. It builds directly on the HiPPO-based initialization framework (Gu et al., 2023) for S4/S4D models, but departs from it by making initialization data-dependent rather than data-independent. The 'curse of memory' analysis for linear RNNs (Li et al., 2021; 2022) provides the conceptual motivation for studying how temporal structure affects SSM behavior, but this paper is the first to formalize that connection through a generalization bound and then use it to derive concrete optimization designs (a scaling rule for initialization and a new regularizer).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key conceptual contribution is showing that generalization in SSMs is governed not by parameter norms alone but by the interaction between the model's memory structure and the data's temporal dependencies — a distinction that norm-based bounds fundamentally cannot capture. The practical impact is modest: the regularization yields small but consistent test accuracy gains (~0.3–0.5%) on LRA, and the initialization scheme is less effective on real benchmarks where batch/layer normalization already mitigates the output-scale instability it targets. Major open questions remain: the theory is restricted to single-layer linear SSMs with an independence assumption across features, and the effects of depth and feature correlations on generalization are left for future work. The finding that weight decay on the hidden matrix A substantially hurts ListOps performance (49.9% vs. 61.6%) while the proposed regularizer does not is a useful practical caution for SSM practitioners.
