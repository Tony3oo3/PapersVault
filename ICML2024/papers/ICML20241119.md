## Problem

Existing BIQA methods treat each image independently, ignoring the global context of relative quality contrast among different images in the training set, and rely on semantic features from pre-trained models that are sub-optimal for distortion-sensitive quality perception. Prior contrastive learning approaches (e.g., CONTRIQUE) define positive/negative pairs by distortion type rather than actual quality labels, failing to capture fine-grained quality correlations across images with different content but similar quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241119/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, randomly crops it into ten 224×224 patches, and encodes them via a ViT-S backbone (patch size 16, embedding dimension 384, 12 encoder layers). The output is a single scalar quality score produced by concatenating the refined QUA and CLS token features from a one-layer transformer decoder and passing them through an MLP. The contrastive loss is computed at every encoder layer during training but incurs no extra inference cost.

## Method

CSIQA integrates two complementary mechanisms: (1) a Quality-Aware Mask Attention module that introduces a learnable QUA token which, via a binary attention mask, attends only to randomly visible patches (mask probability 0.3), forcing the model to encode local distortion features independent of global semantics; and (2) a Quality Context Contrastive Learning module that applies InfoNCE at every encoder layer using an easy-hard sampling strategy, where easy positives/negatives are selected by quality-score distance within the batch and hard positives/negatives are identified via a teacher-student distillation framework (similar quality but different pseudo-labels, or vice versa). A logit distillation loss regularizes the student with both ground-truth labels and teacher pseudo-labels, and the total loss sums the logit loss with a weighted sum of per-layer contrastive losses. The framework is portable: the contrastive learning and distillation components can be plugged into existing BIQA networks (TIQA, DEIQT, DBCNN, HyperNet) without architectural changes, yielding 1–3 point SRCC gains.

## Results

CSIQA achieves state-of-the-art performance on 6 out of 8 standard BIQA benchmarks, with headline results including PLCC/SRCC of 0.985/0.983 on LIVE, 0.941/0.926 on TID2013, 0.920/0.898 on LIVEC, and 0.943/0.929 on KonIQ. In cross-dataset validation (train on one, test on another without fine-tuning), CSIQA surpasses all SOTA methods on all four cross-authentic-dataset experiments, e.g., achieving SRCC 0.814 (LIVEC→KonIQ) and 0.945 (LIVE→CSIQ). The quality context contrastive learning module is also portable: adding it to TIQA yields +1.8 to +2.9 PLCC points, and adding it to DEIQT yields +0.6 to +2.2 PLCC points across CSIQ, LIVEC, and KonIQ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSIQA outperforms the previous SOTA method DEIQT (Qin et al., 2023) on 6 of 8 datasets, with the largest gains on authentic datasets (e.g., +2.6 PLCC on LIVEC in the ResNet-50 portability experiment). Against CONTRIQUE (Madhusudana et al., 2022), which uses distortion-based contrastive learning, CSIQA achieves +0.7% PLCC on synthetic LIVE and +2.6% PLCC on authentic LIVEC with the same ResNet-50 backbone, demonstrating that quality-based (rather than distortion-based) positive/negative sample selection is more effective for real-world images. On cross-dataset generalization, CSIQA surpasses all baselines including DEIQT, HyperIQA, and TReS on every cross-authentic-dataset transfer (e.g., LIVEC→KonIQ: 0.814 vs. DEIQT's 0.794 SRCC). The gMAD competition on the Waterloo Database further confirms superiority: as a defender, CSIQA resists attacks more effectively than DEIQT, and as an attacker it identifies more perceptually distinct quality-difference pairs. The portability experiments (Table 2, 13, 14) show that the contrastive learning module alone adds 1–4.8 PLCC points to diverse backbones (ViT, ResNet-50, EfficientNet-b0, RedNet101) without architectural changes, validating its generality.

## Relation to Prior Work

CSIQA builds on the Vision Transformer-based BIQA line (MUSIQ, TIQA, DEIQT) and the contrastive learning for IQA line (CONTRIQUE, Re-IQA), but departs from both in key ways. Unlike prior contrastive IQA methods that define positive/negative samples by distortion type or content, CSIQA uses actual quality ground truths to construct sample pairs and introduces an easy-hard sampling strategy guided by a teacher's pseudo-labels. It also adapts MAE-style random masking (He et al., 2022) into a quality-aware mask attention mechanism with a dedicated QUA token, and provides a theoretical argument (Theorem A.2) that contrastive learning is more robust to label noise than the rank-learning approaches (RankIQA, DIPiQ) previously used for global quality relations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically impactful finding is the portability of the quality context contrastive learning framework: it can be grafted onto existing IQA networks (TIQA, DEIQT, DBCNN, HyperNet, TReS, ADANet) with 1–2 point SRCC/PLCC gains and no extra inference cost, making it a drop-in enhancement for the field. The theoretical robustness argument for contrastive over rank learning, supported by empirical gaps up to 8% on TID2013, offers a principled reason to prefer contrastive formulations. Open questions include whether the easy-hard sampling and QUA token generalize to video quality assessment or generative-image quality assessment, and whether the reliance on a well-converged teacher of the same architecture limits applicability in low-data regimes.
