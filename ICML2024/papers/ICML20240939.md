## Problem

Domain-independent data augmentation (DA) for regression is underexplored compared to classification, where mixup and its variants are well-established. Existing mixup-based methods, which produce convex combinations of samples, are less effective for regression because they do not account for the geometric structure of the data manifold. This paper asks whether a data-driven, domain-independent augmentation that respects the manifold geometry can improve generalization in deep regression networks.

## Input / Output

FOMA operates on mini-batch tensors of input features X ∈ R^{b×n} and continuous output labels Y ∈ R^{b×m}, which are concatenated column-wise before augmentation. The method can be applied at the raw input level or at any intermediate latent layer Z_l of the network, and the augmented samples are fed back through the same forward pass. Batches are optionally constructed from label-close points (rather than random sampling) to better approximate the local tangent plane, and the method is fully differentiable so gradients flow through the SVD back to the network weights.

## Method

FOMA generates augmented training samples by computing the SVD of the concatenated [Z_l, Y] matrix and scaling down the singular values beyond index k by a random factor λ ~ Beta(α, α), then reconstructing the matrix. The index k is set to the intrinsic dimension of the data (via the TwoNN estimator) in FOMA, or to the smallest number of components explaining a fraction ρ of total variance in FOMA_ρ. The key insight is that this operation samples from the tangent plane of the data manifold, producing points that lie close to the true distribution rather than in arbitrary convex combinations as in mixup. Unlike mixup, FOMA does not scale the loss function, treating augmented samples as if drawn from the true distribution. The approach is framed as a Vicinal Risk Minimization procedure where the vicinal distribution is defined over tangent planes rather than convex hulls, and perturbation theory analysis shows FOMA is complementary to additive noise (which tends to increase small singular values, while FOMA decreases them).

## Results

On in-distribution regression benchmarks, FOMA and FOMA_ρ achieve the best RMSE on 4 out of 5 datasets (Airfoil: 1.471, NO2: 0.512, Exchange-Rate: 0.013, Echo: 5.215), with the most dramatic improvements on Airfoil (~37% reduction over the best baseline) and Exchange-Rate (~38% reduction). On Electricity, FOMA matches ERM on RMSE (0.058) but trails C-Mixup on MAPE (14.614 vs. 13.372). On out-of-distribution robustness, FOMA_ρ achieves the best average RMSE on RCF (0.159) and Crimes (0.128), and the best worst-domain RMSE on Crimes (0.158), while showing a notable gap to C-Mixup and ADA on DTI and PovertyMap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the reproduced baselines (starred), FOMA/FOMA_ρ clearly outperform Mixup and Manifold Mixup on all datasets where those methods are available; notably, vanilla Mixup actually degrades Airfoil RMSE from 2.901 (ERM) to 3.730, while FOMA_ρ reduces it to 1.471. Compared to C-Mixup*, FOMA wins on Airfoil (1.646 vs. 2.748 RMSE), Exchange-Rate (0.013 vs. 0.024), and Echo (5.215 vs. 5.362), but C-Mixup retains a slight edge on NO2 RMSE (0.516 vs. 0.521) and Electricity MAPE (13.349 vs. 14.614). Against ADA*, FOMA dominates on Airfoil (1.646 vs. 2.357) and Exchange-Rate (0.013 vs. 0.022), while ADA is marginally better on NO2 RMSE (0.515 vs. 0.521). On out-of-distribution tasks, FOMA_ρ matches or beats the best reproduced baselines on RCF and Crimes, but lags C-Mixup† and ADA† on DTI (R: 0.503 vs. 0.498/0.493) and PovertyMap (R: 0.832 vs. 0.81/0.794), where the gap is smaller but consistent.

## Relation to Prior Work

FOMA positions itself within the domain-independent data augmentation (DI-DA) lineage, drawing on the manifold hypothesis and SVD-based tangent plane approximation (Singer & Wu, 2012) to generate synthetic samples, and on the TwoNN intrinsic dimension estimator (Facco et al., 2017) to determine how many dominant components to preserve. Conceptually, it is a data-driven alternative to mixup: both solve a Vicinal Risk Minimization problem, but FOMA defines 'vicinity' as the tangent plane of the data manifold (via SVD of the batch) rather than as a convex hull of sample pairs, making augmentations depend on the empirical data distribution. The authors explicitly contrast FOMA with additive noise via perturbation theory, showing that random perturbations tend to *increase* small singular values while FOMA *decreases* them, positioning the two as complementary rather than equivalent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a recognized gap—domain-independent DA for regression was underexplored relative to classification—and provides evidence that mixup-style convex mixing is systematically less effective for regression than manifold-aware augmentation. The conceptual finding that both augmented and non-augmented regression networks achieve lower MSE on data projected toward the manifold (λ < 1) than on the original data (λ = 1) challenges the standard overfitting narrative for small datasets and suggests a structural preference in deep regression models. Limitations include the SVD computational overhead (which scales with batch size), mixed results on out-of-distribution benchmarks where FOMA trails C-Mixup and ADA on DTI and PovertyMap, and the absence of clear hyperparameter selection guidelines. Open questions raised by the authors include whether the vicinal distribution interacts fundamentally with learned representations, whether FOMA adapts cleanly to classification, and its connection to generative modeling.
