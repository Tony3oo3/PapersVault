## Problem

Gradient descent-ascent on Lagrangian min-max formulations of constrained optimization problems exhibits unstable oscillatory dynamics in the Lagrange multipliers, limiting the adoption of constrained optimization in deep learning. Existing momentum methods (Polyak, Nesterov) fail to resolve these oscillations, and the ML community lacks a reliable, general-purpose update scheme for the dual variables that is compatible with arbitrary primal optimizers.

## Input / Output

The ν PI controller receives the current constraint violations c(x_t) = [g(x_t), h(x_t)] as its error signal and the current multiplier vector θ_t as its internal state, and outputs an updated multiplier θ_{t+1} with a single scalar hyperparameter κ_p (plus the step-size κ_i and optional EMA coefficient ν). It operates as a black-box on the dual variables only: the primal optimizer (e.g., Adam, SGD with momentum) is left unchanged, and the controller is invoked once per primal update step. For inequality constraints, a projection onto R_+ is applied after each update to enforce non-negativity.

## Method

The ν PI algorithm augments a standard PI controller by replacing the raw error in the proportional term with an exponential moving average ξ_t = νξ_{t-1} + (1-ν)e_t, yielding the update θ_{t+1} = θ_t + κ_i·e_t + κ_p(ξ_t - ξ_{t-1}). Theorem 1 proves that ν PI generalizes Polyak, Nesterov, and the optimistic gradient method via a specific hyperparameter mapping, revealing that positive momentum corresponds to a negative κ_p that counter-intuitively increases multipliers when feasibility improves — the root cause of oscillations. The κ_p hyperparameter monotonically controls the damping of the system: larger values widen the 'optimistic' region where the controller proactively decreases multipliers upon constraint improvement, preventing overshoot. A continuous-time spectral analysis (Theorem 4) shows that ν PI can achieve critical damping of the Lagrangian oscillator, a regime unreachable by plain gradient ascent (κ_p = 0).

## Results

On the hard-margin SVM (Iris), ν PI is the only tested method that converges to the optimal Lagrange multipliers, while all baselines (GA, Polyak, Nesterov, Adam) exhibit persistent oscillations. On the fairness task (Adult), ν PI (ν=0.99) achieves 92.4% training accuracy versus 89% for GA, with both reaching ~1.7% max constraint violation. On CIFAR-10 layer-wise sparsity (30% density target), ν PI (κp=8.0) achieves 86.2% test accuracy and 95.1% train accuracy with a violation range of only 1.8, compared to 84.1% test / 92.0% train and a violation range of 22.0 for GA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ν PI decisively outperforms gradient ascent (GA) across all three tasks: it is the only method converging to optimal multipliers on SVM, achieves 3.4% higher training accuracy on the fairness task, and reduces the sparsity violation range from 22.0 to 1.8 on CIFAR-10. Against Polyak momentum (both positive β=0.3 and negative β=−0.5, −0.3), ν PI gains 2.1–3.7% test accuracy on CIFAR-10 sparsity and is the sole convergent method on SVM. Nesterov momentum is strictly dominated since its κp is always non-positive (Eq. 9), preventing the damping behavior needed for Lagrangian games. GA with dual restarts is the closest competitor on sparsity (85.3% vs 86.2% test accuracy), but ν PI edges it out on both train and test accuracy while maintaining a comparable violation range (1.8 vs 1.2). Adam, tested on the SVM task, also fails to converge to the optimal multipliers, confirming that adaptive per-coordinate scaling does not resolve the oscillatory dynamics inherent to the min-max structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Stooke et al. (2020), who first proposed PID controllers for updating Lagrange multipliers in safety-constrained reinforcement learning, by providing an optimization-oriented analysis and proving that their ν PI variant (a PI controller with an exponential moving average on the error signal) generalizes both POLYAK and NESTEROV momentum methods (Thm. 1). It also builds on Gidel et al. (2019a)'s observation that negative momentum helps in adversarial games, and on Gallego-Posada et al. (2022)'s dual restarts heuristic for sparsity constraints, positioning ν PI as a principled alternative that subsumes both. The key departure from prior work is the unifying framework that explains *why* standard momentum methods fail in Lagrangian games through a relative-update analysis, rather than merely proposing a new controller.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Constrained optimization is becoming essential for enforcing fairness, sparsity, and safety in neural networks, but the instability of gradient descent-ascent on Lagrangian problems has been a practical barrier to adoption. ν PI offers a simple, composable dual-update rule whose hyperparameters (particularly κ_p) have a predictable monotonic effect on damping, making it easier to tune than step-size-based methods. Limitations include the restriction to dual-variable updates only (assuming a well-tuned primal optimizer), relatively small-scale experiments, and the open question of how ν PI behaves for non-linear players in general min-max games beyond the Lagrangian structure.
