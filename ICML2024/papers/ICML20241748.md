## Problem

Autoregressive LLM inference is inherently slow because tokens are generated sequentially, underutilizing accelerators optimized for matrix-matrix operations. It remains unclear how much model capacity is needed for prompt understanding (NLU) versus response generation (NLG), and existing speedups like speculative decoding rely on less accurate smaller drafters while parallel decoding fails to fully leverage the base model's rich representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241748/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The Tandem model takes a text prompt (token sequence) and produces generated text tokens in blocks of length γ. The large model M_L processes the prompt and each subsequent block of γ tokens simultaneously (non-autoregressive), while the small model M_S generates γ tokens autoregressively per block. When deployed within SPEED, the output is guaranteed identical to the large model's standalone output, with the small model serving as a drafter whose tokens are verified block-by-block by the large model.

## Method

Tandem Transformers pair a small autoregressive model M_S with a large model M_L operating in block mode: M_L processes γ tokens at once to compute representations, and M_S generates the next γ tokens autoregressively while attending to M_L's projected representations for all prior-block tokens (via a linear projection layer) and its own representations for current-block tokens. Training initializes both models from pretrained PaLM2 checkpoints (Bison and Gecko), freezes M_L, and fine-tunes M_S plus the projection layer with cross-entropy loss, optionally adding a logit-distillation loss from M_L's outputs. The key insight is that NLU and NLG capacity can be decoupled—allocating more parameters to prefill processing than to token generation—without significant accuracy loss. Within SPEED, the Tandem small model acts as a superior drafter (attending to M_L's representations improves draft quality by ~11% per-block acceptance rate), and an adaptive block-length router MLP dynamically decides when to continue drafting versus verifying, enabling much larger effective block lengths.

## Results

On SuperGLUE (1-shot), Tandem-Distil (PaLM2-Bison + PaLM2-Gecko, γ=3) achieves 78.8, substantially outperforming standalone PaLM2-Gecko (62.8) by 16 points and matching PaLM2-Otter (79.0) within 0.2 points, while being 1.16× faster than PaLM2-Otter. Within the SPEED framework, Tandem-Distil achieves 2.47× end-to-end speedup over PaLM2-Bison on Reddit (num-samples=1), compared to 2.17× for standard SPEED with PaLM2-Gecko-Distil, yielding a 1.14× relative gain. With adaptive block length, the speedup further increases to 2.85× over PaLM2-Bison on LM1B (γ_max=27). On pretraining next-token accuracy, Tandem-Distil reaches 58.61% vs 55.06% for PaLM2-Gecko (a 3.3% absolute improvement).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standalone PaLM2 models (Anil et al., 2023), Tandem-Distil dramatically outperforms PaLM2-Gecko across all benchmarks (e.g., +16 points on SuperGLUE, +14.2 on TydiQA, +9.1 on WMT22) while remaining within 0.2–3.3 points of PaLM2-Otter and PaLM2-Bison respectively, at 1.16× lower latency than PaLM2-Otter. Against standard SPEED with PaLM2-Gecko-Distil as the drafter (Leviathan et al., 2023), Tandem+SPEED achieves 1.11×–1.17× additional speedup across all datasets and num-samples settings (e.g., 2.47× vs 2.17× on Reddit, 2.61× vs 2.35× on LM1B), driven by an 11.24% higher per-block token acceptance rate. The paper also shows that replacing PaLM2-Gecko with the even smaller PaLM2-XXXS as the secondary model further widens the gap: Tandem-Distil with PaLM2-XXXS reaches 3.04× speedup vs 2.45× for PaLM2-XXXS-Distil alone (1.24× relative gain on LM1B, num-samples=1). Overall, Tandem consistently wins on both accuracy (vs smaller models) and latency (vs SPEED baselines) without sacrificing output quality, since SPEED guarantees identical outputs to the primary model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the speculative decoding (SPEED) framework of Leviathan et al. (2023) by replacing the standalone drafter with a Tandem architecture where a small autoregressive model attends to a large model's block-level representations. It departs from parallel decoding (Stern et al., 2018) and MEDUSA-style approaches that learn classifiers on top of the primary model's outputs, instead preserving autoregressive generation in the small model while enriching its context with the large model's richer representations. The architecture is positioned as a generalization of logit distillation (Hinton et al., 2015; Zhou et al., 2023) to the representation level, and is explicitly complementary to distillation rather than a replacement. Conceptually, it draws on encoder-decoder ideas but applies them iteratively within the decoder itself, refreshing the large model's representations every γ tokens rather than processing the prompt once.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for LLM deployment: a 2.19×+ end-to-end speedup with guaranteed output equivalence to the primary model, and a 1.16× speedup over a mid-size model (PaLM2-Otter) at comparable accuracy, directly addresses the cost bottleneck of autoregressive inference. The architectural insight that NLU (prefill) and NLG (generation) capacity can be decoupled without substantial quality loss opens a design space for future LLMs. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to the PaLM2 family, fixed block length during training, and adaptive γ only validated for num-samples=1. Open questions raised by the authors include whether the large model can plan future tokens, whether Tandem with γ=0 can replace LoRA for fine-tuning, and how to extend adaptive block length to larger batch sizes.
