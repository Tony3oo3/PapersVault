## Problem

Differentially private training introduces new hyperparameters (noise scale, clipping threshold) and shifts the optimal values of existing ones (learning rate, number of steps), making hyperparameter optimization prohibitively expensive in both compute and privacy budget. Prior private HPO methods rely on Rényi DP and are only validated on simple benchmarks like MNIST and CIFAR-10, while most large-scale DP papers simply ignore the privacy cost of HPO entirely. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a linear scaling rule that reduces HPO cost by an order of magnitude while fully accounting for privacy expenditure across 22 diverse tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a private dataset, a pretrained model architecture (e.g., BEiT, ViT, GPT-2, RoBERTa), a target privacy budget expressed as (ε, δ) or equivalently a set of f-DP parameters (μ₁, μ₂, μ_f), and a logarithmic search space for learning rate η ∈ [10⁻⁷, 10⁻⁴] and iterations T. It outputs a single optimized scalar r = η × T (the total step size) that is decomposed into concrete η and T values, followed by a fully DP-trained model. A key design choice is that all other hyperparameters (batch size = full batch, clipping norm C = 1, momentum ρ = 0.9, zero initialization) are fixed, reducing the search to one dimension.

## Method

The core insight is that the optimal total step size r = η × T is approximately linear in the privacy budget ε, derived from combining the original linear scaling rule (η ∝ 1/σ) with DP composition theory (σ grows sublinearly in T). The algorithm performs two cheap random-search sweeps at small privacy budgets (e.g., ε₁ = 0.1, ε₂ = 0.2), each with n runs, to obtain two empirical points (ε₁, r₁) and (ε₂, r₂), then fits a line r = αε + b and extrapolates to the target ε_f to estimate r*. The final training uses full-batch DP-GD with unit gradient clipping, momentum (ρ = 0.9), and PLV (privacy loss variable) accounting, which together maximize the signal-to-noise ratio of updates and enable tight privacy calibration at very small ε values. The total privacy guarantee composes the HPO trials and the final run via f-DP, so the HPO cost is only the small gap between ε_f and the target budget (e.g., ε = 0.88 vs. 1.0), making the method competitive with grid search at a fraction of the privacy and compute cost.

## Results

Across 22 benchmark tasks, the linear scaling rule achieves a Relative Error Rate Reduction (RERR) of 77–87% over random search while approaching oracle (grid search) performance at a fraction of the privacy cost. On CIFAR-10 without public data (ε=1), the method reaches 62.63% accuracy vs. 44% for random search and 68% for grid search. On ImageNet (ε=1), it achieves 87% accuracy, outperforming DPAdamWOSM (79%) and Mehta et al. (81%). For NLP, fine-tuning OPT-13B on SQuAD yields 78.08% vs. 49.33% for random search (oracle: 82.43%), and GPT-2 on PersonaChat achieves perplexity 17.69 at ε=∞, beating the prior SOTA of 18.52.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method significantly outperforms random search on every dataset, with RERR ranging from 77.63% (CIFAR-10) to 86.85% (SQuAD), and approaches grid-search oracle performance while accounting for the privacy cost of HPO (grid search does not). Against prior private HPO methods using Rényi DP, the linear scaling rule achieves 70.5% on CIFAR-10 at ε=1, beating Koskela & Kulkarni (67%) and Papernot & Steinke (66%) by 3.5 and 4.5 points respectively, attributed to a better search prior and tighter PLD accounting. On ImageNet at ε=1, the method reaches 87%, outperforming DPAdamWOSM (79%) by 8 points and Mehta et al.'s single-step DP-Adam approach (81%) by 6 points. For language modeling, GPT-2 fine-tuned on PersonaChat achieves perplexity 17.69 at ε=∞, improving over Li et al.'s 18.52, and on WikiText-2 the method obtains perplexity 27.69 at ε=3 versus Shi et al.'s 28.84 at the same budget. The method also matches or exceeds Berrada et al. on ImageNet finetuning for ε > 0.5 while explicitly accounting for HPO privacy cost, which prior work does not report.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the original linear scaling rule from Malladi et al. (2022) — which states the optimal learning rate is inversely proportional to noise scale in GD — by combining it with DP composition to derive a new rule: the total step size r = η×T scales linearly with ε. Prior private HPO methods (Papernot & Steinke, 2021; Koskela & Kulkarni, 2023; Wang et al., 2023) relied on Rényi DP with random search as the base and were validated only on MNIST/CIFAR10. This work departs from that lineage by introducing a linear scaling prior that reduces HPO to a 1D problem, using PLV accounting (tighter than RDP), full-batch DP-GD (rather than subsampled DP-SGD), and validating on 22 tasks spanning CV and NLP with models up to 13B parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine open problem: prior DP papers routinely perform hundreds of HPO trials without accounting for their privacy cost, undermining the final guarantee. The linear scaling rule reduces both compute and privacy cost of HPO by an order of magnitude while maintaining competitive accuracy, making private HPO practically feasible for large-scale tasks. Key limitations include: for very small ε on difficult datasets (e.g., ImageNet), insufficient budget remains after HPO for the final run; the theoretical analysis relies on convexity/smoothness assumptions that do not hold for neural networks; and the method requires more runtime than random search due to its adaptive nature. Open questions include whether RDP analysis of the adaptive method is possible, whether the linear relationship holds in settings where HPs are genuinely non-linear, and whether the approach extends to federated or semi-private threat models.
