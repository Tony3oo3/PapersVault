## Problem

LoRA and other PEFT methods consistently underperform full fine-tuning, a gap previously attributed solely to the limited number of trainable parameters. This paper reveals a deeper cause: LoRA and FT exhibit fundamentally different learning patterns in how they update weight magnitude versus direction—LoRA couples them proportionally (positive correlation), while FT decouples them (negative correlation), limiting LoRA's effective learning capacity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20241299/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DoRA takes a pre-trained model (LLM, LVLM, or vision-language encoder-decoder) and fine-tunes it on downstream tasks ranging from text-only commonsense reasoning to image/video-text understanding and text-to-image generation. The only additional parameters beyond standard LoRA are per-layer magnitude vectors of size 1×k (adding ~0.01% trainable parameters). After training, the decomposed magnitude and direction components merge back into the original weight matrix, so inference latency is identical to the base model with no architectural change.

## Method

DoRA reparameterizes each pre-trained weight matrix W₀ as W = m · V/||V||_c (magnitude × unit-direction), where m = ||W₀||_c is a trainable 1×k vector and V is updated via a LoRA low-rank product BA. The fine-tuned weight becomes W' = m · (W₀ + BA)/||W₀ + BA||_c, so LoRA handles only directional adaptation while magnitude is learned explicitly. A gradient analysis shows the column-wise normalization projects ∇W' away from the current weight direction, aligning the gradient covariance toward identity and stabilizing optimization; it also provably induces a negative ΔM–ΔD correlation matching FT's pattern. To reduce backpropagation memory by ~24%, the paper detaches ||V+ΔV||_c from the computation graph, treating it as a constant during gradient computation with negligible accuracy loss. The method is also compatible with other low-rank variants (e.g., VeRA → DVoRA) and with quantized fine-tuning (QLoRA → QDoRA).

## Results

On commonsense reasoning (8 tasks averaged), DoRA achieves 78.4% on LLaMA-7B, 81.5% on LLaMA-13B, 79.7% on LLaMA2-7B, and 85.2% on LLaMA3-8B, outperforming LoRA by +3.7, +1.0, +2.1, and +4.4 points respectively. Even with the rank halved (DoRA†), DoRA still surpasses LoRA by +2.8 to +4.2 points while using only ~half the trainable parameters. On image/video-text understanding with VL-BART, DoRA reaches 77.4% (image) and 85.4% (video), matching or exceeding full fine-tuning (77.3% and 87.5%) with only ~6% of parameters. On visual instruction tuning with LLaVA-1.5-7B, DoRA achieves 67.6% average across 7 benchmarks, beating both LoRA (66.9%) and FT (66.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DoRA consistently outperforms LoRA across all four LLM backbones on commonsense reasoning: +3.7% on LLaMA-7B (78.4 vs 74.7), +1.0% on LLaMA-13B (81.5 vs 80.5), +2.1% on LLaMA2-7B (79.7 vs 77.6), and +4.4% on LLaMA3-8B (85.2 vs 80.8), with only a marginal 0.01% increase in trainable parameters. Against adapter-based baselines, DoRA on LLaMA-7B (78.4%) surpasses the Parallel adapter (72.2%) by 6.2 points while using only 24% of its parameters (0.84% vs 3.54%) and without adding inference latency. DoRA on LLaMA-7B also exceeds ChatGPT's zero-shot CoT accuracy (77.0%) by 1.4 points. On visual instruction tuning (LLaVA-1.5-7B), DoRA (67.6%) beats both LoRA (66.9%) and full fine-tuning (66.5%), suggesting FT may overfit in this setting. For the VeRA comparison on MT-Bench, DVoRA (DoRA + VeRA) achieves 5.0 on LLaMA-7B and 6.0 on LLaMA2-7B, outperforming VeRA (4.3 and 5.5) by 0.7 and 0.5 points respectively while using only 0.04% of parameters. On image/video-text understanding with VL-BART, DoRA (77.4% image, 85.4% video) matches or exceeds FT (77.3%, 87.5%) with ~6% of parameters, and beats LoRA (76.5%, 83.5%) by ~1% and ~2% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

DoRA extends the LoRA family of parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods by incorporating the magnitude-direction decomposition from Weight Normalization (Salimans & Kingma, 2016), but applied in a fundamentally different way: rather than training both components from scratch, DoRA initializes both from pre-trained weights and uses LoRA exclusively for the directional component while adding a small learnable magnitude vector. The paper's weight decomposition analysis provides a new diagnostic lens on why LoRA underperforms full fine-tuning, revealing that the gap is not merely a parameter-count issue but stems from LoRA's inability to decouple magnitude and direction updates. DoRA is also shown to be composable with other low-rank variants (VeRA → DVoRA) and quantized frameworks (QLoRA → QDoRA), positioning it as a general wrapper rather than a standalone method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DoRA offers a near-zero-cost drop-in replacement for LoRA (only ~0.01% additional parameters, no inference overhead) that consistently narrows the gap to full fine-tuning across LLMs, vision-language models, and diffusion models, making it practically significant for the PEFT ecosystem. The weight decomposition analysis provides a reusable diagnostic framework for understanding learning dynamics in other PEFT methods. Open questions include whether the magnitude-direction principle generalizes to audio or other modalities, whether the negative ΔD–ΔM correlation observed in FT is universal across architectures, and whether further decompositions (e.g., per-row vs. per-column norms) could yield additional gains. The method's integration into HuggingFace PEFT and the QDoRA/FSDP pipeline for consumer GPUs suggests rapid practical adoption.
