## Problem

Pre-training large time series foundation models has been hindered by three gaps: the absence of a large, cohesive public time series corpus, the heterogeneity of time series characteristics (varying lengths, channels, amplitudes, resolutions) that makes multi-dataset training difficult, and the lack of benchmarks that evaluate these models under realistic limited-supervision conditions. MOMENT addresses all three by compiling the Time Series Pile, designing strategies to handle heterogeneous series in a single pre-training run, and building a multi-task benchmark focused on zero-shot and linear-probing settings.

## Input / Output

MOMENT accepts a univariate time series of fixed length T=512 (longer series are sub-sampled, shorter ones zero-padded on the left) along with a binary mask indicating observed vs. unobserved timesteps. After reversible instance normalization, the series is split into 64 disjoint patches of length 8, each mapped to a D-dimensional embedding; masked patches receive a learnable [MASK] vector instead. Multivariate series are handled via channel independence (each channel processed independently along the batch dimension). The reconstruction head outputs per-patch values for imputation and anomaly scoring, while a swapped forecasting head projects the flattened patch embeddings to H future steps; for classification, the encoder's sequence-level representation is fed to a linear probe or SVM.

## Method

MOMENT is a T5-style encoder-only transformer (pre-norm, relative positional embeddings, no LayerNorm bias) that operates on patch embeddings rather than individual timesteps, quadratically reducing attention cost. Pre-training uses masked time series modeling: 30% of patches are uniformly masked with a learnable [MASK] embedding, and a lightweight linear reconstruction head is trained to minimize MSE over masked patches only. The model is pre-trained from scratch on the Time Series Pile (13M time series, 1.23B timestamps across 13 domains) in three sizes (40M, 125M, 385M parameters) for 2 epochs with AdamW, cosine LR schedule, and mixed-precision training. A key empirical finding is that random initialization converges to lower pre-training loss than initializing with Flan-T5 language-model weights, and that the frozen encoder transfers to image, text, and binary sequence classification on par with GPT-2/Flan-T5 of similar scale.

## Results

On long-horizon forecasting (linear probing, look-back 512), MOMENT-Large achieves near state-of-the-art MSE, e.g. 0.154 on Weather-96 and 0.387 on ETTh1-96, second only to PatchTST (0.149 and 0.370 respectively) while outperforming GPT4TS, TimesNet, and Time-LLM. In zero-shot classification on 91 UCR datasets, MOMENT achieves a mean accuracy of 79.4% (median 81.5%) without any dataset-specific fine-tuning, substantially outperforming TimesNet (57.2%) and GPT4TS (56.6%), though trailing TS2Vec (85.1%) and T-Loss (83.3%). For anomaly detection (linear probing), MOMENT achieves a mean adjusted F1 of 0.628 and VUS-ROC of 0.684, beating TimesNet (0.537/0.679) and GPT4TS (0.424/0.611), with k-NN slightly ahead on VUS-ROC (0.706). In imputation, MOMENT with linear probing achieves the lowest MSE across all ETT datasets (e.g., 0.031 on ETTm2 vs. 0.052 for GPT4TS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On long-horizon forecasting, MOMENT_LP is consistently second to PatchTST across all 9 datasets and 4 horizons, with gaps typically under 0.01 MSE; it clearly outperforms GPT4TS, TimesNet, Time-LLM, FEDFormer, DLinear, Stationary, and LightTS, while N-BEATS is competitive on Weather and Electricity but degrades sharply on ETTh2-720 (MSE 1.454 vs. MOMENT's 0.403). In zero-shot classification on 91 UCR datasets, MOMENT (mean 79.4%) beats TimesNet (57.2%), GPT4TS (56.6%), TST (65.8%), CNN (75.1%), and FCN (80.9% median but 80.9% mean), yet trails TS2Vec (85.1%), T-Loss (83.3%), and ResNet (82.5%)—all of which are trained per-dataset with labels. For anomaly detection, MOMENT_LP achieves the best mean adjusted F1 (0.628) and median (0.778), surpassing TimesNet (0.537), GPT4TS (0.424), Anomaly Transformer (0.492), and DGHL (0.425), though k-NN edges it out on VUS-ROC (0.706 vs. 0.684). In imputation, MOMENT_LP achieves the lowest MSE on every ETT dataset (e.g., 0.031 on ETTm2 vs. 0.052 for GPT4TS and 0.112 for TimesNet), while in zero-shot it outperforms all statistical interpolation methods except linear interpolation. On zero-shot short-horizon forecasting, statistical baselines (Theta, ETS) outperform all deep methods including MOMENT, though MOMENT beats ARIMA on several M3/M4 subsets.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOMENT builds on PatchTST's patch-based tokenization for time series and the T5 encoder architecture (with pre-norm and relative positional embeddings), while adapting the masked prediction pre-training objective from NLP/vision to time series using a learnable [MASK] embedding rather than zero-filling. Its key conceptual departure is from prior time series models (PatchTST, TimesNet, TST) that were each trained on a single dataset: MOMENT demonstrates that large-scale multi-dataset pre-training is feasible by compiling the Time Series Pile and addressing heterogeneity in length, amplitude, and channel count via fixed-length input, reversible instance normalization, and channel independence. It also positions itself against LLM-reprogramming approaches (Time-LLM, GPT4TS) by showing that a model trained from scratch on time series data outperforms billion-parameter LLMs on many tasks while requiring far fewer resources.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first open-source family of time series foundation models accompanied by a large public pre-training corpus (Time Series Pile, 13M series, 1.23B timestamps) and a systematic limited-supervision benchmark, lowering the barrier for the community to build on and evaluate time series foundation models. The finding that random initialization outperforms LLM-weight initialization for time series pre-training challenges the prevailing assumption that language pre-training provides a useful inductive bias for temporal data. Open questions include whether the fixed 512-token context and univariate channel-independence strategy limit performance on very long or highly correlated multivariate series, and whether the scaling laws observed (larger model → lower loss) translate into downstream task gains at larger scales.
