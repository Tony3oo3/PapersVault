## Problem

Despite the rapid proliferation of visually-conditioned language models (LLaVa, InstructBLIP, PaLI-3), key design decisions around image preprocessing, visual backbones, language model choice, and training procedure remain underexplored, and the absence of objective, standardized evaluations makes it difficult to attribute performance differences to specific architectural choices. This paper asks: which design decisions actually drive VLM capabilities, and can they be identified through controlled, single-variable ablations?

## Input / Output

The VLM consumes an RGB image (resized to 336×336 or 384×384 via letterbox padding, resize-crop, or naive aspect-ratio warping) concatenated with a text prompt, and produces either free-form natural language (for VQA, captioning, spatial reasoning) or normalized bounding box coordinates expressed as a text string (for referring-expression localization). Evaluation uses greedy decoding for determinism; localization accuracy is measured at task-specific IoU thresholds (0.5 for RefCOCO/+/g, 0.25 for OCID-Ref), and challenge tasks (VSR, POPE, TallyQA, AI2D) are reformulated as closed-set multiple-choice questions.

## Method

The paper systematically ablates four design axes of the standard patch-as-token VLM architecture (pretrained ViT backbone → 2-layer MLP projector → autoregressive LM) under a unified FSDP/BF16 training pipeline: optimization procedure, image processing and visual representations, language model selection, and scaling of training time and data. Key findings are that single-stage training (skipping the projector-alignment stage) saves 20–25% compute without performance loss; fusing DINOv2 and SigLIP patch features along the channel dimension yields the strongest visual representations, with 5–10% gains on localization and spatial-reasoning tasks; base LMs (Llama-2) match instruct-tuned LMs (Vicuña) quantitatively while producing less verbose, less hallucinated outputs; and training for two epochs with diverse data (LRV-Instruct) significantly outperforms single-epoch training. These insights are consolidated into the PRISM family of 7B and 13B VLMs, which strictly outperform LLaVa v1.5 and InstructBLIP across all 12 benchmarks in the paper's standardized evaluation suite, with statistical significance assessed via Z-score normalization and one-sided Fisher T-tests.

## Results

The best PRISM model (PRISM-DINOSIGLIP 7B) achieves 80.97 on VQAv2, 65.27 on GQA, 77.78 on RefCOCO, 54.12 on OCID-Ref, 88.12 on POPE, and 66.70 on TallyQA, strictly outperforming LLaVa v1.5 7B across all 12 benchmarks. At 13B, PRISM-DINOSIGLIP reaches 81.66 on VQAv2, 66.13 on GQA, and 79.39 on RefCOCO. The paper reports that PRISM models save more than 30% training compute relative to LLaVa v1.5 (via single-stage training and fused visual backbones) while achieving these gains. The largest absolute improvements over LLaVa v1.5 7B are on localization (RefCOCO: +22.7 pts) and TextVQA (+9.5 pts).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaVa v1.5, PRISM-DINOSIGLIP wins on all 12 benchmarks at both 7B and 13B scale. At 7B, the largest margins are on RefCOCO (77.78 vs. 55.12, +22.7 pts), RefCOCO+ (73.08 vs. 49.47, +23.6 pts), and TextVQA (55.64 vs. 46.13, +9.5 pts), while VQAv2 improves by 4.4 pts (80.97 vs. 76.54). Against InstructBLIP 7B, PRISM achieves substantially larger gains: GQA improves by 16.9 pts (65.27 vs. 48.41), TallyQA by 51.2 pts (66.70 vs. 15.51), and AI2D by 22.8 pts (55.65 vs. 32.90); InstructBLIP does not report localization results. The paper also notes that PRISM models are trained with single-stage training (eliminating the projector pretraining stage) and fused DINOv2+SigLIP visual backbones, which together account for the performance gains while reducing training compute by over 30%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaVa v1.5 architecture and training pipeline (Liu et al., 2023b), which has become the de facto template for open VLMs, and on the feature-fusion approach from LERF (Kerr et al., 2023) for combining visual representations from different backbones. Rather than proposing a new architecture, the paper's contribution is a systematic, controlled ablation across four design axes—optimization procedure, image processing/visual representations, language model choice, and scaling—using a standardized 12-benchmark evaluation suite. It departs from prior VLM work by treating design choices as hypotheses to be tested with statistical significance rather than as inherited defaults, and by showing that several widely-adopted practices (multi-stage training, frozen visual backbones, instruct-tuned LMs) are either unnecessary or actively harmful.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides both a practical recipe (single-stage training, fused DINOv2+SigLIP features, base LMs, two epochs, diverse data) and a reusable evaluation framework that the community can adopt for future VLM development. Key open questions remain: whether findings generalize to Perceiver-based or patch-downsampling architectures, to scales beyond 13B, and to open-ended multi-turn dialogue settings that the standardized benchmarks do not capture. The paper also highlights an underexplored safety concern—removing language-only co-training data degrades safeguards in base-LM VLMs—pointing to a tension between performance and safety that the field has not yet resolved.
