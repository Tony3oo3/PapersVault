## Problem

Large-scale, high-quality multi-modal datasets for 6D object-in-hand pose estimation are scarce, with existing works limited to two-finger grippers, fingertip-only tactile sensing, or small real-world collections that cannot handle multi-finger occlusion scenarios. This gap hinders the deployment of accurate in-hand perception in the Perception-Planning-Control paradigm for dexterous robotic manipulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICML20242041/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images, depth images (post-processed to replicate Kinect Azure TOF noise characteristics), a local touch point cloud computed via forward kinematics from 620–679 activated taxels spanning fingertips, pulp, and palm, and proprioception data (finger joint angles and hand poses). The output is a 6D object pose (rotation matrix R and translation vector t) in the robot camera frame, recovered from predicted 3D keypoints via least-squares fitting against a pre-selected keypoint template on the object mesh.

## Method

VinT-Net comprises two sub-modules: a Sensing Aggregation Module that processes color via UNet, depth and touch point clouds via separate PointNet++ networks, then fuses color and depth pixel-wise and merges touch features with depth features to produce a fused visual-tactile embedding F_vt; and a 3D Keypoint-Based Pose Estimation Module that feeds F_vt into three MLP heads predicting per-point semantic labels, object center, and keypoint offsets. Training uses a multi-task loss combining Focal Loss for segmentation and L1 Loss for center/keypoint regression, followed by clustering and least-squares fitting to recover the final 6D pose. The dataset generation pipeline bridges sim-to-real by modeling each taxel as a force sensor with non-linear response in MuJoCo, replicating real curved-surface taxel distributions via 3D scanning, and rendering photorealistic RGB-D in Blender with HDRI illumination and sensor-specific depth noise.

## Results

On the VinT-6D dataset, training on the combined VinT-6D split (Sim + Real) consistently outperforms either split alone across all 7 objects, with ADD(S) AUC scores ranging from 83.76 (Stick) to 97.09 (Power Drill). The largest gains from combining simulated and real data appear on Power Drill (+5.37 over Sim-only) and Blue Bottle (+6.63 over Sim-only). In direct comparison against prior methods on the tomato soup can, VinT-Net achieves 82.43 ADD-0.05d, surpassing PVN3D (74.60) and Object-Hand-Pose (76.74).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VinT-Net outperforms both baselines on the tomato soup can ADD-0.05d metric: it achieves 82.43 versus PVN3D (He et al., 2020) at 74.60 (+7.83) and Object-Hand-Pose (Wen et al., 2020) at 76.74 (+5.69). The Object-Hand-Pose baseline was re-implemented with the paper's three-finger Trx hand to ensure a fair hardware comparison. Beyond the direct method comparison, the ablation in Table 4 shows that adding touch and proprioception to vision-only consistently improves performance across all objects (e.g., Tomato Soup Can: 86.78 → 94.65, +7.87; Large Shaker: 87.04 → 94.66, +7.62). Under increasing occlusion (20%–50%), the vision+touch model degrades far less than vision-only (88.81 vs. 80.43 at 50% occlusion), confirming the robustness benefit of multi-modal fusion in multi-finger grasping scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 6D object-in-hand pose estimation line of work (PVN3D, DenseFusion, Object-Hand-Pose) by introducing the first large-scale multi-modal dataset (VinT-6D) that integrates vision, whole-hand touch, and proprioception for multi-fingered robotic hands. It departs from prior visual-tactile datasets (VITA, PoseFusion, FeelSight) in three key ways: (1) whole-hand tactile sensing covering fingertips, pulp, and palm rather than fingertips alone, (2) supporting both three- and four-fingered hand configurations, and (3) providing both a 2M-sample simulation split and a 0.1M-sample real-world split with sub-millimeter motion-capture ground truth. The VinT-Net baseline adopts the pixel-wise RGB-D fusion strategy from DenseFusion and the 3D keypoint voting paradigm from PVN3D, but augments them with a PointNet++-based tactile branch and a multi-modal fusion module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VinT-6D addresses a critical bottleneck in robotic in-hand manipulation: the absence of a publicly available, well-aligned, multi-modal dataset at scale for multi-fingered hands. The demonstrated sim2real bridging (Table 3) and the robustness gains under 50% occlusion (Table 6) validate the dataset's utility for the 'Perception-Planning-Control' paradigm. However, the paper acknowledges limitations: only 25 objects and 10 real-world objects limit generalization, VinT-Net is an instance-level method with a simple fusion strategy, and category-level or zero-shot object-in-hand pose estimation remains open. The dataset's whole-hand tactile representation and calibrated multi-sensor alignment protocol could become a reference standard for future tactile-visual research.
