## Problem

Causal analysis of customer churn under multiple interventions faces three unresolved challenges: (1) churn outcomes are binary and time-monotone, making direct low-rank tensor completion on the outcome tensor inappropriate; (2) no data-driven method exists to cluster interventions with similar effects over time, reducing the treatment space; and (3) standard matrix/tensor completion assumes uniform missingness, ignoring the endogeneity of treatment assignment that introduces confounding bias. This paper is among the first to provide a principled causal framework combining grouped latent factors with non-asymptotic error guarantees for churn analysis.

## Input / Output

The method takes N customers each with d-dimensional covariates, a k-dimensional binary treatment vector (yielding 2^k possible interventions indexed as a third tensor mode), T time points of retention status, and censoring indicators. Only the single realized treatment trajectory per customer is observed, so the potential outcome tensor (N × T × 2^k) is mostly missing. The output is the estimated hazard parameter tensor Θ, from which survival probabilities under all interventions are computed to derive individual optimal treatments and data-driven cluster labels grouping interventions with homogeneous effects.

## Method

The core model is a tensorized latent factor block hazard model where the parameter tensor Θ = S ×₁ U₁ ×₂ U₂ ×₃ M uses a Tucker decomposition with a binary membership matrix M on the intervention mode, replacing a continuous factor with discrete cluster assignments. Churn outcomes are modeled as Y ~ Bernoulli(f(θ)) via a 1-bit tensor completion framework, where f is a log-concave link function (e.g., logistic), and the time-monotone retention probability is S_i^(a)(t) = ∏_{s=1}^t f(θ_{i,s,l}). Estimation proceeds by maximizing an IPTW-weighted log-likelihood (using CBPS-estimated propensity scores to adjust for confounding) via projected gradient descent on (S, U₁, U₂) with alternating nearest-neighbor spectral clustering updates on the third mode. The paper establishes non-asymptotic Frobenius-norm bounds on tensor recovery error and misclassification rate bounds under a signal-to-noise ratio condition, with the error scaling as r₁r₂r₃(N∨T)/(NT·max(r₁,r₂,r₃)) · polylog(N+T+L).

## Results

On the Bank Customer Churn dataset, the proposed tensorized latent factor block hazard model achieves a C-index of 0.58±0.02 and average time-dependent AUC of 0.57±0.02, outperforming the best competing methods (SVM at 0.49 C-index, gradBoost at 0.50 C-index) by 8–9 percentage points in C-index. On the E-commerce Customer Churn dataset, the proposed method attains 0.41 C-index and 0.44 AUC, again leading all baselines. In simulation studies, the proposed method dominates all competitors in both cumulative regret and decision accuracy across all combinations of N, T, and k.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method clearly outperforms both survival baselines listed in the comparison set. Against Random Survival Forests (Ishwaran et al., 2008, 'surv-RF'), the proposed model gains 12 percentage points in C-index (0.58 vs. 0.46) and 17 points in AUC (0.57 vs. 0.40) on the bank churn data. Against Cox-modelBoost (Binder et al., 2009), the advantage is 12 points in C-index (0.58 vs. 0.46) and 15 points in AUC (0.57 vs. 0.42). Among the broader set of 11 baselines (including binary classifiers like SVM, gradBoost, and survival models like Cox-PH), the proposed method achieves the highest C-index and AUC on both real datasets. In the simulation experiments, the proposed method also dominates all 11 competitors in cumulative regret and decision accuracy across all tested configurations of sample size, time horizon, and treatment dimensionality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads: 1-bit tensor completion (Cai & Zhou 2013; Wang & Li 2020), low-rank tensor factorization with non-asymptotic guarantees (Xia et al. 2021), and tensor block model clustering (Han et al. 2022), into a single causal inference framework for customer churn. The key conceptual departure from prior tensor completion work is applying low-rankness to the latent parameter tensor rather than the observed binary data tensor, motivated by the time-monotone nature of churn. For confounding adjustment, it adopts the covariate balance propensity score (Imai & Ratkovic 2014) rather than the more common debiased matrix completion approach (Mandal & Parkes 2019; Agarwal et al. 2021), and introduces data-driven spectral clustering of interventions as an alternative to prior-knowledge-based grouping (Laber et al. 2014; Ma et al. 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is among the first works to provide non-asymptotic error bounds for a low-rank tensor block hazard model in a causal setting, filling a theoretical gap for practitioners who need guarantees on both parameter recovery and intervention clustering accuracy. The practical value lies in automatically identifying homogeneous intervention groups, which reduces the treatment space and makes optimal retention strategies more implementable. Key limitations include the restriction to time-invariant treatments and the strong ignorability assumption (all confounders must be observed); the authors note that extending to time-varying treatments and latent ignorability (Lewis & Syrgkanis 2021) are natural but unresolved directions. The framework's reliance on a correctly specified link function and the need for sufficient minimum retention probability (p_min) also constrain applicability in settings with very high churn rates.
