## Problem

CNN-based ligand binding site prediction methods suffer from four critical limitations: defective representation of irregular protein shapes via fixed-size voxelization, sensitivity to protein rotation, insufficient characterization of surface geometry, and poor generalization across variable protein sizes. While E(3)-equivariant GNNs naturally resolve the first two issues, naively applying them still underperforms CNN baselines because they fail to capture fine-grained surface geometry and remain vulnerable to protein size shift.

## Input / Output

The method takes a protein's 3D atomic structure (coordinates and 5-dimensional chemical features per atom) and generates solvent-accessible surface probes via MSMS with a 1.5 Å probe radius, yielding ~24,000 surface points per protein on average. It outputs per-atom binding site probabilities (used with mean-shift clustering to identify binding site centers), plus an E(3)-equivariant coordinate vector per atom that encodes the predicted relative direction to the nearest ligand atom. The model operates directly on the atom graph without any voxelization or fixed-size cropping, so it handles proteins of arbitrary size.

## Method

EquiPocket comprises three modules: (1) a local geometric modeling module that extracts E(3)-invariant descriptors (distances, angles, relative positions) from each surface atom's surrounding MSMS probes; (2) a global structure modeling module that processes the full protein graph via GAT on chemical bonds followed by EGNN on spatial edges; and (3) a Surface-EGNN module performing multi-channel equivariant message passing over the surface subgraph, where each node carries both an invariant feature and a 3×2 equivariant position matrix initialized with the atom coordinate and its local surface-probe centroid. A novel dense attention output layer adaptively weights the hidden features from all message-passing layers based on the spatial density distribution of neighboring atoms, mitigating over-smoothing on small proteins and under-propagation on large ones. Training combines Dice loss for binding site classification with a cosine loss on the equivariant coordinate output to predict the relative direction to the nearest ligand atom.

## Results

EquiPocket achieves DCC (success rate at 4 Å) of 0.423, 0.337, and 0.545 on COACH420, HOLO4K, and PDBbind2020 respectively, with corresponding DCA values of 0.656, 0.662, and 0.721. The failure rate is only 5.1%, the lowest among all compared methods. With just 1.70M parameters, EquiPocket outperforms the strongest CNN-based baseline (DeepSurf, 33.1M params) by 9–10% in DCC across all three benchmarks while using roughly 20× fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EquiPocket outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset for both DCC and DCA. Against the strongest CNN-based method DeepSurf (33.1M params), EquiPocket gains +3.7 DCC on COACH420 (0.423 vs 0.386), +4.8 on HOLO4K (0.337 vs 0.289), and +3.5 on PDBbind (0.545 vs 0.510), while using ~20× fewer parameters and a comparable failure rate (5.1% vs 5.4%). Versus the best spatial-graph baseline SchNet, EquiPocket improves DCC by 2.5× on COACH420 (0.423 vs 0.168) and 2.1× on PDBbind (0.545 vs 0.263), demonstrating that equivariant surface message passing with explicit geometric features is far more effective than distance-only spatial GNNs. Topological GNNs (GAT, GCN, GCN2) perform poorly (DCC < 0.05) because they ignore 3D spatial structure entirely. The geometric method Fpocket and ML method P2rank, while having zero failure rate, are substantially outperformed (e.g., P2rank DCC 0.366 vs EquiPocket 0.423 on COACH420), confirming that learned equivariant representations surpass hand-crafted features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant CNN-based paradigm (DeepSite, Kalasanty, DeepSurf) that voxelizes proteins into fixed-size 3D grids, instead adopting an E(3)-equivariant GNN framework built on EGNN (Satorras et al., 2021) and its multi-channel extension GMN (Huang et al., 2022). The key architectural departure is the integration of MSMS-derived surface probe geometry as explicit local features and a dense attention output layer to handle variable protein sizes—neither of which appears in prior equivariant GNN work. The paper also positions itself against simpler GNN baselines (GCN, GAT, SchNet) that lack surface geometric awareness, showing that equivariance alone is insufficient without explicit surface modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that naive application of equivariant GNNs actually underperforms CNN-based methods for binding site prediction, a useful negative result that clarifies why surface geometry must be explicitly encoded rather than assumed to emerge from equivariant message passing. The dense attention mechanism addressing protein size shift is a practical contribution that could transfer to other variable-size molecular tasks. Open questions include whether the MSMS surface probe pipeline (a fixed-radius geometric preprocessing step) can be replaced by fully learned representations, and whether the approach generalizes to other protein-ligand tasks beyond binding site localization (e.g., affinity prediction, docking).
