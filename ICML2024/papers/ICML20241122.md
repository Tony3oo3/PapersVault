## Problem

Autoregressive language models suffer from inherent sequential and unidirectional generation that cannot be remedied by scale, while Generative Masked Language Models (GMLMs) offer parallel decoding but lack mathematical understanding of their fundamental power and limitations. Specifically, there is no theoretical framework connecting the training objective (masked-prediction losses) to sample complexity, inference speed, or the representational constraints of parallel decoding architectures.

## Input / Output

The model takes a source text sequence (encoded by a T5-style encoder) and a partially masked target sequence as input, where at each refinement step the decoder receives the current hypothesis with some positions masked. It produces a full target text sequence by iteratively refining all positions in parallel over a fixed number of steps (typically 4), starting from pure noise. The key constraint is that each decoder forward pass must produce a product distribution over positions, which fundamentally limits the Markov chain transitions the model can implement.

## Method

The paper develops a theoretical framework showing that the asymptotic sample complexity of weighted pseudolikelihood training is governed by the Poincaré constant of the corresponding block-dynamics Markov chain, proving that larger masks strictly improve statistical efficiency (Theorem 1). It further shows that Transformers restricted to parallel decoding can only implement Markov chains whose transitions are product distributions over positions, and that such independent-parallel samplers can be exponentially slower than k-Gibbs samplers on distributions with strong coordinate dependencies (demonstrated on Ising models). Empirically, the authors adapt T5 into PaDIR (Parallel Decoding by Iterative Refinement), where each decoder forward pass predicts updates to all target positions simultaneously, achieving 2–3× speedup in machine translation with minimal quality loss. Key practical findings include the importance of large masking ratios, custom vocabulary, distillation from AR models, and positional attention, while identifying 'stuttering' as a characteristic failure mode tied to the product-distribution limitation.

## Results

PaDIR (the paper's non-autoregressive GMLM-based parallel decoding approach) achieves BLEU and BLEURT scores comparable to autoregressive T5 on WMT14, WMT16, and WMT17 machine translation benchmarks. With 4 decoding steps, PaDIR achieves a >2× end-to-end speedup at the median and >5× at the 99th percentile latency on TPU v3 (batch size 1), corresponding to 7–8× fewer decoder forward passes. However, word-level stutter (consecutive repeated words not in the reference) is 2 or more times more frequent for the non-autoregressive model, indicating a quality gap in readability that BLEU/BLEURT do not fully capture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between PaDIR (non-autoregressive, 4-step iterative refinement) and autoregressive T5 on WMT14 En→De, WMT16 En→Ro, and WMT17 En→Cs. PaDIR matches AR T5 on both BLEU and BLEURT across all three datasets, confirming that the speed gain comes at minimal quality cost in standard metrics. The speed advantage is substantial: 4 parallel decoding steps replace 28–33 sequential AR steps, yielding >2× median and >5× 99th-percentile latency reduction. The key trade-off is in word-level stutter, which is 2+× more frequent for PaDIR, suggesting that while n-gram-overlap metrics are matched, the non-autoregressive model struggles more with strong inter-token dependencies (consistent with the paper's theoretical analysis of product-distribution transitions in Section 2.4). The paper notes that for longer sequences the constant number of GMLM decoding passes becomes increasingly advantageous over AR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: the statistical learning theory of pseudolikelihood estimation (Besag, 1975; Huang & Ogata, 2002) and the Markov chain mixing analysis used in inference for non-autoregressive sequence generation (Ghazvininejad et al., 2019; Savinov et al., 2022). It draws on functional inequality techniques from the discrete diffusion literature (Koehler et al., 2023; Caputo & Parisi, 2021) to connect training-time statistical efficiency with inference-time mixing speed, and extends the classical information matrix equality to weighted pseudolikelihood losses. A key departure from prior work is the characterization (Proposition 3) that parallel-decoding Transformers can only implement Markov chains with product-distribution transitions, a structural limitation not previously formalized for this architecture class.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous theoretical framework for GMLMs, providing practitioners with principled guidance on masking size (larger is statistically better), the fundamental representational ceiling of parallel decoding, and why GMLMs struggle with strongly correlated outputs (the 'stuttering' error mode). The product-distribution restriction on parallel-decoding Transformers is a conceptual result that explains a persistent empirical gap: GMLMs have not robustly surpassed autoregressive models beyond machine translation. Open questions include designing Markov chains that mix fast under strong correlations while remaining implementable by standard architectures, and whether the theoretical separation between k-Gibbs and independent parallel sampling can be bridged by architectural modifications (e.g., positional attention, which the paper identifies as helpful empirically).
