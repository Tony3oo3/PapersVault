## Problem

Existing robust conformal prediction methods (e.g., Cauchois et al., 2023) guard against the worst-case joint distributional shift of (X, Y) without distinguishing between the identifiable covariate shift (X) and the unidentifiable conditional shift (Y|X). This monolithic treatment is overly conservative in practice because the X shift is estimable from data while the Y|X shift is not, and conflating them inflates prediction intervals unnecessarily. The paper asks: can we decompose the distributional shift into its two components and apply targeted, more efficient treatments to each?

## Input / Output

The method consumes a training set of (X, Y) pairs, test covariates X, a nonconformity score function s(x,y), a robust parameter ρ controlling the f-divergence radius of the Y|X shift, and optionally a known likelihood ratio w(x). It outputs a prediction interval Ĉ_{f,ρ}(X_{n+1}) that achieves approximate 1−α marginal coverage under the target distribution Q. A key structural detail is that the X shift is handled by reweighting calibration scores with the estimated likelihood ratio, while the Y|X shift is handled by inflating the quantile level via the function g_{f,ρ}^{-1}(1−α), so the two sources of shift enter the interval construction through fundamentally different mechanisms.

## Method

WRCP (Weighted Robust Conformal Prediction) reweights calibration-fold nonconformity scores by the covariate likelihood ratio w(x) = dQ_X/dP_X to correct for the identifiable X shift, then computes a weighted quantile at the inflated level g_{f,ρ}^{-1}(1−α) to protect against the worst-case Y|X shift within an f-divergence ball of radius ρ. The key technical insight is that the worst-case quantile inflation is distribution-free (it depends only on f and ρ, not on the observed data), so a single adjusted confidence level suffices across all covariate values, enabling a clean generalization of both weighted conformal prediction (ρ=0) and robust conformal prediction (w≡1). For high-dimensional covariates where estimating w(x) is difficult, D-WRCP introduces a doubly robust correction: it decomposes the coverage probability into a training-data term and a test-data term involving a conditional CDF estimator m̂(x;t), so that the miscoverage depends on the product of the weight estimation error and the CDF estimation error rather than either alone. The f-divergence formulation (KL, TV, χ²) is more flexible than pointwise odds-ratio bounds (Γ-selection), allowing the average shift to be bounded even when the pointwise shift is unbounded.

## Results

In simulations (X ∈ R^50, n=2000, target coverage 90%, true ρ*=0.01), WRCP and D-WRCP achieve near-exact 90% coverage when ρ=ρ*, with coverage remaining close to target as ρ increases, while CP and WCP undercover and RCP is overly conservative (often producing infinite-length intervals). On the NSLM dataset (target coverage 80%), WRCP and D-WRCP achieve approximate 80% coverage across a wide range of ρ values with substantially shorter prediction intervals than RCP. On the ACS Income classification task (target coverage 80%, 76-dimensional features), both proposed methods deliver valid and efficient prediction sets, with D-WRCP showing particular advantage given the high-dimensional covariates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: standard conformal prediction (CP, Vovk et al. 2005), weighted conformal prediction (WCP, Tibshirani et al. 2019), and robust conformal prediction (RCP, Cauchois et al. 2023). CP and WCP consistently fail to achieve the target coverage level (90% in simulations, 80% on real data) because they do not account for the Y|X shift; WCP improves upon CP by adjusting for the X shift but still undercovers. RCP, which guards against the worst-case joint (X,Y) shift, is overly conservative — it achieves coverage well above the target and frequently outputs prediction intervals of infinite length (replaced by an upper bound of 17 in the simulation plots). WRCP and D-WRCP win on both validity and efficiency: they achieve approximately exact coverage at the true ρ* and maintain near-target coverage across a range of ρ values, while producing prediction intervals that are substantially shorter than RCP's. D-WRCP further improves upon WRCP in high-dimensional settings (e.g., the 76-dimensional ACS Income task) by leveraging double robustness, so its miscoverage depends on the product of estimation errors rather than either alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends two previously separate lines of conformal prediction: weighted conformal prediction for covariate shift (Tibshirani et al., 2019) and robust conformal prediction for worst-case joint distributional shift (Cauchois et al., 2023). The key conceptual departure is decomposing the joint (X,Y) shift into an identifiable X shift (handled by reweighting) and a non-identifiable Y|X shift (handled by an f-divergence ball), rather than treating the joint shift as a single worst-case object. The doubly robust variant (D-WRCP) draws on semi-parametric doubly robust calibration ideas from Yang et al. (2022) and Qiu et al. (2022), generalizing them to the combined shift model and achieving a local (rather than global) dependence on nuisance estimation error. The application to ITE sensitivity analysis connects to the f-sensitivity model of Jin et al. (2022), showing that the causal inference problem fits exactly within the proposed framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that prediction intervals under distributional shift can be both valid and informative, rather than valid but excessively wide (as with RCP) or informative but invalid (as with standard CP). This matters for high-stakes deployment where overly conservative intervals reduce the utility of uncertainty quantification. Open questions include: how to calibrate the robust parameter ρ from limited target data, whether the X/Y|X decomposition is optimal or other decompositions might be more efficient in specific settings, and how to extend the framework to multi-group or sequential shift models. The doubly robust variant's local estimation error dependence is a meaningful technical improvement for high-dimensional covariates where estimating the likelihood ratio is challenging.
